# Movement Ecology of Imperilled Fish in a Novel Ecosystem: River-Reservoir Movements by Razorback Sucker and Translocations to Aid Conservation

#ichthyology #Catostomidae #endangered_species #movement_ecology

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is an endangered, endemic species of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The species is long-lived and exhibits a periodic life-history strategy characteristic of bet-hedging in variable environments. Fish in this study averaged 485 ± 57 mm TL (mean ± SD) for Colorado River arm captures and 492 ± 39 mm TL for translocated fish. The species experiences a recruitment bottleneck in early life stages. Age, maturity parameters, fecundity, and specific growth rates are not reported in this document. The species is hypothesized to have evolved to use spatially and temporally disjunct habitats including the Colorado River Delta, volcanically impounded reaches, and lentic environments created by high water events.

**Habitat Associations**: [[Razorback Sucker]] utilize a variety of habitats including mainstem rivers, smaller tributaries, floodplain wetlands, and reservoirs. The species occupies novel ecosystems created by damming and lentification of rivers. In [[Lake Powell]], fish utilize lacustrine-transition zones (inflow areas) of both the [[Colorado River]] arm and [[San Juan River]] arm. Upstream river habitat includes the unfragmented Colorado River system and the waterfall-fragmented San Juan River. Fish aggregate below the [[Piute Farms Waterfall]] (6 m tall barrier, approximately 30 km upstream of river-reservoir transition) during spawning periods when temperatures and flows approach spawning conditions. Spawning habitat in the San Juan River may occur 30–40 km upstream of the waterfall where fish aggregate downstream of rapids and cobble bars in spawning condition.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Extensive movement occurred within and across river and reservoir habitats. Of 722 fish captured in the [[Colorado River]] arm of [[Lake Powell]] (2014–2016), 36% (n = 107 of 295 re-encountered fish) were detected outside the capture area, including upstream in the Colorado or Green rivers or across the reservoir in the San Juan River arm. Fourteen fish moved more than 600 km. The distribution of movement distances showed significant leptokurtosis (kurtosis = 3.86, P = 0.01), indicating most individuals moved relatively short distances while a smaller contingent made long-distance movements. Fifty-six fish were detected at the [[Tusher Diversion]] weir on the Green River, approximately 300 km upstream of the reservoir. Fourteen individuals were detected approximately 600 km upstream in the upper Green River. Seventeen acoustically tagged fish (of 44 total) moved from the Colorado River arm through lacustrine habitat to the San Juan River arm, a minimum distance of 170 km. In the San Juan arm, 29% (95% CI [21–36%]) of 147 fish captured in 2017 and 20% (95% CI [12–30%]) of 74 fish captured in 2018 moved upstream to the waterfall within a year. Fish at large for longer periods were more likely to be encountered outside the capture area (GLM: likelihood ratio = 57.8, P < 0.001), but fish total length was not a significant predictor (likelihood ratio = 3.2, P = 0.08). For fish that left the capture area, distance moved was not correlated with days at large (r = −0.08, df = 105, P = 0.39) or fish total length (r = 0.04, df = 105, P = 0.67).

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: Not reported in detail. The document notes that inflow areas offer abundant food resources such as phytoplankton and zooplankton, and that early life stage razorback sucker feeding ecology has been studied relative to other sucker species.

**Environmental Drivers**: Movement patterns appear associated with spawning behavior, with fish aggregating below barriers during periods when temperatures and flows approach spawning conditions. The [[Piute Farms Waterfall]] emerged as a complete barrier to upstream movement since 2001 (except for a 2-week inundation in 2011) after reservoir elevations dropped and the river cut a new channel through deposited sediments. Mean annual discharge from the Colorado and Green rivers combined was approximately eight times that of the San Juan River (2014–2018). Fish movements may be driven by search for spatiotemporally dynamic habitats offering favorable conditions including spawning habitat, feeding opportunities, cover in turbid water, and complex habitat structure.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: All razorback sucker occurring in [[Lake Powell]] were stocked from upstream rivers and moved downstream into the reservoir. Between 2002 and 2015, a total of 627 fish (of 722 captured in Lake Powell 2014–2016) had stocking records, with total basin-wide stocking ranging from 11,941 fish (2002) to 59,970 fish (2010). Most fish were stocked in the Green River (n = 574) and Colorado River (n = 37), with smaller numbers from the Gunnison River (n = 33). Recruitment to adulthood is rarely documented in areas with most access to river habitat, suggesting connectivity alone is insufficient for population recovery. The species is listed as endangered under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: Primary threats include habitat alterations from dams and reservoirs, fragmentation by barriers (both large dams and smaller diversion structures), restricted movement, altered flow and temperature regimes, decreased floodplain connectivity, and competition/predation from introduced fishes. Eight relatively large diversion structures on the Green, Colorado, Gunnison, and San Juan rivers present varying degrees of passage capability. The [[Piute Farms Waterfall]] represents a complete barrier preventing upstream access to approximately 30 km of river habitat. Non-native species such as [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*) pose additional threats. Early life stage recruitment bottleneck remains a critical limiting factor. Habitat degradation and blocked migration routes challenge conservation efforts.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: Recovery and maintenance relies on intense management through two federal recovery programs (Upper Colorado and San Juan River recovery programs) involving multidisciplinary stakeholders. Management actions include: (1) Coordinated stocking efforts with PIT-tagging prior to release; (2) Centralized database (STReaMS) for compiling stocking, capture, tagging, and detection records across the entire basin; (3) Installation and maintenance of PIT-tag antenna arrays at strategic locations including diversion weirs with fish passages; (4) Facilitated translocations to bypass barriers and provide access to upstream spawning habitat. For the San Juan River, annual pre-spawn translocations would be necessary for fish to gain access to spawning habitat upstream of the waterfall, as 80% of translocated fish (243 of 303 fish from 2016–2017 combined) returned downstream of the waterfall within a year. Management should promote and maintain connectivity while acknowledging that connectivity alone may not override other limiting factors such as temperature or rearing habitat necessary for successful recruitment. Habitats should be managed holistically, recognizing functional connections between rivers and reservoirs rather than as independent systems.
