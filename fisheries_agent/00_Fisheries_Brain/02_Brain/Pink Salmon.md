# Pink Salmon

## Summary
Pink salmon (Oncorhynchus gorbuscha) are a Pacific salmon species characterized by a two-year life cycle and anadromous migration pattern, wherein juvenile fry emerge in freshwater streams and rapidly migrate seaward, creating temporary high-density aggregations that represent significant prey availability for predatory fish and wildlife. As a keystone species in North Pacific ecosystems, pink salmon are important both as a food source for numerous predators during their vulnerable early life stages and as nutrient vectors that transport marine-derived nutrients back to freshwater and terrestrial ecosystems during spawning migrations. Their population dynamics and habitat requirements make them relevant indicators for assessing ecosystem health and informing conservation strategies in coastal and riverine environments.
