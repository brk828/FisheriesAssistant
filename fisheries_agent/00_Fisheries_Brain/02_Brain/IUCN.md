# Iucn

## Summary
The IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature) is a global authority that evaluates and classifies the conservation status of species, including fish, using standardized criteria to assess extinction risk and inform conservation priorities. In ichthyology and aquatic conservation, the IUCN functions as a coordinating body that establishes threatened species lists, supports working groups focused on particular taxa or ecosystems (such as freshwater fish groups), and guides international conservation policy and management strategies. Through its assessments and action plans, the IUCN provides the scientific framework that governments and conservation organizations use to protect endangered fish species and develop recovery programs.
