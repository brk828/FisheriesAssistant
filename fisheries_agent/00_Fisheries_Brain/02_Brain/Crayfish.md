# Crayfish

## Summary
Crayfish are freshwater decapod crustaceans that serve as important components of aquatic food webs, functioning as both predators of invertebrates and prey for fish, amphibians, and other vertebrates in streams, rivers, and wetlands. In ichthyology and fisheries ecology, crayfish are significant as natural forage organisms contributing to fish productivity and nutrition, while their introduction into non-native ecosystems can have substantial negative impacts on native aquatic communities, particularly affecting sensitive species like amphibians. These chitinous invertebrates are commonly studied in aquatic ecology research to assess ecosystem health, trophic dynamics, and the effects of invasive species on native biodiversity.
