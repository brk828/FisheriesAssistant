# Instream Flow Incremental Methodology

## Summary
The Instream Flow Incremental Methodology (IFIM) is a quantitative approach used to determine minimum water flow requirements needed to maintain suitable aquatic habitat for fish species, particularly endangered populations. This method integrates field measurements of microhabitat characteristics—such as water depth, velocity, and substrate composition—with species-specific habitat preferences to model how changes in streamflow affect the availability of suitable habitat. IFIM has been widely applied in water management and conservation planning to balance competing demands for river water while ensuring sufficient flows to support fish populations and ecosystem health.
