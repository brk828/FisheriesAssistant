# Endangered Species Act

## Summary
The Endangered Species Act (ESA) is landmark U.S. federal legislation that mandates the protection of fish species and aquatic ecosystems threatened with extinction through listing mechanisms, habitat conservation requirements, and permitting systems that regulate human activities affecting critical aquatic habitats. In the context of fisheries and conservation biology, the ESA establishes legal frameworks requiring federal agencies and private entities to conduct biological assessments, implement recovery plans, and obtain permits for research or management activities affecting listed fish species and their designated critical habitats. This legislation fundamentally shapes aquatic conservation policy by restricting damaging practices such as flow manipulation, habitat degradation, and water extraction while requiring stakeholders to adopt protective measures like riparian buffers and habitat restoration to ensure the survival and recovery of imperiled fish populations and freshwater ecosystems.
