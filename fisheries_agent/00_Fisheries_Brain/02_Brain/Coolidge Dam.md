# Coolidge Dam

## Summary
Coolidge Dam is a water management structure on the Gila River in Arizona that serves the San Carlos Irrigation Project and has significant implications for fish conservation and aquatic ecology in the desert Southwest. As a dam impoundment, it fundamentally alters riverine habitat conditions for native fish species that historically inhabited large, free-flowing desert river systems with natural flow fluctuations, making proposed modifications to the dam relevant for evaluating impacts on endemic species like bonytail chub and Colorado squawfish. The dam's management and any safety-related modifications are therefore subjects of environmental assessment in relation to conservation of the region's native aquatic fauna and their remaining suitable habitats.
