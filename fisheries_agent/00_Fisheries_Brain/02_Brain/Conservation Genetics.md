# Conservation Genetics

## Summary
Conservation genetics is the application of genetic analysis and theory to understand and preserve the genetic diversity of fish populations, particularly those facing extinction risk or living in fragmented habitats. In the context of endangered fishes like desert pupfish and razorback suckers, conservation genetics involves assessing allelic variation, population structure, and evolutionary significance to inform management decisions such as captive breeding programs, refuge design, and subspecies designation. This interdisciplinary approach combines molecular techniques with field sampling and policy analysis to ensure that conservation efforts maintain both the genetic integrity and adaptive potential of threatened aquatic populations.
