# Gila River

## Summary
The Gila River is a major tributary system in the southwestern United States that serves as critical habitat for multiple federally protected fish species, particularly in its desert and montane reaches across Arizona and New Mexico where naturally variable flow regimes and riparian corridors support endemic and threatened aquatic communities. As a river system characterized by high erosion potential, vulnerability to water withdrawal, and dynamic flood-driven ecological processes, the Gila River represents a key conservation focus for desert fish recovery efforts and aquatic ecosystem management in the arid Southwest. The river's diverse habitats, including free-flowing reaches, tributary systems, and associated riparian vegetation, are essential for maintaining populations of species like the razorback sucker and other endemic desert fishes adapted to these specialized southwestern aquatic environments.
