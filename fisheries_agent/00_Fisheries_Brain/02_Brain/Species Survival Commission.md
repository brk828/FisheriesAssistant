# Species Survival Commission

## Summary
The Species Survival Commission (SSC) is the largest global network of volunteer experts working for species conservation, established in 1949 as one of six voluntary networks within IUCN. As of 1994-1996, it comprised approximately 5,000 members in 170 countries organized into approximately 102 Specialist Groups (mostly taxonomic) plus disciplinary groups, with a mission to conserve biological diversity by developing and executing programs to save, restore, and manage species and their habitats. The Commission operates through a structure including a Chair, Deputy Chair, Steering Committee, Regional Members, and program offices in Switzerland, UK, USA, with core activities including Action Planning, species assessments, policy development, and maintaining the global expert network.
