# Suckers

## Summary
Suckers (family Catostomidae) are freshwater fishes native to North America and Asia, characterized by specialized bottom-feeding morphology including subterminal mouths adapted for benthic foraging and a Weberian apparatus connecting their swim bladder to the inner ear. In conservation and fisheries contexts, suckers are significant as native cypriniform fishes whose populations have declined due to habitat degradation, competition from invasive species, and altered river dynamics, making them important indicators of freshwater ecosystem health and subjects of restoration efforts in systems like the Colorado River. These ecologically important benthic feeders play crucial roles in aquatic food webs and sediment dynamics, and several species are of conservation concern in their native ranges.
