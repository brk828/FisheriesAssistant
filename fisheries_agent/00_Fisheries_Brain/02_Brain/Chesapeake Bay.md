# Chesapeake Bay

## Summary
The Chesapeake Bay is a major Atlantic estuarine system and historically significant habitat for anadromous fish species, particularly striped bass (Morone saxatilis), that has experienced considerable population decline and now serves as a focal point for fish restoration and conservation programs aimed at reestablishing self-sustaining wild populations in this critical breeding and nursery ground. As one of the largest and most productive estuaries in North America, the Chesapeake Bay represents an important case study in aquatic ecology and fisheries management, demonstrating both the ecological value of Atlantic coastal spawning systems and the challenges of restoring depleted fish stocks through hatchery programs and habitat rehabilitation efforts.
