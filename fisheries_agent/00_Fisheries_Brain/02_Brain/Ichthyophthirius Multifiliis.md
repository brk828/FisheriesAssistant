# Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis

## Summary
Ichthyophthirius multifiliis, commonly known as "ich," is a parasitic ciliate protozoan that causes one of the most significant infectious diseases in freshwater fish populations, both in wild and captive settings. The parasite infects the skin, gills, and fins of fish hosts, creating characteristic white spots and potentially causing severe morbidity and mortality if left untreated. In the context of fish restoration and hatchery operations, ich represents a critical disease management concern that must be controlled to ensure the health and survival of cultured fish destined for reintroduction into natural river systems.
