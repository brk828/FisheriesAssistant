# Pumpkinseed Sunfish

## Summary
The pumpkinseed sunfish (Lepomis gibbosus) is a small, highly adaptable centrarchid species commonly used as an indicator organism in freshwater ecological studies and fisheries management. Its tolerance for low dissolved oxygen conditions, prolific reproduction, and tendency toward stunted population dynamics in closed systems make it ecologically significant for understanding eutrophication, competition, and overpopulation dynamics in lakes and rivers. Pumpkinseeds are frequently documented in limnological research as a resilient competitor that can reach high densities while exhibiting poor individual growth, serving as both a model species for studying fish population regulation and a challenge for fisheries managers seeking to maintain balanced warm-water communities.
