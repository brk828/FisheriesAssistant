# Whirling Disease

## Summary
Whirling disease is a parasitic infection caused by the myxozoan parasite *Myxobolus cerebralis* that affects salmonid fishes, particularly young trout and salmon, causing neurological damage that leads to characteristic spinning or whirling behavior and skeletal deformities. The disease has significant implications for wild fish populations and hatchery operations, as it can cause high mortality rates in affected populations and spread through water systems, making it a major concern in fisheries management and conservation efforts. Whirling disease is often discussed in the context of hatchery programs and fish stocking practices, as artificial propagation and translocation of fish can facilitate the spread of the parasite to naive wild populations.
