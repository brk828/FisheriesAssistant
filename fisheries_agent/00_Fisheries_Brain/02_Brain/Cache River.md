# Cache River

## Summary
The Cache River is a significant aquatic system in Illinois that historically supported populations of the Cypress Minnow (*Hybognathus hayi*) and serves as a key reference site in ichthyological surveys documenting the distribution and conservation status of this species. As a sluggish, backwater-dominated drainage system characterized by cypress swamps, oxbow lakes, and soft substrates with accumulated woody debris, the Cache River represents the type of low-gradient, wetland habitat critical for understanding the ecological requirements and historical range of specialized cyprinids in the Mississippi River basin. The Cache River drainage is particularly important in conservation assessments because it remains one of the documented historical localities for H. hayi in Illinois, making it a focal point for evaluating species persistence and habitat loss in southeastern Illinois fluvial ecosystems.
