# Recovery Implementation Program

## Summary
A Recovery Implementation Program (RIP) is a structured, multi-stakeholder administrative and scientific framework established to coordinate recovery efforts for endangered aquatic species, typically mandated by regulatory mechanisms such as the Endangered Species Act's Section 7 consultation process. Within the context of the Upper Colorado River Basin, the RIP integrates biological research, monitoring data, habitat management, and operational modifications to dams and water management systems to address threats that jeopardize the continued existence of endangered fish species such as humpback chub, bonytail, Colorado squawfish, and razorback sucker. The program functions as both a planning document (through recovery action plans or RIPRAPs) and an operational coordination mechanism, synthesizing input from federal agencies, state wildlife departments, and scientific reviewers to guide management decisions and conservation actions.
