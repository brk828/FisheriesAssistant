# Basin and Range Orogeny

## Summary
The Basin and Range Orogeny was a geological event that established north-northwest trending basins in the modern drainage system of the American Southwest. This mountain-building episode caused progressive uplift and the development of northwest-flowing tributaries that eroded headward, capturing ancestral southwest-oriented rivers during the late Pliocene to early Pleistocene period. According to the source's geological hypothesis, this orogeny shifted the ancestral Rio Sonora/San Pedro headwaters toward Mexico, forcing living K. f. arizonense southward while leaving its fossil type locality at Benson, and eventually caused the San Pedro River to commence northward flow toward the Gila River as Arizona and Mexico drainages separated.
