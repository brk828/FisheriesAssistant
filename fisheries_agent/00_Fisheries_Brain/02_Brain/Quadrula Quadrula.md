# Quadrula Quadrula

## Summary
Quadrula quadrula, commonly known as the Macroshell or Mapleleaf mussel, is a freshwater bivalve characterized by its distinctively thick and heavy shell, which serves as a defensive adaptation against fish predation and other environmental stressors in riverine ecosystems. This species appears in ichthyological and conservation literature primarily as a bioindicator of mussel community composition and ecosystem health in impounded and flowing river systems, particularly in North American drainage systems such as the Tennessee River where its presence and population dynamics reflect responses to major hydrological changes like dam construction. Understanding Quadrula quadrula's ecology and distribution is important for assessing the impacts of water management on freshwater mussel populations and for informing conservation strategies in regulated river systems.
