# Walker Wildlife Area

## Summary
Walker Wildlife Area is a flooded gravel pit near Grand Junction, Colorado, that is permanently connected to the Colorado River via a 6-meter canal. During the 1974-76 study, this 25.6-hectare site with depths of 0.5-2.0 meters served as one of only two locations where razorback suckers were found in relatively large concentrations in the upper Colorado River Basin, with the species composing 0.8-4.2% of fish catches there. The site had a gravel bottom covered by thick silt layers and cobble-lined shores, and contained both native fish (43% of catch) and introduced species like carp, green sunfish, and black bullhead (nearly one-third of catch), with possible spawning attempts by razorback suckers observed but no evidence of successful reproduction, likely due to predation by introduced species.
