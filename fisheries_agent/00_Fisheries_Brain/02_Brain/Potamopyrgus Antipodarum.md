# Potamopyrgus Antipodarum

## Summary
Potamopyrgus antipodarum, commonly known as the New Zealand mudsnail, is an exotic hydrobiid gastropod that has become a problematic invasive species in North American freshwater ecosystems, particularly in spring-fed stream systems. As a non-native mollusk, it poses significant conservation challenges to endemic aquatic species and native macroinvertebrate communities, making it a key monitoring concern in sensitive habitats like Box Canyon Creek in Idaho. Its rapid colonization of permanent freshwater springs represents a critical threat to the ecological integrity of these rare and isolated aquatic systems in arid regions.
