# Central Platte River

## Summary
The Central Platte River is a 59-mile reach in central Nebraska that has been designated as critical habitat for the whooping crane and serves as habitat for other listed species including interior least tern and piping plover. This river reach is hydrologically connected to the South Platte and North Platte Rivers many miles upstream, making water use activities throughout the Colorado-Wyoming-Nebraska basin subject to ESA Section 7 scrutiny. The Central Platte River Recovery Program aims to improve target flows at Grand Island, Nebraska and protect at least 10,000 acres of habitat between Lexington and Chapman, Nebraska.
