# Zebrafish Summary

Zebrafish (Danio rerio) are small freshwater cyprinids native to South Asia that have become a crucial model organism in fish science and developmental biology, valued for their transparent embryos, rapid development, and genetic tractability. In the context of fisheries and conservation biology, zebrafish are often studied to understand fundamental principles of fish physiology, genetics, and responses to environmental stressors such as inbreeding depression, which manifests as reduced viability, impaired growth rates, and increased incidence of abnormal phenotypes. Their utility as a reference species makes them important for establishing baseline understanding of fish biology that can inform conservation strategies and fisheries management practices for commercially and ecologically significant species.
