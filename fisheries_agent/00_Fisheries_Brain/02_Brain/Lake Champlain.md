# Lake Champlain

## Summary
Lake Champlain is a major freshwater system on the Vermont-New York border that serves as an important habitat for both native and introduced fish populations, including landlocked American smelt and anadromous species. As documented in mid-20th century fisheries research, the lake supported significant commercial and ecological populations of smelt, with specific bay areas yielding substantial catches that were used to monitor population age structure and recruitment dynamics. The lake's ecological significance extends to its role as a potential invasion pathway and refuge system for introduced fish species that may threaten other connected aquatic ecosystems in the region.
