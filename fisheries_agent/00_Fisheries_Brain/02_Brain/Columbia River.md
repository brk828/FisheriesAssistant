# Columbia River

## Summary
The Columbia River is a major Pacific Northwest river system and critical geographic focus for anadromous salmonid (salmon and steelhead) conservation and restoration efforts in North America. The river basin encompasses numerous tributaries and watersheds where multiple U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service hatcheries were established under the Mitchell Act and other federal programs to mitigate fish habitat loss caused by large-scale dam construction, particularly the Grand Coulee Dam, and to sustain wild and hatchery fish populations. The Columbia River system represents one of the most significant regions for salmonid fisheries management, serving as the outmigration corridor and oceanic destination for millions of hatchery-reared and wild anadromous fish.
