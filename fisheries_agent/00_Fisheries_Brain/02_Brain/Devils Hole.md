# Devils Hole

## Summary
Devils Hole is a spring-fed aquifer in southern Nevada that serves as a critical refugium for the endangered Devils Hole pupfish (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), one of the most restricted fish species on Earth with a population confined to a single underground pool. The site represents an extreme example of endemism and conservation vulnerability, as the entire species depends on this single geothermal spring system with minimal genetic diversity and no geographic buffering against environmental disturbance. Beyond its ichthyological significance, Devils Hole's calcite vein deposits have also provided paleoclimate data spanning hundreds of thousands of years, making it valuable for understanding historical groundwater conditions and regional hydrological changes.
