# Colorado River Drainage

## Summary
The Colorado River Drainage refers to the extensive hydrological system and watershed encompassing the Colorado River and its tributaries, which is a critically important region for fish science and conservation due to its unique endemic species, altered flow regimes from dam operations, and ecological challenges from introduced species. In fisheries management and aquatic ecology contexts, the Colorado River Drainage represents a distinct biogeographic unit where conservation efforts focus on native fish species adaptation to highly modified river conditions and the management of non-native species interactions within this arid western North American system. This drainage has been a primary focus for fish reestablishment programs and predation studies examining how native and introduced species interact in one of the world's most extensively managed river ecosystems.
