# Coronado National Forest

## Summary
The Coronado National Forest in southeastern Arizona is a critical management area for endemic and endangered desert fish species, particularly within isolated riparian corridors such as Sycamore Canyon that support relict aquatic populations like the Sonora chub (*Gila ditaenia*). As a federal land managing agency, the Forest Service collaborates with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and state wildlife agencies to implement recovery plans and manage habitat conditions—including grazing impacts and water availability—essential for the conservation of these federally protected fish species adapted to the Sonoran Desert's harsh and fragmented aquatic ecosystems.
