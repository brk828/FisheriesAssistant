# Anguilla Rostrata

## Summary
Anguilla rostrata, commonly known as the American eel, is a catadromous fish species that inhabits freshwater riverine systems throughout North America, including major drainage systems like the Río Bravo/Rio Grande, where it occupies main channels, tributaries, and spring-fed habitats as part of its complex life history. This species is ecologically significant as a key component of freshwater and estuarine ecosystems, connecting oceanic and continental aquatic environments through its migratory behavior, while also serving as an important indicator species for assessments of riverine habitat quality and connectivity in conservation biology. Understanding the distribution and habitat requirements of A. rostrata across different freshwater systems is essential for evaluating ecosystem health and implementing effective management strategies for this species, which faces significant population declines from various anthropogenic stressors.
