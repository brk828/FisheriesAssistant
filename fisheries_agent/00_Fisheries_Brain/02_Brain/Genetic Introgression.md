# Genetic Introgression

## Summary
Genetic introgression in fish science refers to the flow of genetic material from one population or species into another, typically occurring when hatchery-reared or non-native fish interbreed with wild populations. In conservation contexts, this process poses a significant risk to wild fish populations because it can dilute locally adapted genes, reduce population fitness, and compromise the genetic integrity that wild populations have developed through natural selection to their specific environments. Management agencies have identified genetic introgression as a critical concern when considering fish reintroduction and supplementation programs, as uncontrolled breeding between introduced and native stocks can ultimately diminish the adaptive capacity and viability of wild populations.
