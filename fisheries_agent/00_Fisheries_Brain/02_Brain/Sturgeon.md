# Sturgeon

## Summary
Sturgeons (family Acipenseridae) are large, long-lived, anadromous or potadromous fish species characterized by their distinctive bottom-feeding morphology and notably lower fecundity compared to many other fish groups, typically producing around 200,000 eggs per spawning female. These ancient fish are ecologically and economically significant in freshwater and marine systems, particularly in North America where species like the pallid sturgeon (*Scaphirhynchus albus*) and shovelnose sturgeon (*S. platorynchus*) inhabit major river systems such as the Mississippi River, making them important targets for conservation and stock restoration efforts. Sturgeons are managed at federal fish hatcheries and are valued for both their ecological roles as benthic organisms and their cultural and fisheries significance, though many populations remain vulnerable to habitat loss and reproductive challenges.
