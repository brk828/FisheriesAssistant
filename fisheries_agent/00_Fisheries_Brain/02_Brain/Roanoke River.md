# Roanoke River

## Summary
The Roanoke River is a major river system in Virginia that serves as an important study site for fish ecology and conservation research, particularly regarding the physiological tolerances and habitat preferences of freshwater fish species across different stream segments. The river's upper drainage, including tributaries like Mason Creek, provides comparative ecological conditions between headwater and mainstream habitats, making it valuable for investigating how fish species adapt to varying physicochemical parameters such as dissolved oxygen, temperature, and pH. As a central Appalachian aquatic ecosystem, the Roanoke River supports diverse fish assemblages that have been documented in ichthyological surveys and used as a model system for understanding fish community structure and species-specific environmental tolerances.
