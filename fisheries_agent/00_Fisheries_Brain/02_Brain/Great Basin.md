# Great Basin

## Summary
The Great Basin is a vast endorheic (internally draining) desert region of the American Southwest characterized by isolated aquatic habitats, including endemic desert springs and disconnected water bodies that support highly specialized and geographically restricted fish populations. In fish conservation and aquatic ecology, the Great Basin is significant as a biodiversity hotspot for endemic species, particularly cyprinodontids and other desert fishes facing extinction due to habitat fragmentation, water loss, and human alteration of spring and groundwater systems. The region's hydrological isolation and extreme environmental conditions have fostered unique evolutionary lineages, making it a critical focus area for desert fish conservation efforts and refugium management strategies.
