# Pacific Salmon

## Summary
Pacific Salmon refers to anadromous fish species (primarily Oncorhynchus spp.) native to North Pacific ecosystems that undergo complex life history migrations between freshwater natal streams, estuarine transition zones, and open ocean environments, making them ecologically and culturally significant organisms in both marine and freshwater systems. These species are central to conservation biology and fisheries management, particularly regarding habitat restoration, artificial propagation through hatchery programs, and endangered species recovery efforts under regulatory frameworks such as the Endangered Species Act. Pacific Salmon serve as keystone species linking oceanic and freshwater ecosystems while supporting commercial, recreational, and subsistence fisheries, indigenous communities, and numerous dependent predator species across the Pacific rim.
