# Cottonball Marsh

## Summary
Cottonball Marsh is a spring-fed desert wetland habitat in the Death Valley region characterized by restricted geographic range and high endemism, making it a critically important but vulnerable refugium for specialized aquatic species with minimal genetic diversity and limited capacity to buffer against environmental disturbance or species introductions. As one of several isolated spring-fed marshes in the closed-basin desert ecosystem, Cottonball Marsh represents the type of highly specialized microhabitat that requires targeted conservation attention due to its role as a potential refuge for endemic fish populations facing threats from habitat alteration and introduced competitor species.
