# Radiotelemetry

## Summary
Radiotelemetry is a tracking technique used in fish science that involves surgically implanting small radio transmitters into fish bodies to monitor their movements, behavior, and survival in their natural aquatic environments. This method allows researchers to locate and follow individual fish over extended periods, providing critical data on habitat use, migration patterns, and population dynamics while minimizing the need for recapture. In riverine fish studies, successful radiotelemetry requires careful attention to transmitter size relative to fish body mass—typically maintaining transmitter weight at 2% or less of the fish's body weight—to avoid negative physiological effects and ensure the fish's natural behavior remains uncompromised.
