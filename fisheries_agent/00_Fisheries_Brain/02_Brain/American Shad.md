# American Shad

## Summary
American Shad (Alosa sapidissima) is a diadromous fish species of significant ecological and economic importance in North American Atlantic coastal waters, characterized by its anadromous migration pattern from marine environments into freshwater rivers for spawning. The species has served as a valuable subject in fisheries research, particularly in fish tagging and movement studies that have demonstrated its extensive migratory capabilities, with documented movements spanning up to 1,650 miles between spawning grounds in northern rivers (such as the St. Lawrence River) and wintering grounds in southern coastal areas (including the Chesapeake Bay). Conservation and recreational management of American Shad populations remain important concerns in North American fisheries due to their commercial and recreational value and their sensitivity to habitat degradation and dam obstruction in natal rivers.
