# Anadromous Salmonids

## Summary
Anadromous salmonids are fish species that are born in freshwater streams and rivers but migrate to the ocean to grow and mature before returning to their natal freshwaters to spawn, completing a complex life cycle that requires access to both marine and riverine ecosystems. These species, including Pacific salmon and Atlantic salmon, have been subjects of significant conservation concern and recreational and commercial fisheries management due to their ecological importance, economic value, and vulnerability to habitat fragmentation, dams, and other anthropogenic barriers that interrupt their migratory pathways. The introduction of anadromous salmonids to non-native waters, such as Pacific salmon species introduced to the Great Lakes, demonstrates both the species' adaptability and the potential ecological consequences of nonindigenous species transfers.
