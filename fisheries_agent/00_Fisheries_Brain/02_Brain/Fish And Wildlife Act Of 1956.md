# Fish And Wildlife Act Of 1956

## Summary
The Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956 is landmark federal legislation that established the legal authority and organizational framework for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to manage fish conservation, propagation, and disease control programs throughout the United States. This act provided the statutory basis for the establishment and operation of federal fish hatcheries and fish disease control facilities dedicated to sustaining fish populations and supporting both recreational and commercial fisheries. The legislation represented a comprehensive federal commitment to fish conservation and management, authorizing the Service to conduct research, maintain breeding and rearing facilities, and implement conservation measures to protect aquatic resources and support the nation's fisheries.
