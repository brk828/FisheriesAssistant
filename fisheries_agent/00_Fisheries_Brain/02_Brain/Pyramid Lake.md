# Pyramid Lake

## Summary
Pyramid Lake is a terminal freshwater lake in Nevada that serves as the primary habitat for the endangered cui-ui (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endemic catostomid fish species requiring specific management and recovery efforts. The lake functions as a critical spawning and rearing ecosystem within the Pyramid Lake/Truckee River system, where conservation efforts focus on maintaining population viability through fish passage structures like the Marble Bluff Fish Facility that enable lacustrine migrants to access upstream spawning habitat. Pyramid Lake represents a case study in aquatic conservation biology, involving multi-stakeholder recovery teams addressing habitat fragmentation, water allocation, and the restoration of anadromous-like migration patterns essential for the survival of this endemic species.
