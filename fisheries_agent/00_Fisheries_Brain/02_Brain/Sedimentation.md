# Sedimentation

## Summary
Sedimentation in aquatic ecology refers to the process of fine sediment particles accumulating and settling in streams, rivers, and aquatic habitats, which can significantly impact fish populations and ecosystem health. In the context of fish conservation and watershed management, sedimentation is of concern because excessive sediment deposition can degrade spawning habitat, reduce water clarity, alter stream channel morphology, and affect fish survival and reproduction. The study of sedimentation in experimental watersheds like Workman Creek helps scientists understand how land management practices and hydrological changes influence sediment dynamics and their downstream effects on native fish communities.
