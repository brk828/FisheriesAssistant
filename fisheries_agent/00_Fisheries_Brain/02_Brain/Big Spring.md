# Big Spring

## Summary
In the context of fish science and conservation biology, "Big Spring" refers to a discrete thermal spring habitat that serves as either a native refuge or an established population site for endemic and endangered fish species, particularly springfish and other thermophilic fauna adapted to geothermal aquatic ecosystems. These isolated spring systems are critical conservation targets because they represent distinct microhabitats with unique physicochemical conditions that support specialized fish populations found nowhere else, making them vulnerable to extinction from localized habitat degradation yet valuable as refugia for species recovery and translocation programs. The management and protection of such springs, whether preserving native populations or establishing introduced populations as genetic insurance against extinction, is central to endangered species recovery strategies in arid regions of North America.
