# Longnose Gar

## Summary
Longnose Gar (*Lepisosteus longirostus*) is a primitive freshwater fish species characterized by an elongated snout and needle-like teeth, inhabiting river systems across North America and serving as both an ecological indicator of riverine habitat quality and a significant faunal resource in archaeological and paleontological assemblages. In the context of aquatic ecology and conservation, longnose gar are important components of freshwater fish communities, particularly in river drainages of the southwestern United States such as the Rio Grande, Salt River, and Pecos River systems. As a relatively long-lived predatory species with specific habitat requirements, longnose gar populations reflect broader conditions of riverine ecosystems and have been utilized as archaeological evidence for understanding human subsistence patterns and fish resource availability in arid regions.
