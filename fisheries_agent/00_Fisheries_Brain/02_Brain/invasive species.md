# Invasive Species

## Summary
An invasive species in fish science refers to a non-native fish species that has been introduced into an aquatic ecosystem, either intentionally or accidentally, and subsequently establishes self-sustaining populations that can outcompete, prey upon, or otherwise negatively impact native fish communities and overall ecosystem integrity. Invasive fish species are distinguished from merely introduced or exotic species by their ability to spread and cause ecological harm, making them a significant concern for conservation biology and natural resource management, particularly in sensitive ecosystems like the Colorado River basin. The formal scientific study and categorization of invasive fish species emerged in the 1960s through collaborative efforts between ichthyological organizations to develop standardized terminology and management strategies for addressing their ecological impacts.
