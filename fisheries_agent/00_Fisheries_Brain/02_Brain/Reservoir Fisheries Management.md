# Reservoir Fisheries Management

## Summary
Reservoir Fisheries Management encompasses the scientific and practical approaches to sustaining productive fish populations and aquatic communities within impounded water bodies, while accounting for the unique limnological conditions created by dam construction, including altered nutrient cycling, water quality stratification, and downstream ecological effects. This management discipline integrates principles of fisheries biology with reservoir-specific considerations such as serial nutrient interception between cascading reservoirs, trophic status changes, and sediment dynamics that fundamentally differ from riverine or natural lake ecosystems. Effective reservoir fisheries management requires understanding how impoundment structures modify water chemistry, nutrient availability, and habitat conditions to maintain or restore fish populations and ecosystem functions across both the reservoir and downstream reaches.
