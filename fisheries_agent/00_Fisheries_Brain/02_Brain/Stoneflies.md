# Stoneflies

## Summary
Stoneflies (order Plecoptera) are aquatic insects whose nymphs serve as critical bioindicators of freshwater ecosystem health and water quality in streams and rivers. As sensitive organisms that require cool, well-oxygenated, and relatively unpolluted waters, stoneflies are widely used in fisheries science and aquatic ecology to assess the ecological integrity of lotic habitats and the suitability of environments for supporting fish populations. Their presence, diversity, and community composition reflect conditions such as flow regimes, temperature stability, and substrate quality, making them valuable indicators for conservation and management decisions regarding aquatic ecosystems.
