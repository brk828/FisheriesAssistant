# Teays River

## Summary
The Teays River is a significant paleogeographic and biogeographic feature that refers to an ancient river system that once flowed through the central United States before being disrupted by glaciation during the Pleistocene epoch. In fish science and conservation biology, the Teays River system is important for understanding historical drainage patterns and fish distribution, as it helps explain the current geographic separation and genetic differentiation of fish populations across modern river systems like the Missouri and Mississippi rivers. The concept is used to interpret phylogeographic patterns and population structure in stream-dwelling fishes, where populations that were historically connected through the Teays drainage system became isolated as the ancient river was diverted by advancing glaciers, resulting in the distinct upland-stream adapted populations observed today.
