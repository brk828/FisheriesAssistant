# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program

## Summary
The Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program is a comprehensive, multi-agency federal initiative coordinated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6) established to manage and recover endangered fish species in the Colorado River Basin through integrated research, propagation efforts, and adaptive management strategies. Operating under the framework of the Endangered Species Act, the program facilitates coordination among federal agencies, state wildlife divisions, and research institutions to implement conservation measures including instream flow management, population monitoring, and species propagation planning across critical river reaches. The program's structure, evident from its Biology Committee and annual researcher meetings beginning in the late 1980s, represents a long-term commitment to balancing water resource management with the recovery of native fish populations threatened by dam operations and habitat alteration.
