# African Clawed Frog

## Summary
The African Clawed Frog (*Xenopus laevis*) is a non-native amphibian species that has become established in aquatic ecosystems, particularly in California, where it poses significant ecological threats through predation on native fish larvae, competition for resources, and potential disease transmission. In the context of ichthyology and fisheries conservation, this invasive species is of concern because its introduction into sensitive aquatic habitats like the Santa Clara River system can disrupt native fish communities and contribute to the decline of endemic or endangered fish species. Understanding the distribution and impact of African Clawed Frogs is important for aquatic conservation management and for implementing strategies to prevent further spread and protect native fish populations.
