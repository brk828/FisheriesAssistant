# Toxaphene

## Summary
Toxaphene is a chlorinated camphene-based insecticide that was employed as a piscicide (fish poison) in mid-20th century fisheries management to selectively eliminate undesirable fish species such as carp and yellow perch from aquatic ecosystems. Developed and tested by fisheries agencies like the Arizona Game and Fish Commission in the 1950s, toxaphene was applied experimentally as a population control method to manage rough fish and improve conditions for more desirable game fish species. Though effective as a fish toxicant, toxaphene's use in aquatic systems raised environmental and ecological concerns that eventually led to restrictions and phase-out of this and similar chemical piscicides in modern conservation practice.

## Context
> This 1951 report by Jack E. Hemphill of the Arizona Game and Fish Commission documents the discovery and experimental application of **[[Toxaphene]]**, a chlorinated camphene insecticide, as a piscicide for controlling undesirable "rough fish" populations (primarily [[Carp]] and [[Yellow Perch]])...
