# Oncorhynchus Mykiss

## Summary
Oncorhynchus mykiss, commonly known as rainbow trout, is a highly adaptable salmonid species that has become a dominant introduced predator in many western North American aquatic ecosystems, particularly in tailwater fisheries below dams and in coldwater streams where it often displaces native fish species through predation. In conservation contexts, this species represents a significant ecological trade-off: while it supports economically important recreational fisheries in regulated river systems like those below Glen Canyon Dam, its introduction has documented negative impacts on native fish communities, as exemplified by its documented displacement of species like Lepidomeda vittata in southwestern creek systems. The species thrives in cold, clear, nutrient-rich environments created by dam operations, making it both a fisheries management tool and a conservation concern depending on regional management objectives and native species protection priorities.
