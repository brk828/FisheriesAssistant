# Trout

## Summary
Trout are a group of freshwater and anadromous fish species (including Brown, Rainbow, and Eastern Brook trout) that occupy significant ecological and economic roles in aquatic systems, serving as important indicators of water quality and primary targets for fisheries management and restocking programs. In scientific practice, trout are characterized by specific morphological features used in population monitoring, such as standardized scale collection sites on the body, and their reproductive biology is studied to understand fecundity patterns and life history strategies across different habitat types. Their presence in both freshwater streams and transitional estuarine environments makes them valuable model organisms for understanding habitat use, growth rates, and the effectiveness of artificial propagation efforts in conservation and fisheries science.
