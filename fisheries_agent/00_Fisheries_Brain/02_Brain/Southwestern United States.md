# Southwestern United States

## Summary
The Southwestern United States in fish science contexts refers to a biogeographic region encompassing aquatic ecosystems in states such as Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, and surrounding areas, which are characterized by unique freshwater fish assemblages and endemic species requiring specialized conservation attention. This region is significant for ichthyological research and management due to its arid and semi-arid hydrology, threatened native fish populations, and complex taxonomic issues that have necessitated coordinated policy approaches among federal agencies like the Fish and Wildlife Service. The establishment of scientific archives and databases documenting the region's ichthyofauna represents a critical infrastructure effort for consolidating knowledge on fish taxonomy, distribution, and conservation status in this ecologically distinctive area.
