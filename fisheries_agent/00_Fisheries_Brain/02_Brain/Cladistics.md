# Cladistics

## Summary
Cladistics is a systematic method for classifying organisms, including fish, based on shared evolutionary ancestry and the branching patterns of evolutionary history rather than overall morphological similarity. In fish science and conservation biology, cladistic approaches enable researchers to construct phylogenetic classifications that reflect true evolutionary relationships, facilitating more accurate identification of species groups, understanding of trait evolution, and informed conservation strategies. This framework has become increasingly important as ichthyologists recognize that traditional taxonomic systems must be reorganized to align with modern understanding of fish evolutionary lineages, particularly among groups like cyprinids and catfishes where morphological diversity can obscure true genetic relationships.
