# Colorado River Basin Fish Recovery

## Summary
Colorado River Basin Fish Recovery refers to the coordinated, multi-agency conservation effort to restore and maintain populations of native fish species, particularly endangered endemic species like bonytail chub and razorback sucker, throughout the Colorado River system and its tributaries. This initiative involves establishing refugial populations in hatcheries and managed water bodies, implementing monitoring protocols, and managing habitat conditions to counteract decades of environmental degradation from dam construction, water diversion, and non-native species invasion. The recovery program integrates research, captive breeding, translocation, and inter-agency collaboration among federal and state wildlife agencies to reverse population declines and achieve long-term species viability in their native ecosystems.
