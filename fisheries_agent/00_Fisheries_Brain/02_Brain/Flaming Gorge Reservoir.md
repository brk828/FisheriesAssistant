# Flaming Gorge Reservoir

## Summary
Flaming Gorge Reservoir is a large impoundment on the Green River created by federal dam construction as part of the Colorado River Storage Project, which fundamentally altered native fish habitat by replacing warm, turbid riverine conditions with cold, clear lacustrine environments. The reservoir became central to both fisheries management and conservation conflicts, serving as the site of fish eradication programs in the 1960s and later as a mitigation resource for hatchery production aimed at offsetting habitat losses to native species caused by the same water development project. Its creation exemplifies the ecological trade-offs between water resource development and the preservation of endemic desert fish communities that evolved in the pre-impoundment Colorado River system.
