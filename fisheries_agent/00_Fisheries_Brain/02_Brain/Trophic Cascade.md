# Trophic Cascade

## Summary
A trophic cascade is a series of ecological changes triggered by the addition or removal of a top predator or key species in an aquatic food web, where alterations at one trophic level propagate through lower levels and fundamentally restructure community composition and ecosystem function. In fish ecology and conservation contexts, trophic cascades are particularly significant when invasive fish species or changes in predator populations (such as alewife populations behind barriers) alter the abundance of intermediate predators and prey species, ultimately affecting primary productivity and water quality. Understanding trophic cascades is essential for predicting the ecosystem-wide consequences of species invasions, barrier removal, and predator management in freshwater and marine systems.
