## Summary
CITES (the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora) is an international treaty that regulates and restricts commercial trade in species threatened by overexploitation, requiring permits and documentation for the import and export of listed aquatic organisms to prevent their extinction. In the context of Mexican fisheries and conservation policy, CITES listings work in conjunction with domestic regulations like NOM-059-ECOL-1994 to establish legal frameworks that govern which fish species can be commercially harvested, traded, or exported based on their conservation status. Understanding CITES designations is essential for fisheries management and conservation biology, as it directly impacts which species are legally available for commercial exploitation and which populations require protection from international trade pressures.
