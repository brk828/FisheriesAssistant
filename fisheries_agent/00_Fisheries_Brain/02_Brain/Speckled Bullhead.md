# Speckled Bullhead

## Summary
The Speckled Bullhead is a native North American catfish species that was evaluated in mid-20th century comparative aquaculture studies as a candidate for commercial pond fish production in the U.S. Southeast, particularly in relation to introduced tilapia species and other freshwater fish. In experimental trials conducted at Auburn University's Agricultural Experiment Station during the late 1940s and 1950s, the Speckled Bullhead's production efficiency, growth rates, and market viability were assessed alongside species such as Channel Catfish, Java Tilapia, and Nile Tilapia under managed pond conditions. These foundational fisheries studies examined whether native bullhead species could compete economically and ecologically with introduced cichlids in commercial freshwater aquaculture systems during a period of significant agricultural innovation in regional fish farming.
