# Gill Net Selectivity

## Summary
Gill net selectivity refers to the differential capture efficiency of gill nets across fish of different sizes, shapes, and species, whereby mesh size determines which individuals are retained while others escape or are never encountered. This selectivity bias is critical in fisheries science because it means catch samples do not represent the true population structure—smaller individuals may be underrepresented or entirely absent due to mesh size constraints, while very large fish may avoid or pass through nets, leading to truncated or distorted estimates of age structure, population demographics, and stock assessment. Understanding and accounting for gill net selectivity is essential for accurate fisheries management and conservation, as failure to correct for these biases can result in misleading conclusions about population health and sustainable harvest levels.
