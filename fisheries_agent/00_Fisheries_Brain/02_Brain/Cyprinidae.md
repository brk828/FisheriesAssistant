# Cyprinidae

## Summary
Cyprinidae is the largest family of freshwater fishes, encompassing minnows, carps, shiners, and chubs distributed across North America, Europe, Asia, and Africa. Members of this family are characterized by pharyngeal teeth, the absence of teeth in the jaw, and typically small to moderate body sizes, making them ecologically important as forage fish, indicators of water quality, and subjects of conservation concern due to widespread threats from habitat degradation and non-native species competition. In conservation and aquaculture contexts, cyprinids present unique management challenges, as many native species exhibit high vulnerability to predation and environmental stressors during early life stages, necessitating specialized recovery strategies such as adult stocking programs.
