# Lake Lahontan

## Summary
Lake Lahontan was a large Pleistocene pluvial lake system in the Great Basin that is significant to fish science as a historical drainage basin whose extinct aquatic ecosystem influenced the biogeography and genetic structure of endemic fish species, particularly cui-ui and other endemic fishes now restricted to its relict terminal lakes. The lake's hydrological connections to other Great Basin watersheds during wetter climatic periods, such as potential spillover events from Sierra Nevada drainage systems, shaped patterns of fish dispersal and genetic diversity that continue to inform modern conservation strategies for these isolated populations. Understanding Lake Lahontan's paleoclimatic and geological history is essential for interpreting the evolutionary relationships and management priorities of remaining endemic fish species, such as decisions regarding hatchery spawning protocols based on age-class genetics and population structure.
