# Sculpins

## Summary
Sculpins are benthic (bottom-dwelling) fish characterized by their adaptation to life on the substrate of streams and aquatic habitats, making them less susceptible to capture by standard fishing methods compared to midwater species. In the context of conservation biology and aquatic ecology, sculpins serve as important indicators of habitat quality and are often studied in relation to environmental degradation and species vulnerability assessments, particularly in freshwater ecosystems where their bottom-dwelling behavior influences their exposure to anthropogenic threats and sampling biases in population surveys.
