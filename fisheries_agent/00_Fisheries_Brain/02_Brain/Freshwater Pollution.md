# Freshwater Pollution

## Summary
Freshwater pollution, in the context of fish science and aquatic ecology, refers to the contamination of inland water systems by organic compounds, ammonia, heavy metals, and other toxic substances that degrade water quality and threaten fish survival and ecosystem health. The presence or absence of fish species has historically served as a biological indicator of pollution levels, though this approach has significant limitations as fish may persist in waters with high organic and nutrient loading despite serious water quality degradation. Understanding freshwater pollution requires comprehensive assessment of multiple water chemistry parameters—including dissolved oxygen, pH, hardness, and specific toxic contaminants—rather than relying solely on fish presence as evidence of acceptable environmental conditions.
