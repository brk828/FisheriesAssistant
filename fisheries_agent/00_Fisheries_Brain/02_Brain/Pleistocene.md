# Pleistocene

## Summary
The Pleistocene epoch (approximately 2.6 million to 11,700 years ago) is significant in fish science and aquatic ecology as a period of dramatic climatic fluctuations and glacial cycles that profoundly shaped the distribution, evolution, and diversity of fish populations. During this time, repeated glacial advances and retreats created and destroyed aquatic habitats, isolated fish populations in refuge areas, and drove both speciation events and extinctions that have lasting effects on modern fish fauna and biogeography. Understanding Pleistocene biogeographic patterns, such as sympatric distributions and the formation of present-day species assemblages, is essential for interpreting contemporary fish diversity and informing conservation strategies for aquatic ecosystems.
