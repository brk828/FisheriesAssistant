# U.S. Forest Service

## Summary
The U.S. Forest Service is a federal agency responsible for managing public forest lands and their associated aquatic ecosystems, including streams, rivers, and springs that serve as critical habitat for endangered fish species such as Gila trout and Chihuahua chub in the southwestern United States. In the context of fish conservation and recovery, the Forest Service plays a key management role in implementing habitat restoration, monitoring protocols, and reintroduction programs for threatened aquatic species within designated national forests and wilderness areas, often collaborating with other agencies and conservation organizations on recovery initiatives. As custodian of extensive headwater and montane stream systems in the Gila River drainage and other southwestern watersheds, the Forest Service's land management decisions directly influence water availability, habitat quality, and the viability of recovery efforts for endemic and relict fish populations.
