# Endangered Species Recovery Program

## Summary
An Endangered Species Recovery Program is a coordinated, multi-institutional effort to prevent the extinction of threatened fish species through a combination of research, captive propagation, habitat management, and strategic reintroduction. In the context of Colorado River basin fish conservation, such programs involve federal and state agencies, recovery teams, and hatchery facilities working collaboratively to monitor wild populations, maintain genetically viable captive populations, and conduct extensive research to inform science-based recovery strategies. These programs are essential for species like bonytail, which have experienced near-extirpation and depend on intensive management interventions including hatchery production and experimental stocking to rebuild self-sustaining populations in their native riverine habitats.
