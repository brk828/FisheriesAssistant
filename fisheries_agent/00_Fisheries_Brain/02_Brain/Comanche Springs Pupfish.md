# Comanche Springs Pupfish

## Summary
The Comanche Springs Pupfish (*Cyprinodon* sp.) is a critically endangered cyprinodontid endemic to isolated thermal spring systems in the southwestern United States, serving as an important model organism for understanding the thermal ecology and conservation requirements of desert spring fishes. This species exemplifies the conservation challenges faced by spring-dependent aquatic fauna, as its extremely restricted range and dependence on specific geothermal conditions make it highly vulnerable to habitat alteration, water extraction, and introduced species competition. Research on the Comanche Springs Pupfish's thermal preferences and physiological adaptations has contributed significantly to broader understanding of how small, isolated fish populations persist in specialized environmental niches and informs management strategies for other endangered spring endemics.
