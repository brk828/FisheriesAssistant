# Great Artesian Basin

## Summary
The Great Artesian Basin is a vast underground aquifer system underlying much of inland Australia whose naturally discharged groundwaters create isolated mound springs—particularly prominent in arid northern South Australia—that form discrete, permanently flowing aquatic "island" ecosystems of critical importance for endemic fish conservation. These spring-fed wetlands emerge along fracture zones and fault lines where crystalline basement rocks meet Jurassic and Cretaceous sedimentary layers, providing stable, year-round freshwater habitats in otherwise arid regions. For ichthyology and conservation biology, the Great Artesian Basin's mound springs represent highly specialized and vulnerable ecosystems supporting unique fish populations adapted to isolated spring environments, making them essential subjects for understanding groundwater-dependent aquatic biodiversity and ecosystem resilience in water-scarce regions.
