# Upper Basin

## Summary
The Upper Basin, in the context of Colorado River fish science and conservation, refers to the upstream portions of major river systems (such as the mainstem Colorado River and its tributaries including the Salt, Verde, and Gila Rivers) that are geographically and hydrologically distinct from the Lower Basin management zone. This geographic designation is significant for fish recovery and conservation efforts because Upper Basin conditions—including dam operations, flow regimes, and habitat characteristics—directly influence the survival and recovery prospects of imperiled fish species in downstream Lower Basin areas, making coordinated management across both zones essential for successful conservation of native fish assemblages and their supporting food webs.
