# Territoriality

## Summary
Territoriality in fish refers to the behavior by which individual fish or groups defend and maintain exclusive access to specific areas of aquatic habitat, including resources such as food, shelter, and breeding sites. This behavior is fundamental to understanding population dynamics and spatial distribution in fish communities, as territorial establishment influences habitat use patterns, intraspecific competition, and ultimately population density and structure. The degree of territoriality varies among species and environmental conditions, with factors such as resource availability, population density, and sex ratios affecting the expression and intensity of territorial defense behaviors.
