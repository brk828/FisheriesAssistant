# Spawning Migration

## Summary
Spawning migration refers to the directional movement of fish from their typical feeding or rearing habitats to specific spawning grounds where reproduction occurs, often involving long-distance travel to natal streams or designated breeding areas. This behavior is essential for completing the life cycle of many fish species and is particularly important in conservation efforts for migratory species, as successful migrations depend on access to unobstructed river corridors and suitable spawning habitat. Understanding spawning migrations is critical for fisheries management and ecosystem health, especially for species that have experienced habitat fragmentation or population declines due to dams and water diversions.
