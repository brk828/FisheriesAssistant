# Bioindicator Species

## Summary
Bioindicator species are organisms whose presence, absence, or physiological condition reflects the quality and environmental characteristics of aquatic ecosystems, making them valuable proxies for detecting ecological change and water quality degradation. Because different fish and aquatic species possess specific physiological tolerances and intolerances to environmental parameters such as temperature, dissolved oxygen, and pollution, their distribution patterns and range shifts can be used to assess ecosystem health and monitor responses to environmental stressors. This makes bioindicator species essential tools in aquatic conservation biology and fisheries management for evaluating the impacts of water development, habitat modification, and other human activities on freshwater and marine ecosystems.

## Context
> These physiological tolerances (vs. intolerances) form the functional basis for using species' presence/absence and range shifts as proxies for environmental change (see [[Bioindicator Species]]).
