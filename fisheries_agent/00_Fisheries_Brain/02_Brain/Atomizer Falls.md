# Atomizer Falls

## Summary
Atomizer Falls is a geomorphological and hydrochemical boundary in the Lower Colorado River ecosystem, located near river kilometer 13.5, that marks a distinct transition zone characterized by sharp changes in water chemistry (particularly elevated dissolved CO₂ levels and lower turbidity/conductivity) and habitat structure. This location represents a physiological and chemical barrier that limits the upstream distribution of native fish species, particularly humpback chub and native suckers, making it an important reference point for understanding niche partitioning and species-specific habitat requirements in the Colorado River system. The falls and associated water chemistry gradients serve as natural boundaries for studying fish community structure and the environmental factors that constrain fish distributions in this managed river ecosystem.
