# Arctic Grayling

## Summary
Arctic Grayling (Thymallus arcticus) is a freshwater salmonid species characterized by its distinctive large, colorful dorsal fin and preference for cold, clear streams and lakes in northern regions. This species has experienced significant population declines and extirpations across much of its historical range in the lower 48 United States due to habitat loss, stream fragmentation, and competition from introduced species, making it a conservation concern in states like Montana where it now occupies only a fraction of its former distribution. As a cold-water specialist and indicator of ecosystem health, Arctic Grayling serves as an important focal species for stream conservation and restoration efforts in Arctic and subarctic aquatic ecosystems.
