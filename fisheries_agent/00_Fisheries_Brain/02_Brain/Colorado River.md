# Colorado River

## Summary
The Colorado River is a major southwestern North American river system that serves as a critical focal point for fish conservation and aquatic ecology research, particularly concerning endangered native species such as humpback chub and razorback sucker. Management of the Colorado River's fish communities is complicated by extensive damming infrastructure, especially Glen Canyon Dam, which has fundamentally altered flow regimes, temperature profiles, and habitat structure downstream through the Grand Canyon and into reservoirs like Lake Mead and Lake Mohave. Fisheries science and conservation efforts on the Colorado River involve coordinated management between multiple agencies and research institutions to monitor native fish populations, assess reproductive success, and develop recovery strategies for species adapted to the river's pre-dam ecological conditions.
