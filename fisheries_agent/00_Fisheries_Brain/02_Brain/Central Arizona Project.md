# Central Arizona Project

## Summary
The Central Arizona Project (CAP) is a large-scale water delivery system that diverts Colorado River water across Arizona, and it has become a focal point of aquatic conservation concern due to its documented impacts on endangered native fish species including the Gila Topminnow, Desert Pupfish, Loach Minnow, Spikedace, and Razorback Sucker. From a fisheries science and conservation perspective, CAP operations have prompted extensive Endangered Species Act consultations and biological assessments to determine whether water diversion and canal operations jeopardize the continued existence of these protected species, particularly in the Santa Cruz River Basin. The project exemplifies the complex regulatory and ecological conflicts between large-scale water infrastructure and the conservation of endemic desert fish populations in the American Southwest.
