# Dinosaur National Monument

## Summary
Dinosaur National Monument refers to a protected landscape area in the Green River watershed whose aquatic ecosystems were profoundly altered by the 1960s fish eradication and rehabilitation program conducted downstream of Flaming Gorge Dam. The monument's native Colorado River Basin fish fauna, including endemic cyprinids, were exposed to rotenone toxicant and subsequent detoxification efforts as part of a large-scale management intervention designed to shift fish communities toward non-native trout species in tailwater habitats. This case exemplifies the ecological consequences of chemical fish management in protected areas and the challenge of balancing native species conservation with recreational fisheries development in regulated river systems.
