# Plains Killifish

## Summary
The Plains Killifish (*Fundulus kansae*) is a small cyprinodontid fish native to prairie streams in south-central North America that has become a species of conservation concern in some southwestern aquatic ecosystems due to its establishment as a nonnative warmwater species. As a nonnative fish, it exhibits characteristics typical of invasive species, including obligate dependence on slackwater habitats, high reproductive capacity with multiple spawning events per year in warm, low-velocity environments, and potential competitive or predatory impacts on native species like the Humpback Chub. Research on Plains Killifish thermal tolerance and developmental rates has provided important data for understanding how nonnative warmwater fishes respond to temperature gradients and environmental conditions, contributing to broader knowledge about fish invasions and ecosystem management in altered aquatic systems.
