# Livestock Grazing

## Summary
Livestock grazing, in the context of aquatic ecology and fish conservation, refers to the practice of allowing domestic animals to forage in riparian zones and upland watersheds, which alters vegetation structure and hydrology in ways that degrade fish habitat. The removal of riparian vegetation and understory plants through grazing increases erosion, sedimentation, and stream temperature while reducing shade and food sources for aquatic organisms, particularly affecting sensitive species like Gila trout. When combined with fire suppression, livestock grazing contributes to forest overstocking and watershed degradation, making it a major factor in fish habitat decline and a key management concern for stream restoration efforts.
