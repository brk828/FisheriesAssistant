# Greaser Blackfish

## Summary
Greaser Blackfish (Orthodon microlepidotus) is a cyprinid fish species that was the most important forage fish for young largemouth bass in Clear Lake, California during this 1948 study. This source found that blackfish appeared in 28 bass stomachs with a total volume of 37.8 cc—more than any other forage species—and were preferred over the more abundant bluegill, likely because blackfish inhabit the littoral zone without close cover association and have slender, fine-scaled bodies that are easier to consume than deep-bodied, prickly bluegill. The study suggests that as lake-spawning plankton feeders, Greaser Blackfish may be extremely valuable as forage in fluctuating reservoirs whose food webs depend primarily on plankton.
