# Black Grub

## Summary
Black Grub is an encysted larval trematode (fluke) that appears as conspicuous black spots on the skin and fins of freshwater fish, with the blackness resulting from pigment deposits around the cyst wall rather than the worm itself. This parasite has a complex life cycle involving kingfishers as definitive hosts, freshwater snails (Helisoma species) as intermediate hosts, and various sunfish and bass species as fish hosts, with black spots developing 22 days after cercariae penetrate the fish.
