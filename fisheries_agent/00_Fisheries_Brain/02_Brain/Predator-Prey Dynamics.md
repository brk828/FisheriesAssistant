# Predator-Prey Dynamics

## Summary
Predator-prey dynamics in fish ecology refers to the complex interactions between predatory fish species and their prey organisms, including how predator populations regulate prey abundance and how prey availability influences predator growth, reproduction, and distribution within aquatic ecosystems. These dynamics are particularly important in conservation contexts, especially when non-native predatory species like muskellunge are introduced into systems, as they can dramatically alter native fish communities and ecosystem balance through selective predation. Understanding predator-prey relationships is essential for managing fish populations, predicting community structure changes, and implementing effective conservation strategies in both natural and managed aquatic environments.
