# Colorado River System

## Summary
The Colorado River System, in the context of fisheries and aquatic ecology, refers to the integrated network of water resources, dams, diversions, and associated aquatic habitats managed across multiple southwestern U.S. states under the Colorado River Compact and subsequent interstate agreements. Fish science and conservation efforts within this system address the complex challenges of maintaining native fish populations and aquatic biodiversity while managing competing demands for water allocation among agricultural, municipal, and environmental stakeholders across the Lower and Upper Basin states. The system's ecological significance lies in its role as habitat for endemic and threatened fish species, though its heavily regulated hydrology presents ongoing challenges for fish conservation and restoration.
