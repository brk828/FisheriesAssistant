# Flint River

## Summary
The Flint River is a Georgia river system that has served as an important study site for understanding introduced fish species and their ecological impacts, particularly following the establishment of invasive Flathead Catfish (*Pylodictis olivaris*) around 1950. This river, especially the 50-km reach between Albany Dam and Newton, has been the focus of quantitative fisheries research examining population dynamics, abundance, and growth rates of non-native predatory fishes in freshwater ecosystems. As a transitional aquatic system connecting to the larger Apalachicola River drainage, the Flint River represents a key location for monitoring how introduced species alter native fish and mollusk communities in southeastern United States river systems.
