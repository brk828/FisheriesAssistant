# Lake Mead

## Summary
Lake Mead is a large reservoir on the Colorado River that functions as a critical habitat for native and non-native fish species in the Lower Colorado River Basin, serving as both a fisheries resource and a focal point for conservation efforts aimed at endangered native species like razorback suckers. As one of a chain of interconnected but ecologically distinct reservoirs within the Colorado River system, Lake Mead represents a key study area for understanding how dam-created impoundments affect fish community composition, species distribution, and the ecological interactions between native and exotic fish populations. The reservoir's management involves cooperative fisheries programs between state and federal agencies that must balance recreational fishing opportunities with conservation objectives for threatened native fish species.
