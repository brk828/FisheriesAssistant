# Perca Flavescens

## Summary
Perca flavescens, commonly known as yellow perch, is a temperate freshwater teleost fish species notable in aquatic ecology and fisheries science for exhibiting dynamic sex ratio patterns that shift with age and life stage, primarily due to differential fishing-related mortality affecting males more heavily than females. This species demonstrates habitat segregation by sex, with adult females occupying different depth zones or salinity conditions than males, a behavioral pattern relevant to understanding population structure and vulnerability to exploitation. Yellow perch serves as an important model organism in conservation biology for studying how anthropogenic pressures such as selective fishing and environmental changes influence sex ratio demographics and population sustainability in freshwater ecosystems.
