# Desert Spring Endemism

## Summary
Desert spring endemism refers to the phenomenon where fish species evolve in isolation within geographically restricted desert spring and stream systems, resulting in high rates of species and subspecies uniqueness found nowhere else on Earth. These isolated aquatic habitats, such as those in the Gila River Basin and similar arid regions, create evolutionary "islands" where populations diverge rapidly from their ancestral forms, leading to extraordinary biodiversity concentrated in small areas highly vulnerable to extinction. Conservation of desert spring endemic fishes requires targeted protection of these specific spring ecosystems, as the loss of a single spring habitat can mean the irreversible extinction of entire species that have nowhere else to survive.
