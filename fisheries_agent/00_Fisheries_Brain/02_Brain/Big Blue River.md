# Big Blue River

## Summary
The Big Blue River is a tributary system in northeastern Kansas that served as an important reference site in mid-20th-century ichthyological surveys documenting fish population recovery and drought resilience in Great Plains prairie streams. Compared to other Kansas river basins affected by the severe 1952-1956 drought, the Big Blue River demonstrated greater hydrological stability and maintained more robust populations of rough fish and forage fish species, including Red Shiners, Sand Shiners, and Gizzard Shad, making it a valuable case study for understanding differential drought impacts on regional fish fauna. This river system has been extensively documented through historical collections and long-term monitoring, providing critical baseline data for understanding prairie stream fish ecology and conservation strategies in semi-arid regions.
