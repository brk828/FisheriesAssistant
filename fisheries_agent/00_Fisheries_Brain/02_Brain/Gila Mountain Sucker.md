# Gila Mountain Sucker

## Summary
The Gila Mountain Sucker (Pantosteus clarki) is a native endemic sucker species of the Gila River basin in the southwestern United States that has become a subject of conservation concern, particularly regarding its exposure to introduced populations through artificial water infrastructure such as Salt River Project canals. As a benthic cyprinid adapted to the specific ecological conditions of its native Gila River drainage, this species represents an important component of the region's native fish fauna and its management is relevant to broader efforts to protect desert aquatic ecosystems and endemic ichthyofauna. The species' presence in monitoring surveys and its documented introduction pathways highlight the challenges of maintaining genetic integrity and ecological separation of native fish populations in heavily engineered river systems.
