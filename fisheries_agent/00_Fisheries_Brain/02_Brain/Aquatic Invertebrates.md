# Aquatic Invertebrates

## Summary
Aquatic invertebrates are small animals without backbones that inhabit freshwater and marine ecosystems, serving as critical indicators of water quality and essential food sources for fish populations. In fish science and aquatic ecology, these organisms—including insects, crustaceans, mollusks, and other benthic fauna—are fundamental to ecosystem health as they process organic matter, cycle nutrients, and form the base of aquatic food webs that support fish communities. The abundance and diversity of aquatic invertebrate communities are commonly used as bioindicators of habitat quality and ecosystem integrity, making them central to both conservation assessments and fisheries management decisions.
