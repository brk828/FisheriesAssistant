# Coachella Canal

## Summary
The Coachella Canal is an unlined earthen irrigation channel in southeastern California that conveys Colorado River water through the Colorado Desert to agricultural valleys within the Salton Sea basin, functioning as a lotic freshwater habitat with slow-current conditions similar to a lowland river system. As a constructed waterway, the canal has become an important ecological feature for studying introduced fish populations (such as Flathead Catfish) and native aquatic communities, though its partial lining as part of salinity control efforts represented a significant management intervention affecting fish and benthic invertebrate populations. The canal's habitat heterogeneity and flowing-water characteristics made it a valuable baseline study site for understanding aquatic resource composition and the ecological impacts of water infrastructure modification.
