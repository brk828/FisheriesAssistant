# Aquatic Vegetation

## Summary
Aquatic vegetation encompasses submerged and emergent plants in freshwater ecosystems that form essential fish habitats by providing food, shelter, and spawning substrate while also influencing water quality and oxygen dynamics. In fisheries management and conservation contexts, aquatic vegetation is a critical ecological component whose density and composition directly affect fish populations, community structure, and ecosystem productivity, making it a central concern in both habitat restoration and biological control strategies. The species composition and abundance of aquatic plants—ranging from submerged species like *Utricularia* and *Ceratophyllum* to rooted emergent macrophytes—can significantly impact fish recruitment, feeding opportunities, and predator-prey interactions across different water systems.
