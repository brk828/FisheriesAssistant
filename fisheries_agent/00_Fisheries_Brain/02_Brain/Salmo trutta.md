# Salmo Trutta

## Summary
Salmo trutta, commonly known as brown trout, is a freshwater salmonid species native to Europe that has become one of the most widely studied model organisms in ichthyology and aquatic ecology due to its ecological importance, widespread distribution, and amenability to laboratory and hatchery maintenance. This species is extensively used in experimental fish biology, physiological research, and ecotoxicological studies, while also serving as a key subject for investigations into feeding ecology, diet composition, and responses to environmental conditions in natural stream ecosystems. Brown trout's significance in conservation biology and fisheries management stems from both its intrinsic value as a coldwater species indicator and its role in supporting recreational fisheries across multiple continents.
