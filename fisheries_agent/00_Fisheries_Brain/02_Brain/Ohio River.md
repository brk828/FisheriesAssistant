# Ohio River

## Summary
The Ohio River serves as a significant freshwater aquatic ecosystem and model system for studying fish populations, community dynamics, and environmental impacts on ichthyofauna in North American riverine systems. As a major tributary of the Mississippi River drainage, it provides critical habitat for diverse fish species and has been extensively studied to understand how environmental stressors—including atomic radiation, invasive species establishment, and anthropogenic pollution—affect fish physiology, population genetics, and ecosystem health. The river's ecological importance makes it a key focal point for conservation biology research and fisheries management in the eastern United States.
