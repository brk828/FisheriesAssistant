# Fish Anesthesia

## Summary
Fish anesthesia refers to the pharmacological immobilization of fish using chemical agents such as MS-222 (tricaine) to facilitate safe handling, medical procedures, and research activities in both wild and captive settings. Understanding the physiological mechanisms of fish anesthesia—including how anesthetic compounds are absorbed across gill membranes and distributed to the central nervous system—is critical for developing standardized, species-appropriate anesthetization protocols in fisheries management, conservation efforts, and aquatic research. Effective fish anesthesia minimizes stress and injury to specimens during capture, sampling, and experimentation, making it an essential tool in ichthyological and conservation biology work.
