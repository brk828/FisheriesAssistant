# Razorback Sucker

## Summary
The Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large native catostomid fish endemic to the Colorado River basin, characterized by a distinctive dorsal keel or hump-like structure behind its head, and is recognized as a federally endangered species due to habitat loss, water management alterations, and competition from non-native species. As a big-river specialist requiring specific flow regimes and spawning substrates, the Razorback Sucker serves as a key indicator species for Colorado River ecosystem health and has been a central focus of conservation and restoration efforts, including captive breeding and translocation programs across multiple western states and refuges. Its ecological significance lies in its role as a large omnivorous benthic feeder within native Colorado River fish communities, making it important for understanding aquatic ecosystem dynamics and the effects of anthropogenic alterations to riverine systems in the southwestern United States.
