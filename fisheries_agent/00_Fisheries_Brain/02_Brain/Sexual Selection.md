# Sexual Selection

## Summary
Sexual selection in fish science refers to the evolutionary process by which certain traits become exaggerated or preferred within a population because they enhance reproductive success, particularly through mate choice and intrasexual competition. This mechanism is especially significant in cichlid fishes, where rapid diversification in traits such as coloration, body morphology, and behavioral displays—often driven by female preferences for specific male characteristics—has contributed to exceptional speciation rates in isolated lake systems. Sexual selection can act independently of natural selection and represents a powerful evolutionary force that shapes both individual fitness and population-level biodiversity patterns in aquatic ecosystems.
