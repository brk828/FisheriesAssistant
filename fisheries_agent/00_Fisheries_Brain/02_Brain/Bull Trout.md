# Bull Trout

## Summary
Bull Trout are a native, cold-water salmonid species valued in conservation and fisheries science as an indicator of high-quality aquatic ecosystem health due to their dependence on pristine habitat conditions and sensitivity to environmental perturbation. As wild, self-sustaining populations, Bull Trout populations directly reflect underlying stream habitat quality, including geomorphic processes, water chemistry, and availability of spawning and overwintering habitat, making them superior biological indicators compared to hatchery-origin or exotic trout species. Their presence and population dynamics thus serve as a critical measure of ecosystem integrity in freshwater systems, with habitat restoration and dam removal efforts often prioritized to support their recovery and maintain their role as sentinel species for watershed health.
