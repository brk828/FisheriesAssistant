# Temperature Preference

## Summary
Temperature preference in fish refers to the range of water temperatures that an individual species actively selects and maintains when given a choice among available thermal conditions, reflecting the fish's physiological optimum and ecological niche requirements. This concept is fundamental to understanding fish behavior, distribution, and vulnerability to environmental stressors such as thermal pollution from industrial sources like power plant effluents. Temperature preference data is typically measured using standardized laboratory apparatus with thermal gradients and is essential for conservation planning, assessing impacts of climate change, and predicting how fish populations will respond to altered thermal regimes in aquatic ecosystems.
