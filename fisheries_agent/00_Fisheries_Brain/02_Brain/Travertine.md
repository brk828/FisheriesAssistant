# Travertine

## Summary
Travertine in aquatic ecology refers to porous, calcium carbonate mineral deposits that accumulate around thermal springs and freshwater habitats, creating structurally complex substrates that support specialized microbial and invertebrate communities. These deposits provide essential microhabitats for adapted organisms such as scraper snails and interstitial crustaceans, which exploit the hard surfaces for algal grazing and the crevices and tube-like channels for refuge and foraging in thermally unique ecosystems. Travertine formations are particularly significant in desert spring systems where they function as the primary structural habitat supporting endemic and thermophilic fauna adapted to these geographically isolated environments.
