# Circadian Rhythm

## Summary
Circadian rhythm in fish refers to the endogenous biological cycles of approximately 24 hours that regulate physiological and behavioral processes such as feeding activity, metabolism, growth, and locomotion in response to environmental light-dark cycles. These internally driven daily rhythms allow fish to synchronize their activities with predictable environmental changes and optimize energy allocation for feeding, growth, and other vital functions. Understanding circadian rhythms is critical in aquaculture and conservation biology, as photoperiod manipulation and light regimes can significantly influence growth rates, feeding behavior, and overall fitness in both cultured and wild fish populations.
