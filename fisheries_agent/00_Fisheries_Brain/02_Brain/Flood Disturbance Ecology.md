# Flood Disturbance Ecology

## Summary
Flood Disturbance Ecology is the study of how periodic flooding events structure and maintain fish communities and aquatic ecosystems, recognizing that natural flood regimes are essential disturbances that shape species composition, habitat heterogeneity, and ecological stability in river systems. This concept emphasizes that floods are not merely destructive events but rather critical ecological processes that prevent ecological stagnation, reset competitive hierarchies among fish species, and sustain the dynamic physical and biological conditions necessary for diverse fish assemblages. Understanding flood disturbance ecology is fundamental to conservation efforts, particularly in arid and semi-arid regions where native fish communities have evolved to depend on natural flood regimes that are increasingly altered or eliminated by human water management practices.
