# Douglas Reservoir

## Summary
Douglas Reservoir is a Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) storage reservoir in the eastern United States that served as a key study site for mid-20th century research on fish distribution, growth rates, and condition factors across game and panfish species. As a relatively shallow reservoir with comparable morphology to Cherokee Reservoir, Douglas was characterized by turbid water conditions and elevated organic loading from its sparsely forested, agricultural watershed, making it an important model system for understanding how physicochemical factors—particularly thermal stratification and dissolved oxygen availability—influence fish habitat use and population dynamics in managed impoundments.
