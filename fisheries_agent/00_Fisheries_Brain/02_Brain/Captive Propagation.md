# Captive Propagation

## Summary
Captive propagation in fish science refers to the controlled breeding and rearing of fish species in artificial hatchery environments, typically implemented as a conservation strategy for endangered or depleted populations. This approach aims to increase population numbers and genetic diversity for eventual reintroduction into wild habitats, though it presents inherent tensions between maintaining genetic integrity and achieving demographic recovery. The scientific rigor of captive propagation programs—including breeding protocols, genetic management, and reintroduction planning—is critical to their success in supporting wild population restoration efforts.
