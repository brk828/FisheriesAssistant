# Gene Flow

## Summary
Gene flow refers to the movement of genetic material between fish populations through migration and reproduction, which can homogenize genetic diversity across populations or introduce new alleles into isolated groups. In conservation biology, gene flow is a critical consideration for fish populations separated by physical barriers such as dams, waterfalls, or degraded habitat zones, as restricted gene flow can lead to inbreeding depression and loss of adaptive genetic variation. Understanding gene flow patterns helps fisheries managers assess population connectivity, design effective conservation strategies, and determine whether artificial breeding programs or habitat corridors are needed to maintain genetically viable populations.
