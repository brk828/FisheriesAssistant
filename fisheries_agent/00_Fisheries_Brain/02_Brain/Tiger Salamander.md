# Tiger Salamander

## Summary
The Tiger Salamander (*Ambystoma tigrinum*) is an aquatic amphibian whose larval and neotenic forms are relevant to aquatic ecology and fisheries management, particularly as a predatory organism in freshwater pond ecosystems and as a commercially traded bait species in the aquatic trade industry. As apex predators in their larval stage, tiger salamanders are important subjects for studying food web dynamics and trophic interactions in shallow pond environments, while their commercial harvesting and sale as "waterdogs" for fishing bait represents a historically significant fisheries commodity in regions like Arizona. Conservation considerations for this species involve balancing its ecological role as a native predator with management of commercial harvest pressures and potential impacts on native fish populations.
