# Gammarus Tigrinus

## Summary
Gammarus tigrinus is a North American amphipod species that has become an invasive taxon in European waters, particularly documented in the Netherlands where it expanded its range during the 1970s following probable introduction in 1964. This euryhaline crustacean displays notably broad salinity tolerance, capable of surviving in freshwater and brackish environments ranging from approximately 100 to several thousand mg/L chlorinity, making it ecologically competitive across diverse aquatic habitats. Its invasion and range expansion represent a significant concern in aquatic conservation biology, as the species' physiological flexibility and taxonomic distinctiveness (requiring careful epimeral morphology examination for accurate identification) have enabled it to establish populations in non-native ecosystems.
