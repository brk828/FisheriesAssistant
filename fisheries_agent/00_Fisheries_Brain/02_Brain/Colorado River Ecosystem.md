# Colorado River Ecosystem

## Summary
The Colorado River Ecosystem refers to the integrated aquatic system encompassing the main stem of the Colorado River and its associated reservoirs (including Lake Powell and Lake Mead), along with tributary systems such as the Grand Canyon reach, which collectively support distinct fish communities and ecological processes shaped by natural flow regimes and now heavily influenced by dam operations. In fish science and conservation contexts, this ecosystem concept emphasizes the interconnected relationships between physical habitat conditions (water temperature, flow patterns, sediment transport), reservoir operations, and native and non-native fish populations across multiple management jurisdictions. Understanding the Colorado River Ecosystem is essential for developing ecosystem-based monitoring and management strategies that address cumulative impacts of hydroelectric development while considering long-term conservation goals for endemic fish species and aquatic biodiversity.
