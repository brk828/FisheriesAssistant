# Lagochila Lacera

## Summary
Lagochila lacera, commonly known as the Harelip Sucker, is a member of the family Catostomidae that was historically abundant in Ozark Mountain streams, particularly in the White River drainage near Eureka Springs, Arkansas, but has since disappeared entirely from the fossil record of ichthyological collections. This species exemplifies the broader pattern of freshwater fish community shifts documented in mid-20th century conservation literature, where the decline of native suckers and other sensitive fishes alongside the proliferation of pollution-tolerant species like common carp served as a direct biological indicator of water quality degradation and habitat loss in North American freshwater ecosystems.
