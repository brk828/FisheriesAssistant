# Gulf Of Mexico

## Summary
The Gulf of Mexico is a major biogeographic region and drainage basin in ichthyology and fisheries science that encompasses coastal, estuarine, and riverine ecosystems along the southeastern United States, particularly the Texas Gulf Coast and Louisiana regions. This area serves as a critical transition zone supporting diverse fish assemblages including tropical, temperate, and Gulf-endemic species, while also functioning as an important watershed for major river systems like the Rio Grande and Colorado River that extend fish distributions well upstream from the gulf proper. The Gulf of Mexico drainage is particularly significant for understanding fish biogeography, conservation status of native species, and supporting major fisheries hatchery operations that manage both game fish and culturally important aquatic species.
