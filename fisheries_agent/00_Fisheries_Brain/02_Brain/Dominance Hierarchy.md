# Dominance Hierarchy

## Summary
A dominance hierarchy in fish science refers to a linear or structured social ranking system within a group where individuals establish relative positions through agonistic (aggressive) encounters, with dominant fish gaining priority access to resources such as food, shelter, and mates while subordinate fish exhibit submissive behaviors. These hierarchies can be experimentally modified through social conditioning, demonstrating that dominance status is not entirely fixed and can be reversed under certain ecological or behavioral circumstances. Understanding dominance hierarchies is important for fisheries management, aquaculture practices, and conservation efforts, as social rank significantly influences individual fitness, population dynamics, and resource competition in aquatic communities.
