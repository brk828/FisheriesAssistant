# Salmonidae

## Summary
Salmonidae is a family of teleost fishes that includes salmon, trout, and char species, characterized by an adipose fin and highly variable life history strategies ranging from purely freshwater residents to anadromous migratory forms that utilize both marine and freshwater ecosystems. Members of this family are ecologically significant in temperate and northern aquatic systems, serving as keystone species in food webs and supporting major commercial and recreational fisheries, while also functioning as important model organisms in experimental fish biology and physiological research. Conservation efforts for Salmonidae species are widespread due to their sensitivity to habitat degradation, water quality changes, and population declines, making them central to freshwater and marine ecosystem management and restoration initiatives.
