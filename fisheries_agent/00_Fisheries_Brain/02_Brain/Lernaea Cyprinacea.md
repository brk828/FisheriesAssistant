# Lernaea Cyprinacea

## Summary
Lernaea cyprinacea, commonly known as the anchor worm, is a parasitic copepod that infects freshwater fish species, particularly cyprinids, by embedding itself in the host's flesh and causing tissue damage, inflammation, and potential secondary infections. This ectoparasite is significant in fisheries science and conservation biology as it affects wild fish populations and cultured stocks, making it an important consideration in fish health monitoring, aquaculture management, and the assessment of overall aquatic ecosystem health. Understanding Lernaea cyprinacea's prevalence and impact is crucial for implementing effective disease control strategies and maintaining sustainable fish populations.
