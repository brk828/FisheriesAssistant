# Rainbow Trout

## Summary
Rainbow Trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) is a highly adaptable salmonid species native to western North America that has become one of the most widely stocked fish in fisheries management programs globally, particularly in reservoirs and tailwater reaches of regulated rivers. In conservation contexts, rainbow trout stocking is frequently employed as a recreational fisheries management tool to sustain angling opportunities, though this practice can have complex ecological implications for native fish communities, including competition and predation pressure on endangered species such as bonytail and razorback sucker in systems like the Colorado River. The species' ecological role and management status represent a central tension in aquatic conservation between sustaining human uses of aquatic ecosystems and protecting native biodiversity.
