# Spring Chinook Salmon

## Summary
Spring Chinook Salmon (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) are an anadromous population of Pacific salmon that migrate upstream in spring to spawn in natal freshwater rivers, particularly in the Columbia River basin and its tributaries. This life-history variant is ecologically and culturally significant in the Pacific Northwest and has been a major focus of hatchery mitigation programs operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to counteract habitat loss and population declines caused by dam construction and river modification. Spring Chinook populations serve as both conservation targets and aquacultural research subjects due to their economic importance and sensitivity to environmental changes in freshwater spawning and rearing habitats.
