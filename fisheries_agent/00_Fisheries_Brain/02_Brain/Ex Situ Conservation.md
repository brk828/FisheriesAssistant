# Ex Situ Conservation

## Summary
Ex situ conservation refers to the protection and management of fish species outside their natural habitats, typically through captive breeding programs, aquarium maintenance, or cryopreservation of genetic material. This approach is particularly valuable for imperiled aquatic species facing extinction in the wild, as it allows scientists to maintain genetically viable populations while simultaneously conducting research and preparing organisms for potential reintroduction into restored natural habitats. Ex situ efforts often involve multi-agency collaboration and serve as a critical complement to in situ (in-habitat) conservation strategies when wild populations become too depleted to ensure species survival.
