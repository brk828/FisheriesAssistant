# Grand Canyon Ecosystem

## Summary
The Grand Canyon Ecosystem, in the context of fisheries science, refers to the integrated aquatic and riparian system of the Colorado River corridor within the Grand Canyon, bounded by Glen Canyon Dam upstream and Lake Mead downstream. This ecosystem encompasses the physical, chemical, and biological components of the river and its associated habitats, which are critical for understanding fish population dynamics, species interactions, and the effects of dam operations on native and non-native fish communities. Research within this framework, such as aerial photography surveys and statistical analyses of habitat conditions, aims to inform adaptive management strategies and conservation efforts for the economically and ecologically significant fish assemblages that depend on the Colorado River's unique environmental conditions.
