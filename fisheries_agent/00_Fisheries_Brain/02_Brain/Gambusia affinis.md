# Gambusia Affinis

## Summary
Gambusia affinis, commonly known as the western mosquitofish, is a small live-bearing poeciliid fish native to the southeastern United States that has been widely introduced across North America and globally for mosquito larval control in artificial and natural water bodies. As an invasive species, it has significant ecological consequences, often outcompeting native fishes and reducing populations of aquatic invertebrates and amphibians through predation and resource competition, making it a major conservation concern in many freshwater ecosystems. The species exhibits sexual dimorphism including modified pectoral fin rays in males that facilitate internal fertilization, and it readily hybridizes with related Gambusia species, further complicating native fish conservation efforts in already compromised habitats.
