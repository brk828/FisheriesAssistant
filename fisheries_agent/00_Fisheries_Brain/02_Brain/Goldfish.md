# Goldfish

## Summary
Goldfish (*Carassius auratus*) are small cyprinid fishes that have become widespread non-native species in freshwater ecosystems beyond their native Asian range, often establishing self-sustaining populations in lakes and reservoirs through aquarium releases or escape. In aquatic ecology and fisheries surveys, goldfish are monitored as indicators of fish community composition and potential invasive species, though they typically occur at low abundance relative to native cyprinids like common carp in most North American systems. Their presence in invaded ecosystems reflects broader concerns about ornamental fish trade impacts on native fish communities and water quality.
