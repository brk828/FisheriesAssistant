# Lepisosteidae

## Summary
Lepisosteidae, commonly known as gars, is a family of primitive freshwater and brackish-water fishes characterized by elongated bodies covered in ganoid scales, elongated snouts, and archaic actinopterygian morphology, representing ancient lineages within fish evolution. Members of this family, such as Lepisosteus osseus (longnose gar), L. oculatus, and Atractosteus spatula, inhabit North American freshwater systems including major river drainages like the Rio Grande and its tributaries, where they serve as important predators in aquatic ecosystems. These fishes are significant in ichthyological surveys and conservation assessments of freshwater fish communities due to their ecological role and distinctive taxonomic position as relict taxa.
