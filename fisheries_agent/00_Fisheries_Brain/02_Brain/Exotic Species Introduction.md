# Exotic Species Introduction

## Summary
Exotic species introduction refers to the deliberate or accidental translocation of non-native fish species into aquatic ecosystems where they do not naturally occur, often resulting in significant ecological consequences for native fish populations and community structure. In the context of fisheries management and conservation biology, exotic species introductions have been a major concern since at least the 1970s, as documented in scientific and administrative records, because introduced species can compete with, prey upon, or hybridize with native fish, potentially leading to population declines or extinctions of endemic species. Understanding the mechanisms and impacts of exotic species—including their temperature tolerance and establishment potential—has become critical for developing strategies to protect threatened native fish communities and manage aquatic ecosystems sustainably.
