# Castle Lake

## Summary
Castle Lake is a high-elevation cirque lake in California that served as a significant research site for studying fish population dynamics and ecological recovery following a 1946 rotenone treatment that eliminated all native fish species. The lake's distinct physical characteristics—including thermal stratification, varied basin depths, and seasonal ice cover—made it an ideal natural laboratory for investigating trout recolonization, community assembly, and the impacts of parasites and other ecological factors on fish populations during the post-treatment recovery period (1947–1954). This long-term investigation contributed fundamental knowledge about how fish communities rebuild following disturbance and how environmental conditions influence population structure in alpine aquatic ecosystems.
