# Critical Thermal Maximum

## Summary
Critical Thermal Maximum (CTM) is the highest water temperature at which a fish can maintain equilibrium and coordinated movement before losing motor control and becoming immobilized, serving as a key physiological threshold for determining thermal tolerance limits. This metric is widely used in fisheries science and conservation biology to assess species' vulnerability to temperature stress, predict impacts of thermal pollution and climate change on aquatic populations, and establish water temperature criteria for environmental protection. By measuring CTM across different species and populations, researchers can identify thermal bottlenecks for endangered species and inform management decisions regarding habitat preservation and thermal pollution controls in aquatic ecosystems.
