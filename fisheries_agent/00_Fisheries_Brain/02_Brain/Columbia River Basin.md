# Columbia River Basin

## Summary
The Columbia River Basin is a vast freshwater watershed in the Pacific Northwest encompassing portions of the United States and Canada that serves as the primary geographic and ecological context for studying and managing anadromous salmonid populations and other freshwater fish species. This basin contains numerous tributaries (such as the Little White Salmon River and Big White Salmon River) and represents a complex system of interconnected river habitat, reservoirs, and hatchery operations that are central to fish conservation and aquaculture efforts in the region. The basin's significance in ichthyology stems from its historical importance as spawning grounds for Pacific salmon and steelhead, as well as its role as a distinct management unit for multiple fish populations, including genetically isolated stocks like Kootenai River white sturgeon.
