# Crappie

## Summary
Crappie refers to freshwater sunfish species in the family Centrarchidae, commonly comprising black crappie (Pomoxis nigromaculatus) and white crappie (Pomoxis annularis), which are popular sport fish species widely cultured in hatcheries and stocked in reservoirs and lakes for recreational fisheries. These panfish are characterized by their compressed bodies, spiny-rayed dorsal fins, and moderate size, making them valuable for both fisheries management and aquaculture production in North American freshwater systems. Crappies have been historically propagated at facilities like the Carbon Hill National Fish Hatchery as part of broader fisheries enhancement programs aimed at supporting recreational fishing opportunities in impounded waters.
