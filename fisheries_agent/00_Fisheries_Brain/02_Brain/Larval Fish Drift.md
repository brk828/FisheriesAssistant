# Larval Fish Drift

## Summary
Larval fish drift refers to the passive downstream movement of newly hatched or early larval fish as they are carried by river currents from their spawning grounds toward nursery habitats. This phenomenon is particularly important in lotic (flowing water) systems where fish species, such as those in the Susquehanna River, broadcast spawn nonadhesive demersal eggs that hatch and allow larvae to disperse downstream through water column transport. Understanding larval drift is critical for fisheries management and conservation because it determines recruitment patterns, population connectivity, and the availability of suitable rearing habitat for early life stages.
