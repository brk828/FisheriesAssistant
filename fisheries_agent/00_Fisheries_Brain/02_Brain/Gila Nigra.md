# Gila Nigra

## Summary
Gila nigra, commonly known as the headwater chub, is a cyprinid fish species endemic to the Gila River Basin of the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, historically confused with the closely related Gila robusta due to overlapping morphological characters. This species is distinguished by specific meristic features including lateral line scales typically numbering 73-90 (mode 78-82), 8-9 dorsal and anal fin rays, and characteristic body proportions of the caudal peduncle and head, along with generally dark but grayish (rather than truly black) coloration. The species has been the subject of extensive taxonomic and conservation surveys due to its restricted range, habitat fragmentation, and uncertain conservation status, making it an important subject of ichthyological and fisheries management research in the southwestern United States.
