# Cochise Filter Barrier

## Summary
The Cochise Filter Barrier is a dry, 100-200 km wide gap between the Sierra Madre Occidental and Colorado Plateau in southeastern Arizona, southwestern New Mexico, northeastern Sonora, and northwestern Chihuahua, characterized by high plains at approximately 1500 m elevation interrupted by steep north-south trending mountain ranges reaching 3000 m. This barrier has profoundly influenced distributions of semi-aquatic organisms and sets the eastern limit for Kinosternon sonoriense and western limit for K. f. flavescens, with complete allopatry documented between these species on either side of the barrier. The barrier prevents eastward dispersal of K. f. flavescens and westward dispersal of K. sonoriense, making it a critical zoogeographical feature for understanding turtle distributions in the American Southwest.
