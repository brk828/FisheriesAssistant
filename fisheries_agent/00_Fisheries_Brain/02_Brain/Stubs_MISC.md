### Summary
The 15-Mile Reach refers to a critical 15-mile segment of the Colorado River between the Grand Valley diversion and the Gunnison River confluence that serves as essential habitat for the endangered Colorado squawfish and other native fish species. This highly altered river reach was the subject of intensive management studies and flow recommendations under the Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program and the Endangered Species Act to restore suitable instream flows and habitat conditions for fish reproduction and survival. The reach represents a focal point for aquatic conservation efforts requiring coordinated water releases and flow management to maintain viable populations of endangered species in an otherwise heavily regulated river system.
