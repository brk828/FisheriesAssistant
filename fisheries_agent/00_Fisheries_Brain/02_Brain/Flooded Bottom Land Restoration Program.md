# Flooded Bottom Land Restoration Program

## Summary
The Flooded Bottom Land Restoration Program was a 1994 initiative in the Upper Colorado River Basin aimed at recovering the endangered Razorback Sucker by restoring flood plain habitats for larval rearing. The program systematically inventoried and ranked approximately 20 sites across 870 miles of the Green, Colorado, and Gunnison Rivers, proposing to restore both terraces (inundated during high flows) and depressions (isolated pools and oxbows) with a three-year budget of $6.6 million. The program faced fundamental questions about whether to use intensive management (screens, predator removal, controlled water levels) versus natural flooding approaches, and whether restored habitats could support survival given predation by non-native fishes.
