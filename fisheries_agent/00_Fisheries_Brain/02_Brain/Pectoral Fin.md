# Pectoral Fin

## Summary
The pectoral fin is a paired appendage located on the lateral sides of a fish's body just behind the gill covers, functioning primarily in steering, braking, and fine locomotor control during swimming. In fisheries science and systematic studies, the pectoral fin serves as an important anatomical reference point for standardized sampling procedures, such as scale collection from the posterior tip of the fin laid against the body, which facilitates consistent data collection across species and populations. The morphology and positioning of pectoral fins can vary significantly among fish taxa and are sometimes used as diagnostic characters in taxonomic identification and species differentiation.
