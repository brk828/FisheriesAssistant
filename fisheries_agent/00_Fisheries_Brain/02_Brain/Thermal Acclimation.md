# Thermal Acclimation

## Summary
Thermal acclimation refers to the physiological and biochemical adjustments that fish undergo in response to sustained exposure to different water temperatures, allowing them to maintain metabolic performance and survival across varying thermal environments. This process involves changes in enzyme activity, membrane composition, and other cellular mechanisms that enable fish to adapt their temperature tolerance limits and optimize their metabolic efficiency to match the ambient conditions they experience seasonally or chronically. Understanding thermal acclimation is critical for fisheries management and conservation, particularly in assessing how fish populations respond to environmental temperature fluctuations and their capacity to tolerate changing aquatic conditions.
