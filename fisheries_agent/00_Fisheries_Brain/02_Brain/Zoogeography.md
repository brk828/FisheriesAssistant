# Zoogeography

## Summary
Zoogeography in fish science refers to the study of how fish species are distributed across geographic regions and the ecological and evolutionary factors that determine their spatial patterns of occurrence. In aquatic contexts, zoogeographic affinity describes the biogeographic relationships and similarities among fish fauna within particular drainage systems or biomes, reflecting their shared evolutionary history, environmental tolerances, and dispersal capabilities. This concept is essential for understanding fish community assembly, conservation priorities, and how species distributions respond to environmental gradients such as salinity transitions in coastal-freshwater systems.
