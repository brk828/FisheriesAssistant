# Zooplankton

## Summary
Zooplankton are small, often microscopic aquatic animals that form a critical intermediate trophic level in freshwater and marine ecosystems, consuming phytoplankton and serving as essential food sources for fish larvae and planktivorous fish species. In aquatic ecology and fisheries science, zooplankton communities—particularly crustaceans like copepods and cladocerans—are fundamental indicators of ecosystem productivity, water quality, and food web dynamics, making their species composition and seasonal distribution important variables for understanding fish population recruitment, growth, and overall aquatic system health. The abundance and diversity of zooplankton directly influence fish productivity and are therefore critical considerations in fish conservation and aquaculture management.
