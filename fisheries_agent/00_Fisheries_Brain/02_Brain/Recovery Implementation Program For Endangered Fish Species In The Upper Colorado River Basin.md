# Recovery Implementation Program For Endangered Fish Species In The Upper Colorado River Basin

## Summary
The Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin is a comprehensive federal conservation initiative designed to protect and restore four endemic fish species—the Colorado pikeminnow, razorback sucker, humpback chub, and bonytail—that face extinction due to habitat modification, water management practices, and non-native species introduction in the upper Colorado River system. The program coordinates inter-agency efforts, research, and water management modifications across multiple states to balance endangered species recovery with existing water allocation and hydroelectric power generation needs. Established in the late 1980s, this program represents a multi-jurisdictional approach to aquatic ecosystem restoration through habitat enhancement, propagation, monitoring, and adaptive management strategies tailored to riverine fish conservation.
