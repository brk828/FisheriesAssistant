# Chihuahuan Desert

## Summary
The Chihuahuan Desert is a major arid ecoregion in northern Mexico and the southwestern United States that contains isolated freshwater aquatic systems, particularly within interior basins and river drainages like the Río Conchos and Cuatrociénegas, which serve as biodiversity hotspots harboring relictual and endemic fish and turtle species. These desert aquatic systems are of significant conservation concern due to geographic isolation that has produced distinctive faunal assemblages through long-term separation and biogeographic mixing of species from different biotic zones. The Chihuahuan Desert's hydrologically fragmented landscape makes its freshwater fauna particularly vulnerable to extinction and has made it a focal region for studies on conservation biology, endemism, and the ecology of desert aquatic communities.
