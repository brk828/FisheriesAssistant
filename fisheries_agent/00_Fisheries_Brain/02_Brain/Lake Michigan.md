# Lake Michigan

## Summary
Lake Michigan is one of the five Great Lakes and serves as a critical freshwater ecosystem that has experienced significant ecological disturbances from invasive species, particularly the sea lamprey and rainbow smelt, which dramatically altered native fish communities and fisheries productivity throughout the twentieth century. As a large, deep oligotrophic lake system, it represents an important model for understanding how major aquatic habitats respond to biological invasions, species dispersal, and conservation management interventions in interconnected freshwater systems. The lake's ecological dynamics and fisheries history provide essential data for understanding the broader impacts of species introductions and habitat degradation in the Great Lakes basin.
