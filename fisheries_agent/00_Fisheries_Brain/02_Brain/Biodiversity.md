# Biodiversity

## Summary
In fish science and aquatic ecology, biodiversity refers to the variety of fish species, populations, and genetic diversity within aquatic ecosystems, as well as the structural complexity of habitats—including riparian vegetation, wetlands, and in-stream features—that support this biological variation. Maintaining aquatic biodiversity is essential for ecosystem function, resilience, and the sustainability of fisheries resources, as it ensures the presence of diverse ecological roles and genetic adaptability to environmental changes. Conservation efforts focused on biodiversity protection aim to preserve both the species themselves and the integrated riparian and aquatic systems upon which they depend.
