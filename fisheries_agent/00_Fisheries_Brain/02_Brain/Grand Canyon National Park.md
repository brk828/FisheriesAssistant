# Grand Canyon National Park

## Summary
Grand Canyon National Park encompasses a critical stretch of the Colorado River system that serves as designated critical habitat and a focal research area for the conservation of endangered native fish species, particularly the humpback chub and razorback sucker. This reach of river, including key tributary confluences like the Little Colorado River, represents essential spawning, rearing, and refugial habitat for endemic species adapted to the canyon's complex geomorphological and hydrological conditions. Management of the park's aquatic ecosystems involves coordinated research, monitoring, and adaptive management strategies—such as experimental flood releases—to understand and mitigate the ecological impacts of upstream dam operations on native fish populations and their habitats.
