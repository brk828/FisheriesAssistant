# Chironomus Riparius

## Summary
Chironomus riparius, commonly known as the midge or chironomid, is a benthic invertebrate larva widely used as a bioindicator organism in aquatic ecology and pollution studies due to its high tolerance for organic pollution and ability to achieve extremely dense populations (exceeding 100,000 individuals/m²) in degraded freshwater sediments. As a key component of benthic communities in contaminated waters, C. riparius serves as both a model organism for assessing sediment quality and pollutant bioavailability, and as an important food source for fish in aquatic food webs. Its presence in high densities typically indicates eutrophic or organically enriched conditions, making it valuable for monitoring ecosystem health and the effects of pollution on benthic community structure.
