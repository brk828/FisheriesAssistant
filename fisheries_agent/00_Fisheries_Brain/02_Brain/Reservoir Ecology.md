# Reservoir Ecology

## Summary
Reservoir ecology encompasses the study of how artificial impoundments function as aquatic ecosystems, including the physical, chemical, and biological processes that occur in these modified water bodies and how they differ from natural riverine systems. In fish science and conservation, understanding reservoir ecology is critical for managing fish populations, predicting impacts on native species (particularly endangered or culturally important species like trout), and balancing competing demands between water development for human use and the maintenance of natural aquatic communities. Reservoir ecology examines factors such as water stratification, flow regimes, temperature profiles, and nutrient cycling to inform management strategies that can mitigate negative effects on fish diversity and ecosystem function.
