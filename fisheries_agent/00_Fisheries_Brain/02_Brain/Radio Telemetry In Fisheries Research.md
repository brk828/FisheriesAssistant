# Radio Telemetry In Fisheries Research

## Summary
Radio telemetry in fisheries research is a tracking technology that uses surgically implanted or externally attached radio transmitters to monitor the movements, migration patterns, and behavior of individual fish in their natural aquatic environments. This non-lethal technique allows researchers to collect real-time spatial data on fish populations, document habitat use, assess survival rates, and evaluate the effectiveness of conservation measures or environmental management actions. Radio telemetry has been particularly valuable in studying large fish species in rivers and reservoirs, enabling scientists to understand population dynamics and inform evidence-based management decisions for threatened or endangered species.
