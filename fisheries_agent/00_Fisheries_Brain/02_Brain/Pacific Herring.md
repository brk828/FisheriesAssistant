# Pacific Herring

## Summary
Pacific Herring (Clupea pallasi) is a forage fish species characterized by early rapid growth that decelerates later in life (Growth Type A), making it a valuable model organism for understanding population dynamics and yield theory in fisheries science. This species forms dense spawning aggregations that make it particularly vulnerable to predation and exploitation, and its relatively insensitive yield response to fishing mortality rates across moderate to high exploitation levels has made it an important case study for developing fisheries management principles. Pacific Herring's life history traits—including high natural mortality, moderate to high susceptibility to fishing pressure, and schooling behavior—have made it central to theoretical and empirical research on population regulation and sustainable harvest optimization in aquatic ecology.
