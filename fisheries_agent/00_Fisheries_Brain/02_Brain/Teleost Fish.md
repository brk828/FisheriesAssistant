# Teleost Fish

## Summary
Teleost fish represent the largest and most diverse infraclass of bony fishes (Osteichthyes), characterized by a specialized swim bladder, homocercal tail fin, and highly derived skeletal and muscular systems that have enabled their ecological success across virtually all aquatic environments. As the dominant vertebrate group in most freshwater and marine ecosystems, teleosts are central subjects of study in ichthyology, conservation biology, and aquatic ecology due to their tremendous species diversity, economic importance to fisheries, and role as sentinel organisms for assessing ecosystem health. Their osmoregulatory capabilities and physiological plasticity make them particularly valuable model organisms for understanding how fish adapt to and maintain homeostasis in varied aquatic conditions.
