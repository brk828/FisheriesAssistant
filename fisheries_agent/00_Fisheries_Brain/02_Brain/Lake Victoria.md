# Lake Victoria

## Summary
Lake Victoria is a major African freshwater lake and globally significant center of aquatic endemism, renowned for harboring an extraordinary cichlid species flock of approximately 500 haplochromine species that evolved from a single ancestor in roughly 12,400 years, representing one of the most rapid speciation events known in vertebrate evolution. The lake's endemic fish fauna, particularly its cichlid assemblage, has become a critical conservation priority due to catastrophic ecological collapse following the introduction of the predatory Nile Perch, which has driven numerous native species to extinction and fundamentally altered the lake's aquatic ecosystem. Lake Victoria serves as a paradigmatic case study in conservation biology for understanding both the mechanisms of explosive speciation and the devastating impacts of invasive species on isolated freshwater ecosystems.
