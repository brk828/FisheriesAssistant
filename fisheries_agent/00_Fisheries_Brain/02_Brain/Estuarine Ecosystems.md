# Estuarine Ecosystems

## Summary
Estuarine ecosystems are transitional aquatic environments where rivers meet ocean waters, characterized by fluctuating salinity gradients and dynamic physical conditions that create highly productive habitats critical for numerous fish species. These systems serve as essential nurseries, spawning grounds, and migration corridors for many fish populations, including anadromous species like Atlantic sturgeons that depend on estuary access for reproduction and early life stage development. From a conservation perspective, estuarine ecosystems are particularly vulnerable to anthropogenic impacts such as pollution, habitat degradation, and altered water flow, making them focal points for fisheries management and restoration efforts aimed at sustaining economically and ecologically important fish populations.
