# Asellus

## Summary
Asellus is a genus of freshwater isopods (small crustaceans) that encompasses both epigean (surface-dwelling) and hypogean (cave-dwelling and groundwater) species, with significant importance in aquatic ecology and evolutionary biology due to their adaptation to subterranean environments. The genus, established by Geoffroy in 1762 with Asellus aquaticus as its type species, includes multiple troglobitic species such as Asellus incurvus, Asellus circulus, and Asellus scyphus, which inhabit limestone cave systems and serve as important indicators of groundwater ecosystem health and evolutionary processes in subterranean aquatic communities. These isopods are distributed across diverse freshwater habitats ranging from surface streams to deep karstic and interstitial groundwater systems, making them valuable model organisms for studying cave ecology and the evolutionary adaptations of freshwater fauna to extreme environments.
