# Connecticut River

## Summary
The Connecticut River is a major anadromous fish habitat and recovery system in the northeastern United States that historically supported Atlantic salmon populations before their extirpation due to anthropogenic barriers and habitat degradation. In contemporary fisheries science and conservation, the Connecticut River represents a critical restoration focus for Atlantic salmon reintroduction programs, with multiple federal fish hatcheries (including White River and Berkshire National Fish Hatcheries) dedicated to producing juvenile salmon for restocking efforts to re-establish the species' migratory lifecycle between the North Atlantic and its natal freshwater spawning grounds. The river system exemplifies large-scale anadromous fish conservation, requiring coordinated hatchery production, dam removal or mitigation, and habitat restoration to facilitate species recovery in a historically productive but severely altered riverine ecosystem.
