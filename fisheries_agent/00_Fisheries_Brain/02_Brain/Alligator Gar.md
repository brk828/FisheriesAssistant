# Alligator Gar

## Summary
The Alligator Gar (Atractosteus spatula) is a large, primitive freshwater fish species inhabiting major river systems including the Mississippi, Ohio, Cumberland, and Tennessee drainages, characterized by its elongated body and distinctive tooth-laden jaws. As a long-lived, slow-reproducing apex predator, the species has experienced significant population declines throughout its range due to habitat loss, water management practices, and historical overfishing, making it a subject of conservation concern in freshwater fisheries management and restoration efforts across multiple states. Its ecological role as a top predator and status as a species of concern underscore its importance in maintaining the integrity of large river ecosystems and aquatic food webs.
