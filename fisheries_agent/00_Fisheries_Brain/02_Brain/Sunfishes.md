# Sunfishes

## Summary
Sunfishes are a diverse family of small to medium-sized freshwater fishes characterized by their flattened, disk-like bodies and are among the most common and ecologically important members of North American freshwater fish communities. These colorful fishes, which include species such as bluegill and pumpkinseed, serve as important forage fish in aquatic food webs and are frequently used as model organisms in ecological and physiological research due to their abundance, ease of identification using taxonomic keys, and wide geographic distribution across freshwater habitats. In conservation and fisheries contexts, sunfishes are valued both as indicators of aquatic ecosystem health and as popular recreational fish species.
