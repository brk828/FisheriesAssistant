# Dam Construction

## Summary
Dam construction fundamentally alters aquatic ecosystems by fragmenting river habitats, modifying natural flow regimes, and creating barriers to fish migration and dispersal. In the context of fish conservation, dam construction is recognized as a major anthropogenic threat that disrupts the ecological integrity of riverine systems, particularly affecting native fish species dependent on continuous river connectivity and seasonal flow patterns for reproduction, feeding, and survival. This is especially critical for species like the bonytail chub and fountain darter, where dam-induced habitat fragmentation and flow alterations have contributed to population decline and range contraction in affected basins such as the Colorado River system.
