# Roosevelt Dam

## Summary
Roosevelt Dam, constructed on the Salt River in Arizona, is a major water infrastructure project that fundamentally altered the aquatic ecosystem of the Tonto Basin and downstream habitats by regulating river flow, creating reservoir conditions, and fragmenting fish populations. In the context of fish science and conservation, the dam represents a critical case study in how large-scale water management structures impact native fish communities, their migration patterns, and habitat availability, particularly affecting species that historically inhabited the Salt River system. The archaeological record from the Tonto Basin provides evidence of the pre-dam fish assemblages and species composition, offering insight into how human-engineered hydrological changes have transformed aquatic faunal communities over time.
