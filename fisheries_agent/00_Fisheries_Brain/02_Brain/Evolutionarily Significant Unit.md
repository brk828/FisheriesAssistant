# Evolutionarily Significant Unit

## Summary
An Evolutionarily Significant Unit (ESU) is a population or group of populations of a species that is considered distinct for purposes of conservation and management under frameworks like the Endangered Species Act, based on evidence of genetic differentiation, reproductive isolation, and adaptive significance rather than requiring fixed morphological or genetic discontinuities. ESUs recognize that evolutionarily important populations may lack obvious physical differences yet maintain distinct genetic lineages and local adaptations that make them significant for long-term species survival. This concept has been particularly important in salmon conservation, where artificial propagation and hatchery programs must account for the genetic integrity and evolutionary distinctiveness of different ESUs within a species.
