# Swingle 1950

## Summary
Swingle 1950 refers to foundational fisheries management principles established by H.S. Swingle regarding optimal stocking ratios and predator-prey balance in farm pond ecosystems, particularly the proportioning of largemouth bass to sunfish species to maintain productive, balanced fish populations. This seminal work provided quantitative frameworks for achieving sustainable standing crops in managed freshwater systems through manipulating predation pressure and species composition. Swingle's approach became a cornerstone methodology for pond fish management, influencing decades of subsequent research on how predator-forage fish ratios and feeding rates could be optimized to maximize fisheries productivity while maintaining ecological stability.
