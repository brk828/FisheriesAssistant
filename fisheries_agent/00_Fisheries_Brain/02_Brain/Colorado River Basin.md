# Colorado River Basin

## Summary
The Colorado River Basin is a large inland watershed system spanning the southwestern United States that historically supported a unique assemblage of endemic fish species adapted to warm-water, high-turbidity riverine environments, many of which are now critically endangered and subjects of intensive conservation efforts. In fish science and conservation biology, the basin is referenced as both the ancestral range and current focus area for recovery programs targeting native species such as Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub, whose populations have been severely impacted by dam construction, water management practices, and habitat alteration throughout the upper and lower basin reaches. The Colorado River Basin thus represents a critical case study in aquatic conservation, where integrated management across multiple jurisdictions and ex-situ breeding programs must address the ecological challenges posed by a heavily regulated river system.
