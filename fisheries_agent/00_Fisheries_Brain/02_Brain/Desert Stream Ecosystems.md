# Desert Stream Ecosystems

## Summary
Desert Stream Ecosystems are lotic freshwater systems found in arid regions such as the American Southwest that are characterized by episodic catastrophic flash flooding, high biotic mortality events, and rapid post-flood ecological succession on scoured substrates. These ecosystems, including intermittent streams like Sycamore Creek in Arizona, present extreme and unstable environmental conditions that fundamentally structure aquatic communities, particularly affecting specialized species such as desert pupfish that have evolved to occupy marginal shallow habitats within these disturbance-driven systems. The pronounced flood-driven disturbance regime distinguishes desert streams from more stable mesic lotic systems and serves as a primary ecological organizing force shaping community composition and recovery dynamics.
