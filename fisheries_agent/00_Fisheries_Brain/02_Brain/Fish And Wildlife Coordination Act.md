# Fish And Wildlife Coordination Act

## Summary
The Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act is a federal statute that requires federal agencies to consult with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service when planning water resource development projects, such as dams and reservoirs, to assess and mitigate impacts on fish and wildlife populations. This act serves as a regulatory mechanism for ensuring that fish conservation and habitat protection are integrated into the permitting and environmental review process for major water development projects, often requiring the establishment of mitigation measures such as fish hatcheries or habitat restoration programs to compensate for ecological losses caused by these developments.
