# Sediment Transport

## Summary
Sediment transport refers to the movement of soil, sand, gravel, and other particulate materials through fluvial systems by the action of flowing water, which is a critical process governing channel morphology, stream habitat structure, and aquatic ecosystem health. In the context of fish science and conservation, understanding sediment transport dynamics is essential because changes in sediment load and transport patterns—often resulting from watershed modifications, dam operations, or land use changes—directly affect spawning substrates, water clarity, and the physical structure of fish habitats. The balance between sediment supply and transport capacity determines whether streams maintain equilibrium conditions suitable for fish populations or degrade through erosion or sedimentation.
