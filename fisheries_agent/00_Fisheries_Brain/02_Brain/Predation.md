# Predation

## Summary
Predation in fish science and aquatic ecology refers to the ecological interaction in which one fish species or organism hunts, captures, and consumes another, functioning as a critical mechanism that regulates population dynamics, structures aquatic communities, and influences the evolutionary adaptations of both predator and prey species. This trophic interaction affects fish conservation efforts, particularly in isolated ecosystems like desert springs where endemic species may face novel predation pressures or conversely lack natural predators, thereby impacting population viability and community composition. Understanding predation dynamics is essential for fisheries management, as predator-prey relationships determine recruitment success, survival rates, and the overall balance of aquatic food webs.
