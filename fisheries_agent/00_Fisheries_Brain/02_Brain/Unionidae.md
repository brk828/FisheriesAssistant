# Unionidae

## Summary
Unionidae is a family of obligate freshwater mussels that are ecologically significant in aquatic systems as they depend on flowing water habitats for survival and reproduction, with a complex larval dispersal strategy involving temporary parasitic attachment to host fish through specialized larvae called glochidia. These mussels are important indicators of stream health and habitat quality, and their presence requires specific environmental conditions including suitable substrate and connected flowing water systems, making them valuable organisms for assessing freshwater ecosystem integrity and connectivity. Conservation of Unionidae species is critical because their life cycle requirements make them particularly vulnerable to habitat fragmentation, water quality degradation, and alterations in fish populations that serve as their larval vectors.
