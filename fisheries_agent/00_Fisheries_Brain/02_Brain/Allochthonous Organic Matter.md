# Allochthonous Organic Matter

## Summary
Allochthonous organic matter refers to organic material that originates from outside an aquatic ecosystem, such as leaf litter, terrestrial plant debris, and dissolved organic compounds that enter rivers and streams from surrounding terrestrial vegetation and landscapes. In fish science and aquatic ecology, allochthonous inputs are critical energy sources that subsidize aquatic food webs, particularly in forested streams and rivers where riparian vegetation contributes substantially to the nutrient base supporting fish populations and broader ecosystem productivity. Understanding the quantity and quality of allochthonous organic matter is essential for assessing aquatic habitat quality, food web dynamics, and the conservation of fish communities in river systems.
