# Common Carp

## Summary
Common Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) is a large, omnivorous freshwater fish native to Eurasia that has become one of the most widespread and ecologically impactful non-native species in North American aquatic ecosystems. As a benthic forager, common carp are known for their destructive feeding behavior that stirs up sediments, increases turbidity, and degrades aquatic vegetation and habitat quality, making them a significant management concern in conservation efforts aimed at protecting native fish communities. Their rapid growth, high fecundity, and adaptability to diverse environmental conditions have enabled them to establish populations across numerous lakes, rivers, and constructed water bodies, where they often dominate fish assemblages and compete with or prey upon native species.
