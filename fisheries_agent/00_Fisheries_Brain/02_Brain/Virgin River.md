# Virgin River

## Summary
The Virgin River refers to a specific river system in the southwestern United States (primarily Arizona, Nevada, and Utah) that has become a focal point for fish conservation and aquatic ecology due to its endemic and endangered fish species, particularly the Virgin River Chub (*Gila robusta seminuda*). This river drainage represents a critical conservation priority within the Desert Fishes Council and federal resource management agencies, involving habitat management plans, Wild and Scenic River designations, and Endangered Species Act protections to preserve native fish populations threatened by water development, non-native species introductions, and habitat degradation. The Virgin River exemplifies the complex challenges of balancing human water needs with the protection of endemic aquatic biodiversity in arid southwestern ecosystems.
