# Fish Population Dynamics

## Summary
Fish population dynamics refers to the temporal changes in fish population characteristics—including abundance, age structure, catch rates, and overall population size—in response to natural processes and human exploitation such as fishing pressure. This field of study examines how populations respond to harvesting through metrics like declining catch-per-unit-effort and shifts in age composition, providing critical information for understanding population collapse, recovery patterns, and sustainability of fish stocks. Understanding these dynamics is essential for effective fisheries management, conservation planning, and predicting whether populations can sustain human uses like commercial fishing and recreational angling.
