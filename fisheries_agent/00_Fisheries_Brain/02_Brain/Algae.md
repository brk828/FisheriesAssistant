# Algae

## Summary
Algae, encompassing both free-floating phytoplankton and sessile forms, serve as primary producers in aquatic ecosystems and are fundamental to understanding fish ecology, water quality, and contaminant bioaccumulation. In the context of conservation biology, algae are critical indicators and drivers of ecosystem health, as their proliferation through eutrophication (nutrient over-enrichment) can degrade aquatic habitats and alter food web dynamics for fish populations. Additionally, due to their large surface area and direct nutrient uptake from water, algae accumulate contaminants—including radioactive materials—at high concentrations, making them key organisms for tracking pollutant pathways through aquatic food chains to fish and other consumers.
