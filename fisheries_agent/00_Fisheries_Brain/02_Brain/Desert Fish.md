# Desert Fish

## Summary
Desert fish are freshwater fish species adapted to and inhabiting the isolated, often fragmented aquatic systems found in arid and semi-arid regions, typically relying on remnant water bodies such as springs, small lakes, and intermittent streams that persist in otherwise dry landscapes. These fish often represent relict populations with high endemism and conservation significance, as they face particular vulnerability to extinction due to habitat loss, fragmentation, water depletion, and climatic change in resource-limited desert environments. Understanding desert fish ecology and evolution is critical for conservation efforts, as substantial percentages of threatened and endangered aquatic species occur in desert regions, particularly on public lands where management decisions directly affect their survival.
