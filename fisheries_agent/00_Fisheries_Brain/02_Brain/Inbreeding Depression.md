# Inbreeding Depression

## Summary
Inbreeding depression in fish science refers to the reduction in fitness, survival, and reproductive success that occurs when individuals from small, isolated populations interbreed, leading to increased homozygosity and expression of deleterious recessive alleles. In conservation contexts, particularly with threatened and endangered fish species maintained in captive hatchery programs, inbreeding depression represents a critical genetic management concern that can compromise population viability and the long-term success of recovery efforts. This phenomenon is especially problematic in small founder populations where limited genetic diversity and unequal breeding contributions accelerate the loss of genetic variation across generations.
