# Anadromous Fish Migration

## Summary
Anadromous fish migration refers to the life history pattern in which fish species are born in freshwater rivers and streams, migrate to the ocean to grow and mature, and then return to their natal freshwater habitats to spawn and reproduce. This migratory behavior requires unobstructed passage through multiple aquatic environments including rivers, estuaries with varying salinity gradients, and coastal marine waters, making these fish particularly vulnerable to habitat fragmentation caused by dams and other barriers that block migration routes. Understanding and facilitating anadromous migration pathways is critical for conservation efforts, as the loss of connectivity between freshwater spawning grounds and marine feeding grounds can lead to population declines and species endangerment.
