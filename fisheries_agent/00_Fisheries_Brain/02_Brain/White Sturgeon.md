# White Sturgeon

## Summary
White Sturgeon (Acipenser transmontanus) is a large, long-lived anadromous and potamodromous fish species belonging to the family Acipenseridae, characterized by a cartilaginous skeleton with bony plates and found in freshwater systems of western North America, particularly the Columbia River drainage. As a keystone species in riverine ecosystems, White Sturgeon plays an important ecological role and has significant conservation value, though populations have declined substantially due to dam construction, habitat fragmentation, and overfishing, prompting restoration efforts including hatchery programs like those operated at the Abernathy Salmon Culture Technology Center. The species is notable for its inclusion in comprehensive ichthyological surveys and represents one of North America's native sturgeon species requiring multi-jurisdictional management and conservation strategies to maintain viable wild populations.
