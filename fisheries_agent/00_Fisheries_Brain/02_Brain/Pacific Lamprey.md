# Pacific Lamprey

## Summary
The Pacific lamprey (Entosphenus tridentatus) is a jawless fish native to western North American waters that has experienced significant population declines throughout its historical range, particularly in river systems where dam construction and habitat modification have blocked migration routes essential for its anadromous life cycle. As a parasitic feeder that attaches to and feeds on larger fish species, the Pacific lamprey plays an important ecological role in aquatic food webs, and its presence or absence can serve as an indicator of ecosystem health and connectivity in freshwater and marine environments. Conservation efforts have increasingly focused on restoring passage around barriers and understanding the factors contributing to its decline in order to maintain viable populations of this culturally and ecologically significant species.
