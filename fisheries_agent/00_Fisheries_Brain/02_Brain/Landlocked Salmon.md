# Landlocked Salmon

## Summary
Landlocked salmon are populations of Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) that complete their entire life cycle in freshwater lakes rather than migrating to the ocean, representing a non-anadromous form of the species that has become established in inland reservoirs and large lakes across North America. These fish occupy a similar ecological niche to other cold-water salmonids in lacustrine environments, where they serve as apex predators feeding on forage species such as alewives and other small fish, and are valued both as game fish and for their potential in reservoir fisheries management. Landlocked salmon require cold, well-oxygenated water and are sensitive to thermal stratification in reservoirs, making their successful establishment dependent on adequate cool-water habitat availability and suitable spawning substrates or hatchery supplementation.
