# Death Valley Model

## Summary
The Death Valley Model describes a pattern of complete geographic isolation among fish populations in extremely isolated desert springs and streams, with no gene flow (m = 0) between populations. This source presents it as one of two zoogeographic models for desert fish genetics, characterized by very little genetic polymorphism within populations but considerable divergence among populations, exemplified by Cyprinodon pupfishes in Death Valley and similar intermontane basins that were connected during wetter pluvial times but are now completely isolated.
