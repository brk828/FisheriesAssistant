# Thermocline

## Summary
A thermocline is a distinct layer in a stratified water body where temperature changes rapidly with depth, typically separating warmer surface waters from cooler deeper waters. In fisheries and aquatic ecology, the thermocline is significant because it often coincides with sharp changes in dissolved oxygen and other chemical conditions that profoundly affect fish distribution, behavior, and survival. Understanding thermocline dynamics is essential for fisheries management, particularly in reservoirs and lakes where thermal stratification can create inhospitable conditions in deeper waters and influence the downstream thermal and chemical composition of discharged water.
