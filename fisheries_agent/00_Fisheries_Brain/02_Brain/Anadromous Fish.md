# Anadromous Fish

## Summary
Anadromous fish are species that are born in freshwater streams and rivers but migrate to the ocean to grow and mature before returning to their natal freshwater systems to spawn. This life history strategy, exemplified by Atlantic salmon and Chinook salmon, requires these fish to navigate between marine and freshwater environments, making them dependent on the health and accessibility of both ecosystems. Conservation and management of anadromous fish populations involves protecting migration corridors, removing barriers like dams, and maintaining suitable spawning habitat in freshwater systems, as demonstrated by facilities like the Warm Springs National Fish Hatchery that work to enhance and sustain these ecologically and culturally important fish runs.
