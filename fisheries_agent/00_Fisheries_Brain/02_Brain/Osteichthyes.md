# Osteichthyes

## Summary
Osteichthyes refers to the class of bony fishes, characterized by the presence of a bony skeleton (as opposed to cartilaginous fishes like sharks), and represents the vast majority of fish species in both marine and freshwater ecosystems. In ichthyological research and fisheries science, Osteichthyes serves as a fundamental taxonomic classification essential for identification, conservation assessment, and ecological study of fish communities, particularly in freshwater systems where identification keys and systematic treatments have been critical tools since early comprehensive works by naturalists like Jordan and Evermann. Understanding the diversity and biology of osteichthyan fishes is foundational to aquatic ecology and conservation efforts, as this group encompasses most commercially and ecologically important fish species used in research methodologies ranging from specimen collection to non-lethal sampling techniques.
