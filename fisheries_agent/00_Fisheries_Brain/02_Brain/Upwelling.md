# Upwelling

## Summary
Upwelling is the process by which nutrient-rich deep water is brought to the surface of the ocean, typically driven by wind patterns, coastal geography, or oceanic current systems, creating zones of high biological productivity that support diverse fish communities and populations. In the context of ichthyology and conservation biology, upwelling zones are critical ecological features that concentrate prey organisms and support commercially important fisheries, while also influencing fish distribution patterns and species composition across marine biogeographic regions. Understanding upwelling dynamics is essential for predicting fish faunal composition, managing sustainable fisheries, and assessing how oceanographic processes structure marine ecosystems.
