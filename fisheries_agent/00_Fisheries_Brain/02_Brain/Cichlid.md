# Cichlid

## Summary
Cichlids are a highly diverse family of freshwater fish (Cichlidae) characterized by remarkable behavioral complexity, including sophisticated parental care and mate selection behaviors that often involve visual signals like coloration. They are particularly notable in evolutionary and ecological studies for their extraordinary species diversity, especially in isolated lake systems like those in Africa and South America, where rapid speciation has occurred through mechanisms such as sexual selection and ecological specialization. In conservation and invasion ecology contexts, cichlids are significant both as model organisms for understanding speciation and reproductive isolation and as influential species in ecosystems, where some introduced cichlids have become invasive with substantial ecological impacts on native fish communities.
