# Southwestern Willow Flycatcher

## Summary
The Southwestern Willow Flycatcher is a federally threatened riparian bird species whose habitat requirements intersect with freshwater fish conservation efforts in southwestern river systems, particularly the Lower Colorado River and its tributaries. Its dependence on native riparian vegetation such as Fremont Cottonwood and Goodding's Willow creates ecological linkages between avian habitat management and aquatic ecosystem restoration, as these riparian communities are integral to maintaining the hydrological and thermal conditions necessary for native fish species survival. Management of dams, water flows, and flood regimes designed to benefit both the flycatcher's nesting habitat and endangered native fish populations like the Loach Minnow represents a complex conservation challenge requiring integrated approaches across multiple taxa in altered river systems.
