# Sea Lamprey

## Summary
Sea lamprey are parasitic jawless fish that became a major invasive threat to Great Lakes ecosystems following their range expansion through the Welland Canal in the early 20th century, where they decimated native lake trout populations through predation on their tissues and body fluids. Their establishment as an invasive species fundamentally altered Great Lakes fish community dynamics and prompted large-scale restoration efforts, including coordinated lamprey control programs and the propagation of lake trout through hatcheries like those documented in these station profiles. Understanding sea lamprey ecology and implementing effective control measures remain critical components of Great Lakes fisheries management and native species recovery initiatives.
