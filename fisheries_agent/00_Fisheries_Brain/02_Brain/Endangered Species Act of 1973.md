# Endangered Species Act Of 1973

## Summary
The Endangered Species Act of 1973 is a landmark federal legislation that provides legal protection and recovery mechanisms for fish species threatened with extinction, requiring federal agencies to designate critical habitats, develop recovery plans, and coordinate conservation efforts across state and tribal boundaries. In aquatic contexts, the Act establishes regulatory frameworks governing the management and potential utilization of endangered fish populations, such as determining whether limited sport fisheries or research activities can proceed without jeopardizing species recovery. For ichthyologists and fisheries managers, the Act serves as the primary statutory authority directing scientific investigation, population monitoring, and conservation strategies for imperiled fish species like the Colorado squawfish.
