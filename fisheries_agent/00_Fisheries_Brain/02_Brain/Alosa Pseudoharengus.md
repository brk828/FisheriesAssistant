# Alosa Pseudoharengus

## Summary
Alosa pseudoharengus, commonly known as alewife, is a small anadromous clupeid fish that migrates from coastal Atlantic waters into freshwater rivers and streams to spawn, making it an ecologically and economically important species across multiple river drainages. As a diadromous species, alewives play a critical role in aquatic food webs by serving as forage for larger predatory fish and by transporting marine-derived nutrients into freshwater ecosystems during their spawning migrations. This species has been documented across major Atlantic and Ohio River system drainages, where it historically supported significant commercial and subsistence fisheries before experiencing population declines due to habitat fragmentation, pollution, and dam construction.
