# Avian Botulism

## Summary
Avian Botulism is a disease that caused a catastrophic outbreak at the Salton Sea in 1996, killing over 20,000 birds representing 64 species, including 1,229 endangered Brown Pelicans and 8,000 White Pelicans (15% of the western North American population). The outbreak was linked to Vibrio infections in tilapia fish, which weakened the fish and allowed botulism spores to penetrate their tissues, creating a pathway for bird infection.
