# Cyprinus Carpio

## Summary
Cyprinus carpio, commonly known as common carp, is a non-native cyprinid fish species that has become established in North American freshwater ecosystems where it is documented as an invasive species competing with and potentially displacing native fish communities. In the context of southwestern U.S. river systems like the Verde River, carp represents a significant conservation concern as a non-native introduction that, despite its presence, has not yet achieved dominant abundance in reaches where native large-bodied fishes remain prevalent. The species' benthic feeding behavior and high reproductive capacity make it an ecologically disruptive invasive species relevant to aquatic conservation and native fish recovery efforts in desert stream ecosystems.
