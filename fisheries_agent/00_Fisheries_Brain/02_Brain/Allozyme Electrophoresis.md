# Allozyme Electrophoresis

## Summary
Allozyme electrophoresis is a molecular technique that separates protein variants (allozymes) produced by different alleles of the same gene using electrical charge, allowing researchers to assess genetic variation and population structure in fish species. In conservation and fisheries contexts, this method has been instrumental for evaluating genetic diversity in endangered fish populations, distinguishing between natural and artificial populations, and informing captive propagation strategies by identifying genetically distinct individuals or lineages that should be managed separately. The technique proved particularly valuable for threatened species like desert pupfish, razorback suckers, and bonytail where understanding fine-scale genetic variation was critical for developing effective recovery and breeding programs.
