# Backwater Habitat

## Summary
Backwater habitat refers to relatively quiet, low-velocity water areas that form along the margins of rivers, typically in slack water zones behind obstructions or in side channels where water movement is minimal. These habitats are ecologically significant for many fish species, particularly native fishes like razorback suckers, as they provide refuge from strong currents, enhanced feeding opportunities, and favorable conditions for spawning and larval development. Backwater areas are often preferred by fish despite sometimes comprising a smaller proportion of total available habitat, making their conservation and restoration critical for maintaining healthy fish populations in riverine ecosystems.
