# Rio Yaqui

## Summary
The Rio Yaqui is a major desert river system spanning northern Mexico (Sonora and Chihuahua) that serves as a critical conservation focus in ichthyology due to its endemic native fish fauna and ongoing threats from water extraction and habitat degradation. This arid Southwest river drainage is characterized by naturally variable flow regimes and supports a diversity of native fish species, including several of conservation concern, though exotic species have increasingly colonized the main channel habitats. The Rio Yaqui and its tributaries (including the Rio Sahuaripa and Rio Bacanora sub-basins) have been the subject of intensive surveys and capture-and-transport recovery efforts by U.S. and Mexican resource agencies to document ichthyofaunal composition and preserve native species before further habitat loss occurs.
