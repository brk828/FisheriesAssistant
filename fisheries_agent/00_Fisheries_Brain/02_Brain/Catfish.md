# Catfish

## Summary
Catfish are a diverse group of freshwater and brackish-water fishes belonging to the order Siluriformes, characterized by their whisker-like barbels and scaleless bodies, making them important both as native species in aquatic ecosystems and as invasive threats to endemic fish populations. In conservation contexts, catfish species are frequently introduced into isolated habitats where they act as voracious predators capable of decimating vulnerable native fish populations, particularly small endemic species like pupfish that lack evolutionary defenses against such predation pressure. Their ecological impact as non-native invaders, combined with their widespread use in aquaculture and as food sources, makes catfish a significant concern in freshwater conservation biology and ecosystem management.
