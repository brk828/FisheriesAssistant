# Phylogenetic Species Concept

## Summary
The Phylogenetic Species Concept defines species based on their evolutionary history and genetic distinctness rather than reproductive isolation or morphological characteristics, emphasizing that species should represent independently evolving lineages identifiable through diagnostic genetic or molecular traits. In fish science and conservation, this approach is particularly valuable for recognizing cryptic species and previously undescribed diversity, especially in hyperdiverse regions like Southeast Asian freshwaters where morphological differences may be subtle but phylogenetic relationships reveal significant evolutionary independence. This concept provides a more pragmatic framework than traditional species definitions for conservation prioritization, allowing scientists to identify and protect evolutionarily distinct fish populations even before they are formally described or fully understood morphologically.
