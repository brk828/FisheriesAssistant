# Freshwater Fish Biogeography

## Summary
Freshwater Fish Biogeography is the study of geographic distribution patterns of fish species across freshwater ecosystems, encompassing the historical, ecological, and environmental factors that determine where different fish taxa occur across river systems, lakes, and other inland water bodies. This field integrates distributional data, zoogeographic analysis, and landscape-scale factors to understand species ranges, dispersal barriers, and the processes shaping fish community composition across regions. Understanding freshwater fish biogeography is essential for conservation planning, as it helps identify endemic species, recognize range limitations, and assess how fish populations respond to environmental changes and habitat fragmentation.
