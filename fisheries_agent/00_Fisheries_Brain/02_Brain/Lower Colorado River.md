# Lower Colorado River

## Summary
The Lower Colorado River refers to the downstream reach of the Colorado River system, primarily between Lake Havasu and the Mexican border, which represents a critical but highly altered aquatic ecosystem subject to intensive water management, dam operations, and regulatory oversight for endangered species conservation. This segment is characterized by complex interactions between managed reservoir systems (including Senator Wash Reservoir and other impoundments), remnant lotic riverine habitat, and numerous federally listed and candidate species that depend on specific flow regimes and water quality conditions maintained through Biological Opinions and interagency management protocols. The Lower Colorado River serves as a focal point for integrated fisheries science and conservation biology research, requiring coordinated monitoring and adaptive management among federal agencies, tribal governments, and water resource managers to balance hydroelectric power generation, water allocation, and aquatic species recovery.
