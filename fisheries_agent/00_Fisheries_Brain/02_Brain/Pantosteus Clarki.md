# Pantosteus Clarki

## Summary
Pantosteus clarki, commonly known as the Desert Sucker, is a native benthic catostomid fish characterized by a subterminal, cartilage-edged mouth specifically adapted for scraping algae and periphyton from rocky substrates in southwestern aquatic ecosystems. This species is an important component of native fish communities in arid-region streams and is frequently monitored in conservation surveys as an indicator of habitat quality and the presence of intact native fish assemblages. Its presence and reproductive activity, including the development of nuptial tubercles during breeding season, serve as ecological markers in assessments of stream health and aquatic ecosystem integrity in regions threatened by non-native species invasion.
