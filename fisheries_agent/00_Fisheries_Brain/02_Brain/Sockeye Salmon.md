# Sockeye Salmon

## Summary
Sockeye salmon (Oncorhynchus nerka) are anadromous Pacific salmon species characterized by their distinctive life history alternating between freshwater and marine environments, making them ecologically important indicators of watershed health and subject to intensive management through hatchery programs and habitat conservation efforts. These fish are particularly significant in Columbia River drainage systems and other North American river networks, where they support both commercial fisheries and subsistence harvesting while serving as keystone species that transport marine-derived nutrients to freshwater ecosystems during spawning migrations. Understanding sockeye salmon population dynamics, reproductive ecology, and habitat requirements is central to aquatic conservation biology, particularly given their sensitivity to changes in water temperature, flow regimes, and ecosystem degradation.
