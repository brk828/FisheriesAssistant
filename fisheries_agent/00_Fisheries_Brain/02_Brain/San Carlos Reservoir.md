# San Carlos Reservoir

## Summary
San Carlos Reservoir is an impounded lentic (lake-like) water body on the Gila River in Arizona, created by Coolidge Dam as part of the San Carlos Irrigation Project, that serves as critical habitat for native fish species including razorback suckers and other aquatic organisms. As a major water storage and management facility in the southwestern United States, the reservoir's operational characteristics—including water level fluctuations, spillway functionality, and conservation storage capacity—directly influence fish survival, population dynamics, and the overall ecological integrity of the Gila River system. The reservoir represents a key study site for fisheries research focused on understanding how large-scale water management infrastructure affects native fish conservation and aquatic ecosystem health in arid regions.
