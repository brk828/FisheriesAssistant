# Population Bottleneck

## Summary
A population bottleneck in fish science refers to a drastic reduction in population size that causes a loss of genetic diversity and can severely limit a population's adaptive potential and long-term viability. This concept is particularly relevant in conservation efforts where small numbers of founder individuals are used to establish captive breeding programs or reintroduce species, as the genetic diversity captured in these founders directly constrains the genetic variation available for natural selection and species recovery. Population bottlenecks are a critical concern in aquatic conservation because even seemingly adequate numbers of founders (such as 5-10 individuals) may result in substantial genetic losses if not managed carefully to maximize effective population size and maintain heterozygosity.
