# Gas Bubble Disease

## Summary
Gas Bubble Disease is a pathological condition in fish caused by exposure to dissolved gas supersaturation, particularly nitrogen, which leads to the formation of gas bubbles in the fish's tissues and blood vessels. This condition occurs in aquatic systems downstream of dams and during high water flows where atmospheric gases become dissolved at concentrations exceeding saturation levels, and is particularly lethal to salmonids including juvenile and adult salmon and trout. Symptoms include external and internal manifestations, with younger fish generally showing greater physiological resilience than older fish, though prolonged exposure to supersaturated water can cause significant mortality and sublethal stress across all age classes.
