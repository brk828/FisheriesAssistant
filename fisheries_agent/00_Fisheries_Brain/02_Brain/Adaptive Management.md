# Adaptive Management

## Summary
Adaptive management in fish science and aquatic ecology is an iterative decision-making approach that acknowledges uncertainty in complex systems by implementing management actions as experiments, monitoring their outcomes, and adjusting strategies based on what is learned. Rather than relying on perfect predictive models—which are often unattainable for large, complex river ecosystems and their fish communities—adaptive management uses cautious, reversible actions to test hypotheses about how environmental changes (such as flow modifications) affect fish populations and habitat, allowing managers to refine their approach over time as new data emerges. This framework is particularly valuable for endangered fish conservation in systems where mechanistic understanding is incomplete, enabling prudent decision-making despite significant ecological uncertainty.
