# Exoglossum

## Summary
Exoglossum is a genus of North American cyprinid fishes (family Cyprinidae) characterized by their pebble-nest-building reproductive behavior, wherein breeding males construct mound-like structures from small stones to create spawning pits that reduce local water current velocity. This adaptive nesting strategy, shared convergently with related genera like Nocomis and Semotilus, provides a selective advantage by creating hydrodynamically favorable microhabitats for egg deposition and incubation in flowing stream environments. Members of this genus inhabit slow to moderate-flowing freshwater streams in eastern North America and represent an important model system for understanding reproductive ecology and nest-building behavior in cyprinid fishes.
