# Amargosa Pupfish

## Summary
The Amargosa Pupfish is an endemic species of small cyprinodont fish inhabiting isolated spring systems within the Death Valley/Amargosa drainage basin of the southwestern United States, representing a relict population adapted to the unique hydrochemistry and thermal conditions of desert spring environments. As a species of special concern in California and a key indicator of aquatic ecosystem health in one of North America's most arid regions, the Amargosa Pupfish exemplifies the conservation challenges faced by desert aquatic fauna, which are threatened by habitat degradation, groundwater extraction, invasive species, and the isolation of spring populations that limits genetic diversity and dispersal. Management efforts for this species typically focus on habitat protection, population monitoring, and maintaining connectivity between fragmented spring populations to ensure long-term species persistence.
