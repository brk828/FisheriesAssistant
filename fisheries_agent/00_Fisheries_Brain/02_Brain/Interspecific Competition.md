# Interspecific Competition

## Summary
Interspecific competition in fish and aquatic ecology refers to the interaction between different species that share the same aquatic habitat and compete for limited resources such as food, space, or dissolved oxygen. This competition can significantly influence species composition, distribution patterns, and community structure in aquatic systems, as demonstrated in studies of zooplankton and fish assemblages where resource availability constrains the coexistence and relative abundance of competing species. Understanding interspecific competition is essential for fisheries management and conservation, as it helps predict how introduced species may impact native populations and how environmental changes affect community dynamics.
