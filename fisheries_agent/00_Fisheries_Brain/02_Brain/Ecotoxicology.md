# Ecotoxicology

## Summary
Ecotoxicology in fish science is the study of how toxic substances and pollutants affect fish populations and aquatic ecosystems, examining the physiological responses, survival, and health impacts of contaminants on fish species. This field is critical for understanding environmental stressors—such as pesticides, heavy metals, and other chemical compounds—and their cascading effects on fish biology, population dynamics, and ecosystem function. By using model organisms like salmonids in controlled experimental settings, ecotoxicologists can assess toxicity thresholds, bioaccumulation, and sublethal effects to inform conservation strategies and environmental regulations protecting aquatic resources.
