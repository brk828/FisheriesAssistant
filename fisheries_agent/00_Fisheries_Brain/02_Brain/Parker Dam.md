# Parker Dam

## Summary
Parker Dam is a major hydroelectric facility on the Lower Colorado River that plays a critical role in regulating river flows and water management in the southwestern United States, with significant implications for aquatic ecology and fish conservation. The dam's operations, particularly its generation of daily flow fluctuations driven by hydropower demands, directly affect native fish spawning and recruitment success in downstream habitats, making it a focal point in debates between resource managers and conservation biologists regarding impacts on endangered species like the humpback chub. Understanding Parker Dam's operational effects on river hydrology and habitat quality is essential for developing management strategies that balance water delivery, power generation, and protection of native fish populations in the heavily regulated Lower Colorado River ecosystem.
