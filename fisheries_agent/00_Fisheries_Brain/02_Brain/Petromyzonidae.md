# Petromyzonidae

## Summary
Petromyzonidae, commonly known as lampreys, is a family of jawless fishes (agnathans) representing one of the most primitive vertebrate lineages and characterized by their parasitic feeding behavior using a circular, sucker-like mouth lined with keratinous teeth. Members of this family, such as the sea lamprey (Petromyzon marinus) and American brook lamprey (Ichthyomyzon species), are ecologically significant in freshwater and marine ecosystems as both anadromous/diadromous migratory species and invasive predators in certain regions, making them important subjects in conservation biology and fisheries management. Their presence in ichthyofaunal surveys and taxonomic inventories reflects their historical and contemporary roles in North American aquatic ecosystems, though some populations face decline while others, particularly invasive sea lampreys in the Great Lakes, require active management interventions.
