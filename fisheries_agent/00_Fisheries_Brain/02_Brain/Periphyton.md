# Periphyton

## Summary
Periphyton refers to the community of algae, bacteria, and other microorganisms that attach to and grow on submerged surfaces such as rocks, sediments, and aquatic vegetation in river and reservoir systems. In fish ecology and conservation, periphyton is critical as a foundational food source and primary productivity base that supports the trophic food web, ultimately sustaining populations of fish species such as the endangered Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker. Changes in periphyton abundance and composition are closely linked to habitat conditions including substrate type, water quality, flow regimes, and light availability, making it an important indicator of ecosystem health and a key consideration in habitat restoration and conservation management.
