# Bullhead Catfish

## Summary
Bullhead catfish (Ictalurus species, including black bullhead, yellow bullhead, and brown bullhead) are small to medium-sized benthic catfishes commonly found in freshwater impoundments and lentic systems throughout North America, where they serve as secondary fish community members in pond ecosystems alongside dominant species like largemouth bass and bluegill sunfish. These hardy, bottom-feeding omnivores are ecologically significant as prey species for larger predatory fish and as bioindicators of water quality due to their tolerance of turbid, low-oxygen conditions in small impoundments. In conservation and management contexts, bullhead catfish populations are monitored through standard sampling methods like minnow seining to assess overall fish community composition and ecosystem health in managed pond systems.
