# Evolutionary Biology

## Summary
Evolutionary Biology, in the context of fish science and aquatic conservation, is the study of how fish populations change and diversify over time through mechanisms such as natural selection, genetic drift, and adaptation to their environments. This discipline is essential for understanding species relationships, hybridization patterns, and the evolutionary distinctiveness of populations—knowledge that directly informs conservation priorities, endangered species management, and the assessment of whether hybrid fish should receive legal protection. By examining the evolutionary history and genetic variation within and between fish species, evolutionary biologists provide critical evidence for conservation decisions and help predict how populations may respond to environmental change or human intervention.
