# Endangered Fish Species

## Summary
Endangered fish species are aquatic fauna populations officially designated by regulatory agencies such as the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service as facing significant risk of extinction due to habitat loss, environmental degradation, or other threatening processes, necessitating legal protection and specialized research permits for scientific study and conservation efforts. In the context of ichthyological research, particularly in arid regions like Arizona, endangered fish species often represent imperiled native aquatic fauna that require intensive monitoring and collection authority under federal permits to understand their biology and inform recovery strategies. These designations establish strict regulatory frameworks governing how scientists may conduct research on threatened populations, balancing the need for scientific data collection against the imperative to avoid further decline of already vulnerable species.
