# Population Ecology

## Summary
Population ecology in fish science refers to the study of how fish populations change in size, structure, and dynamics over time, examining factors such as reproduction, mortality, migration, and interactions between individuals that influence population-level outcomes. This framework is essential for understanding how environmental conditions, species characteristics, and behavioral traits shape population characteristics and heterogeneity across different populations and geographic contexts. Applied to conservation and management, population ecology provides the theoretical foundation for predicting population responses to environmental change and designing effective strategies for sustainable fisheries and species protection.
