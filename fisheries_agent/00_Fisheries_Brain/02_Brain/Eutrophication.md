# Eutrophication

## Summary
Eutrophication is the process of nutrient enrichment (particularly nitrogen and phosphorus) in aquatic systems that leads to excessive algal growth, oxygen depletion, and degradation of water quality, ultimately harming fish populations and other aquatic organisms. This condition typically results from anthropogenic inputs such as agricultural runoff, wastewater discharge, or urban stormwater, and can be exacerbated by elevated intermediate nitrogen compounds like nitrite, which are toxic to fish. In the context of fisheries science and conservation biology, eutrophication represents a critical threat to aquatic ecosystem health, requiring management strategies ranging from nutrient source control to direct water quality interventions like algaecide application.
