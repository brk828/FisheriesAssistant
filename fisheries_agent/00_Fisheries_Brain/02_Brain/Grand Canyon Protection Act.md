# Grand Canyon Protection Act

## Summary
The Grand Canyon Protection Act is a federal mandate that requires ecosystem-based, long-term biological monitoring of Colorado River resources affected by Glen Canyon Dam operations, with particular emphasis on protecting four endangered fish species: Humpback Chub, Colorado Squawfish, Bonytail Chub, and Razorback Sucker. The Act established legal requirements for environmental impact assessment and operational decision-making frameworks aimed at mitigating the detrimental effects of dam construction and operation on aquatic habitat, including thermal alterations, migration blockage, and loss of backwater environments critical to native fish survival in the Grand Canyon, Lake Powell, and Lake Mead systems. Through mandating comprehensive biological monitoring and the Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement process, the Act formalized conservation biology principles as central to federal water management policy on the Colorado River.
