# Random Colonization

## Summary
Random colonization is a hypothesis proposing that island communities are assembled through random sampling of species from a mainland pool, with observed patterns like species-to-genus ratios arising from statistical effects alone without biological interactions. This source demonstrates that the random colonization hypothesis actually encompasses two distinct processes—random dispersal and random extinction—both of which require independent testing and neither of which has been adequately verified.
