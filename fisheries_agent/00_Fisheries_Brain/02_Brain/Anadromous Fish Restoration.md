# Anadromous Fish Restoration

## Summary
Anadromous fish restoration refers to the active recovery and reestablishment of populations of fish species that migrate from ocean environments to freshwater rivers for spawning, with a primary focus on species like Atlantic Salmon and secondary attention to anadromous Rainbow Trout. These restoration efforts typically target degraded river systems in the North Atlantic basin, such as the Merrimack and Pawcatuck Rivers, and involve habitat rehabilitation and population management to rebuild migratory pathways and spawning grounds that have been damaged by dam construction, pollution, and other anthropogenic barriers. The ultimate goal is to restore functional anadromous fish populations and their critical ecological roles within freshwater river ecosystems.
