# Lake Idaho

## Summary
Lake Idaho was a large Pliocene freshwater lake that existed on the western Snake River Plain of Idaho, representing an important paleontological and paleoecological system for understanding ancient fish distributions and aquatic faunas of the region. The lake's sedimentary deposits, particularly the carbonate sequences of the Glenns Ferry Formation, preserve evidence of fish and mollusk assemblages that help reconstruct drainage patterns, biogeographic connections, and tectonic history of the region, including a documented connection to the Sacramento River drainage approximately 3-5 million years ago. Lake Idaho's formation and evolution in a block-faulted basin provide critical insights into how tectonics, drainage capture, and environmental change influenced the distribution and evolution of aquatic organisms in the Great Basin and Columbia River regions during the Pliocene.
