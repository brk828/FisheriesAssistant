# Source-Sink Dynamics

## Summary
Source-sink dynamics describe population systems where some habitats (sources) produce surplus individuals that disperse to other habitats (sinks) where local reproduction is insufficient to maintain populations. This study demonstrates that upstream reaches of the Yampa River and off-channel reservoirs (Lake Catamount, Stagecoach Reservoir, Elkhead Reservoir) function as source habitats for Northern Pike, producing large numbers of fish that disperse predominantly downstream (90% of movements), with immigration and recruitment rates consistently exceeding removal and mortality in all reaches. The upstream reaches had the highest emigration rates (34% from above Hayden, 19% from Hayden to Craig) and supported 36% of age-0 or age-1 fish captured, while reservoir escapement provided continuous propagule pressure—over 5,500 Northern Pike were removed from Lake Catamount alone in 2008-2010, twice the number removed from three Yampa River study reaches combined.
