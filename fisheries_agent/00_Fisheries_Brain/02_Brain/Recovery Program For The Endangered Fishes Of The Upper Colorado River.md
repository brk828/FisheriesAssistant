# Recovery Program For The Endangered Fishes Of The Upper Colorado River

## Summary
The Recovery Program for the Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River is a multi-agency management and restoration initiative designed to conserve and recover four native fish species (Colorado pikeminnow, humpback chub, razorback sucker, and bonytail) that have been decimated by habitat alterations, dam operations, and non-native species introductions in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The program coordinates scientific research, habitat restoration efforts (such as the Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program), and adaptive management strategies to balance water resource development with the ecological recovery needs of these endangered species. Through collaborative planning and implementation across federal and state agencies, the program synthesizes empirical fish science with operational management decisions to restore critical spawning and rearing habitats while maintaining compliance with the Endangered Species Act.
