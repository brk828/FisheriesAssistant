# Environmental Protection Agency

## Summary
The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) serves as a critical federal regulatory body in aquatic conservation and fisheries management, establishing and enforcing water quality standards, pollution controls, and environmental regulations that directly protect fish habitats and aquatic ecosystems. In the context of ichthyology and fisheries science, the EPA works alongside agencies such as the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, state fish and game departments, and resource management entities to ensure that water quality parameters essential for fish survival—including dissolved oxygen, temperature, and chemical contamination levels—meet scientifically-based standards. The agency's authority extends to managing impacts from major water resource projects and human activities that affect fish populations, making it a central coordinating institution in multi-jurisdictional efforts to conserve endemic species and maintain ecological integrity of aquatic systems.
