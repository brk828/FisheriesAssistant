# Pacific Ocean

## Summary
The Pacific Ocean, in the context of fish science and conservation biology, represents one of the two major oceanic basins of the Americas and serves as a critical habitat region for numerous fish species, including both marine and anadromous populations that depend on Pacific-draining river systems for spawning and rearing. This vast aquatic ecosystem encompasses diverse coastal, estuarine, and freshwater environments where species such as Centropomus occupants and Sacramento River anadromous fishes complete essential portions of their life cycles. The Pacific basin is particularly significant for conservation efforts due to the impacts of human infrastructure like dams on migratory fish populations and their associated freshwater spawning habitats.
