# River Continuum Concept

## Summary
The River Continuum Concept is a foundational framework in aquatic ecology that describes how physical, chemical, and biological characteristics change predictably along a river's length from headwaters to mouth, creating distinct ecological zones and communities adapted to local conditions. This concept builds upon earlier zonation schemes (such as those by Illies & Botosaneanu) to explain how fish communities, macroinvertebrates, and energy sources shift across the longitudinal gradient, with upstream reaches dominated by small, cold-water specialists and downstream areas supporting larger, warm-water species. The framework is essential for understanding fish habitat requirements, niche partitioning, and designing effective conservation and restoration strategies that account for the interconnected nature of riverine ecosystems.
