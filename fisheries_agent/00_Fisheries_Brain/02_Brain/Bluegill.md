# Bluegill

## Summary
Bluegill (Lepomis macrochirus), commonly known as bream, is a small centrarchid sunfish species native to North American freshwater systems that serves as an important forage fish and secondary sport fish in warmwater fisheries. As a prolific panfish with relatively fast growth rates and high reproductive capacity, bluegill are frequently used as indicator species for ecosystem health assessments and are commonly stocked in lakes and ponds for both recreational angling and as a food source for larger predatory fish like largemouth bass. Their ecological role spans multiple trophic levels as both consumers of aquatic invertebrates and prey for larger gamefish, making them a key component of freshwater fish community dynamics and conservation planning in North American aquatic ecosystems.
