# Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program

## Summary
The Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program is a recovery initiative for endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin that aims to restore flood plain habitats by reconnecting them to main river channels at near-historical flooding frequency, timing, and duration. According to this 1994 program document, the short-term goal is to restore flood plain habitats to assist recovery of the Green River Razorback Sucker population and reestablish one self-sustaining population in the Colorado River subbasin, while the long-term goal is to restore flood plain habitats for all endangered fishes in the basin. The program operates through sequential phases including site selection from 57 inventoried bottomland sites, screening for contaminants and biological suitability, design and restoration with features like inlet/outlet control structures and predator screens, and evaluation, with a three-year budget of $6.6 million covering activities across the Green River, Colorado River, and Gunnison River.
