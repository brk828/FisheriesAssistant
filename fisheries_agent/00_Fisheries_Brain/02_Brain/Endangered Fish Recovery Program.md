# Endangered Fish Recovery Program

## Summary
An Endangered Fish Recovery Program is a coordinated conservation initiative designed to restore populations of threatened aquatic species through a combination of captive breeding, head-start rearing, and strategic reintroduction into native habitats. These programs typically employ techniques such as PIT tagging for population monitoring, staged release protocols based on size thresholds, and multi-site management across dam-regulated river systems to maximize survival and reproductive success of critically endangered fish species like the Razorback Sucker. Such programs integrate fisheries management, genetic considerations, and ecosystem restoration to rebuild self-sustaining wild populations while addressing threats posed by habitat fragmentation, altered streamflow regimes, and competition from non-native species.
