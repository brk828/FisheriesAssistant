# Salmo Salar

## Summary
Salmo salar, commonly known as Atlantic salmon, is a highly migratory anadromous fish species that spawns in freshwater rivers and streams across the North Atlantic while spending much of its adult life in the ocean. As a coldwater specialist and economically important species, it serves as a key indicator organism in conservation biology and aquatic ecology research, particularly for studying the impacts of environmental factors such as oxygen concentration, temperature, and habitat degradation on fish physiology and population dynamics. The species' complex life history, involving transitions between marine and freshwater environments, makes it valuable for understanding how multiple stressors affect diadromous fish populations and the health of their natal river systems.
