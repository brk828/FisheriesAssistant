# Sierra Madre Occidental

## Summary
The Sierra Madre Occidental is a major physiographic and tectonic province in northern Mexico characterized by calc-alkaline ignimbrite plateaus and Tertiary uplift that fundamentally shaped the region's aquatic systems and fish biogeography. This mountain range's geological history created numerous endorheic desert basins and montane headwater streams—such as the Nazas-Aguanaval, Parras Basin, and Mezquital drainages—which became isolated habitats supporting disjunct fish populations with distinct evolutionary histories. The Sierra Madre Occidental's complex topography, varied substrates, and perennial water sources in headwater regions make it a critical area for understanding fish endemism, population fragmentation, and conservation priorities in arid and semi-arid North America.
