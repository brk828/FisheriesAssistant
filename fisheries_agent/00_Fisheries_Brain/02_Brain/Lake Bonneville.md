# Lake Bonneville

## Summary
Lake Bonneville was a large Pleistocene pluvial lake that once covered much of the Great Basin in the western United States and is now represented by the Great Salt Lake and other remnant water bodies. In fish science and conservation biology, Lake Bonneville is significant as an ancient biogeographic region whose isolated fish populations have been extensively studied to understand speciation, endemism, and the evolutionary history of western North American freshwater fishes. The lake's historical extent and spillover connections during high water stands are crucial for reconstructing ichthyofaunal distributions and explaining current patterns of fish biodiversity across now-desiccated basins in the American West.
