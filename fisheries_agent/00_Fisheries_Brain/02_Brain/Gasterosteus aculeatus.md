# Gasterosteus Aculeatus

## Summary
Gasterosteus aculeatus, commonly known as the threespine stickleback, is a small freshwater and anadromous fish species found across the Northern Hemisphere that serves as a model organism in evolutionary biology, ecology, and conservation studies due to its phenotypic plasticity, rapid adaptation to diverse aquatic environments, and well-documented population-level variation in morphological traits such as armor plating and spine development. This species is particularly valuable for investigating evolutionary processes, local adaptation, and the ecological factors driving divergence among isolated populations, making it instrumental in understanding how fish respond to environmental changes and human-induced habitat modifications. Gasterosteus aculeatus populations also function as important components of freshwater and estuarine ecosystems, with conservation significance in regions where habitat loss and invasive species threaten native stickleback populations.
