# Aquatic Food Web

## Summary
An aquatic food web is the interconnected network of feeding relationships among organisms in freshwater and marine ecosystems, encompassing energy transfer from primary producers (plants and algae) through consumers at multiple trophic levels (zooplankton, small fish, larger predatory fish) to decomposers. In river and lake systems, the food web is fundamentally structured by the availability of organic matter from various sources—including living macrophytes, phytoplankton, drift organisms, detritus, and dissolved nutrients—which support the growth and survival of aquatic biota at every level. Understanding aquatic food webs is essential for fisheries management and conservation because alterations to habitat structure, nutrient cycling, or energy pathways can cascade through these networks and profoundly affect fish populations and ecosystem productivity.
