# Endangered Species Recovery

## Summary
Endangered Species Recovery in fish science refers to the coordinated management and restoration efforts undertaken to restore populations of fish species listed under the Endangered Species Act, typically involving identification of decline causes, maintenance of genetic refugial populations, and strategic production and reintroduction of captive-bred stock into suitable habitats. These programs, often managed by agencies like the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, employ techniques such as controlled hatchery breeding, overwintering optimization, and habitat restoration to rebuild viable, self-sustaining populations in the wild. The goal is to reduce extinction risk and eventually achieve population recovery sufficient to warrant delisting from protected status.
