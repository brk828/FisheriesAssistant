# Species Translocation

## Summary
Species translocation is the practice of moving organisms from their native range to establish populations in new locations. This letter criticizes the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's increasing use of translocation outside native ranges as a primary conservation strategy for endangered species, arguing it was being used to justify recovery programs and avoid difficult habitat protection decisions. The author contends that such translocations, while potentially useful as temporary measures, were becoming endpoints rather than tools supporting wild population recovery in native habitats.
