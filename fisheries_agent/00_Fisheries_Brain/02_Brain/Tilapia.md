# Tilapia

## Summary
Tilapia refers to a genus of freshwater and brackish-water cichlid fishes native to Africa and the Middle East that have become widely introduced non-native species in aquatic systems worldwide, including the southwestern United States. In conservation and ecological contexts, tilapia are typically regarded as problematic invasive species due to their competitive and predatory impacts on native fish populations, particularly in isolated or vulnerable habitats such as desert springs and small ponds. Their successful establishment in non-native environments is attributed to their hardiness, rapid reproduction, and ability to thrive in degraded water conditions, making them a significant threat to endemic and endangered species in invaded ecosystems.
