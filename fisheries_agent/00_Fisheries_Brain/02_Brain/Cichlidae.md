# Cichlidae

## Summary
Cichlidae is a diverse family of freshwater and brackish-water fish found primarily in Africa, South America, and Asia, characterized by their parental care behaviors, trophic specialization, and ecological importance as both predators and competitors in aquatic ecosystems. In conservation and ecological contexts, cichlids serve as key indicator organisms for understanding ecosystem dynamics, including predator-prey interactions and habitat-specific adaptations, while also being of significant concern when non-native species become established in new environments. The family is particularly notable for its remarkable behavioral complexity, including visual hunting strategies and the prevalence of male brood care, making cichlids important subjects for studying fish ecology, evolution, and the impacts of species introductions on native aquatic communities.
