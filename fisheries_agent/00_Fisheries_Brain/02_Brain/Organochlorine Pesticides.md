# Organochlorine Pesticides

## Summary
Organochlorine pesticides are synthetic chlorine-containing compounds historically used for agricultural pest control that persist in aquatic environments and accumulate in fish tissues through food webs, posing significant threats to fish populations and ecosystem health. These chemicals, including DDT and endrin, are highly resistant to degradation and can remain detectable in sediments and organisms for extended periods, with concentrations increasing at higher trophic levels through bioaccumulation and biomagnification. In ichthyological and conservation contexts, organochlorine pesticides are of primary concern because they concentrate in fish at levels far exceeding environmental water concentrations, leading to physiological impacts, reproductive impairment, and population-level effects that compromise both wild fish conservation and aquaculture sustainability.
