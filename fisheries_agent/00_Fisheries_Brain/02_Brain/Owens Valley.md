# Owens Valley

## Summary
The Owens Valley represents a significant case study in the historical transition of American fisheries management, particularly marking the shift from conventional put-and-take trout fishery operations toward conservation-focused approaches for endemic desert fishes. As documented in Edwin P. Pister's career trajectory, management activities in the Owens Valley during mid-20th century contrast sharply with the later establishment of the Desert Fishes Council and its focus on preserving threatened species in isolated spring systems of the Great Basin and Mojave deserts. This geographic and conceptual distinction underscores how recognition of the precarious status of desert endemic fishes—species like the Pahrump killifish confined to single springs—prompted a fundamental reorientation of conservation priorities among fisheries scientists and resource managers.
