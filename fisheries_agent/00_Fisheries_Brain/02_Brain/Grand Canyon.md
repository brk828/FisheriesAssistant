# Grand Canyon

## Summary
The Grand Canyon, as it relates to fish science, refers to the ecologically significant reach of the Colorado River corridor flowing through Grand Canyon National Park, which represents a critical conservation area for native fish species such as humpback chub (*Gila cypha*) and bonytail chub that face threats from habitat alteration by Glen Canyon Dam and competition from introduced non-native species. This region has been the subject of intensive ichthyological research, monitoring, and management efforts since at least the 1960s, involving multiple federal agencies and academic institutions seeking to understand and mitigate the ecological impacts of regulated river flows and exotic fish fauna on endemic and declining native fish populations. The Grand Canyon Colorado River corridor exemplifies the complex challenges of managing aquatic ecosystems in highly altered riverine systems where conservation of native biodiversity must be balanced against competing water resource demands.
