# Radiotelemetry In Fisheries Research

## Summary
Radiotelemetry in fisheries research is a tracking technique that uses surgically implanted or externally attached radio transmitters to monitor the movements, habitat use, and behavior of individual fish in their natural environment. This technology enables researchers to collect detailed data on fish migration patterns, home range size, spawning site fidelity, and responses to environmental changes or management actions, making it particularly valuable for studying endangered species recovery, assessing habitat quality, and evaluating the effectiveness of conservation measures in aquatic ecosystems.
