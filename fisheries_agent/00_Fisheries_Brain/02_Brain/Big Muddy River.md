# Big Muddy River

## Summary
The Big Muddy River is a major river system in southern Illinois that serves as an important reference point for studying fish distribution and habitat conditions in the region's aquatic ecosystems. As documented in historical and contemporary surveys, the Big Muddy River and its tributaries (such as Kinkaid Creek) have been sites of significant ichthyological research, particularly for understanding species like the Cypress Minnow (*Hybognathus hayi*) and the impacts of hydrological changes such as backwater flooding on fish communities. The river's sluggish, sediment-laden characteristics with soft mud and silt substrates define the habitat conditions that support or limit various fish species, making it a key system for monitoring aquatic biodiversity and conservation status in Illinois.
