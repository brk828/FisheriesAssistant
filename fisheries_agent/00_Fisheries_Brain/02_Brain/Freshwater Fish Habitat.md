# Freshwater Fish Habitat

## Summary
Freshwater fish habitat encompasses the physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of inland aquatic environments that support fish populations and communities, including water quality parameters such as dissolved solids concentration, stream discharge dynamics, and watershed-scale land use conditions that collectively influence fish survival and ecosystem health. This concept integrates the study of how terrestrial and aquatic factors interact within watersheds—such as land management practices, vegetation patterns, and human development—to determine the suitability of freshwater systems for sustaining viable fish communities and associated macroinvertebrate assemblages. Understanding freshwater fish habitat is essential for conservation efforts and fisheries management, as it requires assessing both in-stream conditions and upstream watershed characteristics that directly affect water quality and ecosystem integrity.
