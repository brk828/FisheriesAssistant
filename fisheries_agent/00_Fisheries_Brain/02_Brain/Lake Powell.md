# Lake Powell

## Summary
Lake Powell is a major reservoir in the Colorado River Basin that serves as a critical habitat for native and non-native fish species, making it central to conservation discussions regarding endangered fish populations and ecological management in the American Southwest. As a large impoundment with distinct ecological zones, Lake Powell and its associated tailwaters (particularly Lee's Ferry) represent a complex aquatic system where introduced forage fish species have been proposed or introduced to support declining predatory fish populations, though such introductions raise significant conservation concerns for native species. The reservoir's fish communities and ecological dynamics have been the subject of extensive interagency study and debate regarding habitat restoration, exotic species management, and the conservation of endemic species like humpback chub and other Colorado River endemics.
