# Endangered Species

## Summary
An endangered species, in the context of fish science and conservation biology, refers to a fish population or taxon that has been officially designated as facing a high risk of extinction in the wild due to factors such as habitat loss, pollution, overexploitation, or other anthropogenic or natural threats. The designation typically triggers legal protections and management requirements under legislation such as the Endangered Species Act, necessitating coordinated conservation efforts by government agencies and resource managers to prevent the species' extirpation or extinction. In aquatic ecology, endangered fish species serve as important indicators of ecosystem health and often represent critical targets for habitat restoration and recovery programs.
