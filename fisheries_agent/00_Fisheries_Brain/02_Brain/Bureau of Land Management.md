# Bureau Of Land Management

## Summary
The Bureau of Land Management (BLM) is a federal agency within the U.S. Department of the Interior responsible for managing vast tracts of public lands in the American West, including critical desert aquatic ecosystems that support endemic and endangered fish species. In the context of fish and aquatic conservation, the BLM plays a central role in developing management policies, conducting environmental assessments, and implementing conservation strategies for sensitive aquatic habitats such as springs, streams, and riparian zones, often in collaboration with other agencies like the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The BLM's authority encompasses designating protected waterways under the National Wild and Scenic Rivers System, monitoring fish populations, and establishing limits of acceptable change frameworks to balance resource protection with sustainable use of public lands.
