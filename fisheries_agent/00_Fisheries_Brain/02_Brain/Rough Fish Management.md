# Rough Fish Management

## Summary
Rough fish management refers to the deliberate control or removal of non-game fish species that are considered undesirable or economically less valuable, often to enhance conditions for sport fish populations or to reduce competition for resources in aquatic ecosystems. This management approach, which was particularly prevalent in mid-20th century fisheries practices, sometimes involved the use of chemical toxicants or mechanical removal methods to suppress rough fish populations and their ecological impacts. The concept reflects historical fisheries management priorities that valued certain fish species over others, though modern conservation perspectives have largely reconsidered the ecological roles that rough fish species play in maintaining healthy aquatic communities.
