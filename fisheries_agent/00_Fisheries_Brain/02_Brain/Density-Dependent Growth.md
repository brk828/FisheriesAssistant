# Density-Dependent Growth

## Summary
Density-dependent growth refers to the inverse relationship between population density and individual fish growth rates, where fish in populations at higher densities experience reduced growth rates due to increased intraspecific competition for limited resources such as food, space, or dissolved oxygen. This phenomenon is a key regulatory mechanism in fish populations that helps maintain population stability and can significantly influence fish size structure, reproductive potential, and overall population dynamics in both wild and managed aquatic systems. Understanding density-dependent growth is essential for effective fisheries management and conservation, as it affects yield predictions, stock assessment models, and the ecological outcomes of species introductions or population manipulations.
