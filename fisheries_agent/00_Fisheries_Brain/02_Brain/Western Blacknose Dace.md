# Western Blacknose Dace

## Summary
The Western Blacknose Dace (*Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris*) is a small, short-lived cyprinid fish native to eastern North American stream systems, notable for its ecological role as a common inhabitant of cool, spring-fed tributaries and its utility as a model organism for studying age, growth, and reproductive dynamics in temperate freshwater ecosystems. This species has been the subject of detailed ichthyological research examining its population demography, life history characteristics, and reproductive biology in order to better understand the population dynamics and ecological requirements of small stream-dwelling cyprinids. The Western Blacknose Dace serves as an important indicator species for assessing the health and integrity of small stream ecosystems in the Ohio River drainage and adjacent regions.
