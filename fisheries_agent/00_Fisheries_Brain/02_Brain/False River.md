# False River

## Summary
False River is a 3,212-acre oxbow lake in Louisiana that was cut off from the Mississippi River in 1722, with a maximum depth of 55-96 feet and stable water levels year-round. This study found that largemouth bass in False River grew more slowly than those in nearby Old River, with six-year-old bass reaching only about 14 inches compared to 17 inches in Old River, attributed to the lake's stable hydrology lacking nutrient pulses from flooding. The lake had a standing crop of 197.6 pounds per acre and exhibited summer thermal stratification with an anoxic hypolimnion.
