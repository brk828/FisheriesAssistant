# Desert Fishes Conservation

## Summary
Desert Fishes Conservation refers to the scientific and management efforts to protect and recover endemic fish species found in isolated desert aquatic systems, particularly in the southwestern United States where species like the woundfin, Virgin roundtail chub, and Virgin spinedace face extinction threats from habitat loss, water management practices, and invasive species introduction. This conservation approach integrates ichthyological research, habitat restoration, and regulatory frameworks such as the Endangered Species Act to preserve the unique evolutionary adaptations and biodiversity of these geographically isolated desert aquatic ecosystems. Desert fishes conservation represents a critical intersection of aquatic ecology, conservation biology, and natural resource management, addressing the vulnerability of species with extremely limited ranges and small population sizes.
