# Ennerdale Water

## Summary
Ennerdale Water is an oligotrophic (nutrient-poor) lake in the English Lake District characterized by low fertility, minimal lime and potash content, and well-oxygenated bottom waters year-round, making it a key study site for understanding how lake trophic status influences sediment geochemistry and the preservation of organic matter. In comparative limnological research, Ennerdale Water serves as a reference system for examining the relationships between lake productivity, redox conditions in bottom sediments, and the accumulation of chlorophyll degradation products and sulfur compounds in freshwater deposits. Its deep, oxygenated hypolimnion and predominantly allochthonous (terrestrial) organic matter inputs make it an important model for investigating how oxidation-reduction regimes affect carbon-sulfur ratios and the diagenesis of sedimentary organic matter in pristine freshwater ecosystems.
