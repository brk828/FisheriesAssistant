# Lake Washington

## Summary
Lake Washington is a freshwater ecosystem in the Pacific Northwest that has served as a critical study site for understanding predator-prey dynamics in salmonid populations, particularly regarding the impact of native predators like squawfish on juvenile salmon survival. The lake is notable for its well-documented case of high predation rates on sockeye salmon (estimated at 3 million fish consumed annually in the early 1970s), making it a key reference system for evaluating the ecological significance of predation versus other factors influencing salmon recruitment in freshwater environments. As a historically productive but increasingly nutrient-enriched water body, Lake Washington also exemplifies the eutrophication challenges facing temperate lakes and the cascading effects of nutrient loading on aquatic food webs and fish communities.
