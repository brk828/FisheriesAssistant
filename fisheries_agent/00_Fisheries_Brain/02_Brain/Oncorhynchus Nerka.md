# Oncorhynchus Nerka

## Summary
Oncorhynchus nerka, commonly known as sockeye salmon, is a Pacific salmon species notable for its life-history plasticity and ability to thrive in diverse freshwater environments, including landlocked lakes where anadromous populations can persist as resident forms. This species is ecologically significant as a key predator in lacustrine food webs, interacting with zooplankton communities in thermally stratified lakes and serving as an important indicator of ecosystem health and population dynamics in both marine and freshwater systems. Research on O. nerka populations has provided crucial insights into salmon biology, conservation strategies, and the effects of environmental factors such as thermal structure and wind-driven plankton distribution on fish habitat utilization and survival.
