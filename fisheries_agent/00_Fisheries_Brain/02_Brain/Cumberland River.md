# Cumberland River

## Summary
The Cumberland River is a major river system in the southeastern United States (primarily Tennessee) that has been significantly altered by federal water development projects, including dam construction that has substantially impacted native fish populations and their spawning and rearing habitats. In fisheries management and conservation contexts, the Cumberland River system serves as a key case study for mitigation stocking programs designed to offset the loss of natural riverine fish habitat caused by reservoir development and flow regulation. The river's ecological changes have made it an important focus for monitoring fish distribution, species range extensions, and evaluating the effectiveness of conservation strategies to maintain aquatic biodiversity in engineered riverine ecosystems.
