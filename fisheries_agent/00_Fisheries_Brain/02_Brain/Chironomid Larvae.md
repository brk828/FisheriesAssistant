# Chironomid Larvae

## Summary
Chironomid larvae are aquatic insect larvae of the family Chironomidae (non-biting midges) that represent a crucial benthic food resource in freshwater ecosystems, particularly for benthic-feeding fish species such as catfish and bluegill. These organisms are abundant in lake sediments and serve as a primary prey item for assessing fish digestion rates, daily food consumption, and trophic dynamics in aquatic food webs. Chironomid larvae are especially important in shallow, eutrophic lakes where they compose a significant portion of the benthic macroinvertebrate community and support the nutritional requirements of bottom-feeding fishes.
