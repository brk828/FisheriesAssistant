# Lake Huron

## Summary
Lake Huron, one of the North American Great Lakes, is a critical freshwater ecosystem that historically supported important commercial fisheries for species such as American smelt and lake trout, but has faced significant ecological challenges including the invasive sea lamprey that entered the system in the mid-20th century. As a large, interconnected freshwater system, Lake Huron serves as an important case study for understanding how invasive species and fishing pressure impact native fish populations and the dynamics of Great Lakes fish communities. The lake's tributaries and embayments, particularly around Manitoulin Island, have been focal points for both traditional fisheries management and conservation efforts aimed at maintaining native fish stocks in the face of ecological disruption.
