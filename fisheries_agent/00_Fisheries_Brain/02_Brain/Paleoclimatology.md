# Paleoclimatology

## Summary
Paleoclimatology in fish science refers to the study of ancient climate conditions and their effects on aquatic ecosystems, fish distributions, and evolutionary adaptations over geological timescales. By reconstructing past environmental conditions through various proxy records, paleoclimatologists can understand how historical climate shifts—such as droughts, temperature fluctuations, and changes in water availability—have shaped fish populations, caused range shifts, and influenced speciation events in both freshwater and marine systems. This knowledge is crucial for conservation biology, as it provides context for understanding how current fish species may respond to future climate change based on their evolutionary history and past habitat requirements.
