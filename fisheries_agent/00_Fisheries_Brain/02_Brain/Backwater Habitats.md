# Backwater Habitats

## Summary
Backwater habitats are low-velocity, off-channel water bodies adjacent to main river channels that serve as critical nursery and rearing areas for fish populations, particularly young or vulnerable life stages. These habitats, characterized by reduced water flow and often connected seasonally or permanently to the main channel, provide essential refuge from high current velocities, predation, and harsh environmental conditions while offering abundant food resources. In the context of endangered river species like Colorado squawfish in the Green River system, backwater habitats function as vital refugia that support early growth and development before juvenile fish transition to mainstem river environments.
