# Estuarine Fish Ecology

## Summary
Estuarine fish ecology is the study of fish populations, communities, and ecological processes in transitional environments where freshwater rivers meet marine waters, characterized by fluctuating salinity, temperature, and nutrient gradients. These dynamic systems serve as critical nurseries, feeding grounds, and migration corridors for numerous fish species, making their ecological understanding essential for fisheries management and conservation of both estuarine-dependent and anadromous/catadromous fishes. Research in this field examines how physiological adaptations, behavioral responses, and interspecific interactions allow fish to survive and thrive in these highly variable conditions while informing strategies for protecting estuarine habitats from anthropogenic disturbance.
