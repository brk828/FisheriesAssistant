# Desert Fish Conservation

## Summary
Desert Fish Conservation refers to the scientific and management efforts to protect and recover native fish species endemic to arid and semi-arid aquatic ecosystems, particularly in the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, where populations face acute threats from habitat destruction, water withdrawal, and environmental degradation. These conservation initiatives, often coordinated through multi-agency recovery teams and councils, focus on species inhabiting vulnerable desert river systems and isolated basins characterized by naturally variable flow regimes and high sensitivity to anthropogenic disturbance. The field emphasizes both the ecological significance of relict desert fish faunas and the urgent need for integrated management strategies to prevent extinction of highly endemic and often geographically restricted species.
