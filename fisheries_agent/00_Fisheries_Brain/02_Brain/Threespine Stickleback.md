# Threespine Stickleback

## Summary
The threespine stickleback (Gasterosteus aculeatus) is a small, euryhaline fish species found across the Northern Hemisphere that serves as an important model organism in evolutionary biology and ecology due to its rapid adaptive radiation in isolated freshwater systems and well-documented phenotypic plasticity. In the context of conservation and fisheries science, threespine sticklebacks are significant as both indicators of aquatic ecosystem health and as subjects of concern regarding invasive species introductions, as populations introduced outside their native ranges can outcompete native fishes and disrupt freshwater communities. The species' presence in fossil records and contemporary ecosystems makes it valuable for understanding long-term changes in fish faunas and the impacts of environmental disturbance on native aquatic biodiversity.
