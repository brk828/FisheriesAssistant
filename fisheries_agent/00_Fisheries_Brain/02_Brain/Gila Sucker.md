# Gila Sucker

## Summary
The Gila Sucker (*Catostomus insignis*) is a native cyprinid fish species endemic to the Gila River basin that has become a subject of conservation concern due to its unintended introduction into non-native habitats via human-engineered water conveyances, such as the Salt River Project canal system in 1987. As a sucker species dependent on riverine ecosystems, the Gila Sucker's ecological role and population status are relevant to broader recovery efforts for native Gila River fishes, particularly in understanding how introduced species interact with threatened endemic fish communities. Its presence in recovery team discussions reflects the complex interplay between aquatic ecosystem management, species introductions, and conservation planning in the arid Southwest.
