# Ammonia Toxicity

## Summary
Ammonia toxicity in fish refers to the harmful physiological effects caused by elevated concentrations of ammonia (NH₃) and ammonium (NH₄⁺) in aquatic environments, which can impair osmoregulation, oxygen transport, and neural function in fish species. The toxicity of ammonia is particularly influenced by water chemistry parameters such as pH and salinity, which alter the proportion of toxic un-ionized ammonia relative to the less harmful ionized ammonium form, thereby affecting the resistance and survival of sensitive species like rainbow trout across different habitat conditions. Understanding ammonia toxicity is critical for assessing water quality impacts on fish populations, managing aquaculture systems, and protecting anadromous species during their migrations through environments with varying salinities and pollution levels.
