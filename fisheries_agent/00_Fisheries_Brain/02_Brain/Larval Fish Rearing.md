# Larval Fish Rearing

## Summary
Larval fish rearing refers to the controlled cultivation and husbandry of fish larvae in laboratory or hatchery settings, typically employed to study growth rates, survival outcomes, and physiological responses under defined environmental conditions. This practice is fundamental to fisheries science and conservation biology, allowing researchers to investigate the critical early life stages of fish species, optimize breeding protocols, and inform management strategies for wild populations or aquaculture operations. Larval fish rearing experiments provide essential baseline data on species-specific requirements for temperature, nutrition, and water quality that inform both theoretical ecological understanding and practical applications in fish restoration and stock enhancement programs.
