# Gars

## Summary
Gars are a family of primitive freshwater and brackish water fishes (Lepisosteidae) characterized by elongated bodies, sharp teeth, and armor-like ganoid scales, representing an ancient lineage with significant ecological importance in North American aquatic systems. These ambush predators occupy important niches in freshwater and estuarine ecosystems, functioning as both predators and prey for larger species, and have become subjects of interest in fisheries management and conservation due to their ecological roles and interactions with introduced species. Their presence across multiple research contexts reflects their relevance to understanding native fish communities, ecosystem dynamics, and the impacts of non-native species introductions in aquatic environments.
