# Grand Coulee Dam

## Summary
The Grand Coulee Dam is a massive hydroelectric structure constructed on the mainstem Columbia River that completely blocked upstream migration of anadromous salmon and steelhead populations, eliminating access to extensive historical spawning habitat in the upper Columbia Basin. In response to this catastrophic habitat loss, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service established multiple mitigation hatcheries in 1938 under the Mitchell Act, including the Leavenworth and Entiat National Fish Hatcheries, to artificially propagate and release salmon and steelhead populations to compensate for natural production lost due to the dam's construction. The Grand Coulee Dam therefore represents a seminal example in conservation biology of how major infrastructure development necessitates large-scale, long-term mitigation strategies to sustain fisheries resources in altered aquatic ecosystems.
