# Growth Studies

## Summary
Growth studies are fishery research applications that rely on accurate length measurements of fish over time. This study was prompted by concerns that sedation might introduce bias into growth studies after DeRoche and Bond (1955) noted that recaptured tagged fish appeared shorter than when originally measured while drugged, though this study found no significant difference between lengths of drugged versus normal fish.

## Context

> This 1958 study by Wilbur L. Hartman investigated whether sedating fish with [[Urethane]] reduced measurement error and improved accuracy when recording fork and total lengths.
