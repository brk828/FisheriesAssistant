# Invertebrate Drift

## Summary
Invertebrate drift refers to the downstream movement of aquatic invertebrates (primarily insect larvae) through the water column in streams and rivers, which serves as both a natural colonization mechanism for repopulating benthic habitats and a crucial food source for drift-feeding fish such as trout. This phenomenon exhibits temporal patterns, with invertebrates drifting at different rates depending on environmental factors like temperature and time of day, making it a key ecological process linking prey availability to fish foraging behavior and population dynamics in lotic ecosystems.
