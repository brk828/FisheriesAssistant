# Genetic Drift

## Summary
Genetic drift in fish science refers to the random changes in allele frequencies that occur in small, isolated populations over time, particularly in hatchery-reared or captive fish populations. This phenomenon is a significant conservation concern because small founding populations of endangered fish species are especially vulnerable to unpredictable genetic changes that can reduce population fitness and adaptive potential, independent of natural selection. In hatchery management contexts, genetic drift represents a critical risk factor that can erode the genetic integrity of threatened and endangered fish stocks if populations are not carefully managed through adequate breeding numbers and strategic reproductive practices.
