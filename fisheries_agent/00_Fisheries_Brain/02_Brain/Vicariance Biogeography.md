# Vicariance Biogeography

## Summary
Vicariance biogeography in fish science refers to the process by which geographic barriers (such as changes in water availability, landscape fragmentation, or paleohydrological shifts) physically isolate populations of ancestral fish species, leading to allopatric speciation and the formation of sister species in different regions. This concept is fundamental to understanding fish biodiversity patterns, particularly in isolated aquatic systems like desert basins and inland seas, and has major conservation implications for reserve design and management of geographically separated fish populations that have diverged through geographic isolation rather than dispersal.
