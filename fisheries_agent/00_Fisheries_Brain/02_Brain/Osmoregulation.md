# Osmoregulation

## Summary
Osmoregulation refers to the physiological processes by which fish maintain homeostasis of their internal osmotic environment and ion concentrations despite changes in external salinity and water conditions. This includes active and passive mechanisms for regulating water uptake or loss and sodium/ion transport across gill, kidney, and intestinal tissues, allowing fish to survive in environments ranging from freshwater to marine to brackish waters. Understanding osmoregulation is critical for fisheries management, aquaculture practices, and conservation efforts, particularly as fish populations face changing environmental salinities due to habitat alterations and climate variability.
