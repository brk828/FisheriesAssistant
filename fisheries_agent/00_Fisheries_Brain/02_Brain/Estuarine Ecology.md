# Estuarine Ecology

## Summary
Estuarine ecology is the study of the physical, chemical, and biological processes occurring in transitional zones where freshwater rivers meet and mix with marine environments, with particular emphasis on understanding how fish and other organisms utilize these dynamic habitats during different life stages and seasonal migrations. In fisheries science, estuarine ecology examines the crucial role these productive ecosystems play as nurseries, feeding grounds, and migration corridors for economically and ecologically important fish species, requiring quantitative and comprehensive approaches to characterize growth patterns, species distributions, and environmental conditions across spatial and temporal scales. This field bridges freshwater and marine biology, recognizing that estuaries represent distinct ecological systems where fluctuating salinity, nutrient inputs, and habitat complexity fundamentally shape fish community structure and population dynamics.
