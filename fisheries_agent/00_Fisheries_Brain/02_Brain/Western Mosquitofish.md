# Western Mosquitofish

## Summary
The Western Mosquitofish (Gambusia affinis) is a small, highly adaptable livebearing fish native to the southeastern United States that has become one of the most widespread and problematic invasive species in southwestern aquatic ecosystems, particularly in Arizona's desert streams and springs. In conservation biology and aquatic ecology contexts, this species is documented as a significant threat to native fishes, especially federally endangered species like the Gila Topminnow, through predation on larvae and juveniles, aggressive competition for resources, and ecological displacement. Its presence in isolated desert water systems represents a major management challenge for fisheries scientists and conservation biologists working to protect endemic species in fragmented southwestern watersheds.
