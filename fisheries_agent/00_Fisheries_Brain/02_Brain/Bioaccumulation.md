# Bioaccumulation

## Summary
Bioaccumulation in aquatic systems refers to the process by which toxicants and persistent compounds accumulate in the tissues of aquatic organisms, particularly fish and invertebrates, through exposure to contaminated water and sediments over time. This phenomenon is especially significant in fisheries science because organisms at higher trophic levels can accumulate higher concentrations of toxicants than their environment, posing risks to both fish populations and human consumers. Understanding bioaccumulation is critical for assessing the long-term effects of pesticides, heavy metals, and other pollutants on fish health and for informing conservation strategies in contaminated aquatic ecosystems.
