# Clean Water Act

## Summary
The Clean Water Act, particularly Section 404, is a federal statute that regulates the discharge of pollutants and alterations to waters of the United States, including those that affect aquatic ecosystems and fish habitat. In the context of fish conservation and aquatic ecology, the Clean Water Act serves as a critical regulatory mechanism for evaluating and permitting projects like reservoir construction that could impact water quality, flow regimes, and fish populations, often requiring coordination with environmental agencies to protect native fish species and their habitats. Combined with complementary legislation like the Endangered Species Act and Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act, Section 404 provides a legal framework for ensuring that development activities do not irreparably harm aquatic resources and the fish communities that depend on them.
