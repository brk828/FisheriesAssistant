# Endangered Species Conservation

## Summary
Endangered Species Conservation in fish science refers to the integrated management strategies employed to prevent the extinction of threatened aquatic species through multiple complementary approaches, including both in situ (wild habitat) and ex situ (captive breeding and refugial) population maintenance. This conservation philosophy emphasizes maintaining genetically viable, geographically dispersed populations across natural and institutional settings to create redundancy against catastrophic loss from disease, environmental degradation, or localized extirpation events. By combining habitat protection, captive propagation, and strategic reintroduction efforts, endangered fish conservation aims to sustain species viability while addressing the underlying ecological and anthropogenic threats that led to their decline.
