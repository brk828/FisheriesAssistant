# Sierra Madre Oriental

## Summary
The Sierra Madre Oriental is a major mountain range in northeastern Mexico that creates distinct aquatic habitats through its orographic effects and elevation gradients, with intermontane valleys on its eastern edge hosting isolated desert basins characterized by extreme aridity, limited water resources, and unique endemic fish communities adapted to harsh environmental conditions. These high-altitude, water-scarce systems are of particular conservation significance because their geographic isolation and environmental extremes have fostered specialized aquatic fauna with restricted ranges and high vulnerability to human-induced changes in hydrology and water availability. The region's ichthyofauna and associated aquatic ecosystems represent important subjects for conservation biology research given the threats posed by climate variability, groundwater extraction, and habitat degradation in these already fragile desert aquatic systems.
