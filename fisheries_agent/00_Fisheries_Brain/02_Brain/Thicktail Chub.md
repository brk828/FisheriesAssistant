# Thicktail Chub

## Summary
The Thicktail Chub (*Gila crassicauda*) is a now-presumed-extinct California minnow native to the San Francisco Bay region and Central Valley drainages that was formally redescribed by Robert Rush Miller in 1963 based on museum specimens. This cyprinid species experienced severe range contraction and habitat loss due to water diversion, dam construction, and introduced species competition, becoming one of North America's extinct fishes alongside other native California species like the Harelip Sucker. The Thicktail Chub serves as a significant case study in ichthyology and conservation biology, illustrating the broader decline of native fish fauna in California's aquatic ecosystems during the 20th century.
