# Outbreeding Depression

## Summary
Outbreeding depression in fish science refers to the reduction in fitness and survival of offspring that results from crossing genetically divergent populations or stocks of the same species, particularly when individuals from one stock are transferred into waters inhabited by another genetically distinct stock. This phenomenon occurs because populations adapted to specific environmental conditions over time accumulate locally beneficial genetic combinations that become disrupted when stocks interbreed, leading to the loss of important adaptive traits and potentially compromised viability of hybrid offspring. Outbreeding depression is a critical conservation concern in fish management, as stock transfers intended to bolster populations or restore genetic diversity can paradoxically harm native populations by introducing maladaptive genetic material and disrupting locally evolved genetic structures.
