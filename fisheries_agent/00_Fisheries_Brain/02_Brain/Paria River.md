# Paria River

## Summary
The Paria River is a tributary of the Colorado River in Arizona that serves as a critical study site for monitoring native fish assemblages and ecosystem health in the Grand Canyon region. As a free-flowing desert river system within the Colorado River basin, the Paria River and its lower reaches (particularly the lower 10 km) have been extensively surveyed to assess populations of native species such as longfin dace, speckled dace, and desert sucker, which represent core native fish communities historically distributed throughout the Sonoran Desert. The river's ecological significance relates to both its qualification under the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act for fish and wildlife values and its importance as a sensitive indicator of broader patterns in native fish distribution and conservation status throughout the arid southwestern aquatic ecosystems.
