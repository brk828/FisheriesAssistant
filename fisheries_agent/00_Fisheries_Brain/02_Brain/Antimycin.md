# Antimycin

## Summary
Antimycin is a potent piscicide (fish toxicant) used in fisheries management and conservation biology to selectively remove non-native or undesirable fish populations from aquatic ecosystems, particularly in isolated habitats where eradication is necessary for native species restoration. As a natural antibiotic compound that inhibits cellular respiration in fish, antimycin causes mortality while typically breaking down relatively quickly in the environment, making it useful for renovation of small streams and constructed wetlands in sensitive areas such as desert canyon systems. Its application in programs like those proposed for Fossil Creek represents a management strategy to eliminate introduced species barriers and restore native aquatic communities, though such treatments remain controversial among stakeholders concerned with broader ecological impacts.
