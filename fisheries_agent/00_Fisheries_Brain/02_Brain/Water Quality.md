# Water Quality

## Summary
Water quality refers to the physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of aquatic environments that directly influence the health, survival, and reproductive success of fish populations and other aquatic organisms. In the context of fish science and conservation, water quality encompasses parameters such as temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, nutrient levels, turbidity, and contaminant concentrations, all of which must remain within species-specific tolerance ranges to support viable fish communities. Effective watershed management and monitoring of water quality across jurisdictional boundaries—including public lands, municipal areas, and private holdings—is essential for protecting endangered and threatened fish species and maintaining sustainable fisheries, particularly in regions like the Safford, Arizona watershed where multiple land management agencies must coordinate conservation efforts.
