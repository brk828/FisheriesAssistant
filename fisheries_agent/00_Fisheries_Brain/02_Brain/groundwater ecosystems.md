# Groundwater Ecosystems

## Summary
Groundwater ecosystems are subsurface aquatic environments including phreatic (saturated zone) and hyporheic (interstitial) habitats that support specialized communities of organisms adapted to low-light, low-nutrient conditions. These systems are characterized by unique assemblages of stygobiotic fauna—such as blind copepods and amphipods—that have evolved distinctive morphological and physiological adaptations including loss of pigmentation and vision. In fish science and conservation biology, groundwater ecosystems represent important but often understudied biodiversity reservoirs where speciation patterns are shaped by geological barriers and hydrogeological connectivity, making them critical for understanding subterranean aquatic evolution and endemism.
