# Gambusia Holbrooki

## Summary
Gambusia holbrooki, commonly known as the eastern mosquitofish, is a small livebearing fish species native to the southeastern United States that has become widely distributed globally, often introduced for mosquito control purposes. This species exhibits distinctive morphological characteristics including a small body size at hatching (approximately 4.7 mm), post-flexion larval development, and moderate pigmentation, along with a reproductive strategy involving seasonal breeding cycles that typically peak in spring through fall months. In conservation and ecological contexts, G. holbrooki is notable both as a predatory species that can impact native fish populations and as a subject of reproductive and behavioral studies relevant to understanding invasive fish dynamics in aquatic ecosystems.
