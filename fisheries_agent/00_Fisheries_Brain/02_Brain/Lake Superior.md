# Lake Superior

## Summary
Lake Superior is the largest of the Great Lakes and serves as a critical freshwater ecosystem for fish conservation and management, particularly for native species restoration and invasive species control programs. In the context of fisheries science, Lake Superior is a major focus area for hatchery operations like the Hiawatha Forest National Fish Hatchery, where fish are propagated and directly stocked into the lake to support population recovery efforts. The lake's ecological significance extends to addressing invasive species threats, such as sea lampreys that have entered through human-modified waterways like the Welland Canal, making it a central concern for aquatic conservation biology and Great Lakes management.
