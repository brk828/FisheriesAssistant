# Colorado

## Summary
The Colorado River, in the context of fish science and conservation, refers to a major North American river system whose hydrology and ecology have been fundamentally altered by dam construction, most notably Hoover Dam, which created coldwater tailwater habitats suitable for introduced salmonids like rainbow trout rather than supporting the native warmwater cyprinid communities that historically dominated the system. This anthropogenic transformation has driven significant changes in fisheries management and conservation priorities, including the establishment of hatcheries such as Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery (founded 1962) specifically to produce coldwater fish species for recreational fisheries in the altered tailwaters. The Colorado River exemplifies the complex management challenges of balancing competing interests in aquatic ecosystem restoration, endangered species protection, and consumptive recreational use within federally managed waters.
