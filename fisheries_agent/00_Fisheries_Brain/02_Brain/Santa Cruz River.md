# Santa Cruz River

## Summary
The Santa Cruz River is a critical aquatic system within the Gila River basin of Arizona and Sonora that historically supported endemic desert fish species, particularly in its perennial spring-fed reaches and ciénega wetland habitats. As a drainage exhibiting complex hydrological patterns ranging from perennial effluent-dependent segments to intermittent seasonal flows, the Santa Cruz River system serves as a key reference for understanding native fish population genetics, range distributions, and conservation challenges related to non-native species introductions and habitat fragmentation. Its ecological significance in fish recovery programs stems from its role in maintaining the genetic integrity and historical range of specialized desert cyprinodont populations and other endemic species dependent on these localized aquatic refugia.
