# Cyprinodontiformes

## Summary
Cyprinodontiformes is an order of small, primarily freshwater and brackish-water fishes that includes families such as Cyprinodontidae (killifishes and pupfishes), characterized by their adaptability to extreme and isolated aquatic environments including hot springs, desert pools, and other ephemeral or geographically restricted habitats. These fishes are particularly significant for conservation biology and evolutionary studies due to their tendency to exhibit rapid speciation and adaptive radiation in isolated spring systems, making them valuable models for understanding how environmental pressures and geographic isolation drive evolutionary change. Many cyprinodontiform species, such as endemic pupfishes in desert ecosystems, face severe conservation challenges due to their restricted ranges and dependence on specific thermal and chemical conditions, making them indicators of broader aquatic ecosystem health and climate change impacts.
