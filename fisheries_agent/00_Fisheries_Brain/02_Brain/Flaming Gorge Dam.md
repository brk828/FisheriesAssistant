# Flaming Gorge Dam

## Summary
Flaming Gorge Dam is a major hydroelectric structure on the Green River in Utah whose construction and operation fundamentally altered downstream aquatic ecosystems, creating tailwater habitats that have been the focus of intensive fish monitoring and management research since the 1960s. The dam's regulation of water temperature, flow regimes, and habitat structure has profoundly affected native fish populations, particularly Colorado pikeminnow and humpback chub, requiring long-term electrofishing surveys and habitat studies to assess population dynamics and recruitment success in the modified Green River environment. Flaming Gorge therefore represents a critical case study for understanding how large dams reshape fish communities and the conservation challenges associated with managing native species in post-impoundment aquatic ecosystems.
