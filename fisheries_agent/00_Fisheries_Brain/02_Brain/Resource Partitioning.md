# Resource Partitioning

## Summary
Resource partitioning in fish science refers to the ecological process by which different fish species coexist in the same habitat by utilizing distinct or complementary resources such as food types, feeding zones, or temporal feeding periods, thereby reducing direct competition. This mechanism is particularly important in stream ecosystems where multiple species share limited space and resources, allowing for greater biodiversity and community stability. Understanding resource partitioning is essential for fisheries management and conservation, as it helps explain how fish assemblages maintain diversity and how disturbances to resource availability can affect community structure.
