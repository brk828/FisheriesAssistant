# Fall Chinook Salmon

## Summary
Fall Chinook Salmon are a specific run or population of Chinook salmon (Oncorhynchus tshawytscha) that migrate upstream and spawn in late summer and fall, typically in the Columbia River basin and other Pacific Northwest river systems. This ecologically and economically important species has been a primary focus of hatchery mitigation and restoration efforts by U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facilities since the mid-20th century, as dam construction and habitat loss have severely depleted wild populations. Fall Chinook Salmon are distinguished from spring-run populations by their spawning timing and have been central to salmon recovery programs aimed at maintaining viable runs in damaged river systems.
