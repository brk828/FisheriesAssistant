# Winterkill

## Summary
Winterkill refers to the die-off of fish populations that occurs during winter, typically in shallow or ice-covered lakes and ponds where oxygen depletion becomes critical due to reduced light penetration and photosynthetic activity under ice. This phenomenon is particularly significant in fisheries management and conservation biology, as winterkill can dramatically reduce or eliminate fish populations in affected water bodies, making it an important consideration when evaluating stocking programs, assessing fish habitat quality, and managing put-and-take fisheries. Understanding winterkill dynamics is essential for predicting survival rates of stocked fingerlings and natural populations in temperate aquatic ecosystems.
