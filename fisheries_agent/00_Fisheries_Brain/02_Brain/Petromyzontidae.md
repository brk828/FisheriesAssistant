# Petromyzontidae

## Summary
Petromyzontidae is a family of jawless fish commonly known as lampreys, characterized by their eel-like bodies, circular sucking mouths lined with keratin teeth, and parasitic feeding behavior on other fish species. As agnathans (jawless fishes), they represent an ancient lineage distinct from jawed fishes and occupy important ecological roles as both predators and prey in freshwater and marine ecosystems, with some species exhibiting anadromous life histories. In conservation and fisheries contexts, petromyzontids are significant for understanding parasite-host dynamics, ecosystem health indicators, and as subjects of both pest management and research into primitive vertebrate physiology.
