# Cross 1967

## Summary
Cross 1967 refers to a foundational study characterizing the ecological and physiological tolerances of fish species inhabiting thermally variable aquatic environments, particularly small prairie streams subject to extreme temperature fluctuations. This work appears to have documented the thermal regime tolerance and habitat requirements of prairie stream fishes, establishing baseline information about how certain species adapt to or persist in highly variable thermal conditions. The citation is commonly referenced in ichthyological literature examining fish habitat preferences, thermal ecology, and the relationship between environmental extremes and species distribution patterns.
