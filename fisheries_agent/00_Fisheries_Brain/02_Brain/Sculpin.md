# Sculpin

## Summary
Sculpins are small benthic fishes found in freshwater and marine environments that occupy important ecological niches as opportunistic predators and prey species in aquatic food webs. Characterized by their bottom-dwelling habits, broad heads, and lack of swim bladders, sculpins feed on invertebrates and small fish with rapid, temperature-dependent digestion rates, making them significant indicators of stream and lake health in conservation assessments. As both predators of invertebrate communities and forage fish for larger game species, sculpins play a crucial intermediary role in aquatic ecosystems and are often monitored in fisheries surveys to evaluate habitat quality and ecosystem integrity.
