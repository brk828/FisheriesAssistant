# U.S. National Museum

## Summary
The U.S. National Museum served as a major institutional repository for fish specimens and scientific collections in North America, housing extensive ichthyological materials including specimens collected over decades that were often left unidentified or unprocessed. As a research institution with ichthyologist staff members, it functioned as a critical center for taxonomic work, species description, and the documentation of fish distributions across North American freshwater and marine ecosystems. The museum's collections and associated expertise were instrumental in advancing knowledge of fish biodiversity, enabling the formal description of new species and the clarification of distributional records that might otherwise have remained scientifically unknown.
