# Fish Barrier Design

## Summary
Fish Barrier Design refers to the engineering and ecological approach of constructing physical or behavioral barriers in aquatic systems to control fish movement, prevent unwanted species dispersal, and protect native fish populations from non-native species competition or predation. This design strategy is particularly critical in conservation efforts for endangered species and native fish populations, where barriers can be strategically implemented to isolate vulnerable populations, manage habitat connectivity, and support recovery programs in compliance with regulations like the Endangered Species Act. Effective barrier design must balance ecological objectives with considerations of fish swimming ability, species-specific behaviors, and hydrological conditions to achieve conservation goals while minimizing unintended ecological consequences.
