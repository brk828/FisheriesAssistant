# Ephemeroptera

## Summary
Ephemeroptera, commonly known as mayflies, is an order of aquatic insects whose nymphal stages serve as critical bioindicators of freshwater ecosystem health and constitute a major food source for fish populations in streams and lakes. The order is characterized by a unique life history including an aquatic nymphal stage, a brief winged subimago stage, and a short-lived adult imago stage, with the aquatic nymphs being particularly important in fish diet studies and benthic community assessments. In fisheries science and aquatic ecology, Ephemeroptera abundance, diversity, and community composition are widely used to evaluate water quality, assess habitat conditions, and understand predator-prey dynamics in lotic and lentic environments.
