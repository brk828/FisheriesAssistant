# Atlantic Salmon

## Summary
Atlantic Salmon (*Salmo salar*) is a diadromous fish species native to the North Atlantic that serves as a key indicator of ecosystem health and a focal species for conservation efforts in North American river systems, particularly in the Merrimack River basin and other northeastern watersheds. Characterized by their anadromous life history—spawning in freshwater rivers and maturing in ocean environments—Atlantic Salmon populations have declined significantly due to habitat loss, dam construction, and other anthropogenic stressors, necessitating extensive restoration programs through federal hatcheries that produce and stock juvenile fish to replenish wild populations. The species' sensitivity to environmental stressors including temperature, salinity changes, and water quality contaminants makes it valuable for research on fish physiology and toxicology while simultaneously underscoring the challenges in restoring self-sustaining wild populations.
