# Four New Species of the Fish Genus Ecsenius with Notes on Other Species of the Genus (Blenniidae: Salariini)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #blenniidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic monograph by [[James F. McKinney]] and [[Victor G. Springer]] (Smithsonian Institution, National Museum of Natural History) describes four new species of the blenny genus [[Ecsenius]]: *E. isos* (New Hebrides), *E. melarchus* (Java Sea, Celebes, Borneo), *E. pictus* (Moluccas and Solomon Islands) and *E. schroederi* (Moluccas). It adds locality records and geographic and individual morphological variation for 13 previously described species and provides a key to all 25 known Ecsenius species. The authors also conclude that *E. oculus* comprises six allopatric color-pattern types, and that a specimen previously attributed to the Banda Sea was most likely collected at Samoa.

## Background & Description

[[Ecsenius]] is probably the most speciose genus of blenniids, and the authors consider that additional undescribed species remain, particularly in under-collected areas such as the Philippines. Diagnostic characters used throughout include dorsal-fin spine and segmented-ray counts, pectoral and caudal-fin rays, procurrent caudal-fin rays, gill rakers, pseudobranchial filaments, dentary incisor and posterior canine teeth, vertebral counts, epipleural ribs, lateral-line extent, dorsal-fin notch depth, and nasal cirri. Measurements are given as mm standard length (SL). Specimens are housed mainly in the [[National Museum of Natural History]] (NMNH), with material also held by the [[Bernice P. Bishop Museum]], the [[Australian Museum]], the [[California Academy of Sciences]] and others.

Species are arranged in subgroups within the *yaeyamaensis* species group, including the *prooculis* subgroup (*E. bandanus*, *E. bimaculatus*, *E. collettei*, *E. isos*, *E. prooculis*), the *oculus* subgroup, the *stigmatura* group (*E. melarchus*, *E. stigmatura*, *E. lividinalis*), and the newly proposed *pictus* subgroup.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Species of Ecsenius live on coral reefs. *E. melarchus* occurs from 1.5 m to 30.5 m; *E. pictus* from 1 to 38 m, mostly below 10 m; and *E. midas* was recorded at 55 m, the deepest record for the genus. *E. oculus* inhabits the shallower ends of surge channels ("spur and groove" areas), whereas [[Ecsenius opsifrontalis]] favors vertical fore-reef walls and small barren patches. The two species co-occur only at Fiji, where their habitat preferences differ.

Biogeographic context follows [[Thorne (1963)]]: the Javan and Celebesian districts of the Indo-Malayan subregion, the Moluccan province of the Papuan subregion, and the New Hebridean district of the Fijian province of the Polynesian subregion. Distributions are often allopatric, and the [[Andesite Line]] separates *E. oculus* collections (west) from most *E. opsifrontalis* collections (east). Clinal variation in meristic counts with latitude is described for *E. oculus*.

## Key Findings

- **Four new species described:**
  - *E. isos*: holotype BPBM 12122 (male, 26.2 mm SL, Efate, New Hebrides). It has dark ventral head spots in both sexes, a feature unique within the *prooculis* subgroup. It was previously confused with *E. yaeyamaensis* and *E. trilineatus*.
  - *E. melarchus*: holotype USNM 212229 (male, 39.0 mm SL, Karimundjawa, Java Sea). Diagnosed by a black mark extending from the anus and the absence of the caudal-peduncle spot of *E. stigmatura*.
  - *E. pictus*: holotype USNM 213853 (female, 33.3 mm SL, Moluccas). It has pale pinstripes on a dark body and a pale pectoral-fin base stripe, leading to the proposal of a fourth subgroup within the *yaeyamaensis* group.
  - *E. schroederi*: holotype USNM 209743 (male, 24.0 mm SL, Ambon Island). It shares with *E. mandibularis* the trait of more than two posterior canine teeth per side of the lower jaw, and is named for illustrator Jack R. Schroeder.
- **Redescription of *E. bandanus*:** Based on 124 specimens from Indonesia, the species was redescribed. Males have longer caudal rays (22.3–43.8% SL) than females (20.1–25.6%). Males reach 33.5 mm SL and females 29.2 mm SL. Ventral head spots show sexual dichromatism.
- **Distribution and identity corrections:** *E. bandanus* and *E. trilineatus* are confirmed from the Banda Sea, supporting earlier hypotheses. A *E. oculus* specimen series (USNM 195716) previously attributed to the Banda Sea is reassigned to Samoa based on color pattern type F and meristics.
- **Colour variation in *E. oculus*:** 146 specimens were examined. Six color-pattern types (A–F) were recognized, all occurring allopatrically. The authors decline to name them formally, but note that color pattern varies independently of meristic clines.
- **Range extensions:** *E. lineatus* records now extend to Ceylon, Mauritius, Réunion and Western Australia, and to northern Japan according to [[Masuda et al. (1975)]]. *E. midas* has a new deep-water record at 55 m (Maré). *E. nalolo* and *E. yaeyamaensis* are separated at about 75°E longitude and by pectoral-fin base markings (single stripe versus Y-shaped mark).
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Several species show sexual dichromatism in ventral head spots (the *prooculis* subgroup) and male-biased elongation of caudal rays (*E. bandanus*, *E. melarchus*). Significant sex differences in dentary tooth counts were found in *E. bandanus* and *E. namiyei*.
- **Geographic variation:** *E. namiyei* from Taiwan has higher segmented-ray, caudal-vertebra and dentary-tooth counts, and all Taiwanese specimens have lateral-line pores, whereas most other populations lack them. *E. pictus* from the Moluccas has lower counts than Solomon Islands specimens.
- **Identification key:** A dichotomous key to all 25 Ecsenius species was provided. Key characters include dentary teeth, posterior canine teeth, dorsal-fin spine and ray counts, lateral-line extent, pectoral-fin base markings and nasal cirri.
- **Future work:** The authors predict further undescribed species, especially from the Philippines, and recommend collecting around the southern coast of India to test the validity of the *E. nalolo* and *E. yaeyamaensis* distinction.
