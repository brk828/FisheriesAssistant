## Executive Summary

This 1951 bulletin from the Kentucky Division of Game and Fish presents growth rate and population composition data for fishes sampled across six Kentucky water bodies: [[Herrington Lake]], [[Slate Creek]], [[Elkhorn Creek]], [[Kentucky Lake]], [[Beaver Creek]], and [[Dale Hollow Lake]]. The work compiles scale-based age and growth data collected by field technicians as part of routine fisheries management surveys rather than as a dedicated academic age-and-growth study. Calculated total lengths at time of annulus formation are reported for key sport and forage species, primarily within the family [[Centrarchidae]] (basses, bluegill, crappie, rock bass), alongside standing crop and species composition data (game fish vs. pan fish vs. rough fish, by weight percentage) for each system. The report emphasizes regional variation in productivity driven by underlying geology (limestone/bluegrass soils), reservoir depth, and impoundment age.

## Background & Description

The paper does not describe external morphology in detail, but reports **calculated total length (in inches) at each annulus** derived from scale readings — a standard back-calculation method for age and growth analysis. Key growth-relevant data points include:

- **Scale-based aging**: All scale samples were read or re-read by Carter to standardize interpretation across sampling sites, minimizing inter-reader variability.
- **Weighted arithmetic means** were used to calculate average lengths per age class, appropriately weighting for sample size differences across stations.
- **[[Kentucky Bass]]** (*Micropterus punctulatus*) and **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus dolomieu*) were tracked separately from bluegill and crappie growth curves in the annulus-length tables, indicating multi-year age-class data were available at Herrington Lake, Elkhorn Creek, and Kentucky Lake.
- **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*) growth data were combined across multiple lakes to generate pooled "combined average" length-at-age tables, suggesting an attempt to establish regional growth standards despite variable local productivity.
- Tables are heavily degraded in the OCR source; precise numeric length-at-age values could not be reliably reconstructed for most age groups.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Each water body represents a distinct freshwater habitat type with corresponding differences in fish community structure:

### [[Herrington Lake]]
- Impounded 1925 for hydroelectric power; average depth 73 ft; surface area 2,940 acres.
- Stable productivity over ~25 years with no observed decline.
- Principal forage fish: **[[Gizzard Shad]]** (*Dorosoma cepedianum*).
- 1950 composition by weight: Game fish 23.5%, pan fish 13.0%, rough fish 63.5%.

### [[Slate Creek]]
- East-central Kentucky, edge of the Bluegrass Region; limestone-derived soils yield high productivity (181 lbs fish/acre average).
- Slow-moving, deep channel with predominantly silt substrate; some rocky stretches favor game fish.
- Sampled at 4 stations in 1950; composition: Game fish (Kentucky and Smallmouth bass) 12.93%, pan fish 20.00%, rough fish 66.99%.

### [[Elkhorn Creek]]
- Located in the heart of the Bluegrass Region; ~35 miles long, headwaters near Lexington, mouth north of Frankfort.
- Clear, fast-flowing stream with rubble/gravel substrate — noted as one of Kentucky's premier smallmouth bass streams; wadable nearly its entire length.
- Principal game fish: [[Smallmouth Bass]]; [[Rock Bass]] provide significant pan-fishing value.
- Secondary species present: [[Channel Catfish]], [[Longear Sunfish]], and crappie.

### [[Kentucky Lake]]
- 260,000-acre impoundment of the Tennessee River, 17 miles above its mouth; pool extends 184 miles through western Kentucky/Tennessee.
- Average depth only 23 ft — shallow relative to size, favoring high rough fish biomass.
- Eradication survey of 4 bays (12 surface acres) found standing crop of 249 lbs/acre: 80.6% rough fish (65.5% gizzard shad alone), 19.4% game and pan fish combined.

### [[Beaver Creek]]
- Small stream entirely within Wayne County, south-central Kentucky; part of pre-impoundment study for Wolfe Creek Reservoir on the [[Cumberland River]].
- Composition: Game fish 12.3%, pan fish 11.9%, rough fish 75.8%.

### [[Dale Hollow Lake]]
- Formed by impoundment of the [[Obey River]]; ~30,000 acres spanning south-central Kentucky and north-central Tennessee.
- Average depth 55 ft; providing strong bass and bluegill fishing since 1943.
- Based on 1,300 lbs of fish recovered from poisoning 8 coves (10.6 acres): Game fish 11.5%, pan fish 23.9%, rough fish 64.6%.

## Key Findings

- **Rough fish dominance across systems**: In every water body surveyed, rough fish constituted the majority or a very large plurality of standing fish biomass (ranging from ~63.5% in Herrington Lake to 80.6% in Kentucky Lake), underscoring a management challenge common to Kentucky's mid-20th-century reservoirs and streams.
- **Geology strongly shapes productivity**: Limestone-derived soils in the Bluegrass Region (Slate Creek, Elkhorn Creek) correlate with elevated fish biomass per acre (e.g., 181 lbs/acre in Slate Creek) compared to other systems.
- **Reservoir depth influences community composition**: Shallower reservoirs (Kentucky Lake, 23 ft average depth) showed substantially higher rough-fish and forage-fish (gizzard shad) dominance than deeper reservoirs (Herrington Lake, 73 ft; Dale Hollow, 55 ft).
- **Long-term reservoir stability**: Herrington Lake, one of Kentucky's oldest impoundments (1925), showed no measurable decline in productivity over 25 years according to both scientific observation and local angler/operator reports — notable for early reservoir fisheries management.
- **Standardized aging methodology**: Centralizing all scale reading/re-reading through a single analyst (Carter) represents an early effort at methodological consistency in Kentucky state fisheries science, despite the study's non-academic, management-driven origin.
- **Species-specific management value**: [[Smallmouth Bass]] and [[Rock Bass]] are highlighted as flagship species of high-quality stream habitats (Elkhorn Creek), while [[Smallmouth Bass|Smallmouth]]/[[Kentucky Bass]] dominate reservoir sport fisheries.
- **Data limitations**: The underlying numerical growth tables (calculated length at annulus) are significantly degraded by OCR/scan quality, limiting quantitative reuse of this source without access to the original bulletin figures.
