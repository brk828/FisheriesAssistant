# Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center: Establishment, Organization and 1997 Peer Review

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fish

## Executive Summary

The document is a set of U.S. Department of the Interior memoranda and peer review records concerning the [[Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center]] (GCMRC). The Center was created under the [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] of 1992 to run long-term monitoring and research on how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affect the canyon's natural and cultural resources. The records cover (1) the Center's governance and funding structure, (2) the retention of its organizational location in 1997, and (3) the September 12–14, 1997 peer review of roughly 41 research proposals across five review areas. Fish-related proposals concerned [[Humpback Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Rainbow Trout]], and the [[Kanab Ambersnail]] was also a focal species.

## Background & Description

**Establishment (October 1995 memorandum).** The Deputy Assistant Secretary for Water and Science directed that the program be run from a facility called the GCMRC, following recommendations from a subcommittee of the [[Transition Work Group]]. Key guidelines were:

- **Interim governance:** For up to two years, the Deputy Assistant Secretary, the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] Director and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Commissioner provided joint oversight, in consultation with the Center Chief.
- **Leadership:** A Senior Level Center Chief reported to the USGS Director, with oversight from the Deputy Assistant Secretary.
- **Location:** The Center was co-located with the USGS facility in Flagstaff, Arizona, with a small administrative and scientific staff.
- **Research model:** Work was to be selected through an open call for proposals and contracts, judged on scientific merit and cost, with eligibility extended to federal and state agencies, universities, the private sector and Native American tribes.
- **Funding:** All costs (salaries, travel, space, equipment, research and monitoring) came from electric power sales of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]], reimbursed by Reclamation. The budget was proposed by the Center Chief with concurrence from Reclamation and USGS, after consulting the [[Adaptive Management Work Group]] and an independent scientific review panel.
- **Early deadlines:** A long-term monitoring and research plan and staffing plan were due by August 1, 1996, and an organizational location plan by October 1, 1996.

**Organizational review (later memorandum, undated in source).** After the two-year review, the Deputy Assistant Secretary accepted a white paper from GCMRC Chief Dave Garrett and kept the Center in the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Water and Science for at least one more year. Oversight was assigned to a management team drawn from that office, Reclamation and USGS. The Center was described as an independent science center meant to give objective advice to the Secretary, the Adaptive Management Work Group and the land-managing agencies. The memo confirmed support for the processes for setting management objectives, science planning, competitive programming and independent peer review.

**Peer review (September 12–14, 1997, Park City, Utah).** Reviewers were organized into panels: [[Aquatic Biology]], [[Terrestrial Biology]], [[Physical Science]], [[Cultural and Socioeconomic Resources]] and [[Conceptual Modeling]]. Facilitators were assigned to panels other than their own program areas to limit conflicts of interest. Panels used different rating scales, and conflicts of interest were declared and managed by recusal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposals addressed the Colorado River system downstream of Glen Canyon Dam, and the main concerns were:

- **Native and trout fish:** Humpback chub, flannelmouth sucker and rainbow trout populations, their thermal tolerance, recruitment, growth and dependence on the aquatic food base. Reviewers stressed that cause-and-effect links between dam operations and fish survival were hard to establish with the proposed methods.
- **Aquatic food base:** Benthic and forage organisms, zooplankton and phytoplankton, and trophic energy flows, including stable isotope food-web work.
- **Kanab ambersnail (KAS):** Habitat extent, feeding behavior and predation by *Peromyscus* (deer mice), with habitat analysis extending above the level affected by dam operations.
- **Riparian vegetation and birds:** Riparian plant communities, exotic plant invasion, and avifauna, including starlings and cowbirds, whose increase was regarded as undesirable. Groundwater-fed seeps and springs above current river fluctuation levels were identified as an overlooked habitat.
- **Sediment and geomorphology:** Sandbars in Upper Marble Canyon, sediment inputs from the Paria River and Moenkopi Wash, debris flows, and sediment transport at flows up to 100,000 cfs.
- **Cultural resources:** Erosion of archaeological sites linked to flow and sediment processes.
- **Constraints:** Requirements of the [[Biological Opinion]] shaped the research objectives.

## Key Findings

**Governance and funding**
- The GCMRC was retained in its existing organizational location for at least one year, with management oversight from a three-agency team.
- Long-term monitoring proposals were expected to run two years initially, with renewal options for up to five years. The goal for FY2000 was a final long-term plan with a research framework.
- Reviewers and GCMRC agreed on the need for a data management workshop and for making future data available to the scientific community and the public. GCMRC said it was working to own the key models so that no single entity would gain a competitive advantage.

**Fish-related proposals (Aquatic Biology panel)**
- **Native Fish RFP (BI-1, BI-2, BI-4):** BI-2 was judged clearly ahead and recommended for funding with revision. BI-1 was rated 2 (fund with major changes); its thermal tolerance experiments were considered worth funding, with partial funding suggested. BI-4 was unanimously not recommended for funding because its hypotheses were too broad to test.
- **Trout Fishery RFP (BI-6, BI-8):** BI-6 was recommended for funding with revision. Reviewers found BI-6's hydroacoustic testing a positive, but noted that correlating growth with flow regime could be misleading. BI-8 was judged to have merit but insufficient detail, and future submissions were encouraged.
- **Aquatic Food Base (BI-13, 14, 15):** BI-15 was recommended for funding with modifications and described as one of the best proposals reviewed. BI-13 was unanimously not recommended. BI-14 was seen as more a data collection effort than basic monitoring.
- **Unsolicited BI-16:** Parts were judged worth including in future work, but it did not integrate energy-flow modeling sufficiently to merit funding then.

**Terrestrial Biology panel**
- Avifauna RFP: BI-5 ranked 3, BI-17 ranked 2, and BI-3 ranked 1 (scale 1–3).
- Kanab Ambersnail RFP: BI-12 was preferred over BI-11, but only because a choice had to be made. A combined proposal was recommended.
- Riparian vegetation: BI-9 was recommended for funding with revisions (ranked 1 of 1). BI-7 was not funded because it did not respond to the RFP objectives, and BI-10 was not recommended, though its remote sensing technology was flagged for consideration.

**Physical Science panel (scale 0–3)**
- Rated 3 (fund as is): PH-1, PH-4 and PH-8. PH-8 was judged fully responsive, with a high probability of success.
- Rated 2 (fund with modification): PH-5 and PH-9. PH-9 was over the RFP budget ceiling and had murky end goals.
- Rated 0 (does not meet objectives): PH-2, PH-3, PH-6 and PH-7. PH-7 was judged too narrow, and its RFP was recommended for rewriting.

**Cultural/Socioeconomic panel**
- CU-1 was rated 0. CU-5 was described as comprehensive and well conceived, while CU-6 did not address the RFP. Ratings for other cultural and socioeconomic proposals were not recorded in the excerpt. Reviewers noted that a 2-year proposal was reduced to a 1-year effort.

**Conceptual Modeling**
- Panel ratings used a five-level scale (Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair, Poor) and then an ordinal rank of 1–6.
- The panel recommended funding its top-ranked proposal, conditional on responding to panel comments, and suggested a second-ranked proposal as a fallback. Several proposals were criticized for weak hypotheses and limited modeling expertise.

**Recurring reviewer recommendations**
- Clearer hypothesis testing and stated management objectives in RFPs.
- Greater integration between disciplines and more cross-panel discussion.
- Separation of basic descriptive data from monitoring data.
- Longer proposal development time (the 1-month window was considered too short), wider advertising, and a pre-proposal conference.
- An RFP for unsolicited proposals, which GCMRC said it could not issue, while stating that innovative proposals would be reviewed quarterly as funding permitted.
- A debriefing with the review panel the following year, and a future RFP for protocols that would "reset the monitoring approach for a decade."
