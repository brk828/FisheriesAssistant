# Two New Interstitial Ameiridae (Copepoda Harpacticoidea) from Portugal

#marine-biology #copepoda #ameiridae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1968 taxonomic paper by [[Maria Helena Galhano]] of the [[Institute of Zoology Dr. Augusto Nobre]], University of Porto, describing two new harpacticoid copepods of the family [[Ameiridae]] from the interstitial (psammic) fauna of Portuguese sandy beaches. The two taxa are *[[Interleptomesochra Noodti]]* sp. n., from [[Cabedelo Beach]], and *[[Nitocra Reducta Fluviatilis]]* ssp. n., from [[Areinho de Valbom]]. Both sites lie on the [[Douro River]] mouth and experience variable salinity in their interstitial water. The paper also reproduces the 1968 offprint page and includes a bibliography of roughly 100 publications issued by the Institute between 1940 and 1968. The body of the text is a formal species description, with the Institute's publication list appended as front matter.

## Background & Description

**Context.** The author states that the psammic fauna of Portuguese marine and river sandy beaches is dominated by Crustacea, and that [[Copepoda Harpacticoidea]] are among the most numerous of these, both qualitatively and quantitatively. Several new forms were found; two are described here and others were to be published later. The work was supported by a [[NATO Research Grant]], and the species were offered to [[Wolfgang Noodt]] (Kiel), with whom the author began the study of harpacticoid copepods.

**Genus and family.** Both species belong to the family [[Ameiridae]]. The genus *Interleptomesochra* was described recently by Lang (1965), and at the time comprised few species.

### 1. *Interleptomesochra noodti* sp. n.

- **Material:** Numerous adult males and females and copepodites from Cabedelo, a relatively sheltered beach at the mouth of the Douro (south bank). Interstitial salinity is highly variable.
- **Type specimens:** The holotype is a female, 0.596 mm long (antennule and furcal rami excluded), registered as D. 9.10. Holotype and paratypes are held at the Institute of Zoology, Porto.
- **Morphology (selected):**
  - Body slender, about six times as long as wide.
  - Cephalothorax scarcely longer than the two following somites combined.
  - Rostrum shorter than half the first antennule segment.
  - Antennule 8-segmented in the female and 7-segmented in the male, showing sexual dimorphism; aesthetasc on the fourth segment.
  - Ovisac carries 4 to 6 eggs.
  - Telson has three ventro-lateral rows of spinules plus a fine distal row.
  - Furcal rami nearly quadrangular, each with two strong terminal plumose setae.
  - Maxilliped prehensile, with a strong, spinulose claw on the second endopodite segment.
- **Swimming legs:** P1 endopodite is 3-segmented. Relative lengths are P1 = 1, P2 = 1.33, P3 = 1.58, P4 = 1.65. The author gives the female dimensions as average length 0.595 mm (with a second value of 0.535 mm in the text).

### 2. *Nitocra reducta fluviatilis* ssp. n.

- **Material:** Numerous specimens (three males and females, plus adults and copepodites) from Areinho de Valbom, a sheltered beach on the north bank of the Douro about 9 km from its mouth. Interstitial water ranges from limnic to oligohaline.
- **Type specimens:** The holotype is a female, 0.53 mm long, registered as D. 11.4, with paratypes at the same Institute.
- **Morphology (selected):**
  - Body about seven times as long as broad.
  - Cephalothorax nearly as long as the next three somites combined.
  - Genital double-somite divided dorsally and laterally, undivided ventrally.
  - Anal operculum as long as the telson, bearing 6 to 10 spines.
  - Ovisac holds 10 eggs in two rows.
  - Antennule 8-segmented, with an aesthetasc on an apical prominence of the fourth segment.
  - Average length: female 0.49 mm, male 0.45 mm.
- **Swimming legs:** Relative lengths P1 = 1, P2 = 1.26, P3 = 1.39, P4 = 1.30. The P5 of the female has five setae on the baseoendopodite. The male P5 has three setae on the baseoendopodite.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Both species were collected from the interstitial spaces of sandy beaches, which is the typical habitat of the psammic (interstitial) fauna.
- **Salinity gradient:** The two localities lie on the same river mouth but differ in salinity regime. Cabedelo has highly variable interstitial salinity. Areinho de Valbom is limnic to oligohaline, reflecting its position about 9 km upstream of the mouth. The author uses this gradient to explain the occurrence of the freshwater-influenced form.
- **Beach type:** Cabedelo and Areinho de Valbom are both described as sheltered beaches, which the author does not analyse further in relation to the copepod fauna.
- **Broader fauna:** The text notes that crustaceans dominate the psammic fauna of Portuguese beaches, and that harpacticoids are among the most abundant of these.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new taxa are described.** *Interleptomesochra noodti* sp. n. (Cabedelo) and *Nitocra reducta fluviatilis* ssp. n. (Areinho de Valbom) are both new to science.

2. ***I. noodti* is distinguished within its genus.** The endopodite of P1 is 3-segmented, unlike *I. attenuata* and *I. reducta*, which have a 2-segmented P1 endopodite. The last endopodite segment of P3 and P4 bears four setae. This differs from *I. eulitoralis* (2 and 3 setae) and from *I. elongata* and *I. tenuicornis* (3 and 4 setae). On the chaetotaxy of P5 in the female, *I. tenuicornis* is the closest relative.

3. ***N. r. fluviatilis* is a subspecies, not a full species.** It differs from *N. reducta* Schäfer mainly in the seta formula of P1. The Schäfer form lacks an inner plumose seta on the second exopodite segment, which the new subspecies and all congeners possess. Other differences include the baseoendopodite of P5 in the female, which carries four bare setae in the known form.

4. **Taxonomic decision.** The author considers the differences insufficient for a separate species. The author proposes that the Schäfer form be designated the type subspecies *N. reducta reducta*, together with the Areinho form as *N. reducta fluviatilis*.

5. **Sexual dimorphism and reproduction.** Antennule segment counts differ between sexes in *I. noodti* (8 in the female, 7 in the male). Egg numbers in the ovisac are 4 to 6 in *I. noodti* and 10 in *N. r. fluviatilis*.

6. **Type deposition.** All holotypes and paratypes are lodged with the [[Institute of Zoology Dr. Augusto Nobre]] at the University of Porto.

7. **Institutional context.** The appended publication list (about 100 numbered items, 1940–1968) covers taxa such as isopods (notably [[Stenasellus]] and [[Asellus]], mainly by J. M. Braga), spiders, pseudoscorpions, Lepidoptera, bats, birds, foraminifera, amphipods, and the [[Sardine Fishery]] of northern Portugal (J. A. Machado-Cruz). It also documents the Institute's journal exchange programme, which is printed in the exchange notice on the final front page of the list.

8. **Literature cited.** The paper relies on the key harpacticoid monographs of Lang (1948, 1965), Noodt (1952, 1957), Bozic (1964), Sars, Schäfer, and Kunz, among others. It is closely tied to the systematic work of the Kiel group.
