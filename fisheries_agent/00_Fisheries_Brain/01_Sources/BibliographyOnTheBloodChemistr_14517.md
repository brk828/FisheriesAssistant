# Bibliography on the Blood Chemistry of Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-physiology #hematology #reference-work

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive bibliography compiled by Joseph B. Hunn for the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] in 1967 assembles more than 850 references on fish blood chemistry. The document serves as a guide to widely scattered literature on the subject, addressing the lack of standard reference sources providing "normal" values for fish blood chemistry. The compilation includes references from multiple languages and scientific traditions, covering cyclostomes, elasmobranchs, and teleosts across freshwater and marine environments. The bibliography emphasizes the need for more complete data on fish with known histories (age, sex, nutritional state) to establish comprehensive ranges of normal blood chemistry values essential for evaluating physiological responses to environmental changes and for pharmacological and toxicological testing programs.

## Background & Description

The bibliography was published as Research Report 72 by the [[U.S. Department of the Interior]], [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] in 1967. The work originated from the [[Fish Control Laboratories]] in La Crosse, Wisconsin. The compilation builds upon and expands earlier bibliographic work by Hunn (1959, 1960) that brought together pertinent fish blood chemistry references.

The document provides extensive guidance on reference sources and abbreviations used, including [[Biological Abstracts]], [[Chemical Abstracts]], [[Index Medicus]], [[Zoological Record]], and various specialized fisheries publications. The bibliography includes references not personally read by the author, with source citations indicated where appropriate. Journal title abbreviations follow standard conventions from major abstracting services.

The scope encompasses multiple aspects of fish blood chemistry including: hemoglobin content and oxygen transport, blood cell counts and morphology, serum proteins and electrophoresis patterns, osmotic regulation and ion balance, blood glucose and metabolic compounds, hormones and endocrine factors, immunological properties, blood coagulation, and comparative biochemistry across fish taxa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography reflects growing recognition in the 1960s that fish blood chemistry provides critical indicators of physiological condition and environmental stress. References address how blood parameters vary with:

**Environmental factors**: salinity changes, temperature fluctuations, oxygen availability, water chemistry, and pollution exposure. Studies document osmotic regulation in euryhaline species, effects of asphyxia on blood composition, and responses to thermal shock.

**Biological factors**: seasonal cycles, reproductive state, age and growth, nutritional status, disease conditions, and species-specific differences. Multiple references examine blood changes during spawning migrations in [[Pacific Salmon]] and other anadromous species.

**Experimental conditions**: effects of handling and confinement stress, muscular activity and fatigue, anesthesia, hormone administration, and various toxicological exposures. The compilation includes studies on blood responses to exercise, transport stress, and chemical treatments.

The bibliography encompasses research across diverse aquatic habitats from deep-sea environments to freshwater lakes and streams, covering both wild-caught and hatchery-reared fish. Geographic scope is international, with substantial contributions from North American, European, Japanese, and Soviet researchers.

## Key Findings

**Scope and Coverage**: The bibliography contains over 850 references spanning approximately 60 years of research (early 1900s to 1967), representing work from multiple countries and scientific traditions. References cover cyclostomes (hagfish, lampreys), elasmobranchs (sharks, rays), and teleosts (bony fishes) across marine and freshwater environments.

**Major Research Areas Identified**:

- **Hemoglobin and oxygen transport**: Multiple hemoglobins in fish species, oxygen-carrying capacity, effects of environmental factors on blood oxygen properties, comparative studies across taxa
- **Blood proteins**: Serum protein fractions, electrophoretic patterns, immunological properties, species-specific differences useful for population identification
- **Osmotic regulation**: Ion balance, effects of salinity changes, comparative studies between stenohaline and euryhaline species
- **Metabolic compounds**: Blood glucose levels, effects of exercise and stress, lactic acid accumulation, nitrogen compounds
- **Endocrine factors**: Thyroid hormones, corticosteroids, sex hormones, effects on blood composition during reproduction
- **Hematology**: Red and white blood cell counts, cell morphology, seasonal variations, effects of disease

**Methodological Developments**: The bibliography documents evolution of analytical techniques including paper electrophoresis, immunoelectrophoresis, spectrophotometry, microhematocrit methods, and serological approaches for population studies.

**Data Gaps Identified**: The compiler explicitly notes the scarcity of data on fish with known histories (age, sex, nutritional state, environmental background), emphasizing this as a critical limitation for establishing reliable baseline values and interpreting physiological responses.

**Applied Significance**: References address practical applications in fisheries management, aquaculture, pollution assessment, disease diagnosis, and population identification. The compilation supports evaluation of fish physiological condition and responses to environmental stressors.

**Taxonomic and Comparative Perspectives**: The bibliography includes comparative studies examining blood chemistry evolution across vertebrate classes, phylogenetic relationships revealed through blood proteins, and species-specific adaptations to different environments.

**International Collaboration**: The compilation reflects substantial international research effort, with references in English, German, French, Russian, and Japanese, demonstrating the global nature of fish blood chemistry research during this period.
