# Razorback Sucker Management and Hatchery Escape Correspondence

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of two agency letters about the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The first, a January 1981 letter from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], agrees in principle to a [[Cooperative Agreement]] for managing the species and to its [[Reintroduction]] into the Salt and Verde Rivers. It opposes federal listing because listing would limit state work at the local level. The second, a December 1977 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to the [[California Department of Fish and Game]], reports the accidental release of 274 hatchery-raised juveniles into the [[Colorado River]] at [[Lake Mohave]].

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is a native sucker of the Colorado River system. The documents refer to it as a species of concern whose survival is the main goal of both agencies. The 1981 letter notes that the species was considered for federal listing, and that an unlisted population was available for reintroduction. The 1977 letter describes the fish involved in the escape:

- **Number released:** 274 young fish.
- **Year class:** 1974, hatched at [[Willow Beach Fish Hatchery]] from Lake Mohave parent stock.
- **Size:** 3 to 12 inches total length.
- **Identification:** no specific identification marks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Release site:** Colorado River at Lake Mohave, September 25, 1977.
- **Cause:** a clogged screen in the hatchery water system.
- **Water temperature:** approximately 55°F in both the hatchery and river water at the time of the escape (OCR reading; the value is partly illegible).
- **Predation:** the 1981 letter warns that juveniles must be stocked at a size that minimizes predation by [[Piscivore Predation]] from fish already present in the Salt and Verde Rivers.
- **Genetic and population concern:** the 1977 letter notes that the added stock could confuse the assessment of natural reproduction of this native sucker in Colorado River reservoirs. The release was relatively small and all fish came from one year class, which limited the potential for confusion.

## Key Findings

**1. Support for a cooperative management agreement (1981)**
- The Arizona Game and Fish Department agrees with the proposed [[Cooperative Agreement]] for managing the razorback sucker.
- Listing the species under the Endangered Species Act would severely limit the state's ability to work with the fish at the local level. The state therefore opposes listing.
- The department is willing to reintroduce the unlisted razorback into the Salt and Verde Rivers.
- The department supports stocking and monitoring for five to ten years or more, which it considers necessary for re-establishment.

**2. Concerns about the number and size of stocked fish**
- The number of offspring available for reintroduction, about 100,000, is considered low. The department believes a larger number would improve the chances of establishment.
- Stocked juveniles should be large enough to minimize predation by existing piscivores.

**3. Timeline**
- The department asks that the cooperative agreement be initiated as soon as possible so that reintroduction efforts can begin in 1981. It proposes an interagency meeting to lay the groundwork.

**4. Accidental release at Lake Mohave (1977)**
- On September 25, 1977, a clogged screen at Willow Beach Fish Hatchery allowed 274 young razorback suckers to enter the Colorado River.
- The fish were from the 1974 year class, had no identification marks, and were relatively few in number.
- The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service acknowledged the potential for confusion over natural reproduction and reported that it had taken action to prevent a recurrence.
