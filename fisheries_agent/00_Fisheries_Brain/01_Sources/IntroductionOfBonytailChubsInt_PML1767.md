# Memo: Introduction of Bonytail Chubs into Lake Havasu (9 February 1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

A memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Parker Field Office reports the transport and release of 35 adult [[Bonytail Chub]] and two adult [[Razorback Sucker]] into [[Lake Havasu]] on 3 February 1994. The fish came from the Hassayampa River Preserve and were released at a cove on the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]]. The action was part of the Lake Havasu Native Fish Program, a cooperative effort among [[The Nature Conservancy]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Arizona State University]], and the Service. The bonytail were F2 offspring produced at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. The fish were tagged, held briefly, and released in good condition.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Use [[Parker Lake]] as a growout facility for native big river fishes, with the fish placed in Lake Havasu as part of the Native Fish Program.
- **Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*):** 35 adults transported. Total length 303–421 mm; weight 250–570 g.
- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*):** Two adults transported. Lengths were 484 mm and 567 mm; weights were 1.35 kg and a second value that is partly illegible in the scan.
- **Reproductive condition:** The razorback suckers were reported as ripe, one male and one female.
- **Origin:** The bonytail were F2 fish from a hatchery-reared lineage at Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- **Marking:** All fish were PIT-tagged by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] before transport and release.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The release site lies within a national wildlife refuge on the Bill Williams River arm of the Lake Havasu system, an area used for conserving native big river fishes of the lower Colorado River. The memo does not report water chemistry, habitat measurements, or biological monitoring beyond the handling observations. Its ecological relevance is as a restocking effort for species that are native to large river systems and are now rare in regulated waters. Because the memo gives no survival or population data, its contribution is documentation of a translocation event, not a demonstration of establishment.

## Key Findings

- **Transport and release:** 35 adult bonytail and two adult razorback suckers were moved to Lake Havasu on 3 February 1994 and released together.
- **Holding period:** Fish were held in a live car for less than 30 minutes before release.
- **Behavior on release:** The fish were active and left the release point as a single school into the lake.
- **Health observations:** No evidence of fungus or excessive damage was seen on the fish.
- **Tagging:** All fish were PIT-tagged before release, which allows individual identification in later recaptures.
- **Genetic/origin note:** The bonytail were F2 progeny from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], which is relevant to the genetic management of hatchery-reared stock.
- **Limitations:** The memo reports no post-release monitoring, survival rates, or follow-up sampling. Some OCR-damaged text (notably the second razorback weight and the cove name) could not be read with certainty.
