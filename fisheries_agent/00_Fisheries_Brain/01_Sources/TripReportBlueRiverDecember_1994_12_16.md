# Trip Report: Blue River, December 13, 1994

## Executive Summary
This document is a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] field trip report, not a taxonomic or morphological study, but it is highly relevant to [[Freshwater Fish Conservation]] because it was generated under an **Endangered Species Act Section 7 consultation** framework (case no. 2-21-94-F-243), routed to the agency's "Endangered Fish/Herp Section." The report documents post-flood emergency road and channel repair work conducted by Greenlee County along the [[Blue River (Arizona)]], following flood events on **November 11–12, 1994** and **December 5, 1994**. Although no fish species are explicitly named in the text, the involvement of the Endangered Fish/Herp Section strongly implies concern for federally listed native fishes historically found in the [[Gila River Basin]], such as [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), both of which depend on unembedded gravel/cobble substrates and natural channel dynamics of the type being disturbed by this repair work. The report catalogs approximately 35 sites requiring emergency or long-term repair, several of which involve direct in-channel work, fill borrowing from the active streambed, and floodplain/road reconstruction — activities with direct implications for aquatic habitat integrity.

## Background & Description

**Channel & Habitat Structure Notes (relevant to aquatic habitat quality):**
- The Blue River floodplain is characterized as an **unstable cobble floodplain**, with braided and anastomosing channel segments.
- At the **Alex Thal property/island site**, the river has shifted from using only the eastern channel historically to now flowing through **both eastern and western channels**, effectively bisecting a floodplain "island."
- Several reaches (e.g., **Blue Box**, **Balke crossing**, **Between Bush Creek and Cow Canyon**) show classic **ford crossing dynamics**, with rivers depositing gravel bars beside deeper cut channels — a natural process being repeatedly overridden by grading/flattening for vehicle crossings.
- A concrete ford pad at **Blue Crossing** was found buried under **4–5 feet of gravel**, indicating significant sediment transport and deposition during flood events.
- Riprap (boulder) and levee reconstruction were used at multiple mainstem and side-channel bridge sites, altering natural bank and channel morphology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
This report describes disturbance to a **desert riverine ecosystem** in the [[Blue River (Arizona)]] watershed, a tributary system feeding into the [[San Francisco River]] and ultimately the [[Gila River]] — a basin recognized for endemic and imperiled native fish fauna.

Key habitat-relevant observations:
- **In-channel fill borrowing**: Road repair crews repeatedly borrowed fill material directly from the active river channel (e.g., "Between Bush Creek and Cow Canyon," "Balke crossing"), which can degrade spawning/foraging substrate for benthic-associated native fishes.
- **Floodplain/road encroachment**: Multiple road segments lie directly within or immediately adjacent to the active floodplain (Robard–Smith property road, Downstream from Sweetie Canyon, Downstream from Sawmill Canyon, Downstream from Lamphier Canyon), creating chronic bank instability and repeated need for cut-and-fill repairs after each flood event.
- **Debris/riparian vegetation removal**: An alder patch below a mainstem bridge was slated for removal to prevent debris buildup — riparian vegetation removal can reduce cover, leaf litter input, and bank stabilization important to aquatic and riparian-dependent species.
- **Hillside excavation near Sawmill Canyon**: Approximately 75% of a large hillside excavation was completed before the Corps of Engineers and USFWS halted work, indicating substantial sediment mobilization potential into the river before consultation review could occur.
- **Regulatory context**: The work falls under a combination of **emergency consultation**, a **Section 404 (Clean Water Act) permit**, and pre-existing **informal Section 7 consultation** for the Sawmill Canyon area — confirming that federally protected aquatic species/habitat considerations were an active part of the Forest Service and USFWS review of this road system.
- **Land ownership mosaic**: The corridor is a patchwork of [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] land, private residences (Robard, Smith, Lee Ranch, Thal), and county-maintained roads/crossings, complicating consistent habitat protection and permitting.

## Key Findings
- **~35 sites** along the Blue River road corridor were identified by Greenlee County as needing emergency or long-term repair following the November and December 1994 floods.
- Significant **in-stream and floodplain disturbance** occurred at multiple sites through fill borrowing, channel clearing, hillside excavation, and levee/riprap construction — actions with direct bearing on native fish habitat quality (substrate embeddedness, channel stability, bank cover).
- Work at the **Sawmill Canyon hillside** was halted by the **Army Corps of Engineers** and **USFWS** at ~75% completion due to consultation requirements, highlighting active regulatory oversight of aquatic habitat impacts.
- Confusion existed between the **Forest Service** and **FEMA** regarding responsibility for NEPA documentation on the Sawmill Canyon work, indicating **interagency coordination gaps** that could delay or complicate proper environmental review, including impacts to aquatic resources.
- The county expressed long-term interest in an [[RS-2477 Road]] claim along the Blue River corridor, potentially connected to a **long-range plan to build a highway up the Blue River** to replace Highway 666 — a proposal with potentially major implications for riverine habitat if pursued.
- A private landowner (**Lee Ranch**, 83-year-old resident) faces an active bank erosion threat, with the County seeking government funding to address channel migration threatening the residence — illustrating the ongoing tension between infrastructure/property protection and natural channel dynamics important to native aquatic species.
- No direct fish sampling, population data, or species-specific morphological/ecological data are presented in this report; its value lies in documenting **anthropogenic disturbance regimes** (flood-response road repair) affecting a river system relevant to [[Endangered Freshwater Fish of the Southwest]].
