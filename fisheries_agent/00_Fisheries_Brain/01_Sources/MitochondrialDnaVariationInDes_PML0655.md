# Mitochondrial DNA Variation in Desert Pupfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, presented at the [[Desert Fishes Council]] symposium and published in its proceedings, uses [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) variation to assess the genetic structure of the [[Desert Pupfish]]. Eleven wild populations and one captive stock were sampled. The analysis supports treating the [[Quitobaquito Springs]] and [[Rio Sonoyta]] populations as a separate species, [[Cyprinodon Eremus]], distinct from the more widespread [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] of the [[Salton Sea]] and [[Colorado River Delta]] region. The authors use these results to recommend genetic management actions. The document is a bilingual (English and Spanish) abstract, and parts of the OCR text are garbled.

## Background & Description

- **Study taxa:** Desert pupfish, a small cyprinodontid fish of desert aquatic systems. The two taxa addressed are *Cyprinodon eremus* (described by Miller and Fuiman) and *Cyprinodon macularius* (described by Baird and Girard).
- **Research question:** Whether the southwestern populations represent a separate species and how genetic variation is partitioned among regions.
- **Methods:** Polymerase chain reaction single-strand conformation polymorphism analysis ([[PCR-SSCP]]) of the mitochondrial [[ND2 Gene]]. Representative haplotypes were sequenced for the entire ND2 gene (1,047 bases) and part of the mtDNA d-loop, which allowed [[Phylogenetic Analysis]].
- **Sample sources:** Eleven wild populations and a captive stock held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center.
- **Authors and affiliations:** A. A. Echelle, R. A. van den Bussche, C. O. Minckley, T. Manoy, P. C. Marsh and A. Timpe, from Oklahoma State University, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker, Arizona), and Arizona State University. Marsh presented the paper.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The populations sit in a desert landscape shaped by spring-fed aquatic habitats and large river systems. The study area spans:

- **Quitobaquito Springs and Rio Sonoyta**, the source of the southern group.
- **Salton Sea area**, a large saline lake (three localities sampled).
- **Lower Colorado River delta** (six localities sampled).

Genetic isolation between these habitats is relevant to conservation because fragmented desert aquatic systems are vulnerable to loss of local diversity.

## Key Findings

- **Haplotype diversity:** Thirteen mtDNA haplotypes were detected among 259 specimens examined.
- **Phylogenetic structure:** Two reciprocally monophyletic groups were recovered:
  1. Quitobaquito/Rio Sonoyta samples.
  2. Samples from three Salton Sea localities and six lower Colorado River delta localities.
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** The authors argue that the Quitobaquito/Rio Sonoyta populations represent a separate species, *Cyprinodon eremus*, distinct from *C. macularius*.
- **Within-group structure:**
  - No significant genetic difference was found between the *C. eremus* samples from Quitobaquito Springs and Rio Sonoyta.
  - No significant differences were detected in pairwise tests among samples within the Salton Sea area or within the lower Colorado River delta.
- **Among-region variation:** Significant but rather weak genetic variance was found among regions in *C. macularius*. The same haplotype predominated in both the Salton Sea area and the lower Colorado River delta.
- **Management implications:** The authors use these results to formulate [[Genetic Management]] recommendations for the desert pupfish, based on the recognition of separate evolutionary units.
