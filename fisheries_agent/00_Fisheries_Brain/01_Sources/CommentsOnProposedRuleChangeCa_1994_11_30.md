## Executive Summary
This document is a formal comment letter authored by Dr. David L. Hardy, Sr., addressed to the Arizona Game and Fish Commission, opposing a proposed rule change that would legalize commercial captive propagation of five native Arizona snake species. The letter synthesizes input from 12 breeders, hobbyists, and biologists (7 in favor, 5 opposed) and evaluates the ecological risks of commercializing wild-collected founder stock, with special concern raised for the poorly-studied **[[Green Rat Snake]]** (*Senticolis triaspis intermedia*).

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source. The document is regulatory/policy-focused rather than descriptive of species biology. Taxa mentioned by common reference only:

- **[[Common Kingsnake]]** (*Lampropeltis getula*)
- **[[Gopher Snake]]** (*Pituophis catenifer*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Green Rat Snake**: Distribution restricted to the extreme southeastern portion of Arizona (Sky Islands / Madrean woodland habitat); life history described as "virtually unknown." Typically located via road-cruising, which is noted as a lower-impact collection method that does not usually destroy habitat.
- **Common Kingsnake & Gopher Snake**: Juveniles are noted to be collectible in significant numbers during late summer and fall from wild populations, raising concern about illegal trade circumventing proposed size limits.
- Broader ecological concern: increased commercial collecting pressure and associated **[[Habitat Destruction]]** on wild snake populations if a legal monetary value is attached to these species.
- Cross-state legal asymmetry allows non-resident collectors to legally sell Arizona-collected specimens/progeny under other states' laws, creating a regulatory loophole exploited outside of Arizona jurisdiction.

## Key Findings
- No species discussed are federally or state-listed as Endangered or Threatened, though the **[[Green Rat Snake]]** is flagged for precautionary exclusion from the rule change due to its restricted range and unknown vulnerability to captive-breeding-driven collection.
- Proposed mitigating regulatory tools include: permits, **PIT tag identification** of captive breeder adults, and size limits on offspring sold — the author judges these insufficient to prevent laundering of wild-caught juveniles into the commercial pet trade.
- The author argues that in-state captive production volumes would be negligible relative to the broader interstate/national snake trade (e.g., California), undermining the argument that legal captive breeding would relieve wild population pressure.
- Recommendation: reject the proposal as written; if approved, exclude the Green Rat Snake pending further study; separately pursue rule changes explicitly prohibiting **habitat destruction** during snake collection activities.
- Document illustrates a policy tension between wildlife commercialization, enforcement feasibility, and conservation risk — relevant to **[[Wildlife Trade Regulation]]** and **[[Captive Breeding Programs]]** as conservation tools, but has **no ichthyological or aquatic ecosystem content**.
