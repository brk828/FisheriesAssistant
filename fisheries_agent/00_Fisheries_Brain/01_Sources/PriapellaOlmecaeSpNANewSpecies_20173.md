# Priapella olmecae sp. n.: A New Poeciliid from Veracruz, México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper formally describes *[[Priapella Olmecae]]* sp. n., a new freshwater poeciliid from the Los Tuxtlas region of [[Veracruz]], México. The type locality is [[Rio de la Palma]]. Morphological comparison based on [[Gonopodium]] structure, fin-ray and [[Gill Rakers]] counts, and body proportions places the species as the closest relative of *[[Priapella Bonita]]*, a species from the northern [[Papaloapan River]] drainage that has apparently not been collected for many years. The authors treat the two as [[Sibling Species]]. The paper also contrasts the new species with *[[Priapella Compressa]]* and *[[Priapella Intermedia]]*.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** [[Regan]] (1913) described the genus *Priapella* with *P. bonita* (Meek, 1904) as its only species. *P. compressa* (Alvarez, 1948) and *P. intermedia* (Alvarez, 1952) were described later. The genus is characterized by a primitive gonopodium: ray 3 bears a long hook and a dorsal membranous blade, ray 4a curves over the blade, and rays 4p and 5 are much shorter than rays 4a and 3. Earlier work (Meyer, 1987) placed *Priapella* as the adelphotaxon of *[[Xiphophorus]]*, with *P. compressa* as the adelphotaxon of *P. intermedia*. The present study identifies *P. olmecae* as the adelphotaxon of *P. bonita*.

**Subfamily context.** The authors follow Parenti (1981), in which Poeciliinae equals the family Poeciliidae of Rosen & Bailey (1963). The subfamily includes 27 freshwater genera, and some populations in several genera are adapted to saltwater in the New World.

**Type material.**
- Holotype: male (SMF 21157), SL 38.8 mm, Rio de la Palma, about 10 km NNW of Sontecomapan, 25 km NNE of Lake Catemaco, collected 25.6.1987 by E. Müller, A. Martinsen & M. K. Meyer.
- Paratypes: several series from Rio de la Palma (1987), Rio Agua Fria (13.6.1985), and Laguna Escondida (11.7.1986). Collections are deposited in IBUNAM and SMF. Table 1 lists measurements for 15 paratypes, which were grouped by locality.

**Etymology.** The name *olmecae* refers to the Olmec people, who inhabited southern Veracruz before the Spanish arrival.

**Diagnosis and description.**
- Body moderately deep and sharply compressed posteriorly. Head short, 3.60 to 4.30 in SL.
- Longitudinal scale series 28 to 30 (rarely 31); vertebrae 29 to 31 (rarely 28).
- Gill rakers on the first arch 1+7 to 9+2 to 3 (total 11 to 13; rarely 10 or 13).
- Gonopodium long and slender, 2.60 to 2.75 in SL. Ray 3 terminates in a long hook with a thin dorsal crescent blade. Ray 4p has 7 to 9 rose-like backward-directed thorns followed by 2 to 3 broad bicuspid segments.
- Dorsal fin 9 to 10 rays; caudal 27 to 29 rays (rarely 30 or 31); anal 10 rays (rarely 9); pectoral 13 to 15 rays.
- Preopercular canal with 7 pores; preorbital and mandibular canals with 4 pores each.
- Adult males have a large keel from the caudal peduncle to the gonopodium base.

**Coloration.** Males and females lack sex-specific color patterns. Body is greyish-brown and blue, iris intense blue, unpaired fins orange or red, and pectoral and ventral fins clear. In captivity the unpaired fins show only an orange border, whereas wild specimens display full coloration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The species is known only from three sites:
- **Rio de la Palma**, near La Palma village at 30 m a.s.l., on the road to Playa Escondida. The river is surrounded by tropical wet forest, is 2 to 6 m wide, 0.10 to 1.20 m deep, flows slow to fast, and drains into the Laguna de Sontecomapan. The water is clear, the substrate is mainly gravel and mud, and aquatic vegetation is absent, with dense vegetation along the banks.
- **Rio Agua Fria**, about 3 to 5 m wide and 0.30 to 0.70 m deep, draining into Laguna Escondida.
- **Laguna Escondida**, Los Tuxtlas.

**Physical conditions.** Water temperatures during collection ranged from 21 to 27 °C and air temperatures from 28 to 35 °C. Collections were made in June and July.

**Associated fauna.** Co-occurring species include *[[Xiphophorus Helleri]]*, *[[Poecilia Mexicana]]*, *Xiphophorus maculatus*, *Heterandria bimaculata*, *Astyanax*, *Agonostomus*, *Atherinella*, *Gobiomorus*, *Synbranchus* and *Cichlasoma*. Assemblages differ among the three sites. Atherinella and Gobiomorus were recorded only from Rio de la Palma, and Synbranchus only from Rio Agua Fria.

## Key Findings

1. **New species confirmed.** *P. olmecae* is unequivocally assigned to *Priapella* on the basis of gonopodial structure, skull, and body and fin coloration.

2. **Closest relative is *P. bonita*.** Three synapomorphies link the two species:
   - well-developed membranous tissue on gonopodial ray 3;
   - a long gonopodium (2.60 to 2.75 in SL) with short rays 3 and 4a;
   - well-developed ray 4p serrae with bicuspid proximal segments.

3. **Diagnostic differences from *P. bonita*:**
   - dorsal rays 9 to 10 in *P. olmecae* versus 8 in *P. bonita*;
   - first-arch gill rakers 11 to 13 versus 15 to 16;
   - males of *P. olmecae* are extremely deep-bodied, whereas *P. bonita* males are slender;
   - *P. bonita* is reported not to have orange unpaired fins, though its in-vivo coloration is poorly known.

4. **Contrast with *P. compressa*:**
   - fewer first-arch gill rakers in *P. olmecae*;
   - scale series 28 to 30 versus 25 to 28;
   - *P. compressa* males lack well-developed membranous tissue on ray 3 and have smaller thorns and less developed bicuspid segments;
   - gonopodium short (3.10 to 3.40 in SL) in *P. compressa*.

5. **Contrast with *P. intermedia*:** *P. intermedia* has 13 to 14 first-arch gill rakers, a short gonopodium (3.10 to 3.35 in SL), and the same reduced membranous tissue and thorn features as *P. compressa*.

6. ***P. compressa* and *P. intermedia* are sister species.** They share a lemon-yellow body, yellowish unpaired fins, and white comet-like stripes on the caudal fin. These features contrast with the blue-grey body and orange fins of *P. olmecae*.

7. **Conservation status of *P. bonita*.** The authors note that *P. bonita* has not been collected for many years and may be extinct or very rare, based on personal communication from Prof. R. R. Miller. This makes the new species a potentially important lineage for understanding the genus.

8. **Distribution.** *P. olmecae* is restricted to small rivers and a lagoon in the Los Tuxtlas region of Veracruz. Its restricted range may make it vulnerable, though the paper does not assess conservation status.
