# Effects of Environmental Factors on the Toxicity of Chloramine-T to Fish

#ichthyology #fish-health #toxicology #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1988 investigation by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[National Fisheries Research Center]] examined the toxicity of [[Chloramine-T]] (n-sodium-n-chloro-para-toluenesulfonamide), a therapeutant used to treat [[Bacterial Gill Disease]] in hatchery fish. The study evaluated how environmental factors—temperature, water hardness, and pH—affect chloramine-T toxicity to three fish species: [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*). Results showed that pH was the most critical factor, with the chemical being approximately 6 times more toxic at pH 6.5 than at pH 9.5. The 96-h LC50 values were 2.80 mg/L for rainbow trout, 3.75 mg/L for channel catfish, and 7.30 mg/L for fathead minnows in soft water at 12°C.

## Background & Description

[[Bacterial Gill Disease]] (BGD) is the most common disease of hatchery-reared salmonids and causes more fish losses than any other bacterial disease. The disease is strongly mediated by stressful environmental conditions and marginal nutrition associated with intensive aquaculture. [[Flavobacteria]] and [[Flexibacteria]] are the associated pathogenic agents. Gills damaged by BGD pathogens are also prone to secondary invasion by fungi.

[[Chloramine-T]] was demonstrated by From (1980) to be effective for controlling BGD. The compound is a chlorinated sulfonamide that requires approval by the [[U.S. Food and Drug Administration]] for use on food fish. Previous research by Tooby et al. (1975) and Cross and Hursey (1973) indicated that chloramine-T activity is affected by pH, water hardness, and temperature, being more toxic in soft, acid waters than in hard, alkaline waters.

The commercial grade chloramine-T used in this study (lot 12605, Badger Pharmacal Inc.) yielded 98.4% available chlorine when tested using [[U.S. Pharmacopeial Convention]] methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study was conducted at the [[National Fisheries Research Center]] in LaCrosse, Wisconsin, using fish obtained from Federal fish hatcheries or produced at the research center. Test fish were maintained according to standard bioassay procedures and acclimated to desired water chemistries and temperatures for 24 hours before testing.

**Water Chemistry Parameters:**
- Water hardness ranged from very soft (10-13 mg/L as CaCO₃) to very hard (280-320 mg/L as CaCO₃)
- pH levels tested: 6.5, 7.5, 8.1, 8.5, and 9.5
- Temperature range: 7°C to 22°C
- Test waters were oxygen-saturated and prepared using standardized reconstitution procedures

**Test Methodology:**
- Static and flow-through test procedures followed protocols from the [[Committee on Methods for Toxicity Tests with Aquatic Organisms]] (1975), [[ASTM Committee E-35]] (1980), and [[U.S. Department of Agriculture]] (1986)
- 20 fish exposed per concentration in 15 L glass jars
- Mortalities recorded at 1, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours
- [[High Performance Liquid Chromatography]] (HPLC) used to determine chloramine-T concentrations at 0, 6, 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours

## Key Findings

**Species Sensitivity:**
- 96-h LC50 values in soft water at 12°C:
  - [[Rainbow Trout]]: 2.80 mg/L (95% CI: 2.41-3.26)
  - [[Channel Catfish]]: 3.75 mg/L (95% CI: 3.30-4.26)
  - [[Fathead Minnow]]: 7.30 mg/L (95% CI: 6.71-7.94)
- Rainbow trout and channel catfish were approximately twice as sensitive as fathead minnows

**Temperature Effects:**
- Chloramine-T was more toxic in warm water than cold water at exposures ≤24 hours
- For rainbow trout, 3-h LC50 was 28.0 mg/L at 17°C versus 43 mg/L at 12°C; no mortality at 60 mg/L at 7°C
- For channel catfish, 6-h LC50 values were 14.0 mg/L at 22°C, 25.3 mg/L at 17°C, and >60.0 mg/L at 12°C
- Temperature had no significant effect on 96-h LC50 values for either species
- Temperature affected time of response rather than ultimate toxicity

**Water Hardness Effects:**
- Water hardness had only slight effect on rainbow trout toxicity and no effect on channel catfish
- For rainbow trout, 96-h LC50 was 7.35 mg/L in very soft water versus 14.2 mg/L in very hard water
- Channel catfish showed no significant difference across hardness ranges

**pH Effects (Most Important Factor):**
- Chloramine-T toxicity increased significantly as pH decreased
- At pH 9.5, no mortality occurred in either species after 24 h exposure to 60 mg/L
- Chemical was approximately 6 times more toxic at pH 6.5 than at pH 9.5
- Rainbow trout 96-h LC50: 1.89 mg/L at pH 6.5 versus 10.8 mg/L at pH 9.5
- Channel catfish 96-h LC50: 1.75 mg/L at pH 6.5 versus 12.3 mg/L at pH 9.5

**Use Pattern Safety:**
- Rainbow trout exposed to recommended use concentration (12 mg/L for 1 hour) and x3 and x5 levels showed no significant mortality
- No abnormal responses observed during 14-day post-treatment recovery period
- Little chance of causing mortality with overtreatment under tested conditions

**Persistence and Deactivation:**
- Toxicity of aged chloramine-T solutions decreased over time
- After 4 weeks aging, toxicity to rainbow trout decreased by factor of 1/2
- 96-h LC50: 6.00 mg/L for 4-week aged solution versus 2.85 mg/L for fresh solution
- Deactivation index = 2.10
- HPLC analysis showed measured concentrations decreased less rapidly than toxicity (6 mg/L calculated concentration measured at 6.12 mg/L initially, 5.50 mg/L after 4 weeks)

**Time-Independent Toxicity:**
- Rainbow trout were considerably more resistant in flow-through tests than static tests
- 96-h LC50 values: 23.0-30.0 mg/L (flow-through) versus 4.6-7.0 mg/L (static)
- Speculation that chlorine is released more completely under static conditions, causing higher toxicity
- Mortalities did not continue beyond 96 hours
- Time-independent LC50 values similar to 96-h LC50 values in flow-through tests

**Analytical Methods:**
- HPLC verification of calculated concentrations (6, 10, 40 mg/L) from 40 tests showed means ± SD: 5.90±0.17, 9.89±0.27, and 38.9±1.39 mg/L
- Waters Associates HPLC system with Model 481 spectrophotometer at 229 nm wavelength
- Retention time approximately 3.2 minutes
- Mobile phase: acetonitrile:phosphate buffer (50:50, v/v) at 2.0 mL/min flow rate
