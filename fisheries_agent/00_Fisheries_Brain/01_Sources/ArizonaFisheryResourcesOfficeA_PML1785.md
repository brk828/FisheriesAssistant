# Razorback Sucker Monitoring at Lake Mohave, March 2005

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report documents the annual [[Razorback Sucker]] roundup on [[Lake Mohave]] (AZ-NV) from 15 to 18 March 2005. The effort used trammel netting and submerged-light larval collection to monitor the population and gather life-history data. Seventy-three razorback suckers were captured, and 66% of them were identified as [[Repatriated Fish]]. Five of the six fish species taken were introduced. The roundup also collected an estimated 14,000 wild razorback sucker larvae for rearing at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. The work supports the Lake Mohave Native Fish Group goal of replacing the aging adult population with younger fish.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. It was listed as endangered in 1991, its recovery plan was completed in 1998, and [[Critical Habitat]] was designated in 1994. Populations have declined throughout the basin, especially in the lower basin and at Lake Mohave.

The roundup began in 1979 through [[Arizona State University]] to monitor the population and collect life-history information. Current participants include:

- [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]]
- [[Bureau Of Reclamation]]
- National Park Service
- Nevada Division of Wildlife
- [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Fishery Resources Office, Parker)

The week-long effort used four camps on Lake Mohave, all dedicated to monitoring this species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Sampling covered the shoreline from Yuma Cove (river mile 25) to Owl Cove (river mile 30), Mohave County, AZ. Water fished was generally shallower than 5 m, though some sets were deeper.

**Methods.**
- **Netting:** Six [[Trammel Net]]s (100 m × 2 m; 25 cm outer and 2.5 mm inner wall) were fished for three nights. Nets were tied to shore and set perpendicular or at an angle to it. They were set between 5 and 6 PM, run between 10 and 11 PM, reset, and pulled in the early morning.
- **Processing:** Fish were identified and counted in the nets. Non-target species were released immediately. Razorback suckers were held briefly, measured for total length (mm) and weight (g), sexed, and checked for a [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tag]] (PIT tag). Untagged fish were tagged.
- **Repatriate screening:** Fish with traits suspected of occurring only in hatchery-origin repatriates (lack of scars, blindness, parasites) were flagged. Confirmation was to come from comparing PIT numbers with the ASU database.
- **Genetics:** Twenty-five larvae from the Yuma Cove area and fin clips from repatriated fish were preserved in 100% ethanol for [[Genetic Analysis]] at ASU's request.
- **Larval collection:** Larvae were attracted with a submerged light over the boat side and dipped with fine-mesh nets into five-gallon buckets. They were transferred to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery within two days. Collection time, larval count, number of lights, and number of crew were recorded for the Bureau of Reclamation.

## Key Findings

**Netting effort and catch rates**
- 18 twelve-hour net sets were made, totaling 1,800 m of net and 216 hours of netting.
- Daily catch ranged from 5.4 to 18 fish per net set.
- Catch ranged from 2.2 to 3 fish per 100 m of net.
- Table 1 summarizes net counts, total catch, and catch rates by date. Its OCR text is partly garbled, so only the ranges above are reported with confidence.

**Species composition**
- Six species were taken, in decreasing order of abundance: [[Common Carp]], [[Striped Bass]], Razorback Sucker, [[Channel Catfish]], [[Rainbow Trout]], and [[Largemouth Bass]].
- Five of the six are introduced. Razorback sucker is the only native species.
- Carp, striped bass, and razorback sucker were taken in almost equal numbers.
- A total of 226 fish were handled during the trip.

**Razorback sucker captures**
- 73 razorback suckers were taken. Of these, 61 (84%) were recaptures, indicating that most individuals had been tagged on previous occasions. This recapture rate suggests a [[Mark-Recapture]] design.
- 48 (66%) were classified as repatriated fish.
- Mean length and weight for all razorback suckers were 542 mm and 1,559 g (the OCR text is partly unclear on the weight).
- Males averaged 527 mm and 1,869 g. Females averaged 542 mm and 2,852 g.
- Repatriated fish averaged 530 mm and 1,848 g. Repatriated males averaged 524 mm and 1,054 g, and repatriated females averaged 596 mm and 2,444 g.
- Table 3 gives these summaries. Its header lists "2004" as the collection year, which conflicts with the 2005 dates in the text.

**Recruitment and population goals**
- The authors describe the presence of young fish in the catch as encouraging. This supports the Lake Mohave Native Fish Group goal of replacing the aged population with younger fish to sustain it.

**Larval collection**
- The four camps collected an estimated 14,000 wild razorback sucker larvae and transferred them to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery.
