# Humpback Chub Monitoring in Yampa Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This FY 2000 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] describes the third year of a three-year study to develop procedures for monitoring population trends of the [[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Yampa River]] through [[Yampa Canyon]]. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] project team used [[Electrofishing]] rafts to capture and [[PIT Tagging|PIT tag]] adult fish. Preliminary [[Population Estimation|population estimates]] suggest the population may number only a few hundred individuals. The project is ongoing and on track, with a final report due January 2001.

## Background & Description

- **Population status:** The Yampa River population is one of only five known populations of humpback chub. It is poorly known because the reach is inaccessible and the species has been relatively rare in recent collections (Karp and Tyus 1990; Tyus 1998).
- **Critical habitat:** The Yampa River within Yampa Canyon has been designated [[Critical Habitat]], and the population is considered important for species recovery.
- **Monitoring rationale:** Long-term monitoring would provide baseline status data and a response measure for management actions such as [[Non-Native Fish Removal]] or flow alterations. It would also yield estimates of population size, survival, and recruitment needed to refine life-stage models and set management objectives (Crowl and Bouwes 1997; Lentsch et al. 1997).
- **Study design:** The 1998–2001 study aims to develop sampling designs (sample sizes, procedures, and assumptions) for measuring trends in both adult and juvenile populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The species inhabits a remote canyon reach, which limits access and sampling.
- Monitoring is framed around life-stage population processes (survival and recruitment), not only abundance.
- The report does not provide water chemistry, habitat measurements, invertebrate or plankton data, or community-level information beyond the target species and the mention of non-native fish removal as a management concern.

**2000 sampling trips (electrofishing, Yampa Canyon)**

1. **6–9 June:** One electrofishing raft. The capture count is garbled in the OCR text. The reported 2000 total of 43 implies 8 fish from this trip (inferred, not stated). None were recaptures. This trip was part of the Recovery Program's annual field trip.
2. **19–21 June:** Two rafts captured 18 humpback chub, including 2 recaptures from the June 6–9 trip.
3. **26–28 June:** Two rafts captured 17 humpback chub, with no recaptures.

**Population estimates**

- 2000: 150 to 750 fish, depending on assumptions.
- 1999: about 200 to 400 fish.
- Preliminary assessment: the population may consist of "just a few hundred fish."
- The authors consider a few more years of similar effort likely to produce a worthwhile population estimate.

**Work not completed in 2000**

- Larval and juvenile sampling with [[Seining]] and [[Light Traps]] (planned for early July) was not done. Efforts focused on adults. In 1998, juvenile seining drew sampling but juveniles were difficult to identify in the field.

**Recommendations and plans**

- Consider monitoring that estimates population size, survival, and recruitment, in addition to trend information, when designing the long-range program.
- Consult a statistician and analyze data each year to refine the sampling design for trends and recruitment.
- Submit the final report in January 2001; data submissions are due annually by 15 February.

**Project status and budget**

- Status: ongoing and on track. The budget was reduced from $40.0K to $21.0K in FY-99 and is scheduled to continue through FY-01.
- FY 2000 funds provided: $21.0K. Funds expended: $21.0K. Difference: $0. No Recovery Program funds were spent on publication charges.
