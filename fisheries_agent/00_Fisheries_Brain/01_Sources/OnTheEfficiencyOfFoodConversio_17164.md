# Food Conversion Efficiency of Young Plaice (*Pleuronectes platessa*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pleuronectidae

## Executive Summary

This study by J. A. Colman, published in the *Journal of the Marine Biological Association of the United Kingdom* (1970, vol. 50), measured the food conversion ratio of O-group [[Pleuronectes Platessa]] (European plaice) fed raw, chopped [[Mytilus Edulis]] (blue mussel). Experiments were run in August 1965 and August 1966 at the [[Marine Biological Station, Port Erin]], using wild fish from [[Port Erin Bay]], [[Isle of Man]]. Plaice needed about 5 g of mussel to gain 1 g of body weight, with wide variation among tanks (under 4 g to as much as 7 g). Conversion efficiency did not appear to depend on how much food fish ate, but it was higher at 16 °C than at 13 °C in 1966. The author notes that the 1965 result cannot be compared directly with 1966, because efficiency appeared to vary from year to year.

## Background & Description

**Study species.** The European plaice is a right-eyed [[Flatfish]] of the family Pleuronectidae. The study focused on O-group fish, meaning the first-summer age class.

**Aims.** The experiments had two stated objectives:
1. Determine the weight of chopped mussel needed by O-group plaice to add 1 g of body weight.
2. Test whether the conversion efficiency changes with [[Water Temperature]] and with the amount of food supplied.

**Methods overview.**
- **1965:** Four glass tanks (60 × 30 × 30 cm), each stocked with five wild fish sorted by size (3.3–5.8 cm; 0.34–1.83 g). Water temperature stayed within 0.5 °C of 15 °C. Fish acclimatized for one week, then were fed daily for 10 days. Feeding was about 10% of body weight per day. Fish were anaesthetized with M.S. 222 for weighing and measuring after 24 h without food.
- **1966:** Fish were less carefully sorted by size, and weights were recorded to the nearest 0.02 g. Two controlled-temperature rooms held tanks at 13 °C and 16 °C. Six tanks per room received about 5%, 10%, or 15% of body weight per day, two tanks per level. Feeding ran for 12 days.
- **Conversion ratio** was calculated as weight of food eaten divided by weight gained. Only fish that gained weight were used, on the assumption that the food was eaten by them.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Diet:** Mussel was drained on filter paper (10 min, later reduced to 5 min) before feeding. Plaice nearly always consumed all food offered, usually within 1 h.
- **Temperature:** The 13 °C and 16 °C levels were chosen to lie close to, above, and below the sea temperature at the time.
- **Maintenance requirement:** Extrapolation from [[Dawes 1930]] and the present work suggested that O-group plaice need about 3% of body weight per day just to maintain themselves. Because part of the ration goes to maintenance, the true growth efficiency is higher than the reported ratios.
- **Starvation losses:** Fish that lost weight were assumed to be non-feeders. Their loss rate (about 0.7% per day) did not differ significantly from that of 20 starved fish held at 15 °C (about 1% per day), which supports this assumption.
- **Comparative species:** Literature values span several flatfish and other taxa, including [[Limanda Yokohamae]], [[Kareius Bicoloratus]], and [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*).

## Key Findings

### 1965 experiment (15 °C, 4 tanks, 20 fish)
- 17 of 20 fish gained weight. One fish in one tank and two in another lost weight.
- Overall conversion ratio: **4.0** (23.60 g food for a 5.89 g gain; roughly 25% efficiency).
- Tank ratios were 4.0, 3.9, 3.8, and 4.7. The three larger-fish tanks varied little, while the tank with the smallest fish was less efficient.
- No clear size effect was found across the size range tested.

### 1966 experiment (two temperatures, 12 days)

| Temperature | Fish (fed / total) | Mean conversion ratio | Range of tank means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 °C | 26 / 30 gained | **6.2** | 4.6 to 7.0 |
| 16 °C | 23 / 30 gained | **5.0** | 4.1 to 5.6 |

- At 16 °C, efficiency was higher than at 13 °C, which the author describes as a strong indication.
- Individual variation was likely larger than the tank-level averages suggest.

### Effects of size and food level
- Within these experiments, efficiency could not be shown conclusively to depend on fish size or ration size.
- Comparing fish that all fed, the author suggested efficiency might be lower in larger fish.
- Some support for this comes from [[Hatanaka et Al. 1956]] (flatfish) and from a comparison of plaice across age classes: the ratio rose from 4.0–6.2 in the first summer to 5.8–8.5 in the second (Buckmann, 1952) and to over 10 in the third year (Dawes, 1930, 1931).

### Year-to-year variation
- Results from [[Buckmann 1952]] and Dawes also differed between years. The author therefore advises against directly comparing the 1965 and 1966 results.

### Comparison with other studies on temperature
- [[Baldwin 1956]] found that brook trout efficiency decreased as temperature rose from 9 to 17 °C, which contrasts with the plaice result.
- Hatanaka et al. (1956) on *Limanda yokohamae* found the conversion ratio was 6.2 at 7.7–9.5 °C, 5.5 at 11–15 °C, and 5.0 at 17–21 °C, so efficiency improved with temperature. This agrees with the plaice findings.

### Conclusions
- The conversion ratio for O-group plaice fed chopped mussel in August is about **5 at around 15 °C**.
- Individual fish vary considerably, and efficiency can differ from year to year.
- Both fish size and water temperature appear to influence conversion efficiency.
