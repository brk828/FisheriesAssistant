# Effects of Electrofishing Fields on Juvenile Humpback Chub and Developing Eggs and Early Larvae of Razorback Sucker—A Phase II Study

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species #electrofishing-impacts

## Executive Summary

This research proposal, submitted by Robert T. Muth (Principal Investigator) to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] in December 1993, outlines a Phase II laboratory study to evaluate acute and latent effects of electrofishing on endangered [[Colorado River]] fishes. The study focuses on young-of-year (YOY) and yearling [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and developing embryos and larvae of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). This proposal responds to concerns about potential adverse effects of electrofishing on endangered native fishes during monitoring and collection activities in the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program and the Five-Year [[Flaming Gorge Flow Investigations]] (FGFI) program. The study employs a three-part approach: pilot experiments (Fall 1993-Early Spring 1994), definitive experiments (Spring 1994-Winter 1994/95), and final report preparation (Spring-Early Summer 1995). [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) will serve as a surrogate species when [[Humpback Chub]] specimens are unavailable. The research will test various electrofishing waveforms and voltage gradients under controlled laboratory conditions to identify settings that minimize injury while maintaining collection effectiveness.

## Background & Description

The [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (Upper Colorado Region) recommended a three-phase approach to address concerns over electrofishing impacts on endangered and native fishes of the [[Colorado River System]]. Phase I consisted of a comprehensive literature review and position paper by D.E. Snyder (December 1992) synthesizing existing information on electrofishing effects and methods for reducing adverse impacts. Phase II (this proposal) involves controlled laboratory and/or field experiments to address unanswered questions. Phase III would consist of field experiments to verify Phase II results.

An ad hoc working group convened in Salt Lake City, Utah (summer 1992) set priorities for Phase II studies, focusing on four endangered "big-river" fishes: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The working group included biologists from GCES, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Colorado River Fisheries Project), and the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at [[Colorado State University]].

Within the GCES program, electrofishing of juvenile [[Humpback Chub]] in shoreline habitats of the lower [[Colorado River]], [[Grand Canyon National Park]], has proven successful and is being considered for long-term monitoring. Within the FGFI program, electrofishing over spawning grounds during spawning season is the preferred method for monitoring and capturing adult [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]], Colorado-Utah, as it is believed less stressful than trammel netting. However, repeated exposure to electrofishing fields may injure or stress spawning adults or developing embryos and larvae before they disperse from spawning grounds.

Information on electrical field effects on fish early-life stages (embryo through early juvenile) is sparse and limited primarily to salmonids. The occurrence and significance of harmful effects of electrofishing on fish early-life stages was not well documented in literature reviewed by Snyder (1992).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addresses critical conservation concerns for endangered [[Colorado River]] fishes in two distinct ecological contexts:

**[[Grand Canyon]] Juvenile Habitat**: Electrofishing in shoreline habitats of the lower [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon National Park]] targets young-of-year and yearling [[Humpback Chub]]. Water conditions are hypolimnetically controlled by [[Glen Canyon Dam]], with temperatures of 9-12°C that are fairly constant with slight diel and seasonal fluctuations depending on distance downstream from the dam. Laboratory experiments will replicate these water quality parameters (temperature, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, pH).

**[[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] Spawning Grounds**: Electrofishing over spawning bars in the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] (Colorado-Utah, in and below [[Dinosaur National Monument]]) occurs during the spawning season to monitor adult [[Razorback Sucker]]. Recent research has documented successful reproduction through larval collection. The concern is that repeated electrofishing exposure may harm spawning adults, developing embryos, and larvae before swim-up and dispersal.

The proposal notes that [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) is a very close relative of [[Humpback Chub]] and the most similar non-endangered species in the [[Colorado River Basin]], making it a suitable surrogate when endangered species are unavailable. [[Roundtail Chub]] is common and readily available in upstream portions of the basin. [[Bonytail]] from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] could also serve as a surrogate, though some researchers suggest hatchery fish may be less susceptible to electrofishing injuries.

## Key Findings

**Study Objectives**:
1. Evaluate acute and latent effects of presently used and potentially less harmful electrofishing fields (GCES) on body (spinal and soft-tissue injuries), survival, and growth of young-of-year juvenile (YOY) and possibly yearling [[Humpback Chub]].
2. Evaluate acute and latent effects of presently used and potentially less harmful electrofishing fields (FGFI) on body (spinal and soft-tissue injuries; developmental anomalies), survival, and growth of embryonic and larval [[Razorback Sucker]].

**Study Goal**: Provide information required to establish more definitive policy and guidelines on the use of electrofishing to capture and monitor juvenile [[Humpback Chub]] in the lower [[Colorado River]], [[Grand Canyon National Park]], and adult [[Razorback Sucker]] over spawning grounds in the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]].

**Experimental Design - Part 1 (Pilot Experiments, Fall 1993-Early Spring 1994)**:

*GCES Humpback and Roundtail Chubs*:
- Approximately 60 YOY [[Humpback Chub]] reared from [[Little Colorado River]] stock (15-40 mm TL as of August 30) will be available through [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) by mid-December, to be reared to at least 50-60 mm TL before testing.
- YOY [[Roundtail Chub]] will be collected from rivers of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] as surrogates.
- Fish will be randomly divided into treatment and control groups (10 fish per group, 2 groups per trial, 3 replicates).
- Voltage gradients (volts/cm) at which various behavioral responses (twitch, taxis, narcosis, tetany) occur will be determined.
- Treatment groups will be exposed to predetermined voltage gradient at tetany using [[Coffelt CPS]] equipment in rectangular concrete raceways with full cross-sectional electrodes to ensure homogenous field.
- Water conductivity and temperature will approximate field conditions; fish acclimated 24h prior to treatment.
- Post-testing: immediate and short-term mortalities documented; surviving fish anesthetized, measured, examined for external injuries, frozen, and necropsied for internal injury.
- Analysis: Fisher exact test using 2x2 contingency tables to test association between injuries and electrofishing exposure; comparison between [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Roundtail Chub]].

*FGFI Fathead Minnow Embryos and Early Larvae*:
- [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) eggs from commercial source will be used initially (razorback sucker eggs unavailable until spring 1994).
- 20 viable eggs randomly assigned to replicated treatment and control groups.
- Treatment groups exposed to electrical fields using [[Coffelt VVP-15]] equipment with waveforms and field intensities commonly used to sample [[Razorback Sucker]] over spawning bars.
- Separate trials conducted shortly after fertilization, at organogenesis, and shortly prior to hatching.
- Post-testing: eggs examined for chorion damage; monitored daily during incubation; time of hatch, hatching success, larval growth, development, and survival recorded.
- Analysis: Two-way ANOVA to test effects of electrical field exposure and developmental stage on damaged eggs, hatching success, larval growth, developmental anomalies, and larval survival.

**Experimental Design - Part 2 (Definitive Experiments, Spring 1994-Winter 1994/95)**:

*GCES Humpback and Roundtail Chubs*:
- Preferably AGFD will propagate [[Humpback Chub]]; alternatively [[Roundtail Chub]] or [[Bonytail]] will serve as surrogates.
- YOY chubs randomly divided into replicated treatment and control groups (20 fish per group).
- Four currents/waveforms tested: rectangular 80-Hz PDC with 5-ms pulses (40% duty cycle), rectangular 60-Hz PDC with 4-ms pulses (25% duty cycle), rectangular 30-Hz PDC with 4-ms pulses (12% duty cycle), and [[Coffelt CPS]] (pulse train of three 240-Hz, 1.6-ms pulses every 15th of a second, 7.2% duty cycle).
- Predetermined voltage gradients for three behavioral responses (taxis, narcosis, tetany) will be evaluated for each current/waveform.
- Post-testing: mortalities frozen/preserved, measured, examined; surviving fish anesthetized, measured, examined for external injuries, allowed to recover; random subsamples killed for necropsy; remaining fish reared 2-3 months with survival and growth monitored.
- Analysis: Two-way ANOVA to test effects of voltage gradients and waveforms on injuries, growth, and long-term survival.

*FGFI Razorback Sucker Embryos and Early Larvae*:
- [[Razorback Sucker]] eggs obtained from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]].
- 20 viable eggs randomly assigned to replicated treatment and control groups.
- Same four currents/waveforms tested as for chubs.
- Separate trials conducted shortly after fertilization, at organogenesis, shortly prior to hatching, and with early larvae.
- Post-testing: eggs examined for chorion damage; monitored daily; hatching success, larval growth, development, and survival recorded; surviving larvae anesthetized, measured, examined, reared 1-4 weeks (possibly longer) with survival, growth, and development monitored.
- Analysis: Two-way ANOVA to test effects of treatments and developmental stages; one-way ANOVA to test differences in growth and survival among larvae exposed to different treatments.

**Part 3 (Final Report, Spring-Early Summer 1995)**:
- Thesis prepared by Jack Ruppert for M.S. degree consisting of several chapters, each representing a manuscript suitable for peer-reviewed publication.
- Thesis revised according to [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] requirements and submitted as final report.

**Key Concerns Addressed in Proposal Revisions**:
- Water quality parameters (temperature, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, pH) must reflect riverine conditions, particularly the 9-12°C hypolimnetically controlled temperatures in [[Grand Canyon]].
- Exposure duration to electrical fields and physiological effects (respiratory arrest) will be documented.
- Video documentation of fish behavior during and immediately after exposure will be provided as deliverable.
- Justification for [[Roundtail Chub]] as surrogate: very close relative of [[Humpback Chub]], most similar non-endangered species in [[Colorado River Basin]], common and readily available.
- Disease problems with wild [[Roundtail Chub]] collected in fall attributed to 8°C temperature difference between river and laboratory well water; future collections will use chilling unit and gradual temperature acclimation over 3-week period.
