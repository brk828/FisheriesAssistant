# Growth and Reproduction of Tilapia aurea in Iowa Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This study tested whether [[Tilapia Aurea]] (Cichlidae), a warm-water fish widely cultured in other regions, could grow and reproduce in small [[Iowa Ponds]]. Tilapia were stocked in experimental pens and in several ponds and lakes in 1968 and 1969. Fish were tracked for growth and sampled for reproductive condition. The authors report that the species grew and survived in Iowa waters during the warm season, that males grew faster than females, and that females brooded eggs and fry in their mouths from mid-July into September. Because the fish could not overwinter in Iowa, the species cannot persist there without restocking. The authors did not test whether tilapia control algae or aquatic weeds.

## Background & Description

Tilapia are widely cultured fish in the family [[Cichlidae]]. The authors wanted to see whether the species could be grown in Iowa, where winters are too cold for it to survive in open water. They previously noted that tilapia can survive Iowa winters only if they are held indoors, such as in laboratory tanks. The work was carried out in association with [[Iowa State University]] in Ames, and it drew on fishery management students and the Iowa conservation agency for field support.

The study sites included:

- **Experimental pens** in a pond, stocked on dates from late May into June 1969 and sampled biweekly through September 1969.
- **[[Lost Lake]]**, a 0.93 ha (2.3 acre) pond in [[Ledges State Park]], which is usually less than 1 m deep in fall and winter.
- **[[Lake LaVerne]]**, an artificial lake on the Iowa State University campus with a maximum depth of about 2.4 m. Goldfish and sunfish were abundant there.
- **[[Bailey's Pond]]**, a 0.29 ha (0.72 acre) pond northeast of Ames with a maximum depth of about 4 m. It receives runoff from fertile cropland.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Surface water temperatures in the pens were 23–29 °C from July 15 to September 2. They dropped to 21.9 °C by September 9.

Other species shared the water with tilapia. [[Channel Catfish]] were stocked alongside tilapia in Bailey's Pond. [[Black Bullhead]] were the only fish in that pond when tilapia and catfish were introduced. Earlier largemouth bass and bluegill stocking had been lost to winterkill.

Aquatic vegetation was common. Coontail, duckweed, and waterweed became very abundant in Lost Lake during the summer. Lake LaVerne had had a plant problem that was controlled with Aquathol in 1968.

Population density in the pens did not affect fish condition. Body condition, measured as the [[Condition Factor]] (K), was similar across density treatments.

## Key Findings

### Growth

- **Sex-based growth difference:** Males grew faster than females and had higher condition factors. Growth in the pens slowed sharply for most females after mid-July. The authors attributed this partly to the intensive breeding behavior of the males.
- **Territorial aggression:** In a 38-liter aquarium on July 25, 1968, two males fought, and one died. Nearly all scales on both sides of the loser's body behind the fins had been pecked off. No scale loss from fighting was seen in the pens.
- **Escapes:** Three males escaped from pen 4 between August 12 and 26. The remaining female in that pen then grew rapidly.

### Condition Factor

Condition factor was computed as weight in grams divided by the cube of standard length in millimeters.

- The mean K for fish in August and September was reported as 3.?? (values partly unreadable in the source) with a range of 2.73–4.45 for females in that period.
- In June and July, mean K was 3.55 (range 3.09–4.45).
- Condition increased with length in August and September but not earlier.
- Females tending fry had the lowest mean K, 3.48. Females with eggs had a mean K of about 3.69, and females without eggs had a mean K of 3.79.

### Reproduction and Brood Care

- **Nest building:** One male that had escaped from a pen was observed building a nest on August 28 and 29. The nest was in water about 14 cm deep, 12 cm deep from rim to base, and about 35 cm wide. When a person approached, the male left the nest briefly, performed a lateral display, then moved to deeper water until the intruder left.
- **Mouthbrooding:** Females carried eggs or fry in their mouths from July 15 through September 9 (see [[Mouthbrooding]]). Egg-carrying females were found in pens throughout the sampling period. Females carrying young had no food in their guts, which is consistent with brood care.
- **Breeding without males:** One of two females in pen 2 incubated eggs on August 12 and September 9, even though no males were in that pen.
- **Smallest breeder:** The smallest female found incubating eggs was 97 mm long and weighed 31 g.
- **Gonad condition:** Females brooding eggs had the smallest eggs in their gonads. Females that had finished brooding had the largest eggs. Digestive tracts were empty in 15 of 15 females brooding eggs, while females brooding young contained food.

### Yearling Growth in Ponds

- **[[Lost Lake]]:** Fifteen tilapia were stocked in June 1968. Eight fish were recovered after a rotenone treatment on October 8, 1968. They measured 229–264 mm in total length, averaging 249 mm and 299 g. This was an increase of 96 mm and 230 g over the average stocked size. The average daily weight increment in Lost Lake was 1.94 g per day.
- **[[Lake LaVerne]]:** Seventy-five tilapia were stocked from May 31 to June 4, 1968. Fish were recovered with electric shocking, seines, gill nets, and traps in September 1968 and 1969. Fish taken September 2–19, 1968 measured 201–264 mm, averaging 242 mm and 299 g, an increase of 110 mm and 244 g. Three females taken September 18–23, 1969 measured 187–204 mm, averaging 196 mm. Two males measured 229–264 mm and averaged 248 mm. The average daily increment in Lake LaVerne in 1969 was 2.71 g.
- **[[Bailey's Pond]]:** Tilapia and channel catfish were stocked from May 29 to June 8, 1969. Tilapia were held in wire pens, but some escaped into the pond in June and mid-August. Seven females collected September 25 to October 1, 1969 measured 196–222 mm and averaged 208 mm and 170 g. Six males measured 205–288 mm. Females were significantly smaller than males.

### Population Estimate in Bailey's Pond

A fishery management class conducted a marking and recapture estimate of the fish population in Bailey's Pond on September 27, 1969. Fish were sampled with rotenone, and the estimate was corrected for vegetation and sampling effort. The fish population was estimated at 2,000–2,500 fish per hectare.

### Length–Weight Relationship

A length–weight relationship was fitted to 180 young tilapia (range 42–145 mm total length) from Bailey's Pond. The slope of 3.33 was tested and found significantly above 3.0. The authors took this as evidence that young tilapia were becoming more robust as they grew.

### Conclusions

- Tilapia aurea can grow and survive in Iowa ponds during the warm season.
- The species cannot overwinter in open Iowa waters.
- Males outgrow females, and females brood eggs and fry in their mouths during the summer.
- The authors did not test whether tilapia can control algae or aquatic weeds.
