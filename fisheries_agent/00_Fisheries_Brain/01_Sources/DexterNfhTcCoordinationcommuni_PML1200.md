# Dexter NFH & TC coordination/communication re Region 2 fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-culture

## Executive Summary

This 1992 internal memorandum from the Director of the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to the Regional Director of [[Region 2 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in [[Albuquerque New Mexico]] documents how Dexter staff have coordinated with Region 2 on the maintenance of Region 2 fish. It describes the communication channels used, the annual coordination meetings, and the capacity constraints that forced the hatchery to hold fish from more than one program in shared ponds. The memo is a defensive account of past practice rather than a research report. It states that the hatchery has kept Region 2 informed, has been open about its limits, and may have been too accommodating to cooperators.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service memorandum, dated March 1992 (the day is garbled in the scan).
- **Author:** [[Buddy L. Jansen]], Director, Dexter NFH, Dexter, New Mexico.
- **Addressee:** Regional Director, Region 2, Albuquerque, New Mexico.
- **Subject:** Coordination and communication between Dexter NFH and Region 2 concerning Region 2 fish.
- **Personnel named:** Author and [[Roger Hamman]] (Dexter staff); [[Holt]], [[Frank Pfeifer]], and Lea Milla (Region 2 contacts); [[Jim Hutcheson]] and [[George Divine]] (present at some formal meetings).
- **Disclaimer:** The author states that an exact record of times, places, circumstances, and personnel is not possible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo is not a biological study, so it gives little direct ecological data. Its operational context is relevant, however:

- **Shared rearing ponds:** Fish for [[Upper Basin Programs]] were often held in a single pond, and the memo admits that some Region 2 staff were uncomfortable with this arrangement.
- **Capacity limits:** The author says that "pond space and water" were critical limiting factors. Separate rearing for each upper-basin species would have required more ponds and water.
- **Disease management:** The memo notes that newly introduced fish were infected with [[Anchor Worm]] (*Lernaea*, as written in the text). The whole population then had to be treated again, which shows how pond sharing spreads disease risk.
- **Hatchery-to-hatchery sharing:** Dexter also held two species for another region in one pond when capacity was exhausted. Holding decisions were left to cooperators.

## Key Findings

1. **Communication was frequent and mostly informal.** Telephone contact was the primary channel. The author and Holt kept a regular dialogue on the status of the "F-6" fish. Region 2 staff also called Dexter often. Telephone logs were said to document this.
2. **Pond sharing was a known, routine condition.** Routine updates told Region 2 the fish were held together in one pond, even though this was not discussed as a standing issue. The author states that the arrangement was disclosed "up front" from the beginning of the program.
3. **Annual coordination meetings were attempted.** For more than three years, the hatchery tried to hold a formal annual meeting with Region 2. The meetings were held in both regions and were sometimes scheduled around the "upper basin researchers meeting" to save travel costs. One meeting was held at Dexter.
4. **Capacity was the main constraint.** At a meeting in [[Grand Junction Colorado]] three or four years earlier, the "only one pond" arrangement was explained in detail. Upper-basin program commitments limited pond space and water.
5. **Shared holding was a deliberate trade-off.** When a facility was "maxed out," the hatchery had to choose between holding two or more species together or leaving one out. The memo says cooperators were told this in advance and could decide for themselves.
6. **Self-criticism and stated intent.** The author admits that the hatchery should have said "NO" more often when cooperators asked to bring in fish. He says Dexter has gone to great lengths to meet the needs of this critical program.
