# Evolution of Lake Whitefish in North America during the Pleistocene: Genetic Differentiation between Sympatric Populations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Bodaly]] and colleagues (Department of Fisheries and Oceans, Freshwater Institute, Winnipeg, with the University of Manitoba) uses starch gel [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]] of dehydrogenase enzymes to test the genetic relationships of sympatric populations of [[Lake Whitefish]] ([[Coregonus Clupeaformis]]). It provides isozyme evidence for an [[Acadian Race]], a glacial refuge race surviving [[Wisconsinan Glaciation]] on the exposed coastal plain of northeastern North America. Allele frequencies differed significantly between sympatric forms in most pairs, indicating they are wholly or substantially reproductively isolated. Sympatric pairs in the Yukon, Ontario and Labrador were genetically typical of their regional glacial races, which suggests a postglacial origin, though an older origin is possible if recent gene flow is masking divergence. Geographic races, by contrast, differ substantially in genetic distance while showing little ecological or morphological divergence.

## Background & Description

- **Study organism:** Lake whitefish is a northern North American coregonid whose species complex occurs across most of the northern half of the continent, including Alaska, most of Canada and parts of New England.
- **Race structure:** The complex was previously considered to comprise about five geographic races or subspecies that diverged through isolation in different glacial refugia. Earlier work recognized the Bering and Mississippi–Missouri races. [[Nahanni Race]] was described by [[Foote]] et al. (1992).
- **Sympatric forms:** Within each race there is (or was) at least one instance of sympatric forms in individual lakes. Examples include high and low [[Gill Raker]] forms in Yukon lakes, a dwarf and normal pair in Ontario and Maine, and the Ossokmanuan Reservoir pair in Labrador.
- **Prior work:** [[Lindsey]] et al. (1970) used three protein systems and found that one sympatric pair each in the Yukon, Ontario and Maine did not share a common origin. The present paper extends this by adding loci and sympatric pairs, including newly discovered pairs in Labrador and Ontario and three additional pairs in the Yukon.
- **Rationale:** The authors argue that genetic markers are preferable to morphological or behavioural traits for phylogenetic inference because they are less prone to convergent or parallel evolution.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Glacial history:** The races are interpreted as descendants of fish that survived Wisconsinan glaciation in separate refugia: [[Bering Race]] (Bering refugium), [[Mississippi-Missouri Race]] (Mississippi–Missouri refugium), [[Nahanni Race]] (Nahanni refugium) and [[Atlantic Race]] (Atlantic refugium). The [[Acadian Race]] is presumed to derive from the Northeastern Banks refugium.
- **Acadian distribution:** Present in New England, the Gaspé Peninsula of Québec, New Brunswick and Nova Scotia.
- **Hydrographic history:** [[Tempelman-Kluit]] (1980) proposed that pre-Pleistocene drainage in southwestern Yukon differed markedly from today. The Alsek River basin, which contains [[Dezadeash Lake]], may have acted as a subrefuge in which the high raker form developed, with isolation from the Squanga Creek watershed persisting into the Pliocene and early Pleistocene.
- **Introgression zones:** Geographic barriers limit allele exchange in places. The Liard Canyon blocks movement of Nahanni alleles into the Bering race, and Mississippi–Missouri alleles have not moved up the Peel River. Introgression is evident in the Mackenzie Delta and at the Liard–Mackenzie confluence at Fort Simpson, and Alberta populations may be intergrades.
- **Sympatry distribution:** In Maine, sympatric pairs are known from at least 22 lakes, compared with only 10 instances over the rest of the species' range.

## Key Findings

### Methods and sampling
- 119 lake whitefish populations from across North America were sampled between 1968 and 1986.
- Sympatric pairs included Dezadeash, Squanga, Little Teslin and Teenah lakes (Yukon, high and low gill raker forms), Como Lake (Ontario, dwarf and normal forms), Ossokmanuan Reservoir (Labrador), and Cliff Lake (Maine, dwarf and normal forms). Allele frequencies at [[LDH-B-2 Locus]] from Musquacook Lake, Maine, were taken from [[Kirkpatrick]] and [[Selander]] (1979).
- The enzyme products of 15 genetic loci for four dehydrogenase systems were examined: [[Glycerol-3-Phosphate Dehydrogenase]], [[Isocitrate Dehydrogenase]], [[Lactate Dehydrogenase]] and [[Malate Dehydrogenase]].
- Sample sizes per population and enzyme ranged from 2 to 518 and averaged 46. Como Lake was sampled with 50 fish of each form.
- Published data from several earlier studies were pooled in the analysis.

### Locus-level results
- **LDH-B:** All fish were fixed for the same LDH-B-1* allele. Most populations carried LDH-B-2*b at frequencies usually above 0.75. This allele was absent from Yukon and northern British Columbia. LDH-B-2*d occurred only at Grand Lake, New Brunswick.
- **LDH-A:** All fish were fixed at LDH-A-1*. At LDH-A-2*, the a allele predominated (0.83 to 1.0), with no discernible geographic pattern.
- **G3PDH-1:** Central populations showed moderate frequencies of a, b and d alleles. The b allele was missing from the Yukon, southwestern Northwest Territories and all of British Columbia. The a allele was absent from Maine and New Brunswick. Both Como Lake forms lacked the d allele, and this was not attributed to small sample size.
- **G3PDH-2:** The a allele predominated (0.46 to 1.0) with no geographic pattern.
- **sMDH-A-1,2 and sMDH-B-1,2:** Central and eastern populations were fixed for sMDH-A a. Yukon populations carried sMDH-A b at low to moderate frequency. sMDH-B b was absent from all Yukon populations.
- **mMDH:** Duplicated mMDH-3* varied. Central populations were fixed for a. Many Yukon populations carried mMDH-3*b, and one of four Yukon pairs was polymorphic for it.
- **sIDHP-3:** Central populations carried moderate frequencies of d. The c allele occurred only in Yukon populations. The a allele was much rarer in Maine and New Brunswick.
- **sIDHP-4:** Yukon, British Columbia, Maine and New Brunswick populations were largely fixed for a, while c was found in the centre of the range.

### Sympatric pair differentiation
- Every sympatric pair showed significant allele frequency differences between forms at one or more loci.
- Most Yukon populations were in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, except for low raker populations at the two G3PDH loci. The authors state this is unexplained and not due to a fault in the genetic model.
- In Dezadeash, Squanga and Little Teslin lakes, high and low raker forms differed at sIDHP-3*. Squanga Lake pairs also differed at G3PDH-1*, and Teenah Lake pairs at sMDH-A-1,2*.

### Genetic distances (Nei 1978)
- Mean distance between races, based on Table 2 comparisons:
  - Bering vs Nahanni: 0.15 (24 comparisons)
  - Bering vs Mississippi–Missouri: 0.17 (24 comparisons)
  - Nahanni vs Mississippi–Missouri: 0.05 (16 comparisons)
- [[Bodaly]] et al. (1991b), using 37 loci, estimated 0.10 between Mississippi–Missouri and Bering race fish.
- Mean distance between Yukon sympatric populations was only 0.015. Como Lake pairs were below 0.01.

### Race interpretation
- Allozyme data support four genetically definable races: Bering, Nahanni, Mississippi–Missouri and Acadian.
- The Acadian race differs from Mississippi–Missouri by missing alleles at sIDHP-4* and G3PDH-1*, by distinct sIDHP-3* frequencies, and by unique alleles in two populations.
- Allozymes do not support a distinct Atlantic race separate from Mississippi–Missouri. The authors note that mtDNA ([[Bernatchez]] and [[Dodson]], 1991) provides finer geographic resolution, so the two datasets are not contradictory.

### Reproductive isolation and divergence
- No sympatric populations were found where the Nahanni, Bering or Mississippi–Missouri races have come together.
- The authors argue that sympatric pairs in Maine reflect an allopatric origin followed by reproductive isolation, in contrast to the interpretation by Bernatchez and Dodson (1990). They conclude that sympatric forms in the Bering and Mississippi–Missouri races arose within those races.
- Sympatric pairs differ in gill rakers, feeding, growth and age at maturity but show little genetic divergence. Geographic races show large genetic divergence but little ecological or morphological difference. The authors conclude that allozyme divergence is a poor predictor of reproductive isolation.
- Forms commonly spawn at different times or places. The Como Lake pair shows no allele frequency difference but does differ in mtDNA clone frequencies.

### Origin and timing
- If the Yukon sympatric pairs are postglacial, their divergence is post-Pleistocene. An older origin cannot be ruled out if recent gene flow is masking divergence.
- The Dezadeash pair is genetically more distant than the Squanga pairs, even though the Dezadeash forms are morphologically most distinct and probably lack current gene flow. This makes an older divergence seem unlikely, but it is not excluded.
- The high raker form in the Yukon is probably polyphyletic, a pattern also seen in vendace ([[Vuorinen]] et al. 1981) and Arctic char ([[Hindar]] et al. 1986).

### Limitations and inconsistencies
- The Methods describe 15 loci, while the Discussion refers to "all 11 polymorphic loci" in the present work.
- The authors acknowledge that the Acadian–Mississippi–Missouri contact zone in Maine cannot be resolved with allozymes because of high interpopulation variation and because the distinctive Atlantic alleles occur in only two populations.
- The authors suggest that other genetic techniques may test the alternative divergence hypotheses.
