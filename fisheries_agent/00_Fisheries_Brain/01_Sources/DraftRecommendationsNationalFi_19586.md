# National Fish Hatchery Review Panel Draft Recommendations

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #policy #hatchery-operations

## Executive Summary

This draft document presents recommendations from the National Fish Hatchery Review Panel (McIntyre, Rinne, Minckley, Williams) dated September 23, 1974, addressing the role of federal fish hatcheries within a National Fisheries Program ([[NFP]]). The panel identified significant challenges including low morale among hatchery personnel, lack of clear advocacy from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] ([[FWS]]) Directorate, and confusion about hatchery roles in emerging [[Ecosystem Management]] approaches. The recommendations emphasize integrating hatcheries as tools within ecosystem-level management scenarios, focusing on indigenous taxa and genetic conservation, and clarifying federal versus state responsibilities for recreational fishing enhancement.

## Background & Description

The panel faced difficulty formulating recommendations without clear knowledge of needs, goals, and objectives for a National Fisheries Program. Key observations included:

- Low morale among hatchery personnel from administrators to entry-level employees
- Recent external criticisms of hatcheries coupled with apparent dismantling of existing NFP
- Confusion and defensive postures among staff
- Expectations for greater adaptability and performance despite reduced budgets, staff reductions, and aging facilities with deferred maintenance
- Need for better communication about present and future roles in [[Ecosystem Management]]

The panel identified several potential roles for hatcheries in ecosystem-level approaches to management of fish and wildlife resources. The review was considered timely as ecosystem-level management scenarios were being proposed and planned.

### Core Assumptions

The panel based deliberations on several assumptions:

- **Ecosystem Integration**: Ecosystem-level scenarios integrating terrestrial and aquatic components would be the rule in future NFP management operations
- **Indigenous Focus**: Emphasis on establishment, re-establishment, and enhancement of biological resources would include concern for indigenous taxa and their genetic characteristics
- **Hatcheries as Tools**: Hatcheries are tools of the NFP, not an end in themselves
- **Primary Uses**: Hatchery uses include: (a) establish new fisheries and re-establish/enhance damaged fisheries; (b) aid in recovery of [[Threatened and Endangered Species]] ([[T&E]]); (c) satisfy federal obligations (mitigation, Native American responsibilities, treaties)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document emphasizes a paradigm shift toward ecosystem-level management with specific ecological considerations:

### Genetic and Population Integrity

- All fish stocking should be consistent with approved ecosystem management plans assessing competitive, predative, genetic and other effects on indigenous biota
- Stocking should not reduce natural genetic diversity or fitness of existing fish stocks
- Fish from indigenous populations should be used as broodstock
- Care must be taken to avoid swamping indigenous genetic stocks with hatchery-produced progeny

### Indigenous Species Protection

- Standards should be established to ensure quality (health, fitness, wildness) of stocked fishes
- Pre-stocking and post-stocking evaluations of ecological and genetic performance should be routinely required
- Hatchery staffing should include individuals specifically trained for evaluation duties

### Disease Management

Use of broodfish from indigenous populations requires substantial knowledge of health status of wild fish. [[Fish Disease Center]] personnel must be involved in evaluations and selection of broodfish to control spread of unwanted or unnatural diseases.

### Threatened and Endangered Species

The panel recommended FWS consider captive protection and experimental propagation of selected candidate species before listing to assure:
- Availability of adequate numbers
- Genetic variability for future broodstock
- Culture expertise if propagation decision is made
- NFP, hatchery, and fish-health personnel involvement from outset when taxon or stock is brought into captivity

## Key Findings

### I. Role of Hatcheries in Defined NFP

**Recommendation 1**: FWS should review entire NFP and produce guiding document delineating goals and objectives for:
- Management of aquatic ecosystems
- Integration of aquatic and terrestrial ecosystem components into ecosystem-level management units
- Modification and incorporation of existing management tools (fish hatcheries) into overall programs

**Recommendation 2**: FWS should evaluate each current hatchery to determine compatibility with NFP ecosystem-management vision statements, intended purpose, and general efficiency. Evaluation criteria:
- Current role consistency with FWS goals for ecosystem-level management
- Capacity to produce needed quantities and qualities of fish and services
- Flexibility for adaptation to non-traditional species (T&E)
- Physical condition and potential for modification to "state of the art" without major appropriation
- Potential for cost-effective and efficient modification

### II. Adaptability & Utility of Existing Facilities

**Recommendation 3**: Upon NFP review completion, ascertain quantities and qualities of species, stocks, strains, races and other services required to support ecosystem-level projects for each hatchery in each FWS region.

**Recommendation 4**: Any unit failing to meet tests should be evaluated as potentially excess to NFP needs. Consider use for research and development tied to [[FWS Technology Centers]] or consolidation with [[National Biological Survey]] research activities. Units not needed should be candidates for transfer to states, leasing, or closure. Begin immediate work toward political agreements for divestiture over reasonable time period.

### III. Funding and Administration

**Recommendation 5**: Hatchery budgeting should focus on long-term ecosystem-level management needs rather than specific hatcheries responding to political advocates. This assists hatcheries becoming integral components in Regional ecosystem-level management scenarios. FWS should:
- Increase efforts to involve interested partners and other agencies
- Create customer-service attitude
- Plan, develop, and administer programs and budgets as part of Regional NFP program
- Limit Washington hatchery staff to levels necessary for overall coordination, information management, oversight, policy guidance, and Congressional liaison

### IV. Integrating NFP Operations: Recreational Fishing, Mitigation, and Trust Responsibilities

**Recommendation 6**: Provision for recreational fishing is not a federal responsibility unless part of mitigation, Native American responsibilities, or treaty. No private water should be stocked with federally produced fish except special circumstances under approved plans involving T&E species.

**Recommendation 7**: Enhancement of fish populations beyond natural production levels for recreational opportunity is not federal responsibility. States manage recreational fishing. Requests from federal hatcheries should be accompanied by willingness to assume production costs under approved ecosystem management guidelines. FWS should not use hatcheries to meet these requests if it prevents accomplishment of T&E species work, fishery restoration, etc.

**Recommendation 8**: When federally produced fish are required for newly-created waters, program should be compatible with NFP ecosystem management vision statements and should end when self-sustaining populations are established.

**Recommendation 9**: Mitigation hatcheries should be critically examined to determine if:
- Original goals are being achieved
- Conditions have changed making original goals cross-purposes with ecosystem management
- Facilities created for mitigation are being operated at FWS expense
- Current programs are directed toward mitigation
- Project sponsors and beneficiaries are meeting funding commitments

FWS should initiate actions to restore or modify commitments and funding responsibilities for operating and evaluation costs, plus expenses to correct failed mitigation efforts.

### V. Integrating NFP Operations: T&E and Other Indigenous Species

**Recommendation 10**: All fish stocking should be consistent with approved ecosystem management plans including assessment of competitive, predative, genetic and other effects on indigenous biota. Federal Aid project objectives involving hatchery fish should be compatible with NFP ecosystem-management vision statements.

**Recommendation 11**: Establish standards to ensure quality (health, fitness, wildness) of fishes stocked for recreation, federal obligations for mitigation, trust responsibilities, T&E recovery efforts, or other purposes.

**Recommendation 12**: Stocking should not reduce natural genetic diversity or fitness of existing fish stocks. At minimum, use fish from indigenous populations as broodstock and avoid swamping indigenous genetic stocks with hatchery-produced progeny.

**Recommendation 13**: Require routine pre-stocking and post-stocking evaluations of projected and realized ecological and genetic performance of hatchery-produced fish in achieving program goals (returns, harvest, survival to spawning, impacts on indigenous species). Hatchery staffing should include individuals specifically trained and assigned to evaluation duties.

**Recommendation 14**: FWS should consider captive protection and experimental propagation of selected candidate species before listing to assure availability of adequate numbers, genetic variability for future broodstock, and culture expertise. NFP, hatchery, and fish-health personnel should all be involved from outset.

**Recommendation 15**: Use of broodfish from indigenous populations requires substantial knowledge of health status of wild fish. Fish Disease Center personnel must be involved in evaluations and selection of broodfish. Temporarily postpone decision to discontinue fish-health services to non-federal clients until state and private capabilities are in place. When available, Fish Health Centers should decline assistance and refer requests to those parties.

### VI. Political Considerations: Federal, State, and Public Interactions

**Recommendation 16**: Any changes in FWS policy regarding provision or use of federally produced fish should occur only after states are fully informed well in advance. Allow adequate lead time (no more than two years) for states to prepare necessary political and monetary support. Negotiate phase-in from full support through shared costs through full state takeover.

### VII. Shortfalls: Education, Information Dissemination, and Protected Classes

**Recommendation 17**: Aggressively pursue knowledge and technology gained in FWS hatchery efforts with outside groups. Encourage personnel to share expertise through publication in scientific and trade journals, newsletters, workshops. Credit such endeavor through merit recognition system. Include publication requirements for hatchery-based investigations as part of job descriptions.

**Recommendation 18**: Public and political perceptions of federal hatchery mission are inconsistent with FWS views. Most public sees hatchery role as producing fish, with more being better. FWS reports, news releases, and public education continue to perpetuate "we raise fish" concept. New outreach and education approach required, emphasizing hatchery role as vital support to resource managers in ecosystem stewardship.

**Recommendation 19**: Minority and female representation among hatchery employees is well below national population figures. Problems in recruitment and retention require attention. If FWS Director lacks grant authority to support student expenses for summer interns, seek congressional authorization. If authority exists, use it.

### VIII. Considerations for Aquaculture

**Recommendation 20**: Private aquaculturists desire greater access to healthy stocks and strains maintained in federal broodstock registry. If procedures on acquisition of excess stock can be developed, producers are willing to pay for fish or eggs. They should be allowed to do so if operations comply with ecosystem-level management goals and objectives.
