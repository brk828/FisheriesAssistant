# Lake Mohave Monitoring, March 1999

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #fisheries-monitoring

## Executive Summary
This report summarizes a fish monitoring survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel, with support from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation and volunteers, in [[Lake Mohave]] near Carp Cove, Arizona/Nevada, from 16–19 March 1999. Trammel nets were deployed across 16 net-nights (26.72 standardized units) in shallow littoral habitat (1–5 m deep) along shorelines and gravel spits. A total of 184 fish representing seven species were captured, dominated by [[Common Carp]] (37.0%), [[Striped Bass]] (31.5%), and the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (22.8%). Special attention was given to the Razorback Sucker population, with individuals scanned for PIT tags, measured, sexed, and assessed for health and reproductive condition. Of 42 total Razorback Sucker captures, 22 (52%) were recaptures, and 8 of these (19% of total catch) were identified as repatriated (previously stocked) fish released at various coves between October 1992 and November 1996.

## Background & Description
### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Total catch:** 42 individuals (22.8% of total catch)
- **Sex ratio:** 26 female, 11 male, 5 undetermined sex
- **Mean Total Length (TL):**
  - Females: 61.0 cm (range 47.0–68.6 cm, n = 20)
  - Males: 53.8 cm (range 41.9–58.4 cm, n = 6)
  - Undetermined: 51.1 cm (508 mm and 514 mm, n = 2)
- **Recapture rates by sex:**
  - Females: 13 of 26 (50%)
  - Males: 8 of 11 (72.7%)
  - Undetermined: 1 of 5 (20.0%)
  - Overall: 22 of 42 (52%)
- **Health/condition observations** (from field notes): tuberculate skin, fungal infections, *Lernaea* (anchor worm) infestations, cloudy eyes (unilateral and bilateral), blindness, torn or damaged opercula, missing eyes, fin/caudal damage, and "calico" pigmentation patterns. Several fish were noted as "ripe" (expressing reproductive products), indicating active spawning condition during the survey period.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat sampled:** Shallow shoreline habitat (1–5 m depth) and gravel spits at cove mouths within [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the lower [[Colorado River]] system spanning Arizona and Nevada.
- **Community composition:** The catch reflects a mixed assemblage of non-native sport/forage fishes ([[Common Carp]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Rainbow Trout]]) co-occurring with the native, federally endangered [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Conservation context:** The presence of repatriated Razorback Suckers—individuals released as part of active stocking/reintroduction efforts at Dandy, North Chemeuheve, Placer, and Yuma coves (Oct 1992–Nov 1996)—demonstrates ongoing population augmentation and monitoring under the [[Lake Mohave Native Fish Work Group]] program.
- **Reproductive activity:** Documentation of "ripe" individuals expressing reproductive products suggests the survey period (March) coincides with the species' spawning season in this reservoir system.
- **Health threats observed:** Parasitism (*Lernaea* sp., anchor worm), fungal infections, ocular clouding/blindness, and physical injuries (opercular damage, fin/caudal tears) were commonly noted among captured Razorback Suckers, indicating chronic environmental or biotic stressors within the lake population.

## Key Findings
- Total catch across 16 net-nights (26.72 standardized units) was 184 fish across seven species.
- [[Common Carp]] (37.0%), [[Striped Bass]] (31.5%), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (22.8%) together comprised over 90% of the total catch; remaining species ([[Channel Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Bluegill]]) each contributed less than 5%.
- Of 42 Razorback Suckers captured, over half (52%) were recaptures based on PIT tag scanning, indicating an established, actively monitored resident/repatriate population.
- 8 recaptured individuals (19% of total catch; 36% of all recaptures) were confirmed repatriates released between October 1992 and November 1996 at four different coves, demonstrating multi-year survival of stocked fish.
- Sex ratio among sexed Razorback Suckers was skewed female-biased (26F:11M), with females also averaging larger body size (61.0 cm TL) than males (53.8 cm TL).
- Widespread signs of parasitism (*Lernaea* sp.), fungal infection, and ocular/physical damage were recorded among Razorback Suckers, warranting continued health monitoring.
- A more detailed analysis of repatriate recapture data was noted as forthcoming from the authors ([[Paul C. Marsh]] and [[W.L. Minckley]], Arizona State University).
