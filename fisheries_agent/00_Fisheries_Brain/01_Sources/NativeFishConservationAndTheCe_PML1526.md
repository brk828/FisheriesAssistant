# Desert Fishes Council Proceedings (2000): Roundtail Chub Life History, Native Fish Conservation, and Larval Fish Drift

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #freshwater

## Executive Summary

This volume of the [[Desert Fishes Council]] proceedings (2000 symposium, published November 13, 2001) contains three papers on native fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]] and the [[Gila River Basin]]. The text is bilingual (Spanish abstracts followed by English abstracts). The three studies cover:

1. **Life history of [[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*) in the [[Yampa River Canyon]], Colorado, a study using preliminary 1998 age and maturity data.
2. **Funding and management for native fish conservation** under the [[Central Arizona Project]] Biological Opinion, reporting project spending and status through autumn 2000.
3. **Larval fish drift in [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona**, combining a diffusion model built with neutrally buoyant gel beads and March–May 2000 field collections.

The papers are linked by a shared concern for imperiled southwestern fishes and by the need for basic ecological data on species that are poorly known.

## Background & Description

### Gila robusta complex and Roundtail Chub

The *Gila robusta* complex includes three large, morphologically variable cyprinid species native to the Colorado River Basin: *G. cypha*, *G. elegans*, and *G. robusta*. In the Yampa Canyon, *G. cypha* and *G. elegans* are federally listed as endangered. *G. elegans* is probably extirpated from the Yampa River. *G. robusta* is not federally listed and is relatively common, but it is a candidate for listing.

Little is known about the life cycle of any member of the complex, including age, growth, age at first reproduction, and secondary sexual characters such as breeding tubercles. The authors therefore propose *G. robusta* as a surrogate species for understanding the other two.

### Biological Opinion and native fish species

The [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) issued a 1994 Biological Opinion on transferring Colorado River water to the Gila River Basin through the [[Central Arizona Project]], operated by the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (BR). The opinion found that the project would jeopardize the continued existence of four native fishes and would adversely modify critical habitat for three of them:

- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)

### Larval drift methods

The drift study used neutrally buoyant gel beads as a surrogate for larval fish. Their dispersal was modeled with a one-dimensional advection-diffusion equation. Field collections of drifting larval fishes were compared qualitatively with the model.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Yampa River Canyon:** The *G. robusta* study took place in the canyon reach of the Yampa River within [[Dinosaur National Park]], Colorado. Sampling there was limited by a restricted seasonal window, which affected how annual growth marks could be validated.
- **Gila River Basin:** Federal recovery funding targets the conservation of native fishes and the control of non-native fishes. Piscicides that target a single taxon and the protection of key recovery areas are among the priority actions.
- **Aravaipa Creek:** This desert stream was the site of the drift study. The authors examined larval fish ecology, including species composition, drift density, and diel (day–night) periodicity, in addition to dispersal patterns.

## Key Findings

### 1. Age, growth, and maturity of Roundtail Chub (Yampa Canyon)

- **Sample:** Fish were collected in July 1998 and 1999 by hook and line and by electrofishing. Only the 1998 results are reported because analyses were still in progress.
- **Size range:** Standard lengths were 90–365 mm.
- **Ageing methods:** Age was estimated from otoliths and opercular bones, and scales were also used.
- **Age range:** Assuming one annulus per year on otoliths, fish were 2 to 21 years old.
- **Method agreement:** Opercular-bone ages generally matched otolith ages. Scale-based ages were lower than otolith ages for fish older than eight years.
- **Validation:** Marginal increment analysis was rejected because of the limited seasonal window. Annulus validation is planned by tracking the ages of modal length groups collected in later years.
- **Maturity (preliminary):** Gonadal maturation occurred at about eight years in females and seven years in males.
- **Breeding tubercles:** Detailed drawings were made for eight fish (five males and three females). Both sexes develop tubercles on the head, back, and pectoral fin rays. The authors report a direct relationship between tubercle development and gonadal development, though they note that more data are needed.

### 2. Conservation funding under the Central Arizona Project

- **Mandated transfer:** The reasonable and prudent alternative requires an annual transfer of $500,000 for 25 years from the Bureau of Reclamation to the USFWS.
- **Funds transferred:** Through autumn 2000, a total of $1.5 million had been transferred over three fiscal years.
- **Projects funded:** Funds were allocated to 58 individual or continuation projects with a combined cost of $1.73 million.
- **Project status (of the 58):** Ten projects were completed, nine were deleted, and four were underway. The remaining projects, which by subtraction number about 35, had not yet been contracted.
- **Next fiscal year:** Five additional projects, with a combined value of $589,000, had been identified.
- **Selection priority:** Projects that benefit all native fishes receive the highest priority. Selection is guided by a long-term guidance document and by a multi-agency technical team working with regional experts.

### 3. Larval fish drift in Aravaipa Creek

- **Method:** Neutrally buoyant gel beads served as surrogates for larval fish. Their downstream spread was approximated with a one-dimensional diffusion-convection equation.
- **Field sampling:** Drifting larval fishes were collected from March through May 2000 and compared qualitatively with the model's predictions.
- **Outcomes studied:** Species composition, drift densities, and diel periodicity were examined alongside dispersal patterns. The abstract does not report quantitative results for these outcomes.
