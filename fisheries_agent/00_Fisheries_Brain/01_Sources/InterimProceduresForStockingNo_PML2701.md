# Interim Procedures for Stocking Nonnative Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #salmonidae #centrarchidae #fish-stocking-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover letter and the interim (March 10, 1994) Procedures for Stocking Nonnative Fish Species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], excluding the San Juan River Basin. The procedures were developed jointly by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) and the States of [[Colorado]], [[Utah]], and [[Wyoming]] (States). Their purpose is to ensure that all future stocking of nonnative fish is consistent with recovery of four endangered fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The procedures are tested on an interim basis in spring and summer 1994, revised in fall 1994, and finalized by March 1995. The Colorado Division of Wildlife will then use them as the basis for aquaculture stocking regulations.

## Background & Description

The procedures were built under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin, which required the states and Service to develop procedures for reviewing and resolving disagreements over proposed introductions. The Service administers the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA), while the states hold authority over fish and wildlife resources and fish stocking decisions. The stated goal is consensus between agencies so neither must independently assert its authority.

The document also covers the principle that habitat loss and the proliferation of nonnative fishes both contributed to the decline of the endangered fishes. Nonnative stocking remains an important component of sport fisheries and commercial aquaculture in the basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Management principle:** The Recovery Program states that stocking of nonnative species should be confined to areas where the absence of potential conflict with rare or endangered species can be demonstrated.
- **Salmonids:** Their cold-water preference limits interaction; they survive in historic habitat only where artificial cold habitat exists, and summer temperatures preclude survival in most occupied habitat.
- **Escapement control:** Stocking sites must be isolated (no surface connection) or screened. [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] deep-reservoir releases largely prevent warm-water fish escapement downstream.
- **Floodplain criteria:** Ponds are generally required to sit at least 5 vertical feet above the ordinary high water line of non-critical-habitat waters, an approximation of 50- to 100-year flood elevations. The document notes this value is provisional and may be revised to 4 or 6 feet after hydrologic study.
- **Competition concern:** Recent information showed competition between young-of-the-year Colorado Squawfish and [[Fathead Minnow]], so its routine stocking is restricted to waters outside the 100-year floodplain.
- **Mosquitofish:** Described as aggressive omnivores associated with extirpation of rare native fishes in the American Southwest.

## Key Findings

### Table 1: Species Routinely Stockable (summarized)
Table 1 lists about 15 species/situation combinations that may be stocked routinely, including:
- [[Salmonids]] (all currently in the basin), in all waters of the upper basin.
- [[Triploid Grass Carp]], restricted to triploid fish with certificate of triploidy, in isolated or screened ponds and reservoirs.
- [[Redside Shiner]], [[Threadfin Shad]], and [[Fathead Minnow]], under elevation (6,500 ft mean sea level) and floodplain-based conditions.
- [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Largemouth Bass]], allowed in standing waters separated from occupied habitat by waters with established reproducing populations.
- [[Striped Bass]] and [[Mosquitofish]], in isolated waters, and [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Utah Chub]] in specified locations (Utah chub only in Utah sub-basins where they currently exist).

Stocking in [[Lake Powell]] and waters above [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] is treated separately in the table.

### Table 2: Nonnative Species Present but Not Managed
Table 2 lists 21 species already present in the basin but not currently stocked, including [[Common Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Creek Chub]], [[Utah Sucker]], [[White Sucker]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Northern Pike]]. Any stocking requires case-by-case evaluation following the [[American Fisheries Society]] Position Statement on the Introduction of Aquatic Species (elements a–g).

### Case-by-Case Stocking
Species not listed in Table 1 (e.g., [[Walleye]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Tiger Muskellunge]], [[Hybrid Striped Bass]]) require case-by-case review with, at minimum: purpose and location; species, numbers and rationale; escapement potential and control measures; a remediation plan; and a monitoring plan.

### Previously Approved Management Plans
The Service has approved state management plans for nine Colorado reservoirs (e.g., Rio Blanco, Hollenbeck, Mark Mesa, Chipeta, Crawford, Namaquintnep, Puett, Summit, Totten). These are exempt from further review unless a substantive revision is proposed.

### Review Process
1. Formal stocking proposal, based on sound biological evaluation.
2. Public and agency review for 60 days.
3. Informal ESA consultation within 30 days.
4. (A) Non-federal proposals: agency review within 30 days, escalation to Regional Director and state directors, and possible decision by a State Wildlife Commission with at least 30 days' notice; or (B) federal-nexus proposals: formal Section 7 consultation (Service has 90 days; biological opinion issued within 45 days).

### Annual Reporting and Modification
All nonsalmonid stocking must be reported to the Recovery Program by December 31 each year, including species, location, number, size, Table 1 criteria invoked, and case-by-case proposals. Modifications may be requested by any interested party.
