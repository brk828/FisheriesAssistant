# Distribution of White Catfish and Rainbow Trout in Folsom Lake, California, Determined by Gill Netting, February–November 1961

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Inland Fisheries Administrative Report by C. E. von Geldern, Jr. (California Department of Fish and Game) describes the vertical and horizontal distribution of [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*) and [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*) in [[Folsom Lake]], a large West Slope Sierran reservoir. Standard [[Gill Netting]] was carried out at nine stations from February through November 1961. The study captured 277 white catfish and 82 rainbow trout. The original objective, to map all principal game and rough fish, was not met because most other species were caught too rarely. The report therefore covers only the two species with sufficient catches.

## Background & Description

The study was designed to reveal fish distribution, which supports understanding of [[Reservoir Ecosystem]] function, helps anglers target under-used species, and may identify unoccupied niches. The work formed part of the [[Dingell-Johnson Project]] (California F-18-R, "Experimental Management of Warmwater Reservoirs"), funded through [[Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration]]. The report was submitted June 8, 1964.

**Reservoir characteristics**
- Watershed: 1,875 square miles of mountain terrain.
- Spillway elevation: 475 feet.
- Maximum surface area: 11,930 acres.
- Maximum depth: 275 feet.
- Storage capacity: 1,120,000 acre-feet.
- Shoreline at full pool: about 75 miles.
- Shape: a rough "V" with arms up the North and South Forks of the [[American River]] and a peninsula between them.

**Fishery and use**
- The lake is heavily used for recreation. A 1962 [[Creel Census]] recorded a mean angler catch of 0.32 fish per hour (unpublished data).
- Most of the catch was [[Bluegill]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], and [[Largemouth Bass]]. Other species included brown and black bullheads, redear and green sunfish, and rainbow trout.

**Sampling design**
- Nets were set on five consecutive days near the start of each month.
- Seven stations were deep-water pelagic sites (76 to 250 feet deep at full pool).
- Two stations (8 and 9) were shallow littoral sites (about 20 feet), moved with water-level changes.
- Each standard net had five 25- × 6-foot nylon panels with varying stretched mesh sizes. Scan quality makes the exact mesh sizes unclear.
- Diving nets were set at depths chosen from temperature and oxygen profiles. Some sets were stratified across [[Epilimnion]], [[Thermocline]], and [[Hypolimnion]]. Pelagic nets were not fished on the bottom.
- Sampling was incomplete in the first months because the program was still in development, and some accidents interrupted the schedule.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Limnological data from [[Limnology of Folsom Lake]] (Rawstron, 1961–1963) describe a relatively infertile reservoir:
- Total phosphorus: 99 to 245 parts per billion.
- Total alkalinity: 14.3 to 21.0 ppm as CaCO₃.
- [[Dissolved Oxygen]] was adequate for fish throughout 1961 except in October, when a low of 3.4 ppm was recorded near the bottom. Other readings were generally 7 to 10 ppm.
- The lake was thermally stratified from May until November 1961.
- Water level fluctuated strongly: it rose from 393 feet in January to 459 feet in June 1961. It filled during spring runoff and fell in autumn and early winter.

These fluctuations shift the shoreline and affect [[Littoral Zone]] habitat, which is why the shallow stations had to be relocated repeatedly. Strong [[Thermal Stratification]] and the seasonal [[Lake Turnover]] in November shape fish depth distribution throughout the year.

## Key Findings

### White Catfish
- **Sample size:** 277 fish. Catch per net per sampling period ranged from 0.11 (March) to 2.96 (August).
- **Temperature response:** Catch rate rose with water temperature. Pelagic nets set below 50 °F, 50–60 °F, 60–70 °F, and above 70 °F caught 0.16, 0.14, 0.72, and 1.73 fish per net day. Bottom nets at 50–60 °F, 60–70 °F, and above 70 °F caught 2.75, 5.75, and 6.50 fish per net day.
- **Seasonal depth pattern (open water):**
  - February–March: few fish, widely scattered.
  - April–May: most abundant at 10–30 feet; none caught below 30 feet.
  - June: dispersed, with the greatest density still at 10–30 feet, and some fish from the surface to about 70 feet.
  - July–August: largest catches at 30–50 feet; none below 50 feet.
  - September–October: dispersed. Some fish were at the surface to 30 feet and others at 70–90 feet.
  - November (lake turnover): concentrated at 50–90 feet.
- **Horizontal distribution:** White catfish were most abundant near shore. Seasonal catch rates at 10–30 feet were 5.69 (littoral Stations 8 and 9), 2.64 (shallow pelagic Stations 4 and 7), 0.60 (deeper pelagic Stations 3 and 6), and 0.05 (deepest pelagic Stations 1, 2, and 5).
- **Notable observation:** Appearance of white catfish near the surface in the pelagic zone was unexpected and, according to the author, had not been reported before.

### Rainbow Trout
- **Sample size:** 82 fish. Monthly totals ranged from 1 (March) to 14 (June).
- **Temperature response:** Catch rates per net day were 0.00 (below 50 °F), 0.27 (50–60 °F), 0.79 (60–70 °F), and 0.23 (above 70 °F). Trout concentrated in the 60–70 °F [[Thermal Barrier]] band.
- **Seasonal depth pattern:**
  - February–March: very few caught, near the surface. The author attributed this to spawning in tributaries, supported by spent fish later in the lake and good early-spring tributary angling.
  - April–May: near the surface, where temperatures reached 60 °F or more.
  - June: dispersed, with some fish moving to about 60 feet as surface temperatures reached 70 °F.
  - July–August: 40 to 80 feet, usually at 60–70 °F.
  - September–October: dispersed from the surface to 80 feet. A few surface catches in September could not be explained.
  - November (lake turnover): concentrated at the surface.
- **Horizontal distribution:** Littoral Stations 8 and 9 caught no trout. Pelagic nets at 10–30 feet averaged 0.11 trout per net. Deep stations (1, 2, 5) averaged 0.54 fish per net from the surface to 50 feet, compared with 0.32 at shallower pelagic stations. The author concluded that rainbow trout are primarily pelagic in Folsom Lake, though shore anglers take them off the steep inner dam face.

### Study Limitations and Management Implications
- Small catches, poor gill-net vulnerability of several species, and bottom nets fished only at 10–30 feet limit the conclusions. White catfish abundance near the bottom below 30 feet is therefore speculative.
- The vertical trout pattern "generally followed the 60 to 70 °F thermal barrier" and matched [[Horsetooth Reservoir]] results (Horack and Tanner, 1964).
- Advice to anglers: fish near the bottom for white catfish, and fish the pelagic zone within the 60–70 °F thermal barrier for rainbow trout. Trout angling should be best near the surface when surface temperatures are 70 °F or below.
- The study could not address unoccupied niches because most species were not adequately sampled.
