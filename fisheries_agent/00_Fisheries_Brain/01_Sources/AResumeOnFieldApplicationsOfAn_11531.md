# Field Applications of Antimycin A to Control Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fishery-management #antimycin #fish-control #toxicology

## Executive Summary

This 1970 Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife report by [[Robert E. Lennon]] and [[Bernard L. Berger]] summarizes over 50 field applications of [[Antimycin A]], an antibiotic fish toxicant, across 19 U.S. states, Canada, and Guatemala. [[Antimycin A]] is a powerful respiratory inhibitor produced by [[Streptomyces]] molds, effective against fish at parts-per-billion concentrations while remaining relatively harmless to aquatic invertebrates, amphibians, and higher vertebrates. The compound works in fresh and salt water, across pH ranges from acid to alkaline (pH 7.0-9.3), and in temperatures from below freezing to warm water. Key advantages include non-repellency to fish, lack of color or odor, rapid degradation in water, and availability in multiple formulations (liquid and sand-coated granules: Fintrol-5, Fintrol-15, Fintrol-30) designed for different depth applications. The toxicant proved effective for total reclamations, partial reclamations, and selective control of target species, particularly for removing rough fish while preserving game fish or eliminating scale fish from catfish production ponds.

## Background & Description

[[Antimycin A]] was isolated in 1945 by University of Wisconsin scientists initially testing it against crop-destroying fungi. Its potential as a fish toxicant was recognized in 1963. The Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation obtained a use patent, and the U.S. and Canadian governments registered it as a fish toxicant in 1966. [[Ayerst Laboratories]] obtained registration for Fintrol-5 formulation.

The compound kills fish by inhibiting electron transport at a specific site in the respiratory system. The toxic action is irreversible in most fish species. Fish die slowly without frenzied activity, exhibiting no spectacular response. Concentrations lethal to fish (parts per billion range) are harmless to mammals and birds based on extensive toxicity testing on mice, rats, rabbits, guinea pigs, dogs, lambs, quails, pigeons, chickens, pheasants, and mallard ducks.

**Formulations developed:**
- **Liquid formulation**: Used in laboratory trials and streams, applied via spray apparatus or drip equipment
- **Fintrol-5**: Antimycin coated on 40-mesh sand, releases toxicant evenly within first 5 feet of depth
- **Fintrol-15**: Releases toxicant within first 15 feet of depth
- **Fintrol-30**: Releases toxicant within first 30 feet of depth

The sand formulations offer advantages over liquid: granules bounce off emergent vegetation into water and penetrate submerged vegetation for better distribution.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Field trials encompassed diverse aquatic environments:

**Water Chemistry Ranges:**
- pH: 5.2 to 9.3
- Temperature: 38°F to 83°F (under ice to warm water)
- Hardness: 4 ppm (soft) to 258 ppm (hard)
- Alkalinity: 16 ppm to 310 ppm
- Fresh and marine waters (tidal basin trial at [[Fort Desoto, Florida]])

**Species Exposed (60+ species documented in Table 1):**
- Salmonids: [[Brook Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Atlantic Salmon]], [[Kokanee Salmon]]
- Cyprinids: [[Common Carp]], [[Goldfish]], [[Grass Carp]], numerous minnow species
- Centrarchids: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Warmouth]]
- Catfishes: [[Channel Catfish]], [[Blue Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Brown Bullhead]]
- Suckers: [[White Sucker]], [[Longnose Sucker]], multiple species
- Others: [[Northern Pike]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Walleye]], [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Freshwater Drum]]

**Marine species tested** (38 species in tidal basin): anchovies, triggerfish, flounders, rays (sensitive); catfishes, common jack, redfish (more resistant). Six-hour exposure to 7 ppb killed sensitive species.

**pH Sensitivity:**
- Antimycin persists longer in acid water (pH <7.5), allowing reduced application rates
- Rapid degradation occurs at higher pH (>8.5), requiring greater concentrations
- At pH 9.3 ([[Whitewater Lake, Nebraska]]), 7.3 ppb degraded before achieving complete kill; 12-15 ppb estimated as needed

**Temperature Effects:**
- Fish response slower in cold water (<50°F) due to reduced metabolism
- Tests under ice at 38-42°F: 5 ppb killed trout, carp, bluegills, largemouth bass within 2-7 days
- Lower concentrations and more rapid response expected above 60°F

**Catfish Resistance:**
Bullheads and other catfishes consistently survived concentrations below 25 ppb across all trials, enabling selective removal of scale fish from catfish production ponds.

## Key Findings

### Non-Repellency Demonstrations

**[[Lake Creek Lake]], [[Saratoga, Wyoming]]** (1.8 acres, spring-fed, 3 cfs discharge):
- Clear, colorless water allowed direct observation of fish behavior
- 10 ppb maintained for minimum 8 hours despite 27-hour water exchange rate
- Fish exhibited no alarm or escape attempts into spring flows
- [[Northern Creek Chub]] schools broke up after 2 hours, lost orientation, none sought refuge in springs
- [[Brook Trout]] (one-third ounce to 6.6 pounds) began dying 3 hours post-exposure, made no escape attempts
- Complete kill achieved; antimycin flushed from basin within 48 hours

**[[Westfield Creek]], Wisconsin** (3.5 miles, 17.2 cfs, pH 8.8, 77°F):
- 10 ppb maintained for 10 hours via drip stations and spray pumps
- Clear water allowed observation: no fish attempted downstream escape
- Fish gradually drifted downstream hours later when incapacitated
- Mortality sequence: darters (2 hours) → minnows and dace → shiners, chubs, suckers, panfish → carp (8 hours, thousands drifting)
- Most carp dead within 24 hours, all within 48 hours; none recovered
- Post-treatment electrofishing (1.5 miles): only small minnows, one stickleback, several lampreys found

### Acid Water Applications (pH <7.5)

**New Hampshire trout ponds:**
- 1 ppb eliminated 12 species within 72 hours (including [[Atlantic Salmon]], [[Chain Pickerel]], [[White Sucker]], [[Yellow Perch]])
- Survivors: some bluegills, smallmouth bass, all brown bullheads
- 12 ppb killed all species except bullheads within 48 hours; toxicant persisted 2 weeks

**[[Beauty Lake]], Quebec** (44 acres, maximum depth 42 feet):
- Objective: eradicate [[Northern Pike]], minnows, suckers, pumpkinseeds, yellow perch to restore native brook trout
- 5 ppb as Fintrol-30 applied to entire basin
- Revealed previously undetected [[American Smelt]] population in deep, cold water (died within 2 hours)
- Post-treatment netting: no live fish except brown bullheads
- Complete success on 13 target species

### Alkaline Water Applications (pH 7.5-8.5)

**[[Lake Katrine]], Wisconsin** (35 acres, pH 8.2, 56°F):
- 7.5 ppb achieved complete kill of [[Common Carp]] and [[Fathead Minnow]]

**[[Parker Lake]], Wisconsin** (63 acres, pH 8.2, 48°F):
- 10 ppb eliminated carp, pumpkinseed, bluegill, yellow perch

**[[Barney Lake]], Wisconsin** (34 acres, pH 8.7, 57°F):
- 10 ppb killed all carp

**[[Whitewater Lake]], Nebraska** (490 acres, pH 9.3, 62°F):
- 7.3 ppb degraded before sufficient exposure; incomplete kill
- Bioassays indicated 12-15 ppb needed at high pH

### Partial Reclamations

**Carp spawn control:**
- [[Lake Katrine]] spawning bay: 4 ppb achieved complete mortality of carp eggs; control areas hatched normally
- [[Lake Barney]] shoreline treatments: 2 ppb failed to kill all eggs
- [[Backbone Lake]], Iowa: 50 ppb applied to spawning sites (0.25 to 5 acres); storm caused dilution but demonstrated non-repellency

**Gizzard shad reduction** (Stuttgart, Arkansas, 22-acre pond):
- 50 cc liquid antimycin titrated into 140-gpm inlet for 6 minutes
- Shad dispersed after 1.5 minutes but sustained lethal exposure; large numbers died
- Subsequent 1.5-minute titration killed new congregation
- Third application (100 cc) reduced shad; some green sunfish killed but no channel catfish harmed
- Effective, economical spot treatment

**Stunted panfish control** ([[McGrath Lake]], Wisconsin, pH 5.2, 70°F):
- One bay: 1 ppb killed all stunted [[Yellow Perch]] and 50% of stunted bluegills
- Second bay: 2 ppb eliminated all perch and bluegills

### Selective Toxicant Applications

**Yellow perch removal** ([[Harriet Lake]] and [[Perch Lake]], Wisconsin, soft, acid, warm water):
- 0.5 ppb killed all stunted perch
- No harm to [[Northern Pike]], bluntnose minnows, bullheads, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass

**Sunfish thinning** (Georgia farm ponds, 1-9 acres, soft water):
- 0.4-0.8 ppb reduced overabundant bluegills, golden shiners, crappies
- Minimal damage to largemouth bass
- Typical 2.5-acre pond (0.4 ppb, 75°F): killed 127 lb golden shiners, 172 lb stunted bluegills, 22 lb fingerling crappies, only 7 lb fingerling bass
- No adult bass or crappies killed; subsequent bass reproduction not impaired

**Scale fish removal from catfish ponds:**

*Mississippi catfish farm* (0.9-1.4 acre ponds, 120 lb/acre golden shiners and green sunfish):
- 10 ppb killed ≥99% of scale fish despite high pH
- No catfish mortality
- Treated ponds yielded 330 lb/acre more catfish than untreated ponds at harvest

*[[Victory Lake]], [[Fort Benning, Georgia]]* (26 acres):
- 3.6 ppb in lake, 10 ppb in tributaries/springs
- Killed 5,200 lb scale fish (210 lb/acre): 60 lb chub suckers, 33 lb golden shiners, 55 lb bluegills/warmouth, 19 lb largemouth bass
- Only one channel catfish and one bullhead killed
- No effect on mosquitofish
- Post-treatment netting: channel catfish to 4.5 lb and bullheads in good condition, no other species except mosquitofish

**Minnow removal from stratified trout lake** ([[Pleasant Lake]], Minnesota, 80 feet deep, 20 acres):
- Objective: remove parasitized minnows (redbelly dace, bluntnose minnows, fathead minnows, shiners) while preserving rainbow trout in deep cold water
- Applied when well-stratified in late summer; epilimnion ~15 feet thick
- Fintrol-15 in epilimnion, Fintrol-5 along shore to achieve 5 ppb in warm upper stratum
- Minnows eliminated (confirmed by trapping, netting, SCUBA)
- Minor rainbow trout kill (presumed to have moved into epilimnion to feed)
- More economical than treating entire water body

### Detoxification

**[[Rathbone Creek]], [[Cataract, Wisconsin]]:**
- Several miles treated with 7.5 ppb for ≥8 hours to eliminate minnows and suckers
- [[Cataract Pond]] (small impoundment) drained to low level, refilled 18 hours before antimycin-laden water reached spillway
- Pond treated with 2 ppm potassium permanganate
- Detoxification quick and complete: effluent water did not harm rainbow trout in live cages 500 feet downstream

### Application Guidelines (Table 5)

**Sensitive fish** (gizzard shad, trout, pike, carp, minnows, suckers, sticklebacks, white bass, sunfish, perch, freshwater drum):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| <7.5 | >60°F | 5.0 | 0.5 | 1.4 |
| <7.5 | <60°F | 7.5 | 0.75 | 2.0 |
| 7.5-8.5 | >60°F | 7.5 | 0.75 | 2.0 |
| 7.5-8.5 | <60°F | 10.0 | 1.0 | 2.8 |
| >8.5 | >60°F | 10.0 | 1.0 | 2.8 |
| >8.5 | <60°F | 10.0 | 1.0 | 2.8 |

**Resistant fish** (catfishes, bowfin, gar, buffalo, carp in some conditions):
- Require 15-25 ppb antimycin A (1.5-2.5 ppm Fintrol-5) depending on conditions

### Comprehensive Recommendations

The report provides detailed 12-step protocol for fish control operations (Section: Recommendations for Use):

1. Define problem in full detail
2. Assess fish populations (netting, trapping, electrofishing, creel census)
3. Define target species and characteristics
4. Evaluate all control approaches (biological, environmental, chemical, electrical, mechanical, integrated)
5. Select control method considering specificity, side effects, multiple water uses
6. Determine water characteristics (area/volume, depth, flows, velocity, bottom type, tributaries, gradient, vegetation, chemistry including pH, DO, CO₂, alkalinity, hardness, temperature, color, turbidity, pollution)
7. Correlate data to select toxicant, formulation, concentration, season, distribution method
8. Confirm selection with lakeside/streamside bioassays using target water and species
9. Adjust application based on bioassay results
10. Set live cages with indicator species at various sites/depths 24 hours prior; monitor survival
11. Postpone if conditions changed significantly since bioassays
12. Collect/process/bury dead fish; assess results; evaluate side effects; prepare reports; document post-reclamation management

The report emphasizes that **concentration and duration of exposure** are both critical for lethal dose. Low concentration requires long exposure; high concentration with short exposure may equal lethal dose. This relationship requires greater care in flowing waters.
