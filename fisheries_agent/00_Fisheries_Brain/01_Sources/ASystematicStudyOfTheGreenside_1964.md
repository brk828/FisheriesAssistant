# Greenside Darter (Etheostoma blennioides)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae #Etheostoma #systematics

## Executive Summary
This dissertation presents a comprehensive systematic revision of the [[Greenside Darter]] (*Etheostoma blennioides*), a freshwater percid fish distributed widely across eastern North American drainages. Based on an analysis of ~4,000 specimens from 540 collections, the study examines infraspecific, sexual, and year-class variation across 14 taxonomic characters. The research resolves the taxonomic status of *E. b. gutselli* (previously considered a distinct species) as a subspecies, describes a new undescribed northern subspecies, and reconstructs a detailed biogeographic history explaining the current distribution of four recognized subspecies via Pleistocene stream capture, glacial refugia, and interglacial dispersal events.

## Background & Description
- **Sample size**: Approximately 4,000 specimens examined from 540 collections.
- **Taxonomic characters analyzed**: 14 total, including meristic (countable) traits and qualitative morphological features.
- **Key diagnostic features illustrated**:
  - Lip tip development (variation in lip morphology)
  - Snout shape variation across populations
  - [[Genital Papillae]] distribution patterns (sexually dimorphic structure)
  - [[Nuptial Tubercle]] distribution (secondary sexual characteristic)
- **Variation sources studied**: 
  - Infraspecific (subspecies-level) variation
  - Sexual dimorphism
  - Year-class (age cohort) variation in meristic counts

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Riverine systems, likely riffle/rocky substrate specialist typical of the genus [[Etheostoma]] (darters).
- **Distribution** (four recognized subspecies plus one undescribed form):
  - ***E. b. gutselli***: Confined to the upper [[Little Tennessee River]] and [[Pigeon River]]; intergrades with *newmani* in the [[Hiwassee River]] system.
  - ***E. b. newmani***: Broadly distributed across the [[Tennessee River]] system, [[Cumberland River]] system, and west of the Mississippi in the [[St. Francis River]], [[White River]], [[Arkansas River]], and [[Ouachita River]] systems.
  - ***E. b. blennioides***: Restricted to the upper [[Ohio River]] and tributaries (upstream of the [[Green River (Kentucky)]] confluence), the upper [[Genesee River]] (New York/Pennsylvania), and the upper [[Potomac River]] drainage.
  - **Undescribed northern subspecies**: Found in the [[Missouri River]] and Mississippi River drainage of eastern Missouri, and the [[Wabash River]]–Great Lakes drainage.
- **Population structure within newmani**:
  - Tennessee River population differs at the "race" level from Cumberland River, St. Francis, Black, and Upper White River populations.
  - The latter four populations are subracially distinct from one another.
  - Additional recognized subraces occur in the Arkansas, Ouachita, Saline, and Little Red rivers.
- **Sympatry/Allopatry**: The undescribed northern subspecies and *E. b. blennioides* are allopatric but geographically proximal in the Genesee River, separated by a series of three high waterfalls blocking upstream movement.

## Key Findings
1. **Taxonomic revision**: *Etheostoma blennioides gutselli* (Hildebrand), previously treated as a distinct species allied to *E. blennioides*, is reclassified as a subspecies that intergrades with *E. b. newmani* in the Hiwassee River system.
2. **Center of origin**: *E. blennioides* is hypothesized to have originated in the southern [[Appalachian Mountains]], with *newmani* identified as the most primitive (ancestral) subspecies.
3. **Biogeographic history**:
   - The Tennessee River population of *E. b. newmani* likely dispersed westward in the late Tertiary, giving rise to the Arkansas race.
   - A later westward movement of the Cumberland River population produced the St. Francis, Black, and Upper White River populations.
   - *E. b. blennioides* is believed to have arisen from a headwater transfer of *newmani* from the Tennessee River system (Virginia/North Carolina) into the ancient [[Teays River]] (now the New River).
   - This lineage spread through the Ohio River system during the Pleistocene, reached the Genesee River via the Cuba outlet from the [[Allegheny River]] in the post-Pleistocene, and entered the Potomac drainage via stream piracy through either the [[Youghiogheny River]] or upper [[Cheat River]] systems.
4. **Northern subspecies dispersal**: The undescribed northern subspecies likely crossed the Mississippi River during an early Pleistocene interglacial period, colonizing the Wabash River system. During the Wisconsin glaciation, it persisted in a refugium in the lower Wabash River (Illinois/Indiana), later using the Maumee outlet to enter glacial [[Lake Erie]], and subsequently spreading eastward into [[Lake Ontario]], the lower Genesee River, and the Mohawk River of New York.
5. **Geographic isolation mechanism**: A series of three high waterfalls has historically blocked the undescribed northern subspecies from moving upstream into range occupied by *E. b. blennioides* in the Genesee River, maintaining their allopatric distribution despite geographic proximity.
6. **Overall taxonomic framework**: The study establishes a hierarchical classification of variation within *E. blennioides*, distinguishing subspecies, races, and subraces based on morphological and geographic evidence, reflecting a complex history of Pleistocene stream captures, glacial refugia, and interglacial dispersal events shaping the modern distribution of the species complex.
