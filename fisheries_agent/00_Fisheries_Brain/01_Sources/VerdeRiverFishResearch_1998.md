# Verde River Fish Research

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #Poeciliidae #freshwater-ecology #verde-river

## Executive Summary

This document is an internal USDA Forest Service transmittal (Tonto National Forest, dated 1998) summarizing a fish and habitat survey of the **[[Upper Verde River]]** conducted in **spring 1998** by the **[[Rocky Mountain Research Station]]**, in cooperation with the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Prescott National Forest, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Arizona State University, and SWCA. Seven sites were sampled — **Burnt Ranch, FR638, Duff Spring, Bear Siding, Perkinsville, Black Bridge, and Sycamore** — yielding data on fish species composition, relative abundance, and channel/habitat characteristics (length, width, depth, discharge, velocity, cross-sectional area, and volume). The dataset documents the co-occurrence of native Sonoran Desert fishes (e.g., **[[Roundtail Chub]]**, **[[Spikedace]]**, **[[Longfin Dace]]**, **[[Desert Sucker]]**, **[[Sonora Sucker]]**, **[[Speckled Dace]]**) alongside a substantial nonnative fish assemblage, with **[[Common Carp]]** dominating overall relative abundance (~68% of total catch across sites, per Table 2).

## Background & Description

The source is a data-summary transmittal rather than a morphological study; no detailed morphometric descriptions of individual species are provided. However, the following taxonomic/functional groupings are implied by the species list and are relevant for future cross-referencing:

- **Cyprinidae (minnows)**: [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Spikedace]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Common Carp]], [[Fathead Minnow]] — small-to-medium bodied, benthic/water-column generalists; Spikedace notable for reduced/absent pelvic fin morphology and preference for swift riffle habitat (species of conservation concern in the Southwest).
- **Catostomidae (suckers)**: [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]] — subterminal, ventrally-oriented mouths adapted for algal/detrital grazing on substrate.
- **Ictaluridae (catfishes)**: [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]] — nonnative bottom-dwelling predators/omnivores with barbels.
- **Centrarchidae (sunfishes/basses)**: [[Green Sunfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]] — nonnative piscivorous/opportunistic predators.
- **Poeciliidae (livebearers)**: [[Mosquitofish]] — small nonnative livebearer, tolerant of degraded/warm conditions.
- **Gila robusta ([[Roundtail Chub]])**: native large-bodied cyprinid, robust caudal peduncle, associated with deeper pool habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Perennial, warmwater stream reaches of the upper Verde River spanning multiple mesohabitat types (riffles, runs, pools), sampled across a longitudinal gradient of seven sites from Burnt Ranch downstream to Sycamore/Black Bridge.
- **Physical habitat metrics (Table 3, Spring 1998)**:
  - Total site lengths sampled: 2,067 m (all sites combined)
  - Total cross-sectional area: 8,260 m²
  - Total volume: 2,888 m³ (as reported; internal consistency of OCR values uncertain)
  - Average reach length per site: 441 m
  - Average width: 0.28 m *(likely OCR truncation — probably closer to several meters; treat with caution)*
  - Average depth: 1.32 m *(possible OCR error; typical Verde River depths are shallower — flag for verification)*
  - Average velocity: 0.45 m/sec
  - Average discharge: 0.47 m³/sec (site-level values ranged roughly 0.28–0.63 m³/sec)
- **Community structure**: Native [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]] (dace, suckers, chub) co-occur with a numerically dominant nonnative assemblage, especially **Common Carp**, which comprised the plurality or majority of catch at most sites (e.g., 90.8% at Perkinsville, 70.6% at Black Bridge, 51.8% at FR638).
- **Site-level community variation**:
  - **Burnt Ranch**: Higher relative proportion of native cyprinids/catostomids (Desert Sucker 33.3%, Longfin Dace 16.3%), lower carp dominance.
  - **Bear Siding**: Notably diverse assemblage with substantial nonnative centrarchids/ictalurids (Green Sunfish, Smallmouth Bass, Channel Catfish each ~8.2–12.2%) and no dominant carp presence reported.
  - **Sycamore**: Small overall sample (n=27) with notable Sonora Sucker (28.6%) and Fathead Minnow (18.5%) representation.
  - **Perkinsville & Black Bridge**: Strongly carp-dominated (>70–90%), suggesting more lentic/altered habitat conditions or lower-gradient reaches.
- This spatial pattern suggests a downstream (or site-specific) shift from native-dominated to nonnative (carp)-dominated fish communities, a pattern of concern for **[[Native Fish Conservation]]** in the [[Colorado River Basin]] and broader Southwestern U.S. streams.

## Key Findings

1. **Total catch**: Approximately 1,520–1,820 fish (totals conflict between Table 1 and Table 2 due to OCR degradation) were collected across seven sites in spring 1998.
2. **Species richness**: 15 species recorded, including 6 native taxa (Longfin Dace, Desert Sucker, Sonora Sucker, Roundtail Chub, Speckled Dace, Spikedace) and 9 nonnative taxa (Yellow Bullhead, Red Shiner, Carp, Channel Catfish, Mosquitofish, Flathead Catfish, Green Sunfish, Smallmouth Bass, Fathead Minnow).
3. **Nonnative dominance**: Common Carp alone accounted for roughly two-thirds (~68%) of the total fish collected across all sites, indicating significant nonnative fish pressure in the upper Verde River during this period.
4. **Presence of Spikedace**: The detection of **[[Spikedace]]**, a federally listed threatened cyprinid, at these sites is ecologically significant and central to ongoing monitoring by RMS/AGFD/FWS partners.
5. **Site variability**: Fish community composition varied substantially by site — from native-dominated assemblages (Burnt Ranch, Sycamore) to heavily carp-dominated assemblages (Perkinsville, Black Bridge) — reflecting local habitat heterogeneity captured in Table 3 (discharge, velocity, depth, channel morphology).
6. **Multi-agency collaboration**: The study reflects coordinated fish and habitat monitoring across USFS, AGFD, FWS, ASU, and SWCA, underscoring the upper Verde River's status as a priority reach for **[[Endangered Species Monitoring]]** and native fish recovery efforts.
7. **Data limitations**: The source document is a heavily OCR-degraded internal data summary; several numeric values (widths, depths, percentages, and totals) contain inconsistencies and should be verified against original spreadsheet files (referenced as `SJASFILES/VERDE/RMS_98/98SPRING.XLS`).
