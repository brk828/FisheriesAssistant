# Razorback Sucker Round-up Results, Six-Mile Cove Area, March 18-21, 1991

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #population-assessment

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation field report documents the 1991 annual razorback sucker ([[Xyrauchen texanus]]) capture and tagging effort at [[Lake Mohave]], focusing on Camp No. 3 operations in the [[Six-Mile Cove]] area on the Nevada side of [[Cottonwood Basin]]. The multi-agency effort (March 18-21, 1991) captured 46 razorback suckers using trammel nets and electrofishing, marking them with new PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags. This represented a continuation of ongoing population monitoring that had previously tagged approximately 2,250 of an estimated 65,000 adult razorbacks in the lake. Severe weather limited sampling effectiveness, and biologists noted that peak spawning had already passed, with most fish spent. Five recaptures from previous years (1982-1990) were documented. Incidental catch included 28 [[Rainbow Trout]], 16 [[Striped Bass]], and 13 [[Common Carp]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] ([[Xyrauchen texanus]], family [[Catostomidae]]) is a large-bodied catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River]] basin. This species congregates on shallow spawning shoals during spring months. The 1991 round-up was the fourth consecutive year (1988-1991) of coordinated capture efforts across [[Lake Mohave]]. Previous efforts had captured approximately 7,600 adult razorbacks lakewide, with 2,250 tagged primarily with external spaghetti tags ([[Floy Tags]]). Tag return data yielded a population estimate of 65,000 adult razorbacks in the lake.

The 1991 effort involved three camps: [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] led Camp No. 1 ([[Arizona Bay]]), [[Arizona State University]] operated Camp No. 2 ([[Carp Cove]], east side of [[Cottonwood Basin]]), and [[Bureau of Reclamation]] led Camp No. 3 (west side of [[Cottonwood Basin]], [[Six-Mile Cove]] area). Camp No. 3 included nine participants from Nevada Department of Wildlife, National Park Service, Bureau of Reclamation, and Fish and Wildlife Service.

Captured razorback suckers ranged 521-683 mm total length. Males (n=24) ranged 521-624 mm and 1,550-2,500 g. Females (n=22) ranged 583-683 mm and 2,220-3,660 g, demonstrating sexual size dimorphism typical of the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The primary sampling location was a spawning shoal approximately 5 acres in size on the lake side of [[Six-Mile Cove]], in about 5 meters of water. This shoal had been sampled consistently over the previous four years, providing temporal continuity for population monitoring.

A severe storm struck camp early morning March 19 (ca. 0400 hours) and continued through the day, substantially limiting sampling effectiveness and forcing early camp closure on March 21 instead of the planned March 22. Equipment failure (shocking boat malfunction first evening) further constrained capture efforts.

Biologists reached consensus that peak spawning had already passed. Most examined fish were spent with very few expressible gametes, contrasting sharply with previous years when eggs and milt required washing from measuring equipment. This phenological shift may reflect inter-annual variation in spawning timing related to environmental conditions.

The spawning shoal supported a multi-species assemblage. The majority of 28 [[Rainbow Trout]] (average 237.5 mm total length, range 210-255 mm for measured individuals) came from a single trammel-net haul at the razorback spawning site. Sixteen [[Striped Bass]] (507-766 mm, 1,100-4,180 g) were also captured and euthanized for stomach content analysis.

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker Capture and Tagging:**
- Total razorbacks captured: 46 (24 males, 22 females)
- All fish received [[PIT Tags]] (Passive Integrated Transponder), marking a methodological shift from previous [[Floy Tags]] (external spaghetti tags)
- PIT tags injected with No. 12 hypodermic needle; test fish held overnight showed no handling or tagging stress
- Tag reader powerpack required constant charging (operational issue noted)

**Recapture Data:**
- Five of 46 razorbacks were recaptures from previous years
- One female with green [[Carlin Tag]] originally tagged March 1982 uplake from Camp No. 3 showed only slight length/weight increase over 10-year period
- One fish with white Reclamation tag originally captured 1989 at same Six-Mile Cove spawning shoal
- Three fish with Floy tags (05*** series) from 1990: two white tags from Camp No. 3, one blue tag from Camp No. 1 (Arizona Bay)

**Physical Condition Notes:**
- Multiple individuals exhibited eye abnormalities: 6 fish with blind eyes (right or left), 1 with "bubbled" left eye
- One individual showed severe ichthyophthiriasis (ich)
- One fish had numerous [[Lernea]] (anchor worm) parasites
- Physical injuries noted: deep cut on back (one fish), scar on right dorsal region (one fish)

**Predator-Prey Relationships:**
- 16 [[Striped Bass]] stomachs examined: 9 empty (56%)
- Of 7 with food contents: 5 contained whole trout (mostly skinned), 1 had fish vertebrae and small rock, 1 contained crayfish remains
- One striped bass stomach contained trout plus night-crawler (likely bait)
- Predation on [[Rainbow Trout]] by striped bass documented at razorback spawning sites

**Total Catch Summary:**
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: 46
- [[Striped Bass]]: 16
- [[Rainbow Trout]]: 28
- [[Common Carp]]: 13

**Population Monitoring Context:**
- Ongoing tagging program essential for refining population estimates and understanding razorback behavior and ecology
- Capture success rates lower than preceding years across all three camps
- Spent condition of most fish suggests sampling occurred post-peak spawning
- Continued use of consistent spawning shoal locations enables long-term site fidelity and population trend assessment
