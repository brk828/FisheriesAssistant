# Bonytail Chub Broodstock Development

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Southwest) planning outline for developing a [[Bonytail Chub]] broodstock at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (Dexter NFHTC), New Mexico, with supporting work at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH), Arizona. The stated genetic conservation goal is to maintain broodstock diversity. The plan centers on producing offspring from every individual broodfish to establish an F2 generation, using pedigree-based paired matings, PIT-tagging, and family-lot integrity. It also outlines a collection protocol from [[Lake Mohave]], sperm cryopreservation, and a strategic and tactical framework for eventual reintroduction. The document is an outline; it contains no measured results, sample sizes beyond planned mating numbers, or population statistics.

## Background & Description

The [[Bonytail Chub]] is a fish of the family Cyprinidae, a native species of the [[Colorado River Basin]] that is the subject of recovery-oriented propagation. The document refers to the broodstock as "BTC F1 (1981 YC)," indicating founders from the 1981 year class. Founder stock was held in several places:

- Dexter NFHTC (initial broodstock)
- Arizona State University (ASU) golf course ponds
- Fish captured from Lake Mohave

Fish held in Arizona were to be returned to Dexter. Recorded individual data for each founder include length, weight, sex, reproductive history, PIT-tag number, and genetic identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan's ecological inputs are mainly about source populations and capture timing. Wild fish are to be collected from [[Lake Mohave]], with capture locations and dates recorded relative to spawning. Each wild fish is to be classed by parentage (wild, hatchery, or hatchery-derived such as F1, F2, or backcross). Water quality monitoring is listed as a maintenance task but no parameters, values, or thresholds are given. Diet, reproductive monitoring, and fish health monitoring are also listed without detail.

## Key Findings

**Genetic management framework (planning questions):**
- Identify the origin of founders and whether other broodstocks exist.
- Determine the number of wild female and male founders, a genetic baseline, the mating scheme (paired, factorial, or random), and the effective population size behind the current broodstock.
- Decide whether wild fish will be introduced, and if so how often, in what numbers or percentage of census size (example given: 400 adults), and with what mating scheme.

**Breeding plan:**
- Goal: offspring from every individual broodfish.
- Pedigree mating aimed at unique pairings, avoiding half-sib groups where possible.
- Planned paired matings: 30 per year from 1997 through 2001, for a total of 150 paired matings (300 adults).
- Each family lot to hold about 10 adults (males and females) at first maturity.
- All individuals are to be PIT-tagged before spawning.

**Collection and handling protocol (Lake Mohave to WBNFH):**
- Collect fish from Lake Mohave and transport them to WBNFH.
- On arrival: recovery, PIT-tagging and photographing, tissue collection for genetic analysis, gene banking, and tissue culture, and sperm collection for short- and long-term storage.
- Spawning options considered include Mohave x Mohave and Mohave x Dexter crosses, with the choice partly guided by genetic evaluation.

**Supporting programs:**
- Sperm cryopreservation: develop methods, bank sperm, and maintain a database.
- Genetic monitoring, fish health, and research and technology development are listed for both facilities.
- Alternate broodstock development plans and locations are noted but not specified.

**Strategic and tactical plan (outline items only):**
- Define the need, including biological status, trends, and relationship to recovery.
- Evaluate threats, risks, remedies, alternatives, and contingencies, both genetic and non-genetic.
- Examine policies, permits, and protocols; identify reintroduction sites and strategies; evaluate fish size and number requests.
- Set monitoring, milestones, time frames, roles, costs, funding sources, and cooperators.
- Develop a budget, work plans, and proposals.

The document contains no results, measured data, or completed analyses. It is a planning framework and task list.
