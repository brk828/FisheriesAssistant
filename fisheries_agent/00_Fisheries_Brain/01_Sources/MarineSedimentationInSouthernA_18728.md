# Marine Sedimentation in Southern Arizona

#marine-geology #stratigraphy #paleogeography

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Willard D. Pye]] (University of Arizona) summarizes the history of marine sedimentation in southern Arizona from Precambrian through Cenozoic time. It is a regional synthesis; the detailed lithology, formation descriptions, correlations, and thicknesses are treated in companion papers for individual geologic periods. The sedimentary record is controlled by a tectonic framework of two [[Geosyncline]] belts, a central shelf zone, and positive highland areas. Marine conditions dominated from the Precambrian through the Permian, were absent or interrupted during the Triassic and Jurassic, and gave way mainly to continental deposition, volcanism, and erosion in the Cretaceous and Cenozoic, with a brief Lower Cretaceous marine incursion in the southeast and a single Tertiary marine occurrence near Yuma.

## Background & Description

**Tectonic framework.** Four elements controlled deposition:

- The [[Cordilleran Geosyncline]], along the western edge and northwestern corner of Arizona.
- The [[Sonoran Geosyncline]], in southeastern Arizona, adjacent New Mexico, and Mexico.
- A broad northeast-trending shelf zone across central Arizona, occasionally flooded by seas from either geosyncline.
- Positive areas: the [[Defiance Positive Area]] in northeastern Arizona and northwestern New Mexico, and the postulated [[Mazatzal Highlands]] of central Arizona.

**Stratigraphic scope.** The paper covers the [[Precambrian Era]] through the [[Permian Period]] in marine detail, then summarizes the [[Mesozoic Era]] and [[Cenozoic Era]]. Table 1 is a nomenclature chart correlating rock units across northern and southern Arizona, southwestern New Mexico, and eastern and western Sonora, with ages from Precambrian to Recent. The bibliography is extensive, drawing on university theses, U.S. Geological Survey bulletins, and journal papers.

**Summary of the sedimentary history (southeastern Arizona):**

1. Marine sedimentation during Precambrian and Paleozoic time.
2. Absence of sedimentation and erosion during Triassic and Jurassic time.
3. Primarily continental deposition, volcanism, and erosion during Cretaceous and Cenozoic time.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper addresses ancient depositional environments, not living aquatic ecosystems. No fish, invertebrate, plankton, or water chemistry data are reported. The environmental information concerns inferred paleo-conditions:

- **Water clarity and carbonate production:** open, clear, clean seas favored carbonate deposition (Lower Ordovician, Silurian, Lower Mississippian, and parts of the Pennsylvanian).
- **Restricted and stagnant basins:** during Late Devonian time a clastic barrier (the [[Swisshelm Formation]] accumulation) restricted circulation, producing stagnant, reducing (euxinic) conditions in the basin to the east.
- **Oxidizing conditions:** younger Precambrian red, purple, and brown sediments indicate oxidizing environments at source or deposition.
- **Evaporites:** during Wolfcampian time, western restricted areas accumulated evaporites and clastics.
- **Karst and weathering:** Lower Mississippian emergence produced karst topography and chert-rich residues.
- **Volcanism:** tuffs and basalt flows are interbedded with several Precambrian and Cretaceous sequences.

## Key Findings

**Precambrian**

- *Older Precambrian:* strata are obscure due to metamorphism and deformation. Clastic sediments (shale, siltstone, arkose, sandstone, conglomerate) accumulated in seas presumed to cover most of the area. In the Dragoon Mountain area, accumulation in a eugeosyncline may have reached 20,000 feet. Pinal schist and equivalents are locally exposed.
- *Younger Precambrian:* about 1,200 feet of sediment accumulated in shallow seas, with crossbedding, ripple marks, and algal limestone. The southern shoreline extended southeast from near Tucson. The northern shoreline was an unknown distance north of the [[Mogollon Rim]].

**Cambrian**

- Middle Cambrian seas advanced northward from the Sonoran Geosyncline to about the present Mogollon Rim, depositing a basal sandstone, followed by sandstone, red beds, and shale. By Late Cambrian, carbonates dominated.

**Ordovician and Silurian**

- Lower Ordovician open, clear seas, favorable for carbonate deposition, were confined to eastern-southeastern Arizona, New Mexico, and northern Sonora.
- Upper Ordovician seas existed in southwestern New Mexico, with no evidence of extension into southeastern Arizona.
- Lower and Middle Silurian dolomites occur in southwestern New Mexico; the seas then withdrew, leaving the area emergent.

**Devonian**

- No Lower Devonian record exists in the region. Middle Devonian seas encroached from the south.
- Clean limestone accumulated in open sea to the south and west. Calcareous silt and impure limestone at the Swisshelm Mountains formed a barrier that persisted through Late Devonian time.

**Mississippian**

- Seas remained in the region from late Devonian into Early Mississippian time. Clear, massive carbonate with some chert accumulated in moderately deep water.
- Epeirogenic uplift at the close of Lower Mississippian time withdrew the seas southeastward, producing karst and chert-rich residues.

**Pennsylvanian**

- Seas transgressed again around late Morrowan time. By Des Moinesian time, all of southeastern Arizona was inundated, with carbonate and chert deposition, interrupted by red, green, and gray muds.
- By Virgilian time, clastics increasingly filled the basins, and essentially all of Arizona was covered by shallow seas.

**Permian**

- No withdrawal occurred at the end of the Pennsylvanian. Clastic deposition increased during Wolfcampian time, with evaporites in restricted western areas.
- Leonardian deposits were mainly limestone and dolomite, with two episodes of clean siliceous sand deposition across the entire area.
- Guadalupian time saw renewed shelf carbonates and clastics. The timing of final Permian withdrawal is unknown because later erosion removed all post-Guadalupian sediments.

**Mesozoic and Cenozoic**

- Except in southeastern Arizona, no marine sedimentation occurred in the Mesozoic. Triassic and Jurassic times were erosional, with almost no preserved sediments.
- Lower Cretaceous marine deposits in southeastern Arizona begin with a basal conglomerate, followed by clastic and limestone beds. Shelf clastics with arkose and red beds surrounded the sea, grading westward into continental volcanic deposits.
- Upper Cretaceous marine deposits occur in the northern part of southeastern Arizona. Elsewhere, Upper Cretaceous rocks are non-marine clastic and volcanic deposits.
- Extensive marine Triassic, Jurassic, and Cretaceous deposits occur south of western Arizona.
- About 1,000 feet of Tertiary marine deposits north of Yuma are the only known Tertiary marine occurrence in southern Arizona, representing an incursion from the southwest.
