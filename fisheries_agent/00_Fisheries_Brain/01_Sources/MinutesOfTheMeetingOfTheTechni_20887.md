# Minutes of the Technical Committee of the Colorado River Wildlife Management Committee Meeting, January 23, 1963

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #multi-agency-cooperation

## Executive Summary

Meeting minutes from the Technical Committee of the [[Colorado River Wildlife Management Committee]] held at Davis Camp, Arizona on January 23, 1963. The committee divided into three sub-committees (Law Enforcement, Game Management, and Fisheries) to discuss ongoing cooperative fisheries investigations, stocking programs, and management activities across the [[Colorado River]] system. The Fisheries Sub-Committee, chaired by David I. Foster of Arizona, addressed multiple cooperative studies involving Arizona, Nevada, California, Utah, Wyoming, the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]], and the [[National Park Service]].

## Background & Description

The meeting was called to order by Chairman A. W. Yoder of Arizona. The Fisheries Sub-Committee agenda included ten major topics covering fisheries investigations, cooperative studies, development plans, fish eradication projects, recreational use surveys, and species introduction proposals across the [[Colorado River]] basin. Participating agencies included state wildlife departments from Arizona, Nevada, California, Utah, and Wyoming, along with federal agencies including the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] and [[National Park Service]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] system encompasses diverse aquatic habitats including large reservoirs ([[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Havasu]], [[Glen Canyon]]), river reaches between dams, and tributary systems like the [[Green River]]. Management activities addressed both warmwater and coldwater fisheries. The [[Green River]] drainage renovation project covered approximately 450 miles. Water chemistry and physical conditions were noted as important factors, with colder-than-anticipated water temperatures affecting chemical detoxification operations. The system supports both native and introduced species, with ongoing efforts to manage species composition through stocking and eradication programs.

## Key Findings

**Arizona-Nevada Cooperative Studies**: Norman Wood of Nevada outlined results of cooperative studies along the river (detailed results attached to original minutes).

**California Activities**: Scott Soule reported on California fisheries activities. Food habit information on [[Striped Bass]] was being collected through stomach sampling. Survey work was conducted on [[Lake Havasu]] with plans for additional surveys and creel census work.

**Glen Canyon Fisheries Development**: Jim Eadens of [[National Park Service]] reported plans remained on schedule. Two million bass must be planted by June or numbers should be drastically reduced. Five million trout could be planted when available.

**Green River Fish Eradication Project**: Bob Jackson of Wyoming described renovation of approximately 450 miles of [[Green River]] drainage. Rotenone was injected from barrel stations on banks using one-inch neoprene nylon reinforced hoses fastened over the river by 3/4 inch aluminum cables.

**Recreational Use**: Chief Ranger James W. Packard reported on recreational use of [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]] for the past year (copy attached to original minutes).

**Channel Catfish Investigation**: Robert Gumtoe of [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] reported 404,000 [[Channel Catfish]] fingerlings were stocked between [[Parker Dam]] and [[Palo Verde Weir]]. Plans included following the year class through time. Discussion of continuing inventory with Arizona and California cooperation to avoid duplication, gathering chemical, physical and biological data on continuing basis. Survey of recreational use relating to fishery and economy was recommended. Maxwell reported catfish from six hatcheries were transported at 1,200 pounds per tankfull to New Mexico then to Parker for planting operation, described as one of the biggest in Bureau history.

**Trout Allocations from Willow Beach Hatchery**: Jack Hemphill listed allocations for 1963 and 1964 (in thousands of pounds per quarter):
- Needles to Davis Dam: 1963 total 46,000 lbs; 1964 total 45,000 lbs
- Lower Mohave to Eldorado: 1963 total 55,000 lbs; 1964 total 68,000 lbs  
- Eldorado to Hoover Dam: 1963 total 45,000 lbs; 1964 total 50,000 lbs
- Below Glen Canyon Dam: 1963 total 15,000 lbs; 1964 total 36,000 lbs
- Grand totals: 177,000 pounds (1963); 206,000 pounds (1964)

**Detoxification Operations**: Don Andriano of Utah reported potassium permanganate was spread from a bridge using four automatic fish meal dispensers at detoxification station. River level dropping and colder-than-anticipated water required more chemical than planned. Station located approximately 35 miles below last application station. Water remained "hot" for three and one-half days after rotenone application ceased. Complete report to be published.

**Project Vella Uniform**: Tom Trelease and Al Jonez of Nevada reported excellent cooperation among all agencies. Fish losses substantial but not excessive: approximately 400 fish weighing 800 pounds plus millions of shad. More game fish lost in first test; more scavenger fish lost in subsequent test at same site.

**Freshwater Jellyfish**: Nevada reported appearance of freshwater jellyfish in [[Lake Mead]], last reported there in 1954. Nothing known about it yet.

**Arizona River Investigations**: Foster reported on Arizona's river investigations (copy attached to original minutes).

**Black Molly in Salton Sea**: California reported [[Black Molly]] now found by millions in [[Salton Sea]]. Fishermen can use dipnets to collect them as bait for [[Corvina]]. Fish never exceed four inches in length. California sportsmen requested legalization for bait on river. California recommended no action until more information available about potential injury to river fishery.

**Species Introduction Proposals**: Agenda listed planned introductions by state:
- Arizona: [[Striped Bass]], [[Flathead Catfish]]
- California: [[Brown Trout]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Flathead Catfish]]
- Nevada: [[Cutthroat Trout]], [[Kokanee Salmon]]
