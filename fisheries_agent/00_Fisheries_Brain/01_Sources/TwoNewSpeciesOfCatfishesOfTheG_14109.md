# Two New Species of Catfishes of the Genera Nannorhamdia and Imparales (Family Pimelodidae) from Central America

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pimelodidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes two new small pimelodid catfishes from Central America: [[Nannorhamdia Lineata]] from Costa Rica and [[Imparales Panamensis]] from Panama. The author, William A. Bussing, reports the first record of the genus [[Nannorhamdia]] in Central America, which was previously known only from South America. Both species are interpreted as relict populations, remnants of a once wider distribution linked to northern South America. The paper provides diagnoses, morphometric and meristic counts, coloration, habitat descriptions and distribution data for both species.

## Background & Description

The study draws on recent collections by Horace Loftin (1965, Panama), Albert Greenberg (Costa Rica), and the author and Oscar Blanco B. (1967, Costa Rica). Additional *Imparales* specimens were collected in 1967 in the Chucunaque and Balsas drainages of Panama by members of the [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], Pacific Northwest Laboratory, working under a sub-contract for the Interoceanic Sea Level Canal Feasibility Study. The paper was published in *Contributions in Science* by the [[Los Angeles County Museum of Natural History]]. Previously, [[Imparales]] was known only from a name in Miller's (1966) checklist of Central American fishes.

### Nannorhamdia lineata, new species

- **Holotype:** LACM 30688-1 (2, 74.1 mm SL), Quebrada 36 (elev. 80 m), 12 km W of Pueblo Río Claro, Puntarenas Province, Costa Rica; 5 January 1967.
- **Paratypes:** Five lots totalling 26 specimens (18, 3, 2, 2 and 1 specimens) across UCR, LACM, BMNH, USNM collections, plus one specimen from Río Salamá Nuevo (21 January 1963, A. Greenberg).
- **Diagnosis:** A *Nannorhamdia* with a pronounced lateral stripe running from the snout tip to the caudal base, short maxillary barbels that do not pass the pectoral fin base, subequal caudal lobes, 8–11 total gill rakers, 7–9 anal fin rays (principal rays), no prominent dorsal blotches, and unserrated pectoral and dorsal spines.
- **Closest relative:** [[Nannorhamdia Spurrellii]] from the Río San Juan drainage, Colombia. That species has a narrower internarial and interorbital distance, shallower caudal peduncle, longer maxillary and mental barbels, more gill rakers (12 total in the type), and a more prominent lateral stripe.
- **Dimorphism:** The three smallest specimens (48.7–55.3 mm SL) are males, and the seven larger ones (58.6–74.1 mm SL) are females. Tubular genital papillae occur on males, while females usually have thicker, conical papillae.
- **Etymology:** *lineata* refers to the prominent lateral stripe.

### Imparales panamensis, new species

- **Holotype:** USNM 204692 (4, 62.3 mm SL), creek crossing on the south side of Santa Fe, Veraguas Province, Panama (elev. 305 m); 9 February 1962; H. Loftin and E. L. Tyson.
- **Paratypes:** USNM 204693 (40, 23.4–71.5 mm SL), plus lots from the Río Membrillo, Río Chucunaque (at Ualá), Río Congo and Quebrada Coho in Darién Province, collected by Battelle NW in March 1967.
- **Diagnosis:** Separated from its only known congener, *Imparales mariai* Schultz, by a series of proportional and meristic differences. Key traits include a shorter adipose fin (26.1–33.6% vs. 35.6% of SL), greater head width (13.1–17.5% vs. 12.7%), shorter maxillary barbels (22.1–31.0% vs. 35.3%), more pectoral rays (i,7–8 vs. i,6), and fewer gill rakers (3–7, usually 5, vs. about 7).
- **Taxonomic decision:** The author retains specific rank because the differences are consistent across the range, and the Cordillera Oriental forms a barrier between the Panamanian and Río Meta (Orinoco) faunas.
- **Etymology:** *panamensis* refers to the principal known distribution in Panama.
- **Generic amendment:** The diagnosis of *Imparales* (Schultz 1944b) was revised to broaden pelvic insertion, adipose-caudal confluence, and anal fin ray characters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Nannorhamdia lineata:** The type locality, Quebrada 36, is a small tributary of the Río Esquinas, which flows into the upper Golfo Dulce in southern Costa Rica. The region receives over 4.5 meters of precipitation annually. The stream is one to four meters wide, with shallow rapids and small pools no deeper than 0.5 meter. The current is moderate, the bottom is gravel and sand, and grasses line the shore. Water temperature at collection was 27°C with good visibility. Twenty-two fish species were collected at the site by poisoning with Pro-Noxfish. Other siluriforms present were *Rhamdia wagneri*, *Pimelodella chagresi*, *Hypostomus plecostomus* and *Pygidium striatum*. The Spanish summary states that the species is restricted to the Río Grande de Térraba basin.

**Imparales panamensis:** The type locality near Santa Fe is a swift stream up to 7 meters wide and about 1 meter deep, with large and small rocks and sand on the bottom. The water was clear at collection. The site lies at the foot of Cerro Tuto, a tributary of the Río Santa María, which drains to the Golfo de Parita in the Golfo de Panamá. Twelve other fish species were collected with the holotype, including *Pimelodella chagresi* and *Pygidium striatum*.

**Distribution:** *I. panamensis* occurs from west of the Canal Zone to near the Colombian border on the Pacific slope, and in the Río Chagres Basin on the Atlantic versant. Most specimens examined came from the Río Chucunaque and Río Congo drainages in eastern Panama. Only one locality west of the Canal, in Veraguas Province, was sampled. The author notes that *Imparales* specimens from the Río Chagres and Río Balsas (Battelle NW) were not examined.

**Biogeography:** The disjunct Middle American populations of both genera indicate a formerly much larger range. The center of dispersal for both genera is northern South America. Other *Nannorhamdia* species occur in the Atrato, Magdalena, San Juan and Orinoco drainages of Colombia, as well as in Venezuela, Brazil and Bolivia. The author suggests Middle America once had a more favorable environment that allowed these catfishes to extend at least as far north as Costa Rica and Panama. The environmental factors that now restrict their distribution remain unclear, especially for these stenoecious (narrowly adapted) forms.

## Key Findings

- **New genus record:** *Nannorhamdia* is recorded for the first time in Central America.
- **Two new species:** *Nannorhamdia lineata* (Costa Rica) and *Imparales panamensis* (Panama) are described and diagnosed.
- **Relict status:** Both species are interpreted as relict populations of a formerly wider, continuous distribution linked to South American ancestors.
- **Diagnostic separation:** *N. lineata* differs from its close relative *N. spurrellii* in internarial and interorbital distances, caudal peduncle depth, barbel length, gill raker count and stripe prominence. *I. panamensis* differs from *I. mariai* in adipose fin length, head width, barbel length, pectoral ray count and gill raker number.
- **Morphological characters:** The lateral stripe of *N. lineata* is its key diagnostic feature. Eyes have free dorsal margins in well-preserved specimens, which separates the species from *Cetopsorhamdia*.
- **Spine clarification:** Schultz (1944a) showed that the apparent dorsal and pectoral spines of *Nannorhamdia* are fractured flexible distal segments of the first pectoral ray, which resolves a long-standing confusion with *Cetopsorhamdia*.
- **Diagnostic distance metric:** In *Nannorhamdia* (*lineata*, *spurrellii*, *nemacheir*), the anus-to-anal-fin-origin distance is 17.5–25.1% of SL, versus 10.1–14.0% in *Cetopsorhamdia*. This may aid genus separation.
- **Sexual dimorphism in *N. lineata*:** Size separates sexes in the sampled specimens, and genital papilla shape is usually, but not always, consistent with sex.
- **Taxonomic questions:** The author notes that *Nannorhamdia macrocephala* (Miles 1943) should be re-examined for its reported painful spines.
- **Open questions:** The environmental factors limiting present-day distribution of these narrowly adapted catfishes are not yet known.
