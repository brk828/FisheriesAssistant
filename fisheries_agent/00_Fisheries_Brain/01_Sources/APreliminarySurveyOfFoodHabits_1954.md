# A Preliminary Survey of Food Habits of the Fish and Physico-Chemical Conditions of the Water of Three Strip-mine Lakes

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This 1954 study (Maupin, Wells & Leist; Kansas State Teachers College, Pittsburg) represents the second phase of a cooperative research program with the Kansas Forestry, Fish, and Game Commission aimed at improving fish production in strip-mined areas of Southeastern Kansas. The researchers conducted a comparative limnological and food-habit analysis of three [[Strip-mine Lakes]] in Crawford County, Kansas — **Whitmer Pit**, **North Jones Pit**, and **South Jones Pit** — spanning October 1950–August 1951 and June–July 1952. The study assessed physico-chemical water conditions, benthic organism abundance/volume, and stomach contents of 241 [[Centrarchidae|centrarchid fishes]] to determine dietary reliance on benthos. Key conclusion: benthic organisms, despite being present in adequate numbers in two of the three lakes, are **not the primary food source** for these fish populations, with non-benthic prey (e.g., [[Cladocera]], [[Copepoda]], [[Hyallela]]) featuring prominently in diets.

## Site & Habitat Characteristics

| Lake | Age | Area | Max Depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **North Jones Pit** | ~20 years | ~11 acres | ~24 ft | Long, steep banks, little aquatic vegetation, dense herbaceous spoil-bank growth |
| **South Jones Pit** | ~20 years | ~7 acres | ~29 ft | Long, steep banks, sparse vegetation |

- No lake had drainage except seepage.
- Despite Whitmer's greater age and shallow, vegetated habitat, physico-chemical conditions across all three lakes were broadly similar.

## Physico-Chemical Findings

### Temperature
- Winter surface temps: 2–8 °C.
- Summer (Jun–Aug) surface avg: ~28 °C in all lakes.
- Summer bottom temps: North Jones ~19 °C, South Jones ~21 °C, Whitmer ~26 °C.
- No marked thermal stratification detected in any lake.

### Light Penetration (Secchi disc)
- **Whitmer Lake**: clearest overall (nearly 2× the others); max 133 in (December), min 79 in (July).
- **North Jones Lake**: max 64 in (May), min 22 in (March).
- **South Jones Lake**: max 55 in (July), min 16 in (April).
- Deep light penetration suggested (per Edminster 1947) a potentially inadequate plankton supply.

### Hydrogen Ion Concentration (pH)
- Generally alkaline (7.7–8.3) at surface in all lakes at all times.
- Exception: North Jones Lake bottom water dropped to pH 6.7 (acidic) in July–August.

### Dissolved Oxygen
- Winter: ~12 ppm at surface and bottom in all lakes.
- Minimum surface O₂: Whitmer 5.5 ppm (Aug); North Jones 6.3 ppm (Aug); South Jones 8.1 ppm (Jun).
- Bottom O₂ dropped below 4 ppm in summer months in all lakes; minimums: Whitmer 1.6 ppm (Jun), North Jones 0.0 ppm (Aug), South Jones 1.0 ppm (Aug).
- Dissolved oxygen never fell below the ecologically required 4 ppm except at bottom depths in summer.

### Free Carbon Dioxide
- Typically absent from surface water most months; higher in bottom water, especially summer.
- Max surface CO₂: 2.1 ppm (North Jones, October).
- Max bottom CO₂: Whitmer 5.6 ppm (Jun); North Jones 27.5 ppm (Jun); South Jones 14 ppm (Aug).

### Carbonates/Bicarbonates
- All carbonates likely present as bicarbonates (pH never exceeded 8.3).
- Max bicarbonate: Whitmer 145 ppm (Mar); North Jones 271 ppm (Aug); South Jones 135 ppm (Mar).
- North Jones consistently ~50% higher bicarbonate concentration than the other two lakes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Benthic Organisms Identified
- **Insect nymphs**: [[Hexagenia]], [[Caenis]], [[Zygoptera]], [[Anisoptera]], [[Sialis]]
- **Dipteran larvae**: [[Dixa]], [[Corethra]], [[Chironomus]], [[Ceratopogoniae]]
- Occasional: beetle larvae, [[Nematoda]], snails (Families [[Planorbidae]] and [[Physidae]])
- [[Crayfish]] present but evaded Peterson dredge sampling; excluded from benthos data.
- *Corethra* and *Chironomus* larvae were the numerically dominant benthic taxa across all lakes, with high monthly variability.
- Benthos generally more abundant in winter/spring, declining in summer (likely due to larval emergence into adult insect forms).

### Fish Diet — Key Benthic Food Items
Only a subset of benthic taxa were significantly represented in fish stomachs:
- [[Hexagenia]] nymphs
- [[Anisoptera]] nymphs
- [[Chironomus]] larvae
- [[Corethra]] larvae

- [[Ostracoda]]
- [[Copepoda]]
- [[Cladocera]]
- [[Decapoda]]
- [[Hyallela]] (amphipod) — notably eaten by 100% of *Chaenobryttus coronarius* specimens examined

## Key Findings

1. **Physico-chemical similarity across lake ages**: Despite a 30-year age difference, the older Whitmer Lake and younger Jones Lakes showed largely comparable physico-chemical profiles — all within limits suitable for a thriving warm-water fish population (per Ellis 1937; Lagler 1950).

2. **Whitmer Lake's distinguishing traits**: Substantially clearer water (Secchi transparency) and richer aquatic macrophyte growth compared to both Jones lakes.

3. **North Jones Lake anomalies**: Bicarbonate concentration ~50% higher than other lakes; bottom water became acidic (pH 6.7) in summer; benthic organism *volume* was persistently low despite adequate organism *counts*, classifying it as comparatively poor in potential fish food per Lagler's (1950) productivity criteria (>50 organisms/ft² and >2 cc volume/ft²).

4. **Benthos productivity assessment**: Whitmer and South Jones Lakes met Lagler's (1950) criteria for benthic food richness most of the time; North Jones did not, due to low biomass volume despite adequate counts.

5. **Diet-benthos mismatch**: A large percentage of fish stomachs contained **no benthic organisms** at all, instead containing non-benthic prey, and many stomachs were entirely empty. This indicates benthic organisms are **not the primary food source** for the centrarchid fishes studied.

6. **Proposed explanations for diet-benthos mismatch**:
   - Certain benthic organisms may not be readily available/accessible to fish as prey.
   - Fish may selectively consume only readily available food items.
   - Non-benthic species may be preferentially selected by fish when available (supported by aquarium observations that captive fish would consume all benthic organism types when no alternative was offered).

7. **Recommendation**: A systematic study of lake [[plankton]] communities was recommended to more fully characterize food availability, as the deep light penetration observed suggested plankton might be a limiting factor in these systems.

8. **Broader context**: This study builds on the initial phase by [[Burner and Leist (1953)]] on the [[College Farm Strip-mine Lake]], part of an ongoing effort to inform strip-mine lake fisheries management in Southeastern Kansas.
