# Biochemical Genetics of Crenichthys baileyi and C. nevadae (Pisces: Goodeidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This study applies [[Protein Electrophoresis]] to the springfishes [[Crenichthys Baileyi]] and [[Crenichthys Nevadae]], a genus that had never been examined by allozyme methods. The authors sampled 180 fish from six population groups and scored 80 presumptive loci. Most populations of *C. baileyi* showed almost no biochemical divergence, so distinct genetic profiles for its subspecies could not be built. In contrast, the two species differed by fixed allelic differences at several loci, which the authors interpret as evidence of reproductive isolation. Using a Rogers genetic distance, they estimate separation on the order of 80,000 years, with a large margin of error. The paper also questions the morphometric basis of the existing subspecies designations.

## Background & Description

*Crenichthys baileyi* ([[White River Springfish]]) lives in isolated springs along the fragmented course of the [[Pluvial White River]], a former tributary of the [[Colorado River]]. Five subspecies were recognized by Williams and Wilde (1981), each endemic to a single spring complex:

- *C. b. albivalis*: northernmost form, springs near Preston and Lund, Nevada
- *C. b. thermophilus*: Mormon, Hot River, and Moon River springs
- *C. b. grandis* (ENDANGERED): Hiko and Crystal springs
- *C. b. baileyi* (ENDANGERED): Ash Spring
- *C. b. moapae*: headwater springs of the Moapa River

*Crenichthys nevadae* ([[Railroad Valley Springfish]]) is derived from the same progenitor as *C. baileyi*. Railroad Valley once drained into the Pluvial White River, and the author links the divergence of the two forms to Pleistocene separation of the drainages.

The genus was long placed among killifishes (Cyprinodontidae). Recent work suggests these species belong to [[Goodeidae]], a family previously known only from the Central Highlands of Mexico. The authors note that goodeids were once widespread across western North America during an early Tertiary tropical forest period. Desertification from the Miocene onward eliminated suitable habitat and caused extinctions in the middle of the family's range, leaving the current disjunct distribution.

The authors criticize the morphometric basis of the subspecies designations. Williams and Wilde (1981) used size-adjusted ratios, which the paper argues can confound size correction and are statistically inappropriate (Bookstein et al., 1985; Sokal and Rohlf, 1969). Because ratios did not separate populations, Williams and Wilde adopted a six-distance-measure analysis. The paper argues that this complexity may mean either that real differences are absent or that multivariate variation is what matters for taxon recognition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occupy spring systems that are fragmented by desert basin topography. Populations are geographically isolated, which the authors link to allopatric divergence. Two spring systems are named as particular concerns:

- [[Hiko Spring]] and [[Ash Spring]] host endangered subspecies.
- [[Moapa National Wildlife Refuge]] lies within the range of *C. b. moapae*. The paper suggests that the EST2 variant in this population may reflect a historic connection to the [[Moapa River]] valley, unlike the [[Pahranagat Valley]] distribution of other populations.

The authors frame biochemical genetic data as useful for conservation and management. Identifying variation can clarify population dynamics and guide decisions about these spring-restricted fishes.

## Key Findings

**Study design and sampling**
- The 180 fish were drugged with tricaine methanesulfonate. Comparative runs showed the drug had no effect on allele expression.
- Population sample sizes: *C. nevadae* (30); *C. b. albivalis*, Preston Spring (21); *C. b. thermophilus*, Hot River (20) and Mormon Spring (20); *C. b. grandis*, Hiko Spring (30); *C. b. baileyi*, Ash Spring (30); *C. b. moapae*, Moapa National Wildlife Refuge (29).
- Liver and muscle tissue were homogenized and run on 7.5% acrylamide vertical slab gels using [[Multiphasic Electrophoresis]] at 4–8 °C and 30 V/cm for 2–3 h.
- Each population sample was treated as an operational taxonomic unit (OTU), not as a conventional taxon.
- Analyses were run in BIOSYS-1 using UPGMA clustering, with Rogers (1972) and Nei (1978) genetic distance and identity measures.

**Loci and variation**
- Excluding acetylesterases, 25 presumptive loci were detected, and 23 were monomorphic within and among species.
- The text reports 80 presumptive loci scored in total, including the esterase zones. This count is used in the phenogram analysis.
- *C. b. baileyi*: a more anodal Pgm-3 variant appeared in four Preston Spring fish, giving 3% heterozygosity. No other within-species variation was found.
- Two fixed differences separated *C. baileyi* from *C. nevadae*. *C. baileyi* had less anodal Pep-F and Pgm-3 electromorphs.
- Acetylesterase (EST) activity was scored as five zones, EST1–EST5. EST1 and EST3 differed between species, EST2 differed in *C. b. moapae*, and EST4 showed at least two bands distinguishing the species.
- Including the esterase loci gave a 7% level of polymorphism for *C. baileyi*. *C. nevadae* remained at 0%.

**Phenetic relationships** (see [[Rogers Genetic Distance]] and [[UPGMA Phenetic Analysis]])
- *C. baileyi* and *C. nevadae* were separated by 0.81 distance units, driven by five fixed interspecific differences.
- *C. b. moapae* differed from other *C. baileyi* OTUs by 0.04 units, owing to a fixed difference at EST3.
- The Preston Spring OTU differed by only 0.008 units, related to the uncommon Pgm-3(b) allele.

**Interpretation**
- Almost no divergence exists among most *C. baileyi* populations, so subspecies cannot be defined by biochemical profiles.
- The authors consider the fixed differences evidence that *C. baileyi* and *C. nevadae* are reproductively isolated taxa.
- The genetic distance suggests isolation of roughly 80,000 years, with a large margin of error (Thorpe, 1982).
- The authors suggest the absence of Pgm-3(b) in other samples may reflect sampling artifact.
- Comparable low levels of difference were reported in the [[Death Valley Cyprinodont Complex]] (Turner, 1974).

**Methodological recommendations**
- Over 120 proteins can be assayed electrophoretically (Table 4), but only 80 loci were scored here.
- The authors recommend surveying as many proteins as possible, ideally 30–40, because prior predictions of variability are unreliable in an understudied genus.
- Butyrylesterases were not examined and could be more variable than the acetylesterases studied.
- Defining esterase loci further would require other tissues (such as serum for cholinesterase), alternative substrates (fluorescein, bromoindoxyl, and methylumbelliferyl esters), and selective inhibitors (such as acetazolamide and eserine sulphate).

**Open questions**
- How is variation distributed within and between populations?
- Are there protein variants unique to populations, subspecies, or species?
- Does current taxonomy hold up under genetic evidence?
