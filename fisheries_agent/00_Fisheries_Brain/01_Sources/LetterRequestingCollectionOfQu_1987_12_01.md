## Executive Summary
This document is a 1987 letter from Dr. Mark R. Stromberg (Resident Director, Appleton-Whittell Research Ranch Sanctuary, National Audubon Society) to Dr. R. Roy Johnson of the Cooperative National Park Resources Studies Unit. The letter requests permission and Park Service permits to collect 20 individuals of **[[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*) from **[[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]]** for genetic (electrophoretic) comparison against a captive refugium population established at the Research Ranch Sanctuary in 1978. The core scientific question is one of **stock purity/provenance**: whether the sanctuary's refugium population, founded from 45 fish supplied by Dr. Boyd Kynard, represents a genetically "pure" Quitobaquito lineage or was potentially admixed with fish from other source populations (e.g., Santa Clara Slough).

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological data on the species is presented in this letter itself; however, it references an existing comparative study:
  - **Daphne Rainey (July 1987, unpublished manuscript)**: "A morphological analysis of two subspecies of *Cyprinodon macularius*," conducted under Dr. Jeff Mitton (University of Colorado), with permission from the **[[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]**.
  - This study morphologically compared the **[[Santa Clara Slough]]** population (representing nominal *C. macularius macularius*) against the Research Ranch refugium stock (presumed *C. m. eremus* / Quitobaquito lineage).
- Taxonomic reference cited: Miller & Fuiman (1987), *Copeia* 1987(3):595–609, "Description and conservation status of *Cyprinodon macularius eremus*, a new subspecies of pupfish from Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, Arizona" — the formal description establishing *C. m. eremus* as a distinct subspecies from *C. m. macularius*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native habitat**: [[Quitobaquito Springs]], located within Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, Arizona — an isolated desert spring system representative of relict Sonoran Desert aquatic habitats.
- **Refugium population**: Established at the Appleton-Whittell Research Ranch Sanctuary (Elgin, Arizona) via a translocation of 45 individuals in November 1978, sourced by Dr. Boyd Kynard and documented in a 1979 report to the National Park Service (P.O. No. PX-8100-700351).
- **Conservation status context**: The refugium population is legally referenced under the Federal Register, 1986, Vol. 51(61):10843, indicating formal recognition as part of an endangered subspecies conservation/reintroduction strategy — a common practice for isolated desert fish taxa vulnerable to extinction due to extremely restricted natural range.
- **Population status**: The letter states the refugium population "is thriving," suggesting successful ex-situ conservation management as of 1987.
- Related comparative population: **[[Santa Clara Slough]]** population of *C. macularius macularius*, used as an outgroup/comparison stock in the morphological study.

## Key Findings
- A translocated refugium population of **[[Cyprinodon macularius eremus]]** was successfully established outside its native range at the Research Ranch Sanctuary in 1978 and remained viable/thriving nearly a decade later (as of 1987).
- Concern raised by **[[Dean Hendrickson]]** that the original 1978 founder stock may have been contaminated with individuals from other source populations, raising questions about the genetic "purity" of the refugium stock relative to the true Quitobaquito source population.
- Preliminary morphological comparison (Rainey 1987, unpublished) had already been conducted between the Santa Clara Slough population and the Research Ranch stock.
- **Dr. Jeff Mitton** (University of Colorado) intended to follow up with a detailed **electrophoretic genetic analysis** to formally validate/compare subspecies-level genetic identity between the sanctuary refugium stock and the original Organ Pipe (Quitobaquito) source population.
- This letter formally requests collection of **20 individual specimens** of *C. m. eremus* directly from Organ Pipe National Monument, to be frozen immediately post-capture and shipped to Dr. Mitton's lab in Boulder, Colorado, for this genetic comparison — illustrating a standard protocol for **electrophoretic/genetic voucher sampling** in endangered fish conservation genetics.
- The request required multi-agency coordination and support letters from **[[Dean Hendrickson]]** and **Dr. W.L. Minckley** (Arizona State University), reflecting the collaborative regulatory oversight (National Park Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department) typical of endangered desert fish research.
- This correspondence exemplifies the broader conservation genetics issue of **stock verification in ex-situ refugium populations** for critically endangered, geographically restricted desert fishes such as the pupfishes of the genus *[[Cyprinodon]]*.
