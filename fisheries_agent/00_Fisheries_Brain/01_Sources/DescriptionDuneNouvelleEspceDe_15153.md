# Description of Echinogammarus villaticus sp. n. (Amphipoda, Gammaridae) from Portugal

#amphipoda #gammaridae #carcinology #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1974 taxonomic paper by [[Amilcar Mateus]], published in the *Anais da Faculdade de Ciências do Porto* (vol. LVII, fasc. 1), describes a new amphipod crustacean, [[Echinogammarus villaticus]] sp. n. The species was collected in 1958, 1973 and 1974 from a spring and its irrigation channel at the Quinta das Lagrimas near [[Coimbra]], Portugal. The author separates it from the two closest congeners, [[Echinogammarus aquilifer]] and [[Echinogammarus meridionalis]], on body size, antennal and gnathopod morphology, uropod III, spination and setation. The paper also discusses the unstable taxonomy of the genus [[Genus Echinogammarus]]. It contains no fish data.

## Background & Description

**Context and aims.** The work forms part of an ongoing survey of Portuguese [[Amphipoda]] fauna. The author was preparing an updated faunistic checklist that would include his own collections and those of other workers, notably [[Stock]] and [[Pinkster]], who had added new Gammaridae forms to the known fauna. The study was funded by Project PB4 of the [[Instituto de Alta Cultura]].

**Material and methods.**
- Collection dates: June 1958, April 1973 and March 1974.
- Population density was described as high. Adults were abundant, many females were ovigerous, and many pairs were seen in pre-copulation.
- Specimens were dissected, mounted in polyvinyl-lactophenol with lignin pink stain, and drawn with a camera lucida.
- Material is preserved in 70° alcohol in the "Coleccao de Amphipoda Portugueses" at the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]], Faculdade de Ciências do Porto.
- **Holotype:** male, 6.8 mm, collected 12 July 1958 at Coimbra, Quinta das Lagrimas, by A. Mateus et al. (label no. 20a).

**Morphological description (male).**
- **Body:** arched and brownish-grey. Median length 5.5 mm; largest specimens 6.8 mm. Proportions are roughly head 1/7, mesosome 3.5/7, metasome 1.2/7 and urosome 1.8/7 of total length.
- **Head:** trapezoidal, with inferior lobes strongly projected forward and pointed. Eyes are medium-sized, black and reniform.
- **Antenna I:** longer than half the body. The principal flagellum has 23–28 articles, and the accessory flagellum has 4 articles, the fourth rudimentary.
- **Antenna II:** about 4/3 the length of antenna I. The flagellum has 11–15 articles, and the glandular cone is well developed.
- **Mouthparts:** include mandibles with a molar process, maxillae I and II, a posterior lip, and a maxilliped with spines and a palp of four articles.
- **Gnathopods:** gnathopod I has a palm that is strongly oblique and a dactyl with a long, sharp nail. Gnathopod II has a palm that is slightly oblique and undulate, with a strong spine at its angle.
- **Pereiopods and pleopods:** pereiopods III–VII are relatively weak and armed with spines. Pleopods are of similar length, with plumose setae.
- **Uropods:** uropod II is the shortest and uropod I the longest. Uropod III has a conical outer branch, about five times longer than the inner branch, ending in five strong spines.

**Female.** The female is 5.3 mm long. Its gnathopod I propodus is narrower relative to the carpus, and its palm is less oblique. Its dactyl is less robust. Gnathopod II is also narrower, with a less developed dactyl. Spination and setation are less pronounced, especially on antenna II. Brood plates (lamellae incubatrices) are spatulate and elongate.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Specimens were found among small calcareous pebbles, under stones and among sparse vegetation dominated by hepatics (liverworts). Some animals swam in the water column.
- **Spring:** Water flows slowly and calmly.
- **Irrigation channel (*conduite*):** Flow velocity varies. Animals occur in low-gradient spots among coarse sand that forms the channel bed.
- **Water and substrate:** Water is clear, pH about 7, and flows over calcareous terrain. The spring bed and vault and some U-shaped channel sections are also calcareous.
- **Type locality:** The "terra typica" is the mouth of the Fonte das Lagrimas ("Fountain of Tears") in the Quinta das Lagrimas, south of the [[Mondego River]], near its left bank.
- **Comparative localities:** The author also collected [[Echinogammarus meridionalis]] from the [[Rio Alcaide]] near [[Alcaria]], which he treats as a point of comparison.

## Key Findings

1. **New species.** The author concludes that the material represents a new species, *Echinogammarus villaticus* sp. n., and places it in the genus [[Echinogammarus]] on the basis of the original [[Stebbing]] (1899) generic definition.

2. **Closest relatives.** The species is most similar to *E. aquilifer* [[Pinkster]], 1969 (described from the Pyrenees) and to *E. meridionalis* Pinkster, 1973 (predominantly Iberian). Key differences are:
   - **Versus *E. meridionalis*:** the congener is more than twice as large; cephalic lateral lobes are nearly straight; inferior lobes are short; antennae I and II have more flagellar articles; gnathopods differ; uropod III has a more developed second article in the outer branch.
   - **Versus *E. aquilifer*:** the congener is almost twice as large; its eyes are narrower; its cephalic lateral lobes are less projected forward; its antennal flagella have more articles; the second article of the mandibular palp differs; gnathopods differ; uropod III has a more evident second article.

3. **Diagnostic characters.** The author highlights body size, antennal form, gnathopod shape, uropod III, spination and setation as the most useful separating characters. A comparison table summarises these for the three species.

4. **Taxonomic problems in the genus.** The paper notes that the limits of *Echinogammarus* have widened greatly through the addition of new species and changing criteria. Repeated species transfers and changes in genus and subgenus rank have made the genus's systematics confused. The author suggests that subgenera or "species groups" may be a better framework, but he defers a full revision. For this reason he does not assign the new species to any existing species group (for example, the [[Berilloni-Group]]).

5. **Biological observations.** The population was dense, with many ovigerous females and pre-copulatory pairs, indicating a reproducing population at the type locality.
