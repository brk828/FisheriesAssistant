## Executive Summary

This 1899 paper by Barton A. Bean (Proceedings of the U.S. National Museum, Vol. XXI) documents a collection of eighteen fish species gathered by [[E. W. Nelson]] and [[E. A. Goldman]] during 1892 and 1894 field expeditions for the U.S. Department of Agriculture across various localities in Mexico, including the highland [[Rio Quitzeo]] / [[Lake Quitzeo]] (Michoacán, elevation ~6,000 ft), [[Lake Catemaco]] (elevation ~1,500 ft), [[Santa Maria]], [[Mirador]], and the [[Tehuantepec]] river system. The collection's centerpiece is the description of a new species, **[[Platypoecilus quitzeoensis]]**, a small poeciliid from Lake Quitzeo. The paper also provides valuable field observations on the distribution and habitat preferences of [[Anableps dovii]] in the Tehuantepec drainage, and records several cichlids, gobiids, and eleotrids from coastal/lowland Mexican waters. The work is taxonomically descriptive, providing meristic counts (fin ray and scale formulas) and morphometric ratios for each species, typical of late 19th-century systematic ichthyology.

## Background & Description

### Platypoecilus quitzeoensis (new species)
- **Fin counts:** D. 13; A. 13
- **Scales:** 30 in lateral series, 10 transverse
- **Body form:** Compressed, back elevated; head small, depressed, flat on top; snout short
- **Proportions:** Body depth contained 2.8× in length to origin of middle caudal rays; head 3.5× in this length; eye diameter 3.25× in head length; interorbital width 2.67× in head length
- **Mouth:** Small, obliquely cleft; lower jaw heavy and projecting
- **Dentition:** Conic teeth; upper jaw teeth in irregular series; lower jaw teeth very small, close-set, irregularly arranged
- **Fin placement:** Dorsal origin anterior to anal origin, positioned midway between upper jaw tip and end of caudal rays; first anal ray beneath sixth dorsal ray
- **Coloration (alcohol-preserved):** Light brown body with darker traces on back; dark brown interorbital space and scale edges; three dark bars on posterior body (median line to anal origin; median line to anal base end; midway between anal end and caudal origin); two dark spots on caudal peduncle; fins pale
- **Size:** Type specimen 1.5 inches long
- **Type specimen:** No. 48209, U.S.N.M.

### Other Notable Species (Meristic Data)
- **[[Dorosoma mexicanum]]:** D. 14; A. 22–24; scales 39, transverse 11; scutes 15-16 plus 9-10; head <1/3 body length; eye 3.5× in head length
- **[[Fundulus robustus]]:** D. 14; A. 15; scales 38, transverse 15
- **[[Heros maculipinnis]]:** D. XVI, 9-10; A. VII-VIII, 8; scales 6-29-11
- **[[Heros fenestratus]]:** D. XVIII, 11; A. VI, 10; scales 5-30-13; gill rakers 3-8, short and club-shaped, widely separated
- **[[Sicydium plumieri]]:** D. VI-I, 10; A. I, 10; scales ~76, transverse 23-25; ctenoid scales with brown-bordered exposed portions; black lateral band in juveniles that breaks into cross-bands with age
- **[[Gobiomorus dormitator]]:** D. VI, 11; A. 11; scales 57, 21 transverse; narrower interorbital space than typical Dominican specimens

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Lake Quitzeo]] / Rio Quitzeo (Michoacán, ~6,000 ft elevation):** A high-elevation lake system supporting an endemic poeciliid (*Platypoecilus quitzeoensis*), as well as *[[Leuciscus nigrescens]]* (with well-developed eggs, suggesting local breeding), *[[Chirostoma jordani]]*, *[[Fundulus robustus]]*, *[[Goodea atripinnis]]*, and *[[Notropis altus]]* — indicating a diverse highland lentic freshwater community.
- **[[Lake Catemaco]] (~1,500 ft elevation, south of Vera Cruz):** Habitat for juvenile *[[Dorosoma mexicanum]]* (2–4 inches).
- **[[Santa Maria]]:** A lowland/coastal locality yielding the greatest species diversity in the collection, including *[[Pimelodus petenensis]]*, *[[Symbranchus marmoratus]]*, *[[Agonostoma monticola]]*, *[[Poecilia mexicana]]*, cichlids (*Heros maculipinnis*, *Heros fenestratus*), and gobioid/eleotrid fishes (*Sicydium plumieri*, *Chonophorus mexicanus*, *Gobiomorus dormitator*) — indicative of a mixed freshwater-estuarine assemblage.
- **[[Tehuantepec]] River system:** Detailed ecological notes on *[[Anableps dovii]]* — a surface-dwelling, four-eyed fish present year-round; larger individuals concentrate near the river mouth in the main current, while smaller individuals occupy shallow lagoons and ponds connected to the river, spanning a range of ~3 to 50 miles inland from the sea. Breeding individuals were documented approximately 3 miles above Tehuantepec city (~18 miles from the sea).
- **[[Mirador]]:** Locality for *[[Pseudoxiphophorus bimaculatus]]* (4 males, 24 females), suggesting a female-skewed sex ratio in the sampled population.

## Key Findings

- Description of a new poeciliid species, **[[Platypoecilus quitzeoensis]]**, endemic to Lake Quitzeo, distinguished by body proportions, dentition, and a distinctive three-bar posterior color pattern.
- *[[Leuciscus nigrescens]]* collected from Lake Quitzeo is identified as conspecific with Girard's *Tigoma pulchra* (originally described from the Chihuahua River), extending the known range of this taxon.
- *[[Goodea atripinnis]]* specimens from Lake Quitzeo represent a "deeper" morphological form compared to type specimens (body depth 1/3.25 vs. 1/4 of body length), suggesting intraspecific morphological variation possibly linked to habitat.
- Detailed field ecology of *[[Anableps dovii]]* in the Tehuantepec river system documents ontogenetic habitat partitioning: larger fish in main river current near the mouth, smaller fish in shallow lagoons/ponds further inland, with year-round presence and localized breeding activity.
- The collection spans a significant elevational gradient (sea level to ~6,000 ft), illustrating the diversity of Mexican freshwater fish faunas across highland lake systems and lowland coastal rivers.
- Comparative morphometric data (e.g., interorbital width in *[[Gobiomorus dormitator]]*) suggest possible geographic variation between Mexican populations and those from other Caribbean localities (e.g., Dominica).
- Supplementary 1897 collections (not detailed morphologically in this note) added *Agonostomus* [sic] species, *[[Chirostoma humboldtianum]]*, *[[Heros beani]]*, and *[[Gobius banana]]* to the known Mexican ichthyofauna.
