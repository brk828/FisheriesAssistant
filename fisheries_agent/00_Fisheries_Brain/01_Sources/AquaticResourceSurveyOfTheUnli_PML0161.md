# Aquatic Resource Survey of the Unlined Coachella Canal

#freshwater-ecology #limnology #benthic-invertebrates #phytoplankton

## Executive Summary

This final report, prepared by John R. Baker and Larry J. Paulson of the [[Lake Head Limnological Research Center]] at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas, established a baseline inventory of aquatic resources in the unlined [[Coachella Canal]]. The study was funded by the [[Water and Power Resources Service]] (Order No. 1-01-30-04400) and was undertaken because the canal was losing water through seepage and was scheduled to be replaced with concrete lining under the [[Colorado River Salinity Control Project]]. Sampling took place on 9–10 November 1980, during a scheduled dewatering of the canal. The survey covered physical-chemical conditions, plankton, macrophytes and benthic invertebrates. No fish data are reported. The authors conclude that lining will reduce habitat diversity and benthic production, though periphyton on concrete may partly compensate, and they recommend a follow-up survey of the lined canal.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** The [[Water and Power Resources Service]] was evaluating canal lining as a water-conservation measure in the Lower Colorado River region. Little was known about the aquatic life of these canal systems, so a baseline was needed to judge the effects of lining.
- **Cooperating agencies:** Arizona State University, the California Department of Fish and Game, and the University of Nevada, Las Vegas.
- **Study area:** The canal runs north through the [[Imperial Valley]] and was dewatered for maintenance on 10–11 November 1980. Two stations were sampled:
  - **Station A** was located at checkdrop 5, about 5 miles from the All-American Canal diversion. Sampling was done above and below the checkdrop, and the below-checkdrop area included a side channel.
  - **Station B** was located between siphons 2 and 3, about 12 miles from the diversion, and was adjacent to a [[Phragmites]] stand.
- **Methods overview:**
  - *Physical-chemical:* Temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH and conductivity were measured with a [[Hydrolab Water Quality Analyzer]]. Water samples were collected with a 3-liter [[Van Dorn Sampler]], preserved on ice, frozen, and analyzed for nutrients, following [[Kellar et al. 1980]].
  - *Phytoplankton:* Samples (125 mL) were preserved with [[Lugol's Solution]] and counted with an inverted microscope.
  - *Zooplankton:* A qualitative net sample (80 µm mesh Wisconsin net) was towed in the current for 2 minutes and identified at 40X.
  - *Macrophytes:* Plants were collected after dewatering and identified from regional floras.
  - *Benthos:* Samples were taken with an [[Ekman Dredge]] or [[Ponar Dredge]], washed through a No. 30 sieve, preserved in 10% formalin, and sorted in the laboratory.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry.** Temperature, dissolved oxygen, conductivity and pH were similar to those of the lower [[Colorado River]] system. Dissolved oxygen was at supersaturation levels, which indicates relatively high primary production. Phosphorus and nitrogen concentrations were low to moderate. Using [[Vollenweider 1970]] criteria, total phosphorus was low and total nitrogen was moderate. Total nitrogen to total phosphorus ratios were above 20 at both stations, which the authors interpret as phosphorus limitation following [[Schindler 1977]]. All forms of phosphorus and nitrogen declined between Stations A and B, which the authors attribute mainly to plant uptake and higher downstream production.

**Habitat structure.** The upper reach had a sand-silt substrate with side-channel beds of [[Potamogeton pectinatus]] and heavily vegetated banks. The reach below the checkdrop had a gravel-silt substrate and very little bank vegetation, and the authors describe its habitat as atypical. Station B had a detritus and silt substrate along a [[Phragmites]] stand. Emergent [[Phragmites]] and [[Typha]] formed dense stands along the banks.

**Trophic structure.** Phytoplankton was dominated by diatoms, and zooplankton was sparse. Dominant plankton types are typical of slow-moving water (Hynes 1970). Benthic production was highest in the vegetated side-channel and Phragmites habitats and lower in the mid-channel. The authors describe this pattern as typical of large river systems.

## Key Findings

**Physical-chemical conditions (9 November 1980; Table 1)**
- Total nitrogen was 408 µg/L at Station A and 360 µg/L at Station B.
- Nitrate nitrogen was about 154 µg/L at Station A and 123 µg/L at Station B.
- Ammonia nitrogen was 22 µg/L at Station A and 9 µg/L at Station B.
- Dissolved oxygen was 8.7 mg/L at Station A and 10.1 mg/L at Station B.
- Conductivity was about 1210 and 1220 µmhos/cm.
- Total phosphorus was at low levels, and dissolved phosphorus was lower still.
- Total phosphorus to nitrogen ratios were reported as roughly 20 at Station A and 29 at Station B (values partly garbled in the scan).

**Phytoplankton (Table 2)**
- 18 genera were identified. Diatoms (Bacillariophyta) were the most diverse group, with 12 genera.
- Overall phytoplankton numbers were low, except for [[Cyclotella glomerata]], which increased about tenfold from Station A to Station B.

**Zooplankton**
- The community was very depauperate. Only the protozoan *Centropyxis aculeata*, the rotifer *Euchlanis* sp., and the developmental stages of cyclopoid and harpacticoid copepods were found.
- *Euchlanis* sp. was dominant.

**Macrophytes**
- Only two submergent species were found: [[Potamogeton pectinatus]] (sago pondweed) and [[Myriophyllum exalbescens]] (water-milfoil).
- [[Potamogeton]] beds covered less than 10% of the canal area during dewatering, based on visual estimates.
- Dominant emergent plants were [[Phragmites]] (common reed) and [[Typha]] (cattails).

**Benthic invertebrates (Tables 3–6)**
- A total of 18 taxa were identified. This closely matches the earlier survey by [[Harsh 1980]].
- Above the checkdrop, in the [[Potamogeton]] bed, 8 taxa were found. The [[Corbicula manilensis]] (Asiatic clam) and oligochaetes were dominant, with Turbellaria, two Trichoptera species and chironomids also present.
- Mid-channel samples above the checkdrop were almost entirely [[Corbicula manilensis]], with few oligochaetes and chironomids.
- Below the checkdrop, side-channel fauna resembled the [[Potamogeton]] bed fauna but was less abundant, except for chironomids. Mid-channel samples were again dominated by Corbicula, chironomids and oligochaetes.
- At Station B, nearly all invertebrates were insects from four orders. Chironomids were the most numerous and most diverse group.
- [[Parargyractis confusalis]], a lepidopteran larva reported by Harsh (1980), was not found in this survey.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Lining the canal is expected to reduce habitat diversity. Loss of [[Macrophytes]] and uniform bank conditions would reduce benthic production, which could affect both invertebrate and fisheries resources.
- Macrophytes such as [[Potamogeton]] and [[Phragmites]] are unlikely to become established in the lined canal because the concrete substrate lacks suitable sediment.
- Concrete walls may support substantial [[Periphyton]] growth. Harsh (1980) reported large numbers of *Parargyractis confusalis* larvae associated with periphyton. Periphyton could therefore support a productive invertebrate community, similar to communities described below [[Hoover Dam]].
- Reduced turbidity in a lined canal could increase phytoplankton production, though nutrient concentrations are low.
- The actual aquatic production of the lined canal cannot be determined from these data. The authors recommend a future survey of the lined canal to provide comparative data.
- Community composition and abundance are likely to vary with season.
