# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes - June 1996

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

Minutes from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT) meeting held June 25, 1996 in Tempe, Arizona. The team reviewed progress on native fish conservation actions across the southwestern United States, focusing on status assessments for [[Gila Chub]] and [[Roundtail Chub]], implementation of the [[Central Arizona Project]] biological opinion, recovery planning for [[Gila Topminnow]], and various translocation and habitat management initiatives. Key concerns included listing priorities for imperiled species, nonnative fish management, beaver reintroduction impacts, and restrictions on information sharing regarding work on tribal lands. The meeting involved representatives from Arizona State University, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Bureau of Land Management, U.S. Forest Service, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and Bureau of Reclamation.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] serves as an advisory body for conservation and recovery of imperiled native fishes in the southwestern United States, particularly Arizona and New Mexico. The team includes representatives from federal agencies (USFWS, USFS, USBLM, USBR), state wildlife agencies (AZGFD, NMGFD), academic institutions (Arizona State University), and international partners (Universidad Autonoma de Sonora). The team's mandate includes reviewing species status, recommending listing actions under the Endangered Species Act, advising on recovery plan development, and coordinating conservation actions across jurisdictions.

**Taxonomic Coverage**: The meeting addressed multiple cyprinid species including [[Gila intermedia]] (Gila chub), [[Gila robusta]] (roundtail chub), [[Gila purpurea]] (Yaqui chub), [[Gila ditaenia]] (Sonora chub), [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail), and [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub); poeciliid species [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis]] (Gila topminnow) and [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis]] (Yaqui topminnow); the cyprinid [[Tiaroga cobitis]] (loach minnow); catostomid [[Xyrauchen texanus]] (razorback sucker); and ictalurid [[Ictalurus pricei]] (Yaqui catfish).

**Geographic Scope**: Conservation efforts span major drainages including the [[Gila River]] basin, [[Colorado River]] basin, [[San Pedro River]], [[Salt River]], [[Verde River]], [[Santa Cruz River]], [[White River]], [[Aravaipa Creek]], and [[Virgin River]], extending into Sonora, Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Fragmentation**: Native fish populations face severe fragmentation due to water development, with species like [[Gila Topminnow]] unable to naturally recolonize extirpated sites. Minckley emphasized that topminnow populations historically experienced "feast and famine" fluctuations, with cold-winter die-offs in non-thermal waters followed by reinvasion from thermal refugia (springs, cienegas). Artificial fragmentation now requires human-mediated translocation for reestablishment.

**Nonnative Species Impacts**: Nonnative fish represent a primary threat across all systems. The team discussed exotic fish exclusion structures (EFIS) for [[Aravaipa Creek]], nonnative removal at Sabino Canyon and Kingfisher Ponds, and concerns that beaver reintroduction in the [[San Pedro River]] would increase pool habitat favoring nonnatives while decreasing riffle habitat preferred by native species like [[Agosia chrysogaster]] and [[Pantosteus clarki]].

**Hydrologic Alterations**: Water management through the [[Central Arizona Project]] and other developments continues to impact native fish habitat. The CAP biological opinion mandates monitoring, EFIS construction, nonnative management, and native fish conservation actions. Proposed flow increases in the [[Salt River]] (from 8-10 cfs to 50-60 cfs) raised concerns about impacts to native populations of roundtail chub, suckers, and longfin dace.

**Climate and Thermal Refugia**: Cold stress may regulate topminnow populations in the Gila basin, with thermal springs serving as critical refugia. Recent surveys found [[Sonora Chub]] persisting in pools during drought conditions in Sycamore Creek, Penasco Canyon, and California Gulch.

## Key Findings

**Species Status and Listing Recommendations**:
- [[Gila Chub]] status survey by B.D. DeMarais found declining populations; DFRT recommended moving species to candidate (formerly Category 1) status and proceeding with listing. Transplanted populations in Larry and Lousy canyons (AZGFD/USBLM joint action) are surviving and being monitored.
- [[Roundtail Chub]] considered in worse condition than Gila chub; DFRT reiterated earlier recommendation for listing. Minckley stated all status information needed for listing already exists and recommended listing entire genus *Gila* in the Colorado River basin. Status survey funding being considered under CAP biological opinion.
- USFWS abandoned Category 2 candidate list; former Category 2 species now termed "species of concern," tracked by The Nature Conservancy under contract. TNC list considered proprietary, raising concerns about public access to information.

**Recovery Planning**:
- [[Gila Topminnow]] recovery plan revision behind schedule; review sequence: 1) DFRT, 2) technical, 3) public. Mexican populations excluded from plan due to "unlisting."
- [[Rio Yaqui]] fishes recovery plan finalized and signed.
- Draft extirpation criteria for Gila topminnow and desert pupfish under review; DFRT recommended removing criteria from recovery plan and developing approach more consistent with species' "feast and famine" life history. Meeting scheduled for August 22, 1996 for site-by-site review.

**Translocation and Habitat Management**:
- New Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) signed among AZGFD, USBLM, and USFWS for [[Gila Topminnow]] transplants on USBLM lands. Arizona Game and Fish Commission verbalized (not in writing) that topminnow and desert pupfish transplants in Arizona would only occur under 10J (experimental) designation.
- [[Yaqui Topminnow]] stocked in [[West Turkey Creek]] but did not survive winter. [[Yaqui Catfish]] successfully spawned at Uvalde National Fish Hatchery; stock available for reintroduction.
- [[Loach Minnow]]: USFWS removed 64 individuals from East Fork White River to Alchesay NFH for refugium and propagation; one mortality. Transplants planned for Fort Apache Reservation with full protection under special rule. New population discovered at Three Forks in Black River system.
- Middle Bylas Spring (S2) renovation for [[Gila Topminnow]] underway by AZFRO. DFRT recommended mixing ASU and Hassayampa Spring S2-origin stock (~20 fish) for propagation at Arizona facilities rather than Dexter NFH.

**Beaver Reintroduction Controversy**:
- USBLM proposed beaver reintroduction to [[San Pedro River]] downstream from Highway 90 bridge to restore natural hydrologic processes, increase groundwater levels and base flows. DFRT expressed concerns that beaver would exacerbate nonnative fish problems by increasing pool habitat and decreasing riffles, potentially precluding native fish reintroduction. Team felt proposal lacked adequate detail on monitoring, control provisions, and conflicts with native fish goals. Recommendation to approach as controlled experiment rather than standard management action. USBLM plans to use fertile beaver; DFRT suggested sterilized individuals for better experimental control.

**Central Arizona Project Implementation**:
- [[Aravaipa Creek]] EFIS moving slowly due to landowner opposition; best site on San Carlos Apache land with limited tribal contact success. USBR offering to purchase land from willing sellers.
- Monitoring completed autumn 1995 except some Gila River sites lacking landowner permission. 1996 monitoring: AZGFD doing Gila/Salt Rivers, USBR doing CAP canal/San Pedro River, ASU doing SRP canals.
- No flathead catfish confirmed in San Pedro during 1995 sampling; reported Charleston specimen lacks voucher or photographic documentation.
- First-year project list contains mostly priority 1 recovery tasks with some priority 2. Policy and technical committees established with AZGFD, USBR, USFWS, NMGFD representation.
- DFRT requested clarification of role in CAP opinion implementation, particularly in project funding decisions.
- Santa Cruz River basin consultation using different approach: mitigation (EFIS, monitoring, nonnative management) incorporated upfront to avoid jeopardy opinion. Three applicants participating: Central Arizona Water Conservation District, Tohono O'Odham Tribe, City of Tucson.

**Information Access and Tribal Lands**:
- USFWS agreement with White Mountain Apache Tribe (signed by USFWS Director) grants tribe proprietary rights to all information on work done or funded by USFWS on reservation, including by Alchesay NFH. NEPA documents, plans, and reports unavailable to DFRT, Arizona Ecological Services Office, or public. Similar agreements approached by Navajo Nation and San Carlos Apache Tribe.
- DFRT expressed dismay at restrictions preventing access to information on loach minnow and topminnow work on reservation lands. Questioned how USFWS policy could supersede federal law (NEPA). Recommended Regional Director provide liaison from AZFRO (Pinetop) to DFRT meetings and supply information on reservation activities.

**Additional Conservation Actions**:
- [[Sonora Chub]] surveys in Sycamore Creek, Penasco Canyon, California Gulch found fish persisting in drought-reduced pools. No longfin dace confirmed in California Gulch; earlier AZGFD report identified as chub via photo. Hank-and-Yank Tank successfully plugged and not leaking.
- [[Virgin River]]: New red shiner removal plan under review; mitigation banking program proposed.
- Quitobaquito Spring: Heavy sampling by National Park Service, AZGFD, USFWS found no nonnative fish following earlier alert.
- Asian tapeworm problem at San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge under investigation; possible connection to Dexter NFH stock.
- Crayfish distribution synthesis underway (Marsh); records exist for *Orconectes virilis* and *Procambarus clarki* with taxonomic uncertainties. *Pacifastacus* possibly in Colorado River.

**Team Operations**:
- DFRT recommended increased meeting frequency and field trip incorporation.
- Letter to Regional Director requested to resume DFRT responsibility for lower Colorado River basin "big-river fishes": [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]].
- Genetic work at ASU does not support Vrijenhoek's fitness differences based on heterozygosity/homozygosity in topminnow populations (paper in press, *Conservation Biology*).
