## Executive Summary

This document set is not a primary morphological or ecological field study, but a series of 1996 interagency regulatory letters concerning the **Biological Assessment (BA) on Lower Colorado River Operations and Maintenance** (Lake Mead to the Southerly International Boundary), prepared by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation under Endangered Species Act (ESA) Section 7. The central fish-science issue is whether Reclamation's water management operations on the Lower Colorado River — which govern flow and water quality delivered to Mexico under the 1944 U.S.–Mexico Water Treaty — have downstream effects on the endangered **[[Totoaba]]** (*Cynoscion macdonaldi*), a large sciaenid endemic to the Gulf of California. The National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS) determined that the totoaba is a species of concern warranting further analysis, while other candidate species (vaquito, four sea turtle species) were determined not likely to be adversely affected. The correspondence also documents the parallel **[[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]]** (LCRMSCP) as the preferred long-term mechanism for conserving native fish and wildlife along the river corridor.

## Background & Description

The source texts contain no direct morphological description of the totoaba or any other fish species. NMFS explicitly notes that the draft BA's "descriptive information" on totoaba biology was inadequate, and supplementary reference documents (not reproduced in this text) were provided to Reclamation to correct this deficiency. Known baseline facts referenced only indirectly:
- **[[Totoaba]]** (*Cynoscion macdonaldi*) — a member of family [[Sciaenidae]] (drums/croakers), endemic to the Gulf of California.
- Listed as **endangered** under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, under NMFS jurisdiction.
- No size, coloration, or anatomical data are provided in this correspondence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat dependency on freshwater inflow:** Totoaba populations in the **upper Gulf of California** are ecologically linked to the historic freshwater and nutrient inputs of the **[[Colorado River]]** delta/estuary. Modification of Colorado River flow — in both quantity and quality — reaching the Gulf has been identified as a contributing factor in totoaba population decline (citing Lagomarsino 1991; Cisneros-Mata et al. 1994).
- **Water delivery obligations:** Under the 1944 U.S.–Mexico Water Treaty, Reclamation is required to deliver 1.5–1.7 million acre-feet of water annually to Mexico; however, NMFS notes uncertainty as to whether this treaty-mandated volume is sufficient to maintain adequate estuarine habitat conditions for totoaba in the upper Gulf, especially since Mexico reportedly diverts nearly all delivered water for consumptive use before it reaches the estuary.
- **Compounding anthropogenic pressures:** The totoaba's decline is attributed not only to altered [[Colorado River Delta]] hydrology but also to concurrent fishing pressures (targeted and incidental/bycatch) in the upper Gulf.
- **Co-occurring protected fauna:** The same estuarine/marine system supports the critically endangered **[[Vaquito]]** (*Phocoena sinus*), a small cetacean, and nesting/foraging habitat for four sea turtle species (green, leatherback, loggerhead, olive ridley) — all evaluated in this consultation but determined unlikely to be adversely affected by Reclamation's river operations.
- **Regulatory/geographic scope:** The BA's ecological assessment area is bounded to the mainstem Colorado River from Pierce Ferry (upper end of Lake Mead) to the Southerly International Boundary with Mexico, plus the 100-year floodplain, as defined in an interagency Memorandum of Agreement.

## Key Findings

- **Totoaba flagged for further analysis:** NMFS determined the endangered totoaba may be affected by Reclamation's Lower Colorado River operations due to their influence on freshwater/water-quality inputs to the Gulf of California, and required the draft BA to be substantively revised before a formal effects determination could be made.
- **Vaquito and sea turtles excluded:** NMFS determined the vaquito and four listed sea turtle species are *not likely to be adversely affected* by Reclamation's operations, narrowing the fish/marine-species focus of the consultation to totoaba alone.
- **Causal uncertainty:** NMFS could not conclude definitively whether Reclamation's specific operations (versus independent Mexican water diversion and fishing pressures) are the proximate cause of totoaba habitat degradation, given multiple confounding stressors.
- **Four analytical gaps identified by NMFS** that Reclamation was required to address in the revised BA: (1) Reclamation's ability to control water use in Mexico, (2) discretion to deliver water beyond treaty minimums, (3) Mexico's own treaty obligations for environmental flows to the upper Gulf, and (4) mechanisms to influence Mexican water delivery to the estuary.
- **Possible non-applicability of Section 7 to totoaba:** NMFS suggested that if Reclamation lacks discretionary control over water once delivered to Mexico, a formal ESA Section 7 consultation regarding totoaba impacts might not be legally required.
- **Institutional/conservation planning context:** The Colorado River Board of California emphasized that the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (a Federal/State/Local partnership, including Reclamation and USFWS) is the preferred long-term (50+ year) vehicle for conserving native fish and wildlife of the lower Colorado River, and urged that the BA's 5-year interim scope and geographic boundaries (Lake Mead to SIB, plus 100-year floodplain) not be expanded, to avoid destabilizing the multi-species planning process.
- **No new morphological, population, or survey data** on totoaba, vaquito, or Colorado River native fishes are presented in this correspondence; the documents are purely procedural/regulatory in nature, referencing external biological reports (Lagomarsino 1991; Cisneros-Mata et al. 1994) for substantive species data.
