## Executive Summary
This trip report documents a coordinated field survey conducted May 1–2, 1995, at [[Quitobaquito Spring]] by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, National Park Service, and Arizona Game and Fish Department. The survey's primary objective was to investigate a reported sighting of an unidentified exotic fish observed on April 12, 1995, by Robin Silver. Despite an extensive multi-method sampling effort (gill nets, hoop nets, seines, baited poles, visual observations, and spotlighting), no exotic fish were captured or definitively observed. The only fish species confirmed present was the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), which was found in abundant numbers, displaying strong breeding activity. Ambiguous partial sightings of a possible unidentified fish by two observers (Tim Tibbitts and Ami Pate) keep concern for a potential exotic introduction unresolved, warranting continued monitoring.

## Background & Description
- **Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*)**: Males observed were "highly colored" and "vigorously defending territories," consistent with typical breeding-season sexual dimorphism and territorial behavior in this species.
- **Larval fish**: Individuals presumed to be larval desert pupfish were observed, indicating active spawning/recruitment at the time of survey.
- **Unidentified exotic fish (unconfirmed)**:
  - Partial observation #1 (Tibbitts): dark-colored tail of a "medium sized fish."
  - Partial observation #2 (Pate): head resembling that of a "catfish-type fish."
  - Neither observation yielded a complete view of the organism due to obstruction by the dense submerged vegetation mat; no specimens were captured, precluding formal identification.
- **Non-fish fauna noted**: Two to three [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense*) individuals were observed during the survey.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat structure**: [[Quitobaquito Spring]] pond is characterized by a dense mat of submerged vegetation covering most of the water surface, which significantly impeded netting, seining, and visual detection efforts.
- **Microhabitat use**: Pupfish and turtles were observed moving between the interior of the vegetation mat and adjacent open-water areas, suggesting the mat serves as refuge/cover habitat integrated with open foraging or territorial zones.
- **Spatial sampling zones**:
  - South and southeastern pond areas: gill nets deployed.
  - Western, eastern, and northern pond margins: large hoop nets (baited with sardines/pineapple).
  - "Moat" behind turtle island (vegetation-free): small hoop net (baited with sardines).
  - Southern pond: seine hauls (50-ft seine).
  - West and north end: shorter seine hauls in various locations.
- **Behavioral notes**: Breeding activity (territorial defense by colored males, presence of larvae) indicates the spring was functioning as active spawning habitat for [[Desert Pupfish]] at the time of the survey (early May).
- **Community composition**: Confirmed vertebrate fauna limited to desert pupfish and Sonoran mud turtle; no other fish species were captured or conclusively identified during this effort.

## Key Findings
1. **No exotic fish confirmed**: Despite comprehensive sampling (gill nets, hoop nets, seines, baited poles, visual survey, spotlighting), the suspected exotic fish reported by Robin Silver on April 12, 1995, was not captured or definitively observed during this May 1–2 survey.
2. **Sampling limitations**: The dense submerged vegetation mat was the primary obstacle to effective sampling — one gill net rested ineffectively atop the mat, and the seine could not penetrate the mat at all, likely reducing detection probability for any cryptic or mat-dwelling fish.
3. **Corroborating partial sightings**: Prior to the main survey, both Tim Tibbitts and Ami Pate independently reported partial glimpses of an unidentified fish (a dark tail and a catfish-like head, respectively), each obscured by vegetation. The author notes all three observers (Silver, Tibbitts, Pate) are considered reliable, lending some credibility to the possibility of an undetected exotic species despite the negative survey results.
4. **Healthy pupfish population**: [[Desert Pupfish]] were abundant, exhibiting vigorous breeding behavior (territorial males, larvae present), suggesting the core population at [[Quitobaquito Spring]] was reproductively active and apparently unaffected at the time of survey.
5. **Continued monitoring planned**: The National Park Service indicated intent to continue visual observations and hoop-net trapping efforts to resolve the exotic fish question, reflecting ongoing concern for potential threats to the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] population from a possible non-native predator or competitor.
6. **Co-occurring species**: Presence of [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] documented as incidental bycatch/observation, indicating the spring supports multiple sensitive aquatic/semi-aquatic species reliant on this isolated desert habitat.
