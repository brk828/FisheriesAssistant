# Movements of Sub-Adult Razorback Sucker in a Regulated Colorado River Reach

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen-texanus

## Executive Summary

This interim progress report (dated 01 May 1999) from [[Arizona State University]] and the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] tracks the post-release movements, habitat use, and possible [[Entrainment]] of 20 hatchery-reared, sub-adult [[Razorback Sucker]] released below [[Parker Dam]] on 20 August 1998. Ten fish carried internal and ten carried external [[Radio Telemetry]] transmitters. Most fish stayed near their release sites overnight, and at least 10 dispersed downstream. Two fish were confirmed and four suspected to have passed over or through [[Headgate Rock Diversion Dam]], while no fish were confirmed or suspected of entering the [[CRIT Main Canal]]. Six fish (30%) died. Equipment failures at key monitoring points limited the data, so the fate of many fish remains unknown.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is an endangered fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system of western North America. A large remnant population occupies [[Lake Mohave]], a small population persists in the Green River (Utah), and scattered individuals occur elsewhere, including the lower Colorado River. The species is not known to recruit successfully in nature, so maintaining wild stocks is expected to depend on aggressive [[Stocking Programs]].

A [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (1997) found that operation of lower Colorado River facilities by the [[U.S. Bureau Of Reclamation]] jeopardized the species. Among the Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives were reintroduction below Parker Dam and assessment of potential entrainment through or into water management structures. The report notes that, although many razorback sucker telemetry studies existed, none had specifically examined entrainment.

The study's purpose was to support razorback sucker management by documenting dispersal and entrainment of stocked sub-adults. Fish were sourced from wild Lake Mohave larvae captured in winter-spring 1997 and reared by the Native Fish Work Group at Boulder City Wetlands Ponds, Nevada. Tagging followed established surgical procedures, and passive integrated transponder (PIT) tags were placed in all fish.

Fish were handled as follows:

- **Transport:** Trammel-netted on 19 August 1998, then transported in hatchery tanks at about 16°C with a 5 mg/L [[MS 222]] sedative and 5% sodium chloride.
- **Tagging:** Done adjacent to the river at [[River Lodge]], about 4 km below Parker Dam. Surgical implantation took at most 4-5 minutes, and external mounting took 1-2 minutes.
- **Transmitters:** 13 x 45 mm cylinders with a 25 cm trailing antenna, about 8 g, operating in the 40-42 MHz range, with an estimated battery life of 90 days and reception range generally above 50 m.
- **Recovery:** Fish were held 15-90 minutes in river water before release.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area was the 23.2 km (14.4 mile) reach from Parker Dam (river mile 192.2) to Headgate Rock Diversion Dam (river mile 177.8), known as the [[Parker Strip]]. The reach is flowing water and is one of the most heavily developed recreational areas on the lower Colorado River mainstream. Parker Dam releases controlled discharge, which averaged about 12,050 cfs and ranged from 7,500 to 18,500 cfs during the study.

Conditions in the reach included:

- **Channel:** 50-175 m wide, with depths averaging a few meters and reaching about 8 m near the dams.
- **Substrate:** Cobble-boulder-bedrock below Parker Dam, with silty sediments in deeper mid-channel areas, quiet backwaters, and [[Moovalya Lake]].
- **Banks:** Heavily modified with riprap, concrete walls, and artificial embayments.
- **Vegetation:** Sparse riparian cover; dense stands of giant reed grass (Phragmites) and cattail (Typha) in quiet margins; and [[Sago Pondweed]] forming dense marginal beds along the lower 15 km and throughout Moovalya Lake.
- **Water clarity:** About 2 m upstream, falling to half a meter or less downstream due to recreational watercraft disturbing soft bottoms.

Surveys also covered CRIT and Palo Verde Irrigation District canals, which are mostly strongly flowing, concrete-lined channels with little habitat diversity, and the river reach downstream to [[Imperial Dam]] (river mile 49.3). Release water temperatures were 25°C at River Lodge and 24°C at [[Bass Point]], about 16.5 km below Parker Dam.

## Key Findings

**Release and tagging**
- 20 sub-adults were released on 20 August 1998: 10 at River Lodge (in a backwater, about 4 km below Parker Dam) and 10 at Bass Point (in quiet water, 16.5 km below Parker Dam). Each site received 5 internal and 5 external transmitters.
- Released fish ranged from 360 to 450 mm in length and 445 to 913 g in weight (Table 1). Each fish was assigned a unique frequency and PIT tag number.

**Dispersal behavior**
- Most fish stayed overnight near their release locations, consistent with earlier razorback sucker studies.
- At least 10 fish dispersed downstream. The summary reports one fish moving upstream, while the individual tracking histories describe two upstream dispersers (for example, EB-741 moved 400 m upstream and was then lost). The report does not reconcile this difference.

**Passage and entrainment**
- Two fish were confirmed and four suspected to have passed over or through Headgate Rock Diversion Dam.
- No fish were confirmed or suspected to have entered the CRIT Main Canal.
- Seven fish had no contact after release (five with no post-release contact, two with contact lost within five days). Their fate is unknown. Some may have passed the dam undetected during an early equipment malfunction, or been removed by predators or scavengers.
- If all seven had passed the dam, the total would have been 13 fish, or 65% of those released.

**Survival**
- Five fish were tracked for the full study period, four within the study area and one about 15 km downstream.
- Six fish died (30%): three confirmed and three suspected. The authors attribute the mortality to handling and surgical trauma, worsened by thermal stress from the temperature difference between transport water (16°C) and river water (24-25°C).

**Methodological limitations**
- Fixed data loggers at Headgate Rock Diversion Dam and the CRIT Main Canal intake were not operating reliably before release. The authors state that several or more fish may have passed these structures undetected.
- Supplementary land, air, and boat searches found few signals, so the fate of many fish is unknown.

**Study status**
- Monitoring ran from 20 August to 11 November 1998, with roughly weekly boat surveys.
- The report is interim, so these findings are preliminary and no final conclusions on entrainment risk are drawn.
