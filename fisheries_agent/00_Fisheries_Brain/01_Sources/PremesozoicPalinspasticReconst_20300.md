# Science, 29 September 1989: Congressional Fellowships, Policy Briefings and Great Basin Reconstruction

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cetacea

## Executive Summary

This issue of *Science* (Vol. 245, pp. 1425–1536) contains three kinds of material: a recruitment notice for the [[Congressional Science and Engineering Fellowship]] program, a set of news briefings, and a full research article by Marjorie Levy and Nicholas Christie-Blick on the pre-Mesozoic palinspastic reconstruction of the eastern [[Great Basin]]. The document contains no fish-focused research. Its only aquatic content is a brief news item on Japan's [[Driftnet Fishing]] reduction, which mentions dolphin bycatch. The geology article is the scientific core of the document.

## Background & Description

**Fellowship notice (p. 1426 equivalent).** The [[Congressional Science and Engineering Fellowship]] places postdoctoral to mid-career scientists and engineers for one year as special assistants to members of Congress or congressional committees. Twenty-one professional societies sponsor or co-sponsor the program, and applicants apply directly to the relevant society. The contact address given is the AAAS office in Washington, DC. Minorities and persons with disabilities are encouraged to apply.

**News briefings (pp. 1453 ff.).** Four short items are included:
- **Ivory and fishing nets:** Japan announced a near-total suspension of [[Ivory Trade]] imports until year's end, shortly before the documentary "Ivory Wars" aired. The same announcement cut the number of 40-km fishing nets ("walls of death") from 60 boats to 20. These fine-meshed nets kill dolphins and non-commercial species.
- **Soviet psychiatry:** The [[American Psychiatric Association]] opposed readmitting the [[All-Union Society of Neuropathologists and Psychiatrists]] to the [[World Psychiatric Association]] at the Athens meeting, 11–19 October.
- **Soviet environmental policy:** Fyodor Morgun, 65, resigned as head of [[Goskompriroda]] after 15 months. He had described the country as in "ecological crisis" under [[Glasnost]].
- **Boston University and Chelsea:** [[Boston University]] assumed control of the Chelsea, Massachusetts school system for 10 years under president John Silber.

**Geology article (pp. 1454–1461).** Levy and Christie-Blick (Columbia University) present a working-hypothesis reconstruction of the eastern Great Basin. It restores Mesozoic–early Cenozoic shortening and Cenozoic extension to place middle Proterozoic to Early Cambrian (about 1600 to 535 Ma) rocks in their original regional setting.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fisheries and wildlife material is limited to the [[Driftnet Fishing]] item and the Japanese ivory decision. The Soviet environmental item concerns industrial pollution and "ecological crisis" rather than aquatic systems.

The geology article describes a tectonic setting with environmental significance for regional landscape history:
- **Basin evolution:** Three phases are identified: late Proterozoic to mid-Paleozoic intracontinental extension and passive margin development (~800 to ~375 Ma); late Devonian to early Eocene crustal shortening (~375 to 50 Ma); and mid- to late Cenozoic extension and magmatism (~37 Ma to present).
- **Sedimentary sequence:** Miogeoclinal rocks thicken westward from <150 m to >6000 m. The upper wedge includes peritidal carbonates and mudstones up to 5000 m thick.
- **Western limit of basement:** The [[Strontium Isopleth 0.706]] line approximates the western edge of Precambrian crystalline basement.
- **Extension processes:** Extension involved high- and low-angle normal faults, [[Metamorphic Core Complex]] formation, and strike-slip faults that accommodate part of the [[Pacific-North America Plate Boundary]] motion.

## Key Findings

**Congressional fellowship program**
- One-year placements in congressional offices, with an orientation and year-long seminar series.
- Twenty-one sponsoring societies; stipends, timetables and deadlines vary by society.

**News briefings**
- Japan suspended nearly all ivory imports until year's end, pending the outcome of an international endangered species conference in Switzerland.
- Japan reduced the number of 40-km driftnet boats from 60 to 20.
- The APA planned to oppose Soviet readmission to the WPA; Britain, Switzerland, Australia, New Zealand and West Germany were named as likely opponents of full Soviet membership.
- Morgun criticized a ten-year Soviet protein-from-petroleum program that he described as futile, although it had Academy of Sciences backing. He said the committee's chief should be elevated to the Council of Ministers or Supreme Soviet.
- Boston University plans to raise funds to increase Chelsea school spending from $16 million to $22 million a year. Chelsea, population 26,000, has the highest dropout and teen pregnancy rates in the state, and family income 43% below the state norm.

**Palinspastic reconstruction (Levy and Christie-Blick)**
- Total east-directed shortening was at least 104 to 135 km along the fold and thrust belt (Table 1 by transect). The Idaho–Wyoming segment accounts for 121 km, northern Utah 104 km, and central Utah 120–121 km.
- Overall Great Basin extension is about 247 ± 56 km in the direction 287° ± 12° (the uncertainty is at the 95% confidence level). This is based on work by [[Wernicke et al. (1988)]] in the southern Great Basin.
- The Death Valley region accounts for about 150 km of extension and the Las Vegas Valley region for 90–100 km.
- In the northern Great Basin, the extension uncertainty is ±98 km at 95% confidence, with 247 km as the central estimate. This uncertainty comes mainly from [[Sierra Nevada Rotation]] scenarios.
- Paleomagnetic data from [[Frei (1986)]] suggest a clockwise rotation of the Sierra Nevada of 6° ± 8°. [[Hamilton (1988)]] argued for a counterclockwise rotation of 20–30°, and this view is discounted in the paper.
- The original width of the preserved late Proterozoic–Early Cambrian basin was about 150–300 km, roughly 60% of its present width, and oriented slightly more north–south.
- The authors present the reconstruction as a working hypothesis and identify the southern Great Basin and eastern Nevada as the areas of greatest error. They call for regional cross-sections and improved paleomagnetic constraints on the late Mesozoic polar wander path.
