# Northern Limits of Distribution in Manitoba for Cyprinid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1956 paper by J. J. Keleher, published in the [[Canadian Journal of Zoology]], maps the most northerly recorded collection of each of 23 minnow species (family [[Cyprinidae]]) in [[Manitoba]], Canada. Thirteen species reach the northern limit of their entire published distribution in the province. The author links several northern limits to the 65° July [[Isotherm]], a temperature boundary used to interpret minnow distribution. The records are presented as a baseline for future work, and the author notes the limits are provisional because many areas of the province remain unsampled.

## Background & Description

Published information on the northern boundaries of Canadian freshwater fishes was limited at the time. The study aimed to supplement existing knowledge and provide a standard for future comparison. Its records came from roughly one hundred collections, mainly made by [[Seining]] in areas accessible by road. The author also used the fish records of the [[Royal Ontario Museum]], which were examined through transcription. Although those specimens were not seen directly, they extended the known limits of nine species. Nomenclature followed Dymond (1947), a checklist of Canadian freshwater fishes east of the [[Rocky Mountains]].

Three species had not previously been recorded in Manitoba: [[Nocomis Biguttatus]], [[Rhinichthys Atratulus]], and [[Notropis Rubellus]]. Their presence had been suspected by Hinks (1943) from occurrences in adjacent regions. Three other species had been published earlier: [[Hybopsis Storerianus]], [[Notemigonus Crysoleucas]], and [[Notropis Blennius]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Minnow distribution was examined against the 65° F July [[Isotherm]], following Radforth (1944), who proposed that nine Ontario species showed a distributional trend tied to this line. These were [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]], [[Margariscus Margarita]], [[Rhinichthys Atratulus]], [[Chrosomus Eos]], [[Notemigonus Crysoleucas]], [[Notropis Rubellus]], [[Notropis Cornutus]], [[Notropis Deliciosus]], and [[Hyborhynchus Notatus]]. The 65° isotherm, taken from Thomas (1953), was plotted on the distribution map.

The northern limits of the sampled species lie on a broadly south-to-north gradient across the province. Collection sites range from about 49°50′ N in the south, for example Rennie Creek, to about 58°33′ N in the north at a tributary of Herriot Creek. The paper treats these sites as observed limits, not final boundaries, because sampling was uneven across the province.

## Key Findings

- **Species count and northern limits:** Of the 23 minnow species recorded in Manitoba, 13 have their northernmost known distribution in the province. These are *Cyprinus carpio*, *Semotilus atromaculatus*, *Margariscus margarita*, *Nocomis biguttatus*, *Hybopsis storerianus*, *Rhinichthys atratulus*, *Notemigonus crysoleucas*, *Notropis rubellus*, *Notropis cornutus*, *Notropis blennius*, *Notropis deliciosus*, *Hybognathus hankinsoni*, and *Hyborhynchus notatus*.

- **Most northerly records (Table I):** The table lists one northernmost collection per species, with location, coordinates, collector, and year. Selected examples:
  - *Rhinichthys cataractae*, tributary of Herriot Creek, 58°33′ N, 94°22′ W (B. Kooyman, 1951), the highest latitude in the table.
  - *Margariscus margarita*, Silcox Creek, 57°09′ N, 94°10′ W (B. Kooyman, 1951).
  - *Couesius plumbeus*, South Knife Lake, 58°10′ N, 96°28′ W (K. C. Dey, 1950).
  - *Cyprinus carpio*, Spider Island, 53°20′ N, 97°45′ W (B. Kooyman, 1954).
  - *Hyborhynchus notatus*, Echo Lake, 50°13′ N, 95°22′ W (T. A. Paddock, 1949).

- **Notropis cornutus exception:** A record by Kendall (1924) lies farther north than the Woody River site. The author listed the Woody River record as the main entry because Kendall's locality is geographically indefinite.

- **First provincial records:** *Nocomis biguttatus*, *Rhinichthys atratulus*, and *Notropis rubellus* are reported from Manitoba for the first time.

- **Isotherm relationship:** Of the nine species linked to the 65° July isotherm, all but one have their northern limit in Manitoba between the 64° and 66° isotherms. *Notemigonus crysoleucas* lies only just on the 64° line. *Margariscus margarita* is the exception, occurring a little north of the 60° isotherm.

- **Method limits:** The author states that the limits are not final because many regions of Manitoba remain unsampled. Fish records from the Royal Ontario Museum were used without direct specimen examination, and the Royal Ontario Museum records were verified in part by W. B. Scott.

- **Purpose:** The paper supplements existing knowledge of Canadian freshwater fish limits and sets a reference standard for later surveys.
