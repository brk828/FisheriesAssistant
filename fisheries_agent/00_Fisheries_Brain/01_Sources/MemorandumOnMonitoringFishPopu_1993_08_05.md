# Monitoring Fish Populations at 12 Mile Lake on the CRIT

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #FreshwaterEcology #Colorado-River-Basin

## Executive Summary
This 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum documents a fisheries monitoring effort at 12 Mile Lake, a backwater on the Colorado River Indian Tribes (CRIT) reservation near Parker, Arizona. The primary objective was to assess the survival and growth status of [[Razorback Sucker]]s that had been stocked into the lake in February 1993, alongside a secondary goal of characterizing the broader sportfish and baitfish community using [[Trammel Net]] gear. Two 100-meter trammel nets were deployed overnight (3–4 August 1993) at the north and south ends of the lake, yielding a combined 92 fish across eight species. Only one tagged razorback sucker was recovered, showing modest growth since stocking, while the catch was dominated by introduced species, particularly [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Largemouth Bass]].

### Razorback Sucker Growth Note
The single recaptured [[Razorback Sucker]] (PIT tag #7F7D7C6232) had grown from 412 mm to 442 mm total length — a **3.0 cm increase** — since its stocking in February 1993, and weighed 900 g at recapture. This individual was captured exclusively in the south net.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
12 Mile Lake functions as a low-velocity backwater habitat within the lower Colorado River system, historically significant for native [[Catostomidae]] species such as the razorback sucker. The lake currently supports a mixed assemblage dominated by non-native, introduced sportfish and forage species:
- **Piscivores/Sportfish:** [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Channel Catfish]]
- **Forage/Baitfish:** [[Threadfin Shad]] (dominant by abundance), [[Bluegill]]
- **Benthic/Omnivorous:** [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Common Carp]]
- **Native Endangered Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (reintroduced via stocking, low recapture rate observed)

The co-occurrence of a stocked, imperiled native catostomid with an established community of introduced centrarchids, ictalurids, and clupeids highlights the competitive and/or predatory pressures facing razorback sucker recovery efforts in Colorado River backwaters. The low capture rate of razorback sucker (1 of 92 fish, ~1.1%) relative to introduced species underscores the ecological challenges of reintroduction in habitats with established non-native fish communities.

## Key Findings
- **Total catch:** 92 fish across 8 species from two 100 m trammel nets set overnight (3–4 August 1993).
- **Species abundance (total, descending):** Threadfin Shad (29) > Bluegill (17) > Largemouth Bass (14) > Black Crappie (12) > Yellow Bullhead (11) > Carp (4) = Channel Catfish (4) > Razorback Sucker (1).
- **Razorback Sucker status:** Only one individual recaptured since February 1993 stocking; showed measurable growth (+3.0 cm length) and healthy weight (900 g), suggesting survival and growth potential in the lake, though detection rate was very low.
- **Spatial distribution:** South net catch was more diverse in structure (channel catfish, razorback sucker present); north net catch was more numerous overall (50 fish vs. 42) and dominated by centrarchids (bluegill, largemouth bass, black crappie).
- **Field collaboration:** Data collection supported by Brent Bristow and the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] YCC crew.
- **Management implication:** Continued monitoring of [[Razorback Sucker]] populations at 12 Mile Lake is warranted to track long-term survival, growth, and interaction with non-native fish species in this CRIT-managed backwater habitat.
