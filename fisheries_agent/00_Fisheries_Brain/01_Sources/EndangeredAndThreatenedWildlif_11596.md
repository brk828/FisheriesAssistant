# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants; Animal Candidate Review for Listing

#endangered-species #conservation #regulatory #federal-register #USFWS

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice (Vol. 56, No. 225, November 21, 1991) presents the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's updated compilation of vertebrate and invertebrate animal taxa native to the United States being reviewed for possible addition to the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The document organizes candidate taxa alphabetically by scientific name within major taxonomic groups (class or order), assigns each taxon to one of four categories, identifies the lead FWS Region with responsibility for each taxon, and reports known trends in status. The notice emphasizes that candidate taxa receive no substantive or procedural protection under the Act until formally proposed or listed, though consideration during environmental planning is prudent. The Service requests additional status information to assist in preparing listing documents and monitoring changes in candidate status, with comments requested until publication of an anticipated 1993 update.

## Background & Description

The notice implements regulations at 50 CFR Part 17 and 50 CFR 424.15, which authorize the Service to publish comprehensive notices of review for species considered candidates for listing under the Act. The compilation relies on information from status surveys funded under candidate assessment programs, State Heritage Programs, other Federal agencies ([[Forest Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]]), knowledgeable scientists, and public comments on previous notices of review.

Key organizational changes from previous animal notices include: (1) addition of new candidate taxa; (2) category changes for some candidates; (3) addition of a category for species currently proposed for listing; (4) new alphabetical organization by scientific name within each major group; (5) omission of taxa identified as non-candidates in previous notices; (6) identification of lead FWS Region for each taxon; (7) additions and deletions in State historic distributions; and (8) reporting of known trends in status for each candidate.

The document provides contact information for all seven FWS Regional Offices (Regions 1-7), covering all U.S. states, territories, and possessions. Each region has designated Endangered Species Coordinators responsible for taxa within their jurisdiction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The candidate taxa span an extraordinary taxonomic and ecological breadth across the United States:

**Vertebrates** include:
- **Birds** (Class Aves): Species such as [[Mountain Plover]] (*Charadrius montanus*), [[Black Tern]] (*Chlidonias niger*), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), [[Loggerhead Shrike]] (*Lanius ludovicianus*), and numerous Hawaiian endemics
- **Reptiles**: Including [[Bog Turtle]] (*Clemmys muhlenbergii*), [[Gopher Tortoise]] (eastern population), various snakes, lizards, and turtles across diverse habitats
- **Amphibians**: Salamanders (Plethodontidae, Ambystomatidae), frogs (Ranidae), including [[California Tiger Salamander]] (*Ambystoma californiense*) and [[Northern Red-legged Frog]] (*Rana aurora*)
- **Fishes**: Extensive coverage including sturgeons (Acipenseridae), darters (Percidae), minnows (Cyprinidae), trout (Salmonidae), and numerous desert spring endemics

**Invertebrates** dominate the candidate list:
- **Mollusks**: Freshwater mussels (Unionidae) with dozens of species from southeastern rivers; aquatic snails (Hydrobiidae, Pleuroceridae) including many cave and spring endemics
- **Insects**: Mayflies (Ephemeroptera), dragonflies and damselflies (Odonata), stoneflies (Plecoptera), beetles (Coleoptera), butterflies and moths (Lepidoptera), caddisflies (Trichoptera), and bees/wasps (Hymenoptera)
- **Arachnids**: Cave spiders, pseudoscorpions, harvestmen
- **Crustaceans**: Fairy shrimps (Anostraca), isopods, amphipods, crayfishes (Decapoda)
- **Other invertebrates**: Earthworms, flatworms, hydroids, sponges

Geographic patterns reveal concentration of candidates in:
- **Southeastern rivers**: Particularly [[Alabama]], [[Tennessee]], and [[Georgia]] for freshwater mussels and snails
- **Western springs and desert aquatic habitats**: [[California]], [[Nevada]], [[Arizona]], [[New Mexico]] for springsnails and endemic fishes
- **Cave systems**: Throughout [[Appalachia]], [[Ozarks]], [[Texas]], and western states for troglobitic invertebrates
- **Hawaiian Islands**: Numerous endemic insects, snails, and birds
- **Coastal dune systems**: Specialized beetles and plants in [[California]] and other coastal states

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Distribution**: The candidate list includes representatives from all major vertebrate classes and numerous invertebrate orders, with particularly high diversity in:
- Freshwater mussels (Unionidae): Over 80 candidate taxa, primarily from southeastern U.S. rivers
- Aquatic snails (Hydrobiidae, Pleuroceridae): Dozens of species from springs, caves, and rivers
- Beetles (Coleoptera): Extensive representation including cave beetles, riffle beetles, dune beetles
- Caddisflies (Trichoptera): Numerous species from western mountain streams

**Status Categories**: Taxa are assigned to four categories (specific definitions not provided in excerpt, but categories 1, 2, PE, and others are referenced with trend indicators: D=declining, U=unknown, S=stable).

**Geographic Responsibility**: Lead responsibility distributed across seven FWS Regions:
- Region 1: Pacific states, [[Hawaii]], Pacific territories
- Region 2: Southwest ([[Arizona]], [[New Mexico]], [[Oklahoma]], [[Texas]])
- Region 3: Upper Midwest
- Region 4: Southeast (highest concentration of freshwater mussel candidates)
- Region 5: Northeast
- Region 6: Rocky Mountain states
- Region 7: [[Alaska]]

**Habitat Threats** (implicit from taxa included):
- Freshwater aquatic systems: Degradation affecting mussels, snails, fishes, aquatic insects
- Cave ecosystems: Disturbance threatening troglobitic invertebrates
- Desert springs: Water extraction and habitat modification affecting endemic springsnails and fishes
- Coastal dunes: Development and recreation impacting specialized beetles and other invertebrates
- Island ecosystems: Particularly [[Hawaii]] with numerous endemic species at risk

**Procedural Framework**: The notice establishes that:
- Candidate status confers no legal protection under the Act
- Public comment period remains open until 1993 update
- Information submitted will inform future listing decisions and status monitoring
- Environmental planning should prudently consider candidate taxa

**Data Gaps**: Trend information frequently listed as "U" (unknown), indicating substantial uncertainty about population status for many candidate taxa, particularly invertebrates.

The document represents a comprehensive baseline assessment of U.S. animal biodiversity at risk as of 1991, providing critical foundation for subsequent conservation prioritization and [[Endangered Species Act]] implementation.
