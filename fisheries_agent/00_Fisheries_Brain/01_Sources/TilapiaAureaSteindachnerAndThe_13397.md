# Tilapia aurea (Steindachner) and the Status of Tilapia nilotica exul, T. monodi and T. lemassoni

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

E. Trewavas (British Museum, Natural History) revises the taxonomy of a [[Blue Tilapia]] that was long confused with [[Nile Tilapia]] (*Tilapia nilotica*). The earliest name, *Chromis aureus* Steindachner, 1864, is reinstated as *Tilapia aurea*, with Senegal designated as the type locality. The author synonymizes *T. monodi* and *T. lemassoni* with *T. aurea* and treats *T. nilotica exul* as a provisional subspecies of *T. aurea*. The species is distinguished from *T. nilotica* mainly by caudal-fin pattern, dorsal-spine and vertebral counts, and breeding colouration.

## Background & Description

The study was prompted by Fishelson's observation that two distinct species matched Trewavas's earlier description of *T. nilotica* (1942). Fishelson had hybridized the two species; all hybrids were males (Fishelson, 1962). The paper examines where the "blue" or "Jordan" species belongs in the system, how it is distributed, and which name it should carry.

**Nomenclature**
- *Chromis aureus* Steindachner, 1864 was described from young West African specimens.
- The lectotype is Vienna Museum No. 32874, total length 133.5 mm, labelled "Westafrika."
- Daget (1954) recognized it in the Middle Niger as *T. monodi*. Blache and Miton (1960) described *T. lemassoni* from the Chad basin.
- *T. kacherbi* (Wunder, 1960) from the Nile Delta remains a [[Nomen Nudum]].
- Trewavas notes that rejecting the older name as a *nomen oblitum* under the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] is unclear.

**Diagnostic Characters (T. nilotica vs. T. aurea)**

| Character | T. nilotica | T. aurea |
|---|---|---|
| Caudal fin | Alternate narrow black and clear vertical stripes | Unmarked, vague markings, or dark reticulum |
| Dorsal spines | XVII or XVIII (mode XVII) | XV or XVI (mode XVI) |
| Vertebrae | 30–32 (mode 31) | 28–31 (mode 30) |
| Breeding male | Red suffusion on head and fins | Metallic blue head, vermilion dorsal edge |
| Dorsal lappets | Always separated by notches | Notches obliterated in ripe males |

The main description is based on preserved specimens in the British Museum and on preserved and living fish at Tel Aviv University.

**Morphology (selected values)**
- Body depth: 35–49% of standard length (SL), usually over 40%.
- Head length: 33.0–37.2% of SL.
- Lateral line scales: 30–33 (mode 32).
- Dorsal fin: DXV–XVI, 12–15 rays. Anal fin: A III, 9–11.
- Largest specimen examined by Trewavas: 255 mm SL. Daget reports up to 370 mm in the Middle Niger.
- Teeth: bicuspid outer teeth, tricuspid inner teeth, 52–76 in the outer series of the upper jaw.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution**
- West Africa: Senegal River, Middle Niger (south to Bussa), Lake Chad, and the Logone and Shari pools.
- Nile: Lower Nile from near Cairo to the Delta lakes, in fresh water only.
- Israel and Jordan: Na'aman River, Yarkon River (reported by Steinitz), Jordan Valley, Lake Tiberias, Lake Hula, and Asraq.
- Elsewhere, *T. nilotica* is more abundant. *T. aurea* is absent from the area south of the Nile that Trewavas examined.

**Habitat and Environmental Influence**
- The species is found in muddy-bottomed flood-plain ponds, lagoons, and backwaters. Dark colouration in some environments may reflect the melanophore response.
- The Ein Fashkha population lives in a hot, alkaline spring. Trewavas attributes its differences (a larger head, lower meristic counts) partly to this environment.
- Meristic counts appear to respond to temperature. Two Hawash River samples of *T. nilotica* show lower fin-ray counts where they come from hot springs.

**Relationships**
- *T. leucosticta* is similar in iridescent blue colour and caudal reticulation but has 28–30 lateral line scales and a wider interorbital region. Lowe (McConnell, 1957) suggested it may be a lacustrine derivative of *T. aurea*.
- *T. mossambica* and related eastern species share iridescent male colouration but have finer pharyngeal teeth and an enlarged mouth in breeding males.

## Key Findings

1. **Valid name:** The species is *Tilapia aurea* (Steindachner, 1864). Senegal is designated as the type locality. Its range includes West Africa, the Nile, and the Jordan–Asraq area.
2. **Synonymy:** *T. monodi* and *T. lemassoni* are placed in the synonymy of *T. aurea*. Blache's illustrations and the caudal-shape key do not support separating them.
3. **Subspecies:** *Tilapia nilotica exul* Steinitz, 1951 is kept provisionally as *T. aurea exul*. The Ein Fashkha sample (a new set of 38 young specimens, largest 74 mm SL) shows lower vertebral and dorsal-ray averages than other *T. aurea*. Table II gives the Ein Fashkha vertebral mode as 29 (N = 38). Table III gives a dorsal-ray mode of 27 (N = 43). Trewavas notes that red breast colouration, reported by Steinitz, has not been confirmed in mature fish.
4. **Jordan Valley:** No convincing evidence supports a natural population of *T. nilotica* in the Jordan Valley. Some Lake Tiberias records, including Lortet's figure and a fish described by Bertram, have striped caudals and low counts. Trewavas considers that these may be a natural *T. nilotica* population influenced by temperature, or hybrids, but this cannot be fully dismissed.
5. **Asraq:** Three specimens of 108–112 mm SL have dorsal formulae XV 14 or XVI 13 and lack the striped caudal of *T. nilotica*. They probably belong to *T. aurea*. Later information indicates the Asraq *T. aurea* and *T. zillii* were introduced from Lakes Tiberias and Hula about 30 years earlier.
6. **Egypt:** Egyptian *T. nilotica* has a higher gill-raker count than Palestinian *T. nilotica*. Fishelson reports 21–25 gill rakers in Palestinian *T. nilotica*. In Egypt the main differences between the two species are in breeding colours and caudal markings, not body depth.
7. **Hybridization:** Hybrids of *T. nilotica* and *T. aurea* bred in Tel Aviv all have striped caudals. Hybrids produced in the absence of conspecific females are all male.
8. **Remaining questions:** Trewavas stresses that the habits and breeding colours of the West African forms need more study before final synonymy is settled.
