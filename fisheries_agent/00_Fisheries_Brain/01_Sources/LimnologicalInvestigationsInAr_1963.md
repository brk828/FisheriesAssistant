## Executive Summary
This 1963 annual progress report (NSF Grant GB-154) documents multidisciplinary freshwater limnological research across Arizona, led by Gerald A. Cole with contributions from Melbourne C. Whiteside and William T. Barry. The work spans **paleolimnology/palynology** of [[Potato Lake]] sediments, an anomalous **radiocarbon dating** phenomenon at [[Montezuma Well]] involving aquatic plants and ancient dissolved CO₂, population studies of **calanoid copepods** ([[Diaptomus]]) in temporary versus permanent desert waters, and biotic/chemical surveys of the [[Wilcox Playa]] (a relict of Pluvial Lake Cochise). Notably, no fish taxa are discussed in this source; the ecological focus is on plankton, branchiopod crustaceans, aquatic macrophytes, sediment chemistry, and pollen stratigraphy as proxies for paleoclimate and lake trophic evolution.

## Background & Description
While this report is not fish-centric, it details morphological/taxonomic identification relevant to the aquatic community:
- **Pollen morphotypes**: Spruce (*Picea*) pollen forms the dominant paleo-proxy signal in the [[Potato Lake]] core, with a pronounced peak at ~150 cm depth interpreted as representing full pluvial conditions.
- **Artemisia (sagebrush) pollen**: Unusually high incidence in deep, glacial-age sediments — notable because *Artemisia* is typically associated with high-altitude, cold-climate conditions, raising biogeographic questions for southwestern paleoecology.
- **Long-spine Compositae pollen**: A secondary peak possibly correlative with a dated maximum (~7,300 yr B.P.) from Laguna Salada sediments.
- **Aquatic macrophyte anatomy**: Megafossil remains of *Potamogeton illinoensis* were recovered from sediment cores at Montezuma Well, alongside living specimens of *Chara* sp. in shallow, periodically air-exposed zones.
- **Crustacean fauna**: Anostracans (fairy shrimp), notostracans (tadpole shrimp), and cladocerans (water fleas) proliferated in the newly flooded Wilcox Playa; specific morphological descriptions were not detailed but identification to these three branchiopod orders was completed.
- **Diaptomid copepods**: Comparative population morphology/ecology between temporary-pool and permanent-pond *Diaptomus* populations was the basis of a submitted *Ecology* manuscript (data collected fall 1962).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Potato Lake]]** — A high-altitude montane lake; the first paleolimnological/palynological site studied in Arizona. Sediment chemistry (loss-on-ignition at 500°C, correlated with Mn, Ca, and Fe via mass spectrometry) is interpreted as tracking historical dissolved oxygen levels, tied to lake eutrophication following the spruce pollen decline.
- **[[Montezuma Well]]** — A spring-fed karst sinkhole exhibiting a severe radiocarbon dating anomaly attributed to ancient, ¹⁴C-depleted dissolved CO₂ incorporated into aquatic plant tissue (*Potamogeton*, *Chara*) and even water-column CO₂ itself. A native turtle (*Kinosternon* sp.) was also sampled to test for similar age artifacts. A terrestrial grass transect (shoreline to 70 ft above water) was sampled to test for a clinal gradient in apparent age driven by proximity to CO₂-supersaturated water.
- **Sonoran Desert stock pond** — A shallow, ephemeral-to-permanent pond (depth fluctuating from ~2 m to a few cm) studied intensively by [[William T. Barry]] for physico-chemical dynamics and plankton/phyllopod population turnover; described as supporting simpler plankton assemblages than northern temperate lakes.
- **Temporary vs. permanent waters (Diaptomus habitat contrast)** — Central ecological question of the copepod study: whether interspecific competitive exclusion (favored in stable permanent ponds) versus resource abundance (higher particulate phosphate/seston in turbid temporary pools) drives differing co-occurrence patterns of congeneric *Diaptomus* species.
- **[[Wilcox Playa]] (Pluvial Lake Cochise remnant)** — An ephemeral playa basin that flooded for the first time in years during the study period, triggering an explosive bloom of anostracans, notostracans, and cladocerans; water chemistry sampling was complicated by extreme turbidity.
- **Volcanic lakes near Flagstaff** — Relatively turbid permanent lakes used as comparative sites for water chemistry and particulate phosphate measurements (~25% particulate P, versus mean 30% across permanent AZ waters generally).
- **Montezuma Well outflow ditch** — An effluent stream showing pronounced ionic changes within its first mile, under joint chemical investigation with engineering water-chemist [[John Klock]].

## Key Findings
- The [[Potato Lake]] pollen core contains the best-documented spruce (*Picea*) record yet recovered from Arizona sediments, with the spruce decline marking a shift toward increased organic sediment content and inferred onset of lake eutrophication.
- High deep-sediment abundance of glacial-relict *Artemisia* pollen suggests southwestern paleoclimatic responses may not mirror those documented in northern-latitude pollen studies, complicating direct extrapolation of glacial climate models.
- Radiocarbon dating at [[Montezuma Well]] revealed extreme anomalies: buried *Potamogeton illinoensis* megafossils dated at 21,300 ± 750 yr B.P. despite pollen-based age estimates of only 5,000–6,000 years; modern living *P. illinoensis* dated at 24,750 ± 400 yr B.P.; modern *Chara* sp. at 17,300 ± 400 yr B.P.; and dissolved CO₂ from well water at 21,420 ± 220 yr B.P. — attributed to uptake of geologically ancient dissolved CO₂ by aquatic vegetation.
- Statistical analysis (L.C. Cole's Coefficient of Association) of *Diaptomus* co-occurrence showed **negative association** between congeneric species in permanent ponds but **random co-occurrence** in ephemeral (temporary) pools — interpreted as evidence for either (1) competitive exclusion selected for in stable habitats, or (2) reduced interspecific competition in temporary pools due to greater food (particulate seston) availability.
- Particulate phosphate (retained on 0.8 µm filter) averaged **61% of total phosphate in temporary ponds**, versus only **25–30% in permanent lakes/ponds** (including turbid volcanic lakes near Flagstaff), with some temporary pools exceeding 90% particulate P — supporting a food-supply-based explanation for reduced competitive exclusion in ephemeral waters.
- The 1963 flooding of the [[Wilcox Playa]] (remnant of Pluvial Lake Cochise) produced a dense bloom of anostracan, notostracan, and cladoceran crustaceans, offering a rare opportunity to characterize the biota of this normally dry pluvial lake basin.
- Ongoing collaborative work (Cole, Whiteside, Barry, Klock, and University of Arizona researchers Martin, Hevly, and Damon) was slated to produce multiple manuscripts, including submissions to *Ecology* and *Science*, and presentations at the 1964 Arizona Academy of Science meeting.
- Fieldwork limitations were explicitly noted: a December attempt to re-core [[Potato Lake]] through ice failed due to combined mechanical, meteorological, and human factors, with plans to retry using a borrowed 2-inch Livingston piston corer for deeper sediment penetration than the original 9-foot, 1-inch diameter core.
