# Existing Fish Recovery Teams in Arizona (January 1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This one-page roster, dated January 20, 1989, lists five existing fish [[Recovery Team]]s operating in Arizona. It names each team's U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) coordinator, team leader, members, and consultants. The document contains no population data, habitat measurements, or research results. Its value is as a record of who was working on the conservation of [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Woundfin]], [[Colorado River Fishes]], and [[Desert Fishes]] at that time.

## Background & Description

The roster covers five teams. Team membership spans several agencies, universities, and states:

- **Federal agencies:** FWS, [[Forest Service]] (FS), [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), and [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), plus National Park Service (NPS) for one team.
- **State agencies:** [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) appears on four teams. Other state agencies include the Utah Division of Wildlife, Nevada Department of Game and Fish (Nevada GF), New Mexico Game and Fish (NMGF), California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG), and Colorado Division of Wildlife (CDW).
- **Academic and private institutions:** [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV), New Mexico State University (NMSU), Biowest, and Mescalero and Dexter National Fish Hatcheries (NFH).
- **Foreign:** One member is from Sonora, Mexico.

Several people appear on more than one team. [[Dean Hendrickson]] (AGFD) is listed on the Woundfin, Colorado River Fishes, Desert Fishes, and Gila Trout teams. [[Jerry Burton]] (FWS) serves as coordinator for the Gila Trout and Desert Fishes teams and is a member of the Colorado River Fishes team. [[W.L. Minckley]] leads the Desert Fishes team and is a consultant on the Woundfin team.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not describe the species' ecology, habitats, threats, or environmental conditions. Team names suggest the work is directed at native fishes of Arizona's trout streams and desert and river systems, but the text does not state this. Team membership shows that recovery planning in the region involved land managers (FS, BLM, BR, NPS), state wildlife agencies, hatcheries, and academic researchers, which points to a multi-jurisdictional approach across basins that cross state and national boundaries.

## Key Findings

1. **Five recovery teams were active in Arizona as of January 1989:** Apache Trout, Woundfin, Colorado River Fishes, Desert Fishes, and Gila Trout.
2. **FWS provided coordination for all five teams.** Coordinators include Jim Hanson, Don Archer, John Hamill, Sally Stefferud, Frank Baucom, Ben Robertson, and Jerry Burton.
3. **Team leadership came from state agencies and academia.** Leaders were Jim Novy (AGFD, Apache Trout), Denise Knight (Utah Division of Wildlife, Woundfin), Jim St. Amant (CDFG, Colorado River Fishes), W.L. Minckley (Desert Fishes), and David Propst (NMGF, Gila Trout).
4. **Desert Fishes had the largest roster.** It listed five FWS coordinators, one team leader, roughly 15 members, and four consultants. The Woundfin team had about 10 members and 4 consultants.
5. **Membership overlap indicates shared expertise.** Dean Hendrickson (AGFD) and Jerry Burton (FWS) sat on multiple teams, linking the Gila Trout, Desert Fishes, Woundfin, and Colorado River Fishes efforts.
6. **Cross-border participation.** The Desert Fishes team included a member from Sonora, Mexico (Lourdes Juarez-Romero), reflecting work on fishes shared across the U.S.-Mexico border.
