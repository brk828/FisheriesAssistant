# FY 2001 Summary of Fish Surveys of Salt River Project and Florence-Casa Grande Canals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report summarizes fish surveys conducted by [[Arizona State University]] and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR) during fiscal year 2001 (1 October 2000 to 30 September 2001) under a long-term fish monitoring plan for the [[Gila River Basin]], Arizona. Sampling covered the [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) Arizona (North) Canal, SRP South Canal, and the [[Florence Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG) of the San Carlos Irrigation Project, between 4 October 2000 and 8 January 2001. Across the canal systems, 20 taxa were recorded, none of them new to the monitoring plan's site records apart from striped bass, threadfin shad and bluegill. The four native taxa were a minority of the fauna, and most catches were dominated by non-native fishes. The report also flags sampling biases caused by dewatering lapses and recommends improved coordination and routine sampling in years without canal dry-ups.

## Background & Description

The monitoring program was established roughly six years before this report and follows protocols in Clarkson (1996), a revised long-term plan for fish populations in selected Gila River basin waters. Canal sampling was opportunistic and qualitative, so catches are not strictly comparable among sites. Sampling was timed to scheduled canal outages, when water was shut off at [[Granite Reef Diversion Dam]] (GRDD) and at [[Ashurst-Hayden Diversion Dam]] (AHD).

Fish were collected with several gears:

- [[Electrofishing]] (boat-mounted and backpack units)
- Seine hauls and dip nets
- Hand collection
- [[Gill Netting]] (50 ft multi-filament experimental net, roughly 5 hours)

Voucher specimens were retained for most taxa, though some were only photographed or recorded in the field.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Canal conditions.** The canals were highly modified. Substrates were mostly concrete, with debris, trash, fine mud and silt. Water varied from dry to full capacity, and several reaches were pooled or isolated. Visibility ranged from about 25 cm to about 1 m, and some pools were turbid or milky. Recorded temperature-compensated conductivity at one Arizona Canal site was 643 µS.

**Barriers and deviations.** An [[Electric Fish Barrier]] at GRDD separates the canals from the Gila River above. The Gila River below Coolidge Dam had near-zero flow through the summer of 2000, so the FCG received no inflow.

Sampling was delayed after canal shutdown: about 4 days for SRP South Canal, about 2½ days for SRP Arizona (North) Canal, and no deviation at FCG. The authors note these lapses may have allowed rare species to be removed, but they had no verification that this occurred.

**Native and non-native fauna.** Four native taxa were collected: [[Longfin Dace]] (FCG only), [[Roundtail Chub]] (SRP South Canal only), [[Sonora Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]] (both SRP canals). Natives made up 12.5 to 20 percent of total species but a small fraction of total numbers. Non-native species were numerous, including [[Red Shiner]], [[Grass Carp]], [[Tilapia]] (*Tilapia aurea*), [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Bluegill]], [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Striped Bass]].

Invertebrates noted included crayfish (three observed at one FCG site) and [[Asiatic Clam]], both present in SRP South Canal and FCG.

## Key Findings

**Monitoring overview**
- 20 taxa were captured in FY 2001 canal monitoring, excluding fish taken by the Arizona Game and Fish Department above electric barriers.
- Species counts by system:
  - SRP Arizona (North) Canal: 13 taxa (11 non-native)
  - SRP South Canal: 15 taxa (12 non-native)
  - FCG: 8 taxa (7 non-native)
- Striped bass were captured in SRP South Canal, and threadfin shad and bluegill were taken from FCG. None of these was included in the earlier site records (Clarkson 1996).

**SRP Arizona (North) Canal (January 7–8, 2001)**
- Two sites were sampled: an electrofished 0.2 km reach between the broad-crested weir and the electric barrier (9 species), and a reach from the Indian Bend Wash siphon to the Pima Expressway bridge (10 species, about 20% of habitat sampled, 1333 s of electrofishing).
- Thirteen species plus one hybrid were recorded across both sites. The Sonora × desert sucker hybrid was observed at the second site.
- Red shiner and grass carp were the most common non-natives. Desert sucker was abundant and Sonora sucker was common.

**SRP South Canal (November 27, 2000)**
- Four sites were sampled between the GRDD barrier and the Triple Junction, yielding 15 species.
- Sonora and desert suckers were abundant. Roundtail chub was rare.
- At the McDowell Road de-mossing station, numerous dead and dying fish were impinged against trash racks. Dead and dying Sonora suckers were also seen throughout the Tempe Canal reach.

**FCG (October 4, 2000)**
- Six sites were sampled. Most of the reach was desiccated, with isolated pools holding non-native fishes such as common carp, red shiner, mosquitofish, and channel catfish.
- Pima Lateral Canal (PLC) at the Pima Lateral feeder canal junction yielded longfin dace, a native species, together with red shiner.

**Recommendations**
1. Improve communication between canal operators (SRP, SCID) and monitoring staff (ASU, BR) so that sampling can coincide with scheduled and unscheduled outages. Communication lapses have been a concern since the program began nearly six years earlier, with no clear improvement.
2. Modify the monitoring protocol to allow routine sampling in years without canal dry-ups, which the authors expect to become the norm because annual dewatering for maintenance is no longer required. Boat-mounted electrofishing combined with hoop and/or entanglement netting in selected areas is recommended. This was forwarded in 2000 and partially implemented in the current period.
