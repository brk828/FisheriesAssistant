# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Duties and Functions - BLM Input

#ichthyology #conservation #desert-fishes #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Bureau of Land Management]] [[Tucson Resource Area Office]] to Dr. W.L. Minckley, Team Leader of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], provides input on the Recovery Team's functions and duties. Written by Jeffrey R. Simms and transmitted March 16, 1993, the letter evaluates the team's effectiveness, identifies communication challenges with the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], and proposes specific duties the Recovery Team should and should not undertake. The document emphasizes the team's value in providing expert guidance on conservation of biologically imperiled desert fishes while noting underutilization of the team's expertise by FWS leadership.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] operates under the [[Department of Zoology]] at [[Arizona State University]] and provides technical expertise to the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] regarding conservation and recovery of endangered desert fish species. Jeffrey R. Simms had been attending Recovery Team meetings since fall 1990 at the time of this correspondence. The team's primary functions include making recommendations to the FWS Regional Director, developing and critiquing recovery plans, and providing feedback on FWS initiatives. The team composition includes diverse expertise relevant to desert aquatic ecosystems and imperiled fish species conservation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Desert fishes represent biologically imperiled species inhabiting specialized aquatic ecosystems in the southwestern United States, particularly [[Arizona]]. These species face conservation challenges requiring coordinated management across multiple land management agencies. The Recovery Team's purview includes species sharing ecosystems with other taxa, necessitating coordination with additional recovery teams. The [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 consultation process provides the regulatory framework for conservation actions, though the letter recommends the Recovery Team not be directly involved in routine consultations.

## Key Findings

**Team Effectiveness and Challenges:**
- The Recovery Team provides valuable aid to FWS but experiences occasional dysfunction in communication and free expression
- Emotional responses sometimes create confrontational atmospheres that stifle productivity during controversial discussions
- A recurring issue at meetings since 1990 has been questioning the team's importance and perceived lack of FWS support
- Recovery Team input to the Director's representatives has not received warranted response or action
- Communication lines to the Director need strengthening through more frequent personal or face-to-face meetings

**Recommended Duties (Should Include):**
1. Development of recovery objectives and detailed strategies for meeting those objectives
2. Technical review of FWS plans and initiatives
3. Recommendation of strategies to solve novel or complex conservation problems
4. Coordination with other teams working with species sharing ecosystems
5. Direct interface with Regional Director through written and oral communication
6. Encouraging Director to express philosophies, priorities, and expectations directly to Recovery Team
7. Creation of task-oriented subcommittees from Recovery Team member subsets
8. Distribution of meeting minutes to lead biologists in various land management agencies
9. Limiting attendance to invited guests serving predetermined functions

**Activities to Avoid (Should Not Include):**
1. Section 7 ([[Endangered Species Act]]) consultations - FWS should contact experts as needed independently
2. Assistance for routine problem solving
3. Serving as sounding-board for guest ideas and opinions unless requested
4. Addressing personal or agency questions unrelated to meeting agenda items, which derails focused discussion

**Value Proposition:**
- Recovery Team provides "fresh" and well-conceived input on pressing conservation items
- Offers FWS opportunities to pursue conservation avenues for biologically imperiled fishes that would otherwise be unavailable
- Should remain available to provide best options for conservation based on science and professional experience regardless of whether Director acts on advice
