# Sur la Phylogénie du Genre Hadzia (Crustacea Amphipoda)

#marine-biology #crustacea #amphipoda

## Executive Summary

This 1974 paper by [[Amilcar Mateus]], published as No. 128 of the *Publicações do Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre"* (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), proposes a phylogeny for the amphipod genus [[Hadzia]] and its morphologically close genera. It also discusses the genus's geographic distribution and paleogeographic history. The genus includes six species: four European and two Caribbean. All are groundwater or cave dwellers with troglobiont morphology. The author compares characters across about eleven related genera, groups them by shared traits, and builds a hypothetical family tree from a primitive Atlantic marine ancestor. The author states that the conclusions are tentative and depend on incomplete data, particularly the missing uropod III material of [[Weckelia]] and the lack of fossil evidence.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Hadzia]] was defined by [[Karaman, S.]] in 1932 from two species he described in the same work. Its family placement has been disputed. Some authors treat [[Hadziidae]] as monotypic, while others place the genus in [[Gammaridae]]. Mateus concludes that Gammaridae is the better placement, because no differential characters are strong enough to separate Hadzia from that family.

[[Metaniphargus]] is a synonym of Hadzia, which gives the genus six valid species:

- **European species (4):** [[Hadzia Fragilis]], [[Hadzia Gjorgjevici]] (with the subspecies *H. gjorgjevici crispata*, described by G. Karaman in 1969), [[Hadzia Minuta]], and [[Hadzia Tavaresi]].
- **American species (2):** [[Hadzia Curasavica]] (Curaçao, Lesser Antilles) and [[Hadzia Nicholsoni]] (Dark Cave, Barbuda).

The diagnostic character that separates the European and American species is the relative length of the uropod III branches. In European species the inner branch is nearly as long as the outer. In American species it is about half the length of the outer branch. The author notes that this difference makes the criterion uncertain. The paper was presented at the VI Congresso Latinoamericano de Zoologia (Mexico, September–October 1974) and was produced under Project PB4 of the Instituto de Alta Cultura.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All Hadzia species live in groundwater: wells, springs, and cave pools. Water ranges from brackish to fresh. The author describes all species as showing pronounced troglobiont morphology.

Distribution is discontinuous but follows two patterns. Most localities lie near the sea or along waterways that allow aquatic dispersal:

- ***H. fragilis*** is recorded from a cave pool in Vjeternic, Herzegovina, and from many sites between Rovinj (Istria) and Dubrovnik (Montenegrin coast).
- ***H. gjorgjevici*** comes from Skopje and other wells and springs in the Vardar basin. A separate form, *H. g. crispata*, is known from a well in Titograd (Podgorica).
- ***H. minuta*** comes from brackish water in a branch of the cave "L'Abisso" near Castro Marina, Salentine Peninsula, Italy.
- ***H. tavaresi*** is found in wells near Lagos and Tavira in the Algarve, Portugal, close to the coast. The author notes that the strata at these Portuguese localities are Miocene.

The author describes the distribution of the closest relatives as largely tropical and subtropical. Several genera are restricted to islands, including [[Psammoniphargus]] (Réunion) and [[Liagoceradocus]] (Ifaluk Atoll, Caroline Islands). The author links the distribution to orographic barriers, which he considers strong in the Adriatic coast of Yugoslavia and weaker in the Algarve, but absent in Italy.

## Key Findings

1. **Species count and status.** Six species are accepted. Metaniphargus is a synonym. The European and American forms differ mainly in uropod III branch proportions, a criterion the author himself treats as uncertain.

2. **Morphological comparison.** The author compares about 11 genera, including [[Eriopisa]] (11 species), [[Weckelia]], [[Paraweckelia]], [[Alloweckelia]], [[Mexiweckelia]], [[Quadrivisio]], [[Psammoniphargus]], [[Ceradocus]], [[Cheirocratus]], [[Megaluropus]], and [[Liagoceradocus]]. Characters compared include eye presence, mandibular palp, accessory flagellum articles, uropod III form, telson cleft depth, and lobes on the lower lip.

3. **Grouping by shared characters.** A quantitative table sorts genera into three groups, with percentage similarity used as the criterion:
   - **Group I** is anophthalmous, with a three-articled mandibular palp, a telson cleft to the base, and laminar uropod III branches. It includes Hadzia, Liagoceradocus, Eriopisa, Paraweckelia, and Alloweckelia. Weckelia's placement is uncertain because its uropod III material was lost.
   - **Group II** has a reduced or absent inner lobe on the lower lip, a telson not cleft to the base, and laminar uropods. It includes Mexiweckelia, Quadrivisio, Psammoniphargus, Cheirocratus, and Megaluropus.
   - **Group III** includes Mexiweckelia, Ceradocus, and Cheirocratus. Its exact membership is less settled in the table.

4. **Hypothetical primitive form.** The author proposes that the ancestor had a three-articled mandibular palp, a 10-articled accessory flagellum, probably two reniform eyes, subequal laminar uropod III branches, a lower lip with inner lobes, and a telson deeply cleft but not to the base. Reduction and loss of eyes, palp, and flagellum articles produce the derived forms.

5. **Proposed phylogeny (Fig. 3).** The tree gives three lineages from a common trunk. The author stresses that this is a starting point for future revision, not a settled result.

6. **Paleogeographic scenario.**
   - The primitive form lived in the Tertiary Atlantic.
   - *Eriopisa* likely dispersed through the Atlantic–Mediterranean, Indian, and Pacific connections in the early Miocene. The author suggests it reached the Pacific via the Panama passage and gave rise to *E. garthi* in California.
   - Hadzia likely reached the Antilles westward, possibly in the Pliocene after the Isthmus of Panama closed, and also entered the Mediterranean and gave rise to *H. tavaresi* in the Algarve.
   - *Liagoceradocus* may have reached the Carolines through the Pacific.
   - Eastward movement of Hadzia into the Mediterranean may have been followed by isolation when the Gibraltar connection closed and salinity rose.

7. **Species relationships.** The European species show no clear succession, so no one species can be identified as the ancestor of the others. Each probably arose independently from the marine ancestral form. Differences involve accessory flagellum length (uniarticulate in *H. gjorgjevici*, biarticulate in the rest) and uropod III branch proportions.

8. **Speciation timing.** The author argues that speciation in Hadzia is relatively recent. The Algarve strata are Miocene, and the genus occupies coastal zones that were recently accessible. He also suggests that the Vardar form of *H. gjorgjevici* results from regression of a Aegean-linked gulf, and that the Titograd form marks the early stage of divergence.

9. **Uncertainty and open questions.** The author admits that the data are insufficient, that deductions are contestable, and that the value of key characters is unclear. Priority problems are recovering *Weckelia* material, resolving the evolutionary weight of characters, and refining Mediterranean paleogeography.
