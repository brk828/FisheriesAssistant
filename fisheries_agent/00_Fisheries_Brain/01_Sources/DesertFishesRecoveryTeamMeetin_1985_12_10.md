## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum from Jerry Burton, Endangered Species Biologist for Region 2, notifying members of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT) of an upcoming meeting at Arizona State University (December 10–11, 1985). The memo is administrative rather than a primary scientific study, but it provides a valuable snapshot of the federal conservation status and recovery planning priorities for a suite of imperiled desert fish species across the [[Rio Yaqui]] and lower [[Colorado River]] basins during the mid-1980s. Key agenda items include drafting a Rio Yaqui recovery plan, clarifying the scope of the Team's responsibilities, and reviewing existing recovery plans for listed and proposed species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While detailed habitat ecology is not described, the document implicitly situates all listed taxa within isolated desert aquatic ecosystems of the arid Southwest, characterized by:

- **Rio Yaqui basin** (Sonora, Mexico / Arizona border region) — habitat for [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], and [[Beautiful Shiner]].
- **Lower Colorado River basin** — habitat for [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Humpback Chub]] populations specifically noted as "lower basin" populations, distinguishing them from upper basin counterparts, which may be managed under separate recovery frameworks.
- **Isolated desert springs and streams** — implied habitat for [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], and the [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], all species characteristic of fragmented, small-bodied desert fish assemblages vulnerable to habitat loss, water diversion, and introduced species.
- **Small-stream riffle habitats** — implied for [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]], both historically associated with flowing, gravel/cobble riffle habitat in Gila River basin tributaries.

## Key Findings

- The DFRT was scheduled to convene at Arizona State University on **December 10–11, 1985**, with sessions running from 10 a.m. on the first day to noon on the second.
- Meeting objectives included: developing a **Rio Yaqui recovery plan**, defining the operational scope/mandate of the Team ("what a team does and does not do"), and reviewing existing recovery plans.
- **Listed species requiring newly drafted recovery plans**:
  - [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*)
  - [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*)
  - [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*)
- **Proposed species** for which recovery plans may be needed:
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
  - [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
  - [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
  - [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*)
  - [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
- **Listed species** for which the Team's advisory input was sought regarding revision/implementation of existing recovery plans (lower Colorado River basin populations specifically):
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
  - [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*)
  - [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
  - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* and *P. o. sorensis*)
  - [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
- This memo illustrates the administrative and multi-agency coordination process underlying endangered desert fish conservation in the 1980s, emphasizing basin-specific management (e.g., distinguishing lower vs. upper Colorado River basin populations) and the role of expert recovery teams in guiding federal Endangered Species Act implementation.
