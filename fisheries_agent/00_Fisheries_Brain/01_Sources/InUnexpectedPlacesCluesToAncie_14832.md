## Executive Summary

The article reports on a scientific controversy in **paleoclimatology**: a 560,000-year temperature record derived from a mineral core drilled at [[Devils Hole]], Nevada, appears to contradict the well-established [[Milankovitch Theory]] of ice ages. The core, analyzed by Dr. Isaac J. Winograd (USGS) and colleagues, suggests interglacial periods last ~20,000 years (not 10,000 as conventionally believed), implying Earth's current interglacial period is only about halfway complete rather than nearing its end. This challenges the astronomical (orbital forcing) explanation for glacial cycles and has prompted rebuttals from leading glaciologists including Dr. Nicholas Shackleton, Dr. John Imbrie, and Dr. Wallace S. Broecker.

## Key Findings

1. **Core methodology**: Oxygen isotope ratios (¹⁶O/¹⁸O) in calcite deposits at Devils Hole provide both dating (via uranium isotope radioactive decay) and paleotemperature data spanning 560,000 years.
2. **Contradiction discovered**: The Devils Hole chronology shows the previous interglacial period began several thousand years *before* the increase in Northern Hemisphere insolation predicted by Milankovitch orbital cycles — the opposite of what orbital forcing theory requires.
3. **Duration discrepancy**: The four interglacial periods preceding the current one lasted roughly *twice as long* (~20,000 years) as indicated by oceanic sediment/foraminifera records (~10,000 years).
4. **Unexplained glaciation event**: A major glaciation occurred ~400,000 years ago during a period when solar insolation was not predicted to have changed significantly — inconsistent with Milankovitch predictions.
5. **Alternative explanation proposed**: Dr. Winograd suggests non-periodic internal climate system interactions (heat/moisture exchange among atmosphere, oceans, and ice sheets) may be the primary driver of glacial cycles, rather than orbitally-driven solar radiation changes.
6. **Counter-arguments**:
   - Dr. Imbrie notes local climate effects (e.g., ice-sheet-altered jet streams) could decouple local Devils Hole temperature signals from global ice volume trends.
   - Dr. Fairbanks statistically separated local versus global climate signals in the Devils Hole record and concluded roughly half the variability was local; after removing local effects, the residual record closely matches Milankovitch-consistent global records.
   - Dr. Shackleton and others are scrutinizing Winograd's dating methods and isotope interpretation, citing possible timing distortions from isotope seepage through the aquifer.
7. **Practical/policy implication**: If the Winograd interpretation holds, the current interglacial period may last another ~10,000 years, affecting government policy on nuclear waste isolation timelines (currently mandated for 10,000-year biosphere isolation) and reassessment of glacial versus greenhouse-world research priorities.
8. **Scientific status (as of article date)**: The debate remains unresolved; Dr. Broecker states he would "bet heavily on Milankovitch" while awaiting a reconciling explanation; Dr. Imbrie urges caution given limited experience dating climate events in aquifer/cavern environments like Devils Hole.
