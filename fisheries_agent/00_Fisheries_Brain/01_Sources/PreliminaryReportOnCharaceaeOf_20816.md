# Preliminary Report on Characeae of Australia and the South Pacific

#characeae #charophyta #freshwater-botany

## Executive Summary

This 1962 preliminary report by [[R. D. Wood]] (University of Rhode Island) summarizes a fifteen-month collecting program on the [[Characeae]] (stoneworts) of [[Australia]], [[New Zealand]], [[Fiji Islands]], the [[Society Islands]] and [[Samoan Islands]] during 1960–61. The program produced over 750 collections, from which about 4500 herbarium specimens, 200 vials of cytological material and formalin samples were prepared. The main results are new regional records, a documented eastward decline in genus richness, and an unexpectedly high frequency of [[Chara]] in Australia. The author concludes that the Characeae reached the South Pacific islands from the west, probably from Australia and/or the East Indies.

## Background & Description

The [[Characeae]] are macroscopic green algae that grow submerged in fresh and brackish water. The report deals with four genera: [[Nitella]], [[Chara]], [[Tolypella]] and [[Lamprothamnium]], plus [[Lychnothamnus]]. Following the author's 1962 revision, [[Charopsis]] and [[Protochara]] are treated within Chara.

Field methods were designed to record relative frequency as well as presence. Collectors recorded sites whether or not Characeae were present. Specimens were:

- Mounted as herbarium sheets and dried each evening on a portable field drier (a camp stove with an aluminum rack).
- Preserved in 4 per cent [[Formalin Preservation]] samples, later boxed and shipped.
- Fixed in [[Carnoy's Solution]] (1:3) for cytological study, then stored in 70 per cent ethanol and sent by air mail to [[A. T. Hotchkiss]] at the University of Louisville.

Work was supported by a Fulbright Research Scholar award, a [[National Science Foundation]] grant, and the University of Rhode Island. The program was arranged through [[H. B. S. Womersley]] at the University of Adelaide, who provided laboratory facilities and field equipment. The report notes that [[A. C. Beauglehole]] of Victoria began his own extensive Characeae collecting after two days in the field with the author. By the time of writing he had sent material from 185 collections and over 1200 herbarium sheets.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Australia.** Chara and Nitella are widespread. [[Lychnothamnus]] was found only in eastern Australia, at the southeastern border of Queensland. Tolypella appeared restricted to the central part of the southern coast, near Adelaide in eastern South Australia. Lamprothamnium appeared confined to southern coastal regions, south of about 30° S latitude, within about 50 miles of the ocean, extending from the west to the east coast.

**Great Barrier Reef.** No Characeae were found on the coral "low islands" (Green and Heron Islands), because these islands had no standing fresh water.

**Society Islands.** Characeae were entirely absent from Tahiti, Moorea and Bora Bora, despite diligent searching. A targeted survey of [[Lake Vaihiria]] on Tahiti also found none, even though the lake had a sandy mud bottom, some submerged aquatics, and visits by ducks that might carry spores or propagules.

**Biogeographic gradient.** Genus richness declines steadily eastward:

| Region | Genera |
|---|---|
| Australia | 5 |
| Queensland (NE Australia) | 4 |
| New Caledonia and New Zealand | 3 |
| Fiji | 2 |
| Samoa | 1 |
| Society Islands | 0 |

No Characeae are known from South Pacific islands east of Samoa.

## Key Findings

1. **New records.** [[Lychnothamnus]] (as *Lychnothamnus barbatus* (Meyen) Leonh.) is recorded for Australia for the first time. [[Chara]] is recorded for Fiji. Characeae (a *Nitella*) are recorded for Samoa for the first time.

2. **Confirmation of Tolypella.** *Tolypella glomerata* (Desv. in Lois.) Leonh. was confirmed for Australia at seven localities. Earlier reports, apparently based on [[Nordstedt]]'s 1889 record from Port Phillip (Melbourne), could not be verified with specimens.

3. **Absence from the Society Islands.** No Characeae were found on Tahiti, Moorea or Bora Bora, or at Lake Vaihiria.

4. **Eastward decline.** The genus gradient described above supports introduction from the west, from Australia and/or the East Indies.

5. **Distribution of the genera.** Chara and Nitella are widespread in Australia. Lychnothamnus is restricted to eastern Australia, Tolypella to the central southern coast, and Lamprothamnium to a coastal belt in southern Australia. The author notes that future local collections are needed to define these ranges more precisely.

6. **Frequency of Chara versus Nitella.** Contrary to the author's expectation, Chara is almost as common as Nitella in Australia, at about 40 and 50 per cent frequency respectively. Chara is relatively more frequent in southern regions (South Australia and Victoria) than in the Northern Territory or Queensland. In Victoria, over 46 per cent of genus records are Chara, according to the author's reading of Beauglehole's collections.

7. **Collection totals (Table 1, 1960–61, excluding Beauglehole).**
   - Australia: 221 Nitella, 7 Tolypella, 30 Lamprothamnium, 2 Lychnothamnus and 174 Chara collections.
   - New Zealand: 34 collections, 12 of them Chara.
   - Fiji: 37 collections, 29 of them Chara, with the largest share from Viti Levu (24 collections).
   - Samoa: a single Nitella collection, from Upolu.
   - Society Islands: no Characeae recorded.

8. **Taxonomic status.** Analysis was in progress at the time of writing, and identifications were to genus level only. Chara macropogon, as given in earlier literature by [[Groves and Allen]], is treated here as Lamprothamnium.
