# Mouthpart Structure and Feeding Mechanisms of Gerres; Syndactyly in Bufo maculatus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gerreidae #herpetology #bufonidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines two papers from the *South African Journal of Zoology* (1982, vol. 17). The first, by D.P. Cyrus and S.J.M. Blaber of the University of Natal, examines how five [[Gerres]] species from Natal estuaries capture benthic prey using a [[Protrusible Jaw Mechanism]]. The second, by A.J.L. Lambiris, describes five cases of [[Syndactyly]] in the toad [[Bufo Maculatus]] from near Harare, Zimbabwe. The Gerres paper is the main body of the document; the Bufo paper is incomplete in the source text, which breaks off in the skeletal description of specimen AJL 1544.

## Background & Description

**Gerres (Cyrus & Blaber).** [[Gerres]] is one of seven genera in the [[Gerreidae]] (silver biddies, mojarras, pursemouths), found in most shallow tropical seas. All members feed on benthic invertebrates. Six Gerres species live in southern African coastal waters, occurring in estuaries as far south as the Zwartkops River but uncommon south of the Mtamvuna River in Natal. Five species occur in the [[Kosi System]], where three are abundant over shallow sandy areas and are of some economic importance locally. Earlier work on the genus was limited to Prabhakara Rao (1968), who studied *G. oyena* and *G. filamentosus* in India. The authors' related studies on food, reproduction, identification and distribution are cited as in press or published in 1982.

**Bufo maculatus (Lambiris).** Syndactyly (fusion of adjacent digits) is described as rare in Anura. Five *Bufo maculatus* of various sizes, showing digit fusion, were found in May 1981 at the Atlantica Ecological Research Station, 25 km west of Harare. Three were preserved, fixed in 4% formal-saline, cleared and stained with Alizarin Red Sulphonate (Dawson's method), and examined under a dissecting microscope. The paper defines key terms including aplasia, ectrodactyly, microdactyly, synarthrosis, syndactyly and synostosis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Feeding habitat:** Gerres feed on or just below the substrate, using visual cues to locate prey. Field observations were made by snorkel in the [[Kosi System]] shallows, shelf areas (to about 3 m depth), and channels between the lakes and the estuary.
- **Substrate and particles:** Stomach analyses showed most Gerres stomachs contained sand grains, with a dominant grain size of 400 µm. Sand and other particles are rejected through the gill rakers and opercular opening.
- **Prey selectivity:** Gerres is highly selective. In nearly all cases examined, stomachs contained only the siphon tips of the bivalve [[Hiatula Lunulata]], the most favoured food item in the Kosi system, and no sand grains. This suggests suction feeding is not used when siphons are taken.
- **Bufo context:** The toads were found at an ecological research station; no further habitat or environmental data are given in the source.

## Key Findings

### Gerres feeding mechanics (Cyrus & Blaber)

- **Feeding sequence:** Prey capture involves orientation toward prey, a downward pivot on the tail (body at about 45° to the substrate), a rapid downward dart with mouthparts protruded over the prey, and a return to horizontal to manipulate food. See [[Cine Photography]].
- **Five species, one mechanism:** Feeding mechanisms and mouthpart structure were similar across all five species. The only difference was the shape of the exposed maxilla in specimens of 60 mm S.L. and larger; see [[Maxilla]].
- **Cine-film timing (48 f.p.s.):**
  - *G. rappi*: whole feeding movement 47 frames (n = 3), or 0.97 s (S.D. = 0.09); jaw protrusion 8.5 frames (n = 6), or 0.18 s (S.D. = 0.02).
  - *G. filamentosus*: whole feeding movement 25 frames (n = 4), or 0.52 s (S.D. = 0.04); jaw protrusion 5.4 frames (n = 5), or 0.11 s (S.D. = 0.01).
- **Hovering behaviour:** In the field, fish hovered about 20 cm above the substrate, moved their eyes, then darted to an apparently selected point to take a mouthful.
- **Suction:** Suction pressure is generated as the jaws protrude and the mouth opens, through depression of the hyoid bars and spreading of the branchiostegal membrane, with the operculum closed. Whether suction is used depends on prey type.
- **Jaw mechanism:** Two mandibular adductor muscles (external and internal) and one abductor are involved. The [[Premaxilla]] slides along a groove formed by the [[Maxilla]] heads and the [[Cross-Palatine Ligament]]. The premaxilla is linked to the lower jaw by its own ligament, so the maxilla no longer acts as a lever to protrude it. The authors describe this as a specialized modification, distinct from the basic protrusion mechanism described by [[Millard 1968]] and Eaton (1935).
- **Conclusion:** Gerres has evolved a protrusible jaw mechanism modified for rapid protrusion over the point of capture, which the authors argue improves prey capture success.

### Bufo maculatus syndactyly (Lambiris)

- **Cases:** Five specimens, all from one small area 25 km west of Harare.
  - In three cases, one pair of digits was involved (two on a hand, one on a foot).
  - In a fourth, two pairs of digits on one foot were involved.
  - In the fifth, all digits of one hand were fused, with [[Ectrodactyly]] and marked deformity.
- **Specimen AJL 1544:** Not sexed; snout-vent length 13.0 mm; collected 23 May 1981. It shows multiple syndactyly, microdactyly and phalangeal aplasia of the left pes, involving digits two and three and four and five. The two digit pairs are fused for most of their length, with only terminal phalanges free. The tarsals are unossified and appear fused into one body.
