# Niland Native Fish Ponds: First-Year Experimental Operations (1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum, dated May 1, 1986 and written by Associate Fishery Biologist James A. St. Amant (Region 5), outlines the first-year experimental plan for the [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]] in Imperial County, California. The facility is a first attempt at rearing native fish, so 1986 is treated as an experimental year. The main target species is the [[Razorback Sucker]], with one pond reserved for testing the holding and rearing of the [[Bonytail Chub]]. The plan combines advice from regional biologists and staff at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) and sets out water-quality monitoring, fertilization and stocking schedules, staged sampling and feeding, disease precautions, overwintering, and pond drainage procedures. A program review is scheduled for July 1986.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** First-year experimental rearing of razorback suckers, with one pond used to test bonytail chub holding and rearing.
- **Development:** Strategies were drawn from knowledge of regional desert-area biologists Wayne Parker and Buddy Jensen (USFWS Dexter Hatchery fish culturist).
- **Stocking target:** Razorback suckers were to reach 7-8 inches by November 1986. The stocking number is partly illegible in the scan.
- **Pond types:** New ponds, and "old" ponds that were formerly grass carp ponds (also called CCF ponds in the text).
- **Management and responsibilities:**
  - Kim Nicol (Imperial County Area Biologist) manages the facility day to day.
  - Glenn Black (Associate Fishery Biologist, Salton Sea Field Supervisor) provides supervision.
  - Wayne Parker provides advice as needed.
  - Jim St. Amant (Desert Area Fishery Field Supervisor) coordinates construction and maintenance funding and decides how the reared fish will be distributed.
- **Copies to:** K. Nicol, W. Parker, G. Black, D. Young.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water quality and aeration**
- A [[Thermocline]] is expected to form in the ponds at about 4-1/2 feet (OCR-derived).
- New ponds should stay in good condition the first year, but problems could appear as early as the second year.
- Old grass carp ponds may need [[Aeration]] in the first year. A permanent aeration system and a backup pump for emergencies are being developed.
- [[Dissolved Oxygen]] and temperature are recorded daily. pH is measured every two weeks, since it has stayed relatively stable in the CCF ponds (about 8.1).

**Fertilization and plankton**
- [[Fertilization Rates]] are given in Table I. Stocking rates are kept relatively constant so that different fertilization rates can be tested in the first year, after which optimum stocking densities can be set.
- [[Plankton Tows]] are made weekly in each pond to decide whether extra nitrogen treatments are needed. Weekly plankton samples are preserved in 70% formalin-alcohol for future reference on primary productivity under different fertilization rates.

**Fish disease**
- Swimming pool sand filters used to filter water from the native fish ponds could carry [[Fish Disease]] into the ponds, so the filters are a concern.
- Fish are to be removed every six weeks and sent to Martin Chen for disease checks.

## Key Findings

**1. Staged sampling plan for young razorbacks** (sampling is limited by time and manpower)

| Stage | Size | Duration | Method and frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1/2 to 1-1/2 inches | ~6 weeks | Night lights (3 times per week) draw larvae to the surface for [[Seine Sampling]]-independent survival and abundance estimates. A significant drop in larvae numbers signals a serious problem; Jensen is to be contacted. |
| B | 1-1/2 to 2 inches | ~8 weeks | Seine sampling every two weeks with a 6-10 ft bagless fry net; fry are counted and weighed to get fish/lb. Minnow traps and other methods are to be tested. |
| C | 2 inches and up | Ongoing | Seine sampling once a month with a 20-25 ft bag seine; growth rates are projected between sampling periods. |

**2. Feeding**
- Feed size and rate follow the size of the fish, using trout food size grades, with feed rates set by fish size (a feed table is given in the memo).
- Feed is fed three times daily, enough to cover the pond surface.
- Feeding rates are adjusted from sampling results.

**3. Pond rotation and movement**
- Razorback suckers held in Pond #2 are to be moved to another pond when conditions deteriorate, and moved promptly if dissolved oxygen falls.
- Labor is to be reduced through spreading feed in a more efficient way, and by developing feeding equipment.

**4. Overwintering**
- A subsample of fish is taken from each pond in November to assess growth and size before overwintering.

**5. Pond drainage**
- Where a thermocline has formed, a pump dewaters the anaerobic hypolimnion before the pond is drained. Fresh water is pumped in at the surface, and the pond is drained once it is well mixed.

**6. Program outlook**
- Information on rearing razorbacks will be tested during this first experimental year.
- Techniques and equipment are to be developed to reduce labor, with monitoring kept to the minimum needed.
- The program will be reviewed in July 1986 to decide whether any staffing responsibilities need to change.
