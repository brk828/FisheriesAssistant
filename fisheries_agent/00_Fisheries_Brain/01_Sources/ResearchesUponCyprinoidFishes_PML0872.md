# New American Cyprinoid Genera and Species: Systematic Descriptions (1856)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, dated 1856 by its running headers, describes and rearranges several genera of small American freshwater cyprinoids (minnows). The author proposes new or redefined [[Genus Diagnosis|genera]] including [[Cliola]], [[Alburnops]], [[Codoma]], [[Hudsonius]] and [[Clinostomus]], and places a number of species under the older genera [[Alburnus]] and [[Hybopsis]]. Diagnoses rely on external morphology and, especially, on the [[Pharyngeal Teeth]] and their tooth formulae. The document is OCR-scanned, and many species names and some numerical values are partly garbled; these are noted below.

## Background & Description

The paper is a systematic revision of North American cyprinoids. The author notes that some genera appear to be common to both the Old World and the New World, and that Rafinesque's earlier genus names should be restored where they take precedence. Two issues recur throughout:

- **Pharyngeal dentition as the key character.** Teeth are described as prehensile or raptorial and hooked, usually without a grinding surface, and are scored in tooth formulae such as 2 | 4–4 | 2 or 4–4. The author contrasts these with the dentition of [[Phoxinus]], [[Phoxinellus]] and [[Luxilus]] to separate the new genera.
- **Morphological diagnosis.** Characters include head length as a fraction of total length, mouth position and cleft angle, eye size, fin insertion (ventrals relative to the anterior dorsal margin), lateral line position, scale rows, and the caudal fin, which is bifurcated or deeply furcated throughout.

The text also contains brief comparisons with [[Dionda]], [[Cyprinella]] and [[Hyborhynchus]], and it cites earlier authors and works, including Baird and Girard (1853) in [[Marcy's Exploration of the Red River of Louisiana]] and Cope-era literature on [[Leuciscus]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The text gives collection localities, which show a geographic spread across the southwestern and central United States, the Great Lakes and Atlantic drainages, and northern Mexico:

- Texas: Otter Creek and the Red River system (Arkansas); the San Pedro and Leon rivers and the Rio San Antonio; San Felipe and Live Oak creeks; the Rio Nueces.
- Arkansas: the Arkansas River near Fort Smith; Poteau River; Sugar Loaf area.
- Arizona/New Mexico: Rio San Pedro, a tributary of the [[Gila River]].
- Mexico: Chihuahua River and tributaries; the Valley of Mexico.
- Eastern and Midwestern US: Marietta and Russellville (Ohio and Kentucky); Black Warrior River (Alabama); Chesapeake Bay and the Potomac; Lake Champlain; Chicago Harbor, Lake Michigan; Racine, Wisconsin.

Collectors named include Dr. Geo. G. Shumard, Dr. C. B. Kennerly, John H. Clark, H. Mühlhausen, John Potts and S. F. Baird, mostly working under military survey expeditions (e.g., Col. Graham, Major W. H. Emory, Lt. A. W. Whipple). Ecological information is limited to general coloration and habitat (rivers and creeks) and to the note that one species was "caught" in the Chicago harbor and Chesapeake Bay area.

## Key Findings

**New or redefined genera (as named in the text)**

- **[[Cliola]]**: small cyprinids resembling [[Dionda]] but with curved pharyngeal teeth in a single row of 4–4. Three species are assigned:
  - *Cliola vigilax* (originally *Ceratichthys vigilax*; Otter Creek, Red River tributary, Arkansas).
  - *Cliola velox*: more slender, with a larger eye, larger scales and a black spot on the dorsal and caudal fin bases (San Pedro Creek, Texas).
  - *Cliola vivax*: shorter head and smaller eye than *C. velox*; uniform saffron coloration (Leon River, Texas).
- **[[Alburnellus]]**: a proposed genus for American species otherwise placed in [[Alburnus]]. The author revises the diagnosis of *Alburnus* so that the American species fit a tooth formula of 2 | 5–5 | 2 or 2 | 4–4 | 2. Five species are placed in *Alburnus* in the text:
  - *A. dilectus*: about 3.5 in. long; 10 longitudinal scale rows; uniform yellowish-red body with a silvery lateral streak (Arkansas River, Fort Smith; collected by Dr. Shumard).
  - *A. umbratilis*: shorter and deeper, about 3 in.; 14 scale rows; lateral line deflected on the abdomen (Sugar Loaf area and Poteau River).
  - *A. amabilis*: slender, about 2.5 in.; 9 scale rows; dark reddish-brown back, silvery flanks (Rio Nueces, Texas).
  - A fourth species, *A. [name illegible]*: about 2 in. average; closely resembles *A. amabilis* but with a shorter and deeper head, no dark patch (San Felipe Creek, Texas).
  - *A. socius*: about 2.5 in.; olive dorsum, silvery flanks, golden opercula (Live Oak Creek, Texas).
- **[[Alburnops]]**: a new genus with a smaller mouth, a thickened snout overlapping the lower jaw, a large eye, and a grinding surface on the teeth (absent in *Alburnellus*). Three species from the Arkansas River near Fort Smith:
  - *A. blennius*: about 3.5 in.; 10 scale rows; reddish brown with silvery flanks.
  - *A. shumardi*: about 3.5 in.; 9 scale rows (5 above and 3 below the lateral line).
  - *A. illecebrosus*: about 3 in.; 10 scale rows; the largest eyes in the genus, and perhaps the most graceful.
- **[[Codoma]]**: small cyprinoids with a short head, rounded snout and single-row raptorial teeth. Two species:
  - *Codoma ornata*: described with the fin formula D8+1, A7+1, C6,1,9,8,1,6, V7, P12 (as OCR'd). Purplish-black upper body with golden-red lower parts; from the Chihuahua River and its tributaries.
  - A second, more elongated species with a smaller head and smaller scales (valley of Mexico, collected by John Potts).
- **[[Hybopsis]]**: a tentative genus, reconstructed from *Leuciscus storerianus* and a new Alabama species. It is characterized by a straight lateral line, a barbel at the angle of the mouth, and a tooth formula of 4–4 | 1 or 1 | 4–4 | 1. Two species:
  - *H. storerianus*: from Marietta, Ohio, and Russellville, Kentucky.
  - A second species, shorter and more contracted, with a head forming one-fifth of total length: pale red with a silvery streak (Black Warrior River, Alabama).
- **[[Hudsonius]]**: an elongated, fusiform genus with a lateral line nearly medial, a subterminal protractile mouth, no barbels, and a tooth arrangement that varies (e.g., 4–4 | 2, 2–4–4 | 1). Two species:
  - *H. hudsonius* (regarded as a "Smelt"): collected in Chicago Harbor, Lake Michigan, and the Root River, Racine, Wisconsin.
  - *H. amagus*: closely allied to *H. hudsonius* but with a smaller head and depth; reaches a much larger size, with a golden hue over most of the body; found in Chesapeake Bay and up the Potomac.
- **[[Clinostomus]]**: a new genus of several species and one reassigned former *Leuciscus*. Diagnostic traits include a large mouth with a protruding lower jaw, a very large eye, a strongly declivous head, a downward-curved lateral line, and a tooth formula of 2 | 4–4 | 2. The text does not name the individual species in the extract.
- **[[Mupa]]** (name partly garbled): a single species from the Rio San Pedro, a tributary of the Gila, collected by John H. Clark. The head is a little over one-fifth of total length; the dorsum is reddish yellow with a silvery middle flank.
