# Growth Rates of the White Crappie in the Tennessee River

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae #crappie #age-growth-studies

## Executive Summary

This 1955 fisheries bulletin reports growth rates of [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*) in the [[Tennessee River]] tailwaters below [[Kentucky Dam]], Kentucky. Scale samples from 237 specimens collected May-December 1952 revealed average growth of 3.4 inches in year one, reaching 11.9 inches by year five. Growth rates in the tailwaters were consistently slower than in adjacent [[Kentucky Lake]] proper, with lake fish maintaining approximately a one-inch size advantage throughout life. The study was conducted under Federal Aid to Fisheries Project F-2-R by the Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources.

## Background & Description

The [[Tennessee River]] tailwaters extend 22 miles north from [[Kentucky Dam]] at Gilbertsville, Kentucky, emptying into the [[Ohio River]] at Paducah. The river averages approximately 0.25 miles in width with depths up to 35 feet and exhibits rapid flow rates. The tailwater fishery represents an important component of the overall [[Kentucky Lake]] fishery system.

[[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*) is a centrarchid species of significant recreational importance in this system. This study was undertaken to determine growth trends in the tailwaters and provide comparison with previously conducted research on [[Kentucky Lake]] proper.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Scale samples were collected exclusively from fishermen's creels during May through December 1952. The sampling method resulted in a size-biased sample, with the smallest fish measuring 5.7 inches in length, representing primarily adult specimens.

Scales were collected from beneath the pectoral fin and analyzed using an Eberbach scale machine at 40× magnification. Back-calculation of length-at-age employed direct proportion, assuming a linear body-scale relationship with zero intercept. Total lengths were measured to 0.1 inch and weights to 0.1 pound.

The age-frequency distribution showed the 1951 and 1950 year classes dominated the sample, comprising 38.0% and 37.1% respectively. Age groups I through V were represented, with 90 age-I fish, 88 age-II, 43 age-III, 13 age-IV, and 3 age-V individuals among the 237 specimens analyzed.

## Key Findings

**Growth Rates in Tailwaters:**
- Year I: 3.4 inches (increment 3.4)
- Year II: 6.5 inches (increment 3.1)
- Year III: 8.8 inches (increment 2.3)
- Year IV: 10.4 inches (increment 1.6)
- Year V: 11.9 inches (increment 1.5)

**Year Class Performance:**
The 1951 year class (88 fish, average 7.4 inches at capture) showed 3.6 inches at age I. The 1950 year class (89 fish, 9.6 inches) reached 3.7 inches at age I and 6.8 inches at age II. The 1949 year class (44 fish, 11.2 inches) demonstrated consistent growth through age III (9.2 inches). Older year classes (1948, 1947) were represented by smaller sample sizes (13 and 3 fish respectively) but showed continued growth through ages IV and V.

**Comparative Growth Analysis:**
[[White Crappie]] in [[Kentucky Lake]] proper exhibited superior growth compared to tailwater populations. Lake fish averaged 4.6 inches at age I versus 3.4 inches in tailwaters—approximately one inch larger. This growth advantage persisted throughout life: age II (7.9 vs 6.5 inches), age III (10.4 vs 8.8 inches), age IV (11.9 vs 10.4 inches), and age V (12.8 vs 11.9 inches). The consistent size differential suggests environmental factors in the tailwater habitat (rapid flow, different productivity) constrain growth relative to the reservoir environment.

**Age-Frequency Distribution:**
Modal length groups aligned with expected annulus formation, accounting for collection timing (primarily September-December). The 6.5-6.9 inch mode (25 fish, 10.5%) and 9.0-9.4 inch mode (18 fish, 9.3%) represented dominant size classes. Age groups I and II together comprised 75.1% of the sample, indicating strong recent recruitment or sampling bias toward younger adult fish.
