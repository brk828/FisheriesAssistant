# Michael R. Deland Named to Revive the Council on Environmental Quality

#environmental-policy #water-pollution #federal-agencies

## Executive Summary

This newspaper profile (The Washington Post, Monday, August 7, 1989) covers [[Michael R. Deland]], a regional administrator of the [[Environmental Protection Agency]] (EPA) in New England, who was nominated by the [[Bush Administration]] to chair the [[Council On Environmental Quality]] (CEQ). The article describes Deland's background, his record in Massachusetts and New Hampshire, the decline of the CEQ under [[Ronald Reagan]], and his plans to restore the council's influence. The document does not address fish, aquatic species, or ecological field data; its aquatic relevance lies in the [[Boston Harbor Cleanup]] and wetland protection disputes.

## Background & Description

- **Person:** Deland, the EPA's New England administrator, was described as tough and independent. He has been disabled by a painful back ailment since 1965. He uses a wheelchair at work, drives a car with a hand brake, and sails competitively.
- **Career path:** After a brief stint running a congressional campaign in his native Massachusetts, he took the EPA regional post and later assisted the president of the University of Massachusetts. Earlier he had been considered for EPA chief, but [[William K. Reilly]] was named instead.
- **CEQ background:** Established in 1969, before the EPA, the three-member CEQ advised the president on environmental issues and oversaw implementation of the [[National Environmental Policy Act]], which requires federal agencies to consider the impact of their decisions.
- **Appointment:** [[John H. Sununu]], then governor of New Hampshire and later White House chief of staff, called Deland in May to test his interest. Deland said he would take the post only with a prior commitment to revive the CEQ, and he reported that the president gave one.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Boston Harbor.** The EPA was sued by an environmental group for failing to clean up [[Boston Harbor]]. Under Deland's direction, the agency reversed course: by January 1985 it had become the plaintiff, suing the [[Commonwealth of Massachusetts]]. In May 1986 a federal judge sided with the EPA and ordered the commonwealth to install advanced sewage treatment by 1999.

**Wetlands and development.** As regional administrator, Deland vetoed state-approved plans to site a shopping center on 50 acres of wetland in Massachusetts.

**New Hampshire disputes.** He clashed with then-Governor Sununu over plans to expand a ski resort and to build highways in environmentally sensitive parts of the state.

**CEQ decline.** Under [[Richard M. Nixon]] and [[Jimmy Carter]], the CEQ had a full-time staff of 55 and a budget as high as $3.5 million. Reagan, who had little interest in the environment, cut the staff to 13 and the budget to $850,000 for the current fiscal year. The chairman, [[A. Alan Hill]], did not meet privately with Reagan during his eight-year tenure, and the CEQ issued no environmental initiative of major significance.

## Key Findings

- **Revival plan:** Deland has no detailed road map for the CEQ. His main stated commitments are more money and staff. The administration does not plan to fill the two other council seats, leaving him as the sole voice on the panel.
- **Division of responsibilities:** Deland will defer to Secretary of State [[James A. Baker III]] and to EPA Administrator Reilly on global issues. He intends to oversee federal agencies so they take the lead on pollution prevention and recycling.
- **Philosophy:** He describes himself as a "professional environmentalist" and says the environment is more important than partisan politics.
- **Outlook:** Deland's summary of the council's status: "CEQ is alive and well and on the way back."
