# Pre-Impoundment Surveys of Six Kentucky Streams

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #stream-ecology #reservoir-management #population-survey

## Executive Summary

This 1959 Federal Aid to Fisheries report (Project F-10-R) documents comprehensive pre-impoundment fish population surveys of seven Kentucky streams scheduled for dam construction by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Between 1956-1958, personnel from the Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources sampled fish populations using seining, electric shocking, and rotenone methods to establish baseline species composition and abundance data. The surveys covered the [[Barren River]], [[Middle Fork Kentucky River]], [[Cumberland River]], [[Rough River]], [[Levisa Fork]], [[Russell Fork]] of the Big Sandy River, and [[Drake's Creek]] (not scheduled for impoundment). Species richness ranged from 27 to 51 species per stream. Rough fish dominated by weight across all streams, while forage species were most numerous. The study identified management needs including largemouth bass stocking for several reservoirs and potential for establishing muskellunge fisheries. Continued monitoring during pre- and post-impoundment periods was planned to track population changes.

## Background & Description

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers proposed large-scale reservoir construction throughout Kentucky for flood prevention, navigation improvement, and power production. At the time of this study, dams were under construction on the Rough River, Cumberland River, and Middle Fork of the Kentucky River, with numerous other projects authorized and pending congressional funding approval.

**Study Streams:**

- **[[Barren River]]**: South-central Kentucky, flowing northwest through karsted topography; proposed dam 79.2 miles above mouth creating ~2,300-acre conservation pool
- **[[Middle Fork Kentucky River]]**: Headwaters in Harlan and Leslie Counties; [[Buckhorn Dam]] scheduled for 1960 completion with 555-acre winter pool, 1,200-acre summer pool, 3,610-acre flood pool
- **[[Cumberland River]]**: Multiple-purpose dam 30.5 miles above mouth scheduled for 1963 completion; 62,000-acre summer pool extending 118 miles
- **[[Rough River]]**: Western Kentucky; dam 89.3 miles above mouth scheduled for fall 1959 completion; 1,600-acre permanent pool, 3,000-acre summer pool
- **[[Levisa Fork]]** and **[[Russell Fork]]** (Big Sandy River): Eastern Kentucky mountain streams; [[Fishtrap Dam]] authorized creating ~810-acre conservation pool
- **[[Drake's Creek]]**: Not scheduled for impoundment; sampled due to public interest

**Sampling Methods:**

Electric shocking was employed in shallow, clear waters by moving upstream with crews using dip nets. Rotenone sampling used block nets at upper and lower boundaries, with potassium permanganate detoxification below the lower net. Seine sampling was conducted in limited areas. Stream depth, turbidity, and mechanical failures often necessitated method substitutions.

**Fish Categories:**

Fish were classified into five groups: (1) Game fish—species with regulated creel limits plus grass pickerel; (2) Panfish—centrarchids not classified as game fish, excluding small species like orangespotted sunfish; (3) Edible rough fish—commercial species; (4) Non-edible rough fish—lampreys, gars, bowfin, goldeye, mooneye, and gizzard shad ≥7.0 inches; (5) Forage fish—minnows, shiners, chubs, darters, madtoms, and gizzard shad <7.0 inches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical Characteristics:**

Stream gradients varied considerably: Barren River headwaters averaged 20 ft/mile, Middle Fork averaged 5.4 ft/mile from Greasy Creek to Buckhorn, and Rough Creek (upper Rough River) averaged 32 ft/mile. Maximum pool depths ranged from 3.5 to 12.0 feet across sampling areas.

**Water Chemistry:**

- [[Barren River]]: pH 7.9, total alkalinity 108 ppm, temperature 68°F (September)
- [[Middle Fork Kentucky River]]: pH 7.3-7.5, total alkalinity 8-63 ppm, temperature 71-77°F (June-September)
- [[Cumberland River]]: Data from lock chamber only
- [[Rough River]]: pH 7.7, total alkalinity 144 ppm, temperature 49-73°F (September-October)
- [[Drake's Creek]]: pH 7.9, total alkalinity 124 ppm, temperature 72°F (September)
- [[Levisa Fork]]: pH 6.3-8.3 (historical range at Paintsville), total hardness 40-155 ppm (average 72 ppm)

**Habitat Features:**

Substrates ranged from bedrock and rubble in upland streams to sand and silt in lowland reaches. Fish shelter consisted of boulders, submerged logs, undercut ledges, overhanging roots, and aquatic vegetation (primarily water willow, *Dianthera americana*). Riparian vegetation included flood-plain species (black willow *Salix nigra*, red birch *Betula nigra*, sycamore *Platanus occidentalis*) and upland hardwoods (beech *Fagus grandifolia*, oak *Quercus* spp., hickory *Carya* spp.).

**Pollution Concerns:**

The [[Levisa Fork]] and [[Russell Fork]] experienced significant coal-washing pollution from seven operative mines in Kentucky plus undetermined numbers in Virginia. One dredge operator reported the upper 12 feet of stream bottom was one-third usable sand, one-third rock/debris, and one-third coal, with the upper 4 feet chiefly coal. Oil films were observed on water surfaces in multiple areas.

## Key Findings

**Species Richness:**

Total species collected per stream: [[Middle Fork Kentucky River]] (51 species), [[Rough River]] (50), [[Levisa Fork]] and [[Russell Fork]] combined (44), [[Barren River]] (38), [[Drake's Creek]] (35), [[Cumberland River]] (27). The Middle Fork yielded 37 species in 1957 and 14 additional species in 1958, with 11 of the new species collected by rotenone from a single area.

**[[Barren River]] (2.1 surface acres sampled, September 1958):**

- 1,164 fish, 81.0 pounds total
- Game fish (4 species): 3.9% of number, 17.6% of weight; included [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*M. salmoides*), [[Spotted Bass]] (*M. punctulatus*), [[Rock Bass]] (*Ambloplites rupestris*)
- Panfish (2 species): 2.2% of number, 2.5% of weight
- Edible rough fish: 24.1% of number, 64.4% of weight; redhorses comprised 52.7% of total sample weight
- Non-edible rough fish: 0.3% of number, 4.4% of weight
- Forage species: 69.3% of number, 11.1% of weight

**[[Middle Fork Kentucky River]] (12.9 surface acres sampled, 1957-1958):**

- 2,526 fish, 184.3 pounds total
- Game fish (4 species): 4.3% of number, 11.9% of weight
- Panfish (2 species): 2.5% of number, 1.3% of weight
- Edible rough fish: 34.3% of number, 73.7% of weight; four redhorse species comprised 55.2% of total sample weight
- Non-edible rough fish (longnose gar only): 0.5% of number, 1.7% of weight
- Forage species: 58.3% of number, 11.4% of weight
- **Notable absence**: No [[Largemouth Bass]] collected despite suitable habitat

**[[Cumberland River]] (0.72 surface acre sampled from Lock F, July and September 1958):**

- 2,513 fish, 417.4 pounds total
- Game fish (2 species—[[White Bass]] *Roccus chrysops*, [[White Crappie]] *Pomoxis annularis*): 0.1% of number, 0.5% of weight
- Panfish (2 species): 0.1% of number, 0.1% of weight
- Edible rough fish: 38.3% of number, 40.6% of weight; [[Freshwater Drum]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*) numerically dominant, but [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) dominated by weight
- Non-edible rough fish: 46.7% of number, 55.3% of weight; [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) most abundant and comprised 51.0% of total sample weight
- Forage fish: 14.8% of number, 3.5% of weight
- **Lock-chamber sampling limitations**: Extremely low sport-fish recovery compared to open-stream methods; author recommends supplemental tributary sampling

**[[Rough River]] (2.9 surface acres sampled, 1957-1958):**

- 2,135 fish, 117.3 pounds total
- Game fish (5 species): 1.7% of number, 12.6% of weight; included [[Grass Pickerel]] (*Esox americanus*)
- Panfish (3 species): 3.4% of number, 2.3% of weight
- Edible rough fish: 11.2% of number, 70.2% of weight; redhorses numerically dominant, but [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pilodictis olivaris*) and [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) contributed substantially (catfishes totaled 31.0% of sample weight—highest among all streams)
- Non-edible rough fish: 1.0% of number, 7.4% of weight
- Forage fish: 82.8% of number, 7.4% of weight
- **Notable**: [[Spotted Bass]] comprised 8.9% of total weight; no [[Largemouth Bass]] collected

**[[Drake's Creek]] (1.8 surface acres sampled, September 1957):**

- 982 fish, 159.1 pounds total
- Game fish (4 species): 4.4% of number, 9.4% of weight
- Panfish (3 species): 6.6% of number, 3.3% of weight—**highest panfish composition** among all streams
- Edible rough fish: 30.7% of number, 72.3% of weight
- Non-edible rough fish: 4.3% of number, 10.7% of weight
- Forage species: 54.3% of number, 4.2% of weight
- Excellent shocking conditions (ideal depth and water clarity) yielded high recovery rates

**[[Levisa Fork]] (October 1956, four areas):**

- 524 fish, 53.5 pounds total
- Game fish (3 species): 3.6% of number, 12.7% of weight
- Panfish (longear sunfish only): 0.4% of number, 0.3% of weight
- Edible rough fish: 43.5% of number, 75.4% of weight—**highest edible rough fish composition** among all streams; redhorses comprised 51.1% of total sample weight
- Non-edible rough fish (gizzard shad only): 0.8% of number, 4.2% of weight
- Forage fish: 51.7% of number, 7.3% of weight
- Coal pollution impacts noted; two sampling areas located too far below proposed dam site

**[[Russell Fork]] (October 1956, single area):**

- 59 fish, 27.3 pounds total
- Game fish (4 species): 13.6% of number, 6.9% of weight
- Panfish (bluegill only): 1.7% of number, 0.7% of weight
- Edible rough fish: 23.7% of number, 24.4% of weight
- Non-edible rough fish (gizzard shad only): 37.3% of number, 67.4% of weight—**highest non-edible rough fish percentage** by weight
- Forage fish: 23.7% of number, 0.7% of weight
- Single sample limits reliability; additional sampling near state line recommended

**Management Recommendations:**

1. Stock [[Largemouth Bass]] in [[Buckhorn Reservoir]] (Middle Fork) and [[Rough River Reservoir]] immediately post-impoundment
2. Potential for [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy*) fishery establishment in Buckhorn and Barren River reservoirs; species known from creel records but not captured in surveys
3. Supplement Cumberland River lock-chamber studies with tributary sampling for representative population assessment
4. Relocate Levisa Fork sampling areas closer to proposed reservoir
5. Establish second Russell Fork study area near mouth to evaluate tailwater fishery below Fishtrap Dam
6. Continue intensive pre- and post-impoundment monitoring under Kentucky D.-J. Project F-16-R

**Methodological Insights:**

Electric shocking proved most effective in shallow (<8 feet), clear waters but was limited by turbidity, depth, and mechanical failures. Rotenone sampling in block-netted areas overcame these limitations and yielded higher species diversity (11 additional species from one Middle Fork area). Lock-chamber studies appeared to underrepresent sport fish compared to open-stream methods. Total sampling effort: Barren River 2.1 acres, Middle Fork 12.9 acres, Cumberland River 0.72 acres, Rough River 2.9 acres, Drake's Creek 1.8 acres, Levisa Fork (four areas combined), Russell Fork (single area).

**Taxonomic Notes:**

Several specimens remained unidentified at publication: one madtom (*Noturus* sp.) from Barren River, one madtom and one darter (*Etheostoma* sp.) from Middle Fork, and one darter (*Percina* sp.) from Russell Fork. Specimens were preserved and sent to University of Louisville for verification under Kentucky D.-J. Project F-7-R (Dr. William M. Clay, Leader). One dusky darter (*Percina evides*) from Rough River possessed intermediate characters with blackside darter (*P. maculata*), requiring further taxonomic clarification.
