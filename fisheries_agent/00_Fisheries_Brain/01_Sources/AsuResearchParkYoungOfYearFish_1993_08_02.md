## Executive Summary
This memo reports results of a young-of-year (YOY) fish sampling event conducted on 02 August 1993 at three artificial lakes (Lake A, B, C) within the [[ASU Research Park]] in Tempe, Arizona. Shoreline seining across all lakes yielded 1,136 individuals representing five taxa, all non-native. [[Bluegill]] dominated the catch (86%), followed by [[Largemouth Bass]] (11%) and [[Redbelly Tilapia]] (3%). [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Green Sunfish]] were rare (<1% each). Notably, no native fishes were detected, consistent with prior surveys, and both [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Red Shiner]] — once common-to-abundant — have shown marked declines or absence in recent collections, suggesting long-term shifts in community composition despite overall taxonomic stability.

## Background & Description
- Juvenile **bluegill** ([[Lepomis macrochirus]]) comprising the bulk of the catch were predominantly young-of-year individuals measuring **less than 3 cm total length (TL)**.
- No other detailed morphological or size-class data were reported for the remaining four taxa in this memo; sampling focused on relative abundance rather than morphometrics.
- Gear specifications used to capture these size classes:
  - Seine nets: 1.8 m and 4.6 m long × 1.2 m deep
  - Mesh sizes: 0.32 cm and 0.64 cm (fine mesh suitable for capturing small juveniles)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Three constructed lentic lakes (Lake A, B, C) within an urban research park setting, sampled along shoreline margins during daylight hours.
- **Community structure**: Entirely non-native fish assemblage; no native fishes recorded, reflecting broader patterns of native fish decline in modified/urban Southwestern U.S. waterbodies.
- **Spatial distribution of species varied by lake**:
  - **Lake A**: [[Bluegill]]-dominated (95% of lake catch); also sole location for [[Green Sunfish]] detections.
  - **Lake B**: [[Largemouth Bass]]-dominated (87% of lake catch); sole location for [[Mosquitofish]] detection.
  - **Lake C**: [[Bluegill]]-dominated (93% of lake catch); highest relative proportion of [[Redbelly Tilapia]] (6%).
- **Species interactions/dynamics**: Despite consistent presence of the same five non-native taxa over time, relative abundances fluctuate substantially year to year, indicating unpredictable annual reproductive success and variable year-class strength among species — likely driven by interspecific competition, predation (e.g., largemouth bass on juvenile centrarchids), and/or environmental variability across lakes.
- **Notable absences/declines**:
  - [[Mosquitofish]]: previously common-to-abundant, now sharply reduced (last major encounter noted since autumn 1990 prior to this survey; only 1 individual captured, confined to Lake B).
  - [[Red Shiner]]: absent from seine collections for nearly three years, formerly common-to-abundant, and not recorded at all in this survey.

## Key Findings
1. **Total catch**: 1,136 individual fish across 5 taxa from 90 seine hauls (30 per lake).
2. **Overall species composition**: [[Bluegill]] 86%, [[Largemouth Bass]] 11%, [[Redbelly Tilapia]] 3%, [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Green Sunfish]] each <1%.
3. **Catch-per-effort (CPE)** varied widely among lakes:
   - Lake A: 19.73 fish/haul
   - Lake B: 3.80 fish/haul
   - Lake C: 37.85 fish/haul
4. **Species richness by lake**: Lake A and Lake B each had 4 species; Lake C had 3 species; total of 5 species detected park-wide.
5. **No native fish species were encountered**, consistent with the site's established non-native fish community.
6. **Long-term community trends**: While the same suite of non-native taxa has persisted across years, this survey documents continuing declines of [[Mosquitofish]] and continued absence of [[Red Shiner]], both previously dominant, underscoring instability in relative species abundance and recruitment success over time.
7. **Next steps**: Follow-up sampling scheduled for autumn 1993, to include both entanglement netting and seining, allowing comparison of seasonal recruitment patterns.
