# Hybridization in Nature Between Species of American Cyprinodont Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1943 University of Michigan Laboratory of Vertebrate Biology contribution by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], Boyd W. Walker, and Raymond E. Johnson documents the first known natural hybrids in the oviparous family [[Cyprinodontidae]]. Three hybrid combinations are described from wild collections, each involving one hybrid specimen or a few individuals. The hybrids are morphologically intermediate between their parental species across pigmentation, body proportions, meristic counts and anatomy. The authors also argue that hybrids appear mainly where a rare species occurs alongside an abundant congener, and they suggest this may limit range and local persistence at range margins.

## Background & Description

The study continues a series analyzing [[Natural Hybridization]] in fishes, using methods from earlier papers on [[Japanese Flounder Hybrids]] and [[Sucker Hybrids]]. Earlier claims of mass hybridization among three [[Fundulus]] species near Chesapeake Beach, Maryland, by Weed (1921) were not confirmed. Careful re-examination of museum and field material found no hybrids in that group, though one hybrid between [[Fundulus Diaphanus]] and [[Fundulus Heteroclitus]] was later reported from Prince Edward Island. Natural hybrids were already known in the viviparous [[Poeciliidae]].

Taxonomic status is part of the discussion. [[Chriopeops Goodei]] was once placed in [[Lucania Parva|Lucania]], and [[Plancterus Kansae]] was once placed in [[Fundulus Sciadicus|Fundulus]]. Recent revisers treat each pair as generically distinct, so two of the three crosses are intergeneric.

**Methods.** Specimens were counted and measured following Hubbs and Lagler (1941). A [[Hybrid Index]] was computed for each character on a 0–100 scale, with the more primitive parent set at 0 and the other at 100. Character indices were averaged to give a grand average. Characters were discarded when parental species did not differ significantly or when their ranges overlapped widely. All specimens are deposited in the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fundulus diaphanus × Fundulus heteroclitus (Prince Edward Island).** The single hybrid was collected on 23 August 1939 from [[Lake of Shining Waters]], a freshwater lake with pH 8.9, water temperatures of 17.5–26.6 °C, and abundant emergent and submerged vegetation. Dr. M. W. Smith reported that four lakes on the island lie behind small barriers above high-tide level. Salt water enters only during storms. Clark Pond salinity ranged from 0.7 to 22.2 ppt. Lake of Shining Waters covers 8.1 hectares (2.2 m maximum depth), Clark Pond 12.1 hectares (3.0 m), Long Pond 19.8 hectares (2.0 m), and Dalvay Pond 22.1 hectares (3.7 m). The authors describe these as diaphanus habitats into which a few heteroclitus have wandered.

**Chriopeops goodei × Lucania parva (Florida).** Specimens came from the [[St. Marks Migratory Bird Refuge]] on 7 April 1941. Habitats included a shallow diked lake and tributary, a strongly brackish pond open to the Gulf at high tides, and Gulf shore sand with near-normal salinity. The area was formerly salt marsh, but dikes now keep most pond water fresh. The hybrids occurred with abundant [[Lucania Parva]] and sparse [[Chriopeops Goodei]]. The hybrid site lies near the ecological and geographic limit of Chriopeops, a more strictly freshwater genus than Lucania.

**Fundulus sciadicus × Plancterus kansae (Nebraska).** The hybrid was collected on 21 July 1940 from isolated pools of the [[South Platte River]] in Deuel County. Pools were about 20 feet in diameter and 2 feet deep, with water at 76 °F under shade from a thick [[Spirogyra]] mat and cottonwood and willow trees. Each pool also held 4 Fundulus sciadicus, 554 Plancterus kansae, 4 young Pimephales promelas and 2 Notropis dorsalis × piptolepis. The two genera meet only in the North and South Platte rivers, with Fundulus sciadicus mostly north of the river and Plancterus kansae to the south.

## Key Findings

**1. Fundulus diaphanus diaphanus × Fundulus heteroclitus macrolepidotus**
- Specimen: U.M.M.Z. No. 138833, one adult female from Lake of Shining Waters, seined by M. W. Smith.
- Table I (all four lakes): 3,810 F. d. diaphanus (98.4%), 1 hybrid (0.03%), and 60 F. h. macrolepidotus (1.6%). Lake of Shining Waters alone yielded 637, 1, and 15 respectively.
- Diaphanus was dominant and heteroclitus was scarce. The authors note that the fish were not recognized as mixed species in the field.
- Grand average hybrid index: 50. Measurements: 48. Counts: 55.
- Hybrid features: gill rakers 8 (parents 5–7 and 9–12). Transverse rows 41 (parents 34–36 and 44–51). Dorsal fin origin lies just behind the eye, intermediate between the parents. No overlap with either parent in 11 proportional characters.
- A melanotic-tubercle pattern on the head scales, visible after desiccation, is intermediate.

**2. Chriopeops goodei × Lucania parva**
- Four hybrids were found: one adult male (H41-12), two adult males (H41-15), and one adult female (H41-16). Two of these were used for measurement and comparison in Table IV, and the male hybrid of 28.7 mm standard length is illustrated in Plates IV and VI.
- Table III (all five collections): 1,250 Lucania parva (97.3%), 4 hybrids (0.3%), and sparse Chriopeops goodei. Collections containing hybrids were 96.0% Lucania, 0.5% hybrids, and 3.6% Chriopeops.
- Hybrid index: measurements 52, counts 59, grand average 55, a deviation of 10 per cent from exact intermediacy.
- Pigmentation is intermediate in the lateral dark band, caudal spot, opercular and muzzle bands, predorsal stripe, eye bars, and dorsal and anal fin markings. Some features are less extreme than either parent.
- The dentition shows a few very small teeth behind the main row, which the authors interpret as the inner row typical of Chriopeops. The authors call this "a very strong indication of hybridization."

**3. Fundulus sciadicus × Plancterus kansae**
- Specimen: U.M.M.Z. No. 135141, one half-grown fish of 23.4 mm standard length.
- Table V: random collection No. 266 contained 4 F. sciadicus (0.7%), 1 hybrid (0.2%), and 554 P. kansae (99.1%). All Platte River collections totalled 301 F. sciadicus (16.7%), 1 hybrid (0.1%), and 1,501 P. kansae (83.3%).
- Character index for scale and ray counts: F. sciadicus 127–141 (average 132), P. kansae averaging 229, a gap of 97 points. Hybrid index for this character: 42.
- Average hybrid index for measurements: 62. Grand average: 55.
- Hybrid features: 8–10 cross bars (versus 12–18 in P. kansae and none in F. sciadicus). The predorsal diamond spot is elongated into a stripe. Pelvic fins are intermediately separated. Gill rakers are intermediate. The gill-slit extent is intermediate.

**4. Conclusions and ecological implications**
- The authors conclude that Cyprinodontidae is now a family in which species hybridize in nature, and that this evidence is "reliable and conclusive." They judge natural hybridization in the family to be "one of the rarest of phenomena."
- Freshwater forms appeared more prone to hybridization than marine ones. This extends an earlier principle, since Fundulus heteroclitus macrolepidotus is chiefly marine and the Lucania involved ranges from salt to fresh water.
- In all three cases, hybrids were found where one parent was rare and the other abundant. The authors cite similar patterns in [[Catostomidae]] hybrids.
- If interspecific hybrids are largely sterile, hybridization could reduce the biotic potential of rare peripheral populations. This might accelerate local extirpation and help shape complementary distributions. The authors assume hybridization would usually be only a contributing factor in range limitation.
