# Fishes from the Colorado River Basin: Gilbert and Scofield (Proceedings of the United States National Museum, Vol. XX)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #poeciliidae #gobiidae #paralichthyidae

## Executive Summary

This Proceedings of the United States National Museum paper (Gilbert and Scofield, Vol. XX, No. 1131) is a systematic and descriptive survey of fishes collected from the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The collections were made during an expedition that sampled the main Colorado River, its tributaries (notably the [[Gila River]] and [[Salt River]]), and springs, extending to the river mouth in Sonora, Mexico. The authors report on the distribution of the native fauna, describe several species new to science, and provide morphometric measurements. The document's introductory text notes that the basin's freshwater fish fauna is unusually high in endemic and isolated forms, interpreted as a long period of isolation from other drainages.

## Background & Description

The paper is a taxonomic report, with species accounts organised by family: Catostomidae (suckers), Cyprinidae (minnows), Poeciliidae (livebearers), Cyprinodontidae (pupfishes), Gobiidae (gobies), and Paralichthyidae (flatfishes). Each account typically gives locality, specimen numbers (U.S.N.M.), synonymy, and meristic and proportional measurements (head length, eye, snout, interorbital width, fin ray counts, scale counts, and gill raker counts). Morphological comparisons are made with related species, including [[Pantosteus]] and [[Gila]] species.

Notable species accounts include:

- **Pantosteus arizonae** Gilbert, new species: described from specimens at Tempe, Arizona, with a tabulated set of measurements; the species is recognised by large lips and a flattened, broad head.
- **Catostomus latipinnis** (Baird and Girard): taken in the Salt River, Gila River, and the Grand River, with scale and fin counts and a note on its distribution.
- **Ptychocheilus lucius** Girard: a species described as abundant in the Gila and lower Colorado and "most highly prized" of native fishes, known locally as "Colorado Salmon."
- **Gila elegans** Baird and Girard, and **Gila robusta** Baird and Girard: both common in the Colorado and Salt River systems.
- **Tiaroga cobitis** Girard: collected at Chino, Arizona, from the Rio San Pedro (Gila Basin); the authors note it had not been taken since the discovery of the types in 1851.
- **Agosia couesii** Yarrow, **Agosia osculus** Girard, **Agosia chrysogaster** Girard: Agosia species from the Colorado Basin.
- **Plagopterus argentissimus** Cope: a rare species recorded from the Gila mouth and Salt River, previously known only from San Luis Valley, Colorado.
- **Meda fulgida** Girard: described as "extremely abundant" in the upper Gila at Chino, Arizona.
- **Gillichthys detrusus** Gilbert and Scofield, new species: from the Colorado River mouth, Sonora, Mexico; compared to [[Gillichthys mirabilis]].
- **Paralichthys aestuarius** Gilbert and Scofield, new species: from the Colorado River mouth at Shoal Point, Mexico, distinguished from [[Paralichthys californicus]] by fin ray and gill raker counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses the [[Colorado River]] system and its tributaries, emphasising the basin's high proportion of endemic freshwater fishes. The authors note that many species occur only where the river flows through natural drainage or springs, and that several taxa are isolated from other basins. One species reportedly occurs in the Colorado Basin but is otherwise known only from the Rio Sanbonita of Sonora, Mexico, which the authors consider anomalous and contradictory to the expected pattern.

Habitat notes include: fish living in muddy creeks and channels connected to the river at the Colorado River mouth; a species occurring in a spring-fed pond at Lerdo, Mexico; and a flatfish found at estuarine habitats at the river mouth. Several species are described as sharing habitats with pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and mosquitofish (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*). The authors also note that the river fauna of the Colorado and its tributaries had not been fully explored.

## Key Findings

- The Colorado River Basin fish fauna includes a high proportion of endemic and isolated species, interpreted as a result of a long period of isolation.
- Several new species are described: *Pantosteus arizonae* Gilbert (new species), *Gillichthys detrusus* Gilbert and Scofield (new species), and *Paralichthys aestuarius* Gilbert and Scofield (new species).
- *Gila robusta* and *Leuciscus intermedius* are described as very abundant in the Salt River at Tempe, with *Gila robusta* the most abundant species there.
- *Ptychocheilus lucius* (Colorado Salmon) is the most valued native fish and is taken regularly in the Gila and lower Colorado.
- *Tiaroga cobitis* had not been collected since the discovery of its types in 1851; it was obtained again at Chino, Arizona.
- *Plagopterus argentissimus* was previously known only from San Luis Valley; this paper records it from the Gila mouth and Salt River.
- *Paralichthys aestuarius* is distinguished from *Paralichthys californicus* by its higher fin ray counts and gill rakers (9+20).
- Several taxonomic issues are discussed, including synonymy of *Gila robusta* with *Gila grahami*, and the confused synonymy of *Leuciscus intermedius* and *Leuciscus niger*.
