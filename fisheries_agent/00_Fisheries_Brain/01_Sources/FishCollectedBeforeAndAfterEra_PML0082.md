# Green River Fish Collections Before and After Rotenone Eradication (1962)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 internal memorandum from a [[Fisheries Management]] biologist to the Regional Director summarizes fish collections made in the [[Green River]] within [[Dinosaur National Monument]] before and after a [[Rotenone]] eradication treatment. The collections were qualitative species surveys, not population studies. The author explicitly states that any population figures derived from them are erroneous and may only indicate which species were present, at which locations, and when they were sampled.

## Background & Description

The memo was written by Robert X. Azevedo, Fisheries Management Biologist (Springville, Utah), to Jack Berryhill, Regional Supervisor (Albuquerque, New Mexico), and dated December 1962. Its subject is the log of numbers of fish collected before and after eradication in the Green River.

Two field trips were made to the Green River in July and September–October 1962. The memo notes that no effort was made to estimate fish density. A species-composition collection was the only aim of the surveys. Population estimates would require far more extended sampling across the year.

Collection methods recorded in the tables include:
- [[Gill Net]] sets and soaking gill-nets
- [[Fyke Net]] and fyke-trap sets
- [[Hand Line]] and hook-and-line fishing
- [[Electrofishing]]
- Rotenone application during the eradication phase

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey sites lay within the Green River corridor of Dinosaur National Monument, including [[Split Mountain Canyon]], [[Echo Park]], [[Vermilion Creek]], [[Jones Hole]], and the [[Yampa River]] confluence area. The memo's pre-eradication survey (July 16–23, 1962) and post-eradication collections (September 25 to October 11, 1962) were intended to document the fish community before and after treatment.

The species list reflects a mixed assemblage of native [[Colorado River Basin Fishes]] and introduced [[Exotic Fishes]]. Species marked with an asterisk in the source tables were identified as exotics, including [[Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Brown Trout]].

## Key Findings

- **Purpose and limits:** The surveys documented species composition only. Population figures derived from the collections are explicitly described as erroneous and should be used only to indicate species present during the sampling dates and locations.
- **Pre-eradication survey (July 16–23, 1962):** Collections were made at sites including Vermilion Creek, Jones Hole, and Split Mountain Canyon. The source lists about a dozen species, including [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Redside Shiner]], [[Mottled Sculpin]], and the exotics noted above.
- **Post-eradication collections (September–October 1962):** Sampling was conducted at Green River at Echo Park, Yampa River at Echo Park, and Split Mountain Canyon. Gill-nets, fyke-traps, hand-lines, and electrofishing were used. Species recorded again included Channel Catfish, Bluehead Sucker, Bonytail, Flannelmouth Sucker, Carp, Squawfish, and Speckled Dace, with some species present in lower numbers than before treatment.
- **Eradication context:** The memo's title and tables frame the collections around a rotenone eradication treatment in the Green River. Post-treatment sampling was intended to show which species remained.
- **Data caveat:** The source tables report counts per species, site, and date. No totals or statistical analyses are provided, and the author cautions against reading them as abundance.
