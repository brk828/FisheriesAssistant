## Executive Summary

This 1954 paper by Clark Hubbs describes **Hadropterus scierus apristis**, a new subspecies of darter (common name: **Guadalupe Dusky Darter**) endemic to the San Marcos, Comal, and Guadalupe rivers of central Texas. The paper situates this new taxon within a broader biometric analysis of geographic variation across 14 population samples of [[Hadropterus scierus]] spanning Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, and Indiana. Using graphical statistical methods (a modification of the [[Dice and Leraas Method]] developed with Carl L. Hubbs), the author identifies three major morphological groupings ("morphologic types") corresponding to geography: (1) the Guadalupe system (*apristis*), (2) a broad central Texas/Oklahoma group (nominal *serrula*-like populations), and (3) the Wabash River (typical *scierus*, Indiana). The Arkansas River System population is identified as morphologically intermediate between types 2 and 3.

## Background & Description

**Meristic counts (H. s. apristis):**
- Preopercular serrae: 0–1 (rarely 2–6) — fewest of any population studied
- Dorsal fin rays: X–XIV (usually XI–XII), 11–13 soft rays
- Anal fin: II (rarely I) spines, 7–10 rays (usually 8–9)
- Caudal rays: 15–17
- Pectoral rays: 13–15 (rarely 12)
- Pelvic rays: I, 5
- Lateral-line scales: 63–78 (usually 66–72) — statistically greater than most other populations except Little River sample
- Scale rows, dorsal to lateral line: 8–10
- Branchiostegals: 6
- Gill-rakers: 1–9

**Morphology:**
- Mouth inferior and oblique; broad frenum without groove
- Upper jaw extends to below anterior part of eye
- Moderate eye, approximately equal to snout/upper jaw length
- Outer row of enlarged jaw teeth, inner band of smaller teeth; vomerine patch and palatine row of small teeth
- Dorsal profile rises gently from snout with slight nuchal hump
- Caudal fin bluntly emarginate
- First anal spine notably thicker than second
- Pelvic fins reach 2/3 distance to anus; pectorals reach 3/5 distance to anus
- Enlarged, strongly ctenoid midventral scale row in males (breeding character), separated from normal scales by intermediate scales
- Naked areas: dorsal, anal, pelvic, pectoral fins, distal 2/3 of caudal
- Head partially scaled: opercle imbricate, cheek imbricate above pupil-to-pectoral line, embedded scales on lower cheek and side of head

**Coloration:**
- Ground color olive with 7 rectangular black midlateral blocks (plus indistinct blocks above pectoral base and caudal base)
- Blocks connected to 8 indistinct gray dorsal saddles
- Sexual dimorphism: blocks separated by ground color in females, nearly connected in males
- Three dark spots on caudal base (upper, middle at lateral-line terminus, lower)
- Proximal spinous dorsal black in males, gray in females; distal portion yellow-orange (males) vs. clear-yellow (females)
- Distinguishing trait vs. typical *scierus*: dark blocks narrower/deeper; connecting bars reduced or absent in *apristis*

**Diagnostic comparisons to sympatric congeners:**
- [[Hadropterus maculatus]]: longer upper jaw (~9% SL vs. ≤8% in *H. scierus*), unscaled nape, distinct round black caudal spot
- [[Hadropterus shumardi]]: fewer scales (~50 vs. 60+), narrow frenum, elongate tuberculate anal rays in mature males

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Riffle-dwelling species associated with rocky substrate
- **Microhabitat:** Most commonly found under/around 10–12 inch boulders in main current
- **Water clarity preference:** Prefers moderately turbid water; notably absent from clearest waters (spring heads, main river west of Kerrville); most abundant east of the [[Balcones Escarpment]] where turbidity is higher
- **Flow requirement:** Never collected from non-permanent (intermittent) streams
- **Distribution:** Restricted to San Marcos and Comal rivers (all suitable habitat except near spring heads) and Guadalupe River (not west of Kerrville); absent from all tributaries and from the San Antonio River System
- **Biogeographic isolation:** Colorado and Guadalupe river mouths are separated by ~50 miles of saline coastal water, contributing to reproductive isolation and morphological divergence

## Key Findings

- **New subspecies established:** *Hadropterus scierus apristis* Carl L. Hubbs and Clark Hubbs, distinguished primarily by drastically reduced preopercular serration (0–1 vs. higher counts in all other populations except Arkansas River).
- **Statistical method:** Employed graphical biometric comparison (modified [[Dice-Leraas Method]] via [[Hubbs and Perlmutter 1942]]) plotting range, mean, ±2 SE, and ±1 SD across population samples.
- **Three morphologic types identified** across the species' range:
  1. *Apristis* (San Marcos + Hill Guadalupe samples)
  2. Central group — Colorado, Brazos, San Jacinto, Trinity, Neches, Sabine, Lower Red, Upper Red, Little River samples
  3. Typical *scierus* (Wabash River, Indiana)
- **Arkansas River population** morphologically intermediate between types 2 and 3, complicating clean subspecies boundaries.
- **Clinal variation:** A cline in preopercular serrae count runs from the Arkansas River System toward the Guadalupe system, with Brazos/Colorado samples showing the *most* serrae development and *apristis* showing the least.
- **Taxonomic history clarified:** The name *serrula* (Jordan and Gilbert, 1886) was evaluated but not resurrected for the southern populations because Jordan and Gilbert's original diagnostic characters (smaller scale counts, lighter color, breast squamation) were shown to be invalid or confounded by environmental/seasonal/sex-based variation — largely because their type specimens were likely *apristis* individuals, not a distinct northern-southern split.
- **Possible cryptic species status:** The author raises the possibility that *apristis* could warrant full species status given zero overlap in preopercular serrae counts with adjacent populations and complete geographic isolation, but defers final judgment pending fertility/hybridization tests.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Minimal overall, but present in ctenoid belly scale development, dorsal fin coloration, and block connectivity along the body.
- **Type material:** Holotype UMMZ 162377 plus 41 paratypes (UMMZ 162378) collected Feb 11, 1950, from the San Marcos River near Prairie Lea, Caldwell County, Texas, by Kenneth C. Jurgens and Robert C. MacEwan.
