## Executive Summary

This 1966 completion report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] documents a **Cooperative Water Resources Inventory** conducted under the River Basin Investigations program (Project FW-16-R-6). The study is primarily a **recreational use and license-sales survey**, not a species-level biological study, but it contains valuable secondary fisheries data on stocking programs, creel census results, and fish community composition across seven Arizona lakes: [[Pena Blanca Lake]], [[Parker Canyon Lake]], [[Riggs Flat Lake]], [[Bartlett Lake]], [[Lyman Lake]], [[Lee Valley Lake]], and [[Black Canyon Lake]]. Visitor use surveys (June 1–September 15, 1966) quantified man-days of fishing, camping, picnicking, boating, and swimming per surface acre, correlating recreational value with fishery type (cold-water trout, warm-water, or combination fisheries). A record 320,132 hunting/fishing licenses were sold in 1966 (an 8.1% increase over 1965), reflecting growing angler pressure on these systems.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (meristics, body measurements, or anatomical descriptions) are provided in this source, as the report focuses on recreational use metrics and license statistics rather than specimen-level biology. Species composition is referenced only in the context of creel census results:

- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (Salmonidae) — stocked as fingerlings; managed as the primary cold-water sport species in [[Lyman Lake]], [[Black Canyon Lake]], [[Lee Valley Lake]], [[Riggs Flat Lake]], and [[Parker Canyon Lake]].
- **[[Channel Catfish]]** (Ictaluridae) — introduced to [[Lyman Lake]] in 1965; noted to show "good potential" as a warm-water alternative species.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** and **[[Green Sunfish]]** (Centrarchidae) — present in the mixed warm/cold-water creel at [[Pena Blanca Lake]] alongside catfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report characterizes seven distinct lentic habitats, differentiated by thermal regime, elevation, and management strategy:

- **[[Bartlett Lake]]** — Large warm-water reservoir (~45 mi NE of Phoenix); poor road access limits use to a predominantly angler-driven "fisherman's lake." Absorbed displaced boating/skiing pressure when the nearby [[Saguaro Lake]] was drained in 1966 for dam maintenance.
- **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]** — 49-acre lake in the Ruby Mountains (Coronado National Forest) near Nogales; supports a **mixed cold/warm-water fishery** — trout comprised 54% of the creel, with bass, green sunfish, and catfish making up the remaining 46% (Knipe, 1966).
- **[[Parker Canyon Lake]]** — New impoundment (completed 1964) on the Coronado National Forest; a cold-water trout fishery serving as the only nearby water-based recreation option in southeastern Arizona.
- **[[Riggs Flat Lake]]** — 10-acre high-elevation lake near Mt. Graham (Ponderosa pine forest, Colorado National Forest); the most intensively used lake per surface acre in the survey (1,445 man-days/acre).
- **[[Lyman Lake]]** — Eastern Arizona reservoir managed via fingerling trout stocking, with channel catfish introduced as a secondary species in 1965.
- **[[Lee Valley Lake]]** — Cold-water fishery in the White Mountains near Greer, used primarily by anglers and campers; surveyed for the first time in this project segment.
- **[[Black Canyon Lake]]** — Sitgreaves National Forest reservoir (completed June 1964) on the Mogollon Rim, managed under a fingerling trout stocking program; deliberately kept minimally developed to preserve a natural setting.

These lakes were evaluated using the **Evaluation Standards for Primary Outdoor Recreation Benefits (1964)**, assigning differential man-day economic values based on fishery type:
- Trout (cold-water) fishing: $3.00/man-day
- Warm-water fishing: $1.00/man-day
- Combination fishing: $2.00/man-day
- Non-fishing recreation: $0.50–$1.00/man-day depending on lake type

## Key Findings

- **License sales trend**: 320,132 total hunting/fishing/combination licenses sold in 1966 vs. 296,084 in 1965 — an **8.1% increase**, reflecting rising angler demand on Arizona's limited water resources.
- **Fishery composition**: [[Pena Blanca Lake]] creel census showed trout at 54% of total catch, with the balance from centrarchids (bass, green sunfish) and catfish.
- **Stocking program performance**: [[Lyman Lake]] trout catch rates declined sharply, from 0.32–0.35 fish/man-hour (1965) to only 0.02–0.10 fish/man-hour (1966), a decline attributed to poor fingerling trout survival/recruitment; this correlated directly with reduced angler participation and a shift toward non-fishing recreation (picnicking, boating).
- **Recreational intensity per surface acre**: [[Riggs Flat Lake]] recorded the highest use intensity (1,445 man-days/acre), exceeding [[Pena Blanca Lake]] (564 man-days/acre) — both far surpassing comparable northern Arizona lakes, indicating unmet demand for additional cold-water impoundments in southeastern Arizona.
- **Habitat disturbance effects**: Draining of [[Saguaro Lake]] for dam maintenance displaced boaters/skiers to [[Bartlett Lake]], altering its use profile in favor of pleasure boating over fishing.
- **Facility/management impacts on use patterns**: Prohibition of lakeside camping at [[Black Canyon Lake]] (following construction of a remote campground) produced a 504% increase in picnicking use as former campers reclassified as day-use picnickers — highlighting a methodological caveat regarding how "purpose of visit" vs. "activity type" should be measured in future creel/use surveys.
- **Infrastructure-driven demand**: Improved and expanded picnicking/camping facilities at [[Parker Canyon Lake]] drove a substantial rise in recreational and fishing use in 1966, underscoring the link between facility investment and angler/visitor turnout.
