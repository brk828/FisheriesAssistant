# Lake Mohave Razorback Roundup, March 2010

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

From March 15 to 19, 2010, the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR) Fisheries Group, joined by staff from the USBR Boulder City and Denver offices, took part in the annual March [[Razorback Sucker]] roundup on [[Lake Mohave]]. The team surveyed the Nine-Mile stretch on the Nevada shoreline using [[Trammel Netting]] and larval collections. Razorbacks were processed for length, weight, tag status and pectoral fin sampling, and untagged or 400 kHz-tagged fish were implanted with 134 kHz [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tag]]s. The record is largely a tag and recapture log for hatchery-reared razorbacks, with a smaller set of trammel and larval sampling data.

## Background & Description

The annual March roundup is a recurring USBR operation on Lake Mohave, a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]]. Its main target is the razorback sucker, a large catostomid whose native range has been reduced in the Colorado basin. Captured fish were individually identified by PIT tag, and fish without a tag received a new one. Each fish's record includes tag number, release and capture dates, location, sex, total length (mm), weight (g), and rearing history (hatchery origin and year class).

Roundup operations were based at [[Valhalla Cove]], where captured razorbacks were held in two live cages (male and female) until processing on Thursday, March 18. Sampling methods were:

- **Trammel netting:** 300 ft nets with 1.5-inch mesh.
- **Larval collection:** conducted over two nights in Valhalla Cove and [[Honey Hole Cove]].
- **Genetic sampling:** pectoral fin clips taken from captured fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Lake Mohave is a reservoir, so the sampled habitat consists of shoreline coves and nearshore areas. The document lists several cove and site names used as capture and release locations, including Valhalla Cove, Honey Hole Cove, [[Nine-Mile Cove]] areas, [[Willow Beach]], [[Cottonwood Cove]], [[Dandy Cove]], Davis Cove, Yuma Cove, Placer Cove and Red Tail Cove. Most records are from the Nine-Mile stretch and the Willow Beach area.

Hatchery origins in the tag log are the main source of ecological context. Razorbacks were reared at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Willow Beach NFH and WBNFH, year classes 2003 through 2006), [[Achii Hanyo Rearing]] (2008 year class), [[Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery]] (2002 to 2005 year classes), an unnamed Arizona juvenile rearing group (2006), and a Nevada Egg group (2004). Several releases were made in 2009 and early 2010.

The by-catch was a mix of nonnative and game fish, including [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]]. Its sampling makes it a useful record of the reservoir's broader fish community.

## Key Findings

**Trammel netting results**
- Eight nets were set Monday through Wednesday and four on Thursday, for a total of 28 net nights.
- 52 razorbacks were contacted. 51 were released back into Lake Mohave, and one mortality was recorded.
- By-catch in the species composition chart included carp, channel catfish, smallmouth bass, largemouth bass, striped bass and yellow bullhead. The OCR text garbles most of the carp, channel catfish, striped bass and yellow bullhead counts, so they are not reported here. Smallmouth bass (5) and largemouth bass (3) counts are legible.

**Larval collections**
- Collections over two nights in Valhalla and Honey Hole coves yielded a total of 1,000 larvae captured, plus 25 retained for genetic analysis.

**Tag and recapture log**
- The document contains a long per-fish table covering razorbacks captured between March 15 and 18, 2010. Each row gives tag number, release and capture dates, location, sex, length, weight and rearing history.
- Most captured fish were hatchery-released juveniles (labeled "J") from Willow Beach NFH, Achii Hanyo, Bubbling Ponds FH or the Arizona juvenile group. Captured lengths in the table range from roughly 280 mm to about 650 mm.
- Two entries are flagged as unusual: one unknown-origin female (625 mm, 2,590 g) captured at a location "5 of Willow cluster," and a female recorded as a "possible wild fish" at Honey Hole Cove (652 mm, 3,535 g). Both are out of place among the hatchery-origin records.
- One Honey Hole record is marked "Reo. Cap./Mort" (a recaptured mortality), consistent with the single trammel mortality.

**Operational notes**
- Fish with 400 kHz tags or no tag were implanted with 134 kHz PIT tags during processing.
- Pectoral fin samples were collected from captured fish as part of genetic work.
