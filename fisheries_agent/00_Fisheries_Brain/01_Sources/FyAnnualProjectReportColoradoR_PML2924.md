# Coordinated Hatchery Facility Status Report: Captive-Reared Endangered Fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 1993 annual project report, Recovery Program Project 23, covers the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] recovery effort for four endangered fishes: the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (called "squawfish" in the text). Principal Investigator [[Richard S. Wydoski]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Denver) led the work, which ran from February–April 1993 to December 1993. The project aimed to produce a coordinated hatchery facility status report on the need for captive-reared fish and propagation facilities under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP).

The report identified fourteen presumptive stocks and set propagation priorities. It also found that immediate and short-term needs could be defined, but long-term needs (beyond five years) could not be determined with certainty. No consensus was reached among the [[Biology Committee]]. The document is largely a project record of deliverables, meetings, and handouts, with a final report planned for December 1993.

## Background & Description

The project was tied to the RIP General Recovery Program support action IV.B.1, which calls for identifying fish needs for genetic-stock refugia research, augmentation, and related monitoring, and for education programs. Its stated goals were to:

1. Identify presumptive stocks of endangered fishes in the upper basin.
2. Define the present status and trends of these stocks and set priorities within species.
3. Identify propagation needs for recovery.
4. Define hatchery facilities required to meet those needs.
5. Address needs that existing [[Hatchery Facilities]] can meet.
6. Discuss the need for additional facilities, including at least one in the upper basin.

Also reviewed were the adequacy of existing guidelines and policies, including [[Genetics Guidelines]], a disposition policy, a [[Stocking Policy]], and a coordinated hatchery plan.

The work was carried out with input from the Biology Committee and a review group. The report notes that goals were met "as well as can be expected" given the diversity of opinions among participants. Deliverables included draft and handout documents for the Biology and Management committees, a summary of Biology Committee member perceptions (4 pages), and an implementation committee handout (3 pages).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses the biology of the four endangered fishes only at the level of stock status and propagation needs. Key points include:

- **Razorback Sucker:** Considered to be declining rapidly in the upper basin, though some recruitment was noted from length-frequency analysis. Two broodstocks were attempted, one from the Green River and one from the Colorado River upstream of Westwater. Adult fish collected from a pond near De Beque, Colorado, were found to be hybrids with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and were excluded from the RIP.
- **Bonytail:** Considered extirpated from the upper basin. Broodstock development was to use fish from [[Lake Mohave]], which were being held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Fish Technology Center.
- **Humpback Chub and Colorado Pikeminnow:** Considered relatively stable, based on length-frequency analysis and collection records.

Long-term uncertainty was attributed to missing information on unknown factors, incomplete genetic study results, unclear requirements at different life stages, and unknown responses of the proposed habitat restoration projects.

## Key Findings

- **Stocks identified:** Fourteen presumptive stocks of endangered fishes were identified for the upper basin and summarized in the report (the individual stock list is not reproduced here).
- **Propagation priorities (highest to lowest):** Razorback Sucker (1), Bonytail (2), Humpback Chub (3), Colorado Pikeminnow (4). The report notes that priorities 3 and 4 were the least important.
- **Pond capacity:** A need for ponds to culture and hold endangered fishes was identified. Existing space was judged insufficient. A proposed facility construction in 1994 was expected to add about 11 acres of ponds, against a deficiency of roughly 10–15 acres (exact figures partly illegible in the scan).
- **Augmentation approach:** The Biology Committee generally agreed that all augmentation and stocking should be run as field experiments, with evaluation before any large-scale stocking in the upper basin.
- **Timing of needs:** Captive-rearing needs should be identified as early as possible through Statements of Work and annual review, so that appropriate broodstocks can be developed and fish produced for crucial experiments.
- **Consensus gap:** Consensus on the need for captive-reared fishes and propagation facilities could not be reached by the Biology Committee. Immediate and short-term needs were identified, but long-term needs were not.
- **Budget:** A figure of $6,800 is listed, along with $11,000 for FY 1994. The funds-provided and funds-expended entries are unclear in the scan, and no explanation for any difference is given.
- **Deliverables and timeline:** A coordinated hatchery facility plan was distributed in April 1993. Draft reports went to the Biology Committee for review. The final report was scheduled for December 1993, with the final summary expected in early January 1994.
