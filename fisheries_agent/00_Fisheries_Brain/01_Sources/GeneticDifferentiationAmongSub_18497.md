# Genetic Differentiation Among Subpopulations of Three Gambusia Species in the Pecos River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #population-genetics #desert-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1980 Master's thesis examines genetic heterogeneity among subpopulations of three [[Gambusia]] species in the [[Pecos River]] drainage of [[Texas]] and [[New Mexico]] using electrophoretic analysis of allelic frequencies. The study compared [[Gambusia nobilis]] (endemic spring-dweller in four isolated spring systems), [[Gambusia affinis]] (widely distributed with continuous distribution), and [[Gambusia geiseri]] (introduced endemic from [[Edwards Plateau]]). Results showed exceptionally high genetic differentiation in G. nobilis (average F_ST = 0.755), compared to G. affinis (F_ST = 0.160) and G. geiseri (F_ST = 0.278). The F_ST value for G. nobilis exceeded that previously reported for any fish species, supporting the classical concept that gene flow exerts a dedifferentiating effect on subpopulations. The unusually high genetic variance in G. nobilis may be characteristic of fishes restricted to desert springs.

## Background & Description

The study focused on three [[Poeciliidae]] species exhibiting contrasting patterns of distribution and gene flow opportunity in the [[Chihuahuan Desert]]:

**[[Gambusia nobilis]]** (Pecos Gambusia): Endemic to [[Pecos River]] drainage, occurring only in four widely separated spring systems: springs and gypsum sinkholes at [[Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge]] near Roswell, Chaves County, New Mexico; [[Blue Spring]] near Whites City, Eddy County, New Mexico; spring system near Balmorhea in Reeves and Jeff Davis counties, Texas; and springfed segment of [[Leon Creek]] near Fort Stockton, Pecos County, Texas. This species is restricted to relatively stenothermal, springfed habitats and populations have been effectively isolated since Pleistocene pluvial periods.

**[[Gambusia affinis]]** (Western Mosquitofish): Widely distributed species with more continuous distribution in Pecos drainage, occurring in spring systems and throughout major streams and tributaries. Well adapted to eurythermal habitats showing great fluctuations in physical and chemical features. Two subspecies present: G. a. speciosa in lower reaches and G. a. affinis in upper reaches.

**[[Gambusia geiseri]]** (Largespring Gambusia): Native to [[Comal River]] and [[San Marcos River]] near San Marcos, Hays County, Texas (east side of Edwards Plateau). Introduced into three Pecos drainage springs on west side of plateau, likely in 1920s for mosquito control. Represents recently established peripheral isolates (<100 years).

Collections were made in June 1979 and February 1980 from multiple localities. Horizontal starch gel electrophoresis examined 17 loci (16 for G. affinis due to inconsistent EST scoring), including enzymes: AAT, CK, EST, GPI, GDH, IDH, LDH, MDH, PGM, and SOD.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Pecos River]] system presents a natural experiment in population isolation and gene flow. Desert spring systems are discontinuously distributed, separated by large uninhabitable areas. Springs were larger and more numerous historically, but G. nobilis has been effectively excluded from the Pecos River proper for hundreds or thousands of years due to competition with abundant G. affinis populations.

**Microhabitat Segregation**: G. nobilis and G. geiseri predominate in waters providing environmental stability near spring headwaters, while G. affinis predominates in unstable, eurythermal habitats such as shallow open pools and tailwater habitats. This ecological segregation reflects differential adaptation to thermal and chemical stability.

**Historical Context**: The middle Pleistocene capture of ancestral [[Brazos River]] headwaters by Pecos River likely brought G. affinis into contact with G. nobilis populations. Geographic isolation resulting from competition with G. affinis may have contributed to evolution of various species in the Gambusia nobilis group. Springs at Balmorhea and Leon Creek are isolated from Pecos River proper except during infrequent floods.

**Gene Flow Opportunities**: G. affinis shows high dispersal ability, quickly invading recently flooded areas. The species has been observed in temporary pools on divides between drainages during flooding, facilitating headwater transfers. Interdrainage introductions for malarial control also increased gene flow. In contrast, G. nobilis populations experience essentially zero gene flow for many generations—even occasional strays entering the Pecos River would be unlikely to locate other spring systems.

## Key Findings

**Genetic Heterogeneity (Wright's F_ST values)**:
- G. nobilis: Average F_ST = 0.755 across all four populations (range of polymorphic loci)
- G. affinis: Average F_ST = 0.160 for five populations within and beyond G. nobilis range
- G. geiseri: Average F_ST = 0.278 for four populations

The F_ST value for G. nobilis is the highest reported for any fish species and among the highest for any organism. When only populations from the same aquatic systems (BLNWR, Blue Spring, Leon Creek) were compared, average F_ST values were 0.347 for G. nobilis versus 0.160 for G. affinis.

**Population-Specific Patterns**:
- [[Phantom Lake Spring]] population of G. nobilis showed greatest differentiation; removing it from analysis decreased average F_ST from 0.755 to 0.347, but this remained higher than other species
- Comparing only G. nobilis and G. geiseri from Balmorhea and Leon Creek systems: average F_ST = 1.071 and 0.278, respectively
- Four polymorphic loci in G. nobilis all showed significant heterogeneity (100%)
- Eight polymorphic loci in G. affinis: 63% showed significant heterogeneity
- Five polymorphic loci in G. geiseri: 40% showed significant heterogeneity

**Genetic Identity (Nei's measure, 16-17 loci)**:
- G. nobilis: Range 0.876-0.998 (mean = 0.940)
- G. affinis: Range 0.901-0.995 (mean = 0.958)
- G. geiseri: Range 0.959-0.997 (mean = 0.982)

Phantom Lake Spring population of G. nobilis showed lowest genetic identity with other G. nobilis populations (0.876-0.901, mean = 0.891) compared to identity among other three populations (0.981-0.998, mean = 0.988). Despite high heterogeneity at single loci, genetic identity values remained within range for conspecific populations, not approaching species-level divergence.

**Allelic Variation**: Number of alleles per locus varied by species and locus. Key polymorphic loci included GPI-A, GPI-B, IDH-A, and PGM-B for G. nobilis; GPI-A, GPI-B, IDH-A, IDH-B, LDH-A, LDH-B, MDH-C, and PGM-B for G. affinis; AAT-A, EST-B, GPI-B, IDH-A, and PGM-B for G. geiseri.

**Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium**: Only MDH-C in Bitter Lakes population showed statistically significant deviation (P<0.05), likely representing Type 1 error given 76 total computations.

**Sampling Variance**: Maximum error variance was 0.006 for G. nobilis and G. affinis, 0.007 for G. geiseri. Sampling variance at single loci ranged from 2-6% (mean 3.36%) of observed variance in G. nobilis, 3-26% (mean 17.2%) in G. geiseri, and 2-25% (mean 11.5%) in G. affinis, indicating adequate sample sizes.

**Founders Effect in G. geiseri**: Higher heterogeneity than expected based on recent introduction (<100 years). Despite short isolation period, heterogeneity among Pecos populations comparable to G. affinis over large geographic area with much longer divergence time. Small founding populations and subsequent dispersal to headspring habitats may have amplified genetic drift effects.

**Gene Flow and Differentiation**: Data strongly support classical concept that gene flow exerts dedifferentiating effect on subpopulations, contrary to "new orthodoxy" proposed by Ehrlich and Raven (1969). Positive relationship observed between opportunity for gene flow and degree of interpopulational differentiation. For G. nobilis, gene flow among subpopulations may be zero for many generations, making genetic drift and mutation significant factors alongside natural selection.

**Comparative Context**: Average F_ST for G. nobilis (0.755) exceeded values reported for other fish species including [[Astyanax mexicanus]] (0.386), [[Rhinichthys cataractae]] (0.139), [[Notropis stramineus]] (0.015), [[Lepomis macrochirus]] (0.158-0.563), [[Etheostoma radiosum]] (0.502), and [[Etheostoma spectabile]] (0.046-0.463).

**Conservation Implications**: Extreme genetic heterogeneity in G. nobilis may be characteristic of many desert spring-dwelling fishes. Each population represents unique genetic diversity requiring individual conservation attention. The species' restriction to four isolated spring systems makes it vulnerable to local extinctions with no possibility of natural recolonization.
