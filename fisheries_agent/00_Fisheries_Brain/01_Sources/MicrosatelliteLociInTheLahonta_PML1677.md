# Microsatellite Loci in the Lahontan Tui Chub and Their Utility in Other Chub Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This primer note reports the development of tetranucleotide [[Microsatellite Markers]] for the [[Lahontan Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor obesa*), a freshwater cyprinid from the western United States. From a [[(GATA)n Enriched Library]], 37 primer pairs were designed and 17 gave clearly resolved PCR products in the Lahontan tui chub. These 17 loci were then tested for cross-species amplification in three other *Gila* taxa: [[Arroyo Chub]], [[Owens Tui Chub]] and [[Blue Chub]]. The markers were developed to support [[Gene Flow and Introgression]] studies, which matter for the recovery of rare chubs threatened by [[Hybridization]] and [[Species Introductions]]. The note was authored by Erin P. Meredith and Bernie May of the [[University of California, Davis]] and published in *Molecular Ecology Notes* (2002).

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic context:** The family [[Cyprinidae]] contains over 330 species of freshwater minnows and carps. The genus *Gila* includes nearly 50 species and subspecies of chub found in parts of the western United States (Mayden et al. 1992).
- **Conservation status:** The Owens tui chub (*G. bicolor snyderi*) and the [[Mohave Tui Chub]] (*G. bicolor mohavensis*) are listed as threatened or endangered under the U.S. [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Threats:** Hybridization between species is identified as a prime cause of the near elimination of several chub species (Moyle 1976).
- **Management context:** In California and Nevada, recovery efforts for rare chubs use planned releases and intentional stockings. Genetic markers are needed to track gene flow among these populations.
- **Study species:** The Lahontan tui chub is the focal taxon, and the markers were built from it.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The chubs studied occupy freshwater systems in the western United States. Their conservation depends on managing how populations mix. Introductions and stockings can produce [[Introgression]] between subspecies and species, which can dilute or replace rare genetic lineages. The work was funded by a grant from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] to examine introgression between the Owens and Lahontan tui chubs. The document does not report habitat measurements, water chemistry, population sizes or other environmental variables.

## Key Findings

### Sample Preparation and Library Construction
- Genomic DNA was extracted from **dried fin samples** of *G. bicolor obesa* using a TNES-urea procedure (Belfiore & May 2000).
- Two subgenomic libraries were constructed by digesting genomic DNA with six restriction enzymes (BsrBI, EcoRV, HaeIII, PvuII, ScaI and StyI). An oligonucleotide linker with a HindIII site was ligated to fragments of **400–700 base pairs**.
- Fragments were enriched for (GATA)n repeats by [[Magnetic Bead Capture]] (Jones et al. 2002), then ligated into the HindIII site of pUC19 and transformed into *Escherichia coli* DH5α.

### Sequencing and Primer Development
- **113 clones** from the (GATA)n library were sequenced.
- **37 tetranucleotide primer pairs** were designed using Lasergene 5.1 software (DNASTAR).

### Amplification and Detection
- PCR used 10 ng of genomic DNA in 20 µL reactions. Cycling included 40 cycles with a **52 °C** annealing step.
- Products were separated on **5% denaturing polyacrylamide gels** and detected by fluorescence using an agarose and SYBR-type overlay ([[Agarose Overlay Staining]], Rodzen et al. 1998). Gels were scanned with a Molecular Dynamics 595 fluorimager.

### Marker Performance
- **17 of 37** primer pairs (about 46%) amplified clearly resolved products in *G. bicolor obesa*.
- The same 17 primer pairs, under identical reagent and cycling conditions, were tested in:
  - *G. orcutti* (Arroyo chub)
  - *G. bicolor snyderi* (Owens tui chub)
  - *G. coerulea* (Blue chub)
- Because amplification succeeded in all three other species, the authors concluded the markers have "the potential to be of great use" in future genetic studies of other *Gila* species.

### Conclusions
- The 17 tetranucleotide microsatellite loci are validated for the Lahontan tui chub and show cross-species amplification across related *Gila* taxa.
- These markers provide a tool for studying gene flow, hybridization and introgression in chub populations under restoration and stocking programs.
