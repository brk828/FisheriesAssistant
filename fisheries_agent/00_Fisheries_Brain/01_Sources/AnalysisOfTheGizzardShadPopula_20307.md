# Age Composition and Growth of the Gizzard Shad in Crab Orchard Lake, Illinois

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This 1953 study by William M. Lewis of [[Southern Illinois University]] analyzes the age composition, growth rate and condition of [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) in [[Crab Orchard Lake]], a 6,500-acre artificial impoundment in southern Illinois. A single [[Electrofishing]] sample of 888 shad, taken in August 1952, was aged by the [[Scale Method Ageing]] method. Most fish were under four years old and small enough for adult [[Largemouth Black Bass]] to eat. The author also reports a large die-off of shad in October 1952, estimated at 10,250,000 dead fish along the shoreline, and argues that such die-offs may shape the population's age and size structure.

## Background & Description

Crab Orchard Lake has a surface area of 6,500 acres, an average maximum depth of 15 to 20 feet, and an extremely irregular shoreline. The principal fishes, listed in order of probable abundance, are:

1. Gizzard Shad (*Dorosoma cepedianum*)
2. [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
3. [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*)
4. Largemouth Black Bass (*Micropterus salmoides*)
5. [[Yellow Bass]] (*Morone interrupta*)

Earlier work on the fish community was published by Whitacre (1952), a master's thesis on the fishes of the lake at Southern Illinois University.

The gizzard shad is described as important both for its abundance and as a forage species, citing Lagler and Ricker (1943), Swingle (1949) and Stroud (1949). It occurs in all the larger streams and many lakes, and is especially abundant in Crab Orchard Lake. Of the abundant species there, the largemouth bass feeds most heavily on shad. The author expects that the gizzard shad will be recognized as one of the most important forage fishes for the largemouth bass and yellow pike perch as understanding of forage vulnerability and palatability improves.

**Sampling method:** The electric shocker was a 230-volt, 5060-watt, 180-cycle generator carried in a boat and connected to electrodes on a boom extending forward from the bow. The entire sample was collected at one time and is considered representative of the population. Other field observations of the population are also reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Forage role:** Shad are a major food source for predatory game fish. The sample was composed mainly of fish small enough for adult bass to swallow. The author notes that a two-pound bass could swallow a six-inch shad, and the great majority of the shad measured were below six inches.
- **Population structure:** Most of the population was under four years old. The maximum age recorded was five years, and very few fish were older than three.
- **Mass mortality:** In October 1952, sample strips of shoreline were counted, and about 10,250,000 dead shad were estimated along the shore. All size classes were affected. An electrical sample taken in October found three-inch shad very abundant but larger sizes scarce. The author notes that similar die-offs have occurred in this lake before and suggests that such events may control the age and size composition of the population.
- **Lake type:** Crab Orchard Lake is an artificial impoundment, which places this population within reservoir fish dynamics.

## Key Findings

**1. Sample and length-frequency structure**
- Sample: 888 gizzard shad, collected by electrofishing in August 1952.
- The length-frequency distribution ([[Length-Frequency Distribution]]; Fig. 1) has three modes at approximately 70 mm, 115 mm and 145 mm standard length.
- If all sizes were equally vulnerable to the gear, the modes correspond to age groups 0, I and II, which broadly agree with the scale-based ages.

**2. Age and growth**
- Maximum age: five years. Only a few specimens were older than three years.
- Average length at the end of years one, two and three: 4.0, 5.4 and 6.6 inches (total length), respectively.
- Table 1 gives mean standard lengths of 80, 108, 133, 135 and 129 mm for age groups 1 to 5. Mean total lengths were 4.0, 5.4, 6.6, 6.7 and 6.4 inches, and mean weights were 10, 23, 40, 41 and 36 grams. The annual increment row is partly illegible in the scanned text, so exact increments are not reported here.

**3. Condition**
- The [[Coefficient of Condition]], K, was 1.82, based on 888 specimens in the length range 51 to 216 mm.
- Calculations by length group showed no detectable variation of K with length.

**4. Length-weight relationship**
- A [[Length-Weight Relationship]] was fitted to the same 888 specimens (51 to 216 mm standard length), using the formula reported by Whitacre (1952). The scanned formula is partly garbled, so the coefficients are not reproduced here.

**5. Die-off and population control**
- Estimated 10,250,000 shad dead along the shoreline in October 1952, from counts of sample strips.
- The author recommends repeating length-frequency sampling in later years to test whether recurring [[Fish Die-Off]] events regulate population structure.

**6. Main conclusion**
- The majority of gizzard shad in Crab Orchard Lake were less than four years old and small enough to be eaten by adult bass. The species is therefore a key forage resource in this reservoir.
