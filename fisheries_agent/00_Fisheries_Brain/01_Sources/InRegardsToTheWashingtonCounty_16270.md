# Virgin River Woundfin Decline and the 86 cfs Instream Flow Dispute

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This advocacy letter, dated November 17, 1997 and sent by the Southwest Resource Council to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), argues that the Washington County Water Conservancy District (WCWCD) has continuously violated the 86 cubic feet per second (cfs) instream flow bypass required by the 1982 [[Quail Creek Reservoir]] Biological Opinion (B.O.). The letter contends that this violation is driving declines in endangered [[Woundfin]] and other native fishes in the Virgin River upper reach, and that the [[Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program]] (the "Program") risks legitimizing the violation by adopting it as baseline conditions. The authors request FWS enforcement, critical habitat designation, and retention of the 86 cfs requirement.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:** [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), listed as endangered; [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*), listed as endangered; and four other native fishes, including the [[Virgin River Spinedace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus spinosus*), considered possible candidates for listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Ecosystem context:** The Virgin River and its floodplain support over 366 wildlife species, 31 of which are classed as sensitive. The letter does not give full taxonomic detail for the other species.
- **Key infrastructure:** The [[Quail Creek Diversion]], completed in 1985, diverts mainstem Virgin River water into [[Quail Creek Reservoir]]. The diversion captures flows up to 240 cfs, while the river's average annual flow is given as 180 cfs.
- **Regulatory basis:** The 1982 B.O. issued by FWS found "no jeopardy" on the condition that a minimum flow of 86 cfs bypass the diversion point, unless natural flow is lower, to satisfy downstream water rights. Permit applications were submitted to the [[Bureau of Land Management]].
- **Organizations:** The WCWCD (operator), FWS, BLM, the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], and the Utah Department of Natural Resources (DNR).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat significance:** The upper reach, from La Verkin Creek to Quail Creek Reservoir, was once described as the woundfin's "least disturbed habitat" and is now proposed critical habitat.
- **Flow requirements:** The letter states that experts have consistently recommended 90 to 120 cfs as essential for fish survival. Maintenance of woundfin, chub, and other native fish populations is described as tied to instream flow regimes.
- **Hydrology and operations:** The WCWCD routes a small flow through the Pah Tempe powerplant in La Verkin, pipes water around to the reservoir, and releases it through another powerplant. Augmented flow is then said to reach the Washington Fields Diversion. Utah Division of Water Resources records are cited as showing regular violation of the 86 cfs bypass.
- **Historical precedent:** A 1975 [[Warner Valley Water Project]] with a similar operating plan was refused by FWS because of anticipated jeopardy to endangered fish.
- **Economic tension:** The WCWCD estimates that releasing one cfs at the diversion costs about one million dollars in lost hydropower revenue. Planned uses include filling the new Sand Hollow Reservoir and building a water treatment plant near Virgin.
- **Program scope:** The [[Red Cliffs Desert Reserve]] habitat conservation plan (HCP) excluded a 6-mile Virgin River stretch north of Hurricane containing endangered fish habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Continuous noncompliance:** The letter alleges that the WCWCD operates the Quail Creek Diversion without releasing 86 cfs into the upper reach. The WCWCD disputes that the requirement applies at that location.
- **Population declines:** Utah Division of Wildlife Resources monitoring reported 456 (spring) and 604 (fall) woundfin in 1994, falling to 43 (spring) and 27 (fall) in 1997 in the upper reach. Chub and other native fish show similarly alarming declines, according to the letter.
- **Agency findings:** The FWS questioned the diversion's operation in 1986. By 1991, the BLM and Utah Division of Wildlife Resources agreed that current operation, without the 86 cfs release, was adversely affecting endangered fish and habitat. No corrective action is reported.
- **Scientific attribution:** The letter cites Holden and Zucker (1996) and Gregory and Deacon (1994) as attributing upper-reach fish declines to diversion operation.
- **Program risk:** The authors argue that defining the violation as "existing conditions" would undermine the Program's purpose as a recovery document and turn it into a tool for future development.
- **Exclusion of environmentalists:** After about 15 months of requests, environmental organizations received an October 14, 1997 letter from DNR confirming they would not be included in baseline negotiations until internal talks concluded.
- **Requested actions:** The authors ask FWS to enforce the B.O. as written, designate critical habitat, take enforcement action, ensure the baseline includes an 86 cfs release, and verify that the flow is actually delivered.
