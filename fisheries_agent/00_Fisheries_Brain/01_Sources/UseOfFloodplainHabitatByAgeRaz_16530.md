## Executive Summary

This note (Modde, USFWS Colorado River Fishery Project) documents the first substantial evidence of age-0 [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) using inundated floodplain habitat as a nursery area in the Upper Colorado River Basin. Twenty-eight age-0 razorback sucker were collected from **Old Charley Wash**, a 60-ha managed wetland on the Ouray National Wildlife Refuge (Green River, river kilometer 402), during a controlled draining event in fall 1995. This finding is significant because prior to this study only 13 age-0 razorback sucker had ever been documented in the entire Upper Colorado River Basin, and all were captured in main-channel habitats rather than floodplains. The results support long-standing speculation that seasonally inundated floodplains serve as critical nursery habitat linked to recruitment success in this [[Endangered Species|endangered]] catostomid.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]] — a long-lived sucker endemic to the [[Colorado River Drainage]] (McCarthy and Minckley 1987).
- **Age-0 cohort (Old Charley Wash, fall 1995), n = 28:**
  - Total length: mean 94.4 mm (range 74–125 mm)
  - Weight: mean 9.5 g (range 3–18 g)
- **Historical age-0 comparisons (Upper Colorado River Basin, main-channel captures):**
  - Taba et al. (1965): 8 juveniles, 90–115 mm TL, Colorado River near Moab/Dead Horse Point, Utah (early 1960s)
  - Gutermuth et al. (1995): 2 juveniles, 37 mm and 39 mm, lower Green River main-channel backwater (1991)
  - Utah DWR (unpublished, R. Muth pers. comm.): 2 juveniles, 59 mm and 29 mm, Ouray NWR main-channel backwater (1993)
- **Adult razorback sucker captured in Old Charley Wash (n = 8):**
  - Total length: 461–525 mm
  - Weight: 1,034–1,650 g
- **Comparative growth rates elsewhere (contextual, not from this study site):**
  - Humphrey Pond, CO (Osmundson and Kaeding 1989): stocked at 55 mm (June 1987) → 307 mm / 333 g by mid-November; 404 mm / 920 g by September 1988.
  - Isolated bays, Lake Mohave (Mueller et al. 1993): growth exceeding 400 mm in first growing season.
  - Growth of Middle Green River fish is comparatively slower, attributed to shorter growing season at higher latitude/elevation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Life history context:** Razorback sucker larvae drift downstream from riverine spawning sites (Tyus 1987). Spawning occurs on the ascending limb of the hydrograph, temporally coinciding with peak runoff to maximize larval access to floodplain/wetland habitats (Tyus and Karp 1990).
- **Study site — Old Charley Wash:** A historical 60-ha diked wetland on the [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]], managed for waterfowl, located ~100 river km downstream of the Escalante spawning site on the Green River. Dominant vegetation: smartweed (*Polygonum sp.*) and sago pondweed (*Potamogeton pectinatus*).
- **Hydrology:** In spring 1995, Green River runoff peaked at ~595 m³/s; wetland dikes were inundated (beginning at ~481 m³/s flow) between May 23 and ~July 1, connecting the wetland to the river. Maximum wetland depth exceeded 2 m during peak flow. After runoff receded, the wetland became hydrologically isolated from the river, with no artificial water added through summer.
- **Fish access:** All water entering the wetland came from overbank inundation (inlet/outlet structures remained closed); fish access to the floodplain was therefore tied directly to river discharge exceeding a threshold flow.
- **Sampling effort:** Fyke nets (3), trammel nets (4), minnow traps (3), light traps (3, from 20 June), and seine sets (9) were used weekly during inundation and biweekly afterward through August — no age-0 razorback sucker were captured during this active sampling, despite their later recovery during draining. This suggests age-0 fish are poorly sampled by conventional gear even when present.
- **Capture via drainage:** Wetland drained September 25–October 12, 1995; fish concentrated and collected via seine in the channel between two outlet dikes.
- **Adult use of wetlands:** Presence of 8 adult razorback sucker in the wetland supports Tyus and Karp's (1990) hypothesis that adults move into floodplain wetlands following spawning during high-flow years — adults were previously documented near this wetland's outlet at 17–21°C (avg. 19°C), warmer than spawning temperatures of 9–17°C, consistent with post-spawn movement into warmer off-channel habitats.
- **Ambiguity of origin:** Because adults were also present in the wetland, it could not be determined whether the collected age-0 fish originated from river spawning bars (via larval drift into the floodplain) or were spawned directly within the wetland.
- **Threats to recruitment:** Broader literature cites nonnative fish predation/competition ([[Meffe 1985]], [[Marsh and Langhorst 1988]], [[Papoulias and Minckley 1990]]) and lack of suitable nursery habitat (Tyus and Karp 1990) as key recruitment bottlenecks for [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].

## Key Findings

- **Largest documented cohort:** The 28 age-0 razorback sucker collected represent the largest number of juveniles ever reported from the Upper Colorado River Basin, more than doubling the cumulative total (13) from all previous records.
- **First floodplain nursery evidence:** This is the first documented use of floodplain/wetland habitat by age-0 razorback sucker in the Upper Colorado River Basin; all prior juvenile records came from main-channel backwaters.
- **Sampling gear limitation:** Despite regular, gear-diverse sampling throughout spring and summer, no age-0 fish were captured until the wetland was fully drained, indicating age-0 razorback sucker are difficult to detect with conventional fish sampling methods and may be under-reported in general river surveys.
- **Supports floodplain nursery hypothesis:** Findings corroborate speculation by [[Behnke 1980]], [[Tyus and Karp 1990]], and [[Modde et al. 1995]] that inundated floodplains function as important nursery habitat, and align with [[Modde et al. (in press)]] findings correlating higher numbers of smaller adults in the Middle Green River population with prior high-flow years.
- **Growth rate implications:** Age-0 fish in this Green River floodplain population show slower growth (mean 94.4 mm by fall) compared to lower-latitude/lower-elevation populations (Lake Mohave, Humphrey Pond), consistent with a shorter growing season at higher latitude and elevation.
- **Management relevance:** The controlled outlet/drain structure engineered at Old Charley Wash (as part of the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin) proved effective for capturing fish during wetland drainage and could serve as a model for future floodplain-based recruitment monitoring and possibly fish salvage/augmentation efforts for [[Xyrauchen texanus]].
- **Unresolved question:** Whether age-0 fish were spawned within the wetland itself or drifted in as larvae from upstream riverine spawning bars remains undetermined, representing a key area for future research on [[Floodplain Habitat|floodplain nursery]] recruitment pathways.
