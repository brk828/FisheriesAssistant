# AESFO Activity Report: Topminnow and Pupfish Translocation to Three Arizona Sites

#ichthyology #marine-biology #topminnow-pupfish

## Executive Summary

This AESFO Activity Reporting System (ARS) submission, prepared by Doug Duncan, documents a fish stocking effort in Arizona carried out by [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD). A total of 150 [[Gila Topminnow]] (listed as "topminnow") and 150 [[Pupfish]] were moved from source populations and released into three sites to establish new habitat. The stated outcome is the establishment of three potential Tier 3 populations of topminnow and pupfish. The document is a brief activity summary and does not report monitoring data, survival rates, or post-release results.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** Topminnow and pupfish. The scan does not give scientific names for either, so taxonomic identity is listed as N/A in the frontmatter.
- **Topminnow source:** Fish were taken from [[Bylas Spring]] on the [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] lands. The tribe granted permission, and the [[Mexican Wolf Recovery Program]] is named as facilitating the transfer (MFRO appears in the text, and this expansion is inferred from context).
- **Pupfish source:** Fish were gathered from ponds on TNC property.
- **Transport:** Topminnow and pupfish were transported from San Carlos and ponds to release sites by helicopter provided by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. The document states this saved transport time and reduced the handling time that could stress the fish.
- **Regulatory basis:** A [[Safe Harbor Agreement]] with TNC was in place. A [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] allowed the fish to be released on BLM lands.
- **Partners:** TNC, BLM, AGFD, SCAIR, and the Bureau of Reclamation (BR). Other offices involved were MFRO and the Arizona ES Office (R2).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** The release sites are desert springs and ponds in Arizona. The document names [[Bleak Spring]] and [[Cement Spring]] (both on TNC land) and [[Parson Spring]] (on BLM land).
- **Ecosystem and county coverage:** The ecosystem and county checkboxes are partly illegible in the scan. The text does not reliably confirm which ecosystem or counties apply, so they are not listed here.
- **Program focus:** The Endangered Species program box is selected. Other program areas (Contaminants, Habitat Conservation, NEPA, Outreach) appear in the form, but the text describes no activity under them.
- **Handling and welfare:** Helicopter transport was chosen specifically to minimize capture and handling stress on the fish.

## Key Findings

- **Stocking totals:** 150 topminnow and 150 pupfish were released. The document says they were "evenly split" among the three sites, which implies about 50 of each species per site. Exact per-site counts are not given.
- **Release sites:** Bleak Spring and Cement Spring (TNC) and Parson Spring (BLM). The stockings were described as the first action taken toward the restoration goal.
- **Outcome claimed:** Establishment of three potential Tier 3 populations of topminnow and pupfish. This is a stated objective or potential result, not a confirmed population outcome.
- **Legal pathway:** Section 7 consultation cleared release on BLM lands. A Safe Harbor Agreement with TNC supported the work.
- **Logistics:** Helicopter transport from the Bureau of Reclamation shortened transit and reduced handling.
- **Dollar figures:** The field for total FWS dollars applied is blank in the scan. No cost data is reported.
