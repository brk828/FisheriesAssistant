## Executive Summary

This memorandum reports results of a fish population survey conducted on the Verde River at Perkinsville, Arizona (03–05 April 1986), using seines and trammel nets. The survey was part of an ongoing monitoring effort for reintroduced [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), a large-bodied, federally protected cyprinid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. A total of 378 fish representing 11 species were captured, including 3 recovered [[Colorado Squawfish]] that had been stocked in 1985 as part of a reintroduction program. Analysis of tetracycline marking confirmed the origin and time-at-large of the recovered specimens, providing valuable data on post-stocking survival for this imperiled species.

## Background & Description

Data specific to the 3 recovered *Ptychocheilus lucius* specimens:

| Specimen | Total Length (TL) | Standard Length (SL) | Weight | Sex |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------|--------|-----|
| 1 | 418 mm | 331 mm | 300 g | Male |
| 2 | 426 mm | 351 mm | 500 g | Male |
| 3 | 436 mm | 354 mm | 400 g | Female |

- All three specimens were dead upon retrieval from trammel nets.
- No food items were found in stomach or intestinal contents.
- No parasites were observed in digestive tracts.
- Digestive tracts were preserved separately; remaining carcasses were frozen for further analysis.
- Two of three specimens bore confirmed tetracycline marks, verifying their identity as part of a documented 29 October 1985 stocking cohort (N = 141).
- The third specimen was attributed to earlier 1985 stockings (August/September, part of a total N = 442), implying a time-at-large of 5–7 months.
- All specimens deposited at [[Arizona State University]] for archival/reference collection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Verde River assemblage sampled reflects a mixed native/non-native fish community typical of degraded [[Southwestern US]] warmwater streams:

**Small fishes (N = 257):**
- Dominated by non-native *[[Notropis Lutrensis]]* (42.0%)
- Native *[[Meda Fulgida]]* (23.3%)
- Native *[[Gila Robusta]]* (16.7%)
- Native *[[Catostomus Insignis]]* (10.1%)
- Native *[[Pantosteus Clarki]]* (4.3%)
- Native *[[Rhinichthys Osculus]]* (3.1%)
- Non-native *[[Ameiurus Natalis]]* (0.4%)
- Native species comprised 52% of the small-fish assemblage.

**Large fishes (N = 151):**
- Dominated by native *[[Gila Robusta]]* (51.0%)
- Native *[[Catostomus Insignis]]* (17.2%)
- Native *[[Pantosteus Clarki]]* (16.6%)
- Non-native *[[Ameiurus Natalis]]* (9.3%)
- Non-native *[[Ictalurus Punctatus]]* (2.0%)
- Non-native *[[Cyprinus Carpio]]* (1.3%)
- Non-native *[[Lepomis Cyanellus]]* (0.7%)
- Reintroduced *[[Colorado Squawfish]]* (2.0%, N = 3)
- Native species comprised approximately 85% of the large-fish assemblage.

The persistence of a relatively high proportion of native large-bodied fishes (85%) suggests the Verde River at this reach retains habitat conditions favorable for native [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]] species, despite non-native introductions such as *[[Cyprinus Carpio]]* and *[[Ictalurus Punctatus]]*. This makes the site a candidate location for continued [[Colorado Squawfish]] reintroduction efforts.

## Key Findings

- **Reintroduction monitoring success**: 3 of a stocked cohort were recovered during this survey, part of a cumulative total of 15 individuals recovered to date from 1985 stockings.
- **Tetracycline marking utility**: Confirmed as an effective method for tracking stocked fish origin and time-at-large; two specimens were positively matched to the 29 October 1985 stocking event.
- **Survival timeframe**: Recovered fish had survived in the wild for 5–7 months post-stocking, indicating short-to-medium-term post-release viability.
- **No evidence of feeding activity or parasitism** in examined digestive tracts, though sample size (n=3) limits broader conclusions about diet or health status of stocked individuals.
- **Community composition**: Native fishes remain dominant among large-bodied individuals (85%) despite non-native fish presence, suggesting some ecological resilience of the native assemblage at this Verde River site.
- **Non-native dominance in small size classes**: Non-native *[[Notropis Lutrensis]]* was the single most abundant species among small fishes, potentially indicating recruitment or competitive pressure concerns for native small-bodied cyprinids.
- All voucher specimens retained at [[Arizona State University]] supporting future taxonomic, health, or genetic analyses related to the [[Colorado Squawfish]] reintroduction program.
