# Cranial Osteology of the Clupeoid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, by W. G. Ridewood of St. Mary's Hospital Medical School, describes the cranial osteology (skull bones) of eleven clupeoid genera. It is the third in a series on lower teleostean fishes and is intended to test existing classification schemes and to support the study of fossil forms lying on the boundary between Teleosteans and Ganoids. The author concludes that the skull supports placing [[Engraulis Encrasicholus]] and [[Coilia Nasus]] in the Engraulidae and the remaining Clupeoid genera in the Clupeidae. [[Chanos Salmoneus]] is judged distinct enough to merit family rank, while [[Chirocentrus Dorab]] agrees closely with the Clupeidae in skull structure.

## Background & Description

The study originated with a suggestion from Prof. G. B. Howes in spring 1896. Progress was slow and intermittent. Earlier parts appeared in the *Proceedings of the Zoological Society* (1904) on [[Alopias]], [[Megalops]] and [[Albula]], and in the *Journal of the Linnean Society* (1904) on the Mormyridae, Notopteridae and Hyodontidae. A third paper covering the Osteoglossidae, Pantodontidae and Phractolemidae had been submitted to the Linnean Society.

The eleven genera examined are [[Chirocentrus Dorab]], [[Clupea Finta]] (with [[Clupea Harengus]], [[Clupea Sapidissima]] and [[Clupea Sprattus]]), [[Pellona Motius]], [[Pellonula Vorax]], [[Pristigaster Tartoor]], [[Hyperlophus Copii]], [[Chatoessus Erebi]], [[Dussumieria Acuta]], [[Engraulis Encrasicholus]], [[Coilia Nasus]] and [[Chanos Salmoneus]]. Skulls of six genera were specially prepared by the author by disarticulation. The remainder belong to the British Museum, whose staff, especially G. A. Boulenger, provided access.

Methods were largely descriptive and comparative. Each genus was described under standard headings: cranium, temporal and preopercular series, circumorbital series, maxillary series, mandibular series, hyopalatine series, opercular series and hyobranchial series. The text figures are cited by number, with abbreviations listed at the end. The paper also treats the post-temporal and preopercular as skull elements, a decision the author justifies on the basis of his earlier papers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The source is an osteological study and does not address habitat, feeding ecology, abundance or behaviour. Its ecological content is limited to specimen provenance and to structural features with possible functional or environmental links:

- **Swim-bladder vesicles:** Bony bullae in the pro-otic and squamosal bones house diverticula of the swim-bladder. These are a near-constant feature of the Clupeoids and are absent in [[Chanos Salmoneus]].
- **Accessory branchial organ:** The epibranchial organ, linked to the fourth epibranchial and associated skeletal parts, is discussed in the context of [[Clupea Harengus]], [[Engraulis Encrasicholus]] and [[Chanos Salmoneus]].
- **Jaw and gape specialisation:** The very large gape of [[Coilia Nasus]] and [[Engraulis Encrasicholus]] is linked to a backwardly displaced quadrate. The reduced gape of [[Chanos Salmoneus]] is linked to the forward displacement of the quadrate.
- **Sensory canals and fossae:** The pre-epiotic fossa, which the author compares to the lateral cranial foramen of Mormyroids and Notopterus, may relate to sensory or lateral-line structures.

## Key Findings

**Cranial features shared across the Clupeoids**

- The parietal bones are small and separated by the supraoccipital in most genera. In [[Coilia Nasus]] they nearly meet in front of it, and in [[Chanos Salmoneus]] they appear to meet over it because of fused commissural scales.
- A temporal foramen, usually oval, bounded by the frontal and parietal, is a special Clupeoid feature. It is reduced to a pin-point depression in Coilia and absent in Chanos. The author found no case where the postfrontal forms part of its border.
- A pre-epiotic fossa is present in most genera. It is obliterated in Coilia, largely filled by a bulla in [[Pristigaster Tartoor]] and [[Hyperlophus Copii]], and absent in Chanos.
- An auditory fenestra, bounded by the pro-otic, exoccipital and basioccipital, occurs in most genera but is absent in Chanos and Coilia.
- Only [[Chanos Salmoneus]] has a roofed posterior temporal groove.
- The orbitosphenoid and basisphenoid vary in size. Both are absent in Chanos.

**Specific findings**

- **[[Chirocentrus Dorab]]:** The premaxilla is firmly fused to the maxilla by fibrous tissue. This contrasts with the movable joint in Clupea. The mandible has eight to ten large curved teeth, four or five anchylosed to the bone. The first pharyngobranchial is ossified, and no spicular bone is present. Chirocentrus is the only genus lacking a spicular bone.
- **Clupea species:** In [[Clupea Finta]] the premaxilla bears twenty to thirty minute teeth, whereas [[Clupea Harengus]] has rarely more than three. The hyomandibular has two heads in Clupea finta and one broad head in Clupea harengus. The squamosal bulla is absent in [[Clupea Sprattus]].
- **[[Chatoessus Erebi]]:** The premaxilla alone bounds the upper gape. There is one surmaxilla. Teeth are absent, and the palatine is edentulous. Parasphenoidal wings are absent.
- **[[Coilia Nasus]]:** The maxilla is greatly prolonged behind the mandibular articulation. The author notes a similar but smaller prolongation in some [[Engraulis Encrasicholus]] species. Coilia has a single posterior head on the hyomandibular and eleven branchiostegal rays.
- **[[Engraulis Encrasicholus]]:** The mesethmoid projects well ahead of the vomer. The opisthotic is absent. The hyomandibular has two heads, and the subopercular is easily confused with the last branchiostegal ray.
- **[[Chanos Salmoneus]]:** Chanos has four branchiostegal rays, no teeth, no sesamoid articular in some views (one is present in the specimens), and no orbitosphenoid or basisphenoid. The quadrate is separated from the symplectic and metapterygoid. The author considers these degraded or specialised traits.
- **[[Dussumieria Acuta]]:** Thirteen branchiostegal rays are recorded here, though earlier authors gave fifteen. The premaxilla is unusually short. The hyomandibular has two heads.

**Classification conclusions**

- The author favours grouping [[Engraulis Encrasicholus]] and [[Coilia Nasus]] in the Engraulidae. Coilia's skull is a more specialised version of the Engraulid pattern, with a reduced temporal foramen, obliterated pre-epiotic fossa and loss of several features.
- Clupea, Pellona, Pellonula, Pristigaster, Hyperlophus, Chatoessus and Dussumieria are placed in the Clupeidae. The author notes that Chatoessus does not warrant a separate family on cranial grounds alone, though non-skeletal evidence from [[Gill]] and [[Jordan and Gilbert]] may justify it.
- [[Chirocentrus Dorab]] agrees so closely with the Clupeidae that the case for a separate Chirocentridae must rest on other organs, such as the spiral intestinal valve and pseudobranch.
- [[Chanos Salmoneus]] is judged to merit family rank, separate from both the Clupeidae and the [[Albulidae]]. The roofed posterior temporal groove and lateral temporal groove are features shared with the Albulidae. Absence of the orbitosphenoid, basisphenoid and other traits makes an Albulid affinity uncertain.
- The Chirocentrus skull resembles extinct Cretaceous forms such as Portheus, Ichthyodectes and Saurodon, which suggests it may be a survival of an ancient type. Its teeth, however, are not set in sockets as in the Saurodontidae.

**Nomenclatural notes**

- The author notes that the genus *Diplomystus* is preoccupied by a Siluroid fish, making the name invalid. The Chilean and Australian species placed in Diplomystus are assigned under [[Hyperlophus Copii]] following Ogilby's suggestion.
