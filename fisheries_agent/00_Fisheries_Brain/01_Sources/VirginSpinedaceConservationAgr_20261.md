# Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement: FY1996 Annual Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepidomeda

## Executive Summary

This draft annual assessment, prepared by Jane Perkins of the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR), reports on activities and progress under the [[Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement]] for [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) during fiscal year 1996. The Agreement has two objectives: reducing or eliminating significant threats, and enhancing or stabilizing occupied and historic habitat. It organizes work under four administrative actions: coordination, implementation of conservation schedules, funding, and assessing progress. The document concludes that all actions were on track and consistent with the Virgin Spinedace Conservation Strategy (VSCS), with many actions to be completed within the broader [[Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program]].

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Virgin Spinedace]], a native minnow-family fish of the Virgin River system. The document does not describe its morphology or life history.
- **Regulatory basis:** The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) identified three threats based on federal listing criteria under Section 4(a)(1) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (59 FR 25875). The threats are:
  1. Present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment of habitat or range.
  2. Predation, competition, and disease.
  3. Other natural or manmade factors affecting continued existence.
- **Agreement partners:** UDWR, Utah Department of Natural Resources, FWS, [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), and the [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] (WCWCD). [[Utah State University]] (USU) and the Nevada Division of Wildlife (NDOW) were also active.
- **Reporting structure:** Quarterly reports (Attachments A–D) organize actions by threat. Table 1 summarizes completed actions, and Table 2 lists conservation actions for calendar year 1997.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow management.** Population maintenance flows are central to the strategy. WCWCD provided and maintained 3 cfs below the [[Quail Creek Diversion]]. Plans to deliver 3 cfs into the [[Santa Clara River]] below Gunlock Reservoir remained in progress, pending a pipeline that WCWCD was still negotiating with the Shivwitz Indian tribe. No new diversions or depletions were reported during the year.

**Non-native species.** The main predation and competition concerns involve non-native fishes:
- [[Red Shiner]]: Chemically treated in March, August, and December 2024-style sequence for FY1996 (March and August in the quarterly reports; December in the annual summary). Treatment covered the mainstem Virgin River from the Washington Fields Diversion to the Johnson Diversion, including beaver ponds and side channels.
- [[Green Sunfish]]: Eradication planned for the third quarter was prevented by flow conditions and was rescheduled for FY1997 if conditions allow.
- [[Rainbow Trout]]: NDOW began selective removal in Beaver Dam Wash tributary waters in July using electrofishing, with three passes.

**Physical structures.** The [[Ft. Pearce Wash Fish Barrier]] was completed early in the year, then modified in spring with a new apron and wing walls to prevent erosion. It was monitored for stability before further red shiner work in that tributary.

**Habitat disturbance.** In July, a sinkhole developed in [[La Verkin Creek]], nearly depleting the reach below. Emergency bypass measures were taken. The Natural Resources Conservation Service prepared a ground-penetrating radar report, and BLM prepared a geological report. By September the sinkhole had filled and water was flowing through the channel.

**Regulatory reviews.** The Division of Water Resources and UDWR reviewed approximately 29 stream alteration applications, and FWS reviewed 23. Recommendations were incorporated where negative impacts were identified. Projects reviewed included the WCWCD aqueduct grouting, Zion Canyon Route 11 reconstruction, LaVerkin Creek bridge replacement, and a WCWCD pipeline crossing.

## Key Findings

- **Monitoring:** UDWR and USU conducted annual Virgin Spinedace monitoring in September and October at 12 standard sites using electroshocking. The document reports that "numerous spinedace were sampled," but gives no counts.
- **Sampling below Quail Creek Diversion:** Sampling occurred in March and August. In March, spinedace were collected for the first time since flows were re-established. In August, Virgin Spinedace and [[Virgin River Chub]] were both collected.
- **Red shiner eradication:** The March treatment required 30 personnel over 4 days. Native species, including [[Woundfin]], Virgin River chub, flannelmouth suckers, desert suckers, and Virgin Spinedace, were salvaged the day before and relocated below the treatment area. The August treatment used increased chemical concentrations in select mainstem areas. Two detoxification stations were established below the Johnson Diversion. Nearly all red shiners were believed eradicated, with follow-up planned for FY1997.
- **Reestablishment:** Spinedace reintroduction in Beaver Dam Wash was scheduled for late fall 1996 but postponed twice due to adverse weather. It is planned for FY1997.
- **Program development:** The Technical Advisory Committee met at least twice monthly from January through May and developed a preliminary impact model for the Virgin River and tributaries, under review by the Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team and outside scientists. A Drafting Team met from June through December to develop the Program Strategy and mitigation components.
- **Habitat research:** BLM began baseline riparian monitoring on the Santa Clara River below Gunlock Reservoir and the Virgin River near Grafton to guide recreation and livestock conflict resolution. USU began a study of instream flow recommendations for La Verkin Creek, with preliminary data collected in August.
- **Regulation:** NDOW proposed limiting sport fishing to artificial lures on Beaver Dam Wash and Schroeder Reservoir to reduce introductions of undesirable species. The proposal was pending approval.
- **Federal listing:** A withdrawal notice for the proposed listing and critical habitat was required under the Agreement and was published in the Federal Register on February 6, 1996.
- **Threat 3 actions:** No actions were taken to address "other natural or manmade factors" in any quarter.
- **Assessment:** All actions were judged on track. Many were expected to take multiple years and to be implemented through the Program, so no 1997 implementation actions are listed under the VSCS for protocols that are still in development.
- **1997 priorities:** Additional red shiner treatments, annual spinedace monitoring, response monitoring of the 3 cfs release below Quail Creek Diversion, reestablishment on Beaver Dam Wash, and maintenance of existing population maintenance flows.

**Document note:** The Quarterly Report for July–September is labeled "FY95" in its header but describes FY96 third-quarter activities. The annual summary also gives the red shiner treatment months as March, August, and December, while the quarterly reports list only March and August.
