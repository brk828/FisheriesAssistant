## Executive Summary

The authors reconstruct the pre-extension configuration of the southern Great Basin by restoring offset along major strike-slip faults ([[Garlock Fault]], [[Las Vegas Valley Shear Zone]], and [[Lake Mead Fault System]]). Treating the Sierra Nevada, Mojave Desert, Spring Mountains, and Colorado Plateau as rigid, unextended crustal blocks, they calculate a minimum of 65% crustal extension (140 km of pull-apart) between the southern Sierra Nevada and the Colorado Plateau during the Cenozoic. This is presented as an independent, conservative check on prior extension estimates for the Basin and Range province, which had ranged from 10% to over 100%.

## Core Structural/Tectonic Concepts

- **[[Garlock Fault]]**: Left-lateral strike-slip fault; offset estimated at 48–64 km based on correlation of the Independence dike swarm, Garlock Formation, and Layton Well thrust with the Granite Mountains thrust. Proposed to continue ~30 km east of its surface termination to a zero-offset point between the Kingston Range and Silurian Hills, implying >100% extension across a 115-km-wide terrane (Owlshead Mountains–Kingston Range).
- **[[Las Vegas Valley Shear Zone]]**: Right-lateral shear zone linked to an "oroflex" (large-scale bending, per Albers, 1967) that transitions eastward into discrete faulting. Total displacement estimated at 44–69 km, based on correlation of the Wheeler Pass thrust (Spring Mountains) with the Gass Peak thrust, plus bending-related offset.
- **[[Lake Mead Fault System]]**: Complex left-lateral fault system with ~65 km total displacement, supported by correlation of Frenchman Mountain with the South Virgin Mountains (matching Tertiary stratigraphy, Mesozoic formation pinchouts, and Gold Butte rapakivi granite breccia sheets) and a 40 km offset of the River Mountains stock relative to the northern Black Mountains volcano-plutonic assemblage.
- **Stable crustal blocks**: Sierra Nevada, Mojave Desert, Spring Mountains, and Colorado Plateau — treated as internally rigid/unextended, used as reference frames for reconstruction (Spring Mountains held fixed to the Mojave block).

## Regional/Tectonic Setting

- **Province**: [[Basin and Range Province]], southern Great Basin, at the latitude of Las Vegas, Nevada.
- **Tectonic regime**: Cenozoic intracontinental upper-crustal extension, bounded by transform-like strike-slip faults separating differentially extended domains.
- **Comparative context**: Crustal thickness contrasts between the thinned Basin and Range crust (20–35 km) and thicker Colorado Plateau/Sierran crust (40–50 km) invoked as corroborating evidence for large-magnitude extension.
- **Analog systems**: Modern Andean crustal configuration (thickening from the Brazilian Shield into the back-arc thrust belt) proposed as an analog for the pre-extension Moho geometry following the Mesozoic Sevier orogeny.
- **Related low-angle normal fault terranes**: Death Valley region, Silurian Hills, Kingston Range, and northern Basin and Range — cited as showing similar highly extended structural styles (younger-over-older low-angle normal faulting).

## Key Findings

1. Minimum reconstructed extension between the southern Sierra Nevada and Colorado Plateau: **140 km (65% increase in original width)**.
2. This estimate is a **conservative minimum**; actual extension may be in the range of **80–100%**, given unaccounted-for extension in areas like the Mojave block, northern Spring Mountains, and between the South Virgin Mountains and Colorado Plateau.
3. Individual fault offsets: Garlock Fault ≈ 48–64 km; Las Vegas Valley Shear Zone ≈ 44–69 km; Lake Mead Fault System ≈ 65 km (with a 40 km sub-strand estimate).
4. No evidence of crustal shortening was found anywhere south of the Garlock Fault during the extension event, supporting the pull-apart interpretation.
5. The findings support earlier hypotheses (Hamilton and Myers, 1966) of roughly **twofold (2×) crustal extension** across the Basin and Range province, consistent with geophysical models (McKenzie, 1978) showing that continental lithosphere can extend by a factor of two without generating oceanic crust.
6. By extension of this logic, the northern Basin and Range province — currently thinner-crusted and structurally similar — may have undergone **60–80% (300–500 km) extension**.
7. The study reinforces the interpretation of regional low-angle normal fault systems as **large, rooted detachment complexes** (cf. [[Wernicke 1981]] nappe tectonics model), analogous in geometry (but not origin) to thin-skinned thrust belts.
