# Colorado River Recovery Program: NASRF Native Fish Culture, FY 2004 Annual Project Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers Project 29d, the operation and maintenance of the [[J.W. Mumma Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility]] (NASRF) in Alamosa, Colorado. The facility rears endangered [[Bonytail]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] for stocking in the Upper Colorado River Basin, and also holds [[Razorback Sucker]]. Fish are also grown out at the [[Trinidad State Junior College]] (TSJC) ponds. In FY 2004 the facility stocked 13,400 Bonytail and 4,578 Colorado Pikeminnow (the text gives 4,578; the stocking table gives 4,579). Disease, predation and budget shortfalls were the main problems. The project is ongoing, and the report was signed by Principal Investigator K. David Schnoor on 22 October 2004.

## Background & Description

**Project and Partners**
- Lead agency: [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]].
- Principal Investigators: David Schnoor and Jenn Logan (Alamosa, CO).
- Status: ongoing fish culture.

**Program Linkage (Recovery Action Plans)**
The project supports several recovery actions under the [[Recovery Action Plan]] framework:
- **General Support Action Plan:** Operate and maintain facilities (IV.C.4, Mumma facility) and manage genetic integrity and augment or restore populations.
- **[[Green River]] Mainstem:** Develop and implement a state stocking plan for four endangered fishes.
- **[[Yampa River]] and Little Snake River:** Augment populations in the Yampa River, including [[Dinosaur National Monument]] reaches, under a state stocking plan for four endangered fishes.
- **[[Colorado River]] Mainstem:** Stocking plans for Colorado Pikeminnow and Bonytail.
- **[[Gunnison River]]:** Stocking plan for Colorado Pikeminnow.

**Fish Sources**
Fry were received from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, NM):
- 10,000 Bonytail fry on 5/05/04.
- 10,000 Colorado Pikeminnow fry on 6/03/04.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Rearing Conditions and Infrastructure**
- NASRF ponds hold Bonytail and Pikeminnow at different sizes (for example, Bonytail at about 120/lb and Pikeminnow at 160/lb).
- Improvements at TSJC in FY 2004 included electricity added to ponds 1, 2 and 3, two paddle wheel aerators, four 8-foot round tanks, and bird netting over ponds 1–3 to prevent [[Pelican Predation]].

**Disease and Pest Management**
- In November 2003 the remaining TSJC Bonytail were hit by [[Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis]] (Ich). Only 151 survived, and they were moved to three 8-foot tanks. They were stocked into the Colorado River #1 site on 5/24/2004.
- A second Ich outbreak killed fish from the 2001 and 2002 year classes. The text reads "1509" (2001) and "900" (2002), but the scan is partly illegible, so these counts are uncertain.
- No major disease problems were recorded at the TSJC ponds in 2004.
- Excluding [[Tilapia]] from the ponds worked well. Tilapia were thought to be the main pathogen vector in the previous year.

**Fish Losses**
Losses during the year were attributed to handling during moving, lethal sampling for fish health inspection, and predation.

**Production Milestones**
- The 2003 Bonytail that do not reach 200 mm by spring 2005 will be moved to TSJC ponds for grow-out.
- The Colorado Pikeminnow were placed in pond 3 in late June 2004 and are doing well. They will overwinter there.
- No Bonytail will be overwintered at TSJC this year.
