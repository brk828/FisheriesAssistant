# BIO/WEST Humpback Chub Studies Contract Modification Proposals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #population-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document contains three technical proposals submitted by [[BIO/WEST]] to modify Contract No. 0-CS-40-09110 for [[Humpback Chub]] studies in the [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]]. The proposals, dated December 8, 1993, request additional funding totaling $270,983.63 to complete: (1) a comprehensive Final Report on [[Gila cypha]] life history and ecology ($169,613.35), (2) development of a [[Grand Canyon]] Fisheries Integrated Database ($72,614.52), and (3) creation of a [[Humpback Chub]] population model ($28,755.76). The work addresses data from a 40-month study (September 1990-December 1993) conducted under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program to assess impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on endangered fish populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is an endangered cyprinid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. [[BIO/WEST]] conducted intensive field studies from September 1990 through December 1993 under contract to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES). The investigation employed multiple techniques including small research boats, radiotelemetry, habitat mapping, electrofishing, and net sampling throughout [[Grand Canyon]]. The project generated unexpectedly large volumes of data due to successful access to previously unsampled habitats (up to 600 net sets and 100 electrofishing runs per 20-day trip) and extensive radiotelemetry monitoring (24-72 hour tracking periods plus continuous remote station recordings). Senior staff included Principal Investigator Richard A. Valdez, Project Leaders Bill Masslich and Bill Leibfried, and multiple senior biologists, data analysts, and specialists.

Historic [[Humpback Chub]] collections catalogued by C.O. Minckley include work by McDonald and Dotson (1960), Stone and Queenan (1967), Miller and Smith (1972), Holden and Stalnaker (1975), Minckley and Blinn (1976), Carothers and Minckley (1981), Kaeding and Zimmerman (1983), and Maddux et al. (1987). Concurrent studies by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Arizona State University]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[University of Arizona]] provided additional data requiring integration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]] represents critical habitat for [[Humpback Chub]], with the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) confluence serving as a primary spawning and nursery area. Operations of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] alter natural flow regimes, water temperature, sediment transport, and habitat availability, potentially affecting all life stages of this endangered species. The conceptual population model identifies four geographic components: (1) [[Little Colorado River]], (2) LCR reach of [[Colorado River]], (3) [[Colorado River]] below LCR reach, and (4) lower tributaries. Population dynamics depend on survival rates, fecundity rates, and movement (immigration) rates among these sectors across multiple age classes.

The study area encompasses the entire [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]], with intensive sampling in specific study reaches. Habitat types sampled include mainstem river channels, tributary mouths, backwaters, eddies, and shoreline areas. Water quality parameters, flow conditions, and habitat characteristics were documented concurrently with fish sampling. The database includes spatial data referenced to multiple accuracy levels, from survey-grade precision (<5 cm) to historic records (>1500 m resolution), enabling integration with Geographic Information Systems (GIS).

## Key Findings

**Final Report Requirements:** The 40-month study generated data volumes exceeding original projections, requiring 660 hours from the Principal Investigator, 350-540 hours from Project Leaders, and substantial time from senior biologists, data analysts, and specialists. The Final Report schedule spans four phases: Phase I (data assimilation and quality control, October 1993-February 1994), Phase II (preliminary draft, December 1993-April 15, 1994), Phase III (agency review draft, April 15-August 1, 1994), and Phase IV (final report incorporating comments, September 15-October 15, 1994). Total additional funding requested: $169,613.35 beyond the original $120,762.00 budget.

**Database Integration Needs:** The [[Grand Canyon]] Fisheries Integrated Database (GCFIN) project proposes to identify, catalogue, and document all fisheries data from [[Colorado River]] and tributaries in [[Grand Canyon]], emphasizing native species. Phase I will procure documented database structures and sample datasets from all researchers, catalogue according to Metadata Format, and identify common data fields and codes. Phase II will assist GCES in developing prototype database and integration templates. Phase III will identify GIS informational layers using [[BIO/WEST]] data as prototype. Six levels of spatial accuracy are proposed: Level 1 (survey-referenced, <5 cm precision), Level 2 (1:2400 orthophotos, 5 cm-1 m), Level 3 (1:1200 aerial photos, 1-5 m), Level 4 (1:24,000 quad sheets, 5-75 m), Level 5 (river mile reference, 75-1500 m), and Level 6 (historic records, >1500 m). Total cost: $72,614.52.

**Population Modeling Framework:** Phase II of the population modeling effort will refine the conceptual model through researcher consensus (Task 1, $13,332.00), develop an age-structured model in FORTRAN with modular structure (Task 2, $8,194.56), and evaluate present data to estimate population size, survival, reproduction, and movement by age group and population sector (Task 3, $7,229.20). The conceptual model incorporates state variables (abundance in age groups) and rate variables (survival, fecundity, movement) across four geographic components. Total modeling cost: $28,755.76. Future phases will conduct sensitivity analysis, interpret monitoring data, and perform population viability analysis.

**Data Collection Success:** Successful deployment of small maneuverable research boats enabled access to previously unsampled areas with greater frequency. Radiotelemetry generated large independent datasets from individual fish monitoring and continuous remote stations. More [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fishes were captured in the mainstem than anticipated, increasing linked information databases including meristics files, length-weight files, tag records, habitat associations, and movement patterns. Ongoing database maintenance and quality control throughout the project positioned the team to fully utilize all collected data.

**Coordination Requirements:** Development and completion of all three projects requires close coordination with GCES staff (Dave Wegner, Mike Yard, Allen Haden) to ensure compliance with project objectives, database compatibility with other researchers, consistency in report format and technical content, and integration into [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s Integrated Report on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. The population modeling program must link information from multiple fishery scientists and remain consistent with GCES program objectives and direction.
