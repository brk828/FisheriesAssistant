# Fish Remains from Stoneman Lake Sediment Core (Holocene Paleorecord)

## Executive Summary
This paper is primarily a Holocene paleoclimatic and paleolimnological reconstruction of [[Stoneman Lake]], Arizona, based on sediment stratigraphy, diatoms, pollen, and macrofossils. Embedded within this broader paleoenvironmental record is a discrete but significant ichthyological finding: a cluster of fish scales and bones recovered from the ~1500 yr B.P. sediment horizon. These remains provide rare direct evidence of fish presence in a fluctuating, closed-basin caldera lake on the southern [[Colorado Plateau]], and are tentatively attributed to the native [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), a species historically widespread across Arizona and the Colorado Plateau drainage but now imperiled. The fish evidence is interpreted within the context of a lake environment that had only recently (by ~2000 yr B.P.) transitioned from a shallow marsh to a deeper, more stable, fish-supporting lake following the hot, arid Middle Holocene.

## Background & Description
- **Scales**: Relatively large (3–4 mm), interpreted as indicative of fish that reached a substantial body size (estimated standard/total length ~40–50 cm).
- **Scale surface**: No ctenii (small comb-like projections) present. Ctenoid scales would indicate membership in the [[Percidae]] (perch) or [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfish) families — both recently introduced to Arizona and therefore excluded as sources.
- **Scale morphology**: General form is consistent with cycloid scales typical of native [[Cyprinidae]] (minnow) or [[Catostomidae]] (sucker) family members, though a definitive species-level identification from scales alone was not possible.
- **Skull fragment**: One bone fragment identified as part of a skull bone exhibiting an extensive "honeycomb" network of internal holes/cavities.
- **Diagnostic identification**: This honeycombed skull fragment compares favorably with the **orbito-sphenoid bone** of *Gila robusta* (roundtail chub), per personal communication with ichthyologist Steven James (1994).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Depositional context**: Fish scales and bones were clustered specifically at the 1500 yr B.P. sediment level, coinciding with a period the author interprets as marking the establishment of stable, fish-suitable lake habitat.
- **Habitat inference**: The large scale size is interpreted as evidence of a **stable lake environment** — sufficient in depth, permanence, and water quality — that allowed fish to grow to a relatively large size, implying multi-year habitat continuity rather than an ephemeral or marsh-dominated system.
- **Broader lake history**: Prior to ~2000 yr B.P., Stoneman Lake existed largely as a shallow, alkaline, marsh-like environment (dominated by *Typha latifolia*, Cyperaceae, and *Potamogeton*) unlikely to have sustained fish populations. The shift toward increased effective precipitation, greater sediment/nutrient influx, freshening water chemistry, and expanded open-water habitat after 2000 yr B.P. set the ecological stage for fish colonization or population establishment by 1500 yr B.P.
- **Regional distribution**: Historically, *Gila robusta* was widely distributed across Arizona ([[Minckley 1973]]) and throughout the Colorado Plateau ([[Joseph et al. 1977]]), making its presence in an isolated caldera lake biogeographically plausible, whether via original stocking, spring-fed connectivity, or human introduction (e.g., by indigenous peoples).
- **Co-occurring aquatic fauna**: Fish remains occur alongside mollusk fauna, notably snail shells of the genus *Gyraulus*, common on muddy lake bottoms with submersed plants, and abundant insect macrofossils — collectively indicating a productive, vegetated freshwater habitat.

## Key Findings
- Fish remains (scales + bones, including a diagnostic skull fragment) were recovered from a single concentrated horizon at **~1500 yr B.P.**, making this one of the few direct fish occurrence records from a Holocene Colorado Plateau lake sediment core.
- The **absence of ctenii** rules out perch and sunfish families (both non-native, recently introduced taxa), supporting a **native fish origin** for these remains.
- The tentative identification as **[[Gila robusta]]** (roundtail chub) — based on orbito-sphenoid bone morphology — links this isolated caldera lake to the broader native fish fauna of the Colorado Plateau and Arizona drainages.
- Large scale size (3–4 mm) suggests fish attained sizes of **40–50 cm**, implying a **stable, multi-year lentic habitat** rather than a transient or highly fluctuating water body at this point in the lake's history.
- The timing of fish presence (~1500 yr B.P.) postdates the hot, arid Middle Holocene (8000–2000 yr B.P.) marsh phase and aligns with the broader paleoenvironmental transition toward a **deeper, fresher, more permanent lake system** beginning ca. 2000 yr B.P.
- This fish record is a rare direct biotic proxy — complementing diatom, pollen, and mollusk data — for reconstructing the aquatic habitat quality and hydrological stability of [[Stoneman Lake]] through the Holocene, and offers indirect paleobiogeographic evidence for the historical range of native Arizona [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]].
