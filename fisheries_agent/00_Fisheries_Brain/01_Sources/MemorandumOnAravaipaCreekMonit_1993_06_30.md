## Executive Summary

This 1993 Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD) memorandum summarizes fish and habitat monitoring data collected from **[[Aravaipa Creek]]**, a perennial desert stream in Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona, during May 19–21, 1993. Nine sampling stations were established across three zones: three sites upstream of the **[[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]]** ("east end"), three within the Wilderness Area, and three downstream ("west end"). Sampling combined seining (1/8-inch and 1/4-inch mesh) and backpack electroshocking to quantify native and non-native fish assemblages. Findings confirm a robust native fish community — including **[[Loach Minnow]]**, **[[Longfin Dace]]**, **[[Desert Sucker]]**, **[[Roundtail Chub]]**, and **[[Spikedace]]** — with active breeding condition observed in multiple species, and the first documented detection of a non-native species, **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** (*Ameiurus natalis*), at two downstream sites not previously recorded in March 1993.

## Background & Description

**[[Aravaipa Creek]]** is a spring-fed perennial tributary within the Gila River Basin, renowned for supporting one of the most intact native fish assemblages in the American Southwest. The creek flows through **[[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]]**, managed jointly by the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) and The Nature Conservancy (TNC), which commissioned this monitoring effort in collaboration with AGFD's Native Fish Field Projects program.

Sampling methodology:
- **Mainstream channels**: seined over 200m reaches using a 1/8-inch mesh, 6-foot seine (supplemented by a 1/4-inch mesh, 15-foot seine when necessary).
- **Side channels/margins**: sampled with 1/8-inch mesh, 6-foot seine.
- **Electroshocking**: backpack shockers (150–250 volts, 75–150 pulses/second) applied to the lower 100m of each reach, with dipnets and 1/4-inch seines used to capture stunned fish.
- Catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) metrics were standardized as **Sn** (fish per 10 m² seined) and **Sk** (fish per minute of shocking time).

Habitat composition across stations consisted primarily of run, riffle, and pool sequences with substrates dominated by sand, gravel, cobble, and pebble, with lesser contributions from boulder, rubble, bedrock, and silt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water conditions**: Slightly turbid water; temperatures ranged 17–28°C across the sampling window.
- **Weather**: Mostly clear skies, calm winds, warm mornings transitioning to hot afternoons.
- **Post-flood recovery**: Effects of recent flooding remained evident, but riparian and aquatic vegetation showed increased abundance compared to prior surveys. Woody debris and root wads continued to provide significant in-stream cover.
- **Land use pressure**: Heavy cattle use was documented at east end and west end sites; only minor cattle sign was noted within the Wilderness Area's three interior sites, suggesting the WA boundary provides some grazing protection.
- **Wildlife observations**: Common black hawks (one per stream section), canyon tree frogs (2, within WA), and tracks of coatimundi, deer, and javelina were recorded — indicating a healthy riparian corridor supporting broader vertebrate biodiversity.

## Key Findings

- **Native fish dominance**: Native species — **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*), **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*), **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*), **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*), **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*), and **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) — were found at all nine sites in **greater proportion than in previous monitoring trips**, indicating a positive trend for the native assemblage.
- **Active breeding condition**: Reproductive activity was directly observed in *Tiaroga cobitis*, *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Pantosteus clarki*, *Gila robusta*, and *Meda fulgida*. Several large *Pantosteus* and *Gila* individuals were "running ripe," and some adult *Agosia* and *Gila* displayed tuberculation — both indicators of spawning readiness/activity.
- **Non-native species detection**: Two **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** (*Ameiurus natalis*) were captured — one at the Sycamore Tree site and one at Wagner Ranch (both west end/downstream sites). Critically, these individuals were **not present during the prior March 1993 survey**, suggesting a recent colonization or immigration event of this non-native species into the lower reaches of Aravaipa Creek.
- **Catch magnitude variability**: Total catch varied substantially by station, from 65 fish at Painted Cave (WA) to 1,534 fish at Chimney Rock (east end), likely reflecting differences in habitat complexity, flow, and sampling effort (number of seine hauls).
- **Seine haul efficiency**: The proportion of empty seine hauls was highest within the Wilderness Area stations (37.5–58.5%) compared to the east end (15.3–27.6%) and was variable at the west end (11.6–55.2%), potentially reflecting differences in fish density, habitat patchiness, or sampling conditions between zones.
- **Habitat structure**: All nine stations exhibited a mix of run, riffle, and pool mesohabitats with substrate dominated by sand and gravel, punctuated by cobble, pebble, and occasional boulder/bedrock — consistent with a dynamic, flood-influenced desert stream channel.
- **Flow range**: Discharge across stations ranged from 1.5 to 3.0 cms, reflecting natural spatial variability along the creek's course from upstream to downstream reaches.
