# Fishes of Springs and Spring-Fed Creeks of Calhoun County, Alabama

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Southeastern Fishes Council survey documents the fish fauna of springs and spring-fed creeks in [[Calhoun County]], Alabama. Sampling at 83 stations (May 1986 to February 1987, plus earlier collections from the [[Fort McClellan Military Reservation]]) yielded 45 species and 2 hybrids in 24 genera and 11 families. The study confirmed new populations of two rare spring specialists, documented range extensions for several species, and recorded one undescribed darter. The authors conclude that the county's spring ecosystems are diverse but vulnerable, and call for a protection and conservation plan.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** Calhoun County is drained by the [[Coosa River]] and its tributaries. Most of the county lies in the [[Valley and Ridge Province]] of northeastern Alabama, while a narrow strip along the Calhoun-Cleburne line lies on the Ashland Plateau of the Piedmont Province. The terrain is mountainous, in the foothills of the Appalachians. The county's springs range from small seepages to large thrust-fault springs. The largest is [[Coldwater Spring]], with a minimum daily discharge of 32 million gallons (Warman and Causey, 1962).

**Rationale.** Few previous collections had been made in the county's springs. Population growth and environmental pressure prompted the authors to document the ichthyofauna before further degradation.

**Methods.**
- 71 collections were made by the authors from 20 May 1986 to 7 February 1987.
- 12 additional collections were made by M.F. Mettee and P.E. O'Neil from 24 May to 11 October 1979 on the Fort McClellan Military Reservation, a restricted-access area.
- All collections used 4 or 6 ft minnow seines. Specimens were preserved in 10% formalin and identified in the laboratory.
- Fish taxonomy follows the family sequence of Greenwood et al. (1966).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spring-restricted fishes.** Four species appeared restricted to springs or spring tributaries in the study area: [[Pygmy Sculpin]], [[Flame Chub]], [[Coldwater Darter]], and the blacknose dace (*Rhinichthys atratulus*). The dace is not restricted to springs north of the study area. By number of stations, the spring-restricted species ranked: *R. atratulus* (12), *E. ditrema* (4), *H. flammea* (2), and *C. pygmaeus* (1).
- **Community patterns.** The average was 5.3 species per station and the modal count was 3. Station 73 held 16 species, the highest count. Three springs (Stations 28, 35, and 45) yielded no fishes.
- **Most widespread species.** *Semotilus atromaculatus* (43 stations), *Gambusia affinis* (38), *Cottus carolinae* (33), *Campostoma oligolepis* (31), and *Lepomis macrochirus* (26). Ten species occurred at 15 or more stations.
- **Pollution effects.** An unnamed creek below the Coldwater Spring effluent was formerly polluted, and it now appears pollution-free. The authors believe its warmer, polluted water stopped the downstream spread of the pygmy sculpin, which was not found beyond about 100 m of the spring run.
- **Human influence.** The authors suggest that species such as *Etheostoma trisella* and *Cottus pygmaeus* may have been exterminated or reduced in the county by human activity.

## Key Findings

**Species inventory.** The survey recorded 45 species and 2 hybrids. Notable records include:
- **Hybrids:** a single *Campostoma oligolepis* × *Luxilus chrysocephalus* specimen at Station 1, which the authors judged a natural intergeneric hybrid (Sizemore and Howell, 1987). A *Lepomis cyanellus* × *L. megalotis* hybrid was taken at Station 23.
- **Introductions:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) were stocked in Willett Spring for angling (Station 72).

**Pygmy sculpin (*Cottus pygmaeus*).**
- Taken only at Station 30, [[Coldwater Spring]], the type locality. About 30 specimens were captured and released on 26 September 1986.
- Several physically similar springs were sampled without success, and other workers had also failed to find additional populations in the Valley and Ridge Province.

**Flame chub (*Hemitremia flammea*).**
- Previously known in Alabama from springs in the Tennessee River system and one spring tributary of Kelly Creek near Talladega.
- Two new populations were found, about 24 miles apart: [[Blue Eye Spring]] (Station 3A), which supported a healthy population, and [[Joseph Spring]] (Station 6), which yielded only one specimen.

**Coldwater darter (*Etheostoma ditrema*).**
- Historically present in Martin Spring (now inundated by Weiss Lake) and Coldwater Spring. Thorough sampling of the Coldwater Spring effluent found only three specimens, and one more was found about 400 m downstream.
- Two new localities were located: Smart Spring (Station 40) and Todd Spring (Station 41). The total number of known Alabama populations is now five, including Glencoe Spring and Waxahatchee Creek.
- The species was classed as threatened by Deacon et al. (1979) and as a species of special concern by Ramsey et al. (1986).

**Weed shiner (*Notropis texanus*).**
- One specimen was taken from a large sandy-bottomed spring run at Station 50. This is a significant range extension above the [[Fall Line]], where the species is exceedingly rare.

**Orangespotted sunfish (*Lepomis humilis*).**
- A single specimen at Station 22. This species does not naturally occur in the Coosa River system, so the authors attribute the record most likely to pond stocking.

**Undescribed darter.**
- The "upland snubnose" darter, *Etheostoma* sp., was the only undescribed species collected. Thirteen specimens came from three large spring tributaries in the Talladega National Forest (Stations 55, 58, and 59).

**Conservation conclusions.**
- Calhoun County supports a diverse, partly abundant assemblage of spring fishes, and the survey is described as a thorough sampling of the spring habitat.
- The authors call for a practical protection and conservation plan to safeguard the fragile spring ecosystems and the species they support.
