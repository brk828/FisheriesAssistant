# Artificial Keys for the Identification of the Fishes of the State of Ohio

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Percidae #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This is the 1950 revision (earlier revisions 1946; first edition mimeographed 30 March 1940) of Milton B. Trautman's mimeographed dichotomous keys to the native fishes of Ohio, issued by the [[Franz Theodore Stone Institute of Hydrobiology]] (Put-in-Bay) and the [[Ohio Division of Wildlife]]. It was reissued because copies were unavailable, demand was constant, and an illustrated key would not appear until 1952. The work contains a key to families (27 numbered families) plus genus/species/subspecies keys in four sections, each entry adding a brief statement of Ohio distribution and habitat.

## Background & Description
- **Audience:** fisheries technicians; technical terms are unavoidable. Definitions are referred to the glossary in Forbes and Richardson's *The Fishes of Illinois* (1920).
- **Use:** key to family first, then use the family number in the genus/species key.
- **Organization:**
  - Section I: [[Lampreys]], ganoids and soft-rayed fishes through the suckers.
  - Section II: the minnows ([[Cyprinidae]]).
  - Section III: remaining soft-rayed fishes and spiny-rayed fishes, including darters.
  - Section IV: sunfish family and remaining spiny-rayed groups.
- **Sources compiled:** Bailey (1938), Forbes & Richardson (1920), Gerking (1945), Hubbs (1926, 1930), Hubbs & Bailey (1938), Hubbs & Cooper (1936), Hubbs & Lagler (1939), Hubbs & Trautman (1937), Jordan (1929), Jordan & Evermann (1896-1900), Koelz (1929), and several Trautman papers (e.g., *Parexoglossum hubbsi*, 1931; *Notropis volucellus wickliffi*, 1931; Poecilichthys coeruleus vs. spectabilis, 1930), plus Wickliff & Trautman (1939).
- **Key characters used:** mouth and jaw form, caudal fin type, dorsal/anal ray and spine counts, lateral-line scale counts, barbels, pharyngeal teeth, peritoneum color, gut length, premaxilla protractility, gill-membrane union, and breeding coloration/tubercles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Drainages:** Ohio fishes are repeatedly assigned to the [[Lake Erie]] drainage, the [[Ohio River]] drainage, or both. Specific systems named include the Maumee, Scioto, Muskingum, Mad River, Great Miami, Little Miami, Mahoning, Cuyahoga and Black/Rocky rivers, as well as [[Sandusky Bay]], the Bass Islands and Portage Lakes.
- **Glaciation:** the "line of glaciation" is a recurring boundary; many species (e.g., Cottus bairdii, Poecilichthys variatus, P. zonalis, Eucalia inconstans) are concentrated along it, while others favor unglaciated southern/southeastern Ohio.
- **Habitat associations:** riffles of gravel/boulder streams (darters, redhorses, Rockbass), weedy prairie streams and sloughs (Pug-nosed Minnow, Black-chinned Shiner, Mud Pickerel), deep channels of large rivers (Ohio Channel Chub, Blue Sucker), and cold clear brooks (Brook Trout, Northern Common Shiner subspecies *frontalis*, Brook Stickleback).
- **Environmental change:** several entries note declines tied to turbidity, silting and impoundment (Sand Darter, Big-eyed Shiner, Red-bellied Dace, Crystal Darter, Brook Trout/Brook Stickleback-type clear-water forms), and loss of vegetation and stumps in reservoirs (Yellow Bullhead).
- **Introduced/invading species:** [[Sea Lamprey]] (recent Lake Erie invader), Alewife, American Smelt, Brown and Rainbow Trout, Big-mouthed Buffalofish in Sandusky Bay, [[Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] (hybridize freely, outnumbering both parents locally in western Lake Erie), Red-eared Sunfish (introduced 1931 and 1935, increased "amazingly" in Buckeye Lake), American Eel (introductions >40 years earlier), Eastern Banded Killifish, and Sucker-mouthed Minnow (apparent recent invader increasing rapidly).

## Key Findings
- **Scope:** about 27 family accounts; the family key separates lampreys (jawless, sucking disc, 7 gill openings) from jawed fishes, then heterocercal ([[Paddlefish]], [[Lake Sturgeon]]/Rock Sturgeon, [[Gars]], [[Bowfin]]) from homocercal forms, and soft-rayed from spiny-rayed groups.
- **Lampreys:** seven species/forms, including Northern Lamprey (*Ichthyomyzon unicuspis*), Ohio Lamprey (*I. bdellium*), Northern and Alleghany Brook Lampreys, Sea Lamprey (*Petromyzon marinus*), American Brook Lamprey (*Entosphenus lamottenii*) and Ohio Brook Lamprey (*Lampetra aepyptera*); parasitic vs. non-parasitic forms are separated by size (over/under 8 inches) and myomere counts.
- **Gars:** Long-nosed, Short-nosed and Spotted Gars; the Alligator Gar (up to 10 feet) is known from two specimens near Cincinnati, not recorded since 1838.
- **Salmonids and coregonids:** Brown Trout introduction successful only in Cold Creek; Rainbow Trout introduced widely; Lake Trout rare in Lake Erie; Brook Trout in a few headwater tributaries; Blue-backed Cisco, Erie Cisco and Erie Whitefish in Lake Erie.
- **Suckers ([[Catostomidae]]):** key covers Blue Sucker, buffalofishes (Bigmouth, Black, Smallmouth), carpsuckers (Quillback, Lake Quillback, Northern River Carpsucker, Blunt-nosed/Highfin), Hog Sucker, White Sucker, Spotted Sucker, chubsuckers, and a series of redhorses (*Moxostoma duquesnii*, *M. erythrurum*, *M. anisurum*, *M. rubreques*, *M. aureolum*, *M. breviceps*). Largest redhorse specimens exceeded 5-6 lbs. The Harelip Sucker (*Lagochila lacera*) has three Ohio records, none since 1893, and is possibly extinct.
- **Minnows ([[Cyprinidae]]):** the largest group in the key (roughly 40 species/subspecies), including Carp (lateral line scales >32, 2 barbels per side), Goldfish, *Nocomis* chubs, *Hybopsis*, *Erimystax*, dace (*Rhinichthys*, *Clinostomus*, *Chrosomus*), Creek Chub (*Semotilus atromaculatus*, a prized bait minnow), *Notropis* shiners (with N. volucellus split into three subspecies: Northern, Channel and Ghost Mimic Shiners), Emerald, Silver, Rosy-faced and Steelcolored Shiners, Silver-jawed Minnow, Bullhead and Fathead Minnows, and Stonerollers (*Campostoma*). The Western Silvery Minnow was not recorded in 50 years.
- **Catfishes:** Blue/Mississippi-type Catfish (>100 lbs), Channel Catfish (Lake Erie vs. Ohio River stocks in need of study; fishermen believe 2-6 species exist due to sexual dimorphism), three bullheads (Black, Brown, Yellow), Shovelhead (Flathead) Catfish, Stone Catfish, and madtoms (Tadpole, Brindled, Long-nosed).
- **Other soft-rayed fishes:** American Eel (scales over 100 per inch on a 2.5-ft specimen), Mud Pickerel, Great Lakes and Ohio Muskellunge, Western Mudminnow, killifishes and Blackstripe Topminnow, Trout-perch, Pirate-perch.
- **Percids:** White Bass, Yellow Perch, Eastern Sauger, Yellow and Blue Pike-Perch (Walleye forms; Blue Pike dominant in eastern Lake Erie), and about 25 darters, including Johnny Darters, Sand Darter, River Darter, Channel Darter, Greenside Darter, logperches, Black-sided and Gilt Darters (Gilt Darter recorded twice, both before 1900), Variegated, Eastern Banded, Spotted, Blue-breasted, Rainbow, Orange-throated and Pippecanoe Darters, Iowa Darter, Fantail Darter and Least Darter.
- **Centrarchids:** Smallmouth, Spotted and Largemouth Bass (Smallmouth most common in gravelly moderate streams; Spotted Bass the most abundant bass in extreme southern Ohio despite almost no stocking), Warmouth, Green, Longear, Pumpkinseed, Red-eared, Bluegill and Orange-spotted Sunfishes, Northern Rockbass, White and Black Crappies.
- **Remaining families:** Brook Silverside, Freshwater Drum/Sheepshead (specimens over 15 lbs frequent in the Ohio drainage, rarely 10 lbs in Lake Erie), Northern/Great Lake/Spoonhead Muddlers (sculpins), Brook Stickleback, and Eastern Burbot in deeper Lake Erie.
- **Rare, extirpated or presumed lost taxa flagged:** Alligator Gar, Harelip Sucker, Gilt Darter, Northern Crystal Darter (one Lawrence County record; possibly extirpated), Western Silvery Minnow, and declining Big-eyed Shiner and Red-bellied Dace.
- **Taxonomic notes:** the key notes unresolved or intergrading forms (Carpiodes cyprinus subspecies, Campostoma subspecies, Cottus, Percina caprodes, Stizostedion vitreum forms), and an undescribed *Erimystax* species is indicated ("new species?").
