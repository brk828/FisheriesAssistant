# Viviparity Superimposed upon Ovo-viviparity in the Goodeidae, a Family of Cyprinodont Teleost Fishes of the Mexican Plateau

#ichthyology #marine-biology #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This 1933 morphological study by C. L. Turner, published in the *Journal of Morphology*, examines reproduction in the [[Goodeidae]], a family of cyprinodont fishes confined to the [[Mexican Plateau]] and adjacent streams. All members are viviparous. Fertilization is internal, the eggs contain very little yolk, and embryos develop unique absorptive organs (ribbon-shaped proctodaeal processes) at the anus. The author concludes that the family should be classed as ovo-viviparous with a superimposed viviparity, and that it arose separately from an oviparous cyprinodont ancestor, not from the [[Poeciliidae]]. The paper also proposes a reclassification of the genera based on reproductive features. Materials were collected in the field in February–April 1932 or bred from live specimens shipped from Mexico.

## Background & Description

**Scope.** The abstract gives the family as about nine genera and twenty-six species. The body text gives "eight or more genera" and "about twenty-eight species." The study covers geographic distribution, a comparison with the Poeciliidae, gross morphology and histology of the nutritive processes, ovary structure, embryonic nutrition and respiration, and evolutionary alternatives.

**Earlier classifications.**
- Meek's classification (based on Jordan and Evermann, 1896) placed the eight genera in the Poeciliidae across three subfamilies. It used jaw shape, scale count, teeth, fins and intestine length, and it did not reflect reproductive differences.
- Regan and Hubbs grouped the genera into a single subfamily or family, Goodeinae/Goodeidae, based mainly on a modified anal fin in males (a series of short, stiff anterior rays). Hubbs separated *Balsadichthys* from *Goodea* and [[Lermichthys]] from *Characodon*.
- Turner argues that the reproductive peculiarities are diagnostic because they occur in all members of the family and nowhere else. The closest approaches are the isolated cases of [[Jenynsia Lineata]] and [[Anableps Gronovii]].

**Reproductive contrast with Poeciliidae.** In the typical poeciliid [[Xiphophorus]], the yolk is almost the sole nourishment, the embryo is born soon after hatching, sperm are stored for months, and males have a gonopodium. In *Goodea bilineata*, the yolk is minimal, the embryo leaves the follicle early, it develops inside the intra-ovarian cavity for a prolonged period, and the ovary supplies nutrition. Males lack spermatophores and an elongated gonopodium.

**Key reproductive features of *Goodea bilineata*** (the representative species):
- Ovocytes reach about 4 mm in diameter and are very poor in yolk. Unless fertilized, they degenerate after maximum size.
- Ovigerous tissue is reduced to a pair of dorso-lateral folds suspended in the intra-ovarian cavity.
- Hatching is passive. The ovigerous folds withdraw from the embryos, stripping the vitelline membrane.
- The yolk sac is at most about 0.4 mm in diameter.
- Roughly one-half to two-thirds of embryos are absorbed in the ovisac. In an ovary with sixty embryos at hatching, thirty or fewer are born.
- Born embryos already have differentiated ovaries or spermaries.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic isolation.** The Mexican Plateau is an isolated biological province. High mountains enclose it on all sides except the north and northwest, where a mountainous desert forms the barrier. The southeastern part has no outlet and drains into shallow lakes near Mexico City. The central, western and northern parts drain through the [[Lerma River]] into [[Lake Chapala]]. The [[Rio Santiago]] leaves the lake and crosses the Juanacatlan falls and a gorge, which form an effective barrier to upstream migration. The northeastern margin has been pierced by a branch of the [[Panuco River]], probably by stream capture from the Lerma system.

**Distribution.**
- The Goodeidae are largely confined to the plateau. *Balsadichthys* reaches the headwaters of the Balsas River. *Goodea toweri*, *G. captiva* and *G. atripinnis* occur in the Panuco system, and only *G. toweri* is found below the plateau.
- A few *Characodon* and *Zoogoneticus* occur in the western lowlands (Rio Santiago below the falls, or streams further south). Turner suggests they came from the plateau stock rather than escaping from it.
- *Zoogoneticus* and *Skiffia* occur across the whole plateau. *Characodon* and *Goodea* occur in the west and northwest but not in the Valley of Mexico. *Chapalichthys* is mainly a lake genus around Lake Chapala. *Lermichthys* is confined to Lake Lerma and its draining branch. *Balsadichthys* is known only from the Balsas headwaters. *Girardinichthys* is confined to the Valley of Mexico.
- The atherine *Chirostoma* has an almost identical distribution, suggesting a shared biogeographic history.
- *Poeciliopsis infans* is the only other top minnow on the plateau. Several poeciliid genera occur in lowland headwaters just outside the plateau but not within it. Turner finds no environmental reason for their absence, so he attributes it to the barriers.

**Comparative ovo-viviparous and viviparous teleosts.** Viviparity in teleosts is irregularly distributed. Most such species occur in the orders Cyprinodontes (Poeciliidae, Anablepidae, Jenynsiidae, Goodeidae) and Acanthopteri (Embiotocidae, Scorpaenidae, Blenniidae, Zoarcidae). Turner notes that in all separately derived cases, viviparity is associated with a small yolk supply and an embryonic body surface too small to absorb enough nutrition. Each lineage therefore evolved accessory structures, and high intra-ovarian mortality is common.

## Key Findings

1. **Family-level reproductive uniqueness.** The nutritive processes, the modified ovary and the specialized reproductive sequence are found in all Goodeidae and nowhere else. Turner uses them as the basis for a proposed reclassification.

2. **Classification.** Turner classes the Goodeidae as ovo-viviparous with a superimposed viviparity. The Cyprinodontidae are oviparous, the Poeciliidae ovo-viviparous, and the Goodeidae ovo-viviparous with a later viviparous stage.

3. **Nutritive processes differ among genera.** All Goodeidae genera studied develop processes at the posterior end of the gut, but their number, shape and histology differ. Some examples:
   - *[[Lermichthys Multiradiatus]]*: three processes (two lateral wings and one median) by about 5 mm embryo length, with spongy connective tissue and a wide primary tissue space.
   - *[[Goodea Bilineata]]*: three lobes by 7 mm, with no secondary branches. Processes are lost 4–6 hours after birth.
   - *[[Girardinichthys Innominatus]]*: four filaments initially, with two long posterior ribbon-shaped processes remaining at birth that extend beyond the caudal fin.
   - *[[Zoogoneticus Cuitzeokensis]]*: the most elaborate condition, with about twelve branched divisions of flattened membranes. Turner suggests it needs a larger surface because absorption is less efficient per unit area.
   - *[[Balsadichthys Whitei]]*: studied only just after birth, with processes resembling *Zoogoneticus* but fewer and shorter filaments.

4. **Histological grouping.** Lermichthys, Goodea and Girardinichthys have less numerous but histologically complex processes (cuboidal epithelium, wide primary tissue spaces, capillary plexus). *Zoogoneticus* has more numerous processes with simpler histology (loose epithelium, no primary tissue space, large internal vessels).

5. **Ovary as a nutritive organ.** After fertilization the ovary becomes vascular, its lining develops deep folds, and it forms fluid-filled tissue spaces. Egg production is interrupted, and the ovary supplies embryonic nutrition. Turner reports that more than half of the embryos in each brood are absorbed by the survivors, and that the intra-ovarian epithelium secretes nutritive fluid.

6. **Embryonic feeding and respiration.** Before birth, embryos swallow intra-ovarian fluid, and the gut contains mucous and cellular debris. Respiration depends mainly on the integument and nutritive processes.

7. **Evolutionary origin.** Turner weighs three hypotheses: derivation from the Poeciliidae; a common ovo-viviparous ancestor for both families; or separate origins from an oviparous ancestor. He favors the second, arguing that the gonopodium and spermatophore differences and the opposite modification of the anal fin in the two groups make a single ancestor unlikely. He suggests the Goodeidae derive from *[[Profundulus]]*, a primitive Central American oviparous genus, probably on the Pacific slope near Tehuantepec. The Poeciliidae probably arose on the Atlantic slope further south or east.

8. **Comparative viviparity.** Among [[Cymatogaster Aggregatus]], [[Zoarces Viviparus]], [[Jenynsia Lineata]] and the Goodeidae, Turner finds shared features: small yolk masses, almost yolkless yolk sacs, retention in the intra-ovarian cavity, reliance on ovarian secretions, and feeding on the alimentary canal before birth. Their accessory structures differ. Zoarces has none, Anableps keeps an enlarged yolk sac, Embiotocidae have spatulate fin processes, Jenynsia has filamentous processes entering the pharynx, and the Goodeidae have anal processes.
