# Endangered and Threatened Species Assessment for Hoover Powerplant Modification Project Area

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #endangered-species #Colorado-River

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (September 5, 1979) provides a species list for Section 7 consultation regarding the proposed [[Hoover Dam]] Powerplant Modification Project. The assessment area extends from [[Hoover Dam]] north approximately 3,000 yards to [[Boulder Basin]], south along the [[Colorado River]] to [[Lake Mohave]], and throughout [[Lake Mohave]] to [[Davis Dam]]. The document identifies four listed endangered species and one proposed species that may be present in the project area, including two fish species of particular concern: [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*).

## Background & Description

This memorandum fulfills requirements under Section 7(c) of the Endangered Species Act Amendments of 1978, which mandates that the Fish and Wildlife Service provide lists of threatened and endangered species potentially present in areas involving Federal construction activities. The Bureau of Reclamation requested this information on August 1, 1979.

### Listed Species Descriptions

**[[Devil's Hole Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*): An inch-long pupfish with the entire wild population confined to a small warm pool (50 feet long, 10 feet wide) in [[Devil's Hole]], located in a detached portion of [[Death Valley National Monument]] in [[Ash Meadows]], [[Nye County]], Nevada. A captive population segment is maintained in an artificial refugium immediately below [[Hoover Dam]]. Actions affecting the underground water system of [[Devil's Hole]] could result in species extinction.

**[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) [Proposed Species]: A fairly large fish (12-24 inches) characterized by a small head, humped back, and extremely thin caudal peduncle.

### Non-Fish Species

**[[Peregrine Falcon]]** (*Falco peregrinus*): Medium-sized falcon, slate gray above with dark head and distinctive "mustaches" below each eye. Long pointed wings. May occur as spring or winter migrant.

**[[Bald Eagle]]** (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*): Large eagle with white head and tail in adults; immatures are dark with bare feet. May occur as spring or winter migrant.

**[[Yuma Clapper Rail]]** (*Rallus longirostris yumanensis*): Small hen-sized water bird with long legs and long slender decurved bill. Found in fresh or brackish tule, cattail, or salt cedar marshes. Breeds as far north as [[Topoc Marsh]] on the [[Colorado River]]. Also exists in the [[Colorado River Delta]] in Mexico and the [[Salton Sea]] in California. May be of concern if proposed project significantly alters water levels on lower [[Colorado River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project area encompasses critical habitat within the [[Colorado River]] system, including [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], and riverine sections between major dams. This system supports multiple endangered species with varying habitat requirements:

- **Aquatic habitats**: Support endemic fish species including the critically endangered [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] and proposed [[Bonytail Chub]]
- **Riparian marshes**: Provide breeding and foraging habitat for [[Yuma Clapper Rail]]
- **Open water and cliffs**: Serve as migration corridors and potential nesting sites for raptors

Water level management is identified as a critical factor, particularly for marsh-dependent species like the [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] and for maintaining the underground water system supporting [[Devil's Hole]].

## Key Findings

1. **Species Inventory**: Four listed endangered species and one proposed species identified in the project area, representing diverse taxa (two fish, two birds, one rail).

2. **Critical Habitat Concerns**: The [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] represents an extreme conservation concern due to its entire wild population being confined to a single 50-foot by 10-foot pool, with vulnerability to underground water system alterations.

3. **Refugium Population**: A captive population of [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] is maintained in an artificial refugium immediately below [[Hoover Dam]], providing insurance against extinction.

4. **Regulatory Requirements**: The Federal agency must conduct a biological assessment within 180 days of receiving the species list, including: (1) comprehensive survey results; (2) impact studies on identified species; (3) consideration of cumulative effects on species and critical habitat.

5. **Hydrological Sensitivity**: Multiple species show sensitivity to water level alterations, particularly the [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] in marsh habitats and the [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] dependent on underground water systems.

6. **Geographic Range**: Listed species occupy habitats from [[Topoc Marsh]] in the north through the project area to the [[Colorado River Delta]] in Mexico, indicating regional conservation significance.
