# Adult Fish Field Data Sheets and Sampling Memoranda: Verde and Salt River Surveys

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a compiled field record from electrofish sampling of the [[Verde River]] and [[Salt River]] systems in Arizona, most likely conducted by [[Dames & Moore]] in 1981. It contains roughly 20 "Adult Fish Data Sheets" (per-site field forms) and about 30 "Field Memoranda" (handwritten tallies of fish captured at each station), plus a short letter on scale-based age analysis. The records document species composition, individual length and weight measurements, and sampling conditions at many sites. The key interpretive point is a letter that reports scale-based ageing was unsuccessful for several species and that the community differed between sites above and below reservoirs. Many entries are handwritten and partly illegible, so the totals below are approximate.

## Background & Description

The forms follow a standard template. Each sheet records:

- Project name and number, with location and gear type (mostly [[Electrofishing]] with a "Shocker" unit)
- Date, time of day and water temperature
- Sample duration (recorded in seconds, for example about 230 s on one sheet), number of hauls and net specifications
- Per-individual data: species, total length (mm), weight (g), stomach and scale sample numbers, tag number, sex (M/F) and maturity
- Equipment identifiers (measuring board, scale, Whitney and thermometer numbers)
- Collector's signature and comments

The memoranda use a parallel format. They list individual fish by number, species, length and weight, with notes on collection location. Several memoranda are routing slips and summary tallies, and some are sorted into size-class tables for [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Red Shiner]].

Species recorded across the sheets and memoranda include:

- Game and introduced fishes: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Common Carp]]
- Native and small-bodied fishes: [[Desert Sucker]], [[Gila Sucker]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Red Shiner]]
- Other species: [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Threadfin Shad]]

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling covered a range of habitats, including:

- Shallow shoreline and backwater areas, where many suckers and shiners were taken
- Deeper pools and channels, where larger individuals and catfish were more common
- Sites close to reservoirs and sites farther upstream or downstream

Field notes mention dead suckers on the bottom during sampling (about 20 on one sheet), high-water conditions at some sites, and fish collected "along the bank" or near boulders and rock structure. The letter suggests that reservoir releases may have influenced the fish assemblage downstream, and that the reservoir sites differed from those upstream.

## Key Findings

**Community composition by site.** According to the letter, the species distribution differed between the two sides of the reservoirs:

- [[Desert Sucker]], [[Gila Sucker]], [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Black Bullhead]] were collected only in the upstream or downstream sites (the letter does not clearly specify which side for each).
- [[Common Carp]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Red Shiner]] were collected both above and below the reservoir.
- The letter's author attributed these patterns to possible fish releases from the reservoirs, though this was not established.

**Age determination.** Scale analysis was unsuccessful for [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Gila Sucker]]. Their scales were too small or unreadable to show annuli. Ages for these species were therefore estimated by [[Length-Frequency Analysis]], which the author considered only a rough guide. The letter states that the sucker size classes were probably age classes, but this remains uncertain.

**Size and sample data.** Individual lengths and weights are recorded for hundreds of fish across the sheets and memoranda. Examples include largemouth bass in size classes from about 100 to 350 mm and desert suckers and red shiners of varied lengths. Exact totals per species were not consolidated in the document and would need to be recalculated from the original sheets.

**Field limitations.** Several sheets note problems with collection, such as sites being hard to sample, fish escaping or incomplete collections. The author of the letter cautioned that the findings were preliminary and should not be used for definitive conclusions without further sampling.
