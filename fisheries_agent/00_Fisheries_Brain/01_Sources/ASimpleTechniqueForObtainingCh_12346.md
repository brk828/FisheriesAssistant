# A Simple Technique for Obtaining Chromosomes from Teleost Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #teleostei

## Executive Summary

This short methods note by J. D. McPhail and R. L. Jones (College of Fisheries, University of Washington; *Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada*, 1966) describes a rapid technique for obtaining countable metaphase chromosomes from teleost fishes. Earlier methods were effective but slow and often yielded few countable chromosome sets. The new procedure uses a [[Colchicine]] or Velban injection, followed by preparation of a [[Gill Epithelium]] squash from the posterior gill arch. It produces a high proportion of countable chromosome sets from a single preparation and leaves the specimen intact for further taxonomic work. The method is described as empirical. It works best on freshwater fishes and has given usable results in several marine groups.

## Background & Description

Chromosomes had been widely used in invertebrate taxonomy, but technical problems had restricted their use in vertebrates. The authors list several earlier approaches to fish chromosomes:

- Tissue cultures (Roberts, 1964).
- Colchicine injection followed by squashes of testis or haemopoietic tissue (Roberts, 1964; Ohno et al., 1965).
- Squashes of embryonic material (Simon, 1963; Simon and Dollar, 1963; Swarup, 1959).
- Sectioning of testis (Nogusa, 1960).

These methods gave usable results but were time-consuming and often yielded low percentages of countable chromosomes. The new technique is intended for juvenile fishes of 2–6 inches standard length. For larger fish, the authors advise increasing both the [[Colchicine]] dose and the interval between injection and sampling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The note is a laboratory methods paper and contains no field survey, habitat, or population data. Its ecological relevance comes from the taxa it addresses. The method is most successful on freshwater species, and it was also tested on marine groups. The authors state that the best results come from young, actively growing fish in good condition. For marine fishes, a pre-treatment of 50% sea water for 1 hr, followed by fresh water for 1 hr, improved chromosome spreading in most species tested. ## Key Findings

**Procedure (summarized from the numbered steps):**

1. Make an intermuscular injection of 0.1 ml of 0.01–0.05% colchicine or Velban (dose depends on fish size) into the anterior back.
2. Hold the fish in a well-aerated container for 1–2 hr.
3. Kill the fish and remove the posterior gill arch.
4. Dialyze the arch for 30 min in distilled water.
5. Stain for 15 min in fresh, filtered aceto-orcein.
6. Shake the arch lightly on a clean slide to deposit a light cell slurry, then remove large tissue pieces.
7. Cover immediately with a clean coverslip and squash manually on a flat surface (plate glass or thick plexiglass) using a number 5 or 6 rubber stopper.
8. Examine immediately, or seal with lanolin-paraffin. Sealed preparations kept in a refrigerator last several days.

**Modifications for marine fishes:**

- Use higher colchicine doses (about 0.1 ml of 0.5% colchicine).
- Hold fish at least 12 hr before tissue preparation.
- Place fish in 50% sea water for 1 hr, then fresh water for 1 hr, before removing the gill arch.

**Tissue choice:** Gill epithelium gave consistently the best results. Epithelium from the paired fins was sometimes usable.
