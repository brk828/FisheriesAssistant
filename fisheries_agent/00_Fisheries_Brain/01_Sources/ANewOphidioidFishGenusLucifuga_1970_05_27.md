# Lucifuga spelaeotes (New Species)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Bythitidae #Ophidiiformes #cave-fish #Lucifuga

## Executive Summary
This paper describes **[[Lucifuga spelaeotes]]**, a new species of blind, cave-dwelling ophidioid fish collected from **[[Mermaid's Pool]]**, a limestone sinkhole near Nassau, New Providence Island, Bahamas. The species is placed in the genus **[[Lucifuga]]** (subgenus **Stygicola**) alongside the two known Cuban cave brotulids, **[[Lucifuga dentatus]]** and **[[Lucifuga subterraneus]]**. The authors argue for consolidating all three species into a single genus rather than maintaining separate genera, based on shared morphological characters. *L. spelaeotes* is distinguished by having palatine teeth, higher pectoral-ray and vertebral counts, more caudal fin rays, relatively well-developed eyes, and dark walnut-brown coloration — traits suggesting it may be the most morphologically primitive of the three species.

## Background & Description
**Taxonomic Placement:**
- Genus **[[Lucifuga]]** (Poey) is diagnosed by: absent chin barbel, separate gill membranes, live-bearing reproduction with non-ossified male genitalia, single-rayed adjacent ventral fins originating behind the cleithral symphysis, exserted caudal fin (8-11 rays), naked/partly-scaled head with prominent sensory canals, poorly developed eyes, and small imbricate body scales.
- Two subgenera recognized:
  - **Subgenus Lucifuga** (type: *L. subterraneus*) — palatine teeth absent; pectoral rays 12-14.
  - **Subgenus Stygicola** (type: *L. dentatus*) — palatine teeth present; pectoral rays 15-20.

**Diagnostic Characters of *L. spelaeotes*:**
| Character | *L. spelaeotes* | *L. dentatus* | *L. subterraneus* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pectoral fin rays | 18-20 | 15-17 | 12-14 |
| Total vertebrae | 52-53 | 46-48 | 46 (approx.) |
| Caudal fin rays | 10-11 | 8 | 8 |
| Scales between lateral & preopercular canal | Present | Absent | Present |
| Palatine teeth | Present | Present | Absent |
| Eye development | Relatively well-developed | Reduced | Reduced |
| Coloration | Dark walnut brown | Variable (pale to dark) | Pale pink/lilac |

**Morphological Description Highlights:**
- Dorsal profile strongly arched; body compressed posteriorly, tapering evenly to tail.
- Subterminal mouth; upper jaw slightly projecting.
- Eye small but with protruding lens, visible through a large clear "window" in the skin (window diameter 3.5-3.6% SL).
- Body fully covered in small cycloid scales (~23 rows across body above anal-fin origin); head bears extensive naked areas corresponding to the cavernous cephalic lateralis (sensory canal) system.
- Sensory papillae in two longitudinal series (no pored lateral canal on body).
- Head pore counts: 1 (lateral canal), 2 (supraorbital), 6 (suborbital), 7 (preoperculo-mandibular).
- Teeth present on premaxillae, dentaries, vomer, and palatine (villiform, in broad patches; vomerine teeth needlelike, arranged in a whorl).
- Branchiostegal rays: 7 total (4 attached to epihyal/ceratohyal outer surface, 3 attached ventrally along ceratohyal shaft).
- Pseudobranch absent; 3 short gill rakers plus dentigerous pads on first arch.
- Ribs on centra 1-7; pleural ribs on subsequent abdominal centra (except last).
- Male copulatory apparatus well-developed, non-ossified, borne on a fleshy "papilla proper" with associated lobes (median 1, median process, secondary lobe, basal lobe) — terminology following Hubbs (1938).
- Two short pyloric caeca present.
- Coloration: rich walnut brown overall; whitish margins on vertical fins (more pronounced in smaller female); pectoral fins, ventral ray, lips, and lateralis-system skin whitish/unmarked; branchiostegal membranes dark along isthmus.

**Etymology:** *spelaeotes* — Greek for "cave or cavern dweller," referencing its anchialine habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**Habitat — [[Mermaid's Pool]]:**
- A small limestone sinkhole (~75 feet across) in the SW Nassau area, New Providence Island, Bahamas.
- Represents an exposed section of the freshwater/saline water table (anchialine system), with water level several feet below ground surface.
- Salinity measured at ~1 m depth: **4.6 ppt** (markedly brackish, consistent with anchialine mixing).
- Surrounding vegetation: pine and scrub growth, partially cleared for housing development at time of study.
- Associated fauna: grapsoid crabs observed on the pool's steep walls.
- Conservation concern noted by authors: surrounding land was already largely cleared for housing development, raising uncertainty about the future water quality and survival of this population — an early documented concern for **[[Anchialine Cave Ecosystems]]** conservation.

**Broader Biogeography:**
- Congeners occur in Cuban cave systems (*L. dentatus*, *L. subterraneus*).
- Unconfirmed reports of additional blind cave fish populations on **Eleuthera** (Bahamas) and in **Jamaica**, suggesting a wider Caribbean radiation of cave-adapted *Lucifuga*-like fishes awaiting formal study.
- Closest surface-dwelling relative proposed: **[[Calamopteryx]]** (Böhlke & Cohen, 1966), a small secretive coral-reef fish of the tropical western Atlantic — though unlikely to be a direct ancestor due to its elongate pectoral radials; a common ancestor is hypothesized to remain undiscovered in reef crevice habitats.

## Key Findings
1. **New species description**: *Lucifuga spelaeotes* is formally described from a single sinkhole locality, based on a male holotype (109 mm SL) and female paratype (76.5 mm SL).
2. **Taxonomic revision**: The authors unify Cuban cave brotulids and the new Bahamian species under one genus, **Lucifuga**, with two subgenera (*Lucifuga* s.s. and *Stygicola*), rejecting prior generic separation (Gill's *Stygicola*, 1863) as representing differences "of degree rather than kind."
3. **Phylogenetic inference**: *L. spelaeotes* is hypothesized to be the most plesiomorphic (primitive) member of the genus based on retained palatine teeth, more caudal rays, better-developed eyes, and dark coloration — contrasting with the more derived, depigmented, eye-reduced *L. subterraneus*.
4. **No direct linear phylogeny**: Character conflicts (e.g., scale patterns between preopercular/lateral canals, occipital scalation) indicate the three species do not form a simple ancestor-descendant chain; convergent or parallel cave adaptation is implied.
5. **Habitat vulnerability**: Documented anthropogenic threat (housing development clearing) to the type locality is one of the earliest explicit conservation concerns raised for an anchialine cave-fish habitat in the Caribbean.
6. **Relationship to Brosmophycinae**: Lucifuga is placed within the viviparous ophidioid subfamily **[[Brosmophycinae]]**, distinguished from the *Dinematichthys-Ogilbia* assemblage and dermatopsin genera by its non-ossified male genital claspers; closest deep-water relatives suggested among *Cataetyx* and *Diplacanthopoma*.
7. **Reproductive biology**: Confirmed live-bearing (viviparous) reproduction with well-developed, non-ossified male copulatory structures — a key generic diagnostic trait shared across all three *Lucifuga* species.
