# Effects of Interim Flows from Glen Canyon Dam: Trip No. 4 (March–April 1993)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #colorado-river #glen-canyon-dam

## Executive Summary
This quarterly report (Trip No. 4, March 25–April 6, 1993) documents fish and aquatic resource sampling conducted by BIO/WEST, Inc. along the [[Lower Colorado River]] from Diamond Creek (RM 225.7) to Pearce Ferry (RM 280) on [[Lake Mead]], as part of the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies monitoring interim flow effects from [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. No endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) or [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) were captured. Native species recovery was limited to [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]. The study area had recently experienced major winter flooding (Jan–Feb 1993), which substantially altered tributary mouths (notably Spencer Creek) and mainstem sediment deposition patterns, complicating sampling efforts. [[Common Carp]] dominated catches and were observed in active spawning condition throughout tributaries.

## Background & Description
Limited individual-level morphological data were recorded, but notable specimens include:
- **Flannelmouth Sucker** (*Catostomus latipinnis*): One PIT-tagged individual captured at RM 245.5, weighing 106 g (PIT #7F7F480106). Three total captured; only one successfully tagged due to PIT reader malfunction.
- **Rainbow Trout** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*): First individual recorded in this study — a small female (185 mm, likely a length recorded as "185 cm" in error) captured via backpack electrofishing in Spencer Creek, noted to be extruding eggs (i.e., in spawning condition).
- **Common Carp** (*Cyprinus carpio*): Approximately 75% of captured individuals were expressing milt or eggs, indicating an active spawning population during the sampling period.

No morphometric data were reported for [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Mosquitofish]], or [[Green Sunfish]] beyond species identification and counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Mainstem Habitat Heterogeneity**: The Colorado River corridor sampled exhibited a gradient of physical habitat types — swift, deep runs near Bridge Canyon; gentler flow with vertical cliffs and emergent vegetation near Spencer Canyon; and slow, meandering, sediment-lined channels near Lost Creek and Burnt Spring Canyon, the latter increasingly influenced by rising [[Lake Mead]] levels.
- **Reservoir Inflow Effects**: Rising lake levels reduced the distinction between habitat substrata classified as "C" and "D" (both characterized by slow current and vegetated shorelines), effectively merging these habitat types via inundation.
- **Tributary Disturbance**: Flooding in January–February 1993 dramatically altered tributary geomorphology. Spencer Creek's mouth changed course and lost vegetation, depositing a gravel bar extending into the mainstem — creating a novel opportunity to study creek ecosystem re-establishment. [[Lost Creek Slough]] became heavily silted (4–6 inches of water over silt), rendering detailed sampling infeasible. [[Surprise Creek]] showed comparatively less silt and more gravel substrate.
- **Thermal/Turbidity Conditions**: Mainstem water temperatures ranged from 12°C (Bridge Canyon) to 14°C (Pearce Ferry), with persistently high turbidity — conditions considered unfavorable for detecting juvenile or young-of-year native fish.
- **Carp Spawning Habitat Use**: Carp were observed spawning in all tributaries downstream of Bridge Canyon, including attempts to ascend the normally dry [[Reference Point Creek]] (RM 252.4). No young-of-year or juvenile carp were captured, suggesting spawning had not yet translated to recruitment detectable by current gear.
- **Species-Habitat Associations**: Minnow traps in [[Spencer Creek]] and [[Lost Creek]] were most successful for capturing [[Red Shiner]]. [[Mosquitofish]] were captured via dip net in backwater camp habitat (RM 268.1) but not by repeated minnow trap sets in the same location. A hoop net set at the mouth of Lost Creek captured both carp and channel catfish.

## Key Findings
1. **No endangered species detected**: Neither [[Humpback Chub]] nor [[Razorback Sucker]] were observed or captured during this trip.
2. **Native fish scarcity**: Only [[Speckled Dace]] (n=13) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (n=4) represented native species in the catch; both are far outnumbered by non-native species.
3. **Carp dominance**: [[Common Carp]] was the most frequently captured species (n=328), with the majority in active spawning condition (~75% expressing milt/eggs).
4. **Species inventory** (Total captured, per Table 4):
   - Common Carp — 328
   - Red Shiner — 214
   - Channel Catfish — 49
   - Fathead Minnow — 24
   - Speckled Dace — 13
   - Flannelmouth Sucker — 4
   - Mosquitofish — 4
   - Striped Bass — 3
   - Green Sunfish — 2
   - Rainbow Trout — 1
5. **Flood impact on habitat**: Winter 1993 flooding (Jan–Feb) significantly reshaped tributary mouths and mainstem sand deposition, notably restructuring [[Spencer Creek]] and heavily silting [[Lost Creek Slough]].
6. **Gear effectiveness under flood conditions**: Increased inundated riparian vegetation limited effective net-setting locations; electrofishing and minnow trapping were identified as the most effective techniques going forward, to be emphasized in future trips.
7. **Invertebrate/productivity sampling**: Twelve drift net samples (RM 242.2 and RM 259.5), one Hess sample (Spencer Creek, no invertebrates found due to scouring), incidental algae/invertebrate collections (Travertine and Bridge Canyon Creeks), plankton tows, and one Eckman dredge sample (Lost Creek Slough). A single crayfish (order Decapoda) was captured in a trammel net at RM 260.3.
8. **River stage monitoring**: A ~25 cm stage change was recorded at the Bridge Canyon camp gauge; no new benchmarks established this trip.
9. **Administrative/logistical issues**: Data loss occurred when a clipboard with 40 net-set records fell into the water (half recovered from memory); lack of sampling permits for [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]] was identified and is being resolved; campsite scarcity below Burnt Springs Canyon was noted as an ongoing logistical constraint.
