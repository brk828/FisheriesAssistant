## Executive Summary

This study presents the first limnological characterization of **Azibo Reservoir** (Macedo de Cavaleiros, Trás-os-Montes, Portugal), a reservoir formed in 1984 by damming the River Azibo within the [[Douro Hydrographic Basin]]. Over a two-year sampling cycle (1986–1988), researchers analyzed physico-chemical and biological parameters at four stations (primarily Station 1) to assess trophic state and predict ecosystem evolution. The paper is not fish-focused; rather, it centers on water chemistry, nutrient dynamics, phytoplankton pigments, and waterfowl influence on nutrient cycling. The overarching finding is that Azibo Reservoir is trending toward **eutrophication**, driven by high water residence time, persistent submerged vegetation from incomplete pre-flooding clearance, and waterfowl-mediated phosphorus loading. Cyanobacterial blooms (*Anabaena* sp.) observed in 1988 are cited as direct evidence of this trophic shift.

## Background & Description

No fish or macrofaunal morphological data are reported in this source. The morphological/structural information provided pertains instead to:

- **Reservoir morphometry**: Maximum area of 410 ha; maximum depth of 50 m near the dam; average depth of 15 m.
- **Basin structure**: Bipartite morphology — (1) the ancient river bed with steep slopes and dense vegetal cover (*Pinus* sp., *Quercus* sp.), and (2) a wider left arm formed by flooding of a smooth-sloped agricultural valley.
- **Phytoplankton community structure**: Diatom contribution to total phytoplankton dropped sharply between 1987 and 1988 (e.g., September: 27.2% → 1.9%), attributed to silicate limitation (see [[Silicate Limitation]]).
- **Pigment indicators**: Chlorophyll "a" (0–19.2 mg/m³) and feopigments (0–17.6 mg/m³) used as proxies for phytoplankton biomass and senescence/decomposition, respectively.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although fish are not directly discussed, the paper details the physico-chemical niche structuring the broader aquatic community:

- **Thermal regime**: Azibo is a **warm monomictic** reservoir, stratifying from May to September, followed by a mixing period. Surface temperatures reached a maximum of 26°C (August 1987); bottom minimum was 5.6°C (February 1987).
- **Oxygen stratification**: Dissolved oxygen tracked thermal stratification, with the hypolimnion becoming **anoxic** during stratification (1 mg O₂/L isopleth near 10 m depth). Hypolimnetic oxygen depletion intensified from 1987 to 1988, indicating increasing organic matter load — a classic sign of eutrophication (see [[Hypolimnetic Oxygen Depletion]]).
- **Nutrient cycling**: Strong stratification of nitrate, nitrite, ammonia, and total nitrogen was observed. Nitrates comprised ~79% of surface inorganic nitrogen but only 42% at the anoxic bottom, where ammonia dominated (56% of total N), consistent with denitrification and reduced redox conditions.
- **Phosphorus dynamics**: Phosphate concentrations (0–11.3 μgat P/L) peaked near the surface in spring/early autumn, correlating with waterfowl abundance (*Anas platyrhynchus*, *Podiceps cristatus*), implicating [[Waterfowl-Mediated Nutrient Loading]] as an ecological driver.
- **Silica cycling**: Silicate concentrations (0.24–4.29 mg Si/L) fell below the critical threshold (<0.39 mg Si/L) in August 1988, signaling severe **Si limitation** for diatoms — a niche-narrowing event for silica-dependent primary producers.
- **Trophic state trajectory**: The Trophic State Index (TSI, Carlson 1977) ranged from 12.6 to 55.3, with a general upward trend across the study period, reflecting a shift in the reservoir's primary producer community toward eutrophic conditions, including cyanobacterial dominance (*Anabaena* sp. blooms in spring/summer 1988).
- **Anthropogenic and legacy habitat factors**: Submerged terrestrial vegetation (trees/shrubs not cleared before flooding in 1984) continues to decompose underwater, contributing organic load; high water residence time exacerbates nutrient accumulation.

## Key Findings

- **No direct fish data** (species, morphology, or population metrics) are reported in this source; the study is a foundational limnological/biogeochemical characterization intended to inform future assessment of "living resources," which may include fish in companion studies.
- Azibo Reservoir shows multiple convergent indicators of **eutrophication**: increasing total nitrogen (>35 μgatN/L at bottom by October 1988), rising TSI values, and worsening hypolimnetic oxygen depletion.
- **Correlations** (Table 1, p<0.001 except waterfowl–PO₄ at p<0.05): dissolved oxygen negatively correlated with temperature and conductivity, positively with nitrate; alkalinity positively correlated with ammonia and conductivity, negatively with nitrates; phosphate levels positively correlated with waterfowl density.
- **N:P ratio** showed strong stratification in 1987, with surface values below 1:10 and bottom values up to 200:1, reflecting decoupled nitrogen and phosphorus sedimentation dynamics — relevant to [[Nitrogen-Phosphorus Ratio]] as a eutrophication indicator.
- **Diatom decline** between 1987 and 1988 (attributed to silicate limitation) suggests a shift in the primary producer community structure, potentially favoring cyanobacteria such as *Anabaena* sp.
- **Waterfowl** (*Anas platyrhynchus*, *Podiceps cristatus*) are identified as a contributing factor to phosphorus loading, corroborating findings from Bedard et al. (1980) on seabird-mediated nutrient recycling in other systems.
- The authors conclude that **management interventions** are needed to curb the eutrophication trend, given the combined pressures of high residence time, submerged legacy vegetation, and waterfowl activity — findings relevant to future [[Reservoir Management]] and [[Trophic State Index]] applications in Iberian reservoirs.
- This work forms part of a broader research program by the Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre" characterizing Douro basin reservoirs (e.g., [[Carrapatelo Reservoir]], [[Valeira Reservoir]], [[Torrão Reservoir]]), situating Azibo within a comparative regional limnological context.
