# Mark-Recapture Experiments on Perch, Pike and Charr in Windermere, 1943–1982

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae #esocidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, by C. Kipling and E. D. Le Cren of the [[Freshwater Biological Association]] (Ambleside, Cumbria), reviews four decades of marking experiments on three fish species in [[Windermere]]: [[European Perch]], [[Northern Pike]] and [[Arctic Charr]]. Individual fish were marked with numbered tags, subcutaneous tags and fin clips, or fin punches, and recaptured over periods of up to 12 years for pike and charr and up to 12 years or more for perch. The experiments yielded reliable information on movements, homing, and growth that could not have been obtained otherwise, but population estimates were satisfactory only for pike and, under restricted conditions, for charr.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Mark-recapture experiments were used to study movements, growth and population parameters. Many experiments predated more sophisticated marking methods.
- **Period and scope:** Perch were tagged in 1943–1944, 1948–1950 and 1953; pike every year from 1949 to 1982; charr in 1950–1952 (subcutaneous tags with fin clips) and 1955–1960 (fin clips only).
- **Numbers tagged:** 13,192 perch... (as stated in source: 9,726 perch in the 1948–1953 series plus 3,103 transferred/replaced in 1943–1944, totalling 12,829 perch tagged in the main experiments); 4,696 pike; 2,066 charr.
- **Tagging methods:**
  - Perch: white celluloid discs (1943–1944), later monel loops fixed to the preopercular bone (1948–1953).
  - Pike: monel loops on the opercular bone (1949–1952); from 1953 half the tags were on the jaw and from 1954 jaw tagging was standard.
  - Charr: subcutaneous tags and fin clips or punches, with sequential marks for short-term studies.
- **Study sites:** Tagging and recapture occurred across the north and south basins, with spawning-site beats (e.g. Brathay, Red Nab, Holbeck, Low Wray Bay, Thompson Holme, Millerground Hill).
- **Recapture methods:** Trap nets, seines, gillnets (various bar sizes), angling, and returns via public appeals with rewards.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Perch** spawn in shallow water from late April to early June and congregate in deeper water in autumn; in winter they are found in deep water, some offshore from spawning areas. Movement was largely limited by sampling effort, with no tagged perch recaptured in the south basin despite intensive effort there.

**Pike** move between basins: about 18% of recaptures from 1971–1980 were in a different basin from where they were tagged. Some fish returned consistently to the same place for two or more years.

**Charr** spawn in two seasons: November in shallow water (including the River Brathay) and February in deeper water in the lake. Males arrive early and remain longer; females stay for shorter periods. Red Nab shows a diurnal movement pattern, with males arriving earlier in the evening.

Environmental factors that affected results included tag loss, handling mortality (exacerbated by high temperatures), flood conditions in the River Brathay (1951, 1953), and gillnet selectivity by size and sex.

## Key Findings

- **Homing behaviour:** First discovered for these species in this study. Perch transferred 200–3,200 m showed a constant homing proportion (1.1–3.0%). A few perch homed after more than a year's absence (three of 27 fish released alive), suggesting consistent homing. Of 2,066 charr tagged at four spawning sites, 584 were recaptured after one or more years; 578 were at their original site. Charr transferred to other sites or rivers, sometimes within 24 hours, returned to their original spawning site.
- **Perch movement:** Most perch moved little. In 1948, 50% of recaptures were in the original trap or the next one, 86% on the same beat, and 14% on a different beat. Only 58 perch (0.6% of tagged) moved more than 1.6 km, with the longest absence being 8 years.
- **Pike population estimates:** Tag-recapture estimates agreed well with [[Paloheimo Method]] estimates (e.g. 230 and 209 vs. 255 for females aged 3 in 1958). Discrepancies in 1954 (males) and 1963 (females) were attributed to poor growth and gillnet selectivity. Jaw tags proved reliable, with 64% of jaw-tagged pike recaptured versus 31% for opercular-tagged fish.
- **Pike growth:** Back-calculated lengths from opercular bones matched observed lengths (42 fish: observed mean 470 mm, back-calculated 462 mm, not significantly different; mean individual difference 10 mm).
- **Charr population:** In winter 1951–52, the three autumn spawning sites held approximately 2,000 male and 9,000 female charr; the spring site held about 700 males and 1,300 females. The lake likely contains at least 12 spawning sites, so no whole-lake estimate was possible.
- **Limitations:** Perch population estimates were unreliable due to handling mortality, tag loss and sex-selective trapping. Scale ageing for charr over 300 mm was unreliable.
- **Overall conclusion:** The marking experiments provided results that could not readily have been obtained otherwise, but interpretation depends on background knowledge of fish biology and behaviour.
