# Ecological Studies in South Benfleet Creek with Special Reference to the Amphipod Genus *Corophium*

#marine-biology #estuarine-ecology #corophiidae #benthic-macrofauna

## Executive Summary

This study examines the ecology of South Benfleet Creek, a small, embanked tidal inlet on the north bank of the River Thames in Essex, with a focus on the distribution of two amphipod species, [[Corophium Volutator]] and [[Corophium Arenarium]]. Fieldwork ran from 1 to 29 August 1959, with follow-up sampling into September and a pH measurement in December. The author concludes that substratum composition (the relative proportions of silt, clay and fine sand) is the chief factor governing the distribution of the two *Corophium* species, with water content a secondary factor. Salinity varies too little within the creek to limit distribution. Tidal disturbance, slope and black sulphide mud act as local factors.

## Background & Description

**Study site.** South Benfleet Creek (G.R. TQ 79 85) lies about 7 miles from the Thames mouth at Shoeburyness. It is just over 3 miles long, up to about half a mile wide at the mouth and about 80 yards wide near the bridge. Concrete and stone embankments line both banks because the surrounding land, including [[Canvey Island]] and [[Hadleigh Marsh]], lies below high water mark of medium tides. No freshwater tributaries enter the creek, though a small tributary runs on the north side of Leigh Island.

**Substratum.** The creek floor is generally estuarine mud, but it varies in detail. Soft, sticky muds lie away from the main channel. Firmer, cleaner sand banks occur at intervals, usually at small meanders, where swift incoming currents (about 3 ft/sec) prevent silt deposition while allowing sand to settle.

**Methods.** Samples covered 100 sq. cm to a depth of 15 cm, with two samples per station in the Table IV data. Water content came from core samples taken with a glass tube 5/8 inch in diameter and 10 inches long. Salinity was determined by silver nitrate titration of 10 ml seawater, with total salt taken from [[Knudsen Hydrographical Tables]]. Soil texture was analysed by a pipette method from a soil suspension, which the author describes as superior to that of [[Rees]] (1940).

**Macrofauna scope.** Only organisms retained on a 30-mesh brass sieve were included. The microfauna was excluded.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical and chemical conditions**

- **Salinity:** Salinity varied by only about 1‰ between Leigh Island, Benfleet Bridge and Westcliff Front (total salt about 27.5–32.2‰). Every station was below full North Sea salinity (33‰), reflecting fresh water from the Thames Estuary.
- **pH:** 7.7 on the Area G transect (Benfleet Bridge) and 7.6 on the Area B transect (Leigh Island), both at low water.
- **Water content:** Controlled by tidal exposure period, particle size, packing efficiency (major factors) and climate and slope (minor factors).

**Community and zonation**

The species list comprises about 14 taxa, including the polychaetes *Nereis diversicolor* and *Arenicola marina*, the bivalves *Scrobicularia plana*, *Mytilus edulis* and *Cardium edule*, the gastropods *Littorina littorea* and *Paludestrina stagnalis*, the barnacles *Balanus balanoides* and *Elminius modestus*, the crab *Carcinus maenas* and the two *Corophium* species.

Vertical zonation in the substratum was distinct:
- *Corophium* spp.: upper 5 cm
- *N. diversicolor*: generally to about 15 cm
- *S. plana*: predominantly below 15 cm

**Mussel bed (Area D).** This bed sits on soft mud beneath broken shells, mainly of *S. plana*, and anthracite fragments from an old cinder tip. Its surface is uncovered for about 7 hours per tide, compared with not more than 3 hours for the nearby unoccupied shelly deposit. This difference is offered as the explanation for the presence of surface species on one bed but not the other.

## Key Findings

**1. Species distribution**

- *Corophium arenarium* occurs only in Areas A, B, C, E and F, closely matching the sand banks. Its density in Area B was about 1,700 per m². It is restricted to flat bank tops and absent from soft mud near low water.
- *Corophium volutator* is found only in the upper part of the Area G transect, at about 2,000 per m² at station 4. It disappears rapidly on the firmer, sandier slope down to the stream.
- *N. diversicolor* is the most widespread species. It is scarce in the *C. arenarium* zone but abundant in very soft mud, with about 250 per m² at Long Transect station 2.
- *S. plana* follows a similar pattern to *N. diversicolor*, absent from the *C. arenarium* zone and most common in the softest muds.
- *Paludestrina stagnalis* appears only on the Long Transect, in considerable numbers, and avoids areas occupied by either *Corophium* species. This supports Hart (1930).
- *A. marina* is almost absent from the creek, though abundant at Shoeburyness. It occurs only in temporary pools on the Leigh Island side of Area B.

**2. Substratum composition (chief factor)**

- *C. arenarium* was found where silt and clay content was roughly 14.6–26.1%, giving firm, clean sand. Its habitat is summarised as 14–27% silt and clay, 76–88% fine sand, and water content of 24–28.5%.
- *C. volutator* was found where silt and clay was about 37.1–38.4%, giving soft, muddy substratum. Its habitat is summarised as 37–38% silt and clay, 61–65% fine sand, and water content of about 38–39%.
- Stations outside the *Corophium* zones had 39–57% silt and clay.

**3. Water content (secondary factor)**

- *C. arenarium* occurred only where water content was 23.7–28.3%. It was absent at Area B station 3, where water content was 31.6%.
- The station 4 result (28.1%, below the usual *C. volutator* range) was attributed to recent drying and sun-cracking after neap tides. The author suggests either wide tolerance of water conditions or that soil composition matters more than water content.

**4. Salinity (not limiting)**

- Experiments showed *C. arenarium* survived for 78.8 hours at 2‰ salinity, with optimum near 16‰. Its tolerance fell steeply between 24‰ and 33‰.
- It survived only 0.6 hours in fresh water, compared with 16 days for *C. volutator* (Hart, 1930).
- Because creek salinity varies by under 1‰, the author concludes that salinity does not govern distribution here.

**5. Other local factors**

- Tidal disturbance: *C. arenarium* was absent from the steepest part of each transect (for example, appearing at station 8 after a 13°17′ slope at station 6). Trampled areas were entirely depopulated by the following day.
- Black sulphide mud below about 5 cm excludes *C. volutator*, and *C. volutator* is never found in association with *P. stagnalis*.

**6. Limitations**

The author stresses that conclusions apply only to Benfleet Creek, cover a single month (August 1959), and need multi-year confirmation and testing at other estuaries.
