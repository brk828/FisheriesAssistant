# Draft Agenda: Lower Colorado River/Upper Gulf of California Ecosystem Research Planning Meeting

#marine-biology #aquatic-ecosystem #ecosystem-health

## Executive Summary

This draft agenda outlines a planning meeting held November 2-3, 1994, in Tempe, Arizona, convened by [[Conservation International]] and [[American Rivers]]. The purpose was to define a scientific and technical research project that would provide an objective analysis of the historic, current, and future ecological health of the [[Lower Colorado River]]/[[Upper Gulf of California]] ecosystem. The document is a meeting agenda, not a research report, so it contains no species data, sample sizes, or measured results.

## Background & Description

The document lists the participants and the planned discussion sequence.

**Organizers and facilitators**
- Conservation International: Abbe Reis (Coordinator, Mexico and Central American Programs), Alejandro Robles (Executive Director, Mexico), Debra Gordon (Legislative Programs), and Maria de las Angeles (Sea of Cortez Program).
- American Rivers: Dale Pontius (Director, Southwest Regional Office), Gail Peters (Associate Director), and Pam Hyde (Conservation Associate). Pontius and Robles facilitated the opening session.

**Invited scientists (11 named)**
- Mexican institutions: Dr. Jose Vincio Macias-Zamora and Cuauhtemoc Leon Diez of [[Universidad Autonoma de Baja California]], and Dr. Lloyd T. Findley (Guaymas, Mexico).
- United States institutions: Robert MacNish ([[University of Arizona]], Department of Hydrology), Robert Ohmart, Julie Stromberg, and Paul Marsh ([[Arizona State University]], Center for Environmental Studies), Edward P. Glenn (University of Arizona, Environmental Research Lab), Richard Felger (Director, Drylands Institute), Wendell Minckley ([[Arizona State University]], Department of Zoology), and Don Thompson (University of Arizona, Department of Ecology and Evolutionary Biology).

The disciplines represented span hydrology, ecology, zoology, and water resource management, which fits the study's broad scope.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area is the lower Colorado River and the upper Gulf of California, a transboundary system shared by the United States and Mexico. The agenda names the following ecological and environmental themes for discussion:

- **Biology:** wetlands, riparian and aquatic habitat, and biota.
- **Anthropological:** indigenous communities.
- **Water quantity needs:** volume, timing, and quality of flows.
- **Water quality:** contaminants and salinity.
- **Restoration potential.**
- **Hydrology:** past and future "engineering" (human modification of river flows).

No species, population, or site-level ecological data are reported.

## Key Findings

The agenda sets out the following planning elements. These are the intended agenda items, not results.

1. **Study objective:** an objective, scientific analysis of the historic, current, and future ecological health of the lower Colorado River/upper Gulf of California ecosystem.
2. **Discussion of objectives:** each participant was asked what the study's objectives should be, which disciplines are essential, how an international team should be organized, and which issues need further research.
3. **Priority research issues:** wetlands and riparian/aquatic biota, indigenous communities, water quantity and quality, contaminants and salinity, restoration potential, and historic and future hydrology.
4. **Consensus step:** a session was scheduled to reach agreement on study objectives.
5. **Work plan:** the outline of a proposed work plan, timeline, and budget was to be discussed.
6. **Next steps:** the final agenda item, to be set at the meeting. No decisions, timelines, or budget figures appear in the document.
