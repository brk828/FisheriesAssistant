# Follow-up Questions on Pigeon Creek Reservoir Impacts to Loach Minnow and Riparian Habitat

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #[[Loach Minnow]]

## Executive Summary

This 1989 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Phoenix Ecological Services office) to the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests raises a comprehensive set of pre-consultation questions regarding a proposed reservoir on Pigeon Creek, a tributary of the [[Blue River]] in Arizona. The document identifies that construction of the reservoir would affect the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), a federally threatened species, triggering formal Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act. The correspondence also invokes the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] and [[Clean Water Act]] Section 404 concerns, given anticipated impacts to fish populations, nonnative species dynamics, sycamore/cottonwood riparian vegetation, and downstream hydrology and geomorphology of the Blue River system. A companion data sheet provides detailed engineering and hydrological parameters for the proposed Pigeon Creek dam and reservoir.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document frames the loach minnow's habitat within the **Pigeon Creek–[[Blue River]] system**, a Gila River Basin tributary network in eastern Arizona, and raises the following ecologically relevant unknowns and threats:

**Habitat & Hydrology**
- Current perennial flow extent and seasonal distribution in Pigeon Creek
- Baseline fish community composition, relative abundance, and spatial distribution in Pigeon Creek
- Presence, abundance, and distribution of nonnative fish species in both Pigeon Creek and the Blue River — a key concern given that impounded, stabilized flows often favor nonnative fish over native specialists like the loach minnow
- Potential for the reservoir to **stabilize flood flows and enhance summer base flows**, which could inadvertently create favorable habitat conditions for nonnative fish competitors/predators
- Downstream thermal and chemical alteration from reservoir outlet releases
- Reservoir stratification dynamics and duration, which affect release water quality
- Sediment trapping and reservoir capacity loss over time affecting downstream substrate/geomorphology

**Riparian Community**
- Extent of [[Sycamore-Cottonwood Riparian Vegetation]] within the inundation zone, and whether this habitat type is common or limited across the District/Forest
- Current wildlife use of the riparian corridor, and potential wildlife use if grazing impacts were reduced
- Cumulative impacts from associated development: campgrounds, all-terrain-vehicle use, road development, and increased recreational/ground disturbance

**Watershed-Scale Context**
- Cumulative hydrologic and geomorphologic alteration from existing and planned small reservoirs on other [[Blue River]] tributaries
- Contribution of Pigeon Creek flow to total Blue River discharge at their confluence, both annually and monthly, and how reservoir operation would alter that contribution

- Reservoir construction would affect the federally threatened [[Loach Minnow]], necessitating **Section 7 formal consultation** under the Endangered Species Act.
- Project would also require a **Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act report** and address **Clean Water Act Section 404** concerns.

**Ecological Risk Implications**
- Only modest flood attenuation (4%) at the 100-year event suggests limited buffering of extreme flows downstream, but base flow stabilization could still shift habitat conditions toward those favoring nonnative fish.
- Minimal base flow release (0.084 cfs) relative to calculated mean annual streamflow (1.66 cfs) indicates the reservoir would capture the substantial majority of Pigeon Creek's natural flow, raising concern for downstream perennial habitat available to [[Loach Minnow]] and other native fish.
- Sediment trapping (12 acre-ft/yr) implies altered sediment transport to the Blue River, with potential geomorphic consequences for downstream channel structure important to loach minnow habitat (which favors riffle/cobble substrates).
- Unanswered questions about nonnative fish stocking and existing distribution highlight a critical data gap for assessing cumulative threats to the [[Loach Minnow]] alongside habitat loss from inundation.

**Outstanding Data Gaps Identified by FWS**
- Fish stocking plans for the reservoir
- Baseline native and nonnative fish surveys
- Reservoir spill frequency/seasonality and outlet structure design
- Cumulative reservoir development across the [[Blue River]] watershed
- Riparian vegetation extent and regional rarity
- Wildlife use of affected riparian habitat under current vs. reduced grazing scenarios
