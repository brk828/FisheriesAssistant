# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program: Management Group Meeting Packet (1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This is an administrative packet from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Region 6, dated March 29, 1990. It announces an April 25, 1990 meeting of the Management Group of the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]]. It contains an agenda, a memo from Program Director John Hamill proposing a restructuring of the Technical Group, an example Columbia Basin committee charter, planning notes for an August 1990 river trip, and a short FY 1991 budget status note. It is a program-management document, not a research report, and contains no biological data.

## Background & Description
- **Meeting:** April 25, 1990, 9:00 am–4:00 pm, FWS Third Floor Conference Room, 134 Union Blvd, Lakewood, Colorado.
- **Leadership during absence:** Hamill is on a training assignment with Senator Tim Wirth from April 2 to August 3, 1990. John Green (Deputy Assistant Regional Director, Fish and Wildlife Enhancement) acts as Program Director, Laurie Mathews chairs the Management Group, and Angela Kantola handles administrative and technical matters, including the FY 91 work plan.
- **Agenda items:** Recovery Program update (FY 91 funding, "road maps", action items); reorganization options; Technical Group report; I&E Subcommittee report; Water Rights Acquisition Subcommittee report (Yampa and Colorado River activities); [[Section 7 Consultation]] updates (Flaming Gorge, Price San Rafael and Sandstone opinions); river trip planning; next meeting.
- **Attachments:** the March 26, 1990 reorganization memo, river trip notes and participants, and the FY 91 budget status.
- **Example charter:** the Columbia Basin Fish and Wildlife Authority's Anadromous Fish Production Committee (adopted 9-23-87) is attached as a model for new committee charters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The program concerns recovery of endangered fishes of the [[Colorado River]] basin. The only species named is the [[Humpback Chub]], at [[Westwater Canyon]] and [[Black Rocks]].
- Biology-related ad hoc subcommittees named: [[Gila Taxonomy]], propagation and genetics, standardized monitoring, and sediment.
- Water-rights projects named: the [[Yampa River]] Basin Reservoir Feasibility Study and the [[Little Snake River]] Water Rights Study.
- Proposed future subcommittee topics: native/non-native fish research, instream flow quantification, and sport fish management.
- Management context includes [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and pending wilderness and wild/scenic river legislation for Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon.

## Key Findings
**Five major concerns with the Technical Group (from Lee Mills' paper, which listed over 20 concerns):**
1. Technical experts, especially in endangered fish biology, have inadequate input.
2. Planning to guide recovery is insufficient.
3. Meetings are poorly planned, organized and run.
4. Members lack time to complete work.
5. Technical Group and Management Group are redundant in membership and agenda.

**Problems with the proposed BioRAG (biological review group):** no consensus that it can give objective, combined reviews; it plans to meet only two times a year with discretionary participation; and it would need a substantial time commitment that could harm field and research work.

**Hamill's recommendations:**
1. Abolish the Technical Group; the Management Group (or a Planning Subgroup) takes over planning (annual report, road maps, annual work plan), with Program Director staff support.
2. Create standing Biology, Water Rights and I&E Committees, appointed on technical qualifications, one official representative per agency, with charters modeled on [[Columbia River Technical Committee]] charters.
3. The Biology Committee would review studies, coordinate research, and prepare annual biology guidance. It would draw on Mills, Knight, Harris, Weiringa and Bennett, and include a mechanism to get input from field researchers while minimizing their meeting time.
4. Standing committees form ad hoc subcommittees for specialized, controversial or dispersed work, with paid participation of outside experts.
5. Adopt meeting rules: elected chair, vice chair and secretary; agendas three weeks ahead; background 14 days ahead; draft summaries within 10 days; final summary to the Program Director within two months; decisions and action items recorded.
6. Each committee should document its needed time, money and staff, and the Management Group should ask the Implementation Committee to commit the resources.
7. Make the 5-year road maps a high priority, technically sound first, with policy considerations applied later.
- Recommendations are to be finalized for the Implementation Committee's August 1990 meeting, possibly as a revised charter (original approved February 21, 1988).

**Columbia example charter (AFPC):** it provides oversight of fish production committees, decisions by consensus, agendas one week ahead, minutes within 10 working days, and monthly meetings on the second Tuesday.

**August 23–25, 1990 river trip (Westwater Canyon/Black Rocks):**
- 20 possible Recovery Program attendees from FWS, Bureau of Reclamation, Colorado, Utah, Wyoming, WAPA, environmental groups, water users and the Program Director's office.
- Invited guests might include the FWS Director, the Reclamation Commissioner, congressional staff and members, and press.
- Attendance is limited to about 25–30, with $75 per participant and a deposit by July 1, 1990.
- Support: an FWS fisheries team at Black Rocks and a Utah DWR crew at Westwater Canyon, both working on [[Humpback Chub]]; BLM/NPS discussion of wilderness and wild/scenic legislation; possible orientation session August 22 in Grand Junction.

**FY 1991 budget (Region 6):**
- FY 1990 budget was $624,000: $424,000 of Recovery funds plus a $200,000 congressional line item of [[Section 6 Grants]] to Colorado and Utah.
- The President's FY 1991 budget keeps the same Recovery and Section 6 funding levels as FY 1990, with no language or funds specific to the Colorado River Recovery Program.
