# Aphanius vladykovi: A New Tooth-carp from the Zagros Mountains of Iran

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Brian W. Coad]] describes *Aphanius vladykovi*, a new species of [[Tooth-Carp]] (Cyprinodontidae) from the upper reaches of the [[Karun River]] basin in the [[Zagros Mountains]] of Iran. The species is separated from its closest relative, [[Aphanius sophiae]], by a higher count of lateral line scales, distinct male and female pigmentation, and several statistically significant meristic and morphometric differences, although ranges overlap. The author proposes that isolation arose through [[Orogenic Events]] that formed the Zagros ridges and produced narrow, fast-flowing [[Gorges]] (tangs) that block gene flow between mountain and lowland populations.

## Background & Description

*Aphanius vladykovi* is named in honour of [[Vadim Vladykov]], who worked on Caspian Sea fisheries in Iran and supervised the author's graduate study of those specimens. Four other cyprinodonts were already recorded from Iran: *A. ginaonis* (a single hot spring near Bandar Abbas), *A. mento* (Tigris basin), *A. dispar* (Persian Gulf and Sea of Oman drainages and Baluchestan inland basins), and *A. sophiae* (Tigris, Persian Gulf and several internal basins). The new species is therefore the fifth Iranian member of the genus.

The holotype is NMC 79-0247, a 36.6 mm SL male collected 3 km west of Boldaji on 9 June 1977. Paratypes comprise 35 males (21.6–36.4 mm SL) and 16 females (23.5–40.2 mm SL) from the same pool, plus one male (30.3 mm SL) from a stream 3 km east of Boldaji. Comparative material of *A. sophiae* came from the Kor River basin, its type locality, and included several hundred metres-scale spring and qanat collections. Specimens of the *A. sophiae* type series (collected by Th. Kotschy, catalogued 1844) were examined but not measured because they had shrunk and decoloured.

Key diagnostic characters of *A. vladykovi*:
- **Lateral line scales:** 36–47, versus 32 or fewer in other members of *Aphanius* and in *Kosswigichthys*, and usually 30 or fewer.
- **Male dorsal fin:** much darker, with a wide central blue-black band; anal fin pale.
- **Female markings:** lacks the large basal caudal spot present in *A. sophiae*; has numerous scattered flank spots.
- **Scale rows around the caudal peduncle:** 20–25 (versus 15–20 in *A. sophiae*), indicating smaller, more numerous scales.

Teeth are tricuspid in a single row in both jaws (up to 21 in the upper jaw of *A. vladykovi*). The anal fin origin is enclosed by a fleshy sheath, most apparent in large females. The cephalic sensory pores are reduced to neuromasts, a character shared with *A. sophiae* and *A. mento* that defines the derived group informally termed "*Aphanius*" by [[Parenti 1981]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality:** a large, artificially dammed pool at about 2380 m altitude on a small plain at the eastern edge of the Zagros, near the Kuh-e Kukalar range (peak 4298 m). The pool was about 300 m wide, with specimens taken in shallow water about 40 cm deep over a mud and pebble bottom. It held abundant *Myriophyllum* and marginal rushes. On a warm, sunny afternoon the water was 29 °C, pH 6.5 and conductivity 0.2 millimhos.

**Second locality:** a slightly lower, mud-bottomed stream 3–4 m wide with pools up to 1 m deep, lacking bushes or trees. Water was 22 °C, pH 6.2 and conductivity 0.45 millimhos, with slow current and moderate *Myriophyllum*.

**Zoogeography:** Fossil cyprinodonts are known from the Oligocene of the [[Paris Basin]], the early Tertiary of the Aral Sea region during the [[Tethys Sea]] period, and the Miocene of Iran. Coad argues that the mountain populations probably arose with the post-Pliocene uplift of the Zagros, and that *Aphanius*, as small fishes with low dispersal ability, could not have colonised an already elevated range from the lowlands.

**Drainage evolution:** Following [[Oberlander 1965]], Zagros streams cut transversely across anticlines of Oligocene–Miocene [[Asmari Limestone]], producing tangs up to 2400 m deep with near-vertical sides rising more than 300 m. These clefts isolate upland populations, including *A. vladykovi* in the upper Karun.

**Comparisons with Anatolian forms:** High scale counts also occur in the genus *Anatolichthys* of southwestern Anatolia, where counts reach 55 through scale reduction. Coad notes that the high count in *A. vladykovi* is not due to reduced scale size and that the Iranian species is not living in unusually saline or chemically atypical water.

## Key Findings

- *Aphanius vladykovi* is a valid new species distinguished chiefly by lateral line scale counts of 36–47, plus pigmentation differences from *A. sophiae*.
- Meristic analysis showed 8 of 10 characters significantly different between the two species, and morphometric analysis showed significant differences in 13 of 21 characters. Sexual dimorphism was found in 10 of 21 morphometric characters in *A. vladykovi*.
- No meristic differences between sexes were found within either species.
- Shared traits with *A. sophiae*, including male flank bars and female flank spots, and reduced cephalic pores, support a close relationship. This relationship is described as "apparent," based on preliminary studies.
- Isolation is attributed to orogenic uplift of the Zagros and to tangs that block gene flow. The author judges dispersal from lowland populations into the mountains unlikely.
- The high scale count is not explained by the physiological adaptation seen in *Anatolichthys*.
- The author recommends further work on populations, noting that his conclusions rest on preliminary sampling and that Table counts for some specimens were incomplete.
