# Fishery Management Report: Green River, Colorado and Utah (1963)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This 1963 Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife report documents a three-day fish sampling survey conducted by Colorado State University field crews (under Joseph Banks, William B. Miller, and Mr. Babcock) along the [[Green River]] and [[Yampa River]] in Colorado and Utah. The survey aimed to replicate methodologies from 1961–1962 collections to allow direct comparison, sampling nine stations including [[Lodore Canyon]], [[Echo Park]], [[Split Mountain]], [[Island Park]], and [[Brown's Park]]. Sampling relied primarily on electrofishing (bank shockers and one boat-mounted shocker unit). The report is highly critical of survey execution, citing inadequate crew training, obsolete/limited gear (wading-depth-restricted bank shockers), and a "perfunctory" sampling effort concentrated in easily accessible, tourist-disturbed areas near campgrounds rather than representative habitat. Despite these limitations, the boat-mounted shocker proved disproportionately effective, capturing the only [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (squawfish) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (humpback sucker) recorded, as well as the majority of rare [[Roundtail Chub]] (Gila sp.) specimens, underscoring that deep-water habitat was systematically undersampled by wading-based methods.

## Background & Description

Limited morphological data are reported; most measurements pertain to catch size classes and hybridization rather than detailed anatomy:

- **Gila sp. (Roundtail Chub)**: Specimens ranged 2.5–4.25 inches total length in at least one collection (station 63-39).
- **Flannelmouth Sucker ([[Catostomus latipinnis]])**: One specimen documented as a clear morphological hybrid with an unspecified sucker species (likely bluehead sucker), suggesting natural introgression in sympatric catostomid populations.
- **Bluehead Sucker (referred to as *Pantosteus*)**: Noted as co-occurring with Gila in deep holes, indicating similar habitat preference for pool/run mesohabitats.
- No length-frequency, weight, or meristic data are provided for other taxa (carp, channel catfish, redside shiner, dace, brown bullhead, sculpin).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure**: Stations spanned diverse mesohabitats — blind side channels (Lodore I), main channel canyon reaches (Lodore II, Split Mountain), tributary confluence zones (Echo Park I/II at the [[Yampa River]]–Green River junction), and low-gradient bridge/park reaches (Brown's Park, Island Park). Depths ranged from under 1 foot in side channels/backwaters to 10 feet in main channel boat-sampled zones; widths spanned 15 feet (Lodore I side channel) to 600 feet (Brown's Park).
- **Water chemistry**: Recorded water temperatures ranged 54–59°F; conductivity measured 775–975 micromhos, indicating moderately mineralized water typical of the semi-arid Colorado River drainage.
- **Deep-water refugia**: Rare, large-bodied riverine species — [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, "squawfish"), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, "humpback sucker"), and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila* sp.) — were concentrated in deeper holes accessible only via boat-mounted electrofishing, indicating a strong habitat association with deep pool/run environments distinct from shallow, wadeable, disturbance-prone shoreline zones near campgrounds.
- **Community composition by station**: Catostomids (flannelmouth and bluehead sucker) and carp dominated most bank-shocked collections, while dace and redside shiner were locally abundant in specific side-channel/backwater habitats (e.g., Lodore I with 97 dace).
- **Anthropogenic disturbance**: Sampling at Split Mountain and Echo Park was concentrated in areas of frequent tourist disturbance near campgrounds, potentially biasing community composition data toward disturbance-tolerant generalists (carp, suckers) and away from rare native species occupying less-disturbed, deeper reaches.

## Key Findings

- **Total catch composition** across all stations (n=14 preserved samples + 2 unpreserved): Carp (25), Gila sp. (9), Bluehead Sucker (65), Flannelmouth Sucker (73), Humpback Sucker (1), Channel Catfish (12), Redside Shiner (113), Dace (44), Squawfish/Colorado Pikeminnow (1), Brown Bullhead (2), Sculpin (2). Flannelmouth sucker and redside shiner were the most numerically abundant taxa overall.
- **Boat-mounted electrofishing outperformed bank shockers**: At Split Mountain, the boat shocker took the entire catch, including the sole Gila sp. specimen from that site. At the Echo Park–Yampa confluence, the boat shocker accounted for 74% of the total catch, including the only squawfish and humpback sucker recorded in the entire survey.
- **Rare species restricted to deep water**: Extension of sampling into deeper holes (only possible via boat) was responsible for nearly all captures of Gila, *Ptychocheilus* (Colorado Pikeminnow), and *Xyrauchen* (Razorback Sucker) — species explicitly noted as rare in the study area.
- **Gear limitations**: Bank shockers could only sample wadeable water — approximately 20% of the river channel at Split Mountain — severely constraining habitat coverage and biasing catch composition toward shallow-water species.
- **Methodological critique**: The report's author (D.R. Franklin) concludes the 1963 sampling effort was compromised by untrained/unmotivated student crews, obsolete non-rectified A.C. bank-shocker technology, lack of gear diversity (no nets employed), and a failure to sample representative (non-disturbed) habitat. He explicitly cautions that if prior 1961–1962 collections were conducted under similarly deficient conditions, comparative significance across years should be treated with skepticism.
- **Hybridization noted**: One confirmed hybrid specimen was identified between flannelmouth sucker and another catostomid, suggesting ongoing hybridization pressure among native suckers in this reach — a phenomenon relevant to conservation of pure [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]] populations in the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Preservation/data handling gaps**: Fish collected at Mantle Ranch were not preserved due to lack of formalin and containers, resulting in permanent data loss for that station's community composition.
