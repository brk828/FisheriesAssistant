# Cui-ui Hybridization Study Interim Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #genetics #conservation-genetics

## Executive Summary

This interim report from the [[University of Nevada-Reno]] Department of Biology presents preliminary genetic analysis results examining potential hybridization between [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) and [[Tahoe Sucker]] populations. Dr. William Berg conducted electrophoretic analysis on 26 genetic loci across specimens collected from [[Sand Harbor]] ([[Lake Tahoe]]) and [[Pyramid Lake]]. The analysis examined fish previously classified as "hybrids" between these two catostomid species. Results indicate that specimens classified as hybrids are genetically indistinguishable from pure [[Cui-ui]], suggesting that true hybridization between these species, if it occurs at all, is extremely rare.

## Background & Description

The study examined three groups of fish: [[Tahoe Sucker]] specimens, [[Cui-ui]] specimens, and fish previously classified as "hybrids" between the two species. Samples were collected from [[Sand Harbor]] on [[Lake Tahoe]] and [[Pyramid Lake Bay]]. Specific specimen numbers included:
- [[Tahoe Sucker]]: OFO 1604, 1811, 1813, 1817, 21, and others
- Presumptive hybrids: 1805, 1808, 1818, 1822, 1835, and 1831

Genetic analysis employed protein electrophoresis examining muscle, liver, and eye tissue. Twenty-six presumptive loci were scored, including: AAT, Aconitase, ADH, CK, GL-Peptidase, LDH, MDH, ME, MPI, SDH, and TPI.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Cui-ui]] is an endemic species of conservation concern. Understanding potential hybridization with [[Tahoe Sucker]] populations has important implications for species management and conservation strategies. The study was conducted in collaboration with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) as part of ongoing [[Cui-ui]] conservation efforts.

The geographic context includes [[Lake Tahoe]] and [[Pyramid Lake]] systems in Nevada, representing distinct but potentially connected aquatic habitats for these catostomid species.

## Key Findings

1. **Genetic Distinctiveness**: Fourteen loci showed fixed or nearly fixed allelic differences between [[Tahoe Sucker]] and [[Cui-ui]], providing clear diagnostic markers for species identification.

2. **No Hybrid Evidence**: Six specimens previously classified as "hybrids" (selected randomly from a sample of 30 such classified fish) showed genetic profiles identical to pure [[Cui-ui]] at all 26 genetic loci examined. These fish possessed [[Cui-ui]] alleles and showed no [[Tahoe Sucker]] genetic contribution.

3. **Hybridization Frequency**: The analysis concludes unequivocally that the examined "hybrids" are not crosses between [[Tahoe Sucker]] and [[Cui-ui]]. If hybridization occurs at all between these species, it is a rare event.

4. **Alternative Hypothesis**: The report suggests these misclassified fish may represent [[Cui-ui]] that developed atypical morphology (ecophenotypy) due to different ecological conditions during development, rather than representing true hybrids or backcrosses.

5. **Future Work**: The investigator planned to analyze all remaining individuals (from the original 30 classified as hybrids) upon return from New Zealand in January 1990, with a formal report due by January 31, 1990.

6. **Methodological Note**: While the initial six specimens showed no hybridization, the report acknowledges that some other "hybrids" in the larger sample might represent true [[Cui-ui]]/[[Tahoe Sucker]] hybrids or backcrosses, though this would still indicate hybridization is rare given the random selection results.
