# A New Poeciliid Fish, Phallichthys tico, from Costa Rica

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 paper in the [[Los Angeles County Museum]] *Contributions in Science* (No. 77) by William A. Bussing describes *Phallichthys tico*, a new live-bearing poeciliid fish from Costa Rica. The species was collected in a swamp near Tilarán (1961) and in the [[Rio Puerto Viejo]] (1962). It is distinguished from its congeners by a prominent black blotch on the posterior dorsal fin, and by differences in the [[Gonopodium]] and [[Gonopodial Suspensorium]]. These differences led the author to propose an expanded generic definition for *Phallichthys*. The document also includes the journal's publication instructions, which are summarized only briefly here.

## Background & Description

The material is a formal taxonomic description. The holotype is LACM 2780, a male of 17.4 mm standard length, collected 14 June 1962 in the Rio Puerto Viejo, Sarapiqui drainage. Paratypes are held in the [[Los Angeles County Museum]] (LACM 2781, LACM 2782), the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ 180304), the University of Miami Ichthyological Museum and the [[American Museum of Natural History]]. The paratype series totals about 130 specimens from the two localities (LACM 2781: 20 males and 92 juveniles and females; LACM 2782: 12 males and 18 juveniles and females; UMMZ 180304: 3 males and 3 females).

**Diagnostic characters (values for *P. tico*, with *P. fairweatheri* and *P. amates* in parentheses):**
- Largest male standard length: 17.7 mm (30 mm, 40 mm)
- Largest female standard length: 32.9 mm (44 mm, 56 mm)
- Body depth, adult males: 25–30% SL (39–41%, 33–41%)
- Body depth, adult females: 28–34% SL (37–40%, 37–40%)
- Total gill rakers, first arch: 10–12 (20–25, 20–22)
- Dorsal fin rays usually 8; anal rays 10; pectoral rays 11; pelvic rays 6; principal caudal rays 13
- Lateral scale series 26 or 27; vertebrae usually 29

**General morphology:** The fish is robust but not deep-bodied, with a dorsally positioned mouth. The second and third pelvic rays of males are elongated and extend past the anal fin origin. Two rows of teeth occur in each jaw, with the outer row sharp, incurved and about twice the length of the inner row.

**Gonopodium:** Rays 3–5 of the anal fin form the gonopodium. Ray 3 bears long thin spines on its distal segments, and ray 4p carries retrorse serrae. A membranous knob resembling a bird's head sits at the tip. The gonapophyses and gonactinosts are described in detail. The first gonapophysis bears long distal uncini, which the author considers variable within species.

**Coloration:** Ground color is tan in life, with a reticular melanophore pattern that edges the scale pockets. Fins are clear, with a fleeting iridescent blue on the dorsal and anal fins. Females have one or two small irregular blotches above the anus.

**Etymology:** The name honours the Republic of Costa Rica, the only country where the species is known.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality (Rio Puerto Viejo):** A backwater of about 200 m² that was formerly part of the main river channel. It connects to the river at only one point and becomes nearly isolated in the dry season. Water depth averages under 0.5 m, with a soft mud bottom over 6 m deep that emits decomposition gases. The pool holds a rich plankton population, brown algal scum and no higher aquatic plants.

**Water chemistry (30 °C, 21 November 1962, 11:00 A.M., 5 cm depth):**
- pH 6.4 (reserve pH 7.3)
- Dissolved oxygen 1.9 ppm
- Free carbon dioxide 24.0 ppm
- Bicarbonate alkalinity 115.5 ppm
- Hardness 109.0 ppm
- Chloride 5.3 ppm

The acidity is attributed mainly to dissolved carbon dioxide. The main river, by contrast, is clear, soft, neutral and well oxygenated.

**Tilarán habitat:** A shallow, clear-water swamp covering several hectares. The fish occupies sections about 0.3 m deep and hides in dense grass when approached. The water temperature was 24 °C in the early morning, and no chemistry was measured.

**Associated fauna:** Co-occurring species include *Phallichthys amates pittieri*, *Mollienesia sphenops*, *Rhoadsia eigenmanni*, *Cichlasoma friedrichstahli*, *Brachyrhaphis episcopi* and *Rivulus isthmensis*.

**Behaviour and diet:** The fish stays close to shore, forms aggregations of 20–30 individuals just below the surface near grassy margins, and flees into vegetation at slight disturbance. Its diet is mainly plant material (unicellular green and brown algae and diatoms), with protozoans also taken. Small crustaceans were absent from gut analyses despite being plentiful at the Puerto Viejo site. In aquaria it accepts Tubifex worms and dry prepared food.

**Range:** The species is known only from two localities about 110 km apart, both draining to the Rio San Juan and the Caribbean. The author expects it to occur in the poorly known Atlantic-slope rivers of Nicaragua. It is sympatric in part of its range with *P. amates pittieri*.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Phallichthys tico* is distinguished by a black dorsal blotch on the posterior quarter of the fin in both sexes.
- **Genus expansion:** Differences in the gonopodial suspensorium, the uncini of gonapophysis I, and serration patterns on ray 4p require the generic limits of *Phallichthys* to be broadened. Revised ranges include body depth of 25–30% SL (moderate) to 33–41% SL (deep).
- **Variability:** Several characters previously used to separate species, including uncini position and paired versus unpaired serrae on ray 4p, are shown to vary within species and genera.
- **Affinities:** *P. tico* is the most specialised member of the genus and shows resemblance to *Poeciliopsis* and *Carlhubbsia* in its gonopodial and gonactinostal structures.
- **Habitat specialisation:** The species favours isolated, vegetated, acidic backwaters and swamps with high dissolved CO₂ and low oxygen, conditions unlike those of the main river.
- **Distribution:** Known from only two Costa Rican localities; the author anticipates further records.
