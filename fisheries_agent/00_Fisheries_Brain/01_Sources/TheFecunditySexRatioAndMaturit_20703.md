# Fecundity, Sex Ratio, and Maturity of Sauger in Norris Reservoir, Tennessee

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1958 study from the *Journal of the Tennessee Academy of Science* reports three life-history traits of [[Sauger]] (*Stizostedion canadense canadense*) in [[Norris Reservoir]], Tennessee: [[Fecundity]] (egg production), adult [[Sex Ratio]] by age, and [[Age At Maturity]]. The data were collected largely from [[Gill Net Sampling]] during a tagging study, with ovary material from 14 females analyzed for fecundity and 50 ripe females used for ovarian weight relationships. The main results are an egg-production regression, a female-biased sex ratio after age two, and maturity at about age two, with 50 percent maturity reached at 13.0 inches (males) and 13.5 inches (females).

## Background & Description

The [[Sauger]] is a percid fish that was classed under the genus [[Stizostedion Canadense]] in this period. Published fecundity estimates for the eastern sauger were few, based on small numbers of fish, and covered limited size ranges. The studies cited include:

- [[Langlois 1939]]: 32,312 eggs from one 13.1-inch sauger in [[Lake Erie]] (Ohio fisheries report).
- [[Carlander 1942]]: an average of about 45,000 eggs from three 14.0–14.2-inch sauger in [[Lake Of The Woods]], Minnesota.
- [[Smith 1941]]: 36,000 eggs from an 18-ounce fish, 41,000 from a 26-ounce fish, and an average of 78,000 from two 36-ounce fish.

Maturity data from other waters were also cited. [[Deason 1933]] reported maturity at 9.5 inches (males) and 11.5 inches (females) in Lake Erie. [[Hart 1928]] found the smallest mature female in [[Lake Nipigon]] measured 14 inches. [[Carlander 1950]] found spent females from Lake of the Woods were four years old.

The data were gathered during a tagging study on Norris Reservoir sauger and included fish collected between 1942 and 1951. The author's doctoral dissertation in Zoology at the [[University Of Tennessee]] included this work.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study population lived in a [[Tennessee Valley Authority]] reservoir. Sampling used gill nets of varying mesh sizes (1/2 to 3-inch bar mesh), which the author considered to reduce the chance of selective sampling. Fish were examined for sex, age, and gonadal condition. Ovaries were preserved in five percent formalin and processed by weighing and subsampling.

The author discussed whether sex-specific vulnerability to gill nets explained the sex-ratio pattern. He judged this unlikely, because a sexual difference in vulnerability should decline with age and size, and it did not. The one- and two-year-old age groups were underrepresented in gill-net catches, but the sexes at these ages were judged similarly vulnerable because size differences were not significant. The author favored [[Differential Mortality]] as the more likely explanation, suggesting males are less viable than females after the second year of life.

## Key Findings

### 1. Fecundity

- Egg counts were made on 14 female sauger ranging from 195 g to 1247 g (7 to 44 ounces; 11.3 to 19.0 inches total length).
- Estimated [[Egg Production]] ranged from about 9,000 eggs (7-ounce fish) to 96,000 eggs (2-3/4-pound fish).
- Regression of egg number on total weight of females (Y = total eggs, X = total weight in grams):
  - **Y = −3700 + 72.1X**
- Rounded for comparison with other species, the per-pound relationship is:
  - **Y = −3000 + 32,000X** (X in pounds)
- Figure 1 plots the regression against the empirical data.

### 2. Sex Ratio

Counts of males and females by age group (combined across years), with [[Chi-Square Test]] results for a 1:1 ratio:

| Age | Males | Females | Chi-square | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 29 | 44 | 2.68 | P<0.13 |
| 2 | 379 | 382 | 0.005 | P<0.95 |
| 3 | 690 | 801 | 6.75 | P<0.01 |
| 4 | 176 | 276 | 6.48 | P<0.01 |
| 5 | 49 | 112 | 23.88 | P<0.01 |
| 6 | 6 | 24 | 9.63 | P<0.01 |
| 7 | 1 | 2 | n/a | too few fish |

- Totals: 1,330 males and 1,648 females.
- Sexes were equally abundant at ages 1 and 2.
- Females were significantly more abundant at ages 3 to 6.
- The oldest fish captured were predominantly female.

### 3. Age and Size at Maturity

- Maturity was defined as the age at which 50 percent of fish are mature. Both sexes generally matured at the end of the second year of life.
- Males were a larger percentage mature than females during the first three years, suggesting a tendency to mature earlier, although the age of maturity was about the same.
- The minimum total length at which more than 50 percent of fish were mature was **13.0 inches for males** and **13.5 inches for females**.
- Maturity data were drawn from Norris Reservoir collections (1942–1951), with immature and mature counts by age group reported in Table 3.

### 4. Ovarian Weight

- Total and ovarian weights were recorded for 50 ripe females captured just before spawning. They were grouped in 50-gram total-weight classes (Table 4).
- The smallest ripe female weighed 195 g with ovaries of about 25 g. The largest weighed 1,247 g with ovaries of about 226 g.
- Regression of ovarian weight on body weight (Y = ovary in grams, X = body weight in grams):
  - **Y = 31.63 − 0.02855X + 0.00014665X²**

### 5. Conclusions

- Sauger in Norris Reservoir reach maturity around age two.
- Female-biased sex ratios after age two are likely due to differential mortality rather than gear selectivity.
- Fecundity increases with female body weight and can be predicted from the regression equations above.
