# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes December 1989

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

Minutes from the Desert Fishes Recovery Team meeting held December 7-8, 1989 in Phoenix, Arizona. The team addressed critical conservation issues for [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*), including population status, downlisting proposals, recovery plan revisions, and genetic management strategies. Key decisions included unanimous opposition to downlisting [[Gila Topminnow]], recommendations for emergency captive breeding of critically endangered stocks, and establishment of genetic management protocols prioritizing maintenance of distinct gene pools within native ranges.

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Recovery Team convened to address conservation and recovery efforts for endangered desert fish species in the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. Primary focus species included [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*P. o. sonoriensis*), and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon m. macularius*). The meeting involved representatives from multiple agencies including Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), U.S. Forest Service (USFS), New Mexico Game and Fish Department (NMGFD), California Department of Fish and Game (CADFG), and Arizona State University (ASU).

[[Gila Topminnow]] populations exist in both natural and re-introduced sites across Arizona, with related populations in Mexico facing increasing pressure from invasive [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). [[Desert Pupfish]] historically occurred syntopically with topminnows below approximately 3,000 feet elevation but not at higher elevations, with some exceptions in the Santa Cruz basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The meeting addressed critical habitat and ecological considerations for desert fish conservation. [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] historically co-occurred in lower elevation habitats (below ca. 3,000 ft) but were generally separated at higher elevations. The team discussed using biological discriminants such as hydrobiid snails as indicators for evaluating habitat permanence and suitability for re-introduction sites.

Genetic data presented at the meeting revealed distinct stocks requiring separate protective measures. The team emphasized maintaining original gene pools within their original ranges as primary conservation priority, with secondary focus on maximizing heterozygosity using waters from which species have been extirpated (e.g., lower [[Gila River]]).

Critical natural populations identified for emergency intervention included Bos Spring ([[San Carlos Reservation]]), Sheehy Spring (Santa Cruz County), and other sites at critically low population levels. The team noted continuing decline in both natural and re-introduced populations, with Mexican populations under increasing pressure from invasive [[Mosquitofish]].

The [[Bill Williams River]] populations (introduced) of both [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] were excluded from recovery consideration as they occur outside native range. Controversy surrounded Boghole regarding whether it should serve as native fish refugium versus local bass fishery.

## Key Findings

**Gila Topminnow Status and Management:**
- Team unanimously opposed downlisting at this time based on five criteria: (1) continuing decline in natural and re-introduced populations; (2) current recovery plan inadequately protects natural populations; (3) re-introduction criteria not well-fixed or realistic; (4) new genetic data demands protective measures for distinct stocks not yet provided; (5) Mexican populations under increasing pressure from [[Mosquitofish]] invasions
- Unanimous recommendation for emergency captive breeding from critically endangered stocks: Bos Spring, Sheehy Spring, and any others at critically low levels, as only alternative to extinction
- Consensus that original gene pools should be maintained within original ranges, followed by maximization of heterozygosity using extirpated range waters
- D. Hendrickson assigned revision of [[Gila Topminnow]] recovery plan including downlisting criteria
- Success dependent on active management program
- [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*P. o. sonoriensis*) populations to be incorporated into Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan and excluded from [[Gila Topminnow]] plan

**Desert Pupfish Management:**
- General agreement that pupfish and topminnow could be stocked together below barriers but not above, reflecting historical syntopic distribution below ca. 3,000 ft elevation
- Team unanimously recommends active pursuit of removing non-native pupfish from Boghole and replacement with native form
- Finley Tank (Audubon Society Research Ranch) stock to be removed by spring and replaced with *C. m. macularius*
- Recovery plan being drafted by Paul Marsh (ASU) and Don Sada under USFWS contract, draft completion by March
- Each persisting stock to be treated as distinctive entity and afforded protection in natural sites

**Genetic Management Strategy:**
- Primary re-introduction sites (refugia) to be supplemented by secondary sites
- Secondary sites may or may not be permanently maintained at specific places but must be maintained in numbers to ensure habitat heterogeneity and persistence of heterozygosity
- Multiple secondary sites provide not only genetic insurance but also protection against local or regional catastrophe
- Genetic work being conducted by Echelle (Oklahoma State) and T. Dowling (ASU)

**Administrative and Coordination Issues:**
- USFWS decision to deny permits for display populations despite recovery plan calling for public support through information and education programs
- No recorded action on Yaqui Fishes, spikedace, loach minnow, or Sonoran chub recovery plans despite professed ongoing work
- Recovery plans becoming outdated after 12-18 months, requiring return to authors
- Team members emphasized plans are blueprints for action being awaited by other agencies
- Concern expressed that National Park Service (Organ Pipe) not yet involved; USFWS to contact and solicit inputs
- Arrangements made for Lourdes Juarez (Centro Ecologico, Sonora) to meet with P. Marsh and D. Sada to incorporate Mexican considerations
- Minckley assigned task of establishing databases and literature archives for pupfish management documents
- Team leader Minckley tendered resignation, recommending search for new leader and thorough examination of team successes, failures, and prospects
