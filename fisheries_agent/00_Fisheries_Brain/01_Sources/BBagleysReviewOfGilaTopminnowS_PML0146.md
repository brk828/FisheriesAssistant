# Gila Topminnow Sites in Arizona: Provisional Extirpation Report (1995-1996 Field Season)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This review, written by B. Bagley, comments on a provisional extirpation report for [[Gila Topminnow]] sites in Arizona from the 1995-1996 field season. Bagley's information rests mainly on [[Field Surveys]] he took part in during 1989, so the data may be out of date. The review sorts sites into those meeting extirpation criteria, those not meeting them, and those recommended for restocking by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD). It argues for prioritizing restocking at sites with a high probability of success and raises questions about several sites, including [[Tule Creek]] and [[Sheep Spring]].

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Gila Topminnow]], a small live-bearing fish of the family Poeciliidae, native to desert spring and stream habitats in Arizona.
- **Survey basis:** Most site knowledge comes from Bagley's 1989 field surveys. Several sites were not visited because they had already been found fishless in two or more earlier attempts under old, unofficial criteria.
- **Criteria:** The [[Old Extirpation Criteria]] required two consecutive visits with no topminnow observed to declare a site extirpated. The review asks how many sites declared extirpated under this standard have since been found to hold topminnow again, to show how hard small populations are to detect.
- **Scope:** The report covers roughly 30 named sites, including springs, tanks, creeks and a galvanized tub. Many entries are marked "NO DATA" or "dry in '89".

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types:** Sites range from perennial springs (e.g., [[Government Spring]], [[Campaign Creek]]) and pipe-fed artesian tanks ([[Cottonwood Artesian Spring]]) to shallow, gravel-bed streams with few pools ([[Red Creek]]) and small artificial tanks.
- **Hydrology and drought:** Several sites were dry in 1989, a drought year. [[Tule Creek]] held the entire surveyed population in a pool "toilet bowl size" during the 1987 drought, which raised concern about genetic viability in very small populations.
- **Threats and constraints:** Documented problems include siltation (e.g., a pool silted in by 1996 and a site needing high maintenance), flooding ([[Campaign Creek]], [[Mud Spring Tank]]), damaged water pipes, cattle access, cold-related fish losses ([[Government Spring]]), and limited habitat.
- **Land status:** One site is on private land whose owner was not interested, and another is outside historic range. The [[Forest Service]] was reported as not interested in one site.
- **Mixed-origin stocks:** The [[Tule Creek]] population is of mixed origin, drawn from [[Monkey Tank]], [[Cocio]] and [[Bylas]]. The review advises against using these mixed stocks for restocking.

## Key Findings

**Sites meeting extirpation criteria**
- Bagley agrees that topminnow are gone from the sites he visited.
- He questions why [[Sheep Spring]] was not considered for dredging and restocking. It held some water even in the 1989 drought, and the nearby proposed restocking site [[Government Tank]] would make stocking and monitoring easy.

**Sites not meeting criteria, with high probability of success (priority for management)**
- [[Campaign Creek]]: supported a large population in the past.
- [[Cottonwood Artesian Spring]]: supported a large population but needs maintenance.
- [[Government Spring]]: supported a large population for many years and is easy to monitor.
- [[Spring-Fed Tank]]: reliable water, but needs maintenance.

**Sites with low probability of success but still worth stocking**
- [[Campbell Flat Spring]]: topminnow were never abundant and never occupied all available habitat; several factors limit the site.
- [[Red Creek]]: reliable water but a shallow stream with few pools; never supported a large population.
- [[Tule Creek]] (unnamed spring site): the whole population was found in a single pool during the 1987 drought. Bagley asks whether it can maintain a genetically viable population and advises against stocking it with mixed-origin fish.

**General questions raised**
- The status of [[Seven Springs]] and [[Cave Creek]] is unclear, and the review asks why neither is being considered for restocking.
- The review suggests stocking only pure-origin stocks.
- It asks how many sites declared extirpated under the old two-visit criterion have since yielded topminnow.
- [[Charfibois Spring]] (name as OCR-transcribed) is cited as an example of a site that may fall into this category.

**AZGFD recommendations for restocking (from the review)**
- [[Campaign Creek]]: topminnow present in about 3/4 mile of creek; prone to flooding; needs supplemental stock.
- [[Campbell Flat Spring]]: none seen in 1989; small population; marginal for restocking.
- [[Cottonwood Artesian Spring]]: topminnow abundant; pipe-fed habitat needs upkeep.
- [[Government Spring]]: water present but no fish seen in 1989 (possibly cold-killed); may need occasional supplementation.
- [[Mud Spring Tank]]: reported fishless; small habitat; would need dredging and protection from cattle.
- [[Red Creek]]: few topminnow seen in 1989; mostly shallow gravel run with few pools, so marginal habitat.
- [[Tule Creek]] unnamed spring: no fish seen in 1989; very limited habitat; uncertain whether it can sustain a genetically viable population.

**Site survey summary**
- Of the roughly 30 sites in the tables, most have entries of "NO DATA" or "dry in '89". Abundant topminnow are recorded only for a few sites in 1989, and one site reported mosquitofish (n=87) rather than topminnow.
