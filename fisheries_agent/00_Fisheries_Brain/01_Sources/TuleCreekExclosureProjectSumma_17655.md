# Tule Creek Exclosure Project Summary Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #conservation #riparian-management

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Land Management memorandum reports on the construction and initial effects of a riparian exclosure at [[Tule Creek]], Arizona, designed to protect habitat for the [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis) population. Construction occurred April-May 1991 by Arizona Game and Fish Department and BLM contractors, installing 1.61 miles of fence with water gaps. Within one month, dramatic vegetative response was observed, but unexpectedly impeded water flow through the lower exclosure sections. The lower water gap was temporarily reopened June 19, 1991 to provide livestock access while alternate water sources are developed.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) is a small poeciliid fish requiring riparian stream habitat protection. This project implemented Terms and Conditions from Biological Opinion 2-21-91-F-060 to construct a protective exclosure at [[Tule Creek]] in sections 28 and 29 (T. 8 N., R. 1 E.). 

The exclosure design included:
- 0.51 miles of fence by Arizona Game and Fish Department (upper and lower water gaps, walk-through pass, road gate)
- 1.1 miles of fence by BLM contractor
- Upper water gap: rebar panels suspended from cable
- Lower water gap: fence materials attached to pipe embedded in rock (anticipated to require periodic maintenance during floods)

Construction timeline: April 9 - May 24, 1991 (Arizona Game and Fish); April 22 - May 2, 1991 (BLM contractor). Helicopter sling operations moved materials on April 24, 1991.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Tule Creek]] supports a population of [[Gila Topminnow]] in riparian habitat subject to livestock grazing pressure. The site also provides habitat for [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (*Rana yavapaiensis*), garter snakes (*Thamnophis* sp.), and [[Desert Tortoise]] (*Gopherus agassizii*). The riparian vegetation community includes bulrush (*Scirpus* sp.) as a dominant component.

The exclosure was designed to exclude livestock while maintaining water flow through gaps, balancing habitat protection with livestock watering needs per agreement with the grazing allottee.

## Key Findings

**Construction Monitoring:**
- No [[Gila Topminnow]] mortality observed during construction activities
- No [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] mortality observed during construction
- No garter snakes observed at road crossings during construction
- Three [[Desert Tortoise]] individuals observed along fence line by contractor

**Vegetative Response (June 19, 1991 - less than one month post-construction):**
- Dramatic vegetation growth inside exclosure
- Bulrush (*Scirpus* sp.) increased from 4-6 inches to 36-40 inches height in some locations
- Photographic documentation obtained

**Hydrological Effects:**
- Vegetation proliferation impeded water flow through exclosure area
- Lower stream sections began drying up
- Upper exclosure section retained more water than pre-construction
- Effect not explained by evapotranspiration increase alone

**Management Response:**
- Lower water gap opened June 19, 1991 to provide livestock access
- US Fish and Wildlife Service and Arizona Game and Fish Department immediately informed
- July 10, 1991 visit: conditions unchanged; livestock and burros grazing vegetation but lower water availability unchanged
- July 17, 1991 visit: water beginning to flow through to lower portions (cause uncertain - system recharge vs. grazing reintroduction)
- Planning underway with Service to develop alternate livestock water source for permanent exclosure closure and livestock/burro removal
