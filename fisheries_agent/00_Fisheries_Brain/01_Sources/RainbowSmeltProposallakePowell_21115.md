# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Memorandum on Rainbow Smelt Introduction to Lake Powell

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #endangered-species-act #non-native-species

## Executive Summary

This October 24, 1991 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 2 to Region 6 addresses serious concerns regarding Utah's proposed introduction of [[Rainbow Smelt]] (*Osmerus mordax*) into [[Lake Powell]]. The document challenges the State of Utah Division of Wildlife Resources' interpretation of [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 consultation requirements and warns of potential adverse impacts on multiple listed native fish species throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The Service argues that the proposed action requires formal ESA consultation due to federal involvement through [[Bureau of Reclamation]] management of Lake Powell and potential effects on endangered species.

## Background & Description

The memorandum responds to a July 30, 1991 Summary of Comments from Utah regarding an "Assessment of a Forage Fish Introduction into Lake Powell." The proposed action involves introducing [[Rainbow Smelt]] as a forage species into [[Lake Powell]], a federal reservoir constructed and managed by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] within [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]].

The Utah [[Division of Wildlife Resources]] (DWR) contended this would be a State action not subject to Section 7 provisions of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. DWR's position was that consultation is only required when an action agency determines a proposed action would jeopardize listed species survival. Since they believed the smelt introduction would not constitute jeopardy, they saw no basis for consultation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document identifies multiple listed fish species potentially at risk from [[Rainbow Smelt]] introduction:

**Colorado River Basin species:**
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)

**Additional Arizona species at risk via [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) spread:**
- [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*)
- [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)

The Service emphasizes that non-native fishes are an important factor in the decline of listed [[Colorado River Basin]] native fishes. The memorandum notes ongoing formal consultation with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] regarding the [[Central Arizona Project]]'s potential to introduce or facilitate spread of non-native fishes throughout the [[Gila River Basin]], with a Draft Jeopardy Opinion already issued.

## Key Findings

**ESA Compliance Requirements:**
- The proposed introduction constitutes federal action subject to Section 7 consultation because Lake Powell is a federal reservoir managed by [[Bureau of Reclamation]] within [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]]
- DWR activities and equipment are funded through various federal programs including [[Wallop-Breaux]] and Section 6 programs
- Section 7 regulations require formal consultation if a proposed action "may affect" any listed species, regardless of whether effects are adverse or beneficial and regardless of significance level
- Substantial information indicates the introduction may have some effect on listed species

**Potential Ecological Impacts:**
- Long-range effects of introducing another non-native fish species will likely have adverse impacts on several listed fishes
- [[Rainbow Smelt]] has potential to spread throughout large portions of the [[Colorado River Basin]]
- Spread could extend to [[Gila River System]] via [[Central Arizona Project]]
- Multiple listed species populations could be put at risk across Arizona and the broader basin

**Legal and Policy Concerns:**
- Possible violations of Section 9 incidental take provisions may occur if introduction proceeds without consultation
- Failure to enforce ESA requirements could damage Fish and Wildlife Service credibility
- Non-compliance could undermine ongoing efforts to stop decline of native [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes
- Service recommended serious consideration of withdrawing federal funding (Wallop-Breaux and Section 6 programs) if DWR will not comply with ESA requirements

**Interagency Coordination:**
The memorandum was distributed to multiple agencies including Arizona Game and Fish Department, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, Bureau of Reclamation, Glen Canyon National Recreation Area, Grand Canyon National Park, and FWS Field Supervisors in Albuquerque and Phoenix.
