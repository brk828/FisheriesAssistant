# Western Water Bulletin 1992: Flow of the Colorado River and Other Western Boundary Streams

#hydrology #water-quality #transboundary-water #colorado-river

## Executive Summary

This is the thirty-third annual Western Water Bulletin, issued by the [[International Boundary and Water Commission]] (IBWC) of the United States and Mexico. It reports streamflow, reservoir storage, water quality, climatological and irrigation data for 1992 on the western boundary streams: the [[Colorado River]] (below Imperial Dam to the Gulf of California), the [[Tijuana River]], the [[Santa Cruz River]], the [[San Pedro River]], [[Whitewater Draw]], the [[Alamo and New Rivers]] and the [[Salton Sea]]. The bulletin is a compilation of gage records, tables and maps rather than a research study, so it contains no fish or biological sampling. Its aquatic content concerns flow, stage, salinity, temperature and chemistry.

## Background & Description

The bulletin was prepared jointly by the U.S. and Mexican sections of the IBWC, with the Colorado River Basin data supplied by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] and Mexican agencies, and the Tijuana, Santa Cruz and San Pedro data gathered by the U.S. Geological Survey and the Mexican Comisión Nacional del Agua. Its tables cover daily mean discharge and gage height at numbered stations, annual and period-of-record summaries, reservoir contents, precipitation, evaporation, air temperature, specific conductance and chemical analyses.

Unit conversion tables are included for metric and U.S. customary units. The colorado river is regulated by [[Hoover Dam]] (Lake Mead), [[Davis Dam]] and [[Parker Dam]]. Diversions run to the [[All-American Canal]], [[Mexicali Valley]] and the [[Yuma Project]]. The Tijuana basin is regulated by [[Barrett Reservoir]], [[Morena Reservoir]] and [[Rodríguez Reservoir]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bulletin contains no species lists, fish surveys or invertebrate data. Its environmental content is limited to physical and chemical conditions:

- **Colorado River basin:** the drainage area is about 637,100 square kilometers. Irrigated area totals about 0.24 million hectares in the United States and about 0.197 million hectares in Mexico. Salinity and temperature are measured at the Colorado River stations and at the Mexican diversion points.
- **Salton Sea:** the sea lies in a closed basin of 21,652 square kilometers. Surface elevation is recorded below sea level. Water quality is sampled from inflowing drains and rivers.
- **Tijuana River:** the basin covers 4,483 square kilometers, about 27 percent of it in the United States. Tributary flow is largely regulated by reservoirs and diverted for irrigation.
- **Santa Cruz and San Pedro rivers:** flows are intermittent and dominated by monsoon-season runoff. Groundwater pumping and wastewater discharge affect base flow. The Nogales International Wastewater Treatment Plant discharges treated effluent into the Santa Cruz River.
- **Water chemistry:** chemical analyses of Alamo River and Tijuana River samples report metals (for example arsenic, chromium, copper, lead, zinc, iron, manganese), nutrients (nitrate, phosphate), total dissolved solids, and specific conductance in the Colorado and Mexican irrigation canals.

## Key Findings

- **Colorado River at Imperial Dam (1992):** the Mexican delivery totaled 1,450,231 thousand cubic meters in the schedule referred to in the text, and the river flows to the Gulf of California. Mean annual discharge is given in the bulletin tables; regulated releases come mainly from Hoover Dam.
- **Lake Mead storage and releases** are summarized in the Colorado River tables. Records are shown from 1935 onward.
- **Specific conductance (salinity)** of Colorado River samples at the International Boundary is reported monthly, typically in the 1,300 to 1,700 microsiemens/cm range in the samples presented.
- **Tijuana River:** Tijuana River at the International Boundary has mean flows that are very low, and in many months the flow is zero. Dry-weather flow is dominated by irrigation return and wastewater.
- **Salton Sea elevation:** the daily mean stage in 1992 ranged roughly from 69.2 to 69.7 metres below sea level, with the record low of the period given in the text as 69.28 metres below sea level.
- **Santa Cruz River at Nogales:** flows are highly variable, with mean annual discharge in the thousands of cubic meters and peaks during summer storms.
- **Climate:** the bulletin reports below-normal precipitation at several Colorado basin stations and a 1992 air temperature of about 23 degrees C at Yuma (long-term average shown in the tables).
- **Data scope:** the bulletin is a hydrologic and water-quality record. It does not contain fish, species or habitat-use data, and no biological statistics are reported.
