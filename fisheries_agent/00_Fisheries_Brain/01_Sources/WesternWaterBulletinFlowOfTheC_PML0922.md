# Western Water Bulletin 1992: Flow of the Colorado River and other Western Boundary Streams and Related Data

#hydrology #aquatic-ecosystem #transboundary-rivers

## Executive Summary

This is the thirty-third annual compilation (1992 data year) of stream discharge, stage, water-quality, climatological and irrigation data prepared jointly by the United States and Mexican Sections of the [[International Boundary And Water Commission]] (IBWC). It covers the [[Colorado River]] below [[Imperial Dam]] to the Gulf of California, the [[Tijuana River]] basin, the [[Alamo River]] and [[New River]], the [[Santa Cruz River]], the [[San Pedro River]], and [[Whitewater Draw]]. The document is a hydrologic data record rather than a biological study; it contains no fish or invertebrate survey data. 1992 was a drought-affected year for most basins, with runoff well below average on the Colorado, Tijuana and San Pedro systems, while the Santa Cruz and Alamo/New systems received above-normal precipitation.

## Background & Description

The bulletin was compiled by the US Section (El Paso, Texas; Yuma, Arizona) and the Mexican Section (Cd. Juarez, Chihuahua; Mexicali, Baja California). Stream gaging on the lower Colorado at Yuma began in 1902, and continuous gaging on the Tijuana River and its tributaries began in 1936. Each country operates the gages within its own territory.

Key hydrographic descriptions:
- **Colorado River below Imperial Dam:** flows are largely controlled by [[Hoover Dam]] (completed 1935), with regulation at Davis Dam (1950) and Parker and Imperial Dams (1938). Mexico's scheduled 1992 entitlement was 1,850,234 thousand cubic meters under the 1944 Water Treaty; no diversions were made to a substitute canal in 1992.
- **Tijuana River basin:** total drainage area 4,483 km², 27% in the US and 73% in Mexico. Formed by Cottonwood Creek and Rio de las Palmas; partly controlled by Barrett, Morena and Rodriguez reservoirs.
- **Whitewater Draw:** drainage area above the Douglas gage is 2,650 km²; flows to the Yaqui River and Gulf of California.
- **San Pedro River:** rises in Mexico; 1,919 km² drainage at the boundary, of which 1,681 km² lies in Mexico.
- **Santa Cruz River:** 1,380 km² above the Nogales station, of which 901 km² lies in Mexico; discharges into the Gila River southwest of Phoenix.

The bulletin also documents suspended silt sampling methods, chemical analyses and specific conductance of water samples, and a metric-to-English conversion table.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The text addresses hydrology and water quality rather than organisms, so no species, fish communities or invertebrate data are reported. Environmental context relevant to aquatic systems includes:

- **Reservoir regulation and drought:** Colorado River storage at year end was 27,015.9 million m³ (77% of usable capacity of 35,263.2 million m³), with Lake Mead holding 24,335 million m³. No shortages for irrigation were reported from drought or system accidents.
- **Salton Sea:** the water surface stayed about the same during 1992, ranging from 69.28 m (maximum) to 69.71 m (minimum) below mean sea level. The sea is a closed-basin sink receiving irrigation drainage from the Imperial and Coachella valleys and Mexicali Valley waste water.
- **Waste and drainage inputs:** the lower Colorado receives Wellton-Mohawk drainage via the bypass drain to the Santa Clara Slough, Yuma Project waste water, and Mexican canal waste; the New River carries Mexicali waste and sewage, and the Nogales and Douglas treatment plants discharge effluent to Mexico or the Santa Cruz River.
- **Irrigation:** 484,887 hectares were irrigated from the Colorado below Imperial Dam (287,566 ha in the US; 197,321 ha in Mexico, of which about 34% relies on groundwater pumping).
- **Climate:** 1992 precipitation on the lower Colorado averaged 17 mm at five index stations, against a 62 mm average (1959–1992). Barrett Dam temperatures averaged 19.0 °C, 2.6 °C above the 62-year mean.

## Key Findings

- **Colorado River at Imperial Dam:** 6,386,714 thousand m³ in 1992, about 63% of the 1935–1992 average (10,186,587 thousand m³).
- **Northerly boundary:** 1,790,863 thousand m³, about 37% of the 4,896,119 thousand m³ average. Peak instantaneous flow was 191 m³/s on March 9, 1992.
- **Southerly boundary:** only 27,808 thousand m³, under 1% of the 3,473,364 thousand m³ average.
- **Total Colorado flow entering Mexico:** 2,074,569 thousand m³, 38% of the 5,517,821 thousand m³ average.
- **Tijuana River runoff:** above Morena Reservoir 3,152 thousand m³ (about 25% of mean); above Barrett 4,828 thousand m³ (about 33%); at Rodriguez 9,538 thousand m³ (about 34%). Boundary flow records were incomplete.
- **Whitewater Draw:** runoff at Douglas was 538 thousand m³, about 8% of average, despite precipitation at 107% of normal.
- **San Pedro River:** boundary flow 5,999 thousand m³, 23% of the 1951–1992 average; precipitation 120% of the 531 mm mean.
- **Santa Cruz River:** Nogales runoff 29,329 thousand m³; roughly 28,515 thousand m³ (about 97%) was contributed by the reach in Mexico; precipitation about 159% of the 54-year mean.
- **Alamo and New Rivers:** New River boundary flow 176,622 thousand m³ (about 128% of the 1943–1992 average); precipitation at El Centro was 187 mm (about 267% of average).
- **Salton Sea:** stable surface elevation, with a 0.43 m seasonal range between the maximum and minimum stages recorded.
- **Storage:** Lake Mead, Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu together held 27,015.9 million m³ at year end.
- **Water quality:** the bulletin reports specific conductance and chemical analyses (including metals, nutrients and BOD) for samples from the Colorado, Alamo and New Rivers, but the tabulated values are not reproduced in summary form here.
