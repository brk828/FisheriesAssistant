## Executive Summary

This internal hatchery report from **Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery** (Boulder City, Nevada) documents early efforts (1974–1978) to develop captive spawning and rearing protocols for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large-bodied catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Broodstock were collected from [[Lake Mohave]] near Cottonwood Cove, approximately 26 miles above Davis Dam, and transported to the hatchery for artificial spawning, egg incubation trials, and larval/juvenile rearing. The study compares two egg-incubation methods, tracks early larval development and feeding transitions, and includes multi-year growth (length/weight) monitoring of juveniles and adults reared at the facility. The work represents foundational applied research supporting what would become long-term [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery and refugial stocking programs in the lower Colorado River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native range/habitat:** Large-river and reservoir habitats of the [[Colorado River Basin]], including [[Lake Mohave]], a mainstem impoundment between Hoover Dam and Davis Dam.
- **Spawning habitat:** Adults collected from Cottonwood Cove, suggesting nearshore or cove habitat use during the spawning period (late March).
- **Water temperature dependence:** Egg incubation and hatching success were highly sensitive to temperature; eggs held at a constant 53°F (Nest-in-jar method) suffered high mortality and fungal infection, while eggs incubated in shallow trays allowed to warm (56°F rising to 66–68°F) hatched successfully within 5–6 days.
- **Early larval diet:** Transitioned from endogenous yolk reserves to strained beef liver (hatchery diet) within the first week, then to natural zooplankton prey (cladocerans, copepods) and supplemental trout starter feed once moved to outdoor raceways.
- **Rearing environment progression:** Eggs/fry moved through three habitat stages — (1) hatching jar/tray in indoor hatchery tank, (2) hatchery rearing tank (2 ft deep × 15 ft long × 3 ft wide), (3) outdoor raceway (~8 ft wide, unspecified length, 3 ft deep) — reflecting a staged intensification protocol common in [[Conservation Aquaculture]] of imperiled catostomids.
- This work is a precursor to modern refugial and grow-out programs used for the [[Razorback Sucker]] as part of endangered species recovery efforts under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]].

## Key Findings

1. **Comparative incubation success:** Screened-tray incubation within a temperature-rising hatchery tank was far superior to constant-flow jar incubation at cold (53°F) river water temperatures; the latter resulted in total mortality within 8 days due to fungal infection compounded by low temperature.
2. **Hatch timing:** Under improved incubation conditions (56–68°F), eggs hatched within 5–6 days (April 3–4, 1974).
3. **Early feeding response:** Fry began actively feeding on strained beef liver just 6 days post-hatch, indicating rapid transition to exogenous feeding.
4. **Growth trajectory:** Fry grew from ~5–7 mm at hatch to ~10 mm by day 14, and to ~35 mm by day ~57 (5/30/74), demonstrating substantial early growth under raceway conditions with natural zooplankton supplementation.
5. **Water quality sensitivity:** Deteriorating water quality in the indoor hatchery tank necessitated early transfer of fry (at ~10 mm) to outdoor raceways, highlighting the importance of water quality management in intensive early rearing of [[Razorback Sucker]].
6. **Long-term broodstock development:** A subset of adults (32 of 40) was transferred to the Arizona State Hatchery, indicating early efforts to establish a distributed captive broodstock network for the species — a strategy that presaged later multi-facility refugial programs.
7. **Growth monitoring:** Multi-year length/weight data (through at least 1978) show juveniles/subadults ranging from roughly 210–600 mm in length and 90–2800+ g in weight, indicating substantial size variation among cohorts and years, consistent with variable-age year classes being reared simultaneously at the facility.
