# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Recommendations and Action Items

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinodontidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director documents recommendations and action items from their April 17, 1986 meeting in Tucson, Arizona. The team addresses critical conservation issues for multiple endangered desert fish species, including genetic contamination concerns with [[Desert Pupfish]] (Cyprinodon macularius), habitat renovation needs, experimental stocking protocols, and operational recommendations for [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Key concerns include removal of non-native genetic lineages, protection of native fish assemblages in [[Aravaipa Creek]], clarification of experimental stocking policies, and expansion of refugium and research capabilities.

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Recovery Team operates under the Department of Zoology at Arizona State University and provides technical guidance for endangered fish recovery efforts in the southwestern United States. The team met on April 17, 1986, to address multiple conservation priorities for desert fishes in Region II of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

**Species Addressed:**

- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (Cyprinodon macularius): Recently listed as endangered with critical habitat at [[Quitobaquito Spring]] (Arizona), [[San Felipe Creek]] and [[San Sebastian Marsh]] (California)
- **[[Mimbres Chub]]** (Gila nigrescens): Subject of experimental stocking proposal
- **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (Gila purpurea): Endangered species recommended for reintroduction
- **[[Woundfin]]** (Plagopterus argentissimus): Species requiring propagation technique development
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (Tiaroga cobitis): Proposed for listing, present in [[Aravaipa Creek]]
- **[[Spikedace]]** (Meda fulgida): Proposed for listing, present in [[Aravaipa Creek]]
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (Agosia chrysogaster): Used for biogeographic analysis

**Genetic Contamination Issue:**

Two populations of [[Desert Pupfish]] at Bog Hole (Santa Cruz County, Arizona, Coronado National Forest) and Research Ranch near Elgin (Santa Cruz County, Arizona, private land) originated from unauthorized stockings of experimental animals. Dr. Bruce Turner (Virginia Polytechnic Institute) confirmed through electrophoretic examination that Bog Hole fish are genetically identical to [[Quitobaquito Spring]] samples, indicating a different genetic lineage than native to the [[Gila River]] basin. Possible hybridization involving Gila and Sonoita River forms of C. macularius and potentially [[Pecos Pupfish]] (C. pecosensis) may have occurred in laboratory stocks of origin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**[[Aravaipa Creek]] System:**

[[Aravaipa Creek]] (Graham and Pinal counties, Arizona) supports one of the most diverse native fish assemblages in the Southwest, including [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] (both proposed for listing). No other stream of comparable size maintains such a large component of native fauna. Cattle-watering tanks in the drainage contribute non-native species to the system during high runoff periods, especially green sunfish (Lepomis cyanellus). While sunfish have not persisted in the main stream due to scour during floods, they present a continuing threat. Additional concerns include potential introduction of [[Red Shiner]] (Notropis lutrensis), known to interact detrimentally with native minnows.

Recent land exchanges between the Bureau of Land Management and purchases by Defenders of Wildlife have consolidated holdings near [[Aravaipa Creek]], presenting opportunities for removal of non-native fish populations.

**Biogeographic Evidence:**

Morphological and electrophoretic examination of [[Longfin Dace]] (Agosia chrysogaster) by Dean Hendrickson (ASU, now AGFD) provided evidence for derivation of the Willcox Playa basin's fauna from the Rio Yaqui, justifying transplant of [[Yaqui Chub]] to [[West Turkey Creek]]. Yaqui chub was reported from the stream (Morse Canyon) in the 1800s, but specimens were lost in the San Francisco earthquake and the record cannot be directly confirmed.

**Non-native Species Threats:**

Green sunfish from cattle tanks pose ongoing contamination risk to [[Aravaipa Creek]]. Construction of additional tanks and introductions of other species present more ominous possibilities, including [[Red Shiner]] and other deleterious species.

## Key Findings

**1. Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan Development:**

The team requests designation as lead for development of the [[Desert Pupfish]] recovery plan, citing their location in Region II as convenient for travel and interactions required to produce a viable and realistic recovery program.

**2. Genetic Contamination Removal and Replacement:**

The team unanimously recommends removal and replacement of non-native [[Desert Pupfish]] populations at two localities:

- **Bog Hole**, Santa Cruz County, Arizona (Coronado National Forest)
- **Research Ranch tank** near Elgin, Santa Cruz County, Arizona (private land)

**Justification:**
- Evidence indicates different genetic lineage than native to [[Gila River]] basin
- Possible hybridization with Gila and Sonoita River forms and potentially [[Pecos Pupfish]]
- Risk of non-native genome dispersing to invade other parts of [[Gila River]] basin
- Would compromise future regional recovery efforts

**Recommended Stock for Replacement:**

Material of C. macularius originating from Santa Clara Slough on the Colorado River Delta in Sonora, Mexico, is available from breeding stocks at Dexter, ASU, and Boyce Thompson Arboretum. This stock is morphologically similar to the original inhabitant of the Gila basin and is recommended for reintroduction. Both habitats have demonstrated ability to support closely related pupfish forms for ca. 1977 to present.

**Renovation Protocol:**
- Drawdown and application of fish toxicants
- Restocking with native subspecies after detoxification
- Renovation of two or three tanks upstream from Bog Hole containing other exotic species
- All sites are closed, artificial habitats that can be effectively and safely treated
- State-listed salamander (Ambystoma tigrinum stebbinsi) occupies Bog Hole area but rarely occurs with fishes and has terrestrial adult phase; populations will be influenced only locally and minimally, potentially enhanced long-term by removal of predatory fishes

**Specimen Preservation:**

Series of preserved, living, and frozen specimens of non-native pupfish should be collected for deposition in museums and/or ongoing research. Reference material will be invaluable if introgression into native gene pools from this non-native stock is suspected in the future.

**Implementation:**

Renovation and restocking efforts should be accomplished as multiagency cooperative effort involving FWS, Forest Service, AGFD, and university personnel. Preliminary communication with private landowners indicates willingness to cooperate.

**3. Aravaipa Creek Watershed Renovation:**

The team strongly recommends renovation of [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed (Graham and Pinal counties, Arizona). Cattle-watering tanks in the drainage contribute non-native species during high runoff periods.

**Recommended Actions:**
- FWS contact Bureau of Land Management (Safford District) and George Whittell Aravaipa Trust (Tucson, Arizona) to encourage action against non-native fish populations
- Survey to identify target ponds
- Progressive eradication plan beginning in headwaters and working downstream to avoid recontamination
- Great care to prevent toxicants from entering [[Aravaipa Creek]] in lethal concentrations
- Possible use of helicopters assigned for summer fire suppression during off periods
- Multiagency program with extensive FWS involvement

**4. Experimental Stocking Policy Clarification:**

The team unanimously requests clarification of pending "experimental, nonessential" proposal for introduction of hatchery-produced [[Mimbres Chub]] (Gila nigrescens) into native range in [[Mimbres River]], New Mexico.

**Concerns:**
- Such stocking could compromise remnant native stock as experimental animals become unidentifiable due to recruitment or migration
- Does stocking as experimental nonessential preclude use of that stream at later times or for other species in experimental essential manner or otherwise under full protection of the Endangered Species Act?
- Request for comments on internal policies discussed or adopted at Regional level relevant to experimental stockings of fishes and other listed organisms

**5. Yaqui Chub Reintroduction:**

The team recommends endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] (Gila purpurea) be stocked as soon as possible in [[West Turkey Creek]], Cochise County, Arizona.

**Justification:**
- Morphological and electrophoretic examination of [[Longfin Dace]] (Agosia chrysogaster) by Dean Hendrickson provided evidence for derivation of Willcox Playa basin fauna from Rio Yaqui
- Yaqui chub reported from stream (Morse Canyon) in 1800s (specimens lost in San Francisco earthquake)
- Stocking from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], which has surplus of Yaqui chub

**Implementation:**
- FWS initiate request to expedite project with Forest Service Regional Office in Albuquerque
- Solicit approval from AGFD and Coronado National Forest
- Survey at [[West Turkey Creek]] habitats before and after introduction
- Continuation of monitoring after stocking
- Dr. David Galat (ASU) has ongoing project on G. purpurea that should be modified to include survey and monitoring activities

**6. Dexter National Fish Hatchery Operations:**

Team members expressed concern that [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] operations are being dominated by production requests (greater numbers or larger sizes), causing research/development and refugium goals for critically endangered forms to be deemphasized. Facility is almost certain to be overtaxed to meet production demands for razorback suckers of 1.5 years age (10-12 inches total length).

**Recommendations:**
- Restate and redefine goals of research/development, production, and refugium
- Explore alternatives allowing increased emphasis and expansion of all three goals
- Utilize facilities, personnel, and expertise available elsewhere while maintaining Dexter as base of operations

**Specific Suggestions:**
- Transfer [[Woundfin]] (Plagopterus argentissimus) propagation technique development to recirculating raceways at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] with assistance from Dr. James Deacon (UNLV), who has vast experience and has succeeded in spawning the species in circulating systems
- Delegate maintenance of refugium stocks of pupfish to states or educational institutions for nominal fees with control over population sizes and heterozygosity maintenance
- Accomplish growout of some fishes to larger sizes using tanks or aquatic habitats on National Wildlife Refuge lands (preliminary exploration already occurred for Imperial, San Bernardino, and Buenos Aires refuges)

**Technical Guidance Committee:**

The Desert Fishes Recovery Team recommends appointment of technical guidance committee to advise and assist in planning and operation of Dexter Facility. Team has no major questions or criticisms of operations to date but offers suggestion as way to "fine-tune" an otherwise highly successful facility. They consider it a focal point of recovery in western portion of Region II and perhaps elsewhere. A flexible but specific long-term plan must be expedited to ensure continuation and improvement of its irreplaceable contributions.
