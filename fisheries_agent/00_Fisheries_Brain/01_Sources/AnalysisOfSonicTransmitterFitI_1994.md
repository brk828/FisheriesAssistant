# Analysis of Sonic Transmitter Fit in Abdominal Cavities of Subadult Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by Glen W. Knowles (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) as part of a larger [[Lake Mohave]] telemetry initiative, evaluated the minimum body size of subadult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) that could safely accommodate a standard sonic transmitter. Six preserved museum specimens were used to test whether a 16 mm × 60 mm, 6 g sonic tag could be surgically inserted into the abdominal cavity without inducing structural stress. The results established a minimum threshold standard length (SL) of 300 mm for safe transmitter implantation, informing surgical protocols for tracking [[Subadult Razorback Sucker|subadult]] individuals in the field as part of the broader [[Sonic Telemetry]] research effort led by the [[National Biological Survey]], [[Arizona State University]], and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].

## Background & Description
- **Specimens Examined:** Six preserved subadult razorback suckers from the Arizona State University Museum collection.
- **Measurements Taken:** Total Length (TL) and Standard Length (SL), measured to the nearest millimeter.
- **Sex Determination:** Based on the size and shape of the anal fin.
- **Pre-existing Incision:** All specimens had a ventral incision (45–60 mm long) located just right of the ventral midline, extending from within 10 mm of the right pectoral fin insertion to within 20 mm of the right pelvic fin insertion (a preservation artifact, not related to the experimental surgery).
- **Transmitter Specifications:**
  - Diameter: 16 mm
  - Length: 60 mm
  - Weight: 6 g
  - Type: Standard sonic tag used in [[Sonic Telemetry]] studies.
- **Size Threshold for Fit:**
  - **< 300 mm SL:** Extremely tight fit; tag strained abdominal walls and organs (unacceptable/high injury risk).
  - **≥ 300 mm SL:** Tag accommodated without abdominal stress (acceptable fit).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species Context:** The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large-bodied catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], now considered imperiled, with conservation-driven research focused on tracking movement and habitat use of both adult and subadult life stages.
- **Study System:** Specimens originated from Arizona populations, including:
  - ASU Research Park, Tempe, AZ (collected 09 Sept. 1987, P. Marsh et al.)
  - Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge (Rock Tank and Carrizo Dam Tank), Pima Co., AZ (collected 09 Dec. 1988, P. Marsh et al.)
- **Research Purpose:** This morphological/surgical feasibility study directly supports the [[Lake Mohave]] telemetry project (Mueller 1994), which aims to characterize [[Razorback Sucker]] distribution and movement patterns using implanted sonic transmitters, building on prior successful adult implantation studies (Tyus 1987; Valdez and Masslich 1988; Marsh and Minckley 1989).
- **Habitat Implication:** Establishing a safe minimum size for transmitter implantation ensures that field studies of subadult razorback suckers in reservoir and riverine habitats (e.g., Lake Mohave, Green River, Gila River) can proceed without compromising fish survival due to surgical injury.

## Key Findings
- Transmitter insertion was technically possible in **all six** specimens tested, but feasibility of insertion did not equate to safety of fit.
- Specimens **smaller than 300 mm SL** exhibited a tight fit that placed measurable stress on the abdominal walls and internal organs, indicating unacceptable risk of injury to a live fish.
- Specimens **300 mm SL or greater** accommodated the 16 mm × 60 mm transmitter without abdominal stress, indicating an acceptable, safe fit.
- **Conclusion/Recommendation:** A 16 mm × 60 mm sonic transmitter should only be surgically implanted in razorback suckers **≥ 300 mm SL**. Subadults below this threshold lack sufficient abdominal cavity volume to safely house this transmitter size.
- This finding provides a critical size-based surgical guideline for future [[Sonic Telemetry]] studies on [[Subadult Razorback Sucker|subadult razorback suckers]], reducing the risk of tag-induced injury or mortality in field-based tracking efforts.
