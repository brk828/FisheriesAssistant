#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Snyderichthys

## Executive Summary
This 1944 taxonomic paper by Robert R. Miller establishes **Snyderichthys**, a new monotypic genus for the leatherside chub, previously classified as "Gila" copei. Based on detailed examination of pharyngeal teeth and arches across 34 specimens, Miller demonstrates that this species does not belong in any of the six genera to which it had historically been assigned ([[Squalius]], [[Leuciscus]], [[Tigoma]], [[Cheonda]], [[Richardsonius]], [[Gila]]). The genus is named in honor of ichthyologist [[John Otterbein Snyder]]. The species is endemic to the Pleistocene [[Lake Bonneville]] basin and adjacent Upper Snake River and Wood River drainages.

## Background & Description
- **Diagnostic dental formula**: Only **four teeth** in the main row of each pharyngeal bone (bilateral symmetry), distinguishing it from other Gila-like fishes which typically show an asymmetric formula of five teeth on the left side and four on the right.
- **Observed dental formula variation**:
  - Dominantly **2,4-3,2** in [[Bonneville Basin]] specimens.
  - Dominantly **1,4-4,1** in specimens from the [[Little Wood River]].
  - Occasional abnormalities noted (e.g., 2,4-4,2 in *Squalius aliciae* types), with apparent tooth fusion in the second pharyngeal arch.
- **Gill membrane attachment**: Attached very close together, rather than comparatively wide apart as seen in true *Gila* species — a key generic-level distinguishing trait.
- **Absence of barbels**: Distinguishes *Snyderichthys* from [[Rhinichthys]] (including subgenus [[Apocope]]), [[Oregonichthys]], and [[Couesius]], the only other four-tooth biserial cyprinid genera in the region.
- **Absence of basal radii on scales**: Further differentiates *Snyderichthys* from [[Rhinichthys]].
- Comparison genus [[Oregonichthys]] noted as monotypic with very large, specialized scales — clearly distinct.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Freshwater river and stream systems in the western interior United States.
- **Distribution / Endemism**: Confined to:
  - The basin of Pleistocene [[Lake Bonneville]] (Utah, Wyoming, Idaho)
  - The [[Upper Snake River]] drainage (Idaho, Wyoming)
  - The [[Wood River]] drainage of western Idaho
- **Biogeographic significance**: *Snyderichthys* joins a small group of peculiar cyprinid genera endemic to the Bonneville and Columbia River drainage basins, including [[Ictichthys]], [[Mylocheilus]], [[Acrocheilus]], and [[Oregonichthys]] — suggesting a distinct evolutionary lineage shaped by Pleistocene lake basin isolation.
- No syntopic barbeled competitor genera ([[Rhinichthys]], [[Couesius]]) are closely related, indicating niche differentiation via dental and gill morphology within shared drainage systems.

## Key Findings
1. **Taxonomic revision**: The leatherside chub, historically shuffled among six genera (*Squalius*, *Leuciscus*, *Tigoma*, *Cheonda*, *Richardsonius*, *Gila*), warrants placement in a wholly new genus, **Snyderichthys**.
2. **Genotype designation**: *Squalius copei* Jordan and Gilbert is designated as the type species.
3. **Primary diagnostic character**: Symmetrical four-tooth pharyngeal main row (vs. the asymmetric 5/4 pattern in *Gila*) is the principal generic distinction.
4. **Secondary diagnostic character**: Close-set gill membrane attachment (vs. wide-set in *Gila*).
5. **Synonymy clarified**: *Squalius aliciae* is confirmed as a synonym of *Snyderichthys copei*.
6. **Geographic scope**: The genus is monotypic and narrowly endemic to the Bonneville Basin and adjacent Snake/Wood River drainages, reinforcing patterns of endemism tied to Pleistocene lake systems in the Intermountain West.
7. **Nomenclatural tribute**: Genus named for [[John Otterbein Snyder]] in recognition of his contributions to the study of western North American fishes.
