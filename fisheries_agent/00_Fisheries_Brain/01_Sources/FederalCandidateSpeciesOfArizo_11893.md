## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 regulatory candidate species list compiled by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service enumerating plants, invertebrates, fish, reptiles/amphibians, birds, and mammals in Arizona under consideration for listing under the Endangered Species Act. It is **not** a primary biological or morphological study, but a taxonomic/conservation-status inventory. No fish species appear in **Candidate Category 1** (highest-priority candidates with sufficient biological data to warrant listing). Ten fish taxa appear in **Candidate Category 2** (species for which listing may be warranted but data are insufficient to support a proposed rule), all native to Arizona's Gila River, Little Colorado River, Colorado River, and Bill Williams River drainages. The list is dominated by cyprinids (minnows) and catostomids (suckers), reflecting the endemic freshwater fish fauna of the arid Southwest. The document also situates these fish within a much larger candidate-species assemblage, including aquatic invertebrates (springsnails, riffle beetles, caddisflies) that co-occur in the same spring and stream habitats, underscoring shared conservation concerns across trophic levels in Arizona's desert aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description

The source provides no direct morphological descriptions, measurements, or diagnostic characters for any fish taxon — it is strictly a nomenclatural/status list. Morphological inference is limited to family-level ecomorphology implied by common names:

- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — small-bodied cyprinid; genus name suggests elongated fin morphology.
- **[[Mexican Stoneroller]]** (*Campostoma ornatum*) — cyprinid with the herbivorous, cartilaginous-jawed "stoneroller" scraping mouth typical of the genus *Campostoma*.
- **[[Little Colorado Sucker]]** (*Catostomus* sp.) — undescribed/undetermined sucker species restricted to the Little Colorado River drainage.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Catostomus* [*Pantosteus*] *clarki*) — subgenus *Pantosteus*, associated with a specialized ventral, cartilaginous-edged mouth for algae/detritus scraping on rocky substrates.
- **[[Zuni Mountain Sucker]]** (*Catostomus discobolus yarrowi*) — subspecies of mountain sucker, disc-shaped sucking mouth (per species epithet "discobolus").
- **[[Sonora Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*) — larger-bodied catostomid.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*, lower Colorado River basin population) — broad, fleshy ("flannel") lips characteristic of the species.
- **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*) — deep-bodied cyprinid chub.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*) — robust-bodied cyprinid with rounded caudal fin base.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*, Gila & Bill Williams Basins populations) — small benthic cyprinid with a subterminal, sucker-like mouth (genus *Rhinichthys*, "nose fish").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All listed fish are restricted to **inland freshwater habitats** within Arizona — springs, small streams, and rivers of the **[[Gila River Basin]]**, **[[Little Colorado River]]**, **[[Colorado River Basin]]**, and **[[Bill Williams River Basin]]**.
- Several taxa are defined at the **population/subspecies level tied to specific drainages** (e.g., Flannelmouth Sucker — lower Colorado River basin population; Speckled Dace — Gila & Bill Williams basin populations; Zuni Mountain Sucker — a drainage-restricted subspecies), indicating fine-scale endemism and vulnerability to habitat fragmentation, dewatering, and impoundment typical of arid-land streams.
- These species co-occur with, and share habitat degradation pressures alongside, a broad suite of Category 2 aquatic invertebrates also listed in the source, including:
  - [[Page Springsnail]] and [[Huachuca Springsnail]] (Category 1, gastropods)
  - Multiple *Pyrgulopsis* springsnails (Grand Wash, Kingman, Verde Rim, Montezuma Well, Fossil, Brown)
  - Riffle beetles (*Cylloepus parkeri*, *Heterelmis stephani*, *Huleechius marroni carolus*)
  - Water penny beetles (*Psephenus arizonensis*, *P. montanus*)
  - Caddisflies (*Protoptila balmorhea*, *Metrichia volada*)
  - The freshwater mussel [[California Floater]] (*Anodonta californiensis*)
- This co-listing pattern reflects a shared ecological niche: cool, perennial, spring-fed desert streams and their associated riffle/pool microhabitats, which are increasingly rare and threatened by groundwater pumping, livestock grazing, non-native species introduction, and stream diversion across the Southwest.
- Several fish (e.g., [[Gila Chub]], [[Roundtail Chub]]) are also currently or subsequently recognized as vulnerable to competition/predation from introduced non-native fishes, a common driver of decline in Gila Basin cyprinids and catostomids (though not explicitly detailed in this source).

## Key Findings

- **Zero fish species** were placed in **Candidate Category 1** in the January 1995 review — meaning no Arizona fish at that time had sufficient data to be a top-priority ESA listing candidate.
- **Ten fish taxa** were placed in **Candidate Category 2**, all belonging to two families: **Cyprinidae** (minnows/chubs/daces — 4 taxa) and **Catostomidae** (suckers — 5 taxa, including one undescribed species-level taxon).
- The genus ***[[Catostomus]]*** is the most heavily represented, with five distinct taxa (Little Colorado Sucker, Desert Sucker, Zuni Mountain Sucker, Sonora Sucker, Flannelmouth Sucker), highlighting the Catostomidae as a conservation-priority group in Arizona's desert rivers.
- Population-level candidate designations (e.g., Flannelmouth Sucker–lower Colorado River basin population; Speckled Dace–Gila & Bill Williams basin populations) demonstrate that ESA candidate assessments in this period were increasingly resolved below the species level to capture distinct, geographically isolated management units.
- Several fish on this list (e.g., **[[Gila Chub]]**, **[[Desert Sucker]]**, **[[Sonora Sucker]]**, **[[Speckled Dace]]**) are now well-documented flagship species for Southwestern freshwater conservation, and this 1995 candidate list represents an early regulatory milestone preceding their eventual formal ESA listing or continued monitoring.
- The overall document totals **124 animal candidates and 104 plant candidates** for Arizona, of which fish constitute a small but taxonomically concentrated fraction (10 of 124 animal candidates, ~8%), entirely within Category 2.
- No fish are marked with an asterisk denoting "addition/status change from the 1991 review" for **Longfin Dace**, **Desert Sucker**, **Sonora Sucker**, and **Speckled Dace** — indicating these four taxa had status changes between the 1991 and 1995 candidate reviews, while the other six fish taxa were carried over unchanged.
