# Larval Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in Lake Mohave, AZ-NV

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines larval [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. The lake's adult population is large and old, with no evidence of recruitment for several decades. Adults still spawn successfully and produce larvae, but few larvae were found in open water, and predation and food availability appear to shape survival. Predation impacts are likely significant but remain unquantified. The author is Daniel R. Langhorst of the [[Center for Environmental Studies]], Arizona State University.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]], a large catostomid (family Catostomidae).
- **Population status:** Adults are large and old. No recruitment has been documented for several decades, which makes larval survival the central question.
- **Larval stage:** Hatchlings measured about 7 mm. Larvae grew to 10-12 mm, then disappeared at about 12 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use:** Larvae were abundant in the [[Littoral Zone]] from mid-January into April 1984-85. Intensive [[Trawling]] failed to find larvae in the open waters of the lake.
- **Predators:** Stomach analyses of littoral and [[Epilimnion]] predators showed little consumption of significant numbers of larvae.
- **Diet:** Larval diets were dominated by [[Zooplankton]], mainly [[Bosmina]] and [[Daphnia]].
- **Backwater comparison:** Zooplankton communities differed qualitatively and quantitatively between the backwater and Lake Mohave, but larval diets did not. Greater food availability in the backwater may partly explain larger size and higher survival there.
- **Predation event:** A cohort produced by wild adults in a predator-free backwater reached about 20 mm within a month. After the backwater was breached, these larvae were eliminated by predation. Nearly 40% of invading [[Green Sunfish]] contained an average of 3.9 larval razorback sucker.

## Key Findings

1. **Reproduction occurs, recruitment does not:** Adults spawn successfully and larvae are produced, yet there is no evidence of recruitment for several decades.
2. **Seasonal abundance:** Larvae were abundant in the littoral zone from mid-January into April 1984-85.
3. **Growth and loss:** Larvae grew from about 7 mm at hatch to 10-12 mm, but disappeared at about 12 mm.
4. **Open-water absence:** Intensive trawl sampling did not locate larvae in open waters of the lake.
5. **Caging versus wild growth:** Caged larvae exceeded 30 mm, nearly three times the length of wild fish.
6. **Backwater survival:** In a predator-free backwater, the wild-adult cohort reached 20 mm in one month. Predation removed these larvae after the backwater was breached.
7. **Predation pressure:** Nearly 40% of invading green sunfish contained an average of 3.9 larval razorback sucker. Predation impacts are likely significant but have not been quantified.
8. **Diet and food resources:** Zooplankton (*Bosmina* and *Daphnia*) dominated larval diets. Differences in zooplankton communities between the backwater and the lake may help explain the backwater's greater larval size and survival.
