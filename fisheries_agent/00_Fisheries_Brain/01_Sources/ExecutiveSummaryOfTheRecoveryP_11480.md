# Executive Summary of the Recovery Plan for Cui-ui

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is a partially legible, OCR-degraded fragment of the recovery plan for the endangered [[Cui-ui]]. It includes the executive summary page, a cost table by recovery need, and the implementation schedule. The only extant population lives in [[Pyramid Lake]] in western Nevada. The text notes that the species was also found in [[Lake Winnemucca]], which dried up in the 1930s. The plan sets out five "Needs" (action categories), assigns responsible agencies, and projects annual costs from 1991 through 2011. The goal is to delist the species once recovery criteria are met.

## Background & Description

- **Status:** listed as endangered.
- **Distribution:** the only population is in Pyramid Lake. The text states it was "recently" also found in Lake Winnemucca, which dried up in the 1930s.
- **Life history (partly legible):** cui-ui live in Pyramid Lake for most of the year and enter the lower [[Truckee River]] to spawn in spring.
- **Habitat constraints named in the text:** access to spawning habitat is restricted by attraction flows and by [[Marble Bluff Dam]]. Spawning and rearing habitat is affected by water depth, water quality and availability. These factors are controlled by upstream water use and by point and nonpoint pollution sources. The text also mentions a facility in the area (details illegible).
- **Recovery objectives (fragmentary):** delisting criteria are tied to demonstrating that the population is persisting and to Truckee River inflow to Pyramid Lake at a minimum rate. A benefit of about 60,000 acre-feet/year is mentioned, but the surrounding context is illegible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The cui-ui depends on Truckee River flow and temperature for spawning migrations and larval rearing.
- Water rights, agricultural diversions and [[Newlands Project]] efficiency are central to the plan.
- Fish passage structures are targeted: [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]] and the [[Numana Dam Fish Ladder]].
- Toxic spill risk is addressed through determining potential sources and developing and implementing spill prevention and remediation plans.
- Hatchery support includes maintaining a rotating [[Brood Stock]] and evaluating hatchery effectiveness.
- Responsible parties named: Department of the Interior, [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[US Geological Survey]], [[Army Corps of Engineers]], State of Nevada, State of California, [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]], Sierra Pacific Power Company, Federal Water Master, and Washoe County water resource agency.

## Key Findings

**Actions needed (as legible):**
1. Need 1: secure cui-ui spawning inflow through improved Truckee River flow and water quality. This includes developing and implementing an operating agreement, improving Newlands Project efficiency, revising the [[OCAP]] (operating criteria and procedures), recouping water, securing Truckee Division and other water rights, retiring water rights from detrimental agricultural sources, and improving fish passage facilities. It also covers updating the hydrologic database and refining the relation between river discharge and cui-ui attraction, river flow and water temperature, cui-ui survival, and rearing habitat.
2. Need 2: conduct an evaluation of conservation measures and set priorities for proposed measures (a model is mentioned).
3. Need 3: use cui-ui monitoring (annual and five-year programs).
4. Need 4: manage cui-ui through rotating brood stock and hatchery evaluation.
5. Need 5: protect cui-ui from toxic spills.
