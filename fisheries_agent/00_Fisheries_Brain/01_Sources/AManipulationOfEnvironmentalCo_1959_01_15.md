# A Manipulation of Environmental Conditions Pertaining to Minor Jobs of a Development Nature in District I

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #limnology #Salmonidae #Catostomidae #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #aquatic-vegetation-control

## Executive Summary

This 1958-1959 Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report (Project F-7-R-1, Job D-1) documents four distinct freshwater fisheries management interventions in District I aimed at counteracting adverse environmental conditions affecting fish populations. The work encompassed: (1) mechanical aeration to prevent winter kill at [[Big Lake]]; (2) chemical piscicide renovation of [[Black River]] to remove an overabundant [[Mountain Sucker]] population and replace it with stocked trout; (3) comparative field trials of granular and liquid aquatic herbicides at [[Crescent Lake]] and [[Porter Springs]] to control nuisance vegetation; and (4) selective rotenone/seine removal of young-of-year [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Bluegill]] at [[Show Low Lake]] to protect the trout fishery. Despite intensive effort, the Big Lake aeration system proved inadequate against extreme ice/snow conditions, resulting in a substantial winter kill, while the other three interventions were reported as largely successful management actions.

## Background & Description

While this report is primarily a management/water-chemistry document rather than a morphological study, it includes limited size and demographic data relevant to the species involved:

- **Trout mortality sizing (Big Lake)**: 18 dead trout observed ranging from 7 to 15 inches in total length, indicating a mixed-age/size population was affected by the winter kill event.
- **Mountain Sucker population structure (Black River)**: Both young-of-year (fry) and adult life stages of suckers, principally [[Mountain Sucker]], were documented as heavily represented throughout the stream reach — fry were observed "in almost every still water area."
- **Black Bullhead / Bluegill (Show Low Lake)**: Only young-of-year (juvenile) age classes were targeted and observed in shallow shoreline schools; no adult size data reported.
- **Biomass estimates (Black River renovation)**: Sample-based extrapolation yielded estimates of approximately 5,350 lbs of rough fish (predominantly suckers) versus 220 lbs of trout removed across the treated reach, underscoring a heavily sucker-dominated biomass structure prior to renovation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Big Lake]]**: A high-elevation lake subject to seasonal ice-over (Nov 20, 1957 – Apr 12, 1958) and persistent snow cover, compounded by low water levels (16.5 ft below spillway) and dense aquatic macrophyte growth. These conditions created a classic **winter kill** scenario driven by light-limited photosynthesis, oxygen depletion, and hydrogen sulfide production under the ice.
- **[[Black River]] (Three Forks area)**: A coldwater mountain stream ecosystem where [[Mountain Sucker]] dominated the fish community, occupying still-water microhabitats favorable for fry recruitment, effectively limiting available niche space and food resources for stocked trout.
- **[[Crescent Lake]]**: Shoreline littoral zone habitat dominated by submersed macrophytes *Myriophyllum* sp. and *Anacharis* (Elodea) sp., representing nuisance vegetation competing for space/light in trout-supporting shallow zones.
- **[[Porter Springs]]**: A spring-fed pond ecosystem with dense emergent/floating vegetation (*Ranunculus* sp., *Lemna* sp.) and algal mats (*Cladophora* sp., *Spirogyra* sp.), representing a eutrophic-tending habitat requiring vegetation management to maintain fishery viability.
- **[[Show Low Lake]]**: A warmwater/coolwater mixed reservoir where shallow shoreline margins served as nursery habitat for young-of-year [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Bluegill]], species considered competitive or predatory threats to the lake's trout fishery.

## Key Findings

### Big Lake Winter Kill
- Ice cover persisted an unusually long 143 days (Nov 20, 1957 – Apr 12, 1958), with snow cover compounding light limitation after Jan 10.
- A 210 cfm air compressor supplying ~6,400 ft of 1-inch air line maintained ~8 acres of open water but operated only ~1,100 of 3,200 total ice-cover hours.
- Dissolved oxygen dropped to lethal levels in late February, producing hydrogen sulfide and killing at least 18 observed trout (7–15 in.); gill netting in April caught **zero** trout, confirming a significant kill.
- The aeration system inadvertently stirred bottom sediments, increasing turbidity and further reducing oxygen and light penetration.
- **Primary causal factors identified**: extended snow/ice cover (reducing light penetration — deemed most critical), low water level, and excessive aquatic vegetation.
- **Recommendations**: seek additional runoff/water diversion into the lake basin; reposition air lines to leverage wind-driven ice breakup and avoid sediment disturbance; intensify experimental herbicide control of aquatic vegetation.

### Black River Renovation
- Surveys revealed dense [[Mountain Sucker]] fry and adult populations throughout ~11.5 miles of stream (Crosby Crossing to Diamond Rock Lodge), with negligible catchable trout present.
- Treatment used **Pro-Noxfish** piscicide (1.5 gal/mile; ~20 gallons total), applied via drip method; toxicity was effective up to 5 miles downstream at a piscicide travel rate of ~4 mph.
- Estimated removal: **5,350 lbs rough fish** (mostly suckers) and **220 lbs trout**; sucker fry losses were uncounted but "certainly in the thousands."
- Restocked at 250 catchable rainbow trout per mile two days post-treatment; follow-up observations indicated successful suppression of rough fish recovery.

### Aquatic Weed Control Experiments
- **Crescent Lake**: Granular herbicide trial (10% active ingredient on attaclay carrier) tested five treatments — polychlorobenzoic acid (10 & 15 lb/acre), 2,4-D acid (10 lb/acre), 2,4,5-T propionic acid (10 lb/acre), and Simazine (5 & 10 lb/acre) — on *Myriophyllum*/*Anacharis* plots. Only the 15 lb/acre polychlorobenzoic acid treatment showed effect (~20% control of *Anacharis*; no effect on *Myriophyllum*).
- **Porter Springs**: Liquid desiccant "FB-2" applied at 2 ppm achieved complete algal control (*Cladophora*, *Spirogyra*) and ~90% control of *Lemna*/*Ranunculus*. A safety trial exposing rainbow trout to 15 ppm FB-2 showed **no adverse effects**, suggesting fish-safe application potential.
- **Recommendations**: continue granular herbicide trials at higher application rates; secure more FB-2 for expanded testing against *Anacharis* and *Myriophyllum*; evaluate mechanical/shading methods (black polyethylene sheeting, induced turbidity).

### Show Low Lake Selective Renovation
- Young-of-year [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Bluegill]] schools were targeted with localized Pro-Noxfish spray applications (2–3 ppm) in shallow shoreline zones, chosen for minimal risk to trout under prevailing temperature stratification.
- First rotenone treatment removed an estimated **68%** of observed bullhead schools; combined rotenone + seining (15×4 ft minnow seine) raised total removal to **~83%**.
- Bluegill kill was substantial but not quantitatively estimated due to manpower constraints.
- **Recommendations**: evaluate long-term effects of bullhead removal on remaining population dynamics; continue experimental selective control in 1959 with refined techniques to assess benefit to the overall trout fishery.
