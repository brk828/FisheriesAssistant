# Integrated Stocking Plan for Razorback Sucker, Bonytail, and Colorado Pikeminnow

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This March 2003 addendum, prepared for the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] by T.P. Nesler, K. Christopherson, J.M. Hudson, C.W. McAda, F. Pfitzer, and T.E. Czapla, merges the separate [[Colorado Stocking Plan]] and [[Utah Stocking Plan]] into one integrated plan for three endangered species: [[Razorback Sucker]] (first priority), [[Bonytail]] (second priority), and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (third priority). The integration was recommended at an August 2002 workshop on evaluating stocked fish in the Upper Colorado, Green, and San Juan River subbasins, which found the state plans inconsistent in the number of fish to stock, years of stocking, adult age, and number of adult age classes to maintain.

The integrated plan sets a goal of maintaining four adult age classes. This requires stocking razorback sucker and bonytail for six years and Colorado pikeminnow for eight years. Projected adult totals for each species are close to the recovery targets, and the plan includes production levels at two or three hatcheries.

## Background & Description

The document is a planning and administrative text, not a research study. It covers:

- **Introduction:** The 2002 workshop found the state plans inconsistent and recommended integration. The integrated plan is an addendum to earlier plans, intended to create consistency across the Upper Colorado River Basin.
- **State plans (Section 2):**
  - *Colorado* (Nesler 2001) used recovery-goal abundance plus a mortality buffer, survivorship curves, and three mature age groups maintained for three years. Its goals were to restore unoccupied reaches, augment very small populations, and establish self-sustaining populations.
  - *Utah* (Hudson 2001) was revised around recovery goals. It stocks only razorback sucker and bonytail and relies on other Program activities for instream flow protection, habitat restoration, and monitoring.
- **Integrated plan (Section 3):** The plan uses multiple adult age groups and a standard age at adult stage. Adopting four adult age classes instead of three reduces the number of fish that must be stocked each year. A 20% buffer is used for Colorado pikeminnow, based on average annual adult mortality at ages 7 to 10.
- **Production and facilities (Section 4):** The plan specifies hatchery output, stocking site selection, excess fish disposition, and fish health requirements.
- **Evaluation (Section 5):** Early evaluation relies on population estimates and nonnative fish control work carried out under other projects.
- **Appendix A:** The Program's guidelines for disposition of captive-reared endangered fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recovery priorities and reaches**

- *Razorback sucker* stocking targets the Colorado River (Rifle to Debeque Canyon, Palisade to Stateline), the Gunnison River (Hartland to Redlands dams), and, in the integrated plan, the Middle and Lower Green rivers. A redundant population is planned for the Lower Green River in case of a catastrophic event.
- *Bonytail* stocking targets the Middle Green/Yampa rivers in Dinosaur National Monument and the Colorado River (Palisade to Loma). The Lower Green River is also used as a redundant population because the species' life-history and habitat requirements are uncertain. Stocking bonytail near existing [[Humpback Chub]] populations was judged an "unacceptable" risk of hybridization.
- *Colorado pikeminnow* stocking targets reaches that no longer produce juveniles in the Upper Colorado River Subbasin. If stocked fish do not remain in those reaches, the effort will be re-evaluated.
- *Humpback chub* is not planned for stocking, but augmenting small populations may become necessary. Supplemental stocking may be needed in reaches such as Westwater and Black Rocks.

**Stocking and survival**

The document gives the following survival assumptions:

- Utah plan, razorback sucker: 0.5, 0.6, and 0.7 for ages 3, 4, and 5.
- Utah plan, bonytail: 0.3, 0.5, 0.6, and 0.7 for ages 2, 3, 4, and 5.
- Integrated plan, adult survival: 0.70 for razorback sucker; 0.70 to 0.85 for Colorado pikeminnow.

Acclimation is identified as the factor with the greatest potential to increase survival of stocked fish. Razorback sucker and bonytail should be stocked in floodplain areas where fish can overwinter, or in side channels that can be netted briefly before entering the main river. Colorado pikeminnow should be stocked into river backwaters.

**Size and season**

- Razorback sucker: at least 300 mm TL (age 2+), mainly in fall.
- Bonytail: at least 200 mm TL (age 2+), mainly in fall or late summer.
- Colorado pikeminnow: at least 150 mm TL (age 3+), mainly in fall.

Smaller fish overwinter until they reach stocking size. They are then culled from grow-out ponds and released in spring or summer. This gives the smaller fish more room to grow.

**Hatchery production (Section 4)**

- *Razorback sucker:* [[Grand Junction Endangered Fish Facility]] and [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] each produce 14,895 fish of 300 mm TL or more annually for 6 years. The document says the Green River portion would come from Ouray broodstock, but recent information shows fish moving between river reaches. It therefore suggests the fish will mix across the basin.
- *Bonytail:* [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] produces 10,660 fish for Utah waters, and the [[Mumma Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility]] produces 5,330 for Colorado waters, each for 6 years at 200 mm TL or more.
- *Colorado pikeminnow:* Mumma produces 2,250 fish of 150 mm TL or more annually for 8 years.

Table 8 lists survival and output by year for each facility. The document also notes that fish raised in Colorado for the Lower Green River must be sacrificed for diagnostic disease testing, so extra fish must be raised to cover this. Bonytail raised in Colorado must be treated for [[Asian Tapeworm]] before stocking.

**Excess fish**

Excess fish are those beyond production needs. The 1995 [[Excess Fish Disposition Policy]] (Stemple and Wydoski 1995) covers them. Options include:

1. Distributing excess larvae to riverside ponds, then tagging and releasing them.
2. Tagging excess juveniles and releasing them into floodplain wetlands.
3. Offering excess razorback sucker and Colorado pikeminnow to the San Juan Program. Excess bonytail go to the Upper Colorado River Basin, divided evenly among three populations.

## Key Findings

**Integrated targets and projected adults (Tables 2 to 4)**

- **Razorback sucker:** the target is 7,540 adults per population. Projections are 7,561 (Colorado plan), 7,541 (Utah plan), and 7,546 (integrated plan). The integrated plan uses four adult age classes, a 30% buffer, and 0.70 adult survival.
- **Bonytail:** the target is 6,160 adults per population. Projections are 3,636 (Colorado plan, with a miscalculation noted, originally reported as 5,7xx), 6,751 (Utah plan), and 6,161 (integrated plan). Colorado's figures were corrected, and the integrated plan matches the target most closely.
- **Colorado pikeminnow:** Colorado's target was 1,150 and the integrated target is 1,200. Projections are 1,158 (Colorado plan) and 1,205 (integrated plan). The integrated plan uses a 20% buffer, compared with 15% in the Colorado plan.

**Stocking requirements**

- Colorado plan: razorback sucker stocked at 5,480 per reach each fall for 8 years. Bonytail at 12,000 per reach for 7 years. Colorado pikeminnow at 1,340 per reach for 9 years.
- Utah plan: 18,500 razorback sucker per year for 5 years (goal of 5,800 by year 10). 5,427 bonytail per year for 5 years (goal of 4,500 by year 10).
- Integrated plan: razorback sucker stocked for 6 years, bonytail for 6 years, and Colorado pikeminnow for 8 years.

**Plan structure**

- The integrated plan standardizes adult age and number of adult age classes across species. This produces consistent buffers and reduces the initial annual stocking numbers.
- Colorado and Utah share stocking of bonytail to create one population each in the Middle Green and Colorado rivers.
- Colorado pikeminnow stocking is designed to re-establish populations in reaches where juvenile production has stopped.

**Management guidelines (Appendix A)**

- Captive-reared fish should be used for Program needs before any surplus is offered to other users.
- Unfit fish (such as hybrids or those with untreatable diseases) are euthanized using accepted fisheries protocols.
- Lead times are long: about 5 years to produce mature bonytail and 7 years for adult razorback sucker and Colorado pikeminnow. Stocking plans must therefore be prepared well ahead of field work.
- All propagation must follow the Program's genetics and stocking guidelines. Wild broodstock fish are returned to the wild once pedigreed family lots have been established.

**Evaluation and open issues**

- Initial evaluation will rely on population estimates and nonnative fish control work carried out under other projects.
- Later evaluation must measure spawning success and recruitment of subadults to adults.
- The Colorado pikeminnow stocking effort will be re-evaluated if stocked fish are not retained in the reaches where they are released.
