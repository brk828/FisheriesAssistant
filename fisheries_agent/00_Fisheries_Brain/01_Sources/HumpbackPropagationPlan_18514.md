# Humpback Chub Propagation Plan: Little Colorado River, Arizona (1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1993 request for review and permit authorization for a [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) propagation plan. The [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) proposed collecting reproductive products from the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) in [[Grand Canyon]] to rear larvae and juveniles for laboratory experiments on temperature effects, and to verify otolith growth-ring formation. The package includes an April 5, 1993 interoffice memo from [[Robert Clarkson]], the full plan written by [[Mark Lupher]], Diann M. Smith, and Clarkson, a cover fax, and an April 6, 1993 letter from C.O. Minckley, a fishery biologist with the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), addressed to [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]] reviewer Pat Ryan. Minckley recommended that the permit be issued. The main review question was whether to approve egg acquisition (the primary request) and, separately, transport of adult chub to a hatchery (contingent on egg acquisition failing).

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Gila cypha*, an endangered cyprinid (minnow) of the Colorado River system.
- **Range and status:** The Grand Canyon population is the largest of seven remaining population centers (USFWS 1990). It is the last remaining population in the Lower Colorado River Basin, since the species historically occurred downstream to below present-day Hoover Dam.
- **Decline:** Closure of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] in 1963 altered the river downstream. [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Roundtail Chub]] were extirpated from Grand Canyon. Concern was also expressed for [[Razorback Sucker]] and humpback chub.
- **Life history constraint:** Dam operations depressed spring–summer water temperatures. The plan states this likely prevents successful mainstem reproduction because of incubation mortality. The LCR, a perennial tributary, is the only known reproduction site.
- **Early life stage risk:** Movement of young chub from the LCR into the cold mainstem is thought to reduce survival and growth. Effects of cold rearing on Colorado Basin fishes have been studied poorly in Grand Canyon.
- **Prior work:** Adults were previously held in captivity, hormone-spawned, and used for culture experiments. Fertilized ova from the [[Black Rocks]] population were reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Three field spawning attempts on the LCR produced no live larvae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal regime:** The central ecological problem is the altered temperature regime of the mainstem versus the LCR. The experiments are designed to quantify how larval and juvenile mortality responds to thermal shock.
- **Hatchery site:** [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]], near Cornville, Arizona, maintains water at 18–20 °C year-round, which the plan judges suitable for rearing.
- **Field monitoring:** Water temperature, conductivity, pH, and dissolved oxygen were to be logged hourly with a Hydrolab Surveyor 3. Turbidity was to be measured twice daily with a Milton Roy Spectronic Mini-20 nephelometer.
- **Sediment:** Experiments were planned to test whether LCR sediment affects egg survival. Desilted LCR water and in-situ incubation were used for comparison.
- **Pathogens:** The plan identifies fungal spread on eggs, *Ichthyophthirius* (white spot), *Bothriocephalus acheilognathi* (Asian tapeworm), and *Lernaea* as concerns for handled adults and eggs.

## Key Findings

**Review outcomes**
- Minckley (USFWS) judged the revised plan likely to succeed. The plan had been simplified, qualified hatchery staff would conduct the work, and direct transport to Bubbling Ponds was seen as improving success.
- Minckley recommended two changes: preserve thermal-shock larvae in alcohol for otolith research, and return adults to the LCR once gametes are produced, or hold them at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], which has the expertise and facilities for endangered species.
- Minckley recommended that the permit be issued.
- Clarkson's memo asked reviewers to assess the egg-acquisition request and the adult-transport request separately, because the adult transfer depends on egg acquisition failing.

**Revisions after prior review**
- Reviewers said the earlier plan was too complex. Four procedures were dropped: transport of water between sites, the sperm diluent treatment, the LCR Heath tack and desilting assembly, and the egg transport treatment.
- Two treatments were kept to help diagnose failure: in-situ incubation in desilted LCR water and incubation at Bubbling Ponds.
- Reviewers also asked for defined personnel roles. Lupher, a fish culturalist with nearly 30 years' experience, became principal investigator. He is to set up field apparatus, lead the initial spawning, and accompany the first egg shipment to Bubbling Ponds. Clarkson and [[Phillip Hines]] stay at the LCR for later spawning and on-site incubation.
- The team planned a "dry" equipment run on April 6–8, 1993, and a field egg-acquisition attempt starting April 12, 1993, with [[Arizona State University]].

**Quantitative targets**
- Egg request reduced to about **10,000 ova** from **four or more females**, fertilized with sperm from an equal or greater number of males.
- Assumed **80% hatch** and **75% post-hatch survival through two months** yield about **6,000 larvae and juveniles** for experiments.
- Expected to handle only 4–5 females; no adult mortality expected from earlier experience.
- Contingent adult request reduced from **20 to 10** (5 females, 5 males), used only if egg culture fails in 1993.
- Genetic concern was judged minor because products are for experiments only. Gametes will be pooled across parentage to remove genetic effects on outcomes. No request had been made for refugium or F1 broodstock use.

**Spawning and incubation**
- Ripeness was checked by gentle flank pressure. Ripe males were required at 2–3 per female.
- Unripe females received intraperitoneal injections of acetone-dried carp pituitary at 4 mg/kg (0.4 cc/kg of a 10 mg/cc suspension), daily for up to three days. Final maturation was expected 20–24 hours after injection.
- Egg complements were kept separate by female to record fertilization, survival, and time to hatch.
- Eggs were split between a 5-gallon bucket in the river and an instream hatching box, with at least five boxes planned. Approximately 50% of eggs were to go by helicopter to Bubbling Ponds the same day.
- Eggs were double-bagged in 4-mil plastic, shipped with a 1/3 water and 2/3 oxygen fill, and acclimated over about two hours.

**Thermal shock experiment**
- Four treatment temperatures: **16, 14, 12, and 10 °C**, with a **20 °C control**.
- Design: five replicates of 20 fish per temperature, with observation up to 4 hours.
- Fish tested at 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 60, and 90 days post-hatch. A total of **3,500 fish** may be needed.
- Recorded: time to first, 50%, and last mortality; percent mortality at 4 hours; behavioral responses.

**Otolith age verification**
- Goal: confirm that growth rings form daily and determine when the first ring forms.
- Fish reared at 20 °C: five per day for the first week, then twice weekly for four weeks, preserved in 95% ethanol. Total **75 fish**.

**Long-term rearing**
- Remaining fish to be reared at 10, 15, and 20 °C to compare survival and growth. A full protocol was to follow.

**Contingency (adult broodstock)**
- Adults to be moved by helicopter in 80-gallon ice chests (maximum five fish per chest) with an oxygen bubbler, tempered over 1–2 hours, and held in circular raceways at Bubbling Ponds.
- Disease treatments: manual removal of *Lernaea*, 1.0 ppm Droncit for 24 hours for tapeworm, and formalin for *Ichthyophthirius*. The plan states 167 ppm formalin every other day for seven treatments, then 25 ppm weekly prophylaxis, as written.

**Personnel and equipment**
- Lupher, Clarkson, and Hines are the key personnel. Vitae are in Appendix A. A minimum of four experienced AGFD LCR technicians will assist.
- Equipment (Appendix B) includes 10 hoop nets (1/4" mesh), 6 live cars, 2 Hydrolabs, 10 Heath incubation trays, 10 hatching boxes, and 30 ten-gallon aquaria in the wet lab with two chiller units.

**Conclusion**
- The plan aims to obtain experimental humpback chub products, test causes of past LCR spawning failures, and provide data on cold-water effects on early life stages. The reviewers' main recommendation was approval, with the Dexter fallback for adults.
