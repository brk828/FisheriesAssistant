## Executive Summary
This 1994 news article from *The Phoenix Gazette* reports on a formal petition filed by three environmental groups—the Greater Gila Biodiversity Project, the Southwest Center for Biological Diversity, and the Biodiversity Legal Foundation—requesting that the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service designate critical habitat for the endangered [[Gila Trout]]. The petition sought protection for 764,800 acres across seven watersheds in Arizona and 744,080 acres across 18 watersheds in New Mexico. The article highlights the species' historical decline, current population status (~2,600 individuals in the wild), and ongoing threats from land-use practices, framing the petition as a necessary intervention beyond simple species listing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type:** Cold, high-elevation mountain streams within the [[Gila River]] drainage system.
- **Key Watersheds (Arizona):** [[Oak Creek]], [[Sycamore Canyon]], and the East Fork of the [[Verde River]].
- **Key Watersheds (New Mexico):** 18 unspecified watersheds totaling 744,080 acres.
- **Historical Range:** Native to Arizona, but extirpated from the state in the 1930s.
- **Community Interactions:** Negatively impacted by the introduction of the non-native [[Rainbow Trout]], likely via competition and/or hybridization—a common threat mechanism for southwestern native trout species.
- **Habitat Requirements:** The petitioners' "ridge top to ridge top" strategy implies that Gila Trout habitat quality is highly dependent on watershed-level integrity, including riparian vegetation and sediment/flood dynamics, rather than just the stream channel itself.

## Key Findings
- **Conservation Status:** Listed as an endangered species since [[1967]].
- **Cause of Extirpation (Arizona):** Livestock grazing and introduction of competing [[Rainbow Trout]] drove the species out of Arizona in the 1930s.
- **Cause of Ongoing Decline (New Mexico):** Continued habitat degradation from logging and grazing practices.
- **Population Trend:** Since 1988, 4 of 12 known Gila Trout populations have been eliminated entirely, with 3 additional populations drastically reduced.
- **Current Population Estimate:** Approximately 2,600 individuals remaining in the wild (as of 1994).
- **Expert Warning:** A 1992 report by Gila Trout experts cited a "distinct possibility of extinction," recommending a re-evaluation of existing protection efforts.
- **Regulatory Gap:** Advocates argued that despite its endangered status, the primary driver of decline—habitat loss—was not being adequately addressed by existing management.
- **Related Litigation:** The same coalition of groups concurrently sued the federal government to expedite protections for two native minnow species in the [[Gila River]] and its tributaries (Arizona/New Mexico), indicating broader concern for the aquatic ecosystem of the region, potentially including species like the [[Loach Minnow]] or [[Spikedace]].
