# Evaluation of Seth E. Meek's Contributions to Mexican Ichthyology

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This review by Robert Rush Miller (Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan; published in Fieldiana: Zoology, Vol. 69, No. 1, 1976) re-evaluates the systematics of the freshwater fishes described or recorded by [[Seth Eugene Meek]] in his 1904 monograph on the freshwater fishes of Mexico north of the Isthmus of Tehuantepec. Miller examined the type specimens (most paratypes as well as holotypes) of the new species Meek described and corrects nomenclature, synonymies, and locality records. The review serves as the systematic basis for Miller's forthcoming book on Mexican freshwater fishes. Many of Meek's collections are presented as irreplaceable because habitat modification has since destroyed much of the original fauna.

## Background & Description

- **Meek's fieldwork:** Expeditions in April–June 1901 and February–May 1903, the first accompanied by F. E. Lutz. Travel was by railroad because serviceable roads were lacking.
- **Publications:** A preliminary contribution (Meek, 1902), a biogeographic paper (Meek, 1903), and the definitive monograph (Meek, 1904), which lists the 1901 and 1903 localities and maps some of them. All material from the 1902 paper is included in the 1904 work.
- **Scope of review:** Taxa whose identifications differ from Meek's are treated in detail; generic changes are also included. Meek's introductory material (1904, pp. xxv–lxii) is not commented on. Families are followed in Meek's sequence for comparison.
- **Institutional abbreviations:** CAS-SU (Stanford University material at the California Academy of Sciences), FMNH (Field Museum of Natural History), UMMZ (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology), USNM (United States National Museum of Natural History).
- **Limitations:** Southeastern Mexico was inaccessible to Meek. This is the richest fish fauna of northern Middle America, in the [[Río Usumacinta Basin]], and some of his cichlid misidentifications from Veracruz and Oaxaca reflect unfamiliarity with it. Up to 10 *Cichlasoma* species were recorded living together in a single small lagoon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Meek recognized Mexico's biogeographic interest, arising from the mix of tropical lowland and temperate highland faunas and from elements unique to the country. He also saw the value of freshwater fishes for reconstructing former hydrographic connections.
- Many of Meek's localities have been degraded or lost through damming, water diversion for irrigation, excessive pumping and groundwater mining, pollution from the oil and sugar cane industries, and displacement of native species by introduced (exotic) competitors and predators.
- Several taxa are geographically restricted: [[Lampetra spadicea]] and its relatives in the [[Río Lerma Basin]]; a goodeid confined to the Mezquital basin; *Cyprinodon latifasciatus*, extinct in the Parras basin, Coahuila; and *Gila elegans* and *Ptychocheilus lucius* at the edge of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- Miller notes that the Mexican fauna shows several distributional anomalies. Some records, such as *Plagopterus argentissimus*, *Phenacobius*, and *Lepomis cyanellus* from Brownsville, were shown to be erroneous or to refer to species outside Mexico.

## Key Findings

**Overall**
- Meek's 1904 monograph contains numerous misidentifications, synonymies and locality errors that Miller corrects after examining type material.
- Miller's revision draws on about 25 years of his own Mexican fieldwork (1950–1974) and on unpublished studies by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and Myron Gordon on northeastern Mexican fishes (begun 1930).

**Taxonomic and nomenclatural changes (selected)**
- **Lampreys:** Meek's species now placed in *Tetrapleurodon*; the nonparasitic derivative *T. geminis* from the Río Lerma basin. Generic placement remains uncertain.
- **Gars:** Four species occur in Mexico: *Lepisosteus osseus*, *L. oculatus*, *L. spatula* and *L. tropicus*. *L. tropicus* is the only one that is not confined to eastern Mexico.
- **Catfishes:** Meek's "Siluridae" comprised Ariidae, Ictaluridae and Pimelodidae. *Galeichthys aguadulce* is transferred to *Cathorops*; *Conorhynchus nelsoni* to *Potamarius*; *Rhamdia oaxacae* is a synonym of *R. guatemalensis*.
- **Suckers:** *Catostomus sonorensis* and *C. conchos* are synonyms or geographic representatives of *C. bernardini*; *Pantosteus* is recognized as distinct, against the synonymy proposed by Smith (1966); the razorback sucker is *Xyrauchen texanus*.
- **Cyprinids:** Many names are synonymized or moved. Examples include *Algansea dugesi* and *A. rubescens* into *A. tincella*; *Gila minacae* into *G. robusta*; *Notropis macrostomus* into *N. amabilis*; *Notropis santarosaliae* into *N. jemezanus*; and *Hybognathus* records into *Dionda*. *Notemigonus crysoleucas* is excluded from the Mexican fauna because it has never been verified there. The species *Codoma ornata* is tentatively retained because it lacks the diagnostic features of *Cyprinella* and spawns upside down, a behaviour like that of *Pimephales promelas*.
- **Poeciliids and goodeids:** Meek's "Poeciliidae" actually covers four families (Cyprinodontidae, Goodeidae, Poeciliidae, Anablepidae). *Poecilia sphenops* in Meek's account is a mixture of *P. sphenops* and *P. mexicana*. *Platypoecilus variatus* is *Xiphophorus montezumae*.
- **Cichlids:** *Cichlasoma mojarra* is a synonym of *C. trimaculatum*. *C. labridens* and *C. bartoni* are both valid, and Meek's description of *C. bartoni* is a composite. *C. tenue* is probably *C. trimaculatum*, and its type locality may be wrong.
- **Gobies and others:** *Gobius parvus* is *Evorthodus breviceps*, which is the same as *E. lyricus*. *Gobius claytoni* is a valid species of *Gobionellus*. Meek's *Lepidopomus* is an unwarranted emendation of *Lepomis*.

**Notable distributional corrections**
- *Micropterus salmoides* does not inhabit the Río Pánuco–Tamesi basin and is not native south of the Río Soto la Marina basin.
- *Strongylura marina* has no valid freshwater records in Mexico; the freshwater needlefish records refer to *S. hubbsi* and *S. timucu*.
- *Cyprinodon elegans* is confined to Texas.

**Unresolved issues flagged by the author**
- Catfish taxonomy (Ictaluridae) cannot be settled until Mexican catfishes are collected with effective gear and studied in depth. Miller argues that gill-raker number, karyotypes and biochemical data are needed beyond barbel length, anal-ray count and pectoral spine ornamentation.
- The occurrence of *Couesius* in Mexico remains an enigma, since the genus is otherwise Canadian. The three syntypes of *Couesius adustus* contain at least two genera.
- The validity of *Yuriria* and whether *Hybopsis* should be combined with *Notropis* remain under study.
