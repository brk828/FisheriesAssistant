# Temperature Preference and Avoidance by Adult Razorback Suckers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

Bulkley and Pimentel (Utah Cooperative Fishery Research Unit, Utah State University) used an electronic [[Shuttlebox]] to measure the thermal preferences and avoidance temperatures of adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a rare endemic of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The final thermal preferendum was estimated at 22.9 to 24.8 °C. Upper avoidance temperatures ranged from 27.4 to 31.6 °C and lower avoidance temperatures from 8.0 to 14.7 °C. The authors conclude that water-control structures in the upper Colorado River should be designed to maintain summer temperatures between 22 and 25 °C if habitat preservation for this species is a goal.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is a rare, endemic catostomid of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Four native upper-basin fishes, including this species, have been formally listed as endangered because of declining abundance. Major causes include [[Impoundment]], water withdrawals, and effluent returns, with altered downstream river temperatures below large dams being especially conspicuous.

Experimental fish were offspring of a single female spawned in 1974 at the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Arizona). Seven hundred fish (15–30 cm) were transferred to the Utah Water Research Laboratory, Logan, in June 1980. Test animals were in their seventh year. Fish were acclimated to 8, 14, 20, or 26 °C, with temperature changed 1 °C per day. Fish were not fed during tests, and a columnaris infection (*Flexibacter columnaris*) was treated with [[Terramycin]]. Animals exposed to disease were excluded from testing for 21 days after treatment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors frame thermal requirements as central to predicting the effects of reservoir construction and water development. Final preferendum is generally treated as the optimal temperature for physiological functions and is considered innate and species-specific.

A key case study involves [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] on the Green River, Utah. After dam construction in 1963, mean summer (June–September) water temperature below the dam fell from about 18 °C to 6.8 °C, with annual post-impoundment temperatures between 1.7 and 10 °C. These values fall below the measured lower avoidance temperatures (8.0–14.7 °C). Razorback suckers were no longer captured for 105 km downstream, to the confluence with the warmer [[Yampa River]], where 1964 mean summer temperature was 16.9 °C and fish were still found.

Comparative data place the species within a wider group of freshwater fishes. The white sucker (*Catostomus commersoni*) had a final preferendum of 24.1 °C, and other native Colorado River fishes showed similar values, including [[Colorado Squawfish]] (25.4 °C), [[Humpback Chub]] (24.0 °C), and [[Bonytail]] (24.2 °C).

## Key Findings

- **Final thermal preferendum:** Fish acclimated to 20 and 26 °C gave the most reliable estimates, with mean modal preferences of 22.7 and 23.1 °C (mean 22.9 °C). The geometric mean across all four acclimation groups was 24.8 °C. The authors conclude the true value lies near 23 °C, within 22–25 °C.
- **Upper avoidance temperature:** Mean values by acclimation group were 27.4 °C (8 °C, n = 4), 31.6 °C (14 °C, n = 10), 27.4 °C (20 °C, n = 20), and 28.2 °C (26 °C, n = 20). The overall average was 28.6 °C. Fish acclimated to 14 °C avoided significantly higher upper temperatures than those acclimated to 8, 20, or 26 °C.
- **Lower avoidance temperature:** Values ranged from 8.0 °C (8 °C group) to 14.7 °C (26 °C group), averaging 11.8 °C. Lower avoidance temperature was positively correlated with acclimation temperature (P < 0.01, r = 0.39).
- **Diel pattern:** Day and night modal temperatures did not differ significantly, so the data were pooled.
- **Low thermal responsiveness:** Fish acclimated to 8 °C were sluggish. Of 19 fish, 10 died from overheating (maximum temperatures 30–41 °C), five did not move, and four operated the apparatus successfully. At 14 °C, six of 21 fish died or jumped out, and 10 operated the device. No mortality occurred at 20 or 26 °C.
- **Relation to other species:** The upper avoidance temperature averaged 3.7 °C (range 2.4–5.3 °C) above the final preferendum. This exceeds the roughly 2 °C offset reported by [[Coutant]] (1975), which the authors attribute to their method of measuring avoidance over the first 24 hours.
- **Management recommendation:** Summer water temperatures of 22.9–24.8 °C should be optimal. Temperatures above about 29 °C are likely to be avoided, and those below about 12 °C will also be avoided. Alterations outside these bounds should be avoided in new development projects if habitat preservation is a goal.
