# Speciation in the North American Genus Dionda (Teleostei: Cypriniformes)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #phylogenetics #biogeography #speciation

## Executive Summary

This study investigates evolutionary relationships and speciation patterns in the cyprinid genus [[Dionda]] using allozyme electrophoresis of 35 gene loci across all known species. The research addresses competing hypotheses of sympatric versus allopatric speciation to explain the unusual biogeographic pattern of sympatric species pairs distributed allopatrically with respect to other pairs. Results strongly support allopatric speciation driven by vicariant events (drainage captures, orogenies, desiccation) rather than sympatric speciation. The phylogenetic analysis reveals that the traditionally recognized [[Dionda episcopa]] and [[Dionda ipni]] are not monophyletic, requiring taxonomic revision. The study documents complex paleohydrological connections between interior Mexican basins, the [[Rio Grande]] system, and Gulf Slope drainages that shaped diversification over the past 65 million years.

## Background & Description

[[Dionda]] species are small cyprinid fishes inhabiting streams and springs in arid and semi-tropical regions of southwestern North America. The genus ranges from the Colorado River in Texas to the Rio Misantla in Veracruz, Mexico, with two species on the Pacific versant in the upper Rio Mezquital. Prior to this study, eight species were recognized (Hubbs and Miller 1977), though [[Dionda episcopa]] was known to represent a complex of forms and two undescribed species existed in the Rio del Tunal.

The genus exhibits a unique biogeographic pattern among North American fishes: pairs of species occur sympatrically, with each pair distributed allopatrically relative to all other pairs. Morphological similarities within sympatric pairs and their habitat preferences led to the "species pairs" nomenclature. This pattern suggested two competing evolutionary hypotheses: (1) multiple instances of sympatric speciation where sympatric members are sister species, or (2) vicariant speciation involving multiple ancestral lineages responding to similar geographic isolating mechanisms.

The study employed horizontal starch gel electrophoresis on skeletal muscle and eye/brain tissue from specimens collected across the range. Products of 35 presumptive structural gene loci were resolved using standard histochemical staining methods. Phylogenetic analysis used FREQPARS to calculate the shortest evolutionary tree using allelic frequencies from polymorphic loci and maximum parsimony. Outgroup taxa included [[Hybognathus regius]], [[Hybognathus placitus]], [[Campostoma oligolepis]], [[Campostoma ornatum]], [[Nocomis micropogon]], and [[Cyprinella camura]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Dionda]] species typically inhabit headwater streams, springs, and spring-fed systems in regions characterized by complex geological history. Many species show restricted distributions in isolated spring systems or upper tributaries, making them vulnerable to habitat alterations and extinction. The genus has experienced significant impacts from Quaternary desiccation events in interior Mexican basins.

The evolutionary history of [[Dionda]] is intimately tied to major geological events including: the Hidalgoan Orogeny (early Eocene) that formed the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]], isolating Coastal Plain from intermontane drainages; Pliocene-Pleistocene entrenchment and headwater captures by the [[Pecos River]]; middle Pleistocene connection of upper and lower [[Rio Grande]]; and extensive Quaternary desiccation of interior basins in the Bolsón de Mapimí.

Genetic variability measures revealed relatively low heterozygosity across populations. Mean heterozygosity ranged from 0 in some populations of [[Dionda mandibularis]], [[Dionda ipni]], and [[Dionda episcopa]] to 0.062 in San Felipe Spring [[Dionda argentosa]]. The mean number of alleles per locus averaged 1.15, ranging from 1.0 to 1.4. Percentage of polymorphic loci averaged 12.0%, ranging from 0% to 28.1% in [[Dionda erimyzonops]].

## Key Findings

**Phylogenetic Relationships:**
- Monophyly of [[Dionda]] is supported by four derived electromorphs: Cbp-1(b), M-Me(b), G6pdh-A(b), and G6pdh-A(c)
- [[Dionda]] and [[Hybognathus]] form sister groups, together sister to [[Campostoma]]
- The sister group relationship between [[Dionda]] and [[Hybognathus]] is supported by three synapomorphic characters: M-Aat-A(b), Ada(b), and Ldh-B(a)
- Six characters support monophyly of the [[Campostoma]]-[[Hybognathus]]-[[Dionda]] clade

**Taxonomic Revisions Required:**
- [[Dionda episcopa]] as traditionally recognized is paraphyletic and requires division into multiple species: [[Dionda episcopa]] (restricted to Rio Grande and tributaries above Devils River), [[Dionda serena]] (Nueces/Frio drainages), [[Dionda couchi]] (Rio San Juan), [[Dionda argentosa]] (Devils River and San Felipe Spring), an undescribed species from Rio Conchos, and an undescribed species from Guadalupe/Colorado rivers
- [[Dionda ipni]] is paraphyletic; populations from Rio Axtla represent a distinct species more closely related to the [[Dionda episcopa]] complex than to other [[Dionda ipni]] populations
- Two undescribed species from Rio del Tunal are confirmed as distinct

**Speciation Patterns:**
- In only one case (the two Rio del Tunal species) are sympatric species sister taxa, supporting sympatric or parapatric speciation
- All other sympatric species pairs have sister species distributed allopatrically, strongly supporting allopatric speciation via vicariance
- This pattern is consistent with the observation that approximately 70% of vertebrate speciation events involve strict allopatric speciation

**Biogeographic History:**
- The ancestral [[Dionda]] species likely inhabited rivers from the ancestral Rio Grande/Pecos east along the Gulf Slope to the Old Colorado River, evolving in allopatry from the ancestral [[Hybognathus]] (eastern counterpart) after the Cretaceous, probably in the Paleocene
- The Hidalgoan Orogeny (early Eocene) correlates with speciation separating Coastal Plain [[Dionda erimyzonops]] from intermontane [[Dionda mandibularis]], and Coastal Plain [[Dionda ipni]] from intermontane [[Dionda dichroma]]
- Within the [[Dionda episcopa]] complex, early diversification involved isolation of Colorado-Guadalupe species, then Nueces-Frio [[Dionda serena]], followed by northern forms ([[Dionda episcopa]] in ancestral Pecos, [[Dionda argentosa]] in Devils River)
- Southern members of the [[Dionda episcopa]] complex evolved in an extensive Old Rio Nazas system including Rios del Tunal, Aguanaval, Nazas, Parras Basin, Laguna de las Palomas, portions of Rio Conchos headwaters, and possibly Rio Lerma
- The Rio Axtla species likely evolved in the Old Rio Lerma Basin after Miocene isolation from the Old Rio Nazas-Rio Grande Basin, subsequently transferred to Rio Panuco by headwater stream entrenchment
- Extensive Pleistocene-Quaternary desiccation caused extinction of [[Dionda]] from interior Mexican basins (Bolsón de Mapimí, Rio Lerma)

**Paleohydrology:**
- Evidence supports the Old Rio Nazas outlet through the lower Rio Grande (Arellano 1951 hypothesis) based on sister group relationship of Rio San Juan [[Dionda couchi]] to western clade members
- Mid-Pleistocene capture of Rio del Tunal from Rio Nazas System by Rio Mezquital is well-corroborated by phylogenetic placement of the two del Tunal species
- Upper Rio Conchos [[Dionda]] represents a form from interior basins transferred via headwater capture, with the lower Conchos likely containing different species more closely related to [[Dionda episcopa]]
- Rio Moctezuma captured portions of Old Rio Lerma and other Mesa Central rivers, facilitating faunal exchange between interior and Gulf Slope drainages

**Alternative Hypotheses Rejected:**
- Tree length for imposed monophyly of [[Dionda ipni]] ranged from 399.821 to 409.172 versus 388.730 for the shortest tree
- Imposed monophyly of [[Dionda episcopa]] increased tree length to 389.728
- Relationships consistent with sympatric species being sister species increased tree length to 400.680
- Distance Wagner trees using Prevosti, Rogers, and Cavalli-Sforza and Edwards measures all produced longer trees (394.110 to 402.967) than the maximum parsimony solution

**Conservation Implications:**
- [[Dionda diaboli]] (Devils River minnow) has extremely limited distribution and was known from only a few locations, with some populations possibly extirpated
- Many species occupy isolated spring systems vulnerable to desiccation and human impacts
- The Parras Basin population of [[Dionda episcopa]] represents an isolated, potentially extinct population of significant biogeographic interest
