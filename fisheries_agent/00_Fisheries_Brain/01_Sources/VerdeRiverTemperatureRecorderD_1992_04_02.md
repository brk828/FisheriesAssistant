# Verde River Temperature Recorder Data at Paulden and Clarkdale Gages

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #verde-river #riverine-thermal-regime

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal USDA Forest Service memorandum (dated April 2, 1992) authored by Fishery Biologist Rome A. Stefferud, transmitting continuous water temperature datalogger records from two sites on the upper [[Verde River]] — the **Paulden gage** and **Clarkdale gage** — in Yavapai County, Arizona. The recorders operated from November 1989 through March/April 1991, spanning roughly 17 months of near-continuous thermal monitoring. The data were distributed to key regional fish and wildlife stakeholders, including the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and ichthyologist [[W.L. Minckley]] of Arizona State University, indicating the dataset's intended use for evaluating thermal habitat conditions relevant to native Southwestern fish conservation. No explicit species-level morphological or population data are presented; the report consists primarily of raw temperature time-series charts and station metadata.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **System:** Upper [[Verde River]], a perennial desert river in central Arizona and key tributary within the Gila/Colorado River drainage, historically significant as native fish habitat.
- **Monitoring sites:**
  - **Paulden gage** — T18N R01W, S34SE, Yavapai County, AZ.
  - **Clarkdale gage** — T17N R03E, S17SE, Yavapai County, AZ.
- **Temporal coverage:**
  - Paulden: 02 Nov 1989 – 15 Jul 1990 (recorder SN901203) and 15 Jul 1990 – 14 Apr 1991 (recorder SN901199).
  - Clarkdale: 03 Nov 1989 – 15 Jul 1990 (recorder SN901207) and 15 Jul 1990 – 14 Apr 1991 (recorder SN901200).
- **Thermal regime:** Charted data show a classic seasonal sinusoidal pattern typical of temperate/desert lotic systems — temperatures ranging approximately from near 0–5°C in winter months (December–February) to peaks around 20–25°C in summer months (June–September) at both gages.
- **Relevance to fish ecology:** Water temperature is a primary abiotic driver of spawning cues, growth rates, and distributional limits for native [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]] species in Southwestern rivers; such recorder data are foundational to assessing thermal suitability of the Verde River for native versus non-native fish species, and to detecting anthropogenic or hydrologic alterations (e.g., diversion, groundwater pumping) that could shift the natural thermal regime.

## Key Findings
- Two paired temperature dataloggers were deployed at Paulden and Clarkdale gages on the [[Verde River]] to capture nearly 17 continuous months of thermal data (Nov 1989–Apr 1991), bridging two recorder deployment periods at each site.
- Both sites exhibit strong seasonal temperature cycling consistent with a naturally-flowing, unregulated desert river reach, with approximate annual range of 0°C to 25°C.
- The memorandum indicates inter-agency data-sharing among the [[Prescott National Forest]], [[Coconino National Forest]], Arizona Game and Fish Department, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and academic ichthyological expertise ([[W.L. Minckley]]), underscoring the dataset's role in broader native fish conservation and habitat assessment efforts on the Verde River.
- No direct fish population, abundance, or morphological data are included; the document functions as a raw data transmittal supporting downstream ecological and management analyses (e.g., habitat modeling, native fish recovery planning) rather than a standalone research finding.
- The specific gage locations (T18N R01W S34SE at Paulden; T17N R03E S17SE at Clarkdale) provide precise georeferencing for future replication or continuation of the [[Verde River Thermal Monitoring]] effort.
