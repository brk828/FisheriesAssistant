# Bonytail Chub Status Report #03

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum dated April 18, 1996, is Status Report #03 on a single adult [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) collected on April 17, 1996, at [[Tequila Cove]], [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona. The fish was transported to the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and held under observation for about 24 hours. The fish was scanned for a P.I.T. tag and none was found. The report documents the capture, transport, holding conditions, post-arrival behavior, and a review of handling procedures that identified delays and gaps in the response.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** One adult bonytail chub, a species whose handling is coordinated by hatchery staff and federal managers.
- **Capture:** Reportedly collected around 8:00 AM on April 17, 1996, at Tequila Cove near Cottonwood Cove by Bill Pelli and a person named "Bryn" (name uncertain in the scan). These individuals were working for either the [[National Park Service]] or the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
- **Notification:** [[Chuck Minckley]] reported that he did not learn of the capture until about 4:30 PM on April 17. The fish had been kept in a live car at the campsite. Minckley contacted the hatchery and arranged transport. He did not handle the fish directly.
- **Transport:** The fish was moved in 15 gallons of water with 285 grams of salt and 3 ml of [[Stress Coat]]. No [[MS-222]] (tricaine methanesulfonate) or [[Furacin]] was used during transport.
- **Arrival:** Hatchery staff, including [[Gary Carmichael]] and Walters, received the fish at about 5:55 PM on April 17. The fish was placed in a plastic container and floated in hatchery tank #15 until water temperatures matched (17°C). It was then released into the tank and covered with plywood to create a dark area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The observations describe the fish's condition and responses while in captivity:

- **Holding conditions:** The holding tank was filled with water, recirculation and heating systems were started, and salt (0.5%), Stress Coat, and Furacin were added. The dose of Furacin is unclear in the OCR text.
- **Physical signs at capture:** Net marks were visible around the snout and gill area. A dark blotch on the caudal peduncle suggested the fish had been held or pinned. The fish was swimming in a lazy fashion and showed no flight reaction.
- **Water temperature:** Recorded at 17°C (April 17, 7:45 PM) and 16°C (April 18, 6:45 AM).

## Key Findings

**Observations after arrival (April 17–18, 1996):**

| Time | Observation |
|---|---|
| 6:25 PM, 04/17 | Lethargic; dark marks on snout and caudal peduncle; no flight reaction; full-body motion present. |
| 7:45 PM, 04/17 | Still lethargic, but full-body motion continued. Water 17°C. |
| 6:45 AM, 04/18 | No change in appearance or motion. Oriented into the current. Water 16°C. |
| 10:20 AM, 04/18 | No change. The fish did not startle when the cover was removed and hands were waved. |

**Review of events and recommendations:**

- Word of the capture did not spread quickly enough. The fish should have reached the hatchery by about 11:00 AM if collected at 8:00 AM.
- MS-222 should be used to anesthetize all bonytail chub before transport, since handling and transport stress strongly affect survival.
- All biologists should keep pre-measured supplies of salt, MS-222, Furacin, and Stress Coat, plus oxygen.
- The temperature of the transport water should be reported to the hatchery so staff can match it to the holding system.
- Minckley confirmed that the NPS had called to report that no fish would be transported on April 18, 1996, and the memo treats this as a good method to follow.
- The writer will notify the recipient offices of any new solutions or changes in the status of the species.

**Distribution:** Fisheries Resources (Albuquerque), Endangered Species (Albuquerque), AzFRO (Pinetop & Parker), and the NFH&TC.
