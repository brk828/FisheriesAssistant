## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum and agenda from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Regional Environmental Officer) announcing a June 29, 1993 meeting of the **[[Native Fish Work Group]]** at Katherine's Landing, Arizona. The meeting reviewed 1992 progress and planned 1993 actions for the **[[Lake Mohave Native Fish Rearing Program]]**, a conservation effort targeting the endangered **[[Razorback Sucker]]**. The most significant scientific finding reported is the recapture of five hatchery-reared razorback suckers during the spring 1993 spawning period, four of which were reproductively mature ("ripe") males — evidence that rear-and-release stocking can produce fish that survive to reproductive maturity in the wild. Despite this milestone, the memo emphasizes that recruitment remains far below the numbers needed to replace the aging, senescing wild adult population.

## Key Findings
- **Recruitment success indicator**: Five razorback suckers reared in prior program years were recaptured during the 1993 spring spawning period; four were ripe (reproductively mature) males — the first concrete evidence cited of hatchery-reared fish reaching sexual maturity and re-entering the spawning population.
- **Program shortfall**: Despite this success, recruitment remains well below the numbers required to replace the existing wild adult population, underscoring the severity of the **[[Razorback Sucker]]** recruitment failure crisis driving this species toward extinction in the wild.
- **Multi-agency collaboration**: The Native Fish Work Group integrates the National Park Service (Lake Mead NRA), USFWS, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Nevada Department of Wildlife, and Arizona State University research personnel — reflecting an interagency approach to native fish recovery.
- **Adaptive cove management**: Two primary rearing coves (Yuma Cove and Davis Cove) employed distinct management strategies (liners/macrophytes/rotenone vs. barrier nets/predator removal), suggesting an experimental, comparative approach to optimizing juvenile survival techniques.
- **Monitoring methodology diversity**: The program employed light traps, scuba monitoring, net integrity checks, and annual "roundup" surveys — a multi-method monitoring framework for tracking larval, juvenile, and adult native fish survival.
- **Program expansion**: New rearing coves and additional regional sites (Lake Havasu, No-Name Lake, Pretty Water) were being incorporated in 1993, indicating scaling-up of the conservation strategy beyond the original Yuma/Davis Cove pilot sites.
- **Hatchery linkage**: Reference to "Dexter Production" ties this field program to the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, a key captive propagation facility for endangered Colorado River fishes.
