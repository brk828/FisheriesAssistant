# Taxonomy and Distribution of the Darter *Percina nigrofasciata* (Agassiz)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1956 *Tulane Studies in Zoology* monograph by Ronald W. Crawford revises the taxonomy and distribution of the darter [[Percina Nigrofasciata]] across the southeastern United States. Based on meristic counts and proportional measurements from several hundred museum and field specimens, the author recognizes two subspecies: the nominal *P. n. nigrofasciata* and a new subspecies, *P. n. raneyi*, described from the upper Savannah River system. Intergrades occur in three river systems, and eight geographic races are designated within the nominal subspecies. The study also proposes hypotheses on the species' origin from a [[Percina Sciera]]-like ancestor and on its dispersal, including possible stream capture by the Savannah River.

## Background & Description

The study grew from a 1951 collecting trip in which the author noticed the species' apparent adaptability to black-water coastal plain streams, piedmont rivers and mountain headwaters. Five objectives guided the work: defining range and pattern, testing whether the species varies or is stable, identifying variable characters, reconstructing origin and dispersal, and clarifying relationships to other *Percina*.

**Methods.** Scale and fin counts followed [[Hubbs and Lagler Counting Conventions]] (1947). Specimens over 40 mm standard length were used where possible, with some 20–30 mm individuals from certain drainages. The anal ray count (usually 9) was discarded after nearly 500 specimens showed no consistent variation. Belly scale counts were also dropped because adventitious scales confounded them. Proportional measurements were taken with dial calipers (0.1 mm accuracy), generally on fish 55–65 mm, usually ten specimens per drainage (twenty from Apalachicola Bay, fifteen from St. Johns). Sexual dimorphism was tested on 50 males and 50 females across the range and 10 males and 10 females of *P. n. raneyi*; no appreciable differences were found, so sexes were pooled.

**Diagnosis and description.** The species has discrete, elliptical dark lateral bars (up to sixteen), seven dark dorsal blotches, and three dark spots at the caudal base. Lateral line scales range 46–71 (usually 50–62), dorsal rays IX–XV (usually XI–XIII), pectoral rays 23–32 (usually 27–30), and anal rays II, 7–10 (usually 9). Males are larger and darker, with enlarged ctenoid scales on the mid-ventral line. Dentition includes outer enlarged conical teeth on the upper jaw, vomerine and palatine teeth, and a detailed head-pore system is described.

**Material examined.** The report lists collections from more than 100 localities across the Mississippi, Pearl, Pascagoula, Mobile Bay, Black Warrior, Coosa, Apalachicola, Suwannee, St. Johns, Savannah, Edisto and other drainages, housed mainly at Cornell, Michigan (UMMZ), Tulane (TU), Florida State (FSU) and the U.S. National Museum. The full checklist is summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*P. nigrofasciata* occupies currents of streams and rivers throughout the coastal plain and piedmont, and occurs in mountain tributaries of Alabama, Georgia and South Carolina. Sandy, rock or rubble bottoms are preferred, though lower coastal plain specimens were taken over mud or silt.

The author emphasizes that meristic characters show no consistent link between altitude and scale count: high scale counts occur both in lowland (Lake Pontchartrain) and upland populations, and the Apalachicola Bay race is found from the coastal plain to the mountains. The Suwannee system offers two distinct microhabitats, and spring populations there differ significantly from river populations in scale counts. The Pearl River and Lake Pontchartrain drainages support both *P. nigrofasciata* and [[Percina Sciera]] without evidence of intergradation. Gene flow between adjacent coastal plain rivers, through lowland flooding and connecting swamps, is considered to limit further differentiation.

Relevant comparative species mentioned include [[Percina Maculata]], [[Percina Palmaris]], [[Percina Copelandi]] and [[Percina Evides]] (the last a comparison species in the taxonomic keys), each distinguished by color pattern, dorsal blotch number, or caudal spot arrangement.

## Key Findings

- **Two subspecies recognized.** *P. n. nigrofasciata* (formerly *Hadropterus nigrofasciatus*) and the new *P. n. raneyi* (holotype CU 23344, Abbeville County, South Carolina; 41 paratypes) differ in scale counts and several body proportions.
- ***P. n. raneyi* diagnosis.** Lateral line 55–71 (usually 60–66) versus lower counts in the nominal subspecies. The subspecies is restricted to the Savannah River system above the fall line.
- **Intergrades.** Populations intermediate in scale counts occur in the Altamaha, Combahee and lower Savannah systems. Proportional measurements did not show consistent intermediacy.
- **Westfalli synonymized.** *Hadropterus nigrofasciatus westfalli* is placed in synonymy; eye size and pectoral ray differences were judged insignificant.
- **Eight races of *P. n. nigrofasciata*.** These are the Western (Lake Pontchartrain), Upper Alabama (Coosa, Black Warrior), Apalachicola Bay, Chattahoochee and Flint, Coastal (Perdido Bay, Blackwater), Suwannee River, Suwannee Springs, St. Johns River, and Eastern (Ogeechee, Edisto) races.
- **Stability of anal rays.** The anal ray count was constant at about 9 across nearly 500 specimens.
- **Sexual dimorphism minimal.** Proportional measurements showed no appreciable sex differences; males are larger, darker, and show enlarged mid-ventral scales.
- **Origin hypothesis.** The species likely descended from a *P. sciera*-like ancestor west of the Mississippi River, isolated by flooding or westward drainage shift, then dispersed eastward through swampy connections.
- **Dispersal routes.** Southward movement into peninsular Florida via the Suwannee and St. Johns systems; northward ascent into the piedmont and mountains possibly forced during Pleistocene submergences of the coastal plain, or gradual.
- **Origin of *P. n. raneyi*.** Two hypotheses were tested: gradual ascent over the fall line, or stream capture of the Chattahoochee by the Tugaloo-Savannah system. The author favors stream capture, which explains the isolation needed for differentiation.
- **Divergence values.** Average divergence for lateral line scales between *P. n. raneyi* and *P. n. nigrofasciata* was 92.0%, with a line of separation at 59–60 scales.
- **Abstract range.** The range is bounded by the Mississippi River (west) and the Edisto River (east), extending into peninsular Florida and northward to Chattahoochee and Savannah headwaters.
