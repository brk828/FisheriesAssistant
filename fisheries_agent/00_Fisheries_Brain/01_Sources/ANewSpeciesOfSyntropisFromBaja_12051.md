# A New Species of Syntropis from Baja California Sur, Mexico, with Notes on its Biology

#arachnology #desert-ecology #scorpiones #vejovidae #syntropis

## Executive Summary

This 1969 paper, by [[Stanley C. Williams]] of San Francisco State College, formally describes *Syntropis longiunguis*, a new scorpion species from the sand dunes of Baja California Sur, Mexico. The genus was previously known only from its type specimen, *[[Syntropis Macrura]]*, described by [[Kraepelin]] in 1900. Field work in summer 1968 yielded several dozen *S. macrura* and over 1,000 specimens of the new species. The paper provides a detailed morphological diagnosis, sexual dimorphism notes, population data, behavioural and habitat observations, and a comparison with other North American scorpions.

## Background & Description

- **Genus context:** *[[Syntropis]]* was distinguished by a single, unpaired inferior median keel on the metasoma, unlike the paired condition typical of [[Vejovidae]].
- **Type material:** The holotype male and allotype female of *S. longiunguis* were collected on the sand dunes of San Angel, 13 miles west of San Ignacio, on 27 June 1968 by S. C. Williams and M. A. Cazier and party. Both are deposited in the [[California Academy of Sciences]].
- **Diagnosis:** Moderate-sized, uniform pale yellow scorpion. Distinguished from *S. macrura* by:
  - Uniform yellow colouration (not rusty)
  - Shorter, scalloped pedipalp fingers and swollen palm
  - Elongate, non-bulbous telson
  - Very elongate pretarsal claws, with the outer claw longer than the inner (2.0 mm vs 1.5 mm on hind walking legs)
  - Reduced unguicular spine and densely hirsute distal tarsomere
- **Holotype measurements (male / female allotype):** Total length 58.7 / 58.1 mm; carapace length 7.2 / 7.8 mm; metasoma length 28.1 / 27.3 mm; telson length 10.4 / 10.0 mm; pectine teeth 31/30 (male) and 27/26 (female).
- **Name origin:** *longiunguis* refers to the highly elongate ungues of the pretarsus.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Little overall. Males have distinct genital papillae, a more completely divided genital operculum, larger pectines and more pectinal teeth (28–35 vs 24–31 in females), though counts overlap.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sample base:** 690 paratopotypes (255 males, 325 females, 110 early instar nymphs) plus 793 paratypes from three localities:
  - 3 miles south of Rancho Tablon, elevation 1,500 ft (23 June 1968): 29 males, 40 females
  - 5 miles west of San Angel (28 June 1968): 69 males, 92 females, 37 young juveniles
  - About 5 miles north of La Laguna, east shore of [[Laguna De San Ignacio]] (29 June 1968): 229 males, 216 females, 81 young juveniles
- **Habitat preference:** Strong preference for unstabilised, wind-deposited dune sand on Baja California's west coast, from Rancho Tablon to near La Laguna. Dunes range from large, extensive systems (San Angel) to small, isolated patches (Rancho Tablon).
- **Abundance:** Conspicuously the numerically dominant scorpion in its habitat.
- **Behaviour:** Nervous and responsive; intolerant of other scorpions; aggressive toward local disturbance. Emerges at dusk and sits still; moving individuals were rarely seen. When disturbed, it glides downhill to a lower spot 10–15 ft away.
- **Adaptations:** Elongate, reddish-hair-covered pretarsal claws and dense tarsal hairs give traction on loose sand and allow fast ascent of steep leeward dune faces. The streamlined metasoma and telson may help avoid being buried.
- **Distribution on dunes:** Densest on sloping sections, not crests or basins.
- **Life cycle:** Adult females had already given birth for the season, and reproductive systems looked synchronised. The author infers a single litter per female per year. Very young juveniles (probably second and third instars) were abundant; intermediate subadults were rare on the surface.
- **Comparisons:** *S. longiunguis* is ecologically and morphologically closest to *[[Vejovis Mesaensis]]* of the Sonoran and Colorado deserts, its "ecological counterpart" in dune habitats. Although *S. macrura* shares the single inferior median keel, the two are not closely related. Its ecology and behaviour instead resemble *[[Vejovis Minckleyi]]* of talus and cliff habitats in Coahuila, with *S. macrura* living in the Sierra de la Giganta.

## Key Findings

- *Syntropis longiunguis* is a new species, uniform pale yellow and clearly separated from *S. macrura* by pedipalp, telson, pretarsal and keel characters.
- The species is strictly associated with unstabilised dune sand on the Baja California Sur coast, where it is the dominant scorpion.
- Its pretarsal and tarsal modifications, together with streamlined body form, are adaptations to sand-dwelling and rapid movement on dunes.
- Sexual dimorphism is minimal, and population structure shows a single annual litter with synchronised births.
- Its ecological and behavioural convergence with *Vejovis mesaensis* supports treating the two as counterparts in separate desert regions.
