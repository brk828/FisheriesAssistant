# Bubbling Ponds Hatchery Development for Native Fishes: Protocol for Collection, Transport, Quarantine, Maintenance, Propagation and Repatriation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 2005 protocol, prepared by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) and funded by the [[U.S. Bureau Of Reclamation]] through the [[Central Arizona Project]] Fund Transfer Program, sets out a step-by-step framework for building captive refuge populations of native and listed Arizona fishes. It covers collection from wild source stocks, transport, quarantine, maintenance, propagation and rearing, and repatriation to the wild. Its emphasis is on minimizing genetic loss and handling risk, with [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] as the focal species for which propagation methods were already established.

## Background & Description

The document is a protocol rather than a research study. It applies mainly to Arizona translocation, repatriation and introduction work. Most future projects are expected to involve simply moving wild fish from a source population to a new locality without captivity, except during transport. The full captive protocol applies when a refuge population must be established or when propagation is needed because wild numbers are low.

The species named in the text are:
- **Loach minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): a small, bottom-dwelling, secretive cyprinid with strong sexual dimorphism; males can be about twice the size of females.
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*): a small cyprinid for which captive propagation and rearing techniques were previously documented.
- **Chubs of the genus *Gila*** and **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): large-bodied species held in the refuge, notable for jumping and for developmental deformities in young.
- **Speckled dace** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): mentioned as needing cover similar to loach minnow.
- **[[Asian Tapeworm]]** (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*): an internal parasite requiring treatment before fish enter the refuge.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water quality and habitat are treated as operational concerns. Transport water must be pathogen-free and aerated, and it contains a 0.6% unionized NaCl solution plus Stress Coat at the manufacturer's dosage. Quarantine water must match the facility's temperature and pH. Prophylactic treatments are given at 18–20 °C. Tanks in the refuge building are flow-through, so outflow must be managed and diverted away from the refuge. Substrate and cover are important for bottom-dwelling species, and shaded areas matter for *Gila* chubs.

Repatriation must be preceded by consultation with resource managers (conservation biologists and sportfish managers), landowners (state, federal and private), and grazing permittees. Environmental assessment must show no negative consequences for the species or the environment.

## Key Findings

- **Collection:** Collect at least 100 adults, specifically the first 50 males and 50 females found, with no visible deformities, parasitic disease or infection. If fewer than 100 are collected, repeat collections (at least every other year, annually preferred) until a captive population of 100 adults is reached. Use the smallest practicable seine mesh to avoid size and sex bias, which would reduce effective population size and genetic diversity.
- **Transport:** Transport is high-risk. Use an insulated container, carry backup O₂, and acclimate fish if the hauling tank and source water differ by more than 3 °C. Stop at least every two hours to check health, temperature and oxygen. Permits and field data must accompany the fish.
- **Quarantine:** Label all tanks and separate them from other facility systems. Give 24-hour prophylactic treatments for 3 consecutive days at 18–20 °C using a malachite green, nitromersol and acriflavine mixture (Ich Guard). Treat Asian tapeworm with praziquantel at 0.67 ppm for 24 h before fish enter the refuge.
- **Maintenance:** Feed small-bodied fish Tetramin flake and freeze-dried bloodworms, and large-bodied fish trout chow or specialized feeds (a [[Bozeman Technology Center]] razorback sucker diet and a Rangen catfish diet with Spirulina and krill, both reported to reduce developmental deformities). Feed once or twice daily; if using flow-through tanks, shut off inflow for 30–60 min to allow feeding. Provide each tank its own broom and dip net, and clean tanks monthly.
- **Mortality:** Preserve dead fish in 95% absolute ethanol for possible genetic analysis, and record mortality by species in a logbook and spreadsheet.
- **Propagation and rearing:** Loach minnow and spikedace methods were established by Childs (2004) and Parmenter and Platania (2004). Spikedace tanks are lined with pea gravel to protect eggs, and as many as 2,000 F1 progeny may be reared if space allows. Keep all F1 progeny in separate tanks to avoid backcrossing.
- **Repatriation:** Decided case by case after stakeholder consultation and environmental assessment. A goal of at least 100 adults (1:1 sex ratio) should be used for direct translocation. If the source stock is too small, stock a minimum of 500 F1 progeny, augmented over time with wild fish (preferred) or additional F1 progeny, followed by monitoring of short- and long-term success.
