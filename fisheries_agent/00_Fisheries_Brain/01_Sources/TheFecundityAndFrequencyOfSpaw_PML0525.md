# Fecundity and Spawning Frequency of Tilapia aurea; Salinity and Temperature Effects on Mugil Fry

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae #mugilidae

## Executive Summary

This partial scanned document contains two linked studies from Israeli fish culture work. The first, by [[Stephen Dadzie]], measures the [[Fecundity]] and spawning behaviour of [[Tilapia aurea]] held in laboratory tanks. The second, by D. Miree of the Ministry of Agriculture Extension Service, is a preliminary report on how changes in [[Salinity]] and [[Water Temperature]] affect [[Mugil capito]] fry during transfer to farm ponds. The tilapia study reports a mean fecundity of 719 eggs in 17 mature females and finds that egg removal shortens the spawning interval. The mullet note documents high fry losses (over 50%) during handling and transport, and it sets out an experimental design that tests salinity responses. The scan is incomplete: the mullet section ends mid-methods, and no mullet results are given.

## Background & Description

**Tilapia aurea fecundity study (Dadzie).** Fecundity is treated as a key measure of reproductive potential, relevant to population estimation, population dynamics and fish production forecasts. For this study it is defined as the number of eggs a female produces in one spawning season. The author notes that Israeli tilapia have a well-defined breeding season, and that egg output depends on fish condition, fish size, the length of the spawning season and spawning frequency. Climate also affects these factors.

- **Animals:** 68 mature tilapia (males and females), 26–332 g, in their second summer. They were raised at the [[Fish Culture Research Station]] in Dor, then moved to laboratory tanks.
- **Husbandry:** Water temperature 25–30 °C, fed ad libitum on commercial pelleted feed, under a normal light regime.
- **Experiments:** Three spawning experiments were run between April and October (1969, per the OCR text). The first had four series and used hormone injections ([[Luteinizing Hormone]] and human [[Chorionic Gonadotropin]]) on carp pituitary-induced fish. Its results were reported separately. In the second experiment, only the first spawning series was induced, using a homogenised carp pituitary extract. Eggs were removed from the female's buccal cavity after each spawning, counted and measured. The third experiment was the untreated control: fish spawned naturally, and eggs were left to incubate and hatch in the mouth.

**Mullet fry study (Miree).** Young [[Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus* at 18–25 mm and *M. capito* at 15–18 mm) are caught in the littoral from October to February. They are stocked into freshwater ponds that also hold carp and tilapia. Losses during transport and stocking exceed 50%. Fry are transferred from estuarine water to a bucket and then to a transport tank holding tap water, tap water mixed with seawater, or brackish river water. Pond salinity generally ranges from 300 to 1000 mg Cl/L (the OCR text reads "mg C/1"). The author asks whether sudden changes in salinity, temperature or both cause the die-offs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Reproductive biology:** Egg production in tilapia is linked to season, climate and spawning frequency. The author notes that most fecundity data for *Tilapia* come from fish in natural habitats, whereas this study used laboratory fish, often induced to spawn. The author therefore concludes that comparisons with other *Tilapia* species are "not very feasible."
- **Culture systems:** Mullet fry are transferred between estuarine, brackish and freshwater environments. The abrupt changes in salinity and temperature during these moves are the central problem in the mullet study.
- **Hatchery context:** Fish were kept under controlled temperatures and photoperiods in tanks. Carp and tilapia were reared together in ponds, so the two systems interact in aquaculture practice.

## Key Findings

**Tilapia aurea (Dadzie)**

- **Sample:** 17 mature female *T. aurea* were used for fecundity measurement.
- **Mean fecundity:** 719 eggs per female per spawning season. Fecundity ranged across the fish, and it tended to increase with body size.
- **Egg size:** Egg size was related to female size (Table 2). Egg size increased over successive spawnings from the same female. The table's numeric values did not survive the scan reliably, so exact dimensions are not reproduced here.
- **Incubation:** Eggs held in the female's mouth took 8–10 days to hatch at 29 °C.
- **Spawning frequency:** Removing eggs from the mouth right after spawning had the same effect as administering exogenous gonadotrophin. It shortened the spawning interval to about 2–3 weeks but did not increase spawning frequency.

**Mugil fry (Miree)**

- **Problem:** Fry mortality after transfer is very high, over 50%, and fry die suddenly after moves between bucket, tank and pond.
- **Hypothesis under test:** Whether young mullet are sensitive to changes in salinity, temperature or both together.
- **Design:** Six plastic containers, each holding 20 L of water at different salinities, were set up to test fry reaction to salinity (Table 1). The scan ends before the results are reported.
