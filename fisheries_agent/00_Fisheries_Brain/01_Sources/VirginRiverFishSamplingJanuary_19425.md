# Virgin River Fish Sampling Post-Quail Creek Flood, January 1989

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #flood-disturbance

## Executive Summary

This field report documents emergency fish sampling conducted 07-08 January 1989 along the [[Virgin River]] (Arizona-Nevada-Utah) immediately following the catastrophic failure of [[Quail Creek Reservoir]] dike on 01 January 1989. Personnel from [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) and [[University of Nevada Las Vegas]] (UNLV) sampled seven mainstem locations to assess post-flood fish community status. The flood released approximately 25,000 acre-feet of water with peak discharge estimated at 60,000 CFS (approximately 2X historical high flow). Sampling revealed six native and introduced fish species, with [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), an endangered species, present at multiple sites. The survey was conducted in context of ongoing State of Utah and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] efforts to eradicate [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) to benefit endangered woundfin populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Quail Creek Reservoir]] dike failure on 01 January 1989 released an uncontrolled flood down the Virgin River channel, causing severe damage between Quail Creek and Bloomington, Utah, near the head of the [[Virgin River Narrows]]. The flood eventually entered [[Lake Mead]]. Multiple research interests converged to necessitate immediate post-flood sampling: (1) ongoing joint poisoning project by Utah and USFWS to eradicate red shiner for woundfin conservation, (2) ASU research on colonization-recolonization dynamics associated with fish poisoning operations, and (3) UNLV research evaluating Virgin River fish community status in context of reclamation efforts.

Sampling was conducted by seine (1.8 and 3.7 m long, 3.2 mm mesh) at seven locations: (1) approximately 0.75 km upstream from [[Quail Creek]] confluence (QCC), (2) above [[Washington Fields Diversion]] (WFD), (3) below I-15 bridge at [[Bloomington]] (I15), (4) at [[Bureau of Land Management]] rest area within Virgin River Narrows (BLM), (5) at [[Beaver Dam Wash]] confluence (BDW), (6) at [[Mesquite]]-Bunkerville bridge (MES), and (7) at [[Riverside]] (RIV). Four additional localities were visited but deemed too hazardous for sampling due to excessive depth (>2 m), current velocity (>1.5 m/s), and debris.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Post-flood habitat conditions varied substantially along the river gradient. Upstream sites (QCC, WFD, I15) exhibited extreme turbidity (visibility <1-20 cm), predominantly sand substrate, monotonous run habitat, and severely reduced or absent pool and riffle development. The Washington Fields Diversion structure was completely destroyed. Channel morphology showed dramatic alterations including increased depth and velocity, elimination of gradual bank slopes replaced by sheer 2 m embankments, and destruction or downstream bending of riparian vegetation (predominantly [[Tamarisk]]).

Mid-reach sites (BLM, BDW) were characterized by extreme turbidity, swift turbulent flow, deeper channels (>0.5-1 m), and substrate of cobble-boulder imbricated within sand and silty-mud matrix. The BDW site showed braided channel morphology with dense riparian accumulations of coarse organic debris. Downstream sites (MES, RIV) maintained extreme turbidity but showed less dramatic morphological changes, with riparian vegetation thinned and leaning downstream but otherwise intact. Aquatic vegetation and algal films were absent or wanting at all sites. The lower artificial barrier at the head of Virgin River Narrows remained intact and continued to function as a barrier to upstream fish movement at low flows.

## Key Findings

**Species Composition and Distribution:**
Six fish taxa were collected across seven sites (all specimens returned to stream):
- *Notropis lutrensis* ([[Red Shiner]], Notlut): 643 individuals, dominant at downstream sites
- *Plagopterus argentissimus* ([[Woundfin]], Plaarg): 15 individuals including one juvenile ca 1.5 cm TL
- *Rhinichthys osculus* ([[Speckled Dace]], Rhiosc): 19 individuals
- *Catostomus latipinnis* ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]], Catlat): 5 individuals
- *Pantosteus clarki* ([[Desert Sucker]], Pancla): 3 individuals
- *Lepidomeda* sp. (Lepcya, likely [[Virgin Spinedace]]): 2 individuals
- One dead rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdneri*, Salgai) ca 30 cm TL found at WFD

**Catch Statistics by Location:**
- QCC: N=13, CPE=0.24 (13 hauls); Plaarg 41%, Rhiosc 31%
- WFD: N=1, CPE=0.05 (22 hauls); 100% Plaarg
- I15: N=3, CPE=0.05 (57 hauls); 100% Plaarg
- BLM: N=0, CPE=0 (24 hauls); no fish captured
- BDW: N=77, CPE=1.83 (42 hauls); Notlut 66%, Rhiosc 17%, Plaarg 14%
- MES: N=301, CPE=13.09 (23 hauls); Notlut 99%
- RIV: N=295, CPE=5.74 (52 hauls); Notlut 99%

**Key Patterns:**
Red shiner overwhelmingly dominated downstream Nevada sites (MES, RIV: 99% composition), while upstream Utah sites showed more diverse assemblages with woundfin, speckled dace, and native suckers. The endangered woundfin was present at four of seven sites (QCC, WFD, I15, BDW) despite catastrophic flood disturbance. Catch per unit effort (CPE) increased dramatically downstream, from 0.05-0.24 at upstream sites to 5.74-13.09 at downstream sites. The BLM site within the Virgin River Narrows yielded zero fish despite 24 seine hauls, suggesting either complete displacement or mortality in this high-velocity, turbulent reach. Total sampling effort was 233 seine hauls encompassing approximately 2,330 m² of habitat.
