# Additional Razorback Sucker Genetics Studies

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1994 internal memorandum from the Illinois Natural History Survey (Dave Philipp's lab, analysis by Todd Kassler) presents electrophoretic genetic analyses conducted to independently test Dr. Buth's hypothesis that [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS, *Xyrauchen texanus*) individuals from Etter Pond, Colorado, were the product of hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (FMS, *Catostomus latipinnis*). A second objective assessed allelic variation among captive Green River RBS broodstock at the Vernal, Utah hatchery to evaluate the feasibility of creating genetically-tagged offspring lots for tracking differential survival in flooded riverside wetlands (e.g., [[Old Charley Wash]]).

Analysis of 56 allozyme loci across young-of-year (YOY) RBS from Etter Pond, Ouray, and Green River, plus adult FMS from the Upper Colorado River, found **no evidence of recent hybridization** in the Etter Pond fish. The loci Dr. Buth had cited as diagnostic (CK-A*, MDH-A1*, IDHP-A*, and possibly ADA*) instead reflect **shared ancestral alleles** between the two species rather than fixed species-diagnostic markers. However, discrepancies between INHS genotype results and Buth's reported parental genotypes for specific offspring families raised concerns about either scoring inconsistencies or **mixed hatchery lots** — a potential logistical problem for future broodstock studies.

## Background & Description

This memorandum does not report external morphological data; the analysis is restricted to **biochemical/genetic characterization** via starch-gel allozyme electrophoresis. Diagnostic characters used to distinguish [[Razorback Sucker]] from [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] were allelic states at 56 protein-coding loci rather than morphometric traits.

**Sample composition analyzed:**
- 28 YOY RBS — Etter Pond, CO
- 6 YOY RBS — Ouray, CO
- 38 YOY RBS — Green River, UT
- 4 adult FMS — Upper Colorado River

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Focal system:** Colorado River Basin, including riverine reaches (Green River, Upper Colorado River) and off-channel/riverside wetland habitats subject to seasonal flooding (e.g., Old Charley Wash), which serve as nursery habitat for stocked RBS.
- **Sympatry and hybridization risk:** [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] co-occur in the Upper Colorado River system, raising conservation concern over introgressive hybridization, particularly in captive/hatchery-supplemented populations such as those at [[Etter Pond]].
- **Conservation application:** Genetic characterization of captive Green River broodstock was intended to support the design of **genetically-tagged cohorts** released into flooded riverside wetlands to measure differential survival — a tool for evaluating habitat restoration and stocking strategies for this federally significant species.
- **Management context:** Findings directly informed the Genetics Panel and Biology Committee overseeing razorback sucker recovery efforts (relevant to entities such as Frank Pfeiffer and Tim Modde's field programs in Colorado and Utah).

## Key Findings

### 1. Locus categorization (56 loci examined)
- **9 loci with fixed differences** between all RBS and the 4 FMS (species-diagnostic): `ACO-1*, CAT-1*, CBP-1*, FH-1*, GPI-A1*, IDPH-A*, mMDH-2*, SDH-1*, SOD-2*`
- **11 loci fixed in FMS, polymorphic in RBS** (RBS shares the FMS allele): `CK-A*, CK-B1*, EST-2*, EST-3*, EST-4*, IDHP-B1*, LDH-C*, MDH-A1*, MDH-B*, mMDH-1*, TPI-A2*`
  - Of these, Buth had reported **CK-A\*** and **MDH-A1\*** as fixed differences between RBS and FMS — contradicted by the INHS dataset, which found these to be shared/polymorphic alleles.
- **6 loci fixed in RBS, polymorphic in FMS** (FMS shares the RBS allele): `ACP-1*, CK-B2*, GPI-A2*, IDHP-B2*, PGDH-1*, PROT-1*`
- **1 isolocus polymorphic in both species**: `LDH-B1,2*`
- **ADA\*** could not be reliably resolved by INHS; Buth's preliminary report had called it diagnostic but noted scoring difficulty in some individuals.

### 2. Etter Pond hybridization conclusion
- The nine loci with **true fixed differences** between all sampled RBS and FMS showed **no hybrid genotypes** in Etter Pond offspring — conclusively indicating the Etter Pond fish are **not recent hybrids**.
- Apparent discrepancies at CK-A*, MDH-A1*, IDHP-A*, and possibly ADA* in Buth's original hypothesis are attributed to **interspecific allele sharing**, which may reflect ancient/non-recent hybridization or retained ancestral polymorphism rather than contemporary introgression.

### 3. Parentage/genotype discrepancies
- Comparison of INHS-scored offspring genotypes (CK-A*, mMDH-A1*, IDHP-A*) against Buth's reported parental genotypes revealed **mismatches** in specific families (e.g., Family A3, Family A7I, Family C71, Family B2).
- Re-sampling and re-analysis of hatchery lots reproduced similar results for Family A3 but **not** for Family A7I, ruling out sample handling/packaging as the sole cause.
- Conclusion: either **parental genotyping error** occurred in Buth's original analysis, or **fish lots were mixed** prior to hatchery distribution — flagged as a **substantial potential problem** for future genetic-tagging work.

### 4. Green River broodstock variability (genetic tagging feasibility)
- Approximately **14 potentially informative polymorphic loci** identified among Green River RBS captive adults, suitable for constructing genetically distinguishable family lots (though not all loci may ultimately prove reliable).
- Provisional parental genotypes at polymorphic loci (`CK-A, CK-B1, EST-2, EST-3, EST-4, FH-1, GPI-A1, IDH-A1, IDH-B1, LDH-B1, LDH-C, MDH-A1, MDH-B, MDH-M1`) were estimated from small YOY samples (3–4 per family lot), with some ambiguity in assigning genotypes to specific parents.
- Recommendation: **pre-spawning tissue/fin sampling** of hatchery adults, followed by genetic analysis prior to pairing, would allow **confident identification of correct parental pairs** for constructing genetically-tagged experimental cohorts (e.g., for release studies at [[Old Charley Wash]]).
