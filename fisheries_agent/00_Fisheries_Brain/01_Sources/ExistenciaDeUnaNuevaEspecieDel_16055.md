# Existencia de una Nueva Especie del Género Lile (Clupeidae) en la Costa Occidental del Pacífico Mexicano

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by Castro-Aguirre & Vivero (1987) describes **[[Lile Gracilis]]**, a new species of clupeid fish (Ostheichthyes: Clupeidae) from the western coast of Mexico, ranging from Bahía Banderas, Jalisco to Mar Muerto, Chiapas. The description resolves prior taxonomic confusion in which populations from this region had been misidentified as **[[Lile Stolifera]]** (Jordan y Gilbert). The authors demonstrate that *L. gracilis* and *L. stolifera* are allopatric, with *L. stolifera* exhibiting a "quasi-antitropical" distribution (northern populations in the Gulf of California/Baja California Sur, and southern populations from Ecuador to Peru), while *L. gracilis* occupies the intervening tropical central zone. Morphometric allometry testing (regression slope comparison, significant to 99%) statistically confirms the species-level distinction. The paper also references the related species **[[Lile Piquitinga]]** (Brazil/Venezuela) and **[[Platanichthys]]** (formerly *Lile platana*, Argentina/Brazil) for comparative purposes.

## Background & Description

### Diagnostic Features of *Lile gracilis* sp. nov.
- **Body form:** Slender (gracile), elongated, only slightly compressed; body depth 3.9–4.9 in standard length; dorsal and ventral profiles nearly straight (not strongly convex, in contrast to *L. stolifera*).
- **Operculum:** Posterior margin smoothly rounded (not squared/angular as in *L. stolifera*); conspicuous notch present in lower operculum margin.
- **Gill chamber:** Notable rounded fleshy projection on the lower-right margin of the branchial chamber.
- **Adipose eyelid:** Poorly developed (vs. well-developed in *L. stolifera* and *L. piquitinga*).
- **Scales:** 32–36 (mode 34) small, non-adherent (deciduous) scales in longitudinal series; 10–13 (12) scales in the series from dorsal fin origin to 13th/14th ventral scute; 3–7 (6) peduncular scales; each scale bears only 5 striae (vs. 8–10 in *L. stolifera*).
- **Lateral silver band:** Narrower, covering only a single scale; begins at pupil level; widens only slightly toward caudal peduncle.
- **Fin ray counts:**
  - Dorsal: 16–18 rays
  - Anal: 17–20 rays
  - Pectoral: 14–15 rays
  - Caudal: 17–18 rays
- **Ventral scutes:** 26–30 bony ventral scutes, originating at first scale posterior to isthmus, extending to the anus.
- **Gill rakers:** Lower limb of first branchial arch with 29–32 gill rakers; 6 branchiostegal rays.
- **Ventral angle:** Formed by two lines (one parallel to lower body margin, one tangent to lower jaw) ranging 27°–37°.
- **Coloration:** Body greenish-yellow in life except for the bright silvery lateral band and cephalic region; scale margins on dorsum brown-to-black forming a faint dark dorsal line; fins yellowish/colorless except upper caudal lobe with a diffuse dark/blackish tint (lower lobe colorless); pupil black, iris ochre-yellow.
- **Jaw/teeth:** Lower jaw fits within upper jaw, both nearly vertical; blunt conical tooth-like structures at symphysis of lower jaw; conical unicuspid teeth in cluster arrangement on palatine; tongue toothless, spatulate.

### Comparative Morphology Table (*L. stolifera* vs. *L. gracilis*)
| Character | *[[Lile Stolifera]]* | *Lile gracilis* sp. nov. |
|---|---|---|
| Operculum shape | Squared/angular | Rounded |
| Opercular notch | Absent | Present |
| Gill chamber projection | Low, subquadrate | More evident, rounded |
| Adipose eyelid | Very developed | Poorly developed |

### Morphometric/Allometric Analysis
- Log-transformed cephalic length vs. maximum body height regressed for n=20 individuals per species.
- **Regression slope, *L. stolifera***: b = 1.7054
- **Regression slope, *L. gracilis***: b = 0.8197
- Difference in slopes significant at 99% confidence (Draper 1966 method), statistically confirming distinct allometric growth patterns and supporting species-level separation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat preference:** *Lile gracilis* is euryhaline but shows marked preference for oligohaline to polyhaline waters within coastal lagoons and estuaries with sandy-muddy substrates, turbid waters, and high organic matter content.
- **Salinity tolerance:** Recorded across a broad range (0.5‰ to 41.1‰) in Lagunas Oriental y Occidental, Oaxaca (based on 1,324 individuals; 21.3% found at 37.1‰). Individuals in Mar Muerto, Chiapas were collected in salinities exceeding 50‰. A landlocked population in the La Villita reservoir (Michoacán) apparently completes its entire life cycle in freshwater.
- **Comparative ecology:** *[[Lile Stolifera]]* shows greater preference for euhaline (and even hypersaline) conditions, common in Bahía Magdalena and La Paz, B.C.S. (salinity 34.9–35.1‰, up to 200 individuals per beach seine haul). Freshwater occurrence of *L. stolifera* in Baja California is rare, limited to the Mulegé and San José del Cabo esteros.
- **Shared niche:** Both species are characteristic inhabitants of coastal lagoon/estuarine systems with soft sediment bottoms.
- **Geographic replacement zone:** Around 20°30′ N, 107°30′ W near Bahía Banderas, Jalisco, *L. gracilis* appears to ecologically and taxonomically displace *L. stolifera*, which virtually disappears from collections south of this point.

## Key Findings

1. **New species description:** *Lile gracilis* sp. nov. is formally described based on museum specimens from Laguna Oriental y Occidental (Oaxaca, holotype/paratypes), Laguna de Tres Palos (Guerrero), and Playa Azul (Michoacán), among other Pacific coast Mexican localities.
2. **Taxonomic correction:** Prior records of "*Lile stolifera*" from Guerrero, Oaxaca, and Chiapas (Ramírez 1952, Ricker 1959, Fowler 1944 [in part], Castro-Aguirre et al. 1977, Castro-Aguirre 1978, Yáñez-Arancibia 1978) are reassigned to *L. gracilis*, correcting the taxonomic record ("*non Clupea stolifera* Jordan y Gilbert").
3. **Antitropical distribution pattern:** True *[[Lile Stolifera]]* (sensu stricto) likely comprises two morphologically similar but geographically and genetically isolated populations — one in the Gulf of California/Baja California Sur (north) and one from Ecuador to Peru (south) — consistent with a "quasi-antitropical" distribution (sensu Hubbs 1952), with the geographically intermediate tropical zone occupied by the distinct species *L. gracilis*.
4. **Allopatry confirmed:** *L. gracilis* and *L. stolifera* do not co-occur; the replacement boundary is near Bahía Banderas, Jalisco.
5. **Evolutionary inference:** Comparative morphology across *L. stolifera*, *L. piquitinga*, and *L. gracilis* (using Whitehead 1973 data for *L. piquitinga*) suggests *L. gracilis* may represent a less primitive (more derived) form among the three species, based on reduced/simplified scale striation, opercular shape, and reduced adipose eyelid development.
6. **Open questions:** The authors note the speciation/ecological replacement mechanism cannot be resolved without additional genetic, serological, immunological, and reproductive behavior data — an area for future research.
7. **Species referenced/related taxa:** *[[Lile Stolifera]]*, *[[Lile Piquitinga]]*, *[[Platanichthys]]* (formerly *Lile platana* Regan, reclassified by Whitehead 1968).
