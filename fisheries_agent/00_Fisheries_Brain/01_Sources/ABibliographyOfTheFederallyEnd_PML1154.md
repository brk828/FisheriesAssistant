# Bibliography of the Federally Endangered Bonytail Chub (Gila elegans) in the Colorado River of the American Southwest

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a annotated-style bibliography of the literature on the federally endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), prepared by Terry C. Inman, Paul C. Marsh and W.L. Minckley at Arizona State University for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and ASU Hayden Library Special Collections (June 1995). It is organized into ten volumes of references, ordered alphabetically by author from Abbott (1861) to Wydoski (1991), and it deliberately omits material already contained in Minckley's [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) bibliography, to avoid redundancy. Its scope covers taxonomy, distribution, life history, habitat use, hatchery culture, stocking, reservoir impacts and recovery planning across the upper and lower [[Colorado River Basin]].

## Background & Description

The bonytail chub is a cyprinid of the genus *Gila*, first described by Baird and Girard (1853). The references compiled here trace its taxonomic history, including hybridization questions within *Gila* (e.g., Douglas et al. 1989; Rosenfeld and Wilkinson 1989; Demarais et al. 1992) and its relationship to the [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. Listing and status documents place it among the Colorado River fishes protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, with the first proposal for endangered status published by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in 1978 and a revised recovery plan issued in 1990.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography documents the bonytail's decline in the face of [[Colorado River Dams]] and reservoir development, including [[Lake Powell]] (Glen Canyon Dam), [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] and [[Lake Mohave]]. Several works address:

- **Reservoir and tailwater effects:** post-impoundment studies on the Green River and Glen Canyon tailwater, and Walburg et al. (1981) on reservoir release effects.
- **Habitat and flow requirements:** habitat suitability index curves (Valdez et al. 1987), streamflow needs (Tyus and Karp 1989, 1991), and total dissolved solids tolerance (Bulkley et al. 1983).
- **Reproduction and early life stages:** temperature effects on incubation and survival (Marsh 1985; Marsh and Pisano 1982), and larval/juvenile taxonomy (Muth 1990).
- **Threats and management issues:** non-native species, disease, contaminants (Hamilton and Buhl 1992), the Green River poisoning program (Holden 1991), and the 1993 Lake Mohave bonytail losses.
- **Hatchery culture and stocking:** [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] work, allozyme variation in hatchery stock (Minckley et al. 1989), and stocking protocols (Hamill 1994).

## Key Findings

- **Scope and size:** The bibliography spans ten volumes covering references from 1861 to 1993 (volume index: Abbott 1861 through Scott 1985, with the full list extending to Wydoski and Hamill 1991). Exact total entry count is not stated in the source.
- **Scope exclusion:** Material already covered by Minckley's humpback chub bibliography was not repeated; the volume listing is provided as a cross-reference guide to that work.
- **Taxonomic and genetic work:** Multiple studies address *Gila* systematics, chromosomes, allozymes and hybridization, including Uyeno (1961), Holden and Stalnaker (1970), Rosenfeld (1991) and Marsh et al. (1995).
- **Status and listing:** Key listing and status documents include the 1978 proposed endangered rule, Deacon et al. (1979), Johnson (1987), the Bonytail Chub revised recovery plan (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 1990), and the Recovery Implementation Program documents (1987, 1989).
- **Recovery and conservation actions:** The bibliography includes hatchery propagation work (Jensen 1983, Hamman 1988), Lake Mohave population surveys (Bozek et al. 1984; Marsh and Minckley 1989, 1992), habitat suitability modelling, and the proposed reintroduction into the Gila River basin (Minckley 1986).
- **Scope of data:** As a bibliography, the document contains no original field data, sample sizes or population statistics; its value lies in the cataloguing of literature and its cross-referencing with the humpback chub work.
