# Gila Topminnow Sites in Arizona: Provisional Extirpation Report, 1995-1996 Field Season

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) Technical Report 100, a draft by [[David A. Weedman]] and [[Kirk L. Young]], reviews [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) reintroduction sites across Arizona and provisionally declares a large number of them extirpated. The report documents site histories, survey effort and probable causes of failure for roughly 50 sites, drawing on field data from 1995-96 and on historical records dating back to 1982. Reintroduction began in 1982-83 with stocking from [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]] stock originating from Monkey, Cocio and Bylas springs, and monitoring has been ongoing for at least 13 years.

The report applies provisional [[Extirpation Criteria]] derived from the draft [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]], and states that its conclusions require review by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] before final declaration.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] is a small livebearing poeciliid native to the Gila River drainage in Arizona. Since 1991, AGFD, the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), USFWS and the [[U.S. Forest Service]] (USFS) have cooperated to recover the species through reintroductions to sites within its historic range and beyond it. The report is organized into four parts: an introduction, sites provisionally extirpated during 1995, sites provisionally extirpated before 1995, and sites not meeting extirpation criteria with recommendations for further management.

Extirpation procedures follow Project E5, Job 09 ("Sonoran topminnow and desert pupfish monitoring and reintroduction") of the [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 6 program. Required report contents include survey dates, surveyor qualifications, methods, habitat coverage, survey thoroughness, contiguous waters searched, voucher specimens and probable causes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes the species' habitat requirements and the environmental threats that affected sites:

- **Preferred habitat (from the introduction's habitat table):** perennial, low-gradient stream reaches; complex, heterogeneous habitat protected from major, recurring flash flooding; present, moderate to abundant aquatic vegetation; and a community of only native, nonpredatory fishes plus a variety of insect life.
- **Recurring threats:** flash flooding and sedimentation, drying during drought, cold winters, cattle trampling and heavy grazing, vandalism and failed water delivery systems, wildfire, introduction of [[Mosquitofish]] and other nonnative species, and possible predation by [[Tiger Salamander]].
- **Water quality:** the site table lists water quality as monitored by the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]]; the document notes that low oxygen was suspected at one site.

Several sites lie outside the species' historic range or in drainages with few perennial waters, which the report uses to judge long-term suitability.

## Key Findings

**Scope and method**
- The report assesses approximately 50 sites, each documented with a standard site record (location, stocking history, survey table and recommendations).
- Survey thoroughness and percent of habitat surveyed were inconsistently recorded in historical monitoring. Where these were missing, estimates were based on personal knowledge and communication with prior collectors. Surveys by [[Brian Bagley]], [[Lee Simons]], [[Matt Brown]] and others were judged thorough unless otherwise noted, under a directive to survey all available habitat.

**Sites provisionally extirpated (examples from the 1995 group)**
- [[Big Springs]] (Site #84): stocked with 500 topminnow in 1985; flooding and silting removed the population. Only one fish was recorded in 1991, and none were found after that. Recommendation: annual dredging by BLM would be needed to maintain habitat.
- [[Sycamore Spring]] (Site #21): supported a population from 1982 through 1992. Vandalism and flood damage to the water system led to failure. USFWS declared extirpation in September 1992. The Globe District advised against restocking and proposed alternative sites in Seven Mile Wash, Hess Canyon, West Fork of Pinto Creek and Arnett Creek.
- [[Peoples Canyon]] (Site #28): a 1989 flood reportedly removed all fish. The site is outside the historic range of both topminnow and [[Desert Pupfish]].
- [[Badger Springs]] (Site #180): the drainage was dry in 1993; in 1996 only two small pools remained. Recommendation: a more intensive survey of the Agua Fria River downstream from the Badger Spring Wash confluence.
- [[Yellowstone Tank]] (Site #23): persisted from 1982 to 1987; dried between 1987 and 1989, and was dry again in 1993.

**Sites with continuing potential**
- [[Campbell Flat Spring]] (Site #25): persisted 4-6 years; Category 3. Recommendation: re-evaluate for future augmentation stocking.
- [[Cottonwood Artesian]] (Site #77): persisted at least 11 years. The trough was cleaned out around 1991-92, probably eliminating fish from it. Tonto National Forest built conservation pools in December 1991. Recommendation: restock.
- [[Government Spring]] (Site #33): persisted six to eight years and still has good surface flow. Recommendation: restock.
- [[Campbell Flat Spring]] and [[Campaign Creek]] (Site #91): the Campaign Creek population was present from 1983 until sometime between 1988 and 1991; the report recommends reintroduction.
- [[Mud Spring Tank]] (Site #?): mosquitofish were introduced in 1985-86 and depleted the population; the report suggests verifying mosquitofish removal before restocking.

**Overall conclusions**
- Most sites are provisionally considered extirpated, and the report states that these determinations are not final.
- Several sites are recommended for restocking or additional habitat work, and some require a decision on the source population for restocking.
- Prolonged drought, flooding, cattle impacts and nonnative fishes were the most frequently cited causes of failure.
- Full site-by-site survey records, the list of about 40 surveyors and their affiliations, and the literature cited are included in the report but are summarized here.
