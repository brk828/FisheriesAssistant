# Redrock Canyon Renovation Project: Draft Environmental Assessment and Public Comments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #freshwater-conservation

## Executive Summary

This document is a 2010 Draft Environmental Assessment (DEA) prepared by the [[Coronado National Forest]] Sierra Vista Ranger District, together with the cover letter and two substantive public comment letters (from Paul C. Marsh, PhD, and from Sally and Jerome Stefferud) and a short record of the forest's transmittal correspondence. The proposal would authorize the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) to remove nonnative fishes and bullfrogs from the Redrock Canyon watershed using piscicides ([[Rotenone]] and/or [[Antimycin A]]) and mechanical methods, then reestablish native fishes, amphibians and aquatic reptiles through salvage and restocking. The Forest Supervisor's decision is limited to authorizing chemical nonnative removal. Both commenters oppose the draft as presented, arguing that it is narrower than the 2008 approved project, omits a fish barrier and reinvasion controls, lacks clear scope, and relies on an undisclosed alternative and an inaccessible project record.

## Background & Description

**Project setting.** Redrock Canyon lies in the Canelo Hills in the Gila River Basin. The roughly 20,000-acre watershed is mostly Forest Service land, with two private inholdings, and ranges from about 5,900 ft to 4,075 ft. Redrock Canyon is a headwater tributary of [[Sonoita Creek]], comprising about 15 percent of the drainage area above the Sonoita Creek gauge.

**Species of concern.** The DEA identifies four native fish (longfin dace, speckled dace, desert sucker, and the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]]), along with the federally listed [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] and [[Sonora Tiger Salamander]], Sonora mud turtle, black-necked gartersnake, and Mexican gartersnake. Five nonnative aquatic species are established: mosquitofish, largemouth bass, green sunfish, bluegill and American bullfrog. Commenters add that [[Gila Chub]] (endangered) historically occupied the canyon and was part of the 2008 project, and that the stream contains a natural bedrock cascade known as the Falls.

**Proposed action (as described in the DEA).** Four steps repeated as needed over five years: (1) salvage native animals before treatment; (2) apply piscicides and mechanical removal to eradicate nonnative fish and reduce bullfrogs; (3) restock native species, using captive Gila topminnow and translocations from [[Harshaw Creek]] and Sonoita Creek; (4) monitor for at least five years. Chemical treatments would be applied at low flow, with a second application within two weeks and up to four years of retreatment.

**Procedural history.** A June 2008 Final EA (Native Fish Restoration in Redrock Canyon) approved a project including a fish barrier, piscicide treatment, salvage and restocking, and federal funding through a Reclamation/FWS program. The Forest Service withdrew its decision in October 2008 after one appeal. A March 2009 meeting announced that no barrier would be built on the Forest. The 2010 draft is a new, narrower proposal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Drivers of decline.** The DEA states that nonnative fishes and bullfrogs are now the major cause of native declines, through predation on early life stages, competition, hybridization and habitat change. Drought since 1995 has shrunk perennial water and concentrates nonnatives.
- **Gila topminnow.** Only one individual was found in canyon surveys since 2005. The DEA states it has been "apparently extirpated"; commenters dispute this, noting it is less than a year since the last record and that FWS practice requires more than a decade of failed sampling before declaring extirpation.
- **Routes of reinvasion.** The DEA attributes limited risk to downstream sources, citing AGFD personal communications. Commenters cite a 2007 Reclamation study by the Stefferuds (18 streams in Arizona and Sonora) showing common movement of fish through intermittent channels, including mosquitofish, into lower Redrock Canyon. Stock tanks (about 15 earthen tanks, 3 perennial) are also identified as stepping stones.
- **Water use.** Roughly 45 wells and 15 miles of pipeline exist in the watershed. A proposed Kunde Allotment water development includes a well within 150 feet of the channel, located less than 0.5 miles upstream of the perennial reach at the Falls. Commenters rank water development as the second most important impact on native aquatic species after nonnatives.
- **Ecosystem effects of piscicides.** The DEA expects fish kills and effects on tadpoles and turtles. Commenters note that rotenone is toxic to some turtles and larval amphibians. Invertebrate effects are variable, with recovery reported from weeks to years.
- **Human health.** The DEA concludes that drinking-water and fish-consumption risks are low under label use and closures. It cites potassium permanganate as a neutralizing agent and recommends closures and posting.

## Key Findings

**The DEA**
- Decision scope: The Forest Supervisor will decide only whether to authorize AGFD's chemical and mechanical nonnative removal, and whether a Finding of No Significant Impact or an EIS is required.
- Native species to be restocked: longfin dace, speckled dace, desert sucker, Gila topminnow, Chiricahua leopard frog, Sonora tiger salamander, Sonora mud turtle and Mexican gartersnake.
- The fish barrier was dropped in favor of an undisclosed Sonoita Creek alternative (PR #57). The DEA also states that no tank breaching is needed because stock tanks are managed to limit reinvasion.
- Likely effects are judged "may affect, likely to adversely affect" for Gila topminnow, Chiricahua leopard frog and Sonora tiger salamander, and "not likely to adversely affect" for lesser long-nosed bat.
- Recreation closures are limited to treatment sites for a short time. Environmental justice impacts are judged not to occur.

**Public comments (Marsh, 2010)**
- The EA must reinstate the fish barrier, explicitly include all five native fishes (including Gila chub), and commit to long-term barrier maintenance, monitoring and reinvasion prevention.
- The Redrock Canyon watershed should be closed to recreational angling to prevent bait-fish release.
- The lack of a traceable reference for "PR" documents, which require a trip to Hereford, impedes public review.

**Public comments (Stefferud, 2010)**
- The draft contains verbatim material from Sally Stefferud without attribution, and the Stefferuds were not involved in this version. They call this plagiarism.
- The purpose and need is defined circularly as the proposed action, which eliminates all other alternatives.
- The 2008 barrier alternative and a barrier alternative under negotiation were improperly excluded. The only "new information" identified after 2008 relates to Parkinson's disease literature, which the draft itself considers irrelevant.
- Two federal mitigation barriers (Redrock Canyon and Sonoita Creek) required by a 2008 FWS biological opinion on the Central Arizona Project remain outstanding.
- The Falls is not a complete barrier, and the draft's effects analysis is built on this unverified assumption.
- Cumulative effects of planned livestock water developments on the Kunde Allotment must be analysed in detail.
- Multiple technical errors are flagged, including incorrect statements about speckled dace, mosquitofish distribution, Gila topminnow extirpation, and the Gila topminnow recovery plan status.
- Records requested under FOIA had not been received by the time of comment; the commenters state that public review is effectively limited.
- Conclusion: The commenters oppose the proposal in its current form, but support nonnative removal and restocking of all five native fishes, and offer their expertise to help redesign the project as a comprehensive, scientifically supportable effort.

**Forest Service correspondence**
- The transmittal letter dated February 2, 2010, opens a 30-day comment period from publication in the Arizona Daily Star, and sets out rules for appeal eligibility under 36 CFR 215.2.
