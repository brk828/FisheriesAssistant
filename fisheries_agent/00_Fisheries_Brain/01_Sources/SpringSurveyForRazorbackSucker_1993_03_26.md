# 1993 Spring Survey for Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub — Lower Lake Mohave Camp

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #ConservationSurvey

## Executive Summary

This report documents the sixth annual multi-agency spring survey (March 14–19, 1993) targeting the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) in the lower reaches of [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. Co-led by the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Nongame Branch) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services), the effort combined trammel netting, hoop (trap) netting, and nighttime electrofishing along coves from Chili Pepper Cove to Arrowhead Cove. Only **four razorback suckers** were captured (one juvenile recapture, three untagged adults), and **no bonytail chub** were collected or observed. The survey confirmed successful recruitment from reintroduction efforts via the recapture of a PIT-tagged juvenile razorback sucker originally spawned in Yuma Cove and released from Davis Cove. Incidental data on non-native sportfish (largemouth bass, carp, bluegill, striped bass) and native fish habitat associations were also recorded.

## Background & Description

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Adults captured (n=3):** Mean total length (TL) = 579 mm; mean weight = 2,303 g.
- **Individual electrofished adults:**
  - PIT 7F7D165F23 — TL 625 mm, WT 2,750 g, Female (Arrowhead Cove)
  - PIT 7F7D16433C — TL 558 mm, WT 1,965 g, Female (Desert Cove)
  - PIT 7F7D164A48 — TL 555 mm, WT 2,193 g, Male (Yoke Cove)
- **Juvenile recapture:** PIT 7F7D174A23 — TL 284 mm, WT 314 g (Shoshone Cove); originally released as hatchery-reared stock from [[Davis Cove]].
- Trammel-netted individual (Yoke Cove) was a recapture of PIT 7F7D165F23, previously electrofished earlier in the same week (later recaptured again at TL 630 mm, WT 2,760 g).
- **No [[Bonytail Chub]]** were captured or observed during the entire survey.

### Non-Native / Incidental Species
- **Largemouth Bass (netting, n=29):** Mean TL 360 mm (range 240–504 mm); mean weight 800 g (range 212–2,295 g).
- **Largemouth Bass (electrofishing, first 2 nights, n=12):** Mean TL 409 mm (range 274–553 mm); mean weight 1,193 g (range 214–2,739 g).
- **Largemouth Bass (combined, n=41):** Mean TL 374 mm (range 240–553 mm); mean weight 947 g (range 212–2,739 g).
- Scale samples taken from largemouth bass for age/growth analysis (transferred to AGFD Region 3).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat Associations — Razorback Sucker
- **Arrowhead Cove (north):** Outer cove zone, cobble substrate.
- **Desert Cove:** Shallow end, ~1 m depth, silty substrate, among scattered small inundated shrubs.
- **Yoke Cove:** Narrow (~20 m) back channel, ~2 m depth, silty bottom, woody vegetation lining channel edges; a second unretrieved individual observed here.
- **Shoshone Cove (juvenile):** Edge of inundated [[Tamarix]] (salt cedar), ~0.75 m water depth, silty-red substrate.

These associations reinforce the species' known preference for shallow, silty, vegetated cove margins as nursery and adult habitat within [[Lake Mohave]].

### Bonytail Chub — Spawning Habitat Hypothesis
Trammel nets were deliberately repositioned onto shoreline points with **cobble/gravel substrate**, hypothesized as important spawning habitat for bonytail chub. However, **calm, non-windy conditions** during the survey period likely reduced wave action over these points, potentially decreasing their attractiveness to spawning fish and contributing to the lack of detections.

## Key Findings

- **Reintroduction success confirmed:** Recapture of a PIT-tagged juvenile razorback sucker (spawned in Yuma Cove, released from Davis Cove in 1993) in Shoshone Cove demonstrates survival and dispersal of hatchery-augmented stock — a critical validation for the [[Razorback Sucker Recovery Program]].
- **Low native fish detections:** Only 4 razorback suckers captured (3 adults, 1 juvenile recapture) across combined netting and electrofishing effort of 618 minutes; capture rate of adults via electrofishing was ~0.008 RBS/min overall.
- **Zero bonytail chub detections**, despite targeted gear placement on cobble/gravel spawning points — raising questions about population status or gear/environmental limitations (lack of wind-driven wave action).
- **Effort quantification:** Electrofishing totaled 618 minutes across multiple coves (Arrowhead, Desert, Chili Pepper, Red Light, Tyro, Pyrite, Greenlight, Shoshone, Prospect, Burro, Gasoline Alley, Yoke); trammel/hoop netting totaled 17 net-nights (148 net-hours).
- **Environmental conditions:** Lake elevation at Katherine Landing = 642.1 ft above mean sea level; water temperature = 13.5°C (9 am, March 19, 1993).
- **Management recommendation:** Given the limited yield relative to effort, the report recommends the [[Native Fish Work Group]] reconsider survey intensity for future years — potentially reducing net deployment and personnel while maintaining a monitoring index, given the demonstrated value of tracking reintroduced cohorts.
- **Multi-agency collaboration:** Survey involved AGFD (Nongame, Research, Fisheries Branches, Region 3), USFWS (Arizona and Nevada Ecological Services), Arizona State University, BLM Kingman Resource Office, and Mohave Community College — reflecting the coordinated recovery framework for [[Colorado River Native Fish]] conservation.
