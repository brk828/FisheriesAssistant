# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program: Green River Gila Monitoring (FY93)

## Executive Summary
This FY93 annual report documents the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program]]'s monitoring efforts for [[Humpback Chub]] and sympatric [[Gila]] species in the Desolation/Gray Canyons reach of the [[Green River]], Utah. Conducted by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] under Principal Investigator Leo Lentsch, the study represents a continuation of monitoring efforts begun in 1985. The August 1993 field trip (8 days, 7 sampling nights) employed trammel nets, hoop nets, seines, and electrofishing across four long-term trend sites (Cedar Ridge, Below Surprise, Joe Hutch, Coal Creek) and three "wild-card" sites (Rock Creek, Chandler Falls, Rabbit Valley). Key objectives included developing indices of relative abundance and year-class strength for [[Humpback Chub]], and resolving taxonomic ambiguity among morphologically "intermediate" chub forms found in this reach. A total of 15 fish species (6 native, 9 non-native) were documented, with encouraging signs of Age 1+ chub recruitment and strong young-of-year (YOY) numbers, potentially linked to beneficial 1993 spring flows.

## Background & Description
- **Taxonomic Ambiguity**: Many chubs collected in Desolation/Gray Canyons display a morphology described as "intermediate" between [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), colloquially termed the **"Big Head Desolation Humpback" morph**.
- **Sampling for Taxonomy**: Tissue samples (for allozyme and DNA analysis) and morphometric data (for Principal Component Analysis) were collected annually to resolve this taxonomic confusion.
- **Photographic/Meristic Documentation**: All *Gila* spp. exceeding 150mm TL were photographed; standard meristic counts were collected from 42 *Gila* spp. individuals.
- **Size Ranges (this trip)**:
  - Juvenile/adult chubs (*Gila* spp.): TL range 108–370 mm (n=48)
  - Sub-adult [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): TL range 351–420 mm (n=5)
- **Putative Bonytail**: Two individuals collected in the lower canyon exhibited morphology "more characteristic of bonytail than any other species," suggesting possible presence of *Gila elegans* or further intermediate forms.
- **Aging**: Scales were collected from all captured chubs for age and growth analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type**: Large, turbid desert river canyon system characterized by eddy lines, pool habitats, backwaters, and eddy return channels.
- **Environmental Conditions (Aug 1993)**:
  - Flows: 1800–2000 cfs
  - Main channel temperature: 21–26°C (diel variation)
  - Backwater temperature: up to 28°C
  - Turbidity: moderate-to-high at trip start, decreasing by days 4–5, spiking sharply after thunderstorms near Coal Creek (Aug 29)
- **Microhabitat Use**: Low-velocity habitats were predominantly **eddy return channels** rather than silty backwaters, a notable reduction in backwater silt load compared to previous years.
- **YOY Concentration Zone**: The highest concentrations of both YOY chubs and YOY [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] were found near **Three Fords Rapid** (particularly RM 154.3, ~1.5 miles downstream), though both species occurred canyon-wide.
- **Community Composition**: Native species present alongside chubs included [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*C. discobolus*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*). Non-native community dominated by [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*).
- **Site Productivity**: The **Chandler Canyon/Chandler Falls** site was identified as the most productive sampling location for chub captures via trammel net.

## Key Findings
- **Species Richness**: 15 total species collected (6 native, 9 non-native) via combined seine, hoop net, trammel net, and electrofishing methods.
- **Chub Abundance**: 48 juvenile/adult chubs and 160 YOY *Gila* spp. collected; most classified generically as *Gila* spp. due to intermediate morphology, though some confirmed humpbacks and two putative bonytail-like fish were noted.
- **Recapture Rate**: 3 chub recaptures yielded a **6.25% trip recapture rate**; all recaptured individuals showed **zero net movement** from original capture location, suggesting high site fidelity.
- **Colorado Pikeminnow**: 5 sub-adults collected (2 net mortalities in 1" mesh trammel nets), plus ~190 YOY identified and released. Catch rates were moderate relative to historical trips (highest catches historically occur in July; lowest in September).
- **Gear Performance**:
  - Trammel nets (6 nets, ~350 cumulative net-hours): 22 *Gila* spp. collected; all chubs >200mm TL; net mortality occurred only in 1" mesh (prompting a shift toward 1/2" mesh nets to reduce mortality).
  - Hoop nets (4 nets/night): 11 *Gila* spp. and 1 squawfish collected; also yielded an adult [[Black Crappie]] at Coal Creek — a notable non-native occurrence.
  - Electrofishing: 9 juvenile/adult *Gila* spp. collected; most effective in shallow cobble runs; only [[Smallmouth Bass]] captured via this method.
  - Minnow traps were **discontinued** from the protocol due to consistently poor yield.
  - Seines (47 hauls, 27 habitats, RM 213.4–147): Good numbers of both *Gila* spp. and squawfish; catch dominated by non-native cyprinids (red shiner, fathead minnow) among smaller fish.
- **Recruitment Signal**: Presence of Age 1+ chubs and strong YOY numbers suggests 1993 spring flows may have benefited both reproduction and Age-1 recruitment — a positive indicator after prior concerns about recruitment gaps in older year classes.
- **Budget**: FY93 allocated funds of $10,000, fully expended, with study reported "on track" for data submission.
- **Programmatic Recommendation**: Future monitoring should specifically target assessment of chub recruitment into older year classes, as current data show limited information on this life-stage transition. A follow-up sampling effort at the Chandler Canyon site was also recommended.
