# First List of Indiana Stoneflies (Plecoptera), 1945

#aquatic-insects #plecoptera #freshwater-biology #stoneflies

## Executive Summary

This 1945 paper by W. E. Ricker of [[Indiana University]] presents the first compiled list of stonefly species recorded from [[Indiana]]. The list contains 38 species in seven families, assembled from the [[Purdue University]] and Indiana University teaching and research collections, supplemented by unpublished records from [[Thomas H. Frison]] of the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]]. The author expects the total to rise substantially, possibly by as much as half, with further collecting. The one unexpected record is [[Nemoura Vallicularia]], which is common enough in Indiana but was unknown from Illinois.

## Background & Description

[[Plecoptera]] (stoneflies) are aquatic insect larvae (nymphs) that develop in running water. Adults are terrestrial and are collected mainly in spring and early summer. The paper notes that student collecting at [[Bloomington]] and [[Lafayette]] has been concentrated from March to May, which shapes the seasonal pattern of the records.

Two sources of information shaped the list:

- **Collections:** Specimens were drawn from the Purdue and Indiana University collections, with the author's own collections held at Indiana University. Records are tagged "IU" or "PU" by repository.
- **Frison's work:** Frison's extensive collecting and rearing of adults from nymphs made it possible to separate closely related adults in difficult genera such as [[Acroneuria]], [[Isoperla]], and [[Allocapnia]]. Many Indiana records appear only in his publications, notably his 1935, 1937, and 1942 papers.

The classification follows the author's 1943 work on the stoneflies of southwestern British Columbia.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper describes Indiana's stonefly fauna in terms of its geography and waterways:

- **[[Northeastern Lake District]]:** Occasional spring streams here may hold outlying populations of a northern fauna and should be searched.
- **[[Ohio River]]:** Southern species may extend into the state along this border.
- **Central region:** New additions are expected, especially among the small species of the genus [[Allocapnia]].
- **Major river systems:** Many records come from the [[Wabash River]], [[White River]], and [[Tippecanoe River]], as well as creeks in state parks such as [[Mccormick's Creek State Park]] and [[Turkey Run State Park]].

Records span many localities and the flight periods of the species. Most material was collected from late winter (February) through summer (September), with the greatest concentration in March through June.

## Key Findings

**Species inventory**
- The list contains 38 species in seven families: [[Pteronarciidae]], [[Nemouridae]], [[Leuctridae]], [[Capniidae]], [[Perlidae]], [[Perlodidae]], and [[Chloroperlidae]].
- The largest genera are *Isoperla* (9 species), *Acroneuria* (6), *Allocapnia* (4), and *Taeniopteryx* (3), with *Hydroperla* at 3.
- Notable records include *Pteronarcys pictetii*, *Taeniopteryx maura*, *Taeniopteryx fasciata*, *Neoperla clymene*, *Perlesta placida*, and *Hastaperla brevis*. Several are represented by large series.

**Unexpected record**
- *Nemoura vallicularia* was taken at both Lafayette and Bloomington by casual collectors. It was not anticipated from earlier work and is not known from Illinois.

**Taxonomic clarification**
- Frison's rearing of adults from nymphs was essential for resolving difficult species complexes, especially in *Acroneuria*, *Isoperla*, and *Allocapnia*.

**Expected future discoveries**
- The author predicts the list could grow by about 50% through collecting, particularly in the northeastern lake district, along the Ohio River, and among small *Allocapnia* species.

**Limitations**
- The list rests partly on teaching collections and casual collecting rather than systematic surveys. The author had not yet made systematic collections in Indiana, and many of Frison's Indiana records were unpublished at the time.
