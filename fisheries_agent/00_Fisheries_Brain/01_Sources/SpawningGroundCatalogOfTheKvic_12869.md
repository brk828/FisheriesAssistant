# Spawning Ground Catalog of the Kvichak River System, Bristol Bay, Alaska

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Special Scientific Report (No. 488), compiled by Robert L. Demory, Russell F. Orrell and Donald R. Heinle of the [[Fisheries Research Institute]] (University of Washington), catalogs the spawning streams, ponds and beaches used by [[Red Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*) in the [[Kvichak River]] system of [[Bristol Bay]], Alaska. The catalog compiles spawning ground surveys conducted since 1955, together with earlier partial surveys dating back to the 1920s, and is intended to support management and research agencies with data in one volume. It records physical stream descriptions, potential spawning area, red salmon run characteristics and chronological survey counts for 71 stream and pond areas and 23 beach areas.

## Background & Description

The Kvichak River system is the main contributor to the Bristol Bay red salmon fishery. From 1932 through 1962 the Kvichak-Naknek fishery provided about 60 percent of the total Bristol Bay catch, and during 1955-62 the Kvichak system alone had an estimated average total run (catch plus escapement) of more than 8 million red salmon.

Surveys were financed initially by Bristol Bay salmon canners and later under a [[Saltonstall-Kennedy Act]] contract administered by the [[Bureau of Commercial Fisheries]]. In 1961-62 surveys were expanded to measure spawning area utilized and potential spawning area, to estimate the carrying capacity of the Kvichak spawning grounds. Field observations were made largely by aerial survey, supplemented by ground, skiff and scuba methods, with aerial support from pilot Oren B. Hudson of Iliamna Airways.

The catalog follows a standard format. Each entry gives a stream number encoding the lake and distance from the outlet (tributaries are given as trunk-stream distance with R or L for side), location, physical description (length, width, depth, bottom gravel composition, watershed, gradient, water velocity, flow, temperatures), red salmon run data and survey summaries. Potential spawning area is calculated by deducting non-spawnable pools, riffles and areas with velocities above 5 feet per second.

Key geographic features covered include [[Iliamna Lake]], [[Lake Clark]], [[Kijik Lake]] and [[Gibraltar Lake]], with five maps (figs. 1-5) showing the district and cataloged streams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Spawning habitat is described as ranging from clean gravel streams and spring-fed ponds to coarse boulder channels, silted lower reaches and glacially turbid rivers. Bottom quality (gravel size classes <1/8", 1/8"-3", 3"-12", >12") is a central descriptor, as is water velocity; many large glacial rivers, such as the [[Newhalen River]] and [[Pile River]], exceed 5 fps and are poor spawning habitat. Water temperatures recorded during surveys ranged roughly from 4 to 18 °C, and streams are typically shallow, willow-lined and drain open tundra or glacial valleys.

Beach spawning on lake shorelines and islands is documented separately, with depths of spawning reported from a few feet to over 100 feet at some sites. Shelter, access, personal-use fisheries and co-occurring fish species (for example [[Dolly Varden]], rainbow trout, grayling, northern pike, whitefish and lake trout) and wildlife (notably brown bear, moose and seabirds) are recorded for each area. Flood-driven channel changes are noted in several places, including the 1927 and 1959 diversions between [[Tomkok Creek]], [[Chekok Creek]] and [[Canyon Creek]].

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** 71 stream and pond areas and 23 beach areas cataloged. The index lists roughly 80 named stream and pond entries, many with multiple subsections or forks.
- **Regional importance:** The Kvichak system contributed about 60% of the Bristol Bay catch (1932-1962) and averaged more than 8 million red salmon in total run (1955-62).
- **Largest spawning systems (potential spawning area):**
  - [[Kvichak River]]: 8,992,720 sq yd total accessible area (1,858.0 acres); potential 929.0 acres.
  - [[Newhalen River]]: 3,667,268 sq yd total accessible (757.7 acres); potential 303.1 acres; escapement to 300,000 or more, with 5-9% of the Kvichak total.
  - [[Pile River]]: 1,771,924 sq yd total accessible (366.1 acres), but only 13% potential due to velocities above 5 fps.
  - [[Copper River (Iliamna Lake)]]: escapement range 10,000 to 1,000,000; about 10% of the Kvichak total in 1956.
- **Highest-escapement sites:** [[Knutson Bay]] (escapement 3,000 to 1,000,000; 10% or more in 1960), [[Canyon Creek]] (up to 112,000), [[Pedro Creek and Ponds]] (up to 120,000), [[Gibraltar Creek]] (up to 250,000; 5-9% in 1960).
- **Timing:** Most runs occupied streams from late July or early August into September, with peak spawning typically mid-August to mid-September. Late-season beach spawning extended into October and November at several sites.
- **Carrying capacity:** Surveys in 1961-62 began measuring utilized versus potential spawning area to estimate carrying capacity, an explicit management goal.
- **Data limitations:** Many entries lack gradient, temperature or pools-and-riffles data, and survey coverage is uneven (some beaches surveyed only from 1960). The authors note that several beach areas may be omitted and that pool and riffle distribution data should be gathered later.
- **Hydrologic changes:** Floods in 1927 and 1959 redirected flow between Chekok, Tomkok and Canyon creeks, causing confusion in U.S.G.S. map names; corrections are documented.
- **Naming errors:** Several U.S.G.S. names were corrected in the catalog (for example, Chekok Creek on the map should be Tomkok Creek; Trout Creek was previously confused with Little Gibraltar Creek).
