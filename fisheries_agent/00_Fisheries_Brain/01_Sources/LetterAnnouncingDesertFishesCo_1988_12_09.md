# Letter Announcing Desert Fishes Council Meeting in Tempe, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Ecological Services, Phoenix, AZ), dated December 9, 1988, announcing an upcoming meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Council]] in Tempe, Arizona (January 26–27, 1989). While not a primary scientific research paper, the letter is valuable as a **historical conservation record**, documenting the composition of a multi-agency recovery team and enumerating a suite of imperiled desert fish species under active management concern in the American Southwest. The letter also flags specific taxonomic, recovery-planning, and habitat-restoration issues slated for discussion, offering insight into the state of desert fish conservation science in the late 1980s.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (meristics, body proportions, coloration) are provided in this source, as it is a correspondence/meeting-planning document rather than a taxonomic or field study. However, the letter references an active **taxonomic question** regarding [[Ictalurus pricei]] (Yaqui Catfish), indicating unresolved species-level classification issues within the genus at the time, likely relating to hybridization or distinctiveness from the closely related [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (Channel Catfish).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The species listed inhabit a range of desert aquatic habitats across the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, including:
- **Lotic (flowing water) systems**: rivers and streams of the [[Gila River Basin]], [[Colorado River Basin]], and [[Yaqui River Basin]].
- **Lentic/wetland systems**: cienegas (desert marshland springs), exemplified by the referenced **Bog Hole cienega creation project**, an active habitat-restoration effort.
- Many taxa represent **basin-restricted populations** of wider-ranging species, explicitly limited to the lower Colorado River Basin in this team's purview (e.g., [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]]), implying management is structured around distinct evolutionary/geographic units rather than treating species as range-wide entities.
- These species collectively occupy niches from small headwater specialists (e.g., [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Virgin Spinedace]]) to larger-bodied riverine species (e.g., [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]]), reflecting the ecological diversity within a single regional conservation program despite the overall aridity and habitat fragility of desert systems.

## Key Findings
- **Meeting Purpose**: Coordination among the [[Desert Fishes Council]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] team coordinators, academic researchers (including Dr. W. L. Minckley of Arizona State University), and multiple state/federal consultants to address desert fish recovery efforts.
- **Agenda Items of Scientific Relevance**:
  1. Potential reorganization of recovery teams, including splitting species into separate teams — suggesting the existing single-team model was becoming unwieldy given the number of species involved.
  2. Review of status and next steps for ongoing **species recovery plans**.
  3. The **Bog Hole cienega creation project** — an applied habitat restoration/creation effort for desert wetland fish habitat.
  4. Discussion of newly passed **1988 Endangered Species Act amendments** and their implications for management.
  5. Formation/coordination of an **Upper Basin Razorback Sucker management team**, indicating basin-specific management stratification for [[Razorback Sucker]].
  6. Unresolved **taxonomic work on [[Ictalurus pricei]]** (Yaqui Catfish), highlighting ongoing systematics uncertainty.
- **Species Roster (18 taxa)**: The attached list represents one of the most complete contemporary rosters of federally/regionally recognized imperiled desert fishes under a single coordinating team, spanning the families Cyprinidae, Catostomidae, Ictaluridae, and Poeciliidae:

  - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* and *P. o. sonoriensis*)
