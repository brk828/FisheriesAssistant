# Limnology of the Upper North Platte Reservoir System, Wyoming

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation technical report (REC-ERC-81-10, July 1981) presents the baseline limnological characterization of four reservoirs in series on the [[North Platte River]] in Wyoming: [[Seminoe Reservoir]], [[Kortes Reservoir]], [[Pathfinder Reservoir]], and [[Alcova Reservoir]]. Surveys ran from August 1976 through October 1979 and covered physical and chemical conditions, light, nutrients, heavy metals, [[Phytoplankton]], [[Zooplankton]], [[Benthos]], and sediments. The study combined drought years (1977) with high-runoff years (1978–79). Its stated purpose was to support the [[North Platte River Hydroelectric Study]] by evaluating environmental effects of power augmentation alternatives and to inform the wider understanding of multireservoir systems. The authors describe it as the first comprehensive limnological study of an entire Bureau multireservoir system, and they note that results are preliminary because sampling was limited to the May–October season.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** The North Platte River flows from north-central Colorado through southeastern Wyoming. The four uppermost reservoirs are listed from upstream to downstream in Table 1 of the source:

- **Seminoe Reservoir:** Max. volume 1.255 × 10⁹ m³, operated mainly for power production. It sits at the confluence of the North Platte and [[Medicine Bow River]] (85% and 15% of inflow).
- **Kortes Reservoir:** Max. volume 5.878 × 10⁸ m³, a small reregulating impoundment for power releases.
- **Pathfinder Reservoir:** Max. volume 1.253 × 10⁹ m³, used for storage. It receives inflow from the [[Miracle Mile]] and the [[Sweetwater River]] (10%).
- **Alcova Reservoir:** Max. volume 2.274 × 10⁹ m³, delivers irrigation water to the [[Kendrick Project]] via the Casper Canal.

Between Kortes and Pathfinder, the North Platte flows freely for about 6.4 km as the Miracle Mile, a "blue-ribbon" [[Brown Trout]] fishery. The surrounding terrain is arid, windy sagebrush steppe. Average annual precipitation ranges from about 281 mm at Casper to 265 mm at Rawlins.

**Previous studies.** Earlier work included a 1951 [[Public Health Service]] pollution investigation, 1953 and 1959 sedimentation surveys of Pathfinder and Seminoe, and a 1975 [[EPA National Eutrophication Survey]] of Seminoe (which classified it as eutrophic in the river arms, moderately eutrophic in the upper basin, and mesotrophic near the dam). A 1978 water-quality model by Rinehart and Kerr identified TDS as the only pertinent modelable parameter.

**Methods.** Reservoir stations were sampled at roughly 2 m depth intervals with a Hydrolab probe. Water samples for chemistry were taken with a Van Dorn sampler. Chlorophyll was measured spectrophotometrically from filtered samples at 0.1–15 m. Zooplankton were collected with Clarke-Bumpus and closing nets (No. 10 and No. 20 mesh). Benthos was collected with Ponar and Ekman dredges and sorted on a 600 µm screen. Nine main reservoir stations and six stream stations formed the core network. The sampling schedule varied by year, with the fullest coverage in 1977–79.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry.** The reservoirs are alkaline, hard, and moderately saline. Mean salinity is 369 mg/L, mean TDS is about 318 mg/L, and calcium carbonate hardness is about 184 mg/L. Conductivity ranges from 240 to 577 µS/cm, and pH is always above 7.0. Calcium, sodium, and magnesium are the major cations, and bicarbonate and sulfate are the major anions. TDS and salinity correlate strongly (r = 0.98). A TDS mass balance shows that the Medicine Bow River, with mean TDS of 810 mg/L, supplies about 40% of Seminoe's salinity input from about 15% of its inflow.

**Thermal and oxygen regime.** All four reservoirs are dimictic. Summer stratification is weak and brief because the deep outlets draw off cool hypolimnetic water. Maximum surface temperatures reached about 17–20 °C and bottom temperatures about 14–18 °C. Bottom dissolved oxygen reached a minimum of 0.2 mg/L at S-1 (Seminoe) in 1978 and 4.0 mg/L at K-1 (Kortes). No permanent anoxic zones were found.

**Light and turbidity.** Spring runoff turbidity reduces light in the Seminoe river arms. A turbid underflow then passes through Seminoe, and Kortes and the Miracle Mile become turbid in late summer and fall. Similar density flows of sediment occur in Pathfinder.

**Nutrients and primary production.** Nutrient availability follows annual runoff. In the drought year 1977, phosphorus was detectable throughout the summer and accumulated in the North Platte arm of Pathfinder. This accumulation supported the largest bluegreen bloom and the highest chlorophyll concentrations recorded (mean 120.69 mg/m³ at Pathfinder in 1977). In the high-runoff years, phosphorus appeared only in late summer. Trophic state ranges from moderately eutrophic in the river arms to mesotrophic in the deep basins. The Miracle Mile benthic production reflects nutrient-rich deep discharges from Seminoe and Kortes.

**Biota.** Early-summer phytoplankton are dominated by diatoms (e.g., *Tabellaria*). Late-summer phytoplankton are dominated by the bluegreen alga [[Aphanizomenon flos-aquae]], which can exceed 85,000 cells/mL. The alga produces an alphan toxin that suppresses other biota. Zooplankton decline by 30–50% after the bloom starts. Copepods dominate most of the season, and rotifers increase in late summer. Benthos is dominated by [[Chironomidae]] at Seminoe and by [[Oligochaeta]] at Alcova. Chironomid share declines with increasing depth (r = −0.83). Alcova's fish yield (36.75 lb/acre) exceeded the value predicted by the morphoedaphic index, which the authors attribute possibly to a nutrient subsidy from Pathfinder's deep discharge.

## Key Findings

1. **Water quality:** All four reservoirs are alkaline, hard, and moderately saline (mean salinity 369 mg/L; hardness 184 mg/L; pH always above 7.0).
2. **Mass balance:** The Medicine Bow River supplies about 40% of Seminoe's salinity from about 15% of its inflow. Calculated Kortes outflow TDS (299 mg/L) closely matched the observed value (297 mg/L).
3. **Stratification:** Summer stratification is weak and brief, and all reservoirs are dimictic. Bottom oxygen minima were 0.2 mg/L (Seminoe) and 4.0 mg/L (Kortes), with no severe reducing conditions.
4. **Heavy metals:** Iron often exceeds the EPA domestic supply criterion of 0.30 mg/L, and manganese sometimes exceeds 0.05 mg/L. Most iron appears bound to suspended particles. Zinc, copper, and lead did not reach criteria levels. The authors conclude that heavy metals are not a significant hazard to biota.
5. **Sediments:** Organic content and heavy metal content correlate with clay-sized fines (r = 0.92 and r = 0.91).
6. **Nutrient limitation:** Early-summer phosphorus limitation shifts to late-summer nitrogen limitation in wet years, favoring bluegreen blooms. In dry years, phosphorus accumulates in Pathfinder.
7. **Runoff control:** Runoff, system operation, and deep outlets jointly control nutrient dynamics and primary production.
8. **Productivity:** Mean chlorophyll a reached 120.69 mg/m³ at Pathfinder in 1977. It dropped 61% in 1978 and a further 45% in 1979.
9. **Recommendations:** The authors call for permanent transect-based stations, year-round sampling, monthly surveys for at least one year in affected basins, and studies of the N-P budget, sediment transport, and fish population dynamics in cooperation with the [[Wyoming Game and Fish Department]].
