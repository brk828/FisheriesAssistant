# Flow Regimes for Restoration of Endangered Razorback Sucker and Colorado Pikeminnow: Interim Recommendations for the Palisade-to-Rifle Reach

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared by Douglas B. Osmundson for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (Project No. 74, March 2001), gives interim flow recommendations for the Colorado River between Palisade and Rifle, Colorado. The reach is currently blocked by three diversion dams, so the endangered [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are largely absent there. The flows are designed for the future, assuming fish passage and restoration succeed in repopulating the reach.

Because habitat-use data for the upstream reach are lacking, summer and winter recommendations use the [[15-Mile Reach]] downstream as a surrogate. Recommended summer and winter flows are generally 1,600 to 2,500 cfs at Cameo. Spring flows target channel maintenance and [[Bankfull Discharge]] events that flood bottomlands used by razorback sucker larvae. Irrigation diversions constrain implementation, especially in summer.

## Background & Description

**Species focus.** The two target species are listed as endangered. Recovery requires self-sustaining populations and protected instream flows, which the report describes as a prerequisite for delisting.

**Diversion structures.** Three dams block upstream fish movement within about 13 km (8 miles) above Palisade:
- Grand Valley Irrigation Company Diversion Dam (GVICDD), built 1883, 0.9–1.2 m high; modified by the Bureau of Reclamation in 1998 to allow passage at most flows.
- Price-Stubb Dam, built 1911, 3 m high; blocks fish at all flows, though no water is diverted there.
- Grand Valley Project Diversion Dam (GVPDD), built 1916, 4.3 m high; a barrier at all flows.

**Historic occurrence.** Razorback sucker were historically present in the De Beque-to-Rifle reach, with spawning observed near De Beque in the 1970s and 165 adults captured from Etter Pond during 1992–1993. Colorado pikeminnow have not been verified upstream of Price-Stubb Dam, although one anecdotal 1960s observation appears credible and another is probably a misidentification.

**Lifecycle context.** Adult pikeminnow are expected to establish year-round home ranges up to De Beque if given access. Juveniles need extensive river length and backwater nursery habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Seasonal habitat use.**
- Razorback sucker: Winter uses pools (61%) and slow runs. Spring spawning movements use backwaters and flooded gravel pits (gravel pits 43% of June observations). Summer use is mainly pools and slow runs.
- Colorado pikeminnow: Winter uses pools (42–62%) and runs. Spring uses backwaters and flooded gravel pits (45–49% of April–June locations). Summer uses eddies, pools and backwaters.

**Spawning and reproduction.**
- Razorback sucker spawn in spring, with capture dates of adults in ripe condition concentrated between May 20 and June 17. Main-channel temperatures (averaging 13°C at Cameo) are below the 20°C egg-incubation optimum, so spawning in flooded off-channel habitats is likely important.
- Colorado pikeminnow spawn in summer, at 18–22°C, over gravel-cobble substrate after runoff has peaked. Larvae drift 100–200 km to low-gradient nursery backwaters.

**Hydrology.** Regulation has reduced the median annual peak flow at Cameo from about 26,000 cfs (unregulated) to 16,550 cfs. Bankfull frequency fell from 63% to 37% of years. Mean interval between bankfull events lengthened from 1.6 to 3.6 years.

**Food webs and substrate.** Spring high flows clean gravel-cobble substrate, flush fines, and deliver organic debris to the channel. Razorback sucker larvae need 30–60 food organisms per day, which occur mainly in flooded bottomlands.

## Key Findings

- **Interim summer and winter flows:** 1,600–2,500 cfs at Cameo, with a minimum for dry years taken from natural unregulated averages.
- **Summer constraints:** Canal diversions (Grand Valley Canal about 640 cfs; Government Highline Canal about 1,620 cfs) require Cameo flows of 2,460 cfs (dry), 2,890 cfs (below average) and 3,280 cfs (above average and wet). Flows above 3,280 cfs are considered detrimental and should be stored where possible.
- **Winter GVICDD-to-GVPDD minimum:** 810 cfs, requiring Cameo flows of at least 1,555 cfs in dry winters.
- **Bankfull target:** Median bankfull discharge of 22,000 cfs (De Beque-to-Rifle), 20,500 cfs (De Beque Canyon) and 21,500 cfs (15-Mile Reach). Recommended to be met in all above-average and wet years, with a mean recurrence interval of 2.0 years and an initial duration of three days.
- **Wet-year flooding:** Flows above 23,400 cfs at a rate of one in four years, held at least three weeks, to inundate bottomlands for razorback sucker larvae.
- **Dry and below-average peaks:** Target about 12,900 cfs (minimum) and 11,400 cfs (mean/median) to maintain backwaters and control tamarisk and non-native minnows.
- **Constrained peak targets at Cameo (current operations):** >25,000 cfs (5 in 20 years), 23,250 cfs (5 in 20), 18,200 cfs (6 in 20), 14,400 cfs (4 in 20).
- **Nursery constraint:** Flows near Moab should not exceed 4,000 cfs in summer to preserve nursery backwater habitat.
- **Key uncertainties:** Whether the 15-Mile Reach and upstream reaches maximize mesohabitats at the same flows, the upstream extent of pikeminnow winter and summer use, and the bottomland inundation duration required for larval survival. The report recommends a habitat mapping study at various base flows.
