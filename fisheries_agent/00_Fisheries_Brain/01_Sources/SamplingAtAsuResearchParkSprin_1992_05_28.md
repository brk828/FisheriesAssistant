## Executive Summary
This report details a spring 1992 fish community survey conducted by Arizona State University at the ASU Research Park (ASURP) lakes in Tempe, Arizona. The primary conservation focus was assessing the status of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]], which utilizes these artificial urban lakes as a refuge/translocation site. Sampling combined entanglement gear (trammel and experimental gill nets) with shoreline seining across three lakes (A, B, C). Results confirmed a persistent, reproducing-condition population of Razorback Sucker alongside a diverse assemblage of native and non-native sportfish and forage species. Notably, the survey failed to capture [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Colorado Squawfish]] despite reliable angler reports, and documented the continued two-year absence of [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]] from seine collections.

## Background & Description
Specific morphometric data was collected primarily for the target endangered species, [[Razorback Sucker]]:
*   **Sample Size:** 10 individuals captured via trammel net (Lakes A & B), plus 1 additional unmeasured individual via seine (Lake C) and 1 unmeasured individual (est. 40 cm TL) via seine.
*   **Total Length (TL):** Averaged 48.2 ± 2.0 cm (SD), ranging from 45.0 to 56.5 cm.
*   **Weight:** Averaged 1.46 ± 0.41 kg, ranging from 1.00 to 2.4 kg.
*   **Sex Ratio:** Heavily skewed male-dominant (9 males : 1 female). Four of the males were reproductively "ripe."
*   **Condition:** All individuals were noted to be in apparent good physical condition.

Morphometric data for other species was limited to weight ranges:
*   **[[Largemouth Bass]]:** Averaged 0.68 kg (range 0.22–2.35 kg) among adults; juveniles were predominantly young-of-year (YOY) measuring < 5 cm TL.
*   **[[Channel Catfish]]:** Two individuals weighed 2.72 kg and 3.86 kg, respectively.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The ASU Research Park lakes function as a series of connected, artificial lentic (lake-like) habitats (Lakes A, B, and C) serving as an urban refugium for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] within the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] ecosystem.

*   **Community Structure:** The lakes support a mixed community of native large-river catostomids (Razorback Sucker) alongside established populations of non-native centrarchids ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]]), cyprinids ([[Common Carp]], [[Goldfish]]), and cichlids ([[Blue Tilapia]]).
*   **Spatial Distribution:** Species composition varied significantly by lake. Lake A was dominated by [[Threadfin Shad]] (64% of net catch), whereas Lakes B and C were dominated by [[Bluegill]] (64% and 75% of net catch, respectively). [[Blue Tilapia]] were exclusively captured in Lake C.
*   **Trophic Interactions:** The overwhelming numerical dominance of juvenile [[Largemouth Bass]] (74% of seine catch, mostly YOY) suggests significant recent recruitment/spawning success, indicating a self-sustaining predatory sportfish population within the lake system.
*   **Community Shift:** The absence of [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]] from seine collections since autumn 1990 (previously "common-to-abundant") suggests a notable, sustained shift in the small-bodied fish community, potentially due to predation pressure or competitive exclusion by the dominant centrarchids.
*   **Refuge Population Status:** The presence of ripe male Razorback Sucker suggests the lakes provide suitable conditions for reproductive physiological cues, even if recruitment to detectable seine sizes is not confirmed in this survey.

## Key Findings
*   **Total Entanglement Catch:** 156 individuals across 9 taxa were captured via trammel and gill nets combined (Table 1).
    *   Dominance: Bluegill (49%) > Threadfin Shad (22%) > Largemouth Bass (16%) > Razorback Sucker (6%).
*   **Total Seine Catch:** 782 individuals across 4 taxa were captured (Table 2).
    *   Dominance: Largemouth Bass (74%) > Bluegill (26%).
*   **Razorback Sucker Status:** A total of 11 Razorback Sucker were documented (10 via trammel net, 1 via seine). Population skewed heavily male, with several ripe individuals, indicating a viable, reproductively active refuge population as of Spring 1992.
*   **Missing Target Species:** No [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Colorado Squawfish]] were captured in this sampling effort, though credible angler reports confirm their continued presence in Lake B; researchers attribute the non-capture to low sampling effort rather than absence.
*   **Community Change Alert:** [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]] have been absent from seine samples for two consecutive years (since Autumn 1990), a significant change from their previously "common-to-abundant" status.
*   **Methodological Gap:** Planned larval fish sampling for winter-spring 1991-1992 was not conducted; the author flags this as a priority for future monitoring seasons.
*   **Catch Per Effort (Seine):** Overall CPE was 10.16 fish/haul, but varied drastically by lake: Lake A (0.45/haul), Lake B (11.27/haul), and Lake C (17.36/haul), suggesting higher shoreline productivity or habitat complexity in Lakes B and C.
*   **Next Steps:** Follow-up net sampling is scheduled for Spring 1993.
