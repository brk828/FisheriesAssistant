# Note on the Stoneflies of Iceland

#ichthyology #marine-biology #capniidae

*Note: this document concerns stoneflies (order Plecoptera), not fish. The tags above follow the format requirement, but the taxon-specific tag is #plecoptera. The note is therefore a freshwater-insect record.*

Correction to tags: #plecoptera #freshwater-entomology #capniidae

## Executive Summary

H. B. N. Hynes (University of Liverpool) re-examined nymphs and an adult male of *Capnia* stoneflies from the River Laxa, Myvatn, Iceland, collected by Miss Lucy Buxton in July 1953. Earlier records had identified the Icelandic material as [[Capnia Atra]]. Hynes found that the nymphs and the adult male genitalia matched [[Capnia Vidua]] instead. He proposed the Icelandic population as a new subspecies, *Capnia vidua brachyptera* Tuxen, and designated a holotype. The paper also discusses the zoogeographical implications of this species' presence in Iceland.

## Background & Description

- **Context:** Hynes was preparing a key to the nymphs of British *Capnia* species (Hynes, 1955) when he examined the Icelandic material.
- **Prior identification:** Tuxen (1938) listed *C. atra* as the only Icelandic stonefly. The identification was originally made by Dr. Bengtsson of Lund (Lindroth, 1931), and adults had been recorded from the same locality.
- **Key morphological differences (males):**
  - Shape of the supra-anal processes.
  - Seventh abdominal tergum: deeply cleft and with a clearly defined membranous area in *C. vidua*; uncleft and with an indistinct membranous area in *C. atra*.
  - Wing length: in *C. vidua* the wings reach only the ends of the thoracic segments; in *C. atra* they reach the end of the eighth abdominal segment.
- **Icelandic specimen:** Tuxen (1938) reported that all Icelandic males he had seen were short-winged. The specimen loaned by Dr. S. L. Tuxen (Copenhagen Zoological Museum) had a tergum even more deeply cleft than British *C. vidua*.
- **Subspecies status:** Aubert (1950) recognised three geographical races of *C. vidua* (alpine region, north-west Europe and Britain). Hynes considered the Icelandic material a fourth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Zoogeography:** *C. vidua* has a limited range, from the Alps to north-western and central Europe and England. It is absent from Scandinavia (Brinck, 1949) and Ireland (King and Halbert, 1910), and no close relatives are known from North America.
- **Dispersal inference:** Hynes concluded that *C. vidua* probably reached Iceland directly from the European mainland, or from England or Scotland, rather than through Ireland or Scandinavia.
- **Circumpolar fauna:** *C. atra* and its allies (*C. hantzschi*, *C. nearctica*) are circumpolar northern species. [[Diura Bicaudata]] also occurs in the far north but was not recorded from Iceland in this material.
- **Regional gap:** No Plecoptera have been recorded from the Faroes or Greenland (Tuxen, 1938).

## Key Findings

1. The Icelandic *Capnia* nymphs were morphologically closer to *C. vidua* than to *C. atra*, and the adult male genitalia and supra-anal lobe matched *C. vidua*. The earlier *C. atra* identification was therefore incorrect.
2. Hynes proposed *Capnia vidua brachyptera* Tuxen as a new subspecies. Males are micropterous, with forewings reaching the end of the metanotum and hind wings reaching the end of the second abdominal segment. The name *brachyptera* was used by Tuxen (1938) for Icelandic material as a form name.
3. Holotype: male, Varne, Kilde N. V. of Ustifell, Skagafjörður, Iceland, collected by Tuxen on 14 April 1937, held in the Zoologiske Museum, Copenhagen.
4. The subspecies differs from *C. v. anglica* in being less micropterous, having a less protuberant spinose knob on the eighth tergum, and having a more deeply incised seventh tergum.
5. The presence of *C. vidua* in Iceland is zoogeographically significant, because it is the only one of these Capniidae with a restricted European distribution to reach the island.
