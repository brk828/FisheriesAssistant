# Two New Scorpions from Western North America

#arachnology #scorpions #vejovidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, published in the [[Pan-Pacific Entomologist]] (Vol. 44, No. 4, October 1968) by [[Stanley C Williams]] of San Francisco State College, describes two new scorpion species in the family [[Vejovidae]]. The species are [[Vejovis Utahensis]], a burrowing form from sandy dune habitat near Bluff, Utah, and [[Vejovis Gertschi]], a small dark species from coastal chaparral in San Diego County, California. Each description gives diagnostic characters, a holotype and allotype, paratype series, and comparisons with related congeners.

## Background & Description

Both species belong to the genus *Vejovis*. *V. utahensis* is assigned to the subgenus *Paruroctonus*. Its closest relatives named in the text are [[Vejovis Mesaensis]], [[Vejovis Auratus]], and [[Vejovis Aquilonalis]]. *V. gertschi* is described as closely related to [[Vejovis Wupatkiensis]].

**Vejovis utahensis**
- Moderate to large, uniformly straw yellow, with no dorsal stripes or contrasting markings.
- Pedipalp palms are swollen, with strongly granular keels and short fingers.
- Pectine teeth: 27 to 31 in males, 17 to 21 in females.
- Holotype male: total length 42.0 mm, carapace 5.3 mm.
- Allotype female: total length 54.3 mm, carapace 6.2 mm.
- Females have fewer pectine teeth (18/17) and middle lamellae (14) than the holotype male (31/30 teeth, 23 lamellae).

**Vejovis gertschi**
- Small and dark, with brownish-yellow base color and dusky markings on most body regions.
- Metasomal inferior keels are underlined with dark pigment.
- Pectine teeth: 16 to 18 in males, 14 to 16 in females.
- Holotype male: total length 23.0 mm, carapace 3.1 mm.
- Allotype female: total length 25.2 mm, carapace 3.2 mm.
- Pedipalp chela is elongate, with the fixed finger longer than the palm.
- Chelicerae have a smooth inferior border on the movable finger, lacking denticles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Utah habitat:** *V. utahensis* is a burrowing species in fine-textured, packed sandy soils of a dune community. Plants were low and sparse where scorpions were most abundant. Sand that was coarse and unpacked was unoccupied, and lower areas of the [[San Juan River]] flood plain were not occupied. It was the only scorpion species found in the Bluff area across six sampled localities.
- **Activity and detection:** The 115 *V. utahensis* specimens were collected on the ground surface during early evening using [[Ultraviolet Detection]]. An electrical storm and intermittent light showers occurred during collection.
- **Age structure:** Samples were mostly older adults. Young instars were conspicuously uncommon.
- **California habitat:** *V. gertschi* was common in coastal San Diego County. Specimens were found during the day under rocks and cardboard on a coastal chaparral hillside, under flakes of heat-fractured granite, and in a pit-trap in a chaparral community recovering from fire. Twelve specimens were removed from an active [[Wood Rat]] (*Neotoma* sp.) nest.

## Key Findings

1. **Vejovis utahensis is a new species**, named after the state of Utah. The holotype male, allotype, and 30 paratopotypes were collected 2 miles northeast of Bluff, Utah, on 14 July 1967 by S. C. Williams, M. A. Cazier, and J. Davidson. The holotype and allotype are deposited in the [[California Academy of Sciences]].
   - Paratypes in the Utah series total 83: 66 from 1/4 mile west of Bluff (46 males, 20 females) and 17 from the Bluff city limits in the San Juan River flood plain (9 males, 8 females).
   - The paratopotype series varied little from the type series. Males had carapace lengths of 1.8 to 5.3 mm (n = 12). Females had carapace lengths of 2.8 to 5.8 mm (n = 18).
   - One adult female lacked black pigment on its median eyes and ocular tubercle. One juvenile male, presumed to be a second instar, had faint dusky pigment on the inferior metasomal keels.

2. **Vejovis gertschi is a new species**, named after [[Willis J Gertsch]] in the species epithet. The holotype male, allotype, and 21 paratopotypes were collected 1 mile east of San Ysidro, San Diego County, California, on 31 December 1966 by S. C. Williams.
   - An additional 47 paratypes came from several San Diego County localities, including Otay River Valley (9 males, 13 females), Alvarado Canyon (7 females in 1957; 1 female and 12 first instars in 1963), and Wildcat Canyon (1 female).
   - Paratypes had carapace lengths of 0.7 to 3.0 mm (males) and 0.7 to 3.5 mm (females).
   - First instars differ from adults: the aculeus is blunt, pretarsal claws are absent, and the base color is white with dusky markings.

3. **Diagnostic separation** from close relatives rests on pedipalp finger length, keel granulation, pectine tooth counts, ocular diad width, and metasomal keel structure. Specific comparisons are given for each congener in the text.

4. **Sampling notes:** The paper reports that the *V. utahensis* type series came from a single collecting event and that *V. gertschi* was one of the more common scorpions in coastal San Diego County. Its prevalence is described qualitatively; no density estimates are given.
