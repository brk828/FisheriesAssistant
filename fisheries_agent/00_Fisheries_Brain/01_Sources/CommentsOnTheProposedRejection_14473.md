## Executive Summary

This 1963 communication to the *Bulletin of Zoological Nomenclature* is a formal rebuttal to a proposal (Z.N.(S.) 1279, raised by Dr. China) seeking a ruling that [[Jordan And Evermann]] did **not** validly designate type-species for fish genera in their works of 1896 and 1896–1900 (*Bull. U.S. Nat. Mus. 47*). Authored by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], [[Leonard P. Schultz]], [[Victor G. Springer]], and [[John E. Randall]], with a supporting statement from [[George S. Myers]], the paper argues from historical, bibliographic, and personal-testimonial evidence that the practice of citing a single species name in parentheses following a generic name was **intentional, well-understood shorthand for type-species designation**, consistent with Article 69(a)(iii) of the [[International Code Of Zoological Nomenclature]]. The authors warn that overturning this convention would retroactively destabilize type-species designations across hundreds of genera in ichthyology and beyond (e.g., echinoderm taxonomy), and they recommend the Commission reject or table the China proposal, as well as a related application by Follett and Cohen concerning the genus *[[Bathylagus]]*.

## Background & Description

Not a morphological study; this is a nomenclatural/taxonomic argument. However, several taxonomic type-species identities are clarified as incidental technical content:

- ***[[Conocara]]*** (Goode & Bean, 1895): Originally included two species without type designation; *Conocara macdonaldi* Goode & Bean was retroactively fixed as type by Jordan & Evermann (1896) via parenthetical citation.
- ***[[Rabula]]*** (Jordan & Davis, 1892): A subgenus originally comprising five species; only *Muraena aquae-dulcis* Cope was cited in parentheses, later formalized as "orthotype" by Jordan (1920).
- ***[[Pogonias]]*** Lacépède: Type cited by Jordan & Evermann as "(fasciatus = cromis)," reflecting synonymy between *Pogonias fasciatus* Lacépède and *Labrus cromis* Linnaeus (= *[[Pogonias cromis]]*).
- ***[[Blennius]]***: Type-species inconsistently treated — Jordan & Evermann (1896) cited "(galerita)," while Jordan & Gilbert (1883) and Jordan (1917) recognized *Blennius ocellaris* Linnaeus as the type; considered a *lapsus calami* by the authors.
- ***[[Bathylagus]]*** Günther: Central taxonomic dispute — Jordan & Evermann (1896) designated *B. atlanticus* as type-species, whereas Jordan's later *Genera of Fishes* (1919) cited *B. antarcticus* Günther, an inconsistency the authors attribute to clerical error (*lapsus calami*) rather than intentional redesignation.
- ***[[Metopomycter]]*** Gilbert, 1905: Monotypic new genus of Nettastomidae; type-species *Metopomycter denticulatus* cited in parenthesis without explicit "type" statement, illustrating implicit but unambiguous designation practice.
- ***[[Zoroaster]]*** Wyville Thomson (Asteroidea): Cited as a non-fish example (starfish) of the same parenthetical type-citation convention used by [[Walter K. Fisher]] (1905), demonstrating the practice's breadth across zoological taxa.

## Key Findings

- The parenthetical citation of a single species name following a generic name in works by [[David Starr Jordan]] and colleagues (Evermann, Gilbert, Seale, Snyder, Starks, and others) was **consistently intended as formal type-species designation**, not incidental listing.
- This convention traces back at least to [[Jordan And Gilbert]] (1883, *Synopsis of the Fishes of North America*) and was carried forward, with explicit parenthetical notation first observed in Jordan (1891, review of Labroid fishes).
- Jordan later formalized this retroactively via the term **"orthotype"** in *Genera of Fishes* (1917–1920), defined as "the type of a genus as indicated or distinctly implied by the original author."
- The practice was **not confined to ichthyology** — contemporaneous echinoderm taxonomy (Walter K. Fisher, 1905, on starfishes) used the identical convention, indicating a broader, cross-taxon norm of the era (up to ~1918).
- Several explicit textual statements by Jordan & Evermann (e.g., "This genus, typified by..."; "...the type is...") confirm authorial intent behind the parenthetical citations, refuting claims of ambiguity.
- Jordan & Evermann (1896, *Rept. U.S. Comm. Fish and Fisheries* 21:210) explicitly stated: "The name in parenthesis following the reference to the generic name is that of the species taken by the describer as the type of the genus" — cited as satisfying Article 69(a)(iii) of the Code.
- Acknowledged **inconsistencies** exist between the 1896 checklist and the later *Genera of Fishes* (e.g., *[[Bathylagus]]*, *[[Blennius]]*), which the authors attribute to clerical slips (*lapsus calami*) rather than deliberate re-designation, arguing these should be resolved case-by-case rather than via blanket invalidation.
- The authors explicitly **recommend against** a blanket ruling invalidating Jordan & Evermann's type designations, warning it would create "unpredicted confusion" affecting genera such as *[[Conocara]]* and numerous others described before 1896.
- Specific recommendation to **table or reject** both the China application (Z.N.(S.) 1279) and the Follett & Cohen application regarding *[[Bathylagus]]*, upholding *B. atlanticus* as validly designated type-species under Jordan & Evermann (1896).
- [[George S. Myers]] independently corroborates the interpretation, citing personal association with Jordan and arguing the alternative interpretation would paradoxically imply Jordan & Gilbert (1883) validly designated types while Jordan & Evermann (later) did not — an inconsistency he finds untenable.
