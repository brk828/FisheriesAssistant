## Executive Summary

This 1965 California Department of Fish and Game administrative report evaluates the feasibility of using [[San Dieguito Reservoir]] as a dedicated brood pond for [[Largemouth Bass]] to supply stock for other southern California lakes. San Dieguito Reservoir is a 75-acre terminal reservoir in San Diego County, owned by the City of San Diego and used for irrigation/domestic water supply; it has never been open to public fishing. The Department has used the reservoir sporadically since 1948 to procure bass and other fish for stocking elsewhere. The report concludes the reservoir is **unsuitable** as a steady, intensive brood pond source due to unfavorable basin morphology, an uncontrollable carp population, and the limited management flexibility inherent to a terminal reservoir. Cost-benefit analysis shows that recovering bass via electrofishing or angler transfer is too expensive relative to the limited angling benefit produced. The Department recommends continuing only occasional, non-maintenance-level procurement from the reservoir.

## Background & Description

The report does not provide detailed morphometric data on individual fish (e.g., length-weight relationships), but does report population size-class information relevant to stock assessment:

- **1948 total drainage harvest** (complete lake drawdown for maintenance):
  | Species | Number | Weight (lbs) |
  |---|---|---|
  | [[Largemouth Bass]] | 330 | 660 |
  | [[Bluegill]] | 3,200 | 405 |
  | [[White Crappie]] | 700 | 700 |
  | [[Brown Bullhead]] | 6,280 | 2,398 |
  | **Total** | **10,510** | **4,163** |

- Average weight per bass recovered in 1948 drainage: ~2.0 lbs (660 lbs / 330 fish).
- 1948 restocking event: 580 largemouth bass, sized at **one- to two-pounds each**, introduced in July and December 1948 following the drainage.
- 1958 rotenone treatment removed approximately **6.5 tons (13,000 lbs)** of fish, predominantly [[Common Carp]], with bass and bluegill also present in the kill (species composition not fully recorded).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** [[San Dieguito Reservoir]] is a steep-sided, irregularly configured terminal reservoir constructed in 1918, with 75-acre maximum surface area, 1,131 acre-foot storage capacity, 51-foot dam height, and ~50-foot maximum depth (rarely drawn below 45 feet under normal operations).
- **Hydrology:** Water supply historically from [[Lake Hodges]]; as of 1965, additionally connected to the San Diego Aqueduct for Colorado River water delivery, which was expected to increase water-level fluctuation.
- **Carp invasion pathway:** [[Common Carp]] populations are able to continually recolonize the reservoir via connection with [[Lake Hodges]] Reservoir; this connectivity persists even after the aqueduct connection, and no feasible screening method was identified to permanently exclude carp.
- **Water quality impact:** By 1958, an expanding carp population had caused measurable water quality deterioration via increased turbidity, prompting a rotenone treatment in cooperation with the City of San Diego.
- **Basin morphology constraint:** The steep, irregular basin slope significantly reduces seining efficiency, limiting the practicality of net-based bass recovery and pushing reliance toward hook-and-line or electrofishing methods.
- **Community composition (estimated standing crop, post-1958):** Bass ~16%, bluegill and other sunfish ~26%, other species (predominantly carp) ~58% of an estimated 6.5-ton standing crop.

## Key Findings

- **Potential annual bass yield:** Estimated maximum standing crop of bass under existing conditions ≈ 2,080 lbs/year (100% theoretical harvest), derived from combined data of the 1948 drainage and 1958 rotenone kill.
- **Electrofishing-based transfer program (Department-operated):**
  - Nighttime electrofishing could recover ~60% of potential yield = **1,248 lbs/year**.
  - Estimated transfer cost: **≥ $2/lb**, totaling **$2,496/year**.
  - Anglers would subsequently catch ~50% of restocked bass (624 lbs), yielding a cost of **$4/lb in the creel**.
  - Resulting angler-day support: ~1,248 angler-days/year (at 0.5 lb bass/angler-day), cost ≈ **$2/angler-day**.
- **Angler-driven transfer program (alternative model):**
  - Potential annual yield: 2,080 lbs
  - Catchable bass (>0.5 lb, 75% of yield): 1,560 lbs
  - Angler catch for transfer (50%): 780 lbs
  - Post-transfer survivors (after 20% hooking/transfer mortality): 624 lbs
  - Final angler catch elsewhere (50% of stocked): 312 lbs
  - Angler-days provided: 624
- **Historical brood pond performance:** Despite restocking with 580 bass (1–2 lbs) in 1948, the reservoir proved to be a poor brood pond in practice — steep basin slopes hindered seining, and hook-and-line capture was too labor-intensive/costly. Procuring bass from other non-public southern California waters was found to be more economical.
- **Primary limiting factors identified:**
  1. Unfavorable, steep, irregular basin configuration impeding fish recovery.
  2. Persistent, uncontrollable [[Common Carp]] population degrading habitat and water quality.
  3. Terminal reservoir status limiting water-level and habitat management options.
- **Conclusion/Recommendation:** The California Department of Fish and Game determined that a routine, intensive bass transfer program from San Dieguito Reservoir is not economically justified relative to its limited contribution to public angling. The Department recommends continued **occasional, non-maintenance** procurement of bass and other species as needed, rather than establishing the reservoir as a formal brood pond.
