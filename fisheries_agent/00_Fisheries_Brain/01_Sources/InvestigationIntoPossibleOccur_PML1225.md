# Colorado Squawfish Occurrence Survey in the Green River and Little Snake River Drainages of Wyoming, 1986

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Wyoming Game and Fish Department administrative report (Johnson and Oberholtzer, February 1987; projects 5086-13-8601 and 4486-13-860I) investigated whether [[Colorado Squawfish]], a federally threatened or endangered species, still persists in Wyoming. The species had not been documented in the state since the closure of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], and no recent records existed. Electrofishing surveys were conducted in 1986 across the [[Green River]] drainage and the [[Little Snake River]] drainage. No Colorado Squawfish were captured or observed. The authors recommended that the species be considered extinct in both drainages of Wyoming. Funding came from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] under Section 6 of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Background & Description

The Colorado Squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) historically occurred throughout the Colorado River basin. The report notes that Wyoming populations were last recorded in the Little Snake River drainage, and the species had not been sampled in recent years. Because of its federal listing, the state needed to determine whether any reproducing population remained in Wyoming. The sampling design targeted the most likely habitats, which were large, deep, warm-water reaches typical of the lower [[Yampa River]] and the Green River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Green River drainage.** Five sites were sampled in 1986. Station A on the Green River (about 50 miles long, from the Big Piney area to [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]]) is regulated by [[Fontenelle Dam]], which affects water temperature, sediment and flow. Stations B and C were on the Blacks Fork River, a shallow stream with heavy sediment loads and flow fluctuations from 0 to 9,980 cfs recorded in 1962. Stations D and E were on the Hams Fork River near Granger, with deep holes and willow and brush cover. Substrates were mostly fine silt and sand.

**Little Snake River drainage.** The Little Snake River flows for 43.1 miles in Wyoming. Three reaches (Stations A, B and C) covered about 14.3 miles, roughly one-third of the stream miles in the state. Habitat is dominated by gravel, rubble and boulders, with silt at Station A. Maximum recorded flow ranged from 51,100 cfs in March 1962 to no flow in September 1968. Muddy Creek and Savery Creek were sampled at lower stations, but their small size and limited habitat were judged unsuitable for Colorado Squawfish.

**Environmental constraints.** The report notes that spawning temperatures of 68–72 °F were reached when flows had dropped so far that many gravel bars were exposed or too shallow. USGS records showed short periods of no flow at the gauges near the state line and near Dixon.

## Key Findings

- **No Colorado Squawfish** were collected in any sample in either drainage.
- **Green River drainage:** 17 fish species were identified. Trout catches were dominated by hatchery stocking in 1985 (100,000 brown trout and 67,328 rainbow trout). Suckers made up about 73% of the catch in the Hams Fork and Blacks Fork rivers, and only two Channel Catfish were captured.
- **Little Snake River drainage:** 13 species were collected. Flannelmouth Sucker, White Sucker, Speckled Dace and Mottled Sculpin were widely distributed. Roundtail Chub preferred lower reaches. Mountain Whitefish showed little or no natural recruitment.
- **Overall:** 19 species were collected across the surveys; 11 were common to both drainages. Creek Chub occurred only in the Little Snake River, and six species were found only in the Green River.
- **Channel Catfish**, stocked into Flaming Gorge Reservoir since 1981, were found in the Green, Blacks Fork and Hams Fork rivers and may expand into new fisheries.
- **Conclusion:** The authors recommended treating the species as extinct in Wyoming. The Little Snake River was considered possibly suitable for habitat improvement and as a potential recovery site for endangered fishes.
- **Discussion note:** Appended historical and literature material reported a Colorado Squawfish captured near Baggs, Wyoming, in the Little Snake River in 1988, which was roughly 250 river km from the Yampa River confluence. Whether it was a permanent resident or a temporary visitor remained uncertain.
