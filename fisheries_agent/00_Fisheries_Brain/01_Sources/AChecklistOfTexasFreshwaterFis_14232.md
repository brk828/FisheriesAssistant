# A Checklist of Texas Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This Texas Parks and Wildlife Department Technical Series publication (No. 11, 1972) is a annotated [[Checklist]] of the fishes of [[Texas]], compiled by [[Clark Hubbs]] of the Department of Zoology, The University of Texas. It revises Hubbs's 1961 checklist. It lists 215 numbered taxa across about 44 families, with each species assigned to one or more [[Game Areas]] (numbered 1 to 8, with compass-letter prefixes) or designated as "coastal." The foreword explains nomenclatural decisions, lists recent additions and deletions to the state fauna, and defines the criteria used to decide which euryhaline species count as freshwater fishes.

## Background & Description

The checklist follows common names approved by the [[American Fisheries Society]] Committee on Names of Fishes (Bailey et al., 1970). Scientific nomenclature follows [[Moore 1968]] and Bailey et al. (1970), with several deliberate departures by Hubbs:

- **Genera retained separately:** *Hadropterus* and *Percina* were not synonymized, and *Opsopoeodus* was not merged into *Notropis*, partly because of karyological (chromosome) differences.
- **Retained names:** *Notropis cornutus* stays as the name of the common shiner, because [[Gilbert 1961]] did not demonstrate full specific separation from related forms.
- **Genus changes:** Following [[Drewry 1967]], *Fundulus notatus* and *F. olivaceus* are placed in *Zygonectes*. The warmouth and green sunfish are placed in the same genus, following [[Branson and Moore 1962]].
- **Excluded records:** *Carpioides forbesi*, *Notropis whipplei* and *Gila nigrescens* are not accepted as part of the known Texas fauna, although *G. nigrescens* probably once occurred in the [[Rio Grande]] between El Paso and Presidio.

Range designations use eight modified game areas. Area descriptions in the source are: Area 1, East Texas timber country plus a separated southeastern blackland prairie segment; Area 2, coastal prairie; Area 3, South Texas brushlands; Area 4, main blackland prairie; Area 5, east and west cross timbers and grand prairie; Area 6, Edwards Plateau; Area 7, plains; Area 8, Trans-Pecos region. A letter prefix (N, S, E, W, NE, etc.) indicates that a species occupies only part of an area.

Species designated "coastal" typically inhabit brackish or salt water and enter only coastal streams. Their inclusion follows the criteria of [[Gunter 1945]]. Fishes in highly saline streams, such as the [[Pecos River]] (up to 12.5 parts per thousand chloride), are arbitrarily treated as freshwater fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist reflects a fauna shaped by several pressures documented in the text:

- **Introductions and releases:** Several species were introduced intentionally or accidentally. Examples include the [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) and [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), introduced as game fishes, and the [[Armored Catfish]] (*Plecostomus plecostomus*) and [[Pike Killifish]] (*Belonesox belizanus*), released from aquariums in San Antonio (Barron, 1964). Other species suspected of establishing breeding populations include largemouth bass relatives such as *Micropterus dolomieui*, *Xiphophorus maculatus*, *Lebistes reticulatus*, *Culaea inconstans*, [[Mozambique Tilapia]] (*Tilapia nilotica* in the source), and grass carp (*Ctenopharyngodon idellus*).
- **Endemism and conservation concern:** A localized endemic *Gambusia* was recently discovered by A. Peden. Several species, including *Cyprinodon bovinus* (Leon Springs pupfish), are annotated "extinct" in Game Area 8, and the Pecos and Devils River pupfishes are treated as distinct taxa.
- **Salinity gradients:** Coastal and euryhaline species occupy the boundary between marine and freshwater systems, and the document explicitly addresses where that boundary is drawn for classification.
- **Potential range expansion:** Five species abundant in adjacent watersheds may rarely occur in north or east Texas: *Moxostoma duquesnei*, *Hybopsis biguttata*, *Notropis boops*, *Pimephales promelas* and *Hadropterus copelandi*.

## Key Findings

**Scope and composition**
- The checklist contains 215 numbered taxa spanning about 44 family headings. Families range from lampreys (Petromyzontidae) and sturgeons (Acipenseridae) to sunfishes (Centrarchidae), darters (Percidae), and gobies (Gobiidae).
- The Cyprinidae, with a large *Notropis* assemblage (shiners, numbered 39 to 69), is the largest group. Other large groups include the Centrarchidae (*Lepomis* and related genera) and the Percidae (*Etheostoma* and related darters).
- Many entries are "coastal" species, including sharks, rays, sea catfishes, flounders, drums and gobies, reflecting the inclusion of euryhaline taxa.

**Taxonomic changes (relative to Hubbs 1961)**
- Eight species were added in the previous ten years: *Plecostomus plecostomus* and *Belonesox belizanus* (aquarium releases, San Antonio); *Esox lucius* and *Stizostedion vitreum* (introduced game fishes); *Gambusia georgei* and *Percina macrolepida* (native species previously treated as conspecific with *G. affinis* and *P. caprodes*); one localized endemic *Gambusia*; and *Agonostomus monticola* (a coastal fish occurring in fresh water).
- Four species were deleted: *Notropis lepidus* and *Fundulus zebrinus* (treated as conspecific with *N. lutrensis* and *F. kansae*), and the gobies *Gobionellus stigmaticus* and *G. oceanicus*, which probably do not enter fresh water.

**Distribution patterns**
- Widespread species occurring in all eight game areas include the [[Longnose Gar]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Blue Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Common Carp]] (introduced), [[Red Shiner]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Gizzard Shad]] and [[Freshwater Drum]].
- Several species are restricted to single areas, such as *Notropis chihuahua* and *Gambusia gaigei* (Game Area 8) and *Etheostoma histrio* (Game Area 1).

**Methodological and policy conventions**
- Common names follow the American Fisheries Society (Bailey et al., 1970).
- Game-area ranges are the primary distributional metadata.
- Euryhaline classification follows Gunter (1945), and the Pecos River is treated as freshwater despite high salinity.

**Bibliographic basis**
- The checklist builds on Hubbs (1961), Moore (1968), Bailey et al. (1970), Gunter (1945), Gilbert (1961), Drewry (1967), Branson and Moore (1962) and Barron (1964). The reference list is short and centered on the 1960s literature.
