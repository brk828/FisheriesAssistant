# Two Natural Darter Hybrids Involving Members of the Genus *Etheostoma* (Pisces:Percidae)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae #Etheostoma #darter-hybridization

## Executive Summary
This 1980 note documents two natural interspecific hybrid combinations within the darter genus [[Etheostoma]] (family [[Percidae]]), collected from Tennessee and Alabama tributary streams. The first hybrid, **[[Etheostoma Tippecanoe]] × [[Etheostoma Camurum]]**, occurs within the subgenus [[Nothonotus]] and represents only the second known specimen of this combination. The second, **[[Etheostoma Rufilineatum]] × [[Etheostoma Caeruleum]]**, is notable as the first documented hybrid crossing between the subgenera [[Nothonotus]] and [[Oligocephalus]]. Both hybrids display morphological and pigmentary characteristics intermediate between their parental species, consistent with a hybrid index analysis. The authors attribute both hybridization events to incidental cross-fertilization arising from spatial and temporal overlap in spawning activity (simultaneous riffle-spawning in close proximity) rather than any evolved hybridization mechanism, and consider such events to be rare.

## Background & Description

### Hybrid 1: *E. tippecanoe* × *E. camurum* (INHS 83898)
- Adult male, 41.6 mm SL
- 68% of meristic/morphometric characters matched one parent or the other; 32% were intermediate (hybrid index ≈ 50)
- **Shared with *E. tippecanoe***: incomplete lateral line, scale counts below lateral line, body coloration, dark horizontal side lines, dark blue vertical bars, pectoral fin length/SL ratio, second dorsal fin proportions
- **Shared with *E. camurum***: dorsal ray counts, caudal-peduncle scale rows, infraorbital pore counts, dark fin margins (second dorsal, caudal, anal), belly squamation, larger body size, head length/SL, body depth/SL, snout length/SL, and several other body-proportion ratios
- **Intermediate characters**: lateral-line scale counts, pored lateral-line scales, scales above lateral line, head length/SL, caudal peduncle depth/SL, interorbital width/head width, gape width
- Belly squamation intermediate (~70% scaled vs. fully scaled in *E. camurum* and naked in *E. tippecanoe*)
- Maximum SL of hybrid (41.6 mm) fell between the two parent species' recorded maxima (70.0 mm and 35.0 mm)

### Hybrid 2: *E. rufilineatum* × *E. caeruleum* (INHS 83934)
- Male, 23.7 mm SL (morphometrics not assessed due to small size; only meristic/pigmentary traits used)
- **Shared with *E. rufilineatum***: lateral-line scale row counts, scale rows above lateral line, dorsal/anal/pectoral ray counts, presence of dark humeral spot, extent of nape squamation
- **Shared with *E. caeruleum***: scale counts below lateral line, presence of 7–8 dorsal saddles, vertical bars on posterior half of body, distinct suborbital bar, absence of dark horizontal side lines
- **Intermediate characters**: dorsal spine and dorsal ray counts, snout shape (moderate vs. sharp/blunt), general body physiognomy
- Suborbital marking intermediate: distinct bar in hybrid vs. horizontal dashes (*E. rufilineatum*) and faint bar (*E. caeruleum*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both hybrid pairs were collected from **gravel riffle habitats** in small tributary streams — the preferred spawning substrate for members of this genus.
- **Hybrid 1 locality**: Big South Fork (Cumberland River Drainage), mouth of Station Camp Creek, Scott Co., TN — gravel riffle, 15–20 cm deep, bordered by vegetation. *E. camurum* was more abundant than *E. tippecanoe* at this site, though both were common.
- **Hybrid 2 locality**: Tributary of Cedar Creek (Tennessee River Drainage), Franklin Co., AL.
- [[Etheostoma Camurum]] spawns under/around large rocks near riffle crests, depositing eggs in fine gravel on the downstream side (per [[Mount 1959]]).
- [[Etheostoma Caeruleum]] spawns in gravel-bottomed riffles, burying eggs in gravel (per [[Winn 1958]]).
- [[Etheostoma Rufilineatum]] spawns in fast gravel riffles with buried eggs (per [[Stiles 1972]]), behavior similar to *E. camurum*.
- Spawning behavior of [[Etheostoma Tippecanoe]] is unknown but presumed to occur in gravel riffles or raceways.
- The overlapping use of gravel riffle microhabitats by ecologically similar, closely related species creates conditions for **accidental cross-fertilization** during simultaneous spawning events in close physical proximity.

## Key Findings
- Two novel natural darter hybrid combinations are formally described: **[[Etheostoma Tippecanoe]] × [[Etheostoma Camurum]]** and **[[Etheostoma Rufilineatum]] × [[Etheostoma Caeruleum]]**.
- The *E. tippecanoe* × *E. camurum* hybrid represents only the **second recorded specimen** of this cross; the first was noted by [[Trautman 1957]] but never formally described until [[Zorach 1967]]'s unpublished dissertation work.
- The *E. rufilineatum* × *E. caeruleum* hybrid is the **first documented hybrid** between the subgenera [[Nothonotus]] and [[Oligocephalus]].
- Both hybrids show intermediate character states via [[Hybrid Index]] analysis (following [[Hubbs et al. 1943]] methodology), with character states scored 0–100 between parental means.
- Despite considerable range and habitat overlap between parental species pairs, natural hybridization events appear to be **rare**, supporting the interpretation that these are isolated accidental cross-fertilization events rather than evidence of widespread introgression.
- A previously proposed possible hybrid, *[[Etheostoma Vulneratum]]* (*E. camurum* × *E. rufilineatum*, per [[Bailey 1959]]), was later reclassified as a valid subspecies of *[[Etheostoma Maculatum]]* by [[Zorach and Raney 1967]], underscoring the importance of rigorous morphological/meristic comparison in distinguishing true hybrids from valid taxa.
