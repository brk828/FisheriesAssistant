# Xenotaenia resolanae — Morphological Variation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Goodeidae #Cyprinodontiformes #freshwater-fish #Mexico

## Executive Summary

This study by Lyons (1997) examined 35 preserved specimens of ***[[Xenotaenia resolanae]]***, a poorly-known livebearing fish endemic to the [[Purificación Basin]] and [[Marabasco Basin]] on the Pacific slope of west-central México, to correct errors in the original 1946 description (Turner) and to quantify morphological variation associated with sex and geographic population. The analysis revealed significant **sexual dimorphism** (males with larger heads and more anteriorly-placed, larger anal/dorsal fins, narrower bodies) and **inter-basin divergence** (Purificación fish having more gill rakers, bigger heads, and shorter caudal fins than Marabasco fish), despite the basins' geographic proximity and hydrological connection via the Pacific Ocean. The holotype and paratype were found to be morphologically atypical compared to the broader sample, and several of Turner's original meristic counts (lateral line scales, dorsal/anal/pectoral rays) were determined to be erroneously high.

## Background & Description

### Taxonomic Context
- Family: **[[Goodeidae]]** — a family of viviparous (livebearing) fishes native to México
- Related genera for comparison: *Poecilia*, *Poeciliopsis* ([[Poeciliidae]]), which co-occur with *X. resolanae* and show parallel inter-basin divergence

### Type Specimen Anomalies
- **Holotype (female):** highest gill raker count and tied-lowest lateral line scale count among females; longest relative preanal/mandible lengths; shortest anal fin base and anal-origin-to-caudal distance; unusually small, posteriorly-placed anal fin; short preorbital distance.
- **Paratype (male):** most pelvic rays among males; shortest anal-origin-to-caudal and dorsal-origin-to-caudal distances; shortest dorsal/anal fin base lengths; least preorbital distance; narrow, posteriorly-located anal and dorsal fins.
- Both type specimens shared unusually short preorbital distance and posteriorly-placed anal/dorsal fins, suggesting the extirpated type-locality population may have been morphologically distinct.

### Corrected Meristic Counts vs. Original Description
Turner's (1946) original holotype description contained inflated counts:
| Variable | Turner (1946) | Corrected (Lyons 1997) |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral line scales | 36–40 | 31 (holotype) |
| Dorsal fin rays | 13 | 10 (holotype) |
| Anal fin rays | 16 | 12 (holotype) |
| Pectoral fin rays | 16 | 15 (holotype) |

Discrepancies could not be fully explained by differing counting conventions (e.g., [[Miller 1948 Counting Method]] of combining the last two fin rays).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Small streams and rivers on the Pacific slope of west-central México, within the [[Purificación Basin]] and [[Marabasco Basin]] (Jalisco state)
- **Co-occurring species:** *[[Poecilia chica]]* and *[[Poeciliopsis baenschi]]* (Poeciliidae), both of which independently show similar morphological divergence between the same two basins
- **Biogeography:** Despite basin proximity and a direct Pacific Ocean water connection, populations show pronounced morphological divergence, implying **limited contemporary gene flow** between basins — likely due to lack of recent dispersal opportunities (freshwater fish rarely traverse marine/estuarine barriers)
- **Conservation status:** The type locality (Rio Resolana, Purificación basin) population has apparently been **extirpated**; no additional specimens from that exact site are known to exist (per [[Lyons & Navarro-Pérez 1990]])
- Reproductive note: Many examined females were pregnant (consistent with viviparous reproduction typical of [[Goodeidae]]), which may explain their wider body width relative to males

## Key Findings

1. **Sexual dimorphism** in *X. resolanae* follows general Goodeidae trends (per [[Hubbs & Turner 1939]]): males have larger heads, larger and more anteriorly-positioned anal and dorsal fins, and narrower bodies than females — differences consistent across both basins.
2. **Inter-basin divergence** is present primarily in morphometric (shape) traits rather than meristic (count) traits: Purificación specimens have bigger heads, wider/longer anal fins with longer bases, more gill rakers, and shorter caudal fins than Marabasco specimens, regardless of sex.
3. Meristic variation was substantial but **did not differ significantly** between sexes or basins (except gill rakers), with extensive range overlap.
4. **Sheared Principal Components Analysis (PCA)** of 21 log-transformed morphometric variables: PC1 (size factor) explained 83% of variance; PC2 and PC3 (shape factors) explained 7% and 3% respectively, and successfully separated sex/basin groups by anal fin base length, preorbital distance, body width, and mouth width.
5. Meristic PCA (first 3 PCs, 56% of variance) **did not** clearly distinguish sex or basin groupings, reinforcing that meristic traits are less informative for this population-level differentiation.
6. The **holotype and paratype are morphological outliers**, raising questions about their representativeness as type material and supporting the hypothesis of a distinct, now-extinct type-locality population.
7. Original taxonomic description by [[Turner 1946]] contained multiple erroneous meristic counts, now corrected via re-examination of type material.
8. Parallel divergence patterns in co-occurring [[Poeciliidae]] species (*Poecilia chica*, *Poeciliopsis baenschi*) between the same two basins support a broader biogeographic pattern of limited dispersal in Pacific slope Mexican freshwater fish assemblages.
