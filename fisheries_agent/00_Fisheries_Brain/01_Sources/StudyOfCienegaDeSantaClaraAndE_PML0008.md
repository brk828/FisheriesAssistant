# Field Report No. 1: Study of Cienega de Santa Clara and Environs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This preliminary field report summarizes fish, invertebrate and other fauna surveys conducted at the [[Cienega de Santa Clara]] (also called Santa Clara Slough) in Sonora, Mexico, during October 13–18, 1992, with preliminary field notes on flora from November 12–23, 1992. The work was a joint effort of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the [[Centro Ecologico de Sonora]], and several university and binational institutions. Fish were sampled with seines and dip nets at multiple sites. The survey found a fish community dominated by non-native species, with [[Desert Pupfish]] present only in low numbers, except at one isolated pool. Invertebrate sampling was not yet analyzed, but fly larvae were the most common macroinvertebrate and anaerobic bottom conditions were frequently observed. The report notes that several identifications still need confirmation.

## Background & Description

The report was prepared by the Centro Ecologico de Sonora, the Comisión Internacional de Límites y Aguas, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, the Environmental Research Laboratory and Advanced Technology Program of the University of Arizona, the Drylands Institute, and the Centro Ecológico (Sonora). Field work was led by Francisco J. Abarca (Native Fish Biologist, Arizona Game and Fish Department), Dave Weedman (Arizona Game and Fish Department) and Alejandro Varela (Native Fish Biologist, Centro Ecológico de Sonora).

**Sampling methods:**
- **Seines:** 10 ft long × 4 ft deep, with fine mesh (the scan reads "18 inch," likely 1/8-inch; see [[Seine Netting]]).
- **Dip nets:** used in shallow or small habitats, with mesh size also partly illegible.
- **Collections:** All fish were preserved for later examination at the Centro. Desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*) were retained as [[Voucher Specimens]]; the rest were transferred to alcohol and deposited in the fish collection at the Centro.
- **Shoreline counts** were made where fish were observed.
- No attempt was made to collect fish larvae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish community.** Desert pupfish occurred in shallow (10–20 cm) vegetation-free areas. Where large numbers of exotic species were present, especially [[Sailfin Molly]] (*Poecilia latipinna*), pupfish occurred in low numbers. The only area where pupfish appeared relatively abundant was a large isolated pool about 200 m north of the bridge at the end of the M.O.D.E. on the east side. This pool appeared to lack surface water connections with the main cienega, which the authors suggest may keep pupfish out of the main water body. Competitors and predators present in the main water body included sailfin molly, [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Tilapia]] (*Tilapia* sp.), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) and [[Spotted Sunfish]] (*Lepomis punctatus*). Pupfish identification was flagged as needing confirmation.

**Macroinvertebrates.** Bottom samples were taken at El Campo, Hunter's Camp and the end of the M.O.D.E. during the first trip. Samples had not yet been analyzed. Field observations indicated that fly larvae (possibly Diptera: Simuliidae) were the most common macroinvertebrate. [[Anaerobic Conditions]] were commonly observed while bottom samples were taken, especially at the end of the M.O.D.E. Along the shallow north shoreline, dragonfly and damselfly nymphs (Odonata), adult aquatic beetles (Coleoptera) and an unidentified shrimp dominated seine hauls.

**Other fauna.**
- Mammal tracks were observed on the west shoreline; local residents reported many raccoons ("mapache"), and a coyote was seen on October 16.
- A couple of lizards were seen on a sand bar at the M.O.D.E.
- A [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] was found dead at the end of the M.O.D.E. It was photographed and preserved in formalin for storage at the Centro. Its identification also needs confirmation.

## Key Findings

- **Fish sampling** was carried out at about 10 sites and included multiple seine hauls and dip-net sets at each site. Fish were collected from October 13–18, 1992.
- **Exotic dominance:** Non-native and competitive species, including sailfin molly, mosquitofish, red shiner, tilapia and largemouth bass, dominated the sampled fish community.
- **Desert pupfish** were found mainly in shallow vegetation-free areas and occurred in low numbers. The exception was a large isolated pool with relatively abundant pupfish, probably due to isolation from competitors and predators.
- **Table of fish catches (preliminary):** Species abbreviations used were CYMA (*Cyprinodon macularius*), POLA (*Poecilia latipinna*), GAAF (*Gambusia affinis*), TISP (*Tilapia* sp.), MISA (*Micropterus salmoides*), LEPU (*Lepomis punctatus*) and CYLU (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). Several catch counts in the scanned table are illegible and are not reproduced here. Legible entries include 2 TISP and 2 LEPU at El Campo, and CYLU in several samples.
- **Invertebrates:** Samples were pending analysis. Fly larvae were the most common macroinvertebrate in field observations, anaerobic bottom conditions were common, and odonates, beetles and an unidentified shrimp dominated seine hauls on the north shore.
- **Other fauna:** Raccoon tracks, a coyote sighting, lizards and a dead leopard frog specimen were recorded.
- **Data status:** All results are preliminary. Several identifications (pupfish, leopard frog, shrimp, fly larvae) need confirmation, and the invertebrate samples require laboratory analysis.
