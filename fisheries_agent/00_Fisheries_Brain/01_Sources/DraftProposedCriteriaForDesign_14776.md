# Gila Topminnow Reclassification Proposal and Recovery Site Criteria

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1989 correspondence from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Ecological Services office documents a proposal to reclassify the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from endangered to threatened status. The proposal is based on successful reintroduction efforts by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Forest Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], and [[Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The document establishes draft criteria for determining which of approximately 47 introduced populations qualify as recovery sites under the [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]], which requires 20 populations successfully reestablished in the wild within historic range for at least three years. The correspondence seeks input from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] on standardized definitions for evaluating population status, particularly urgent given that at least 14 introduced sites were lost between 1987 and 1989.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) is a small poeciliid fish native to Arizona and New Mexico river drainages. By 1989, the species had been the subject of intensive reintroduction efforts across its historic range, primarily in spring-fed habitats, artesian wells, and small stream systems.

The [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]] established specific criteria for initiating downlisting from endangered to threatened status: 20 populations successfully reestablished in the wild, within historic range, surviving for at least three years. As of August 30, 1989, approximately 47 introduced populations existed, though many did not clearly meet all recovery criteria.

The document identifies significant population instability, with at least 14 sites documented as lost between 1987 and 1989, including [[Yellowstone Tank]], [[Government Spring]], [[Kino Spring]], [[Tres Alamos]], and others. This attrition rate raised concerns about whether minimum recovery thresholds would be met.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Reintroduction sites spanned diverse aquatic habitats across Arizona, including:

- Natural springs: [[Hidden Water Spring]], [[Sycamore Spring]], [[Walnut Spring]], [[Seven Springs]]
- Artesian wells and associated waters: [[Corner Artesian]], [[Cottonwood Artesian]]
- Creek systems: [[Horse Creek]], [[Red Creek]], [[Tule Creek]], [[Cow Creek]], [[Humbug Creek]], [[Castle Creek]]
- Warm springs: [[Mescal Warm Springs]], [[Big Spring]]
- Modified habitats: [[Roper Lake]], [[Arivaca Pond]], [[Palm Lake]]

The document distinguishes between "wild" populations in natural or semi-natural habitats and "captive" populations maintained in facilities such as:

- [[Arizona State University]]
- [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]]
- [[Phoenix Zoo]] (Cheetah Exhibit Pond)
- [[Desert Botanical Garden]]
- [[Sonoran Desert Museum]]
- [[University of Arizona]]
- [[Arizona Museum of Science and Industry]]

Historic range determination relied on museum specimens, written records, verbal accounts, and inference from drainage connectivity and habitat suitability. The criteria recognized primary evidence (museum specimens from the drainage) and secondary evidence (records from adjacent drainages without movement barriers).

## Key Findings

**Population Status and Recovery Threshold:**
- Approximately 47 introduced [[Gila Topminnow]] populations existed as of August 30, 1989
- At least 14 introduced sites were lost between 1987 and 1989
- Recovery Plan requires 20 qualifying populations for downlisting initiation
- 1989 monitoring data indicated the species "may just barely meet or exceed" the required number

**Proposed Criteria for Recovery Site Designation:**

*Captive vs. Wild Determination:*
- Populations are captive if primary purpose is stock supply, experimentation, education, or exhibition
- Populations within buildings are automatically captive
- Populations are captive if water supply is human-provided AND habitat is human-created AND location is within operated facilities
- All other populations qualify as "in the wild"

*Historic Range Determination:*
- Museum specimens from the river drainage provide definitive evidence
- Reliable written/verbal records plus suitable historic habitat evidence qualify
- Records from adjacent drainages without movement barriers plus habitat evidence qualify (but lower priority)
- Single lines of evidence insufficient

*Success Determination:*
- Topminnow must currently exist at the site
- Records must indicate persistence for at least 3 years
- Both conditions required for success designation

*Three-Year Survival Documentation:*
- Written stocking records with continuous occupation
- Direct verbal accounts from persons involved in stocking
- Reliable circumstantial evidence establishing approximate date (±1 year) with continuous occupation
- Any one form of evidence sufficient

**Site-Specific Information:**
Extant sites listed with stocking years ranging from 1976 ([[Hidden Water Spring]]) to 1989 (multiple sites). Defunct sites documented with both stocking and extirpation dates, providing data on population persistence times.

**Management Context:**
The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] was tasked with developing standardized definitions before September 30, 1989, with subsequent input from [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Forest Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona State Parks]], and [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]]. Internal pressure existed to complete downlisting before the fiscal year end (September 30, 1989).
