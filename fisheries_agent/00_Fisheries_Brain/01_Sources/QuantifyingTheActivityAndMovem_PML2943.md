# Movement and Swimming Activity of European Perch in a Temperate Lake Using Acoustic Telemetry and GIS

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This study quantified the horizontal movement, swimming activity and space use of European perch (*Perca fluviatilis*) in [[Lake Banyoles]], a small temperate lake in northeastern Spain. Seven surgically tagged perch were tracked with an automated [[VRAP System]] (VEMCO Radio-Acoustic Positioning), and the resulting positions were integrated into a [[Geographic Information Systems]] database. Perch were more active during daylight than at night, showed dawn and dusk activity peaks, and alternated between the limnetic and littoral zones while remaining closely tied to the shoreline. The authors conclude that, although perch can be very abundant in open water, they depend strongly on the littoral zone.

## Background & Description

Perch movement had been studied through diel activity, feeding and swimming work, but long-term tracking of natural movement remained difficult. Earlier approaches included mark-recapture, hydroacoustics, underwater observation and biotelemetry. The authors argue that telemetry offers finer spatial and temporal resolution than passive methods such as gillnetting, although it is limited by the number of trackable fish, post-surgery mortality and transmitter battery life.

Lake Banyoles, the second largest lake in the Iberian Peninsula, has a long history of fish introductions. Perch were introduced around 1991 and became common there. The lake comprises six basins, has a surface area of 11.8 ha and a mean depth of 14.8 m, and is of tectonic-karstic origin. Its water is largely subterranean and high in calcium, which limits productivity. The fish assemblage is dominated by exotic species, including [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Pumpkinseed Sunfish]] in the littoral zone, and roach and perch in the pelagic zone.

The stated aims were to describe the swimming activity of perch in a temperate lake and to analyse their diel activity rhythm and diel distribution pattern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** Lake Banyoles (42°07' N, 2°45' E; 172 m a.s.l.), classed as oligotrophic on nutrient and phytoplankton grounds, but with more mesotrophic primary production and benthic community.
- **Other species present:** [[Mosquitofish]], rudd, chub, common carp and European eel.
- **Lake stratification:** The lake is meromictic and anoxic in summer, after which the benthos disappears (Prat & Rieradevall, 1995).
- **Vegetation:** Macrophytes provide refuge from predation; signal detection by the array fell to zero near the littoral zone because of attenuation by vegetation.
- **Environmental effects on tracking:** Detection range varied with noise, refraction (temperature, depth contour), vegetation and distance from the array centre.

## Key Findings

**Study design and tagging**
- Seven perch (247–265 mm fork length, 243–271 g) were surgically implanted with VEMCO ultrasonic transmitters (38 mm long, 5.3 g in air, 3.5 g in water).
- Anaesthesia used 2-phenoxyethanol (40 mg L⁻¹). The surgery took 3–4 min, and fish were released within 60–90 min.
- Positions were recorded approximately every 180 s by three buoys arranged in a triangle of about 400 m in the northern half of the lake.

**Positioning accuracy**
- Accuracy was better than 1 m inside the array and decreased to about 10 m at a 200 m radius from its centre.
- Fish 6 was not tracked automatically because of transmitter failure. Fish 1 and 4 spent most of their time outside the array and were tracked manually; manual data were excluded from the GIS dataset.

**Activity and rhythm**
- All tracked perch showed a circadian pattern, with individual variation in activity levels.
- Mean swimming speed was 6.0 cm s⁻¹ at night and 10.4 cm s⁻¹ during the day.
- Activity peaked at sunrise and sunset, giving a diel rhythm with dawn and dusk maxima and a night-time minimum.

**Space use and movement**
- Two movement patterns were identified: movement between the limnetic and littoral zones, and movement restricted to the shoreline.
- Fish followed similar paths and concentrated their activity in a limited part of the lake.
- Fish returned to the same places after movements, showing site fidelity, but only over a few days.
- Maximum area of activity reached 39 ha for one spring-tagged fish. Range shape ranged from circular (eccentricity near 1, fish 3 and 7) to elliptical (fish 1, 4 and 5).
- At night, perch stayed closer to the littoral zone and made frequent shallow–deep movements, with many nocturnal positions falling outside the array. During the day they explored a larger area.

**Orientation**
- Nocturnal and diurnal movements both showed two principal orientations, NNW and SSE, corresponding to movements between littoral and open water.
- Day and night bearing distributions differed significantly (Kolmogorov–Smirnov Z = 2.462, p < 0.001).

**Interpretation**
- The diel pattern is linked to foraging on light, with daytime activity tracking planktivorous prey such as roach and juvenile perch.
- Night-time resting near the shore is consistent with a homing or site-fidelity strategy, which has been reported for perch in other lakes ([[Homing Behaviour]]).
- Although perch can be abundant in open water, the authors conclude they have a high dependence on the littoral zone.
