# Salt River Project: Irrigation, Reservoirs and Water Management History (c. 1958)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-ecosystem

## Executive Summary

This 1958 corporate and historical memorandum describes the [[Salt River Project]], a United States Reclamation Project operated by the Salt River Project Agricultural Improvement and Power District and the Salt River Valley Water Users' Association. It traces irrigation in the Salt River Valley from prehistoric [[Hohokam]] canals (c. 200 B.C.) through the federal reclamation era, the [[Kent Decree]] of 1910, the 1917 transfer of operations to the Association, and the expansion of storage dams and steam and hydroelectric power. The document is primarily a historical and statistical brochure rather than a biological study. Fish and aquatic fauna are not addressed; the aquatic relevance lies in the regulated river system, reservoirs and watershed hydrology.

## Background & Description

The Salt River Valley was irrigated long before European settlement. The [[Hohokam]] diverted Salt River water through roughly 125 miles of hand-dug canals to corn and cotton fields, and the document cites Dr. Omar A. Turney's view that a larger rural population farmed the valley before the Christian Era than farms it today. Later, Indigenous communities on nearby reservations (Fort McDowell, Salt River and Gila) irrigated 4,713 acres along the Salt and Verde rivers.

Settlers from 1869 built informal ditches, which led to disputes over priority and equitable distribution. The [[Reclamation Act of 1902]] gave the valley a federal route to storage and regulation. The Association was incorporated in Arizona on February 9, 1903, and a basic agreement with the Interior Department was executed on June 25, 1904.

Key construction milestones:
- [[Roosevelt Dam]]: excavation from 1905, cornerstone September 20, 1906, completed early 1911, dedicated March 18, 1911. It remains the world's highest masonry dam.
- [[Granite Reef Diversion Dam]]: built 1906–1908 at a cost of $627,000.
- Association ownership began November 1, 1917, under the contract of September 6, 1917.

Cost history: the original estimate of about $15 per acre rose to a final announced cost of $60 per acre. The Central Board of Review reported a net cost of $10,109,345.96 to June 1, 1915.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes a watershed problem. Average rainfall was stated to be steady, but runoff into the reservoirs fell year by year, by an average of 30% or more. The document attributes this to invasive and uneconomic vegetation (mesquite, willows, [[Salt Cedar]], cedars, manzanita, oak brush and jackpine thickets) consuming water. Overgrazing and erosion were also cited. From 1920 to 1954 the estimated loss was 16 million acre feet. A program starting in 1955 aimed to eradicate this growth in selected watershed areas.

Watershed and hydrology statistics (December 31, 1957):
- Watershed area: about 13,000 sq. mi.; elevation 1,200 to 11,000 feet
- Average annual rainfall: Phoenix 7.72 in.; Salt River 19.07 in.; Verde River 18.65 in.

## Key Findings

**Reservoir system** (Salt and Verde rivers):

| Dam | Location | Construction | Storage (acre-feet) | Dam height |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roosevelt | 80 mi from Phoenix | 1905–1911 | 1,381,580 | 280 ft |
| Horse Mesa | 65 mi | 1924–1927 | 245,138 | 300 ft |
| Mormon Flat | 51 mi | 1923–1925 | 57,852 | 224 ft |
| Stewart Mountain | 41 mi | 1928–1930 | 69,765 | 207 ft |
| Horseshoe (Verde) | 58 mi | 1944–1946 | 142,830 | 60 ft |
| Bartlett (Verde) | 46 mi | 1936–1939 | 179,548 | 283 ft |

Total storage capacity at December 31, 1957 was 2,076,713 acre-feet. Horseshoe Dam cost about $2,517,491 plus $925,000 for spillway gates. Bartlett Dam cost $6,500,000.

**Water rights** (as of 1958):
- Normal flow rights from the [[Kent Decree]] cover 151,083 of the 240,000 acres, dated 1869–1909. Earlier dates have priority, and during severe drought many lands received no normal-flow benefit.
- Stored and developed rights cover all 240,000 acres of Project lands.
- Pump water rights: 156,873 acres had acquired 232,837 acre-feet as of July 1, 1958. The purchase price was $14 per acre-foot, up to two acre-feet per acre.

**Power and infrastructure:**
- Hydroelectric and steam capacity grew to 390,890 kW (the Project's combined generating capacity in 1958). This included the [[Kyrene Power Plant]] (30,000 kW, 1952), Kyrene #2 (60,000 kW, 1954) and the [[Agua Fria Steam Plant]] (two 100,000 kW units, 1957–1958, $25,000,000).
- Electric customers grew from 45 in 1921 to 8,000 in 1941 and 59,455 by 1958.
- The electric system was converted from 25-cycle to 60-cycle from 1949 to 1952.
- The zanjero water-delivery system was replaced around 1950 by radiophone-equipped cars.

**Water supply and city partnership:** The Project supplied about 85% of the City of Phoenix's water needs. Phoenix serves about 350,000 customers. The Project's Pump Water Priority Proposal (1948) was designed to offset drought-related scarcity.

**Project statistics (1957):** 239,200 acres member area; 93,500 landowners; 1,300 miles of canals and laterals; 2,756 miles of power lines; 263 pumping plants; 1,550 employees; total plant in service $128,287,064.

**Outlook:** The document states that the canal rehabilitation programme, begun in 1950, was to continue through 1967 and had reduced water losses. Watershed clearance was ongoing.
