# Key to the Freshwater Fishes of Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an unpublished dichotomous identification key to the fishes of Texas, written by [[Clark Hubbs]] and dated April 3, 1959. The author states it is "not to be considered as published" and welcomes corrections and additions. It begins with a family-level key of about 43 families and then gives species-level keys for most of them. Despite its title, the key also covers brackish, estuarine and marine taxa that occur in Texas waters, so it is a working reference for the state's fish fauna rather than a strictly freshwater list.

## Background & Description

The key is organised in the standard dichotomous format. Each couplet presents a pair of contrasting character states, and the user follows the appropriate branch to a family, genus or species. Diagnostic characters include:

- **Fin and body form:** presence of paired fins, adipose fin, dorsal fin placement, and the shape of the caudal fin (heterocercal, homocercal, forked or rounded).
- **Head and mouth:** scaled or naked head, barbels, snout shape, mouth position (terminal, inferior or superior), and jaw teeth.
- **Meristics:** counts of lateral-line scales, scale rows, fin rays and spines, gill rakers, and pharyngeal teeth.
- **Proportions:** body depth, head length, eye size and snout length, expressed as ratios to standard length or other body dimensions.
- **Colour and markings:** lateral stripes, caudal spots, shoulder spots and fin pigmentation, which are often noted as lost in preservation.

Where a specimen meets both states of a couplet, the author instructs the user to key it out in both branches. This applies to several Cyprinidae couplets.

The document is an OCR-derived text with a number of transcription errors in names and numbers. These were corrected where the intended word was obvious.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The key does not report field data, habitat surveys, water chemistry or population information. Its ecological content is limited to habitat and salinity cues implied by the taxa it covers:

- **Freshwater families** such as [[Cyprinidae]], [[Catostomidae]], [[Centrarchidae]], [[Percidae]] and [[Poeciliidae]] dominate the species-level keys.
- **Marine and estuarine families** such as Carcharhinidae, Pristidae, Dasyatidae, Sciaenidae, Sparidae, Mugilidae and Gobiidae appear in the family key and are keyed to species.
- **Diadromous and migratory taxa** are represented by Anguillidae (*Anguilla rostrata*) and Salmonidae (*Salmo gairdneri*). The key lists only one salmonid.
- **Ancient lineages** include Polyodontidae (*Polyodon spathula*), Acipenseridae (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*), Lepisosteidae and Amiidae (*Amia calva*).

## Key Findings

**1. Family-level structure**
- The family key separates taxa primarily by fin presence, caudal fin type, scale and head characters, and gill-slit number. Cyclostomes (Petromyzontidae) are separated first, followed by elasmobranchs and then teleosts.
- About 43 families are keyed, including Petromyzontidae, Anguillidae, Lepisosteidae, Cyprinidae, Catostomidae, Ictaluridae (listed as Ameiuridae), Centrarchidae, Percidae and Sciaenidae.

**2. Species-level keys**
- **Lepisosteidae:** four species are separated by number of rows of upper-jaw teeth, beak proportions and lateral-line counts. The species are *Lepisosteus spatula*, *L. osseus*, *L. platostomus* and *L. productus*.
- **Clupeidae:** the key separates *Brevoortia gunteri*, *Harengula pensacolae*, *Alosa chrysochloris*, *Dorosoma cepedianum* and *D. petenense*, mainly by fin-ray counts and gill-raker numbers.
- **Catostomidae:** the key covers species such as *Cycleptus elongatus*, *Carpiodes carpio*, *Ictiobus* spp., several *Moxostoma* spp. and *Erimyzon* spp.
- **Cyprinidae:** this is the largest and most detailed section. It covers *Cyprinus carpio*, *Carassius auratus*, *Campostoma* spp., *Hybopsis* spp., *Pimephales* spp., *Dionda* spp. and *Hybognathus* spp. Many *Notropis* species are keyed in a long series of couplets, with separation based on pharyngeal teeth, scale counts, eye size and mid-dorsal stripe width.
- **Ictaluridae (Ameiuridae):** species are separated by anal-ray counts, caudal-fin shape and barbel colour. These include *Ictalurus punctatus*, *I. furcatus*, *I. natalis* and *I. melas*.
- **Cyprinodontidae and Poeciliidae:** Fundulus, Cyprinodon, Gambusia, Poecilia-type taxa (*Mollienesia*) and other genera are separated by fin-ray counts, scale rows and male gonopodial characters (the anal-fin modifications of male Gambusia).
- **Centrarchidae:** the key covers [[Micropterus]] (*M. salmoides*, *M. punctulatus*, *M. treculi*), [[Lepomis]] (about ten species, including *L. macrochirus*, *L. cyanellus*, *L. megalotis*) and *Pomoxis* spp., using dorsal-spine counts, opercular features and gill-raker numbers.
- **Percidae:** species of *Etheostoma*, *Percina*, *Hadropterus* and *Ammocrypta* are separated by lateral-line counts, body-bar patterns and pectoral-fin length.

**3. Key limitations (as stated or evident)**
- The author explicitly notes that the document is not a published work and is subject to correction.
- Some couplets are ambiguous in the OCR text. For example, certain *Notropis* and *Lepomis* couplets contain garbled numbers that were reconstructed where the intended value was clear.
- The key does not include distribution maps, drainage-level records, or specimen data.
