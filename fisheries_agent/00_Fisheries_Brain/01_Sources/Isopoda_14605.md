# Isopoda of European Inland Waters: Limnofauna Europaea (1967)

#isopoda #crustacea #limnology #asellidae

## Executive Summary

This excerpt comes from *Limnofauna Europaea*, a 1967 compendium of all multicellular animal species living in European inland waters, edited by [[Joachim Illies]] and published by Gustav Fischer Verlag. It reproduces the front matter (editorial board, title page) and the chapter on freshwater Isopoda by [[Milan Straskraba]] (Prague). The chapter classifies European limnic isopods into three ecological-historical groups, notes that about one hundred species are known (most of them groundwater dwellers with poorly known taxonomy), and highlights strong endemism, especially in Portugal. It also presents the distribution table, its symbol system and an ecological code list. Literature is included up to 1963.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Isopods are mainly marine and terrestrial crustaceans. Only a small part inhabits fresh water, where they occur from groundwater through flowing and standing waters to brackish water.
- **Three groups of European limnic isopods:**
  1. *Old freshwater inhabitants*: family [[Asellidae]].
  2. *Tertiary marine relicts* preserved in groundwater: families [[Sphaeromidae]], [[Cirolanidae]], [[Microparasellidae]] and [[Microcerberidae]].
  3. *Marine immigrants* that entered brackish water and then connected rivers, streams and lakes, mostly recently: family Janiridae (coastal regions and *Jaera sarsi* in Ponto-Caspian rivers) and family Idotheidae (glacial-marine relict [[Mesidothea Entomon]]).
- **Feeding:** European freshwater isopods feed on decomposing plant material and detritus.
- **Taxonomic status:** Most of the roughly one hundred species are groundwater forms whose taxonomy and distribution are still poorly known. Many are known from only a single locality and are therefore treated as endemics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Endemism:** [[Portugal]] (Region 1) stands out. [[Jose Braga]] described many Portuguese species over the previous two decades that had not yet been compared with other taxa. The author suggests Portugal may be special because its fauna links with that of northeastern North America.
- **Widespread surface species:** Only three widely distributed species occur in surface waters of the limnic region:
  - [[Asellus Aquaticus]], a northeastern immigrant, which west of the [[Rhine-Aare-Rhone Line]] is progressively replaced by [[Asellus Meridianus]].
  - [[Asellus Meridianus]], dominant to the west of that line.
  - [[Asellus Coxalis]], which replaces the others in the circum-Mediterranean region.
- **Regional coverage:** The distribution table covers western and central Mediterranean areas, the Balkans, the central uplands, British Isles, Iceland, the Danube lowland, Fennoscandia and the Caspian region, among others.
- **Ecological codes (Column "Ökologie"):** Numbers indicate the preferred biotope:
  - 1: freshwater in general, no specialisation
  - 2: groundwater, caves and psammon
  - 3: springs ([[Krenon]])
  - 4: brooks and small rivers ([[Rhithron]])
  - 5: rivers and large streams ([[Potamon]])
  - 6: lakes and standing waters
  - 7: temporary small waters, puddles, ponds
  - 8: phytotelmata (for example tree holes and leaf axils)
  - 9: brackish water and estuaries
  - 10: inland saline waters
  - 11: bogs
  - 12: thermal waters
  - 13: hygropetric zone
  - 14: swamps and damp soil
  - Codes 20 to 31 denote parasitic lifestyles (ectoparasites and endoparasites on crustaceans, molluscs, insects, fish, birds, and plant tissues), applicable when a species spends most of its life parasitically.
- **Symbols:** Regional symbols denote endemism, confirmed presence, expected presence, transgressive occurrence, uncertain locality, doubtful records, unknown status, and confirmed absence.

## Key Findings

- European freshwater isopods fall into three ecological-historical groups, reflecting old freshwater lineages, marine relicts in groundwater, and recent marine immigrants.
- About one hundred freshwater isopod species are recognised in Europe. Many are groundwater specialists with incomplete taxonomy.
- Endemism is high, especially in Portugal, where [[Jose Braga]]'s species in [[Asellus]], [[Stenasellus]] and [[Synasellus]] are known from single localities.
- Only three widespread surface-water species occur in the limnic region: *Asellus aquaticus*, *A. meridianus* and *A. coxalis*, with a clear east-west and north-south replacement pattern.
- The distribution table summarises genera and species across roughly twenty regions, with each entry marked by standardised symbols and ecological codes, giving a framework for comparing faunas.
- Literature is included up to 1963, so the table reflects the state of knowledge at that date.
- The front matter lists the international contributor panel (over 50 specialists from many European countries and beyond), indicating the collaborative nature of the compendium.
