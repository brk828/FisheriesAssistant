# Pyramid Lake Fish Survey and Cui-ui Hybridization Assessment - May 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #conservation-biology #hybridization

## Executive Summary

Field survey conducted May 4-5, 1989 at [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada to assess hybridization between endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) and [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*). Approximately 300-400 cui-ui were tagged during one day of trap netting operations; nearly 8,000 fish tagged during the season. Multiple species collected including [[Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor*), [[Sacramento Perch]] (*Archoplites interruptus*), [[Pyramid Lake Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*), and one [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*, second specimen recorded from lake). Specimens collected for meristic analysis to determine extent of hybridization. Water temperature 17°C with greenish hue.

## Background & Description

Survey conducted in collaboration with Gary Scoppettone at [[Pyramid Lake]], located 45 miles from Reno, Nevada. Primary focus on [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered catostomid endemic to Pyramid Lake, and potential hybridization with [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*).

**Hybrid Identification Characters:**
- Lip structure: hybrids show intermediate condition between cui-ui and fleshy, papillose lips of *C. tahoensis*
- Coloration: [[Tahoe Sucker]] exhibits sharp division between darker upper body and light lower body; pattern sometimes present in hybrids
- Scale size: hybrids possess smaller scales than cui-ui, larger than tahoe suckers
- All identified "hybrids" resembled cui-ui more than tahoe suckers

**Species Collected:**
- [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*): 4 pure specimens provided, 300-400 tagged
- [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*): approximately 30 specimens
- Cui-ui × Tahoe hybrids: 8 specimens
- [[Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor*): approximately 60 specimens (30 caught during boat operations, 10 preserved, 30 iced, 30 frozen)
- [[Sacramento Perch]] (*Archoplites interruptus*): 1 specimen during boat operations, 1 additional at Truckee mouth
- [[Pyramid Lake Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*): 1 specimen during boat operations, 1 at Truckee mouth
- [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*): 1 specimen (second record for lake)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Conditions:**
- Water temperature: 17°C
- Water appearance: greenish hue
- Surface conditions: developing chop

**Hatchery Operations:**
Visited [[Cui-ui Hatchery]] where Mark Coleman conducting experiments on effect of female age on egg viability. Larvae observed to be non-phototactic. On May 5, eggs stripped from 2 females and fertilized with one male. Eggs placed in hatching containers, some with bentonite silt, some without; no apparent difference in success.

**Larval Behavior:**
Most larvae remained on bottom of stream tanks. Some individuals very active, moving with anguilliform (eel-like) body thrashings. Active fish appeared smaller than bottom-resting individuals.

**Fish Passage Facilities:**
Fish elevator facility closed due to "hybrid alert." Fish ladder and canal system extends from lake to facility. Cannon deployed to deter pelicans (note: document states "penguins" but likely refers to pelicans). Two dead [[Great Blue Heron]]s located; mortality control measures unclear. [[Cui-ui]] observed below lowermost ladder but not attempting to ascend.

## Key Findings

**Population Monitoring:**
- Trap nets highly efficient for [[Cui-ui]] and [[Tahoe Sucker]]
- Approximately 8,000 fish tagged during 1989 season
- Standard protocol: fork length measurement, sex determination, pure/hybrid identification, tagging of cui-ui and hybrids, release

**Hybridization Concern:**
Fish elevator facility shut down due to hybridization concerns. Meristic analysis requested to determine whether genuine hybrid problem exists in lake population. Quick turnaround requested for management decisions.

**Age Determination:**
Opercle bone used for aging; left or right opercle taken inconsistently.

**Specimen Quality Concerns:**
Material quality for skeletal analysis questionable due to collection methods:
- Fish knocked out on boat transom (potentially breaking frontals and other bones)
- Several hours delay between death and freezing
- Preserved material also sent to R.R. Miller and G. Smith
- Material provided to Peter Moyle (possibly for allozyme analysis)

**Collaborative Opportunities:**
Cooperative relationship established with Gary Scoppettone. Future access to [[Pyramid Lake]] contingent on rapid meristic data analysis and assessment letter regarding hybrid status.
