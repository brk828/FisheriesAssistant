# Telemetry of Sub-Adult Razorback Suckers: Movements, Habitat Use and Entrainment in the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This final report (15 July 2000) from [[Arizona State University]] for the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] presents radio-telemetry results for hatchery-reared, sub-adult [[Razorback Sucker]] stocked into the lower [[Colorado River]]. The study tested whether stocked fish are lost through entrainment at dams, canals and pump stations, a question the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Biological Opinion (1997) identified as a Reasonable and Prudent Alternative requirement.

Sixty sub-adults (20 per iteration) were tracked for more than 90 days in two sections: the regulated [[Parker Strip]] and [[Lake Havasu]]. Entrainment was confirmed only in the summer Parker Strip release, which the authors attribute mainly to stress from high water temperatures during capture, transport, tagging and release. The authors conclude that entrainment is unlikely to depress source populations if stocking protocols are followed, and they recommend a set of stocking practices.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endangered, endemic catostomid of the [[Colorado River]] system. Its range and numbers declined sharply over the past century. The remnant population in [[Lake Mohave]] is large but dwindling, and the species does not recruit successfully in nature, so survival depends on stocking and non-native fish management (Minckley et al. 2000).

Previous razorback sucker telemetry studies in the basin had not specifically examined entrainment. The study's purpose was to supply data on dispersal and entrainment of stocked sub-adults released below [[Parker Dam]]. The study area comprised two segments of the Havasu Division:

- **Parker Strip:** a 23.2 km regulated reach between Parker Dam (RK 309.3) and [[Headgate Rock Diversion Dam]] (RK 286.1), with a strongly flowing main channel, off-channel lagoons and Moovalya Lake. Discharge averaged about 12,050 CFS in summer and 12,558 CFS in spring.
- **Lake Havasu:** a reservoir formed by Parker Dam in 1938, about 42 km long, with a maximum depth near 22 m. The shallow, turbid [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] arm (< 2 m deep) was the main area fish used.

Entrainment sites included the [[CRIT Main Canal]], the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) intake, the [[Metropolitan Water District]] (MWD) intake, and the hydropower and spillway structures at dams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods.** Fish were sourced from Native Fish Work Group ponds at Boulder City, derived from wild [[Lake Mohave]] larvae. Summer fish were transported at 16 °C and spring fish at 13 °C, in water with 5 mg/L MS-222 and 5% sodium chloride. Transmitters (ATS Inc.; 8 g in air, 40–42 MHz, about 90-day nominal battery life) were internally implanted in 10 of 20 summer fish and externally mounted on the rest. All fish were PIT tagged. Fixed receivers monitored dam and canal structures, and mobile surveys were conducted roughly weekly by boat, both day and night.

**Water conditions.** Summer Parker Strip water exceeded 25 °C daily. Spring release water was about 17 °C. Lake Havasu lake elevation ranged from 450.5 to 445.5 feet MSL, and the Bill Williams River was dry for several km upstream of the lake during the study. Daily releases from Parker Dam averaged 11,586 CFS; MWD pumping averaged 1,439 CFS and CAP pumping averaged 2,215 CFS.

**Habitat notes.** Parker Strip substrate ranges from armored cobble-boulder near the dam to silt in backwaters. Quiet margins hold [[Giant Reed]] (*Phragmites australis*), cattail (*Typha domingensis*) and [[Sago Pondweed]] (*Potamogeton pectinatus*). Water clarity fell from about 2 m upstream to 0.5 m or less downstream, largely due to watercraft.

**Historical entrainment cases.** The report reviews multiple records of razorback suckers in water-control structures and canals, including:

- A population in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], California, that was probably seeded by entrained young fish.
- Two larvae captured in 1986 in the Hayden-Rhodes Aqueduct, representing less than 0.1% of collected larvae.
- Four PIT-tagged, stocked Lake Mohave fish (26.0–28.0 cm) confirmed surviving passage through [[Davis Dam]].
- Three adult-size fish (about 50 cm) captured in the Hayden-Rhodes Aqueduct in autumn 1986.

## Key Findings

**Parker Strip, summer release (20 August 1998):**
- Stocked fish were released into water above 25 °C, transported, tagged and released on the same day with no acclimation.
- Headgate Rock fixed stations were not reliably working at release. Five transmitters were never detected, and six were lost after the first week.
- If all these fish passed Headgate Rock, 13 fish (about two-thirds) would have done so. The authors attribute this to stress, weakness and lethargy, not to normal behaviour.

**Parker Strip, spring release (30 April 1999):**
- Water was about 17 °C. Fish were acclimated overnight in a quiet lagoon, and all received external transmitters (no anesthesia).
- All 20 fish were contacted at least once. Contact was maintained with 16 fish through the nominal 90-day transmitter life. All fish remained in the 23 km reach, and no entrainment was suspected.

**Lake Havasu (release 8 December 1998):**
- Water depth prevented signal reception below about 3 m. The MWD intake recorded one signal, but there was no evidence that the fish entered the intake.
- Nine of ten fish released near the CAP intake were later contacted inside the refuge. Four of ten fish released above the MWD intake were also contacted in or around the refuge.
- Roughly 2/3 of study fish ended up in the Bill Williams arm, indicating strong attraction to shallow, turbid water.
- No fish was drawn into the CAP or MWD intakes or contacted near Parker Dam.
- The Parker Dam remote logger lost power for eight hours (26–27 January 1999).

**Assessment:**
- Entrainment was documented in the summer Parker Strip iteration (about 2/3 of experimental fish). It was attributed to stress rather than general passage behaviour.
- Entrainment at the CAP and MWD intakes was not observed, even though release sites were close.
- Entrainment through [[Davis Dam]] and other structures is documented in the literature, and the authors judge that losses to a source population are not substantial.
- The authors hypothesize that young razorback suckers enter canals after winter dewatering, when non-native predators are absent, and that canal populations are then exterminated when predators return.

**Recommendations:**
1. Stock at a minimum size of 30 cm.
2. Stock during cooler months (late autumn, winter, early spring) to reduce thermal stress.
3. Stock in small groups rather than en masse.
4. Stock into quiet, off-channel areas rather than strong currents.
5. Consider conditioning to current and local site acclimation, following [[Mueller and Foster 2000]], as additional protocol steps.

The authors consider the expected losses of stocked fish from entrainment to be acceptable.
