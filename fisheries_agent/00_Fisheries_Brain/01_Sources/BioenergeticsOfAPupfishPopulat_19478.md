# Bioenergetics of a Pupfish Population (*Cyprinodon*) and Its Algal Food Supply in a Thermal Stream

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This doctoral dissertation (Arizona State University, August 1974) by Robert Joseph Naiman presents a bioenergetic study of [[Amargosa Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis amargosae*) and its algal food base in [[Tecopa Bore]], a 47.5°C artesian stream in the Mohave Desert near Death Valley, California. Sampling ran from 1971 to 1973. The system is simple: almost no riparian shading, clear desert skies, negligible allochthonous input, and essentially one primary consumer. Primary production is high year-round, a large share of fixed energy leaves as [[Dissolved Organic Carbon]], and the pupfish population has secondary production roughly ten times that reported for carnivorous fishes in natural waters.

## Background & Description

**Study site.** Tecopa Bore issues from the ground as an artesian well and flows about 1 km into a marsh and the [[Amargosa River]]. It lies in the Middle Amargosa Basin, 1.7 km north of Tecopa Hot Springs (35°55' N, 116°14' W), on the shoreline of Pleistocene Lake Tecopa. The surrounding vegetation is sparse: [[Smooth Cyperus]], [[Salt Grass]], and a few creosote bushes. [[Ruppia]] is rare.

**Rationale.** The site combines features rarely found together in streams: no riparian canopy, a dry climate with few cloudy days, a year-round growing season, and near-total dependence on solar energy. Because invertebrate forage is scarce, the pupfish has become herbivorous, which allowed the first natural-setting study of herbivorous fish production in a thermal system. Earlier thermal-ecosystem work was mostly systematic or focused on upper temperature limits.

**Fauna.** The pupfish is the only primary consumer of note. Invertebrates are present but scarce, including ostracods (*Potamocypris* sp.), cyclopoid copepods (*Eucyclops agilis*), a harpacticoid copepod (*Cletocamptus* sp.), moss beetles (*Ochthebius* sp.) and chironomid larvae. The algal flora is low in diversity: four Cyanophyta species and three common diatoms, dominated by a small *Oscillatoria*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature:** Water cools 8–12°C across the study area on calm days, with faster cooling on cold or windy days. Mid-channel temperatures are warmest. Station 4, nearest the source, averages 44.9°C; station 98 averages 28.8°C.
- **Solar radiation:** Corrected annual input is 757,488 kcal/m²/yr. Monthly values peak in May (95,558 kcal/m²) and bottom out in December (35,805 kcal/m²).
- **Discharge:** A semi-annual cycle with roughly 2,268–6,696 L/min. Annual discharge is about 1.87 × 10⁹ L. Mean mainstream velocity is 0.311 m/s.
- **Water chemistry:** A sulfato-chloride desert water with total alkalinity of 601–645 mg/L (as CaCO₃), inorganic carbon of about 135 mg/L, TDS of 2,558 mg/L, and pH 8.5. Ionic balance was checked (cations 43.538 vs. anions 43.904 meq/L).
- **Algal mats:** Mat area ranged from about 740 m² (December 1972) to 1,178 m² (August 1973). Only the top 2–3 mm is photosynthetically active, so the autotrophic share of standing crop is about 10–30%.
- **Pupfish habitat:** Fish occupied 85–351 m² (6.1–24.8% of the study area), averaging 240 m² (17%). Fish are rare above station 42.

**Methods (brief).** Primary production used the ¹⁴C method on algal-mat cores (monthly, 10 stations). Drift was measured with stream drift nets. Food habits used gut analysis (218 individuals, frequency-of-occurrence and points methods). Population size came from box-trap censuses using the Regier–Robson non-parametric method. Growth used caged fish in 1 m² enclosures, with Ricker's (1946, 1958) instantaneous growth formula. Production was P = GB, and ingestion was estimated from gut volume, food passage time (mean 280 min), and caloric content (4.4 kcal/g AFDW).

## Key Findings

**Primary production**
- Mean annual net productivity is about 3.25 g C/m²/day. Station 4 (source) averages 4.78 g C/m²/day, typical stations about 3.5, and station 98 about 2.03.
- Monthly productivity ranges from 1.16 g C/m²/day (December) to 4.86 g C/m²/day (May).
- Annual fixation is 1,186 g C/m²/yr, or 11,065 kcal/m²/yr. Total ecosystem fixation is 1,409,920 g C/yr (1.315 × 10⁷ kcal/yr).
- Net photosynthetic efficiency is 1.46% of corrected light (0.68% of total).
- Productivity is linearly related to solar radiation (r = 0.812).
- The P/R ratio exceeds 1, making Tecopa Bore an autotrophic stream.

**Standing crop and turnover**
- Mean annual standing crop of organic matter is 718 kcal/m², turned over about 15.4 times per year (mean turnover 23.7 days).

**Export**
- Particulate drift (POM): 14,400 g AFDW/yr, or 633,600 kcal/yr (453 kcal/m²/yr, 4.1% of net production). Peak export runs from late spring through summer and tracks sunlight.
- Dissolved organic carbon (DOC): 279,000 g/yr, or 2,603,070 kcal/yr (1,859 kcal/m²/yr, 16.8% of net production). DOC export is about 4.1 times POM export.
- Combined stream export is 20.9% (2,312 kcal/m²/yr) of primary production.

**Pupfish food and ingestion**
- Gut contents are about 85% algal-detritus, 4% invertebrates, 2% *Ruppia*, and 9% empty.
- Algal-detritus occurs in 99% of fish, invertebrates in 22%, and *Ruppia* in 4% (n = 218).
- Ingestion is about 0.20–0.50 of body energy per day, and up to about 30% in summer. Values are high compared with carnivorous fishes (1–6%).
- Ingestion totals 1,878 kcal/m²/yr, or 17% of net primary production (489,605 kcal/yr total).

**Population**
- The population is lowest in August (about 2,000 fish) and highest in autumn (about 49,465 in October 1972). Peak biomass is about 13 kg wet weight in spring to autumn.
- Young adults (15–30 mm) contribute most biomass. Mean standing crop is 23.6 kcal/m².
- Growth rates (instantaneous, per day): juveniles (<15 mm) 0.0952 mean; young adults (15–30 mm) 0.0111; large adults (>30 mm) 0.0084.

**Secondary production**
- Annual production is 118.6 kcal/m²/yr (155.4 g wet weight/m²), or 31,367 kcal/yr total.
- Production is 6.41% of ingested energy and 1.07% of areal primary production (0.24% of total ecosystem fixation).
- Production is greatest in autumn (14–22 kcal/m²/month) and lowest in winter and late summer (3–6 kcal/m²/month).
- Turnover is about 5 times per year, with a mean generation time of 72.4 days.
- The population appears to be in steady state.

**Energy budget**
- Light input 757,488 kcal/m²/yr. Non-assimilated light is 746,423 kcal/m²/yr.
- Autotroph and heterotroph metabolism uses 72.7% (8,039 kcal/m²/yr), the main energy pathway out of the system.
- Pupfish: ingestion 1,878; growth 119 (1.1%); metabolism about 595 (5.4%); feces about 1,164 (10.5%).

**Comparisons**
- Pupfish production is about 10 times higher than values for carnivorous fishes in natural waters.
- Tecopa Bore primary productivity (22.1–38.6 kcal/m²/day in the study's comparison table) is high relative to most thermal and cooler systems.
- Against [[Bear Brook]] (heterotrophic, >99% allochthonous), Tecopa Bore has a DOM:POM ratio of 4.1 versus 2.3, and metabolism of 73% of input versus 34%. Bear Brook has a much larger standing crop (4,730 kcal/m²).
- Productivity is increased by a year-round growing season, and the study suggests heated effluents may raise production in other systems.

**Conclusions**
- Thermal streams with high solar input can support very high primary and secondary productivity year-round.
- Dissolved organic carbon is a major energy loss in both autotrophic and heterotrophic streams.
- Herbivorous fish can be an important energy conduit when invertebrate forage is scarce.
