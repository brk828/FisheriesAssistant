# Petition to Commission for Environmental Cooperation Regarding ESA Enforcement Moratorium

#environmental-law #endangered-species #conservation-policy #biodiversity #legal-action

## Executive Summary

This document is a legal petition filed July 2, 1995, under Article 14 of the [[North American Agreement on Environmental Cooperation]] (NAAEC) by five conservation organizations: [[Biodiversity Legal Foundation]], [[Consejo Asesor Sierra Madre]], [[Forest Guardians]], [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]], and [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]]. The petition alleges that the [[United States]] is failing to effectively enforce Section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) of 1973 due to the "Hutchison Rider" (also called "ESA Moratorium"), a budgetary amendment attached to Public Law 104-6 signed April 10, 1995. This rider prohibited the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) from making final determinations for species listings or critical habitat designations for the remainder of Fiscal Year 1995 and rescinded $1.5 million from the listing program budget. The petitioners argue this constitutes a failure to effectively enforce environmental law under NAAEC Article 5(1), motivated by economic considerations related to trade, farming, ranching, logging, and fishing activities.

## Background & Description

The [[Endangered Species Act]] is described by the [[U.S. Supreme Court]] in *Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill* (437 U.S. 153, 180, 1978) as "the most comprehensive legislation for the preservation of endangered species ever enacted by any nation." Section 4 of the ESA (16 U.S.C. § 1533) establishes the listing process, which is the foundational mechanism for species protection. Any interested person or organization can petition the [[Secretary of the Interior]] to list a species as threatened or endangered or to designate critical habitat. The Secretary must make an initial determination within 90 days whether the petition presents substantial scientific or commercial information, then a second determination within 12 months, and finalize warranted actions within 18 months.

The Hutchison Rider, named for Senator Kay Bailey Hutchison (R-Texas), was embedded in the Emergency Supplemental Appropriations and Rescissions for the Department of Defense to Preserve and Enhance Military Readiness Act of 1995 (109 Stat 73, 86). According to an April 21, 1995 memorandum from FWS Director Mollie Beattie, the rider: (1) prohibits FWS from making "final determinations" for species or critical habitat designations for the remainder of Fiscal Year 1995, and (2) rescinds $1.5 million from the listing program budget while prohibiting compensation from other programs. FWS interpreted this to mean it cannot publish final rules, including emergency rules, to list species or designate critical habitat under ESA sections 4(a)(1) or 4(a)(3).

The petitioning organizations have extensive experience filing ESA listing petitions and have successfully forced the listing of several species and designation of critical habitats. The moratorium directly prevents them from continuing this conservation work. At the time of the petition, 118 species had been proposed for ESA listing but could not receive protection due to the rider.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The ESA's stated purpose is to "provide a means whereby the ecosystems upon which endangered and threatened species depend may be conserved" (16 U.S.C. § 1531). The listing process under Section 4 is the critical first step that triggers all subsequent ESA protections. Without listing, species receive no federal protection regardless of their conservation status.

The petition emphasizes that species denied ESA protection during the moratorium may go extinct before enforcement resumes. Senator Lautenberg noted during Congressional debate that 118 threatened species would be left unprotected by the amendment. The petition cites conservation biology principles, referencing E.O. Wilson's *The Diversity of Life* and the fundamental value of biodiversity: "the value of biodiversity is the value of everything that there is."

Cross-border species are particularly affected. [[Consejo Asesor Sierra Madre]] works to protect species in the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] of [[Chihuahua]], [[Mexico]], including the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], which exists in both the United States and Mexico. The moratorium undermines binational conservation efforts.

The petition identifies the economic activities motivating the moratorium: farming, ranching, logging, fishing, and hydropower projects—all closely tied to trade among [[NAFTA]] parties. Senator Hutchison stated the rider would prevent "bait fish and golden checked warblers and jaguars and salmon" from taking "precedence over the rights of farmers and ranchers." Senator Gorton noted that threatened or endangered status "automatically results in restrictions of the use of property, restriction in economic activity, and in cultural, social and community disruptions." Senator Craig referenced "hundreds of millions of dollars of lost revenue," and Senator Domenici cited how "listings have decimated the timber industry in communities such as Reserve, NM."

## Key Findings

**Legal Arguments:**

1. **Violation of NAAEC Article 5(1):** The petition argues the United States is failing to "effectively enforce its environmental laws and regulations" as required by NAAEC Article 5(1). The ESA qualifies as an "environmental law" under NAAEC Article 45(2)(a)(iii), which specifically includes laws protecting "wild flora or fauna, including endangered species, [and] their habitat."

2. **Not a Reasonable Exercise of Discretion:** The moratorium does not reflect "a reasonable exercise of discretion in respect of investigatory, prosecutorial, regulatory or compliance matters" (NAAEC Article 5(2)(a)). It is a blanket prohibition on all new listings without distinguishing worthy from unworthy designations.

3. **Not a Bona Fide Resource Allocation:** The rider does not result from a "bona fide decision to allocate resources to enforcement in respect of other environmental matters determined to have higher priorities" (NAAEC Article 5(2)(b)). Rather, it is a punitive budget cut designed to prevent FWS from performing its statutory duties. Senator Hutchison explicitly stated: "no longer are we going to fully fund the implementation of this act."

4. **Suspension vs. Amendment:** The United States has not exercised its right under NAAEC Article 3 to "adopt or modify" its environmental laws. The U.S. District Court for Arizona confirmed in *Silver v. Babbitt* (Civ. No. 94-337 PHX CAM, May 10, 1995) that "there is no substantive amendment in the ESA by the rider." The ESA remains law; enforcement has simply been suspended.

5. **Economic Motivation:** The suspension was explicitly motivated by economic considerations to benefit U.S. trade and commodity exports (timber, farm products) at the lowest possible cost—precisely what the NAAEC is designed to prohibit.

**Procedural Compliance:**

The petition demonstrates compliance with all NAAEC Article 14(1) requirements: written in English; filed by qualified non-governmental organizations; provides sufficient information; aims solely at promoting enforcement; includes prior notification to U.S. authorities (letter dated June 9, 1995, to President Clinton, Senate Majority Leader Dole, Speaker Gingrich, Secretary Babbitt, and FWS Director Beattie); and petitioners reside in NAFTA party territories.

The petition also satisfies Article 14(2) criteria: alleges substantial harm to petitioners' conservation interests; raises matters advancing NAAEC goals (particularly Articles 10(2)(i) and (j) regarding conservation of wild flora and fauna); no private remedies are available; and the submission is not drawn exclusively from mass media reports.

**Organizational Standing:**

- **[[Biodiversity Legal Foundation]]** (Boulder, Colorado): Has successfully filed multiple ESA Section 4 petitions; has pending petitions directly thwarted by the rider; party to the "Fund for Animals" settlement agreement requiring expeditious listing progress, now jeopardized by the moratorium.

- **[[Consejo Asesor Sierra Madre]]** (Mexico): Provides technical and legal services to [[Tarahumara]] and [[Tepehuan]] indigenous communities; cross-border partnership with Forest Guardians to protect threatened species in the Sierra Madre.

- **[[Forest Guardians]]** (Santa Fe, New Mexico): Approximately 1,000 members who use southwestern public lands for wildlife observation and recreation; works through administrative appeals and litigation to uphold ESA provisions.

- **[[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]]** (Silver City, New Mexico): Dedicated to preservation in the [[Greater Gila]] region; staff has surveyed and sought protection for threatened species under ESA Section 4.

- **[[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]]** (Phoenix, Arizona): Staff has researched and sought protection for many threatened species; efforts to add species to the list directly thwarted by the rider.

**Broader Implications:**

The petition warns that Congress is considering additional bills similar to the Hutchison Rider that would suspend enforcement of environmental laws for activities such as grazing and logging. If the Secretariat does not find that NAAEC Article 5(1) prohibits legislative suspensions of enforcement, "environmental laws will remain on the books, but will not be enforced." This would render the NAAEC ineffective and allow parties to avoid compliance through legislative exemptions rather than formal amendments.

The petition concludes by invoking [[Aldo Leopold]]'s conservation ethic: "The last word in ignorance is the man who says of an animal or plant: 'What good is it?' If the land mechanism as a whole is good, then every part is good, whether we understand it or not."
