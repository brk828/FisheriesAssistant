# Bog Hole Project Management Work Session Summary

#ichthyology #native-fish-conservation #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #cyprinidae #habitat-management

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[U.S. Forest Service]] [[Coronado National Forest]] summarizes a March 1989 work session regarding management of the [[Bog Hole]] in the [[San Rafael Valley]], Arizona. The meeting brought together representatives from [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], conservation organizations, and ranching interests to discuss revision of cooperative agreements and future development plans. Primary management objectives were established prioritizing waterfowl, native fish ([[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Gila Chub]]), and non-consumptive recreation. The group identified numerous management issues and proposed several potential habitat development scenarios, with a deadline of October 1, 1989 set for completing a draft cooperative agreement.

## Background & Description

The [[Bog Hole]] is a managed wetland area on the [[Coronado National Forest]] in southern Arizona, operated under a cooperative agreement between the [[U.S. Forest Service]] and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The site includes an existing impoundment created for waterfowl management that also supports populations of native fish species. The area is located within the [[San Rafael Allotment]] and involves multiple stakeholder interests including livestock grazing, wildlife conservation, recreation, and native species protection.

The work session was held at the Stage Stop Inn in Patagonia and attended by representatives from multiple agencies and organizations including Bob Hudson (San Rafael Allotment permittee), Jerry Pratt ([[Cochise Conservation Council]] and [[Arizona Wildlife Federation]]), Tom Spalding, Will Hayes, Dean Hendrickson, John Millican, and Ron Olding ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), Sally Stefferud ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]), and several [[U.S. Forest Service]] personnel including Don Case, Jerry Stefferud, Randy Smith, Karen Riggs, and Tom Deecken.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Bog Hole]] provides critical habitat for three native fish species requiring management attention: [[Desert Pupfish]] (proper subspecies, likely [[Cyprinodon macularius]]), [[Gila Topminnow]] ([[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]]), and [[Gila Chub]] ([[Gila intermedia]]). The site also serves as important waterfowl habitat and is used by [[Bald Eagles]] in the [[San Rafael Valley]].

Current ecological concerns include erosion, habitat degradation, and the presence of non-native species that may require rotenone renovation. Desert pupfish populations exist both within the impoundment and upstream of it. The ecosystem includes riparian vegetation and cienega habitats that could be expanded through proposed development projects.

Water availability is a critical limiting factor, with questions raised about sufficient water supply for proposed expansions. The existing reservoir receives water that could potentially supply downstream cienega development, though water rights and yield documentation are needed. The area experiences flooding that could affect downstream structure stability.

## Key Findings

**Management Objectives Established:**
- Three primary objectives in priority order: (1) waterfowl, (2) native fish, (3) non-consumptive recreation
- Native fish management specifically targets habitat improvement for [[Desert Pupfish]] (proper subspecies), [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Gila Chub]]
- Decrease erosion
- Improve public education and involvement

**Current Management Issues Identified:**
- Excessive fencing may limit effective management
- Need to evaluate continuation of special use permit with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]
- Road closure needed to keep vehicles restricted to upper areas
- Fishing closure under consideration
- Potential ecosystem effects of rotenone renovation require assessment
- Fence maintenance responsibilities need clarification
- Increased law enforcement and public communication needed
- Vandalism and littering problems
- Trapping and boat use require regulation
- Permittee support essential; proposal to use enclosure for bulls 30-40 days in fall

**Proposed Development Options:**
- Construct upstream dike as sediment trap (concerns: water supply, compliance with original objectives)
- Raise existing dam to increase water backing and riparian area (concerns: dam safety regulations, engineering costs)
- Complete original structure plan
- Reduce water level in existing pond for better fish habitat
- Create series of cienegas downstream using existing reservoir water (concerns: flood stability, potential reservoir drying, water sufficiency)
- Build terraces (small impoundments) upstream to create more cienegas and fish habitat
- Alternative view: area has sufficient development at present

**Critical Issues for Future Development:**
- Water rights investigation needed
- Maintain current diversion dike for Cott Tank
- Water yield documentation and yearly hydrologist monitoring may be required
- Potential impacts to permittee's livestock capacity
- Many desert pupfish occur upstream of impoundment; removing upper fence may affect population
- Development impacts on [[Bald Eagles]] using [[Bog Hole]] and [[San Rafael Valley]] require assessment
- Recommendation to keep existing [[Bog Hole]] deep

**Administrative Actions:**
- Deadline: October 1, 1989 for draft cooperative agreement completion
- Extensive contact list developed including representatives from [[American Fisheries Society]], [[Desert Fishes Council]], [[Arizona Nature Conservancy]], [[Arizona Riparian Council]], [[Ducks Unlimited]], [[SW Arizona Bald Eagle Recovery Team]], and multiple other conservation and sportsmen's organizations
