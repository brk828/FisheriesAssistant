# 1988 Razorback Sucker Monitoring Study: Backwater Rearing, Stocking, and Predation (Contract FG-7494)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1988 contract correspondence and set of progress reports (Progress Reports I through V) for the [[Razorback Sucker]] Monitoring Study, run for the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] under Contract FG-7494. The study tested whether isolated backwaters on the lower [[Colorado River]] could rear juvenile razorback suckers to a larger stocking size. Three barrier-enclosed coves at [[Goose Flats Backwater]] were stocked with hatchery-reared juveniles. Heavy losses followed. The main causes were beaver damage to nets, post-stocking stress mortality, and predation by piscivorous fish and wading birds. Dispersal from the coves after barrier removal was limited, and most survivors were later restocked into the mainstem river.

## Background & Description

The Razorback Sucker is a large, long-lived catostomid native to the Colorado River system. It needs protected nursery habitat for juveniles, which led to a 10-year reintroduction program. The 1988 study's primary objective was to secure and isolate backwater habitats and determine their value for rearing juveniles to a larger stocking size.

The 1988 study had several components:

- **Site selection:** Backwaters were chosen by location and by suitability criteria: coves of 0.10 to 0.40 ha that could be isolated, at least 2 m water depth during reduced releases from [[Parker Dam]], rooted aquatic vegetation for escape cover, organic debris and algae as foraging substrate, and low risk of human interference.
- **Barrier construction:** Block netting was suspended in the water column, anchored with steel posts and sandbags, and marked with "Do Not Disturb" signs.
- **Predator removal:** Trammel and gill nets were used in June and July 1988. Captures of larger predators were low.
- **Stocking:** Juveniles from the [[Nilad Native Fish Ponds]] at the [[Imperial Warmwater Fish Hatchery]] were stocked into the coves on 28 July 1988.
- **Post-study operations:** Fish were moved to the mainstem, and electrofishing and predator sampling followed barrier removal.

The correspondence (dated 15 September 1988, from Dan Langhorst, Blythe, to Susan Ellis, Contract Coordinator) raised concerns about a schedule revision and asked about the Department's short-term plans for the 10-year Razorback Sucker Reintroduction Program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Site availability.** Of the 127-mile study area (Headgate Rock Dam to Imperial Dam), 84 miles (66%) were excluded because they lay within the Colorado River Indian Tribes Reservation or the Cibola and Imperial National Wildlife Refuges. The remaining 43 miles from [[Palo Verde Diversion Dam]] to the northern boundary of [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] were 93% channelized, which further limited suitable backwaters. Candidate backwaters included C-5 (Goose Flats), C-8, and C-10.

**Cove conditions.** The three coves ranged from 0.10 to 0.13 ha (0.25 to 0.33 ac) and 2.0 to 2.5 m deep. [[Myriophyllum]] sp. was common to all sites. Substrate was mostly sand and silt with organic matter and algae on the surface. Razorbacks in the channel and coves were mostly found along margins of, or within, weed beds, which suggests a preference for cover.

**Predator community.** Predators found in and around the coves included [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], common carp, sunfishes, and [[Tilapia]]. Fish-eating birds such as [[Great Blue Heron]] and egrets also foraged along the shoreline. Water releases from upstream impoundments lowered depths in the backwaters to less than 1 m during late October 1988.

## Key Findings

**Stocking and growth**
- Juvenile razorbacks stocked on 28 July 1988 averaged about 111 mm TL and weighed about 118 g. Fish were held in Nilad Pond 9-H before stocking.
- Three seine hauls from Pond 9-H yielded 3,007 juveniles, which were divided among the three coves. The Progress Report III summary cites about 900 stocked per cove.
- Mortality during transport was less than five fish.
- Sampled fish grew from about 111 mm TL at stocking to 146–159 mm TL mean length by late October and early November. Growth was estimated at about 20 mm per month.

**Recovery from coves (late October–early November 1988)**

| Cove | Seine hauls | Razorbacks captured | Mean TL (n = 50) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cove 1 | 14 (late Oct) | 112 | Not summarized in text |
| Cove 2 | 14 (1 Nov) | 394 | 159 mm |
| Cove 3 | 14 (26 Oct) | 290 | 146 mm |

- Cove 1 captures were low. Predators there included three large channel catfish (280–472 mm TL), one large flathead catfish (610 mm TL), and five largemouth bass (all under 400 mm TL). All channel catfish stomachs contained razorbacks, one had three. One of five bass contained a razorback.
- Marking used fin clips: a right pelvic fin clip identified 1988 stock, and cove-specific marks identified origin.

**Barrier removal and dispersal (21 November 1988)**
- One day after barrier removal, electrofishing caught 118 razorbacks. Only 19 (16%) were found outside the coves.
- Most of the 19 outside fish were from Cove 2. The farthest movement was about 1,000 m up the backwater channels.
- One week later, 21 razorbacks were caught and 5 (24%) were outside coves. Movement was greater; two Cove 2 fish had moved about 1,400 m and 1,000 m up the backwater.
- Four weeks later, only one razorback (167 mm TL, Cove 3 mark) was caught.

**Predation (post-barrier removal)**
- Day 1: 9 largemouth bass and 2 channel catfish were examined. One bass contained a razorback (146 mm TL). Both catfish stomachs were empty.
- Week 1: 5 largemouth bass were examined. Two contained razorbacks (150 and 160 mm TL). One catfish stomach was empty.
- Week 4: one channel catfish with an empty stomach.

**Causes of low survival (cove summary)**
1. Beaver damage to net barriers in Coves 2 and 3, which may have allowed mass escapes. Chicken wire was added to protect the nets.
2. Stress mortality after transfer, with dead fish found up to two weeks after stocking.
3. Predation by wading birds.
4. Predation by large piscivorous fish that escaped capture and stayed in Cove 1.
- Rotenone could not be used before stocking, which the report calls "extremely unfortunate."

**Other operations**
- **Blythe Municipal Golf Course Pond** (19–23 September): Trap-net captures were split by size. Fish of 250 mm TL or greater were stocked into Goose Flats, and smaller fish were kept. Counts are unreadable in the source OCR. Fish were marked with a left pelvic fin clip and a white tag. Electrofishing of Goose Flats on 29–30 September (1.5 h) found no razorbacks.
- **Nilad Native Fish Pond #6** (20 October): Two seine hauls yielded about 2,200 fish. The subsample averaged about 187 mm TL (range 124–256 mm). A tetracycline-based feed was expected to permanently mark the fish, but scale checks showed no mark. The discrepancy was attributed to miscommunication between the Blythe biologist and the hatchery manager. Fish were hand-marked with a right pelvic fin clip and released into the unchannelized Colorado River at River Mile 87.
- **Nilad Native Fish Pond #7** (7 December): Three seine hauls were taken. The subsample (n = 50) averaged 255 mm TL (range 203–320 mm). Fish of 200 mm TL or greater were stocked, and smaller fish were held for growout.
- **Mainstem electrofishing** (13–15 December, River Miles 88–62): Seven habitat types were sampled. One stocked razorback (217 mm TL) was recaptured at River Mile 81.5. Flathead catfish dominated the predator catch in dense inundated riparian habitats. None of the 33 predator stomachs examined contained razorbacks. Two flathead catfish each contained a tilapia that exceeded the maximum length of stocked razorbacks.
- **C-10 Backwater** (21 December): Electrofishing found no razorbacks.

**Management implications**
- The authors planned to release surviving razorbacks in mass in several weeks and to dismantle the barriers.
- The study revealed that predator removal before stocking was incomplete, and that the [[Backwater Habitat]] approach was limited by predator presence, net damage, and cove isolation.
- The contract was scheduled to terminate on 1 March 1992, and the correspondence requested the Department's long-term plans for the reintroduction program.
