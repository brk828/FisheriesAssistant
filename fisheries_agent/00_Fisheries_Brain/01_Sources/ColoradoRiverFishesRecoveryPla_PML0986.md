# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Plan Update and Format Guidelines

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines a cover letter and a set of US Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) guidelines for preparing and updating recovery plans for endangered and threatened (E/T) species. The letter asks [[Dr. H. L. Minckley]] at Arizona State University to review attached bibliographies of publications on the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] (published and unpublished reports since 1980), plus a list of references on the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The guidelines define the required structure of a recovery plan (Parts I–III and an appendix), the rules for setting recovery tasks and priorities, and the content of the Implementation Schedule.

## Background & Description

- **Sender:** John Hamill, Colorado/Platte River Endangered Species Recovery Coordinator, FWS (Region 6 correspondence, "FWE/SE/Recovery").
- **Recipient:** Dr. H. L. Minckley, Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ.
- **Purpose of letter:** FWS is updating recovery plans for three rare Colorado River fishes. Draft updates produced in 1983 were placed on hold pending the outcome of the [[Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee]] effort. Much new information has since become available, especially for the Colorado Squawfish and Humpback Chub.
- **Requests:** Identify needed changes, additions, or deletions to the bibliographies; supply page numbers and report numbers; send copies of new reports marked on the list. The reviewed results will go to the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] before the planned March–April update meeting (the year is partly illegible in the scan). FWS also aims to keep a current library on Razorback Sucker publications.
- **Recovery plan structure (guidelines):**
  - **Part I, Introduction:** habitat requirements, limiting factors, distribution, ecological and biological status, commercial significance, conservation efforts, and threats.
  - **Part II, Recovery:** objectives, a stepdown outline, a narrative, and literature cited.
  - **Part III, Implementation Schedule:** tasks, agency assignments, schedules, priorities, and funding.
  - **Appendix:** reviewer list and documentary material.
- **Front matter:** Title page, disclaimer (mandatory on drafts and approved plans), optional acknowledgements and executive summary, and table of contents.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The guidelines ask that plans analyze the following adverse influences:

- **Physical:** natural disasters, human activities, and pollution or other contaminants.
- **Biological:** competing species (including other E/T species), hybridization, loss of food and habitat, predation, and disease.
- **Other:** economic losses (for example, from depredation control), political and legal issues, and over-utilization (overfishing, hunting, or trapping).

Strategies to address these influences include habitat identification and protection, regulation and enforcement against take, transplants, captive propagation, control of competing species, research on management practices, monitoring, and public education.

Habitat protection is a recurring concern. Essential habitat should be identified and, where known, specific locations and sizes should be listed. Protection strategies such as fee purchase or easements are not to be named in the plan itself; they follow Department of Interior policy (Federal Register, May 7, 1982). Plans may recommend experimental populations but must not state whether they are essential or nonessential, a determination made by regulation.

## Key Findings

**Scope of the review**
- Updated plans for the Colorado Squawfish, Humpback Chub, and Bonytail Chub are pending, with new data available mainly for the first two species.
- The request covers a bibliography of reports from 1980 onward (count not stated) and a separate Razorback Sucker reference list.

**Recovery plan requirements**
- The recovery objective must be quantifiable and aimed at delisting, reclassification (endangered to threatened), or an interim goal. Interim objectives must be reassessed at a stated point. Objectives should be reviewed at least annually.
- The stepdown outline (objectives, sub-objectives, tasks) must match the Introduction, and each task must be specific enough to assign to an agency or program.
- Recovery plans should be updated as often as needed; the plan must be reviewed at least annually.

**Priority system**
- **Priority 1:** action needed to prevent extinction or irreversible decline in the foreseeable future (example: providing the Socorro isopod a permanent flow of water).
- **Priority 2:** action needed to prevent significant decline in population, habitat, or other negative impact short of extinction (example: Plymouth red-bellied turtle law enforcement).
- **Priority 3:** all other actions needed for full recovery (example: establishing new peregrine falcon nest sites).
- The guidelines warn that misusing priorities reduces the credibility of the ranking system.

**Implementation Schedule**
- It is the working core of the plan and drives funding and task tracking.
- It must cover a three-year planning horizon at minimum, with cost estimates as realistic as possible and manpower estimates in the Comments column when known.
- Priority 1 and 2 tasks are listed first, and tasks funded in the next three years come before later ones.
- Coordination with FWS law enforcement is required during drafting when enforcement is needed.
- Section 7 consultations, permits, and funding proposals are judged against the schedule: actions that contribute to a scheduled task are expedited and more likely to be approved.

**Process**
- Recovery tasks are drafted exclusively by the FWS and its designated agencies, and the Regional Director has discretion over acknowledgements and executive summaries.
- Reviewer comments are kept in the administrative record, and substantive comments go in the appendix.
