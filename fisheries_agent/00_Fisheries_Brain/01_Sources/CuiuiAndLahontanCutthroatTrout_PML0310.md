# Cui-ui and Lahontan Cutthroat Trout Spawning Runs at Marble Bluff Fish Facility, 1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared by [[Chester C. Buchanan]] and [[Howard L. Burge]] (March 1988) for the Great Basin Complex, Reno, Nevada, documents the 1987 spawning runs of endangered [[Cui-ui]] and threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], Nixon, Nevada. It also covers releases from [[Stampede Reservoir]], cui-ui larval production, tagging, and facility mortality. The cui-ui run was about 45 percent of the average run since 1980, which the authors attribute mainly to low attraction flows and restricted passage over the [[Truckee River Delta]]. Storage releases were used to sustain attraction and spawning flows during a drought year, and larval production was far below prediction, likely because of warm incubation temperatures.

## Background & Description

The report supplements earlier work by [[Scoppettone et al. 1986]] and [[Coleman et al. 1987]], and meets reporting requirements under a Take Permit (PRT-70263).

- **[[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*):** A large catostomid endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]]. Average male length in the river trap was 529 mm FL and average female 577 mm FL. Adults were defined as >485 mm FL.
- **[[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*):** Ran into the facility alongside cui-ui. Average female length was 508 mm FL and average male 598 mm FL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Migration drivers:** River access and freshwater inflow were the principal factors affecting the magnitude and timing of cui-ui runs, with water temperature a secondary influence. Freshwater concentration in the delta may accelerate maturation.
- **Attraction flows:** Attraction volume for January to April 1987 was 60,000 acre-feet, within the estimated minimum range of 52,000 to 87,000 acre-feet. Water depth over the delta was about 1.5 feet shallower than required for unobstructed passage. A curvilinear relation between attraction flow and run size was reported.
- **Drought conditions:** Snowmelt runoff was forecast at 45 percent of normal. Pyramid Lake stood at 3814.7 feet above msl, high enough to allow restricted access over the delta.
- **Predation:** Many migrants had bird-inflicted wounds, and deeper water was expected to reduce bird predation.
- **Temperature and survival:** Maximum incubation temperature reached 20°C, well above the 17°C upper limit for suitable incubation identified in a parallel study by the [[Seattle National Fishery Research Center]].
- **Population structure:** The 1969 year class remained dominant. Tag data suggest the adult population is probably below 100,000 individuals.

## Key Findings

### Cui-ui Spawning Run
- The run began in mid-April and continued without a clear peak until mid-May. 5,301 individuals were caught in the river trap, with some released above the dam and others transferred to the [[David Koch Cui-ui Hatchery]].
- The sex ratio was 1 male : 1.91 females overall. Adults had a ratio of 1.00:2.10, and young fish (<485 mm FL) had 1.00:0.48.
- Length-frequency data suggested similar year-class abundance to 1986.

### Stampede Reservoir Storage Releases
- Releases were used to support attraction, incubation, and emigration flows. The target was 102,000 to 162,000 acre-feet, and releases began February 19.
- The total release was 85,470 acre-feet, leaving 92,992 acre-feet in storage. Most spawners entered in late April, which allowed early cessation of releases.
- The authors judged that much of the cui-ui spawning success since 1980 depended on these releases.

### Cui-ui Larvae Survey
- Larval production was estimated at 3.4 million, about 1.4 percent of eggs spawned. The predicted figure was 21 million (8.5 percent). Cooler conditions would have yielded about 32 million larvae (1.4 percent under cool-water assumptions).
- Emigration began about 20 days after the first adult passed the facility and was nearly complete by the last survey day.
- Sampling was reduced because of staffing cuts.

### Tagging and Spawning Frequency
- About 2 percent of the run carried tags. Among tagged fish, 56 percent of males and 44 percent of females had also entered the run in the previous year.
- Results suggest adults return to spawn more often than previously thought, and males more often than females.

### Cutthroat Trout Run and Mortality
- The cutthroat trout run was about one quarter the size of 1986. After correcting for a 33 percent re-entry rate, an estimated 371 trout were passed upstream.
- Mortality was very low: 16 cui-ui (0.3 percent) and one cutthroat trout (0.2 percent) died accidentally. Five cui-ui and 19 cutthroat trout were sacrificed for scientific purposes.
- The authors attributed the low mortality mainly to the small daily runs.

### Facility and Safety Improvements
- New crowder lips, a table/chute, a river trap release mechanism, and a walkway improved fish passage and personnel safety.
- Eggs were not collected from cutthroat trout because nearly all were green and warm water made egg viability doubtful.

### Tribal Contribution
- Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe members took 503 adult cui-ui for spawning at the David Koch Cui-ui Hatchery. Carcasses suitable for consumption went to the tribe's Senior Citizens Program.
