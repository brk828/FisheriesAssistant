# Lake Mohave Sampling Proposal for Bonytail and Razorback Sucker (1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum, dated 28 December 1989 and issued by [[Arizona State University]] researchers Paul C. Marsh and W.L. Minckley, proposes an intensive, cooperative field effort at [[Lake Mohave]] on the Arizona-Nevada border during 18-25 March 1990. It continues sampling conducted in March 1988 and March 1989. The stated goals are to assess the status of the [[Bonytail]] population and to recapture [[Razorback Sucker]] tagged in earlier years while tagging additional fish, so that current population estimates can be refined. Primary methods are [[Trammel Netting]] and [[Electrofishing]]. Cooperating agencies were asked to confirm participation by 5 February 1990.

## Background & Description

The document is a planning circular rather than a research report, so it contains no morphological, life-history or specimen data. It identifies the two focal species, both of which are native to the Colorado River system. The bonytail is placed in the family Cyprinidae and the razorback sucker in the family Catostomidae. Both are the subject of conservation concern, which is why the proposal emphasises population monitoring and tagging.

The memo is addressed to an extensive mailing list of roughly 60 named recipients (summarised here, not reproduced). Recipients include staff from:

- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] offices in Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado, Utah and Washington-area regional offices, including the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]
- [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Phoenix, Yuma, Kingman, Flagstaff and Pinetop offices)
- [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]]
- [[California Department of Fish and Game]]
- [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]
- [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[U.S. Bureau of Land Management]]
- [[National Park Service]] (Lake Mead National Recreation Area)
- University of Arizona Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Lake Mohave is a reservoir on the lower Colorado River. The proposal does not report water chemistry, temperature, habitat structure, invertebrate prey or other environmental measurements. The ecological relevance lies in the monitoring of two long-lived large-bodied fishes whose populations depend on reservoir conditions and on maintenance of tagged-cohort records over multiple years. The proposal's methods, net-based and electrofishing capture, are the standard means of sampling these species in such habitats.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling window:** 8 days, 18-25 March 1990 (Sunday to Sunday), following identical efforts in March 1988 and March 1989.
- **Objective 1:** Assess the status of the bonytail population.
- **Objective 2:** Recapture razorback suckers tagged in previous years and tag additional individuals to refine population estimates.
- **Additional work:** Other sub-projects proposed by other investigators may be undertaken during the same window.
- **Capture methods:** Trammel netting combined with electrofishing.
- **Planned resources (lead team):** Two net boats, one electrofishing boat, the existing trammel net inventory, two crews and support for one base camp.
- **Tagging:** Colored, sequentially numbered floy tags supplied to all participants.
- **Communications:** Radio equipment expected from the Bureau of Land Management, Kingman, to improve field coordination.
- **Cooperator request:** Matching manpower and equipment as available, plus notification of intent to participate by 5 February 1990.
- **Methods reference:** Detailed methods were given in previous years' documents and are not repeated.
