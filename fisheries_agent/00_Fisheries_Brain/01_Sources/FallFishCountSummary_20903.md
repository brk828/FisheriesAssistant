# Fall Fish Count Summary 1988-1994

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department technical report summarizes seven years (1988-1994) of volunteer-collected fish survey data from the [[Fall Fish Count]] (FFC) program, which has accumulated records from more than 140 sample sites. The report tests whether the data can reveal abundance trends for native and nonnative stream fishes, with particular attention to [[Federally Listed Target Species]] funded under [[Section 6 Funding]]. The authors conclude that trend detection is limited by inconsistent effort, gear, and site continuity, but that point-in-time occurrence data remain valuable enough to justify continuing the program with improved protocols.

## Background & Description

The Fall Fish Count was started in 1988 to use volunteers to build long-term records of fish communities in [[Arizona Streams]]. From 1992 the program received Section 6 support from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to monitor ten target species, including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Sonora Chub]], and [[Mexican Stoneroller]].

Sampling used hoopnets, gillnets, dipnets, minnow traps, electrofishers, and several seine types. Site location, participants, date, macrohabitat, gear, effort, and net dimensions were recorded, along with mean depth, mean width, site length, and stream flow. Length and weight were optional. Voucher specimens were deposited in the [[Arizona State University Vertebrate Museum]]. Data were entered into a Fall Fish Count database.

Analysis compared catch per unit effort (CPUE) and relative abundance of native and nonnative species within and among sites, separated by gear type and species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Volunteer effort:** Participants numbered 61 (1988), 80 (1989), 62 (1990), 49 (1991), 34 known participants (1992), 42 (1993), and 57 (1994), supported by school and volunteer groups.
- **Target species occurrence:** Target species were found at 46 sites in 1988, 41 in 1989, 28 in 1990, 18 in 1991, 15 in 1992, and 15 in 1993. 1994 totals are unavailable due to delinquent reporting. Colorado squawfish were never recorded in FFC surveys, and no new target-species localities were found.
- **Survey effort:** Sites visited declined from 57 (1988) to 41 (1992), then rose to 46 in 1993. Data from 33 sites were analyzed for 1994, with about 20 more surveyed but not reported by the report date.
- **Flow event:** Record high flows in January and February 1993 across Gila and Verde drainages may have favored natives over nonnatives, since many nonnatives are thought to be less flood-tolerant ([[Meffe 1984]]; [[Minckley and Meffe 1987]]).
- **Water quality incident:** At [[Oak Creek, Red Rock State Park]], a reported sewage spill and chlorine treatment upstream in 1991, with high flows, may have affected fish populations.

## Key Findings

**Data suitability**
- Of 141 FFC sites, 53 contain at least three years of data; 49 had three consecutive years and 30 were sampled in 1994.
- Only 16 of the 30 recently sampled sites with three consecutive samples had sufficient information to compute CPUE.
- Sites where only dipnets were used were excluded because dipnets yield presence/absence data only.
- Four years or fewer of inconsistent data are, in the authors' view, of questionable value for trend assessment.

**Site-level patterns (selected sites)**
- **Gila River near Box-O Wash (FC0040):** Native proportion showed a steady increase, driven by [[Longfin Dace]], Sonora sucker, and desert sucker, with a 1994 seining decline.
- **Gila River, Cochran Crossing (FC0050):** Nonnatives dominated; the overall trend appeared downward for natives. One spikedace was collected in 1991.
- **Gila River near Riverside (FC0070):** Natives increased and nonnatives decreased in relative abundance.
- **Sycamore Creek (FC0110):** Native CPUE increased each year; speckled dace first appeared in 1993.
- **Agua Fria River (FC0111):** Catches were dominated by natives, with an overall increasing native trend.
- **Santa Cruz River (FC0710):** Native CPUE increased dramatically, mainly from longfin dace; Sonora sucker first appeared in 1993.
- **Tonto Creek (FC2510):** Native relative abundance showed a decreasing trend; roundtail chub had not been collected since 1988.
- **Oak Creek (FC3610):** No natives were collected in 1994; nonnative smallmouth bass and green sunfish CPUE increased over time.
- **San Pedro River sites (FC4309, FC4312, FC4314, FC4315):** Natives dominated several collections (100% of the 1993 catch at Dudleyville Crossing). The Cascabel site at Hughes Ranch showed declining native catches after 1992.

**Species-level observations**
- Longfin dace drove most native increases across sites.
- Sonora and desert suckers were first recorded at eight sites in 1993 or 1994.
- Declines in nonnatives (mosquitofish, fathead minnow, red shiner, green sunfish) occurred at six sites in 1993.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- The authors judge the program's main strength to be point-in-time occurrence and relative abundance data, useful for annual species documentation and management decisions. They recommend continuing the project.
- Recommended improvements:
  1. **Sample continuity:** Resurvey sites with two or more years of historical data, prioritized over new sites.
  2. **Consistent effort:** Use previously employed gear, with consistent effort within similar habitats and minimum effort standards per site.
  3. **Data recording:** Record catch and effort separately for each gear type (e.g., shocking seconds, meters seined).
  4. **Data reporting:** Submit all volunteer data by February 1 each year.
- Trend analysis requires at least three years of data per site, similar gear, and the same area and habitats sampled each year.

**Appendices**
