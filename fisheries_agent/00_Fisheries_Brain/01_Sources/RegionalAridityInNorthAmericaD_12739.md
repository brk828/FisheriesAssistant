# Regional Aridity in North America During the Middle Holocene

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoclimate

## Executive Summary

This paleoclimate synthesis by Dean, Ahlbrandt, Anderson and Bradbury (*The Holocene* 6, 1996) reviews evidence for increased aridity and wind-driven dune activity across the Great Plains and Rocky Mountain region during the mid-Holocene (c. 8000 to 4000 yr BP). The central record is a continuously varved sequence from [[Elk Lake]], northwestern Minnesota, where aeolian clastic influx rose from about 8 ka, peaked near 6 ka, and ended about 3.8 ka. The authors propose that a strengthened westerly zonal wind field, possibly driven by solar-geomagnetic disturbances, linked these regional dune and lake records. The document contains no fish, aquatic invertebrate or species-level biological data; its "aquatic" content concerns lake sediments, ostracodes and diatoms used as palaeoenvironmental proxies.

## Background & Description

- **Altithermal concept:** [[Ernst Antevs]] established the mid-Holocene "Altithermal" warm, dry interval, which the authors note was spatially and temporally variable. Some researchers have advocated dropping the term.
- **Chronology:** Radiocarbon ages (yr BP) are distinguished from varve ages (ka, referenced to AD 1927) and calibrated calendar ages (cal. yr BP). Varve chronology from Elk Lake, built on [[Varve Chronology]] methods, provides the high-resolution time framework.
- **Study scope:** The paper integrates [[Pollen Analysis]], lake-sediment proxies, [[Optically Stimulated Luminescence]] dune dating, and climate-modelling and solar-geomagnetic evidence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Vegetation and climate (pollen evidence):**
- Prairie expanded eastward in the upper Midwest, with maximum expansion between 7800 and 6800 cal. yr BP.
- Varve-calibrated pollen reconstructions for Elk Lake estimated the period 7.5–4.5 ka was about 100 mm drier in annual precipitation and about 2 °C warmer in July temperature than today.
- Ostracode evidence suggests temperatures may have remained cooler than present until about 6.8 ka.

**Lake-level and hydrochemical evidence:**
- Lowest lake levels and most alkaline conditions at Elk Lake occurred between 7.2 and 6.8 ka.
- Ostracode *Limnocythere staplini* marks a low-level, alkaline stand near 7200 yr BP, possibly biased old by hardwater carbonate effects.
- Pickerel Lake (South Dakota) shows low mid-Holocene water levels, but only broadly dated (8000–4000 yr BP).

**Aeolian indicators at Elk Lake:**
- Sodium, aluminium, quartz and plagioclase peaked near 7.0 ka and again near 5.8 ka, with a 1.2 ky high interval between.
- Wind-borne clastic influx ceased within a decade at 5.4 ka, followed by a roughly 600-year calm phase; winds returned by about 4.8 ka.
- Varve thickness and magnetic susceptibility were used as proxies for detrital magnetite and aeolian influx.

**Dune fields:**
- The Lake Winnibigoshish dune field (580 km²) was active about 8000–5000 yr BP under northwesterly winds.
- The Anoka Sand Plain (2200 km²) began dune development about 8000 yr BP and stabilised by about 4000 yr BP.
- [[Nebraska Sand Hills]] and Rocky Mountain basin dunes show four Holocene phases of dune activity, with the main mid-Holocene phase c. 7000–5000 yr BP.

## Key Findings

1. **Elk Lake aeolian record:** Aeolian activity began about 8 ka, peaked around 6 ka, and ended about 3.8 ka, with a diminished period from 5.4 to 4.8 ka.
2. **Wind direction:** Palaeowind studies show the dominant direction during dune activity was from the northwest across Minnesota, the Great Plains and Rocky Mountains, with southwesterly winds in the North Park and Great Sand Dunes fields of Colorado.
3. **Dune timing:** Optically stimulated luminescence dating constrained Ferris dune migration to 8.8–8.1 ka and 4.3–4.0 ka, and Nebraska Sand Hills activity to 8–6 ka and 3–2 ka.
4. **Recent activity:** Significant dune movement occurred within the last 1000 years, and the authors note high potential for dune reactivation in the Great Plains under greenhouse warming.
5. **Oscillation periodicities:** Spectral analysis of Elk Lake varve thickness shows strongest cycles near 200, 40–50 and 20–25 years; the 200-year cycle is also present in ¹⁴C production.
6. **Solar-geomagnetic hypothesis:** The authors observe an inverse correlation between ¹⁴C-production 200-year cycles and Elk Lake varve thickness from 7300 to 5300 cal. yr BP, and suggest increased westerly zonal winds from solar-geomagnetic disturbances may have driven mid-Holocene aridity. This is a proposed mechanism, not a demonstrated causal link.
7. **Forcing context:** Milankovitch insolation peaked near 9000 cal. yr BP, lagging the aeolian maximum by about 3000 years; volcanic aerosols and solar irradiance are considered alternative or contributing controls.
8. **Synthesis:** The mid-Holocene was a dynamic period with rapid transitions between dry and moist intervals, and the Elk Lake site lies near a sensitive forest-prairie boundary and a "climatic triple junction" of airstreams.
