## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Section 7 consultation concurrence letter addressing effects determinations for three land management projects on the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest: the **East Eagle Addition Prescribed Burn**, the **Tutt Creek Trailhead Access Road** relocation, and the **Badger Knoll Fire Hazard Reduction** project. While the primary regulatory focus spans multiple listed species (Mexican Spotted Owl, peregrine falcon, bald eagle, jaguar, Mexican gray wolf), the document contains substantive aquatic ecology data concerning two federally listed fish species, [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and references [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) in the context of contested critical habitat designations. The letter evaluates sediment transport, watershed hydrology, and erosion mitigation as primary mechanisms of potential effect on fish habitat in the [[Eagle Creek Watershed]] and [[Blue River]] systems.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide direct morphological descriptions of the fish species discussed. However, ecological/physiological requirements are noted:

- **Loach Minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): Explicitly noted as requiring **perennial water flow** for its distribution — the species' upstream range in Eagle Creek is limited specifically because the stream does not achieve perennial flow until ~10 miles downstream of the East Eagle Addition project area.
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*): No morphological data provided; referenced only in context of critical habitat status.
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): No morphological data provided; referenced only regarding effects determination concurrence for the Tutt Creek project.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Associations:**
- Loach minnow inhabits perennial reaches of desert streams within the [[Gila River Basin]], specifically documented in **Eagle Creek** (~12 miles downstream of the East Eagle Addition burn) and the **Blue River** (~0.25 miles downstream of the Tutt Creek Trailhead project).
- Distribution is constrained by **hydrologic perenniality** — intermittent/ephemeral upper reaches (e.g., East Eagle Creek within the project boundary) are unsuitable despite proximity to occupied perennial habitat downstream.
- Associated riparian vegetation in these watersheds includes cottonwood, willow, locust, bigtooth maple, alder, and quaking aspen, with adjacent uplands of ponderosa pine, mixed conifer, and pinyon-juniper.

**Watershed Context:**
- The **Eagle Creek Watershed** spans approximately 500,000 acres (jointly USFS/San Carlos Apache Reservation). The East Eagle Addition project area (900 acres) represents <0.2% of this watershed; cumulative multi-year burn impacts (East Eagle Prescribed Burn, Hot Air Prescribed Burn) were estimated at ~600 acres total (~0.1% of watershed) in concurrent burn years.
- The **Blue River** watershed near Tutt Creek is affected by legacy impacts from road construction/maintenance, off-road vehicle use, historic grazing, and fire exclusion, contributing to chronic sedimentation.

**Threats/Stressors Identified:**
- Sediment delivery from unstable clay-soil roads crossing numerous small drainages (Tutt Creek).
- Historic livestock grazing and fire exclusion cited as major drivers of watershed change from prehistoric baseline conditions in both project areas.
- Ash, toxic materials, and sediment mobilization from prescribed burning identified as a potential (but mitigated) pathway of effect to downstream fish habitat.

**Regulatory/Critical Habitat Status:**
- Critical habitat designations for [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], **Spikedace**, and **Loach Minnow** were under injunction by the U.S. District Court for New Mexico (*Coalition of Arizona-New Mexico Counties for Stable Economic Growth v. USFWS*, No. 95-1285-M Civil, D.N.M., filed March 4, 1997) at the time of this letter, suspending Section 7 consultation/conferencing requirements specifically for critical habitat (species-level "may affect" analyses still applied).

## Key Findings

- **East Eagle Addition Prescribed Burn**: USFWS concurred with the Forest Service's determination of "may affect, not likely to adversely affect" the loach minnow. Key mitigating factors included: a minimum quarter-mile riparian buffer along East Eagle drainage, cool-season low-intensity burn design, discontinuous fuel/herbaceous cover limiting fire spread, and the substantial downstream distance (12 miles, with 10 miles being non-perennial) to occupied loach minnow habitat.
- **Tutt Creek Trailhead Access Road**: USFWS concurred with "not likely to adversely affect" determinations for both loach minnow and razorback sucker. The project was assessed as a **net long-term benefit** to loach minnow habitat: relocating the road away from drainage crossings, gravel surfacing, culvert installation with sediment traps, and closure/reseeding of the old eroding road were expected to reduce chronic sediment delivery to an unnamed tributary of the Blue River, despite short-term construction-related erosion risk.
- **Cross-watershed cumulative impact analysis** was used as a key line of evidence for the "not likely to adversely affect" findings, contextualizing project-level burn acreage against total watershed acreage (<0.2% for single project; ~0.1% cumulative).
- No direct fish surveys, population counts, or morphometric data are presented in this document; conclusions rely on distributional records (known occurrence points), habitat suitability inference (perenniality of flow), and sediment/hydrology risk modeling.
- The consultation underscores an established management principle: erosion and sediment control (road relocation, buffers, closures) are treated as the dominant mechanism of effect for lotic-obligate imperiled fishes such as [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in these Southwestern watersheds, more so than direct fire effects.
