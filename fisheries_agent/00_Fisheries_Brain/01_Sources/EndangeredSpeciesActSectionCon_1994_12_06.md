## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum responding to a Bureau of Reclamation Biological Assessment (BE) on the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water delivery operations within the [[Santa Cruz River Basin]]. The Bureau's BE concluded "no effect" on listed aquatic species from CAP water deliveries; the FWS explicitly disputes this conclusion. The Service finds that construction, operation, and interrelated/interdependent water delivery actions of the CAP would likely **adversely affect** the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]], along with two non-fish taxa (lesser long-nosed bat, Pima pineapple cactus). Conversely, due to protective measures already implemented under an earlier (April 20, 1994) biological opinion covering the broader [[Gila River Basin]], the Service concurs that CAP operations in the Santa Cruz Basin will **not** affect [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], or [[Colorado Squawfish]]. The memo is primarily a regulatory/administrative document requesting expanded project scope and additional hydrological and habitat data rather than a primary field or morphological study.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source document. The text is a regulatory consultation memorandum and does not describe body form, coloration, fin counts, or other taxonomic descriptors for any of the listed species. Species are referenced only by common and scientific (binomial) names for regulatory identification purposes:

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The [[Santa Cruz River]] and its major tributaries exhibit a mosaic of hydrological regimes relevant to species persistence:
  - Perennial (year-round) flow reaches, including effluent-dependent segments.
  - Intermittent reaches with seasonal flow (as opposed to storm-driven flash flow only).
  - Isolated sumps and ponded floodplain areas retaining water year-round or seasonally — potentially critical refugia for native fish and amphibians.
- **Habitat connectivity concern**: The Service specifically challenges the Bureau's conclusion regarding the "inability of non-native species to move through areas of the Santa Cruz River and its tributaries," indicating that CAP-related water deliveries and recharge projects could facilitate **invasive/non-native species dispersal**, a known threat to small-bodied native fishes like Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish.
- **Basin-scale management**: Protective measures already implemented for the [[Gila River Basin]] population complex under a prior biological opinion (April 20, 1994) were deemed sufficient to avoid adverse effects on [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]], illustrating how upstream basin-wide conservation actions can mitigate downstream/adjacent basin risk.
- **Co-occurring at-risk taxa**: The aquatic/riparian ecosystem in question also supports category 1 candidate species — [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] (*Rana chiricahuensis*) and [[Sonora Tiger Salamander]] (*Ambystoma tigrinum stebbinsi*) — plus the [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]] (*Leptonycteris curasoae yerbabuenae*) and [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] (*Coryphantha scheeri var. robustispina*), reflecting a multi-taxa desert riverine ecosystem under water-management pressure.
- **Delisted taxon**: The [[Tumamoc Globeberry]] (*Tumamoca macdougalii*) had been removed from the endangered species list, exempting it from further Section 7 consultation.

## Key Findings

1. **No-effect finding rejected**: The FWS could not concur with the Bureau of Reclamation's determination of "no effect" on listed aquatic species (Gila Topminnow, Desert Pupfish) from CAP water deliveries in the Santa Cruz River Basin.
2. **Likely adverse effects identified**: Construction, operation, and interrelated/interdependent actions of the CAP are anticipated to adversely affect:
   - [[Gila Topminnow]] (endangered)
   - [[Desert Pupfish]] (endangered)
   - [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]] (endangered)
   - [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] (endangered)
   - Possibly [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] and [[Sonora Tiger Salamander]] (category 1 candidates at the time)
3. **Concurrence on no-effect for four other listed fishes**: Due to existing protective measures from a prior basin-wide biological opinion, the Service agreed that CAP operations in the Santa Cruz Basin would not affect [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]].
4. **Data insufficiency**: The Biological Assessment lacked sufficient detail (maps of aqueduct routes, turnout locations, water-use descriptions, recharge project specifications, reservoir management plans) to adequately assess project effects, prompting a formal request for expanded information.
5. **Scope expansion recommended**: The Service recommended consolidating consultation to cover all CAP components (construction, operation, water deliveries, and related subprojects like the Tucson Aqueduct System Reliability Investigations) under a single, ecosystem-based Section 7 consultation rather than piecemeal review.
6. **Non-native species pathway concern**: A central unresolved scientific/management question is whether CAP infrastructure (canals, recharge projects, reservoirs) could enable non-native fish species to invade or move through Santa Cruz River habitats, threatening native endangered fish populations.
7. **Procedural outcome**: The Service requested a 30-day extension to complete a biological opinion, at minimum for effects on the Pima Pineapple Cactus, pending clarification of which species the Bureau wished to include in the formal consultation request.
