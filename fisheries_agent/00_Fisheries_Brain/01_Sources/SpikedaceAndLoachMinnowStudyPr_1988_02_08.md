## Executive Summary

This document is a **budget continuation proposal**, not a primary data report, submitted by Arizona State University researchers **W.L. Minckley** (Principal Investigator) and **P.C. Marsh** (Co-Investigator) to the Arizona Game and Fish Department. It requests continued funding for a second year of research into the decline of the native **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) and **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*), with specific emphasis on the ecological displacement of spikedace by the non-native **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). The proposal outlines two possible research directions — continued microhabitat sampling during the breeding season, and experimental field manipulations testing species interactions — and requests $13,856 in additional funds after applying a projected $13,000 carryover from the first contract year.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Native, small-bodied cyprinid fishes of southwestern U.S. desert streams, specifically the Gila River basin.
- **Focal native species:**
  - **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) — a small riffle-dwelling minnow endemic to the Gila River basin, now considered imperiled due to habitat alteration and non-native fish introductions.
  - **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — a benthic, riffle-adapted minnow co-occurring with spikedace in the same drainage, similarly imperiled.
- **Non-native species of concern:**
  - **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) — an introduced cyprinid implicated in the competitive displacement of spikedace from native habitats in Arizona streams.
- **Study site:** **[[Sycamore Creek, Arizona]]**, selected as the field site for planned experimental manipulations examining spikedace–red shiner interactions. Permitting was in process with the **U.S. Forest Service** at time of writing.
- **Contract context:** Continuation of AGFD Contract No. G-70001, "Spikedace and Loach Minnow Study," with the current contract period ending 20 March 1988.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The research addresses a central conservation question in **[[Southwestern U.S. Desert Fish Ecology]]**: the mechanism(s) by which the introduced **[[Red Shiner]]** displaces native **[[Spikedace]]** populations from historically occupied riffle/run microhabitats.
- Two complementary research approaches were proposed to address this:
  1. **Breeding-season microhabitat sampling (March–June):** Intensive field data collection on fish-microhabitat associations during the spikedace/loach minnow breeding period, to compare against previously collected non-breeding season data. Researchers anticipated substantial differences in habitat use between breeding and non-breeding fish.
  2. **Field experimental manipulations:** Controlled field experiments at Sycamore Creek to directly test interaction mechanisms between spikedace and red shiner (e.g., competitive exclusion, microhabitat displacement), building on manipulative work already partially initiated (equipment purchased, site selected, permitting underway).
- The proposal explicitly frames red-shiner-driven displacement as a **priority conservation research question**, stating that experimental interaction data are "critical to delineating and understanding possible mechanism(s) involved in displacement... of the native species."
