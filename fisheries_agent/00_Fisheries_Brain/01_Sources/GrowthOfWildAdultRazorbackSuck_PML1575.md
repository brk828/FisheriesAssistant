# Growth of Wild Adult Razorback Suckers in Lake Mohave, Arizona-Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report, submitted by [[Arizona State University]] to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] in June 1998, analyzes capture-recapture data for wild adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. Using 1,141 usable capture-recapture pairs (CRPs) from capture years 1987-1997, the authors found that average monthly growth in total length was not statistically different from zero for either sex. Measurement and recording errors were larger than the growth rates calculated from long-term data, so the authors concluded that long-term growth was too small to be measured accurately.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is a [[Catostomidae]] fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Tagging of wild adults in Lake Mohave began before 1980 with [[Floy Tag]]s, followed by [[Carlin Tag]]s in 1982. These early programs yielded little usable information. A more systematic effort began in 1987, when state, federal, university and other personnel made annual collecting trips around the lake perimeter. Floy tags proved unsuitable for long-term use and were replaced in 1991 by [[Pit Tag]]s (Passive Integrated Transponder), which improved recapture reliability.

A comprehensive database of all wild adult capture records is maintained at Arizona State University in cooperation with the [[Native Fish Work Group]]. It records collector, date, location, gear, sex, length, weight, health and tag identification. The report summarizes recapture data and compares average incremental growth in length over the past decade.

**Methods**

- **Capture:** Fish were collected primarily in March, May and November using [[Trammel Net]]s and boat-mounted [[Electrofishing]]. Each fish was checked for health, reproductive status and external tags, and scanned for an existing PIT tag. Untagged fish received a PIT tag injected into the abdominal cavity. Total length (TL) was recorded to the nearest mm, and some fish were weighed.
- **Data cleaning:** Pairs with missing TL data were removed. Pairs with mismatched sex were kept only when the true sex could be confirmed from field records. When a fish had several recaptures, only the most recent was kept. Recaptures within the same sampling period were excluded.
- **Seasons:** Each date was assigned to a [[Reproductive Season]] (November 16-March 15), a [[Post-Reproductive Season]] (March 16-May 15) or a [[Growing Season]] (May 16-November 15).
- **Calculations:** For each CRP, the authors calculated change in TL (ΔTL), days at large (DAL), standard months at large (SMAL, where one standard month = DAL/30.42) and monthly growth (G = ΔTL/SMAL). Pairs with fewer than 30 DAL were analyzed separately. Means and standard deviations were computed by sex, year and season. Growth was analyzed using [[Capture-Recapture]] methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study is limited to one reservoir. It does not report water chemistry, habitat conditions, food resources, or community composition. Growth patterns were interpreted against the known reproductive biology of the species in Lake Mohave, citing [[Minckley]] (1983) and [[Minckley et al. 1991]]. Seasonal breaks were chosen to match that biology and to account for variable growth across the year.

The authors also noted that sampling was uneven. Females were about half as common as males in every season (a male-to-female ratio of roughly 2:1), and female presence varied more strongly among years. This sex imbalance and the sporadic sampling limit how far the results can be generalized to the wider population.

## Key Findings

**Data set**

- 79 CRPs had incomplete data. These included 6 recaptures of Carlin-tagged fish (1988-1993) where only wire remained, 8 Floy-tagged fish with unreadable identification, 10 Floy and 8 PIT recaptures with no traceable original capture, and 22 females and 25 males with missing TLs.
- 138 CRPs had uncertain sex. Of these, 39 were confirmed female and 28 male. The remaining CRPs could not be sexed and were removed.
- After removing 150 CRPs (79 incomplete, 71 mismatched), **1,141 usable CRPs** remained: **365 female** and **776 male**.
- Neither sex had CRP data for 1987 or 1988. Both sexes had consistent CRP data from 1991 onward.

**Range of measurements**

- Change in TL: -195 to +140 mm (females); -98 to +138 mm (males).
- Days at large: 37-2,516 d (females); 34-2,563 d (males).
- Monthly growth: -7.25 to +11.14 mm (females); -16.16 to +31.55 mm (males).

**Growth results**

- Grand average monthly growth was **0.17 mm for females** and **0.09 mm for males**. Standard deviations were 9 to 25 times the means, so the authors found no statistical difference from zero.
- Females and males showed the same direction of growth (both positive or both negative) in 7 of 11 recapture years and in 7 of 9 capture years. Males were more numerous in nearly all years.
- 1991 gave the highest male growth (**1.34 ± 0.52 mm/month**, 16 fish). Males captured in 1990 averaged **0.00 ± 1.15 mm/month** (22 fish).
- Among females, 31 capture-recapture year pairs had average monthly growth below 1 mm (352 fish), and 6 pairs exceeded 1 mm (13 fish). Among males, 38 pairs were below 1 mm (764 fish) and 3 exceeded 1 mm (8 fish).
- Positive growth occurred in 82% of female capture years (256 fish), while 22% of female capture years showed negative growth. Males showed positive growth in 64% of capture years and 67% of recapture years.

**Seasonal patterns**

- Most CRPs for females fell in the reproductive and post-reproductive seasons. Females captured in the post-reproductive season and recaptured in the reproductive season made up 33% of female CRPs, and most of these fish grew less than 1 mm/month.
- Males captured in the reproductive season and recaptured in the post-reproductive season showed the most years with above-average growth (38 fish above 1 mm/month).
- Six fish captured in the growing season and recaptured later (2 females, 4 males) all showed negative growth.

**Short-term recaptures (< 30 DAL)**

- 17 females and 15 males were recaptured within 30 days. Daily growth was generally below 1 mm for females. Daily growth for males ranged from -0.50 to +0.43 mm.
- Two large apparent changes (a female from 675 to 580 mm in 29 days; a male from 673 to 560 mm in 27 days) were attributed to measurement or recording error.

**Multiple recaptures**

- 38 fish had three or more recaptures: 8 females and 30 males.
- 73% of multiply recaptured males were recaptured three times, and 32% were captured a fourth time. One male was recaptured five times within four years.
- The longest interval at large for any multiply recaptured fish was 9 years.
- No multiple recaptures were recorded for females PIT-tagged after March 1993, or for males PIT-tagged after March 1995.

**Conclusions**

- Monthly growth rates for both sexes varied only slightly around zero (fractions of a millimeter) across seasons and years, and were not different from zero.
- Length changes in fish captured and recaptured in the same month were larger than the long-term growth rates, which indicates that measurement or recording error outweighed the growth signal.
