# Provisional Annotated Check-List of the Freshwater Fishes of Iran

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cypriniformes

## Executive Summary

This paper is a provisional, annotated check-list of the freshwater fishes of Iran, intended to help students and researchers study the country's ichthyofauna. The author states that the list is provisional because the systematics of Iranian fishes is poorly known: many species are still described only from original brief descriptions based on a few specimens, published in scattered and hard-to-access journals, and synonymy is common. The check-list covers fishes of Iran's internal drainages and of major border rivers, and it is explicitly not exhaustive, since distributional data within river systems is scanty. The list is organised by class, order and family following [[Nelson Classification]] (1976), with species arranged alphabetically within each family. It is accompanied by a discussion of the zoogeographic origin and distribution of the fauna and a call for further systematic work.

## Background & Description

The check-list was prepared by a biologist at Pahlavi University in Shiraz, with field collections supported by the university's research council. It covers the following drainage systems:

- The [[Caspian Sea]], treated as freshwater although its salinity is given as about 1.2–1.3. Fishes recorded by Berg from the northern Caspian are excluded.
- The lower [[Araxes River]] on the northwest border with Azerbaijan and Armenia, and the Lake Urmia (Lake Reza'iyeh) basin.
- Tigris and Euphrates tributaries, including the Diyala and Lesser Zab, and the lower [[Shatt-el-Arab]] on the Iraq–Iran border.
- The [[Helmand River]] and the endorheic [[Seistan Basin]], with the Hari and Tedzhen rivers on the northeast border.
- The Atrek River on the northern border with Turkmenistan.
- Marine-influenced fishes of the [[Persian Gulf]] and Sea of Oman that enter freshwater, although most of these are excluded for lack of definite records.

The check-list contains 26 families across the classes Cephalaspidomorphi, Chondrichthyes and Osteichthyes. Individual entries give the distribution in Iran, synonymy and taxonomic status, and frequently note which records come from Iranian territory and which come only from neighbouring countries. Examples include the Caspian lamprey, endemic to the Caspian Sea and its tributaries, and several sturgeon species of the South Caspian Sea.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper describes Iran's freshwater fish fauna as predominantly Palaearctic, with Cyprinidae and Cobitidae as the principal families, and Gobiidae prominent in the Caspian basin. The author notes a relatively high diversity of families because both Laurasian and Gondwanaland elements are present, yet the fauna is dominated by Laurasian genera: 43 Laurasian genera versus 8 from Gondwanaland.

Drainage-based differences are notable:

- The Caspian Sea basin and the Persian Gulf drainages have the richest fish faunas in terms of families. The Caspian also holds northern families such as Esocidae, Salmonidae, Gasterosteidae and Percidae that do not occur in southern Iran, though salmonids are reported from endorheic basins just south of the Caspian.
- Marine families entered the isolated Caspian basin during the Tertiary, when it was connected to the Atlantic via the Black Sea.
- Persian Gulf drainages show clear affinities with the Tigris–Euphrates fauna. Sea level fall of as much as 120 m during the Pleistocene would have exposed the shallow Persian Gulf, linking rivers into a combined Tigris–Euphrates system draining into the Sea of Oman.
- Endorheic basins such as Lake Famur and the Kor and Shiraz drainages have fewer families than the low-altitude endorheic drainages and rivers draining into the Persian Gulf.
- The Jaz Murian basin in southeast Iran shows Oriental affinities, represented at family level by Channidae.
- The Seistan basin on the eastern border has widely distributed Iranian species together with elements of a High Asian fauna, including the cyprinid tribe Schizothoracini, which reached the lowland via the Helmand River.

Several species are restricted to unusual habitats. [[Iranocypris Typhlops]] is known only from a natural well connected to a subterranean water supply in the Zagros Mountains, and [[Aphanius Ginaonis]] is restricted to a hot spring near Bandar Abbas.

Human activity has changed the fauna. Several species are introduced or transplanted: [[Esox Lucius]] and [[Salmo Gairdneri]] were moved by the Department of the Environment; [[Carassius Auratus]] is found throughout Iran; [[Ctenopharyngodon Idella]] was introduced to Pahlavi Mordab, where it survived until at least 1967 without reproducing; and [[Gambusia Affinis]] was introduced widely for mosquito control. Introduced Black Sea species such as certain gobies and mullets have also established in the Caspian.

## Key Findings

- **Provisional status:** The list is explicitly provisional and non-exhaustive because of poor systematic knowledge, widely scattered original descriptions, extensive synonymy, and scanty within-basin distribution data.
- **Scope:** The check-list covers 26 families, with species arranged alphabetically under each family according to Nelson (1976).
- **Endemism and rarity:** [[Caspiomyzon Wagneri]] is endemic to the Caspian Sea and its tributaries. Several Iranian populations are of uncertain status, including the brown trout of the Lighvanchai River, whose specific or subspecific standing is unknown.
- **Taxonomic revisions:** Many names are treated as synonyms or subspecies by different authors. Examples include the sturgeon [[Acipenser Persicus]], which is regarded as a subspecies of *A. güldenstädti* by some authors and as a full species by others, and the large sharks recorded from the Shatt-el-Arab, whose identity is unconfirmed.
- **Zoogeography:** Laurasian genera dominate the fauna (43 versus 8 Gondwanaland genera). The fauna's high family diversity arises from mixing with Gondwanaland elements. Caspian, Persian Gulf and Seistan basins show distinct faunal histories.
- **Conservation-relevant notes:** The Caspian and Persian Gulf drainages hold the most families. Introductions of salmonids, pike, carp-like species and mosquitofish have altered local fish communities.
- **Research priorities:** The author concludes that much systematic work remains. Priorities include clarifying the zoogeography of Iranian freshwater fishes at the species level, eliminating synonyms, and producing keys and detailed species descriptions to serve as a basis for further studies.
