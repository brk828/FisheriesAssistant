# Lower Basin Native Fishes Subcommittee Comments on the Upper Basin Action Plan (1984)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1984 correspondence packet, compiled by the [[Lower Basin Native Fishes Subcommittee]] of the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]], transmits comments and recommendations on the draft [[Upper Basin Action Plan]] to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Region 6. The subcommittee, made up of agency and academic biologists familiar with "big river" native fishes, argues that the plan could undermine lower basin recovery efforts. Its central objection is that the USFWS [[Memorandum of Understanding]] (MOU) puts mitigation ahead of protection of endangered species, which the subcommittee considers inconsistent with the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The packet also calls for approved recovery plans to guide any upper basin plan and sets out nine recommendations focused on ecosystem-level management and habitat protection.

The packet includes a transmittal letter from J. A. St. Amant (Team Leader), a formal comment document, a September 1984 cover letter from the subcommittee chair, an internal routing memo, a USFWS letter on recovery actions, and short responses from state wildlife agencies in Arizona, Nevada, and Utah.

## Background & Description

The correspondence concerns four endangered big-river fishes of the Colorado River basin:

- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*): described as possibly the most endangered big-river fish. Lake Mohave may be its last refuge.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*): the most viable population in the basin is reported in the [[Little Colorado River]] and its confluence with the mainstem Colorado River.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): named in the recovery-plan discussion.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): named in the USFWS recovery-action letter.

The subcommittee's comments followed presentations by Frank Dunkle and Jim Bennett at its May 30, 1984 meeting. A draft [[Coordinating Committee]] alternatives list was no longer being prepared, but the subcommittee kept its related comments in the package.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and population status**
- The Little Colorado River and its confluence area hold the best humpback chub population in the entire drainage. The [[National Park Service]] (NPS) controls this area, which the USFWS letter describes as a model habitat. Upstream changes in the operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] could have serious impacts on this population.
- Lake Mohave is described as the last refuge of bonytail. Recent investigations were cited as the basis for this view.
- Several Arizona rivers are described as having suitable habitat for reintroducing big-river fishes. All were historic habitats from which the species were extirpated. Segments of the lower Colorado River are also considered potentially suitable.

**Captive propagation**
- The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter NFH, New Mexico) spawned bonytail captured from Lake Mohave, and offspring were returned to the lake.
- Dexter NFH is described as having had "excellent success" in spawning and rearing all big-river native fishes. The subcommittee says the hatchery program took years to develop.
- The USFWS plans to establish a [[Non-Essential Experimental Population]] of bonytail in the Upper Gila River, Arizona.

**Water development and law**
- The subcommittee states that major water development projects and maintenance or expansion of state water rights in the Upper Basin are unclear as to how they would prevent further decline of endangered fishes. It also says fish and wildlife receive low priority on state water-right lists, which it considers inconsistent with the ESA.

## Key Findings

**1. Primary concern: mitigation over protection**
- The subcommittee argues that the USFWS MOU emphasizes mitigation above the protection of endangered species, weakening ESA protection.
- It considers the Upper Basin Coordinating Committee possibly established to speed developers through [[Section 7 Consultation]], which it calls an "invalid approach" to endangered species management.
- Arizona Game and Fish Director Bud Bristow wrote that he "strongly support[s] protection of biological integrity rather than giving it away through mitigation."

**2. Recovery plans must guide Upper Basin planning**
- Lower basin recovery for bonytail and Colorado squawfish is being implemented using individual recovery plans. The subcommittee says it is unclear how these, and the humpback chub plan, will integrate with an Upper Basin plan.
- It recommends that all recovery plans be approved and released, and that they serve as guiding documents for any Upper Basin plan. If they are unsuitable, the Recovery Team should revise them before an Upper Basin plan is developed.

**3. Nine recommendations (summarized)**
1. Adopt an ecosystem approach, managing a "community of native fishes" rather than individual species, to determine which reaches should be protected.
2. Maintain the status quo for species population levels in the Upper Basin until water-resource effects are analyzed.
3. Conduct biological and hydrological evaluations of reaches to determine why big-river fish declines have occurred.
4. Require the Coordinating Committee to include all available big-river fish data in its final analysis.
5. Recognize the difficulty of protecting all Upper Basin habitats, and consider reintroduction to historic habitats as an alternative.
6. Maintain status quo for species populations, as in Recommendation 1.
7. Complete analysis of data before decisions affecting native fishes are made.
8. Protect humpback chub habitat in the Little Colorado River and confluence area, and recognize the population in any Upper Basin plan.
9. Identify and protect natural habitats in specific stream sections.

The subcommittee also recommends:
- Closer coordination between the Upper and Lower basins, including a representative from each to the steering and coordinating committees.
- A revised purpose section for the MOU that (a) stresses the importance of maintaining threatened habitat essential to species survival, (b) links the MOU's intent to the ESA, and (c) reinforces ESA protection rather than mitigation of impacts.
- Reviewing whether the original purpose of the recovery teams and the value of the recovery plans are being used to guide recovery efforts in the Upper Basin.

**4. Discrepancy in bonytail capture counts**
- The subcommittee's document states that 18 adult bonytail were captured from Lake Mohave.
- The USFWS letter of August 15, 1984 gives approximately 10 adults (the OCR reads "f0"). The two figures differ and both are reported as written.

**5. Administrative trail and state responses**
- The Oct. 21, 1984 routing memo from St. Amant asked the subcommittee to return final review comments by Nov. 7, 1984.
- A Nevada Department of Wildlife letter (Oct. 29, 1984) and a Utah Division of Wildlife Resources letter (Oct. 29, 1984) respond to the draft plan. Parts of these letters are illegible in the scan, so their full positions cannot be confirmed.
