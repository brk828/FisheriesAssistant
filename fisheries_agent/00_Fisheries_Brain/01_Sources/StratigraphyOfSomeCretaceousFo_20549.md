# Stratigraphy of Some Cretaceous Formations of Southeastern Arizona

#stratigraphy #cretaceous #geology #paleontology

## Executive Summary

This paper by Don W. Schafroth (Department of Earth Sciences, Portland State College) correlates Cretaceous sedimentary sequences exposed across several mountain ranges of southeastern Arizona. It is a geological study, not an ichthyological one. The document contains no fish species, fish habitat data or aquatic-ecosystem survey data. Its aquatic relevance lies in the marine and brackish depositional environments it reconstructs, which are tied to the [[Bisbee Sea]], and in the invertebrate and vertebrate fossils it records.

The central conclusion is that the [[Lower Cretaceous]] rocks correlate with the type [[Bisbee Group]] and the Upper Cretaceous rocks correlate with the type [[Sonoita Group]]. The two systems are tied together only on the south flank of the [[Empire Mountains]], where Upper Cretaceous rocks lie with angular unconformity on Lower Cretaceous rocks.

## Background & Description

The Cretaceous rocks of southeastern Arizona are thick but structurally and stratigraphically difficult to interpret. Several factors complicate the record:

- **Rapid lateral lithological change** means marker beds are rare and rarely traceable over long distances.
- **[[Basin and Range Province]] deformation** has covered most Cretaceous outcrops with Pliocene basin-fill sediment, forcing long-range correlations.
- **Normal faulting** divides exposures into blocks, so sections must be pieced together from separated outcrops.
- **Tertiary intrusion and metamorphism** have further obscured original relationships.

Earlier workers include Dumble (1902), Ransome (1904), Stoyanow (1936, 1949), Hayes and Landis (1961), Moran (1957), Tyrrell (1957), and Moore and Miller (1960). The paper is an abridgement of a Ph.D. dissertation completed at the University of Arizona.

### Bisbee Group (Type Section)

The [[Bisbee Group]] (Aptian and Albian, Early Cretaceous) is exposed in the [[Mule Mountains]] and [[Ninety One Hills]] of southern Cochise County. It comprises four formations named by Ransome (1904):

- [[Glance Conglomerate]]
- [[Morita Formation]]
- [[Mural Limestone]]
- [[Cintura Formation]]

Hayes and Landis (1961) interpreted Stoyanow's Lowell Formation as equivalent to the upper 375 feet of the Morita Formation and all of the Lower Mural Limestone.

### Other Sections Described

- **[[Huachuca Mountains]]:** An 8,226-foot section in Parker Canyon, described by Moran (1957), divided into six units. Units 1 to 4 are Lower Cretaceous, and units 5 and 6 are correlated with Upper Cretaceous rocks.
- **[[Whetstone Mountains]]:** Tyrrell (1957) recorded an unbroken 8,548-foot section on the southwest flank, containing four formations that are partly marine and partly non-marine.
- **[[Santa Rita Mountains]]:** The Sonoita Group in [[Adobe Canyon]] comprises the [[Fort Buchanan Formation]] (about 2,000 feet of conglomerate, sandstone and shale) and the [[Fort Crittenden Formation]] (over 2,500 feet).
- **[[Empire Mountains]]:** The thickest continuous section, measured by Moore (1960), runs from Total Wreck Ridge to [[Cienega Wash]] and totals 8,100 feet.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper reconstructs a shifting shoreline rather than a single marine habitat. Key environmental interpretations include:

- **Bisbee Sea transgression and regression:** The Lower Cretaceous sequence (approximately 4,300 feet per the Bisbee Group summary) records shoreline deposition during advance and retreat of the Bisbee Sea. The deeper-water [[Mural Limestone]] marks maximum transgression, and the [[Morita Formation]] represents a transitional marine to non-marine phase.
- **Littoral and lagoonal settings:** The author argues the sea did not extend far north. Evidence includes abundant lagoonal sediments, thin littoral limestones alternating with non-marine clastics, rapid marine/non-marine transitions, and the predominance of non-marine rock.
- **Alluvial fans:** The rapid thinning and thickening of the Glance Conglomerate suggests deposition on an irregular surface, possibly alluvial fans.
- **Flood-plain deposition:** The [[Turney Ranch Formation]] is interpreted as flood-plain deposits, with sandstone in channels and siltstone and shale on inter-channel mud-flats.
- **Lagoonal and estuarine conditions:** Tyrrell (1957) interpreted the [[Willow Canyon Formation]] as non-marine, grading upward into lagoonal and estuarine siltstone and shale, with fresh-water branchiopods reported from its upper portions. Tyrrell interpreted the [[Apache Canyon Formation]] as lagoonal at the base, marine in the middle, and lagoonal at the top.
- **Fluctuating sea level:** The [[Shelleburg Canyon Formation]] is interpreted as recording rapid, multiple pulses of sea-level change.

Marine biostromes in the Shelleburg Canyon Formation are described as thin, laterally traceable and physically distinctive. They are used for structural interpretation and for measuring offset on small faults.

## Key Findings

### Thickness and Correlation Data

- **[[Willow Canyon Formation]]:** 6,200 feet thick at Cienega Wash, compared with 433 feet at its type locality in the Whetstone Mountains. The basal conglomerate averages about 50 feet, up to 100 feet, and is absent in places. It lies with angular unconformity on upper Paleozoic rocks ([[Concha Formation]] and [[Rainvalley Formation]] clasts).
- **[[Apache Canyon Formation]]:** 870 feet thick, composed of laminated limestone, siltstone, shale and sandstone. A gypsiferous zone occurs about 200 feet above the base. The limestone is rich in interstitial organic matter.
- **[[Shelleburg Canyon Formation]]:** Approximately 3,600 feet on the west side of Cienega Wash, with unknown thickness missing at the base and top. A 2,650-foot section was measured on the south flank of the Empire Mountains.
- **[[Turney Ranch Formation]]:** About 1,000 feet in the Empire Mountains and Davidson Canyon, overlain unconformably by the Hilton Ranch Conglomerate.
- **[[Hilton Ranch Conglomerate]]:** More than 4,260 feet thick at Davidson Canyon, with a 350-foot section on the south flank of the Empire Mountains. It is interbedded with tuff, andesite flows and agglomerate. It is probably Late Cretaceous and is overlain unconformably by Pliocene basin-fill gravels.
- **Lower Cretaceous thickness:** The Whetstone Mountains section is stated as "in excess of 8,558 feet" in one passage, and the Lower Cretaceous portion of the Huachuca section is 1,500 feet thinner. The author suggests a systematic westward and northward thickening of about 200 feet per mile.

### Fossils and Age Evidence

- **Bisbee Group fauna:** The Morita Formation contains Acanthohoplites and [[Trigonia]]. Trigonia are common in the lower Mural Limestone, and Douvilleiceras occurs in the upper Mural Limestone. The fauna is described as definitive, equivalent to the Nuevo Leon, Trinity and Fredericksburg Stages of Texas.
- **Huachuca Mountains:** Ostrea ragsdalei and Trigonia maloneana occur in upper Unit 2 and Unit 3.
- **Whetstone Mountains:** Trigonia maloneana, Ostrea franklini and Gryphaea mucronata were reported from the Shelleburg Canyon Formation.
- **Shelleburg Canyon, Empire Mountains:** Several marine biostromes contain fragments of the pelecypod genus Mactra. A silicified ostracod-bearing shale lies above the upper biostrome.
- **Dinosaur remains:** [[Gorgosaurus]] libratus is reported from the lower sandstones and shales of the Fort Crittenden Formation. Dinosaur bones were also found by E. P. Hilton about 1,200 feet below the top of the Shelleburg Canyon section.
- **Hilton Ranch Conglomerate:** No fossils were found.

### Age Interpretations

- The Glance Conglomerate is Early Cretaceous, based mainly on structural and stratigraphic evidence.
- The Morita Formation is probably mostly Lower Aptian, and the Cintura Formation is probably Upper Albian.
- Tyrrell (1957) suggested the entire Whetstone section is Early Cretaceous. Schafroth concludes the exposed section is Early Cretaceous (Aptian and Albian), with the Lower/Upper Cretaceous boundary lying south and southwest beneath Pliocene basin fill.
- The Hilton Ranch Conglomerate is probably Late Cretaceous and the same age as the [[Fort Crittenden Formation]], based on similar lithology, contacts and environment.

### Structural and Stratigraphic Relationships

- The Turney Ranch Formation is overlain unconformably by the Hilton Ranch Conglomerate on the south flank of the Empire Mountains.
- Unit 4 is overlain unconformably by Unit 5 at the top of the Huachuca section.
- Blocks of conglomerate similar to the Hilton Ranch Conglomerate are exposed on the east flank of the Santa Rita Mountains.

### Author's Conclusions

- The Lower Cretaceous rocks correlate with the Bisbee Group type section, and the Upper Cretaceous rocks with the Sonoita Group.
- The Empire Mountains area is the only place where the complete Cretaceous System is linked.
- The Cretaceous sequences are interpreted as shoreline deposits of an advancing and regressing Bisbee Sea.
