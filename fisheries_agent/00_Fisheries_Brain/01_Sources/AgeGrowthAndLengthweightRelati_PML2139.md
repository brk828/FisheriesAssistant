# Age, Growth, and Length-Weight Relationship of Flathead Catfish from Southeastern California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This short research note by Paul C. Mars (Arizona State University, Tempe) reports the first age, growth, and [[Length-Weight Relationship]] data for [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) in the [[Colorado River]] basin. The species was introduced into [[Martinez Lake]], Arizona, in 1962 and had spread into canals of the [[Imperial Valley]] and the river below [[Parker Dam]]. Fish were collected from the [[Coachella Canal]], [[Imperial County]], in November 1980. Ages were estimated from pectoral spine sections ([[Pectoral Spine Aging]]), and growth was back-calculated to annual length estimates. Growth rates were comparable to or greater than those reported from native-range reservoirs and rivers, but the length-weight relationship resembled riverine rather than reservoir populations.

## Background & Description

Flathead catfish are large, introduced ictalurids in the lower Colorado River system. Before this study, no age, growth, or length-weight data had been published for the species in the basin. Flathead catfish made up about 1.0% of more than 1,000 fish taken in rotenone samples from the Coachella Canal in November 1980, which provided material for this compilation.

Fish were measured in centimeters from the tip of the caudal fin (total length), and weights recorded in pounds were converted to metric units. Specimens had been preserved, and no corrections were made for shrinkage after preservation. Laboratory work was based on the method of [[Turner]] (1977).

**Methods summary:**
- Pectoral spines were removed after softening tissue in dilute potassium hydroxide, then sectioned with a jeweler's saw.
- Thin sections were polished on emery paper and examined under transmitted and reflected light with a binocular microscope.
- Spine diameter at the sectioning point was measured to the nearest 0.01 mm with vernier calipers.
- Anterior radius and anterior radius to each annulus were measured to the nearest 0.1 mm with an ocular micrometer.
- [[Back-Calculation]] of lengths at each annulus used the Dahl-Lea-type equation L_x = C + (S_x / S)(L − C), where L_x is length at annulus x, L is length at capture, S_x is the spine radius at annulus x, S is total spine radius, and C is the intercept from linear regression of total length on spine diameter.
- Size-frequency distribution separated Age Group 0 fish. This was checked against spines from 10 young-of-year fish across a range of lengths.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampled canal reach had little cover. Flathead catfish were concentrated in an inverted concrete siphon built to carry the canal under a large desert wash. Two 3 × 3 m barrels in the siphon carried strong, relatively constant flow, with discharge of about 70.8 m³/s.

Other abundant fishes in the canal included [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*), and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*). All but carp were of a size suitable as prey for flathead catfish. All are eaten by flathead catfish in the lower Colorado River (Minckley 1981a).

Fish were abundant, at 0.55 to 1.08 individuals/m² overall. [[Biomass]] was 49.4 and 102.0 g/m² in two samples from the open canal and 593.9 g/m² in the siphon. Flathead catfish made up about 25% of the biomass in the siphon (Minckley 1981b).

## Key Findings

- **Growth:** Calculated lengths at each annulus compared well with age groups from the length-frequency distribution and with lengths at capture of the next-youngest year class. Spine annuli did not reach the edges of any age group, so fish were assumed to have grown before annulus formation in January, the coolest month in the region (Jaeger 1957).
- **Spine–length regression:** Spine diameter correlated strongly with total length (r = 0.98). The regression was reported as Y = 13.6 + 137X (the coefficient is as rendered in the OCR scan).
- **Length-weight relationship:** For Coachella Canal fish, log W = −5.2500 + 3.1441 log L.
- **Comparison with other populations:** Growth rates were comparable to or exceeded those in reservoirs and rivers within the native range (Table 2 in the original). The length-weight relationship was more like that of riverine populations than reservoir populations.
- **Sample size:** Size-frequency analysis used 75 flathead catfish from the Coachella Canal (Figure 1). The sample for the Table 1 age–length results is smaller, and the source notes that some mismatch between the two arises from extra fish included in the figure.
- **Aging validation:** Age Group 0 fish were separated by size-frequency and confirmed by spines from 10 young-of-year fish.
- **Management relevance:** The study was funded by the [[Water and Power Resources Service]] (Boulder City, Nevada), with cooperation from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]]. It provided baseline growth data for an introduced predator in a managed canal system.
