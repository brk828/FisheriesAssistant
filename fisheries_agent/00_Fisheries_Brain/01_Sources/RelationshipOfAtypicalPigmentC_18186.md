# Relationship of Atypical Pigment Cell Growth to Gonadal Development in Hybrid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1953 study by [[Olga Berg]] and [[Myron Gordon]] examines whether atypical pigment cell growth (melanosis and [[Melanoma]]) is linked to abnormal [[Gonadal Development]] in [[Platyfish-Swordtail Hybrids]]. Using [[Genetic Methods]], the authors produced F1, F2 and two backcross generations from [[Platyfish]] (*Xiphophorus maculatus*) carrying [[Macromelanophore]] genes and [[Swordtail]] (*Xiphophorus hellerii*) hybrids. Gonads were examined histologically in 783 hybrids and 536 females were dissected. Gonadal abnormalities were common, and about half of adult hybrids had normal gonads. Hybrids with melanomas were not consistently gonad-deficient, and five melanomatous hybrids had no detectable gonads. The authors conclude that gonads are not essential for early melanoma development and that the abnormalities reflect chromosomal and genic incompatibility.

## Background & Description

**Study rationale.** Earlier clinical work had linked mammalian mammary, prostatic and adrenal carcinomas, and human melanomas, to endocrine glands, particularly the gonads. Results were inconsistent: [[Sugiura]] (1948) found castration had no effect on transplanted mouse melanomas. Platyfish-swordtail hybrids offered a model where both atypical pigment growth and gonadal abnormalities occur spontaneously. The authors described their approach as "[[Biological Castration]]", since the hybrids are small (about 14 mm) and physical castration is difficult.

**Study organisms.** The platyfish was a female carrying macromelanophore genes, from a stock obtained at the Rio Jamapa. The swordtail was a male from a stock obtained at the Rio Papaloapan, Mexico. The platyfish was reclassified as a subgenus of *Xiphophorus* by Gordon and Rosen (1951).

**Generations studied.**
- **F1 (PX):** platyfish female × swordtail male.
- **F2 (PX)2:** F1 × F1 inbreeding.
- **Backcross PX-X:** F1 female × swordtail male.
- **Backcross P-PX:** platyfish female × F1 male.

**Pigment classification.** Four categories were used: (1) normal pigment growth, either recessive (no macromelanophores) or dominant (normal patterns); (2) atypical growth, either melanosis (excess macromelanophores replacing tissue) or melanoma (a definitive pigmented overgrowth). Dorsal-fin melanomas were considered relatively benign, while body melanomas were the most malignant.

**Methods.** Gonads were examined under a dissecting microscope, fixed in Bouin's fluid, sectioned at 7 µm and stained with several standard histological stains.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper does not analyse field ecology or water chemistry. Its ecological relevance is limited to natural populations. In wild platyfish, macromelanophore genes produce normal spotted patterns. Under natural selection, multiple genetic modifiers balance the expression of these genes. The authors argue that interspecific hybridization disrupts this balance, which produces atypical growth. Interspecific hybrids also show a high rate of gonadal abnormalities, a pattern the authors attribute to chromosomal incompatibility.

## Key Findings

### Pigment cell growth across generations

- **F1 (PX):** From 104 offspring, 64 were available for analysis. Of these, 66% had melanosis, 9% had melanomas (on the dorsal fin or body) and 25% were normal.
- **F2 (PX)2:** 274 hybrids were produced and 240 were examined. Atypical growth occurred in 80%. Among the 79 Sd hybrids, 68% were melanotic, 11% had dorsal-fin melanomas and 21% were normal. Among the 80 Sp hybrids, 60% were melanotic, 1% had body melanomas and 39% were normal. All 81 hybrids carrying both Sp and Sd genes had atypical growth (90% melanosis, 10% melanomas).
- **Backcross PX-X:** Of 368 Sp hybrids, 73% were melanotic, 20% had body melanomas and 7% were normal. Of 48 Sd hybrids, 58% were melanotic and 42% had dorsal-fin melanomas.
- **Backcross P-PX:** Of 99 hybrids, 62% were normal and 38% were slightly melanotic. None developed melanomas.
- The authors attribute the higher melanoma frequency in PX-X to an increase in swordtail dominant modifier genes A and B. They attribute the low frequency in P-PX to their reduction.

### Gonadal development

- **Males, testes:** Ten F1 males were fertile. Probably functional testes occurred in 22% of 60 (PX)2 males, 46% of 52 PX-X males and 50% of 20 P-PX males. Seven types of abnormality were found in the remaining 85 hybrids. These included excess colloid (43%), free sperm in ducts (32%), scarce early spermatogenesis (19%), immature testes (11%) and teratoid testes (3%). Teratoid testes are described as "great abnormality", not monster formation.
- **Females, ovaries:** 536 females were dissected (64 PX, 206 (PX)2, 144 PX-X, 122 P-PX). Mature ovaries were found in 100% of PX, 62% of (PX)2, 60% of PX-X and 92% of P-PX. Embryonic or arrested ovaries occurred in 38% of (PX)2, 41% of PX-X and 8% of P-PX.
- **Gonadless hybrids:** 101 dissected hybrids had no gonads but excessive fat. These were 21% of 338 (PX)2 and 13% of 225 PX-X. Gonads were found in all PX and P-PX hybrids. Serial sections of 50 of these showed no gonad trace in 47 cases. Three had very small gonads.

### Relationship between pigment and gonads

- Among (PX)2 hybrids, mature gonads occurred in 93% of melanin-free fish and 45% of normal-pigment fish. The authors report a χ² of 10.9 with P = 0.02 for the normal versus atypical comparison, and describe this as "no significant difference".
- For PX-X, the χ² was 2.26 (P = 0.57). For P-PX, the χ² was 1.7 (P = 0.75). The authors conclude that atypical pigment growth is not consistently associated with abnormal gonads.
- Of 38 hybrids with melanomas, 22 had mature gonads, 11 had immature gonads and five had no gonads.

### Sex chromosomes and sex determination

- Platyfish use an XY (male heterogametic) system. The swordtail sex chromosome is designated X'. In F1 hybrids, 94 were female and 10 male, with 6 later changing from female to male.
- In the F2 generation (338 hybrids), gonadal condition varied within each sex chromosome class. Only about one-quarter of Sp/Sd (X/X) hybrids had mature gonads, although they were all expected to be female.
- The authors conclude that sex chromosomes alone do not determine sex. They propose that autosomal sex genes interact with sex chromosomes, in line with [[Winge]]'s modified genic balance theory.

### Fertility

- [[Tavolga]] (1949) tested 33 Sp female hybrids: 2 were fully sterile and 3 had low productivity with over 30% anomalous embryos.
- Of 34 Sd female hybrids, 15 were sterile, 11 produced aberrant broods and 8 produced normal broods.
- Gordon (1948) reported that 4 of 5 Sd male hybrids and 4 of 5 Sp male hybrids were fertile.

### Hormonal balance

- Similar testicular abnormalities have been produced in other poeciliids by [[Sex Hormones]] such as stilbestrol, estradiol and pregnenolone. The authors also cite a sex-reversed platyfish whose testis became sterile, possibly through hormonal imbalance.
- Since gonads were not essential for early melanoma development, the authors suggest that any hormonal role would require a non-gonadal source. They propose the [[Interrenal Tissue]] of teleosts as the counterpart to the mammalian adrenal cortex.

### Conclusions

1. Atypical pigment growth was induced by mating platyfish carrying macromelanophore genes to swordtails.
2. About half of adult hybrids had normal gonads. Male abnormalities were mostly post-spermatogenic, while female abnormalities were mostly in early oogenesis.
3. Gonadal abnormalities are attributed to chromosomal incompatibility between platyfish and swordtail.
4. Five melanomatous hybrids had no gonads. If sex hormones contribute to melanoma, they must come from sources other than the gonads alone.

### Discussion highlights

Peyton Rous raised the "tissue imbalance" concept of Ribbert. Gordon responded that the macromelanophore is the origin of the cancer, and that genetic imbalance from disrupted modifier genes may explain atypical growth. He also suggested that a similar mechanism could operate in other organisms.
