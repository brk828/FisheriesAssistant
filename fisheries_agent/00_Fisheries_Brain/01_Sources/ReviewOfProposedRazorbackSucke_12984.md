# Review of Proposed Razorback Sucker Listing Proposal

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #conservation

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the Management Biologist at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] provides detailed review comments on a proposed rule to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an endangered species. Dated January 12, 1990, the document critiques the technical accuracy, writing quality, and presentation of the proposed listing rule while strongly supporting the endangered species designation itself. The review identifies numerous factual errors, inappropriate citations, and areas requiring clarification in the draft proposal.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]] distributed throughout the lower mainstream [[Colorado River]] in small numbers, with the largest aggregation occurring in [[Lake Mohave]]. Young razorbacks less than 150 mm total length do exhibit a keel, though less pronounced than in larger individuals. The species is planktivorous in feeding habits. The proposed listing rule under review addresses population status, reproduction, growth rates, food habits, and threats to the species across its range in Arizona, New Mexico, and California.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Razorback Sucker]] inhabits the [[Colorado River Basin]], including mainstream river habitats, reservoirs (particularly [[Lake Mohave]]), and associated canal systems. Evidence of natural reproduction has been limited or absent for 25-40 years according to the proposal, though the review notes inconsistencies in this timeframe. Juvenile razorbacks have been collected from the lower river and associated canals, with young-of-year specimens seined from [[Lake Mohave]] in 1987 by Arizona Game and Fish Department biologists. The species faces threats from predation by nonnative fishes, with [[Green Sunfish]] observed preying on razorback sucker larvae. Water quality and stream flow regulations affect habitat suitability.

## Key Findings

**Technical and Editorial Issues:**
- The proposed rule is poorly written, wordy, and confusing; requires careful grammatical revision
- Literature citations are incorrectly ordered chronologically throughout the document
- Growth rate discussions (pages 11-13) are confusing and poorly presented; could be significantly reduced
- Multiple paragraphs require complete rewriting for clarity and conciseness

**Factual Corrections Required:**
- Region 2 has cooperative agreements with Arizona and New Mexico but not California; the New Mexico agreement has never been implemented
- The statement that lower basin stockings produced no results is incorrect; substantial biological information has been obtained including habitat use, food habits, movement patterns, and interactions with nonnative fishes
- Razorbacks are distributed throughout the lower mainstream [[Colorado River]] in small numbers, not restricted to just two reservoirs
- Young razorbacks less than 150 mm do show a keel, contrary to proposal statement
- The claim of no young razorback collections in the lower basin is incorrect; Marsh and Minckley (1989) reported juveniles from the lower river and canals, and two young-of-year were seined from [[Lake Mohave]] in 1987
- [[Green Sunfish]], not redear sunfish, have been observed preying on razorback sucker larvae
- Marsh and Brooks (1989) reported predation on stocked razorback sucker in the [[Gila River]], not [[Salt River]]

**Citation Problems:**
- Inappropriate use of Bestgen (1989), still in initial draft form; Minckley (1983) would be more appropriate
- Brooks et al. (1987) is an unpublished manuscript, not an official report; use is inappropriate
- Vanicek (1967) collection location for suckers examined should be specified
- Unnecessary citation of Bestgen (1989) when original investigators are already referenced

**Substantive Content Issues:**
- Inconsistency regarding evidence of natural reproduction: 25 years (page 2) versus 30-40 years (page 5)
- Food habits paragraph (pages 15-16) should be rewritten with clear lead statement that razorback is a planktivore, then reference specific evidence
- Discussion of other studies and information sources (page 6) is too vague; should specifically address lower basin recovery efforts
- Statement that any action affecting razorback sucker would have to be a water project is narrow-minded; other potential impacts such as agricultural activities should be considered
- Paragraph on water quality and stream flow regulations (page 23) incorrectly implies intentional leniency due to razorback presence; requires rewrite
- Tone of paragraph 3 on page 24 needs revision; while listing would make recovery program more effective through habitat protection and Section 7 consultation, nothing demonstrates listing is the only way to develop an effective program
- Paragraph spanning pages 30-31 on coordination requirements is poorly written; needs shorter, more concise statement or complete elimination
- "Currently listed species" should be named throughout document rather than referenced generically

**Overall Assessment:**
[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] staff strongly supports listing the [[Razorback Sucker]] as an endangered species despite significant deficiencies in the proposed rule document. The proposal can be improved appreciably by addressing editorial, factual, and citation issues identified in this review.
