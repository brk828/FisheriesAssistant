# Provenance and Disposition of Fish Specimens Appearing in Color Plates of Kuehne and Barbour's Book, The American Darters

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Carter R. Gilbert]] and [[Stephen J. Walsh]] documents a museum-curatorial investigation into the origin and current repository status of the 144 fish specimens photographed for the 24 color plates in [[Robert A. Kuehne]] and [[Roger W. Barbour]]'s 1983 monograph *[[The American Darters]]*, which covers nearly all North American members of the family [[Percidae]]. Because the original book omitted locality data for its photographed specimens, the authors undertook a systematic reconstruction effort using Kuehne's field cards, correspondence with collectors and colleagues, and physical comparison of specimens with plate photographs. They successfully determined confirmed locality/collection data for 117 specimens and probable data for 7 more (124 of 144 total). Of these, 100 specimens now reside in the [[Florida Museum of Natural History]] collection, 11 are held at other institutions, and 13 remain of unknown disposition. The remaining ~20 specimens have no recoverable provenance data at all.

## Background & Description

- **Source monograph**: *[[The American Darters]]* (Kuehne and Barbour, 1983, University Press of Kentucky) is a taxonomic monograph on the darters (subfamily Etheostomatinae, family [[Percidae]]), featuring keys, distribution maps, species accounts, and 24 color plates (6 photos each = 144 specimens).
- **Photographic method**: Most specimens were freshly collected by Kuehne and Barbour during peak breeding season to capture species-specific nuptial coloration, then photographed immediately after preservation by Roger Barbour. Some specimens/photos were loaned or donated by other ichthyologists (10 species used external slides/prints).
- **Core problem addressed**: The book lacked locality data for any photographed specimen — a significant taxonomic and curatorial gap given that several darter species show geographic variation in life colors indicative of cryptic species diversity.
- **Data reconstruction methods**:
  - Cross-referencing Kuehne's personal file cards (transferred with the collection).
  - Direct physical comparison of specimens to plate photographs using identifying features (fin tears, tag marks, body distortion, parasite cysts).
  - Correspondence with colleagues and institutions who loaned specimens/photos.
  - Confirming with the [[University Press of Kentucky]] that locality data were never part of the original manuscript.
- **Fate of Kuehne's collection**: Following [[Robert A. Kuehne]]'s death on 18 December 1984, his fish collection (containing most of the book's photographed specimens) was transferred to the Florida State Museum (now [[Florida Museum of Natural History]]) in summer 1985 per his wishes, and subsequently catalogued.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Taxonomic scope**: The genera treated are [[Percina]] and [[Etheostoma]] (including former *Ammocrypta*, now largely reassigned to *Etheostoma* except *[[Crystallaria asprella]]*, elevated to its own genus per [[Simons]] in press).
- **Geographic range of specimens**: Collections span numerous U.S. states — Kentucky, Tennessee, Virginia, Alabama, Georgia, Mississippi, Louisiana, Missouri, Arkansas, South Carolina, North Carolina, Texas, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, Maryland — plus one Mexican locality (Durango).
- **Habitat types represented**: Small to moderate freshwater streams, rivers, springs, spring runs, cave streams (e.g., Horse Lick Creek, Bylew Cave), and lake margins (e.g., Lake Waccamaw), reflecting the characteristic benthic, riffle- and spring-associated habitat preferences of darters.
- **Taxonomic revisions referenced**:
  - *[[Etheostoma maculatum]]* — now split into three species: *E. maculatum*, *[[Etheostoma sanguifluum]]*, and *[[Etheostoma vulneratum]]* (Page 1985).
  - *[[Etheostoma zonale]]* — split into two species: *E. zonale* and *[[Etheostoma lynceum]]* (Etnier and Starnes 1986).
  - *[[Etheostoma stigmaeum]]* — suspected species complex (unpublished, Howell 1968).
  - *[[Etheostoma atripinne]]* — downgraded to subspecies *[[Etheostoma simoterum atripinne]]* (Page and Mayden 1981).
  - *Ammocrypta* — six species moved to *Etheostoma* (subgenus [[Ulocentra]] and others), with *[[Crystallaria asprella]]* elevated to a distinct genus (Simons, in press).
  - Several undescribed "snubnose darter" forms (subgenus *Ulocentra*) documented informally (e.g., "Golden," "Redbelly," "Gulf Coast," "Black Warrior," "Upland," "Tallapoosa" snubnose darters), later formally described taxa such as *[[Etheostoma flavum]]*.

## Key Findings

- **144 total specimens** appeared across the 24 color plates of *The American Darters*.
- **117 specimens** had confirmed locality and collection data successfully reconstructed; **7 additional** had probable data — totaling **124 of 144** (86%) with recoverable provenance.
- **Disposition breakdown of the 124 identified specimens**:
  - **100 specimens** deposited at [[Florida Museum of Natural History]] (UF).
  - **11 specimens** held at other institutions, including [[University of Tennessee]] (UT), [[University of Alabama Ichthyological Collection]] (UAIC), [[National Museum of Natural History]] (USNM), [[Roanoke College]], and [[Baylor University]].
  - **13 specimens** of confirmed identity but unknown current disposition.
- **~20 specimens** (144 − 124) had no recoverable provenance or collection data whatsoever ("provenance and disposition unknown").
- **Five species** (*[[Etheostoma blennioides]]*, *[[Etheostoma spectabile]]*, *[[Etheostoma stigmaeum]]*, *[[Etheostoma whipplei]]*, *[[Etheostoma zonale]]*) were depicted with two different photographs each in the original book, reflecting intraspecific geographic variation in nuptial coloration; *[[Etheostoma maculatum]]* was depicted with three photographs, corresponding to what are now recognized as three distinct species.
- **Notable curatorial anomaly**: The photograph labeled *Etheostoma lynceum* (Plate 10, RC) was determined to be a mirror-reversed image of the specimen's right side, printed to visually match the orientation of other plate photographs.
- **Identification challenges** encountered included: specimens lacking distinguishing marks, large uniform series of similar-sized individuals, body/fin distortion from crowding in storage jars prior to hardening, and fin damage from tagging performed for photography.
- **Key individuals credited** with specimen collection, identification, or provenance recall include [[Robert A. Kuehne]], [[Roger W. Barbour]], [[James W. Small Jr.]] (particularly valuable for recalling specific localities), [[W.J.B. Miller]] (responsible for cataloguing the Kuehne collection at UF), and numerous outside institutional contacts (e.g., [[David A. Etnier]], [[Herbert T. Boschung]], [[Richard L. Mayden]], [[William L. Pflieger]]).
- **Nomenclatural standard**: The authors followed the 5th edition of *Common and Scientific Names of Fishes* (Robins et al. 1991), except adopting [[Page and Burr (1991)]]'s treatment of sand darters, following [[Simons]]' phylogenetic work elevating *[[Crystallaria]]* and expanding *Etheostoma*.
- **Broader significance**: This paper serves as an important curatorial bridge, restoring scientific/locality context to historically significant voucher specimens central to documenting nuptial coloration and cryptic species diversity within [[Percidae]], and formalizing their permanent accessibility via museum catalog numbers for future taxonomic and systematic research.
