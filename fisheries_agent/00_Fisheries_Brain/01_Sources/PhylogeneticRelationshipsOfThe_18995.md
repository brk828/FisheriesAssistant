# Phylogenetic Relationships of Western North American Phoxinins Inferred from Mitochondrial 12S and 16S rRNA

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This molecular phylogenetic study sequenced the complete mitochondrial 12S and 16S ribosomal RNA genes, plus the intervening Valine tRNA gene, in 31 cyprinid specimens representing the major clades of [[North American Phoxinins]] and several outgroups. Parsimony analyses recovered three major clades: the [[Western Clade]], the [[Creek Chub Clade]], and the [[Open Posterior Myodome Clade]]. The study concludes that the western North American cyprinid fauna is not monophyletic in the traditional geographic sense, but that a more restricted Western Clade is supported. Within it, [[Phoxinus]] is most likely the basal sister group, which implies that other North American phoxinin lineages probably have Asian or European relatives.

## Background & Description

The western North American cyprinids have long been difficult to classify. The fauna was defined largely by geography (taxa native to drainages west of the Continental Divide) and contains a high proportion of endemic genera, roughly 50% of which are monotypic (Miller, 1959). Body size and ecology vary widely, from the small herbivorous [[Eremichthys]] (maximum length 65 mm) to the large piscivorous [[Ptychocheilus]] (up to 1.8 m).

Earlier taxonomy was complicated by a large number of names, many of them coined in the late 1800s and early 1900s by workers including Baird, Girard, Snyder, Jordan and Evermann. Miller (1945) revised the [[Gila]] group into subgenera, Uyeno (1961) resurrected [[Richardsonius]] and recognised three Gila subgenera, and Bailey (1956) had earlier merged several genera into Gila. Nomenclatural issues around the names *bicolor* were resolved by Bailey and Uyeno (1964), who assigned the tui chub to *Siphateles* and designated *Gila coerulea* for the blue chub.

Earlier phylogenetic work included an allozyme study of California cyprinids (Avise and Ayala, 1976) and a morphological cladistic analysis of North American cyprinids (Coburn and Cavender, 1992). The latter found the western fauna not to be monophyletic and judged Gila polyphyletic. The present study builds on the authors' earlier 12S-only analysis of the [[Creek Chubs]] (Simons and Mayden, 1997).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study is a molecular systematics project, so it contains no field ecology data. Its geographic framing is relevant, though:

- Western Clade taxa are largely restricted to the Colorado system, the Great Basin and Pacific drainages.
- Several taxa are spring-dwelling Great Basin endemics, notably [[Relictus]] and [[Eremichthys]].
- *Phoxinus* has a holarctic distribution, occurring in eastern North America, Europe and Asia but absent from western North America.
- Biogeographic inference depends on the position of *Phoxinus* relative to the western clade, so the phylogeny directly informs hypotheses of dispersal between North America and Eurasia.

## Key Findings

**Sampling and data**
- 31 cyprinid specimens were sequenced across the three North American phoxinin clades plus outgroups (including *Abramis*, *Leuciscus* and *Notemigonus*).
- Sequence data were deposited in GenBank under accession numbers AF023183 to AF038498 (as listed in the source).
- Missing data were limited: *P. erythrogaster* lacked 12S positions 1-50, and *Eremichthys* lacked 16S positions 860-926.

**Data properties (Table 2)**
- 12S: 200 variable sites, 110 phylogenetically informative.
- Valine tRNA: 11 variable sites, 7 informative.
- 16S: 476 variable sites, 333 informative.
- ALL data: 687 variable sites, 450 informative. With coded gaps, 697 and 463 respectively.
- The 16S gene contains about three times as many informative sites as 12S.

**Saturation and weighting**
- The transition:transversion ratio fell with increasing divergence and reached an asymptote of about 2.5 at divergences of 8% or more, indicating saturation at some sites. Transversions were therefore weighted 1:1, 2:1 and 3:1 relative to transitions.

**Structure**
- Estimated g1 values indicated more phylogenetic structure than random data (P = 0.1) for 12S, 16S and ALL data, including analyses with congeneric taxa removed. The Valine tRNA alone (seven informative sites) did not.

**Major clades**
- Four clades were always recovered: the *Phoxinus* clade, the OPM clade (*Rhinichthys*, *Phenacobius*, *Platygobio*, *Notropis*), the Creek Chub clade (*Couesius*, *Margariscus*, *Snyderichthys*) and the Orthodon clade. Bootstrap support was 98-100%.
- The monophyly of the Western Clade was supported in 100% of bootstrap replicates.
- The Western Clade contains *Acrocheilus*, *Eremichthys*, *Gila*, *Lavinia*, *Orthodon*, *Phoxinus*, *Ptychocheilus*, *Relictus* and *Siphateles*.
- The Creek Chub clade and OPM clade were never resolved as sister to the Western Clade in a way that was strongly supported. The authors favour the Creek Chub clade as sister to the OPM clade.

**Gila**
- *Gila* was monophyletic only when *Siphateles* and *Snyderichthys* were excluded.
- *G. coerulea* is the sister group to the remaining Gila.
- Four sister pairs were consistently resolved: *G. atraria*–*G. cypha*, *G. conspersa*–*G. pandora*, *G. nigrescens*–*G. orcutti*, and *G. ditaenia*–*G. robusta*.
- Relationships within Gila do not match the subgeneric groupings of Miller (1945) or the phylogeny of Uyeno (1961).

**Other relationships**
- *Acrocheilus* is sister to *Gila* in weighted and unweighted analyses of ALL data.
- *Relictus* is sister to *Acrocheilus* plus *Gila* in most analyses. It was not resolved as sister to *Eremichthys* or *Siphateles*, contrary to Coburn and Cavender (1992).
- *Eremichthys*, *Lavinia*, *Siphateles* and *Ptychocheilus* were unresolved relative to one another and to the Relictus clade. Their relationships changed with data and weighting.
- *Orthodon* is basal to the Western Clade apart from *Phoxinus* in 16S and ALL analyses.

**Biogeographic conclusions**
- *Phoxinus* is most likely the basal sister group of the rest of the Western Clade. The authors base this on the complete 12S+16S data, despite weaker support in 12S alone.
- Consequently, the Creek Chub and OPM clades probably have Asian or European relatives. The authors also hypothesise Asian taxa closely related to these clades.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- The authors take a total-evidence approach because the 12S and 16S genes are linked and unlikely to have independent histories.
- Additional data are needed to resolve the relationships among *Eremichthys*, *Siphateles*, *Lavinia* and *Ptychocheilus*.
- Taxa not yet sampled, including *Yuriria*, *Algansea*, *Totichthys*, *Moapa* and *Mylopharodon*, may affect the monophyly of Gila.
