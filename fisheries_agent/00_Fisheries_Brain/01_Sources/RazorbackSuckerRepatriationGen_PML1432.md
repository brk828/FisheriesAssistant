# Razorback Sucker Repatriation Genetics: Lake Mohave Backwater Experiments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1996 letter from [[Paul C. Marsh]] to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] reports three breeding seasons (1992–1994) of [[Razorback Sucker]] experiments in the [[Yuma Cove]] backwater of [[Lake Mohave]]. The experiments tested methods for producing young fish for [[Repatriation]] under the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) program, and checked whether the fish produced represented the genetic diversity of the wild adult population. The assessment uses [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) haplotype data. The central conclusion is that larvae naturally spawned in Lake Mohave and reared in on-site backwaters retained the parental genetic diversity, whereas backwater-spawned (1992) and manually stripped (1993) cohorts did not.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. A comparative control group of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) mtDNA appears in appended tables.
- **Investigators:** Drs. T.E. Dowling, P.C. Marsh, and W.L. Minckley, working under state and federal permits and in coordination with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the NFWG.
- **Purpose:** (1) Identify techniques that maximize the annual number of young razorback suckers repatriated to Lake Mohave. (2) Make sure repatriated fish carry the high genetic diversity of the adult population.
- **Prior work:** A 1993 final report by Dowling and Minckley on razorback sucker genetic diversity, based on restriction endonuclease analysis of mtDNA, was funded by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. Activities before 1992 are not covered.

### Study Cohorts and Methods

| Year | Source | Method | Genetic sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1992 | 60 males and 28 females captured from [[Arizona Bay]] | Stocked into Yuma Cove backwater (27–28 Jan 1992); adults spawned naturally | 15 young fish |
| 1993 | Adults captured from the lake (Feb–Mar) | 20 ripe females manually stripped (50 ml ova each), fertilized with sperm from 2–3 males; about 200,000 ova stocked | 17 juveniles; mtDNA from 18 of 20 mothers |
| 1994 | Larvae naturally produced in Lake Mohave | Captured directly and reared in backwaters ([[Manual Stripping]] not used) | 14 larvae (see discrepancy below) |

The 1994 protocol, refined and still in use, stratifies larval collections in space and time to match adult spawning aggregations. These were located with routine operations and helicopter overflights. Young fish are held at the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] facility in Boulder City, NV, or at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], or placed directly into backwaters beside the lake. They are reared to a target of 30.0 cm total length (TL) before repatriation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Growth in the backwater:** 1992 cohort juveniles reached 5.3 cm TL early in the season and 39.0 cm TL by November 1992.
- **Genetic inheritance:** mtDNA is maternally inherited. This makes the number of contributing females a critical factor in the diversity of each cohort.
- **Reference populations:** Appended mtDNA tables also cover [[Lake Powell]], [[Lake Mead]], the [[Green River]] and [[Yampa River]], the [[Little Colorado River]], the [[Upper Colorado River]] (including [[Highline Lake]] and [[Etter Pond]]), Colorado River Indian Tribe canals, and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (1987, 1989, and 1990 year classes). These support the broader population-genetic context, but the letter's conclusions focus on Lake Mohave.

## Key Findings

### Genetic Diversity by Cohort

Diversity is expressed as the ratio of mtDNA haplotypes to individuals in the sample.

| Group | Individuals | Haplotypes | Diversity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1987–1988 adult reference, Lake Mohave | 49 | N/A | 0.67 |
| 1992 backwater cohort | 15 | 5 | 0.33 |
| 1993 manually stripped cohort | 17 | 9 (as tabulated) | 0.53 |
| 1993 parental females | 18 of 20 | 16 | 0.89 |
| 1994 natural larvae, reared in backwaters | 14 | 10 (text) | 0.71 |
| Lake Mohave parental stock (summary) | N/A | N/A | 0.69 |

### Principal Conclusions

- **Natural spawning works:** Young from natural in-lake spawning, reared in on-site backwaters, adequately represented parental diversity (0.71 vs. 0.69).
- **Backwater spawning fails:** The 1992 cohort, derived from adults that spawned in the backwater, did not represent the parental diversity (0.33).
- **Manual stripping fails:** The 1993 cohort did not represent the parental population. Only about half of the stripped females contributed to the cohort, and the rest were not represented at all. This left the parental diversity (0.89) unmatched.
- **Overall judgment:** Wild-caught larvae are more similar in genetic variability to the wild stock than larvae produced by other means. The author describes the differences as substantial.

### Data Quality Notes

- The text gives the 1994 sample as "74" in one place and 14 in the methods and the table. The 14 figure is consistent with the 10-haplotype and 0.71 values.
- The 1994 table summary lists 11 haplotypes, while the text reports 10.
- The parental-stock diversity is given as 0.67 in one passage and 0.69 in the summary.
- The 1993 mothers table lists 23 entries, while the text describes 20 ripe females.
- One sample in the 93YC1–93YC16 group may be a duplicate.
- Sample LM2 was a sperm sample that also contained eggs from LM3, possibly through cross-contamination. It was excluded from statistical analyses.
