# Razorback Sucker Movements in Lake Mohave and the Bring Back the Natives Desert Fishes Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a pair of abstracts from the Desert Fishes Council *Proceedings, 1996* (Volume XXVIII, published 1997). The first abstract reviews the status of the desert fishes component of the [[Bring Back the Natives]] program, a national partnership initiative. It argues that fisheries funding is declining, that partnership efforts are becoming more important, and that desert fishes need funding proportional to their imperilment. The second abstract reports a 17-month [[Sonic Telemetry]] study of endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. The study documents long-term movements, seasonal spawning aggregations, and strong site fidelity in a reservoir population. The second abstract received the [[Hubbs Student Paper Award]] competitor designation. Both abstracts include Spanish translations, which are not reproduced here.

## Background & Description

**Bring Back the Natives (BTN).** BTN is a national initiative jointly sponsored by the [[USDA Forest Service]], the [[Bureau of Land Management]], and the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]], together with numerous local partners. The Forest Service has completed a variety of projects to assess, protect, and restore native desert fishes, several of which were cooperatively funded through BTN.

**Razorback Sucker.** The razorback sucker is a large, endangered catostomid endemic to the big rivers of the [[Colorado River]] drainage. Lake Mohave holds the largest extant population of the species. The abstract notes that while aspects of its life history are relatively well studied, its movements are poorly known, especially in reservoirs.

**Study Setting.** Lake Mohave is a regulatory reservoir about 100 km long on the Colorado River, bounded upstream by [[Hoover Dam]] and downstream by [[Davis Dam]].

**Research Partnership.** The telemetry study was carried out under a cooperative agreement between [[Arizona State University]] (Department of Zoology and Center for Environmental Studies) and the National Biological Service. The authors' affiliations also include the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Funding context:** Agency funding for fisheries work has declined in recent years. BTN funds have stayed relatively constant but represent a small share of the total fisheries budget. The abstract expects public/private cooperation to become a larger part of fisheries management.
- **Reservoir habitat:** Fish concentrated in specific areas of the reservoir. One such area appeared linked to a highly productive mixing zone that forms each year through thermal stratification of the reservoir.
- **Reproductive season:** Both sexes used several different spawning aggregations between November and March.
- **Spatial scale:** Individuals ranged widely along the reservoir, which is about 100 km long, and showed strong seasonal and annual site fidelity.
- **Management relevance:** The authors say the results may help manage the Lake Mohave population of this imperiled species.

## Key Findings

### Desert Fishes and BTN (Abstract 1)
- Desert fishes need funding and program emphasis proportional to their level of imperilment, according to the authors.
- BTN funding has remained constant, while overall agency fisheries funding has decreased.
- Public/private cooperative efforts are expected to play a growing role in fisheries work.

### Sonic Telemetry of Razorback Suckers, Lake Mohave (Abstract 2)
- **Study design:** In November 1994, 10 adult males and 10 adult females were surgically fitted with sonic transmitters.
- **Survival:** Ten fish (four males, six females) survived the 17-month study. These fish were tracked bi-weekly.
- **Movement range:** Individual locations spanned more than 75 km, from within 5 km of Hoover Dam (upstream reservoir limit) to within 20 km of Davis Dam (downstream limit).
- **Habitat use:** Individuals often concentrated in specific areas. One such concentration appeared related to a productive mixing zone formed each year by thermal stratification.
- **Spawning:** Males and females used several different spawning aggregations during the November through March reproductive period.
- **Site fidelity:** Fish showed strong, year-round affinity for specific reservoir areas and often returned to the same location at about the same time each year.
- **Significance:** The authors say these results substantially add to knowledge of razorback sucker life history and may guide future management of the Lake Mohave population.
