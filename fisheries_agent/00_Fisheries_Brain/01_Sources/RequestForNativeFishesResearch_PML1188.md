# Native Fishes Research Needs Request and Dexter NFH Species Inventory

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This is an interagency correspondence (memorandum with an attached species list) from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in Dexter, New Mexico, to researchers at Arizona State University in Tempe. The hatchery manager, signed by Buckley L. Jensen, asks the university scientists (Hinckley and Paul Marsh) to identify five research problems or areas they would like the hatchery to address. Responses were requested by January 15, 1987. The memo states that the hatchery now has manpower available for research on [[Native Fishes of the Southwest]], an interest the university group shares. The attachment lists the species held at the hatchery, with their federal status as of December 16, 1986.

## Background & Description

The memo, dated 1985 (day garbled in scan), is a request for input rather than a research report. Its purpose is to match hatchery capacity with academic expertise on native fishes. The hatchery holds several federally listed species, which is why its research agenda is tied to [[Endangered Species Act]] priorities.

The attachment, "List and Status of Species Held at Dexter NFH," covers 11 species in four families:

- **Catostomidae:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Cyprinidae:** [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), [[Pahranagat Chub]] (*Gila robusta jordani*), [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*)
- **Cyprinodontidae:** [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon bovinus*), [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
- **Poeciliidae:** [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), [[Big Bend Gambusia]] (*Gambusia gaigei*)

The scan does not clearly align each federal status code with its species, so per-species status should be checked against the original attachment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species list is a set of Southwestern desert and arid-land fishes, most of which are imperiled. Hatchery holdings are described as "native fishes of the Southwest," which points to conservation propagation and recovery work rather than fisheries production. The document does not discuss water chemistry, habitat conditions, invertebrates, or plankton, and it gives no field data.

Federal status key given in the attachment:

- **E** = Endangered
- **T** = Threatened
- **C** = Candidate for future listing

Status is current as of December 16, 1986.

## Key Findings

- **Research solicitation:** The Dexter hatchery has manpower available for research projects and invites Arizona State University researchers to propose five priority problems or areas, with responses due January 15, 1987.
- **Shared interest:** The memo notes the university group's expertise and existing interest in the native fishes the hatchery works with.
- **Holdings:** The hatchery holds 11 species of native Southwestern fishes across four families (Catostomidae, Cyprinidae, Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae). Several are federally listed as endangered or threatened, and at least one is a candidate for listing.
- **Notable species:** Razorback Sucker, Colorado Squawfish, Bonytail Chub, and Woundfin are among the best-known imperiled large-river and desert fishes in the group.
- **Data limitations:** The document contains no survey results, sample sizes, or quantitative data. Its value is as a record of research priorities and species holdings as of late 1986.
