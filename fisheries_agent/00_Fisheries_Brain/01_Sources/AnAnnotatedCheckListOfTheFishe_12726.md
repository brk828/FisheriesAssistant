## Executive Summary

This 1951 paper by [[Ira La Rivers]] and [[T. J. Trelease]] represents the first comprehensive checklist of the fish fauna of Nevada, compiled under the taxonomic framework of Shapovalov and Dill (1950). The list documents **55 established species** (34 native, 21 introduced) across 10 families, plus a supplementary list of **11 species/subspecies unsuccessfully introduced**. The fauna is defined overwhelmingly by Nevada's unique hydrography: relict populations stranded by the desiccation of **Pleistocene Lake Lahontan** and the ancient **White River** system, alongside peripheral incursions from the **Columbia/Snake**, **Bonneville**, **Colorado River**, and **Amargosa/Death Valley** drainages. The paper emphasizes endemism in isolated desert springs (e.g., [[Devil Pupfish]], [[Moapa Dace]], [[Soldier Meadows Dace]]), the collapse of native lacustrine salmonids due to river damming and diversion, and the widespread, often poorly documented, history of game-fish stocking (trout, bass, catfish, sunfish) throughout the state.

## Background & Description

- **[[Colorado River Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — Largest North American cyprinid ("carp" family); pike-shaped body; historically reached lengths up to 5 feet and weights up to 80 lbs.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen tewanus*) — Distinctive large, grotesque hump-backed body form; native to the Colorado River system.
- **[[Cui-ui Sucker]]** (*Chasmistes cujus*) — Deep-water-adapted sucker of [[Pyramid Lake]]; relatively weak swimming powers despite historically ascending ~35 miles up the Truckee River to spawn.
- **[[Gila Robusta|Bonytail Chub]]** complex — *G. r. elegans* is a highly streamlined, swift-water-adapted form of the main Colorado River channel, contrasted with the smaller-stream-adapted *G. r. robusta*.
- **[[Devil Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*) — The smallest species in the genus *Cyprinodon*; restricted to a single limestone pothole (Devil's Hole) with constant ~38°C (92°F) water.
- **[[Amargosa Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis*) — Rarely exceeds two inches in length; warm-water cyprinodont morphology typical of the genus.
- **[[Tahoe Sucker]]** (*Catostomus tahoensis*) — Lacustrine (Pyramid Lake) forms grow to lengths exceeding two feet; smaller stream-spawning individuals (~12") now predominate due to habitat decline.
- **Tui Chub** (*Siphateles bicolor obesus*) — Notable intraspecific polymorphism in **gill raker morphology** (coarse vs. fine rakers) occurring within the same schools, with no sexual correlation and no evidence of habitat/dietary segregation — the authors dispute prior taxonomic subdivision (*pectinifer*) based on this trait.
- **[[Royal Silver Rainbow Trout]]** (*Salmo gairdneri regalis*) and **[[Emerald Rainbow Trout]]** (*S. g. smaragdus*) — Deep-lake ecotypes (Lake Tahoe and Pyramid Lake respectively) with distinct coloration, both likely extinct at time of writing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper organizes Nevada's ichthyofauna around four principal biogeographic provinces, each shaping distinct ecological guilds:

- **[[Lahontan Basin]]** — The dominant native drainage, historically centered on [[Pyramid Lake]] and the Truckee, Carson, and Walker river systems. Lake-spawning species (Lahontan Tui Chub, Lahontan Redside Shiner, three sucker species, plus introduced Sacramento Perch and Carp) persisted despite river degradation, while river-spawning species (Lahontan Cutthroat Trout, Cui-ui Sucker, Emerald Rainbow Trout) suffered severe declines or extinction due to damming, siltation, and reduced streamflow blocking spawning migrations.
- **[[Columbia-Snake System]]** — Represented in northeastern Nevada tributaries (Owyhee, Bruneau, Jarbidge, Salmon rivers), hosting cold-water specialists such as [[Chiselmouth]], [[Columbia Squawfish]], [[Columbia Basin Sucker]], and [[Mountain Whitefish]].
- **[[Colorado River System]]** — Represented in extreme southern Nevada, home to large-river endemics such as the [[Colorado River Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and the *Gila robusta* bonytail chub complex, with the Virgin River hosting the endangered [[Woundfin]].
- **[[White River / Death Valley Relict Systems]]** — Isolated warm-spring endemics stranded by Pleistocene desiccation, including [[White River Springfish]], [[Railroad Valley Springfish]], [[Ash Meadows Springfish]], [[Pahrump Springfish]] (with three micro-endemic subspecies tied to individual ranches/springs), [[Amargosa Pupfish]], and the extreme thermal specialist [[Devil Pupfish]] confined to a single limestone pothole at ~38°C.
- **Introduced game-fish guild** — Salmonids (Brown, Rainbow, Brook, Lake Trout), centrarchids (Largemouth/Smallmouth Bass, Bluegill, Sacramento Perch, crappies), and ictalurids (Channel, White, Brown, Black Bullhead catfishes) were widely and often haphazardly stocked from the 1870s–1950s, frequently causing hybridization with native cutthroat trout and competitive/predatory pressure on native fish assemblages.

## Key Findings

- **55 established species/subspecies** documented in Nevada at time of publication, of which **21 were introduced** (double-asterisked in original text); additional native fishes had been transplanted outside their original ranges (single-asterisked).
- **Extinction/near-extinction of Lahontan endemics**: [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*) extinct in Pyramid Lake and most historic range; possibly surviving as a pure strain only in Summit Lake, Humboldt County. [[Royal Silver Rainbow Trout]] and [[Emerald Rainbow Trout]] both presumed extinct.
- **Cui-ui Sucker crisis**: For four consecutive years (1948–1951) prior to publication, insufficient Truckee River inflow prevented the [[Cui-ui Sucker]] from completing its spawning migration to Pyramid Lake tributaries, forcing lake-spawning as a fallback survival strategy.
- **Hybridization documented** between: Rainbow and Cutthroat Trout (to the detriment of cutthroat); *Richardsonius balteatus balteatus × hydrophlox* (Northern × Bonneville Redside Shiner) in Snake River tributaries; and *Orthodon microlepidotus* (Sacramento Blackfish) with *Siphateles bicolor obesus* (Tui Chub) in an artificial pond near Reno — the only known Nevada record of this species, likely introduced via fish-rescue stock from the Sacramento Valley.
- **Taxonomic revisions cited**: *Catostomus columbianus* resurrected from synonymy with *Pantosteus jordani* (Miller & Miller, 1948); dismissal of *Siphateles bicolor pectinifer* as a valid taxonomic unit based on gill-raker sampling data showing no ecological or sexual segregation.
- **Biogeographic stream capture**: Possible natural transfer of Lahontan species (*Richardsonius egregius*, *Rhinichthys nubilus robustus*, *Pantosteus lahontan*) into the Sacramento drainage via headwater capture near Lake Tahoe/Feather River (Kimsey, 1950; Rutter, 1908).
- **Micro-endemism in desert springs**: The genus *Empetrichthys* (Pahrump/Ash Meadows Springfish) demonstrates fine-scale allopatric speciation, with three named subspecies of *E. latos* each restricted to a single spring/ranch within Pahrump Valley.
- **Conservation intervention**: In winter 1946–47, the authors translocated ~75 [[Devil Pupfish]] individuals from Devil's Hole to a nearby warm spring as an emergency conservation measure against potential habitat destruction — with unknown outcome and risk of hybridization with sympatric *Cyprinodon nevadensis*.
- **Unsuccessful introduction record**: 11 species/forms (including Chum Salmon, Silver Salmon, Atlantic Salmon, Golden Trout, Lake Whitefish, Arctic Grayling, American Shad, and Tench) failed to establish despite documented stocking efforts dating back to the 1870s–1880s.
