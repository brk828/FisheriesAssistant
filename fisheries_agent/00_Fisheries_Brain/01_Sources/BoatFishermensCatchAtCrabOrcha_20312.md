# Boat Fishermen's Catch at Crab Orchard Lake, Lake Murphysboro and Horseshoe Lake, 1955

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1956 creel-census study, published in the *Illinois Academy of Science Transactions* (Vol. 49), measured angler success on three southern Illinois waters during 1955. It was a cooperative project between the [[Illinois Department of Conservation]] and the [[Cooperative Fisheries Research Laboratory]] and the Department of Zoology at [[Southern Illinois University]]. Catch rates were recorded by method (minnow, plug, worm/cockroach, fly) and by water body. Overall catch rates were poor. The only comparatively satisfactory result was the sunfish catch at Lake Murphysboro, and those fish were small. Crab Orchard Lake showed a marked decline in fishing pressure relative to 1954.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** To obtain a dependable measure of fishing success in southern Illinois.
- **Crab Orchard Lake:** A 6,600-acre reservoir. The census ran from March 12 to October 1, 1955, on 28 days distributed across that period. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] granted permission for the census. An aerial boat count from the 1954 census ([[Newton and Lewis 1956]]) indicated the 1955 survey checked 41% of each day's boat fishing.
- **Lake Murphysboro:** A 173-acre artificial impoundment in [[Jackson County, Illinois]], filled in 1950, with a maximum depth of 35 feet. It was treated with [[Rotenone]] to remove the wild fish population, then stocked with adult [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. The present population also includes [[White Crappie]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Warmouth]], [[Common Carp]], and a few miscellaneous forms. The census was taken at the lake's only boat dock on a fixed weekday schedule from June 15 to September 15, 1955.
- **Horseshoe Lake:** Surveyed with minnow, plug, and worm/cockroach fishing, though no census schedule is detailed in the text.
- **Methods:** Catch was expressed as fish per man-hour. Spinning and casting gear were combined as "plug fishing" because the two were often indistinguishable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fishing pressure:** Crab Orchard Lake data were compared with the 1954 season using Saturday censuses only (29 Saturdays in 1954, 27 in 1955). Average man-hours per day were 163 (minnow) and 72 (plug) in 1954, versus 35 (minnow) and 37 (plug) in 1955. The authors concluded that fishing pressure at Crab Orchard had "greatly decreased."
- **Lake condition and reputation:** The authors describe the limited worm-fishing effort at Crab Orchard as indicative of the lake's "poor reputation" for producing [[Bluegill]] and [[Bullhead]] catches.
- **Comparison baseline:** Catch rates were compared with published values in [[Carlander 1950]] (*Handbook of Freshwater Fishery Biology*). Rates for all three lakes were judged low relative to that reference.
- **Stocking history:** Lake Murphysboro's post-rotenone stocking program is a notable management intervention in a newly created impoundment.

## Key Findings

**Crab Orchard Lake (6,600 acres)**
- **Minnow fishing:** 954 man-hours recorded, yielding 0.38 [[Crappie]] per man-hour, down from 0.52 in 1954 and low relative to Carlander (1950).
- **Plug fishing:** 999 man-hours, yielding 0.09 [[Largemouth Bass]] per man-hour, an improvement over the 1954 rate of 0.5 but still very poor.
- **Worm fishing:** 106 man-hours, too small a sample for rate conclusions. The catch was 14 bluegill, 14 carp, 2 freshwater drum, and 1 yellow bullhead.
- **Fly fishing:** One angler, no success in three hours.

**Lake Murphysboro (173 acres)**
- **Worm and cockroach fishing:** The most popular method and the best fishing recorded in the study, yielding 1.29 [[Bluegill]] and 0.26 [[Redear Sunfish]] per man-hour, plus 0.05 combined largemouth bass, crappie, and channel catfish.
- **Plug fishing:** 237 man-hours, yielding 0.07 bass per man-hour.
- **Minnow fishing:** 198 man-hours, yielding 0.13 bass and 0.10 crappie per man-hour. The crappie rate was fairly good; the bass rate was poor.
- **Fly fishing:** 67 man-hours, yielding 0.15 bluegill, 0.02 redear sunfish, and 0.25 largemouth bass per man-hour.

**Horseshoe Lake**
- **Minnow fishing (principal method):** 0.36 crappie per man-hour, with practically no bass or other fish.
- **Plug fishing:** 78 man-hours, with a poor catch.
- **Worm and cockroach fishing:** 141 man-hours, yielding 0.94 bluegill (a good rate) and 0.08 crappie (poor) per man-hour.

**Overall conclusions**
- Across the three lakes, the only satisfactory result was the sunfish catch at Lake Murphysboro, and those sunfish were fairly small.
- Crab Orchard fishing pressure fell sharply between 1954 and 1955, and catch rates for the main methods remained low.
