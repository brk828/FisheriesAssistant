# Evaluation of the Yampa Humpback Chub Population

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This FY 2005 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers Project 133, which evaluates the [[Yampa River]] population of [[Humpback Chub]]. The project ran from 2003 to 2005 under principal investigator Sam Finney of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Vernal, Utah). Sampling was completed in fall 2004, and final data analysis and report writing were still underway at the time of reporting. The project was 100% complete with its full $30,000 budget spent.

## Background & Description

- **Recovery goals:** The [[Humpback Chub]] recovery goals call for no net loss in the size of existing populations and a monitoring program to track their status.
- **Population status:** The Yampa population is one of the smallest known humpback chub populations. A recent [[Recovery Program]] population monitoring workshop identified abundance estimates as especially important for measuring progress toward recovery.
- **Distribution question:** The recovery goals raise the question of how far the "Yampa" population extends. The population may include fish occupying [[Lodore Canyon]], [[Whirlpool Canyon]], and [[Split Mountain Canyon]].
- **Supporting evidence:** Recent collection of age-0 and age-1 humpback chub (per personal communications from Chris Kitcheyan, USFWS, and Dryl Snyder, Colorado State University) supports the presence of the species in at least Whirlpool Canyon. Historical records document humpback chub captures in Split Mountain and Lodore canyons.
- **Project purpose:** This is the first attempt to sample humpback chub across all suspected occupied locations in the Yampa population. It is intended to establish baseline distribution data for future monitoring.
- **RIPRAP link:** The project addresses the [[Green River Action Plan]] (Yampa and Little Snake Rivers, item V.A): conduct a population estimate for humpback chub.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document discusses the habitat only as the canyon reaches where humpback chub are believed to occur: Whirlpool, Lodore, and Split Mountain canyons on the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] system. It provides no data on water chemistry, invertebrates, plankton, or other ecosystem variables, and it does not give the species' scientific name, family, or taxonomy.

Population concerns are framed in terms of abundance and distribution. The Yampa population is described as one of the smallest known humpback chub populations, so [[Population Monitoring]] and [[Abundance Estimation]] are central to tracking recovery.

## Key Findings

- **Project scope:** Evaluate humpback chub distribution and abundance in the Yampa population and set a baseline for future monitoring.
- **Sampling status:** Field sampling was completed in fall 2004. Final analysis and report writing were ongoing.
- **Distribution evidence:** Age-0 and age-1 fish indicate reproduction in at least Whirlpool Canyon. Historical captures are recorded from Split Mountain and Lodore canyons.
- **Management priority:** Abundance estimates are a key need for measuring progress toward recovery goals.
- **Recommendation:** Complete the final report.
- **Project status:** Ongoing and subject to review before continuation.
- **Budget:** $30,000 provided and $30,000 expended, with no difference. Work is 100% complete. No funds were spent on publication charges.
- **Data status:** Data are being entered into Microsoft Excel spreadsheets. They will be sent to the database manager after the project is completed in 2005.
- **Sign-off:** Signed by Sam Finney, Principal Investigator, on November 8, 2005.
