# Metabolism of a Desert Stream

#phycology #freshwater-ecology #cladophora-glomerata

## Executive Summary

Busch and Fisher (1981), published in *Freshwater Biology*, measured [[Gross Photosynthesis]] and [[Community Respiration]] of benthic algal assemblages in [[Sycamore Creek]], an intermittent stream in the [[Sonoran Desert]] of central Arizona. Using in situ light-dark chambers and [[Multiple Regression Analysis]], they estimated that the stream was autotrophic during summer (P/R = 1.7), with gross photosynthesis of 8.5 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹ and community respiration of 5.1 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹. The authors argue that such desert streams are net exporters of organic matter and may be a notable exception to the generalization that most streams are heterotrophic. The study covered summer only, so the authors state that autotrophy could not be demonstrated for the full annual cycle.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Sycamore Creek is a tributary of the Verde River, about 32 km northeast of Phoenix. Its watershed covers 505 km² and is rugged, mountainous terrain of igneous and metamorphic rock with shallow soils.
- **Study site:** A 640 m elevation reach with shallow bedrock where surface flow persists for most of the year. Modal summer discharge is 0.03 m³ s⁻¹, though floods can be intense.
- **Climate:** Annual precipitation ranges from 39 cm (510 m) to 51 cm (1,000 m), with bimodal peaks in winter (gentle, multi-day rain) and summer (localized thunderstorms). Pan evaporation is 313 cm year⁻¹, so the stream is frequently intermittent, especially in summer.
- **Riparian zone:** Cottonwood, walnut, sycamore and ash grow along a channel more than 30 m wide, but the trees are sparse, so the 1–4 m wide wetted stream receives little shade.
- **Water chemistry:** Moderately hard, calcium bicarbonate water; conductance near 500 µS cm⁻¹; total alkalinity near 5 meq L⁻¹; summer pH 7–8; phosphate-phosphorus about 50 µg L⁻¹; nitrate-nitrogen about 20 µg L⁻¹ at the time of study.
- **Methodological challenges:** Water temperature varies daily by up to 10 °C, which makes constant-rate respiration methods inappropriate. Primary producers are attached to loose sand and cannot be removed without disrupting the assemblage, which rules out standard chamber or bottle methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Benthic assemblages.** Benthos fell into two types:

1. **[[Cladophora Glomerata]] mats** covering about half the channel bed, with epiphytic diatoms (*Cocconeis, Gomphonema, Navicula, Nitzschia, Synedra*). These were generally on the channel periphery.
2. **Epipelon** covering the remainder of the bed, composed of diatoms (*Navicula*, *Nitzschia*) and blue-green algae (*Nostoc*, *Oscillatoria*).

**Biomass and chlorophyll (July–August 1978):**

| Assemblage | Biomass (g AFDW m⁻²) | Chlorophyll-a (mg m⁻²) |
|---|---|---|
| Cladophora mat | 363 | 324 (296–353) |
| Epipelon | 174 (144–210) | 59 |

**Organic matter dynamics.** The authors link high gross production, low litter inputs, high algal biomass and intermittent severe floods to net export of organic matter. Modal flows move little organic matter over long distances, so floods probably flush accumulated biomass downstream. Exported material may reach larger rivers, lakes or playas, be dispersed by wind, or be taken up by terrestrial consumers such as termites and ants. The lower 18 km of Sycamore Creek flows only when discharge exceeds 7 m³ s⁻¹.

**Autotrophy versus heterotrophy.** The authors note that an autotrophic ecosystem must show gross production exceeding respiration plus net storage or export, evaluated over a steady-state boundary covering all seasons. Spatial and temporal heterogeneity allows localized autotrophy and heterotrophy, so classification depends on system boundaries.

## Key Findings

**Stream-level metabolism (25–26 July 1978):**
- Gross photosynthesis (P): **8.5 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹**
- Community respiration (R): **5.1 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹**
- P/R: **1.7**, so the stream was autotrophic during the period sampled.
- Dissolved oxygen rose from below saturation before dawn to a high percentage of saturation by 13:00, consistent with daytime photosynthesis.

**Assemblage-level rates (mean, with 95% confidence intervals where reported):**
- *Cladophora* mats: P = 12.5 ± 1.13 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹, R = 5.5 ± 0.35 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹, **P/R = 2.3** (autotrophic)
- Epipelon: P = 4.4 ± 0.39 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹, R = 4.6 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹ (the dispersion value in the scan is partly illegible), **P/R = 0.96** (marginally heterotrophic)

**Predictive regression equations** (based on 110 light-dark pairs from 26 July, 18 August and 21 August 1978):
- *Respiration* as a function of biomass and temperature: significant (F = 155, P < 0.001), r² = 0.74.
- *Gross photosynthesis* as a function of chlorophyll-a, PAR and temperature: significant (F = 127, P < 0.001), r² = 0.78.
- Exponents show that specific photosynthesis declines as algal biomass rises, and that assimilation efficiency declines with light intensity.
- Temperature coefficients (Q₁₀) were **2.5 for respiration** and **1.5 for photosynthesis** over the observed 19–30 °C range.

**Comparison with other streams:**
- Predicted rates exceed those reported for other small, non-thermal streams, which rarely exceed 7.5 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹ gross photosynthesis and 3.0 g O₂ m⁻² day⁻¹ respiration.
- Gross photosynthesis above 1.5 g O₂ m⁻² h⁻¹ was observed, exceeding the 1.0 g O₂ m⁻² h⁻¹ maximum predicted by [[Mclntire]] (1973) for such streams.

**Conclusions and limitations:**
- The authors conclude that Sycamore Creek and similar desert streams are naturally autotrophic and net exporters of organic matter, a notable exception to Hynes' (1969) generalization.
- Likely underestimation of rates arises from unstirred chamber water (which may reduce photosynthesis and respiration) and from open chamber bases, though the authors judged exchange with interstitial water negligible.
- Only summer was studied, so annual autotrophy was not demonstrated. The authors suggest autotrophic streams should occur where canopies are open, insolation is high and litter inputs are low.
