# Native Fish Stocking Records and Translocation at Cibola National Wildlife Refuge

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodon-macularius

## Executive Summary

This document is a short set of agency correspondence and a field memorandum concerning fish management at [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (Cibola NWR) on the [[Lower Colorado River]]. A federal biologist, Dennis Stone, was writing a [[Fish Management Plan]] for the refuge and needed historical records of [[Native Fish Stocking]] and of [[Nonnative Fish Stocking]]. The emails show that stocking records were hard to locate. The 2005 memo documents the translocation of 573 [[Desert Pupfish]] from a refuge pond to a Nature Conservancy preserve.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence (August 23 and 31, 2010):** [[Jeff Lantow]] of the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] referred Dennis Stone to Carol Pacey, Program Manager at Marsh & Associates, LLC. Pacey was described as holding the most comprehensive and accessible native fish stocking records for the Lower Colorado River (LCR).
- **Scope of the request:** Stone was interested in fish stocked in or near Cibola NWR, including [[High Levee Pond]], [[Pretty Water]], [[Hippy Hole]], and [[Cibola Lake]]. Stone already had complete information on High Levee Pond and [[Three-Fingers Lake]].
- **Agencies involved:** Stone reported that the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] had all been involved in this fishery.
- **Memo (10 May 2005):** Chuck Minckley, Project Coordinator at the Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Parker, AZ), issued a memo on desert pupfish activities at Cibola NWR.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stocking history as reported by Mitch Thorson (per Stone's email):**
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] were reportedly stocked historically into Pretty Water. No stocking records or recaptures of any native fish in that lake could be found.
  - Razorback suckers and bonytail were reportedly stocked in the Colorado River just above Cibola NWR. Stone found no records of these stockings.
  - Thorson stated that no nonnative fishes had been stocked in any refuge waters for years. Stone asked whether this was true and, if so, when nonnative stockings ended. No answer appears in the document.
- **Expertise:** Pacey noted that Jeannette Capenter (now Haegele) would know best about razorback sucker in the Cibola area.
- **Translocation context:** The 2005 memo describes a multi-agency project that took two years to develop. Its partners included the Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State University, [[The Nature Conservancy]], the Bureau of Reclamation (Phoenix), the Ecological Services Offices (Phoenix and Tucson), Cibola NWR, and the Arizona Fishery Resources Office.

## Key Findings

1. **Historical stocking records for native fish are incomplete.** Stone could find no records of razorback sucker or bonytail stockings in Pretty Water or in the Colorado River above Cibola NWR, even though Thorson reported that they occurred.
2. **Nonnative stocking status is unconfirmed.** Thorson's claim that nonnative fish have not been stocked in refuge waters for years remained unverified at the time of the emails, and the discontinuation date was unknown.
3. **Desert pupfish were translocated in May 2005.**
   - Source population: the refuge pupfish pond.
   - Sampling: 27 [[Minnow Trap]]s were set from 12–1 PM on 2 May 2005, baited with cat food, and fished overnight. They were checked from 8–9 AM on 3 May.
   - Count: 573 desert pupfish were captured, counted, and transported.
   - Destination: the [[San Pedro River Preserve]] near Tucson, where all fish were released by 3:30 PM on 3 May.
4. **Knowledge gaps remain.** Stone described the Fish Management Plan as difficult because of the number of agencies involved and his limited knowledge of the area. He depended on local experts such as Pacey, Lantow, Thorson, and Haegele.
