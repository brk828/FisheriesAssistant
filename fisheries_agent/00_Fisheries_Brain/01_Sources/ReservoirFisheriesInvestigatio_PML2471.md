# Reservoir Fisheries Investigations: Creel Census and Plankton Studies on Lake Powell (February 1967 - January 1968)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by Joseph L. Stone (Assistant Project Leader) and Ned L. Rathbun (Fisheries Biologist) for the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], covers the fifth segment of a six-year reservoir fisheries program on [[Lake Powell]], the reservoir created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The study combined a [[Creel Census]] at the four major access points with [[Aerial Survey]] counts and [[Plankton Studies]]. Fishing pressure was estimated at 26,967 angler trips, which produced 53,908 fish from 118,186 hours of effort. The catch was dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] (69 percent), and the overall success rate was 0.42 fish per hour, down from 0.52 in the previous period. The plankton survey found zooplankton and phytoplankton in quantities usable as fish food.

## Background & Description

The program is a cooperative venture between the Arizona Game and Fish Department and the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources|Utah Fish and Game Department]], funded under Section 9 of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]]. This segment covers the portion conducted by the Arizona department: fishing pressure and harvest, and plankton population studies.

The stated objectives were:
1. To estimate fishing pressure and harvest on Lake Powell.
2. To measure the relative abundance and seasonal succession of net plankton.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Access points and census design.** Four marinas were sampled: [[Wahweap Marina]] (six days per month), [[Hall's Crossing Marina]] (four days per month), [[Hite Marina]] (two days per month), and [[Bullfrog Marina]] (total census until April, then two days per month). Census days were split evenly between weekdays and weekends. Dock creel censuses checked anglers on returning boats, which was effective because the dock was the only point of exit for boats.

**Species present in the creel.** The report lists 12 species with relative abundance ratings. Abundant species were Largemouth Bass, Channel Catfish, Rainbow Trout, and Black Crappie. Common species included Bluegill, Green Sunfish, Carp, and Black Bullhead. Rare species included Brown Trout, Walleye, [[Bonytail]], and [[Colorado River Squawfish]].

**Baits.** Live bait (minnows, crayfish, and waterdogs) accounted for 66 percent of the Largemouth Bass catch and 64 percent of the Channel Catfish catch. Prepared baits such as corn and cheese took 45 percent of the Rainbow Trout catch.

**Plankton.** Samples came from twelve stations spaced at equal intervals along the reservoir, collected in 100-foot vertical hauls and preserved in five percent formalin. Counting used a [[Sedgwick-Rafter Counting Chamber]] under microscope magnification of 3.5x to 20x. Ten zooplankton genera and twenty phytoplankton genera were identified across 118 samples.

## Key Findings

**Fishing pressure and harvest**
- Creel-based estimate: 26,967 angler trips, 53,908 fish harvested, 118,186 hours of effort.
- Pressure by marina: Wahweap 64 percent, Hall's Crossing 25 percent, Hite 6 percent, Bullfrog 5 percent.
- Wahweap Marina: an estimated 24,355 angler trips produced 32,355 fish in 79,960 hours. This was a 40 percent decrease from the previous year.
- Hall's Crossing Marina: an estimated 6,595 angler trips produced 11,276 largemouth bass, 2,006 channel catfish, and 1,018 bluegill in 34,443 hours.
- Bullfrog Marina: an estimated 1,427 angler trips produced 7,595 largemouth bass, 529 channel catfish, 233 bluegill, and 69 other fish in 6,147 hours.
- Hite Marina: the sampling detail is only partly legible in the source text.

**Catch success**
- Overall success: 0.42 fish per hour, down from 0.52 in 1966-67. The report attributes the drop partly to a decline in the trout fishery.
- Success by marina: Wahweap 0.43, Hall's Crossing 0.44, Bullfrog 0.39 (reported as 0.3p, probably 0.39), Hite 0.46.
- Percent of anglers catching at least one fish: Hall's Crossing 67 percent, Wahweap 57 percent, Bullfrog 57 percent, Hite 47 percent.

**Creel composition**
- Largemouth Bass 69 percent, Channel Catfish 20 percent (up from 8 percent), Rainbow Trout 5 percent (down from 27 percent), Black Crappie 1 percent, other species 5 percent.
- Trout made up 27 percent of the fishery in 1966-67 and only 5 percent in this period, which the authors link to the decline of the trout fishery.

**Best areas by catch rate**
- Wahweap: Rock Creek (0.57 fish per hour).
- Hall's Crossing: Tapestry Wall (0.54).
- Bullfrog: Red Canyon (0.63).
- Hite: White Canyon (0.58).

**Aerial survey**
- Twelve aerial counts observed 1,126 boats and estimated 11,214 angler trips, which compares with 11,218 trips from creel data for the same period. The authors judged the method promising but not yet reliable enough to replace creel data.

**Plankton**
- The most prevalent zooplankton were Daphnia, Cyclops, and Diaptomus. The most prevalent phytoplankton were Asterionella, Staurastrum, and Fragilaria.
- Blooms: Asterionella dominated in March; Staurastrum and Fragilaria, along with Eudorina and Oscillatoria, appeared in the July to October period; a Fragilaria bloom covered the reservoir in January.
- Zooplankton density was highest in June and in warmer months, which the authors tied to higher temperatures.

**Recommendations**
1. Continue creel census at all access points.
2. Continue aerial surveys as a supplement to creel data.
3. Continue plankton studies, using stratified samples in specific areas to correlate with the distribution of trout.
4. Plant part of the 1968 trout stock from trucks, and compare the success of air and truck plants.
5. Plant catchable-size rainbow trout, since subcatchables and fingerlings did not show up in the creel during the period.
