# Annual Census of Cyprinodon macularius eremus at Quitobaquito Pond, 1989

#ichthyology #cyprinodontidae #desert-pupfish #population-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This report documents the 1989 annual census of [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*) conducted at [[Quitobaquito Pond]] in [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]] on September 27-28, 1989, under U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service endangered species permit PRT-676811. Using a depletion-based trapping method with 42 traps over six successive runs, researchers captured 2,420 individuals with only 12 mortalities. Statistical analysis estimated the total population at approximately 3,533 individuals (±281 variance), though researchers concluded this likely underestimates the true population. The census revealed a healthy age/size class distribution with no non-native competitors observed.

## Background & Description

[[Cyprinodon macularius eremus]] census work at [[Quitobaquito Pond]] began with research by Dr. Boyd E. Kynard in 1975 and continued almost yearly through 1981. Historical population estimates ranged from 1,800 individuals (1981 low) to 7,294 individuals (1975 high), with intervening years showing 3,000 to 6,700 individuals. However, the reliability of these figures remained questionable.

Early census methods (pre-1985) employed [[Mark-Recapture]] techniques that distinguished markable fish (≥22 mm length) from submarkable fish (<22 mm length), using left pectoral fin clips on markable-size individuals. This approach required extensive handling of each fish.

In 1985, contract research with the [[University of Arizona]] led to implementation of a new methodology. Dr. Bill Matter assisted in developing a [[Depletion Method]] that bases population estimates on successive trapping efforts. Fish from each trap effort are temporarily held in fiberglass screened holding tanks maintained within the pond, with total catch per trapping run plotted against accumulated catch to estimate total population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Quitobaquito Pond]] represents critical habitat for this endangered subspecies. The 1989 census employed 42 traps placed in optimal known habitat locations around the pond perimeter. Three holding tanks, each with approximately 27 cubic feet capacity, were positioned near the pond inlet to temporarily house captured fish.

Fish were held for up to 30 hours (those trapped during the first run), with trapping mortality limited to 12 individuals out of 2,420 captured—a mortality rate of approximately 0.5%. No non-native fish species or exotic competitors were observed during the census period, indicating relatively pristine habitat conditions.

Size distribution data from approximately 65 fish measured per run revealed a healthy grouping of age/size classes, with individuals ranging from 11-15 mm to 56-60 mm in length, demonstrating successful recruitment across multiple cohorts.

## Key Findings

**Population Estimates:**
- Total captured: 2,420 individuals over six trapping runs
- Captures 39% higher than 1987 and 40% higher than 1988, despite using fewer traps
- Statistical estimate using [[Ricker Method]] (1975): approximately 3,533 individuals
- Alternative estimate using [[Otis-Burnham-White-Anderson Method]] (1978): approximately 3,533 individuals with variance of ±281
- Researchers concluded actual population likely exceeds statistical estimates

**Trapping Methodology:**
- 42 traps deployed at optimal habitat locations
- Six successive runs at two-hour intervals over two days
- Run times: Day 1 (Sept 27): 08:30, 10:35, 12:35; Day 2 (Sept 28): 08:10, 10:10, 12:10
- Sequential runs yielded progressively fewer fish (except run four slightly exceeded run three)
- Day 1 total: 1,550 fish; Day 2 total: 870 fish

**Trap Performance:**
- Highest-yielding traps: Trap 23 (131 total), Trap 24 (134 total), Trap 25 (122 total), Trap 18 (120 total)
- Trap success varied considerably by location (range: 1-134 fish per trap)
- Individual run catches ranged from 502 to 870 fish

**Population Health Indicators:**
- Healthy age/size class distribution observed
- No non-native fish species detected
- No exotic competitors present
- Trapping mortality: 12 individuals (0.5% of captured fish)
- Population size within historical range (1,800-7,294 individuals)

**Conclusion:** Researchers determined that *C. macularius eremus* population at [[Quitobaquito Pond]] is in good condition, with all indicators suggesting a stable, healthy population despite potential underestimation by statistical methods.
