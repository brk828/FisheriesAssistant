## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 administrative memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service circulating meeting materials and an agenda for the **Management Group of the Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee**. It is not a primary scientific paper containing morphological or physiological fish data; rather, it is a programmatic record documenting the management, funding, and policy coordination underlying the **[[Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]]**, which targets the conservation and recovery of four federally listed big-river fishes native to the Upper Colorado River Basin: the **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]**, **[[Humpback Chub]]**, **[[Bonytail]]**, and **[[Razorback Sucker]]**. The memo outlines FY 1990 work plan priorities, water rights acquisition strategies, Section 7 consultation activities, and habitat/flow management actions (e.g., Ruedi Reservoir releases, Muddy Creek Reservoir MOU) intended to support recovery of these species in the **15-mile reach** of the Colorado River and other critical habitat segments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While explicit ecological data are absent, the memo implies key habitat and hydrological management contexts relevant to the ecological niche of the target species:

- **Critical habitat reach**: The **15-mile reach** of the Colorado River near Grand Junction, Colorado, is identified as a priority zone for maintaining instream flows critical to endangered fish survival and spawning.
- **Tributary and reservoir flow management**: Proposed water delivery agreements (e.g., **[[Muddy Creek Reservoir]]** MOU, **[[Ruedi Reservoir]]** release leases) reflect efforts to augment flows supporting fish habitat, particularly during low-flow periods affecting the mainstem Colorado River.
- **Tailwater habitat considerations**: A proposed (though not funded in this cycle) **Flaming Gorge tailwater trout study** highlights ongoing interest in the interaction between introduced sport fish (trout) and native endangered fish populations below dam tailwaters.
- **Basin-wide scope**: References to the **Yampa River**, **Little Snake River**, **White River**, and **Gunnison River** basins indicate the program's spatial scope spans multiple tributary systems feeding into the Upper Colorado River mainstem, each representing distinct habitat and water rights management units.
- **Water rights acquisition**: Activities such as the **Little Snake River water rights evaluation** and use of **Windy Gap** funds for water rights acquisition point to legal/hydrological mechanisms used to secure ecologically significant flow regimes for fish habitat protection.
- **Environmental contamination reference**: A mention of "Yampa oil spill studies" suggests ongoing assessment of anthropogenic contamination impacts on riverine fish habitat.

## Key Findings
- The **FY 1990 Recovery Program Work Plan** was developed by the Technical Group under an optimistic **$2.82 million** budget scenario, contingent on full participant funding and **$400K** in supplemental funds from the Colorado Water Conservation Board (CWCB) for a hatchery feasibility study and Yampa Basin reservoir feasibility study.
- The **Flaming Gorge tailwater trout study** ($66K) was explicitly omitted from the funded work plan despite Technical Group prioritization, indicating budget-driven de-prioritization of trout-native fish interaction research.
- **Kenney Reservoir** funding was excluded from the FY90 work plan, reflecting constrained programmatic scope for reservoir-related recovery actions.
- Legal and policy correspondence included in the materials (e.g., Secretary Lujan's June 23, 1989 letter to Governor Roy Romer, and EDF's June 22, 1989 letter to EPA regarding the **Clifton Dam**) underscore active interagency and stakeholder negotiation over water infrastructure affecting endangered fish habitat.
- A draft **MOU between the Colorado River Water Conservation District and the Service** proposed directing flows from **Muddy Creek Reservoir** to the **15-mile reach**, a direct habitat conservation measure for endangered big-river fishes.
- Section 7 consultation procedures, biological opinion updates, and an ad hoc sediment committee were active program components, reflecting ongoing regulatory compliance under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]** for water development projects impacting the four target fish species.
- The agenda reflects a multi-pronged recovery strategy combining **habitat flow management**, **water rights acquisition**, **regulatory compliance (Section 7)**, and **stakeholder coordination** (Implementation Committee, CWCB, Grand Valley water users) as the operative framework for the **[[Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]]**.
