# Cultural Resources Inventory of Portions of Sulphur Springs Valley and San Bernardino Valley, Cochise County, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #archaeology

## Executive Summary

This technical report documents a Class III (100% coverage) cultural resources survey of a 58-mile, 60- and 120-foot-wide transmission line right-of-way corridor, covering 436 acres, north and east of Douglas, Arizona. The survey was conducted by [[Cultural and Environmental Systems]] (C&ES) for [[Sulphur Springs Valley Electric Cooperative]] (SSVEC) in September and October 1990 under General Permit No. 89-68 from the [[Arizona State Museum]]. The records search found 15 previously recorded sites adjacent to or within the corridor. The field survey identified 20 new and previously recorded sites and 29 isolates. The document is not a fish or aquatic study; it contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, or water-chemistry data, and aquatic features are mentioned only as ephemeral drainages.

## Background & Description

**Project setting.** The corridor is divided into three phases: Phase A (6 miles, 60 feet wide), Phase B (47 miles, 60 and 120 feet wide), and Phase C with its alternate route (5 miles, 60 feet wide). It parallels [[Leslie Canyon Road]], Davis Road, [[Highway 80]], and [[Geronimo Trail]]. Legal descriptions cite nine U.S.G.S. 7.5-minute quadrangles, including Leslie Canyon, Douglas NE, College Peaks, and Guadalupe Spring.

**Project team.** Mary Lou Heuett served as Principal Investigator and Ronald P. Maldonado as Project Director. Field personnel included Susan A. Martin, Kelly J. Schroeder, and Richard A. Paun. The report was prepared for Jim Sober, Line Extension Supervisor, SSVEC, and is Technical Series No. 21.

**Methods.** Two teams walked parallel transects spaced 20 meters (65.6 feet) apart, a standard Class III interval (Teague 1982). Sites were defined as having more than 20 artifacts of multiple classes, 30 or more artifacts of one type, or a single feature. Other finds were recorded as isolates. Temporary numbers (e.g., SSVEC-1) were assigned, and permanent AZSITE numbers were obtained from the [[Arizona State Museum]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geology and landforms.** The corridor crosses the southeastern edge of the [[Sulphur Springs Valley]], the Swisshelm, Pedregosa, and Perilla mountains, and the [[San Bernardino Valley]] volcanic field, with bajadas of the Peloncillo and Guadalupe ranges. During the Pleistocene, [[Lake Cochise]] (about 20 miles long) occupied the Sulphur Springs Valley; the modern Willcox Playa is its remnant. The San Bernardino Valley is a graben with grass-covered cinder cones and maar craters.

**Hydrology.** The drainages are ephemeral, wet-weather washes and secondary streams, including Leslie Creek, Mud Springs Creek, Hog Canyon drainage, Wildcat Wash, Silver Creek, and Cottonwood Creek. Springs and artesian lakes occur in both valleys. Seven secondary drainages in the San Bernardino Valley converge to form the Rio San Bernardino in Sonora, Mexico. Riparian vegetation is present only near standing water in Hog Canyon.

**Flora.** The project lies in the Apachian Biotic Province. Chihuahuan desertscrub dominated by tarbush, creosote bush, sandpaper bush, and whitethorn is intermixed with semidesert grassland of bunch grasses and grama species. Table 1 lists about 25 plant taxa, including agave, cholla, mesquite, ocotillo, and juniper.

**Fauna.** The area is in the Eastern Plains Faunal Area. Animals directly observed during the survey were:
- White-tailed deer (4)
- Western diamondback rattlesnake (5)
- Black-tailed jackrabbit (3)
- Desert cottontail (3)
- Desert tortoise (2)
- Turkey vulture (5)
- Mourning dove and white-winged dove (no counts given)

Coyote scat, deer tracks, woodrat nests, and a mountain lion track were also noted. No fish were reported.

**Soils.** Twelve soil associations were mapped (U.S.D.A. Soil Conservation Service 1971), including Karro, McAllister, Guest, White House-Tubac-Forrest, Bonita-Sontag, Kimbrough-Cave, Graham-Lampshire, Mabray, Krentz, Luzena-Faraway, and Tortugas. Soil types strongly shaped where resources, sites, and ranches were located.

## Key Findings

**Cultural resources recorded**
- Records search: 15 previously recorded sites adjacent to or within the right-of-way.
- Field survey: 7 historic sites, 13 prehistoric sites, and 29 isolates. Site AZ FF:12:27 was located adjacent to the corridor.
- Cultures represented: Hispanic, Anglo, Archaic, Mogollon, and Salado.
- All recorded sites except AZ FF:6:22 are considered eligible for the [[National Register of Historic Places]] under criterion "d."

**Management recommendations**
- Avoidance of all sites through project redesign, right-of-way movement, or protection measures.
- Testing (archival research, oral interviews, mapping, photo-documentation) for any site affected directly or indirectly.
- Data recovery at sites where testing warrants it.
- Isolates: no additional fieldwork recommended.
- AZ FF:11:71 (historic cemetery): if avoidance fails, exhume and relocate burials to an alternate cemetery.

**Notable sites**
- [[AZ FF:11:17]]: a multicomponent site (Archaic, Salado, historic) with a bedrock milling station (SSVEC-6) and burials exposed in a cutbank, eligible for listing.
- [[AZ FF:11:4]]: a 1905–1916 historic trash dump bisected by the Phase B corridor.
- [[AZ FF:11:70]]: a segment of the [[Arizona and Southeastern Railroad]] grade and trash scatter, with a recommendation to nominate the entire grade.
- [[AZ FF:10:19]]: the Wells Family Cemetery, still in use, for which avoidance is highly recommended.
- [[AZ FF:6:20]]: a silver mine complex in the Swisshelm Mountains, possibly the Hiefner Mine.

**Regional history highlights**
- Clovis-period sites were recorded in the San Bernardino Valley.
- [[Spanish Colonial Period]] contact, the Apache presence, and the [[San Bernardino Land Grant]] (73,240 acres, granted 1822, abandoned 1831).
- [[Mormon Battalion]] passed through the San Bernardino Valley in December 1846.
- [[John Slaughter]] leased the grant in 1880; the Slaughter Ranch operated until 1921.
- The town of Douglas was founded after the 1901 [[Phelps Dodge]] smelter site was selected.

**Prior research**
- Fifteen prior surveys were identified; three were negative (1981-071, 1985-67, 1989-10).
- Twelve positive surveys were recorded, documenting Archaic, Mogollon, and Salado settlement.
- The report notes that systematic excavation has been limited in the region.

**Recovery of historic context**
- Historic windmills (1917–1950) were mostly made by the [[Aeromotor Company]] of Chicago.
- The Cottonwood Creek School and Slaughter Ranch school have been removed; oral histories are recommended.
