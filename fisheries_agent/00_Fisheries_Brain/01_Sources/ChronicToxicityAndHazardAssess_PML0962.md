# Chronic Toxicity and Hazard Assessment of an Inorganic Mixture Simulating Irrigation Drainwater to Razorback Sucker and Bonytail

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Hamilton et al. (2000)]] tested the chronic effects of a nine-element inorganic mixture, designed to mimic [[Irrigation Drainage]] from Ashley Creek, on larval [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], two endangered fishes of the [[Middle Green River]] in Utah. Two 90-day exposures used swim-up larvae held in reconstituted Green River water at five multiples (1X, 2X, 4X, 8X, 16X) of the measured environmental concentration (X). Adverse effects on survival, growth, swimming performance and behavior occurred at or near environmental concentrations. The authors concluded that a high hazard exists for these endangered fish in the Ashley Creek area, and that irrigation-derived inorganics may contribute to their decline.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Irrigation drainage monitoring under the [[National Irrigation Water Quality Program]] (NIWQP) identified the middle Green River basin as a site of concern. Ashley Creek, a tributary near Stewart Lake and Ouray NWR, was found to carry elevated boron, selenium and zinc.
- **Focal species:** Both species are endangered. Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and Bonytail (*Gila elegans*) broodstock came from Lake Mohave, and eggs were obtained from Dexter National Fish Hatchery, New Mexico. Larvae were reared at the Columbia Environmental Research Center in Yankton, South Dakota.
- **Toxicant mixture:** Nine elements at measured environmental concentrations at the mouth of Ashley Creek: arsenic 2 µg/L, boron 630 µg/L, copper 10 µg/L, molybdenum 5 µg/L, selenate 51 µg/L, selenite 8 µg/L, uranium 33 µg/L, vanadium 2 µg/L and zinc 20 µg/L.
- **Previous work:** Earlier acute toxicity tests with the same mixture and related studies on selenium in the [[Colorado River Basin]] and [[San Juan River]] informed the chronic design.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat conditions:** Reconstituted water matched the average Green River chemistry (hardness 202 mg/L as CaCO3, alkalinity 107 mg/L, pH about 8.0, calcium 48 mg/L, sulfate 159 mg/L). Water temperature was held at 25 ± 2 °C.
- **Threats to endangered fishes:** The authors note that dams, altered flows, turbidity changes and nonnative species have contributed to declines in the upper Colorado River basin. Irrigation-related contamination is proposed as an additional factor in the middle Green River.
- **Selenium bioaccumulation:** Larval razorback sucker and bonytail accumulated copper, selenium and zinc in a concentration-dependent manner. Whole-body residues of selenium in adverse-effect fish (5.9 to 10.8 µg/g) overlapped with thresholds reported for other fishes.
- **Growth and fitness:** Reduced size is linked to lower swimming ability, higher predation risk, less efficient feeding and poorer overwinter survival.

## Key Findings

- **Survival:** Razorback sucker survival fell after 60 and 90 days at 8X and 16X. Bonytail survival declined only at 16X, after 30 days.
- **Growth:** Razorback sucker growth was reduced at 4X and higher, with the most sensitive effect at 4X after 90 days. Bonytail growth was reduced at 4X and higher for weight and at 8X and 16X for total length. Weight was the more sensitive growth endpoint.
- **Behavior:** Razorback sucker at 8X and 16X became hyperactive, darker in colour and smaller, with less feeding activity. Bonytail at 16X became lethargic, and some fish were unresponsive to disturbance.
- **Swimming performance:** Critical swimming speed fell at 8X after 60 and 90 days for razorback sucker, and at 8X and 16X for bonytail.
- **Residues:** Whole-body copper, selenium and zinc increased with exposure concentration. Regulation (levelling off) occurred at lower treatments for both species, while 16X residues of selenium and zinc did not level off.
- **Hazard ratio:** The lowest adverse-effect treatments (4X for razorback sucker, 8X for bonytail) are within a factor of 10 of the 1X environmental concentration. The authors judged this a high hazard.
- **Acute-chronic ratios:** Razorback sucker ratio 5.8; bonytail ratio 2.0. Both are low, indicating a small margin between acute and chronic toxicity.
- **Conclusion:** Selenium was the element of greatest concern in the NIWQP work, but copper, selenium and zinc together probably contributed to the toxic effects observed. Irrigation-related inorganics may be contributing to the decline of endangered fish in the middle Green River.
