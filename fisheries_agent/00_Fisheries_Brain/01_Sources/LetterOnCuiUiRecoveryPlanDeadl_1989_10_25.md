## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the Cui-ui Recovery Team Leader to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Regional Director, addressing the management timeline for the [[Cui-ui]] Recovery Plan. The letter is not a primary scientific data source but provides critical context on the conservation status, active threats, and the institutional/scientific process surrounding the species. It identifies **hybridization** as an urgent, unresolved threat to the species that must take priority over completing the formal recovery plan. It requests a one-year extension of the recovery plan deadline (from January 31, 1990, to January 31, 1991) to allow time to monitor the 1990 spawning run and consult multidisciplinary experts.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body plan) are provided in this letter. However, contextual notes relevant to taxonomic identity include:
- The [[Cui-ui]] is a member of the [[Catostomidae]] (sucker) family.
- The central conservation concern discussed is **hybridization**, implying morphological/genetic introgression risk with a sympatric species, most likely the [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*), a known co-occurring catostomid in the Pyramid Lake system.
- Determining/monitoring hybrid status is flagged as requiring specialized expertise in **genetics**, suggesting that morphological identification alone is insufficient to distinguish pure Cui-ui from hybrid individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[Cui-ui]] are endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, an isolated remnant of the ancient Pleistocene Lake Lahontan.
- **Reproductive Ecology**: The species undertakes an annual **spawning run**, referenced here as the "1990 cui-ui spawning run," indicating a lacustrine-adfluvial life history (migrating from the lake into tributary streams, historically the Truckee River, to spawn).
- **Threat Interaction**: The timing of the recovery plan is explicitly tied to monitoring this spawning run, indicating that hybridization events or risks are most likely to be observed/managed during this reproductive migration period.
- **Multidisciplinary Management Context**: The letter notes recovery planning depends on integrating expertise from **population dynamics, genetics, hydrology, modeling, and fishery management**, reflecting the complex interplay of lake hydrology (water levels/diversions) and biological factors affecting the species' persistence — issues long associated with Pyramid Lake's altered inflow from the Truckee River.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Timeline Extension**: The Cui-ui Recovery Team formally requested that the draft Recovery Plan deadline be moved from **January 31, 1990** to **January 31, 1991**, citing the need to prioritize the hybridization issue.
- **Hybridization as Priority Threat**: The Team determined that resolving the [[Cui-ui]] hybridization issue must occur *before* finalizing other recovery actions, as those actions are considered dependent on the outcome of the hybridization investigation. This aligns with concerns raised in a companion document, the [[Cui-ui Hybridization Position Letter]] (dated October 24, 1989).
- **Monitoring Timeframe**: An estimated **6 to 8 months** would be needed to monitor and direct efforts related to hybrids during the 1990 spawning run.
- **Expert Consultation Timeline**: Interviews/consultations with outside experts across five disciplines (population dynamics, genetics, hydrology, modeling, fishery management) were not expected to conclude before **Fall 1990**.
- **Administrative/Chain of Command Issue**: The letter also surfaces an organizational ambiguity within USFWS regarding whether the [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] should correspond directly with the Regional Director (Marvin Plenert) or through an intermediary (Robert P. Smith, Assistant Regional Director), highlighting the institutional structure supporting endangered species recovery efforts.
- **Key Personnel**: Thomas A. Strekal (Cui-ui Recovery Team Leader), Marvin Plenert (USFWS Regional Director, Portland), Chester Buchanan (Service liaison to the Team), Robert P. Smith (Assistant Regional Director, Portland).
