# O'Donnell Canyon Fish Sampling, April 18–19, 2007

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

A fishery survey of perennial waters in [[O'Donnell Canyon]] (Santa Cruz County, Arizona) was conducted April 18–19, 2007, by the Bureau of Reclamation, Marsh and Associates, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and the Bureau of Land Management. The stream had been chemically renovated in 2002 to remove invasive [[Green Sunfish]], which threatened the endangered [[Gila Chub]] and other natives. The survey supported a proposal to stabilize existing retention structures, or build a new one, so they function as a [[Fish Barrier]]. Two green sunfish were found below the [[Lower Water Retention Structure]], indicating that a downstream reinvasion route remains open. The authors recommend a fish barrier, augmentation of native populations with several hundred additional individuals, and wide dispersal of repatriates.

## Background & Description

The perennial reach of O'Donnell Canyon was renovated with a chemical treatment in 2002 to eliminate nonnative green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*). Two water-retention structures near the downstream end of the perennial water had served as fish barriers since construction in the 1950s (date uncertain), but lateral erosion now threatens their failure. Failure could allow nonnative fishes to move upstream and undermine the renovated native fishery.

The 2007 survey had two aims: a comprehensive survey of the stream since the 2002 renovation, and sampling immediately below the lower retention structure, where two fish suspected to be green sunfish had been seen on March 26, 2007.

**Methods**

- **Electrofishing:** A [[Backpack Electrofisher]] (Smith-Root, Inc., Model 12B) was used in the pool immediately below the lower structure on the mornings of April 18 and 19.
- **Passive gear:** Baited traps were set upstream of the structure to just downstream of the O'Donnell Ciénega. The gear included 15 standard wire minnow traps, 9 single-throat hoop nets, 10 double-throat hoop nets, 4 collapsible square minnow traps, 6 oval collapsible turtle traps, and 11 double-throat hoop nets. Gear was set in pools from above the lower structure to about 300 m above the [[The Nature Conservancy]] grade control structure. Gear was set in the afternoon of April 18 and retrieved the morning of April 19.
- **Turtle safety:** Traps large enough to capture [[Sonora Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense*) were partly set above the water line to allow air access.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey area includes a [[Grade Control Structure]] that divides the reach into a downstream section below the lower retention structure and an upstream section of deep, slow ciénega pools. Native fishes, including [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), were salvaged before the 2002 renovation and repatriated upstream of the grade control structure. Flooding since the renovation may have moved young fish downstream. Both native fish species are believed to have reproduced since 2002, which would support [[Larval Fish Dispersal]].

The [[Northern Crayfish]] (*Orconectes virilus*) was abundant, with several hundred captured upstream and downstream of the grade control structure.

## Key Findings

**Green sunfish presence below the barrier**
- Two green sunfish were first observed on March 26, 2007, in the pool below the lower retention structure, and were seen again on April 18.
- One fish was captured by electrofishing and dip net on April 18. The other was captured, photographed, and fixed on April 19.
- Total lengths were 109 mm (first value uncertain from scan) and 140 mm. Vouchers were preserved in 70% ethanol and deposited at the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]].

**Passive gear results**
- Of 53 passive sampling gears, 25 were set downstream of the grade control structure (about 450 net-hours) and 28 upstream (about 475 net-hours).
- No fish were captured in the downstream nets. Only nine nets upstream captured fish.
- Total catch was 15 [[Sonora Sucker]] and 11 [[Gila Chub]]. One of each species was retained as a museum voucher.
- Gila chub total lengths ranged from 130 to 229 mm. Sonora sucker total lengths ranged from 133 to 283 mm.
- About one dozen [[Sonora Mud Turtle]] and several hundred [[Northern Crayfish]] were captured upstream and downstream.

**Native fish status and dispersal**
- Gila chub and Sonora sucker do not appear to have dispersed downstream, although size distributions indicate both have reproduced since 2002.
- Deep, slow ciénega pools and the grade control structure may have limited downstream dispersal.
- Salvage before the 2002 renovation was limited to several dozen individuals per species, plus about a dozen dead individuals of each. The authors describe these numbers as well below recommended founding population sizes (Soulé 1980; Rieman and Allendorf 2001).
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*, endangered) were recorded in O'Donnell Canyon after 2002 but not during this survey.

**Management recommendations**
1. Augment both native populations with several hundred additional individuals from the nearest appropriate geographic source stock.
2. Disperse new repatriates widely, including currently unoccupied downstream reaches.
3. Augment longfin dace and Gila topminnow from appropriate source stocks.
4. Install or stabilize a [[Fish Barrier]] on the lower stream to prevent reinvasion by nonnative fishes from downstream.

**Renovation outcome**
- The 2002 renovation appears to have removed green sunfish from the treated reach above the lower structure, since none were detected in later surveys.
- The capture of two green sunfish below the lower structure shows that the barrier is still needed.
