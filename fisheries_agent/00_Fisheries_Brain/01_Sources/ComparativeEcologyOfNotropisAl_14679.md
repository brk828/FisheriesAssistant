# Comparative Ecology of *Notropis albeolus*, *N. ardens* and *N. cerasinus*

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Notropis #FreshwaterEcology #NichePartitioning

## Executive Summary
This study examines resource partitioning among three syntopic minnow species — [[Notropis albeolus]] (white shiner), [[Notropis ardens]] (rosefin shiner), and [[Notropis cerasinus]] (crescent shiner) — coexisting in the upper [[Roanoke River]] drainage, Virginia. Over a full annual cycle (April 1978–March 1979), researchers quantified microhabitat use, vertical water-column position, diet composition, and diurnal feeding chronology to test hypotheses of [[Resource Partitioning]] versus morphological preadaptation as drivers of coexistence. All three species primarily occupied pool habitats, fed diurnally on aquatic insect larvae and terrestrial insects, and showed high horizontal microhabitat overlap. The key axis of ecological differentiation was **vertical position in the water column**: *N. ardens* (subgenus [[Lythrurus]]) occupied midwater/surface zones and was ecologically most distinct from the two [[Luxilus]] species (*N. albeolus*, *N. cerasinus*), which were more benthically oriented and ecologically very similar to one another, differing only subtly by vertical position. These findings support congruence between phylogenetic relatedness (subgeneric grouping) and ecological similarity, consistent with a morphological-preadaptation model of niche structure (per Mendelson, 1975) rather than pure competitive displacement, though character displacement was also invoked for *N. albeolus*/*N. cerasinus* pairs.

## Background & Description
- **Notropis ardens (Lythrurus)**: streamlined, fusiform body; posteriorly positioned dorsal fin — morphology characteristic of subgenus [[Lythrurus]]; associated with midwater and swift-current microhabitat use.
- **Notropis albeolus & N. cerasinus (Luxilus)**: deep-bodied, slab-sided body form typical of subgenus [[Luxilus]]; less streamlined than *Lythrurus* species; associated with more benthic habitat use.
- **Intraspecific geographic variation in N. albeolus**: Northern populations (sympatric with *N. cerasinus*, e.g., Roanoke drainage) are more slim-bodied/streamlined than southern populations (Neuse, Tar, Cape Fear drainages, NC), where *N. albeolus* is the sole *Luxilus* representative and is deeper-bodied. Differences also include jaw angle and mouth/jaw size, pronounced enough that Gilbert (1964) recognized separate races.
- Menzel (1976) proposed the streamlined northern *N. albeolus* body form arose via **character displacement** from historical interaction with *N. cerasinus* — supported by this study's finding of near-complete but not total ecological overlap between the two.
- Hybridization noted in literature: *N. cerasinus* × *N. cornutus* hybrid documented (Menzel, 1977), relevant to *N. albeolus* evolutionary history.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat Use (Horizontal)
- Total of 10,231 Notropis collected across 10 stations; all three species common/abundant at most sites except spring-branch tributary Stations 9–10.
- Strong positive co-occurrence among all species pairs (χ² tests, p<0.01).
- Largest numbers occurred at fifth-order mainstream sites, low gradient, width >10 m (see [[Table 1]] data below).
- All three species primarily used **pool habitats** in both warm and cold seasons; high horizontal microhabitat overlap (>0.87 in most comparisons — see [[Niche Overlap]] section).
- *N. ardens* used **channel habitat** more than the other two and had the widest habitat breadth (B = 2.06 overall vs. 1.51–1.58 for the Luxilus pair).
- Peak occurrence at current speeds of 1–25 cm/sec for all species; *N. ardens* more frequent at velocities >25 cm/sec than the other two.
- **Winter (cold-season) shift**: all species restricted to deep pools at Stations 3, 4, 5; marked reduction in habitat breadth and stomach fullness — likely due to reduced food availability, restricted foraging space, and/or lowered metabolic demand at cold temperatures.

### Vertical Microhabitat Segregation (Water Column)
Primary axis of ecological differentiation, assessed via underwater viewer observations (Sept 1978, Jan 1979, Apr 1979):
- **N. cerasinus**: predominantly benthic — 80% (Sept, n=277) and 66% (Apr, n=493) within 8 cm of substrate.
- **N. albeolus**: intermediate — ~53–54% midwater, ~43–46% near bottom.
- **N. ardens**: predominantly midwater — 82% midwater (Apr, n=152), only 15% near bottom.
- This vertical spacing reduced niche overlap specifically between *N. ardens* and *N. cerasinus*.

### Diet / Trophic Ecology
- 264 stomachs analyzed (adults, spring/summer/autumn, Stations 1 & 5) + 90 additional winter stomachs (Stations 3–4); 22 food item categories identified.
- Method: occurrence-based food analysis (Hynes, 1950) due to macerated gut contents precluding item counts.
- All three species: **omnivorous**, diurnal feeders, no single dominant food item; diet breadth values high and similar across species.
- Most frequent items: aquatic insect larvae ([[Chironomidae]], [[Ephemeroptera]], [[Odonata]], [[Hydropsyche]]), terrestrial insects, and algae.
- **N. ardens** (midwater feeder) consumed more terrestrial insects (surface-derived) than the benthic species.
- **N. albeolus & N. cerasinus** (more benthic) consumed more sand and benthic invertebrates (Chironomidae, Ephemeroptera, Hydropsyche) — items that enter the water column mainly at night via drift, reinforcing that these species feed near substrate during daylight.
- Winter: marked reduction in mean stomach fullness across all species, though fish remained in good physical condition (not emaciated).

### Feeding Chronology
- All three species are **strictly diurnal feeders**: feeding began near dawn, stomach fullness increased through the day, and feeding ceased after sunset (stomach fullness dropped sharply at 2400 h and 0300 h samples).
- Gastric evacuation experiments (20°C): food required ~4 hours to pass through the stomach, meaning ingestion times could precede capture by up to 4 hours.
- Overlap in feeding time among all species pairs was very high (>0.9 in most cases).

### Niche Overlap Summary (Pianka's index)
- **N. ardens vs. N. albeolus**: moderate overall segregation — food use differed moderately year-round; some vertical spatial segregation (N. albeolus lower in water column).
- **N. ardens vs. N. cerasinus**: greatest overall ecological difference of the three pairs — driven by strong vertical segregation plus moderate dietary differences across all seasons.
- **N. albeolus vs. N. cerasinus**: highest overlap of all pairs — near-identical horizontal microhabitat, diet, and feeding time; only subtle vertical separation (N. cerasinus more substrate-oriented).
- Overlap values >0.75 considered "high" per Pianka & Pianka (1976) convention.

## Key Findings
1. **Vertical stratification, not horizontal habitat or diet, is the primary axis of ecological segregation** among these three coexisting Notropis species — contrasting with many other cyprinid studies where diet or horizontal microhabitat differed more strongly.
2. *N. ardens* (subgenus Lythrurus) is ecologically distinct from the two Luxilus species in both vertical position (midwater/surface) and diet (more terrestrial insects), consistent with its streamlined morphology adapted for swifter, more open-water habitats.
3. *N. albeolus* and *N. cerasinus* (both subgenus Luxilus) are ecologically very similar, overlapping strongly in horizontal habitat, diet, and feeding schedule, with only minor vertical differentiation (*N. cerasinus* more benthic).
4. Ecological similarity/differences among the three species pairs are **congruent with their phylogenetic (subgeneric) relationships**, supporting [[Mendelson's Morphological Preadaptation Hypothesis]] — i.e., resource use patterns reflect inherited body plan/morphology rather than solely being products of interspecific competitive interaction.
5. Geographic variation in *N. albeolus* body form (streamlined in the north where sympatric with *N. cerasinus*, deep-bodied in the south where allopatric) supports a **character displacement** explanation (Menzel, 1976) for subtle morphological/ecological divergence between *N. albeolus* and *N. cerasinus* specifically.
6. All three species undergo pronounced **seasonal habitat compression** in winter, aggregating in deep mainstream pools and reducing feeding activity — a pattern typical of many Roanoke River fishes.
7. This study contributes comparative data supporting broader patterns of **vertical microhabitat segregation among coexisting Notropis species** documented elsewhere (Moyle 1973; Baker & Ross 1981), and specifically corroborates prior single-species accounts of *N. ardens* as a midwater/surface feeder (Meredith & Schwartz, 1959) and *N. cerasinus* as bottom-oriented (Schwartz & Dutcher, 1962).
