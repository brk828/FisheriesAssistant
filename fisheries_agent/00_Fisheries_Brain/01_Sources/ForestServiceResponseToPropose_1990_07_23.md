# Forest Service Response to Proposed Endangered Listing of Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1990 letter from the USDA Forest Service (Southwestern Region) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service responds to the proposed listing of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The Forest Service concurs with the endangered designation, citing the historic disappearance of natural populations from the Salt, Verde, and Gila River systems by the early 1950s. The document details a decade-long, large-scale hatchery stocking program (1981–1991) involving over 13 million hatchery-raised razorback suckers, while noting that reestablishment of viable, self-sustaining populations had not been documented as of 1990. The letter also recommends that reintroduced populations be classified as "experimental" and "non-essential" under 1982 ESA amendments, rather than receiving full endangered status protections.

## Background & Description
The source document does not provide direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, fin ray counts, or the diagnostic nuchal "razorback" keel typically associated with this species). ## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historic Range**: Common in the mainstream channels and reservoirs of the [[Salt River]], [[Verde River]], and [[Gila River]] until the early 1950s.
- **Habitat Type**: Large-river mainstream and reservoir habitats; historic use of smaller tributaries to these rivers is undocumented ("little is known").
- **Current Status (as of 1990)**: No natural (wild, self-sustaining) populations remained in the Salt, Verde, or Gila rivers.
- **Habitat Alteration**: Historic habitat is now fragmented by dams and extensive reaches of dry streambed, geographically isolating potential reintroduction sites from any naturally occurring populations elsewhere in the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Co-occurring Species / Threats**: Most former habitat waters are actively managed for sport fisheries and are stocked with, or support resident populations of, exotic gamefish — implying competitive/predatory pressure from non-native species.
- **Land Management Context**: Many reintroduction sites are located on lands administered by the [[National Forest System]] in Arizona; land managers assert that Best Management Practices (BMPs) are sufficient to protect razorback sucker habitat from current/proposed land-use activities.

## Key Findings
- **Population Decline Timeline**: Razorback sucker was common in the Salt, Verde, and Gila rivers until the early 1950s; the cause of the population collapse is described as "unknown."
- **Stocking Program Scale**: Since 1981, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] cooperatively stocked **over 13 million hatchery-raised razorback suckers** into the Salt, Verde, and Gila rivers and their tributaries.
- **Program Duration**: The stocking effort was designed to run for approximately 10 years (initiated 1981), with an original intent to use reintroduction as an alternative to formal ESA listing.
- **Monitoring Gaps**: Annual monitoring of stocked waters occurred, but no final report or interim analyses had been made publicly available as of the letter's date; reestablishment of viable populations had **not** been documented.
- **Critical Habitat Designation**: The Forest Service declined to propose critical habitat on National Forest System lands, citing insufficient biological knowledge of the species' historic habitat use and current habitat requirements.
- **Experimental Population Recommendation**: The Forest Service recommended that reintroduced populations in the Gila, Salt, and Verde systems be designated as "[[Experimental Population|experimental]]" and "non-essential" under the 1982 ESA amendments, based on three justifications:
  1. Absence from these rivers for several decades due to unknown causes.
  2. Complete geographic isolation (via dams and dry streambeds) from naturally occurring populations.
  3. An ongoing decade-long hatchery-based reestablishment program likely to continue.
- **Regulatory Implication**: Even under "experimental/non-essential" designation, the Forest Service acknowledged a continuing obligation to "confer" with the Fish and Wildlife Service on activities potentially affecting the species or its habitat, a lesser requirement than the full "consultation" mandate for fully protected endangered populations.
- **Anthropogenic Pressure**: Sport fishery management practices (stocking of exotic gamefish) in former razorback sucker habitat are identified as an ongoing ecological factor in the rivers where reintroduction was occurring.
