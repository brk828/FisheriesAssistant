# Systematik und Verbreitung von Xiphophorus: Ecological and Morphological Contributions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1967 study by Claus Dieter Zander, published in the *Mitteilungen aus dem Hamburger Zoologischen Museum und Institut*, reports ecological observations from a 1964 Mexican collecting expedition and comparative morphological work on the livebearing genus [[Xiphophorus]] (swordtails and platies). Fieldwork covered 15 sites in the [[Rio Panuco]] system, Veracruz and Oaxaca, with particular focus on [[Xiphophorus Montezumae]], [[Xiphophorus Pygmaeus]] and [[Xiphophorus Helleri]]. The author concludes that the [[Clemenciae Problem]] is resolved in favour of conspecificity with *X. helleri*, that the subspecies divisions within *X. helleri* should be abolished, and that Rosen's (1960) species groups should be replaced by the older split between platies and swordtails based on the caudal fin extension.

## Background & Description

The genus was consolidated by Gordon & Rosen (1951), merging *Xiphophorus* (swordtails) and *Platypoecilus* (platies). Males transform the anal fin into a [[Gonopodium]] used to transfer spermatozeugmata. Females store sperm, so one mating can supply several broods after a gestation of about four weeks. The paper treats platies (*X. gordoni*, *couchianus*, *xiphidium*, *variatus*, *maculatus*, *milleri*) and swordtails (*montezumae*, *pygmaeus*, *helleri*, *clemenciae*) as the main taxa, and reviews the distribution of each, with subspecies, in two distribution maps.

The genus occupies only Middle American river systems draining to the Atlantic. The northernmost members, *X. gordoni* and *X. couchianus*, occur in Rio Grande systems, while *X. helleri guentheri* reaches as far south as Honduras. Several species overlap in range, giving sympatry, and the author lists sites where two or three species occur together.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Field stations (numbered 1–15) described water depth, width, current, substrate, vegetation and temperature (typically 23–28 °C). Key observations include:

- **Rio Panuco system (sites 1–7):** *X. montezumae* favours slow, vegetated side pools; *X. pygmaeus* occupies faster water along steep banks among plants; *X. variatus* prefers still water with dense vegetation. Fish fauna co-occurring included [[Gambusia Vittata]], [[Mollienisia Sphenops]], [[Astyanax]] and cichlids.
- **Catemaco and Isthmus sites (8–15):** *X. helleri* proved very euryoecious, occurring from 1500 m at Jalapa (the highest recorded *Xiphophorus* site) down to about 80 m in the Rio Sarabia area, including isolated, crowded, warm drying pools.
- **Pool survey (site 13):** a quantitative sample yielded 1 *Symbranchus marmoratus*, 1 *Astyanax fasciatus*, 20 *Rhamdia guatemalensis*, 3 *Cichlasoma octofasciatum*, 3 *Rivulus godmani*, 8 *Poeciliopsis gracilis*, 93 *Pseudoxiphophorus bimaculatus*, 85 *M. sphenops* and 88 *X. helleri*.

The author argues that ecological barriers (differing preferences for standing or flowing water and microhabitat partitioning), ethological barriers (female preference) and gametic barriers (conspecific sperm precedence) together achieve sexual isolation in sympatric species.

## Key Findings

**Ecology**
- *X. montezumae*, *X. pygmaeus* and *X. variatus* co-occur in the Rio Axtla. The author concludes they must be regarded as ecologically sympatric, contradicting Gordon (1953).
- *X. variatus* is eurytopic, occurring in both still and flowing water.
- *X. milleri* lives in slightly turbid, moderately flowing water and is sympatric with *X. helleri* at Lake Catemaco.

**Morphology**
- *Colour:* In *X. montezumae*, the cortezi form has one dark-brown zigzag band, whereas montezumae populations show several lines. Colour is polymorphic even within populations. Some *X. pygmaeus* populations contain yellow males carrying carotenoid pigment; 6 yellow males were found in the combined sample.
- *Dorsal fin rays (DFZ):* Montezumae populations did not differ significantly (means 12.34–12.56). *X. pygmaeus* populations differed strongly (PN 12.25, PC 13.76, PP 11.52; all p < 0.0002). The Sarabia (HS) *X. helleri* population has a significantly lower DFZ (12.88) than other populations, and the author notes that Rosen's Sarabia-area values were 12.82.
- *Gonopodium:* Variation within populations often equals variation between them in the distal teeth and claw. *X. milleri* has a claw in only about half of males and occupies an intermediate position between platies and *X. montezumae*.
- *Sword index:* In *X. helleri*, the HL population has the longest sword (mean 0.446). The HS population has the shortest (mean 0.212), not significantly different from the other short-sworded samples in the reduction experiments.

**Clemenciae Problem**
- The HS population (Sarabia) is identified with *X. clemenciae* Alvarez and Rosen. Three of four adult males had a clemenciae-type gonopodium, but 23 further males from the same sites had helleri-type gonopodia.
- The author argues that the three atypical gonopodia do not justify a separate species, and that environmental effects (crowding, temperature) may have produced them.

**Experimental work on sword length**
- Offspring of one HS female and two males differed significantly (1190/1189: mean 0.188; 1190/1250: mean 0.261; p < 0.0002).
- Crowding (less than 11 fish per tank) and higher temperature (28–29 °C) each reduced the sword index. Both together produced the lowest value (0.198). The author concludes that sword length is genetically fixed but also environmentally modified, and is probably polygenic.

**Systematic conclusions**
- A new *X. pygmaeus* population from the Rio Coy is assigned to subspecies *nigrensis*.
- Subspecies divisions within *X. helleri* should be abolished, and the group should be treated as populations of one polymorphic species.
- *X. clemenciae* is not a valid species but a population of *X. helleri*.
- Rosen's species groups should be abandoned. The older platy and swordtail division, based on the caudal fin extension, is recommended instead.

**Dispersal**
- The author proposes that the Rio Panuco was the centre of origin, with a stillwater platy ancestor spreading north and south, and a running-water form giving rise to the swordtails. The Gordon/Rosen view is accepted in outline, with corrections to the placement of several taxa.
