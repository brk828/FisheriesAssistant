# Evaluation of commercial fishing by use of a questionnaire

## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by the North Dakota Game and Fish Department, evaluated the utility of a mail-questionnaire survey method for quantifying commercial fishing yields on the [[Missouri River]] and [[Yellowstone River]] between 1953 and 1957. Commercial fishing in North Dakota was legally restricted to these two rivers and was primarily oriented toward subsistence use, with secondary sale to local markets. The survey was prompted by the anticipated loss of riverine habitat due to the construction of [[Garrison Dam]]. Data were gathered via annual questionnaires sent to all licensed commercial fishermen, with response rates ranging from 45.3% to 59.0%. The study successfully quantified catch composition (food fish, rough fish, game fish) and gear-type efficiency (hoop nets, set lines, seines), establishing questionnaire-based creel/commercial surveys as a viable technique, previously validated for sport fisheries by [[A. J. Calhoun]] and [[Frances N. Clark]].

## Background & Description
This is a fisheries-management/survey paper rather than a morphological study; no anatomical descriptions of individual species are provided. However, the catch was taxonomically stratified into three functional/economic categories, each comprising multiple species:

- **Food Fish**: [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — dominant species; [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*); [[White Crappie]] (*P. annularis*); [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*); [[Pumpkinseed]] (*L. gibbosus*); [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*).
- **Rough Fish**: [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*); [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*S. albus*); [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*); [[Shortnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus platostomus*); [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*); [[Goldeye]] (*Hiodon alosoides*); [[Largemouth Buffalo]] (*Ictiobus cyprinella*); [[Carp Sucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*); [[Common Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersonni*); [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*); [[Flathead Chub]] (*Hybopsis gracilis*); Bullheads and Madtoms (Ictaluridae); [[Burbot]] (*Lota lota*); [[Freshwater Drum]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*).
- **Game Fish**: [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*); [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*); [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*); [[Sauger]] (*S. canadense*). Note: game fish caught with commercial gear were required by law to be released.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The commercial fishery was restricted entirely to two large connected river systems — the [[Missouri River]] and [[Yellowstone River]] in North Dakota — encompassing a range of riverine microhabitats including main-channel currents, slow-current zones, and shallow bays.
- **Depth/Current stratification by gear type** (reflecting habitat use patterns of targeted species):
  - **Set lines**: fished in all current types, depths of 5–15 ft; most widely used gear (72–82% of effort) due to low cost.
  - **Hoop nets**: fished in slow currents, depths of 3–10 ft; required more skill (17–26% of effort).
  - **Seines**: effective in shallow bays (1–3% of effort).
- **Bait used**: cheese, cut bait, and minnows — implying reliance on olfactory/scavenging feeding behavior of targeted species (e.g., catfish, buffalo, carp).
- **Anthropogenic pressure**: The fishery faced imminent habitat disruption from the construction of [[Garrison Dam]], motivating baseline data collection before impoundment altered the free-flowing river ecosystem.
- **Trophic/Economic guild structure**: The three-tiered classification (food, rough, game fish) reflects both ecological guild diversity (benthic feeders, planktivores, piscivores) and socioeconomic value rather than strict taxonomic or ecological grouping.

## Key Findings

### Methodology & Response Rates
- Mail questionnaires sent annually (1953–1957) to all licensed commercial fishermen; non-responders received a follow-up letter after 6 weeks, adding 5–8% more returns.
- Licenses issued: 426 (1953), 488 (1954), 404 (1955), 877 (1956), 1,005 (1957).
- Questionnaire response rates: 45.3% (1953), 59.0% (1954), 50.7% (1955), 52.7% (1956), 52.9% (1957).
- ~5% of respondents reported not fishing at all.

### Gear Usage Patterns
- Set lines: 72–82% of fishing effort (most common, low-cost).
- Hoop nets: 17–26% of effort.
- Seines: 1–3% of effort.
- Total 5-year catch by gear: Hoop nets = 245,508 lbs; Set lines = 149,979 lbs; Seines = 8,785 lbs.
- Average annual catch: Hoop nets ≈ 49,102 lbs/year; Set lines ≈ 29,996 lbs/year.

### Annual Averages
- **Food fish** (mainly [[Channel Catfish]]): averaged 29,045 fish (41,171 lbs)/year — largest catch category.
- **Rough fish** (mainly [[Goldeye]], [[Common Carp]], [[Largemouth Buffalo]], [[Carp Sucker]], [[Burbot]], [[Common Sucker]]): averaged 24,166 fish (34,886 lbs)/year.
- **Game fish** ([[Sauger]], [[Walleye]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Rainbow Trout]]): averaged 3,136 fish (4,797 lbs)/year — smallest category, consistent with mandatory release requirements under the fish code.

### Conservation & Methodological Significance
- Confirms mail-questionnaire surveys as an effective, low-cost tool for tracking commercial fishery trends, corroborating prior successful application to sport-fish creel surveys by [[A. J. Calhoun]] (1950, 1951, 1953) and [[Frances N. Clark]] (1953).
- Provided a pre-impoundment baseline dataset for the Missouri/Yellowstone River fishery, valuable for assessing subsequent ecological changes caused by [[Garrison Dam]] construction.
- Demonstrated inter-annual variability in catch composition and gear efficiency, useful for future fisheries management planning in regulated river systems.
