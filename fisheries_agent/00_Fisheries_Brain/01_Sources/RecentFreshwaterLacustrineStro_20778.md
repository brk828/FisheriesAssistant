# Recent Freshwater Lacustrine Stromatolites, Stromatolitic Mats and Oncoids from Northeastern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyanobacteria

## Executive Summary

The paper describes actively forming stromatolites, stromatolitic mats and oncoids in two adjacent spring-fed lakes, [[El Mojarral]] East and West, in the desert [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico. The structures are built mainly by the calcifying cyanobacterium [[Homoeothrix Balearica]] together with the uncalcified [[Schizothrix Lacustris]]. Fish and a diverse benthic fauna live on and within these structures. Fish grazing alters lamination, whereas microbenthos have little effect on it. Diatom preservation varies, and quartz crystal abundance is inversely related to it. The study is a field-based microbiological and sedimentological account rather than a fish survey; fish are treated only as grazers.

## Background & Description

The Cuatro Ciénegas Basin is a desert valley of about 1200 km², enclosed by the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]]. Its floor lies at roughly 740 m elevation, mean annual air temperature is 23 °C, and annual rainfall is under 200 mm. Springs (12–15 per km²) emerge along alluvial fan margins; most are warm (up to 35 °C), though some are as cool as 19 °C. Spring-lake waters are dominated by calcium, sodium and magnesium cations and by sulfate, with unusually high sulfate relative to alkalinity and low chloride.

El Mojarral West is a roughly constant-level sinkhole about 13 × 48 m, 1–5 m deep, with temperatures of 28–35 °C. El Mojarral East is about 50 × 200 m and 0.5–2 m deep, with a firm carbonate bottom and an outflow to the [[Río Mesquites]]. Table 1 (water sampled July 1983) lists SO₄ of 1373.7 mg/l, Ca of 372.7 mg/l, TDS of 2283 mg/l and conductivity of 2800 µmhos, with pH not reported. Stromatolites occur only in West, oncoids only in East, and stromatolitic mats in both.

Methods were field dissection of living mats, microscopy (brightfield, phase contrast, dissecting), fixation in 3% formaldehyde or glutaraldehyde, culturing of cyanobacteria, diatoms, protozoa and micrometazoa, and decalcified thin sections (3% HCl).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stromatolites and mats.** Stromatolites reach about 20 cm in diameter and are lobate-ridged, with ledges about 2 mm long arranged in shingle-like offset patterns. Mats up to 1.5 cm thick cover bedrock. Lamination alternates calcified, light-coloured Homoeothrix layers with dark, organic-rich Schizothrix layers. Vertical zonation runs from an oxygenic surface layer over anoxygenic phototrophs and sulfate reducers. Calcification is light-dependent and linked to CO₂ depletion by thick Homoeothrix trichomes.

**Light gradient.** Under high light, Homoeothrix has brown sheaths; in shade it becomes purple through increased phycoerythrin. Along the depth gradient toward the inflow cave, filamentous communities give way to coccoid cyanobacteria, and 0.1–0.2 mm endolithic zones of Plectonema terebrans and Iyengariella endolithica bore into bedrock. Carbonate precipitation and dissolution occur side by side in these zones.

**Oncoids.** Spherical oncoids of 10–20 cm diameter are concentrated along the outflow stream to the Río Mesquites, where a layer of them nearly covers the floor. Their cortex is built by Schizothrix and Homoeothrix, and nuclei are calcite pebbles, snail shells or organic debris. Water-filled channels run to the nucleus.

**Benthos and grazing.** The benthos on well-lit mats includes protozoa, rotifers, nematodes, harpacticoids, chironomid larvae, ostracods, water mites and amphipods, while deeply shaded mats have little fauna. Several fish species graze the surface, creating parallel tooth grooves and removing patches of mat. The snail [[Mexipyrgus Churinceanus]] is the dominant sediment component on the shelf.

**Diatoms.** The diatom flora of a well-developed Homoeothrix mat comprises about 5 common and about 30 less common taxa. The five most common are Denticula kuetzingii, Cymbella cesatii, Denticula elegans, Achnanthes affinis and Gomphonema intricatum var. vibrio. All are alkaliphilous.

## Key Findings

- Stromatolites, mats and oncoids are forming in two spring-fed lakes at Cuatro Ciénegas, and stromatolites occur only in El Mojarral West.
- Lobate ridges arise from horizontal coalescence of Schizothrix cushions capped by calcifying Homoeothrix; the authors suggest this reflects "instantaneous" lamination.
- Carbonate precipitation is strongly light-dependent and driven by photosynthesis.
- Fish are the most damaging grazers: they clear areas of a cm or more and removed patches up to 5 cm in diameter from artificial substrates. Microbenthos have little effect on lamination.
- Stromatolite growth is estimated at several mm or more per year from artificial-substrate experiments.
- Diverse animal communities do not prevent stromatolite or oncoid formation. Early cementation may explain preservation despite grazers.
- Diatoms are the most diverse stromatophilic eukaryote group but are not dominant in biomass. Diatom preservation is inversely related to quartz crystal abundance, suggesting diatom opal dissolves to supply in situ quartz growth, possibly mediated by sulfate-reducing [[Desulfovibrio]].
- Conclusions: diatom autecology may aid paleoecological interpretation of fossil diatom-bearing stromatolites, provided diagenesis allows.
