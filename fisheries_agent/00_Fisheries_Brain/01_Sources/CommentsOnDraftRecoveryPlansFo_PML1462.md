# Review Comments on Draft Bonytail and Humpback Chub Recovery Plans

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is a formal review letter from Associate Professor of Research Paul C. Marsh of the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies, addressed to Larry Shanks of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. It comments on the draft recovery plans for the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The letter is dated 13 September, and the year is partly garbled in the scan (most likely 1989).

Marsh finds the bonytail plan generally comprehensive but asks for many corrections: citation errors, unpublished or non-citable sources, factual errors, and an unclear cost table. He strongly opposes proposals to treat any bonytail population as "non-essential." He also questions the suitability of the proposed humpback chub refugia and asks that the two plans' costs and responsibilities be separated by species.

## Background & Description

- **Bonytail (*Gila elegans*):** A Colorado River cyprinid whose recovery plan is the main subject of the letter. Marsh's comments concern its life history, captive stocking, and genetic management.
- **Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*):** The second species covered by the draft plan. Marsh questions whether the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] is an appropriate refuge, since it discharges perennially cold water (about 10–13 °C) from Hoover (Boulder) Dam.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*):** Mentioned as a third recovery plan whose costs and responsibilities overlap with the chub plans.
- **Document type:** A reviewer's comment letter with page-by-page notes on the enclosed drafts, plus a general critique of the implementation schedules and budget tables.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Natural reproduction:** A bonytail captured in [[Lake Mohave]] in March 1988 was aged at about 11 years (the scan reads "tr-r", probably 11). Marsh argues this shows natural reproduction and recruitment in that reservoir.
- **Lake Mohave captures:** Since 1987, several additional bonytail have been collected from Lake Mohave. The letter reports 8 in March and 5 in May for the relevant years (the OCR is partly unclear). Marsh notes that May catches gave a remarkable catch per unit of effort, compared with a large existing Lake Mohave dataset.
- **Refuges and facilities:** Marsh notes that bonytail held at the [[Arizona State University]] research facility, and at the [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Page Springs State Hatchery]], are not part of the university. Length and capture data from these facilities were presented at the Annual Symposium of the Desert Fishes Council.
- **Non-native fish stocking:** Marsh supports a halt to stocking non-native warmwater fish. He asks that the plan identify where such stocking still occurs and in which reaches.
- **Radiotelemetry:** Marsh requests any preliminary results from experimental [[Radiotelemetry]] studies of bonytail in the [[Green River]].

## Key Findings

**Bonytail plan: citations and factual corrections**
- The Tyus and Marsh (1989)-type reference is cited as "in preparation" and is not a citable document. The source of the fish examined in these investigations should be stated.
- The [[Douglas et al. Study]] (1989) was published in *Copeia* in 1988, so the reference must be updated. Roger Harman's middle initial is L.
- Invertebrate names are wrong: "gastropods" should read "gastropods" (scan reads "gastenopodsn") and "caddid worms" should read "caddisworms."
- Several unpublished or non-final sources (e.g., "Huth R.T. 1988" and "Valdez and Williams 1987") should not be cited unless finalized. Other papers (Tyus and Karp 1989; Tyus and Minckley 1988) have been published and need updated references.

**Bonytail plan: genetics and stocking**
- Marsh strongly disagrees with the proposal to stop bonytail broodstock acquisition until genetic studies are complete. He argues that non-reproducing captive individuals still contribute significantly to genetic studies, and that hatchery fish may live longer than wild fish.
- Marsh opposes labelling any bonytail population "non-essential." He says the species' biological and genetic value makes this unwise, and that such a label would be inappropriate after only one year.

**Bonytail plan: budget and schedule**
- The cost table (pages 31 et seq.) is unclear: it is uncertain which dollar amounts belong to which agencies and tasks. Marsh asks for clarification.
- Integration of costs and responsibilities with the humpback chub and Colorado Squawfish plans has not been done. Marsh asks either for a statement that the costs are independent of implementation schedules, or for a detailed analysis of shared costs.

**Humpback chub plan**
- Marsh questions the use of [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] as a refuge. Its perennially cold water is unsuitable for studying humpback chub life history and ecology unless a reliable supply of uncontaminated warm water is secured.
- The draft allows two years for establishing and maintaining humpback chub refugia. Marsh considers this realistic for the humpback chub but inadequate for the bonytail.

**Overall**
- Marsh supports the plans in principle, but the detailed citation errors, unclear budget, inconsistent schedules, and unsupported management proposals need correction before the plans are finalized.
