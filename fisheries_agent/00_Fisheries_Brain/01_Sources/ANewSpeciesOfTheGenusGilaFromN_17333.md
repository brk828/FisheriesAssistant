# *Gila steptoensis*: A New Dace from Steptoe Valley, Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

Caldwell and Davis describe a previously unknown dace, [[Gila Steptoensis]] sp. nov. (common name [[Steptoe Dace]]), from [[Steptoe Valley]] in northeastern [[Nevada]]. The description is tentative and based on 30 paratypes from [[Grass Springs]] on the [[Lasetti Ranch]]. The authors note that the species was first mentioned by [[Hubbs and Miller 1948]] as a "Rhinichthys-like" dace. Based on pharyngeal tooth formula, body shape, lateral line scale count, dorsal fin position and intestine length, they place it in the subgenus [[Siphateles]] of the genus [[Gila]], following [[Uyeno 1961]]. The authors frame the species as a relict form from a fish fauna with high endemism in [[Nevada]], shaped by climatic change over the past 10,000 years.

## Background & Description

The paper opens by placing the species in the context of Nevada's aquatic fauna. Climatic changes over roughly 10,000 years disrupted major pathways of genetic exchange for aquatic vertebrates, leaving relict forms in isolated remnant waters across the [[Great Basin]].

**Material and methods**

- Body proportions were measured on alcohol-preserved specimens. Meristic counts were taken from specimens cleared and stained after [[Evans 1948]]. Meristic data and body proportions followed [[Hubbs and Lagler 1949]].
- The 30 paratypes were chosen to represent all available sizes.
- Material came from the [[University of Nevada Biological Museum]]. Catalogue entries F-169 to F-175 cover Grass Springs, the ranch area and a railroad-station slough in Steptoe Valley. Each entry lists 10 specimens.
- The material was collected in 1962 by J. E. Deacon, Karl Larsen and James Tener.

**Morphology (as described)**

- **Pharyngeal teeth:** uniserial, four on each side, slightly hooked and well developed. No outer row.
- **Mouth and lips:** mouth terminal, no frenum, protractile lips, lip papillae few to numerous on the lower lip and absent to few on the upper lip. No barbel.
- **Scales:** 48–62 along the [[Lateral Line]] (average not given in the description), 10–15 above (average 12) and 8–15 below (average 11). The lateral line canal is moderately recurved and may be complete or incomplete; when incomplete it ends posterior to the dorsal fin origin.
- **Fins:**
  - Dorsal: usually 8 principal rays (occasionally 7 or 9), none spiny.
  - Caudal: 17–22 principal rays, usually 19; moderately forked and equilobate.
  - Anal: usually 7–8, rarely 9.
  - Pelvic: usually 7–8, rarely 9.
  - Pectoral: usually 11, 12 or 14, rarely 13 or 16.
- **Body proportions** (range, with mean where given):
  - Head length: 3.4 (3.0–4.5) times in body.
  - Head depth: 4.5 (4.1–5.0) times in body.
  - Caudal peduncle: 7.6 (6.6–8.7) times in body.
  - Snout length: 4.2 (3.5–4.2) times in head.
  - Eye measurement: 4.1 (3.0–5.3) times in head (label partly garbled in the scan).
  - Standard length: 21–62 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality.** The type locality is a small pond about 20 feet across and 5 feet deep at maximum, with steep banks about 2 feet high. About half the surface is covered by [[Watercress]] (*Nasturtium* sp.). The species was very abundant there. Specimens were collected by pulling watercress from the water and picking out the trapped fish. No other fish species occur in this pond.

**Hydrology.** Grass Springs is fed by several sources that flow into a pasture and then into [[Duck Creek]].

**Associated waters**

- A larger pond near the ranch yard was reported by the owner's wife to have once held a population of *G. steptoensis* in [[Ellen Valley]]. When collected on 27 June (the text gives 1962), it held only [[Sacramento Perch]] (*Archoplites interruptus*).
- At [[Steptoe Ranch]] farther north, carp and goldfish were taken together with *G. steptoensis*.

**Biogeography.** Following [[Hubbs and Miller 1948]], the most probable connections of this isolated valley were with the [[Lahontan Basin]] drainage system during prepluvial times. The authors consider this consistent with their morphological interpretation.

**Possible drivers of variation.** The sometimes incomplete lateral line in smaller paratypes may reflect age or isolation in small warm springs. The authors leave this open.

## Key Findings

1. **New species.** The Steptoe Valley dace is described as *Gila steptoensis* sp. nov. The description is explicitly tentative, based on 30 paratypes.
2. **Rejection of the Rhinichthys link.** The earlier "Rhinichthys-like" assignment is judged to be an error. *Rhinichthys* typically has a much smaller eye and a longer snout relative to head length. Its dentition is rarely uniserial, its body is less fusiform, and it commonly bears barbels. *G. steptoensis* has no barbels.
3. **Subgeneric placement.** The species is placed in *Siphateles* within *Gila*. The main basis is the 4-4 pharyngeal tooth formula, with body shape, lateral line count, dorsal fin position and intestine length also in agreement. The only character not matching *Gila* is the sometimes incomplete lateral line.
4. **Gila-character agreement.** The authors list seven characters that agree with *Gila* as defined by Uyeno: lateral line scales, absent barbels, terminal mouth, protractile premaxillaries, dorsal ray counts, black peritoneum, and dorsal fin origin above or slightly behind the pelvic insertion. Several of Uyeno's heavily weighted osteological characters were not examined.
5. **Relict status.** The species is presented as a relict of prepluvial connections to Lahontan waters, with zoogeographical support for the morphological placement.
6. **Restricted distribution.** Known sites are small, isolated warm springs and ponds in Steptoe Valley, with the type locality supporting an almost monospecific fish community.

**Data notes**

- The lateral line scale range is given as 48–62 in the description but as 43–62 (average 56) in the comparison section. The text gives no explanation for the difference.
- The collection year is given as 1962 in the text, while the museum catalogue entries are dated 27 June 1964.
