# Wahweap Operation and Maintenance: Utah, FY 2004 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 2004 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers the [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] at Big Water, Utah. The hatchery rears [[Bonytail]] for stocking in the [[Colorado River]] and [[Green River]] in Utah and Colorado, and it maintains the Upper Basin's backup [[Razorback Sucker]] broodstock. In 2004 the station stocked 10,100 bonytail, received 15,000 bonytail fry from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and destroyed 15,000 wild-spawned bonytail of unknown parentage. The project is ongoing, and the principal investigator recommends continued bonytail rearing, broodstock maintenance, and pit tagging of 9,000 bonytail for fall stocking.

## Background & Description

The project is a facilities operation and maintenance task under the [[Recovery Program]], supporting its Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP). It is linked to these actions:

- **IV.A. Genetic management:** securing and managing genetic stocks in refugia, including razorback sucker (IV.A.4.a).
- **IV.A.4:** developing and implementing a basin-wide bonytail restoration plan.
- **IV.C. Operate and maintain facilities:** the core function of the Wahweap station.

The principal investigator is Quentin Bradwisch of the [[Utah Division of Wildlife]]. Work is listed as ongoing, with fish culture as the current status. PIT tag data were to be submitted to the database manager by January 1, 2005.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Bonytail and razorback sucker are the two native Colorado River fishes managed here, and both are listed as endangered. Hatchery culture supports population restoration by producing fish for stocking and by holding genetic reserves outside the river. The report describes:

- **Growth monitoring:** Weekly growth in the rearing ponds was tracked by capturing fish with minnow traps, seines, and cast nets.
- **Spawning:** All two-year-old bonytail spawned to some extent in the hatchery ponds during 2004.
- **Fish health:** Some losses were lethal sampling for fish health inspection.
- **Parasite research:** 400 untagged bonytail went to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] in Flagstaff for tapeworm studies.

The report also notes that the excess-fish policy was applied, with [[Biology Committee]] knowledge, to the destruction of wild-spawned bonytail. This policy governs disposition of hatchery surplus when origin is uncertain.

## Key Findings

**1. Bonytail stocking, 2004 (10,100 fish total):**
- 3,500 PIT-tagged fish stocked at the [[Rainbow Park Boat Ramp]] on the Green River.
- 3,100 PIT-tagged fish stocked at [[Green River State Park]].
- 3,100 PIT-tagged fish stocked at the Cisco boat ramp on the Colorado River.
- 400 untagged fish provided to Arizona Game and Fish for parasite research.
- All stocked fish were tagged in 2004, except the 400 untagged fish above. The Utah stocking plan accounted for 9,700 pit-tagged fish.
- Stocked fish measured 175–250 mm.

**2. Hatchery intake and culling:**
- 15,000 bonytail fry were received and reared from Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- 15,000 wild-spawned bonytail (20–100 mm, reported in good condition) were destroyed because exact parentage was unknown.

**3. Fish on station, fall 2004:**

| Species | Year class | Number | Size | Ponds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bonytail | 2002 | 20,000 | 120 mm+ | 4 |
| Bonytail | 2003 | 6,000 | 100 mm+ | 2 |
| Bonytail brood | N/A | 500 | 250+ mm | 2 |
| Razorback brood | N/A | 947 | Adult | 2 |

All other losses during the year came from fish moves, tagging, or lethal sampling.

**4. Recommendations:**
- Continue rearing bonytail at Wahweap to meet Utah's stocking needs.
- Maintain the razorback sucker backup broodstock.
- Supply either species on request from the Biology Committee.
- Pit tag 9,000 bonytail and stock them in the fall.
