## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific research paper but a **procedural strategy memo** outlining a plan to develop a technical report assessing the impact of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), the broader fish assemblage, and the food base of the [[Colorado River]] in the [[Grand Canyon]]. The draft (dated October 26, 1993) lays out a multi-step process for peer review, technical report drafting, and formal review, culminating in scientific hypotheses to support recommendations under the Glen Canyon Dam Adaptive Management Program. It is administrative in nature but establishes the institutional framework through which subsequent biological and ecological findings on the species were later formalized (e.g., via Biological Opinion/Biological Assessment processes under the Endangered Species Act).

## Key Findings

Because this is a planning/strategy document, the "findings" are procedural and administrative rather than biological:

1. **Purpose:** Develop a scientifically defensible technical report on how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affect the [[Humpback Chub]], other fish populations, and the food base in the [[Colorado River]].
2. **Four-Step Process Proposed:**
   - **Step One:** Convene a peer review group (with no prior tie to Grand Canyon work) to review existing technical information on humpback chub and fish populations.
   - **Step Two:** Draft the technical report using technical experts, editors (GCES/BOR/NBS), and outside contract writers.
   - **Step Three:** Conduct a two-tiered review — initial peer review, followed by formal review by outside experts, a National Academy of Sciences committee, and relevant agencies.
   - **Step Four:** Finalize the report, embed testable scientific hypotheses, and integrate feedback into the Adaptive Management Program with defined timetables.
3. **Four Policy Options Considered** for proceeding with the Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act:
   - **Option 1:** No action — proceed directly to Final Draft Biological Opinion.
   - **Option 2 (Recommended):** Meet with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[Bureau of Reclamation]] to reframe strategy, conclude current consultation, and reinitiate consultation on a preferred alternative (per the Advanced Draft EIS) incorporating FWS-proposed flows.
   - **Option 3:** Formally document changes to the proposed Federal Action and seek written FWS concurrence before submitting the technical fish report.
   - **Option 4:** Provide a Biological Assessment between the Draft and Final EIS.
   - **All options:** Conclude with a public meeting/symposium to present results.
4. **Proposed Timetable (Oct 1993 – Oct 1994):** A detailed schedule of meetings (GCES/Patten, BOR/FWS/GCES, Peer Group, Technical Work Group) and deliverables — draft written report (Apr 1994), draft Biological Assessment without fish section (Apr 1994), technical report delivery (Jun 1994), Biological Assessment to FWS (Jul 1994), Final Biological Opinion (Sep 1994), and Final EIS to DOI (Oct 1994).
5. **Institutional Significance:** This document reflects an early 1990s inflection point in Colorado River management, where dam operation policy was being explicitly linked to endangered species science, foreshadowing the formal establishment of the [[Glen Canyon Dam Adaptive Management Program]] and long-term monitoring of the [[Humpback Chub]] and associated native fish assemblage in the [[Grand Canyon]].
