# Description of a New Species of Catostomus (Catostomus cypho) from the Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1880 taxonomic paper by [[Wm N Lockington]], published in the *Proceedings of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia* (vol. 32, pp. 237–240), formally describes a new sucker species, *Catostomus cypho* sp. nov. The description is based on a single, damaged specimen taken from the [[Colorado River]] at its junction with the [[Gila River]]. The specimen was sent to the [[California Academy Of Sciences]] by [[John E Curry]], a civil engineer. The paper gives meristic counts, body proportions, head and fin morphology, scale characters, colour, and detailed skeletal notes, including an unusually large inter-neural bone that supports a dorsal hump.

## Background & Description

The species is assigned to the genus *Catostomus* (suckers). The author gives the following diagnostic counts:

- **Dorsal (D):** 3, 14
- **Anal (A):** 2, 7
- **Caudal (C):** 7-1-16-1-7
- **Pectoral (P):** 18
- **Ventral (V):** 10
- **Lateral line scales (L. lat.):** 79

**External morphology**

- **Head and snout:** Head conical. Snout long and strongly depressed. The dorsal outline rises straight to the occiput, then forms a prominent, elevated hump at its greatest height about one snout-length behind the occiput.
- **Body shape:** Dorsal outline descends rapidly after the hump to about two-thirds of total length. The caudal peduncle is extremely elongated and widens toward the caudal base. The abdominal outline is almost straight.
- **Proportions:** Greatest depth is contained "not quite 4½" times in total length (OCR-ambiguous; the paper gives "not quite 41 times" for depth-related measure). Head is a little more than 4 times in total length. Inter-ocular width equals snout length. Caudal peduncle is about 3⅔ times in greatest depth (OCR-ambiguous).
- **Mouth:** Rather wide and inferior. The lower lip is small, in two ovoid lobes covered with low, flat-topped papillae. The front of the dentary is covered by a horny plate.
- **Nostrils:** Anterior nostril horizontally sub-elliptical. Posterior nostril large, vertical, crescentic, and covered by the anterior flap.
- **Head pores:** Two distinct rows on top of the head, connected on the occiput with a series running behind and below the eye to near the snout tip.
- **Pharyngeal teeth:** Arcuate, numerous, regularly diminishing posteriorly.

**Fins**

- **Pectorals:** Triangular-lanceolate; fourth and fifth rays longest; tips reach beyond the middle of the pubic bones.
- **Ventrals:** Reach beyond the vent; third rays longest.
- **Dorsal:** Fourth and fifth rays longest; first three rays simple, the rest twice bifurcate.
- **Anal:** Considerably shorter than the dorsal, but equal in depth to it.
- **Caudal:** Numerous accessory rays; posterior margin triangularly emarginate.
- **Fin scales:** Fins are scaleless, as is a small patch on the anterior dorsal hump.

**Scales and lateral line**

- Scales are cycloid, variable in size, with 8–16 radiating striae on the exposed portion, crossed by fainter concentric striae.
- Scales enlarge posteriorly, so the largest scales are on the caudal peduncle. Scales diminish faster downward than upward, making the abdominal scales the smallest.
- The lateral line is deflected near its origin and runs along the median line to the caudal base.

**Colour (preserved specimen)**

- Silvery-grey dorsally; light straw or creamy on the abdomen and underside of head; fins light uniform slaty-grey.
- Colour comes from dark dots on scales and membranes, which are sparser on scales, so scale outlines stand out.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper provides limited ecological information. The author reports, second-hand, that the species "is said" to be not uncommon at the collection locality, at the junction of the Colorado and [[Gila River]]. No data on diet, reproduction, abundance, or habitat use are given.

The single specimen was damaged before it reached the author. Its large inter-neural bone had been extracted about two years earlier, the [[Air Bladder]] was destroyed (so whether it is divided into two chambers, as in other catostomids, could not be determined), and the fin tips and body shape were distorted.

## Key Findings

1. **New species:** *Catostomus cypho* is described as new, based on one specimen.
2. **Type locality:** Colorado River at the junction with the Gila River; specimen deposited with the California Academy of Sciences.
3. **Diagnostic dorsal hump:** The hump is supported by a very large trapezoidal [[Inter-Neural Bones|inter-neural]] bone, a central pillar with broad anterior and posterior expansions. The posterior expansion is about twice as large as the anterior.
4. **Skeletal features:**
   - The first vertebra has a broad articulating surface for the first inter-neural and expanded transverse processes joined to large bony plates linking the air bladder to the skull.
   - Processes on the next nine vertebrae diminish in size successively backward.
   - The neural spines of the first two of these vertebrae are bifid.
5. **Meristics and proportions:** The counts and ratios listed above (D 3,14; A 2,7; P 18; V 10; L. lat. 79) are the main diagnostic data given.
6. **Scale pattern:** Large, strongly striated scales on the caudal peduncle grade down to the smallest scales on the abdomen.
7. **Dimensions of the specimen:** A table lists measurements, including total length (given as "114," units in inches; fractional digits lost in scan), length to caudal base ("8"), greatest depth ("25"), and many head, fin and body distances. Full values are not reproduced here because OCR damage makes several fractions unreliable.
8. **Limitations:** The author states that the single specimen is damaged, its air bladder is destroyed, and its fins are broken. He also regrets that only one example is available.
