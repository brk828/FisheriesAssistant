## Executive Summary

This document is a 1997 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) letter to stakeholders finalizing the extirpation status of stocked populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) at 40 sites across Arizona. The report synthesizes the 1995 and 1996 Provisional Extirpation Reports prepared by the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) in coordination with the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]. The letter is primarily a regulatory/administrative record rather than a morphological or ecological field study; it does not describe fish anatomy or physiology, but provides critical population status, site management, and conservation planning data for two federally protected desert fish taxa.

Sites are categorized into two management classes:
1. **Extirpated Sites** (Table 1) — populations confirmed lost and habitats judged unsuitable for restocking; no longer require Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] for these species.
2. **Recovery Sites** (Table 2) — populations presumed extirpated but habitat conditions remain suitable for restocking or additional conservation management; Section 7 consultation may still be required.

## Key Findings

- **40 total sites** across Arizona were evaluated and formally declared extirpated for stocked [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] populations, based on the 1995 and 1996 AGFD Provisional Extirpation Reports.
- Extirpation declarations followed a formal, multi-agency review process involving the USFWS, AGFD, land management agencies, and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]].
- Extirpated sites (Table 1) no longer require Section 7 consultation under the ESA specifically for these two species but retain relevance for other listed/sensitive species and watershed/riparian values.
- A separate set of sites (Table 2, "Recovery Sites") were also assessed as extirpated but retain habitat conditions suitable for future restocking or additional management actions — these sites still may require Section 7 compliance.
- Notable recovery sites include: [[Redfield Canyon]] (Tucson BLM / The Nature Conservancy), [[O'Donnell Creek]]/[[Canelo Cienega]] (The Nature Conservancy), [[Rock Creek 3 Bar C]] (Tonto NF), and [[Sheep Spring]] (Prescott NF).
- The letter establishes a feedback mechanism: stakeholders with evidence of site misclassification or knowledge of potential new reintroduction sites were invited to contact USFWS within 30 days.
- A forthcoming **1997 Provisional Extirpation Report** (AGFD) was announced as in preparation at the time of this letter, indicating an ongoing, iterative site-monitoring program for these species.
- This document underscores the broader pattern of habitat loss and population attrition affecting native desert fishes in Arizona, reinforcing management priorities for [[Endangered Species Act]]-listed aquatic taxa in isolated spring/tank ecosystems.
