# Contribución al Conocimiento de la Biología del Charal Prieto del Lago de Pátzcuaro, Mich.

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1961 monograph by Aurelio Solórzano Preciado, issued by the Dirección General de Pesca e Industrias Conexas of the Secretaría de Industria y Comercio, Mexico, provides a taxonomic separation and a biological study of the [[Charal Prieto]] (*Chirostoma bartoni*) from [[Lago de Pátzcuaro]]. It argues that *Chirostoma bartoni* and *Chirostoma patzcuaro* are distinct species, despite earlier synonymies, and then studies the reproductive season, spawning sites, size at maturity, feeding habits and capture systems of *Ch. bartoni*. The author concludes that the current closed season is ineffective and proposes new management measures, including protected spawning sanctuaries.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** The species was described by [[Jordan and Evermann]] (1896) from a tributary of the [[Río Lerma]]. Subsequent authors (Meek, Regan, Jordan and Hubbs, De Buen, Martín del Campo, Schultz) treated *Ch. attenuatum*, *Ch. patzcuaro* and *Ch. regani* variously as synonyms or varieties. The author retains two valid species from Pátzcuaro: *Ch. bartoni* and *Ch. patzcuaro*. A third local silverside, *Ch. grandocule*, is not confused with them and was excluded.

**Morphological comparison (Cuadro 3).**
- *Ch. bartoni*: two series of premaxillary teeth; generally 19 gill rakers on the first arch; generally 42 scales in the lateral series, 18 predorsal scales; generally 14 or 15 anal rays; 41 vertebrae most often; maximum body height 5.7–8 times standard length; scales smooth or slightly undulate on the free margin.
- *Ch. patzcuaro*: three to four premaxillary series; generally 26 gill rakers; generally 47 or 48 lateral scales, 27 or 28 predorsal scales; generally 17 anal rays; 43 vertebrae most often; body height 5.1–6.6 times standard length; scales with irregularly notched free margins.

The scale border and predorsal and lateral scale counts were judged the most reliable separating characters.

**Sample basis.** Meristic and metric data came from 50 *Ch. bartoni* (59.6–91.8 mm standard length) and 43 *Ch. patzcuaro* (69.8–121 mm). Anal ray and lateral scale counts were added from 1,209 and 1,161 further *Ch. bartoni* specimens respectively.

**Colour.** Living fish are olive-green dorsally (*Ch. patzcuaro* tends toward greenish-brown), pale ventrally, with a silvery lateral band that darkens in formalin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study period and methods.** Biological observations covered one annual cycle, 19 June 1956 to 7 August 1957, with samples taken about every 15 days, using 10,818 specimens (4,285 males and 6,533 females). Gonadal maturity followed the international maturity scale (stages I–VII). Feeding was assessed by frequency of occurrence (Lagler, 1956).

**Lake conditions (after De Buen, 1944).** Temperature rises from February to June, stabilises July–September and cools rapidly October–December; January means were about 14–14.5 °C and August about 21.9–22.8 °C (1943). Surface dissolved oxygen ranged 4.3–6.0 c.c./l (1941). pH was 8.6–8.9 (1940–1943). Maximum depth was 15 m at Seno Quiroga. The lake was shrinking in area and depth, with dense floating and submerged vegetation invading former open sectors.

**Spawning habitat.** Spawning occurs on wave-exposed gravel, sand or stone shores, and on grass-covered margins under thin water. Beds of *Potamogeton foliosus* and *P. lucens* are preferred substrates. Muddy, sheltered, organically rich sites had no eggs. Captive fish also spawned on roots of *Eichhornia speciosa*, but eggs did not adhere to it in nature.

**Predators and losses.** Wind-driven wave action in the first three months of the year strands eggs on shores, where they desiccate. Raccoons (*Procyon lotor*) eat eggs, and cattle and pigs trample or consume egg-bearing vegetation. Garter snakes (*Tamnophis*) prey on spawning adults.

**Egg description.** Eggs are amber, transparent and adhesive, with 4–6 filaments on the capsule. They measure 0.9–1.1 mm in diameter, with oil globules of 0.25–0.35 mm. Embryonated eggs measure 1.1–1.2 mm.

## Key Findings

**Reproduction.**
- Spawning runs from January to September each year; the peak concentration lies between March and May or early June.
- The first eggs in 1957 were seen on 22 January; the last spawning at the observation site, Punta de Santiago, was on 7 August 1957. At Ojo de Agua, eggs were last seen on 25 September.
- Breeding units consist of 5–8 males and one, usually larger, female. Fertilisation occurs in tight groups among stones or plants; the author does not establish whether eggs are shed in one or several bouts.
- Size at first maturity: females from 75–79 mm total length, males from 65–69 mm. Among mature fish, females most commonly fell in the 85–89 mm class; males most commonly in the 75–79 mm class.
- No nest building or parental care occurs.

**Diet.**
- In the wild, the diet is dominated by microcrustaceans: Cladocera (present in 257 of 413 crustacean-containing guts), Copepoda and Ostracoda. *Bosmina* was more frequent than *Daphnia*.
- Of 532 partly or fully filled guts, insects occurred in 54.8%, microcrustaceans in 77.6%, fish remains in 17.8% and conspecific eggs in 1.1%. Phytoplankton was negligible.
- Captive fish showed no cannibalism; fish remains did not appear in pond-reared specimens.

**Fishing.**
- Three gear types are used: the gill net *cherémicua* (the most important commercial gear, which operates mainly on spawners), the *mariposa* lift net, and the *chinchorro* seine (which takes small catches year-round).
- Gill-net nets, 25–30 m per panel string, are set from early morning to noon or 13:00–14:00, and fishermen lay 3–4 rows of nets along the shore during the peak.
- Catches in 1955 and 1956 were considered good (up to 15 kg per canoe-day, rarely 30 kg). In 1957 catches fell to 4–7 kg per canoe-day.
- Official statistics are unreliable: they omit fresh local consumption, mix three species, and misdate catches around the closed season.

**Management recommendations.**
- The existing closed season (February to end of May) is ineffective, largely for socio-economic reasons.
- Proposed measures: (1) ban gill-net use across the whole lake for one month; (2) designate a permanent spawning sanctuary, initially between Punta de Santiago and Vuelta de Cucuchucho, closed to fishing during spawning; (3) close certain spawning areas for one month, while allowing gill netting there before or after; (4) improve spawning grounds by placing shrub branches weighted with stones, a practice trialled in 1957 and 1958.
- The author argues that natural reproduction, protected by sanctuaries, is preferable to hatchery restocking, and that excessive protection could cause overpopulation and competition with more valuable species.
