# Genetics and Geography of Sex Determination in the Southern Platyfish, *Xiphophorus maculatus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 study by Kallman, from the [[Genetics Laboratory of the New York Aquarium]] at the American Museum of Natural History, examines the sex-determining genetics of the platyfish, *Xiphophorus maculatus*, across its range in southern Mexico, Guatemala and British Honduras. Using laboratory stocks and wild-caught fish identified through [[Sex-Linked Pigment Patterns]], the author shows that two sex-chromosome systems coexist: the WY-YY system (females heterogametic, WY; males homogametic, YY) and the XX-XY system. The two systems interbreed freely and together form a single integrated mechanism, which the author proposes should be described as WY-WX-XX-XY-YY. The study also documents the geographic distribution of the W and X chromosomes, rates of crossing over and sex reversal, and the evolutionary origin of the W chromosome.

## Background & Description

*Xiphophorus maculatus* is one of the more than 140 known poeciliid species, of which only about two dozen had been studied in detail. The species is morphologically indistinguishable across populations and mates readily with other members of the genus, including *X. variatus*. In males, the anal fin transforms into a [[Gonopodium]] at sexual maturity, which is the basis for sex identification.

The WY-YY system was discovered independently by Bellamy and Gordon and was confirmed by Breider and Kosswig. The domesticated stocks used in these early studies were imported into Germany around 1900, and their geographic origin was unrecorded until Gordon deduced the Belize River as the likely source. Gordon later showed that fish from the Rios Jamapa, Papaloapan and Coatzacoalcos carried the XX-XY system.

Kallman's material included eight laboratory stocks of known origin (six XX-XY, two WY-YY), listed in Table 1, with the longest-maintained stock, Jp 30, inbred for more than 42 generations. Wild-caught fish were collected at roughly a dozen stations in British Honduras, Guatemala and Mexico, including the [[Belize River]], [[New River]], [[Rio Hondo]], [[Lake Petén]], the [[Rio Usumacinta]] system, the [[Rio Grijalva]] and the [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species inhabits the Atlantic lowlands from British Honduras west across the Petén district of Guatemala to the Rio Jamapa near Veracruz. It is absent from the Yucatán Peninsula. The author notes that platyfish tend to remain near banks, aggregate in favorable locations and rarely disperse long distances, so small local samples may not represent an entire river system.

Geographic findings:

- **WY-YY only:** the New River and Belize River populations, a region the author estimates at no more than about 10 per cent of the species' range.
- **XX-XY only:** the Rios Jamapa, Papaloapan and Coatzacoalcos, roughly 30 per cent of the range including the narrow coastal plain between the Rio Grijalva and Rio Jamapa.
- **Both W and X present:** the Rio Grijalva and Usumacinta drainages, Lake Petén, the Rio Hondo and the Rio San Pedro de Martir, covering about 60 per cent of the range.

The author proposes that the W chromosome arose from the X in the Rio Usumacinta–Rio Grijalva region, which is the largest river system in Central America. Connected lowland lakes and swamps in northern Petén are described as providing dispersal routes between drainages, and lower Pleistocene sea levels may have allowed fish to move between river systems.

## Key Findings

- **Two coexisting systems:** WY-YY and XX-XY sex determination are both present in *X. maculatus*. Fish with WY, WX and XX genotypes almost always become females, and XY and YY fish become males.
- **Sample base:** more than 100,000 platyfish were raised at the laboratory over 25 years. Among the laboratory stocks, sex ratios were close to 1:1 (for example, 240 females to 276 males in the New River strain).
- **Stability:** YY males sired 3,479 offspring from 66 males mated with XX females, all of them male. Crosses of WY females with YY or XY males, and of WX females with XY males, gave expected ratios. WX females crossed with XY males gave a 3:1 sex ratio.
- **Exception in Rio Hondo crosses:** in 18 of 20 crosses between WY females of the Rio Hondo (Douglas and Tikal) and XY males, females outnumbered males. The overall deviation from 1:1 was highly significant.
- **Crossing over:** between W and Y chromosomes in 2 of 1,334 fish (about 0.2%); between X and Y in 9 of 5,136 XX or XY offspring (about 0.2%), rising to about 0.3% if half of the untested exceptions are counted as crossovers.
- **Sex reversal:** about 0.5% after correction for biased sampling. Sex reversals are concentrated in certain pedigrees, and XX males traced back to two Petén fish.
- **Autosomal modifier hypothesis rejected:** the author argues that the sporadic sex reversals, and their clustering in pedigrees, cannot be explained by fortuitous combinations of autosomal "sex genes."
- **Z versus Y:** the Z chromosome of domesticated stocks is equivalent to the Y in intraspecific crosses, so the author recommends dropping the symbol "Z" to avoid implying two separate mechanisms.
- **Evolutionary conclusion:** the WY-WX-XX-XY-YY system is a single integrated mechanism that likely evolved from an XX-XY ancestor, and the W chromosome probably arose from the X in the Rio Usumacinta–Rio Grijalva region.
