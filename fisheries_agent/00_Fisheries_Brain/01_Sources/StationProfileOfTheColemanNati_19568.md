## Executive Summary

This document is a **station profile** for the Coleman National Fish Hatchery (Coleman NFH), a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located on the [[Sacramento River]] near Anderson, California. Rather than presenting original morphological or ecological research, the source is an administrative/programmatic record describing the hatchery's mission, funding, staffing, and fish distribution output. Coleman NFH was established in 1942 as a mitigation hatchery under the **Central Valley Project (CVP)**, created to offset the loss of natural spawning habitat for [[Chinook Salmon]] and [[Steelhead Trout]] caused by the construction and operation of **Shasta Dam**. The data presented spans fiscal years 1991–1995, detailing operational budgets, full-time equivalent (FTE) staffing, and annual numbers of fish released into federal waters, primarily consisting of multiple Chinook salmon runs and steelhead trout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Anadromous freshwater spawning and rearing habitat associated with the Sacramento River system, which drains into the Pacific Ocean.
- **Ecological driver for hatchery establishment:** Construction of **Shasta Dam** (a Bureau of Reclamation Central Valley Project component) blocked access to historic upstream spawning grounds for [[Chinook Salmon]] and [[Steelhead Trout]], necessitating artificial propagation as compensatory mitigation.
- **Distribution pathway:** Fish reared at the hatchery are released into **federal waters** (per the tables, all distributed fish during FY91–FY95 were released into federal waters, with no reported releases to state or private waters in this record).
- **Geographic linkage:** The facility connects the regulated, dam-impacted Sacramento River to the broader [[Pacific Ocean]] basin via the anadromous life cycle of its target species.
- **Multiple run timing:** The presence of Fall, Late Fall, and Winter Chinook runs reflects the historically diverse phenological structure of Sacramento River Chinook populations, an ecological trait of significant conservation concern given differential dam impacts on each run's spawning timing and habitat needs.

## Key Findings

- **Establishment & Authority:** Coleman NFH was established in 1942 under the Emergency Relief Appropriation Act of 1935, First Deficiency Appropriation Act of 1936, and the River and Harbor Act of 1937, which authorized the Central Valley Project; fish and wildlife protection provisions were formally referenced in 1940 legislation.
- **Mission:** The hatchery exists specifically to mitigate the loss of natural salmonid spawning habitat resulting from Shasta Dam's construction and operation.
