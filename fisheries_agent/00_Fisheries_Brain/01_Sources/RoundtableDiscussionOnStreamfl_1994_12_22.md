# Roundtable Discussion on Streamflow, Geomorphology, and Food Web in Recovery of Endangered Fishes, Upper Colorado River

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This document is administrative rather than a primary data paper: it is a letter from Richard S. Wydoski (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Recovery Program) inviting Dr. W.L. Minckley to serve as a peer reviewer at a **Roundtable Discussion** held February 6–7, 1995, in Grand Junction, Colorado. The roundtable was convened by the **Recovery Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin** to synthesize research on the interrelationship of **streamflow regulation, channel geomorphology, and food web dynamics** as they affect four federally listed native fishes: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically called "Colorado Squawfish"), [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila* sp.). The overarching management goal was to generate scientific recommendations for **reoperation of Flaming Gorge Dam and the Aspinall Unit dams** to provide flow regimes that support recovery of these species. While no raw ecological data are presented in the text itself, the agenda catalogs an extensive slate of ongoing Recovery Program studies (identified by project number) addressing flow-habitat-food web linkages across the Green, Gunnison, and Colorado River mainstems.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source. The document is procedural/administrative (meeting logistics and agenda) rather than a species-description or morphometric study. Species referenced are large-river [[Cyprinidae|cyprinids]] (*Ptychocheilus*, *Gila*) and a large-bodied [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), all endemic to the Colorado River Basin and characterized elsewhere in the literature by adaptations to warm, turbid, high-energy river conditions (e.g., streamlined bodies, reduced scales in *Gila cypha*, and specialized nuchal humps in Humpback Chub — not detailed in this text).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The agenda structure implicitly maps the ecological niches under investigation:

- **Nursery habitat / early life stages**: Studies on availability and quality of nursery habitat for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and sympatric species in relation to [[Gunnison River]] flow manipulation (Project 44); overwinter nursery habitat for age-0 Colorado Pikeminnow in the [[Green River]] below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], including effects of ice cover (Project 41).
- **Migration and passage habitat**: Minimum flow requirements for adult passage of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the lower [[Gunnison River]] (Project 57).
- **Canyon-bound mainstem habitat**: Flow effects on [[Humpback Chub]] populations in [[Westwater Canyon]] of the [[Colorado River]] (Project 46); test flows from Flaming Gorge Dam to optimize habitat for *Gila* sp. and Colorado Pikeminnow in the [[Desolation-Gray Canyons]] of the Green River (Project 39, physical and biological components).
- **Food web / trophic base**: Impact of flow regime and channel geomorphology on food availability to the native fish community of the Colorado River (Project 48), linking hydrology and sediment transport (geomorphology) to primary/secondary productivity supporting native fishes.
- **Movement and dispersal**: Effects of winter and spring flow regimes on movement, dispersal, and survival of Colorado Pikeminnow (Project 36).
- **Dam-regulated tailwater ecology**: Role of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations in determining availability of aquatic habitats along the Green River (Projects 33 and 37).

Collectively, these niches reflect a life-history-stage-structured framework (spawning/passage → nursery/rearing → overwinter survival → adult habitat) explicitly tied to **dam operations**, **channel geomorphic processes** (sediment transport, backwater/bar formation), and **trophic resource availability**.

## Key Findings

Because this source is a meeting announcement and agenda rather than a results paper, no quantitative findings are reported. Key programmatic points extracted include:

- The Recovery Program's primary applied goal: developing **flow reoperation recommendations for Flaming Gorge Dam and the Aspinall Unit dams** to benefit endangered Upper Colorado River Basin fishes.
- The roundtable was explicitly designed to integrate three previously siloed research thrusts — **streamflow**, **geomorphology**, and **food web dynamics** — recognizing their interdependence in determining habitat quality for native fishes.
- A structured peer-review process (15-min presentation + 15-min Q&A per project, followed by a brainstorming session) was used to solicit recommendations from an external reviewer ([[Wendell Minckley]], Arizona State University) and to identify **gaps requiring additional study**.
- Numbered "umbrella package" projects (Aspinall Unit and Flaming Gorge) organized the majority of applied research: nursery habitat evaluation, passage flow requirements, food availability modeling, canyon-specific flow-habitat optimization (Desolation-Gray Canyons, Westwater Canyon), movement/dispersal studies, and overwinter/ice-related nursery habitat impacts.
- The deliverable of the roundtable was a **written report of consensus recommendations** intended to directly inform dam operation decisions under the Recovery Program.
