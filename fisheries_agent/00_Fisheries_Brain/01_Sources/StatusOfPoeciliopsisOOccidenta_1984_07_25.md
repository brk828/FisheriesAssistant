# Status of *Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis* Stocks — Reddington Pass, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary
This 1984 field memorandum from W. L. Minckley documents a survey of seven stock tanks in the [[Reddington Pass]] area of Arizona, all previously stocked with hatchery-reared [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] under a 1982 Section 7 consultation between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the [[Coronado National Forest]]. The purpose was to assess survival/persistence of the reintroduced population and to collect new live broodstock to supplement or diversify the genetically narrow Dexter hatchery lineage. Of seven tanks surveyed, only two ([[Yellowstone Tank]] and [[Alambra Tank]]) contained *Poeciliopsis*, with the latter showing an unusually dense population. A total of 525 live fish were successfully transported to Dexter National Fish Hatchery as new broodstock, with minimal in-transit mortality (9 fish over two nights). No contamination by invasive [[Gambusia]] (mosquitofish) was detected at either site.

## Background & Description
- No detailed morphometric data reported; observations were limited to qualitative population/size-structure descriptions.
- **Yellowstone Tank population**: Good size structure noted, ranging from very large adults to newborn young — indicative of a stable, multi-generational, reproducing population.
- **Alambra Tank population**: Skewed toward small adults and juveniles, with comparatively few large females — interpreted as a signature of a population at an unusually high density/recent population boom rather than a stable age structure.
- Voucher specimens (preserved adult series) were collected from both productive sites for future taxonomic/genetic reference.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Small earthen stock tanks (livestock ponds) ranging from ephemeral/temporary pools to semi-permanent and permanent water bodies in Sonoran Desert grassland habitat.
- **Co-occurring fauna** across sites included [[Tiger Salamander]] larvae, [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] hatchlings/adults, [[Canyon Treefrog]], and a suite of aquatic insects characteristic of temporary waters (Nepidae, Corixidae, Notonectidae, Dytiscidae).
- Presence of Sonoran Mud Turtle and Canyon Treefrog at [[Yellowstone Tank]] was used as an indirect indicator of spring-fed, more permanent hydrology — habitat favorable to persistent *Poeciliopsis* populations.
- Absence of fish at sites like [[The Lake]], [[Government Tank]], and [[White Tank]] despite presence of turtles/salamanders suggests these sites experienced severe drawdown or drying events unsuitable for maintaining fish, even though they support amphibians and reptiles with more drought-resistant life histories.
- [[Alambra Tank]] featured dense submergent/floating vegetation (*Nuphar*, *Potamogeton pectinatus*, and an unusually delicate *Chara* sp.) with a well-developed willow riparian fringe, water level down ~1.5 m at time of survey — highly vegetated cover appeared to support markedly high topminnow abundance.
- A critical ecological concern raised throughout is **invasive species contamination risk** — surveys specifically screened for *Gambusia* (Western Mosquitofish), a known competitor/predator implicated in the decline of native *Poeciliopsis* elsewhere in Arizona; none was found in either productive tank.

## Key Findings
- **Survey scope**: All seven tanks previously stocked (per 1982 Section 7 consultation) were sampled using a 20-inch, 1/8-inch mesh seine (≥2 hauls per site along shorelines).
- **Site-by-site results**:
  1. [[Alamo Tank]] — Dry/near-dry; only tiger salamander larvae and temporary-water insects; no fish.
  2. [[Three-Feathers Tank]] — Deeper, likely held water through drought; salamanders abundant, hatchling mud turtle found; no fish reported.
  3. [[Yellowstone Tank]] — Spring-fed characteristics; *Poeciliopsis* common with good size structure; ~300 fish examined directly, ~1,000 scanned in seine; **80 females + 20 males** collected live; no *Gambusia* detected.
  4. [[The Lake]] — Excellent physical habitat (hard bottom, rocky sides, clear water) but **no fish detected**, despite a verbal report from AGF biologist Jim Brooks of prior *Poeciliopsis* sightings below the dam.
  5. [[Government Tank]] (upper/lower) — No fish; dominated by tiger salamanders and temporary-pond insects; evidence of recent very low water levels.
  6. [[White Tank]] — Large (~3 acres), heavily cattle-impacted; no fish, but abundant salamanders, insects, and mud turtles; assessed as potentially able to support fish in wetter climatic cycles.
  7. [[Alambra Tank]] — Large, heavily vegetated with willow riparian zone; **highest *Poeciliopsis* abundance observed by the author to date** — two seine hauls yielded "many thousands" of individuals; ~2,000 examined; random live stock of **>400 fish** collected; no *Gambusia* detected.
- **Broodstock transport**: Combined live stocks were aerated overnight in Tucson, then in Safford; total mortality of **9 fish** over two nights. Fish delivered to hatchery personnel (Jensen and Winham) on 23 July for transport to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Final outcome**: **525 fish** successfully established in a brood pond at Dexter NFH on 24 July 1984, representing a genetically important supplement/diversification of hatchery broodstock.
- **Population status implications**: Only 2 of 7 previously "stocked" tanks (Yellowstone, Alambra) retained detectable *Poeciliopsis* populations three years after the reported 1982 stocking — raising the question of whether all seven sites were actually stocked, given conflicting reports.
- **Management recommendations**:
  - Contact Chuck Kennedy to verify whether all seven tanks were genuinely stocked in 1982, or only two, given conflicting historical records.
  - Determine original stocking numbers for Yellowstone and Alambra tanks to properly assess genetic adequacy/bottleneck risk in the newly collected Dexter broodstock relative to the founding hatchery population.
  - This survey underscores the importance of monitoring reintroduced [[Endangered Species|endangered]] native fish populations in isolated desert stock tanks and screening for [[Invasive Species|invasive species]] contamination (notably *Gambusia*) as part of routine conservation assessment for *Poeciliopsis occidentalis*.
