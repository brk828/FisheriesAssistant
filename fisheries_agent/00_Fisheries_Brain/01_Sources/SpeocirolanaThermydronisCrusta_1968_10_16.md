# Speocirolana thermydronis (Crustacea: Isopoda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cirolanidae #stygofauna #Isopoda #Crustacea

## Executive Summary
This paper documents the re-discovery of *Speocirolana thermydronis*, a stygobitic (groundwater-dwelling) cirolanid isopod previously known from a single female holotype collected in 1964 near [[Posos De La Becerra]], Coahuila, México. In August 1967, 29 additional specimens were collected from two new localities within the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]]: an unnamed spring-fed sinkhole ("poso") and the western limnocrene of [[El Mojarral]]. The authors provide corrections and supplemental morphological data to the original 1966 description, clarify diagnostic characters distinguishing *S. thermydronis* from congeners *S. pelaezi* and *S. bolivari*, and describe the isopod's specialized interstitial travertine habitat and its likely restriction from fish-inhabited open sediments.

## Background & Description
- **Body size:** Small-bodied species; holotype (15 mm) is the largest known specimen. Congeners are considerably larger (*S. pelaezi*: 19–26 mm; *S. bolivari*: 15–35 mm).
- **Telson:** Broad and acutely tipped in both sexes — a key diagnostic character distinguishing it from *S. pelaezi* (telson broadly rounded) and *S. bolivari* (telson truncate).
- **Pereonites:** Second and third pereonites bear epimera (previously thought absent in the holotype due to a preparation artifact, not a true taxonomic feature).
- **Mandibles:** Asymmetrical, with the left incisive process overlapping the right ventrally; pars molaris sub-triangular, bearing ~34 short, cone-shaped teeth along its margin (correction to original 1966 description).
- **Antennae:**
  - First antennal flagellum: 6–14 articles (max in holotype).
  - Second antennal flagellum: 25–35 segments (max in holotype), reaching to the 5th–7th pereonite depending on specimen size.
  - Terminal segment elongation is variable, overlapping with congeneric character states.
  - Contrasts with *S. bolivari* (22–28 / 48–82 segments) and *S. pelaezi* (20 / 30 segments).
- **First maxilla:** Inner plate invariably bears 3 sparsely-plumose spines and 2 delicate setae; outer plate has up to 12 strong distal spines (10 in holotype).
- **Maxilliped:** Highly diagnostic — penultimate and antepenultimate palp segments bear fine hairlike setae plus a strong isolated spine at each distal outer corner; distinguishes it from *S. bolivari* and *S. pelaezi*, which lack these isolated distal spines on the last two segments.
- **Pereopods:** First three pairs subcheliform; propodus palmar margin armament variable (holotype: 2,3,4 spines; other specimens: 2,4,4 spines).
- **Pleopods:** Exopod of the fifth pleopod (not fourth, correcting original description) sparsely invested with plumose setae. Endopodites of all pleopods in both sexes **lack distal setae** — distinguishing this species from *S. bolivari* and *S. pelaezi*, which retain setae on the first one or two pleopod pairs. This trait is specific, not generic, contra Bowman (1964).
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Limited to modified second pleopods in males; copulatory organ arises from the base of the pleopodal endopod, variable in length (slightly shorter to slightly longer than the endopod itself).
- **Coloration (live specimens):** Ivory-white exoskeleton; dark-brown/black masticatory surfaces on mandibles and pereopod terminal claws; straw-yellow pereopod spines; variable red-to-brownish internal pigment visible through the dorsum, fading to light-brown/yellow in alcohol-preserved material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Interstitial spaces within loosely-cemented, porous [[Travertine]] blocks lining the margins of large, spring-fed pools (limnocrenes and sinkholes/"posos") in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]].
- **Key localities:**
  - **Unnamed poso** (~12 km SW of Cuatro Ciénegas): ~10 × 12 m, ~2 m deep, 0.85 m water depth; dark flocculent silt bottom with snail shells (*Paludiscala caramba*, *Durangonella* sp.) and travertine fragments; gypsum/root/travertine banks; inflow temperature 34°C. Also the type locality for the snail *[[Paludiscala Caramba]]*.
  - **El Mojarral (west laguna)**: large, clear limnocrene, ~25 × 100 m, up to 5 m deep; large springs enter at NW end; inflow temperature ~33°C; soft gray flocculent silt substrate stabilized by waterlily (*Nymphaea* sp.) beds; undercut gypsum/travertine banks.
- **Microhabitat specificity:** Isopods occupy interstitial spaces within porous travertine, not the soft flocculent sediments. When dislodged into loose sediment, individuals become entangled and incapacitated — paralleling behavior described for the troglobitic asellid *[[Asellus Stygius]]* in Kentucky marl habitats (Minckley 1961).
- **Co-occurring fauna:** An undescribed aquatic isopod species, an aquatic mite, and an oligochaete — all of which (unlike *S. thermydronis*) also inhabit the softer sediments.
- **Predation pressure:** Surface-sediment life is likely restricted to fish-free habitats; up to 12 fish species (many highly predaceous) occur in the larger springs of the Cuatro Ciénegas basin, suggesting predation shapes isopod microhabitat use.
- **Population history:** The original type locality (Posos de la Becerra marsh-river-poso complex) was destroyed by canalization/diversion of water in late 1964, followed by desiccation; associated fish and endemic snail populations were decimated. By 1967 the water table had dropped ~3 m below the surface at that site, and re-collection there failed. Snail populations were beginning to re-establish by 1967, suggesting possible future recolonization by *S. thermydronis* as habitat conditions stabilize.
- **Distribution potential:** Given the extensive subterranean channel network and shallow water table across much of the Cuatro Ciénegas basin floor, interstitial habitat suitable for *S. thermydronis* is likely abundant and widespread, though the species is difficult to detect ("secretive").

## Key Findings
- Confirmed re-discovery of *S. thermydronis*, 29 specimens collected from two new sites, expanding known distribution within the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]].
- Corrected errors in the original 1966 description regarding mandible morphology, pereonite epimera, and pleopod exopod identity (5th, not 4th).
- Established that **pleopodal endopod setae distribution** is a species-level (not generic-level) diagnostic character within *Speocirolana*, refuting Bowman's (1964) generic-rank hypothesis.
- Identified the **telson shape** and **maxilliped spination pattern** as the most robust diagnostic traits separating *S. thermydronis* from congeners *[[Speocirolana Pelaezi]]* and *[[Speocirolana Bolivari]]*.
- Demonstrated that antennal flagellum length/segment number scale with body size across *Speocirolana* species rather than serving as fixed absolute diagnostic characters.
- Documented specialized microhabitat use — porous travertine interstices rather than open sediment — likely as an adaptation to avoid fish predation and physical entanglement in flocculent silt.
- Highlighted anthropogenic habitat destruction (water diversion/canalization) as a threat to this narrowly endemic, groundwater-dependent species, an early record of hydrological disturbance impacting Cuatro Ciénegas endemic stygofauna.
- Specimens deposited at the U.S. National Museum, Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Biológico Pesqueros (México), and the National Museum of Canada.
