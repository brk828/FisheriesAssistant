# Vegetation and Flora of the Bolsón of Cuatro Ciénegas Region, Coahuila, Mexico: Summary, Endemism and Corrected Catalogue

#botany #flora #plant-taxonomy #chihuahuan-desert #endemism

## Executive Summary

This final installment of the flora series by [[Donald J. Pinkava]] (Arizona State University) summarizes the vascular plant flora of the [[Bolsón of Cuatro Ciénegas]] and surrounding sierras in central [[Coahuila]], Mexico, and provides a corrected catalogue. The study area exceeds 2,000 km² and spans elevations from about 740 m on the basin floor to more than 3,000 m on [[Sierra de la Madera]]. The flora comprises 879 taxa (860 species, 19 infraspecific taxa, in 456 genera and 114 families), a very rich assemblage for the [[Chihuahuan Desert]]. The site is the type locality for 49 taxa, of which 23 are endemic and four are no longer recognized. The author argues that the botanical and zoological evidence justifies protection of the region's habitats and biota, which face threats from grazing, farming, gypsum harvesting, logging, mining and water canalization.

## Background & Description

The Bolsón is an intermontane valley roughly 40 km wide by 25 km long, bounded by limestone sierras capped with conifer forests. Its distinctive aquatic features include lagunas, playas, gypsum dunes, rivers, subterranean tubes, artesian wells, pozos and ciénegas. The town of [[Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza]] lies near the basin center.

The vegetation is divided into eight zones:

1. Basin sacaton grasslands
2. Aquatic and semi-aquatic habitats
3. Gypsum dunes
4. Transition zone (grassland to bajada)
5. Desert scrub (rich in cacti)
6. Chaparral
7. Oak-pine and oak woodlands
8. Montane conifer forests

Botanical exploration began with collections by [[Ivan M. Johnston]] in August 1938, followed by collections in 1939 by several botanists and a further Johnston visit in 1941. Nearly 25 years later, intensive work by teams from Arizona State University, the University of Texas at Austin and the [[Desert Botanical Garden]] renewed study of the flora.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Endemism is concentrated in three areas:

- **Laguna Grande–Laguna Churince complex**, north to Río Mesquites and east onto the north tip of Sierra de San Marcos: approximately 12 endemic taxa.
- **Canyons and bajadas of Sierra de la Madera** (Agua, Hacienda and Charretera canyons): approximately 10 endemic taxa.
- **South-facing bajadas and canyons above Pozo Anteojo**: an additional 2 endemic taxa.

The author notes these areas should be correlated with the endemic fauna of the basin, which is itself rich in endemic fishes and other aquatic biota (see [[Minckley]] 1969, 1978). Threats to endemic plant habitats include goat and cattle grazing, farming, harvesting of gypsum from dunes, and conifer logging. The aquatic setting, scenic value and endemism together create an urgent case for preservation "before this important part of man's heritage is lost."

## Key Findings

- **Flora size:** 879 taxa; 860 species; 456 genera; 114 families. Cultivated plants away from habitation add a further 7 species, 7 genera and 3 families.
- **Division-level composition (Table 2):** Magnoliophyta (angiosperms) dominate with 834 taxa, including 698 dicots and 136 monocots. Pinophyta contributes 18 taxa, Polypodiophyta 29 and Lycopodiophyta 3.
- **Type localities:** 49 taxa described from the region; 45 are currently recognized, 23 are endemic, and 4 are no longer recognized.
- **Largest families:** Asteraceae and Cactaceae are prominent among the cataloged taxa; the catalogue lists numerous cacti (including several [[Coryphantha]] and [[Echinocereus]] taxa) and composites.
- **Endemic plant examples from the type list:** [[Euphorbia pinkavana]], [[Abutilon pinkavae]], [[Sabatia tuberculosa]] (an endemic gypsophile), [[Satureja maderensis]] and [[Poliomintha maderensis]] (Lamiaceae), [[Agave scabra]] ssp. *maderensis*, and [[Dyssodia gypsophila]].
- **Corrections:** The catalogue revises many names, adds new localities (often marked "Add"), and notes species placed in synonymy, such as *Quercus filiformis* within *Q. pringlei*.
- **Collections:** Specimens are deposited mainly at ASU, TEX-LL and GH herbaria; many records are from collectors such as James Henrickson, Ivan M. Johnston and Tom Wendt.
- **Conservation conclusion:** The author concludes there is sufficient botanical and zoological evidence to warrant protection of the unique habitat mosaic, tempered by the rights of residents living in the area.
