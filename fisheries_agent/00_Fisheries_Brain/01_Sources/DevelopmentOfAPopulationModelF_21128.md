# Feasibility of a Population Model for the Endangered Humpback Chub in Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Phase I feasibility evaluation, prepared by [[BIO/WEST, Inc.]] (Ronald J. Ryel and Richard A. Valdez, March 8, 1993), assesses whether a population model should be developed for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in Grand Canyon. The authors conclude that a modelling effort would be a valuable tool for integrating existing data, identifying key state and rate variables, estimating reproduction, recruitment and survivorship, and assessing population viability. They recommend a phased program, beginning with Phase II: refinement of a conceptual model. The work is framed within the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Phase II endangered fish program and its response to the 1978 biological opinion on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Humpback chub (*Gila cypha*), one of four endangered fishes of the mainstem Colorado River. The others are [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. The humpback chub in Grand Canyon is judged the most favorable of these for a productive modelling effort.
- **Research context:** Mainstem studies were conducted by BIO/WEST and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) studies were conducted by Arizona State University, Arizona Game and Fish Department and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Objectives of GCES Objective 1:** Determine ecological and limiting factors for all life stages, covering resource use, reproduction, early survivorship, distribution and movement, and biotic interactions. Objective 2 is to determine the life history schedule, including developing a population model (Objective 2A).
- **Why modelling suits this species:** The fish are accessible for study, a large research database exists, the population occupies a relatively closed system bounded by barriers, and results from other freshwater fish models may transfer.
- **Population model basics:** The document defines state variables (numbers in age groups) and rate variables (survival, fecundity, movement). A conceptual model lists these and the factors affecting them but contains no values.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Population components:** The conceptual model (Fig. 2, Appendix A) recognises four components:
  1. The LCR component, where all or most fish spend most of the year.
  2. The Colorado River/LCR inflow component, where about 95 percent of mainstem chub occur within a 13-km area.
  3. The Colorado River component away from the LCR, which holds about 5 percent of mainstem captures from 1990 to 1992. Little is known about these fish, and there is no conclusive evidence of local reproduction.
  4. The tributary component, including [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], [[Havasu Creek]] and [[Tapeats Creek]].
- **Reproduction constraint:** Cold water released from Glen Canyon Dam prevents egg maturation and larval survival in the mainstem. There is no definitive evidence of reproduction outside the LCR.
- **Environmental uncertainty:** Food supply, competing or predatory fishes, parasites, and dam and LCR flow regimes (affecting temperature, turbidity and volume) are identified as major sources of uncertainty.
- **Catastrophe risks:** Toxic chemical releases, introduction of a deadly disease or parasite, and major storm events that alter habitat.
- **Genetic uncertainty:** Considered unlikely to affect the present population because its size is believed to exceed the level of concern.

## Key Findings

- **Recommendation 1:** Develop a phased modelling program, reviewing effectiveness at the end of each phase.
- **Recommendation 2:** Proceed with Phase II, refining a conceptual model for use in the [[Integrated Humpback Chub Final Report]].
- **Recommendation 3:** Use the modelling effort to integrate data for that report.
- **Recommendation 4:** Use an age-structured model in modelling efforts and integrate it with the monitoring program, for both design and interpretation.
- **Recommendation 5:** Conduct a viability analysis, including the effects of environmental change and a second spawning population.

**Phased program:**
- **Phase I:** This feasibility evaluation.
- **Phase II:** Conceptual model production and refinement.
- **Phase III:** Data assimilation, initial parameter estimation and model development.
- **Phase IV:** Use of the model to design and evaluate monitoring, including sensitivity analysis.
- **Phase V:** Viability analysis. Phases IV and V may overlap.

**Model types:**
- **Simple [[Birth-Death Models]]:** These are judged of limited value because they cannot separate life stages. They may still help assess specific age groups and general persistence.
- **[[Age-Structured Models]]:** The [[Leslie Matrix]] (projection matrix) format is judged most useful. It can incorporate environmental and density-dependent effects and addresses Objectives 2 to 5. A complete model including all state and rate variables is considered unlikely; instead, partial formulations are expected.

**Parameter estimation:**
- Parameter needs fall into four groups: age-class population size, age-specific survival, age-specific reproduction, and movement (immigration and emigration).
- Mainstem and LCR tagging studies should yield much of this information. Younger chubs are harder to assess, though LCR estimates may be possible for individuals greater than 150 mm.
- The authors note that a sizeable database exists for other populations in Black Rocks, Westwater Canyon, Desolation Canyon, Cataract Canyon and Yampa Canyon.

**Viability framework:**
- Shaffer (1981) identifies four uncertainty sources: demographic, environmental, catastrophic and genetic.
- The authors judge environmental and demographic uncertainty, along with population connectivity, the most likely threats to persistence.
- A minimum viable population benchmark is cited (95 percent probability of survival for 1000 years; Allen et al. 1992).

**Transmittal memo (Bureau of Reclamation, GCES, March 1993):** The Glen Canyon Environmental Studies coordinator requested review from Arizona State University investigators Duncan Patten and Wendell Minckley. The memo cites the need for a more definitive basis for integrating endangered fish results and for moving the long-term monitoring program beyond simple sampling. Review and circulation to other Principal Investigators are pending.
