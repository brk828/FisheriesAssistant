# Seventh Annual Upper Colorado River Endangered Fishes Researchers Meeting Agenda

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is the meeting announcement and agenda for the seventh annual [[Upper Colorado River]] Endangered Fishes Researchers meeting, scheduled for February 14-15, 1989, in Grand Junction, Colorado. The meeting convened researchers, agency personnel, and water users working on the recovery of four endangered fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (now [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]), [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]. The agenda covered recovery program updates, standardized monitoring efforts across multiple river sub-basins, habitat studies, reintroduction programs, and agency research plans for 1989.

## Background & Description

The meeting was organized by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Colorado River Fishery Project]], with Chuck McAda serving as Fishery Biologist and primary contact. The gathering brought together researchers from multiple agencies including state wildlife divisions (Utah, Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico), federal agencies ([[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]]), and the [[Recovery Implementation Committee]]. Registration fee was $5.00 to cover refreshments.

The four target species are endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]] and were the focus of coordinated recovery efforts. [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Ptychocheilus lucius) is a large-bodied cyprinid predator. [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus) is a distinctive catostomid. [[Humpback Chub]] (Gila cypha) and [[Bonytail]] (Gila elegans) are smaller-bodied cyprinids adapted to canyon river habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The meeting addressed research across multiple sub-basins of the [[Colorado River Basin]]:

**[[Colorado River Sub-Basin]]**: Studies included endangered fishes in [[Cataract Canyon]], use of the 15-mile reach by target species, streamflow recommendations, instream flow habitat simulations, hydrology and temperature modeling, and [[Colorado Squawfish]] sport fishery in [[Kenney Reservoir]]. Distribution of non-salmonid sportfish in northwestern Colorado was also examined.

**[[Green River Sub-Basin]]**: Research covered [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] activities, fish interactions, [[Yampa River]] [[Humpback Chub]], electrofishing success evaluation, [[Flaming Gorge]] biological opinion schedule, sediment transport and channel stability, backwater habitat mapping, trophic dynamics, winter habitat studies for [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], and use of the [[Little Snake River]] by endangered species.

**[[San Juan River]]**: Research programs in both Utah and New Mexico were represented.

**Arizona**: Reintroduction and monitoring programs for [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] were ongoing.

The [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] coordinated efforts across the [[Upper Colorado River]], [[White River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Lower Green River]], [[Upper Green River]], and [[Colorado River]].

## Key Findings

**Recovery Program Structure**: The meeting agenda reveals a comprehensive, multi-agency recovery program with distinct components including the [[Biotechnical Committee]], [[Recovery Implementation Committee]], and water users' involvement in research planning.

**Geographic Scope**: Research spanned the entire [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], with coordinated monitoring in Colorado, Utah, Arizona, and New Mexico. Major study areas included the [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[San Juan River]], [[Colorado River]], [[White River]], and [[Little Snake River]].

**Research Priorities**: Key research topics included:
- Reintroduction programs ([[Bonytail]] into [[Green River]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in Arizona)
- Habitat assessment (winter habitat, backwater mapping, 15-mile reach utilization)
- Streamflow and instream flow recommendations
- Trophic dynamics and fish interactions
- Monitoring methodology (electrofishing success evaluation)
- Sediment transport and channel stability
- Temperature and hydrology modeling

**Taxonomic Work**: Gila taxonomy effort status was scheduled for update, indicating ongoing systematic work on the genus containing [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail]].

**Management Concerns**: A presentation titled "Research and recovery: are we killing them with kindness?" suggests critical evaluation of recovery methods and potential unintended impacts.

**Agency Coordination**: All major state and federal agencies with jurisdiction in the basin presented 1989 research plans, demonstrating coordinated planning across jurisdictions.
