## Key Findings

1. **Genus validity:** The authors consider *Gila* distinct from related cyprinoid genera, though they note that intergrading forms blur some boundaries and make the genus difficult to define on a single character.
2. **Morphological diagnosis:** Elongate, slightly compressed body; arched back in adults; broad, depressed head; large nearly horizontal mouth; small imbricated scales; hooked pharyngeal teeth without grinding surfaces.
3. **Size and variation:** Adults of the typical form are large, and body proportions change with age, with older specimens showing more pronounced head and dorsal profile changes.
4. **Meristic data:** One described form has 9 dorsal and 10 anal rays. Other counts are partially recovered and should be verified against the original text.
5. **Distribution:** Records are tied to major southwestern rivers, particularly the Gila and Colorado systems.
6. **Taxonomic status:** Several described forms are considered closely related, and the authors caution that the genus lacks a fully satisfactory diagnosis at the time of writing.
