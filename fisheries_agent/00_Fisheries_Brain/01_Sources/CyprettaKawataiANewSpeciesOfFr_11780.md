# Cypretta kawatai, A New Species of Freshwater Ostracoda (Crustacea)

#ostracoda #crustacea #cyprididae #freshwater-ecology #species-description

## Executive Summary

This paper formally validates the new species **Cypretta kawatai** Sohn and Kornicker, 1972, a freshwater ostracod (Crustacea) from Brazil. The species was previously used as a nomen nudum in laboratory experiments demonstrating its effectiveness as a predator of [[Biomphalaria glabrata]], a vector snail of the blood fluke causing [[Schistosomiasis]]. The holotype is an adult female measuring 0.75 mm in length and 0.49 mm in height. The species is distinguished from congeners by carapace morphology and furca characteristics, particularly the short dorsal seta of the furca (less than one-fourth the length of the adjacent claw).

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic Position:**
- Family: [[Cyprididae]] Baird, 1845
- Genus: [[Cypretta]] Vavra, 1895
- Type species of genus: *Cypridopsis (Cypretta) tenuicauda* Vavra, 1895 (by monotypy)

**Type Material:**
- Holotype: Adult female, USNM 139850 (length 0.75 mm, height 0.49 mm)
- Paratypes: USNM 139851 (length 0.70 mm, height 0.42 mm, width 0.43 mm); USNM 140953-140962, 140964-140993
- Material examined: Several hundred specimens in all stages of growth
- Type locality: Aquaria at [[The Johns Hopkins University]], Baltimore, Maryland
- Original habitat: Presumed to be freshwater ponds and ditches in [[Belo Horizonte]], [[Brazil]]

**Etymology:** Named in honor of Dr. K. Kawata, The Johns Hopkins University.

**Morphological Diagnosis:**
- Differs from *C. globula* (Sars, 1889) in having more pointed ends in dorsal carapace outline and a short dorsal seta of the furca (less than one-fourth the length of adjacent claw)
- Differs from *C. globulosa* (Sharpe, 1910) in the length of the "sense club" of the second antenna: 0.040-0.048 mm in *C. kawatai* versus 0.052 mm in *C. globulosa*
- Males unknown

**Key Morphological Features:**
- Furca with characteristically short dorsal seta (Figure 1)
- Second antenna with distinctive "sense club" measuring 0.040-0.048 mm (Figure 2)
- Carapace with pointed ends in dorsal view (Figure 3)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat:** [[Freshwater]] ponds and ditches in tropical Brazil. The species was maintained and studied in laboratory aquaria at [[The Johns Hopkins University]].

**Ecological Significance:** This ostracod species has demonstrated effectiveness as a predator of [[Biomphalaria glabrata]] (Say, 1818), a gastropod that serves as a vector snail for the blood fluke causing [[Schistosomiasis]] in humans. This predatory behavior suggests potential applications in biological control of disease vectors.

**Geographic Distribution:** Known from [[Belo Horizonte]], [[Brazil]]. The full natural range remains to be determined.

## Key Findings

1. **Formal Validation:** The species name *Cypretta kawatai* is formally validated according to the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (I.C.Z.N.), having been previously used as a deliberate nomen nudum in Sohn and Kornicker (1972).

2. **Diagnostic Characters:** The species is distinguished from congeners by:
   - More pointed carapace ends in dorsal outline compared to *C. globula*
   - Short dorsal furca seta (less than 1/4 length of adjacent claw)
   - Sense club of second antenna measuring 0.040-0.048 mm (smaller than *C. globulosa*)

3. **Type Material:** Extensive type series deposited in the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM), including holotype (USNM 139850), multiple paratypes, and several hundred specimens representing all growth stages.

4. **Biological Control Potential:** Laboratory experiments (referenced in Sohn and Kornicker, 1972) demonstrated that *C. kawatai* is an effective predator of [[Biomphalaria glabrata]], suggesting potential applications in controlling [[Schistosomiasis]] transmission.

5. **Taxonomic Status:** Only females are known; males remain undescribed. A detailed species description was noted as being in preparation at the time of publication.

6. **Collection History:** The species was cultured and studied in laboratory aquaria, with original material presumably collected from freshwater habitats in [[Belo Horizonte]], [[Brazil]].
