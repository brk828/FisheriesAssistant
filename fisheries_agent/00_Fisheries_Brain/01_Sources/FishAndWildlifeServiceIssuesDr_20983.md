# Fish and Wildlife Service Draft Jeopardy Biological Opinion for Animas-La Plata Water Project

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

On April 10, 1990, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service issued a draft jeopardy biological opinion regarding the [[Animas-La Plata]] irrigation project in southwest Colorado and northeast New Mexico. The opinion concludes that construction of the project would likely jeopardize the continued existence of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Ptychocheilus lucius). The decision is based on the discovery of a reproducing population in the [[San Juan River]], one of only three such populations remaining. No reasonable and prudent alternatives for project design or operation could be identified through consultations between the Service and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado Squawfish]] is an endangered cyprinid fish native to the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The [[Animas-La Plata]] project would pump water for domestic and irrigation use from the [[Animas River]] in Colorado to an offstream reservoir, with releases going to the [[La Plata River]]. Both rivers are tributaries of the [[San Juan River]] in New Mexico.

In 1979, the Fish and Wildlife Service issued a nonjeopardy biological opinion after only one [[Colorado Squawfish]] had been found in the [[San Juan River]]. At that time, the Service recommended additional fishery studies to clarify the status of the fish. In 1986, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] agreed to initiate limited fishery investigations as recommended in the 1979 opinion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fishery surveys were conducted on the [[San Juan River]] in New Mexico and Utah from 1987 to 1989. These surveys discovered more [[Colorado Squawfish]] than had been previously known to occur. The studies confirmed that [[Colorado Squawfish]] were reproducing successfully in the [[San Juan River]] in 1987 and 1988. Fish were captured at several locations in the [[San Juan River]] from the inflow area of [[Lake Powell]] to upstream about 154 miles.

Studies in other parts of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] suggest that wild populations of [[Colorado Squawfish]] are stable in the [[Green River]] sub-basin but appear to be declining in the [[Colorado River]] sub-basin. This finding adds to the importance of the [[San Juan River]] population as one of only three reproducing populations of this endangered species.

## Key Findings

- The draft biological opinion concludes that construction of the [[Animas-La Plata]] project would likely jeopardize the continued existence of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- No reasonable and prudent alternatives for project design or operation could be identified through extensive consultations
- Fishery surveys from 1987 to 1989 confirmed successful reproduction of [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the [[San Juan River]] in 1987 and 1988
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] were captured at several locations over approximately 154 miles of the [[San Juan River]], from [[Lake Powell]] inflow area upstream
- The [[San Juan River]] supports one of only three reproducing populations of [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- Wild populations appear stable in the [[Green River]] sub-basin but declining in the [[Colorado River]] sub-basin
- The Service recommends that further water depletions in the [[San Juan River]] not occur until studies determine flow requirements to meet habitat needs of [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- The opinion may affect the [[Animas-La Plata]] project, substantial water sales, irrigation projects on Indian lands, and water allocations to New Mexico
- The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] received construction funds and planned a groundbreaking ceremony in 1990
- If the Bureau cannot comply with the opinion, one option is to apply for an exemption under the [[Endangered Species Act]]
