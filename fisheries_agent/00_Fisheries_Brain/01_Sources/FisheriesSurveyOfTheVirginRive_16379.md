# Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team Meeting and Lower Virgin River Fisheries Survey (1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines three items from 1993 concerning the [[Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team]]: meeting minutes from a September 27, 1993 session in St. George, Utah; a BIO/WEST fisheries survey of the Virgin River below [[Riverside Bridge]], Nevada, conducted in July and August-September 1993; and fax transmittal data with woundfin survey tables. The minutes cover monitoring station standards, hatchery production, the listing status of [[Woundfin]] and the [[Virgin River Spinedace]], critical habitat, water management proposals, and the [[Red Shiner Eradication]] effort. The survey documents a river dominated by non-native fish, with native species concentrated upstream of about River Mile 43 and [[Woundfin]] first captured during the second trip.

## Background & Description

**Meeting minutes (September 27, 1993).** The Recovery Team met from 10:00 am to 4:10 pm MST, with population monitoring following for two days. Agenda items included:

- **Monitoring stations:** Ten standardized sites were confirmed, including Near Ash Creek (below confluence), Above Quail Creek, Below Hurricane Bridge, Above Washington Fields Diversion, Twin Bridges, Bloomington, Cedar Pocket, Near Beaver Dam Wash, Above/Below Mesquite Diversion, and Riverside Bridge. Nevada collects six miles below Riverside each effort, but this site is not a monitoring station.
- **Data standards:** Species codes use six characters (two for genus, two for species, two for subspecies). Sampling locations are to be specified to the nearest 1/2 mile. Reports should include summary tables, trend data per site, and adult versus young-of-the-year breakdowns.
- **Fall electrofishing:** Three sites were proposed for the week of October 25-29, split between Utah and Nevada, with [[Bureau of Land Management]] participation.
- **Chub nomenclature and listing:** The [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) treats [[Moapa River Chub]] as the same species as the Virgin River chub. The Moapa River chub will be covered under a separate Moapa River Recovery Team. Region 6 will lead [[Critical Habitat]] designation based on Region 1 work.
- **Recovery plan:** The plan was in the Regional Office awaiting signature. Listing the spinedace will require updating it within 18 months to two years.
- **Hatchery production:** At the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], about 2,500 woundfin (2-3 inches) were on hand. The Team recommended 1,000 for Utah marking studies and 1,500 for Nevada, with about 50 Nevada fish sent to the UNLV fish facility. Next year's production recommendations are due April 1, 1994.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Virgin River below Riverside is a gradient-decreasing system that transitions from confined, faster runs and riffles to slower runs and backwaters toward [[Lake Mead]]. Near River Mile (RM) 40 the channel aggrades and water leaves the main channel through the riparian corridor. The main channel is dry at about RM 38.2. Open channels reappear near RM 37 (east side) and RM 36 (west side). The survey authors describe these disruptions as probably unnatural and as likely shaping fish distribution. Beyond RM 40, little habitat suitable for native fish remained, while non-native species adapted to lentic conditions had much suitable habitat.

Water conditions in the survey and related monitoring were:

- **Temperature (Trip 1, Table 1):** 17.0 to 30.5 °C
- **Conductivity (Trip 2):** 2,300 to 3,300 µmho, within the range suitable for electrofishing
- **Flow:** Trip 2 flows rose sharply on 30 August after thunderstorms, likely the highest flow since the previous trip, forming numerous side channels and inundating vegetation

Other management context includes proposed water conservancy measures by the [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] (WCWCD). These include piping LaVerkin Springs, moving water from [[Ash Creek Reservoir]], and a winter flow reduction of 50 cfs between Quail Creek and the [[Washington Fields Diversion]]. About 10 cfs of usable water would be provided below the diversion. A [[Winter Flow Study]] examined physical habitat and instream flow effects for native species, and a [[Bureau of Land Management Instream Flow Study]] collected hydraulic and fishery data. Flow-related consultation is tied to [[Section 7 Consultation]], which has been re-opened. USGS planned to remove the [[Quail Creek Reservoir]] gages at the end of October 1993 after about 18 months of operation, with an estimated cost of $16,000 for continuing both gages.

## Key Findings

**Survey design and effort**

- Study area began at Riverside Bridge (RM 49.25) and was divided into five reaches, truncated to RM 38.75 for Trip 2 after few native fish were found below RM 40.
- Trip 1 (July 15-18, 1993): 102 habitat units and about 300 seine hauls. Trip 2 (August 29 to September 2, 1993): 146 samples and about 435 seine hauls.
- Electrofishing was used only on Trip 2 and proved ineffective under high turbidity and velocity.

**Fish community**

- The river was dominated by exotic species. [[Red Shiner]] and [[Common Carp]] were far more abundant than any other species. Red shiner, carp and [[Mosquitofish]] were the only exotics found more or less continuously.
- Native fish were more abundant in Trip 2. [[Woundfin]] catches rose from zero (Trip 1) to 46 (Trip 2). [[Speckled Dace]] rose from 5 to 38. [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] fell to less than half of its Trip 1 numbers.
- Only two [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]] were captured. The authors note these represent two of three chubs captured below Riverside in the past 16 years. A single other record came from May 1991.
- Young-of-the-year woundfin (<35 mm) were not captured. Adults of most native species were present, but no adults of flannelmouth sucker, desert sucker or largemouth bass were captured.

**Habitat use**

- Woundfin preferred sandy substrates and average depths under 1.0 ft, mainly in main-channel runs, with some in riffles and shallow side channels.
- Speckled dace favored coarse substrate and shallow side-channel riffles.
- [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Black Bullhead]] used deep eddies against bedrock or debris jams. [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Green Sunfish]] used deeper backwaters with woody debris. [[Mosquitofish]] were found in shallow backwaters.

**Interpretation**

- The rise in woundfin and speckled dace between trips "strongly suggests" they were displaced from upstream after high summer flows. Flannelmouth sucker declines suggest they moved downstream before mid-July.
- Woundfin appear to have been found as far down as about RM 36-37 in small groups (four or fewer), and the authors suggest [[Halfway Wash]] may mark the lowest extent of native distribution.

**Recovery and management actions**

- The USFWS is proceeding with listing the [[Virgin River Spinedace]] as Threatened. The 12-month petition finding was due by November, Federal Register publication by January, and critical habitat designation was expected in the following year.
- Red shiner eradication depends on barrier construction, which is delayed by lack of funding. The authors urge the Department of the Interior to act quickly.
- Woundfin marking studies were recommended to track stocked fish, and the USFWS was asked to test a latex injection method for small cyprinids.
- Survey data remain preliminary and hand-tabulated, with final reporting pending computerization and verification.
