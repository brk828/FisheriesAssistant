## Executive Summary

This document is a **research proposal**, not a primary field study, outlining a statistical analysis plan for an aerial photography database collected during the GCES-II Test Flow Program on the [[Colorado River]] between [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and [[Lake Mead]]. The proposal's stated objective is to develop predictive models of **sandbar size** using hydrologic, geomorphic, and geographic parameters, and to determine optimal sampling strategies for long-term sandbar monitoring. Notably, **no direct fish species data, morphological data, or ichthyological findings are present in this text**. The relevance to aquatic ecology is indirect: sandbar and sediment dynamics in the regulated Colorado River are foundational habitat features tied to the broader [[Grand Canyon Ecosystem]], which supports native fish species (e.g., [[Humpback Chub]]) whose habitat management is a core goal of the GCES program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While the proposal does not directly address species ecology, it operates within the broader habitat-management framework of the **[[Glen Canyon Dam]] Test Flow Program**, which investigates how engineered dam releases (test flows) affect physical habitat structures — sandbars — that are ecologically important in the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem downstream of the dam. Key hydrologic parameters tracked, which relate to habitat-forming processes relevant to fish and invertebrate habitat, include:

- Duration of test flow
- Ramp rates
- Maximum and minimum discharge
- Time at maximum discharge
- Discharge volume
- Estimated sediment transport capacity

These parameters govern the formation and persistence of sandbars, which serve as important geomorphic habitat units in the regulated river corridor, potentially influencing backwater habitats used by native fish such as the [[Humpback Chub]] and other Grand Canyon endemic species (not directly discussed in this text but contextually relevant to the GCES program's broader mission).

## Key Findings

- **No fish science data, species observations, or ecological findings are contained in this source.** The document is purely a project proposal for statistical methodology.
- The aerial photography dataset spans an extensive spatial range: **River Mile -6.5 to 225**, offering a robust sample size for sandbar analysis compared to other fluvial sediment studies.
- Proposed statistical outputs include:
  - A model predicting sandbar size from hydrologic, geomorphic, and geographic variables.
  - Recommendations for **efficient sample sizes** for monitoring sandbars over varying time scales.
- Proposed products: Draft Report (due Dec 31, 1995), Final Report, peer-reviewed journal article(s), and a Master's thesis (Colorado State University, Department of Statistics).
- Total proposed budget: **$3,695.00**, covering student salary, overhead, and minor operational costs.
- This proposal is best understood as **infrastructure/methodology** supporting downstream ecological and fish-habitat research under the [[GCES-II Test Flow Program]], rather than a direct source of ichthyological data itself.
