# Etheostoma radiosum (Orangebelly Darter) in the Washita River System, Oklahoma

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #percidae #etheostoma

## Executive Summary
This 1986 note documents the discovery of four substantial, previously unrecorded populations of **[[Etheostoma radiosum]]** (Orangebelly Darter) in tributaries of the **[[Washita River]]** system (part of Lake Texoma), Oklahoma. This constitutes a significant range extension southwest of the species' previously accepted distribution, which spanned from the **[[Blue River (Oklahoma)]]** eastward to the **[[Ouachita River]]** system in Arkansas. The authors also resurface an overlooked 1959 record from Butcher Pen Creek. Preliminary meristic (scale count) comparisons suggest that Washita drainage populations are morphologically distinct from the geographically nearest subspecies, **E. r. cyanorum** (Blue River), instead resembling subspecies from more distant, eastern drainages — raising unresolved questions about the taxonomic status of the Washita population.

## Background & Description
- **Diagnostic taxonomy basis**: Subspecies of *E. radiosum* are historically distinguished by lateral line (LL) scale counts (Moore and Rigney, 1952).
- **Unpored lateral line scales**:
  - Washita drainage specimens (n=20): mean = 11.25 (SD = 4.12)
  - Blue River specimens (n=15, *E. r. cyanorum*): mean = 4.86 (SD = 2.64)
- **Pored lateral line scales**:
  - Washita drainage specimens: mean = 43.90 (SD = 4.59)
  - Blue River specimens: mean = 50.07 (SD = 3.97)
- **Interpretation**: On both scale-count metrics, Washita drainage fish diverge substantially from *E. r. cyanorum* and align more closely with *E. r. paludosum* or *E. r. radiosum*, populations found in drainages further east — contrary to the geographically parsimonious prediction.
- **Hybrid identification**: Putative hybrids between *E. radiosum* and **[[Etheostoma spectabile]]** (Orangethroat Darter) were identified using coloration characters described by Echelle et al. (1974), with individuals exhibiting a full range of backcrossing phenotypes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Typical habitat**: Flowing, clear-water riffles with gravel or limestone rubble substrates — the species' preferred habitat type.
- **Atypical habitat records**: One population (Rock Creek) was found in a long, isolated pool with muddy water and a gravel-bedrock bottom, demonstrating some habitat plasticity beyond the typical lotic riffle niche.
- **Co-occurring species**:
  - **[[Etheostoma spectabile]]** (Orangethroat Darter) — hybridizes with *E. radiosum*, well-documented phenomenon across the species' range.
  - **[[Etheostoma gracile]]** (Slough Darter) — found alongside *E. radiosum* at Rock Creek.
  - **[[Percina macrolepida]]** (Bigscale Logperch) — co-occurred at Rock Creek and the Glasses Creek tributary site.
- **Population density**: Mark-recapture at a Little Glasses Creek riffle suggested a population of >500 individuals (*E. radiosum*, *E. spectabile*, and hybrids combined) in a short stream reach, indicating locally abundant populations.
- **Hybrid zone dynamics**: Of 132 individuals recaptured on 15 July 1985, 56% were classified as pure or nearly pure *E. radiosum*, indicating substantial but incomplete species integrity within a zone of active hybridization.

## Key Findings
1. **Range extension**: Four new populations of *E. radiosum* were documented in Washita River tributaries — Little Glasses Creek, Rock Creek, Butcher Pen Creek, and an unnamed tributary of Glasses Creek — all in Marshall and Johnston Counties, Oklahoma, well southwest of the previously known range (Blue River to Ouachita system).
2. **Overlooked historical record**: A 1959 record (Riggs and Bonn) of four *E. radiosum* specimens from Butcher Pen Creek had been missed by all subsequent authors describing the species' range; the original specimens are no longer housed at the Stovall Museum, making the new 1985 collections the only extant voucher series from this drainage.
3. **Active hybridization documented**: Extensive hybridization between *E. radiosum* and *E. spectabile* was confirmed at Little Glasses Creek, consistent with known hybridization patterns for these species elsewhere in their ranges.
4. **Systematic implications**: Meristic data (LL scale counts) show Washita drainage *E. radiosum* are morphologically more similar to distant eastern subspecies (*E. r. paludosum*, *E. r. radiosum*) than to the geographically proximate *E. r. cyanorum*, challenging simple geographic models of subspecific differentiation and warranting formal taxonomic re-evaluation before any subspecies designation is applied.
5. **Future work**: A comprehensive analysis of all *E. radiosum* subspecies, incorporating the newly discovered Washita material, was reported as in progress at time of publication, with specimens to be deposited in the Stovall Museum, University of Oklahoma.
