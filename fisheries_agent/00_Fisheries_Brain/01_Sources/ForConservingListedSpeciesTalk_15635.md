# For Conserving Listed Species, Talk is Cheaper than We Think: The Consultation Process Under the Endangered Species Act

#endangered-species-act #conservation-policy #federal-agencies

## Executive Summary

This February 1992 report by [[World Wildlife Fund]] analyzes Section 7 consultation data under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) from Fiscal Years 1987-1991. The study examines consultation records from every regional office of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NMFS) to assess claims that ESA consultation blocks hundreds of federal projects annually. The analysis demonstrates that such allegations are unfounded: of 73,560 total consultations conducted during this period, fewer than 1% of formal consultations resulted in permanently blocked projects. The vast majority of activities subject to consultation proceeded to completion while accommodating endangered species needs through reasonable modifications.

## Background & Description

Section 7 of the ESA establishes federal agency responsibilities for conserving endangered and threatened species. It requires agencies to consult with FWS or NMFS before authorizing, funding, or carrying out activities that may adversely affect listed species or critical habitat. The consultation process involves two levels: **informal consultation** provides abbreviated assessment to determine if formal review is needed, while **formal consultation** requires written biological opinions within 90 days. A "jeopardy" opinion indicates an action would "reduce appreciably the likelihood of both the survival and recovery of a listed species" but typically includes "reasonable and prudent alternatives" allowing modified project completion. This study complements an earlier [[National Wildlife Federation]] analysis covering 1979-1986.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report contextualizes ESA consultation within accelerating species loss in the United States. Approximately 500 plant and animal species became extinct in the nearly 500 years since Columbus, with 34 species lost in the decade preceding this report while awaiting federal protection. A 50-state inventory suggested 9,000 U.S. species were at risk as of 1990. Human activities—poorly planned development, unsustainable agriculture and forestry, pollution, ecosystem mismanagement, and environmental disasters like the [[Exxon Valdez]] oil spill—have increased extinction rates possibly 10-fold above historical levels. Regional variation in consultation outcomes reflects differences in listed species numbers and development intensity, with [[Region 1]] (Pacific coast) and [[Region 4]] (Southeast) experiencing most conflicts due to high species diversity and development pressure.

## Key Findings

**FWS Consultation Results (FY 1987-1991):**
- FWS conducted 71,560 informal and 2,000 formal consultations
- 350 jeopardy biological opinions issued (17.5% of formal consultations)
- Only 18 projects positively identified as blocked/cancelled due to Section 7 (less than 1% of formal consultations; average 3.6 per year nationwide)
- 35 additional proposals remain under discussion or deactivated for unknown reasons; even if all ultimately blocked, total would reach only 2.65% of formal consultations

**Abnormal Consultation Patterns:**
- Two consultation requests accounted for 221 of 350 jeopardy opinions (63%): [[EPA]] pesticide program (169 opinions for 113 chemicals/169 uses) and [[Bureau of Land Management]] Oregon timber sales (52 opinions for 175 individual sales in FY 1991)
- All 169 EPA pesticide activities proceeded with reasonable and prudent alternative labeling requirements
- Of 52 BLM timber sales receiving jeopardy opinions, 8 were dropped due to ESA and 44 sought exemptions from the [[ESA Exemption Committee]]
- If these had been programmatic consultations (one opinion each), jeopardy opinions would drop from 350 to 131 (average 26 per year)

**Regional Variations:**
- [[Region 1]] (Washington, Idaho, Oregon, California, Nevada, Hawaii): 79 jeopardy opinions; 8 confirmed blocked projects (BLM timber sales)
- [[Region 4]] (Southeast states, Puerto Rico, Virgin Islands): 76 jeopardy opinions; 2 confirmed blocked projects (both [[Corps of Engineers]] projects threatening [[Florida Manatee]])
- [[Region 2]] (Southwest): 8 jeopardy opinions; 3 blocked activities (average <1 per year)
- [[Region 3]] (Midwest): 3 jeopardy opinions; 1 blocked activity
- [[Region 5]] (New England/Mid-Atlantic): 6 jeopardy opinions; 1 blocked activity (carbofuran pesticide affecting [[Bald Eagle]])
- [[Region 6]] (Rocky Mountains/Plains): 9 jeopardy opinions; 3 blocked activities (strychnine use affecting [[Grizzly Bear]], [[Black-Footed Ferret]], others)
- [[Region 7]] (Alaska): 0 jeopardy opinions; 0 blocked projects
- Regions 2, 3, 5, 6, and 7 combined issued only 26 jeopardy opinions over five years (average 5.2 per year) across three-fourths of U.S. land area, with only 8 blocked activities (1.6 per year)

**NMFS Consultation Results:**
- 788 informal and 248 formal consultations conducted
- Only 3 jeopardy opinions issued (1.2% of formal consultations)
- 1 activity blocked, 1 completed with reasonable alternatives, 1 on hold pending biological studies
- Average 0.2 blocked activities per year nationwide

**Project Outcomes:**
- Vast majority of projects receiving jeopardy opinions proceeded through adoption of reasonable and prudent alternatives
- Many projects cancelled for reasons unrelated to ESA
- Consultation process demonstrated flexibility in accommodating both development and species conservation

**Conclusion:**
The study demonstrates irreconcilable conflicts under Section 7 occurred very rarely from FY 1987-1991. Results consistent with earlier NWF study (1979-1986) showing <1% of formal consultations blocked. Section 7 permanently blocked only a minute fraction of reviewed activities while successfully allowing federal actions to proceed with species protection accommodations.
