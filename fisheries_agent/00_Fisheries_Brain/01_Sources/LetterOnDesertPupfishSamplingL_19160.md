## Executive Summary
This document is a 1991 correspondence letter from Jason Dunham (Arizona State University) to Betsy C. Bolster (California Department of Fish and Game) outlining criteria and preferred sampling localities for a genetic study of the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*). Rather than presenting original morphological or ecological data, the letter establishes a sampling strategy to compare genetic variation across wild and artificial (refugium) populations in California, and to compare present-day genetic variation against a historical baseline established by [[Turner 1984 Copeia Study]]. The letter identifies specific wild and captive/refugial populations associated with the [[Salton Sea]] basin and ranks them by priority for inclusion in the study.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body form) are presented in this source. The document is administrative/planning correspondence focused on population sampling logistics for a genetics study, not a taxonomic or anatomical description of the [[Desert Pupfish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The letter identifies the known distribution of wild [[Desert Pupfish]] populations in California as of 1991, situated within desert aquatic habitats connected to the [[Salton Sea]] ecosystem:

- **Wild populations (California):**
  1. [[San Felipe Creek]] and [[San Sebastian Marsh]]
  2. Upper [[Salt Creek]]
  3. Shoreline pools of the [[Salton Sea]] (recently discovered/rediscovered populations)

- **Artificial/Refugium populations:**
  - [[Simone/McCallum Pond]] refugium — considered a useful genetic comparison point to wild [[Salt Creek]] fish
  - [[Palm Canyon Refugium]]
  - [[Living Desert Refugium]]
  - [[Butte County Mosquito Abatement District]] (BCMAD) populations — noted as extant as of January 1990 per the Draft Recovery Plan, though not on the recipient's available sampling list

These habitats represent a mix of natural desert spring/marsh/creek systems and human-established refugia created for conservation purposes, reflecting the species' status as an imperiled desert fish reliant on both wild remnant habitats and artificial refuge populations for persistence.

## Key Findings
- **Study Rationale:** The primary goals of the proposed genetic study were to:
  1. Compare genetic variation between **wild populations** and **artificial (refugium) populations**.
  2. Compare **current genetic variation** to levels reported in [[Turner 1984 Copeia Study]] (Copeia, 1984(2): 364–369).
  3. Compare refugia that received **supplemental stockings** after establishment versus refugia with **no additional stocking** (i.e., isolated/closed populations vs. those with immigration).

- **Sampling Priority Ranking (descending preference):**
  1. [[Salt Creek]] (wild population)
  2. [[Simone/McCallum Pond]] (refugium, paired comparison to Salt Creek)
  3. [[Butte County Mosquito Abatement District]] population(s) (valuable for comparison to historical [[Turner 1984 Copeia Study]] data)
  4. [[Palm Canyon Refugium]] and [[Living Desert Refugium]]

- **Data/Logistics Notes:**
  - The [[Draft Recovery Plan]] (DRP) and a diagram by Kim Nicol were used as reference sources for identifying refugium-wild population pairings.
  - The BCMAD populations, despite being of high scientific interest, were not included on the recipient's list of available sampling sites, creating a gap between desired and available samples.
  - The author expressed interest in personally assisting with sample collection, inspecting habitats firsthand, and gathering historical management records (stocking records, memos) from refugium managers.
  - A Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) tied to the study was to be handled separately by Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]].

- **Broader Significance:** This letter illustrates the practical, collaborative process underlying [[Conservation Genetics]] studies of endangered desert fishes, highlighting how historical baseline data (Turner 1984) and structured refugium management inform contemporary sampling design for the [[Desert Pupfish]].
