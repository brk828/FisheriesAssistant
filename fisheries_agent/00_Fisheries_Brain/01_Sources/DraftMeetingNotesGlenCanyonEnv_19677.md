# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Phase II Implementation - Meeting Notes and Program Document

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #dam-impacts

## Executive Summary

These draft meeting notes and program documents from September 1988 detail stakeholder discussions and the implementation framework for Phase II of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES). The meetings in Las Vegas and Flagstaff addressed organizational structure, constituent representation, and study priorities for assessing impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on downstream resources in the [[Grand Canyon]]. Key concerns included endangered fish species (particularly [[Humpback Chub]]), trout fishery, beach erosion, economic impacts on power generation, and compliance with [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) and [[Endangered Species Act]] requirements. The Phase II Implementation Document outlines a two-year study program focusing on effects of low and fluctuating flows on aquatic resources and detailed economic analysis of operational alternatives.

## Background & Description

The GCES Program originated from a December 1982 Department of Interior directive requiring environmental studies concurrent with generator uprating at [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. Phase I (1982-1988) collected baseline data on dam operations' impacts on downstream resources. The May 1988 Executive Review Committee Report identified critical information gaps, leading to Department of Interior approval (June 16, 1988) for Phase II studies.

The program involves cooperative efforts among the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[National Park Service]], and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], with participation from [[Western Area Power Administration]], seven [[Colorado River Basin States]], power users, environmental groups, and recreation interests. The organizational structure includes an Environmental Study Team, Economic Study Team, Technical Integration Group, and Executive Review Committee.

The [[Biological Opinion]] process under Section 7 consultation addresses endangered species concerns. A 1978 Jeopardy Opinion was reissued as a draft Non-Jeopardy Opinion based on Phase I data, but included seven Conservation Measures to be integrated into Phase II studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] ecosystem below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] has been fundamentally altered by dam operations, particularly through flow fluctuations driven by hydroelectric peaking power demands (maximum releases of 31,500 cfs). Key ecological concerns include:

**Endangered Species**: The [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fish face threats from altered flow regimes. Stakeholders questioned what ideal flows would benefit this species and how those flows would impact beach erosion.

**Trout Fishery**: Non-native trout populations established in the tailwater require evaluation under various low-flow scenarios (proposed test flows: 1,000, 3,000, 4,000, 5,000, and/or 8,000 cfs).

**Sediment Transport and Beach Dynamics**: Flow fluctuations affect sediment transport, beach aggradation and degradation, impacting both ecological and recreational resources. Studies must evaluate whether periodic high flows materially affect beach erosion.

**Riverine Habitat**: The studies encompass vegetation, wildlife, fishery, and recreation resources throughout the [[Grand Canyon]] reach, examining both current operations and historic operational impacts.

The [[Law of the River]] governs water allocation and dam operations, creating complex legal and policy constraints on operational modifications.

## Key Findings

**Organizational and Process Issues**:
- Focus Groups identified as best mechanism for monitoring process, but questions remained about composition (single vs. multiple groups, open vs. appointed membership)
- Seven Basin States emphasized they are not fully represented by Reclamation and require representation at both policy and technical levels
- Natural coalitions expected to form as Focus Groups define themselves
- Regular meeting frequency needed for effective participation and constituent notification

**Economic Model Concerns**:
- Constituent groups must be involved in economic study development
- Studies should extend beyond power analysis to include water supply optimization
- Management and releases at [[Glen Canyon Dam]] may impact [[Hoover Dam]] operations
- Concern that studies stop at Glen Canyon Dam without considering upstream impacts
- [[Western Area Power Administration]] representation required on integration team, Executive Review Committee, and economics team

**Endangered Species and Biological Opinion**:
- [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Biological Opinion characterized as "open-ended process" with changing endpoints
- No clear connection shown between Biological Opinion and constituent input in implementation diagrams
- Concern about whether Biological Opinion operates on same or independent timeframe from GCES
- Need to identify specific action prompting the Biological Opinion

**NEPA Compliance**:
- Environmental groups concerned that NEPA process be ready to continue at GCES completion
- Questions raised about why NEPA has not been initiated and whether GCES investigations obstruct EIS initiation
- Suggestion that NEPA could be instituted simultaneously with GCES effort
- Recognition that continued dam operation without NEPA process is itself a decision
- NEPA process expected to be driven by endangered species issues

**Policy and Legal Framework**:
- Need to establish long-range criteria for dam operation addressing downstream resource needs within [[Colorado River Storage Project]] and Law of the River
- Conflict within Department of Interior agencies over Law of the River interpretation
- Hierarchy of priorities needed (e.g., relationship between endangered species and Colorado River Storage Project)
- Governor representatives from all seven Basin States should serve on Executive Review Committee
- Policy team needed to evaluate Law of the River to expedite decision process

**Study Design Requirements**:
- Phase II must address: (1) effects of low and fluctuating flows on endangered fish, trout fishery, and beach dynamics; (2) detailed economic analysis of operational options
- Low flow studies for trout fishery to initiate by December 1988
- Minimum one-year study duration, with endpoint determined by flow levels and technical requirements
- Studies to examine range of minimum flows and fluctuating flows during normal dam operations
- Contingency plans required if flow conditions (too high or too low) constrain observations
- Test flows to be worked into release programs with effort to eliminate impacts on normal power operations

**Technical Oversight**:
- Senior scientist to oversee environmental work and integration of economic studies
- Scientific oversight/review processes to be established
- [[National Academy of Sciences]] to continue technical oversight role
- Integrated Study Plan to address: (1) monitoring of critical Grand Canyon resources; (2) completion of studies to fill gaps; (3) identification of resources for long-term monitoring post-GCES

**Timeline and Urgency**:
- Concern expressed that program is "conceptually no closer to changes" than nine years earlier with Peaking Power Studies
- Recognition that final answer may come too late and mitigation needed immediately
- Two-year Phase II timeline questioned regarding usefulness if NEPA is subsequently initiated

**Phase III Decision Process**:
- Executive Review Committee to develop revised report addressing additional Phase II information, refined recommendations, and constituent comments
- Department of Interior to determine if operational changes should be implemented
- If changes approved, informal consultation with Basin States to determine if changes can be accommodated through Annual Operating Plan
- If not accommodated through existing criteria, formal review under PL 90-537 and NEPA process initiation
- Alternatives could include: reregulation dam at [[Lee's Ferry]], additional transportation access, alternative flow regimes, or no action
