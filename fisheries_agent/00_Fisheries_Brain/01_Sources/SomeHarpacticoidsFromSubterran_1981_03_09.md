# Some Harpacticoids from Subterranean Waters of Greece

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Copepoda #Harpacticoida #Ameiridae #Canthocamptidae #StygobiontFauna

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper documents harpacticoid copepod fauna collected from subterranean (phreatic) freshwater and brackish wells across Greece — including the Peloponnesus, the Northern Sporades islands (Skiros, Skiathos, Alonissos, Skopelos), Attica, and the island of Zante — during surveys conducted by the Zoological Institute of the University of L'Aquila (1976–1980). Three new species are formally described: **[[Elaphoidella moreae]]** (endemic to the Peloponnesus), **[[Parapseudoleptomesochra hellenica]]** (endemic to Skiros), and **[[Nitocrella achaiae]]** (endemic to the Peloponnesus). Additionally, new locality records are reported for several previously known stygobiont and stygophilic species, expanding known distributions within the Balkan subterranean water fauna. The paper also discusses taxonomic ambiguities within the "[[Elaphoidella]] group IV" complex and the biogeographic implications of thalassoid (marine-derived) species colonizing subterranean freshwater habitats.

## Background & Description

### *Elaphoidella moreae* n. sp.
- Body length (excluding antennae/setae): 0.75–0.85 mm
- Posterior dorsal margins of body segments denticulate; abdominal segments with hair-like setulae rows
- Anal operculum slightly convex, armed with 12–15 stout spines
- Furcal rami short, subconical (L/W ratio 1.4–1.5), lacking inner-margin cilia/spinules
- Dorsal furcal seta very long (>2× ramus length), implanted on chitinous lamella
- Antenna 1: 8-segmented; Antenna 2 exopod 1-segmented with 2 apical + 2 subapical setae
- P5 basiendopod naked; exopod armed with 4 plumose spines
- Sexually dimorphic: male P3 endopod 3-segmented with distinct spine/setal arrangement; P6 reduced to chitinous lamella

### *Parapseudoleptomesochra hellenica* n. sp.
- Body cylindrical, elongated, **unpigmented and eyeless** (typical stygobiont adaptation)
- Female length: 0.42–0.49 mm; Male length: 0.39–0.44 mm
- First two abdominal segments partially fused
- Anal operculum with numerous medial spinules plus 5–6 stouter lateral spines
- Antennula 8-segmented; Antenna 2 exopod triangular with 3 apical setae
- P1 exopod shorter than endopod (reversed ratio vs. P2–P4)
- P5 exopod oval (L/W 1.5–1.6), armed with 6 long setae
- Caudal rami subparallel (L/W 1.7–1.9), asymmetric apical setae

### *Nitocrella achaiae* n. sp.
- Female length: 0.45–0.53 mm; Male length: 0.39–0.43 mm
- Furcal rami long (L/W 1.7–1.8) with markedly asymmetric apical setae
- Anal operculum: 4–5 stout medial spines + 4–5 lateral spines of similar size
- P5 basiendopod: 4 plumose setae + 1 outer slender seta (female); males show 2 plumose spines + 1 outer seta
- P6 (male): chitinous lamella bearing 3 plumose setae of differing lengths

### Comparative Notes
- *E. moreae* distinguished from *E. karamani*/*E. eucharis* by: 6-spine armature on P3/P4 exopod distal segment (vs. 5), shorter caudal rami ratio, longer dorsal furcal seta, and male P2 endopod armature.
- *P. hellenica* differs from *P. iranica* (Iran) and *P. italica* (Italy) via basiendopod armature, anal operculum morphology, and body size/slenderness.
- *N. achaiae* placed in the "chappuisi" group of *[[Nitocrella]]*, closest to *N. stammeri*, differentiated by furcal ramus proportions, genital field morphology, and P5 armature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Phreatic subterranean waters — freshwater and brackish wells, springs (some within ~5–150 m of the sea coast), indicating groundwater-marine interface habitats.
- **Ecological classification**:
  - **Stygobiont** (obligate subterranean) species: *E. moreae*, *P. hellenica*, *N. achaiae*, *N. skirensis*, *E. karamani* — several are single-island or single-region endemics.
  - **Stygophil/eustygophil** (facultative subterranean, also found epigean) species: *[[Elaphoidella elaphoides]]*, showing wide morphological variability across its Balkan range.
- **Biogeography**: Several species (e.g., *[[Nitocrella stammeri]]*) are hypothesized to be **thalassoid relicts** — marine-derived lineages that colonized continental subterranean waters during **Pliocene marine regressions** of the Mediterranean, consistent with brackish-water well records near coastlines (e.g., Zante).
- **Distributional pattern**: High endemism observed at the island/regional scale (Skiros, Peloponnesus), suggesting limited dispersal capacity typical of groundwater fauna and long-term isolation in phreatic aquifers.
- Associated fauna context: species co-occur with other Canthocamptidae and Ameiridae harpacticoids in the same well/spring microhabitats, indicating a diverse but taxonomically narrow subterranean copepod assemblage.

## Key Findings

- **Three new species described**: *Elaphoidella moreae*, *Parapseudoleptomesochra hellenica*, and *Nitocrella achaiae*, each currently regarded as **endemic** to specific Greek localities (Peloponnesus and Skiros respectively).
- **New locality records** established for *Elaphoidella karamani*, *Elaphoidella elaphoides*, *Attheyella (Brehmiella) dentata*, *Attheyella (Attheyella) crassa*, *Nitocrella skirensis*, *Nitocra spinipes*, *Bryocamptus (Bryocamptus) minutus*, and *Canthocamptus staphylinus*, extending their known Balkan/Mediterranean subterranean distributions.
- **Taxonomic discussion**: The systematic status of *E. karamani*, *E. eucharis*, and *E. elaphoides* is reassessed; *E. eucharis* is tentatively synonymized with *E. karamani* (noted as "= karamani?"). *E. elaphoides* is confirmed to show substantial intraspecific morphological variability (furcal ramus ratio 1.2–1.7), supporting Petkovski's "Rassenkreis" (species-complex/ring-species) hypothesis linking it to *E. minos*, *E. varians*, and *E. juxtaputealis*.
- **Ambiguous record**: A single damaged specimen tentatively assigned to *Nitocrella cfr. stammeri* highlights identification challenges from limited/damaged material in stygobiont surveys.
- **Biogeographic hypothesis**: The presence of species like *N. stammeri* in phreatic waters of Italy, Turkey, and Greece supports a model of Pliocene marine regression-driven colonization of groundwater by originally marine (thalassoid) copepod lineages.
- **Methodological note**: Species identifications in poorly preserved or incomplete material (e.g., *Nitocra cfr. spinipes* from Zante) required cautious, comparative diagnosis based on setal formulae and general body architecture rather than definitive confirmation.
