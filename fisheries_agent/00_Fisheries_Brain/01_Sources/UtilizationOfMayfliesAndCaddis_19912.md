# Utilization of Mayflies and Caddisflies by Some Mississippi River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #Acipenseridae #Hiodontidae #Ephemeridae #Hydropsychidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 study by [[David Townsend Hoopes]] (Iowa State University) quantifies how far several commercially and recreationally important [[Mississippi River]] fishes depend on the immature stages of mayflies and caddisflies. Stomach contents were collected from fish taken in [[Pool 19]] near Burlington and Keokuk, Iowa, and in the tailwaters of [[Lock and Dam 19]] during 1958. Mayfly naiads of the genus [[Hexagenia]] were the dominant food for most species, while larval [[Potamyia flava]] was the main caddisfly food, especially for [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]]. The nuisance caddisflies [[Cheumatopsyche campyla]] and [[Hydropsyche orris]] were largely absent from fish diets. The work was motivated by plans of river towns to control adult insect emergences.

## Background & Description

Large summer emergences of mayflies and caddisflies along the impounded upper Mississippi River produce swarms of winged adults that invade river towns, causing health, transportation, economic, and nuisance problems. Towns were considering programs to reduce these insects. The author argued that any control effort affecting aquatic immature stages should follow a thorough investigation of their distribution, ecological importance, and biology. The study began at Keokuk, Iowa, in 1957 with support from the [[National Science Foundation]], and this paper reports the food-habit component based on stomach-content analysis.

Fish were obtained mainly through commercial fishermen and fish markets. Food composition was expressed as percentages of total stomach contents and presented in radial diagrams (Figure 1). Collection gear included seines, trammel nets, gillnets, hoop nets and trot lines, and fish were sampled from April to November 1958 (Table 1). Sampled species and sample sizes with food were:

- Paddlefish: 64 examined (17 + 24 + 10 + 12 across seasons, per Table 1 food counts)
- Shovelnose sturgeon: 74 examined
- Mooneye: 136 examined
- Goldeye: 44 examined
- Channel catfish: 143 examined
- White bass: 56 examined
- White crappie: 83 examined
- Freshwater drum: 33 examined

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area is an impounded reach of the Mississippi River, where dams create pool habitats and tailwater zones below locks. Mayfly and caddisfly immatures form a substantial part of the benthic food web here. Hexagenia naiads are the same taxon that causes nuisance problems as adults, which means fish predation on naiads links directly to the emergence issue. Cladocerans, copepods, and algae were also significant food items, mainly in late summer and fall. The author found little seasonal variation in the use of caddisfly and mayfly immatures across the fish species studied.

## Key Findings

- **Hexagenia naiads were the dominant mayfly food** for most species: 46% of paddlefish food (n = 64), 68% of channel catfish stomach content (n = 143), 66% of mooneye (n = 136), 56% of goldeye (n = 44), 54% of white bass (n = 56), 42% of white crappie (n = 83), and 75% of freshwater drum (n = 33) stomach content.
- **Shovelnose sturgeon relied heavily on caddisfly larvae:** 68% [[Potamyia flava]], 7% [[Cheumatopsyche campyla]], 17% Hexagenia naiads and 8% other material across 74 fish, with caddisfly larvae forming over 60% of its diet year-round.
- **Potamyia flava** was also a notable food for mooneye (17%), goldeye (19%), white bass (9%) and white crappie (roughly 10–20% range for several species), but only 3% in channel catfish.
- **Mayfly subimagos** appeared only in channel catfish collected between June 24 and July 16, coinciding with peak mayfly emergence.
- **Nuisance caddisflies were not significant food:** [[Cheumatopsyche campyla]] and [[Hydropsyche orris]], the species causing problems in river communities, were rarely important in fish diets.
- **Potamyia flava** does not enter towns in large numbers, so it is a minor nuisance but an important food source, especially for [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]].
- **Management implication:** Control programs targeting Hexagenia would also affect important forage for multiple fish species, so the author emphasized a thorough biological investigation before any action.
