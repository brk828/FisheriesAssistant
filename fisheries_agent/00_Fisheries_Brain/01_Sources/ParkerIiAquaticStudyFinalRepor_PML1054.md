# Parker II Aquatic Study: Field Studies 1983 to 1985

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries #cyprinidae #centrarchidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation report presents baseline limnological, benthic, plankton and fishery data for the Parker II section of the [[Lower Colorado River]], collected before planned [[Riverbank Stabilization]] (riprap) work. Twelve bimonthly field trips (8-day duration) ran from October 1983 to August 1985 across eight stations within a 29-mile study area. Fish were sampled with [[Boat Electrofishing]], [[Gill Net]], [[Hoop Net]], [[Minnow Trap]] and seine methods, and habitats were compared by species composition, relative abundance and catch per unit of effort (CPUE). The assemblage consisted of 17 species, all introduced. Riparian structure supported the most fish, and [[Poston Wasteway]], an agricultural drain, differed chemically from the main river.

## Background & Description

The Parker Division extends from Headgate Rock Dam to Parker Dam within the [[Sonoran Desert]]. The region has a hot, arid climate, and the river is regulated by upstream dams. The study was designed to augment the earlier survey by [[Minckley]] (1979), which covered the whole lower Colorado River, with a more detailed look at one section. Its purpose was to describe the fishery and water quality, and the value of major habitat types, to support planning for bank stabilization and fish enhancement.

Target habitats were defined as coarse gravel/Asiatic clam substrate, sand or silt, cutbanks (Type A: steep, near-vertical with no beach; Type B: sloped sand beach at the base), riparian bank vegetation, riprap, cattail-bulrush, and dense macrophyte areas. Approximately 38 percent of the shoreline was cut or eroding. Riparian structure covered about 15 percent of the bankline, and cattail-bulrush about 32 percent. Riprap was rare.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water quality.** Temperature followed a seasonal pattern, with a low of 9.2 °C recorded in February 1984 and a high of 23.9 °C in August 1985. Dissolved oxygen was highest in February (11.5 mg/L) and lowest in August 1984 and June 1985. The river was well mixed. Poston Wasteway had higher total dissolved solids (TDS), sodium, calcium, sulfate and manganese, and it served as a thermal refuge for fish in winter. Its light extinction coefficient ranged from 1.18 to 2.44, against 0.44 to 1.91 in the main river.

**Nutrients and metals.** Nitrate-nitrogen concentrations in the main river ranged from 0.643 to 0.802 mg/L. Orthophosphate was higher in Poston Wasteway (0.006 to 0.012 mg/L). Heavy metals did not exceed U.S. Public Health drinking-water standards. Manganese was significantly higher in Poston Wasteway (362 µg/L versus 33.3 µg/L in the main river). Erosion of cutbanks was judged not to contribute significant nutrients.

**Plankton and benthos.** Diatoms dominated phytoplankton in winter and spring, and blue-green algae rose from April to October. Zooplankton peaked in spring and again the following spring. Benthic macroinvertebrates were sparse on cutbanks and riprap, while Asiatic clams and snails were relatively abundant in cattail-bulrush habitat.

**Flow.** High flows (roughly 21,000 to 34,000 ft³/s) prevailed through much of the study, which limited low-flow sampling and may have altered habitat characteristics.

## Key Findings

- **Fish assemblage:** 17 species, all introduced; the dominant fish were [[Bluegill Sunfish]] and [[Common Carp]]. [[Largemouth Bass]] were the most abundant large game fish.
- **Habitat use:** Fish abundance was greatest at riparian structure habitat and lowest at nonvegetated eroding cutbanks. Fish species composition did not differ significantly among homogeneous reaches for most species.
- **Electrofishing:** Carp and bluegill were the most abundant taxa in all habitat types. Bluegill averaged 11.4 fish per 100 m in riparian vegetation.
- **Gill nets:** Cattail-bulrush and Poston Wasteway had the highest CPUE. Riprap had the highest share of largemouth bass (43 percent of captures). Gill nets set over sand beaches caught no fish.
- **Seining:** 731 shiners (*[[Red Shiner]]*, *Notropis lutrensis*) were seined in 143 hauls. Sand substrate held 4.6 times more shiners than Asiatic clamshell substrate.
- **Threadfin shad:** About 1 percent of catch, concentrated in Poston Wasteway in cooler months.
- **Tagging:** 960 fish were tagged, with 70 returns (7.4 percent). Largemouth and smallmouth bass returns were over 60 percent from anglers. Largemouth bass moved mainly downstream.
- **Carp diet:** Of 401 carp examined in April 1985, about 12 percent contained Asiatic clams, mostly in carp over 400 mm.
- **Other taxa:** Spiny softshell turtles, crayfish and glass shrimp were recorded incidentally. No warmouth were collected.
- **Methodological limits:** Gill and hoop nets clogged with drifting debris in flowing water, backpack shocking failed in high-conductance water, and beaver damaged hoop nets.
- **Study conclusions:** Riparian structure and cattail-bulrush are valuable fish habitats, and Poston Wasteway is an important overwintering refuge. Riprap placement was expected to have limited effect on nutrient budgets. Authors noted that high-flow conditions may have made results unique to this survey period, and recommended further sampling at lower flows.
