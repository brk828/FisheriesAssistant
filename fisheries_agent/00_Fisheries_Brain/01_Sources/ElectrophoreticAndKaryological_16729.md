# Electrophoretic and Karyological Characters of Gyrinocheilus aymonieri

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Gyrinocheilidae

## Executive Summary

This 1986 study by [[Rainbroth Walter]] (Rainboth), [[Donald Buth]] and [[Gerard Joswiak]] characterizes the karyotype and allozyme (isozyme) profile of the freshwater cypriniform fish [[Gyrinocheilus Aymonieri]], a member of the family [[Gyrinocheilidae]]. The authors confirm a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 48 and find that the species' allozyme variability is typical of diploid cypriniforms. Its isozyme systems, however, show several derived features, including two non-interactive acid phosphatase loci in brain tissue and restricted interlocus heteropolymer assembly in several multimeric enzymes. The authors also note an unusually low DNA content per cell. These traits are proposed as potential autapomorphies, pending comparison with other [[Cobitidae]] and Old World cyprinids.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomy and nomenclature.** The genus *Gyrinocheilus* was erected by [[Vaillant]] for *G. pustulosus* from the Kapuas River of Borneo. The mainland species *aymonieri* was originally described by [[Tirant]] as a member of *Psilorhynchus*, and was later placed in *Gyrinocheilus* by [[Hora]]. Berg described *G. kaznakovi* and Fowler described *Gyrinocheilops pennocki*. Taki considered both to be synonyms of *aymonieri*, and the present authors agree, attributing head-shape differences to size, maturation and breeding condition. Two Chinese names (*rouxi* and *pellegrini*) belong to cyprinids now treated as synonyms of *Semilabeo prochilus*. [[Hanel]] had assumed four gyrinocheilid species, but the authors consider his justification for more than one mainland species unsupported.

**Family placement.** [[Boulenger]] gave Gyrinocheilidae subfamily rank, and Hora later raised it to family level. Osteological characters have hinted at a sister-group relationship with [[Catostomidae]], although this has never been stated explicitly. The [[Wu et al. Cladogram]] shows an unresolved trichotomy among Catostomidae, Cobitidae and Gyrinocheilidae, though the figure is not drawn that way.

**Materials.** Specimens were obtained from commercial importers in Ann Arbor, Michigan, and Los Angeles, California. Vouchers were deposited at UCLA (W84-14).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes the species' distribution as the Mekong basin (including the type locality near Phnom Penh, and Pailin and Khemerat), the Chao Phraya drainage, and the Kapuas River of Borneo. The paper provides no field ecology, habitat measurements, diet, or water-chemistry data. Its evolutionary context concerns cypriniform phylogeny, with the aim of using karyological and [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]] characters to resolve relationships among cyprinoids. The samples were commercially sourced, so their exact geographic origin is unknown.

## Key Findings

**1. Karyotype.** The diploid number is 2n = 48, which agrees with [[Hinegardner]] and Rosen (1972). The authors interpret this as a primitive condition within Cypriniformes. Because the species is not tetraploid, it does not share the tetraploidy seen in Catostomidae, which weakens the case for sister-group status with that family.

**2. DNA content.** *G. aymonieri* has 0.65 picograms of DNA per cell, which the authors describe as among the lowest reported for any teleost. They consider this an autapomorphy within Cypriniformes.

**3. Allozyme survey.** Gene products of 23 presumptive enzyme loci were examined in two samples of 10 specimens each. Eighteen loci were monomorphic in both samples, and five showed variation in one or both samples. Observed heterozygosity was 0.048 ± 0.029 (sample 1) and 0.057 ± 0.031 (sample 2). Expected heterozygosity was 0.045 ± 0.025 and 0.052 ± 0.027. Mean alleles per locus were 1.17 ± 0.08 in both samples, and 17.4% of loci were polymorphic in each. These values fall within the wide ranges reported for cyprinids and catostomids. All genotype arrays conformed to [[Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]] expectations.

**4. Sample comparability.** The two samples shared four of six polymorphisms, and their variability estimates were nearly identical. The combined chi-square test (χ² = 7.365, df = 5, P = 0.195) did not reject the hypothesis that both samples came from one population. The locus M-Icdh-A was the exception (χ² = 5.714, P = 0.017), but the small sample size limited its influence on the combined test. Genetic distance was D = 0.003 and similarity I = 0.997, so the authors consider the samples essentially uniform.

**5. Acid phosphatase.** Unlike the single-locus pattern typical of cyprinids and tetraploid catostomids, *G. aymonieri* expresses two loci (Acp-1 and Acp-2) in brain. The products do not form interlocus heterodimers, which indicates restricted subunit assembly. Because homology with the *Phenacobius mirabilis* locus Acp-A could not be established, the loci were numbered provisionally following [[Buth]] (1984).

**6. Glucose-6-phosphate isomerase.** Products of G6pi-A and G6pi-B are expressed in brain and muscle. Interlocus AB heterodimers are absent, although they occur in the cyprinid control. Intralocus heterodimers do form at the polymorphic G6pi-B locus, so restriction applies only between loci.

**7. Lactate dehydrogenase.** The species expresses A4 and B4 homotetramers and a single heterotetramer, the asymmetrical A1B3 form, in skeletal muscle. It is the only cypriniform examined that does not show the standard five-banded LDH pattern.

**8. Malate dehydrogenase.** The AB heterodimer of the cytosolic MDH loci is absent, although it is present in the cyprinid control. The S-Mdh-B polymorphism nevertheless forms intralocus heterodimers. This pattern, restricted interlocus but random intralocus assembly, is unusual in cyprinids and resembles that of some catostomids.

**9. Derived conditions summary.** The authors list three derived characters relative to other cypriniforms: (a) low DNA content per cell; (b) two non-interactive acid phosphatase loci expressed in brain; and (c) interlocus-restricted heteropolymer assembly in G6PI, LDH and MDH. Whether these are autapomorphic or synapomorphic will depend on comparable data from additional cobitids and Old World cyprinids.

**10. Methods.** Mitotic chromosome spreads came from 12 specimens using colchicine treatment and standard Giemsa staining. Starch-gel electrophoresis followed [[Buth]] et al. (1984) with 23 enzyme loci and multiple buffer systems. Genetic statistics were computed in BIOSYS-1 ([[Swofford]] and Selander, 1981).

**Conclusion.** The authors conclude that *G. aymonieri* is karyologically primitive and genetically typical for a diploid cypriniform, but distinctive at the isozyme level. Its unusual isozyme and DNA-content traits may help resolve its position among cypriniform relatives.
