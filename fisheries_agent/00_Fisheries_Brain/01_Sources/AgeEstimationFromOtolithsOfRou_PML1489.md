# Age Estimation of Roundtail Chub from Otoliths in the Verde River, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report, prepared by Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]] for the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (order E0035288, dated 20 June 1997), tests whether [[Otolith]] growth-ring counts can estimate the age of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*[[Gila Robusta]]*). Nineteen fish from the [[Verde River]] were aged. Otoliths were found to be a usable and repeatable ageing structure, while [[Scale Analysis]] was judged unreliable for this genus. The report notes that the method has not yet been validated against fish of known age.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Roundtail chub is a cyprinid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]] of western North America. It was once widespread in warm streams and large tributaries.
- **Former Arizona range:** The [[Gila River]] and [[Salt River]] systems and their tributaries (including the Black, San Pedro and Verde rivers), the [[Little Colorado River]], and the Colorado River mainstream near present-day [[Lake Powell]].
- **Conservation status:** Populations are severely depleted, and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Desert Fishes Recovery Team has recommended candidate status.
- **Size:** Typical adults are 250–350 mm total length (TL), with mature individuals reported at 150–650 mm.
- **Habitat use:** Most abundant in deep main channels of large rivers, or near protective cover in smaller streams.
- **Prior ageing work:** Earlier studies used scales in the Gila River and Turkey Creek (New Mexico) ([[Bestgen 1985]]), the Green River ([[Vanicek And Kramer 1969]]), and Fossil Creek, Arizona ([[Neve 1976]]). Otolith methods had not been well documented for western cyprinids. Earlier [[Bonytail Chub]] otolith work provided the basis for this study.

**Rationale:** Calcified structures (bones, scales, otoliths) record age, but resorption and scale regeneration during stress can give inaccurate results. Otoliths record daily and annual growth and are generally considered the more reliable structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** The [[Verde River]] at Perkinsville and Childs, Arizona.
- **Sample collection:** Eighteen fish were captured 12–16 February 1996 and supplied frozen by AZGFD, with capture location, sex, TL (mm) and weight (g). One additional male (230 mm) was collected in May 1997 at Perkinsville by ASU personnel.
- **Growth comparison:** Verde River fish were larger at each age than fish from the cooler Green River and the Gila River/Turkey Creek system. The author suggests this may reflect real thermal differences between streams, or a methodological artifact.
- **Spawning timing:** Roundtail chub spawn in spring. Fish captured in February therefore had an incomplete final growth season, which affects age assignment (see Key Findings).

## Key Findings

**Sample and size**
- 19 fish total, measuring 220–371 mm TL and weighing 90–560 g.
- Age estimates ranged from 2 to 5 years.
- Most fish (74%) were age 3, belonging to the 1993 year class.
- Mean length by age (Verde River):
  - Age 3: about 245 ± 22 mm (range 220–291, n = 14). The OCR-degraded mean could not be read with certainty.
  - Age 4: 337 ± 49 mm (range 281–371, n = 3).
  - Age 5: 314 ± 36 mm (range 289–339, n = 2).
- No clear relationship between age and size was found. Samples for ages 4 and 5 were too small for reliable size-at-age means, so the authors advise against using these estimates unless sample sizes are stated.

**Otolith method**
- Lapilli (one per pair) gave the most readable mounts. Asterisci from two fish did not yield usable mounts.
- Otoliths were removed through a cranial opening, air-dried, encased in super glue, and mounted on glass slides with CrystalBond thermoplastic cement.
- Grinding used 600–2000 grit sandpaper. Finer grits gave the best results. Otoliths were ground on one side until rings were visible at 40×, then flipped and ground to a thin section.
- Three experienced readers independently counted annuli at 40×. Their combined readings gave an age range, mean and year class for each fish. Agreement among readers was high, though this alone does not validate the method.
- Tested alternatives (burning whole or polished otoliths, and Alizarin red staining) did not improve annulus contrast. Burning in two cases increased cracking.

**Processing time**
- Otolith preparation took about "1–2 hours" per otolith after the method was established. The OCR text reads "172 hours", which is almost certainly a scanning error.
- Scale mounts take a few minutes. Reading either structure takes 2–5 minutes.

**Edge-age convention**
- Because fish were captured in February and spawn in spring, a "virtual" annulus was added at the otolith margin. The standard convention of 1 January birthdays was rejected as inappropriate here.

**Comparison with other studies**
- Turkey Creek (Gila River, New Mexico) back-calculated lengths from scales: 133 mm (age 3), 162 mm (age 4, n = 24), and 186 mm (age 5, n = 3).
- Green River (scales, Vanicek and Kramer 1969): 165 mm at age 3 (bonytail and roundtail combined, n = 56), 230 mm at age 4 (n = 16), and 277 mm at age 5 (n = 13) for roundtail alone.
- Because scales are considered unreliable for this species, the author does not treat these comparisons as validated.

**Recommendations**
- Validate the otolith method using fish of known age, from a hatchery or mark-recapture studies.
- Compare the suitability of different ageing structures.
- After validation, back-calculate length at age to examine growth history in relation to environmental factors and population dynamics.
