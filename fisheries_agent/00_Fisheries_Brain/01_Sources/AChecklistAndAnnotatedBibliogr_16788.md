# Checklist and Annotated Bibliography of the Subterranean Aquatic Fauna of Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #troglobitic-fauna

## Executive Summary

This Texas Water Resources Center special report (WRC 69-6, August 1969), by [[James R. Reddell]] and [[Robert W. Mitchell]] of Texas Tech University, is a guide to the biology of the state's subterranean waters. It has two parts. Part I is a species checklist of every aquatic animal taken from Texas underground waters for which a published or significant unpublished record exists, with cave-by-cave county records. Part II is an annotated bibliography of primary and substantive secondary references, running from roughly 1896 to 1969. Obligate cave dwellers ([[Troglobite]]s) are marked with an asterisk.

The authors argue that underground waters are "one of our most important resources" and deserve attention comparable to surface waters. Biological knowledge is uneven: the limestone aquifers of the Edwards Plateau, Stockton Plateau and Llano Region are the best known and are rich in troglobites, while deep aquifers and non-cavernous areas are largely unstudied.

## Background & Description

The document is a reference compilation rather than a single research study. It acknowledges taxonomic specialists for identifications, including isopods, crayfish, ostracods, amphipods, beetles and copepods, and thanks collectors for records of fishes from [[River Styx Cave]].

The checklist covers these groups:

- **Cnidaria:** *Hydra* sp.
- **Platyhelminthes:** Turbellarians, including five troglobitic planarians of the genus [[Sphalloplana]].
- **Nematomorpha:** an undetermined gordioid.
- **Mollusca:** snails of the genus [[Horatia]] (including *H. micra*), *Physa*, and a fingernail clam (*Sphaerium*).
- **Crustacea:** cladocerans, ostracods (*Candona*, *Cypridopsis*, *Physocypria*, *Prionocypris*, and the entocytherid *Ankylocythere sinuosa* and *Entocythere reddelli*), copepods (*Cyclops*, *Macrocyclops*, *Mesocyclops*, *Paracyclops*), isopods ([[Asellus]] spp. and [[Cirolanides Texensis]]), a thermosbaenacean (*Monodella texana*), amphipods of the genus [[Stygonectes]], crayfish, a crab (*Hemigrapsus estellinensis*), and shrimp (*Palaemonetes antrorum*).
- **Insecta:** a chironomid larva and several dytiscid and hydrophilid beetles, which the authors describe as common epigean (surface-dwelling) species.
- **Vertebrata:** fishes, including the blind catfishes *Satan eurystomus* and *Trogloglanis pattersoni*, and cave-dwelling salamanders of the genus [[Eurycea]].

The checklist records numerous Texas caves, sinkholes and wells, such as [[Ezell's Cave]], [[Cascade Caverns]], [[Devil's Sinkhole]], [[Valdina Farms Sinkhole]] and the [[Artesian Well at San Marcos]]. Most entries list only a few localities, and the full county-by-county records are best consulted in the original report.

Part II contains several hundred annotated references, from Stejneger's 1896 description of the San Marcos salamander to 1969 works. Its major themes are:

- Taxonomy and redescriptions of cave crustaceans, including Holsinger's work on [[Stygonectes]] and Maguire's work on [[Monodella]].
- Morphology and degenerate eyes in cave vertebrates, including studies by [[Eigenmann]].
- Endocrinology and metamorphosis in neotenic salamanders.
- Distribution, speciation and evolution of cave faunas.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors describe the Texas subterranean fauna as rivaled in richness by few places in the world. The most notable feature is the number of [[Troglobite]]s, obligate cave-dwelling species with unique forms.

Key environmental points from the text:

- **Aquifers and artesian wells.** Several key species are known only from artesian wells, which tap deep aquifers. The blind catfishes came from wells up to 2,000 ft deep near San Antonio. [[Uhlenhuth]] (1921) described the hydrology of the [[Purgatory Creek System]] near San Marcos, where the San Marcos salamander and blind shrimp occur.
- **Light sensitivity.** [[Maguire]] (1960) found that troglobitic ostracods (*Candona* sp.) died under light intensities about one-twentieth of normal sunlight. Maguire (1961) used cave crustaceans and crayfish to show different stages of regressive evolution.
- **Geographic origin.** [[Holsinger]] (1966) suggested that the obligate subterranean habit of *Stygonectes* evolved in the Pleistocene. Holsinger (1967) concluded that the ancestral stock invaded fresh water in the early to middle Cenozoic, and that present distribution reflects geographic isolation.
- **Comparative cave ecology.** [[Mitchell]] (1969) compared the central Texas cave communities with those of the Sierra de El Abra, Mexico, and presented hypotheses about temperate versus tropical cave faunas.
- **Endemism.** [[Curtis]] (1951) and [[Hubbs]] (1957) treated the Edwards Plateau as a center of endemic amphibians and fishes, with *Satan eurystomus* and *Trogloglanis pattersoni* listed as local endemics.

## Key Findings

- **Blind catfishes.** [[Satan Eurystomus]] and [[Trogloglanis Pattersoni]] are the only troglobitic fishes known from Texas. They come from artesian wells in Bexar County, near San Antonio, and are among the first blind catfishes known from North America. Trogloglanis was described by [[Eigenmann]] (1919), and Satan by [[Hubbs]] and Bailey (1947).
- **Blind salamanders.** The San Marcos salamander, first described as *Typhlomolge rathbuni* by Stejneger (1896), is known from the Artesian Well at San Marcos, Ezell's Cave and other sites. Mitchell and Reddell (1965) moved it to *Eurycea* and described [[Eurycea Tridentifera]] from Honey Creek Water Cave, Comal County. The guide also records *Eurycea latitans* from Cascade Caverns and *E. troglodytes* from Valdina Farms Sinkhole. Gorbman (1957) found thyroid follicles in *T. rathbuni*, which had been thought to lack a thyroid gland, though the tissue appeared inactive.
- **Cave crustaceans.** The checklist lists about a dozen *Stygonectes* species from Texas caves, with Holsinger (1966, 1967) describing or redescribing several. [[Cirolanides Texensis]] (Benedict) is a widespread troglobitic isopod. [[Monodella Texana]] (Maguire, 1965) was the first thermosbaenacean reported from the Western Hemisphere, and it was found in Ezell's Cave.
- **Palaemonetes antrorum.** This troglobitic shrimp was described by Benedict (1896) from the Artesian Well at San Marcos. Holthuis (1949) placed it in the subgenus *Alaocaris*.
- **Eye degeneration.** Eigenmann (1900, 1909) found the eyes of *T. rathbuni* to be more degenerate than those of any other cave vertebrate he had examined, and more degenerate than those of the European *Proteus anguinus*.
- **Life history.** Herald (1955) recorded a *T. rathbuni* that survived 32 months in an aquarium on brine shrimp. Dundee (1957) and Kezer (1952) describe thyroxin-induced changes in related salamanders, and Dundee found that *T. rathbuni* did not metamorphose in the same way.
- **Vertebrates in caves.** Jones and Hettler (1959) reported green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*) feeding on bats in Prassell Ranch Cave, Kendall County, where channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*) also occur. River Styx Cave, King County, yielded several epigean fishes, including *Hybognathus placita*, *Fundulus kansae* and *Cyprinodon rubrofluviatilis*.
- **Bibliographic gaps.** The authors state that no attempt was made to include references with only passing mention of a species name, and that the deep aquifers and non-cavernous areas remain largely unsampled.
