# Netting Fish Without Contaminating the Work Area

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-handling

## Executive Summary

This short technical note from [[The Progressive Fish-Culturist]] (page 55) describes a practical method for netting fish in laboratory settings where [[Radioactive Contamination]] or [[Toxic Materials]] are in use. The method attaches a plastic skirt and a rigid plastic cover to a standard net frame, which catches the water that fish scatter when they thrash. The note also includes a fragment from a separate contribution on an activity-respiration recording device. No species, sample sizes, or quantitative results are reported.

## Background & Description

The note contains two separate contributions:

- **Activity-respiration record (fragment):** Written by [[Theodore H. Blahm]] of the [[Bureau of Commercial Fisheries]], Biological Laboratory, Seattle, Washington. Only the closing lines survive. They state that a chart illustrates activity patterns detected by a device that records activity and respiration simultaneously. The chart itself and the device's method are not described in the surviving text.
- **Contamination-prevention method:** Written by [[Helen Russell Ball]] of the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare, [[Robert A. Taft Sanitary Engineering Center]], Cincinnati, Ohio.

The problem addressed is that fish lifted from water in an ordinary net thrash about, throwing water and any dissolved or suspended material onto the net, the bench, and the technician's clothing. This is a concern when radioactive or toxic substances are being handled.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document concerns handling and containment in controlled settings, mainly [[Aquarium Handling]] and experimental work with fish. It does not address natural habitats, species distributions, or environmental conditions. The contamination concern is set in a laboratory context, where fish may have been exposed to test substances and the surrounding work area must be kept clean.

## Key Findings

- **Contamination problem:** Water on thrashing fish and on the net spreads across the working area and onto the technician's clothing, which is a risk when [[Radioactive Contamination]] or [[Toxic Materials]] are used.
- **Plastic skirt:** A thin plastic sheet hung from the metal net frame like a skirt covers the outside of the net and extends about 2 inches below its bottom edge. This is the [[Plastic Skirt Netting Apparatus]].
- **Rigid top cover:** A hard plastic cover cut from 1/8-inch [[Lucite]] covers the top of the net.
- **Operation:** When fish are netted, scattered water strikes the plastic and runs back into the original container if the net is held over the aquarium for a few seconds.
- **Secondary benefit:** The apparatus tends to quiet the fish during handling.
- **Activity-respiration device (fragment):** The surviving text refers to a chart of activity patterns detected by a simultaneous activity-respiration recording device, but gives no further detail.
