## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Station Profile (Exhibit 1) for the [[Neosho National Fish Hatchery]], located in Neosho, Missouri. Established in 1888 under the Appropriation Act (25 Statute 521), it is the oldest continuously operating fish hatchery in the United States. The station's core mission is the production and distribution of catchable-size [[Rainbow Trout]] to fulfill congressionally mandated mitigation obligations tied to the [[White River]] system, principally supplying [[Lake Taneycomo]], the [[Fort Riley Army Installation]] (Kansas), and [[Capps Creek]]. In FY 1993, the hatchery also engaged in experimental production of three non-salmonid species of conservation and recreational interest: [[Paddlefish]], [[Striped Bass]], and [[Pallid Sturgeon]]. The facility additionally serves an educational and outreach function, hosting an estimated 40,000 annual visitors and featuring a permanent/portable exhibit on the endangered, cave-dwelling [[Ozark Cavefish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Facility as artificial habitat**: Rearing infrastructure comprises eight hatchery concrete tanks, sixteen concrete raceways, six concrete warmwater tanks, and eight earthen ponds — indicating differentiated thermal/flow regimes suited to coldwater (trout) versus warmwater (paddlefish, striped bass, pallid sturgeon) species husbandry.
- **Waste management**: A settling basin removes trout waste, which is pumped into the Neosho municipal sewer system, reflecting the hatchery's effluent-water interaction with the surrounding urban ecosystem.
- **Mitigation ecology**: The trout program functions as compensatory mitigation for hydrological alteration of the [[White River]], with stocked fish supporting the tailwater trout fishery of [[Lake Taneycomo]] — an artificial thermal/flow regime created by upstream dam operations.
- **Conservation linkage**: Experimental culture of [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (a federally listed species) and [[Paddlefish]] situates the hatchery within broader Mississippi River basin large-river fish conservation efforts.
- **Native fauna documentation**: The hatchery's proximity to karst habitat is evidenced by its role in publicizing the [[Ozark Cavefish]], a regionally endemic, groundwater-dependent species found in southwest Missouri cave systems.

## Key Findings

- **Production mandate (FY93)**: 200,000 nine-inch trout for [[Lake Taneycomo]] mitigation (White River, congressionally mandated 1984); scheduled to increase to 225,000 ten-inch fish in FY94.
- **Additional trout allocations**: 7,200 ten-inch trout to [[Fort Riley Army Installation]] (Kansas) and 4,500 ten-inch trout to [[Capps Creek]].
- **Experimental species production**: Paddlefish, striped bass, and pallid sturgeon cultured experimentally in FY93, expanding the hatchery's role beyond traditional trout stocking.
- **Historical significance**: Founded 1888, making Neosho NFH the oldest operating hatchery in the U.S.; located within Neosho city limits, approximately 30 miles from the Arkansas, Kansas, and Oklahoma borders.
- **Visitation and outreach**: Approximately 40,000 annual visitors; multiple 1993 public events (Hatchery Appreciation Day, Fishing Clinic/Derby, Kids Day) drew 150+ attendees each, including participation from congressional and state officials.
- **Media/education**: KODE Channel 12 (Joplin) filmed the Ozark Cavefish in August 1993 for hatchery display materials.
- **Fiscal trends (1990–1994, $000)**: Operations budget rose from 253.6 (1990) to an estimated 302.9 (1994); Maintenance funding was highly variable ($0 in 1990, up to $480.9 in 1993, dropping to est. $70.0 in 1994); staffing (FTE) held steady at 5.2–5.5.
- **Distribution totals across fiscal years**:
  - FY91: 466,955 fish (198,600 to federal waters; 268,355 to state waters; none to FWS/other or private waters)
  - FY92: 316,057 fish (42,700 federal; 242,832 other; 30,525 state)
  - FY93: 338,180 fish (122,645 federal; 215,535 other; 0 state/private)
- **Species/state distribution (FY93)**: Rainbow trout, paddlefish, striped bass, and pallid sturgeon distributed to Missouri, Kansas, and Arkansas.
- **Funding partner**: Cost-recoverable funding support from the U.S. Army (Fort Riley) noted as a contributing agency.
