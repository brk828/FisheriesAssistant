# Blue River Road Emergency Repairs and Best Management Practices: Interim Biological Opinion

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) biological opinion, dated June 16, 1997, addresses formal Endangered Species Act (ESA) section 7 consultation on normal and flood-related maintenance and repair of county-maintained roads in the upper [[Blue River]] drainage, Greenlee County, Arizona. The lead action agency is the [[U.S. Forest Service]] (Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests), with the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] (COE) in support. The opinion finalizes six emergency consultations (December 1994 to March 1995) and adopts [[Best Management Practices]] (BMPs) for a 12-month interim period, while initiating steps toward a long-term road solution.

The Service concluded the proposed action is **not likely to jeopardize** the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bald Eagle]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] and [[Peregrine Falcon]]. For the [[Loach Minnow]], the Service found that the degraded environmental baseline combined with the road effects constituted a jeopardy situation, which the 12-month interim measures and restrictions were judged sufficient to alleviate within the interim period. Further action is required before the interim period ends.

## Background & Description

The consultation was requested by the Forest Service on February 13, 1997, and the formal consultation period began February 14, 1997. The consultation history spans over four years, beginning with a 1993 inquiry by Greenlee County about [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]] (FEMA) flood-repair funding and ESA compliance.

**Proposed action:**
- Interim authorization of normal maintenance and repair under BMPs on roads along roughly 30 miles of the upper Blue River and tributaries ([[Campbell Blue Creek]], [[Dry Blue Creek]]), for 12 months.
- Formation of a multi-party team (Forest Service, COE, Service, Greenlee County, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) to develop study plans in geomorphology, historic habitat conditions, and a long-term strategic plan.
- Roads covered: [[Forest Road 281]] (about 27 miles), FR 30 (about 3 miles), FR 16 (about 0.75 miles), FR 567 (about 12 miles), FR 232 (about 4 miles), and maintained spur roads. Total mapped mileage in Table 2 is 46 miles, of which 8 miles are private and 38 miles Forest Service land.
- Activities fall into nine BMP categories, including wet and dry crossings, culverts and bridges, resurfacing, slide removal, gravel borrowing, bank restoration, channelization, and routine maintenance.

The Blue River is described as a seriously degraded ecosystem; [[Aldo Leopold]] (1921) called it "ruined." Erosion, overgrazing, timber harvest, and road construction on the floodplain are major contributing causes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Environmental baseline:** The Blue River has a wide, braided, unvegetated cobble-gravel channel with sparse riparian vegetation. The Service describes sedges as uncommon and herbaceous cover as limited. Base flows are low, with surface flow lost in some reaches in early summer, notably near Joy Crossing. Human-caused channel changes have created a "flashy" hydrograph.

**Hydrology:** The only USGS gauge (about 25 miles downstream) recorded a maximum of 30,000 cfs (1972), a minimum of 1.4 cfs (1978), a median yearly mean of 43 cfs, and a 50% exceedance level of 12 cfs. The Forest Service gauge on Campbell Blue Creek recorded a median yearly mean of 2.8 cfs.

**Fish community:** Surveys found loach minnow throughout the Campbell Blue and Blue River system. No razorback sucker was found in recent surveys, and the species was extirpated before 1904 before reintroduction attempts in 1986–1989. The Service concludes that up to nine native species (65% of the native fauna) have been extirpated in the past century. The four remaining native species are [[Speckled Dace]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]]. Nonnative species include rainbow trout, brown trout, fathead minnow, western mosquitofish, red shiner and channel catfish.

**Bald eagle:** No nests are known on the Blue River; the nearest is at [[Luna Lake]] (about 20 miles away). Three adult eagles were counted along the upper Blue in a 12-minute helicopter survey in winter 1996. Arizona's 1996 winter count totaled 361 birds.

**Southwestern willow flycatcher:** No birds have been found on the Blue River, though an auditory response was recorded there in 1989. Habitat is currently unsuitable for nesting, but the upper end and Campbell Blue Creek offer potential if riparian recovery occurs.

**Peregrine falcon:** Three active eyries lie 1.5 to 6 miles from the river and road. Approximately 16 linear miles of potential nesting cliffs (95 sites) lie within half a mile of the road. Surveys have not been conducted.

## Key Findings

**Effects and jeopardy determination**
- The Service did not concur with the Forest Service's "not likely to adversely affect" findings for razorback sucker, southwestern willow flycatcher, bald eagle and peregrine falcon, and provided a formal opinion for them.
- The Service concluded the project is not likely to jeopardize any of the five species, but judged the loach minnow situation to be jeopardy-level within the baseline, mitigated by the 12-month interim measures.
- The Blue River population is considered genetically distinct and critical to loach minnow survival. The road directly affects about 20% of the species' known range.
- Road effects were identified as the primary drivers of channel instability, sediment input, riparian suppression, and loss of large woody debris. Gravel bar skimming was flagged as a particular concern.

**Incidental take thresholds (loach minnow and razorback sucker)**
- More than 20 dead fish of any species in or within 500 yards downstream of work.
- Work outside the road easement (33 feet from centerline).
- Any toxic spill in the river or floodplain.

**Incidental take for peregrine falcon:** up to 10 adult and 20 young over 12 months, from disturbance to up to five potential nesting sites.

**Terms and conditions (highlights)**
- No work in the wetted channel during loach minnow spawning season (March 1–June 1 and September 1–October 31), except in emergencies.
- Peregrine falcon breeding season (March 1–July 15) restrictions within one-half mile of suitable cliffs, except in emergencies; annual surveys using Arizona Game and Fish protocol.
- Minimize large woody debris removal, river rerouting, riparian vegetation loss, and bypass roads.
- Biologist present during river diversions; annual reporting to Forest Service, Service and COE.

**Conservation recommendations (discretionary)**
- Terminate gravel mining in the Blue River and tributaries pending long-term solutions.
- Conduct annual willow flycatcher surveys and a standardized riparian mapping project.
- Continue bald eagle winter surveys and determine prey bases for wintering and Luna Lake breeding birds.

**Reinitiation:** Consultation must be reinitiated if take limits are exceeded, new information emerges, the action is modified, or a new species is listed. The interim approach is to be replaced by a five-year proposal before the 12-month period ends.

**Cumulative effects:** The Service notes that BMPs may only partially offset past and future habitat alterations; substantial cumulative impacts remain that must be addressed for recovery.
