# Newlands Project Biological Opinion: Cui-ui and Bald Eagle Impacts of Long-Term Water Procedures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cui-ui

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] under Section 7 consultation of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It evaluates the effects of proposed long-term operating procedures for the [[Newlands Project]] in western Nevada on two protected species: the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (a lake sucker) and the [[Bald Eagle]] (a wintering raptor). The consultation is directed at the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], which operates the project.

Modelling shows that cui-ui survival depends on how much water is diverted from the [[Truckee River]]. Under a moderate 80-year hydrologic scenario, the Service concludes that the species would survive in [[Pyramid Lake]] and the lower Truckee River if the allowable diversion is held at 320,000 acre-feet or less under the preferred diversion criteria. Wetland losses from the preferred procedures are expected to affect wintering bald eagles at [[Stillwater Wildlife Management Area]], but not the species as a whole.

## Background & Description

**Bald Eagle.** The bald eagle is the only North American representative of the sea eagles. It breeds primarily in Alaska, Canada, the Pacific Northwest, the Great Lakes, Florida and Chesapeake Bay. The last recorded breeding in Nevada was in 1886 at Pyramid Lake. Some wintering occurs in western Nevada. Historical declines were linked to reproductive failure from [[Organochlorine Pesticides]], and reproduction is thought to have improved since DDT was banned in the early 1970s. Habitat destruction from development, logging and mineral exploration is currently the greatest threat.

**Cui-ui.** The cui-ui is a federally listed endangered fish that is endemic to Pyramid Lake and the lower Truckee River. The historic population may have numbered in the millions. The adult female population is now approximately 50,000 to 75,000 individuals, mostly old females from the 1969 year class. The document gives the lower figure as 50,000 to 75,000 in one place and 60,000 to 75,000 in another. Spawning runs depend on the [[Truckee River]] inflow to Pyramid Lake and on lake elevation.

**Lahontan Cutthroat Trout.** This threatened fish is also benefited by [[Stampede Reservoir]] water releases, but it is discussed only briefly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water sources and wetlands.** Stillwater wetlands depend on seepage from unlined canals, irrigation returns and spills from [[Lahontan Reservoir]]. The draft preferred procedures would cause an annual average wetland loss of 7,000 to 9,000 acres across the project, of which 5,815 acres would be at Stillwater. Remaining wetlands would also suffer reduced water quality. Losses would affect migratory birds and forage fish, and could indirectly reduce bald eagle use.

**Bald eagle use of Stillwater.** Mid-winter counts from 1964 to 1984 averaged 5 to 15 birds, with large year-to-year variation. In winters 1986 and 1987, 35 to 40 eagles were present. Eagle numbers could not be directly correlated with the water reaching Stillwater in any year. The Service judged that eagles would disperse to other areas, and that even a complete loss of wintering use would not jeopardize the species as a whole.

**Irrigation use.** [[Table 1: Newlands Project Water Use]] reports gross diversions and irrigated acreage for 1967 to 1987. Average gross diversion was 369,112 acre-feet and average irrigated area was 62,976 acres. Diversions ranged from 225,242 acre-feet (1977) to 426,826 acre-feet (1967). The 1987 figure is projected from year-to-date data.

**Cui-ui Index Subroutine.** The [[Cui-ui Index Subroutine]] is a population model built into the [[Truckee-Carson Hydrologic Model]]. It tracks the number of swim-up larvae recruited each year and the survival of each year class. Inputs include river access, attraction flows, instream flow and temperature relationships, fecundity, egg viability, temperature tolerance, annual mortality and population size. The Service stresses that the absolute projections are uncertain. The index is meant to compare the relative effects of different procedures.

**Spawning run dynamics.**
- The share of the adult female population entering the run depends on January to April inflow to Pyramid Lake and on lake elevation.
- If the lake is below 3,812 feet, passage is only through the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]. Under those conditions, 4.6% of females enter at 349,000 to 676,000 acre-feet of inflow.
- If the lake is above 3,811 feet, passage is usually available over the delta. Then 12.0% enter at 491,000 acre-feet and 23.9% at 716,000 acre-feet.
- Maximum run size is 30,000 fish through the fishway and 150,000 through the river trap at [[Marble Bluff Dam]]. Runs are then reduced by 12.6% for fish swept over the spillway.
- Eggs are reduced by 10% for retention.

## Key Findings

**1. Wetland and eagle impacts.** The preferred procedures are expected to cause adverse effects on overwintering eagles in some years. No known nesting occurs in Nevada, so the species as a whole would not be jeopardized. The Service recommends a study linking Stillwater water supply to eagle usage.

**2. Diversion criteria comparison.** Fourteen combinations of allowable diversions (seven levels) and diversion criteria were modelled. The two criteria are labelled C and E in the tables. The text refers to Criteria "B" in some places, which is probably an OCR error for E. Criteria E consistently:
- maintains higher average Pyramid Lake inflow and elevation,
- diverts significantly less from the Truckee River,
- reduces spills at Lahontan Reservoir noticeably,
- slightly increases shortages to the [[Carson Division]].

At 320,000 acre-feet, Criteria C saves 1,250 acre-feet for irrigators but spills 6,800 acre-feet more from Lahontan Reservoir, about five times as much water wasted as saved.

**3. Hydrologic sensitivity.** The cui-ui index depends strongly on the hydrologic period chosen:
- Under a "wet" scenario (1901-17 plus 1940-80), survival is assured under any diversion.
- Under a "drought" scenario (1918-80), the index falls so low that extinction is assured.
- The 80-year record includes both extremes. The 1940-80 period (doubled to 80 years, "x2") is judged the most reasonable moderate scenario.

**4. Cui-ui index values (Table 3, 1940-80 x2).**

| Allowable diversion (acre-feet) | Criteria C | Criteria E |
|---|---|---|
| 280,000 | 41,608 | 67,768 |
| 300,000 | 37,430 | 66,178 |
| 320,000 | 32,122 | 48,083 |
| 340,000 | 9,881 | 33,332 |
| 360,000 | 2,671 | 16,830 |

The baseline index with no diversions is 136,667. The text states that Criteria E raises the index by 16,039 at 320,000 acre-feet. Table 3 gives a difference of 15,961, so the text and table differ slightly.

**5. Return on reduced diversion (Table 4).** Gains are much larger at higher diversion levels. Lowering the allowable diversion from 360,000 to 320,000 acre-feet raises the index by 204%. Lowering it from 320,000 to 280,000 acre-feet raises it by 32%, though the 280,000 value was interpolated rather than modelled. The 320,000 acre-foot level is the threshold below which further gains decline markedly.

**6. Survival conclusion.** Given the current female population of 60,000 to 75,000 and the moderate 1940-80 scenario, only Criteria E at 320,000 acre-feet or less would maintain or nearly maintain the index. The Service therefore expects cui-ui to survive as a species in Pyramid Lake and the lower Truckee River over the next 80 years under that scenario.

**7. Stampede Reservoir.** A 1982 federal court decision allocated [[Stampede Reservoir]] storage to conservation of the cui-ui and Lahontan cutthroat trout. Releases in May and June protect egg incubation and larval outmigration. In 1987, Stampede held 90,000 acre-feet. A crediting system would let the district store water in Stampede in exchange for water left in the lower river, benefiting cui-ui and reducing spills and evaporation losses at Lahontan Reservoir. This credit was not included in the model.

**8. Interim procedures.** The Service's earlier 1987 Biological Opinion concluded that continued annual interim procedures may be inadequate for cui-ui survival. The preferred long-term procedures are judged superior to all previously evaluated options.

**9. Cumulative effects.** Pending water-right transfers from agricultural to municipal use are not expected to affect listed species. The maximum annual allowable diversion will be fixed at 320,000 acre-feet, and most transfer lands are already included in the calculations. The date for this limit is illegible in the scan.

**10. Incidental take.** Incidental take is not expected and cannot be documented, so no measures to minimize it are proposed.

**11. Conservation recommendations (non-binding).** These are not required by the Endangered Species Act:
- Reclamation should consider buying water rights, including those being transferred between private owners, for conservation use.
- Reclamation should study modifying operations at Lake Tahoe Dam and the Floriston Rates to benefit Pyramid Lake.
- Reclamation should help the Service solve cui-ui passage problems at Marble Bluff Dam and the Pyramid Lake Fishway.
- Agencies should assess Stillwater's water requirements and sources, and relate them to eagle usage.

**12. Reinitiation triggers.** Consultation must be reopened if long-term procedures are violated and enforcement fails, or if the annual diversion limit is raised.
