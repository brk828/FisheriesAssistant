# Nature Correspondence Compilation (September 1955): Hemoglobin Variants, Predation in Lake Nyasa, and Endosperm Embryos in Isomeris

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This OCR-scanned compilation of letters from [[Nature]] (September 3, 1955) contains three unrelated items. The first is a medical report on [[Mediterranean-Hemoglobin E Disease]] and [[Hemoglobin Z]] in Southeast Asia. The second, and the only one with a fish focus, is a rebuttal by G. Fryer and T. D. Iles of [[Worthington]]'s hypothesis that predator absence drove the [[Adaptive Radiation]] of cichlids in [[Lake Nyasa]]. The third is a plant cytology note on double fertilization in [[Isomeris arborea]]. The fisheries content is the main ichthyological contribution.

## Background & Description

**Hemoglobin letter.** Luan Eng (Department of Parasitology and General Pathology, University of Indonesia, dated March 25) reports cases of Mediterranean-hemoglobin E disease, including a case whose father could no longer be observed. Two further cases were found. [[Hemoglobin E]] was the minor component in carriers, while in [[Mediterranean-Hemoglobin H Disease]] the pathological hemoglobin was the major component. Dr. Itano and colleagues confirmed that the pathological hemoglobin matched the original hemoglobin E described in 1954.

**Lake Nyasa letter.** Fryer and Iles (Joint Fisheries Research Organization, Nkata Bay, Nyasaland) respond to [[E. B. Worthington]], who argued that the [[Cichlidae]] radiation in Lake Victoria, Lake Edward and Lake Nyasa followed from the absence of active predators. Worthington held that large predators such as [[Lates]] and [[Hydrocynus]] restrict radiation elsewhere, and that Lake Nyasa's weaker [[Barilius]] predators permitted it. [[J. S. Huxley]] accepted the idea, and [[Ernst Mayr]] criticized it.

**Isomeris letter.** [[O. Billings]] (1937) described reproductive cytology in *Isomeris arborea*, reporting a haploid plant with 17 chromosomes, no true meiosis or syngamy, and a haploid endosperm. The authors use a later study to challenge these points.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Lake Nyasa letter gives a food-web picture of an African rift lake with many trophic guilds:

- **Cichlid predators:** *Rhamphochromis* (at least eight species, with a dentition and diet similar to pike), *Serranochromis thumbergi*, *Aristochromis christyi*, and several *Haplochromis* species (*H. kiwinge*, *H. dimidiatus*, *H. compressiceps*, *H. macrostoma*).
- **Non-cichlid piscivores:** *Bagrus meridionalis*, *Barilius microcephalus*, *Barbus rhoadesii*, and several [[Clarias]] species.
- **Mild predators:** many other species take fish as part of their diet.
- **Opportunistic predation:** almost any fish, carnivore or herbivore, will eat the eggs or fry of others when it can.

The authors argue that the percentage of predators is "extremely high" in every major habitat. They also reject [[Ernst Mayr]]'s colonization-by-river-systems explanation as an important cause of the present radiation, while accepting that it may have played a role early on.

## Key Findings

**Hemoglobin variants (Luan Eng)**
- Two additional cases of Mediterranean-hemoglobin E disease were found, in addition to the index case.
- In carriers, hemoglobin E was the minor component. In Mediterranean-hemoglobin H disease, the pathological hemoglobin was the major component (judged from spot intensity on paper electrophoresis).
- No increase in fetal hemoglobin was found in any carrier examined, using [[Singer]], Chernoff and Singer's method.
- Lehman (personal communication) found two carriers of [[Hemoglobin Z]] trait among people from Ceylon.
- Itano's moving-boundary electrophoresis at pH 6.5 and 8.6 confirmed that the pathological hemoglobin is identical to the 1954 hemoglobin E.

**Predation and radiation in Lake Nyasa (Fryer and Iles)**
- Fryer and Iles reject Worthington's claim that Lake Nyasa's fish fauna radiated because predators were absent.
- Lake Nyasa "harbours a large number of markedly predaceous fishes," so predation is not reduced there.
- Predation by Lates and Hydrocynus may be stronger, but this is probably offset by the greater numerical abundance of Nyasan predators.
- The authors conclude that the lake has probably always had its own predatory species, except possibly in the earliest stages of colonization.
- They do not adopt Mayr's view. They argue that a succession of colonizations from different river systems is not an important cause of the radiation today.

**Endosperm embryos in Isomeris (Billings reassessed)**
- Billings's haploid claim (17 chromosomes, no syngamy, haploid endosperm) is contradicted.
- Two polar nuclei were found side by side in the embryo sac, and sperm–egg fusion (syngamy) was shown in Fig. 1.
- The embryo is therefore diploid and the endosperm triploid, as in most angiosperms.
- Billings never observed double fertilization, so he wrongly inferred a haploid endosperm.
- Unresolved questions include the nature of primary endosperm nuclei and the origin and function of endosperm nodules.
