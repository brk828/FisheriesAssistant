# A Reassessment of the Distributional Status of Five Kentucky Cyprinids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology #biogeography

## Executive Summary

This 1980 study by Burr, Retzer, and Mayden re-evaluates the distributional status of five [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] species in Kentucky, updating information from Clay's 1975 "Fishes of Kentucky" and Babcock's 1977 status assessments. Three species — [[Hybognathus hayi]] (cypress minnow), [[Notropis lutrensis]] (red shiner), and [[Notropis fumeus]] (ribbon shiner) — were previously classified as having "indeterminate status" due to insufficient data, but new collections demonstrate they are more common and widespread than previously known. Two additional species, [[Ericymba buccata]] (silverjaw minnow) and [[Notropis stramineus]] (sand shiner), long known only from eastern Kentucky, were newly documented in western Kentucky drainages (lower Green, Tradewater rivers, and Mayfield Creek respectively), representing significant westward range extensions or relict populations. The authors argue for revised conservation status assessments, particularly recommending [[Hybognathus hayi]] be placed on Kentucky's endangered/threatened species list.

## Background & Description

This paper serves as a taxonomic and distributional update to Clay's (1975) "Fishes of Kentucky," driven by continued field and museum surveys following its publication. The authors compiled new distributional records from their own collections (deposited at Southern Illinois University Carbondale) plus institutional records from Cornell University, Illinois Natural History Survey, Kentucky Fish and Wildlife Resources Agency, Murray State University, Northeast Louisiana University, Tulane University, University of Louisville, and the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology. Additional data were drawn from Kentucky Fish and Wildlife river surveys (Kentucky River, Licking River, Big Sandy River).

Species accounts are presented alphabetically by genus:

- **[[Ericymba buccata]]** (silverjaw minnow) — historically restricted to Doe Run, Meade County, eastward; newly discovered at 19 sites in the lower Green and Tradewater rivers (Ohio, Daviess, McLean, Hopkins, Henderson, Webster counties).
- **[[Hybognathus hayi]]** (cypress minnow) — previously known only from Obion Creek (Hickman Co.) and Bayou du Chien (Fulton Co.); now documented additionally in Mayfield Creek and Ohio River oxbow lakes.
- **[[Notropis fumeus]]** (ribbon shiner) — abundant in the Coastal Plain province, notably in the Tradewater River and lower Green River drainage.
- **[[Notropis lutrensis]]** (red shiner) — two distinct centers of distribution: the Coastal Plain province and tributaries of the lower Tradewater River.
- **[[Notropis stramineus]]** (sand shiner) — common in the Appalachian Plateau province (eastern KY); newly found as a relict population in Mayfield Creek (western KY).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat associations**: Most species favored small to medium streams (3–7 m wide) with sand, gravel, mud, or silt substrates and negligible to moderate flow; vegetation was generally absent or minimal at collection sites.
- **Co-occurring species**: [[Ericymba buccata]] was commonly associated with [[Notropis whipplei]] (steelcolor shiner), [[Notropis umbratilis]] (redfin shiner), [[Pimephales notatus]] (bluntnose minnow), [[Phenacobius mirabilis]] (suckermouth minnow), and [[Semotilus atromaculatus]] (creek chub).
- **Syntopy and competitive dynamics**: [[Notropis fumeus]] and [[Notropis umbratilis]] were found together at nearly all sites, with N. umbratilis typically more abundant (documented across 11 paired collections, Table 1). Notably, N. umbratilis was rare in Bayou du Chien/Obion Creek where N. fumeus dominated.
- **Competitive exclusion hypothesis**: The absence of [[Notropis lutrensis]] from Clarks River was attributed to possible competitive exclusion by [[Notropis whipplei]] and/or [[Notropis spilopterus]] (spotfin shiner).
- **Range expansion drivers**: Several species show evidence of anthropogenically-influenced range shifts:
  - [[Ericymba buccata]] has expanded ranges in Ohio, Pennsylvania, Illinois, and the Mobile River basin; possible drivers include channelization creating suitable habitat in the lower Green River, or previously inadequate sampling.
  - [[Notropis fumeus]] may be expanding/increasing in abundance in the Green River in response to increased siltation and turbidity from human disturbance.
  - [[Notropis lutrensis]] is characterized as a tolerant, aggressive disperser thriving in turbid waters, mirroring recent range expansions documented in Illinois.
- **Biogeographic patterns**: The disjunct north-south range pattern of E. buccata (per Wallace 1973) is discussed in context of either failed establishment in intervening drainages or extinction of formerly connected populations; the Green River's main channel is proposed as a dispersal corridor.
- **Conservation concerns**: [[Hybognathus hayi]] is already extirpated in Missouri and Illinois; planned stream modifications in Kentucky threaten remaining populations.

## Key Findings

- **[[Ericymba buccata]] (Silverjaw Minnow)**: Newly confirmed at 19 sites in the lower Green and Tradewater rivers (1978–1979 collections); >50 individuals collected at several sites. Absent from Pond River due to unsuitable habitat. Represents either a recent range expansion (possibly from southern Indiana populations) or a previously undetected long-standing population.

- **[[Hybognathus hayi]] (Cypress Minnow)**: Previously restricted to Obion Creek and Bayou du Chien; now also documented in Mayfield Creek and Ohio River oxbow lakes. Found in muddy, slow-moving creeks, sloughs, and lakes, rarely with vegetation. Up to 13 individuals collected from a single Ballard County locality. Rarely co-occurs with the congener [[Hybognathus nuchalis]] in Kentucky. Recommended for endangered/threatened listing due to habitat modification threats and extirpation precedent in Missouri and Illinois.

- **[[Notropis fumeus]] (Ribbon Shiner)**: One of the most abundant Notropis species in Kentucky's Coastal Plain; most numerous shiner in the Bayou du Chien drainage. A prior Kentucky River record (Jackson, Breathitt County) is deemed a likely misidentification of [[Notropis ardens]] (rosefin shiner). Found in quiet pools over sand, mud, or silt. Status upgraded from "indeterminate" to common/widespread.

- **[[Notropis lutrensis]] (Red Shiner)**: Exhibits two distinct Kentucky distribution centers — the Coastal Plain and lower Tradewater River tributaries. Found in main channels of the Ohio and Mississippi rivers. A prior South Fork Little Barren River record is considered a likely misidentification (confused with N. spilopterus or N. whipplei). Common to abundant despite prior "indeterminate" status classification; noted for aggressive dispersal behavior and turbidity tolerance.

- **[[Notropis stramineus]] (Sand Shiner)**: Common across the Appalachian Plateau province from Doe Run to the Big Sandy Basin; absent from the upper Cumberland drainage in Kentucky (though present in adjacent Tennessee). Newly documented as a relict population in Mayfield Creek (Graves and Carlisle counties), associated with [[Phenacobius mirabilis]], [[Notropis lutrensis]], and [[Etheostoma spectabile]] (orangethroat darter). No significant morphological divergence noted between eastern and western Kentucky populations. Notably absent from seemingly suitable adjacent creeks (Obion, Shawnee) despite collecting efforts.

- **Table 1 Data**: Paired collection counts of N. umbratilis vs. N. fumeus across 11 Kentucky localities (1978–1979) consistently show N. umbratilis as more abundant, with ratios ranging from roughly 2:1 to over 4:1 in most samples.

- **Taxonomic note**: An undescribed form related to N. stramineus or [[Notropis procne]] (swallowtail shiner) occurs in the Little South Fork of the Cumberland River (per R.E. Jenkins, pers. comm.), highlighting unresolved taxonomic complexity in the region.
