# Establishment of the Red Shiner (Notropis lutrensis) in the Lower Colorado River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

Carl L. Hubbs (Scripps Institution of Oceanography) reports the recent establishment of the [[Red Shiner]] ([[Notropis Lutrensis]]) in the [[Lower Colorado River System]], based on [[Field Collections]] made in January and December 1953. The species is a small, nonpredatory forage fish that may support [[Game Fish Management]]. Establishment is attributed to escapes from the [[Arizona Fish Farms]] ponds near Blythe, California, and the stock is traced to the vicinity of [[Lake Buchanan]], Texas. Specimens are provisionally identified as intergrades between [[Notropis Lutrensis Lutrensis]] and [[Notropis Lutrensis Suavis]]. The paper also separates the introduced fish from [[Notropis Mearnsi]].

## Background & Description

- **Common name origin:** "Red shiner" refers to the body color, especially the fins of breeding males. The species is valued for its abundance, small size, and use as a home aquarium fish.
- **Context:** The native fish fauna of the lower [[Colorado River]] had been largely replaced by introduced game and pan fishes (Dill, 1944; more recent field work). Hubbs suggests that an established forage fish could raise game fish production.
- **Bait introductions:** Many bait minnows have been brought into the region ([[Robert Rush Miller]], 1952). The red shiner was not found in earlier collections by [[Gilbert and Scofield]] (1898) or [[Dill]] (1944), nor in more recent seining, which indicates a recent establishment.
- **Morphology (as described):**
  - Anal rays typically 9, rarely 8 or 10.
  - Lateral line scales: 32 to 34 in 10 river specimens (mean 33.33); 33 to 36 in 30 specimens from the Coachella Valley canal basin (mean 34.07).
  - Pale general color, with a well-developed dark shoulder bar and a weak, diffuse lateral band.
  - Young have a middorsal file of melanophores, usually evident behind the dorsal fin.
- **Subspecies identity:** Introduced stock is slenderer than typical [[Plains Red Shiner]] and resembles the slender form of the southern Texas coastal plain. Body depth relative to standard length averages 4.29 (range 3.7 to 4.7; n = 113) in the introduced stock, compared with 2.7 to 3.2 in Illinois adults (Forbes and Richardson, 1920).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collection sites:**
  - Colorado River and backwaters from Baja California Norte and Arizona, including sites near [[Gadsden, Arizona]], [[San Luis, Sonora]], and the Rio Hardy slough below El Mayor.
  - The settling basin of the [[Coachella Valley County Water District]], about 124.5 miles from the river by open canal ([[All-American Canal]] and [[Coachella Main Canal]]).
- **Breeding:** Size-frequency data show that the species breeds over a prolonged period, as it does in its native range. By winter, young-of-the-year overlap in size with yearling and older fish.
- **Habitat fit:** Waters where the species has established resemble the larger streams of Oklahoma and western Arkansas, where it is abundant. The annual temperature range of the [[Coachella Valley Branch of the All-American Canal]] is 48 to 86 °F (C. S. Hale), which approximates much of its native range.
- **Dispersal pathways:**
  - Escapes from the Arizona Fish Farms brood ponds, which drain through screens each time they are emptied (J. Bruce Kimsey, letter of December 17, 1953).
  - Escapes into ditches on the Arizona side of the river.
  - Possible contribution from escaped or dumped bait.
- **Native range:** From Illinois and South Dakota west and southwest through the Great Plains, south to the Rio Grande and related systems of northeastern Mexico.

## Key Findings

**1. Recent establishment and first records (January 1953):**
- Collection H53-1 (January 18, 1953, 2 miles south of Gadsden, Arizona): 149 young to adult specimens, 17 to 46 mm standard length.
- Collection H53-2 (January 18, 1953, 2 miles north of San Luis, Sonora, in a backwater): 9 half-grown to small adults, 20 to 31 mm.
- Station 1153-3 (January 20, 1953, below El Mayor): one small adult, 30 mm.
- Downstream collections (H53-4 to 7, 9, 10) contained no red shiners.

**2. Settling basin collections (December 1953):**
- December 3, 1953 (J. E. Fitch and W. J. Baldwin): 500 to 700 shiners seined; 94 young to adult specimens, 15 to 50 mm, preserved.
- December 15, 1953 (R. D. Beland): one dead and one live specimen.
- December 28, 1953 (J. E. Fitch, "Common Sense" seine): 284 specimens, 18 to 56 mm. Size frequencies were approximately the same as the December 3 collection.

**3. Meristic data:**

| Locality | n | Mean anal rays |
|---|---|---|
| River, Arizona and Baja California | 153 | 8.91 |
| Settling basin, Coachella Valley Canal | 94 | 9.00 |

- Dorsal-to-anal origin distance, standard length proportion: males (n = 88, 37 to 56 mm) 0.25 to 0.30, mean 0.278; females (n = 31, 37 to 51 mm) 0.25 to 0.29, mean 0.273. These values agree with [[Notropis Lutrensis Suavis]] (less than 0.31 in males, less than 0.29 in females) rather than typical [[Notropis Lutrensis Lutrensis]] (more than 0.33 in males, more than 0.30 in females).
- Suavis is reported to average about 8.7 anal rays, and typical lutrensis about 9.2.

**4. Identification conclusion:**
- Species identity is "indubitable."
- Subspecific status is provisional: the introduced stock is classified as intergrades, *Notropis lutrensis lutrensis* × *suavis*.
- The genus *Notropis* had not previously been reported from California or Baja California Norte. The species is new to those states and to Arizona.

**5. Taxonomic separations:**
- The fish are not [[Notropis Mearnsi]] Snyder, which has about 40 lateral line scales and usually 8 anal rays. Hubbs suggests that form be treated as *Notropis formosa mearnsi*.
- *Notropis forlonensis* from the Rio Pánuco system is regarded as a subspecies, *N. lutrensis forlonensis*.

**6. Origin of the introduction:**
- Circumstantial evidence points to escapes from the [[Arizona Fish Farms]] at Ehrenburg, Arizona (near Blythe). The farm reportedly first propagated the species in 1948 and sold it as bait from Needles to Imperial Dam.
- The brood stock came from the vicinity of Lake Buchanan (Llano and Burnet counties, Texas), a Colorado River reservoir in the Gulf of Mexico drainage.

**7. Ecological implications:**
- Rapid population increase suggests the new waters are well suited to the species.
- The paper points to a general "lack of faunal saturation" in western streams after depletion of native fishes.
- The species may serve as an important forage fish in game fish management.

**8. Summary statement:** The red shiner has recently become established in the Colorado River and connected waters of Arizona, Baja California Norte, and California. Its establishment is attributed to escapes from Arizona Fish Farms. Its status as a forage fish is considered potentially important for game fish management.
