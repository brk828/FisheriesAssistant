# Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811: Bonytail Chub & Colorado Squawfish Management

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 administrative memorandum issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque, NM) authorizing subpermittee activities under blanket Endangered Species Permit PRT-676811. It does not contain primary morphological or ecological field data, but rather establishes legal authorization for the **collection, holding, propagation, marking, and interstate transport** of two federally endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] cyprinids: the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, now commonly termed [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]). The permit formalizes a coordinated recovery pipeline between [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Nevada) and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico), reflecting interagency species recovery efforts within USFWS Region 2 and Region 6.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological descriptions are provided in this regulatory text. However, contextual/taxonomic notes can be inferred:

- **Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*)**: Member of family [[Cyprinidae]]; historically found throughout the mainstem [[Colorado River]]; one of four large-river "big-river fishes" endemic to the basin, characterized generally (per species literature) by a slender, streamlined body, small head, and thin caudal peduncle adapted to swift, turbid water — though these traits are not stated explicitly in this document.
- **Colorado Squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)**: Largest North American cyprinid; the document notes fish are handled at the **fry stage** and raised to **fingerlings of 2–5 inches (5–12.7 cm)** in length prior to release, indicating a managed early-life-stage growth benchmark used operationally by the hatchery program.
- All fish destined for release are subject to **physical marking** prior to removal from the hatchery, implying use of standard fisheries marking techniques (e.g., fin clips, tags) for post-release identification — a proxy indicator of study/monitoring design rather than morphology per se.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are native to the **[[Colorado River]] mainstem and associated large-river tributaries**, historically occupying warm, turbid, swift-flowing riverine habitat typical of the pre-dam Colorado River Basin.
- The permit distinguishes **two basin populations**: 
  - Bonytail chub collected from the **Colorado River** (lower basin, near Willow Beach, AZ/NV) for **ex-situ propagation and species recovery**.
  - Colorado squawfish fry sourced from **Dexter National Fish Hatchery** for grow-out and release into the **Upper Colorado River Basin**, indicating active reintroduction into historic range.
- The operation exemplifies a **hatchery-supported recovery strategy** for basin-endemic big-river fishes whose wild populations have been severely reduced by damming, flow regulation, and introduction of non-native competitors/predators — issues central to the decline of both [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]].
- Specimens not used for live propagation may be **salvaged/preserved** and distributed to scientific/educational institutions, extending the ecological/taxonomic value of mortality events within the recovery program.

## Key Findings
- **Permit Scope**: Subpermittees (L.R. Miller, P. Carboni, J. Bigelow, R. Escoffier, E. Kelly, H. Reimer, P. Locher, and hatchery staff) are authorized under blanket permit PRT-676811 through **December 1990**.
- **Bonytail Chub Recovery Pipeline**: Fish taken from the **Colorado River** are to be held and transported to **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, New Mexico, for propagation and recovery — establishing Dexter as a central captive breeding hub for this species.
- **Colorado Squawfish Stocking Program**: Authorization to raise **50,000 fingerlings** (2–5 in.) from fry supplied by Dexter NFH, for distribution to **USFWS Region 6** and release into the **Upper Colorado River Basin**, representing a large-scale population augmentation effort.
- **Marking Protocol**: All squawfish must be marked prior to hatchery removal, in coordination with USFWS personnel (Grand Junction, CO) and the **Colorado Division of Fish and Game**, suggesting integrated multi-agency monitoring of stocked cohorts.
- **Specimen Distribution Protocol**: Salvaged/preserved specimens may be shared with scientific/educational institutions, contingent on documentation (permit copy, memorandum, and cover letter specifying recipient and quantities), with compliance copies filed to the **Technical Services Division, Fish and Wildlife Enhancement**, Albuquerque.
- **Conservation Context**: This document is a primary-source artifact of late-20th-century **endangered species recovery infrastructure** for the [[Colorado River Basin]], illustrating the regulatory and logistical mechanisms (permitting, interagency coordination, hatchery propagation) underlying recovery of two federally listed cyprinids under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
