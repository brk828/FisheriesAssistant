# Record of Decision: Sanchez Copper Project Environmental Impact Statement

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-ecosystem

## Executive Summary

This Record of Decision (ROD), issued by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Arizona State Director on March 8, 1993, approves the Mining Plan of Operation submitted by AZCO Mining Inc. for an open pit copper mine on BLM-administered claims in Graham County, Arizona. The project combines conventional open pit mining with [[Heap Leaching]] and solvent extraction-electrowinning (SX-EW) to produce an estimated 25,000 tons of copper per year over 17 years, employing about 210 people with an annual payroll of 8.15 million dollars.

The decision is conditional on the mitigation, monitoring and permitting requirements of the Final Environmental Impact Statement (FEIS). The document is primarily a land-use and mining decision; its aquatic content concerns groundwater and surface-water protection of the [[Gila River]] system rather than fish or invertebrate populations. No fish species are named in the text.

## Background & Description

The proposed action covers:

- **Mining:** Conventional open pit mining at an average of 25 million tons of ore and waste rock per year, with a pit about 4,000 feet in diameter and 1,200 feet deep.
- **Disturbance:** About 1,400 acres overall, of which roughly 1,000 acres are waste dumps, leach pad and roads, and about 277 acres the pit itself. Much of the pit area was disturbed by earlier exploration.
- **Processing:** Heap leaching with sulfuric acid, followed by SX-EW, with facilities adjacent to the mine.
- **Access:** Split between the Sanchez and Solomon Pass roads, with about 74% of heavy truck traffic, including all acid tankers, routed to Solomon Pass.
- **Power and water:** Electricity from the Graham County Electrical Cooperative (about 87,000 MWh per year). Water use averages 2,600 acre-feet per year (about 1,600 gpm, seasonally 1,000 to 2,000 gpm), drawn from pit dewatering and confined aquifer wells, with all water consumed on site except septic effluent.
- **Financial assurance:** A surety bond recalculated in three-year increments, plus a 50,000-dollar (1992) trust fund for five years of post-closure fence maintenance.

The ROD also reviews five alternatives plus a no-action alternative, which is identified as environmentally preferable. The alternatives concern access routes, [[Heap Leach Pad]] configuration, groundwater disposal (irrigation canals or reinjection into the [[Lower Gila Conglomerate Aquifer]]), reclamation slope treatment, and partial pit backfilling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water resources.** The analysis relies on a 1989 Sergent, Hauskins and Beckwith (SHB) study and an October 7, 1992 study by [[Geothermal Surveys Inc.]] (GSI). The project is expected to have little or no effect on surface water in the Gila River. Water-rights disputes over the Upper Gila Basin, involving federal tribal rights and state rights, are in litigation. The ROD does not shield AZCO from that litigation.

**Water quality.** Protection depends on permits, including an [[Aquifer Protection Permit]] from ADEQ, a [[Clean Water Act Section 404 Permit]] from the Army Corps of Engineers, and a Storm Water Discharge permit from EPA. Mitigation includes interception wells or a cut-off wall if upper Gila Aquifer water seeps into the pit, diversion ditches with rip-rap, and sedimentation ponds for runoff.

**Air and aesthetics.** Modeling used EPA-approved methods and predicts that all applicable standards will be met, with dust controlled by suppressants, sprays and foggers. The pit and waste piles are the most visible permanent features.

**Wildlife.** Mitigation covers migratory birds and Endangered Species Act animals, with netting of solution ponds, $2,500 for local bat research, a possible wildlife water source, and a wildlife monitoring plan developed with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

**Socioeconomics.** The project is expected to bring a beneficial effect to the Safford area through about 8 million dollars in annual wages, indirect economic stimulus, and an increased tax base for Graham County schools.

## Key Findings

- **Groundwater clarification (Appendix 2):** Following GSI's additional testing, AZCO committed to extract all project groundwater from confined aquifers below the site, namely the upper or lower Gila Conglomerate aquifers. These are expected to be non-tributary to the Gila River. The study found that the Upper Gila Conglomerate aquifer is confined at the project site, which corrects earlier FEIS wording. The GSI findings were that the aquifer does not discharge to the flood-plain alluvium.
- **Streamflow:** The additional study concluded that the project will have no measurable impact on Gila River streamflow.
- **Reclamation:** Revegetation with native plants will cover 765 of the 1,400 disturbed acres. This is reduced from roughly 900 acres in the Draft EIS. Success is defined as a native community of similar diversity and density sustained for two years.
- **Public involvement:** Fifty-eight people attended the August 28, 1991 information meeting. Fifty-two registered at the September 27, 1991 scoping meeting, where 11 gave oral testimony and 37 letters were received. The DEIS drew 39 comment letters from over 275 copies distributed. The FEIS drew 18 comment letters, addressed as six issues (public participation, water rights, water quality, legal compliance, the Undue and Unnecessary clause of 43 CFR 3809, and technical matters).
- **Issue responses:** BLM concluded that NEPA procedures were met, that water-rights litigation lies beyond the EIS scope, that water-quality analysis is adequate given the permitting requirements, and that the project conforms to current 3809 interpretation.
- **Monitoring:** BLM will inspect the site at least twice a year. ADEQ leads water and air monitoring, and a wildlife monitoring plan must be approved before solution ponds are filled.
- **Decision:** The Arizona State Director authorized the project subject to permits, mitigation, and monitoring. The project is judged to conform to accepted environmental standards.
