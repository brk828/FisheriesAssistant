# Removal of Humpback Chub from the Colorado River and Little Colorado River, October 1978

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This field report, written by C. O. Minckley of the Biology Department, documents a collection survey of [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) at the confluence of the [[Little Colorado River]] and the [[Colorado River]] during 15–17 October 1978. The goal was to remove additional adult humpback chubs from the wild to supplement a captive rearing program at the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. Three adults were transported to the hatchery. Humpback chubs were found in very low numbers in the mainstream Colorado River, which the author links to low flows from [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. Smaller chubs were more common in the Little Colorado River.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] is a cyprinid fish of the Colorado River system. The 1978 survey was a continuation of earlier removals made for the hatchery's rearing program. Collections used [[Trammel Net]] sets in the mainstream and [[Seining]] in the Little Colorado River. Sampling was done at the Colorado River and the Little Colorado River, with some work upstream in the Little Colorado River and in its smaller downstream section.

Field methods included:

- **Transport:** Adult fish were carried in styrofoam shipping containers. Smaller fish were moved in plastic garbage bags holding five gallons of water with an oxygen atmosphere.
- **Holding:** Fish were moved from the field to the hatchery and placed in a raceway about five hours after leaving the Colorado River.
- **Permitting:** The work was done under an endangered species permit issued to Minckley by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (the permit number is partly illegible in the scan).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water conditions and flow regime strongly shaped what the crews found:

- **Flow:** Releases from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] were minimal during the survey. Shoreline areas normally fished for this species were dry.
- **Temperature:** The mainstream Colorado River measured 21 °C. The author did not think this temperature explained the lack of humpback chubs.
- **Fish use:** Few fish of any species used the mainstream study area. [[Common Carp]] were the only consistently abundant species there.
- **Little Colorado River backwater:** Water temperature was 21 °C. The backwater received heavy use by young-of-year and yearling fish.
- **Downstream seining area:** This area was either dry or held mainstream Colorado River water when sampled, and no humpback chubs were taken there.

Species associated with humpback chubs in the Little Colorado River included [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Mountain-Sucker]], and [[Speckled Dace]]. Several [[Larval Fish]] (catostomids) were also present.

## Key Findings

- **Mainstream Colorado River:** Trammel net sets yielded six adult *Gila cypha* (humpback chubs). The report also lists flannelmouth suckers, bluehead mountain-suckers, and two carp. Three of the adult chubs were transported to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] to add to the rearing program. The count of humpback chubs is partly illegible in the scan, and the figure of six is taken from the discussion.
- **Unexpectedly low numbers:** The extremely low numbers of adult humpback chubs in the mainstream were not expected. The author attributes them to low mainstream flows from Glen Canyon Dam.
- **Little Colorado River:** Smaller humpback chubs and smaller individuals of other species were present. The chubs were taken from a small backwater area and from the mainstream Little Colorado River.
- **Downstream seining area:** No humpback chubs were taken. Large numbers of fathead minnows were present, along with several larval catostomids.
- **Seining totals (Table 2, partly illegible):** The table lists humpback chub (22 at the upstream station), flannelmouth sucker (7), fathead minnow (40), common carp (2), bluehead mountain-sucker (1), channel catfish (1), and larval fish. Column assignments are not fully legible.
- **Implications for sampling:** The report suggests the continued use of this area by many species indicates a need for further study of the system. Larger-fish use patterns did not appear to affect the smaller fish present.
