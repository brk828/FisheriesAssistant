## Executive Summary

This document is a **legal stipulation** (settlement agreement) from a 1998 U.S. District Court case (Arizona) concerning livestock grazing management on National Forest allotments and its impact on federally protected/at-risk species, prominently including the **[[Loach Minnow]]** (a small benthic cyprinid fish native to the Gila River Basin). While not a primary scientific research paper, the stipulation contains ecologically actionable data: it mandates livestock exclusion from ≥99% of occupied, suitable, and potential habitat for the species named in the underlying litigation, establishes biological survey protocols for loach minnow habitat assessment in **East Eagle Creek**, and creates riparian protection measures (fencing, monitoring) relevant to fish habitat conservation in the **San Francisco River**, **Stone Creek**, and **Dry Blue Creek** systems within the Apache-Sitgreaves and Gila National Forests.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:** The stipulation explicitly names the **[[Loach Minnow]]** as a species requiring habitat evaluation, alongside implicit co-occurring species referenced generally as those in "Forest Guardians' Motion for Preliminary Injunction" (likely including [[Spikedace]] and the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], a non-fish riparian-obligate species also covered by this agreement).
- **Taxonomic identity:** Loach minnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*) is a small (<9 cm), benthic, riffle-dwelling cyprinid endemic to the Gila River drainage in Arizona and New Mexico, USA — notable for its dorsally flattened body and reduced swim bladder, adaptations for holding position in fast-flowing riffle habitats.
- **Legal/administrative context:** The case involved multiple plaintiffs (Southwest Center for Biological Diversity, Forest Guardians) against the U.S. Forest Service, with intervention by the Arizona and New Mexico Cattle Growers' Associations, centered on the impacts of livestock grazing on federally listed aquatic and riparian species habitat across multiple National Forest allotments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Perennial and intermittent stream riparian corridors within semi-arid montane and desert grassland ecosystems of the Gila River Basin (Arizona/New Mexico).
- **Key waterways referenced:**
  - **[[San Francisco River]]** riparian corridor (Luna and Pleasant Valley allotments) — subject to weekly livestock monitoring due to sensitivity.
  - **[[East Eagle Creek]]** — targeted for direct field evaluation of loach minnow habitat suitability and species presence.
  - **[[Dry Blue Creek]]** (Luna allotment) — approximately 50 yards of perennial stream slated for fencing to exclude livestock.
  - **[[Stone Creek]]** — evaluated jointly for loach minnow and southwestern willow flycatcher habitat suitability.
- **Threats to habitat:** Livestock grazing was identified as the primary anthropogenic stressor, causing streambank degradation, riparian vegetation loss, sedimentation, and water quality decline — all factors known to degrade riffle habitat critical for loach minnow spawning and foraging.
- **Habitat protection mechanism:** Fencing and exclusion of livestock from riparian corridors is the primary intervention, reflecting broader recognition in southwestern fisheries conservation that grazing-induced riparian degradation is a leading cause of decline for imperiled Gila Basin fishes (e.g., loach minnow, spikedace, Gila trout, Gila chub).

## Key Findings

- **Habitat exclusion commitment (Table A allotments):** The U.S. Forest Service certified that livestock had already been excluded from **at least 99%** of occupied, suitable-but-unoccupied, and potential habitat for the species at issue (including loach minnow) across a list of allotments in the **Apache-Sitgreaves** and **Gila National Forests** (e.g., East Eagle, Luna, Pleasant Valley, Cedar Breaks, Devils Park, Eagle Peak, Mangas Valley allotments).
- **Planned future exclusion (Table B allotments):** Additional allotments were scheduled for habitat exclusion completion **by August 15, 1998**, including Cow Flats, Blue, Devils Park, Gila River, Largo Gulch/R-Park, McCarty Basin, and Taylor Creek allotments.
- **Monitoring protocol:** Excluded habitat is required to be inspected **at least biweekly** (weekly for the sensitive San Francisco River corridor) to check fence integrity and livestock intrusion, establishing a systematic habitat-protection monitoring regime directly benefiting fish habitat quality.
- **Direct field assessment of loach minnow habitat:** Within 10 days of the agreement, a **Forest Service fish biologist and/or fisheries scientist** was required to evaluate **East Eagle Creek** for (a) suitability as loach minnow habitat and (b) actual species presence — with plaintiff-designated biologists and permittees permitted to participate, reflecting a collaborative, multi-stakeholder scientific verification process.
- **Contingent grazing removal:** If suitable loach minnow habitat was confirmed in the East Eagle drainage, the grazing permittee would be requested (and if necessary, directed) to remove livestock pending completion of NEPA review and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service consultation — establishing a precautionary, science-triggered management action tied directly to fish habitat findings.
- **Riparian infrastructure commitment:** Fencing of the perennial reach of Dry Blue Creek (~50 yards) was mandated by August 15, 1998, to physically exclude cattle from this fish-bearing perennial water source.
- **Joint habitat assessments:** Field reviews of the **San Francisco River** and **Stone Creek** (Luna allotment) were mandated to determine suitability for both loach minnow and the co-occurring southwestern willow flycatcher, indicating a multi-species riparian conservation approach linking aquatic and terrestrial riparian-dependent taxa.
- **Enforcement and compliance mechanisms:** A structured, time-bound escalation protocol (verbal notice → certified letter → permit suspension/cancellation or impoundment under 36 CFR provisions) was established to ensure rapid remediation of livestock incursions into excluded fish habitat, with impoundment timelines of 5–20 days depending on ownership status.
- **Transparency provisions:** The Forest Service agreed to provide plaintiffs with maps of exclusion areas (by May 15, 1998) and access to biweekly inspection data, supporting ongoing independent verification of habitat protection efficacy for loach minnow and associated species.
