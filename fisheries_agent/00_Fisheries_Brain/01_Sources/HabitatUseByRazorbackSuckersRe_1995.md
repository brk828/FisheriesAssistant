# Habitat Use by Razorback Suckers Released into the Imperial Division, Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a **Phase Two study plan** (proposed research design, not final results) prepared by the Arizona Game & Fish Department for the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, designed to investigate habitat use by the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) following its release into the Imperial Division of the [[Lower Colorado River]]. The [[Razorback Sucker]] was listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act in November 1991, with critical habitat designated in April 1994 covering much of its historic range, including the 100-year floodplain of the Imperial Division. Because prior habitat-use research was concentrated in the upper Colorado River basin and in reservoirs (e.g., [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Mead]], [[Senator Wash Reservoir]]), which differ substantially from the lotic, side-channel, and backwater-dominated habitat of the Imperial Division, this study proposes to track hatchery-reared, telemetry-tagged adult razorback suckers to determine actual habitat use patterns. The plan builds on a prior Phase One effort (Bradford & Vlach 1995) that developed predictive "razorback sucker habitat assessment indices," which Phase Two aims to validate against in situ tracking data.

## Background & Description

The source text is a study/methods plan and does not describe detailed morphological traits of *Xyrauchen texanus*. The only morphological criterion specified is:

- **Study specimens:** Hatchery-raised adult razorback suckers, standard/total length **>380 mm**, sourced from stocks already acclimated to natural foods.
- **Tagging method:** Fish were to be surgically implanted with **ultrasonic transmitters** and given a post-surgical recovery period prior to release.

No further anatomical, coloration, or life-history morphological data (e.g., the species' characteristic dorsal keel typical of [[Catostomidae]]) are discussed in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historic range:** Formerly abundant throughout the mainstem and major tributaries of the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]], adapted to a naturally fluctuating hydrologic regime.
- **Habitat alteration:** River regulation for domestic, industrial, agricultural, and hydropower use has converted the river into a series of artificially stable reservoirs connected by lotic reaches, fundamentally altering the species' niche.
- **Compounding threats:** Introduction of non-native fish species has contributed to near-extirpation from the lower Colorado River.
- **Current extant populations:** Concentrated mainly in [[Lake Mohave]], with smaller populations in [[Lake Mead]] and [[Senator Wash Reservoir]].
- **Study area habitat types:** The Imperial Division (~64 km, restricted study focus to the lower 13 km between [[Imperial Dam]] and [[Martinez Lake]]) is characterized by:
  - Long, uninterrupted **lotic mainstem** river
  - Numerous **side channels**
  - **Backwaters**
  - Bordering **wetland vegetation**
  - ~70% of available backwaters and most side channels in the division occur within this 13 km reach; upper portions are largely channelized and inaccessible to fish.
- **Microhabitat variables to be assessed:** bottom/mid-column temperature, dissolved oxygen, conductivity, turbidity, water velocity, substrate type (boulder, cobble, gravel, sand, silt, clay), percent submerged aquatic vegetation coverage, channel width, connectivity, shape, and macrohabitat features (% runs, % pools, % eddies) in main channels.
- **Regulatory context:** Designated [[Critical Habitat]] under the Endangered Species Act (1973), defined as geographic areas containing features essential to species conservation, potentially requiring special management under Section 7 consultations for federal actions.

## Key Findings

Since this document is a *proposed study plan* rather than a results paper, there are no empirical findings yet — instead, it outlines hypotheses, methodology, and objectives:

- **Objective 1:** Quantify relative proportions of main channel, side channel, and backwater habitat within the study area using AutoCAD/GIS digitization of aerial imagery.
- **Objective 2:** Determine whether released razorback suckers (n = 30–35, telemetry-tagged) use habitat types randomly or disproportionately relative to availability, tested via **Chi-square goodness-of-fit** and **Bonferroni confidence intervals**. A secondary **Chi-square homogeneity test** will examine seasonal (high-flow vs. low-flow) differences in habitat use distribution.
- **Objective 3:** Characterize microhabitat variables (temperature, DO, conductivity, turbidity, velocity, substrate, vegetation cover) at backwater, side channel, and main channel sampling sites during low-flow (December) and high-flow (June) periods. A two-part statistical approach will classify sites into **high use, medium use, low use, and not-used** categories (via Chi-square/Bonferroni intervals), then test for microhabitat differences among these categories using **Kruskal-Wallis one-way ANOVA by ranks**.
- **Objective 4:** Test the predictive validity of the pre-existing **"Razorback Sucker Habitat Assessment Indices"** (from Phase One, Bradford & Vlach 1995) against actual telemetry-based habitat use, using **Spearman's Rank correlation**.
- **Contingency plan:** If tagged fish disperse beyond the study area, the "not-used" habitat characterization component would be dropped in favor of expanded fish-tracking effort, at the cost of losing used-vs-unused comparative habitat data.
- **Methodological framework:** Combines GIS-based habitat mapping, ultrasonic telemetry (via hydrophone and GPS/UTM waypoint tracking), and multivariate microhabitat sampling — with quarterly interim reporting and a final report due within three months of field data completion.
