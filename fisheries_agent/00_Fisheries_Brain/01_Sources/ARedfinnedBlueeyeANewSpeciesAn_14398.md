# Redfinned Blue-eye (Scaturiginichthys vermeilipinnis)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pseudomugilidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, published in the Journal of the Australia New Guinea Fishes Association (Vol. 6, No. 4, July 1991), describes a new species, genus and subfamily of blue-eye, [[Scaturiginichthys Vermeilipinnis]], from inland springs in central western Queensland. It is the first member of the family [[Pseudomugilidae]] recorded from the [[Lake Eyre Drainage]], which had previously been known only from coastal streams, rivers and estuaries. A companion note by Unmack and Brumley gives preliminary observations on habitat, breeding and conservation status. The species is known only from five pools on [[Edgbaston Station]] and is considered at risk from habitat change and the introduced [[Mosquitofish]].

## Background & Description

**Discovery and taxonomy.** The fish was found in very shallow springs at Edgbaston, a grazing property near Aramac that runs cattle and sheep. It was discovered by Peter Unmack and collected with Jason Cornford in November 1990, and the holotype was collected on 18 December 1990. Of 22 pools visited, the species occurred in 5. The authors erected the subfamily [[Scaturiginichthyinae]], the genus [[Scaturiginichthys]] (monotypic), and the species *S. vermeilipinnis*. The genus name refers to bubbling springs (Latin *scaturigo*) and the species name refers to the vermilion-edged fins of males (from *vermeil*, old French for vermilion, and *pinnis*, Latin for fins).

**Diagnosis.** The subfamily shares the diagnostic characters of Pseudomugilidae (no mesethmoid, one anterior infraorbital, articular bone equal in height to the dentary). It differs from other members of the family in having:
- 5 branchiostegal rays (6 in others)
- very reduced gill rakers
- no interhyal bone
- elongated cleithrum and scapular bones, which place the pectoral fin low on the body

The fish has an emarginate or rounded caudal fin and reduced ventral fins, with fewer than 5 rays. The ventrals may be absent entirely, a feature not seen in other blue-eyes.

**Morphology (holotype and 30 paratypes).**
- Holotype: 16.5 mm standard length (SL). Paratypes: mean SL 14.8 mm (range 12.8–20.0 mm; SD 1.45).
- Head 3.6 SL; maximum body depth 4.6 SL; pectoral length 6.2 SL.
- Midlateral scales 26.1 (24–28); predorsal scales 14.1 (12–16); transverse scales 6.1.
- Fin rays: first dorsal 4.0; second dorsal 6.0; anal 9.7 (6–10); pectoral 10.9 (10–11).
- Vertebrae 26.9 (26–27); gill rakers 10.7 (9–12).
- Mouth oblique and large, with sharp teeth visible on the lower jaw. Scales are large and nearly round.

**Colouration.** Live fish are silvery to translucent with a visible swimbladder, and have a dusky back and an iridescent midlateral band. Adult males have fins edged with brilliant vermilion in the natural habitat, which fades once the fish are handled. Females and juveniles have clear or faintly coloured fins. Preserved specimens are creamy with a reticulate scale pattern, and males' vermilion fin edges become a broad dark band.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The region is arid and receives low rainfall, but a number of permanent springs are fed by Artesian Basin waters. The known pools are small, very shallow, and often resemble puddles left after rain. Grass tussocks grow through the water, which makes the pools hard to distinguish from the surrounding land. The water is generally clear, bottom substrate is clay with some vegetation, and fish form small aggregations between tussocks. In the holotype pool, fish congregated in large numbers in sunny, open areas.

**Physicochemical conditions (holotype pool, 18 December 1990).** Water temperature 26 °C; air temperature 28 °C; salinity 0.

**Associated fauna and threats.** Two other fish occur in this habitat: a possible new species of desert goby and the mosquitofish. The mosquitofish is non-native and was probably introduced recently, which makes its effect on the blue-eye a priority concern. New crustaceans and molluscs have also recently been found in the area.

**Relationships and biogeography.** The authors note that the species is clearly a pseudomugilid but is not closely related to other members, which suggests a long, separate lineage. It resembles the [[Phallostethidae]] of south-east Asia and the Philippines in having a reduced ventral fin apparatus, and the authors argue that the reduction is still underway. They also suggest that blue-eyes once had a wider range and that relict populations may still be undiscovered, as happened with [[Craterocephalus]].

## Key Findings

- **New taxa:** *Scaturiginichthys vermeilipinnis* is a new species, genus and subfamily (Scaturiginichthyinae) in the Pseudomugilidae. It is the first pseudomugilid recorded from inland waters of the Lake Eyre Drainage.
- **Restricted range:** The species is known only from 5 of 22 pools examined on Edgbaston Station, Aramac (22°44′S, 145°25′E).
- **Small size:** The holotype is 16.5 mm SL. The text gives a maximum of about 26 mm total length in the introduction and 20 mm SL in the description.
- **Distinctive anatomy:** Five branchiostegal rays, very reduced gill rakers, no interhyal bone, a low pectoral fin position, and ventral fins with fewer than 5 rays, sometimes absent.
- **Type material:** The holotype (AMS 31189-001) and paratypes are lodged in the [[Australian Museum]] (AMS), [[Queensland Museum]] (QM) and Western Australian Museum (WAM). Paratypes include 30 AMS specimens (12.8–20.0 mm SL), 20 alizarin-stained AMS specimens, 15 QM specimens (9.9–15.2 mm SL) and 19 WAM specimens (12.3–16.5 mm SL).
- **Evolutionary significance:** The authors describe the species as having a long, independent lineage and regard it as evidence of undiscovered relict blue-eye populations.
- **Conservation concern:** The authors call for a comprehensive survey of all springs in the area, because the mosquitofish is a recent, non-native arrival and the species is so restricted.

**Companion note on spawning and conservation (Unmack and Brumley, pp. 282):**
- The fish is hardy and reasonably easy to breed in captivity when basic conditions are met. The authors describe these observations as preliminary.
- Water is very shallow (maximum 8 cm) and fully sunlit for most of the day.
- Mid-summer air temperatures are usually about 40 °C and occasionally reach 48 °C in the shade. On winter nights, temperatures can fall below freezing. Winter water temperatures are not known.
- On 11 November 1990, water temperature was 36 °C and pH about 8 (air temperature not recorded). A water sample gave calcium/magnesium hardness of 20 ppm, conductivity of 100 (units given as "Ec" in the source), and salinity below 1%.
- On 2 April 1991, water temperature just before dark ranged from 23 to 25 °C.
- The authors state that much more research is needed before the breeding biology is understood.
