# Kokanee in California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #landlocked-salmon #species-introduction

## Executive Summary

This 1948 report documents the introduction and establishment of [[Kokanee]] (Oncorhynchus nerka kennerlyi), a landlocked form of [[Pacific Sockeye Salmon]], into California waters beginning in 1941. The [[Bureau of Fish Conservation]] initiated experimental plantings in [[Salt Springs Reservoir]] and subsequently expanded to four additional water bodies. Results varied by location, with [[Donner Lake]] producing the largest fish (12-16 inches) and [[Strawberry Lake]] providing the best fishing. The species demonstrated successful reproduction and survival, with spawning observations providing detailed behavioral data. The introduction was motivated by the species' plankton-feeding habits, making it suitable for fluctuating reservoirs with poor benthic food production.

## Background & Description

[[Kokanee]] is a landlocked form of the [[Pacific Sockeye Salmon]] (red salmon), native to the [[Pacific Northwest]]. The species exhibits the following characteristics:

**Physical Characteristics:**
- Average size: 12-14 inches
- Maximum size under favorable conditions: 5 pounds
- Minimum size under poor conditions: 8 inches maximum
- Tender mouth structure complicating landing

**Life History:**
- Matures and spawns at end of third or fourth year
- Semelparous (dies after spawning)
- All California observations showed maturation at end of third year

**Feeding Ecology:**
- Diet consists largely of [[Plankton]] (minute drifting animals)
- Plankton-feeding habit was primary rationale for California introduction

**Behavior:**
- Preeminently a lake resident
- Spawns on gravelly lake shores and in connecting streams
- Prefers cool waters, moves deeper as summer temperatures rise
- Catchable on hook and line in spring and autumn
- More difficult to catch in summer

**Angling Methods:**
- Trolling with flashers and baited hook
- Still-fishing a few feet off bottom
- Fly-fishing in late afternoon during spring or fall when fish are near surface
- Provides good fight but difficult to land due to tender mouth
- Excellent eating quality

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Initial Introduction Site Selection:**

[[Salt Springs Reservoir]] on the [[North Fork Mokelumne River]] (off [[Carson Pass Road]]) was selected for initial testing based on:
- Extensive water level fluctuations
- Poor bottom food production due to fluctuations
- Isolated location minimizing risk if species proved undesirable

**Water Bodies Stocked:**

1. **[[Salt Springs Reservoir]], [[Amador County]]**: Initial 1941 planting site
2. **[[Strawberry Lake]] (Pinecrest), [[Tuolumne County]]**: Multiple plantings 1944-1947
3. **[[Waterhouse Lake]], [[Tuolumne County]]**: 1944 planting
4. **[[Echo Lake]], [[El Dorado County]]**: 1944-1945 plantings
5. **[[Donner Lake]], [[Nevada County]]**: 1944-1947 plantings

**Spawning Habitat Observations ([[Donner Lake]], November 1947):**
- Water depth: 0.5 to 3 feet
- Distance from shore: Right along shoreline to 20 feet out
- Substrate: Sandy bottom with some pebbles and rocks
- Location: Concentrations near mouths of small inlet streams on north side; along north shore where water trickled from road culverts
- Water temperature: 45°F

**Dispersal Events:**
- ~100 fish found spawning in stream from [[Tahoe Hatchery]] (December 1946), having escaped from rearing troughs in 1944
- Spawning kokanee appeared in [[Truckee River]] below [[Tahoe Dam]] (winter 1947-1948), presumed migrants from [[Donner Lake]]
- Fish migrated from [[Strawberry Lake]] to [[Lyons Reservoir]] with declining water (1946)
- No harm documented from any escape events

## Key Findings

**Initial Introduction (1941):**
- 100,000 eggs obtained from [[Idaho]] via [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (late 1940)
- Hatched at [[Basin Creek State Fish Hatchery]] near [[Sonora]]
- 67,000 fingerlings planted July 1941 at length just under 2 inches (27 per ounce)
- Area closed as war measure shortly after planting

**First Generation Success ([[Salt Springs Reservoir]]):**
- Spring 1948 tests: Kokanee abundant and easily caught at ~10 inches
- November 1943 (end of third year): Fish ready to spawn at 11-12 inches
- 300,000 eggs taken from 626 females
- Over 3,000 fish caught in seines (44% of number planted)
- Good survival indicated as many more fish present than netted

**Performance by Water Body:**

*[[Echo Lake]]:* Most disappointing
- Poor growth: Fish not exceeding 8-9 inches at maturity
- Little angler interest
- High, rocky basin proved challenging for fish production

*[[Strawberry Lake]]:* Best fishing
- Excellent catches when drawn down in late summer 1946
- Fish reaching 10 inches
- Large catches reported

*[[Donner Lake]]:* Largest fish
- Spawners running 12-16 inches
- Initially little angler interest
- Popularity growing with more fish caught each season
- May 1948 (post-publication): Excellent fishing with many limit catches, fish weighing ~1 pound each

**Spawning Behavior Observations ([[Donner Lake]], November 1947):**

*Nest Building:*
- Female rolled on side and dug vigorously with posterior body for few seconds
- Female then circled away from nest
- Occasional interruptions from general milling of nearby fish

*Spawning Pattern:*
- Pairing of fish was the rule
- Male and female circled nest, one behind the other
- Female passed over nest center (sometimes first pass, sometimes second or third)
- Male joined female over nest
- Both vibrated (~10 seconds total), presumably depositing sex products
- Female invariably dug into sand near end of vibrations, raising small cloud of fine particles
- Circling resumed after spawning act
- Individual pairs repeated process 4-5 times over same nest
- Egg covering apparently accomplished by female's digging action at end of each vibration period
- Possible further final covering after completion of egg deposition

*Environmental Impact:*
- [[Donner Lake]] drawn down heavily January-February
- All spawning beds left high and dry in frozen ground

**Stocking Summary (1941-1947):**

| Water Body | Years | Total Planted | Egg Sources |
|------------|-------|---------------|-------------|
| [[Salt Springs Reservoir]] | 1941 | 67,000 | [[Idaho]] |
| [[Strawberry Lake]] | 1944-1947 | 489,700 | Salt Springs, [[Montana]], [[Washington]] |
| [[Waterhouse Lake]] | 1944 | 69,000 | [[Montana]] |
| [[Echo Lake]] | 1944-1945 | 162,000 | [[Montana]] |
| [[Donner Lake]] | 1944-1947 | 257,000 | [[Montana]], [[Washington]] |

**Size at Planting:**
- Varied from 19 per ounce (~1.9 inches) to 280 per ounce (very small fry)
- [[Washington State]] favored planting at very small size

**Management Implications:**
- Plankton-feeding habit makes species suitable for fluctuating reservoirs
- Variable success suggests site-specific factors critical to establishment
- No harm documented from dispersal to unplanted waters
- Species provides valuable angling resource where conditions favorable
- Spawning bed protection from water level fluctuations important for recruitment
