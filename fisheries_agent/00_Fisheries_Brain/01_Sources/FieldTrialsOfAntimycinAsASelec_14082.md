# Field Trials of Antimycin as a Selective Toxicant in Channel Catfish Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #fisheries-management #toxicology #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service field study (Burress & Luhning, 1969) evaluated **antimycin A** (Fintrol-5 formulation) as a selective piscicide to eliminate undesirable "scalefishes" — primarily [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Golden Shiner]] — from commercial [[Channel Catfish]] rearing ponds in Columbus, Mississippi. An initial treatment of 5.0 parts per billion (p.p.b.) (7.5 p.p.b. in one pond) removed nearly 99% of scalefish populations without harming yearling catfish. A follow-up 10 p.p.b. treatment further suppressed survivors. At harvest, treated ponds produced 27.4% more catfish (1,155 additional fish) and far fewer undersized fish than untreated ponds. Economic analysis showed a net return of **$2.48 for every $1 invested** in antimycin, demonstrating that selective chemical control of competing scalefish is both biologically effective and economically profitable for pond aquaculture.

## Background & Description

- **Context**: Commercial catfish production in the U.S. (~35 million lbs/year in 1966, with a projected potential of 60 million lbs/year) was frequently compromised by unwanted "scalefish" (wild fish) competing with stocked catfish for food and space.
- **Prior work**: [[Fred P. Meyer]] (1965) had tested organophosphate insecticides (e.g., Guthion) for selective removal of trash fish from catfish/buffalo ponds; this study tested antimycin as an alternative selective toxicant.
- **Study site**: Private fish farm in Columbus, Mississippi; 6 of 14 dug ponds were used (three treated, three untreated control pairs), conducted August 1, 1966 – January 25, 1967.
- **Pond characteristics**:
  - Surface area: 0.94–1.39 acres
  - Steep-sided, with a scooped catch basin (~15×40×2 ft)
  - Max depth: ~7 ft (Ponds 1 & 3), ~5 ft (Pond 5)
  - Soft water (total hardness 14–20 p.p.m. as CaCO₃), high daytime pH (up to 9.4–10.2) due to dense phytoplankton blooms
  - No filamentous algae or submersed vegetation; fed 25 lbs commercial catfish feed/day/pond
  - Water source: Ponds 1 & 5 from well; Pond 3 from an open pit already containing scalefish
- **Stocking**: Each pond stocked (1965–1966) with ~2,000 fingerling [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), 2.5 in. at stocking. Naturally present/invasive scalefish included [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Warmouth]] (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*), and [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Toxicant behavior**: Antimycin A efficacy is strongly pH-dependent — companion lab work ([[Berger, Lennon & Hogan 1969]]) found LC100 (96 hr, 12°C) for fingerling goldfish was 0.20 p.p.b. at pH 5, 1.10 p.p.b. at pH 8, and 60 p.p.b. at pH 10. Rising afternoon pH (driven by phytoplankton photosynthesis) rapidly degraded the toxicant, reducing treatment efficacy in these soft, fertile, high-pH ponds.
- **Formulation mechanics**: Fintrol-5 is a sand-based formulation designed to release toxicant uniformly as it sinks through the top 5 feet of water. In shallow ponds (Pond 5, avg. depth 3.5 ft) with soft mud bottoms, sand grains sank into the mud before fully releasing the toxicant, leaving pockets of surviving scalefish.
- **Species selectivity**: Antimycin selectively killed scalefish (Cyprinidae, Centrarchidae) while sparing yearling channel catfish (Ictaluridae) at the tested concentrations — demonstrating the differential toxicity central to its use as a "selective" piscicide.
- **Non-target effects**: Tadpoles in Pond 3 were also killed by the second (10 p.p.b.) treatment (~1,990 tadpoles, 33.25 lbs recovered), indicating broader amphibian sensitivity to antimycin.
- **Degradation/safety window**: Caged golden shiners and green sunfish bioassays confirmed toxicant degradation to sub-lethal levels within 24 hours (5.0–7.5 p.p.b. doses) and within 48 hours (10 p.p.b. dose), informing safe re-stocking or handling timelines.
- **Competitive dynamics**: Scalefish were shown to directly compete with juvenile catfish for supplemental feed once they grew large enough to consume food pellets, exacerbating stunting and reducing marketable yield in untreated ponds.

## Key Findings

### Treatment Efficacy
- **First treatment**: 5.0 p.p.b. antimycin (7.5 p.p.b. in Pond 5) eliminated ~99% of scalefish across all three treated ponds regardless of species composition.
  - Pond 1: 187 lbs/acre scalefish removed (88.4% by number, 78.8% by weight = golden shiner)
  - Pond 3: 146 lbs/acre removed (>99% green sunfish)
  - Pond 5: 44 lbs/acre removed (>99% green sunfish)
- **Second treatment**: 10 p.p.b. applied 3 days later further reduced survivors; golden shiner survival dropped to <0.01% (Pond 1) and 0.0% (Pond 5), though 32.4% survived in Pond 3.
- **Final reduction at harvest**: >99.99% reduction of green sunfish in Ponds 1 & 3; no golden shiners or warmouth remained in these ponds. Pond 5 results were confounded by an overflow from an adjacent untreated pond introducing new scalefish; even so, an estimated >98.12% reduction of original green sunfish was achieved.
- **Catfish safety**: No apparent mortality or harm to yearling channel catfish at any treatment concentration (5.0, 7.5, or 10 p.p.b.).

### Harvest Comparison (Treated vs. Untreated)
- **Treated ponds** (3.37 acres total): 5,363 catfish harvested, 4,855 lbs total, avg. weight 0.91 lbs/fish.
- **Untreated ponds** (3.79 acres total): 4,208 catfish harvested, 3,088 lbs total, avg. weight 0.73 lbs/fish.
- Untreated ponds produced 1,474 lbs of scalefish (avg. 389 lbs/acre) and had **27.4% fewer catfish** (1,155 fewer) than treated ponds.
- Undersized (sub-marketable) fish: 421 total in treated ponds vs. **1,183** in untreated ponds (nearly 3× more).

### Economic Analysis
- Adjusted weight-gain comparison (accounting for differing stocking/harvest dates) showed treated ponds gained an additional **1,015 lbs** of catfish post-treatment, valued at **$507.50** (at $0.50/lb).
- Cost of antimycin (first treatment only): **$145.79**.
- **Net profit: $361.71**; **net return of $2.48 per $1 invested** in toxicant.
- Secondary savings: reduced feed costs to grow out undersized fish — treated ponds required only $15.52 vs. $48.47 for untreated ponds (difference of $32.95).
- Hypothetical: if feed consumed by scalefish had instead produced catfish, untreated ponds could have yielded an additional 1,474 lbs of catfish.

### Water Chemistry
- Total hardness: 14–20 p.p.m. (as CaCO₃) — classified as soft water.
- pH swung dramatically diurnally: as low as 6.9–8.2 (morning) to 9.4–10.2 (afternoon), driven by phytoplankton photosynthesis.
- Pond 5 was chemically more fertile than Ponds 1 and 3 (based on nutrient parameters).

### Conclusions Drawn by Authors
1. ~99% of scalefish eliminated by initial 5.0–7.5 p.p.b. antimycin application.
2. Follow-up 10 p.p.b. treatment further reduced scalefish and incidentally killed tadpoles.
3. Channel catfish were unharmed by the toxicant at tested concentrations.
4. Incomplete eradication attributed to (a) afternoon pH spikes accelerating antimycin degradation, and (b) shallow pond depth (Pond 5) causing incomplete toxicant release before sand substrate sank into mud.
5. Toxicant degraded to safe levels within 24–48 hours depending on dose.
6. Untreated ponds produced ~184,000 scalefish (~1,474 lbs) and yielded 1,155 fewer catfish than treated ponds.
7. Excluding the mismatched-stocking pond pair, treated ponds yielded ~464 more fish (558 lbs more) per acre than untreated ponds.
8. Overall economic benefit: $507.50 in increased yield value vs. $145.79 toxicant cost — a **$2.48 return per dollar invested**.
9. Antimycin is a safe, selective, and economically viable tool for removing undesirable scalefish from catfish aquaculture ponds.
