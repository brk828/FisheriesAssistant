# Colorado Squawfish Specimen Locality and Collection Correspondence (1970)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #colorado-squawfish

## Executive Summary

This is a set of three letters from November 1970 between [[Robert R. Miller]], Curator of Fishes at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], and [[Paul B. Holden]] of the [[Utah Cooperative Fishery Unit]] at [[Utah State University]]. The letters concern photographed specimens of [[Colorado Squawfish]] and related [[Colorado Basin Fishes]] published in Holden's article in [[Copeia]] (1970, No. 3), which was co-authored with [[Stalnaker]]. Miller requested missing locality and collection-date data for the figures, Holden supplied it, and the exchange also covers live-specimen losses, karyotyping, and lost blood serum samples. The document contains no quantitative study results, but it provides useful [[Specimen Locality Data]] for the Colorado River system.

## Background & Description

- **Subject species:** [[Colorado Squawfish]], a large native cyprinid-type fish of the Colorado River basin. The letters do not give its scientific name, family, or morphological descriptions.
- **Correspondents:**
  - [[Robert R. Miller]] (Curator of Fishes, Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan, Ann Arbor) wrote on 10 November 1970 and 25 November 1970.
  - [[Paul B. Holden]] (Graduate Research Assistant, Utah Cooperative Fishery Unit, Logan, Utah) replied on 20 November 1970.
- **Publication context:** Holden's paper with Stalnaker appeared as the lead article in *Copeia* No. 3 (1970). Miller noted that it lacked precise locality information for several photographed fishes (Figs. 1 and 4).
- **Related work:** Miller and [[Gerald Smith]] were preparing a publication on data Miller and a student collected on a [[Blood Serum Sampling]] and [[Karyotype Analysis]] project, referred to as a "complex" in the letters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimens came from several reaches of the [[Colorado River]] system:

- **[[Glen Canyon Dam]] tailwater:** a juvenile collected just below the dam on 25 July 1967 (Fig. 1, top fish).
- **[[Echo Park]] area, below the mouth of the [[Yampa River]]:** collected 24 August 1964 (Fig. 1, middle fish).
- **[[DeBeque]] area, Colorado River:** collected about 10 miles below DeBeque on 9 October 1964 (Fig. 1, bottom fish).
- **[[Lake Powell]]:** Miller suspected some Figure 4 specimens were attributed to Lake Powell without adequate basis. Holden's reply lists a Lake Powell collection dated 1966.
- **[[Dolores River]] mouth:** the single live individual Miller received in late November 1970 came from the Colorado River just below the mouth of the Dolores River. It was the only one of its kind collected in several hours.

These sites span free-flowing canyon reaches, a major impoundment tailwater, and a reservoir, which is relevant to [[Colorado Basin Fishes]] distribution studies. Miller said the locality data would be useful for his own distribution work.

## Key Findings

1. **Figure 1 localities were supplied.** Holden's reply gives three collection records for the Figure 1 fishes: Glen Canyon Dam (25 July 1967), Echo Park/Yampa mouth (24 August 1964), and DeBeque (9 October 1964).

2. **Figure 4 contains a duplicate and an uncertain provenance.** Miller found that the top fish in Figure 1 was repeated at the top of Figure 4, and that the bottom Figure 4 fish matched the middle Figure 1 fish. He also questioned whether the Figure 4 specimens came from Lake Powell. Holden's reply to that question is partly illegible in the source, so the exact Figure 4 assignments cannot be confirmed here.

3. **Live-specimen mortality.** A juvenile Colorado Squawfish that Holden had collected died about 36 hours after leaving the field. A smaller live individual was later obtained and used for [[Karyotype Analysis]]. Holden wrote that another individual survived. The letters do not state the total number collected in each case.

4. **Only one live individual from the Dolores River reach.** Miller reported that the one live specimen he obtained came from the Colorado River below the Dolores mouth, and that it was the only individual of its kind collected in several hours.

5. **Loss of blood serum samples.** Holden lost frozen [[Blood Serum Sampling]] material when a freezer failed during shipment or storage, and he described the lost data as potentially valuable for native Colorado Basin fishes. Miller expressed sympathy and offered to send back some retained specimens.

6. **Requests for publications.** Miller asked Holden for reprints of his southwestern fish papers and any other ichthyology papers he had written, which points to ongoing exchange among regional [[Colorado Basin Fishes]] researchers.

7. **Outstanding actions.** Holden was to send locality and collection-date data for all photographed fishes and clarify whether the Figure 4 specimens were from Lake Powell. Miller planned to select specimens still held by Holden for return, and both parties expected to meet again.
