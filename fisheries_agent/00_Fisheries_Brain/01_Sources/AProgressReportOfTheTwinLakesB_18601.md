# Kokanee Salmon and Catchable Trout Fishery at Twin Lakes, California (1958–1964)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This California Department of Fish and Game progress report evaluates a six-year management experiment in which [[Kokanee Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*) were introduced into the [[Twin Lakes]] of Mono County in 1958. The primary goal was a dependable local source of eggs from a late-spawning strain, with the salmon also assessed as a supplement to stocked [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*). Kokanee became established, but their behavior diverged sharply between the two lakes: [[Upper Twin Lake]] produced abundant, early-maturing, small fish, while [[Lower Twin Lake]] retained a late-spawning population with four-year-old fish appearing in 1963. Kokanee made up about 28 percent of the catch, but the fishery deteriorated in 1964. The authors conclude that species interaction or angler effort transfer may have reduced trout catchability, recommend discontinuing early-spawning stock, and propose trapping and control experiments in the upper lake.

## Background & Description

The Twin Lakes are glacial remnants separated by alluvial deposition and augmented by dams at their outlets, about 15 miles southwest of Bridgeport. Surface levels fluctuate 7 to 10 feet annually for irrigation releases on [[Robinson Creek]]. Vestal (1950) reported that the lakes produced relatively few plankters but had enough benthic and terrestrial food to once support wild [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarkii henshawi*) and [[Mountain Whitefish]] (*Coregonus williamsoni*).

Following the loss of the native cutthroat fishery after construction of the Bridgeport dam in 1923, [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) were released in 1925, followed by rainbow trout and [[Eastern Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*). Rough fish, principally tui chub and suckers, degraded the fishery, so both lakes were treated with [[Rotenone Treatment]] (Upper Twin Lake in 1949, Lower Twin Lake in 1950) and restocked with rainbow trout. From 1952 to 1957, 768,382 catchable trout were planted (Table 2).

Fishing pressure was high because of nearby campgrounds and a resort, and angling peaked on Mondays. Data came from the [[Creel Census]] program, with 11,805 fishermen interviews recorded in 1952–1957 (Table 3). The authors note that the kokanee program was never designed as a formal research project, and that data were often opportunistic.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Food and plankton.** In August 1964, plankton hauls showed that Lower Twin Lake had relatively few benthic [[Entomostracans]] but abundant phytoplankton, while Upper Twin Lake reversed this pattern. [[Cyclops]] was dense in deep water. Ekman dredge samples from both lakes contained immature [[Chironomids]], small annelids, amphipods, and pelecypods, with chironomid larvae averaging about 60 individuals per square foot. Thermoclines occurred between 30 and 60 feet.

**Stomach contents.** Of 34 Lower Twin Lake kokanee, [[Cyclops]] sp. and immature chironomids were the main items. Fish from shallow water had few copepods, while deep-water fish had few chironomids. In 12 Upper Twin Lake stomachs, most of which were from shallows, diets were dominated by chironomid larvae, culicids, terrestrial insects, and bait. The authors note that these diets closely paralleled [[Rainbow Trout]] diets, and cite Pister (1960), who found 89 percent of Crowley Lake trout stomach volume to be chironomids.

**Egg-source strains.** Eggs were originally obtained from [[Donner Lake]] and [[Lake Arrowhead]], then imported from British Columbia (1959) and [[Colorado Strain]] sources (1960). The [[British Columbia Strain]] was an early spawner, and the Colorado strain was a later one. Eggs were first taken from Twin Lakes fish in fall 1960.

**Population structure.** The late-spawning run in 1960 was the largest recorded, with 1,234,000 eggs procured. Thereafter, the lakes diverged: Upper Twin Lake hosted early-run fish at Age Group II+ (about 3,000 to 5,000 fish each year), while Lower Twin Lake kept a late run. Age Group III+ and four-year-old kokanee appeared in Lower Twin Lake in 1963 and 1964, which is the first record of four-year-olds at the lakes.

## Key Findings

**Stocking and cost**
- Kokanee were planted annually from 1958 onward; total plantings were 1,218,355 fish (1958–1964), with fry comprising 82 percent of the stockings.
- Estimated 1960–1964 harvest was 299,043 kokanee (Table 12), representing an indirect return of about 24 percent of stocked fish.
- Cost per harvested kokanee was about $0.02 for fry and $0.08 for fingerlings, versus $0.19 per catchable trout.
- The authors estimate that the minimum annual expenditure for catchable trout at Twin Lakes is about $13,000.

**Angler use and harvest**
- Kokanee comprised 28.2 percent of the catch of all interviewed anglers, 1960–1964.
- About 22 to 24 percent of interviewed anglers caught kokanee (Table 11), mostly at Lower Twin Lake.
- Trout harvest estimates ranged from 90.4 to 98.6 percent for marked lots in 1964, based on Walters' unpublished estimates.
- Trout catchability was highly correlated between lakes in 1960–1964 (r = +0.94), but the correlation was +0.12 before kokanee were introduced.
- Kokanee and trout catch per hour were negatively correlated (r = −0.63 Lower; −0.71 Upper), suggesting transfer of angler effort from trout to kokanee during trout scarcity, although creel census timing bias was also possible.
- Catch per hour of trout was moderately negatively correlated with planting numbers after kokanee introduction (−0.26 to −0.47 at Lower Twin Lake).

**Spawning and maturity**
- Late runs at Lower Twin Lake: 5,000–6,000 fish in 1961, about 600–800 in 1962, about 1,500 in 1963, and none in 1964.
- In 1964, about 5,000 small early-run fish (5 to 7 inches) entered Upper Twin Lake.
- In July 1964, 79 Lower Twin Lake fish comprised Age II+ (mean 7.5 inches) and Age III+ (mean 8.6 inches) with no overlap.
- Upper Twin Lake fish were mostly Age I+ with a mean length of 5.8 inches.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Discontinue early-spawning kokanee in Inyo-Mono catchable trout waters.
- Limit late-spawning stock to Twin Lakes until further investigations justify expansion.
- Plant late-fall spawning stock in Lower Twin Lake in 1965 to replace the lost 1965 year class.
- Cease kokanee plantings in Upper Twin Lake and reduce its early-spawning population by trapping, with the option of reverting it to a pure [[Catchable Trout Fishery]] as a control.
- Modify the Upper Twin Lake outlet drain with a standpipe to prevent upstream movement.
- Intensify research on kokanee growth, migration, stomach contents, and angler responses, including a "like, dislike, undecided" [[Popularity Poll]].
