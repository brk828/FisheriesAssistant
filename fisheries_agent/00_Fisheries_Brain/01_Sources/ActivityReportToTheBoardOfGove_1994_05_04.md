# 1993-94 ASIH Environmental Quality Committee Activity Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #nonindigenous-species #endangered-species-act

## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary morphological or ecological research paper**, but rather an administrative activity report and policy memorandum from the [[ASIH Environmental Quality Committee]], chaired by [[Walter R. Courtenay Jr]]. It summarizes the committee's regulatory and advocacy activities during 1993-94, with substantial relevance to ichthyology through its engagement with **nonindigenous fish species introduction policy**, **endangered fish stocking practices in the Upper Colorado River Basin**, and broader **U.S. Endangered Species Act (ESA)** matters. The report also raises a novel institutional question: whether ASIH, as a professional scientific society, should engage in environmental litigation to support conservation-related lawsuits, using a case involving nonnative fish stocking in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] as the precipitating example.

## Background & Description

No morphological data on fish species are presented in this document. The report is procedural/policy-focused rather than taxonomic or anatomical. Species named (e.g., [[Lake Erie Water Snake]], [[Copperbelly Water Snake]], [[Mojave Desert Tortoise]]) are non-fish taxa referenced only in the context of ESA listing support letters. The "endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado" are referenced collectively without species-level identification or morphological description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although morphological detail is absent, the report provides important **ecological and management context** relevant to freshwater ichthyofauna conservation:

- **[[Upper Colorado River Basin]] Endangered Fishes**: The committee objected to a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service draft policy, "Procedures for Stocking Nonnative Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin," arguing that the proposed procedures would effectively prioritize the persistence of **introduced/nonnative fish species** over the recovery of **native endangered fishes**. This reflects a core ecological concern: competitive and predatory pressure from nonnative species on threatened native fish populations in western U.S. river systems.
- **[[Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area]]** (Safford, Arizona): The committee reviewed and supported the most protective management alternative ("Alternative 1") in a draft Interdisciplinary Activity Plan/Environmental Assessment, emphasizing riparian habitat protection important to aquatic biological resources.
- **Nonindigenous Aquatic Species Policy**: Following the 1990 [[Nonindigenous Aquatic Nuisance Prevention and Control Act]], the committee (jointly with the [[Desert Fishes Council]]) submitted detailed commentary to the federal Aquatic Nuisance Species (ANS) Task Force regarding intentional introductions policy — a topic central to invasive fish species management in U.S. freshwater systems.
- **Congressional testimony**: Courtenay submitted written testimony to Senator Daniel K. Akaka's office (Hawaii) on the **pathways and ecological consequences of nonindigenous fish introductions into U.S. waters**, entered into the Congressional record (11 March 1994 hearing).

These activities collectively illustrate the committee's role in advocating for native fish conservation against pressures from **habitat modification**, **nonnative species stocking**, and **regulatory rollback of species-specific protections** in favor of habitat-based management approaches.

## Key Findings

- The [[ASIH Environmental Quality Committee]] actively supported ESA listing/critical habitat proposals for non-fish species (Lake Erie water snake, copperbelly water snake, Mojave desert tortoise) as part of its broader biodiversity conservation mandate.
- The committee's most substantive fish-related action was formal objection to a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service draft policy on **nonnative fish stocking in the Upper Colorado River Basin**, arguing it favored introduced species survival over recovery of native endangered fishes; partial revisions followed but the document remained "flawed."
- This objection led to an unprecedented request from the **Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund** for ASIH to join environmental litigation against the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — the first such litigation request received by the society.
- ASIH's Executive Committee declined to join the litigation pending establishment of a formal society policy on environmental litigation involvement, deferring the decision to the **Board of Governors** at the 1994 annual meeting.
- Courtenay's accompanying memo to the Board of Governors outlines key policy considerations for future litigation involvement: capped financial liability (~$500 in this case), pro bono legal representation by sponsoring environmental groups, reimbursement of pledged funds if the suit prevails, and unresolved liability risk if the litigation is lost.
- The report underscores an emerging institutional tension within ichthyological/herpetological professional societies regarding advocacy limits — balancing scientific neutrality against active conservation engagement, particularly concerning [[Nonindigenous Species Introductions]] and their threat to native freshwater fish biodiversity.
- Broader legislative context: anticipated contentious reauthorization battles over the **Endangered Species Act**, including a proposed federal shift from species-specific listings toward habitat-based protection strategies — a shift with direct implications for future endangered fish conservation policy.
