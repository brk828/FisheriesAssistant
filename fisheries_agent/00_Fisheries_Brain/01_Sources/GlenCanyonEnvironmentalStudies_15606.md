# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Phase II Implementation Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a Bureau of Reclamation implementation guide for Phase II of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES). The program assesses how the operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] affects downstream natural and recreational resources in the [[Grand Canyon]]. Phase II focuses on two Department of the Interior concerns: (1) effects of low and fluctuating flows on endangered fish, the [[Trout Fishery]], and [[Beach Aggradation and Degradation]]; and (2) detailed economic analysis of operational options. The package includes a cover letter (December 23, 1988) inviting comment at a constituent meeting on January 24, 1989, in Phoenix, Arizona, along with the draft Implementation Plan (Draft No. 2, November 1988), a constituent directory, a [[Table 1 Study Group Composition]] summary, and two historical attachments (a December 1982 initiation memo and a June 1988 Assistant Secretary letter).

## Background & Description

**Program partners.** GCES is a cooperative effort among the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (lead, primary coordination), the [[National Park Service]] (NPS), and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS). Support came from the U.S. Geological Survey, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], private consultants, and academic institutions. Phase II adds input from [[Western Area Power Administration]], power users, [[Colorado River Basin States]], environmental and recreation groups, and additional consultants.

**Historical timeline.**
- **December 1982:** The Department of the Interior initiated GCES as part of the environmental assessment for uprating and rewinding the generators at Glen Canyon Dam. The primary objective was to study how present flow patterns affect the riverine environment and how low-flow periods affect rafting and fishery resources.
- **Phase I (completed):** Technical studies quantified present impacts. The GCES Technical Report, issued in January 1988, documented the findings. Impacts of low and fluctuating flows could not be fully addressed, and the relationships among environmental, recreational, and operational components were found to be complex and dynamic.
- **May 1986:** The [[Executive Review Committee]] (ERC) concluded that critical information was missing and that no operational decision could yet be made.
- **June 1988:** The Department concurred with the ERC and directed Phase II work on the two focus areas above.

**Study teams and structure.**
- **Environmental Study Team:** Develops technical plans for trout, endangered species, and beach aggradation and degradation.
- **Economic Study Team:** Develops plans and analysis of economic relationships tied to dam operations.
- **[[Technical Integration Group]]:** Combines the environmental and economic work into a final [[Integrated Study Plan]] and the final technical report, and develops contingency plans.
- **Senior scientist:** Oversees technical aspects, sets up scientific review, and follows a recommendation from the [[National Academy of Sciences]] (NAS) that senior scientists be involved early.

**Regulatory context.** The FWS issued a jeopardy opinion on dam operations in 1978 regarding endangered and threatened species. Formal [[Section 7 Consultation]] was reinitiated during Phase I, and FWS has reissued a draft [[Non-Jeopardy Biological Opinion]]. That opinion carries seven Conservation Measures, whose field components are being integrated into the Phase II study plans.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regimes under study:** The Department's 1982 memo specified low-flow scenarios of 1,000, 3,000, 4,000, 5,000, and/or 8,000 ft³/s, plus fluctuating patterns and periodic high-flow periods, to identify thresholds at which high flows materially affect beach erosion, recreation, and fisheries.
- **Operating constraint:** Uprated generators were restricted to present maximum releases of 31,500 ft³/s until a long-term operating criteria decision, with appropriate [[National Environmental Policy Act]] compliance, is complete.
- **Sediment and beach processes:** Sediment transport and beach erosion below the dam through the Grand Canyon form the basis for biological impact studies.
- **Biological scope:** Studies also cover vegetation, wildlife, fishery, and recreation effects of present and historic dam operations.
- **Power operations:** Test flows must minimize disruption to normal power operations, coordinated through Reclamation and Western's release programs.
- **Timing:** Low-flow studies tied to trout concerns were to begin by December 1988 and run at least one year, with the endpoint set by flow levels and technical needs.

## Key Findings

1. **Phase II scope:** The Department limited Phase II to low and fluctuating flow effects on endangered fish, the trout fishery, and beach aggradation/degradation, plus detailed economic analysis of operational options.
2. **Decision sequence:** The Environmental Study Team follows a four-step process (technical needs agreement; Western's power impact review; [[Executive Review Committee]] decision to proceed; then field studies). The Economic Study Team uses a shorter two-step process because its work does not require specific yield conditions.
3. **Contingency planning:** If flows are too high or too low to test, the affected study components are postponed. Contingency plans are to be developed among agencies and given to the technical study group.
4. **Integrated Study Plan:** It will focus on (1) monitoring critical Grand Canyon resources for short- and long-term impacts, (2) completing studies to fill identified gaps, and (3) identifying resources to monitor after GCES ends. A draft goes to NAS, constituents, Basin States, and the ERC for review.
5. **Phase III and decision pathway:** A revised ERC Report will incorporate Phase II results and constituent comments. If the Department decides no operational change is warranted, GCES ends except for low-level monitoring. If changes are warranted, they may be implemented through the [[Annual Operating Plan]] with state agreement; otherwise a formal review of operating criteria under [[Colorado River Storage Project]] authority (Public Law 90-537) and a NEPA process begin.
6. **Alternatives named for possible NEPA review:** A [[Reregulation Dam]] at [[Lee's Ferry]], additional transportation access points, alternative flow regimes, and a no-action option.
7. **Funding:** GCES has been funded through CRSP power revenues under the operation and maintenance program, and this will continue for the additional tasks.
8. **Basin State consultation:** Changes will be made only in consultation with Basin States under Public Law 90-537. Reclamation assumes that gubernatorially appointed representatives reflect water, power, recreation, and environmental interests.
9. **Uprating restriction:** Uprated generators remain economically viable for maintenance and emergency use and offer added flexibility, but their use is capped at the present maximum release until a long-term criteria decision is made.
10. **Urgency:** Concurrence on the general process must come before field studies begin, and the program's completion depends on starting it promptly. A full public information transfer program with review schedules is being developed.
