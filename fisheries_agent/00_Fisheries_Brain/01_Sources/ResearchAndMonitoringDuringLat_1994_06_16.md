## Executive Summary

This 1994 memo from Duncan T. Patten (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) to Jan Balsom (Grand Canyon National Park) provides programmatic guidance on research and monitoring priorities in the [[Grand Canyon]] and [[Colorado River]] corridor during the interim flow period preceding the Glen Canyon Dam Record of Decision. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological fish data, the document is a management-planning communication that outlines which research and monitoring activities should continue, be scaled back, or be prioritized — with particular emphasis on the [[Humpback Chub]] population in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), an upcoming experimental flood event, and baseline monitoring of native fish, water quality, and physical habitat parameters (sediment, [[Cladophora]], riparian vegetation).

## Background & Description

No morphological data on fish species are presented in this source. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Humpback Chub]] (LCR population)**: Identified as an ongoing research priority; population is being actively studied for age-class structure via annual surveys within the [[Little Colorado River]], a key tributary spawning/rearing habitat within the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem.
- **Native fish community (mainstem Colorado River)**: Native fish and associated by-catch are recommended for annual survey via extended mainstem river trips, indicating a broader native fish assemblage beyond humpback chub is of monitoring concern.
- **Trout (non-native)**: Noted as a subject of anticipated study during the planned experimental flood event, specifically regarding trout population response and food base dynamics — implying trout occupy a competing/predatory ecological role relative to native fishes in the regulated river reach.
- **Food base / aquatic organic debris**: Flood-driven effects on aquatic food base (invertebrates, organic debris, [[Cladophora]] algae) are explicitly linked to fish community dynamics, underscoring a bottom-up trophic linkage between hydrologic regime, primary/secondary production, and fish populations.
- **Habitat drivers**: Sediment transport, eddy circulation dynamics, and riparian/marsh vegetation coverage are flagged as physical habitat variables influencing aquatic ecosystem structure, monitored via aerial photography and beach/bathymetric surveys.
- **Water quality linkage**: Recommended water quality monitoring at Lees Ferry and Grand Canyon sites (chemical, physical, biological, and microbiological/pathogen parameters) reflects concern over dam discharge and tributary inputs affecting aquatic habitat quality for fish and other biota.

## Key Findings

- **Experimental Flood (planned March/April following year)**: Identified as the central research priority for the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, expected to generate data on:
  - Sediment transport and eddy circulation
  - Erosive/scouring effects of flood flows
  - Vegetation resistance and resilience to flooding
  - Effects on aquatic organic debris and food base
  - Effects on native fish (e.g., [[Humpback Chub]]) and non-native trout populations
- **Research wind-down**: Most ongoing Grand Canyon research projects (except humpback chub work in the LCR and select sediment transport studies) are recommended to transition from active fieldwork to data analysis, report writing, and peer-reviewed manuscript submission.
- **Future research policy recommendation**: Investigators proposing new Grand Canyon research should be required to demonstrate that their hypotheses can only be tested within the Grand Canyon environment, rather than at comparable external sites — a proposed criterion to limit unnecessary in-canyon research impacts.
- **Monitoring recommendations during the interim hiatus** (until long-term adaptive management monitoring begins, approximately 18 months out):
  - Annual or semi-annual low-flow aerial photography (sediment storage, [[Cladophora]] abundance, riparian/marsh vegetation)
  - Annual beach survey, potentially paired with bathymetric survey (sediment storage change)
  - Annual mainstem native fish (and by-catch) survey via extended river trip
  - Annual LCR native fish age-class survey (already occurring under existing research program)
  - Annual surveys for other endangered species of concern
  - Regular water quality sampling at Lees Ferry and Grand Canyon (chemical, physical, biological, microbiological/pathogen parameters)
- **Institutional/administrative note**: Cultural resource monitoring is addressed under a separate agreement and is not detailed in this memo. The memo explicitly states it is not a comprehensive research/monitoring plan but a set of guiding suggestions for [[Grand Canyon National Park]] resource management planning.
