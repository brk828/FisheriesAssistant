# Desert Fishes Council 1992 Proceedings: Cutthroat Trout Taxonomy and Young-of-the-Year Feeding Ecology

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This note covers the front matter and two papers from the *Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council*, Volume XXIV (1992 annual symposium, 13–23 November, Phoenix area, Arizona). The volume was edited by Dean A. Hendrickson and published on March 30, 1993. The two papers are:

1. A genetic and morphological study that supports recognizing [[Humboldt Cutthroat Trout]] as a distinct subspecies, separate from the adjacent [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]].
2. A study of the feeding behavior and ecology of three young-of-the-year (YOY) native fishes in the [[Little Colorado River]] of the [[Grand Canyon]]. The species are [[Humpback Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]], and [[Bluehead Sucker]].

The volume is published by the [[Desert Fishes Council]]. Each paper has a bilingual (English/Spanish) abstract. The document text is partial and does not include sample sizes for the feeding study or full methods for either paper.

## Background & Description

**Volume context.** The proceedings are dedicated to the conservation of Mexico's desert fishes. The cover lists the Texas Memorial Museum, University of Texas at Austin, as the editorial institution, and the Desert Fishes Council (Bishop, California) as the publisher.

**Humboldt cutthroat trout.** The Humboldt cutthroat trout is a subspecies of the cutthroat trout found in northeastern Nevada. It is compared with the adjacent Lahontan cutthroat trout. The authors examined mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) restriction fragment length polymorphisms (RFLP) and morphological characters, including branchial arch gill-raker counts and scale counts.

**Feeding ecology study.** The authors studied three YOY native fishes in the Little Colorado River:
- *Gila cypha* (humpback chub)
- *Rhinichthys osculus* (speckled dace). The OCR text reads "Minichthys osculus," which has been corrected here.
- *Catostomus discobolus* (bluehead sucker)

Feeding was observed in four general habitat localities: bottom, plants, water column, and surface. Vertical position in the water column was recorded as a habitat variable. Specimens were collected, preserved, and their stomach contents analyzed in the laboratory. Drift samples were also collected and analyzed for invertebrate composition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Genetic structure:** The Humboldt and Lahontan cutthroat trout populations show clear molecular and morphological separation. This indicates that their divergence reflects long-term evolutionary history, not simply local variation.
- **Resource partitioning in a canyon river:** The three YOY species use different parts of the habitat. This partitioning is presented as a mechanism that may reduce competition among them, which is relevant to conservation of native desert-river fishes in the Colorado River system.
- **Drift as a food source:** Humpback chub diets most closely resembled the invertebrate composition of the drift, linking the fish's feeding to water-column invertebrate supply.
- **Conservation relevance:** Formal recognition of the Humboldt subspecies provides a basis for management, which matters for populations facing habitat pressures in arid western waters.

## Key Findings

### Humboldt vs. Lahontan Cutthroat Trout
- **Mitochondrial DNA:** 64 of 66 Humboldt cutthroat trout (96.9%) from 7 populations shared a single mtDNA haplotype. This haplotype differs from the Lahontan haplotype by 0.13% sequence divergence.
- **Lahontan haplotype:** The Lahontan haplotype was found in 64 of 66 (97%) Lahontan cutthroat trout sampled from 9 populations in northwestern Nevada, southeastern Oregon, and eastern California.
- **Morphology:** Discriminant function analysis of gill-raker counts on the first branchial arch, scales above the lateral line, and lateral-line scale counts correctly classified Lahontan trout at about 95% (the OCR reading is uncertain) and 93.2%. The same analysis separated the two forms.
- **Conclusion:** The authors conclude that Humboldt cutthroat trout are genetically and morphologically distinct from Lahontan cutthroat trout. They recommend formal recognition as a unique subspecies of cutthroat trout.

### Feeding Ecology of YOY Fishes
- **Humpback chub:** Feeds mainly at the water surface and on the bottom. YOY humpback chub preferentially use the upper pelagic and surface zones.
- **Speckled dace:** Similar to humpback chub in feeding position, but feeds extensively on vegetation as well.
- **Bluehead sucker:** Feeds primarily on the bottom and, to a lesser extent, on algae and aquatic macrophytes. YOY bluehead suckers preferentially use benthic zones.
- **Diet similarity:** Humpback chub and speckled dace diets did not differ significantly from each other. Both differed from bluehead sucker diets.
- **Drift relationship:** Humpback chub diets were most similar in invertebrate composition to the drift samples.
- **Niche separation:** Differences in behavior, spatial use, and diet may reduce competition among the three species.
