# Cui-ui Recovery Plan (Revised, 1983)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This revised recovery plan, published by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in Portland, Oregon, sets out the strategy for recovering the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered sucker endemic to the [[Truckee River]] and [[Pyramid Lake]] system in Nevada. The goal is to restore a non-endangered, self-sustaining population and to reclassify the species from endangered to threatened. The strategy combines protecting existing fish, augmenting the population through artificial culture, and rehabilitating essential habitat. The plan is a second update, covering FY 1984 through FY 1986. The document states that goals and objectives depend on appropriations, priorities, and other budgetary constraints, and that it is subject to modification as new findings emerge.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Cui-ui, *Chasmistes cujus* (common name "cope"), a large lake sucker of the Truckee River/Pyramid Lake system.
- **Plan history:** The original plan was approved on January 23, 1978 (direction through FY 1980). An update in 1980 covered FY 1983, and this document is the second update.
- **Recovery concept:** Restore an optimum, self-sustaining population in the Truckee River/Pyramid Lake system. Recovery is framed in three components: (1) identify, rehabilitate, and maintain sufficient habitat; (2) protect and manage the self-sustaining population; and (3) educate the public.
- **Causes of decline (as described):** Water diversions since the late 19th century reduced river flows into Pyramid Lake. Inadequately treated sewage, nonpoint pollution, channelization, grazing, and timber harvest degraded habitat. A delta at the river mouth blocks access to spawning grounds except in rare high-water years.
- **Organisation:** Part I (Introduction), Part II (Recovery goal, stepdown outline, narrative), Part III (Implementation Schedule with priorities, agencies, timelines, and costs).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and water quality.** Knowledge of spawning substrate, cover, and redd space is considered adequate. Preferred spawning temperature, depth, and velocity are not adequately known. Adult river water-quality requirements are essentially unknown. Required [[Dissolved Oxygen]] and pH levels for egg incubation are unknown.

**Lake ecosystem.** [[Pyramid Lake]] is described as nitrogen deficient and phosphorus rich. Large nitrogen loading could alter the nitrogen-phosphorus ratio and disrupt the ecosystem. Increasing [[Total Dissolved Solids]] may reduce zoo- and phytoplankton survival, which affects the food chain, but long-term effects on cui-ui longevity, growth, and reproduction are largely unknown.

**Threats and pressures.**
- Water diversions for agriculture, municipal, and industrial use reduced and altered river flow.
- Excessive nutrient loading and toxic conditions linked to growth of Reno and Sparks.
- Channelization increased flow velocity, erosion, and silt load; riparian canopy loss raised water temperatures.
- Non-native and native fishes may compete with cui-ui for space and food; extent unknown.
- Illegal dredge-and-fill activity in the lower river.

**Fishways and passage.** [[Marble Bluff Dam]] and [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] (built 1975) were intended to bypass the delta, but the fishway has never functioned properly and the dam blocks access during high-water years. [[Numana Dam Fishway]] also requires evaluation.

## Key Findings

- **Recruitment failure:** The plan states that cui-ui have had no significant recruitment since 1969, the last high-water year before Marble Bluff Dam was built. Spawners are dominated by one age class (14 years old). Reported runs were about 14,000 (1982) and 6,000 (1983) fish ascending the Marble Bluff Fish Facility, though it is unknown whether these numbers are adequate.
- **Likely cause:** Inadequate access to spawning areas, attributed to the delta and fishway failure.
- **Population dynamics:** Little is known; estimates of mortality, longevity, recruitment, and age composition are needed. Aging studies suggest extinction risk may be greater than originally thought.
- **Flow regime:** An interim estimate of the flow regime needed for river habitat exists but is described as speculative because of an incomplete database. Accurate determination is essential.
- **Hatchery augmentation:** The plan calls for producing 10 million larvae annually at the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe's hatchery, with eggs sourced from wild Pyramid Lake fish. Survival and contribution of released fish are not yet determined. Releases are to be made at pre-selected sites, and culture techniques are to be developed to rear larvae up to 20 mm, since current releases are 10 to 13 mm.
- **Priority tasks (Part III):** Priority 1 actions prevent extinction; priority 2 actions prevent significant decline; priority 3 actions provide full recovery. Key tasks include improving Marble Bluff and Numana fishways, securing flows (task #42/#333), negotiating Truckee/Carson water conflicts, enforcing the [[Endangered Species Act]] and Clean Water Act Section 404, and developing a [[Resource Management Plan]].
- **Implementation:** Lead agencies include [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Nevada Department of Environmental Protection]], [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]], [[U.S. Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[U.S. Geological Survey]], and the [[U.S. Environmental Protection Agency]]. Specific costs and timetables are in the Part III schedule table, which is not reproduced here.
- **Public outreach:** Tasks include news releases and a supplemental leaflet on cui-ui life history and the recovery effort.
