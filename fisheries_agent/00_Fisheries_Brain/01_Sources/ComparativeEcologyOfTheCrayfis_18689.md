# Comparative Ecology of the Crayfishes *Orconectes rusticus rusticus* and *Cambarus tenebrosus* in Doe Run, Meade County, Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cambaridae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Ruperth Prins]] compares the ecology and life histories of two freshwater crayfishes, [[Orconectes Rusticus Rusticus]] and [[Cambarus Tenebrosus]], in [[Doe Run]], a torrent spring stream in Meade County, Kentucky. Fieldwork ran from September 1962 through September 1964 at four permanent stations. The two species differed most clearly in habitat preference and distribution, feeding, temperature response, parasite infestation by the harpacticoid copepod [[Attheyella Carolinensis]], and reproduction and growth. The work was a dissertation project at the University of Louisville, supported in part by the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission.

## Background & Description

- **Prior survey:** A stream survey by [[Minckley]] (1963) found that *C. tenebrosus* occurred throughout Doe Run while *O. r. rusticus* was restricted to the lower three-fourths, which prompted this study.
- **Study site:** The main spring lies about 3 miles east of Ekron, Kentucky. The lotic reach is about 4 miles long and empties into Doe Valley Lake (about 500 acres), which drains to the [[Ohio River]]. The spring source sits at 575 ft msl and the stream reaches the Ohio at 374 ft msl (average gradient 20.7 ft per mile).
- **Physical features:** The stream is 30–40 ft wide along the lotic reach. Modal discharge was 15–25 cfs in 1962–1963 and 25–35 cfs in 1963–1964. Total alkalinity was 200–300 mg/l, pH near 7.0 at the source, dissolved oxygen 7–9 mg/l, and free CO2 usually above 40 mg/l.
- **Stations:** Station I was at the spring source (Mile 0.0). Stations II, III and IV lay 0.5, 2.0 and 3.1 miles downstream. Each was sampled at least monthly by seines, dip nets and hand collecting.
- **Taxonomy:** *O. r. rusticus* (Girard, 1852) has a type locality at Cincinnati, Ohio. The Doe Run form is not typical of the nominate subspecies. The *C. tenebrosus* population is tentatively assigned to that species but may be an intergrade with [[Cambarus Ornatus]]. Earlier authors had misidentified these crayfishes as *C. bartonii* forms.
- **Key terms:** Form I males are capable of copulation; Form II males are immature or reverted. Shedders are recently molted animals; busters are about to molt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature:** Spring-source water stayed near 13 °C (range 12.0–14.0 °C). Station III reached 17.5 °C (July 1963) and 1.0 °C (December 1963). Station IV reached 18.5 °C (August 1964) and 0.0 °C (December 1963) after nine days of subzero weather.
- **Molting limits:** Molting of *O. r. rusticus* essentially ceased between 10 and 12 °C. Torpor occurred near 4 °C.
- **Vegetation and substrate:** [[Vaucheria]] carpeted at least 80% of the bottom at Station II in fall 1963 and largely washed out each March. [[Fissidens Fontanus]] occurred on rubble, and [[Cladophora Fracta]] covered mid-channel areas at Station III. Water milfoil (*Myriophyllum heterophyllum*) and forget-me-not (*Myosotis scorpioides*) grew along banks.
- **Spring floods:** High discharges in March shifted substrate and washed out vegetation, and turbidity from silt reduced light.
- **Marl deposition:** Marl sealed rubble into solid slabs in the reach between Dam 3 and Station IV, an unfavorable substrate for *O. r. rusticus*.
- **Dams:** Three mill dams were not considered barriers, since both species were observed moving past them.

## Key Findings

**Distribution and habitat**
- *O. r. rusticus* occurred from below Dam 1 to Doe Valley Lake. Only one female (21 mm) was found above Dam 1, on 26 June 1963. Stations III and IV held the largest numbers.
- *C. tenebrosus* was most abundant in the headwaters. Station I and II totaled 3,374 specimens, Station III 724 and Station IV 181, so about 75% came from the upper mile.
- Adults of *C. tenebrosus* were mostly hypogean (in burrows). Juveniles were epigean in eddies and vegetation. Adults made up 13.6% of Station I catches, 5.1% at Station II and 14.9% at Station III.

**Parasitism**
- *C. tenebrosus* was more heavily infested by *Attheyella carolinensis* than *O. r. rusticus* because it spent more time in vegetation, where the copepod concentrated.

**Feeding**
- *O. r. rusticus* relied mainly on detritus (21.6% of volume at Station III; 13.2% at Station IV) and vascular plants.
- *C. tenebrosus* relied on vascular plants (22.9% at Stations I–II; 23.4% at Station III) and filamentous algae (25.8% at Stations I–II, mainly Vaucheria).
- Both species were opportunists. Stomach samples were 316 *O. r. rusticus* and 116 *C. tenebrosus* from the main stations.

**Reproduction and growth**

| Trait | *O. r. rusticus* | *C. tenebrosus* |
|---|---|---|
| Sexual maturity | 18 mm carapace length | ~40 mm |
| Maximum size | 45 mm | 65 mm |
| Hatching | May | July–August |
| Oviposition | March–May, peak April | June–July peak, extended |
| Lifespan | ~2.5 years | 36–38 months (probably) |

- *O. r. rusticus* matured in 14–15 months, and eggs took 4–6 weeks to hatch.
- *C. tenebrosus* molted through winter, matured after 20–22 months, and eggs took about 20 days at room temperature.
- Fecundity of *O. r. rusticus* rose with size: ovarian eggs ranged from 54 (18 mm female) to 357 (37 mm female). Regression for ovarian eggs gave r = 0.92 (n = 43).
- A 219-crayfish mark-recapture experiment at Station III recaptured 25 (11.4%) animals, showing no growth in the first winter.

**Conclusions**
- Temperature probably limited *O. r. rusticus* to the lower reaches. Its reproduction and maturation depend on molting, which the cooler headwaters did not support.
- *C. tenebrosus* is best described as a troglophile, not a troglobite, since it leaves burrows during spring floods.
