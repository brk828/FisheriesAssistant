# Draft Conservation Agreement for Virgin Spinedace

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #conservation #threatened-species

## Executive Summary

This draft Conservation Agreement addresses the proposed listing of [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*) as a threatened species under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. The [[Virgin Spinedace]] is a small cyprinid minnow endemic to the [[Virgin River]] drainage in southwestern Utah, northwestern Arizona, and southeastern Nevada. Once common throughout clearwater tributaries, current populations are highly fragmented and occur almost exclusively within Utah, representing a 40% reduction in historic distribution. Primary threats include stream dewatering for agricultural purposes, mining, urban growth, and introduction of non-native fishes. The agreement involves the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Utah Department of Natural Resources, Washington County Water Conservancy District, Bureau of Land Management, and National Park Service. Implementation will also benefit five additional native fish species including two endangered species ([[Woundfin]], *Plagopterus argentissimus*; [[Virgin River Chub]], *Gila robusta seminuda*) and the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*).

## Background & Description

The [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*) is a small cyprinid minnow endemic to the [[Virgin River]] drainage system. Earliest records (Tanner 1932, 1936) indicated the species was common in the [[Santa Clara River]] and North Fork of the [[Virgin River]]. Collection records from multiple institutions (University of Nevada Las Vegas, Brigham Young University, University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, United States National Museum) documented a general decline in range and population (Cross 1975).

Historical distribution included areas near Bunkerville, Nevada (collected by Hubbs in 1938), upper [[Beaver Dam Wash]], and lower [[Santa Clara River]]. By 1942, the species was absent from previously occupied areas near Bunkerville, and subsequent surveys failed to find [[Virgin Spinedace]] below Littlefield, Arizona (Cross 1975). The species prefers clear, cool flowing streams comprised of pools, runs, and riffles (Rinne 1971; Deacon and Rebane 1990; Addley and Hardy 1993) and is not adapted to lacustrine environments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat Destruction and Modification

The [[Virgin River]] Basin is significantly altered by dams and diversions built for agricultural and municipal purposes. These structures have resulted in degradation or elimination of [[Virgin Spinedace]] habitat through:

**Complete Dewatering**: Multiple reaches historically containing [[Virgin Spinedace]] are now dewatered, including DI ranch (East Fork of [[Beaver Dam Wash]]), [[Santa Clara River]] below Gunlock Reservoir, Magotsu Creek, [[Ash Creek]] near Toquerville, Leeds Creek, and mainstem [[Virgin River]] between Quail Creek Diversion and Pah Tempe Springs (Addley and Hardy 1993).

**Flow Alteration**: Dams and diversions affect aquatic vegetation, dissolved oxygen levels, temperature, pH levels, and turbidity. Low water levels and resulting elevated water temperatures, altered water chemistry, and low dissolved oxygen are detrimental to [[Virgin Spinedace]]. These structures also create barriers to fish movement, resulting in population fragmentation and reduced genetic exchange.

**Reservoir Construction**: Four reservoirs have inundated historical habitat: Schroeder Reservoir on [[Beaver Dam Wash]] (0.5 km), Baker Dam Reservoir (1.0 km), Gunlock Reservoir (2.9 km) on [[Santa Clara River]], and Quail Creek Reservoir (3.4 km) on Quail Creek. [[Quail Creek Reservoir]], filled in 1985, eliminated high-quality habitat where [[Virgin Spinedace]] were formerly common to abundant (Addley and Hardy 1993).

**Livestock Grazing**: Cattle and sheep utilize riparian areas causing devegetation, stream bank erosion, siltation, and degraded water quality. Valdez et al. (1991) indicated 10 of 13 populations were threatened by grazing in riparian areas and runoff from cattle feed lots. No exclosures exist to prevent livestock damage within riparian zones.

### Non-Native Species Impacts

**Predation**: [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) selectively prey on [[Virgin Spinedace]] (Espinosa and Deacon 1973). [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) and [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) stocked into reservoirs can move into [[Virgin Spinedace]] habitat. [[Rainbow Trout]] below Schroeder Reservoir probably prevent upstream migration and recolonization (Addley and Hardy 1993). Crayfish (Astacidae) and [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) prey on larval and young-of-year life stages.

**Competition**: [[Red Shiner]], a non-native species, is considered a primary cause of decline in endangered [[Woundfin]] populations and has likely had similar negative impacts on [[Virgin Spinedace]] (USFWS 1993). [[Redside Shiner]] (*Richardsonius balteatus*) was considered a threat (Rinne 1971; Cross 1975) but has significantly declined (Valdez et al. 1991).

**Parasites**: Asian tape worm (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*) arrived with [[Red Shiner]] in 1986 and weakens infested fish. An unknown parasite was found in [[Virgin Spinedace]] in the [[Ash Creek]] drainage (Addley and Hardy 1993).

### Additional Threats

**Drought**: Directly impacts habitat through dewatering, limiting usable habitat, and isolating populations. Artificial barriers prevent fish from retreating to areas with sufficient water. Drought combined with diversions causes greater habitat loss than would occur naturally.

**Pollution**: Return flows from municipal drains and agriculture contaminated with pesticides or herbicides, cattle waste, erosion, and siltation. Low flows increase the impact of mineral springs on water chemistry.

**Mining**: Cyanide contamination in [[Beaver Dam Wash]] from a gold mine on the East Fork is under study. Mining activities threaten habitat in [[Beaver Dam Wash]] and Moody Wash (Valdez et al. 1991).

## Key Findings

### Conservation Goal and Management Actions

**Primary Goal**: Remove threats warranting listing and restore historical [[Virgin Spinedace]] habitat through a 3-year Conservation Agreement.

### Habitat Maintenance and Enhancement

**Flow Requirements**: 
- Determine channel forming flows through frequency and magnitude analysis, comparing historic and current channel characteristics
- Establish minimum flow recommendations by comparing [[Virgin Spinedace]] population numbers, stream flows, and habitat characteristics throughout the [[Virgin River]] basin
- Monitor and maintain flows in currently occupied areas

**Flow Restoration**: Re-establish minimum and channel forming flows in approximately 24 miles of habitat, with priority areas:
- [[Santa Clara River]] between Gunlock Reservoir and confluence with [[Virgin River]] (16.7 miles)
- [[Virgin River]] between Quail Creek Diversion and Pah Tempe Springs (2.8 miles)
- Additional 5 miles in areas including [[Beaver Dam Wash]], North Creek, [[Ash Creek]], LaVerkin Creek, and Leeds Creek

**Habitat Enhancement**: Examine feasibility of enhancement projects to improve habitat quality and implement site-specific recommendations.

### Population Management

**Genetic Surveys**: Examine need for baseline genetic data on isolated populations and solutions to migration barriers to connect disjunct populations.

**Reintroduction**: Determine feasibility of re-establishing [[Virgin Spinedace]] into historically unoccupied habitat including Left and Righthand Creeks of North Creek, upper Leeds Creek, upper LaVerkin Creek, and Headwaters Creek of [[Beaver Dam Wash]]. South Creek and East Fork of [[Virgin River]] identified as potential sites but currently support native fish communities.

### Non-Native Fish Management

**Stocking Procedures**: Adapt and implement American Fisheries Society procedures for stocking non-native fishes throughout [[Virgin River]] basin. Evaluate proposals for introduction of non-native species for potential impacts to [[Virgin Spinedace]] and other native species.

**Species-Specific Actions**:
- Nevada Department of Wildlife to evaluate options for managing [[Rainbow Trout]] in Schroeder Reservoir
- Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR) to determine feasibility of eradicating green sunfish in [[Santa Clara River]] and review options for managing [[Brown Trout]] above Baker Dam to Moody Wash
- Eradicate [[Red Shiner]] in [[Virgin River]] from Johnson diversion to Utah-Arizona border; distribution studies completed and treatment procedures under development

**Stocking Guidelines for Virgin River Basin**:
- [[Bonneville Cutthroat Trout]] considered native/historic species; future introductions limited to areas above 5,000 feet elevation where interactions with other native fishes would not occur
- [[Rainbow Trout]]: allowed where they do not directly conflict with native species or establish self-sustaining populations in association with native species of special concern
- [[Brown Trout]]: generally not to be stocked; consideration for replacing existing populations above 5,000 feet where no conflicts with native species
- Non-native minnows, smallmouth bass, green sunfish: not to be stocked

### Monitoring and Administration

**Population Monitoring**: Develop monitoring plan to assess [[Virgin Spinedace]] and habitat response to management actions and determine population trends.

**Progress Reporting**: Annual reports required; all parties report progress quarterly. Conservation Agreement reviewed at 12th and 24th months to determine if revisions warranted. By 36th month, agreement must be reviewed and either modified, renewed, or terminated.

### Current Status Summary

Recent studies indicate a 40% reduction in [[Virgin Spinedace]] habitat from historic range (Valdez et al. 1991). Current and future threats (mining, non-native fishes, dewatering, livestock impacts, proposed water development projects) remain high. Continued fragmentation could lead to loss of genetic and population viability. The species is highly susceptible to future habitat loss and population declines.
