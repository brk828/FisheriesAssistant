# Aravaipa Valley Well Information: Groundwater Basic-Data Compilation

#hydrogeology #groundwater #aquifer-data #arizona

## Executive Summary

This [[United States Geological Survey]] open-file report (Water-Resources Investigations 76-107, September 1976, Tucson) compiles basic well data for the Aravaipa Valley area of southeastern Arizona, prepared in cooperation with the [[Arizona Water Commission]]. The document is a hydrologic inventory, not an aquatic biology study. It reports no fish, invertebrates, plankton, or surface-water ecological surveys, so the species, family, and ocean-basin fields are not applicable.

The compilation covers roughly 500 mi² of valley terrain. Groundwater development is described as small, with pumpage estimated at less than 3,000 acre-feet per year for irrigation, livestock, and domestic use. Water levels show no long-term decline, and the water is generally of good chemical quality based on limited data.

## Background & Description

The report is a basic-data compilation organized as a set of standardized well and spring inventory tables, explanatory keys, and an index map of [[Ground Water]] areas in Arizona. The area of this report is shaded on the index map. It was compiled by USGS personnel from well records, with supporting material held in computer-printout form at the Arizona Water Commission in Phoenix and at USGS offices in Tucson and Phoenix.

Key reference and coding systems used in the report include:

- **[[Well Numbering System]]:** Locations are given to the nearest 10 acres using the [[Gila and Salt River Meridian]] land survey, with quadrant letters (A, B, C, D), township, range, section, and 160-, 40-, and 10-acre tract letters. Oversize sections and half-township designations are handled with separate conventions.
- **[[Site Identification Number]]:** A unique 15-digit identifier. The first six digits encode latitude, the next seven encode longitude, and the last two are a sequence number. It is an identifier, not a locator.
- **County codes:** Graham (009), Greenlee (011), and Pinal (021), among 14 Arizona counties listed.
- **Use-of-water codes:** Including irrigation, domestic, stock, public supply, industrial, power generation, and unused.
- **[[Principal Aquifer]] codes:** A coded geohydrologic classification of stratigraphic units. The principal aquifer is defined as the unit contributing at least 50 percent of a well's water. The code list spans Cenozoic, Mesozoic, Paleozoic, and Precambrian units.
- **[[Water-Level Measurement]] methods:** Codes for airline, pressure-gage, manometer, steel-tape, and electric-tape measurements, among others.
- **[[Geophysical Logs]] and well construction:** Codes for drilling methods (air-rotary, cable-tool, hydraulic rotary, dug, driven, and others) and log types (driller's, gamma ray, temperature, and others).

Units are English, with metric conversions supplied: 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 acre-ft = 0.001233 cubic hectometers, and 1 mi² = 2.590 km².

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The valley's groundwater setting is shaped by [[Aravaipa Creek]] and its alluvial fill. According to the report:

- **[[Alluvial Aquifer]] (main water-bearing unit):** Along the creek, the younger alluvium (unconsolidated sand and gravel, a few tens of feet thick) and the underlying older alluvium (unconsolidated to semi-consolidated sand and gravel, at least 350 ft thick near the creek) form the principal productive zone. Near the creek, these units yield as much as 1,200 gal/min to irrigation wells.
- **Valley margins:** Older alluvium along valley sides may yield small to moderate quantities of water.
- **Elsewhere:** Small amounts of water may be obtained from volcanic rocks and tightly cemented conglomerate.
- **Irrigated land:** Mapped irrigated area is based on 1972–73 data from the Arizona Crop and Livestock Reporting Service and was not field-checked.
- **Water quality:** A stock well in section 32, T. 9 S., R. 22 E., drawing from older alluvium, had a [[Dissolved Solids]] concentration of 172 mg/L. Five irrigation wells sampled before 1962 (cited from [[Smith and Others 1964]]) ranged from 250 to 423 ppm, averaging about 340 ppm, with water drawn mainly from younger alluvium. The report notes that ppm and mg/L are nearly synonymous.
- **Water-level trends:** [[Hydrographs]] of selected wells show no long-term decline.

Specific conductance (µmhos/cm at 25 °C) and temperature are tabulated for many wells alongside water levels.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** The report is a hydrologic basic-data compilation for about 500 mi² of Aravaipa Valley, Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona. It contains no fish or aquatic biological data.
- **Development level:** Groundwater use is small. Pumpage is estimated at under 3,000 acre-ft annually for irrigation, livestock, and domestic uses.
- **Trend:** Hydrographs of selected wells show no long-term water-level decline.
- **Productivity:** Irrigation wells near [[Aravaipa Creek]] yield up to 1,200 gal/min from the younger and older alluvium.
- **Water quality:** Chemical quality is described as generally good, based on limited data. Dissolved solids were 172 mg/L in one stock well and 250–423 ppm (average about 340 ppm) in five pre-1962 irrigation wells.
- **Well inventory:** The well tables (OCR-transcribed) list roughly 40 wells with local numbers, site IDs, depth, altitude, water level, and measurement dates. Most are in Graham County (code 009), with some in Pinal County (code 021). Water-level measurement dates range from the 1940s to 1976.
- **Data availability:** Hydrologic data are held as computer printouts at the [[Arizona Water Commission]] (Phoenix) and at USGS offices in Tucson and Phoenix. Copies may be made at private expense at the Tucson and Phoenix offices.
- **Key references:** [[Simons]] (1964, Klondyke quadrangle geology, USGS Prof. Paper 461); [[Krieger]] (1968, Brandenburg Mountain and Holy Joe Peak quadrangle geologic maps, GQ-668 and GQ-669); [[Smith and Others 1964]] (quality of Arizona irrigation waters).
