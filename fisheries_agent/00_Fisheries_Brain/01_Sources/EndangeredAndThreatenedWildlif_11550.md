# Determination of Experimental Population Status for Colorado Squawfish and Woundfin

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology #reintroduction

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service establishes "nonessential experimental" populations of [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) in the [[Gila River]] drainage of Arizona under Section 10(j) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973. This designation allows these endangered species to be managed as threatened, providing greater regulatory flexibility while reestablishing populations within their historic range. The rule permits take in accordance with State and Tribal law and exempts these populations from Section 7(a)(2) protections, treating them instead as proposed species. Both fishes were historically widespread in Arizona river systems but have been extirpated from the state due to dewatering, dams, and competition with exotic species.

## Background & Description

[[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) once occupied the entire [[Colorado River]] system including the [[Gila River]] system in Arizona, with presence in tributaries including the [[Salt River]], [[Verde River]], and [[San Pedro River]]. The last specimen from Arizona waters was collected in the early 1950s. The species is currently listed as endangered, with viable populations remaining only in portions of the Green, Colorado, and Yampa Rivers in the upper basin.

[[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) were originally distributed in the mainstream Colorado, Gila, Salt, and Virgin Rivers. Only the [[Virgin River]] continues to maintain a woundfin population. The species was collected in the Gila River drainage as far upstream as the Gila-Salt confluence and up to 3,400 feet elevation in the Verde River.

Both species declined due to dewatering, dam construction, and competition with exotic species, particularly the red shiner. However, suitable habitat remains in selected stream areas for reintroduction efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The reintroduction sites are totally isolated from existing populations. The nearest [[Colorado Squawfish]] population is above [[Lake Powell]] in the Green and Colorado Rivers, an upstream distance of at least 800 miles, 6 mainstream dams, and 200 miles of dry riverbed. [[Woundfin]] are similarly isolated (450 miles distant, 200 miles of dry streambed, and 5 mainstream dams).

Donor populations for both species will come from stocks at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. For woundfin, if hatchery production is insufficient, additional adults will be gathered from the [[Virgin River]] from intermittent stream areas below the Washington and Mesquite water diversions where fish become stranded and die during irrigation season. A Section 7 consultation concluded such removal will not jeopardize the species.

The presence of [[Red Shiner]], an exotic species implicated in woundfin decline through competitive interaction, exists at some reintroduction sites. However, woundfin and red shiner have coexisted in the lower Virgin River for at least 10 years, indicating coexistence may be possible under appropriate conditions. Reintroduction sites were selected for habitat conditions most favorable to woundfin.

## Key Findings

**Regulatory Framework:**
- Experimental populations designated as "nonessential" under Section 10(j) of the Endangered Species Act
- Populations treated as threatened rather than endangered, allowing greater management flexibility
- Not subject to Section 7(a)(2) protections; treated as proposed species under Section 7
- Special rule establishes that taking will be governed under applicable State and Tribal laws and regulations
- Incidental take by licensed recreational fishermen not a violation if fish returned to habitat alive

**Reintroduction Sites:**

*Colorado Squawfish:*
1. Arizona, Gila County: [[Salt River]] from [[Roosevelt Dam]] upstream to U.S. Highway 60 Bridge
2. Arizona, Gila and Yavapai Counties: [[Verde River]] from [[Horseshoe Dam]] upstream to Perkinsville

*Woundfin:*
1. Arizona, Gila and Yavapai Counties: [[Verde River]] from backwaters of Horseshoe Reservoir upstream to Perkinsville
2. Arizona, Graham and Greenlee Counties: [[Gila River]] from backwaters of [[San Carlos Reservoir]] upstream to Arizona/New Mexico State line
3. Arizona, Greenlee County: [[San Francisco River]] from junction with Gila River upstream to Arizona/New Mexico State line
4. Arizona, Gila County: [[Tonto Creek]] from Punkin Center upstream to Gisela
5. Arizona, Yavapai County: [[Hassayampa River]] from Red Cliff upstream to Wagoner

**Management Plan:**
- Annual stocking planned for 10 years beginning 1985
- First Colorado squawfish stocking: up to 100,000 individuals distributed between 2 sites, all from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] captive-bred stock
- First woundfin stocking: at least 5,000 individuals distributed among 5 areas; from hatchery stock if possible, otherwise from Virgin River (not to exceed 25,000 fish from wild)
- Annual monitoring via seining surveys to determine population expansion/contraction, reproductive success, and general health
- Fish moving upstream or downstream from release sites retain nonessential experimental status

**Justification for Nonessential Status:**
- Colorado squawfish populations remain viable in Green, Colorado, and Yampa Rivers; sufficient brood stock available at Dexter NFH; propagation techniques established; loss of reintroduced populations will not reduce likelihood of wild survival
- Woundfin population in Virgin River relatively stable with moderately secure habitat; reintroduction designed to increase population numbers rather than prevent decline

**Public Comment Summary:**
- 17 comments received: 7 in support, 3 supportive with reservations, 4 opposed, 2 informational requests, 1 no comment
- Key concerns addressed: tribal jurisdiction over reservation lands, water rights litigation, potential effects on bald eagles, presence of red shiner, habitat management plans, potential for status upgrade
- Special rule modified to cover applicable Tribal as well as State laws and regulations

**Effective Date:** August 23, 1985
