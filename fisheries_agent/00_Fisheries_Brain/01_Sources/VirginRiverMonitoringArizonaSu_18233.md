# Virgin River Monitoring: Arizona Summary Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1995 memorandum from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] transmits fish survey results from two Arizona stations on the [[Virgin River]], sampled on April 26, 1995, and hoop-net collections retrieved on May 27, 1995. Sampling occurred during high flows of roughly 500 to 600 cfs, which the author judged a probable cause of the low catch numbers. Only three native fish were captured in the hoop nets: one Desert Sucker and two Flannelmouth Suckers. Seine-haul catch tables are largely illegible in the source scan, so the seine results cannot be reliably summarized.

## Background & Description

The memo was written by Robert H. Bettaso and addressed to a distribution list of fisheries biologists and managers from state wildlife agencies, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and universities, including [[Arizona State University]] and [[Utah State University]]. It is part of the [[Virgin River Monitoring Program]], which tracks native and introduced fish along the river.

The memo lists eleven species codes used in the data:

- Native and listed fishes: [[Woundfin]] (PLAR), [[Virgin River Chub]] (GIROSE), [[Virgin Spinedace]] (LEMO), Desert Sucker (CACL, *Catostomus* [=*Pantosteus*] *clarki*), and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (CALA, *Catostomus latipinnis*).
- Introduced or non-native species: [[Red Shiner]] (CYLU), [[Western Mosquitofish]] (GAAF), [[Speckled Dace]] (RHOS), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (AMNA), [[Black Bullhead]] (AMME), and [[Green Sunfish]] (LECY).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow conditions:** Flows were high (about 500 to 600 cfs) during the April sampling, and the author attributed the low collections to these conditions.
- **Reproduction:** Desert Suckers were spawning and expressing gametes at the time of sampling.
- **Hoop-net habitat:** Nets were set in backwater pools and boulder-formed pools next to deep runs on the river's right bank. Net #1 was about 20 feet below the mouth of [[Beaver Dam Wash]]. Nets #2 and #3 were about 200 to 300 feet above the I-15 bridge.
- **Sampling effort:** All three hoop nets fished from 1500 hours on April 26 until 0830 hours on April 27, 1995.
- **Gear:** Hoop nets had 1/2-inch mesh. Mouth widths were 2 feet for Net #1 and 3 feet for Nets #2 and #3.

## Key Findings

- **Hoop-net catch:** Three hoop nets were deployed, but only two captured fish.
  - Net #1 (Beaver Dam Wash, backwater pool) caught one Desert Sucker, 250 mm total length.
  - Net #2 (boulder pool above I-15) caught two Flannelmouth Suckers, 215 mm and 233 mm total length.
  - Net #3 twisted shut during its set and caught no fish, so its effort was lost.
- **Low catch:** The author linked the small catch to high flows during sampling.
- **Spawning:** Desert Suckers were spawning at the time of the survey.
- **Seine data:** The Cedar Pockets Station seine-haul table is only partly legible after scanning, with sample numbers 1, 7 and 10 and a total column. Rows for several species, including those coded CYLU, GAAF and RHOS, cannot be read reliably. The Beaver Dam Wash seine table has no legible data. These results should be checked against the original field records.
- **Scope:** The memo is a transmittal and summary document. It does not contain a formal analysis, population estimates, or management recommendations.
