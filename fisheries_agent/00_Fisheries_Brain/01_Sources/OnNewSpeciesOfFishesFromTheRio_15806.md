# On New Species of Fishes from the Rio Meta Basin of Eastern Colombia and on Albino or Blind Fishes from near Bogotá

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #taxonomy #Lebiasinidae #Loricariidae #Callichthyidae #Trichomycteridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by Carl H. Eigenmann, published as a contribution from the Zoological Laboratory of Indiana University, describes three new fish species collected from the Rio Meta Basin in Eastern Colombia in 1914: **[[Copeina metae]]**, **[[Otocinclus spectabilis]]**, and **[[Corydoras metae]]**. These species were collected by Manuel Gonzales from localities including Barrigona (Rio Meta) and Quebrada Cramalote (Villavicencio). Additionally, the paper documents anomalous **albino and partially depigmented specimens** of the "capitán" fish, **[[Eremophilus mutisii]]** (family [[Trichomycteridae]]), collected from the Rio Funjuelo near Usme Sur, close to Bogotá — providing early documentation of pigment-loss phenomena in Andean freshwater catfish possibly linked to subterranean or low-light habitat conditions.

## Background & Description

### Copeina metae (sp. nov.)
- **Type specimens:** Holotype 35 mm; paratypes up to 43 mm (I.U.M. 13251a, 13251)
- **Meristics:** Head 4 (in SL); depth 5; Dorsal rays 10; Anal rays 10 (rarely 11); lateral line scales 22–24
- **Eye:** ~equal to interorbital width, slightly greater than snout length, ~3x into head length
- **Caudal peduncle length:** equal to head length (snout to upper angle of gill opening)
- **Dorsal fin origin:** nearer to base of middle caudal rays than to head
- **Coloration:**
  - Black spot on middle of anterior dorsal rays (sometimes extending to ray tips)
  - Anal fin faintly margined with dark pigment
  - Broad dark band running from chin to caudal fin, most intense just behind head, nearly as wide as the eye
  - Narrow light band above the dark band, running from mouth angle to base of upper caudal lobe
  - Dusky dorsal surface
- **Taxonomic note:** Likely conflated in part with *Copeina eigenmanni* Regan (previously reported from Bogotá); Eigenmann suggests Regan's Bogotá specimens partly represent this new species.

### Otocinclus spectabilis (sp. nov.)
- **Type specimens:** Holotype 38 mm; 9 paratypes, 29–38 mm (I.U.M. 13253a, 13253)
- **Meristics:** Head (to occipital) ~2.5 in SL; depth 5.5; Dorsal I,7; Anal I,5
- **Eye:** 3.5 in snout length; 7 in head length; 3 in interorbital width
- **Plates:** 22 lateral plates
- **Ventral plate arrangement:** Large anal plate; variable irregular plates anterior to it (sometimes 2–3 series across belly); broad coracoid plate anterior to this irregular region
- **Temporal plate:** irregularly, imperfectly perforate
- **Fins:** No adipose fin; dorsal origin above middle of ventral fins; plates lack keels
- **Coloration:**
  - Dusky stripe from eye forward, joining stripe from snout to nares
  - Dark crescent behind nares
  - Faintly spotted back (obscure continuation of prenasal stripe)
  - Obscure barring on first rays of pectoral, dorsal, anal fins and entire caudal fin

### Corydoras metae (sp. nov.)
- **Type specimen:** Holotype 54 mm (I.U.M. 13451)
- **Meristics:** Head 3.25 (in SL); depth 2.38; Dorsal I,8; Anal I,6; plates 28
- **Eye:** 4x into head; interorbital 2+ in head
- **Snout:** nearly half head length
- **Suborbital:** about half eye length
- **Barbel:** lower maxillary barbel reaches gill opening
- **Dorsal spine:** equals head length minus opercle
- **Pectoral spine:** nearly as long as head, extends beyond middle of ventrals
- **Humeral shields:** widely separated ventrally, not encroaching on breast/belly
- **Coloration:**
  - Dark band across nape, through eyes, to lower head edge
  - Black band across end of caudal peduncle, continuing forward along mid-back
  - Band expands over dorsal fin, covering entire fin except distal 0.4 of last seven rays
  - Otherwise unmarked

### Eremophilus mutisii — Albino/Depigmented Specimens
Three anomalous specimens examined from Rio Funjuelo, Usme Sur (near Bogotá):

| Specimen | Length (mm) | Pigmentation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~160+ | Fully unpigmented body; eye visible only via lens/hyaline skin; eye diameter 2 mm, unpigmented |
| 2 | 130 | Body unpigmented except eye; eye appears fully pigmented, ~2 mm |
| 3 | 133 | Caudal fin & most of caudal peduncle dorsum unpigmented; body from peduncle to head faintly pigmented with irregular pigment-free patches; head sides behind eyes unpigmented; head dorsum/snout pigmented; eye normally pigmented, slightly <2 mm |

- Otherwise morphologically identical to normal "Capitán" specimens except for pigmentation anomalies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Copeina metae]]** and **[[Corydoras metae]]**: Inhabit lowland tributaries of the Rio Meta basin (Orinoco drainage), collected at Barrigona — likely occupying slow-moving streams/backwaters typical of small characiform and callichthyid catfish habitats.
- **[[Otocinclus spectabilis]]**: Collected from Quebrada Cramalote near Villavicencio — a small tributary stream habitat typical for algae-grazing loricariid catfish.
- **[[Eremophilus mutisii]]** ("Capitán"): A trichomycterid catfish native to high-altitude Andean waters near Bogotá (Rio Funjuelo, Usme Sur). The occurrence of albino and partially depigmented individuals raises ecological questions about:
  - Possible subterranean or low-light microhabitats (springs, underground water sources) common in Andean highland drainages
  - Parallels to cave-adapted or spring-adapted fish lineages that lose pigmentation in the absence of selective pressure for coloration
  - This aligns with broader patterns of [[Depigmentation In Freshwater Fishes]] observed in isolated or light-limited aquatic environments

## Key Findings

1. **Three new species** are formally described and distinguished from congeners using detailed meristic counts (fin ray counts, lateral line/plate counts) and pigmentation patterns: *Copeina metae*, *Otocinclus spectabilis*, and *Corydoras metae*.
2. **Taxonomic revision implication:** Eigenmann suggests that Regan's earlier records of *Copeina eigenmanni* from Bogotá were likely a misidentification, with specimens actually representing the newly described *Copeina metae*, collected from the Rio Meta rather than Bogotá proper.
3. **Intraspecific variability** is noted in *Otocinclus spectabilis*, particularly in ventral plate arrangement (2 vs. 3 plate series across the belly).
4. **Novel albinism documentation:** The paper provides an early natural history record of albinism and partial depigmentation in *Eremophilus mutisii*, a species otherwise well known and abundant near Bogotá, suggesting these pigment anomalies occur naturally within an otherwise typical population rather than representing a distinct cave-adapted species.
5. **Morphological consistency:** Aside from pigmentation, the anomalous *Eremophilus mutisii* specimens are noted as morphologically identical to normal-colored conspecifics, supporting an intraspecific pigment variation rather than speciation event.
