# Task C — Draft Report: Summary Documentation and Preliminary SI Curves for Endangered Upper Colorado Basin Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This draft report, prepared by [[BIO/WEST]] for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 6, Office of Endangered Species), documents the [[SI Curve Development Project]] (Contract No. 14-16-0006-86-055). The project aims to produce the best possible [[Suitability Index Curves]] for depth, velocity, substrate, cover and temperature for all life stages of the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The [[Bonytail Chub]] was excluded because of its extreme rarity and lack of quantitative data.

The project combined assimilation of about 40 source files (over 100,000 records), two expert workshops, and statistical partitioning of data into category 2 (data-generated) curves. The report is a draft: final curve approval and category 1 (judgement-based) curves were still pending Workshop #2 (March 30 to April 3, 1987).

## Background & Description

The [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM) simulates habitat as a function of streamflow. Suitability Index (SI) curves are integral to IFIM and describe relative preference for habitat variables. A 1984 [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] curve set was criticized for excluding non-FWS data, poor documentation, and lacking expert consensus on methods. This project responded by integrating all available data and using a formal consensus process with a panel of five species experts.

Key project objectives were to:
1. Identify which database records are suitable for quantitative curve development.
2. Develop category 2 curves using agreed methods.
3. Develop interim category 1 curves where data are sparse.
4. Fully document the project.
5. Obtain expert consensus on all phases.

Work elements and schedule: Task A (database summaries) due December 12, 1986; Task B (Workshop #1, Salt Lake City, January 5–9, 1987) documented January 23, 1987; Task C (this draft) March 20, 1987; Task D (Workshop #2, held at the [[National Ecology Center]], Fort Collins) March 30–April 3, 1987, documented April 17, 1987; Task E (draft final report) May 8, 1987; Task F (final report) June 19, 1987.

### Task A: Database Assimilation

- Data were drawn from about 40 computerized files and [[CWC Database]] (1964–1984), with FWS-direct data used for 1979–1986.
- The working [[FWS Database]] contained about 8,000 records with depth, velocity or substrate measurements on the three target fish.
- The database spans 23 years (1964–1986) and includes collections of 17 principal investigators from FWS, Bureau of Reclamation, Colorado and Utah Divisions of Wildlife, and private interests.
- Data formats were CATCH (all species), RARE (juvenile and adult rare fish) and RADIO (radiotelemetry of adults). Fish under 60 mm used CATCH; 60 mm and longer used RARE; all RADIO data were used.
- 86 datafiles were identified (Table A-1) and 108 summary tables were built, one per life stage per species per river.
- Two datasets (Wick and Stoneburner 1984; Haynes 1982–1985) could not be entered.
- Errors included conflicting larval length definitions, inconsistent missing-data codes and differing fieldnames.

### Previous Studies

Three previous studies that produced habitat curves were reviewed: Holden (1977) on the Green River and juvenile squawfish habitat; Twedt and Holden (1980) on the [[San Juan River]] (156 adult squawfish, with seasonal splits); and Valdez et al. (1982) in the [[Upper Colorado River Investigations]] (377 YOY, 34 juveniles and 105 larger squawfish; 36 juvenile and 184 adult chub; 52 adult razorback suckers).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three target fishes are endangered native species of the upper basin. Habitat use is structured by life stage, river, season, time of day and sampling gear. Experts found that radiotelemetry and conventional gear (electrofishing, passive nets, active seines) often yielded significantly different habitat measurements, and that April–June habitat use differed from other months in several rivers. Backwaters were a distinct habitat for young squawfish, and the Colorado River, Green River and Yampa River populations showed significantly different habitat use across most parameters.

Substrate classes were silt (SI), sand (SA), gravel (GR), cobble/rubble (CO/RU), boulder (BO) and bedrock (BE). Habitat classes included backwater (BA) and other habitat types.

Workshop #1 used eight partitioning criteria: life stage, river, strata, year, gear, time of year, time of day and sample design. Five experts made all consensus decisions.

## Key Findings

**Workshop #1 outcomes**
- Consensus of five experts produced 291 data partitions for possible category 2 curves and 48 partitions for category 1 curves.
- After BIO/WEST testing and pooling, 25 partitions were retained for razorback sucker, 24 for humpback chub and 79 for Colorado squawfish.
- Testing rules: a "test" pooled partitions unless the probability level was below 0.05, with automatic pooling if either partition had fewer than 20 fish; a "decision/test" was reported but never pooled, leaving the decision to the experts at Workshop #2.
- Statistical methods were the Kolmogorov/Smirnov test for depth and velocity, and the Chi-square test for substrate and habitat. Curves were fitted to optimised histograms using exponential polynomial, exponential or generalized Poisson models.

**Razorback sucker**
- No data were available for larvae, young-of-year, juveniles or spawning adults; category 1 curves are needed.
- Green River nonspawning adults: four category 2 curve sets (radiotelemetry and conventional gear, each split April–June vs other months). Radiotelemetry differed significantly from conventional gear, so the report recommends two curve sets and leaves the choice of data source to the experts.
- Colorado River nonspawning adults: two curve sets (electrofishing plus passive nets, April–June vs other months), retained despite some non-significant tests because of expert opinion.

**Humpback chub**
- Larvae and Yampa River young-of-year and juveniles lacked data.
- Category 2 sets exist for young-of-year and juveniles in the Green and Colorado rivers, but the Green vs Colorado decision test was not possible because of small samples.
- Nonspawning adults: Yampa River (one set), Green River (one pooled set), Colorado River (four sets). The report recommends one Colorado River curve pooling Black Rocks and Westwater conventional gear data, because radiotelemetry samples were too small.

**Colorado squawfish**
- Larval curves came from a pooled set (CS000) of 699 fish records (1498 measurements, per the table).
- Size classes 2 to 5 yielded numerous category 2 curves across the Colorado, Green, Yampa and White rivers. Intensive (1979–1981) and post-intensive sampling differed significantly, raising possible sampling bias.
- Nonspawning adults (Size 5) had the most curve sets: six for the Colorado, three for the White, five for the Green and five for the Yampa, split by gear type and season. Cross-river tests showed significant habitat differences, arguing against pooling.
- Spawning: category 2 curves were produced only for radiotelemetry (Green and Yampa staging and deposition). Electrofishing and net data were insufficient. Colorado River spawning data were unavailable.

**Special tests**
- Radiotelemetry "nose" measurements differed significantly from "inside" and "outside" measurements for humpback chub and squawfish in the Colorado and Green rivers.
- 24-hour (diel) and 2-hour point sampling differed significantly in the Green and Yampa rivers.

**Planned confidence rating system (for Workshop #2)**
Experts were to rate each category 2 curve as 1 = Excellent, 2 = Good, 3 = Fair, 4 = Poor (use as a guide for a category 1 curve) or 5 = Unacceptable (replace with a category 1 curve). Results were not yet available.

**Next steps**
- Workshop #2 will approve category 2 curves, assign confidence ratings and develop category 1 curves for data-poor life stages and variables, using polygon overlays projected at the workshop.
- BIO/WEST will then produce the Task E Draft Final Report, followed by the final report (June 19, 1987).
