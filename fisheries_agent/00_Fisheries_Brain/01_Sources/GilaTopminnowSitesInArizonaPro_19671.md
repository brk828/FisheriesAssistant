# Gila Topminnow Sites in Arizona: Provisional Extirpation Report, 1996-97 Field Season

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliopsis

## Executive Summary

This draft technical report from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) Nongame Branch reviews the status of introduced [[Gila Topminnow]] populations at 26 sites in Arizona. Most sites were stocked between 1975 and 1983 with fish from [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]], which originated from [[Monkey Spring]], Cocio and Bylas springs. Field surveys were compiled from 1982 through 1996, and the report evaluates whether each population has been extirpated.

Most reviewed populations have been lost. The main causes recorded are drying of springs and tanks, flood scouring, livestock impacts, water diversion, dense vegetation, and competition or predation from nonnative fishes. Based on the site accounts, the authors recommend restocking 11 sites, not restocking 12 sites, and resurveying 3 sites before a decision is made.

## Background & Description

The report is a provisional, field-based assessment prepared by David A. Weedman and Adele L. Girmendonk, and it is labelled a draft (February 1997). Its purpose is to decide which Gila topminnow sites should be written off as extirpated and which should be kept for recovery. Funding came from the AGFD Heritage Fund, Arizona fishing license revenues, and Project E5, Job 09, under Title VI of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

The species is a small live-bearing fish. Its status is judged against several reference sources, including [[Brooks 1985]], [[Bagley et al 1991]], [[Brown and Abarca 1992]] and [[Simons 1987]]. A companion review of the [[Gila Chub]] is also cited.

The statewide site catalogue in the appendix sorts each locality into one of four status groups:

- **Augment:** sites with past introductions that should be restocked for recovery, even if they were not evaluated under the extirpation criteria.
- **Extant:** sites that currently support Gila topminnow.
- **Extirpated:** sites that the team has written off, with superscripts (1, 2, 3) marking which report evaluated them.
- **Failed:** sites not yet evaluated against the extirpation criteria, or that do not meet them but should probably be written off. Most of these are on [[Fort Huachuca]].

Each entry records stocking date, stock source, the date the site was first reported extirpated, the last sample date and the surveyor.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sites are mostly small, unstable habitats in semi-arid Arizona. The report repeatedly describes the following pressures:

- **Drying and low water:** Many sites were dry or nearly dry during surveys, including [[T.T. Spring]], [[Unnamed Spring #2]], [[Fig Spring]], [[Blue Mountain Spring]] and [[White Rock Spring]]. Spring flow was often piped away, as at [[White Rock Spring]] and [[T.T. Spring]].
- **Flooding and scouring:** Floods removed populations at [[Bain Spring]] (April 1991), [[Cave Creek]] (January 1993, described as the largest on record) and [[Cow Creek]] (January 1993). Flash-flood scour is also a concern at [[Camp Creek]] and [[Sheep Creek]].
- **Livestock and recreation:** Cattle grazing, trampling and bank erosion are noted at many sites. Heavy public use affected [[Sabino Canyon]].
- **Vegetation:** Dense [[Cattail Typha]] beds and tules overgrew several ponds, including [[Thicket Spring]] and [[Howard Well]], and reduced open water.
- **Nonnative fishes and predators:** [[Green Sunfish]] are a threat at [[Sabino Canyon]] and in lower [[Lime Creek]]. [[Fathead Minnow]] were first collected at [[Bain Spring]] in 1989. [[Mosquitofish]] were suspected at [[Howard Well]]. [[Bullfrog]] abundance was noted at [[Howard Well]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] habitat.
- **Barriers:** Waterfalls and pools at [[Josephine Canyon]] are considered barriers that would prevent upstream recolonization after flood displacement. Waterfalls also occur at [[Lime Cabin Spring]].

Co-occurring native fishes at several sites include [[Longfin Dace]], Gila chub, desert suckers and [[Desert Pupfish]].

## Key Findings

**Overall outcome by site (26 site accounts):**

- **Restock recommended (11 sites):** [[T.T. Spring]] (site 14A), [[Thicket Spring]] (15), [[Bain Spring]] (26), [[Cave Creek]] (49B), [[Cow Creek]] (72), [[Tule Creek Seep]] (73, modify habitat then restock), [[Howard Well]] (83, modify then restock), [[The Lake]] (121), [[Camp Creek]] (160), [[Lime Cabin Spring]] (248, using fish from Lime Creek) and [[Sabino Canyon]] (250, after renovation).
- **Do not restock (12 sites):** [[Unnamed TT Spring]] (14B), [[Unnamed Spring #2]] (29), [[Pasture Well]] (31), [[Unnamed Spring #5]] (65), [[Unnamed Spring #6]] (66), [[Mansfield]] (93), [[Josephine Canyon]] (197), [[Blue Mountain Spring]] (244), [[Fig Spring]] (246), [[White Rock Spring]] (249), [[Bronco Canyon Spring Tank]] (54) and [[Rock Tank Spring]] (64).
- **Resurvey before deciding (3 sites):** [[Canada del Oro]] (79), [[Apache Canyon]] East Fork (176) and [[Romero Canyon]] (252). Romero Canyon has no AGFD monitoring records.

**Notable population data:**

- [[T.T. Spring]]: 3000 to 4000 adults and 3000 to 4000 young were recorded in 1983. The last viable population was reported in 1986. Explanations conflict: Simons attributed the loss to low water, while [[Bagley et al 1991]] concluded that three consecutive floods probably eliminated it in 1987.
- [[Pasture Well]]: topminnow persisted for at least 4 years and possibly 6 years. The site was dry during the last three visits.
- [[Cow Creek]]: topminnow were present from 1981 through 1991 and were last collected in August 1991.
- [[Cave Creek]]: topminnow were present from at least 1989 through 1992 and have not been collected since December 1992. Surveys in fall 1995 and 1996 found [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Speckled Dace]] but no topminnow.
- [[Lime Cabin Spring]]: the spring itself is extirpated, but Gila topminnow were locally abundant in [[Lime Creek]], restricted to the upstream 2.5 miles of watered channel below the spring confluence.
- [[Thicket Spring]]: about 50 adults and 50 to 75 young were seen six days after stocking in 1983. The population was reported dense in 1987, then extirpated by 1989.
- [[The Lake]]: fish were abundant in December 1982 but absent after 1984, with no clear cause identified.
- [[Canada del Oro]]: 50 to 100 topminnow were found in 1982, but the site has cold water, high flows and a steep gradient, so it is probably unsuitable.

**Management recommendations:**

- Evaluate water flow and pipe extraction before restocking [[T.T. Spring]].
- Deepen the pond, rebuild the exclosure fence and manage Typha before restocking [[Thicket Spring]], following [[Brown and Abarca 1992]] and [[Bagley et al 1991]].
- Create additional pools and protect the spring from flood conditions at [[Bain Spring]].
- Control [[Green Sunfish]] in Lime Creek so topminnow can expand their range, and restock [[Lime Cabin Spring]] from Lime Creek when needed.
- Reintroduce topminnow repeatedly at flood-prone streams such as [[Cave Creek]] and [[Cow Creek]], because flooding is the most likely cause of repeated losses.
- Keep [[Augment]] sites in the recovery program when habitat is adequate, even if they are currently dry.

**Limitations:** The report is marked as a draft. Many site records are based on single visits, unknown sampling effort, or secondary references (AGFD files and unpublished field notes). Some entries contain inconsistencies, such as the [[Apache Canyon]] location question and a possible misidentification at [[Howard Well]].
