# Fish Survey of Salt River Project Canals, Phoenix Metropolitan Area, Arizona (1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This summary report, prepared by Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]]'s Center for Environmental Studies for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Phoenix Area Office under federal grant 6-FG-32-00480 (dated 21 February 1996), documents a 1995 fish survey of two canals operated by the [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) in the Phoenix metropolitan area. The [[Arizona Canal]] and the [[South Canal]] were sampled in late 1995. Twenty-two fish species were collected or reliably observed across the two canals, with 20 recorded in the Arizona Canal and 18 in the South Canal. The fauna is dominated by introduced species, though three native catostomids and a native cyprinid were present. The authors report no substantive change in the canal ichthyofauna since the last published account, apart from the stocking of [[Grass Carp]].

## Background & Description

The SRP system diverts water from the Salt and Verde rivers at [[Granite Reef Dam]] for agricultural, domestic, and industrial use. The Arizona Canal runs on the north side of the riverbed and the South Canal on the south side. An [[Electrical Fish Barrier]] located below Granite Reef Dam on each canal limits fish movement downstream, so survey collections were divided into sections above and below the barrier. Additional collections were made between Granite Reef Dam and the electrical barrier on each canal, with participation from Arizona Game and Fish Department personnel.

Field methods included seine and other capture collections, with captured fish measured for total length, then photographed or fixed in 10% formalin. Voucher specimens were deposited in the ASU Collection of Fishes (Department of Zoology). Relative abundance was estimated from collection counts combined with visual assessment, using these categories (percentage of total fishes):

- Rare: < 2%
- Uncommon: 3-5%
- Common: 6-20%
- Abundant: > 21%

The Arizona Canal was visited 25-26 November 1995 and the South Canal 21-22 October 1995. Field notes, measurements, and data summaries for each canal appear in Appendices A and B, which are not reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The canal fish community is a mixture of native species that persist in the canals and introduced species that are established or stocked. The baseline for comparison is the published account by [[Marsh and Minckley 1982]], which listed 23 species in these canals.

Native species recorded in both canals:
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (rare in both canals)
- [[Desert Sucker]] (abundant in both canals)
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (abundant in both canals)

Common or abundant introduced species:
- [[Red Shiner]] (abundant in both canals)
- Grass carp (common in both canals; stocked by SRP)
- Channel catfish and [[Mosquitofish]] (common in both canals)

Species that may occur upstream of the electrical barrier but were not captured downstream include bigmouth buffalo, smallmouth bass, and walleye. The authors state these appearances are not unexpected given their known occurrence upstream or intentional stocking.

Taxa from the 1982 list not encountered in 1995 were longfin dace (considered rare in 1982), fathead minnow, black bullhead (rare), yellow bullhead (uncommon), redear sunfish (uncommon), and Mozambique tilapia (stocked by AZGFD). The authors attribute these absences most likely to rarity rather than local extirpation, since these species occur upstream or elsewhere in the area.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 22 species were collected or observed across the Arizona and South canals in 1995 (Table 1). The Arizona Canal yielded 20 species and the South Canal 18.
- **New records relative to 1982:** Four species were not in the 1982 list of 23 species but were expected in the system: bigmouth buffalo, smallmouth bass, walleye, and grass carp. The hybrid glass x bighead carp was listed as stocked.
- **Missing species:** Six species from the 1982 account were not encountered: longfin dace, fathead minnow, black bullhead, yellow bullhead, redear sunfish, and Mozambique tilapia.
- **Regional total:** The total number of fish species recorded from the Phoenix metropolitan area now stands at 38.
- **Canal comparison:** No distinctive differences in species richness or relative abundance were found between the Arizona and South canals.
- **Temporal stability:** With the exception of grass carp stocking, the authors found no evidence of substantive change in the ichthyofauna since the last published report.
- **Native persistence:** Juvenile and adult life stages of all three native species (roundtail chub, desert sucker, Sonora sucker) were collected in both canals.
- **Species-level status (Table 1):** Red shiner, desert sucker, and Sonora sucker were abundant in both canals. Carp, goldfish, roundtail chub, and rainbow trout were rare in both. Grass carp, channel catfish, and mosquitofish were common in both.
- **Barrier-dependent records:** Bigmouth buffalo, smallmouth bass, and walleye were recorded in the sections above the barrier or on the fish-barrier boundary, but were not captured downstream of the electrical barrier in these surveys.
