# The Genus Zealeuctra and Its Position in the Family Leuctridae (Plecoptera, Insecta)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #leuctridae

*Note: This document concerns stoneflies (order Plecoptera, an aquatic insect group), not fish. The tags above follow the family-level taxon; the ichthyology and marine-biology tags are retained only as required by the note template and do not reflect the subject matter.*

## Executive Summary

Ricker and Ross (1969) revise the stonefly genus [[Zealeuctra]] (family [[Leuctridae]], order Plecoptera), originally erected by Ricker in 1952 with type species [[Leuctra claasseni]] Frison 1929. The genus contains seven known species, six of which are newly described: *arnoldi*, *fraxina*, *hitei*, *narfi*, *wachita*, and *warreni*. All occur in the central Mississippi valley region of the United States, with minor overlap into adjacent watersheds in Texas and West Virginia. The authors conclude that *Zealeuctra* occupies a fairly central position among Leuctridae, that its large epiproct and basally undivided subanal probe are primitive characters, and that its species are closely allied to one another but distinct from other genera. The paper also shows that the name *claasseni* had been applied to a mixture of species from the outset, so earlier published records of *claasseni* are reliable only where confirmed in this study.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic history:** [[Leuctra claasseni]] was described by T. H. Frison (1929) from two male specimens collected in streams near Herod, Pope County, Illinois. The female was described in 1935. Hanson (1941) placed the species in *Paraleuctra*, and Ricker (1952) erected subgenus *Zealeuctra* for it. Illies (1966) elevated it to generic rank, which the authors accept.
- **Problem of identity:** Reexamination of older material showed that the 1929 and later records mixed several species. The holotype and allotype of *claasseni* were confirmed as true *claasseni*, while some paratype and figured specimens belong to *fraxina* or *narfi*.
- **Type material:** Holotypes and allotypes of new species are deposited in the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]] collection.
- **Diagnostic features:** Males are separated mainly by the epiproct (spine number, accessory cusps, and origin relative to the base), the sclerotized margins of the ninth tergite, and the cerci. Females are separated mainly by the seventh and eighth sternites, including the presence, shape, and color of a terminal lobe.
- **Body size:** Male body lengths range from about 5.5 to 7 mm. Female body lengths range from about 5.5 to 8 mm (wing tip lengths are given per species in the original).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collecting season:** Most specimens were collected in winter and early spring (roughly January to April), consistent with the "winter stonefly club" collaborators' sampling effort.
- **Habitat:** The document reports collections from named creeks, branches, and rivers (for example, Gibbons Creek, Ash Cave in Ohio, Caddo River, Ouachita River, Otter Creek in Wisconsin). It does not describe water chemistry, substrate, or community composition.
- **Distribution pattern:** The species are largely allopatric or parapatric. *Z. narfi* reaches the northernmost known locality for the genus (Sauk County, Wisconsin). *Z. wachita* complements *fraxina* west of the Mississippi.
- **Named collectors and localities:** Over 60 localities are listed across the states above, most with single to a few specimens. The authors note that Arkansas and Oklahoma collections usually contained only one specimen per site.
- **Known sites:** Ash Cave, Hocking County, Ohio, is described as a prolific stonefly site.

## Key Findings

1. **Seven species in *Zealeuctra*:** Six species are newly described (*arnoldi*, *fraxina*, *hitei*, *narfi*, *wachita*, *warreni*), in addition to the retained *claasseni*.
2. **Taxonomic correction:** Older records of *Leuctra claasseni* combined several species. Only specimens confirmed in this paper should be accepted as *claasseni*.
3. **Generic status:** *Zealeuctra* is retained at generic rank, following Illies (1966), though the authors state this is "with some regret."
4. **Phylogenetic position:** *Zealeuctra* lies in a fairly central position within Leuctridae. Its large epiproct and basally undivided subanal probe are primitive characters, while the absence of dorsal sclerotization on the female abdomen is advanced.
5. **Evolutionary inference:** *narfi* is considered the most primitive species within the genus. A suggested evolutionary sequence (Fig. 28) branches from an ancestral form toward *narfi*, *hitei*, *claasseni*, *wachita*, *fraxina*, *arnoldi*, and *warreni*. The authors note that the position of *wachita* remains uncertain until the male is discovered.
6. **Unique traits:** The deeply cleft male ninth tergite and the posterior lobe of the female seventh sternite are peculiar to *Zealeuctra*.
7. **Family-level framework:** The authors review morphological trends across Leuctridae, including changes in the subanal apparatus, the epiproct, and the female sternites. They propose a 19-step evolutionary sequence (Fig. 29) for the family, with *Megaleuctra* considered the most primitive living member.
8. **Terminology:** The authors discuss confused homologies for the subanal probe, specilla, titillators, and epiproct, and they propose the term "specilla" (after Brinck 1956) for the split outer probe halves.
