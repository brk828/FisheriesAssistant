# Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area Management Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Land Management (BLM) document, issued January 1998 by the [[Safford Field Office]], contains the final 15-year management plan, [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA No. AZ-040-08-03), and Finding of No Significant Impact/Decision Record for the [[Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area]] (RNCA) in southeastern Arizona. The plan was mandated by the [[Arizona Desert Wilderness Act of 1990]] (Public Law 101-628), which required conservation, protection and enhancement of riparian areas and their aquatic, wildlife and cultural resources.

The plan combines actions from five alternatives analyzed in the 1993 Draft Plan/EA. Its central decisions are: deferring livestock grazing in riparian areas, reducing maintained riparian roads in [[Bonita Creek]] from about 15 miles to 2 miles, closing the [[Gila River]] floodplain to motorized vehicles including [[Sand Rail Use]], and pursuing [[Instream Flow Water Rights]]. The EA rates the overall impact on soil, water, riparian vegetation, wildlife and fish as high positive.

## Background & Description

**Location and setting.** The RNCA lies about 180 miles east of Phoenix, 12 miles east of Safford, and covers roughly 21,767 acres of public land and 1,720 acres of private land. It includes a 15-mile segment of Bonita Creek and 23 miles of the Gila River, and it receives two additional perennial waterways, Eagle Creek and the San Francisco River, for four perennial systems in total. The area is described as steep-walled desert canyons and "one of the most significant riparian zones in the southwest."

**Planning process.** A seven-member [[Gila Box Advisory Committee]] was appointed under the Act. Scoping began in 1991, the committee met 19 times between October 1991 and November 1992, and the Draft Plan/EA was released in August 1993. Six tribes were contacted; none responded, and the [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] declined to meet with BLM. The tribe's boundary dispute was judged outside the scope of this planning effort.

**Governing law.** Key constraints include the [[Wild and Scenic Rivers Act]] (Gila River, Bonita Creek and San Francisco River found suitable in 1996), the [[National Historic Preservation Act]], the [[Endangered Species Act]], [[Executive Order 11990]] (wetlands), [[Executive Order 11988]] (floodplains), and the Clean Water Act. Bonita Creek is a designated Unique Water under Arizona code. The RNCA was withdrawn from mineral entry, subject to valid existing rights.

**Planning framework.** The document identifies 17 public issues, consolidated into nine management categories: riparian area, recreation and transportation, cultural resources, fish and wildlife and T&E species, water quality, private lands, the City of Safford water system, research and education, and fire management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Riparian and aquatic habitat.** Riparian vegetated area totals only about 635 acres along the Gila River and about 160 acres along Bonita Creek (795 acres combined). Management objectives call for cottonwood, willow and sycamore tree-to-sapling ratios and densities to be met within seven years after major floods. Flood events are described as natural drivers that remove vegetation and reshape channels.

**Fish community.** Bonita Creek still supports five native fish species at risk from non-native fish moving in from the Gila River. Native fishes are considered relatively flood-resistant, while non-natives are not. Non-native fish invade the lower creek, and the [[Non-Native Fish Barrier]] proposal aims to protect native species. Razorback sucker is either extirpated from Bonita Creek or below detection. The plan lists 10 native species as candidates for reintroduction analysis: razorback sucker, bonytail chub, Colorado squawfish, roundtail chub, flannelmouth sucker, woundfin, spikedace, loach minnow, desert pupfish and Gila topminnow.

**Water resources.** The Gila Decree (1935) apportioned nearly all Gila River flow, so new water rights in the RNCA can only be non-consumptive instream flow rights. The City of Safford holds a right for 3,240,000 gallons per day from Bonita Creek, and the City holds four rights-of-way along the creek. The City's system is noted as providing a barrier that protects native fish in the upper creek.

**Wildlife.** Priority species include [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Bald Eagle]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Cactus Ferruginous Pygmy-Owl]], [[Bighorn Sheep]] and [[Common Black-Hawk]]. Population objectives include 3 to 5 wintering bald eagles, 200 bighorn sheep and 10 existing common black-hawk pairs. Gila chub objectives are 150 adults per mile in Segment 1 and 185 adults per mile in Segments 3 and 5.

**Recreation pressure.** Visitor use exceeds 20,000 visitor use days per year. The area supports camping, fishing, hunting, tubing, boating and four-wheel-drive use. Commercial river outfitting is capped at five companies, and a permit system is triggered when more than 80 persons are in the Gila River canyon.

**Grazing.** The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Programmatic Biological Opinion (September 26, 1997; 2-21-96-F-160) covers 288 allotments totaling 1,588,258 acres and an average of 145,537 animal unit months. Its terms are non-discretionary. Nine of 11 permittees had already voluntarily kept livestock out of riparian areas.

**Cultural resources.** Sites include Pueblo Devol cliff dwellings, the Mimbres Village, the Plumed Serpent pictograph, Serna Cabin and Old Lady Gay Cabin. Visitor impacts are a central concern.

## Key Findings

- **Riparian grazing is deferred for the life of the plan.** BLM found that "no riparian grazing" best meets the mandate to "conserve, protect and enhance." It estimates about two dozen head of livestock may be removed.
- **Bonita Creek roads are cut sharply.** Public riparian road access drops from about 15 miles to 2 miles. The City of Safford keeps about 1.5 miles of riparian road for administrative access to its water system.
- **No road is designated in the Gila River floodplain.** Motorized vehicles, including sand rails, are prohibited there. The EA estimates 20 to 30 sand rail enthusiasts are displaced.
- **Native fish protection.** The plan proposes a feasibility study for a fish barrier in lower Bonita Creek, to be built only if it is under about 8 feet tall and has minimal visual impact. It also calls for intensive Gila chub population surveys and periodic fishery inventories with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
- **Pollution safeguards.** Diesel pumps require spill containment for 110% of on-site diesel volume. The Lee Trail septic system is replaced by a portable unit. Macroinvertebrates will be sampled annually at three Bonita Creek sites and five Gila River sites.
- **Recreation management.** The plan calls for 15 to 35 access and recreation sites, including two campgrounds (Owl Canyon, 10 units; River View, 15 units) and a boat access at Dry Canyon. Fees are deferred, and entry fees are unlikely to apply to the whole RNCA.
- **Land acquisition.** BLM will pursue private inholdings by exchange, purchase, or donation, and it recommends against acquiring an 11-mile Eagle Creek segment affected by the Phelps Dodge pumping operation. The lower 12 miles of Eagle Creek are described as high quality and are recommended for acquisition if they become available.
- **Impact summary.** The plan is rated high positive for soil and water, riparian vegetation, wildlife and fish. It is rated moderately negative for recreation-driven cultural impacts and low to moderately negative for grazing-dependent ranch operations, with mitigation proposed.
- **Consultation outcome.** The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] issued a conditional concurrence (November 6, 1997) that the plan "may affect, but is not likely to adversely affect" cactus ferruginous pygmy-owl and Arizona hedgehog cactus. The condition is updated protocol surveys before any vegetation removal.
- **Decision.** The Safford Field Manager approved the plan on signature for 15 years. The Decision Record also notes that a plan amendment is needed for any new road, and that the [[Wild and Scenic Rivers Act]] designations may add restrictions.
