# Imperial Ponds Conservation Area 2018 Annual Report

#ichthyology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #conservation_management

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: The Imperial Ponds Conservation Area (IPCA) targets two native fish species: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, family Catostomidae) and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*, family Cyprinidae). Each pond was stocked with approximately 300 adult fishes in even male-to-female ratios during FY17. Razorback suckers averaged 426–436 mm total length at stocking; bonytail averaged 271–272 mm. Age, maturity parameters, fecundity, and growth rates are not reported in this document. Spawning events were documented: razorback sucker larvae detected in Pond 1; a mass bonytail spawning event observed in Pond 2 on April 20, 2018, involving thousands to tens of thousands of fish in open water 3–4 meters from shore along a 20-meter stretch. Young-of-year bonytail were captured only from Pond 2, documenting successful recruitment.

**Habitat Associations**: The IPCA consists of six disconnected backwaters totaling 80 acres created specifically for razorback suckers and bonytail. These backwaters are managed with controlled water delivery: approximately 8.5 acre-feet per pond per month (October–June), increasing to 17 acre-feet per month July–September, totaling 771 acre-feet annually. Actual 2018 delivery was 738 acre-feet across all six ponds. The site also includes 12 acres of managed marsh for [[California Black Rail]] (*Laterallus jamaicensis coturniculus*), [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (*Rallus longirostris yumanensis*, also known as Yuma Ridgway's rail *R. obsoletus yumanensis*), and [[Western Least Bittern]] (*Ixobrychus exilis hesperis*). An additional 34 acres are designated for future cottonwood-willow (*Populus fremontii-Salix gooddingii*) riparian habitat for [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*) and [[Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]] (*Coccyzus americanus occidentalis*), but planting is on hold pending canal upgrades. Water is supplied from two groundwater wells drawing from Imperial National Wildlife Refuge's 28,000 acre-foot entitlement (or 23,000 acre-feet consumptive use, whichever is less) with priority date February 14, 1941.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Population monitoring used passive integrated transponder (PIT) tags and remote scanners (2–3 permanent scanners per pond running continuously, with weirs attached to one antenna in Ponds 4, 5, and 6). Data downloaded every 2–4 weeks tracked individual fish movements. Not reported: specific migration patterns or seasonal movement behaviors beyond spawning aggregations.

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: Not reported in detail. Monitoring captured non-target species during surveys but trophic relationships and food web dynamics are not described.

**Environmental Drivers**: The mass bonytail spawning event in Pond 2 occurred on April 20, 2018, suggesting seasonal/temperature cues for reproduction. Water quality parameters (temperature, dissolved oxygen, specific conductivity, pH) were monitored but FY18 data are not reported due to personnel changes and instrument errors. However, successful adult survival, reproduction, and recruitment suggest water quality remained within acceptable ranges throughout the year. Water management adjusts seasonally to accommodate evapotranspiration rates and wildlife habitat requirements.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: Razorback sucker population estimates (September 2018, 21 months post-release): Pond 1 = 180 fish (60% survival), Pond 3 = 198 fish (66% survival), Pond 4 = 198 fish (66% survival). Bonytail population estimates (September 2018, 18 months post-release): Pond 2 = 47 fish (16% survival), Pond 5 = 42 fish (14% survival), Pond 6 = 110 fish (37% survival). Bonytail survival was substantially lower than razorback sucker survival. Recruitment documented: 3 razorback sucker larvae captured (Pond 1 only via larval dip net), 18 young-of-year bonytail captured (Pond 2 only via minnow traps). Hoop nets captured 1,534 fish total; trammel nets captured 701 fish total across all monitoring.

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: Not explicitly reported. The site is closed to public access, reducing human disturbance. Law enforcement provided by USFWS with support from Arizona Game and Fish Department, Yuma County Sheriff's Office, and Bureau of Land Management. Wildfire management follows LCR MSCP Fire Management & Law Enforcement Strategy (2010), with full suppression strategies available that prioritize firefighter/public safety and cost-effectiveness.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: No adaptive management recommendations are made for IPCA at this time. The document notes that adaptive management relies on analysis of new information to inform future project design/direction. Post-development monitoring over multiple years will be analyzed to potentially recommend site improvements. Preliminary analyses suggest airborne lidar may not provide necessary detail for evaluating marsh habitat; alternative techniques will be explored. Vegetation monitoring using light detection and ranging (lidar) was conducted in FY18 to measure vegetation structure, identify structural diversity, and assess successional growth stages; a procedure to analyze and provide vegetation structure metrics will be developed for future reports.

**Biological Reference Points**: Target water delivery schedule: 8.5 acre-feet per pond per month (October–June), 17 acre-feet per month (July–September), totaling 771 acre-feet annually for all six ponds. Stocking density: approximately 300 adult fish per pond (150 males, 150 females). Creditable habitat acres under LCR MSCP conservation measures (cumulative through 2018): 80 acres backwater habitat for BONY2 and RASU2, 12 acres marsh habitat for CLRA1, BLRA1, and LEBI1. No new creditable acres added in FY18 because lidar analysis was not completed. The IPCA operates under a Land Use Agreement (signed 2006) between Bureau of Reclamation and USFWS securing 126 acres of program lands for the 50-year duration of the LCR MSCP.
