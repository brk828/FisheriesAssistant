# AZ: Gila Co., Sierra Ancha Mt field notes

#mammalogy #rodentia #chiroptera

## Executive Summary

These are student field notebooks from a [[Sierra Ancha]] survey in Gila County, Arizona, covering 24–30 September. The team set [[Sherman Trap]] transects across several habitat types, attempted [[Mist Netting]] for bats at [[Pocket Creek]], recorded diurnal birds, and listed plant species at each site. The notes are the main record of the small-mammal community. Trap success was low at most sites, with only about three species caught per location. No bats were captured. The notebook also contains a short commentary on bird indicator species and the ecological position of the Sierra Ancha. The document has no fish or aquatic-invertebrate data. Its aquatic content is limited to a description of the creek habitat.

## Background & Description

The notes were kept by a class working under the supervision of instructors and a field team. Named participants include Traci Main, Matt Birnbaum, Fred Esparza, Gene Elms, Dean Foster, Tim Hurst, Mark Wooden, Stephanie Coleman, Heidi Allen, Doug Van Dam and Andy Holycross. Plant identifications were attributed to Dave Brown. The field crew moved among sites along an elevational and vegetation gradient, from desert scrub at the lowest elevations to riparian woodland at [[Pocket Creek]].

Trapping was done with [[Sherman Trap]]s set about 5–10 m apart along transect lines. Baits were a bird-seed and oats mix, and oats alone at some sites. Mist nets were set across the creek in a V-shaped configuration to maximize capture in the narrow corridor (about 7 ft wide, with overhanging vegetation).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat sequence and vegetation.** The notes describe a series of distinct communities:

- **A-Cross Road (Sonoran Desert Scrub).** Plants include *Mimosa* spp., *Prosopis glandulosa*, *Acacia greggii*, *Cathestecum erectum*, *Cercidium microphyllum* and *Gutierrezia sarothrae*. Cacti and succulents are also present.
- **Grassland Bench (Semidesert Grassland).** Recorded species include *Bouteloua gracilis* (blue grama), *Sporobolus* spp., *Juniperus monosperma*, *Opuntia* spp., *Yucca torreyi* and *Echinocereus* spp.
- **Upper Chaparral.** Recorded species include *Ceanothus greggii* (buckbrush), *Quercus turbinella* (scrub oak), *Arctostaphylos pungens* (pointleaf manzanita), *Rhamnus* sp. and *Cercocarpus betuloides* (mountain mahogany).
- **Pocket Creek (Riparian).** Recorded species include *Quercus arizonica* (Arizona white oak), *Platanus* spp. (sycamore), *Acer* spp. (maple), *Fraxinus* spp. (ash), *Vitis arizonica* (canyon grape) and *Prunus emarginata* (cherry). The notes describe the creek as heavily vegetated and shaded, and noticeably cooler than the upland sites.

Across the sites, the field team listed roughly 40 plant taxa. The complete list is in the source, and the summary above gives the dominant and most diagnostic ones.

**Bird indicators.** The notes record Scrub Jay, Acorn Woodpecker and Western Bluebird. The authors use jay species as habitat indicators. *Aphelocoma* (Scrub Jay) is said to indicate chaparral, *Cyanocitta stelleri* (Steller's Jay) indicates coniferous forest, and the Mexican Jay indicates pine–oak woodland. The notes also describe the [[Sierra Ancha]] as the northernmost [[Madrean Evergreen Woodland]] in the surveyed region, and they treat it as an ecotone indicator.

**Riparian and aquatic setting.** Pocket Creek is a narrow, shaded stream with oaks, ashes, maples and sycamores along its banks. The notes describe overhanging vegetation that made net placement difficult. No aquatic fauna (fish, invertebrates or water chemistry) were recorded. The bat netting was set at dusk, from about 6:05 PM to 10:30 PM, and the team monitored the nets from the bank.

**Weather.** The notes record cool, clear evenings at about 70 °F during the Pocket Creek session.

## Key Findings

- **Small-mammal captures.** Trap success was low across the transects, with the notes stating that only about three species were caught at several sites (for example, at A-Cross Road). Species recorded in trap data include:
  - *Dipodomys merriami* (Merriam's Kangaroo Rat)
  - *Neotoma albigula* (White-throated Woodrat)
  - *Chaetodipus baileyi* (Bailey's Pocket Mouse)
  - *Chaetodipus intermedius* (Intermediate Pocket Mouse)
  - *Peromyscus maniculatus* (Deer Mouse)
- **Trap measurements.** Individual records give sex and body measurements (total length TL, left ear length EL, and left hind foot length HF, in mm). Several values are illegible in the scan or marked N/A. Legible examples include a female *Neotoma albigula* at 244 mm TL and a male *Chaetodipus baileyi* at 205 mm TL. Treat these as OCR-derived.
- **Mammals observed incidentally.** *Sciurus nayaritensis* (Apache Squirrel) was seen. A *Sylvilagus* cottontail was recorded and marked as probably a Mountain Cottontail (*S. nuttallii*), with the Eastern Cottontail (*S. floridanus*) noted as an alternative.
- **Bat survey.** Mist nets were opened at Pocket Creek at about 6:05 PM and closed at about 10:30 PM. No bats were captured. A few bats were seen flying over the nets, and the notes attribute the lack of captures partly to the cramped riparian setting.
- **Bird observations.** Scrub Jay, Acorn Woodpecker and Western Bluebird were recorded on the morning of 30 September, with notes on their use as indicators of habitat type.
- **Field protocol.** The notes document standard trapping and netting procedure, daily timing of openings and closings, bait composition and trap spacing. This makes them a usable record of the survey methods.
