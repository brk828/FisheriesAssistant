# Pacific Institute Newsletter, Winter 1997-98: Water, Climate, and Environmental Security

#aquatic-ecosystems #water-resources #environmental-policy

## Executive Summary

This Winter 1997-98 newsletter from the [[Pacific Institute]] (Oakland, California) reports on the Institute's activities across climate policy, water resources, public education, and environmental publishing. Its major features are a North-South dialogue on climate change and [[Technology Cooperation]] ahead of COP3, a study of [[Groundwater Management]] and local water-harvesting responses to scarcity in South Asia, a binational workshop on [[Colorado River Delta]] restoration, and a summary of the Institute's tenth-anniversary celebration. Shorter items cover staff speaking engagements, international appointments, and book reviews. No fish species, ichthyological data, or primary field measurements are reported. The aquatic content concerns water quantity, quality, and aquifer and river-delta ecosystems.

## Background & Description

The newsletter is a publication of an non-profit research institute founded in 1987. The Pacific Institute does multidisciplinary research and policy analysis on global environment, sustainable development, and international security. Its staff and affiliates are listed in the newsletter, with [[Peter Gleick]] as President. Newsletter editor is Patricia Brenner.

The issue contains several feature articles:

- **North-South Dialogue on Climate Change.** A consortium of NGOs, co-led by [[Irving Mintzer]], held three regional workshops in 1997 (ENDA in Senegal, COPPE/UFRJ in Rio de Janeiro, and BCAS in Dhaka). The goal was to prepare for the Third Conference of the Parties to the [[UN Framework Convention on Climate Change]] (COP3, Kyoto).
- **Local Supply and Conservation Responses to Water Scarcity in South Asia.** Written by [[Marcus Moench]], this article describes a case-study project in three Indian locations and one in Nepal.
- **Tenth Anniversary Celebration.** Held October 18 at Preservation Park, Oakland, this event highlighted the Institute's first decade.
- **New Voices, New Visions.** A book and poetry review piece covering [[Edna St. Vincent Millay]] and [[Eco-Pioneers]] by [[Steve Lerner]].
- **Binational Cooperation on the Colorado River Delta.** A workshop in Mexicali, Mexico, written by [[Jason Morrison]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Groundwater and irrigation in South Asia

Article content on India emphasizes groundwater depletion and sustainability:

- India's population is projected to exceed 1.5 billion by the middle of the next century. Future food production relies on irrigation, which has driven yield increases since the 1960s.
- Over 40% of India's potentially irrigated land, a total of 35 million hectares, is supplied by groundwater. Groundwater irrigation may account for 75-80% of the value of irrigated production. The article states much of this may be unsustainable.
- Since 1951, the number of shallow tubewells in India has grown at a continuous rate of 19% per year, doubling every 3.7 years.
- Reported impacts include falling water tables, declining quality, increasing pollution, threats to surface systems, and common waterlogging and salinization.
- In Rajasthan, water tables fell 5-10 meters across much of the central state from 1984 to 1994. Over the same period, they rose by an average of 5-10 meters in parts of the [[Gang & Bhakra Canal]] and [[Indira Gandhi Canal]] command areas.
- Water levels under Jodhpur city are declining 2 meters per year, and the city's expanding demand is drawing on sources that supply surrounding agriculture.

The article argues that historical responses, including [[Interbasin Transfers]], large surface irrigation, and centralized domestic supply, have been widely criticized on environmental, economic, and social-equity grounds. It notes that local water-management approaches are not systematically documented in South Asia.

### Colorado River delta

The Mexicali workshop, co-sponsored by the Pacific Institute, the International Sonoran Desert Alliance (ISDA), and Defenders of Wildlife, addressed the socio-ecological status of the delta region and the protection of its ecological, cultural, and economic values. The article does not report delta species counts, flows, or water-quality data. A subsequent meeting of the Colorado River Delta and Upper Gulf Restoration Project Research Advisory Committee refined the Institute's research agenda.

### Other aquatic and water-related items

- Peter Gleick presented on water-related conflicts and global water issues at several venues, including Columbia University, Yale University, and the Australian Institute for International Affairs.
- A conference in Colorado Springs addressed the implications of [[Climate Change]] for water resources.
- Gleick briefed the Pioneer Hi-Bred Company, the world's leading producer of agricultural products, including 80% of the seed corn used worldwide, on future water problems that may affect its operations.

## Key Findings

**1. Developing-country climate initiatives offer multiple benefits.** Workshops identified national-priority projects in energy, agriculture, transport, and housing that also reduce net greenhouse gas emissions. Examples included affordable efficient housing in Africa, truck fleet-maintenance programs in Latin America, demand-side management in Asian electricity systems, precision agriculture, cogeneration with alcohol production, and rural solar energy.

**2. Past technology cooperation has been mixed.** Over the previous fifteen years, many programs failed to produce self-sustaining markets. Some programs, particularly in [[Information Technology]], [[Capacity Building]], and local empowerment, accelerated market acceptance of widely applicable technologies.

**3. Access to information and partnerships are critical.** Restricted information, limited investment capital, and weak institutional capacity have limited the spread of low-emissions technologies. Expanded networking and Internet use were identified as important tools.

**4. Principal recommendation to COP3.** Parties to the UN FCCC should substantially increase support for technology cooperation, including information-technology diffusion, networking among non-Annex I institutions, and stable financing for capacity building and know-how transfer in renewable energy, energy efficiency, agricultural intensification, transport emissions, and efficient housing. The final report was to be presented at Kyoto.

**5. Local water solutions must be matched to scale.** The South Asia project aims to determine whether [[Local Water Management]] can replace larger transfer schemes. Its expected policy outcome is to clarify the level and scale at which different management needs are best addressed. The authors argue that the centralized versus decentralized debate may be misleading.

**6. Environmental health is linked to individual health.** Steve Lerner's *Eco-Pioneers* (MIT Press, 1997) presents 25 case studies from a 10,000-mile trip, including inner-city recycling, organic cotton, green architecture, sustainable ranching, and traditional Zuni agriculture. The reviewer argues that such efforts must be embedded in national frameworks, such as taxes based on pollution and national green plans like those of Holland and New Zealand.

**7. Binational coordination is ongoing for the Colorado River delta.** The Mexicali workshop furthered cooperation toward ecosystem recovery and sustainable water use, and it launched an advisory committee to refine the delta restoration research agenda.

**8. Institutional and public engagement.** Staff contributed to policy forums, media, and professional bodies. Gleick was elected to the Board of Directors of the [[International Water Resources Association]] and briefed [[Green Cross International]] on water conflicts. [[Jason Morrison]] was named one of three expert US delegates to the [[ISO 14000]] standard process (ISO/TC 207, Life Cycle Impact Assessment). Morrison also briefed the Sierra Club's Colorado River Task Force.

**9. Funding and governance.** The newsletter acknowledges 1997 grants from foundations, water agencies, and corporations, including the Bureau of Reclamation, the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California, the Santa Clara Valley Water District, and the Ford and Hewlett foundations. The tenth-anniversary celebration honored outgoing Board President Anne Ehrlich.
