# Reproductive Ecology of Humpback Chub and Roundtail Chub in the Upper Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This study examined the spawning ecology and movements of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and the more abundant [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) at [[Black Rocks]] on the [[Colorado River]] in western Colorado during 1983 and 1984. The two species are sympatric in some deep-water habitats, and the authors note that gene exchange (hybridization) may occur, raising concern that further alteration of the river ecosystem could jeopardize the genome of the humpback chub. Spawning periods overlapped temporally in both years, but the species spawned at different times. Gonadosomatic indices and mean ovum diameter were the most reliable indicators of spawning time, and radio telemetry showed that humpback chub stayed in a restricted area while roundtail chub moved long distances.

## Background & Description

The humpback chub is an endangered [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] of the [[Upper Colorado River]] system. The roundtail chub is more abundant and occurs in the same deep-water habitats. Because both species occupy the same river reaches, the document identifies possible hybridization as a conservation concern. The research was carried out by [[Bob D. Burdick]] and [[Lynn R. Kaeding]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Colorado River Fishery Project]], based in Grand Junction, Colorado.

Fish were classified as humpback or roundtail chub using [[Discriminant Analysis]] applied to nine taxonomic characters measured on fish in the field.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The study site was the Black Rocks reach, a restricted habitat of about 2 km according to the text (the OCR reads "Z-lm," which is probably "2-km"). Humpback chub spawned in deep-water areas next to metamorphic rock formations.
- **Species interactions:** Sympatry between the two species creates the possibility of [[Hybridization]], which is the main genetic concern in the abstract.
- **Threat context:** The authors state that further alteration of the river ecosystem could endanger the humpback chub genome. The document does not give specific threats beyond this.
- **Movement:** Humpback chub showed little longitudinal movement away from Black Rocks. Roundtail chub moved long distances both upstream and downstream and were located outside the Black Rocks reach during the 1984 estimated spawning period.

## Key Findings

**Spawning timing**

| Year | Species | Estimated spawning period |
|---|---|---|
| 1983 | Roundtail chub | Between 1 June and mid-July |
| 1983 | Humpback chub | Near the end of June |
| 1984 | Roundtail chub | Between mid-June and mid-July |
| 1984 | Humpback chub | Near the end of July |

- Spawning of the two species overlapped temporally in both 1983 and 1984. Spawning in 1984 occurred later than in 1983 for both species.
- **Most reliable spawning indicators:** Gonadosomatic indices and mean ovum diameter gave the clearest estimates of spawning time. The occurrence of expressible ova correlated with changes in gonadosomatic indices and was the best non-lethal method for estimating spawning time.
- **Limited indicators:** Nuptial tubercles, expressible milt, and relative body condition had limited value as definitive indicators of spawning time.

**Spawning location and movement**

- Radio-equipped humpback chub during the spawning period in both years appeared to spawn in deep-water areas adjacent to metamorphic rock formations, close to where they were first captured.
- Data from humpback chub fitted with radiotransmitters over a three-month period in both years, together with tag recaptures, indicated little longitudinal movement from the Black Rocks habitat.
- Radio-equipped roundtail chub showed long-distance movement in both directions and were outside the Black Rocks reach during the estimated 1984 spawning period.

**Conservation implications**

- Because the species spawn at different times and the humpback chub remains in a small area, the authors' data support managing the Black Rocks reach as a critical habitat for the endangered humpback chub. The main concern is preventing hybridization with the more abundant roundtail chub. The abstract does not state management recommendations directly.
