# Preliminary Report on Razorback Suckers from Etter Pond

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 preliminary report from UCLA (Buth, Haglund, and Drill) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Colorado River Fishery Project documents severe **introgressive hybridization** between the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) in a captive breeding stock population at **Etter Pond**. Using allozyme electrophoresis at four diagnostic marker loci, the authors found that **all 55 examined Etter Pond specimens** carried flannelmouth-derived alleles at the Ck-A locus, indicating the population is genomically "polluted" and unsuitable as a source of pure razorback breeding stock. The authors strongly recommend destruction of all Etter Pond specimens and their hatchery progeny to prevent further genetic contamination of the broader razorback sucker population. This case is presented as a significant cautionary tale for [[Conservation Genetics]] programs relying on wild-collected broodstock without prior genetic screening.

## Background & Description

This report is genetic/allozyme-based rather than morphometric; however, relevant taxonomic and field-identification notes include:

- **Species involved:**
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — endangered catostomid endemic to the Colorado River Basin.
  - [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) — congener/co-occurring catostomid capable of hybridizing with razorback sucker.
- Two specimens (WRB232, WRB233) were **field-identified as "white sucker x razorback hybrids"**, though genetic data treats them within the broader flannelmouth-introgression framework.
- **Diagnostic allozyme marker loci** used to distinguish parental species and detect hybridization:
  - **Ck-A** (creatine kinase) — muscle-expressed; primary marker used in this study.
  - **mIcdh-A** (mitochondrial isocitrate dehydrogenase) — muscle-expressed.
  - **mMdh-A1** (mitochondrial malate dehydrogenase) — newly identified marker locus (non-diagnostic in Buth et al. 1987 due to suboptimal buffer).
  - **Ada-A2** (adenosine deaminase) — newly identified marker locus; enzyme is labile and loses activity upon frozen storage, complicating analysis.
  - Two additional loci from Buth et al. (1987), **Gpi-A2** (brain-expressed) and **sSod-A2** (liver-expressed), could not be assessed due to reliance on non-lethal muscle/fin sampling.
- Genetic identity between the two species (Nei's 1978) was surprisingly high at **0.80**, unusual for catostomids not considered closely related (per Smith 1992).
- **Mitochondrial DNA** (maternally inherited) from the original 13 Etter Pond specimens was found by Tom Dowling to be purely of razorback origin — indicating the original hybridization cross involved **female razorback × male flannelmouth**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context:** Etter Pond functioned as an off-channel impoundment used as a **breeding stock source** for razorback sucker conservation/hatchery programs within the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Comparative populations sampled:**
  - [[Highline Lake]] (4 specimens, 1990) — no evidence of hybridization; pure razorback genotypes at all loci.
  - [[Colorado River]] proper (3 specimens, 1988) — no evidence of hybridization.
  - A 1991 specimen from a **pond adjacent to the Colorado River** (RSB-008) — homozygous for flannelmouth Ck-A allele, suggesting off-channel impoundments may facilitate interbreeding between the two species.
  - Three 1993 "Colorado River" specimens — showed flannelmouth Ck-A alleles; origin unclear (uncertain if from river proper or adjacent impoundment), raising concern about whether the main-stem Colorado River population itself is introgressed.
  - A single [[Horsethief]] offspring mortality specimen showed pure razorback alleles at all four loci, consistent with expected 1:2:1 Mendelian segregation from two heterozygous (RF × RF) parents.
- **Ecological implication:** Off-channel ponds/impoundments may create ecological conditions (e.g., altered spawning cues, habitat overlap, reduced species isolation) that are more conducive to interspecific hybridization between razorback and flannelmouth suckers than conditions in the free-flowing river, though sample sizes remain small.

## Key Findings

1. **Complete introgression at Etter Pond:** All 55 examined specimens (13 original + 42 additional) were either heterozygous (RF) or homozygous flannelmouth (FF) at the Ck-A locus — **not a single individual carried the homozygous razorback (RR) genotype**, a statistically striking pattern suggesting this genotype may now be selectively disfavored in the admixed population.
2. **Asymmetric introgression across loci:** Introgression was heavily concentrated at Ck-A, with much lower levels detected at mIcdh-A and mMdh-A1, and none detected in Etter Pond samples at Ada-A2 in the original 13, though some Ada-A2 introgression appeared in the expanded 42-specimen sample.
3. **No F1 hybrids detected:** Genotypic patterns across all four loci are inconsistent with F1 hybrid status; data support the population representing **F2 × F2** crosses (or backcross-equivalent), based on close-to-equal RF:FF ratios (27:28) at Ck-A.
4. **Maternal lineage:** Because Etter Pond specimens carry exclusively razorback mtDNA (per T. Dowling), the original hybridization cross is inferred to have been **female razorback × male flannelmouth**.
5. **Management recommendation:** The authors "strongly recommend that all Etter Pond specimens, and hatchery progeny, be destroyed" to prevent perpetuation of genomic pollution in the wider razorback sucker population — a serious [[Conservation Genetics]] concern given the species' endangered status.
6. **Timeline of the incident:** Broodstock collections at Etter Pond occurred in **late April 1993**; introgression was not discovered until **late May 1993**, after specimens had already been spawned — underscoring the importance of pre-emptive genetic screening before use of wild stock in captive breeding programs.
7. **Uncertain status of other populations:** While [[Highline Lake]] and historical Colorado River samples (1988) showed no hybridization, the genetic status of the **contemporary Colorado River mainstem population** remains uncertain due to ambiguity in collection locality for the 1993 samples, some of which may have come from an adjacent introgressed pond rather than the river proper.
