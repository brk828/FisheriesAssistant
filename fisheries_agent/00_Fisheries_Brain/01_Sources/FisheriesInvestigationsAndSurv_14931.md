# Fisheries Problems Determination - Texas Region 3-B (1962-1963)

#fisheries-management #aquatic-vegetation #reservoir-management #federal-aid

## Executive Summary

This segment completion report documents fisheries problems identified in Texas Region 3-B during February 1, 1962 - January 31, 1963 under Federal Aid Project F-3-R-10. The investigation, led by John N. Dorchester, identified excess aquatic vegetation in larger reservoirs as the primary fishery problem, largely attributed to inadequate timber clearing prior to impoundment. The report found rough fish populations were not a major concern (70% rough fish to 30% game fish ratio overall) and noted occasional industrial pollution issues. Recommendations emphasized continuing problem assessment and improving pre-impoundment planning consultation between fisheries biologists and reservoir planners.

## Background & Description

This report was prepared under the [[Federal Aid in Fisheries Restoration Act]] for the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department. The investigation covered waters throughout [[Region 3-B Texas]], including major reservoirs such as [[Lake Palestine]], [[Lake O' the Pines]], [[Lake Striker]], [[Dam B Reservoir]], [[Lake Murvaul]], and [[Caddo Lake]], as well as the [[Angelina River]].

The analysis utilized field data analysis and visual observations to determine specific fisheries problems. The investigation examined aquatic vegetation issues, rough fish populations, water access, and pollution concerns across the region's water bodies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The region's reservoirs were primarily constructed for flood control or water supply, with limited consideration for secondary recreational purposes including fishing, boating, and skiing. Most reservoirs maintained near-constant water levels, which contributed to vegetation establishment and persistence.

Timber and brush left standing in reservoir basins created conditions favorable for submerged vegetation establishment in clear, calm waters. These heavily vegetated areas proved impassable by boat and netting records showed very few fish inhabited these well-protected zones, effectively removing them from productive fishery habitat.

[[Caddo Lake]] historically experienced natural water level fluctuations from floods and drought prior to construction of Ferrell's Bridge Dam ([[Lake O' the Pines]]) upstream. Following dam installation, approximately one-third or more of the lake became choked with various species of aquatic moss due to stabilized water levels.

The [[Angelina River]] faced pollution from [[Southland Paper Mill]] effluent near Lufkin, with black waters creating aesthetic concerns and bottom deposits threatening spawning grounds in the planned [[McGee Bend Reservoir]].

## Key Findings

**Primary Problem - Noxious Aquatic Vegetation:**
- Excess vegetation in larger reservoirs represented the major fishery problem in Region 3-B
- Chemical control was economically and physically prohibitive in large water bodies
- Lack of timber clearing prior to impoundment identified as the biggest contributing factor
- Inadequate pre-impoundment consultation between Game and Fish Commission field biologists and reservoir planners
- Timber and brush left standing became inundated, creating impassable areas lost to recreation
- Constant water level maintenance on lakes including [[Lake Palestine]], [[Lake O' the Pines]], [[Lake Striker]], [[Dam B Reservoir]], and [[Lake Murvaul]] exacerbated vegetation problems

**Specific Vegetation Cases:**
- [[Dam B Reservoir]]: Water hyacinths (*Eichornia crassipes*) established in timbered areas; three years of 2,4-D spraying ineffective due to lack of access
- Winter 1962-63 drawdown request to [[Lower Neches Valley Authority]] denied due to water supply reserve concerns
- Four hard freezes occurred that winter that would have aided hyacinth control if drawdown had been approved
- Authority indicated willingness to reconsider drawdown after [[McGee Bend Reservoir]] completion upstream
- [[Caddo Lake]]: Approximately one-third or more choked with various moss species following upstream dam installation
- Commercial harvest pilot operation in final negotiation stages: moss to be harvested on shallow draft barges, dehydrated, and pelleted for cow feed supplement, chicken feed, and other supplements
- If successful, commercial harvest could change lake management concepts

**Proposed Solutions:**
- Water level manipulation recommended as advantageous control method
- Alternative approach: impound less water but clear basin before impoundment
- If total clearing not economically feasible, clear from shoreline outward rather than middle toward shore
- Leave small timber patches in strategic locations for cover

**Rough Fish Populations:**
- Not a major problem in larger Region 3-B reservoirs
- Overall rough fish to game fish ratio: approximately 70% to 30%
- No single fish species dominated
- Different situation in smaller lakes and ponds due to less predation
- Evaluation of certain rough fish species as predators in progress

**Access and Facilities:**
- Lack of access and facilities on public streams, particularly major rivers
- River fisheries utilized by only small percentage of people
- Problem discussed in previous reports

**Pollution Issues:**
- Industrial and oil field pollution occurred occasionally
- [[Southland Paper Mill]] effluent near Lufkin entering [[Angelina River]] identified as biggest current problem
- Black waters from paper mill would pollute planned [[McGee Bend Reservoir]]
- Aesthetic damage anticipated
- Bottom deposits expected to destroy spawning grounds

**Recommendations:**
- Continue Job D-1 as effective means of reviewing and identifying specific fisheries problems in Region 3-B
- Improve pre-impoundment consultation processes between fisheries biologists and reservoir planners
