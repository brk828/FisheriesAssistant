# Fish and Riparian Survey of the Clifton Ranger District, Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #stream-fish #riparian-ecology

## Executive Summary

This draft final report, prepared by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) for the USDA Forest Service in December 1988, surveys fish communities and riparian habitat on 14 grazing allotments within the [[Clifton Ranger District]] of the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]]. Twenty-four tributaries and main-stem rivers in the [[Blue River]], [[Eagle Creek]] and [[San Francisco River]] drainages were sampled, and historical collections were compiled. The study aimed to give Forest Service managers information on fish distribution, abundance and riparian condition to guide management, planning and project evaluation. The authors (Nongaile Branch, Diana Papoulias, David Valenciano and Dean Hendrickson) found that native-only fish assemblages persist in several streams, that [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] are present in the region, and that livestock grazing has heavily degraded riparian zones.

## Background & Description

The Clifton District streams and fishes were described as among the least surveyed in Arizona. Important populations of threatened and endangered fishes occur there, but their distributions and abundances were poorly known. Table 1 lists the state and federal status of species found or potentially occurring, including [[Gila Trout]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Apache Trout]].

Survey methods were as follows:
- Fieldwork in January, February and June 1988, totaling roughly 147 field-person days (text reads "approximately :A47," interpreted as 147).
- Streams were surveyed on foot from the mouth or access point upstream, divided into reaches by changes in canyon width, grade, terrace development, substrate and flow permanence.
- Perennial reaches were identified by hydrophytic trees (alder, willow) and summer flow; ephemeral reaches by upland vegetation (ponderosa pine, juniper).
- Fish were sampled by [[Backpack Electrofishing]] (Coffelt BP4, 12 V, about 200 V and 0.5 A), with catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) expressed as fish per 100 seconds of shocking time. Population estimates used depletion sampling and the [[Everhart and Youngs]] (1981) formulae.
- Riparian condition was documented at photo points using the U.S. Forest Service riparian scorecard (Appendix B), with DBH estimates for tree age classes.
- Most fish were released; voucher specimens were preserved and deposited at Arizona State University.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Riparian vegetation varied from alder, sycamore, narrowleaf cottonwood and boxelder in perennial reaches to ponderosa pine, juniper and oak on ephemeral terraces. Cottonwood and alder reproduction was often poor, and terrace understories were frequently depauperate.

Fish distribution was mainly limited by permanence of water and by barriers such as falls and dry reaches. Fish were typically found in lower reaches near the mouth or near springs (e.g., [[Hannah Springs Creek]]). Stream reaches too rugged for cattle access often retained flowing water and fish.

Livestock grazing was the dominant human impact. The authors concluded that riparian areas on the Clifton District were heavily and negatively affected by cattle, with shrub and herbaceous layers absent on many terraces, and that [[Little Blue Creek]] and [[Chitty Creek]] showed severe trampling and erosion near salt licks and round-up sites. A 1983 flood caused notable downcutting and bank loss in Yam Canyon and Little Blue Creek. [[Dark Canyon]] and [[Whitewater Creek]] were the only streams with [[Arizona Cypress]] in the riparian community.

## Key Findings

- **Native-only assemblages:** Of the three drainages, only Eagle Creek had no tributary with an exclusively native fish population. The San Francisco and Blue drainages combined contained 8 streams with no exotic species. [[Harden Cienega Creek]] was the most notable, with five native species.
- **Common species:** [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were the most widespread and abundant fishes in the tributaries.
- **Threatened and endangered taxa:** No federally listed species were taken in the tributaries. Spikedace were found at many Eagle Creek sites, and Loach Minnow occurred in the Blue and San Francisco Rivers. A 1950 Eagle Creek collection of Loach Minnow suggests the species has since been extirpated from that drainage.
- **Razorback sucker:** Recaptures of re-introduced [[Razorback Sucker]] in the Eagle and Blue Rivers were encouraging for recovery in the [[Gila River Basin]].
- **Possible Gila trout:** Trout in [[Chitty Creek]] may be [[Gila Trout]]; the authors noted the identity is unresolved, with hybrid and [[Apache Trout]] alternatives discussed. A definitive identification was recommended.
- **Chub taxonomy:** The chub in Harden Cienega Creek may be a state-threatened subspecies and warrants taxonomic study.
- **Dominant threat:** Cattle grazing has heavily trampled, eroded and denuded riparian vegetation, with [[Little Blue Creek]] and [[Chitty Creek]] among the most degraded.
- **Riparian uniqueness:** Arizona cypress communities in Dark Canyon and Whitewater Creek are distinctive and contain large trees.
- **Management need:** Historical data are sparse, so changes in the fish fauna over the past century are difficult to quantify. Continued monitoring and grazing management are implied priorities.
