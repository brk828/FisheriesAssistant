#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This study presents age and growth analyses of [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and [[Pumpkinseed]] (*Lepomis gibbosus*) from Clear Lake, Iowa, based on scale analysis of 1,215 bluegill and 336 pumpkinseed collected between 1947 and 1954. Both species exhibited faster growth rates than most Midwest populations. Male bluegill outgrew females at age groups II and III. Year-class strength fluctuated substantially, with 1951 producing exceptionally strong cohorts for both species and 1949 producing very weak cohorts. These fluctuations in abundance were closely linked to changes in the density of submerged aquatic vegetation, presumably through effects on predation pressure on young-of-year fish. Length-weight relationships showed strongly allometric (positive) growth, with bluegill weight scaling as the 3.45 power of standard length and pumpkinseed as the 3.19 power, indicating both species became relatively more robust ("plump") with increasing size.

## Background & Description

### Length Conversion Factors
**Bluegill** (n=950, range 2–10.5 in.):
- Standard Length (SL) = 0.7831 × Total Length (TL)
- Fork Length (FL) = 0.9558 × TL
- SL = 0.8203 × FL

**Pumpkinseed** (n=150, range 1.5–6.5 in.):
- SL = 0.8022 × TL
- FL = 0.9572 × TL
- SL = 0.8381 × FL

### Body-Scale Relationship
Both species displayed a sigmoid (third-degree polynomial) relationship between body length and scale radius, though departures from linearity were slight, permitting use of the [[Frazer Modification]] of the direct proportion method for back-calculating growth.

- **Bluegill:** L = 1.15 + 0.7365R (based on mean body lengths; preferred model)
- **Pumpkinseed:** L = 1.16 + 0.6577R (based on mean body lengths)

Where L = total length (inches), R = anterior scale radius (×50).

### Length-Weight Relationships
- **Bluegill:** Log W = -5.3046 + 3.4468 Log L (SL in mm, W in grams)
  - Exponent significantly > 3.0 (t = 8.141, df = 30, p<0.01) → positive allometric growth (increasing plumpness with size)
  - Average K factor: 4.52 (range 2.23–4.90 across 40–221 mm SL)
- **Pumpkinseed:** Log W = -4.7404 + 3.1856 Log L (SL in mm, W in grams)
  - Exponent significantly > 3.0 (t = 2.993, df = 133, p<0.01)
  - Average K factor: 4.32 (range 4.05–4.60 across 55–135 mm SL)

### Sexual Dimorphism in Growth (Bluegill)
Males were significantly larger than females by the 3rd–4th year in both the 1951 and 1952 year classes (F=8.73 and F=11.96 respectively, p<0.01), consistent with findings for other [[Centrarchidae|centrarchids]] (per Hubbs & Cooper 1935; Sprugel 1954), though contrary to some studies (Beckman 1949; Morgan 1951) showing no or opposite sex-based differences.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat:** Clear Lake, a mesotrophic Iowa lake, plus the adjoining shallow (~600 acre) [[Ventura Marsh]], separated from the main lake by a carp trap/dam. Marsh habitat warms earlier in spring than the main lake body.

**Habitat Comparison:** No significant growth differences were detected between bluegill from Ventura Marsh and the main lake for the 1951 year class, despite differing thermal regimes — contrasting with findings for [[Black Crappie]] (faster marsh growth; Erickson 1952) and [[Black Bullhead]] (slower marsh growth due to *Clinostomum complanatum* parasite infestation; Forney 1955).

**Diet (Bluegill, n=21 stomachs, July–Aug 1949):**
- Immature aquatic insects (12/21 stomachs): chironomid pupae/larvae, dragonfly nymphs, mayfly nymphs, caddisfly larvae, horsefly larvae, lepidopteran larvae
- Terrestrial insects (9/21): Coleoptera, Hemiptera, grasshoppers, houseflies, crickets, wasps
- Plant material (9/21): *Chara*, *Najas*, *Potamogeton*

**Vegetation-Abundance Linkage:** Fluctuations in year-class strength for both species tracked closely with submerged aquatic vegetation density (qualitatively assessed as "dense" vs "sparse"):

| Year | Vegetation Density | Bluegill Year Class | Pumpkinseed Year Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1946 | Dense | Strong | Strong |
| 1947 | Sparse | Average | ? |
| 1948 | Sparse | Average | ? |
| 1949 | Sparse | Weak | Weak |
| 1950 | Sparse | Weak | ? |
| 1951 | Dense | Strong | Strong |
| 1952 | Dense | Strong | ? |

Young-of-year fish were consistently abundant in seine hauls regardless of year class strength recorded at older ages, suggesting spawning success was not the limiting factor — rather, **post-hatching survival**, likely mediated by vegetation-related predation refuge, was the primary driver of year-class strength. This mirrors mechanisms proposed for [[Yellow Bass]] population dynamics in the same lake (Carlander et al. 1953).

## Key Findings

- **1,215 bluegill** and **336 pumpkinseed** aged via scale analysis (1947–1954); both species grew faster than typical Midwest populations (per Carlander 1953 compilation).
- Clear Lake bluegill growth was comparable to [[East Lake]] and [[Red Haw Lake]] populations and faster than [[Ike Lake]] (Marion County, Iowa).
- **Strong bluegill year classes:** 1946, 1951, 1952. **Weak year classes:** 1949, 1953.
- **Strong pumpkinseed year classes:** 1946, 1951 (and likely 1953). **Weak year class:** 1949.
- Male bluegill significantly outgrew females at ages II–III (1951, 1952 year classes).
- Both species showed **positive allometric growth** (weight increasing disproportionately faster than length cubed): bluegill exponent = 3.4468, pumpkinseed exponent = 3.1856.
- Seasonal growth analysis indicated most annual length growth is completed by end of August; age-I fish attained 85–100%+ of expected annual growth by early August in most years examined (1947, 1952, 1953).
- Apparent "over-100%" growth increments and internal inconsistencies in back-calculated lengths were attributed to **selective mortality of faster-growing individuals** (via angling or differential natural mortality) rather than measurement error.
- Poor growth was documented broadly across species in 1949 (also observed in [[Yellow Bass]] populations), though no definitive cause was established.
- The study concludes that neither bluegill nor pumpkinseed in Clear Lake are likely to require specific fisheries management, given bluegill comprised only 3.3–16% of the angler catch (1953–1955) and pumpkinseed were rarely targeted.
- Key uninvestigated variable: quantitative vegetation density data were lacking; only qualitative/descriptive characterizations were available from Unit biologists, limiting statistical rigor of the vegetation-abundance relationship.
