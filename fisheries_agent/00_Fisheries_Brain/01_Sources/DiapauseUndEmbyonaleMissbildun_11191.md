# Diapause and Embryonic Malformation in Egg-Laying Killifishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 study by [[Nicolaus Peters]] (Zoologisches Staatsinstitut und Museum, Hamburg) examines embryonic development in egg-laying killifishes (subfamily [[Rivulinae]]) from periodically drying waters. Embryos of annual killifishes in genera such as [[Nothobranchius]] and [[Aphyosemion]] pass through obligatory and facultative [[Diapause]] stages, which are timed by oxygen availability. The author reports that lethal malformations in these embryos are not randomly distributed: long-surviving malformed embryos occur almost exclusively at three diapause-associated stages (Ib, II and V). The paper proposes that these defects reflect faults in a "differentiation pattern" that block reorganisation into the next morphogenetic phase, and it argues that this challenges the classic principles of phase and action specificity in teratogenesis.

## Background & Description

- **Diapause concept:** The author defines diapause as a prolonged depression of growth in embryonic or larval tissue, whether controlled by environmental factors, endogenous conditions, or both. Vertebrates were previously known to show such arrest only in this group of fishes among those studied, with other cases being prolonged gestation in certain mustelids and cervids.
- **Life cycle:** Eggs of rivulid killifishes survive dry seasons in the substrate of dried pools, while the adults die at the onset of the dry season. Eggs are laid in peaty-muddy bottoms during the rainy season and begin development under anaerobic conditions.
- **Developmental stages:** Gastrulation is teleost-type, combining invagination, delamination and epiboly. Blastopore closure (Stage Ib) marks entry into a facultative diapause ended by oxygen exposure, typically when the pool bottom dries. Stage II (embryonic shield with heart, somites and tail bud, with intermittent heartbeat) enters an obligatory diapause lasting several weeks. Stage V (hatch-ready, larval characters present) has a final diapause ended by oxygen deprivation at the next rainy season.
- **Malformation rates:** In normal fish, lethal malformations are usually below 5% of germs. In the studied killifish broods, lethal malformations ranged from 3% to 80%, including eggs dying during cleavage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temporary versus permanent waters:** Annual species from temporary pools use diapauses that are timed by the drying of the substrate. Most Rivulinae inhabit permanent waters, lay eggs in plant tangles, and develop without diapause, yet still show blocked development at Stages Ib, II and V.
- **Oxygen dependence:** Development up to Stage Ib can proceed anaerobically, but later stages require oxygen. In the experiments, Stage Ib eggs of [[Nothobranchius guentheri]] kept submerged for 3 or more months under anaerobic conditions suffered increasing failure of development (17% after 3 months, 21% after 5 months, and none surviving beyond Stage Ib after 10 months).
- **Experimental conditions:** Standard embryo culture was carried out at normal oxygen partial pressure (about 160 mm Hg). UV irradiation of [[Rivulus cylindraceus]] embryos during cleavage raised the rate of long-surviving malformed germs from 3% to 5–6% at Stages Ib and II, though the author notes the sample was too small for statistical confidence.

## Key Findings

- **Three diapause-linked stages:** In temporary-water killifishes, development is segmented by diapauses at Stage Ib (facultative, ended by oxygen), Stage II (obligatory, lasting several weeks independent of environment) and Stage V (ended by oxygen deprivation at the next rainy season).
- **Nothobranchius guentheri:** Of 100 eggs, 76 survived to blastopore closure. Of these, 73 went on to Stage II, and one egg remained arrested at Stage Ib and died after about 6 weeks. Of 73 Stage II embryos, 64 reached hatching readiness; two showed a spirally curved tail and disrupted circulation and died after hatching.
- **Nothobranchius rachowi:** Nearly 20% of eggs could not develop beyond Stage Ib and survived up to 7 months before dying. Arrest at Stage II or V occurred in fewer than 1% of eggs.
- **Cynolebias nigripinnis (South American):** Long-lived arrested embryos occurred at Stage Ib (about 25%) and Stage V (about 30%). More than 22% arrested at Stage II, which mimicked diapause before pericardial swelling, circulatory degeneration and clouding developed over weeks. Pterolebias was reported to show similar patterns.
- **Rivulus cylindraceus (permanent waters):** About 3% of Stage Ib embryos showed growth of cellular tissue at the expense of yolk over 14 days without a germ band, surviving about 3 weeks. About 3% of Stage II eggs survived 4 weeks, with continued growth, pigmentation and sometimes pectoral fin buds, and some spiral tails. Malformations at the final stage were under 1%.
- **Aphyosemion calliurum:** Malformations at the final stage were more common, at about 6%, with the same types as in Nothobranchius guentheri.
- **Interpretation:** The author proposes that development is organised into phases of executing existing differentiation patterns and phases of reorganising them. Defects appear when reorganisation is blocked, impairing pending but not already initiated morphogenesis. Diapause, by contrast, is a reversible central switch that delays the next morphogenetic section.
- **Challenge to teratological principles:** Long-surviving malformations occur at only three fixed stages, so the phase-specificity principle of [[Stockard]] (1921) and the action-specificity principle of [[Ancel]] (1947) apply only in restricted form to these cases.
- **Sparse reporting elsewhere:** Such diapause-linked malformations have apparently not been reported in other fishes or vertebrates, possibly because a broad range of anomalies would otherwise mask them.
