# Bibliography of Scale-Based Age and Growth Studies in Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #scale-age-determination

## Executive Summary

This Inland Fisheries Administrative Report, submitted by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (Region 5, Inland Fisheries) in August 1966, is an annotated-style bibliography of **106 papers and books** on the [[Scale Method]] of determining fish age and growth rate from growth rings (annuli) laid down on scales. It was compiled as a convenient index for researchers working on [[Age And Growth]] studies. The references are dated from 1926 to 1966. The fish treated are freshwater or anadromous species, and the studies are restricted largely to the United States. Most cited works are held in the Resources Library in Sacramento or the California State Fisheries Laboratory Library at Terminal Island.

## Background & Description

The document is a finding aid rather than a research study. Its scope is defined by three features:

- **Method focus:** Every entry concerns scale-based ageing, including validation of the method, scale preparation techniques and growth calculation.
- **Taxonomic scope:** The studies cover many freshwater and anadromous families, mainly [[Percidae]] (walleye, sauger and yellow perch), [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes, crappies and black basses), [[Salmonidae]] (trouts and whitefishes), [[Coregonidae]] (ciscoes and whitefishes), and [[Moronidae]] (white and yellow bass).
- **Geographic scope:** Most studies are from Iowa, Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Ohio, Tennessee, Oklahoma and the Great Lakes. Several are from California, and a few from Canada and Alaska.

The compilers thank Lee Miller for references and encouragement, and Marvin J. Whalls for additional references. The document is affiliated with the Water Projects Branch.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography reflects a central problem in freshwater fishery management: estimating the age structure, growth rate and productivity of exploited populations, and how these change with population density, reservoir age and species introductions. Several cited works address these environmental drivers directly:

- **Population density and winterkill:** [[Beckman]] (1950) examined changes in growth rates after population reduction by winterkill.
- **Reservoir age and introduced species:** [[McCaig]] and Mullan (1960) studied growth of eight species in [[Quabbin Reservoir]] in relation to reservoir age and smelt introduction.
- **Lake and reservoir fisheries:** Many studies concern individual waterbodies, including [[Clear Lake, Iowa]], [[Lake Of The Woods]] (Minnesota), [[Lake Michigan]], [[Lake Erie]], [[Lake Huron]], [[Norris Reservoir]] (Tennessee), and [[Folsom Lake]] (California).
- **Food and growth linkages:** Studies such as [[Bonn]] (1953) on young white bass in Lake Texoma and [[Marcy]] (1954) on white crappie food and growth in Pymatuning Lake link growth to diet.
- **Temperature and growing season:** [[Jones]] (1941) analysed the growing season length of black basses in Norris Reservoir.
- **Anadromous fish:** [[Koo]] (1955) used scales to study red salmon in Bristol Bay, Alaska.

## Key Findings

The bibliography's main content falls into four thematic groups.

**1. Validation and reliability of the scale method**

- [[Van Oosten]] (1929) provided an early critique of the scale method using Lake Huron lake herring.
- [[Alvord]] (1954) tested age determination from scales of brown, rainbow and brook trout.
- [[Cable]] (1956) validated scale ageing against marked [[Lake Trout]] from Lake Michigan.
- [[Cooper]] (1951, 1953) validated and examined periodicity of growth in brook trout.
- [[Mottley]] (1942) used rainbow trout scales for direct growth comparisons.
- [[Hansen]] (1937) determined the timing of annual ring formation in white crappie scales.
- [[Ward]] and Leonard (1954) examined the order in which scales appear in black crappie.
- [[Whitney]] and Carlander (1956) developed [[Body-Scale Regression]] for computing fish body length.

**2. Scale preparation and recording techniques**

- [[Butler]] and Smith (1953) developed cellulose acetate impressions of fish scales and measured their reliability.
- [[Smith]] (1954) described plastic impressions of scales made without heat.
- [[Lewis]] and Carlander (1949) described a simple method of mounting scales.

**3. Growth-rate and age studies of individual species and waterbodies**

Growth and age studies cover a broad range of species. Representative examples include:

- **Walleye and sauger:** [[Carlander]] (1945a, 1950) on Lake of the Woods; [[Vanicek]] (1964) on sauger in Lewis and Clark Lake; [[Hassler]] (1957) on sauger in Norris Reservoir; [[Eschmeyer]] (1950) on walleye in Michigan.
- **Yellow perch:** [[Jobes]] and Hile (1941) on Saginaw Bay; [[Joeris]] (1957) on Green Bay; [[Parsons]] (1950) on Clear Lake, Iowa.
- **Bluegill and sunfishes:** [[Sprugel]] (1954) on bluegill in a new lake, noting false annuli; [[Lane]] (1954) on a Missouri impoundment; [[Puckett]] (1965) on bluegill at Cachuma and Lower Otay Reservoirs, California.
- **Black basses:** [[Viosca]] (1943) reported phenomenal growth rates of largemouth bass in Louisiana; [[Fraser]] (1955) studied the smallmouth bass fishery in South Bay, Lake Huron.
- **Whitefish and cisco:** [[Edsall]] (1960) on lake whitefish in Munising Bay, Lake Superior; [[Kennedy]] (1953) on the unexploited whitefish of Great Slave Lake; [[Deason]] and Hile (1947) on kiyi in Lake Michigan.
- **Crappies:** [[Erickson]] (1952) on black and white crappies in Clear Lake, Iowa; [[Johnson]] (1945) on crappies of Greenwood Lake, Indiana.

**4. California-relevant studies**

- [[Murphy]] (1951) described the fishery of Clear Lake, Lake County, California.
- [[Puckett]] (1965) studied bluegill age and growth in two southern California reservoirs.
- [[Tharrett]] (1966) studied centrarchid age and growth in Folsom Lake, California.

**Overall assessment**

The document is a comprehensive index rather than an analysis. Its value lies in the breadth of the 1926 to 1966 literature. Most entries are from Midwestern and Great Lakes waters, and the majority concern freshwater species, so the compilation is most useful as a starting point for scale-based age and growth work in US freshwater fisheries.
