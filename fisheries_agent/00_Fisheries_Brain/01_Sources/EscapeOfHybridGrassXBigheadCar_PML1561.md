# Escape of Hybrid Grass x Bighead Carp into Central Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This short comment on fisheries management reports the unintended escape of about 1,100 putatively triploid hybrid [[Grass Carp]] x [[Bighead Carp]] stocked in August 1982 for aquatic vegetation control in [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) canals in the [[Phoenix Metropolitan Area]]. Only 13 fish were recovered in January 1983. Inspection found a gap at a pond outflow grate through which centrarchid fishes were seen escaping. The authors judge the spread of hybrid carp to open waters uncertain but treat the event as a serious precedent for containment. The comment also corrects the published record of grass carp and [[Silver Carp]] occurrences in Arizona.

## Background & Description

The study was a cooperative project between SRP, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD), and the [[University of Arizona]] (UA). Its purpose was to determine the efficacy of hybrid carp for controlling aquatic vegetation in SRP-operated canals. The hybrids were F1 crosses of grass carp and bighead carp, spawned in 1981 by [[Malone's Chinese Fish Farms]] in Lonoke, Arkansas. This comment updates an earlier report by [[Paul C. Marsh]] and W. L. Minckley (1982).

**Fish at stocking (August 1982):**
- Total length: 193–289 mm
- Head width: 25.4–38.2 mm
- Weight: 67–206 g (N = 36 measured)
- Ploidy: all were putative triploids, presumed sterile

**Stocking sites:**
- A 1.2-km reach of Lateral 9.5 of the [[Consolidated Canal]], Mesa (N = 500)
- A 0.01-hectare pond on SRP property in Phoenix, adjacent to the [[Grand Canal]] (N = 600)

Both sites were enclosed by vertical bar grates with 25.4-mm spacing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recovery attempt (January 1983):** The canal segment was drained to a depth of a few centimeters to about 50 cm, and the pond was pumped down. Both were seined thoroughly. Twelve fish were recovered from the canal and one from the pond.

**Possible explanations:** SRP proposed theft, poisoning, or escape. It considered the first two most likely. However, the authors found a gap at the base of the pond outflow. Centrarchid fishes were seen escaping through it during the recovery attempt (B. Taubert, personal communication). Other findings:
- Erosion around the concrete support apron of the downstream canal gate.
- Bent bars that enlarged the original 25.4-mm openings to about 32 mm, potentially letting all but the largest fish pass.
- High runoff that raised canal water levels near or above the tops of the grates.

**Potential spread to open waters:** The canal system is hydrologically connected, during high water, to the [[Salt River]] through storm drains, and from there to the [[Gila River]] and the lower [[Colorado River]]. Runoff events in August and October 1982 and January through March 1983 inundated the Salt and Gila river channels from Phoenix to [[Painted Rock Reservoir]], about 150 km downstream. The Gila River below Painted Rock was largely dry until February 1983, when releases from the reservoir carried water to the Colorado River at Yuma. Upstream passage into the Salt and Verde rivers was presumably blocked by the [[Granite Reef Diversion]] and other dams.

**Ploidy verification:** Subsamples of the 1981 brood, from which the stocked fish came, included both triploid and diploid individuals. Up to 400 fish were examined independently by R. Wattendorf (Florida Game and Fish Department), A. Mitchell (USFWS, Stuttgart Laboratory, Arkansas), and D. Phillip (Illinois Natural History Survey), using cell nuclear diameter or electrophoretic techniques. Results indicated 92–98% triploid hybrids. In at least one sample, the diploid fish (about 2%) were [[Gynogenesis]]-derived grass carp and [[Androgenesis]]-derived bighead carp. Fish stocked in 1982 were considered 100% triploid because they were separated from diploids by size, owing to differential growth rates (J. Malone, personal communication).

## Key Findings

- **Escape confirmed in mechanism, not in outcome.** Of 1,100 stocked fish, 13 were recovered (12 from the canal, 1 from the pond). The fate of the remainder is unknown, and the ecological consequences are unknown.
- **Containment failure:** Grate integrity was compromised by erosion and bent bars, enlarging openings to about 32 mm. High runoff contributed.
- **Precedent:** The authors stress that the greatest concern is the precedent. Agencies were aware of liabilities and took security measures, yet the fish escaped. They call for greater care to prevent release of a fish "capable of serious environmental disturbance."
- **Triploidy is imperfect:** Brood samples showed 92–98% triploids, so some diploid individuals were present in the source population, which bears on the presumed sterility of stocked fish.
- **Hydrological pathway:** Connections via storm drains and flood events could carry fish from the canals to the Salt, Gila, and lower Colorado rivers, though upstream passage was presumably blocked.
- **Corrected Arizona records:**
  - [[Grass Carp]] were first imported from Hong Kong in 1965 by AZGFD (ASU 2149) and held at [[Page Springs Hatchery]]. That stock was destroyed soon after importation, as were the stocks held at UA and Northern Arizona University.
  - An unauthorized stocking at [[Arizona City]] before 1976 (ASU 7691) may still be represented by living individuals in an urban lake.
  - A stocking at [[Sun Lakes]] was destroyed by Arizona State University personnel under AZGFD direction in 1978 (ASU 7880). Silver carp were killed at the same locality (ASU 7879, 7881; two adult specimens).
  - Two localities mapped by Guillory and Gasaway (1978) and Guillory (1980) are considered invalid: one on the north side of the Gila River near the San Pedro River inflow, and one near the Salt River Arm of Roosevelt Lake (mapped as an aquarium record). Both were based on unverified verbal reports.
- **Valid Arizona sites** (north to south, per Guillory 1980): NAU experimental stock, Page Springs Hatchery stock, Sun Lakes, Arizona City, and UA experimental stock.
