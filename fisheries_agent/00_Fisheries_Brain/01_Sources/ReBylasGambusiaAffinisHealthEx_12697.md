# Re: Bylas Gambusia affinis health examination

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Poeciliidae #fish-parasitology #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary
This USFWS memorandum documents a parasitological health examination conducted on **[[Gambusia affinis]]** (Western Mosquitofish) populations collected from two spring sites (S-1 and S-3) at Bylas, Arizona. Because *G. affinis* belongs to the same family as the endangered **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and co-occurred with native topminnows upon invading these springs, it was utilized as a **surrogate species** to assess parasite loads and potential disease risks within the spring ecosystem. The examination revealed only light infections of a monogenetic trematode and a single protozoan parasite, with no significant pathogenic threat identified.

## Background & Description
*Note: The source document is a health/parasitology report rather than a morphological study; specific morphometric data on *G. affinis* were not provided. However, relevant physical examination sites are noted below.*

- **Examined Tissues/Organs:** Skin, gills, liver, eyes, intestinal tracts, and body cavity.
- **Parasite Location:** Both identified parasites (*[[Clejdodiscus]]* and *[[Ambiphyra]]*) were localized specifically to the **gill** tissue.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Spring-fed aquatic systems (S-1 and S-3) at Bylas, Arizona.
- **Species Interaction:** *G. affinis* is documented as an **invasive species** in these specific springs, having colonized habitats previously occupied by native **[[Gila Topminnow]]** populations.
- **Trophic Role/Diet:** Direct observation confirmed *G. affinis* in site S-1 preying upon:
  - The common pond snail, ***[[Physa virgata]]***
  - The spring snail, ***[[Tryonia gilae]]***
- **Taxonomic Surrogate Rationale:** Selected for necropsy due to close family relation (Poeciliidae) to the native Gila topminnow, making it a useful indicator species for parasite prevalence affecting the native fauna.

## Key Findings
1. **Parasite Prevalence (Site S-1):** Light infections of the monogenetic trematode *[[Clejdodiscus]]* were found on the gills of two fish (approx. 1 parasite/fish).
2. **Parasite Prevalence (Site S-3):** Light infection of *Clejdodiscus* was found on the gills of one fish; a separate gill sample from another fish yielded one protozoan parasite, *[[Ambiphyra]]*.
3. **Overall Health Status:** No other parasites were detected across the sampled population. Infection levels for both identified parasites were characterized as light and would be treatable/eliminable under controlled hatchery or captive conditions.
4. **Dietary Observation:** Mosquitofish in S-1 were actively predating on native gastropod species (*Physa virgata* and *Tryonia gilae*), suggesting a potential trophic impact on invertebrate populations within the invaded spring habitat.
5. **Management Implication:** The low parasite burden suggests that disease transmission from invasive *Gambusia* to native topminnow populations (in areas of sympatry) may be a lesser concern compared to other invasive pressures (e.g., predation or competition).
