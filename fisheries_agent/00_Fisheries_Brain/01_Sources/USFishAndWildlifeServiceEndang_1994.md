This document is an **administrative permit acknowledgment letter** from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Ecological Services, Albuquerque, NM) to Dr. James P. Collins, Chair of the Department of Zoology at Arizona State University, dated December 7, 1994. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

While the letter does not describe habitat or ecological relationships directly, all listed species are known from the scientific literature to occupy specific niches within Southwestern U.S. and northern Mexican desert aquatic ecosystems:

- **[[Colorado River Basin]]** endemics: [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Woundfin]] — large-river specialists adapted to historically turbid, swift, and thermally variable conditions, now heavily impacted by dam construction and flow regulation.
- **[[Gila River Basin]] and [[Rio Yaqui Basin]]** endemics: [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Gila Topminnow]] — small-stream and spring-associated species vulnerable to habitat desiccation, groundwater pumping, and introduced predatory/competitor species.
- **[[Virgin River]]** endemic: [[Virgin River Chub]] — adapted to a unique tributary system of the Colorado River with distinct hydrology.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]**: inhabits isolated desert springs, marshes, and shallow saline/thermal waters in the Sonoran Desert region (e.g., Salton Sea basin, Rio Sonoyta).

All nine taxa are federally listed as Endangered or Threatened under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, reflecting their shared vulnerability to habitat fragmentation, water diversion, and invasive species pressures characteristic of desert freshwater ecosystems.
