# Interbasin Transfers and Aquatic Species Dispersal Through Canals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Clupeidae #Moronidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) information request and compiled table of inter-basin water transfers worldwide. It lists engineered canals, aqueducts, diversions and waterways that connect previously separate drainages, together with the fish and other aquatic taxa recorded as having moved through them, and the literature sources for each record. It is a compilation of dispersal records rather than a primary field study. The stated purpose is to gather further information from readers, with contact details given for USFWS staff in Phoenix, Arizona. The core message is that engineered connections between basins allow the spread of [[Invasive Species]] and non-native taxa across natural biogeographic barriers.

## Background & Description

The document is organized as a table with three columns: the transfer or connection, the basins linked, and the species recorded along with the supporting reference. It spans roughly six pages, with a reference list of about 40 entries dated from 1930 to 1999.

Transfers catalogued include:

- **Great Lakes and Mississippi systems:** [[Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal]] (Chicago Diversion), [[Erie Canal]]/New York Barge Canal, [[Trent-Severn Waterway]], Champlain Canal, and [[Welland Canal]].
- **Southeastern United States:** [[Tenn-Tom Waterway]] (Tombigbee River to Tennessee River).
- **Western United States:** [[Coachella Canal]], [[All-American Canal]], [[Los Angeles Aqueduct]], [[California Aqueduct]], [[Colorado River Aqueduct]] and [[Central Arizona Project]].
- **Europe:** Ely Ouse to Essex, [[Severn-Thames Transfer]], and [[Tajo-Segura Transfer]] in Spain, plus canals linking Aral, Black, Caspian and Baltic drainages in Russia and Europe.
- **Southern Africa:** [[Orange River Project]] (Orange-Fish Tunnel).
- **Marine canals:** [[Panama Canal]] and [[Suez Canal]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document frames interbasin transfers as a pathway for biological invasion. Connections often join drainages with distinct faunas, so species can cross barriers that would otherwise isolate them. Several records describe fish from one basin appearing in another after a canal or aqueduct was built. Examples include [[Alewife]] and [[White Perch]] moving into the lower Great Lakes, [[Zebra Mussel]] spreading through European and North American systems, and [[Sea Lamprey]] entering new waters. Marine canals allow species to cross between oceans and seas. Records include Atlantic and Pacific gobies and pipefish in Panama and [[Suez Canal]] migrants between the Red Sea and Mediterranean.

Cited literature frames these movements as ecological concerns for regulated rivers and water management. Key sources include Meador (1992) on [[Interbasin Water Transfer]] ecology, Scott and Crossman (1973) on Canadian freshwater fishes, and Swift et al. (1993) on southern California freshwater fishes.

## Key Findings

1. **Scale of connections:** The table records more than 30 engineered transfers and connections across North America, Europe, Africa, Central America and the Middle East.

2. **Freshwater fish transfers:** Many records involve freshwater species moving between drainages. Examples include [[Alewife]], [[White Perch]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Shortnose Gar]], [[Sea Lamprey]] and [[Blueback Herring]]. The Great Lakes and Mississippi connections account for many entries.

3. **Invertebrate and non-fish records:** The list includes [[Zebra Mussel]] (*Dreissena polymorpha*) and, in the Rio Grande leopard frog entry, a non-fish amphibian (*Rana berlandieri*) from USFWS personal communication. The reference list also notes [[Spiny Water Flea]] and [[Rainbow Smelt]] dispersal in the Great Lakes and upper Mississippi.

4. **Suez Canal counts:** The document gives counts for species moving through the [[Suez Canal]]:
   - Red Sea to Mediterranean: algae 2 species, plants 12 species, invertebrates 72 species, fish 27 species.
   - Mediterranean to Red Sea: algae 1 species, invertebrates 44 species, fish 15 species.

5. **Marine transfers through Panama:** The [[Panama Canal]] entries describe Atlantic and Pacific taxa crossing between oceans, including an Atlantic pipefish (*Oostethus brachyurus lineatus*), a marine goby (*Lophogobius cristulatus*), [[Snook]] (*Centropomus* sp.) and [[Tarpon]] (*Megalops atlanticus*). Cited sources include Chickering (1930) and Rubinoff and Rubinoff (1970).

6. **Southern Africa and Europe:** The [[Orange River Project]] records include [[Sharptooth Catfish]] (*Clarias gariepinus*), smallmouth yellowfish and Orange River mudfish. European transfers record [[Roach]] (*Rutilus rutilus*), dace, zander, and zebra mussel.

7. **Purpose:** The document is a request for information, asking readers to send any further records to the USFWS Phoenix office (Sally Stefferud). It is an intake of dispersal evidence for interbasin transfer management, rather than a statement of results or conclusions.
