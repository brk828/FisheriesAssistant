## Executive Summary

This document is a **Station Profile** for the [[Lahontan National Fish Hatchery]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Gardnerville, Nevada, operating under USFWS Region I. Established in 1965 under the Bureau of Reclamation's Washoe Project authority, the hatchery's primary mission is the conservation and restoration of two imperiled Great Basin fish species: the threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] and the endangered [[Cui-Ui Sucker]]. The facility supports stocking efforts in [[Pyramid Lake]] and [[Walker Lake]], Nevada, and operates the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facilities]] to assist fish passage above Marble Bluff Dam near Nixon, Nevada. While not a pure biological research paper, this profile provides valuable operational and population-distribution data relevant to conservation aquaculture and endangered species management in the [[Lahontan Basin]] ecosystem.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphological descriptions (e.g., meristics, coloration, body proportions) of the [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] or [[Cui-Ui Sucker]]. However, key production-related biological benchmarks are noted:

- **Target stocking size**: Lahontan cutthroat trout are raised to approximately **six inches** in length prior to distribution.
- **Production infrastructure** reflects life-stage-specific rearing needs:
  - 30 eight-tray incubators (egg incubation stage)
  - 24 rearing tanks (early juvenile rearing)
  - 36 raceways (grow-out to stocking size)
  - 3 adult holding raceways (broodstock/spawning adults)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Endorheic (closed-basin) lake and tributary river systems in the Great Basin of Nevada, notably [[Pyramid Lake]] and [[Walker Lake]] — both terminal desert lakes lacking ocean outlets.
- **Species roles**:
  - [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] — a threatened salmonid native to the Lahontan Basin, historically the top native piscivore/predator in these lake systems.
  - [[Cui-Ui Sucker]] — an endangered, endemic catostomid found only in Pyramid Lake, with a spawning migration into the Truckee River.
- **Fish passage dependency**: The [[Marble Bluff Dam]] and its associated fish facilities are critical infrastructure enabling upstream migration of both species past an anthropogenic barrier near Nixon, Nevada — indicating the ecological importance of maintaining river-lake connectivity for spawning migrations.
- **Water supply constraint**: The hatchery explicitly notes that a **limited water supply** constrains fish production capacity, highlighting the ecological/hydrological limitations of operating in an arid Great Basin environment.

## Key Findings

- **Conservation Mandate**: The hatchery's dual mission directly addresses restoration of a threatened species (Lahontan cutthroat trout) and passage assistance for an endangered species (cui-ui sucker), reflecting broader Endangered Species Act-driven conservation aquaculture in the Great Basin.
- **Production Output**: Annual fish production target was approximately **700,000 six-inch Lahontan cutthroat trout**.
