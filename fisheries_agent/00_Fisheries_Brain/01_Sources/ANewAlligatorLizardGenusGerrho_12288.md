# A New Alligator Lizard (Gerrhonotus lugoi) from the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, Coahuila, Mexico

#herpetology #anguidae #gerrhonotus

## Executive Summary

This 1970 taxonomic paper by C. J. McCoy (Carnegie Museum, Pittsburgh) describes [[Gerrhonotus Lugoi]], a new species of alligator lizard from the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in [[Coahuila]], Mexico. The species is known from two specimens, a holotype and a paratype, both immature females. It differs from its congeners in a combination of scale and color characters, most notably smooth dorsal scales. Unlike the montane populations of [[Gerrhonotus Liocephalus]] around the basin, it inhabits talus slopes near the desert floor. The author proposes that it is a relict of ancestral Gerrhonotus-[[Elgaria]] stock isolated in the basin since the early Tertiary.

## Background & Description

The Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, an intermontane basin in central Coahuila, has a rich endemic fauna. Aquatic groups studied there, including snails, isopods, fishes and turtles, contain many endemic subspecies, species and genera, which the author takes as evidence of long isolation. Terrestrial fauna is less well known. Preliminary work on scorpions and reptiles had revealed extensive local speciation, and the new lizard is one of the endemic reptiles.

**Type series**
- **Holotype:** Carnegie Museum 49012, immature female, collected 6 July 1968 by Max A. Nickerson and John N. Rinne at the northern tip of Sierra de San Marcos, about 11 km SW of Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza, at ca. 800 m.
- **Paratype:** ASU 8818, immature female, collected December 1965 by José Lugo, Jr., at the "rastro municipal" 2.7 km SW of Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza, at ca. 740 m.

**Diagnosis:** Dorsal scales smooth; anterior internasals present; supranasals not enlarged; postrostral absent; suboculars not in contact with lower primary temporals; dorsal crossbands, lips and venter immaculate.

**Holotype measurements (mm, after preservation):** snout-vent length 79.4; axilla to groin 44.0; fore leg 16.0; hind leg 20.5; head width 11.4; tail incomplete.

**Paratype measurements (mm):** snout-vent length 74.8; axilla to groin 40.0; fore leg 17.5; hind leg 21.0; head width 10.0.

**Scalation and pattern (holotype):**
- Dorsal scales smooth, slightly convex and highly polished, in 18 longitudinal and 56 transverse rows.
- Ventrals in 14 longitudinal and 64 transverse rows.
- Supralabials 14-14; infralabials 11-11.
- Dorsum with 10 light bands between neck and tail base, each a single irregular row of whitish scales.
- Venter immaculate pearly white.

**Paratype differences:** An extra postrostral scale from an irregular division of the right anterior internasal; 13-13 supralabials; 12-12 infralabials; 53 transverse dorsal rows; 65 transverse ventral rows. Long formalin storage has faded the color pattern, and the tail is fragmented.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The holotype was found under natural conditions in a rock slide near the base of Sierra de San Marcos. It was seen at dusk in loose rocks and retreated underground when pursued. It used its tail to maneuver among rocks and partly wrapped the tail around the collector's fingers after capture. Two other similar-sized individuals were seen within 0.5 km of the type locality in 1968; both escaped into loosely piled boulders. Similar habitat occurs along the eastern and northern edges of the basin.

The paratype came from the municipal slaughterhouse on the sandy, saline flats of the town. The author suggests it may not occur naturally there and was perhaps transported with livestock.

Anguids are poorly adapted to arid regions. Desert alligator lizards are typically confined to mesic montane islands or desert oases. Other *Gerrhonotus liocephalus* populations in Coahuila were collected in Upper Sonoran and Transition zone forests at or above 1500 m. The author considers *G. lugoi* a desert-floor or lower-canyon species. Its polished scales, shortened snout and shorter toes (28 lamellae under the fourth hind toe, versus 33–37, average 34.7, in Coahuila *liocephalus*) may be adaptations to a burrowing, skink-like existence.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Gerrhonotus lugoi* is described from two immature females, and it is named for José Lugo, Jr., in recognition of his contributions to the basin's fauna.
- **Diagnostic traits:** Smooth, highly polished dorsal scales are the most striking feature and are unlike those of any other *Gerrhonotus*. Key scale characters are the presence of anterior internasals, absent postrostral, unenlarged supranasals, and suboculars not contacting lower primary temporals.
- **Closest relative:** Coloration and head scalation most closely resemble [[Gerrhonotus Liocephalus]], especially the Coahuila subspecies. The main differences are the reduced postrostral series and the smooth body scales. The author regards the smooth scales as significant, since *liocephalus* has keels already at hatching.
- **Distinction from Coahuila *infernalis*:** *G. l. infernalis* is a large, keeled, montane form with usually 12 longitudinal ventral rows and fewer than 54 transverse dorsal rows.
- **Habitat divergence:** *G. lugoi* occupies desert-floor talus, while *liocephalus* occupies montane forests, which the author argues reflects long divergent evolution.
- **Biogeographic hypothesis:** The species may be a relict of ancestral Gerrhonotus-Elgaria stock isolated in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] since the early Tertiary. Simple in situ differentiation from surrounding *liocephalus* populations is not supported. The author links the basin's refugium status to earlier work by [[Cole and Minckley]] and [[Taylor]] (1966).
- **Open questions:** Relationships will be clarified once adult specimens are collected. Needed data include maximum dorsal keeling, tail length, definitive color pattern and reproductive mode.
- **Conservation-relevant note:** The species is known with certainty only from the holotype's locality, although similar habitats occur nearby.
