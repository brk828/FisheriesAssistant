# Freshwater Fishes of Coahuila, Mexico: Zoogeographic and Ecological Checklist

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #cyprinidae #cyprinodontidae #zoogeography

## Executive Summary

This document is a conference paper presented at the VIII Congreso Nacional de Zoología (Saltillo, August 1985) by Salvador Contreras Balderas of the [[Laboratorio de Ictiología]] at the Facultad de Ciencias Biológicas, UANL, Monterrey. It presents the first published checklist of the fishes of [[Coahuila]], a largely arid Mexican state. The checklist records 80 species and subspecies: 69 native and 11 introduced. These are grouped into 15 families and 40 genera. Of the native fauna, 28 species are endemic to the state or to a particular physiographic area, 11 are endangered, 13 are vulnerable, and 5 have gone extinct in historical times through human causes. The author concludes that Coahuila is the largest center of fish extinction in Mexico.

A second, short item in the file is the introduction to a study of the estuarine ichthyofauna of Veracruz, Mexico. It gives context only, with no results.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and scope.** Before this work, no published list of Coahuila's fishes existed. Knowledge was scattered across short papers and larger works, including [[Alvarez 1970]] (freshwater fish keys), [[De Buen]] (Mexican fish list, 1940–1947), [[Lee et al. 1980]] (*Atlas of North American Freshwater Fishes*), and many regional studies by [[Hubbs]], [[Minckley]], [[Miller]], and [[Contreras Balderas]]. The stated objectives were to:

- summarize the ichthyological collection data held by the [[Colección Ictiológica UANL]];
- update scientific names;
- establish a zoogeographic and ecological account of native and introduced fishes;
- report endangered species and important fish areas;
- synthesize the bibliography and compare it with the author's own collections.

**Material and methods.** Between 1964 and 1985, more than 80 collecting trips yielded about 30,000 specimens, now in the collection. Collections were intensive and maximizing (after Contreras, 1975). Species were classified by zoogeographic origin and ecology. The full specimen list was deferred to a larger work in preparation.

**Taxonomic notes.** The paper lists many species described from Coahuila in the 19th and 20th centuries, including taxa described by [[Girard]] (1856), [[Garman]] (1881), and [[Hubbs]] & [[Miller]]. Several names have since changed. For example, *Pantosteus plebeius* replaces *Catostomus nebulifer*, and *Dionda episcopa* replaces *Hybognathus punctifer*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geography.** Coahuila covers about 151,578 km² and lies mostly in arid climates (BS and BW). Its drainage is dominated by the [[Rio Bravo]] (Rio Grande) and its tributaries, notably the [[Rio San Carlos]], [[Rio San Rodrigo]], and [[Rio de Nava]]. Most of the centre drains to the [[Rio Salado]], which joins the Rio Bravo after passing through Nuevo León and Tamaulipas. The southwest contains terminal lagoons of endorheic basins, including Guaje, Palomas, Mayrán (Río Nazas), and Viesca (Río Aguanaval). The [[Cuatro Ciénegas]] Valley is essentially endorheic and is the centre of many endemic species. Springs near Múzquiz, in the Río Sabinas division, are the only habitat of several local endemics.

**Habitat and ecological classes.** Of the 69 native species, 50 are primary freshwater fishes, 16 are secondary (salt-tolerant) species, and 3 are peripheral marine invaders in freshwater. By origin, 50 are Nearctic, 12 Neotropical, and 7 belong to other groups.

**Threats.** The paper identifies exotic species, habitat alteration, and other causes of disappearance as drivers of decline. The author cites long-term monitoring in the region and earlier work on the effects of introduced fishes (for example, [[Contreras & Escalante 1984]] on exotic fishes in Mexico).

**Estuarine context (Veracruz).** The Veracruz introduction states that Mexico's Gulf and western Caribbean coasts have about 2,611 km of lagoon shoreline. The state's main estuarine systems are [[Tamiahua]], [[Tuxpan]], [[Alvarado]], and [[Coatzacoalcos]]. The authors argue that estuaries provide the highest food availability of any ecosystem for juvenile and other marine organisms, serving as nursery, feeding, refuge, and spawning grounds. They also note that fishes in these systems have commercial value. The general fish fauna of Veracruz's estuaries had not been assessed as a whole.

## Key Findings

**Checklist summary (Coahuila):**
- 80 species and subspecies recorded: 69 native and 11 introduced.
- 15 families and 40 genera.
- 28 species are endemic to Coahuila or a specific physiographic area.
- Conservation status: 11 endangered, 13 vulnerable, 5 extinct in historical times.
- The author calls Coahuila the largest center of fish extinction in Mexico.
- A table of 14 species not yet recorded in the state but known from adjacent areas is included as a short-term prediction list. Examples are *Gambusia senilis* (Devils River, Texas), *Lepomis microlophus*, and *Campostoma anomalum* (Río Bravo).

**Notable taxa in the checklist:**
- *Prietella phreatophila* (a blind, depigmented subterranean catfish from Múzquiz springs).
- *Cyprinodon nazas*, *C. atrorus*, and *C. bifasciatus* (Cuatro Ciénegas pupfishes, all listed as endangered or vulnerable).
- *Cichlasoma minckleyi* (a polymorphic cichlid from Cuatro Ciénegas).
- *Notropis saladonis* (endemic to the Río Salado).
- *Xiphophorus gordoni* and *Gambusia marshi* (endemic poeciliids).
- *Percina macrolepida* (Río San Carlos and Río San Rodrigo).
- *Cyprinodon latifasciatus*, recorded as extinct from Coahuila.

**Introduced species (11 total):** Examples include *Cyprinus carpio* (Río Bravo, Parras), *Carassius auratus* (El Chorro, Parras), *Poecilia reticulata*, *Xiphophorus helleri*, *Sarotherodon aureus* (Río Bravo), and *Morone saxatilis* (Presa La Amistad).

**Conclusions and priorities:**
- The author argues that fish conservation in Coahuila is urgent because of endemism and high extinction rates.
- The checklist is intended as groundwork for a full, species-by-species treatment of the state's ichthyofauna.
- The paper ends with a long reference list spanning 1853 to 1985, a sign of the decades of work the checklist draws on.

**Veracruz estuarine paper:** The available text is only the introduction, which sets out the ecological importance of estuaries and their fish fauna and frames a planned regional survey. No species data, methods, or results are included in this file.
