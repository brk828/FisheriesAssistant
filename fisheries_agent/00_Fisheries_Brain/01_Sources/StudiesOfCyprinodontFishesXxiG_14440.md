# Glaridodon latidens Redescribed and Referred to Poeciliopsis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1954 taxonomic paper redescribes [[Glaridodon Latidens]], a species named by [[Garman]] in 1895 from four female type specimens labelled "Chihuahua, Mexico," and places it in the live-bearing genus [[Poeciliopsis]] (family [[Poeciliidae]]). The authors argue the type locality is probably wrong. The species is not from the [[Rio Grande]] drainage as commonly assumed, but most likely from the upper [[Rio Del Fuerte]], a Pacific-drainage river. The species is common in Sonora and Sinaloa and is recognised as *Poeciliopsis latidens*. The paper rests on fin-ray and scale counts, lateral-line pores, pigmentation, and a detailed comparison of the male [[Gonopodium]] with the type species [[Poeciliopsis Presidionis]] and with [[Poeciliopsis Fasciata]].

## Background & Description

**Nomenclatural history.** The species was described as *Glaridodon latidens* by Garman (1895) from the [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]] (MCZ No. 1307). It was later listed under *Glaridichthys latidens* by [[Meek]] (1904), who doubted the locality and wrote that the genus was not known from northern Mexico. Other authors cited it without describing it, and some doubted its identity. The MCZ catalogue assigns the specimens to F. W. Putnam, April 1861, with no collector or collection date recorded.

**Type material.** The series consists of four adult females of about 22 to 25 mm standard length. The specimens are brown, shrivelled and mostly finless. Some have lower jaws removed, probably by Garman when he examined the teeth. No males are present in the type series.

**Diagnosis.** The authors describe *P. latidens* as a small, banded or spotted *Poeciliopsis* with:
- Outer teeth few, asymmetrical, long, firm, hooked backward and semi-erect, in a broad, evenly curved arc without a median indentation.
- Inner teeth in an even arc that extends farther back in each jaw.
- About 7 to 12 narrow, dark transverse bars and spots in adult females (most often 8 or 9). Males have about 6 to 10 (typically 7 or 8).
- A preorbital that is naked, and a preopercular pore count of 5 to 7 (typically 7–7).

**Meristic and morphometric data (types).**
- Dorsal fin 7 rays; anal fin 9; pectorals 12 or 13; pelvics 5 or 6; caudal 13 or 14.
- Lateral scale series 30.
- Vertebrae 13 + 19 = 32 (including urostyle).
- Gill rakers on the first arch 13 to 14.
- Standard length: dorsal origin lies over or slightly ahead of the anal base. Body depth 4.3 to 4.7 times in standard length. Head length 3.7 to 3.9 times in standard length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution and drainage.** *Poeciliopsis* is largely confined to the Pacific drainage, from Arizona to Colombia. Its nearest Atlantic-slope record is the Rio Chachalacas basin in Veracruz. The Chihuahua drainage is partly Pacific, through the [[Rio Yaqui]], [[Rio Del Fuerte]], [[Rio Sinaloa]] and [[Rio Culiacan]]. Extensive collections in the Yaqui system contained no species comparable to *latidens*, so the authors conclude the types were not from that system. They were more likely from the upper Del Fuerte, which has not been sampled, or from another high, sparsely populated part of Chihuahua.

**Current known range.** The species occurs in Pacific-drainage streams from the Del Fuerte basin, near the boundaries of Sonora, Sinaloa and Chihuahua, south to the [[Rio Quelite]] north of [[Mazatlan]], and along the coast to the Mazatlan bay. The northernmost specimens came from a tributary of the Del Fuerte 7 miles northeast of [[Alamos]], Sonora. A footnote notes later material extended the range 35 miles farther south. The species is described as one of the common poeciliids of northwestern Mexican Pacific drainages.

**Habitat indications.** The nearly straight body axis and equally curved dorsal and ventral contours anteriorly suggest a midwater habitat.

**Sexual dimorphism in markings.** Males usually have fewer but more intense bars and spots than females. Development and spacing vary with sex and with age; immature fish often have fewer bars that extend below the mid-sides.

**Relationship to *P. fasciata*.** *Poeciliopsis fasciata* (Meek) occurs in the Pacific drainage of southern Mexico. The two species differ mainly in colour and markings:

| Character | *P. fasciata* | *P. latidens* |
|---|---|---|
| Mature male lateral markings | 2–6 (typically 3–5) narrow bars | 5–12 (usually 7–10) bars and spots |
| Adult female lateral markings | 3–6 (usually 4–5) bars | 7–14 (usually 8–12) bars and spots |
| Front of dorsal fin | Jet-black streak, often a spot at base | Dark streak, not concentrated into a spot |
| Front of anal fin, males | Black spot at gonopodium base | No black spot at base; outer rays 3 and 5 dusky |

Table data are from the source. The authors found a broad geographic gap between the species. *P. latidens* is not known south of Mazatlan, and *P. fasciata* has not been recorded north of the [[Isthmus Of Tehuantepec]]. Intermediate material from Guerrero is not yet clear, and the authors suggest the two may eventually be treated as one species.

## Key Findings

1. **Generic placement confirmed.** The four female types are referred to *Poeciliopsis* on the basis of characters of the fins, pit organs (neuromasts) and the outer dentition. Full generic certainty requires males, but the gonopodial match with local material is described as remarkably close.

2. **Locality revised.** The "Chihuahua" type locality is considered doubtful. The species is most likely from the upper [[Rio Del Fuerte]] and is not from the [[Rio Grande]] drainage.

3. **Gonopodium is the key taxonomic character.** The authors' study of 57 fully developed males from the Rio Yecorato (UMMZ 164642), a tributary of the Rio Sinaloa, provides the detailed description. Gonopodial structure is treated as taxonomically more important, above species level, than all other morphological features combined.

4. **Sinistral asymmetry.** All 57 males, plus 7 more or less transformed specimens, have a left-sided gonopodium. This matches the pattern for the genus and subfamily (other than *Xenophallus*).

5. **Consistent differences from *P. presidionis*.** Despite a strong shared basic pattern, the gonopodia differ in degree: insertion point, length, thickness, the shape of segments on rays 3, 4 and 5, the trough angle, ray tip softness, and the crescentic horn.

6. **Distinct from *P. fasciata*.** The species differ in bar and spot counts, pigmentation, and the male anal fin marking. The authors describe them as closely related but specifically distinct, while acknowledging the unresolved geographic gap.

7. **Variation is substantial.** Samples from the Rio Quelite had almost no bars, while a Rio Piaxtla sample had more numerous bars. Still, all 25 samples examined could be distinguished from *P. fasciata* by one or more of the listed characters.

8. **Museum resources.** About 25 collections are held at the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]], with additional material examined at the University of California, Los Angeles.
