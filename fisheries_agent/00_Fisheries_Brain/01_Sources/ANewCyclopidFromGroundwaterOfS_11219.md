# A New Cyclopid from Groundwater of South Italy: *Diacyclops bicuspidatus lucanus* n.ssp. (Crustacea: Copepoda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyclopidae

*(Note: the source concerns a groundwater copepod, not fish. The tag line follows the required format, but the taxon is non-fish, so [[Copepoda]] and [[Groundwater Fauna]] are the relevant concepts.)*

## Executive Summary

This 1985 taxonomic paper by Giuseppe L. Pesce and Diana P. Galassi, published in the *Bulletin Zoologisch Museum* (Universiteit van Amsterdam), describes a new subspecies of cyclopoid copepod, *Diacyclops bicuspidatus lucanus* n.ssp., from phreatic freshwater wells in Lucania (Basilicata), South Italy. The new taxon belongs to the [[Diacyclops Bicuspidatus Complex]] and is most closely related to *D. bicuspidatus odessanus*. It is distinguished chiefly by its remarkably elongated caudal rami and an unusual length ratio between the innermost and outermost furcal setae. The holotype and paratypes were collected from multiple wells in May–July 1983.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic context:** The paper belongs to a series on underground water fauna of central and southern Italy (contribution XXIX), focusing on [[Groundwater Fauna]] of Lucania.
- **Type locality:** Freshwater well at Sasso di Castalda (Potenza), station LU. 12, sampled 28 May 1983 by L. D'Addario. Water surface at about 3.5 m, bottom at 7.5 m, temperature 14.9 °C, pH 6.9.
- **Material examined:** Holotype (female, completely dissected and slide-mounted in Faure's medium) plus paratypes from about seven wells at Pergola, Noliterno, Tito, Balvano, Filiano, and S. Giorgio Pietragalia, among others. Female counts per site are 2 to 7; males are rare (1 to 3 individuals).
- **Female morphology:**
  - Total length 0.90–1.85 mm (mean 1.37 mm, n = 23).
  - Antennula 14-segmented; antenna 4-segmented.
  - Leg 4 endopod elongated (L/I = 2.9–3.2) with spine formula 2333.
  - Leg 5 distal segment elongated (L/I = 2.5–2.7) with a strong spine and one long plumose seta.
  - Leg 6 a chitinous lamella with two stout short spines and one spiniform seta.
  - Caudal rami parallel and very elongate (L/I = 8.9–10.1), longer than the last three abdominal segments combined.
- **Male morphology:** Body length 0.9–1.1 mm (mean 1.0 mm, n = 3). Leg 6 with a well-developed inner spine and two setae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Phreatic (groundwater) freshwater network, sampled from wells at depths to water surface of 2.2–5.8 m and to bottom of 5.5–10.0 m.
- **Water conditions:** Temperatures 13.9–15.0 °C; pH 6.9–7.1, consistent across all stations.
- **Associated fauna:** Harpacticoid copepods, ostracods, [[Amphipoda]], asellid isopods, water mites ([[Hydrachnidia]]), oligochaetes, cyclopids, and Diptera larvae.
- **Biogeography:** The complex is largely cosmopolitan. The new subspecies is a regional endemic known from Lucanian groundwater.
- **Repositories:** Holotype and 3 paratypes are in the Zoölogisch Museum Amsterdam (ZMA); other paratypes are with the first author at the Istituto di Scienze Ambientali, L'Aquila (GPC).

## Key Findings

1. **New subspecies described:** *Diacyclops bicuspidatus lucanus* n.ssp., known from multiple phreatic wells in Lucania.
2. **Closest relative:** *D. bicuspidatus odessanus* (Schmankevitch, 1875), separated mainly by female antennula structure and armature.
3. **Diagnostic traits:** Remarkably elongated caudal rami (L/I 8.9–10.1) and an unusual ratio between innermost and outermost furcal setae (inner distal seta about twice the outer).
4. **Differentiation from other members of the complex:**
   - Versus [[Diacyclops Bicuspidatus Navus]] (North America): 14- versus 17-segmented female antennula; longer caudal rami.
   - Versus *D. bicuspidatus thomasi* (North America): shorter distal segment and shorter spines on the P4 endopod.
   - Versus *D. bicuspidatus limnobius* (Lake Constance): longer caudal rami and a different furcal seta ratio.
   - Versus the typeform: differs in female antennula segment count and caudal ramus length.
5. **Sampling scope:** Seven or more wells sampled in May–July 1983, with consistent associated invertebrate communities.
6. **Status:** Published 1985 (received 14 June 1984, distributed 29 March 1985). The authors reference a forthcoming work on cyclopoid and harpacticoid copepods of Basilicata groundwater.
