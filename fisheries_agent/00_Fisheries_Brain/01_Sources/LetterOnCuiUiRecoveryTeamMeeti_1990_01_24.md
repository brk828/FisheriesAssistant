## Executive Summary

This document is a formal letter from the **Cui-ui Recovery Team Leader** (Thomas A. Strekal) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director, summarizing outcomes of the third Cui-ui Recovery Team meeting (January 12, 1990). The central scientific finding reported is that an **electrophoretic survey** conducted by Dr. Peter Brussard (University of Nevada-Reno), independently confirmed by Dr. Minckley, indicates that the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) has **not hybridized** with the sympatric [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*), contrary to previous assumptions. This genetic finding directly reversed prior management decisions, including the recommendation to establish an oversight task force for the **Marble Bluff Fish Facility** during the 1990 spawning run, and prompted a request to extend the draft recovery plan deadline.

## Background & Description

- Previous observations had identified **morphological differences** across several **cui-ui year classes**, which were originally hypothesized to result from **genetic hybridization** with the [[Tahoe Sucker]].
- New genetic (electrophoretic) evidence reclassifies these morphological variations as products of **ontogenetic (developmental/environmental) factors** rather than introgressive hybridization.
- The team notes that a **complete morphological description** of *Chasmistes cujus* year classes is still pending, requiring **additional targeted research** to disentangle environmental plasticity from taxonomic/genetic variation.
- No specific meristic, morphometric, or coloration data are provided in this letter; the source is administrative/correspondence in nature rather than a primary morphological dataset.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species**: [[Cui-ui]] — a large, long-lived lake sucker endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, and closely associated with the terminal lake ecosystem of the [[Truckee River]] watershed (Lahontan Basin).
- **Sympatric species**: [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*), a congener historically suspected of interbreeding with cui-ui due to overlapping spawning habitat and morphological ambiguity in certain cohorts.
- **Spawning migration**: Cui-ui undertake an annual **spawning run**, historically up the [[Truckee River]], with population and passage management occurring at the **[[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]**.
- **Water management linkage**: Population recruitment and spawning success are tied to reservoir operations, specifically releases from **[[Stampede Reservoir]]**, coordinated between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Reno office), the Bureau of Reclamation (Carson City office), and the **[[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]]**.
- The species' conservation status as **federally endangered** places its habitat management under formal recovery planning requirements, coordinated by the **Cui-ui Recovery Team**.

## Key Findings

1. **No Hybridization Confirmed**: Electrophoretic analysis by Dr. Peter Brussard, corroborated independently by Dr. Minckley, found **no evidence of hybridization** between [[Cui-ui]] and [[Tahoe Sucker]] — refuting a long-standing conservation concern.
2. **Morphological Variation Reattributed**: Distinct morphological differences among cui-ui year classes are now attributed to **ontogenetic/environmental causes**, not introgressive hybridization, though further research is needed for a full morphological description.
3. **Management Reversal**: Because hybridization was ruled out, the previously recommended **task force to oversee the 1990 spawning run** at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] was deemed unnecessary; standard annual management (per facility manager Duane Wainwright's plan) will proceed instead.
4. **Interdiction Recommendations Rescinded**: Recommendations from the November 7, 1989 management meeting (including **interdiction** measures during the spawning run) were formally disregarded in light of the new genetic data.
5. **Recovery Plan Timeline Extended**: The team reiterated its request to extend the draft recovery plan deadline to **January 31, 1991**, citing delays caused by the hybridization investigation.
6. **Institutional Coordination Noted**: The letter highlights procedural concerns regarding delayed correspondence from the FWS Regional Office, requesting improved response times (within two weeks) to support recovery team efficiency.
