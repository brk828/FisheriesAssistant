# Razorback Sucker Growth Bimodality Breeding Protocol and Hatchery Egg Data (1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989 letter from [[Minckley]], Professor of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]], to Buddy L. Jensen at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], New Mexico. It proposes a breeding protocol to test whether bimodal growth in [[Razorback Sucker]] cohorts has a genetic basis. The letter is accompanied by scanned hatchery field sheets (length-weight records and egg-count worksheets) and two summary tables for spawning females and egg incubation from March 1, 1989. The OCR-degraded sheets are only partly legible, so the numbers below come mainly from the tables and the letter text.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large catostomid fish of the Colorado River system, managed in the Arizona reintroduction program.
- **Origin of problem:** Size distributions of a single cohort showed bimodality. The fish were isolated at Dexter in 1985 (1984 year-class), and the author notes that some nose tags may have been lost, so the groups may now form an almost or fully unimodal distribution.
- **Key partners:** [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] (Wayne Shifflett), the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Dean Hendrickson), and Paul Marsh of the ASU Cooperative Ecosystem Studies unit (CES). Marsh had already cleared the project with the refuge and selected fishless ponds for rearing.
- **Letter details:** Dated 18 January 1989, received at Dexter 24 January 1989.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Rearing environment:** Progeny were to be reared in fishless ponds at the refuge, backed up by [[Laboratory Aquaria]] at ASU under constant, as far as possible identical, tank conditions.
- **Size categories:** Large-group fish were identified by nose tags (originally large). Small-group fish were non-tagged (originally small). Selection of small-group fish was left to the hatchery's judgment because tags may have been lost.
- **Biosecurity and management:** If a strong genetic component to growth were found (which the author did not expect), the progeny would be destroyed rather than released, to prevent artificially selected traits from entering wild populations. Some fish might be kept for other purposes.
- **Timeline:** Results were expected by mid-summer 1989. Fish would then be stocked with the general Arizona reintroduction program.

## Key Findings

**1. Proposed crossing design (four cross types, two replicates each)**
- a. Large-group male × large female (within-group; expected to give large young)
- b. Small-group female × small male (within-group; expected to give small young)
- c. Large-group male × small female (between-group)
- d. Small-group male × large female (between-group)

Cross types c and d were expected to give a bimodal size distribution at about 50–100 mm total length or larger. The author proposed three replicates but limited the study to two because of time, labor, and tank space. Each cross was to yield roughly 5,000–10,000 progeny to ensure enough survivors for pond recovery.

**2. Hypotheses**
- Within-group crosses (a, b) produce uniform sizes matching their parents' groups.
- Between-group crosses (c, d) produce bimodal sizes.
- Maternal effects may produce a statistical tendency toward the female parent's size in crosses c and d. The direction of this effect could be positive or negative.

**3. Spawning female summary (Table 1, March 1, 1989, Lot 1, TDW)**
- Fourteen spawning females were recorded.
- Weight: mean 5.005 lb (range 3.563–6.500 lb).
- Fecundity: mean 99,700 eggs (range 18,495–145,188).
- Eggs per lb body weight: mean 20,010 (range 4,227–28,898).

**5. Field and hatchery records**
- Scanned [[Length-Weight Data]] sheets record individual fish length (mm) and weight (g) across several dated sheets. Sheets cover Razorback Sucker groups, tagged and untagged fish, and separate "Study" and "Wiwek's Exp." sets. OCR quality prevents reliable transcription, so individual values are not reproduced here.
- Egg-count worksheets list sample weights, sample egg counts, tray numbers, and total egg numbers from multiple female lots, and show calculated eggs per gram. These are the source data for the Table 1 and Table 2 summaries.

**6. Conclusions and plans**
- The author expected no strong genetic basis for growth. If one were found, the progeny would be destroyed rather than released.
- The protocol was designed to be simple to run alongside the hatchery's spring production schedule.
- The Dexter team was asked to report spawning dates so that the timing of the project could be tracked.
