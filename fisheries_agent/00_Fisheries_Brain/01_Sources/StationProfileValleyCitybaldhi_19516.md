# Valley City/Baldhill Dam National Fish Hatchery 1993 Station Profile

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-culture

## Executive Summary

This 1993 station profile from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) Fisheries program describes the [[Valley City National Fish Hatchery]] in Valley City, North Dakota, and its substation at [[Baldhill Dam]]. The hatchery's primary mission is to produce cool- and warm-water fish for stocking National Wildlife Refuges and Tribal lands, to mitigate federal water development projects on the [[Missouri River]], and to support state-cooperative stocking. The station also develops new fish culture techniques. The profile reports egg and fry production, facility inventory, budget and staffing trends from 1991 to 1995, and fish distribution across five states for fiscal years 1991 to 1993.

## Background & Description

- **Established:** 1938.
- **Region:** FWS Region 6.
- **Location:** 11515 River Road, Valley City, ND 58071-9619.
- **Congressional district:** 24. Listed senators are Byron Dorgan and Kent Conrad; the representative is Earl Pomeroy.
- **Authority:** Cited as the "White Bill," Public Law 2-40, 1930, 71st Congress (as printed in the document).
- **Project leader:** Mathew Bernard.

**Station mission:** The hatchery produces cool- and warm-water species for stocking refuge and Tribal waters, provides fish to mitigate federal water projects, and develops fish culture methods. It has an intensive culture building and, working with the Bozeman Technology Center, conducts dry diet research on cool-water and [[Endangered Fish Species]].

**Physical facilities:** 14 ponds, 3 quarters, 1 office, 1 hatchery and laboratory building, 1 generator building, 1 sewage treatment plant, 1 holding house, 1 garage, 1 shop, 5 storage buildings, 1 grain bin, 1 pumphouse, and 1 oil and paint house.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery focuses on cool- and warm-water species, which supports stocking of waters in the [[Missouri River]] basin and other North Dakota systems. The document records fish distribution to five states (Illinois, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota) and nine species, but gives no habitat measurements, water chemistry, or population data. Ecological outcomes of stocking are therefore not assessed in this profile.

## Key Findings

**1. Budget and staffing (in $000 and FTE):**

| Cost recoverables FTE | 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.1 |

Operations funding fell from $285.9K (1991) to $234.5K (1993) and is estimated at $235.0K for 1994 and 1995. Maintenance spending rose sharply in 1993 to $48.9K, with no maintenance funding estimated for 1994 or 1995. Staffing dropped from 5.7 FTE to 4.0 FTE in the estimates.

**2. Production:** In the reporting year, the station produced 19,847,504 northern pike eggs and 25,390,000 walleye eggs, plus 5,059,050 northern pike fry and 2,960,250 walleye fry.

**3. Fish distribution (number of fish, excluding eggs and fry):**

| FY | FWS waters | Other waters | State waters | Private waters | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1993 | 1,014,450 | 3,064,540 | 1,209,183 | 273,636 | 5,561,809 |
| 1992 | 992,312 | 2,697,328 | 1,480,109 | 1,980 | 5,171,729 |
| 1991 | 765,571 | 3,593,175 | 959,817 | not reported | 5,318,563 |

Total distribution was 5,561,809 fish in FY1993, up from 5,171,729 in FY1992. In FY1993, the largest share (about 3.06 million) went to other waters, and the private-waters share rose from 1,980 in FY1992 to 273,636.

**4. Species distributed in FY1993:** [[Walleye]], [[Bluegill]], [[Tiger Muskie]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Black Crappie]].
