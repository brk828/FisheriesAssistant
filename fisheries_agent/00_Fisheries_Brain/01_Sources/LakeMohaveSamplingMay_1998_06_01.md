# Lake Mohave Sampling, May 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This report summarizes a four-day fisheries monitoring effort conducted by Arizona State University (ASU) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) on [[Lake Mohave]], located on the Arizona-Nevada border, from May 18-21, 1998. The primary objective was to monitor populations of two federally endangered, native Colorado River fishes: the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Sampling was conducted using 91m trammel nets set at depths of 1-10m in the Cottonwood East area, extending uplake to Carp Cove. A total of seven fish species were captured, dominated by non-native sportfish and baitfish, with only a small number of the target endangered species recovered. Concurrent sampling by partner agencies (USBR, NPS, AGFD, USGS-BRD) in nearby coves yielded additional bonytail chub and razorback sucker captures, several of which were identifiable repatriates via PIT tags.

## Background & Description
Specific morphometric data was limited to total length (TL) measurements for recaptured endangered repatriates:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]] (ASU/USFWS camp, Carp Cove):**
  - Individual 1: 538 mm TL
  - Individual 2: 404 mm TL
- **[[Bonytail Chub]] (ASU/USFWS camp, Waterwheel Cove):**
  - Single untagged male: ~480 mm TL
- **[[Razorback Sucker]] (USBR/NPS camp, Bill Gayes Camp/Half-way Wash):**
  - PIT Tag TF7D7C6618: recaptured 5/98
- **[[Bonytail Chub]] (USBR/NPS camp, Half-way Wash):**
  - PIT Tag 1F0F484C3E: 590 mm TL (recaptured 5/98)

No morphological data beyond length was recorded in this survey; species identification relied on standard external characteristics for *Gila elegans* (slender, streamlined body, small head) versus *Xyrauchen texanus* (pronounced dorsal keel/hump).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
[[Lake Mohave]] represents a reservoir habitat on the [[Colorado River]] system, providing critical refugia for native endangered fish amid a fish community dominated by introduced species. Sampling effort focused on the littoral zone (1-10m depth) along shorelines, points, and embayments — habitat types thought to support recruitment and foraging for both target species.

- **Community Composition:** The catch was numerically dominated by non-native species — [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*), and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) — reflecting the heavily altered, non-native-dominated fish assemblage typical of lower Colorado River reservoirs.
- **Native Species Rarity:** Both [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] were captured in very low numbers relative to total effort, consistent with their status as critically endangered, relict populations sustained primarily through active repatriation/stocking programs rather than natural recruitment.
- **Repatriation Program Linkages:** Recaptured individuals traced back to specific stocking sites and dates (e.g., N. 9 Mile Cove in 1995, Yuma Cove, Carp Cove) via PIT tag records, indicating an active multi-agency reintroduction effort feeding the extant population in the lake.
- **Spatial Distribution:** Target species were recovered across multiple discrete coves (Waterwheel Cove, Half-way Wash, Arrowhead Cove, Owl Pt. Cove), suggesting a wide but sparse distribution of repatriated fish throughout the reservoir.

## Key Findings
- **Total Effort:** 25.05 net-units were fished (1 unit = 100 m² of netting fished for 24 hours) using four to six 91m trammel nets.
- **Species Richness:** Seven fish species were documented in the Cottonwood East/Carp Cove trammel net catch.
- **Endangered Species Capture (ASU/USFWS camp):**
  - 2 [[Razorback Sucker]] captured near Carp Cove — both confirmed repatriates (stocked 1994 and 1997).
  - 1 [[Bonytail Chub]] captured at Waterwheel Cove — untagged male (~480mm TL).
- **Multi-Agency Captures:** An additional 3 bonytail chub were captured by partner agencies during the same sampling week:
  - 1 tagged bonytail recovered near Half-way Wash by USBR/NPS (Bill Gayes camp), originally stocked at N. 9 Mile Cove in 1995.
  - 2 untagged bonytail captured by AGFD/USGS-BRD/USFWS crews near Arrowhead Cove.
- **PIT Tag Verification:** PIT tag histories (e.g., tag 201231415, TF7D7C6618, 2241187B63, 1F0F484C3E) confirmed multiple recaptures link directly to known hatchery-reared and released cohorts, underscoring reliance on the [[Repatriation Program]] for population maintenance in [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Conservation Implication:** The very low capture rate of native endangered species relative to non-native catch highlights ongoing recruitment challenges for [[Gila elegans]] and [[Xyrauchen texanus]] in this reservoir system, reinforcing the necessity of continued stocking and monitoring efforts.
