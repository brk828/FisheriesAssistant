# The Distribution of *Sarotherodon mossambicus* in Estuarine Systems

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Alan K. Whitfield]] and [[Stephen J.M. Blaber]] (1979) reviews the distribution of the euryhaline freshwater cichlid **[[Sarotherodon mossambicus]]** (Mozambique tilapia) across south-east African estuarine systems. The species is common in **closed estuaries** and **coastal lakes** but conspicuously **absent from open tidal estuaries**. When a closed estuary breaches and opens to the sea, *S. mossambicus* retreats to the upper, less saline reaches. Using field distribution surveys (1970–1978) across eleven estuarine systems and a laboratory choice-chamber experiment, the authors demonstrate that the fish actively avoids water currents exceeding **370 m·hr⁻¹**. The paper concludes that distribution patterns are governed by a multifactorial interplay of **salinity stability, water current speed, availability of suitable breeding substrate, marginal/aquatic vegetation cover, presence of marine competitors, and marine piscivorous fish** — rather than any single limiting factor.

## Background & Description

- **Standard Length (experimental subjects):** 5–10 cm SL.
- **Ontogenetic refuge size (Lake Kariba comparison):** Juveniles remain in vegetated littoral zones until reaching ~18 cm, after which they exceed predation size range of the tigerfish *[[Hydrocynus vittatus]]*.
- No detailed external morphological description is given in this ecological paper; focus is on physiological tolerance and behavioral ecology rather than anatomy.
- **Physiological tolerances:**
  - Salinity: 0–117‰ (extremely euryhaline; [[Wallace 1975]])
  - Temperature: 12°C–38°C (eurythermal; [[Allanson et al. 1971]])

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat affinity:** Strong preference for calm, still-water habitats — quiet pools, sheltered littoral zones, and vegetated margins in both perennial/annual rivers ([[Gaigher 1973]]; [[Pienaar 1968]]) and estuarine lakes.
- **Current avoidance threshold:** Choice-chamber experiments (n=5 trials, 20–21°C) show fish increasingly avoid chambers with current ≥370 m·hr⁻¹; at 517 m·hr⁻¹ only ~37% of fish still entered the current chamber, indicating avoidance is strong but not absolute.
- **Field current data:** Present in [[Kosi Reed Channel]] margins (current reduced to 110 m·hr⁻¹ by *Phragmites* beds) despite channel center speeds of 1970–2200 m·hr⁻¹; absent from Kosi Estuary mouth and Umgeni Mouth where currents reach 810–1300 and 1270 m·hr⁻¹ respectively.
- **Breeding requirements:** Builds nests in calm water, 1–2 m depth, over sandy substrate ([[Jubb 1967]]); nests predominantly in sheltered littoral areas with macrophyte cover ([[Bruton & Boltt 1975]]). Open estuaries lack stable substrate and marginal vegetation due to tidal exposure and current scour.
- **Trophic ecology:** Highly generalist diet; large dietary component of particulate organic matter and microbenthos ([[Whitfield & Blaber 1978a]]). Diet overlaps with *[[Mugil cephalus]]*, *[[Chanos chanos]]*, and Mugilidae at Lake St. Lucia, but competitive overlap is reduced via differing feeding mechanisms/microbenthos partitioning.
- **Predator interactions:**
  - At [[Lake Kariba]], presence of *Hydrocynus vittatus* alters shoaling/habitat-use behavior of juveniles (littoral refuge use), unlike unpredated Rhodesian highveld dam populations ([[Donnelly 1969]]).
  - At [[Lake St. Lucia]], piscivorous fish do NOT significantly predate *S. mossambicus* ([[Whitfield & Blaber 1978b]]), but piscivorous birds exert high predation pressure ([[Whitfield & Blaber 1978c]], [[1979a]], [[1979b]]) — yet this does not drive emigration from the lake.
- **Estuary type responses:**
  - Closed estuaries: colonizes fully, including lower reaches, following prolonged closure (e.g., 95% of total catch in lower [[West Kleinemond Estuary]] after 18 months closure).
  - Open estuaries: restricted to upper freshwater reaches only, or entirely absent.
  - Exception: [[Mhlanga Estuary]] — recorded in lower reaches even during open phase, attributed to shallow upper reaches (<1 m) and reduced overall water surface area forcing redistribution.

## Key Findings

1. **Distribution pattern (Table 1):** Across 16 system records spanning Mozambique to the Cape, *S. mossambicus* is "common" or "present" in all sampled closed estuaries and coastal lakes (e.g., [[Lake Poelela]], [[Lake Nhlange]], [[Mgobozeleni]], [[St. Lucia]], [[Sinkwazi]], [[Mpenjati]], [[Kleinemond]]) but consistently "absent" from open estuaries (e.g., [[Kosi Estuary]], [[Umlalazi]], [[Umgeni]], [[Mtentu]], [[Msikaba]]).
2. **Current avoidance experimentally confirmed:** Fish preferentially occupy calm chambers once current exceeds 370 m·hr⁻¹, though avoidance is incomplete even at highest tested speed (517 m·hr⁻¹).
3. **Current speed alone is insufficient to explain distribution:** Umgeni Lagoon and Lake St. Lucia have comparable current speeds (220 vs. 20–210 m·hr⁻¹), yet *S. mossambicus* is absent from the former and present in the latter — implicating additional factors.
4. **Salinity regime shifts correlate with abundance changes:** At Lake St. Lucia, relative abundance rose from 1.2% of total catch (1971, salinity 80‰) to 12.3% (1975, salinity 10‰), coinciding with increased macrophyte colonization and marginal vegetation inundation ([[van der Elst et al. 1976]]; [[Ward 1976]]).
5. **Behavioral response to estuary mouth state:** Closure of an estuary produces gradual (not abrupt) salinity change via evaporation/freshwater inflow balance, permitting colonization of the lower reaches; opening produces rapid salinity fluctuation, driving retreat upstream (documented longitudinally at West Kleinemond, 1970–1973).
6. **Six-factor assessment model (Table 4):** Across 11 estuarine systems, abundance category correlates with number of favorable factors — "common" when ≥5 of 6 factors favorable (salinity stability, slow currents, suitable breeding sites, marginal vegetation, absence of marine competitors, absence of marine piscivorous fish); "present" with 4 favorable; "absent" with <4.
7. **Marine competition is not the primary limiting factor:** Despite dietary overlap with mullet and milkfish, resource partitioning via differing feeding mechanisms reduces competitive exclusion, and competitor presence does not fully explain absence from open systems.
8. **Marine piscivore predation is not the primary limiting factor at St. Lucia:** Despite high piscivorous fish abundance, they do not significantly prey upon *S. mossambicus*; avian predation is high but does not induce emigration.
9. **Colonization via marine route confirmed:** *S. mossambicus* is salt-tolerant enough to have colonized south-east African coastal estuaries via the sea itself ([[Harrison 1966]]), underscoring that avoidance of open estuaries is behavioral/ecological rather than purely physiological.
