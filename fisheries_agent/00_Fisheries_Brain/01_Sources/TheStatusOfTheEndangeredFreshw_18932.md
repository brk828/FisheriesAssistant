# The Status of Endangered Freshwater Fishes in China and Analysis of Endangered Causes

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #conservation-biology #endangered-species #China

## Executive Summary

This report documents the endangered status of freshwater fishes in [[China]], identifying 92 species (10% of China's ~800 freshwater fish species) as endangered. The analysis covers multiple taxonomic groups including [[Cyprinidae]] (52 species), catfishes (11 species), [[Acipenseridae|sturgeons]] (5 species), [[Salmonidae|salmons]] (6 species), and others (18 species). Endangered species are classified into four grades: extinct (4 species), rare (23 species), endangered (28 species), and gradually endangered (37 species). The report identifies six primary causes of endangerment: habitat modification through dam construction, land reclamation, natural catastrophes, reproductive habitat damage, excessive exploitation, water pollution, and unchecked species introductions. China's freshwater fish fauna includes numerous endemic species, monotypic genera, and species of significant phylogenetic and biogeographic importance, making their conservation critical for maintaining biodiversity.

## Background & Description

China's inland water systems support more than 800 species of freshwater fishes, representing precious biological resources. This fauna includes a great number of endemic and precious groups, with many monotypic genera and species that serve as special research material for phylogenetics and biogeography studies.

The endangered species list encompasses diverse taxonomic groups:

**[[Petromyzontidae|Lampreys]]**: [[Lampetra reissneri]], [[Lampetra japonica]]

**[[Acipenseridae|Sturgeons]]**: [[Acipenser schrencki]], [[Acipenser dabryanus]], [[Acipenser sinensis]] (Chinese sturgeon), [[Huso dauricus]], [[Psephurus gladius]] (Chinese paddlefish), [[Macrura reevesi]]

**[[Salmonidae|Salmons]]**: [[Oncorhynchus masou formosanus]], [[Hucho taimen]], [[Hucho bleekeri]], [[Brachymystax lenok tsinlingensis]], [[Stenodus leucichthys nelma]], [[Coregonus ussuriensis]], [[Thymallus arcticus grubei]]

**[[Cyprinidae]]**: 52 species including [[Luciobrama macrocephalus]], [[Gobiocypris rarus]], multiple [[Schizothorax]] species, [[Cyprinus]] species (including [[Cyprinus yilongensis]], [[Cyprinus megalophthalmus]]), cave-dwelling species ([[Sinocyclocheilus anophthalmus]], [[Typhlobarbus nudiventris]])

**[[Siluridae|Catfishes]]**: 11 species including [[Silurus soldatovi]], [[Silurus mento]], [[Cranoglanis bouderius]], [[Bagarius bagarius]]

**[[Cobitidae|Loaches]]**: [[Oreonectes anophthalmus]], [[Noemacheilus gejivensis]], [[Triplophysa siluroides]], [[Lefua elongata]]

The document notes that many regional economic fishes from several decades prior have become endangered species, with fish populations becoming smaller-sized and lower-aged. Both total resource numbers and species diversity have declined significantly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The endangerment of Chinese freshwater fishes occurs within the broader context of human-induced environmental change. The report emphasizes that human activity has become the main factor in species extinction globally, accelerating beyond the natural balance between speciation and extinction that characterized evolutionary history.

**Habitat Modification**: The construction of hydroelectric dams represents the most significant habitat alteration. The [[Gezhouba Dam]] and [[Three Gorges Dam]] on the [[Changjiang River]] exemplify large-scale projects that fundamentally transform riverine environments from running water to still water conditions. These modifications particularly affect migratory species like [[Acipenser sinensis|Chinese sturgeon]], whose reproductive cycles depend on access to specific spawning areas. Dams create multiple stressors: they block migratory routes, eliminate spawning grounds, alter flow regimes, and restrict population distribution, leading to genetic resource decline.

**Land Reclamation**: The reclamation of land from lakes and rivers reduces aquatic habitat area, particularly affecting shallow zones critical for fish reproduction and juvenile development. These projects disproportionately impact lake-dwelling species by eliminating essential living space.

**Natural Catastrophes**: Deforestation and vegetation damage in upper watersheds trigger mountain torrents and floods that carry excessive soil and sediment into rivers, directly affecting fish respiration and causing mass mortality events.

**Reproductive Habitat Loss**: Fixed spawning areas required by most riverine species become damaged or eliminated by dam construction and industrial development. Water plant communities that provide food and habitat for herbivorous fishes have declined, leading to population decreases or local extinctions of plant-eating species.

**Pollution**: Industrial wastes and agricultural chemicals increasingly contaminate rivers and lakes, reducing available living space and affecting fish respiration. The cumulative effects of industry, agriculture, and capital construction have progressively degraded water quality throughout China's freshwater systems.

**Species Introductions**: Unchecked introduction of non-native species disrupts ecological balance. The introduction of [[Perca fluviatilis]] (European perch) from the [[Erqisi River]] into [[Bosten River]] resulted in the extinction of the endemic [[Aspiorhynchus laticeps]], demonstrating how introduced species with superior adaptive capacity can monopolize niches and eliminate native fauna.

## Key Findings

**Endangerment Statistics**: 
- 92 species (10% of China's freshwater fish fauna) are endangered
- Taxonomic distribution: [[Cyprinidae]] 52 species (of ~600 total), catfishes 11 species, [[Acipenseridae|sturgeons]] 5 species, [[Salmonidae|salmons]] 6 species, others 18 species
- Endangerment grades: 4 species extinct, 23 species rare, 28 species endangered, 37 species gradually endangered

**Primary Endangerment Causes**:

1. **Water Conservancy Projects**: Dam construction on major rivers including [[Changjiang River]] fundamentally alters fish habitat, converting running water to still water environments, blocking migration routes, and eliminating spawning areas. The [[Gezhouba Dam]] and [[Three Gorges Dam]] have profoundly impacted [[Acipenser sinensis|Chinese sturgeon]] reproduction and growth.

2. **Excessive Exploitation**: Multiple forms of overfishing threaten populations:
   - General overfishing driven by increasing human food demands has reduced formerly abundant economic species like [[Macrura reevesi]] and [[Trachidermus fasciatus]] to rare or endangered status
   - Production of economic fishes in [[Changjiang River]] has declined to very small scales, reducing professional fisherman numbers
   - Unchecked elimination of carnivorous fishes from lakes for aquaculture purposes damages biodiversity and ecosystem function, simplifying fish communities and creating small-sized, low-aged populations
   - Mass capture of parent fish, juveniles, larvae, and overwintering fishes disrupts population balance and prevents fry replenishment
   - Non-selective fishing methods capture all size classes including larvae and juveniles, severely limiting population development
   - Use of water birds and otters as fishing tools results in capture of fish of any size, especially juveniles and larvae

3. **Habitat Loss**: Land reclamation from lakes and rivers reduces aquatic area, particularly affecting shallow zones critical for reproduction and juvenile development.

4. **Reproductive Habitat Damage**: Fixed spawning areas are damaged or eliminated by dams and industrial development. Decline of aquatic vegetation reduces food availability and habitat for herbivorous species.

5. **Water Pollution**: Industrial wastes and agricultural chemicals contaminate water systems, directly affecting fish respiration and progressively reducing available living space.

6. **Species Introductions**: Introduction of [[Perca fluviatilis]] from [[Erqisi River]] to [[Bosten River]] caused extinction of endemic [[Aspiorhynchus laticeps]], demonstrating how introduced species with superior adaptive capacity can eliminate native fauna through niche competition.

7. **Regulatory Challenges**: While China has established regulations for fish resource protection and fishery limits, enforcement remains difficult in some areas. The report identifies propaganda and education as necessary to improve compliance.

**Conservation Implications**: The report emphasizes that China's freshwater fishes include numerous endemic species and monotypic genera of special importance for phylogenetic and biogeographic research. These species represent irreplaceable biological resources whose loss would significantly diminish global biodiversity. The analysis provides scientific reference for researching and saving endemic precious freshwater fishes in China, supporting biodiversity protection efforts.
