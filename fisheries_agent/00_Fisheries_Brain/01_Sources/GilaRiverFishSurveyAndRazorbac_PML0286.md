# Gila River Fish Survey and Razorback Sucker Stocking Records (c. 1981)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is a partial, heavily degraded (OCR-scanned) field record, dated approximately 1981, of fish surveys and stocking activities in the [[Gila River]] system of Arizona. It refers to fish collected from the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]], and to surveys of [[Bonita Creek]] and [[Eagle Creek]]. Stocking of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is described, with fish hauled from a [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] hatchery. Survey work used seine hauls. The record is fragmentary, so sample totals and several site details cannot be reliably recovered.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Field survey and stocking log fragment, with a second page of site and location notes (township, range and section entries, partly illegible).
- **Survey methods:** Seining. Four seine hauls are mentioned in one survey. Two seine hauls are reported as turbid, with no fish caught in them. Seine catches were used to determine species presence.
- **Stocking:** A [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] hatchery truck transported fish. The stocking plan mentions a target of 1000 fish, but the text does not confirm the number actually released.
- **Survey sites:** Gila River, [[Bonita Creek]], [[Eagle Creek]], and a creek rendered as "Cherrat Cr." (possibly Cherry Creek; uncertain due to OCR).
- **Personnel:** A name rendered as "Johnson, FWS" appears, likely a Fish and Wildlife Service staff member.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water conditions:** One survey reports little surface water flow and that the water was "turbid." Flow is noted as approximated, not measured.
- **Dominant taxa:** One passage states that the catch consisted primarily of *Agosia* (longfin dace). This is the most abundant group in the surviving text.
- **Species present:** The surveys list species including [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Common Carp]], [[Western Mosquitofish]] and one or more [[Shiner]] species. The list suggests a mix of native and non-native fishes, with non-natives including [[Common Carp]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Western Mosquitofish]].
- **Habitat context:** Survey locations are in the arid Southwest, where surface flow is intermittent and turbidity is high. The text does not give quantitative habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

1. **Razorback Sucker stocking:** Razorback suckers (*Xyrauchen texanus*) were collected from hatchery stock and transported for stocking into the Gila, Salt and Verde river systems. The plan mentions stocking 1000 fish, though the number actually stocked is not confirmed.
2. **Survey outcomes:** Surveys of the Gila River and associated creeks were conducted by seining. Some hauls were turbid and yielded no fish; the remaining hauls documented a mix of native and introduced species.
3. **Dominance of native dace:** Longfin dace (*Agosia*) made up most of the catch in at least one survey.
4. **Non-native presence:** Several introduced species (common carp, fathead minnow, black bullhead, western mosquitofish) were recorded, an early indicator of the species composition shift in Arizona's desert rivers.
5. **Data limitations:** The source is badly degraded. Sample counts, exact locations (township, range and section entries are partly illegible) and survey dates cannot be verified from the text. Species names were reconstructed from fragmentary OCR; the identification of some entries (for example, "hen texanus" as *Xyrauchen texanus*) is inferred from context.
