# Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Lower Colorado River: Reference List

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the front matter and References section (Section VII) of a final report titled *Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Lower Colorado River, Southwestern United States*. W. L. Minckley of the Arizona State University Department of Zoology and Center for Environmental Studies prepared it in May 1979 under Contract No. 14-06-300-2529 for the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Lower Colorado River Region, Boulder City, Nevada. The excerpt contains no results, sample data, or species accounts from the report body. Its scientific content is the bibliography, which reflects the report's scope: fish ecology, food habits, aquatic invertebrates, phytoplankton, water chemistry, and riparian habitat in the [[Lower Colorado River]] and its associated waters.

## Background & Description

The title page identifies the study area, funding source, and final report status. The reference list contains roughly 200 entries (approximate count) dated from 1914 to 1979. Its citations include:

- **Basic and regional fish biology:** Taxonomic and distributional works such as [[Miller]] (1961) on the fresh-water fishes of western North America, [[Minckley]] (1973) *Fishes of Arizona*, and [[Hubbs]] and [[Miller]] regional checklists for California and Colorado.
- **Food habits:** A large group of starred items cited for the feeding biology of fishes now present in the Lower Colorado River. Examples include [[Gerking]] (1954) on bluegill food turnover and [[Haskell]] (1959) on the diet of striped mullet. These are flagged as relevant to report sections IV-2-A, IV-2-D, and V-4.
- **Invertebrates and plankton:** Benthos, Copepoda and Cladocera, aquatic macroinvertebrates, and phytoplankton studies from the Lower Colorado River, Missouri River reservoirs, and Sonoran Desert streams.
- **Water chemistry and methods:** Hach analytical procedures, plankton pigment extraction methods, and sediment and organic transport studies.
- **Riparian habitat and birds/mammals:** Lower Colorado River valley bird and rodent surveys, climatology, and riparian case histories.
- **Hydrology and geography:** Drainage maps, river geomorphology, and historical zoogeography.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The references describe a system heavily altered by impoundment and introduction. Several entries address:

- **Reservoir fisheries:** Lake Mohave, Lake Mead, Lake Havasu, Salton Sea, and central Arizona reservoirs (including [[Threadfin Shad]] population studies), along with reservoir zooplankton and fish-habitat work.
- **Non-native and introduced species:** Sportfish and forage introductions, including [[Striped Bass]] (Morone saxatilis), [[Red Shiner]] as a forage fish in the lower Colorado River, [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], and mosquitofish. Biological control of aquatic weeds is also cited.
- **Native and endangered fishes:** Studies of the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Red River Pupfish]], and Gila species (including [[Gila Cyprinids]] and the Gila orcutti x Hesperoleucus symmetricus hybridization study). Recovery planning for the Colorado squawfish and listings of endangered endemic warmwater fishes also appear.
- **Desert aquatic systems:** Groundwater-recharged desert ponds, desert springs, and fishes of Death Valley-related and Sonoran Desert habitats.
- **Riparian change:** Saltcedar (*Tamarix*) spread in western states and its effect on riparian bird and rodent communities.

## Key Findings

The excerpt does not report study results. Its main content and implied conclusions are as follows:

1. **Scope of the report.** The study covered the fishes, aquatic habitats, and associated biota of the Lower Colorado River. Its bibliography spans fish taxonomy, food habits, invertebrate ecology, limnology, and riparian biology.
2. **Starred food-habit references.** Items marked with an asterisk were included because they are relevant to the food habits of fishes in the Lower Colorado River, not because they were cited in the text.
3. **Dominant topical clusters:**
   - Food habits and diets of game and forage fishes (e.g., [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Channel Catfish]])
   - Life history and population dynamics of native and introduced species, including threadfin shad and red shiner
   - Reservoir limnology and fish habitat in arid western reservoirs
   - Invertebrate and phytoplankton communities of rivers and reservoirs
   - Endangered and threatened endemic fishes (razorback sucker, Colorado squawfish, Gila species)
4. **Notable regional sources:** Historical surveys of the Colorado River fishery (e.g., [[Miller]] 1946 and [[Minckley]] 1971 lower-basin surveys), species checklists for Arizona and California, and early distribution records such as [[Evermann]] (1895) for the Colorado Basin.
5. **Taxonomic conventions.** Several references use older generic names (e.g., *Pylodictis olivaris* for flathead catfish, *Ptychocheilus lucius* for Colorado squawfish). These names reflect the 1970s taxonomy used in the report.
