# Synsedimentary Lacustrine Phosphorites from the Pliocene Glenns Ferry Formation of Southwestern Idaho

#geology #sedimentary-petrology #phosphorite #lacustrine-deposits

## Executive Summary

This study describes thin, laterally extensive freshwater lacustrine phosphorite horizons within the Pliocene [[Glenns Ferry Formation]] of southwestern Idaho. The phosphatic units are confined to coarse-grained nearshore and shoreface facies that crop out along about 50 km of the southwestern shoreline of [[Pliocene Lake Idaho]]. The phosphate is almost entirely [[Francolite]] (carbonate fluorapatite), formed by replacement of ooid and oncoid cortices and by isopachous fibrous cements. The authors interpret phosphatization as a synsedimentary diagenetic event occurring just below the sediment-water interface, driven by high pore-water phosphate released from organic matter during periods of low terrigenous sedimentation and lake transgression.

This is a sedimentary geology and geochemistry paper, not a fish study. Fish bone fragments appear only as a minor component of the phosphatic horizons.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Sedimentary petrology, mineralogy, and paleoenvironment of phosphorites. Fish content is incidental.
- **Context:** Phosphorites are rare in both the rock record and modern settings. Most documented examples are marine, from upwelling margins such as the [[Peru-Chile Continental Margin]] and southwest Africa. Lacustrine phosphorites are poorly studied, which makes the Glenns Ferry examples notable.
- **Stratigraphy:** The Glenns Ferry Formation unconformably overlies lake-center silts of the Miocene [[Chalk Hills Formation]]. Its basal transgressive sequence includes fluvial, floodplain, and lacustrine units. Phosphatic horizons occur only in coarse nearshore facies.
- **Phosphate petrology:** Matrix is microcrystalline and shows low-order birefringence. Phosphate occurs as ooid and oncoid cortices, as stubby euhedral apatite crystals in oncoids, and as radial fibrous isopachous cements (crystals about 1.5–2.0 µm long and 0.3 µm wide).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Depositional setting:** A productive, nutrient-rich, low-salinity freshwater lake. Phosphatic units cap massive oolite formed on drowned lake-margin bench platforms during transgression, when sediment supply was low.
- **Pore-water chemistry:** Decay and dissolution of organic matter under anaerobic conditions release phosphate. An oxygenated sediment-water microzone then fixes it by adsorption onto ferric oxides and by apatite precipitation. This prevents upward diffusion into the lake, allowing pore-water phosphate to exceed overlying water concentrations.
- **Biological indicators:** Burrows associated with phosphorite horizons support the presence of oxygenated pore waters. Abraded fish bones occur in phosphatic sands, but no preferential phosphatization surrounds the bones, so bone was not the phosphate source. Fish bones also occur abundantly in non-phosphatic sands.
- **Comparisons:** Similar repeated, thin, laterally continuous phosphorites occur in ancient marine deposits, such as the Middle Devonian of New York ([[Baird 1978]]) and the Cenomanian of southeastern England ([[Kennedy and Garrison 1975]]).

## Key Findings

1. **Mineralogy:** X-ray diffraction shows that all components (phosphatized carbonate, phosphate cement, phosphatized wood, fish bone) contain francolite. The mean a-cell dimension of phosphate cement is 9.323 Å (s.d. = 0.005, n = 14), and the mean a-cell dimension of phosphatized carbonate is 9.328 Å (n = 13 for the carbonate group).
2. **Carbonate content:** Estimated CO₂ content averages 5.4 wt% from a-cell dimensions. Electron microprobe values are 3.70 wt% for phosphatic ooids and 2.99 wt% for phosphate cements, as calculated from the chemical formula.
3. **Chemistry:** Ooids have higher Ca and lower P than cements, which indicates incomplete phosphatization. Higher Si, Fe, and S in ooids reflect incorporated terrigenous and organic debris. Mean P₂O₅ is 34.68 wt% in ooids and 36.21 wt% in cements.
4. **Timing:** Phosphatization was synsedimentary, forming repeatedly at or near the sediment-water interface during transgressions. Sharp upper contacts and gradational lower contacts support this, and some phosphorite horizons were reworked into intraformational conglomerates.
5. **Mechanisms:** Two processes occurred: replacement of calcium carbonate by carbonate fluorapatite, and direct precipitation of isopachous francolite cements. Unphosphatized sands lack fibrous calcite cements, which argues against cement replacement as the origin of the francolite overgrowths.
6. **Controls on phosphogenesis:** Decreased terrigenous sedimentation during lacustrine transgression increased the relative importance of organic phosphorus. The association with hiatal surfaces suggests that high productivity alone was not the main driver.
7. **Conclusions:** Glenns Ferry phosphorites are mineralogically and compositionally similar to marine phosphorites. They are the product of a low-salinity lacustrine system, and their formation required both low sedimentation rates and an oxygenated sediment-water microzone.
