## Executive Summary
This 1970 short paper documents an empirical test of the exponential decay model in fish population dynamics, using a stocked population of [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) in an experimental farm pond (S-6) at Auburn University. Fathead minnows had been added to standard bass-bluegill stocking regimes as a forage species to boost early [[Largemouth Bass]] survival, but were observed to nearly disappear from the population within a year. Weekly seine sampling from July–October 1960 showed a statistically significant log-linear decline in catch numbers, yielding an instantaneous mortality rate of ~0.044/day and a daily survival rate of ~96%. The study provides one of the few explicit empirical validations of the exponential decrease model widely assumed in [[Fish Population Dynamics]] literature, and implicates [[Predation]] by largemouth bass as the primary driver of minnow decline.

## Background & Description
- Species studied: [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), family [[Cyprinidae]].
- Size class recovered at pond draining (October 1961): all survivors were in the ~3-inch (63–86 mm) size group.
- No detailed external morphological description provided; paper is focused on population-level metrics rather than individual anatomy.
- Co-occurring species with relevant size constraints: [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) present at sizes under 15.5 inches (39.4 cm), determined too small to prey upon fathead minnows.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Experimental pond S-6, 25.5 acres (10.3 ha), max depth 15 ft (9.5 m), built 1946, substantial submerged stump cover, no higher aquatic plants present. Managed with standard fertilization ([[Pond Fertilization]]) and *Microcystis* control via copper sulfate.
- **Trophic role**: Fathead minnows were stocked specifically as a **forage species** to support early survival of stocked [[Largemouth Bass]] fingerlings prior to heavy [[Bluegill]] reproduction — a key application in [[Bass-Bluegill Pond Management]].
- **Stocking composition (Feb 1960)**:
  - Fathead minnow: 25,500 individuals (1,000/acre, 2,470/ha)
  - Redear sunfish (*Lepomis microlophus*): 5,400 (212/acre, 524/ha)
  - Bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*): 12,000 (471/acre, 1,163/ha)
  - Channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*): 5,100 (200/acre, 494/ha)
  - Largemouth bass (added May 3, 1960): 2,555 fingerlings (100/acre, 247/ha)
- **Predator-prey dynamics**: Largemouth bass identified as the most probable major predator driving the minnow population decline, based on cursory stomach content examination and broader field observations at the Auburn Farm Ponds Project. [[Channel Catfish]] were ruled out as predators due to insufficient size.
- **Population trajectory**: Reproduction and natural mortality (chiefly predation) acted simultaneously on the population; for the observed constant exponential decline to hold, these opposing rates must have remained constant or varied reciprocally throughout the study period.

## Key Findings
- **Sampling method**: 11 seining stations around pond margin; weekly quadrant seine hauls using a 15-ft (4.5 m) seine with 0.12 in (3 mm) bar mesh; 22 hauls per sampling date, July 12, 1960 – October 1961.
- **Observed decline**: Average catch per seine haul fell from >500 fish/haul in July 1960 to ≤10 fish/haul by October 1960.
- **Near-extirpation**: By summer 1961, fathead minnows rarely appeared in samples; only an estimated 67 minnows recovered upon final pond draining (all ~3 in / 63–86 mm size class).
- **Exponential decay model fit**: Natural log of average catch vs. time (July 11–Oct 31, 1960) produced a significant (P < 0.05) linear regression:
  - Equation: **Y = 6.2006 − 0.0442X**
    - Y = ln(average number per seine haul)
    - X = days since July 11, 1960
  - Equivalent exponential form: **N = 493·e^(−0.044t)**
- **Mortality/survival estimates**:
  - Instantaneous mortality rate: **0.044 ± 0.006** (95% CI)
  - Estimated daily survival rate: **~96%** (range 95–96%)
- **Significance**: This is presented as rare explicit empirical support for the [[Exponential Population Decline Model]] frequently assumed but seldom directly validated in [[Fish Population Dynamics]] studies.
- **Applied implication**: Confirms practical observation at Auburn that fathead minnows stocked as forage are essentially eliminated from bass-bluegill ponds within about one year, consistent with their intended transient forage role rather than establishment as a persistent population component.
