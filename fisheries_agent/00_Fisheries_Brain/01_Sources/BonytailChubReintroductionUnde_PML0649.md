# Bonytail Chub Reintroduction Under Experimental Nonessential Designation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This 1986 letter from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Region 2), signed by Acting Regional Director R.D. Earnest, responds to a meeting with [[Temple Reynolds]], Director of the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]]. It concerns recovery efforts for several endangered fish in Arizona, with particular focus on a Service proposal to reintroduce the [[Bonytail Chub]] under the [[Experimental Nonessential Designation]]. The Service describes the species as the most endangered fish in the Colorado River drainage and reports that a captive [[Brood Stock]] now allows large-scale production of juveniles. The letter explains that the nonessential designation is the Service's best option for obtaining agency approval, and it asks the Department for its opinion on the matter.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Bonytail Chub]], a fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Wild status:** Only old, adult fish have been captured over the past 15 years (figure as printed in the scan), and all came from [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Search effort:** A concerted search the previous month failed to find any of these old fish in Lake Mohave, suggesting the remaining wild supply may be exhausted.
- **Captive source:** A brood stock was established at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] from a few Lake Mohave fish. The Service reports it has been "very successful" at culturing the species.
- **Proposal context:** Letters of intent to designate experimental nonessential bonytail populations were issued for the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], and [[Verde River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Agency concerns:** Land and water management agencies are reluctant to approve reintroduction of an endangered species into areas where it was extirpated, because of the added responsibilities this creates.
- **Regulatory tool:** The [[Experimental Nonessential Designation]] removes most endangered-species responsibilities and most legal protections, which the Service judged would reduce agency objections.
- **Habitat stewardship:** Commenters urged that stocking nonessential fish should not preempt future use of high-quality habitat.
- **Public comment range:** Responses ranged from "good idea" to "unacceptable because of extreme threat of extinction in the wild," and from objections that the designation offered too little future protection.
- **State position:** The Arizona Game And Fish Department's letter of January 8, 1986 supported the proposal, suggested several additional areas for inclusion, and also proposed stocking under full protection.

## Key Findings

1. The bonytail chub is "without doubt" the most endangered fish in the Colorado River drainage.
2. Wild capture records are limited to old adults, all from Lake Mohave, and a recent search failed to locate any of them.
3. A captive brood stock at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] can supply "an almost unlimited number" of young for recovery.
4. Reintroduction into mainstream rivers faces resistance from land and water agencies, which is the main obstacle identified.
5. The Service views the experimental nonessential designation as "the best chance we have at this time" to start recovery, despite its limited protection.
6. The Service acknowledges that full-protection stocking gives the best chance for long-term recovery but judges it unlikely to gain agency support.
7. Recovery must begin soon for the species to survive, according to the Service.
8. The Service asks the Arizona Game And Fish Department for a further opinion on the proposal and requests cooperation in resolving the matter.
