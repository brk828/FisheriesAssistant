# Studies of Neotropical Caddisflies, XIV: A Collection from Northern Argentina

#entomology #trichoptera #freshwater-biology

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Oliver S. Flint Jr.]] reports caddisfly (Trichoptera) material collected in northern Argentina from 30 March to 9 April 1971, in the provinces of [[Santa Fe Province]] and [[Misiones Province]]. The collections yielded a number of poorly known species, describes **13 new species**, documents **new country records for Argentina**, resurrects one species, proposes **5 new synonymies**, and records two new combinations. Two zoogeographic patterns are proposed: a widespread lowland fauna of the [[Parana Basin]], and a restricted fauna of the low mountains of Misiones and adjacent Brazil.

## Background & Description

The study belongs to the author's series *Studies of Neotropical Caddisflies*, published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (Vol. 85, No. 17, 1972). Santa Fe was the type locality for many species described by [[Lorenzo Navas]], so topotypic collections were needed to confirm species identities. Misiones was of interest because of its faunal affinity with the adjacent Brazilian state of Santa Catarina, whose fauna is comparatively well known.

Collecting was carried out mainly with [[Ultraviolet Light Trapping]] supplemented by beating vegetation and collecting larvae from rubble. Sites included:

- **Santa Fe:** the Rio Salado at Santo Tomé (a slow, silty river in front of the Instituto Nacional de Limnología) and Arroyo Saladillo, near Santa Fe (a riffle with shallow weed-filled ponds).
- **Misiones:** the Rio Paraná near Puerto Rico (broad, swift, silty river), Capiovi (a shallow, milky, bedrock-and-gravel stream reduced to a trickle at its falls by a diversion to a sawmill), and Mbopicua (a stream of similar size but lower gradient, alternating gravel riffles and pools).

The paper is organized taxonomically by family, with each species listing diagnostic characters, adult and male genitalic descriptions (or larval descriptions where relevant), and voucher material. Holotypes are deposited in the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study describes the aquatic habitats as lotic (flowing) systems: large lowland rivers, riffle-pool streams, and small shallow streams with bedrock, boulders, gravel, and silt. Immature stages of [[Abtrichia Antennata]] were found attached tightly to rocks in riffle areas at Mbopicua. Larval case morphology is used diagnostically, for example the silken, oval, domed cases of Abtrichia antennata.

Biogeographically, the author identifies two patterns:

1. **Widespread lowland fauna.** Species of the genera *Cyrnellus*, *Nyctiophylax*, *Leptonema*, *Neoleptonema*, *Leptocella*, *Oecetis*, and *Brachysetodes* have extensive distributions, particularly in the Parana basin and often across most of lowland South America, sometimes extending into Central America. These were mainly collected at Santa Fe and Puerto Rico.
2. **Restricted montane fauna.** A group of endemics of isolated taxonomic position occurs in the low mountains of Misiones and adjacent Brazil. Species in this group include *Atopsyche longipennis*, *Mexitrichia albolineata*, *M. teutonia*, *M. unota*, *Chimarra brasiliana*, *Abtrichia antennata*, and *Smicridea weidneri*, mostly collected at Capiovi and Mbopicua.

The author notes that the distributions of the remaining species were not yet well enough known to suggest patterns.

## Key Findings

### New species described (13)
- *Protoptila misionensis* (Glossosomatidae): the first species of the genus known from Argentina; related to Mexican congeners *P. guata* and *P. spangleri*.
- *Chimarra parana* (Philopotamidae): close to *C. brasiliana*; distinguished by golden spots on the forewing and golden head hairs.
- *Polyplectropus hamulus* (Psychomyiidae): close to *P. banksianus* of the central Amazon.
- *Cernotina medioloba* and *C. chelifera* (Psychomyiidae): both related to *C. subapicalis*.
- *Betrichia argentinica* (Hydroptilidae): has three ocelli, unlike the two of the type species *B. zilbra*.
- *Smicridea discalis*, *S. appendiculata*, *S. weidneri*, *S. pallidivittata*, *S. minima*, and *S. spinulosa* (Hydropsychidae, subgenus *Rhyacophylax*). *S. minima* is described as one of the smallest species in the genus.
- *Cochliopsyche opalescens* (Helicopsychidae): closely related to *C. clara* of Santa Catarina.

### Resurrected species
- *Mexitrichia teutonia* Mosely is resurrected as a valid species, distinct from *M. albolineata*. Earlier synonymy proposed by Flint (1966) is withdrawn.

### Taxonomic changes
- **New combinations:** *Smicridea argentina* (Navas), transferred from *Badallus*; *Brachysetodes duodecimpunctatus* (Navas), transferred from *Setodes*.
- **New synonymies (5):** *Leptocella ornata* and *L. fulvocapilla* (both Navas) are synonymized with *L. nigricapilla* and *L. muhni*, respectively; *Oecetis castilleja* and *O. muhnia* are synonymized with *Oecetis excisa*; *Oecetis bridarollina* is synonymized with *Oecetis punctipennis*.
- **Lectotype:** Weidner's (1964) designation of a Hamburg specimen as lectotype of *Rhyacophylax brasilianus* is treated as preceding and invalidating Flint's 1966 designation. The author also found that the original Ulmer series contained two species.

### New records for Argentina
- *Atopsyche longipennis* (previously known only from Santa Catarina, Brazil).
- *Mexitrichia unota*, *Leptonema crassum*, *Leptonema dissimile*, and *Neoleptonema aspersum* (the last not previously reported from Argentina).
- *Chimarra brasiliana*, recorded from females only, which the author notes is tentative.
- *Cyrnellus mammillatus* and *C. arotron*, both recorded from the upper Rio Paraná or Misiones.

### Other notes
- Several *Mexitrichia* and *Smicridea* records from the wider literature are flagged as needing caution, since many similar species occur in these genera.
- *Leptonema columbianum* identification is based on females, using the dilated midleg tibia and tarsus.
- Records of *Mexitrichia albolineata* from Rio de Janeiro and Salta are held in abeyance pending examination of male genitalia.
