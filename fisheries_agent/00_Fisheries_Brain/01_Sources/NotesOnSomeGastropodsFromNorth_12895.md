## Executive Summary
This paper by Branley A. Branson documents the land and freshwater gastropod fauna of northern Louisiana based on 251 specimens representing 20 genera and 27 species collected between 1950–1960. The study addresses a significant gap in regional conchological knowledge, noting that the area was "very poorly known" at the time. The work catalogs both native and introduced (exotic) species, provides new distributional records for the state (e.g., [[Mesodon Clausus]], [[Deroceras Reticulatum]]), and offers taxonomic commentary on problematic groups such as [[Viviparus]], [[Campeloma]], and the [[Physidae]]. While the paper is explicitly malacological rather than ichthyological, it is included here as a broader aquatic/terrestrial ecosystem survey of an under-documented region.

## Background & Description
Although the paper is primarily a distributional/taxonomic survey, some morphometric and shell-character data are given:

- **[[Helix Aspersa]]** (introduced European land snail): three adult specimens measured with height/diameter/whorl counts:
  - 29.5 mm height × 31.5 mm diameter, 4½ whorls
  - 29.0 mm height × 31.0 mm diameter, 4 whorls
  - 31.5 mm height × 34.0 mm diameter, 4¼ whorls
- **[[Anguispira Alternata]]**: specimens noted as strongly ribbed dorsally and somewhat depressed, resembling the "crassa" form (per Walker), though the author cautions against taxonomic over-splitting given high intraspecific variation.
- **[[Polygyra Texasiana]]**: described as a "characteristically small, heavily striated form," consistent with populations from southern Oklahoma.
- **[[Mesodon Thyroidus]]**: specimens correspond morphologically to the form *M. t. bucculenta*, though the author argues this sporadic variant does not merit subspecies status.
- Shell banding noted in juvenile Polygyrids (banded periphery), tentatively identified as immature **[[Polygyra Texasiana]]**.
- Diagnostic caution is raised for **[[Succinea Luteola]]**, as shell-based identification within *Succinea* is considered unreliable without living material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The species documented occupy a range of freshwater and terrestrial microhabitats across northern Louisiana:

- **Freshwater/aquatic taxa** (Prosobranchia & Basommatophora): [[Viviparus Subpurpureus]] and [[Campeloma Decisum]] inhabit bayous and lakes (e.g., D'Arbonne Bayou, Iatt Lake). [[Helisoma Trivolvis]], [[Lymnaea Columella]], [[Physa Anatina]], and [[Physa Halei]] occupy ponds, camps, and nursery-associated waters, often found on submerged vegetation (notably [[Menetus Dilatatus]]).
- **Woodland/upland terrestrial taxa**: [[Mesodon Inflectus]], [[Mesodon Clausus]], and [[Triodopsis Cragini]] favor wooded, rocky, uplifted habitats. [[Mesomphix Vulgatus]] and [[Ventridens Demissus]] are characteristic of well-drained upland forests within the Austroriparian biotic province.
- **Grassland/wood-edge generalists**: [[Retinella Indentata Paucilirata]] is described as a common wood- and grassland species statewide.
- **Moisture-dependent microhabitats**: [[Zonitoides Arboreus]] is most common under rocks and in decaying wood, favoring moisture-conserving microsites.
- **Synanthropic/introduced species**: [[Helix Aspersa]], [[Deroceras Reticulatum]], and [[Limax Flavus]] are non-native, largely associated with greenhouses and disturbed/nursery habitats, though the author suspects feral populations exist in the wild given their presence in neighboring states.
- **Biogeographic context**: Several species (e.g., [[Ventridens Demissus]], [[Physa Halei]]) are tied to the broader Austroriparian and Carolinian biotic provinces spanning Louisiana, Oklahoma, Texas, and Arkansas, with the Red River cited as a potential dispersal barrier (e.g., for [[Viviparus Subpurpureus]] into Oklahoma).

## Key Findings
- **First state records**: [[Mesodon Clausus]] and [[Deroceras Reticulatum]] represent new molluscan records for Louisiana, consistent with known populations in southeastern Oklahoma and southwestern Arkansas.
- **Taxonomic revisions suggested**:
  - The subspecies name *V. s. texanus* (applied to [[Viviparus Subpurpureus]]) should be suppressed in favor of recognizing *baleanus* as a minor variant.
  - The genus [[Campeloma]] is described as "very badly confused" and in need of taxonomic revision; regional specimens are attributed to *C. decisum* rather than *C. integrum*.
  - Subspecies status for *Mesodon thyroidus bucculenta* is questioned due to widespread sporadic occurrence of the form.
  - [[Physa Halei]] and [[Physa Anatina]] may represent variants of a single multivalent species, reflecting broader taxonomic confusion within [[Physidae]].
- **Introduced species distribution**: [[Helix Aspersa]], previously known in Louisiana only from New Orleans and Baton Rouge, is now recorded from Shreveport (Caddo Parish), extending its known range northward. [[Limax Flavus]] and [[Deroceras Reticulatum]] are hypothesized to occur more broadly in greenhouses statewide despite limited formal records.
- **Faunal gaps identified**: The author explicitly notes the conspicuous absence of Pupillidae and other terrestrial genera, as well as pelecypods (bivalves), from the survey, flagging these as priorities for future malacological work in the region.
- **Regional biogeographic affinities**: Multiple species (e.g., [[Triodopsis Fosteri]], [[Stenotrema Leai Aliciae]], [[Strobilops Labyrinthica Texasiana]]) show distributional links to the western Ozarks, southeastern Oklahoma, and the Lake Pontchartrain region, suggesting northern Louisiana functions as a faunal transition zone.
- **Collection methodology**: All specimens were collected 1950–1960 primarily in Lincoln Parish (centered on Ruston), with specimens deposited in the Museum of Zoology at Louisiana Tech, in collaboration with Dr. Walter J. Harman and students.
