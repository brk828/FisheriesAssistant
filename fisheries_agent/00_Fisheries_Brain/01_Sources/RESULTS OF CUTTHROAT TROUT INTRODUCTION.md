## Executive Summary
This brief field report, authored by Norman Wood on January 21, 1963, presents the disappointing results of a [[Yellowstone Cutthroat Trout]] stocking initiative conducted in April 1961. Following a 21-month monitoring period post-release, angler return rates were extremely low, with only 31 of the original 14,400 stocked fish recovered via creel checks. The report specifically highlights three catches during the calendar year 1962, noting that growth rates for the surviving cutthroat were significantly stunted compared to [[Rainbow Trout]] populations inhabiting the same geographic area.

## Background & Description
Specific biometric data was limited to length measurements taken from angler-caught specimens during the 1962 monitoring window:
* **Specimen 1 (March 1962):** 14 inches in length.
* **Specimen 2 (June 1962):** 15 inches in length.
* **Specimen 3 (December 1962):** 15 inches in length.

**Growth Comparison:** The report explicitly notes that the growth rate of the recovered cutthroat trout was **"much less"** than that observed in [[Rainbow Trout]] populations occupying the identical stocking region, suggesting potential competitive disadvantages or habitat mismatches for this specific strain.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The stocking and subsequent recovery efforts occurred within a specific stretch of the Colorado River system, characterized by the following key locations:
* **Eldorado Canyon:** Serving as a primary geographic reference point for two of the three catches (one 2 miles north, one several miles south).
* **[[Ringbolt Rapids]]:** A specific catch site located above Willow Beach.
* **[[Willow Beach]]:** A key downstream landmark in the survey area.

This habitat suggests a riverine/rapids environment likely situated in the tailwater fishery below a major dam (implied by the Eldorado Canyon/Willow Beach geography near Lake Mohave), where cutthroat trout were introduced to diversify the sport fishery alongside existing Rainbow Trout stocks.

## Key Findings
* **Extremely Low Return Rate:** Only 31 fish (0.21%) of the 14,400 stocked Yellowstone Cutthroat were reported caught over 21 months.
* **Sparse Annual Data:** Only 3 specific catches were documented and detailed for the year 1962.
* **Comparative Growth Deficiency:** Cutthroat trout exhibited significantly slower growth rates than co-habiting [[Rainbow Trout]], suggesting the species may be less suited to the specific thermal or trophic conditions of this river reach compared to established salmonid competitors.
* **Management Implication:** The poor survival/catchability and inferior growth performance data suggests this specific introduction strain may not be a viable candidate for continued stocking efforts in this particular river system without further adaptive strategy changes.
