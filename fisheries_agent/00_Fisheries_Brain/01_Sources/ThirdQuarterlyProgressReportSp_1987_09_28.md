## Executive Summary

This document is the Third Quarterly Progress Report (1 August–20 September 1987) for the "[[Spikedace and Loach Minnow Study]]," a field and analytical research effort conducted by Arizona State University under contract to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (nongame program). The report is primarily administrative/logistical rather than a presentation of results, detailing field sampling activities at three Arizona/New Mexico stream sites intended to characterize microhabitat use by [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Red Shiner]], and to compare habitats where imperiled native cyprinids coexist with or are replaced by the invasive red shiner. Data entry and preliminary statistical analyses (principal components analysis, ANOVA, chi-square tests) on fish/microhabitat datasets were underway on the ASU mainframe, with definitive analyses deferred to the next reporting period. A site visit and analytical consultation with Dr. William Matthews (University of Oklahoma) was planned for October 1987, along with pilot studies for experimental habitat manipulations.

## Background & Description

The report contains no direct morphological measurements or descriptions of [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], or [[Red Shiner]]. Sampling methodology implies focus on microhabitat association metrics (e.g., paired fish/microhabitat data) rather than morphometric or meristic data. No length, weight, fin-ray counts, or other anatomical data are reported in this quarter's update.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Field efforts targeted three distinct stream reaches representing different community compositions, allowing comparative inference about niche partitioning and interspecific habitat overlap:

- **[[Sycamore Creek]]** (tributary of the lower [[Verde River]]), at the mouth of Mesquite Wash: Sampled in early August as a reference site for **[[Red Shiner]]** microhabitat use *in the absence of* imperiled native fishes ([[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] do not occur there). This allows isolation of red shiner's baseline habitat preferences without competitive or interactive effects from the native species.
  - Duplicate diel (day/night) aquatic drift samples
  - 100 paired fish/microhabitat collections
  - Triplicate benthic macroinvertebrate kick samples

- **Upper (east) reach of [[Aravaipa Creek]]**: Sampled in mid-August as a site supporting relatively abundant **[[Loach Minnow]]** and **[[Spikedace]]**, with red shiner *absent*. This reach serves as a reference for native-species microhabitat use free from red shiner influence, contrasting with the Sycamore Creek red-shiner-only baseline.
  - 100 paired fish/microhabitat samples
  - Triplicate benthic kick samples
  - Duplicate diel aquatic drift samples of loach minnow and spikedace

- **Upper [[Gila River]]** between Virden and Red Rock, New Mexico: Sampled in mid-September to attempt collections from a reach of *co-occurrence* between spikedace and red shiner. This goal was **not achieved** — spikedace was abundant near Red Rock, red shiner was abundant near Virden, but both species were uncommon in the intervening reaches. Despite this, a series of ~70 paired fish/microhabitat samples was collected to supplement Second Quarter data from this system.

This tri-site design reflects an underlying ecological question about whether **[[Red Shiner]]** invasion drives habitat displacement or competitive exclusion of native **[[Spikedace]]** and **[[Loach Minnow]]** in Gila River Basin streams.

## Key Findings

- **Fieldwork completed for the quarter** at three sites: Sycamore Creek (red shiner only), Aravaipa Creek (spikedace/loach minnow only, no red shiner), and the upper Gila River, NM (mixed but patchy distribution of spikedace and red shiner).
- **Sympatry between spikedace and red shiner was not achieved** in the target Gila River reach between Virden and Red Rock, NM — the two species showed largely non-overlapping abundance patterns, undermining the original sampling goal for that site but still yielding useful supplementary data (~70 paired samples).
- Sampling protocol was standardized across sites: paired fish/microhabitat collections (~70–100 per site), triplicate benthic macroinvertebrate kick samples, and duplicate diel aquatic drift samples.
- **Data processing status**: All datasets except the two most recent samplings had been entered onto the ASU mainframe computer for analysis. Preliminary statistical work included principal components analysis (PCA), analysis of variance (ANOVA), and chi-square tests examining relationships between microhabitat variables and fish occurrence/abundance data.
- **Definitive statistical analysis was deferred** to the next quarterly reporting period, pending completion of remaining samples and additional technical support allocated in September.
- **Planned collaboration**: Dr. William Matthews (University of Oklahoma) scheduled to visit ASU and study sites in October 1987 to consult on data analysis and help design pilot experimental manipulation studies, contingent on continued funding.
- Report distribution included Arizona Game and Fish Department (Nongame program), ASU Office of Research and Development Administration (ORDA), New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (D.L. Propst), and USFWS Region II (G.L. Burton), indicating a multi-agency collaborative framework for the [[Spikedace and Loach Minnow Study]].
