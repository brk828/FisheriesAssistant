# Cottonwood Springs Cooperative Agreement — Biological Evaluation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #freshwater-ecology #cienega-ecosystems

## Executive Summary

This 1993 Intra-Service Section 7 Biological Evaluation assesses impacts of a Partners for Wildlife Cooperative Agreement at **Cottonwood Spring** (Sonoita Creek, Santa Cruz County, Arizona) on the endangered **[[Gila Topminnow]]** and several candidate species, including the **[[Huachuca Water Umbel]]**, **[[Huachuca Springsnail]]**, and **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]**. The proposed action—fencing to exclude livestock from a cienega wetland, installation of a solar-powered water pump and pipeline system for livestock drinkers, and long-term habitat restoration—was determined **"likely to adversely affect"** the Gila topminnow in the short term (via water diversion and trench construction) but expected to produce **significant long-term benefits** through habitat protection and livestock exclusion. The site is ecologically significant as one of only three natural, mosquitofish-free populations of Gila topminnow remaining in the United States.

## Background & Description

- **Site**: Cottonwood Spring is a 20-acre privately owned parcel (owner: Davis Merwin) along Sonoita Creek, secured under a 20-year Partners for Wildlife Cooperative Agreement (signed July 1, 1992) between the landowner, [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
- **Target taxon**: **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), family **[[Poeciliidae]]**, listed endangered March 11, 1967; no critical habitat designated.
  - Small livebearing fish, 25–50 mm in length (Minckley 1973).
  - Native range: Gila, Sonora, and de la Concepción River drainages (Arizona, New Mexico, Sonora, Mexico).
  - Historically one of the most common fishes in the Gila River system (Hubbs and Miller 1941).
  - Currently reduced to **9 naturally occurring populations** in the U.S. and ~20 reintroduced populations.
  - Cottonwood Spring hosts one of only **3 natural, mosquitofish-free** populations — considered critical for the species' long-term survival.
- **Co-occurring native fish**: **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — the only other fish species present at the site.
- **Primary threat species**: **[[Western Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*), a non-native competitor/predator implicated in range-wide topminnow decline.
- **Associated candidate/listed taxa**:
  - **[[Huachuca Water Umbel]]** (*Lilaeopsis schaffneriana recurva*) — Category 1 candidate; small semi-aquatic perennial plant (leaves <3 mm diameter, ~50 mm tall); endemic to cienega habitats between 3,500–6,500 ft elevation; known from only 9 U.S. sites.
  - **[[Huachuca Springsnail]]** (*Pyrgulopsis thompsoni*) — Category 1 candidate; family Hydrobiidae; shell 1.7–3.2 mm; known from 5 cienega localities in SE Arizona + 2 in Sonora, Mexico; occupies shallow rocky seep zones near spring sources.
  - **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]** (*Empidonax traillii extimus*) — Category 1 candidate, state-listed endangered in Arizona; riparian obligate nesting in dense willow/mesquite/tamarisk thickets 13–23 ft tall.
  - Other candidate species listed in the action area: Northern gray hawk, Northern Mexican garter snake, Golden aster, Bartram stonecrop, Lowland leopard frog, Canyon spotted whiptail, Yellow-billed cuckoo, Buff-breasted flycatcher.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology**: Cottonwood Spring discharges at an average rate of **75 gallons per minute (gpm)**. The proposed pump system is designed to divert a maximum of **6 gpm** — always under 10% of total spring flow — for two livestock drinkers, seasonally operated (fall for north drinker; November–April for south drinker).
- **Habitat type**: Cienega wetland and adjacent mesquite bosque, fed by the spring and grading into Sonoita Creek's downstream channel — the entire spring-to-channel corridor supports Gila topminnow across springhead, canal, cienega, and downstream reaches.
- **Degradation drivers (regional, historical)**: Water diversion, stream downcutting, backwater draining, vegetation clearing, channelization, impoundment, and livestock overgrazing have driven range-wide decline of Gila topminnow and cienega-dependent species.
- **Restoration actions**:
  - Livestock exclusion fencing around cienega and mesquite bosque (AGFD wildlife-fence specifications; barbless top/bottom wires).
  - Removal of an existing fenceline crossing the cienega; new fencing to protect slopes above the cienega and reduce turbidity inputs to Sonoita Creek.
  - Buried 1.25-inch galvanized pipeline (min. 12 inches deep, hand-trenched, 4–6 inch trench width) constructed in late spring (post-runoff, pre-monsoon) to minimize cienega disturbance; trench backfilled and resodded.
  - Drinkers equipped with float valves and covered mechanisms to prevent breakage; drained when unused to prevent mosquito propagation.
- **Monitoring program** (TNC + AGFD, under Wildlife Management Plan):
  - Photomonitoring and visual surveys for Gila topminnow, Huachuca water umbel, and Huachuca springsnail.
  - Presence/absence tracking of Gila topminnow vs. longfin dace population trends and distributional shifts.
  - AGFD holds netting/voucher-specimen permits; TNC does not net fish.
  - Water umbel: transect-based patch location, length/width, and leaf density measurements (no collection).
  - Springsnail: visual surveys plus inspection of pump filtration unit for entrained snails (returned to spring mouth; no collection).
  - Hydrology: base flow velocity measured biannually at spring mouth; wetted-area extent monitored quarterly relative to pre-project baseline.

## Key Findings

- **Effect determination (Gila topminnow)**: **"Is likely to adversely affect"** — formal consultation requested.
  - Short-term risks: entrainment/mortality in pipeline and drinkers during water diversion; mortality/habitat disturbance during pipeline trench excavation across the cienega.
  - Mitigations: microfilter and metal screen on water intake to reduce fish/snail entrainment; hand-trenching timed to dry-season window; limited diversion volume (<10% of total flow; ≤1,500 gal/day at peak livestock use).
  - Long-term outlook: **Net benefit** expected from livestock removal, reduced grazing-driven turbidity/erosion, and cienega habitat recovery.
- **Effect determination (Huachuca Water Umbel)**: "Not likely to adversely affect" — concurrence/informal consultation requested. Minor risk of individual plant loss during trenching; annual monitoring planned to detect hydrology-driven population effects.
- **Effect determination (Huachuca Springsnail)**: "Is likely to adversely affect" — concurrence requested. Risks from filter entrainment and trench construction; but diversion of only ~10% of spring flow deemed unlikely to significantly affect the population long-term.
- **Effect determination (Southwestern Willow Flycatcher)**: "Not likely to adversely affect" — informal consultation/concurrence requested. Project does not remove riparian woody habitat; livestock exclusion expected to promote natural regeneration of nesting habitat and reduce nest disturbance risk.
- **Species significance**: Cottonwood Spring's Gila topminnow population is characterized as a **large, natural, mosquitofish-free population** of exceptional conservation value given the species' overall rarity (9 natural U.S. populations remaining).
- **Regulatory outcome**: Because adverse effects to the endangered Gila topminnow were anticipated, the Arizona Ecological Services Office formally requested that the Albuquerque, NM Field Office prepare an **Intra-Service Biological Opinion** for the project.
- **Broader conservation context**: The agreement functioned as a pilot case for the **Partners for Wildlife** program, pairing private landowner cooperation with TNC and USFWS to protect a multi-species cienega ecosystem — a habitat type recognized as historically declining and disproportionately important to endemic aquatic and semi-aquatic taxa in the American Southwest.
