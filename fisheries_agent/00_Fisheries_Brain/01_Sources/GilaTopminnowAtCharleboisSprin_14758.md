# Gila Topminnow at Charlebois Spring

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #gila-topminnow

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Forest Service memorandum, dated May 26, 1993, reports a site check of [[Charlebois Spring]] in the [[Superstition Wilderness]] on April 21, 1993, to confirm the presence of [[Gila Topminnow]]. The fish had been stocked in 1983 with 200 individuals from [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]] and were recorded as extirpated by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] in 1985. The 1993 survey found an estimated 50 to 100 fish in one pool about 300 feet downstream of the spring source, with no current-year reproduction observed. Habitat conditions were judged compatible with the species, and the document recommends no specific management action beyond continued observation.

## Background & Description

- **Author and recipient:** Jerome Stefferud, Fishery Biologist, to the Range, Wildlife, Fisheries Staff Officer, [[Tonto National Forest]], Mesa Ranger District.
- **Survey team (April 21, 1993):** Don Van Driel, Russ Orr, Pete Libby, Lisa Bizios, Michael Burroughs, and the author.
- **Access:** About a 3.5-hour ride from the First Water trailhead via Forest Trail 104, in the LaBarge Creek drainage.
- **Spring stream:** At 2,600 ft elevation, flowing through bedrock and boulders for about 500 feet.
- **Stocking history:** On June 2, 1983, 200 Gila topminnow from Boyce Thompson Arboretum were released in the first pool at the spring source. Stocking was carried out by Jeanette Kaiser and Russ Orr (Tonto National Forest) and Bill Powers (Arizona Game and Fish Department). Bank vegetation density was recorded as moderate and canopy cover as sparse at that time.
- **Documentation:** Ten Gila topminnow were preserved as voucher specimens. Three larval red-spotted frogs (*Bufo punctatus*) were collected for identification. Eleven photographs (Figures 1–11, dated 1982 and 1993) accompany the report.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pool occupied in 1993:** About 300 feet downstream of the spring source, measuring 10 × 25 × 2.5 ft (units as printed). Substrate was sand over bedrock, and aquatic vegetation was mostly algae. Emergent cattail (*Typha*) was sparse.
- **Cover use:** Fish used floating algae or stayed deep in the water column.
- **Unoccupied habitat:** Two to three other pools of similar size in the drainage were available but had no fish. District staff reported never seeing fish in them.
- **Riparian vegetation:** Sycamore, cottonwood, willow, walnut, oak, wild grape, seep willow, and other shrubs typical of Sonoran desert elevations. Bank vegetation and canopy cover were dense overall. The occupied pool had less canopy cover and sparser bank shrubs than the rest of the channel.
- **Grazing:** The Superstition Allotment has not been grazed since the late 1980s. Comparison with 1982 photos and descriptions showed no apparent change in vegetation composition or site condition at the spring.
- **Recreational use:** Fire rings, litter, and bare, compacted soil indicated moderate trail-user activity. Boulders protected the streambanks and spring from livestock and human access. The author judged this use not incompatible with Gila topminnow habitat needs.
- **Other riparian areas:** Sites along the trail route showed improved riparian conditions, including herbaceous reproduction and growth.

## Key Findings

1. **Persistence despite a 1985 extirpation record:** The 1985 Arizona Game and Fish Department check found no fish, but the 1993 survey confirmed an estimated 50 to 100 Gila topminnow in the pool about 300 feet below the spring. No formal monitoring occurred between 1985 and 1993, though District staff were aware fish were still present.
2. **Limited reproduction:** No current-year young were noted. Some males were beginning to darken and follow females, which the author described as early breeding behavior.
3. **Restricted distribution within the drainage:** Fish were confined to a single pool, and the 2–3 other similar pools remained unoccupied, a pattern the author considered consistent over time.
4. **Habitat judged suitable:** Canopy cover, shrubby riparian vegetation, boulder protection of banks, and the absence of grazing since the late 1980s were considered compatible with species needs.
5. **Voucher material collected:** Ten Gila topminnow were preserved as voucher specimens, and three red-spotted frog larvae were collected for identification.
6. **Recommendation context:** The memo gives no formal management prescription. It documents current conditions and the species' status for the Forest Service and partner agencies, including the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Phoenix Ecological Services office and [[Arizona State University]] (W. L. Minckley).
