# Native Fishes of Tonto Creek, Tonto National Forest, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department report, prepared by Francisco J. Abarca and David A. Weedman (November 1993), surveys the stream fishes of the [[Tonto Creek]] drainage in western [[Gila County]], Arizona. Sampling ran from August 1991 to June 1992 under a Challenge Cost-Share Agreement with the [[Tonto National Forest]]. The study found five native and 11 nonnative species, with natives still numerically dominant (94% upper basin, 86% lower basin). The authors conclude the native fauna is reduced by habitat modification and nonnative invasion following construction of [[Roosevelt Lake]], yet still shows high diversity and abundance compared with other Arizona streams. The report closes with management and research recommendations tied to the [[Tonto Creek Resource Development Plan]].

## Background & Description

**Historical context.** Early accounts describe Tonto Creek as timbered and perennially flowing, with fish over a foot long easily caught. By the early 1920s, overgrazing, drought, cutting and floods had left the channel incised with gravel (Croxen 1926).

**Historic fauna.** Twelve native species may have been present historically, including [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Topminnow]] (federally listed), [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]. Only the razorback sucker was previously recorded in the drainage among the big-river fishes.

**Stocking history.** The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] began stocking in 1917. Most stocked fish were [[Rainbow Trout]], with [[Brown Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Smallmouth Bass]] and razorback suckers also introduced (Appendix A records annual numbers from 1933 to 1991).

**Study area.** The basin covers 955 square miles. Tonto Creek is a third-order stream rising at 6,800 ft on the Mogollon Rim and running about 50 miles to Roosevelt Lake (2,135 ft). Average gradient is 9.3 ft/mile (1.8%), but the lower 17 miles average only 2.3 ft/mile (0.4%). Flow is perennial in the upper reach and seasonally intermittent below Gun Creek. The hydrograph shows bimodal peaks: winter/spring snowmelt and late-summer monsoon floods. Riparian vegetation is dominated by [[Fremont Cottonwood]], [[Goodding Willow]], mesquite and the burrobrush/seepwillow association.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods.** A total of 27 person-days of sampling was conducted across four months (August 1991, November 1991, February 1992, June 1992). Twenty-one sites on Tonto Creek and nine sites on five tributaries were sampled. Gear included seines and a 12-volt backpack electrofisher. Fish over 100 mm were measured, vouchers were deposited at the [[Arizona State University Museum of Fishes]], and water temperature, conductivity and pH were recorded. The basin was split into upper and lower sections at Site 17, based on a gradient jump from 0.4% to 1.5% between Sites 17 and 18 and a change in species composition.

**Hydrology.** Monthly mean discharge at the Gun Creek gaging station ranged from 2.2 to 2,268 cfs during water years 1990–1992. Water year 1990 had no flood flows, 1991 had strong spring flows and no summer peak, and 1992 was closer to typical. Summer low flow was consistent across all three years.

**Water quality.** Temperatures ranged from 6 °C (November 1991, Site 19) to 34 °C (August 1991, Site 11). Specific conductance ranged from 140 µS (Site 15, after rain) to 600 µS (Site 14, base flow). Tributary conductance was 340–550 µS. pH was circumneutral to moderately alkaline.

**Habitat use.** Longfin dace preferred runs and pools, and retreated into isolated pools during low water. Desert sucker and rainbow trout were found mainly in riffles, which made up less than 20% of the sampled area. Roundtail chub used a combination of pool and backwater habitats. Interconnected pools (under 30% of sampled area) held 50% or more of individuals of seven species.

**Threats.** Nonnative species that entered from Roosevelt Lake pose a continuing threat in the lower basin. Gravel pit operations destroy floodplains and riparian habitat. Overgrazing by livestock is a recognized major ecosystem threat, and off-road vehicle use affects Houston Creek.

## Key Findings

- **Species count:** Five native and 11 nonnative species were collected. Previous work recorded nine native species (excluding stocked "native" trout) and 13 nonnative species.
- **Relative abundance (Table 1):** Longfin dace, Sonora sucker, desert sucker, speckled dace and roundtail chub were the natives. Native species accounted for 94% of catch in the upper basin (816 fish) and 86% in the lower basin (11,707 fish, 10,191 native). The most abundant overall was longfin dace.
- **Nonnatives:** Red shiner (985 in lower basin) and fathead minnow (231) were the most abundant nonnatives. Seven nonnative species occurred only in the lower basin, while both trout species occurred only in upper Tonto Creek.
- **Possible extirpations:** Spikedace, razorback sucker and Gila chub were recorded historically but not collected during this study. A single museum record of Gila chub exists from Rye Creek, outside its known historic range.
- **Gila topminnow:** Historically abundant in the drainage, it likely disappeared by about 1951. Only mosquitofish have been taken since. A reintroduced population in Ikyler Spring is threatened by red shiners and green sunfish, and none was collected here.
- **Sensitive species:** Roundtail chub was the only sensitive species collected, restricted to canyon reaches (Sites 9–11 and 18) and the Marsh Creek tributary. No federally threatened or endangered species were taken.
- **Colorado squawfish:** An unverified angler report from Roosevelt Lake (August 1991) was received; no specimen exists.
- **Razorback sucker stocking:** More than 35,000 were stocked in 1987–1988, but no recaptures were reported.
- **Rainbow trout:** Of 33 rainbows collected, 11 exceeded 100 mm, with the largest at 210 mm. Their presence likely reflects stocking, hatchery escapes or natural reproduction.
- **Tributaries:** Most had low species richness. Rye Creek held longfin dace and desert sucker plus nonnatives. The Marsh Creek tributary (Site 29A) held roundtail chub.
- **Management recommendations:** (a) periodic drainage-wide surveys; (b) more thorough surveys of Houston and Haigler creeks; (c) evaluate Gila topminnow stocking in Houston Creek and possible ORV limits; (d) detailed study of native fishes in the Hellsgate Wilderness; (e) feasibility study for a fish barrier at the lower canyon; (f) survey Marsh Creek tributaries for roundtail chub.
- **Restoration plan:** The [[Tonto Creek Resource Development Plan]] (USBR-USFS 1990) aims to recover riparian communities through grazing systems, fencing and water sources. It also bans off-road vehicles on Forest land within the Tonto Creek Riparian Unit. The authors recommend aggressive implementation and effectiveness monitoring, citing a "woefully lacking" knowledge base for desert Southwest riparian-stream linkages.
