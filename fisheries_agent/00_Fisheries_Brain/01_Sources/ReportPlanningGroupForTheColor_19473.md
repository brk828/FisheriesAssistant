# Planning Group for the Colorado River Delta and Upper Gulf of California Ecosystem

#ecosystem-management #conservation-planning #binational-cooperation #Colorado-River-Delta

## Executive Summary

This planning document reports on a July 9, 1996 meeting in Guaymas, Sonora, convened by the Planning Group established at a May 15-16, 1996 meeting in Mexicali. The group, coordinated by Carlos Nagel ([[Friends of PRONATURA]]), developed recommendations for creating an interdisciplinary, binational task force to address the integrated [[Colorado River]] fluvial ecosystem, including the delta and [[Upper Gulf of California]]. The document outlines plans for a Spring 1997 Festival/Conference and establishes thematic working groups covering education, economics, scientific research, technical issues, and political/legal matters. The geographic scope encompasses the entire [[Colorado River Basin]], emphasizing the fluvial ecosystem and transcending political boundaries between the United States and Mexico.

## Background & Description

The Planning Group meeting was co-hosted by [[PRONATURA Sonora]] and [[Friends of PRONATURA]] at ITESM (Instituto Tecnológico y de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey) in Guaymas, Sonora. The initiative began in early 1996 in Tucson with a small group but generated unexpected interest, with over 70 participants attending the May meeting. The planning effort addresses the need for coordinated, binational management of the [[Colorado River Delta]] ecosystem, which represents an integrated fluvial system spanning international boundaries.

The geographic area of focus includes:
- The [[Colorado River]]
- The [[Colorado River Delta]]
- The [[Upper Gulf of California]]
- The entire drainage basin of the Colorado River system

Key locations mentioned include the [[Hardy River]], [[Salton Sea]], and [[Santa Clara Slough]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document emphasizes the integrated nature of the [[Colorado River]] fluvial ecosystem, recognizing that effective management requires transcending political boundaries between the United States and Mexico. The ecosystem faces multiple pressures requiring coordinated assessment and monitoring.

Environmental concerns identified include:
- Water quality issues (pesticides, fertilizer residues)
- Need for desalinization
- Habitat restoration requirements
- Conservation of germplasm
- Endangered species protection
- [[Fisheries of the Upper Gulf]]
- Carrying capacity assessments
- Effects of land degradation on productivity
- Industrial discharge regulation
- Ground water management

The planning framework acknowledges the need to support healthy ecosystems while addressing economic, social, and political dimensions of resource management in the binational region.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Regional Task Force Structure:**
The Planning Group recommends creating an interdisciplinary, multicultural Task Force organized as an independent consortium involving public, private, and civic sectors. This task force would:

1. Develop capability for rapid assessments and coordinate long-term monitoring throughout the system
2. Gestate a multinational, multicultural process potentially leading to an institute or consortium
3. Focus on studying, moderating, advocating consensus, and promoting conflict transformation for [[Colorado Basin]] ecosystem issues
4. Respond to long-term economic, political, social, scientific, and educational needs
5. Conduct environmental monitoring on a broadly coordinated regional basis

**Planned Conference Format (Spring 1997):**
- Day 1: Festival Event
- Day 2 AM: Plenary session
- Day 2 PM: Thematic breakout groups
- Day 3 AM: Create task force
- Day 3 PM: Work with foundation representatives on project proposals

**Five Thematic Working Groups Established:**

1. **Education/Public Information**: General environmental education, information dissemination, network creation, disaster prevention, tours and field trips

2. **Economic**: Financial aspects (multilateral funding, cost sharing, true costs of water, social costs), entrepreneurial activities (ecotourism, sister cities), civic foundations

3. **Scientific/Research**: 
   - Hydrologic: water quality monitoring, pesticides, fertilizer residues, desalinization
   - Biologic: ecosystem health, germplasm conservation, habitat restoration, carrying capacity, endangered species, [[Upper Gulf fisheries]]

4. **Technical**: Engineering (water reuse, water quality, water flows, alternative sewage treatment), comprehensive mapping of entire system

5. **Political/Legal**: International treaties (US/Mexico/Native communities), natural resource agreements, ground water regulation, industrial discharge regulation, NAFTA/GATT effects

**Key Action Items:**
- Prepare comprehensive map of entire drainage basin emphasizing fluvial ecosystem
- Compile binational directory of organizations involved in the region
- Develop list of potential funding organizations
- Convene planning meeting September 29, 1996 in Mexicali, BC
- Review specific projects: [[Hardy River]], [[Salton Sea]], [[Santa Clara Slough]], EDF proposal
- Promote implementation of tangible, short-term actions

**Participating Organizations Include:**
[[CEDO]], [[PRONATURA]], [[Conservation International]], [[Arizona State University]], [[University of Arizona]], [[CICESE]], [[Pacific Institute]], [[American Rivers Association]], [[ITESM]], International Boundary and Water Commission (IBWC), Comisión Internacional de Límites y Aguas (CILA), and numerous other academic, governmental, and non-governmental organizations from both the United States and Mexico.

**Financial Status:**
[[Friends of PRONATURA]] coordinated the effort without initial budget allocation and sought financial contributions from participants to continue serving as mediators for the planning process.
