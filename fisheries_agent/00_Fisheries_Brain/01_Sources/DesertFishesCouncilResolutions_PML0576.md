# Desert Fishes Council Resolutions and Bonneville Basin Report (1984–1985)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #salmonidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This document combines two items from the [[Desert Fishes Council]]: two resolutions dated April 5, 1985, and a 1984 fisheries report on the [[Bonneville Basin]] by Terry J. Hickman of Western Ecosystems. The resolutions urge protection of the Yampa River in Colorado and oppose live bait angling in several Colorado River reservoirs and reaches that hold endangered fishes. The report summarizes 1984 management, listing, and recovery work for several native Great Basin and Colorado Basin fishes.

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Council is an organization of more than 400 persons. Its membership is international and includes federal, state, and university scientists, resource specialists, conservation organization members, and concerned individuals. Both resolutions were passed without dissent at its Sixteenth Annual Symposium, held at the Universidad Autónoma de San Luis Potosí, San Luis Potosí, Mexico, on 15–17 November 1984.

The resolutions concern endangered and rare fishes of the upper [[Colorado River]] system:
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Bonytail]] (listed as endangered federally and by Colorado)

The Bonneville Basin covers about 34 million acres across Utah, eastern Nevada, southeastern Idaho, and southwestern Wyoming. The report covers five fishes: [[June Sucker]], [[Least Chub]], [[Bonneville Cutthroat Trout]], and [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Yampa River (Resolution 84-5).** The Yampa River is described as the last largely free-flowing river in southwestern Colorado. It retains natural features needed by the native endangered fishes. The lower Yampa in [[Dinosaur National Monument]] contains critical habitat for spawning of Colorado Squawfish. The resolution states that continued water development in other Colorado rivers threatens these fishes.

**Live bait restrictions (Resolution 84-6).** The resolution covers [[Colorado Division Of Wildlife]] Regulation #103. That regulation allowed the use, possession, and importation of fish, amphibians, and crustaceans in:
- the Colorado River (Mesa County) from I-70 at Palisade to the Utah line
- Rifle Gap Reservoir (Garfield County)
- Rio Blanco Reservoir (Rio Blanco County)

The stated concerns are:
- Live bait raises the chance of establishing nonnative fishes that compete with or prey on early life stages.
- Collection and use of live bait can increase angling pressure and encourage commercial bait dealerships.
- Early life stages are hard to tell apart from each other and from common species, and they suffer higher mortality when collected or used as bait.
- Experimental stocks of young Colorado Squawfish, introduced by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] as part of recovery efforts, would be lost to bait use.
- Piscivorous adult squawfish are vulnerable to hooking or removal from the water.
- Better and less harmful angling methods exist for [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*).

The resolution calls the regulation "an unfortunate reversal" of progressive policy and asks the Colorado Wildlife Commission to rescind it at the earliest possible date.

**Bonneville Basin (1984).** The report notes that Least Chub sites in their historic range were flooded by rising waters of the Great Salt Lake. Work on these fishes took place in the Provo River, Utah Lake, and hatcheries in Logan, Utah.

## Key Findings

**Resolutions (April 5, 1985)**
- The Desert Fishes Council asks the Governor of Colorado and the Director of the Colorado Department of Natural Resources to secure policy protecting the Yampa River and the listed fishes.
- It asks the Colorado Wildlife Commission to rescind Regulation #103 in the listed areas.
- Copies go to the Colorado Division of Wildlife, Dinosaur National Monument's superintendent, and the Colorado congressional delegation.

**Bonneville Basin Report (1984)**
- **June Sucker:** A proposed endangered listing with critical habitat was published in the *Federal Register* on July 2, 1984. A public hearing was held October 11, 1984, in Provo, Utah. The Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR) began a management plan. Adult suckers were trapped in the Provo River and spawned artificially. Larvae were reared at the Logan experimental hatchery and stocked in three ponds near Utah Lake.
- **Least Chub:** Sites were identified outside the flooded historic range. Two more sites are being evaluated.
- **Bonneville Cutthroat Trout:** A proposed threatened listing was sent to the Denver regional office of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in May 1984. The Washington office revised it, and it is under review by the FWS and UDWR. Trout from Trout Creek were taken to the Logan hatchery and will be used for propagation and introductions.
- **Lahontan Cutthroat Trout:** In September 1984, UDWR and BLM personnel electrofished Bettridge Creek and captured 17 fish, including nine under 90 mm total length. This is the first documented natural reproduction in Bettridge Creek. The UDWR is negotiating with a private landowner in Box Elder County, Utah, to use a 5-acre pond for raising Lahontans and collecting eggs for a hatchery program.
