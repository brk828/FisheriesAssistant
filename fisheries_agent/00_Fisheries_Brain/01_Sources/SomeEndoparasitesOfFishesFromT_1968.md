# Some Endoparasites of Fishes from the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #parasitology #Lepocreadiidae #Trematoda #Cestoda #Acanthocephala

## Executive Summary

This 1968 study by Edwards & Nahhas surveyed **236 fishes representing 26 species** collected from the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] (primarily at the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's Tracy Pumping Plant, plus sites on the Middle, Old, Sacramento, San Joaquin, and Calaveras Rivers) between March 1966 and June 1967. The survey documented endoparasitic helminths including three metacercariae, three adult digeneans, several cestodes (adult, immature, and larval), one acanthocephalan, and unreported nematodes/caryophyllaeid cestodes. A major taxonomic contribution is the description of a new trematode species, **[[Lepocreadium Californianum]]**, recovered from [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*), notable as the first freshwater record for the genus *Lepocreadium*, whose members are otherwise strictly marine. The paper also provides a dichotomous key to 23 species of the genus *Lepocreadium*.

## Background & Description

### Trematoda

**[[Posthodiplostomum Minimum Centrarchi]]**
- Encysted metacercaria found in liver and on heart of centrarchid hosts.

**Neascus sp. (from [[Hysterocarpus Traski]])**
- Elongate body; forebody 1.200 × 0.480 mm, hindbody 0.480 × 0.360 mm.
- Oral sucker 0.053 mm diameter (terminal); pharynx 0.044 × 0.031 mm.
- Ventral sucker equatorial, 0.077 × 0.083 mm.
- Holdfast organ posterior to ventral sucker, 0.181 × 0.165 mm, with distinct holdfast gland.
- Bursa copulatrix subterminal, 0.107 × 0.165 mm.
- Reserve bladder branching/anastomosing throughout forebody.
- Encysted on the heart; possibly a new species due to unusual host family ([[Embiotocidae]]).

**Neascus sp. (from [[Lepomis Cyanellus]])**
- Spatulate body, 1.080 mm long; forebody 0.816 × 0.445 mm; hindbody 0.264 × 0.354 mm.
- Oral sucker subterminal, 0.044 × 0.048 mm; pharynx 0.036 mm.
- Ventral sucker 0.060 × 0.067 mm; holdfast organ with serrated margin, 0.132 × 0.107 mm; holdfast gland indistinct.
- Bursa copulatrix oval, subterminal, 0.071 × 0.108 mm.
- Found **unencysted in the vitreous humor of the eye** — a unique infection site.

**[[Pisciamphistoma Stunkardi]]**
- Distinguished from *Paramphistomum* by presence of an esophageal bulb.
- Recovered from posterior intestine; deposited as USNM No. 70959.

**[[Alloglossidium Corti]]**
- Six specimens from *Ictalurus catus*; larger than typical (1.70–2.00 × 0.370–0.430 mm), eggs 28–36 × 15–21 µm.
- Indistinct lobation on anterior pharynx margin noted in all specimens.
- Deposited as USNM No. 70960.

**[[Lepocreadium Californianum]] sp. n. (Holotype: USNM No. 70961)**
- Body oval to pyriform, 1.248–1.536 × 0.576–0.648 mm at ventral sucker level; cuticular spines extend to posterior end; eye spot pigments present.
- Oral sucker 0.156–0.178 × 0.165 mm (subterminal); ventral sucker 0.115–0.119 mm diameter; sucker ratio 1:0.70–0.72.
- Prepharynx 0.027–0.040 mm; pharynx 0.065–0.083 × 0.073–0.081 mm; esophagus 0.042–0.071 mm; ceca long, narrow, extending to posterior end.
- Testes two, oblique, triangular to irregular; **right testis anterior** (a key distinguishing trait vs. related species); cirrus sac well developed with spherical internal seminal vesicle and muscular cirrus (0.206–0.263 mm).
- Ovary pretesticular, entire margin, 0.082–0.107 × 0.091–0.132 mm; seminal receptacle spherical to ovoid.
- Eggs few (max 4), large: opaque eggs 112–128 × 51–72 µm.
- Vitelline follicles large, mostly extracecal, confluent posterior to testes.
- Distinguished from congeners (*L. archosargi*, *L. micropogoni*, *L. caballeroi*, *L. ovale*, *L. setiferoides*, *L. trullaforme*, *L. elongatum*) by reversed gonad position (sinistral ovary, right testis anterior), body shape, vitellaria extent, and egg size/number.

### Cestoda

- **[[Corallobothrium Giganteum]]** and **[[Corallobothrium Fimbriatum]]**: recovered from intestine of *Ictalurus catus* (and *I. punctatus* for *C. fimbriatum*).
- **[[Proteocephalus sp.]]**: 8 larval/immature specimens from *Ictalurus catus* intestine, four simple suckers, no apical organ; 2 additional larvae from *Lepomis gibbosus* liver with nonmuscular apical organ.
- **[[Bothriocephalus sp.]]**: 4 immature worms from intestine; scolex with two elongated bothria and indented apical disc.
- **[[Pelichnibothrium sp.]]**: 3 larval tapeworms from *Salmo gairdnerii* intestine; scolex with four nonserrated sessile bothridia, each with accessory sucker, plus a fifth apical sucker (distinguishing it from *Phyllobothrium*).

### Acanthocephala

- **[[Rhadinorhynchus sp.]]**: single female specimen from *Salmo gairdnerii* intestine, identified by trunk spination; genus is characteristically marine.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collection habitat**: Freshwater to brackish-transition environment of the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]], sampled mainly at the Tracy Pumping Plant fish-salvage facility and several tributary rivers.
- **Host-parasite specificity**: *Posthodiplostomum minimum centrarchi* shows apparent host specificity to Centrarchidae; its absence from *Hysterocarpus traski* (Embiotocidae) supports the description of a separate *Neascus* sp.
- **Marine-freshwater connection**: The discovery of *Lepocreadium californianum* in a young [[Striped Bass]] (~5 inches, ~1 year old) at Tracy is ecologically significant — genus *Lepocreadium* (subfamily Lepocreadiinae) is otherwise strictly marine. The authors infer this represents a recent infection acquired during a temporary marine/estuarine residence by the anadromous host, consistent with Manter's (1962, 1963) observation that freshwater fish harboring Lepocreadiidae parasites usually have marine connections.
- **Rhadinorhynchus sp.** in *Salmo gairdnerii* (steelhead/rainbow trout) similarly reflects a marine-derived acanthocephalan in an anadromous freshwater host, consistent with a prior Oregon record (Shaw, 1947).
- **Introduced hosts**: *Lepomis gibbosus* and *Chaenobryttus gulosus*, both introduced to California from the eastern United States, harbor *Pisciamphistoma stunkardi*, the same parasite-host association as in the species' native range (North Carolina), suggesting co-introduction or parasite transfer.

## Key Findings

- First major freshwater fish parasite survey in California since [[Haderlie 1953]] (which recorded 59 helminth/copepod/hirudinian species from 2,010 fish, 36 species, 11 families in northern California).
- Description of **new species [[Lepocreadium Californianum]]** — first record of genus *Lepocreadium* from a freshwater fish host, highlighting a marine-to-freshwater parasite dispersal via an anadromous host (striped bass).
- New host record (marked with asterisk in original text): *Archoplites interruptus* as host for *Posthodiplostomum minimum centrarchi*.
- Identification of a possible undescribed *Neascus* sp. from *Hysterocarpus traski* (heart) due to lack of prior Embiotocidae host records for *Posthodiplostomum*.
- Report of an unusual, unencysted ocular infection site (vitreous humor) for a *Neascus* metacercaria in *Lepomis cyanellus*.
- A comprehensive taxonomic key to 23 valid species of *Lepocreadium* Stossich, 1903, is provided, clarifying generic boundaries with *Lepidapedon*, *Opechona*, and *Pseudocreadium*.
- Correction of a prior misidentification: Haderlie's (1953) *Phyllobothrium*-like larvae from *Oncorhynchus tshawytscha* are argued to actually belong to **[[Pelichnibothrium sp.]]** based on the presence of a fifth apical sucker.
- Nematodes and caryophyllaeid cestodes were excluded from formal treatment due to poor specimen preservation, indicating incomplete parasite inventory of the system.
