# Discovery of the Percid Genus *Ammocrypta* in the Apalachicola Drainage, Florida

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae

## Executive Summary
This note documents the first confirmed occurrence of the sand darter genus [[Ammocrypta]] in the [[Apalachicola River]] drainage, Florida — a significant eastward range extension for the genus in the Gulf coastal region. Two specimens of [[Ammocrypta bifascia]] (Florida sand darter) were identified: one collected by the authors on 24 June 1977 near Woodruff Dam (Gadsden County), and a previously unreported specimen collected on 11 August 1974 further downstream near Florida Highway 20 (Liberty County). The species had not been included in prior faunal surveys of the Apalachicola River (e.g., [[Yerger 1977]]), highlighting the difficulty of detecting rare, burrowing, sand-dwelling darters even with substantial collecting effort.

## Background & Description
- **Specimen 1 (UF 24415):** 48.7 mm SL female, collected 24 June 1977, ~400 m below Woodruff Dam, Gadsden County, FL.
- **Specimen 2 (FSU 23263):** 53.8 mm SL male, collected 11 August 1974, ~0.4 km N of Florida Highway 20, Liberty County, FL.

**Meristic data (following methods of [[Williams 1975]]):**
| Character | Value |
|---|---|
| Dorsal fin elements | IX-11 |
| Anal fin elements | I-10 |
| Left pectoral fin rays | 12 |
| Lateral line scales | 73 and 75 |
| Transverse scale rows | 3 and 5 |
| Caudal peduncle (midventral) | Unscaled |

These counts closely approximate typical western Florida populations of *A. bifascia*, with the exception of **higher lateral line scale counts** in the Apalachicola specimens, suggesting possible local population differentiation.

*A. bifascia* is described as a form closely allied to the more westerly [[Ammocrypta beani]] (naked sand darter), from which it was recently split as a distinct species by [[Williams 1975]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Sand-gravel substrate, moderate current, ~1 m depth.
- **Hydrological conditions at capture:** Flow from Woodruff Dam = 336 cm³/s; river stage = 13.6 m (per [[Corps of Engineers]] data).
- **Associated species:** Only co-occurring darter recorded was [[Percina nigrofasciata]] (blackbanded darter).
- **Behavior:** *Ammocrypta* species are burrowing, sand-dwelling darters, notoriously difficult to detect via standard collecting methods — populations may only be reliably sampled following periods of high reproductive success, or with specialized collecting techniques targeting burrowed individuals.
- **Rarity:** The species was entirely absent from many prior collections at the Woodruff Dam locality and elsewhere in the lower river, indicating low population density or patchy distribution rather than true absence.

## Key Findings
- **First record** of genus *Ammocrypta* in the [[Apalachicola Drainage]], a major river system draining western Georgia and portions of Alabama and Florida.
- The species was **absent from Yerger's (1977)** comprehensive faunal list of Apalachicola River fishes, underscoring how rare/cryptic taxa can be overlooked even in well-studied systems.
- Two temporally (1974 vs. 1977) and geographically separated specimens argue **against bait-bucket introduction** as an explanation — darters are rarely used as bait locally.
- Proposed biogeographic explanations:
  1. **Natural coastwise dispersal** during Late Pleistocene sea-level reductions, or
  2. **Stream capture / flooding connections** between the Apalachicola and the adjacent [[Choctawhatchee Drainage]] to the west.
- This finding **revises previous zoogeographic models** (e.g., [[Bailey, Winn, and Smith 1954]]; [[Yerger and Suttkus 1962]]) that posited a faunal break between the Choctawhatchee and Apalachicola drainages — *Ammocrypta* can no longer be considered restricted by this barrier.
- Parallel case: In the [[Tennessee River Drainage]], *Ammocrypta* was rediscovered at a heavily sampled locality **85 years after its initial discovery** ([[Starnes et al. 1977]]), reinforcing the pattern that sand darters are easily missed despite intensive survey effort.
- **Future research recommendation:** Concentrated, specialized collecting in suitable sandy habitats (e.g., [[Chipola River]], unimpounded reaches of the [[Flint River]] in Georgia) may reveal additional populations of *A. bifascia* within the Apalachicola system.
