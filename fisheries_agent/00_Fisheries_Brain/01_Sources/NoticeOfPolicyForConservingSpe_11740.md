# Notice of Policy for Conserving Species Listed Under the Endangered Species Act While Providing Recreational Fisheries Opportunities

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice, published June 3, 1996, sets out a joint policy by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Interior) and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (Commerce), collectively "the Services." The policy addresses the conservation needs of species listed, or proposed for listing, under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) while providing for the continuation and enhancement of [[Recreational Fisheries]]. It was issued at the direction of Section 4 of [[Executive Order 12962]] and takes effect July 3, 1996. The policy does not alter any ESA obligation. It is a framework of guidance intended to reduce conflicts between ESA administration and recreational fishing, to improve consistency between and within the two agencies, and to strengthen collaboration with Federal, State, and Tribal partners.

## Background & Description

The document is a policy notice with a comment-response section, not a research report. It contains no original field data. Its structure is:

- **Background:** Statutory context under the ESA and other Federal aquatic-resource laws, the state of North American aquatic ecosystems, and the economic value of recreational fishing.
- **Summary of Comments and Recommendations:** Responses to public comments on the draft policy, which was published December 13, 1995 (60 FR 64070).
- **Policy:** Four numbered sets of actions, covering goals and objectives, recovery participation, public outreach, and evaluation.
- **Scope, Authorities, and Signatures:** The policy applies to all pertinent Service activities relating to recreational fisheries and ESA implementation. It was signed by Mollie H. Beattie (Director, Fish and Wildlife Service, dated May 14, 1996) and Rolland A. Schmitten (Assistant Administrator for Fisheries, NMFS, dated May 20, 1996).

Editors were David Harrelson (Fish and Wildlife Service, Division of Endangered Species), Bob Batky (Fish and Wildlife Service, Division of Fish Hatcheries), and Marta Nammack (NMFS, Endangered Species Division).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice presents the following conservation status and ecological background:

- **Habitat degradation:** Most North American aquatic environments and biological communities have been significantly altered by human impacts. Degraded and altered habitats are the most frequently cited factors in the extirpation and decline of federally protected aquatic species.
- **Listed species:** As of May 1, 1996, 106 fish taxa and 57 freshwater mussel species were on the Federal threatened or endangered list (50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12).
- **Imperilment:** Approximately 36% of U.S. fishes, 64% of crayfishes, and 69% of freshwater mussels are considered imperiled or extinct. Data came from the [[NatureServe Network]] (National Network of Natural Heritage Programs and Conservation Data Centers) and The Nature Conservancy, Eastern Regional Office.
- **Management lessons:** Habitat alteration, heavy fishing pressure, and introduction of non-native species have caused unexpected negative effects on other ecosystem components. The notice calls for an [[Ecosystem Approach]] grounded in species' life histories, habitat requirements, and ecosystem processes.
- **Natural factors:** Environmental factors beyond human control, such as [[El Nino]] events, have also contributed to declines. The Services say they must therefore focus on reducing mortality from controllable factors.
- **Economic value of recreation:** About 50 million anglers spend $24 billion annually. Recreational angler spending generated over $69 billion in total economic output in 1991, over $2.1 billion in Federal tax revenues, and about 1.3 million jobs nationwide.

## Key Findings

**Legal framework and Service commitments**

- The Services affirm that the policy does not diminish or abrogate Federal responsibilities under the ESA, including Sections 6 (Cooperation with the States), 7 ([[Interagency Consultation]]), 9 (Prohibited Acts), and 10 (Exceptions).
- The ESA's stated purpose is conservation, defined as the use of all methods necessary to bring listed species to the point where protective measures are no longer needed.
- Incidental take permits would be issued only when actions are not likely to jeopardize a species' continued existence or recovery. Direct take is authorized only for research and enhancement purposes, and, for threatened species, under a [[Section 4(d) Rule]] conservation plan.
- For proposed species, Federal agencies must still confer on actions likely to jeopardize them.

**Policy actions (summarized from the four numbered sets)**

1. **Goals and consistency (Point 1):** Develop mutually accepted goals among agencies, States, Tribes, conservation groups, and anglers. Ensure consistency within and between the Services, promote information sharing, and inform Federal and non-Federal entities of ESA requirements.
2. **Recovery participation (Point 2):** Involve all interested parties in recovery planning. Encourage proactive habitat conservation. Support management compatible with recovery and with existing recreational fisheries. Identify habitat restoration priorities. Evaluate proposed [[Non-Native Species Introduction]] or hybrid introductions based on genetics, disease control, ecological principles, recovery objectives, and socio-economic objectives. Periodically adjust recovery actions to minimize adverse effects on recreational fisheries, and eliminate unnecessary recovery-based restrictions in unoccupied historical range. Encourage State participation through Section 6 grants.
3. **Public understanding (Point 3):** Inform anglers and the public about ESA topics such as incidental take, 4(d) rules, and [[Habitat Conservation Planning]]. Involve the public in identifying enhancement opportunities. Assist in providing comparable alternative angling opportunities when existing ones are curtailed, contingent on appropriations.
4. **Evaluation and restoration (Point 4):** Work with the recreational fisheries community to evaluate progress. Restore and enhance aquatic habitats to conserve listed species while increasing fishing opportunities.

**Key themes from the comment responses**

- Of 28 comment letters, 23 were supportive, 4 were critical, and 1 expressed no position.
- The Services clarified that the policy does not extend fishery goals beyond recovery criteria in recovery plans.
- The Services will not modify necessary recovery tasks merely to minimize impacts on recreational fisheries. They will correct unnecessary impediments where possible.
- The policy treats "stakeholders" and "partners" inclusively, covering conservation groups, local governments, land and water users, power consumers, and others, rather than only recreational anglers.
- Recreational fishing is not always the cause of decline, and it is not necessarily an inherent threat to listed species.
- The [[Snake River Salmon Recovery Plan]] draft recommends reducing mortality from harvest, hydropower operations, habitat degradation, and hatchery practices. The Services note that no single sector is typically responsible for a species' recovery.
- Hatchery releases are a known impediment to wild salmonid recovery, which is why the Services emphasize that decisions unpopular with some constituents may still be necessary for recovery.
- The Services acknowledge that the policy's effectiveness depends on implementation, and they have identified mechanisms to pursue.
