# Parker Canyon Lake Northern Pike Management Decision Process and Timeline

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) correspondence, dated November 29, 1999 and December 3, 1999, sets out the process, timeline, and candidate management alternatives for [[Northern Pike]] established in [[Parker Canyon Lake]]. The pike population resulted from illegal, unauthorized stocking and threatens the lake's management as a year-round [[Coldwater Fishery]] and native fish management in the [[Santa Cruz River]] watershed. The Region V plan targets a preliminary decision memorandum by end of February 2000, a recommended preferred alternative by March 1, 2000, and implementation planning after about May 1, 2000.

## Background & Description

The [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*, family Esocidae) is a predatory freshwater fish. In this document it is discussed as an introduced, non-native species in Parker Canyon Lake. The document describes it as established and successfully increasing in numbers, and notes that personal communications with other agencies revealed "total incompatibility" with stocked [[Rainbow Trout]] management.

The issue was first recognized in October 1999. Public awareness of the introduction later moved the decision process onto a fast track. The original Region V plan aimed to assess likely impacts on sportfish and native fish and to avoid radical steps such as draining the lake.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Threat to fisheries:** Pike undermine the lake's year-round coldwater fishery management and pose a possible risk to native fish in the Santa Cruz River watershed.
- **Literature outlook:** Most of the reviewed literature indicates low or no success for partial or total removal actions.
- **Jurisdiction:** Some alternatives may involve the [[Coronado National Forest]], requiring coordination with the Sierra Vista Ranger District and [[NEPA Compliance]], and potential effects on Forest permittees and concessionaires.
- **Stakeholders:** Potential external challenges and community economic effects were flagged, particularly for the most complex options.

## Key Findings

**Decision timeline**

1. Draft management assessment of Northern Pike completed by December 31, 1999.
2. Draft assessment distributed for peer review by mid-January 2000.
3. Decision team formed to review findings, develop management alternatives, and recommend a preferred alternative by March 1, 2000. The team should include a Forest Service representative.
4. Decision memo published and distributed, followed by a 45-day public review and comment period, possibly with public meetings.
5. Implementation planning initiated after approximately May 1, 2000.

**Four management options considered**

1. **No action / manage a pike fishery:** Described as unattractive; would require changes to rules, orders, and management focus.
2. **Annual partial control of pike:** Planning would begin May 2000, followed by an EA/BE and NEPA documentation. Implementation possible as early as June 2001.
3. **Habitat removal:** Removing habitat required by northern pike.
4. **Total removal:** Via chemical application (piscicide), draining, or a combination of chemicals and drawdown. This is the most complex option, with the highest cost, longest timeline, and greatest risk of external challenge. A best-case scenario targets completion by fall 2001 for first application.

Evaluation logic: as alternatives grow more complex, costs and time rise and the probability of achieving planned results falls.

**Interim actions (Region V)**

- Intensified survey and inventory work at the lake, with unscheduled field sampling planned for January 2000.
- Creel surveys greatly intensified between January and June 2000, also querying anglers on the controversy.
- Polling of Northern Pike managers in northern tier states and review of the [[Esocid Literature]] to evaluate vulnerability and management range.
- Tracking size and age information of removed fish to test whether pre-spawning or spawning-period trapping can target individuals based on vulnerability to gear.

**Personnel**

Memos were addressed to Larry Riley (Chief, Fisheries Branch) and Bruce D. Taubert (Assistant Director, Wildlife Management Division), prepared by Will Hayes (Fisheries Program Manager, Region V), with copies to regional and branch staff.
