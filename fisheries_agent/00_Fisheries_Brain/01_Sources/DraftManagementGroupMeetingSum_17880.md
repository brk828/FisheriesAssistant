# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program Management Group Meeting Summary - July 11, 1990

#administrative #recovery-program #endangered-species #Colorado-River #program-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a draft meeting summary and associated materials from the July 11, 1990 Management Group meeting of the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]]. The program is a cooperative effort among federal agencies (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, Western Area Power Administration), three states ([[Colorado]], [[Utah]], [[Wyoming]]), water developers, and environmental groups to recover four endangered fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The meeting focused on reviewing and modifying the FY-91 Work Plan with a budget of $2.56 million, addressing organizational structure, and coordinating recovery activities across the Upper Colorado River Basin.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program was established through a cooperative agreement signed in January 1988 by the Secretary of the Interior and the states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming. The program aims to recover endangered fish species while allowing water development to proceed in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The Management Group serves as an intermediate coordinating body between the Recovery Implementation Committee (policy level) and technical working groups.

The FY-91 budget totals $2,557,900 from the following sources: Fish and Wildlife Service ($654,900, though noted as $30,000 below agreed amount), Bureau of Reclamation ($1,636,500 from CRSP power revenues), Colorado ($113,500), Utah ($79,700), and Wyoming ($25,200). Additional contributions come from water users through Section 7 depletion charges. The program operates through six recovery elements: Program Management, Habitat Management (defining and acquiring flows), Habitat Development, Stocking Native Fishes, Nonnative Fish Control, and Research/Monitoring/Data Management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The program addresses four endangered fish species endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system. Key habitat areas include the [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Colorado River]] 15-mile reach, [[White River]], and various tributaries. Major water infrastructure affecting fish habitat includes [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Aspinall Unit]] dams, and numerous diversions.

Critical ecological issues addressed include: flow regime alterations from dam operations, habitat availability (particularly backwater nursery habitat for young-of-year fish), competition and predation from nonnative warmwater game fish, sediment transport changes, water temperature modifications, and fragmentation of populations. The program recognizes that recovery requires both habitat protection/restoration and active propagation efforts, including genetics management to maintain population integrity.

## Key Findings

**FY-91 Work Plan Priorities:**
- [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] cooperative research continues as highest priority ($308,800), with 5-year study plan testing hypotheses about seasonal flow recommendations and effects on native versus nonnative fish communities
- [[Yampa River]] flow quantification ($58,800) and feasibility study for compatible reservoir development ($130,000 total, jointly funded)
- [[Colorado River]] 15-mile reach flow recommendations ($75,400) and water availability study ($47,000) to support instream flow appropriations
- Young-of-year [[Colorado Squawfish]] backwater habitat mapping using video imagery ($157,000)
- Endangered fish propagation and genetics management ($347,000 total across multiple projects), including [[Razorback Sucker]] operations at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] and broodstock development

**Major Budget Concerns:**
- FWS contribution $30,000 short due to Congressional appropriation difficulties; program will seek FY-93 base funding increase
- Approximately $100,000 in FY-90 Gila taxonomy study funds may not be obligated before fiscal year end
- [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] contract overrun of approximately $26,000
- Declining discretionary funds limit ability to respond to new initiatives
- Overhead charge of 17.65% on Bureau funds transferred to FWS ($119,100 in FY-91) reduces available project funding; waiver request to be submitted to Secretary

**Section 7 Consultation Activities:**
- Multiple biological opinions issued for water depletions since January 1988
- Depletion charges at $10/acre-foot generating funds for recovery activities
- Major pending consultations: [[Animas-La Plata Project]] (154,800 ac-ft), [[Flaming Gorge]] operations, [[Sandstone Reservoir]] (30,600 ac-ft)
- Approximately $500,000 in Section 7 funds available in non-interest bearing account; $300,000 potentially stipulated for [[Redlands]] fish passage work

**Organizational Restructuring:**
- Proposed reorganization to abolish Technical Group and create three standing technical committees: Biology Committee, Water Acquisition Committee, and Information and Education Committee
- Planning functions returned to Management Committee (renamed from Management Group)
- Credit for committee participation limited to technical committee chairs only; program management costs to be distributed to individual projects
- Concern expressed about insufficient "pure biological input" and lack of communication between biological researchers and management structure
- Outreach plan requested from Biology Committee to improve researcher involvement

**Propagation Strategy:**
- Population status assessed across basin using categories: SA (sustaining with adequate recruitment), SI (sustaining with inadequate recruitment), RI (relict population), OI (occasional captures), UU (unknown status), XX (not present)
- Propagation priorities vary by status: SA locations receive lowest propagation priority but highest recovery priority; RI and SI locations need immediate augmentation
- Genetics survey and nonlethal tissue sampling procedures under development
- Evaluation of [[Colorado Squawfish]] stocked in [[Kenney Reservoir]] 1958-1990 as sportfish ($20,000)

**Research Priorities Not Funded:**
- Definition of quantitative recovery goals (considered premature without more baseline data)
- [[White River]] work plan development
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] genetics survey (later added using Section 7 funds)
- [[Aspinall]] consultation work plan (later added using Section 7 funds)
- Intensive culture studies for both target species
- Various Gila taxonomy field studies beyond core work

**Specific Study Concerns:**
- Water users expressed concern about high costs in 15-mile reach studies and lack of coordination between [[Yampa River]] and 15-mile reach flow methodology
- Service maintains different methodologies appropriate for different river systems
- Sediment discussion paper to be distributed to Technical Group; ad hoc sediment group to reconvene
- Five-year review of standardized monitoring program to be conducted

**Next Steps:**
- Recovery Implementation Committee meeting scheduled August 22, 1990
- River trip planned August 23-25 through [[Westwater Canyon]] with 36 participants including agency directors and congressional staff
- Next Management Group meeting September 19, 1990 to address Implementation Committee outcomes, reorganization implementation, and FY-91 scope of work modifications
- Certificates of appreciation to be prepared for Reed Harris, Bill McDonald, and Carse Pustmueller
