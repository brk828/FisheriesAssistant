# Late Cenozoic Molluscan History of the San Pedro Valley, Southeastern Arizona

#malacology #marine-biology #non-marine-mollusks #quaternary-paleontology

## Executive Summary

This preliminary study by [[Richard H. Russell]] (with [[Bequaert]] of the University of Arizona) describes fossil non-marine mollusk assemblages from nine Late Cenozoic localities in the [[San Pedro Valley]]. Across these sites, 57 non-marine mollusk species were identified. The assemblages span the [[Blancan Age]] through the [[Rancholabrean Age]] and sub-Recent deposits, and they are used to infer past climate and aquatic habitats. The author stresses that the data are preliminary, and that shell transport and reworking may compromise age and habitat interpretations, particularly in the flood-prone Southwest.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** The San Pedro Valley contains numerous Quaternary localities, made more important by early human sites at [[Murray Springs Site]] and elsewhere. Several sites have been dated by radiocarbon methods, and work by [[Lance]] and [[Lindsay]] and their students relates some to Blancan, Irvingtonian, and Rancholabrean mammal ages.
- **Stratigraphic framework:** The term "[[St. David Formation]]" (proposed by R. S. Gray, 1965) covers Upper Pliocene to Middle Pleistocene fine-grained sediments near Benson and St. David.
- **Methodological assumption:** The study assumes that transport and reworking of shells were minimal. The author notes this assumption probably does not hold in the Southwest, and that quantitative analyses are needed to test it.
- **Age utility:** Non-marine mollusks show little species extinction or origination through the Pleistocene, so they are of limited use for age determination. Speciation occurred mainly where aridity isolated populations, as in the American Southwest.
- **Taxonomy:** The author notes that Hydrobiidae systematics are unsettled and that shell-based identification of hydrobiid species is difficult or impossible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Blancan sites**
- [[Post Ranch]]: Mollusks indicate a more mesothermal climate than today, probably with cooler summers, and a perennial or sub-permanent shallow pond or bog. Annual precipitation may have been slightly greater.
- [[California Wash]]: A hydrobiid snail suggests a permanent, spring-fed, perennial water body. Climate was similar to Post Ranch.
- [[El Paso Gas Line]]: A subhumid, mesothermal climate. Aquatic snails point to a temporary marsh or bog, and land snails suggest scrub or riparian woodland.
- [[Wolf Ranch]]: The most xeric Blancan site. Aquatic species suggest an intermittent stream or marsh.

**Rancholabrean sites**
- [[Choate Ranch-2]]: Climate similar to today, possibly slightly cooler summers. Intermittent stream and considerable riparian vegetation.
- [[Murray Springs Arroyo]]: Climate similar to Choate Ranch-2, with an intermittent stream or marsh.
- [[Murray Springs Wash]] (Units D, E, F): The Unit F mammoth layer implies a perennial, spring-fed marsh or bog. Large numbers of terrestrial species point to a wooded, riparian, subhumid setting. Unit D may have been a shallow, near-shore habitat rather than a lake.

**Late Pleistocene to sub-Recent sites**
- [[Murray Springs Wash]] (Unit G): Increasing aridity over time is suggested, but Unit G has a large proportion of aquatic species, including two no longer found in Arizona. Rapid deposition may have redeposited these species from other sources.
- [[Lehner Mammoth Site]]: Few aquatic species. Terrestrial snails indicate a subhumid climate with marsh or cienega conditions and extensive riparian woodland.
- [[The Narrows]] (San Pedro River): Late Pleistocene or sub-Recent. Aquatic species dominate, and intact valves of [[Sphaerium simile]], a lake-associated clam, suggest limited reworking. Molluscs suggest a lake or impoundment, probably part of a permanent river. This supports the postulated [[Late Pleistocene Lake]] in the valley.

## Key Findings

- **Overall richness:** Nine localities yielded 57 non-marine mollusk species in preliminary work. The author describes this as "as rich a fauna of Pleistocene non-marine mollusks as occurs anywhere in North America."
- **Post Ranch (Blancan):** Nine species, including the extinct [[Omalodiscus pattersoni]].
- **California Wash (Blancan):** Five species added to the ten reported by Gray, for 15 total. This is the most species-rich Blancan site. Most species still live in Arizona, and the OCR text gives the share found in southern Arizona as ".85%."
- **El Paso Gas Line (Blancan):** Ten species, half aquatic. The extinct subspecies *Promenetus exacuous kansasensis* occurs here.
- **Wolf Ranch (Blancan):** Twelve species, about one-third aquatic. Drier than other Blancan sites.
- **Choate Ranch-2 (Rancholabrean):** 13 species, all Recent in Arizona.
- **Murray Springs Arroyo (Rancholabrean):** 13 species; two are not known from Arizona.
- **Murray Springs Wash (Rancholabrean to sub-Recent):** 47 species in total, 42 from the three Rancholabrean units. More than 20 samples were analyzed.
- **Unit F mammoth layer:** 40 species collected, 12 of them aquatic. This is the richest assemblage.
- **Lehner Mammoth Site:** 31 species, of which five are aquatic (16.1%).
- **The Narrows:** 17 species, 11 aquatic (64.7%). Intact valves of *Sphaerium simile* indicate in-place deposition.
- **Dating discrepancy:** The Unit D caption gives 234,000 years BP, while the text cites Haynes (1968) at ca. 30,000 years BP for the same unit. The source does not resolve this.
- **Caveats:** The author states the data are "preliminary at best." Shell transport and reworking in flood-prone streams remain a concern, and hydrobiid identifications are uncertain.
- **Planned work:** The author notes that quantitative analyses are needed to evaluate transport, and that the revised 1971 checklist (May 1971) is the current working list.

**Revised 1971 checklist (summary):** The list contains 57 numbered taxa: 5 bivalves, 18 aquatic gastropods, and 34 land snails and slugs, across 20+ families. Representative entries include *Sphaerium simile*, *Stagnicola elodes*, *Gyraulus parvus*, *Fossaria obrussa*, *Physa virgata*, *Vertigo ovata*, *Gastrocopta pentodon*, and *Helicodiscus singleyanus*.
