# A New Softshell Turtle (Genus Trionyx) from Coahuila, Mexico

#herpetology #trionyx

## Executive Summary

Wees and Legler (1960), in the [[University Of Kansas Science Bulletin]], describe a new softshell turtle, *Trionyx ater* ("Black Softshell"), from the basin of [[Cuatro Ciénegas]] in central Coahuila, Mexico. The species was identified among 14 *Trionyx* specimens collected during a ten-day field party in September 1958 by the [[University Of Kansas Museum Of Natural History]]. The authors separate it from the co-occurring [[Trionyx Spinifer Emoryi]], report partial ecological separation between the two, and note that *T. ater* is the second softshell species known from Mexico.

## Background & Description

- **Taxon described:** *Trionyx ater* sp. nov. (Latin *ater* = black), named for its uniform blackish coloration.
- **Holotype:** KU 46903, alcoholic, female, collected 16 km south of Cuatro Ciénegas on September 6, 1958, by John M. Legler, Wendell L. Minckley, and Robert B. Wimmer (original number JML 1708).
- **Paratypes:** Nine alcoholic specimens (KU 46904–6, 46908–10, 46912 females; KU 46911 male) collected September 6–8, 1958, plus IU 43510 (female), collected 5.7 miles west of Cuatro Ciénegas on July 10, 1958, by Pete S. Chrapliwy and Kenneth L. Williams.
- **Specimen count:** 14 *Trionyx* examined; 5 assigned to *T. s. emoryi*; the remaining nine KU specimens plus IU 43510 represent *T. ater*.
- **Diagnostic characters:**
  1. Uniform blackish carapace, dorsal limbs, neck, and head (no pale/dark pattern)
  2. Ventral surfaces heavily speckled with black
  3. No pale marginal band on carapace (females)
  4. Longitudinal corrugations on the posterior carapace (most females)
  5. No septal ridges in the nostrils of males
  6. Small white tubercles on posterior half of carapace (males)
  7. Ovoid carapace (adult and nearly adult specimens)
- **Holotype morphology:** Carapace width contained in length 1.4 times; height contained in width 2.8 times; bony bridge width 32 mm; least interorbital distance 4 mm. Forefeet and hind feet fully webbed with five digits each. Tail tip to posterior carapace edge 24 mm; cloaca to tail tip 20 mm.
- **Holotype measurements (table):** Carapace length 243 mm, carapace width 178 mm, plastron length 183 mm.
- **Paratype series:** One adult male (mature, with elongate preanal region and developed penis) and eight subadult females.
- **Etymology/vernacular:** Local people call softshells "tortuga blanca" and do not distinguish *T. s. emoryi* from *T. ater*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study area:** Cuatro Ciénegas lies in an intermontane basin at roughly 2400 ft elevation, about 30 miles long (west–east) and 5 to 15 miles wide. The [[Rio Chiquito]] originates in the southwest, receives intermittent tributaries, and exits through an eastern gap, eventually joining the [[Rio Salado]] and draining into the lower [[Rio Grande]] near Zapata, Texas.
- **Ponds:** Central basin is marshy with clear, deep ponds, mainly west and south of town, plus a hot spring about nine km south of town. Ponds were formerly isolated but are now interconnected by man-made ditches. Local residents report water remains in ponds and the Rio Chiquito year-round.
- **Type-locality pond:** About 2.5 acres in area; average depth about six feet, with holes two to three times deeper; water about 80°F and clear. A narrow intermittent channel runs about four miles to the Rio Chiquito.
- **Vegetation:** Submergent stonewort (*Chara zelandica* var. *inconstans*) covers about half the bottom. Water lilies (*Nymphaea* sp.), cattails (*Typha latifolia*), and spike-rushes (*Eleocharis rostellata*) occupy shallows and edges. Surrounding grassy/marshy zone extends several hundred yards, then gives way to xeric cacti, yucca, and mesquite.
- **Sampling:** Hoop-nets baited with canned sardines, rodent bodies, and fresh cichlid fish captured *T. ater* alongside large numbers of [[Pseudemys Scripta]]. Turtles entered nets within 80 minutes of bait placement.
- **Relative abundance:** One *Trionyx* per 4.2 *Pseudemys* in trapping records at the pond.
- **Activity:** Heads were visible at dusk with binoculars; no turtles were seen during daylight. Trapping success dropped sharply in bright sunlight and continued through overcast days.
- **Diet:** The stomach of paratype KU 46908 held 23 larvae of long-eared leaf beetles (Chrysomelidae: *Donacia* sp.) and short root fragments of spike-rushes, probably ingested with the larvae.
- **Reproduction:** The second and third largest paratypes (KU 46906, 46908) had immature ovaries; the holotype is probably also immature. Largest adults may be excluded by hoop-net openings just under one foot wide.
- **Habitat partitioning:** *T. ater* occurred in the pond habitat; *T. s. emoryi* was taken in the Rio Chiquito (30–50 ft wide, swift current, 10 km south of town), with only one emoryi (KU 46907) found at the pond. Similar partitioning occurs between [[Trionyx Ferox]] (ponds/lakes) and *T. s. asper* (fluvial) where their ranges overlap.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Trionyx ater* is described as a full species, distinguished from all other American *Trionyx* by its blackish dorsal coloration, lack of pale marginal rim, absence of septal ridges in males, and longitudinal posterior carapace corrugations.
- **Closest relative:** *T. s. emoryi*, which occurs sympatrically; the authors consider the two full species because no intermediate populations are known.
- **Related species:** *T. ater* is also closely allied to [[Trionyx Ferox]] of Florida (shared dark dorsal coloration and ovoid carapace) and resembles [[Trionyx Muticus]] in smooth male carapace edges and lack of septal ridges in males.
- **Relict status:** *T. ater* is considered a relict population of pre-*spinifer* stock.
- **Mexican diversity:** The report brings the number of softshell turtle species known from Mexico to two (*T. ater* and *T. s. emoryi*).
- **Hybridization potential:** Partial ecological separation is incomplete, so interspecific matings and hybrids could occur. Paratype IU 43510 shows intermediate features and may be a hybrid.
- **Historical record:** [[Miller 1878]] listed *Amyda mutica* from "Mexico" without locality; the authors infer this was a male *T. ater*, since *T. muticus* does not occur in Mexico.
- **Biogeographic context:** Pond vertebrates in the basin were isolated in the past, producing varying degrees of speciation. Modern drainage connections allowed some forms to hybridize with Rio Salado and Rio Grande populations, while *T. ater* kept its distinctness through ecological specialization.
- **Ongoing work:** Webb is conducting a taxonomic study of American *Trionyx*.
