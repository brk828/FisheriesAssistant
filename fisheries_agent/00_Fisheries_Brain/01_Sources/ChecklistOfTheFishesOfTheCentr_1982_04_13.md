## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Stauffer Jenkins Burr Hocutt 1982]] is a **faunal checklist** — an update and expansion of [[Jenkins Lachner and Schwartz 1972]] — cataloguing the freshwater fish fauna of the [[Central Appalachian Mountains]] and [[Northern Appalachian Mountains]]. The list documents **398 fish forms (species and subspecies) plus 5 intergrade/hybrid populations across 28 families**, spanning two major hydrological regions: the **Atlantic Slope** (from the [[Susquehanna River]] in New York/Pennsylvania south to the [[Peedee River]] in North/South Carolina) and the **Ohio River Basin** (from the [[Monongahela River]] in Pennsylvania south to the [[Tennessee River]] in Alabama/Tennessee). The authors deliberately used a **conservative inclusion criterion**: any species with uncertain historic or current presence in a drainage was excluded, and no distinction was drawn between historically extirpated and currently extant populations. The work is intended as a baseline reference for ichthyologists, fisheries scientists, and environmental consultants working across this biogeographically significant region, which is noted for high species diversity and numerous **endemic taxa**.

## Background & Description

As a distributional checklist rather than a taxonomic or morphological study, the source does not provide detailed morphometric or meristic data for individual species. However, it does encode taxonomic structure relevant to identification and classification:

- **Subspecies-level resolution**: Trinomials (subspecies designations) are used only where subspecific distribution could be confidently determined (e.g., *[[Clinostomus funduloides|Clinostomus f. funduloides]]*, *[[Clinostomus funduloides|Clinostomus f. estor]]*, *[[Etheostoma blennioides|Etheostoma b. gutselli]]*, *[[Etheostoma blennioides|Etheostoma b. newmanii]]*).
- **Intergrade/hybrid populations**: Five documented intergrade populations are listed (e.g., *Etheostoma blennioides: newmanii × blennioides*, *Etheostoma nigrum: nigrum × susanae*, *Etheostoma olmstedi: olmstedi × atromaculatum*, *olmstedi × vexillare*), indicating zones of secondary contact and hybridization among darter subspecies within the [[Percidae]].
- **Undescribed forms**: Numerous provisional/undescribed taxa are flagged with informal designations (e.g., *[[Notropis]] sp.* "paleband shiner," "sawfin shiner"; *[[Etheostoma]] (Ulocentra) sp. A–F* including the "golden snubnose darter" and "splendid darter"; *[[Cottus]] sp.* "smoky sculpin"; *Phoxinus cumberlandensis*), reflecting active taxonomic work on cryptic diversity in the region during this period.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist classifies each taxon by **habitat category** and **drainage occurrence status**, providing a structured ecological framework:

### Habitat Categories
- **Big River** — large mainstem riverine habitat
- **Stream** — moderate-order flowing water
- **Creek** — small-order flowing water
- **Lowland / Upland / Montane** — elevation/gradient-based zonation

### Drainage Occurrence Codes
| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| E | Endemic |
| N | Native |
| NP | Probably present, native |
| NI | Native but possibly introduced |
| I | Introduced |
| IP | Probably/possibly introduced |
| Ma | Euryhaline or diadromous |

### Native Extralimital Distribution Codes
| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| So | South on Atlantic Slope |
| No | North on Atlantic Slope |
| O | Predominantly Ohio Basin form |
| M | Lower/central Mississippi Basin |
| G | Gulf of Mexico slope |

### Drainages Covered
- **Atlantic Slope**: [[Susquehanna River]], [[Potomac River]], [[Rappahannock River]], [[York River]], [[James River]], [[Roanoke River]], [[Tar River]], [[Neuse River]], [[Cape Fear River]], [[Peedee River]]
- **Ohio Basin**: [[Monongahela River]], [[Little Kanawha River]], [[Kanawha River]] (below/above falls), [[Guyandotte River]], [[Big Sandy River]], [[Licking River]], [[Kentucky River]], [[Green River (Kentucky)]], [[Cumberland River]] (below/above falls), [[Tennessee River]]

Notable ecological patterns include:
- **Diadromous/euryhaline species** (coded Ma) such as [[American Eel]] (*Anguilla rostrata*), [[Alosa|Alosa spp.]] (shads/herrings), and gars (*[[Lepisosteus]] spp.*) occur across nearly all Atlantic Slope drainages, reflecting connectivity to the [[Atlantic Ocean]].
- **Cave-adapted taxa** in the [[Amblyopsidae]] (e.g., *[[Amblyopsis spelaea]]*, *[[Typhlichthys subterraneus]]*, *[[Chologaster agassizi]]*) reflect karst/subterranean habitat specialization in the Ohio Basin.
- **Introduced/invasive species** (I, IP, NI codes) are widespread, including *[[Cyprinus carpio]]* (common carp), *[[Ctenopharyngodon idella]]* (grass carp), *[[Carassius auratus]]* (goldfish), and stocked salmonids (*[[Oncorhynchus kisutch]]*, *[[Salmo trutta]]*).

## Key Findings

- **Total diversity documented**: 398 fish forms + 5 intergrade populations across **28 families** in the central/northern Appalachian region.
- **Geographic scope expansion**: This checklist extends the original [[Jenkins Lachner and Schwartz 1972]] table by adding the **Susquehanna, Licking, Green, and Kentucky river** drainages.
- **Conservative methodology**: Species of uncertain occurrence were excluded rather than speculatively included, making this a minimum-diversity baseline.
- **High endemism**: Numerous taxa are coded "E" for endemic, concentrated heavily in [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows, e.g., *[[Phenacobius crassilabrum]]*, *[[Phenacobius teretulus]]*, *[[Phoxinus cumberlandensis]]*), [[Percidae]] (darters, e.g., *[[Etheostoma osburni]]*, *[[Etheostoma sellare]]*, *[[Percina rex]]*, *[[Percina tanasi]]*, *[[Percina gymnocephala]]*), and [[Catostomidae]] (suckers, e.g., *[[Moxostoma atripinne]]*), underscoring the Appalachians as a center of freshwater fish endemism in North America.
- **Notable rare/restricted endemics**: *[[Fundulus albolineatus]]* (whiteline topminnow, likely extinct), *[[Lagochila lacera]]* (harelip sucker, extinct), *[[Notropis mekistocholas]]* (Cape Fear shiner), *[[Notropis semperasper]]* (roughhead shiner), *[[Etheostoma sellare]]* (Maryland darter).
- **Family-level richness**: [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Percidae]] dominate the species list by far, consistent with their status as the most speciose freshwater fish families in eastern North America.
- **Cross-basin biogeography**: The Ohio Basin and Atlantic Slope share relatively few native species, with distinct assemblages separated by the Appalachian divide; extralimital distribution codes (So/No/O/M/G) trace dispersal corridors and glacial-era range shifts.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty acknowledged**: The list explicitly flags undescribed species complexes (e.g., multiple *Etheostoma* (Ulocentra) forms, *Notropis* sp. groups), signaling ongoing systematic revision needs at time of publication.
- **Utility**: Designed as a practical reference table (Table 1) for zoogeographic analysis, environmental impact assessment, and conservation planning across 20+ drainages.
