# University of Michigan Museum of Zoology Fish Collections from Arizona Counties

#ichthyology #museum-collection #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Arizona-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document comprises catalogued fish collection records from the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ) Division of Fishes for three Arizona counties: [[Yavapai County]], [[Gila County]], and [[Coconino County]]. The collections span 1926-1978 and document native and introduced fish species from the [[Colorado River]] basin and [[Gila River]] drainage systems. Major collectors include [[Carl L. Hubbs]], [[Robert Rush Miller]], and [[Gordon R. Smith]]. The records detail specimen counts, size ranges, collection localities, and taxonomic identifications for over 200 collection events, providing critical historical baseline data for southwestern aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description

The UMMZ fish collections represent systematic surveys of Arizona's freshwater ichthyofauna conducted primarily between the 1930s and 1970s. Collections focused on major drainages including the [[Verde River]], [[Salt River]], [[Tonto Creek]], [[East Clear Creek]], [[Oak Creek]], and the [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]]. Field numbers reference specific collecting events with associated locality data, collector names, dates, and environmental notes.

Specimens were preserved in alcohol (alc), prepared as skeletal material (skel), or cleared and stained (C&S). Size measurements are reported in standard length (SL) in millimeters. The collections document both native southwestern fishes and introduced species including [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and various salmonids.

Taxonomic identifications were made by leading ichthyologists including Hubbs, Miller, Smith, and Greenbank. Many specimens represent paratypes or reference material for species descriptions, including [[Lepidomeda mollispinis]] paratypes (UMMZ 141662) and the holotype of *Catostomus clarki* subspecies *delphinus rheophilus* (UMMZ 141664).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collections document fish assemblages across diverse aquatic habitats from high-elevation mountain streams to the main-stem [[Colorado River]]. Water temperatures ranged from 56°F in spring-fed tributaries to 91°F in desert streams. Many localities represent permanent streams, though some collections were made in desiccating pools or intermittent flood tributaries.

Native fish assemblages typically included multiple [[Catostomus]] species (suckers), [[Gila robusta]] (roundtail chub), [[Rhinichthys osculus]] (speckled dace), and specialized species like [[Meda fulgida]] (spikedace) and [[Tiaroga cobitis]] (loach minnow). Hybridization between sucker species was documented, particularly *Catostomus clarki* × *C. insignis* hybrids in the [[Verde River]] system.

[[Grand Canyon]] collections (1968, 1975) documented the native [[Colorado River]] fauna including the endangered [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub) and [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado pikeminnow). Introduced species were increasingly prevalent in lower-elevation waters, including [[Gambusia affinis]], [[Ictalurus]] catfishes, and centrarchid sunfishes.

Habitat notes frequently mention substrate types (sand, gravel, rocks), flow conditions (pools, riffles, rapids), and vegetation. Temperature data and elevation records provide environmental context for species distributions.

## Key Findings

**Yavapai County Collections (1937-1950):**
- [[Beaver Creek]] and [[Verde River]] assemblages included abundant *Catostomus clarki* (up to 321 specimens), *C. insignis* (up to 316 specimens), and hybrids between these species
- [[Meda fulgida]] populations documented with up to 281 specimens from single localities (UMMZ 125030)
- [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (longfin dace) extremely abundant with collections exceeding 1,300 specimens (UMMZ 146702)
- Paratypes of *Pantosteus eremicus* (now *Catostomus clarki*) collected from [[Fort Rock Creek]] (UMMZ 162830, n=351)
- [[Gila robusta]] specimens noted as intergrades between *G. r. intermedia* and *G. r. robusta*

**Gila County Collections (1926-1968):**
- [[Tonto Creek]] and [[Salt River]] supported diverse native assemblages including *Catostomus* spp., *Gila robusta*, *Rhinichthys osculus*, and *Tiaroga cobitis*
- Large collections of juvenile suckers documented (990 *C. clarki* from UMMZ 162776)
- [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado pikeminnow) documented from [[Salt River]] (UMMZ 131110, n=11; UMMZ 162779, n=2)
- Introduced salmonids (*Salmo gairdneri*, *Salvelinus fontinalis*) established in upper [[Tonto Creek]] tributaries
- *Catostomus latipinnis* (flannelmouth sucker) documented from [[Salt River]] above [[Roosevelt Dam]]

**Coconino County Collections (1934-1978):**
- [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]] supported *Catostomus discobolus*, *C. latipinnis*, *Gila cypha*, and *Rhinichthys osculus*
- [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub) documented from multiple [[Grand Canyon]] localities (RM 61.5-64.5) with specimens 44-211 mm SL
- [[Lepidomeda mollispinis]] paratypes from [[Beaver Dam Creek]] (UMMZ 141662, n=381, 22-77 mm SL)
- [[East Clear Creek]] populations of *Lepidomeda vittata* (Little Colorado spinedace) extensively sampled (up to 858 *Rhinichthys osculus* in UMMZ 179571)
- Hybridization documented between *Catostomus discobolus* × *C. latipinnis* (UMMZ 179569) and *Pimephales promelas* × *Notropis lutrensis* (UMMZ 189220)
- [[Little Colorado River]] supported *Fundulus zebrinus* (plains killifish) populations (UMMZ 198455, n=19)
- Temperature gradients documented in [[Grand Canyon]] tributaries: 56°F in [[Colorado River]] vs. 91°F in [[Chuar Creek]]

**Taxonomic and Conservation Significance:**
- Collections include holotype and paratype material for multiple taxa
- Historical baseline data predates major dam construction and habitat alteration
- Documentation of native species now extirpated or greatly reduced in range
- Hybrid specimens provide evidence of natural introgression zones
- Size-frequency data indicate successful reproduction in historical populations
