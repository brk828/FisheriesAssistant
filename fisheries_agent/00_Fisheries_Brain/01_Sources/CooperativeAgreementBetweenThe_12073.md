# Cooperative Agreement for Taxonomic Clarification of the Gila Complex in the Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-complex

## Executive Summary

This document is a cooperative agreement between the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (the Service) and the [[Smithsonian Institution]] to complete Phase I of a three-phase study of the taxonomy and systematics of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and the [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The humpback and bonytail chub are listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. The agreement states that recovery planning is hampered by unresolved questions about the taxonomy of the [[Gila Complex]], so a basinwide review of existing knowledge is needed before an effective recovery program can be developed. The agreement sets out schedules, deliverables, funding, project officers, and special provisions. Phase I is funded at no more than $53,000.

## Background & Description

The three species are difficult to identify because of high morphological variation and the lack of definitive criteria separating them. The Service and researchers in the Colorado River basin raised five questions:

1. Are roundtail, bonytail, and humpback chub three distinct species, and are these classifications supported by recent data?
2. Is genetic and morphological variation within the species adequately described, and do current data support the existing taxonomy?
3. What characteristics separate the three species, and can definitive identification criteria, including criteria for hybrids, be developed?
4. Have the genetic or morphological characteristics of the Gila complex changed recently, and if so, are the changes linked to environmental disturbance?
5. How far must genetic integrity be protected to preserve Gila species in the Colorado River system?

No comprehensive basinwide review had been attempted, and several investigators were working on these questions independently.

The agreement is made under Section 4 of the Endangered Species Act, as amended. The [[Smithsonian Institution]] is the performing party. Its [[Project Manager]], Dr. Wayne Starnes of the Division of Fishes, leads the work. The Service's technical project officer is Dr. Holt Williamson. The contracting officer is David Short, and the administrative project officer is John Hamill, Director of the Colorado River Recovery Program. The agreement incorporates the Service's General Provisions for Grants and Cooperative Agreements (Attachment 2).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area covers the Colorado River basin, with primary emphasis on:

- the [[Little Colorado River]]
- the [[Grand Canyon]] of the Colorado River
- the upper mainstem Colorado River, including Debeque Canyon, Black Rocks, Westwater Canyon, and Cataract Canyon
- the [[Green River]], including Desolation, Gray, and Whirlpool canyons
- the [[Yampa River]] in Dinosaur National Monument

The document does not describe the biology, diet, life history, or population status of the species. Its environmental focus is on how genetic and morphological characteristics may respond to "environmental perturbations," and on protecting the genetic integrity of Gila populations across these river reaches.

The agreement lists nine museum and research collections to be located and described for taxonomic specimens. These include the Smithsonian Institution, Tulane University, the University of Michigan, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Research Unit at Fort Collins, the [[Larval Fishes Laboratory]] at Colorado State University, the University of Utah, Arizona State University, Utah State University, and the University of Colorado at Boulder.

The review task also covers ten groups of ongoing researchers, including Dr. R. R. Miller and Gerald Smith (University of Michigan), Dr. Robert Muth (Colorado State University), Dr. Mark Rosenfeld (University of Utah), Dr. W. L. Minckley and Michael Douglas (Arizona State University), Dr. Paul Holden and Richard Valdez (BioWest, Inc.), Dr. Glen Clemmer (The Nature Conservancy), Dr. Royal Suttkus (Tulane University), C. O. Minckley (University of Northern Arizona), Lynn Kaeding (Fish and Wildlife Service, Grand Junction), and Dr. Harold Tyus (Fish and Wildlife Service, Vernal).

## Key Findings

**Program structure**

- **Phase I** (this agreement) is a review of existing information and collections, followed by a draft work plan and a data collection protocol. It is funded at no more than $53,000 from account 65115-1113.
- **Phase II** covers the development and implementation of specific research proposals. Projects are selected by the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]]. Contracts may be issued by the Smithsonian, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the Service, or state agencies, whichever is most cost-effective.
- **Phase III** is an integration and synthesis of all research. The Project Manager will prepare a final report that (1) summarizes current knowledge of Gila taxonomy and systematics, (2) integrates the project's research results, and (3) answers the five questions above.

**Review and oversight**

- An [[Ad Hoc Review Panel]] will review the work plan and protocol. It is chaired by the Project Manager and meets no more than twice a year. Panel members are scientists with expertise in taxonomy and systematics, genetics (including electrophoresis, cytogenetics, and [[Mitochondrial DNA]] analysis), and fish behavior and [[Hybridization]]. Gila species experts from the Service, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, the Colorado Division of Wildlife, and the Bureau of Reclamation also serve on it. Scientists competing for project funding are excluded.
- Contractors must submit annual progress reports and draft final reports. Drafts go to [[Peer Review]] after review by the Review Panel.
- The Service must review deliverables and provide comments within 20 days.

**Deliverables**

Three deliverables are required in Phase I:

1. A review of existing information, activities, and collections. A draft (50 copies) is due September 29, 1989, and the final (75 copies) is due March 2, 1990.
2. A work plan identifying priority needs. A draft (50 copies) is due November 16, 1989, and the final (75 copies) is due March 2, 1990.
3. [[Data Collection Protocol]] guidelines for sampling intra- and inter-population variability of Gila species across the basin. A draft (50 copies) is due November 16, 1989, and the final (75 copies) is due March 2, 1990.

**Terms and conditions**

- The agreement may be renewed yearly for up to five years, subject to Congressional appropriations. If funding is not appropriated, both parties are released from liability.
- Phase II and III work cannot start until a signed modification sets out the Statement of Work and performance period.
- The Smithsonian may publish research results without restriction after Service review.
- Either party may terminate on 30 days' written notice, unless the special provisions apply.
- Modifications must be in writing (SF-30) and signed before any extra work begins.
- Payment is made on completion of each deliverable, through the administrative project officer.

**Document notes**

- The period of performance ends March 2, 1990, but the Phase II and III schedule runs through December 1992. The later phases depend on renewal and modification.
- The schedule lists a convening date of "January 15, 1989" for the review panel. This date precedes the April 28, 1989 signing and may be a typographical error.
