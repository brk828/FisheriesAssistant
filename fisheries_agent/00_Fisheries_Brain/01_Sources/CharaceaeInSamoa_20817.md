# First Record of Characeae in Samoa: Nitella furcata var. sieberi

#characeae #phycology #aquatic-botany

## Executive Summary

This 1963 botanical note reports the first known occurrence of Characeae (stoneworts) in the Samoan Islands. The author, [[Wood]] of the University of Rhode Island, identified a single population of [[Nitella Furcata Var. Sieberi]] on [[Upolu]], [[Western Samoa]], making it the easternmost Characeae record in the central South Pacific. The alga was found in one of 62 habitats surveyed on Upolu and none on [[Tutuila]]. Because the same habitat types in [[Fiji]] supported Characeae, the author concludes that the scarcity in Samoa likely reflects a lack of propagules rather than a lack of suitable habitat. The work was part of a broader project testing whether the Characeae of oceanic islands are linked to those of the adjacent continent, [[Australia]].

## Background & Description

**Study context.** Earlier botanical surveys of Samoa by [[Reinecke]], [[Setchell]], and [[Christopherson]] did not record Characeae. The author notes that [[Setchell]] was a skilled phycologist and avid algal collector, and that collections by [[Huber-Pestalozzi]] and [[Rechinger]] also lacked Characeae, according to correspondence from their descendants or institutions. The field work was carried out in August 1961 and was supported in part by a [[Fulbright Research Scholar Award]] and a [[National Science Foundation]] grant.

**Geographic setting.** The Samoan Islands lie near 171°W and 14°S, roughly two-thirds of the way from the United States to Australia and about 1100 miles south of the equator. They form part of a stepwise archipelago chain extending eastward from Australia at intervals of about 800 miles. Fiji lies to the west and the Society Islands to the east.

**Survey effort.** Major excursions on Upolu covered the north shore from Apia, the mountainous [[Lemafa Pass]] route to Amaile on the south shore, and [[Lake Lanato'o]] in the uplands. On Tutuila, collecting followed the road from Pago Pago westward to Malaelae.

**Taxonomy.** The classification follows the author's 1962 revision, placing the Samoan plant in subgenus and section Tieffallenia of the genus [[Nitella]]. The author states that the names N. furcata and var. sieberi have priority over N. mucronata and var. microcarpa used in his earlier preliminary revision. The full nomenclatural discussion appears in an unpublished monograph (Part I of the [[Wood and Imahori Revision]]).

**Morphological description (Samoan material):**
- Monoecious plants up to 10 cm high, soft and diffuse.
- Stems 400–900 µm in diameter; internodes 3/4–1 times the length of the branchlets.
- Fertile branchlets about 7 per whorl, up to 1.5 cm long, 2–3(–4)-furcate.
- Dactyls 2–4 per branchlet, mostly minute, 2-celled; reduced dactyls up to 120 µm long.
- Oogonia (immature) 2–4 per node; largest observed 190 µm long and 180 µm wide, with 5 convolutions.
- Antheridia (mature) about 230 µm in diameter.
- Oospores not yet formed, so membrane features could not be checked.
- The specimens are small for the species and resemble the variant f. microglochin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat comparison with Fiji.** Many habitat types were common to both islands, including woodland pools, barrier beach ponds, ditch and culvert pools, rivers, and small streams. Characeae occurred in these habitats in Fiji but not in Samoa. In Samoa, the single record came from a taro patch, a habitat where the species was rarely reported in Fiji.

**Coastal pools.** Spring-fed coastal pools lacked Characeae. The author found that these pools are flushed by tidal waters and have been modified for bathing by Samoans, which the author considered a plausible explanation for their absence.

**Dispersal hypothesis.** The author concludes that habitat availability does not explain the scarcity. The more likely cause is the absence of propagules. The species is known from Fiji and Australia, which fits a hypothesis of migration from the west. However, the author notes it is unusual that the Samoan population is a rare form, since the species is one of the common Characeae in Fiji.

**Distribution.** Globally, the species is recorded from Indonesia, New Guinea, Australia, the Philippines, Japan, New Caledonia, and Fiji. It is not known from any South Pacific island further east than Samoa. The taxon is highly variable and has been split into several "species" by various authors.

**Locality details.** The single population grew in a shaded spot in shallow water between taro plants, in Tapuotaota Stream between a pool and a concrete wall, about 8 miles east of Apia.

## Key Findings

- **First Samoan record:** [[Nitella Furcata Var. Sieberi]] is the first Characeae reported from Samoa, found only at Tapuotaota on Upolu.
- **Low frequency:** The species occurred in 1 of 62 habitats examined on Upolu, a frequency of about 1.6%, compared with 19% in Fiji.
- **No records on Tutuila:** No Characeae were found on Tutuila.
- **Chromosome number:** 18, determined from antheridial filaments.
- **Biogeographic significance:** The record is the easternmost Characeae in the central South Pacific, supporting a western source (Fiji, Australia) for the Samoan population.
- **Propagule limitation:** Habitat absence does not explain the scarcity; the author attributes it to limited dispersal of propagules.
- **Nomenclature:** The names N. furcata and var. sieberi take priority over the earlier N. mucronata and var. microcarpa.
- **Specimen deposition:** Herbarium specimens are being deposited at US, K, and L, with a set kept in the author's herbarium (RDW).
- **Specimen data:** Collected August 1, 1961 by [[Wood]], Eti Tia'i, and Esekia Sev'a, in about 1 inch of water over mud in a taro patch by a spring-fed pool.
