# FY 1999 Summary of Fish Surveys of Salt River Project and Florence-Casa Grande Canals, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report, prepared by [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under a cooperative agreement, summarizes FY 1999 fish sampling in canals of the Gila River basin in central Arizona. Sampling covered four [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) canals, namely the [[Arizona Canal]] (SRPN), the [[South Canal]] (SRPS), the [[Consolidated Canal]] and the [[Tempe Canal]], plus the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG) operated by the Bureau of Reclamation. Sampling was opportunistic and qualitative in part, so catch totals are not strictly comparable among canals. Non-native species dominated nearly all canals, and native fishes were scarce, especially in the Florence-Casa Grande Canal.

## Background & Description

The survey is part of a long-term monitoring program for fish populations in regulated waters of the Gila River basin, guided by the [[Long-Term Monitoring Plan]] of Clarkson (1996). It was submitted to Robert W. Clarkson of the Bureau of Reclamation Phoenix Area Office by Paul C. Marsh, ASU Department of Biology, on 15 April 1999. Sampling was conducted between 17 October and 5 December 1998, with some SRP dates extending into 1999 (for example, 5 November 1999 in the South Canal tables).

Standard gear included [[Seine Netting]] (bag, straight and mesh seines), trammel and gill nets, hoop nets, visual observation, and [[Electrofishing]] (backpack shocker and boat-mounted electrofisher). Species codes followed the established protocol. The [[Electric Fish Barrier]] at Granite Reef Dam and other barriers influence fish movement between canal reaches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native species present:** [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were the native fishes recorded.
- **Non-native species present:** [[Red Shiner]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Tilapia]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Common Carp]] and [[Lepomis Hybrids]] were recorded. The report refers to these as non-natives and notes that they dominated most stations. See also [[Non-Native Species]].
- **Operational constraints:** Sampling was affected by lapses in communication with SRP about canal outages and drawdowns, which meant crews sometimes arrived after water had been drawn down. Locked gates along canal roadways caused further delays.
- **Data limits:** Fish data from above the fish barrier, collected by the Arizona Game and Fish Department and reported elsewhere, were excluded. The authors note that including them would substantially change the relationships based on the ASU and BR samples alone.

## Key Findings

**Overall**
- A total of 20 entries (OCR-garbled count, likely taxa) was captured during FY 1999 monitoring, including Lepomis hybrids. No new species were encountered.
- Of these, 16 came from SRPN, 15 from SRPS and 8 from FCG. Four native species were taken: roundtail chub, longfin dace, Sonora sucker and desert sucker. Three native species were taken from SRPN and SRPS, and two from FCG.
- Total fish numbers varied widely among stations, reflecting differences in canal flows, sampling effort and gear.

**SRP South Canal (ASU sampling, 24 October to 5 November 1999)**
- Twelve species, including three natives, were taken from SRPS.
- Natives made up 31.3% of the total catch. Desert sucker was the second most abundant species overall (17.7%), and Sonora sucker added 9.2%.
- Non-native red shiner was the most abundant species overall, at 59.9% of the catch.

**SRP North (Arizona) Canal (ASU and BR sampling, 3 November to 22 December 1998)**
- Thirteen species, including three natives, were captured from SRPN and divided into upper, middle and lower reaches for illustration.
- Natives comprised 14.3% of total numbers. Desert sucker made up 9.3%, Sonora sucker 4.2% and roundtail chub 0.8%.
- Red shiner was the most abundant species overall (76.9%), followed by desert sucker (9.3%), largemouth bass (4.4%) and Sonora sucker (4.2%).
- Red shiner dominated the upper reach. Sonora sucker was most abundant in the middle reach. The lower reach had very few fish, so results there are inconclusive.

**Florence-Casa Grande Canal (sampled 17 October 1998)**
- Eight species, including two natives, were taken. Sonora sucker was the only native that occurred below the Ashurst-Hayden Dam and above the China Wash barrier.
- Native species made up only 1.4% of total numbers.
- Red shiner was the predominant species overall (74.9% of the catch), followed by mosquitofish and common carp.
- The FCG was the only artificial stream in which the survey recorded a native species in a reach with no barrier.

**SRP Consolidated and Tempe Canals**
- The Consolidated Canal (sampled 24 October 1998 during a drawdown, along roughly 500 m) yielded ten species. Mosquitofish, red shiner, redbelly tilapia and channel catfish were among the most common.
- The Tempe Canal (sampled 24 October 1998) yielded five species, including native desert sucker and Sonora sucker. Both native species were considered common, and several stocked fish were observed.

**Arizona Canal (SRP, 4 to 5 December 1998)**
- Sampling at the Granite Reef Dam barrier, 0.25 miles above Beeline Highway, 7.8 miles below Granite Reef Dam (below Eversreen Drain) and at other stations recorded Channel Catfish, Red Shiner, Longfin Dace, Mosquitofish and Largemouth Bass in varying numbers. Some stations yielded no fish.

**Recommendations**
- Obtain keys to locked gates along SRP canal roadways.
- Improve communication among SRP, the Bureau of Reclamation and the agencies performing fish monitoring, so that sampling can be coordinated with scheduled canal outages.
