# Desert Fishes Council Fish Count Program Correspondence

#ichthyology #desert-fishes #monitoring-program #fish-survey

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from [[Steven P. Platania]] at the [[University of New Mexico]] to Dr. W.L. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]] outlines organizational details for a [[Desert Fishes Council]]-sponsored "Fish Count" monitoring program. The letter, dated July 6, 1988, describes program modifications based on participant feedback from multiple southwestern U.S. states and establishes protocols for sampling, data collection, and specimen curation across desert aquatic systems.

## Background & Description

The [[Fish Count Program]] represents a collaborative monitoring initiative sponsored by the [[Desert Fishes Council]] involving participants from Arizona, California, New Mexico, Nevada, Oklahoma, and Texas. The program aims to establish standardized fish population monitoring across desert aquatic habitats in the southwestern United States. Participants select monitoring sites and employ various sampling methodologies appropriate to their locations, with flexibility in approach but requirements for quantifiable data collection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The program targets desert fish populations across multiple southwestern states, representing diverse desert aquatic systems. The monitoring framework accommodates various habitat types by allowing flexible sampling methodologies including seining, electrofishing, and other appropriate techniques. The initiative recognizes the importance of long-term monitoring for desert fish species and emphasizes specimen preservation for future verification of species identifications. Collections are to be deposited in maintained museum collections to ensure data integrity over time.

## Key Findings

**Program Schedule Modifications:**
- Sampling period moved from July to October based on participant feedback
- Extended from three weekends to the entire month of October to accommodate participant schedules

**Sampling Protocols:**
- Flexible methodology allowing participants to determine best sampling means for their selected sites
- Required quantification parameters for seining: seine size, number and approximate area of hauls, time in water
- Electrofishing quantification based primarily on shocking time
- Field notes must include information allowing data quantification

**Data Management Plan:**
- Collectors to provide detailed site descriptions, species composition, abundance data, and field note copies
- Data to be compiled using database program (Lotus)
- Hard copies distributed to all participants
- Museum accession numbers to be included when available

**Specimen Curation:**
- Strong recommendation to preserve collections and deposit in maintained collections
- Specimens essential for future verification of species identifications
- Arrangements available for curation if local museums unavailable

**Program Communication:**
- Annual [[Desert Fishes Council]] meetings to serve as forum for discussing procedures and improvements
- Progress updates presented as 12-minute papers every few years or as needed
- Several months allowed for data submission following October sampling
- Deadlines for data submission to be established through group discussion
