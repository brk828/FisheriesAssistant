## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific research paper** but an internal Arizona State University (ASU) memo and administrative proposal dated September 1993. Authored by Julie Stromberg and Duncan T. Patten (Center for Environmental Studies), it proposes establishing an interdisciplinary "Institute for Riparian and Aquatic Research" to consolidate ASU's expertise in [[Riparian Ecology]], [[Wetland Ecology]], and [[Aquatic Ecosystems]]. No morphological, taxonomic, or population-level fish data are presented. However, the document is contextually relevant to ichthyology because it identifies ASU faculty (notably those in the Department of Zoology) whose research programs address desert fish biology, aquatic community ecology, and freshwater conservation in the arid Southwest — a region defined by imperiled native fish faunas such as the [[Gila Chub]] and other Sonoran Desert fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no direct ecological data (e.g., diet, habitat parameters, water chemistry) are given, the memo frames the **riparian-aquatic ecosystem** conceptually as a continuous unit:

- The [[Riparian Ecosystem]] is defined as spanning the **entire river floodplain**, "edge to edge," integrating:
  - [[Riparian Vegetation]]
  - [[Wetland Habitat]]
  - The river/[[Aquatic System]] itself
- This systems-level framing implies that fish and aquatic organism research at ASU (e.g., by zoologists) cannot be separated from upland floodplain vegetation and hydrological processes — an ecosystem-based rather than species-based niche description.
- Justification section notes that riparian, wetland, and aquatic ecosystems in the **Southwestern United States** are undergoing significant loss and degradation, with ecological functions including:
  - Water quality improvement
  - Flood damage reduction
  - Groundwater recharge enhancement
  - Support of high [[Biodiversity]] of plant and animal species (implicitly including native fish assemblages)
- Then-Secretary of the Interior [[Bruce Babbitt]] is cited as having called for increased protection/restoration of U.S. riparian areas, reflecting the political-ecological context driving this proposal.

## Key Findings

- **No empirical fish or aquatic species data** are reported; the document is a call-to-action and administrative proposal, not a data-driven study.
- Proposes creation of an **"Institute for Riparian and Aquatic Research"** administered through the ASU Center for Environmental Studies, integrating ecology, hydrology, geomorphology, water resource management, planning, and policy disciplines.
- Identifies a **Preliminary Resource Pool** of ASU faculty relevant to riparian/aquatic/wetland science, including several with strong ties to freshwater ichthyology and aquatic ecology in the Southwest:
  - **Dr. Paul Marsh** (Center for Environmental Studies) — recognized researcher on Southwestern native and endangered fish species conservation.
  - **Dr. W. L. Minckley** (Department of Zoology) — prominent ichthyologist known for extensive work on [[Desert Fishes]] and Sonoran Desert aquatic ecosystems.
  - **Dr. Thomas Dowling** and **Dr. Michael Douglas** (Zoology) — associated with fish systematics/evolutionary biology research.
  - **Dr. Stuart Fisher** and **Dr. Nancy Grimm** (Zoology) — stream and aquatic ecosystem ecologists, relevant to [[Stream Ecology]] and nutrient cycling in desert streams.
  - **Dr. Robert Ohmart** (Center for Environmental Studies) — riparian wildlife habitat researcher.
- Establishes a formal data-collection instrument (the "ASU Riparian/Wetland/Aquatic Resource Pool" form) to catalog faculty research interests, ongoing projects, and publications — intended to seed future interdisciplinary collaboration.
- Frames the initiative as a response to regional environmental policy pressures and prior unsuccessful attempts to secure an NSF-funded "Center for Aquatic and Riparian Research."
- Positions [[Arizona State University]] as a hub of nationally recognized expertise in riparian/aquatic ecology, aiming to formalize and enhance interdisciplinary coordination rather than report new empirical findings.
