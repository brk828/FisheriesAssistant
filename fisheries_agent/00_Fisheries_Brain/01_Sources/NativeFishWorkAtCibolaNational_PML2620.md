# Native Fish Work at Cibola National Wildlife Refuge: Pond Maintenance, Sampling and Recruitment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum, dated 2 February 2004 and issued by the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] in Parker, reports on two activities at [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (CNWR). The first was a routine maintenance check of the pupfish refuge pond, where a recurring algae problem was addressed with chemical treatments. The second was a combined night and next-day sampling effort at [[High Levee Pond]], carried out with an [[Arizona State University]] wildlife management class. Sampling captured [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] and four [[American Bullfrog]]s. The authors concluded that recruitment of native fish occurred over the previous two years and that High Levee Pond is functioning as native fish habitat.

## Background & Description

The memorandum is an internal project update. It was addressed to refuge, regional and university staff and was written by the project coordinator at the Parker office.

**Pupfish refuge pond.** A seasonal problem has been recorded at the pond. In summer, algae production resumes and forms a thick mat on the surface. Dead material accumulates on the bottom and raises the [[Biological Oxygen Demand]] of the system. In previous years, the layer of dead and senescent algae on the bottom reached 6 to 8 inches thick. The author attributes the bloom to phosphorus loading from the pond's concrete walls.

**High Levee Pond.** This pond is a site where native fish are monitored. The memorandum describes it as a habitat that supports both adult and juvenile native fish.

**Methods.** Two sampling methods were used:
- [[Trammel Netting]]: two 300-foot trammel nets were set overnight and then run the next morning.
- [[Electrofishing]]: the pond was electrofished on the evening of 30 January 2004.

Baited lines on small buoys were also set to target a large [[Flathead Catfish]] reported in the pond. Captured fish were measured for length and weight, identified by species, sexed where possible, and checked for [[PIT Tagging]] status. Untagged fish longer than 15 cm were tagged with 125 MHz PIT tags.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Algae and water quality.** On 30 January 2004 the pupfish pond was visited at 4 PM. The water was clear and floating algae covered less than 10 cm², so the bottom layer could be seen to be much smaller than in earlier years. The memorandum links the earlier bloom to excess [[Phosphorus Loading]] and to decaying organic matter that increased oxygen demand. This pattern is consistent with [[Eutrophication]] in small, enclosed ponds.

**Remedial treatments.**
- An alum/bentonite mixture was added to bind and remove excess phosphorus.
- A commercial bacterial product (marketed as "Bacteria in a Bag") was added to break down bottom debris.

The memorandum reports that these treatments appear to have worked so far. Bi-annual monitoring of the pond will continue.

**Fish in cold conditions.** No fish were seen in the pupfish pond during the January visit. The author assumes they were buried in the substrate because of cool water temperatures. Refuge staff have reported fish on warmer days during the month.

## Key Findings

**1. Pupfish pond status (30 January 2004).**
- Floating algae covered less than 10 cm² and the water was clear.
- Bottom algal debris was much reduced compared with prior years, when it reached 6 to 8 inches.
- No fish were observed; they were presumed buried due to cold water.

**2. High Levee Pond catch.** Two species were captured, plus bullfrogs.

| Species | Total | Details reported |
|---|---|---|
| Bonytail (*Gila elegans*) | 81 | 2 larger recaptures; 24 adults over 15 cm, untagged and PIT tagged; 55 under 15 cm |
| Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) | 42 | 6 tagged recaptures; 7 untagged large fish over 650 g; 29 juveniles |
| American Bullfrog (*Rana catesbeiana*) | 4 | Taken during sampling |

**3. Razorback sucker details.**
- Seven untagged large individuals (over 650 g) were captured. Two of these were ripe, tuberculate males.
- No running-ripe females were captured.
- The 29 juveniles measured 13 to 26 cm and weighed 30 to 90 g.
- In total, 13 adults and 29 juveniles were captured.

**4. Recruitment.** During the previous three years, only a small number of juvenile razorback suckers had been recorded, which raised concern about recruitment. The author states that the large number of juveniles captured cannot be directly linked to recruitment levels. However, the author argues it shows that recruitment has occurred over the last two years at High Levee Pond. Together with the many small bonytail, the author concludes that the pond is functioning as native fish habitat.

**5. Management actions.**
- Six baited lines were set in the pond to target the large flathead catfish. They will be checked periodically by refuge staff.
- Bi-annual monitoring of the pupfish pond will continue.

**6. Acknowledgments.** The memorandum credits refuge staff, [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] personnel and the Arizona State University class for their help.
