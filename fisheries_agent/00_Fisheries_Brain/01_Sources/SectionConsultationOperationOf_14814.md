# Section 7 Consultation - Operation of Glen Canyon Dam Impacts on Endangered Fish Species

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This document consists of interagency correspondence between the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Upper Colorado Regional Office and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] initiating formal Section 7 Consultation under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. The consultation addresses potential impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on two endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River]] between [[Lee's Ferry]] and [[Lake Mead]]: the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The Fish and Wildlife Service's threshold examination determined that adverse impacts have occurred to these native fish species due to altered flow regimes and water quality changes, particularly temperature modifications resulting from dam operations.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) are both members of the family Cyprinidae and are listed as endangered species under federal protection. These native fish species inhabit the [[Colorado River]] system in the [[Grand Canyon]] region, specifically the reach between [[Lee's Ferry]] and [[Lake Mead]].

[[Glen Canyon Dam]] regulates water releases on the [[Colorado River]], fundamentally altering the natural hydrological regime that these species evolved with. The dam is part of the Colorado River Storage Project (CRSP) managed by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] has materially altered both flow patterns and water quality in the downstream [[Colorado River]] ecosystem. Key environmental changes include:

- **Flow Regime Alterations**: Water releases from the dam have changed natural flow patterns that historically characterized the river system
- **Temperature Changes**: Significant temperature modifications have occurred as a result of dam operations and the resultant water release regime
- **Water Quality**: Other water quality parameters beyond temperature have been affected by dam operations

These alterations are described as being "of a magnitude sufficient to be detrimental to the native fish fauna below the Dam and downstream to Lake Mead." The impacts affect the entire native fish community in this river reach.

## Key Findings

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's threshold biological opinion determined that:

1. **Adverse impacts are or have occurred** to endangered fish species as a result of both [[Glen Canyon Dam]] construction and its water release operations
2. **Two listed species are adversely affected**: the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
3. **Material alterations** to flows and water quality, including significant temperature changes, have been detrimental to native fish fauna
4. **Formal Section 7 Consultation** will proceed with a more complete evaluation of existing data before rendering a final biological opinion
5. **Data requested** from the Bureau of Reclamation includes: water release regimes, water temperatures, other water quality data, predictions on future releases (maximums and minimums), biological data, and pertinent studies completed, underway, or planned

The consultation was initiated on June 28, 1977, by the Bureau of Reclamation and the threshold determination was provided by Jerry L. Stegman, with Jack Woody designated as the contact for the Endangered Species Office in Albuquerque, New Mexico.
