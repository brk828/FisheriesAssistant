## Executive Summary
This is an internal Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report outlining fishery management problems and future research needs for "District III," which encompasses [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], and the [[Colorado River]] below [[Hoover Dam]] and [[Davis Dam]]. The document is not a primary morphological or ecological study, but rather a management planning summary identifying gaps in basic survey data, stocking practices, and forage-gamefish dynamics. Priority issues include interstate coordination of [[Rainbow Trout]] stocking between Arizona and Nevada, an emerging study of [[Threadfin Shad]] as a forage species, and ongoing warm-water fishery evaluation of [[Largemouth Bass]] in Lake Mohave.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Colorado River]] (below Hoover/Davis Dams)**: A tailwater fishery supporting stocked [[Rainbow Trout]], managed jointly (in effect) by Arizona and Nevada due to shared jurisdiction and angler access from both states.
- **[[Lake Mead]] & [[Lake Mohave]]**: Large reservoir systems where [[Threadfin Shad]] serve as a key forage species; their utilization, migration, and distribution patterns were identified as poorly understood and requiring cooperative interstate study.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]] in Lake Mohave**: Positioned as the apex warm-water sport fish in this reservoir; food habits study proposed using electric shocker sampling across seasons, implying a predator-forage relationship with [[Threadfin Shad]] and other prey.
- **Small District Lakes**: Numerous unspecified minor lakes within District III are flagged as needing periodic condition surveys, indicating a broader, understudied lentic fishery network.

## Key Findings
- **Data Gaps**: The district lacked basic survey data on fish population statistics and physical/chemical water conditions for the Lower Colorado River.
- **Stocking Uncertainty**: No reliable method existed for proportioning [[Rainbow Trout]] stocking between Arizona and Nevada in the shared Hoover/Davis Dam tailwater fishery; a comparative study of angler license origin was suggested as a proxy for allocation.
- **Stocking Size Class Question**: The optimal size class of [[Rainbow Trout]] for stocking below the dams was unresolved and flagged for restudy using varied methods and stocking locations.
- **Forage Species Knowledge Gap**: The ecological role of [[Threadfin Shad]] in [[Lake Mead]] and [[Lake Mohave]] (utilization, migration, distribution) was largely undocumented and recommended for a joint Arizona-Nevada investigation.
- **Ongoing Bass Study**: A warm-water fishery study on [[Lake Mohave]] was already underway, focused on length-weight relationships in [[Largemouth Bass]], with plans to expand into electric-shocker-based food habits sampling across seasons.
- **Management Recommendation**: Periodic surveys of the district's smaller lakes were recommended, pending availability of time and personnel, to assess fishery condition and inform management practices.
- **Institutional Context**: The report emphasizes interstate cooperation (Arizona–Nevada Game and Fish Departments) as essential to resolving several of the identified data and management gaps.
