## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative memorandum from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Phoenix Area Office), dated September 13, 1994, announcing a stakeholder meeting to discuss native fish conservation projects mandated by a Biological Opinion issued April 15, 1994. The Opinion addressed the environmental impact of transporting and delivering [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water to the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona and New Mexico. Critically, the Opinion determined that this water delivery action would **jeopardize the continued existence** of four federally significant fish species: the [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The memo does not contain primary morphological or ecological research data itself, but serves as a key regulatory record establishing the legal and administrative framework for subsequent conservation, recovery, and habitat protection funding for these species and other candidate taxa in the basin.

## Background & Description

No morphological data (e.g., meristics, body plan, coloration) is presented in this source document, as it is a regulatory/administrative memorandum rather than a biological field study. For reference, the four species named belong to distinct families with markedly different body plans:
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) — small-bodied [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] minnow, historically endemic to Gila Basin.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — small benthic [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] minnow adapted to swift riffle habitats.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — small livebearing [[Poeciliidae|poeciliid]], surface-oriented.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — large-bodied [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] characterized by a pronounced dorsal keel ("razorback") behind the head.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Basin/Watershed:** All four species are native to the [[Gila River Basin]], a tributary system of the Colorado River drainage spanning Arizona and New Mexico.
- **Threat Vector:** The primary ecological stressor identified is anthropogenic water management — specifically the **transportation and delivery of [[Central Arizona Project]] water** into the basin, which alters native flow regimes, habitat connectivity, and potentially facilitates introduction of non-native competitors/predators (a common concomitant risk with interbasin water transfers, though not explicitly detailed in this memo).
- **Regulatory Status:** The jeopardy finding under the Endangered Species Act indicates these species occupy narrow, sensitive ecological niches highly vulnerable to hydrological alteration — consistent with known ecology of Southwestern desert fishes dependent on natural flow variability, riffle habitats (spikedace, loach minnow), spring/backwater refugia (Gila topminnow), and large-river mainstem habitats (razorback sucker).
- **Multi-agency stakeholder ecology:** The document lists an extensive network of managing entities (FWS, AGFD, NMDGF, USFS, BLM, universities, and Mexican research institutions), reflecting the transboundary and multi-jurisdictional nature of Gila Basin fish conservation, including binational interest via [[Universidad de Sonora]] involvement (Alejandro Varela Romero), suggesting shared range/genetic stock considerations with Sonoran, Mexico populations.

## Key Findings

- A final [[Biological Opinion]] (issued April 15, 1994) concluded that CAP water transport/delivery to the Gila River Basin would **jeopardize** the continued existence of four listed/candidate fish species: [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- A **Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA)** was identified requiring the Bureau of Reclamation to fund conservation actions (recovery and habitat protection) for these species and "other Gila River basin listed or candidate fish species."
- Expenditure of conservation funds required **joint agreement** between the U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], in consultation with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) and the [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]] (NMDGF).
- A stakeholder planning meeting was convened for September 28, 1994, at Reclamation's Phoenix Area Office to generate project ideas for future native fish conservation and recovery studies, with a submission deadline of September 30, 1994, for proposed project synopses.
- The distribution list documents the breadth of the Gila Basin native fish conservation network, including federal agencies (USFWS, USFS, BLM), state wildlife agencies (AGFD, NMDGF), federal hatcheries ([[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]), academic researchers (Arizona State University, University of Arizona), and NGOs ([[The Nature Conservancy]]), plus a Mexican research institution (Universidad de Sonora), underscoring the cross-border and multi-institutional scope of recovery planning for these species.
- This memo functions as a procedural precursor document establishing the administrative basis for what would become ongoing, funded native fish conservation programs in the Gila River Basin through the mid-to-late 1990s.
