## Executive Summary

The Workman Creek Experimental Watershed study (Rich, Reynolds & West, 1961) describes a paired-watershed hydrological experiment located within the humid forest zone of Central Arizona. Three watersheds were studied: one control and two treatment watersheds ([[North Fork Watershed]] and [[South Fork Watershed]]). The research evaluated how vegetation removal and timber harvest practices affect [[Water Yield]] and [[Sedimentation]] in a forested stream system. No fish, invertebrate, or plankton data are present in this text; the study is purely a forest hydrology and land management investigation, relevant to aquatic ecologists primarily as a baseline for understanding stream discharge and sediment regimes that would affect downstream aquatic habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no species-level ecological niche data are provided, the study describes **habitat-scale environmental manipulations** with implications for aquatic ecosystems:

- **[[Riparian Cut]]**: Removal of broadleaf trees along the stream channel on the North Fork Watershed. This treatment altered riparian vegetation structure but had **no measurable effect on streamflow**.
- **[[Moist Site Cut]]**: A subsequent treatment removing trees on moist bottomland sites and replacing them with perennial grass. After two years:
  - Non-significant effect during a below-average streamflow year.
  - Significant effect on streamflow during an above-average streamflow year.
- **[[South Fork Watershed]] Timber Management**: Managed for high-quality timber production, testing effects of harvest on water and sediment yield.
  - First cut removed ~50% of merchantable timber volume, followed by an improvement cut.
  - This harvest **did not significantly affect water yields**.
- **Control Watershed**: Maintained untreated to serve as a baseline for detecting treatment-driven changes in [[Streamflow]] and [[Sedimentation]].

These vegetation and land-use manipulations represent upstream habitat and hydrologic drivers that could indirectly influence downstream aquatic communities (e.g., via altered flow regimes, sediment loading, and riparian shading), though the source does not directly address biotic responses.

## Key Findings

- Three experimental watersheds were established near Globe, Arizona, within humid forest habitat: one control, two treatment ([[North Fork Watershed]], [[South Fork Watershed]]).
- **Riparian broadleaf tree removal** (Riparian Cut) had no detectable effect on streamflow.
- **Moist-site tree removal and grass conversion** (Moist Site Cut) showed inconsistent effects depending on annual streamflow conditions — significant only in an above-average flow year.
- **Timber harvest** (~50% merchantable volume removal plus improvement cut) on the South Fork Watershed did not affect water yield.
- The study used [[Regression Analysis]] to assess treatment effects on [[Water Yield]] and [[Sedimentation]].
