# Pecos Pupfish (Cyprinodon pecosensis)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1997 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) request-for-information letter and status summary regarding the proposed federal listing of the [[Pecos Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*) under the Endangered Species Act. First proposed for listing consideration in 1991, the species' status was reassessed in 1997 due to continued habitat degradation and, most critically, genetic introgression from the introduced [[Sheepshead Minnow]] (*Cyprinodon variegatus*). The species has been effectively eradicated as a genetically pure lineage from its entire historic Texas range within the Pecos River mainstem, with only relictual, status-unknown populations persisting in isolated Texas sites (Salt Creek, a gravel pit near Grandfalls). Genetically pure populations are now almost entirely restricted to New Mexico, and even these are under direct threat from an advancing "hybrid swarm." The species was, at the time, a federal candidate species and state-listed as threatened in both New Mexico and Texas.

## Background & Description

- **Size:** Small-bodied fish; standard length 2.8–4.6 cm (1.1–1.8 inches).
- **Body shape:** Deep-bodied.
- **Coloration (non-breeding/general):** Gray to brown.
- **Lateral markings:** Seven to nine dark lateral bars that expand into blotches along the body.
- **Scalation:** Belly is partly unscaled.
- **Caudal fin:** Dark crescent marking at the base of the caudal fin.
- **Breeding male coloration:** Grayish-blue to iridescent body; caudal fin bears a black terminal band; pectoral fins pale yellow.

These traits distinguish *C. pecosensis* from related taxa such as [[Cyprinodon Bovinus]] (Leon Springs pupfish) and [[Cyprinodon Rubrofluviatilis]] (Red River pupfish), with which it was historically confused prior to its formal taxonomic description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historic range:** Pecos River system from Roswell, Chaves County, New Mexico, downstream to the mouth of Independence Creek, Terrell County, Texas — an aggregate range of approximately 650 river-kilometers.
- **Habitat types:** Saline springs, gypsum sinkholes, playas, and desert streams; most characteristically found in highly saline habitats that support very low species diversity, indicating a specialized tolerance for extreme osmotic/salinity conditions typical of the [[Pecos River]] drainage.
- **Current range (New Mexico):** Restricted to the Pecos River mainstem, some tributaries, and gypsum sinkholes in Chaves and Eddy counties.
- **Current range (Texas):** Considered eradicated from all historic mainstem Pecos River habitat; relictual populations of unknown status/purity persist in Salt Creek and a gravel pit near Grandfalls.
- **Land ownership/management context:** Habitat spans a mix of private, public (state and federal), and political-subdivision lands (e.g., Carlsbad Irrigation District), complicating conservation management.
- **Interspecies interaction:** Sympatric contact and hybridization with the non-native [[Sheepshead Minnow]] represents a critical ecological threat, effectively displacing pure *C. pecosensis* genotypes via introgression.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic history:** *Cyprinodon pecosensis* was formally described by Echelle and Echelle (1978); prior literature (Koster 1948, Knapp 1953, Drewry 1967) misidentified it as *Cyprinodon bovinus*, while other authors (Lui 1965, 1969; Turner and Lui 1977) referred to it as *Cyprinodon rubrofluviatilis*. Multiple other authors referred to the undescribed taxon simply as *Cyprinodon* sp. prior to 1978.
- **Genetic introgression is the primary emergent threat:** Echelle and Connor (1989) documented rapid, geographically extensive genetic introgression following secondary contact between *C. pecosensis* and *C. variegatus*. The Texas mainstem population is believed to be entirely replaced by hybrids.
- **Hybrid swarm advancing into New Mexico:** Hoagstrom and Brooks (1995) documented seven hybrid individuals in the Pecos River near Loving, Eddy County, New Mexico — upstream of the historically pure population at [[Malaga Bend]], New Mexico.
- **Loss of a key pure population:** The Malaga Bend population, once considered genetically pure, was suspected to be extirpated as of 1996 (J. Brooks, USFWS, pers. comm.), highlighting the rapid pace of hybridization-driven decline.
- **Compounding anthropogenic threats:** Beyond hybridization, the species faces habitat dewatering from irrigation depletions, water quality degradation from industrial/municipal/agricultural discharges, and lowering of water tables in sinkholes/playas due to groundwater pumping — threats persistent over at least 25 years prior to this 1997 report.
- **Vector for hybrid spread:** Natural upstream progression of the hybrid swarm, combined with human-mediated transport (e.g., bait bucket release) across natural barriers, is identified as a mechanism that could extend hybridization into currently isolated, pure sinkhole populations, risking full species extinction.
- **Regulatory status at time of report:** Candidate species under the USFWS (initial listing process begun 1991, delayed due to staffing/priority issues); listed as Threatened by both New Mexico and Texas state wildlife agencies.
- **Related taxa referenced:** The report situates *C. pecosensis* among a broader group of vulnerable arid-land pupfishes, including [[Cyprinodon Elegans]] (Comanche Springs pupfish), [[Cyprinodon Tularosa]] (White Sands pupfish), and [[Cyprinodon Bovinus]] (Leon Springs pupfish), reflecting a pattern of imperilment across the genus in the American Southwest.
