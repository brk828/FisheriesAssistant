# Federal Register Notices: Mining EIS, Desert Planning, Land Conveyance and ESA-Fisheries Policy (1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-policy

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register compilation (December 1995) contains four unrelated federal notices. Three concern land management in the western United States: a Draft [[Environmental Impact Statement]] for expanding the [[Lone Tree Mine]] in Nevada, public workshops on the Northern and Eastern [[Colorado Desert]] Coordinated Management Plan in California, and an order opening reconveyed public lands in Oregon and Washington. The fourth, the most relevant to aquatic science, is a joint [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] proposed draft policy for conserving species listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] while sustaining [[Recreational Fisheries]]. The document contains no primary field data, species inventories or water-quality measurements; its aquatic content is limited to background statistics on listed species and angler economics.

## Background & Description

The document is a set of notices from several agencies, each with a distinct purpose:

- **Lone Tree Mine Draft EIS (Nevada):** Analyzes the environmental effects of expanding gold mining operations. Alternatives are the Proposed Action and No Action. The expansion would enlarge the existing pit, ore processing facilities and overburden disposal areas, moving disposal from private land onto public lands. Signed by Nevada State Director Ann J. Morgan, dated November 30, 1995.
- **Northern and Eastern Colorado Desert Coordinated Management Plan (California):** A [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM)-led multi-agency plan. The notice schedules eight evening public workshops (7–10 p.m.) between January 8 and January 22, 1996, across Riverside, Long Beach, Joshua Tree, Palm Springs, Needles, Blythe, El Centro and Rancho Bernardo. Cooperating agencies are the National Park Service, Fish and Wildlife Service, National Biological Service, Marine Corps Air Station Yuma and the California Department of Fish and Game. Workshops cover wildlife habitats and vehicle route inventories.
- **Land Conveyance Order (Oregon and Washington):** Issued under Section 206 of the [[Federal Land Policy and Management Act]] (43 U.S.C. 1716). Patents transferred 3,310 acres in Harney County, Oregon, and 1,846.04 acres in Grant County, Washington, from federal to private ownership. A 1,280-acre reconveyed parcel (640 acres in Oregon, 640 acres in Klickitat County, Washington) opens to surface entry, effective January 18, 1996 at 8:30 a.m.
- **Draft ESA-Fisheries Policy:** A proposed joint policy from the two Services, issued under Section 4 of [[Executive Order 12962]]. Comments were due by February 12, 1996.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fisheries notice provides the only aquatic-ecological material. It states that most North American aquatic environments and biological communities have been significantly altered by human activity, and that degraded habitats are among the most commonly cited causes of decline and extirpation of federally protected aquatic species.

Key conservation status figures given (as of November 1, 1995):

- **105** fish species and **57** clam and mussel species are on the federal threatened or endangered list (50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12).
- About **36%** of U.S. fishes, **64%** of crayfishes and **69%** of freshwater mussels are considered imperiled or extinct. Data are attributed to the [[NatureServe Network]] (National Network of Natural Heritage Programs) and The Nature Conservancy.

The document also states that habitat alteration, heavy fishing pressure and introduction of non-native species have caused unexpected negative effects on ecosystem components. It argues for an [[Ecosystem Approach]] to management, grounded in species' life histories, habitat requirements and ecosystem processes. Conflicts between species recovery and recreational fishing are described as a particular concern where the two responsibilities are seen as mutually exclusive.

The Lone Tree and Colorado Desert notices contain no species data. Their environmental relevance lies in habitat disturbance from mining and in wildlife habitat planning for desert lands.

## Key Findings

**1. Lone Tree Mine expansion (Nevada)**
- Draft EIS evaluates a Proposed Action and a No Action alternative.
- Expansion would move overburden disposal onto public land. The notice gives no quantitative impact results.

**2. Colorado Desert planning (California)**
- Eight public workshops scheduled in January 1996.
- Led by BLM, with eight cooperating agencies. Focus areas include wildlife habitat and vehicle travel routes.

**3. Land conveyance (Oregon and Washington)**
- 3,310 acres (Oregon) and 1,846.04 acres (Washington) transferred to private ownership.
- 1,280 acres of reconveyed land opened to surface entry on January 18, 1996.

**4. Draft ESA-Fisheries policy (FWS and NMFS)**
- Aims to conserve listed species while continuing and enhancing recreational fisheries.
- Seeks consistency between the two agencies and collaboration with State, Tribal and Federal fisheries managers.
- Responds to Executive Order 12962, which requires federal agencies, where practical and in cooperation with States and Tribes, to improve the quantity, function, sustainable productivity and distribution of U.S. aquatic resources.

**5. Economic context for recreational fisheries**
- About **50 million** anglers spend **$24 billion** annually on tackle, equipment, food, lodging and other fishing-related costs.
- Angler-stimulated economic output exceeded **$69 billion** in 1991.
- Fishing generated over **$2.1 billion** in federal tax revenues and employed approximately **1.3 million** people nationwide.

**6. Cooperation and comment**
- Comments on the draft policy were due February 12, 1996, to the Division of Endangered Species (FWS, Arlington, Virginia) or the Endangered Species Division (NMFS, Silver Spring, Maryland).
