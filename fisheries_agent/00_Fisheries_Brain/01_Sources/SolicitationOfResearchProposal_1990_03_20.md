## Executive Summary

This document is a **Fiscal Year 1991 research solicitation letter** from the Technical Group of the Recovery Implementation Committee (RIC) for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] targeting the four endangered/rare fishes of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the text outlines administrative and scientific priorities guiding a ~$2.7 million FY91 research budget, of which a majority ($1.782 million) was earmarked for 24 "ongoing" projects (Attachment 1), with the remainder allocated to new initiatives addressing flow requirements, sediment dynamics, chemoreception, genetics, and interactions with non-native gamefish. The document reflects a multi-agency (FWS, Bureau of Reclamation, State of Colorado, State of Utah, Forest Service) collaborative recovery framework centered on the [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], and mainstem [[Colorado River]] systems.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological data are **not provided** in this administrative source; however, indirect taxonomic/genetic notes are referenced:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): Subject of electrophoretic genetic studies to assess relatedness among upper-basin populations and hatchery stocks; genetic sampling was previously limited by small sample sizes and incomplete gene pool representation.
- **[[Gila Taxonomy]]** and field studies (Project #38, lead: Forest Service) were funded to clarify taxonomic status among *Gila* species (Humpback Chub, Bonytail Chub, and Roundtail Chub complex) — indicating ongoing ambiguity in species-level identification within the genus at the time.
- Larval fish identification (Project #34, lead: BR) was a funded ongoing project, implying morphological differentiation challenges among larval stages of the four target species and co-occurring native/non-native fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document emphasizes **flow-habitat relationships** critical to the life history of these species across the Green, Yampa, and Colorado River sub-basins:

- **Spawning & Reproductive Habitat**: Studies sought to link river flow and sediment transport to substrate conditions (composition, particle size, percent fines, percent embeddedness) required for successful [[Colorado Squawfish]] spawning, embryo survival, and larval emergence.
- **Larval Drift Dynamics**: Proposed basinwide studies aimed to quantify downstream larval drift from the Yampa, Green (Desolation/Gray Canyons), and mainstem Colorado Rivers, including mortality sources, recruitment to fall young-of-year (YOY) populations, and unintended transport into [[Cataract Canyon]] and [[Lake Powell]].
- **Nursery & Backwater Habitat**: Backwater mapping and monitoring of nursery habitats (particularly in the Green River) were prioritized, alongside assessment of flooded bottomland habitat importance for both Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker.
- **Flow Regime Management**: Priority given to refining/corroborating Yampa River and 15-mile Reach (Colorado River) flow recommendations, and evaluating impacts of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations on downstream endangered fish populations.
- **Chemoreception & Imprinting**: A proposed study on [[Colorado Squawfish]] examined olfactory-mediated homing to spawning/nursery habitat, the role of imprinting/learning/genetics in habitat selection, and potential disruption by water quality degradation (heavy metals, pesticides, petroleum products).
- **Tributary Habitat**: Emphasis on evaluating the value of tributaries as refugia for remnant Razorback Sucker stocks, and the role of small tributaries in Colorado Squawfish reproduction.
- **Non-Native Species Interactions**: High-priority research on competitive interactions between endangered fishes and abundant exotic gamefish during early life stages (larvae to juvenile), and predation by non-native species on endangered fishes — reflecting a niche-overlap conservation concern in the basin.
- **Population Genetics as Ecological Context**: Recognition that multiple, potentially genetically distinct spawning stocks of Colorado Squawfish may exist within the Yampa-Green sub-basin, relevant to defining discrete population segments for recovery planning.

## Key Findings

- **Funding Structure**: FY91 program funding (~$2.7M) was split between 24 "ongoing" priority projects (Attachment 1, totaling $1,782,000) and new initiatives; lead agencies included [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], State of Colorado, State of Utah, and U.S. Forest Service.
- **Six Core New-Initiative Topics (Attachment 2)**:
  1. Refine/validate Yampa River flow recommendations.
  2. Flow-sediment-substrate relationships for Colorado Squawfish reproduction.
  3. Chemoreception/imprinting mechanisms in Colorado Squawfish.
  4. Development of quantitative recovery goals for the four species.
  5. Flow/habitat requirement studies tied to Section 7 consultation on the [[Aspinall Unit]].
  6. Evaluation of water rights acquisition for instream flow protection.
- **Recovery Goals Gap**: As of 1990, no scientifically justified, quantitative definitions of "recovery" or "self-sustaining populations" existed for any of the four species — identified as the **highest priority** research need by the Biology Research Advisory Group, requiring standardized approaches to population estimation, monitoring database review, and demographic parameters (population size, distribution, density, recruitment, age structure, spawning stock structure).
- **Larval Drift Study** flagged as highest priority by the Research Advisory Group but noted by the Technical Group as high-risk/high-cost, requiring phased, methodical implementation; any hatchery-larvae stocking component would require genetic/imprinting risk assessment to wild populations.
- **Razorback Sucker Priorities**: Determining spawning requirements/locations in the Green sub-basin and upper Colorado mainstem, confirming natural reproduction occurrence, quantifying mortality across life stages, assessing flooded bottomland habitat value, evaluating tributary refugia, and assessing reintroduction feasibility.
- **Non-Native Gamefish Conflict**: Identified as a program-wide management tension between game-fish stocking/management mandates and endangered species recovery goals — an issue paper was commissioned to formalize a mitigation strategy.
- **Habitat Development Program**: A parallel initiative evaluated artificial/enhanced habitat creation feasibility in the upper Colorado River basin as a recovery tool, pending a future demonstration project.
- **Genetics as Cross-Cutting Priority**: Genetic stock structure of Colorado Squawfish (and by extension the *Gila* taxonomy question) was deemed foundational to nearly all other recovery research areas — influencing refuge/propagation facility design, recovery goal-setting, and population augmentation protocols.
- **Process Transparency**: A standardized proposal ranking system (Attachment 4) scored new proposals 0–10 on technical merit (program consistency, urgency/jeopardy avoidance, essentiality to recovery, timeliness, feasibility, prerequisite status) plus 0–5 on proposal quality, with a formal Scope-of-Work template (Attachment 5, "Form 2") mandated for all submissions.
- **Institutional Governance**: The full annual planning cycle (Attachment 6/Schedule) spanned ~18 months from annual report submission (Dec 1989) through RIC final approval of the FY91 Annual Work Plan (Aug 1990) and contract development (Sept–Dec 1990), illustrating the multi-tiered (Technical Group → Management Group → Recovery Implementation Committee) governance structure of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]].
