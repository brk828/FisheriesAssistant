# Fishes of Deep Creek, Riley County, Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This short note reports the fish fauna of Deep Creek, Riley County, Kansas, and documents the establishment of the [[Spotted Bass]] (*Micropterus punctulatus*), a species introduced from Cowley County in 1957. The authors combine roughly 11 years of seasonal sampling with earlier published records. The checklist comprises about 40 species and taxa, plus three hybrid combinations. The note also reports morphological measurements that support intergradation between two subspecies of [[Northern Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma macrolepidotum*) in the Kansas River basin.

## Background & Description

The note is a checklist and short report prepared for the Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science. Its stated purpose is to list the fishes of Deep Creek, most of which had been recorded in earlier surveys by [[Minckley]] and colleagues, Cross, and Metcalf. Most previously known species are given with no added comment. Newly reported or noteworthy taxa receive more detailed treatment.

The fauna is grouped by family:

- **Clupeidae:** [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*), not represented by preserved specimens but repeatedly observed near the confluence of Deep Creek with the Kansas River.
- **Hiodontidae:** [[Goldeye]] (*Hiodon alosoides*).
- **Cyprinidae:** roughly 20 minnows and shiners, including [[Southern Redbelly Dace]] (*Chrosomus erythrogaster*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*), Topeka shiner (*Notropis topeka*), and Plains minnow (*Hybognathus placitus*). The Plains minnow is known from a single specimen held by the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology.
- **Catostomidae:** suckers and carpsuckers, including [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*), [[High-finned Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes velifer*), White Sucker (*Catostomus commersonii*), and Northern Redhorse.
- **Ictaluridae:** bullheads, channel catfish, stonecat (*Noturus flavus*), and [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*).
- **Centrarchidae:** Spotted Bass, [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), green sunfish, orange-spotted sunfish, and bluegill-type sunfishes.
- **Percidae:** logperch (*Percina caprodes*) and [[Orangethroat Darter]] (*Etheostoma spectabile*).

Sunfish and other taxa are cross-referenced with specimen collections from Arizona State University, the University of Kansas Museum of Natural History, and the Kansas Fish and Game Commission. A longear sunfish (*Lepomis megalotis*) recorded by Minckley was reclassified because the original record was a misidentification of orange-spotted sunfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Deep Creek is part of the Kansas River system. Sampling focused on the mainstream below and above [[Pillsbury Crossing]], where pools and riffles provide habitat for both riffle-dwelling and pool-dwelling species. The authors note that farm ponds throughout the drainage support [[Largemouth Bass]] populations, while the mainstream holds them only infrequently.

Some species may reach Deep Creek from the Kansas River, particularly the Flathead Catfish, which is expected to enter the mouth of Deep Creek during high water. Pan fishes raised in ponds may also introduce species into the creek. The authors report three hybrid combinations known from the system:

- *Chrosomus erythrogaster* × *Semotilus atromaculatus*
- *Chrosomus erythrogaster* × *Notropis cornutus*
- *Chrosomus erythrogaster* × *Notropis anogenus* (as read in the scan)

The note also lists species not yet reported from Deep Creek but likely to occur there, based on their presence in neighboring waters.

## Key Findings

- **Spotted Bass establishment:** On 16 August 1957, the authors stocked 12 adult and a number of young-of-the-year Spotted Bass into Deep Creek (Secs. 23 and 26, T.11S, R.11E, Riley County). The source was Otter Creek in Cowley County (Secs. 5, 6, and 8, T.33S, R.8E). The OCR text for the young-of-the-year count is ambiguous. The authors describe this as the first introduction of the species into the Kansas River system. Later stockings are recorded by Metcalf (1966) and Cross (1967).
- **Spotted Bass persistence:** Fish from the original stocking were collected in 1957 and 1958. No Spotted Bass were obtained anywhere in the area from 1959 to 1966. In 1957, seven individuals measuring 50 to 260 mm standard length were taken from the two pools immediately below Pillsbury Crossing.
- **Northern Redhorse intergradation:** A series of 31 specimens (107 to 150 mm standard length), collected at Pillsbury Crossing in 1967 (Arizona State University collection), substantiates intergradation between *M. m. macrolepidotum* and *M. m. pisolabrum* in the Kansas River basin. Mean upper lip width-to-lower lip width ratios were 4.3 mm (n = 31) for the Deep Creek fish, compared with 3.9 mm (n = 7) for *M. m. pisolabrum* and a range of roughly 4.8 to 5.6 mm for the typical subspecies *M. m. macrolepidotum* (n = 7). The measurement range for the Deep Creek series is 3.5 to 5.2 mm.
- **Gizzard Shad:** Repeatedly observed near the confluence with the Kansas River, though not collected.
- **Logperch:** Specimens were taken both above and below Pillsbury Crossing from 1966 to 1967.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** The longear sunfish record was a misidentification of orange-spotted sunfish. Three hybrid combinations are documented for the system.
- **Checklist status:** The fauna comprises about 40 species, with the Spotted Bass as the principal new establishment, and the authors identify the list as a basis for continued study by others.
