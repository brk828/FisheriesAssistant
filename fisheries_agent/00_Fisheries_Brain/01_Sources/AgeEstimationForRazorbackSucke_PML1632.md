# Age Estimation for Razorback Sucker from Lake Mohave, Arizona and Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study by McCarthy and Minckley (Arizona State University, 1987) tests seven bony structures for estimating the age of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) from [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. Only sectioned [[Otolith Sectioning|otoliths]] gave readable, interpretable annuli. Wild Lake Mohave fish were estimated at roughly 24 to 44 years old at capture in 1980-83, and no recruitment was evident. The authors conclude that the stock should disappear before the year 2000 unless the trend reverses.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The razorback sucker is a large [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. It was historically abundant and was an important food fish for Indians and early settlers.
- **Range change:** The species once ranged from the Green River in Wyoming south to the Colorado River delta in northwestern Mexico. It is now largely confined to the upper basin and to Lake Mohave.
- **Lower-basin decline:** Lower-basin mainstem reservoirs were initially colonized by large numbers of razorback suckers. The species disappeared from Gila River system reservoirs in the 1950s, about 20 years after dam closure. Recent lower-basin records are infrequent and mostly of large adults.
- **Lake Mohave population:** The authors describe the Lake Mohave fish as consistently large (females averaged 50 cm and males 45 cm SL in 1981) and presumably old. Young disappear before reaching about 12 mm total length, so there is no evidence of recruitment. High incidences of blindness and tumors were also noted.
- **Prior work:** [[Minckley]] (1983) could not age the stock from scales but proposed that it originated before the construction of [[Davis Dam]] (1942-51) and closure of [[Lake Mohave]] (1951).
- **Objectives:** The study aimed to (1) assess the value of various bony structures for age estimation, (2) test the hypothesis of substantial age for Lake Mohave fish, and (3) examine apparent growth patterns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling and material**
- Wild fish were collected from Lake Mohave in 1980-81 with trammel nets and beach seines. Specimens were sexed, measured, weighed, and prepared as dried skeletons. Wild-caught fish were drawn from several sources, including [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] broodfish and Arizona and California agency collections. The scan's total for wild-caught fish is partly illegible; the best reading is 99.
- Forty-five hatchery-produced, pond-reared fish of known age were provided by Dexter and Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery. They came from four year classes: 1974 (4 fish), 1979 (6), 1981 (5), and 1984 (30). Of these, 25 hatchery fish and 14 wild fish were used for structure study.
- Lake Mohave is monomictic. Otolith annuli form mainly in summer, consistent with seasonal temperature variation.

**Diet and feeding**
- The species has a protrusible, almost terminal mouth and closely spaced gill rakers. These traits indicate facultative [[Planktivory]].
- Adults in Lake Mohave feed mostly on zooplankton (Marsh 1987). Rapid growth in the filling reservoir may reflect greater availability of planktonic food.

**Threats and population trends**
- The population shows no detectable recruitment since 1958 and little since 1954.
- High rates of blindness and tumors in Lake Mohave specimens were noted.
- The authors predict extirpation before the year 2000 unless recruitment resumes.

## Key Findings

### 1. Structure evaluation
- **Usable structures:** Complete, widely spaced annuli on known-age hatchery fish were visible on all 7 structures tested (opercular bone, cleithrum, branchiostegal rays, vertebral centra, scales, pectoral fin rays, and otoliths). Hatchery young-of-year showed no annuli.
- **Wild fish:** Annuli were hard to read in large wild fish. Crowding, dense ossification, and articular-end masking led to underestimation.
- **Scales:** Scales were unreliable. One individual gave estimates from 0 to more than 25 marks. Three of seven scales from another fish showed no annuli. The scale method was judged "questionable to perhaps useless."
- **Pectoral fin rays:** These showed problems similar to the scales, and rays from the same fish gave different ages.
- **Otoliths (sectioned only):** Of 88 fish examined, 84.1% showed countable annuli. Unsectioned otoliths were also compromised by dense ossification.

### 2. Age estimates
- Lake Mohave fish were estimated at 24 to 44 years old at capture in 1980-83. The 44-year value is the greatest apparent age from unquestioned, continuous marks in the otolith section.
- Using otoliths, the authors estimated that 62 of the sampled fish (about 68%) hatched before or during the construction and closure of Lake Mohave.
- An otolith-aged 34-year-old female is illustrated in the figures.

### 3. Recruitment and population
- No recruitment is evident in Lake Mohave since 1958, and little since 1954.
- All individuals taken in recent years were large adults.
- The authors predict that Lake Mohave razorback suckers will be extirpated before 2000 unless the trend reverses.

### 4. Growth patterns
- Back-calculation from sagittal radius was highly correlated with standard length at capture (r² = 0.97, log-transformed data).
- First-year growth averaged 26.7 ± 0.8% of SL at capture.
- Approximately 50% of SL at capture was attained by the fifth or sixth year.
- Females reached an average SL of 140 ± 4 mm at the first otolith annulus and 276 ± 7 mm at the fifth. Males were significantly smaller (103 ± 4 and 214 ± 8 mm; p < 0.05). This indicates sexual dimorphism established early.
- Fish that hatched after Davis Dam completion showed accelerated growth. Growth to the first annulus did not differ significantly, but growth to the fifth annulus was significantly greater for both sexes (p < 0.05). The authors attribute this to lentic conditions.

### 5. Longevity and size records
- Maximum natural longevity is unknown.
- Largest Lake Mohave specimens include an unweighed female of 57.6 cm SL and an unmeasured 4.99 kg female.
- Anecdotal weights of 5.45-7.26 kg from Saguaro Lake and archaeological estimates of length are regarded as suspect or indicative only.
- A 52.4 cm SL male from the lower Colorado River was otolith-aged to a considerable age, supporting long life in the species.

### 6. Methodological conclusion
Sectioned otoliths are the most reliable structure for age estimation in this species. Other structures are useful only for young fish or for confirming the earliest annuli. The authors recommend [[Otolith Sectioning]] over scales, fin rays, and whole bones for aging large adults.
