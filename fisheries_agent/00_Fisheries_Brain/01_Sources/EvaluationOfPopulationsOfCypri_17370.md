# Evaluation of Native Fish Populations in Shared Arizona–Sonora Watersheds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #ictaluridae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1996 technical report, prepared by José R. Campoy-Favela of the [[Instituto del Medio Ambiente y del Desarrollo Sustentable del Estado de Sonora]] (IMADES) for the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), evaluates the status of five target native fishes: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Sonoran Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Sonoran Chub]] and [[Gila Chub]]. Field surveys were conducted in August 1995 and March, July–August 1996 across six watersheds shared by Arizona and Sonora. The fish were rare in most collections. Non-native species were common at several sites, and the desert pupfish appeared to be declining in the [[Colorado River Delta]]. The report recommends further surveys and control of exotic fauna.

## Background & Description

The project was approved by the U.S.–México Joint Committee in 1991 and started in 1994 under a cooperative agreement between AGFD and [[Centro Ecologico De Sonora]] (CES), signed July 25, 1994. Funding came from AGFD's Nongame and Endangered Wildlife Program, with additional support from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and IMADES.

The border region of about 400 miles contains true desert, arid grassland, Madrean oak-pine forest and river basins. Human demand for water has extirpated several native fish populations from streams and ciénegas on both sides of the border. Some populations persist, mainly on the Arizona side. Protected areas include the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] and the [[San Pedro River]] Riparian Conservation Area on the Arizona side. On the Sonora side, only the Colorado River Delta (including the Santa Clara and El Doctor ciénegas) and the Pinacate and Gran Desierto areas held reserve status at the time.

Study watersheds, from east to west, were:
- [[Rio Yaqui Basin]]
- [[Gila River Basin]] (San Pedro and Santa Cruz rivers)
- [[De La Concepcion River]] basin
- Sonoyta River basin
- Lower [[Colorado River]] basin

Streams are small and vary greatly in water level and riparian habitat. Eastern watersheds (Yaqui, Gila, De la Concepción) are mesic and support more diverse fish communities. Western watersheds (Sonoyta, Colorado) are more arid.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.** Crews used seines (5 m long, 1 m deep, 3.2 mm and 1 mm mesh), dip nets, hand nets and [[Electrofishing]] over reaches of at least 200 m. Species composition, relative abundance, life stage, associated biota and external parasites were recorded. [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPUE) was calculated for most sites. Habitat variables included flow, turbidity, salinity, temperature, width, depth, microhabitat type, and plant cover. Voucher specimens were preserved in 10% formalin in the field and later stored in isopropyl alcohol at the CES Fish Reference Collection in Hermosillo.

**Associated biota.** Surveys noted [[Red Swamp Crayfish]] (*Procambarus clarki*) in very large numbers at Rancho El Sauz and on the San Pedro mainstream, mostly juveniles. The authors flagged crayfish as a potential threat because they serve as intermediate hosts for parasitic helminths, including those infecting native fishes. Amphibians, aquatic turtles and waterbirds were also recorded.

**Water quality.** The authors did not sample water quality formally but observed generally good conditions. Poor conditions occurred at Arroyo San Bernardino (cattle use) and near José María Morelos on the San Pedro (Arroyo San Rafael). Gomez et al. (1994) reported high chloride, sulfate and heavy metals in water from near Cananea. Salinity was 5–7 ppt at Cienega de Santa Clara and 0–5 ppt at El Doctor.

**Exotic species.** The report documents [[Mosquitofish]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Sailfin Molly]], fathead minnow and green sunfish as non-native. Black bullhead and green sunfish were new records for the San Pedro mainstream.

## Key Findings

**Species inventory.** Appendix B lists 18 species across 6 families. Thirteen are native and five are exotic: fathead minnow, black bullhead, sailfin molly, mosquitofish and green sunfish.

**Rio Yaqui Basin**
- Arroyo San Bernardino (Site 1): [[Longfin Dace]] was the most abundant native fish. Mosquitofish numbered 15 individuals. A single juvenile [[Yaqui Chub]] was collected, indicating the species is still rare here.
- Cajón Bonito (Site 2, March 26, 1996): a healthy community of seven native species. [[Yaqui Catfish]] were present in good numbers and multiple age classes. The Yaqui topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*) was relatively abundant and occurred at all sites.
- The Yaqui topminnow was not found in San Bernardino Creek, though earlier surveys recorded it.

**San Pedro River**
- Arroyo El Sauz–Villa Verde: 116 longfin dace collected (most juveniles), plus 51 mosquitofish and a juvenile black bullhead.
- Mainstream at Rancho Los Corrales: longfin dace (13 preserved) was most abundant, followed by [[Desert Sucker]] (9). Black bullhead was abundant in deep pools. Heavy external parasite infestation was noted.
- Las Palomas (Site 3): no native species were found. Only fathead minnow and green sunfish were present.
- [[Cienega Los Fresnos]]: sampling by electrofishing and nets found no fish. Frogs, toads, tiger salamanders and aquatic turtles were seen.
- Arroyo Los Alisos, Arroyo Las Nutrias and Arroyo San Rafael: only exotics (green sunfish, mosquitofish).

**Santa Cruz River**
- Ojo Zarco: longfin dace (3), [[Sonora Sucker]] (11), Sonoran topminnow (9), mosquitofish (10).
- Mainstream at railroad junction (Site 3): longfin dace (79), Sonoran topminnow (14), Sonora sucker (24), desert sucker (19). No exotics.
- Ejido Mascarefias (Site 4): longfin dace (125) was most abundant, with 3 Sonoran topminnow. The site is a weekend recreation area with habitat alteration and solid waste.

**De La Concepción River**
- Arroyo Milpillas: longfin dace (114) and [[Sonoran Chub]] (43) were abundant. No exotics were found, and habitat was rated good.

**Colorado River Delta (Santa Clara and El Doctor)**
- Desert pupfish was extremely rare: one individual at Hunter's Camp and one at a small stream at El Doctor.
- Sailfin molly and mosquitofish were abundant. At Rancho La Atlantida, sailfin molly made up 95% of the sample.
- The report concludes pupfish is declining, mainly due to competition and aggression from exotics.

**Conservation status (Mexican NOM, DOF 1994)**
- Threatened: longfin dace; Sonoran topminnow.
- Endangered: Mexican stoneroller, Sonora sucker, Gila chub, Yaqui chub, desert pupfish, Yaqui catfish.
- The authors argue that several listings do not match abundance on either side of the border. Longfin dace, Mexican stoneroller, Sonora sucker and Sonoran topminnow are abundant, while Yaqui chub, Yaqui catfish, Gila chub and desert pupfish are endangered at international, national and state levels.
- Sonoran chub remained stable and healthy in Sonora.

**Status and recommendations**
- The overall status of target species is secure, but Gila chub in the San Pedro and desert pupfish at Santa Clara and El Doctor are uncertain because few individuals were found.
- Gila chub was not collected in the San Pedro, though earlier reports placed it at Cienega Los Fresnos and La Cieneguita.
- Habitat was judged appropriate near the San Pedro border and downstream of Santa Cruz town. Impacts were observed at Arroyo San Bernardino, Arroyo El Sauz, Arroyo Las Nutrias and north of Santa Cruz town.
- A survey of the Sonoyta watershed is needed for desert pupfish.

**Proposed work plan (1996–1997)**
- Remaining funds (IMADES, $1,900 USD) cover at least two evaluation trips: Sonoyta River (pupfish) and De la Concepción (Sonoran chub).
- Planned schedule: Sonoyta survey (1996); De la Concepción survey (November 1996); presentation at the Desert Fishes Council symposium in La Paz, B.C.S. (November 1996); San Bernardino survey for Yaqui topminnow and chub (December 1996); Arroyo Los Fresnos survey for Gila chub (January 1997); draft report (February 1997); final report (March 1997).
