# Yuma Cove Backwater Razorback Sucker Restoration Activities October 1990 - October 1991

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #restoration #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This chronology documents a year-long restoration effort at [[Yuma Cove]] backwater on [[Lake Mohave]] to create spawning habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The project involved chemical renovation to remove non-native fish (October 1990), stocking of 90 adult razorback suckers (30 females, 60 males) in January 1991, and intensive monitoring through October 1991. Larvae were successfully collected February through April 1991, indicating spawning occurred. However, no young fish were found after May, and the backwater experienced severe macrophyte overgrowth and deteriorating water quality by summer. Of the 90 stocked adults, 55 were recovered alive and returned to the lake, 2 were found dead, leaving 33 unaccounted for.

## Background & Description

The [[Yuma Cove]] backwater restoration was a collaborative effort involving [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW), and [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS). The backwater was chemically renovated to eliminate non-native species before stocking with adult [[Razorback Sucker]].

Initial renovation on October 4, 1990 used rotenone, killing approximately 1000 fish, mostly [[Carp]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Bluegill Sunfish]], with few [[Catfish]] and a single [[Striped Bass]]. A second treatment with Antimycin on January 11, 1991 yielded no fish, confirming complete eradication.

Adult suckers were collected from [[Arizona Bay]] on January 23-24, 1991 using trammel net seining. All fish were weighed, measured, sexed, and PIT-tagged before stocking: 30 females and 60 males.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The backwater experienced dramatic ecological changes during the monitoring period. Initial conditions supported spawning, but by summer 1991, severe problems emerged:

**Vegetation**: By June 10, tremendous aquatic macrophyte growth occurred, with vertical fronds reaching 10-14 feet in some areas. By July 29, the majority of the backwater was choked with macrophytes, with over 90% coverage by August 29.

**Water Quality**: July 2, 1991 measurements revealed supersaturated oxygen conditions in the backwater rather than expected oxygen sag. However, by August 29, bottom sediments showed extremely reducing conditions with strong hydrogen-sulfide odors and color at the mud/water interface.

**Water Depth**: Maximum depth declined from approximately 4.5 meters in June to 1.9 meters in July, 1.8 meters in August, and 1.5 meters by early September, before lake elevation rose in October.

**Parasites**: Adult fish were heavily infested with the parasitic copepod *Lernea*, with infestation severity increasing from June through July. Three of four participants contracted swimmer's itch during June sampling.

## Key Findings

**Spawning Success**: Larvae were collected during February, March, and April 1991 using night-lighting techniques. Mean larval sizes were:
- February 21: Lake 11.0 mm (range 10.4-12.1, n=6); Backwater 10.5 mm (range 10.2-10.9, n=7)
- March 18: Lake 11.3 mm (range 10.5-13.7, n=39); Backwater 12.1 mm (range 10.0-13.9, n=30)
- April 15: Lake 12.0 mm (range 10.6-13.1, n=28); Backwater 12.0 mm (range 9.6-19.6, n=44)

**Recruitment Failure**: No young fish were found in May sampling or during intensive June 10 snorkeling and scuba diving surveys, despite successful larval production.

**Adult Recovery**: Of 90 stocked adults, 55 were recovered alive and returned to the lake, 2 were found dead (1 recovered with readable tag), leaving 33 unaccounted for. Recovery efforts occurred primarily June through September 1991.

**PIT Tag Success**: Most recovered fish (e.g., 11 of 12 in June, 16 of 18 in July, 10 of 12 in August 29) had readable PIT tags, demonstrating effective tagging protocol.

**Water Quality Data**: Detailed limnological measurements documented temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, and conductivity at multiple dates, revealing deteriorating conditions through summer despite initial supersaturation.

**Habitat Degradation**: The combination of extreme macrophyte growth, declining water levels, reducing sediment conditions, and parasite infestation created unsuitable conditions for juvenile fish survival and adult fish health by late summer 1991.
