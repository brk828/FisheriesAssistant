# Razorback Sucker Capture Records, Lake Powell and San Juan River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is a short, partly illegible internal correspondence memo (OCR-degraded, dated circa 1982) from [[Wayne Gustaveson]] to [[Randy Radant]] about captures of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Powell]] and the [[San Juan River]]. It reports recent gill-net and angler captures and attaches a second page listing earlier collection records by location, month, year and count. Exact figures and some place names could not be reliably recovered from the scan and are flagged below.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Memorandum, with a transmittal of fish capture records.
- **Subject:** Capture of rare fish in Lake Powell.
- **Species:** Razorback Sucker, a large catostomid of the [[Colorado River Basin]] that is rare enough to be tracked individually.
- **Sender/recipient:** Wayne Gustaveson to Randy Radant. A later signature block names Dale Hepworth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Piute Farms Wash (San Juan County), April 1982:** Experimental gill nets were used, and a total of 6 fish were collected. Fish were measured in total length (TL). The lengths are only partly legible, roughly in the range of 355 to 560 mm TL. The memo describes mottled coloration and pronounced tubercles on the body, which are associated with spawning condition. Fish were released alive.
- **Neskahi Canyon (San Juan County), November 1984:** Gill nets with experimental effort captured one fish, described as about 15 inches long. Net-caught fish were released alive.
- **Angler capture, September 1977:** An angler caught one Razorback Sucker, which was subsequently sent to a museum or collection. The memo indicates the specimen's storage status.
- **Second page, collection records:** A list of earlier captures by location (including Red Canyon, listed twice), month (March, April, June), and year (1972, 1973, 1975, 1976). Counts per record are small, mostly 1 or 2 fish. The page states that sampling has been conducted each year since the earlier records. Several entries are too garbled to transcribe reliably.

## Key Findings

- Six Razorback Suckers were captured in April 1982 at [[Piute Farms Wash]] using [[Gill Net Sampling]], and all were released alive.
- Captured fish showed spawning-season traits (pronounced tubercles and coloration), suggesting adults were in reproductive condition.
- A single fish was netted at [[Neskahi Canyon]] in November 1984.
- An angler capture in September 1977 yielded a specimen that was sent for storage or study.
- Historical records (1972 to 1976) document repeated, low-count captures at [[Red Canyon]], and the memo states that annual sampling has continued since then.
- Because Razorback Sucker is rare, the memo emphasizes individual-level tracking and live release of captured fish.
