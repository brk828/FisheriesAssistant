## Executive Summary
This memo documents a two-day (July 6–7, 1998) fish community survey conducted by The Nature Conservancy (TNC) and Bureau of Land Management (BLM) personnel along [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona. The primary objective appears to have been monitoring the distribution and relative abundance of native cyprinids — particularly the [[Aravaipa Shiner]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]] — while also tracking the presence/extent of invasive species, notably [[Red Shiner]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]]. The survey used a systematic electroshocking protocol across 11 discrete 100 m stream segments plus two additional pool habitats. Results indicated a low incidence of non-native fishes and strong recruitment (abundant young-of-the-year) among native species, suggesting favorable conditions for the native fish assemblage at the time of sampling.

## Background & Description
The source document is a field survey memo and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of the species encountered. However, the taxa referenced are well-characterized in the literature as follows:

- **[[Aravaipa Shiner]]** (*Notropis formosus*): A small-bodied cyprinid endemic to the [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed, notable as a narrow-range endemic minnow specifically targeted in this monitoring effort.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*): A small native cyprinid with a distinctive spiny dorsal ray, adapted to swift riffle habitats.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): A small, slender-bodied benthic cyprinid with a subterminal mouth, adapted for clinging to substrate in fast-flowing riffles.
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*): A non-native, deep-bodied cyprinid known for aggressive competitive and hybridization impacts on native southwestern minnows.
- **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** (*Ameiurus natalis*): A non-native ictalurid catfish with barbels, tolerant of warm, sluggish water including pooled/impounded habitats.

No length, weight, or meristic data were recorded in this memo.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: [[Aravaipa Creek]] is a perennial Sonoran Desert stream characterized by riffle-run-pool sequences, supporting a rare assemblage of native fishes recognized as a conservation priority.
- **Survey extent**: Sampling occurred from the confluence with [[Parsons Canyon]] upstream to ~600 m below [[Turkey Creek]], encompassing riffle/run habitat (sites A–K) and two lentic habitats — the [[Tapia Diversion Pond]] and a pool downstream of the Guest House station.
- **Native species niche**: [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] are riffle-obligate species requiring swift, well-oxygenated flowing water — their abundance (including young-of-the-year) indicates suitable spawning and recruitment conditions during summer 1998.
- **Invasive species niche**: [[Yellow Bullhead]] were found primarily in slower-water/pool habitats, consistent with their ecological preference for lentic conditions; their occurrence was concentrated downstream of the [[Goat Cave Road]] crossing (Site H), suggesting a possible upstream invasion boundary at that time.
- **Absence of Red Shiner**: No [[Red Shiner]] were captured during either survey day, indicating that this invasive competitor had not established (or was undetectable) within the surveyed reach as of July 1998.

## Key Findings
- **Sampling design**: Electroshocking was conducted in 100 m transects, spaced at 300 m intervals moving upstream, yielding 11 total site segments (A–K) across two survey days.
- **Day 1 (July 6, 1998)**: Sites A–F sampled from the [[Parsons Canyon]] confluence upstream toward [[Turkey Creek]]. Catch: 0 [[Red Shiner]], 8 [[Yellow Bullhead]]. No mention of exact spikedace/loach minnow counts, but qualitatively "good numbers" with abundant young-of-the-year.
- **Day 2 (July 7, 1998)**: Sites G–K sampled, plus two additional pools ([[Tapia Diversion Pond]] and a pool downstream of the Guest House station). Only a single juvenile [[Yellow Bullhead]] was captured, taken downstream of the [[Goat Cave Road]] crossing (Site H); no exotic species were found upstream of this point.
- **Native fish status**: Both survey days documented healthy populations of native [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], with strong young-of-the-year recruitment — a positive indicator for the reproductive health of the native assemblage in this reach of [[Aravaipa Creek]].
- **Invasive species distribution**: Non-native [[Yellow Bullhead]] appear restricted to lower/downstream reaches near pooled habitat, with an apparent upstream limit near the [[Goat Cave Road]] crossing; [[Red Shiner]] were entirely absent from the sampled reach during this survey.
- **Field team**: Survey conducted by Ed Brunson, Mark Haberstich, and Dave Gori (TNC), and Ben Robles (BLM), in coordination with Heidi Blasius of the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
