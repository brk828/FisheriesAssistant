# Economic Value of Fishing and Hunting in Utah (1955 Survey)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-economics

## Executive Summary

This Utah State Department of Fish and Game publication (Publication No. 7, Federal Aid Division, submitted February 1957) reports an economic survey of sportsmen's expenditures on hunting and fishing in 1955, conducted by the [[Bureau Of Economic And Business Research]] at the University of Utah. The study estimates that total hunting and fishing spending in Utah reached about $44 million, of which residents spent $41 million and non-residents $3 million. The authors frame this as the economic value of the state's [[Hunting And Fishing Industry]], measured through monetary expenditure rather than esthetic or recreational value. Species and habitat data are not reported; the focus is on sportsmen, spending categories, and economic comparisons.

## Background & Description

- **Motivation:** Utah lacked reliable state-level data, since the [[National Survey Of Fishing And Hunting]] (1956, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) could not be applied to individual states because state samples were too small.
- **Commission:** Authorized by the [[Utah Fish And Game Commission]] under Federal Aid Project FW-2-R-1 of the [[Federal Aid In Fish And Wildlife Restoration]] program.
- **Scope:** Expenditures only. The survey did not measure recreational or esthetic value of Utah's forests and streams.
- **Population:** All holders of 1955 hunting and/or fishing licenses. Licensing was near-universal: anyone over 12 must be licensed to fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is primarily economic and does not describe species ecology, aquatic habitats, or water chemistry. Relevant context includes:

- Utah's fishing and hunting opportunities span "almost all parts of the state," within easy reach of most of the population.
- Named game and fish categories include deer, elk, antelope, pheasants, ducks, geese, sage grouse, quail, rabbits, varmints, stream fishing and lake fishing.
- Fishing in Utah Lake (catfishing) is mentioned only as an example in questionnaire instructions.
- Comparisons with other states (Washington, Colorado, Idaho, New Hampshire, Arizona, Tennessee) note that coastal and freshwater fishing access influences spending levels.

## Key Findings

**Total expenditure**
- Residents spent over $41 million (Table 1 adjusted basis: about $34 million).
- Non-residents spent $3 million in Utah (about $2.4 million excluding license fees).
- Total: $44,054,000 including license fees and without adjustment; $36,355,000 with adjustment and excluding non-resident license fees.

**Participants and per-person spending (residents, 1955)**
- 185,000 individuals hunted and/or fished (roughly one in four Utahns).
- Fished only: 35,500 people, average $93, total $3.3 million.
- Hunted only: 54,000 people, average $105, total $5.7 million.
- Hunted and fished: 95,500 people, average $335, total $32 million.

**Spending categories (residents)**
- Equipment: about $16 million (nearly 40% of spending).
- Transportation: over $10 million.
- Food and meals: over $5.5 million.
- Lodging, supplies, dog expenses and other items make up the remainder.

**Spending by activity (residents, with allocation of joint costs)**
- Fishing: about $20.7 million.
- Big game: about $10.5 million.
- Waterfowl: about $4 million.
- Upland game birds: about $3.3 million.
- Small game: about $2.6 million.

**Popularity (share of resident license holders in each activity)**
- Deer hunting: 62.4%.
- Stream fishing: 49.6%.
- Lake fishing: 45.6%.
- Pheasant hunting: 44.2%.
- Duck hunting: 20.2%.

**Typical Utah sportsman (Table 5)**
- Average age 36, hunting experience 16 years, fishing experience 17 years.
- Equipment investment of $318 per person; about 12.5 days per year at favorite recreation.
- Women make up 12.4% of sportsmen.

**Non-resident spending**
- Hunting accounted for $2.1 million; fishing for less than $1 million.
- Deer hunting was the largest single account, over $2 million.

**Out-of-state spending by Utah residents**
- About 27,500 residents hunted or fished outside Utah, spending about $3 million (average $109).
- Fewer than 5% went out of state to hunt.

**Economic comparisons**
- Sportsmen's spending exceeded the value of Utah wheat ($9.2 million) and sugar beets ($5.1 million); total farm crops were $37 million.
- It exceeded cattle and calves ($31.7 million) and dairy products ($25.2 million).
- It was greater than several retail categories (for example, apparel at $36 million).
- Only copper ($43.4 million in minerals listed) exceeded hunting and fishing spending among major minerals.
- Roughly 5% of all retail trade is estimated to originate in hunting and fishing activity.

**Methodology and reliability**
- Resident sample: 1,100 drawn from 208,000 resident licenses; 850 interviewed.
- Non-resident sample: 2,055 mailed from 25,000 non-resident license holders; 1,886 delivered; 70.9% return rate (75.7% including late replies).
- Estimates are reported at a 95% confidence level.
- Equipment investment figures are considered the least reliable, as respondents valued their own gear.
- Memory bias likely leads to underreporting. Hunting-dog expenses were reported at $28.36 per owner, which the authors judged too low to cover a year of feeding.

**Interpretation**
- The authors conclude that hunting and fishing is "big business" in Utah and the state's most important recreational activity, and that wildlife warrants serious planning and development consideration.
