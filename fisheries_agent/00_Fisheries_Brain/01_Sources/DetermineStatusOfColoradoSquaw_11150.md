# Determine status of Colorado squawfish (population structure analysis) in the upper Colorado River including the evaluation of previously stocked fish

## Executive Summary
This FY93 Annual Project Report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project #26) presents findings from a multi-year (1991–1993) mark-recapture and radio-telemetry study of the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), a large endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River Basin. The study, led by Douglas B. Osmundson (USFWS Colorado River Fishery Project), aimed to determine population status/trend over a 15-year comparison window, generate population size estimates via PIT-tag mark-recapture, and evaluate the survival and reproductive integration of hatchery-stocked squawfish released in 1982–1984. The "scare-and-snare" (trammel netting of backwater mouths + shoreline electrofishing) technique proved effective during spring runoff and became a model for a new adult monitoring program. Key outputs include population estimates for upper and lower river segments, evidence of ongoing recruitment and downstream-to-upstream colonization dynamics, and confirmation that surviving hatchery fish behaved similarly to wild fish during spawning migrations.

## Background & Description
- **Size classes tracked**: Subadults and adults, ranging from ~350 mm TL to 600+ mm TL.
- **Cohort growth trajectory**: A strong cohort tracked across years grew from 350–400 mm TL (1991) → 400–450 mm TL (1992) → 450–500 mm TL or larger (1993), indicating detectable annual growth increments in wild subadults.
- **Regional size disparity**: 
  - Upper river (above [[Westwater Canyon]]) adults were predominantly in the 500–600 mm TL range, stable across the 14-year record.
  - Largest mean size recorded in the uppermost 15-mile reach: **629 mm TL**.
- **Age estimates**: Nose-tagged (coded-wire) hatchery recaptures aged at 8–9 years old for individuals measuring 535–560 mm TL, consistent with independent aging work by Hawkins (1992).
- **Tagging methods used**: PIT tags (primary mark-recapture tool), coded-wire nose tags (hatchery origin marker, 1982–1984 stocking cohort), and radio transmitters (movement tracking).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use**: Adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] seasonally occupy backwaters during spring runoff, making this the optimal capture window. Side-canyon mouth backwaters are critical habitat but highly vulnerable to siltation during low-flow years, which blocks fish access.
- **Riverwide distribution pattern**: 
  - **Subadults** concentrate in lower reaches, particularly the **Moab to confluence** section.
  - **Adults** concentrate in upper reaches, especially the **[[Grand Valley]]**.
  - This spatial segregation suggests the upper river adult population is sustained via continuous colonization/immigration of subadults and young adults from lower-river nursery areas rather than local recruitment.
- **Spawning site fidelity**: Confirmed spawning-period aggregations near the new **Clifton drop structure (RM 180)** and a previously identified (1986–88) suspected spawning site near **RM 130–134** at the Colorado-Utah state line, still in use as of 1993.
- **Hatchery vs. wild interaction**: Radio-tagged hatchery-origin fish exhibited spawning-season movements overlapping with wild adults (e.g., co-location near Clifton drop structure), supporting the hypothesis that non-imprinted stocked fish integrate behaviorally with wild populations upon reaching adulthood.
- **Interconnectivity**: Two fish PIT-tagged in the lower river in 1992 were recaptured in the upper river in 1993, providing direct evidence of the lower-to-upper river migratory/colonization pathway.

## Key Findings
- **Sampling effort/yield**: Riverwide adult/subadult captures increased across years — 84 (1991), 91 (1992), 163 (1993) — reflecting both effort and possibly improved technique refinement.
- **Population estimates**:
  - Upper river (above Westwater Canyon): **~200 adults** (95% CI: 120–350), or ~3.2 fish/mile — indicating a relatively small adult population.
  - Lower river (Green River confluence to Westwater Canyon), Lincoln-Petersen estimate: **1,535 subadults/adults in 1992** (95% CI: 63–3,210), declining to **754 in 1993** (95% CI: 340–1,168).
  - Wide confidence intervals attributed to low recapture rates; the apparent 1992–1993 decline may reflect mortality and emigration of young fish transitioning into the upper river population.
- **Hatchery stocking evaluation**: Of fish stocked 1982–1984, only 3 coded-wire nose-tagged individuals were recovered pre-study (1990), plus one each in 1991 and 1992 (none in 1993) — suggesting low overall survivorship to adulthood but confirming some successful recruitment into the spawning population.
- **Radio telemetry results**: Tracked hatchery- and wild-origin fish showed convergent spawning-season movement patterns toward known/suspected spawning sites (RM 180 and RM 130–134), supporting behavioral compatibility between stocked and wild fish.
- **Methodological insight**: Electrofishing was the only reliable capture method during low-flow years when backwater habitats became silt-plugged; a flexible, multi-gear approach is recommended for future monitoring, especially for larger rivers like the Green River, where greater simultaneous crew effort would be needed.
- **Program integration**: Data were pooled with the broader monitoring program and analyzed in collaboration with Dr. Ken Burnham (CSU), feeding into the development of the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP).
- **Budget**: FY93 funds provided and expended were fully matched at $44,600, with no discrepancy.
