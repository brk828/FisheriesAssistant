# Comments on the Preliminary Draft Lower Colorado River, Big River Fishes Management Plan

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #conservation-policy #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is a formal comment letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regarding the Advance Copy of the Preliminary Draft **Lower Colorado River, Big River Fishes Management Plan** (1995). Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological field data, the letter critiques the scope, rationale, organizational structure, and taxonomic/geographic classifications used in the draft plan. It addresses management of federally listed **big river fishes** — notably the [[Colorado Squawfish]] — within the Lower [[Colorado River]] basin, including reintroduction efforts in the Salt and Verde Rivers, bio-regional delineation issues, and jurisdictional authority over fish population manipulation in Arizona. The letter is a key primary source illustrating inter-agency conservation policy negotiation during the mid-1990s for endangered Southwestern U.S. riverine fishes.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological descriptions of species are provided in this source, as the document is a policy/management review rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study. However, several taxa relevant to the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] "big river fishes" assemblage are named and their taxonomic/status designations disputed:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — large-bodied [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] historically requiring "large-sized individuals" for successful reintroduction; hatchery-reared progeny referenced as being of insufficient size for establishment success.
- **[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) — cyprinid recommended for inclusion in the Bio-Region II indigenous species list (previously omitted).
- **[[Virgin Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*) — corrected listing status from "Candidate 2" to "proposed threatened."
- **[[Yaqui Sucker]]** (*Catostomus bernardini*) — corrected status from "endangered" to "unlisted."
- **[[Sonoran Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*), referred to inconsistently in the draft as "Gila sucker" — common name standardization recommended for Bio-Region IV.
- **[[Gila Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) — flagged as erroneously listed as indigenous to Bio-Region II.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The document frames ecological context primarily through **hydrologic and administrative geography** rather than species-specific niche descriptions:

- The Lower [[Colorado River]] basin is institutionally divided from the Upper Basin per the **1922 Colorado River Compact**, a jurisdictional (not ecological) split, though it correlates with real ecological differences: presence of a high dam (Hoover/Glen Canyon-type infrastructure) fragmenting the river, differing degrees of riverine habitat modification, and differing administrative responsibility (Bureau of Reclamation vs. basin states).
- The draft plan proposes **"bio-regions"** as management units, but AGFD criticizes this scheme as ecologically arbitrary:
  - **Bio-Region II** improperly lumps the ecologically disjunct **Little Colorado River** and **Virgin River** drainages together despite dramatically different stream assemblages.
  - **Bio-Region III** ambiguously includes an unspecified portion of the **[[Bill Williams River]]**.
- AGFD recommends replacing artificial bio-regions with the **USGS Hydrologic Unit Map (1974)** classification system for ecologically and organizationally coherent basin-based management.
- Reintroduction habitat for Colorado squawfish progeny is disputed — the draft's reference to release into **Lake Mohave** is flagged as likely erroneous; AGFD recommends more general reference to "suitable habitats," implying Lake Mohave may not represent appropriate rearing/release habitat for this riverine species.
- The **Salt River** and **Verde River** are highlighted as sites of experimental Colorado squawfish reintroduction, currently under an "experimental, non-essential" designation under the Endangered Species Act.

## Key Findings
- **Scope/Rationale Conflict**: The plan's stated rationale (maintaining populations until downlisting) is narrower than its stated goals/objectives, which include full recovery — AGFD recommends resolving this inconsistency.
- **Species List Errors (Appendix A)**: Multiple taxonomic and listing-status errors identified, including misplacement of [[Gila Trout]], omission of [[Woundfin]], incorrect status for [[Virgin Spinedace]] and [[Yaqui Sucker]], and inconsistent common-name usage for [[Sonoran Sucker]].
- **Bio-Region Delineation Flawed**: Current bio-regions do not reflect real hydrological/ecological boundaries (e.g., Little Colorado + Virgin River grouping); a standardized hydrologic-unit-based system is recommended instead.
- **Jurisdictional Authority**: AGFD asserts sole state authority over manipulation (removal/re-establishment) of resident fish populations within Arizona, regardless of land ownership (federal or non-federal), and demands full coordination on any such actions under the Plan.
- **Opposition to Redesignation**: AGFD strongly opposes changing the [[Colorado Squawfish]] reintroduction designation in the Salt and Verde Rivers from "experimental, non-essential" to "experimental, essential," arguing that limited success in reintroduction stems from hatchery production limitations (inability to produce large numbers of large-sized fish) rather than from the regulatory designation itself.
- **Citation/Reference Errors**: Multiple in-text citations (Johnson et al. 1993; USFWS 1993) lack corresponding entries in the References Cited section, while several references (Carmichael et al. 1992; Minckley et al. 1986; Morizot et al. 1990; USFWS 1991a, 1994b) appear in the References Cited but are never cited in text.
- **Stakeholder Engagement**: AGFD emphasizes the necessity of early and sustained engagement with water/power authorities, anglers, and sportsmen groups for successful implementation of controversial actions such as altered water release patterns and species restoration efforts.
- **Plan Coordination**: AGFD flags the need to coordinate this Plan with two concurrent initiatives — the **Lower Colorado River EcoRegion Plan** and the **Lower Colorado River Basin States/Tribes Endangered Species Steering Committee** initiative — to avoid duplicated or conflicting management frameworks.
