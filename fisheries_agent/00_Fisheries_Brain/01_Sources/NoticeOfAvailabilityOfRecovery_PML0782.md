# Federal Register Notices: Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Goals and Related Agency Notices

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register compilation (August 2002) is a bundle of agency notices. Only one item is ecological: the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) announcement of the availability of [[Recovery Goals]] for four endangered fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]]: [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The goals amend and supplement existing [[Endangered Species Recovery Plan|recovery plans]] by setting measurable criteria for [[Downlisting]] and [[Delisting]]. The other items are a [[Food and Drug Administration]] request for comments on reprocessed single-use medical devices, a [[Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration]] advisory council meeting notice, two [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]] (BIA) information collection notices on tribal colleges, and a BIA Final Environmental Impact Statement (FEIS) notice for the Agua Caliente Indian Reservation.

## Background & Description

**Fish recovery goals (FWS)**

- **Species covered:** Humpback chub (*Gila cypha*), bonytail (*Gila elegans*), Colorado pikeminnow (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*). All four are listed as endangered.
- **Purpose:** The goals describe site-specific management actions and tasks, give objective and measurable recovery criteria, and estimate the time needed to achieve recovery.
- **Recovery units:** Humpback chub, bonytail, and razorback sucker are managed in two units, the upper basin (upstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], Arizona) and the lower basin. The Colorado pikeminnow is considered only in the upper basin unit.
- **Criteria:** Downlisting and delisting criteria cover listing factors, management actions, and demographic criteria for populations within each recovery unit. The goals also include updated life-history information.
- **Public process:** Draft goals were released for public comment in September 2001 (Federal Register, 66 FR 47033-47034). A reopening notice followed on November 23, 2001 (66 FR 58748). Comments were grouped by topic and answered in a separate response document.
- **Contact:** Requests go to the Director of the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]], Dr. Robert Muth, in Denver, Colorado. The goals are also posted online as PDF files.

**Other notices in the compilation**

- **FDA (opened-but-unused devices):** A follow-up to the August 14, 2000 guidance, "Enforcement Priorities for Single-Use Devices Reprocessed by Third Parties and Hospitals." That guidance defined "opened-but-unused" devices as single-use, disposable devices whose sterile package was opened or whose sterility was compromised, but which have not contacted blood or bodily fluids. The guidance did not cover hospital reprocessing of these devices, and FDA now seeks comments on (1) written hospital policies, (2) how hospitals decide whether such devices are contaminated, and (3) which device types are resterilized. Comments were due 90 days after publication.
- **SAMHSA (CMHS National Advisory Council):** Meeting held September 5-6, 2002, at the Double Tree Hotel in Rockville, Maryland. Parts were closed for grant review. Open sessions covered the New Freedom Commission on Mental Health and the disparities grant program.
- **BIA (tribal college information collections):** Renewal requests for the Tribal Colleges and Universities Annual Report Form (OMB No. 1076-0105) and the Grants Application for Community Colleges (OMB No. 1076-0018), both required under 25 CFR part 41. Comments were due October 28, 2002.
- **BIA (Agua Caliente FEIS):** Final Environmental Impact Statement for the Section 14 Master Development Plan on the [[Agua Caliente Indian Reservation]] in Palm Springs, Riverside County, California. Comments were due September 28, 2002, and the Record of Decision was to be issued on or after September 30, 2002.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All four fishes are native to the Colorado River system and are federally endangered. The recovery goals treat them as self-sustaining populations that need both demographic and genetic viability.
- Recovery is organized by basin position. Upper-basin populations sit above Glen Canyon Dam, while lower-basin populations are managed separately. The Colorado pikeminnow is assessed only in the upper basin.
- The goals aim to "minimize or remove threats" through site-specific actions. The source text does not name specific threats, population sizes, or habitat measurements.
- The source does not report field data, population counts, or quantitative habitat conditions for any of the four species.

## Key Findings

1. **Recovery goals were published as amendments to existing plans.** The FWS released objective, measurable criteria for downlisting and delisting the four endangered Colorado River fishes, with time-to-recovery estimates.
2. **Two recovery units are defined.** Upper and lower basin units govern recovery planning for three species. The Colorado pikeminnow is limited to the upper basin.
3. **Public comments shaped the final goals.** Draft goals were noticed in September 2001 and reopened in November 2001. Responses to comments were issued as a separate document.
4. **The FDA is reviewing hospital handling of opened-but-unused single-use devices.** The agency is soliciting information on hospital policies, contamination assessment, and resterilization practices.
5. **The SAMHSA council meeting combined open and closed sessions.** The closed portion covered grant applications and the open portion covered program updates.
6. **BIA renewed two tribal college information collections.** The Annual Report Form estimates 3 hours per response and 78 total burden hours across 26 responses. The Grants Application estimates 1 hour per response and 26 total burden hours across 26 responses.
7. **The Agua Caliente FEIS is open for review.** The BIA released the Final EIS for the Section 14 Master Development Plan in Palm Springs, with comments due September 28, 2002, and a Record of Decision expected on or after September 30, 2002.
