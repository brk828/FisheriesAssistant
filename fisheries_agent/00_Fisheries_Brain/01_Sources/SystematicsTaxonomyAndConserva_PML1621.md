# Systematics, Taxonomy, and Conservation Status of the Alabama Sturgeon *Scaphirhynchus suttkusi* (Acipenseridae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

Mayden and Kuhajda (Copeia, 1996) re-evaluate the taxonomic status of the [[Alabama Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*) and its relationship to the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*S. platorynchus*) and the [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*S. albus*). Using expanded morphometric and meristic data, multivariate analyses, and a phylogenetic analysis of 36 morphological characters, they conclude that *S. suttkusi* is a distinct species, and that it is sister to *S. platorynchus*. The paper also reviews the species' conservation status, noting a severe decline and a proposed-then-withdrawn federal listing. The authors argue that recovery requires restoration of spawning habitat, natural flow regimes, and reduced silt loads.

## Background & Description

- **Original description:** *S. suttkusi* was described by [[Williams and Clemmer]] (1991) as an endemic of the Mobile River system (Alabama and Mississippi). Its validity had been debated; the species had been recognized informally since the 1970s.
- **Purpose of the study:** To redescribe and reevaluate its taxonomic status using a phylogenetic analysis of Acipenseridae, to amend the original description (removing inconsistencies and completing the diagnosis), and to update conservation status.
- **Diagnostic features (as summarized):** Lack of sharp retrorse spines on the tip of the snout; lack of spines dorsal to the eye; poorly developed squamation on the venter (belly); a unique adult coloration (uniform brassy to orangish-yellow); and a larger orbit length relative to head width.
- **Material examined:** Specimen lists include museum lots and recent captures for *S. suttkusi* (n about 31 large specimens in the OL/HW analysis), *S. platorynchus* (n = 181 large specimens in the OL/HW analysis, with 127 for lateral plate angle), and outgroups including [[Acipenser]], [[Huso]], and [[Pseudoscaphirhynchus]].
- **Methods:** Analyses used [[Principal Component Analysis]] (PCA), [[Sheared Principal Component Analysis]], ANOVA and ANCOVA on arcsine-transformed or log-transformed ratios, Wilcoxon two-sample tests for meristic characters, and Bonferroni corrections. The phylogenetic analysis used PAUP with an exhaustive search and *Polyodon spathula* and *Psephurus gladius* (Polyodontidae) as outgroups.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *S. suttkusi* prefers relatively stable gravel and sand substrates in river channels with current, but also occurs over softer sediments. Stomach contents from 12 specimens (397–576 mm SL, collected 21 March 1969 at the Cahaba–Alabama junction) were dominated by benthic macroinvertebrates, including dipteran larvae (Ceratopogonidae, Chironomidae), mayfly nymphs (mostly Heptageniidae), and taxa that burrow in sand. This suggests sandy depositional areas with slow to moderate flow, as well as riffle and tailwater habitats.
- **Sister species habitat:** *S. platorynchus* frequents high-velocity river reaches, often near channel training devices, where silt deposition is low and macroinvertebrate faunas are richer.
- **Spawning:** Spawning biology of *S. suttkusi* is poorly known. Species of *Scaphirhynchus* spawn in freshwater and migrate upstream; spawning in *S. platorynchus* runs from late February to July, and eggs are adhesive and require current and silt-free substrate.
- **Threats:** Dam construction, channel modification for navigation, gravel mining, land-use habitat degradation, and historical overfishing. The authors note that a 1898 U.S. Commission report recorded 19,000 kg of "shovelnose sturgeon" from Alabama and Black Warrior rivers, a harvest estimated at about 19,000 specimens if averaging 1 kg each.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic status**
- *S. suttkusi* is a distinct species, separable from *S. platorynchus* by at least eight mensural characters and from *S. albus* by morphology.
- Reanalysis of Williams and Clemmer's (1991) data and new data confirm that six meristic characters differ between *S. suttkusi* and *S. platorynchus*. Combined data show that all but anal-fin rays differ significantly.
- OL/HW ratio: *S. platorynchus* (n = 181 large specimens) mean OL/HW 0.091 (range 0.062–0.125); *S. suttkusi* (n = 31) mean 0.136 (range 0.121–0.154). Only nine of 212 specimens overlapped, giving 96% separation on this character alone.
- No geographic cline was found between *S. suttkusi* and *S. platorynchus*; some northern and southern *S. platorynchus* populations differ in meristic counts.

**Phylogeny**
- A single shortest tree (length 62, CI = 0.855, RI = 0.845) supports the monophyly of *Scaphirhynchus*, with *S. albus* as sister to *S. platorynchus* plus *S. suttkusi*. This departs from Williams and Clemmer's hypothesis that *S. suttkusi* and *S. platorynchus* were sister species.
- *Pseudoscaphirhynchus* is paraphyletic. Subfamilies Acipenserinae and Scaphirhynchinae are each monophyletic.
- *S. suttkusi* has five autapomorphies; *S. albus* has one (plates posterior to anal fin).

**Diagnostic characters**
- Head armature: *S. suttkusi* lacks left and right preorbital spines, and its parietal, tabular, and posttemporal spines are mostly fused. Most *S. platorynchus* have full, exposed spines.
- Lateral plate angle: the fifth and tenth lateral plates are angled more anteriorly in *S. suttkusi* than in *S. platorynchus* (which are more vertical).
- Venter: *S. suttkusi* has a smooth belly with small embedded scales, while *S. platorynchus* has large exposed rhomboid scales.
- Coloration: adult *S. suttkusi* has a uniform brassy to orangish-yellow coloration, which is absent in *S. platorynchus* and *S. albus*.

**Conservation status**
- Before 1993, only 31 museum specimens were known. The last vouchered records before the proposed listing were from 1984–1985, an 8-year gap.
- The USFWS proposed endangered status with critical habitat on 15 June 1993, postponed the decision on 21 June 1994, and withdrew the proposal on 15 December 1994 because no specimens were captured during sampling from March to December 1994.
- Three specimens were captured in 1993 and 1995, including one mature male (UAIC 10885.01) on 2 December 1993 in the Alabama River at RM 58.8, Monroe County. This confirmed that the species still persists.
- The authors conclude that *S. suttkusi* is rare but not extinct, and that it is one of the most endangered fish species in the United States.

**Recovery recommendations**
- Recovery should include restoring spawning habitat, providing passage across dams to upstream and downstream reaches, establishing minimum flow regimes, and reducing silt loads.
- Sampling should target winter and spring, when captures are most frequent.
- Captured adults could be used in a propagation program, similar to efforts for the [[Pallid Sturgeon]] in the Missouri River.

**Broader context**
- The authors cite several fish species rediscovered after long hiatuses (e.g., *Etheostoma kennicotti*, rediscovered after 91 years in the Green River, Kentucky; the smokey madtom, rediscovered after 23 years) to illustrate that apparent extinction does not always mean actual extinction.
