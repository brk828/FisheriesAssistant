# Sycamore Creek Fish Survey (1973)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary
In July–August 1973, U.S. Forest Service personnel (Verde District, Prescott National Forest) and Arizona Game and Fish Department biologists conducted two fish surveys of [[Sycamore Creek (Arizona)]] to evaluate a proposal to eradicate native trout via rotenone and replace them with [[Gila Trout]] from New Mexico. Electrofishing ("stream shocking") revealed a diverse native fish assemblage, including several taxa (e.g., [[Longfin Dace]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]]) exhibiting notable morphological differentiation from typical populations of their species. An unidentified native trout population, initially assumed to be [[Rainbow Trout]], was suspected to be related to [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gila*), prompting taxonomic consultation with Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University), Dr. R.R. Miller (University of Michigan), and Dr. Robert Behnke (Colorado State University). The discovery of this potential undescribed/relict lineage halted plans for chemical treatment (rotenone) of the stream pending positive identification, illustrating an early case of interagency caution regarding cryptic native biodiversity in Southwestern desert streams.

## Background & Description
- **Unidentified native trout**: Resembled [[Rainbow Trout]] in general appearance but tentatively linked by Dr. Minckley to [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gila*). Dr. Miller noted the specimens showed **very little obvious genetic variation**, suggesting they were **likely not hybrids** with stocked rainbow or brown trout. Specimens were shipped to Ann Arbor, Michigan for further testing.
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): Sycamore Creek population had **differentiated to the point of lacking barbels**, a trait normally characteristic of the species.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Found to **differ morphologically** from typical populations elsewhere.
- **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*): Also noted to **differ from other populations** of the species; considered rare and endangered.
- **[[Gila Mountain Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*): The **only native species examined that showed no notable differentiation** in Sycamore Creek.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*): Present in multiple stream sectors; concern raised that specimens might belong to the rare/endangered variety *Gila robusta intermedia*.
- **[[Spike Dace]]** (*Meda fulgida*): **Not detected** in the 1973 sample; historically recorded near Dugas around the turn of the century but not collected since — suggesting possible local extirpation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Stream zonation**: Species composition varied distinctly by stream sector:
  - **Sycamore Ranger Station & above T-Anchor Ranch**: [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Longfin Dace]].
  - **Above Lower Watergap (150-yard reach)**: [[Brown Trout]] (up to 8 in, n=11), [[Rainbow Trout]] (up to 8 in, n=22), and ~100–150 trout fingerlings — a cold-water trout reach.
  - **Double T Falls to Rock Bottom Box**: [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and a few surviving stocked [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*).
  - **22 Mesa Road Crossing to Sycamore Ranger Station**: [[Longfin Dace]], [[Roundtail Chub]], suckers, and [[Green Sunfish]] — a warm-water assemblage.
  - **Chalk Tank Canyon pool** (above Horner Mountain Ranch): [[Roundtail Chub]] only.
  - **Little Sycamore Creek** (above Dugas) and area above Horner Mountain headquarters: similar warm-water native species found, indicating connected tributary habitat requiring protection.
- **Introduced/stocked species**: [[Woundfin]] had been stocked twice over the prior winter (once near Sycamore Ranger Station/T-Anchor Ranch, once at Double T Waterfalls) as part of an experimental introduction from the Virgin River. Neither stocking event appeared to succeed — Sycamore Creek habitat was deemed ecologically dissimilar to the Virgin River, the Woundfin's native range.
- **Watershed considerations**: Investigators recommended that all tributary drainages feeding Sycamore Creek (including Little Sycamore Creek and Chalk Tank Canyon) be protected from **watershed deterioration** and **exotic species introduction** to preserve the differentiated native fish populations.

## Key Findings
- The proposed **rotenone treatment** to eliminate native trout and introduce [[Gila Trout]] was placed on hold pending taxonomic clarification of the native Sycamore Creek trout, which may represent a distinct or relict lineage.
- Arizona Game and Fish Department expressed concern that surviving native trout could **hybridize with introduced Gila trout**, potentially destroying the genetic integrity of either strain; alternatively, Gila trout might fail to establish, leaving the stream fishless and necessitating rainbow trout reintroduction.
- Dr. Minckley (ASU) identified **multiple locally differentiated native species** ([[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Gila Chub]]) previously unnoticed by both the Forest Service and Arizona Game and Fish Department — underscoring a critical **gap in pre-management biological survey work**.
- The [[Gila Mountain Sucker]] was notable as the only examined species **lacking observable differentiation**, serving as a baseline comparison for the unique Sycamore Creek fish fauna.
- [[Spike Dace]] (*Meda fulgida*), historically documented near Dugas, was **not found**, raising concern about possible local extirpation from the Sycamore Creek system.
- Recommendation was made for **continued interagency coordination** among the Forest Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department, and academic ichthyologists (Minckley, Miller, Behnke) before any further management action (stocking or chemical treatment) in Sycamore Creek.
- The case highlights how **retroactive taxonomic investigation** revealed that earlier management decisions (i.e., Woundfin stocking) were made **without adequate knowledge of native species uniqueness**, reinforcing the need for baseline biological surveys prior to fisheries interventions in isolated desert streams.
