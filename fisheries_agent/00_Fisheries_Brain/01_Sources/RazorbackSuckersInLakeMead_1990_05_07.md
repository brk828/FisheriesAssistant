# Razorback Suckers in Lake Mead

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #razorback-sucker #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1990 field correspondence from the Nevada Department of Wildlife (NDOW) documents opportunistic and targeted observations of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mead]] and supplemental captures of razorback suckers and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) in [[Lake Mohave]]. A fisherman report led biologists to a spawning aggregation site on a gravel bar near Vegas Wash, where visual confirmation of a multi-male spawning group around a single female was made. A follow-up electrofishing survey documented a shift in fish location correlating with declining reservoir water levels, and three razorbacks were captured, tagged, and sampled for gametes as part of a broader Bureau of Reclamation (BOR)/Arizona State University (ASU) research effort. Fish from Lake Mead showed notably better body condition (no scarring or fungal infection) compared to conspecifics from Lake Mohave.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning habitat:** Razorback suckers were located over a **gravel bar** substrate composed of well-washed coarse gravel and small cobbles, at the terminus of a peninsula between a point and a small offshore island — situated between Gypsum Wash and Vegas Wash in [[Lake Mead]], near Vegas Wash Marina.
- **Depth range:** Fish were observed at depths of **1–4 feet** over the spawning bar.
- **Site exposure:** The site was exposed to wave action but sheltered from boat traffic, a condition believed favorable for undisturbed spawning; other candidate sites in the area were rejected due to heavy boat traffic or silted substrates.
- **Reservoir level sensitivity:** Between April 12 (1188.6 ft) and May 1, 1990 (1186.7 ft), a ~1.9-foot drop in [[Lake Mead]] water level (attributed to downstream water releases) exposed the original spawning site, converting it to fine gravel/silt and rendering it unsuitable. Fish relocated to a comparable gravel bar ~100 meters west, at the opposite end of the same small island, underscoring the tight linkage between **reservoir hydrology and spawning habitat availability**.
- **Spawning behavior:** A spawning aggregation of approximately 4 males surrounding 1 large female was visually documented on April 12 — consistent with known polyandrous group-spawning behavior of the species over gravel substrates.
- **Comparative population health:** The apparent absence of fungal infection and scarring in Lake Mead razorbacks (versus [[Lake Mohave]] fish) suggests differing environmental stressors or exposure histories between the two reservoir populations.

## Key Findings

- Confirmed active **razorback sucker spawning activity in Lake Mead** in spring 1990, based on a citizen report followed by biologist field verification — an important distributional record for this endangered catostomid.
- Documented a **spawning aggregation** (~15 individuals observed on April 12; group of 4 males + 1 female) at a specific gravel-bar site, providing baseline data for future systematic inventory of spawning/use sites on Lake Mead.
- Demonstrated that **fluctuating reservoir water levels** can rapidly alter substrate suitability and displace spawning fish to new but similar habitat within the same water body.
- Three razorback suckers were captured, **tagged with numbered floy tags (#501–#503)**, and sampled for **eggs and milt** (cryopreserved and forwarded to ASU) as part of an ongoing multi-agency (NDOW, BOR, ASU) genetic/reproductive research contract.
- Supplemental data from Lake Mohave (May 4, 1990) recorded **9 female razorback suckers** (no tags) and **2 bonytail** individuals (one previously fin-clipped, indicating a mixed wild/hatchery-origin population), illustrating parallel monitoring efforts across the two reservoirs.
- Noted a clear **health/condition disparity** between Lake Mead and Lake Mohave razorback sucker populations, with Lake Mead fish free of the fungal infection and scarring commonly seen in Lake Mohave individuals — a finding relevant to habitat quality assessments and conservation prioritization.
- Establishes intent for continued **systematic surveys** of Lake Mead to document razorback sucker distribution, abundance, and habitat use, coordinated with [[Arizona State University]], [[University of Nevada Las Vegas]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
