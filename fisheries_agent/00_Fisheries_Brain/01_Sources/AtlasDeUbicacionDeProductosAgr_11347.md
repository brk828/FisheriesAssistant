# Atlas de Ubicación de Productos Agropecuarios Utilizables en la Planificación y Desarrollo de la Acuicultura en México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquaculture

## Executive Summary

This atlas, prepared under the FAO/AQUILA regional aquaculture project (GCP/RLA/075/ITA) with the Dirección General de Acuacultura of the Secretaría de Pesca, catalogues agricultural, livestock and fishery by-products and wastes in Mexico that could be used as aquafeed ingredients or fertilizers. It pairs the inventory with a national profile (geography, population, economy, agriculture, livestock, forestry, fisheries and [[Aquaculture]] development, feed and fertilizer industries) and maps regional availability across eight economic regions. The stated aim is to reduce dependence on imported feed ingredients by identifying low-cost local inputs, their volumes, seasonality, costs and nutritional value. The document is a reference atlas rather than a research study; its figures are mostly 1983–1987 statistics.

## Background & Description

- **Origin and purpose:** Compiled as part of FAO/Italy support for regional aquaculture in Latin America and the Caribbean. Each member country was to receive a directory of feed ingredients covering composition, production, supply, availability, cost, current use and potential feeding value.
- **Authors:** Biol. Gloria Guadalupe Palomo Martínez and Biol. Raúl Arriaga Becerra; FAO expert in fish nutrition Dr. Albert G. J. Tacon was acknowledged.
- **Edition:** First edition 1988; second edition March 1993, Pachuca, Hidalgo, Mexico.
- **Structure:** Country profile (Section 2), agricultural, livestock and by-product data (Section 3), feed and fertilizer industry (Section 4), by-products of potential aquaculture use (Section 5), with 21 tables and 32 figures (maps and charts) plus references.
- **Geography:** Mexico is divided into 31 states and a Federal District; the document recommends similar state-level studies for each entity.
- **Aquaculture context:** Historical roots in pre-Hispanic times; carp propagation on the central plateau from 1960–1970; the Dirección General de Acuacultura created in 1972; 136 species identified as having culture potential, 57 used in Mexican aquaculture, 13 with full life-cycle culture technology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Climate and vegetation:** Ten climate types, from hot-dry to tropical with year-round rain; tropical rainforest in wet tropical zones, desert flora in arid regions, and mixed pine-oak forest in highlands.
- **Land use:** 355,084 km² suitable for agriculture (18.1% of territory); 246,726 km² pasture (12.6%); 524,006 km² forest and jungle (26.8%). Irrigated land is about 4.3 million ha; rain-fed valley land 20.1 million ha; rain-fed upland 11 million ha.
- **Aquatic resources:** 11,592.7 km of coastline, continental shelf of 357,000 km², 2.5 million ha of protected and epicontinental water bodies, and an exclusive economic zone of 3.0 million km² (declared 1976, 200 nautical miles). Estimated fishery potential is about 6.0 million tonnes.
- **Extensive aquaculture:** Practised on more than 1.2 million ha of freshwater bodies and 1.6 million ha of coastal lagoons and estuaries.
- **Saline land:** About 470,000 ha of potentially usable saline land for shrimp culture, per the National Shrimp Culture Programme.
- **Regional fishery structure:** Five fishing regions (Pacific North, Pacific Center-South, Gulf of Mexico North, Gulf-Caribbean, and inland states without coastline), each with characteristic fisheries such as sardine–anchovy, tuna, shrimp, oyster, tilapia and carp.

## Key Findings

**Country profile**
- Population of 80.9 million in 1986; population density 41 inhabitants/km²; 67.5% urban; 59 ethnic groups speaking 48 dialects.
- Inflation averaged 82.6% per year in 1982–1986 and reached 160% in 1987. The minimum real wage fell 49.8% from 1976 to 1986.
- Current consumption: 2,705 kcal/day and 68.1 g protein/day; 29.2% of protein from animal sources. Around 40% of the population did not meet minimum requirements.
- Agricultural trade deficit in 1983 of US$560.7 million; exports fell to 5% of total national exports by 1983 while imports rose to 21%.

**Agriculture and livestock**
- About 200 agricultural products are grown; maize, bean, sorghum and wheat cover 70% of harvested area.
- Maize yield averages 1.8 t/ha; sugarcane 74 t/ha; rice 3.5 t/ha; wheat 4 t/ha.
- Livestock output: cattle 665,000 t/year; pigs 500,000 t/year (all for domestic consumption); sheep 17,000 t/year; goats 20,000 t/year; poultry is the most intensive sector.

**Fisheries and aquaculture**
- Fisheries capture reached 1,460,889 t in 1987 (62% for direct human consumption); fishery exports of 165,000 t valued at US$581 million in 1987.
- Fishing employed 173,768 people in 1987.
- Aquaculture produced 720,314 t in 1983–1987 at about 9% annual growth; 169,433 t in 1987, the highest on record.
- Tilapia led in 1983–1987 with 328,358 t (46%), followed by oyster (203,477 t) and carp (88,678 t).
- The Federal government ran 51 aquaculture centres across 29 states, producing over 125 million juveniles in 1987.
- Intensive aquaculture units: 1,914 registered in 1986–1987, covering 120,107 ha.
- Feed for cultured organisms is based on compound feeds averaging 36% protein.

**Feed and fertilizer industry**
- 108 compound-feed plants with installed capacity of 7.5 million t/year; 3.7 million t produced in 1986 (49.3% of capacity).
- Only 8 plants make fish and crustacean feed; about 33,000 t was produced in 1987 (≈0.9% of national output).
- FERTIMEX produced 3.6 million t of fertilizers in 1987 from 11 plants.

**By-products with potential for aquaculture (Section 5)**
- Rice husk: over 83,000 t/year in the Northwest region; cotton husk: over 54,000 t/year in North and Northwest.
- Sugarcane tops and bagasse: over 762,000 t/year in several regions.
- Molasses: available from the Gulf and Center-South regions with volume of 10.6 million t/year; cost about 200,000 pesos/t.
- Shrimp head: over 7,000 t/year in Northwest, Northeast and Yucatán; obtained free of charge at source (transport cost only).
- Cattle manure (vacaza): about 9.9 million t dry weight/year; free at source; recommended for pond fertilization.
- Mixed meat-and-bone meal from slaughterhouses: over 190,000 t/year; high protein but relatively costly.
- Coffee, cocoa and henequen pulps: free at source, annual availability above 17,000 t.
- Cassava, soybean meal, cottonseed meal, sunflower meal and sesame meal are identified as protein-rich ingredients, with prices given per tonne.

**Recommendations and conclusions**
- Local and by-product inputs can substantially reduce feed costs; transport and distance from source must be included in cost calculations.
- A state-by-state study is recommended for all 31 states and the Federal District.
- The document concludes that Mexican aquaculture is expanding, with a large untapped potential and abundant natural resources.
- It notes the absence of national quality standards for aquafeed, a gap in sanitary regulation.
