# Basic Survey of the On-Stream Verde River and Impoundments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Commission Wildlife Restoration Division work plan (Project F-2-R-1, Work Plan 2, Job I, by Richard A. Wagner) describes a basic survey of the [[Verde River]] system in central Arizona and its on-stream impoundments. The authors note that the state lacked permanent, high-quality fishing water and that concrete limnological and ecological data for the Verde system were missing. The survey aimed to identify fish species and their abundance, build food indexes, collect limnological data, evaluate forage-game fish relationships, assess silt effects on [[Smallmouth Bass]] spawning, and make recommendations for habitat improvement and species introductions. The document combines field methods, water-quality tables (mostly 1953 data), reservoir and lake descriptions, and management recommendations.

## Background & Description

**Objectives (six):**
1. Determine fish species present and their relative abundance.
2. Establish a food index for each lake or river section.
3. Collect limnological data.
4. Evaluate the forage fish–game fish relationship.
5. Determine the effects of silt on spawning success and survival of young Smallmouth Bass.
6. Recommend habitat improvement, introductions of game and forage species, aquatic weed control, and development of potential hinterland areas.

**Methods:** Fish were sampled with [[Gillnetting]] and spot poisoning (rotenone). Food indexes used a dredge, square-foot counter, plankton net, and aquatic insect net. Limnological data were collected with standard instruments. Forage–game relationships were drawn from game fish stomach analysis. Silt effects were assessed during the spawning season, with notes on nest depth, nest location, and flash floods.

**Study area:** The Verde River and its tributaries and dry washes lie in the central part of the state. The river is said to originate at Sullivan Lake, with the watershed head about 50 miles northwest, and flows generally east and south to its confluence with the Salt River. About two-thirds of the basin lies between the desert metropolitan areas and the high-country recreation areas, making it a key recreation zone for the greater Phoenix region.

**Fish community:** [[Table I]] lists the most common species across the system, including [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Common Carp]], common sucker, [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Red Plains Shiner]], and [[Mosquitofish]]. The table lists the water bodies where each species was found, such as Pecks, Stehr, Watson, Sullivan, Bartlett, and Horseshoe waters. Scan quality prevents reliable reproduction of every entry.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow and silt:** The Verde is spring-fed and normally clear, but summer flash floods from dry washes carry heavy silt loads. On occasion, silt made up over a quarter of river volume (exact figure partly illegible in the scan). At [[Bridgeport]], the document reports silt at almost 30 percent of river volume during flood events. Silt has smothered benthic food organisms and affected catfish and bass populations.
- **Horseshoe Reservoir:** The first flood-control station on the river. About 70 percent of the silt load is deposited in the Horseshoe Lake basin.
- **Bartlett Reservoir:** Muddy river water underflows the surface and passes through [[Bartlett Dam]], producing a turbid river below but clearer surface water in the reservoir. Bartlett releases were about 30 percent less turbid than inflows. Light penetration (Secchi disc) averages about 20 feet, and plankton averages 0.015 cc per foot, mostly zooplankton.
- **Temperature:** Water above Horseshoe Reservoir runs about two to five °F colder than at Camp Verde in winter and four to six °F warmer in summer, so reservoir inflow and the river below behave differently.
- **Watson Lake:** Dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], and carp. Its water level fluctuates strongly, and it is used for irrigation.
- **Sullivan Lake:** A shallow, muddy lake (maximum depth about 8 ft, Secchi roughly 8 inches). About 60 percent of its area is covered by sago pondweed. Runoff from Chino Wash greatly reduces its fishery value.
- **Pecks Lake:** Strongly fluctuating, with submerged aquatic vegetation. Historic weed problems led to discussion of [[Aquatic Weed Control]] and herbicide use. Early 1930s sportsmen had introduced fish, but productivity was short-lived.
- **Stehr Lake:** Connected to the Verde River and used for small-scale hydropower production.
- **Lower river (below Bartlett Dam):** Operates mainly as an irrigation canal. A coarse sand, gravel, and rubble bottom covers roughly two-thirds of the distance to the Salt River confluence. Food organisms are scarce; snails and freshwater shrimp are mostly absent. [[Channel Catfish]] is the dominant game fish, and carp is the most abundant non-game fish.

## Key Findings

- **Fishery scarcity:** Permanent high-quality fishing water is scarce in Arizona, so developing the Verde and its impoundments to full potential is a priority.
- **Silt is the main limiting factor:** Flash-flood silt loads damage spawning and food supply, especially for bass and catfish.
- **Species compatibility:** Bartlett Reservoir conditions appear most suited to [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Black Crappie]], but the reservoir lacks forage, cover, and food organisms.
- **Growth rates:** Largemouth bass growth in Bartlett is slow, with reported lengths of about eight inches in the first year, three in the second, and two in the third (values as given in the source text).
- **Forage recommendations:** Introduce [[Red Plains Shiner]] at the head of the main river (where current is present) and [[Threadfin Shad]] into the main body of Bartlett Reservoir.
- **Watson Lake plan:** Reduce annual water-level fluctuation to less than ten feet, remove all present species, restock with catchable-size rainbow trout (count partly illegible; possibly 10,000), and introduce forage organisms.
- **Sullivan Lake:** Not recommended for fishery development given muddy, weed-dominated conditions and chronic inflow problems. Other sites were preferred.
- **Priority development sites:** (1) the Verde River about 1.5 miles below Perkinsville, and (2) a site about four miles east of Prescott in the Agua Fria drainage. The first offered a cooling and flow-control opportunity and was linked to water-supply and waterfowl goals (about 300 acres of waterfowl habitat).
- **Lower river invertebrates:** Benthic invertebrate food is scarce across most of the lower river.
- **Recommendations:**
  1. Introduce larger freshwater shrimp in the sago pondweed areas of the river.
  2. Introduce Red Plains Shiner in the main river at Perkinsville, Bridgeport, and/or Camp Verde, and below Bartlett Dam.
  3. Introduce Threadfin Shad in Bartlett Reservoir.
- **Stream classification:** The region was divided into lotic (streams and river reaches) and lentic (lakes and stream impoundments) habitats for management purposes.
- **Data caveat:** Much of the water chemistry and storage data (for example, the 1953 Horseshoe and Bartlett storage tables) predates the current survey, and several values are partly illegible in the scan.
