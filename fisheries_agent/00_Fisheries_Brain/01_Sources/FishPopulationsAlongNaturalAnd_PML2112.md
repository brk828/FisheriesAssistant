# Fish Populations Along Natural and Revetted Banks on the Lower Mississippi River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This study compares fish assemblages at two natural banks and two [[Revetment]] structures on the main stem of the [[Lower Mississippi River]] near Greenville, Mississippi, during 1979. Revetments are erosion-control structures built by the [[U.S. Army Corps Of Engineers]]. Sampling used [[Hoop Netting]] and [[Electroshocking]] in April, June, September and November. Species richness was similar between habitats (24 species at natural banks, 27 at revetted banks). Six species were more abundant on revetted banks and four on natural banks. Habitat type and location appeared to be important factors affecting fish populations, and natural-bank assemblages were more stable across seasons than revetted-bank assemblages.

## Background & Description

Since the early 1900s, the [[U.S. Army Corps Of Engineers]] has built revetments on large navigable rivers, including the Mississippi and Missouri, to control erosion and deposition for navigation. Early revetments used stone riprap, wire fencing, asphalt and concrete. Most current structures are [[Articulated Concrete Mattress]] systems covered with riprap, with asphalt on the upper bank.

As of September 1980, about 1,252 km of the Lower Mississippi River were revetted (Head of Passes, Louisiana, to Cape Girardeau), with an additional 360 km authorized before 1996. The authors note that revetment substantially changes the natural character of a bank, but that its value as fish habitat had received little study.

**Study sites:**
- **[[Lakeport Revetment]]:** river miles 524.8-526.0, built 1961-1962.
- **[[Walnut Point-Kentucky Bend Revetment]]:** east bank, river miles 515.8-523.9, built in several sections 1944-1968.
- **[[Anconia Natural Bank]]:** immediately upstream of Lakeport Revetment. Substrate was hard clay to fine sand.
- **[[Island 88 Natural Bank]]:** just downstream of and across from Walnut Point Revetment.

Revetted banks had high current speeds (usually above 1 m/s) with few slow eddies. Natural banks had slumping banks and fallen trees and slower currents, except during spring flood stages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Revetments alter erosion and accretion, which changes bank structure and hydraulics. The authors suggest that habitat type and the position of each study area are important factors shaping local fish assemblages. The natural banks were similar to each other across all seasons, while the two revetted banks showed greater variability over time. Sampling sites were grouped by proximity, and similarity was greater among the two natural banks than between natural banks and revetments.

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- **Sampling periods (1979):** spring high water (April), spring-summer falling water (June), summer low water (September), and fall rising water (November).
- **Hoop nets:** unbaited, 25.4 mm square mesh, 61 cm and 91 cm diameter. Four stations were used at Lakeport Revetment and each natural bank, and 12 at Walnut Point Revetment. Nets fished for two consecutive 48-hour periods.
- **Electroshocking:** 460 m transects, worked downstream near the shoreline between hoop-net stations. Three transects were fished at Lakeport and each natural bank, and additional transects at Walnut Point.
- **Analysis:** catch per unit effort (CPUE) by number and weight was compared among locations with one-way ANOVA (P < 0.05). Community similarity was calculated with the [[Coefficient Of Community Similarity]] and percentage similarity.

**Species composition and abundance**
- Species richness was similar: 24 species at natural banks versus 27 at revetted banks.
- Six species were more abundant on revetted banks, including [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]], [[Smallmouth Buffalo]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Sauger]] and [[Blue Sucker]].
- Four species were more abundant on natural banks, including [[Freshwater Drum]], [[Flathead Catfish]] and [[Skipjack Herring]].
- The largest differences in percentage composition by weight involved sport and commercial species, which were more abundant by weight on revetted banks.
- [[Gizzard Shad]] was among the most common species in many samples.

**Catch per effort**
- Mean CPUE by number and weight was higher on natural banks in June. At other sampling times it was higher on revetted banks.
- Natural-bank populations were similar at all seasons, while revetted-bank populations showed greater variability.

**Conclusions**
- Habitat type and study-site location appear to influence fish community structure on the Lower Mississippi River.
- Revetments create a distinct, variable fish habitat that supports different and, for some sport and commercial species, greater abundance than natural banks.
