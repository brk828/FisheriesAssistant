# Fossil Cyprinodont Fishes of Eastern California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This note covers a 1945 paper by [[Robert R. Miller]] in the *Journal of the Washington Academy of Sciences* (vol. 35, no. 10) describing four new fossil cyprinodont species from desert deposits of eastern California. Three are [[Fundulus]] species and one is a [[Cyprinodon]]. The fossils were examined from museum collections, and the paper builds on the author's studies of living desert fishes. The page also carries an unrelated ethnographic fragment on the [[Requickening Address]] of the Haudenosaunee [[Condolence Council]], which is summarized briefly below.

## Background & Description

**Page context (non-ichthyological).** The top of the page contains an excerpt on the Requickening Address, a ceremonial text of the [[Haudenosaunee Confederacy]]. It lists numbered symbolic drawings and their meanings, including death, the council fire, the matron and warrior, and the torch. The closing passage describes the short string passed to both phratries when danger arises. The text belongs to a different discipline and is not part of the fish study.

**Study material.** The fish specimens came from three institutions: the Department of Paleontology of the [[California Institute of Technology]], the [[Museum of Paleontology, University of California]], and the [[University of Michigan Museum of Paleontology]]. Comparative material of [[Fundulus nevadensis]] was obtained from the Lahontan beds near Hazen, Churchill County, Nevada. The paper notes that the fossil fish fauna of the eastern California desert had received little attention before this work. The author says a broader survey for fossil fish remains "would no doubt" yield important finds.

**Taxonomic framework.** The paper places the material in the family Cyprinodontidae (killifishes). The author treats *Parafundulus nevadensis* Eastman (1917) as a species of *Fundulus*. He also treats *Parafundulus* as a synonym of *Fundulus*, following [[David Starr Jordan]] (1925).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Age and horizon.** Age assignments are uncertain for most localities:

- ***Fundulus curryi* and *Cyprinodon breviradius*** came from beds reportedly of lower Oligocene age in the [[Titus Canyon Formation]], east of Death Valley. The author calls the Oligocene assignment "very questionable." A park naturalist stated that the dating of the fish site to the Titus Canyon Formation rests on "purely lithologic grounds." Because a *Cyprinodon* is present, the author argues the beds are "no earlier than Late Pliocene," since the genus was previously unknown as a fossil and is of recent origin.
- ***Fundulus eulepis*** came from the [[Furnace Creek Formation]] Tertiary section, in the Black Mountains.
- ***Fundulus davidae*** came from near Black Mountain in the Mohave Desert. The horizon is undetermined, but the matrix suggests Pliocene or early Pleistocene age.

**Paleoenvironment.** The paper notes that the *eulepis* scales show one sharp annulus, which suggests cold winters. A scale from *curryi* shows a single year mark, which the author reads as evidence of marked seasonal temperature differences. Growth was rapid in the first year in *eulepis*, and the slowdown at the end of that year may correspond to maturity.

## Key Findings

**1. *Fundulus curryi* n. sp.**
- Holotype: California Institute of Technology no. 10239 (Loc. 335), about 44 mm standard length, collected by H. Donald Curry. Paratypes include nos. 10240, 10247, 10249, and others from the same locality.
- Diagnosis: dorsal fin 14–15 rays; anal fin 15–16 rays; 30–31 vertebrae (10–11 precaudal, 20 caudal); body and caudal peduncle short and deep.
- Head length enters standard length about 3.6 times; body depth about 3.5 times.
- Dorsal and anal fins are set far back, with the anal fin base about 1.5 times the peduncle depth.
- Compared with living Pacific coast species (*F. parvipinnis* and *F. lima*), the fins are more posterior and the anal fin has more rays.
- Named for H. Donald Curry.

**2. *Fundulus eulepis* n. sp.**
- Holotype: U.S. National Museum no. 16883, about 93 mm (estimated length; the text also gives 90 and 98 mm alternative estimates).
- Diagnosis: large, long, slender body; dorsal fin well in advance of the anal fin; dorsal and anal with 13–14 rays; posterior anal rays elongated.
- Pelvic fins have 6 rays. Scales have 8–10 radii and many circuli (23–25 or more).
- Paratypes include a head (U.S.N.M. no. 16884) with strong conical teeth, indicating a predatory habit, and a scale-rich specimen (U.S.N.M. no. 16885).
- Name means "well-scaled."

**3. *Fundulus davidae* n. sp.**
- Holotype: California Institute of Technology no. 10276, about 35 mm long, the only known specimen. It was received by [[Lore R. David]] from a wildcat oil driller.
- Dorsal and anal fins have 11–12 rays each, both set well behind the midpoint.
- Head length about 2.9 times in standard length; about 30 vertebrae (11 precaudal, 19 caudal).
- Estimated 45–50 scales in the lateral series. This exceeds living Pacific slope species (about 30–37) but is fewer than *F. nevadensis*.
- Jordan's *Parafundulus erdisi* from northern Los Angeles County is the closest comparison. *F. davidae* has a deeper body and more posterior fins, though the author considers the differences possibly related to sexual dimorphism.
- Named for Lore R. David.

**4. *Cyprinodon breviradius* n. sp.**
- Holotype: California Institute of Technology no. 10245, about 33 mm (estimated), from the same locality as *F. curryi*. The head is missing.
- Dorsal fin has 10–11 rays and sits almost over the pelvic origin. Anal fin has 10–11 rays. Pelvic fins have 6–7 rays.
- About 20 caudal vertebrae, more than most living regional species.
- Resembles *C. macularius* in fin position and *C. salinus* in body shape. The author notes that this is the first fossil *Cyprinodon* recorded.
- Name means "short-rayed," referring to the short fin rays.

**5. Other specimens.** Additional *eulepis*-like specimens from the *curryi* locality were assigned to *eulepis* tentatively. They are too slender to be *curryi*, and the holotype's slender form is similar to *eulepis*.

**6. Overall conclusion.** Four new species were described: three *Fundulus* and one *Cyprinodon*. The author states that a broader search for fossil fish remains in the region is needed. The paper's dating of the Titus Canyon beds is treated as doubtful, and the presence of *Cyprinodon* suggests a Late Pliocene or younger age.
