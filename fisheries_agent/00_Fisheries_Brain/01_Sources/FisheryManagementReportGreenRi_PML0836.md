# Green River Fish Collection Report, Colorado and Utah, October 11–13, 1963

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ptychocheilus

## Executive Summary

This 1963 field report, issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Division of Sport Fisheries (Albuquerque, New Mexico), summarizes a three-day fish collection on the [[Green River]] in Colorado and Utah, carried out October 11–13, 1963. Crews of volunteer students, with fishery personnel, sampled stations from [[Lodore Canyon]] to [[Split Mountain]] campground using a [[Boat Electrofishing]] shocker boat, bank shockers and nets. The report's main message is methodological: the catch composition depended heavily on the gear used and on the crews' skill, so the collections should be read as partial samples rather than a complete inventory. The author is [[Robert L. Azevedo]], Fishery Management Biologist, [[Cooperative Fishery Unit]].

## Background & Description

The survey was a joint effort of the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] and the Cooperative Fishery Unit, with fish collected for study at [[Colorado State University]] and [[Utah State University]] (Logan). The report notes that the field crews were volunteer students whose conduct was typical of students on field trips, and it states that these crews were not meant to discredit any individual, [[Mr. Banks]] included.

Fish were preserved in 10% formalin. Specimens from Lodore Canyon and the Browns Park Bridge area went to Colorado State University after full records were kept. Specimens from Echo Park and Split Mountain were taken to Logan for inventory before shipment to Colorado State University. Fish from Mantle Ranch (OCR reading uncertain) were not preserved because formalin and containers were unavailable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The stations were distributed along the river and its tributary mouths, and each differed in channel type:

- **Lodore I and II:** a side channel adjacent to the main channel and the main stream from the Gates of Lodore to the area opposite the ranger station.
- **Browns Park:** main channel immediately west of the bridge.
- **Island Park:** small channels just downstream of the Whirlpool Canyon river exit.
- **Split Mountain:** main channel at the campground up to a point about 1 mile upstream.
- **Echo Park I and II:** Green River at Echo Park, from the campground to just upstream of the Yampa confluence, and from the Yampa confluence upstream.
- **Yampa River and Pool Creek** area were also sampled.

Depth and shallowness shaped sampling. At Split Mountain, only about 20% of the river was shallow enough to wade. At Echo Park, much of the Green River above the Yampa confluence and the Yampa River itself were too deep to wade. The deeper holes could only be worked from a boat.

Tables 1 and 2 of the original report summarize the individual collections; they are not reproduced here.

## Key Findings

**Gear determined catch composition.** The report states that each gear type reflects its own capacity to cover the environment and its electrical characteristics. Bank shockers were limited to wadeable water, so the boat-mounted shocker was essential.

- At Split Mountain campground, the shocker boat took the entire catch, including the only specimen of one unidentified taxon.
- At the Echo Park–Yampa confluence, the shocker boat took **74%** of the total catch. It also captured the only squawfish and humpback suckers taken in the survey.
- Adding [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus*) and other taxa considered rare in the area came mainly from extending sampling into deeper holes that only the boat could reach.

**Collector quality affected sample quality.** The authors stress that the field crews' attitudes and diligence "reflect rather closely the overall quality of the samples obtained." They warn that earlier collections made by similar crews should not be given much significance. Sampling a complex river like the Green requires both diligence and competence.

**Comparability is limited.** The report says results should be compared with earlier collections with caution. Differences in gear (boat shocker, bank shocker, and nets of varying mesh) and in effort and crew make direct comparisons unreliable without accounting for those factors.

**Rare species present.** The squawfish and humpback sucker are the two named taxa in the Key Findings text. Both are listed as rare in the Echo Park area, and the report highlights the importance of the boat-based sampling that found them.

**Outcome.** The report's conclusions boil down to this: the sample is a product of gear, location and crew quality, and it should be interpreted as a partial picture of the fish community rather than a complete census.
