# Evaluation of p,p'-DDT as a Reference Toxicant in Bioassays

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ecotoxicology #Salmonidae #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #Percidae #DDT #FreshwaterToxicology

## Executive Summary

This 1966 USFWS study by [[Leif L. Marking]] evaluated **p,p'-DDT** as a potential standardized [[Reference Toxicant]] for use in fish [[Bioassay|bioassays]]. The impetus was the lack of a standardized test fish or reference chemical, which hampered reproducibility and cross-comparison of toxicity results between laboratories (echoing concerns raised by [[Hart, Doudoroff and Greenbank 1945]] and [[Warren and Doudoroff 1958]]). The study tested 19 species of freshwater fish (39 lots, 10 hatchery/field sources) in 96-hour static bioassays at 12°C (La Crosse, WI) and 17°C (Warm Springs, GA facility comparisons for [[Bluegill]]).

**Key conclusion:** p,p'-DDT failed to meet the criteria of an ideal reference toxicant — it lacked the required **nonselectivity** (LC50 ranged from 0.8 ppb in largemouth bass to 68 ppb in northern redbelly dace, an ~85-fold difference) and **consistency** (intraspecific LC50 variation, e.g., goldfish ranged 21–180 ppb). It was also deemed a poor reference compound because DDT is common in the environment (agricultural runoff, hatchery feed contamination), risking pre-exposure and acquired tolerance in test fish.

## Background & Description

While this is primarily a toxicology paper rather than a morphological study, key **physiological/behavioral observations** during DDT intoxication were recorded across species:

- **Behavioral progression of toxicosis:** Irritation and excitation → erratic swimming (upside-down, on-side, surfacing, wall-collisions) → continuous swimming until final stages → total loss of equilibrium → loss of reflex reactivity → cessation of locomotion → death.
- **Terminal signs:** Moribund fish often turned **light in color**, operculums became **distended**, and the spine sometimes **curved** (suggestive of convulsive activity at death).
- **Species-specific response intensity:** Reactions in **catfishes** (Ictaluridae) were markedly less visible/obvious compared to trout (Salmonidae).
- **Unique pathology in [[Fathead Minnow]]:** Marked **abdominal distention** beginning at 24 hours, intensifying by 96 hours, correlating with an **enlarged air bladder** upon necropsy of the peritoneal cavity. Cause was undetermined but paralleled findings by [[Henderson, Pickering and Tarzwell 1959]] in other chlorinated hydrocarbon exposures.
- Body condition metrics (weight, fat/water content) were tracked as they correlated strongly with resistance (see Ecological Niche).

## Ecological & Environmental Context / Experimental Ecology Context

- **Test conditions:** Static 96-hour bioassays in 5-gallon glass jars; stock DDT dissolved in acetone (≤1 ml/L carrier). Fish held/tested at 12°C (La Crosse) or 17°C (Warm Springs).
- **Quarantine protocol:** 10-day quarantine pre-test, with food withheld for the final 3 days — introducing a **starvation stress variable** that significantly influenced results.
- **Body condition and resistance:** Fish in good nutritional condition (feeding, gaining weight) consistently showed **increasing tolerance** to DDT over successive tests (e.g., [[Rainbow Trout]] lot 17, [[Brook Trout]] lot 18, [[Goldfish]] lot 19, [[Carp]] lot 83, [[Green Sunfish]] lot 25). This aligns with [[Holden 1962]], who found fish with low fat reserves are more DDT-sensitive, and [[Phillips, Livingston and Dumas 1960]], linking starvation in brook trout to increased water content and decreased body fat/protein.
- **Seasonal cycling in Centrarchids:** [[Green Sunfish]], [[Pumpkinseed]], and [[Bluegill]] displayed **higher DDT tolerance in autumn**, potentially linked to seasonal anabolic-catabolic blood chemistry shifts as described in smallmouth bass by [[Shell 1961]].
- **Source/rearing effects:** Fish from different hatcheries showed different baseline tolerances — attributed to geographic location, water quality, pond fertilization, herbicide history, and feeding/handling practices. E.g., [[Brook Trout]] from Lake Mills NFH vs. St. Croix Falls SFH varied considerably, while [[Bluegill]] from Lake Mills NFH and Warm Springs NFH responded similarly.
- **Prior environmental exposure confound:** Because DDT is a widely used, environmentally persistent agricultural pesticide, hatchery fish (and their feed) may have prior sublethal exposure, potentially inducing acquired tolerance — consistent with findings in [[Gambusia Affinis]] (mosquitofish) by [[Vinson, Boyd and Ferguson 1963]] and [[Boyd and Ferguson 1964]], and in guppies by [[King 1962]].
- **Bioaccumulation context:** [[Cope 1960]] found DDT retained in trout and whitefish tissue nearly a year after aerial spruce budworm spraying, underscoring persistence relevant to pre-exposure risk.

## Key Findings

### Toxicity Ranking (Mean 96-hr LC50, ppb) — from most to least sensitive

| Species | Mean LC50 (ppb) | Consistency |
|---|---|---|
| [[Largemouth Bass]] | 0.8 | Single lot only |
| [[Yellow Perch]] | 0.9 | Rapid & consistent |
| [[Northern Pike]] | 1.7 | Single lot only |
| [[Green Sunfish]] | 4.5 | Rapid & consistent |
| [[Pumpkinseed]] | 4.5 | — |
| [[Bluegill]] | 4.5 | Rapid & consistent (confirmed across 2 hatchery sites) |
| [[Longear Sunfish]] | 8.7 | **Inconsistent** (4.9–12.5 ppb, significant biweekly increase) |
| [[Rainbow Trout]] | ~7–14 (weight-dependent) | **Inconsistent** |
| [[Channel Catfish]] | 17.5 | Consistent (only 1 lot) |
| [[Black Bullhead]] | 25.8 (range 17–42) | Declined over 7 weeks |
| [[Goldfish]] | 58.7 (range 21–180) | **Highly inconsistent** |
| [[Brook Stickleback]] | 67.0 | Least toxic tier |
| [[Northern Redbelly Dace]] | 68.0 | Least toxic overall |
| [[Fathead Minnow]] | Not determinable | Unreliable dose-response up to 1,000 ppb |

- **Species meeting "rapid and consistent" toxicity criteria (6 of 19):** [[Lake Trout]], [[Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Yellow Perch]].
- **Species failing criteria (5 of 19):** [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Goldfish]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Longear Sunfish]].
- **Salmonid selectivity:** Among the four trout species tested, toxicity was intermediate overall, but **Lake Trout was most sensitive and Brook Trout least sensitive** — indicating meaningful selectivity even among closely related salmonids. This parallels [[Hatch 1957]]'s field observation that aerial DDT application was sublethal to rainbow and brook trout but lethal to landlocked Atlantic salmon in the same watershed.
- **Size/weight effect:** DDT was generally **more toxic to smaller fish** within a species. Example: Rainbow trout >1.5g had mean LC50 ≈14 ppb vs. ≈7 ppb for fish <1.5g.
- **Regression slope analysis:** Log-concentration/response curves showed steep, well-defined slope for [[Rainbow Trout]] (slope = 1.16, narrow confidence intervals, precise toxicity threshold) vs. flat, poorly-defined slope for [[Goldfish]] (slope = 6.02, wide confidence intervals) — illustrating goldfish's erratic dose-response relationship.
- **Discrepancy with prior literature:** Authors could not confirm [[Henderson, Pickering and Tarzwell 1959]]'s reported fathead minnow LC50 values (32 ppb at 24h, 26 ppb at 96h), instead finding no reliable LC50 obtainable.

### Overall Conclusion

p,p'-DDT **partially** fulfills reference toxicant criteria (rapid/consistent toxicity in 6 species; adequate but undertested in 7 more) but **fails the nonselectivity requirement** critical for a universal reference standard, and its environmental ubiquity undermines the "uncommon in nature" criterion. The authors conclude p,p'-DDT has **limited usefulness as a general bioassay reference standard** across many species but remains valuable for **evaluating other candidate reference compounds** and underscores the ongoing need for a standardized reference fish and/or chemical in the [[Fish Control Program]].
