# Cave, Spring, and Swamp Fishes of the Family Amblyopsidae of the Central and Eastern United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #amblyopsidae

## Executive Summary

Woods and Inger (1957), of the [[Chicago Natural History Museum]], present a systematic revision of the North American fishes of the family [[Amblyopsidae]]. They recognize five species in three genera: [[Chologaster]] (two species), [[Typhlichthys]] (one species) and [[Amblyopsis]] (two species). The study combines meristic and morphometric counts, alizarin-stained skeletal preparations, a species key, a comparison with the [[Poeciliidae]]-group families, and a discussion of distribution and dispersal. The authors conclude that the cave-dwelling genera disperse through groundwater channels below the water table, while [[Chologaster Cornutus]] can disperse through surface streams.

## Background & Description

- **Historical literature:** Work on the family began with [[DeKay]] (1842), followed by Putnam (1872), Packard (1886), Cox (1905) and Eigenmann (1909). Eigenmann's 1909 monograph was the last full study of the family before this paper.
- **Scope:** The authors limited the study to North American amblyopsids and did not compare them with other blind cave fishes.
- **Methods:** Fin rays were counted (including rudimentary anterior rays), only branched caudal rays were counted, and measurements followed [[Hubbs]] and Lagler (1947) as a proportion of standard length. Vertebrae were counted on stained and X-rayed specimens. Alizarin staining with partial dissection revealed ligament origins and bone attachments that standard clearing could not show.
- **Material:** Specimens came from eight museum collections, including the Charleston Museum, University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, and United States National Museum, plus the authors' own collection.
- **Key diagnostic characters:**
  - Presence or absence of a postcleithrum (easily checked by slitting the axilla skin).
  - Eye development and pigmentation.
  - Presence of pelvic fins.
  - Rows of sensory papillae on each half of the caudal fin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *C. cornutus* lives in sluggish, sheltered streams and cypress swamps and has never been found in caves or springs. *C. agassizi* occurs in caves, spring mouths, swamps and creeks. *T. subterraneus* and the *Amblyopsis* species are confined to underground waters, including caves, spring mouths and wells.
- **Behaviour:** The species hide under bottom debris such as leaves and stones in swampy streams or pools at spring mouths.
- **Temperature:** Cave and spring waters inhabited by amblyopsids range from 50° to 60°F. Swamp and stream temperatures fluctuate more, and the range used for *C. cornutus* is about 39° to 80°F in the mud.
- **Climate and food:** The region rarely has surface water frozen for long, so food flow into caves is more or less continuous, though reduced in winter and drought.
- **Light:** Reduced light is the selective factor linked to eye degeneration, embedded scales and sensory papillae.
- **Reproduction:** Eggs are carried in the gill cavity only in *Amblyopsis*.
- **Physiography:** The cave species occur in the Interior Low Plateau and Ozark Plateaus, underlain by nearly horizontal Mississippian limestone with solution channels.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomy:** The authors recognize five species in three genera, based on pectoral girdle structure, scleral cartilages and eye presence. They could not separate *C. agassizi* and *C. papilliferus* at the species level, and they found no basis for subspecies within *C. agassizi* or *T. subterraneus*.
- **Synonymy:** *Forbesella* (later *Forbesichthys*) was separated from *Chologaster* by tactile papillae, a difference the authors found was not supported by *C. cornutus*. *Troglichthys* was not recognized, since *A. rosae* is a more specialized *Amblyopsis*. *T. osborni* and *T. wyandotte* were treated as synonyms of *T. subterraneus*.
- **Interspecies vs. intraspecies variation:** Table 1 shows consistent interspecies differences and only sporadic intraspecific differences.
- **Eye size:** Relative eye size in *Chologaster* decreases with standard length, so earlier reports of differing eye size do not hold.
- **Phylogeny:** Two independent lines of optic degeneration are assumed. *Chologaster* is considered the most primitive, with eyes and scleral cartilages absent in *Typhlichthys* and *Amblyopsis*. The authors favour a hypothesis in which *Amblyopsis* is one branch from a basal stock and *Chologaster* and *Typhlichthys* form another.
- **Morphology:** Amblyopsids differ from the Poecilioidea in premaxilla shape, postcleithrum, scale size, vertebral bend, shoulder girdle attachment, suborbital bones, sensory papillae and anterior neural spine shape.
- **Distribution:** All species except *C. cornutus* lie between the glacial limit and the Cretaceous Mississippi embayment shoreline, spanning about 6° of latitude (32° to 39° N).
- **Dispersal:** Surface dispersal of the cave genera is considered highly improbable. The authors propose that *Amblyopsis* and *Typhlichthys* disperse through groundwater solution channels below the water table. Surface divides are not barriers in horizontal limestone regions, based on a sight record from a spring well in Lawrence County, Missouri.
- **Relict pattern:** The two *Amblyopsis* populations are interpreted as relicts separated by competitively superior *Typhlichthys*. The authors suggest the apparent scarcity of *A. spelaea* near Mammoth Cave may reflect competition with *T. subterraneus* or sampling difficulty.
- **Conservation note:** Commercial collecting was considered a possible cause of reduced numbers, but the authors rejected it because *T. subterraneus* would also have been affected.
