# Dexter National Fish Hatchery & Technology Center – FY 1991 Annual Report
#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This is the Fiscal Year 1991 annual report of the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico. It was designated a Technology Center during FY 91, one of only five in the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The station is a refuge, research and propagation facility for imperiled fishes of the American Southwest and northern Mexico. Seventeen of the 78 listed freshwater fishes (of about 500 in the U.S.) are held there. Main events were:
- spawning of [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Woundfin]], [[Chihuahua Chub]] and [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]];
- salvage of Klamath Basin suckers;
- two severe storms that destroyed a razorback sucker fry lot and damaged the station;
- a request for funding to build proper culture facilities and for a formal genetics plan.

## Background & Description
- **History:** Authorized under the White Act of 1931. The 540-acre site was bought on August 31, 1931 and operations began in 1932. It entered the endangered species program in 1978 with three goals: refuge, study and production.
- **Station objectives:** maintain a protected gene pool; develop culture techniques; study biological requirements; supply live and preserved specimens; exchange expertise; publish; and run public information programs.
- **Water supply:** shallow aquifer wells (135–150 ft) at a constant 64°F and pH 7.5. Total hardness averages 2,100 mg/L and total dissolved solids 3,500 mg/L. The 1,000-ft artesian well collapsed during FY 91; the station plans to trade its 318 acre-ft artesian right for more shallow water to add to the 1,795 acre-ft shallow right.
- **Facilities:** a holding house with sixteen 350-gallon concrete tanks, four raceways, ten fiberglass circular tanks and 40 earthen ponds (22 surface acres in total). Effluent drains to two isolated sumps. Part of the land is co-managed with New Mexico Department of Game and Fish as a Wildlife Management Area.
- **Staff:** eight employees (seven permanent positions) plus two volunteers. Center Director Buddy L. Jensen. Fishery biologist Donald C. Hales and maintenance man Joe C. Owens retired.
- **Species held (Table 1):**
  - Catostomidae: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Lost River Sucker]], [[Shortnose Sucker]] (the Warner sucker was also received).
  - Cyprinidae: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]], [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]], [[Woundfin]], [[Guzman Beautiful Shiner]].
  - Ictaluridae: [[Yaqui Catfish]].
  - Cyprinodontidae: [[Leon Springs Pupfish]], [[Desert Pupfish]].
  - Poeciliidae: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Big Bend Gambusia]].
  - Region 1 species ([[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]], Warner, shortnose and Lost River suckers) are the only ones from outside New Mexico or its neighboring states.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Southwestern rivers have been dammed and diverted, and native fishes have declined sharply as a result. The [[Endangered Species Act]] also requires protecting habitat, and Dexter is described as a refuge and not a final solution.
- The station lies in the northern [[Chihuahuan Desert]] at 3,500 ft elevation, with about 12 inches of rainfall and a 180–200 day growing season.
- **Klamath Basin:** drought and irrigation releases from Clear Lake (Modoc County, California) led to a Section 7 jeopardy opinion to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. One prudent alternative was to hold [[Lost River Sucker]] and [[Shortnose Sucker]] in refuge.
- **Warner Basin:** water conditions will take longer to recover, and the [[Bureau of Land Management]] plans holding ponds in Warner Valley.
- **Water rights:** these appear fairly secure despite suits by Texas and the Mescalero Apache Tribe. Rights junior to the mid-1940s could be at risk if New Mexico loses. About $300,000 is available in FY 92 for pond lining.
- **Chara:** this rooted alga is a persistent problem in earthen ponds. Seventy triploid grass carp were purchased to control it; 30 were sacrificed for disease checks, 23 were lost in a power failure and 17 were stocked with squawfish brood. Squawfish were found to eat 8–10" grass carp.
- **Parasites:** anchor worm (*Lernaea*) was brought to the station five times in the year. Also reported were yellow grub (*Clinostomum marginatum*), leeches, "Ich" in woundfin and Asian tapeworm in Guzman beautiful shiner, woundfin and Virgin River chub. The director recommends that no more wild fish be brought to Dexter until drug and chemical use issues are resolved.

## Key Findings
**Razorback sucker**
- Twelve F1 domestic females (mean 5.91 lb) were spawned with chorionic gonadotropin. About 959,483 eggs were taken, a mean of 13,236 per pound and 79,957 per female, both lower than in previous years.
- Swimup fry totaled 154,357, a 16.6% survival from egg to swimup (FY 90: 11%). Fry averaged 183/gram versus 236/gram in FY 90.
- 247 adults (824 lb) went to the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
- A new stocking protocol favors larger, fewer fish: a 15" minimum for razorbacks (about 30 months to produce) and 8" for squawfish (about 40 months). The goal is 3,000–5,000 razorbacks and 10,000–12,000 squawfish stocked annually in Arizona. No fingerling or subadult distribution is expected for two or more years.
- Seven wild upper-basin fish (three females, four males) were spawned March 19. Two females ovulated and gave 73,301 and 115,815 eggs.
- A March 26 dust storm (winds above 55 mph) silted the pre-swimup fry tanks, with up to 1/2 inch accumulating, and a cold snap followed (water 41°F, air 19°F by March 31). The lot was lost.
- Broodstock held: 245 F1 domestic (from Lake Mohave stock) and 9 wild upper-basin fish, all PIT tagged.

**June 6 storm**
- Winds above 100 mph lasted 8–10 minutes, snapped the power pole and cut power for about 27 hours.
- Over 200 one-year-old Colorado squawfish were lost to hail, and 300 adult bonytail to oxygen depletion. A further 155 young squawfish were lost to a second hailstorm.
- Twelve of nineteen fiberglass tanks were destroyed.
- Losses exceeded $30,000. Together with the dust storm, this is used to justify the request for proper culture facilities.

**Klamath and Warner sucker salvage**
- The crew collected few Lost River suckers. The final truck load was 75 Warner, 85 shortnose and 24 Lost River suckers.
- There was no transport mortality, and parasites were removed manually. Many Warner suckers were emaciated on arrival.

**Colorado squawfish**
- Twenty F1 females (1974 year class) were spawned with carp pituitary and all spawned. They yielded 2,209,350 eggs and 545,220 swimup fry (29.9% survival).
- Three wild females were each crossed with seven males, giving 21 egg lots flown to Bellvue-Watson State Hatchery in Colorado.
- Pond survival of young-of-the-year was poor, with 7,924 fingerlings harvested.
- Brood held: 145 F1 (1974), 198 F1 (1981) and 10 wild.

**Bonytail chub**
- Four females were spawned for contaminant research (119,136 eggs; 30,000 sent to Yankton, SD). Three females gave 117,418 eggs for an otolith growth study, with fry reared at three temperatures. Growth was greatly reduced at the coolest temperature and similar at the two warmer ones.
- Brood held: 471 F1 (1981) and 8 Lake Mohave fish.

**Other species**
- **Woundfin:** 50 females were injected but only eight released eggs (about 800 eggs, 345 swimup fry). At year end there were 250 adults and 205 young-of-the-year.
- **Chihuahua chub:** 19 females gave 128,152 eggs. The reintroduction stocking was delayed to spring or fall 1992 pending paperwork.
- **Pahranagat roundtail chub:** held for Region 1. Ten of 30 injected females ovulated (39,835 ova), and 2,629 swimup fry were stocked. 200 fingerlings were added to 75 adults, and 50 were PIT tagged.
- **Virgin River roundtail chub:** pond spawning gave recruitment of 50,000 or more.
- **Guzman beautiful shiner:** still positive for Asian tapeworm despite treatment.
- **Yaqui beautiful shiner:** no longer held. The remaining fish went to San Bernardino NWR.
- **Yaqui catfish:** electrophoresis showed 33 hybrids, 11 channel catfish or backcrosses and 178 apparently Yaqui catfish. Genetic and morphometric data will be compared to select pure stock.
- **Pupfish, topminnow and gambusia:** [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] populations are strong (5,000 or more of each overwintered). 372 desert pupfish went to Arizona and 35 to the New England Aquarium. More than 10,000 [[Gila Topminnow]] were in winter holding. [[Big Bend Gambusia]] overwinter poorly outdoors, and the report proposes moving maintenance to Uvalde NFH.

**Cooperation and outreach**
- A Region 6 tagging study compared PIT, Carlin, VI and acrylic dye marks and is being prepared for publication.
- The station worked with many agencies and universities, including the [[National Fishery Contaminant Research Center]].
- About 2,500 people visited, including 8 guided tours.
- Staff taught at the Fisheries Academy and co-hosted a free fishing day.
- Cyclical maintenance obligations were $38,027 (wells and water lines), $50,118 (buildings) and $3,578 (equipment).

**Outlook and recommendations**
- Pond lining is expected to conserve water, cut pumping costs, and reduce Chara and the need for grass carp.
- Develop a formal [[Genetic Management Plan]] for all species, built on Dr. Tony Echelle's genetic diversity review.
- Add laboratory capacity for genetics and refill the vacated biologist position with someone qualified in genetics.
- Replace a deteriorated storage shed.
