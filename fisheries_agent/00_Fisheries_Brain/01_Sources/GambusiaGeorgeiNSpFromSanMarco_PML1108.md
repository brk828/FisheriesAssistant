# Gambusia georgei n. sp.: A New Endemic Mosquitofish from the Upper San Marcos River, Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gambusia

## Executive Summary

Hubbs and Peden (1969, *Copeia* No. 2, pp. 357-364) describe [[Gambusia Georgei]] as a new species from the upper [[San Marcos River]] at San Marcos, Texas. Three *Gambusia* species occur sympatrically there, each largely confined to a distinct habitat type. [[Gambusia Georgei]] was previously unrecognized in the literature. Its gonopodial and color characters distinguish it from [[Gambusia Affinis]] and [[Gambusia Geiseri]]. Hybridization with [[Gambusia Affinis]] is localized and does not appear to have altered the species' distinctness. The authors consider the species extremely vulnerable: minor habitat modification could exterminate it.

## Background & Description

The upper San Marcos River has long attracted attention for its endemic fauna. Hubbs and Peden (1968) reported on the endemic fishes of this area. [[Clark Hubbs]] and [[Michael M Stevenson]] collected the [[Holotype]] on 7 March 1968. The holotype is a male, 29.3 mm standard length (SL), deposited at the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]]. The 13 male paratypes measure 21.3 to 26.3 mm SL, and the female paratypes (31 measured) range from 25.6 to 39.2 mm SL. Additional specimens were examined from several collections, including material from [[San Marcos Springs]] and the San Marcos River between Rio Vista Dam and the Federal Fish Hatchery.

**Diagnostic characters (males):**
- Dorsal fin rays: 7 in 43 specimens, 8 in 1.
- Anal fin rays: 9 in 9 specimens, 10 in 1.
- Lateral scale counts: 27 in 3 specimens, 28 in 1.
- Gonopodium broadly acute. Ray 3 is strongly bowed over the elbow.
- Elbow composed of five to six enlarged segments fused on their anterior surfaces. Distal segments of ray 4a are slender and unfused.
- Ray 4p bears four or five retrorse, slightly curved serrae. A small retrorse claw is present on the posterior branch of ray 4.
- Usually two gonopophyses. The first lacks uncini and forms an angle of about 40° with the vertebral column. The second bears well-developed uncini. A weakly developed third gonopophysis occasionally occurs, possibly the result of introgression with [[Gambusia Affinis]].
- All males have thickened upper pectoral rays, the attribute [[Rosen And Bailey 1963]] used to define *Gambusia*.

**Coloration:** In preserved specimens, dark pigment cross-hatches the scale pockets. Lateral and mid-dorsal stripes are present. In life, the median fins show a pronounced lemon-yellow color, which provides a field identification character. Aggressive males develop black on the dorsal fin margin, the pectoral patch, and the cheek bar. Submissive males lose this pattern, and the diffuse lateral stripe becomes more intense.

**Etymology:** The species is named for [[George S Myers]], in recognition of his work on cyprinodont fishes and his interest in endangered species such as this one.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors studied the ecology of the three sympatric *Gambusia* species, which show clear habitat partitioning:

- **[[Gambusia Georgei]]:** A localized endemic of thermally consistent shallow areas with sparse aquatic vegetation.
- **[[Gambusia Affinis]]:** Predominates in shallow areas with dense vegetation.
- **[[Gambusia Geiseri]]:** The most morphologically and ecologically distinct of the three. It occurs in areas of dense vegetation and avoids the swift or moderate currents where the other two are rare.

Neither *G. affinis* nor *G. georgei* is abundant in areas of fast or moderate current. Thermal conditions in the San Marcos system are altered by seasonal conditions, and mud-bottomed areas are rare. The authors estimate that fewer individuals of [[Gambusia Georgei]] occur in the San Marcos River than of the other species. Its restriction to thermally stable, sparsely vegetated shallows makes it especially sensitive to changes in river conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **New species recognized.** Specimens had been confused with other *Gambusia* species. Careful study of dimorphic samples revealed two distinct morphs, one of which the authors describe as *G. georgei*.
2. **Diagnostic gonopodial structure.** The combination of an acute gonopodial tip, a strongly bowed ray 3, a thickened elbow, and a modified ray 4 separates the species from its congeners. Its gonopodium shares traits with both the [[Affinis Group]] and the [[Nobilis Group]], which complicates classification.
3. **Relationships are unresolved but favor affinis.** The authors compared the species with keys by [[Rivas Key]] and [[Rosen And Bailey 1963]]. Using the [[Character Index]], the species matches the [[Nobilis Group]] somewhat more closely on gonopodial traits. However, the hybridization evidence favors placing it in the [[Affinis Group]].
4. **Localized hybridization with *G. affinis*.** Five putative hybrid males were identified from the San Marcos River, along with several female intermediates. Their pattern is consistent with some hybridization, but the authors note that interbreeding is localized and the two morphs maintain their identities. Sampling was insufficient to demonstrate backcrossing conclusively. Despite extensive sympatry with [[Gambusia Geiseri]], no hybrids with that species were found.
5. **Conservation concern.** The species is extremely localized and could be exterminated by minor habitat modification, such as changes to thermal regime, vegetation density, or flow.

**Overall conclusion:** The authors conclude that [[Gambusia Georgei]] is a distinct species. Its taxonomic placement in the [[Affinis Group]] is supported by the hybridization evidence, but its mixed morphology keeps its relationships open to debate.
