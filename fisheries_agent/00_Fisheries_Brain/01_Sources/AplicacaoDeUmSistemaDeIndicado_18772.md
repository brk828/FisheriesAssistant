# Applying a Faunistic Indicator System (Vertebrates) in Northern Portugal: Relevance for Defining Protected Areas

#terrestrial-vertebrates #conservation-biology #faunistic-indicators

## Executive Summary

This 1991 study by A. Paulo Fontoura, published by the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências, Universidade do Porto), applies a vertebrate-based faunistic indicator system to evaluate the natural value of northern Portugal. It uses the [[Hiraldo and Alonso Index]] (1985), scoring 18 indicator species for each municipality (concelho) and each protected area. The main conclusions are that exceptional natural value occurs in the [[Montesinho Natural Park]] area and along the international stretch of the [[Douro Internacional]], that the Portuguese protected area network is reasonably distributed but needs expansion, and that the index is useful for wildlife management while requiring further research.

## Background & Description

The author notes that a central difficulty in wildlife management, whether nature conservation or game management, is the inability to objectively quantify the natural value of an area. Such valuation is presented as essential for rational resource use, for tracking environmental quality over time, and for predicting the effects of impacts.

Zoologists have used many criteria, from rarity and threat status of bioindicators to species diversity. Citing [[Helliwell Conservation Assessment]] (1978), the author argues that conserving a few rare species provides high conservation value, because such species are sensitive to habitat degradation and support rapid decisions in urgent situations.

The study adopts the index proposed by Hiraldo and Alonso for the Iberian Peninsula. Its components are:

- **ETP (population size):** from 4 points (fewer than 500 pairs, "exceptionally rare") down to 1 point (more than 20,000 pairs, "relatively common").
- **EA (area occupied):** from 4 points (local enclave, under 4,000 km²) down to 1 point (over 60,000 km²).
- **TE (population trend):** from 4 points (very rapid decline over the last 60 years) down to 1 point (very slow decline).

A global adjustment, V(world) = (V + T)/2, incorporates IUCN-style threat status T (67 for endangered, 50 vulnerable, 33 rare, 17 reduced numbers with a declining trend, 0 large and increasing populations). Because Portuguese population data were inadequate, the author used the Spanish values that Hiraldo and Alonso calculated for the Peninsula (Table 1), a choice made to avoid subjective results.

Species selection was restricted to breeding species in the Iberian Peninsula that are reasonably detectable. Excluded were introduced species, managed game species, species with low indicator values, species at the edge of their range (e.g., Black-winged Kite), and all aquatic species, which the author says require wetland-specific methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The indicator set is dominated by large raptors, carnivores and other vulnerable vertebrates. Their values range from 3 to 56 at the Iberian level. The highest-scoring species include the Imperial Eagle (V = 48, world value 56) and the Iberian Lynx (V = 36, world value 50). The Brown Bear and Bearded Vulture are marked as already extinct in Portuguese fauna.

Several species were recorded as lost from specific protected areas:

- In the Peneda-Gerês, the Lynx, Brown Bear and Griffon Vulture have disappeared, according to Pimenta (personal communication). These three highly valued species account for the high degradation estimate.
- In Montesinho, only indications of Brown Bear disappearance remain (Moreira, 1980).
- In the Alvão, the Golden Eagle has disappeared.
- In the Serra do Marão, the Griffon Vulture has disappeared (Reis Júnior, 1924).

The author notes that protected areas received more research effort, which made it possible to reconstruct historical species lists and estimate degradation. The Serra da Aboboreira, then under study for protected status, has a poorer fauna but no detected degradation.

The indicator distribution map (Fig. 1) covers the Egyptian Vulture, Golden Eagle, Bonelli's Eagle, Peregrine Falcon, Short-toed Snake Eagle, Hen Harrier, Marsh Harrier and Grey Wolf, among others.

## Key Findings

**Protected area evaluation (Table 3):**

| Area | Natural value (index) | Degradation |
|---|---|---|
| Peneda-Gerês National Park | 97 | 47% |
| Montesinho National Park | 127 | 17% |
| Alvão Natural Park | 66 | 14% |
| Alvão + Marão | 97 | 9% |
| Serra da Aboboreira | 42 | 0% |

**Municipal evaluation (Table 2):** About 50 municipalities were scored. The highest values were:

- Figueira de Castelo Rodrigo (Guarda): 128 (world value 144)
- Mogadouro (Bragança): 116 (146)
- Freixo de Espada à Cinta (Bragança): 116 (146)
- Miranda do Douro (Bragança): 110 (143)
- Braganca municipality: 70 (107)
- Montalegre: 54 (74)

The values were used to map natural value across northern Portugal (Fig. 2). Main patterns:

- **Exceptional value:** all municipalities bordering the Portuguese side of the Douro Internacional, and Bragança (Montesinho).
- **Very high value:** Vinhais, Vimioso, the upper Douro riverside municipalities (Alijó, Carrazeda de Ansiães, Torre de Moncorvo, Vila Nova de Foz Côa, S. João da Pesqueira), and the mountain massifs of Marão, Alvão, Peneda and Gerês.
- **High value:** parts of the "terra quente transmontana", Melgaço, and south-bank Douro municipalities (Cinfães, Mêda, Penedono).
- **Null value:** most coastal municipalities of Viana do Castelo, Braga and Porto, except Valongo and Paredes.

**Conclusions and recommendations:**

- The existing creation of Peneda-Gerês, Alvão and Montesinho is judged faunistically sound.
- A protected area in the Douro Internacional is strongly justified, as the author had argued previously (Ferrand de Almeida and Fontoura, 1986).
- Protection is also suggested for the Valongo and Paredes area (Serras de Santa Justa and Pias) and for the Serra da Aboboreira (Amarante, Baião, Marco de Canaveses), mainly because of its lack of degradation.
- Caution is advised. Mountain areas may be overvalued relative to others, and municipal boundaries do not match the nesting ranges of some species, so some municipalities may be overvalued.
- Further research is needed on the biology of indicator species to derive Portuguese-specific values, on new vertebrate and invertebrate indicators (to assess small areas and wetlands), and on wetland-specific methods.
- Municipal-level valuation is still considered useful for local-government resource management.

**Overall conclusion:** the existing protected area network is well distributed, but expansion is needed and urgent for the Douro Internacional, the Serra da Aboboreira, and possibly the Serras de Santa Justa and Pias.
