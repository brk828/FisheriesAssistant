# Fishery Investigations in Region VI: Periodic Survey of Waters (1966)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae #limnology

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report documents a year-long (1966) limnological and fisheries survey of two Region VI impoundments: **[[Parker Canyon Lake]]** and **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]**. Parker Canyon Lake reached spillway level for the first time since its 1963 construction, following a low point of nine feet below spillway in July. The central management concern is an **unauthorized introduction of [[Largemouth Bass]]** (discovered November 1965) into a lake previously managed for **[[Rainbow Trout]]**, which is now driving a documented decline in trout catch rates due to competitive and predatory pressure. At Pena Blanca Lake, a 49-acre put-and-take fishery, electrofishing (delayed to January 1967) revealed a healthy, multi-age-class largemouth bass population alongside black crappie, but flagged a growing **[[Green Sunfish]]** population as a potential competitive threat to more desirable centrarchids.

## Background & Description

### Size Class and Growth Data (Parker Canyon Lake, 1966)
- **Rainbow Trout**: Average size increased from 7.5 in (January) to a peak of 11.6 in (September), leveling to 9.9 in (December); several 14 in individuals recorded; largest reported specimen 17.5 in / 2.5 lb (June).
- **Channel Catfish** (stocked as 2 in fingerlings, August 1965): Averaged 8 in by June 1966, growing to 13 in by October 1966; some individuals reached 15 in — growth rate exceeded expectations.
- **Largemouth Bass**: Fishermen-caught individuals averaged ~8 in, with some in the 2–3 lb class; young-of-year observed in large numbers April–May 1966.

### Gill Net Sample (Parker Canyon Lake, Dec 19–20, 1966; 6,500 sq ft net, 19 hrs soak)
| Species | N caught | Avg. Length |
|---|---|---|
| Trout | 7 | 11.0 in |
| Channel Catfish | 2 | 10.7 in |
| Largemouth Bass | 9 | 4.7 in |

Notably, **no small (fingerling) trout were captured** despite appropriate mesh sizes being fished, strongly suggesting high mortality/predation of the April and October fingerling plants.

### Length-Frequency Data — Pena Blanca Lake Largemouth Bass (n=165, electrofished Jan 4–5, 1967)
- Size range: 2.3 in – 21.1 in
- Modal size classes at 3.7 in (n=28) and 4.4 in (n=45), consistent with strong recent year-class recruitment.
- Heavy angler cropping evident in the second year of life size classes.

### Other Species (Pena Blanca Lake)
- **Black Crappie**: 22 sampled, size averages of 5.0 in and 10.1 in, primarily adults in the 9–11 in range.
- **Green Sunfish**: 92 sampled; modal frequency at 4.5–5.4 in (n=54).
- **Bluegill**: 34 sampled; average size 6.3 in (20 at ~6 in, 10 at ~5 in, 4 at ~7 in).
- **Threadfin Shad**: 1 individual.
- **Channel Catfish**: 1 large individual, 22.3 in / 5 lbs.
- **Bullhead**: 2 individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Parker Canyon Lake]]** functions as a coldwater/coolwater transitional reservoir historically managed as a **[[Put-and-Take Trout Fishery]]**. The unauthorized bass introduction has shifted the system toward a **mixed warmwater-coldwater fishery**, creating direct competitive and predatory interactions between juvenile trout and an expanding bass population.
- Limnological conditions show stratification patterns typical of a temperate mesotrophic lake: surface temperatures ranged from 39°F (February) to 77°F (August); dissolved oxygen remained adequate to 15 ft depth year-round; pH stable (8.0–8.25); Secchi disk turbidity ranged from 3 ft 8 in (January) to >13 ft (August); plankton density (40 ft vertical haul) was low throughout, ranging 1.2 ml (August) to 5.1 ml (October) — indicative of an oligotrophic-to-mesotrophic productivity regime that may limit forage availability.
- **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]** represents a warmwater centrarchid-dominated system with an established **[[Largemouth Bass]]**–**[[Black Crappie]]**–sunfish assemblage. The disproportionate abundance of **[[Green Sunfish]]** relative to [[Bluegill]] suggests interspecific competitive exclusion dynamics within the Centrarchidae guild, a concern for maintaining game fish quality.
- Marginal, shallow habitat and cool water temperatures during the January electrofishing survey likely suppressed catchability of catfish and shad, both more effectively sampled via gill netting in warmer months.

## Key Findings

- Trout catch rates collapsed from ~1 fish/man-hour (Jan–Feb) to 0.1 fish/man-hour (August), attributed to competitive/predatory effects of introduced bass and other warmwater species.
- Total estimated 1966 trout catch (17,700) was less than half of 1965's catch (43,000), despite comparable stocking rates (60,000 fingerlings/year in both years).
- An estimated 700 largemouth bass and 300 channel catfish were creeled at Parker Canyon Lake in 1966.
- Gill net sampling revealed a complete absence of small trout size classes, despite 60,000 fingerlings stocked in 1966 — strong evidence of predation-driven fingerling mortality.
- Parker Canyon Lake reached spillway level for the first time since 1963 (August 20, 1966), following recovery from a 9 ft below-spillway low in July, aided by above-normal fall/winter precipitation.
- Management recommendation: defer a final trout-vs-bass management decision at least one more year to evaluate bass growth potential; if trout management is retained, a full **[[Rotenone Renovation]]** will be required; if bass management is chosen, introduction of forage species (e.g., plains red shiner) and possibly black crappie is recommended alongside the existing channel catfish fishery.
- At Pena Blanca Lake, population structure indicated good recruitment and multiple size classes of bass for 1967, but the growing green sunfish population was flagged as a candidate for future control measures if competitive interference with other game species is confirmed.
