# Bonytail Chub Stocking Authorization Request, ASU Research Park (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-elegans #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This 23 July 1992 letter from Paul C. Marsh of the [[Center for Environmental Studies]], [[Arizona State University]], asks ASU Research Park planning director Doug McQueen to authorize the stocking of 18 [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), an imperiled species, into the main lake at the [[ASU Research Park]] in Tempe. The fish are salvaged animals from [[Palm Lake]] at the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] that could no longer be held at the [[Cottonwood Ponds Facility]] of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD). The stocking was scheduled for 24 July 1992, pending the park's signed concurrence.

## Background & Description

The bonytail chub is described as an imperiled species. Its history at the park is as follows:

- **November 1985:** 879 juveniles were stocked into water features at the park by AZGFD and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), as part of a cooperative program with the park and the university.
- **November 1987:** An additional stocking of young fish augmented the population. The letter gives the number as "41500," which is most likely a scan error for 4,500 (not confirmed in the source).

The fish in the current request trace their origin to FWS's [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico. They were then held at the [[Niland Native Fish Facility]] of California Fish and Game. In 1990, AZGFD placed them into a recently renovated Palm Lake.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stress at Palm Lake:** In early summer 1992, AZGFD staff judged that fish in Palm Lake were stressed by extreme temperatures and low dissolved oxygen. They removed a number of individuals to the Cottonwood facility.
- **Capacity problem:** The Cottonwood installation no longer had adequate space to maintain these fish.
- **Conservation rationale:** Because of the origin and age of the fish, the authors considered it important to the species' overall conservation that they be kept under captive conditions.
- **Site suitability:** The park was selected because bonytail already inhabit its waters, routine bi-annual monitoring is ongoing as part of a continuing research program, and park administration has shown consistent interest and commitment to endangered species conservation.

## Key Findings

1. **Request:** Authorization is sought to stock 18 bonytail chub into the main lake at ASU Research Park.
2. **Timing:** Stocking by AZGFD personnel was scheduled for 24 July 1992, contingent on the park director's countersignature.
3. **Origin and rationale:** The fish are of FWS hatchery origin and were salvaged because of thermal and oxygen stress at Palm Lake and capacity limits at the Cottonwood facility.
4. **Documentation:** A follow-up memorandum and a copy of the stocking slip were to be sent to the park after stocking.
5. **Site history:** The species has occupied park waters since November 1985, with a cumulative stocking record of at least 879 juveniles in 1985 and a second stocking in 1987.
