# Comparison of Some Anesthetic Properties of Benzocaine and MS-222

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Moronidae #fish-anesthesia #pharmacology

## Executive Summary
This short paper by McErlean and Kennedy (1968) compares the anesthetic efficacy and toxicity of two chemically related compounds — **[[MS-222]]** (tricaine methanesulfonate) and **[[Benzocaine]]** (ethyl-p-aminobenzoate) — using **[[White Perch]]** (*Morone americanus*, syn. *Roccus americanus*) as the test organism. The study was motivated by regulatory uncertainty surrounding MS-222's "new drug" status in the United States, which threatened continued supply for use on food-relevant aquatic organisms. The authors demonstrate that benzocaine, a structurally similar but cheaper and more available compound, induces narcosis faster at low concentrations, though it carries greater lethality risk during prolonged exposure compared to MS-222.

## Background & Description
*Not applicable in the traditional anatomical sense — this paper focuses on chemical structure–activity comparison rather than fish morphology.*

**Chemical Structural Comparison:**
- Both **[[MS-222]]** and **[[Benzocaine]]** are homologues of **[[Benzoic Acid]]**, sharing:
  - An ethyl substitution (CH₃-CH₂–) on the acid side chain at position 1
  - An amine group (-NH₂) at position 4
- **MS-222** (Molecular Weight = 261.3): differs from benzocaine by addition of a methyl-sulfonate group (-HSO₃CH₃) to the amine
- **Benzocaine** (Molecular Weight = 165.2): lacks the sulfonate addition
- This structural difference accounts for large solubility disparity:
  - MS-222: 11–23% solubility
  - Benzocaine: 1 g / 2.5 L (markedly less soluble)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Test Species:** [[White Perch]] (*Morone americanus*), an estuarine fish native to Atlantic coastal waters of North America, commonly inhabiting brackish tidal systems such as the **[[Chesapeake Bay]]**.
- **Environmental conditions tested:**
  - Trial 1: Salinity 5.5 ppt, Temperature 15.3°C
  - Trial 2: Salinity 8.0 ppt, Temperature 11.8°C
  - Survival trial: Salinity 12.0 ppt, Temperature 12.0°C
- The use of estuarine water matching test salinity/temperature for post-exposure recovery observation reflects the species' natural physiological tolerance range within brackish [[Estuarine Ecosystems]].
- Relevance to fisheries management: as a potential human food organism, regulatory restrictions on anesthetic drugs used on white perch have direct implications for aquaculture and fisheries research practices.

## Key Findings

### Narcosis Induction Speed
- **3-minute trial** (n=64, 8 individuals/level; 4 concentrations: 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 mM/L; 5.5 ppt, 15.3°C):
  - Benzocaine induced narcosis **faster** than MS-222 at lower concentrations.
- **10-minute trial** (n=80, 10 individuals/level; 8.0 ppt, 11.8°C):
  - Low concentrations of MS-222 (0.05, 0.1 mM/L) failed to induce anesthesia within the time criterion and were replaced with higher concentrations (0.4, 0.5 mM/L).
  - Results **corroborated** the 3-minute trial: Benzocaine is more effective (faster-acting) at lower concentrations than MS-222.

### Lethality / Survival Assessment
Conducted at 12.0 ppt, 12.0°C; 5 individuals per concentration level; observed at 1, 2, 4, and 8 hours contact time, followed by ≥4-hour recovery observation.

| Concentration (mM/L) | 1 hr (B/M) | 2 hr (B/M) | 4 hr (B/M) | 8 hr (B/M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 100/100 | 100/100 | 100/100 | 100/100 |
| 0.10 | 100/100 | 100/100 | 80/100 | 80/80 |
| 0.20 | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/0 | 0/0 |

*(B = Benzocaine, M = MS-222; values = % survival)*

- At **0.05 mM/L**: Both anesthetics permit full recovery even after 8 hours contact.
- At **0.10 mM/L**: Full survival up to 2 hours; longer exposure causes partial mortality, with **MS-222 being less lethal** than benzocaine at extended (4–8 hr) contact times.
- At **0.20 mM/L**: Unsuitable for survival with either compound — near-total mortality.

### Practical/Economic Considerations
- **Benzocaine** does not dissolve as readily as MS-222 — noted as its only practical disadvantage.
- **Benzocaine** is freely available from standard chemical supply houses (unlike MS-222, which faced regulatory/supply restrictions under the emerging **[[FINQUEL]]** brand via Ayerst Laboratories, requiring a "Statement of Investigator" form).
- **Cost comparison:** Benzocaine costs $3.20/100g — approximately **1/11th the cost** of MS-222, offering substantial economic advantage for research and fisheries applications.
- Long history of safe use of benzocaine as a **human topical anesthetic** (throat lozenges, sprays, sunburn creams, hemorrhoid treatments) is cited as supporting precedent, though its acceptance for anesthetizing food organisms like fish remained undetermined at time of publication.

### Overall Conclusion
Benzocaine is presented as a viable, cost-effective, and readily available alternative to MS-222 for fish anesthesia, particularly effective at low concentrations for rapid induction, though users should be mindful of its lower solubility and slightly greater lethality risk during prolonged high-concentration exposure relative to MS-222.
