# Building Successful Partnerships in the Sonoran Desert: An Alliance is Born

#marine-biology #ichthyology #conservation #ecosystem-management #binational-cooperation

## Executive Summary

This document describes the formation and mission of the [[International Sonoran Desert Alliance]], a nonprofit organization incorporated in January 1994 to develop comprehensive, binational approaches to natural resource management in the western [[Sonoran Desert]] and [[Upper Gulf of California]] bioregion. The alliance emerged from partnerships between [[Friends of PRONATURA]], the [[Sonoran Institute]], and the [[Tohono O'odham Nation]], addressing the need for unified management of one of the world's largest intact arid ecosystems. The region harbors numerous endemic and endangered species including the [[Vaquita]] dolphin, [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Sonoran Pronghorn]] antelope, and marine species such as [[Green Sea Turtle]] and [[Leatherback Turtle]].

## Background & Description

The western [[Sonoran Desert]] and [[Upper Gulf of California]] bioregion represents one of the largest, primarily intact arid ecosystems globally. In the United States, protected areas include [[Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge]], [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], and [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]]. In Mexico, the region encompasses the recently designated [[Pinacate Biosphere Reserve]] and [[Upper Gulf of California Biosphere Reserve]]. The [[Tohono O'odham Nation]] homelands comprise a substantial portion of the region.

Endemic and endangered species documented in the region include:
- [[Vaquita]] dolphin (marine mammal)
- [[Elegant Tern]] (seabird)
- [[Green Sea Turtle]] (marine reptile)
- [[Leatherback Turtle]] (marine reptile)
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (freshwater fish, family [[Cyprinodontidae]])
- [[Sonoran Pronghorn]] antelope (terrestrial mammal)
- [[Lesser Long-nosed Bat]] (terrestrial mammal)

The alliance originated from a 1988 symposium on the [[Pinacate]] volcanic area cosponsored by [[Friends of PRONATURA]] and the Sonora-Arizona Commission. [[Friends of PRONATURA]], founded in 1986, is affiliated with the Mexican conservation organization [[PRONATURA A.C.]] (established 1981), one of Mexico's first nonprofit conservation groups. The [[Sonoran Institute]], founded in 1990, focuses on preserving protected natural areas while meeting economic needs of adjacent communities through locally-driven, public-private partnerships.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Despite substantial protected land status, the region lacks unified management strategy due to multiple barriers: language differences, sovereignty issues, the international border, economic disparity, differences in international law, diverse cultural perspectives, fragmented land-management authority, competing mandates, and inadequate agency budgets. The [[North American Free Trade Agreement]] (NAFTA) and projected tourism increases threaten to exacerbate these management challenges.

The [[Pinacate]] region holds particular cultural significance as sacred land to the [[Tohono O'odham Nation]], historically part of their homeland. The O'odham expressed strong desire to participate in future land-use planning during the 1988 symposium.

Ecosystems transcend arbitrary preserve boundaries, necessitating international networks with community participation to protect the broader ecosystem upon which protected areas and surrounding communities depend.

## Key Findings

**Organizational Structure and Formation:**
- The [[International Sonoran Desert Alliance]] was incorporated as a nonprofit organization in Arizona in January 1994
- The alliance board comprises 3 members each from the United States, Mexico, and the [[Tohono O'odham Nation]] (9 total members)
- Monthly meetings average 40 attendees and are open to the public
- Efforts are underway to create a sister nonprofit alliance in Mexico

**Community Engagement Events:**
- October 1992: Binational town-hall meeting in Ajo, Arizona, attracted more than 200 participants from communities including Ajo, Why, Lukeville, Gila Bend (Arizona), Sonoyta, and Puerto Peñasco (Sonora), plus business leaders, government representatives, and O'odham Nation members
- December 1992: Core group began regular meetings following the town-hall conference
- January 1994: "Bridging Borders: A Cross-Border Exchange" conference convened by the alliance, [[Friends of PRONATURA]], and [[Sonoran Institute]]

**Strategic Recommendations from 1994 Conference:**
Nine working groups developed strategies addressing:
- Health care
- Tourism development
- Management of protected areas and biosphere reserves
- Information exchange across international borders
- Economic development
- Environmental education
- Trade and transportation

Two panels (Stories of the Region; Science & Research) featured researchers and scientists sharing knowledge and historical information.

**Key Conservation Proposals:**
- Fund environmental education programs with tourist dollars
- Combat substance abuse through promotion of cultural traditions
- Develop a cooperative international mechanism modeled on biosphere reserve frameworks
- Establish international networks incorporating community participation for ecosystem protection

Conference proceedings were published with support from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and Bank One.
