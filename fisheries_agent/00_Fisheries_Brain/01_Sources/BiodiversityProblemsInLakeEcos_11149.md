# Biodiversity Problems in Lake Ecosystems (Yangtze River Basin, China)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #limnology #biodiversity-conservation

## Executive Summary

This review by Xie Ping and Chen Yiyu (Institute of Hydrobiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences) synthesizes multi-decadal evidence of biodiversity decline in the shallow lake ecosystems of the middle and lower [[Yangtze River]] basin, China. Freshwater resources — though only ~0.4% of global water — support ~8,400 described [[Freshwater Fish]] species worldwide (~40% of all fish species), with China hosting over 800 primary freshwater species, ~500 endemic. The paper identifies five interconnected drivers of biodiversity loss in Yangtze basin lakes: (1) lake shrinkage/fragmentation from sedimentation and agricultural conversion, (2) isolation of lakes from the river disrupting migratory fish life cycles, (3) over-fishing causing dominance shifts toward small-bodied fishes, (4) secondary extinction cascades following destruction of macrophyte communities by over-stocked grass carp, and (5) accelerated eutrophication in urban lakes, including toxicological risks from cyanobacterial [[Microcystin]] production. Case studies center on [[Lake Dongtin]] (Dongting), [[Lake Honghu]], and [[Lake Donghu]] (Wuhan).

## Background & Description

### Freshwater Resources Context
- Freshwater constitutes only 3% of total global water; of this, 77.2% is ice, 22.4% is groundwater/soil water, and just ~0.4% exists in lakes, rivers, and other surface water bodies (IIED, 1987).
- ~43 nations (60% of world land area) face water shortage; ~200 million people are water-stressed and 100 million lack adequate drinking water.
- China's per-capita annual freshwater supply (~2,600 tons) is only 1/4 of the world average.

### Freshwater Fish Diversity
- Fishes represent the largest vertebrate group, with >22,000 described species globally.
- Marine landings comprised 86.2% of 1989 global fish landings; inland fisheries (aquaculture + capture) made up 13.8% (FAO, 1991). In China (1990), inland fisheries accounted for 42% of total landings.
- ~8,400 freshwater fish species exist globally (~40% of all fish species); ~1,500 species in Asia (Nelson, 1984).
- China has >800 primary freshwater fish species, of which ~500 are endemic.
- Freshwater ecosystems support far smaller species ranges than marine systems: ~15 km² per freshwater species vs. ~100,000 km² per marine species, making freshwater fish populations inherently smaller and more extinction-prone (Groombridge, 1992).

### Species Extinction & Endangerment
- An estimated ≥20% (~1,800 species) of the world's freshwater fish are threatened or extinct, primarily from habitat modification, introduced species, and commercial exploitation.
- In North America, nearly 1/3 of fish species are endangered, threatened, or of special concern (Williams et al., 1989).
- China has 90 vulnerable or endangered freshwater fish species.
- [[Cyprinus Yilongensis]] (endemic to Yunnan) is extinct; [[Psephurus Gladius]] (Chinese paddlefish), [[Macrura Reevesi]], and [[Hucho Bleekeri]] are critically endangered.
- Case example: Erroneous introduction of [[Pseudorasbora Parva]] (intended to be grass carp) into Lake Luguhu drove three endemic [[Schizothoracine]] fish species toward extinction (Liang, 1992).

### Yangtze River Basin Lake Ecosystems
- Chinese lakes total ~74,300 km² surface area; 42% located in humid eastern China.
- The Yangtze River (6,300 km, 3rd longest river globally) basin lakes constitute ~1/8 of the region's surface area and produce ~2/3 of China's total fish landings.
- These shallow, unstratified lakes formed via late Tertiary river flooding, interconnected with the main river as a complex river-lake ecosystem, supporting high productivity and developed littoral macrophyte communities.
- ~300 fish species occur in the Yangtze basin; over half belong to family [[Cyprinidae]], including most commercially important species.
- Key migratory species historically dependent on river-lake connectivity: [[Hypophthalmichthys Molitrix]] (silver carp), [[Aristichthys Nobilis]] (bighead carp), [[Ctenopharyngodon Idellus]] (grass carp), [[Mylopharyngodon Piceus]] (black carp), [[Ochetobius Elongatus]], [[Luciobrama Macrocephalus]], [[Squaliobarbus Curriculus]], and [[Parabramis Pekinensis]] — the first four comprising the "four domestic carps" central to Chinese aquaculture.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### (1) Lake Shrinkage and Fragmentation
- Sedimentation and dike-driven agricultural conversion have drastically reduced lake surface areas:
  - [[Lake Dongtin]] (Dongting): 4,350 km² (1949) → 2,619 km² (1983)
  - [[Lake Honghu]]: 760 km² (early 1950s) → 350 km²
  - Jiang Han Plain: 1,066 lakes (8,300 km²) → 309 lakes (5,600 km²) by early 1980s
- Per Welcomme (1979), fish species richness correlates positively with river basin area; shrinking lake area reduces maximum viable population sizes, increasing extinction risk for freshwater fish.

### (2) River-Lake Isolation from Hydroelectric/Irrigation Projects
- Dike construction has severed lake-river connectivity, disrupting migratory life cycles (juveniles rear in lakes, adults return to river to spawn) essential to the "four domestic carps."
- Fishery resource declines in the Yangtze:
  - 1980s marketable-sized catch: half of 1950s levels
  - Natural fry catch: 1/4 of 1960s levels
  - Compositional shift toward small-bodied, lake-dwelling species
- Genetic degradation from prolonged artificial-stock inbreeding in [[Hypophthalmichthys Molitrix]] (silver carp):
  - Natural population: females mature at 3–4 years (4.85 kg); males at 3 years (3.81 kg)
  - After 5 generations of inbreeding: females mature at 2 years (1.25 kg, min. 0.3 kg); males mature at 1 year (0.69 kg)
  - 11× higher disease incidence and elevated malformation rates in inbred offspring
- Over 30 fish species have had migratory routes altered or destroyed by river-lake isolation projects.

### (3) Over-fishing and Dominance of Small-Sized Fishes
- Natural Yangtze basin lakes historically supported ~100 fish species; now reduced to 30–40 species.
- [[Lake Honghu]] case: >100 species pre-isolation → 74 species (1964) → 54 species (1981–82); only 31 species were true resident lake fish (1/3 of natural fauna).
  - Fish yield: ~10,000 tons (1950s, dominated by four domestic carps, [[Cyprinus Carpio]], [[Parabramis Pekinensis]]) → 3,000–4,000 tons (1980s), 87% composed of small-bodied [[Carassius Auratus]], [[Pseudobagrus Fulvidraco]], and [[Culter Erythropterus]].
- [[Lake Dongtin]] case: max yield 45,000 tons; 1950s average 30,700 tons → 15,000 tons (1980s); shift from migratory carps to lake-dwelling [[Cyprinus Carpio]], [[Carassius Auratus]], [[Silurus Asotus]].
- Similar species dominance shifts (toward small [[Coilia]] species) documented in Lake Caohu, Lake Taihu, and Lake Hongzhe.

### (4) Secondary Extinction from Macrophyte Community Destruction
- Over-stocking of grass carp ([[Ctenopharyngodon Idellus]]) destroys submerged macrophyte beds via direct grazing and suppression of shoot regrowth.
- Case study — Guozheng area, [[Lake Donghu]]: macrophyte biomass collapsed from 1,779.8 g/m² to 5.8 g/m², triggering a primary-producer regime shift from macrophyte to phytoplankton dominance (analogous to deforestation dynamics).
- Mechanism: macrophyte decline releases stored nutrients → favors algal (r-selected) growth → reduced water transparency → further suppresses macrophyte recovery (vicious cycle).
- Secondary extinctions cascade to periphyton and molluscs dependent on macrophyte substrate, and to fish species relying on macrophytes for spawning substrate or food:
  - Reduced spawning substrate for [[Cyprinus Carpio]], [[Carassius Auratus]], and [[Ophicephalus Argus]]
  - Reduced mollusc-based food supply affecting [[Cyprinus Carpio]] and [[Mylopharyngodon Piceus]]
  - Community shift toward filter-feeding planktivorous fishes
- Biodiversity indices (Margalef, Simpson, Shannon-Weaver) for diatoms showed significant negative correlation with nutrient levels across four zones of Lake Donghu (Lei, 1994).
- Rotifer Margalef index at Lake Donghu declined to 1/3 of 1962 levels by 1991 alongside eutrophication (Zhuge, 1992).
- Reduced ecosystem biodiversity/stability associated with more frequent cyanobacterial bloom outbreaks and bacterial "blood poisoning" fish disease outbreaks — paralleling monoculture-driven pest outbreaks in agriculture.

### (5) Accelerated Eutrophication in Urban Lakes
- Natural lake eutrophication occurs over centuries; anthropogenic acceleration (untreated waste discharge + unreasonable fishery management) can shift lakes from mesotrophic to hypertrophic within decades.
- Lake Donghu (Wuhan) case: macrophyte biomass fell from 1,779.8 g/m² (1963) to 5.8 g/m² (1975), while phytoplankton production rose from ~1 g O₂/m²/day to 4.1 g O₂/m²/day; total fish yield rose from 346 to 802 tons, with silver/bighead carp proportion increasing from 84% to >98%.
- Consequent water quality decline: reduced transparency, cyanobacterial ("water bloom") outbreaks, malodor — undermining drinking water supply and recreational value.
- Toxicological concern: [[Microcystin]] (MCRST), a secondary metabolite of [[Microcystis]] cyanobacteria associated with eutrophication, is hepatotoxic, inhibits protein phosphatase activity, and acts as a tumor promoter (Falconer 1983; Eriksson 1990; Honkanen 1990; Nishiwaki-Matsushima 1991) — posing direct human health risks given China's freshwater scarcity.

## Key Findings

- Freshwater fish biodiversity is disproportionately vulnerable relative to marine fish due to smaller habitat area per species and smaller population sizes, making freshwater ecosystems far less resilient to disturbance.
- Five compounding anthropogenic pressures — lake shrinkage, river-lake disconnection, over-fishing, macrophyte loss, and eutrophication — interact synergistically to drive cascading biodiversity loss in Yangtze basin lakes.
- Species richness in Yangtze lakes has fallen from historical baselines of ~100 species to 30–54 species in documented case studies (e.g., [[Lake Honghu]]), with concurrent shifts from large migratory commercial species to small-bodied, lake-resident species.
- Genetic inbreeding depression in artificially propagated stocks of the "four domestic carps" is producing smaller, earlier-maturing, disease-prone fish, undermining long-term restocking viability; wild recruitment from unconnected lakes ([[Lake Boyang]], [[Lake Dongtin]]) is now critical to sustaining Yangtze River fish populations.
- Grass carp over-stocking is a primary driver of submerged macrophyte community collapse, initiating a self-reinforcing regime shift toward phytoplankton dominance and triggering secondary extinctions across periphyton, molluscs, and macrophyte-dependent fish.
- Eutrophication is correlated with measurable declines in biodiversity indices for diatoms and rotifers, and coincides with increased frequency of cyanobacterial blooms and fish disease outbreaks, suggesting reduced ecosystem stability at low biodiversity states.
- Microcystin production in hypertrophic urban lakes represents a direct public health hazard, linking aquatic biodiversity degradation to human water security in a nation already experiencing severe freshwater scarcity.
