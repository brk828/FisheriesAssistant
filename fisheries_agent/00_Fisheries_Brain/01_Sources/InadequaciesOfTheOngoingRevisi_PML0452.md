# Critique of the Draft Revised Apache Trout Recovery Plan (2005 Letter)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This March 9, 2005 letter from three members of the [[Apache Trout Recovery Team]] (Robert W. Clarkson, Jerry G. Ward, and Alex A. Puglisi) to Dale Hall, Regional Director of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 2), objects to the draft revision of the [[Apache Trout]] recovery plan. The authors argue that the Plan, as written, will not conserve the species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). Their main objections are that the Plan:

1. Does not aim to conserve the species as defined by the ESA.
2. Largely ignores the best available science on effective population size, minimum stream length, and connectivity.
3. Does not protect the species' remaining genetic variability.

The authors request that the Team reconsider its direction and that their recommended changes be incorporated before the Plan is finalized.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), a [[Salmonidae]] trout native to the mountain streams of east-central Arizona and western New Mexico.
- **Document type:** A formal comment letter on a draft recovery plan. It was copied to the [[U.S. Forest Service]] Regional Forester (Albuquerque) and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Phoenix), and to other Recovery Team members.
- **Authors:** Robert W. Clarkson (Phoenix, AZ), Jerry G. Ward (Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest, Springerville, AZ), and Alex A. Puglisi (Santa Fe, NM). The letter says they are members of the Recovery Team.
- **Literature cited:** Three papers:
  - [[Allendorf et al. 1997]]: prioritizing Pacific salmon stocks for conservation (*Conservation Biology* 11:140–152).
  - [[Hilderbrand and Kershner 2000]]: conserving inland cutthroat trout in small streams, focusing on how much stream is enough (*North American Journal of Fisheries Management* 20:513–520).
  - [[Reiman and Allendorf 2001]]: effective population size and genetic conservation criteria for bull trout (*North American Journal of Fisheries Management* 21:756–764).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Historic range and current status.** The letter states that the Plan estimates Apache trout, even if all draft strategies and objectives are met, would remain extirpated from much of the species' estimated historic range. The authors consider this inadequate for recovery.

**Habitat requirements.** Apache trout are linked to small, headwater streams. The letter says the Plan does not intend to support populations in mainstem rivers such as the White River, and that the Plan's range is restricted to extreme headwater streams. The authors argue that this restriction ignores the species' evolutionary ecosystem, which included variable conditions such as wet periods and extreme low temperatures.

**Climatic and demographic risk.** The authors note that population performance and suitability in small streams are susceptible to climatic fluctuations. Small, isolated populations therefore face a higher risk of random extinction events. In their view, the Plan does not reduce this risk over the long term.

**Stream length and connectivity.** The letter cites [[Hilderbrand and Kershner 2000]] to argue that where local stream length cannot support large populations, interconnected stream networks should be established to reach population minima.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Plan does not conserve the species as defined by the ESA
- The ESA's stated purpose is "to provide a means whereby the ecosystem upon which endangered species and threatened species depend may be conserved."
- The ESA defines an **endangered species** as one "in danger of extinction throughout all or a significant portion of its range." A **threatened species** is one "likely to become an endangered species within the foreseeable future."
- The authors argue that the Plan's delisting actions do not meet these standards, because the species would remain at high extinction risk across much of its range.

### 2. Best available science is not incorporated into recovery criteria
- **Effective population size:** [[Reiman and Allendorf 2001]] recommend an effective population size averaging **1,000 spawning adults per year** to ensure long-term persistence of trout populations.
- **Total population size:** [[Allendorf et al. 1997]] suggest total population sizes of approximately **2,500 individuals**.
- **Stream length and connectivity:** Where local stream length cannot support these sizes, [[Hilderbrand and Kershner 2000]] recommend establishing interconnected stream populations.
- The Plan itself states that such population sizes are "likely unattainable in most locations based on stream size and existing population estimates." Even so, it proposes delisting. The authors argue this leaves Apache trout highly vulnerable to local extinction.

### 3. The Plan does not protect genetic variability
- An earlier draft included criteria to **replicate each of the remnant historical lineages** of Apache trout. The authors say this was removed in the latest revision.
- Replicating lineages preserves genetic variation, which lets the species adapt to changing climate and other stochastic factors.
- Removing this requirement allows greater reliance on [[Hatchery Stocking]], which the authors describe as "among the least desirable management actions," especially when better alternatives are available.
- The authors argue that failing to conserve genetic variability is incompatible with the use of best scientific information under the ESA.

### 4. Recommended solutions
The authors recommend:
- Significantly increasing the amount of suitable habitat protected behind fish barriers, with networks of interconnected streams.
- Ensuring that remnant lineages are replicated within expanded recovery stream networks.

The authors expect these actions to:
1. Allow population sizes large enough to overcome the genetic effects of small populations.
2. Maintain genetic variability so the species can continue along its historical evolutionary trajectory.
3. Provide larger streams and more diverse habitat to support a [[Metapopulation]] model of range expansion and contraction.
4. Allow the species to expand into a much greater proportion of its historic range, as the ESA directs.

### 5. Overall conclusion
The authors conclude that implementing the revised Plan as written will not conserve Apache trout in line with ESA provisions. They believe it will eventually lead to further genetic degradation and possible extinction of the species. They ask the Recovery Team to reconsider its direction and to incorporate their comments before finalizing the Plan.
