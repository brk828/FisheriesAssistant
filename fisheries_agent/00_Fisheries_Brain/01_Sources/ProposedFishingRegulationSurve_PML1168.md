# 1998 Arizona Proposed Fishing Regulations and Angler Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a September 30, 1997 package from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (Wildlife Management Division) to the Commission. It covers proposed changes to [[Commission Order 40]] (Fish), which sets open seasons, open areas, and bag and possession limits for 1998. It includes a statewide angler survey, public comments, and Department recommendations on twelve proposals. The Department's main goal in most proposals is to protect [[Native Fish]] in critical habitat, mainly to meet obligations under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Several proposals drew strong public opposition, especially the requirement that all fish be kept when caught.

## Background & Description

**Public process.** The Department mailed 1,800 surveys on August 13, 1997 to licensed anglers, stratified by region (300 per region). Forty-three were undeliverable, leaving a valid sample of 1,757. 418 were returned, a response rate of 23.8%, with consistent returns across regions. Public outreach included eight public meetings in August, seven letters, 15 e-mails, three internet pages, three radio talk shows, and coverage in 12 newspapers.

**Survey format.** Each proposal was rated on a 1 to 10 scale (1 = strongly disapprove, 10 = strongly support), with mean ratings and comment sections. Survey comments were often uninformed or off-topic (for example, several respondents said they were unfamiliar with the waters), which the Department acknowledged as a limit on interpretation.

**Species and waters.** The proposals concern warmwater sport fish (largemouth and smallmouth bass, channel and flathead catfish, sunfish, striped bass), trout, and small native fishes listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Waters include the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], [[Verde River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Blue River]], [[Virgin River]], [[Colorado River]] in the [[Grand Canyon]], [[Aravaipa Creek]], [[Sycamore Creek]], and several northern Arizona trout waters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fish recovery.** Several proposals respond to critical habitat designated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service for threatened and endangered fish. The Grand Canyon section of the Colorado River is critical habitat for the [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. East Clear Creek, Nutrioso Creek and Chevelon Creek are critical habitat for the [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]].
- **Predation.** The Department's stated rationale for removing non-native warmwater fish (Proposal 2) is to reduce predation on native fish. It states that it does not plan to eliminate non-native warmwater species entirely.
- **Live bait.** Proposals 5 to 7 restrict the movement of live bait fish between river sections to limit the spread of non-native fish into native-fish reaches. Above Tuzigoot Bridge on the [[Verde River]] there is a large native fish population and the reach is a reintroduction site for razorback sucker. Below it, a large catfish population is easily caught with sunfish or carp bait.
- **Trout and Apache trout.** The proposal to change trout harvest in the Little Colorado watersheds is intended to remove rainbow and brown trout that compete with or prey on spinedace. The Department noted that the trout-spinedace relationship is unclear, though literature shows trout eat spinedace. Possible reintroduction of [[Apache Trout]] into these watersheds was raised as a concern.
- **Coldwater fisheries.** Proposal 2 does not affect coldwater trout fisheries such as Oak Creek Canyon and the winter trout program on the Verde River.

## Key Findings

**Proposal 1: Close Aravaipa Creek (Pinal and Graham counties) and Sycamore Creek (Santa Cruz County) to fishing.**
- Justification: native fish reaches with little fishing pressure, closed to prevent introduction of non-native fish. Department recommends adopting as written. Public opinion was mixed; some opposed setting a precedent for closures.

**Proposal 2: Unlimited harvest of largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, channel catfish and flathead catfish in eight waters (Gila, Virgin, Salt, Verde, San Francisco, Blue, Campbell Blue Creek, and Eagle Creek), with all fish to be kept.**
- Public split: 51% in favor, 49% against. 16% strongly supported and 28% strongly disapproved.
- Department recommends adopting with one exception: drop "all fish must be kept when caught." The requirement would push anglers to violate [[Commission Order 40]] or the wasting-game law if they discarded a fish, and it was seen as discouraging fishing.

**Proposal 3: Unlimited harvest of trout, channel catfish and striped bass in the Colorado River, Grand Canyon.**
- Public split 51% for and 49% against. Strong opposition to the "all fish must be kept" wording, which was called "wanton waste" in comments.
- Department recommends reducing the area from Separation Canyon to Marble Canyon Bridge to Separation Canyon to Mile 21 Rapids, including all tributaries, and dropping the keep-all-fish sentence.

**Proposal 4: Unlimited harvest of trout from September 1 to May 1 in Blue Ridge and Knoll reservoirs, East Clear Creek, Nutrioso Creek, Nelson Reservoir and Chevelon Creek.**
- Justification: maintain summer trout angling while removing rainbow and brown trout to aid spinedace recovery.
- Public support was weak: about 14% strongly supported, with strong disapproval also substantial.
- Department recommends adopting with two changes: limit the harvest to "rainbow and brown trout" (because Apache trout reintroduction is possible) and drop the keep-all-fish sentence. The trout bag limit for other species remains six.

**Proposal 5: No live bait fish above Tuzigoot Bridge on the Verde River; only legal non-native bait caught from the Verde may be used below the bridge.**
- Public strongly supportive (about 31% strongly approving; few arguments against).
- Department recommends adopting, dropping "no live bait fish may be stocked back into the Verde River," which was seen as redundant and confusing.

**Proposal 6: No live bait fish transported to the Salt River above Roosevelt Diversion Dam (Gila County).**
- Public strongly supportive (about 33%). Department recommends adopting with the stocking sentence dropped, for the same reason as Proposal 5.

**Proposal 7: No live bait fish transported to the Gila River from the New Mexico border downstream to San Carlos Indian Reservation.**
- Public strongly supportive (about 35%). Department recommends adopting with the stocking sentence dropped.

**Proposal 8: Increase the two-day trout possession limit from 6 to 12.**
- Survey: over 50% of respondents agreed. Public meetings were largely opposed. Trout Unlimited and the bass club opposed it, citing difficulty of enforcement, angler confusion over limits at different locations, and risk to restricted waters.
- Department recommends the Commission not change the limit; it stays at six trout.

**Proposal 9: Eliminate cast nets, seines and dip nets on designated urban lakes.**
- Comments were mixed, including a concern about swimmers. The source contains no Department recommendation for this proposal.

**Proposal 10: Allow four catfish to be caught and possessed at Granite Basin Lake (Yavapai County).**
- Justification: the 5-acre lake on the Prescott National Forest is heavily used; during 1996 anglers caught 10 or more catfish at a time, many 12 inches or longer.
- About 90% of those surveyed or attending meetings supported or strongly supported. Department recommends adopting as written.

**Proposal 11: Add Quitobaquito Pupfish, Yaqui Topminnow and Gila Topminnow, and delete Sonoran Topminnow, from the protected species list.**
- Justification: clarification of current regulations. Public support in the survey was reported (figure garbled in scan), with little discussion at meetings. Department recommends adopting as written.

**Proposal 12: Create a catch-and-release trout fishing area on the Salt River below Saguaro Lake.**
- Public comments only (some suggested fly-fishing-only or guided operation). No Department recommendation is recorded in the source.

**General public concerns and conclusions.**
- Opposition to proposals seen as a "knee-jerk reaction" to fear of federal takeover of fish management. Some asked whether the Attorney General had reviewed legality, and suggested testing regulations on a smaller scale or adding a sunset clause.
- Concern that sportsmen's license and stamp money, not general fund money, funds these programs, and that the Department should represent anglers more strongly.
- Some argued the proposals are not covered by the [[Wildlife 2000 Plan]], which emphasizes catch and release and "don't catch your limit."
- Department employees were split: some felt the Department must cooperate with the Endangered Species Act; others felt the timing risked sport fishing in Arizona.
- The Department's stated position is that its legal duty is to protect, restore and maintain all fisheries, and that opinion and legal fact often differ.
