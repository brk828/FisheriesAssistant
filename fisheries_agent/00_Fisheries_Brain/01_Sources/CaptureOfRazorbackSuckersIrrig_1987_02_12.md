## Executive Summary

This 1987 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum documents an incidental but scientifically significant capture of **38 [[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) individuals from irrigation canals on the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes Reservation]], south of [[Parker, Arizona]]. The fish were discovered when canal water was drained for repairs beginning January 8, 1987, stranding the population over roughly two weeks of recovery effort by Tribal Wardens and USFWS personnel. Of the 38 fish recovered, 21 were frozen for further laboratory analysis and 32 were measured for length and weight. The authors note this collection likely represents the **largest documented recovery of subadult Razorback Suckers to date** (excluding larval collections), with speculation that these individuals originated from a May 21, 1986 stocking of 60,000 unmarked 2-inch fingerlings released from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] into the [[Parker Strip]] of the Colorado River. Because the stocked fish were unmarked, definitive confirmation of their hatchery origin was not possible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context:** The fish were recovered from **agricultural irrigation canals** fed by the lower [[Colorado River]], adjacent to the CRIT Reservation south of Parker, Arizona. This suggests that subadult Razorback Suckers were entrained from the river/reservoir system into diversion canals, likely via irrigation intake structures.
- **Stranding event:** The canals were drained for maintenance/repairs starting January 8, 1987, which stranded and concentrated the fish, allowing for an unusually complete and efficient recovery over approximately two weeks.
- **Population origin hypothesis:** The fish are hypothesized to derive from a documented stocking event — 60,000 two-inch [[Razorback Sucker]] fingerlings released from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] into the [[Parker Strip]] on May 21, 1986. Since the stocked fish were unmarked, this connection remains inferential, based on size-at-age consistency (~8 months post-stocking) rather than direct verification.
- **Conservation significance:** This event is notable within the broader context of [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]] management, as Razorback Sucker populations in the wild had been severely depleted, making recruitment and survival of stocked cohorts a key conservation question.

## Key Findings

- **38 Razorback Suckers** were recovered from irrigation canals on the CRIT Reservation over roughly two weeks (January 8–23, 1987) following canal draining for repairs.
- **21 of 38 fish** were frozen for subsequent laboratory analysis; **32 of 38** had length and weight data recorded in the field.
- Recovered fish were subadults, size range ~234–331 mm and ~141–455 g, consistent with growth from 2-inch fingerlings stocked roughly 8 months earlier.
- This collection was characterized by USFWS staff as potentially the **largest single collection of subadult Razorback Suckers** documented up to that time (excluding larval sampling efforts).
- Because stocked fish from the May 1986 [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] release were **unmarked**, the origin of these canal-stranded fish could not be conclusively confirmed, though circumstantial size/timing evidence strongly supports this connection.
- The event highlights an unintended but valuable **entrainment monitoring opportunity**, demonstrating that irrigation canal systems adjacent to the Colorado River can passively capture and concentrate stocked or wild juvenile/subadult razorback suckers, offering a potential future tool for population assessment.
- Distribution of the report to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[CRIT Fish and Game]], and CRIT tribal leadership indicates inter-agency coordination on management of this federally significant species.
