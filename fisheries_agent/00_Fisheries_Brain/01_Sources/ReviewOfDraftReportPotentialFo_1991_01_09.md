## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) interagency review memorandum (dated January 9, 1991) critiquing a draft Bureau of Reclamation report on the **potential for non-native fish transfer from Central Arizona Project (CAP) canal waters into the Gila River system**. Rather than presenting original data, the memo functions as a technical peer-review, identifying gaps in the draft's treatment of hydrology, dam operations, canal connectivity, and species occurrence records. Key concerns include the lack of defined "high" and "low" flow thresholds, insufficient documentation of [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]] operations as a fish passage barrier, omission of recently documented non-native species in [[Aravaipa Creek]], and unclear inter-canal connectivity pathways that could facilitate the spread of non-native fishes into native fish refugia within the [[Gila River]] drainage.

## Background & Description

No morphological data are presented in this source. The document is a management/policy review memo, not a taxonomic or morphometric study. Species are referenced only by common and scientific name for the purpose of distributional and risk-assessment critique:

- **[[Gambusia affinis]]** (western mosquitofish) — observed in abundance in a pool immediately below [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]].
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Notropis lutrensis*) — newly documented in Aravaipa Creek (1990); notably absent from the draft report's species table.
- **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** (*Ictalurus natalis*) — previously reported from Aravaipa Creek.
- **[[Black Bullhead]]** (*Ictalurus melas*) — possibly documented for the first time in Aravaipa Creek during the same 1990 survey that recorded red shiner.
- **White bass x striped bass hybrids** (Moronidae) — cultured by Ak-Chin aquaculturists; disposition/end-use unclear per reviewer.
- **[[Tilapia]]** (Oreochromis sp.) — noted as potential beneficiary of additional habitat created during low-flow water delivery periods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo highlights several ecological and hydrological pathways relevant to non-native fish dispersal risk in the CAP canal–Gila River system:

- **Canal-to-river connectivity**: The [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] connects to the Gila River system, with an electric fish barrier at **China Wash** cited as the primary containment measure. The reviewer notes this barrier was an emergency "quick-fix," not a validated long-term solution, and flags the eventual likelihood of barrier failure as an unaddressed risk.
- **Picacho Reservoir** has a more direct hydrologic connection to Ashurst-Hayden Dam than accounted for in the draft, meaning fish present in the reservoir could have a "clear run" to the Gila River above the dam if the China Wash barrier is bypassed or fails.
- **[[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]]** is discussed as an incomplete barrier to fish passage. Field observations from November 1990 documented the river gate wide open with continuous flow both immediately below and visibly upstream of the dam, contradicting the draft's assumption that a flood event is required for fish to move above the dam. A large pool below the dam supported abundant *Gambusia affinis*.
- **Aravaipa Creek** and the **San Pedro River** are identified as sensitive native-fish habitats vulnerable to non-native species establishment; the reviewer stresses the need for updated species occurrence data (e.g., 1990 red shiner and black bullhead records) not reflected in the draft.
- **Olberg Siphon pond** was found dry during a November 1990 site visit, suggesting this off-channel habitat may not hold permanent water annually — relevant to assessing it as a viable non-native fish refuge/transfer point.
- **Gila River Indian Reservation sumps** are identified as additional understudied features requiring spatial context (map) relative to river and canal infrastructure.
- **Low-flow water delivery periods** may inadvertently create additional standing-water habitat favorable to tilapia establishment.
- **Coolidge Dam** release data and other gaging station records are cited as available but underutilized resources for quantifying "high" and "low" flow conditions relevant to fish movement probability.

## Key Findings

- The draft report's species list for Aravaipa Creek is incomplete: it omits **red shiner** (documented 1990) and likely omits **black bullhead**, a first-record species alongside red shiner in the same survey.
- Terms like "high flow" and "low flow" are used throughout the draft without quantitative definitions, undermining the rigor of probability-of-transfer assessments; cited flow/release data (Coolidge Dam, gaging stations) exist but were not incorporated.
- Ashurst-Hayden Dam is not a reliable barrier to upstream fish movement even absent flood conditions — continuous flow was observed passing the dam structure in November 1990, contradicting the draft's operating assumptions.
- The China Wash electric fish barrier on the Florence-Casa Grande Canal is a stopgap measure; its eventual failure is a plausible risk not addressed in the draft's risk analysis.
- Picacho Reservoir represents an underappreciated, more direct non-native fish transfer pathway to the Gila River (via Ashurst-Hayden) than the pathways emphasized in the draft.
- Off-channel and reservation-adjacent water features (Olberg Siphon pond, Gila River Indian Reservation sumps) require better spatial and hydrological characterization to assess their role as stepping-stone habitats for non-native fish.
- Management-relevant unresolved questions raised by FWS include: disposition plans for Ak-Chin striped bass x white bass hybrids, the minimum flow threshold below which white bass cannot be supported, and whether other irrigation districts (e.g., Hohokam Irrigation District) maintain additional connections to the Gila River near Ashurst-Hayden Dam.
- Internal inconsistency flagged in the draft regarding temperature tolerance data between two report sections (p. 43 vs. p. 44).
