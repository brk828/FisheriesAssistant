# Fishes of Great Smoky Mountains National Park

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #biodiversity-survey #species-inventory #national-park

## Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive inventory of 72 fish species documented in [[Great Smoky Mountains National Park]] as of November 1960, compiled by Robert E. Lennon of the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]]. The list represents a revision of earlier surveys, incorporating collections made between 1935 and 1958 by multiple researchers from federal and state agencies. The inventory spans 12 fish families, from lampreys to sculpins, and includes both native species and introduced game fish. The compilation acknowledges that the list is tentative due to incomplete watershed surveys, recent impoundment effects (particularly [[Fontana Lake]] in 1944 and [[Chilhowee Lake]] in 1957), and taxonomic complexities arising from intergradations between northern-southern and Atlantic-Mississippi Valley forms in this biogeographic transition zone.

## Background & Description

The fish inventory builds upon surveys conducted by:
- Robert Burrows, Jr. ([[U.S. Bureau of Fisheries]]) in 1935
- Dr. Willis King ([[National Park Service]]) in 1937-42
- Mr. Ancil D. Holloway ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) in 1945
- Dr. Lloyd L. Smith, Jr. ([[National Park Service]]) in 1947
- Dr. Robert E. Lennon ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) in 1953-58
- Mr. Eugene Whitney ([[Tennessee Game and Fish Commission]]) in 1957

Taxonomic identifications were contributed by Dr. Reeve M. Bailey and Mr. Carter R. Gilbert ([[University of Michigan]]) and Dr. C. Richard Robins ([[University of Miami]]).

The list is organized by families in phylogenetic order, with species listed alphabetically by genus and species within each family. For each species, the document provides scientific name, common name, origin (native or introduced), abundance status (abundant, common, rare), and distribution notes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Great Smoky Mountains National Park]] represents a biogeographically complex region where northern and southern fish forms intergrade, as do Atlantic Coast and Mississippi Valley forms. This transitional nature complicates taxonomic classification and species boundaries.

Major watersheds mentioned include:
- [[Abrams Creek]] (multiple species records)
- [[Little Tennessee River]] and tributaries
- [[Tuckasegee River]]
- [[Tennessee River]] headwaters
- [[Hazel Creek]]

The construction of [[Fontana Lake]] (1944) and [[Chilhowee Lake]] (1957) on the [[Little Tennessee River]] bordering the park has influenced fishery resources in tributary waters to an unknown extent, though specific impacts are not quantified in this document.

Game fish management is evident, with introduced species including [[Rainbow Trout]] (Salmo gairdneri, from western North America), [[Brown Trout]] (Salmo trutta, from Europe), and undesirable introductions like [[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio) and [[Goldfish]] (Carassius auratus) from the Orient.

## Key Findings

**Species Richness by Family:**
- Family [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows and carps): 26 species (largest family representation)
- Family [[Percidae]] (perches and darters): 14 species
- Family [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes): 9 species
- Family [[Catostomidae]] (suckers): 4 species
- Family [[Salmonidae]] (trouts): 3 species
- Family [[Ictaluridae]] (catfishes): 6 species
- Family [[Cottidae]] (sculpins): 2 species
- Families [[Petromyzonidae]], [[Lepisosteidae]], [[Clupeidae]], [[Cyprinodontidae]], and [[Sciaenidae]]: 1-3 species each

**Principal Game Fishes:**
1. [[Rainbow Trout]] (Salmo gairdneri): introduced from West, abundant, park-wide distribution; principal game fish
2. [[Brook Trout]] (Salvelinus fontinalis): native, common in headwaters park-wide; second most important game fish; also called speckled or mountain trout
3. [[Brown Trout]] (Salmo trutta): introduced from Europe, rare, restricted distribution

**Notable Native Species:**
- [[Warpaint Shiner]] (Notropis coccogenis): native, abundant, park-wide; described as "strikingly-colored fish, appropriately named"
- [[Stoneroller]] (Campostoma anomalum): native, abundant, park-wide; locally considered a food fish
- [[Mottled Sculpin]] (Cottus bairdi): native, abundant, park-wide
- [[Fantail Darter]] (Etheostoma flabellare): native, abundant, park-wide

**Endemic or Restricted Species:**
- [[Saffron Shiner]] (Notropis rubricroceus): rare, restricted to headwaters of [[Tennessee River]]
- [[Mirror Shiner]] (Notropis spectrunculus): rare, restricted to headwaters of [[Tennessee River]]
- [[Stargazing Minnow]] (Phenacobius uranops): rare, restricted to upper [[Tennessee River]] drainage
- [[Tuckasegee Darter]] (Etheostoma gutselli): rare, in tributaries to [[Tuckasegee River]]
- [[Swannanoa Darter]] (Etheostoma swannanoa): rare, restricted to upper tributaries of [[Tennessee River]]

**Abundance Categories:**
- Abundant (park-wide): 8 species
- Common: 23 species
- Rare: 41 species

**Undesirable Introduced Species:**
- [[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio): introduced from Orient, rare in tributaries to [[Little Tennessee River]]
- [[Goldfish]] (Carassius auratus): introduced from Orient, observed in [[Abrams Creek]], establishment status unknown

**Lamprey Diversity:**
Three lamprey species documented:
- [[Chestnut Lamprey]] (Ichthyomyzon castaneus): parasitic, freshwater species
- [[Mountain Brook Lamprey]] (Ichthyomyzon hubbsi): non-parasitic
- [[American Brook Lamprey]] (Lampetra lamottei): non-parasitic

**Survey Limitations Acknowledged:**
- Major watershed surveys inadequate
- Impoundment effects on tributary waters unknown
- Taxonomic difficulties due to intergradation of forms
- Future studies may add or subtract species from the list
