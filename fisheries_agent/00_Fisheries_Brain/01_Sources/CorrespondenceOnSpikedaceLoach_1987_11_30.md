## Executive Summary
This document is a set of administrative correspondence between the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD) and Arizona State University (ASU) regarding contract billing, quarterly progress reporting schedules, and funding status for a multi-year conservation research project on two imperiled southwestern cyprinid fishes, the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). The letters do not present primary biological, morphological, or ecological data; rather, they document the administrative and funding framework ("Phase One" and prospective "Phase Two") supporting field research conducted under [[W. L. Minckley]] and [[Dean Hendrickson]] at ASU, in cooperation with [[New Mexico Game and Fish Department]] and contingent on [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Section 6 funding tied to the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Key Findings
- The "Spikedace-Loach Minnow study" was a cooperative research contract between AGFD and ASU, with field/technical work led by [[W. L. Minckley]] and [[Dean Hendrickson]].
- **Billing status (as of Nov 30, 1987):** Six billings totaling **$16,258.35** had been received and submitted for payment; a shift to quarterly billing was requested to align with progress and final reporting deadlines.
- **Reporting schedule for Phase One:**
  - 30 June 1987 (met via a May progress report)
  - 30 September 1987
  - 31 December 1987
  - Final report due on or before **31 March 1988**, allowing time for review/revision.
- **Phase One extension:** New Mexico Game and Fish agreed to extend the Phase One end date to **31 March 1988**.
- **Phase Two contingency:** Continuation of the study (a projected 3–5 year total project) was contingent on additional **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Section 6** funding allocated to Arizona and New Mexico for FY 1988 (beginning October 1987), itself dependent on annual federal appropriations under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Project termination risk:** Absent additional federal funding, the project was scheduled for termination in spring 1988, though the AGFD Director expressed optimism about securing at least three years of continued funding.
- **Interagency coordination:** An annual coordination meeting between AGFD, ASU, and NMDGF was to be arranged, reflecting a formal interstate cooperative agreement structure for managing shared, at-risk fish populations.
- Correspondence copies were distributed to key personnel including [[Warner B. Poppleton]] (Funds Coordinator), [[David Propst]] (NMDGF), and [[Alice Permenter]] (Budget Analyst), indicating a formal multi-agency administrative oversight structure for the project.
