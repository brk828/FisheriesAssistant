## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary research paper but an official **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service survey instrument** (Bureau Form No. 3-1919, August 1960) designed to collect standardized data on outbreaks of **hepatoma** (liver tumor/neoplasia) in [[Rainbow Trout]] and other hatchery-reared trout across federal and state hatcheries. The survey's stated purpose was to centralize data on individual hepatoma outbreaks, catalog the diets fed to affected fish, and compare these to trout populations fed similar diets but not yet showing disease, in order to investigate a suspected **dietary or environmental etiology** for the condition. The form mandates histopathological examination of liver tissue from sampled fish and rapid, standardized reporting to Washington, D.C. headquarters by September 7, 1960, reflecting urgency around what was likely an emerging hatchery health crisis linked to prepared/formulated feeds.

## Background & Description
The form does not describe morphological characteristics of trout directly, but specifies **pathological and anatomical data points** to be collected per lot of fish:
- **Lot definition**: Fish of the same age originating from eggs from a single source.
- **Sample size**: Preferably 10 fish per lot for microscopic liver examination.
- **Biometric data requested**: Number of fish in lot, total weight, average size, and age (in months).
- **Hepatoma staging categories**: Classified as (1) obvious, (2) probable, or (3) not apparent — indicating a graded severity/certainty scale for tumor diagnosis.
- **Tissue examination protocol**: Fish are to be euthanized, body cavity slit, preserved in **5% formalin solution overnight**, dried, and wrapped in aluminum foil for shipment to a pathologist.
- **Additional pathology checks**: Assessment of whether hepatoma has spread to the flesh (musculature) and whether other internal organs are affected, alongside gross vs. microscopic examination distinction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The "ecological niche" here is entirely **artificial/captive** — describing hatchery conditions rather than a natural aquatic habitat:
- **Environment**: Fish and Wildlife Service and state-run [[Fish Hatchery|hatcheries]] across the United States.
- **Water parameters tracked**: Water temperature range over the total holding period (low–high °F) and average temperature over the preceding six months; water chemistry analysis (if available).
- **Environmental stressors considered**: Forest or field spraying (pesticide/herbicide chemical use) on the watershed supplying hatchery water, including chemical name, purpose, and timing — suggesting agrochemical runoff as a suspected co-factor in hepatoma etiology.
- **Diet as ecological/husbandry variable**: Full chronological feeding history required, including proprietary or custom feed mixes, trade names, and any medicants included — central to investigating a **nutritional/toxicological cause** of hepatoma (an early hypothesis pointing toward what would later be identified as **aflatoxin-contaminated feed components**, e.g., cottonseed meal, in historical trout hepatoma outbreaks).
- **Correlated mortality events**: Unusual mortality patterns were to be logged alongside each dietary period to identify temporal links between specific feeds and disease onset.

## Key Findings
- This document represents a **data-collection instrument**, not a results paper — its "findings" are procedural and structural rather than empirical.
- The survey establishes a **standardized national reporting protocol** for trout hepatoma across [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] facilities, with mandatory original + 3 copies distributed to Washington headquarters, the hatchery manager, the administrative office, and the pathologist.
- The design explicitly links **[[Hepatoma]]** investigation to **dietary composition**, treating diet as the primary suspected causal variable, alongside secondary consideration of water chemistry and watershed pesticide/herbicide exposure.
- Comparative sampling design: hatcheries were required to report not only on fish with confirmed/suspected hepatoma but also on **unaffected lots fed identical diets**, enabling case-control-style comparison — an early epidemiological approach to a fish disease outbreak.
- The urgency of the timeline (forms due before September 7, 1960) implies hepatoma was recognized as a **widespread and acute problem** in American trout hatcheries at the time, foreshadowing later research identifying aflatoxin-contaminated feed as a major cause of hepatocellular carcinoma in hatchery-reared [[Rainbow Trout]], a finding that became a landmark model in comparative carcinogenesis research.
- The protocol's emphasis on histopathology (10 fish/lot, formalin-fixed liver tissue) reflects the foundational role this survey played in what later became extensive research on **trout as a biomedical model organism for liver carcinogenesis**.
