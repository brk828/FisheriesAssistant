# Yampa River Fishes Study: Colorado Squawfish Spawning and Habitat, 1981

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1982 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) final report documents the 1981 Yampa River Fishes Study, conducted under a cooperative agreement with the [[National Park Service]] within [[Dinosaur National Monument]] (DNM). The study addressed potential conflicts between planned water projects and endangered fishes, and supplied information for Biological Opinions under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The work covered 124 mi of the [[Yampa River]] and 26 mi of the [[Green River]], combining a standardized sampling program, special investigations on young and adult [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Physical Habitat Simulation]] (PHABSIM) modeling.

The most significant result was the first documented spawning of Colorado squawfish in the lower Yampa River, between 1 and 9 July 1981. Long-distance spawning migrations were radiotracked, and the authors conclude that the Yampa may be key to the species' survival in the Green River system.

## Background & Description

The Colorado River Fishery Project (CRFP) began in 1979 as a mainstem study of the upper Colorado River basin and was expanded in 1981 to include the Yampa and White rivers. The Yampa, the largest tributary of the Green River, arises near Yampa, Colorado, flows about 200 mi (320 km), and has an average annual discharge of 1.2 million acre-feet. Its historic flows remain unaltered by dams, but the Juniper-Cross Mountain Project and the Cheyenne Water Diversion Project were planned.

The study area's fish community was sampled with electrofishing, seines, and trammel nets across eight Yampa strata and four Green River strata. Target species were the endangered Colorado squawfish, [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]], plus the [[Razorback Sucker]], which is not federally listed but is proposed for listing and on the Colorado state endangered list.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors classify upper Colorado basin streams into three zones after Joseph et al. (1977): cold, clear headwaters; intermediate warm, turbid reaches dominated by cyprinids and catostomids; and lower large-river reaches dominated by cyprinids and catostomids and strongly influenced by introduced species. The Yampa departs from this framework: its entire study area is affected by introduced species, and Stratum 5 (Cross Mountain Canyon) has zone-1 gradient and substrate. The Green River within DNM is colder than expected because of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], and it supports trout.

Runoff in 1981 was below normal because of low winter snowpack. The Yampa runoff period ran from 15 April to 26 June, and the Green runoff from 19 April to 18 June. Yampa strata were sampled in rotations I through V from April to October 1981.

The principal habitats were shorelines, eddies, backwaters, pools, riffles, runs, main channels, and side channels. Substrates ranged from silt and sand through gravel and rubble to boulder and bedrock.

## Key Findings

**Fish community**
- 24 species from 9 families were collected: 19 in the Green, 24 in the Yampa, and 11 in the Little Snake River.
- Nine species were native (six endemic to the Colorado basin) and 15 were exotic.
- Most abundant natives: bluehead and flannelmouth suckers, roundtail chub, and speckled dace.
- Most abundant exotics: red shiner, sand shiner, redside shiner, fathead minnow, and channel catfish.
- The Yampa's species count rose upstream, except at Cross Mountain Canyon (Stratum 5) and Juniper Canyon (Stratum 7). The increase came from exotic species.
- Stratum 5 held the fewest Yampa species, probably because of low habitat diversity.

**Colorado squawfish**
- 87 adult fish (more than 400 mm TL), including 11 recaptures, were collected in 1981. Of these, 64 were captured in the Yampa and 15 in the Green in the standardized sampling count reported in the Summary.
- No juveniles were collected. Seven young-of-year (50-60 mm) were taken in a backwater at Echo Park in September.
- Adults were present in all strata except Stratum 3 on the Yampa.
- Thirty-six of the 40 squawfish in lower Yampa Canyon (Stratum 1) were taken between 1 and 10 July during spawning studies. Outside that window, the species was rare.
- Radiotelemetry: seven Yampa fish and one Green fish moved into the lower Yampa in early July. Movements from 88 to 350 km were recorded for the eight tagged fish, and spawning migrations accounted for 96% of movement for six fish.
- Spawning habitat (lower Yampa, medium depth 3-7 ft, velocity 0-2.8 ft/s averaging 1.2 ft/s, cobble/rubble substrate averaging 7.5 cm) was identified. Adjacent deep pools were considered important.
- PHABSIM indicated flows of 800-2,000 cfs in the lower Yampa gave the highest spawning weighted usable area (WUA).
- Larval squawfish (9-13 mm) were collected only in the lower 12.5 mi (20 km) in 1981. The authors cite drift behavior to quiet backwater nurseries.
- Age analysis (Musker 1981) reported an average annual growth rate of 56.5 mm for Yampa fish.

**Humpback chub and bonytail chub**
- Humpback chub was extremely rare: six fish were identified as humpback or humpback-type, including one preserved specimen.
- The bonytail chub, once abundant at Echo Park (1964-66), was not collected in 1981 and appears absent from the study area.
- Taxonomic identification of Gila species is unreliable. Of 19 preserved specimens, 4 were identified as G. robusta, 5 as G. cypha, and 1 as G. elegans, with 9 others intermediate or unknown.

**Razorback sucker**
- Five adults were collected in 1981; only one came from standardized sampling.
- Four fish, including a ripe female, were taken in spawning condition near the mouth of the Yampa in May.
- No young or juveniles were found, and only 92 individuals were collected in three years of Green River sampling below Split Mountain.

**Recommendations**
- Do not fragment the Yampa River before squawfish life-history interactions are understood, and develop a basin-wide fishery management plan.
- Research the ability of migratory cyprinids to use fishways.
- Verify that squawfish return to the same spawning areas.
- Continue DNM follow-up data analysis, which was incomplete at report time.
- Flows of 800-2,000 cfs are estimated to give the highest spawning WUA in the lower Yampa.

**PHABSIM (Appendix A)**
- Eight reaches on the Yampa and White rivers were modeled, five on the Yampa and three on the White.
- The Cleopatra's Couch site on the Yampa produced the highest spawning WUA of any Yampa or White site.
- Adult habitat preference curves were built from 200 captures in the Green River only and are considered the least reliable.
