# Lake Havasu Fish Management Report 1984-1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #Moronidae

## Executive Summary
This Arizona statewide fisheries report (Federal Aid Project F-7-R-30, 1988; author Wm. Bradford Jacobson, Regional Fisheries Specialist) summarizes fish population surveys, creel census and habitat work on [[Lake Havasu]] from 1984 to 1987. Electrofishing, trammel netting and creel interviews showed a three-species sport fishery of [[Striped Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]]. The largemouth bass population improved in size structure and the other two fisheries looked stable. The department began a [[Christmas Tree Habitat]] project in 1987. The report ends with six recommendations, including continued monitoring of the 13-inch size limit.

## Background & Description
- [[Parker Dam]] formed Lake Havasu in 1938. The concrete-arch dam is 320 ft high but extends only 85 ft above the riverbed. It lies on the [[Colorado River]] between Parker and Lake Havasu City, Arizona.
- The lake runs about 25 miles upstream of the dam (river mile 192.25) and borders California on one side and Arizona on the other.
- The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] built the dam with funds advanced by the [[Metropolitan Water District]]. Its main purpose is diverting water to the southern California coast. It has four 30,000 kW generators (120,000 kW total).
- The [[Central Arizona Project]] intake sits on the Bill Williams River arm. It diverts Colorado River water to the Phoenix and Tucson areas.
- Maximum surface area is about 20,390 acres at elevation 450 ft. Storage figures in the OCR text are partly garbled (active storage about 180,000 acre-feet; available storage 619,400 acre-feet).
- Vegetation consists of submerged beds of *Najas marina*, *Potamogeton pectinatus*, *Typha* and *Scirpus*. Terrestrial vegetation was removed because it was a hazard to water skiers.
- Eleven species are generally collected in annual surveys. These include largemouth bass, striped bass, channel catfish, bluegill, redear and green sunfish, black crappie, flathead catfish, yellow bullhead, carp and goldfish. Also listed are threadfin shad, rainbow trout, red shiner, fathead minnow, mosquitofish and white sturgeon.
- The only native species collected is the [[Razorback Sucker]]. [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] historically inhabited the river.
- [[Threadfin Shad]] has become the primary forage species. Introductions of [[White Sturgeon]] were unsuccessful.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Survey schedule:**
  - Lake Havasu was placed on an annual survey schedule in 1983-84.
  - Electrofishing was done in fall (November in 1984; October in 1986 and 1987). The 1985 survey was cancelled because of weather.
  - Trammel netting was done in July-August (1984 and 1986).
  - Vertical gillnetting for striped bass was done in winter, because spring-summer boat traffic is too heavy to risk setting nets.
- **Creel census** ran in 1984, 1985 and 1987. Months were missed (six in 1984, three in 1985, three in 1987). The procedure was changed in July 1987, which may account for some late-1987 differences rather than a real fishery change.
- Striped bass monitoring remains problematic, and no effective survey method exists for channel catfish.
- Heavy boat traffic and the lake's recreational use shape both sampling and habitat work.

## Key Findings
**Electrofishing**
- Catch per unit effort (CPUE) was highest in 1986 at 117.6 fish per 15 min of pedal time. The OCR text suggests 106.7 for 1984 and 42.7 for 1987.
- In 1984 largemouth bass made up 39% of the sample, followed by bluegill (26%) and carp (14%).
- Bluegill were most abundant in 1986 (47%) and 1987 (41%).
- By weight, carp dominated, with largemouth bass second.
- Largemouth bass proportional stock density (PSD) rose to 54.9% in 1987. Earlier values in the table range from 21.7% to 46.1%.
- Relative stock density (RSD) for bass over 379 mm rose to 25.5% in 1987, from 16.8% in 1986.
- Relative condition factor (K) for bass was below the five-year weighted mean in 1987 for all size groups:

| Size group | 1987 K | Five-year mean K |
|---|---|---|
| <200 mm | 0.99 | 1.10 |
| 200-299 mm | 1.21 | 1.24 |
| 300-379 mm | 1.29 | 1.31 |
| >379 mm | 1.35 | 1.38 |

- Bass in 1987 numbered 58, 23, 15 and 13 across the four size groups.

**Trammel netting**
- 192 fish were caught in 1984 and 89 fish (seven species) in 1986. The 1986 catch rate was 14.2 fish per 1000 ft² of net per 12-hr set.
- By number, carp made up 58% (1984) and 43% (1986). Channel catfish made up 19% and 26%. Striped bass were 12% in 1984 and largemouth bass 21% in 1986.
- By weight, carp made up 78% (1984) and 67% (1986). Channel catfish made up 14% in 1986. Largemouth bass were 3% (1984) and 9% (1986). Striped bass were 7% (1984) and 3% (1986).

**Creel census**
- 1,693 anglers were interviewed (350 in 1984, 718 in 1985, 625 in 1987).
- Angler success rose slightly each year. Peak months were September 1984 (39%), April 1985 (31%) and October 1987 (51%).
- Annual catch rate for fish kept was 0.11 fish/hr in 1984, 0.09 in 1985 and 0.20 in 1987. Monthly peaks reached 0.4 fish/hr in July and November 1987.
- Striped bass were 47% of the catch in 1984 and 64% in 1987. Channel catfish led in 1985 at 38%. Largemouth bass ranged from 15% (1984) to 28% (1985).
- Striped bass was the preferred species in all years (48%, 44% and 55%). "No preference" was second, falling from 27% to 20%.
- Mean length of kept fish was relatively stable. Striped bass ranged from about 38 cm in 1985 to 44 cm. Largemouth bass peaked at 41 cm in 1984 and channel catfish at 45 cm in 1987.

**Habitat management**
- A 1987 pilot placed 510 Christmas trees in bundles of 10 in Steamboat Cove and Black Rock Cove.
- In 1988 the project expanded to 1,500 trees in four coves: Steamboat (350), Black Rock (510), Shady Gulch (400) and Blue Dock (240).
- Partners were the Lake Havasu Bass Club and local businesses.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- The channel catfish and striped bass fisheries appear stable; the largemouth bass fishery appears to be improving.
- Recommendations:
  1. Continue bass population surveys to monitor the effects of the 13-inch size limit.
  2. Develop an effective survey method for channel catfish.
  3. Continue artificial habitat placement in the four approved coves and evaluate further areas.
  4. Keep working to establish an effective striped bass monitoring method.
  5. Evaluate current regulations for possible changes.
  6. Develop a method to evaluate the effectiveness of habitat structures.
