# Mining Activities and Water Quality Report - Globe-Miami Area

#water-quality #mining-impacts #environmental-assessment #geochemistry #groundwater-contamination

## Executive Summary

This report presents findings from the Globe-Miami National Prototype Groundwater Study, a collaborative investigation funded by the [[U.S. Environmental Protection Agency]] ($450,000), three active mining companies ($138,045), and the [[U.S. Bureau of Mines]] ($125,100). The study examined water quality impacts from copper mining operations in the [[Pinal Creek Basin]] and [[Pinto Creek Basin]] of eastern Arizona. The investigation utilized extensive surface and groundwater sampling, hydrogeologic analysis, and geochemical modeling to characterize contamination sources, transport mechanisms, and natural attenuation processes. Key findings reveal significant groundwater degradation in the Pinal Creek Basin over 40+ years, with total dissolved solids at [[Pringle Well Field]] increasing from 800 mg/L (1942) to 2,500-2,900 mg/L (1982-1983). Multiple contamination plumes were identified, originating from process solution losses, heap-leach operations, and historical tailings facilities. Natural geochemical removal mechanisms (primarily calcite dissolution and metal hydroxide precipitation) provide finite but measurable contaminant retardation.

## Background & Description

The study area encompasses the Globe-Miami copper mining district in eastern Arizona, focusing on the [[Pinal Creek]] and [[Pinto Creek]] drainage basins. The region has experienced intensive copper mining and milling operations since the early 1900s. The hydrogeologic framework consists of four principal aquifer systems: (1) fractured Precambrian rocks and Tertiary volcanic rocks with limited secondary permeability, (2) Paleozoic limestone aquifers providing high-quality municipal and industrial water supply, (3) Tertiary [[Gila Conglomerate]] serving as a major basin-fill aquifer, and (4) Holocene stream alluvium along major washes. These aquifers are separated by structural features including the [[Miami Fault]] and residual clay barriers. The investigation employed standardized sampling protocols (documented in METF 3) and established monitoring well "nests" at nine locations to characterize vertical and horizontal water quality gradients. Active mining operations during the study period included [[Cities Service Company]], [[Inspiration Consolidated Copper Company]], and [[Ranchers Exploration and Development Corporation]]. Water quality parameters analyzed included pH, sulfate, total dissolved solids, and dissolved metals (copper, iron, aluminum, zinc, manganese).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The aquatic environment of the Pinal and Pinto Creek systems has been fundamentally altered by mining activities. [[Pinal Creek]] exhibits intermittent surface flow, with water sinking into alluvial aquifers in the Miami Wash area and re-emerging as perennial flow near the [[Pringle Well Field]] before discharging to the [[Salt River]]. [[Pinto Creek]] maintains better water quality overall, with periodic violations of Arizona Stream Standards for copper at the Old Highway 60 Bridge attributed to surface runoff from the abandoned [[Bellview-Gibson Mine]] site. The creek exhibits losing and gaining reaches, with subsurface flow through alluvium near [[Horrell Ranch]] and Henderson Ford before discharge to [[Roosevelt Lake]]. Geochemical processes controlling contaminant mobility include: calcite dissolution (primary neutralization mechanism), metal hydroxide precipitation (sequential removal of Fe, Al, Cu, Zn, Mn based on pH), gypsum precipitation (sulfate control), cation exchange, and sorption onto mineral surfaces. Three conceptual geochemical zones characterize acidic plumes: (1) acid/core zone with pH <3 and extremely high metal concentrations matching source water, (2) neutralizing zone with active calcite dissolution and metal hydroxide precipitation, and (3) neutralized zone with elevated total dissolved solids (calcium sulfate saturation) but near-neutral pH and low metal concentrations.

## Key Findings

**General Conclusions:**

1. **Groundwater Degradation in Pinal Creek Basin**: Total dissolved solids at Pringle Well Field increased threefold over 40 years (800 mg/L in 1942 to 2,500-2,900 mg/L in 1982-1983), reflecting long-term seepage of acidic mining and milling process solutions. Present water is predominantly calcium sulfate type, indicating sulfuric acid leach solution impacts.

2. **Inactive Mine Impacts**: Overland runoff from old, inactive facilities causes localized surface and groundwater pollution. The [[Bellview-Gibson]] area represents the most significant impact, with surface runoff from past heap-leach attempts causing Arizona Water Quality Standard violations in [[Pinto Creek]]. The [[Old Dominion]] tailings pile produces extremely acidic runoff with high dissolved toxic metals, though contamination is neutralized over short distances during stream flow periods.

3. **Natural Geochemical Attenuation**: Natural removal mechanisms (primarily carbonate dissolution and metal hydroxide precipitation) have retarded toxic pollutant movement, but these mechanisms are finite and can be overwhelmed by massive acidic inputs. Pinal Creek alluvium exhibits higher carbonate content than Miami Wash alluvium, resulting in greater contaminant retardation below the Miami Wash confluence.

4. **Aquifer Vulnerability**: The [[Gila Conglomerate]] and Paleozoic limestone aquifers are generally protected by overlying residual clay barriers and upward hydraulic gradients, but can be contaminated when Gila aquifer head decreases. Pumped sampling demonstrated induced recharge of contaminated water from alluvium into underlying Gila Conglomerate. Water quality changes between sampling rounds showed both contamination (Burch No. 2) and improvement (Sewer Nest Deep Well) resulting from shifts in industrial pumpage.

**Site-Specific Conclusions:**

5. **Ranchers Bluebird Facility**: Process solution losses cause surface water degradation in [[Bloody Tanks Wash]] and a contamination plume in wash alluvium. Geochemical processes effectively reduce plume extent, with evidence of decay before reaching western Miami. Reduction of Bluebird operations prior to Round 3 sampling resulted in marked water quality improvements at Ranchers No. 3 Well.

6. **Miami Town Area**: Additional contaminant enters Bloody Tanks Wash alluvium from the north within Miami town limits. This plume is slightly larger than the Bluebird plume and is partially removed by geochemical mechanisms. Possible sources include [[Inspiration Consolidated Copper Company]] acidic heap-leach solutions moving down [[Live Oak Gulch]] alluvium and leaching of metal salts from capillary crusts on old [[Miami Copper Company]] tailings.

7. **Webster Gulch Contamination**: A severe contamination plume results from subsurface flow into the former [[Webster Gulch]] alluvial channel. Water is highly acidic with extremely high ferrous sulfate complex and dissolved toxic metals; copper concentrations exceed 300 mg/L in Gila Conglomerate groundwater underlying tailings deposits. Possible sources include [[Webster Lake]], minor ponds near Inspiration mill, and metal leaching from past tailings by infiltrating acidic waters.

8. **Pinal Creek Plume Advancement**: A contamination plume is advancing downgradient along Pinal Creek alluvium as geochemical reactions remove carbonate material. Pinal Creek alluvium has greater carbonate content than Miami Wash alluvium, providing greater contaminant retardation, but this capacity is finite. Miami Wash plumes are localized but have largely exhausted carbonate material where present.

9. **Alkaline Tailings Seepage**: Present-day tailings ponds are alkaline due to lime addition during milling. Seepage from these ponds enhances natural removal mechanisms; the Miami Wash plume would be more extensive without such seepage.

10. **Kaiser Pumping Plant Hydrology**: Present groundwater pumping from the [[Kaiser Pumping Plant]] area along Miami Wash appears slightly less than existing seepage inflow rates, evidenced by greatly flattened groundwater gradients and diminished velocities. Cessation of pumping would lead to elevated water levels, possible surfacing of acidic water, and accelerated contaminant spread.

11. **Pinto Creek Basin Status**: Present-day mining activities in Pinto Creek Basin have had minimal groundwater and surface water quality impacts. Existing tailings ponds contain alkaline water near gypsum saturation and are subject to seepage collection and pumpback. Greatest future impact potential is associated with waste-rock and dump-leach runoff and seepage. Current stream standard violations result largely from past dump-leach activities at Bellview-Gibson.

12. **Inactive Mine Drainage**: Special Round 3 sampling of inactive adits and natural mineralization areas (including Copper Springs Adits, Big Johnnie Mine area, Stock Tank Adit, Cactus and Carlotta sites) found no evidence that drainage from old mines constitutes a significant pollution source. Such drainage and natural mineralized areas have minimal adverse impacts on basin groundwater.

**Temporal Variations**: Water quality monitoring revealed temporal trends related to precipitation recharge events and changes in industrial pumping rates. The Sewer Plant Nest Gila well showed contamination during Rounds 1-2 but significant improvement in Round 3, attributed to reduced withdrawal rates and changed flow patterns. Burch No. 2 well (Paleozoic limestone aquifer) showed contamination in Round 3 as static water levels recovered to within 25 feet of adjacent Miami Wash alluvium elevation, demonstrating vulnerability of deeper aquifers to contamination when hydraulic gradients reverse.
