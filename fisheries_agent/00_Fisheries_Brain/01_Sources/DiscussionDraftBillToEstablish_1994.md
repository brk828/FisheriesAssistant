## Executive Summary

This document is not a species-specific scientific study but the legislative text (and accompanying committee summary) of the **National Fish Hatchery System Act**, a U.S. federal bill establishing and consolidating a **National Fish Hatchery System** under the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] within the Department of the Interior. The Act's stated purpose is to organize federal fish production and support activities into a unified system that serves six defined objectives: mitigation of federal-project impacts on fisheries, restoration of depleted fish stocks, stocking of [[National Wildlife Refuge System]] and [[National Park System]] waters, stocking of State/Tribal-managed waters, fisheries research and development, and implementation of [[Endangered Species Act|Endangered Species]] recovery plans. The bill also establishes a cost-reimbursement framework requiring beneficiaries (water/power users, States, Indian tribes, private entities) to progressively assume 100% of production costs, and phases out fish production for private/commercial use by the fourth anniversary of the Act's effective date. While it does not contain morphological or ecological species data, it is foundational to understanding the **institutional and regulatory context of fish stocking, restoration, and mitigation hatcheries** in North American freshwater and anadromous fisheries management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although not a biological study, the Act defines the **functional ecological roles that hatchery-produced fish are intended to fill**, effectively describing anthropogenically managed niches:

- **Mitigation stocking**: Compensating for fishery resource losses caused by [[Federal Project|Federal Projects]] (dams, diversions, channel modifications) that impound, divert, or otherwise alter stream and water body habitats.
- **Restoration stocking**: Rebuilding depleted stocks or populations of recreational, commercial, or ecological value, guided by mandatory **restoration plans** that must address:
  - Fishery resource and habitat description
  - Causes of stock depletion
  - Target population levels and timelines
  - Production, harvest, and escapement goals
  - Habitat protection measures
  - Treaty obligations (international and Indian tribal)
- **Refuge and park stocking**: Populating waters within the [[National Wildlife Refuge System]] and [[National Park System]].
- **State/Tribal jurisdictional stocking**: Supporting fisheries management goals of state governments and [[Indian Tribe|Indian Tribes]] outside of mitigation, restoration, or refuge/park contexts.
- **Research and development**: Supporting fisheries science programs.
- **Endangered species recovery**: Direct implementation of species recovery plans under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

The Act emphasizes that, where feasible, hatcheries should produce **stocks native to the receiving waters**, and that production must be coordinated with habitat improvement/protection programs and harvest management goals — reflecting an ecosystem-based rather than purely production-based management philosophy.

## Key Findings

- **System Structure**: Establishes the National Fish Hatchery System as a formal list of designated installations, with mechanisms for adding facilities (via Congressional authorization, federal property transfer, direct federal operation, or contract-based production) and removing them (only by Congressional authorization).
- **Six Statutory Objectives** (Sec. 4): mitigation, restoration, refuge/park stocking, state/tribal stocking, research & development, and endangered species recovery.
- **Cost Accounting Framework** (Sec. 2(6)): Defines "production cost" comprehensively — construction, maintenance, operating, research, and distribution costs — prorated based on the proportion of an installation's output devoted to System objectives.
- **Reimbursement Policy** (Sec. 5(d)): Establishes an **incremental cost-recovery schedule**:
  - Mitigation costs reimbursed by water/power project beneficiaries, scaling to 100% by calendar year 1989 (with federal agencies covering any shortfall).
  - Stocking costs for State/Tribal waters similarly scaled to 100% reimbursement by 1989.
  - Private-use production costs scaled to 100% reimbursement by 1988, with a **hard phase-out of private/commercial fish production** no later than the fourth anniversary of the Act's effective date.
- **Restoration Plan Requirements** (Sec. 5(c)): Fisheries subject to the [[Magnuson Fishery Conservation and Management Act]] require restoration plans co-approved by the Secretary of the Interior and Secretary of Commerce; all other fisheries require Secretarial approval alone. Plans must be developed in consultation with federal agencies, state fishery agencies, regional fishery management councils, interstate/international fishery commissions, and Indian tribes.
- **Periodic System Review** (Sec. 7): Mandates triennial reviews (beginning October 1, 1986) of each installation's fish production capacity relative to System objectives and funding source (appropriations vs. reimbursement).
- **Effective Date**: October 1, 1985, per Section 8.
- **Legislative Context**: The bill emerged from a September 1984 House Subcommittee on Fisheries and Wildlife Conservation and the Environment hearing, with the enclosed text representing an April 19, 1985 staff discussion draft — indicating this is a **pre-enactment policy proposal** rather than a finalized statute, despite the "1994" title reference suggesting later reintroduction or continued deliberation.
