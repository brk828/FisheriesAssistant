## Executive Summary
This 1959 report documents the first known occurrences of two parasitic copepod species in eastern South Dakota freshwater fish populations. [[Lernaea cyprinacea]] ("anchor worm"), a well-known ectoparasite embedded in host flesh, was identified in [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) and [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*) collected from the Big Sioux River. Separately, [[Achtheres ambloplitis]], a gill-inhabiting copepod, was recorded for the first time on [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*) from two South Dakota lakes. Both findings represent range extensions and new host records for these parasitic Eucopepoda, contributing to the broader understanding of copepod parasite distribution and taxonomy across North America.

## Background & Description
- **Lernaea cyprinacea** ("anchor worm"): A parasitic copepod exhibiting an anchor-like body morphology that embeds into host musculature (flesh); taxonomically confused historically with synonyms *L. carassii* Tidd, 1933, and *L. elegans* Leigh-Sharpe, 1927 — both now considered synonymous with *L. cyprinacea* Linnaeus, 1758, per [[Uzmann And Rayner 1958]].
- **Achtheres ambloplitis**: A gill-dwelling parasitic copepod found attached to gill arches and gill rakers of host fish. Morphologically very similar to *Achtheres pimelodi*, a related species previously recorded from bullheads and catfish. Taxonomic distinction between *A. ambloplitis* and *A. pimelodi* remains uncertain, with expert opinion (T. E. Bowman, U.S. National Museum) suggesting *A. ambloplitis* may eventually be synonymized as a smaller form of *A. pimelodi*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Host-parasite associations**:
  - *Lernaea cyprinacea* infests the flesh (subcutaneous musculature) of freshwater cyprinids and catostomids, specifically [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[White Sucker]] in the Big Sioux River.
  - *Achtheres ambloplitis* colonizes the gill arches and gill rakers of [[Black Bullhead]], a freshwater ictalurid, in Wall Lake and Lake Hendricks.
- **Habitat**: Both parasite-host systems occur in eastern South Dakota freshwater habitats — a lotic river system (Big Sioux River) and lentic lake systems (Wall Lake, Lake Hendricks).
- **Geographic distribution**: *L. cyprinacea* has a documented expanding range across western North America, with prior reports in Colorado ([[Cook 1954]]), California ([[Haderlie 1953]]), and Oregon/Washington ([[Uzmann And Rayner 1958]]). This South Dakota record represents further westward/eastward range documentation for the species within its known distribution.
- **Host specificity**: *A. ambloplitis* was previously known primarily from centrarchids (e.g., bass, per genus name "ambloplitis," likely referencing *Ambloplites*), making this the first documented case on ictalurid bullheads, suggesting broader host plasticity than previously recognized.

## Key Findings
- **First South Dakota record** of *Lernaea cyprinacea* infecting fathead minnows and white suckers in the Big Sioux River.
- **First report of *Achtheres ambloplitis*** parasitizing black bullheads (*Ictalurus melas*), previously undocumented in this host genus.
- Taxonomic identifications were confirmed by T. E. Bowman (U.S. National Museum), lending authority to species determinations.
- The paper highlights ongoing taxonomic ambiguity in the *Lernaea* genus, noting that *L. carassii* and *L. elegans* are likely junior synonyms of *L. cyprinacea*, based on [[Uzmann And Rayner 1958]].
- Similarly, taxonomic uncertainty is noted between *Achtheres ambloplitis* and *Achtheres pimelodi*, with expert opinion suggesting potential future synonymy pending further study.
- These findings collectively support a pattern of **range expansion** for *Lernaea cyprinacea* across western/central North American freshwater systems (Colorado → California → Oregon/Washington → South Dakota).
- This work adds two new parasite-host distribution records to South Dakota's aquatic parasitological literature, relevant to fisheries management and fish health monitoring in the Big Sioux River watershed and associated lakes.
