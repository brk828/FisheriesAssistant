# Determining Age of Atlantic Menhaden from Their Scales

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Fishery Bulletin (Volume 60, Bulletin 171, 1960) by [[June and Rothschild]] tests whether recognizable rings on the scales of [[Atlantic Menhaden]] (*Brevoortia tyrannus*) are reliable age marks. Scale samples from commercial and survey catches along the [[Atlantic Coast]] were measured, read independently, and checked against fish of known age held in a [[Rearing Pond]] for 14 months. The authors conclude that scale rings are formed once per year and are a valid basis for age determination, which underpins [[Stock Assessment]] of the [[Menhaden Fishery]].

## Background & Description

Knowledge of age is essential for understanding menhaden life history, year-class strength, growth rates, and fishing and natural mortality. Earlier attempts to age the species from scales, by [[Rush]] (1952), [[Westman and Nigrelli]] (1955), and [[McHugh, Oglesby and Pacheco]] (1959), were either limited in validation or were the only prior records of this approach.

Scales of *Brevoortia* differ from most herring-family scales: the posterior margin is serrate or pectinate in adults, whereas most herrings have smooth, rounded posterior margins. Scales are adherent, translucent, and thin. The anterior field is sculptured with ridges parallel to the anterior margin, and age rings appear as narrow bands where the ridges are discontinuous. Scales were taken from a median lateral band between the tip of the flexed pectoral fin and the dorsal fin insertion, where the largest, most symmetrical scales with the clearest rings occur.

Scales begin as cycloid scales at body lengths of 24 to 30 mm, and are fully formed at 30 to 43 mm during metamorphosis. Young-of-the-year scales in [[Indian River]] showed no ring markings through the first summer, so the first ring forms later in life.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling sites:** Purse-seine landings at [[Lewes, Delaware]], [[Wildwood, New Jersey]], [[Amagansett, New York]], [[Port Monmouth, New Jersey]], [[Beaufort, North Carolina]], [[Gloucester, Massachusetts]], and other ports from Maine to South Carolina. Supplementary material came from pound-net, gill-net, fyke-net, and beach-seine catches, [[Chesapeake Bay]] waters, and [[Estuarine Nursery Grounds]] in Indian River, Delaware.
- **Seasonal fishery:** The southern fishery (Beaufort to Fernandina Beach) starts about the first week of May and relies mainly on 1- and 2-ring fish. Chesapeake Bay catches are mostly 1- and 2-ring fish, middle Atlantic catches are mostly 2- and 3-ring fish, and waters north of Long Island begin in June with older fish.
- **Spawning:** Based on gonadal development, spawning occurs from late fall to late spring, so January 1 was adopted as the common birthday for age assignment.
- **Fish movement:** Fish size at a locality generally stayed constant or declined through summer, and fish were younger later in the season, suggesting movement or differential mortality.
- **Predation:** In the rearing experiment, [[Herring Gulls]] caused heavy mortality in December 1955 and January 1956.

## Key Findings

**Sample base and reading protocol**
- Scales were obtained from 13,510 juvenile and adult menhaden across gill-net, pound-net, fyke-net, purse-seine, and beach-seine catches.
- Random samples of 100 fish per day were used, with scales taken from every fifth fish; about 25 scales were removed per fish.
- Scales were measured at 40X to the nearest millimeter. Each sample was read independently at least twice, and disagreements were resolved jointly or the scale was discarded.
- Agreement between independent readings ranged from 90.2% to 95.9% across years (1952–1956). Most disagreements involved the first ring (51.3%) and the number and location of rings (39.5%). Disagreements were higher among older fish (chi-square = 50.26, P < 0.001).

**Ring validity**
- Monthly frequency distributions of scale measurements for the 1951 and 1952 ring classes showed one or more homologous, dominant modes.
- Scale length increased with each new ring, the distance between the last two modes decreased with age, and the distance from the last submarginal ring to the margin increased through the warm months, peaking in fall.
- Marginal growth was greatest in warm months. Ring formation occurred once per year, between March and May, and was completed by late July in older fish.
- Ring formation was earliest in southern waters and progressively later northward. Ring formation in younger fish appeared earlier than in older fish, though slower growth in older fish may delay visibility.

**Known-age validation**
- About 500 fish of the 1955 year class (74–93 mm) were transplanted into an outdoor pond at Beaufort on July 15 and 22, 1955. Only 64 fish remained when the pond was drained on September 27, 1956, after 14 months.
- Of these 64 fish, none showed more than one ring. The first ring appeared between March 18 and May, with all specimens completed by May 2, 1956, confirming that one ring forms per year.

**Discrepancies and biases**
- Modes for the same ring class shifted between months, attributed mainly to inadequate sampling, fish movement, and selective purse-seine catches. A downward shift in the first and second year's scale growth was observed.
- The 1952 ring class grew faster than the 1951 class (first-year mode 113 mm vs. 83 mm for 2-ring scales), which the authors took as evidence of consistent ring-class identity.
- Some distributions appeared bimodal, possibly reflecting subpopulations or differential spawning.

**Irregularities**
- Accessory (false) rings, regenerated central areas, rotated scales, erosion at ring locations, and radial fissures were identified. Scales with regenerated areas were excluded.

**Age conventions**
- Ring formation is protracted, so January 1 was adopted as the transition to the next age group. A fish with one ring and a wide marginal zone in February 1955 was assigned to age 2 and the 1952 year class.
- Year classes are named for the calendar year of spring spawning.
