# Gila Trout Recovery and Sport Fishery Management Proposal

#ichthyology #salmonidae #endangered-species #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]] to the [[Gila Trout Recovery Team]] proposes an alternative to downlisting [[Gila Trout]] from endangered to threatened status. The author argues that limited sport fishing can be permitted while maintaining endangered status through a special rule under Section 6 of the [[Endangered Species Act]], eliminating the need for premature downlisting. The proposal addresses recovery team concerns about local hostility in [[Catron County]] and [[Grant County]] while maintaining full legal protections for the species.

## Background & Description

[[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) is a [[Salmonidae]] species listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The [[Gila Trout Recovery Team]] met in early December 1994 and recommended downlisting the species from endangered to threatened status. Team members believed this action would reduce hostilities toward the species and facilitate recovery efforts, particularly by opening at least one stream ([[Mogollon Creek]]) to fishing to placate hostile residents and interest groups in southwestern [[New Mexico]].

The correspondence identifies key stakeholders including [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], [[Alchesay National Fish Hatchery]], [[New Mexico State University]], [[Gila National Forest]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[U.S. Forest Service]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document characterizes the [[Gila Trout]]'s current status as "precarious," indicating the species faces ongoing conservation challenges. Recovery efforts involve stream renovation and reintroduction programs in [[Gila National Forest]] and [[Tonto National Forest]]. The proposal acknowledges that "limited, well regulated fishing is not likely to be a threat to the recovery of the Gila trout," suggesting the species could sustain carefully managed harvest in certain stream reaches.

The correspondence references habitat management challenges, noting that the [[Forest Service]] faces management difficulties "every time new streams are proposed for renovation." This indicates ongoing habitat restoration and population establishment efforts across multiple stream systems in the species' range.

## Key Findings

**Legal Framework for Maintaining Endangered Status While Allowing Fishing:**

The correspondence identifies Section 6(g)(2) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] as providing an exception to Section 9(a)(1)(B) prohibitions against "take" of endangered species. This exception applies within states that are party to cooperative agreements with the Secretary under Section 6(c), which allows the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to enter cooperative agreements with states maintaining "adequate and active programs for the conservation of endangered species and threatened species."

The author notes that the supporting fund is called the "Sport Fishing Restoration Account" (Section 6(i)(1)), suggesting the program was designed with sport fishery restoration in mind.

**Proposed Alternative to Downlisting:**

The special rule mechanism would:
- Fully protect [[Gila Trout]] by maintaining endangered status
- Maintain trust of environmental groups concerned about premature downlisting
- Offer concessions to "wise use" groups by opening certain streams to fishing while temporarily closing others
- Reduce management complications for the [[Forest Service]] during stream renovation proposals
- Create new management opportunities, including potential auction of limited fishing permits to raise conservation funds

**Conservation Organization Position:**

The [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]] and [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] agree that:
- The downlisting proposal "is not based on sound biology, and is therefore premature"
- Team members are "nervous regarding the actual biological status of the Gila trout"
- The organization is "prepared to mount a legal challenge" if downlisting proceeds
- The special rule represents "an option that everyone can support"

The correspondence was distributed to southwestern fish, aquatic, and riparian habitat experts soliciting support letters to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Southwestern Regional Director encouraging adoption of the special rule alternative.
