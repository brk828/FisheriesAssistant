## Executive Summary
This document is a 1987 comment letter from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regarding a Federal Register proposal (52[165]:32143-32145) to designate an introduced population of the [[Colorado Squawfish]] as an "experimental, nonessential population" under the Endangered Species Act. While not a primary field study, the letter contains important management and population-genetics commentary relevant to the species' reintroduction biology, expected reproductive viability in the target habitat, and regulatory/ecological interactions with a National Wildlife Refuge. AGFD supports the proposal but raises concerns about whether the introduced population can become self-sustaining and outlines conditions under which the effort should or should not be credited toward species recovery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: The proposed experimental, nonessential population reach lies within the [[Colorado River Basin]], with approximately one-third of the designated reach overlapping the [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]].
- **Reproductive ecology concern**: AGFD explicitly states that, "In light of the habitat conditions, we do not believe that there will be any natural reproduction by the stocked squawfish," implying that current habitat conditions in the reach are considered unsuitable for natural spawning/recruitment of this large-river [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]].
- **Population management model**: Because natural reproduction is not expected, AGFD anticipates managing the introduced population as a **put-and-take fishery**, reliant entirely on hatchery-produced stock rather than a self-sustaining wild population.
- **Connectivity and refuge interaction**: There are no barriers preventing movement of stocked squawfish from the experimental reach onto the Cibola National Wildlife Refuge, creating **geographically separate population components** with differing legal protections (see Key Findings).

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory framing**: The introduced population was proposed under the "experimental, nonessential" designation, a mechanism under the Endangered Species Act intended to facilitate reintroduction efforts with reduced regulatory constraints.
- **Conditional support for recovery credit**: AGFD asserts this population should **not** count toward formal species recovery goals unless it is "thoroughly documented to be self-sustaining through natural reproduction" — a standard the agency doubts will be met given habitat limitations.
- **Genetic management requirements**: Because the population will depend on hatchery-produced brood stock, AGFD calls for:
  - Extensive attention to **population genetics** in brood stock management.
  - Regular infusion of genetic material from **newly captured wild individuals** to maintain genetic representativeness of natural gene pools.
  - Maintenance of a **pedigree** and **controlled breeding program** to avoid genetic drift or domestication effects in hatchery-reared squawfish.
- **Legal/jurisdictional complexity**: Squawfish stocked in the experimental reach that move onto the [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] would fall under **full Endangered Species Act protection** (including Section 7 consultation requirements), despite originating from an "experimental, nonessential" population elsewhere. This creates two legally distinct management units within a single introduced population.
- **Institutional collaboration**: AGFD designated internal points of contact (Fisheries Branch Supervisor and Endangered Species Coordinator) for coordinating with USFWS on implementation, indicating an active, ongoing management partnership rather than a one-time stocking event.
