# Thermal Environment of a Death Valley Pupfish and Photoperiod Effects on Green Sunfish Gonads

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This *Copeia* (1973, No. 2) ichthyological note contains two short papers. The main paper measures the thermal environment of a [[Death Valley Pupfish]] population in [[Cottonball Marsh]], [[Death Valley]], California. The authors find that the fish experience little heat stress under normal summer conditions, even though air temperatures reach record levels. The second, shorter note is a truncated abstract on how [[Photoperiod]] and temperature affect seasonal [[Gonadal Regression]] in [[Green Sunfish]].

## Background & Description

**Pupfish study.** The [[Death Valley Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon* sp.; epithet not legible in the scan) lives in small saline pools and channels on the floor of Death Valley, where few other forms of life exist. The species is of interest because of its high tolerance of salinity and temperature. Its habitat is shrinking, and the authors wanted to know whether selection had favored high heat tolerance.

**Study site.** Cottonball Marsh, the largest marsh in Death Valley, lies about 80 m below sea level on the west side of the salt pan. It is a barren salt crust with scattered clumps of [[Pickleweed]] and [[Saltgrass]] at the margins. A few permanent pools persist through the hot summer. Water is supplied by ground water, possibly from [[Mesquite Flat]]. Channels are generally less than 1 m wide. Pools are 2–10 cm deep and up to about 90 m across, and channels are 35–40 cm deep.

**Sunfish note.** The second paper examines how temperature and photoperiod affect the regression of gonads after spawning in *Lepomis cyanellus*. The text states that regression proceeds more rapidly at high temperatures than at low ones. The excerpt ends before the methods and results.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Air temperature:** Summer maxima commonly reach 48–50 °C. The record of 56.2 °C at [[Furnace Creek]] is the highest officially recorded in the United States. Long-term mean maxima are 46.1 °C in July and 45.0 °C in August. Furnace Creek also has the lowest average annual rainfall in the United States, 4.16 cm.
- **Water temperature:** The average summer water temperature falls from 29.0 °C in the channel at the source to 27.7 °C in the last shallow pool. Diurnal range increases from 2.1 °C in the channel to 14.8 °C in the last shallow pool. Shallow areas heat and cool faster because of greater surface exposure to sun and wind.
- **Subsurface water:** Water 30 cm below the surface stays close to the seasonal average air temperature. The annual average air temperature (23.9 °C) matches the water temperature at 1.2 m depth (Hunt et al., 1966).
- **Winter:** Water temperatures recorded in winter ranged from 9.0 °C to 21.5 °C.
- **Thermal tolerance benchmarks:** The upper lethal limit for prolonged exposure is about 43 °C, and the critical thermal maximum is comparable to other members of the genus. These values are lower than those in some other taxa, so the fish's heat tolerance is not exceptional.
- **Salinity:** The salinity at Cottonball Marsh was reported as 45% (as printed in the scan).

## Key Findings

1. **No severe heat stress under normal summer conditions.** Water temperatures in the sampled habitat stayed well below the lethal limit. Fish could move from shallow pools to cooler deep channels, and the authors saw no stress or mortality during the observation period.
2. **Estimated temperature in sections.** Points 1–5 have no thermal problem. Points 6–9 reach about 0.5 °C below the prolonged-exposure lethal limit. Points 10–11 may exceed the critical thermal maximum (the exact values are partly illegible in the scan).
3. **Thermal profile by location.** Average temperatures ranged from 28.6 to 28.9 °C in Points 4a–c (diurnal range 3.3–3.8 °C). The tunnel beneath the salt crust at Point 3 averaged 28.8 °C (diurnal range 3.9 °C).
4. **Behavior and stability.** Pupfish of all sizes were seen throughout the habitat with no preference for channels or pools. Ten fish held in a screen pen at 5 cm depth survived 48 hours in good condition. When water levels dropped 4–5 cm on August 4, about 200 fish moved into the channels. The fish remaining in isolated pools showed no stress until the water rose on August 5.
5. **Mapping mid-summer water temperatures.** On August 5 at 11:00 am, water temperatures across the marsh ranged from 26.4 to 31.9 °C.
6. **Projected extreme.** Assuming a linear relationship between air and water temperature, the authors estimate maximum water temperatures of about 32.2 °C at Points 1–5 on a day when air reached 56.2 °C. The estimate for deeper water is likely an overestimate.
7. **Other localities.** [[Salt Creek]] and the [[Amargosa River]] may expose pupfish to stressful temperatures because their shallow, long flow paths allow water to equilibrate with air temperature, and barriers such as distance and waterfalls limit movement to cooler water. The authors recommend these sites for future study.
8. **Conclusion.** The authors conclude that the pupfish is not living under severe thermal stress. Its notable adaptations are high salinity tolerance and the ability to withstand wide daily temperature fluctuations (up to 14.8 °C), rather than extreme heat tolerance.
9. **Sunfish note.** The abstract states that gonadal regression after spawning progresses faster at high temperatures than at low ones. Full results are not included in the excerpt.
