# Humpback Chub Research Data Request: Grand Canyon Fish Handling Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] office asks principal investigators of the [[Humpback Chub]] Fisheries Investigations Program to submit capture and handling data for an assessment of stress to the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Grand Canyon]]. Concern has been raised by the public and cooperating agencies that concentrated research effort may be harming the native fish. The office needs compiled data by a deadline of the last working day of September 1991. It also requests a detailed, standardized methods document for each project. No quantitative results are reported; the document is an administrative data call.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency memorandum (not a research paper), with a letterhead from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Upper Colorado Region, and the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] office in Flagstaff, Arizona.
- **Subject:** Potential impacts of concentrated research on the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the Grand Canyon, and a request for "Fish Handling Assessment" data.
- **Addressees:** Principal investigators of the Humpback Chub Fisheries Investigations Program.
- **Copy list:** About 15 recipients, including staff of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS, Pinetop office), consultants at BioWest, Inc., and a Bureau of Reclamation representative.
- **Context:** Many research groups work in the Grand Canyon. The office needs cumulative capture numbers for this target population to judge its total handling burden.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The [[Humpback Chub]] is described as a native, endangered fish of the Grand Canyon. Concentrated research (capture, marking, and handling) is the stressor under review.
- The memo gives no population estimates, capture counts, mortality figures, or water chemistry data. Any ecological interpretation must wait for the compiled submissions.
- Invasive techniques named as possible stressors include [[Radio Telemetry]], [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging]], and [[Hormonally Induced Ovulation]].

## Key Findings

The memo sets out four data requirements and one planning requirement:

1. **Capture data.** Total number of captured fish, sorted by size class, species, and type, with capture dates.
2. **Recapture data.** Information on [[Capture-Recapture]] events, by study reach where applicable.
3. **Invasive technique data.** Counts of fish handled with invasive methods ([[Radio Telemetry]], tagging, and [[Hormonally Induced Ovulation]]), reported by project.
4. **Mortality and collection data.** Numbers of fish that died, fish collected for [[Voucher Specimens]] or other purposes, the gear type used, and the disposition of specimens. Any field observations, necropsy or pathological findings, and suspected causes of death should also be reported.
5. **Methods document (planning).** A detailed, standardized methods summary for each project, covering gear types, handling procedures, and processing techniques for captured fish. This is required for coordination with resource agencies, as described under [[Fish Handling Assessment]].

**Deadline:** Submit analyses of capture data and handling protocols to the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] office by the last working day of September 1991.

**Intent:** The memo states that the request is not meant as a criticism of researchers' professionalism or competence. It says a shared, online database is planned to make future coordination easier.
