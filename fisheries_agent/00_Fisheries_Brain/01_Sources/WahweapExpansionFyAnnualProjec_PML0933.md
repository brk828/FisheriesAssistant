# Wahweap Pond Expansion: Colorado River Recovery Program FY 1999 Annual Project Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 1999 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project Number CAP-7c) covers expansion of captive-rearing facilities at the [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] on [[Lake Powell]]. The hatchery serves as a backup refuge for the [[Grand Valley Endangered Fish Facility]] and [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]], and as a production site for endangered fishes before their release into upper basin rivers. The project focuses on [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Construction of 12 additional growout ponds began in January 1999 and was nearly complete by fiscal year end. Construction of a residence was delayed by restrictive bidding. The report was prepared by the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR), with Wayne Gustaveson and Kevin Christopherson as principal investigators.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** Wahweap Expansion (CAP-7c), operated under the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] endangered fish recovery effort.
- **Principal Investigators:** Wayne Gustaveson (UDWR, Lake Powell–Wahweap Installation, Page, AZ) and Kevin Christopherson (UDWR, Vernal, UT).
- **Facility:** Eleven existing ponds at Wahweap, each approximately 0.4 acres (OCR-corrected), are available for propagation.
- **Species held:**
  - Bonytail are reared at Wahweap for a reintroduction stocking effort.
  - Razorback Sucker are held to develop a brood stock for future experimental augmentation of existing populations in the middle and lower [[Green River]].
- **Goal:** Operate a genetically sound propagation program for high-priority endangered fish species to meet recovery needs in the Upper Colorado River Basin, in accordance with the [[Annual Propagation Facilities Operation Plan]].
- **Objective:** Operate and maintain propagation facilities needed to hold, rear, or produce captive-reared endangered fishes.
- **Study schedule:** Initial year 1993; final year ongoing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project supports the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIPRAP) under the following actions:

- **General Recovery Program Support Action Plan, Item IV:** Manage genetic integrity and augment or restore populations.
- **Action IV.A.4 (Genetics Management):** Secure and manage genetic stocks in refugia.
  - IV.A.4.a: Razorback Sucker
  - IV.A.4.b: Bonytail

Captive propagation and genetic management are used as tools to maintain and augment wild populations of federally endangered Colorado River fishes. Land for the expansion was obtained after a transfer of [[Bureau Of Land Management]] lands under a land bill passed by Congress.

## Key Findings

- **Construction delay in FY 1998:** The 12 new growout ponds were delayed by the transfer of BLM lands. UDWR negotiated purchase of land for the additional space.
- **Planning completed:** The [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) process, design, and engineering were completed before construction began.
- **Pond construction:** Construction started in mid-January 1999 and was nearly complete at the end of FY 1999.
- **Residence construction:** Delayed because the contract was put out for restrictive bid. It was also held back by the delay in pond construction.
- **Pump procurement:** Utah received $50,000 in excess FY 1998 capital funds to buy a pump and hardware for Wahweap. The bid suggested a cost of approximately $100,000. At the November 3, 1999 Management Committee meeting, the committee asked Utah to hold costs down but approved up to an additional $58,000 for the necessary equipment.
- **Recommendation:** The report recommends carrying over FY 1999 funds into FY 2000.
- **Budget:** The budgeted amount is listed as approximately $250,000 (OCR-garbled figure). No funds were obligated, and $0.00 was spent on publication charges.
- **Status:** Ongoing. Data submission is not applicable.
- **Sign-off:** Signed December 1999.
