# The Fishes of the Poteau River, Oklahoma and Arkansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #cyprinidae #catostomidae #percidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

Cross and Moore (1952) present an annotated checklist of the fishes of the Poteau River, a tributary system of the [[Arkansas River]] flowing from Scott County, Arkansas, into Le Flore County, Oklahoma. The list draws on a August 1947 survey conducted during an unusually dry summer, on earlier published records from the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and on unpublished collections by the authors and colleagues. The paper documents roughly 90 numbered species or subspecies, plus several hybrid combinations, and corrects many historical misidentifications. It was prepared in cooperation with the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] and the [[Oklahoma Game and Fish Commission]] ahead of construction of the [[Wister Dam]].

## Background & Description

The Poteau rises from springs in the uplands of Scott County, Arkansas, and flows about 128 miles, draining 1,895 square miles with its tributaries. Its major tributaries include the [[Fourche Maline]], [[Black Fork]], [[Brazil Creek]] and [[James Fork]]. The river runs through an extensive flood plain with numerous oxbow lakes, particularly near Poteau, Oklahoma. Stream bottoms are predominantly mud, with shale outcrops and boulder-gravel reaches in places. The watershed is underlain by folded Pennsylvanian sandstones and shales that form mountainous ridges upstream and low rolling hills near the mouth.

Historical work on the fauna began with collections by [[Mollhausen]] and [[Shumard]] (1853), whose specimens were described by [[Charles Girard]] (1856, 1858, 1860). Later contributors included [[David Starr Jordan]] and [[Charles H. Gilbert]] (1886), [[Seth E. Meek]] (1893–1896), [[Henry W. Fowler]] (1904), [[Arthur I. Ortenburger]] and [[Carl L. Hubbs]] (1927, 1929). The authors note that Meek's tables contain errors that make them unreliable for other collectors' records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 1947 survey took place in August during a severe drought. Several tributaries and upper reaches, including the Fourche Maline and the Poteau above Wister, were intermittent, and collecting in riffles was difficult. Water temperatures were high, ranging from 18 °C in water released from the bottom of [[Lake Carlton]] to 35 °C in the Poteau near Hodgen. Most waters were slightly alkaline (pH 7.0–8.6), while two stations reached pH 6.8 because of decaying vegetation. Lake Carlton supported dense aquatic plants, including American lotus (*Nelumbo*), which produced a layer of decaying material 3 to 4 feet thick. Other plants included water willow (*Dianthera americana*), *Jussiaea*, *Najas* and filamentous algae.

The authors describe frequent floods and high turbidity as normal for the system. Habitat use was clearly partitioned among some species. For example, *Boleosoma nigrum* occurred in steeper, clearer tributaries, while *B. chlorosomum* occupied quiet main-stem waters, and the two were never taken together. The survey aimed to establish baseline fish populations before flood-control impoundment, which would raise the surface area of the Wister impoundment from 4,000 acres at conservation pool to 23,000 acres at flood-control pool.

## Key Findings

- **Survey scope:** The 1947 survey sampled 37 numbered stations in Latimer and Le Flore counties, Oklahoma, between August 18 and 28, 1947.
- **Species richness:** The annotated list includes approximately 90 numbered taxa spanning lampreys to sheepshead, plus hybrid combinations and an anomaly.
- **Most widespread species:** The green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*) was the only species taken at every survey station.
- **Abundance changes:** Several species that early collectors described as scarce, such as the bluntnose minnow (*Hyborhynchus notatus*) and central stoneroller (*Campostoma anomalum pullum*), are now among the most abundant fishes. Others, such as the harlequin darter (*Ulocentra histrio*) and southwestern sand darter (*Ammocrypta vivax*), have declined or are now rare.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** Of 35 specimens that Jordan and Gilbert (1886) recorded as *Notropis illecebrosus*, the authors identified 18 as *N. boops*, 5 as *N. volucellus buchanani*, 3 as *N. volucellus* subspecies and 1 as *H. storerianus*, and found no *N. illecebrosus*.
- **New records:** The paper reports first Oklahoma records for the blue catfish (*Ictalurus furcatus*) in the Arkansas River system and the tadpole madtom (*Schilbeodes mollis*), and a first state record for the Ohio shad (*Alosa ohiensis*).
- **Hybrids:** Several sunfish hybrids were recorded, including *L. humilis* × *L. macrochirus* (Sta. 5, 14, 25, 32, 34) and *L. macrochirus* × *L. megalotis* (Sta. 21).
- **Anomaly:** A specimen of *Notropis percobromus* from Station 28 lacked pelvic fins entirely.
- **Conservation context:** The authors predicted that impoundment would change the fish community, and they recommended baseline documentation before flooding. The paper also notes that the Wister impoundment was already filled when the paper was published.
