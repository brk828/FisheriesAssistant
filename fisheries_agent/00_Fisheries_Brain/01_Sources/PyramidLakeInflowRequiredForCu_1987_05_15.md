## Executive Summary

This 1987 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report by Chester C. Buchanan establishes minimum flow and lake-elevation requirements for two imperiled Lahontan Basin fishes: the Endangered [[Cui-ui]] and the Threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]], both obligate stream spawners dependent on the lower [[Truckee River]] for reproduction and on [[Pyramid Lake]] for adult rearing. Historic channelization of the Truckee River and diversions for the Newlands Project severed lake-river connectivity, degrading spawning/rearing habitat and forcing near-total reliance on hatchery supplementation (via [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]) and reservoir-augmented flows ([[Stampede Reservoir]]). The report defines two categories of minimum water requirements: (1) a minimum average annual lake inflow to sustain an elevation permitting spawner access to the river (via delta passage or the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]), and (2) a minimum instream flow regime covering attraction, spawning/incubation/rearing, and larval outmigration phases. A combined minimum flow regime (224,000 acre-feet/year) integrating both species' needs is proposed, alongside an "optimum" regime (507,000 acre-feet/year) representing more favorable conditions. The author stresses that even the minimum regime may not occur frequently enough for long-term species recovery given variable water availability, and recommends maintaining lake elevation ≥3,815 ft MSL to maximize the probability of achieving adequate flows.

## Background & Description

While this report is primarily hydrological/ecological rather than morphological, it references key life-history and physiological traits relevant to flow design:

- **[[Cui-ui]]** (*Chasmistes cujus*): A lake sucker (Catostomidae) endemic to Pyramid Lake; reproduces via broadcast spawning over gravel substrate in the lower Truckee River. Individual spawning fish require an estimated average of only **2 ft² of substrate** for spawning activity.
- **[[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Salmo clarki henshawi* — now *Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*): Adfluvial trout population historically stream-spawning; gravid females are physiologically sensitive to river water temperature during upstream migration.
- Both species exhibit **redd-based reproduction** (buried/incubating eggs in gravel), making them vulnerable to flow-driven scouring or dewatering of nests.
- Larval life stage ("swimup larvae") of cui-ui is markedly more thermally tolerant than the embryonic stage, permitting gradual flow reduction post-hatch without excess mortality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake–River Migratory System**: Both species depend on connectivity between the lacustrine environment of [[Pyramid Lake]] (adult rearing) and lotic spawning/incubation habitat in the lower [[Truckee River]] between Numana and Marble Bluff Dams — a classic adfluvial life history.
- **River Access Pathways**: Two access avenues exist — (1) natural **delta passage** across the lake-river interface (preferred: earlier, larger runs, less stressful, avoids fishway-related predation) and (2) the artificial **[[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]**, used only when lake elevation is too low for delta passage (operates between 3,784–3,810 ft MSL).
- **Environmental Cueing**: Photoperiod likely regulates the annual reproductive cycle; freshwater lens formation at the lake's south end and inflowing freshwater trigger pre-spawning aggregation; water temperature and river access modulate the timing/magnitude of the spawning run; daily upstream movement is driven primarily by water temperature.
- **Predation Pressure**: Shallow water over the delta or in-river increases vulnerability of migrating cui-ui to **white pelicans and cormorants**, reinforcing the need for adequate passage depth.
- **Thermal Niche Partitioning**:
  - Cui-ui egg/embryo incubation critical upper limit: **63°F**; survival declines precipitously above this threshold.
  - LCT egg incubation optimum: **50–56°F**; fry preferred range 55–60°F (tolerate to 70°F); adults prefer 55–68°F (survive brief exposure to 80°F).
- **Habitat Degradation Context**: Anthropogenic channelization and Newlands Project diversions have structurally and thermally degraded lower river habitat, particularly disadvantaging LCT, whose full reproductive flow needs (>4,000 cfs for safe incubation temperatures) are rarely achievable under current water supply conditions — motivating a proposed fish ladder over **Derby Dam** to access superior upstream habitat.

## Key Findings

### Lake Elevation / Inflow Requirements
- Minimum lake elevation for **delta passage**: **3,810–3,815 ft MSL** (at least 3 ft of water depth over delta needed for unrestricted passage/predation avoidance).
- Minimum average annual inflow to maintain this elevation: **406,000–413,000 acre-feet/year**.
- Below 3,810 ft, fish must use the **Pyramid Lake Fishway** (operable 3,784–3,810 ft MSL): full discharge of 50 cfs between 3,784–3,800 ft; reduced to 15–35 cfs above 3,800 ft.

### Cui-ui Instream Flow Requirements
- **Attraction flow (Jan–Apr)**: minimum 52,000 acre-feet (delta passage scenario) to as much as 87,000–176,000 acre-feet (fishway-dependent scenario) needed to draw substantial spawning runs (see Table 1 run-size data, 1978–1987).
- **Spawning/Incubation flow (May–June)**: 1,000 cfs maintains water temperature ≤63°F under normal air temperatures; up to 2,400 cfs (May) and 4,500 cfs (June) needed under warm air conditions.
- Maximum spawning habitat area occurs at **750 cfs** (8,274 ft²/1,000 linear ft); flows ≥3,000 ft²/1,000 linear ft substrate area deemed sufficient for runs as large as 36,000 fish.
- **Larval outmigration (July)**: gradual flow reduction (100 cfs/day) required once ≥75% of larvae have emerged, to allow thermal acclimation.
- **Minimum instream regime total**: **197,000 acre-feet/year** (Table 2); alternate (87,000 acre-foot attraction) version totals **232,000 acre-feet/year** (Table 3).
- **Optimum instream regime total**: **507,000 acre-feet/year** (Table 4), using higher attraction (175,000 acre-feet) and much larger May–June flows (2,500 cfs), yielding cooler incubation temperatures (60°F in May, 65.5°F in June).

### Lahontan Cutthroat Trout Flow Requirements
- Passage flow: **200 cfs** (delta reach) or **50 cfs** (fishway operation).
- Egg incubation temperature threshold (56°F) requires **900 cfs in April**; **>4,000 cfs in May** — generally unattainable under current water supply.
- Fry rearing at 60°F achievable with **2,500 cfs**; upper tolerance (70°F) achievable with **700 cfs**.
- **Full reproductive minimum flow regime totals 478,500 acre-feet/year** (Table 5) — rarely achievable, resulting in high egg mortality in May and premature fry displacement in July under current habitat conditions.
- **Egg-collection-only regime** (for hatchery supplementation at Marble Bluff Fish Facility) requires far less water: **~69,000 acre-feet** via delta passage or fishway passage (Table 6).

### Combined Species Regime
- The **combined minimum flow regime** (Table 7) takes the higher monthly flow requirement between species, using the smaller cui-ui attraction volume and fishway-passage LCT egg-collection flows as a conservative baseline.
- **Total combined minimum annual instream flow: 224,000 acre-feet**, concentrated January–July with zero flow allocation August–December.
- This combined regime is explicitly a **subset** of the larger 406,000–413,000 acre-foot total annual inflow needed to maintain delta-passage lake elevation.

### Management Implications
- Full reproductive-flow regime for LCT is **not implemented** in most years due to water scarcity; it is only pursued when it will not compromise cui-ui's water needs.
- Even the "minimum" regime may occur too infrequently long-term to ensure species recovery, given variable precipitation, [[Stampede Reservoir]] storage limits, and competing agricultural/domestic diversions.
- Recommended strategy: maintain Pyramid Lake **≥3,815 ft MSL** to raise the probability of achieving needed instream flows; use annual snowmelt forecasts and reservoir storage data to design a conservative, spawning-run-specific water release plan each year.
- Concern is raised over the cui-ui population's **low year-class diversity** and small adult numbers, underscoring the urgency of maximizing reproductive success in every spawning run.
