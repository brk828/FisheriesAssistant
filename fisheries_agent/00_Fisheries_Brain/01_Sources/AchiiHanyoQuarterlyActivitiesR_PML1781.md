# Achii Hanyo Bonytail Chub Rearing Program: Quarterly Activities Report (October 1999 to January 2000)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This quarterly report from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Parker Fisheries Resource Office covers the first operational quarter of the [[Achii Hanyo]] project, which renovates a commercial fish hatchery to rear large numbers of endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] for the [[Lower Colorado River]]. An estimated 27,504 bonytail chubs were received from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. About 1,500 were sent to Niland, California. The report states that 22,262 fish were stocked into four ponds at 5,000 fish/acre, plus 6,527 fish added to ponds 1 and 2.

Water temperatures fell from 20.35 °C in October to 6.69 °C in January. Dissolved oxygen (DO) ranged from 8.58 mg/L in October to 20.45 mg/L in January. A [[Saprolegnia]] (fungal "tail rot") outbreak in November was controlled with medicated feed. Aquatic vegetation was managed with aquatic herbicides, and bird predation was limited by [[Bird Netting]] over all ponds. Four protocol changes were adopted during the quarter.

## Background & Description

The "big river fishes" are listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The document names four species: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]. Several biological opinions require captive propagation of these fish. Existing hatcheries are at capacity, which created the need for the project.

The project goal is to renovate an existing commercial hatchery to raise large numbers of Colorado River endangered fish. A pilot phase began in 1997 with renovation of six earthen ponds and associated piping. Fish were first reared at the facility in 1999.

Annual need, as identified by the Endangered Species Program:
- 55,000 bonytail chubs (10–12 inches) per year for five years for [[Lake Mohave]]
- 30,000 (total) for [[Lake Havasu]]

At full operation, the target is 100,000 ten-inch bonytail chubs per year. As of the report, four ponds totaling 3.2 acres had been developed for bonytail production.

Stocking rates were based on a protocol developed with the manager of [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. Fish were held in three 750-gallon fish farms for 17 days while bird netting was completed over ponds 3A, 3B, and 3C.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stocking and distribution.** Bonytail chubs were delivered from Dexter on 13 October 1999. Numbers were estimated at 57 fish/pound, and this was reconfirmed before distribution. Fish were distributed by pound weight and approximate count across Fish Farms A, B, and C and ponds 1–3. The OCR-degraded table of exact counts was not reliably recoverable. The text reports 31,802 fish moved to the Office Cove at PFRO headquarters (figure as printed) and an estimated 22,262 fish present at Achii Hanyo.

**Feeding.** Feed rate is set by water temperature:
- Above 70 °F: 3% of body mass per day
- 60–70 °F: 2% of body mass per day
- Below 60 °F: 1% of body mass per day

Feeding began on 18 October at 3%. It was discontinued in some farms after DO fell. By the end of the quarter the rate was 1%. Fish are fed twice daily, mainly by an [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]] SCA volunteer.

**Dissolved oxygen crisis.** On 19 October, DO in Fish Farms A and B dropped below 3 mg/L. Hundreds of fish were seen at the surface. Oxygen was applied for several hours, and on 20 October Farm A was drained into ponds 1 and 2.

**Water quality.** Readings are taken twice daily with a Hydrolab H2O meter. The parameters are pH, DO, and temperature. Redox and specific conductivity were added on 24 December. Lab analysis by NEL Labs (Nevada) for heavy metals and other constituents found all parameters "well within normal standards." Pond water use was about 58 acre-feet for the quarter.

**Vegetation.** [[Copper Sulfate]] and Sonar were applied to all ponds to control filamentous algae and aquatic plants. Filamentous algae reappeared on 13 December despite treatment. Salt cedar and emergent vegetation are removed manually in dry ponds and the drainage canal. The report states that no herbicide has been used in any pond for terrestrial vegetation.

**Birds.** All ponds are covered by bird netting. Waterfowl that get in are removed. The [[Kingfisher]] is the only nuisance bird that netting cannot exclude. Observed birds include cormorants, grebes, coots, killdeer, grackles, sandpipers, a kingfisher, and over a dozen ducks (redheads, mallards, goldeneyes).

## Key Findings

- **Production:** About 27,504 bonytail chubs were received; roughly 22,262 were stocked into four ponds at 5,000 fish/acre. Another 6,527 were added to ponds 1 and 2.
- **Growth and condition:** Monthly samples of 50 fish per pond (measured and weighed individually) show that fish were losing body mass as weather cooled. Pond 1 mean total length and weight declined from 130.5 mm / 17.3 (November) to 94.3 mm / 6.1 (January). The report attributes this to reduced feeding and movement in colder water. Units are not stated for the appendix values.
- **Sampling change:** Hoop nets were replaced by a 100 × 10 × 1/4-inch seine after catches fell after the first month.
- **Disease:** Saprolegnia (tail rot) was detected 2 November and treated with medicated feed for 13 days starting 4 November, using a formulation of 0.5 g of an antibiotic per 10 lb of feed with 1/4 cup of corn oil. Fish were sent to the [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]] on 6 and 16 November. The fungus was not observed after about 18 November.
- **Water quality (Appendix B):** Average conditions in November were about 16.5 °C and 11.9 mg/L DO in Pond 1. In January, Pond 1 averaged about 11.3 °C and 14.0 mg/L DO.
- **Vegetation control:** Copper sulfate and Sonar were applied in November–January, and algae persisted.
- **Staffing:** Operations depend on SCA volunteers and Tribal staff, including Sally Hoyt (16 hours/week, living on site). Outside technical help came from [[Manuel Ulibarri]] of Willow Beach NFH.
- **Protocol changes:**
  1. Seine netting replaced hoop nets for sampling.
  2. Fish shipments to the health center now occur only when necessary, not with every weekly sample.
  3. Daily water quality now includes redox and specific conductivity.
  4. The 24-hour Hydrolab readings were discontinued.
