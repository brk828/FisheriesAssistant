# Saline Lakes and Marine Fish Potential in Southeastern New Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sciaenidae

## Executive Summary

This 1967 seminar paper by [[Warren McNall]], prepared for the Northern Great Plains Limnology Seminar in Hastings, Nebraska (January 25, 1967), reviews the limnology of saline waters in southeastern New Mexico and evaluates their potential for stocking marine and alkali-tolerant fish. Limnological collection has been kept minimal, and the work relies on bioassay experiments with transplanted species, including [[Sargo]], [[Bairdiella]], [[Orange-Mouth Corvina]], and [[Sacramento Perch]]. The study sites are seven saline lakes at [[Bottomless Lakes State Park]] and roughly 20 stream miles of the [[Pecos River]]. The author describes the approach as a trial-and-error method, with detailed limnological study to follow only if species survive. Stocking in 1965 and 1966 produced mixed results, and [[Lea Lake]] had lost its fish population in a winter die-off.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Assess whether marine and alkali-tolerant fish can survive in New Mexico's saline waters. Bioassay species are to be tested first, and other marine fish may be tested if the present species fail.
- **Study context:** The work is part of a [[Dingle-Johnson Project]], whose inspectors suggested the same trial-and-error sequence: establish a potentially productive marine fishery first, then conduct detailed limnological studies if it proves feasible.
- **Study sites:**
  - Seven saline lakes at Bottomless Lakes State Park, 45 miles east of Roswell.
  - About 20 stream miles of the Pecos River immediately above the Texas-New Mexico state line.
- **Lake classification:** All seven lakes are "sink" lakes, as described by Reid (1961).
- **Lake sizes:**
  - Devil's Inkwell: 0.36 surface acres (smallest).
  - [[Lazy Lagoon]] (No Name Lake): usually about 3 surface acres, occasionally up to 26.10 surface acres.
  - Lea Lake: 13.46 surface acres, described as the largest permanent lake.
- **Maximum depths:** 18 feet at Pasture Lake to over 90 feet at Lea Lake.
- **Water origin:** Subterranean, possibly from several aquifers. The author infers this from the varying ionic composition among lakes and from the fact that one lake oscillates while others do not.
- **Ionic composition:** Reported in Table 1, which is not legible in the scanned text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake thermal regime (Bottomless Lakes)**
- Surface temperatures approach 80°F during summer.
- Multiple stratification layers occur near the 5-foot level and again at the 15–20-foot level.
- Benthic temperatures approach 50°F in the deeper lakes during summer.

**Pecos River conditions**
- Salinity is more extreme than at Bottomless Lakes.
- The highest salinities occur at [[Malaga Bend]], 15 miles southeast of Carlsbad, where chlorides exceed 16,000 ppm.
- The normal flow of the affected reach is 10–50 cfs. Periodic floods sweep through this area and are a major stressor.
- The OCR text dates a flood to "1955," shortly after an orange-mouth corvina release. This is likely a scan error, since the surrounding context points to a later release.

**Indigenous and established fauna**
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon* sp.) is the most common native fish.
- [[Southwest Plains Killifish]] (*Fundulus zebrinus*) occurs in moderate abundance in all lakes except Lea Lake.
- Black bass, crappie, channel catfish, and rainbow trout have been stocked since 1907. Pasture, Bay, and Cottonwood lakes are currently managed for catchable-size channel catfish and rainbow trout during cooler months.

**Lea Lake status**
- Lea Lake had no known living fish after a die-off of unknown cause in winter 1966.
- A survey in October 1966 using desert pupfish as indicators reported on the lake's condition. The scanned sentence is partly garbled.

## Key Findings

1. **1965 introduction:** Sargo from the [[Salton Sea]] in California were released into Lea Lake and Lazy Lagoon. Extensive gill netting in 1966 captured none of them.
2. **November 1966 introduction:** Orange-mouth corvina, sargo, and bairdiella from the Salton Sea were released into Lazy Lagoon. No recapture had been attempted at the time of writing. A school of active sargo was observed 48 hours after release, and two bairdiella were seen 72 hours after release.
3. **Other stocking, 1965:** About eight fish obtained from Nebraska through Bruce McCarraher were placed in live cars and all died within 72 hours. The species name is garbled in the scan.
4. **Sacramento perch supply:** 5,000 one- to two-inch [[Sacramento Perch]] were collected from the Valentine Hatchery in October 1966 and released into a relatively pure lake near Santa Rosa, New Mexico. If they survive, they will serve as bioassay fish for Bottomless Lakes and other saline and alkali waters in southeastern New Mexico.
5. **Corvina potential:** [[Orange-Mouth Corvina]] are considered the species with the greatest potential for this environment because of their wide salinity tolerance. The greatest numbers were captured in a relatively pure stream entering the Salton Sea. Releases at Malaga Bend are scheduled for 1967.
6. **Malaga Bend fate:** The whereabouts of the corvina released near Malaga Bend are unknown.
7. **Research approach:** The author characterizes New Mexico's limnological work on marine fish potential as trial and error. Detailed limnological studies will follow only if marine and alkali-tolerant species survive in the study waters.
8. **Data limitations:** Pages 4–5 contain water-quality tables. The scan is too degraded to transcribe their values reliably, so no numerical values are reported from them beyond those in the text above.
