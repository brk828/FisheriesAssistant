# Public Law 100-478: Endangered Species Act Amendments of 1988

#marine-biology #herpetology #sea-turtles

## Executive Summary

Public Law 100-478, enacted October 7, 1988 by the 100th Congress (102 STAT. 2306–2315), amends the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 and authorizes appropriations for fiscal years 1988 through 1992. The law revises definitions, [[Recovery Plans]] requirements, monitoring of recovered species, state cooperation and funding, plant protections, civil and criminal penalties, [[Scrimshaw Certificates]], and annual cost reporting. It also contains a dedicated sea turtle section (Sec. 1008) that delays shrimp-trawling regulations and orders an independent scientific review of sea turtle conservation.

This document is legislation, not fish research. It contains no fish species data, sampling, or field observations.

## Background & Description

The Act responds to the conservation needs of listed endangered and threatened species. Its sections include:

- **Sec. 1001 Definitions:** Redefines "person" to cover individuals, corporations, governments, and other entities under U.S. jurisdiction. Amends the definition of "Secretary."
- **Sec. 1002 Listing:** Requires the Secretary to monitor candidate species and to use emergency authority to prevent significant risk. Adds "regulation of commerce or taking" to the similarity-of-appearance provision.
- **Sec. 1003 Recovery Plans:** Sets priorities without regard to taxonomic classification, requires site-specific actions, measurable delisting criteria, and time and cost estimates, and mandates public review.
- **Sec. 1004 Monitoring of Recovered Species:** Requires monitoring for not less than five years after delisting.
- **Sec. 1005 Cooperation with the States:** Allocates financial assistance to states and creates the [[Cooperative Endangered Species Conservation Fund]].
- **Sec. 1006 Protection of Plants:** Revises prohibitions on removing or damaging listed plants.
- **Sec. 1007 Penalties and Enforcement:** Raises civil and criminal penalties.
- **Sec. 1008 Sea Turtle Conservation:** Addresses [[Shrimp Trawling]] mortality of sea turtles.
- **Sec. 1009 Authorization of Appropriations:** Sets funding ceilings by department.
- **Sec. 1010 Education, Study and Report:** Addresses pesticide labeling and agricultural impacts.
- **Sec. 1011 Scrimshaw Certificates:** Revises certificate rules for pre-Act scrimshaw products.
- **Sec. 1012 Federal Cost of Protecting Endangered Species:** Requires an annual cost report.
- **Sec. 1013 Technical Amendments:** Corrects punctuation and wording.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sea turtle species named in Sec. 1008** ("relevant species of sea turtles"):

- [[Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle]]
- United States breeding populations of the [[Loggerhead Sea Turtle]], the [[Leatherback Sea Turtle]], and the [[Green Sea Turtle]], plus other significant breeding populations of these three species

**Threats and management focus:**

- Drowning in shrimp nets, which is the central concern. The review must assess whether more or less stringent measures are needed in inshore and offshore areas.
- Mortality from other causes in U.S., Mexican, and other national waters, with nesting and reproductive locations identified.
- Head-start and other programs to increase production and population size.
- Use of the [[Turtle Excluder Device]] by shrimp fishermen.

**Geographic scope:** U.S. waters, Mexican waters, and other nations' waters, with regulations for inshore and offshore areas. The Canaveral area of Florida is exempt from the delay, and its existing regulations remain in effect.

**Agencies involved:** [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (sea turtle coordinator), [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (annual cost analysis), Environmental Protection Agency, Department of Agriculture, Department of the Interior, and Department of Commerce.

## Key Findings

**Sea turtle regulations (Sec. 1008)**

- Effective dates of regulations promulgated June 29, 1987 are delayed to May 1, 1990 for inshore areas and May 1, 1989 for offshore areas. Regulations already in effect in the Canaveral area of Florida remain in force.
- Inshore regulations take effect May 1, 1990 unless the Secretary finds other measures are "equally effective" in reducing shrimp-trawl sea turtle mortality.
- An independent review by the [[National Academy of Sciences]] must be submitted by April 1, 1989. If incomplete, priority goes to the Kemp's ridley sea turtle.
- The review must cover population status, distribution, mortality causes, nesting sites, and conservation measures in U.S., Mexican, and other waters.
- A Sea Turtle Coordinator is to be designated by the National Marine Fisheries Service.
- $1,500,000 is authorized for the Department of Commerce through fiscal year 1989.

**Recovery plans and monitoring (Sec. 1003–1004)**

- Plans must include objective, measurable delisting criteria and estimated time and cost.
- The Secretary reports to Congress every two years.
- Public notice and comment are required before approval of new or revised plans.
- Recovered species must be monitored for not less than five years after delisting.

**Funding (Sec. 1005, 1009)**

- The Cooperative Endangered Species Conservation Fund receives 5 percent of combined amounts covered into the Federal aid to wildlife restoration fund under the Act of September 2, 1937, and credited to the Sport Fishing Restoration Account under the Act of July 18, 1984.
- Authorized appropriations (not to exceed):

| Department | FY1988 | FY1989 | FY1990 | FY1991 | FY1992 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interior | $35,000,000 | $36,500,000 | $38,000,000 | $39,500,000 | $41,500,000 |
| Commerce | $5,750,000 | $6,250,000 | $6,250,000 | $6,750,000 | $6,750,000 |
| Agriculture | $2,200,000 | $2,400,000 | $2,400,000 | $2,600,000 | $2,600,000 |

- Section 7 exemption support: $600,000 for each of fiscal years 1988–1992.
- Convention implementation under Sec. 8A(e): $400,000 for each of fiscal years 1988–1990, and $500,000 for each of fiscal years 1991–1992.

**Penalties (Sec. 1007)**

- Civil penalties: $10,000 raised to $25,000; $5,000 raised to $12,000.
- Criminal penalties: $20,000 raised to $50,000; $10,000 raised to $25,000.
- Penalty balances exceeding $500,000 are deposited into the Cooperative Endangered Species Conservation Fund.

**Scrimshaw (Sec. 1011)**

- Certificates renewed after October 13, 1982 and in effect on March 31, 1988 are deemed renewed for six months from enactment. Holders may apply for one more renewal of up to five years.
- After January 31, 1984, pre-Act finished scrimshaw may be sold only with a valid certificate and only if held on October 13, 1982.

**Reporting and education**

- Annual cost reports to Congress are due by January 15, 1990 and each January 15 thereafter, with species-by-species accounting of federal and state expenditures (Sec. 1012).
- EPA must inform agricultural producers about pesticide labeling requirements. A study and report are due within one year of enactment (Sec. 1010).
- The Secretary must run an educational effort for shrimp fishermen on turtle excluder devices (Sec. 1008).
