# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin - FY 2000 Program Guidance

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #program-management #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document provides comprehensive fiscal year 2000 program guidance for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 6 on April 1, 1999, it outlines funding priorities, project scopes, and work plans across seven recovery elements: instream flow protection, habitat restoration, nonnative fish control, propagation and genetics, research and monitoring, public involvement, and program management. The guidance organizes projects into ongoing, ongoing-revised, and new categories, with total projected FY 2000 budget exceeding $8 million across multiple recovery actions. Key endangered species addressed include [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]].

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program represents a collaborative effort among the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, Upper Basin water users, environmental organizations, and the [[Colorado River Energy Distributors Association]]. The program operates under a Recovery Action Plan ([[RIPRAP]]) that identifies all activities currently believed necessary and feasible to recover endangered fish in the Upper Basin.

The FY 2000 guidance covers over 800 miles of critical habitat across the basin. Program structure includes technical committees (Biology Committee, Water Acquisition Committee), a Management Committee, and an Implementation Committee that oversee project development and approval. The document establishes standardized formats for scope of work submissions, peer review processes, and report evaluation procedures.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] encompasses multiple river systems including the [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Gunnison River]], and [[Duchesne River]]. Critical habitat features include backwaters, floodplain depressions, spawning gravels, and migration corridors. The basin faces multiple ecological challenges including flow regulation from dams ([[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Ruedi Reservoir]]), habitat fragmentation from diversion structures, nonnative fish competition and predation, and altered hydrological regimes.

Water management infrastructure significantly affects endangered fish habitat. The program addresses coordinated reservoir operations, instream flow protection, and fish passage restoration at multiple barriers including [[Redlands Diversion Dam]], [[Hartland Diversion Dam]], [[Price-Stubb Diversion Dam]], and [[Grand Valley Project Diversion Dam]]. Channel monitoring programs track geomorphological changes in critical reaches including the 15-Mile Reach.

## Key Findings

**Instream Flow Protection (Element I)**: Projected FY 2000 budget of approximately $3.3 million includes ongoing gage operation ($25.4K), water acquisition support ($47.5K), [[Steamboat Lake]] water lease ($32K), coordinated reservoir operations ($50K), and [[Grand Valley]] water management ($2.56M). New projects include evaluation of fluctuating flow effects on overwintering [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] ($60-100K annually for 3 years) to assess impacts of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] winter operations on age-0 fish survival, movement, and habitat use.

**Habitat Restoration (Element II)**: Budget approximately $3.2 million focuses on fish passage restoration and floodplain enhancement. Ongoing projects include [[Redlands Fish Passage]] evaluation ($64.8K), [[Price-Stubb Fish Passage]] ($1.3M), [[Grand Valley Project Fish Passage]] ($1.58M), and [[Hartland Fish Passage]] ($300K). New projects include [[GVIC Fish Screening]] ($700K FY00, $1.15M FY01) and demonstration of larval/juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] survival to recruitment in floodplain depressions with nonnative fishes present ($50-60K). The floodplain restoration program includes contaminant screening, floodability assessments, levee removal, and evaluation activities.

**Nonnative Fish Control (Element III)**: Approximately $570K allocated for removal programs targeting small nonnative cyprinids in Utah ($91.8K) and Colorado ($54K), [[Colorado River]] centrarchid removal ($52K), and [[Yampa River]] nonnative fish control ($100K). [[Colorado River]] pond reclamation continues ($272K). New projects include [[Highline Reservoir]] screening and evaluation ($50K) and monitoring to evaluate effectiveness of Colorado stocking regulations ($30K annually for 2 years) limiting nonnative fish stocking in floodable areas within 100-year floodplain.

**Propagation & Genetics (Element IV)**: Budget approximately $795K includes [[Bonytail]] introduction ($92K) and operation/maintenance of propagation facilities ($703.2K) at [[Grand Valley Endangered Fish Facility]] ($220K), [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] ($87.2K), and [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] ($376K). New project proposes [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] translocation evaluation ($30-45K annually) in the Palisade to Rifle reach, targeting 10 adult fish per river mile comprising less than 1% of biomass, with complete monitoring of movements and behaviors using appropriate tagging.

**Research & Monitoring (Element V)**: Over $800K allocated for [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP) including coordination ($33K), [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] population monitoring ($45K), [[Black Rocks]] [[Humpback Chub]] population estimate ($22K), Colorado ISMP ($36.4K), Utah ISMP ($221.1K), and basinwide [[Razorback Sucker]] monitoring ($220K). Additional funding for larval fish identification and curation ($53.8K), database management ($35.9K), and [[Razorback Sucker]] stocking ($49K). New projects include assessment of ISMP objectives and methodology ($20-30K) and development of habitat monitoring program ($15K) to identify essential habitat features linked to population increases.

**Public Involvement (Element VI)**: Approximately $157K for project-specific public involvement plans covering [[Yampa River]] management ($1K), Grand Valley capital projects ($30K), coordinated reservoir operations ($4.2K), [[Ruedi Reservoir]] allocation ($10K), [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] releases ($28K), floodplain restoration ($16.9K), pond reclamation ($10K), and Utah combined involvement ($2K). General information and education receives $45K.

**Program Management (Element VII)**: Total $1.51M includes Utah program management ($75.3K), Bureau of Reclamation management ($150K), Fish and Wildlife Service management ($760K), Colorado management ($110K), Wyoming management ($12.3K), and capital projects coordination ($400K).

**Process Requirements**: New project scopes of work due March 19, 1999 for peer review. Ongoing and ongoing-revised biological and water acquisition project scopes due April 23, 1999. Information and education scopes due April 24, 1999. Program management scopes due July 1, 1999. All submissions required in WordPerfect format with standardized evaluation forms. Biology Committee review follows established protocols with ratings of Excellent, Good, Fair, or Questionable based on clear objectives, methodological validity, integration with existing knowledge, feasibility, and timeliness.

**Critical Research Gaps Identified**: Overwinter survival estimates for age-0 [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] show losses above 50% in selected backwaters; validation studies needed to test assumptions and determine effects of dam operations. [[Razorback Sucker]] recruitment to adulthood not demonstrated in floodplain habitats despite juvenile production. ISMP methodology requires outside peer assessment to ensure $700K+ annual expenditure effectively monitors trends and provides population estimates within reasonable confidence intervals across 800+ miles of critical habitat.
