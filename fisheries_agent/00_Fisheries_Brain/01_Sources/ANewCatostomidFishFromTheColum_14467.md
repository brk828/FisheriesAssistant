#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1932 taxonomic paper by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Leonard P. Schultz]] formally describes a new species of sucker, **[[Catostomus syncheilus]]**, from the [[Columbia River]] system. Prior to this work, the fine-scaled Columbia River sucker had been erroneously identified as *[[Catostomus catostomus]]* by multiple earlier authors (Eigenmann, Gilbert and Evermann, Evermann, and Evermann and Nichols). The authors distinguish *C. syncheilus* primarily by the wide union of its lower lips, which allows several rows of papillae to cross the ventral midline—a character reflected in the species name ("syncheilus" = fused/united lips). The paper situates the new species within the broader taxonomic context of western North American catostomids, comparing it against close relatives including *[[Catostomus pocatello]]*, *[[Catostomus griseus]]*, *[[Catostomus rimiculus]]*, and *[[Pantosteus plebeius]]*.

## Background & Description

**Holotype:** Postnuptial male, 183 mm long to caudal fin; Cat. No. 94076, Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan; collected June 16, 1926, in Crab Creek, Washington.

**Body form:**
- Chubby body with strongly curved anterodorsal profile
- Greatest body depth (below dorsal origin): 4.1 (range 3.9–4.7) in standard length
- Body rather thick, considerably wider than head is deep
- Head slightly wider than deep

**Caudal peduncle:**
- Abruptly constricted, slender
- Least depth: 2.0x in peduncle length; 3.0x in head length; 12.6x (12.6–13.0) in standard length
- This slenderness distinguishes it from *[[Catostomus rimiculus]]*, *[[Catostomus tahoensis]]*, *C. microps*, and *C. griseus*

**Head and eye:**
- Snout contained ~0.95–1.0x in postorbital head length
- Eye moderate, submedian; contained 6.1 (4.2–7.0) times in head
- Interorbital width: 2.5–2.6x in head

**Lips (diagnostic feature):**
- Lower lip only **moderately incised**, allowing 2–4 rows of papillae to cross the midline anterior to the incision — this is the key diagnostic trait separating *syncheilus* from *[[Catostomus catostomus]]* and its close relatives (which have deep incision leaving only one row of papillae across midline)
- Upper lip papillo-plicate with strongly crenate edge; inner face bears two rows of large papillae opposite jaw edge, becoming more numerous toward mouth angle
- Lower lip papillae large, round, and isolated anteriorly; becoming lower, oval, and seriated into radial rows distally
- Jaw edges hard, sharp, weakly curved; no conspicuous angulation

**Fins:**
- Dorsal fin slightly falcate; height 1.0–1.2x its base
- Dorsal base enters total length 5.2–6.5x
- All fins rounded at tips
- Fin ray counts (holotype): dorsal 12, anal 7, pectoral 16, pelvic 10
- Pectoral rays: 16–17 in paratypes; pelvic rays occasionally 11

**Scales:**
- Much reduced in size anteriorly, especially on nape and belly
- Holotype counts: lateral line 106; dorsal-to-lateral-line 22; anal-to-lateral-line 22; before dorsal 58
- Comparative counts tabulated against *C. rimiculus* populations from the Klamath and Rogue River systems

**Fontanelle:**
- Wide and open in adults (not reduced to narrow/obsolete slit) — a key character separating *syncheilus* from *[[Catostomus rimiculus]]*, which has a closing/obsolete fontanelle

**Coloration:**
- Adults uniformly dark dorsally, pale ventrally
- Yearlings show typical dorsal mottling and three lateral blotches

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat/Range:** *Catostomus syncheilus* occupies the upper Columbia River system broadly, with confirmed records from:
- Crab Creek, Washington (type locality) and Sylvan Lake
- Moses Lake, Washington (same basin, isolated by lava flow)
- Tributary of the Yakima River at Ellensburg, Washington
- Tributary of the Snake River near King Hill, Idaho
- Lake Chelan, Washington (Evermann record)
- Columbia River at Golden, British Columbia (Eigenmann record)
- Multiple localities in Idaho, eastern Washington, and northeastern Oregon (Gilbert and Evermann records)

**Sympatry:** Notably, *C. syncheilus* co-occurs with *C. pocatello* in the same system (both recorded from Ross Fork of the Snake River near Pocatello, Idaho), supporting their status as distinct species rather than variants.

**Related taxa and comparative distribution:**
- *[[Catostomus macrocheilus]]* — coarse-scaled congener sharing the Columbia system (subgenus Decactylus)
- *[[Catostomus pocatello]]* — fine-scaled congener, Snake River basin and Clark Fork of the Columbia (Montana)
- *[[Catostomus griseus]]* — Missouri River system (Colorado, Wyoming, Montana), opposite the Continental Divide
- *[[Catostomus rimiculus]]* — Klamath and Rogue River systems; considered an "isolated variant" of the Columbia River lineage
- *[[Catostomus tahoensis]]* — Lahontan system
- *[[Pantosteus plebeius]]* — Rio Grande system; morphologically the closest analog despite generic separation

**Biogeographic note:** The authors argue that *C. rimiculus* being shared between the Klamath and Rogue River systems (while all other Klamath-characteristic fish, e.g. *[[Chasmistes brevirostris]]*, *[[Deltistes luxatus]]*, are absent from the Rogue) casts doubt on prior assumptions that the Rogue fauna is simply a subset of Klamath fauna. Instead, they propose *rimiculus*, *[[Catostomus warnerensis]]*, an unnamed Harvey-Malheur Lake basin form, and *syncheilus* may constitute a natural group distinct from both the coarse-scaled (*commersonnii*) and fine-scaled (*catostomus*) series.

## Key Findings

1. **New species established:** *Catostomus syncheilus* is formally distinguished from *[[Catostomus catostomus]]* and its close relatives by the **wide union of the lower lips**, permitting multiple papillae rows across the midline (vs. single row in true fine-scaled *catostomus* relatives).

2. **Taxonomic correction:** Several prior records misidentified this species as *C. catostomus*, including works by Eigenmann (1894), Gilbert and Evermann (1894), Evermann (1899), and Evermann and Nichols (1909).

3. **Sympatry with *C. pocatello*** in the Snake River basin confirms specific-level distinction rather than subspecies status.

4. **Differentiation from *C. rimiculus***: Despite superficial similarity in lip structure, *syncheilus* differs in having a wider/more persistent fontanelle, larger eye, larger scales, and higher average dorsal ray counts.

5. **Possible closer affinity to *Pantosteus* than to *Catostomus***: The authors note *C. syncheilus* resembles *[[Pantosteus plebeius]]* more closely in body form, caudal peduncle shape, lip incision depth, and cartilaginous jaw sheath development than it resembles other *Catostomus* species — suggesting the genus-level classification of western suckers inadequately reflects true phylogenetic relationships.

6. **Proposed natural grouping:** The authors hypothesize a distinct lineage comprising *C. rimiculus*, *[[Catostomus warnerensis]]*, the unnamed Harvey-Malheur form, and *C. syncheilus*, cutting across the traditional coarse-scaled/fine-scaled dichotomy used to classify western *Catostomus*.

7. **Synonymy clarifications:**
   - *Catostomus lactarius* and *C. retropinnis* are treated as synonyms of *[[Catostomus griseus]]*
   - *Catostomus rex* is a synonym of *[[Deltistes luxatus]]*
   - *Catostomus warnerensis* was reassigned from fine-scaled to coarse-scaled group by Snyder (1917), though the present authors suggest it may instead relate to *syncheilus*

8. **Diagnostic morphometrics** for *C. syncheilus*: caudal peduncle least depth contained ~3x in head and ~12–13x in standard length (vs. <3x in head and 10–11x in standard length for *C. pocatello*); head length <25% of standard length even in large yearlings.
