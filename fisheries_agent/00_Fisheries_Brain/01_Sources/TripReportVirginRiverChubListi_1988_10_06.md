# Trip Report: Virgin River Chub Listing

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae #conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1988 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service trip report detailing the convergence of two significant events: the ongoing regulatory process to list the [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*) as endangered, and an unplanned, large-scale fish kill caused by rotenone treatment. FWS personnel from Salt Lake City were conducting a piscicide (rotenone) reclamation of 27 miles of the Virgin River in Utah to eradicate the invasive [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). The toxicant unexpectedly passed through the Virgin River Narrows, unoxidized, and killed native and non-native fish at least 20 miles into Arizona, reaching the confluence with [[Beaver Dam Wash]]. Field surveys documented substantial native fish mortality alongside non-native mortality, complicating a concurrent stakeholder meeting with the Washington County Water District regarding the chub's listing status and flow management on the river.

## Background & Description
Species identification in the field was complicated by observer inexperience, highlighting key taxonomic distinctions among sympatric Virgin River cyprinids and catostomids:
- **[[Virgin River Chub]]** (*Gila robusta seminuda*): A subspecies of the [[Roundtail Chub]] complex; long-lived desert cyprinid.
- **[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*): A federally endangered cyprinid; the report notes it was likely **misidentified as [[Speckled Dace]]** by an inexperienced surveyor, suggesting field identification between these two small silvery cyprinids is non-trivial.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Small native cyprinid, morphologically similar enough to woundfin to cause survey confusion.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and **[[Bluehead Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus discobolus*): Two native catostomids noted as easily confused in the field; some fish counted as flannelmouth sucker were suspected to actually be bluehead sucker.
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*): Non-native invasive cyprinid, present in overwhelming numbers relative to natives in seine samples.
- **[[Black Bullhead]]** (*Ictalurus melas*): Non-native ictalurid catfish present in the kill zone.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The Virgin River system supports a distinct desert fish assemblage adapted to fluctuating, harsh hydrological conditions:
- **Mainstem habitat**: Occupied by [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Woundfin]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and [[Bluehead Sucker]] alongside invasive [[Red Shiner]] and [[Black Bullhead]].
- **Tributary refugia**: [[Beaver Dam Wash]] appeared to function as a refuge — native [[Speckled Dace]] persisted there in kick-seine samples even as the mainstem showed zero native fish captured in seine hauls, suggesting tributaries may buffer populations against mainstem disturbances (natural or anthropogenic).
- **Deep pools**: Unsampled deep pools in the Virgin River were noted as potential unassessed refugia where native fish may have survived the toxicant plume.
- **Population dynamics**: The chub is characterized as a "long-lived desert fish" exhibiting natural year-to-year fluctuation in abundance and breeding success, with documented successful spawning years (1983, 1986) and poor recruitment years (1987, 1988) since Water District monitoring began in 1983.
- **Threat interactions**: Anthropogenic flow alteration from water withdrawals is hypothesized to amplify the natural boom-bust population cycle by increasing the frequency/severity of recruitment failures — an example of a threat multiplier acting on baseline demographic variability.
- **Invasive species pressure**: [[Red Shiner]] invasion into upstream habitat was the primary driver for the Utah rotenone reclamation effort, indicating competitive/ecological displacement pressure on native cyprinids justified large-scale chemical intervention.

## Key Findings
- **Unintended toxicant spread**: Rotenone (carried in a fuel oil base) applied in the 27-mile Utah reclamation zone passed through the [[Virgin River Narrows]] unoxidized and caused fish mortality at least 20 miles downstream into Arizona, reaching [[Beaver Dam Wash]] — a spread not anticipated by the Salt Lake City reclamation crew.
- **Seine survey results (10/7/88)**:
  - 8 hauls in Virgin River above Beaver Dam Wash inflow: 55 Red Shiner, **0 native fish**.
  - 1 kick-seine haul in Beaver Dam Wash: 65 Red Shiner, 4 Speckled Dace.
  - 1 haul below Beaver Dam Wash confluence: 280 Red Shiner.
  - 2 hauls on far side of river (upstream of Beaver Dam Wash influence): 0 fish.
- **Streamside mortality transects**: Four 100-yard transects (400 yards total) along the west bank of the Virgin River documented **198 dead or dying fish**, of which **~65% were native species**, including:

| 1 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 22 | 0 | 2 | 3 | 0 |
| 3 | 36 | 12 | 14 | 12 | 4 |

- **Identification caveats**: Field surveyor inexperience likely led to underreporting of endangered [[Woundfin]] (misidentified as Speckled Dace) and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (misidentified as Flannelmouth Sucker), meaning true native mortality and species composition may differ from raw counts.
- **Extent of kill**: Investigators hypothesized possible total extirpation of native fish along ~52 stream miles from Washington Fields, Utah to downstream of Beaver Dam Wash, Arizona, though deep pools and tributaries were not fully sampled and may have served as refugia.
- **Listing meeting outcome**: Despite the fish kill controversy, the FWS proceeded with plans to finalize the endangered listing of the [[Virgin River Chub]], rejecting the Washington County Water District's request to withdraw the listing in exchange for funding recovery studies, since the District could not guarantee no further flow alterations from water withdrawals.
- **Policy tension**: The case illustrates a direct conflict between water resource development (Washington County Water District's withdrawal plans) and endangered species conservation, with the Water District threatening legal/political action against the listing.
