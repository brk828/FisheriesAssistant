## Executive Summary

This limnological study, conducted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department from July 1964 to June 1965, characterizes the physical, chemical, and biological conditions of the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] tailrace between the dam and Lee's Ferry on the [[Colorado River]]. Water quality parameters were generally suitable for maintaining a [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery, though temperatures periodically approached the upper thermal tolerance limit for trout. Severe and rapid fluctuations in water discharge (ranging from 920 to 58,100 c.f.s.) were identified as the dominant stressor on the ecosystem, causing constant substrate shifting that prevented benthic macroinvertebrate colonization. Planktonic biomass increased substantially compared to prior study periods due to a change in the dam's water release point, correlating with increased zooplankton occurrence in trout diets. Fish sampling via gill nets, explosives, cove rotenone treatments, and angling/creel census identified at least 15 fish species, dominated by introduced Rainbow Trout, Carp, and Channel Catfish, alongside native Colorado River fishes including [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]].

## Background & Description

**Study Purpose:** Establish baseline limnological and fisheries data in the newly regulated tailrace reach below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to inform trout fishery management at [[Lee's Ferry]].

**Sampling Stations:**
- **Station I** — Lee's Ferry boat ramp (primary station, sampled throughout study)
- **Station II** — Eight-mile rapid (intermittent access due to flow)
- **Station III** — U.S. Bureau of Reclamation water pumping station (~1 mile below dam)
- **Station IV** — At water plant (limited sampling, 3 months)

**Methods:**
- Bi-monthly water quality testing: temperature, pH, alkalinity, total hardness, dissolved oxygen, visibility, water color
- Benthic sampling via Surber and Eckman dredges
- Plankton drift sampling (timed hauls)
- Fish collection via overnight gill nets, 40% dynamite charges (primacord-strung), rotenone cove renovation, and angling
- Stomach content analysis (qualitative) on 373 [[Rainbow Trout]]
- Condition factor (C) calculated for game fish

**Data sources integrated:** Arizona Game and Fish Department, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Arizona Public Health Department Laboratory, and U.S. Geological Survey (discharge, sediment, chemistry).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Thermal Regime:** Water temperature ranged from 74°F (September) to 48°F (May). A notable event in September 1964 saw penstock-released water reach 78°F, with diversion tunnel flow tempering the river to 74°F — near the upper thermal limit for trout survival. This was linked to the release point being ~300 feet from the diversion tunnel to the penstocks (20 feet below reservoir surface).

**Hydrology & Flow Fluctuation:** Identified as the primary adverse factor for the fishery. Daily mean flows varied from 920 c.f.s. (July) to 58,100 c.f.s. (June), with daily fluctuations exceeding 20,000 c.f.s. This caused:
- Continuous silt resuspension and redeposition ("boiling action")
- Chronic substrate instability preventing benthic organism establishment
- Turbidity and water color fluctuating between clear and turbid correspondingly

**Water Chemistry:** No chemical parameters were detrimental to trout. Ranges recorded:
- Dissolved oxygen: 5–10 p.p.m. (lower in winter, still within suitable range)
- pH: ~8.0 (consistently stable)
- Alkalinity: 103–188 p.p.m.
- Total hardness: 176–427 p.p.m. (showed a decline attributed to changed dam release point)

**Benthic Community:** Severely limited — only three organism types recorded across all stations: a small black snail, [[Chironomid Larvae]] (bloodworms), and an incidentally-collected green fly (likely surface-floating, not a true benthic sample). Bloodworm/snail densities were low and non-improving relative to the prior study period, directly attributed to substrate instability from flow fluctuation.

**Plankton Community:** Marked increase in plankton density (up to 2.75 mL per haul) compared to trace amounts in the previous study period. This shift was directly attributed to the dam's altered release point — moving from plankton-poor bottom water (diversion tunnel) to plankton-rich water 20 feet below reservoir surface (penstocks). This translated ecologically into a dietary shift, with zooplankton appearing in 22.9% of trout stomachs versus none previously recorded.

**Fish Kill Event:** In September 1964, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation shut down generators and left the river outlet tunnel open, drawing fish into the tunnel. Subsequent closure and draining for inspection killed an estimated 75–100 game fish and a larger, unquantified number of rough fish, generating public concern.

### Condition Factor (C) — Rainbow Trout
- Overall average condition factor: **0.48** (per abstract); method-specific averages ranged **0.34–0.57**
- Gill net-collected trout: C = 0.34–0.57 depending on month
- Creel census trout: C averaged **0.49** across the year (range 0.38–0.56)
- Angling-collected trout: C averaged **0.41** (range 0.28–0.50)

### Stomach Content Analysis (n=373 Rainbow Trout)
Dominant food items by percent occurrence in stomachs:
- **Algae**: 55.2%
- **Bait** (angler-introduced): 31.8%
- **Zooplankton**: 22.8%
- **Debris**: 24.9%
- **Aquatic plants**: 13.3%
- **Insects**: 10.7%
- **Empty stomachs**: 16.4%

Algae and zooplankton were identified as the principal natural food organisms sustaining the trout fishery.

### Cove Renovation Highlights (Rotenone Treatments, June 1965)
- **Water Holes Canyon**: Mixed catch including Rainbow Trout, Largemouth Bass, Green Sunfish, Flannelmouth Sucker, Carp
- **Paria River**: Large catch of Channel Catfish (31), Carp (13), Suckermouth Minnows (83), Flannelmouth Sucker (21), Plains Red Shiner (14)
- **Lee's Ferry Cutoff**: Massive Carp catch (281) and ~1,000 Suckermouth Minnows; also Rainbow Trout (5), Channel Catfish, Fathead Minnows, Plains Killifish

### Creel Census (Angler-Caught Fish, n=1,068 Rainbow Trout across year)
- Overall average length: 11.33 inches
- Overall average weight: 0.715 lbs
- Overall average condition factor: 0.49
- Peak catches occurred in March (300 fish) and July (134 fish)

### Recommendations for Future Management
1. Additional marked fingerling trout plants to study growth and evaluate sustaining the fishery via stocking.
2. Mark-recapture tagging program to study individual growth and movement patterns.
3. Continued evaluation of introducing new game fish species and/or food organisms at [[Lee's Ferry]].
4. Close monitoring of benthic organism recovery as dam-regulated flow stabilizes over time.

### Data Quality Note
Flow fluctuation severely constrained study consistency — only **Station I** could be sampled continuously throughout the full study period; Stations II–IV suffered access limitations due to excessive water flow or insufficient water depth during multiple months.
