# Data on the Life History, Variation, Ecology, and Relationships of the Kelp Perch, *Brachyistius frenatus*, an Embiotocid Fish of the Californias

#ichthyology #marine-biology #embiotocidae

## Executive Summary

This 1954 study by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and Laura C. Hubbs, published in the *California Fish and Game* journal, examines the kelp perch (*Brachyistius frenatus*), a viviparous surfperch of the North Pacific. It was prompted by [[Fred Harald Tarp]]'s 1952 revision of the family [[Embiotocidae]], which synonymized *Brachyistius* with *Micrometrus*. Based chiefly on a collection of 350 specimens from the [[La Jolla Kelp Bed]] (August 1, 1950), the authors show that *Brachyistius* differs fundamentally from *Micrometrus* and *Amphigonopterus* in life history, meristic variation, habitat, diet, and morphology, and is probably more closely related to [[Shiner Perch]] (*Cymatogaster aggregata*). They propose a revised classification of the family.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Brachyistius frenatus*, the kelp perch, a very small viviparous embiotocid.
- **Material:** Collection of 350 specimens, including 13 term embryos, taken with derris in giant kelp off La Jolla Point, from surface to bottom by dip-netting and diving. Gulls consumed an unknown number, probably several hundred, of surfacing fish.
- **Comparison genera:** [[Micrometrus]] (dwarf perch, *M. minimus*) and [[Amphigonopterus]] (reef perch, *A. aurora*), which the authors regard as highly specialized.
- **Second species:** *Brachyistius aletes* (Tarp, 1952), known from Guadalupe Island, living among *Sargassum* and *Eisenia* and descending to about 150 feet.
- **Method:** Size-frequency analysis, sexing and maturity examination, meristic (fin-ray and vertebral) counts, and underwater observation by [[Conrad Limbaugh]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Mid-water among the columns of [[Giant Kelp]] (*Macrocystis pyrifera*); about 95 percent or more of the population lives in kelp at all ages. A few were taken in traps as deep as 90 feet. Rarely enters the intertidal reef belt or tide pools.
- **Contrast with micrometrins:** *Micrometrus* is typically on exposed rocky reefs and tide pools; *Amphigonopterus* lives in intertidal shallow pools.
- **Feeding:** Carnivorous, unlike the largely herbivorous micrometrins. Nibbles food from kelp and picks ectoparasites from other fishes, similar to [[Senorita Wrasse]] (*Oxyjulis californica*). Mouth is oblique and superior, suited to mid-water feeding.
- **Coexisting embiotocids (La Jolla sample):** *Embiotoca* sp. (4), *Hypsurus caryi* (1, observed only *Rhacochilus toxotes*), and *Damalichthys vacca* (3).
- **Dispersal:** The authors suspect *B. aletes* reached Guadalupe Island on drifting kelp (*Macrocystis*, *Pelagophycus*, *Nereocystis*), contrary to Tarp's vicariance explanation, since Guadalupe is an oceanic island.

## Key Findings

**Life history**
- Males of *Brachyistius* do not mature before birth, unlike *Micrometrus minimus* and *Amphigonopterus aurora*, whose males mature before birth and are unique among acanthopterygian fishes in this respect.
- Both sexes mature late in the first year; females first give birth at one year of age.
- Birth occurs at a standard length of about 32–33 mm, chiefly in spring, rarely as late as August.
- By August 1, young-of-the-year reach about 50–75 mm (modal about 60 mm); yearlings reach about 85–105 mm; the largest specimen was 114 mm.
- Growth after the natal annulus is about 0.5 mm per day; the growth increment from first to second summer is about 33 mm.
- Few individuals likely survive to two years.

**Sexual variation and meristics**
- Dorsal and anal ray numbers are essentially identical between sexes (150 males, 200 females), unlike the micrometrins.
- Mean dorsal spines 8.51, dorsal soft-rays 14.22, anal soft-rays 22.78 (n = 350).
- Anal spines are invariably 3; principal caudal rays 7+7 in all 346 counted; pelvic formula I,5 in 349 of 350 (one specimen had I,4 on the left).
- Seasonal analysis of 308 young-of-the-year: larger (earlier-born) fish have more rays, consistent with cooler development temperatures.
- Correlations: dorsal spines vs. dorsal soft-rays r = −0.47 ± 0.04; total dorsal vs. total anal rays r = +0.38 ± 0.05; dorsal vs. anal soft-rays r = +0.24 ± 0.05. Chi-square tests of low/high count association were all significant (P < 0.01).

**Morphology and taxonomy**
- Teeth are conic rather than broad tricuspid incisors; the micrometrins lack the front-jaw canine specialization seen in *Brachyistius*.
- Lacks the concave oval depression above the anal fin found in the micrometrins.
- Fins and spines are more delicate; males show sex-limited thickening of anal fin rays.
- Recommended classification: divide [[Embiotocidae]] into subfamilies Amphistichinae and Embiotocinae; within Embiotocinae recognize tribes Embiotocini (including *Brachyistius*), Micrometrini (*Micrometrus* and *Amphigonopterus*, each monotypic), and Hysterocarpini (freshwater *Hysterocarpus*).

**Conclusion:** *Brachyistius* should not be synonymized with *Micrometrus*; its closest relative appears to be *Cymatogaster*.
