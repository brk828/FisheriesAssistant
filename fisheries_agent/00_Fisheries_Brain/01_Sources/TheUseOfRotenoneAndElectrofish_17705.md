# The use of rotenone and electrofishing in surveying small streams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #freshwater-ecology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary
This 1963 study, conducted as part of a fish management program in the [[Allegheny National Forest]], compares the sampling efficiency of **electrofishing** versus **rotenone** treatment for estimating [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) population density in seven small Pennsylvania streams. Using a mark-recapture design (electrofishing with fin-clipping on day one, followed by rotenone kill-sampling on day two), the authors found that rotenone consistently captured a higher percentage of the trout population than electrofishing, though both methods showed substantial and unpredictable variability across streams. The paper also details a practical field protocol for neutralizing rotenone in flowing water using potassium permanganate, a technique with direct implications for minimizing downstream fish kill during stream survey operations.

## Background & Description
- **Primary study species:** [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), sampled across size classes from 2 to 8 inches.
- Capture efficiency by size class was examined; unlike prior studies (Cooper 1952; McFadden 1961) showing higher capture rates for larger fish via electrofishing, this study found **brook trout of different sizes (2–5 inches) were approximately equally susceptible to capture** — likely an artifact of small stream size and narrow size range encountered.
- Electrofishing was consistently **less efficient than rotenone across all size classes** (Table 1 summary):
  - 2 in: 52% (electrofishing) vs. 63% (rotenone)
  - 3 in: 45% vs. 80%
  - 4 in: 45% vs. 80%
  - 5 in: 62% vs. 80%
  - Smaller sample sizes for 6–8 inch fish rendered those percentages less reliable.
- Associated non-target species in [[Cherry Run]] included a mixed assemblage of 11 species, with [[Blacknose Dace]] (*Rhinichthys atratulus*) being the only abundant secondary species; [[Mottled Sculpin]] (*Cottus bairdi*) was a common associate in 4 of 7 streams, though not quantitatively sampled.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Small headwater streams (960–2,800 ft test sections; 5–6 ft average width; 0.12–0.39 acres) within the Allegheny River drainage.
- **Substrate:** Large stones and broken shale, with minor sand/silt — indicative of moderate-to-steep gradient streams (100–300 ft/mile).
- **Water chemistry:** Low total alkalinity (12–38 ppm, mostly <20 ppm) and near-neutral pH (6.8–7.3), consistent with soft, infertile waters derived from sandstone-shale geology (per [[Moyle 1956]]) supporting sparse plant and invertebrate communities.
- **Watershed size:** All streams drained less than 3 square miles.
- **Community structure:** Streams were deliberately selected for near-monotypic brook trout populations, avoiding [[Brown Trout]], suckers, and minnows; only [[Cherry Run]] deviated, hosting a diverse 11-species assemblage alongside brook trout.
- **Population density:** Estimated standing crop ranged from 15 to 144 lbs of trout per acre across the seven streams (Table 2), with [[Crystal Run]] showing markedly higher density (144 lbs/acre) than others.
- **Biotic interference:** Crayfish populations in some streams were noted as potential scavengers of rotenone-killed fish, possibly reducing apparent rotenone sampling efficiency (crayfish were unaffected by rotenone concentrations used).

## Key Findings
1. **Rotenone outperformed electrofishing** in capture efficiency in all seven streams, with rotenone capturing 59–90% of estimated trout populations versus 34–66% for electrofishing (Table 2).
2. **High inter-stream variability** in capture efficiency for both methods was observed despite similar stream characteristics, undermining the validity of estimating fish standing crop from a single sample based on subjective assessment of stream features (size, cover, turbidity, bottom type).
3. **Mark-recapture methodology** (electrofishing day 1, rotenone day 2) is recommended over single-method sampling for accurate population estimates; single samples are still useful for assessing species composition or eradication efficacy.
4. **Potassium permanganate** effectively neutralizes rotenone in flowing water when applied at sufficient concentration to visibly color the water purple, halting fish mortality downstream of the application point without need for blocking seines in these small streams.
5. **Practical dosing guidance:** At water temperatures of 55–75°F, 2 oz of 5% emulsified rotenone per cubic foot per second of flow produced effective kills, neutralizable with approximately 2 lbs of permanganate crystals — a simplified protocol suitable for streams under 30 feet wide, avoiding the need for resistivity-meter-based concentration monitoring (cf. [[Lennon 1959]]).
6. Minimal marked-fish movement out of test sections was detected, suggesting blocking seines are not strictly necessary in small, low-flow streams, though the authors note recent experience favors using drift nets during rotenone operations to improve fish recovery.
7. Findings, while specific to brook trout, are proposed as broadly **applicable to other stream fish populations** for survey methodology design.
