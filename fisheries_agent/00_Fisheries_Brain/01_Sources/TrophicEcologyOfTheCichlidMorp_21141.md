# Trophic Ecology of the Cichlid Morphs of Cuatro Ciénegas, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This M.S. thesis in zoology (University of Maine at Orono, May 1982, by David C. Smith) examines the feeding ecology of the endemic [[Cichlasoma]] morphs of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico. Field observations and gut analyses were conducted over three consecutive summers. The two major morphs, the [[Papilliform Morph]] and the [[Molariform Morph]], differ strongly in pharyngeal and gut anatomy, yet their diets overlap substantially. The author argues that the molluscivore "tracking" hypothesis for maintaining the [[Trophic Polymorphism]] is not supported by summer diet data, and that snails may instead serve as a competitive refuge or that the polymorphism is a historical artifact.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic status.** The Cuatro Ciénegas cichlids were recognized by evolutionary biologists after their discovery by E.G. Marsh, Jr. in 1939. Earlier authors proposed three species (Taylor and Minckley 1966), then four (LaBounty 1974). Biochemical work found near-identical isoenzymes between the two major morphs (Kornfield and Koehn 1975). Sage and Selander (1975) proposed a single species with a genetically controlled trophic polymorphism, and the author cites later reproductive evidence (Kornfield et al., in press) of random mating between morphs.

**Morphology.** The morphs are externally indistinguishable and differ only internally:
- Papilliform fish have fine villiform pharyngeal teeth and a narrow lower pharyngeal element.
- Molariform fish have broad, flat pharyngeal molars, hypertrophied branchial musculature, and a broader, saddle-shaped neurocranial articulation.
- Gut length differs significantly. Papilliforms average about 3.3 × standard length (SL; range 2.5–4.5), and molariforms about 1.6 × SL (range 1.0–2.5).
- Intermediate individuals, with enlarged central molars bearing pointed cusps, made up less than 5% of the author's collections.

**Associated snail fauna.** The principal snail prey are endemic gastropods: [[Mexipyrgus]] (six species in the basin, copraphagous, 4–7 mm), [[Mexithauma]] (one species, up to 8 mm, highly sculptured), and [[Nymphophilus]] (one species, up to 10 mm, associated with waterlilies).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study localities.** Three sites were sampled:
- [[Laguna Churince]]: the smallest and most eutrophic site, with about 50% of the bottom vegetated, depth 0.5–3.0 m, and no waterlilies.
- [[Pozos de la Becerra]]: sparsely vegetated (20% or less), disturbed by a 1964 canal that lowered water levels by more than a meter, and about 5 m deep at the center.
- [[Laguna El Mojarral]]: the largest study area, with about 40% of the bottom vegetated, dominated by *Nymphaea* beds, and deep flocculent detritus at 5–6 m.

All sites share a [[Detrital Aggregate]] of fish and snail feces, crushed shells, decaying plant matter, and associated microbes.

**Vegetation note.** The source describes *Utricularia* as "stoneworts" and *Chara* as "blue-green algae." These labels appear to be mix-ups, since *Utricularia* is a bladderwort and *Chara* is a stonewort.

**Historical setting.** The author cites evidence that local aquatic habitats have persisted since the early Tertiary, with stable basin-floor conditions through the Quaternary (Meyer 1973). Long isolation is supported by the high endemism of the basin's fauna. Snail shells are unusually hard and sculptured for freshwater gastropods, which the author links to possible coevolution with molluscivorous cichlids.

## Key Findings

1. **Diets overlap strongly.** Pooled 1979 data gave a Schoener diet-overlap index of α = 0.85. Locality values were 0.58 (Churince), 0.77 (Becerra), and 0.58 (El Mojarral). Overlap is generally considered significant above 0.60.

2. **Habitat-specific divergence at El Mojarral (1980).** Overlap was α = 0.74 in deep flocculent sediment but only α = 0.44 on travertine ledge, where molariforms ate more snails and insect larvae (chironomids, odonates). Pooled across habitats, overlap was 0.65, which masks the divergence.

3. **Snails are not a dominant summer diet item.** Snail prey were consistently low in summer guts. In contrast, a March 1975 sample (12 fish per morph) found snail shells in nearly 60% of molariforms but only 8% of papilliforms.

4. **Feeding behavior is similar.** Fish foraged at comparable substrate depths and winnowed sediment at similar rates. Molariforms had a marginally longer time between feeding bouts (6.15 s vs. 4.64 s, P = 0.10, one-sided, Table 1).

5. **Gut passage rates differ marginally.** In laboratory trials, papilliforms passed food at 4.10 ± 0.34 cm/h (n = 30) and molariforms at 3.03 ± 0.17 cm/h (n = 17). The difference was marginally significant (0.05 < P < 0.10). The author suggests slower passage may aid digestion in the longer papilliform gut.

6. **Gut length is size-dependent.** Regression equations were GL = −8.736 + 0.300 SL (papilliform) and GL = −2.297 + 0.192 SL (molariform). The slopes differed significantly (F = 10.58, P between 0.005 and 0.010). The lines intersect near 60 mm SL, so gut lengths are similar in juveniles.

7. **Snail patchiness and shell defense.** Mexipyrgus densities in the top 2 cm of detritus ranged from 280 to 3,120 per m² across five microhabitats. Snails showed nocturnal vertical migration and none moved by day. Crushing trials compared the three endemic snails with the marine periwinkle [[Littorina Littorea]].

8. **Diet conclusions.** The author rejects the hypothesis that molariforms specialize on snails and track snail abundance in summer. Three alternatives are proposed: snails as a competitive refuge, a historical polymorphism, or a time lag in morph response. The author notes that a seasonal study would be needed to test these ideas.

9. **Methodological cautions.** The author notes that 36% of stomachs were empty, which may reflect sampling or rapid evacuation. Scale counts may be inflated by trap-net agonism. One female guarding eggs had 116 scales in her stomach. The Schoener index is sensitive to habitat-specific sampling, so researchers should sample homogeneous habitats separately.

10. **Relevance to niche theory.** The author cites Liem's view that anatomical specialization need not narrow the diet, and that the most specialized cichlids are "jacks-of-all-trades." This connects the case to broader theories of [[Niche Theory]] and [[Optimal Foraging Theory]], which predicted greater specialization over time in this stable system.
