## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific research paper** but a **Request for Proposal (RFP)** issued jointly by the Arizona Department of Water Resources, the Colorado River Board of California, and the Colorado River Commission of Nevada. Its purpose is to solicit a feasibility assessment study on management alternatives for reconciling [[Colorado River]] water and power operations with obligations under the federal [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The RFP centers on four federally listed endangered fish species native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], three of which occur specifically in the Lower Basin: the [[Razorback Sucker]], the [[Bonytail Chub]], and the [[Humpback Chub]]. A fourth species (implied to be the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], though not explicitly named in the OCR text) is noted as inhabiting other portions of the basin. The document contains no direct morphological, physiological, or population-level biological data; rather, it frames these species within a regulatory and conservation-planning context, referencing potential development of a multi-species [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] (HCP) or a Recovery Implementation Plan (RIP).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/Range**: All three explicitly named species — [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]] — inhabit the mainstream **Lower Colorado River Basin**, encompassing waters shared by Arizona, California, and Nevada.
- **Critical Habitat Designation**: The U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service ("the Service") has designated **critical habitat** for four endangered fish species across portions of both the **Lower and Upper Colorado River basins**, indicating a broader basin-wide distribution for at least one of the four species not covered in the Lower Basin.
- **Regulatory/Ecosystem Context**: The Lower Colorado River Basin supports approximately **22 federally/State-listed threatened or endangered species** (including invertebrates, fish, reptiles, amphibians, birds, mammals, and plants), with an additional **~56 candidate species** likely to be listed in the near future. This underscores the [[Lower Colorado River]] as a broader biodiversity hotspot facing cumulative regulatory pressure, not just a fish-specific concern.
- **Anthropogenic Pressures**: The species' critical habitat overlaps substantially with water and power infrastructure and operations, creating recurring conflict points requiring [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the ESA for actions ranging from facility maintenance to new water/power project development.
- **Conservation Planning Models**: The document references existing RIP frameworks developed for the **Upper Basin**, the **San Juan River**, and the **Platte River** as potential templates, alongside a **Lower Basin Native Fish Management Plan** stated to be in development by the Service at the time of writing.

## Key Findings

- Three endangered fish species — [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]] — are confirmed inhabitants of the Lower Colorado River Basin; a fourth listed species (unnamed in text, likely [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]) has designated critical habitat in the Upper Basin.
- Critical habitat designation for these species increases the likelihood of triggering additional [[Section 7 Consultation]] requirements for federally permitted, funded, or approved water/power projects along the Colorado River corridor.
- Three management alternatives are proposed for feasibility evaluation:
  1. **No Action** — continuation of case-by-case Section 7 consultations without an overarching program.
  2. **Recovery Implementation Plan (RIP)** — a comprehensive Lower Basin plan for listed species used as a substitute for "reasonable and prudent alternatives" in individual consultations, modeled on Upper Basin, San Juan River, and Platte River precedents.
  3. **Multi-species Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP)** — an ecosystem-based, basin-wide conservation approach under ESA Section 7 or 10, including "incidental take" authorization for both listed and candidate species.
  4. A fourth "Other Alternatives" category is left open, potentially including program-level, multi-year Section 7 consultations for recurring actions like facility maintenance.
- The assessment scope explicitly calls for a **biological assessment** and a **summary of biological requirements/life history information** for the relevant species, indicating that detailed species biology was intended to be compiled in a *subsequent* deliverable/report — not within this RFP itself.
- The broader regulatory motivation stems from concern among Lower Division water/power users (Arizona, California, Nevada) that critical habitat designations and potential future listings (of both aquatic and terrestrial species) will increase consultation burdens and operational conflicts.
- No population counts, abundance trends, reproductive data, or direct threats analysis (e.g., habitat fragmentation, non-native species competition, flow alteration) are detailed in this text, despite these being standard concerns for these taxa in the broader literature on [[Colorado River Fish Conservation]].
