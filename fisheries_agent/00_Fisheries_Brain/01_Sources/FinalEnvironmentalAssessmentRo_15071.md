# Roosevelt Diversion Dam Modifications for Public Safety: Finding of No Significant Impact

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) issued by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Reclamation) for [[Salt River Project]]'s (SRP) proposed modification of the [[Roosevelt Diversion Dam]] on the Salt River, Gila County, Arizona. The selected action, Alternative 2, builds a stepped concrete apron on the downstream face of the dam to remove the hydraulic jump implicated in fatalities, while retaining the dam as a fish barrier. Dam removal (Alternative 1) was dropped after objections centered on native fishes, the [[Bald Eagle]] and the catfish fishery. The FONSI rests on the final [[Environmental Assessment]] (June 1989) and on public comments on the revised draft (April 24, 1989 release, 30-day comment period).

## Background & Description

- **Structure:** Completed in 1906 by the U.S. Reclamation Service to divert flow to a hydroelectric powerplant that built [[Theodore Roosevelt Dam]]. The dam is about 400 ft long and 7 ft high, built on alluvium rather than bedrock, with a 15 ft apron. A 230 ft section washed out in January 1916, and the dam was rebuilt and modified in 1937 with an extended apron and baffle blocks. It was withdrawn from service in 1955.
- **Safety record:** Five injuries and five fatalities since 1979, all recreation-associated. Three incidents occurred (13 April 1979, 20 April 1979, 2 July 1985). Warning signs and buoys were installed in 1981, 1982 and 1986, but hazards persisted, with popular recreation and disregard for warnings cited as contributing factors.
- **Legal context:** A December 1985 Reclamation letter noted that the 9th Circuit Court of Appeals found public safety responsibility at the dam does not rest with the United States.
- **Location:** Within the [[Tonto National Forest]], about 85 miles northeast of Phoenix, roughly one mile downstream of the State Route 288 bridge and at the upper end of Roosevelt Lake.
- **Process:** SRP formed a Task Force in May 1986, expanding the list to 17 alternatives. Four viable alternatives were carried forward: (1) demolition, (2) stepped apron with barriers/signs, (3) about 2,000–2,500 linear feet of chain-link fencing with a log boom, and (4) No Action.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Vegetation and habitat.** The project area covers about 45 acres of vegetation (Salt Cedar-mixed scrub about 28 acres, Honey Mesquite about 6 acres, Paloverde-mixed cactus about 11 acres) and about 25 acres of nonvegetated channel. Alternative 2 would result in total loss of about 0.5 acre and disturbance of about 0.3 acre of cactus community from barriers.

**Fish community.** Fishes are typical of central Arizona reservoir and riverine habitats. Introduced species dominate both Roosevelt Lake and the river below the dam (common carp, bigmouth buffalo, largemouth bass, black crappie, threadfin shad, catfish). Key findings from a May 1986 AGFD survey (112 fish sampled):
- 60% (67/112) were introduced species; channel catfish were the most prevalent.
- Flathead catfish made up 8% (9/112), including fish over nine pounds.
- Sonora Sucker was the most frequently sampled native fish above the dam.
- A March 1988 electrofishing study found no bigmouth buffalo above the dam versus 28 individuals below it, consistent with the dam acting as a barrier.

**Special status species.**
- [[Bald Eagle]]: the only federally listed endangered species identified in the area; a Pinal Creek breeding pair may forage below the dam on concentrated spawning fish. No construction is permitted November 1 through May 31 without U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) authorization.
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]: experimentally introduced in the upper Salt River. They are not fully protected under the Endangered Species Act's experimental population provisions.
- Desert tortoise and Gila monster: probable limited numbers; both Arizona State-listed.

**Hydrology and sediment.** The Salt River watershed is 4,306 square miles, with an average discharge of 886 cfs over a 70-year record (117,000 cfs peak in 1941; 59 cfs minimum in 1955). The dam formed a sand bar upstream that serves as a river take-out; removal would mobilize sediment and affect the USGS gauge.

**Other fauna.** Mule deer, javelina, beaver (probably present, per AGFD), muskrat, and many birds and reptiles are listed in the vertebrate tables (about 16 mammals, 16 birds, 9 herptiles and 20 fishes in total species checklist).

**Land use.** Private irrigation diversion in the north abutment serving about 80 irrigated acres; the parcels are within the historical Salt River floodplain.

## Key Findings

1. **Selected action:** Alternative 2 (stepped concrete apron with barriers and/or signs). Reclamation concluded it will not significantly impact the environment with the mitigation measures in the final EA.
2. **Fish barrier retained:** The dam keeps non-native fish from moving upstream of Roosevelt Lake. FWS and AGFD wanted the structure retained to support native fish reintroduction and the catfish fishery below the dam.
3. **Hazard reduction:** The stepped apron dissipates the hydraulic jump responsible for fatalities, moving swept-over persons into shallower water. It does not totally eliminate risk; barrier access may remain.
4. **Eagle protection:** No effect on endangered species. Dam removal was dropped partly because evidence was insufficient to predict effects on eagles; Section 7 consultation was suspended as a result.
5. **Management:** An operation and maintenance agreement transfers care of the dam from SRP to FWS and AGFD in perpetuity; Reclamation retains ownership.
6. **Cultural resources:** The dam was documented under HAER in 1984. An unexpected-discovery clause requires work to stop pending assessment.
7. **Recreation:** Rafters and tubers lose the existing take-out during construction (about two months); an interim take-out upstream will be provided after completion. A permanent take-out is planned under [[Central Arizona Project]] Plan 6, likely not before 1992–1993.
8. **Disposal rejected options:** Of the debris disposal options (about 18,000 cubic yards for demolition), most were dropped; off-Forest disposal and an on-site parking lot remained under consideration.
9. **Public comment:** Nine letters on the revised draft (five requiring response) raised concerns about rafter egress, landowner water rights and flooding, and a mammal (muskrat) observation. No objections to the proposed action were received.
10. **Agency support:** FWS (Ecological Services), the State Historic Preservation Officer, Tonto National Forest and the Arizona State Clearinghouse endorsed the selected alternative or raised no objection.
11. **Outstanding concerns:** A fish specialist (W.L. Minckley, Arizona State University) argued a FONSI was inappropriate, citing risks to native fish, reintroduced species and the Bald Eagle; the landowners sought guarantees of irrigation water access and flood protection.
