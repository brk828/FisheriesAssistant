# Okaloosa Darter (*Etheostoma okaloosae*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
The **[[Okaloosa Darter]]** (*Etheostoma okaloosae*) is a small, federally endangered percid fish endemic to a highly restricted range in the panhandle of northwestern Florida, confined to the Boggy Bayou and Rocky Bayou drainages of Choctawhatchee Bay. First described by Fowler in 1941, this species is threatened primarily by habitat degradation from siltation (linked to poor land-use practices on Eglin Air Force Base) and by competitive displacement from the introduced **[[Brown Darter]]** (*Etheostoma edwini*). Despite its limited range (~243 total stream miles across both watersheds), the species persists in most stream reaches, with population estimates in the low thousands. Ongoing research by the National Fisheries Research Center focuses on distributional surveys, microhabitat comparison, and interspecific competition with *E. edwini*.

## Background & Description
- **Size**: Small to moderate darter; maximum standard length (SL) 49 mm; average adult size 28–38 mm SL.
- **Coloration**: Red-brown to yellow-brown dorsally, lighter ventrally; pigment broken into small dots arranged in 5–8 rows along body sides.
- **Markings**:
  - Well-developed **humeral blotch**
  - 9–10 dark dashes/blotches along midside of body (variable prominence)
  - Thin, non-prominent **suborbital bar**
  - Well-developed spotting on cheeks and prepectoral area (without a prominent bar)
  - No red spots on body in either sex; dorsal fin bears a thin orange-red submarginal stripe
- **Lateral line**: Very slightly arched anteriorly (nearly straight); separated from dorsal-fin origin by 3–4 scale rows; complete or nearly complete, with 32–37 scales (usually 34–36) and no more than 4 unpored scales.
- **Scalation**: Cheeks, opercle, belly, and nape fully scaled; breast fully or partly scaled.
- **Premaxillary frenum**: Present and well developed.
- **Branchiostegal membranes**: Narrowly connected, forming a broad "V" apex below the posterior cheek.
- **Fin ray counts**:
  - Dorsal fin: VIII–X, 10–13 (usually IX, 11–12)
  - Pectoral fin: 12–14 rays (usually 13)
  - Anal fin: II, 6–8 (usually II, 7)
- **Larval development**: Poorly documented; newly hatched prolarva measured 4.9 mm TL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
### Habitat
- Small- to moderate-sized streams (4–40 ft wide), low gradient, with persistent groundwater discharge producing clear, cool, thermally stable water.
- Water temperature range: 12.0°C (winter) to 24.5°C (summer high), mean summer temp ~22.0°C.
- Substrate typically sand, draining sandhill terrain with mixed deciduous/coniferous forest.
- pH range: 5.6–8.4 (subneutral typical; upper extreme considered possibly erroneous).
- Strongly associated with **cover**: submerged macrophytes in sunlit reaches, detritus in canopied reaches.
- Sensitive to **siltation**; abundance reduced in heavily silted stream sections.

### Diet
Based on stomach analysis of 325 specimens (Ogilvie 1980):
- Chironomid midge larvae — 70.3% of diet
- Ephemeropteran (mayfly) larvae — 17.4%
- Trichopteran (caddisfly) larvae — 7.6%
- Minor items: oligochaetes, copepods, ostracods, gastropods, odonates, plecopterans, dipterans (simuliids, ceratopogonids, culicids)

### Reproduction
- Spawning season: March–October, peak in April, minor secondary peak in October.
- Courtship: Male follows female through fine-stemmed algae/macrophytes (*Nitella* sp., *Myriophyllum heterophyllum*, *Eleocharis* sp.); male mounts female dorsolaterally with lateral vibrations.
- Polygynous — males observed spawning with multiple females.
- **Fecundity**: Low; mean total ova = 79, mean mature ova = 29. Likely a **fractional spawner**.
- Egg size: mature ova 2.0 mm diameter; water-hardened ova 2.9 mm diameter (relatively large for darters).
- Egg deposition by attachment (non-pendulous genital papilla); likely one egg released per spawning act.
- Longevity: Typically 2 years, occasionally 3 years.
- Maturity: Smallest mature female observed at 27 mm SL; early-spawned young may mature and spawn by the same fall.

### Interspecific Interactions
- The introduced **[[Brown Darter]]** (*Etheostoma edwini*) is considered the most significant biotic threat.
- First recorded in Okaloosa darter range in 1964 (Rocky Bayou system, Niceville).
- Likely introduced via bait-bucket release or possibly via contaminated hatchery stock from Welaka, FL during 1950s–60s rotenone "reclamation" and stocking efforts.
- Pattern of **competitive replacement**: *E. edwini* dominates in slower-current, detritus-rich reaches; *E. okaloosae* dominates in moderate-current reaches with submerged macrophytes.
- Sympatry occurs in many areas, but *E. edwini* is usually more abundant when co-occurring.
- No established brown darter population confirmed in Boggy Bayou system (only a single 1971 record).

## Key Findings
- **Endemism**: One of the most geographically restricted vertebrates in North America, confined to two bayou systems (Boggy and Rocky) draining ~115,000 acres of Eglin Air Force Base plus ~12,000 acres off-base.
- **Taxonomy**: Originally placed in subgenus *Villora* with **[[Brown Darter]]** (Collette & Yerger 1962); later reassigned by Page (1981) to subgenus *Belophlex* alongside *Etheostoma mariae* and *Etheostoma fricksium* (Atlantic slope endemics).
- **Population status**: Estimated at 1,500–10,000 individuals historically (Yerger 1978); recent (1988–present) surveys suggest population "in the thousands."
- **Primary threats**:
  1. **Siltation** from poor land-use practices (road crossings, sand/clay pits, golf course runoff) on Eglin AFB — documented population crash in Mill Creek linked directly to golf course sediment runoff.
  2. **Sprayfield effluent** from Niceville sewage sprayfields entering Swift and Turkey creek headwaters.
  3. **Competitive displacement** by introduced *Etheostoma edwini*.
- **Listing**: Federally listed as Endangered under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service on 4 June 1973 (Federal Register 38:106), primarily due to extremely limited range.
- **Conservation actions**: Collaborative research between the National Fisheries Research Center and Eglin AFB personnel targeting siltation abatement and comparative ecological study of *E. okaloosae* and *E. edwini*.
- **Research gaps**: Population dynamics, precise mechanism of competitive exclusion (ecological vs. behavioral), and larval biology remain poorly understood.
