# Colorado River Big-River Fishes: Comments on the Upper Basin Management Plan (1984)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1984 correspondence packet, a letter from [[Paul C. Marsh]] (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) to [[Linda Ulmer]] of the California Department of Fish and Game, encloses comments on the draft [[Upper Basin Management Plan]] for the [[Colorado River]]. The enclosure is a set of recommendations from the Lower Basin Native Fishes Subcommittee, chaired by Ulmer. The central argument is that protection of endangered big-river fishes must take priority over mitigation, and that the upper basin plan must be tied to species recovery plans. The authors also object to the [[Memorandum of Understanding]] that frames upper basin management, which they see as favoring developers over the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence:** Marsh's letter, dated November 15, 1984, states that his comments are "entirely appropriate" and calls for "strong, positive recommendations" stated "in no uncertain terms." He offers wording changes to add emphasis.
- **Subcommittee:** The Lower Basin Native Fishes Subcommittee, based in Long Beach, California, writes that the lower basin has experienced "loss of habitat and extirpation of native fishes." It argues that better communication between the upper and lower basins is needed and that its recommendations are "essential for recovery," even if some see them as unrealistic.
- **Species of concern:** The letters focus on three endangered big-river fishes: [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The subcommittee notes that the bonytail is "the most endangered of all the big-river fishes."
- **Regulatory context:** Key instruments include the [[Endangered Species Act]], [[Section 7 Consultation]], and an MOU involving the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). The [[Upper Basin Coordinating Committee]] is named as the body that handles developer consultation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat loss and extirpation:** The subcommittee reports that big-river fishes have been extirpated from many lower basin reaches. It asks for biological and hydrologic evaluation of reaches where extirpation occurred, to determine whether upper basin water development caused it.
- **Fragmentation:** The authors state that river fragmentation by dams and diversions isolates segments and undermines the ecosystem. They recommend managing the river as a connected community rather than as separate segments.
- **Flow and water rights:** The letter questions how major water development projects and state water rights in the upper basin can prevent further decline of endangered fishes. It argues that state water rights that give fish and wildlife low priority are "not consistent with the ESA."
- **Glen Canyon Dam:** Upstream operational changes at [[Glen Canyon Dam]] could seriously affect one population located in a [[National Park Service]]-controlled area. The authors ask that any upper basin plan recognize this population and support its maintenance.
- **Lake Mojave:** Region 2 USFWS captured 10 adult bonytail from [[Lake Mojave]] and successfully spawned them at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Plans were underway to stock the species into the lower [[Gila River]] in Arizona as a nonessential experimental population.
- **Refuge habitat:** The letter describes the confluence area of the [[Little Colorado River]] and the mainstem Colorado River as holding the best population of humpback chub in the drainage. The passage is partly illegible, so this description should be read with caution.
- **Reintroduction habitat:** The authors identify suitable habitat in Arizona and the lower Colorado River that could support reintroduction. Upper basin research on habitat requirements should guide the choice of sites and any habitat modifications.

## Key Findings

**Main concerns of the authors**
1. **Mitigation versus protection:** The MOU emphasizes mitigation over protection of endangered species. The authors consider this a "presupposition of development" and argue that the upper basin coordinating committee exists to "ease" developers through Section 7 consultation. They call this approach invalid.
2. **Water rights and the ESA:** State water rights that rank fish and wildlife low conflict with ESA obligations and should be resolved.
3. **Recovery plans:** Recovery plans for the bonytail and Colorado squawfish, along with the humpback chub plan, should be approved and released. They should serve as guiding documents for any upper basin plan. If the plans are unsuitable, the Recovery Team should revise them before the upper basin plan proceeds.
4. **Overall objective:** All plans should lead to the primary objective of species recovery.

**Recommendations from the Subcommittee (nine listed; key points summarized)**
- Maintain the status quo for species populations and habitat in the upper basin until developers can show that projects will not cause losses comparable to those already seen in the lower basin.
- Identify and protect natural habitats in specific stream sections, with reference to the stream-section recommendation.
- Conduct biological and hydrologic evaluation in river reaches where big-river fishes have been extirpated.
- Request basin-wide life history data on big-river fishes from upper and lower basin studies.
- Complete analysis of existing data before final decisions, and ensure that the analysis is finished before population losses occur.
- Consider less restrictive alternatives only if they can be shown to protect essential segments, and give maximum protection to upper basin reaches essential to conservation.

**Supporting programs and positions**
- The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] has had strong success spawning and rearing all of the big-river native fishes. The authors state that its reintroduction programs would need adequate manpower and funding, and that the program took many years to develop.
- The authors call for closer coordination between upper and lower basin agencies as a "positive step" toward a common goal: preventing extinction of big-river fishes caused by ignorance or political pressure.
- The authors support Region 6 in its efforts to protect these species, but they will oppose any plan that emphasizes mitigation over protection.

**Signatories:** Linda Ulmer, Chairman, Lower Basin Native Fishes Subcommittee. Paul C. Marsh, Assistant Professor, Research, Arizona State University (enclosure comments).
