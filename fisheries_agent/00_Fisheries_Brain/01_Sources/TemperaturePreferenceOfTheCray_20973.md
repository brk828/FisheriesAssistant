# Temperature Preference of the Crayfish *Orconectes obscurus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cambaridae

## Executive Summary

This 1978 laboratory study by [[Lenwood W. Hall Jr.]] and colleagues at the [[Appalachian Environmental Laboratory]] measured the acute temperature preference of the crayfish *Orconectes obscurus*, a freshwater decapod of clear gravel-bottom streams in the eastern United States. Crayfish collected from the [[Savage River]] in Maryland were acclimated over a range of 6° to 33°C and tested in a thermal gradient trough. Acclimation temperature shaped preference: animals acclimated from 6° to 26°C chose warmer temperatures than their acclimation temperature, while those held at 30° and 33°C chose cooler ones. The estimated [[Final Temperature Preferendum]] was approximately 30°C. The work supports regulatory [[Thermal Discharge Standards]] for aquatic invertebrates affected by [[Waste Heat]] from power production.

## Background & Description

*Orconectes obscurus* is a large crayfish that prefers clear gravel-bottom streams of the Piedmont and mountain regions of the eastern United States (Meredith and Schwartz 1962). Temperature is a major determinant of organismal life processes, and mobile animals such as crayfish display locomotor responses to thermal stimuli that help optimize survival and reproduction (Reynolds 1977).

Thermal preference data are central to [[Section 316(a) Technical Guidance]] impact evaluations. Temperature preference data exist for more than 100 fish species (Coutant 1977), but data for freshwater decapods were limited at the time. Prior invertebrate work included heat-resistance and thermal-shock studies (Becker et al. 1975; Spoor 1955; Becker and Genoway 1974) and preference studies on *Orconectes immunis* (Crawshaw 1974) and *O. virilis* (Fast and Momot 1973).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collection:** Crayfish were gathered in spring 1976 from the Savage River with seines, D-frame dip nets and A-frame benthic samplers. Field temperatures ranged from 6.5° to 20°C.
- **Acclimation:** Animals were held in 38 or 76 L aquaria and acclimated at 1°C per day to 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26, 30 and 33°C, with a 5-day hold at each temperature before testing.
- **Husbandry:** Animals were fed about 0.01 g of trout chow every two days (not on test days), with aeration maintaining 90–100% oxygen saturation. Photoperiod followed natural spring conditions (11L:13D), and light at the aquarium surface was 1.4–2.3 lux.
- **Test apparatus:** A 3.6 m aluminum trough (about 2 cm water depth), modeled on [[Meldrim and Gift Method]], held twelve heat lamps and 22 thermistors. Cold water from a circulating bath at one end steepened the gradient to widen the selectable temperature range. Crayfish were introduced at their acclimation temperature into a gradient of roughly 9°C above and below it, after a 45-minute orientation period.
- **Behavioral scoring:** Eight crayfish were tested per acclimation temperature. Position relative to thermistors was recorded every minute for 20 minutes via overhead mirrors, and the mean was taken as the preferred temperature. Carapace length, weight and sex were also recorded.

## Key Findings

- **Preference relative to acclimation:** The mean acute preferred temperature exceeded acclimation temperature for acclimation from 6° to 26°C. The largest gap occurred between acclimation at 14° and 18°C. Above 26°C, preferred temperature fell below acclimation temperature.
- **Regression models:** The linear equation P = 0.65A + 9.3 explained 48% of the variation. The quadratic P = 1.6A − 0.02A² + 1.66 explained 52%. No other transformation or polynomial improved the fit meaningfully.
- **Estimated final preferendum:** Using the linear equation, quadratic equation and "eyeball" plots, the estimates were 26.6°C, 32.6°C and 30°C. The authors concluded the linear model underestimated and the quadratic overestimated the value, and adopted approximately 30°C as the final diurnal preferendum.
- **Two effective levels:** Acclimation below 14°C produced selected temperatures of 13–17°C, whereas acclimation above 18°C produced 26–30°C.
- **Comparison:** [[Crawshaw 1974]] reported preference values of 22°C at night and 18°C during the day for *Orconectes immunis*.
- **Physiological basis:** The authors cite temperature-sensitive neurons in crayfish abdominal ganglia, whose firing rates change with water temperature between 10° and 30°C (Prosser 1936; Kerkut and Taylor 1958). They also note that crayfish tend to be more active under unfavorable temperatures, which concentrates them within a temperature band (Crawshaw 1974).
- **Implications:** Preference data of this kind inform the setting of temperature standards for aquatic systems receiving heated discharges, a process described by [[Stauffer et al. 1976]] and [[Richards et al. 1977]].
