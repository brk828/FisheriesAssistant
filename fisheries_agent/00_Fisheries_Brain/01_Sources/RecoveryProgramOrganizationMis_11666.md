# Recovery Implementation Program Organization, Mission, and Staffing Plan (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) planning document, Region 6, Denver, dated September 8, 1994, sets out the organization, mission, staffing, and review procedures for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for four endangered fishes of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The plan results from a year-long review, prompted by a Recovery Program budget expected to average about $10 million per year over the next 5 years and by increased emphasis on large capital projects. It reorganizes responsibilities among the [[Implementation Committee]], the [[Management Committee]], the technical committees, the [[Program Director]], and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], and adds outside [[Peer Review]] to protect scientific integrity.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program is a cooperative effort among the Service, the Bureau of Reclamation, the Western Area Power Administration, the States of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, Upper Basin water users, and conservation organizations. The Colorado River Energy Distributors Association is a nonvoting member.

- **Origin:** Since 1978, Service [[Biological Opinion]]s concluded that water project flow depletions were jeopardizing the endangered fishes. To avoid conflict between resource protection and development, a Coordinating Committee developed the program. After 3 years of analysis and negotiation, a Cooperative Agreement was executed in January 1988.
- **Goal:** Recover and delist the fishes by restoring self-sustaining populations and protecting sufficient habitat. The initial time frame was 15 years, or by 2003.
- **Recovery Elements:** (1) habitat management; (2) habitat development and maintenance; (3) native fish stocking; (4) nonnative species and sportfishing; (5) research, data management, and monitoring (Appendix A).

The governance structure comprises:

- **Implementation Committee:** oversees Service implementation, reviews instream flow recommendations, recommends budget priorities, and operates by consensus. Its membership includes Service and Bureau of Reclamation regional directors, one appointee each from the three Governors, the Western Area Power Administration area manager, and water development and conservation representatives, plus three nonvoting members.
- **Management Committee:** directs the Water Acquisition, Biology, and Information and Education Committees, recommends the annual work plan and [[RIPRAP Plan]] updates, and resolves management issues.
- **Technical committees:** the [[Biology Committee]] (with Propagation/Genetics, Nonnative/Sportfish, Instream Flow, and Habitat/Life History subcommittees), the [[Water Acquisition Committee]], and the Information and Education Committee provide advice rather than administration.
- **Program Director's office:** plans, coordinates, budgets, maintains the technical library and database, and provides committee staff support.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is administrative and does not report fish population data, sample sizes, or biological measurements. Its ecological content is limited to management needs:

- **Flow and habitat:** identifying and acquiring [[Instream Flow]] needs under State water law, reoperating [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]], [[Blue Mesa Reservoir]], and [[Ruedi Reservoir]] to reduce project impacts, and studying reoperation of other Reclamation projects.
- **Physical habitat:** developing backwaters, spawning and nursery habitat, and [[Fish Passage]] facilities, and restoring [[Flooded Bottomland Restoration]] sites.
- **Genetics and stocking:** the [[Propagation Genetics Management Plan]] aims to avoid immediate extinction, maintain self-sustaining populations, preserve adaptive capacity, and maintain capacity for continued speciation.
- **Nonnative species:** curtailing [[Nonnative Species Stocking]] where it conflicts with endangered fishes, studying competition and predation, reviewing sportfishing regulations, and enforcement.
- **Water depletion offsets:** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and [[Section 7 Consultation]], new depletion projects make a one-time contribution of $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion, adjusted annually for inflation, applied case by case.

## Key Findings

1. **Five major organizational changes:**
   - Expanded Program Director responsibilities and staff.
   - Transfer of administration and staffing from the Management and technical committees to the Program Director's office and the Bureau of Reclamation.
   - Technical committees advise the Management Committee rather than administering the program.
   - An outside peer review process for selected plans and reports.
   - Increased Bureau of Reclamation staff to oversee capital projects, including fish ladders, hatcheries, water acquisition, and flooded bottomland restoration.

2. **Peer review:** [[Technical Advisory Group]]s of 3 to 5 senior scientists are assembled, with a chair elected by the group. Scientists competing for Recovery Program funding are excluded. The panels advise on strategy and review work plans and reports. Coordinators provide staff support, and local contact persons supply first-hand technical input.

3. **Product review:** Administrative products (staffing plan, work plans, RIPRAP, annual reports) and issue papers receive at least 30 days of technical committee review, followed by a meeting, then Management Committee review (30 days) and, where needed, Implementation Committee review (30 days). Disputes are documented by the technical committee chair and resolved at the Management Committee level.

4. **Capital projects:** The Program Director's staff leads research, conceptual development, planning, and evaluation. The Bureau of Reclamation takes lead responsibility for construction once the Management Committee approves conceptual plans or recommended flow regimes. Reclamation's [[Capital Project Coordinator]]s handle design, construction, and fish passage, reoperation, habitat, legal flow protection, and propagation/refugia facilities.

5. **Staffing:** The Program Director is a Service employee appointed by the Regional Director after consulting the Implementation Committee. The office is headquartered in the Denver metro area, and field Recovery Coordinators (e.g., Grand Junction) may be used if cost-effective. Funding comes through the annual work plan. Staff generally do not represent any participant on a committee.

6. **Meeting and procedural rules:**
   - Draft meeting summaries are due within 10 working days, with final summaries within 2 months.
   - Agendas are mailed 3 weeks in advance.
   - Background materials go out 14 days in advance (at least 10 days).
   - Committee chairs and vice chairs serve 2-year terms, and committee meetings are open to the public.

7. **Funding credit:** Agencies earn credit toward their annual contribution only for technical committee participation (Biology, Water Acquisition, Information and Education), not for the Implementation, Management, or technical subcommittees.

8. **Budget note:** In FY 1995, the Flooded Bottom Lands Coordinator position is to be funded under Capital Projects.
