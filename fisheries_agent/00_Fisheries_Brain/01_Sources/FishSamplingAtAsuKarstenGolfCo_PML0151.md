# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, May 1995

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

A memo from [[Arizona State University]] staff reports a gill and trammel net survey of the two lakes at the [[Arizona State University Karsten Golf Course]] (ASUGC) on 4–5 May 1995. The survey aimed to assess the status of stocked [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]]. A total of 116 fish of eight species was captured. Only one native fish, a female razorback sucker, was collected. It was released untagged because it was stressed. Catch per unit effort remained low compared with past surveys, and the causes were unknown.

## Background & Description

The ASUGC lakes were stocked with all three native species in September–October 1988, and bonytail were restocked in April 1991. All stock came from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Research Center in Dexter, New Mexico, a facility operated under the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

The memo was written by [[Brian Bagley]] and addressed to interested parties. Field personnel were Brian Bagley, Terry Inman, Scott Lusher, and Patti Sowka.

**Sampling design:**
- Eight nets were set in total, with four in each lake (North and South).
- Each lake received three 300 ft [[Trammel Net]]s and one 125 ft experimental [[Gill Net]].
- Nets were set on the night of 4 May 1995 and retrieved about 10 hours later on the morning of 5 May.
- Netting effort was three times higher than in 1994, when no native fish had been collected from the ASUGC.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The lakes are managed as golf course water features. Their fish community is a mix of stocked native species and established non-native species. The sampled community was dominated by non-native fishes, including [[Common Carp]], [[Yellow Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Threadfin Shad]]. Two grass carp were also captured. The memo does not describe water chemistry, habitat conditions, or invertebrate or plankton data.

The author states that catch per unit effort is low compared with earlier surveys of the ASUGC, but the cause is unknown. No explanation is offered in the document.

## Key Findings

**Catch totals (4–5 May 1995):**
- Total catch: **116 individuals** representing **8 species**.
- North Lake: 57 individuals. South Lake: 59 individuals.

**Species composition (total catch, with percentage of the 116 fish):**

| Species | North Lake | South Lake | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Common Carp]] | 32 (56%) | 18 (31%) | 50 (43%) |
| [[Yellow Bass]] | 5 (9%) | 19 (32%) | 24 (21%) |
| [[Green Sunfish]] | 2 (4%) | 18 (32%) | 20 (17%) |
| [[Threadfin Shad]] | 11 (19%) | 1 (2%) | 12 (10%) |
| [[Yellow Bullhead]] | 5 (9%) | 1 (2%) | 6 (5%) |
| [[Grass Carp]] | 1 (2%) | 1 (2%) | 2 (2%) |
| [[Bluegill]] | 1 (2%) | 0 | 1 (1%) |
| [[Razorback Sucker]] | 0 | 1 (2%) | 1 (1%) |

**Native species status:**
- Only one native fish was captured: a female [[Razorback Sucker]] measuring 401 mm.
- She appeared to be in good physical health but was stressed from capture.
- She was released **untagged**.
- No [[Bonytail]] or [[Colorado Squawfish]] were captured.

**Planned work:**
- The next ASUGC survey is scheduled for autumn 1995.
- Interested parties were invited to participate or ask questions through [[Brian Bagley]].
