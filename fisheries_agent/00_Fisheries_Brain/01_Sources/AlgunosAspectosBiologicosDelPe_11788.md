# Algunos Aspectos Biologicos del Pescado Blanco del Lago de Patzcuaro, Mich. (Chirostoma estor)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 technical report from the [[Instituto Nacional De Investigaciones Biologico-Pesqueras]] (Secretaria de Industria y Comercio, Mexico), written by biologist Aurelio Solorzano Preciado, documents basic biology of the pescado blanco, [[Chirostoma Estor]], a commercially important native silverside of [[Lago De Patzcuaro]] in Michoacan. The study covers feeding habits (184 stomachs examined) and reproductive seasonality (217 specimens examined), and it concludes that the species feeds mainly on small crustaceans as juveniles, shifts to larger crustaceans and fish as adults, and spawns from roughly March to July. The report also notes that the species does not tolerate captivity or transport well, which limits stocking and aquaculture.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Chirostoma Estor]] Jordan, 1879, family [[Atherinidae]] (silversides).
- **Local importance:** Described as one of the most commercially valued fishes of Patzcuaro, sustaining the economy of islands and shoreline communities. Its white coloration, silvery lateral band, and flavor make it preferred by consumers over other lake species.
- **Sampling context:** Fish were measured for total length (60 to 383 mm in the feeding study; 165 to 323 mm in the reproductive dissections). Stomach contents were examined with a dissecting binocular and compound microscope, and the frequency-of-occurrence method (Lagler, 1956) was used.
- **Related species cited:** [[Chirostoma Bartoni]], [[Chirostoma Grandocule]], [[Chirostoma Jordani]], [[Algansea Lacustris]], and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), mentioned in earlier lake studies by Martin del Campo (1940) and de Buen (1944).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Diet and trophic position**
- Feeding study: 184 fish were sampled across 12 catches (June, September to December 1958; March 1959). Of these, 56 had empty digestive tracts, 37 were full, 27 half full, 20 a quarter full, and 44 contained only traces. Stomach-content observations were made on 128 fish.
- Crustaceans occurred in 89.0% of guts and were the dominant food group. Ostracods were most abundant, followed by copepods and cladocerans. *Bosmina* was the most common cladoceran. Decapods of the genus *Cambarus* (crayfish, 120 to 160 mm) appeared in 10 specimens.
- Fish remains (scales, vertebrae) were found in 60.9% of guts; *Chirostoma bartoni* was identified in 14 partly digested tracts.
- Insects occurred in 21.8% of guts, always mixed with other prey. Odonata larvae, beetles, and microhymenopterans were rare.
- Algae appeared in 31.2% of guts, but always in very small numbers (diatoms *Melosira* and *Navicula*; green algae *Closterium*, *Pediastrum*, and *Trochiscia*). Rotifers (*Keratella cochlearis* and *K. stipitata*) occurred in seven samples.
- Plant debris (non-algal, 30.4%) was considered accidental.

**Reproduction**
- 217 specimens (165 to 323 mm; 126 females, 91 males) from 17 catches in 1956, 1957, and 1958 were examined for gonadal maturity using an international maturity scale.
- Most mature individuals occurred from March to July. The reproductive peak was from the second half of March to late May. De Buen (1944) reported a broader spawning period, January to August.
- Spawning areas were not located, so the exact limits of the season could not be defined.

**Captivity and aquaculture**
- Only two females (220 and 235 mm) survived for two years in 8 x 7 x 0.9 m concrete tanks at the Estacion Limnologica de Patzcuaro; dissection showed mature gonads.
- The species transports poorly in live containers and often dies after confinement. Locating spawning grounds would allow transport of embryos. Artificial fertilization is suggested as a feasible route, as already achieved with related species.

## Key Findings

1. **Juvenile diet:** Juveniles (under 100 mm) feed mainly on microcrustaceans (ostracods, cladocerans, copepods), with occasional insects and scales.
2. **Adult diet shift:** Fish above about 150 mm consume more decapod crustaceans (*Cambarus*) and fish. Dominance of larger prey is marked at 200 mm and above.
3. **Algae as accidental food:** Algae were present in 31.2% of guts but in negligible numbers, so the authors conclude phytoplankton is not part of the diet.
4. **Spawning season:** Reproduction occurs at least from March to July, with a peak in late March to May. Immature or spent fish dominate outside this window.
5. **Limited captivity success:** Captive maintenance is difficult; only two adult females were kept for two years.
6. **Management implications:** Stocking larger reservoirs is constrained by transport losses. Identifying spawning grounds would enable embryonic transport. Artificial propagation is recommended as a research avenue.
