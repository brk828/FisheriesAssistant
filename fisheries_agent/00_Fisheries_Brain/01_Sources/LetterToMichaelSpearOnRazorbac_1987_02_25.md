## Executive Summary

This 1987 letter from Dr. W.L. Minckley (Leader, Desert Fishes Recovery Team) to Michael Spear (USFWS Region 2 Regional Director) outlines two proposed reintroduction/supplementation actions for endangered southwestern fishes in New Mexico: (1) stocking large, hatchery/grow-out **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) into the Gila River mainstream below Redrock, NM, and (2) supplemental stocking of **[[Chihuahua Chub]]** (*Gila nigrescens*, also called Mimbres Chub) into portions of the Mimbres River. The letter emphasizes the need for genetic verification of hatchery broodstock prior to any supplementation of extant wild populations, and proposes a marking protocol (tetracycline) to track stocked individuals. This document represents an early, pragmatic conservation-management strategy balancing recovery urgency against genetic risk to remnant wild populations.

## Background & Description

- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**: A large-bodied catostomid native to the Colorado River Basin, characterized by a distinctive dorsal keel. By the 1980s, wild populations were in serious decline, driving reliance on hatchery "grow-out" programs.
  - Source stock referenced: 1984-cohort fish reared at **[[Mormon Lake]]** on the **[[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]]**.
  - Proposed release site: Gila River mainstream, below **[[Redrock, New Mexico]]**.
  - Rationale for using *large* fish: to reduce predation mortality, a problem previously documented for smaller stocked razorback sucker in the Gila River, Arizona.

- **Chihuahua Chub / Mimbres Chub (*Gila nigrescens*)**: A cyprinid endemic to the **[[Mimbres River]]** drainage in New Mexico (with historical range into Chihuahua, Mexico). At the time of writing, only a small, geographically restricted wild population persisted.
  - Hatchery source: **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, holding a captive stock derived from **10 original wild-caught founders**.
  - Genetic status of hatchery stock (heterozygosity relative to wild fish) was **undetermined** at time of writing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Desert Fishes Recovery Team**: The oversight body (led by Minckley) responsible for coordinating recovery actions for both species, operating under the authority framework of the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**.
- **Predation pressure**: Identified as a limiting factor for razorback sucker survivorship following stocking in the Gila River, Arizona — informing the strategy of releasing larger, more predator-resistant individuals.
- **Population fragmentation**: The wild Chihuahua chub population was confined to a "limited section of stream" within the Mimbres River, indicating a highly restricted and vulnerable range at the time.
- **Genetic bottleneck risk**: The Dexter hatchery stock's origin from only 10 founder individuals raises concern regarding reduced genetic diversity (heterozygosity) relative to wild populations — a classic conservation genetics concern for captive-breeding-based recovery programs.
- **Regulatory nuance**: The letter clarifies that accidental take, release, or unintentional influence on Chihuahua chub during stocking operations would not constitute a violation under the Endangered Species Act, intended to reduce stakeholder hesitancy.

## Key Findings

- **Proposed Action 1 — Razorback Sucker**: Stock large (1984-year-class) grow-out fish from Mormon Lake (Buenos Aires NWR) into the Gila River mainstream below Redrock, NM; recommended as a formal request to NMDGF Director Harold Olsen.
- **Proposed Action 2 — Chihuahua Chub**: Supplement/restock unoccupied portions of the Mimbres River (not currently holding wild fish) using Dexter National Fish Hatchery stock; author explicitly recommends **against** direct stocking into areas still supporting the remnant wild population due to genetic risk.
- **Genetic Verification Protocol**: Before any supplementation near wild populations, recommends a statistically valid comparative genetic sampling design:
  - **n = 30** hatchery broodstock progeny
  - **n = 30** wild-caught individuals
  - Comparison to confirm "commonality of genetic features" (heterozygosity/homozygosity comparison).
  - Suggested collaborator: **Dr. Donald Buth (UCLA)** for electrophoretic tissue analysis, noted as already conducting related *Gila* electrophoresis work funded by USFWS Region 2.
- **Anticipated Benefits of Successful Stocking** (if genetic comparability confirmed):
  1. Excess stocked fish would disperse, naturally expanding population range upstream/downstream.
  2. Establishes a precedent for active management of an otherwise "generally neglected" species.
  3. Generates empirical data on the feasibility of reintroduction as a recovery tool for endangered cyprinids.
- **Marking Protocol**: All stocked fish recommended to be marked with **tetracycline** prior to release, enabling later identification of stocked individuals versus natural residents/recruits.
- **Institutional Coordination**: Highlights collaborative recovery infrastructure involving NMGFD, USFWS Region 2, Dexter National Fish Hatchery, Buenos Aires NWR, and academic genetics support (UCLA).
