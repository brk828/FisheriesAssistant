# Draft San Pedro River Management Plan and Environmental Impact Statement

#ichthyology #marine-biology #riparian-ecology #conservation-management

## Executive Summary

The [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) released a Draft San Pedro River Management Plan and Environmental Impact Statement for public review in 1988. The plan addresses 47,668 acres along a 36-mile stretch of the upper [[San Pedro River]] near [[Sierra Vista]] in [[Cochise County]], [[Arizona]]. The primary purpose is to protect and enhance the riparian ecosystem, with pending legislation to designate the area as the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]]. Four management alternatives are proposed: preservation, utilization, preferred (balanced approach), and no action. Public comment period extends to September 21, 1988, with public hearings scheduled in [[Tucson]] and [[Sierra Vista]] in August 1988.

## Background & Description

The study area encompasses 47,668 acres along a 36-mile stretch of the upper [[San Pedro River]]. Most lands were obtained through private exchange, historically referred to as the [[San Juan de las Boquillas y Nogales]] and the [[San Rafael de Valle]] land grants. Since 1986 acquisitions, BLM added more lands through private and state exchanges, extending the area to the [[Mexican Border]].

Work on the draft plan began with scoping meetings held in [[Tucson]], [[Phoenix]], and [[Sierra Vista]] in June 1986, followed by subsequent meetings with individuals, organizations, and state agencies. Legislation was pending in Congress to officially designate these lands as the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The primary purpose for obtaining the lands was to protect and enhance the [[Riparian Ecosystem]] along the upper [[San Pedro River]]. Key issues addressed include:

- [[Wildlife Habitat]] protection
- Sand and gravel operations management
- Recreation use and development
- Protection of [[Cultural Resources]] and [[Paleontological Resources]]
- [[Water Quality]] and [[Water Quantity]]

Two issues were considered but not analyzed in detail: [[Wilderness Designation]] (lands failed to meet requirements) and [[Livestock Grazing]] (prohibited for the 15-year life of the plan to protect riparian ecosystem, with re-evaluation scheduled at plan's end).

## Key Findings

**Four Management Alternatives Identified:**

1. **Preservation Alternative**: Protect and enhance resources to the maximum extent possible
2. **Utilization Alternative**: Emphasize public use availability while protecting wildlife, cultural, paleontological, vegetation, and water resources
3. **No Action Alternative**: Protect and/or improve resources by reducing human activity to minimal levels
4. **Preferred Alternative**: Balance resource protection and public use activities, allowing public use where natural resources are not significantly impacted

**Management Decisions:**

- [[Livestock Grazing]] prohibited for 15-year plan duration to protect [[Riparian Ecosystem]]
- [[Wilderness Consideration]] excluded as lands failed to meet requirements
- Public comment period established through September 21, 1988
- Public hearings scheduled: August 3, 1988 ([[Tucson]], Hilton East Hotel) and August 4, 1988 ([[Sierra Vista]], Ramada Inn)

**Document Availability:**

Draft plan and EIS available for review at BLM offices in [[Phoenix]], Fairbank, and Safford; libraries in [[Phoenix]], Safford, [[Sierra Vista]], and [[Tucson]]; and at [[University of Arizona]], [[Arizona State University]], and [[Northern Arizona University]].
