# Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Lower Colorado River – Laguna Division

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This appendix describes the aquatic habitats, fishes, benthos, vascular plants, algae, pigments, and physical-chemical conditions of the [[Laguna Division]] of the [[Lower Colorado River]], a reach of less than 9.0 km. The division comprises an artificial channel and settling basin, a remnant natural channel, a large backwater called [[Mittry Lake]], and numerous small marshes. The survey was conducted in June 1975 and related sampling in 1975–76. No seine collections were made. Fishes were characterized by observation and by gill-net sets in Mittry Lake. Of 21 fish kinds recorded from the division historically, 16 were caught or observed in the present survey. Chlorophyll, carotenoid, and diatom values increased down the reach, and natural habitats generally held higher pigment concentrations than artificial ones.

## Background & Description

The division lies within the regulated lower river, between [[Imperial Dam]] upstream and [[Laguna Dam]] downstream. Its habitats differ strongly in character:

- **Artificial channel and settling basin:** Bottom sediments were consistently sandy. Depths in the settling basin reached about 7.0 m.
- **Natural channels:** Depths range from 0.5 to 3.0 m. Banks are low, sandy, and densely overgrown by introduced [[Tamarix]].
- **Mittry Lake:** Depths range from 2.0 to 5.0 m. Most habitat is shallow enough for wind-induced mixing.
- **Other habitats:** Most bottoms, apart from the artificial channel, contained a high proportion of black, malodorous, organic material.

Flow in the reach is slow outside the artificial channels. It varies with the operations of local water-delivery and control systems.

The source is an appendix within a larger study. Its contents list includes sections on fishes, benthos, higher vascular plants and algae, pigment analyses, and physical-chemical analyses, with supporting data tables (A–E).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish assemblage.** Observations indicated that [[Red Shiner]] dominated open, flowing water in the channel. [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Sailfin Molly]] were common along banks. Juvenile [[Mozambique Mouthbrooder]] were abundant in quiet, densely vegetated areas. The centrarchids [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and [[Largemouth Bass]] were abundant in deeper, more open backwaters, and mouthbrooders were most abundant where vegetation was dense. [[Warmouth]] occurred in stands of cattail and sedges in water 0.5 to 1.5 m deep. Unvegetated, quiet-water habitats held sparse schools of [[Threadfin Shad]].

**Benthic invertebrates.** Four dredge samples from Mittry Lake yielded oligochaete worms (60% of the total number of organisms) and chironomid dipteran larvae (40%). These averaged 15.5 kg/ha. Qualitative sampling found a general absence of one mollusk (name garbled in the scan) in areas with highly organic bottoms. Large numbers occurred in natural channels on coarser bottoms in current.

**Vascular plants.** Extensive stands of sedges and [[Cattail]] line all natural banks. Cattail is clearly dominant, followed by [[Scirpus californicus]] (bulrush), smaller sedges (*Eleocharis*, *Juncus*, *Carex*), and some stands of giant African reed. [[Spiny Naiad]] is by far the most abundant submergent plant, with sparse stands of hornwort and another submergent. Deeper backwaters generally lacked higher aquatic vegetation.

**Algae.** Algal films were abundant on bottom muds in shallows. They consisted mostly of [[Bluegreen Algae]] and were visible at depths of up to 2.5 m in clearer water.

**Physical-chemical setting.** See Key Findings for values.

## Key Findings

### Fishes

- **Species richness:** 21 fish kinds have been recorded from the division. Sixteen were caught or observed in the present survey (Table L-1 lists all, with 1975–76 captures marked).
- **Mittry Lake daytime gill-net sets:** Five species were taken: carp, black crappie, threadfin shad, largemouth bass, and flathead catfish, in decreasing numerical abundance. Sets averaged 11.5 individuals weighing 5.4 kg.
- **Mittry Lake night sets:** Sets averaged 19 fishes weighing almost 12 kg. [[Black Crappie]] dominated the numerical catch. Channel and flathead catfishes together made up more than 4.6 kg per set.
- **Angler creels:** Black crappie, catfishes, and largemouth bass were the species most frequently observed in angler creels.
- **Native fishes:** [[Colorado River Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] appear in the species checklist. The table includes a note that common names follow Minckley (1973) and that records for fishes not taken in the present survey are from Bailey et al. (1970), unless otherwise modified.

### Benthos

- Oligochaetes (60%) and chironomid larvae (40%) dominated the four Mittry Lake dredge samples, averaging 15.5 kg/ha.
- The mollusk was absent from organic-rich bottoms but abundant on coarse, current-swept natural-channel bottoms.

### Pigments and Primary Production

- Chlorophylls, carotenoids, and computed diatom values all rose substantially from the uppermost division (just below Imperial Dam) to just upstream of Laguna Dam.
- Exchange of water among artificial channels (carrying the greatest discharge), natural channels, and backwaters explains only a small part of this increase.
- Biological pigments rose considerably in the artificial channel upstream of the backwater input.
- Natural habitats averaged higher pigment concentrations than artificial areas in all pigments except chlorophyll c.

### Water Quality: Flowing Reach

- **Temperature (June 1975):** 23.2 to 27.4 °C in 52 samplings.
- **Dissolved oxygen:** Consistently high, averaging about 10.0 mg/l overall, with site averages ranging from 9.3 to 11.6 mg/l.
- **Conductance:** Rose slightly down the reach, averaging about 1,250 µmhos/cm near Imperial Dam and 1,313 µmhos/cm at Laguna Dam. The widest range (1,000 to 1,400 µmhos/cm) occurred at the upstream station.
- **pH:** 8.0 to 8.4 in 11 samples.
- **Turbidity:** Almost always detectable, reaching 45 JTU and averaging 4 to near 13 JTU across three sites. Its presence was linked to operations of the [[Laguna Settling Basin]].
- **Phosphate (PO4-P):** Present in all samples, ranging from 0.02 to 0.29 mg/l. No consistent spatial pattern was evident.
- **Nitrate (NO3-N):** Determined at only one site near Imperial Dam, where concentrations were not detected to 0.15 mg/l.

### Water Quality: Mittry Lake

- **Temperature:** Surface 24.x to 28.0 °C; bottom 20.7 to 26.7 °C. The coolest water was at the deepest sampled point (4 m).
- **Dissolved oxygen:** Only 0.2 mg/l at 4 m. Surface waters held 8.9 to 10.8 mg/l. Bottom waters at 1–3 m ranged from about 7.2 mg/l upward (upper bound garbled in scan).
- **Conductance:** Significantly higher than in the channel, ranging from about 1,425 to 2,980 µmhos/cm across vertical profiles. No clear upstream-to-downstream trend indicated considerable mixing.
- **Turbidity:** Relatively high, roughly 25 to 59 JTU, and highest where carp were actively feeding.
- **Phosphate:** Abundant. Six surface samples averaged 0.13 mg/l (range 0.03 to 0.31 mg/l).
- **Not obtained:** pH and NO3-N data for Mittry Lake.

### Overall Comparison

Data from the artificial channel and settling basin did not differ from those of the long-term sites at the upper and lower ends of the division.
