# Correspondence on Pupfish Taxonomy and Collaborative Research Projects

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence between Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) and Dr. Michael L. Smith (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology) documents ongoing collaborative research on southwestern North American pupfishes and other fishes during August-September 1981. The letters discuss specimen exchanges, field collection results from the [[Green River]], taxonomic work on [[Cyprinodon macularius]] (desert pupfish) and [[Guzman Basin]] pupfishes, and plans for collaborative publications. Key topics include hybridization between [[Gila elegans]] and [[Gila cypha]], geographic variation in desert pupfish populations, and a proposed three-authored paper on [[Guzman Basin]] pupfish taxonomy.

## Background & Description

The correspondence involves specimen transfers and research planning between major ichthyological institutions. Minckley reports shipping multiple specimen series including Gila species (four series), [[Ictalurus]] from Texas and Mexico including Rio Conchos pupfishes (UMMZ cut), and dried Ictalurus specimens from [[La Media Luna]] representing half a series that had been allowed to dry up. The institutions are coordinating on multiple taxonomic projects involving pupfishes and other southwestern fishes.

Smith's response outlines a systematic approach to [[Guzman Basin]] pupfish taxonomy using 26 morphometric and 21 meristic characters. The study encompasses [[Cyprinodon fontinalis]], [[Cyprinodon artatus]], and [[Cyprinodon albivelus]], with 210 cases entered from UMMZ specimens by September 1981. Material from FHNH (including Meek's historical collections) and comparative specimens of [[Cyprinodon nevadensis]], [[Cyprinodon macularius]] and others are being incorporated.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Minckley's field observations from the [[Green River]] indicate complex hybridization dynamics between [[Gila cypha]] and [[Gila elegans]]. No [[Gila robusta]] were collected; all specimens appeared to be [[Gila cypha]] or hybrids. He notes this represents a "mess" requiring electrophoretic data for resolution and cautions against premature conclusions.

For [[Cyprinodon macularius]], Minckley identifies distinct geographic patterns: 1) the [[Gila River]] form (including [[San Pedro River]], [[Salt River]], lower Gila) shows uniformity similar to river populations of [[Cyprinodon eximius]] and resembles eastern Delta populations; 2) [[Quitobaquito]] represents a morphologically distinct subspecies (with "sonoytae" appearing as nomen nudum in a Colorado River fish checklist); 3) [[Salton Sea]] populations differ, possibly from mixing of multiple springhead endemics when the basin last filled, potentially recognizable as subspecies "californicus"; and 4) [[Monkey Spring]] population was differentiated to a "good" morphological species level.

The [[Rio Sonoyta]] problem may require resolution based on morphological data alone, as Minckley lacks material from that system and suspects the population may be extirpated. He plans December 1981 field work in Sonora and [[Baja California]] to collect Delta pupfish and re-sample the upper [[San Pedro River]], [[Santa Cruz River]], and entire [[Rio Sonoyta]] if water persists.

## Key Findings

**Gila Hybridization and Taxonomy:**
- [[Green River]] collections revealed apparent hybridization between [[Gila cypha]] and [[Gila elegans]]
- No [[Gila robusta]] specimens collected; all appeared to be [[Gila cypha]] or hybrids
- Electrophoretic data needed for definitive resolution

**Cyprinodon macularius Geographic Variation:**
- [[Gila River]] drainage form (San Pedro, Salt, lower Gila) relatively uniform, resembling eastern Delta populations
- [[Quitobaquito]] confirmed as morphologically distinct subspecies
- [[Salton Sea]] populations potentially recognizable as distinct subspecies ("californicus" available)
- [[Monkey Spring]] population differentiated to species level

**Guzman Basin Pupfish Research Plan:**
- Proposed three-authored paper on taxonomy, geographic variation, and distributional history
- [[Cyprinodon fontinalis]] suspected to be related to [[Cyprinodon macrolepis]] and [[Cyprinodon alvarezi]] rather than [[Cyprinodon artatus]]
- Occurrence of fontinalis between artatus drainages implies possible Pleistocene sympatry
- Data file includes 26 morphometric and 21 meristic characters
- 210 cases entered by September 1981 covering 10 localities in [[Guzman Basin]] and environs:
  - Arroyo de Nopahechic (Sustillos Basin)
  - Ojo Caliente (Santa Maria)
  - L. Guzman
  - Zaragoza, main stream at bridge (Santa Maria)
  - Springs near Palomas
  - Flores Magon (Carmen)
  - Rio Santa Maria de Bavicora
  - Ojo de los Reyes
  - Rio Santa Clara at Santa Clara (upper Carmen)
  - Rio de la Concha (Papigochic)

**Collaborative Framework:**
- Minckley proposes collaboration on [[Cyprinodon macularius]] manuscript combining historic data, life color information, morphometry, ecology, and pending field operations
- Smith proposes collaborative framework for [[Guzman Basin]] pupfishes with flexible authorship arrangements for new species descriptions
- One month estimated for data entry completion and preliminary analyses
