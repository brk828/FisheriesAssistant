# Natural Hybridization Between Catostomus and Xyrauchen (Catostomidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1953 paper by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Robert Rush Miller]] reports natural hybrids between sucker genera in western North America. Two new interspecific combinations are described, both involving the endemic [[Humpback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and a species of [[Catostomus]]: *C. latipinnis* ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]]) in the upper [[Colorado River]] system (eight specimens) and *C. insignis* ([[Gila Sucker]]) in the [[Gila River]] system (six specimens). The authors argue that the aberrant specimens are hybrids rather than a distinct species, that the name *Xyrauchen uncompahgre* should be deleted, and that hybridization is linked to the recent scarcity of *Xyrauchen* and to the unstable nature of freshwater habitats.

## Background & Description

The Catostomidae (suckers) are among several freshwater teleost families that hybridize commonly in nature. Earlier work had documented hybrids among *Catostomus* species and with *Pantosteus*. The present study extends this to *Chasmistes* and *Xyrauchen*.

**Parental species and diagnostic features**

- *Catostomus latipinnis* has full, strongly papillate lips, a slender caudal peduncle, and a weakly curved anterodorsal profile. Its lower lip lobes elongate with age, giving the vernacular name "flannelmouth sucker."
- *Xyrauchen texanus* is the humpback sucker, with a high, keeled nuchal hump supported by expanded neural spines and interneurals, a flattened breast and depressed head, and a wide, U-shaped gape. It is endemic to the Colorado River system and reaches roughly twelve to fourteen pounds, occasionally sixteen.
- *Catostomus insignis* has a deeper caudal peduncle than *X. texanus* and is the parental species in the Gila system.

The hybrids were identified by intermediacy in meristic and morphometric characters and by the absence of [[Pantosteus]] lip modifications. The authors used the criteria and methods of [[Hubbs, Hubbs, and Johnson (1943)]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat shifts:** *Xyrauchen* formerly occupied main channels and large tributaries of swift water. Damming and diversion greatly reduced its numbers, but it now persists in impoundments, such as Sahuaro Lake, where it spawns. It is extirpated from Roosevelt Lake according to local testimony.
- **Population imbalance:** Local testimony from the upper Colorado indicates that *Xyrauchen* became increasingly scarce. In 1946, only seven *Xyrauchen* were among about seven hundred suckers caught at the mouth of the Gunnison River. Seven of the eight known *C. latipinnis* × *X. texanus* hybrids were collected after this decline.
- **Spawning:** *Xyrauchen* spawns from February into April in the lower Colorado and later in the upper Colorado system. *Catostomus* species spawn in spring. Overlap in spawning sites and timing allows contact between the two genera.
- **Freshwater versus marine hybridization:** The authors cite the unstable nature of freshwater habitats and the chance mixing of gametes in stream currents as reasons freshwater fishes hybridize more often than marine fishes.
- **Phylogeny:** Nelson's work on the Weberian apparatus and opercular series places *Xyrauchen* and *Chasmistes* in the tribe Catostomini with *Catostomus*. *Chasmistes* is interpreted as a lacustrine offshoot of *Catostomus*.

## Key Findings

**Hybrid specimens**

- ***C. latipinnis* × *X. texanus* (eight specimens):** One wholly immature male, 102 mm (U.S.N.M. No. 75992, holotype of *X. uncompahgre*, collected 1889 near Delta, Colorado). One immature female, 256 mm (U.S.N.M. No. 143594, 1947). Six large hybrids, 379–442 mm, from Hideout Canyon of the Green River, Utah (1950). Sex ratio: three males, five females.
- ***C. insignis* × *X. texanus* (six specimens):** Six young, 54–78 mm, from Tonto Creek, Gila County, Arizona (September 15, 1926).

**Meristic results**

- Lateral-line scales in the *latipinnis* hybrids range from 72 to 110 (mean 85.25), which is extraordinarily variable. The parental means are 103.02 (*C. latipinnis*) and 77.61 (*X. texanus*).
- Dorsal rays in the *latipinnis* hybrids average 12.75, against 12.22 (*C. latipinnis*) and 14.50 (*X. texanus*). The authors describe the lower *Catostomus* count as imperfectly dominant.
- Gill rakers in the *latipinnis* hybrids (33–38 in the table) fall between the parental ranges of 25–32 and 44–50.

**Morphometric results**

- Caudal-peduncle depth, as a proportion of standard length, is 0.093–0.099 in the *insignis* hybrids and 0.076–0.083 in the *latipinnis* hybrids. This contrast reflects the parental peduncle depths.
- The gape-to-lip ratio (B/A) is intermediate in both combinations.
- Interpelvic space is extremely variable and not intermediate on average.

**Unquantified characters**

- Scale structure, nuchal keel development, and neural-spine expansion are intermediate. The hybrids show partial hump development, with the bones more expanded than in *C. latipinnis* but less than in *X. texanus*.
- Lip structure is intermediate. The [[Gila Sucker]] hybrids show intermediate pigmentation and melanophore patterns.

**Hybrid indices (0 = *Catostomus*, 100 = *Xyrauchen*)**

- *C. latipinnis* × *X. texanus*: counts and measurements average 43, or 47 without interpelvic space.
- *C. insignis* × *X. texanus*: counts average 30, measurements average 67, and combined indices average 57.

**Taxonomic conclusions**

- *Xyrauchen uncompahgre* is a hybrid and should be deleted; the name is unavailable.
- Crossability is only one systematic criterion. The authors decline to reduce species that hybridize to subspecies or to synonymize genera on that basis alone.
- Hybridization in the Catostomidae does not cross tribal limits. The authors propose that cyprinid group ranks may be too high.

**Management and conservation implications**

- The authors identify reduced *Xyrauchen* abundance as a probable cause of increased hybridization. They also note that the species has declined strongly under damming and diversion.
- Hybridization in the lower Colorado and Gila systems is now likely to be very low, because reservoirs contain few other sucker species.
