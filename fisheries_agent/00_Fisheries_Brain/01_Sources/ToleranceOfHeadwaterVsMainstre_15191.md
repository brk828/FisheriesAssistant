## Executive Summary

This 1981 study by Matthews & Styron compares the physicochemical tolerance of headwater versus mainstream fish species in the upper [[Roanoke River]] drainage (Mason Creek, Virginia). The authors tested tolerance to abrupt changes in dissolved oxygen, temperature, and pH in a headwater cyprinid ([[Phoxinus oreas]], mountain redbelly dace) versus three mainstream cyprinids ([[Notropis ardens]], [[Notropis albeolus]], [[Notropis cerasinus]]), and tolerance to low oxygen in a widespread darter ([[Etheostoma flabellare]], fantail darter) versus two mainstream-restricted darters ([[Etheostoma podostemone]] and [[Percina roanoka]]). In every interspecific comparison, species characteristic of intermittent headwaters were significantly more tolerant of abrupt physicochemical stress than mainstream-restricted species. Additionally, *E. flabellare* individuals collected from headwater populations were more tolerant of hypoxia than conspecifics from the mainstream, suggesting possible intraspecific ecotypic variation. The results support the long-standing but previously untested hypothesis (Thompson & Hunt, 1930) that headwater fish tolerate environmental instability better than mainstream fish, and that physicochemical tolerance correlates with the upstream distributional limits of stream fish species.

## Background & Description

The paper is physiological/ecological rather than morphological in focus; only body size ranges relevant to test design are reported:

- Fish standard length range across all species tested: **27–80 mm** (typical adult sizes).
- *Notropis cerasinus* subsample for oxygen/body-size analysis: 30–70 mm SL, 0.5–1.4 g body weight.
- No significant relationship was found between body size (standard length or weight) and survival time under hypoxia (r = 0.147 for length, r = 0.276 for weight) or under thermal/pH stress — indicating tolerance differences are species-level (or population-level for *E. flabellare*), not size-related artifacts.
- Darters ([[Etheostoma flabellare]], *E. podostemone*, *Percina roanoka*) are benthic and made little use of the oxygenated surface microlayer during hypoxia trials, unlike the cyprinids, reflecting their more strictly benthic morphology/ecology relative to the water-column-associated shiners.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Headwater specialist:** *[[Phoxinus oreas]]* (mountain redbelly dace) — abundant in intermittent headwaters of [[Mason Creek]], uncommon/absent in the river mainstream. Headwaters cease flowing in summer, leaving shallow, isolated, leaf-litter-rich pools subject to rapid temperature swings, poor buffering (alkalinity 6–12 ppm), pH fluctuation (up to 2.1 pH units over weeks), and likely oxygen depletion from detrital respiration.
- **Mainstream specialists:** *[[Notropis ardens]]* (rosefin shiner), *[[Notropis albeolus]]* (white shiner), *[[Notropis cerasinus]]* (crescent shiner), *[[Etheostoma podostemone]]* (riverweed darter), and *[[Percina roanoka]]* (Roanoke darter) — restricted to lower tributaries and the main river channel, characterized by continuous flow, higher buffering capacity (alkalinity 121–222 ppm), and stable, near-saturation dissolved oxygen.
- **Eurytopic species spanning both zones:** *[[Etheostoma flabellare]]* (fantail darter) — abundant from extreme headwaters to the mainstream throughout the drainage, allowing an intraspecific (headwater vs. mainstream population) comparison of hypoxia tolerance.
- **Habitat template:** Mason Creek is an upland Ridge-and-Valley stream (~6.6 m/km gradient, 2–5 m wide in headwaters) flowing over noncalcareous shale/sandstone in its headwaters (poorly buffered) and calcareous strata downstream (well buffered). The Roanoke River mainstream near Salem is 20–40 m wide with continuous flow and gravel/rubble/silt substrate.
- **Community structure implications:** Piscivorous fish are typically absent from harsh intermittent headwaters, and competing species diversity is reduced there — potentially allowing small, physicochemically tolerant species like *P. oreas* to avoid competition/predation by occupying rigorous habitats, consistent with ideas from [[Connell 1975]] and [[Wiens 1977]] regarding reduced biotic interaction intensity in physically harsh environments.
- Comparable ecological patterns cited from other systems: *[[Notropis lutrensis]]* (red shiner) vs. *[[Notropis camurus]]* (bluntface shiner) in prairie streams (Cross & Cavin 1971); *[[Phoxinus eos]]* (northern redbelly dace) in hypoxic habitats; tidepool sculpins *Oligocottus maculosus* vs. *O. snyderi* (Nakamura 1976).

## Key Findings

**Oxygen tolerance (interspecific, cyprinids, 1.0–1.5 ppm O₂):**
- *N. ardens* (n=14): mean survival 19.3 min (range 10–40)
- *N. albeolus* (n=18): mean survival 14.4 min (range 10–55)
- *N. cerasinus* (n=24): mean survival 16.4 min; 3 individuals survived to test end (120 min)
- *[[Phoxinus oreas]]* (n=17): **all survived**; some survived up to 240 min with no ill effects — precluded statistical comparison, but clearly the most tolerant species.
- No significant difference among the three *Notropis* species (F = 1.87, p > 0.10).

**Oxygen tolerance (interspecific, darters, ~1.1 ppm O₂):**
- *[[Etheostoma flabellare]]* (n=11): mean survival 73.9 min (range 20 min–survivors to 240 min)
- *E. podostemone* (n=7): mean survival 27.1 min (range 20–50)
- *Percina roanoka* (n=12): mean survival 21.7 min (range 20–25)
- Highly significant species difference overall (F = 13.43, p < 0.01); *E. flabellare* significantly more tolerant than the other two darters (F = 21.34, p < 0.001).

**Oxygen tolerance (intraspecific, *E. flabellare*, Mason Creek headwater vs. Roanoke River mainstream populations):**
- Headwater population: mean survival 326.6 min among those dying (60 min–day 1 endpoint), 11 survived beyond day 1 (excluded from mean).
- Mainstream population: mean survival 170.2 min among those dying, 12 survived beyond day 1.
- Headwater fish that died on day 1 survived significantly longer than mainstream fish that died on day 1 (F = 5.59, p < 0.05).
- Roughly equal numbers from each population (9 mainstream, 10 headwater) survived the full 76-hr test at stabilized 1.0 ppm O₂ — suggesting the tolerance difference may matter most for short-duration (diel-scale) hypoxic events rather than prolonged hypoxia.

**Temperature tolerance (thermal shock from 15 °C acclimation):**
- *[[Phoxinus oreas]]* showed markedly higher survival at all shock magnitudes tested (T−14, T−12, T+12, T+14 °C) compared to pooled mainstream *Notropis* species (p < 0.005).
- Example survival counts (live/dead) at T+14 °C: *N. ardens* 1/14, *N. albeolus* 8/15, *N. cerasinus* 14/7, *P. oreas* 16/1.
- *N. cerasinus* was more heat-tolerant than *N. ardens* or *N. albeolus* (p < 0.005) but not more cold-tolerant.
- No intraspecific body-size effect on thermal survival detected.

**pH tolerance:**
- Transfer from pH 7.3 → 4.3: no deaths in any species after 14 hr (test discontinued).
- Transfer from pH 7.3 → 3.9: all fish eventually died (by 240 min), but *P. oreas* survived significantly longer than the three *Notropis* species (F = 38.95, p < 0.001).
  - *N. ardens*: mean 116.3 min (range 80–160)
  - *N. albeolus*: mean 81.4 min (range 60–100)
  - *N. cerasinus*: mean 101.1 min (range 80–120)
  - *P. oreas*: mean 168.6 min (range 110–220)
- No significant differences among the three mainstream *Notropis* species in acid tolerance.

**Overall conclusions:**
- Across dissolved oxygen, temperature, and pH stressors, the headwater-endemic *[[Phoxinus oreas]]* consistently outperformed mainstream cyprinids in survival time, supporting the [[Longitudinal Zonation]] hypothesis that headwater fishes are more eurytopic/stress-tolerant than mainstream species.
- The eurytopic darter *[[Etheostoma flabellare]]* was more hypoxia-tolerant than two strictly mainstream darters, and even within this single species, headwater-derived individuals outperformed mainstream-derived individuals under short-term hypoxic stress — hinting at local physiological adaptation or ecotypic differentiation.
- *[[Notropis cerasinus]]*, which penetrates further upstream than *N. ardens* or *N. albeolus* (though not into true headwaters), showed intermediate tolerance (greater heat tolerance, slightly greater — though nonsignificant — hypoxia tolerance), reinforcing a graded relationship between physicochemical tolerance and upstream distributional limits.
- Findings support the hypothesis of [[Thompson and Hunt 1930]] and align with broader ecological theory ([[Connell 1975]], [[Wiens 1977]], [[Diamond 1978]]) that physically harsh, fluctuating environments may have reduced competitive/predatory pressure, favoring species with wide physicochemical tolerance; in more stable mainstream habitats, biotic interactions (competition, predation) likely play a larger role in shaping fish distribution than abiotic tolerance limits.
- The study represents the first reported physicochemical tolerance data for *[[Notropis ardens]]*, *[[Notropis albeolus]]*, *[[Notropis cerasinus]]*, *[[Phoxinus oreas]]*, *[[Etheostoma podostemone]]*, and *[[Percina roanoka]]*.
