# Endangered Species Subpermit and Blanket Permit Authorization for Arizona State University Research, 1992–1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) permitting records that authorize [[Arizona State University]] researchers to take, hold, propagate and study federally listed species. The core item is a subpermit letter from the Regional Director, dated 1992 and valid through December 1993, issued under the Regional Director's blanket permit PRT-676811. The package also includes the blanket permit's general conditions, species-by-species activity lists (mammals, birds, amphibians, reptiles, fishes, crustaceans, plants, insects and arachnids), two 1992 Special Conditions sheets, a 1985 USFWS memorandum on blanket permit procedures, a Utah Certificate of Registration for collecting Virgin River fishes, and a habitat and abundance coding key. The fish content is the most relevant to aquatic ecology.

## Background & Description

The subpermit is addressed to Dr. W.L. Minckley, Professor of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]] in Tempe. It names ten additional sub-permittees working under his supervision, including Dr. Michael E. Douglas, Dr. Thomas E. Dowling, Dr. Paul C. Marsh, Ross J. Timmons and one graduate student. Authorization runs through December 1993.

The blanket permit, PRT-676811, was issued by the USFWS Region 2 Regional Director (Albuquerque, NM). It became effective 5/28/92 and expires 11/30/94. Its stated purposes are scientific research, enhancement of propagation, and survival of listed species under recovery documents. The permit is conditioned on the Endangered Species Act and on NEPA compliance, and it must be used together with valid State permits.

The subpermit's fish authorizations include:

- **Maximum wild-take limits per locality** (in brackets in the letter): [[Gila Trout]] (5), [[Bonytail Chub]] (10), [[Virgin River Chub]] (20 each, 1992 and 1993 year-classes), [[Humpback Chub]] (10), [[Yaqui Chub]] (10), [[Woundfin]] (5), [[Desert Pupfish]] (10), Quitobaquito pupfish (20), [[Gila Topminnow]] incl. Yaqui (20), and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (5).
- **Razorback sucker** ([[Razorback Sucker]]): unlimited capture and release in Arizona for research and recovery, and removal of up to 20 ripe females from [[Lake Mohave]] for egg stripping (no hormone injections). Milt may be taken from adult males in Lake Mohave. Eggs go to Arizona State University for hatching. Wild larvae may also be taken from Lake Mohave.
- **Special Rules species** (not requiring a USFWS subpermit under 50 CFR 17.44, but requiring a valid State permit): [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], Little Colorado spinedace, Sonora chub, beautiful shiner, [[Apache Trout]], and [[Chihuahua Chub]]. For the Chihuahua chub, New Mexico Game and Fish requested no wild take in 1993. Specimens should come from captive stock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] instead.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The permit records show a wide range of taxa in the Southwest. Fishes make up the largest aquatic group. The attachments also list crustaceans (one isopod, *Thermosphaeroma thermophilus*), amphibians (*Bufo houstonensis*; cave salamanders), and many terrestrial and cave species.

Several conditions show how the permitting system protects populations:

- **Mortality triggers:** Operations must stop if 10 razorback suckers die during permitted activity, and the Endangered Species Division must be contacted within 5 working days.
- **Incidental take:** No incidental take is allowed unless specifically noted. For razorback sucker, 2 injuries or deaths are allowed per subpermit, up to 8 fishes total across Regions 2 and 6. Neosho madtom and Virgin River chub have no incidental take authorized. Desert tortoise incidental take is prohibited, with a 24-hour notice to the Office of Management Authority (OMA).
- **Disposal and transfer:** Live wildlife may not be released or transferred without written USFWS approval. Dead specimens may be preserved for scientific use.
- **Handling standards:** Living wild fishes must be handled under professionally established protocols. Marking and tissue sampling must be done in a controlled setting by skilled personnel. Disease transmission must be prevented during invasive procedures.

A separate Utah Certificate of Registration (COR No. 5COLL01380) covers collecting in the Virgin River basin downstream of the Washington Fields diversion, Washington County, Utah. It permits seine collection of 20 young-of-the-year [[Virgin River Chub]], 30 flannelmouth suckers, 30 desert suckers, 30 speckled dace and 30 red shiners for starch-gel electrophoresis, with disposition to Arizona State University. The 1992 Utah COR form includes a habitat coding key, which classifies habitats such as permanent streams with low gradient and silt bottoms, springs, marshes, reservoirs and riparian zones. It also codes abundance (abundant, common, rare, undetermined) and age class (immature, adult).

## Key Findings

- **Scope of authority:** The subpermit ties Arizona State University researchers to a blanket permit, with fishes as the largest group of listed species. Work must follow the species-specific activities in the attachments and stay within the stated numbers.
- **Largest fish authorizations:** Razorback sucker work is the most detailed, with specific gear (dip nets, electrofishing, seines), marking methods (radio implants, numbered tags, fin clips, freeze branding, dye marking), and a 2-per-subpermit injury/death allowance.
- **Attachment content:** The fish attachments list about 30 fish taxa, including native cyprinids, catostomids, poeciliids, cyprinodontids, a salmonid pair, an ictalurid (Yaqui catfish, *Ictalurus pricei*), a percid (leopard darter, *Percina pantherina*) and a Neosho madtom. Most are federally listed. The attachments also list 9 mammals, about 13 birds, 2 amphibians, 3 reptiles, 1 crustacean, about 43 plants, 2 insects and 3 arachnids, along with a general invertebrate category.
- **Reporting obligations:** An annual report of activities, including copies of published and unpublished reports and data, is due to the Regional Director by 1 December 1993. Failure to report can lead to revocation or denial of future subpermits.
- **Administrative guidance:** A 1985 USFWS memo (Chief, Federal Wildlife Permit Office) tells Regional Directors to list only species they expect to work with in the next two years. Public notice for endangered species requires applications about 35–40 days before work begins. Subpermits should be issued only for work covered by a recovery plan or Service document. Surveys with little or no harassment need no permit.
- **Permit constraints:** Taking live fish for research and salvaging dead specimens are allowed. Live release at the capture site is required unless otherwise authorized. Land access still requires landowner or land-manager permission.
