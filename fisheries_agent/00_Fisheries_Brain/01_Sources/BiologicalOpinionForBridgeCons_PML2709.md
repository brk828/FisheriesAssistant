# Biological Opinion for Bridge Construction and Road Reconstruction on the Aravaipa Creek Road

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service issued a [[Biological Opinion]] dated November 1, 1996 (letter of November 7, 1996) on a project funded by the [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]] (FEMA) to repair flood damage from 1993 on the Aravaipa Creek road in Pinal County, Arizona. The project includes a 260-foot prefabricated bridge at Site 1 and reconstruction of about 1,000 feet of roadway at Site 2. The Service concluded that the action is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the threatened [[Spikedace]] or [[Loach Minnow]], and is **not likely to destroy or adversely modify** their designated critical habitat. The opinion sets an [[Incidental Take Statement]] with four [[Reasonable and Prudent Measures]] and 14 mandatory terms and conditions under [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7.

## Background & Description

**Consultation history.** The road has a long consultation record. An emergency [[Section 7 Consultation]] followed the 1993 flood, and an unauthorized earthen diversion dam placed by Pinal County in November 1993 was removed on December 4, 1993. Formal consultation was initiated in January 1994, withdrawn in March 1994, and restarted in October 1994 for a rerouting. A February 15, 1995 opinion on that rerouting was later abandoned for technical and funding reasons. The present consultation began September 30, 1996, and was amended October 31, 1996 to include Site 2. Only the FEMA funding action is covered, because the work lies within the existing right-of-way.

**Proposed action.**
- **Site 1 (southeast corner of section 27):** About 250 feet of road was washed out. The road there had also washed out in 1967, 1978, 1983, and 1993. A 260-foot long, 21-foot wide, one-lane panel bridge would span a stream meander with no piers or abutments in the channel. Work windows were November 1 to February 28, or June 16 to August 31.
- **Site 2 (center of section 26):** About 900 feet of damaged road is to be rebuilt as a 1,000-foot section. Roadway width would be 18 feet, raised 4.5 feet of fill plus 0.5 feet of gravel, using about 3,000 cubic yards of fill. Wire gabion baskets would protect the creek side. No vegetation removal or in-channel work is planned.
- **Traffic:** The 1996 BA estimates 12 to 14 vehicles per day, corrected from the 1994 draft EA figure.
- **Construction logistics:** Heavy equipment is expected to make 410 crossings of the existing low-water crossing over a 65-day period, about five per day. Water for dust control must come from off-site.

**Species status.**
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*) was listed as threatened on July 1, 1986. It is a small silvery fish named for a well-developed dorsal spine. It spawns March to May over gravel and cobble, lives about two years, and feeds mainly on aquatic and terrestrial insects. Populations in Aravaipa Creek differ morphologically and genetically from those in the Verde River.
- **Loach minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was listed as threatened on October 28, 1986. It is a small, slender, benthic fish with upwardly directed eyes. It lives in shallow, swift water over gravel and cobble, and it is absent from habitats with fine sediment filling interstitial spaces. It spawns March to May, with some autumn spawning, lives about two years, and feeds exclusively on aquatic insects. Its eggs attach to the underside of rocks, and males may guard nests. Its range has declined about 85 percent.
- Both species were proposed for uplisting to endangered, but work was delayed by higher-priority actions.
- Critical habitat was designated March 8, 1994, including about 15 miles (loach minnow) and about 18 miles (spikedace) of Aravaipa Creek. That designation was set aside and enjoined from enforcement pending NEPA compliance following a 1996 Tenth Circuit Court decision.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Aravaipa Creek.** The creek is a tributary of the [[San Pedro River]] with a watershed of about 537 square miles. Base flow is about 10 cfs, with a median of 16 cfs. The 2-year flood is estimated at 3,790 cfs, and the January to February 1993 flood peaked near 13,000 cfs. Perennial flow runs about 15 to 20 miles within [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness]] country. The stream is moderate-velocity with gradient below one percent, gravel-cobble substrate, and riffle-run habitat.

**Fish community.** Aravaipa Creek has a relatively intact native fish community, rare in the Gila basin. All native species are federally listed or of concern, including roundtail chub, longfin dace, speckled dace, desert sucker, Sonora sucker, spikedace, and loach minnow. Nonnative fishes are increasing. By 1992, eight nonnative species were recorded, at least four of them reproducing. Predatory nonnatives include [[Largemouth Bass]], green sunfish, and yellow and black bullheads. [[Red Shiner]] invaded in 1990 to 1991 but did not establish a population.

**Riparian and land use.** Riparian vegetation includes cottonwood, willow, sycamore, ash, and seep-willow. Human uses have included grazing, mining, irrigated agriculture, channelization, and diversion dams. A lead mill tailings pile at Klondyke erodes into the creek. The watershed has lost perennial length, and riparian forest has declined. Land ownership is mostly BLM, with six private parcels and a Nature Conservancy preserve.

**Prior consultations.** Five earlier formal consultations concerned Aravaipa Creek: the 1990 [[Bureau of Land Management]] Safford District Resource Management Plan (non-jeopardy), two 1993 to 1994 rip rap emergency consultations (non-jeopardy), and a 1994 [[Central Arizona Project]] opinion (jeopardy, with a reasonable and prudent alternative of paired fish barriers).

**Effects.** The Service expects mixed effects.
- **Short-term adverse:** Heavy equipment in the channel can crush fish and eggs, cause harassment, disrupt feeding, and increase sediment. Construction may compact substrate and destroy point-bar vegetation.
- **Site 1 long-term benefit:** The bridge is expected to end the washout-and-repair cycle. Its clear span avoids piers in the flow.
- **Site 2 long-term adverse:** The road remains vulnerable to inundation and erosion, producing cumulative adverse effects.
- **Toxic risk:** Raw concrete and petroleum products could enter the creek.
- **Cumulative:** Rising recreation, residential development, and bank stabilization threaten both species. Irrigated agriculture is declining.

## Key Findings

- **Conclusion:** Non-jeopardy and no adverse modification of critical habitat for spikedace and loach minnow.
- **Take:** Take cannot be directly quantified. It is indexed to the fish community and project parameters. Take is exceeded if more than 20 dead or dying fish of any species are found in the project area or within 500 yards downstream in any two-week period, if a toxic spill occurs in the creek or floodplain, or if equipment is used in the stream more than 30 linear feet upstream or downstream of the existing low-water crossing midlines.
- **Reasonable and prudent measures:** (1) minimize direct mortality; (2) minimize habitat loss and alteration; (3) monitor the fish community and habitat; (4) maintain complete records.
- **Terms and conditions (14 total):**
  - Minimize wetted-area and point-bar activity.
  - Prevent pollutants from entering surface waters.
  - No construction during spawning periods of September 1 to October 31 and March 1 to June 15.
  - No water withdrawal from the creek.
  - FEMA works with BLM to limit public access to the low-water crossing.
  - Use silt and chain-link fencing; no construction debris or structures in the creek.
  - Protect riparian trees, with priority for cottonwoods and willows, and remove no mature trees.
  - A biologist must be present during construction; dead or dying fish are monitored for 500 yards downstream, with immediate telephone notice to the Service.
  - A written report with before-and-after photographs is due within 60 days of completion for each site.
- **Conservation recommendation:** Install interpretive signs at the road reconstruction site, with draft content reviewed by the Service.
- **Reinitiation of consultation** is required if incidental take is exceeded, new information reveals unconsidered effects, the action is modified, or a new species is listed or critical habitat designated.
- **Overall assessment:** Replacing the Site 1 low-water crossing with a bridge is expected to reduce long-term harm, while the short-term construction impacts are judged acceptable for that benefit.
