## Executive Summary

The [[Welaka National Fish Hatchery]] is a federally operated fish culture station located in Welaka, Florida, originally established in 1926 by the Florida Shell Fish Commission as the **Welaka Fish Cultural Station** and transferred to Federal ownership in 1938 under the Farm Tenant Act. Initially dedicated to rearing [[Largemouth Bass]] for statewide stocking and replenishment of the [[St. Johns River]], the hatchery's mission has since expanded to include restoration of depleted [[Striped Bass]] and [[Sturgeon]] populations across Gulf of Mexico drainage systems, along with multi-strain [[Atlantic Striped Bass]] production for regional lakes and rivers in Florida, Georgia, Alabama, and South Carolina. The station also supports cooperative stocking programs for [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]] on Federal lands.

## Background & Description

*Note: The source document is an administrative station profile and does not provide morphological data on the species reared. Species under cultivation include:*

- **[[Striped Bass]]** (*Morone saxatilis*) — anadromous species, multiple genetic strains maintained for regional stocking.
- **[[Sturgeon]]** (Acipenseridae) — species not specified in source; targeted for stock restoration in Gulf drainage systems.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*) — primary historical broodstock species of the hatchery.
- **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and **[[Redear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis microlophus*) — panfish species distributed to state and private waters.
- **Catfish** (Ictaluridae) — species not specified; included in distribution summary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat systems**: Freshwater pond and raceway culture facilities across two operational units — the **Welaka Unit** (8 ponds, aquarium house, raceways) and the **[[Beecher Springs Unit]]** (33 ponds, holding houses with chiller room).
- **Source/receiving ecosystems**: The hatchery interfaces with the **[[St. Johns River]]** (historical largemouth bass replenishment) and **Gulf of Mexico drainage systems** (striped bass and sturgeon restoration), indicating anadromous and riverine life-history considerations for these species.
- **Regional distribution range**: Reared fish are stocked across a multi-state footprint spanning **Florida, Georgia, Alabama, and South Carolina**, supporting both Federal and State-managed waters as well as private stocking programs.
- **Infrastructure supporting ecological function**: 22-tank aquarium house and 20-tank holding houses suggest controlled-environment rearing for sensitive early life stages (larvae/fingerlings) prior to release into natural drainage systems.

## Key Findings

- The hatchery transitioned from a **State-run largemouth bass program (1926)** to a **Federally managed multi-species restoration facility (1938–present)**, reflecting a shift in mission from single-species stocking to broader watershed-level stock restoration.
- Current mission priorities emphasize restoring **depleted striped bass and sturgeon stocks** in Gulf of Mexico drainages — indicating historical population declines in these anadromous/riverine species.
- Multiple **genetic strains of Atlantic striped bass** are actively cultured for distribution across four states, suggesting a stock-diversity management approach to regional fisheries restoration.
- **Distribution summary (1991)** reported approximately **1,524,000 fish** distributed to "Others" federal-adjacent waters, **1,202,000** to State waters, **13,000** to FWS federal waters, and **30,000** to private waters — indicating State-water stocking as the dominant distribution channel.
- Budget data (1991–1994) show **increasing operational funding** (from $284.0K to an estimated $356.7K) and a sharp rise in **maintenance allocations** (from $5.0K in 1991 to $418.0K allocated in 1993), possibly reflecting infrastructure investment or facility repair needs.
- Staffing remained stable at **7.0 FTEs** across the reporting period despite funding increases, suggesting capital/infrastructure investment rather than staffing expansion drove budget growth.
- The facility maintains dual-site operations (**Welaka Unit** and **[[Beecher Springs Unit]]**), each with distinct infrastructure suited to different production stages (broodstock/holding vs. active rearing ponds).
