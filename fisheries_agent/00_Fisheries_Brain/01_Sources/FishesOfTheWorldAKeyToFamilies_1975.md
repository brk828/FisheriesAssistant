## Executive Summary
This is a critical book review, published in the *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* (1975), of G. U. Lindberg's *Fishes of the World: A Key to Families and a Checklist* — an English translation of the 1971 Russian text. The book is a comprehensive taxonomic manual intended for a broad audience (ichthyologists, fishery students, commercial fishermen, and naturalists), providing identification keys to all fish families globally, alongside a compiled checklist with synonymy, distribution, and commercial data. The reviewer, David W. Greenfield, praises the scope and illustrative quality of the work while flagging significant issues with classificatory currency, key construction, and production cost/quality.

## Background & Description
The review does not describe specific fish morphology in depth but highlights the book's use of morphological illustration as a key strength:
- The book contains predominantly **line drawings** compiled from numerous sources, covering representative members of **all fish families** (not limited to [[Teleostei]], unlike prior works such as Greenwood et al. 1966).
- Illustrations include important **morphological diagnostic features** used in family-level identification.
- A specific morphological identification pitfall is noted: the South American freshwater genus **[[Belonion]]** (family [[Belonidae]]) has only an elongated **lower jaw**, causing superficial resemblance to [[Hemiramphidae]] (halfbeaks) and creating potential misidentification within the keys.

## Key Findings
- **Scope and structure**: The book includes a family list with higher classification (order, suborder, superfamily), a glossary, keys to orders and families, an extensive checklist (with synonyms, local names, genus/species counts, distribution, commercial value), a large bibliography, and multiple name indices (Latin, Western common names, Russian, Chinese/Japanese).
- **Classificatory framework**: Lindberg's higher classification is based primarily on **[[Berg 1940]]**, supplemented by **[[Norman 1966]]** and **[[Matsubara 1955]]**, predating the influential reclassification of teleosts by **[[Greenwood et al. 1966]]**, though notes on the latter were retroactively added to the checklist.
- **Outdated taxonomy identified by reviewer**: Several family placements are flagged as needing correction per the 1973 treatment in *Fishes of the Western North Atlantic*:
  - **[[Gibberichthyidae]]** → belongs in [[Stephanoberyciformes]], not [[Beryciformes]].
  - **[[Anaplogasteridae]]** → belongs in [[Beryciformes]], not [[Stephanoberyciformes]].
  - **[[Korsogasteridae]]** → synonym of [[Trachichthyidae]]; belongs in [[Beryciformes]].
  - **[[Caristiidae]]** → not a beryciform; should be in [[Perciformes]], likely near [[Pteraclidae]].
  - **[[Rondeletiidae]]** → not stephanoberyciform; belongs in [[Cetomimiformes]] (Lindberg does list it there as well).
  - **Kasodoridae** (placed in [[Miripinniformes]] by Lindberg) → based on a juvenile specimen of *Gibberichthys pumilis* (family [[Gibberichthyidae]]) within [[Stephanoberyciformes]] — effectively invalid as a distinct family.
- **Key construction issues**: Some identification couplets rely on locality rather than diagnostic morphology (e.g., "Catfishes of Central and South America"), which the reviewer considers weak taxonomic key practice.
- **Production quality concerns**: Despite a high price ($42.50 in 1974), the binding and paper quality were considered poor, and typographical errors were present.
- **Overall assessment**: The reviewer concludes the book is a valuable **library reference** for unfamiliar fish families and offers a strong bibliographic compilation, but questions its value as a personal working text given its cost and the rapid pace of change in fish higher classification during the 1960s–1970s (citing concurrent work by **[[Gosline 1971]]**).
