## Background & Description

*Tiaroga cobitis* is a small cyprinid endemic to the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona and New Mexico, USA, and Sonora, Mexico. Historic populations have declined because of habitat loss and interactions with nonnative fishes. The species was federally listed as threatened in 1986.

The second abstract, on the biogeography of freshwater fishes, notes that northwestern Mexico forms a connecting link between the continental ichthyofaunas of the Western Hemisphere. Its assemblage results from a varied biogeographic history involving the [[Nearctic Region]] to the north and the [[Neotropical Region]] to the south. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Black River reach:** Loach minnow was collected along a 3.6 km (2.25 mi) reach of the Black River near [[Three Forks]], while the author was making routine fish collections on the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]].
- **Population status:** All size (age) classes were present, including mature individuals of both sexes. The authors state that this presumably represents a reproducing population.
- **Isolation:** The authors know of no other loach minnow occurrences in the Black River system. The nearest population within the watershed is more than 200 stream km (130 mi) downstream in the [[White River]].
- **Threats:** Habitat loss and interactions with nonnative fishes are cited as causes of historic decline.
- **Biogeographic framework:** The second study uses three levels of specimen records to analyze regional fish distributions:
  - Level I: museum specimens
  - Level II: peer-reviewed literature
  - Level III: "gray" literature

  The stated question is whether within-region distributions reflect ecological or geological (historical) factors. The second question is not preserved in the source text.

## Key Findings

1. **New locality:** The Black River, Arizona, population is a new locality for loach minnow. Its nearest known conspecific population is over 200 stream km away in the White River.
2. **Reproductive population:** Mature males and females and all size classes were found, which suggests a self-sustaining population.
3. **Conservation status:** The species is federally threatened because of habitat loss and nonnative fish interactions, and its range is limited to the Gila River basin and adjacent Sonora.
4. **Biogeographic position:** Northwestern Mexico is a transition zone between the Nearctic and Neotropical regions, and its freshwater fish fauna reflects this mixed history.
5. **Methodological approach:** The biogeographic analysis integrates museum records, peer-reviewed literature, and gray literature to test ecological versus geological explanations for distribution patterns.
