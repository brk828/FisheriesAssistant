# Continued Range Expansion in Florida of the Walking Catfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Clariidae #invasive-species #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This short communication updates a prior report by [[Walter R. Courtenay Jr]] on the invasive spread of the [[Walking Catfish]] (*Clarias batrachus*) in Florida. New data from the Florida Game and Fresh Water Fish Commission extend the species' confirmed range by an additional 75 km northward, adding two more counties (Indian River and south-central Brevard) to the previously documented 18-county range. The findings reinforce concerns that *C. batrachus* could eventually colonize up to three-fourths of peninsular Florida, facilitated by an interconnected network of agricultural canals linking to the [[St Johns River]] system.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Clarias batrachus* Linnaeus — the [[Walking Catfish]], family [[Clariidae]].
- **Origin**: Introduced (exotic) species, non-native to Florida.
- **Prior documented range** (Courtenay 1978, *Environmental Conservation* Vol. 5, No. 4): established in 18 counties, spanning the lower east coast of Florida from just north of [[Fort Pierce]] (St Lucie County) to near [[Homestead]] (Dade County) — a distance of approximately 215 km.
- **Updated range (this report)**: Extended ~75 km further north into Indian River County and south-central Brevard County, based on new field reports from David T. Cox (Florida Game and Fresh Water Fish Commission).
- **New confirmed locations**:
  - West and north of [[Vero Beach]] (Indian River County)
  - Extreme southeastern Brevard County
  - A tributary of [[Lake Washington]], northwest of [[Melbourne]] (Brevard County), part of the southern [[St Johns River]] system

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Dispersal pathway**: Range expansion occurred via a series of interconnected agricultural drainage canals running from northern St Lucie County through Indian River County into southeastern Brevard County.
- **Key hydrological link**: These canals connect to the [[St Johns Marsh]], an extensive swamp system that forms the headwaters of the [[St Johns River]] — Florida's longest river (>325 km), which empties into the Atlantic Ocean near Jacksonville (Duval County).
- **Overwintering potential**: The St Johns River system contains several springs that could allow *Clarias* to survive severe cold winters, a critical factor in enabling further northward range expansion.
- **Geographic context**: Melbourne is ~40 km south of [[Cape Canaveral]] and the John F. Kennedy Space Center; Jacksonville lies ~200 km north of Cape Canaveral, indicating substantial remaining habitat available for potential colonization.
- **Habitat types utilized**: Agricultural drainage canals, freshwater swamps, tributary systems, and river/spring networks — highlighting the catfish's tolerance for a range of freshwater habitats and its capacity for overland/canal-based dispersal.

## Key Findings
1. The Walking Catfish's confirmed Florida range has expanded by ~75 km northward since the original 1978 report, now including Indian River and south-central Brevard Counties (2 additional counties beyond the previously reported 18).
2. Dispersal occurred through human-engineered agricultural drainage canal networks, illustrating how anthropogenic water infrastructure facilitates invasive species spread.
3. The species now has hydrological access to most of eastern peninsular Florida via the St Johns Marsh/St Johns River system.
4. Springs within the St Johns River system may provide thermal refugia, potentially allowing *Clarias* to persist through cold winters and continue expanding northward.
5. The findings reconfirm Courtenay's (1978) prediction that *C. batrachus* has the potential to colonize nearly three-fourths of peninsular Florida.
