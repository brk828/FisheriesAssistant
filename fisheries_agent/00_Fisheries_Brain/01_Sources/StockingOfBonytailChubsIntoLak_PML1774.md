# Memorandum on Stocking Bonytail Chubs into Lake Mohave, Arizona (November 1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A November 1994 memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Parker office, addressed to a project leader, Region 2 coordinator and interested parties, reports the stocking of an estimated number of [[Bonytail Chub]] (count illegible in the scan) into [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona. The fish were nose-tagged by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and Service staff, transported in an aerated tub, and released in [[Yuma Cove]]. The fish were heavily infested with a parasite and showed stress during transport, but they schooled once aeration was running and dispersed rapidly after release.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency memorandum (dated 17 November 1994 per the scan header; the stocking date is partly illegible, most likely in late November).
- **Subject fish:** Bonytail chubs, a cyprinid. The document does not give the scientific name; the species is identified here from its common name.
- **Size:** Two to six inches in total length.
- **Count:** Stated as "an estimated" number, but the figure is unreadable in the OCR text.
- **Marking:** All fish were nose-tagged with code 1830 by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] personnel. [[Fish Tagging]] was used to identify the released cohort.
- **Transport:** A 150-gallon tub with an aeration system, carried by pontoon boat to the release site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Release site:** A small cove in Arizona waters between river miles 115 and 116, known as Yuma Cove, in [[Lake Mohave]] on the [[Colorado River]].
- **Prior research:** The Service had camped at this cove during earlier [[Razorback Sucker]] research. The document's OCR reads the species as "Marsh x.azgrbaclk," which is not reliably decipherable, so it is not used here. The cove was therefore already familiar to the Service's field program.
- **Health concerns:** The fish were heavily infested with a parasite. The OCR garbles the name ("lctr"), but it is most likely [[Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis]] (ich). Parasite load is a recognized stressor for hatchery-reared and transported native fish.
- **Native status:** Bonytail are a federally listed endangered species in the Colorado River basin, so the stocking is a conservation action. This context is inferred from the species and location, not stated in the memo.

## Key Findings

1. **Stocking event:** An estimated number of bonytail chubs, 2 to 6 inches long, were stocked into Lake Mohave in November 1994 (exact count unreadable).
2. **Marking:** Every fish was nose-tagged (code 1830) before release.
3. **Transport method:** A 150-gallon aerated tub moved the fish by pontoon boat. Oxygenation was the key to keeping them alive.
4. **Health status:** The fish were heavily infested with a parasite before release.
5. **Behavior during transport:** The fish were initially stressed when the aeration system was started. Once it was running, they schooled in the tub.
6. **Release outcome:** When released, the fish dispersed rapidly into the cove.
7. **Site significance:** Yuma Cove was chosen because of the Service's earlier field camps there, so the site had been studied before.
