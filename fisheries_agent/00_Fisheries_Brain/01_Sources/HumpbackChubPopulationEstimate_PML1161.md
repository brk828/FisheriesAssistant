# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Desolation/Gray Canyon, Green River, Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY2004 annual project report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 129) describes work by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] to estimate the population of endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Desolation Canyon]] and [[Gray Canyon]] on the [[Green River]], Utah. The project has two objectives: (1) obtain a population estimate of late juvenile/adult humpback chub, and (2) test whether a relationship exists between [[ISMP]] catch rates and population size. The report was about 90% complete at the time of writing and was to be finalized in 2005. Its recommendations focus on sampling timing, gear selection, the number of sampling passes, sampling locations, and species identification.

## Background & Description

- **Species focus:** [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), listed as endangered. The [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) recently identified recovery goals that depend partly on maintaining humpback chub populations at several locations, including the Desolation/Gray Canyon population.
- **Project status:** Ongoing. The project supports the General Recovery Program Support Action Plan, item V.A.1 (conduct standardized monitoring program).
- **Principal investigators:** Julie A. Jackson and J. Michael Hudson, [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], Moab Field Station, Moab, Utah.
- **Reporting year:** 2004. Report signed by Julie A. Jackson, dated 11/25/2004.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling conditions:** Water temperature, turbidity, and storm events appear to affect catch rates. Lower fall temperatures probably reduce activity of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and humpback chub, which may lower catch rates and, in turn, population estimates.
- **Sampling gear:** Recommended methods include [[Electrofishing]], [[Hoop Nets]], and [[Minnow Traps]] to capture juvenile humpback chub. A combination of ¼-inch and 1-inch mesh [[Trammel Nets]] was suggested to improve juvenile catch.
- **Sampling sites:** The current Desolation/Gray Canyon sampling locations should be re-examined. The report suggests considering a shift from the [[Surprise Canyon]] site to the [[Big Bend]] site.
- **Species identification:** Morphological characteristics of chub in Desolation/Gray Canyon are ambiguous. The report recommends the full set of morphometric measurements from Douglas et al. (1995) or as updated in Douglas et al. (2001), along with [[Geometric Morphometrics]] and a genetic component, to verify identifications and the degree of intermediacy within species.

## Key Findings

**Project output**
- A final report will present three point estimates of humpback chub abundance in Desolation and Gray Canyons for 2001–2003.
- The report will summarize the three years of sampling and serve as a basis for refining future [[Population Estimate]]s.
- About 90% of the report was complete at the time of writing (the scanned value is partly illegible and may read 90% or 95%). Finalization is planned for 2005, pending approval from the Biology Committee and the program.

**Recommendations**
1. **Seasonal timing:** Account for seasonal temperature effects when interpreting population estimates, since fall sampling may yield lower catch rates and lower estimates.
2. **Gear:** Continue electrofishing, hoop nets, and minnow traps. Add trammel nets to increase juvenile catch.
3. **Replication:** Conduct at least three sampling passes. Several data points help interpret extreme high or low [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPUE) values.
4. **Site selection:** Re-examine sampling locations, with a possible move from Surprise Canyon to Big Bend.
5. **Species verification:** Combine full morphometric measurements, geometric morphometrics, and genetic analysis to confirm chub identifications.
