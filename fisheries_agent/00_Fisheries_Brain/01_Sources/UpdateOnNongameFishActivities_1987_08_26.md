## Executive Summary
This 1987 Arizona Game and Fish Department inter-office memo summarizes nongame fish monitoring activities conducted from late May through August, focused primarily on reintroduced populations of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). Surveys spanned numerous Arizona streams (Salt, Verde, Blue, and Gila River tributaries) using electrofishing, seines, trammel/gill nets, and snorkeling, often in collaboration with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Arizona State University. Key findings include limited but measurable survival and growth of stocked razorbacks in several streams, near-total site fidelity of stocked squawfish in Canyon Creek (contrasting with rapid downstream dispersal observed in the Verde River the prior year), and continued absence of juvenile razorback recruitment in Lake Mohave despite documented spawning. Two native cyprinids of conservation concern — [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — were found to be common to locally abundant in Eagle Creek and the Blue River, respectively, alongside a largely intact native fish assemblage.

## Background & Description
- **Razorback Sucker growth**: Stocked at ~50 mm TL; fish recovered from a Cherry Creek backwater after ~5 months had grown to 125–150 mm TL. Growth in Canyon Creek over the same period was minimal.
- **Parasite infestation**: Razorbacks recovered between Perkinsville and Alvarez Ranch on the Verde River exhibited heavy *Lernaea* (anchor worm) infestations, with some individuals carrying up to 6 attached parasites, including on lateral body wall surfaces. The report notes that stocking fish before scale development is complete appears to increase susceptibility to *Lernaea* infestation, raising concerns about long-term survival of affected cohorts.
- **Colorado Squawfish**: Recaptured individuals from Fall 1986 stockings at OW Ranch bridge (Canyon Creek) showed little growth over the winter months, consistent with reduced metabolic activity and limited foraging in cooler/low-productivity backwater habitats.
- **Juvenile razorback suckers**: Only 3 juvenile specimens (collected by Region III in early July) are documented from Lake Mohave over "the last several decades," underscoring extreme rarity of the juvenile life stage in this system despite adult presence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use — Razorback Sucker**: Consistently captured in quiet margins of runs or in backwaters lacking current, at water temperatures of 19–27°C. In main-channel settings, razorbacks co-occurred with [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*). In the Cherry Creek backwater, co-occurring species were dominated by non-native [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Green Sunfish]].
- **Habitat use — Colorado Squawfish**: Displayed pronounced habitat specificity for quiet, shallow backwater or edge areas in Canyon Creek. Notably, these fish exhibited negligible downstream movement, contrasting sharply with squawfish of the same size/cohort in the Verde River the previous year, which moved rapidly and extensively downstream — suggesting stream-specific behavioral or hydrological drivers of dispersal.
- **Co-occurrence with sportfish**: Squawfish persisted in Canyon Creek despite large resident trout populations, with survival apparently good based on low recapture effort needed to obtain specimens.
- **Native fish assemblages**: Eagle Creek (between Sheen Wash and the Phelps Dodge diversion above the pump station) and the Blue River retained largely native fish faunas, in contrast to most other surveyed streams, which were dominated by non-native species. Common native species in both streams included [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Gila Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*).
- **Reservoir habitat (Lake Mohave)**: Juvenile razorback habitat identified in early July had desiccated by late July due to declining reservoir levels, illustrating water-level fluctuation as a likely limiting factor for juvenile recruitment. Seining and overnight hoop-net sets failed to detect juveniles, while trammel nets captured only adults — reinforcing a pattern of documented spawning without successful recruitment to the juvenile stage.

## Key Findings
- [[Razorback Sucker]] was recaptured in 5 of the surveyed streams: Verde River, Blue River, Eagle Creek, Cherry Creek, and Canyon Creek.
- Fish recaptured in the Verde and Blue Rivers and Eagle Creek were from stockings 4–6 weeks prior; those in Cherry and Canyon Creeks had overwintered from stockings ~5 months prior.
- Heavy *Lernaea* parasite loads observed in Verde River razorbacks (Perkinsville–Alvarez Ranch reach) suggest a size-at-stocking vulnerability tied to incomplete scale development.
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] were recaptured only in a defined reach of Canyon Creek (1/4 mile below OW Ranch bridge to beaver ponds near the access road), all traced to Fall 1986 stockings.
- Squawfish in Canyon Creek showed strong site fidelity and negligible downstream dispersal, unlike same-cohort fish in the Verde River, indicating stream-specific movement ecology.
- Planned follow-up surveys on the White Mountain Apache Reservation stretch of Canyon Creek were cancelled due to unrelated tribal-Department disputes.
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) was common to locally abundant across a full size range in Eagle Creek; [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was common and locally abundant throughout the Blue River.
- Lake Mohave surveys reaffirmed that while adult razorback sucker reproduction has been repeatedly documented, recruitment to the juvenile stage remains essentially undetected, with only 3 juveniles known from recent decades (all collected by Region III, not by the follow-up survey team).
- Stomach content analysis of recaptured razorback suckers was underway at the time of this report to assess diet.
- Additional razorback and squawfish stocking activities continued through summer 1987, with fall stocking plans to be detailed in forthcoming memos.
