# Monitoring of Cottonwood Artesian Cienega Improvement Project

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This Tonto Basin Ranger District report (April 12, 1994) by Jerome A. Stefferud and Don Pollock documents a habitat improvement project at [[Cottonwood Artesian Cienega]] in [[Tonto National Forest]], Arizona. The project aimed to improve conditions for the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]], after the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) issued a "beneficial concurrence" on February 25, 1991. The site had been stocked in 1982 with 200 topminnow, which persisted for about a decade before disappearing. Monitoring by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), the Forest Service, and the FWS traced a decline driven by plumbing clogging, vegetation encroachment and drying. Pools were built and the site fenced in winter 1992–1993, but no topminnow had been found since 1992. The authors recommend reestablishing the species.

## Background & Description

**Location and setting.** The site (T05N, R13E, Section 34 NW¼) lies in [[Connor Wash]] and consists of:
- An artesian well drilled in 1966, capped, with flow piped about 20 feet to a concrete drinker (5 × 10 × 1.5 feet deep).
- Overflow that supported a 75-foot marshy area with [[Goodding Willow]] overstory.
- A dirt [[Stock Tank]] about 300 feet from the well, once dense with [[Cattails]].
- A second pipeline feeding drinkers downslope toward [[Roosevelt Lake]].
- Discharge at the well exceeded 5 gallons per minute in May 1992.

**Stocking and monitoring.** On June 9, 1982, 200 Gila topminnow from [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]] were stocked. AGFD checked the site in alternate years from 1985 through 1993, with occasional USFS and FWS visits. AGFD gave no population estimates but documented a steady decline in occupied habitat.

**Chronology of observations (Table 1, 12 entries).** Key records:
- 1987: topminnow present in tank and drinker, most abundant in the drinker.
- Aug 1989: tank dry; topminnow present around the drinker.
- Dec 1990: topminnow abundant in the drinker.
- 1991: topminnow confined to the drinker only.
- May 1992 through June 1993: topminnow absent at every visit (four records).
- Jan 1994: topminnow absent; longfin dace noted in Connor Wash near the site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Causes of habitat loss.** The decline outside the drinker was attributed to clogging of the well-head pipe and restriction of flow by roots and vegetation. By fall 1991 the marsh around the drinker had dried, many large willows had died and fallen, and the stock tank had dried and lost its cattails. No single cause of the population loss was identified. The authors suggest it may have been human-caused. In May 1992 the concrete drinker appeared to have been cleaned: silt, debris, and sod were gone. Before that, the drinker held several inches of sediment and about 70% surface cover of *Potamogeton* sp. in December 1990. [[Leopard Frog]] were typically observed in the drinker.

**Scope of the broader problem.** Fewer than 10% of the more than 200 sites stocked with Gila topminnow over the past decade remain extant. Most were lost to desiccation even though sites were chosen for likely perennial water.

## Project Activities

- **Planning:** Began September 1989. The original March 1982 evaluation had already suggested building pools.
- **Actions consulted upon (January 1991 request):** corral reconstruction, new drinker with float valve, plumbing renovation, drinker protection with pipe rail fence, two to three cienega-type pools with channels, cattail removal from the stock tank, boundary fence relocation, and livestock exclusion fencing.
- **Work completed (winter 1992–1993):** three pools dug; artesian well plumbing and down-country pipeline replaced; wetland fenced with interior fences removed; two new gates installed; a circular steel trough with a wildlife escape ramp added; existing corrals repaired and made smaller. No cattail removal was attempted and no new corrals were built.
- **Water control:** 1-inch gate valves at the well head control flow to the new trough and the down-country pipeline. Flow to the concrete drinker and cienega is unrestricted. During extreme drought, water flows to the new drinker first, at the permittee's request.
- **Labor:** the district backhoe dug the potholes on December 16, 1992. Arizona Boys' Ranch crews did most fencing and corral work, and Don Pollock capped the well.
- **Pool design:** three pools, each about 10 × 30 × 5 feet deep, were modeled on long, narrow, deep cienega pools with vertical sides. Excavated soil was piled around the perimeters.

## Key Findings

1. **Pools filled and persisted.** Heavy rain in January 1993 filled all three pools and saturated the soil. Overflow from the drinker has kept them full since.
2. **Aquatic invertebrates returned.** Damselflies and beetles were observed in the pools in January 1994.
3. **Vegetation established.** Dense herbaceous cover (mostly bermuda grass) grew around the pools after fencing. Submerged *Chara* and emergent sedges and rushes appeared in the pools. Willows planted in 1993 around the middle pool; one exceeded six feet by the report date.
4. **Topminnow not recovered.** The stock tank remained dry, and no topminnow were found after 1992.
5. **Site suitability.** The authors judge the habitat "ideal" for Gila topminnow and other species needing quiet water and dense riparian cover. Overhanging herbaceous growth limits open water, and straight pool sides plus shade should restrict cattail encroachment.
6. **Recommendations:**
   - Continue habitat monitoring with photo documentation to track riparian development.
   - Maintain fences to exclude livestock.
   - Reestablish Gila topminnow at the site. The suspected cause of loss was vandalism or drinker maintenance by someone unaware of the fish.
   - Retain sites that supported topminnow for a significant period in the recovery effort, since such sites are rare and should not be dropped because of an unexplained loss.
