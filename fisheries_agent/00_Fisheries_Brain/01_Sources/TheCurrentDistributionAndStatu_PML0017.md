# Spinedace Distribution and Sampling Across the Virgin River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #spinedace

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of map and figure captions from a spinedace survey of the [[Virgin River]] Basin in southwestern Utah. The source text contains no narrative, methods section, or summary statistics. Its figures show where [[Spinedace]] were found, where streams have perennial flow but no spinedace, and where reaches are naturally dry or dewatered by diversions. The maps cover [[Beaver Dam Wash]], the [[East Fork Virgin River]], the [[Santa Clara River]], [[Moody Wash]], [[Magotsu Creek]], [[Quail Creek]], [[Leeds Creek]], [[Ash Creek]], [[La Verkin Creek]], [[North Creek]], the [[North Fork Virgin River]], [[Shunes Creek]], and the main stem Virgin River. The survey's main apparent concern is that diversions dewater reaches that could otherwise support the fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stream categories used in the maps:**
- Stream sections where spinedace were found.
- Perennial stream sections where spinedace were not found, described as reaches "with perennial flow but not presently occupied."
- Stream sections that are naturally dry.
- Stream sections dewatered by diversions.

**Hydrologic and human-use features mapped:**
- [[USGS Gaging Station]] sites, some with [[Utah Division of Water Quality]] water quality sampling points.
- Springs. One spring is noted at approximately 0.75 cubic feet per second (cfs). Other springs are referenced by name (e.g., Ash Grove Springs, Toquerville Springs) with no flow values.
- Water diversions, which are the most numerous mapped features. Named examples include Littlefield/Frisby, Iverson, Lytle Ranch, Mathews Ranch, and Tennaco diversions in the Beaver Dam Wash area, and Bloomington, Sand Cove, Hydropower, and Central diversions along the Santa Clara and Virgin systems.

**Sampling approach:**
- Sampling stations are numbered and placed along each stream.
- Population results are reported as [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (fish per 100 m²).
- Where [[Multi-Pass Depletion Sampling]] was done, population estimates (fish per 100 m²) are shown alongside catch per unit effort.

## Key Findings

1. **Spinedace distribution is patchy.** The figures separate occupied perennial reaches from perennial reaches where spinedace are absent. This suggests that perennial flow alone does not guarantee occupancy.
2. **Diversions are a mapped threat.** Dewatered reaches, flagged separately from naturally dry channels, appear across all drainages. The Beaver Dam Wash and Santa Clara maps show the greatest number of diversion points.
3. **Naturally dry reaches are distinguished from human-caused dewatering.** This separation implies that the survey aims to tell natural limits on habitat apart from human-caused losses.
4. **Population data are provided at each station.** Figures 13, 20, 28, and 36 show catch per unit effort, and multi-pass depletion estimates where available, for the Beaver Dam Wash and East Fork, Santa Clara system, Quail/Leeds/Ash/La Verkin/North Creek system, and North Fork/East Fork/Shunes Creek system. The numeric values are not legible in the OCR text, so no specific abundance figures are recorded here.
5. **Quantitative details are limited.** The only numerical values in the text are the 0.75 cfs spring flow, map scale bars, and the diversion count of 15 total noted for Mathews Ranch diversions. Sample sizes, station counts, and abundance totals are absent or illegible.
