# Cui-ui Recovery Plan (1977)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

The Cui-ui Recovery Plan, prepared by the [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] and published by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in December 1977, sets out a strategy to downlist the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endemic [[Sucker]] of [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, from endangered to threatened. The plan covers the species' history, the causes of its decline, recovery objectives, a prioritized action outline, proposed [[Essential Habitat]], and comments from federal, state, tribal and local agencies.

The document is primarily a management and policy plan rather than a research paper. Its central message is that the species' decline follows reduced inflow from the [[Truckee River]], rising salinity in the lake, and blocked access to spawning grounds, and that life-history knowledge is too thin to guide restoration without further study.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The cui-ui is a large sucker, one of three *Chasmistes* species in the Great Basin. The others are the [[Short-Nose Sucker]] (*C. brevirostris*, Klamath Lake, California/Oregon) and the [[June Sucker]] (*C. liorus*, Utah Lake). The cui-ui is restricted to Nevada's Pyramid and Winnemucca lakes, remnants of [[Pleistocene Lake Lahontan]].
- **Cultural significance:** The [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]] has a close historical relationship with the species. The ancestral tribal name, *Kuyuidokado* ("sucker eaters"), reflects the reliance on spring spawning runs of cui-ui and [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*). Fish were eaten fresh, dried and smoked, and traded.
- **Anadromy:** The species shows strong anadromous tendencies, migrating from the lake to the mouth of the Truckee River and upstream to spawn.
- **Life history:** Very little was known. The plan notes that [[Snyder]] (1917) published the first basic life-history information, and [[Koch]] (1972) completed a doctoral study at the University of Nevada, Reno.
- **Listing:** The cui-ui was listed as endangered in the *Federal Register* (Vol. 32, No. 48, March 11, 1967).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology.** The Truckee River, which rises in the [[Sierra Nevada]] and flows through [[Lake Tahoe]], is the only permanent inflow to Pyramid Lake. Lake level depends almost entirely on this inflow, which must offset evaporation and seepage. The [[Derby Dam]] and canal, completed in 1905 by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], diverted Truckee water toward the [[Newlands Project]]. Lake level fell by nearly eighty feet during the 20th century. [[Winnemucca Lake]] dried completely around 1938.

**Causes of decline.** The plan identifies three interacting factors:
1. Insufficient spring flows.
2. An impassable delta at the river mouth.
3. The inability of spawners to pass above Derby Dam.

These factors are said to have led to the extinction of the [[Pyramid Lake Cutthroat Trout]] around 1938 and to a steep decline in the cui-ui spawning run.

**Salinity.** Total dissolved solids (TDS) in Pyramid Lake rose from 3486.1 ppm (1882) to 3980 ppm (1933), a 14 percent increase. From 1933 to 1968 they rose to 5420 ppm, a 36 percent increase. The plan states that the concentration has risen by nearly 50 percent overall. The evaporation estimate is approximately 400,000 acre-feet per year. Several snail species have become extinct, and at least one survives in a stunted form.

**Sedimentation.** A study of the lower Truckee River spawning gravels found that sediment deposition impeded subsurface water flow, reducing the habitat's value for trout eggs and fry. The plan suggests similar effects on cui-ui spawning areas may require stream bed renovation.

**Essential habitat.** The proposed essential habitat comprises:
- The Truckee River from Derby Dam downstream about 35 miles to Pyramid Lake.
- The area of Pyramid Lake below the 3795-foot elevation.
- Hardscrabble Creek, from its headwaters downstream about 7 miles to its confluence with the lake.

The plan states that these waters support the entire world population of the species.

## Key Findings

**Recovery objectives**
- The primary goal is to reclassify the cui-ui from endangered to threatened, and ultimately to non-endangered status.
- The plan calls for protecting the existing population, augmenting it through [[Artificial Propagation]], restoring essential habitat, and studying the reproductive requirements of the species.

**Progress to date**
- A fishway at the [[Marble Bluff Fishway]], completed in 1976, provides access to spawning grounds. The plan states that optimism must be tempered by concern for habitat quality and fishway use.
- The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] has developed hatchery techniques. Nearly 7.5 million fry were released into the lower Truckee River and the lake during 1973–1976. The success of this effort cannot yet be determined because suitable assessment techniques are lacking.
- The [[Nevada Department of Fish and Game]] has reared cui-ui larvae to adulthood, providing life-history data and showing the species can be reared outside Pyramid Lake.
- Members of the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe have been trained in netting, fish transport and culture techniques.
- Protective measures include a daily bag limit (first imposed) and, from 1969, a prohibition on non-Indian fishing for cui-ui.

**Priority actions (from the outline)**
- Maintain a self-sustaining population in Pyramid Lake, and protect stream-spawning fish in the Truckee River and all age classes in the lake.
- Assess predation by [[Sacramento Perch]], [[Lake Chub]], other fish, and birds such as pelicans and herons.
- Restore Truckee River access through fishway maintenance, diversion screens and removal of barriers.
- Maintain optimum stream flow for spawning, control riverbank damage (including fencing about 35 miles of bank), and reduce competing "rough" fish.
- Determine optimum lake habitat, and the effects of TDS on eggs and juveniles.
- Operate the [[Cui-ui Hatchery]] at Hardscrabble Creek and release at least two million hatchery-reared fish annually.
- Select and monitor possible transplant sites if the lake habitat deteriorates.
- Educate the public through leaflets and news releases.

**Acquisition and cost**
- Acquiring the old Peigh Ranch is proposed to secure the Hardscrabble Creek water supply. The Marble Bluff Fishway failed to pass cui-ui in spring 1976 and 1977, which the plan takes as evidence that artificial culture must continue.

**Agency comments (Appendix C; summary)**
- **Nevada Governor's office** questioned designating critical habitat along the whole historic range before study results were available, and proposed limiting it to the reach through the Pyramid Lake Indian Reservation. The state also supported the plan on the condition that its suggested corrections were adopted.
- **U.S. EPA (Region IX)** will act on five administrative tasks, focusing on pollution abatement under the Federal Water Pollution Control Act Amendments of 1972. It requested interim water-quality criteria for cui-ui in local planning.
- **Nevada Division of Environmental Protection** asked for an assessment of the costs of the plan and its effect on Truckee water users, and questioned who would enforce regulations. It also said a full socio-economic impact statement is needed before any critical habitat is designated.
- **Bureau of Land Management** said its only involvement would be the Hardscrabble Creek drainage, which is mostly private land.
- **U.S. Army Corps of Engineers** is studying flood protection for Reno and Sparks and will factor cui-ui protection into its design.
- **Bureau of Reclamation** questioned its authority to fund fishery work after fiscal 1978, asked for an environmental assessment, and queried how recovery and reclassification would be measured.
- **Desert Research Institute** noted that Walker Lake may also have held cui-ui and recommended additional citations, including Koch's work.
