## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 letter from the Maricopa Audubon Society (Robin Silver, M.D.) to the Commissioner of the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, alleging failure to comply with Section 7(a)(2) of the Endangered Species Act (ESA). The letter argues that the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) has introduced and spread deadly non-native fish species throughout the [[Gila River]] basin, causing a "jeopardy" situation for multiple federally listed native fish species. Despite a formal consultation initiated in February 1991 and a draft "jeopardy" opinion issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in May 1991, the Bureau of Reclamation had taken no corrective action for over 26 months at the time of writing. The letter is not a primary ichthyological study but serves as a policy-relevant historical record documenting the decline of Arizona's native freshwater fish fauna due to habitat loss and invasive species introduction.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of the listed species. However, it references a key physiological adaptation:

- **[[Desert Pupfish]]**: Noted for extreme euryhaline/osmoregulatory tolerance — capable of surviving in water conditions ranging from **maximally dilute fresh water** (flood conditions) to **maximally concentrated brine** (drought conditions). This adaptation is highlighted as potentially relevant to biomedical research on human kidney function and disease (e.g., as a model for renal osmoregulation, with implications for dialysis treatment research).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historical Range**: Arizona historically supported **33 native fish species**, adapted over "tens of thousands of years" to a highly volatile hydrological regime characteristic of arid environments.
- **Environmental Pressures**: Native species evolved to withstand:
  - Extreme temperature swings
  - Flash floods
  - Prolonged droughts
- **Habitat**: Primary habitat referenced is the **[[Gila River]]** and **lower [[Salt River]]** systems, part of the broader Colorado River basin in Arizona.
- **Role as Bioindicators**: The letter explicitly frames Arizona's native fish as "living barometers" of river ecosystem health.
- **Primary Threats**:
  1. **Habitat loss** (linked to water diversion infrastructure such as the CAP)
  2. **Introduction of non-native, predatory, and competitive exotic species** via CAP feeder canals and "inadequate protective systems" (i.e., insufficient fish screens/barriers)

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Failure**: The Bureau of Reclamation initiated formal Section 7 consultation with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] on **February 12, 1991**, regarding CAP's role in spreading non-native species in the Gila River basin.
- **Draft Jeopardy Opinion**: The FWS issued a **draft "jeopardy" opinion** on **May 30, 1991**, indicating that continued CAP operations were likely to jeopardize listed species. As of the letter's date (August 1993), the Bureau had failed to act on this opinion for **~26 months**.
- **Species Decline Statistics**:
  - Of 33 original native fish species in Arizona:
    - **23 species** are listed by the State of Arizona as species of concern.
    - **18 species** are listed under federal ESA protections (threatened or endangered).
    - **1 species**, the **[[Monkey Spring Pupfish]]**, is confirmed **extinct**.
- **Extinction Case Study**: The **Monkey Spring Pupfish** extinction is directly attributed to the introduction of predatory **largemouth bass** (*Micropterus salmoides*) into its habitat — cited as a cautionary precedent for the risk posed by continued exotic species introductions via CAP infrastructure.
- **Federally Listed Species Named in the Letter**:
  | Species | Federal Status |
  |---|---|
  | [[Desert Pupfish]] | Endangered |
  | [[Spikedace]] | Threatened |
  | [[Loach Minnow]] | Threatened |
  | [[Gila Topminnow]] | Endangered |
  | [[Colorado Squawfish]] | Endangered |
  | [[Razorback Sucker]] | Endangered |
  | Desert Nesting Bald Eagle | Endangered (non-fish, co-listed concern) |
- **Legal Basis Cited**: ESA Section 7(a)(2), requiring federal agencies to ensure actions do not jeopardize listed species or destroy/adversely modify critical habitat.
- **Federal listing timeline note**: Some listed species have held federal endangered status since **1967**, indicating decades-long awareness of decline prior to this letter.
