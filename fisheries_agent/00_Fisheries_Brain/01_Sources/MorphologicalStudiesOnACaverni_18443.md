# Morphological Studies on a Cavernicolous Form of Mollienesia sphenops (Pisces, Poeciliidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1970 morphological study by [[Jakob Parzefall]] (Zoologisches Institut, Hamburg) compares a cave-dwelling population of [[Mollienesia Sphenops]] with its surface ancestor, [[Mollienesia Velifera]], and [[Xiphophorus Helleri]]. The work examines three traits thought to support life in darkness: the female genital pad, the distribution and number of taste buds, and head-scale protuberances in males. The cave form shows constructive (progressive) development of the genital pad and taste buds, and reduced head-scale protuberances. The author proposes that taste (chemical) signalling replaces visual courtship in the dark.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Male [[Mollienesia Sphenops]] court females by "nipping," touching the female's genital region with the snout. Surface males show a visual display, but the author found none in the cave-related context. The cave form has reduced eyes and little pigmentation, and [[Gordon and Rosen]] (1962) first described it.
- **Prior work:** [[Zeiske]] (1968) showed that cave and surface forms reproduce in darkness and that olfaction and taste, rather than vision, are essential for recognising conspecific females.
- **Material:** Fixed specimens from surface pools near Palomares, Mexico, and cave populations from chambers II–III, VI, X and XIII, collected during a 1964 Mexico expedition led by C. Kosswig. A laboratory stock descended from chamber XIII was also used, along with a [[Liberty Strain]] from the Berlin Aquarium, a commercial [[Mollienesia Velifera]] strain, and descendants of a wild [[Xiphophorus Helleri]] guentheri stock from British Honduras.
- **Methods:** Genital pad size was measured under a binocular microscope with a net eyepiece after methylene blue staining. Histology used Bouin fixation, 7–10 µm sections and Azan staining. Taste buds were mapped in whole mounts (chromic acid fixation) and counted in four head-top areas at 32x magnification. Cephalic canal pores were scored using Gosline's (1949) nomenclature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The cave is described as a limestone system about 300 m long in Tabasco, Mexico.
- Eye size decreases along the cave stream, from the surface stream to the innermost chamber XIII. Gordon and Rosen (1962) and Peters and Peters (1968) interpreted this gradient as a gene-flow effect, with inward migration from the surface population.
- Light intensity differs among chambers. Chamber V is about 10 times brighter (about 30 foot-candles) than chamber II (2–3 foot-candles), while chamber XIII is completely dark.
- Cave fish in chamber XIII feed on midges (*Tendipes fulvipinis*, Tendipedidae), which they take from the water surface.
- Cave fish in chamber XIII were reared under a 4 °C-scale temperature regime, which the author notes can alter taste-bud counts in related species (Campos, 1969).

## Key Findings

- **Genital pad (females):** The cave form has a well-developed genital pad, which is strongly folded and rich in mucous cells. The surface form has only a flat, less developed genital field. [[Xiphophorus Helleri]] has a thin, unfolded epidermis.
- **Genital pad size gradient:** Pad size decreases from chamber XIII toward chamber X. Chamber X is significantly smaller, and chambers VI and II–III are similar to chamber X. The surface population has the smallest pad. The author found no clear gradient in pad size comparable to the eye-size gradient.
- **Mucous cells:** In the cave form, mucous cells concentrate in the pad's folds. In the surface form they are spread across the entire genital region.
- **Taste buds (males and females):** Cave fish show a strong increase in taste-bud numbers in all four head areas compared with surface fish. The increase is smallest in the lip area (area I). Females show consistently higher values than males, which the author interprets as a feeding function shared by both sexes rather than a mate-location function.
- **Taste-bud comparison across species:** Surface *M. sphenops* is better equipped than *M. velifera* and *X. helleri* only in areas III and IV. Cave fish are signi-ficantly better equipped than both comparison species.
- **Head-scale protuberances (cephalic processes):** These occur in males of *M. sphenops* and *M. velifera* but not in *X. helleri* (n = 20 each). In a five-class scoring scheme, 12 of 20 cave males (chamber XIII) lacked processes entirely. All 20 surface males had at least some processes, and 9 of them showed the strongest development. Processes thus regress in the cave, and the author's gradient hypothesis is supported by the other cave samples.
- **Cephalic canals:** Canal pores often remain open in the cave form. In chamber XIII, 14 of 20 fish had at least one unclosed canal segment, against 11 of 20 surface fish. The author suggests two explanations: juvenile fish, or a shift toward surface feeding.
- **Overall conclusion:** The author interprets genital pad development and taste-bud enrichment as constructive cave traits that could compensate for loss of vision. Eye degeneration proceeds at the same time. The author calls for more samples and environmental analysis, and for infrared-light observation of cave behaviour, to confirm these interpretations.
