## Executive Summary
This document is a formal critique letter authored by Dr. Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) addressed to the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR), specifically to Mr. Timothy H. Provan. The letter contests the scientific validity of an assessment report (Gustaveson et al. 1990) proposing the introduction of **Rainbow Smelt (*Osmerus mordax*)** into **[[Lake Powell]]** as a forage fish for **[[Striped Bass]]**. Marsh argues that the Utah assessment is scientifically incomplete, failing to adequately review existing literature on the negative ecological impacts of smelt introductions elsewhere in North America. The letter serves as a formal objection, citing peer-reviewed evidence (Evans & Loftus 1987) that contradicts the state's claim that smelt pose a "low risk" to native fishes of the **[[Colorado River Basin]]**.

## Background & Description

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The central ecological debate concerns the introduction of a non-native "forage fish" into a large reservoir ecosystem to support a sport fishery.

*   **Target Habitat:** [[Lake Powell]], a reservoir within the [[Colorado River Basin]], impacting jurisdictions across Utah, Arizona, Colorado, California, Nevada, and New Mexico.
*   **Proposed Role:** Rainbow Smelt were intended to serve as a forage base to support and enhance the [[Striped Bass]] fishery in Lake Powell.
*   **Niche Interactions:** Citing [[Evans and Loftus 1987]], the letter notes that introduced smelt exhibit strong ecological interactions across a "wide spectrum" of prey and predator species, impacting both fish and invertebrate populations through:
    *   **Direct impacts:** Predation on native larval/juvenile fishes.
    *   **Indirect impacts:** Competition with indigenous species for resources.
*   **Risk to Native Ichthyofauna:** The letter emphasizes that native fishes in the Colorado River Basin—several of which are federally recognized as imperiled—are potentially vulnerable to recruitment failure due to smelt competition/predation, mirroring impacts documented in the Great Lakes region.
*   **Geographic Transferability Concern:** Marsh acknowledges the valid counter-argument that data from Eastern U.S./Canadian lakes (cold/cool-water ecosystems) may not be directly predictive of impacts in Western U.S. reservoir systems, but argues this uncertainty cuts both ways, undermining the Utah assessment's optimistic risk assessment.

## Key Findings
1.  **Contested Claim:** The Utah assessment (Gustaveson et al. 1990) claims that Rainbow Smelt introduction poses a "low risk" to native Colorado River Basin fishes and will provide a "desired impact" on Lake Powell Striped Bass populations. Marsh contends neither claim is adequately substantiated.
2.  **Key Literature Omission:** The critique heavily relies on the omission of **[[Evans and Loftus 1987]]** (*Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences*), a study analyzing 24 case histories of smelt introductions.
    *   Of 34 published documents analyzed in that study, **16 reported an association between smelt introduction and recruitment decline** in one or more indigenous species.
    *   The Utah assessment reviewed **only 1 of these 16** critical studies, indicating a lack of thorough literature review.
3.  **Scientific Recommendation Ignored:** Evans and Loftus (1987) explicitly recommended that "managers should attempt to exclude this species from lakes with naturally reproducing stocks of cold- and cool-water species" due to high invasion risk—a recommendation seemingly disregarded in the Utah proposal.
4.  **Burden of Proof:** The letter directly challenges UDWR's stance that the burden of proof for demonstrating *harm* lies with outside critics, rather than the introducing agency needing to prove *safety*. Marsh argues the precautionary principle should apply given the documented history of negative impacts.
5.  **Recruitment Failure as Mechanism:** The letter connects the specific impact mechanism of smelt (recruitment decline in native species) directly to the currently imperiled status of certain regional native fishes, suggesting a plausible causal link if the introduction proceeds.
6.  **Recommendation:** The letter formally urges the State of Utah to abandon the Rainbow Smelt stocking proposal, asserting that the potential recreational/fishery benefits for Striped Bass are outweighed by the substantial ecological risk to the native fauna of the wider Colorado River Basin region.
