# Checklist of Texas Fresh-Water Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #taxonomy #species-checklist

## Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive taxonomic checklist of freshwater fishes occurring in Texas, compiled by Kenneth C. Jurgens (Aquatic Biologist, Texas Game and Fish Commission) and Clark Hubbs (The University of Texas). Originally published in the March 1953 issue of Texas Game and Fish Magazine, the checklist enumerates 197 species across 43 families, including both strictly freshwater species and marine species that regularly enter freshwater habitats. The classification follows Berg (1940) for higher taxonomic groups and Bailey (1951) for genera. Marine species inclusion is based on the work of Gunter (1945) documenting marine fishes in Texas waters.

## Background & Description

The checklist represents a collaborative effort involving multiple biologists from the [[Texas Game and Fish Commission]] and [[The University of Texas]]. Contributors include Marion Toole, Cecil W. Reid, Edward W. Bonn, Jack C. Ball, William H. Brown, Lawrence S. Campbell, Alvin G. Flury, Robert A. Kuehne, Robert J. Kemp, Leonard D. Lamb, Leo D. Lewis, and Kirby H. Walker. Additional assistance was provided by Carl L. Hubbs, Kelly Bonham, and Gary H. Soulen.

The taxonomic arrangement begins with primitive groups ([[Petromyzonidae]]—lampreys) and progresses through cartilaginous fishes (sharks, rays, sawfishes), primitive bony fishes (paddlefish, sturgeons, gars, bowfins), and numerous teleost families. Each entry includes scientific name with authority, common name(s), and an asterisk (*) denoting common names accepted by the Committee on Common Names, [[American Fisheries Society]] (Special Publication No. 1, 1948).

Notable taxonomic groups represented include:
- **Primitive fishes**: 2 lamprey species ([[Ichthyomyzon]]), 4 marine cartilaginous fishes entering freshwater
- **Ancient bony fishes**: 1 paddlefish ([[Polyodon spathula]]), 1 sturgeon, 4 gar species ([[Lepisosteidae]]), 1 bowfin
- **Cypriniformes**: Extensive representation with 44 minnow/shiner species ([[Cyprinidae]]) and 11 sucker species ([[Catostomidae]])
- **Siluriformes**: 11 catfish species including 2 blind cave catfishes ([[Trogloglanis pattersoni]], [[Satan eurystomus]])
- **Centrarchidae**: 18 sunfish and bass species including [[Largemouth Bass]] ([[Micropterus salmoides]]) and [[Bluegill]] ([[Lepomis macrochirus]])
- **Marine-freshwater interface species**: Multiple families including [[Mugilidae]] (mullets), [[Sciaenidae]] (drums), [[Gobiidae]] (gobies), and flatfishes

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist reflects Texas's diverse aquatic habitats spanning multiple ecological zones:

**Freshwater systems**: Major river drainages including the [[Rio Grande]], [[Brazos River]], and [[Trinity River]] support endemic species such as [[Notropis brazosensis]] (Brazos River shiner) and an undescribed Trinity River shiner. Spring systems harbor specialized endemic species including [[Cyprinodon elegans]] (Texas pupfish), [[Cyprinodon bovinus]] (Leon Springs pupfish), and [[Etheostoma fonticola]] (large spring small darter).

**Cave ecosystems**: Two blind catfish species represent highly specialized subterranean adaptations: the toothless blindcat ([[Trogloglanis pattersoni]]) and widemouth blindcat ([[Satan eurystomus]]).

**Marine-freshwater interface**: The [[Gulf of Mexico]] influence is evident in 30+ marine species that regularly enter freshwater, including [[Carcharhinus leucas]] (bull shark), [[Pristis pectinatus]] (sawfish), [[Tarpon atlanticus]] (tarpon), various drums and croakers ([[Sciaenidae]]), mullets, and flounders. This reflects the extensive estuarine and coastal plain habitats characteristic of the Texas Gulf Coast.

**Introduced species**: The checklist includes non-native species established in Texas waters: [[Cyprinus carpio]] (carp), [[Carassius auratus]] (goldfish), and [[Salmo gairdneri]] (rainbow trout).

## Key Findings

**Species Diversity**: The checklist documents 197 fish species from 43 families occurring in Texas freshwater habitats, representing one of the most diverse freshwater fish faunas in North America.

**Taxonomic Composition**:
- [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows/shiners): 44 species, the most diverse family
- [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes/basses): 18 species
- [[Catostomidae]] (suckers): 11 species  
- [[Cyprinodontidae]] (killifishes): 16 species
- [[Percidae]] (darters): 19 species
- [[Gobiidae]] (gobies): 10 species

**Endemic and Specialized Species**: Multiple Texas endemics documented including [[Notropis brazosensis]], [[Cyprinodon elegans]], [[Cyprinodon bovinus]], [[Gambusia nobilis]], [[Gambusia gaigei]], [[Micropterus treculi]] (Texas spotted bass), and two blind cave catfishes.

**Marine Influence**: Approximately 15% of listed species are marine forms regularly entering freshwater, including large predators ([[Carcharhinus leucas]], [[Pristis pectinatus]]), forage fishes (herrings, anchovies), and bottom-dwelling forms (flounders, gobies, drums).

**Common Names**: Standardized common names following [[American Fisheries Society]] nomenclature are provided for 89 species (marked with asterisks), representing approximately 45% of the total fauna.

**Geographic Coverage**: Species distributions span diverse Texas regions from the [[Rio Grande]] drainage in the west to eastern river systems, from spring-fed habitats to large rivers, and throughout the extensive Gulf coastal zone.

**Historical Baseline**: This 1953 checklist provides a critical historical baseline for assessing subsequent changes in Texas fish fauna due to habitat modification, species introductions, climate change, and conservation efforts over the past seven decades.
