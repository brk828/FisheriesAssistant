# A List of the Fish Found in the Verde River, July 1962

## Executive Summary
This brief 1962 field survey documents the fish fauna of the [[Verde River]] in Arizona, identifying 19 named taxa spanning native desert fishes, sport/food fishes, and a notable suite of introduced exotic species — including several South American characids and livebearers. The study's primary significance lies not in a comprehensive biodiversity assessment, but in documenting an early instance of intentional and incidental exotic fish introduction into a southwestern U.S. river system, with tropical fish trade activity identified as a direct vector. Four additional specimens collected could not be identified with the keys available at the time, indicating incomplete taxonomic resolution.

## Background & Description
The report is a species checklist rather than a morphometric study, so detailed morphological data are not provided. However, the taxa represent several distinct morphological guilds:

- **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]** (longfin dace) — small-bodied native cyprinid minnow, characteristic of desert Southwest streams.
- **[[Pantosteus]] spp.** (mountain suckers) — benthic, ventrally-mouthed catostomids adapted for algae/detritus scraping in flowing water.
- **[[Ictalurus Punctatus]], I. melas, I. natalis** — ictalurid catfishes with barbels and smooth scaleless skin, ranging from riverine (channel catfish) to more lentic-tolerant (bullheads) morphotypes.
- **[[Gambusia Affinis]]** — small, sexually dimorphic poeciliid livebearer with a modified male gonopodium.
- **[[Mollienisia (Poecilia)]] spp.** (latipinna, sphenops, velifera) and **[[Limia Tricolor]]** — livebearing poeciliids, several exhibiting sailfin dorsal fin morphology (notably *M. velifera*, the "green sailfin molly").
- **[[Gymnocorymbus Ternetzi]]** (black tetra) and **[[Aphyocharax Rubropinnis]]** (bloodfin) — small, laterally compressed South American characids with distinctive fin pigmentation (black caudal/anal fin blotch; red-tipped fins, respectively).
- **[[Cyprinus Carpio]]** and **[[Carassius Auratus]]** — large-bodied, heavily scaled cyprinids of Eurasian origin.
- **[[Mugil Cephalus]]** (striped mullet) — euryhaline, torpedo-shaped mugilid, an unusual find this far inland.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The Verde River in 1962 supported a mixed assemblage combining native desert-adapted cyprinids and catostomids with an increasing number of non-native warmwater and tropical species. Ecological niche partitioning likely occurred along:

- **Trophic axis**: benthic algal grazers (*[[Pantosteus]]*), omnivorous minnows (*[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]*, *[[Notropis Lutrensis]]*), invertivorous/piscivorous catfishes (*Ictalurus* spp.), planktivorous mosquitofish and mollies, and predatory centrarchids (*[[Lepomis Macrochirus]]*, *[[Pomoxis Nigromaculatus]]*).
- **Habitat axis**: flowing riffle/pool habitat used by native cyprinids and suckers versus warmer, slower canal and pond-like habitats favored by introduced poeciliids and characids.
- **Introduction pathway**: The tropical fish trade (a wholesale tropical fish and animal business in Mesa, Arizona) served as a direct anthropogenic vector, with intentional releases of poeciliids (*[[Mollienisia Latipinna]]*, *M. sphenops*, *M. velifera*, *[[Limia Tricolor]]*) aimed at establishing a wild "farm" source population near Phoenix. Incidental escapes/releases from fish dealers are implicated in the presence of South American characids (*[[Gymnocorymbus Ternetzi]]*, *[[Aphyocharax Rubropinnis]]*) in canals, ponds, and the river itself.
- **Bait-bucket introduction**: *[[Carassius Auratus]]* (goldfish) is attributed to release by anglers using it as live bait, as well as unwanted pet disposal — a classic informal introduction pathway.

This assemblage illustrates an early-stage biological invasion process in a southwestern U.S. desert river, with warmwater conditions and interconnected canal systems facilitating exotic establishment.

## Key Findings
- A total of 19 identifiable fish species/taxa were documented in the Verde River in July 1962, plus 4 unidentified specimens.
- At least **8 species were confirmed introduced exotics**, marked explicitly in the original source: *[[Gambusia Affinis]]*, *[[Carassius Auratus]]*, *[[Mollienisia Latipinna]]*, *M. sphenops*, *M. velifera*, *[[Limia Tricolor]]*, *[[Gymnocorymbus Ternetzi]]*, and *[[Aphyocharax Rubropinnis]]*.
- The **primary vector for tropical species introduction** was identified as Mr. Richard McClain, operator of a wholesale tropical fish and animal establishment in Mesa, Arizona, who intentionally released multiple poeciliid species to establish a nearby breeding source population.
- Other unnamed regional fish dealers were also implicated in releasing exotic fish into canals, permanent ponds, and the Verde River near Phoenix, suggesting a broader, multi-source introduction pattern rather than an isolated incident.
- The presence of South American characids (**black tetra** and **bloodfin**) — species not typically expected to survive in a temperate desert river — highlights unexpected ecological plasticity/tolerance of some tropical ornamental fish species, or at minimum recent, ongoing releases sustaining detectable populations.
- The author's central conclusion is that **introduced South American fish species were becoming established** in the Verde River, representing the most ecologically significant finding of the study and warranting continued monitoring.
- The study explicitly calls for **further systematic surveys** to track the establishment and spread of these exotic species over time, reflecting early recognition of invasive species risk in freshwater conservation.
