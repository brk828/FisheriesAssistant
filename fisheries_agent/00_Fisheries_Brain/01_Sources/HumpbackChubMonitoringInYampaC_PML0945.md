# Humpback Chub Monitoring in Yampa Canyon (FY 2001 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This FY 2001 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers Project 22a4, a three-year [[Humpback Chub]] monitoring study in [[Yampa Canyon]], [[Dinosaur National Monument]], run from 1998 to 2001 by G. B. Haines and Tim Modde of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Vernal, Utah). Field work is complete and a draft final report was due to the program coordinator in January 2002. The study found a stable but sparse population, yet a precise [[Population Estimate]] proved infeasible. The authors therefore recommend a two-phase monitoring program built on [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] measures, length-frequency data and [[Surrogate Species]].

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** Yampa River, from Deerlodge to Echo Park (75 km), within [[Dinosaur National Monument]].
- **Study design:** Three sampling passes per year, each lasting 4–5 days, with subsequent passes 7–10 days apart.
- **Methods:** [[Electrofishing]] from rafts and angling for adults. Captured humpback chub were scanned for a [[PIT Tag]]; untagged fish were tagged and released. [[Seining]] was used for young fish in shallow shorelines, pools, backwaters and eddies.
- **Program context:** Filed under General Recovery Program Support, Monitor Populations, and Conduct Standardized Monitoring Program.
- **Investigators:** G. B. Haines and Tim Modde, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** Catches extended from Laddie Park (river km 17) upstream to Disappointment Draw (river km 72), similar to historic records. Distribution was patchy, with most fish in deep pools and eddies associated with large boulders.
- **Population status:** The Yampa Canyon population appears stable and is successfully recruiting, but sparse. The authors estimate only a few hundred to a few thousand adults in the study reach (the distance unit in the scan is illegible).
- **Native community:** Surrogate species named include [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]], which may indicate the health of the wider native fish community.
- **Management relevance:** Monitoring is needed to judge possible down-listing or the need for further protection, and to track responses to management actions such as [[Non-Native Fish Control]].
- **Comparison species:** The proposed adult monitoring approach follows Modde et al. (1996) on [[Razorback Sucker]].

## Key Findings

**Sampling results**
- **Adult captures:** 89 humpback chub captured by electrofishing and angling.
- **Mark-recapture:** 83 fish captured, PIT tagged and released; 3 recaptured (one tagged the previous year, two tagged earlier in the same year).
- **Young fish:** 426 young *Gila* were seined across the entire study length. 33 were identified as humpback chub. Field identification of young *Gila* is difficult; the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at Colorado State University is helping to build identification skills.

**Population estimate**
- A satisfactory [[Population Estimate]] could not be produced. Although the design met most [[Capture-Recapture]] assumptions, too few tagged fish were recaptured.
- A [[Simulation Study]] showed that a satisfactory estimate would need an extraordinary sampling effort. The authors judged this infeasible, given the two electrofishing rafts already in use and concerns about added electrofishing and additional passes.

**Recommendations**
1. Because a population estimate with CV ≤25% may be neither economically feasible nor healthy for the fish, monitor the population mainly through catch per unit effort surveys of adults and young fish. Length-frequency distribution, distribution through the canyon and juvenile abundance are the best available status indicators.
2. Track surrogate species, such as roundtail chub, flannelmouth sucker and bluehead sucker, as indicators of the wider native fish community.

**Proposed two-phase monitoring plan**
- **Phase 1:** Long-term (more than 10 years) but less intensive capture-recapture monitoring of adult humpback chub, with annual electrofishing, combined with seining to assess reproductive success.
- **Phase 2:** Monitor surrogate species to detect short-term population responses to management. The authors suggest using capture-recapture on roundtail chub, since catch per unit effort measures for that species are too variable. Seining would also monitor surrogate species reproduction.
