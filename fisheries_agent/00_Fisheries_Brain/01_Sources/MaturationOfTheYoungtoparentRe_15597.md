# Young-to-Parent Glancing Behavior in the Cichlid Etroplus Maculatus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This conference abstract, accompanied by a 15-minute motion picture, describes a behavior called glancing in [[Etroplus Maculatus]] (a [[Cichlidae|cichlid]] fish). Free-swimming young strike the flank of an adult during schooling. The contact becomes more localized over development, is tied to the adult's [[Color Pattern]], and varies with time of day, hunger, and school size. The work was supported by a [[National Science Foundation]] grant (No. G-24016).

## Background & Description

The abstract covers the **young-to-parent response** in *Etroplus maculatus*, documented in a motion picture. The authors are listed as Barlow and J. W. Tate. The affiliation appears garbled in the scan, so it is not reported here.

Once the fry become free-swimming, they school around the parents and perform **[[Glancing Behavior]]**:

1. The young orients along the posterior flank of a parent.
2. It swims forward and strikes the adult's lateral surface.
3. It continues forward and rejoins the school.

During the brief contact, the young appears to strike a specific area and may touch the parent's side with its mouth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The abstract reports these behavioral patterns:

- **Development of targeting:** Glancing changes from near-random contact to contact at a limited locus. This locus is organized with reference to the adult's color pattern.
- **Daily rhythm:** Glancing fluctuates over the course of the day.
- **Multi-week change:** The behavior changes in a predictable way over a period of 3 weeks.
- **Influencing factors:** Relative hunger and school size both affect how often glancing occurs.
- **Adult-adult analogue:** Similar behavior occurs between paired adults, but without mouth contact.

These observations fall under [[Parental Care]] and social behavior in a cichlid that rears its young in a school.

## Key Findings

- Free-swimming young of *Etroplus maculatus* perform glancing, a directed strike on the parent's lateral flank, while schooling.
- Glancing becomes more localized with development, and its target is linked to the adult color pattern.
- The behavior fluctuates daily and follows a predictable change over about 3 weeks.
- Hunger level and school size modulate the frequency of glancing.
- Paired adults show a similar behavior, but without mouth contact.
- No sample sizes, quantitative rates, or statistical results are given in the abstract.
