# San Pedro River and Aravaipa Creek Streamflow Records, Water Years 1980–1986

#hydrology #freshwater-ecology #stream-discharge

## Executive Summary

This document is a multi-year series of [[United States Geological Survey]] (USGS) daily mean discharge tables for six stream gauging stations in southeastern Arizona. It covers the [[San Pedro River]] mainstem and its tributary [[Aravaipa Creek]], for [[Water Year]]s 1980 through 1986. The text contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, or water-chemistry data, so the species-related front matter fields are recorded as N/A. The records describe flow volume and variability only, which matters ecologically because flow controls aquatic habitat in arid-land streams.

## Background & Description

The records were produced by the USGS [[Water Resources Division]] and processed on 29-JAN-87. Each table reports daily mean discharge in cubic feet per second (cfs), with monthly and annual totals in acre-feet (AC-FT), monthly means, maxima and minima, and calendar-year and water-year summaries. Water years run from October through September. The 1985 and 1986 tables are marked as provisional data.

The six stations, with their USGS numbers and drainage areas, are:

- **09470500 San Pedro River at Palominas** (741 sq mi), see [[Palominas Gage]]
- **09471000 San Pedro River at Charleston** (1,219 sq mi), see [[Charleston Gage]]
- **09471550 San Pedro River near Tombstone** (1,740 sq mi), see [[Tombstone Gage]]
- **09472000 San Pedro River near Redington** (2,939 sq mi), see [[Redington Gage]]
- **09473000 Aravaipa Creek near Mammoth** (541 sq mi), see [[Aravaipa Creek Gage]]
- **09473100 San Pedro River below Aravaipa Creek near Mammoth** (4,360 sq mi), see [[San Pedro River Below Aravaipa Creek]]

The series moves downstream from Palominas to Redington and includes the Aravaipa Creek tributary. Drainage areas rise accordingly, from 741 sq mi at Palominas to 4,360 sq mi below the Aravaipa confluence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The tables show the hydrologic regime of an arid-to-semiarid river system. Several patterns matter for aquatic habitat:

- **Intermittent flow at upstream sites.** The Palominas record includes many days with zero or near-zero discharge, especially in the early part of the record. Persistent dry reaches limit the year-round habitat available to aquatic organisms.
- **Flashy, high-magnitude events.** Discharge at the main-stem stations often moves from near zero to several hundred or thousands of cfs within days. Examples include a Tombstone peak of 1,460 cfs in WY1981, a Redington peak of 8,730 cfs in WY1984, and an Aravaipa peak of 16,000 cfs in WY1984. Such events can scour channels and disrupt habitat.
- **Relatively persistent Aravaipa Creek flow.** Aravaipa Creek's minimum daily flows were consistently above zero, with recorded minima of 6.0 cfs (WY1981) and 22 cfs (WY1984). This suggests more reliable baseflow than the main-stem reaches.
- **Seasonal and interannual variability.** Monthly means shift strongly between dry and wet periods. Annual totals also vary widely between years.

The document does not discuss fish, other organisms, water quality, habitat condition, or management actions, so these aspects cannot be assessed from the source.

**Main patterns:**

1. Discharge increases downstream, consistent with drainage area and tributary inflow. Aravaipa Creek contributes a substantial share of flow below its confluence.
2. Upstream main-stem flows show frequent zero-flow periods, while Aravaipa Creek maintains non-zero minimum flows.
3. Annual totals and peak events vary by more than an order of magnitude across the record, underscoring the variability of the system.
4. The 1984 water year was the wettest in the record for most stations, with the largest recorded peaks at Redington and Aravaipa Creek.

**Limitations:** The document is a data compilation with no analysis, interpretation, or biological content. Some values are garbled by OCR and are reported here only where the source is clear. The 1985 and 1986 entries are provisional and may be revised.
