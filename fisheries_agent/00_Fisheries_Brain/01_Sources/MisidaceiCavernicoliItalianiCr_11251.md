# Italian Cave Mysidacea (Crustacea, Peracarida)

#marine-biology #crustacea #mysidacea

## Executive Summary

This 1978 (issued 1982) paper by G. L. Pesce of the [[Istituto di Zoologia]] at the Università dell'Aquila reviews the [[Subterranean Mysidacea]] of the world and documents the hypogean species known from Italy. Of roughly 750 marine and freshwater mysid species, 23 are known from underground waters. Only three occur in Italian subterranean waters: two [[Stygobiont]] species endemic to [[Apulia]] ([[Spelaeomysis Bottazzii]] and [[Stygiomysis Hydruntina]]) and one [[Stygoxene]] species, [[Siriella Armata]], recorded from caves in [[Campania]] and [[Sardinia]]. The paper also proposes a colonization pathway for these crustaceans and gives a systematic and biogeographic synthesis.

## Background & Description

The order [[Mysidacea]] contains about 750 described marine and freshwater species. The subterranean members form an ancient group in hypogean waters, with a marked concentration of stygobiont species in the Central American region. The author classifies them into three ecological categories:

- **Stygobionts:** highly specialized, truly subterranean forms (e.g., *Stygiomysis hydruntina*, *Stygiomysis holthuisi*, *Spelaeomysis quinterensis*).
- **Less specialized or recent immigrants:** forms such as *Spelaeomysis servatus*, *Spelaeomysis bottazzii* and *Antromysis cenotensis*.
- **Stygoxenes:** occasional invaders of underground waters or similar habitats, such as *Spelaeomysis cardisomae*, *Heteromysoides spongicola* and *Siriella armata*.

Most subterranean mysids are phylogenetically linked to marine ancestors and retain a high degree of [[Euryhalinity]]. They occur in fresh, brackish, nearly marine and fully marine waters. The systematic placement follows [[Gordon 1957]] and [[Mauchline 1977]]: all belong to the suborder Mysida, in the families [[Mysidae]], [[Lepidomysidae]] and [[Stygiomysidae]]. Because some Lepidomysidae and Stygiomysidae show specialized traits and intermediate characters between Mysida and Lophogastrida, [[Gordon 1960]] proposed a separate grouping for them.

The paper lists the species by genus, with their systematic position, ecology and distribution:

- **[[Spelaeomysis]] Caroli 1924:** the largest group, with about seven or more listed species from Italy, Africa, Mexico, India, Cuba and elsewhere.
- **[[Stygiomysis]] Caroli 1937:** three species, from Puglia, Jamaica and Puerto Rico.
- **[[Antromysis]] Creaser 1936:** about seven species, mainly from Cuba and Mexico.
- Single species of *Troglomysis*, *Heteromysoides*, *Siriella*, *Antromysis* (Hansen), *Hemimysis* and *Leptomysis* from Yugoslavia, the Canary Islands, Cuba, Europe, Morocco, Madagascar, France and elsewhere.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spelaeomysis bottazzii** is recorded from the Zinzulusa, Buco dei Diavoli and Abisso caves at Otranto, and from freshwater or slightly brackish wells around Bari, Taranto, Lecce and Gallipoli. It is also reported from Brindisi. Its known distribution is endemic to Apulia. It is fully depigmented, has no visual organs and shows marked elongation of the antennae and other appendages. It tolerates a range of salinities, temperatures and light levels.

**Stygiomysis hydruntina** occurs in the same Otranto caves and in the phreatic waters around Gallipoli. It is less frequent and abundant than *S. bottazzii*, and appears restricted to subterranean systems of the [[Salento Peninsula]]. The author suggests that, like the amphipod [[Metaingolfiella Mirabilis]] (known only from a deep phreatic aquifer at Veglie, Lecce), it inhabits deep, isolated phreatic systems and only occasionally contacts surface aquifers.

**Siriella armata** was reported from the cave waters of the [[Grotta di Nettuno]] in Sardinia (Puddu and Pirodda, 1973) and from marine caves in Napoli. Patrizi (1952) considered it a troglophile (stygophile). Its general distribution covers Italy, northern Europe and North Africa, in marine and brackish waters. The author concludes it is an occasional visitor to cave environments, not a stable component of their fauna.

**Colonization model:** Subterranean mysids most likely invaded continental hypogean systems from coastal areas between the Miocene and the late Pliocene. They followed the same route as other marine-derived groups such as Thermosbaenacea, Microparasellidae, Cirolanidae and amphipods of the genera *Hadzia*, *Metahadzia* and *Bogidiella*. Adaptation to mesohaline and limnic conditions occurred during successive sedimentary cycles, consistent with the [[Regression Model]] of Stock (1977).

## Key Findings

- **Global count:** 23 hypogean mysid species are known out of about 750 described in the order Mysidacea.
- **Italian fauna:** only three species are recorded from Italian subterranean waters: two stygobionts (*Spelaeomysis bottazzii* and *Stygiomysis hydruntina*) and one stygoxene (*Siriella armata*).
- **Endemism:** both stygobiont species are endemic to Apulia. Their known distributions are limited to the Salento area and the surrounding region.
- **Age of colonization:** genetic variability data (Colognola et al., in press) suggest *S. bottazzii* invaded the subterranean domain relatively recently, during the Pliocene, when Apulia was only partly emerged. *S. hydruntina* is considered older and more specialized.
- **Ecological status:** *S. bottazzii* and *S. hydruntina* are regular cave and phreatic inhabitants, while *S. armata* is only an occasional visitor to cave waters.
- **Systematics:** the author supports the idea that Lepidomysidae and Stygiomysidae deserve separate status because of their specialization and mixed traits with Lophogastrida.
- **Physiology:** the high euryhalinity of subterranean mysids shows their marine origin and their adaptation to freshwater and brackish conditions.
