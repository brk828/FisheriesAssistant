# Yampa River Humpback Chub Population Evaluation (FY 2003–2004)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Colorado River Recovery Program annual report covers Project 133, which evaluates the [[Humpback Chub]] population of the [[Yampa River]]. The project tests whether the Yampa population also occupies [[Lodore Canyon]], [[Whirlpool Canyon]] and [[Split Mountain Canyon]], as the recovery goals imply. Sampling in 2003 captured nine adult humpback chub, seven in Whirlpool Canyon and two in Yampa Canyon. In 2004 nine fish were captured, all in the Yampa system. Both years' budgets were fully spent and work was reported as 100% complete. The project is ongoing and subject to review before continuation. The report notes that the sample is too small to show that humpback chub are absent from Lodore or Split Mountain canyons.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a federally listed endangered cyprinid of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The document refers to it as a native species of the Green River system.
- **Recovery goals:** These require no net loss in the size of existing humpback chub populations and a monitoring program to track their status.
- **Population status:** The Yampa River population is described as one of the smallest existing humpback chub populations.
- **Monitoring need:** A workshop recommended that abundance estimates are very important for measuring progress toward recovery.
- **Distribution question:** The recovery goals include fish found in Lodore, Whirlpool and Split Mountain canyons in the Yampa population. Evidence is mixed: age-0 and age-1 fish were found in Island Park (supporting presence in Whirlpool Canyon), and historical records document captures in Split Mountain and Lodore canyons.
- **Project design:** This is the first attempt to sample the distribution of humpback chub across all suspected occupied locations in a single year, providing baseline distributional data for future monitoring.
- **Schedule and status:** The project is listed as running from 2003 to 2005. The FY 2003 report calls it the first of three years, while the FY 2004 report calls 2004 the last year of a two-year study. The document does not resolve this discrepancy.
- **Lead agencies:** [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Sam Finney, Fishery Biologist; Tim Modde, Assistant Project Leader) and the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife]] (Kevin D. Christopherson).
- **Relationship to plans:** Action item V.A of the Green River Action Plan (Yampa and Little Snake Rivers) calls for a population estimate for humpback chub.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study reaches:** Lodore, Whirlpool and Split Mountain canyons (river miles roughly 364 to 219), plus Yampa Canyon (river mile about 47 to 10; the scan is ambiguous).
- **Sampling conditions:** Low summer flows in 2003 prevented a third electrofishing pass on the Yampa River.
- **Methods varied by site:** Sampling techniques differed among locations because of other ongoing projects and logistical limits. Effort in 2003 was partly coordinated with [[Colorado State University]] and USFWS sampling under Project 115.
- **Nonnative fish dominance:** In 2003, juvenile seine samples from backwaters and other slow-water habitats in [[Island Park]] and Yampa Canyon were dominated by [[Nonnative Fish]] species, with a very high proportion of the catch, though the exact percentage is illegible in the scan.
- **Native fish:** Of 255 native fish collected in the 2003 seines, 237 were [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]. Nearly all came from two isolated pools in [[Lily Park]].
- **Competing species:** [[Red Shiner]] was the most abundant species in 2003 seines, with 2,334 total (2,103 from Island Park and 231 from Yampa River).

## Key Findings

**Budget and administration**
- FY 2003: $94,700 provided and $94,700 expended (difference $0); work 100% complete; no publication charges. Signed by Sam Finney on November 7, 2003.
- FY 2004: $99,960 provided and $99,960 expended (difference $0); work 100% complete; no publication charges. Signed by Sam Finney on November 8, 2004.
- Data were being entered into [[Microsoft Excel]] spreadsheets and will go to the database manager when the project is complete.

**2003 sampling results**
- Gear used included [[Electrofishing]], [[Trammel Netting]] and [[Angling]]. Electrofishing passes were made in Lodore Canyon and the Yampa River; netting and angling passes in Whirlpool and Split Mountain canyons.
- Nine adult humpback chub were captured: seven in Whirlpool Canyon by trammel net and two in Yampa Canyon (one by angling, one by electrofishing).
- Two humpback chub in Whirlpool Canyon were recaptured. The text gives a rough population estimate of "14+76" for Whirlpool Canyon, but the scan is garbled and the value should be checked against the original.
- Catch rates were low, with CPUE values such as 0.017 fish/h and 0.008 fish/h, and zero catches for several method–area combinations.
- All humpback chub captured in 2003 were of similar size and condition.
- Seventeen seine samples of juvenile fish were taken in 2003 and will be analyzed as time allows. CSU also obtained additional juvenile samples.
- Humpback chub adults were recaptured or found in the Yampa River (between river miles 28 and 37) and in Whirlpool Canyon. None were found in Lodore or Split Mountain canyons. Because only nine fish were captured, the report says the evidence against humpback chub in Lodore and Split Mountain canyons is weak.
- Angling for nonnative fish removal in Yampa Canyon provided additional data.

**2004 sampling results**
- Sampling dates were spread across Lodore Canyon (September and July), Yampa Canyon (April through July), and Split Mountain and Whirlpool canyons (August and September).
- Nine humpback chub were captured in 2004: four by electrofishing and five by angling. All were in the Yampa system, and none were recaptures.
- The report notes that no humpback chub were caught in Whirlpool Canyon in 2004, unlike the previous year. Other work captured humpback chub in Whirlpool Canyon in 2004 ([[Bestgen]] et al., Project 115).
- The 2004 report states that 32 seine samples of juvenile fish were taken and would be analyzed that winter (the scan reads "Tltrty two").

**Recommendations and status**
- 2003: concentrate sampling in areas with high observed catchability.
- 2004: complete the annual report, and localize future sampling to capture more humpback chub and produce better estimates of population size and structure.
- Status: the project is ongoing and subject to review before continuation.
