# Revision of the Gila robusta Species Complex (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report, submitted to the Joint ASIH-AFS Committee on Names of Fishes, re-examines the [[Gila Robusta]] species complex of the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]. Recent treatments recognize three species: [[Gila Robusta]], [[Gila Intermedia]], and [[Gila Nigra]]. The authors combine a historical review of nomenclature, morphological comparison of [[Type Specimens]] and fresh material, and [[RAD Sequencing]] phylogenetics. They conclude that the three species are not monophyletic and that their morphological and genetic divergences are insufficient to justify species-level recognition. The authors recommend that the complex be treated as a single species.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** The authors identify fifteen specific names and seven generic names applied to members of this group. Confusion arose from a complex array of phenotypes. Earlier workers assigned populations to species by stream drainage, following [[Rinne 1969]] and [[Minckley and DeMarais 2000]]. Those assignments relied on mean morphological differences among populations.

**Type locality dispute.** The syntypes of [[Gila Robusta]] (USNM 246) were originally reported from the Zuni River, New Mexico. [[Smith Et Al 1979]] proposed the Little Colorado River below Grand Falls, Arizona, as the true locality. [[Sublette Et Al 1990]] disputed this, arguing that multiple clerical errors across expeditions were implausible. The authors side with Sublette et al. and retain the Zuni River as the type locality.

**Synonymy of Gila grahamii.** [[Gila Grahamii]] was treated as a subspecies of [[Gila Robusta]] for much of the twentieth century. [[Minckley and DeMarais 2000]] recognized the population Rinne had called *G. r. grahamii* as a distinct species under the earliest available name, [[Gila Nigra]]. The present authors treat *G. grahamii* as a synonym of [[Gila Robusta]].

**Other names.** Several names once treated as synonyms are now considered valid, including [[Gila Elegans]], [[Gila Jordani]], [[Gila Pandora]], [[Richardsonius Egregius]], and [[Gila Seminuda]]. [[Gila Cypha]] and [[Gila Elegans]] served as outgroups in the molecular analyses.

**Morphological methods.** Meristic and morphometric characters follow [[Hubbs and Lagler 1958]]. Measurements were standardized to standard length, head length, or body depth. Linear regression confirmed that characters scale linearly with size. The analysis used 28 morphological variables.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The specimens come from streams throughout Arizona within the Lower Colorado River Basin. Collection localities for the three species are mapped in Figure 1 of the source.
- **Stream-driven variation:** Some authors had attributed population differences to water depth and current speed. The authors note that gradation in characters with stream size and flow is widespread in fishes. [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*) shows a similar pattern. Using mean population differences to define species can therefore lead to paraphyly within species.
- **Historical isolation:** Molecular divergence estimates suggest that the oldest splits among lineages date to roughly 10,000 years ago. These dates may reflect isolation linked to [[Post-Glacial Warming]] and the shift from the wetter [[Last Glacial Maximum]] climate to the arid climate of the current southwestern United States.
- **Conservation implications:** Previous studies using [[Allozyme Analysis]], mtDNA, and [[Microsatellite Markers]] also found no diagnostic characters separating the three species. The authors argue that species-level recognition may misguide management and conservation of these populations.

## Key Findings

**Sampling and genomic data**
- Five specimens of each recognized species and one each of *G. elegans* and *G. cypha* (outgroups) were analyzed, with one individual per location across each species' range.
- Tissue was processed with the [[EzRAD Protocol]]. DNA was digested with DpnII and libraries were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq.
- Mitochondrial genome reconstruction achieved >95% completeness for all specimens, mapped to the *G. robusta* reference (GenBank DQ536424.1).
- The [[SNP Analysis]] pipeline (PEAR and AftrRAD) yielded 2633 polymorphic loci with 100% coverage across samples.
- Phylogenies were built with [[RAxML]] under a GTR+GAMMA model with 500 bootstrap replicates. Divergence times were estimated in MEGA 7.0 using a [[Molecular Clock]] of approximately 2% per million years.

**Nomenclatural conclusions**
- The three recognized species and *G. grahamii* do not align with their [[Type Specimens]].
- Of the type specimens, four of *G. intermedia* and one of *G. nigra* were misassigned by the morphological characters (lateral line scales, head length to caudal peduncle depth, and caudal length to depth).
- Under the [[International Code Of Zoological Nomenclature]], a species-group name applies to the population of its name-bearing type. Because the names do not match the populations, the authors conclude these names should not be applied to the populations currently using them.

**Type material morphology**
- Type series sizes: *G. robusta* (n=2), *G. intermedia* (n=4), *G. nigra* (n=3), and *G. grahamii* (n=7).
- Differences between type series were comparable to differences between *G. grahamii* and the other species, so the characters do not separate the nominal species.

**Fresh material morphology**
- Within-species variation exceeded among-species variation for the fresh specimens.
- No single diagnostic character identified species. Principal components analysis of the 28 variables failed to resolve the currently recognized species.
- Fresh *G. robusta* matched the type material for only 30% of characters. It was assigned to *G. nigra* for 51% and to *G. intermedia* for 54% of characters (Table 5).
- No character correctly assigned all specimens to a single type series.

**Molecular results**
- Mitochondrial and SNP trees from 17 locations showed high concordance. The only discrepancy was that the SNP tree placed Silver Creek (*G. intermedia*) in Clade 4.
- No currently recognized taxon was monophyletic. Clade 5 consists exclusively of *G. nigra*, likely due to close geographic proximity of its sampling sites. *G. nigra* also occurs in Clade 4 with *G. robusta*.
- No geographic pattern was evident in either the mtDNA or nuclear trees.

**Morphology across phylogenetic clades**
- Clades showed nearly 100% overlap in morphological characters, with no morphological divergence among clades. This rules out the possibility that taxonomic units exist but were misaligned.

**Summary conclusions**
- Within-species morphological variation is high, and between-species variation is insufficient to justify three taxonomic units.
- Phylogenetic analyses reveal extremely shallow coalescence of putative species.
- The authors recommend recognizing the *G. robusta* complex as a single species.
