# Evidence of American Shad Reproduction in a Landlocked Environment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary
This short 1965 note documents apparent successful reproduction of [[American Shad]] (*Alosa sapidissima*), a normally [[Anadromous Fish|anadromous]] species, in a completely landlocked freshwater environment — [[Millerton Lake]], California. Prior literature acknowledged that adult shad could reside in fresh water indefinitely, but no confirmed case of successful **spawning** in a landlocked system had been reported. Age analysis of gill-net-captured shad suggests that a majority of the sampled population originated from in-lake reproduction rather than from the original stocking event, implying that American shad can complete their full life cycle without ever reaching the ocean.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: American Shad, *Alosa sapidissima* (family [[Clupeidae]])
- **Sample size**: 26 individuals captured via intensive gill-net sampling in 1963
- **Fork length (FL) range**: 6.3 to 14.6 inches
- Two smallest individuals (6.3 in and 7.7 in FL) were aged as **Age II**, notably smaller/younger than the founding stocked cohort — a key morphological/age clue supporting local recruitment.
- Aging was performed via **scale analysis** using the method of [[Borodin 1924|Borodin's Method]] (1924), a technique historically used for shad age determination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Normal life history**: American shad are typically anadromous, requiring **2 to 5 years at sea** before completing their reproductive life cycle; adults may take up freshwater residence but historically were not known to reproduce without a marine phase ([[Greeley 1939]]).
- **Habitat in this study**: [[Millerton Lake]], a reservoir system in Fresno/Madera Counties, California — a fully landlocked, non-tidal, non-oceanic freshwater environment.
- **Species introduction pathway**: American shad fry were presumably introduced **incidentally** alongside intentional transplants of [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*) from the Mendota Canal and Tracy Pumping Plant between 1955–1957, both source locations known to harbor abundant shad.
- **Isolation from marine recruitment**: No fish were introduced into Millerton Lake after 1957, and no source populations with access to American shad were used in subsequent stocking — meaning any younger cohorts must reflect **in-lake reproduction**, not new introductions.
- This represents a rare ecological case of a diadromous [[Clupeidae|clupeid]] species establishing a **self-sustaining, non-migratory population** entirely within a closed freshwater system.

## Key Findings
- Of the 26 shad captured, **18 were successfully aged** via scale analysis.
- Most aged fish fell into **Age Groups IV–VII**, consistent with the original 1955–1957 stocking event (which would place original founders at ages VI–VIII by 1963).
- **Two individuals were aged at Age II** — far too young to be part of the original stocked cohort, indicating they were spawned within the lake.
- Based on Borodin's (1924) scale-aging method, **11 of the 18 aged fish (~61%)** were interpreted as products of **lake propagation** (i.e., born in Millerton Lake) rather than originating from the original introduction.
- The author notes that [[Cating 1953|Cating's]] (1953) work suggested Borodin's method may **overestimate age** in American shad — implying that if corrected, an even **higher proportion** of the sampled shad may have resulted from successful lake reproduction.
- This constitutes one of the first documented cases suggesting **complete reproductive success of American shad without any marine phase**, challenging assumptions about the obligate anadromy of the species.
- Study was conducted under **Dingell-Johnson Project California F-18-R** ("Experimental Management of Warmwater Reservoirs"), funded via Federal Aid to Fish Restoration.
