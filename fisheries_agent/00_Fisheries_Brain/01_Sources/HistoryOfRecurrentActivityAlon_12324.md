## Executive Summary

This source is a structural geology and stratigraphy paper describing the history of recurrent fault movement (the "San Marcos fault") in Coahuila, northeastern Mexico, spanning the Jurassic, Early Cretaceous, and Tertiary periods. **The paper contains no ichthyological, fish, or aquatic-ecosystem science data.** It does not discuss fish species, fish habitats, water chemistry, plankton, invertebrates, or any biological/ecological subject matter. Its focus is entirely on regional tectonics, fault kinematics (vertical and possible left-lateral displacement), and the sedimentary/stratigraphic record (conglomerates, limestones, arkoses) used to infer the timing and geometry of fault movement associated with the hypothesized **[[Mojave-Sonora Megashear]]** system in northern Mexico.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** A 280-km-long, west-northwest-trending fault zone in Coahuila, Mexico, redefined by the authors as the **San Marcos Fault** (originally named by Charleston, 1981).
- **Nature of evidence:** Stratigraphic (detrital and chemical lithosomes of the Coahuila Series) and structural (crush breccias, cataclasites, drape folds, thrust faults) evidence for fault movement.
- **Key geological units discussed:** Coahuila Series, Cupido Formation, La Virgen Formation, Colorada Formation, San Marcos Arkose, La Mula Formation, Padilla Formation, La Casita and Zuloaga Groups, and 242 ± 2 m.y.-old granites at Valle San Marcos.
- **Paleogeographic features:** Coahuila Island (Late Jurassic–Early Cretaceous emergent landmass), La Mula Island, and the Parras Basin (a marine sedimentary basin south of Coahuila Island).
- No biological taxa (fish, invertebrates, plankton) are described; the only fossils mentioned are **ammonites** (*Proniceras* and *Substeueroceras* spp.) used solely for Tithonian-age biostratigraphic dating of Jurassic marine strata, and unspecified "abundant clams" — these are invoked purely as chronostratigraphic markers, not as subjects of ecological or paleobiological analysis.

  - Circumstantial (not conclusive) evidence for large-scale left-lateral displacement.
