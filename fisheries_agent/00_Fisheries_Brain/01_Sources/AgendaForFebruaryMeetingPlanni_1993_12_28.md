# Agenda for February 3–4, 1994 Meeting: Planning for USGS Participation in the 1995 Flood Experiment

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal USGS planning memorandum (Dick Marzolf, 28 December 1993) organizing a meeting of Colorado River researchers to design USGS field science for the **1995 experimental flood release from [[Glen Canyon Dam]]**. The controlled flood (up to 45,000 ft³/s) was mandated under the **[[Grand Canyon Protection Act]]** to test predictions about flow, sediment transport, and ecosystem "resetting" processes in the [[Grand Canyon]] riparian corridor, and to evaluate management potential of controlled flooding as a restoration tool. While the memo is primarily geomorphological and programmatic, it explicitly designates **native fish population assessment** — centered on the endangered **[[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)** — as one of ten core "candidate variables" to be measured before and after the flood, alongside invertebrates, riparian vegetation, primary productivity, organic drift, and water chemistry.

## Background & Description
The source contains no direct morphological description of fish anatomy. However, it specifies the **biometric data to be collected** during the experiment for native and non-native fish populations:
- **Biomass** and **species composition** (via electroshocking surveys)
- **Length and weight** measurements for recaptured **[[Humpback Chub]]** individuals
- Non-native fish were slated for **sacrifice** to permit **stomach content analysis** (numbers and prey taxa identified to family level), implying dietary/morphological (gut) examination as a secondary data stream.
- **PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tagging** was to be used for individual-level tracking of humpback chub, enabling recapture-based growth and movement analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The plan situates fish sampling within a broader river ecosystem monitoring framework tied to flow and sediment dynamics:

- **Study reaches**: Electroshocking and population surveys were planned near the **[[Little Colorado River]] (LCR)** confluence — a key spawning and refuge area for humpback chub — before and after the flood pulse.
- **Trophic linkages**: Non-native fish stomachs were to be sampled specifically to assess predation/consumption of **[[Invertebrates|invertebrate]]** taxa (identified to family), connecting fish diet to the broader **organic matter drift** (Section VII) and **invertebrate** (Section VIII) sampling efforts conducted at Lees Ferry, Grand Canyon (Phantom Ranch), and tributary confluences (National Canyon, Diamond Creek).
- **Habitat contexts** flagged for high biological relevance included:
  - **[[Lees Ferry]]** (logistic efficiency, tailwater reach below Glen Canyon Dam)
  - **[[Cardenas Marsh]]** and **[[Vasey's Paradise]]** (management-priority riparian/backwater habitats)
  - Nankoweap fan, Furnace Flats, Upper Granite Gorge, and the Tapeats Creek–Deer Creek reach (geomorphically variable, "suspected biologically active" reaches)
- **Water quality context**: Fish population dynamics were to be interpreted against paired water chemistry tracking — daily sampling at Lake Powell penstock depth and twice-daily (dawn/dusk) sampling at Lees Ferry, above the LCR, Grand Canyon, and Diamond Creek — plus planned but underdeveloped sampling of **eddies and backwaters**, key nursery/refuge habitat for native fish.
- The experiment's ecological rationale explicitly linked flow manipulation to **"endangered species survival"** as one of the resetting phenomena of interest (Objective 2).

## Key Findings
- The **1995 experimental flood** (a controlled release up to 45,000 ft³/s from Glen Canyon Dam) was designed as a management experiment under the Grand Canyon Protection Act, intended to reset sediment and riparian ecosystem conditions altered since dam construction (1963–1993 baseline period per Glen Canyon Environmental Studies).
- Six selection criteria were established for choosing ecosystem measurement variables: relation to discharge, direct/indirect dependency on flow, alignment with management-relevant time scales (seasons–decades, avoiding diel or century-scale variables), relevance to future flood planning, methodological simplicity/repeatability across large field teams, and minimal invasiveness to wilderness values.
- **Native fish population estimates (Section IX)** were formally designated as a required "before-and-after" dependent ecosystem variable, to be measured via:
  1. Electroshocking surveys near the LCR confluence (biomass, species composition)
  2. Sacrifice of **non-native fish** for invertebrate stomach analysis (prey identified to family)
  3. **PIT-tag-based recapture** of **[[Humpback Chub]]** to obtain length and weight data
- Fish data collection was explicitly nested within a multi-trophic sampling design spanning flow/sediment transport (independent variables I–IV) and biological/chemical response variables (V–X: riparian vegetation, primary productivity, organic drift, invertebrates, native fish, water chemistry).
- The memo acknowledges a fundamental **scale mismatch** between fine-scale biological/chemical process observations (days–growing seasons, local reaches) and coarse-scale management needs (5-year to decadal, canyon-wide), identified as the central modeling and monitoring challenge for the long-term Colorado River restoration program.
- No fish species other than **Gila cypha (Humpback Chub)** is named; "non-native fish" are referenced only functionally (as invertebrate predators/gut-content sources), without species-level identification.
