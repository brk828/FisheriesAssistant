# Colorado River Management Group Meeting Summary - October 12, 1988

#ichthyology #endangered-species #recovery-program #river-management

## Executive Summary

This meeting summary documents the October 12, 1988, [[Colorado River Management Group]] meeting held at the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Regional Office in Lakewood, Colorado. The meeting addressed administrative and programmatic aspects of the [[Colorado River]] endangered fish recovery effort, including updates on propagation planning, water rights acquisition, instream flow studies, and the [[Flaming Gorge]] Biological Opinion. Key discussions centered on funding mechanisms through the [[Fish and Wildlife Foundation]], water rights acquisition strategies for the [[Yampa River]] (particularly Juniper-Cross Mountain rights), and expansion of recovery efforts to include the [[San Juan River]] and potentially [[Grand Canyon]].

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] operates through a collaborative management structure involving federal agencies ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Western Area Power Administration]]), state representatives (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming), water developers, environmental groups, and other stakeholders. The program addresses recovery of endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River]] basin through habitat management, flow recommendations, water rights acquisition, and propagation efforts.

John Hamill continued as chairman of the Management Group. The meeting addressed ongoing legal challenges, including dismissal of the [[Uintah Mountain Club]] lawsuit without prejudice, followed by a Freedom of Information Act request. Environmental groups including the [[Environmental Defense Fund]] and [[Colorado Wildlife Federation]] were developing supporting resolutions for the Recovery Program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery program operates across multiple river systems within the [[Colorado River]] basin:

- **[[Yampa River]]**: Focus on water rights acquisition (Juniper-Cross Mountain rights valued at $6M) and instream flow quantification including sediment/channel maintenance studies
- **[[San Juan River]]**: Development of endangered fish management plan/recommendations initiated, with invitation for New Mexico and Colorado participation
- **[[Flaming Gorge]]**: Biological Opinion delayed from May 1989 to March 1990 due to 1988 drought conditions and delays in processing larval fish samples
- **15-mile reach**: Instream flow report on schedule for early December 1988; [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] evaluating delivery options for [[Ruedi Reservoir]] releases

The program expanded geographic scope to include the White, Colorado, and [[Little Snake Rivers]] for water rights acquisition beyond the [[Yampa River]]. Potential inclusion of [[Grand Canyon]] in the Recovery Program was under consideration.

## Key Findings

**Administrative Actions:**
- August 18-19, 1988 meeting summary approved without modification
- John Hamill elected to continue as Management Group chairman
- FY-89 work plan approved with errata correction to recognize Lloyd Greiner as Implementation Committee member
- Lead agencies requested to prepare detailed scopes of work by December 15, 1988

**Program Development:**
- Position to head endangered fish propagation and rearing plan implementation being readvertised due to unsatisfactory applicants; 120-day detail assignment being explored as interim solution
- Information and Education work group established FY-89 priorities: 3-5 year work plan/strategy, Recovery Program newsletter, endangered fish brochure and poster, angler information signs
- Technical Group tasked with developing peer review recommendations and evaluating [[National Academy of Science]] review of [[Glen Canyon Studies]] for relevance to recovery effort

**Water Rights and Flow Management:**
- Survey of available water rights (contingency project C1) recommended for FY-89 implementation, expanding focus to White, Colorado, and [[Little Snake Rivers]]
- Discussions ongoing for Juniper-Cross Mountain water rights acquisition with [[City of Craig]], [[Moffat County]], and [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]]; lease vs. purchase options under consideration
- [[River District]] expressed concerns that $6M offer underestimated value and that selling rights would preclude future [[Yampa River]] development
- Instream flow work plan for [[Yampa River]] developed jointly by Service and [[Resource Consultants Inc]]; comments due October 31, 1988
- Instream flow ad hoc work group report to be finalized by December 1, 1988

**Biological Opinion Status:**
- [[Flaming Gorge]] Biological Opinion completion delayed to March 1990 (from May 1989) due to 1988 drought and larval fish sample processing delays
- Opinion will address only endangered fish needs; trout fishery impacts to be addressed separately
- [[Integrated Technical Report]] to serve as Bureau's Biological Assessment

**Funding Mechanisms:**
- Service pursuing mechanisms to transfer Section 7 contributed funds and donated funds to [[Fish and Wildlife Foundation]]
- Future appropriations should contain language directing FWF to administer water rights acquisition funds
- Service may fund water rights survey with "Windy Gap" funds
- Strategy development scheduled for requesting water rights acquisition funds in FY-90 and beyond under Section 8 of CRSP

**Next Meeting:** November 22-23, 1988, Grand Junction, Colorado, including field trip of [[Grand Valley]] irrigation system and 15-mile reach
