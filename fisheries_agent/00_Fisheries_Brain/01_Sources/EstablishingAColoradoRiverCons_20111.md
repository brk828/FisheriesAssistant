## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific paper but a **congressional briefing memo and draft legislative amendment** (Owens/Miller Amendment) concerning the reauthorization of the **[[Colorado River Storage Project]] (CRSP)**. It addresses the ecological damage caused by CRSP dams and diversions and proposes establishing an **Upper Colorado Basin Conservation Council and Fund** to mitigate ongoing environmental harm, with explicit emphasis on **endangered fish species management** in the Colorado River drainage. The proposal is modeled on the **[[Northwest Power Planning Council]] (NPPC)**, credited with restoring salmon populations to the **[[Columbia River]] Basin**. While it lacks morphological or taxonomic species-level data, it provides significant policy-relevant ecological and conservation-funding data pertinent to freshwater ichthyology and habitat management in the American Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat degradation**: CRSP dams and diversion facilities have inundated or degraded approximately **800 miles of streams**, **200,000 acres of wetlands**, and hundreds of miles of **riparian corridors**, alongside loss of critical big-game rangelands.
- **Endangered species management**: The CRSP actively manipulates habitats for **7 endangered fish species** within the Upper Colorado Basin, though federal funding for their protection is described as "increasingly scarce."
- **Mitigation shortcomings**: Structural habitat mitigation efforts (e.g., in-stream structures) have only a **5–7 year functional lifespan**, with no funding mechanism for long-term maintenance.
- **Wetland mitigation costs**: Compensatory wetland creation is expensive to sustain — **80% of Utah's Division of Wildlife Resources' wetland budget** is consumed simply maintaining existing wetland mitigation sites, illustrating unsustainable "no net loss" mitigation economics.
- **Basin-wide ecosystem framing**: The proposed Council is directed to treat the **Upper Colorado River drainage as a single, unitary ecosystem**, extending conservation jurisdiction to the Colorado River between **Glen Canyon Dam** and the boundary of **Grand Canyon National Park**, plus surrounding areas used by migratory fish populations.
- **Comparative model**: The **[[Northwest Power Planning Council]]** is cited as a successful precedent for basin-scale, multi-stakeholder ecological restoration, notably crediting it with the recovery of salmon runs in the **Columbia River** system — offered as an aquatic conservation-funding template for the Colorado River Basin.

## Key Findings

- **Underfunded mitigation**: Of **$1.2 billion** spent on Central Utah Project (CUP) developments, less than **$10 million** (<1%) was allocated to fish and wildlife mitigation.
- **Proposed funding mechanism** (Colorado Basin Conservation Fund) comprised of three revenue streams:
  1. **$1/acre-foot** fee on Colorado River water depleted for **irrigation** by federally constructed facilities.
  2. **$2/acre-foot** fee on water depleted for **municipal/industrial** use (memo text vs. bill text differ slightly: memo cites "$1/acre-foot" generally and a "2 mils/kWh" power surcharge; bill text specifies $1 for irrigation and $2 for M&I).
  3. A **2 mils/kilowatt-hour** surcharge on CRSP-generated electric power (per briefing memo) — bill text instead specifies a flat **$20,000,000/year** contribution from Western Area Power Administration customers.
  4. Non-federal applicants for funding must contribute a **20% cost-share**.
- **Scale comparison**: Proposed fund is designed to be roughly **1/10th the size** of the NPPC's **$300+ million** annual budget.
- **Power rate context**: CRSP power recipients pay **9.9 mils/kWh**, substantially below Northwest public power rates (**23 mils/kWh**) and the federally mandated independent wholesale rate (**99 mils/kWh**), suggesting minimal ratepayer burden from the proposed surcharge.
- **Governance structure**: Proposed **Upper Colorado Basin Conservation Council** — 19 voting members, including state fish and wildlife directors from **Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, Utah, and Wyoming**, federal agency representatives ([[Bureau of Land Management]], [[National Park Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], [[Western Area Power Administration]]), two Indian tribal representatives, and five technical experts (Steering Committee) holding advanced degrees in fisheries/wildlife biology, aquatic/terrestrial ecology, zoology, or conservation biology.
- **Funding priorities** mandated by the bill: (1) treat the basin as a unitary ecosystem, (2) prioritize threatened/endangered species needs, (3) prioritize mitigation of CRSP-related project impacts, and (4) consider broader fish/wildlife/ecological enhancement projects.
- **Precedent invoked**: The **[[Spotted Owl]]** controversy is referenced as a cautionary example of the disruption caused by inadequate early management of threatened/endangered species — used to justify proactive, dedicated funding for Colorado River Basin endangered fish.
