# Phytoplankton Water Quality Relationships in U.S. Lakes, Part VIII: Algae Associated With or Responsible for Water Quality Problems

#phycology #limnology #phytoplankton

## Executive Summary

This EPA [[Environmental Monitoring Systems Laboratory]] project summary (EPA-600/S3-80-100, February 1981) presents environmental data for freshwater algae linked to water quality problems in lakes. The work covers species and genera associated with six problem categories: [[Eutrophication]], [[Organic Pollution]], [[Taste and Odor]], [[Filter Clogging]], [[Toxicity]], and [[Aesthetic Nuisance]]. The data come from algal occurrence records and physical-chemical measurements from 250 lakes in Eastern and Southeastern States, sampled in 1973.

The central finding is that most algae associated with eutrophic conditions also occur in nutrient-poor waters. Their value as indicators of nutrient enrichment is therefore limited. The authors state that the presence of a "eutrophic" form does not necessarily mean the water body will have associated problems.

## Background & Description

The study identifies algal species reported to be responsible for, or associated with, specific lake water quality problems and reports the environmental conditions surrounding their occurrence. The authors argue that understanding these conditions is essential to any rational approach to controlling algal problems.

Stated applications include:
- Helping states meet lake classification requirements under [[Section 305b]] of the [[Federal Water Pollution Control Act]].
- Monitoring the success of [[Clean Lakes Program]] restoration efforts under Section 314.
- Supporting development of water quality criteria under [[Section 304 Water Quality Criteria]].
- Informing predictions of biological responses to nutrient and other parameters by area-wide planners under [[Section 208 Area-Wide Planning]].

The report is Part VIII of a series titled *Phytoplankton Water Quality Relationships in U.S. Lakes*. Its authors are W. D. Taylor, L. R. Williams, S. C. Hern, V. W. Lambou (EPA Project Officer), C. L. Howard, and F. A. Morris and M. K. Morris (University of Nevada, Las Vegas). The full report (Order No. PB 81-156 831, $23.00) is available through the [[National Technical Information Service]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling base:**
- Approximately 25,000 phytoplankton records and 750,000 physical and chemical data points were analyzed.
- The data came from 250 lakes in Eastern and Southeastern States sampled during 1973.
- Comparisons tested the environmental conditions associated with the absence, presence, and dominance of problem algae.

**Taxa coverage:**
- The Project Summary abstract reports data for 117 species and 24 genera.
- The procedure section states that the study summarizes conditions for more than half of 220 species and 34 genera associated with one or more problem categories. The two counts differ in the source and are reported here as given.

**Habitat notes:** Taxa with no data are mostly attached forms (on rocks, sticks, or other algae) or free-living in shallow water among other vegetation. Their rarity in the plankton is expected.

**Dominant problem taxa:** Certain blue-green algae recur across problem types:
- [[Microcystis Aeruginosa]] and [[Gloeotrichia Echinulata]] were each associated with five of the six problem categories.
- [[Aphanizomenon Flos-Aquae]], [[Coelosphaerium Kuetzingianum]], [[Anabaena Circinalis]], and [[Oscillatoria Rubescens]] were each associated with at least three categories.

All major algal groups, including [[Diatoms]] and green algae, were associated with at least one problem. The [[Blue-Green Algae]] group, however, showed the most repeated associations.

## Key Findings

1. **Limited indicator value for eutrophication.** Algae commonly associated with eutrophic conditions also occur in nutrient-poor waters. With few exceptions, they are weak indicators of [[Nutrient Enrichment]].

2. **Presence does not equal problem.** A eutrophic-associated form in a lake does not necessarily mean that water body will experience associated problems.

3. **Broad problem associations.** Many taxa are linked to several problem types. The most widely associated are the blue-green algae listed above, followed by taxa such as [[Diatoms]] and green algae.

Table 1 contains too many entries to reproduce here. Its genera include [[Asterionella]], [[Ceratium]], [[Cyclotella]], [[Diatoma]], [[Melosira]], [[Stephanodiscus]], [[Synedra]], and [[Tabellaria]].

5. **Implications for management.** The authors state that continued review of species-specific physical and chemical data will:
   - Modify existing approaches and develop new approaches to biological classification and water quality monitoring.
   - Improve the ability to predict and prevent or control algal problems.
   - Provide input for water quality criteria and area-wide planning.
