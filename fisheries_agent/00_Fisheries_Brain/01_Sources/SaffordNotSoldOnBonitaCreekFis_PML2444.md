# Safford Council Questions Proposed Bonita Creek Fish Barrier

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-chub

## Executive Summary

A proposed [[Fish Barrier]] on [[Bonita Creek]] would create critical habitat for the endangered-status candidate [[Gila Chub]] by removing non-native fish and treating the stream with [[Piscicide]]. The [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) presented the project to the [[Safford City Council]] during the scoping stage of an environmental assessment. Council members, city staff and the utilities director voiced doubts, mainly about restrictions on public access, the loss of game fish, and the future use of capped city wells above the barrier. The BLM argued the barrier could prevent the Gila Chub from being listed as endangered. The article reports the discussion and an upcoming public open house; it contains no survey data or population counts.

## Background & Description

The article, written by managing editor Aimee Staten, reports on a Monday night council meeting at which BLM representative [[Bonnie Winslow]] described the scoping portion of the environmental assessment. The project is described as a conservation measure for the [[Gila Chub]], a native stream fish. The article does not give the Gila Chub's scientific name, physical description, or life history.

Public input is central to the project. The article notes that, unless enough people lodge complaints, the barrier will be built. An open house is scheduled for Thursday, July 14, from 7:00 to 8:30 p.m. at the Graham County General Services building.

Key participants named in the article:
- **Bonnie Winslow**, BLM, who presented the project
- **Heidi Blasius**, BLM, who addressed listing concerns
- **Ron Green**, Safford Mayor, who asked about keeping sport fish
- **Jay Howe**, Utilities Director, who raised well access concerns
- **Long**, Safford city manager (cited by name in the article)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Proposed treatment method.** The plan involves three steps:
1. Remove all native fish above the barrier.
2. Inject the stream with [[Piscicide]] to kill non-native species.
3. Return the native fish to the stream.

**Non-native species targeted.** The article lists nine non-native fish to be eliminated:
- [[Green Sunfish]]
- [[Smallmouth Bass]]
- [[Channel Catfish]]
- Flathead minnow
- Red shiner
- [[Common Carp]]
- [[Mosquitofish]]
- Black bullhead
- Yellow bullhead

**Water supply.** Capped wells lie above the proposed barrier. Council concerns focus on whether listing the Gila Chub as endangered would restrict access to these wells if the city needs them. Howe reported that a larger water pipeline has already been installed in the area and that the city plans to use the wells as it grows.

**Regional precedent.** Council members compared the proposal to the [[Red Squirrel]] conflict on [[Mount Graham]]. The city manager said that during a recent visit, the public could not use most of the recreational areas there because of protections for the squirrel. A council member summarized the concern as having to "swear an oath that you won't step on a squirrel."

## Key Findings

- **The project is at the scoping stage.** The BLM is preparing an environmental assessment, and a public open house is scheduled for July 14 at 7:00 to 8:30 p.m.
- **The purpose is habitat for the Gila Chub.** The barrier would create critical habitat by eliminating non-native fish above it.
- **Nine non-native species would be removed.** Removal would be carried out with piscicide, after native fish are temporarily taken out and later returned.
- **Officials are divided on the project.** Staff and council members expressed doubts, citing the Mount Graham precedent and loss of public access.
- **Game fish concerns were raised.** Mayor Green asked why smallmouth bass and channel catfish could not remain for sport fishing.
- **Well access is a major local concern.** Howe and the city manager worried that an endangered listing could limit access to capped wells above the barrier. Winslow said these concerns should be included in the city's comments and written into the agreement between Safford, the BLM and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].
- **The BLM says the barrier may prevent listing.** Blasius argued the project could protect the Gila Chub from being added to the endangered species list, which would remove the need for concern.
- **Public comment could affect the outcome.** The article states the barrier will be built unless enough people lodge complaints.
