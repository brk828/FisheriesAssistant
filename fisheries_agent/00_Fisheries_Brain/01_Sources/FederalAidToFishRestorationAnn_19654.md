# Water Quality Investigations - Idaho Fish & Game Department Annual Report F 34-R-8 (1965)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #water-quality #toxicology #eutrophication #pesticide-impacts

## Executive Summary

This annual completion report documents water quality investigations conducted by the [[Idaho Fish and Game Department]] from November 1, 1964 to October 31, 1965 across multiple Idaho water bodies. The study comprised two primary jobs: (1) general water quality investigations focusing on nutrient loading and eutrophication in [[Cascade Reservoir]] and [[Lake Lowell]], and (2) toxicity studies examining pesticide effects on fish and fluoride toxicity testing. Key findings include significant nutrient additions (phosphates and nitrates) to Cascade Reservoir from agricultural return flows and raw sewage, extreme eutrophication in Lake Lowell with phosphate concentrations reaching 1.45 ppm, detection of chlorinated hydrocarbon pesticides in [[C.J. Strike Reservoir]] fish tissues, and determination of 96-hour median tolerance limits for fluoride toxicity to [[Rainbow Trout]]. The investigations revealed widespread eutrophic conditions characterized by blue-green algae blooms, oxygen deficiencies in deeper waters, and potential pesticide contamination affecting warm-water fisheries.

## Background & Description

### Cascade Reservoir
[[Cascade Reservoir]] is an impoundment of the [[North Fork Payette River]] in central Idaho, formed in a wide, shallow basin with sloping shorelines. The reservoir receives water from an infertile stream heading in the central Idaho granitic batholith, with naturally low dissolved solids (18-20 ppm), hardness (8 ppm), and alkalinity (10 ppm). Despite these oligotrophic source waters, the reservoir exhibits high biological productivity. The village of [[McCall]] discharges raw sewage into the North Fork approximately one-half mile downstream from [[Payette Lake]]. Multiple tributaries enter from the east side, flowing through cattle ranches and irrigated farmland, while smaller west side streams drain forested [[West Mountain]].

### Lake Lowell
[[Lake Lowell]] is a 10,000-acre off-river reservoir of the [[Boise River]] located near [[Nampa]] in southwestern Idaho. The reservoir has a gross capacity of 177,150 acre-feet with maximum depth of approximately 30 feet, though large portions are only 5-10 feet deep. Formed by two 30-foot high earth embankments and operated by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] for irrigation, the reservoir is managed as a national wildlife refuge by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Over one million waterfowl overwinter annually on this water body. Water is transported from the Boise River via the concrete-lined [[New York Canal]].

### C.J. Strike Reservoir
[[C.J. Strike Reservoir]] is a 7,500-acre impoundment in south-central Idaho, seven miles southeast of [[Grandview]]. Storage began in 1952, backing water 22 miles up the [[Snake River]] and 8 miles up the [[Bruneau River]]. The reservoir has 250,000 acre-feet capacity with minimal annual fluctuation (average 3 feet). Owned by [[Idaho Power Company]], it is operated for power generation and supports warm-water fisheries, particularly [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) and [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Eutrophication Processes
The investigations documented advanced eutrophication across multiple Idaho reservoirs. [[Cascade Reservoir]] exhibited extensive blooms of blue-green algae (*Aphanizomenon holsaticum*) during summer months, with subsequent oxygen deficits in the hypolimnion due to algal decomposition. Despite mineral content similar to oligotrophic [[Payette Lake]], Cascade Reservoir produces significantly more fish food and supports excellent growth of all fish species, attributed to its shallow basin, sloping shorelines, and surrounding fertile soils versus Payette Lake's steep-sided deep basin.

[[Lake Lowell]] demonstrated extreme eutrophic conditions with dissolved salt content averaging 310 ppm compared to 40-88 ppm in the inflowing [[Boise River]]. The wide, shallow basin at low elevation with warm summers creates conditions highly conducive to biological productivity. Prodigious blooms of *Aphanizomenon holsaticum* along with other algae forms covered the surface during warm months. Decomposition of algae and tons of waterfowl fecal material on the reservoir bottom contributed significant biochemical oxygen demand (BOD).

### Nutrient Sources and Loading
Multiple nutrient sources were identified:

**Agricultural Return Flows**: East-side tributaries of [[Cascade Reservoir]] transported variable concentrations of soluble phosphates and nitrates from irrigation return water passing through cattle ranches and farmland.

**Municipal Sewage**: Raw sewage effluent from [[McCall]] entered the [[North Fork Payette River]] approximately one-half mile below [[Payette Lake]], contributing nutrients directly to Cascade Reservoir.

**Domestic Wastes**: Summer home areas along west-side tributaries contributed comparatively high phosphorus concentrations from raw domestic wastes.

**Waterfowl Contributions**: At [[Lake Lowell]], decomposition of fecal material from over one million overwintering waterfowl significantly enriched bottom sediments with nutrients.

### Oxygen-Temperature Dynamics
Thermal stratification and oxygen depletion characterized summer conditions. In [[Lake Lowell]], dissolved oxygen ranged from supersaturation at the surface to zero on the bottom. By August, oxygen levels 10-15 feet below surface were approximately half of surface concentrations. Surface temperatures ranged from lower to mid-70s°F from June through August, with lowest bottom temperature recorded at 60.5°F. The shallow basin allowed wind action to easily circulate much of the water mass, though deeper areas still developed stratification.

### Pesticide Contamination
[[C.J. Strike Reservoir]] investigations revealed chlorinated hydrocarbon pesticide presence in both water and fish tissues. During spring 1964, young-of-the-year [[Largemouth Bass]] observed in distress along shorelines contained heptachlor (30 ppb), DDT (8 ppb), and dieldrin (7 ppb). Water samples from May 1965 during spring runoff showed relatively high aldrin concentrations (up to 10,000 ppb) in the turbid [[Bruneau River]] arm. Later samples yielded only minute amounts of DDT and metabolites. Fish analyses revealed [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) contained highest hydrocarbon concentrations (260 ppb DDE), possibly related to their phytoplankton diet, as phytoplankton assimilates hydrocarbon materials.

## Key Findings

### Cascade Reservoir Nutrient Investigations
Monthly water samples collected from April through October 1965 from tributaries ([[Gold Fork Creek]], [[Boulder Creek]], [[North Fork Payette River]], [[Lake Fork Creek]], and unnamed west-side tributary) plus reservoir outlet revealed:

- Tributaries transported measurable nutrient loads, particularly phosphorus, into the reservoir
- West-side tributary showed comparatively high phosphorus concentrations attributed to raw domestic wastes from summer homes
- Nutrient additions from agricultural return flows and municipal sewage contribute to eutrophic conditions
- Extensive blooms of *Aphanizomenon holsaticum* occur during summer months
- Oxygen deficits develop in hypolimnion during summer stratification
- Despite low mineral content similar to [[Payette Lake]], the reservoir exhibits significantly higher productivity due to shallow basin morphometry and nutrient enrichment

### Lake Lowell Water Quality Assessment
Summer 1965 surveys (June-September) documented:

**Chemical Parameters**:
- Soluble orthophosphate concentrations: 0.23 to 1.45 ppm (extremely high)
- Total dissolved solids: averaged 310 ppm (versus 40-88 ppm in source water)
- Hardness: 22-98 ppm
- Alkalinity: 26-80 ppm
- pH: 7.2-9.2 (highly variable, reaching alkaline extremes)
- Sulfates: 8.5-29.0 ppm

**Physical Parameters**:
- Surface temperatures: lower to mid-70s°F (June-August)
- Thermal stratification present in deeper portions
- Dissolved oxygen: supersaturation at surface to zero on bottom
- By August, oxygen at 10-15 feet depth approximately 50% of surface levels

**Biological Conditions**:
- Prodigious blooms of blue-green algae (*Aphanizomenon holsaticum*)
- Fishery deteriorated from excellent (1960) to poor perch, mediocre bass, and fair bullhead fishery (1965)

### C.J. Strike Reservoir Pesticide Assessment
Investigations from April through October 1965 established:

**Water Contamination**:
- May samples from [[Bruneau River]] arm during spring runoff: high aldrin concentrations (2,500-10,000 ppb)
- Post-runoff samples: minute amounts of DDT and metabolites only
- July samples: all negative
- August-September: trace levels (0.0-6.0 ppb) of various compounds

**Fish Tissue Contamination** (May-June 1965):
- [[Carp]]: 260 ppb DDE, 4 ppb DDD (highest concentrations)
- [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*): 41-44 ppb DDE, 5 ppb aldrin, 10 ppb DDT
- [[Black Crappie]]: 41 ppb DDE, 8 ppb DDD, 5 ppb aldrin (May); 40 ppb aldrin, 30 ppb DDT, 10 ppb dieldrin (June)
- [[Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus oregonensis*): 53 ppb DDE, 10 ppb DDD
- Suckers (*Catostomus* spp.): 18-41 ppb DDE, 8-9 ppb DDD

**Fish Community Composition** (by numerical abundance):
- Largescale sucker (*Catostomus macrocheilus*) and bridgelip sucker (*C. columbianus*): 43%
- Carp: 23%
- Chiselmouth (*Acrocheilus alutaceus*): 8%
- Squawfish: 7%
- Black crappie: 5.5%
- Peamouth (*Mylocheilus caurinus*): 5.5%
- Yellow perch: 4%
- Bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*): 3%
- Largemouth bass: 0.6%
- Brown bullhead (*Ictalurus nebulosus*): 0.6%

### Fluoride Toxicity Bioassays
Laboratory standing-jar bioassays using hatchery [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) fingerlings (average 60 mm length) and sodium fluoride as toxicant in [[Snake River]] water (comparable to [[Portneuf River]] chemistry) at 55°F determined:

- 96-hour median tolerance limit (TLM): 160-220 ppm fluoride
- Concentration required to kill 50% of test fish in 96 hours: 160-220 ppm
- Industrial fluoride effluent target: reduced below 3 ppm (previously as high as 57 ppm)
- Results consistent with literature showing higher toxicity in soft water and lower temperatures

### Management Recommendations

**Cascade Reservoir**:
1. Conduct detailed eutrophication study to assess magnitude and guide management
2. Monitor indicators of organic enrichment:
   - Slow year-over-year decrease in hypolimnetic dissolved oxygen
   - Increase in dissolved solids, especially nitrogen and phosphorus
   - Steady decrease in light penetration
   - Increase in suspended solids, especially organic materials

**Lake Lowell**:
- Comprehensive limnological study needed to determine oxygen-temperature limitations on aquatic biota
- Research influence of chemical pesticides on aquatic environment
- Measure nutrient loading from inflowing waters and bottom muds

**C.J. Strike Reservoir**:
- Full-scale quantitative sampling and bioassay program to measure pesticide effects on environment
- Long-term research to determine effects on aquatic life beyond presence/uptake documentation
