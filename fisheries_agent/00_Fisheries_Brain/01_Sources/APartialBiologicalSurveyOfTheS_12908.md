# A Partial Biological Survey of the Spring River Drainage: Collecting Sites, Basic Limnological Data, and Mollusks

#ichthyology #marine-biology #unionidae #freshwater-mollusca

Note on scope: this paper is a freshwater mollusk and limnological survey. It does not report fish data; the author states that crayfish and vertebrates would be treated in later contributions.

## Executive Summary

Branley A. Branson's 1966 (published 1967) report in the *Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science* is Part I of a partial biological survey of the [[Spring River Drainage]], a tributary system of the Neosho (Grand) River spanning Kansas, Oklahoma and Missouri. It documents 92 numbered collecting stations (with additional historical museum records), water-quality and bacteriological data, and an annotated checklist of pelecypod and gastropod mollusks collected between 1961 and 1964. The study found strong pollution gradients in [[Cow Creek]] and [[Turkey Creek]], relatively unpolluted [[Shoal Creek]], and a diverse unionid fauna, including the rarely recorded [[Ptychobranchus fasciolaris]].

## Background & Description

- **Scope and aims:** No comprehensive faunal study of Spring River had previously been published. The paper is explicitly partial, offering site ecology and mollusk data as a foundation for later work.
- **Field effort:** Collections spanned 1961 to 1964 and involved students and staff from Kansas State College of Pittsburg, plus collaborators from the Missouri Conservation Commission and several universities. Data from the U.S. Public Health Service (Joe K. Neel) on Shoal Creek were incorporated.
- **Methods:**
  - Specimens were taken with gill and trammel nets, seines, electric shockers and SCUBA gear.
  - Plankton was sampled with a 30 cm cone net of 6,000 meshes per square cm.
  - Water quality followed *Standard Methods* (American Public Health Association). Measured variables included pH, carbonate and bicarbonate (alkalinity), free CO2, dissolved oxygen (Alsterberg–Winkler azide method) and specific conductance.
  - Bacteria were enumerated as total counts and [[Coliform Bacteria]] on agar pour-plates at 37 °C after 24 h, with lactose-fermentation confirmation.
- **Museum abbreviations:** KU (University of Kansas), M (University of Missouri), OSU (Oklahoma State University), NO (Northeastern Oklahoma A. and M. College).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical setting.** The Spring River rises from springs near Aurora, Lawrence County, Missouri, and flows southwest across Pennsylvanian, Permian and Cretaceous formations to join the Neosho about six miles north of Wyandotte, Ottawa County, Oklahoma. Elevation falls from 1,542 ft at the headwaters to about 780 ft at the mouth. The river runs through forested and farmed Ozarkian foothills, with a gravel and boulder bed except where siltation is heavy.

**Tributary conditions:**

- **[[Cow Creek]]** (about 30 miles long, Crawford and Cherokee counties, Kansas) is heavily polluted. Station 6 receives raw sewage from Frontenac. Station 7 receives meat-packing floor washings and dairy and water-treatment wastes. Stations 14, 16 and 17 are acidic (pH 3.5) from strip-mine effluent, and only insects were collected there. The author concluded that the stream was unfit for human recreation.
- **[[Shoal Creek]]** (headwaters near Exeter, Barry County, Missouri; 76 miles to the confluence) is relatively unpolluted and used as a municipal water source by Neosho and Joplin. Sewage, cannery waste and effluent from Monett and Pierce City enter upstream reaches.
- **[[Turkey Creek]]** is described as "veritably a cess pool," receiving roofing, lead and insulation plant wastes and Joplin sewage.
- **Center Creek** is badly polluted by a powder factory and by sewage from Oronogo. Honey, Five-Mile, Lost and Sycamore creeks and Warren's and Flint branches were only moderately affected.

**Water chemistry and bacteria.** Tables 4 and 5 give total bacteria, coliforms and specific conductance by station. Examples from Table 4 (Cow Creek):

- Station 10: 120,000 total bacteria/ml and 3,000 coliforms/ml, the highest count in the set.
- Station 9: 4,000 total bacteria/ml and 670 coliforms/ml.
- Station 12: 70 total bacteria/ml and 5 coliforms/ml.

Specific conductance is reported in µmhos. Acidic stations had markedly different conductance values, and station 14 was too acidic to count bacteria.

**Biological indicators.** Much of Cow Creek's benthic habitat was occupied by polysaprobic organisms, which correlated with the bacterial findings. Physa anatina, a pollution-tolerant snail, was abundant there, with 303 individuals at station 38 on Shoal Creek noted as a large population.

## Key Findings

1. **Collection effort:** 92 numbered collecting stations are mapped in Figures 1 to 5, including historical museum records from 1932 to 1960. Only stations attended by the author were mapped.

2. **Pollution gradient:** Cow Creek and Turkey Creek are the most degraded. Shoal Creek is the cleanest of the major tributaries. Acid mine drainage (pH 3.5) eliminated most macroinvertebrate life at several Cow Creek sites.

3. **Mollusk diversity:**
   - Bivalves: 4 species of Sphaeriidae (in *Sphaerium* and *Pisidium*, following Herrington 1962) and 31 kinds (species and subspecies) of unionid mussels from 22 genera, based on 878 specimens.
   - Gastropods: 11 species in 7 genera, totaling 5,450 specimens.

4. **Notable unionids:**
   - *Ptychobranchus fasciolaris* was represented by a single live specimen (98.2 mm long), the first record in Kansas since 1890 according to the cited handbook.
   - *Alasmidonta marginata* was reported from Kansas, with specimens resembling southern Illinois material.
   - *Lasmigona costata* was extremely rare, known previously only from the Neosho.
   - *Lampsilis luteolus* was the most common and widely distributed species, tolerant of pollution, and associated with the host fish *Lepomis macrochirus*.
   - *Elliptio dilatatus* was among the most abundant species in Spring River proper.

5. **Habitat specificity:** Several species show clear habitat preferences: *Sphaerium transversum* and *S. partumeium* favor sluggish waters and are common in Cow Creek. *Actinonaias carinata* is intolerant of pollution and siltation.

6. **Biogeography:** The fauna is Ozarkian and most closely related to the Cumberland region. The presence of *Alasmidonta* and *Lasmigona* suggests possible stream capture of the Spring River Drainage from the White River system in Arkansas.

7. **Life history observations:** *Anodonta grandis* glochidia infested the fins and gills of *Lepomis cyanellus* and a larval *Necturus maculosus* in aquarium trials, and dropped off after two weeks. Mudalia potosiensis showed a banded headwater form at stations 67 and 68.

8. **Taxonomic notes:** Following Herrington (1962), *Musculium* is placed in *Sphaerium*. *Ferrissia shimekii* is treated as a synonym of *F. fragilis* (Basch 1964).

9. **Stated gap:** The author notes that crayfish and vertebrates (including fishes) would be treated in future papers, so this report offers no fish data.
