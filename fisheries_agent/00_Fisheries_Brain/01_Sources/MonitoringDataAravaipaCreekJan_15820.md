# Aravaipa Creek Fish Monitoring Memorandum, January-February 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from [[Arizona State University]] (Department of Zoology, Tempe) reports seine-based fish sampling on [[Aravaipa Creek]] on 23 January and 7 February 1993. Six sites were sampled: three upstream and three downstream of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]]. Sampling followed major flooding since mid-December that had reshaped the channel. The survey recorded five species, dominated by [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Spikedace]], with [[Speckled Dace]], [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] also present. The memo is addressed to several state and federal agencies and conservation partners.

## Background & Description

The memo was prepared by W. L. Minckley, Tanmy Velasca and Chris Reimus of Arizona State University and sent to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], ASU Center for Environmental Studies, [[The Nature Conservancy]], the [[Bureau of Land Management]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

**Sampling methods**
- Mainstream channels were seined over a total of 228 m.
- Gear: a 1/8-inch mesh, 6-foot seine, plus a 1/4-inch mesh, 15-foot seine when necessary (see [[Seine Sampling]]).
- Collectors in January: Reimus, Rich Broughton (ASU), Russ Timmons (ASU) and Laura Cratz. In February: Velasca and Keith Malmas (ASU).

**Sites**
- Upstream of the wilderness area: gauge station, Chimney Rock and the TNC guest house.
- Downstream of the wilderness area: Wood's Ranch (gauge station), White's Ranch and the sycamore tree site.
- The east-end (upstream) reach was described as runs, riffles and pools with sand, gravel, cobble and boulder substrates. Mean widths ranged from about 3 to 18 m and mean depths from about 0.2 to 0.5 m. The west-end (downstream) reach was dominated by boulder riffles and runs, and substrate could not be observed at one site because of turbidity and velocity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flood disturbance.** Catastrophic flooding since mid-December 2024 reshaped the channel. The memo reports:
- **Upstream (east end):** High flows scoured the creekbed from Rattlesnake Canyon to the wilderness area parking lot, and a path about 140 m wide was cleared through much of the upper end. Fine sediment plugs were deposited, and at the guest house reach most of the riparian band was lost. The wilderness area parking lot was destroyed, incising about 2 m. At Chimney Rock, a band about 100 m wide was scoured and more than 1 m of fine sediment was deposited.
- **Downstream (west end):** The White's Ranch channel shifted and incised, destroying much of the terrace the White family lives on. At the sycamore tree site, the old sycamore stump was washed about 220 m downstream. Sampling at Wood's Ranch was limited to the margins because of large substrate and discharge.
- **Incision and recovery:** Creeks were already incising the new sediment plugs, forming high-gradient riffles at their bases.

**Water and weather.** The stream was very [[Turbidity]]-affected in January. In February, air temperatures averaged 17.5 °C, and a low-pressure system with rain brought much colder conditions later in the day. Water temperature averaged 15.0 °C at the east end.

**Breeding condition.** Fish were in various stages of breeding condition. Several seine hauls held what appeared to be breeding pairs, but no nests were found when larger substrates were inspected.

## Key Findings

**Species codes used in the memo:** AGCH = *Agosia chrysogaster* (Longfin Dace); RHOS = *Rhinichthys osculus* (Speckled Dace); MEFU = *Meda fulgida* (Spikedace); LECY = *Lepomis cyanellus* (Green Sunfish); MISA = *Micropterus salmoides* (Largemouth Bass). The memo lists about ten further column codes (e.g., GIRO, TICO, PACL, CAIN, CYCA, CYLU, PIPR, AMME, AMNA, GAAF), but their identities are not given in the text.

**Catch summaries (counts as reported; some OCR values were ambiguous):**

| Reach | Seine hauls | Jan total | Dominant species (Jan count) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper (headwaters to above Turkey Creek confluence) | 126 (68 empty) | 349 | MEFU 212; AGCH 97; AGCH about 27.8% |
| Middle (Turkey Creek confluence to Wagner Ranch) | 117 | 146 | MEFU 64; AGCH 23 (about 15.8%); MEFU about 43.8% |
| Lower (Wood's Ranch to San Pedro River) | 92 | 219 | AGCH 125 (about 57.1%); MEFU 31 (about 14.2%); a third species at 46 (about 21.0%) |

**Main takeaways**
- [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Spikedace]] made up most of the catch in all three reaches.
- The lower reach had the highest proportion of Longfin Dace. The middle and upper reaches had more Spikedace.
- [[Speckled Dace]] was scarce, with one individual recorded at each of the upper and middle reaches.
- Non-native [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] were recorded at low numbers.
- The percentages for the February sampling were partly illegible in the source. Column totals were checked against the counts where possible.
- The memo presents these data as a post-flood baseline. Recovery of fish populations after the channel disturbance is not assessed.
