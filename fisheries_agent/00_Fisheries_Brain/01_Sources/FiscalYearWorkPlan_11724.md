# Recovery Implementation Program FY 1996 Work Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) document, dated September 7, 1995, sets out the work plan and budget for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] for Fiscal Year 1996 (October 1, 1995 to September 30, 1996). The [[Implementation Committee]] approved the plan. It compiles 98 scopes of work from state and federal agencies, universities, contractors and Program staff, and it implements priority actions from the Program's Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP, March 1995).

The recommended FY 96 Program budget is $2.99 million, a 2.6 percent increase over FY 95. Including Reclamation capital funds and other sources, the total FY 96 budget is $10.93 million. The plan is organized around seven recovery elements: instream flow identification and protection, habitat restoration, nonnative fish control, propagation and genetics management, research and monitoring, information and education, and program management.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program is a cooperative effort among federal and state agencies, water users and environmental representatives. Its Implementation Committee includes representatives from the Service, [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the Colorado, Utah and Wyoming state governments, the [[Western Area Power Administration]], environmental groups, Upper Basin water users, and the Colorado River Energy Distributors Association (nonvoting). The Program's Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP) is its operational plan for identifying actions needed to recover the endangered fishes.

The fishes covered are the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. The document also refers to the Gila complex (bonytail, humpback and roundtail chubs). Management objectives are being developed for the Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker and humpback chub.

The annual work plan follows a fixed calendar. Principal investigators submit annual reports in December, technical and management committees review drafts in February and July, and the Implementation Committee approves the draft plan on September 7 and the final plan takes effect October 1. The goal is for contracts to be issued and funds allocated by December 1995.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Upper Basin rivers named in the plan include the [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], [[Gunnison River]], [[Yampa River]], [[White River]], [[Duchesne River]] and [[Price River]]. The Program's central ecological problem is the effect of altered flows, dams, diversions and nonnative fishes on the endangered species.

Key habitat concerns named in the document:
- **Barriers:** [[Redlands Diversion Dam]] has blocked upstream passage on the Gunnison River since near the turn of the century. [[Price-Stubb Diversion Dam]], a 15-foot dam, has blocked passage for about the same period. On the Yampa River, 119 diversion structures were identified between Hayden and Dinosaur National Monument, 31 of which may be barriers to passage, especially at low flows. Taylor Draw Dam (built 1985) cut off the upper one-third of the home range of Colorado squawfish on the White River.
- **Flow regulation:** Operations at [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and the [[Aspinall Unit]] are being studied for biological opinions. Flow effects on larval and young-of-the-year squawfish, nursery backwaters, and winter survival are central questions.
- **Nonnative fishes:** Northern pike, largemouth bass, green sunfish, smallmouth bass and cyprinids are named as predators or competitors. The Program states that established nonnative fishes are unlikely to be eliminated, so its aims are prevention and active control.
- **Floodplains and contaminants:** Floodplain restoration depends on relative floodability, landowner cooperation, restoration cost, fish species composition and contaminant levels. Selenium tolerance of razorback suckers in floodplain habitats is being tested at Walter Walker and Adobe Creek.

## Key Findings

**Instream flow identification and protection**
- Field studies on the [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and [[Aspinall Unit]] are being completed and synthesis begun. Flow recommendations are being developed for the White River and upper Colorado River (Palisade to Rifle).
- The [[Yampa River Operation and Management Plan]] is the largest single item, with a $1,500,000 placeholder in the FY 96 budget. The Implementation Committee will make a go/no-go decision in December 1995.
- Water acquisition relies on [[Section 7 Funds]], which are charged at $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion, adjusted for inflation (to $13.04 per acre-foot in FY 96). These funds are held by the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]]. Projected FY 96 expenditures of $115,900 leave an estimated balance of $134,100.
- A long-term lease of 2,000 acre-feet from Steamboat Lake would stabilize late-summer flows in the Yampa River.

**Habitat restoration**
- Redlands passage is scheduled for completion by April 1996, with evaluation using radio-tagged squawfish and a fish trap.
- Planning and design for Price-Stubb passage will use FY 95 carryover funds, with a total outyear cost estimated at $336,000 to $1,800,000.
- Passage at Grand Valley diversion structures is on hold pending the Redlands evaluation, with $125,000 for planning and design.
- Floodplain restoration (FY 96 budget $1,874,800) will continue hypothesis testing at controlled facilities, including Old Charlie Wash and a gravel pit at 29 5/8 Road. Levee removal, land acquisition and basinwide monitoring will begin.

**Nonnative fish control**
- A Nonnative Fish Control Strategic Plan will guide future action, with $10,000 set aside in FY 96 for implementation (total outyear cost estimated at $1.5 million).
- Three new projects were identified: fish control structures for large reservoirs, control devices in gravel pit outlets on the Colorado River (funded by a private donation, $17,000), and pond reclamation within the 10-year floodplain ($100,000; outyear cost $900,000). The pond reclamation project is approved unless a major reason to stop it is found.
- Selective mechanical removal of nonnative predators from the Gunnison River within Colorado is being tested ($15,000).

**Propagation and genetics management**
- The primary refuge for the Upper Colorado River will be near Grand Junction, using ponds at the Horsethief State Wildlife Area. About 14 acres of off-channel ponds are needed for the proposed razorback sucker experimental stocking. Existing ponds in the Grand Valley will be leased and modified.
- Ouray Endangered Fish Facility serves as the primary refuge for Green River razorbacks. A backup refuge at Craig, Colorado, is planned.
- The [[Gila Taxonomy Study]] (mitochondrial DNA, FY 96 cost $81,300) was delayed several years after the death of its principal investigator. Morphometric and allozyme portions are nearly complete.
- Developing pedigreed family lots requires small rearing ponds (0.2 acre). The target is 25 paired matings within three years.

**Research, monitoring and data management**
- The [[Standardized Monitoring Program]] (FY 96 budget $126,000) will place more emphasis on monitoring trends in nonnative fishes.
- Larval fish identification ($122,600) and database management ($27,400) support the monitoring program.
- Interim management objectives ($50,000) will identify life history data gaps.

**Information, education and public involvement**
- Public involvement plans cover roughly 15 projects, including Colorado instream flow protection, Ruedi Reservoir releases, Flaming Gorge releases ($45,000, funded by Reclamation), Gunnison nonnative removal, and Utah flow protection.
- The Information and Education budget is $22,000, with an outyear cost of $65,000. Planned products include a newsletter, a simplified annual report, and a grand opening for the Redlands fish ladder.

**Program management**
- Program management costs are about 11 percent of the FY 96 budget. The Service's program management is budgeted at $682,200.

**Budget safeguards**
- Place-holder projects that lack finalized scopes of work by their review dates (for example, the Yampa operation plan in December 1995 and pond reclamation in January 1996) will have their funds reallocated by the Management Committee.
