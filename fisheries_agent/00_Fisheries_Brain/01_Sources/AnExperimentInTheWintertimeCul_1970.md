## Executive Summary

This study evaluated the feasibility of overwintering [[Rainbow Trout]] in floating cages in a southern Illinois pond that is normally used during warm months to raise [[Channel Catfish]]. The goal was to determine whether trout culture could economically supplement catfish production by using the same infrastructure during the cold season. Trout were stocked in February 1970 and fed through mid-May, with water temperatures ranging from 35.2°F (1.8°C) to 73.4°F (23.0°C). Growth and food conversion were markedly better when water stayed below 59°F (15°C); performance deteriorated sharply as temperatures rose and dissolved oxygen fell. Although high initial handling mortality and overfeeding made the pilot trial an economic failure, follow-up trials begun in November 1970 showed much improved survival and conversion. The authors conclude that ~60°F (15.5°C) represents a practical "exchange point" between trout and catfish culture in cages, allowing a single cage system to be used year-round.

## Background & Description

- **Initial stocking size (Rainbow Trout):** average total length 8.1 inches; average weight 0.21 lb.
- **Growth to first sampling (April 13):** average weight 0.331 lb (east cage), 0.354 lb (west cage).
- **Final size (May 15, harvest):** average weight 0.42 lb (east cage) and 0.43 lb (west cage); overall survivor average 0.425 lb.
- Survivors more than doubled their initial body weight (0.21 lb → 0.42 lb) over the 87-day feeding period.
- No detailed external morphological descriptors (fin counts, coloration) were reported; the study is physiological/production-focused rather than taxonomic.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Cages (4 ft wide × 11.5 ft long × [depth] ft, floated on styrofoam to ~3.33 ft submerged depth; ~60 cubic feet / 1.7 m³ water volume) suspended in a 1-acre pond with 6-foot-deep water; two cages placed 10 feet apart.
- **Thermal regime:** Water temperature ranged from 35.2°F (1.8°C) at stocking to 73.4°F (23.0°C) by late season; ice cover present at stocking (mid-February).
- **Thermal tolerance / performance threshold:** Best growth and conversion occurred below 59°F (15°C); conversion rates collapsed as temperatures exceeded ~59–71.6°F (15–22°C) and dissolved oxygen declined concurrently.
- **Seasonal niche partitioning:** Southern Illinois waters are cool enough to support trout culture roughly mid-October to mid-May (>6 months), while temperatures above ~60°F (15.5°C) favor [[Channel Catfish]] production — supporting a proposed seasonal "cage exchange" model between the two species.
- **Comparative context:** Contrasts with [[Speckled Trout]] (Salvelinus fontinalis) cage culture in Quebec (Sequin 1970), conducted June–September in continuously cold northern lake water — a different ecological niche/season than the southern Illinois trial.
- **Feeding ecology:** Fed formulated diets (Purina Catfish Chow initially, then Purina Trout Chow) at rations ranging from 1.6% to 4.75% of body weight/day, adjusted for temperature-driven appetite fluctuations; feeding activity dropped sharply during cold spells despite continued ration delivery, leading to significant feed waste.

## Key Findings

- **Mortality:** Of 824 fish stocked, 28% (231 fish) were dead or unaccounted for by March 9 (early "handling" mortality). Total mortality over the full 87-day study reached 34.5% (284 fish).
- **Follow-up improvement:** A subsequent trial starting November 10, 1970 reduced early handling mortality to just 2.5%, with a food conversion ratio (FCR) of 1.30 over the first 30 days — a major improvement over the initial trial.
- **Food conversion by period:**
  - Pre–April 13 (cooler water, ≤57°F/14°C): FCR = 2.15 (west), 2.53 (east), mean 2.32.
  - April 13–May 15 (warmer water, lower O₂): FCR = 8.80 (west), 6.49 (east), mean 7.47 — a dramatic decline in efficiency.
  - Whole study: mean FCR ≈ 4.00.
  - Comparison: Missouri raceway trials (Camenisch 1970) achieved FCRs of 1.35–1.42, far superior to this cage study.
- **Growth distribution:** 67% of total weight gain occurred during the early, cooler period (water ≤57°F/14°C), reinforcing that cool-water conditions favored efficient production.
- **Economic outcome:**
  - Overall: net gain of 102.79 lb of trout flesh on ~411.3 lb of feed, at ~40 cents feed cost per pound of trout gain (using $0.10/lb feed) — deemed an economic failure relative to Missouri benchmarks (11.65–15.9 cents/lb).
  - Early period only (March 2–April 13): 69.29 lb gain on 161.1 lb feed, yielding a much more favorable cost of ~23.24 cents/lb of gain.
- **Proposed management threshold:** ~60°F (15.5°C) identified as the approximate temperature at which catfish production becomes most efficient and trout production efficiency ends, marking the ideal point to "exchange" species in the cage system.
- **Literature cross-check on temperature tolerances:**
  - [[Borel and Scheffer 1961]]: optimal trout production water temperature range 52–62°F (11–17°C).
  - Multiple authors (Tiemeier & Deyoe 1967; Lewis 1969; Lopinot & Fisher 1969; Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife 1970) cite 55–65°F (13–18°C) as the lower practical feeding limit for [[Channel Catfish]].
- **Practical/applied conclusion:** Wintertime trout cage culture could feasibly supplement, rather than replace, existing catfish cage operations at minimal additional infrastructure cost, with potential applications for home consumption or put-and-take recreational fishing in small ponds during late winter/early spring.
