# Draft Biological Opinion: Glen Canyon Dam Modified Low Fluctuating Flow Alternative

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ColoradoRiver #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary

This document is a **draft Biological Opinion summary** issued under Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act, addressing the effects of continued operation of **[[Glen Canyon Dam]]** on endangered aquatic and riparian species in the **[[Grand Canyon]]** ecosystem. The consultation evaluates the **Modified Low Fluctuating Flow Alternative (MLFF)**, the preferred alternative identified in the Draft Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for dam operations.

The Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) concluded that the proposed action is **likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) and **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), two endangered fish species endemic to the Colorado River system. The opinion is **not likely to jeopardize** the **[[Bald Eagle]]**, **[[Peregrine Falcon]]**, or **[[Kanab Ambersnail]]** (*Oxyloma haydeni kanabensis*), a terrestrial snail species affected by flow-related habitat disturbance.

A **Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA)** comprising seven elements was proposed to remove jeopardy, focused on restoring more natural hydrologic patterns, protecting critical tributary habitat, and implementing adaptive management informed by ongoing research.

## Background & Description

While this document is primarily a regulatory/management opinion rather than a morphological study, it identifies the following taxa by scientific nomenclature, providing baseline taxonomic anchoring:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Cyprinid fish endemic to the Colorado River Basin, notable for a pronounced dorsal hump (not described morphologically in text, but taxonomically confirmed).
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Catostomid fish, named for its distinctive dorsal keel ("razorback"); large-bodied endangered sucker of the Colorado River.
- **[[Kanab Ambersnail]]** (*Oxyloma haydeni kanabensis*) — A subspecies of terrestrial/semi-aquatic snail with a single known population in the Grand Canyon at the time of writing.
- **[[Bald Eagle]]** (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*) and **[[Peregrine Falcon]]** (*Falco peregrinus anatum*) — Avian species included for conservation recommendations due to their reliance on aquatic-linked food resources (e.g., fish prey) and riparian habitat.

No detailed anatomical, meristic, or morphometric data are provided in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document frames the Colorado River in Grand Canyon as an **endangered-species-dependent riverine ecosystem** substantially altered by dam operations:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** and **[[Razorback Sucker]]** occupy mainstem and tributary habitats affected by flow regime, water temperature, and sediment dynamics controlled by **[[Glen Canyon Dam]]** releases.
- The **[[Little Colorado River]] (LCR)** is identified as critical habitat requiring a dedicated management plan, indicating its importance as a spawning and rearing area, likely due to warmer water temperatures relative to the cold hypolimnetic releases from **[[Lake Powell]]**.
- A **selective withdrawal strategy** for Lake Powell was proposed specifically to modify water temperature released downstream — implying that cold-water discharge from the dam is a limiting factor for native fish reproduction and survival.
- The **Kanab Ambersnail** occupies flood-prone riparian habitat near the river, making it vulnerable to incidental take during high-flow/flood events tied to dam operation.
- **Bald Eagles** and **Peregrine Falcons** utilize aquatic resources (fish prey base) and riparian habitat features shaped by dam-controlled hydrology, linking raptor conservation indirectly to fish population health and river operations.
- The opinion explicitly recognizes an **ecosystem-level cascading risk**: continued loss of functional native species increases the difficulty of ecosystem recovery, reflecting a systems-based rather than single-species conservation approach.

## Key Findings

1. **Jeopardy Determination**: The proposed MLFF operation of Glen Canyon Dam is likely to jeopardize the **[[Humpback Chub]]** and **[[Razorback Sucker]]**, but not the **[[Bald Eagle]]**, **[[Peregrine Falcon]]**, or **[[Kanab Ambersnail]]**.

2. **Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (7 Elements)**:
   - **(1a)** Pattern water releases to mimic a natural hydrograph in low-release years (8.23 MAF): high spring flows (≥20,000 ± 1,000 cfs), steady July flows (12,500 ± 1,000 cfs) or reduced to ~9,000 ± 1,000 cfs, with steady low flows August–October.
   - **(1b)** Operate per standard MLFF during medium and high water years.
   - **(2)** Protect the **[[Little Colorado River]]** via a dedicated management plan.
   - **(3)** Conduct research on endangered and native fishes to determine cause-and-effect relationships with dam operations.
   - **(4)** Sponsor a workshop and recovery plan specifically for **[[Razorback Sucker]]** in Grand Canyon.
   - **(5)** Establish a **second spawning aggregation** of **[[Humpback Chub]]** to reduce extinction risk from reliance on a single population.
   - **(6)** Implement a **selective withdrawal strategy** at Glen Canyon Dam to modify release water temperature, with a feasibility study program.
   - **(7)** Develop and implement an **adaptive management program** with Service oversight.

3. **Incidental Take (Kanab Ambersnail)**: Some incidental take is anticipated during flood events based on the only known population in Grand Canyon. Reasonable and prudent measures require survey of population/habitat before and after flood events (>2 cfs threshold) to establish take-triggering flow levels.

4. **Conservation Recommendations** (non-mandatory):
   - Monitor **[[Peregrine Falcon]]** breeding sites in Glen Canyon and Grand Canyon.
   - Monitor peregrine falcon habitat utilization tied to aquatic resources.
   - Continue monitoring **[[Bald Eagle]]** habitat use and foraging patterns relative to dam operations.
   - Determine habitat requirements of **[[Kanab Ambersnail]]** and survey for additional populations.

5. **Management Philosophy**: The Service explicitly states that actions benefiting one species (e.g., humpback chub) are expected to support the broader native fish and ecosystem community, reflecting an early instance of **ecosystem-based endangered species management** in the Colorado River Basin.

6. **Regulatory Context**: This opinion was developed to meet a Draft EIS printing deadline (May 1993) and is explicitly labeled as a **draft**, with elements subject to change pending further agency review and NEPA compliance processes.
