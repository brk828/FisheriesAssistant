# Report upon the Collections of Fishes Made in Portions of Nevada, Utah, California, Colorado, New Mexico, and Arizona during 1871–1874

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This historical survey report, prepared by [[Paul C. Marsh]] with [[Edward D. Cope]] describing the new taxa and [[Henry C. Yarrow]] compiling comparisons and synonymy, reports on freshwater fish collected in the western interior of the United States during 1871–1874. The material supports the establishment of three new genera and nine new species, plus several varieties. Its most notable results are a distinctive cyprinid group, the [[Plagoptera]] (Plagopterus), restricted to the Colorado and San Luis basins; the first western record of [[Ceratichthys biguttatus]] at Harmony, Utah; and documentation of economically important food fishes of Utah, notably [[Utah Lake Trout]] and [[Mountain Herring]]. The report is largely taxonomic, with descriptions, synoptic keys, locality and collector tables and notes on fisheries.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Collections from Nevada, Utah, New Mexico, Arizona, California and Colorado, made by several naturalists of the [[Wheeler Survey]] expedition. The 1871 collection by F. Bischoff was largely destroyed before reaching Washington; the 1872, 1873 and 1874 collections arrived in good condition, apart from one can of specimens lost to alcohol leakage.
- **Taxonomic authorship:** Cope described the new genera and nearly all new species; Yarrow prepared the synonymy, bibliography, comparative notes and specimen lists.
- **Format:** Each species entry gives a diagnosis, meristic counts (fin rays, scales, teeth), morphometric ratios and measurements, color, and locality and collector data. Several genera are keyed.
- **Scale:** The report covers roughly 50 cyprinid, catostomid, salmonid, cottid, percomorph and other taxa, with the Cyprinidae forming the bulk of the new material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Colorado basin:** The [[Plagoptera]] group (Plagopterus, Lepidomeda, Meda) has two strong, osseous dorsal and ventral spines, and lives only in the Colorado and San Luis basins. Its ventral-ray ossification is otherwise known only in extinct [[Ganoid Fishes|Ganoid]] forms of the Cretaceous, and in the living Amphiacanthus. Its carnivorous dentition and intestines indicate predatory habits.
- **Great Basin (Utah):** Utah Lake, Provo River, Panquitch Lake and Beaver River support large populations of [[Utah Lake Trout]] (Salmo virginalis), [[Mountain Herring]] (Coregonus williamsoni), [[Mullet]] (Siboma atrarius), [[Utah Sucker]] and minnows. Trout feed heavily on small cyprinids such as Tigoma tania (Clinostomus tania), and the Provo River minnows are a favoured trout food.
- **Parasites:** Trout from the Yellowstone region were found to be infested with large tapeworms (later named Dibothriocephalus) that made the flesh unpalatable in summer; Utah trout were not reported to carry parasites.
- **Rio Grande and Southwest:** Sturgeon (Scaphirhynchus platorynchus) was recorded from the Rio Grande near Albuquerque, extending its known western range. Gila species and Ptychocheilus-related catostomids are common in Arizona streams, and the Gila chub form is described as abundant in shallow pools.
- **Habitat notes:** Gunnison (Utah) yielded Lepidomeda specimens from an alkaline, stagnant ditch about one mile from the Sevier River; Sevier Lake was too alkaline to contain fishes.
- **Fisheries:** Trout netting and hook fishing in Utah Lake were already reducing catches, with a reported decline of about one-third, and the authors urged protective legislation against small-mesh nets.

## Key Findings

- **New genera (3):** Plagopterus and its allies (Plagoptera group), Lepidomeda, and Apocope/Syllaemus-related forms; several species are described from the Colorado basin only.
- **New species (9 or more):** Including Plagopterus argentissimus, Lepidomeda vittata, Lepidomeda jarrovii, Rhinichthys henshawi, Apocope vanhyningi (not "vanhyningi" in original; Apocope vanhyningi as recorded), Apocope ventricosa, Catostomus discobolus, Catostomus fecundus (with Yarrow) and Gila phlegethontis.
- **Range extensions:** Ceratichthys biguttatus was found at Harmony, Utah (southern Utah), extending its known western range beyond the Smoky Hill River; Coregonus williamsoni (Mountain Herring) was found in the Provo River, the first and only record for the Great Salt Lake valley.
- **Notable food fish:** [[Utah Lake Trout]] (Salmo virginalis), with an average length of about 14 inches and weight of about 1.5 lb, sells for 20–30 cents per pound retail; Siboma atrarius (Mullet) sells at about 3 cents per pound.
- **Taxonomic notes:** Gila and Clinostomus were considered by Cope to form a single natural genus (Gila), and Minomus/Acomus were reassigned to Catostomus after a fontanelle was found in specimens of M. insignis.
- **Conclusion:** The authors state that the list of Western fishes is far from complete and expected to grow with further exploration.
