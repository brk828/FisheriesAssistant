# Review of Spikedace and Red Shiner Interactions Study

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #threatened-species #species-interactions

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the Arizona Game & Fish Department provides a detailed review of a draft final report examining interactions between the threatened [[Spikedace]] (*[[Meda fulgida]]*) and the introduced [[Red Shiner]] (*[[Cyprinella lutrensis]]*). The review, dated August 2, 1989, was conducted by Dean A. Hendrickson, Native Fish Biologist, and identifies major deficiencies in the manuscript requiring revision before finalization. The study included field observations, experimental stream work, habitat use analyses, and dietary comparisons. Five additional independent reviewers also provided comments through Dr. Minckley.

## Background & Description

The reviewed study investigated competitive interactions between native [[Spikedace]] and non-native [[Red Shiner]] in [[Arizona]] streams. The research was conducted by Dr. Paul C. Marsh at the Center for Environmental Studies, [[Arizona State University]]. The study included a first year's report (Douglas et al. 1988) and the current draft final report under review. Research methods included habitat availability and use measurements (depth, velocity, substrate), experimental stream studies with net placements, dietary analyses through food habits examination, and benthos and drift sampling. The study also collected data on [[Tiaroga]], though this genus received less emphasis in the draft report.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Spikedace]] is a threatened species native to [[Arizona]] streams facing competition from introduced [[Red Shiner]]. The study examined habitat partitioning through measurements of depth, velocity, and substrate preferences in both allopatric (single species) and sympatric (both species present) conditions. Habitat availability was assessed using 10m² sampling areas, though the reviewer notes the selection process and exact sampling locations within areas were inadequately described. The research included benthos and drift sampling to characterize food availability in the aquatic environment, though these data were not compared to actual diets in the draft manuscript. The experimental stream component excluded the breeding period of [[Meda fulgida]], a limitation acknowledged by the reviewer.

## Key Findings

The review identified seven major categories of deficiencies requiring correction:

**Integration and Documentation**: The first year's report (Douglas et al. 1988) must be integrated with the current draft to form one comprehensive final document. Much valuable data compiled during the study was not provided in the report, particularly histograms of habitat availability and use for all species including [[Tiaroga]].

**Writing Quality**: Much of the manuscript suffered from unclear and confusing writing or omission of needed details, making interpretation difficult.

**Field Methods**: Field methods were inadequately described, leading to difficulties in interpretation of statistical analyses. Critical details missing included: how 10m² areas were selected and where within each area depth, velocity, and substrate samples were located and taken; sample sizes and variances were not reported; the process of data transformation, standardization, or other preparation from field to analyses was not mentioned.

**Statistical Analyses**: The presentation of statistical analyses was wholly inadequate. Specific deficiencies included: exact probabilities should be stated in text rather than general significance statements; difficulty deciphering which statistical test was being discussed; lack of ANOVA output tables including sample sizes and exact probabilities; no discussion of test assumptions or data fit to those assumptions; unclear pooling of samples across streams or sites; no statistical description of "availability" of habitat parameters; frequent lack of null hypothesis statements despite references to "available" habitats or significant differences.

**Food Habits Analysis**: In the food habits section, the rationale for considering any index score > 0.6 as "significant" was not explained. Though statistical tests were apparently not performed, discussion referred to "significant" (i.e., > 0.6) values of these indices. Lack of confidence intervals or probabilities rendered Table 9 of little value. The presentation of three different indices was not explained. Critically, food availability determined from benthos and drift was never compared to actual diets, raising questions about why these samples were collected.

**Study Design Limitations**: Several concerns extended beyond analysis to fundamental study design: possible effects of net location in experimental stream; inadequate sample sizes; limitation of dietary analyses to a short time period; limitation of experimental stream study to a period exclusive of [[Meda fulgida]] breeding. While these deficiencies could not be corrected retrospectively, discussion of these issues should be included in the final report.

**Discussion and Context**: Results and factors affecting their interpretation were not adequately discussed. The results were not placed in context of other similar studies, including previous work on the same species. A much more thorough discussion relating Results to Conclusions was needed.

The reviewer requested a meeting on August 22, 1989 at 2:00 PM with study participants, agency representatives including David Propst, Terry Johnson, and a U.S. Fish and Wildlife representative to discuss resolution of reviewer issues and ability to provide a final version by August 30, 1989.
