## Executive Summary
This document set is not a primary research paper but a series of 1993 professional correspondence letters addressing a taxonomic dispute over the genus placement of the loach minnow, formerly classified as the monotypic genus *Tiaroga* (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and reclassified as *[[Rhinichthys cobitis]]*. The letters — authored by Dr. Richard L. Mayden (University of Alabama, Curator of Fishes), Dr. J.S. Nelson (University of Alberta, chair of the AFS/ASIH names committee), and Dr. David L. Propst (New Mexico Department of Game & Fish) — defend and clarify the scientific basis for synonymizing *Tiaroga* with *[[Rhinichthys]]*, in response to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's (Phoenix office) refusal to adopt the new nomenclature pending further community review. The correspondence documents the process of phylogenetic systematics resolving generic classification and highlights institutional tension between regulatory agencies and the taxonomic/systematics research community.

## Background & Description
- The loach minnow (*Rhinichthys cobitis*) is described as morphologically and biologically distinct ("unlike other species of *Rhinichthys* for some biological features"), which had historically justified its separate monotypic genus, *Tiaroga*.
- Despite these distinguishing biological/morphological features, phylogenetic (cladistic) analyses using heritable traits demonstrated that these differences do not reflect a separate evolutionary lineage distinct from *[[Rhinichthys]]*.
- Dr. J.S. Nelson notes colloquially that the species "certainly looks like a *Rhinichthys*" to him, suggesting general morphological consistency with the genus despite historical treatment as distinct.
- No detailed meristic, morphometric, or coloration data are provided in this correspondence; the source is nomenclatural/systematic in nature rather than descriptive.

## Key Findings
- **Taxonomic revision:** Two independent phylogenetic systematic studies — (1) by Woodman, and (2) by Coburn and Cavender — both concluded, using cladistic analysis, that *Tiaroga cobitis* is phylogenetically nested within *[[Rhinichthys]]*, rather than being a distinct sister genus.
- **Publication basis:** Both studies were peer-reviewed and published in 1992 in *Systematics, Historical Ecology, and North American Freshwater Fishes* (Stanford University Press), edited by Richard L. Mayden. Woodman's cladogram appears on p. 384 of this volume.
- **Rationale for reclassification:** Recognizing *Tiaroga* as a separate genus would misrepresent the evolutionary history of *Rhinichthys* by implying all *Rhinichthys* species are more closely related to one another than to *T. cobitis* — an inaccurate reflection of the nested phylogenetic relationship.
- **Community acceptance:** The name change was accepted in the American Fisheries Society/American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists (AFS/ASIH) common and scientific names list, reviewed by taxonomic authorities including Dick Robins and Reeve Bailey.
- **Alternative scenario for retaining *Tiaroga*:** Dr. Nelson notes that resurrecting *Tiaroga* would only be scientifically justified if new evidence refuted Woodman's cladogram, or if relationships were shown to be genuinely unresolved (a polychotomy), which would support retaining a monotypic genus provisionally.
- **Dispute resolution:** Dr. David L. Propst clarified that his informally expressed "reservations" about the taxonomic change (voiced at a February 1993 Desert Fishes Recovery Team meeting) were personal opinion, not data-backed evidence, and were mischaracterized as formal scientific evidence against the reclassification by the USFWS Phoenix office.
- **Institutional boundary:** Both Propst and W.L. Minckley agreed that taxonomic decisions fall outside the purview of the Desert Fishes Recovery Team, and such issues should be resolved through appropriate scientific/systematic forums (e.g., AFS/ASIH names committee) rather than regulatory bodies.
- **Outcome:** The consensus among the correspondents is that *[[Tiaroga cobitis]]* should remain synonymized under *[[Rhinichthys cobitis]]*, pending any future peer-reviewed data to the contrary.
