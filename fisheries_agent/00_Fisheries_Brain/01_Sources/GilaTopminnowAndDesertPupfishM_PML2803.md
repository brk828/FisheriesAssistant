# Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish Monitoring and Management Activities on BLM Lands in Arizona, October 2002 Through October 2003

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) technical report documents monitoring and management of two endangered desert fishes, [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]], on [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) lands from October 2002 through October 2003. The work was funded through a BLM Cooperative Agreement and aimed at monitoring natural and reestablished populations, evaluating new reestablishment localities, and setting a recovery strategy consistent with the [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]] and [[Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan]]. The report recommends stocking 15 to 20 sites per year beginning in 2004.

## Background & Description

The Gila topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*) are managed as a joint project by AGFD, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), and other agencies. In October 2002, BLM and AGFD entered a Cooperative Agreement under which BLM funded monitoring and management through October 2003. Background on these species and AGFD management history is summarized in [[Weedman and Young (1997)]] and [[Voeltz and Bettaso (2003)]].

Species abbreviations used in the report (Table 1) include POOC (Gila topminnow), CYMA (desert pupfish), AGCH ([[Longfin Dace]]), GIIN ([[Gila Chub]]), CYLU ([[Red Shiner]]), GAAF ([[Mosquitofish]]), LECY ([[Green Sunfish]]), PORE ([[Guppy]]), CYCA ([[Common Carp]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.** Sites were sampled between April and November using dipnets, seines (1/8-in mesh), minnow traps, or backpack electrofishers. Dissolved oxygen, pH, temperature, and conductivity were recorded at all sites, along with notes on habitat, riparian condition, and threats. Voucher specimens were deposited in the [[ASU Vertebrate Museum]], and photographs were archived by AGFD.

**Natural populations.** Of 14 natural Gila topminnow populations in Arizona, one is on BLM land: [[Cienega Creek]], which contains roughly 13 km of topminnow habitat and is the largest remaining natural topminnow habitat in Arizona. Topminnows there coexist with longfin dace and Gila chub, with no nonnative fishes present. No natural desert pupfish populations remain in Arizona except [[Quitobaquito Spring]] (National Park Service land, *C. eremus*); the two wild sites with *C. macularius* on BLM lands are noted in the report.

**Reestablished populations.** Twenty-three BLM sites in Arizona have been stocked with Gila topminnow, and nine with desert pupfish. Six sites (one natural, five reestablished) supported topminnows on BLM land during the study period; [[Yerba Mansa Spring]] lies outside historic range. Two extant reestablished pupfish localities were monitored.

**Threats.** Recurring threats include flooding (e.g., [[Tule Creek]] in 1978 and 1993, Humbug Creek in 1993), desiccation and drought, cattle impacts, cattail overgrowth, nonnative fish introductions, bullfrogs, and water-table depletion from agricultural wells near Bowie, which affected artesian ponds such as [[Howard Well]].

## Key Findings

- **Category 1 (established) sites on BLM land** include [[Empire Gulch]] (stocked 2001 with 689 topminnows from Cienega Creek, with later augmentations of 70 in May 2003 and 475 in October 2003), [[Lousy Canyon]] (topminnows and pupfish reproducing), and [[Tule Creek]] (topminnow persisting since 1981 restocking).
- **[[Cold Springs]]** (Category 2) supported topminnows and pupfish; red shiners were removed by seining and electroshocking in autumn 1998, and only topminnows remained in 2003.
- **Failed sites recommended for restocking** total about 20 entries in the appendices, including [[Aravaipa Creek]] (all three introductions failed), [[Big Spring]], [[Cow Creek]], [[Green Tanks]], [[Howard Well]], [[Humbug Creek]], [[Martin Well]], and [[Watson Wash]]. Most have been flagged for reevaluation, cleanup, or additional stocking.
- **Failed populations not yet meeting extirpation criteria** include [[Mesquite Spring]] (pupfish die-off, likely low oxygen, 1991) and [[Pupfish Spring]] (flood-eliminated, no fish found in 1997).
- **Watson Wash** has held only guppies and mosquitofish since 1999; BLM is considering capping the well or drying the habitat, which would require mitigation sites in the Safford District.
- **Candidate localities** evaluated in Table 2 include Buckhorn Spring, Larry Creek, Oak Grove Canyon, Parsons Grove, Tule Creek, Unnamed Pond near Bitter Creek (pH 3.9, no aquatic insects or vegetation in 1992), and Virgus Canyon.
- **Aravaipa Creek watershed:** AGFD assisted BLM with an Environmental Assessment and Biological Assessment/Evaluation for reestablishment on the south rim, with completion and USFWS Section 7 consultation anticipated by spring 2004.

**Recommendations:**
- Protect natural populations and habitats, reestablish populations within historic range, implement a genetic exchange protocol among reestablished populations, and monitor biotic and genetic integrity.
- Finalize the draft revised Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan.
- Complete NEPA and ESA compliance site by site, watershed by watershed, or allotment by allotment before restocking.
- Stock 15 to 20 sites per year beginning in 2004, more than the Recovery Plans require, to offset occasional population losses.
- Downlisting could be pursued about 10 years after the necessary Level-2 and Level-3 populations are established, possibly by 2020.
