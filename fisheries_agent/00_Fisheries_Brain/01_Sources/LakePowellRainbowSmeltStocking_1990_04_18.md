# Lake Powell Rainbow Smelt Stocking

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 advocacy letter from the Arizona State Council of Trout Unlimited to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, opposing a Utah Division of Wildlife Resources proposal to stock [[Rainbow Smelt]] in [[Lake Powell]]. The stated management goal was to enhance a forage base for the [[Striped Bass]] fishery, itself a prior introduced predatory species. The letter is not a primary field or laboratory study but a regulatory/policy correspondence citing existing scientific literature ("nearly every study on the predatory smelt") to argue against introduction, on the grounds of risk to endangered native fishes and to the economically important [[Lee's Ferry]] trout fishery. The letter invokes Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] and requests formal federal consultation and environmental assessment prior to stocking.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source. The document is a policy letter and contains no direct species descriptions, measurements, or diagnostic characters for [[Rainbow Smelt]], [[Striped Bass]], or native Colorado River basin fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target introduction species**: [[Rainbow Smelt]] — proposed as a forage/prey species intended to bolster the [[Striped Bass]] fishery in [[Lake Powell]].
- **Existing introduced predator**: [[Striped Bass]] — described as a "predacious" species already established in the reservoir via a prior federally funded introduction; the smelt stocking is characterized as potentially "compounding" this original ecological alteration.
- **Habitat connectivity risk**: The letter states it is "apparently conceded" that smelt would disperse ("infest") the [[Colorado River Basin]] both upstream and downstream of [[Lake Powell]], indicating an open hydrological system with limited containment potential for the introduced species.
- **Affected ecosystems**:
  - **Endangered native species** in the Colorado River basin (unspecified taxa in text, but consistent with historically imperiled Colorado River native fishes) — cited as at risk from predation/competition by an expanding smelt population.
  - **[[Lee's Ferry]] Trout Fishery** — a tailwater fishery below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] described as "world-class," flagged as vulnerable to downstream smelt colonization and associated trophic disruption.
- **Regulatory/ecological framing**: The letter frames [[Lake Powell]] as a major federal reservoir subject to federal funding obligations, situating the ecological risk within the jurisdiction of the [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 consultation requirements.

## Key Findings
- A 1990 proposal by the State of Utah sought to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Powell]] specifically to enhance forage for the introduced [[Striped Bass]] fishery.
- The author (Arizona State Council, Trout Unlimited) asserts that the broader scientific literature on rainbow smelt introductions generally "counsels against" such stocking programs.
- It was reportedly conceded (by proponents or reviewers) that smelt would not remain confined to Lake Powell but would spread through the [[Colorado River Basin]] both above and below the reservoir.
- Two primary ecological risks are identified: (1) impacts on endangered native fish species in the basin, and (2) impacts on the downstream [[Lee's Ferry]] trout fishery.
- The letter argues the stocking proposal triggers federal regulatory obligations: Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and at minimum a formal environmental assessment, due to prior and ongoing federal funding (Federal Aid to Fisheries) associated with [[Lake Powell]] and the original [[Striped Bass]] introduction.
- No independent field data, population estimates, or experimental results on smelt or striped bass in Lake Powell are presented; the document functions as a stakeholder policy intervention rather than a primary research record.
- Distribution list indicates this was a multi-agency advocacy effort involving the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, Trout Unlimited national leadership, and both Utah and Arizona state wildlife agencies (Timothy H. Provan, [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]; Duane Shroufe, [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]]).
