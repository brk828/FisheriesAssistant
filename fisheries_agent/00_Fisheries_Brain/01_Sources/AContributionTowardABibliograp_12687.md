## Executive Summary

This 1959 document, compiled by Victor W. Lambou of the [[Louisiana Wild Life And Fisheries Commission]] under the Dingell-Johnson Project F-1-R, is **not a biological or ecological study** but an annotated bibliography cataloguing early literature on the use of **machine methods** (punch-card tabulating equipment, IBM accounting machines, and nascent electronic digital computers) for processing fishery and biological research data. The author explicitly notes that few fishery workers of the era had published on applying such methods directly to fishery problems, so most entries are drawn from adjacent fields—statistics, psychometrics, agronomy, wildlife management, and general computing science—that could plausibly be adapted to fisheries data workflows. The bibliography reflects the transitional period between manual data tabulation and the adoption of electronic data processing (EDP) in fisheries science, anticipating the increased importance of computational methods in the field.

## Key Findings

- **Purpose**: The bibliography aims to support fishery workers seeking to apply machine methods (punch-card systems, IBM tabulating equipment, electronic computers) to processing fishery data, acknowledging that direct fishery applications in the literature were sparse at the time.
- **Scope of sources**: Entries span statistical computation techniques (e.g., punched-card methods for computing sums of squares, correlation coefficients, regression analysis), general computing science texts (digital computers, numerical analysis), and applied case studies in wildlife/fishery management.
- **Direct fishery applications identified**:
  - Lambou (1959) — *Simplified Creel Census Program for Punch Card Tabulation* and *Use of Machine Methods in Processing Fishery Data* (presented at the 13th Annual Conference, [[Southeastern Association Of Game And Fish Commissioners]]).
  - Leeper, Stern, and Lambou (1958) — punch card methods for tabulating and analyzing fishery research data.
  - Mansueti (1959) — advocacy for critical fishery research planning integrated with electronic data processing.
  - Hammer, Hensel, and Tiller (1948) — Maryland commercial fishing statistics.
  - U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (1959) — Maryland commercial fishery survey machine tabulating procedure.
  - Van Hangenard and Dale (1959) — new instrumentation tools for fisheries biologists.
  - El-Zarka (1959) — population fluctuation study of [[Yellow Perch]] used as a data-processing case example.
- **Adjacent wildlife management applications**: Several entries address analogous data-handling problems in non-fishery wildlife contexts (e.g., hunter report analysis, waterfowl area ecology, game bird management via punch cards), illustrating cross-disciplinary methodological transfer relevant to fisheries science.
- **Technological context**: The bibliography documents the era's dominant tools—IBM punched-card tabulating/accounting machines (e.g., IBM 405, 604, 650, 701, 704 systems)—as the primary "machine methods" preceding widespread adoption of true electronic digital computers in fisheries research.
- **Historical significance**: This document serves as an early marker of the shift toward computational and statistical data processing in [[Fisheries Science]], foreshadowing the growth of quantitative and automated methods in aquatic ecology and fishery management research through the latter half of the 20th century.
