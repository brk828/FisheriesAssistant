# Six Distinctive Cyprinid Fish Species Referred to Dionda Inhabiting Segments of the Tampico Embayment Drainage of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This revision by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Robert Rush Miller]] (San Diego Society of Natural History Transactions, 1977) treats six sharply differentiated minnows provisionally referred to the genus [[Dionda]] in the [[Rio Panuco]] stream complex of eastern Mexico. Three species had been named recently; [[Dionda catostomops]] and [[Dionda dichroma]] are described as new. The six species form three allopatric, physiographically separated pairs, each pair showing extensive sympatry within itself but no evidence of interspecific hybridization. The authors stress that the generic placement is provisional, because traditional generic characters (maxillary barbel, intestinal coiling) break down within the group.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Six species of Cyprinidae (Dionda mandibularis, D. rasconis, D. catostomops n. sp., D. dichroma n. sp., D. ipni, D. erimyzonops) plus two additional Dionda species from northern Mexico (D. episcopa, D. diaboli) used for comparison.
- **Taxonomic history:** Species were frequently confounded with [[Hybognathus]] or with one another. [[Dionda ipni]] was originally described as [[Notropis ipni]] by [[Alvarez and Navarro]] (1953); [[Dionda mandibularis]] was described by [[Contreras-Balderas and Verduzco-Martinez]] (1977) and was earlier misassigned to D. rasconis by [[Richard T. Gregg]] (1956 thesis).
- **Diagnostic contrasts:** Dionda differs from Hybognathus in jaw structure, mouth shape, pharyngeal teeth, dorsal-fin position, and dark body pigmentation with a dark lateral stripe ending in a basicaudal spot. Nuptial males show colored fins (jet-black, yellow, or blue) that Hybognathus lacks.
- **Key characters separating the groups:**
  - *Lowland pair:* D. ipni (barbel lacking, high anal-ray counts, 33-34 lateral-line scales, strong nuptial tubercles and fin blotches) and D. erimyzonops (dwarfed, 21-39 mm SL nuptial adults, snout strongly downturned, dorsal head striping).
  - *Lower intermontane pair:* D. rasconis (pale coloration, small maxillary barbel, 33-39 lateral-line scales) and D. catostomops (sucker-like body form, rounded fins, barbel well developed, 37-45 lateral-line scales in adults, vertebral total 37-39).
  - *Upper intermontane pair:* D. dichroma (bicolored body, variable, blue-edged fins, moderate-to-strong dorsal stripe) and D. mandibularis (elongate, trenchant mandible, barbel virtually absent, bold melanic coloration).
- **Fin-ray and scale counts:** Dorsal rays are almost uniformly 8. Caudal rays follow 10 + 9 = 19 with rare 18s. Pectoral rays average about 13 in five species, rising to a mean of 15.08 in D. ipni. Pelvic rays are typically 8, with reductions to 0-7 in several species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lowland systems (ipni, erimyzonops):** Coastal-plain streams of the Rio Tamesi and Rio Panuco. D. ipni extends southward into five minor drainages (Tuxpan, Cazones, Tecolutla, Nautla, Misantla), where it is the only cyprinid, and reaches about 1,700 m in the Rio Metztitlan. These coastal streams lie south of the Tampico Embayment, at a volcanic lava-flow barrier near Punta del Morro, which marks the southern limit of the family on the tropical Atlantic coastal plain.
- **Lower intermontane area (rasconis, catostomops):** Rio Gallinas (= Rio Ojo Frio) and Rio Tamasopo, San Luis Potosi. Rasconis favors deeper, slow water with soft sandy-silt bottoms; catostomops favors swift, stony riffles and pool heads.
- **Upper intermontane area (dichroma, mandibularis):** Rio Verde basin, with spring-fed marshes, Puerta del Rio, Rio Santa Catarina, and La Media Luna. Water temperatures in springs ranged from about 26 to 29 °C. Mandibularis is restricted to a roughly 60 km range near Rioverde, avoiding spring heads preferred by dichroma.
- **Fauna:** The Rio Panuco fish fauna blends Nearctic, Neotropical, Middle American, and Mexican Plateau elements. The ten Cyprinidae of the system include six Dionda and four others: Notropis lutrensis forlonensis, N. tropicus, Algansea tincella, and Aztecula sallaei. Endemism is high across the system.
- **Sympatry records (ratios reported by the authors):**
  - ipni : erimyzonops: 14:33 (Rio Guayalejo), 7:3 (Rio Forlon), 2:1 (Rio Axtla), 5:14 (Rio Valles).
  - rasconis : catostomops: 67:1 (Rio Gallinas), 15:6 and 13:58 (Rio Tamasopo).
  - dichroma : mandibularis: 7:7 and 72:33 (Puerta del Rio), 16:6 (spring marsh 10 km S of Rioverde).
- **Spawning:** Mandibularis is an early-spring spawner. Catostomops appears to spawn in late winter (water 27 °C). Ipni males congregate on rocky riffles in mid-December, performing pectoral-fin signaling. Dichroma has a protracted breeding season, from late winter to fall.
- **Threats and gaps:** Recently introduced exotics (Gambusia panuco and Tilapia aurea) reached the outflow ditches from La Media Luna by 1970s. The main course of Rio Gallinas and the lower Rio Santa Maria remained unexplored.

## Key Findings

1. **Three allopatric pairs.** Between-pair ranges are physiographically separated; within each pair, sympatry is common, and no interspecific hybridization was found. The authors attribute this partly to the absence of glacial disruption, citing [[Hubbs 1955]].
2. **Generic placement is provisional.** Intestinal coiling (all six species have rather extensively coiled intestines) and maxillary barbel presence (absent in ipni and erimyzonops; small in rasconis and dichroma; larger in catostomops) vary within the group, undermining earlier criteria. The authors note the breakdown is most evident toward the southern limit of the family.
3. **Nomenclatural correction.** Ipni is a valid name for the coastal-plain form previously included as a subspecies of D. rasconis. The authors concluded that [[Notropis ipni]] is specifically inseparable from that coastal-plain form.
4. **Distinct species diagnoses.** Each species is separated by mouth position, lower-lip grooves, pharyngeal arch shape, scale and vertebral counts, nuptial tubercle patterns, and color (e.g., blue-edged fins in dichroma and catostomops; jet-black blotches in ipni).
5. **Morphometric and meristic overlap.** Lateral-line scale means run 32.61 (erimyzonops), 33.53 (ipni), 34.48 (rasconis), 37.97 (catostomops), 39.74 (mandibularis), and 40.92 (dichroma). Dichroma shows the most variation among the six, especially in orbit size and anal-ray counts.
6. **Dichroma variation.** Mean orbit-length permillage values range from 59 (Rio Santa Catarina) to 79.5 (spring marsh south of Rioverde), and anal rays shift toward 9 in the marsh south of Rioverde.
7. **Sexual dimorphism.** Ipni shows the most extreme sexual color difference of any American cyprinid studied; catostomops females are larger than males, whereas in ipni males attain larger sizes and build nests.
8. **Conservation-relevant endemism.** Mandibularis is confined to roughly 60 km of range; catostomops is known from a few localities in the Rio Tamasopo and Rio Ojo Frio systems; both are vulnerable to habitat change.
