# New Species and New Records of Costa Rican Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #neotropical-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic report surveys the freshwater fishes of [[Costa Rica]], an area poorly known to ichthyologists. It describes five undescribed species, reports 12 species new to the country's fauna, and proposes a synonymy between two cichlid names. The author also presents a tentative checklist of Costa Rican freshwater fishes and discusses the region's zoogeography. The collections were made mainly between 1961 and 1966, most of them near the [[Interamerican Highway]].

## Background & Description

**Scope and collections.** Collections were made mainly by the author during July 1961 to December 1962, January 1964 and the first half of 1966. Intensive work was concentrated in the [[Rio Puerto Viejo]] during 1962, where an ecological study of the fishes was carried out. Collections were also made in southern Costa Rica in January 1964. Most material was deposited in the [[Los Angeles County Museum Of Natural History]] (LACM), with additional material in the University of Costa Rica, University of Miami, and University of Michigan museums.

**Methods.** Counts and measurements follow standard ichthyological practice. Body measurements are in mm standard length (SL), and body proportions are given as a percentage of SL. Fin-ray counts use Roman numerals for anterior unbranched rays. Two last dorsal and anal rays with closely approximated bases are counted as one ray.

**Species described.** The five new species are:

- [[Pseudocheirodon Terrabae]], a characid with a complete lateral line, known only from the [[Rio Grande de Terraba]] basin.
- [[Hyphessobrycon Savagei]], a characid from Rio Grande de Terraba tributaries.
- [[Piabucina Boruca]], a lebiasinid lacking a dark lateral band.
- [[Brachyrhaphis Holdridgei]], a surface-dwelling poeciliid from the Atlantic slope.
- [[Poeciliopsis Paucimaculata]], a poeciliid with two or three dark side blotches.

**Other taxa.** The author records 12 species reported for the first time from Costa Rica (the introduction states 14 new records, but two are omitted as identifications are uncertain). Examples include [[Pygidium Striatum]], the first record of its genus in Costa Rica, and *Pseudophallus starksi* (Syngnathidae), first recorded from the country. The report also notes the first Costa Rican record of *Hypostomus plecostomus* from a confirmed specimen series.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat preferences.** Several species show clear habitat associations. *Gephyrocharax intermedius* prefers small streams and backwaters of larger rivers. *Rivulus hildebrandi* was collected in cattle tracks and small pools along sandy shores without vegetation, whereas the similar *Rivulus isthmensis* is associated with dense vegetation. *Brachyrhaphis holdridgei* is a surface-frequenting fish with an upturned snout and a dorsal fin set far back on the body.

**Elevation and pigmentation.** In *Pygidium striatum*, the pattern of the lateral band correlates with altitude in Panama: high-altitude populations have prominent median bands, while lowland adults lack them. Such patterns are not seen in Colombian material.

**Zoogeographic barriers.** The author describes the Cordilleras of Costa Rica as a double barrier, separating the South American fauna on the Pacific slope from the Central American fauna on the Atlantic slope. Many species moving north along the Pacific slope are funneled into the dead-end [[Rio Grande de Terraba]] drainage. The coastal route is blocked by the steep, narrow Pacific lowlands north of the Terraba.

**Dispersal routes.** Central American cichlids and poeciliids appear to be moving south along the Atlantic lowlands, and their passage is disrupted by the [[Cordillera de Talamanca]]. A highland passage through the [[Central Plateau]] is indicated by intermediate populations of *Cichlasoma alfari*. Widespread genera such as *Astyanax*, *Roeboides*, *Brycon* and *Poecilia* probably have considerable salinity tolerance, allowing them to cross barriers through the sea or estuaries.

**Endemism.** About 20 indigenous species, mainly in the families Pimelodidae, Characidae, Poeciliidae and Cichlidae, are recorded only from Costa Rica.

## Key Findings

- **Five new species** were described: *Pseudocheirodon terrabae*, *Hyphessobrycon savagei*, *Piabucina boruca*, *Brachyrhaphis holdridgei* and *Poeciliopsis paucimaculata*.
- **Pseudocheirodon terrabae** differs from its congener *P. affinis* in having a complete lateral line (rarely incomplete, vs. the reverse in *P. affinis*). It also has 21-22 branched anal rays (usually 22, vs. 19-20 in *P. affinis*) and 3-6 maxillary teeth (usually 5, vs. 3-4). The holotype is LACM 9238-1 (49.5 mm SL), and the species is restricted to the Rio Grande de Terraba basin.
- **Hyphessobrycon savagei** has 20-23 branched anal rays (vs. 23-26 in *H. panamensis*), 16-18 total gill rakers (vs. 19-21), and 31-33 longitudinal scale rows. Its holotype is 36.7 mm SL. It was collected only in Rio Grande de Terraba tributaries, between 560 m and near sea level.
- **Piabucina boruca** lacks a dark lateral band. Large specimens have 28-29 total gill rakers, compared with 21-25 in *P. panamensis*. It is restricted to the Rio Grande de Terraba and Rio Coto drainages.
- **Brachyrhaphis holdridgei** was collected in the Rio Madre de Dios, Rio Sarapiqui and Rio Arenal drainages on the Atlantic slope, between about 50 and 600 m. The species is named for Leslie R. Holdridge, and its gonopodial structure is detailed in the text.
- **Poeciliopsis paucimaculata** has two or three dark side blotches, compared with eight thin bars or no markings in related species, and 19-20 gill rakers (vs. 27-34). It has been collected only in tributaries of the Rio General, where it occurs alongside *P. retropinna*.
- **Synonymy:** The cichlid *Cichlasoma lethrinus* Regan is placed in the synonymy of *C. alfari* Meek. Populations of *C. alfari* vary between Pacific and Atlantic slopes, and the highland Valle Central material is intermediate. The author considers the species polytypic and does not recognize the two slope populations as separate species.
- **Records:** *Compsura gorgonae* is recorded from the Pacific slope to the Rio Coto drainage. *Gymnotus cylindricus* is recorded from both slopes, and the author concludes that Atlantic material is not *G. carapo*. *Curimata magdalenae* from Costa Rica is noted as lacking differences from Panamanian and Colombian populations, apart from a slight difference in lateral-line scale counts.
- **Biogeography:** Costa Rican fish fauna is shaped by the Cordilleras, the Rio Grande de Terraba dead end, and highland exchange through the Central Plateau. About 20 species are endemic.
- **Checklist:** A tentative checklist of Costa Rican freshwater fishes is provided, grouped by family and marked by slope (A for Atlantic, P for Pacific). Marine species that enter rivers are included with caveats, and some doubtful nominal species are retained because their relationships are unclear.
- **Future work:** The author states that the variation in lateral-line pore counts in *Compsura gorgonae* and *Pseudocheirodon affinis*, and the environmental factors behind it, will be treated in a later report. He also calls for more complete collections across Costa Rica before the distribution of the fauna can be analyzed in detail.
