## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker Fishery Resources Office) summarizing the outcomes of the **Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Meeting** held 12–13 April 1993 in Grand Junction, CO. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the memo reports on regulatory and conservation-planning discussions concerning the **four big-river fishes** of the Colorado River Basin (implied to include [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]). Key agenda items included proposed **critical habitat** designations, review of the draft **Recovery Implementation Plan and Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP)**, and an associated Section 7 consultation agreement. A razorback sucker recovery plan was distributed but not discussed. No quantitative biological, morphological, or habitat-use data are presented in this source.

## Key Findings
- **Critical Habitat Review**: Recovery team members reviewed and suggested additions/deletions to proposed critical habitat listings for the four endangered [[Colorado River Big-River Fishes]]; recommendations were compiled into a letter to the USFWS Regional Director (Region 6).
- **RIPRAP Concerns**: The team expressed general support for the concept of a formal agreement governing [[Section 7 Consultation]], "sufficient progress," and historic water projects, but raised concerns about specifics of both the current agreement draft and the broader **Recovery Implementation Plan and Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP)**. These concerns were also communicated via letter to Region 6.
- **Razorback Sucker Recovery Plan**: A draft recovery plan for the [[Razorback Sucker]] was distributed to team members but was **not discussed** at this meeting — flagged as an outstanding item for future review.
- **Interagency Participation**: Attendance indicates participation from multiple agencies, including the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker FRO, AZFRO), U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City), National Park Service (Dinosaur NM), and state wildlife agencies (e.g., NDOW), reflecting the multi-jurisdictional nature of [[Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] efforts.
