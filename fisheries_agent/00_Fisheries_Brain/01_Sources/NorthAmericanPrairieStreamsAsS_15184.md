# North American Prairie Streams as Systems for Ecological Study

#ichthyology #marine-biology #stream-ecology

## Executive Summary

This review, written by [[William J. Matthews]] (University of Oklahoma Biological Station), synthesizes the state of knowledge on North American [[Prairie Streams]] and argues that they are under-studied yet valuable systems for stream ecology. Prairie streams are shaped by unstable, often intermittent flow, and by physicochemical harshness such as drought, flood, high temperature, low oxygen and high salinity. The paper compares historical accounts from early explorers (1845-1853) with conditions in 1978-1988, finds that tributary creeks have changed most, and identifies gaps in knowledge about productivity, microbial processing, biota, biotic interactions, disturbance and hydraulics. It closes with twelve research questions and a call for coordinated comparative studies at a single latitude.

## Background & Description

- **Region:** The Great Plains and Osage Plains cover a large part of the [[Central Lowland]] (about 1,680,000 km²) and the semiarid [[Great Plains Province]] (about 1,490,000 km²). The region is defined by low relief, highly variable rainfall and mostly open grassland, with [[Gallery Forest]] along stream borders.
- **Major drainages:** The Missouri, Platte, Kansas, Arkansas, Cimarron, Canadian, Washita and Red Rivers flow west to east; the Trinity, Brazos, Colorado and Pecos drain the southern plains toward the Texas Gulf coast.
- **Stream types:** Prairie streams range from large mainstreams to tens of thousands of kilometers of intermittent or ephemeral creeks. Stream types are differentiated mainly by substratum geology: sand, clay or cobble.
- **Definition problem:** The author notes that a "prairie stream" is hard to define as a distinct type, and that the paper mainly concerns central and southern plains streams.
- **Historical change:** Settlement brought fire suppression, plowing, cattle grazing replacing bison, cutting of gallery forests and agricultural chemical inputs. Whether present streams resemble pre-settlement streams is unknown.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology.** Central and southern prairie streams have a wet-dry cycle with spring and early-summer rains, and many desiccate from late summer through winter. Desiccation is attributed mainly to transpiration and evaporation rather than to a lack of precipitation. The [[Konza Prairie]] water budget reports 820 mm annual input, with about 180 mm lost to groundwater, 450 mm to evapotranspiration and 190 mm to streamflow.

**Water chemistry and turbidity.** Mainstream turbidity varies widely: the Washita River is described as the most turbid river the author has encountered, while the Ninnescah and Chikaskia are clear over sand. The [[Red River]] has conductivity approaching sea water. A salt-spring creek near Oscar, Oklahoma, exceeds sea-water salinity much of the year and is dominated by salt-tolerant pupfish.

**Substrate and interstitial biota.** Clay-bottomed streams are hypothesized to have impermeable beds that limit exchange with deep sediments. Sand-bed streams may support an active psammon, and gravel riffles may influence alkalinity and phosphorus removal.

**Productivity.** Prairie streams with measurements appear highly productive, approaching desert streams. Reported values include:
- North Texas gross primary productivity of 0.7-7.1 g O₂ m⁻² d⁻¹ and 24-h community respiration of 0.6-5.3 g O₂ m⁻² d⁻¹ ([[Hill and Gardner 1987a]]).
- Kansas gross primary productivity of 0.5-1.2 and 24-h respiration of 0.5-1.8 g O₂ m⁻² d⁻¹ ([[Gelroth and Marzolf 1978]]).

**Leaf litter and organic matter.** The [[River Continuum Concept]] predicts a shift from allochthonous to autochthonous to heterotrophic function downstream. Prairie streams differ: headwaters are sunlit, grass litter is a major input (grasses were 57% of direct litterfall in open prairie), and periphyton such as Spirogyra can be a dominant production source. Seston is dominated by fine particulate organic matter, and shredder insects are scarce.

**Biota.** Fishes are the best-known group. Richness is highest in the eastern prairie and low in the western plains. Notable species include [[Campostoma anomalum]] (stoneroller), [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Arkansas River Shiner]] and [[Red River Shiner]]. Invertebrate richness is lower than in comparable streams at lower latitudes; one northern prairie stream had 133 taxa. Intermittent streams had benthic abundance of about 1-2% of that of comparable perennial upland streams. Springs support rich invertebrate faunas.

**Disturbance.** Drought is judged a more drastic disturbance than flood when prolonged. Spates reduce larval fish and macrobenthos but adult fish recover within days, and algae recover in three to four weeks. Ice, fire and impoundments are also considered disturbances; tailwater effects of cold releases are documented at Possum Kingdom Dam on the Brazos River.

## Key Findings

1. **Harsh, variable flow shapes prairie stream biota.** Irregular flow, desiccation and extreme events limit community structure and function.
2. **Tributaries have changed most since settlement.** Early explorers described many creeks as clear, while the same creeks are now turbid and intermittent. Mainstream turbidity probably predated agriculture in some rivers.
3. **Fish declines are documented.** Cross and Moss (1987) report that small-stream fish faunas declined first, and that tolerant big-river species have declined markedly in the last 30 years.
4. **Productivity can be high but is poorly compared.** Methods differ, and prairie stream rates are largely undocumented for most systems.
5. **Grazing fishes and predators structure algae.** In [[Brier Creek]], Campostoma regulated attached algae, and piscivorous bass influenced Campostoma habitat use, creating a cascade.
6. **Largemouth bass protect larval fish.** Harvey (1987) found that large bass suppress medium-sized predatory fish, providing refuges for larval fish.
7. **Competition may be implicated in shiner decline.** The invasion of Red River Shiner into the range of Arkansas River Shiner coincided with the latter's severe decline; this is circumstantial evidence.
8. **Tolerance predicts distribution.** Prairie Notropis are more tolerant of temperature and low oxygen than upland congeners, and fish recolonization after rewatering correlated with oxygen tolerance.
9. **Drought is typically more damaging than spates.** Drought kills more fish and can eliminate microbes, while recolonization after spates is rapid.
10. **Hydraulic models need modification.** Hydraulic approaches assume flow is available, which may not hold in prairie streams with long low-flow periods.
11. **Research agenda.** The author proposes four categories of study (description, adaptation, controlling mechanisms, and comparative or experimental work) and twelve specific questions, including:
    - How biota differ among perennial, intermittent and ephemeral streams.
    - Whether droughts increase heterogeneity while high discharge homogenizes watersheds.
    - How microbes sustain metabolism in prairie streams.
    - How the diversity of stream types at one latitude can be used for comparative studies.
