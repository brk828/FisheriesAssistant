# First List of the Fishes of the Vicinity of Plummers Island, Maryland

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fauna #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1915 paper by W. L. McAtee and A. C. Weed, published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (vol. XXVIII, pp. 1–14), gives the first annotated checklist of fishes for the stretch of the [[Potomac River]] between [[Little Falls]] and [[Great Falls]] and its tributaries, centred on [[Plummers Island]]. The authors record 54 species. Ten are known or possibly introduced. Their survey shows that the fish fauna is shaped by the steep, rocky, vegetation-poor river, by abrupt creek descents that act as barriers, by anadromous runs that stop at the falls, and by the [[Chesapeake and Ohio Canal]].

## Background & Description

The paper updates earlier work by [[Smith and Bean 1899]] and [[Bean and Weed 1911]]. Collecting was done by the [[Washington Biologists' Field Club]], which has held Plummers Island since 1901. Systematic collecting began in 1905 and was intensified in 1912, when A. C. Weed and W. L. McAtee collected vigorously. J. D. Shafer, keeper of Lock No. 11 on the canal, provided substantial help.

The study area is deep, fast, and rocky, with several important rapids, and contains almost no aquatic plants. This contrasts with the vegetated lower Potomac, [[Little River]], and [[Eastern Branch]], and with the broad shallow reaches above Great Falls.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Tributaries and falls.** The creeks drain the Lafayette-formation ridge west of Washington, which reaches 400–500 ft. Each creek is assessed by its falls and fauna:

- **[[Difficult Run]]** (Virginia): the largest creek system in the area, but collecting was thwarted, so only a brief list exists. It is the only known local water body with [[Brook Trout]], which is probably a relict of earlier stocking or colonisation.
- **[[Scotts Run]]** (Virginia): about 43 mi long, with a 25 ft fall at its mouth and about 100 ft of total descent within half a mile. It has the largest number of species above the falls, which the authors attribute to non-natural factors such as bait-fish release near an old mill.
- **[[Dead Run]]** (Virginia): about 24 mi long, falls 120 ft in its last half mile through steep rapids. Only [[Fall Fish]] and [[Black-Nosed Dace]] have passed the falls.
- **[[Turkey Run]]** (Virginia): about 14 mi long, falls about 100 ft in its last half mile through many small rapids rather than one large fall. This allowed two more species (*Hybognathus nuchalis* and *Leuciscus vandoisulus*) to reach the upper reaches.
- **[[Rock Run]]** (Maryland): about 5 mi long, with a culvert under the canal. The culvert marks the boundary between creek fauna and river intruders. Large numbers of suckers (*Catostomus* and *Moxostoma*) are netted there each spring.
- **[[Cabin John Run]]** (Maryland): about 7.4 mi long, also crosses the canal through a culvert. Most of its species were recorded at this crossing.

**Anadromous fishes.** Three herring species and two sturgeon species reach Little Falls but no further. [[American Shad]], [[Striped Bass]] and [[White Perch]] stop at Great Falls. The falls therefore act as hard limits for these species.

**Canal and channel.** The [[Chesapeake and Ohio Canal]] carries abundant submerged vegetation that the river lacks. It is drained every winter, which prevents permanent fish establishment but makes collecting easy. The channel between Plummers Island and the Maryland shore is narrow at low water, reaches about 8 ft deep, and rises up to 15 ft during floods. It serves as a breeding ground for [[Black Bass]], sunfishes and catfishes.

**Distribution patterns.** The authors note that the upper creek courses are dominated by Fall Fish and Black-Nosed Dace, which are expert climbers. [[Etheostoma flabellare]] occurs only in shallow riffles, while *Exoglossum*, *Leuciscus* and *Rhinichthys cataractae* occur in swirling pools beneath small cataracts. Some restrictions are ecological, but others cannot be explained this way.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 54 species were recorded. Approximately 83% belong to five families. The Cyprinidae (minnows) rank first with 19 species, followed by Centrarchidae (sunfishes and basses) with 10, Ictaluridae (catfishes) with 6, and Catostomidae (suckers) and Percidae (perches) with 5 each. The remaining 9 species represent 8 families.
- **Introductions:** 10 species are known or suspected introductions. These include two catfishes ([[Channel Catfish]] and *Ictalurus furcatus*), [[Common Carp]], two crappies (*Pomoxis sparoides* and *P. annularis*), [[Goggle-Eye]] (*Ambloplites rupestris*), [[Warmouth]] (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*), one sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and two basses.
- **Barrier effect of falls:** Shad, striped bass and white perch ascend only to Great Falls. Herrings and sturgeons stop at Little Falls. Above the falls on creeks, fauna is restricted to climbing specialists.
- **Restricted species:** [[Brook Trout]] is limited to Difficult Run. *Percopsis omiscomaycus* was found only in Cabin John Run. *Campostoma anomalum* and *Hybognathus nuchalis* were found only in Turkey Run. The [[Chain Pickerel]] (*Esox reticulatus*) occurred only in the canal. The [[Sea Lamprey]], forked-tailed catfish and [[Pike-Perch]] were known only from the river.
- **Hybridisation:** A specimen from Rock Run appeared to be a hybrid between *Chaenobryttus gulosus* and *Lepomis cyanellus*. The authors note that *C. gulosus* seems to hybridise with all sunfish species present, and that *Ameiurus nebulosus* and *A. natalis* are poorly differentiated locally.
- **Diet notes:** A May 1907 channel catfish stomach was 98% white elm seeds (*Ulmus americanus*). A July 1908 specimen had 90% adult mayflies (*Hexagenia bilineata*).
- **Anadromy:** Shad ascend only to spawn and do not linger. Striped bass adults behave similarly, but young linger. Adult white perch are resident and present year-round.
- **Brook Trout history:** Smith and Bean (1899) reported that the species had been thought extinct in Difficult Run, but a few were recently found there.
