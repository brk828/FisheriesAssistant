# Draft Recovery Goals for Four Endangered Colorado River Fishes and the Wasatch Front Spotted Frog

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ranidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register compilation contains three items. The first is a [[Housing and Urban Development Debenture Interest Rate]] notice, which is administrative and unrelated to aquatic ecology. The second is a U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] notice announcing draft recovery goals that supplement and amend the recovery plans for four endangered fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The third is a notice of intent to conduct a status review of the [[Wasatch Front Spotted Frog Population]] in Utah. The fish recovery goals are the main ecological content. They set objective, measurable criteria for downlisting and delisting and estimate the time needed to reach recovery. The Service asked agencies and the public to comment, particularly on the use of demographic and genetic data in setting minimum viable population standards.

## Background & Description

**Four endangered fishes (draft recovery goals):**
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), Cyprinidae
- [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), Cyprinidae
- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), formerly called Colorado squawfish, Cyprinidae
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), Catostomidae

**Spotted frog (status review):** [[Spotted Frog]] (*Rana luteiventris*), a true frog of the family Ranidae. Adults have large, dark back spots and abdominal coloration ranging from yellow to red. Utah frogs along the Wasatch Front have a salmon-colored venter. West Desert and Sanpete County frogs are yellow to yellow-orange. Utah frogs are reported to have fewer and lighter spots than other populations. The species is closely associated with water, including marshy pond and lake edges, slow-moving cool streams, and springs.

The document is a supplement and amendment to existing recovery plans. It gives site-specific management actions, measurable recovery criteria, and time estimates. The draft goals cover downlisting and delisting criteria by listing factor, management action, and demographic criteria for each population within a recovery unit. They also include updated life-history information and statistical monitoring criteria.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recovery units:** Humpback chub, bonytail, and razorback sucker have two recovery units, the upper basin (above Glen Canyon Dam, Arizona) and the lower basin. The Colorado pikeminnow is considered for recovery only in the upper basin.

**Spotted frog threats (Utah):**
- The [[Wasatch Front Spotted Frog Population]] declined mainly because of habitat loss and modification from reservoir construction and urban and agricultural development. The 1993 finding assigned it listing priority 3 because threats were high and imminent.
- The [[West Desert Spotted Frog Population]] faces degradation of spring habitat and water quality from cattle grazing and other agricultural activities. It was assigned listing priority 1 because threats were moderate to low.

**Conservation Agreement:** The [[Utah Spotted Frog Conservation Agreement]] was developed by the Utah Department of Natural Resources with the Bureau of Land Management, Bureau of Reclamation, Utah Reclamation Mitigation and Conservation Commission, Central Utah Water Conservancy District, the Confederated Tribes of the Goshute Reservation, and the Service. Its goal is long-term conservation of the spotted frog within its historical range in Utah. Because of activities under the agreement and related studies, the Service found the species' status in Utah had improved.

## Key Findings

- **Draft fish recovery goals:** The Service seeks review of the minimum viable population standards, especially how existing demographic and genetic data are applied. Reviewers should also examine the downlisting and delisting monitoring periods for each species.
- **Comment and contact:** Comments on the draft goals and the spotted frog status review are directed to [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] staff. The contact is Jessica L. Gourley, Fish and Wildlife Biologist, and the frog review contact is Henry Maddux, Field Supervisor, in Salt Lake City, Utah. The frog status review comments were due by November 9, 2001, and the document also gives an October 25 deadline for the status review.
- **Spotted frog petition history:** The Utah Nature Study Society petitioned the Service on May 1, 1989, asking that the species be listed as threatened or endangered. A 90-day finding was published October 17, 1989. The 12-month finding was published May 7, 1993 (58 FR 27260), and it found listing warranted but precluded for both Utah populations.
- **Status change:** After the Conservation Agreement was signed on February 13, 1998, the Service issued a new 12-month finding of "not warranted" for the Utah populations. It then announced a status review and a revised 12-month finding for the Wasatch Front population.
- **Interest rate notice (unrelated):** A separate [[Housing and Urban Development Debenture Interest Rate]] notice, dated August 27, 2001, set the interest rate for debentures issued during the six-month period beginning July 1, 2001, at 6 3/8 percent (OCR text reads "63/e"). It is categorically excluded from environmental review under 24 CFR 50.20(a)(6) (OCR text reads "rs(cX6)"). This item has no ecological content.
