# Movement, Migration and Habitat Preference of Radiotelemetered Colorado Squawfish in the Green, White and Yampa Rivers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under a memorandum of understanding, analyzes radiotelemetry data for the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the [[Green River]], [[White River]] and [[Yampa River]] (Colorado and Utah) during 1980 and 1981. Its main objectives were to locate spawning sites and describe movement patterns. The study implanted 31 fish (15 Green, 7 Yampa, 9 White) and identified two movement patterns, sedentary and mobile, linked to sexual maturity. It also documented a spawning migration into the lower [[Yampa Canyon]], the first such documentation for the species in this system. The work was produced within the [[Colorado River Fishery Project]].

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] (CRFP) was started in 1979 by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to study endangered fishes of the Green and Colorado rivers. In 1981 the Green River component was expanded to include the White and Yampa rivers. Radiotelemetry, a key part of the project, aimed to locate spawning sites and document movement. The study area consisted of turbid waters: the Green River from Gray Canyon to [[Dinosaur National Monument]] (about 340 km), the Yampa River from its mouth to Juniper Springs (160 km), and the White River from its mouth to Rangely, Colorado (140 km).

Fish were captured mainly by electrofishing, with some trammel nets and seines. Surgical implantation followed a method adapted from [[Bidgood]] (1980). Transmitters were [[AWI]] SM-1 modules (about 215-day theoretical battery life) in the 40.660-40.700 MHz range, each with a unique pulse rate for individual identification. Tracking used [[Smith-Root]] RF-40 and RF-40 receivers with whip and loop antennas, and a helicopter was the most successful aircraft because airplanes cannot safely fly in most canyon areas. Two sampling approaches were used: long-distance locations on roughly 12-day intervals, and 24-hour local observations of habitat use. Data were managed with the [[MANAGE]] database system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Colorado squawfish use turbid mainstem habitats, mostly shoreline areas with sand substrates, though eddies, runs, backwaters and pools were also used depending on river and season. Spawning occurred in the lower 32 km of the Yampa River, at sites with cobble/rubble substrate and deep pool/riffle structure. At the main spawning site (RK 26.4), water temperature was 22°C, conductivity 340 micromhos/cm, and spawning depths ranged from 0.9 to 2.1 m at velocities of 0.0 to 0.08 m/s.

The report places the species within the wider context of cyprinid migration. Long-distance spawning migrations are common in freshwater fishes of North America, and related species such as the [[Northern Squawfish]] also migrate. The authors argue that water-resource development in the Colorado River Basin probably blocked access to spawning sites and caused loss of subpopulations.

## Key Findings

- **Sample size:** 31 Colorado squawfish were implanted with radio modules in 1980 and 1981: 15 in the Green River, 7 in the Yampa River and 9 in the White River.
- **Movement patterns:** Two patterns were identified. Sedentary fish moved less, while mobile fish made long-distance movements. Total distance moved by six sedentary fish was reported separately from ten mobile fish, which moved up to 245 km. Mobile behavior was associated with sexual maturity, and mature fish undertook spawning migrations.
- **Spawning migration (1981):** Eight radiotagged fish (seven from the Yampa, one from the Green) migrated to the lower Yampa River, where seven moved about 205 km to 160 km downstream and the Green River fish moved 154 km upstream. Fish remained in the lower Yampa Canyon from June 26 to July 10. Fish returned to their former locations after spawning, which suggests a homing ability whose mechanism is unknown.
- **Spawning confirmation:** Thirteen ripe adults were found at RK 26.4 on July 5, 1981. A subsequent survey of the lower 32 km captured 35 ripe or spent adults between July 1 and 9. Including radiotagged fish, at least 43 adults were present in the lower Yampa Canyon between June 5 and July 9. Larval fish (8-10 mm) were collected only in the lower 20 km.
- **Diel activity:** Results were inconclusive. Movement was not strongly rhythmic except after spawning, when activity peaked from 0800 to 1600 hours. During the spawning period, Yampa River activity was higher at night.
- **Activity and environment:** Movement was correlated weakly with flow (r = 0.1) and temperature (r = 0.006), both statistically significant. Activity was highest during the proposed late-June to early-July spawning period.
- **Habitat use:** Mean depths and velocities at fish locations were 1.4 m and 0.2 m/s in the Green River, 0.9 m and 0.1 m/s in the Yampa, and 0.7 m and 0.5 m/s in the White River. Radiotelemetry habitat data agreed closely with collection data, but radiotelemetry was judged more representative because fish were not disturbed and 24-hour data were obtained.
- **Size and maturity:** Mean length of four sedentary fish was 479.5 mm, versus 575.6 mm for mobile fish. [[Seethaler]] (1978) reported that 50% of male and 50% of female Colorado squawfish were mature by 503 mm.
- **Methodological caution:** The authors note that counting only captured ripe males does not by itself indicate a spawning area, and that past distribution and abundance estimates may have been biased by unrecognized migration.
- **Management implications:** Spawning habitat appears highly restricted and specific. Migration is likely a key factor in the survival of Colorado squawfish, and remaining spawning sites may be isolated from suitable adult and nursery habitat. The authors recommend deferring habitat preference curves until radiotelemetry studies are complete.
