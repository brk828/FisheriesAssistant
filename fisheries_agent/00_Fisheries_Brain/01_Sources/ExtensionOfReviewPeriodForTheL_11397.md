# Long Range Plan for Recovery Implementation Program - Upper Colorado River Basin Endangered Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #recovery-planning #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a draft Long Range Plan (dated October 3, 1991) for the Recovery Implementation Program targeting four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The plan outlines a systematic approach to recovery through four major components: (1) establishing recovery goals, (2) determining population status and trends, (3) evaluating controlling variables (physical, chemical, biological factors limiting populations), and (4) implementing management activities to overcome these variables. The document includes a memorandum extending the review period to December 20, 1991, and a critical response letter from Arizona State University dated December 18, 1991. The plan emphasizes that recovery cannot proceed without understanding habitat requirements, flow regimes, channel structure, nonnative fish impacts, and reproduction/recruitment failures.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program addresses four endangered fish species across the upper [[Colorado River]] and [[Green River]] basins. Current recovery goals vary by species: [[Razorback Sucker]] faces imminent extinction with no evidence of successful recruitment (all captured individuals exceed 20 years of age); the immediate goal is preventing extinction in the wild. [[Colorado Squawfish]] recovery targets naturally self-sustaining populations in the Green River Basin (Green River from Colorado River confluence to Yampa River confluence, lower 220 km of Yampa River, lower 240 km of White River), Colorado River from Palisade to Lake Powell, Salt and/or Verde River, and lower 305 km of San Juan River. [[Humpback Chub]] recovery requires protection or restoration of five viable, self-sustaining wild populations plus two refugia populations as safeguards. [[Bonytail Chub]]'s short-term goal is preventing extinction, with quantitative goals to be developed once that threat is removed.

The plan differs from individual species recovery plans by identifying activities necessary for all four species' recovery specifically in the upper Colorado and Green River basins, though it includes Lower Basin and San Juan River research necessary for upper basin recovery (e.g., identification of genetically distinct populations).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Physical habitat degradation represents a primary threat across the basin. Channel structure has been altered through channelization from levees, dikes, rip-rap, vegetative encroachment (particularly tamarisk), and sediment deposition. Historically important habitats—floodplains, wetlands, bottomlands, side channels, backwaters, and embayments—have been cut off from main channels or filled with silt and vegetation. Sediment accretion is causing pools and slow runs to become shallow.

Flow regimes have been substantially modified by mainstem impoundments. Cold water releases from [[Flaming Gorge]] and other dams have contributed to extirpation of warmwater fishes downstream. Flow alterations affect not only direct fish requirements but also channel structure, sediment transport, substrate characteristics, vegetative encroachment, water temperatures, contaminants, food supply, and interactions with nonnative fishes.

Of 55 fish species within the Upper Basin, 42 are nonnative. Many introduced species are piscivorous or compete for resources. Nearly as many fish extinctions in North America have been attributed to introduced species as to physical habitat losses. Sportfishing mortality is estimated at 5-20% in the Yampa/Green system based on approximately 10% angler harvest of tagged adult squawfish.

Chemical factors include contaminants (heavy metals, pesticides, selenium) from irrigation drainwater. Preliminary investigations suggest potential contaminants problems in localized areas, with comprehensive results expected by 1994.

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Approach:**
- The plan organizes recovery into a logical sequence: establish recovery goals → determine status/trends → evaluate controlling variables → conduct management activities
- If management activities succeed, populations should respond such that status and trends eventually match goals
- A Species/Sub-basin Matrix framework is proposed but left blank for future priority and status tracking
- Species Action Plans will contain specific task descriptions for each species and sub-basin; only a draft for [[Razorback Sucker]] in Colorado River sub-basin is included

**Population Status and Critical Needs:**
- Population Viability Analysis (PVA) scheduled for [[Colorado Squawfish]] in FY 92 (June 1992 start, September 1992 results)
- PVA proposed for Little Colorado River [[Humpback Chub]] population in 1992
- Genetics studies of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] populations underway with FY 93 results expected
- Investigation of systematics, taxonomy, and genetics of Gila (chub) species began FY 89, scheduled complete FY 94
- Interagency standardized monitoring program being evaluated during 1991; results expected February 1992

**Razorback Sucker Crisis (Colorado River Sub-basin):**
- All razorbacks captured in recent years thought to be >20 years old
- Assuming existing razorbacks live to age 60, species will become extinct within 40 years without intervention
- No evidence of successful recruitment
- First recovery priority: prevent extinction in the wild
- Intensive effort needed to collect razorbacks for refugia protection before populations disappear entirely
- Three theories for decline: (1) predation on eggs/larvae causing 100% mortality, (2) channelization combined with flow alteration eliminating flooded lowlands needed for reproduction/rearing, (3) contaminants interfering with reproduction/recruitment

**Research and Monitoring Activities:**
- Channel monitoring program begun FY 90 using video and ground data at regular intervals
- Larval drift assessment ongoing in Yampa/Green, Colorado/Gunnison, and San Juan systems
- Projects approved for FY 92 to quantify hatchery fish in Colorado and White rivers
- Flow recommendations developed or in development for Yampa, Green, and Colorado rivers
- Instream flow senior scientist to be contracted FY 92 to evaluate studies and resolve technical issues
- Old Charley Wash wetlands restoration study underway to test hypotheses about flooded habitat importance
- Chemoreception/imprinting study began 1991, continues through 2000

**Management Priorities Identified:**
- Restore channel structure, particularly floodplain connectivity
- Maintain, enhance, and protect instream flows
- Manage sediment variables through land-use and flow management
- Explore passage restoration opportunities (Redlands: 50 miles habitat; Price-Stub and roller dam: 80 miles; Taylor Draw: 50 miles)
- Control nonnative fish impacts (informal agreement with Colorado to review stocking proposals; one being developed with Utah)
- Minimize sampling/handling mortality
- Develop refugia protection and propagation facilities (experimental/research facility at Ouray, Utah; refuge facility at Horsethief Wildlife Management Area, Colorado, in development FY 92)

**Critical External Review:**
Arizona State University letter (December 18, 1991) provides harsh criticism: considers the plan's philosophical approach fundamentally flawed by assuming (1) all four species can be recovered using the same strategy and (2) water development and endangered species recovery can be accomplished concurrently. Reviewers argue the plan provides "little substantive guidance," consisting mainly of "abbreviated rhetorical justification for perpetuation of RIF activities exclusive of active endangered species management." They express concern that bonytail and razorback sucker lack capability to survive until management can begin given the rate of progress, and criticize the plan for emphasizing information gathering over specific recovery actions specifying how, where, who, and what needs attention.
