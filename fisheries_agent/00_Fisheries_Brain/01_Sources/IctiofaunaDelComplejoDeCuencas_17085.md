# Ictiofauna del Complejo de Cuencas Nazas, Aguanaval, Parras y El Chorro, Durango, Zacatecas y Coahuila, México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #centrarchidae #percidae #characidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 undergraduate thesis from the [[Universidad de Nuevo León]], Facultad de Ciencias Biológicas, presents a taxonomic and distributional survey of the freshwater fish fauna of the interior basins of the [[Nazas River]], [[Aguanaval River]], Parras (Río San Lorenzo) and [[El Chorro]] in Durango, Zacatecas and Coahuila, Mexico. The author, Ma. Gregoria Guadalupe Salas Martínez, examined 11,648 specimens from 26 collections at 24 localities (1964–1968), using 811 specimens for counts and measurements. The work describes native, introduced and extinct species, proposes possible new taxa (Notropis sp., Gila conspersa ssp., Astyanax fasciatus ssp.), and documents the decline of the Parras fauna.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Compare the ichthyofauna of the closed interior basins (Nazas, Aguanaval, Parras, El Chorro), tied to the zoogeographic history of the [[Rio Grande Basin]] and the Nearctic–Neotropical faunal interface described by [[Fernando de Buen]] (1947).
- **Methods:** Specimens from the [[UNL Fish Collection]] were preserved by the method of Hall et al. (1962). Meristic and morphometric data followed Hubbs and Lagler (1970), using a minimum of 10 specimens per species per collection; measurements were in millimetres. Each species account includes diagnosis, description, colour in life and preservative, local and general distribution, ecology, examined material and literature records.
- **Historical context:** [[Garman]] (1881), [[Meek]] (1903, 1904) and De Buen (1947) established the early species lists. Meek assumed the Nazas once connected to the Río Grande and so shared its fishes, citing 12 species in 1903.
- **Study area:** The Nazas rises in central Durango from the union of the Ríos del Oro and Ramos and drains to the Laguna de Mayrán (catchment 42,450 km², of which 34,580 km² produces runoff). The Aguanaval rises in Zacatecas, drains toward the Laguna de Viesca (catchment 20,800 km²), and loses much of its upper flow in sand dunes. The Parras basin has almost no natural streams left, and El Chorro (Arteaga, Coahuila) is a spring-fed complex of about 5 km within a national park.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature:** Recorded at roughly 12–24 °C across collection sites; the Aguanaval is generally cooler than the Nazas.
- **Substrate and vegetation:** Sandy, muddy, gravelly or cobbled bottoms. Aquatic vegetation includes algae, *Eleocharis*, *Cyperus*, *Polygonum*, *Scirpus* and *Marsilea*. Banks support willows, poplars (alamillos) and grassland.
- **Habitat change:** Irrigation diverts the Nazas during the dry season, so the river often ceases flowing at the exit of the Cañón de Fernández. Industrialisation, canalisation, reduced flow and domestic waste were identified as drivers of extinction in Parras.
- **Introductions:** Common carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), goldfish (*Carassius auratus*), guppy (*Poecilia reticulata*), swordtail (*Xiphophorus helleri*), largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*), bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and green sunfish-type Centrarchids (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*) are reported. At El Chorro, goldfish threaten the native Gila modesta, which the author estimated at about 3,000–4,000 individuals.
- **Biogeography:** Mexico lies between the Nearctic and Neotropical regions. Northern families (Percidae, Centrarchidae, Cyprinidae) reached the basin through the Rocky Mountain–Sierra Madre Occidental corridor; Neotropical forms such as Astyanax reached northwards through lowland routes.

## Key Findings

**Faunal inventory (approximate, from the text)**
- **Nazas basin:** 11 native species were known before this study, plus 2 introduced species (Micropterus salmoides, Lepomis macrochirus).
- **Aguanaval basin:** 10 native species and 3 introduced species (Micropterus salmoides, Lepomis macrochirus, Chaenobryttus gulosus).
- **Parras (Río San Lorenzo):** 2 native species surviving (Gila sp.) and 4 introduced species (Poecilia reticulata, Xiphophorus helleri, Carassius auratus, Cyprinus carpio). Four species are now extinct: Stypodon signifer, Dionda episcopa punctifer, Cyprinodon latifasciatus, Characodon lateralis.
- **El Chorro:** 1 native species (Gila modesta) plus introduced Carassius auratus.

**Taxonomic results**
- *Astyanax fasciatus* ssp.: first record for the Aguanaval and Parras. The Parras population is possibly introduced and may represent a new subspecies, based on morphological and colour differences from *A. f. mexicanus*.
- *Notropis lutrensis garmani*: two forms, one in the Nazas and a slightly different one in the Aguanaval; assigned subspecific rank by Contreras (unpublished).
- *Gila conspersa* (Nazas) and *Gila conspersa* ssp. (Aguanaval): separated on pharyngeal tooth formula (1,4-4,1 vs. 2,4-4,2) and colour, suggesting subspecific separation.
- *Gila* sp. (Parras): distinguished from Nazas and Aguanaval forms by colour pattern and tooth formula (2,5-4,2 and 2,4-4,2); treated as an endemic, undefined species.
- *Notropis* sp. (Aguanaval): considered a possible new species related to *N. amabilis*, but material is limited.
- *Cyprinodon* sp. (Nazas and Aguanaval) and *Etheostoma* sp.: left unresolved; Minckley and Barbour were studying the *Etheostoma pottsii* complex.

**Conservation status**
- Four Parras species are extinct: Stypodon signifer (known from two 1880 specimens; Hubbs searched unsuccessfully in 1953), Dionda episcopa punctifer, Cyprinodon latifasciatus (last nine specimens obtained by Hubbs in 1903), and Characodon lateralis (last collected 1939).
- Gila modesta is a relict and the southeasternmost member of its genus. The author urged eradication of carp at El Chorro to protect it.

**Specimens examined:** 11,648 total from 26 collections and 24 localities; 811 used for counts and measurements; half of the specimens were sent to the Instituto Mexicano de la Pesca.

**Principal distributional points**
- Astyanax fasciatus is recorded from six Nazas collections, three Aguanaval montane collections and one Aguanaval lowland collection (the lowland one only), plus Parras.
- Carassius auratus and Cyprinus carpio are introduced in Parras, with the goldfish in the Parras tanks.

**Conclusions**
- The Nazas basin is well studied; the Aguanaval is not, so this work aims to add to its ichthyofauna.
- Parras has a single surviving endemic complex (Gila sp.) that is threatened by introductions and habitat alteration.
- Several taxa cannot be resolved without more material and geological data, and the author notes that future collections may revise these decisions.
