# Follow-Up Items to the January 25, 1991 Aquatic Coordination Team Meeting

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative and scientific coordination memorandum from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program, summarizing action items from an Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT) meeting focused on endangered fish species management in the Colorado River, Grand Canyon. The primary taxa of concern are the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Bonytail Chub]], all federally listed species. Key scientific/methodological topics include stomach content sampling (gastric lavage), radio-telemetry implantation mortality, meristic data collection protocols, larval fish sampling overlap, and an emerging exotic species (Striped Bass) concern. The letter to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] seeks regulatory clarification on "take" thresholds, permitting for propagation efforts, and handling protocols for captured Bonytail Chub.

## Background & Description
Limited direct morphological data is provided, as this is a program-management document rather than a primary research paper. Notable data points:
- **Legal "take" size threshold**: A specific size of **20 mm** is referenced as the potential regulatory cutoff defining a "take" of endangered fish under Fish & Wildlife Service designation — clarification was explicitly requested from FWS.
- **Meristic data collection**: Ongoing meristic measurements (countable morphological traits, e.g., fin rays, scale counts) are being collected from **1 in every 10** captured [[Humpback Chub]] individuals during photographic identification efforts.
- **Photographic identification**: Continued photography of captured Humpback Chub is used as a non-lethal identification/monitoring technique, to be evaluated against thermal stress risk during summer months.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Little Colorado River (LCR)**: Identified as the primary spawning habitat for [[Humpback Chub]] in the Grand Canyon system; the 1991 spring spawning study was to be led by Arizona Game & Fish (AGF) in coordination with GCES.
- **Paria River confluence**: Flagged (per Wendell "Chuck" Minckley's recommendation) as an important sampling location, suggesting ecological significance of tributary mouths as habitat/refugia for native fish.
- **Bubbling Ponds Facility**: An off-site propagation facility proposed for rearing Humpback Chub larvae removed from the LCR, indicating a captive-breeding/head-starting conservation strategy to supplement wild populations while mitigating risk of stocking hazards (e.g., invasion of Oak Creek by escaped chub).
- **Exotic species interaction**: Striped Bass is identified as an exotic predator/competitor of concern in the Colorado River mainstem, prompting a two-phase study (literature review, then targeted field studies) coordinated between GCES, FWS, and AGF.
- **Endangered species community structure**: The memo implies a multi-species assemblage of conservation concern co-occurring in the Grand Canyon reach — [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] — with overlapping larval fish sampling efforts between BIO/WEST and AGF requiring de-confliction.

## Key Findings
- **Stomach Pump (Gastric Lavage) Study**: BIO/WEST and Rich Valdez's preliminary work shows promise but was cautioned against "post hoc hypotheses" framing (raised by Michael Douglas). A three-phase follow-up proposal was mandated: (1) evaluate technique efficiency/fish stress effects, (2) historical review of prior food-habit studies, (3) identify necessary follow-up studies.
- **Radio-telemetry Mortality**: Elevated mortality of [[Humpback Chub]] was observed following radio-transmitter implantation during a BIO/WEST field trip. Action items include evaluating antibiotic coating of surgical sites to reduce infection-related mortality, and standardizing protocol for removing/clipping antennas from non-functional transmitters.
- **Propagation Program Logistics**: Arizona Game & Fish is preparing a site plan (due Feb 2, 1991) and security plan to prevent propagated chub from escaping into Oak Creek, a non-native waterway, highlighting containment as a key conservation risk-management issue.
- **Permitting Ambiguity**: Uncertainty exists regarding the exact regulatory size threshold (~20 mm) constituting an illegal "take" of listed larval/juvenile fish — clarification formally requested from FWS.
- **Razorback Sucker Status**: Listing status of the [[Razorback Sucker]] was still being finalized at time of writing; GCES/FWS requested official documentation as it was issued.
- **Bonytail Chub Handling Protocol Gap**: No clear guidance existed on whether captured wild [[Bonytail Chub]] should be removed from the Grand Canyon system or left in situ, nor on species-specific handling protocols (versus those used for Humpback Chub) — this represents a critical gap flagged for FWS response.
- **Interagency Coordination Structure**: Multiple entities (BIO/WEST, AGF, ASU, FWS, GCES) were tasked with resolving overlapping larval fish sampling methodologies and consolidating monitoring efforts (e.g., coordinated sampling at the Paria River mouth).
- **Population Modeling**: GCES sought participation in an external population modeling meeting (contact: Pat Nelson) to assess applicability of demographic models to Colorado River endangered fish populations, with [[Wendell Minckley]] potentially representing ACT interests.
