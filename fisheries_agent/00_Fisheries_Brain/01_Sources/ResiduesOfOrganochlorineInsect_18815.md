# Organochlorine Insecticide and PCB Residues in Fish from Lakes Saint Clair and Erie, Canada (1968–1976)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae

## Executive Summary

This monitoring study, reprinted from the *Pesticides Monitoring Journal* (Vol. 12, No. 2, September 1978), reports [[Organochlorine Insecticides]] and [[Polychlorinated Biphenyls]] (PCBs) in edible fish flesh from [[Lake Saint Clair]] and [[Lake Erie]] collected between 1968 and 1976. Eighteen species from Lake Saint Clair and 19 from Lake Erie were analyzed. [[DDT]] residues (ΣDDT) declined across species by 1975–76, [[Dieldrin]] residues rose over the same period, and PCB residues rose in some species and fell in others. Several fish exceeded the 2.0 ppm PCB tolerance of [[Health and Welfare Canada]]. Lake Erie sediments were five to ten times more contaminated than Lake Saint Clair sediments, but fish tissue patterns did not follow that gradient for DDT and dieldrin. PCBs were generally higher in Lake Erie fish.

## Background & Description

**Study rationale.** The work began in 1968, before Ontario restricted the use of aldrin, dieldrin and heptachlor (1969) and DDT (1970–71), to document contaminant levels before regulation. Monitoring continued afterward to assess the effect of these restrictions. PCBs were added because they were identified in fish from both lakes and because voluntary restrictions on their use began in 1971. The watersheds on the Canadian side of both lakes drain Ontario's most intensive agricultural belt, which before the restrictions accounted for 90 percent of organochlorine insecticides used in Ontario.

**Sampling.** Fish were taken by gill net or trap net, mostly from Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources field staff, with some large fish from commercial gill net catches. Nomenclature followed the American Fisheries Society list.

**Analytical methods.** Eviscerated, headless fish were minced and homogenized, then extracted by Soxhlet with hexane. Cleanup used a Florisil column, followed by charcoal fractionation to separate PCBs from insecticides (see [[Florisil Cleanup]] and [[Soxhlet Extraction]]). Quantification used [[Gas Chromatography]] with electron-capture detection. Thin-layer chromatography confirmed identity on random samples, which was essential for [[Chlordane]] because co-extractives cause misidentification. Average recoveries were 89% (p,p′-DDT), 96% (p,p′-DDE), 94% (p,p′-TDE), 91% (o,p′-DDT), 89% (dieldrin), 92% (cis-chlordane), 90% (trans-chlordane) and 85–90% (PCBs). Data were not corrected for recovery. Quantitation limits were 0.005 ppm (fat basis) for organochlorine insecticides and 0.05 ppm for PCBs. PCBs were quantified against [[Aroclor]] 1254 and 1260 standards, which varied from 5:1 to 4:1 in mixture ratio.

**Reference levels.** The ΣDDT action level was 5.0 ppm, set by both the Canadian and US governments. The PCB limit was 5.0 ppm under the US Food and Drug Administration and 2.0 ppm under Health and Welfare Canada.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pesticide inputs.** Miles and Harris and Frank et al. showed that creeks draining intensive pesticide-use areas carried DDT and dieldrin into Lake Erie. Frank et al. found that fish from these streams had ΣDDT and dieldrin residues one order of magnitude (10–15 times) higher than fish from the adjoining lake waters, notably [[Bluegill]], [[Brown Bullhead]], [[Pumpkinseed]] and [[Rock Bass]].

**Sediment reservoirs.** Lake Erie sediments held five to ten times more ΣDDT, dieldrin and PCBs than Lake Saint Clair sediments. The authors attribute this to sediment being transitory through Lake Saint Clair but accumulating in Lake Erie's basins. Western basin sediments held two to three times more ΣDDT and PCBs than the central or eastern basins. Parent p,p′-DDT was low or absent in Lake Erie sediments and was not found in many Lake Erie fish.

**Feeding and fat.** Higher residues were associated with higher fat content, and residues in migratory species (white bass, freshwater drum, coho salmon, rainbow smelt) tracked fish size more than basin of capture. Walleye with the highest dieldrin residues were the largest in their catch (2.0 and 2.3 kg) and carried 20–22% fatty tissue.

## Key Findings

**Lake Saint Clair**

- **ΣDDT:** No species averaged above the 5.0 ppm action level. The highest mean was 1.19 ppm in [[Longnose Gar]], the only species above 1.0 ppm. Eight of 12 gar from Tremblay Creek ranged 1.10–2.35 ppm.
- **Trend:** Seven species showed ΣDDT declines by 1971; [[Quillback]] showed no apparent change. In all years, ΣDDT was below 0.5 ppm except in the individuals noted above.
- **Smallmouth bass and walleye:** [[Smallmouth Bass]] mean ΣDDT was 0.42 ppm in 1968 and 0.76 ppm in 1976, but the fish were heavier (453 g vs. 853 g). For similar weight classes, the residue declined slightly. [[Walleye]] ΣDDT fell steadily from 0.20 ppm (1968) to 0.11 ppm (1971) to 0.08 ppm (1976).
- **Dieldrin:** All species averaged below 0.10 ppm, and 12 species averaged at or below 0.01 ppm. The highest mean (0.09 ppm) was in smallmouth bass in 1975. Dieldrin increased in smallmouth bass between 1968 and 1976 by weight class.
- **PCBs:** One 435 g [[Mooneye]] exceeded the 5.0 ppm FDA level. Several species exceeded the 2.0 ppm Canadian level. Smallmouth bass (2.1 ppm mean, 1975) and [[Channel Catfish]] (2.3 ppm mean, 1971) had means above it. Four of six channel catfish from Mitchell Bay contained 2.0–4.0 ppm. Smallmouth bass PCBs rose between 1968 and 1975.
- **Other compounds:** [[Hexachlorobenzene]] was below 0.1 ppm, with a highest mean of 0.024 ppm in redhorse mullet. [[Chlordane]] and [[Heptachlor Epoxide]] were confirmed at low levels only in freshwater drum and walleye by 1976. No [[Endrin]], [[Methoxychlor]] or [[Mirex]] was detected.

**Lake Erie**

- **ΣDDT:** No species averaged above 5.0 ppm. Three [[Coho Salmon]] from the central basin in 1970 (the largest fish, 1,963–2,640 g) contained 8.23, 7.67 and 7.61 ppm. The full 1970 catch of 11 averaged 2.80 ppm. Coho in 1971 averaged 1.76 ppm.
- **Other high ΣDDT:** Eastern basin smallmouth bass averaged 1.2 ppm in 1971, and individuals in 1968 reached 1.53 and 4.28 ppm. [[White Bass]] and walleye also had individuals above 1.0 ppm.
- **Basin trends:** Eastern basin ΣDDT declined in rock bass, white bass and yellow perch. Central basin coho peaked in 1971 and declined thereafter. Western basin data were too sparse for a clear trend.
- **Dieldrin:** Only white bass and walleye caught in 1975 averaged 0.1 ppm or more. Three western white bass had 0.12–0.19 ppm, and two central basin white bass had 0.17 ppm. Dieldrin increased in several species between 1968–71 and 1975–76, including [[Alewife]], smallmouth bass, white bass, [[Freshwater Drum]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Gizzard Shad]] and walleye.
- **PCBs:** Eastern basin smallmouth bass averaged above 5.0 ppm in 1971, as did western basin channel catfish (5.0 ppm). Individual white bass, central basin coho (1970) and western basin walleye (1975) exceeded 5.0 ppm. Mean residues above the 2.0 ppm Canadian limit occurred in alewife (eastern, 1971; 3.0 ppm), white bass (western, 1971 and 1975), freshwater drum (central, 1971; 3.7 ppm), coho (central, 1970) and gizzard shad (western and central, 1971). PCB residues in central basin coho peaked in 1970 and declined through 1975. In the eastern basin, smallmouth bass and yellow perch peaked in 1971, while weight-class comparisons showed a 1972 peak.
- **Chlordane and heptachlor epoxide:** Cis-chlordane was first confirmed in 1972 in white bass and yellow perch. In 1975–76, both cis- and trans-chlordane were detected in several species, with the highest residues in [[Rainbow Smelt]] in 1976. [[Heptachlor Epoxide]] was first identified in rainbow trout from Silver Creek and later in several other species.
- **Other compounds:** [[Endosulfan]] (mean 0.025 ppm) was found in three rainbow trout from Silver Creek in 1974. Mirex was not detected.

**Comparison with earlier studies**

- Reinert (1967–68) reported ΣDDT and dieldrin two to nine times higher than this study for 13 Lake Erie species. Gizzard shad values were similar (0.50 vs. 0.32 ppm), but yellow perch differed (0.9 vs. 0.1 ppm).
- Reinke et al. (1970) reported values 1.5–10 times higher for six Lake Erie species. Alewife were similar (0.34 vs. 0.24 ppm).
- Carr et al. (1970–71) data corresponded more closely with this study's values.
- Coho salmon ΣDDT in 1970 matched Reinert and Bergman's 1969 coho values.
- Spottail shiner dieldrin and ΣDDT matched Suns and Rees; their PCB values were an order of magnitude lower here.

**Overall conclusion:** The authors concluded that restrictions on DDT and dieldrin were followed by declining ΣDDT residues in most species, while dieldrin and PCB patterns were mixed. PCB contamination remained the main concern, with Lake Erie fish carrying higher PCB burdens than Lake Saint Clair fish.
