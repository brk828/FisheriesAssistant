# Age Determination and Growth of the Grayling *Thymallus thymallus* (L.) of the River Lugg, Herefordshire

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 study by J. M. Hellawell, published in the *Journal of Fish Biology*, validates scale-based [[Age Determination]] in European [[Grayling]] (*Thymallus thymallus*) from the River Lugg, Herefordshire, and describes their growth. Monthly samples of 258 fish were collected by electrofishing between February 1964 and February 1965. The author establishes that an annual scale "check" (winter ring) forms in November, which resolves contradictory earlier reports. Length and weight growth curves, a length-weight relationship, and seasonal growth patterns are presented. Growth was most rapid in spring and early summer.

## Background & Description

Grayling are unusual among the [[Salmonidae]] as a "coarse-fish" member of the family, and British populations had received little fishery-biology attention. Most prior work focused on competition for food with [[Brown Trout]], as seen in studies by Dahl (1962) on Danish streams and Radforth (1940) on the Tweed watershed. Earlier British scale studies by Hutton (1923), Gerrish (1938, 1939) and Jones (1953) gave conflicting accounts of seasonal ring formation.

The study sits within a wider survey of fishes of the [[River Wye]] system (Hellawell, 1969). Scale morphology is described as follows:
- A corrugated anterior margin is present on scales of very young fish.
- Concentric rings vary in spacing, producing a banding effect.
- Jones (1953) classified bands into three categories: wide "summer" rings, narrow "summer" rings, and "winter" rings (slender, often broken rings). The author adopts the term **check** for the third category, noting that "summer" and "winter" labels have been applied inconsistently in the literature.

**Methods (summary):**
- Sampling: Monthly electrofishing from a punt, with stop nets when conditions allowed. No grayling were caught in January 1964 or January 1965.
- Measurements: Weight (nearest 0.1 g), standard length (nearest mm), sex (macroscopic, or microscopic for immature fish), and age from scales.
- Scale preparation: Scales from the shoulder region were macerated in 2–5% [[Potassium Hydroxide]] and examined by transmitted light.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Lugg grayling were sampled in a river reach between Hampton Court and Bodenham. Seasonal scale growth paralleled feeding activity (Hellawell, 1969). Age-class dominance and population structure were compared with [[Peterson (1968)]] for the River Indalsalven and the Baltic Sea:
- Ages 1+ and 2+ made up 66% of the total in the Indalsalven, 83% in the Baltic, and 86% in the Lugg.
- Peterson's 5+ to 8+ classes were 3%, 1.5%, 1.5% and 1% of the total in the Indalsalven. No fish older than 4+ was taken from the Baltic.
- Most grayling are recorded up to 7+, and both Hutton (1923) and Peterson (1968) recorded one 8+ fish each.

Comparative growth curves are drawn from the River Test ([[Hutton]], 1923), [[Lake Storsjön]] (Gustafson, 1949), [[Llyn Tegid]] (Jones, 1953) and the River Indalsälven (Peterson, 1968). The author notes these curves are not strictly comparable because length measurement methods vary across studies.

## Key Findings

**Scale check formation**
- Monthly frequency distributions of rings beyond the last check (1963 and 1962 year classes, June–December) show that check formation occurs in **November**.
- From June to October, no fish had fewer than eight rings at the scale margin. In November and December, most had fewer than eight.
- Timing at the Lugg is only slightly different from Llyn Tegid, which the author considers insignificant. Welsh grayling differ markedly from Scandinavian and Montana populations.
- Earlier conflicting reports of check timing (Jones, 1953: August–September; Brown, 1943: April–May; Peterson, 1968: May–early June) are reconciled by the November result.
- Spawning was assumed to occur in March. Ages are counted as elapsed years of life, not scale checks.

**Age validation and structure**
- Length-frequency modes were composed mostly of single year classes, and modes advanced through the year. This supports age determination from scales.
- Hutton's (1923) scale-based ages were judged valid.
- Spawning marks were not confirmed: Jones (1953) found none even in males with recently spent testes.

**Maturity**
- Most Lugg grayling were immature. Maturity was reached near the end of the third year of life (2+), and spawning occurred at the start of the fourth year (4th year of life).
- October–December ratios: 1+ males 2 mature/24 immature (8%); 1+ females 2 mature/20 immature (9%); 2+ males 4 mature/1 immature (80%); 2+ females 8 of 8 mature (100%).

**Growth**
- Mean length and weight at each age were computed from pooled monthly samples. Sexes were combined because male and female means were almost identical.
- Length increases rapidly and linearly in early years, with accelerated body mass gain.
- Length-weight relationship: log W = 3.07 log L − 1.89, equivalent to **W = 0.0129 L^3.07** (W in g, L in cm).
- Seasonally, 0+ fish grew rapidly through their first season. Older fish grew fastest from April to June, then grew little until November.

**Summary and conclusions**
- Scale-based age determination in Lugg grayling is validated.
- Check formation occurs in November.
- Growth is greatest in spring and early summer.
