# Temperatures Selected and Avoided by Fish at Various Acclimation Temperatures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #thermal-ecology #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 study by Cherry, Dickson, and Cairns (J. Fish. Res. Board Can. 32: 485-491) investigated **behavioral thermoregulation** in 13 freshwater fish species collected near a thermally-influenced discharge site at the confluence of the East and New Rivers, Glen Lyn, Virginia. Using a semi-controlled mobile field laboratory, researchers measured **selected (preferred)** and **avoided** temperatures across a range of acclimation temperatures (6–30°C, in 3°C decrements), simulating seasonal transitions from summer to winter. Both selected and avoided temperatures declined with decreasing acclimation temperature across all taxa, but the relationship differed by family: [[Centrarchidae|centrarchids]] and [[Cyprinidae|cyprinids]] showed an increasing acclimation-selection gap as acclimation temperature fell, while [[Salmonidae|salmonids]] displayed a U-shaped relationship centered on an ~18°C final preferendum. [[Eurythermal Species|Eurythermal]] taxa (most cyprinids, centrarchids, ictalurids) exhibited wide avoidance temperature ranges (≥10°C), indicating high thermal tolerance/flexibility, whereas [[Stenothermal Species|stenothermal]] cold-water salmonids showed narrower ranges (6–9°C) and greater physiological sensitivity to thermal extremes.

## Background & Description

*Note: This is primarily a behavioral/physiological ecology study; morphological data are limited to specimen selection criteria.*

- **Size range tested:** Fish differed in total length (TL) by >50 mm but <120 mm within a species, standardizing for ontogenetic effects on thermal preference.
- **Age/reproductive constraints:** Only fish ≤1 year old were tested; gravid individuals were excluded to avoid confounding reproductive physiology with thermal response.
- **Sex differences:** Preliminary testing found no significant sex-based differences in temperature response; trials were conducted without sex discrimination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat context:** Specimens were collected from the East and New Rivers near Glen Lyn, VA, an area subject to thermal loading from a power station discharge (ambient river temp up to 27°C, with up to 8°C differential between heated discharge channels and unaffected areas).

**Thermal guild classification:**

- **Eurythermal species** (broad thermal tolerance): Most [[Cyprinidae|cyprinids]], [[Centrarchidae|centrarchids]], and [[Ictaluridae|ictalurids]] (e.g., [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Spotted Bass]], [[Spotfin Shiner]]). These species were captured in both thermally-loaded and unheated river zones, particularly in fall/winter, indicating flexible habitat selection independent of strict thermal constraints. This flexibility allows forage fish to select habitats based on predation pressure rather than temperature alone, and allows predatory basses/sunfish to forage across the thermal gradient.

- **Stenothermal species** (narrow thermal tolerance): [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], and to a lesser extent [[Bluehead Chub]] (Nocomis leptocephalus). These cold-water species were physiologically constrained by ambient river temperatures exceeding their lethal limits; trout were entirely absent from the thermally-impacted system, while bluehead chub persisted only in small numbers in cooler backwater refugia.

**Species with no measurable thermal responsiveness** (excluded from primary analysis due to poor/high-variance responses): *Catostomus commersoni*, *Etheostoma blennioides*, *Percina crassa roanoka*, and *Hypentelium nigricans*.

**Mortality factors:** Attempts to acclimate trout above 24°C caused direct thermal mortality (lethal heat stress). In cyprinids, mortality at high acclimation temperatures was attributed not to heat directly but to opportunistic infection by pathogenic myxobacteria, which proliferate at elevated temperatures.

## Key Findings

### Temperature Selection (Preference)
- **Highest temperature selectors** (all acclimation levels): [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and [[Spotted Bass]] (*Micropterus punctulatus*), followed by [[Channel Catfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Spotfin Shiner]], and other cyprinids.
- **Lowest temperature selectors:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) and [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*).
- As acclimation temperature decreased, the gap between acclimation and selected temperature **increased** for centrarchids and cyprinids.
- Salmonids showed a distinct pattern: near their **final thermal preferendum** (~15–18°C), selected temperature ≈ acclimation temperature. Above 18°C acclimation, selected temp was lower than acclimation; below 15°C, the relationship reversed (selected > acclimation).
- Reported final preferenda align with Ferguson (1958): 13.6°C for rainbow trout, 14–16°C for brook trout.

### Temperature Avoidance
- Avoided temperatures **declined** as acclimation temperature decreased.
- The **range** between upper and lower avoidance temperatures **increased** as acclimation temperature declined (i.e., greater thermal "bandwidth" tolerated at colder acclimation).
- Cyprinids generally avoided temperatures 1–6°C lower than centrarchids; [[Bluehead Chub]] avoided temperatures ~9°C lower than centrarchids.
- Maximum avoided temperature for rainbow/brook trout: 25°C (at 24°C acclimation) — 4–7°C lower than cyprinid upper avoidance limits.
- **Channel catfish** showed the widest avoidance range among species tested: 12°C range at 30°C acclimation, expanding to 21°C range at 6°C acclimation.
- Sunfish/minnow avoidance ranges: 9–10°C (warm acclimation) to 12–17°C (cold acclimation).
- **Salmonids** maintained the narrowest avoidance range overall: 6–9°C, consistent with their stenothermal classification.

### Methodological Notes
- Selection trials used a horizontal thermal gradient trough (363 × 36 × 20 cm) heated by infrared lamps, with fish position monitored every 2 min for 40 min following a 20–60 min orientation period.
- Avoidance trials used a dual-subtrough apparatus (per [[Meldrim and Gift 1971]] design) creating four discrete 3°C temperature zones per side.
- Food was withheld 2.5–3 days pre-trial to standardize gut content (per [[Javaid and Anderson 1967]] on starvation effects on thermal preference).
- Trials conducted midmorning–afternoon to control for circadian effects.
- Results contrast with [[Garside and Tait 1958]], who found an *inverse* relationship between acclimation and preference in rainbow trout — attributed by the authors to differences in fish size, gradient orientation (horizontal vs. vertical), and acclimation protocol (gradual seasonal vs. rapid 12-week ramp).

### Broader Ecological Implication
The wide avoidance ranges of eurythermal species (≥10°C) suggest that thermal effluent discharge alone is insufficient to exclude these species from otherwise suitable habitat, whereas stenothermal salmonids are far more vulnerable to displacement or exclusion by anthropogenic thermal loading.
