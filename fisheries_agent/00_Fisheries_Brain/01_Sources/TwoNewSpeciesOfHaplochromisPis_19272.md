# Two New Species of Haplochromis (Pisces, Cichlidae) from Lake Victoria

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

P. H. Greenwood of the [[British Museum (Natural History)]] describes two new cichlid species from [[Lake Victoria]], both belonging to the [[Haplochromis]] species-flock. [[Haplochromis Phytophagus]] is an algal-feeding species that appears to browse directly on rooted higher plants, ingesting large volumes of phanerogam tissue along with epiphytic diatoms. [[Haplochromis Welcommei]] has a distinctive multiseriate dentition and gut contents containing cichlid caudal-fin scales, making it the first known scale-eater (lepidophage) of this trophic group in Lake Victoria. The paper also discusses the evolutionary origins of algal grazing and scale-eating within cichlid lineages.

## Background & Description

**Haplochromis phytophagus sp. nov.** Holotype: an adult male, 82+ mm standard length (SL), from Kisumu, Kenya (B.M. reg. no. 1965.8.6.6). The description is based on the holotype and 29 paratypes measuring 61.0–86.0 mm SL. Key morphometrics include:

- Body depth 36.7–42.3% SL (mean 39.9%); head length 31.0–35.3% SL (mean 33.4%).
- Lower jaw 33.7–40.8% of head length; eye diameter 26.9–32.0% of head length.
- Gill rakers mostly 8 or 9 on the lower limb of the first arch.
- Lateral line with 30–32 scales; dorsal fin with 24–26 rays; anal fin with 11–12 rays.
- Outer jaw teeth 34–54 (mean 42) per upper jaw, compared with 50–74 in [[Haplochromis Nuchisquamulatus]].
- Lower pharyngeal bone relatively stout, with coarse, weakly bicuspid teeth.

Males are brown above with a broad lachrymal stripe; females and juveniles are light grey-brown with up to eight faint vertical flank bars. Diagnosis separates it from [[Haplochromis Nuchisquamulatus]] by coarser pharyngeal and jaw dentition, and from [[Haplochromis Lividus]] by tooth shape and pharyngeal bone size.

**Haplochromis welcommei sp. nov.** Holotype: an adult female, 104.0 mm total length, collected near Damba Island, Lake Victoria (B.M. reg. no. 1965.8.6.1). Only five specimens are available. The species is named after Mr. Robin Welcomme of the [[East African Freshwater Fisheries Research Organization]]. Notable features:

- Teeth in broad crescentic bands extending almost to the posterior limits of the dentigerous surfaces; inner bands multiseriate.
- Outer teeth are movably implanted, recurved bicuspids with a clearly demarcated crown and neck.
- Expanded dentary and premaxillary dentigerous surfaces, unlike any other Lake Victoria Haplochromis.
- Elongate, slender body with a short gut (less than body length).
- Adult males show a red flush on flanks and cheeks; anal ocelli are bright yellow in life.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** *H. phytophagus* was collected at four localities within the shallow littoral zone, over sandy bottoms with dense stands of rooted plants, especially [[Potamogeton]]. *H. welcommei* specimens came from the shallow inshore "Trappa zone" over muddy bottoms near Jinja, and from about 45 feet of water over rocky bottom near Lumya Point, Damba Island.

**Diet.** Stomach and intestinal contents of 20 *H. phytophagus* specimens were dominated by finely divided phanerogam leaf tissue, with epiphytic diatoms (notably *Surirella*, *Gomphonema*, *Navicula* and *Cyclotella*), filamentous green algae and blue-green algae in smaller amounts. No animal remains were found. Greenwood interprets this as browsing directly on plants rather than grazing. Of five *H. welcommei* guts, four contained small cycloid scales resembling cichlid caudal-fin scales, along with diatoms ([[Melosira]]) and, in one fish, macerated plant tissue. This suggests [[Lepidophagy]] and a facultative feeding strategy.

**Breeding.** No breeding data are available for *H. phytophagus*. Females may mature at 67 mm SL; the smallest sexually active male was 75 mm SL. Breeding information for *H. welcommei* is also unavailable; of its three males, all were adult and sexually active.

**Distribution.** *H. phytophagus* is known only from Lake Victoria, though poorly preserved specimens from [[Lake Kioga]] may represent the same species or a close relative.

**Trophic comparisons.** Scale-eating is known in seven cichlids in [[Lake Tanganyika]] and [[Lake Nyasa]] (e.g., [[Plecodus]], [[Genyochromis mento]], [[Corematodus]]), but was previously unrecorded in Lake Victoria. Greenwood proposes two distinct scale-eating methods: a wide-mouthed biting action in *Plecodus*-like species and a filing/scraping action in *Corematodus* and *H. welcommei*.

## Key Findings

- **Two new species described:** *Haplochromis phytophagus* and *Haplochromis welcommei*, both from Lake Victoria.
- ***H. phytophagus* is a plant-browsing algal feeder**, with gut contents dominated by higher-plant tissue and epiphytic diatoms, and robust pharyngeal and oral dentition adapted to macerating plant material.
- ***H. welcommei* is the first recorded lepidophage in Lake Victoria**, with caudal-fin scales found in four of five stomachs/intestines. It is considered facultative, with diatoms and plant fragments also present.
- **Taxonomic status:** *H. welcommei* is so morphologically distinct that Greenwood argues for at least subgeneric, possibly generic, recognition, though he retains it within *Haplochromis* pending a wider review of the species-flock.
- **Evolutionary hypothesis:** Phytophagy and lepidophagy are proposed to have evolved from an algal-grazing ancestor. *H. phytophagus* is seen as an intermediate stage between plant-biting species like *H. nubilus* and specialized grazers like *H. nuchisquamulatus*.
- **Dental characters** (outer tooth number, pharyngeal bone shape, multiseriate banding) are the main diagnostic traits separating these species from close relatives.
