# Razorback Sucker Growth Rate Analysis from Wild and Repatriated Recaptures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document explains how growth rate tables and graphs were built for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS), using recapture records from wild and [[Repatriation|repatriated]] fish. It describes the data-filtering rules, the [[Annual Growth Rate Calculation]] method, and the regression results for 15 graphs. The main finding is that repatriated fish grouped as a whole show a nearly flat growth trend, while year-class subsets differ. A group of nine small fish captured in 1994 showed little growth, so the 1994 repatriated data were excluded from most graphs. The author notes that measurement errors were kept in the data set to preserve interpretive integrity.

## Background & Description

The analysis relies on [[Mark-Recapture Data]] from two groups:

- **Wild fish**, separated into males and females.
- **Repatriated fish**, released back into the system and recaptured later, covering release years 1993 to 1997.

Each record was arranged into a table with the following fields:

- Tag number
- Sex
- Date of first contact (initial capture or repatriation date)
- Date of last contact
- Days between first and last contact
- Conversion factor (365 divided by days between contacts)
- Total length (TL) at first contact
- TL at last contact
- Actual difference in TL
- Annual increase

Annual increase equals the conversion factor multiplied by the actual difference in TL. All length values are in millimeters (mm).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Growth was assessed as a function of body size. Each graph plots initial TL (X-axis) against annual increase (Y-axis), with a linear trendline and its regression equation and R-squared value. Because the analysis compares size-dependent growth, the results inform understanding of [[Somatic Growth]] in the species, especially for fish of different sizes and capture histories.

The author notes that repatriated fish grow more rapidly than large, old fish. This is relevant to [[Population Recovery]] efforts, since released individuals may contribute differently to growth than established wild adults.

## Key Findings

**Data handling and exclusions**

- Records without total length data were omitted.
- Records showing negative growth values were omitted.
- Errant TL measurements that were visibly inaccurate were retained, to maintain the integrity of the interpretations. Their effects could not be fully evaluated in repatriated fish.

**Graph set (15 graphs total)**

- **All repatriated fish (1993 to 1997, grouped):** shallow declining slope (annual increase declines as TL at capture increases); R² = 0.0013.
- **Repatriated fish by year of last contact (1993, 1994, 1995, 1996, 1997):**
  - All years except 1994 showed steeper declining slopes. R² values ranged up to 0.7148 (the lower bound of the range is illegible in the scan).
  - The 1994 group showed an inclining slope (annual increase rises with TL at capture) with R² = 0.1469.
- **Wild males and females:** shallow slopes and low R² values. The wild male graphs showed a slightly inclining trendline, which the author links to errant length values.

**Anomalous 1994 group**

- Nine razorbacks captured under 200 mm TL showed little growth. Their average annual growth was lower than that of the larger 1994 repatriated fish.
- These nine fish reversed the 1994 trendline and lowered the trendline for all repatriated fish.
- The cause of their low growth is unknown.
- In response, the 1994 fish were excluded from all repatriated graphs except "All Repatriated Growth Rates" and "Repatriated RBS Growth Rate 94."

**Interpretive conclusion**

- Apart from the 1994 anomaly and measurement errors, the graphs show "somewhat predictable" growth trends.
