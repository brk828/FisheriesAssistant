## Executive Summary
This report documents recapture data for juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]]s (*Xyrauchen texanus*) reared in isolated backwater rearing ponds ([[Yuma Cove]] and [[Davis Cove]]) on [[Lake Mohave]] as part of a conservation stocking and monitoring program. In 1992, 297 juveniles were harvested from Yuma Cove; 167 were transferred to Davis Cove and 130 were PIT-tagged and released directly into Lake Mohave. An additional 23 fish were recaptured from Davis Cove, PIT-tagged, and released in early 1993, bringing the total 1992 year-class at large in the lake to 153 individuals. During the "Razorback Sucker Roundup" (March 13–19, 1993), five of these PIT-tagged fish were recaptured, providing direct data on growth, movement, and habitat association over periods of 62–116 days at large. Findings indicate substantial lake-wide dispersal (up to ~21 miles), measurable growth in length and mass, and strong association of recaptured fish with known or suspected [[Razorback Sucker Spawning Sites]].

- Growth ranged from minimal (1 mm TL / 22 g in the shortest interval) to more substantial gains (15 mm TL / 62 g) over ~112 days at large.
- Secondary sexual characteristics (tuberculation, "ripeness") were observed in at least three of five recaptured males, indicating reproductive readiness coincident with the spawning season.
- One individual (7F7D182A12) exhibited external parasitism (anchor worm, *Lernaea* sp.) and a prior fin-clip mark (RP1), suggesting a history of prior handling/marking beyond the PIT tag.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Rearing habitat**: [[Yuma Cove]] and [[Davis Cove]] function as isolated, predator-free backwater ponds used for grow-out of juvenile razorback suckers prior to release into the open lake environment of [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Release habitat**: Fish were released at river mile 24 (open lake) and river mile ~1 (Davis Cove), spanning a range of lentic and shoreline habitats.
- **Recapture habitat**: Recaptured fish were found in diverse settings, including:
  - Shallow (<3 ft), soft-substrate backwater habitat (solitary fish, tag 7F7D174A23).
  - Areas of known or suspected [[Razorback Sucker Spawning Sites]], in direct association with other adult razorback suckers (four of five recaptured fish).
- **Movement patterns**: Recaptured individuals showed significant dispersal from release points, both downstream and upstream:
  - 5 miles down-lake (7F7D1A4A5F)
  - 15 miles up-lake (7F7D303C05)
  - 18 miles up-lake (7F7D18215D)
  - 21 miles up-lake (7F7D182A12)
- **Capture gear**: Recaptures were made using electroshocker, trammel net, and gill net, reflecting multi-method monitoring across agencies ([[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona State University]]).

## Key Findings
- Of 153 PIT-tagged 1992 year-class razorback suckers at large in Lake Mohave, 5 (~3.3%) were recaptured during the week-long March 1993 "Razorback Sucker Roundup," demonstrating measurable post-release survival and detectability using PIT-tag methodology.
- Recaptured fish demonstrated wide-ranging lake dispersal (up to 21 miles from release site) within approximately 2–4 months, indicating high post-stocking mobility of juvenile/sub-adult razorback suckers.
- Growth data show fish gained modest length (1–15 mm TL) and mass (22–62 g) over the observation period, consistent with juvenile growth rates in the wild.
- Reproductive maturation was evident in multiple recaptured males (ripe, tuberculate condition), suggesting that hatchery-reared/backwater-reared juveniles can reach sexual maturity and participate in wild spawning activity within roughly one year of stocking.
- The strong spatial association of most recaptured fish (4 of 5) with other adult razorback suckers at known/suspected spawning sites supports the hypothesis that stocked juveniles integrate into wild breeding populations.
- The presence of an old fin clip (RP1) and anchor worm infestation on one fish indicates cumulative handling history and exposure to parasites in the lake environment.
- These findings support the viability of the Yuma Cove/Davis Cove backwater rearing and PIT-tagging strategy as an effective augmentation approach for endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] populations in [[Lake Mohave]].
