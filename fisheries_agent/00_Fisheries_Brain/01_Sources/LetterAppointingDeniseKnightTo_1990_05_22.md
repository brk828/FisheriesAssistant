# Letter Appointing Denise Knight to Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae #conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6, Denver) formally appointing Ms. Denise Knight (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources) to the **[[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]]**, replacing outgoing member Mr. Randy Radant. The letter, signed by Regional Director Galen L. Buterbaugh, contextualizes the Team's mandate under the **[[Endangered Species Act of 1973]]** and confirms the recent completion of a revised recovery plan review for three federally listed fish species native to the Colorado River Basin. While not a primary data source, it provides critical institutional and regulatory metadata regarding the conservation management structure for these species.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or physiological data are presented in this source document. The text is purely administrative/regulatory in nature. For species-specific morphological data, refer to primary literature on:
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*)
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*)
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, historically referred to as "Colorado Squawfish")

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat System:** The three fishes are endemic to the **[[Colorado River Basin]]**, a large freshwater riverine system in the southwestern United States.
- **Conservation Status:** All three species are classified as endangered under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**, indicating significant historical population decline, likely attributable to habitat alteration (damming, flow regulation), non-native species introduction, and water diversion — common threats to native Colorado River ichthyofauna (though not explicitly detailed in this letter).
- **Management Jurisdiction:** Recovery efforts are coordinated across state and federal agencies, including the **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]** and the **[[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]**, reflecting the multi-jurisdictional range of these species across Colorado River tributary states.

## Key Findings
- The **[[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]]** was originally formed in **December 1975** to advise the Service on the development and implementation of recovery plans for three listed species: the **[[Humpback Chub]]**, **[[Bonytail Chub]]**, and **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]**.
- As of the date of this letter, the Team had just completed its **final review of revised recovery plans** for these three endangered fishes, marking a significant programmatic milestone in the recovery planning process.
- The Team's formation and appointments (such as Ms. Knight's) exemplify the collaborative, multi-agency governance structure required for managing wide-ranging, complex endangered species recovery programs, consistent with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery planning guidelines.
- Team membership is drawn from state wildlife agencies (e.g., **[[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]**) in addition to federal Service personnel, underscoring the interstate cooperative management model necessary for a shared river basin resource.
- This correspondence serves as a historical administrative record documenting personnel continuity within the recovery team structure, important for tracking the institutional history of Colorado River native fish conservation efforts.
