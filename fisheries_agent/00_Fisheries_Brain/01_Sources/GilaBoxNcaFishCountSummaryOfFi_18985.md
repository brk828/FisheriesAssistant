# Gila Box Fish Count Field Data

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a field data record for a fish count on the [[Gila River]] in the [[Gila Box]] reach of Arizona, conducted by Ben Robles and Dave Oakey. The main dataset is a numbered list of individual observations, mostly of [[Red Shiner]], with smaller numbers of [[Longfin Dace]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Crayfish]]. Each entry records bottom substrate, current speed, water depth, a length estimate and bank or cover remarks. The document also includes a set of handwritten pages that are largely illegible after OCR, and a [[U.S. Geological Survey]] topographic map sheet for the Lone Star Mountain quadrangle, Arizona.

## Background & Description

- **Survey title:** "Gila Box Fish Count," dated in the header as 30/41 (OCR reading; exact date uncertain).
- **Field crew:** Ben Robles and Dave Oakey.
- **Sampling layout:** Records are grouped by location relative to a marked point ("above" and "below" the collection site), and a later section is labelled as upstream from [[Bonita Creek]].
- **Data fields per record:** Entry number, species, bottom substrate, current speed, depth (feet), length (metres), and remarks.
- **Handwritten pages (pages 1, 5 and 6):** Mostly illegible after OCR. They appear to contain notes and sign-offs but no interpretable data.
- **Map pages (pages 7 to 14):** A USGS provisional topographic map, Lone Star Mountain, Arizona, 7.5-minute quadrangle, provisional edition 1985, reprinted by USGS in 1996. It uses the 1000-metre [[Universal Transverse Mercator]] grid, Zone 12, and the National Geodetic Vertical Datum of 1929. Map legend and grid notes are present, but the map contains no survey data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Substrate:** Sandy bottoms predominate in the fish records, with gravel and rocky/gravel substrates also present. Some entries note a "muddy sand" bottom.
- **Current:** Recorded as moderate, moderate-fast, fast, slow, or swift. Most records are moderate or moderate-fast.
- **Depth:** Mostly between about 1 and 4 feet, with several deeper records of roughly 10 to 18 feet (the measured unit in these records is as recorded in the source).
- **Cover and banks:** Frequent remarks cite grassy banks, overhanging brush, brushy banks, rocky banks, bedrock and boulders. Cover near the bank is a repeated feature of the sampling sites.
- **Other notes:** A "pool" site, a "cross current" site, and a "channel catfish" site are noted. One record mentions a flathead catfish in a canyon-wall bank setting.

## Key Findings

- **Dominant species:** [[Red Shiner]] makes up most of the numbered entries across all three record pages. This is consistent with a small-bodied cyprinid dominating this riverine reach.
- **Secondary species:** [[Longfin Dace]] appears in a few records, including one sandy, slow-to-moderate site. [[Flathead Catfish]] appears in several records, including one noted at about 27 inches in length. [[Channel Catfish]] is noted once, with a length of about 3 feet, from a channel site.
- **Invertebrates:** [[Crayfish]] appear in several records, often on grassy banks or in sandy or gravel habitat, and are recorded alongside red shiners at the same sites.
- **Habitat associations:** Records cluster around banks with cover (grass, brush, boulders) and sandy or gravelly bottoms with moderate current. These are consistent with typical habitat preferences for these taxa, though the document itself does not analyse this.
- **Record count:** The numbered entries run from 1 to about 50 across pages 2 to 4. Exact tallies per species are not reliable because several counts are unclear after OCR.
- **Data quality:** Several values (dates, counts, and some depths and lengths) contain OCR ambiguity. No summary statistics, sample sizes, or analysis are included in the document.
- **Map context:** The USGS sheet provides the topographic base for the survey area and is useful for locating sampling sites, but it contains no ecological data.
