# Instream Flow Requirements of Native Fishes of Aravaipa Creek, Arizona (Incremental Methodology)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1983 report by Paul R. Turner and Robert J. Tafanelli (New Mexico State University), submitted to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], evaluates the instream flow needs of native fishes in [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona, using the [[Incremental Methodology]] and its [[IFG4 Hydraulic Simulation]]. The study focused on two endemic riffle-dwelling cyprinids, [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), both under consideration for "threatened" status. Based on weighted usable area (WUA), a discharge of about 20 ft³/s provided the most habitat for adults of both species, and a non-consumptive minimum flow of 13 ft³/s at the BLM East Aravaipa gaging station was recommended. The report also makes general methodological recommendations for sampling fishes in desert streams.

## Background & Description

The study was funded from unspent [[Arizona Phase II Instream Flow Study]] money ($13,125.00 of $35,000.00 originally allocated to the Arizona Game and Fish Department) after that study was terminated. Sites were chosen in 1980 after floods in the 1979 water year destroyed earlier survey work at the original AGFD sites (Aravaipa Creek, Black River, West Clear Creek). Aravaipa Creek was retained because it still supported an essentially unmodified native [[Gila River Ichthyofauna]].

The native fish assemblage of Aravaipa Creek comprises five cyprinids and two catostomids:
- Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*)
- Loach minnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- Roundtail chub (*Gila robusta grahami*)
- Longfin dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
- Speckled dace (*Rhinichthys osculus*)
- Sonora sucker (*Catostomus insignis*)
- Desert sucker (*Catostomus clarki*)

Spikedace and loach minnow were the focus because their status was most seriously impacted by reservoir construction and desiccation. Aravaipa Creek was also under consideration as [[Critical Habitat]] for both species, and the BLM-managed portion was expected to become the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site.** A 358-foot reach about 3/4 mile upstream from Turkey Creek (T.6S., R.19E., NE¼, Sec. 19), on Defenders of Wildlife property in the George Whittell Wildlife Preserve. The reach had six transects; pool habitat at Transect 1 reached nearly 2 ft deep, most depths rarely exceeded 1.1 ft, and substrate was dominated by sand and gravel with some cobble. Channel width ranged from 16 to 26 ft. Riparian vegetation included cottonwood, willow, sycamore and velvet ash.

**Flow regime.** Minckley (1981) found Aravaipa flows were usually below 20 ft³/s (90% of the time), with a base flow near 5 ft³/s and floods over 100 ft³/s occurring less than 3% of the time. The largest flood since 1931 peaked at 16,200 ft³/s on December 18, 1978. In 1981, mean daily flow at East Aravaipa averaged 18.7 ft³/s (range 13.7–30.9), versus a provisional mean of 20.4 ft³/s at the USGS gage near Mammoth. In 1982, the East Aravaipa mean was 14.7 ft³/s (range 7.3–34.3).

**Substrate and streambed stability.** Sand and gravel substrates moved readily during even short increases in flow, which complicated the no-streambed-movement assumption of IFG4. Two of six transects were dropped from the simulation for this reason.

**Other taxa.** Water cress (*Rorippa nasturtium-aquaticum*) occurred at shallow margins in summer 1981 but was absent in May 1980 after floods. Algal beds (e.g., [[Cladophora]] and [[Pithophora]]) are associated with loach minnow habitat.

## Key Findings

**Methodology and hydraulics**
- Field measurements were taken at discharges of 11.1, 16.5 and 24.4 ft³/s, plus 14.7 ft³/s on May 15, 1980. The 1980 set was dropped because of streambed movement.
- The IFG4 simulation covered 5 to 40 ft³/s. CIFSG guidelines would have allowed 4.4 to 61.0 ft³/s.
- The authors judged that a single set of measurements at 12–15 ft³/s with a Water Surface Profile (WSP) simulation would have sufficed.

**Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*)**
- Probability-of-use data came from 54 adults (Gila River) and 140 adults (Aravaipa Creek), plus 7 juveniles.
- Frequency analysis showed a plateau-like velocity preference of about 1.3–2.7 ft/s, with very few captures below 0.6 or above 3.0 ft/s.
- Depth preference was 0.60–0.75 ft in the computer-drawn marginal, with a stronger frequency-based peak at 0.5–0.6 ft.
- Adults preferred sand and gravel substrates.

**Loach minnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*)**
- Probability-of-use data came from 141 adults (Aravaipa Creek), 112 adults (Gila River), and 14 juveniles (Aravaipa Creek, August 27, 1981).
- Velocity preference was sharply peaked. Gila River fish preferred slower water (about 1.0 at 0.1–1.2 ft/s), while Aravaipa fish preferred 1.5–2.4 ft/s. The combined data set was used for the final weighting factors.
- Depth preference peaked at 0.50–0.55 ft. No adults were captured deeper than 1.0 ft in 1981, although eggs were found at depths up to 29.9 inches (Britt 1982).
- Adults strongly favored cobble and gravel. Eggs were attached to the undersides of flattened cobble (average 5.3 × 7.1 in), so embeddedness by fines is a concern.

**Weighted usable area and recommendations**
- Peak WUA for adult spikedace occurred at 20 ft³/s. Run 2 (frequency-based weights) gave a 30–70% reduction relative to Run 1, and WUA for spikedace fell from 4400 to 900 ft² per 1000 ft of stream between 10 and 5 ft³/s.
- Peak WUA for loach minnow occurred at 15–20 ft³/s. Loach minnow WUA decreased 45% between 10 and 5 ft³/s.
- Suckers and roundtail chub showed little sensitivity to discharge (WUA varied less than 10% for suckers between 7 and 25 ft³/s), so these results were treated as comparative only.
- Recommended management: an optimum of about 23 ft³/s during late spring through early fall; a minimum of 13 ft³/s at the East Aravaipa gage (3 ft³/s added to the study-site values for monitoring); no further upstream withdrawals; and acquisition of a non-consumptive water right. Purchase of irrigation water rights was also suggested.

**Sampling bias and general recommendations**
- Electrofishing was rated for unbiased habitat data: loach minnow 9.5, spikedace 8.0, Sonora and desert suckers 3.5–4.0, roundtail chub 1.0 (scale 1–10).
- The probability-of-use curves reflect environmental bias, since depth and velocity covary in the sampled reaches. The curves should not be transferred to streams that differ substantially from the sampled ones.
- Researchers should consult [[Cooperative Instream Flow Service Group]] (CIFSG) early in studies, measure hydraulics in the 2–4 week window of declining flow, and consider block-seine depletion sampling and grid-based observation for mobile species.
