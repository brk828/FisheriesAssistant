# A Gazetteer of the Chihuahuan Desert Region: A Supplement to the Chihuahuan Desert Flora

#gazetteer #biogeography #chihuahuan-desert

## Executive Summary

This 1976 publication by [[James Henrickson]] and [[Richard M. Straw]] (California State University, Los Angeles) is a geographic gazetteer, not a fish or aquatic-fauna study. It was prepared at the suggestion of [[Marshall C. Johnston]] as a companion to a forthcoming flora of the region. It catalogues place names, coordinates, elevations and locality types across the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] Region and surrounding non-desert margins. The document contains no species data. Its relevance to aquatic ecology is indirect: it locates rivers, streams, springs, lakes, reservoirs and wells, which may serve as a reference for aquatic habitat survey work in the region.

## Background & Description

**Scope.** The Chihuahuan Desert Region occupies a plateau between the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] and the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]] in north-central Mexico, extending into Arizona and into the [[Rio Grande]] and [[Pecos River]] basins of New Mexico and Texas, to about 34°20' N. In Mexico it reaches about 22°15' N, giving a north-south span of some 2000 km (1300 miles).

**Area.** As interpreted by Johnston, the region covers approximately 507,000 km² (196,000 sq mi), about one and one-fourth times the size of California. Excluding marginal diluted areas, the true desert covers approximately 378,000 km², slightly smaller than California. The gazetteer extends beyond the desert margin, so not every entry is a desert locality.

**Data fields.** Each entry includes:
- Place name
- State (12 states, using standard abbreviations such as CHI, COA, DUR, NL, SLP, SON, TAM, ZAC, ARI, NM, TEX)
- Type of locality (17 codes)
- Latitude and longitude
- Elevation in meters
- Source code
- Arbitrary serial number

**Locality types of note for aquatic work.** These include RIVR (river, permanent and intermittent), STRM (stream, mostly dry or intermittent), LAKE (wet and dry lakes), RSVR (reservoir or man-made water-holding site), SPNG (spring, *ojo de agua*), WELL (*pozo*), PLN (plain) and PASS. Other codes cover canyons (CNYN), hills (HILL), mountains (MT), populations (POPL), ranches (RNCH), mines (MINE) and railroad places (RRP).

**Sources.** Twelve source series were used, including:
- Army Map Service (AMS) 1:250,000 Mexican sheets (1962)
- CETENAL 1:50,000 Mexican sheets (the "excellent series of modern maps" used south of 24° N)
- USGS 1:62,500 and 1:250,000 maps for Texas, New Mexico and Arizona
- Geologic and historical maps (including Abbott's 1905 Coahuila map and the 1927 Chihuahua map)
- Personal communications and published records of plant collectors

The gazetteer was compared with the *Gazetteer of Mexico* (1956), which relied on 1:500,000 maps and therefore gave only generalized data for the region.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses landscape and place-name geography rather than ecology. It notes:
- The region includes grasslands, mountains, pinyon and pine-fir forest, and desert pockets mixed with adjacent vegetation.
- Landscape change from roads, ranchos, *ejidos* and reservoirs is ongoing, so names become outdated.
- Many canyon and stream names known to local inhabitants are missing, and some collection localities on old herbarium labels may be lost.

Water-related features are recorded as names and coordinates only. The document provides no hydrological measurements, water chemistry, aquatic biota or habitat condition data.

## Key Findings

- **Dataset purpose:** A reference index of place names, coordinates and elevations for the Chihuahuan Desert Region. It is a supplement to a flora, not a biological inventory.
- **Scale and coverage:** The region covers about 507,000 km² (approx. 378,000 km² excluding marginal areas), spanning roughly 2000 km north to south.
- **Map basis:** Most entries derive from 1:250,000 maps. Selected regions use 1:50,000 (CETENAL) or 1:62,500 (USGS) maps. The Gazetteer of Mexico (1956, 1:500,000) was used as a less detailed comparison.
- **Naming conventions:** Place names are alphabetized by the first proper noun. Articles and generic terms (e.g., RANCHO, CERRO, ARROYO, LAGUNA, PRESA) move to the end. SAN and SANTA stay part of the name. Initials follow full names. Diacritics and punctuation are omitted.
- **Known accuracy limits:**
  - Coordinates were read from maps with a T-square and triangle; some were rounded to the nearest minute.
  - US elevations were converted from feet and rounded to the nearest 10 m.
  - Elevations for sloping features (canyons, streams) refer only to the named point.
  - Some names were transposed or omitted, and some appear twice (e.g., Ciudad Juarez and Juarez Ciudad).
- **Incompleteness:** The authors state that the gazetteer is incomplete and that a definitive version is not possible. Detailed CETENAL maps for the whole region could add roughly 100,000 place names (about 300 additional maps, around 1500 pages in the same format).
- **Future work:** A supplement or second edition was planned for 1980. Corrections and additional localities were requested, with sources cited.
- **Data access:** Straw's computer programs can extract subsets (e.g., all rivers and streams, all places above 2000 m, all Coahuila localities) and sort them alphabetically, by elevation or by latitude on request.
- **Editorial notes:** The printout includes a large alphabetical locality listing, cross-referenced to source codes and serial numbers, but the document itself contains no aquatic species, abundance or habitat data.
