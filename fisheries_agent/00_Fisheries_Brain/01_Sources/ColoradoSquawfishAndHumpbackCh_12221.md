# Colorado Squawfish and Humpback Chub Population and Habitat Monitoring Program (1979-1980)

#ichthyology #endangered-species #cyprinidae #catostomidae #population-ecology

## Executive Summary

This job progress report documents endangered fish monitoring in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] from July 1979 through December 1980. Sampling was conducted across trend zones in the [[Yampa River]], [[White River]], [[Colorado River]], and [[Gunnison River]] using electrofishing, seining, dip-netting, and gill netting. The 1979 effort yielded 49,371 fish from 486 collections; 1980 produced 63,061 fish from 485 collections. A total of 391 endangered fishes were captured over the two-year period (168 in 1979, 223 in 1980), including [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The study documented reproductive success, habitat preferences, and movement patterns through mark-recapture tagging programs, providing critical baseline data for recovery planning of these federally listed endangered species.

## Background & Description

Both [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]] were listed as endangered by Colorado and federal authorities. These species have become functionally extinct throughout much of their former range and are now restricted largely to sections of the [[Upper Colorado River]] drainage. Habitat alterations from impoundments, diversions, reservoir evaporation, and water losses have promoted major changes in river morphometry, flows, and water quality, disrupting natural seasonal cycles. Secondary impacts include establishment of exotic species that may compete with native fishes.

This report continues work initiated in 1977 (SE-3-1, Job 1) and 1978 (SE-3-2, Job 2), sampling the same trend zones with consistent methodology. New areas sampled in 1980 included [[Yampa Canyon]] in [[Dinosaur National Monument]] and [[Cross Mountain Canyon]].

**Collection Methods:**
- **Electrofishing:** Primary method for adults using a 4.8-m Polarcraft flatboat equipped with Coffelt Electronics VVP-15 variable voltage pulsator. DC pulsed current proved most effective in low conductivity (<1000 µmhos/cm). Deep pool technique involved 5-6 m negative electrodes drawing fish from considerable depth.
- **Gill and trammel nets:** Used sparingly in deep pools, high conductivity waters, and inaccessible canyons.
- **Hook and line:** Unexpectedly effective; grasshoppers caught humpback chubs in pools; brass spoons caught squawfish in deep areas.
- **Seine and dipnet:** Primary methods for young-of-year (YOY), juveniles, and small adults using 1/4", 1/8", or 1/16" mesh.

**Habitat Classification (revised 1980):**
*Primary types:* Main channel, chute channel, side channel, tributary stream, off-stream impoundment, ditch.
*Secondary types:* Backwater, isolated backwater, pool, shoreline, riffle, run, eddy, embayment, rapid, puddle.

**Tagging Program:**
Small (6 x 16 mm) plastic Carlin tags color-coded by river (red=Colorado, green=Green, white=White, blue=Gunnison, yellow=Yampa) were attached to endangered fishes beginning in 1979.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**River Systems:**
- **[[White River]]:** Native fishes maintained greatest dominance. [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) most abundant nonnative. [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) not collected above RM 142 in 1978-1980 but appeared below RM 142 in 1980. High velocities, cool temperatures, and few backwaters kept introduced species at low densities.

- **[[Yampa River]]:** Native fishes well represented but several introduced species established: [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*), [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Redside Shiner]] (*Richardsonius balteatus*), [[Sand Shiner]] (*Notropis stramineus*), [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*). Red shiners observed in [[Little Snake River]]-[[Lily Park]] area 1979-1980 but not above [[Cross Mountain Canyon]]. First YOY [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) collected in lower 10 miles in 1980, coinciding with YOY squawfish captures. Several [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) collected.

- **[[Colorado River]]:** Introduced fishes had greatest dominance. Red shiners and carp abundant. [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) present as potential predators on young fishes.

**Habitat Maintenance:**
High spring flushing flows create and maintain critical habitat features: deep pools/runs, boulders for cover, unsilted sand/gravel bars. Low water years (e.g., 1977) caused habitat deterioration through siltation, filling deep holes and covering boulders. High water cycles restore habitats by flushing silted areas. Native fishes appear adapted to natural flow cycles; exotic species thrive under varied flow conditions.

**Preferred Endangered Fish Habitat Characteristics:**
1. Near high gradient river reach
2. Deep pool or run nearby (3-20 m depth)
3. Cover: large boulders in water, overhanging rock or vegetation
4. Habitat diversity: sand/gravel bars, eddies around islands and boulders

**[[Black Rocks]] Area:** Unique habitat with distinctively high percentage of endangered fishes, especially humpback chubs. River flows through ~1.5 km of hard Precambrian metamorphic gneiss rock with holes approaching 20 m depth. Habitat includes deep holes (8-20 m), eddies and upwells around boulders, steep dropoff cliff faces, sand/gravel bars among boulders.

**Endangered Fish Collections:**

*1979:* Total 168 endangered fishes
- 97 humpback chubs (Loma-Utaline trend zone)
- 8 YOY, 1 juvenile, 37 adult squawfish (Colorado River)
- 1 razorback sucker (Walker Wildlife Area, May)
- 7 razorback suckers (flooded backwater near Clifton bridge, May)
- 14 squawfish, 1 razorback sucker (Yampa River)

*1980:* Total 223 endangered fishes
- 20 adult humpback chubs (Loma-Utaline, all from Black Rocks)
- 77 YOY, 8 adult squawfish (Loma-Utaline)
- 1 razorback sucker (Colorado River proper, first collected, March)
- 1 squawfish (near Walker Wildlife Area, March)
- 1 squawfish (Palisade-Clifton near Labor Camp)
- 8 razorback suckers (gravel pits near Clifton bridge, June; sexual products obtained for rearing series)
- 1 squawfish (gravel pits)
- 46 YOY, 33 adult squawfish (Yampa River)
- 12 YOY humpback chubs, tentative ID (Dinosaur National Monument)
- 1 adult humpback chub (upper Cross Mountain Canyon)
- 1 razorback sucker (Lily Park area, second consecutive year)
- 1 adult squawfish (White River, 21 RM above Rangely)

**Tagging Results:**

*1979:*
- Colorado River: 66 humpback chubs, 34 squawfish tagged (26 squawfish and 63 chubs from Black Rocks)
- Yampa River: 14 squawfish, 1 razorback sucker tagged

*1980:*
- Colorado River: 18 humpback chubs, 9 squawfish, 1 razorback sucker tagged
- Yampa River: 31 squawfish, 1 razorback sucker tagged
- White River: 1 squawfish tagged

**Recapture Information:**

*1979 Recaptures:*
- 5 humpback chubs, 4 squawfish recaptured on Colorado River (Black Rocks)
- Most recaptures occurred near original capture sites within weeks to months
- Humpback chub #515: electrofished morning, recaptured afternoon same day on hook/line
- Squawfish #528: recaptured twice (hook/line Oct 14, electrofishing Nov 3)
- Yampa: Squawfish #79911 electrofished Oct 10, recaptured hook/line Oct 11

*1980 Recaptures:*
- Squawfish #516: recaptured March 25 at RM 143.4, same location as Oct 13, 1979 capture; no growth (595 mm both times)
- Squawfish #0521: recaptured by USFWS May 28 at Walker Wildlife Area (RM 163.6); originally captured Oct 13, 1979 at RM 143.7 (20 RM upstream movement)
- Razorback sucker #680: recaptured June 8 in gravel pit, same area as May 28 capture
- Humpback chub #0719: recaptured March 27 in same Black Rocks side channel pool as day before
- Humpback chub #586: recaptured Nov 1 in Black Rocks, same area as Aug 25, 1979 capture
- **Most significant:** Squawfish Green River tag #110 recaptured Oct 10, 1980 by hook/line in lower Cross Mountain Canyon, Yampa River (RM 55.3 from Green-Yampa confluence). Originally captured Aug 6, 1979 near Jensen, Utah on Green River (298.4 RM from Colorado-Green confluence). Moved ~80 RM upstream over 14 months; grew 21 mm (443→464 mm). **Demonstrates no physical barriers impede squawfish movement between Green and Yampa Rivers.**
- Squawfish #1055: captured by hook/line 1610 hrs, recaptured electrofishing 2145 hrs 0.1 mile downstream same day

**Movement Patterns:**
- Squawfish tend to remain in same general area late August through early spring
- Lack of recaptures during May-June runoff months suggests movement/migration during late spring-early summer
- Humpback chubs remain in same general area but captures above/below Black Rocks in spring/high water suggest some movement
- Habitat preference changes based on season/temperature: easily electrofished in shallow water spring-early fall; move to deep water in fall when temperatures 3-6°C (confirmed by USFWS trammel net captures in deep holes in November)

**Young-of-Year Collections:**

*1979:*
- 8 YOY, 1 yearling squawfish (Colorado River, August)
- 4 YOY squawfish 15.1-19.6 mm TL (Station E, RM 139, Aug 24) plus 79 mm yearling
- 4 YOY squawfish 19-24.7 mm TL (below Black Rocks, RM 135.3, Aug 26)
- 2 suspected yearling humpback chubs (Black Rocks, Aug 25)
- 4 YOY humpback chubs, tentative ID (Black Rocks)
- No YOY endangered fishes from other trend zones

*1980:*
- **67 YOY squawfish** (Loma-Utaline, Aug 13-16): 12-21 mm TL from 9 sites between RM 152.7 (below Loma) to RM 131.9 (Colorado-Utah line). Majority (46 specimens) from shoreline vegetation in chute channel at RM 151.6
- 10 YOY squawfish 30-38 mm (Colorado River, Oct 31-Nov 2): 5 at RM 148.8, 5 at RM 135.3
- 65 suspected humpback chubs, tentative ID, 27-43 mm TL (Colorado River, Aug 15-16): RM 135.7 (Upper Black Rocks) to RM 131.9
- **46 YOY squawfish 14-29 mm** (Yampa River, Aug 23-25): lower 9.5 miles before Yampa-Green confluence, 12 separate samples, variety of habitats all <1 m depth, velocities <0.5 ft/sec
- 12 suspected YOY humpback chubs, tentative ID (Yampa, August): RM 30.3 to Yampa-Green confluence
- 1 YOY razorback-flannelmouth hybrid, tentative ID (Echo Park, Yampa, August)
- **No razorback sucker YOY collected from wild in any area**

**Reproductive Success:**
- Colorado River: YOY squawfish collected three successive years (1978-1980) on Loma-Utaline trend zone; reproductive success peaked in 1980
- Distribution and size of 1980 larvae indicate widespread spawning over entire area, as far upstream as Loma (vs. restricted areas near Black Rocks in 1979)
- Good 1980 reproduction followed exceptional year (1979) of adult squawfish captures (30 fish, mostly 525-600 mm category in Black Rocks)
- Suggests reproduction may occur on cyclic/pulsating schedule influenced by river conditions, year class strength, or combination
- Yampa River: 46 YOY squawfish in lower Yampa **verifies squawfish reproduction in Yampa River** (first year sampling lower portions through Dinosaur National Monument)
- Single confirmed humpback chub at Cross Mountain Canyon plus suspected immatures from Dinosaur National Monument suggest recent reproduction in Yampa River, at least limited basis
- Exceptionally large number of adult squawfish collected upper Yampa 1980 suggests increased chances for successful 1981 reproduction if water conditions optimal

**Razorback Sucker Observations:**
- Early year sampling (March-April 1979, 1980) on Colorado River collected several humpback-flannelmouth hybrids and one pure razorback between Loma and Ruby Canyon (shoreline runs among flannelmouth suckers)
- No razorbacks or hybrids collected summer/fall in same area
- May-June 1979, 1980 collections in shallow gravel pits near old Clifton bridge yielded several razorbacks; none in August
- **Pattern suggests razorbacks move in early spring seeking spawning areas, congregate in gravel pits via irrigation ditches and drains**
- Reason for congregation unknown; fish in various states of sexual maturity observed
- Unknown if spawning occurs in gravel pits
- Razorbacks collected year-round in Walker Wildlife Area gravel pit, thought to undergo spring spawning activities

**Taxonomic Challenges:**
- Considerable hybridization between catostomid species in Yampa River makes positive ID of larval/early juvenile suckers difficult
- Larval razorback ID aided by series reared at CSU in 1980 from stripped sexual products (collected near Clifton, June 8, 1980)
- **No larval or juvenile razorback suckers collected from wild during entire study**
- Larval/juvenile humpback chub ID extremely tentative; cannot readily distinguish from [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)
- Preliminary principal component analysis using wild *Gila* specimens and hatchery-reared specimens suggests morphometric-meristic variations exist but findings tentative, incomplete
- Field-collected suspected humpback chubs tentatively classified pending additional taxonomic work
- Several Yampa River fish tentatively designated *Richardsonius balteatus* x *Rhinichthys osculus* await confirmation
- [[Plains Killifish]] (*Fundulus kansae*) and [[Rio Grande Killifish]] (*Fundulus zebrinus*) now classified as synonyms; *F. zebrinus* accepted name

**Conclusions:**
1. Reproducing populations of Colorado squawfish exist in mainstem Colorado River (Loma to Utah border) and Yampa River (adults to Juniper Springs Canyon, immatures in Yampa Canyon)
2. No real physical barriers impede squawfish movement throughout undammed Green and Yampa basins
3. Substantially lower adult numbers in White and lower Gunnison Rivers; no YOY collected in these rivers in Colorado (less extensive efforts, more difficult sampling conditions)
4. Humpback chubs reach greatest density in Black Rocks area, Ruby Canyon, Colorado River; single chub in Cross Mountain Canyon suggests possible small Yampa population
5. Immature humpback chub taxonomy incompletely understood; cannot make definitive recruitment conclusions. However, large numbers of *Gila* spp. larvae/juveniles at Black Rocks and Yampa Canyon, plus statistical separation into two morphometrically dissimilar groups, tentatively confirm humpback reproduction in these stretches
6. No humpback chub captures in Gunnison or White Rivers
