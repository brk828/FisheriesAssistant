## Executive Summary

This document is a 1987 transmittal letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Colorado River Endangered Species program, accompanying a technical report prepared by BioWest, Inc. The report presents **Habitat Suitability Index (HSI) curves** for three rare and endangered fish species native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. These curves were developed from habitat utilization data combined with expert consensus from multiple federal and state agencies and private organizations, intended for use with **PHABSIM** (Physical Habitat Simulation) hydraulic modeling. Notably, the FWS explicitly states it has **not formally endorsed** these curves, flagging them as provisional pending further validation.

## Background & Description

No morphological data (e.g., body measurements, fin ray counts, coloration) are provided in this source document. The text is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not describe physical characteristics of the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], or [[Razorback Sucker]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: All three species are endemic to the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], a river system in the western United States subject to significant hydrological alteration (damming, flow regulation).
- **Conservation status**: All three species are identified as "rare and endangered," consistent with their federal listing status under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.
- **Habitat modeling application**: The HSI curves are designed for integration with [[PHABSIM]] hydraulic simulation models, which are used to quantify how streamflow alterations affect available physical habitat (depth, velocity, substrate, cover) for target species.
- **Data provenance**: Habitat suitability was derived from field-based **habitat utilization data** combined with structured expert elicitation ("species experts") drawn from federal agencies, state agencies, and private groups — indicating a hybrid empirical/expert-judgment modeling approach typical of HSI curve development in the 1980s.

## Key Findings

- Three sets of **Habitat Suitability Index (HSI) curves** were developed for the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] by BioWest, Inc.
- The curves represent a **consensus product** of multiple stakeholder agencies rather than a single-source empirical dataset, reflecting the collaborative recovery-planning framework for these endangered species.
- As of the report date (1987), **none of the curves had been tested for compatibility** with actual PHABSIM hydraulic simulation sites in the upper Colorado River basin — a critical validation gap.
- The FWS anticipated that the curves **would require refinement** before being reliably used to generate accurate model outputs.
- The agency committed to issuing **follow-up reports** identifying specific sites/conditions of curve applicability, and to periodically revising curves as new research on [[Endangered Fish Habitat Requirements]] became available.
- Users were directed to consult the **Colorado River Endangered Species Recovery Coordinator** (John Hamill) for the most current curve versions and application guidance — underscoring the provisional, living-document nature of this habitat modeling effort.
- This document exemplifies the broader institutional process of adaptive management and iterative model refinement in endangered species habitat assessment within the [[Colorado River Endangered Species Recovery Program]].
