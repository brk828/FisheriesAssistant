# Caudal Vertebrae of Ophiotrus ceroni and Colorado Squawfish from the Salt River, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This short two-part note contains two separate contributions. The first is a morphological comparison of [[Caudal Vertebrae]] in *Ophiotrus ceroni*, a taxon described from material collected in [[Levy County, Florida]]. The second reports collections and meristic data for the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*[[Ptychocheilus Lucius]]*) from the [[Salt River]], Arizona. The vertebral comparison concludes that *O. ceroni* is a distinct form, but its closest relative among the compared species remains unclear. The squawfish note documents a 1958 collection of seven specimens and a failed 1960s resampling attempt.

## Background & Description

**Part 1: Caudal vertebrae.** The vertebrae were originally described by Etheridge. The authors compare them with *O. attenuatus*, *O. ventralis*, and *O. compressus*. The OCR text gives the genus name inconsistently, so its exact spelling should be verified against the original publication. The author is J. Alan Holman of the Department of Biological Sciences, Illinois State University, Normal, Illinois.

**Part 2: Squawfish collection.** The authors, Branley A. Branson (Eastern Kentucky State College, Richmond), Morgan E. Smith (Murray State College, Kentucky), and C. J. McCoy Jr. (Carnegie Museum, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania), collected seven specimens of *Ptychocheilus lucius* on 8 or 9 August 1958 from the Salt River below the U.S. 60 Highway bridge, Globe. The voucher material is catalogued as Oklahoma State University 5388.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Colorado squawfish is described as rare in the lower Colorado River drainage, which explains the interest in the 1958 Salt River material (Miller, 1961). Branson returned to the same locality in July (196-, as OCR-garbled) and used both netting and SCUBA gear, but failed to obtain any specimens. This suggests the species was no longer easily found at the site.

The vertebral comparison is ecological only in a taxonomic sense. It relies on skeletal features, not on habitat or environmental data.

## Key Findings

**Caudal vertebrae of *Ophiotrus ceroni***

- *O. ceroni* resembles *O. attenuatus* and *O. ventralis* and differs from *O. compressus* in having fracture planes and secondary neural spines on its caudal vertebrae.
- Its neural spines are less dorsally directed than those of one *O. attenuatus* specimen and two *O. ventralis* specimens.
- Its transverse processes are delicate and narrow at the base. In *O. attenuatus* and *O. ventralis* they are stouter and wider.
- Its haemal arches are shorter than those of *O. attenuatus* and *O. ventralis*.
- Its secondary neural spine is short, as in *O. attenuatus*, but longer in *O. ventralis*.
- **Conclusion:** *O. ceroni* is more similar to *O. attenuatus* and *O. ventralis* than to *O. compressus*. It is a distinct form, but the evidence does not show which of the two closely related species it is most closely related to.

**Colorado squawfish from the Salt River**

- Seven specimens were collected in August 1958 from below the U.S. 60 Highway bridge, Globe.
- Branson's July 196- resampling using netting and SCUBA produced no specimens.
- Standard lengths (SL) for the seven specimens range from 34.0 mm to 145.0 mm, based on the values legible in Table 1.
- Table 1 records lateral line, scale, fin ray, and proportional measurements. Much of the table is garbled in the OCR, so counts are not reproduced here and should be checked against the original.
