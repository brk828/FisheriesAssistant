# Two Years of Fishing in Lakes with Liberalized Regulations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This 1948 report by H. E. Predmore, Jr. of the [[Institute For Fisheries Research]] (Fish and Fisheries Division, [[Michigan Department of Conservation]]) evaluates two years of creel census data from 12 experimental lakes where fishing regulations were liberalized under authority granted by the [[Michigan Legislature]] in 1945. Across the test lakes, fishing quality showed no general decline, even though year-round fishing was permitted for certain species. Most of the added open season fell in spring, when other southern lakes were closed, and total angling increased. The author concludes that it is too early for firm conclusions, but that relaxing restrictions, especially on panfish, had no harmful effect on summer fishing and provided extra sport in a slow period of the year.

## Background & Description

The experimental program rests on three rationales given in the report:

1. **Low harvest rate:** Experimental data show that in most lakes only a small proportion of available legal game fish are harvested by [[Hook and Line Angling]].
2. **Stunted populations:** Many lakes hold large numbers of stunted, slow-growing undersized fish that seldom reach legal length. Too many small breeders leave too little food for normal growth.
3. **Unused surplus:** Unrestricted fishing trials in other states removed only about half of the catchable fish, leaving ample breeders.

In 1945 the Legislature allowed the [[Conservation Commission]] to designate up to 20 lakes and 10 streams as experimental waters, with no more than one lake and one stream per county.

**Creel census method:** Fishermen were enumerated on about 30% of all days. Between 10% and 90% of fishing trips were recorded using [[Stratified Random Sampling]]. Data were grouped into three seasons:
- **Spring:** March through June 24
- **Summer:** June 25 through September 15
- **Fall:** September 16 to about the first week of November

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Regulation changes:** On six lakes ([[Lobdell Lake]], [[Pontiac Lake]], [[Whitmore Lake]], [[Craig Lake]], [[Duck Lake]], [[Fine Lake]]), year-round fishing was allowed for perch, crappies, rock bass and non-game fish. Northern pike and walleye had a closed season of March 15 to May 15. Bass fishing was closed January 1 to June 24, and bluegill and sunfish were closed February 28 to June 24. These rules matched those on most non-trout lakes north of [[Highway M-46]] since 1946.

**Other lakes:** [[Bear Lake]] and [[Saddle Lake]] had no size or season limits for bluegills, sunfish, perch, rock bass and crappies, but creel limits applied to fish six inches or longer. [[Big Portage Lake]], [[Fife Lake]], [[Minnewaukon Lake]] and [[Sugarloaf Lake]] allowed year-round bluegill and sunfish harvest. Ordinary closed seasons applied to other species.

**Angler behavior:** The experimental status attracted anglers who would normally fish other waters. The spring extension also gave anglers fishing opportunities when other lakes were closed. [[Whitmore Lake]], a heavily fished site, was open year-round for species without closed seasons.

## Key Findings

**Overall pressure and catch (six lakes with full liberalization):**

| Period | 1946 Anglers | 1946 Fish | 1947 Anglers | 1947 Fish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spring | 9,200 | 14,630 | 8,950 | 11,263 |
| Summer | 69,400 | 224,500 | 100,000 | 403,900 |
| Fall | 7,160 | 18,900 | 13,850 | 39,000 |

- Combined over both years, 208,560 anglers caught 712,193 fish during ice-free seasons (OCR-recovered figure).
- Across the 12 lakes, fishing pressure rose 37% in 1947 over 1946, and total angling increased 16%.

**Catch per hour (six lakes):**
- **Spring:** Declined on Craig, Fine and Pontiac; about the same on Lobdell and Whitmore; improved on Duck.
- **Summer:** Improved on Craig, Pontiac and Whitmore; about the same on Duck, Fine and Lobdell; no decrease overall.
- **Fall:** Declined on Duck and Pontiac; unchanged on Whitmore; improved on Craig, Fine and Lobdell.
- Across the 12 lakes, 6 improved, 5 stayed the same and 1 became somewhat poorer in summer.

**Bear Lake:**
- 1946: 3,350 anglers caught 8,090 legal and 3,040 undersized fish.
- 1947: 4,280 anglers caught 7,400 legal and 3,220 undersized fish.
- About 12% of legal fish were taken before June 25. Spring catch per hour fell, while summer and fall improved.

**Saddle Lake:**
- 1946: 6,920 anglers took 28,300 legal and 27,600 undersized panfish.
- 1947: 13,600 anglers took 41,400 legal and 52,360 undersized panfish.
- Nearly half of the 69,700 legal fish from both years were taken before June 25. Spring improved, summer held steady, and fall declined slightly.

**Big Portage, Fife, Minnewaukon and Sugarloaf lakes:**
- 1946: 23,290 anglers took 56,140 fish.
- 1947: 24,680 anglers took 64,800 fish.
- Spring catch per hour dropped on one lake, held on two and rose on one. Summer improved on two and declined on two. Fall declined on one and improved on three.

**Extra fishing from liberalized rules (12 lakes):**
- 39,480 anglers took 93,993 legal and 50,820 undersized fish under the extended rules.
- An extra 35,400 undersized fish were taken during the ordinary season.

**Conclusion:** The author states that the evidence so far does not justify firm conclusions, but suggests that relaxing some restrictions, mainly on panfish, has not harmed summer fishing and has added sport when little other angling is available.
