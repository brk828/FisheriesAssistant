# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants; Animal Notice of Review

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation #federal-policy #endangered-species-act

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice, published January 6, 1989, by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, presents a comprehensive review of vertebrate and invertebrate animal taxa native to the United States being considered for possible addition to the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended. The document categorizes species into three groups: Category 1 (substantial information supports listing, but precluded by other activities), Category 2 (listing possibly appropriate but conclusive data lacking), and Category 3 (no longer under consideration due to extinction, taxonomic issues, or greater abundance than previously thought). This notice does not constitute a proposal for listing and provides no substantive or procedural protection, though it encourages consideration of these taxa in environmental planning.

## Background & Description

The notice represents a periodic update to previous reviews (vertebrates: September 18, 1985; invertebrates: May 22, 1984) and covers an extensive taxonomic range including fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals, and numerous invertebrate groups (sponges, crustaceans, arachnids, insects, snails, and bivalve mollusks). The document was compiled by endangered species staff across Regional and Field Offices of the Service and the Division of Endangered Species and Habitat Conservation in Washington, DC, with Dr. George Drewry serving as editor.

The review explicitly excludes taxa that have already received emergency, proposed, or final determinations of endangered or threatened status. Geographic coverage spans all U.S. states, territories, and possessions including [[American Samoa]], [[Guam]], [[Puerto Rico]], [[Virgin Islands]], and various Pacific island territories.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice addresses taxa across diverse habitats and ecosystems throughout the United States. For aquatic species, coverage includes:

**Freshwater Systems**: Numerous fish species from river systems including [[Lake Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*), [[Gulf Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser oxyrhynchus desotoi*), [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus albus*), and various minnows, suckers, catfish, pupfish, darters, and sculpins distributed across major river basins.

**Marine and Estuarine Habitats**: Coastal and marine species including the [[Tidewater Goby]] (*Eucyclogobius newberryi*) from California and various Hawaiian gobies.

**Cave and Spring Systems**: Specialized fauna adapted to subterranean aquatic environments, including cave fish, spring snails, and aquatic invertebrates with highly restricted distributions.

The document emphasizes that many taxa face threats from habitat loss, water quality degradation, restricted distributions, and small population sizes. Federal agencies are encouraged to consider these candidate species in environmental planning despite the lack of formal protection.

## Key Findings

**Category Structure**: The review establishes three distinct categories:
- **Category 1**: Taxa with substantial biological information supporting listing appropriateness, but proposals precluded by other listing priorities
- **Category 2**: Taxa possibly warranting listing but lacking conclusive vulnerability and threat data
- **Category 3**: Taxa no longer under consideration, subdivided into 3A (presumed extinct), 3B (taxonomic issues), and 3C (more abundant/widespread than previously thought)

**Fish Species Representation**: The notice includes extensive coverage of freshwater fish taxa across families including [[Petromyzontidae]] (lampreys), [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons), [[Salmonidae]] (trout and salmon), [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows), [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), [[Ictaluridae]] (catfish), [[Cyprinodontidae]] (pupfish and killifish), [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfish), and [[Percidae]] (darters and perch).

**Geographic Patterns**: Candidate species occur throughout the United States with notable concentrations in:
- Southwestern desert springs and streams (Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico)
- Southeastern river systems (Alabama, Tennessee, Georgia, Florida)
- California coastal and valley systems
- Hawaiian Islands (endemic species)
- Appalachian cave and spring systems

**Invertebrate Diversity**: The review documents extensive invertebrate candidate species including freshwater mussels (Family [[Unionidae]]), aquatic snails (multiple families), cave-adapted crustaceans and insects, and specialized terrestrial invertebrates. Sample sizes and population data are not provided in this regulatory notice.

**Data Solicitation**: The Service explicitly requests information on: (1) appropriate category assignments, (2) taxa nominations not included, (3) critical habitat recommendations, (4) threat documentation, (5) taxonomic changes, (6) nomenclature suggestions, and (7) error corrections including distributional data.

**Taxonomic Challenges**: The notice acknowledges ongoing systematic revisions, particularly for invertebrate groups, with some taxa listed as "sp." (undescribed species) or "ssp." (undescribed subspecies). Common names are provisional for many obscure taxa.

**No Protection Conferred**: The document emphasizes that inclusion in this notice does not provide substantive or procedural protection under the Endangered Species Act, distinguishing candidate species from those formally proposed or listed.

**Future Actions**: The Service indicates intentions to consider all submitted data, make appropriate amendments to the table, and announce substantive status changes through periodic Federal Register notices.
