# Virgin River Rotenone Eradication Downstream Fish Kill, October 1988

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (October 12, 1988, from Field Supervisor Sam F. Spiller to the Assistant Regional Director, Region 2, Albuquerque) reports limited follow-up work on the [[Virgin River Exotic Fish Eradication Project]]. The project used [[Rotenone]] from October 4 to 6, 1988 to remove exotic fish, mainly [[Red Shiner]], from 21 miles of habitat for the endangered [[Woundfin]] and the proposed endangered [[Virgin River Chub]]. Reports indicated the treatment had reached further downstream than planned. Field sampling on October 6 and 7 found dead and dying fish, including native species, roughly 27 miles below the project's downstream end. The memo concludes that few if any native fish survived at [[Littlefield Arizona]], while [[Speckled Dace]] at the mouth of [[Beaver Dam Wash]] suggest some recolonization may occur.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency internal memorandum with a field report attached. Copies went to the Salt Lake City Field Office, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, the Bureau of Land Management (Arizona State Office), and the [[Arkansas Fish and Wildlife Cooperative Unit]].
- **Project:** A joint [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[Utah Division of Wildlife]] effort, with help from the [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]].
- **Target area:** From the Washington Fields irrigation diversion downstream to a fish barrier under construction near the Utah/Arizona border (21 miles).
- **Treatment:** Rotenone applied October 4 to 6, 1988. Fuel oil served as the carrier substance.
- **Sampling team:** Sally Stefferud and Sam Spiller (Phoenix Field Office) and Jim Johnson (former Chief of Endangered Species, Albuquerque Regional Office; then Leader of the Arkansas Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit).
- **Species of concern:** Woundfin (*Plagopterus argentissimus*, endangered) and Virgin River Chub (*Gila robusta seminuda*, proposed endangered).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Virgin River]] flows through Utah, Arizona, and Nevada. The sampled reaches include a mainstream channel, the mouth of [[Beaver Dam Wash]] (a tributary that enters the river near Littlefield), and the [[Virgin River Gorge]], where a Bureau of Land Management campground is located.

Sampling observations:
- **October 6, approx. 5:00 p.m.:** About 24 hours after the last rotenone application, at the mouth of Beaver Dam Wash, about 27 miles below the project's downstream end. Dead and dying fish of several species were present. Fish were drifting in the current, washed onto sandbars, lying in pool bottoms, or trapped behind rocks. Most dead fish appeared recently dead, and some had been partially eaten by other animals.
- **Virgin River Gorge (October 6, dusk):** Dead and dying fish of most of the same species were present, but in smaller numbers. The smell of fuel oil was noted.
- **Mixing zone:** Water in the left channel below Beaver Dam Wash was roughly half Beaver Dam Wash and half Virgin River water. The right channel received no Beaver Dam Wash water.

Sampling was limited in time and scope. Spiller was not familiar with Woundfin, so any present may not have been identified during transect counts.

## Key Findings

**1. Rotenone extended beyond the planned treatment area.**
The treatment's effects reached roughly 27 miles below the project's downstream end, into Arizona. The memo cites information from several agencies in addition to the field observations.

**2. Native fish mortality was widespread at Littlefield.**
Dead or dying fish included [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis* and *C. clarki*), Speckled Dace, [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus melas*), Virgin River Chub, Red Shiner, and Woundfin. Only three dead Woundfin were noted, and numerous Virgin River Chub of several sizes were present, many dead and others stressed.

**3. Live-fish survey results (October 7 seining).**
- Virgin River above Beaver Dam Wash (six hauls, about 6 feet of ⅛-inch mesh seine): 51 Red Shiner.
- Beaver Dam Wash (one 15-foot kick disturbance into a block seine at the mouth): 55 Red Shiner and 4 Speckled Dace.
- Virgin River below Beaver Dam Wash, left channel (one haul): 282 Red Shiner.
- Virgin River below Beaver Dam Wash, right channel with no Beaver Dam Wash water (two hauls): no live fish.

**4. Dead-fish transect counts (October 7, 100-yard transects upstream from the dirt road near Beaver Dam Wash).**
- Transect 1: 5 suckers, 2 Virgin River Chub, 1 Speckled Dace.
- Transect 2: 22 suckers, 3 Red Shiner, 2 Speckled Dace.
- Transect 3: 36 suckers, 14 Speckled Dace, 12 Red Shiner, 4 Black Bullhead (one partly alive), 12 Virgin River Chub.
- Transect 4: 22 suckers (one partly alive), 11 Speckled Dace, 4 Black Bullhead, 46 Red Shiner (some still dying), 2 Virgin River Chub.

Counts from the transects were not expressed as densities, and the area sampled was not stated.

**5. Survival and recovery assessment.**
The authors concluded that few if any native fish survived at Littlefield. Red Shiner, the primary target species, was the only species found alive and well in the mainstream Virgin River. The presence of Speckled Dace at the mouth of Beaver Dam Wash suggests that native fish populations may recover through repopulation from the wash.

**6. Limitations.**
The authors describe the work as "extremely limited" in time and scope. The findings are preliminary observations from one sampling period and should not be read as a full mortality assessment.
