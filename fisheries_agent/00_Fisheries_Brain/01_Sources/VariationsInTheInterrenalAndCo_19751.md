# Variations in the Interrenal and Corpuscles of Stannius of Mugil cephalus from the Colorado River and Its Estuary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #mugilidae

## Executive Summary

This 1972 study by Donald W. Johnson examines how the interrenal tissue and corpuscles of Stannius (CS) of the [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*) vary with environmental salinity, season, and health status. Fish were sampled from a freshwater population below [[Imperial Dam]] on the [[Colorado River]] and a saltwater population at [[El Golfo de Santa Clara]] in the [[Gulf of California]]. Interrenal tissue was more active in fresh water, while corpuscle activity was greater in salt water. The author concludes that interrenal tissue has a strongly supported osmo(iono)regulatory role and that a link between CS activity and osmoregulation is probable.

## Background & Description

Euryhaline fishes offer a natural model for studying the endocrine control of hydromineral balance, because intraspecific variability in structure and function is absent in stenohaline species. Prior work relied largely on laboratory experiments with exogenous hormones, which may be confounded by trauma and pharmacological effects. The study addresses the gap by analysing a natural population.

*Mugil cephalus* was chosen for three reasons: its holoeuryhaline nature, its availability, and its worldwide distribution and economic importance. The species occurs in coastal waters and estuaries of tropical and subtropical zones (roughly 42° N to 42° S) and requires a minimum average monthly temperature above 16 °C.

The interrenal tissue of the species consists of anastomosing, tubule-like cylindrical cords. Chromaffin cells lie adjacent to the posterior cardinal vein and its branches. The corpuscles of Stannius are dorsomedial structures in the posterior mesonephric kidney, encapsulated and lobulated, with cell cords that may be folded or form an anastomosing mass. Corpuscles can reach 1–2 mm in diameter and 3–4 mm in length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Saltwater site (El Golfo):** surface temperatures 17–34 °C, salinity about 39‰. Collections used a beach seine.
- **Freshwater site (below Imperial Dam):** temperatures 19–28 °C, salinity about 0.2‰, about 150 miles from the Gulf. Collections used a gill net.
- **Collection schedule:** samples taken at 2-month intervals.
- **Reproduction and migration:** well-developed gonads appeared in river fish only after water cooled from 28 °C (August) to 21 °C (November). Maturation was linked to a possible seaward spawning migration. Salton Sea mullet, described as a land-locked stock of Colorado River origin, peak in reproduction in December and January.
- **Size and age:** mean standard length of Colorado River fish was 50% greater than that of saltwater fish (33.1 vs 22.4 cm). River fish were mainly in the 3–5 year age classes, while Gulf fish spanned ages 1–5.
- **Population identity:** analysis of serum transferrins, esterases and albumins showed no population discontinuity, and the author argues the two groups are one population, though a tagging program would be needed for definitive proof.
- **Parasites and disease:** [[Nematode]] infestation (tentatively *Neogoezia* sp.) was common in saltwater fish, with 61% (January) and 54% (June) carrying nematodes in the mesonephric kidney. Freshwater fish had uncommon nematode infestation but a high incidence of infectious kidney disease, with up to one third (December) showing abdominal fluid and kidney necrosis.

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- Interrenal and CS tissue was assessed with cytometric scoring on a 0–4 scale and nuclear diameter measurements (10 nuclei per fish, with camera lucida checks).
- Statistics included ANOVA, the non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis H and Mann–Whitney U tests, and the Spearman rank correlation. Nuclear distributions were often non-normal, so non-parametric tests were used.

**Hematology**
- Plasma sodium and chloride differed significantly between fresh and salt water. The chloride:sodium ratio ranged from 1:1.11 (January) to 1:1.52 (August) in fresh water, and from 1:1.10 (January) to 1:1.30 (April) in salt water.
- Mean hematocrit was 40% in Colorado River fish versus 31% at El Golfo.
- Hemoglobin showed no significant difference between areas but was inversely related to temperature, ranging from 5.43 g/100 ml (August) to 8.52 g/100 ml (December).
- The author concludes that hydromineral regulation in the striped mullet is slow and influenced by temperature, reproductive state and migration. The species does not hold plasma electrolytes within narrow limits, tolerating fluctuations above 25%.

**Interrenal Tissue**
- Freshwater mullet showed more frothy, less granular cytoplasm, larger nuclei, a more pronounced tubular arrangement, and more abundant spherical nuclei. These features indicate greater activity in fresh water.
- Hemosiderin granules were found in 100% of Colorado River fish versus 8% of Gulf fish.
- Chromophobes were more common in saltwater fish.
- Nuclear diameter was highest in November freshwater fish (4.20 µm) and January saltwater fish (4.17 µm). Nuclear sizes were similar between areas only in January and April.
- The most significant environmental factor for interrenal activity appears to be the osmotic quality of the habitat, with temperature and gonadal development also contributing.

**Corpuscles of Stannius**
- Nuclei were larger in saltwater fish in all sampling periods. Cytoplasmic abundance, nuclear orientation, shape and size all indicated greater secretory activity in salt water.
- Chromophobes were found in 44% of freshwater fish and 7% of saltwater fish.
- Parasitized marine fish had significantly larger nuclei in April (P < 0.0197) and June (P < 0.0003). Diseased freshwater fish had larger nuclei in June (P < 0.0062) but smaller nuclei in August (P < 0.0162).
- Corpuscle activity correlated directly with hematocrit. The author suggests a renin-angiotensin pressor role for CS secretions.

**Conclusions**
- Interrenal and CS activity are closely related to environmental factors, with temperature and gonadal activity stimulating interrenal tissue.
- Evidence suggests a spring feeding or migratory movement between habitats, with interrenal activity linking the two populations in April.
- The data support an osmo(iono)regulatory role for the interrenal organ and a probable osmoregulatory role for the corpuscles of Stannius, consistent with laboratory studies on other species.
