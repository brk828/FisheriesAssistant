# Interagency Letter Approving Golf Course Pond Rearing of Endangered Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1988 reply letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 2, Division of Endangered Species) to Dr. Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]]. It responds to Marsh's June 29, 1988 request to use university golf course ponds to rear endangered fish. The Service supports the proposal and lists three operational conditions: stocking timing, coordination of fish delivery, and expected stocking numbers. The letter also copies the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and the Phoenix Field Office, which are coordinating the effort.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence type:** Official letter from the Division of Endangered Species, signed by E. Johnson.
- **Addressee:** Dr. Paul C. Marsh, Associate Professor, Research, Center for Environmental Studies, Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ.
- **Copies to:** Buddy Jensen (Dexter NFH, Dexter, NM) and the Field Supervisor, Ecological Services, FWS, Phoenix, AZ.
- **Subject:** Use of ASU golf course ponds to rear endangered fish.
- **Target species** (as named in the letter):
  - [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)

The Service's stocking stock comes from the Dexter hatchery, which supplies fish to Arizona. The letter's tone is informal and collegial (it closes with "watch out for golf balls").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses only the rearing logistics for three endangered native fishes. It contains no data on water chemistry, habitat conditions, pond size, invertebrates, plankton, or population status. The only environmental factor noted is survival, which affects final stocking numbers. The ponds are used as a temporary rearing environment before the fish are released, and the seasonal timing of fish shipments (September and October) is linked to ongoing hatchery operations at Dexter.

## Key Findings

1. **Approval:** The Service supports using the ASU golf course ponds to rear endangered fish.
2. **Stocking timing:** Fish must be stocked earlier than November because of ongoing Dexter hatchery operations. Fish are normally shipped to Arizona in September and October, and the ASU fish could be delivered during that window.
3. **Delivery logistics:** ASU staff may need to meet the distribution truck along its normal route. Timing and location are to be arranged with Buddy Jensen at Dexter.
4. **Target numbers:** The Service will try to provide 1,500 each of Colorado squawfish, razorback suckers, and bonytail chub (4,500 fish total). Final numbers may vary slightly because of survival and stocking rates.
5. **Interagency coordination:** The letter formally coordinates the effort with Dexter and the Phoenix Field Office.
