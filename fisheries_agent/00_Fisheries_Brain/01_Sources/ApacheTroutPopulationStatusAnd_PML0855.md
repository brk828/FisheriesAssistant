# Apache Trout Population Status and Restoration Recommendations in the White Mountains, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This review, prepared by the [[Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office]], evaluates a genetic analysis by Genetic Laboratories (Smithville, Texas) of [[Apache Trout]] populations in the White Mountains, Arizona. The office accepts the findings at face value for the purpose of planning. It concludes that the main management task is to remove hybrid populations and populations contaminated by introduced trout, then restock them with pure Apache trout. The plan would make about forty-six streams with pure populations available for restoration and supports a goal of thirty self-sustaining populations before the species is delisted.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Apache Trout]] (APT), a native salmonid of the White Mountains. The document does not describe its morphology beyond the classification methods used.
- **Classification methods:** Population status was assessed with [[Morphometric Analysis]] and [[Meristic Analysis]], which were later supplemented by [[Genetic Analysis]]. Under the status codes used in the document:
  - **MP** (mostly pure): classified as mostly pure from morphometric and meristic measurements, but recent genetic studies characterize it as hybrid.
  - **IP** (introduced pure): a population of introduced but genetically pure fish.
  - **IMP** (impure): a population that is not pure.
- **Introduced competitors:** [[Brook Trout]] (BKT) and [[Brown Trout]] (BNT) are present in several waters and are the main threat, through [[Hybridization]] and competition.
- **Study area:** The waters span the [[White Mountains]] on the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] and the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]]. Surveys are referenced as [[GAWS Surveys]], completed between 1987 and 1990 for most streams.
- **Source of fish:** Pure stock is produced at [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]] (WCNFH), which raises the [[East Fork Strain]] of Apache trout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is mainly a management plan rather than an ecological study. Its ecological content is limited to:

- **Hybrid and introduced-species threat:** Populations with [[Brook Trout]] or [[Brown Trout]] are considered contaminated and must be cleared before restocking. Waters such as Christmas Tree Lake, Moon Creek, Sun Creek, Flash Creek and Little Diamond Creek contain introduced species.
- **Genetic history:** Original [[Ord Creek]] stock, used to establish [[Manie Creek]] in the 1950s, was lost during renovation in 1977. Manie and/or [[Coyote Creek]] fish, found to be pure, are proposed to rebuild the Ord Creek stock.
- **Restoration waters:** Table 2 lists about thirty waters with the actions needed before delisting, such as [[Barrier Construction]], [[Stream Renovation]], restocking, habitat improvement and monitoring. Examples include Becker Creek, Big Bonito Creek and Soldier Creek.
- **Sport fishery:** Christmas Tree Lake, Moon Creek and Sun Creek are managed as sport fisheries, so introduced trout will not be removed there for now.

## Key Findings

1. **Hurricane Lake and Hurricane Creek are pure.** Fish in [[Hurricane Lake]] are genetically pure, so the fish in Hurricane Creek above the lake are treated as one pure population.

2. **Sport-fishery waters are retained.** [[Christmas Tree Lake]] and [[Reservation Lake]], together with [[Sun Creek]] and [[Moon Creek]], contain BNT and BKT but will not be renovated now. They are managed as one sport fishery.

3. **One discrepancy in Sun Creek.** Two samples from Sun Creek were tested. One was hybrid and the other pure. This was the only known discrepancy in the analysis. Sun Creek fish will not be killed.

4. **Coyote Creek and Manie Creek are considered pure.** Coyote Creek fish were introduced from Manie Creek and are pure, so Manie Creek fish are assumed pure even though they were not sent for analysis. The original [[Ord Creek]] stock was lost in 1977, and Manie and/or Coyote Creek stock is recommended for reestablishing it once Ord Creek is renovated.

5. **East Fork strain waters are pure.** [[Hayground Creek]], [[Hone Creek]], [[Lee Valley Creek]], [[Wildcat Creek]] and [[Lee Valley Reservoir]] originated from the East Fork strain at Williams Creek NFH and are considered pure.

6. **Hybrid populations require removal.** Fish in hybrid populations are to be killed and replaced with pure stock from Williams Creek NFH or from adjacent streams containing pure stock. For pure populations contaminated by BKT or BNT, the method is to collect broodstock, remove all remaining fish, and restock the stream with the held broodstock.

7. **Restoration capacity.** If these recommendations are followed, forty-six streams with pure Apache trout populations would be available for restoration.

8. **Recovery target.** The [[Recovery Plan]] calls for thirty self-sustaining populations before delisting. The document states that the number of pure streams appears to be more than enough to meet this target, which should make it easier to reach. Agencies should consider adding other streams within the historic range if necessary.

9. **Population status summary.** [[Table 1]] sorts waters into three groups: genetically pure, pure populations on the Fort Apache Indian Reservation (mainly Williams Creek NFH stock), and hybrid populations. Hybrid populations include Diamond Creek (North and South Forks), Hughey Creek, Little Diamond Creek, Ord Creek and Paddy Creek.
