# Monthly Normal Temperatures, Precipitation, and Degree Days

#climatology #meteorology #weather-data #united-states

## Executive Summary

This technical paper presents comprehensive climatological normals for temperature, precipitation, and heating degree days across the [[United States]], including [[Alaska]], [[Hawaii]], [[Puerto Rico]], and Pacific territories. Published by the [[U.S. Weather Bureau]] in November 1956, the document provides standardized 30-year climate normals (1921-1950) for hundreds of weather stations. This represents the first systematic compilation adopting the [[World Meteorological Organization]]'s standard 30-year period for climatic normals, with separate tabulation of maximum and minimum temperatures and careful adjustment for station relocations.

## Background & Description

The normals are calculated from records spanning 1921-1950, representing a departure from previous methodologies in three key aspects: (a) adoption of the 30-year period consistent with international standards, (b) meticulous adjustment of values to avoid artificial smoothing when station locations changed, using "difference factors" for temperature and "ratio factors" for precipitation, and (c) first-time separate tabulation of maximum and minimum temperature normals. Monthly precipitation normals represent means of monthly values across the 30-year period for stations with continuous records. Temperature normals were derived from normal maximum and minimum values, with annual normal temperature calculated by averaging the annual normal maximum and minimum values. Degree day normals use a standard base of 65°F, with monthly normals less than 5 degree days shown as zero. February normals are calculated for a 28-day month.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document covers all major climate zones of the [[United States]], from arctic conditions in [[Barrow, Alaska]] to tropical environments in [[Hawaii]] and the [[West Indies]]. Station elevations range from sea level coastal locations to high-altitude sites exceeding 6,000 feet. The data encompasses diverse ecosystems including temperate forests, grasslands, deserts, coastal zones, and arctic tundra. Temperature extremes span from winter minimums below -30°F in northern interior locations to summer maximums exceeding 100°F in southwestern deserts. Precipitation patterns vary dramatically, from less than 8 inches annually in arid regions to over 200 inches in tropical Pacific locations like [[Mount Waialeale]]. These climate normals provide baseline environmental conditions affecting aquatic ecosystems, fish distributions, spawning timing, and freshwater/marine habitat characteristics across all U.S. territories.

## Key Findings

- **Standardized Climate Normals**: Comprehensive 30-year (1921-1950) climate normals established for hundreds of stations across all U.S. states and territories, providing baseline data for temperature, precipitation, and heating degree days.

- **Temperature Data**: Monthly normal maximum and minimum temperatures tabulated separately for the first time, with annual normals derived from these values. Extreme ranges documented from arctic to tropical conditions.

- **Precipitation Patterns**: Monthly and annual precipitation normals ranging from under 8 inches in desert regions ([[Yuma, Arizona]]: 3.39 inches annually) to over 200 inches in tropical locations ([[Mount Waialeale]], Hawaii implied by regional context).

- **Geographic Coverage**: Data includes all 48 contiguous states, [[Alaska]] (15 stations), [[Hawaii]] (multiple stations), [[Puerto Rico]], [[Virgin Islands]], and Pacific territories including [[Wake Island]], [[Canton Island]], and [[Guam]].

- **Degree Days**: Heating degree day normals calculated on 65°F base, with seasonal totals equivalent to annual totals, providing energy and agricultural planning metrics.

- **Station Adjustments**: Careful methodology applied to adjust records for station relocations using parallel comparisons and adjustment factors, ensuring data continuity and accuracy.

- **Elevation Effects**: Station elevations documented, ranging from sea level to over 6,000 feet, with corresponding temperature and precipitation variations clearly reflected in the normals.

- **Coastal vs. Interior**: Marked differences between coastal maritime climates (moderated temperatures, higher precipitation) and interior continental climates (greater temperature extremes, variable precipitation).

- **Regional Patterns**: Clear documentation of major climate regions including Pacific Northwest maritime, Southwest desert, Great Plains continental, Southeast humid subtropical, and Northeast humid continental zones.

- **Data Quality**: Trace precipitation amounts indicated with "T"; missing or relocated stations marked with asterisks; thermometer heights and ground elevations provided for context.
