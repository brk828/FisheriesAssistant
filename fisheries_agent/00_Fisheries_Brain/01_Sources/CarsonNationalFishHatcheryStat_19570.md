# Carson National Fish Hatchery — Station Profile

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Exhibit 1 "Station Profile" for the **Carson National Fish Hatchery** located on the Wind River Highway near Carson, Washington (M.P. 14). Established in 1938 under authority 50 STAT 220, the station's mission is to restore and maintain depleted **upriver Columbia River [[Chinook Salmon]]** stocks — specifically the **Spring Chinook Salmon** run — to levels observed in the mid-1950s, focusing on the area near [[Bonneville Dam]]. The profile documents facility infrastructure, funding/personnel trends (FY1991–FY1995), fish distribution numbers, and annual production/harvest statistics rather than primary biological/morphological research data.

## Background & Description

The source document is an administrative hatchery station profile and does **not provide morphological data** (e.g., length, weight, meristic counts, coloration) for [[Spring Chinook Salmon]]. It focuses instead on operational and production metrics. Species identity is limited to:

- **Common name:** Spring Chinook Salmon
- **Scientific name (inferred):** *Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*
- **Family:** [[Salmonidae]]
- **Life stages referenced:** green eggs, eyed eggs, fry, adults (returning spawners)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Anadromous life history — hatchery-reared juveniles are released into the **Wind River**, a tributary system feeding the **Columbia River**, with adults returning from the North Pacific Ocean to spawn near [[Bonneville Dam]].
- **Hatchery infrastructure supporting rearing:**
  - 46 raceways (8×80 ft)
  - 2 dirt rearing/release ponds
  - 2 adult holding ponds with associated facilities
  - Service buildings, hatchery building, shop/storage, 6-stall garage, oil house, 9 residences
- **Population role:** The station functions as a **supplementation/restoration hatchery** for depleted upriver Columbia River [[Chinook Salmon]] stocks, interacting with wild population dynamics, tribal fisheries (Yakima Indian Nation), and recreational (sport) fisheries.
- **Interagency water/fish distribution network:** Eggs and fish are transferred to other stations, indicating a broader regional hatchery ecosystem:
  - [[Umatilla Hatchery]] — received 1.99 million green eggs
  - [[Oxbow Hatchery]] — received 480,000 eyed eggs
  - [[Spring Creek National Fish Hatchery]] — received 500,000 fry (for Big White Ponds)

## Key Findings

### Production & Returns (Fiscal Year of Report)
- **Adult Chinook Salmon returns to Wind River:** 11,500
  - Sport fishermen harvest: 2,400
  - Yakima Indian Nation harvest: 3,000 (plus 13% of surplus fish allocated)
- **Green eggs taken:** >7.6 million
  - 1.99 million shipped to [[Umatilla Hatchery]]
  - 480,000 eyed eggs shipped to [[Oxbow Hatchery]]
  - 500,000 fry shipped to [[Spring Creek National Fish Hatchery]]
- **Visitors:** ~5,550 people visited the hatchery during the fiscal year.
- **Research initiative:** A **density study** was initiated to evaluate effects of rearing density on post-release survival of Spring Chinook Salmon.
