# Regional Response to Recovery Team Recommendations

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (dated February 7, 1992) rather than a primary scientific study. It does not contain new ichthyological, ecological, or population data. Instead, it addresses an administrative and procedural issue: the lack of timely Regional Office response to recommendations made by the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] and the [[Gila Trout]]/[[Chihuahua Chub]] Recovery Team. The memo proposes formal communication protocols to ensure recovery team recommendations are acknowledged and acted upon in a timely manner. While it lacks direct biological findings, it is scientifically relevant because it documents the institutional and conservation-management context surrounding two imperiled southwestern fish taxa groups: desert fishes generally, and the [[Gila Trout]] / [[Chihuahua Chub]] species pair specifically.

## Background & Description
- **Species/Taxa Referenced:**
  - **[[Gila Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) — a federally listed salmonid endemic to headwater streams of the Gila River drainage in New Mexico and Arizona.
  - **[[Chihuahua Chub]]** (*Gila nigrescens*) — a cyprinid minnow native to the Río Casas Grandes and Río Santa Maria basins in New Mexico and Chihuahua, Mexico.
  - **[[Desert Fishes]]** — a broader, unspecified assemblage of fish species addressed collectively by the Desert Fishes Recovery Team, likely including pupfish, chub, and other arid-adapted freshwater taxa native to the American Southwest.
- **Document Context:** The memo originates from a Senior Staff Biologist addressing the Field Supervisor of the New Mexico Ecological Services Field Office (Albuquerque), following concerns raised at a Desert Fishes Recovery Team meeting.
- **Core Issue:** Recovery team leaders (volunteer scientific experts, many unaffiliated with state/federal agencies) reported frustration that their formal recommendations to the Service were not being acknowledged or acted upon by the Regional Director.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The species involved — [[Gila Trout]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], and other [[Desert Fishes]] — inhabit fragile, isolated desert aquatic habitats (headwater streams, springs, and river segments) characteristic of the arid Southwestern United States and northern Mexico.
- These habitats are typically small, hydrologically vulnerable, and subject to threats such as water diversion, habitat fragmentation, invasive species, and drought — conditions that necessitate active, science-based recovery planning.
- Recovery Teams composed of leading species experts (including non-agency volunteer scientists) serve as the primary technical advisory bodies guiding conservation strategy for these taxa under the Endangered Species Act framework.
- Effective recovery of such narrowly distributed, habitat-limited species depends on rapid institutional responsiveness to expert recommendations — the central concern raised in this memo.

## Key Findings
- **No new biological, distributional, or population data are presented in this document.**
- **Procedural Finding:** Both the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] and the [[Gila Trout]]/[[Chihuahua Chub]] Recovery Team independently reported the same institutional problem — lack of Regional Office feedback on submitted recommendations.
- **Recommended Administrative Protocol** (proposed by the Senior Staff Biologist):
  1. Service recovery team coordinators must submit team recommendations to the Regional Office within **two weeks** of a recovery team meeting.
  2. The Regional Office must **acknowledge receipt** within **one week**, indicating anticipated action, to both the team leader and coordinator.
  3. The Regional Office, in cooperation with the relevant field office, must issue a **formal response** to recommendations within **60 days** of receipt, copied to both the team leader and coordinator.
- **Rationale:** Recovery teams provide critical scientific expertise, often on a volunteer basis; failure to engage with their input undermines both morale and the effectiveness of species recovery efforts.
- **Institutional Significance:** This memo functions as an early internal acknowledgment of the need for structured, transparent communication between scientific recovery teams and federal regional management for imperiled aquatic species such as [[Gila Trout]] and [[Chihuahua Chub]].
