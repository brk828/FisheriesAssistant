# Bonytail Chub Collection and Transportation Protocol, Lake Mohave (1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum sets out a field protocol for collecting wild [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) from [[Lake Mohave]] during the week of May 12-18, 1996. The goal is to secure wild fish as potential [[Broodstock]]. Collected fish are to be transported to the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Willow Beach NFH), where they will be held and treated prophylactically. The protocol focuses on minimizing handling and stress, preventing gilling and injury, and keeping fish alive for future spawning. Partner agencies outside the Service are not bound by the protocol unless it is made a condition of their [[Endangered Species Permit]], but the memo asks Service staff to brief them on it.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Secure wild bonytail chub for potential broodstock, supporting the species' recovery.
- **Scope:** Collection from Lake Mohave, May 12-18, 1996.
- **Issuing office:** Regional Geographic Manager, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 2 (Albuquerque, New Mexico).
- **Addressees:** Leader, Willow Beach NFH (Boulder City, Nevada); Leaders, Parker and Arizona Fishery Resource Offices; Leader, [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]] (Pinetop, Arizona).
- **Action requested:** Review the attached protocol, discuss it with partners, and send critiques. Recommendations for future collection efforts are to be reported after the effort ends.
- **Key personnel:**
  - [[Chuck Minckley]]: coordinates transport from collection sites to Willow Beach NFH and maintains contact with the hatchery.
  - [[Ronald Major]]: fish health biologist, on site and reachable by cellular phone or in person throughout the week.
  - Jim Hanson and John Seals: daytime contacts (7:40 A.M. to 4:30 P.M., Monday to Friday) and evening contacts for Hanson, as listed in the protocol.
- **Meeting:** Minckley and Major were to meet at each camp on Monday, May 13, 1996, to establish lines of communication and collection procedures before transport began.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The protocol is built around the stress sensitivity of bonytail chub during capture and holding. Key risks identified in the document include:

- **Handling injury:** The caudal peduncle is described as especially sensitive, so fish should not be grasped there.
- **Gilling:** Trammel nets are to use center panels of about 1 to 1.5 inches to reduce gilling of captured fish.
- **Abrasions and infection:** Abrasions are to be treated with stress coat to reduce the risk of infection.
- **Holding conditions:** Fish are not to be held in live-cars in the lake. Once removed from nets, they go into holding tanks, and tank conditions must be controlled throughout.
- **Darkness and transport:** Fish are to be moved to the hatchery during hours of darkness and kept covered (in the dark) to reduce stress.

## Key Findings

The memorandum does not report data from collections. Its key content is a set of operating procedures and responsibilities.

**1. Capture and handling**
- Minimize handling. Cut captured fish out of the net and transfer them with a soft, fine-mesh dipnet.
- Keep hands wet and avoid grasping fish by the caudal peduncle.
- Use trammel nets with center panels of about 1 to 1.5 inches.
- Monitor nets at least every 6 hours. If schedules cannot be met, nets should be pulled.
- Do not weigh, measure, or scan adults being retained for transport.

**2. Holding and treatment**
- Prepare a holding tank before running nets. It must hold at least 80 gallons and be escape-proof.
- The protocol specifies a prophylactic treatment with salt, [[Furacin]], and [[MS-222]] (anesthetic), plus stress coat for abrasions. The OCR-degraded scan makes the exact concentrations uncertain. As legible, they read roughly 1 g/gal salt, 1 mg/gal Furacin, and 95 mg/gal MS-222. These should be verified against the original document before use.
- Stock all treatment materials, stress coat, and an oxygen bottle with regulator before nets are run. Oxygen is to be turned on before netting begins.
- Minckley and Major are to supply premeasured packets of salt, Furacin, and MS-222 at each camp during their Monday visit.

**3. Communication and logistics**
- Cellular phones are to be available at each lakeside camp to provide 24-hour communication.
- Fish are to be delivered to Willow Beach NFH during darkness.
- Service staff are asked to brief partner collectors and to use the protocol as a permit condition where feasible, since non-Service partners are not otherwise required to follow it.

**4. Follow-up**
- After the collecting effort, the Service expects a report with critiques of the protocol and recommendations for future collection efforts.
