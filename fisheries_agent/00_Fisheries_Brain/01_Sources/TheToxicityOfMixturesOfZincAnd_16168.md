# The toxicity of mixtures of zinc and copper sulphates to rainbow trout (Salmo gairdnerii Richardson)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #ecotoxicology #water-pollution

## Executive Summary
This 1961 study by R. Lloyd at the Water Pollution Research Laboratory investigates the combined toxic effects of zinc and copper sulphates on [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*), a species of major concern in freshwater [[Ecotoxicology]]. The research directly tests claims from earlier literature (Bandt 1946; Doudoroff 1952) that zinc-copper mixtures act synergistically on fish. Using controlled toxicity bioassays in both hard and soft water, Lloyd demonstrates that at **low concentrations**, the combined toxicity of zinc and copper conforms to **similar joint action** (i.e., is additive and predictable from single-metal toxicity curves), rather than the previously assumed synergism. However, at **higher concentrations in soft water specifically**, true **synergistic toxicity** was observed, confirming prior reports under those particular conditions. This has important practical implications for setting safe discharge limits for [[Heavy Metal Toxicity]] in river water quality management.

## Background & Description
This paper is a toxicology/physiology study rather than a morphological description, but relevant test-subject characteristics are noted:
- **Species**: Rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdnerii* Richardson), now more commonly classified as *Oncorhynchus mykiss*.
- **Test animal size**: Approximately 3 inches (~7.6 cm) in length — juvenile fish.
- **Physiological endpoint measured**: Cessation of all respiratory (opercular/gill) movement, used as the definition of death/end of survival time.
- **Acclimatization protocols**:
  - Hard-water group: acclimatized 1 day at 15.5–17.0°C to room temperature and free CO₂ concentration.
  - Soft-water group: previously reared in hard water; acclimatized 5 days at 17.0–18.0°C to dilution water, with final-day acclimatization to free CO₂.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Freshwater rivers and streams; test waters were designed to simulate two contrasting natural water chemistries:
  - **Hard water**: Total hardness 320 mg/L as CaCO₃ (unchlorinated borehole water).
  - **Soft water**: Total hardness 15–20 mg/L as CaCO₃ (borehole water diluted with demineralized water, plus 15 mg/L NaCl added).
- **Environmental relevance**: The study models real-world scenarios where industrial and waste-water discharges introduce multiple heavy metal pollutants (zinc and copper) simultaneously into rivers inhabited by [[Rainbow Trout]] and other salmonids.
- **Water chemistry interactions**: The authors hypothesize that **calcium concentration** (a proxy for water hardness) may modulate the joint toxic action of zinc and copper, potentially explaining why synergism appeared only in soft water. This links toxicant bioavailability to broader [[Water Hardness]] effects on metal toxicity — a recurring theme in aquatic [[Heavy Metal Toxicity]] research.
- **Experimental conditions**: 40 L test aquaria, aerated with a controlled air/CO₂ mixture to maintain ~8.0 mg/L free CO₂; 10 rainbow trout per test, with randomized allocation of fish and test sequence.

## Key Findings

### Test Design & Metrics
- Toxicity was quantified via **median period of survival** (time to death for 50% of test fish), derived from log-normal survival time distributions (per Bliss 1937 probit methodology).
- Metal concentrations were normalized as ratios relative to a threshold concentration (**Znₜ** and **Cuₜ**) — defined as the concentration producing a median survival time equal to the experiment duration.

### Hard Water Results (Total hardness 320 mg/L CaCO₃)
- Threshold concentrations for a 3-day median survival:
  - **Znₜ = 3.5 mg/L**
  - **Cuₜ = 1.1 mg/L**
- Zinc and copper tested singly produced a single fitted curve relating log(survival time) to log(concentration ratio), indicating **similar mode of toxic action** between the two metals.
- Mixture tests (Zn:Cu ratio 6:1, achieved ratio ~2:1 in Znₛ/Znₜ vs Cuₛ/Cuₜ) fit the same curve as the single-metal data.
- **Conclusion**: In hard water, zinc and copper exhibit **similar joint action** (additive toxicity) — no synergism detected at tested concentrations.
- Note: Higher concentrations could not be tested in hard water due to precipitation of the metals as basic salts.

### Soft Water Results (Total hardness 15–20 mg/L CaCO₃)
- Threshold concentrations for a 7-day median survival:
  - **Znₜ = 0.56 mg/L**
  - **Cuₜ = 0.044 mg/L**
- Single-metal data again fit a common toxicity curve, as in hard water.
- Mixture tests (Zn:Cu ratio 6:1, achieved ratio ~1:2 in Znₛ/Znₜ vs Cuₛ/Cuₜ):
  - At **low concentrations**, data fit the similar joint action curve well.
  - At **high concentrations**, survival times were **significantly shorter than predicted**, indicating **synergistic toxic action**.
- **Conclusion**: Synergism between zinc and copper occurs specifically at high concentrations in soft water, confirming earlier literature (Doudoroff 1952).

### Interpretive & Practical Implications
- The mechanism underlying the soft-water synergism is unknown but may relate to the **relative proportions of zinc, copper, and calcium** in solution — calcium potentially competing for uptake sites or modifying metal speciation/bioavailability.
- For **practical water-quality management**, Lloyd concludes that environmentally realistic (sub-lethal, low) concentrations of zinc and copper in rivers are unlikely to produce synergistic effects; **similar joint action** (additive toxicity) is a reasonable and sufficient assumption for setting safe discharge limits, without requiring correction factors for synergism.
- This work provides an early quantitative framework for classifying combined [[Toxicant Interactions]] (similar joint action, independent joint action, synergism, antagonism) as defined by [[Bliss 1939]], directly relevant to modern [[Mixture Toxicity]] assessment in aquatic ecotoxicology.
