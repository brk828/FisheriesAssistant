# Observations on the American Species of the Genus *Esox*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

Girard's short taxonomic note, submitted to the Committee for publication in the Proceedings, examines the North American pikes and pickerels of the genus [[Esox]]. Vernacular names were applied inconsistently, so the author proposes a morphological split. Species with naked (scale-free) cheeks and opercular apparatus are called pikes, and species with scaled cheeks and opercles are called pickerels. The split also tracks geography: pikes occupy the Great Lakes and western waters, while pickerels occupy rivers and ponds draining to the Atlantic. The document contains no measurements, sample sizes or statistics.

## Background & Description

- **Problem:** The names "pike" and "pickerel" were used interchangeably across North America. Local fishermen reported no consistent distinction, except that large individuals were called pikes and small ones pickerels.
- **Diagnostic character:** The main contrast is the covering of the cheeks and opercular apparatus. In [[Esox Estor]] these parts are smooth and naked. In [[Esox Reticulatus]] they are scaled all over.
- **Naked-cheeked group (pikes):** [[Esox Nobilior]] (maskallonge, Thompson), [[Esox Boreus]] (pike of [[Lake Superior]]), and several other species previously confused with [[Esox Estor]].
- **Scaled-cheeked group (pickerels):** [[Esox Americanus]] (also called [[Esox Niger]]), [[Esox Fasciatus]] (Dekay), and [[Esox Clathratus]] (Bd., manuscript name), which resemble [[Esox Reticulatus]].
- **Size:** Pikes are the largest members of the genus when full grown. [[Esox Reticulatus]] is the largest known pickerel and is comparable in size to the largest [[Esox Boreus]] the author examined. The author suspects [[Esox Boreus]] reaches a considerably larger size.
- **Nomenclature proposed:** Retain "Pickerel" for [[Esox Reticulatus]] and allied species, and apply "Pike" to the maskallonge and its allies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Geographic pattern:** Pikes are confined to the [[Great Lakes]] and western waters generally. Pickerels occur in ponds and rivers whose waters drain to the [[Atlantic Drainage]]. The author describes this as a "curious fact" of distribution and the morphological split as corresponding to it.
- **Introduction:** A pike was introduced into the [[Connecticut River]]. It has multiplied there, but the author considers this consistent with the general distribution pattern.
- **Possible exception:** A pickerel is reported from the [[Ohio River]]. No specimen was available, and the author considers the record unverified. If it is confirmed as native, it would be an exception to the general rule.
- **Unresolved taxa:** [[Lesueur]] described [[Esox Vermiculatus]], [[Esox Lineatus]] and [[Esox Depravus]] from the [[Wabash River]]. No type specimens are preserved, so they cannot be identified directly. The author notes that cheek and opercular scale condition can still be checked in the field. [[Esox Ingubrosus]] (Lesueur, eastern states) and [[Esox Phaleratus]] (Say, eastern Florida) are also undetermined in this respect.

## Key Findings

1. **Diagnostic character:** Cheek and opercular scalation separates two groups of American *Esox*: naked-cheeked pikes and scaled-cheeked pickerels.
2. **Nomenclature:** The author recommends restricting "Pike" to the naked-cheeked group, including [[Esox Nobilior]] and [[Esox Boreus]], and "Pickerel" to the scaled-cheeked group, including [[Esox Reticulatus]].
3. **Size:** Pikes are the largest species in the genus. [[Esox Reticulatus]] is the largest pickerel, and [[Esox Boreus]] may exceed it.
4. **Distribution:** The two groups follow a geographic pattern, with pikes in the [[Great Lakes]] and western waters and pickerels in [[Atlantic Drainage]] waters.
5. **Open questions:** The occurrence of a pickerel in the [[Ohio River]] needs verification. Several [[Lesueur]] and [[Say]] species from the Wabash, eastern states and Florida lack preserved specimens and cannot be assigned to the proposed groups without field re-examination of cheek scalation.
