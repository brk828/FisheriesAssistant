# Hybridization in Nature Between Species of Catostomid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1948 monograph from the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]] (Laboratory of Vertebrate Biology, Contributions No. 22) by Carl L. Hubbs, Laura C. Hubbs and Raymond E. Johnson documents the first described natural hybrids between species of catostomid fishes ("suckers"), a family of almost exclusively North American freshwater fish. The authors analyze 182 hybrid suckers representing 3 crosses within [[Catostomus]] and 7 combinations between [[Catostomus]] and [[Pantosteus]]. Evidence is circumstantial, based on intermediate morphology, counts and measurements, with no breeding experiments or cytology. The paper also critiques earlier claims of sucker hybridization in [[Ictiobus]], [[Carpiodes]] and [[Moxostoma]], finding them unsupported.

## Background & Description

Hybridization between distinct fish species and genera was, by the authors' account, well established in other families (notably [[Cyprinidae]]), but no catostomid hybrids had previously been described. The study used the [[Hybrid Index]] and [[Character Index]] methods, which the authors developed or adapted to quantify intermediacy across many characters. Key diagnostic traits include:

- **Mouth structure**: degree of upper lip flattening, papillation, the isthmus and lower-lip incision, and the width and transversality of the cartilaginous jaw ridges. These are the most distinctive differences between [[Catostomus]] and [[Pantosteus]].
- **Counts**: [[Dorsal Fin Rays]], [[Lateral Line Scales]], scales above and below the lateral line, and scales around the caudal peduncle, plus pelvic ray counts.
- **Proportions**: head length and depth, snout length, body and caudal peduncle depth, and depressed dorsal fin length, all expressed as thousandths of standard length.
- **Coloration and pigmentation**: lateral blotches, scale-pocket outlining, and peritoneal pigment.

Methods included about 2000 counts and measurements on hybrids and a much larger number on parental species, with statistical treatment by standard error and coefficient of variability, and a formula for the standard error of the hybrid index.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors describe several ecological conditions linked to hybridization:

- **Introduced species**: [[Catostomus commersonnii sucklii]] appears to have been introduced into the upper [[Colorado River]] system, probably via bait fishermen, around 1926, and hybridizes with the native [[Pantosteus delphinus delphinus]]. Hybrids were most frequent where the introduced form was scarce.
- **Habitat disruption**: In the [[Santa Clara River]] system, the rare [[Catostomus]] species and the abundant [[Pantosteus santa-anae]] were forced together, possibly because irrigation withdrawals and flood washings reduced deep holes. The authors suggest this favors hybridization and local extirpation of the rare parent.
- **Habitat preferences**: [[Catostomus syncheilus]] favors smaller, more rapid waters, while [[Catostomus macrocheilus]] is ecologically widespread. Both spawn on gravel riffles in spring.
- **Hybrid rarity**: Hybrids make up about 1 sucker in 100 in river reaches where both parents occur.

## Key Findings

- **Natural hybridization is confirmed** in the Catostomidae, supported by intermediacy across multiple uncorrelated characters, a unimodal frequency distribution of indices, and no increase in variability. The authors state that the evidence strongly supports hybridity but is not experimentally verified.
- **Hybrid sample**: 182 hybrids in total. Within [[Catostomus]], 76 hybrids across 3 crosses. Between [[Catostomus]] and [[Pantosteus]], 105 hybrids across 7 combinations.
- **Hybrid frequency**: Hybrids represent 11.5% and 7.2% of the specimens of the more primitive and more specialized parental species collected with them, but only 1.8% and 1.5% of the specimens from the same river systems. In combinations where both parents occur, roughly 1 sucker in 100 is an interspecific hybrid.
- **Intermediacy**: The grand average hybrid index is 51 (50 = exact intermediacy). Mean indices for the 10 combinations range from 41 to 56, with [[Catostomus]] × [[Pantosteus]] averaging 45 and [[Catostomus]] × [[Catostomus]] averaging 53. The lowest mean (41) was in the [[Santa Clara River]] cross.
- **Notable examples**:
  - *Catostomus macrocheilus × C. syncheilus syncheilus*: 68 hybrids, mean index 53; 33 of 68 fell between 45 and 75.
  - *Catostomus insignis × Pantosteus clarkii*: 21 hybrids, index range 25–67, with 17 of 21 between 39 and 63. The authors call this the most clear-cut combination.
  - *Catostomus, species × Pantosteus santa-anae*: 38 hybrids collected in 1942 from Sespe Creek; the hybrids show a parietal fontanelle intermediate between the parents.
- **Inheritance**: Results conform to the authors' view that systematic characters in fishes show a multiple-factor, nearly Galtonian inheritance rather than simple Mendelian segregation. Hybrids show no significant increase in variability and little evidence of backcrossing. No hybrid populations were established.
- **Evolutionary significance**: The authors conclude that hybridization has not played a speciational role in the Catostomidae. They attribute similarities between [[Catostomus syncheilus]], [[Catostomus catostomus]] and [[Pantosteus]] to parallel evolution on swift, shallow-stream bottoms, not past hybridization.
- **Critique of earlier claims**: Reported hybrid origin of [[Ictiobus niger]] was rejected because its characters are not intermediate. Two [[Moxostoma]] specimens treated as hybrids were reidentified: one as [[Placopharynx carinatus]], the other as an aberrant [[Moxostoma aureolum]].
- **Taxonomic note**: The authors argue that [[Evermann]] and [[Cox]] (1896) erred in treating Platte River fine-scaled suckers as two full species. They consider *griseus* a southern, montane subspecies of *C. catostomus*.
