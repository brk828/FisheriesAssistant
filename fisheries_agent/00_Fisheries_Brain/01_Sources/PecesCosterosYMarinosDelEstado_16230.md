# Coastal and Marine Fishes of the State of Veracruz

#ichthyology #marine-biology #checklist

## Executive Summary

This paper presents an updated checklist of the marine and coastal fishes of [[Veracruz]], Mexico, on the southwestern [[Gulf of Mexico]]. It combines literature records with the results of 22 collecting trips that yielded 1,989 specimens. The checklist lists 290 species in 171 genera and 71 families. Of these, 91 species (6 undetermined), 66 genera and 45 families are documented by specimens. The authors report 22 new records for the state or Mexican waters and a large number of new localities. Most species are widely distributed, which places the area in a transition zone between the [[Tropical Western Atlantic]] and [[Warm Temperate Northwest Atlantic]] provinces. The authors intend the list to support ecological policy and sustainable management of fisheries.

## Background & Description

Interest in the [[Gulf of Mexico]] ichthyofauna stems from growing fishing pressure, which could be diversified to exploit resources without threatening their production. Baseline inventories are needed for fisheries management, aquaculture and sport fishing. Earlier regional works, such as [[Hoese and Moore 1977]] and [[Walls 1975]], covered the Gulf broadly, but few mentioned [[Continental Shelf]] catches off Veracruz specifically.

Coastal lagoons have received more attention because of their fisheries. Earlier surveys include Darnell (1962, 45 species in Tamaulipas) and Reséndez-Medina (1981a, 62 species in the El Carmen-Machona-Redonda system, Tabasco; 1981b, 118 species in [[Laguna de Terminos]], Campeche). Reséndez-Medina and Kobelkowsky-Diaz (1991) reported 318 species across 13 Gulf coastal lagoons. For Veracruz, Reséndez-Medina (1983) recorded 52 species in [[Sontecomapan Lagoon]], and Fuentes-Mata et al. (1989) added 30 more. For the continental shelf, only one prior study existed: Castro-Aguirre and Marquez-Espinoza (1981), with 130 species from [[Isla Lobos]].

The study area covers the Veracruz continental shelf and adjacent coastal lagoons. Fish were collected with a 3 m beach seine (chinchorro) and trawl nets. Specimens were fixed following Hall et al. (1962), and taxonomy follows Greenwood et al. (1966). Nomenclature changes follow Robins et al. (1991). Zoogeographic assignments use the schemes of Ekman (1953) and Briggs (1974), as summarized by Vermeij (1978), with subprovince categories adapted from Bond (1979), Lagler et al. (1977) and Banarescu. Collection localities are listed in Appendix 1, mapped in Figure 1. Trawl stations 8 to 17 were made from the research vessel B/O Virgilio Uribe de la Marina.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Veracruz coast combines open shelf waters, reef areas (including Isla Lobos, Isla Verde, Isla de Sacrificios and the "Bajos de Tuxpam" reefs), and estuarine systems. Major estuaries and lagoons named in the checklist include the [[Tuxpam River]] estuary, [[Tamiahua Lagoon]], [[Tampamachoco Lagoon]], [[La Mancha Lagoon]], [[Mandinga Lagoon]], [[Alvarado Lagoon]], [[Sontecomapan Lagoon]] and [[Ostion Lagoon]], as well as the mouths of the [[Papaloapan River]] and [[Coatzacoalcos River]].

Many species recorded are euryhaline and move between marine and estuarine habitats. The authors state that most species are widely distributed and frequently recorded in coastal lagoons, which explains why they were expected in the area even when unreported. The region is also described as a zone of biogeographic mixing. It may be the northern limit of the Tropical Western Atlantic fauna, or a transition zone in its own right.

Context from the reference list points to the wider [[Sistema Arrecifal Veracruzano]], where artisanal use of marine animals and reef conservation were under discussion. Those works are cited but not analysed in this paper.

## Key Findings

**Inventory and sampling**
- Total checklist: 290 species, 171 genera, 71 families (the Discussion gives 291 species; this is an internal inconsistency).
- Specimens: 1,989 examined from 22 collecting trips, representing 91 species (6 undetermined), 66 genera and 45 families.
- Localities: 66 new localities are given in the English abstract; the Spanish abstract says 65, and the Discussion says 62.
- The checklist draws on the authors' own collections (by collectors including S. Contreras-Balderas, M.L. Lozano-Vilano and the ECB group) and on earlier literature.

**New records**
- The abstract reports 22 new records. The Discussion gives 12 for the Veracruz coast and 11 for Mexican waters, but names only 11 species, including *Anchoa nasuta*, *Trachinocephalus myops*, *Scorpaena agassizi*, *Prionotus evolans*, *Trachinotus goodei*, *Lutjanus mahogoni*, *L. vivanus*, *Scarus croicensis*, *Katsuwonus pelamis*, *Scomberomorus regalis* and *Citharichthys cornutus*.
- *Prionotus roseus* was previously reported for Cabo Rojo in an unpublished thesis (Huidobro, 1992).
- The record of *Citharichthys gilberti* from Sontecomapan Lagoon is questioned, because the literature cited places the species in the Pacific.

**Zoogeography**
- Provinces: Tropical Western Atlantic 87 species (31%); Warm Temperate Northwest Atlantic 38 (13%); both regions 142 (50%); endemic 16 (6%).
- Subprovinces: amphiamerican 23 (8%); amphiamerican Atlantic 119 (42%); Caribbean 79 (28%); Carolinean 38 (13%); tropicoamerican 8 (3%); endemic 16 (6%).
- The authors conclude that the area is one of mixing or transition, with the majority of species belonging to the amphiamerican Atlantic group.

**Notable taxa in the checklist**
- Elasmobranchs include [[Carcharhinus leucas]], *Galeocerdo cuvieri*, several hammerheads (*Sphyrna* spp.), sawfishes (*Pristis* spp.), and rays such as *Rhinobatos lentiginosus*, *Dasyatis sabina* and *Aetobatus narinari*.
- Commercially important families are well represented, including Carangidae, Lutjanidae, Sciaenidae, Centropomidae, Gerreidae, Mugilidae, Clupeidae and Engraulidae.
- Reef-associated species recorded from Isla Lobos include parrotfishes (Scaridae), wrasses (Labridae), damselfishes (Pomacentridae), angelfishes (Pomacanthidae) and butterflyfishes (Chaetodontidae).

**Management implications**
- The authors state that the checklist aims to inform ecological policy and sustainable exploitation of coastal fishery resources in Mexico.
- The paper documents the diversity of commercially exploited groups in lagoons and on the shelf, which is the baseline needed for managing their use.
