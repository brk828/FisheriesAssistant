# A Revision of the Osmerid Fishes of the North Pacific

#ichthyology #marine-biology #osmeridae

## Executive Summary

This 1925 taxonomic paper by [[Carl L Hubbs]] revises the smelt family [[Osmeridae]] in the North Pacific. Based on examination of nearly all osmerid material preserved in American museums, Hubbs presents a dichotomous key to the genera and species, reassesses the validity of several named forms, corrects misidentified records, and proposes one new genus, [[Allosmerus]]. The revision separates the North Pacific fauna into five genera and 11 species (including the circumpolar [[Mallotus villosus]]), and argues that many previously reported intergrades and synonyms are not supported by the specimens.

## Background & Description

The Osmeridae, or true smelts, are confined to Arctic and North Temperate waters. Like many Holarctic families, they are more diverse in the North Pacific than in the North Atlantic. The Atlantic has a single osmerid genus with one species of [[Osmerus Mordax]]-type smelt, plus [[Mallotus villosus]]. The North Pacific fauna includes [[Allosmerus attenuatus]], [[Thaleichthys pacificus]], three species of [[Spirinchus]] and four species of [[Hypomesus]].

Hubbs distinguishes taxa primarily by meristic and morphometric characters:

- **Lateral-line scale count** (fewer than 80 in most genera; about 150 in *Mallotus villosus*).
- **[[Vomerine Dentition]]**: canine-like, fang-like teeth confined to the vomer's lateral tips in *Osmerus* and related genera, versus numerous, convex, uniserial teeth in *Spirinchus* and *Hypomesus*.
- **[[Gill Rakers]]** counts on the first arch.
- **[[Anal Fin]]** height (relative to head length) and ray count.
- **Pelvic fin insertion** relative to the dorsal fin origin.
- **Mouth size and upper jaw extension** relative to the orbit.
- **Breeding condition**, including whether teeth are deciduous at spawning and whether males develop tubercles or villous bands.

The genus-level key separates *Osmerus*, *Allosmerus*, *Thaleichthys*, *Spirinchus*, *Hypomesus* and *Mallotus*, and the species key uses the same characters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Hubbs notes a range of life histories among the taxa:

- **Stream spawners**: [[Osmerus dentex]], [[Thaleichthys pacificus]] (which ascends streams), [[Spirinchus thaleichthys]] (which ascends the [[Sacramento River]]), and [[Hypomesus olidus]] (which breeds in fresh water at small size).
- **Surf or ocean spawners**: [[Hypomesus pretiosus]] (spawns in the surf of ocean beaches), and probably [[Allosmerus attenuatus]] (which spawns in the ocean).
- **Mostly pelagic**: [[Thaleichthys pacificus]] is probably pelagic for most of its life.
- **Freshwater and lacustrine forms**: [[Hypomesus olidus]] ranges from mountain lakes of central Japan to the upper reaches of [[San Francisco Bay]].

Geographic notes include:

- [[Allosmerus attenuatus]] is common in and about [[San Francisco Bay]] and ranges north at least to central Oregon.
- [[Thaleichthys pacificus]] ranges from the [[Klamath River]] in northern California to the [[Bering Sea]].
- [[Spirinchus starksi]] (the "night surf smelt") was collected by seine on the beach near the mouth of the [[Mad River]], [[Humboldt County]], California. [[Hypomesus pretiosus]] is known locally as the "day surf smelt."
- [[Hypomesus japonicus]] and [[Hypomesus verecundus]] are represented in northern Japan, [[Kamchatka]] and Korea.
- [[Osmerus dentex]] occurs from the northern coast of Alaska to Japan.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic revisions and new names**

- *Allosmerus* Hubbs is proposed as a new genus, with *Allosmerus attenuatus* (Lockington) as type. It is intermediate between *Osmerus* and *Thaleichthys*.
- The generic name *Hypomesus* Gill is adopted in preference to *Mesopus* Gill, which Hubbs treats as a typographical error.
- *Osmerus sargenti* Norris is proposed as a possible name for a smaller southern form of the Atlantic smelt, with a higher anal fin and fewer gill rakers.
- *Spirinchus* is recognized as generically distinct from *Osmerus*, based on its vomerine dentition.

**Atlantic comparisons**

- *Osmerus dentex* and the Atlantic smelt differ mainly in gill-raker count and anal fin height. Hubbs concludes that the Pacific form cannot be satisfactorily separated from the Atlantic smelt if the Atlantic smelt is treated as one species.
- The Atlantic smelt is divided into at least two forms on each side of the ocean. The common New England and Canadian smelt (*O. mordax*) has 10–11 + 19–24 gill rakers and anal fin 2.6–3.4 in head. The southern form (*O. sargenti*) has 9–10 + 15–18 gill rakers and anal fin 2.4–2.6 in head.
- The most reliable difference between Pacific and Atlantic forms is the posterior extent of the upper jaw. In *dentex* the maxillary end lies roughly on the vertical from the posterior orbit margin, whereas in all *mordax* examined it falls short.

**Corrected records and synonymies**

- The Matsushima Bay, Japan, record of *Osmerus dentex* was based on *Plecoglossus altivelis*, a fish of a different family.
- Monterey, California, records of *Osmerus thaleichthys* were based on *Spirinchus starksi*.
- Records of *S. thaleichthys* from the Nushagak River, Alaska, refer to *Hypomesus olidus*.
- Records of *S. thaleichthys* from San Luis Obispo and Puget Sound are not supported.
- *Osmerus oligodon* Kner is a synonym of *Hypomesus olidus*, not of *H. japonicus*, as previously claimed.
- Material from Petropavlovsk, [[Kamchatka]], identified by Jordan and Gilbert as *Mesopus olidus*, belongs to *H. japonicus*.
- *Hypomesus verecundus* is confirmed as a typical *Hypomesus*, based on re-examination of the three known Korean type specimens.

**Other observations**

- *Spirinchus lanceolatus* is very similar to *S. starksi* but differs in fin and head proportions. A redescription was planned by Jordan and Hubbs in the *Memoirs of the Carnegie Museum*.
- In *Thaleichthys pacificus*, Hubbs traced a complete transition between the pelagic stage named *Osmerus albatrossis* (with well-developed teeth) and breeding adults (largely edentulous).
- Hubbs found no good evidence that *H. olidus*, *H. pretiosus* and *H. japonicus* intergrade or are identical, despite repeated claims.
- The Eastern Asian *Mallotus villosus* (capelin) was not critically studied. Hubbs suggests that such an investigation "might yield results of considerable interest."
