# Southwest Center for Biological Diversity Legal Notice - Colorado River and San Pedro River Protection

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-law #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This October 12, 1994 correspondence from the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] (SWCBD) announces two pending lawsuits targeting federal agencies for violations of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) and [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA). The first lawsuit addresses dewatering of the [[Colorado River]] and exotic species introduction via the [[Central Arizona Project]]. The second lawsuit focuses on protecting the [[San Pedro River]] from uncontrolled growth impacts. Multiple native fish species are at risk, including [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and others. The organization solicits co-plaintiffs from conservation groups.

## Background & Description

The notice identifies two distinct legal actions with overlapping species concerns:

**July 30, 1994 Notice** targets U.S. Department of the Interior, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service for ESA violations related to [[Colorado River]] dewatering and exotic species introduction through [[Central Arizona Project]] operations.

**August 18, 1994 Notice** targets multiple federal agencies including Department of Defense, U.S. Army Intelligence Center and Ft. Huachuca, HUD, Interior, FWS, Department of Transportation, Federal Highway Administration, and federal mortgage corporations for NEPA and ESA violations concerning uncontrolled growth impacts on the [[San Pedro River]].

Protected species include multiple cyprinids ([[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Colorado River Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]]), catostomids ([[Razorback Sucker]]), poeciliids ([[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]]), salmonids ([[Apache Trout]] *Oncorhynchus apache*, [[Gila Trout]] *Oncorhynchus gilae*), sciaenids ([[Totoaba]] *Cynoscion macdonaldi*), and non-fish species including [[California Brown Pelican]] (*Pelecanus occidentalis*), [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (*Rallus longirostris yumanesis*), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), and [[Vaquita]] (*Phocoena sinus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] system faces ongoing dewatering pressures from water diversion projects, particularly the [[Central Arizona Project]], threatening native fish assemblages adapted to natural flow regimes. Exotic species introductions compound threats to endemic species already stressed by habitat modification.

The [[San Pedro River]] in Arizona experiences pressure from uncontrolled urban and military growth, particularly associated with [[Ft. Huachuca]] expansion and federally-backed housing development. This riparian corridor supports multiple endangered fish species requiring perennial flow and intact habitat.

Both river systems represent critical habitat for southwestern native fish fauna, many of which are narrow endemics with no populations outside these watersheds. Water extraction, flow modification, and habitat degradation pose existential threats to species survival.

## Key Findings

**Legal Strategy**: SWCBD planned to file the [[San Pedro River]] lawsuit within approximately two weeks of October 12, 1994, with [[Huachuca Audubon]] as co-lead plaintiff. The [[Colorado River]]/[[Central Arizona Project]] lawsuit would follow 2-3 weeks later.

**Targeted Agencies**: Lawsuits name multiple federal agencies with jurisdiction over water management, military operations, housing finance, and transportation infrastructure, establishing broad federal responsibility for cumulative impacts.

**Species Protection Requests**: Legal actions demand adequate protection for 13 fish species, 3 bird species, and 1 marine mammal species under ESA provisions.

**Coalition Building**: The correspondence solicits participation from approximately 25 conservation organizations and individuals as co-plaintiffs, emphasizing that financial contribution should not determine involvement.

**Legal Grounds**: Claims rest on ESA violations (failure to protect listed species) and NEPA violations (inadequate environmental analysis of growth impacts and water project operations).
