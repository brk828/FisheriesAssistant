## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper but a **1995 Scientific Collecting Permit renewal packet** issued by the [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]] (AGFD), governing the legal "take" (collection, handling, and disposition) of wildlife — including native fish — under Arizona Administrative Rule **R12-4-418** (Scientific Collecting Permit) and **R12-4-428** (Captivity Standards). While largely administrative, the packet includes an important scientific reference: the **1988 Arizona State List of Threatened Native Wildlife (TNW)**, cross-referenced against federal **Endangered Species Act (ESA)** status. This list identifies **26 fish taxa** native to Arizona as state-listed Threatened or Endangered, several of which are also federally listed, making the document a valuable snapshot of Arizona ichthyofaunal conservation status as of the mid-1990s.

## Background & Description

The permit system requires researchers ("permittees") collecting [[Aquatic Wildlife]] — including [[Sport Fish]] and non-game native fish — to submit a study proposal, facility descriptions (for live holding), and methodology (e.g., [[Electroshock]] sampling, seining, angling, gill nets, trammel/hoop nets). Permittees must report all specimens collected, with heightened reporting detail (legal locality, UTM coordinates, habitat description, disposition) required specifically for species on the **State List of Threatened Native Wildlife**.

Embedded in this administrative framework is a **taxonomic checklist of Arizona's imperiled fish fauna**, spanning several families:

- **Salmonidae**: [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*)
- **Cyprinidae** (minnows/chubs): [[Arizona Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum pricei*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Colorado Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta robusta*), [[Virgin Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*), [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*), [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Yaqui Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus mearnsi*), [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- **Catostomidae** (suckers): [[Yaqui Sucker]] (*Catostomus bernardini*), [[Little Colorado Sucker]] (*Catostomus sp.*), [[Zuni Mountain Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus ssp.*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Ictaluridae**: [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*)
- **Cyprinodontidae** (pupfish): [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon sp.*) — noted Extinct, [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*), [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*)
- **Poeciliidae** (livebearers): [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

These fish taxa inhabit fragmented, often isolated desert aquatic habitats across Arizona, largely within the [[Colorado River Basin]], [[Gila River Basin]], and Rio Yaqui drainages. Their imperiled status reflects well-documented regional pressures affecting Southwestern freshwater ichthyofauna:

- **Habitat fragmentation and dewatering** from water diversion, groundwater pumping, and impoundment (relevant to lotic specialists like [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Woundfin]]).
- **Non-native species introductions** (e.g., predatory and competitive fishes) threatening small-bodied endemics such as [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]].
- **Isolated spring and cienega habitats**, critical for pupfish and topminnow taxa, are highly vulnerable to desiccation and habitat alteration — exemplified by the **extinction** of the [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]] (status: SX, "Extinct" per the 1988 TNW list).
- **Large-river specialists** such as [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] are tied to mainstem Colorado River ecosystem processes disrupted by damming and flow regulation.

The permit's captivity standards (R12-4-428) also implicitly acknowledge the ecological needs of aquatic taxa held in captivity, mandating "sufficient access to safe water to meet aquatic behavioral needs" for any wildlife naturally living in an aquatic environment.

## Key Findings

- **26 fish taxa** were listed on Arizona's 1988 State List of Threatened Native Wildlife (TNW), spanning six families, reflecting broad-based decline across Arizona's native aquatic fauna by the late 1980s/early 1990s.
- **Status designations** used: **SE** (State Endangered), **ST** (State Threatened), **SC** (State Candidate), **SX** (Extinct). Several species carried simultaneous **federal ESA "Listed Endangered" (LE)** status, though the OCR did not clearly resolve which specific fish taxa carried the LE designation (this appears in the ESA Status column but is obscured/incomplete in the extracted table).
- The **[[Monkey Springs Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon sp.*) is explicitly noted as **SX (Extinct)** — a documented Arizona extinction event tied to spring/cienega habitat loss.
- The permit rules impose **heightened reporting requirements** (R12-4-418.G) specifically for TNW-listed fish, big game, and select herpetofauna, requiring detailed locality (legal description and/or UTM), date of take, and disposition — indicating that even permitted "scientific collecting" of these species is treated as a conservation-sensitive activity requiring granular data reporting to AGFD.
- Collecting methods explicitly authorized under the permit relevant to fish sampling include **angling, seining, gill nets, trammel/hoop nets, and electroshock**, with electroshock and other more invasive methods subject to a "least onerous practical method" standard.
- The document underscores that **scientific collecting permits expire December 31** of the issuance year, requiring **annual renewal and mandatory reporting** even when no collection activity occurred — a structural mechanism ensuring continuous monitoring data flow to the state agency for these at-risk species.
