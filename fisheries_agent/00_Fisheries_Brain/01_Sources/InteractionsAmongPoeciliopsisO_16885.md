# Interactions Among *Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*, *Gambusia affinis* and *Gila purpurea*

## Executive Summary

This is a 1984 research proposal submitted by Dr. David L. Galat (Arizona State University) to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, targeting management needs at the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] (SBNWR) in the Yaqui River Basin, Arizona. The proposal addresses two federally endangered fishes: the [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*) and the [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*). The core research problem is twofold: (1) understanding the poorly documented mechanisms allowing *P. o. sonoriensis* to coexist with the invasive [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), a predator implicated in the decline of the related Gila Topminnow, and (2) generating foundational life history data for *Gila purpurea*, a species newly listed as endangered (1984) with almost no prior published biology. The study is explicitly comparative, contrasting lentic (springpond) versus lotic (stream) habitats, and sympatric versus allopatric fish assemblages, to inform reintroduction and recovery planning.

## Background & Description

This document is a project proposal (not a completed morphological study), so no direct morphometric data are reported. However, the proposed methodology specifies morphology-adjacent data to be collected on preserved specimens of *Gila purpurea*:

- **Length/weight relationships** — to be measured on preserved specimens
- **Growth patterns** — assessed via size-frequency distribution analysis
- **Size class stratification** — sampling for *Poeciliopsis* and *Gambusia* is designed to include all size classes present at each site

No morphological descriptors (fin ray counts, coloration, sexual dimorphism, etc.) are provided in the source text for any of the three taxa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Context:**
- Both target endangered fishes occupy **springponds** and one **stream** (Black Draw) at [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]], within the Yaqui River Basin.
- They are also documented as sympatric in **Leslie Creek**, a tributary of the Rio Yaqui in the Swisshelm Mountains (citing [[Hendrickson Et Al 1980]]).
- The study explicitly contrasts **lentic** (springpond) vs. **lotic** (stream) systems as a major axis of ecological variation affecting both species.

**Sympatric/Allopatric Fish Assemblages (Study Design for *Gila purpurea*):**

| Contrast Type | Location | Coexisting Fishes |
|---|---|---|
| Lentic–Lotic | North Pond, SBNWR (lentic) | *P. o. sonoriensis* |
| Lentic–Lotic | Leslie Ck, Swisshelm Mtns (lotic) | *P. o. sonoriensis*, *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Campostoma ornatum* (?) |
| Sympatry–Allopatry | North Pond, SBNWR | *P. o. sonoriensis* |
| Sympatry–Allopatry | House Pond, SBNWR | None (?) |
| Climate contrast | House Pond, SBNWR vs. Dexter National Fish Hatchery | None |
| Newly-established populations | House Pond, SBNWR & Dexter NFH | None (?) |

**Predator-Prey Dynamics:**
- *Gambusia affinis* is a documented predator of *Poeciliopsis occidentalis*, cited as a primary driver of topminnow decline in prior literature ([[Minckley Et Al 1977]], [[Schoenherr 1981]], [[Meffe Et Al 1983]]).
- The proposal notes that while predator-prey dynamics between *Gambusia* and the *P. o. occidentalis* subspecies (Gila Topminnow) are well studied, the equivalent dynamics for *P. o. sonoriensis* (Yaqui Topminnow) remain poorly understood — a key knowledge gap this study aims to fill.

**Co-occurring Native Species Mentioned:**
- *Agosia chrysogaster* (Longfin Dace)
- *Campostoma ornatum* (Mexican Stoneroller) — occurrence tentative (marked "?")

## Key Findings

*Note: As a research proposal, this document outlines planned methodology and objectives rather than final results. Key elements include:*

1. **Conservation status**: *P. o. sonoriensis* and *G. purpurea* are both federally endangered; *G. purpurea* was only listed as endangered in 1984 ([[U.S. Department Of The Interior 1984]]), with critical habitat designated concurrently with two threatened species (beautiful shiner and Yaqui catfish).

2. **Primary threats identified**: Habitat loss (cited across [[Minckley 1973]], [[Hendrickson Et Al 1980]], [[Meffe Et Al 1983]]) and predation by introduced *Gambusia affinis* specifically threatening *Poeciliopsis*.

3. **Study Design — Objective 1 (Poeciliopsis-Gambusia coexistence)**: Quarterly/monthly sampling at four sites — two where the species coexist (Black Draw [lotic within SBNWR], Border Spring [lotic, SBNWR]) and two where *Gambusia* is absent (Leslie Creek [lotic] and an SBNWR spring pond [lentic, site TBD]). Parameters to evaluate predation sensitivity:
   - Fecundity and recruitment
   - Growth rate
   - Habitat use
   - Evidence of harassment
   - Direct predation quantification (via *Gambusia* gut content analysis)

4. **Study Design — Objective 2 (*Gila purpurea* life history)**: Comparative sampling across four habitat contrasts (lentic vs. lotic; sympatric vs. allopatric with topminnow; climate; newly-established vs. established populations), spanning SBNWR, Leslie Creek, and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, NM). Parameters measured:
   - Length/weight
   - Growth (size-frequency distribution)
   - Diet
   - Reproductive cycles/fecundity
   - Sex ratios
   - Relative abundance by habitat type

5. **Anticipated management value**: Results intended to directly inform the [[Sonoran Topminnow Recovery Plan]] ([[Stefferud 1984]]) and support development of a forthcoming Recovery Plan for *Gila purpurea*, as well as guide reintroduction site selection and cienega habitat development at SBNWR.

6. **Project logistics**: One-year study (Dec 1, 1984 – Nov 30, 1985), budgeted at $4,257 total (including travel, supplies, and 25.2% indirect costs), led by PI Dr. David Galat with field support from SBNWR and Dexter NFH personnel. Specimens to be archived in the ASU fish collection.
