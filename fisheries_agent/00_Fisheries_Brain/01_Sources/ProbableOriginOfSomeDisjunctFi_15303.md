## Executive Summary

This paper by Robert Rush Miller addresses two related but distinct topics in Mexican freshwater ichthyology. First, it examines **disjunct fish distributions** in northern Mexico (Chihuahua, Durango, Zacatecas), arguing that these patterns are biologically real (not sampling artifacts) and are best explained by **Tertiary-to-Pleistocene tectonic and volcanic reorganization of drainage basins**, rather than by present-day hydrography. The genus [[Dionda]] is presented as a key case study of vicariant speciation driven by progressive subdivision of ancient basins, alongside similarly disjunct taxa such as [[Moxostoma austrinum]] and [[Gila cf. pandora]]. Second, the paper (or an appended dataset) provides a comprehensive **checklist of the freshwater and euryhaline fishes of the Yucatan Peninsula**, comprising 30 families, 51 genera, and 100 species, classified by ecological affinity (primary, secondary, euryhaline, vicarious freshwater fishes).

## Background & Description

The source is primarily biogeographic and geological rather than morphometric, but it does note key taxonomic and systematic points:

- **Dionda**: a speciose cyprinid genus with at least 13 species, including 3 undescribed forms from the Mezquital drainage; 7 species form a **species flock** endemic to the Río Parruco basin — evidence of rapid, basin-confined diversification.
- **Cyprinodontidae** in the Yucatan checklist is flagged as **non-monophyletic** by the author, who explicitly rejects splitting off [[Profundulidae]], [[Fundulidae]], and [[Valenciidae]] as separate families.
- The Yucatan checklist documents multiple **undescribed species** (e.g., *Atherinella* n. sp. A, B, C; *Cichlasoma* n. sp. A, B), indicating unresolved taxonomic diversity within regional endemics such as [[Atherinella]] and [[Cichlasoma]].
- Notable strictly freshwater specialists are highlighted within otherwise euryhaline families, e.g., *Potamarius nelsoni* (Ariidae) and *Diapterus mexicanus* (Gerreidae), both noted as strictly freshwater.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Northern Mexico disjunct fishes:**
- Habitats are **endorheic desert basins** and montane headwater streams (e.g., Nazas-Aguanaval system, Parras Basin, Mezquital drainage, Río Grande headwaters) shaped by Tertiary uplift of the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] and volcanic activity along the [[Transvolcanic Axis]].
- The Transvolcanic Axis (Pliocene, ~19°–21°N) acts as a **zoogeographic boundary** between Holarctic and Neotropical biotas, though the exact boundary varies by taxon.
- Basin fragmentation via tectonism created isolated refugia promoting [[Vicariant Speciation]], directly paralleling patterns seen in Pliocene Lake Idaho mollusk and fish faunas (Smith 1975).

**Yucatan Peninsula fishes:**
- The checklist spans a strong **freshwater-to-marine ecological gradient**, from strictly freshwater primary fishes (e.g., Characidae, Catostomidae, Cichlidae) to fully euryhaline/estuarine taxa (e.g., Ariidae, Centropomidae, Sciaenidae, Carangidae) inhabiting coastal lagoons such as **Laguna de Términos** and **Lago Petén**.
- Ecological categories tallied: **Primary** freshwater (4 families, 7 spp.), **Secondary** freshwater-tolerant (6 families, 47 spp.), **Euryhaline** (19 families, 34 spp.), and **Vicarious** freshwater invaders (12 spp.) — illustrating a peninsula-wide continuum of salinity tolerance tied to karstic hydrology and proximity to the Gulf of Mexico.
- Cave-adapted and cryptic habitats are represented by synbranchid eels (*Ophisternon aenigmaticum*, *O. infernale*) from the Bythitidae/Synbranchidae, indicating specialized use of cenote and subterranean karst waters.

## Key Findings

- Disjunct fish distributions in [[Chihuahua]], [[Durango]], and [[Zacatecas]] are confirmed as **genuine biogeographic patterns**, not artifacts of incomplete sampling.
- **Progressive Tertiary subdivision of hydrographic basins** — driven by uplift of the Sierra Madre Occidental and associated volcanism — is proposed as the principal mechanism generating vicariant speciation in taxa such as *Dionda*, *Moxostoma austrinum*, and *Gila* cf. *pandora*.
- Most *Dionda* species differentiated **>2 million years ago**, though some diversification likely occurred within the Pleistocene (last 1.7 my).
- Comparative mollusk fossil data (Taylor 1985) show **no clear evidence of new species evolving during the Quaternary** (last 1.5 my), with extinctions concentrated in the latest Pleistocene–early Holocene; by contrast, at least some fishes appear to have evolved into distinct taxa within this same interval, suggesting differing evolutionary tempos between mollusks and fishes.
- Fish distributions in central and northern Mexico correlate more strongly with **ancient geological features** (paleodrainages) than with **modern hydrographic boundaries**, reinforcing the value of integrating geology and paleontology into biogeographic inference — the discipline the author terms [[Paleohydrology]].
- The Yucatan Peninsula freshwater ichthyofauna checklist (30 families, 51 genera, 100 species) provides a baseline taxonomic and ecological inventory, useful for comparative biogeographic study relative to the tectonically driven patterns documented in northern Mexico.
- Key geological factors proposed as necessary context for interpreting fish distributions: (1) timing of tectonic events and relief development, (2) disruption of drainages and formation of endorheic basins, and (3) depositional and paleoenvironmental evidence of ancestral fish habitats.
