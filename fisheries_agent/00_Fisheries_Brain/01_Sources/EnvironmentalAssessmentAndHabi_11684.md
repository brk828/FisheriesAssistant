# Environmental Assessment and Habitat Conservation Plan for El Coronado Ranch, West Turkey Creek, Arizona

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #endangered-species-act #habitat-management

## Executive Summary

This Environmental Assessment and Habitat Conservation Plan (EA/HCP) addresses the issuance of an Incidental Take Permit under Section 10(a)(1)(B) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] for [[El Coronado Ranch]] (ECR) in [[Cochise County]], [[Arizona]]. The document covers ranching operations on 6,153 ha (15,204 acres) in the [[West Turkey Creek]] watershed that may result in incidental take of endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), threatened [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*), and the unlisted [[Yaqui Form Of Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*). The applicant (Josiah and Valer Austin) seeks a 25-year permit to conduct lawful ranching activities including water diversion, pond maintenance, and livestock grazing while implementing conservation measures that support recovery of [[Rio Yaqui]] native fishes. The HCP provides mitigation through habitat enhancement, non-native species control, watershed restoration, and monitoring protocols. The preferred alternative authorizes incidental take on private lands while requiring conservation actions that exceed anticipated losses, contributing to recovery objectives outlined in the [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]].

## Background & Description

### Regulatory Context

The document is a combined Environmental Assessment (prepared by [[US Fish And Wildlife Service]]) and Habitat Conservation Plan (prepared by the applicant) required for issuance of an incidental take permit under ESA Section 10(a)(1)(B), as implemented by 50 CFR 17.22. The permit would authorize take resulting from otherwise lawful ranching activities over a 25-year period beginning November 1997.

### Species Covered

**Primary Species (Plan Species):**
- **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*): Endangered. Historically occurred in West Turkey Creek; reintroduced to ECR ponds in 1986 from [[Astin Spring]] via [[Leslie Creek]] stock. Subsequently dispersed into West Turkey Creek. Demonstrates high resilience with rapid population expansion (tens to thousands in weeks/months).
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*): Threatened. Planned for release into ECR ponds and [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]].
- **[[Yaqui Form Of Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): Unlisted. West Turkey Creek supports the only known stock persisting in [[Sulphur Springs Valley]]. Permit coverage effective upon listing if species is ever listed.

**Other Rio Yaqui Species (potential future introductions):**
- [[Arizona Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*)
- [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Cyprinella formosa*)
- [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*) - recently reintroduced but did not persist; altitudes above typical habitat range

**Secondary Concern Species:**
- [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (*Strix occidentalis lucida*): Two territories in upper watershed
- [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] (*Rana chiricahuensis*): Historically present; unlikely to persist due to bullfrog presence

### Site Description

**Geography:** Northwest side of [[Chiricahua Mountains]], elevations 1,652-2,629 m (5,452-9,854 ft). Total area 6,153 ha: 778 ha (1,920 ac) deeded private land, 5,376 ha (13,284 ac) [[Coronado National Forest]] grazing allotment (>50% unsuitable for grazing). All drained by West Turkey Creek and tributaries.

**Hydrology:** West Turkey Creek baseflow 0.15-0.35 m³/min (0.09-0.21 cfs) during drought, declining to few liters/min in prolonged drought. Approximately 6.5 km (4.0 mi) wetted channel persists on/upstream from ECR during low flow. Stream responds rapidly to summer rains; post-[[Rattlesnake Fire]] (1994) peak discharges exceeded 500 m³/min (294 cfs). At baseflow: <0.5 m wide, 0.2 m deep except pools to 1-2 m wide, 10 m long, 1 m deep.

**Water Infrastructure:** Five diversions on West Turkey Creek. Approximately 2.5 km (1.6 mi) permanently to intermittently flowing ditches (20-50 cm wide, 5-20 cm deep). Total 18 water retention structures and stock ponds ranging <0.1-6.5 ha (0.2-16 ac), total surface area ~25 ha (62 ac). Five ponds large/deep enough (to 8.0 m) to sustain fish through severe drought. Lowermost pond ([[Big Tank]]) may be stocked with non-native, non-reproducing game fish for limited private recreational fishing.

**Vegetation:** Desert grassland at low elevations transitioning through [[Madrean Evergreen Woodland]] and Chaparral (dominated by evergreen oaks) to Mixed Conifer stands at higher elevations. Historically heavily grazed; desert scrub expanded and grasslands deteriorated. Riparian/aquatic habitats reduced to disturbed remnants.

### Livestock Management

Current stocking: 60-135 cattle rotated through deeded and Forest Service pastures. ECR adopted non-use for resource protection in 1995. Livestock numbers reduced in drought years; permitted to exceed 66 animals on FS land in wet years but not to exceed 867 animal months. A [[Coordinated Land Management Plan]] completed 1993 (Appendix B) covers both private and Forest Service allotment lands.

### Non-Native Species Threats

**Fishes:** Rainbow trout, fathead minnow, channel catfish, western mosquitofish, green sunfish, bluegill sunfish, largemouth bass.

**Amphibians:** Bullfrog (*Rana catesbeiana*), tiger salamander (*Ambystoma tigrinum*).

These organisms negatively impact native species. Some control/removal efforts undertaken by ECR; more effort necessary.

### Rattlesnake Fire Impact (1994)

Summer 1994 fire in upper watershed on CNF land resulted in massive ash, soot, and sediment runoff with summer rains, nearly eliminating fish and aquatic organisms from stream and ponds. Ash accumulations required immediate pond maintenance. Native fishes reinvaded from unidentified refugia and are currently repopulating West Turkey Creek, ECR ponds, and connecting waterways along with some non-native species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Biogeographic Significance

The [[Sulphur Springs Valley]] (including West Turkey Creek) is now endorheic but is included in the [[Rio Yaqui]] system based on historic connections. The [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]] (FWS 1994) embraces this basin as part of a broader southeastern Arizona "Area of Ecological Concern." Proximity to Mexico plus diversity of habitats in this "sky island" ecosystem provides for many rare or imperiled species in the Chiricahua Mountains.

### Aquatic Habitat Characteristics

Aquatic diversity far lower than terrestrial. Bedrock near surface results in rapid runoff with little groundwater storage. Most drainage channels ephemeral; West Turkey Creek mainstem becomes intermittent during drought between biseasonal precipitation periods (winter and late summer). Few seeps in tributaries and hillsides; West Turkey Creek is only natural surface water on ECR.

### Habitat Enhancement Through Water Management

Watershed storage and available water enhanced through:
- Watershed and riparian management
- Maintenance and development of aquatic habitats
- Seepage from ditches and ponds maintaining wetlands and high water tables within West Turkey Creek terraces
- Increased reliability of portions of West Turkey Creek during drought

Reach of permanent flow in West Turkey Creek increased over past decade with improved watershed management. Water quality enhanced by increased sediment entrapment above water-retention devices and infiltration through sediments and floodplains.

### Population Resilience

[[Yaqui Chub]] demonstrates high resilience (DeMarais and Minckley 1991, 1993). Populations expand rapidly from few tens to thousands in weeks/months. Large viable populations established in nearly all recovery reintroduction sites with greater success than other species (Hendrickson and Brooks 1991; Minckley 1995). Species persists for long periods under severe conditions and rebounds dramatically when conditions improve. Thousands lost through suffocation/poisoning from Rattlesnake Fire runoff; both dace and chub recruited immediately after event.

### Recovery Plan Alignment

HCP aligns with step-down objectives 2.2, 2.4.3, 2.5, 4.4, and 5.4 of the [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]] (FWS 1994). Primary Recovery Plan objective: stabilize and reintroduce Rio Yaqui fishes in waters within historic range in USA.

## Key Findings

### Alternatives Analyzed

**Alternative 1 - No Action:**
- Water diversion, ditch/structure use, pond maintenance continue for livestock watering
- Incidental take avoided where possible but might occur without permit
- Could jeopardize ranching operations and development
- Actions to enhance/recover chub hampered by lack of agreements
- Would result in restrictions on land use and conservation

**Alternative 2 - Proposed (Preferred) Action:**
- Direct issuance of Section 10(a)(1)(B) permit authorizing incidental take
- Implementation of Habitat Conservation Plan
- Allows continued ranching while offsetting adverse impacts
- Provides measures complementing Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan
- Selected because it allows ranching operations while supporting recovery

**Alternatives Considered but Rejected:**
1. Request removal of chubs from private lands - does not support recovery; unacceptable given applicant's conservation dedication
2. Petition to down- or delist - downlisting criteria not met; delisting unlikely due to limited USA range
3. Await regional HCP and Section 10(a)(1)(B) permit - delay does not serve best interests; regional HCP unlikely

### Anticipated Incidental Take

**Total take indeterminable** for all three Plan Species due to: difficulty finding dead fish, uncertain cause of death, unreliable population estimates due to sampling difficulties, losses masked by seasonal fluctuations, variable nature of habitats, and 25-year permit duration.

**Forms and Causes of Take:**

1. **Water Diversion:**
   - Direct take (mortality): rare, few individuals in natural habitat
   - Indirect take (habitat reduction): only under severe circumstances (drought); may be compensated by ponds/artificial structures
   - Injury/kill at diversions or control structures
   - Take: indeterminable

2. **Maintenance:**
   - Limited to low discharge periods when possible
   - Ditches cleaned periodically to remove sediment
   - Individual fish harassed/lost during maintenance/repair
   - Alterations in water quantity/quality
   - **Anticipated level exceeded when:** maintenance on >6.5 acres ponds and >0.5 miles ditches in one calendar year (average year considerably less)
   - All stream diversions and water gates may be maintained annually without exceeding take
   - Take: indeterminable

3. **Non-Native Game Fish Introduction (Big Tank):**
   - Take through predation or competition
   - Occurs when listed species move downstream into Big Tank or non-natives escape
   - Take relatively high in years of high Plan Species reproduction; functionally zero in low reproduction years
   - Take: indeterminable

4. **Yaqui Catfish Introduction:**
   - Similar amounts of incidental take as non-native game fish
   - Catfish may prey on other fish if cooccur
   - Likelihood of take low

5. **Additional Non-Native Introductions:**
   - Purposeful, negligent, or population expansion of existing species
   - Generally beyond applicant control
   - Current problem: many non-natives survived Rattlesnake Fire and expanding
   - Removal/control actions result in incidental take

6. **Emergency Renovations:**
   - Far greater take than "normal" year
   - Example: explosive spread of newly introduced non-native requiring rapid eradication
   - **Safeguards:** Before renovation, backup populations representing ≥50% total estimated numbers (including all known genetic diversity) demonstrated in other uncontaminated habitats or established
   - Every effort made to remove Plan Species to other locations before renovation
   - All Plan Species in contaminated pond/ditch undergo take (harassment if moved; kill if remain during renovation)

### Habitat Conservation Plan Provisions

**Duration:** 25 years from permit issuance

**Geographic Scope:** All ECR private land and Forest Service grazing allotment in West Turkey Creek watershed

**Applicant Commitments:**

1. **Water Diversion Management:**
   - Maintain balance of water supply in West Turkey Creek and ponds
   - Promote Plan Species survival
   - Maintain water levels and biological conditions dependent on environmental conditions

2. **Routine Maintenance:**
   - At applicant expense on ECR
   - Ensure all components in good repair, minimal excessive leakage
   - Shutoff/manipulations allow fish to reach refuge areas
   - Contact partners (Arizona Game and Fish Department, FWS, Forest Service) when drying/conditions cause high extirpation probability
   - Relocate fish to maintain population size and genetic variability
   - Considered in-kind contribution to recovery

3. **Habitat Provision:**
   - Maintain water levels, nutrients, water quality, biological requirements
   - Prevent habitat/ecosystem degradation
   - Continue until impossible due to drought or reasons beyond control
   - Ponds serve as significant habitat, refuges, sources for natural/purposeful repopulation
   - Eliminate non-indigenous fishes; reduce non-indigenous competitors/predators

4. **Grazing Management:**
   - Current grazing plan (FS 1993) limits adverse impacts on riparian/aquatic habitats
   - Potential modifications addressed post-HCP signature
   - Confer with Forest Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department, FWS, external advisors
   - Regular review of operations
   - Forest Service continues implementing current plan complementing HCP

5. **Watershed Protection - Four Major Concerns:**
   - Prohibition/prevention of non-indigenous species introduction/establishment (except non-reproducing game fish in Big Tank); facilitate removal of existing populations
   - Examination of water quantity/quality changes related to recreation, development on federal lands
   - Continued examination/evaluation of grazing practices
   - Increased cooperation in wildfire fuel and fire management

6. **Native Species Management:**
   - Other Rio Yaqui fishes may be reintroduced: Arizona stoneroller, Yaqui catfish, Yaqui sucker, beautiful shiner, Yaqui topminnow
   - Yaqui chub may be augmented for genetics/management
   - Yaqui catfish reintroduction considered post-permit issuance subject to schedules, funding, approval
   - (Other species may require additional NEPA compliance, HCP amendment, ESA compliance)

7. **Monitoring Program:**

   **Objectives:**
   - Monitor populations to ensure viability
   - Monitor habitat to ensure species needs met and properly functioning ecosystem
   - Monitor during take-causing actions to ensure anticipated take not exceeded and minimization effective
   - Respond to threats and emergencies (drought, non-native invasion)

   **Implementation:**
   - Monitoring plan written by Arizona Game and Fish Department and FWS, reviewed by Forest Service, approved by applicant
   - Can be changed over time with all parties' agreement without HCP/permit amendment
   - Professional fishery biologists conduct monitoring
   - Coordinated between private and public lands

   **Frequency:**
   - Annual minimum, preferably autumn (evaluate adults and seasonal reproduction)
   - 5-year intervals: thorough linear survey of West Turkey Creek through HCP area
   - 10-year review to determine if program meets goals

   **Responsibility:**
   - Arizona Game and Fish Department and FWS co-leads
   - Forest Service on Forest Service lands
   - Agencies conduct when adequate funding/personnel available
   - When agencies lack resources, applicant ensures monitoring on private lands (annual expenditure not to exceed $500)
   - Qualified biologists conduct monitoring

   **Sites:**
   - Annual: ponds and several West Turkey Creek localities on private and leased lands
   - Opportunistic: after natural/operational changes affecting habitat/populations

   **Techniques:**
   - Variety determined by on-site conditions
   - Consistent with San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge methods for comparability
   - Minimal requirements: species composition, relative abundance, catch-per-unit effort when practicable
   - Consistent effort, season, locations, techniques for index and trend data

   **Emergency Response:**
   - If extirpation danger exists: immediately inform partners, intensive monitoring, evaluate/develop remedial actions
   - Plan identifies party responsible for which actions
   - All applicable FWS policies (current and future) implemented

   **Take Monitoring:**
   - Applicant responsibility
   - Information: action taken, dead fish count by species, extent of aquatic habitat affected (linear feet ditch, pond acres)
   - $500 expenditure limit does not apply to take monitoring or emergency response
