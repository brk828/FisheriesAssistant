# Studies of the Abundance, Distribution and Ecology of the Arizona Native Trout (*Salmo gila*) in the White River Drainage

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #Salmo-gila #Apache-Trout #native-trout-conservation

## Executive Summary
This 1960 Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report documents a field survey of seventeen stream sites within the [[White River Drainage]] and [[Black River Drainage]] on the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] to assess the distribution and abundance of the [[Arizona Native Trout]] (*Salmo gila*, Miller). Electric shocking gear was used to sample fish populations across ten primary tributary streams. While all streams surveyed showed evidence of native-type trout or native-rainbow hybrid populations, taxonomic identification by [[Robert R. Miller]] (Curator of Fishes, University of Michigan) confirmed that only the population in **Ord Creek** met the morphological criteria for a genetically pure *Salmo gila*. The study concludes that introgressive hybridization with introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]] represents the primary existential threat to the persistence of this native species, and it recommends conservation measures including stocking moratoriums and habitat isolation.

## Background & Description
- **Taxonomic identity**: *Salmo gila* (Miller) — Arizona native trout, distinguished from introduced *Salmo* species by a specific combination of diagnostic characteristics assessed by expert taxonomic examination.
- **Pure vs. hybrid distinction**: Fish were classified based on possession (or absence) of the "necessary combination of characteristics" required for pure-species status; specimens lacking full purity were classified as crosses or backcrosses with [[Rainbow Trout]] or [[Cutthroat Trout]].
- **Field-level phenotype**: Fish from most surveyed streams were described as "basically of native type," lacking strong rainbow or cutthroat characteristics, though not confirmed as genetically pure.
- **Growth and behavior (hatchery observations at Pinetop Hatchery)**:
  - Growth rates comparable to [[Rainbow Trout]].
  - Tolerates close confinement and handling without notable difficulty.
  - Readily strikes artificial lures and flies, similar to rainbow trout — relevant to angling/sport fishery potential.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: High-elevation, cold-water montane streams within the upper [[White River Drainage]] and [[Black River Drainage]] systems in east-central Arizona.
- **Distribution surveyed**: Ten streams on the Fort Apache Indian Reservation:
  - [[Little Bonito Creek]]
  - [[Crooked Creek]]
  - [[Lofer Ciénega]]
  - [[Flash Creek]]
  - [[Squaw Creek]]
  - [[Big Bonito Creek]]
  - [[Hughy Creek]]
  - [[Pacheta Creek]]
  - [[Ord Creek]] — *only site with confirmed pure native trout*
  - [[East Fork White River]]
- **Drainage connectivity**: All streams checked were tributaries of the [[Black River]] except East Fork and Ord Creek, which drain directly into the [[White River]].
- **Sympatric/competing species**: [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]] — both capable of hybridizing with *Salmo gila*, occupying overlapping habitat due to historical stocking practices.
- **Limiting factor**: Hybridization (introgression) rather than habitat loss or other abiotic stressors is identified as the dominant ecological threat limiting distribution of genetically distinct native trout populations.

## Key Findings
1. **Survey scope**: 17 stream areas checked across the White River and Black River drainages using a 115V AC, 750-watt portable electric generator (electrofishing).
2. **Widespread hybridization**: All 17 streams contained either native-type trout or hybrid (native × rainbow/cutthroat) populations — no stream except Ord Creek yielded a population confirmed as taxonomically pure.
3. **Ord Creek significance**: Identified by Dr. Robert R. Miller as the sole location harboring a genetically pure representative of *Salmo gila*, making it a critical conservation priority site.
4. **Primary threat**: Hybridization with introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]] is the singular identified factor limiting the persistence and distribution of pure native trout populations — no other limiting ecological factors (e.g., water quality, temperature, habitat degradation) were emphasized in this study.
5. **Management recommendations**:
   - Cease further stocking of rainbow or cutthroat trout near known native trout populations.
   - Screen any newly constructed lakes in proximity to native trout habitat to prevent hybrid species incursion.
   - Propagate native trout in hatcheries (e.g., [[Pinetop Hatchery]]) for reintroduction into streams that can be effectively isolated from hybridizing species.
   - Continue survey efforts to locate additional pure or economically restorable native trout populations.
6. **Aquacultural viability**: Hatchery-held native trout exhibited growth rates and handling tolerance comparable to rainbow trout, indicating strong potential for propagation-based conservation and possible recreational fishery development.
7. **Methodological limitation**: Due to remoteness of survey sites, no location was sampled more than once, and limnological (water chemistry/habitat) data collection was deprioritized in favor of species presence/absence sampling.
