# Etheostoma ditrema, a New Darter of the Subgenus Oligocephalus (Percidae) from Springs of the Alabama River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

The paper by [[John S. Ramsey]] and [[Royal D. Suttkus]] (Tulane University) formally describes *Etheostoma (Oligocephalus) ditrema*, the Coldwater Darter, a small, sexually dimorphic percid. The description is based on 133 specimens from three localities in the upper [[Coosa-Alabama River Basin]]. The species is restricted to springs and spring-fed ponds above the [[Fall Line]]. It is most closely related to, but sharply differentiated from, [[Etheostoma swaini]] of the eastern Gulf Coastal Plain. Its unusual retention of two coronal pores suggests it is a paedomorphic member of the [[Etheostoma asprigene Species Group]].

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The subgenus [[Oligocephalus]] is the most complex and speciose group within the North American darter genus [[Etheostoma]]. [[Bailey and Gosline (1955)]] delimited the group and recognized 19 nominal species. Subsequent revisions, including the recognition of *E. lepidum*, the revival of *E. okaloosae*, and the reduction of *E. spilotum* to a subspecies of *E. sagitta*, brought the count to 21 with the redescription of *E. hopkinsi* by [[Bailey and Richards (1963)]].

**Nomenclatural history.** [[Jordan (1879)]] first collected the species in millponds near Rome, Floyd Co., Georgia, but misidentified it as *Boleichthys elegans*, probably a synonym of *E. grahami*. A single surviving specimen from Jordan's collection, held by the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia (ANSP), was later examined by the authors.

**Type material.** The holotype (TU 35703) is an adult male, 33.9 mm standard length, collected 18 July 1962 in a spring flowing into a tributary of Mills Creek, Chattooga County, Georgia. Paratopotypes were drawn from several dates between 1962 and 1964. Additional paratypes include a specimen from the Rome millpond (ANSP 20649) and a third population from Coldwater Creek, Calhoun County, Alabama, which was not designated as type material.

**Diagnostic characters.**
- Small, moderately robust body; largest male 42.1 mm and largest female 40.7 mm standard length; adulthood apparently reached at about 25 mm.
- Incomplete, moderately arched lateral line.
- Usually two coronal pores, a distinctive feature within the subgenus.
- Usually complete infraorbital canal and interrupted supratemporal canal.
- Dorsal fin rays VIII to XII plus 9 to 12; anal rays II, 6 to 8; pectoral rays 11 to 13; vertebrae 35 to 37.
- Nuptial tubercles absent.
- Breeding males dark brown with orange on the belly and lower caudal peduncle; females indistinctly mottled; submarginal orange band in the spinous dorsal fin of both sexes.
- Three dark caudal blotches; humeral spot absent.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species is restricted to springs and spring-fed ponds above the fall line in the Coosa-Alabama drainage. The holotype locality is a spring feeding a small tributary of Mills Creek (Chattooga River system), and the Alabama population occurs in Coldwater Spring and Coldwater Creek, a tributary of Choccolocco Creek. Aquatic mosses were identified from the type locality by Winona H. Welch, providing the only habitat-associated biological information in the document. Comparative material of *E. swaini* came from the Alabama River drainage, including Montgomery, Clarke and Dallas counties. The paper notes that geographic variation in *E. swaini* and the *asprigene* complex is treated separately.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Etheostoma ditrema* is described on the basis of 133 specimens from three localities in the upper Coosa-Alabama basin.
- **Habitat restriction:** The species occurs only in springs and spring-fed ponds above the fall line.
- **Relationship:** It is closely related to, but distinct from, *E. swaini*.
- **Unique coronal pore condition:** Two coronal pores are present in most specimens, because the two coronal canal branches do not fuse. This is unusual among percids and supports a paedomorphic origin within the *asprigene* group.
- **Scale counts:** Total lateral-line scales range from 41 to 54 (usually 44 to 50); pored scales 19 to 35; unpored scales 13 to 30.
- **Infraspecific variation:** Breast squamation varies. Most Chattooga and Etowah specimens have exposed breast scales, whereas only one of 16 Coldwater Spring specimens does.
- **Historical misidentification:** Jordan's 1879 material was assigned to *Boleichthys elegans* and is now considered a probable synonym of *E. grahami*.
- **Comparative data:** Scale-row distributions (Table 2) show that *E. ditrema* and *E. swaini* differ in lateral-line and scale counts across drainages.
