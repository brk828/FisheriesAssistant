# Preliminary Checklist of Marine Fishes of the Chiapas Coast (1959–1960 Shrimp-Trawl Collections)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #marine-fish-checklist

## Executive Summary

This document is a preliminary annotated checklist of marine fishes from the coast of [[Chiapas]], Mexico, published by the [[Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Biológico Pesqueras]] (I.N.I.B.P.) under the Secretaría de Industria y Comercio. The material was collected from shrimp-fishing vessels in August and September 1959 and in April, May and June 1960 by biologist [[Manuel Flores Villegas]]. The list records 66 numbered taxa in 36 families and is presented as the total known marine catch for the region at the time. It is the first in a planned series of I.N.I.B.P. publications, and it serves as a foundation for later work on the fauna accompanying commercial shrimp and for monographic studies of commercially important species.

## Background & Description

The publication was prepared by [[Ernesto Ramírez H.]] (head of the Division of Marine Vertebrates), [[Guillermo Carrillo]], and [[Daniel Lluch B.]] (assistant biologist). The introduction states that the Institute's Division of Marine Vertebrates was charged with cataloguing the fishes of Mexico's coasts. Related research topics named in the text include distribution and ecology, fecundity, feeding habits, and fisheries management.

The list is a summary, not a full taxonomic treatment. The authors state that time limits in the research program dictated the abbreviated format, and that the next phase would move beyond species lists toward monographic study of commercially valuable species, with technical assistance to fishers and industry guiding the research priorities.

Collections were mostly made aboard shrimp vessels that operated at 15 to 28 brazas (fathoms) and no more than 16 miles from the Chiapas coast. The authors describe the work as a starting point for studying the bycatch fauna of shrimp species.

The list is organized by family in numbered order. Each entry gives the scientific name with its authority and one or more Spanish common names, many of them local. Taxonomic names follow mid-20th-century usage. Several names in the source appear as older synonyms, so modern nomenclature should be checked before the list is used.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fauna is characteristic of tropical eastern Pacific shelf waters, and the collection depths and distances from shore place the sampled community on the continental shelf, within the reach of shrimp trawls. The taxa include cartilaginous fishes (sharks, rays, and skates), coastal and estuarine-associated fishes (e.g., [[Centropomus]] snooks, [[Mugil curema]] mullets, and ariid catfishes), reef- and sand-associated perciforms, and flatfishes. Several species are noted by the authors as being of high commercial value, which is the basis for their call for further biological studies (feeding, reproduction, and conservation).

No environmental variables (temperature, salinity, sediment, or catch per unit effort) are reported in the document. Catch numbers and abundance data are also absent. The checklist records presence only.

## Key Findings

- **Scope and sampling:** 66 numbered taxa in 36 families were recorded from shrimp-fishery catches on the Chiapas coast, collected August–September 1959 and April–June 1960. The list is described as the total marine catch of the region to that date.
- **Depth and distance:** Catches came from 15–28 brazas and no more than 16 miles offshore.
- **Dominant families by number of taxa:** Sciaenidae (e.g., *Cynoscion reticulatus*, *Micropogon altipinnis*, *Isopisthus remifer*), Carangidae (e.g., *Caranx hippos*, *Oligoplites* spp., *Chloroscombrus orqueta*), Lutjanidae (*Lutianus guttatus*, *L. argentiventris*, *L. jordani*), Pomadasyidae (*Pomadasys* spp., *Brachydeuterus leuciscus*), and Pleuronectidae plus Soleidae and Cynoglossidae flatfishes.
- **Commercially important taxa highlighted:** [[Centropomus robalito]] (Robalo Blanco), [[Mugil curema]] (Lisa Blanca), [[Caranx hippos]] (Jurel), [[Coryphaena hippurus]] (Dorado), [[Scomberomorus maculatus]] (Sierra), and the snappers *Lutianus* spp. (Huachinango).
- **Shark and ray diversity:** Four cartilaginous families are listed, including *Mustelus dorsalis* (Carcharhinidae), *Rhinobatos leucorhynchus* (Rhinobatidae), *Urotrygon mundus* (Trygonidae), and *Narcine entemedor* (Torpedinidae).
- **Local names:** Many taxa carry several Spanish vernacular names (e.g., Huachinango, Lenguado, Mojarra, Roncador), reflecting regional fisher terminology.
- **Future direction:** The I.N.I.B.P. states that work will continue with taxonomy, distribution, biology of commercial species, and monographic studies, and that similar regional surveys will follow in other Mexican states.
- **Source base:** The checklist cites 11 references, including [[Jordan and Evermann]] (1896–1900), [[Meek and Hildebrand]] (1923–1928), [[Hildebrand]] (1946 and 1947), [[Berdegué]] (1956), and [[Ramírez G. and Sevilla]] (1963).
- **Publication details:** Printed April 29, 1964, in an edition of 1,000 copies.
