# A Key to the British Species of Malacostraca Occurring in Fresh Water

#carcinology #marine-biology #malacostraca

## Executive Summary

This Freshwater Biological Association publication (Scientific Publication No. 19, 1960) is a taxonomic key to the British Malacostraca that live in fresh water. Its authors are [[Hynes]], [[Macan]] and Williams, working with several contributors. The key is restricted to species known to breed and live permanently in fresh water in Britain, which excludes most brackish-water forms. Its scope covers British species and records only. It addresses [[Syncarida]], [[Decapoda]], [[Mysidacea]], [[Amphipoda]] and [[Isopoda]]. Distribution maps were possible only for the Amphipoda and Isopoda, and the authors invite further data on other Crustacea.

## Background & Description

The Malacostraca are predominantly marine. Of their five sub-classes, only Syncarida, Peracarida and Eucarida contain freshwater species. The Syncarida and Eucarida are described first in prose because their freshwater members are few and easily recognised. The Peracarida, which hold most of the freshwater diversity, are keyed in full.

- **[[Syncarida]]:** represented in Britain by a single rather atypical species, [[Bathynella Natans]] (synonym *B. chappuisi*). It is eyeless, colourless, subterranean and no more than about 1.5 mm long, with no carapace. It is known from Devon, Wiltshire, Berkshire, Oxfordshire and Yorkshire.
- **[[Decapoda]]:** only the sub-order Macrura and Brachyura are discussed.
  - [[Palaemonetes Varians]] is a prawn tolerant of fresh water as an adult. It occurs in marsh pools and drainage dykes, mainly in coastal and estuarine areas of southern and eastern England, Lancashire, Anglesey and south Wales. It is absent from the Isle of Man.
  - [[Eriocheir Sinensis]] (Chinese mitten crab) breeds in the sea but travels far up rivers. It was first noted in Europe in the Weser in 1912 and was a pest in Germany by 1927.
  - The British crayfish is [[Astacus Pallipes]]. A translated key to the Continental *Astacus* species is included because introductions are possible. The document notes that *A. astacus* is the species most likely to have been introduced.
- **[[Mysidacea]]:** [[Mysis Relicta]] is the only true freshwater mysid. [[Neomysis Integer]] is brackish and is found in fresh water only where the sea has recently reached.
- **[[Amphipoda]]:** the keyed group with the most species, covering [[Gammarus]], [[Corophium]], [[Orchestia]], [[Crangonyx]] and [[Niphargus]].
- **[[Isopoda]]:** only [[Asellus]] breeds in fresh water. It has three British species.

The key uses the criterion of breeding and living throughout life in fresh water to separate freshwater from brackish species. The authors note that this line is hard to draw in a group that inhabits brackish water so widely.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Subterranean and interstitial fauna:** several species are known from wells, caves, springs and river gravels. [[Bathynella Natans]] is probably an inhabitant of interstitial water in gravels, and may be widespread in the permanent water table. [[Niphargus]] species occur in the same settings, and [[Asellus Cavaticus]] is recorded from wells and caves in southern England and south Wales.
- **Glacial relicts:** [[Mysis Relicta]] is thought to be an arctic estuarine species spread by the last ice age and isolated afterwards. It is restricted to cold water, and in the British Isles it is recorded from Ennerdale Water, several Irish loughs and the Shannon and Bann rivers.
- **Salinity and brackish influence:** several species are linked to brackish water. [[Gammarus Duebeni]] is found in brackish water with weak currents, and [[Gammarus Tigrinus]] and [[Gammarus Zaddachi]] are coastal brackish species that also enter fresh water where pollution has raised ion content. [[Neomysis Integer]] persists in isolated pools on the Wirral, probably cut off by a sea wall about a century ago.
- **Water chemistry and pollution:** [[Gammarus Pulex]] has not been found in water with pH below 5.8 and is excluded from brackish influence. [[Crangonyx Pseudogracilis]] tolerates saline and polluted water. [[Asellus Aquaticus]] is more tolerant of organic pollution than [[Asellus Meridianus]]. [[Eriocheir Sinensis]] may be sensitive to pollution, which might explain why it was not established in the Thames.
- **Altitude:** [[Gammarus Pulex]] extends into mountain streams, for example at Brownrigg Well near the summit of Helvellyn at 2850 ft.
- **Terrestrial-margin habitats:** [[Orchestia Bottae]] lives under stones just above the water's edge on river and canal banks.
- **Biogeography:** several species have spread into Britain or are still spreading. [[Crangonyx Pseudogracilis]] is a North American species that has been introduced recently. [[Gammarus Pulex]] has been introduced into the Bann watershed in Ireland. [[Gammarus Tigrinus]] was shown to be the American *G. fasciatus*.

## Key Findings

- **Scope and method:** the key covers only British species and records, and it treats criteria of breeding and life in fresh water as the basis for inclusion. It is built on a dichotomous format, with figures for diagnostic structures (antennae, gnathopods, telson, uropods, pleopods). Sex identification is needed for some groups, and the authors note that males are generally easier to identify.
- **Species count and composition:** the group contains about 30 British freshwater taxa. The most species-rich genera are *Gammarus* (six keyed species) and *Niphargus* (four keyed species, with two subspecies of *N. kochianus*). *Asellus* has three species.
- **Eriocheir spread:** the authors expect the Chinese mitten crab to return to British shores, and warn that if it entered an unpolluted river it could become as numerous as on the Continent. It is recognised by the dense hairs on its claws.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** the errata sheet corrects the legend of Fig. 12, reverting the *Crangonyx* names so that *C. subterraneus* and *C. vejdovskyi* are assigned correctly, and changes one Devon record to gravel sites at Whitchurch.
- **Recent taxonomic revisions:** Hynes (1954b) showed that British *G. tigrinus* was identical with the American *G. fasciatus*. Bousfield (1958) later revived *G. tigrinus* for the brackish species and retained *G. fasciatus* for a freshwater species.
- **Distribution data:** distribution maps were possible only for Amphipoda and Isopoda (Figs. 35 and 36). Notable records include *Orchestia bottae*, first taken in England in 1942, and *Asellus meridianus*, which is the only species recorded from the Isle of Man and several offshore islands.
- **Gaps:** the authors state that detailed ecology of *Asellus* is poorly known, and that information on *Corophium curvispinum* var. *devium* was not confirmed at its Avon site in April 1960.
- **Conservation and planning:** the authors invite collection of distribution data for all Crustacea, to be sent to the Association for a future edition.
