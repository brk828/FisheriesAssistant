## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Hiawatha Forest National Fish Hatchery**, a substation of the **[[Pendills Creek National Fish Hatchery]]**, operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 3) near Brimley, Michigan. The facility's sole mission is the propagation and rearing of **[[Lake Trout]]** (*Salvelinus namaycush*) fingerlings in support of the **[[Great Lakes Rehabilitation Program]]**. Annual production totals approximately 800,000 spring-stocked fingerlings and 250,000 fall-stocked fingerlings, with fish distributed to federal waters in Michigan, Wisconsin, and Minnesota via **[[Lake Superior]]** shore plantings. The station operates under shared funding/staffing with the main Pendills Creek station and reported 3,113 visitors and a 768,900-fish yearling plant during FY93.

## Background & Description

While this document is an administrative station profile rather than a morphological study, it references specific life-stage classes of **[[Lake Trout]]** relevant to hatchery production and identification:

- **Fingerling stage**: Primary production target (800,000 spring release; 250,000 fall release).
- **Yearling stage**: Fish subjected to fin-clipping prior to stocking (768,900 yearlings clipped and planted in FY93).
- **Fin-clipping**: A morphological marking technique used to distinguish hatchery-origin lake trout post-release for stock assessment; the FY93 clipping operation (March 22–May 3, 1993) processed 769,500 fish total.

No morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data (length, weight, fin ray counts) are provided in the source text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Reared in sixteen concrete raceways at the hatchery facility before release into natural systems.
- **Release environment**: Stocked directly into **[[Lake Superior]]** via shore planting by truck — no barge or aerial stocking methods used at this station.
- **Geographic distribution of stocked fish**: Federal waters spanning Michigan, Wisconsin, and Minnesota (all within the Lake Superior basin).
- **Ecological role**: Lake trout serve as a keystone piscivore in Great Lakes food webs; this hatchery's output directly supports restoration of native lake trout populations decimated historically by overfishing and **[[Sea Lamprey]]** predation, central to the broader **[[Great Lakes Rehabilitation Program]]**.
- **Land/water infrastructure**: Facility includes an effluent abatement tank, sludge tank, and sludge holding pond, indicating managed wastewater discharge affecting local watershed water quality.

## Key Findings

- **Single-species mission**: Facility exclusively rears **[[Lake Trout]]** for Great Lakes restocking; no other species are produced.
- **Production trend (numbers distributed, excluding eggs/fry)**:
  - FY93: 768,900
  - FY92: 760,500
  - FY91: 791,500
  - Distribution shows relative stability (~760,000–790,000 fish/year) over the three-year reporting window, all delivered to FWS federal waters (none to state or private waters).
- **Stocking methodology**: All FY93 releases were shore-planted by truck directly into Lake Superior.
- **Fin-clipping program**: 769,500 fish clipped in spring 1993 (March 22–May 3) as a marking method for post-release tracking; 768,900 of these yearlings were subsequently planted.
- **Administrative structure**: Operates as a substation under **[[Pendills Creek National Fish Hatchery]]**; all operations, maintenance funding, and FTE staffing are consolidated under the main station's budget rather than tracked independently.
- **Staffing**: Minimal dedicated staff identified — Roger Gordon (Fishery Biologist).
- **Physical infrastructure**: Includes hatchery building (office, lab, cooler, crewroom, shop, four-stall garage, boiler room), fin-clipping building, oil house, effluent abatement/sludge systems, generator building, sixteen concrete raceways, two metal storage buildings, and two staff residences.
- **Identified capital needs**: 
  - Replacement of raceway dam boards with slide gates (~$8,000)
  - Rehabilitation of the fin-clipping building with tanks, pumps, and incubators to support a future **[[Broodstock]]** strain development program (~$6,000)
- **Visitation**: 3,113 visitors recorded in FY93, concentrated in warmer months, consistent with patterns at the main Pendills Creek station.
- **Political/administrative context**: Station falls within Michigan's 11th Congressional District; represented by Senators Carl Levin and Donald Riegle, Representative Bart Stupak, and relevant state legislators — included as standard station-profile metadata rather than ecological data.
