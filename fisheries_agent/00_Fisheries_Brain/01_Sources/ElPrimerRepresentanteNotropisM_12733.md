# Notropis Moralesi: The First Cyprinid Record in the Neotropical Fauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1956 taxonomic paper by [[Fernando De Buen]], published in the *Anales del Instituto de Biologia* (Mexico), describes [[Notropis Moralesi]] as a new species of minnow. The type series was collected from the small [[El Capulin Reservoir]] near Tepelneme, Oaxaca, in the upper [[Papaloapan River]] basin. The author regards it as the first member of the family [[Cyprinidae]] recorded from the [[Neotropical Realm]], which had previously been considered Nearctic in Mexico. The paper combines a detailed morphometric and meristic description, sexual and size-related variation, a comparison with related Mexican *Notropis*, and a biogeographic discussion.

## Background & Description

**Study context.** The specimens were collected during a field trip under the auspices of the Comisión del Alto Papaloapan, with engineer [[Salvador Morales]], for whom the species is named. Fish were examined at the Laboratorio de Vertebrados of the [[Instituto De Biologia]].

**Type material.**
- Holotype: 73 mm total length (TL), 60 mm standard length (SL), deposited at the Instituto de Biologia.
- Paratypes: 33 specimens, 40–79 mm TL and 30.5–61.5 mm SL, distributed among several Mexican institutions and the Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan.
- Total collected on the date given in the text: 34 specimens, fixed in formalin.

**Diagnostic morphology (holotype, as reported).**
- Body short and stocky; greatest body depth 23.3% SL (SL is 4.28 times depth); greatest width 17.5% SL.
- Head 27.5% SL. Snout blunt, mouth inferior, fleshy cheeks.
- Head naked of scales; eye diameter 21.2% of head length.
- Lateral line with 38 scales in the longitudinal series; 10 scales in the transverse series between anal and dorsal fins.
- Dorsal fin with 8 branched rays; anal fin with 8 branched rays; pectoral fin with 13 rays; pelvic fin with 8 rays; 19 central caudal rays.
- Males show rows of blunt tubercles on the distal part of the first five pectoral rays, interpreted as a secondary sexual character used to hold females during spawning.
- Coloration: pale olive back, silvery-white belly, a diffuse dark lateral band, and a dark dorsal stripe. Blood-red ventral coloration appears only on adults in breeding condition.

**Internal anatomy (two dissected paratypes).**
- Female, 80 mm TL / 64 mm SL, with mature gonads: swim bladder in two chambers, the anterior making up 57% of the bladder; intestine with two sharp bends; 38 vertebrae (formula 21 + 17); nearly spherical otoliths.
- Second paratype, 64 mm TL / 49 mm SL: long, densely packed gill lamellae; reduced gill rakers.
- Pharyngeal teeth were difficult to observe and were apparently absent or lost in this species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir conditions (visit of 26 February 1955).**
- Maximum depth 5.5 m; mean depth about 3 m; volume about 6,000 m³.
- Built on a limestone bed; water clear during visits but likely turbid with sediment in heavy rains.
- Banks sparsely vegetated, few trees giving shade, and few aquatic plants.
- Surface water temperature uniform at 17.5 °C, both near the dam wall and farther out; air 20 °C; dissolved oxygen 6.4 cc (units as given).

**Habitat of collection.** Cyprinids were abundant in a small artificial pit at the foot of the dam with a permanent water current. Fish were caught with improvised nets.

**Population structure.** Pooling all 34 specimens from 24 February 1955 yields two size classes:
- Juveniles: 38–46.5 mm TL (30–36.5 mm SL).
- Adults: 56–79 mm TL (44–61.5 mm SL).

The published length-frequency table lists counts of 6, 6, 1, 0, 5, 4, 5, 6 and 2 across nine size bins, which sum to 35 rather than the 34 stated in the text. The discrepancy is noted here as it appears in the source.

**Reproduction.**
- Most females on 24 February 1955 had swollen bellies, and dissected females held formed eggs, though in small numbers.
- Males appeared less advanced in gonad development, though the author notes this rests on few dissections.
- Spawning is inferred to run from late winter to early summer. Post-larvae and juveniles were seen along the margins on 19 and 24 February and 24 March 1955.

**Sexual dimorphism.** Females have a broad genital pore with a median crest that reaches the first anal ray. Males have a small, short pore set apart from the anal fin origin. Females are deeper-bodied when gravid; males are smaller with a shorter anal base and longer anal rays.

**Ontogenetic change.** Juveniles are paler with a less developed lateral band. The adult ventral red coloration is absent or faint in juveniles. The intestine lengthens with growth.

## Key Findings

1. **New species.** *Notropis moralesi* is described from a single reservoir in the upper Papaloapan basin, based on a holotype and 33 paratypes.

2. **Biogeographic first.** The author states that this is the first Cyprinidae species recorded from the Neotropical region, a notable extension, since the family had been treated as Nearctic in Mexico. The author also notes that penetration is probably limited based on current knowledge.

3. **Meristic variation.** Across the type series:
   - Longitudinal scale count 36–42, most commonly 38–39 (70% of cases).
   - Transverse scale count usually 9 (71%).
   - Branched dorsal rays usually 8 (82%).
   - Anal principal rays usually 8 (84%).
   - Pectoral rays usually 13 (88%).
   - Pelvic rays usually 8 (90%).

4. **Relationships.** The species is placed in *Notropis* on the basis of characters including the absence of barbels, a short intestine, reduced gill rakers, and fewer than 40 longitudinal scales. Among Mexican *Notropis* with a blunt snout and a superior mouth, it is considered closest to [[Notropis Boucardi]] rather than [[Notropis Braytoni]], based on dorsal fin origin position and anal ray count. Other comparisons were made with [[Notropis Simus Orca]] and [[Notropis Nazas]].

5. **Differences from N. boucardi.** Per the author, *N. moralesi* differs in:
   - Pointed (vs. blunt) pectoral fins, and a longer pectoral fin (76% vs. 62% of the distance to the anal fin origin).
   - Anterior swim bladder chamber 57% of the bladder vs. 45%.
   - Longer dorsal and anal rays and thinner lips.
   - A sharp, well-defined lateral band (vs. pale and diffuse).

6. **Taxonomic caution.** The author considers the possibility that *N. boucardi*, *N. braytoni* and *N. moralesi* could represent subspecies of one variable species, but declines to adopt that view without more data, following the prevailing classification.

7. **Biogeographic pattern of Mexican Notropis.** Species are abundant in northern Mexico, absent from a central zone the author calls the "islote de Algansea," and reappear in the Balsas and, with this discovery, the Papaloapan basins.

8. **Methodological basis.** Measurements follow standard length ratios, with counts of scales, fin rays, vertebrae and gill structures, and the author's comparison with the original description of *Leuciscus boucardi* by Günther (1868) and the redescription by Regan (1908). Additional comparative material of *N. boucardi* from Oaxtepec, Morelos, was supplied by [[Leopoldo Navarro]].

9. **Conservation or management implications.** The source does not discuss conservation status, threats, or management recommendations.
