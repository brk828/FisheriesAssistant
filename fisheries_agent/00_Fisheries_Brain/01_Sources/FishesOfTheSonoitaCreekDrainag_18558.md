# Fishes of the Sonoita Creek Drainage, Santa Cruz County, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #conservation #historical-fauna

## Executive Summary

This compilation by W.L. Minckley documents the fish fauna of the [[Sonoita Creek]] drainage in [[Santa Cruz County]], [[Arizona]], based on six major collection periods from 1904 to 1966. The document provides critical historical documentation of faunal changes over 62 years, revealing significant declines and extirpations of native species alongside introductions of non-native fishes. Of particular conservation concern are the [[Monkey Spring Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon* sp.), a unique differentiated form restricted to [[Monkey Spring]] on the Rail-X Ranch, and the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), last collected from mainstream [[Sonoita Creek]] in 1904. The introduced [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) is identified as a threat to native fauna through competition and predation.

## Background & Description

Collections were made during six major periods: 1904, 1927-28, 1933, 1959, 1963, and 1966, with data from Chamberlain (1904), Miller (1961), and unpublished records. The drainage supports both native and introduced species across various habitat types including permanent stream reaches, springs, and artificial ponds.

**Native Species:**

- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): One of Arizona's most resistant native fishes, persisting in drying streams. Present in almost all areas of permanent water through 1966.

- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*): Not recorded in mainstream [[Sonoita Creek]] but common in [[Monkey Spring]] and associated pond on Rail-X Ranch through 1966. Formerly widespread in southern Arizona until the 1950s, now very rare.

- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Characteristic of headwater montane streams. Rare in southern Arizona and extremely rare or possibly extinct in [[Sonoita Creek]] (last collected 1963).

- **[[Sonoran Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*): Last collected 1904. Now rare or extinct south of mountain tributaries of the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]].

- **[[Mountain Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*): [[Sonoita Creek]] and the [[San Pedro River]] near Charleston represent the last strongholds of this species in southern Arizona (present through 1966).

- **[[Mexican Channel Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*): Introduced in late 1800s on Rail-X Ranch. Maintained populations in ponds until recently but appeared to have disappeared by 1959.

- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*): Last collected 1927. Extinct throughout most of former range in southern Arizona; possibly still present in Mexican portion of [[San Pedro River]].

- **[[Monkey Spring Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon* sp.): A differentiated form unique to [[Monkey Spring]] on Rail-X Ranch, closely related to *C. macularius*. Status under study as of 1966.

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*): Last taken in mainstream [[Sonoita Creek]] in 1904. Formerly distributed throughout [[Gila River]] basin from near Yuma to [[San Francisco Hot Spring]] in New Mexico, now restricted to [[Monkey Spring]].

**Introduced Species:**

- **[[Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*): First collected 1963 from pond on Rail-X Ranch. Implicated in decline and disappearance of many native fishes through rapid population build-up, competition, and possibly predation on indigenous fauna.

- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*): Reported as introduced in stock tanks.

- **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*): Probably present in drainage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Sonoita Creek]] drainage represents a critical refugium for several native fish species that have experienced dramatic range contractions across southern Arizona. The drainage contains both permanent and intermittent stream reaches, with springs providing critical habitat for endemic forms.

[[Monkey Spring]] on the Rail-X Ranch serves as a particularly important refuge, supporting populations of [[Roundtail Chub]], the unique [[Monkey Spring Pupfish]], and the remnant population of [[Gila Topminnow]]. The spring-fed pond system has maintained relatively stable conditions compared to the mainstream.

The document notes that game fishes pose little threat to native fauna in [[Sonoita Creek]] proper, but could effect significant changes if they become abundant in springs or ponds harboring *P. occidentalis*, *Cyprinodon* sp., or *Gila robusta*, potentially resulting in extirpation of native species.

## Key Findings

1. **Dramatic Faunal Changes**: The 62-year collection record (1904-1966) documents significant declines in native fish diversity, with multiple species showing range contractions or local extirpations.

2. **Critical Refugia**: [[Monkey Spring]] and associated habitats on the Rail-X Ranch serve as the last refuge for three taxa: [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Monkey Spring Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon* sp.), and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*).

3. **Species Persistence**: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) demonstrates exceptional resistance to environmental degradation, persisting in drying streams and occurring in almost all permanent water areas through 1966.

4. **Local Extirpations**: [[Sonoran Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*) disappeared from the drainage after 1904. [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) was last collected in 1927. [[Gila Topminnow]] disappeared from mainstream habitats after 1904.

5. **Last Strongholds**: [[Sonoita Creek]] and the [[San Pedro River]] near Charleston represent the last strongholds for [[Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*) in southern Arizona.

6. **Introduced Species Threat**: [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), first detected in 1963, poses significant threat to native fauna through competition and predation. Further introductions should be discouraged.

7. **Game Fish Introductions**: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Green Sunfish]] have been introduced to stock tanks. While posing little immediate threat in the main creek, these species could cause extirpations if they colonize spring habitats supporting native species.

8. **Historical Catfish Introduction**: [[Mexican Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) were introduced in the late 1800s and maintained populations in ponds until disappearing by 1959.

9. **Documentation Value**: The [[Sonoita Creek]] drainage provides better documentation of faunal changes than most other waters in Arizona, making it valuable for understanding long-term trends in southwestern fish communities.
