It is **not** a fish-focused study; fish are mentioned only incidentally, as a cited ecological threat (predation) to the [[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]]. This note is structured to preserve the requested format while faithfully representing the source's actual focus on cienega wetland biodiversity.

## Executive Summary

This is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) 90-day finding, published in the *Federal Register* (Vol. 58, No. 238, December 14, 1993), addressing petitions to list three species endemic to **southern Arizona cienegas** (spring-fed marshy wetlands) as endangered under the Endangered Species Act: the **Canelo Hills ladies'-tresses** (orchid), the **Huachuca water umbel** (semi-aquatic plant), and the **Sonoran tiger salamander** (amphibian subspecies). The FWS found that petitioners presented substantial information warranting a formal 12-month status review for all three taxa. While no fish species are the subject of protection here, **non-native centrarchid and ictalurid fishes** are explicitly identified as a predation threat contributing to the decline of the [[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]] within its remaining aquatic habitat — making this document relevant to understanding **invasive fish impacts on cienega ecosystems**.

A secondary, unrelated notice in the same *Federal Register* issue addresses NOAA/NMFS public hearings on managing **live rock harvest** under the Gulf of Mexico and South Atlantic Coral FMP, relevant to reef fish and crustacean habitat conservation, but contains no direct fish population data.

## Background & Description

Fish-specific morphological data is **not provided** in this document. The two fish groups mentioned are identified only at the family level, without species-level detail:

- **Centrarchidae** (sunfishes/basses) — cited generically as "exotic fish" predators.
- **Ictaluridae** (catfishes) — cited generically as "exotic fish" predators.

For context, morphological detail is instead given for the co-petitioned taxa:
- **[[Canelo Hills Ladies'-Tresses]]**: Slender terrestrial orchid, ~50 cm tall; 5–10 grass-like basal leaves (18 cm × 1.5 cm); spiraling stalk bearing up to 40 white flowers.
- **[[Huachuca Water Umbel]]**: Herbaceous semi-aquatic perennial with cylindrical, hollow leaves (1–3 mm diameter, 3–20 cm height) arising from creeping rootstalks; 3–10 minute flowers borne on an umbel shorter than the leaves.
- **[[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]]**: A subspecies of tiger salamander (*Ambystoma tigrinum*) distinguished from congeners primarily by color pattern (per Lowe 1954) and confirmed via mitochondrial DNA analysis (Collins 1988).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes a shared **cienega wetland ecosystem** in the San Rafael Valley and adjacent foothills of the Patagonia and Huachuca Mountains (Santa Cruz County, Arizona, extending toward Sonora, Mexico), characterized by:

- Perennial water sources, gentle stream gradients, small-to-medium drainage basins, and mild winters.
- Finely grained, highly organic, saturated soils supporting endemic wetland flora.
- **[[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]]** habitat: currently restricted to **manmade or altered habitats** — specifically perennial stock tanks — since natural cienega habitat was destroyed prior to the subspecies' scientific discovery. Known from 15 remaining sites (reduced from 17) in the San Rafael Valley.
- **Introduced/non-native fish** (centrarchids and ictalurids), along with non-native bullfrogs (*Rana catesbeiana*), are cited as **predators contributing to salamander population decline** — an example of how fish stocking (often for sport or "bait bucket" introductions) can disrupt native amphibian communities in isolated aquatic habitats.
- Additional stressors on the shared wetland system: overgrazing, groundwater pumping, surface water diversion, impoundment construction, and general watershed degradation — all of which also affect any incidental native fish or aquatic invertebrate communities in these cienegas, though none are named in this document.

## Key Findings

- The FWS determined that **substantial information exists** to warrant listing consideration for all three petitioned taxa; formal 12-month status reviews were initiated (due by May 31, 1994).
- **Predation by exotic fish (Centrarchidae, Ictaluridae)** was explicitly identified by petitioners as one of several threats to the [[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]], alongside disease susceptibility, non-native bullfrog predation, bait-bucket genetic swamping from other tiger salamander subspecies, low heterozygosity/reduced fitness, and habitat pollution.
- The Sonoran tiger salamander had already been extirpated from **2 of 17 known historical sites** (the type locality, due to Parker Canyon Lake creation, and Bog Hole), reducing occupied sites to 15.
- No natural, undisturbed habitat is known to currently support the subspecies — all extant populations occupy **manmade stock tanks**, which petitioners argue may amplify vulnerability to the fish/bullfrog predation and other risks noted above.
- All three species had previously been elevated to **Category 1 candidate status** (sufficient biological data to support listing) prior to this petition, reinforcing the credibility of the petitioners' claims.
- The document underscores a recurring conservation theme relevant to [[Cienega Ecosystem]] management: introduction of **non-native sport or bait fish** into isolated desert wetland habitats poses a documented predation risk to native, range-restricted amphibians, even when fish species richness/data in these systems remains poorly quantified.
- A separate, unrelated NOAA notice in the same register addresses live rock harvest regulation relevant to reef fish habitat conservation in the [[Gulf of Mexico]] and South Atlantic, but provides no species-level fish data.
