## Executive Summary
This document is a brief technical correspondence (letter) from Rob Clarkson (Arizona Game & Fish Department) to Sally Stefferud (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service), dated January 5, 1993. It transmits length-weight data for five [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) specimens collected during annual monitoring of the [[Verde River]] on October 6, 1992. The data was collected as part of routine population monitoring for this species, which is of conservation concern. The specimens were slated for transfer to the Arizona State University Collection of Fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Backwater habitat within a larger river system, characterized by:
  - Mean depth: 25 cm (shallow, low-velocity microhabitat)
  - Substrate: sand-gravel-cobble bottom
- **Location:** [[Verde River]], Arizona, at River Mile 118.1 (measured from Horseshoe Dam), approximately 2–3 miles below Perkinsville, Arizona (T17N, R2E, NW1/4 of NW1/4 of SE1/4, Section 5; elevation 3,740 ft).
- **Sampling method:** Seining, conducted as part of annual monitoring efforts by the Arizona Game & Fish Department.
- **Co-occurring species:** Not specified; no additional spikedace individuals were captured during the broader survey, suggesting low relative abundance at this site/date or limited detectability.
- This backwater/shallow mesohabitat preference is consistent with known ecological associations of [[Spikedace]] for low-velocity, fine-to-coarse substrate microhabitats within larger warmwater river systems of the [[Gila River Basin]] and its tributaries.

## Key Findings
- Confirmed presence of [[Spikedace]] in the [[Verde River]] as of October 1992, at River Mile 118.1.
- Only five individuals were captured during this survey event, suggesting a low local population density or limited sampling effort/success on this date.
- Specimens ranged from 46–58 mm in length, all juveniles or small adults based on size, each weighing approximately 1 g — consistent with the small maximum size typical of this species.
- Data collected as part of a long-term, annual monitoring program tracking [[Spikedace]] populations, relevant to conservation assessments under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Endangered Species oversight (spikedace is federally listed as threatened).
- Specimens were preserved and designated for archival deposition at the Arizona State University Collection of Fishes, supporting future taxonomic, morphological, or genetic reference use.
