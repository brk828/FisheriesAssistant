# Fossil Fishes from the Bituminous Shales at Riacho Doce, State of Alagoas, Brazil

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae #chirocentridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[David Starr Jordan]] describes a collection of fossil fishes from bituminous shales at [[Riacho Doce]], [[Alagoas]], Brazil. The material was gathered in 1907 under the direction of [[John Casper Branner]] for the [[Carnegie Museum]] of Pittsburgh, with duplicates held at [[Stanford University]]. The paper establishes new species and a new genus of herring-like fishes, proposes a new genus name for double-armored herrings, and argues that the shales are Lower Eocene rather than Upper Cretaceous. Most of the material consists of compressed fish impressions in black shale, with numbered specimens and many fragments.

## Background & Description

The collection is treated in the context of earlier work on American and Old World herrings. The paper's central concerns are:

- **Genus *Diplomystus* (Clupeidae):** Type species *Diplomystus dentatus* from the [[Green River Shales]] (Eocene, Wyoming). Its defining features are strong ventral plates and smaller plates on the dorsal line before the dorsal fin. The name *Diplomystus* is preoccupied (a catfish genus named *Diplomystus* by Bleeker), so *Copeichthys* was proposed as a substitute by Dollo (1904).
- **Genus *Knightia*:** Based on *Knightia eocena*, a slender herring with a short anal fin and fewer vertebrae than *Diplomystus*. Jordan's paper lists it as a distinct genus and also gives its synonymy.
- **Double-armored herrings:** Jordan recognizes three American groups representing two or perhaps three genera. He proposes the name *Ellipes* for the Brazilian group, with *Diplomystus* and *Knightia* as the other two.

Jordan also reviews Australian and Chilean genera, including *[[Hyperlophus]]* (Ogilby, 1892) and *[[Potamalosa]]* (Ogilby, 1897). He concludes that *Ellipes*, *Potamalosa*, *Hyperlophus* and *Knightia* can be treated as distinct subgenera even if all double-armored herrings are placed in *Diplomystus*.

**Key diagnostic characters of the three American groups (as given):**

| Group | Vertebrae | Caudal vertebrae | Anal fin rays | Teeth | Scales |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Diplomystus* (type *D. dentatus*) | 42 | ~23 | 25–40 | Present, moderate | ~60, small |
| *Ellipes* (type *D. branneri*) | ~32 | 12–17 | 8–12 | None known | Apparently large, thin, deciduous |

Jordan notes that the *D. dentatus* specimen figured in Plate V shows large irregular scales on the opercle, which he regards as probably loose scales out of place, since no other known herring has scales on the head.

An editor's note by W. J. Holland records a further specimen of *D. dentatus*, in finer preservation, from the Green River Shales. Holland donated it to the museum, and it was photographed as Plate VI.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper gives little direct ecological information. Its environmental statements are inferences:

- **Depositional setting:** Judging from similar living fishes, the Riacho Doce shales were probably deposited in an **estuary**.
- **Age:** The fauna is judged **Lower Eocene**, "possibly but not probably" Upper Cretaceous.
- **Biostratigraphic contrast:** No species from Riacho Doce were found in the Cretaceous deposits near Bahia or Ceará, and the Riacho Doce species are unlike any Cretaceous forms seen so far. Most known *Diplomystus* species are later than the Cretaceous. Jordan concludes that the rocks of Alagoas are Eocene and those around Bahia are Upper Cretaceous.
- **Preservation:** Fishes are preserved as flattened impressions and fragments in black shale. Shining black opercles and scale imprints are conspicuous in several specimens.

The Brazilian Cretaceous species *[[Ellipes Longicostatus]]* (originally *Diplomystus longicostatus* Cope, 1886) comes from Bahia. Jordan did not examine specimens of it, but he compared it with the new species using Woodward's 1895 figure.

## Key Findings

### 1. New species of *Ellipes* from Riacho Doce

- ***Ellipes branneri* sp. nov.** Type is a small, much compressed herring, total length given as "2 to 34 inches" (OCR-affected; likely 2 to 3¼ in). Key counts: vertebrae 32–33 (15+17 or 16+17 in the type; 14+19 in Number 3), about 20 ribs, dorsal fin with about 12 rays, anal fin with about 12 rays, and about 17–20 belly plates. Ventral fins are very small and inserted behind the front of the dorsal fin. Dozens of numbered specimens were collected (Collector's Nos. 1–130 range), with the best preserved listed by number, plus many fragments. Number 125 is about an inch long and very well preserved.
- ***Ellipes riacensis* sp. nov.** Type is Collector's No. 4, length 4¾ inches (OCR-affected). Key counts: vertebrae 31 (13+18), about 19 ribs, dorsal fin about 15 rays, anal fin about 14 rays, and about 23 belly plates. The ventral fins are small and inserted behind the front of the dorsal fin. It differs from *E. branneri* in its longer head, smaller mouth, shorter lower jaw, shorter ribs, more elongate body and broader, striated opercle. Only the type shows ventral fins.
- **Comparison with *E. longicostatus*:** The Bahia species has a much elevated back, much longer ribs, enlarged posterior ventral plates, and ventral fins inserted well before the dorsal fin (under the eleventh rib). The Brazilian *E. branneri* and *E. riacensis* have ventral fins behind the dorsal front.
- **Scale study:** Professor T. D. A. Cockerell (University of Colorado) examined *E. branneri* scales. He could not see outlines of complete scales, but found the transverse circuli well developed and the scales evidently large, and could not distinguish them from those of the common herring except possibly in sculpture.

### 2. New genus *Dastilbe* and species *Dastilbe crandalli*

- Jordan proposes the new genus ***Dastilbe***, which he describes as a herring not closely related to *Diplomystus*. Diagnostic traits include a very large, smooth, convex, shining black opercle (longer than the eye), no dorsal or ventral scutes, no anal finlets, and scales represented by depressions or pits (about 50 in a lateral series).
- ***Dastilbe crandalli* sp. nov.** Based chiefly on Number 91 (about 2¾ inches; Plate IX). Vertebrae are 17+13 = 30 (to 32); dorsal rays about 12; anal rays about 12; ventral fins about as large as the pectorals, with about 8 rays. Ribs are about 19 in number.
- Jordan compares *Dastilbe* with *Halecopsis* (enlarged preopercle in *Halecopsis*, but moderate opercle in *Dastilbe*), with *Scombroclupea scutata* (Woodward, 1908; about 40 vertebrae, finlets behind the anal fin, and armored ventral ridge), and with *Clupea* (over 50 vertebrae in the common herring). He concludes that *D. crandalli* is distinct from all of them.
- The species was named for Roderic Crandall, Branner's assistant on the expedition, later geologist to the Geological Survey of Brazil.

### 3. *Chiromystus alagoensis*

- ***Chiromystus alagoensis* sp. nov.** Chirocentrid herring from the Eocene. Specimens include fragments of a large fish, about a foot long, with jaws, pectoral fins and pelvic fins. The posterior vertebral column in Number 114 contains 30 vertebrae, and Jordan estimates the total was 50–60. The pectoral fin has a very broad first ray and five well-defined further rays. Jordan judges it distinct from *C. mawsoni* Cope, partly because ventral fin size differs.
- Jordan rejects an identification of the caudal fin of Number 114 with *Mawsonia* (a coelacanth).

### 4. Stratigraphic and paleoenvironmental conclusions

- The Riacho Doce shales were probably deposited in an estuary and are Lower Eocene.
- The fauna differs from Cretaceous faunas at Bahia and Ceará, and the rocks of Alagoas are Eocene, whereas those around Bahia are Upper Cretaceous.

### 5. Taxonomic framework

- Jordan recognizes three American double-armored herring groups (*Diplomystus*, *Ellipes*, *Knightia*) and proposes that *Ellipes*, *Potamalosa*, *Hyperlophus* and *Knightia* be considered distinct subgenera.
- He disputes Woodward's synonymy of *Hyperlophus* with *Diplomystus* as "quite unlikely."
