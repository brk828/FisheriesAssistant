# Rio Magdalena Basin Fish Survey Travel Report

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #conservation

## Executive Summary
This 1986 field report documents a collaborative survey between the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Dean Hendrickson, Lee Simons) and the Centro Ecológico de Sonora (Lourdes Juárez, Alejandro Varela) to assess the abundance, distribution, and status of [[Gila Ditaenia]] (Sonoran Chub) within the Mexican portion of the Rio Magdalena basin, Sonora, Mexico. The species had recently been listed as [[Endangered Species]], and prior monitoring had focused almost exclusively on the single U.S. population in Sycamore Canyon (Sycamore Creek), northwest of Nogales, Arizona. This survey represented the first systematic assessment of the species' status in its core Mexican range since its original description from specimens taken in 1941. Aerial reconnaissance and extensive ground-based electrofishing across the [[Rio Altar]], [[Rio Cocospera]], and adjacent tributaries revealed that the Sonoran population is large, widely distributed, and generally healthy — in contrast to the small, unstable Arizona population, which is peripheral to the species' Sonoran core range. The survey also yielded significant incidental findings, including a new distribution record for [[Ambystoma Tigrinum]] (Tiger Salamander) and discovery of an undescribed species of [[Poeciliopsis]].

## Background & Description
The report is primarily focused on distribution, abundance, and habitat status rather than detailed morphology; however, several taxonomically relevant notes are included:

- **[[Gila Ditaenia]]**: Morphometric analysis is planned as a follow-up taxonomic study to clarify systematic relationships between the Sonoran and Arizona populations. Comparative work will assess whether Sonoran populations are morphologically consistent with the type series (collected 1941).
- **[[Gila Purpurea]]**: Specimens were specifically collected in the Rio Sonora basin (near Saracachi) for taxonomic comparison with Gila populations from the Rio Magdalena, suggesting possible morphological or genetic overlap/confusion between these congeners.
- **Undescribed *Poeciliopsis* sp.**: A new, previously unrecognized species within the genus *[[Poeciliopsis]]* was discovered at cienega habitats between Imuris and Nogales. Frozen tissue material was collected for electrophoretic and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis, to be compared against the two other known *Poeciliopsis* taxa in the region.
- A presently **unidentified parasite** was observed impacting *Gila ditaenia* populations, particularly near Tubutama, with lower-abundance occurrence at one additional site. Identification pending expert analysis (Jerry Landye, Page Springs Hatchery).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The Rio Magdalena basin supports a mosaic of aquatic habitats ranging from ephemeral desert streams to permanent spring-fed cienegas:

- **Habitat types surveyed**: Intermittent pools, flowing stream reaches, dry sandy streambeds, natural and impounded cienegas, hot springs (e.g., [[Agua Caliente]]), and artificial stock tanks/reservoirs.
- **Core native fish habitat**: Large, nearly pure native fish assemblages were found in short but substantial stream reaches at [[Agua Caliente]] and [[La Cienega]] (a tributary of the Rio Altar above Saric), as well as at sites near [[Cocospera]] and [[Imuris]].
- **Cienega habitats**: Two previously unsampled cienega habitats were identified between Imuris and Nogales, believed to be unique within the basin — these sites yielded the undescribed *Poeciliopsis* species.
- **Exotic species interactions**: Non-native species (Largemouth Bass, Bluegill, Black Bullhead, Channel Catfish, Green Sunfish) were documented primarily in artificial impoundments (stock tanks, water storage reservoirs, e.g., at La Cienega and above Imuris), rather than in natural flowing habitats. Threats from exotics to native fish communities were characterized as "limited and local."
- **Hydrology**: Surface water was highly discontinuous across the western basin; the Rio Altar (Sycamore Creek) disappeared into dry sandy bed near the international boundary, with no visible surface flow for at least 20 km downstream of the border. Stock tanks were unexpectedly abundant across the landscape, sometimes representing impounded natural springs with potential to harbor native fish.
- **Cross-basin comparison**: [[Gila Purpurea]] was sampled in the neighboring Rio Sonora basin (Saracachi) for comparative taxonomic purposes, indicating ecological/geographic proximity between the two basins' native fish faunas.

## Key Findings
- **Range status**: The [[Gila Ditaenia]] population in the Mexican Rio Magdalena basin is large, widely distributed, and mostly in healthy condition — contrasting sharply with the rare, unstable peripheral population in Sycamore Canyon, Arizona.
- **Habitat condition**: Aquatic habitats in Sonora are generally in relatively good condition, with limited and localized threats from exotic species interactions.
- **Parasite impact**: An unidentified parasite is negatively affecting Sonoran Chub populations near Tubutama; impact assessment pending species identification.
- **New records**:
  - First new distribution record for [[Ambystoma Tigrinum]] in a stock tank near Saric.
  - Discovery of an undescribed *[[Poeciliopsis]]* species in cienega habitats between Imuris and Nogales.
- **Sampling methodology**: Standardized timed electrofishing at each site enabled catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) measures of fish community composition and abundance; supplemental qualitative shocking captured additional specimens (including parasitized individuals) not represented in timed samples.
- **Collaborative & institutional outcomes**:
  - Specimens deposited at Arizona State University (ASU) fish collection; 50% of collection to be transferred to Centro Ecológico de Sonora.
  - Salamander specimens transferred to Dr. J.P. Collins (ASU).
  - Frozen *Poeciliopsis* tissue sent to Dr. Robert Vrijenhoek (Rutgers) for electrophoretic and mtDNA analysis.
  - Future collaborative studies proposed on *Poeciliopsis* population genetics and reintroduction strategies, targeting NSF or USFWS funding.
- **Conservation application**: Data will directly inform the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]'s Recovery Plan for *Gila ditaenia*.
- **Planned publications**:
  - Bilingual status survey manuscript targeting *Southwestern Naturalist* or *Arizona-Nevada Academy of Science* (submission ~April 1987), co-authored by Hendrickson and Juárez.
  - Taxonomic study targeting *Southwestern Naturalist* or *Copeia* (submission ~December 1987).
