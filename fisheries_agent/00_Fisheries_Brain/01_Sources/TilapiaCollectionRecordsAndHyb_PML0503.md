# Tilapia Collection Records and Hybridization in Arizona (1983–1984)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a site-by-site collection register of tilapia (Cichlidae: *Oreochromis* and *Tilapia*) sampled at 15 entries across Arizona in 1983 and 1984. Specimens were collected at public parks, a zoo, a nursery, a borrow pit, the [[Lower Colorado River]], the [[Gila River]], a pump station and state hatchery stocks. Most sites were sampled by [[Arizona State University]] researchers, including [[Minckley]] and [[Marsh]]. The register links each sample to a morphological or genetic assignment, and it shows that many populations were hybrids. Pure [[Tilapia Zillii]] occurred at some sites, while others held [[Introgressive Hybridization]] among [[Oreochromis Mossambicus]], [[Oreochromis Aureus]], [[Oreochromis Hornorum]] and possibly [[Oreochromis Nilotica]]. The sum of listed specimen counts is 216 (derived from the table).

The Latin names are abbreviated in the source (for example "O." for *Oreochromis* and "T. zilli" for *Tilapia zillii*). Sampled material included preserved voucher specimens held at Arizona State University.

Four categories of material appear in the register:

1. **Wild or semi-wild populations** in parks, ponds and rivers
2. **Zoo and nursery populations** (Phoenix Zoo and a Phoenix nursery)
3. **Riverine and borrow-pit populations** (Painted Rock Borrow Pit, Lower Colorado River, Gila River)
4. **Hatchery stocks** held by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] at its Boundary Pump Station facility, labeled as [[Golden Hybrid Stock]], [[Oreochromis Mossambicus]] stock, [[Oreochromis Aureus]] stock and a Golden Hybrid × *O. aureus* cross

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All sites lie in the Sonoran Desert region of southwestern Arizona, a setting in which warm-water tilapias are non-native. The register shows that these fish persisted and interbred in multiple habitat types, including:

- **Desert lakes and ponds** (Roper Lake State Park, Ecanto Park, Phoenix Zoo)
- **Urban and nursery ponds** (Phoenix nursery)
- **Large river systems** (Lower Colorado River, Gila River)
- **Borrow pits and pump-station waters** (Painted Rock Borrow Pit, Boundary Pump Station)

The presence of hybrid individuals across this range implies repeated introductions and mixing of source stocks. Two site-specific notes suggest management relevance: the Roper Lake sample was tied to a control context (the wording is partly garbled in the source), and hatchery stock records show introgression with *O. hornorum* and *O. nilotica* in some lines. The document does not describe population sizes, habitat measurements, water chemistry or ecological impacts.

**Main points**

- **Pure *Tilapia zillii* sites:** Ecanto Park (5 of 5 specimens), Phoenix Zoo 23B (20 specimens) and Phoenix nursery 24B (6 specimens) were assigned to *T. zillii*.
- **Pure *O. mossambicus* record:** Roper Lake State Park is the only site where the source reports a single species with no evidence of other species (18 specimens, May 1983).
- **Widespread introgression:** Most *Oreochromis* sites show mixed ancestry, with *O. aureus*, *O. mossambicus* and *O. hornorum* present in combination. Possible *O. nilotica* contribution is reported at several sites.
- **Non-random mating:** Lower Colorado River sample 26A is noted as "not randomly mating," which the source does not explain further.
- **Hatchery stocks:** Arizona Game and Fish stocks are reported as either pure lines or hybrid crosses, and several contain introgressed material. This suggests that hatchery sources may have contributed to the hybrid populations in the wild.
- **Sampling scope:** The source gives counts and assignments only. It contains no methods, genetic markers, statistical tests or conclusions about invasion pathways.
