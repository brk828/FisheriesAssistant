# Characterization of the Life History and Ecology of the Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*) in the Grand Canyon: 1992 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Annual Report from [[BIO/WEST]] to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] covers the calendar year 1992 of a four-year study of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the mainstem [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]]. The study tests how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affect the species' life history, distribution, movement and energetics. Eleven monthly field trips were made in 1992, covering roughly 170 river miles from Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56) to Diamond Creek (RM 226). Work was organized around Objectives 1A to 1E and 2A and addressed two conservation measures from the 1978 Biological Opinion on dam operations.

The main findings concern habitat use, where most adult chub occupied [[Eddy Complexes]]; a size-based shift from shoreline to offshore habitat near 180 mm TL; a Reach 1 population estimate of 3,449 fish; and strong turbidity and time-of-day effects on near-surface and horizontal activity. The document is an excerpt. It ends partway through the Chapter 7 (Energetics) drift results, so the Chapter 7 food-habit results, the Chapter 8 water quality chapter and the Chapter 9 summary and recommendations are not included.

## Background & Description

**Study purpose and context.** The work was conducted with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]], and fed into the [[Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement]]. It was designed to address Conservation Measure 5 (identify dam impacts on humpback chub) and Conservation Measure 7 (establish a second spawning population in Grand Canyon). The project began on September 1, 1990, with a final report due October 15, 1994.

**Study reaches.**
- **Reach 1:** 20.6 miles, Kwagunt Rapid to Hance Rapid (RM 56.0 to 76.6). Includes the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) confluence at RM 61.3. This reach was the focus of most work.
- **Reach 2:** 83.4 miles, Hance Rapid to below Havasu Creek (RM 160.0). Steep, narrow granite gorges with eight perennial tributaries.
- **Reach 3:** 66.0 miles, below Havasu Creek to Diamond Creek (RM 226.0). Described as an important nursery and rearing area, though no spawning sites or larvae were found.

**Sampling design.** Trips alternated between 12-day and 20-day durations. The 20-day trips ran two field teams, and each reach received about 10 days of effort. Sampling used a stratified random design across eight geomorphic strata divided into 24 sampling substrata. Five substrata were tributary inflows, which were sampled at least once per season.

**Field team.** The principal investigator was [[Richard Valdez]], and the report was edited by Valdez and M. Hugentobler. Chapter primary authors included Tony Wasowicz, Bill Masslich, Bryan Cowdell, Lydia Trinca, Leslie Brown, Erika Prats and Bill Leibfried.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology.** Releases from Glen Canyon Dam govern mainstem flow. [[Interim Flow Criteria]] have applied since August 1, 1991. Under them, flows range from 5,000 to 20,000 cfs, and daily fluctuation is limited to 5,000 cfs in low-volume months, 6,000 cfs in medium-volume months and 8,000 cfs in high-volume months. Rising flows may ramp at no more than 2,500 cfs per hour, and falling flows at no more than 1,500 cfs per hour.

- Annual discharge in 1992 was 8.3 million acre-feet, about 2 million acre-feet below the 30-year average of 10.5 million acre-feet.
- July and August averaged about 14,200 cfs. The highest daily mean was 15,700 cfs (July 10), and the peak instantaneous discharge was 18,800 cfs (July 24).
- The lowest daily discharge was 7,300 cfs (April 27), and the lowest instantaneous discharge was 6,300 cfs (October 4).
- The LCR contributes about 170,000 acre-feet per year, which is under 2 percent of mainstem runoff, though its basin covers nearly 20 percent of the Colorado River basin. LCR flows range from nearly zero to several thousand cfs.

**Geomorphology and habitat.** Reach 1 has two geomorphic strata (Lower Marble Canyon and Furnace Flats) with bed composition of 30 to 36 percent bedrock and boulders. Tapeats Sandstone ledges provide cover, and talus shorelines enhance fish habitat. Reach 2 is narrower and steeper, with channel slopes of 0.12 to 0.23 percent. Its Upper Granite Gorge and Muav Gorge are compared to the schist and gneiss habitats that support the largest upper-basin chub populations at [[Black Rocks]] and [[Westwater Canyon]].

**Water temperature.** The LCR warmed earlier in 1991 (above 15°C by mid-February) than in 1992 (above 15°C by mid-April). The authors suggest this temperature difference may cue spring staging at the confluence.

**Native and non-native fishes.** Fourteen species were captured in 1992, comprising four native and ten non-native species across six families. The non-native fishes included [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Common Carp]], [[Brown Trout]] and [[Striped Bass]]. The native fishes included [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]].

## Key Findings

**Sampling and gear.**
- Seventeen gear types were used in 1992: eight net types, three trap types, four seine sizes, electrofishing and angling.
- Netting caused no direct mortality over the year. Three juvenile chub died during electrofishing in May and July, apparently from jumping out of live wells. A bucket-and-lid procedure was adopted in August 1992, and no further juvenile deaths were recorded. Three chub died in minnow traps.
- Electrofishing captured the most chub of any gear (457 fish, 16.92 fish per 10 hours). It was the only gear that captured all three life stages (75 YOY, 340 juveniles, 42 adults).
- Trammel nets were about three times more effective than gill nets for adult chub. No YOY were taken in nets.

**Abundance and population.**
- Total 1992 chub captured and processed: 1,065 (119 YOY, 526 juveniles, 420 adults).
- The last [[Schnabel Method]] estimate for juveniles and adults (≥175 mm TL) in Reach 1 was 3,449 individuals (95% CI 3,104 to 3,737). Estimates rose from below 2,000 in the first six months to 3,110 to 3,449 in months 15 to 20.
- Sex was determined for 367 of 420 adults. The male to female ratio was 45.5:54.5.
- PIT-tag recapture rates in 1992 were 17.6 percent for adults tagged by BIO/WEST, 30.2 percent for adults tagged by other researchers, and 45.5 percent overall for adults. The recapture rate for YOY and juveniles with fin marks was 2.5 percent.

**Habitat use.**
- Eddy complexes covered only about 20 percent of surface area, yet held 93.5 percent (1990-91) and 86.4 percent (1992) of chub over 200 mm TL captured. They also accounted for 82.8 percent and 74.7 percent of radio contacts with adults in those periods.
- Habitat mapping in Reach 1 at 4,000 to 16,000 cfs found runs at 69 percent of surface area, eddies at 19 percent and pools at 11 percent.
- Length-frequency analysis showed a shoreline mode at 80 to 100 mm TL and an offshore mode at 360 to 390 mm TL. The authors infer a habitat transition near 180 mm TL, roughly age 3, although age was not yet confirmed.
- Juvenile talus habitat at the SALT site showed no significant change in velocity or depth across the observed flow range.

**Growth and condition.**
- The length-weight exponent was 2.938, close to isometric growth (b = 3.0).
- Monthly mean relative condition (Kn) ranged from 0.824 (June) to 1.063 (April). Condition was higher in March and April before spawning and dropped in May and June. Fish caught below the LCR confluence had significantly higher condition (p = 0.003) than those caught above.
- Rainbow trout (2,122 fish over 200 mm) showed an earlier, different condition cycle. Kn was 0.941 in January and 1.230 in August.
- PIT-tagged recaptures showed no significant Kn difference from first captures, suggesting tagging had no measurable detrimental effect.

**Movement and activity.**
- Of 27 radiotagged adults in 1992, 44 percent (12) were classified as migratory and 56 percent (15) as non-migratory. Mean net displacement was 2.32 km (1.58 km after excluding one fish tracked 15 km up the LCR). Mean gross displacement was 5.13 km.
- Of the 27 fish, 77.8 percent had net displacement under 2 km. Fifty-eight percent reoccupied the same locale after moves of at least 0.1 km.
- The average percentage of fish located near the surface was 40 percent during spawning (February to May) and 25 percent during nonspawning periods.
- Near-surface activity was 48 percent under high turbidity and 20 percent under low turbidity. Turbidity had the strongest influence of the three factors tested (season, time of day, turbidity).
- Netting catch rates were 5.31 fish per 100 ft per 100 hours by day and 24.91 at night under low turbidity, and 11.98 by day and 24.15 at night under high turbidity.
- Horizontal activity was 230 percent higher under low turbidity when stage change was ≥5 cm/hr. Average absolute stage change fell from 14.4 cm/hr to 5.4 cm/hr after interim flows began.

**LCR staging and spawning.**
- Adults entered the confluence area from February. Use peaked in March for the staging area (six fish) and in April for the LCR itself (five fish).
- Fish moved in and out of the LCR with flow spikes (for example, 2,210 cfs on March 5 and 2,720 cfs on April 13, 1991). In 1992, the LCR plume was more stable and extended farther downstream.
- Netting CPE in Reach 1 was significantly higher in April 1992 than in January or March.

**Energetics and drift.**
- In 1991 and 1992, 398 drift samples were collected. Simuliidae, Chironomidae and the amphipod *Gammarus lacustris* dominated.
- Drift density upstream of the LCR was highest during falling flows (380.6 organisms per 100 m³ filtered), compared with 177.2 under rising flows and 97.9 under steady flows.
- Stomach pumping was used on 43 chub in 1992 (eight in 1991) and was found to be non-injurious.

**Other species.**
- Striped bass: three captured in 1992 (all in Reach 3, May to July), compared with 15 in 1991. Authors suggest the decline may reflect lower Lake Mead levels or sampling differences.
- Fathead minnow: 144 captured electrofishing (0.53 per hour) and 438 captured by seine (0.012 per m²) in 1992, versus nine total in 1990-91. Interim flows may have created more low-velocity habitat.

**Planned work for 1993.** The authors plan to:
- Intensify electrofishing and seining across Reach 1 to fill sampling gaps.
- Expand angling to assess rainbow trout predation on YOY and juvenile chub around the LCR.
- Shift netting toward a full 24-hour diel design.
- Increase stomach pumping below the LCR and analyze diet differences.
- Complete scale-based age estimates for fish under 200 mm TL.
