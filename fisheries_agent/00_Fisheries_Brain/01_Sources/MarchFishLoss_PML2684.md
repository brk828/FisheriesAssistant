# March 23 Fish Loss

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an internal memorandum from the director of the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in [[Dexter, New Mexico]], sent on March 24, 1992 to the Regional Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region 2, in Albuquerque. It reports a serious fish loss on March 23, 1992, and a second wave of deaths on March 24. Affected fish included [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Cutthroat Trout]] held as forage. The author's reconstruction points to a valve on the [[Municipal Water Supply]] that a contractor left open. That allowed chlorinated town water to enter the [[Backup Water Supply]] line and reach the holding house and [[Incubation System]]. The memo also commits to a review of backup water infrastructure and accepts responsibility for the lapse.

## Background & Description

The memo covers a hatchery operation holding several wild and domestic native fish stocks in 350-gallon concrete tanks with flow-through water and aerators:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: nine upper basin wild adults and eight domestic adults, held in separate tanks.
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (called "Colorado squawfish" in the text): ten upper basin wild adults and one domestic adult that had been neutered for a [[San Juan River]] experimental study. Wild and domestic groups were held separately.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: six adults collected from [[Lake Mohave]] and two domestic adults, all in one tank.
- **[[Cutthroat Trout]]**: about 50 forage fish that had survived predation by the adult pikeminnow.

The hatchery also used artificial spawning. On March 23, after the initial losses, staff removed the ovaries and oviducts of a dead female razorback sucker and fertilized the eggs with milt from three males in a three-way cross. The eggs were placed in incubators. The memo describes the backup supply as a standard safeguard for the hatchery's water delivery system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a single incident report and gives no broader ecological survey. Its environmental content is limited to:

- **Water quality on March 23**: [[Dissolved Oxygen]] was 6 mg/l and pH was 7.8. Staff considered [[Gas Supersaturation]] and ruled it out because the die-off was acute and no gas bubbles were seen under fish skin. Water samples were collected from four tanks for analysis at the Roswell laboratory.
- **Chlorine exposure**: a check on the backup water found slightly over 1 mg/l of [[Chlorine Toxicity|chlorine]]. The chlorinated water flowed through the heaters and incubators, and the eggs from the dead female were lost.
- **Hydraulic conditions**: the municipal supply, delivered through a 2-inch line, had enough pressure to push the hatchery's pressurized water out of about 50 feet of 2-inch line and 125 feet of 6-inch line. It then reached the supply line serving the east side of the holding house, where the affected fish were held.
- **Context of supply risk**: the hatchery's water system has a history of failures from power outages and pump problems, so the backup supply has repeatedly been needed to protect fish and eggs. The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] Visitor Center aquaria also draw on the same municipal backup line. The municipality had recently been required by the State to flush chlorine through the system periodically, and the hatchery kept track of those flushes.

## Key Findings

**Timeline of the event**

- **March 23, about 2:00 PM**: staff found the trout dead and noticed early signs of stress in the other fish. The team moved the surviving fish to a pond. Staff then tried to keep four upper basin razorback suckers alive by moving water over their gills. Four razorback suckers died: one female and three males.
- **March 23, afternoon**: incoming water was shut off and aerators unplugged. Water samples were taken and the team went home at about 6:00 PM.
- **March 24, morning**: one more upper basin razorback sucker, four upper basin pikeminnow, five domestic razorbacks, six bonytail chub, and the neutered pikeminnow were found dead. All carcasses were frozen, with the upper basin fish and bonytail placed in ultra-cold storage.

**Cause identified**

- A hatchery crew member had been called to repair the incubation heater. He shut off the regular water supply to do the work. When he reopened it and got no water, he opened the backup line valve, got water, and left, leaving both valves open.
- The backup valve was found wide open and was not marked or locked. The author notes it sat in an isolated location away from foot traffic and that no one outside the hatchery crew was expected to touch it.
- The author's reconstruction is that chlorinated municipal water entered the holding system and reached the fish, though the memo presents this as the most probable cause rather than a confirmed laboratory result.

**Author's conclusions and recommendations**

- The memo accepts personal responsibility. It says the valve should have been marked, locked, or had its handle removed so that only authorized personnel could change it.
- It restates an earlier recommendation to explore a new backup water supply. It notes that any alternative would be expensive.
- The author argues that a backup supply is essential to planning. Without it, the hatchery could have lost the broodfish or several million eggs.
- The memo closes by describing the incident as "a lose-lose situation."

**Document status**

This is an incident and administrative memo, not a research study. It includes no statistical analysis, no sample sizes beyond the stock counts above, and no final laboratory results for the water samples. Mortality counts are reported as given and are not analyzed further.
