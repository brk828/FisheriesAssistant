# Fish Loss Incident Memo at the Dexter National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This memo from the Director of the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) to the Regional Director in Albuquerque reports a fish kill discovered on the morning of March 23. Endangered and hatchery-reared fish died after a backup water supply valve was opened by mistake, letting chlorinated municipal water flow into the incubation system. The memo describes the events, the losses, the corrective steps taken, and the hatchery's accountability for the incident. The year is partly illegible in the scan, but the memo references the 1990 and 1991 year classes.

## Background & Description

The hatchery held several groups of fish in 3–5 gallon concrete tanks in the holding house, supplied with well water and aerated. Fish present at the time included:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): two groups of wild adults and domestic adults, held in separate tanks. Several were broodstock. Fish from the upper basin and the domestic stock were kept separately.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): ten upper-basin wild adults and one domestic adult, the latter neutered at the San Juan River experimental facility. Upper-basin and domestic fish were held in separate tanks.
- **A chub species** (name garbled in the scan): six wild adults collected from a lake and two domestic adults, all held in the same tank.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*): about 50 fingerlings, most of which were lost.
- **[[Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii*): fry, reported as lost through predation by adult fish, which the memo attributes to squawfish.

Fish were spawned as part of the hatchery's propagation program. Eggs were fertilized and placed in incubators under a chilled water system, separated from the holding house supply.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery's primary water source is a well, with a backup municipal supply. The memo notes that the backup supply is isolated from the main system and that it was normally closed. The hatchery's standing practice was to keep the backup system closed and to check chlorine levels in the municipal supply regularly. A chlorine measurement taken during the event was about 1 mg/L, which the memo identifies as the cause of the mortality.

The memo also explains that the municipal supply can have high pressure, and that the backup line runs a long distance to the holding house, which complicates normal use.

## Key Findings

**Timeline**
- **March 23:** Fish were found dead or dying in the holding house around 3:00 PM by the hatchery staff after a meeting with the regional director and Congressional staff. Staff found the holding house water flow failing and most fish in distress. Several razorback suckers and squawfish died, and about 50 bonytail fry were lost. Water samples were taken for analysis. Dissolved oxygen was measured at 6 mg/L and pH at 7.8, with no evidence of supersaturation and no bubble trauma. The memo rules out these causes.
- **March 24:** Additional razorback suckers and bonytail were found dead. Five domestic razorbacks and one squawfish died. Surviving fish were moved to freezers and an incubator system. A chlorine check showed about 1 mg/L in the incubator water, which was traced to the municipal supply reaching the holding house.

**Cause**
- A valve in the backup municipal supply line was open when it should have been closed. Investigation indicated that an employee had opened the valve while working on the heater, and that another valve had also been opened, so chlorinated city water reached the holding house and incubators.
- The valve handle had been removed, so no one had to be authorized to operate it. The memo acknowledges the director's responsibility for this.

**Losses and Egg Management**
- Eggs were stripped from one female, with milt from three males, to produce fertilized eggs in three trays. The memo expresses hope that a good number of eggs survived the exposure, though the outcome is not stated.
- Two lost squawfish and a razorback were preserved for records. Remaining fish were moved to the ultra-cold storage.

**Corrective Actions and Recommendations**
- The valve was secured, and the handle was removed so that only authorized staff could change its position.
- The hatchery plans to keep the backup supply as a standby option, though the memo states that the backup line is not reliable on its own because of its distance from the main system and high municipal pressure.
- The memo suggests exploring a new backup supply, a proposal the director first made several years earlier.
- Staff will check chlorine levels regularly and use the water bath in the incubator room to monitor conditions.

**Conclusion**
The director characterizes the event as a "close call" that could have been far worse if the fish had been in critical stages or if no backup supply had existed. The memo accepts responsibility for the incident and notes that the backup supply has previously prevented losses during power outages and pump failures.
