# SNWA Comments on Lower Colorado River Biological Opinion

#administrative #endangered-species #ESA-consultation #water-management #razorback-sucker #bonytail-chub #southwestern-willow-flycatcher

## Executive Summary

This document comprises formal comments submitted by the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]] (SNWA) to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] regarding the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Final Biological and Conference Opinion on [[Lower Colorado River]] Operations and Maintenance from [[Lake Mead]] to the Southerly International Boundary (April 30, 1997). SNWA supports the conservation measures recommended in the Biological Opinion but disputes the analytical methodology, regulatory process, and jeopardy conclusions under Section 7(b) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The letter, dated July 25, 1997, endorses implementation of conservation measures under Section 7(a)(1) authority (furthering ESA purposes) rather than as mandatory Section 7(a)(2) requirements (avoiding jeopardy). SNWA commits to supporting these measures through the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP) and providing fair-share funding.

## Background & Description

The SNWA submitted these comments in response to Reclamation's May 16, 1997 notice requesting input on the Biological Opinion, with the comment deadline extended to July 25, 1997. The Biological Opinion addresses three species for which "jeopardy" findings were made: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*). The consultation covers Reclamation's "discretionary program of continuing operations of Lower Colorado River (LCR) dam facilities, maintenance of river control features, and other activities such as endangered species conservation for the next five years."

SNWA has participated in and funded the MSCP through the [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]], with representation on the MSCP steering committee. The organization emphasizes its commitment to preserving endangered species and their habitats while ensuring compliance with state and federal endangered species laws allows continued water and power project operations and future development opportunities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Lower Colorado River]] system has been fundamentally altered by dam construction and water management infrastructure, creating what the Biological Opinion characterizes as conditions that "have already exceeded the jeopardy threshold" for the three species in question. The environmental baseline includes impacts from [[Hoover Dam]], [[Davis Dam]], [[Parker Dam]], [[Imperial Dam]], and associated water delivery and hydropower operations governed by the "Law of the River" (compacts, statutes, Supreme Court injunctions, and contracts).

For **[[Razorback Sucker]]** and **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: The species have been eliminated from approximately 85% of historic range. Effects identified include: (1) impacts to spawning and nursery areas due to fluctuations in riverine reaches; (2) entrainment in canals via diversion facilities; and (3) potential passage through powerplants. The Biological Opinion notes that actions comprising the environmental baseline "have been directly and indirectly responsible for the collapse" of these fish populations.

For **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]**: The primary factor is that dam operations "preclude the development and persistence of large, contiguous tracts of cottonwood-willow habitat in the large floodplain areas that historically contained such habitat." The Biological Opinion identifies anticipated loss of 1,148 acres in the [[Lake Mead]] delta as "catastrophic" given the species' overall status. Historical riparian habitat may have encompassed approximately 400,000 acres (Mearns' figure cited).

Reclamation acknowledged in a February 4, 1997 memorandum that "for all practical purposes and to all measurable effects" it lacks discretion over management of reservoir levels in [[Lake Mead]]. Table 1 of the Biological Opinion (pp. 35-37) summarizes seven instances where Reclamation has "limited discretion" over operations and maintenance activities.

## Key Findings

**SNWA's Position on Conservation Measures:**

1. SNWA "fully supports" and endorses the Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs), Reasonable and Prudent Measures (RPMs), and Terms and Conditions recommended in the Biological Opinion, with specific exceptions noted.

2. These conservation measures are characterized as "prudent, sensible, likely to be economically feasible (assuming the availability of funding), and technically feasible."

3. SNWA believes these measures "will significantly improve the status of these species during the next five years."

4. SNWA commits to providing "its fair share of funding for these conservation measures" through the MSCP process.

**Legal and Analytical Objections:**

1. **Wrong "Agency Action" Analyzed**: The Biological Opinion failed to distinguish between Reclamation's limited discretionary actions and the aggregate effects of all historic river development. SNWA argues the Opinion "analyzed the wrong question" by focusing on effects of past actions (dams built, contracts entered) rather than prospective discretionary management actions.

2. **Baseline Confusion**: The Opinion erroneously concluded that because "there are no fundamental changes to management proposed, the effects will be the same as the ones that have created the 1996 baseline," thereby confusing environmental baseline with effects of the proposed action.

3. **Jeopardy Standard Misapplied**: The Opinion failed to demonstrate that the proposed action would "reduce appreciably the likelihood of both the survival and recovery" of listed species (50 C.F.R. § 402.02). Instead, it concluded jeopardy exists because endangered species "will continue to be adversely affected in the same manner as in the past."

4. **Overstated Discretion**: The Biological Opinion ascribes discretionary authority to Reclamation that either does not exist or does not constitute "agency action" under ESA Section 7(a)(2). For example, water delivery contracts are "permanent service" contracts mandated by statute (Boulder Canyon Project Act § 5, 43 U.S.C. § 617d), yet the Opinion suggests renegotiation authority.

**Specific Limitations on Implementation:**

Several RPAs and RPMs may not be fully implementable:

- **RPA 3**: May require increased consumptive water use beyond Reclamation's acquisition authority.
- **RPAs 7, 9, 10, 13, 14**: May require reservoir elevation manipulation or dam re-operation beyond Reclamation's discretion.
- **RPA 11**: "Ecological restoration" may require water management scenarios beyond Reclamation's discretion; if based on 400,000-acre historical habitat figure, likely infeasible.
- **RPA 13(6)**: Beyond Reclamation's authority if it requires modification of existing permanent water service contracts or power contracts.
- **RPA 10**: May not be technically feasible due to difficulties in aging razorback suckers (McCarthy and Minckley, 1987) and pinpointing successful recruitment conditions in [[Lake Mead]]/[[Grand Canyon]] complex.

**Recommended Course of Action:**

SNWA recommends Reclamation:

1. Concur with SNWA's conclusions regarding invalidity of the regulatory process, analytical methodology, and jeopardy conclusions.

2. Advise [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] of these conclusions while re-affirming commitment to conservation measures under Section 7(a)(1) authority (utilizing discretionary actions to further ESA purposes).

3. Proceed with implementation under 50 C.F.R. § 402.15 notification provisions rather than requesting changes under Step 7 of the modified Consultation Agreement.

4. Continue full commitment to the MSCP process and species conservation, as demonstrated by Reclamation's native fish program at [[Lake Mohave]].

**Data Gaps and Technical Issues:**

- No data provided on magnitude or frequency of "seasonal and daily fluctuations" affecting spawning/nursery areas.
- Unknown whether fish passage through powerplants is fatal.
- Cumulative impacts (human population growth, economic development, non-federal fish stocking) weakly defended with minimal supporting data.
- Location and extent of future development impacts not specified.
- No correlation shown between population projections (Table, p. 117) and described impacts.
