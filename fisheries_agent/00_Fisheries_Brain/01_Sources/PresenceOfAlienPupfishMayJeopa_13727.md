# Desert Pupfish Unauthorized Introduction and Recovery Concerns in Southern Arizona

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinodontidae #endangered-species #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This letter from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director documents unauthorized introductions of the Quitobaquito subspecies of [[Desert Pupfish]] ([[Cyprinodon macularius]]) outside its natural range in southern Arizona. These alien populations, established in at least four locations within the historic range of the native subspecies (C. m. macularius), jeopardize recovery efforts by threatening genetic integrity. The DFRT recommends immediate eradication of alien stocks from [[Bog Hole Tank]], [[Fort Huachuca Military Reservation]], and [[Findley Tank]] (Research Ranch), followed by restocking with genetically appropriate native lineages from the [[Colorado River Delta]].

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Pupfish]] (Cyprinodon macularius) is an endangered species with multiple subspecies. The document distinguishes between:
- The Quitobaquito form (C. macularius subspecies) from [[Quitobaquito Spring]], [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]]
- The typical subspecies (C. m. macularius) native to the [[Santa Cruz River]] and [[San Pedro River]] drainages

Alien pupfish were first discovered in 1976 at [[Bog Hole Tank]], a habitat constructed by the [[U.S. Forest Service]] and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] for [[Mexican Duck]] (Anas platyrhynchos diazi). Specimens were identified morphologically by W.L. Minckley and confirmed through electrophoretic analysis by Dr. Bruce Turner ([[Virginia Polytechnic Institute]]) as originating from Quitobaquito Spring.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Four populations of questionable origin are established in the uppermost [[Santa Cruz River]] and [[San Pedro River]] basins:

1. **[[Bog Hole Tank]]**: Quitobaquito form confirmed by electrophoretic analysis. Population became large and dispersed into upper [[Santa Cruz River]] (up to 1 kilometer downstream). Currently depleted by introduced predators but persists along tank margins.

2. **[[Findley Tank]]** ([[Research Ranch]], Elgin, Arizona, [[San Pedro River]] drainage): Origin from undocumented stocking; never critically examined morphologically or electrophoretically. Established approximately 20 years prior to 1986.

3. **[[Fort Huachuca Military Reservation]]** (two populations, [[San Pedro River]] drainage): Discovered during [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis) monitoring by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. Breeding colors suggest Quitobaquito form identity. Reportedly transferred from Findley Tank population. Possible hybridization with [[Boyce Thompson Desert Arboretum]] stock (where both pupfish and topminnow are maintained together).

These alien populations occur directly within the former range of the typical subspecies, threatening recovery efforts already underway using stocks from the [[Colorado River Delta]] in Sonora, Mexico.

## Key Findings

**Genetic Integrity Threats:**
- Four established populations of alien or hybrid desert pupfish stocks in native subspecies range
- Strong possibility of hybridization at Fort Huachuca between two subspecies
- Potential for further spread through flooding into [[San Pedro River Conservation Area]] (Bureau of Land Management planning stage)
- Vandalism incident at Research Ranch: Findley Tank partially drained toward [[Babacomari River]], which connects to San Pedro River

**Recovery Obstacles:**
- Repeated eradication recommendations (at least seven years for Bog Hole) met with little success
- Research Ranch personnel resistance to Findley Tank eradication based on:
  - Concerns for extended preservation of Quitobaquito form
  - Concerns for [[Mexican Garter Snake]] population at Findley Tank
  - Hypothesis that population may have developed apomorphic characters over ~20 years
  - Ongoing electrophoretic work by Jeff Mitton ([[University of Colorado]])

**DFRT Recommendations to Regional Office:**
1. Contact [[U.S. Forest Service]] ([[Coronado National Forest]]) for immediate removal of alien pupfishes from Bog Hole and adjacent Santa Cruz River; renovate other tanks to remove predators affecting endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] in [[Redrock Canyon]] and upper Santa Cruz River
2. Contact Fort Huachuca for immediate removal of alien pupfishes from Military Reservation waters
3. Contact Research Ranch personnel; if unsuccessful, notify [[Audubon Society]] (ranch owner/operator); if still unsuccessful, engage other Federal/State agencies and private groups ([[Desert Fishes Council]], Arizona-New Mexico Chapter of [[American Fisheries Society]])
4. Restock all proven supportive habitats with native genetic lineage from Colorado River Delta stock

**Management Concerns:**
- Pupfish removal from Santa Cruz River requires careful consideration of sympatric Gila topminnow welfare
- Public popularity of pupfish increases risk of unauthorized transfers
- Unknown extent of additional unauthorized introductions
- Need to alert technical personnel in southeastern Arizona to possibilities of alien pupfish introductions
