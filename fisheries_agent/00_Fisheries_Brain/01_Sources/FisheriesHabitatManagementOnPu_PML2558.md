# Fisheries Habitat Management on Public Lands: A Strategy for the Future

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This January 1989 strategy, prepared for the Director of the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) by an interdisciplinary Fisheries Habitat Management Team, assesses the agency's fisheries program and proposes a goal-oriented path forward. The report was commissioned after the Director approved [[Fish and Wildlife 2000]] in May 1987, and responds to a proposal from the [[American Fisheries Society]]. BLM manages over 270 million acres of public land, including more than 35,000 miles of stream fish habitat and numerous lakes and reservoirs. Fisheries on these lands contribute an estimated $198 million to the economy.

The team found that BLM's professional fisheries staffing fell by nearly two-thirds during the past decade, leaving many field offices without adequate fisheries technical skills. The report makes recommendations across nine findings covering policy, administration, cooperation, training, and research, and lists specific positive actions with responsible offices and target dates.

## Background & Description

The report sits within the framework of the [[Federal Land Policy and Management Act]] of 1976 (FLPMA), which requires multiple-use and sustained-yield management of public lands. Its stated purpose is to clarify and strengthen the BLM fisheries habitat program and to build a management structure capable of delivering goal-oriented results.

The team consisted of senior BLM managers and biologists, directors of two state fish and wildlife agencies, and the Deputy Director of the American Fisheries Society. The team's stated view was that Fish and Wildlife 2000 provides a sound foundation for a more realistic and responsive habitat management program.

The desired program was characterized as clearly defined in scope and function, adequately staffed by qualified personnel, supported by strong internal and external partnerships, based on sound data, coordinated, on-the-ground, and proactive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Anadromous fish.** BLM administers nearly 13,000 miles of spawning and rearing habitat for [[Pacific Salmon]] and [[Steelhead Trout]] in five states: Alaska (at least 10,000 miles), California (190 miles), Idaho (1,500 miles), Oregon (1,400 miles), and Washington (50 miles). The number of anadromous fish in Pacific Northwest waters has declined by nearly one-third from historic levels. Causes cited include overharvest, hydroelectric and irrigation dams, timber harvest, grazing, agricultural development, channel alteration, road building, mining, and dredging and filling of streams.

**Resident fish.** Resident habitat is divided into three regions. The Northwest includes [[Arctic Grayling]], rainbow, brown, and cutthroat trout, and bass. The Mountain States contain cutthroat, rainbow, and brown trout, plus warm-water species such as walleye and sauger. The Desert Southwest contains many small springs and streams supporting endemic native fishes, several listed under the Endangered Species Act.

**Threatened and endangered species.** BLM manages habitat for 35 listed threatened or endangered fish species and 64 candidate or sensitive species. Many are confined to desert areas and have been isolated since the end of the last ice age, making them highly vulnerable to habitat change.

**Riparian areas.** Riparian vegetation provides shade, cover, and bank stability and helps filter sediment. A 1985 BLM initiative and a 1987 Riparian Area Management Policy emphasized interdisciplinary management. A General Accounting Office review found that overall riparian condition on public lands is poor, primarily due to poor grazing management, and that lack of personnel, funding, training, and management commitment hinders the initiative.

## Key Findings

**Program status and funding**
- BLM's fisheries biologist staffing peaked at 63 in 1980 and dropped to 26 in 1987, while the agency's overall budget for wildlife and fisheries averaged about $15.4 million per year.
- Fisheries funding is estimated at under 10 percent of the wildlife and fisheries budget, roughly $1.4 million, though current accounting is poor.
- Oregon, with 11 biologists, had the most active program, attributed to biologist involvement in timber sale planning and strong public support.

**Anadromous fish projects**
- Following the Oregon coastal salmon decline, a Five-Year Comprehensive Anadromous Fish Habitat Management Plan for Oregon Coastal Rivers was developed.
- Projects in FY 1987 added 14 miles of habitat, 76 rearing pools, 5 channel developments, and one fish passage project.
- Full implementation of the anadromous fish plan is projected to increase anadromous fish production on public lands by 20 percent, or 12,000 fish per year.

**Recreation and economic value**
- More than 58 million pounds of anadromous fish were harvested on public lands in the Pacific Northwest, valued at $40 million.
- About 3.5 million recreational fishing days occur annually on public lands, valued at nearly $56 million.
- Over 15 million angler days on resident fish waters were reported, with roughly $14 million in primary benefits.

**Habitat plans**
- Fish habitat management plans (HMPs) cover about 335 miles of stream, less than 3 percent of BLM's total stream miles containing fish habitat.
- About $0.2 million in labor and money from partner groups has been spent implementing these plans, and over $21 million is needed to fully implement existing plans.
- Nearly half of the HMPs address threatened, endangered, or candidate species; most others address anadromous fish.

**Staffing and training**
- Universities rarely train fish biologists for the multiple-use context of BLM, so the agency operates training at the Phoenix Training Center.
- Field offices often lack fisheries expertise, which limits the agency's ability to conduct multiple-use management effectively.

**Partnerships and public perception**
- Public perception is that BLM has no fisheries resources or has abdicated management to state agencies and the Forest Service.
- Conservation groups such as Trout Unlimited, the Izaak Walton League, and Boy Scouts contribute volunteer labor and funds to habitat projects.

**Principal recommendations (Findings 1–10)**
- Strengthen fisheries recognition in land use planning and budgeting, and develop a BLM Recreational Fisheries Policy under the National Recreational Fisheries Policy.
- Recruit fishery biologists, provide technical training for managers, and build interagency personnel exchanges.
- Conduct an analysis of fisheries resources and workload by state and district to guide funding and staffing.
- Revise outdated manuals, develop standardized fisheries data systems, and clarify research priorities and approval processes.
- Pursue alternative funding and Congressional support, including the Land and Water Conservation Fund and Sikes Act habitat plans.

**Implementation**
- Positive actions are assigned to Washington Office, State Offices, District Offices, and Resource Area Offices, with target dates from 1989 to 1992.
- Full implementation is expected over several years as recommendations are integrated into state, district, and area planning.
