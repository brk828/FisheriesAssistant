# Razorback Sucker Cooperative Management Proposal (1981 Federal Letter)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

A letter dated February 3, 1981, from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Phoenix Area Office to Robert Jantzen, Director of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], proposes a cooperative management agreement for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an alternative to keeping the species on the federal endangered species list. The agency proposes an active reintroduction program in the Colorado River basin and the Verde River, with stocking and population monitoring over 5 to 10 years or more. The Service argues this approach would be more flexible and better for the species' survival than formal listing. The letter is OCR-scanned and several passages are garbled, so some details below are uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The [[Razorback Sucker]], a large freshwater catostomid (sucker) native to the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Regulatory status:** The species is listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The letter refers to several requests about the listing, including requests to reclassify it and to reinstate critical habitat. The exact wording is partly illegible.
- **Correspondence:** The letter is from the Acting Assistant Regional Director (signature illegible in the scan), copied to the Area Office in Phoenix and the Regional Director.
- **Recipient:** Robert Jantzen, Director, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Phoenix, Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range concern:** The letter discusses restoring razorback suckers to the [[Colorado River Basin]], with the [[Verde River]] named as a candidate reintroduction site. Other localities in the basin are mentioned in garbled passages.
- **Management rationale:** The Service states that an active reintroduction program would protect the species well enough to remove the need for listing it under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Management burden:** The letter describes the costs and administrative burdens placed on listed species and suggests these are a reason to prefer a cooperative approach.
- **Monitoring:** Wild populations would be monitored over a prolonged period of roughly 5 to 10 years or more.

## Key Findings

1. **Proposal:** The Service recommends a [[Cooperative Agreement]] between the federal and state agencies instead of a formal listing for razorback suckers.
2. **Management approach:** The agreement would cover supplying fish for stocking (a [[Reintroduction Program]]) and monitoring wild populations over 5 to 10 years or longer.
3. **Rationale:** The letter presents the cooperative approach as a stopgap measure that could give the species sufficient protection, with the results potentially eliminating the need for listing.
4. **Preference:** The Service believes both agencies would favor a cooperative agreement over a formal listing, and that it would better serve the species' survival.
5. **Requested action:** The Service asks the Arizona Game and Fish Department to state its views as soon as possible. If it agrees, the Service will draft the agreement immediately so management can begin.
