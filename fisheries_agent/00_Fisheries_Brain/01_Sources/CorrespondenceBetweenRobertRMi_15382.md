## Executive Summary
This note synthesizes a 1990s scientific correspondence between [[Robert R. Miller]] (ichthyologist, [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]]) and [[Robert Hershler]] (malacologist, [[Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History]]) concerning field collections made at station **M50-8**, a freshwater spring near **Las Palomas** in the southwestern desert region. The correspondence documents the co-occurrence of an undescribed [[Pupfish]] species (genus *Cyprinodon*) and a now-extinct hydrobiid snail species originally described by Drake in the 1950s. The exchange highlights the value of comprehensive field collection practices and provides historical documentation relevant to the extinction of both taxa, likely tied to spring habitat decline typical of SW desert aquatic systems.

## Background & Description
- **Pupfish (undescribed species):** No morphological description is given directly in this correspondence; the species was "new" and "yet to be described" at the time of original field collection. Field notes and observations were made by [[Howard E. Winn]], Miller's first doctoral student (later Professor of Oceanography, University of Rhode Island).
- **Hydrobiid Snail (extinct species):** Originally described by Drake in the 1950s; specimens were recovered from an uncataloged alcohol lot in the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] mollusk collection, associated with field label M50-8. Hershler was preparing a **redescription** of this species at the time of writing, implying the original description lacked detail or type material clarification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Isolated freshwater spring near Las Palomas, characteristic of relict desert spring ecosystems in the southwestern United States/northern Mexico region.
- **Co-occurring taxa:** The spring supported both an endemic pupfish population and an endemic hydrobiid snail population — a common pattern in isolated desert spring systems that harbor narrowly endemic, relictual aquatic fauna.
- **Collection methodology:** Miller credits his mentor [[Carl L. Hubbs]] for training him to preserve all incidentally captured organisms (amphibians, reptiles, snails, etc.) while seining for fish — a practice that inadvertently preserved the only known voucher specimens of the now-extinct snail species.
- **Decline/extinction context:** [[W.L. Minckley]] visited the site at later dates and could provide information on the observed extinction event. Additional context is referenced in a document titled **"Extinction and Management of Southwestern Desert Fishes"** (Deacon/Minckley volume).

## Key Findings
- A field collection (station M50-8) made by [[Robert R. Miller]] and [[Howard E. Winn]] at a spring near Las Palomas yielded specimens of both an undescribed pupfish species and a hydrobiid snail later described by Drake in the 1950s.
- The hydrobiid snail species is now **extinct**, and the alcohol-preserved lot in the UMMZ collection represents a rare/important voucher sample for taxonomic redescription work by [[Robert Hershler]].
- The pupfish taxon remained undescribed as of the correspondence date, with Miller noting his own mailing address label had been pasted over the "new name" — indicating active but unpublished taxonomic work.
- The extinction of the spring's fauna is linked to habitat decline, consistent with widespread desert spring degradation documented in regional [[Extinction and Management of Southwestern Desert Fishes]] literature.
- This correspondence underscores the scientific value of **incidental faunal preservation** during ichthyological fieldwork, a methodological practice traced to [[Carl L. Hubbs]]'s training influence, which proved critical for later taxonomic and conservation-related research on extinct desert endemics.
