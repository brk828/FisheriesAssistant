# Orchestia spinifera sp. n. (Crustacea, Amphipoda, Talitridae)

#marine-biology #amphipoda #talitridae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 taxonomic paper by [[Emília de Oliveira Mateus]], published from the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]] at the Faculdade de Ciências do Porto, describes *Orchestia spinifera* sp. n., a new talitrid amphipod. The type material came from sands on the right bank of the [[Guadiana River]] at [[Vila Real de Santo António]], Portugal. The species is named for the hooked dorsal spines on the metasome of adult males. The paper separates it from the closely related *Orchestia bottae* using seven morphological differences. The document also includes a long exchange list of the Institute's earlier publications, which is summarized below.

## Background & Description

The paper states that Portuguese marine amphipods had been studied only unsystematically. The authors cite only three earlier works on the group: [[Baltazar Osório]] (1889), R. [[Nogueira de Carvalho]] (1930) and [[Augusto Nobre]] (1938). The study was supported by subsidies from the Instituto de Alta Cultura for zoological exploration.

**Material and methods**
- Specimens were collected from the sands of the Guadiana's right bank at Vila Real de Santo António during field surveys in southern Portugal. The material was rich, with many males and females.
- Selected males and females were dissected to examine their appendages.
- Specimens were mounted and stained in "polyvinyl-lactophenol" with "lignin pink".
- Drawings were made with a camera lucida.
- The [[Holotype]] and paratypes are deposited at the Institute.

**Holotype data (label as given):** No. 90, *Orchestia spinifera* ♂ E. Mateus; length 16 mm; locality Vila Real de Santo António; date 26-11-1961; collector Emília Mateus.

**General description**
- Animals are very robust. Adult males reach 16 mm and females 14 mm.
- Adults are greenish; juveniles are brownish.
- **Head:** sub-pentagonal profile, very large eyes with an irregular oval outline.
- **Mesosome:** coxal plates I–VII are described in detail, with spines and serrations on the lower and posterior margins. Coxal plate VI has a strongly serrated posterior margin.
- **Metasome:** the first segment bears a pair of hooked protuberances directed backward. The second bears two pairs, and the third one pair directed forward.
- **Telson:** wider than long, clearly notched at the distal margin, with medium marginal and distal spines.
- **Antennae:** Antenna I has a flagellum of 5–6 articles and no accessory flagellum. Antenna II is about one-third of body length, with a flagellum of 19–22 articles.
- **Mouthparts:** the mandibles lack a palp and have a robust molar process. Maxilla I has a lobe with nine spines. Maxilliped palps are short and lack article IV, and the inner lobe bears three robust, tooth-like spines.
- **Gnathopods:** Gnathopod I has a translucent lobe on the carpus and propodus. Gnathopod II has a cordiform propodus with an oblique, sinuous palm and a notch for the dactyl.
- **Pereiopods and pleopods:** Pereiopods VI and VII have strongly expanded basal articles, and the basal article of VII is deeply toothed. Pleopods are similar, with two well-developed, roughly equal branches.
- **Uropods:** Uropod III is a single branch longer than its peduncle.

**Sexual and juvenile differences**
- Juvenile males lack the dorsal metasomal spines of adults. These spines are replaced by tubercles that grow larger with age, forming the hooks characteristic of adult males.
- Juvenile males also differ from adults in the basal articles of pereiopods VI and VII (less dilated), in the tergal margin of pereiopod VII (barely serrated), and in the acute posterior angle of coxal plate III.
- Females are less robust than males. They resemble juvenile males in several features, including the lack of hooked metasomal protuberances.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species was found in coastal sand habitats near the mouth of the Guadiana, on the Portugal–Spain border in southern Portugal. The document provides no data on population density, depth, substrate chemistry or seasonal occurrence beyond the single collection date of 26 November 1961.

The authors compare the new species with *Orchestia bottae* (M.-Edw.), the closest known relative, which it resembles in some characters. The document does not report any ecological or behavioral observations for either species.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Orchestia spinifera* sp. n. The specific name refers to the hooked dorsal spines on the metasome of adult males.
- **Distinguishing features from *O. bottae*** (seven differences given):
  1. Smaller size.
  2. No lobe on the merus of gnathopod I in males.
  3. Different shape of the female gnathopod.
  4. Marked enlargement of the basal articles of pereiopods VI and VII in adult males.
  5. Well-developed teeth on the tergal margin of the basal article of pereiopod VII.
  6. Hooked dorsal protuberances on the metasome of adult males.
  7. Shallow notch on the distal margin of the telson.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** adult males have hooked metasomal spines and dilated pereiopod bases. Juveniles and females share a similar form.
- **Type material:** the holotype is a 16 mm male from Vila Real de Santo António. Holotype and paratypes are held at the Institute.
- **Scope of prior work:** only three earlier studies of Portuguese marine amphipods are cited, so the authors describe the group as poorly documented.

**Publication list (appended exchange offprints).** The document ends with a list of roughly 86 numbered offprints from the Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre" (1940–1962). Most are by Braga, Machado, Mateus, Monteiro and Santos Júnior. Topics include:
- Cave and groundwater fauna, especially Asellidae (*Asellus*, *Stenasellus*, *Synasellus*), Bathynellidae (*Parabathynella*) and troglobitic amphipods. These are the largest group.
- Lepidoptera, spiders, pseudoscorpions and myriapods.
- Fisheries and fish biology, including *Sardina pilchardus* studies by J. A. Macedo-Cruz on vertebral counts, age structure and reproduction, along with parasites such as *Lernaeocera* on *Merluccius*.
- Marine invertebrates and biology, including bryozoans, foraminifera and sea anemones.
- Ornithology, including bird ringing and the Hirundo daurica record.

The list is an exchange catalogue and contains no new data on *O. spinifera*. It is summarized here rather than reproduced.
