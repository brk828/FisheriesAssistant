# Recreational Fisheries and Endangered Species Act Policy Review

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This April 1999 review, published by the Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife with the National Marine Fisheries Service, examines how federal policy balances the conservation of endangered and threatened species against recreational fishing. It centres on [[Executive Order 12962]] (1995), which aims to conserve, restore, and enhance aquatic ecosystems for more recreational fishing opportunities, and on the resulting [[Joint Policy on ESA and Recreational Fisheries]] (1996) issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]]. The authors argue that conflicts between conservation and angling are often overstated, and that the joint policy offers a framework for partnership, consistent implementation, and mutually beneficial actions. The document also summarises FY1997 restoration accomplishments and reproduces the relevant executive orders and policy text.

## Background & Description

The paper is a policy review rather than a species study. It was produced as a contribution to a symposium proceedings (see [[Lassuy 1995]]) and draws on federal and state documents as its basis.

- **Context of conflict:** Habitat alteration, heavy fishing pressure, and non-native species introductions have caused unexpected ecosystem impacts. Managers now recognise that actions benefiting one species can harm non-target species or habitats.
- **Economic importance:** The document cites Department of the Interior figures for 1996. Nationwide, about 35 million anglers spent roughly 620 million angler days afield and nearly $38 billion on tackle, equipment, licences, and related expenses. In Oregon alone, anglers spent nearly 9 million angler days afield and over $620 million on these items. (Some numerals are OCR-affected and should be checked against the original.)
- **Oregon position:** In the late 1990s (year partly illegible in the scan), the [[American Fisheries Society]] Oregon Chapter recommended that the [[Oregon Fish and Wildlife Commission]] require ODFW to follow [[American Fisheries Society Introduction Policy]] for aquatic species.

### Executive Orders and Policies

| Instrument | Date | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| [[Executive Order 12962]] | June 1995 (Federal Register June 9, 1995) | Conserve and restore aquatic systems to expand recreational fishing |
| [[Joint Policy on ESA and Recreational Fisheries]] | Effective July 3, 1996 | Reduce conflicts between ESA administration and angling |
| [[Executive Order 13112]] | February 3, 1999 | Prevent introduction and control invasive species |

**Executive Order 12962** has four main elements:
- **Section 1:** Defines federal agency duties, including partnerships with states, tribes, and the private sector.
- **Section 2:** Creates the [[National Recreational Fishing Coordination Council]].
- **Section 3:** Requires a [[National Recreational Fishery Resources Conservation Plan]] with measurable objectives. The plan had been published and was available from the authors.
- **Section 4:** Requires the joint ESA policy.
- **Section 5:** Creates the [[Sport Fishing and Boating Partnership Council]].

**The Joint Policy** contains three core actions:
1. Ensure consistent ESA implementation, encourage collaboration, and improve ESA outreach. For example, the services now use a jointly developed handbook for [[ESA Section 7 Consultation]].
2. Involve partners in recovery planning and restoration, including restoration of habitat that benefits both listed species and fisheries.
3. Keep the public informed and provide alternative angling opportunities when restrictions are imposed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document identifies several ecological links between conservation and recreational fishing:

- **Habitat linkages:** Improving desert riparian habitat for a threatened neotropical migrant bird likely also improves conditions for [[Redband Trout]] production, and therefore angling.
- **Introductions:** Evaluations of non-native introductions must consider genetic, disease, competition, and predation effects. Where listed species may be affected, recovery goals must also be considered.
- **Reintroduction example:** The [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] reintroduction to Oregon Canyon Creek in southeastern Oregon was not carried out. The decision was reached jointly by the USFWS, the [[Bureau of Land Management]], the [[Izaak Walton League]], and local anglers. The authors state that the collaborative process improved relations for other conservation efforts.
- **Disease:** The USFWS funded research on [[Whirling Disease]] and other fish pathogens.
- **Salmonids:** The policy references mass-marking of salmonids as one area of management focus. The scanned text is partly illegible here.

### Invasive Species Framework

[[Executive Order 13112]] defines invasive species as alien species whose introduction causes or is likely to cause economic or environmental harm or harm to human health. It requires federal agencies to prevent introductions, control populations, restore native species and habitats, and avoid actions that promote spread unless benefits clearly outweigh harm. It also establishes an [[Invasive Species Council]] (co-chaired by the Secretaries of the Interior, Agriculture, and Commerce) and requires a National Invasive Species Management Plan within 18 months.

## Key Findings

- **Accomplishments in FY1997:** Partner-funded USFWS projects:
  - Reopened 149 stream miles to fish passage.
  - Restored 106 miles of instream habitat, 460 miles of riparian habitat, and 39,000 acres of wetland.
- **Hatchery and stocking output:** USFWS hatcheries distributed over 120 million fish for mitigation and recreation. Native fishes were stocked on 80 [[National Wildlife Refuges]].
- **Conflict is often overstated:** The authors stress that conservation and angling frequently share interests. They describe the "us vs. them" framing as obscuring real common ground.
- **Partner engagement is required:** Partners must now be involved in developing new ESA recovery plans. Existing plans must be re-evaluated with strategies to minimise impacts on fisheries.
- **Accountability:** The joint policy requires evaluation and reporting. A full report was available from the authors.
- **Introductions:** Proposed introductions of non-indigenous species or hybrids must be based on management plans addressing genetics, disease control, ecological impacts, and recreational fisheries. Oregon Commission directives required ODFW to draft rules to prevent aquatic introductions.
- **Reference documents:** The appendices reproduce EO 12962, the joint policy, the Oregon AFS introduction resolution, and EO 13112. The Oregon resolution cites the [[Aquatic Nuisance Species Task Force]] (1991) and [[Lassuy 1995]] on non-native species as a factor in native fish extinction and endangerment.
