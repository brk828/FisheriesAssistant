# Survey of West Chevelon Creek and Upper Clear Creek Tributaries for Potential Little Colorado Spinedace Stocking Sites, June 2006

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

In late June 2006, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD, Region 1) surveyed five streams in the upper [[West Chevelon Creek]] and upper [[East Clear Creek]] drainages for potential stocking sites for the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), a species listed as threatened by the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] in 1987. The survey was conducted during a drought year, before the summer monsoon, when stream flows are likely at their lowest. The goal was to identify habitat able to withstand back-to-back drought years, with particular interest in finding adult [[Bluehead Sucker]], a relatively long-lived species whose presence may indicate stable habitat.

Only two fish species were found: [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) and Bluehead Sucker (*Catostomus discobolus*). No non-native fishes or crayfish were observed. Adult Bluehead Suckers were found in Willow Creek and Gentry Canyon, but not in West Chevelon Creek. Turkey and Beaver Creeks held no fish.

## Background & Description

The Little Colorado Spinedace was listed as threatened in 1987, and the West Chevelon and East Clear Creek drainages are part of its historical range (USFWS 1998 recovery plan). AGFD surveys before 2002 had already recorded Speckled Dace and mature Bluehead Suckers in the upper West Chevelon and East Clear Creek drainages, which prompted this follow-up survey.

**Methods:** Stream reaches were visually assessed for surface water. Fish were detected visually or by [[Electrofishing]], seining, and dip netting. For each segment containing fish, the team photographed the habitat and measured maximum depth, average depth, length (paced), and width (estimated) of each habitat unit (riffle or pool). Coordinates were recorded at the upstream and downstream ends of each fish-bearing segment (NAD27 datum).

**Habitat criteria:** A segment was coded and mapped only if it contained fish and continuous flow connecting at least one pool and one riffle unit.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**West Chevelon Creek (segments WCH1 and WCH2):** These segments run through grassy meadow areas. Pools are linked by short riffles. Riffles between most pools were dry during the survey but are probably flowing in wetter periods. Speckled Dace were present in most pools, and very large individuals (>100 mm) were collected in WCH2. No Bluehead Suckers were recorded here.

**Willow Creek (segments WIL1–WIL4):** These segments occur in rocky, scoured streambeds with pools connected by flowing riffles. WIL4 contained one long riffle (about 700 m) with many shallow pocket pools. Bluehead Suckers were present in several segments, and the deepest pools (over 2 m in WIL1 Pool 4 and WIL4 Pool 1 at over 1 m) were the most important habitat in the survey.

**Gentry Canyon (segment GEN):** This rocky, scoured streambed has flowing riffles connecting all pools. Large adult Bluehead Suckers (approximately 200 mm) were observed in the larger pools.

**Turkey Creek and Beaver Creek:** The full lengths of both creeks were surveyed. Only small, shallow, isolated pools were found, and no fish were observed. Turkey Creek was also surveyed from its headwaters to McGuire Crossing on the 2006 visit dates recorded in [[Table 1]].

## Key Findings

**Fish-bearing segments:** Seven segments contained fish: two in West Chevelon Creek (WCH1, WCH2), four in Willow Creek (WIL1–WIL4), and one in Gentry Canyon (GEN). Speckled Dace occurred in all seven. Bluehead Suckers occurred in five (WIL1–WIL4 and GEN).

**Bluehead Sucker life stages:**
- Adult Bluehead Suckers were recorded in WIL1 Pool 4, WIL3 Pool 1, WIL4 Pool 1, GEN Pool 3, and GEN Pool 4.
- A small Bluehead Sucker was recorded in WIL2 Pool 1.
- The presence of adults is the key indicator of habitat that may persist through sustained drought.

**West Chevelon Creek pools:** Speckled Dace were recorded in 15 of the 18 pools listed for WCH1 and WCH2 (the remaining pools were dry or had no fish). The 2006 table lists 6 pools in WCH1, with fish in the first three, and 18 pools in WCH2.

**Non-native species:** No non-native fishes or crayfish were observed in any surveyed segment.

**Stocking candidates (from Table 1):** The survey identified potential Little Colorado Spinedace stocking sites in the segments listed above. The Willow Creek and Gentry Canyon segments were the most promising because they combine flowing riffles and deep pools with adult Bluehead Suckers, which suggests stable habitat. Turkey Creek, Beaver Creek, and the upper Turkey Creek reach had no fish and were not suitable.
