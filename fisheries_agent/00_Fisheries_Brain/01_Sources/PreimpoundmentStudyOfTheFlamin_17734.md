# Pre-Impoundment Study of the Flaming Gorge Reservoir

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 Wyoming Game and Fish Commission technical report (Fisheries Technical Report No. 9, by [[Clifford E. Bosley]]) assesses the fish, water chemistry, and habitat of the lower [[Green River]] before the [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] was impounded behind [[Ashley Dam]]. Surveys found game fish populations to be negligible, while "trash fish" such as suckers, [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Carp]] dominated. The authors judged the reservoir main body and canyon suitable for salmonids and the bays and arms suitable for warm-water species. They recommended rotenone treatment to reduce rough fish, stocking of [[Rainbow Trout]] as the initial game species, and caution about [[Kokanee Salmon]] because of low winter egg-incubation temperatures. They also warned that the planned [[Seedskadee Project]] upstream would degrade downstream habitat.

## Background & Description

The study ran from July 1, 1958 to April 30, 1960 under Dingell-Johnson Project F-25-R-1, as part of an interstate cooperative program with Utah. Ashley Dam was to be completed in fall 1962. It would be 495 feet high, impound about 3,930,000 acre-feet, and create a reservoir of roughly 43,000 surface acres at full pool. The drainage area is about 14,300 square miles. The authors expected about 300,000 recreational users per year.

Objectives were to determine:
- Species composition of the river and tributaries
- Seasonal water chemistry
- Plankton, invertebrate, and algal productivity
- Whether resident sport fish could be established above the impoundment
- Natural and artificial spawning potential
- Best-suited sport species for the reservoir

Methods included experimental gill nets (150 ft, mesh 1 to 2¼ inches), modified wide-mouth fyke nets, emulsified rotenone, three electroshockers, and an air boat for shallow reaches. Seven sampling stations were established from [[LaBarge]] to the Wyoming-Utah state line. Invertebrates were sampled with a Surber sampler, zooplankton with a Wisconsin plankton sampler, and algae with a modified diatometer. Water quality followed standard methods, and turbidity was measured with a Jackson turbidimeter.

Ten fish species were found with the following composition (approximate abundance):
- Bigmouth Sucker (most uniformly distributed large sucker)
- Bonytail
- Bluehead Sucker
- Sculpin
- Rocky Mountain Whitefish
- Utah Chub Minnow
- Green River Spring Dace
- Brown Trout
- Rainbow Trout
- Colorado Squawfish

The report also lists Carp, Channel Catfish, Yellow Perch, Hybrid Trout, Fathead Minnow, Humpback Sucker, Black Bullhead, and Brook Trout, several of which were recorded by Utah personnel.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical setting.** The lower river crosses Bridger, Wasatch, and related formations of gravel, sandstone, and shale, overlain by highly erosive sandy soils. Spring silt loads are heavy. Annual flow ranges from about 12,000 cfs at spring run-off to about 350 cfs in late fall. Summer water temperatures near the Green River town reached about 78–79°F, close to the trout tolerance limit.

**Station-level observations.**
- **Station 1 (Names Hill):** Zooplankton averaged 2.7 per liter; the most common diatoms were Nitzschia, Tabellaria, and Cymbella.
- **Station 2 (Little Colorado Bridge):** Zooplankton averaged 0.9 per liter. Total dissolved solids rose relative to Station 1.
- **Station 3 (Big Island Bridge):** Zooplankton averaged 7.2 per liter, the highest of the stations. Diatoms were more abundant here.
- **Station 4 (Toll Gate Rock):** Zooplankton averaged 0.35 per liter. Coliform counts rose with nearby housing.
- **Station 5 (Kinkaid Ranch):** Invertebrates were limited to Diptera, Ephemeroptera, Annelida, and Gastropoda, a community typical of organic waste.
- **Station 6 (Holmes Ferry):** Zooplankton averaged 0.95 per liter. Bottom fauna was the lowest of any station.
- **Station 7 (Linwood Bridge):** Zooplankton averaged 0.42 per liter.

**Pollution sources.**
- *Green River town:* about 4,500 people with no sewage system, so raw sewage entered the river.
- *Union Pacific Railroad:* oil-separation plant processing 3,000–4,000 gallons per day. Accidental spills still occurred.
- *[[Bitter Creek]]:* intermittent, but flow after July is sustained by the outfall of the [[Rock Springs Sewage Disposal Plant]] (about 900,000 gpd). Coliform counts of 132–134 per cc at the creek mouth exceeded California criteria for freshwater fish. The authors called Bitter Creek "a sterile stream."
- *Big Sandy River:* contributes very high silt loads and dissolved solids (3,240 ppm total dissolved solids in May 1959).

**Tributaries.** [[Fontenelle Creek]] and [[Carter Creek]] were of most interest for spawning. [[Henry's Fork]] has silt-dominated lower reaches, which will be inundated. [[Sheep Creek]] supports trout.

## Key Findings

**Fish populations and abundance**
- Gill nets, electrofishing, and toxicant applications showed game fish populations within the reservoir footprint to be negligible. Rough fish were very abundant.
- Bigmouth Sucker made up 48.5% of fish taken in gill net sets and 51.4% of their total weight.
- Bonytail was concentrated in the lower Green River. In one gill net set, over 66% of bonytails were humpbacked. The report notes some writers consider these a subspecies, but the authors did not treat them as one.
- Bonytail fingerlings (2–5 inches) in the [[Black's Fork River]] indicate successful reproduction there.
- Brown Trout made up 90.7% of all fish planted below LaBarge since 1939. A 3-inch brown trout at Sage Creek suggested natural reproduction.
- A fish identified as a rainbow × native (hybrid) cross in Carter Creek showed natural reproduction. The authors thought the hybrids came from a 1955 native fry plant.
- [[Channel Catfish]] were restricted to the lower river. Numerous reports from 12–15 years earlier indicated decline, attributed by townspeople to pollution.
- [[Yellow Perch]] escaped from [[Eden Valley Reservoir]] into the [[Little Sandy River]]. Toxicant was applied in May 1959 and the reservoir was treated in September 1959.

**Stocking history**
- Since November 3, 1939, 223,449 fish have been planted in the study area. Brown trout make up 202,596 of these; channel catfish 8,425; black bullhead 5,000; eastern brook trout 4,800; rainbow trout 2,628.
- Planting density averaged 6.4 fish per surface acre per year over twenty years.

**Spawning experiments**
- Eyed brown trout eggs (2,800 planted, December 1958): survival among recovered eggs was 91.8% in Basket 2 and 99.4% in Basket 1 (Basket 1 recovery was 67.5%).
- Eyed kokanee eggs (1,500 planted, January 1959, water 34°F): recovery survival was 42.8% and 41.7%. Losses were attributed to advanced development, alevin escape through mesh, and rust.
- Green kokanee eggs (December 1960, Granby Reservoir stock): eggs in 40 Rod Creek (42°F) hatched at high rates. Eggs in Pine Creek and the Green River (near 32°F) suffered 100% mortality.
- Green rainbow eggs (April 1960): 50–60% hatch in experimental groups versus 83% in the hatchery control.
- Citing [[Salmon Cultural Laboratory]] work on sockeye, the authors noted that eggs below 42.5°F suffered abnormally high losses. Losses exceeded 80% at 35°F, but only in early development.

**Reservoir operation and habitat**
- Average annual maximum fluctuation is about 34 feet, but the practical minimum fluctuation is 90 feet.
- Minimum pool is 5,871 feet, giving about 6,000 surface acres. Full pool is 6,040 feet.
- Reservoir filling runs from about late April or early May through July. This coincides with carp spawning, and drawdown tactics used on the Platte River would not be feasible.
- The reservoir was expected to suit salmonids in the main body and warm-water species in bays and arms. Largemouth bass need water temperatures of 60–68°F for spawning.
- Projected fishing pressure was about 90,000 angler-days, mainly June to August.

**Access plan**
- Seven access sites were planned. Four are in Utah (Dutch John, Cedar Springs, Sheep Creek, Antelope Flats) and three in Wyoming (Lucerne Valley, Holmes Ferry, Marsh Creek).
- Holmes Ferry and Marsh Creek were recommended for Wyoming development. Estimated expenditure for access was $32,600, including two concrete launching ramps at $30,000.

**Seedskadee Project impacts**
- Fontenelle Dam would impound a reservoir of 7,000 acres and 16 river miles, holding 285,000 acre-feet. It would supply irrigation for 59,620 acres.
- Expected effects include inundation of 16 river miles, dewatering of about 10 miles below the dam in late summer, higher water temperatures (possibly eliminating trout), higher total dissolved solids, and increased turbidity into late September.

**Recommendations**
1. Chemically treat rough fish in the Green River above the impoundment, including the New Fork River, Horse Creek, and tributaries. Estimated cost was $126,290.45 for about 322 55-gallon drums of rotenone. Treatment should occur in late September or early October 1962.
2. Stock approximately 600,000 two- to four-inch [[Rainbow Trout]] from each of Wyoming and Utah (200 fish per surface acre) in spring 1963, planted from a boat in the canyon.
3. Remove [[Kokanee Salmon]] from the candidate list under adverse eyeing conditions.
4. Delay [[Walleye]] stocking until other species have been tested.
5. After initial rainbow plantings, study the feasibility of [[Largemouth Bass]].
6. After Fontenelle Reservoir is impounded, stock channel catfish in the river between the reservoirs and in the Flaming Gorge backwaters.
7. Do not plant fish in the Green River below Green River town until an adequate sewage system is installed. If coliform counts remain high after the proposed lagoon is built, ask Rock Springs to improve its treatment plant.
