# Copper Basin Wash Colorado Squawfish Refugium Monitoring, 1977–1979

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence file, compiled by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (Region 5) with the [[Metropolitan Water District]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], documents the establishment and monitoring of a native fish refugium for the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] in Copper Basin Wash near Blythe, California. Ninety-two hatchery-reared squawfish were stocked in August 1977, confined by a waterfall upstream and a gravel percolation barrier downstream. Subsequent surveys recorded declining counts (from 92 introduced, to 20, then 14 and 5 observed, and 3 near the barrier by 1979), with predatory [[Yellow Bullhead]] and [[Green Sunfish]] identified as threats. The file also covers the planned transfer of 100 young squawfish to California and the sampling protocol for water quality and fish growth.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), listed by state and federal governments as endangered. Stocked fish from the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] averaged 7.6 ± 0.2 cm (range 6.3–15.2 cm).
- **Desert pupfish:** [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) were introduced or considered for stocking; the document notes 200 pupfish from the Salton Sea were introduced (10-19 entry) and 150 pupfish introduced above the refugium upper portion. Pupfish were not trapped or observed in the 1979 survey.
- **Co-occurring species:** [[Yellow Bullhead]] (YB), [[Green Sunfish]] (GSF) and [[Western Mosquitofish]] (GAM) are described as predators or competitors; YB and GSF were removed by electroshocking and spearing.
- **Refugium structure:** A 2 m waterfall marked the upstream boundary, and a gravel percolation barrier, built 19–21 July 1977 by Blythe personnel with [[Metropolitan Water District]] cooperation, formed the downstream boundary.
- **Stakeholders:** Department of Fish and Game staff (Darrell Wong, J. A. St. Amant, Louis Courtois), Metropolitan Water District (Robert E. Whaley), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 2 ([[Donald Toney]]), and the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature:** Hatchery water was 14.4 °C (58 °F); transport water was 16.6–17.7 °C. Holding temperature was raised to 25.5 °C (78 °F) over about 4 hours, and fish were released into 28 °C (82.5 °F) water.
- **Habitat:** Fish were found in shaded cattail stands, slow backwaters with aquatic vegetation (*Chara* and *Najas*) in 18–20 inches of water, and in faster riffles near pool heads. Dense emergent vegetation limited visual surveys.
- **Barrier failure:** A violent local rainstorm on 22 August 1977 breached the percolation barrier, which was repaired on 9 September 1977.
- **Water chemistry:** Monitoring protocols specified pH, alkalinity, hardness and chloride. A 1979 snorkel survey suggested chemical drip treatment (applied in the wash) may have produced lethal concentrations, as aquatic organisms and plants were absent below the barrier.
- **Forage:** Forage was described as "extremely sparse" in the squawfish area in 1979.

## Key Findings

- **Initial stocking (August 1977):** 92 squawfish were released into a 150 m section on 1 August 1977; no mortality was noted.
- **Post-stocking counts:** A visual survey on 8/11/77 counted 20 of the 92 introduced fish. Five were seen in a large pool and six immediately below the barrier on 8/28/77.
- **Decline by 1979:** A 1979 snorkel survey (about 75% of usable habitat sampled) found a single squawfish of about 28 cm (11 in) above the barrier, described as a significant reduction from 92 in 1977.
- **Predator control:** Electroshocking in July 1977 removed ten YB (7.3–8.2 in). Later removals included two adult GSF and 12 adult YB during one snorkel survey.
- **Transfer to California (June–July 1977):** The Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team approved stocking 100 young squawfish (Lot 75DYAWB) into Copper Basin, with transfer planned for early July 1977.
- **1979 recommendations:** (1) Survey the creek above the refugium for better habitat and protection; (2) transplant GAM from below the barrier to provide forage; (3) remove YB and GSF during every survey; (4) test drip-treatment chemical concentrations and consider the area for desert pupfish.
- **Management conditions:** Metropolitan Water District required two weeks' notice before work affecting fish, personnel to be identifiable in uniform, and notification of Assistant Regional Operations Manager Calvin Twiggs before each operation.
- **Monitoring plan (May 1977):** Weekly temperature and pH readings, feeding and general observations; monthly water quality and fish growth (fork length to nearest mm, wet weight to nearest gram).
