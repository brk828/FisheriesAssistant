# Pre-proposal for Studies of mtDNA Genetics of Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a cover letter (dated 26 December 1989) from W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology) to Bob Williams of the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, accompanying a pre-proposal for mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) genetic studies of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The letter outlines a division of labor for a genetics research program: Minckley's team at ASU will handle mtDNA analysis, while allozyme (protein electrophoresis) work will be conducted independently by Don Buth at UCLA. The correspondence emphasizes logistical planning, sampling constraints for the imperiled razorback sucker, and considerations for studying hybridization with other co-occurring sucker species.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide direct morphological descriptions of the [[Razorback Sucker]]. However, it references tissue sampling methodology relevant to specimen handling:
- **Sampling method for razorback sucker**: Non-destructive sampling only, specifically via **muscle plugs** (biopsy-style tissue extraction), due to the species' protected/imperiled status.
- **Sampling method for other suckers**: For non-razorback sucker species (relevant to hybridization studies), full tissue harvesting and carcass freezing were considered acceptable, implying these species are more abundant or less protected.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] is implicitly identified as a species of conservation concern, given the restriction to non-lethal, non-destructive sampling protocols (muscle plugs only), consistent with its status as an imperiled native fish of the Colorado River Basin.
- The letter references **hybridization** between the razorback sucker and other sucker species as a research consideration, suggesting sympatric occurrence and potential introgression among [[Catostomidae]] taxa in the study area (likely Colorado River drainage, given USBR involvement and Salt Lake City/Las Vegas contacts).
- Collaborators referenced (e.g., [[Jim Deacon]]) are noted aquatic ecologists associated with Las Vegas/Nevada desert fish research, further supporting a Colorado River Basin / Great Basin desert fish conservation context.
