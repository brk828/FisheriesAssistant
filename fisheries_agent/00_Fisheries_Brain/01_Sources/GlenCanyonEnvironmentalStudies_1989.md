## Executive Summary
This document is a **Study Plan** from the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program, outlining a coordinated research effort during a controlled, **steady 5000 cfs flow release** from Glen Canyon Dam (October 6–9, 1989). While the majority of the plan addresses geomorphology, hydrology, and nutrient/sediment dynamics, several components directly target fish habitat and population science, most notably concerning the **[[Rainbow Trout]]** fishery in the tailwater reach between Glen Canyon Dam and Lee's Ferry. Key fish-relevant objectives include mapping trout spawning bars, assessing redd (nest) survivorship, evaluating the distribution and biomass of **[[Cladophora]]** (a filamentous algae serving as a key food-web substrate), and generating bathymetric/substrate maps to characterize habitat quality. The underlying rationale is that a stable, non-fluctuating flow regime may reveal spawning and survival potential obscured by the hydropeaking (fluctuating flow) operations typical of dam management, with implications for reducing dependency on hatchery stocking.

## Background & Description
This study plan does not describe fish morphology directly, as it is a river-operations and habitat-monitoring protocol rather than a species-level anatomical study. However, it references habitat features that structure fish life-history traits:
- **Redds**: Trout spawning nests constructed in gravel substrate on spawning bars; their structural integrity and survivorship are a key endpoint.
- **Substrate/Bar Composition**: Spawning success is tied to gravel bar substrate type, mapped via aerial photography and ground-truthing.
- **Cladophora Substrate**: This algal mat is identified as a critical structural and trophic substrate, with its distribution and biomass estimated relative to the 5000 cfs flow stage (above/below waterline).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The studies collectively characterize the ecological niche of the tailwater trout fishery and the broader riverine food web:
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** occupy the upper 16-mile reach of the Colorado River between [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and [[Lee's Ferry]], described as a "blue ribbon trout fishery" of significant economic importance to Arizona.
- Trout habitat suitability is governed by **stream hydraulics**, **beach/bar topography**, and **bathymetry**, all of which are altered by dam-controlled flow fluctuations.
- **[[Cladophora]]** functions as a primary basal food resource in the river's trophic structure, linking water chemistry (nutrient dynamics) and flow regime to fish food availability.
- **Nutrient dynamics** (phosphorus, nitrogen species, silica, organic matter) support primary productivity, which underpins all trophic levels including fish, and are hypothesized to shift significantly between fluctuating and steady flow regimes.
- **Beach and bank-storage dynamics** affect sediment stability and eddy chemistry, indirectly shaping nearshore/eddy habitats often used by fish as refugia.
- The study frames flow management (constant vs. fluctuating discharge) as an ecological lever affecting spawning ground availability, redd survivorship, and food-web productivity — with direct implications for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and the Environmental Impact Statement process.

## Key Findings
Because this is a *prospective study plan* rather than a results paper, "findings" are framed as objectives and expected outcomes:

- **Trout Spawning & Redd Survivorship**: At steady 5000 cfs, researchers planned to map spawning area boundaries at three selected bars and assess redd survival, then extrapolate these results across the full upper 16-mile reach using 1984 and 1989 aerial photo comparisons.
- **Cladophora Baseline Mapping**: Establishing a baseline distribution of Cladophora at a constant flow was intended to enable comparison with past/future flow regimes, since substrate alteration under fluctuating flows may disrupt this key food source.
- **Bathymetric & Substrate Mapping**: A preliminary bathymetric map of the Lee's Ferry–to–Glen Canyon Dam reach was to be generated via sonar and ground-truthed against 1984-derived substrate maps, directly supporting future trout habitat and angling-access research.
- **Flow-Habitat Linkage**: The rationale throughout is that comparing steady vs. fluctuating flow conditions will clarify how dam operations influence beach/bar stability, spawning substrate availability, and nutrient supply — all factors relevant to sustaining a self-recruiting trout population and potentially reducing hatchery stocking needs.
- **Interdisciplinary Integration**: Fish-relevant objectives were explicitly tied to concurrent studies on **[[Stream Discharge]]**, **[[Beach Dynamics]]**, and **[[Water Chemistry]]**, reflecting a systems-level approach connecting hydrology, geomorphology, and fish ecology under the broader **[[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]]** program.
