## Executive Summary

This document is an internal administrative summary from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Region 2, dated October 14, 1996, outlining the agency's top ten fisheries management priorities. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research data on a single species, the report synthesizes a broad portfolio of conservation, restoration, and technical support activities spanning endangered species recovery, interjurisdictional river management, habitat mitigation, aquaculture technology development, fish health monitoring, and invasive species control. Key taxa referenced include ESA-listed native fishes, [[Paddlefish]], anadromous [[Striped Bass]], [[Alligator Gar]], and invasive bivalves such as the [[Zebra Mussel]] and edible brown mussel. The scope reflects a regional, multi-jurisdictional approach to freshwater fisheries conservation in the American Southwest, particularly along the [[Colorado River]], [[Arkansas River]], [[Red River]], and [[San Juan River]] systems.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Refugia and Isolated Populations**: 15–20 ESA-listed fish species are maintained at isolated refugial sites to serve as genetic/demographic backup populations, mitigating extinction risk from habitat loss or invasive species interactions.
- **Interjurisdictional River Systems**: Species and habitats span multiple states/jurisdictions, including the [[Colorado River]], [[Arkansas River]], and [[Red River]], necessitating cooperative, cross-boundary fisheries management.
- **Reservoir and Dam-Impacted Habitats**: Fisheries mitigation efforts address habitat and fishery losses caused by large Federal reservoir construction (e.g., Oklahoma reservoirs, [[Lake Mohave]]).
- **Tribal and Federal Lands**: Fisheries are established/maintained on Indian tribal lands, national wildlife refuges, military bases, and National Forest lands, indicating a niche role in multi-agency land stewardship.
- **Wild Stock Health Monitoring**: Wild fish populations in the [[San Juan River]], lower [[Colorado River]], and Fort Apache Indian Reservation are surveyed for disease and health status, reflecting concern for wild-hatchery disease interactions.
- **Invasive Species Pressure**: Non-native species (zebra mussel, edible brown mussel) threaten native ecological niches, prompting monitoring and public education to slow their spread.
- **Native–Non-native Interactions**: Explicit concern is raised regarding interactions between listed native species and introduced non-native fish, indicating competitive or predatory ecological pressures.

## Key Findings

1. **[[Endangered Species Recovery]]**: USFWS Region 2 manages 15–20 ESA-listed fish species, focusing on identifying causes of decline, maintaining refugial backup populations, and producing stock for reintroduction.
2. **[[Depleted Species Restoration]]**: Non-listed but nationally significant species — [[Paddlefish]], anadromous [[Striped Bass]], and [[Alligator Gar]] — are targeted for population restoration before ESA listing becomes necessary.
3. **Technical Assistance**: The agency provides fisheries establishment/maintenance support to Indian tribes, national wildlife refuges, military installations, and the Forest Service.
4. **[[Interjurisdictional Fisheries Management]]**: Cooperative management extends across state lines on rivers such as the lower [[Colorado River]], [[Arkansas River]], and [[Red River]].
5. **[[Habitat Mitigation]]**: Programs address fishery and habitat losses caused by large Federal reservoir projects (e.g., Oklahoma reservoirs, [[Lake Mohave]]).
6. **[[Fish Culture Technology Development]]**: Facilities such as Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center (NFH&TC) develop culture techniques for non-traditional species and monitor breeding to preserve genetic diversity; Mora NFH&TC evaluates water reuse systems and cultural equipment.
7. **[[Fish Health Support]]**: Disease inspection and diagnostic services are provided to Federal, State, and private hatcheries, including testing of new therapeutic drugs.
8. **[[Wild Stock Health Surveys]]**: Wild fish populations are monitored for health status in systems including the [[San Juan River]], lower [[Colorado River]], and Fort Apache Indian Reservation.
9. **[[Invasive Species Management]]**: Monitoring and public education efforts target the spread of non-indigenous aquatic nuisance species, notably the [[Zebra Mussel]] and edible brown mussel.
10. **Public Outreach**: Limited public engagement is conducted via hatchery tours and participation in local fairs to raise awareness of fisheries conservation work.
