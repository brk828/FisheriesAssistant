## Executive Summary

This 1990 memorandum, authored by Dean A. Hendrickson of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], documents the grassroots formation of a binational scientific network focused on freshwater fishes of northern México. The initiative emerged from informal discussions among Mexican biologists and American counterparts during a field trip following the [[Desert Fishes Council]] meeting in Albuquerque (November 1989). The core motivation was frustration with Mexican research permit bureaucracy (administered by agencies such as [[SEDUE]] and [[SEPESCA]]), which was perceived as impeding scientific collaboration and information exchange across the U.S.–México border. The document is not a primary data report but an organizational record establishing the groundwork for a regional chapter or special-interest group under the newly formed [[Mexican Ichthyological Congress]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no specific habitat or species ecology data are presented, the document situates its subject matter within the broader ecological and conservation context of **desert freshwater fish communities in northern México**, an ecoregion of long-standing interest to organizations such as the [[Desert Fishes Council]]. This group has historically focused on isolated desert aquatic habitats (springs, streams, and endorheic basins) that support endemic and often imperiled fish faunas shared across the [[Sonoran Desert]] and adjacent U.S.–México border regions. The memo implicitly underscores the transboundary nature of these ecosystems, necessitating cooperative, cross-border research and permitting frameworks to study and conserve fish populations that span political boundaries.

## Key Findings

- A discussion during the **1989 Desert Fishes Council meeting** in Albuquerque catalyzed the effort to form a new binational collaborative group for Mexican freshwater fish research.
- Discussions followed a formal panel on **Mexican research permit regulations**, which were identified as a bureaucratic barrier to scientific collaboration and data exchange between Mexican and U.S. researchers.
- The inaugural meeting of the **[[Mexican Ichthyological Congress]]** in La Paz (1989) provided a model analogous to the [[American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists]] (ASIH) or the [[American Fisheries Society]] (AFS), offering a national framework under which regional chapters or special-interest groups (e.g., a "desert fishes" chapter) could be organized.
- The proposed group aimed to:
  - Facilitate information exchange and collaborative research programs between Mexican and U.S. biologists.
  - Potentially offer independent expert review of Mexican research permit proposals to agencies such as [[SEDUE]] and [[SEPESCA]].
- **Carlos Yururetagoyena** was identified as the ad hoc leader tasked with organizing the new group in México.
- Dean A. Hendrickson was assigned to compile a list of biologists (Mexican and American) known to be conducting, or planning, freshwater fish research in northern México, to be shared with Carlos Yururetagoyena as a foundational step toward reciprocal information sharing.
- The **Second Mexican National Ichthyological Congress** was scheduled for Monterrey, Nuevo León, timed near the **1990 Desert Fishes Council meeting in Ensenada**, with intent to formalize the new chapter/group at or before that event.
- The memo highlights an early, informal model of binational scientific diplomacy aimed at improving research access and cooperation for the study of imperiled desert fish species in the borderlands region.
