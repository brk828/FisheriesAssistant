## Executive Summary

This document is the 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Station Profile for the [[Saratoga National Fish Hatchery]] in Saratoga, Wyoming, established in 1915 under authority of 36 Stat. (March 4, 1911). The station's core mission centers on egg production and distribution for three [[Salmonidae]] species: [[Lake Trout]] (from the Lewis Lake strain), [[Brown Trout]], and [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]] (Trapper Lake strain). The hatchery supports the federal [[Great Lakes Restoration Program]] via lake trout egg supply and assists the State of Wyoming in developing a [[Little Snake River]]-Colorado River cutthroat trout broodstock program. The facility was undergoing a $2.1 million reconstruction during FY1993–1994, with on-site construction slated to begin in FY1994. Approximately 3,000 visitors attend the hatchery annually.

## Background & Description

The source document is an administrative station profile and does not report morphological, meristic, or anatomical data on the species reared. Species involved are identified only by common name/hatchery strain designation:
- **[[Lake Trout]]** — Lewis Lake strain broodstock
- **[[Brown Trout]]** — no strain specified
- **[[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]]** — Trapper Lake strain

No length, weight, coloration, or fin-ray data are provided in this record.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not a field ecology study, the profile indicates the ecological/management context for each species:

- **[[Lake Trout]] (Salvelinus namaycush)**: Eggs produced at Saratoga NFH (Lewis Lake strain) are shipped to support restoration stocking efforts in the [[Great Lakes]], where native lake trout populations had been historically depleted.
- **[[Brown Trout]] (Salmo trutta)**: Non-native salmonid propagated for distribution to state and federal waters across multiple states, supporting recreational and restoration fisheries.
- **[[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]] (Oncorhynchus clarkii pleuriticus)**: A regionally native subspecies of conservation concern; the hatchery is developing a broodstock sourced from the [[Little Snake River]]–[[Colorado River]] drainage to support state conservation and restoration efforts in Wyoming.

Physical rearing habitat includes 16 small (3'×30') and 9 large (6'×60') concrete raceways, 12 hypalon-lined ponds (20'×40'), and a quarter-acre dirt pond, supplied by 5 on-site wells — indicating a controlled freshwater hatchery environment rather than open aquatic habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Station mission**: Produce 5–7.5 million eyed [[Lake Trout]] eggs annually for the Great Lakes restoration program and 3.5 million [[Brown Trout]] eggs; assist Wyoming in establishing a [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]] broodstock.
- **FY1993 egg shipments**:
  - Lake trout: 3,803,564 eyed eggs + 2,065,561 green eggs
  - Brown trout: 2,213,816 eyed eggs
  - Colorado River (Trapper Lake strain) cutthroat trout: 200,532 eyed eggs
- **FY1993 fish distribution** (excludes eggs/fry): 105,227 total fish distributed — 12,937 to other federal waters and 85,290 to state waters; none to FWS or private waters.
- **Distribution trend (FY1991–1993)**: Total fish distributed declined year-over-year — 162,894 (FY91) → 152,586 (FY92) → 105,227 (FY93).
- **Distribution range**: Fish (species codes BNT, CUT, LAT) were shipped to 15 states: California, Colorado, Idaho, Kentucky, Michigan, Montana, New Hampshire, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Pennsylvania, Tennessee, Utah, Wisconsin, and Wyoming.
- **Budget/staffing (1991–1995)**: Operations budget grew from $243.1K (1991) to a projected $285.0K (1995); maintenance funding was highly variable ($38.0K in 1991, $0 in 1993, projected $35.0K in 1995); FTE staffing remained stable around 5.0–5.6.
- **Infrastructure**: Major $2.1 million reconstruction project underway, with on-site construction beginning in FY1994; contributing agency includes the [[Bureau of Land Management]].
- **Public engagement**: The hatchery serves as a public resource, hosting approximately 3,000 visitors per year.
