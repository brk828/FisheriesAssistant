# Bonita Creek Unique Waters Nomination

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #riparian-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1989 nomination, prepared by the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] Safford District Office and the City of Safford for the [[Arizona Department Of Environmental Quality]], proposes a 15-mile segment of [[Bonita Creek]] for designation as a Unique Water under Arizona Administrative Code R18-11-303. The creek is a major municipal water source for roughly 15,000 people in the Gila Valley, supports critical riparian habitat for threatened and endangered species, and offers recreational, scientific and cultural value. The authors conclude that the designation would ensure protection of water quality better than statewide standards in most parameters, with no known conflicts with current or anticipated uses. Proposed standards are presented in Tables 2 and 3.

## Background & Description

**Location and extent.** The creek drains a watershed of about 236,000 acres (370 sq mi). The nominated segment (about 32,300 acres, 50 sq mi) begins where the creek leaves the [[San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation]] and flows south-southeast for 15 stream miles to the [[Gila River]], about 15 miles northeast of Safford. Elevations range from 4,976 ft to 3,200 ft.

**Land ownership.** Within the segment, the BLM administers 92% of land, the City of Safford 2% and private holdings 6%. The upper watershed lies on the reservation and is outside BLM or City control.

**Hydrology.** The creek is chiefly perennial, one of the few perennial streams in southeastern Arizona. Some reaches dry during hot summers and droughts. The [[U.S. Geological Survey]] gage (Station 09447800, operating from August 1, 1981) recorded a minimum of 0.68 cfs (December 1982) and a maximum of 19,400 cfs (October 2, 1983). The BLM holds an application for an instream flow right of 5 cfs, and the City holds a water right of 3,240,000 gallons per day (granted 1953).

**Soils and geology.** Shallow volcanic-derived soils dominate the mountains (Limpia-Graham-Rock outcrop; Peloncillo-Orthents-Pinaleno complexes). Torrifluvents-Riverwash soils occur along the channel.

**Climate.** Annual precipitation averages 10–15 inches, mostly in late summer. Summer temperatures average 85–90°F in July and winter minima 40–45°F in January. Exceptional floods have had the largest impact on canyon riparian vegetation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish.** Five native fish species breed in the creek. The nomination reports one of the greatest fish biomasses recorded in any southwestern stream, 46.7 g/m². Listed fish include the [[Gila Chub]] (federal candidate Category 2; state threatened) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (introduced by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]]). Other fish noted are the Gila sucker, desert mountain sucker, speckled dace and longfin dace.

**Birds and other wildlife.** Bonita Creek supports 64 breeding bird species, among the highest recorded in the United States. More than 100 Migratory Bird Treaty Act species have been recorded (Appendix 3). Listed birds include the [[Southern Bald Eagle]] (federally endangered), [[Peregrine Falcon]] (federally endangered), [[Black Hawk]], [[Northern Beardless Tyrannulet]] and [[Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]]. Reptile and amphibian species total 55 and 9. The area also has 58 confirmed mammal species, and the [[Gila Monster]] is a federal candidate.

**Riparian vegetation.** The creek hosts one of the largest continuous riparian stretches in the region. Historic grazing degraded the original [[Fremont Cottonwood]], [[Arizona Sycamore]], Goodding willow and Arizona walnut community toward mesquite bosque and scrub. Recent livestock management has allowed overstory trees and herbaceous layers to recover.

**Threats to water quality** identified in the nomination include uncontrolled cattle use (raising coliform counts), watershed grazing (erosion and sediment), off-road vehicle use, mining claims, woodcutting, floods, brush invasion after fire control, and the uncontrolled upper watershed.

**Management framework.** Relevant plans include the [[Geronimo Management Framework Plan]], the [[Gila Box Coordinated Resource Management Plan]], the [[Gila-Peloncillo Habitat Management Plan]], the [[Upper Gila-San Simon Grazing EIS]] and the [[Cooperative Management Agreement]] (1986) between the BLM and the City.

## Key Findings

- **Designation basis:** The nomination argues that Bonita Creek qualifies under antidegradation criteria R18-11-202C (high-quality water and exceptional ecological significance) and 202D (critical habitat for threatened or endangered species). It also meets eight of ten secondary criteria under R18-11-303.
- **Water quality (Sommerfeld 1977–78):** Overall quality was good. Total coliforms ranged from under 500 to about 25,000 and exceeded surface water standards about one-third of the time. About 15% of contamination was attributed to human sources.
- **Water quality (BLM 1986–88):** Most parameters met state standards. Exceptions were iron, fecal and total coliforms, and one mercury and one copper result. Iron exceedances were linked to a major flood in August 1987.
- **Proposed standards:** New standards are proposed for arsenic (0.010 mg/L), lead (0.020), iron (1.9), sulfates (23.0), phosphates (21.0), total dissolved solids (309 mg/L) and turbidity (2.74 NTU), among others. The proposed fecal coliform single-sample value is 150 CFU/100 mL, with a geometric mean of 20. The interim fecal streptococcus standard is 3,034 CFU/100 mL, with a final value due June 1990.
- **Livestock and bacteria:** Cattle reductions on one allotment (from a permit of 287 to under 100) and riparian fencing lowered bacterial input. The allottee now runs the full permit of 287, so further data collection is required.
- **Management commitments:** Riparian planting (about 170 trees in 1985, 200 in 1986, 71 in 1987, 9 in 1988), fencing, a proposed 2,747-acre [[Area Of Critical Environmental Concern]], and a planned ACEC management plan by 1993.
- **Recognition:** In 1988 the Western Division of the [[American Fisheries Society]] gave the Safford District its top national award for riparian management, recognizing the Bonita Creek Project.
- **Cultural resources:** Eight known cliff dwellings (one NRHP-eligible in 1976), six rock art sites and a 13th-century Anasazi migration route. The nomination suggests the canyon could merit NRHP district listing.
- **Conclusion:** No known conflicts with current or anticipated use are expected, and designation is compatible with municipal supply, wildlife habitat and recreation.
