# Annotated Checklist and Identification Keys to the Freshwater Fishes of Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #texas-fishes #checklist

## Executive Summary

This 1991 supplement to the *Texas Journal of Science* by Huss, Edwards, and Garrett provides an annotated checklist of 247 fish species in 45 families recorded from Texas waters, together with dichotomous keys to families, genera and species. Of the 247 species, roughly two-thirds are exclusively freshwater; the remaining 78 are estuarine or marine but may enter low-salinity habitats. Five native taxa are considered [[Extinction|extinct]] and three are extirpated from the state. About 20 percent of Texas freshwater fishes are of conservation concern. The authors attribute declines mainly to water quantity problems (reservoir impoundment and dewatering), habitat degradation and [[Introduced Species]].

## Background & Description

The state's fauna is notable for diversity and endemism, owing to its large area, discontinuous drainages and position in a broad transition zone between major physiographic provinces (Hubbs, 1957b; Edwards et al., 1989). The recorded count has grown over time, from 154 species (Evermann and Kendall, 1894) to 190 (Knapp, 1953), 226 (Hubbs, 1982) and the present 247. Changes arise from synonymy (e.g., *Fundulus kansae* placed under *F. zebrinus*), taxonomic splits (e.g., *Dionda serena* and *D. argentosa* split from *D. episcopa*, Mayden et al., 1992), 18 established exotic species, and range extensions such as [[Least Killifish]] in the Sabine River.

The authors provide dichotomous keys to families and species, with notes on range, status and ecological association (Freshwater, Estuarine, Marine). Scientific and common names follow Robins et al. (1991), with proposed new common names such as Nueces roundnose minnow (*Dionda serena*) and manantial roundnose minnow (*D. argentosa*). The keys are adapted from Hubbs's earlier teaching keys and are described as preliminary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Conservation status is summarized in Table 1, which lists species judged extinct, extirpated, endangered, threatened or of special concern by the Texas Organization for Endangered Species (TOES), Texas Parks and Wildlife Department (TPWD) and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS). Key threats identified include:

- **Water quantity:** too much (reservoirs) or too little (dewatering) flow.
- **Habitat quality:** degradation of streams and springs.
- **Introduced species:** Table 2 lists exotic fishes with established populations, including [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Common Carp]], [[Grass Carp]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Blue Tilapia]] and [[Guppy]].

Regional patterns are severe: in the Chihuahuan Desert (west of the Pecos River), about half of native fish species are threatened or extinct. Springs and spring-fed headwaters host many endemic and listed taxa, notably the [[Edwards Aquifer]] blindcats and several pupfish and gambusia. The authors note that 47 species are listed in Table 1 and that TOES lists 35 species as listed or deserving listing; the USFWS lists six, and TPWD lists 21.

## Key Findings

- **Richness:** 247 species in 45 families; 169 native species rarely enter estuaries; 78 are estuarine or marine.
- **Extinct (5):** [[Phantom Shiner]] (*Notropis orca*), [[Rio Grande Bluntnose Shiner]] (*N. simus simus*), Maravillas red shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis blairi*), [[Amistad Gambusia]] (*Gambusia amistadensis*) and [[San Marcos Gambusia]] (*G. georgei*).
- **Extirpated (3):** [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarki virginalis*), [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]] (*Hybognathus amarus*) and [[Blotched Gambusia]] (*G. senilis*).
- **Conservation concern:** about 20% of freshwater species; more than 5% of strictly freshwater native species lost to extinction or extirpation.
- **Listed taxa:** six listed as threatened or endangered by USFWS; 21 by TPWD; 35 by TOES; 47 species combined in Table 1.
- **Notable endangered species:** [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*, TPWD endangered), [[Fountain Darter]] (*Etheostoma fonticola*), [[Devils River Minnow]] (*Dionda diaboli*), [[Clear Creek Gambusia]] (*G. heterochir*) and several *Cyprinodon* pupfish.
- **Taxonomic changes:** species added through splits, exotic establishment and range extensions; some synonymized.
- **Recommended protection:** the authors call for protection of threatened species and a fuller separate volume on Texas fishes.
