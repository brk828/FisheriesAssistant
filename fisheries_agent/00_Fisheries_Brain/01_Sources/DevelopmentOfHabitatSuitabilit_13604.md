# Development of Habitat Suitability Index Curves for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a project package issued by [[BIO/WEST Inc]] on December 12, 1986, to participants in a contract from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS). The contract aims to produce habitat suitability index (SI) curves for [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the upper Colorado River Basin (UCRB). The package summarizes a computerized database of roughly 100,000 records in 86 datafiles, describes previous curve-development studies, and sets out the workshop procedures and schedule. The [[Bonytail]] is excluded because of its extreme rarity and lack of quantitative data.

## Background & Description

**Regulatory and analytical framework.** The FWS uses the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM), a modular system that simulates habitat as a function of streamflow (Bovee 1982; Milhous et al. 1984). SI curves are central to IFIM and describe relative preference and tolerance for depth, velocity, substrate, cover and temperature. The 1984 FWS curves were criticized for excluding non-FWS data, poor documentation, and lack of agreement on methods.

**Project objectives.** The goal is to generate the best possible SI curves for all life stages of the three species, using existing data and expert judgement. Objectives are to:
- classify database datasets as suitable for quantitative analysis or only for substantiating habitat use;
- develop "category two" (data-generated) curves using agreed methods;
- identify data gaps and develop interim "category one" (judgement or literature-based) curves;
- fully document the project; and
- obtain expert consensus on all phases.

**The working database (FWS/DATABASE).** Collections date from May 1964 (C. David Vanicek) to 1985, with some 1986 data. Records are stored in 86 datafiles, generally one investigator, year and river. Each datafile has a narrative and is summarized into datasets by life stage. Table 2 defines the hierarchy: datafiles → datasets → partitions → SI curves. Problems noted by BIO/WEST include inconsistent definitions of life stages (especially "larvae"), keypunch errors in fish counts, inconsistent missing-data codes, and inconsistent terminology.

**Partitioning.** Of 108 summary tables produced, only 39 contain depth, velocity and substrate data. Partitions were organized by life stage (adult, juvenile, young-of-the-year, larvae), river, stratum, month, gear type, and availability of depth/velocity/substrate data.

**Previous curve development.** Three earlier studies are reviewed:
- [[Holden 1977]] (Green River, Jensen to Green River, Utah): habitat preferences from fish collected by seining and electrofishing; noted backwater use by young squawfish.
- [[Twedt and Holden 1980]] (San Juan River, New Mexico and Utah): used 156 adult squawfish with depth, velocity and substrate data; adult curves split into pre-runoff, runoff and post-runoff periods.
- [[Valdez et al. 1982]] (Upper Colorado River, Colorado River Fishery Project): suitability criteria from 377 young-of-the-year, 34 juvenile and 105 larger squawfish, 36 juvenile and 184 adult humpback chub, and 52 adult razorback suckers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** The database covers the Green, Yampa, Colorado, White, Gunnison, Dolores and Duchesne rivers, plus Ashley Creek. Key river-mile ranges include the Green River (about RM 22 to 410) and the Colorado River (about RM 0 to 223). Major structures discussed include [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] (impounded 1962; penstock modified June 1982), the [[Taylor Draw Dam]] on the White River, and the [[Redlands Diversion Dam]] on the Gunnison River.

**Data sources.** Datafiles were produced by [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] (CRFP) sampling, [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] (CDOW) monitoring, university theses, and consultant work (e.g., [[Ecosystems Research Institute]]). The standardized CRFP design divided river reaches into strata, randomly selected sample sites within pre-runoff, runoff and post-runoff periods, and sampled diel periods.

**Gear and life stages.** Gear included electrofishing, trammel and gill nets, seines (various mesh sizes), and radiotelemetry. Many records lack life-stage or size information; most fish were not recorded by size class.

**Data restrictions.** Only part of each datafile is habitat data for target species; the remainder concerns non-target fish.

## Key Findings

**Scope and schedule**
- Workshop #1: January 5–9, 1987, Salt Lake City (Quality Inn; $35/night rooms; optional luncheon about $5/day).
- Workshop #2: March 9–13, 1987, Fort Collins.
- Deliverables: draft report about February 20, 1987; documentation of Workshop #1 by about January 16, 1987 (exact date as given in the table); draft final report April 17, 1987; final report May 29, 1987.
- Panel: 25–30 attendees, of whom 6 are designated species experts holding decision authority. Facilitator, database manager and data analyst (all BIO/WEST) have no decision-making authority.

**Database contents**
- About 100,000 records in 86 datafiles.
- Partitioning produced 108 summary tables; 39 include depth, velocity and substrate data.
- Workshop #1 aims: decide which datasets to use and how to stratify, pool and analyze them.
- Workshop #2 aims: approve category two curves and develop category one curves for data-poor life stages and variables.

**Selected quantitative records from datafile narratives** (counts as reported; most fish were not recorded by life stage)
- [[GR64VAN]] (1964, Green and Yampa rivers, 780 records): 240 Colorado squawfish and 38 razorback suckers recorded in total; no humpback chub captured.
- [[GR79I]] (1979, Green River, 1,260 records): 233 young-of-the-year and 39 juvenile Colorado squawfish, with 1 adult razorback sucker.
- [[CO79DATA]] (1979, upper Colorado River, 866 records): 259 young-of-the-year, 45 juvenile and 54 adult Colorado squawfish; 96 adult humpback chub.
- [[RADIO]] (1980–1985, Green River, 1,052 records): 973 adult and 22 juvenile Colorado squawfish, and 32 adult razorback suckers, all from radiotelemetry.
- [[HBCHUBS]] (1980–1986, Green River, 97 records): 25 juvenile and 71 adult humpback chub, identified by photographs and morphometrics.
- [[CO84BOR]] (1984, upper Lake Powell, 234 records): 47 adult Colorado squawfish and 11 adult razorback suckers.
- Several datafiles (e.g., [[DOLORES]], [[CO74KID]], [[CO72KID]]) contain no target-species records.

**Known data issues to be reviewed before Workshop #1**
- Inconsistent life-stage definitions across datafiles.
- Keypunch errors in fish counts.
- Varied missing-data formats.
- Inconsistent terminology within agencies.

**Participant tasks**
- Review datafile narratives, the summary tables in the Appendix, and the datafiles each participant knows well.
- Report suspected errors to BIO/WEST before Workshop #1.

**Agenda highlights (Workshop #1)**
- Days 1–2: orientation, database description, analyses overview, dataset evaluation.
- Day 3: expert consensus on datasets; day 4: consensus on data analysis methods.
- Facilitated by [[P. Holden]]; database presentation by [[R. Valdez]]; analysis methods by [[T. Hardy]].
