# Trip Report: White River, September 19-21, 1988

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #loach-minnow

## Executive Summary
This field report documents a two-day fish sampling survey conducted on the North Fork and mainstem of the [[White River]] (White Mountain Apache Reservation, Arizona) in September 1988. The survey was conducted in support of a Section 7 consultation with the Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) regarding the proposed **Miner Flat Dam and Reservoir**. The primary objective was to determine the current distribution and status of the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), a species of conservation concern, within the project impact area. Sampling across nine sites using backpack/ATV-mounted electrofishing and seine nets found only **one loach minnow** individual, despite the presence of seemingly suitable habitat throughout much of the surveyed reach. The report also assesses potential barriers to fish movement and habitat/thermal impacts anticipated from the proposed dam.

## Background & Description
The report does not provide detailed morphometric data but notes habitat-specific substrate associations relevant to species identification and habitat suitability:

- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): Associated specifically with large rubble substrate in swift, shallow water (6 inches to 1 foot deep); a benthic riffle specialist.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Widely distributed across all sites sampled; found in similar riffle/rubble habitats as loach minnow.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*) and **[[Sonora Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*): Common throughout, found co-occurring with dace across gravel, rubble, and embedded-fine-sediment substrates.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Salmo gairdneri*): Collected only at North Fork sites near Alchesay Hatchery, likely reflecting hatchery influence/stocking.
- **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus dolomievi*): A single small individual taken in a deep, slow-moving side channel — noted as a non-native predator of concern.

Specimens of speckled dace and loach minnow were preserved and sent to Arizona State University for identification confirmation and museum lodging.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat structure**: The White River system features riffle-dominated reaches with large rubble/gravel substrate in swift, shallow (2 in–1 ft) flows — the preferred [[Loach Minnow]] microhabitat. Some riffle areas were noted as degraded by fine sediment embeddedness, reducing interstitial habitat quality.
- **Riparian condition**: Riparian vegetation (large willow and cottonwood) was generally in good condition along both the North Fork and mainstem, contributing to bank stability and canopy cover.
- **Thermal regime**: Water temperatures recorded during the survey ranged from 57–60°F. The report raises concern that a proposed bottom-release dam at Miner Flat would discharge cold water, and that the deep, heavily shaded canyon geomorphology downstream would slow thermal recovery, potentially altering thermal regimes unfavorable to native fish.
- **Anthropogenic disturbance**: A large active gravel mining operation on the North Fork (near Site 7) had dredged and rerouted approximately 0.25–0.5 miles of the channel against the canyon wall, altering natural riffle habitat.
- **Geomorphic barriers**: A approximately 2.5-mile-long, vertical-walled canyon (approximately 100 feet deep) near the town of Whiteriver, containing deep pools between sheer cliffs, is hypothesized as a potential dispersal barrier preventing upstream [[Loach Minnow]] colonization of the North Fork from the East Fork/mainstem population.
- **Exotic species pressure**: Presence of non-native [[Smallmouth Bass]] and [[Rainbow Trout]] raises concern regarding predation pressure on native cyprinids and catostomids, particularly if the Miner Flat project increases exotic predator abundance in the [[East Fork White River]].

## Key Findings
- Only **one loach minnow** was captured during the entire two-day, nine-site survey, at Site 2 (mainstem White River near Canyon Day, approximately 2.6 river miles below the East/North Fork confluence).
- No loach minnow were found at the East/North Fork confluence (Site 1), despite this being a historically productive collection site (per [[Propst et al. 1985]] and [[Elliott 1988]] surveys).
- No loach minnow were detected in any North Fork sites (Sites 4–9), despite habitat that appeared visually suitable, suggesting either a real distributional limit or a sampling/detection issue.
- The [[East Fork White River]], which historically held the largest documented loach minnow population, was **not sampled** during this trip due to time constraints and recent (Feb 1988) survey data; however, it will not be directly impacted by the Miner Flat project's physical footprint, though indirect impacts from increased exotic predators are anticipated.
- A significant canyon barrier near the town of Whiteriver is proposed as a possible explanation for the apparent absence of loach minnow in the North Fork, isolating it from source populations downstream/in the East Fork.
- The proposed diversion for the Canyon Day irrigation portion of the Miner Flat project is located near Site 3, approximately 1 river mile below the [[Loach Minnow]] capture site at Site 2 — raising direct concern for the sole recent mainstem occurrence.
- Field logistics: A pulsed DC backpack/ATV-mounted electrofishing unit with a single anode and cable cathode was used; an 8-foot seine proved unwieldy in swift current and was replaced with a 4-foot kick seine after Site 1.
- The report recommends further investigation (e.g., walking the canyon rim) to determine whether suitable riffle habitat exists within the Whiteriver canyon barrier, which could clarify loach minnow distributional limits.
