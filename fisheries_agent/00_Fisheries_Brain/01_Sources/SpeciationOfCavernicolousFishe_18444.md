# Speciation of Cavernicolous Fishes: Comparative Studies of Two American Synbranchidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #synbranchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1972 comparative study by [[Jakob Parzefall]] and [[Horst Wilkens]] (Zoological Institute and Museum, University of Hamburg) compares the widespread surface swamp eel *Synbranchus marmoratus* with its eye- and pigment-reduced cave derivative *Furmastix infernalis* from Yucatan caves. The authors examine noses, lateral line systems, neuromasts, taste buds, meristic counts, eyes and pigmentation. They find the two forms largely alike in structure, with the cave fish showing more free neuromasts and taste buds and a longer tail. They conclude that the generic and specific separation, based mainly on eye and pigment reduction, is questionable. They also argue that *F. infernalis* is the phylogenetically younger cave fish, a conclusion they link to geological history.

## Background & Description

- **Study organisms:** *Synbranchus marmoratus* is a light-avoiding, nocturnal species that hides under stones and occurs across Middle and South America. *Furmastix infernalis* is an elongated, nearly cylindrical, eyeless cave fish with reduced fins. Its eye is buried under the skin and is visible only as a dark spot in young, half-grown individuals.
- **Meristics (Table 1):** Total length ranged from 215 to 325 mm in *F. infernalis* and from 88 to 290 mm in *S. marmoratus*. The A–S index (after-snout-tip distance relative to total length) was about 0.37–0.54 in *F. infernalis* and 0.67–0.70 in *S. marmoratus*. Vertebral counts were 144–145 (caudal) and 67 (trunk) in *F. infernalis*, and 119–125 (caudal) and 69–75 (trunk) in *S. marmoratus*. The longer tail of *F. infernalis* is reflected in these vertebral counts.
- **Behaviour:** In the laboratory, *F. infernalis* was active by day, alternating activity and rest phases, and sheltered between stones or under glass. *S. marmoratus* hid in the substrate during the day and left only in darkness.
- **Sample:** The study used 4 specimens of *F. infernalis* (plus the type specimen after Hubbs, 1938) and 5 of *S. marmoratus*. The authors note that the small sample may have missed variability.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Eyes:** In *S. marmoratus*, the eye lies under a secondary "lens" of skin (cornea formed from dermal and scleral layers). The retina has photoreceptors, but the outer nuclear and fibre layers are thin, so the eye is interpreted as a reducing organ. In *F. infernalis*, the eye has lost photoreceptors, outer retinal layers, and most of the optic nerve. The pigment epithelium has flattened and lost its retinomotor function.
- **Pigmentation:** Cave fish are paler than surface fish. The authors report that *F. infernalis* has only 3.3% of the body melanophores of *S. marmoratus* in light-reared individuals. Melanin per cell, however, is unchanged, so the authors interpret the reduction as genetic (loss mutations).
- **Nose:** Paired inhalant openings lie under the lip, and paired exhalant openings lie between the eyes. Water flow is directed through the nasal tube by sac expansion and contraction, and this was confirmed with dye-marked water.
- **Prey and sensing:** The authors suggest that *F. infernalis* detects blind cave shrimp (*Creaseria morleyi*) through its neuromasts. Shrimp approach did not trigger the escape response that other stimuli did.
- **Geology:** The authors argue that the northern Yucatan rose from the sea in the Pleistocene. This isolated cave populations of *S. marmoratus*, whose surface relatives are still found only in coastal waters.

## Key Findings

1. **Nose and lateral line:** The structure of the nose and cephalic lateral line system is the same in both forms. The main difference is that the cave fish has one fewer lateral line pore in the temporal canal (the surface fish has an additional pore at the head base).
2. **Neuromasts and taste buds:** *F. infernalis* has more free neuromasts (about 5–10 per row, distributed across intervening head areas) and a slightly higher number of taste buds, with a wider covered area. The authors view this as compensatory enhancement, similar to patterns in [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] cave forms and [[Poecilia Sphenops]] cave forms.
3. **Meristics:** Apart from tail length (more vertebrae in the caudal region of *F. infernalis*), the meristic data show no differences. The authors question the generic and specific separation of the two forms.
4. **Eye and pigment reduction:** Eye and pigment reduction are less advanced in *F. infernalis* than in [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] cave populations. The authors conclude that *F. infernalis* is the phylogenetically younger cave fish, a view they support with geological evidence.
5. **Mechanism:** Cave isolation following Pleistocene uplift allowed degenerative alleles to accumulate under relaxed selection (see [[Degenerative Evolution]]).
6. **Taxonomic implication:** The authors question whether the separate species and genus status of the two Yucatan forms is justified, in line with earlier revisions of *Astyanax mexicanus*.
