# Addition of *Misgurnus anguillicaudatus* (Cantor) to the Californian Fauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cobitidae #invasive-species #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1969 note documents the first verified record of free-living [[Japanese Weatherfish]] (*[[Misgurnus Anguillicaudatus]]*) in California, discovered April 12, 1968, in the Westminster flood control channel, Orange County. Approximately 300 individuals (16–224 mm TL) were collected across five surveys (April–July 1968), with size-range evidence indicating active reproduction rather than mere escapement from a nearby aquaculture facility ([[Pacific Goldfish Farm]]). The species, native to northeastern Asia, was likely introduced via pond escapement during the 1950s and continued low-level escape from indoor tanks whose waste water drains into the channel. The authors conclude *M. anguillicaudatus* is now established in California, joining a prior established population in Michigan ([[Schultz 1960]]).

## Background & Description
- **Total length range observed:** 16–224 mm TL (approx. 300 specimens collected).
- **Body form:** Elongate, eel-like body typical of [[Cobitidae]] (loaches); habit of burrowing into substrate.
- **Comparative morphology:** Closely resembles the European weatherfish, *[[Misgurnus Fossilis]]*, in both appearance and life habits.
- **Respiratory adaptation:** Capable of facultative intestinal (aerial) respiration — fish surface, swallow air, and pass it through the vascularized hindgut, with exhausted air expelled as bubbles from the vent (Sterba 1966). This adaptation permits survival in hypoxic waters.
- **Voucher specimens:** Deposited at Scripps Institution of Oceanography fish collection (SIO 68-213); identified by [[Carl Hubbs]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Found within ~3 miles of the 9.5-mile-long Westminster flood control channel; distribution bounded by tidal influence to the west (near Graham Street) and intermittent water flow to the east (railroad overpass west of Central Memorial Park).
- **Tolerance:** Intestinal air-breathing allows persistence in low-dissolved-oxygen environments typical of degraded urban channels — an ecological advantage over strictly gill-respiring competitors.
- **Burrowing behavior:** Substrate-burrowing habit, combined with air-breathing capacity, makes eradication difficult.
- **Co-occurring exotic fauna:** The channel supported a diverse assemblage of non-native species, suggesting the site functions as an ornamental-fish release/escape point:
  - Fishes (12 spp. total, including the weatherfish): [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Guppy]] (*Lebistes reticulatus*), [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Swordtail]] (*Xiphophorus helleri*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), [[Sunset Variatus]] (*Xiphophorus variatus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Platy]] (*Xiphophorus maculatus*), [[Molly]] (*Poecilia sphenops*), [[Angelfish]] (*Pterophyllum sp.*), [[Zebrafish]] (*Brachydanio rerio*).
  - Exotic snails (3 spp.): [[Mystery Snail]] (*Ampullaria cuprina*), [[Papershell Snail]] (*Lymnaea auricularia*), [[Japanese Livebearing Snail]] (*Viviparus malleatus*).
  - Exotic amphibian: [[African Clawed Frog]] (*Xenopus laevis*).
- **Presumed origin/vector:** [[Pacific Goldfish Farm]] on Bolsa Avenue, Westminster — all exotic species collected are commercially handled by this ornamental fish farm; owner Joe Akiyama confirmed loaches were reared in outdoor ponds with subsequent escape into the channel, and waste water discharge continues to provide an escape pathway from indoor tanks.
- **Broader biogeographic context:** Native distribution spans northeastern Asia to central China; also established as an introduced species in Michigan, USA ([[Schultz 1960]]), indicating a pattern of repeated introduction via the aquarium/bait trade.

## Key Findings
1. First documented free-living population of *Misgurnus anguillicaudatus* in California, confirmed via identification by Carl Hubbs and voucher deposition at Scripps Institution of Oceanography (SIO 68-213).
2. Population size (~300 collected, size range 16–224 mm TL) and presence of multiple size classes provide direct evidence of successful in-situ reproduction, not just repeated escapement.
3. Species' unique intestinal (aerial) respiration allows survival in oxygen-poor waters, and its burrowing behavior complicates potential eradication efforts.
4. The [[Pacific Goldfish Farm]] is identified as the probable point source for this and numerous other exotic aquatic species (fish, snails, amphibians) found in the channel.
5. Potential future risk: Japanese weatherfish are used as bait fish elsewhere, raising concern about secondary introduction into sport fishing waters; ecological interactions with game fish species remain unknown.
6. This California record parallels an earlier established introduction in Michigan (Schultz 1960), suggesting *M. anguillicaudatus* has repeatedly established viable populations outside its native range in North America.
