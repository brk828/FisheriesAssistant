# Biological Opinion for San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge Master Plan

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This biological opinion, issued March 11, 1986, evaluates a 10-year Master Plan for [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] in [[Cochise County]], [[Arizona]]. The refuge was established to provide and enhance habitat for 6 species of native [[Rio Yaqui]] fish. The Master Plan proposes experimental habitat restoration and species reintroduction strategies to establish self-sustaining populations of rare fish species. At acquisition in 1982, only 2 of the 6 target species remained: endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] (Gila purpurea) at one site and endangered [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis) in 7 populations. The opinion concludes the action is not likely to jeopardize listed species and would promote their conservation, though acknowledges the experimental nature and potential for population losses.

## Background & Description

[[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] was acquired by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in 1982 specifically to conserve 6 rare [[Rio Yaqui]] fish species. The Master Plan addresses:

**Endangered Species:**
- [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) - found at one site in 1982
- [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*) - found in 7 populations in 1982

**Threatened Species:**
- [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*) - historically known from refuge
- [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) - believed to have occurred historically

**Other Target Species:**
- [[Yaqui Sucker]] (*Catostomus bernardini*) - historically known from refuge
- [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*) - candidate category 2, believed to have occurred historically

**Common Species for Native Assemblage:**
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)

The Plan also briefly addresses two raptor species: [[Peregrine Falcon]] (*Falco peregrinus*) and [[Aplomado Falcon]] (*Falco femoralis*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The refuge's aquatic habitats depend on an incompletely understood aquifer system. A 3-year aquifer study initiated in 1985 was designed to answer basic questions about water availability and sustainability. The biological opinion acknowledges critical information gaps regarding species viability in different aquatic habitats and species interactions.

**Habitat Types:**
- Springs (16 existing water sources)
- Ponds
- Streams
- [[Cienega]] (wetland) habitat

**Terrestrial Restoration:**
The Plan proposes grassland restoration through reseeding, brush control, and mesquite thinning. [[Black Draw]] restoration includes check dams to slow erosion and potentially restore surface flow, which would raise water table levels and recreate historical cienega habitat.

**Aquatic Habitat Development:**
Development projects at 16 existing springs, ponds, and wells aim to create diverse, isolated systems to:
- Promote genetic diversity among [[Rio Yaqui]] fish populations
- Prevent refuge-wide infestations of exotic species
- Provide specialized feeding, cover, and spawning areas

Water levels would be maintained strictly by artesian flows until aquifer study completion. Pumping might be implemented if studies demonstrate aquifer capacity.

## Key Findings

**Implementation Strategy:**

*Phase I (Years 1-5):*
- [[East Border Spring]]
- [[West Border Spring]]
- [[Bathouse Spring]]
- [[Bunting Spring]]
- [[Black Draw]]
- [[House Pond Channel]]

*Phase II (Years 6-10):*
- [[NE Windmill]]
- [[Tule Pond]]
- [[Twin Pond]]
- [[Cienega Spring]]
- [[Middle Spring]]
- [[Cottonwood Spring]]
- [[Upland Spring]]

*Completed or Unmodified Sites:*
- [[House Pond]] (already developed)
- [[North Pond]] (no physical modification)
- [[Snail Spring]] (no physical modification)

**Target Population Goals (10-year completion):**
- 7 populations of [[Yaqui Chub]]
- 5 populations of [[Beautiful Shiner]]
- 5 populations of [[Yaqui Catfish]]
- 15 populations of [[Sonoran Topminnow]]
- 6 populations of [[Yaqui Sucker]]
- 4 populations of [[Longfin Dace]]
- 5 populations of [[Mexican Stoneroller]]
- 1 population of [[Roundtail Chub]]

**Biological Opinion Conclusions:**
The action is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Sonoran Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], or [[Yaqui Chub]]. If successful, the Plan would promote conservation of these species and support [[Peregrine Falcon]] and [[Aplomado Falcon]] through terrestrial habitat restoration. The Plan's strategies could enable downlisting or delisting of threatened and endangered fish species.

**Management Approach:**
The biological opinion emphasizes the experimental nature of the program, requiring:
- Trial and error methodology
- Flexible management to incorporate new information
- Refinement, alteration, or elimination of strategies based on completed phases
- Acknowledgment that project features may fail, potentially resulting in loss of listed fish populations

**Consultation Requirements:**
Formal [[Section 7 Consultation]] should be reinitiated if:
- Substantive changes occur in Master Plan features
- Significant new information emerges on listed species
- Additional species are listed
