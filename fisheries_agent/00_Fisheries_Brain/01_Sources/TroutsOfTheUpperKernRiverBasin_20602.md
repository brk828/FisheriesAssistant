# Trouts of the Upper Kern River Basin, California, with Reference to Systematics and Evolution of Western North American Salmo

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

Schreck and Behnke (1971) revise the five endemic trout names described from the upper [[Kern River]] drainage of the southern [[Sierra Nevada]] into two subspecies of *Salmo aguabonita*: *S. a. aguabonita* ([[California Golden Trout]]) and *S. a. gilberti* ([[Kern River Trout]]). The authors argue that the golden trout is not a local isolate of the coastal [[Rainbow Trout]] (*S. gairdneri*) as previously assumed, but a member of a distinct "golden trout complex" of nonrainbow, noncutthroat trouts. Based on meristic, karyotypic, morphological and zoogeographic evidence, they elevate *S. aguabonita* to full species status.

## Background & Description

The study addresses five names: *S. aguabonita* Jordan, *S. roosevelti* Evermann, *S. gairdneri gilberti* Jordan, *S. whitei* Evermann and *S. rosei* Jordan & McGregor. Fish were collected by angling at 67 sites in the summers of 1967 and 1968, preserved in 10% buffered formalin, and examined for coloration, spotting, basibranchial teeth, fin ray counts, vertebrae (by X-ray), lateral line and scale counts, pyloric caeca, and gill rakers. Coloration and spotting were observed in more than 1000 trout, and roughly 500 specimens were examined morphologically. Museum type and reference material from [[Stanford University]], the [[California Academy of Sciences]] and the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]] was used for comparison.

Historical taxonomy is marked by conflicting interpretations. [[Jordan]] variously linked the Kern trout to the [[Shasta Rainbow Trout]] and to cutthroat ancestry, [[Evermann]] (1906) separated *S. roosevelti* on spotting differences, and [[Curtis]] (1934–1935) concluded *S. roosevelti* was only a color variant. Wording in the original descriptions and stocking records were frequently inaccurate, which complicated identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fauna:** Apart from the trouts, the only other native fish known from the upper basin is the [[Sacramento Sucker]] (*Catostomus occidentalis*).
- **Glacial history:** [[Wisconsin Glaciation]] covered much of the drainage. Relict populations survived south of the ice, and glacial barriers on vacated waters prevented reinvasion, shaping current distributions.
- **Refugia:** The Kern and [[Mount Shasta]] areas both served as Pleistocene refugia, supporting a shared disjunct distribution in the [[Foxtail Pine]] and [[Hydromantes]] salamanders, which the authors cite as supporting common ancestry with the [[Red-Banded Trout]].
- **Hybridization and introductions:** Stocking has produced widespread hybridization. Golden trout hybridize freely with rainbow and cutthroat trout, and the main Kern River and most tributaries now hold hybrids of recent origin. Only some upper [[Little Kern River]] tributaries (Upper Soda Springs, Wet Meadows and Rifle creeks) hold essentially pure *S. a. gilberti*.
- **Coloration:** Earlier beliefs that coloration reflects water mineral content were rejected; coloration is genetically fixed, although fish lost color in hatcheries (attributed to a genetotrophic artifact).

## Key Findings

- **Revised classification:** Two subspecies recognized within *S. aguabonita*: *S. a. aguabonita* (Golden Trout Creek and South Fork of the Kern) and *S. a. gilberti* (main Kern River and lower Little Kern River). *S. roosevelti* is a synonym of *S. a. aguabonita*; *S. whitei* is a synonym of *S. a. gilberti*; *S. rosei* is likely a hybrid based on introductions and not a valid taxon.
- **Karyotype:** *S. a. gilberti* has a diploid number of 58 with 106 arms, identical to *S. a. aguabonita* and to the [[Apache Trout]]. Centric fusion (Robertson's Law) makes this karyotype more readily derived from [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*S. clarki*) than from rainbow trout.
- **Meristic evidence:** Golden trout have low vertebral numbers (mean 59.4 in *S. a. aguabonita*; 59.5 in Apache trout) and low pyloric caeca counts, and resemble cutthroat trout more than rainbow trout. Cutthroatlike trouts typically have nine pelvic rays; rainbow trouts generally have 10.
- **Basibranchial teeth:** Absent in *S. a. aguabonita* but present in some *S. a. gilberti* specimens collected before cutthroat stocking in 1895–1896, suggesting a primitive salmonine character retained in *S. a. gilberti*.
- **Hybridization limits species concept:** Hybrids are relatively fertile and no genetic incompatibility was found among western Salmo. Because *S. a. aguabonita* does not form a biological species with rainbow or cutthroat trout, the authors treat it as a full species of the golden trout complex.
- **Origin hypotheses:** The authors favor two independent invasions by already divergent golden trout forms rather than a single ancestor in the Kern basin. They propose an origin in the lower [[Colorado River]] basin, with the Apache, Gila and [[Mexican Golden Trout]] as relatives.
- **Gilberti as a continuing lineage:** Similarity of *S. a. gilberti* to undescribed red-banded trout (dentition on the glossohyal, coloration, spotting) suggests shared ancestry.
- **Conservation concern:** Stocking and hybridization have endangered the genetic integrity of native forms, and the authors call for karyotype and biochemical sequence studies to clarify evolutionary pathways.
