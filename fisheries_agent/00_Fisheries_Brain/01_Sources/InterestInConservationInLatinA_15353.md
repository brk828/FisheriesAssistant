# Freshwater Conservation Correspondence: Protecting Mexican Aquatic Habitats (1970)

#ichthyology #aquatic-conservation #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1970 exchange of letters between American ichthyologist [[Robert R. Miller]] and Mexican botanist [[Arturo Gomez Pompa]] of the [[Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México]] (UNAM). Miller writes to urge protection of two Mexican aquatic sites of high biological value: the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in Coahuila and [[La Media Luna]] near Rioverde, San Luis Potosí. He stresses the risk of introduced predators and proposes cooperation on a list of threatened Mexican freshwater fishes. Gomez Pompa's reply (May 1970) reports that UNAM is establishing protected areas but lacks funds to manage them.

## Background & Description

- **Miller's letter (April 14, 1970):** Written from the University of Michigan. Miller apologizes for missing Gomez Pompa during a March visit to Mexico City and mentions a former student, [[Jorge Carranza]], who works at the same institution.
- **Miller's credentials:** He has studied North American aquatic fauna for about 30 years and chaired the Freshwater Fish Group of the [[International Union for Conservation of Nature]] (IUCN) for six years. The group recently published the first listing of threatened freshwater fishes of the world as Volume IV of the [[IUCN Red Book]].
- **Gomez Pompa's reply (May 9, 1970, Spanish; English version dated May 4):** Written as Head of the Department of Botany, Instituto de Biología. The English text carries an OCR-garbled date ("1479"), which is read here as 1970.
- **Correspondents' roles:** Gomez Pompa was newly named to a Preservation and Conservation Committee of the Association for Tropical Biology. Miller said he had written to [[Maria Buchinger]] about drafting a list of threatened Mexican freshwater fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Cuatro Ciénegas Basin (Coahuila):** Miller describes this as an area of great scientific value, with many endemic fishes, mollusks, aquatic plants and other organisms. Scientific work there had been intensive over the previous decade. Miller understood that efforts were underway to persuade the President of Mexico to designate it a National Park.

**La Media Luna (San Luis Potosí):** Located roughly SSW of Rioverde, off the highway between Valles and San Luis Potosí, this area contains large springs and lagunas with many outflow channels. Miller has visited it in 1955 and several times since, most recently in mid-February 1970.

- The fish fauna includes one endemic genus and another genus described as the most primitive representative of the family [[Goodeidae]], a family confined to Mexico.
- Other fishes are restricted to the large springs and outflow ditches.
- The site also supports a notable turtle, mammals and birds that use its water, and it has archaeological interest.
- Between 1955 and 1970 Miller saw no visible change in the environment, so the springs and marshes had not yet been noticeably altered by people.

**Threat:** Miller warned that a single introduction of a predatory bass into the springs could rapidly destroy the native biota. Invertebrates of the spring area were essentially unstudied, but given the fish endemism, he expected significant endemism among invertebrates and aquatic plants as well.

**Mexican protected areas (per Gomez Pompa):** UNAM manages three areas:
- A small zone in the [[Pedregal de San Angel]]
- The [[Bahía de Chamela]]
- A very small area in the [[Los Tuxtlas]] region of Veracruz, which UNAM intends to enlarge. Gomez Pompa describes Los Tuxtlas as the last zone of high perennial rainforest in the northern range of the American tropics.

## Key Findings

1. **Two priority sites:** Miller identifies Cuatro Ciénegas and La Media Luna as the two most important aquatic conservation sites in Mexico, citing endemic fishes, mollusks and plants at the former and endemic fishes and a primitive goodeid genus at the latter.
2. **Introduced predators as a major threat:** Miller emphasizes that one introduced bass could destroy the native biota of the La Media Luna springs.
3. **Knowledge gap:** Invertebrates and aquatic plants at La Media Luna were unstudied, and endemism there was expected but unconfirmed.
4. **Need for a threatened-fish list:** Miller offered a list of rare and endangered Mexican freshwater fishes and proposed collaboration on a national list, with Buchinger drafting it.
5. **Institutional limits:** Gomez Pompa acknowledges that UNAM is not the ideal body to hold protected areas, but it is the only institution engaged with the problem. Lack of funds is the main barrier, because maintaining an area requires substantial investment beyond acquiring it.
6. **Proposed cooperation:** Both men agreed that pooling efforts could improve results. Miller hoped to meet Gomez Pompa on his next Mexico trip and to start a continuing correspondence.
