# Resolution 84-4: Listing of Meda fulgida as a Threatened Species

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #conservation #Cyprinidae #Meda-fulgida

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, but a formal conservation resolution (84-4) issued by the [[Desert Fishes Council]] at its Sixteenth Annual Symposium (November 15–17, 1984, University of San Luis Potosí, Mexico). The resolution addresses the severe range contraction of the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), a small cyprinid fish native to the [[Gila River Basin]]. The Council formally petitioned the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) to list the species as "Threatened" under the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]], citing habitat destruction and its designation as a "Category 1" candidate species. The resolution was passed unanimously and forwarded as an official petition on March 11, 1985.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While detailed ecological data (diet, spawning behavior, specific microhabitat preferences) are not enumerated in this policy document, the text confirms critical habitat and threat context:
- **Native Range:** Historically distributed throughout the [[Gila River Basin]] (Arizona/New Mexico, USA).
- **Habitat Status:** Extirpated from **more than 85%** of its historic range.
- **Primary Threats Identified:**
    - Construction of dams
    - Water diversions (specifically flagged: the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]]'s **Central Arizona Project**)
    - General habitat degradation
    - Biotic interference via competition from **exotic (non-native) species**
- **Trophic/Community Context:** The mention of competition from exotic species implies the spikedace occupies a niche vulnerable to displacement by introduced fauna, a common pattern among endemic [[Desert Fishes]] of the American Southwest.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Status (1984):** *Meda fulgida* was classified as a **"Category 1"** species in the USFWS 1982 *Notice of Review of Vertebrate Species*, meaning sufficient biological data existed to support a formal listing decision (Threatened or Endangered).
- **Range Loss:** Quantified extirpation from over 85% of the species' range within the Gila River basin.
- **Active Threat:** The Central Arizona Project posed an ongoing, additional threat to remaining habitat via further diversions and dam construction.
- **Institutional Action:** The [[Desert Fishes Council]] (representing 400+ federal, state, university, and independent stakeholders) passed Resolution 84-4 **without dissenting vote**, formally urging immediate listing action.
- **Legal Mechanism:** The resolution was submitted as a formal listing petition under the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]] (as amended), addressed to USFWS Director Robert A. Jantzen, with a copy directed to the Region 2 Regional Director (Albuquerque, NM).
- **Timeline:** Resolution passed November 1984; formal petition letter sent March 11, 1985, signed by Executive Secretary [[Edwin P. Pister]].
