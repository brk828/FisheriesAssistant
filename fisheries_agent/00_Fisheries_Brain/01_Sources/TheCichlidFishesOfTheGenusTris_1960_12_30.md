# The Cichlid Fishes of the Genus Tristramella Trewavas

## Executive Summary
This taxonomic revision by Steinitz & Ben-Tuvia (1960) re-examines the genus [[Tristramella]], an endemic cichlid genus of the Palestino-Syrian region, using large comparative samples of *T. sacra* and *T. simonis* from the [[Jordan River]] system. The authors demonstrate that the vertebral apophysis supporting the swim bladder — previously described by [[Trewavas 1942]] as a fixed, symmetric generic character positioned on the 4th vertebra — is in fact highly variable, asymmetric, and involves the 3rd–5th vertebrae in varying proportions across populations. Based on morphometric, meristic, and reproductive-behavior differences between Lake Tiberias and Lake Huleh populations of *T. simonis*, the authors formally validate the subspecies **Tristramella simonis intermedia** (a name previously used as a nomen nudum), positioning it morphologically and geographically as an intermediate form between *T. s. simonis* and *T. magdalenae*. The paper also proposes a phylogenetic hypothesis deriving Tristramella from an ancestral *[[Tilapia]]*-like form via directional caudal shift and destabilization of the swim-bladder apophysis.

## Background & Description
- **Genus:** *[[Tristramella]]* Trewavas, 1942 — an endemic cichlid genus restricted to Syria and Palestine.
- **Recognized species (Trewavas 1942):**
  - *[[Tristramella sacra]]* (Günther) — restricted to Lake Tiberias; distinguished by strongly projecting lower jaw, large head, conical teeth.
  - *[[Tristramella simonis]]* (Günther) — found in the Jordan River system, Lake Huleh and Lake Tiberias.
  - *[[Tristramella magdalenae]]* (Lortet) — least known, known only from Lortet's syntypes (Lyons Natural History Museum), from the wider Damascus area.
- The name ***Tristramella simonis intermedia*** was first mentioned by Steinitz (1953, 1954) as a nomen nudum; it is formally validated and described in this 1960 paper.
- Prior taxonomic history: originally placed in genus *[[Tilapia]]*; removed by Boulenger (1899) and Trewavas (1942) into the new genus *Tristramella*, though retained a shared character (upper pharyngeal–skull articulation) linking it to *Tilapia*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Freshwater lakes and rivers of the Jordan Rift Valley — [[Lake Tiberias]] (Sea of Galilee), [[Lake Huleh]], and the Damascus basin (for *T. magdalenae*).
- **Distribution notes:**
  - *T. sacra*: confirmed only in Lake Tiberias; earlier Lake Huleh record (Tristram) unconfirmed by later collecting.
  - *T. s. simonis* (typical form): inhabits the Jordan River system south of Lake Huleh, primarily Lake Tiberias and lower Jordan tributaries.
  - *T. s. intermedia*: predominantly Lake Huleh, occasionally found in Lake Tiberias (attributed to passive downstream transport during high-water conditions across the ~16.3 km, ~300 m elevation drop between the lakes).
  - **Conservation concern:** *T. s. intermedia* population noted as "dwindling" due to drastic reduction of Lake Huleh through mid-20th-century reclamation/drainage projects; authors predicted possible extirpation within years.
- **Reproductive/ecological divergence:**
  - Tiberias *T. simonis*: spawning season April–August; **only females** mouth-brood eggs.
  - Huleh *T. simonis*: spawning season March–June; **both sexes** participate in mouth-brooding (a trait shared with *T. sacra*, per Ben-Tuvia 1959).
- **Paleogeographic context:** The three Tristramella forms (*sacra*, *simonis*, *magdalenae*) are hypothesized descendants of a single ancestral population once distributed across a formerly interconnected Palestino-Syrian water body later dissected into the Tiberias, Huleh, and Damascus basins (Steinitz 1954).
- **Comparative taxa:** *[[Tilapia nilotica]]* and *[[Tilapia galilaea]]* used as comparative outgroups for vertebral apophysis stability; *Tilapia nilotica exul* (Steinitz 1952) cited as parallel case of meristic reduction without compensatory body elongation.

## Key Findings

### Vertebral & Meristic Data
- **Vertebral counts:**
  - *T. sacra*: range 29–30, mean ≈ 29.08 (Table I)
  - *T. simonis*, Lake Tiberias: range 28–30, mean 29.05 (n=21)
  - *T. simonis*, Lake Huleh: range 28–29, mean 28.84 (n=25)
  - Genus-wide revised statement: vertebral count predominantly **29**, with occasional 28 or 30 (amending Trewavas's fixed count of 29).
- **Dorsal fin (D) supporting elements:**
  - Lake Tiberias: range 24–26, mean 24.83 (n=66)
  - Lake Huleh: range 23–25, mean 24.35 (n=40)
  - Spine counts: Tiberias mean 14.94 (XIV–XVI); Huleh mean 14.58
- **Anal fin (A) supporting elements:**
  - Lake Tiberias: mean 11.06 (n=65); Lake Huleh: mean 11.13 (n=40) — minimal difference between populations
- **Longitudinal line scale counts:**
  - Lake Tiberias: mean 27.72 (n=53); Lake Huleh: mean 27.55 (n=33); difference = 0.17

### Vertebral Apophysis (Swim-Bladder Support) — Central Finding
- Contrary to Trewavas's claim of a fixed, generic 4th-vertebra position:
  - **T. sacra:** 4th vertebra involved in only ~33.5% of cases exclusively; combinations of 3/4, 3/4/5, and 4/5 vertebrae also occur; apophysis is **never fully symmetric**.
  - **T. simonis, Lake Tiberias:** 4th vertebra alone involved in 38% of cases; 3rd vertebra frequently co-opted (3/4 combination in 57%).
  - **T. simonis, Lake Huleh (intermedia):** 4th vertebra alone involved in 72% of cases; occasional posterior shift to 5th vertebra (4% of cases); reduced 3rd-vertebra participation (24%) compared to Tiberias fish.
  - Asymmetry is essentially universal in Tristramella (no cases of complete symmetry observed), contrasting sharply with *Tilapia* (8/9 *T. nilotica* and 7/8 *T. galilaea* specimens showed near-complete symmetry, with the 3rd vertebra exclusively involved in nearly all cases).
- **Revised generic diagnosis of Tristramella (this paper):**
  1. Vertebral count predominantly 29 (occasionally 28 or 30).
  2. Apophysis frequently involves more than one vertebra (3rd–5th).
  3. Apophysis structure is strongly asymmetric in the great majority of cases.
  4. The 4th vertebra plays the leading/most frequent role.
  (Caveat: *T. magdalenae* not examined directly — provisional pending further material.)

### Morphometric Comparison: T. s. simonis vs. T. s. intermedia
All differences below statistically significant at 0.1% level (Table VI):
| Ratio | Huleh (intermedia) | Tiberias (simonis) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard length/head | 2.60 | 2.74 |
| Head/snout | 2.56 | 2.94 |
| Head/interorbital | 3.67 | 3.34 |
| Head/upper jaw | 3.17 | 3.42 |
| Head/lower jaw | 3.24 | 3.53 |
| Head/eye | 5.15 | 4.71 |

- Standard length/depth and head/preorbital ratios: **not** significantly different.
- Huleh fish show reduced relative body length (SL minus head) compared to Tiberias fish, exceeding what would be expected from reduced vertebral/scale counts alone — indicating true body foreshortening, not just meristic compensation (parallel to *Tilapia nilotica exul*, Steinitz 1952).

### Pharyngeal Dentition
- *T. s. simonis* (Tiberias): two rows of enlarged teeth on lower pharyngeal bone (4–6 teeth/row).
- *T. s. intermedia* (Huleh): three to four rows of enlarged teeth (middle rows 5–8 teeth; adjacent rows 1–5 teeth) — more numerous, blunter, more prominent upper pharyngeal teeth than typical form.
- Distinction between subspecies via pharyngeal dentition alone is not always clear-cut.

### Taxonomic Conclusion
- **Tristramella simonis intermedia** subsp. nov. formally validated — morphologically and geographically intermediate between *T. s. simonis* (Lake Tiberias) and *T. magdalenae* (Damascus basin), consistent with the historical hydrological connection of the three basins.
- Proposed **phylogenetic scenario**: *Tilapia* is hypothesized ancestral genus to *Tristramella*; evolution involved (1) specialization of pharyngeal teeth (uniform → enlarged middle row, most advanced in *sacra* and *magdalenae*), and (2) progressive caudal destabilization/asymmetry of the vertebral apophysis (3rd vertebra in ancestral/*Tilapia* condition → 4th in *simonis* → occasional 5th in *intermedia* and *sacra*).
- Instability of meristic/skeletal characters in *Tristramella* (vs. stability in *Tilapia*) interpreted as evidence of the genus's relatively recent evolutionary age and ongoing diversification following fragmentation of an ancestral Palestino-Syrian water body.
