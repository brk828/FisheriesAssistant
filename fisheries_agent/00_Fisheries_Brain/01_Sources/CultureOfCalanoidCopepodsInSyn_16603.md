# Culture of Calanoid Copepods in Synthetic Sea Water

#ichthyology #marine-biology #calanoida

## Executive Summary

This 1969 short note from the [[Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada]] tests whether two commercial synthetic sea salts can sustain laboratory populations of the calanoid copepods [[Acartia Tonsa]] and [[Eurytemora Affinis]]. Success was judged by whether a population persisted under a modest harvest regime. [[Instant Ocean]] supported *A. tonsa* at a level comparable to filtered bay water, while [[RILA Marine Mix]] largely failed for that species. *E. affinis* persisted in synthetic media but showed recurrent reproductive failure.

## Background & Description

Calanoid copepods are important planktonic prey and a common focus of mariculture and laboratory production work. Earlier studies had cultured them in natural sea water (Jacobs, 1961; Zillioux and Wilson, 1966; Corkett, 1967; Mullin and Brooks, 1967). Natural or neritic sea water has drawbacks: its organic content must be removed by filtration or photo-oxidation (Armstrong et al., 1966; Hamilton and Carlucci, 1966), the resulting composition varies, and cost is high for laboratories far from the sea. The author tested two commercially produced synthetic sea salts to address these problems.

Two species were studied:
- *Acartia tonsa* (Dana), a neritic calanoid.
- *Eurytemora affinis* (Poppe), a species sometimes found in fresh water (Northcote et al., 1964).

Copepods were collected from the Patuxent River estuary, Maryland, in winter 1965–66, and acclimated in filtered Chesapeake Bay water diluted to 12‰ salinity. Synthetic sea water was likewise diluted to 12‰ with distilled water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Culture conditions**
- Constant temperature of 15 or 20 °C, continuous light, in 0.5 or 2.0 L Erlenmeyer flasks covered with black polypropylene to limit epiphyte growth.
- Copepods were fed three times a week with a mixture of three algae: *Chaetoceros* sp. (contaminated with at least one bacterial species), [[Chlamydomonas Reinhardtii]] (Indiana University Strain 90), and [[Isochrysis Galbana]]. *I. galbana* made up roughly 80–90% of the optical density.
- Algae were fed at 12.5 mL per liter of copepod culture; optical density ranged 0.080–0.130 at 550 nm.
- Cultures were not axenic. Rotifers and protozoans were present and may have contributed trace nutrients.

**Medium-specific observations**
- Bacterial and blue-green algal coatings on flask walls were linked to cessation of egg laying in bay water and RILA cultures, but not in Instant Ocean.
- A rust-coloured precipitate formed in Instant Ocean and stained flask walls and fecal pellets.
- Early whole-culture mortalities were attributed to detergent residues on glassware and stopped after acid cleaning.

## Key Findings

- **Instant Ocean suited *A. tonsa*.** Culture 2 (Instant Ocean, 20 °C, 161 days, 45 harvests) was the only synthetic-medium culture that performed as well as the bay-water controls. Its harvest-time-to-harvest ratio was comparable to the bay-water cultures (culture 1: 260 days, 65 harvests; culture 3: 183 days, 43 harvests).
- **RILA Marine Mix failed for *A. tonsa*.** Repeated attempts were unsuccessful. For *E. affinis*, RILA with 25 mg/L NaHCO₃ (culture 7: 106 days, 21 harvests; culture 8: 62 days, 15 harvests) and with added vitamins (culture 9: 22 days, 6 harvests) were reported.
- **Reproductive failure in *E. affinis*.** Rapid population declines in synthetic media were caused by premature dropping of egg sacs. Hatching success of dropped eggs ranged from 0 to 100% and was roughly proportional to the fraction of normal brooding time completed.
- **Temperature adjustment.** Lowering the Instant Ocean culture of *E. affinis* to 15 °C (culture 6: 227 days, 45 harvests) allowed a low but persistent population under modest harvest. The bay-water culture at 15 °C (culture 4: 179 days, 30 harvests) flourished.
- **Long-term outcome for *E. affinis*.** Although cultured for 15 months in artificial media, reproductive success remained poor, possibly due to nutrient deficiency or toxicity.
- **Conclusion.** The author judged Instant Ocean a suitable medium for *A. tonsa*, while *E. affinis* in synthetic media remained problematic.
