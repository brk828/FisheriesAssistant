# Status of the Bluehead Shiner (*Notropis hubbsi*) in Illinois

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1986 status report by Burr and Warren documents the historical and contemporary occurrence of the [[Bluehead Shiner]] (*Notropis hubbsi*) in Illinois, where it represents one of the state's rarest vertebrates. The Illinois population is remarkably disjunct (~443 air km) from the nearest population in southern Arkansas. First discovered in [[Wolf Lake]] (Union County, IL) in 1954, the species was documented via small collections through 1974, after which two pollution events (an acid compound spill in 1974 and a toxic chemical spill in 1979 from train derailments) caused massive fish kills and destruction of littoral vegetation. Despite an intensive 1.5-year survey (1981-1982, ~500 person-hours) and continued monitoring through 1985, no specimens were recovered after 28 March 1974, raising serious concern that *N. hubbsi* has been extirpated from Illinois. Two previously overlooked museum specimens (from 1952 and 1969) extended the species' known Illinois range to Pine Hills springs and [[Otter Pond]] within the LaRue-Pine Hills Ecological Area, suggesting possible persistence outside Wolf Lake. The authors recommend continued surveys, retention on the Illinois endangered species list, and potential future reintroduction using Arkansas stock.

## Background & Description

- **Maximum size:** 54 mm standard length (SL) (tuberculate male, collected July 1969, Otter Pond)
- **Sexual maturity size:** Females mature at ~47 mm SL (males' maturation size unknown)
- **Sexual dimorphism:**
  - Adult males possess breeding tubercles irregularly arranged on the head, edges of body scales, and fin rays
  - Both sexes display azure-blue heads, a reddish-orange lateral body stripe, and reddish fins (per [[Bailey and Robison 1978]])
- **Egg (ovum) characteristics:**
  - Diameter range: 0.7–0.9 mm (mean = 0.8 mm, N = 10)
  - Color: orange
  - Fecundity: 781 mature ova counted in a single gravid female (46.6 mm SL)
- **Longevity:** Maximum lifespan of 1+ to under 2 years, consistent with life-history patterns in other *Notropis* species (cf. [[Heins and Clemmer 1976]] on *Notropis longirostris*)
- **Age at maturity:** 1 year

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat:** Vegetated periphery of lentic/slow-moving freshwater systems, typically in water ≤1.8 m deep
- **Substrate association:** Mud, detritus, and decayed plant matter bottoms; near debris of standing timber, submerged logs, and stumps
- **Water characteristics:** Tea-colored (tannin-stained) water typical of southern Illinois swamp-influenced lakes
- **Habitat analog:** Ecologically similar to the microhabitat used by the [[Bantam Sunfish]] (*Lepomis symmetricus*) in the same system (Burr 1977)
- **Known Illinois localities:**
  1. **[[Wolf Lake]]** (Union County) — population nucleus and presumed primary nursery area; all confirmed 1973–1974 records came from within 5 m of the Powder Plant bridge
  2. **Pine Hills springs** (LaRue-Pine Hills Ecological Area) — inferred from a 1952 gravid female collected by J.G. Weise, habitat characterized by luxuriant submerged aquatic vegetation along bluff springs
  3. **[[Otter Pond]]** — a single tuberculate male collected in 1969; geographically situated between Wolf Lake and the Pine Hills springs, connected to both via bottomland swamp
- **Reproductive ecology:**
  - Spawning period: late May through July
  - Young-of-the-year (10–15 mm SL, estimated 1–2 weeks old) first collected late June 1973
  - Juveniles appear to school (large numbers taken in single seine hauls during June–August), while adults were caught singly
- **Predation:** No evidence of predation found — stomach contents of 6 adult spotted gar (*Lepisosteus oculatus*) and 5 adult bowfin (*Amia calva*), both potential predators, contained no *N. hubbsi*
- **Broader range:** Populations also documented in Arkansas, Texas, Oklahoma, and Louisiana within the Mississippi River basin

## Key Findings

1. **Apparent extirpation from Wolf Lake:** Despite ~500 person-hours of intensive sampling (1981–1982) using seines, electroshocking, minnow traps, and trawling, plus continued surveys through 1985, no *N. hubbsi* were collected after 28 March 1974 — strongly suggesting the Wolf Lake population may be extirpated.
2. **Pollution events implicated in decline:** Two chemical spills from train derailments (1974 acid compound; 1979 toxic chemical) caused littoral vegetation destruction and fish kills in Wolf Lake, coinciding with the disappearance of the species despite recovery of other fish populations and vegetation.
3. **Expanded historical range discovered:** Rediscovery of two overlooked museum specimens (1952 Pine Hills gravid female; 1969 Otter Pond tuberculate male) demonstrated a previously unrecognized Illinois distribution beyond Wolf Lake, into the LaRue-Pine Hills Ecological Area.
4. **Life history quantified for Illinois population:** Based on 201 specimens (1952–1974), researchers established sexual maturity at 1 year/47 mm SL, a spawning window of May–July, fecundity of 781 ova in one female, ova size averaging 0.8 mm, and a lifespan under 2 years.
5. **Genetic considerations for conservation:** Electrophoretic analysis of Arkansas populations ([[Dimmick 1984]]) revealed low genetic variability in *N. hubbsi*, suggesting that mixing geographically distant stocks (Arkansas x Illinois) for reintroduction purposes may pose minimal genetic risk relative to the benefit of preventing state-level extirpation.
6. **Conservation priority status:** The Illinois Endangered Species Technical Advisory Committee on Fishes (1984) ranked *N. hubbsi* as having the second-highest overall endangerment priority in Illinois, and the top ranking for recovery potential among endangered Illinois fishes.
7. **Management recommendations:**
   - Continue and diversify survey efforts (e.g., diel sampling) concentrated in Wolf Lake, Pine Hills springs, and Otter Pond
   - Retain species on the Illinois endangered species list
   - Pursue Illinois state purchase of Wolf Lake and riparian buffer zones
   - Establish protective agreements with the U.S. Forest Service for the [[LaRue-Pine Hills Ecological Area]]
   - Consider supplemental stocking from Arkansas source populations if native Illinois populations remain undetected
