# Arizona Game and Fish Department Comments on Parker Division Channelization, Lower Colorado River (1964)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-and-wildlife-mitigation

## Executive Summary

This is a 1964 letter from the Director of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to the Regional Director of the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Region 3, in Boulder City, Nevada. It submits the state's comments and recommendations on a draft report, *Comprehensive Plan, Colorado River Channelization, Parker Valley Division*, which is part of the [[Colorado River Front Work and Levee System]]. The letter argues that channelization of the [[Parker Division]] will cause extensive and largely irreplaceable losses of fish and wildlife habitat, and that full mitigation is not physically possible. It proposes partial mitigation through five [[Bypass Lakes]] with surrounding wildlife areas, and asks for immediate re-evaluation of the project and dedicated funding for fish and wildlife management.

The document contains no species-level data, sample sizes, or measured statistics. Its content is policy and planning in nature.

## Background & Description

- **Authority:** Comments were submitted under the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] (48 Stat. 401, as amended; 16 U.S.C. 661 et seq.). The Department acts as the state agency responsible for administering Arizona's fish and wildlife resources.
- **Project system:** The Colorado River Front Work and Levee System was authorized by Congress under several acts, the latest being Public Law 469 of June 28, 1946. It extends from [[Davis Dam]] to the International Boundary, a distance of about 280 miles.
- **Parker Division:** One of nine project divisions. It extends about 45 miles downstream from [[Headgate Rock Dam]] (north of Parker, Arizona) to the [[Palo Verde Diversion Dam]] (about 10 miles north of Blythe, California).
- **Jurisdiction:** The upper 24 miles of the division on both sides of the river, and the lower 21 miles in Arizona, lie wholly within the [[Colorado River Indian Reservation]]. The Department acknowledges the Reservation's jurisdiction over these lands.
- **Prior reports:** The letter accepts, without repeating, the descriptions of fish, wildlife, habitat, and losses in two earlier reports: the California Department of Fish and Game report of November 19, 1964, and the preliminary report of the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] dated October 21, 1964.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter sets out four general factors that shape any mitigation effort:

1. **Limited mitigation potential:** Full mitigation is not possible because of the terrain and the way the river will be controlled. Lost habitat cannot be re-established beyond the measures proposed, since no areas within the division are suitable for new fish or wildlife habitat.
2. **Water dependence:** All proposed mitigation features depend on a permanent water supply. If that water cannot be assured, mitigation is impossible.
3. **Irreplaceable habitat loss:** Habitat destruction from channel construction and rehabilitation will cause extensive and largely irreplaceable losses of fish, wildlife, and related hunting and fishing. These losses will occur in a region where demand for hunting and fishing is growing rapidly and is expected to keep growing.
4. **Recreational and economic value:** The letter cites recreation for "hundreds of thousands" of hunters and anglers from Arizona, California, and other states. It also stresses the economic importance to the [[Colorado River Indian Tribe]] and nearby communities. The Tribe is developing fishing and hunting as a major tribal enterprise, which the letter describes as a potential "million dollar plus" economic asset for Parker Valley.

The letter states that, after channelization as currently proposed, it would be questionable whether the Tribe could develop an economically feasible fish and wildlife program.

## Key Findings

**Proposed partial mitigation (Department recommendations):**

- **Five bypass lakes:** Develop and maintain five bypass lakes in the existing river channel, with site selection coordinated with the California Department of Fish and Game and the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife. Operation, maintenance, and recreational use would be coordinated with the Tribe.
- **Wildlife buffer:** A wildlife area about 1/4 to 1/2 mile wide should be set aside around each bypass lake.
- **Revegetation:** Disturbed areas should be revegetated with native and suitable plants, including mesquite, willow, and grasses, at project expense.
- **Management funds:** Sufficient funds should be provided at project cost to the appropriate fish and wildlife agencies to acquire and manage suitable areas and to carry out fish and wildlife management techniques.

**Conclusions and requests:**

- The Department states that its efforts to mitigate losses are limited without a water supply that is guaranteed on a permanent basis.
- It asks for an immediate reconsideration and re-evaluation of the Parker Division project, including whether the Colorado River channelization plan is appropriate given the fish and wildlife and recreational losses.
- It requests that Interior and the Bureau of Reclamation make funds available for development, operation, and maintenance of the mitigation measures, with planning coordinated among the federal and state agencies.
- It recommends that the [[Lower Colorado River]] channelization and related water-development decisions be re-evaluated in light of the resource losses. (Parts of the closing recommendations are illegible in the scan, so their exact wording cannot be confirmed.)

The letter closes by thanking the U.S. Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and the California Department of Fish and Game for their cooperation, and it signals a willingness to work with them on the Parker Division.
