# Wahweap Operation and Maintenance, Utah: FY 2001 Annual Project Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 2001 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers the operation and maintenance of the [[Wahweap Hatchery]] in Big Water, Utah. The facility rears [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) for stocking in the Upper Colorado River Basin and maintains a backup brood stock of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). In 2001 the hatchery stocked or transferred 94,490 bonytail, and all were coded-wire tagged. The project was fully funded at $160,400.00, with no difference between funds provided and expended. The project is ongoing.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** Wahweap Operation and Maintenance, Utah.
- **Principal investigators:** Wayne Gustaveson and Quent Bradwitch, Utah Division of Wildlife Resources.
- **Study schedule:** Ongoing.
- **Program linkage:** The work supports the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIPRAP) under the general recovery program's support action plan. Relevant actions include:
  - Protecting genetic integrity and augmenting or restoring populations.
  - Securing and managing genetic stocks in refugia.
  - Developing a basin-wide bonytail restoration plan.
  - Operating and maintaining facilities.
- **Broodstock supply:** Wahweap received 64,000 bonytail fry from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and 400 razorback sucker brood stock from [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stocking purpose:** Bonytail are reared to meet stocking needs in the Upper Basin, including Colorado and Utah.
- **Rearing conditions:** Pond growth was monitored weekly by capturing fish with minnow traps, seines, and cast nets. Growth rates improved over previous years after the ponds were fertilized.
- **Losses:** Mortality occurred during fish moving, tagging, lethal sampling for fish health inspection, and a short period of time on station. Where possible, dead fish were preserved in ethanol for future genetic work.
- **Public outreach:** Endangered Colorado River fishes were displayed at the Utah State Fair in Salt Lake City for two weeks (seven bonytail and four razorback sucker). Afterward the fish were moved to aquaria in the zoology building at [[Brigham Young University]].

## Key Findings

**Stocking and transfers (2001)**

- Total bonytail stocked or transferred: **94,490**.
  - 46,522 stocked in the Green River.
  - 17,968 stocked in the Colorado River.
  - 30,000 transferred to the State of Colorado.
  - Most fish stocked were about 80 to 100 mm in length.
- All fish stocked or transferred were coded-wire tagged in 2001.

**Tag retention**

- Tag retention was evaluated on bonytail tagged in 1999 and held over the winter. Retention was **98%** on the 100 fish checked.
- Initial tag retention after tagging normally ran **95% or better** when spot checks were done.

**Budget**

- Funds provided: $160,400.00.
- Funds expended: $160,400.00.
- Difference: $0.00.
- No Recovery Program funds were spent on public art projects.

**Data submission**

- Coded-wire tag information for bonytail is being maintained. It is to be submitted to the database manager by January 1, 2002.

**Recommendations**

1. Continue maturing bonytail at Wahweap to meet Colorado stocking needs.
2. Maintain the razorback sucker backup brood stock in the Upper Basin.
3. Supply other species as requested by the Biology Committee.
4. PIT-tag bonytail and stock them in the fall.

**Project status:** Ongoing fish culture.
