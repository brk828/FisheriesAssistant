# Draft Environmental Assessment: Fish Barrier in Hot Springs Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a public-comment package issued by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and the [[Bureau of Land Management]]. It solicits comments on a [[Draft Environmental Assessment]] for a proposed [[Fish Barrier]] in [[Hot Springs Canyon]]. The barrier would block the upstream invasion of nonnative fishes into reaches of the canyon occupied by threatened and endangered fish species. The canyon lies within the [[Hot Springs Area of Critical Environmental Concern]], part of the 57,500-acre [[Muleshoe Ranch Cooperative Management Area]] in [[Cochise County]], Arizona. Comments were due by December 7, 2008. The package also includes a supporting letter from fishery scientist [[Paul Marsh]], who endorses the project.

## Background & Description

**Agency transmittal memorandum**
- Issued by the Bureau of Reclamation, Phoenix Area Office (Glendale, Arizona), to interested persons, organizations, and agencies.
- Subject: the Draft EA for construction of a fish barrier in Hot Springs Canyon, with action required by December 7, 2008.
- The draft EA was provided on CD-ROM. A paper copy could be requested by phone or email, and the document was also posted on the Phoenix Area Office website.
- Comments could be submitted by mail, fax (623-773-6486), or email to the project contact, Mr. John McGlothlen.
- Commenters could ask that their names and/or addresses be withheld from the public record. Organizational and business comments are fully public.

**Supporting comment letter**
- Dated November 25, 2008, from Paul Marsh, PhD, of Morsh & Associates, LLC (Tempe, Arizona). The letter responds to a November 8, 2008 request for comments.
- The author describes nearly three decades of experience with southwestern fishes and involvement in the Hot Springs Canyon barrier project since its inception.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Core problem:** Nonnative fishes could move upstream into canyon habitat occupied by threatened and endangered fish. The barrier is designed to prevent this invasion.
- **Protected area status:** The canyon is within an Area of Critical Environmental Concern, a designation that signals special management attention for sensitive resources.
- **Land management setting:** The site is part of a cooperative management area in southeastern Arizona that combines multiple land-management partners.
- **Species data:** The document does not name the threatened and endangered species, the nonnative fishes of concern, or any population counts, habitat measurements, or water-chemistry data.

## Key Findings

1. **Proposal:** Construct a fish barrier in Hot Springs Canyon to stop upstream movement of nonnative fishes into habitat used by threatened and endangered species.
2. **Process:** A draft EA was released for public comment, with a deadline of December 7, 2008. Comments were requested from interested persons, organizations, and agencies.
3. **Expert support:** Paul Marsh, PhD, supports the proposal as a beneficial conservation action for native Arizona fishes. He states that he foresees no environmental downside to implementing the barrier. He also commends the authors for their thorough background analysis and professional preparation of the EA.
4. **Information gaps:** The source does not report survey results, species lists, sample sizes, or quantitative impact assessments. Its findings rest on the agency's description of the proposal and one expert endorsement.
