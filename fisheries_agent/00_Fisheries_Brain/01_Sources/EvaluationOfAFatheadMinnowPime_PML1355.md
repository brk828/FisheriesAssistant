# Fathead Minnow Embryo-Larval Test Guideline Evaluation Using Acenaphthene and Isophorone

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This EPA Project Summary reports an evaluation of a proposed standard [[Embryo-Larval Test]] protocol for the [[Fathead Minnow]] (*[[Pimephales Promelas]]*). Four [[Flow-Through Bioassay]] tests were run, two each with [[Acenaphthene]] and [[Isophorone]]. The protocol was judged workable, with one exception: the feeding regime, where control weights varied. The work was conducted by the [[Environmental Research Laboratory Duluth]] of the [[Environmental Protection Agency]] as part of a larger multi-laboratory study.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Pimephales promelas*, the fathead minnow, used as the test organism.
- **Objective:** Evaluate a specific standard method for embryo-larval toxicity testing, not to assess the fish's biology.
- **Test design:** Tests began with embryos less than 24 hours old and ended 28 days post hatch.
- **Endpoint:** Each surviving fish was weighed individually. Mean weights of exposed animals were compared with controls using [[Dunnett's Test]].
- **Feeding:** Fish were fed newly hatched brine shrimp nauplii ([[Artemia]]), with a small amount of prepared fish food added weekly.
- **Quality:** The document states that all parameters were within the guidelines, which are attached to the full project report.
- **Report:** The full report, *Evaluation of a Fathead Minnow Pimephales promelas Embryo-Larval Test Guideline Using Acenaphthene and Isophorone* (EPA-600/3-83-062, September 1983), is available from the [[National Technical Information Service]] (Order No. PB 83-243 436; cost given as $8.50, subject to change).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a laboratory toxicity-test evaluation. It does not address habitat, natural population ecology, food webs, or field conditions. The only environmental context is the controlled test setting: flow-through exposure, the feeding regime, and comparison against control groups. The two test chemicals are presented as toxicants, and their environmental occurrence is not discussed.

## Key Findings

**No-effect levels relative to controls (reported as given):**

| Toxicant | Test values (mg/L) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [[Acenaphthene]] | 0.208 and 0.226 (Summary); 0.208 and 0.026 (Conclusions) | See discrepancy below |
| [[Isophorone]] | 19.5 and 6.89 (Summary); 19.5 and 6.88 (Conclusions) | Minor difference in second value |

- **Data inconsistency:** The Project Summary and the Conclusions section give different values for the second acenaphthene test (0.226 vs. 0.026 mg/L) and for the second isophorone test (6.89 vs. 6.88 mg/L). Both are reproduced as printed. The source should be checked against the full report before the figures are used.
- **Protocol performance:** The only problem found was in the feeding regime, which the authors say may be improved. Control weights varied.
- **Suggested fixes:** The authors point to two possible causes: the spacing of feeding times, and the variable size of the nauplii during the first week after hatching.
- **Overall assessment:** Apart from the feeding issue, the protocol performed acceptably across the four tests, with all parameters within guideline limits.
