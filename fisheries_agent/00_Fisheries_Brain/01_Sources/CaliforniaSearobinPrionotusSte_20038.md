# California Searobin (Prionotus stephanophrys), A Fish New for the Fauna of Southern California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Triglidae #species-distribution #first-record

## Executive Summary

This paper reports the first confirmed specimen of [[Prionotus stephanophrys]] (California searobin) from southern California waters, collected in [[Santa Monica Bay]] on October 24, 1944. The species, belonging to the family [[Triglidae]], had previously been recorded only from central California (San Francisco area, Point Reyes) with the type specimen collected in 1880. The new specimen, a 12.5-inch female caught at approximately 30 fathoms depth, represents the first adult described for the species and extends the confirmed range southward by several hundred miles. The author clarifies historical confusion regarding Pacific [[Prionotus]] species distributions and calls for critical revision of the genus.

## Background & Description

[[Prionotus]] species, known as searobins, are common on the Atlantic Coast of the United States and tropical Middle America but rarely found on the Pacific Coast north of Mexico. [[Prionotus stephanophrys]] was first described by Lockington in 1881 from a type specimen collected in October 1880 in deep water between the [[Farallones]], [[San Francisco Bay]] entrance, and [[Point Reyes]]. Prior to this report, only two or three specimens had been collected north of the Mexican border.

The new specimen is a female with eggs approaching ripeness, measuring 12.5 inches overall length (252 mm standard length). Detailed morphological description includes:

- Body moderately robust with greatest depth 260 thousandths of standard length
- Head long and massive (390 thousandths to end of opercular membrane)
- Head spines weakly developed compared to other [[Prionotus]] species
- Dorsal fin formula: X, 12 (10 spines, 12 soft rays)
- Anal fin formula: I, i, 9 (one spine, one unbranched soft-ray, 9 branched rays)
- Pectoral fin with 18 rays plus three separate finger-like rays below
- Lateral line with 52 pores
- Scales thin, weakly ctenoid, poorly imbricated

Coloration in formalin: upper body purplish brown with blackish specks and blotches; lower side and ventral surfaces white; dorsal and caudal fins dusky purplish with blackish purple spots; pectoral fin purplish black when folded, dusky purplish with elongated blackish spots when spread.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimen was caught on a handline by Michael Waxman four to five miles off [[El Segundo]] in [[Santa Monica Bay]] at approximately Lat. 33° 53' N., Long. 118° 30' 30" W. The capture location had a depth of about 30 fathoms with sand and gravel bottom.

Historical records indicate the species inhabits "tolerably deep water" ranging from 27 to 47 fathoms. Previous specimens were collected by trawl (type specimen) and by the Albatross research vessel at stations off [[Magdalena Bay]], [[Baja California]].

The paper notes significant confusion in the literature regarding Pacific [[Prionotus]] distributions. Records for [[Gulf of California]] may represent misidentifications or clerical errors. The validity of Mexican reports for P. stephanophrys remains uncertain, and the species may prove inseparable from [[Prionotus quiescens]]. A critical revision of Pacific [[Prionotus]] species is needed, with material available in multiple museum collections.

The author corrects previous erroneous reports of [[Prionotus gymnostethus]] from southern California, finding no evidence to support its inclusion in the regional fauna.

## Key Findings

1. **First Southern California Record**: The October 24, 1944 specimen represents the first authenticated record of [[Prionotus stephanophrys]] from southern California, collected 64 years after the type specimen from San Francisco.

2. **Morphological Description**: This is the first adult specimen described for the species. Key differences from previous descriptions (based on half-grown specimens) include smaller eye and lower ridges and shorter head spines attributable to adult size.

3. **Distribution Clarification**: Only one species, [[Prionotus stephanophrys]], has been recorded on authentic grounds from California. Only two California specimens (both near [[Point Reyes]]) had been located in museums prior to this report. The [[Monterey]] locality record remains unvalidated.

4. **Taxonomic Confusion**: Historical records show significant confusion regarding Pacific [[Prionotus]] species, with station numbers transposed between P. stephanophrys and [[Prionotus quiescens]], and questionable [[Gulf of California]] records. The two nominal species may prove inseparable.

5. **Commercial Potential**: The discovery illustrates incomplete knowledge of moderate-depth fish fauna along southern California coast. In 1943, more than 300,000 pounds of searobins were taken in New England and North Atlantic commercial fisheries, suggesting potential commercial significance for California waters.

6. **Specimen Details**: The female specimen (Stanford University No. 39788) measured 12.5 inches overall, with detailed proportional measurements provided for all body parts, fins, and meristic counts, establishing baseline data for future taxonomic work.

7. **Research Need**: Material for critical revision of Pacific [[Prionotus]] is available in collections at New York Zoological Society, American Museum of Natural History, Bingham Oceanographical Collections, United States National Museum, Stanford University, California Academy of Sciences, and Allen Hancock Foundation.
