# A Chemical and Biological Assessment of Water Quality Impacts from Acid Mine Drainage in Monitor Creek

## Executive Summary
This study (Vinyard, 1996) is **not a fish-centric investigation**; rather it assesses freshwater ecosystem degradation from historic gold-mining acid mine drainage (AMD) in Monitor Creek, a first-order Sierra Nevada tributary of the [[Carson River]]. The paper focuses on water chemistry and two biological bioassay approaches: a standard chronic toxicity test using the cladoceran **[[Ceriodaphnia dubia]]**, and a novel functional bioassay measuring gross primary production of natural **periphyton** communities colonizing glass microscope slides. No fish species were directly sampled or assayed in this study, though standard toxicological reference organisms (e.g., *Pimephales promelas*, fathead minnow) are mentioned only as examples of commonly used bioassay stock species in the broader toxicology literature. Results demonstrate that AMD-impacted sites (particularly site M2) exhibit severely depressed pH and dissolved oxygen, elevated sulfate/iron/TDS, negligible periphyton chlorophyll, and complete mortality/reproductive failure in *Ceriodaphnia* — findings corroborated independently by both bioassay techniques.

## Background & Description
Because the study is chemically and functionally oriented rather than morphologically descriptive, biological "morphology" data are limited to periphyton community composition rather than fish anatomy:

- **Periphyton biomass composition** at heavily impacted site M2 was dominated by **non-photosynthetic filamentous material**, primarily the acid-tolerant chemolithotrophic bacterium **[[Thiobacillus]]**, rather than true algae.
- Despite negligible chlorophyll *a* at M2, this site showed the **highest dry weight and ash-free dry mass (AFDM)** values among all sites — an apparent contradiction explained by luxuriant non-algal (bacterial) growth.
- Inorganic chemical precipitates, likely **ferric hydroxide [Fe(OH)₃]** from pyrite oxidation, formed on slides and the streambed at M2.
- Test organism **[[Ceriodaphnia dubia]]** (Cladocera, Daphniidae) was used as a standardized chronic toxicity indicator; no morphological alterations were reported, only survival and reproductive endpoints.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Monitor Creek is a first-order, ~10 km stream draining a 34.3 km² catchment in Alpine County, California, historically subjected to ~70 years of gold mining (mine shafts, tailings piles, an abandoned mill) through the 1950s.
- **Site gradient**: Seven sites (M1–M7) spanned a pollution gradient along Monitor Creek, plus two Carson River reference sites (C1 upstream, C2 downstream of confluence). Sites M2–M4 were downstream of major mining impacts; M5–M7 were upstream, relatively unimpacted.
- **Flow regime**: Intermittent, extremely low discharge (0.02–0.19 L/s at M2) during a multi-year regional drought, likely exacerbating AMD concentration effects.
- **Periphyton community role**: Serves as the primary functional/ecological indicator taxon, reflecting integrated impacts of AMD on primary production and community structure — an alternative to reliance on standardized laboratory stock organisms.
- **Toxicant sources**: Diffuse non-point AMD sources (oxidizing pyritic tailings, mine drainage) elevate sulfate, biologically active iron, and total dissolved solids (TDS) while depressing pH and dissolved oxygen, especially at site M2.
- Comparison ecosystem: The [[Carson River]] (sites C1/C2) served as a relatively higher-quality reference system, showing the greatest periphyton chlorophyll accumulation of any site sampled.

## Key Findings

### Water Quality Degradation
- Site **M2** consistently showed the most degraded conditions: lowest pH and dissolved oxygen; highest conductance, TDS, ammonia, sulfate, and biologically active iron among Monitor Creek and Carson River sites.
- Turbidity and total phosphorus were paradoxically highest in the **Carson River**, unrelated to Monitor Creek mining inputs.
- Prior year-long monitoring (Vinyard & Watts 1992) found frequent violations of Lahontan Regional Water Quality Control Board / EPA standards for sulfate, TDS, total N, total P, and pH; heavy metals (As, Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Pb, Hg, Ag) remained below toxic thresholds.

### Periphyton Bioassay (Novel Technique)
- Highest periphyton chlorophyll concentrations occurred at upstream Monitor Creek sites (**M6, M7**), but overall algal growth was far greater in the **Carson River (C1)** than anywhere in Monitor Creek.
- Site **M2** periphyton showed negligible chlorophyll but highest AFDM/dry weight due to *Thiobacillus* dominance.
- ANOVA of gross primary production (factorial design: slide origin × water origin) showed **significant main effects and interaction terms** across all three bioassay dates (Aug, Sept, Nov 1990), indicating that periphyton communities from different sites respond differentially to water of different origins — evidence of local community adaptation/structuring.
- Slides incubated in **M2 water** showed suppressed primary production regardless of slide origin (except M6-origin slides), most severely in September (lowest-flow period).
- Correlation between chlorophyll content and gross primary production was weak (r = 0.128, P = 0.727), suggesting factors beyond simple algal biomass (e.g., toxicant stress, community composition) drive functional impairment.

### Ceriodaphnia dubia Chronic Toxicity Bioassay
- EPA chronic bioassay (8-day exposure, 100% site water, *Ceriodaphnia dubia* neonates) found:
  - **Complete mortality and zero reproduction** at sites **M2 and M3** — confirming acute/chronic toxicity.
  - No significant difference in survival/reproduction among **M6, M7, and C1** (relatively unimpacted sites).
- Despite methodological caveats (>20% control mortality), qualitative toxicity conclusions were robust and **concordant with the periphyton bioassay results**, cross-validating both approaches.

### Comparative Bioassay Value
- The novel periphyton primary-production bioassay is proposed as a **rapid, low-cost, ecologically relevant alternative** to standard single-species toxicity tests (e.g., *Ceriodaphnia*, *Selenastrum capricornutum*, *Pimephales promelas*), requiring minimal equipment while assessing **existing natural community function** rather than standardized laboratory stock organisms.
- Unlike taxonomic diversity-based periphyton assessments, the functional bioassay does not require extensive taxonomic expertise and yields results more rapidly, though it does not identify specific toxicant mechanisms.

### Broader Implications
- Adverse ecological impacts from 19th–20th century gold mining persist ~30+ years after mining cessation, underscoring the long legacy of unreclaimed mine lands.
- Findings support continued monitoring of any future mining resumption in the [[Monitor Creek]] basin, given demonstrated ongoing risks to the [[Carson River]] water quality and downstream agricultural/municipal water use.
