## Executive Summary
This document is a press release from the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish detailing an international fish stocking event rather than a primary morphological or ecological research paper. Approximately 10,000 [[German Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) fry, hatched and reared at New Mexico's [[Red River Hatchery]], were transported via private aircraft to Mexico's state of Sinaloa for experimental introduction into the [[El Fuerte River]]. The initiative was privately funded by New Mexico State Game Commission Chairman Eddie Munoz and coordinated through the New Mexico-Mexico Border Commission, marking a notable early example of a cold-water species stocking effort executed over a long-distance aerial transport (8+ hours), a logistical feat previously reserved only for warm-water fish species.

## Background & Description
*Note: The source text is a fisheries management press release and does not contain detailed morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data. The following represents contextual life-stage information extracted from the text.*

- **Species:** German Brown Trout (*Salmo trutta*), a salmonid of European origin, widely introduced into North American waters.
- **Life Stage at Transport:** **Fry** — early post-hatch juvenile stage, indicating the fish were transported prior to significant growth or development of full adult morphological traits.
- **Origin of Stock:** Eggs were purchased specifically for this project (not sourced from existing wild New Mexico broodstock allocations) and incubated/hatched at the [[Red River Hatchery]] in "spare areas," implying standard hatchery-rearing conditions were used up to the fry stage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range Context:** *Salmo trutta* is a **cold-water species**, typically requiring well-oxygenated, cool freshwater streams — a habitat niche distinct from the warm-water fish species more commonly relocated across long distances at the time.
- **Introduced Habitat:** The fry were released into **El Fuerte River**, a river system in the Mexican state of **Sinaloa**, representing a novel, non-native ecosystem for this species. This constitutes an **experimental introduction/translocation** rather than a native stocking event.
- **Interspecies/Transboundary Management:** The stocking was explicitly requested by the **New Mexico-Mexico Border Commission**, suggesting an interest in establishing a viable cold-water sport fishery or assessing the ecological viability of [[Salmonidae]] species outside their typical range in transboundary river systems.
- **Habitat Suitability Concerns:** As an "experimental planting," there is an implicit acknowledgment that the suitability of the El Fuerte River for supporting a self-sustaining German Brown Trout population was untested and unconfirmed at the time of this release.

## Key Findings
- **Logistical Milestone:** This event marked one of the first known instances of the New Mexico Game and Fish Department transporting **cold-water fish** (as opposed to routine warm-water species stocking) over a long-distance flight (over 8 hours) via private aircraft.
- **Survival Post-Transport:** Despite the extended transit time, the fry "arrived in good shape" and were stocked shortly after arrival, suggesting effective hatchery transport protocols (e.g., appropriate water temperature/oxygenation management) were employed for fry transport logistics.
- **Funding & Governance:** The project was **privately funded** by State Game Commission Chairman Eddie Munoz, distinguishing it from standard state-budgeted hatchery stocking programs, and was executed as a **donation to the Mexican government**.
- **No Domestic Impact:** The press release explicitly notes that New Mexico's own streams and stocking allocations were **not reduced** as a result of this international donation — the eggs were procured as a separate, dedicated batch for the Mexico project.
- **Institutional Collaboration:** The event demonstrates transboundary cooperation between the [[New Mexico Game and Fish Department]], the [[New Mexico-Mexico Border Commission]], and Mexican governmental authorities for aquatic species introduction and fisheries development.
