## Executive Summary
This document is a 1992 BIO/WEST, Inc. memorandum (from Rich Valdez to Larry Crist and Dave Wegner) clarifying the division of research responsibilities between BIO/WEST and the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGF) regarding [[Humpback Chub]] studies in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. While not a primary scientific data paper, it contains important ecological and methodological context regarding life-stage definitions, habitat classifications, and sampling jurisdiction that directly bear on how humpback chub distribution, abundance, and habitat-use data were collected and interpreted during the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., body form, meristics, coloration) are presented in this source. The document is administrative/programmatic in nature and focuses on sampling jurisdiction rather than physical description of the species. See [[Humpback Chub]] for morphological characteristics documented elsewhere.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species of focus:** [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a cyprinid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], notable for its canyon-bound, swift-water habitat associations.
- **Life stage definitions:**
  - **Juvenile:** Fish age one calendar year and older (age group **I+**), per clarification from the Adaptive Management/Advisory Committee Team (ACT) on April 19, 1991.
  - **Age-0 (young-of-year):** Explicitly excluded from BIO/WEST's sampling responsibility; assigned to other research groups (e.g., AGF).
- **Habitat types sampled by BIO/WEST:**
  - Shoreline runs
  - Eddies and large eddy return channels (navigable by boat)
  - Ephemeral backwaters
  - Low-velocity runs
  - Side channels
  - Mainriver slackwater areas
- **Habitat partitioning between research groups:**
  - **AGF:** Responsible for nursery/[[Backwater Habitat]] sampling, March through September, targeting age-0 humpback chub ecology.
  - **BIO/WEST:** Responsible for shoreline and eddy return channel habitats year-round, targeting age I+ juveniles and adults; also covers backwaters when AGF is not actively sampling.
- **Habitat use finding:** Combined sampling data from BIO/WEST (shorelines/eddies) and AGF (backwaters) indicate that **age I+ humpback chub use backwaters, or are at least transient users of this habitat type**, reinforcing the need for coordinated, non-overlapping sampling efforts.
- **Note on terminology:** Many habitats colloquially called "backwaters" by BIO/WEST are more precisely **eddy return channels** — boat-navigable habitats used by all age classes of chub, distinct from the shallow nursery backwaters used primarily by age-0 fish.

## Key Findings
- **Contractual scope (Bureau of Reclamation Contract No. 0-CS-40-09110):** BIO/WEST's mandate is to collect data on **distribution, abundance, and habitat use of juvenile (age I+) and adult** humpback chub in the mainstem Colorado River — explicitly excluding age-0 fish.
- **Jurisdictional resolution (ACT Meeting, April 19, 1991):** Sampling responsibilities were divided by **gear type and habitat**:
  - AGF → backwaters (March–September)
  - BIO/WEST → shoreline/shallow habitats year-round, including backwaters when not covered by AGF.
- **Expert program review (Dr. W.L. Minckley et al., letter to D. Patten, January 17, 1992):** Reaffirmed BIO/WEST's role in studying **dispersion, dispersal, and mainstem adult demography** of humpback chub, while de-emphasizing habitat assessment (assigned to [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) and mainstem larval/juvenile ecology (assigned to AGF).
- **Sampling methodology used by BIO/WEST:**
  - Electrofishing of shorelines and large eddy return channels
  - Small-mesh gill and trammel nets in eddies, shorelines, and return channels
  - Large-mesh seines in shoreline areas
- **Explicit exclusions:** BIO/WEST does **not** sample nursery backwaters, nor assess distribution, abundance, habitat use, or survival of **age-0 humpback chub** — this responsibility belongs to other research entities (AGF, FWS), with data-sharing cooperation.
- **Programmatic emphasis:** The memo underscores an institutional priority on **inter-agency coordination and data integration** among BIO/WEST, AGF, [[Arizona State University]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to avoid redundant or conflicting sampling efforts within the [[Grand Canyon Ecosystem]].
