# Bonytail Recovery Program: Draft Field, Handling, and Propagation Guidelines (1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This undated-in-scan **draft** (headers show 1995) is a multi-part protocol for research, collection, handling, transport, genetic sampling, captive propagation, and disposition of [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), a threatened and endangered fish of the Southwest. It was prepared under the [[Bonytail Recovery Plan]] and coordinated by the [[Arizona Fisheries Resources Office]] as lead station. The document combines a permit-and-study-plan checklist, detailed standard operating procedures, attachments (sampling and equipment lists), a stress-physiology primer, and a Region 2 memorandum setting out responsibilities for investigators, the lead station, the [[Fish Health Center]], and the [[Regional Endangered Species Office]]. Its central aims are to minimize stress and mortality during captive work, standardize data and tissue collection, and limit surplus production of fish.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** Bonytail, a [[Threatened and Endangered Fishes]] species in the Southwest. The document does not give morphological or life-history details.
- **Participating agencies (listed as cooperators):** U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (including [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], the [[Southwestern Fisheries Technology Center]], and the Parker subunit), Arizona Game and Fish Department, Nevada Department of Game and Fish, [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the National Biological Service, [[Arizona State University]], the Arizona Ecological Services Office, and the National Park Service.
- **Scope of the protocols:** Study investigator responsibilities; field and hatchery permit documents; fish health; handling and collection (seining, electrofishing, angling, trammel netting); tagging and sampling; hatchery and broodstock management; trucking and box/bag/cooler shipping; acclimation; disposition; and reference material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water quality and holding conditions**
- Holding and hauling water should be kept at or near river temperature. The source notes that water temperature for hauling should not exceed 76 °F and should be tempered slowly to receiving-water temperature.
- Dissolved oxygen must be maintained at saturation at all times.
- Hatchery water-quality monitoring covers hardness, pH, ammonia, nitrite, temperature, oxygen, and gas saturation.

**Stress physiology**
The stress-physiology attachment explains that transport mortality can come from severe stressors (e.g., overcrowding with inadequate aeration, abrupt temperature change, net entanglement, crushing), from mild stressors that act together (vehicle movement, crowding, poor water quality, high CO₂, low dissolved oxygen, temperature extremes), or from disease that appears one to two weeks after transport. The stress response raises metabolic rate, causes toxic metabolite buildup (e.g., lactic acid), depletes energy reserves, disrupts ionic balance, and suppresses immunity.

## Key Findings

### 1. Investigation Design and Permitting
- Investigators must contact the lead station early, submit a study plan and permit applications, and file a formal report on completion. Permits are not issued until study plans are in place, and are not renewed for investigators with overdue final reports.
- Study plans must include communication networks, scheduling, and protocols for field handling, collection, transport, acclimation, sampling, housing, propagation, and disposition. Protocols must address equipment sterilization, back-up life-support systems, and emergency procedures.
- Lead stations give investigators two weeks' advance notice of field collections, and must notify the Fish Health Biologist, Fisheries Resources Office, and cooperators.

### 2. Handling and Collection Standards
- Personnel must be trained in fish handling. Handlers should touch fish as little as possible, use wet hands and clean gloves, and avoid squeezing or gripping. Fish should be covered with wet towels to protect them from sun and wind.
- Holding tanks contain 0.5% NaCl and a pre-determined MS-222 concentration. The source gives the MS-222 range as "126–260 mg/L," although the scan is garbled and the figure should be checked against the original.
- Optional stress-reduction additives include Furacin at 10 mg/L, "stress coat," Polyaqua, Propolyaqua, and Novaqua.
- Fish must recover from anesthesia in fresh water or a live box before release, and must regain equilibrium and orient to the current before release.
- **Seining:** Mesh must be fine (1/4" delta mesh). Nets must not contain creosote or knotted line. Bonytail are collected with trammel nets but not gill nets, and are cut from nets to speed release.
- **Electrofishing:** Operators must be trained or certified. Settings (volts, pulse, frequency) are left blank in the draft, and the anode mesh and size are unspecified.
- **Angling:** Live bait such as insects and earthworms is allowed, but no fish may be used as bait. Only barbless hooks are allowed.
- Piscicides (rotenone, antimycin/Fintrol) are prohibited for collecting Bonytail.

### 3. Tagging, Sampling, and Genetic Collection
- All fish must be sampled nonlethally and must be tagged or marked before transport and acclimation at Dexter or Willow Beach NFH.
- Length and weight are recorded on wet scales or boards. Anesthetized fish should be on wet boards for no more than one minute. No fish are measured before transport.
- **Genetic samples:** Gametes are cryopreserved separately without pooling. Muscle plugs (4–5 mm) are taken from both sides of the dorsal fin, and fin clips (about 5 mm) from both lobes of the caudal fin. Blood (about 0.25 mL) is drawn from the caudal vein into an EDTA tube. Sampling tools and gloves are changed for each fish to prevent cross-contamination.

### 4. Captive Propagation
- Captive propagation is a major component of the recovery program. Its aims are to (a) prevent immediate extinction, (b) preserve unique genetic resources, and (c) establish self-sustaining populations in suitable historical habitat. Research and public education depend partly on hatchery-produced fish.
- Production numbers are set by six criteria: propagation goals, techniques, fecundity, mortality under culture conditions, production uncertainty, and available facilities.
- The source gives hatchery requirements for water supply, rearing tanks, broodstock management, and feeding, but leaves quantitative values blank in the scan.

### 5. Transport Protocol
- **Truck transport:** Density should not exceed 30 g/L. Water should be saturated with oxygen and contain 0.6% NaCl, 25 mg/L MS-222, 8 g/L stress coat, and 10 mg/L Furacin. Food is withheld 24 hours for fish under about 10 g and 48 hours for larger fingerlings and broodfish. Salt is added for two days after transfer.
- **Monitoring:** Dissolved oxygen and temperature are checked at least every 30 minutes during transport, using a long-lead oxygen meter.
- **Box and bag shipping:** Recommended for larvae and fish up to 3 inches total length. Bags are double-sealed and inflated with oxygen. Water contains 0.5% NaCl, 25 mg/L MS-222, and 10 mg/L Furacin. Stocking density should not exceed 60 g/L.
- **Cooler shipping:** Used for low densities (about 50 g/L). Battery aerators and a spare aerator must be carried, along with oxygen cylinders for emergencies.
- **Loading:** This is the most stressful phase. Fish are loaded by "wet transfer" using buckets, and nets must not be overloaded.

### 6. Surplus Fish and Disposition
- Planned production aims to minimize surplus fish. Surplus is a by-product that costs as much to maintain as production fish, and can become a disease risk.
- Incidental take that dies is preserved in 10% formalin for 7 days and then transferred to 70% ethanol. Voucher specimens go to the Arizona State University ichthyology museum and become University property.
- Surplus fish are the property of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. They are released only if captured and produced for approved stocking projects. Surplus fish may be provided alive to permitted requesters, who must pay for care and shipping.
- Willow Beach NFH must have a current disposition plan. Surplus fish may be euthanized with MS-222 at about 600 mg/L. Carcasses are disposed of ecologically and legally.

### 7. Regional Responsibilities (Region 2 Memorandum)
- The memorandum's purposes are to set general protocols for biological investigations of T&E fishes in the Southwest Region, to obtain accurate biological data, protect fish, avoid duplicated work, and prevent studies that could threaten species.
- The **Fish Health Center** reviews proposals involving handling, may require quarantine, and approves treatments for disease outbreaks.
- The **Regional Endangered Species Office** maintains study-plan records, issues permits, and coordinates communications.
- Final reports must include the approved proposal, permit documents, executive summary, methods, results, discussion, and conclusions and recommendations, plus sampling and housing information.

### 8. Reference Base
The document cites about 17 sources, including fish hatchery management manuals, stocking-plan guidance for threatened and endangered fishes, American Fisheries Society guidelines on stripped bass culture, studies on stress and transport of largemouth bass, a survey of fish transport equipment, and guidelines for fish use in field research (ASIH, AFS, AIFRB). It also includes American Veterinary Medical Association euthanasia guidance and federal and state permit references.
