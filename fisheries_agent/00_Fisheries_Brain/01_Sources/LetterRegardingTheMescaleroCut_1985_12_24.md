# Mescalero Cutthroat Trout - Correspondence Summary (1985)

## Executive Summary
This document is a personal correspondence letter dated December 24, 1985, from Terrence J. Nerkel to Gerald L. Burton (Endangered Species Biologist, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service). The letter serves as an informal field report summarizing the author's firsthand knowledge of a small, isolated population of [[Mescalero Cutthroat Trout]] located in Indian Creek on the Mescalero Apache Reservation, New Mexico. The letter is primarily concerned with establishing the population's location, its uncertain taxonomic status, and the mysterious history of its introduction, rather than presenting formal quantitative data. It highlights the involvement of taxonomist Dr. Robert Behnke in identifying the specimens and raises unresolved questions regarding the native origin of the stock relative to the [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]].

## Background & Description
* **Taxonomic Identification:** Specimens collected from Indian Creek were preserved in formalin and sent to Dr. Robert Behnke (Colorado State University) for identification.
* **Distinguishing Traits:** The author recalls that Dr. Behnke's findings indicated this population "differed somewhat" physically from [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]] populations found elsewhere in the region, suggesting a potentially unique or distinct physical characteristic set for the Mescalero population.
* **Note:** No specific meristic counts, coloration details, or morphometric data are provided in this letter; the author defers to Dr. Behnke's formal publications for detailed physical descriptions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
* **Habitat Location:** The population is restricted to a specific, limited portion of Indian Creek on the Mescalero Reservation.
* **Accessibility:** The specific stream reach supporting the cutthroat population was remote and not accessible by road, requiring a significant hike to reach.
* **Sympatric Species/Risks:** The surrounding terrestrial habitat has a notable reputation for high rattlesnake density (anecdotally, the author killed a 43-inch snake during a site visit).
* **Population Density:** The population size in Indian Creek was reported as "quite small," limiting the author to collecting only a few specimens at any given time to avoid depleting the population.
* **Regional Context:** The author conducted broader surveys of fish populations in various small streams on and near the Mescalero Reservation but was unsuccessful in locating any additional cutthroat populations beyond the one in Indian Creek.

## Key Findings
* **Uncertain Origin/Provenance:** The most significant scientific question raised is the origin of the stock. Local history suggests the trout were transplanted into Indian Creek around 1915 by Forest Service or Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) personnel.
* **Stocking Source:** The stock is believed to have been carried over the mountains from the **Rio Bonito**, a headwater tributary of the **Pecos River**.
* **Native vs. Introduced Debate:** It remains unresolved whether the original stock in the Rio Bonito was native to that specific drainage or if it was itself a product of earlier state stocking efforts.
* **Historical Records:** Dr. Behnke noted that cutthroat trout were historically reported in the Pecos drainage as far back as the 1800s.
* **Confounding Factor:** The author notes that the state (N.M. Fish and Game Dept.) is known to have later stocked [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]] into the Rio Bonito, potentially confounding or hybridizing with any native stock that might have existed there, complicating the ability to trace the true genetic lineage of the Mescalero population.
* **Conservation Interest (Historical):** At the time of the author's tenure, Mescalero Tribal employees expressed interest in creating a high-elevation reservoir (near Sierra Blanca) specifically to stock with Mescalero cutthroats, intending to create an exclusive, high-value recreational fishery.
* **Data Gaps:** The author explicitly offers to provide the FWS with a list of the specific streams surveyed (and those left unsurveyed) during the broader search for cutthroat populations, indicating that supplementary unpublished field data exists.
