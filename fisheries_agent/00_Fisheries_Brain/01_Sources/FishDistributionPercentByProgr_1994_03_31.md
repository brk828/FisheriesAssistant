## Executive Summary

This document is not a species-specific ichthyological study but an **administrative/statistical report** compiled by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Division of Fish Hatcheries, summarizing **[[Fish Distribution]]** (by egg numbers, fish numbers, and fish weight) across the National Fish Hatchery (NFH) system for **Fiscal Year 1993**. The report breaks down output from ~60 individual hatchery stations by **program code** (e.g., Restoration/Mitigation, Management Assistance, Research and Development, Endangered/Threatened Species, Egg Shipment, Farm Pond Program) and by **receiving agency** (e.g., BIA, State Governments, Tribes, other federal agencies). It quantifies the relative percentage contribution of each program to total eggs, fish numbers, and biomass (weight) distributed per station, and provides a system-wide aggregate total. This is a key primary-source dataset for understanding the **structure and allocation logic of the U.S. federal hatchery system** rather than the biology of any single fish taxon.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source. The report is purely quantitative/administrative, tracking:
- **Egg Numbers** distributed
- **Fish Numbers** distributed
- **Fish Weight** (aggregate biomass, presumably in pounds) distributed

No species identifications, size classes, life-stage descriptions, or morphometric measurements are included in the extracted text. Species-level data would require the underlying "by Station, Species, Program and Agency" detail tables, which are aggregated to station totals in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no direct habitat or niche descriptions are given, the report's structural codes imply an ecological/management framework:

- **Water Type Codes** used by hatcheries include Aquarium (AQ), Estuarine (ES), Hatchery-Federal (HF), Hatchery-Other (HO), Lake/Pond (LK), Reservoir (RS), Stream/River/Canal (SR), Tailwater (TL), and Research (RE) waters — indicating hatchery stock is destined for a broad range of **lentic, lotic, and estuarine systems**.
- **Program Codes** reveal the ecological/management purpose of distributed fish and eggs:
  - **[[Restoration/Mitigation]] (RM)** — dominant program at most stations (e.g., Abernathy 100%, Hagerman 100%, Quilcene 100%, Jordan River 100%), reflecting compensatory mitigation for habitat loss (e.g., dam impacts).
  - **[[Endangered and Threatened Species Program]] (ES)** — present at stations like Alchesay/Williams Creek, Hotchkiss, Mesalero, Natchitoches, Orangeburg.
  - **[[Management Assistance Program]] (MA)** — supports state/tribal fisheries (e.g., Bowden 79–98%, Leadville, Wolf Creek).
  - **[[Research and Development Program]] (RE)** — smaller but consistent allocation across nearly all stations.
  - **[[Farm Pond Program]] (FP)** — present at Mammoth Spring, Meridian, Private John Allen — indicating stocking of small impounded ponds, likely for put-and-take recreational fisheries.
  - **[[Egg Shipment Program]] (BS)** — large egg-only transfers (e.g., Carson 1,986,600 eggs; Genoa 27,784,684 eggs; Garrison Dam 5,520,000 eggs), indicating interstation/interagency egg exchange supporting broodstock and rearing elsewhere.
- **Agency Codes** show recipients spanning federal (BLM, BOR, COE, NPS, FWS), state, tribal (BIA, Indian Tribe non-BIA), university, and private entities — reflecting the multi-jurisdictional ecological management role of the hatchery system in stocking public waters, tribal waters, and mitigation sites tied to federal water infrastructure (e.g., **[[Lower Snake River Compensation Plan]]**).

## Key Findings

- **System-wide FY1993 totals**: **143,344,827 eggs**, **199,928,782 fish (numbers)**, and **6,147,825 (weight units, likely lbs)** distributed across all reporting NFH stations.
- **[[Restoration/Mitigation Program]] (RM)** was the single largest program by fish numbers and weight at the majority of stations, frequently accounting for 70–100% of distribution (e.g., Abernathy, Hagerman, Jordan River, Kooskia, Makah, Quilcene, Quinault, Pendills Creek, Pittsford — all 100% RM for fish numbers).
- **Egg-only large-volume stations** dominated by **Egg Shipment (BS)**: Genoa (27.78M eggs), Gavins Point (11.2M eggs), Garrison Dam (5.52M eggs), Ennis (26.49M eggs), and Valley City/Baldhill (6.02M eggs) — indicating these facilities function significantly as **egg-source/broodstock stations** feeding other hatcheries.
- **Highest total egg producers**: Ennis (35,027,719 eggs total distributed across programs), Genoa (40,129,204), Gavins Point (34,378,000), Garrison Dam (11,555,900).
- **Mixed-program stations** (e.g., Alchesay/Williams Creek, Bo Ginn, Bowden, Bozeman, Carbon Hill, Creston, Lamar, Leadville, Mammoth Spring, Meridian, Uvalde, Walhalla, Wolf Creek) show diversified allocation across MA, OT (Other), RE, and RM, reflecting multi-purpose hatchery operations serving both conservation and recreational/management goals.
- **Endangered/Threatened Species (ES) allocations** were comparatively minor in most stations where present (typically <3% of fish numbers), except Alchesay/Williams Creek (20.98% egg-adjacent / significant fish % via MA/ES mix).
- The report demonstrates the **administrative percentage-based accounting method** used by USFWS to track hatchery output allocation — a structural precursor to modern **[[Fisheries Information System]] (FIS)** data used in federal hatchery performance reporting.
- No individual **fish species** are named in the extracted station-level tables; species-level breakdowns would appear in the full "by Station, Species, Program and Agency" report tables, of which only the program/agency summary layer is captured here.
