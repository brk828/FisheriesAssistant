# Beal Slough and Needles Dredge Yard Razorback Sucker Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This trip report from the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR) Fisheries Lab (Boulder City, NV) documents a January 3-5, 2011 sampling effort at the [[Needles Dredge Yard]] and [[Beal Slough]] near Needles, California. The goal was to recapture enough [[Razorback Sucker]] to generate a population estimate for Beal Slough, where native fish were first stocked in April 2010. Recaptures were too few for an estimate, but the stocked fish appeared healthy and growing at rates comparable to other backwater habitats, and sampling is planned to continue through 2011.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Beal Slough is a small backwater north of Park Moabi. It connects to the [[Colorado River]] through a single 24-inch culvert at its north end.
- **Stocking history:** Beal Slough received 755 razorbacks (mean total length [TL] 337 mm) from the [[Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery]] (Cornville, AZ) on April 1, 2010. This was the first native fish stocking at the site.
- **Previous trip:** A December 15-17, 2010 trip contacted 26 razorbacks.
- **Trip personnel:** Jeff Anderson, Jeff Lantow, Eric Loomis, and Jenny Smith (USBR Fisheries Lab).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study goals.** The team had two objectives:
1. Determine whether native fish moved in and out of Beal Slough through the culvert connecting it to the Colorado River.
2. Assess short-term survival of fish stocked the previous spring.

**Sampling methods.**
- *Needles Dredge Yard:* Two 150-ft and one 300-ft trammel nets (1.5-inch mesh) were set between 2:00 and 2:30 pm on a Monday and fished overnight.
- *Beal Slough:* Six trammel nets (five 150-ft, one 300-ft) were set between 2:00 and 2:30 pm on a Tuesday and fished overnight. See [[Trammel Net Sampling]].
- Captured fish were held in fresh water during processing and released into the backwater of capture.
- Native fish were measured, weighed, and scanned for [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging]] (PIT) and wire tags. Fish without a tag received a 134 kHz PIT tag.

**Non-native species.** Non-native fish were captured at both sites. These included [[Largemouth Bass]], bluegill sunfish, yellow bullhead, redear sunfish, channel catfish, [[Common Carp]], and, in Beal Slough, smallmouth bass. Full net composition appears in the report's Tables 1 and 2 and is summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Key Findings

**Needles Dredge Yard (January 3, 2011)**
- 10 razorbacks were captured (mean TL 507 mm; range 457-574 mm).
- All 10 already carried 134 kHz PIT tags.
- The non-native catch was larger in number, with 15 redear sunfish, 14 yellow bullhead, 13 common carp, 11 largemouth bass, and 5 bluegill, plus 2 channel catfish (mean TL 685 mm).

**Beal Slough (January 5, 2011)**
- 37 razorbacks were captured (mean TL 474 mm; range 415-656 mm).
- Twelve fish were tuberculate, and ten had marked lernaea infestations, as the report describes them.
- All but two fish had previously been PIT tagged. The two untagged fish were tagged on site.

**Evidence of movement**
- A very large female (TL 656 mm, weight 3,525 g) had no PIT or wire tag, indicating a new contact that had likely entered from outside the stocked population.
- A male (TL 530 mm) stocked into the Needles Dredge Yard on March 19, 2008 was recaptured in Beal Slough, documenting movement between the two sites.
- Table 4 lists individual recaptures from Beal Slough. Most were April 2010 Bubbling Ponds stocked fish (2008 rearing year) recaptured on January 5, 2011, with TLs and weights recorded for each.

**Stocked-fish survival**
- Of the April 2010 stocked fish, 25 unique individuals were caught on the first trip and 31 on the second.
- Only one recapture occurred between trips. This was too few to calculate a [[Population Estimation]] using [[Mark-Recapture]] methods.
- A total of 55 stocked fish were caught across both trips.
- The authors judged the fish to be in good health and growing at rates comparable to other backwater habitats.

**Planned work.** Sampling trips will continue through 2011 to build recapture data for a future population estimate.
