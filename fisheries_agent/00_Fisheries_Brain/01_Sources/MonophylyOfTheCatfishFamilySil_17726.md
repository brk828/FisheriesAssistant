# Monophyly of the Catfish Family Siluridae (Teleostei: Siluriformes), with a Critique of Previous Hypotheses of the Family's Relationships

#ichthyology #marine-biology #siluridae

## Executive Summary

This 1991 paper in the [[Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society]] by Alan H. Bornbusch tests whether the Eurasian catfish family [[Siluridae]] is a natural (monophyletic) group. Using a phylogenetic analysis of derived character states, the author identifies five synapomorphies unique to the family and finds that previous hypotheses linking silurids to other catfish families are poorly supported. The issue also contains a separate, non-aquatic paper by Walter Stalker Greaves on premolar loss in selenodont artiodactyls, which is outside the scope of this note.

## Background & Description

The Siluridae comprises 101 extant and four extinct species, making it one of the more diverse Eurasian catfish families. Only the Sisoridae and Bagridae show comparable Eurasian diversity. [[Regan 1911]] established the widely accepted diagnosis of the family, based on a short, broad palatine and a lateral ethmoid whose posterior extension meets the sphenotic so that the frontal has no free edge. Regan's concept included [[Silurus]] and Asiatic genera such as [[Wallago]], [[Ompok]], [[Kryptopterus]] and [[Hemisilurus]]. Several further genera were later added without changing his diagnosis, including Hito, Pterocryptis, Ceratoglanis and the Pliocene fossil Pliosilurus.

Earlier classifications were less restrictive. [[Bleeker 1863]] placed silurids and schilbeids in one Siluriformes but split them into subgroups, while [[Gunther 1864]] and [[Gill 1872]] grouped silurids with many externally similar catfishes. Regan's osteological criteria therefore narrowed the family to a more coherent set of genera.

Three hypotheses about silurid relationships are reviewed:

- **Tilak (1963):** Siluridae are more primitive than [[Plotosidae]] and closely related to them, based on the Weberian apparatus and neurocranium.
- **Chardon (1968):** Siluridae, [[Helogenidae]] and [[Amblycipitidae]] form the primitive suborder Siluroidei.
- **Howes (1985):** Siluridae are not monophyletic. [[Wallago]] and [[Silurus]] are proposed as sister taxa related to the African [[Malapteruridae]] (electric catfishes), and the remaining silurids are linked to the Afro-Asian [[Schilbeidae]].

The methods follow Hennig's phylogenetic approach, with polarity assigned by outgroup comparison. The outgroups are [[Diplomystidae]] and [[Hypsidoridae]], following Grande (1987). Representatives of about 30 siluriform families were examined, including all 18 silurid genera recognized by Bornbusch (1988), along with gymnotiforms, cypriniforms and characiforms. Specimens were cleared and stained following Potthoff (1984).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper is taxonomic and anatomical rather than ecological. It does not report habitat measurements, abundance, or water chemistry. Its ecological relevance lies in the Eurasian freshwater distribution of silurids and in the function of their anatomy, such as the palatine-maxillary barbel mechanism. Gosline (1975) described this mechanism as "one of the most specialized of all catfishes." The silurid extensor tentaculi is longer and more longitudinally oriented than in other siluroids, which the author interprets as a probable correlate of the shortened palatine.

## Key Findings

**1. Five synapomorphies support silurid monophyly:**
1. Palatine reduced to a small nodule with no posterior extension.
2. Frontal laterally occluded by slender extensions of the lateral ethmoid and sphenotic, so the frontal has no free lateral edge.
3. Ossified transscapular ligament compressed into a thin blade.
4. Distal radials of dorsal-fin pterygiophores absent.
5. Anal-fin ray articulations shifted posterodorsally, so each ray articulates directly with the proximal radial of the next pterygiophore.

**2. Tilak's hypothesis is poorly supported.** No shared derived Weberian apparatus character states link silurids and plotosids. Several character states Tilak treated as primitive are in fact derived within Siluroidea. The shared derived states that do exist (such as epaxialis invasion of the skull roof and loss of a levator arcus palatini crest) are not unique to the two families.

**3. Chardon's Siluroidei is not supported.** Of the three derived character states Chardon shared by silurids, helogenids and amblycipitids, none is unique to those taxa. Two are widespread among siluroids. Silurids and helogenids share several other derived states, but these are also not unique.

**4. Howes' non-monophyly hypothesis is not tenable.** Howes' evidence is poorly enumerated, and many of the features he cited show silurids to be more similar to one another than to other siluriforms. He also overlooked that Regan (1911) gave two distinguishing features, and that nasal barbels are absent in the schilbeid genus Laides, contrary to his claim that barbels distinguish the two families.

**5. The Siluridae–Malapteruridae link is unsupported.** Only two derived states are shared (anterior extension of the epaxialis onto the skull roof, and dorsal-fin reduction). The skull roof modifications are non-homologous, so epaxialis invasion evolved independently in the two groups.

**6. The Siluridae–Schilbeidae link is unsupported.** Silurids share several derived states with the schilbeid genus Schilbe (third infraorbital, epaxialis, skull roof, hypaxialis and posterior cardinal vein grooves), but none is unique to the two groups.

**7. Appendix character states.** The appendix lists silurid traits that are derived within Siluroidea but occur in other siluroids, which may inform future higher-level hypotheses. Examples include:
- A modified third infraorbital with a non-canal-bearing process, also seen in Schilbe and, to a lesser extent, some pangasiids.
- Absence of a levator arcus palatini crest on the hyomandibular.
- A supraoccipital process lacking a horizontal lamina.
- Asymmetrical posterior cardinal vein grooves, with the right groove larger than the left.
- A loosely attached supracleithrum, which differs from Regan's description of it as "wedged."
- Reduced pectoral-fin spine anterior process and reduced dorsal-fin spines.
- A posteriorly shifted dorsal fin. In [[Hemisilurus]], [[Ceratoglanis]] and some [[Kryptopterus]], the dorsal fin is absent.

**8. Conclusion.** The author concludes that phylogenetic analysis "offers strong evidence of silurid monophyly." No known set of character state distributions supports an alternative sister-group relationship, and additional character evidence is needed before one can be proposed.

**9. Separate article in the same issue.** Greaves (1991) argues that as jaws lengthen in later selenodont artiodactyls, anterior premolars fall in front of the buttressing tracts that resist skull torsion. This leads to premolar loss, even when a long diastema leaves enough space. This paper concerns mammals and is not aquatic.
