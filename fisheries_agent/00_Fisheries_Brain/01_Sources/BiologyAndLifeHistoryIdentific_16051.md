# Identification of Muskellunge, Northern Pike, and Their Hybrids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This 1986 American Fisheries Society chapter by [[Casselman Et Al.]] reviews morphological, meristic, osteological, cytogenetic, and electrophoretic criteria for separating [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy*), [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*), and their hybrid, the [[Tiger Muskellunge]]. The authors state that live or dead esocids can be hard to tell apart, and that anglers and law enforcement officers inadvertently and sometimes illegally harvest the rarer, more valued muskellunge. The work provides a dichotomous key to the family, detailed diagnostic features, a discriminant-function morphometric method, and electrophoretic loci suitable for forensic use. The authors conclude that no single criterion should be used alone.

## Background & Description

The family Esocidae comprises one genus and five species. Northern pike is circumpolar, Amur pike is endemic to Siberia and China, and muskellunge, chain pickerel, and the grass and redfin pickerels are endemic to North America. Members are elongate, laterally compressed fishes with a deeply forked caudal fin, dorsal and anal fins set far back and opposite each other, a duck-bill snout, large jaw teeth, and physostomous swim bladders. Fossil records extend from the Middle Eocene in Eurasia and the Paleocene in North America.

The authors note that the Amur pike is included in the key because it was released into Pennsylvania public waters. Hybrids occur naturally, in some populations up to 17% of large muskellunge-like esocids (unpublished data cited by the authors). Culture and stocking of tiger muskellunge create a need for reliable field identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Muskellunge and northern pike cohabit in many waters, and in such areas muskellunge are caught infrequently, so casual anglers often cannot recognise them. Survey responses indicated that most management agencies regard muskellunge identification as a problem. The authors stress that hybrids may be differently regulated across jurisdictions where all three forms occur.

Morphological differences are interpreted functionally. Head and anterior-body measurements are linked to prey type and abdominal vertebral number, while posterior fin-base and caudal-peduncle measures are linked to the burst acceleration used in predation (Webb 1978). Because all esocids feed similarly, these posterior traits are thought to be constrained within functional limits. Scale and cleithrum size differences are linked to the onset of maturity, since the sterile hybrid shows no growth inflection.

## Key Findings

**Color and pattern**
- Muskellunge show clear, spotted, or barred patterns. Northern pike show light, bean-shaped spots on a dark ground, with a juvenile barred pattern. Hybrids show narrow, chain-like vertical or oblique bars and often exaggerated cheek spotting.

**Key characters**
- Mandibular pores: muskellunge 6–10 per side, northern pike about 5 per side, and hybrids intermediate.
- Branchiostegal rays (divided count): muskellunge modal 8/10, northern pike modal 7/8 (or 6/8), hybrid modal 6/9 with a range of 3/9–9/9.
- Lateral line scales: muskellunge 140–176 (notched 55–77); northern pike 105–148 (notched 42–55); hybrid 126–145 (notched 45–56).
- Vertebrae: muskellunge modal 65; northern pike modal 60; hybrids 61–65, mostly 62–63.
- Cheek scalation: muskellunge only the upper half of the cheek and operculum scaled; northern pike full cheek scaling. Hybrids usually 7/10–9/10 on cheek.

**Morphometrics**
- Samples: 90 northern pike and 100 muskellunge from across the range. Ten measurements were taken, and ratios to standard length were used.
- Discriminant function classification accuracy was 99% overall, with two northern pike misclassified.
- Cranial depth, trunk length, interorbital width, lumbar length, and preorbital length contributed most to discrimination.
- Known hybrids scored intermediate, though 7 of 10 were nearer the muskellunge centroid.

**Scales and cleithra**
- Muskellunge scales are rounder (about 10% longer than wide), while northern pike and hybrid scales are about 25% longer than wide.
- Northern pike show a prominent hyaline annulus and paired focal circuli. Muskellunge foci are sparse and fragmented. Hybrid foci form networks.
- Cleithral shape differs clearly between parents. Hybrid cleithra fall into four types (A 16%, B 29%, C 8%, D 45%, N = 129), often abnormal, and are relatively larger than in either parent (hybrid cleithral radius about one-tenth of total length).

**Dentition and teeth**
- Muskellunge vomerine patch is constricted at the neck with a pair of canine-like teeth; northern pike is thicker with four small teeth in the neck row.
- Muskellunge dentary teeth are robust and tapered; northern pike teeth are laterally compressed and S-shaped. Hybrids are intermediate, generally closer to northern pike.

**Internal anatomy**
- Muskellunge have a dorsal aorta and aortic groove on the right side of the vertebral column after about the sixth vertebra; northern pike have a deep, strongly striated groove along the mid-ventral line. Hybrids are intermediate.

**Karyotype**
- Muskellunge and northern pike both have a diploid number of 50, with acrocentric chromosomes. Hybrids have the same number, with secondary arms on five chromosome pairs. No karyotypic distinction separates the species.

**Electrophoresis**
- Of 41 presumptive loci (75 allelic products, 3 buffer systems), 18 separated muskellunge from northern pike. Hybrids showed intermediate banding at all but two loci.
- Gmp, Mdh-1,2, and Ldh-1 were recommended as ideal loci because they store well, are monomorphic in both species, resolve well, and are expressed in skeletal muscle.
- Gpi-1, Pgd-1, and Sdh should not be used alone because of polymorphisms.

**Recommendations**
- No single criterion should be used in isolation.
- Hybrid recognition should emphasize characters showing heterosis, such as relative cleithrum size.
