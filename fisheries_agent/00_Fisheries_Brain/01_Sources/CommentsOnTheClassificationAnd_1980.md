# Comments on the Classification and Nomenclature of Lampreys — An Alternative View

#ichthyology #marine-biology #petromyzontidae #agnatha

## Executive Summary

This 1980 commentary by Reeve M. Bailey, presented as part of the Sea Lamprey International Symposium (SLIS), critically evaluates the incongruent classification schemes for [[Lampreys]] proposed by two leading contemporary specialists, [[I. C. Potter]] and [[V. D. Vladykov]]. Bailey, writing as a representative of the American Fisheries Society Names Committee, argues that existing taxonomies are "unnecessarily split," creating confusion for non-specialists. He proposes a consolidated, more conservative classification: all modern lampreys placed in a **single family**, divided into **three subfamilies** and **six genera**, with most species retained within a broadly defined genus *Lampetra* containing **six subgenera**. The paper also resolves a long-standing orthographic dispute over the family name stem (**Petromyzontidae** vs. **Petromyzonidae**), concluding on classical Greek grammatical grounds that "Petromyzontidae" is correct. A key biological finding is that intraspecific variation in dental characters (previously used as key diagnostic/generic traits) is far greater than previously recognized, undermining several genus-level distinctions based on tooth morphology.

## Background & Description

- **Supraoral lamina**: Uniquely in *Lampetra* (sensu lato), this is a broad, transverse or crescent-shaped plate bearing an enlarged cusp at each end, with variable development of smaller intermediate cusps. This configuration is interpreted as a **derived character state** uniting the genus.
- **Posterior circumoral tooth series ("posterials")**: Characteristically complete in the [[Petromyzontini]] tribe (*Ichthyomyzon*, *Petromyzon*, *Caspiomyzon*), in *Entosphenus*, *Tetrapleurodon*, and *Eudontomyzon*; **invariably absent** in *Lampetra* (sensu stricto). However, this series shows **variable development** in *Lethenteron* and *Okkelbergia*, undermining its reliability as a genus-diagnostic trait.
  - In *Lampetra zanandreai* (Italy), the posterior circumoral series was present in only 74 of 180 individuals studied by Zanandrea (1958), ranging from fully developed to broken arcs of 3-4 teeth.
  - Typical lateral circumoral cusp formula in this species: **2-2-2** (as in *Lethenteron*), though Vladykov reported 2-3-2 (typical of *Lampetra* s.s.).
- **Villiform teeth**: Numerous, sharply pointed teeth on anterior, lateral, and sometimes posterior oral disc fields — shared by *Tetrapleurodon*, *Eudontomyzon*, and (in minute form) *Okkelbergia*, but Bailey argues this may reflect **shared primitive states** rather than close phylogenetic relationship, since it also occurs across Southern Hemisphere lampreys and the Petromyzontini.
- **Lampetra (Okkelbergia) aepyptera**: Exhibits extreme dental reduction — teeth few, small, poorly cornified, often invisible without alizarin staining. Posterior circumoral series may be entirely absent, partial, or complete. Marginal teeth irregularly arranged, sometimes forming multiple rows. Has a reduced velar tentacle count (**3**).
- ***Entosphenus tridentatus*** characters historically considered diagnostic (strong median cusp on supraoral plate, 11–16 velar tentacles, myomere count 62–74) have been shown, following description of five new congeners, to grade into states with **no median cusp**, as few as **3 velar tentacles**, and myomere counts as low as **53**.
- **Myomere counts**: Suggested as a potentially unreliable species-diagnostic trait if southern populations exhibiting reduced counts prove to be ecophenotypes rather than fixed genetic differences.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Modern lampreys (Recent Petromyzontiformes) comprise a small monophyletic group of roughly **three dozen species**, distributed across the [[Holarctic]] (Northern Hemisphere: subfamily Petromyzontinae) and the Southern Hemisphere (subfamilies **Geotriinae** — one species, and **Mordaciinae** — three species).
- **Life-history modes** referenced include both **anadromous** and **freshwater-resident (nonparasitic)** forms:
  - *Entosphenus* species: anadromous or freshwater.
  - *Tetrapleurodon*: confined to the **Lerma River basin, Mexico** — interpreted as an isolated southern geographic relict derived from a formerly anadromous ancestor shared with *Entosphenus*, both of **North Pacific distribution**.
  - *Eudontomyzon*: Eurasian distribution, offering no biogeographic support for close relation to *Tetrapleurodon* despite shared dental features.
  - *Lampetra (Okkelbergia) aepyptera*: small, nonparasitic species native to eastern United States.
  - *Lampetra zanandreai*: nonparasitic species from northern Italy.
- The paper implicitly touches on the ecology of **paired species** (parasitic/nonparasitic species pairs), a major theme of the broader symposium (cf. [[Potter 1980]]).

## Key Findings

- **Proposed higher classification**: One family (**Petromyzontidae**), three subfamilies (**Petromyzontinae**, **Geotriinae**, **Mordaciinae**), reflecting Potter and Strahan's (1968) original scheme rather than the family-level elevation proposed by Hubbs and Potter (1971) or the eight-genus, three-subfamily scheme of Vladykov and Kott (1979).
- **Tribes within Petromyzontinae**: Two tribes proposed — **Petromyzontini** (*Ichthyomyzon*, *Petromyzon*, *Caspiomyzon*) and **Lampetrini** (*Lampetra*, broadly construed).
- **Genus-level recommendation**: Most Holarctic lamprey diversity within Lampetrini should be unified under a single genus, ***Lampetra***, containing **six subgenera**:
  1. *Lampetra* (sensu stricto)
  2. *Entosphenus* (reduced from generic to subgeneric rank, contra Vladykov and Kott)
  3. *Tetrapleurodon*
  4. *Lethenteron*
  5. *Eudontomyzon*
  6. *Okkelbergia*
- **Nomenclatural stem ruling**: Following consultation with classicist Howard D. Cameron, the correct Greek-derived stem for names based on *Petromyzon* is **Petromyzont-** (genitive singular stem of the present participle of *myzō*, "to suck"), validating **Petromyzontidae** over T. Gill's (1895) "Petromyzonidae," a conclusion independently reached by Steyskal (1980).
- **Taxonomic synonymies proposed**:
  - *Lethenteron meridionale* (Vladykov, Kott, and Pharand-Coad 1975) = synonym of ***Lampetra aepyptera***.
  - *Petromyzon lamottenii* Lesueur recommended for suppression as unidentifiable (per Bailey and Rohde, in prep.), with ***Lampetra appendix*** (DeKay) to be adopted as the valid name for the American brook lamprey.
- **Critique of classificatory philosophy**: Vladykov and Kott's (1979) scheme is characterized as an unnecessarily "splitter's classification" that elevates subgeneric-level distinctions to full genus/subfamily rank without employing intermediate (subgeneric) ranks.
- **Character reliability**: Both major dental characters used across competing classifications — inner disc teeth (overemphasized by Hubbs and Potter) and posterior circumoral closure/"posterials" (overemphasized by Vladykov and Kott) — are shown to be more variable intraspecifically than previously recognized, weakening their diagnostic value at the genus level.
- **Close relationship affirmed**: *Entosphenus* and *Tetrapleurodon* are judged so morphologically similar and phyletically close (shared four lateral circumorals, similar transverse lingual lamina, median supraoral cusp in parasitic forms, shared North Pacific anadromous ancestry) that even subgeneric separation is considered questionable.
