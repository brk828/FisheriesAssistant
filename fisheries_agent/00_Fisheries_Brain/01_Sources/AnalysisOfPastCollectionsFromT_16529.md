# Historic Collections of Young Razorback Sucker and Humpback Chub from the Green River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 1993 annual project report, submitted under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project Number 18-6), describes a desk-based study that re-examines existing preserved collections of young fishes from the [[Green River]] to determine whether late larvae and early juveniles of [[Razorback Sucker]] or [[Humpback Chub]] are present. The work is led by Robert T. Muth at the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]], [[Colorado State University]]. The study aims to link historic captures to date, time, location, and seasonal environmental conditions (flow, temperature, habitat) to clarify past reproductive and recruitment success and environmental requirements of these endangered fishes. Reducing field sampling of endangered species is a stated recovery priority, and this study uses existing material to do so.

As of the report, the study was not complete. Work began in late July 1993 after contracting delays. About 500 sucker and 300 chub specimens had been pulled from collections, and meristic (fin-ray) data were being compiled. Completion is planned for March 1994. The FY 1993 budget was $5,000, of which $2,000 was spent.

## Background & Description

The study addresses two closely related endangered fishes of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Young razorback suckers and humpback chubs are difficult to separate from related species at early life stages. Before recently developed [[Diagnostic Characters]] were available at the Larval Fish Laboratory, field-collected and laboratory-processed young potential razorback or humpback specimens were recorded only as "tentative," "possible," or left at the genus level (*Gila*).

The project objectives were:

1. Identify late larvae and early juveniles of razorback sucker or humpback chub in past collections housed mainly at the Larval Fish Laboratory, using the new diagnostic characters.
2. If young fish of either species are identified, relate those collections to sampling and environmental conditions at the time of capture.

The end product is a report to be synthesized with other studies in the 5-Year [[Flaming Gorge Flow Investigations]] final report.

The project is linked to the Recovery Program's Recovery Implementation Program (RIPRAP) actions for instream flows, habitat management, and research. Relevant actions cover flow recommendations for the Green River above and below the [[Duchesne River]], operation of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] for summer, fall, winter, and spring flows, habitat restoration at [[Old Charlie Wash]], and identification of additional razorback sucker spawning areas in the lower Green River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most of the specimens examined were collected from the middle and lower Green River in Utah during summer and fall of 1986 to 1992. The study links these captures to annual and seasonal conditions such as flow regime, water temperature, and habitat, which the report identifies as key to understanding recruitment in the Green River system. The Flaming Gorge flow actions are the main managed factor in this context. The report states the project supports several hypotheses in the overall Flaming Gorge Research Program, especially those on reproduction and recruitment.

Co-occurring species in the sample pool include several sucker and chub taxa that can be confused with the target species at early life stages, notably [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[White Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Roundtail Chub]].

## Key Findings

**Study status and progress**
- The study was not yet complete at the time of reporting. Work started in late July 1993 because contract finalization was delayed, which also delayed hiring of technicians.
- Completion is planned for March 1994.

**Specimens and data assembled**
- Approximately 500 sucker specimens and 300 chub specimens were pulled from existing collections. All are late larvae and early juveniles.
- Most come from middle and lower Green River reaches in Utah, collected summer to fall 1986–1992.
- Meristic data, mainly [[Fin-Ray Counts]], were compiled. Analysis was ongoing at the time of reporting.
- No specimens had yet been confirmed as razorback sucker or humpback chub. Only after meristic screening would candidates be selected for further work.

**Planned analytical methods**
- Sucker specimens will undergo [[Soft-Tissue Clearing And Skeletal Staining]] to examine the size and shape of [[Interneural Bones]] and the [[Frontoparietal Fontanelle]]. These features separate young razorback, flannelmouth, bluehead, and white suckers, following Snyder and Muth (1990).
- Chub specimens will undergo [[Morphometric Analysis]] using criteria from Muth (1990) for separating young humpback chub, bonytail, and roundtail chub.
- If young razorback suckers or humpback chubs are identified, capture information will be requested from the original collectors and related to environmental conditions in the year or season of capture.
