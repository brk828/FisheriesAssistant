# Bibliography of Fishes of the United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-faunistics

## Executive Summary

This document is a state-by-state bibliography of ichthyological literature for the United States, produced by the [[Smithsonian Institution]]'s [[Division of Fishes]] at the [[National Museum of Natural History]] in Washington, D.C. The cover page is signed by Paul C. Marsh and states that it was compiled with the assistance of the Division's curators. It was prepared to answer frequent requests for literature on regional fish faunas. Entries cover general works, individual states, and a closing list of additions and corrections. Because the bibliography is a reference list rather than a research study, it reports no new field data, sample sizes, or statistics.

## Background & Description

The bibliography is arranged by a General heading followed by individual U.S. state headings, roughly in alphabetical order from Alabama to Wyoming. Each entry gives author, year, title, publisher or series, and page or figure counts. Entries range from standard faunal keys and checklists to popular field guides and museum publications.

The compilers note several limitations:

- **Coverage is uneven.** Some states have large lists of works, while others have few. Iowa, Vermont, and West Virginia do not appear as headings.
- **Regional works are cross-listed.** Some states have no direct treatments, so the compilers suggest consulting publications for nearby areas.
- **Nomenclature changes.** Taxonomic names in recent publications may differ from older works. The compilers recommend a current scientific source for names.
- **Availability.** Many listed publications are out of print. Most can be obtained through [[Inter-Library Loan]] or local bookstores.

Key contributors cited across the General and regional sections include [[Reeve M. Bailey]], [[John Fitch]], [[Ernest Lachner]], [[Carl L. Hubbs]], [[Karl F. Lagler]], [[Samuel Eddy]], [[Samuel F. Hildebrand]], and [[William C. Schroeder]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography spans the full range of U.S. aquatic habitats, including:

- **Freshwater systems:** river basins (for example, the Red, Rainy, and Missouri rivers), Great Lakes waters, Appalachian drainages, lakes, and springs. Several entries focus on [[Freshwater Fishes]] of particular watersheds.
- **Marine and coastal systems:** the Atlantic coast from Labrador to Texas, the Gulf of Mexico, the Pacific coast, Puget Sound, Bodega-Tamales Bay, and tidepool zones. Entries also cover [[Marine Fishes]] of deep water, offshore areas, and estuaries such as the Delaware estuaries.
- **Island and remote regions:** Hawaiian waters, the Hawaiian Islands, and Alaskan and Arctic fauna, including the Western Arctic and Eastern Arctic Siberia.

The sources include regional faunal surveys, distributional studies, and checklists. Together they document how fish communities vary across geography, habitat type, and drainage. The bibliography does not report environmental measurements such as water chemistry or habitat parameters.

## Key Findings

**Scope and structure**

- The bibliography covers the United States state by state, with a General section for national or multi-region works.
- It includes entries from the late 1800s (for example, Jordan and Evermann, 1896, on the fishes of North and Middle America) through the late 1970s and early 1980s.
- The compilers list works on freshwater, marine, and deep-water fishes, plus national and regional identification keys.

**Notable general and multi-state works (as listed)**

- [[Bailey]] and colleagues (1970), a list of fishes of the United States and Canada (3rd edition).
- Breder (1929), a field book of marine fishes of the Atlantic coast from Labrador to Texas.
- Hubbs and Lagler (1947 and 1964), on the fishes of the Great Lakes region.
- Jordan and Evermann (1896), on the fishes of North and Middle America.
- Miller and Robison (1973), on the fishes of Oklahoma.
- A multi-part Fishes of the Western North Atlantic series from Yale University (1948 onward).

**Regional and state highlights (examples)**

- **Alabama:** Boschung (1957) on the fishes of Mobile Bay and the Gulf Coast.
- **Alaska:** multiple checklists and keys, including Morrow (1980) and Walters (1955).
- **California:** the largest cluster of marine and freshwater works, including Barnhart (1936, marine fishes of Southern California) and Fitch and Lavenberg (1968, 1971, 1975) on deep-water, food and game, and tidepool species.
- **Florida:** Briggs (1958), Gilbert, and Springer (1969) on the Tampa Bay area.
- **Illinois:** Forbes and Richardson (1908) on the fishes of Illinois, with distribution maps.
- **Texas:** Hoese and Moore (1977) on the Gulf of Mexico, and Knapp (1953) on the freshwater fishes of Texas.

**Addenda and corrections**

The final pages list additions and corrections, including:

- A revised 3rd edition of Eddy (1979), *How to Know the Freshwater Fishes*, which supersedes the 1977 edition.
- New state works for Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Maryland, Nevada, New York, North Carolina, Texas, Virginia, and Washington.
- Corrections to several author names (for example, Motle to Moyle, La River to La Rivers, Somon to Simon).

**Practical use**

The bibliography is meant as a starting point for regional fish studies. Users should check current names and the availability of out-of-print works before relying on it.
