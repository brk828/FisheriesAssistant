# DNA Variation in Desert Pupfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, published in the [[Desert Fishes Council]] symposium volume (1998 symposium, published November 15, 1999), assesses mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) variation in [[Desert Pupfish]] across 11 wild populations and one captive stock held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]]. The authors conclude that the Quitobaquito/Río Sonoyta populations represent a separate species, [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon eremus*), distinct from the more widespread *[[Cyprinodon macularius]]* of the Salton Sea and Colorado River delta region. The results are used to formulate genetic management recommendations.

The first page also contains a fragment from an earlier, unrelated paper on [[Rio Pecos]] pupfish. It reports that storm events displaced and lost a large share of reproductive effort downstream toward [[Brantley Dam]] and other unfavorable habitats, compared with summer storms, and that this was an anthropogenic effect.

## Background & Description

The study addresses the taxonomic status of pupfish from the Quitobaquito Springs and Río Sonoyta area relative to desert pupfish from the Salton Sea and lower Colorado River delta. Genetic structure was examined to inform conservation and management.

Authors and affiliations:
- R. A. Van Den Busschen (Zoology Department, Oklahoma State University, Stillwater, OK)
- C. O. Minckley (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Parker, AZ)
- T. Marsh (Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ; presenter, marked with an asterisk in the source)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study covers desert aquatic habitats in the [[Sonoran Desert]] and [[Colorado River Delta]] region. The Quitobaquito and Sonoyta populations occupy spring-fed systems, while the other populations are associated with the [[Salton Sea]] and the lower Colorado River delta. The presence of a captive stock at a federal hatchery reflects active management of these fishes.

Methods used:
- [[Polymerase Chain Reaction]] followed by single-strand conformation polymorphism analysis (PCR-SSCP) targeting the mitochondrial ND2 gene
- Sequencing of representative haplotypes for the complete ND2 gene and part of the mtDNA D-loop
- Pairwise comparisons and analysis of among-region genetic variance

## Key Findings

- **Sample size and haplotypes:** Of 259 specimens examined (OCR-garbled in the English abstract, confirmed in the Spanish abstract), 13 mtDNA haplotypes were detected across 11 wild populations and one captive population.
- **Sequence length:** The complete ND2 gene was sequenced for representative haplotypes. The English abstract gives "7,W bases" (OCR error); the Spanish abstract gives 1,047 bases, which is used here. The D-loop was also sequenced.
- **Phylogenetic structure:** Two reciprocally monophyletic groups were found:
  1. Samples from the Quitobaquito/Río Sonoyta area.
  2. Samples from three localities in the Salton Sea area and the lower Colorado River delta.
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** The authors argue that Quitobaquito/Río Sonoyta populations constitute a separate species, *C. eremus* (Miller and Fuiman), distinct from *C. macularius* (Baird and Girard) of the Salton Sea/Colorado River delta region.
- **Within-group structure:**
  - No genetic difference was detected between the *C. eremus* samples from Quitobaquito Springs and Río Sonoyta.
  - No differences were found in pairwise tests among *C. macularius* samples within the Salton Sea area or within the lower Colorado River delta.
- **Among-region variance:** Significant but weak genetic differentiation was found in *C. macularius* between the Salton Sea and the lower Colorado River delta. The same haplotype predominated in both regions, at approximately 90–95% (the value is OCR-ambiguous in both abstracts).
- **Management implications:** The findings are used to formulate genetic management guidelines for the species, particularly for maintaining the distinct Quitobaquito lineage.
