# Fish Population Monitoring of Canals and Rivers Connected to the Central Arizona Project: Sample Year 2015

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of linked records for the 2015 sample year (SY 2015) of a long-term fish monitoring program in the [[Gila River Basin]], Arizona. It includes a draft final report by [[Marsh & Associates]] for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the request for proposal and statement of work, the contractor's proposal and budget, a purchase order, station access and route tables, a field memo for pumping plant sampling, and database code tables. The monitoring tracks non-native fish that may move from the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) canal system into the Gila basin. A total of 22 fish taxa were captured, including 3 native species. Native fishes made up 23% of individuals captured at Gila basin stations. The draft report is largely a data summary and does not offer a formal conclusion beyond its recommendations.

## Background & Description

The [[Central Arizona Project]] has delivered Colorado River water from Lake Havasu since 1993. Several [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] biological opinions required a baseline and long-term monitoring program. The goal is to detect non-native fish in the CAP aqueduct and selected rivers and canals. Monitoring is now done once every five years, per an August 11, 2011 memorandum from Reclamation to FWS. Native fishes in these waters are largely imperiled and some are listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], which raises the stakes for detecting non-natives.

The contractor's statement of work (January 6, 2015) and performance work statement set out the tasks:

- Monitor the CAP, [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) South and Arizona canals, the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG), the Gila, Salt and San Pedro rivers, and the electrical fish barriers.
- Obtain collecting permits from the Arizona Game and Fish Department and FWS.
- Use fixed stream stations, with quantitative 200 m electrofishing samples and qualitative searches of unusual habitats.
- Submit voucher specimens to the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]].
- Deliver the database by May 1, 2016 and the draft report by March 1, 2016, with the final report by May 1, 2016.

The proposal was submitted by [[Marsh & Associates]] (Paul C. Marsh and Brian R. Kesner) on April 16, 2015. The quoted cost was $69,939 (about $62,420 GSA schedule labor and $7,513 open-market travel and operations). The purchase order was issued on April 30, 2015 under BPA Call No. R15PB00198. The contract period ended May 1, 2016.

The report describes field methods, including [[Electrofishing]] at fixed stations, seines and dip nets in canals during flow outages (12–24 hours after headgate closure), and boat electrofishing when flows could not be stopped. Habitat types were classified as pool, riffle, run, or isolated pool.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Gila basin waters sampled include:

- **San Pedro River:** 7 species; the native desert sucker and longfin dace dominated the catch.
- **Gila River:** 6 species, all non-native. No native species were found in 2015 apart from one desert sucker captured in 2007.
- **[[CAP Canal]]:** 11 taxa, mostly non-native, with 5 to 11 species per canal type across streams.
- **SRP South Canal (SRPs):** 12 species, 2 native.
- **SRP North (Arizona) Canal (SRPn):** 13 species, 2 native.
- **FCG Canal:** 5 species, all non-native.

The canals are artificial reaches. Electrical fish barriers separate "above" and "below" sections, and these barriers strongly affect community structure. Above the SRP barriers, native sonora sucker and desert sucker were present, but non-native fishes dominated the catch below the barrier. Non-native species found in these waters include [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Blue Tilapia]] and [[Grass Carp]]. The non-native bullfrog and northern crayfish were also recorded.

Several cited comparisons are not made in this report, so trends across years are not assessed here.

## Key Findings

- **Taxa richness:** 22 fish taxa were captured in SY 2015 (excluding undetermined sunfish). Two new taxa were detected. Of these, 3 were native (about 14%) and 19 were non-native.
- **Native proportion:** Native fishes made up 23% of individuals captured at Gila basin stations. Their share of each station's catch ranged from 0% (Gila River, CAP canal, FCG canal) to 92% (San Pedro River, 91.7% of the catch).
- **San Pedro River:** 324 fish were captured. Longfin dace and desert sucker together made up 90% of the catch. Non-natives were 10%, with mosquitofish the most common non-native.
- **Gila River:** 101 fish were captured, all non-native. Red shiner was the most abundant species (35%), followed by channel catfish (32%).
- **CAP canal:** 198 fish were captured. Common carp was the most abundant (about 19%), followed by channel catfish and several sunfish species. Largemouth bass dominated the lower reach.
- **SRPs canal:** 302 fish were captured, 77% native by count in the statement. Non-native channel catfish dominated above the barrier (51%). Red shiner made up 93% of the 135 fish below the barrier.
- **SRPn canal:** Above the barrier, channel catfish were most abundant (63% of 277 fish). Below the barrier, grass carp dominated (56% of 57 fish).
- **FCG canal:** 5 species were recorded, with channel catfish most abundant above the electrical barrier (54%). Below the barrier, threadfin shad made up 10% after non-native channel catfish (80%).
- **Recommendations:** Begin the process of obtaining access permissions early in the sample year. The lowest Gila River station (Box Canyon / 2-4-3) was relocated to just upstream of [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]] to avoid access problems. The new site is named "Ashurst-Hayden Dam."
- **Pumping plant sampling (memo, October 2015):** Reclamation planned sampling at Salt-Gila, Brady, Red Rock and San Xavier pumping plants during October 19–23, 2015, using set nets and boat electrofishing.
