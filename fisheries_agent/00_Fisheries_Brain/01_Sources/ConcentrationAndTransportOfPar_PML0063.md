# Concentration and Transport of Particulate Organic Matter Below Glen Canyon Dam, Colorado River, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Ted R. Angradi]] (USDA Forest Service) and [[Dennis M. Kubly]] (Arizona Game and Fish Department) examines the concentration and transport of particulate organic matter (POM) in a 25 km tailwater reach below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] on the [[Colorado River]]. Coarse POM (CPOM) tracked hydropower-driven flow fluctuations, while fine POM (FPOM) was weakly related to flow and largely inherited from [[Lake Powell]] releases. Net export of POM from the reach exceeded inputs mainly in spring, and autochthonous (in-river) contributions did not raise tailwater POM much above reservoir inputs.

## Background & Description

Flow fluctuations below hydroelectric dams can alter the concentration and transport of suspended particles that feed [[Trout]] and other consumers, and may affect export of organic matter downstream into [[Grand Canyon]]. The authors set out to determine how water releases from Glen Canyon Dam affect POM concentration, and to examine seasonal and longitudinal patterns and the reach's sources and sinks.

Study sites were penstock samples within the dam and sites at [[Lee's Ferry]], 25 km downriver. Lake Powell is a long (300 km), deep (mean depth 51 m), warm-monomictic reservoir whose hypolimnial outflow is perennially cold (7–11 °C), chemically stable and clear. The tailwater is dominated by the filamentous green alga [[Cladophora glomerata]] and is bordered by riparian tamarisk (*Tamarix ramosissima*) and willow. Sampling ran from September 1990 to December 1991 (15 sample dates), with a second phase in 1992 focusing on February–June releases.

Methods included filtration of pre-filtered (750 µm) river water onto glass fiber filters (0.7 µm pore size), drying, ashing at 550 °C, and reporting results as mg AFDM L⁻¹ (ash-free dry mass). CPOM (>750 µm) was collected with a metered high-speed net behind a boat. Statistics included ANOVA, regression of flow relationships and log-log rating plots.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regime:** Releases ranged from steady flows of about 225 m³/s to fluctuating hydropower flows, with daily hydrographs strongly shaping particle inputs.
- **Source of POM:** Reservoir-derived FPOM dominates downstream; local algal and debris sources had little effect except during peak flushing.
- **Benthic storage:** Backwaters act as temporary POM stores that are flushed by elevated peak flows.
- **Downstream relevance:** Coarse POM is likely too coarse for most consumers and is probably assimilated only after fragmentation into finer particles; its ecological significance to downstream communities is uncertain.
- **Comparison:** Tailwater POM concentrations were lower than those reported for reaches below [[Lake Mead]] and [[Lake Havasu]] (0.80 and 0.89 mg L⁻¹), possibly reflecting differences in downriver nutrient levels.

## Key Findings

- **Coarse POM and flow:** CPOM concentration was positively related to flow during fluctuating (hydropower) releases, with no diel variation during steady flows. At 1600 h, mean CPOM during fluctuating flows (0.03 ± 0.009 mg L⁻¹) was about three times higher than at 0400 h (0.01 ± 0.003 mg L⁻¹; t-test, p < 0.01). CPOM was predominantly *Cladophora* debris, most likely sloughed from the permanently inundated channel rather than the zone of daily fluctuation.
- **Fine POM and flow:** FPOM was only weakly related to flow, and penstock FPOM was not related to discharge. Reservoir inputs damped diel and seasonal variation in tailwater FPOM.
- **Mean concentrations:** Mean FPOM at 25 km below the dam (0.58 ± 0.01 mg L⁻¹) was only slightly higher than in penstocks (0.55 ± 0.02 mg L⁻¹), and FPOM made up more than 96% of total POM. Total POM at Lee's Ferry averaged 0.6 ± 0.04 mg L⁻¹.
- **Seasonal patterns:** CPOM concentration varied significantly among sample dates (F = 2.88, p < 0.01), peaking in spring and fall and lowest in winter. FPOM at Lee's Ferry exceeded penstock values only in spring.
- **Net export:** Total POM export substantially exceeded imports only in spring. CPOM export was net but represented only 3–4% of total POM at Lee's Ferry.
- **Backwater flushing:** On June 2 (1992), FPOM concentration rose to roughly twice ambient at the sampling site, and up to about 1.5 times ambient 15–18 km below the dam. The effect followed peak flows that flushed a large (ca. 1.5 ha) backwater, and was short-lived with sustained high flows removing stored POM.
- **Longitudinal increase:** FPOM increased gradually and continuously downriver, with a slight initial decline in some months (2–6 km) suggesting a shift from reservoir-derived to reach-derived particles.
- **Autochthonous contribution:** Lotic algal and debris inputs did not substantially raise tailwater POM above reservoir inputs. Floods, dewatering episodes and seasonal sloughing account for most export of autochthonous POM.
- **Conclusion:** The reach was a net exporter of POM, mostly reservoir-derived fine particles modified by backwater flushing and flow-driven processes.
