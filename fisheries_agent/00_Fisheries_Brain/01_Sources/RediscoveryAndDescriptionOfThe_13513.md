# Rediscovery and Description of the Ictalurid Catfish, Noturus flavipinnis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #catfish #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This paper documents the rediscovery of [[Noturus flavipinnis]] (yellowfin madtom), an [[Ictaluridae]] catfish previously known only from 13 faded specimens collected between 1884 and 1893 from three widely separated localities in the [[Tennessee River]] drainage. The species was feared extinct until Jenkins collected one adult from [[Copper Creek]], Virginia in May 1969. Subsequent night collecting revealed the species to be fairly common in lower Copper Creek, with 28 specimens collected during two night collections compared to only 2 specimens from 26 daylight collections. The authors provide detailed morphological descriptions, meristic data, color patterns in life and preservation, and ecological observations. The species is characterized as rare and endangered, having likely disappeared from much of its former range.

## Background & Description

[[Noturus flavipinnis]] was originally described by Taylor (1969) from specimens collected between 1884 and 1893 from only three widely separated localities in the [[Tennessee River]] drainage of Georgia, Tennessee, and Virginia. The lack of recent collections led to suggestions that the species might be extinct.

**Morphological Characteristics:**
- Dorsal rays: I,6 (44 specimens)
- Anal rays: 14-16 (mean 14.8)
- Total caudal rays: 54-63 (mean 58.1)
- Vertebrae (excluding fused anterior vertebrae): 33-35 (mean 34.4)
- Pelvic rays: i,7-8 (mean i,8.0)
- Pectoral rays: I,7-9 (mean I,7.8)
- Preoperculo-mandibular pores: 9-12 (mean 10.8)
- One epural and six hypurals present
- Caudal fin truncate with rounded corners
- Mouth included with lower jaw inferior

**Color Pattern in Preservation:**
- Four prominent dorsal saddles or bars on moderately light to darkly pigmented brownish tan body
- Dark brown saddle beneath dorsal fin with pair of light spots anterior to dorsal fin (similar to [[Noturus stigmosus]] and [[Noturus munitus]])
- Rectangular saddle between dorsal and adipose fins
- Dark brown to black adipose bar extending to virtual margin of adipose fin
- Dark brown obovate patch on posterior caudal peduncle
- Caudal fin yellowish to light brown basally with broad crescentic brown band near margin
- Dark brown to blackish spot near middle of anal fin base (similar to [[Noturus flavater]])
- Light spot (size of eye) behind eye
- Undersurface unpigmented except for scattered chromatophores

**Life Colors (observed at night, October 1969):**
- Axial streak pinkish orange
- Adipose bar and basicaudal bar jet black
- Two anterior saddles dark gray to dark grayish black
- Pelvic fins whitish
- Clear area of adipose fin medium lemon yellow
- Basal half of caudal fin medium yellow
- Tips and filaments of dorsal and pectoral spines bright lemon yellow
- Cheek yellow

Young specimens show characteristic pigmentation of older non-breeding specimens but pattern may be incompletely developed. Greatest color contrast exists between young and adult specimens collected at night. Specimens collected in daylight are more uniformly pigmented with less contrast.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Characteristics of [[Copper Creek]]:**
- Flows through narrow U-shaped valley
- Generally short, swift riffles and moderate to moderately long shallow pools (normally ≤4 feet deep, mostly <3 feet)
- Stream width up to ~60 feet; riffles 30-60 feet wide
- White water without evidence of harmful waste or pollution
- Sparse higher vegetation providing limited cover
- Bottom mostly gravel and rubble with some boulders and bedrock
- Small areas of fine sand present but no shifting sand
- Pools with slow to still current
- Thin layer of fine silt covers bottom of some pools

**Microhabitat Preferences:**
[[Noturus flavipinnis]] appears characteristically a species of pools and slow running water. None was taken from riffles. Adults and young were often taken from shallow areas, among felled trees, or with detritus. Jordan (1890) found the species "Not rare in the weeds..." but authors found very little vegetation in [[Copper Creek]] and conclude it does not furnish an effective hiding place.

**Associated Fauna:**
44 fish species collected from lower [[Copper Creek]] locality. Significant new records for upper [[Clinch River]] system in Virginia include:
- [[Ichthyomyzon bdellium]]
- [[Notropis lirus]]
- [[Ictalurus melas]] (possibly introduced)
- [[Noturus eleutherus]] (14 specimens; large individuals from swift gravel and rubble riffles; young mainly from riffles)
- [[Noturus flavus]] (one specimen from large rubble in swift riffle)
- Multiple [[Etheostoma]] species (E. camurum, E. maculatum, E. stigmaeum, E. tippecanoe, E. (Catonotus) sp.)
- Multiple [[Percina]] species (P. aurantiaca, P. burtoni, P. caprodes, P. evides, P. macrocephala, P. sciera)

**Collection Success and Behavior:**
Night collecting dramatically more successful than daylight collecting. Only 2 specimens taken in approximately 26 daylight collections from lower [[Copper Creek]] (1965-1969), compared to 28 specimens in two night collections (10-11 October 1969). This pattern suggests nocturnal activity or emergence from hiding places at night.

**Conservation Status:**
Authors regard [[Noturus flavipinnis]] as a rare and endangered species that has probably disappeared from much of its former range. Past occurrence in [[Clinch River]] based on 1893 capture. A devastating alkaline waste kill during June 1967 beginning at Carbo, Russell County, Virginia may have wiped out any existing populations in the main stream down to the Virginia-Tennessee state line.

## Key Findings

**Rediscovery and Distribution:**
- Species rediscovered in May 1969 after being feared extinct
- New specimens collected from [[Copper Creek]], Virginia (tributary of [[Clinch River]], upper [[Tennessee River]] drainage) and [[Powell River]], Tennessee
- Total material studied: 13 original specimens plus 31 newly collected specimens
- Known localities remain limited to upper [[Tennessee River]] drainage

**Meristic and Morphological Data:**
- No significant geographic variation detected in meristic characteristics
- Hypurals remain separate in small specimens; in specimens >60 mm standard length, hypurals 2 and 3 consistently fused, hypurals 4 and 5 joined or tending to join
- Upper simple caudal rays: 22-27 (modal 24, mean 23.7)
- Upper branched rays: 7-8, exceptionally 11 (modal 8, mean 7.7)
- Lower branched rays: 9-13 (modal 11, mean 10.8)
- Total branched rays: 16-22 (mean 18.5)
- Lower simple rays: 13-18 (modal 15, mean 15.5)

**Taxonomic Relationships:**
Relationship appears closest to [[Noturus miurus]] and secondarily to [[Noturus flavater]]. Several features suggest members of the furiosus species group:
- Similarly colored dorsal fins lacking terminal black blotch but with variously developed medial brownish bands
- Two lightly pigmented spots in dorsal saddle anterior to dorsal spine
- Fairly truncate posterior margin of caudal fin rather than rounded to pointed
- Increased frequency of seven soft pectoral rays

General coloration, especially color patterns of caudal fin, caudal peduncle, and adipose fin, plus increased number of caudal rays point strongly to relationship with miurus species group.

**Ecological Observations:**
- Habitat specialist: pools and slow running water, not riffles
- Found in shallow areas among felled trees or with detritus
- Strongly nocturnal in activity or emergence behavior
- Night collecting 14 times more effective than daylight collecting (28 specimens in 2 night collections vs. 2 specimens in ~26 daylight collections)

**Conservation Concerns:**
- Rare and endangered species
- Likely disappeared from much of former range
- Alkaline waste kill in June 1967 may have eliminated populations from main [[Clinch River]] in Virginia
- Limited current distribution despite comparatively rich fish fauna in [[Copper Creek]] (44 species)

**Taxonomic Revision:**
Newly collected specimens show variation in adipose bar extension that disagrees with statements in Taylor's (1969) original key and description. In most specimens the black bar extends to margin or submargin of adipose fin, but several medium-sized specimens show various extensions into the fin.
