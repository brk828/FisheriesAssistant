# Population Estimates for Refugia Populations of Desert Pupfish on Imperial and Cibola National Wildlife Refuges

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared by Dr. C.O. Minckley of the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] in Parker, Arizona, gives the first census of two constructed refugia populations of the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*[[Cyprinodon Macularius Macularius]]*), an endangered fish. Mark-recapture sampling in October 2001 produced a population estimate of 668 fish at [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (CNWR). No estimate could be made at [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] (INWR) because no marked fish were recaptured. The document is the first of an annual monitoring program. A December 2001 cover memorandum, which accompanies the report, asks interested persons for comments.

## Background & Description

The desert pupfish was once widespread and abundant in southern Arizona. By the early 1950s, habitat destruction and interactions with exotic fishes had extirpated all natural populations in Arizona. Natural populations now persist near the [[Salton Sea]] in California and at several localities along the lower [[Colorado River]] in Mexico. The species was listed as Endangered in 1986, and the [[Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan]] was completed in 1992. Its recovery criteria call for securing, maintaining and replicating all extant populations, and re-establishing replicate populations in natural habitats within the probable historic range.

Two constructed refugia were studied:

- **CNWR refugium:** A concrete-lined pond of 10 m × 5 m beside refuge headquarters, enclosed by a fence, with a depth of 0.5–1 m. The bottom is river gravel supporting heavy algae growth, with rock piles and bulrush for cover. Unchlorinated water comes from the refuge water system. On 17 September 1999, 37 fish were transferred from the [[Phoenix Zoo]]. The stock originated from the [[El Doctor Marshes]] in northern Sonora, Mexico, and was collected by Arizona State University in the 1970s.
- **INWR refugium:** A plastic-lined pond of 5 m × 5 m about 100 m from refuge headquarters, with a depth of 0.1–1 m. It has gravel bottom, cattails around the perimeter and established desert vegetation. Unchlorinated water comes from the refuge water system. On 6 March 2000, 23 fish from CNWR stock were introduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling used unbaited minnow traps set in a 4 × 3 grid in each pond, weighted and aligned parallel to the pond sides, and fished from 0900 to 1200 hrs. Captured fish were held in shaded buckets with water replenished every 30 minutes to reduce stress. Water temperature was measured with a standard centigrade thermometer 0.1 m below the surface.

The population estimate used the [[Peterson-Lincoln Index]], N = MC/R, where M is the number marked and released in the first sample, C is the number captured in the second sample, and R is the number of recaptures. The method assumes a closed population. The authors judged the physical nature of the refugia to rule out immigration and emigration. Marking used anal fin clipping, with 69 fish clipped at INWR on 2 October 2001 and 176 at CNWR on 3 October 2001. Recapture sampling was done on 15–16 October 2001.

Water temperatures were 28°C at INWR and 25°C at CNWR. The authors note that pupfish can [[Aestivation]], which may make part of a population unavailable to trapping. For comparison, water at [[Quitobaquito Spring]] stays near 26°C year-round, and pupfish there remain active.

For comparison, [[Douglas et al. 2001]] estimated 9,556 fish annually for the entire natural [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] complex at [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]] (spring, channel, island and pond). Mean estimates for the channel and spring together were 591 fish, which is similar to the constructed-refugia estimate. The authors caution that the systems cannot yet be compared.

| INWR | 69 | 69 | 3 | 0 | None | 29.5 mm (22–37 mm) |

*Table values are as reported in the source. The text states that 4 fish were taken at INWR and 19 at CNWR on recapture sampling, and that five marked fish were recaptured at CNWR. The table's recapture and marked-recapture columns do not match this text, and the CNWR confidence interval is partly garbled in the scan.*

- **No estimate at INWR:** The authors attribute this to disturbance when the minnow traps were set, which may have made fish hide and become less catchable. They report no such disturbance at CNWR.
- **Size structure:** Mean length was smaller at INWR. The smallest fish at both sites were similar in size, but larger fish were captured at CNWR.
- **Condition:** Visual inspection indicated good condition at both sites.
- **Genetics:** The authors plan to mix wild fish from Mexico, if they can be obtained, with the refugia populations. The goal is to maintain the initial genetic mix of the founding stock.
- **Future monitoring:** Annual estimates will be made at each site, scheduled from mid-August into September, when water is warmer. Fish from both sites will be used for any future refugia.
- **Interpretation:** The authors state that extended discussion is inappropriate until more data are collected.
