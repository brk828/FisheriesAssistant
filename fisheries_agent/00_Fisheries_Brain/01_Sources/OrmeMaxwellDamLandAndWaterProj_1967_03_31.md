## Executive Summary

This 1967 completion report from the Arizona Game and Fish Department evaluates the fish and wildlife impacts of the proposed **[[Orme Dam]]** (also called Maxwell Dam), a Central Arizona Project feature planned for the confluence of the **[[Salt River]]** and **[[Verde River]]** near Phoenix, Arizona. The report synthesizes a Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife feasibility-grade analysis projecting impacts over a 100-year horizon. Construction of Orme Dam would inundate 15 miles of river and adversely affect a total of 27 miles of "fair quality" stream fishery, converting a lotic (riverine) system supporting largemouth bass, channel catfish, trout, yellow bullhead, and bluegill into a lentic warm-water reservoir fishery. While stream fishing opportunity would decline drastically (from ~27,000 to ~4,000 man-days annually), the created **[[Orme Reservoir]]** was projected to substantially increase total sport fishing opportunity (to ~329,000 man-days annually) via a warm-water fishery dominated initially by largemouth bass, crappie, channel catfish, and sunfish, though long-term risk of nongame fish (carp, suckers) dominance was anticipated. A nine-point mitigation program was jointly recommended by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and the Arizona Game and Fish Department.

## Background & Description

This report is a fisheries-impact and habitat-management document rather than a taxonomic or morphological study; no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data for individual species are presented. Species are referenced only by common name in the context of angling/sport-fishery composition:

- **Game/sport species:** Largemouth Bass (*[[Micropterus salmoides]]*), Channel Catfish (*[[Ictalurus punctatus]]*), Trout (unspecified, likely stocked *[[Salmonidae]]*), Yellow Bullhead (*[[Ameiurus natalis]]*), Bluegill (*[[Lepomis macrochirus]]*), Crappie (*[[Pomoxis]]* spp.), various unspecified sunfish (Centrarchidae).
- **Nongame species:** Carp (*[[Cyprinus carpio]]*) and native suckers (Catostomidae), noted to constitute **over 99% by weight** of the pre-project fish population biomass in the Salt and Verde Rivers — a striking indicator of nongame biomass dominance in this desert river system despite low angler interest in these species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pre-project habitat (riverine):** The Salt and Verde Rivers within the project area supported a "fair quality" warm-water/coolwater stream fishery over approximately 27 miles, providing habitat for a mixed assemblage of introduced game fish and abundant native/naturalized nongame fish (carp, suckers).
- **Post-project habitat (reservoir):** Orme Reservoir was projected to have a gross capacity of 1,647,000 acre-feet, covering 24,000 surface acres at full pool, with a minimum conservation-recreation pool of 43,000 acre-feet (2,300 surface acres) maintained roughly 50% of the time. This lentic warm-water reservoir environment favors a different fish community structure than the original stream — primarily centrarchids (largemouth bass, crappie, sunfish) and ictalurids (channel catfish) in early successional years.
- **Habitat degradation mechanisms identified:**
  - Reservoir water-level fluctuation between minimum and maximum pool would degrade fish habitat along 3 miles of the Salt River and 5 miles of the Verde River.
  - Silt deposition on inundated river bottoms would cause progressive deterioration of residual stream fish habitat.
  - Dam construction would eliminate 3.5 miles of stream fish habitat in the Salt River between the dam site and the Granite Reef diversion dam due to flow alteration.
- **Successional risk:** The report anticipates that in later years of reservoir operation, nongame fish (carp and suckers) could become dominant/problematic, necessitating an active rough-fish control program — a common trajectory in Southwestern U.S. reservoir fisheries where native/naturalized nongame species outcompete stocked sport fish over time.
- **Linked terrestrial ecosystem:** The same 24,000-acre project footprint also supported significant wildlife habitat (desert mule deer, javelina, Gambel quail, doves, cottontail rabbits, waterfowl), with the highest quality habitat located on the Verde River floodplain within the **[[Fort McDowell Indian Reservation]]**. This underscores the tightly coupled riparian-aquatic ecosystem the dam would fragment.

## Key Findings

- **Stream fishery loss:** Orme Dam/Reservoir would adversely affect ~27 miles of fair-quality stream fishery; 15 miles would be directly inundated, and 3.5 miles below the dam would lose stream habitat entirely due to flow diversion into the Orme–Granite Reef Canal.
- **Recreation trade-off:** Stream-fishing recreation would fall from an estimated 27,000 man-days/year (without project) to ~4,000 man-days/year (with project), but total sport fishing (stream + reservoir) was projected to rise dramatically to ~329,000 man-days/year due to the new reservoir fishery.
- **Commercial fishery potential:** Nongame fish in Orme Reservoir were projected to support a commercial harvest of up to 10,000 pounds annually.
- **Nongame fish dominance:** Carp and suckers already constituted >99% of pre-project fish biomass by weight in the Salt and Verde Rivers, despite negligible commercial/sport value, highlighting an existing imbalance the reservoir could exacerbate.
- **Management risk:** Long-term reservoir fish community stability was uncertain; a rough-fish (carp/sucker) control program was anticipated as necessary once nongame species proliferated.
- **Mitigation program (9 points)** recommended jointly by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and Arizona Game and Fish Department, including:
  1. Formal recognition of fish/wildlife conservation as a project purpose.
  2. $10,000 for pre-impoundment toxicant application for nongame fish eradication.
  3. $100,000 over five years for post-impoundment fishery management investigations.
  4. $120,000 for fisherman access, boat launching ramps, and sanitary facilities.
  5. $10,000 for two cleared, marked seining areas to support commercial fishing and reservoir fish population management.
  6. Cooperative development of a boating zonation/safety plan among the Bureau of Reclamation, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State Parks Board, **[[Fort McDowell Indian Community]]**, **[[Salt River Indian Tribe]]**, Bureau of Indian Affairs, and Forest Service.
  7. Vegetation (tree/brush) retention within the reservoir clearing plan to support fish habitat structure, except where clearing is required for safety, health, operations, or seining.
  8. Investigation of a dedicated wildlife management area as a project feature.
  9. Prompt marking of project land boundaries to define public hunting/fishing access areas.
- **Wildlife impact parallel:** 9,700 acres of excellent wildlife habitat would be inundated outright, with an additional 11,400 acres subject to unpredictable flood-pool inundation; upland game hunting was projected to fall from 4,000 to 300 man-days annually, with big game and nongame hunting becoming insignificant — no feasible mitigation for wildlife losses was identified at the time of the report.
- **Site uncertainty:** The analysis was based on only one of several candidate dam sites under consideration due to unresolved land acquisition and administrative issues, meaning final impacts remained provisional pending site selection.
