# Clam and Quagga Mussel Field Survey Notes, Bill Williams River

#malacology #freshwater-biology #bivalvia

## Executive Summary

This is a 2008 email from [[Chuck Minckley]] to [[Paul Marsh]] reporting a two-day search for freshwater clams along the lower [[Bill Williams River]]. The search, undertaken with [[Terry Myers]], was prompted by an earlier collection from the river delta in the early 1990s. The team found five live clams, which were preserved for genetic analysis, and the clams were identified as [[Utterbackia]] (formerly [[Anodontia Imbecillis]]). The email also reports very rare [[Quagga Mussel]] occurrences in the surveyed habitat.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence:** Email dated April 30, 2008, from Chuck Minckley, Ph.D., to Paul Marsh, subject "Clams - general information."
- **Trigger for survey:** Terry Myers's interest in clams and a specimen Minckley collected from the Bill Williams River Delta in the early 1990s.
- **Prior identification:** Minckley sent a specimen to a colleague named Herschler, who identified it as *Anodontia imbecillis* (as Minckley recalled). Myers later located this information and joined the search.
- **Taxonomic update:** The genus is now *Utterbackia*. The source states that the species and its relationships could be clarified through genetic work.
- **Survey method:** Sampling was done by dipping material with clam rakes. The team worked the delta, the river upstream to the end of surface water, and the return route to the lake.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Clam habitat:** The clams were found in the delta area and the upstream river reaches surveyed.
- **Quagga mussel habitat:** Quagga mussels were found on a large piece of gravel. They were absent from the Bill Williams River bridge pillars. Minckley also checked the bases of the cattails, where they were not found.
- **Population uncertainty:** Minckley wrote that the clams are present for anyone who looks for them, but he would not estimate the size of the population.

## Key Findings

- **Clam count:** Five individuals were found: 3 adults and 2 very small individuals. Several shell fragments and a few complete shells were also collected.
- **Genetic sampling:** Terry Myers preserved the live material for genetic analysis, intended to resolve the species identity and relationships.
- **Quagga mussel occurrence:** Quagga mussels were described as very rare in the surveyed habitat. Five dead and three live individuals were found on one large gravel piece.
- **Survey limitation:** Population size for the clams was not estimated.
