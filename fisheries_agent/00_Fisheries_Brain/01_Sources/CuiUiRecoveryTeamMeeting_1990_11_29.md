# Cui-ui Recovery Team Meeting (Reno, Nevada — November 29–30, 1990)

## Executive Summary
This document summarizes a [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] meeting convened under the authority of the **Truckee-Carson-Pyramid Lake Settlement Act (P.L. 101-618)**, which mandated expeditious development of a recovery plan for the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered lake sucker endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]] and the lower [[Truckee River]], Nevada. The meeting addressed ongoing population dynamics research, stochastic hydrologic-population modeling, genetic/hybridization analysis with [[Tahoe Sucker]], water allocation and conservation strategy for the [[Newlands Project]], critical habitat designation, fish passage facilities at [[Marble Bluff Dam]] and [[Numana Dam]], and a draft outline for the formal recovery plan and appendices.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide direct morphometric or anatomical descriptions of the cui-ui. However, taxonomic and genetic context is provided:
- The cui-ui (*Chasmistes cujus*) is a member of family [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), closely associated ecologically and genetically with the [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*), which co-occurs in the same watershed.
- [[Peter Brussard]]'s starch gel electrophoresis analysis of proteins concluded that **cui-ui have not hybridized with Tahoe suckers**, supporting the species' genetic distinctness.
- The 1969 year class (and younger) of cui-ui showed **very low genetic variation** within the population, raising concern about a possible reproductive "bottleneck" in either the 1950 or 1969 spawning runs. Analysis of the 1950 year class was recommended to determine whether historical variation was higher and to estimate the effective number of spawners during the suspected bottleneck event.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Cui-ui inhabit [[Pyramid Lake]], a terminal desert lake in the Lahontan Basin, and migrate up the [[Truckee River]] and through the river delta to spawn. Spawning migration is currently obstructed by delta sedimentation and structures at [[Marble Bluff Dam]] and [[Numana Dam]], necessitating fish passage facilities.
- **Life history stages of concern**: juvenile and adult stages are being tagged for population dynamics research; early life stage (larval/juvenile) survival rates remain poorly quantified and are a major source of uncertainty in population models.
- **Water dependency**: Cui-ui recovery is tightly linked to Truckee River flow volumes, timing of flow, and water quality, all of which are governed by upstream reservoir operations and irrigation diversions to the [[Newlands Project]] (Truckee and Carson Divisions).
- **Threats/ecosystem interactions**:
  - Diversion of Truckee River water via the **Truckee Canal** (average annual diversion ~120,000 acre-feet) reduces inflow to Pyramid Lake and access to spawning habitat.
  - Concerns were raised about whether increased freshwater inflow to Pyramid Lake could facilitate invasion of **exotic species**, altering lake ecosystem dynamics.
  - Nutrient loading from Reno/Sparks sewage effluent and irrigation return flows from the Truckee Meadows affects Pyramid Lake water quality; land application/rapid infiltration of effluent was discussed as a potential tool to recharge groundwater available for river augmentation during critical periods.
  - Restoration of **flood plain and hyporheic habitat** was identified as a needed remedial action for enhancing reproduction and survival of young.

## Key Findings

### Population Dynamics
- [[Gary Scoppettone]]'s team tagged several thousand juvenile and adult cui-ui over the preceding several years.
- Sufficient tagged-fish recoveries for robust estimates of population size, year-class structure, and stage-specific annual survival rates are not expected until approximately **1996**.
- Preliminary/rough population statistics are possible now and will improve with additional data.

### Stochastic Population Modeling
- [[John Emlen]] presented a preliminary interpretation of a cui-ui population dynamics model (developed by Strekal, Young, and Buchanan) run against **two hundred 200-year stochastically-generated hydrologic series**.
- The model estimated the probability of maintaining the current population level under **worst, good, and best-case vital statistic scenarios**, combined with additional water allocations ranging from **0 to 80,000 acre-feet**.
- Model limitations noted:
  1. Density-dependent factors were not incorporated, making estimates likely overly optimistic.
  2. Stochastic hydrologic events were based only on the **1901–1989** water record, excluding potential long-term climatic trends.
  3. Survival rates for early life stages were only crudely estimated.
- Additional model runs using better-substantiated early life stage survival rates were planned within weeks of the meeting.

### Genetics and Hybridization
- Electrophoretic analysis found **no evidence of hybridization** between cui-ui and Tahoe sucker.
- Low genetic variation detected in the 1969-and-younger year classes suggested a possible genetic bottleneck around 1950 or 1969.
- Recommended follow-up: electrophoretic analysis of the archived **1950 year class** (proposed for spring analysis) to clarify historical genetic variability and bottleneck spawner numbers, informing whether intensified genetic management is warranted.

### Water Management and Recovery Actions
- Estimated Newlands Project irrigation demand ranges from **245,000 to 277,000 acre-feet/year** under various operational scenarios.
- A **water rights purchase program** targeting the Truckee Division was proposed as first priority, potentially lowering demand to ~200,000–210,000 acre-feet, since the Carson Division could largely be served by the Carson River.
- Recommended coordinated purchase strategy: cui-ui water rights sourced from the **Truckee Division**; Lahontan Valley wetlands water rights sourced from the **Carson Division**.
- Twenty-two potential **water conservation measures** for the Newlands Project were tabulated, with estimated savings ranging from ~750 to 20,000 acre-feet/year (e.g., improved water ordering, canal lining, drain water reuse, automation, pricing incentives). The first seven measures were used in the primary water budget calculations due to cost-effectiveness.
- Team recommended taking an immediate position on:
  - Initiating a cui-ui water purchase program.
  - Addressing recoupment of **~1 million acre-feet** allegedly illegally diverted by the Newlands Project (1973–1983).
  - Establishing a target date for eliminating diversions through the Truckee Canal.
  - Ensuring the Secretary maximizes cui-ui benefits under P.L. 101-618.

### Critical Habitat
- [[Randy McNatt]] outlined Endangered Species Act requirements for critical habitat designation.
- [[Bob Pelcyger]] argued the Fish and Wildlife Service was legally obligated to designate critical habitat (contrary to FWS's stated position), noting designation would simplify Section 7 consultation analyses (adverse modification) without necessarily restricting private property use unless significant Section 9 "take"/harm occurs.
- Recommendation: complete critical habitat identification in time to inform evaluation of the Truckee River **Operating Agreement**.

### Fish Passage and Facilities
- [[Duane Wainwright]] proposed eliminating the **Pyramid Lake Fishway** in favor of a provision enabling cui-ui passage directly over the river delta.
- Recovery plan actions include maintenance, evaluation, and potential modification/removal of fish passage facilities at Marble Bluff Dam and improvements to the fish trap/fishway at Numana Dam.

### Draft Recovery Plan Structure
Two draft outlines were proposed, organized generally around:
1. Continued basic research (population dynamics, lake habitat, genetics).
2. Enhancement of reproduction and juvenile survival (water security, water quality, floodplain/hyporheic restoration, delta access, passage facilities).
3. Hatchery/captive breeding operations as a catastrophe back-up and broodstock quality program.
4. Long-term monitoring across all program areas.
5. Bibliography compilation.
- Recovery was tentatively defined as maintaining a population of a specified size and genetic quality within an acceptable probability threshold, with near-term goal of **reclassification (downlisting)**, based on natural reproduction rates and heterozygosity (genetic diversity) metrics.
