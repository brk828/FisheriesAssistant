# Endangered Species Conservation Update, Tucson Field Office (August 5, 2005)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This internal update from Fishery Biologist Jeff Simms, dated August 5, 2005, reports on the [[Endangered Species Conservation Update|Tucson Field Office]] program for federally listed and sensitive aquatic species in southeastern Arizona. Work covers habitat construction, regulatory compliance ([[NEPA Review]] and [[Section 7 Consultation]]), spring inventories, and planning for the [[San Pedro River Riparian National Conservation Area]]. The update also records severe 2005 drought effects on local streams, including a week-long dry period on the [[San Pedro River]] and a loss of surface flow on [[Cienega Creek]].

## Background & Description

The document is a status memo, not a research paper, and it does not describe the morphology, life history, or taxonomy of the target species. The species named are:

- **Fishes:** [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Chub]], and [[Desert Sucker]] (written as "desert zucker" in the scan).
- **Frogs:** [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] and [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]].
- **Plant:** [[Huachuca Water Umbel]].

Scientific names and family assignments are not given in the source, so the frontmatter reports them as listed by common name only.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Management is centered on spring-fed and riparian habitats. Key locations include:

- **Augmentation and construction:** An 800 ft² pond was built at [[Little Joe Spring]] near [[St. David Cienega]]. Water augmentation at [[Martinez Canyon]] is underway to improve Gila chub habitat, with a cost share between BLM and AGFD.
- **Refugia:** A Chiricahua leopard frog refugium is planned at [[Empire Gulch]]. It has completed NEPA review but still needs Section 7 consultation.
- **Inventories:** Springs are being surveyed for habitat suitability for native fishes, frogs, and aquatic plants. About 20 further springs in the field office are tentatively scheduled for inventory in FY 2006.
- **Sites needing development (10 locations):** Lewis Spring/Sump, Donlevy Artesian #2, #3, and #4, Kartchner pond (being acquired by BLM), McDowell-Craig spring and pond, and two cienega springs at St. David Cienega. Other "low-hanging fruit" include Lewis Spring South, Murray Spring, Little Nogales and Nogales springs, Road Canyon Ponds (grazing permit held by BLM), Horse Thief Draw Spring, Frog Spring, and Post Canyon, the last of which requires coordination with the Audubon Society.
- **Planned reuse:** Two ponds currently used for sport fishing will become available for native fish and frog management when planning for the San Pedro River Riparian National Conservation Area begins next year.

Drought effects in summer 2005 were severe. The San Pedro River went dry for a week at the Charleston gage. Water temperatures in that reach stayed above 30 °C for several weeks, and the desert sucker population there may have been lost this year. Cienega Creek dried back, losing about 42% of its surface flow because of drought and late monsoon rains. Mattie Canyon lost 1 mile of perennial flow, and Cienega Creek lost 2.5 miles.

## Key Findings

1. **Section 7 consultation is complete** for augmenting the Empire Gulch refugium for Gila topminnow, but the Little Joe Spring project still needs final Section 7 consultation. Proposed species for the site are Huachuca water umbel, lowland leopard frog, desert pupfish, and Gila topminnow.
2. **Chiricahua leopard frog refugium** at Empire Gulch has passed NEPA review and awaits Section 7 consultation.
3. **Three additional frog conservation sites** are tentatively planned for FY 2006.
4. **Spring inventories** for habitat suitability are starting this month, with NEPA and Section 7 consultation to follow. About 20 more springs are planned for FY 2006.
5. **Ten spring sites** are listed as needing development, and several "low-hanging fruit" sites are identified for early work.
6. **Martinez Canyon water augmentation** is in progress to improve Gila chub habitat, with BLM and AGFD cost sharing.
7. **San Pedro River Riparian National Conservation Area planning** starts next year, with two sport-fishing ponds potentially reassigned to native fish and frog management.
8. **2005 drought impacts** were severe: San Pedro River dry for one week, sustained water temperatures above 30 °C, a possible loss of the desert sucker population, a 42% surface-flow loss on Cienega Creek, and perennial-flow losses on Mattie Canyon (1 mile) and Cienega Creek (2.5 miles).
