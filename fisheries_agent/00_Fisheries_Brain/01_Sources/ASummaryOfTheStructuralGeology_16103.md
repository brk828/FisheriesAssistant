# Lower Cretaceous Structural Geology and Tectonics of Coahuila, Northern Mexico

#geology #structural-geology #tectonics

## Executive Summary

This document is a geological field-trip guidebook excerpt (West Texas Geological Society Publication No. 81-74, field trip November 11-16, 1981). It summarizes the [[Structural Geology]] and tectonics of the Mexican state of Coahuila, written by Santiago Charleston. It contains no fish, invertebrate, or other biological data, so the ichthyology and aquatic-ecology framing does not apply. The content is limited to tectonic provinces, faulting, orogenic history, and a bibliography. The author's main contributions are a reinterpretation of the [[San Marcos Fault]] as a left-lateral transcurrent fault rather than an overthrust, and a proposed mechanism for the deformation of the [[Coahuila Folded Belt]].

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Coahuila is described as a "structural and stratigraphic laboratory" that has informed regional stratigraphy and tectonics of northern Mexico.
- **Historical research:** Early work was done by Burckhardt, Baker, Bose, and Cavins. Investigations by the University of Michigan under L.B. Kellum provided the basic reconnaissance. Key later syntheses were by Humphrey (1956) and De Cserna (1956), with many further papers through Tardy (1980).
- **Data sources:** Several key results are unpublished reports held by Mexican institutions, including Petróleos Mexicanos, Instituto Mexicano del Petróleo, Consejo de Recursos Minerales, and the Instituto de Geología of UNAM.
- **Framework:** The author uses the nine-province scheme of [[Humphrey Tectonic Framework]] (1956), revised here to six provinces based on newer data.
- **Imagery:** Interpretation relies on [[LANDSAT Imagery]] and a Gemini XII image. The guidebook includes three figures and a tectonic map (Plate A). The bibliography lists roughly 60 references across the reference and extended-bibliography sections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address ecology, habitats, water chemistry, or biota. Its environmental content is geological:

- **Basement:** Upper Paleozoic metamorphic rocks, apparently related to the interior of the [[Marathon-Ouachita Geosyncline]], underlie the [[Coahuila-Texas Craton]].
- **Cover sequence:** About 2000 m (6500 ft) of shallow-water Lower and Upper Cretaceous limestone and shale overlie the basement, studied in detail by Smith (1970).
- **Evaporites:** The Lower Cretaceous [[La Virgen Evaporites]] acted as a decollement surface that eased folding and thrusting. Jurassic evaporites served a similar role in Tardy's proposed regional nappe.
- **Paleogeography:** The provinces roughly correspond to Upper Jurassic highs, the Coahuila and [[Tamaulipas Peninsula]], and to areas of Cretaceous downwarping such as the Sabinas Gulf and the Mexican Geosyncline.
- **Drainage:** The Sabinas-Salado River may be a fault-controlled drainage system that parallels the [[La Babia Fault]].

## Key Findings

### Tectonic Provinces (revised)
1. [[Sierra Madre Transverse Folded Belt]] (conspicuous, folded belt)
2. [[Parras Basin]]
3. [[Coahuila Platform]] (broad, gentle folds, NW-SE trend)
4. [[Coahuila Folded Belt]] (tightly folded anticlines)
5. [[Coahuila-Texas Craton]] (includes the Serranía del Burro Anticlinorium and the Salado Arch)
6. [[Basin and Range Province]] (northwestern corner, adjoining the Big Bend region of Texas)

### Major Structural Findings
- **San Marcos Fault:** Named for the Sierra de San Marcos. Pemex geologists had considered it an overthrust. Field work and LANDSAT interpretation by the author show it is a transcurrent fault with left-lateral offset. Many folds are nearly cut in half along it.
- **La Babia Fault:** Divides the Serranía del Burro Anticlinorium (north) from the Coahuila Folded Belt (south). It continues southeast, roughly parallel to the Sabinas-Salado River.
- **Basin and Range faulting:** Tertiary normal faults, mostly NW-SE trending with displacements up to several hundred meters, cut or parallel Laramide structures. The Sierra del Carmen and Santa Elena faults bound a graben south of the Big Bend.

### Orogenic History
- Deformation began at the end of the Cretaceous and the start of the Tertiary, linked to the [[Laramide Orogeny]].
- Deformation was controlled by Upper Jurassic paleogeographic elements. Downwarped areas were strongly deformed, while the Jurassic peninsulas were weakly deformed into broad domes.
- Most structural axes suggest southwest-to-northeast compression. Humphrey (1956) proposed opposed movement of the Coahuila and Tamaulipas peninsulas, or transmission through basement, but the author considers neither probable.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Northwest-southeast shearing between the Coahuila-Texas Craton and the Coahuila Platform generated the southwest-northeast compression. This also produced the La Babia and San Marcos left-lateral wrench faults that bound the Coahuila Folded Belt.
- **Sierra Madre and Parras Basin:** Compression acted from north to south, forming tight, northward-overturned anticlines and synclines that wrap around the Coahuila Platform. Tardy (1980) interprets the northward thrusts as part of a regional nappe moving along Jurassic evaporites.
- **Final episode:** Normal faulting in the northwestern corner (Basin and Range) cut earlier structures and formed independent blocks.
