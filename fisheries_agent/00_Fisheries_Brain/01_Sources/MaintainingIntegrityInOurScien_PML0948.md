# Maintaining Integrity in Scientific Decision-Making (FWS Director Memorandum, 2006)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #science-policy

## Executive Summary

This February 3, 2006 memorandum from the Director of the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] to the agency Directorate sets out internal guidance on how scientific information is evaluated, handled and released in agency decision-making. It does not report any fish, aquatic species, habitat or field data. Its central demands are that the strength of the science be assessed honestly, that confidence in it be stated explicitly, and that drafts and scientific information remain internal until conclusions are formed.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal policy memorandum (two pages), signed by the Director (H. Dale).
- **Subject:** Maintaining integrity in the agency's [[Scientific Integrity]] and decision-making process.
- **Core concept:** Science develops in three steps: basic data become information, and information becomes knowledge. Work may sit at any of these steps when agencies assess [[Best Available Science]].
- **Confidence scale:** Information is judged on three levels: (1) we don't know what it indicates; (2) we think we know but aren't sure; (3) we are very confident we have knowledge.
- **Stated rationale:** Public confidence in the process, the [[Administrative Record]], and open debate on data interpretation all depend on these practices.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memorandum does not address any ecological, environmental or aquatic subject. It names no species, water bodies, habitats, sampling methods or regions. Its context is organisational, concerning how the agency handles science in policy work. Any ecological relevance is indirect, since the guidance governs the scientific basis for [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] decisions.

## Key Findings

1. **Two required evaluations.** Each body of science must be assessed for (a) its strength (data, information or knowledge) and (b) the confidence placed on it (don't know, might know, know). The Director states that sound policy discussions require both assessments.
2. **Honest presentation.** Drafts must include all legitimate information, together with a candid evaluation of its strength. Science is treated as a starting point, not a final answer.
3. **Internal control of drafts.** All documents, assessments and drafts stay within the Service. The exceptions are discussions with recognized [[Federal Peer Review|federal]] and state peers. Requests for release or premature briefings must go to the Director's office.
4. **Protecting debate.** Premature release of drafts or briefings is said to undermine public confidence, the Administrative Record and open debate. The memorandum calls for a culture of internal scientific debate before conclusions are formed.
5. **Timely submission.** Draft documents must reach Headquarters on schedule so policy-level review can take place.
6. **Assigned follow-up.** The Director will ask the [[Service Science Team]] to recommend how to implement the two evaluations.
7. **Role of science.** Science provides the foundation for finding solutions, but not the solutions themselves. It is the starting point for policy-level discussions of contentious issues.
