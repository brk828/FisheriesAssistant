# Proposal for C. Hart Merriam Life Zone Centennial Celebration

#ecology #biogeography #life-zones #historical-ecology #Arizona

## Executive Summary

This 1987 memorandum from Professor W. L. Minckley to the Chair of Arizona State University's Department of Zoology proposes a Centennial Celebration commemorating [[C. Hart Merriam]]'s pioneering work on life zones. Merriam's 1890 publication "Results of a Biological Survey of the San Francisco Mountain Region and Desert of the Little Colorado, Arizona" established the life-zone concept that revolutionized habitat classification across [[North America]]. The proposal outlines a decade-long (1990-1999) program including field activities, research programs, and a culminating symposium with published proceedings. The initiative would involve collaboration among [[Arizona State University]], [[University of Arizona]], and [[Northern Arizona University]], with potential federal funding through the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], or [[Bureau of Land Management]].

## Background & Description

[[C. Hart Merriam]] published his groundbreaking biological survey in 1890, introducing the life-zone concept that equated elevational zones with biogeographic realms. This system proved extremely useful in the Southwest where it was developed and has been applied in habitat descriptions for over 80 years. The concept stimulated extensive subsequent work by the [[Bureau of Biological Survey]], including:

- Cockerell's Life-Zones in New Mexico (1897, 1898)
- V. Bailey's Biological Survey of Texas (1902) and Life-Zones and Crop-Zones of New Mexico (1913)
- [[Joseph Grinnell]]'s work with the University of California, including The Biota of the San Bernardino Mountains (1908) and studies of the [[San Jacinto Mountains]] (1913) and [[Lower Colorado River]] (1914)
- E. Raymond Hall's Mammals of Nevada (1946)

By the 1920s, correlations with climatic variables were recognized on a continental basis. The 1930s saw further development including the successional and climax principles of Weaver and Clements (1938), biome concepts of Shelford and Dice, Wieslander's vegetation maps of California, [[Forest Shreve]]'s treatment of the [[Sonoran Desert]] (1951), and analyses of eastern deciduous forests and Great Plains grasslands.

Arizona's relatively recent settlement history (within 100 years of 1987) provides the advantage of documented baseline conditions through photographs and written descriptions, allowing direct documentation of environmental change—a luxury unavailable in regions settled 200-300 years earlier.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The life-zone concept established by Merriam provided the first systematic correlation of plants and animals occurring in Southwestern environments. The system facilitated:

- Development of common language for habitat description
- Additional ecological surveys across the Southwest and beyond
- Integration of vegetative descriptions within the life-zone framework
- Mapping and description of natural environments throughout western [[North America]], including [[Mexico]]

Life-zone definition and description continue to form a large part of the basis for generalizations in natural resource inventory and analysis, as reflected in contributions by C. H. Lowe (1964), Brown et al. (1979), and Brown (1982).

The [[Bureau of Biological Survey]] gave rise to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Department of Interior), with elements also transferred to the [[U.S. Forest Service]] (Department of Agriculture). These agencies control large percentages of forest and other public lands in Arizona.

## Key Findings

The proposal outlines three major components for a Centennial Decade (1990-1999):

**1. Field-Oriented Educational Activities (First Year)**
- Retracing original routes, campsites, and survey locations of Merriam's expeditions
- Stimulating worker participation
- Familiarizing politicians and funding agencies with the area and program

**2. Research Programs (Throughout Decade)**
Documentation and study of changes in:
- Natural habitats over the century
- Field study approaches and methodology
- Conceptual approaches to life-zones and community ecology
- Integration of past and present ecological understanding
- Demonstration of the original concept's worth and subsequent progress

**3. Culminating Symposium and Publication (End of Decade)**
- Major event possibly associated with a National Meeting
- Symposium covering historical aspects, past-present comparisons, and conceptual syntheses
- Publication of Centennial Decade Volume (1990-1999) commemorating Merriam's contributions

**Funding and Institutional Considerations:**
- Requires relatively large funding spread over extended period
- Best approached through legislative action at federal level
- Precedent: [[John Wesley Powell Centennial]] funded through U.S. Geological Survey via Senator Goldwater
- Requires consortium of [[Arizona State University]], [[University of Arizona]], and [[Northern Arizona University]]
- University of Arizona contributed early life-zone research through extension activities and Carnegie Institution funding
- Northern Arizona University located at exact site of Merriam's original research
- Arizona State University offers central location and active ecology researchers

**Implementation Requirements:**
- University commitment to procurement
- Advisory committee to explore community interest and funding
- Immediate action required given time constraints
- Plan formulation before major public announcement
- Active pursuit by personnel with time, interest, and incentive

**Scientific Benefits:**
The project would greatly benefit biology through examination of the life-zone concept in historical perspective and thorough documentation of changes in environment, scientific thought, methodology, and the concept itself over the past century.

**Author's Role:**
Professor Minckley indicates he will not personally organize or administer the project due to other commitments, but will serve in advisory capacity and participate as investigator when approved and executed.
