## Executive Summary

This field report documents a 16-day research expedition (21 April–7 May 1993) on [[Kanab Creek]], a tributary of the [[Colorado River]] in the [[Grand Canyon]], conducted by the Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit. The study had three objectives: (1) characterize seasonal habitat availability along established transects, (2) assess seasonal distribution and relative abundance of native and introduced fishes in the lower 10 km, and (3) document spawning behavior of [[Catostomus discobolus]] (bluehead sucker) and other native species. Researchers observed extensive bluehead sucker spawning activity, mapped over 100 spawning acts at a primary observation site, documented micro-habitat parameters at 29 spawning sites, and tracked larval fish emergence and drift. The clear-water conditions that developed during the trip allowed precise delineation of the upstream distribution limits of native suckers, revealing bedrock and boulder-jam features as apparent velocity/physical barriers to upstream sucker movement. Native species ([[Catostomus discobolus]], [[Catostomus latipinnis]], [[Rhinichthys osculus]]) dominated upstream reaches, while non-native species ([[Cyprinus carpio]], [[Lepomis cyanellus]], [[Pimephales promelas]], [[Fundulus zebrinus]]) were restricted to the confluence area with the Colorado River.

## Background & Description

**Catostomus discobolus (Bluehead Sucker)**
- Total length (TL) range documented: gravid females as small as 102 mm TL; most gravid females >150 mm TL
- Males ≥95 mm TL nearly universally exhibited tuberculation and were "running ripe" (secondary sexual characteristics of spawning readiness)
- 23 individuals seined at the 1.35 km spawning site; lengths, weights, and sexual condition recorded
- PIT tags implanted in all native fish >150 mm TL captured (until tag supply exhausted)
- Post-spawning mortality observed: >10 dead individuals examined in final days; all sexable specimens were female

**Catostomus latipinnis (Flannelmouth Sucker)**
- Sub-adults ranging 130–300 mm TL captured in lower 80 m of Kanab Creek during high mainstem flow periods
- Not observed spawning during this trip (possible pre-arrival spawning)

**Rhinichthys osculus (Speckled Dace)**
- Young-of-year (YOY) measured 23–35 mm TL
- Actively spawning concurrently with C. discobolus
- Individuals were not weighed during surveys (methodological note)

**Lepomis cyanellus (Green Sunfish)**
- Single specimen: 97 mm TL, captured in minnow trap 40 m upstream of confluence; preserved as voucher specimen

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spatial Distribution & Habitat Partitioning**
- [[Catostomus discobolus]] distribution was strictly bounded to the lower 6.0 km of Kanab Creek, terminating at a bedrock reach (6.4–6.6 km) and an associated boulder jam/vertical drop velocity barrier at 6.3 km
- A secondary partial barrier — a boulder jam at 3.2 km — corresponded with a sharp decline in suckers >150 mm TL upstream, suggesting this population segment may represent offspring from historic high-flow passage events
- [[Rhinichthys osculus]] (speckled dace) occupied the full 10 km survey extent, demonstrating broader habitat tolerance than sucker species
- Non-native species ([[Cyprinus carpio]], [[Lepomis cyanellus]], [[Pimephales promelas]], [[Fundulus zebrinus]]) were confined to the confluence zone and short distances upstream, consistent with prior survey years
- Sub-adult [[Catostomus latipinnis]] used the lower 80 m disproportionately during high mainstem (Colorado River) flow events, both under clear and turbid conditions, but vacated as creek turbidity cleared — suggesting turbidity-mediated habitat use tied to mainstem hydrology

**Habitat Structure**
- Riffle/pool ratios (R/P) calculated per Western Division AFS standardized methods (Helm et al. 1985) across ten randomly selected 200 m sites (five upper, five lower reaches) to contextualize representativeness of pool-based seine survey sites
- All standard fish survey sites fell into the "pool" category; riffle habitats were visually surveyed (not seine-sampled) due to clear water conditions, providing qualitative relative abundance comparisons between habitat types

**Spawning Ecology**
- [[Catostomus discobolus]] spawning observed continuously from arrival (21 April), peaking early and declining sharply by trip's end (5–7 May)
- Spawning temperature range: 17.0–23.5°C
- Micro-habitat parameters measured at 29 total spawning sites (19 at primary 1.35 km site + 10 supplemental sites in lower 2.7 km): depth, velocity (via Marsh-McBirney meter), substrate particle size, and imbeddedness
- Spawning substrate mapped using a colored-rock grid system (2 m spacing, 8×8 m grid) for precise site relocation
- Findings corroborate and extend prior spawning behavior observations by Maddux and Kepner (1988)

**Larval Emergence & Drift**
- Mass emergence event observed 5 May: thousands of larval fish in quiet pools/backwaters, most abundant in lower 2 km, detectable to 3.5 km upstream
- Larval habitat characterized (location, depth, velocity, substrate) at 13 sites in lower 2 km
- Drift-net sampling (6–7 May) at creek mouth: two nets run 15 min every 2 hours across 9 sampling events yielded 11 larval fish, providing a preliminary emigration/export estimate

## Key Findings

- **Physical barriers structure native fish distribution**: Bedrock reaches and boulder jams (at 6.3–6.6 km and 3.2 km) function as complete and partial velocity/physical barriers, respectively, constraining upstream range of [[Catostomus discobolus]] despite the creek's otherwise suitable 10 km extent.
- **Species-specific range differences**: [[Rhinichthys osculus]] occupies the entire surveyed 10 km reach, while [[Catostomus discobolus]] is restricted to the lower 6 km — indicating differential habitat/passage requirements between cyprinid and catostomid natives.
- **Non-native fish are confluence-restricted**: Introduced species remain concentrated near the Kanab Creek–Colorado River confluence, likely reflecting mainstem-tributary connectivity and possibly thermal/flow preferences.
- **Turbidity-mediated habitat use by flannelmouth sucker**: Sub-adult [[Catostomus latipinnis]] use of the lower reach appears linked to turbid mainstem conditions, vanishing as water clarity increased — suggesting turbidity provides refuge or foraging opportunity from mainstem incursions.
- **High reproductive output coupled with post-spawning female mortality**: Despite robust spawning activity (100+ observed acts at one site alone), all sexable post-spawning mortalities recovered were female, hinting at sex-differential spawning costs in this population.
- **Absence of previously recorded species**: Notably, no [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish) were captured this trip despite prior surveys recording them at the confluence — a possible seasonal or population fluctuation worth follow-up.
- **Methodological rigor for spawning micro-habitat**: The grid-mapping approach (colored rocks, 2 m spacing) for spawning site relocation represents a reusable protocol for future micro-habitat spawning studies in similar desert stream systems.
- **Emigration/drift quantification**: The drift-net protocol (paired nets, 15-min intervals, 2-hour frequency) yielded a modest but non-zero larval export rate (11 fish/9 samples), providing baseline data for understanding recruitment contribution of Kanab Creek to mainstem Colorado River populations, relevant to [[Grand Canyon]] native fish conservation efforts.
