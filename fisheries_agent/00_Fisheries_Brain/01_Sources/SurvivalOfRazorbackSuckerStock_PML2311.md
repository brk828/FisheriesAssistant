# Survival of Razorback Sucker Stocked into the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This draft final project report, prepared by Jason D. Schoofey and Darren J. Thombrugh and submitted by Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]] for the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (Agreement 02FG30OO4), documents an opportunistic survey of post-stocking survival of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) between October 2002 and September 2004. Surveys covered about 282 km of the [[Lower Colorado River]] from Parker Dam downstream to Laguna Dam, using [[Boat Electrofishing]] and [[Trammel Netting]]. Crews handled 20,756 fish from at least 20 species, including 119 individual razorback suckers (126 capture events), which made up less than 1% of the catch. The report also documents a parasite pattern linked to the [[Palo Verde Outfall Drain]] and recommends changes to [[PIT Tagging]] protocols for hatchery releases.

## Background & Description

Razorback suckers have been repatriated to the lower Colorado River for nearly 25 years. Stocking has recently been accelerated to meet requirements of a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] biological opinion (1997). The stated purpose of this project was to assess survival of stocked fish through an intensive, opportunistic survey targeting razorback suckers in the main channel and all watercraft-accessible backwaters and side channels. Population abundance was to be estimated by [[Mark-Recapture]] methods if adequate data were available. Sixteen interim trip reports were submitted to USBR, and this document serves as the final project report.

The razorback sucker is a critically imperiled native catostomid. Its repatriation depends on stocking from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD) Bubbling Ponds State Fish Hatchery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** The survey covered main channel, backwaters, side channels, reservoirs, and floodplain lakes between Parker Dam (river mile RM 192) and Laguna Dam (RM 43.5). The reach is split into six USBR administrative divisions. Waters within the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]] (CRIT) boundaries were largely excluded for permitting reasons; a separate 1.6 km CRIT survey was reported elsewhere.

**Stocking zone.** The primary known occupation area is the "stocking zone" in the [[Palo Verde Division]], a 24.4 km (15.25 RM) reach between the southern CRIT boundary and C-10 backwater. It includes six named backwaters (A-7, A-10, C-3, C-5, C-7, C-10) and three unnamed backwaters. Fish have been consistently repatriated only to A-7 Backwater since 2000.

**Palo Verde Outfall Drain.** This second occupation region is the [[Palo Verde Outfall Drain]] in the [[Cibola Division]], about 49.6 km (31 RM) downriver of the stocking site. It is a historical reach of the main channel, now an agricultural irrigation drain with elevated conductivity and total dissolved solids, nearly twice those of the main channel. Its flow is supplied by irrigation returns, and the reach is physically isolated by [[Channelization]] and levees from the modern river.

**Historical modification.** A six-year dredging and redirection project beginning in 1964 diverted the main-channel Colorado River into a rip-rapped, levee-bound channel. Backwaters and lakes were bypassed or isolated in the process.

**Other areas.** Four juvenile razorback suckers were captured once above Imperial Dam, and later surveys there yielded no further captures.

## Key Findings

- **Catch composition:** 20,690 fish were captured from January 2003 to September 2004 (Table 4). Razorback suckers made up 0.6–0.7% of the total catch, and at least 20 species were encountered. The most abundant non-natives included [[Common Carp]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Western Mosquitofish]], and [[Sunfish]] species.
- **Razorback sucker captures:** 126 capture events represented 119 individual fish. Of these, 106 were tagged with PIT tags, 6 were short-term recaptures, and 1 was a recapture. There were 13 mortalities.
- **Size:** Mean total length was 39.9 cm (range 26.5–59.8 cm).
- **Sex ratio and age structure:** Recorded sex ratio was 26 female, 22 male, 57 juvenile, and 14 unknown. Juveniles (<40.0 cm TL) made up about 48% of records.
- **Origin:** All captured razorback suckers are thought to be repatriated fish. Evidence includes wire hatchery tags on most fish, a high juvenile proportion, and growth-rate data suggesting that a 39.9 cm fish is about 2–3 years old.
- **Recapture limitations:** Only one recapture occurred, too few to support mark-recapture analysis or population estimation. Recaptures were expected to rise as zones of consistent occupation became established.
- **Spatial distribution:** Most captures coincided with the stocking zone. Captures in the stocking zone (79 events) had a mean length of 41.5 cm and a different gender balance from other areas (27 individuals outside the zone, mean 29.5 cm range).
- **Parasites:** An increased incidence of *Lernaea cyprinacea* (anchor worm) occurred in the Palo Verde Outfall Drain. Pooling Palo Verde and other captures against stocking-zone captures showed significantly higher proportions of parasitized fish outside the stocking zone (χ² = 18.95, p < 0.001, Yates correction, df = 1).
- **Size distribution:** Length distributions differed significantly between the stocking zone and other areas (Mann-Whitney U, z = −3.768, p < 0.001). The stocking zone showed a bimodal length distribution, while other locations were unimodal.
- **Striped mullet:** 14 [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*) were captured, under 1% of the catch. Most were large adults (to 74.0 cm TL), and 10 of 14 came from the Palo Verde Division near Blythe.
- **Encrypted PIT tags:** About 253 large adult razorback suckers released into the Imperial Division by AZGFD carry encrypted 125 kHz PIT tags. ASU does not own a compatible scanner.
- **Recommended protocol change:** The authors proposed PIT tagging about 10% of each AZGFD release batch for at least two years: 2.5% randomly selected fish and 7.5% larger fish (near 40.0 cm TL) to improve first-year survival. The aim is to recover juveniles with known age, release date, and growth data.
- **Equipment:** A new Smith-Root electrofishing boat was acquired by USBR, and a digital field balance, electrical field-strength meter, and Eureka water-quality multi-parameter unit were added to planned data collection.
