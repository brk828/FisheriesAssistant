# Grass Carp Growth and Stocking in Arizona Artificial Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This short note from the *California Fish and Game* journal (1985, vol. 71, no. 2, pp. 107–110) by Paul C. Marsh examines the growth of [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) stocked into seven small artificial ponds in Arizona. The species was introduced to North America as a biological control agent for aquatic vegetation. Marsh reports that the fish grew consistently across ponds during the first two years, that growth slowed in later years, and that growth appears to have been density dependent. The note notes that the author was not aware of comparable growth data from other North American populations. The OCR scan is heavily garbled, so some numbers below are uncertain and are flagged as such.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), a cyprinid native to western Asia, transplanted to North America for vegetation control and now widely distributed.
- **Context:** The note sits within the literature on [[Aquatic Weed Control]] and the use of [[Grass Carp]] in artificial and fish-culture settings, including hybrids with [[Bighead Carp]].
- **Rearing source:** Fish were produced at a hatchery and collected through the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], which also provided direction for the study.
- **Study ponds:** Seven artificial ponds. Specifications were taken from prior reports. The stocking size was reported as roughly 152–203 mm, and stocking occurred in spring (April or May; the OCR is unclear).
- **Aging method:** Scales were used to age fish. Scale radius was measured with a [[Microprojector]] from the focus to each annulus, and back-calculation was used to estimate length at each annulus. Scales were sometimes unreadable due to loss or damage during netting, handling, or transport.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pond conditions:** Growth differed among ponds, and the note suggests that pond position and standing crop influenced growth. Fish growth declined with increasing [[Standing Stock]] density.
- **Density dependence:** Mean fish size decreased as stocking density increased. The author attributes this to [[Density-Dependent Growth]], a pattern that the document suggests was previously reported.
- **Comparative data:** The author states he was unaware of growth histories for [[Grass Carp]] populations in North America beyond the studied ponds, and was not aware of any successful reproduction in the sampled waters.
- **Prior work cited:** The reference list includes work on aquatic weed control (Cross 1959), the [[Grass Carp]] in the Indo-Pacific region, and Marsh's own work on centrarchids and grass carp in artificial ponds.

## Key Findings

1. **Aging:** All sampled scales showed seven clear annuli. Tissue beyond the last annulus suggested that a new growth period was about to begin at capture.
2. **Consistent early growth:** Annual growth was consistent among ponds during the first two years after stocking. The OCR does not clearly give the exact mm values for each year.
3. **Slower later growth:** Growth slowed after the second year, with the note describing the change as abrupt at the third year. Growth was reported to be relatively slow during the period of the second pond fill.
4. **Maximum sizes:** The document mentions lengths of about 280 mm and a comparison with growth reported for other fish; the OCR does not reliably confirm the full range. Few comparable growth records were available.
5. **Density effect:** Growth was density dependent. Fish in more crowded ponds were smaller, and the author proposes that density rather than stocking size explains much of the variation.
6. **Limitations:** Scale quality was reduced by handling and transport. The author notes that available growth data for the species were limited, which constrained the reliability of the growth model and its comparison with other populations.
7. **Management relevance:** The note concludes that growth of stocked grass carp depends on stocking density and time since stocking, which matters for planning vegetation-control stockings.
