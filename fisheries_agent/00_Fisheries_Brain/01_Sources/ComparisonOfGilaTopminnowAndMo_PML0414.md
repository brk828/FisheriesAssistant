# Comparison of Gila Topminnow and Mosquitofish as Biological Control Agents of Mosquitoes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This final report (November 9, 2001) by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], funded by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] and the Arizona Game and Fish Department Heritage Fund, compares the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, listed as endangered) with the introduced [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) as agents for mosquito control in constructed wetlands. The study tested thermal tolerance, ammonia tolerance, use of simulated vegetation, diet, and effects on invertebrates. Both species were effective at suppressing mosquito larvae under experimental conditions. The authors recommend topminnow (and the [[Desert Pupfish]]) for future mosquito control in Arizona to support native fish recovery and reduce stocking of exotic mosquitofish.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Arid Southwest wastewater wetlands, which provide water purification but also breed mosquitoes that can transmit disease such as encephalitis and malaria.
- **Mosquitofish:** Introduced worldwide for mosquito control. Robust and tolerant of low oxygen, but associated with open water and linked to declines of native fishes.
- **Gila topminnow:** Once among the most common fishes of the lower [[Colorado River]] basin. It declined sharply and was listed as endangered in 1967. Its decline is largely attributed to interactions with mosquitofish.
- **Study goals:** Compare acute tolerance to ammonia and high temperature, use of simulated emergent vegetation, diet and food selectivity, and impact on invertebrates, to judge each species' potential to control mosquito larvae in constructed wetlands.
- **Methods overview:**
  - Fish were collected in 1999 and 2000 from Bubbling Ponds Hatchery (mosquitofish), Boyce Thompson Arboretum and a Tortilla Flat drainage (topminnow).
  - Fish were treated with praziquantel for Asian tapeworm.
  - Critical thermal maximum (CTM) trials used a ramp of about 0.1 °C/min, repeated five times.
  - Ammonia tests used 24-h [[Median Lethal Concentration]] (LC50) bioassays with probit analysis, repeated five times.
  - Vegetation penetration used a 5 × 0.5 m raceway with simulated vegetation at high and low density, 6-h video recording, and 10 adults per test.
  - Foraging used six outdoor 1,900-L tanks (two per treatment, plus controls) over about two weeks, with invertebrate sampling and diet analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal tolerance:** CTM values were similar between species, and intraspecific differences exceeded interspecific ones. Topminnow attempted to jump out of the water near lethal temperatures, a behavior that may allow escape from local hot spots. Mosquitofish congregated near the surface and never jumped.
- **Ammonia tolerance:** Mosquitofish tolerated about twice the unionized ammonia of topminnow. Female mosquitofish were more tolerant than males. Juveniles of both species were unavailable for testing.
- **Vegetation use:** Both species were less active in dense vegetation. Mosquitofish were more active overall and crossed vegetation more often. Topminnow stayed near vegetation and used it for cover.
- **Diet:** Both species strongly selected mosquito larvae and pupae. Diet shifted to [[Chironomid Midges]] after mosquitoes were depleted. Topminnow consumed much more "other organic material" (mainly algae and digested matter), reflecting omnivory. Mosquitofish showed notable cannibalism; topminnow showed none.
- **Field relevance:** Ammonia levels in treatment wetlands usually decline downstream, so tolerance differences are unlikely to matter in most artificial wetlands. Dense vegetation develops quickly in new wetlands and may favor topminnow.

## Key Findings

- **Mosquito control:** Both species reduced mosquito larvae and pupae to near-zero levels within days. Mosquito larvae were nearly eliminated from topminnow tanks within 1 day and by day 5 in mosquitofish tanks. Control tanks retained mosquitoes throughout.
- **Opportunistic predation:** A small overwintered topminnow population (about 20 adults and several hundred juveniles) consumed over 1,000 mosquito larvae in under 4 hours.
- **Chironomids:** Neither species meaningfully reduced chironomid populations.
- **Diet overlap:** Schoener's index was 0.61 for topminnow and 0.74 for mosquitofish between sexes. Diets were 33 food categories for mosquitofish and 22 for topminnow.
- **Gut contents:** Empty stomachs were 54% for mosquitofish and 40% for topminnow.
- **Conclusion:** Topminnow appear as effective as mosquitofish for mosquito control under most conditions. Their use would support refugia populations and reduce exotic stocking. The authors recommend using both topminnow and desert pupfish.
- **Data limits:** Experimental and tank-scale, with a short two-week foraging period. Results are not validated in natural wetlands.
