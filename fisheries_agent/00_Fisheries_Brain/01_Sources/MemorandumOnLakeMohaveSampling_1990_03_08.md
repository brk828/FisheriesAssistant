# Memorandum on 1990 Lake Mohave Sampling

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #Moronidae #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This document is an operational field memorandum from Arizona State University (Paul C. Marsh and W.L. Minckley) coordinating a multi-agency, 9-day intensive fish sampling effort at [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada border), scheduled for 17–25 March 1990. The effort targeted native and non-native fish populations in a Colorado River impoundment, with particular conservation emphasis on the endangered [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Three field camps were deployed across distinct basin sub-regions (Little Basin/Arizona Bay, Cottonwood Basin East, and Cottonwood Basin West), each using different capture methodologies (seining, trammel netting, electrofishing) based on prior-year efficacy. The memo does not present new morphological or ecological findings but establishes standardized data-collection and tagging protocols for an ongoing multi-year population monitoring program.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological descriptions of species are provided in this memorandum; however, the data protocol implies specific measurements to be taken in the field for key taxa:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: Total length, weight, sex, tag color/number (new or recapture), and capture location to be recorded.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** and **[[Striped Bass]]**: To be measured (length), weighed, and scale samples taken for age/growth analysis.
- **[[Striped Bass]]**: Additional protocol to determine sex and gross stomach contents (diet analysis).
- **[[Bonytail]]**: No morphometric protocol specified; live specimens are prioritized for immediate transport to a conservation hatchery rather than field processing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Context**: [[Lake Mohave]] is a Colorado River reservoir spanning the Arizona-Nevada border, divided into distinct basin zones (Little Basin, Arizona Bay, Cottonwood Basin East and West) exhibiting differential capture success by method, suggesting spatial heterogeneity in fish distribution, habitat structure, or behavior across the reservoir.
- **Spatial capture patterns**:
  - Arizona Bay: High seining success (noted as "particularly rewarding" in prior year).
  - Cottonwood Basin East: Electrofishing historically unproductive; trammel netting favored.
  - Cottonwood Basin West: Trammel netting historically unproductive; electrofishing favored.
  - Lowermost reach of Lake Mohave: Exceptionally low capture/recapture rates, prompting relocation of a camp to more productive up-lake areas — implying a low-density or low-accessibility zone for target species, possibly linked to depth, thermal stratification, or reduced littoral habitat.
- **Conservation species interaction**: [[Bonytail]] captured during sampling are removed from the lake and transported live to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], indicating an active head-start/refugia conservation strategy rather than in-lake release, reflecting the species' critically endangered status within the [[Colorado River Basin]] native fish community.
- **Community composition**: The sampling captures a mixed assemblage including endangered native cyprinids and catostomids ([[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) alongside established non-native sport fish predators ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]]), highlighting the co-occurrence of imperiled native fauna with introduced piscivores in this reservoir ecosystem.

## Key Findings

- **Multi-agency collaborative monitoring**: The 1990 effort involved ASU, Arizona Game and Fish Department, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (multiple offices), U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, U.S. Bureau of Land Management, U.S. National Park Service, and Nevada Department of Wildlife — indicating this is a long-term, large-scale interagency native fish recovery monitoring program on the [[Colorado River]].
- **Method-habitat matching**: Historical catch data directly informed gear-deployment decisions per basin (seining in Arizona Bay, trammel netting in Cottonwood Basin East, electrofishing in Cottonwood Basin West), reflecting adaptive management based on prior sampling performance.
- **Tagging protocol**: A standardized colored Floy tag system was implemented to track recaptures and prevent inter-camp data confusion — Camp 1 (Little Basin/Arizona Bay) = blue, Camp 2 (Cottonwood Basin East) = yellow, Camp 3 (Cottonwood Basin West) = white.
- **Data standardization emphasis**: Strong emphasis on accurate, unambiguous recording of tag numbers and specimen data, particularly for [[Razorback Sucker]] recapture records, which were to be maintained separately from general incidental catch data — underscoring the importance of long-term individual-based population tracking (growth, movement, survival) for this endangered catostomid.
- **Conservation action for Bonytail**: Live bonytail were prioritized for rapid transport to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] for ex-situ care, reflecting the species' critical conservation status and the reservoir's role as a wild source population for hatchery supplementation.
- **Incidental/non-native catch monitoring**: Non-native sport fish ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]]) were also systematically sampled (length, weight, scale samples, and for striped bass, sex and stomach contents), suggesting concurrent interest in predator population dynamics and potential predation impacts on native fish recovery efforts.
