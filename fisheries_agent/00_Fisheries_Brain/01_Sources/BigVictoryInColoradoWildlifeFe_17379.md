# Razorback Sucker Critical Habitat Litigation: Summary Judgment Victory Memorandum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This confidential memorandum, dated October 30, 1992, announces a court victory in litigation over [[Critical Habitat]] for the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The [[Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund]] (SCLDF) Rocky Mountain Office reports that Judge Finesilver granted its motion for [[Summary Judgment]]. The court ordered the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] to publish a draft critical habitat rule within 90 days, while the agency had requested one year. The court also awarded costs, and SCLDF planned to file for [[Attorneys' Fees]]. The authors note that an appeal by the agency remains possible.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal confidential memorandum on SCLDF letterhead (Denver, Colorado office; 1631 Glenarm Place, Suite 300), with a fax transmittal header.
- **Authors:** Drew Caputo (Associate Attorney) and Lori Potter (Staff Attorney).
- **Recipients:** A coalition of conservation groups and their staff, including representatives of the Sierra Club, CEC, CWF, FCAC, TAR and SUWA. Individual names are listed in the original header and are not reproduced here.
- **Subject:** Litigation over critical habitat for the razorback sucker, a fish species.
- **Other SCLDF offices named on letterhead:** San Francisco (home office), with regional offices in Honolulu, Juneau, New Orleans, Seattle, Tallahassee and Washington, D.C.

The memo describes the case only in legal and procedural terms. It contains no biological, population, or habitat data for the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document contains no ecological data. It gives no information on the razorback sucker's distribution, population status, life history, habitat requirements, threats, or the specific river reaches involved in the critical habitat designation. Its environmental relevance lies in the stated goal of protecting the species through a formal critical habitat designation. The authors describe the ruling as "the best thing for the razorback." The memo also calls the opinion "an excellent precedent for future endangered species litigation."

## Key Findings

1. **Summary judgment granted for conservation plaintiffs.** Judge Finesilver ruled in favor of SCLDF's motion in the razorback critical habitat case.
2. **Accelerated timeline ordered.** The agency must publish a draft critical habitat rule within 90 days. The agency had requested one year, and the plaintiffs had asked for the 90-day deadline.
3. **Costs awarded.** The court granted the plaintiffs' costs.
4. **Attorneys' fees to be sought.** SCLDF planned to file simultaneously for attorneys' fees.
5. **Precedential value.** The authors describe the opinion as a strong precedent for future [[Endangered Species Act]] litigation. (The memo does not name the statute explicitly; the phrase "endangered species litigation" is used.)
6. **Possible appeal.** The authors state that an appeal by the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] is always possible and that they would keep the group informed.
7. **Distribution plan.** A copy of the opinion was to be mailed to recipients the same day.
