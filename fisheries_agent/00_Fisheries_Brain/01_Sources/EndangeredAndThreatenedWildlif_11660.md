# Endangered and threatened wildlife and plants; extension of the final decision to list the Mobile River system population of the Alabama Sturgeon as an ednangered species with critical habitat.

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

The **[[Alabama Sturgeon]]** (*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*), formerly called the Alabama shovelnose sturgeon, is a small, critically rare, freshwater sturgeon endemic to the **[[Mobile River System]]** of Alabama and Mississippi. Once common enough to support commercial fisheries in the late 19th century (nearly 18,000 kg landed from the Alabama River alone in 1898), the species has undergone a catastrophic decline in both range and population size over the past century. By the time of this 1994 Federal Register notice, only a **single verified specimen** had been captured since 1985 (a mature male taken December 2, 1993, below Claiborne Lock and Dam), prompting the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) to extend the deadline for a final listing decision under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]** due to substantial scientific disagreement over whether the species still persists. The taxon was formally described by Williams and Clemmer (1991) and is most closely related to the **[[Shovelnose Sturgeon]]** (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*) of the Mississippi River system.

## Background & Description

- **Body form**: Elongated, heavily armored, dorsoventrally depressed body with an attenuated caudal peduncle.
- **Caudal fin**: Long filament on the upper lobe, characteristic of the genus *Scaphirhynchus*.
- **Size**: Relatively small sturgeon; maximum standard length ~72 cm (28 in).
- **Sexual dimorphism**: Slight.
- **Diagnostic features distinguishing it from the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]]**:
  - Larger eyes (almost always)
  - Different plate counts posterior to the anal fin
  - Differing dorsal fin ray counts
  - Three diagnostic head armature characters (Mayden & Kuhajda 1994)
- **Juveniles**: Morphology essentially unknown; only a single ~2-year-old juvenile has been documented.
- **Comparison to [[Gulf Sturgeon]]** (*Acipenser oxyrinchus desotoi*): Gulf sturgeon are larger (up to 2 m), more robust, have shorter/deeper caudal peduncles, and are anadromous — unlike the exclusively freshwater, non-migratory Alabama sturgeon.
- **Genetics**: Mitochondrial cytochrome *b* sequence comparisons (Schill & Walker 1994) found the Alabama sturgeon genetically indistinguishable from shovelnose and pallid sturgeons at that locus — comparable to intrageneric cytochrome *b* conservation seen in other well-supported species pairs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Deep, swift-current river channels with stable gravel and sand substrates; radio-tagged individuals occupied depths of 7.5–12.0 m (25–40 ft) in swift current (Burke & Ramsey 1985).
- **Associated structures**: Possible use of channel-training devices (dikes, wing walls, closing dams) analogous to shovelnose sturgeon habitat use in the Mississippi River, though a targeted Corps-funded study found no confirming captures.
- **Life history**:
  - Non-anadromous; members of *Scaphirhynchus* are strictly freshwater and do not undertake seasonal sea migrations (Bailey & Cross 1954).
  - Longevity >20 years, based on comparison to related species (shovelnose sturgeon aged to 12–14 years; pallid sturgeon aged 8–41 years).
  - Spawning likely requires swift currents for adhesive egg development (5–8 days to hatch); shovelnose sturgeon spawn April–July depending on temperature/flow.
  - Population dynamics may be cyclical, with peak abundance possibly occurring every 7–8 years, correlating with a documented hiatus pattern in museum collection records since 1953.
- **Diet**: Aquatic insect larvae are a major component; also consumes fish eggs, snails, mussels, and fish (Burke & Ramsey 1985) — broadly opportunistic, similar to the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] in the Missouri River (Modde & Schmulbach 1977).
- **Threats/decline drivers**:
  - Dams and altered river flows (e.g., Claiborne Lock and Dam)
  - Gravel-mining operations
  - General habitat degradation from land-use practices
  - Historic overfishing, particularly around the turn of the 20th century

## Key Findings

- **Historic abundance**: 1898 U.S. Commission of Fish and Fisheries report recorded 19,500 kg of "shovelnose sturgeon" landed from Alabama waters, with 18,000 kg from the Alabama River — later understood to represent the Alabama sturgeon specifically.
- **Collection chronology**: Only 32 total museum/university/private specimens documented as of 1991 (Clemmer 1983; Williams & Clemmer 1991); a roughly 7–8 year hiatus has occurred between major collection events since 1953 (Mayden & Kuhajda 1994).
- **Recent survey effort**: Extensive multi-year sampling (Burke & Ramsey 1985; ADCNR/Corps surveys 1990, 1992; USFWS/ADCNR joint effort 1993) yielded only:
  - 5 specimens (1985): 2 gravid females + 1 juvenile (~2 yrs) + others
  - 9 additional specimens documented by Williams & Clemmer (1991)
  - 1 mature male captured live via gill net on December 2, 1993, in the free-flowing Alabama River below Claiborne Lock and Dam — the only verified record in nearly a decade.
- **Anecdotal reports**: Commercial fishermen reported two additional sturgeon captures upstream of Claiborne Lock and Dam, possibly attributable to either the Alabama sturgeon or the co-occurring [[Gulf Sturgeon]].
- **Taxonomic status**: Formally described as a distinct species by Williams & Clemmer (1991); recognized as a distinct undescribed taxon since Ramsey (1976). Some disagreement persists among ichthyologists regarding species-level distinctiveness, but USFWS affirmed species status is warranted under the [[Endangered Species Act]] regardless of eventual taxonomic revision (species vs. subspecies), provided the population is not extinct.
- **Regulatory history**:
  - Candidate species status: Category 2 in 1982 and 1985 notices; upgraded to Category 1 by 1989 and 1991 notices.
  - Proposed rule to list as endangered with critical habitat published June 15, 1993 (58 FR 33148).
  - Multiple public hearings held/rescheduled/canceled (Aug–Nov 1993, Jan 1994) amid litigation (*Alabama-Tombigbee River Development Coalition v. Fish and Wildlife Service*) that enjoined USFWS from relying on a scientific panel report addressing taxonomy and extinction likelihood.
  - Final decision deadline extended to **December 15, 1994** due to "substantial disagreement among scientists" regarding the sufficiency of data on continued species existence, per Section 4(b)(6)(A) of the Act.
  - Public comment overwhelmingly opposed the listing, though several hundred individuals/organizations supported it.
- **Scientific data gaps**: Little to no information exists on spawning habitat, detailed reproductive biology, or current/historic population size — the Service noted the Act does not require exhaustive data, only that best-available evidence support a status determination.
