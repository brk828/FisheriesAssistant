## Executive Summary

This document is a 1986 letter from malacologist Robert Hershler (Smithsonian Institution, Dept. of Invertebrate Zoology) to ichthyologist [[W.L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University), proposing a collaborative field expedition to survey approximately 45 large springs in a desert cienega basin (the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] in Mexico). The letter is primarily a research planning communication concerning [[Hydrobiidae|hydrobiid snail]] biogeography, with fish data (specifically on [[Cyprinodon minckleyi]] morph abundance) requested as a secondary, correlative dataset. A phenogram of 12 Arizona *Pyrgulopsis* species (based on continuous morphological character data) is referenced as an enclosure, and a cluster analysis tree diagram (Euclidean distance, single linkage/nearest-neighbor method) is present in the scanned figures, though it pertains to snail population/morphological groupings rather than fish taxa.

## Background & Description

The letter outlines a planned field expedition (September 2–10, 1986) to a spring-fed desert basin, in collaboration with a USGS hydrologist/geochemist, with the stated goals of:

- Surveying ~45 large springs within the basin
- Collecting snail and, where possible, other invertebrate fauna from each spring
- Documenting qualitative patterns of distribution and abundance of the mollusk fauna
- Recording physical and biological habitat features of each spring for descriptive purposes
- Correlating habitat/physical data with snail morphological variation
- Collecting water chemistry samples (via USGS collaborator) for comprehensive analysis

The requested fish-specific data pertains to **[[Cyprinodon minckleyi]]** (a pupfish species named in honor of Minckley, endemic to the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin), specifically the **abundance of molariform morphs** across the different springs. This species is well known for extreme trophic polymorphism, including molariform (mollusk-crushing) and normal/piscivorous morphs — a detail consistent with Hershler's specific interest in correlating fish morph abundance with snail (prey) abundance/distribution across spring habitats.

An enclosed phenogram covers 12 Arizona species of **[[Pyrgulopsis]]** (family Hydrobiidae), constructed from continuous morphological character data only. Hershler explicitly expresses skepticism that this phenetic clustering approach reliably estimates true phylogeny, describing it as merely "a start."

The tree diagram reproduced in the OCR is a cluster analysis dendrogram using Euclidean distance and the single linkage (nearest neighbor) clustering method, with population/taxon labels including codes such as "cochise" and "Cuatro" — consistent with a hydrobiid population-morphology study rather than fish data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Desert spring systems ("cienegas") — isolated, often thermally and chemically stable freshwater spring habitats characteristic of arid basin-and-range terrain, notable for high endemism.
- **Basin significance**: The [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] in Coahuila, Mexico, is globally recognized as a biodiversity hotspot with extraordinary levels of aquatic endemism among fish, mollusks, and other invertebrates, often compared to an evolutionary "laboratory" due to habitat replication (many isolated springs with varying physicochemical conditions).
- **Habitat replicates concept**: Hershler frames the basin's numerous springs as a series of natural "habitat replicates," valuable for comparative studies of faunal distribution, morphology, and correlation with physical/chemical spring parameters.
- **Water chemistry**: Planned comprehensive water chemistry sampling by USGS collaborator was intended to support correlative analyses between physicochemical spring conditions and both snail and fish morphological/distributional patterns.
- **Trophic ecology linkage**: The specific interest in molariform *Cyprinodon minckleyi* morph abundance per spring suggests a hypothesized ecological linkage between snail (molluscan) prey availability/abundance and the frequency of durophagous (molariform, snail-crushing) pupfish morphs — a classic example of resource-driven intraspecific trophic polymorphism.

- No quantitative fish population, morphometric, or distributional data are directly presented in this letter; it is a planning/request document, not a data report.
- Hershler requests that Minckley contribute existing fish abundance data, particularly molariform morph frequency of **[[Cyprinodon minckleyi]]**, across the basin's springs — implying such data may have already been partially collected by Minckley prior to this correspondence.

- No direct ocean basin relevance; system is a closed/endorheic desert freshwater basin, distinct from marine or oceanic habitats.
