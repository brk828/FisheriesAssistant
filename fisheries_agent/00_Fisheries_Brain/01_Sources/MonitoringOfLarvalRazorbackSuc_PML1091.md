# Monitoring of Larval Razorback Sucker Growth in Lake Mohave, Arizona, Spring 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This monitoring report by [[Michael J. Horn]] tracks the growth of larval and early juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Yuma Cove]] and the adjacent [[Arizona Bay]] of [[Lake Mohave]] during spring 1992. Sampling used nighttime light trapping and dipnetting, plus daytime dipnetting and seining, and measured total length to 0.1 mm. Larvae first appeared in Arizona Bay on February 6, 1992, and in Yuma Cove on February 26, about four weeks after spawning adults were introduced. Mean total length rose steadily through the season. The data are presented as summary tables and length-over-time figures.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large catostomid native to the [[Colorado River]] system. Its early life stages are small and difficult to sample, so larval monitoring is central to assessing recruitment after stocking. This study was conducted from January 15, 1992 onward and targeted larval fish in two reservoir habitats: the shallow cove and the bay.

Methods included:
- **Night collection:** a spotlight with dipnet, and plexiglas larval traps baited with a 55-watt or 12-volt [[Halogen Light Trap]].
- **Day collection:** dipnetting of individual fish, and seining with 6-1/16-inch mesh.
- **Preservation:** 5% calcium carbonate buffered formalin, or freezing.
- **Measurement:** total length (mm) to the nearest 0.1 mm, using a magnifying ring and vernier calipers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Yuma Cove and Arizona Bay are nursery-type habitats within Lake Mohave, where larvae were found in nearshore areas, including next to the Yuma Cove berm. Larval presence in the lake was time-limited: after the late-spring sampling, larvae no longer appeared to be present. Sampling methods changed over the season. Nighttime light traps were used from February through April in Yuma Cove, and daytime dipnetting was added from late April onward. Changing methods means that day and night catches may not be directly comparable.

## Key Findings

- **First occurrence:** larvae were first collected in Arizona Bay on February 6, 1992, and in Yuma Cove on February 26, 1992, roughly four weeks after the introduction of spawning adults.
- **Collection frequency:** sampling was weekly until about April 8 in Yuma Cove, then became bi-weekly. Arizona Bay sampling ran weekly until April 14, after which larvae were no longer detected.
- **Growth trend:** mean total length increased over the season in both areas. Yuma Cove means rose from about 11 mm in late February to roughly 37 mm by the final summarized sampling dates. Table values are partially illegible in the source, so exact means should be checked against the original tables.
- **Size range:** Figure 3 shows minimum and maximum lengths for each sampling date, with error bars representing ± one standard error. The spread widens in later samples as the cohort grows.
- **Most recent sampling:** the text gives the latest collection date as 3/6/92, which is likely a scan error for 5/6/92, given that Figure 3 identifies 5/4/92 as the last date with analyzed fish.
- **Figures and tables:** Figure 1 compares mean total length of larval and juvenile fish in Yuma Cove (ascending curve) and Arizona Bay (flat curve). Figure 2 and Table 1 summarize the data by date, site, and length (mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum).

Overall, the report documents successful early recruitment after adult stocking and provides a length-based baseline for tracking Razorback Sucker growth in Lake Mohave. Further analysis would need the full, legible data tables.
