# Notropis saladonis Type Description, Mid-1960s Fish Collection Records and Community Ecology Research Plans

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This mixed archive combines four kinds of material: (1) a reprinted description of the new minnow [[Notropis Saladonis]] from the [[Rio Salado]] system of northeastern Mexico (Hubbs and Hubbs, *Copeia* 1958, no. 4, pp. 298–299); (2) a long series of handwritten and scanned field collection and measurement sheets, dated roughly 1964–1966, from localities in Nuevo León and Coahuila; (3) a two-part ecologist's research plan on disturbance and community structure and on endangered fishes; and (4) a reference list. Much of the scanned material is severely degraded by OCR, so most numerical tables cannot be reliably transcribed. The figures reported below are those that could be read with reasonable confidence.

## Background & Description

**Notropis saladonis** is a small cyprinid described from the Rio Salado system. The authors distinguish it from other *Notropis* by a combination of characters rather than a single diagnostic feature. Reported traits include:

- Pharyngeal teeth 4–4 (rarely 3 in one row).
- Principal anal rays 8.
- Scales usually 32 along the lateral line and 20–24 in greatest body circumference.
- Lateral-line scale rows reported as 10–13 (the scale count before the dorsal fin is partly garbled in the scan).
- Body moderately deep and strongly compressed, with width less than half the depth.
- Very thin scales, slender fin rays and a delicate, emarginate anal fin.
- A lateral line that is moderately decurved and reaches the lateral band behind the dorsal fin; the infraorbital canal is complete or, rarely, narrowly interrupted.

The maximum standard length is given as 88 mm on one page and 38 mm on the duplicate page. The scan quality makes it impossible to tell which is correct, so neither value should be cited without checking the original. The species name follows the Latin genitive of "Salado." The authors note that they retain the masculine endings for *Notropis* despite the classical rules, following precedent.

**Type and paratype material** is catalogued in several institutions (TNHC, UMMZ, CNHM). Localities include:

- Rio Salado, 4 miles east of the Anahuac Road at Ejido Nuevo Camarón, Nuevo León (collected by Guerra, 10 June 1951; 2 adults, 28–29 mm, TNHC 5563).
- Rio Salado, at the holotype (type) locality, locality No. 7 (6 adults, 27–30 mm, UMMZ 173133).
- Rodrigues, Nuevo León (locality 8; 7 adults, 29–38 mm, CNHM 62169, collected by Seth Eugene Meek in 1906 or 1907). These specimens were found while resorting a series of [[Notropis Braytoni]] and were judged to be of the same provenance.
- Rio Sabinas de Coahuila sites, including Rancho de los Borregos (5 adults, 24–29 mm, UMMZ 130349, collected 7 April 1939), Presita (2 specimens, 25 mm, UMMZ 173134) and San Felipe, 2 miles southwest (collected by Guerra, 15 June 1951).

The authors use a map of northern Mexican drainages to place these localities, based on the World Aeronautical Chart sheets (revised 1951–1952).

**Field collection sheets** (roughly 25 to 30 pages, dated mostly 1964–1966) record collection numbers in the form "64-0620" or "65-0173," locality names and dates. Localities that can be partly read include La Angostura, Rio Salado, Laguna Grande (marsh), Rio Sabinas and other Nuevo León/Coahuila marsh sites. The sheets contain columns for standard and total length, wet, dry and tare weights, and percentage moisture and fat. Several pages also contain fitted regression equations (for example, "Y = 2.148x − 47.06" and "Y = 0.0377X − 3.770"), with the variables and sample sizes illegible in most cases. Individual rows cannot be reliably transcribed, so the numerical content is recorded here only at the level of its structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The archive places the Rio Salado and Rio Sabinas systems of Nuevo León and Coahuila in a stream and marsh context. Two strands of the research plan (see [[Disturbance Ecology]] and [[Endangered Fish Conservation]]) are directly relevant here.

**Research plan: Disturbance and community structure.** The author proposes a five-year program on fish communities in non-equilibrium settings. The working paradigm treats natural disturbance (especially repeated flooding and drought) as a major force on community structure. The planned study site is a set of small, biotically simple, repeatedly flooded streams. Measurements would include:

- Age-specific population change.
- Resource availability and use.
- Reproductive and somatic characteristics.
- Microhabitat dispersion relative to disturbance events.

The author states that an NSF proposal (Ecology Section) was under review at the time, and that the research would also address artificial disturbance such as damming, pollution and introduced species.

**Research plan: Endangered fishes.** The author states that several hundred North American fishes are threatened or endangered at state or federal level, and that many are poorly known in terms of ecology and biology. The proposed work covers life histories and interactions with introduced fauna, treating exotic species as a biotic disturbance and including human impacts on native habitats. The author cites the dissertation work as the basis for this continuation.

**Cited conservation examples** in the reference list include:

- [[Gambusia Gaigei]], an endangered poeciliid (Hubbs and Brodrick, 1963).
- [[Colorado Squawfish]], spawned in raceways (Hammon, 1981).
- Endangered Species Act implications for southwestern fishes (Johnson and Rinne, 1982).
- Physiological swimming-endurance differences between LDH-B genotypes in [[Fundulus Heteroclitus]] (DiMichelle and Powers, 1982).

The reference list is largely population genetics and conservation biology (Fisher 1930; Frankel 1974; Frankel and Soulé 1981; Falconer 1981). The list also includes a monarch butterfly genetics paper (Eanes 1978), which is not directly related to the fish material.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Notropis saladonis* was described from the Rio Salado system, Nuevo León, Mexico, and distinguished by a combination of meristic and morphological characters, not a single trait.
- **Meristic and morphological summary:** pharyngeal teeth 4–4 (rarely 3), principal anal rays 8, usually 32 lateral-line scales, 20–24 scales in body circumference, body width less than half its depth. The maximum standard length is internally inconsistent in the scan (88 mm vs. 38 mm).
- **Type series and voucher material:** specimens in TNHC, UMMZ and CNHM, with several localities across the Rio Salado and Rio Sabinas de Coahuila systems, collected between 1906/07 and 1951.
- **Nomenclatural decision:** the authors keep masculine specific endings for *Notropis* despite the classical gender rules.
- **Field collection program:** approximately 1964–1966 collections from Nuevo León and Coahuila marshes and rivers, recorded with length, weight and moisture data, but largely unreadable in the scan.
- **Research direction:** a five-year plan on disturbance-driven community structure in small flooded streams, with a pending NSF proposal, and a parallel program on endangered and introduced fishes.
- **Conservation scope:** several hundred North American fish species are at risk, many poorly studied; the author proposes integrating endangered-species work with disturbance theory.
