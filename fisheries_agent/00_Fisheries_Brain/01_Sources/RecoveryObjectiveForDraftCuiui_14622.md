# Cui-ui Recovery Plan Objectives and Criteria

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] proposes quantifiable recovery criteria for the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endemic sucker of [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. The document establishes probability-based persistence thresholds tied to specific water inflow targets: reclassification from endangered to threatened requires 0.85 probability of 200-year persistence (equivalent to 60,000 acre-feet annual inflow increase), while delisting requires 0.95 probability (equivalent to 100,000 acre-feet increase). Recovery actions focus on restoring hydrologic dynamics of the [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] system through water rights acquisition, improved reservoir operations, and habitat rehabilitation.

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] is an endangered fish species endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]] in Nevada. Human activities have disrupted the natural hydrologic dynamics of the [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] system through extensive storage, diversion, and use of river water. The species requires adequate habitat in the lower [[Truckee River]] for spawning and in [[Pyramid Lake]] for rearing. The [[Newlands Project]], including both Truckee Division and Carson Division, has significantly impacted water availability for cui-ui habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The natural condition of the [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] system has been fundamentally altered by water management infrastructure. Restoration of natural hydrologic dynamics would maximally improve cui-ui survival potential. Critical habitat components include:

- Lower [[Truckee River]] spawning habitat
- [[Pyramid Lake]] rearing habitat
- Adequate water flows during spawning season
- Functional river delta and floodplain connectivity

The [[Newlands Project]] diverts water from the [[Truckee River]], with approximately 28,000 acre-feet of active water rights in the Truckee Division identified for potential acquisition. Preliminary examination indicates that more than 100,000 acre-feet may be available yearly through conservation measures under discussion by State and Federal authorities.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Recovery Criteria:**

1. **Reclassification (Endangered to Threatened):** Species demonstrates 0.85 probability of persisting for 200 years, corresponding to increasing annual inflow to [[Pyramid Lake]] by 60,000 acre-feet acquired at minimum rate of 5,000 acre-feet/year

2. **Delisting:** Species demonstrates 0.95 probability of persisting for 200 years, corresponding to increasing annual inflow to [[Pyramid Lake]] by 100,000 acre-feet acquired at minimum rate of 5,000 acre-feet/year

**Primary Recovery Actions:**

1. **Water Rights Acquisition:** Purchase approximately 28,000 acre-feet of active water rights in Truckee Division of [[Newlands Project]] and transfer to [[Pyramid Lake]]; minimum purchase rate of 3,000 acre-feet per year until all acquired

2. **Reservoir Operations:** Develop and implement operating agreement for upper [[Truckee River]] reservoirs to improve timing of water releases

3. **Diversion Reduction:** Reduce diversions from [[Truckee River]] to Carson Division of [[Newlands Project]]

4. **Habitat Rehabilitation:** Rehabilitate lower [[Truckee River]] floodplain and provide fish passage over delta; implement in conjunction with water recoupment from [[Newlands Project]]

**Implementation Strategy:**

- Conservation measures should yield benefit equivalent to purchase of 5,000 acre-feet per year
- Short-term approach focuses on increasing lower river flows for reclassification
- Long-term objective evolves toward full recovery by securing spawning and rearing habitat
- Progress evaluated through cui-ui population model (referenced as Appendix B)
- Adaptive management approach: conservation measures adjusted based on new information about life history and habitat needs
- Watershed improvements including timing of water releases and efficient use would promote recovery
- Draft completion deadline: May 15, 1991
