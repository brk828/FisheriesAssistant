# Arizona Game and Fish Department Fisheries Publications List (1967–1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Freshwater-Fishes

## Executive Summary

This is a bibliographic list of fisheries publications issued by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], dated 28 August 1995. It catalogs roughly 220 reports, covering 1967 through 1995, on fish populations, stocking, habitat projects, angler surveys, hatchery operations, water quality, and native fish recovery across Arizona's lakes, reservoirs, streams, and the Colorado River. The document contains no original data. Its value lies in showing the department's long-running management priorities and the geographic scope of its work.

## Background & Description

The list is organized chronologically by a code that combines the year and a sequence number (for example, 85-1 or 91-22). Most entries are "Fish Management Report" series for individual waters, typically covering multi-year periods. Many entries are repeated annually for the same water, such as the [[Woods Canyon Lake]] fertilization and crappie studies from 1967 to 1971. The list also includes annual hatchery reports, evaluation reports for stocking programs, creel and angler surveys, and a few proposals and general reviews.

Authors are department biologists and staff. Frequently appearing names include James J. Warnecke, Will Hayes, James Novy, Thomas A. Liles, Scott J. Reger, and Bradford Jacobson. Several entries are dated 1988 but cover earlier periods (for example, 1983–1987), which reflects the department's practice of issuing consolidated reports.

The document contains a few scan errors, and some entries are incomplete or unnumbered. Titles and counts are reproduced as printed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The publications address a wide range of aquatic systems:

- **Reservoirs and lakes:** [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Powell]], [[Canyon Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[Alamo Lake]], [[Saguaro Lake]], [[Apache Lake]], [[Lake Pleasant]], and many smaller lakes.
- **Colorado River:** Reports on the Lee's Ferry tailwater, the Imperial, Yuma, Parker, and Ehrenberg divisions, and Lake Havasu. A 1985 study assessed the effects of fluctuating flows from Glen Canyon Dam on the [[Lee's Ferry]] fishery.
- **Streams:** Surveys of the Sitgreaves National Forest, [[Verde River]] tributaries, Tonto Creek, the Black River system, Pinaleno Mountain streams, and Rucker Canyon.
- **Desert and southern lakes:** Arivaca, Parker Canyon, Patagonia, and Pena Blanca lakes, which were surveyed repeatedly from 1985 to 1995.

Management concerns include native fish recovery ([[Gila Topminnow]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] reintroductions), introduced and exotic species ([[Tilapia]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Threadfin Shad]]), angler compliance, and fish disease ([[Whirling Disease]]).

## Key Findings

The list does not report data, but its topic distribution shows the department's main activities over three decades:

1. **Fish management reports dominate.** The largest share covers individual lakes and streams, with frequent reports for [[Canyon Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[Apache Lake]], [[Saguaro Lake]], [[Lake Pleasant]], and [[Alamo Lake]]. Many of these are multi-year summaries (for example, 1983–1988).

2. **Native fish recovery was an early and continuing priority.** Reintroduction and monitoring reports cover the [[Gila Topminnow]] (including a 1984 study of success factors on four national forests), [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the Gila River Basin. Annual reports on the razorback sucker appear in 1986 and later.

3. **Exotic and introduced species were reviewed carefully.** A 1985 review addressed tilapias and a sound exotic fishes policy. A 1986 review examined potential forage fish introductions for [[Lake Mead]]. A 1987 study described the relationship between [[Striped Bass]] and [[Threadfin Shad]] in Lake Mead.

4. **Alamo Lake slot limit was evaluated.** The [[Slot Size Limit]] at [[Alamo Lake]] was studied in 1989 for initial angler awareness and compliance. A 1992 summary covered its first three years.

5. **Angler and creel surveys were routine.** Reports cover urban fishing programs ([[Urban Angler Compliance]]), statewide angler surveys (1986 and 1989), tournament fishing (1986–1987), and creel surveys at Patagonia, Roper, Tucson urban lakes, and Parker Canyon.

6. **Stocking programs were evaluated.** Winter and summer trout stocking was reviewed at [[Canyon Lake]], the Lower Salt River, the East Verde River, and [[Lake Pleasant]]. A 1986 study examined tournament-released [[Largemouth Bass]] survival. Trout stocking evaluations at [[Rainbow Trout]] waters continued into the 1990s.

7. **Habitat enhancement projects were common.** Projects include fish cover enhancement at Lake Pleasant (1986–1989), habitat development at Canyon Lake, and artificial habitat at [[Lake Powell]] (1989 proposal, field report, and 1992 final report).

8. **Disease and water quality were monitored.** A preliminary whirling disease report appeared in October 1988, and Lee's Ferry trout pathology was evaluated for 1990–1992. Water quality inventories cover 1983–1987, 1988–1992, and related periods.

9. **Hatchery operations were documented annually.** Yearly Arizona Game and Fish Department Annual Hatchery Reports appear from 1986 through 1994. A 1988 computerized hatchery management system was also evaluated at [[Page Springs State Fish Hatchery]].

10. **Long-term monitoring continued.** Several series were repeated across years: the [[Woods Canyon Lake]] fertilization and crappie studies (1967–1971), the Arivaca, Parker Canyon, Patagonia, and Pena Blanca aquatic wildlife surveys (1985–1995), and Largemouth Bass spot surveys (1989–1994).

11. **Notable one-off reports:** A 1985 assessment of fluctuating flows at [[Lee's Ferry]] (vii + 93 pp), a 1978 distribution survey of fishes in the Sitgreaves National Forest (49 pp), a 1986 survey of spear and spear gun fishing regulations, and a 1985 critique of competitive fishing management.

Overall, the list reflects a long-term shift from early single-species studies (crappie, fertilization) toward multi-water management reports, native species recovery, angler compliance, and regulatory evaluation.
