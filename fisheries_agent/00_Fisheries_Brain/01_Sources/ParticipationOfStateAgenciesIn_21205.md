# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Director's Order on State Agency Participation in Endangered Species Act Activities

#policy #endangered-species-act #wildlife-management #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This memorandum and Director's Order No. 94, dated February 1993, establishes official [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] policy regarding the participation of State agencies in activities conducted under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The order clarifies coordination and cooperation requirements between the Service and State fish and wildlife agencies across all aspects of ESA implementation, recognizing States' primary authority and expertise in managing fish, wildlife, and plant resources within their borders. The policy became effective immediately upon issuance and was set to remain in effect until September 30, 1994, or until incorporated into the Fish and Wildlife Service Manual.

## Background & Description

The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] recognizes that States possess broad trustee and police powers over fish, wildlife, plants, and their habitats within their borders. Unless preempted by Federal authority, States maintain primary authority and responsibility for protection and management of these resources. State agencies possess valuable scientific data and expertise on the status and distribution of endangered, threatened, and candidate species. Section 6 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] mandates that the Service cooperate to the maximum extent practicable with States in carrying out programs authorized by the Act.

The order defines "State agency" as any State agency, department, board, commission, or other governmental entity responsible for management and conservation of fish, plant, or wildlife resources within a State. State agencies maintain close working relationships with local governments and landowners, positioning them uniquely to assist in ESA implementation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The policy framework addresses conservation across multiple ecological scales and taxonomic groups. State agencies conduct population status inventories and geographical distribution surveys essential for determining which species warrant listing protection. Their expertise extends to designing and implementing prelisting stabilization actions for species and habitats to remove or alleviate threats.

The order recognizes State capabilities in implementing recovery plans for listed species and establishing monitoring programs for species removed from the [[Endangered And Threatened Wildlife And Plants]] list. State agencies' authority and local relationships make them well-positioned to provide long-term conservation following delisting.

## Key Findings

**Prelisting Conservation**: The Service will utilize State agency expertise and information in determining candidate species lists, conducting population inventories and distribution surveys, designing prelisting stabilization actions, and responding to listing petitions.

**Listing Process**: State agencies will be consulted in preparing proposed and final rules for listing species, defining take prohibitions for threatened species, designating [[Critical Habitat]], and reclassifying or delisting species. States receive notification of all proposed regulations per Act provisions.

**Consultation Procedures**: State agencies will be informed of Federal actions that may affect listed or proposed species or critical habitat during formal consultation. The Service will request information updates from States prior to preparing final biological opinions to ensure findings are based on best available scientific and commercial data. States receive copies of biological opinions (excluding classified or confidential business information) either from action agencies or from the Service after 10 working days.

**Habitat Conservation Planning**: State agencies will participate in all aspects of the [[Habitat Conservation Planning]] (HCP) process, contributing expertise and information throughout plan development.

**Recovery Planning and Implementation**: States will participate in all aspects of recovery planning for species under their jurisdiction and in implementing recovery plans. State agencies possess capabilities to carry out many recovery actions and maintain relationships essential for successful implementation. States will design and implement monitoring programs for delisted species, exercising their primary authority for post-delisting conservation management.

**Implementation Timeline**: Regional Directors were directed to immediately forward the order to respective State agencies and develop appropriate procedural agreements. The Assistant Director of Ecological Services was tasked with preparing a status report on implementation by June 30, 1994. The order incorporated comments from all Service Regions, the [[International Association Of Fish And Wildlife Agencies]], and the Office of the Solicitor.
