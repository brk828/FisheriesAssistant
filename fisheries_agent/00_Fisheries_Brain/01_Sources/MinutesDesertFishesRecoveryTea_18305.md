# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes (September 9, 1994, Phoenix, Arizona)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #catostomidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

These draft minutes record the September 9, 1994 meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), led by Team Leader W.L. Minckley, at the Arizona Game and Fish Department in Phoenix. The meeting reviewed the status of action items from earlier 1993 and 1994 meetings and covered several recovery priorities: the [[Sonora Chub]] transplant and monitoring program, critical habitat for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], the draft [[Gila Topminnow]] recovery plan, the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) biological opinion, [[Aravaipa Creek]] nonnative fish barriers, [[Conservation Agreements in Lieu of Listing]], the draft [[Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]], and the new [[July 2 Policy on Recovery Teams]]. The Team agreed to meet twice a year, with the next meeting tentatively set for March 1995 at the San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge.

## Background & Description

The Team is a federal advisory body for desert fish recovery. Attendees included representatives of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Bureau of Reclamation (BR), U.S. Forest Service, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, The Nature Conservancy, and American Rivers, along with consultants and guests. The species discussed are small-bodied, range-restricted desert fishes of the Gila, Yaqui, and Virgin River systems, many of which have declined through habitat alteration and competition or predation by [[Nonnative Fish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sycamore Canyon:** AGFD surveys found Sonora chub in Penasco, Atascosa, and Little Tinaja Canyons. No mosquitofish were found in the drainage despite past records. Bullfrogs were found only at Hank and Yanks Spring.
- **California Gulch:** Repeated sampling by Minckley found only green sunfish. Other AGFD biologists observed longfin dace, which is the only record of that species in the U.S. portion of the Rio Altar.
- **Sulphur Springs Valley and Turkey Creek:** Morphometric and genetic data (Dean Hendrickson; Alana Tibbets) indicate that the local longfin dace belong to the Yaqui form, not the Gila form. An 1800s Turkey Creek record of Yaqui chub and longfin dace was lost when samples were destroyed in the San Francisco earthquake.
- **Eagle Creek:** A new population of loach minnow was rediscovered.
- **Aravaipa Creek:** Only three tanks upstream of the barrier sites still contain nonnative fishes, according to Minckley.
- **Sonoita Creek:** Bulldozing of several hundred feet of stream led to the take of Gila topminnow trapped in stagnating ponded water.
- **Wet Canyon:** The talussnail has a single population, located entirely on Forest Service land.
- **Virgin River:** Listing has had limited success in protecting its fishes.

## Key Findings

**Recovery status and actions**

- Five species were officially designated category 2 candidates: longfin dace, desert sucker, Sonora sucker, Little Colorado sucker, and an undefined unit of speckled dace. Propst dissented from the recommendation.
- Critical habitat for spikedace and loach minnow was finalized as originally proposed in 1985 after a lawsuit settlement with the Greater Gila Biodiversity Project. The Team recommended additional areas for any future reproposal, including the Eagle Creek loach minnow population.
- The Team stressed that not finding a species in surveys for one or several years does not mean it is extirpated.
- Minckley and Phil Hedrick received an NSF grant for nuclear genetics of Gila topminnow. They maintain stocks from Bylas, Sharp, Monkey Springs, and Cienega Creek, each founded by 20 females.
- AGFD, BLM, and FWS discussed renovating Roper Lake State Park and restocking it with Bylas Springs stock.
- Stefferud noted that a sharp increase in fish biological opinions occurred in 1994. The Team agreed that public awareness of ongoing recovery actions should be raised, and that the recovery action list should go to the Regional Director, Congressional offices, and the minutes.

**Monitoring and planning**

- Minckley recommended that most monitoring answer three questions: Is the species present or absent? Is the population healthy? Are there new dangers?
- The FWS Gila topminnow draft recovery plan revision, completed by AGFD, was delayed at FWS. A new fish biologist position in the Arizona Ecological Services Office was expected to help reduce the backlog.
- The Team wanted another review of the Gila topminnow draft before general technical review.

**CAP biological opinion**

- The opinion's reasonable and prudent alternative addresses jeopardy for spikedace, loach minnow, razorback sucker, and Gila topminnow. It establishes a recovery fund (threats from CAP that cannot be feasibly removed) and a nonnative management fund (barriers and control of nonnatives, plus an emergency response plan).
- No funds had been transferred due to legal difficulties. An interim cooperative agreement between FWS and BR was needed for fiscal year 1995, with an organized project process planned for fiscal year 1996. A project-idea meeting was set for September 28.
- The Team's role in implementation was debated. Burke (BR) and Propst opposed Upper Colorado-style consensus processes; Propst suggested the Team act as a reviewer. Minckley opposed after-the-fact review and asked for a written statement from the Regional Director. Divine stated the Team was not viewed as window-dressing. Pontius called for more public input.

**Aravaipa Creek barriers**

- Minckley argued for more hydrologic and biologic analysis to design barriers, noting that nonnatives most likely move upstream on the descending limb of the flood hydrograph. He also raised concerns about sediment transport.
- Propst warned that upstream nonnative sources could negate barrier value and urged a public package combining barriers and management.
- The Team voted unanimously to support a system of nonnative preclusion for Aravaipa Creek.

**Conservation agreements and HCPs**

- Conservation agreements in lieu of listing apply to candidate species, while Section 10 HCPs apply to listed species and non-Federal parties. The Team expressed strong skepticism that conservation agreements delay listing while development proceeds, and noted the lack of public input.
- The Virgin River spinedace agreement was being pursued, and the Wet Canyon talussnail agreement was nearly complete. The Team agreed Gila chub was unlikely to suit a conservation agreement given its many landowners and threats.

**Rio Yaqui fishes recovery plan**

- Minckley was consolidating comments for Cobble by October 7. Only two comment letters had been received, and Cochise County and Mexico had not been sent copies. The Team asked that they be sent copies immediately.
- The Turkey Creek drainage (Joe Austin's property) may support a small habitat conservation plan, since Austin agreed to remove nonnatives and wants a take permit. The Team recommended tightening the down- and de-listing criteria.
- The target date for the final plan is December 31, 1994.

**July 2 policy and riparian team**

- The FWS Washington Office will not issue guidance, leaving each Region to write its own. Region 2 expected draft guidance soon. MacMullen asked for assurance that the DFRT would not be disbanded.
- A new riparian recovery team, prompted by the proposed listing of the southwest willow flycatcher, will cover New Mexico and Arizona and handle all aquatic and riparian species.
- Members questioned the policy's definition of "peer" and its reliance on "primary and original" data.

**Team business**

- Meetings will increase to twice a year, with some held at field sites. The next meeting is tentatively set for March 1995 at the San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge.
