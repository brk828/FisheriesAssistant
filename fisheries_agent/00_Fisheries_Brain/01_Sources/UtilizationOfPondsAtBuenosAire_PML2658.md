# Utilization of Ponds at Buenos Aires NWR for Rearing Native Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1986 interoffice memorandum from the Chief, Endangered Species, to the Assistant Regional Director, Region 2, proposes using several ponds at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] (NWR) in Arizona to rear five native fish species, including four federally endangered species. Spring surveys of more than 50 ponds found only exotic fishes, which suggests native fish could also have survived there. The fish are currently held and reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (NFH), which cannot meet production goals. The plan uses refuge ponds as grow-out sites, with minimal monitoring and no expected Section 7 impact.

## Background & Description

The memorandum describes the refuge ponds as the subject of a spring survey. Its purpose was to determine species presence and the extent of permanent water. Pond permanence may reflect better-than-normal water years that Arizona has experienced recently.

Five native species are proposed for rearing:

- [[Razorback Sucker]] (no Federal listing)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Endangered)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (Endangered)
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (Endangered)
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (Endangered)

The first three are "big-river" fishes. The Dexter facility holds all five species, and production goals for several exceed its hatchery capacity.

The survey captured five exotic species: [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Mosquitofish]], and [[Largemouth Bass]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The presence of exotic fishes in the ponds suggests that native fish could also have persisted there. Successful reintroduction depends on stocking fish of adequate size. The planned ponds would therefore serve as grow-out sites, with larger ponds preferred for the big-river species.

The memorandum states that refuge impact would be limited to truck and personnel movement to and from the ponds. It also states that no [[Section 7 Consultation]] impact is anticipated, because the grow-out facilities would extend Dexter NFH rather than attempt a new species reestablishment.

## Key Findings

- **Survey results:** More than 50 ponds were sampled. Five exotic fish species were captured, and all of them are non-native.
- **Proposed rearing:** Five native species are to be reared. Three big-river species (razorback sucker, Colorado squawfish, bonytail chub) would use the larger refuge ponds as [[Grow-Out Ponds]].
- **Pond selection:** One to three ponds would be selected for each of the three big-river species. Existing exotic fishes would be eliminated before stocking.
- **Stocking source:** Fry would be transferred from Dexter NFH to the selected ponds, reflecting the hatchery's limited capacity.
- **Monitoring and harvest:** Fish Service and Fishery Resources personnel would monitor the fish infrequently, 1 to 2 times per year. Fish would be removed and released into the wild at a suitable size, estimated at 2 to 3 years.
- **Environmental impact:** Impact to the refuge is limited to vehicle and personnel traffic. The memorandum states no Section 7 impact is expected.
- **Coordination:** The memorandum is copied to Dexter NFH, Arizona State University staff, and the Ben Robertson and Fishery Resources offices in San Bernardino. It offers continued coordination on any questions.
