## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (dated 25 October 1994) from the Parker Fish and Wildlife Resource Office (FRO) to the Arizona Fisheries Resource Office (AZFRO), reporting on the second shipment and stocking event of [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center. The memo documents logistics, mortality rates, distribution of fish across grow-out and release sites, and a correction to a prior memo regarding broodstock origin. This is a management/conservation record rather than a primary research paper, but it contains quantitative data relevant to [[Endangered Species Reintroduction]] and hatchery production of this imperiled [[Colorado River Basin]] cyprinid.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological measurements were reported in this memo.
- Aggregate biomass data: 73,824 fish weighing a total of 769 lbs, yielding an average individual weight of approximately **0.0104 lbs (~4.7 g)** per fish, consistent with juvenile/sub-adult life stage typical of hatchery-reared [[Bonytail Chub]] used in stocking programs.
- Fish were derived from 50 paired matings conducted by Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center personnel during the preceding spring, indicating a captive breeding program with controlled parentage — relevant to genetic management and [[Allozyme Analysis]] of stocked populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat types referenced:**
  - Artificial hatchery "fish farms" (grow-out ponds) at Parker FRO
  - Constructed ponds at the Emerald Canyon Golf Course, Parker, AZ
  - "No entry cove," a protected/restricted-access habitat on [[Lake Havasu]] designated for native fish conservation
- **Program context:** Stocking was conducted as part of the **[[Lake Havasu Native Fish Program]]**, indicating a broader initiative to reestablish native [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes, of which the bonytail chub is a critically endangered member.
- **Release strategy:** Fish were split between a controlled, low-predation pond environment (Emerald Canyon Golf Course ponds) and a semi-natural lake cove environment (no entry cove, Lake Havasu), suggesting a dual approach balancing survivorship monitoring with naturalized habitat conditioning.

## Key Findings
- **Total fish transported:** 73,824 bonytail chub (769 lbs total biomass), delivered via truck from Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center on 24 October 1994.
- **Distribution of stock:**
  - ~36,672 fish retained at Parker FRO fish farms, scheduled for stocking into Emerald Canyon Golf Course ponds on 26–27 October 1994.
  - ~37,152 fish stocked directly into the no entry cove on [[Lake Havasu]].
- **Mortality rate:** Estimated at **less than 5%** during transport and handling.
- **Genetic sampling:** A subset of fish was frozen for [[Allozyme Analysis]], supporting population genetics assessment of the hatchery-produced cohort.
- **Personnel involved:** Stocking and sampling effort was a coordinated interagency activity involving USFWS staff (Parker FRO) and Gordon George of the [[Bureau of Land Management]].
- **Broodstock correction:** A prior memo (18 October 1994) had erroneously reported the fish as recruits from a broodstock pond; this memo clarifies that all fish originated from the same set of 50 controlled paired matings performed at Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center, not from natural recruitment.
- **Significance:** This record documents an active, genetically tracked captive-propagation and reintroduction effort for the [[Bonytail Chub]], a species listed as endangered, and illustrates methodology for large-scale hatchery-to-wild stocking transfers in the [[Colorado River Basin]] native fish recovery program.
