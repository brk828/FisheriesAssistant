# The Toxicity of Nine Therapeutic and Herbicidal Compounds to Striped Bass

## Executive Summary
This study (Wellborn, 1969) evaluated the acute toxicity of nine therapeutic and herbicidal compounds commonly used in fish hatchery management to [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*) fingerlings. Using standardized static bioassay methods, LC50 values were determined at 24, 48, and 96 hours for [[Copper Sulfate]], [[Diquat]], [[Dylox]], [[Formalin]], [[Globe Terramycin Pet Tabs]], [[Karmex]] (diuron), [[Potassium Permanganate]], [[Polyotic]] (tetracycline), and [[Simazine]]. The research found striped bass fingerlings to be considerably more sensitive to most of these compounds than other common freshwater fishes such as [[Bluegill]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Channel Catfish]], with important management implications for aquaculture practices involving disease and vegetation control in [[Striped Bass Hatcheries]].

## Background & Description
- **Test organism**: Striped bass fingerlings sourced from Edenton National Fish Hatchery, North Carolina.
- **Average weight**: 2.7 grams.
- **Average total length**: 60 millimeters.
- Fish were held in troughs for 1-week acclimation prior to testing and fasted 48 hours before bioassay exposure.
- No specific morphological anomalies or effects were documented; the paper focuses on physiological toxicity response rather than morphological alteration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Striped bass fingerlings were reared under hatchery conditions to support stocking and sport-fishery management programs.
- Test water chemistry (dechlorinated tap water) closely approximated conditions relevant to hatchery/pond rearing environments:
  - Oxygen: 7.8 p.p.m.
  - Carbon dioxide: 3.0 p.p.m.
  - Total hardness (CaCO3 equivalent): 35.0 p.p.m.
  - Total alkalinity (CaCO3 equivalent): 64.0 p.p.m.
  - pH: 8.2
  - Temperature: 21.0°C
- The study underscores the interaction between [[Water Chemistry]] (particularly hardness) and chemical toxicity, notably for [[Copper Sulfate]], whose toxic threshold is water-hardness dependent.
- Ecologically, the paper addresses management challenges balancing disease control ([[Ectoparasites]], bacterial pathogens) and aquatic vegetation control (algae, submerged/emergent weeds) against the sensitivity of a valuable anadromous/euryhaline sport and management species reared in freshwater hatchery ponds.

| [[Potassium Permanganate]] | 2.5 | 2.1–2.9 |

### Compound-Specific Notes
- **[[Simazine]]**: Most toxic compound tested. LC50 far below recommended aquatic weed-control application rates (10 lb/acre or 2 p.p.m. active), making it **unsuitable** for use in striped bass rearing ponds. Striped bass were found to be 10–100× more sensitive to simazine than other freshwater fishes (bluegill, rainbow trout, largemouth bass, channel catfish, pumpkinseed).

- **[[Copper Sulfate]]**: Second most toxic compound. Toxicity is highly dependent on water hardness; results are specific to the soft-water conditions of this study (35 p.p.m. CaCO3 equivalent hardness). Toxic at low concentrations under these test conditions, cautioning against unqualified toxicity statements.

- **[[Potassium Permanganate]]**: Recommended ectoparasite treatment concentrations (2–8 p.p.m.) exceed the 96-hr LC50 of 2.5 p.p.m., rendering standard dosing **unsafe** for striped bass.

- **[[Dylox]]**: Striped bass are markedly less tolerant than goldfish, golden shiners, fathead minnows, and channel catfish (LC50 45–80 p.p.m. in those species vs. 5.2 p.p.m. in striped bass). Despite high sensitivity, Dylox has a comparatively wide safety margin relative to its effective therapeutic dose (0.25 p.p.m. active) for controlling anchor parasites, trichodinids, and monogenetic trematodes.

- **[[Formalin]]**: Striped bass tolerate brief high-concentration exposure but are sensitive to prolonged low-concentration exposure. The commonly recommended pond treatment (15 p.p.m.) carries meaningful risk given a 96-hr LC50 of only 18 p.p.m. (CI 10.0–32.4 p.p.m.).

- **[[Diquat]]**: Found to be **safe** for use at herbicidally effective concentrations (0.2–0.5 p.p.m. cation) for aquatic weed control, given a 96-hr LC50 of 80 p.p.m. commercial formulation — a wide margin of safety.

- **[[Globe Terramycin Pet Tabs]] & [[Polyotic]]** (tetracycline/oxytetracycline antibiotics): Both exhibited very low toxicity (LC50 >150 p.p.m.), providing at least a 10-fold safety margin over the standard therapeutic bath concentration (15 p.p.m. active) used for bacterial disease control.

- **[[Karmex]]** (diuron): Effective aquatic herbicide at 1 lb/acre application rates; the 96-hr LC50 (3.1 p.p.m.) supports safe use at recommended rates. However, striped bass fingerlings are notably **more sensitive** to diuron than bluegill (8.5–25 p.p.m. depending on size) and brown bullhead (11.0 p.p.m.).

### Overarching Conclusion
Striped bass fingerlings demonstrate markedly greater sensitivity to most tested therapeutic and herbicidal compounds compared to other freshwater fish species commonly used in aquaculture (bluegill, largemouth bass, channel catfish, goldfish, fathead minnows). This heightened sensitivity necessitates species-specific toxicity data before applying standard hatchery chemical treatments to [[Striped Bass Aquaculture]] operations, and compliance with relevant Federal and local regulatory frameworks is required for any operational use of these compounds.
