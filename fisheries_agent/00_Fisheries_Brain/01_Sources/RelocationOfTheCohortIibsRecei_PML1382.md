# Relocation of 1974 Cohort HBS to West Pond, Imperial County (1980)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #unspecified-fish

## Executive Summary

This 1980 interoffice memo from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] documents the marking, tagging, and transfer of 80 fish from the 1974 cohort (abbreviated "HBS" in the text, with "IIBS" likely an OCR error) from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] to [[West Pond]] in Imperial County on 16 July 1980. Fish were measured, marked with [[Freeze Branding]], disc tags and fin-ray clipping, then transported and released after temperature acclimation. The memo also notes a planned second transfer of approximately 20 to 40 additional fish and states that some tagged fish served as controls in an [[Ultrasonic Telemetry]] transmitter-implant experiment.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal memorandum dated 30 July 1980, from Bill Loudermilk, Fishery Biologist, to the Associate Fishery Biologist, Long Beach. Copies went to M. Elkins, B. Rinnier, L. Courtois, J. St. Aman, the Resources Agency and G. Ferrier.
- **Subject:** Relocation of the 1974 cohort HBS from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery to West Pond.
- **Fish sample:** 80 fish received at the holding facility were marked, tagged and relocated.
- **Personnel:** K. Stinemetz (WAPR-YPo), L. Hilmer (CSU Long Beach), R. Maher and T. Yoshimura assisted with the effort.
- **Support:** Two liters of [[Liquid Nitrogen]] were supplied by [[Cal Poly Pomona]] as the cooling medium for branding irons.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Receiving water (West Pond):**
- Surface area: approximately 50 acres
- Average depth: 1 m (maximum 2 m)
- Described as well oxygenated during the summer months (the OCR reads "narmer," likely "warmer" or "summer")

**Temperature conditions during transfer:**
- Holding pond temperature at loading (1530 hrs): 28.5 °C
- Transport tank temperature: 22.5 °C
- West Pond surface temperature (2330 hrs): 32.0 °C
- Tank water was gradually tempered with West Pond water before release to reduce thermal shock.

**Transport and health management:**
- Fish were moved in a 400-gallon tank.
- The water was treated with an antibacterial soluble powder at 0.25 ppm (0.5 oz per 100 gallons) to deter bacterial infection. The product name is garbled in the scan.

## Key Findings

1. **Marking and measurement protocol**
   - Total length, fork length and net weight were recorded before marking to allow growth-rate estimates in later sampling.
   - Marking methods were combined to test how well individual fish could be identified over time:
     - Freeze brands on the nuchal keel and caudal peduncle area, applied with a 1/8-inch copper-wire branding iron cooled in liquid nitrogen.
     - Numbered disc-dangler tags below the dorsal fin.
     - Partial removal of specific dorsal fin rays using a paper punch.

2. **Brand visibility**
   - Freeze brands were not visible immediately after branding but appeared as defined black marks by 2330 hrs that night.

3. **Fin-ray treatment**
   - Of the 16 fish that received paper-punch fin-ray marks, 8 were treated by searing the remaining fin-ray stub with the freeze-branding iron.

4. **Control fish**
   - Three of the larger fish (lengths given as 503, 596 and 600, unit shown as "m," probably mm) came from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery and were used as controls in the ultrasonic transmitter-implant experiment.

5. **Planned follow-up**
   - Approximately 20 to 40 additional fish were to be relocated to West Pond from the hatchery, using the same procedures.

6. **Stated purpose**
   - The combined marking and tagging scheme was intended to evaluate the reliability of individual identification over time and to support future growth-rate estimates.
