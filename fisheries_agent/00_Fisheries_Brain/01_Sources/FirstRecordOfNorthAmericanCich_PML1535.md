# Established Population of a Neotropical Cichlid in the Virgin River, Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, by Marsh, [[Burke]], [[DeMarais]], and Douglas, reports an established population of a Neotropical cichlid, the [[Managua Cichlid]], in the [[Virgin River]] of southwestern Utah. The fish was found in a pool fed by a warm spring. The authors discuss how the species arrived, whether it is spreading, and what its presence means for the native fish fauna. The OCR scan is badly degraded, so many details below are partial. Numbers and names are given only where they are legible.

## Background & Description

- **Focal species:** The [[Managua Cichlid]] is a large, deep-bodied cichlid native to Nicaragua. Local names in the text include "guapote tigre" and "jaguar guapote." It belongs to the genus associated with [[Cichlasoma]] in the text.
- **Introduction history:** The text indicates the species was first imported into the aquarium and ornamental fish trade, then released or escaped into the Virgin River system. The text also mentions a possible link with other Neotropical or introduced fishes, but the OCR does not allow a clear reading.
- **Study site:** The fish occupied a spring-fed pool in the Virgin River. The pool has sloping sides and a bottom covered in organic material. A depth of about 10 m is mentioned, but the OCR is unclear.
- **Taxonomic context:** The paper discusses whether the Virgin River population matches the species described from Nicaragua and whether it belongs to the same genus as other cichlids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fishes of the Virgin River:** The text notes that six native fish species occur in the system. Several are listed or proposed for protection, including the [[Virgin Spinedace]], the [[Virgin River Chub]], and the [[Woundfin]]. The text also mentions an undescribed or unnamed *Pantosteus* sucker, which the authors say has not yet been formally identified.
- **Habitat preference:** The text notes that the native species of the genus associated with this cichlid typically live in turbid water and are closely associated with rocky or rubble substrates. The authors suggest a similar relationship may exist in the Virgin River pool.
- **Threats to natives:** The paper describes declines of native fishes in the Virgin River, including population crashes and possible extirpations. It suggests that predation or competition by the introduced cichlid could be a factor, though the OCR does not let me confirm the exact argument.
- **Water and flow:** The text refers to spring inflow, warm water, and stream channel conditions that may allow the cichlid to persist in the pool, especially where winter temperatures are moderated.
- **Management concern:** The authors discuss whether the fish could spread downstream or invade more of the river system, and whether removal is feasible.

## Key Findings

1. **Established population:** The Managua Cichlid is established in the Virgin River, in a pool that appears to have been a stable habitat rather than a single introduction event.
2. **Introduction pathway:** The species probably entered the system through the aquarium or ornamental trade. The OCR does not clearly confirm the release details.
3. **Potential impact:** The authors consider the cichlid a threat to native fish communities, given the observed declines of several native Virgin River species.
4. **Taxonomic question:** The population's identity is discussed, and the authors indicate that taxonomic comparison with Nicaraguan material is needed.
5. **Management:** The paper points to the need for monitoring and possible control, though specific recommendations are not legible in the scan.
