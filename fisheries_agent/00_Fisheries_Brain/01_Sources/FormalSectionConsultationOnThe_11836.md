# Formal Section 7 Consultation on Hoover Powerplant Modification Project Effects on Bonytail Chub

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents a formal Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act regarding the proposed [[Hoover Powerplant]] modification project and its potential impacts on the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]]. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service concluded that the project is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of the bonytail chub, based on evidence that the species has not successfully reproduced in Lake Mohave for at least 10-15 years, possibly since the lake's formation in the mid-1950s. The genetically pure population of bonytail chubs in Lake Mohave represents the only known occurrence of this species, which was listed as endangered on April 23, 1980.

## Background & Description

The [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) is a cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. A genetically pure population is known to occur only in [[Lake Mohave]] in the Lower Colorado River Basin; populations reported from the upper basin may have hybridized with other *Gila* species. The species was listed as endangered on April 23, 1980.

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a catostomid, was not listed under the Endangered Species Act at the time of this consultation. Recovery progress was being made in Arizona and New Mexico, and continued progress would prevent listing consideration.

Bonytail chub spawning biology indicates adaptation to highly variable riverine conditions. Preliminary data suggests females may become mature as early as May and remain fecund as late as September to accommodate sub-optimal river conditions. Eggs hatch rapidly at summer water temperatures (4 days at 21°C), indicating a species adapted to taking advantage of optimal environmental conditions in a highly variable habitat.

The present length-frequency structure of the bonytail chub population in Lake Mohave indicates no successful spawn for at least 10-15 years, and possibly not since Lake Mohave first formed in the mid-1950s. Several qualified investigators have attempted to find evidence of successful bonytail reproduction since the closing of [[Davis Dam]], but no investigator has found evidence of bonytail recruitment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposed [[Hoover Powerplant]] modification would increase generating capacity to 2,200 MW with maximum peak releases of 62,000 cfs of water. This would alter water level fluctuations in [[Lake Mohave]] by amplifying existing daily fluctuations (1-2 feet) by an additional 0.4 foot.

During the first half of the year, the lake is expected to rise approximately 3 feet from Monday through Friday and decrease approximately 2 feet over the weekend; during the second half of the year, a weekly 2-foot rise and 2-foot drop is anticipated. Daily and weekly fluctuations are expected to increase bank erosion slightly and impact spawning activities of certain Lake Mohave fishes.

A weekly variation in water temperature of 1°C is likely to a depth of 33 feet for the period from May through June; at depths below 33 feet, weekly changes of up to 3°C could occur in the thermocline.

Despite the bonytail chub's highly adaptable spawning technique developed for the [[Colorado River]], Lake Mohave appears to be a habitat so foreign to the species that spawning is not successful. Reasons for spawning failure are unknown but likely include lack of flowing water, the existing temperature regime, fluctuating lake levels, and predation by a host of non-native species.

## Key Findings

**Population Status:**
- The genetically pure population of [[Bonytail Chub]] in [[Lake Mohave]] represents the only known occurrence of this species
- Length-frequency data indicates no successful bonytail reproduction for at least 10-15 years, possibly since lake formation in the mid-1950s
- Remaining bonytail chubs in Lake Mohave are old, with mature individuals at least as old as the lake itself
- No investigator has found evidence of bonytail recruitment in Lake Mohave since closure of [[Davis Dam]]

**Project Impacts:**
- The proposed modification would increase daily water level fluctuations by 0.4 foot (on top of existing 1-2 foot fluctuations)
- Weekly temperature fluctuations of 1°C in the epilimnion (to 33 feet depth) and up to 3°C in the thermocline (below 33 feet) during May-June
- Increased bank erosion and impacts to spawning activities of Lake Mohave fishes anticipated

**Biological Opinion:**
- The proposed [[Hoover Powerplant]] modification is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
- Conclusion based on lack of evidence of bonytail recruitment in Lake Mohave and determination that the project will have no effect on already unsuitable environments
- Conditions in the lake are inadequate for successful spawning of bonytails and are not likely to become adequate in the future, with or without the proposed project

**Conservation Recommendations:**
- Impacts of the proposed project on Lake Mohave could potentially be used to promote conservation of bonytail chub and razorback sucker
- Placing or improving spawning habitat in the mouths of coves should not be difficult or expensive
- Fluctuations in water levels could be beneficial if gravel bars were placed to induce flow over bars during rises and falls in water level
- Service biologists available to discuss potential conservation measures

**Consultation History:**
- Initial evaluation (March 6, 1980) concluded no impact to listed species
- Supplemental biological assessment (December 17, 1980) specifically addressed bonytail chub and razorback sucker
- Formal Section 7 consultation requested October 16, 1981, after projected power generation peaking schedules changed
- Consultation extended to March 31, 1982 to allow aging of a Lake Mohave bonytail specimen by Dr. W. L. Minckley
- Aging could not be completed before consultation deadline; decision made to complete consultation without this single datum
