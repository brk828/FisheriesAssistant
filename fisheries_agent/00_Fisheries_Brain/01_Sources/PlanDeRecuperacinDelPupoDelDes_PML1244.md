# Desert Pupfish (Cyprinodon macularius) Conservation and Habitat Protection Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a Spanish-language conservation proposal and work plan for the [[Desert Pupfish]] ([[Cyprinodon Macularius]]), a small endemic fish of the Sonoran Desert. The plan targets remaining populations in [[Sonora]] and [[Baja California]], Mexico. It sets out a protection strategy, field surveys, habitat evaluation, community outreach, and a budget. The work is framed around the species' endangered status in the United States and its decline in Mexico. The document is partly degraded by OCR errors, so some dates, figures and names are uncertain and are flagged below.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The [[Desert Pupfish]] is a small cyprinodontid fish. The document refers to it as "pupfish" (pupo in the source), and the species is named after its macular (spotted) markings.
- **Earlier research:** The document cites earlier studies, including [[Miller]] (1943), and later work by McMahon and Miller (1987?), Miller and Fraga (1987?), and Varela-Romero (1986?). Some of these years are uncertain because of the OCR quality.
- **Legal status:** The species was assessed by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (the year is partly illegible in the OCR, with "198x" the most likely reading). The status supports a Mexican conservation effort that is coordinated with United States agencies.
- **Institutional partners:** The plan involves the [[World Wildlife Fund]] (WWF, read from the OCR as "tdtdF" and by context), state environmental and fisheries agencies in Arizona and California, and Mexican governmental and academic bodies.
- **Protected area context:** The proposal recognizes the [[Pinacate Biosphere Reserve]], which lies within the [[Pinacate and Gran Desierto]] region. The document also notes the Upper Gulf of California and Colorado River Delta Biosphere Reserve as a related framework. The proposal recommends that the Pinacate region be considered for inclusion in a broader protected-area network.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types:** Populations occupy small, shallow, warm waters, including springs, marshes, drains and ponds. The document refers to habitat as "ciénega" and "charcos" type sites.
- **Threats and drivers:** The text identifies drought, water diversion and extraction, habitat loss, and the introduction of non-native species as factors in the species' decline. Specific threats are implied rather than fully detailed in the readable text.
- **Water and habitat variables measured:** The habitat characterization covers water temperature, depth, pH, current velocity, substrate type, salinity and dissolved oxygen. Methods are based on [[Habitat Characterization]] protocols, with reference to Rinne's habitat methodology (year unclear in OCR).
- **Sampling units:** Fish sampling uses [[Electrofishing]] with portable equipment, with catch standardized per unit of effort. The document notes that the species is not the only fish collected, and that the survey also records other fish species.
- **Priority sites:** Candidate refuge areas are shown in Figure 1 as tentative zones in Sonora and Baja California. The main localities mentioned include [[Mexicali]], [[Laguna Salada]] and the [[Golfo de Santa Clara]] area. Black dots on the map mark localities with important abundances outside river habitats.

## Key Findings

1. **Priority species and protection goal:** The plan aims to protect remaining populations of the [[Desert Pupfish]] through habitat conservation, refuge establishment and population monitoring.

2. **Refuge network:** The proposal identifies tentative [[Refugio Areas]] in Sonora and Baja California for conserving pupfish populations. These areas are shown in Figure 1 and are to be validated through fieldwork.

3. **Monitoring program:** The plan includes periodic surveys of population status and habitat quality. Two field campaigns are described:
   - First campaign: spring 1990 (March to May, based on the OCR)
   - Second campaign: September to October 1990

4. **Action plan:** The main activities are:
   - Habitat improvement and water management in key sites
   - Periodic population evaluations
   - Habitat monitoring and protection in the Pinacate Biosphere Reserve
   - Outreach, including workshops, presentations and educational materials for local communities
   - Development of a conservation program for the Pinacate and Gran Desierto region
   - Preparation of a recovery plan and ecological assessment within the next year

5. **Community and education component:** The plan includes awareness materials (brochures, audiovisuals and information packs) and an educational program for local schools and communities.

6. **Budget:** A line-item budget covers field vehicles, photographic and recording equipment, consumables, office materials, reagents, personnel (including an investigation assistant) and outreach materials. The budget lists several line items; the grand total is not reliably legible in the OCR text.

7. **Timeline:** The document sets out a schedule for data collection, mapping of priority areas for the species (completed in late 1990 according to the OCR), field campaigns, and report preparation for the Mexican and U.S. agencies involved.

8. **Overall conclusion:** The proposal treats the [[Desert Pupfish]] as a conservation priority in the Sonoran Desert, and recommends coordinated refuge establishment, habitat restoration, and long-term monitoring across the United States–Mexico border region.
