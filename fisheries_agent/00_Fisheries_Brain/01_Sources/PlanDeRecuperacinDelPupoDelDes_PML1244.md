# Plan de Recuperación del Pupo del Desierto (Cyprinodon macularius)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This Spanish-language proposal, prepared by the [[Centro Ecológico de Sonora]] (CES), Área de Ecología Acuática, outlines a conservation plan for the [[Desert Pupfish]] ([[Cyprinodon Macularius]]) in the Sonoyta and Colorado river basins of Sonora and Baja California, Mexico. The document is dated January 1990 (the OCR reads "i730," which matches the 1990 calendar). The author listed on the cover is Paul C. Marsh.

The species was listed as Endangered by the [[International Union for Conservation of Nature]] in 1977 and by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in 1986. Little was known about its Mexican populations, so the plan proposes collaboration between Mexican researchers, the CES, and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The plan includes habitat characterization, population monitoring, a captive reserve population, reintroduction planning, habitat restoration, community outreach, protected-area proposals, a budget, and a timeline running from January 1990 to October 1991.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Cyprinodon macularius*, the Desert Pupfish, a small endemic fish of the Sonoran Desert region.
- **Conservation status:** Endangered under [[IUCN]] (1977) and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (1986) listings. The OCR text reads "1756" for the USFWS citation, which is most likely 1986.
- **Problem identified:** Mexican populations in the Sonoyta and Colorado rivers were poorly documented. Binational research had been underway in the United States, and the document notes that the species also occurs in the southwestern United States.
- **Research history:** The document relies on earlier work by Miller (1943), McMahon and Miller, Miller and Fuiman (1957), and Hendrickson and Varela-Romero (1986). The OCR does not reliably preserve all years, so these dates should be verified against the original reference list.
- **Binational context:** Mexican researchers began coordinating with the CES and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] around the late 1980s. The OCR reads the start year as "1927," which is most likely 1987. The stated goal is a Mexican conservation plan that parallels U.S. plans for the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat characterization.** The plan follows the methodology proposed by [[Rinne Habitat Protocol]] (Rinne, 1985). Field work will record:
- habitat type;
- water depth and current velocity, by life stage of the population;
- photographic series for a visual habitat record of the sampled sites.

**Protected-area framework.** Conservation efforts are linked to a state protected-area system proposed by the [[World Wildlife Fund]] (WWF) and others. The network covers:
- the [[Pinacate Biosphere Reserve]] region;
- the [[Colorado River Delta]] and the northern [[Gulf of California]];
- the Sonoyta and Colorado river corridors, which hold natural habitats for the pupfish.

The plan notes that the CES's administration of the Pinacate area, through the SEMDUE (OCR: "SEMUE"), would allow expansion of the originally proposed Pinacate Biosphere Reserve. That expansion would support specific actions for endangered species, including the pupfish.

**Threats and management needs** (inferred from the proposed actions): the plan calls for removing exotic fauna, securing water supply, and restoring degraded habitat. These measures suggest that invasive species, water availability, and habitat degradation are key pressures on the population.

## Key Findings

The OCR text is heavily degraded, so the points below are limited to content that can be read with reasonable confidence.

**1. Conservation actions for the Sonoyta River (Sonora):**
- Improve habitat in the Sonoyta River portion, including removing exotic fauna and securing water supply.
- Conduct periodic population evaluations.
- Maintain a reserve population of pupfish at CES facilities for breeding and reintroduction into historical habitats.
- Identify potential reintroduction zones if the existing populations are lost.
- Rehabilitate available habitat in support of the actions above.

**2. Community outreach and education:**
- Run an awareness campaign for residents, especially in the Sonoyta River zone.
- Produce audiovisual materials on the importance of native species and their protection.
- Give conferences in schools and to local leadership groups.
- Carry out a cleanup campaign as part of an environmental education program.

**3. Colorado River basin:**
- Hold an October workshop (year stated as the current year, probably 1990) to develop an Operational Plan for protecting and decreeing the Colorado River Delta and the northern Gulf of California as a Natural Protected Area.
