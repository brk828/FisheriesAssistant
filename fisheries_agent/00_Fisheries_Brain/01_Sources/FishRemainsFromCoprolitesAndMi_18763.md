# Fish Remains from Coprolites and Midden Deposits at Lovelock Cave, Churchill County, Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report by W. I. Follett identifies the fish remains recovered from human coprolites and midden deposits at [[Lovelock Cave]], Nevada, collected in June 1965 by the [[University of California Archaeological Research Facility]]. More than 13,000 skeletal remains, representing at least 306 small fish, came from 30 coprolites. A further 123 remains, representing at least 37 fish, came from midden refuse. Four species in two families were identified: [[Tui Chub]], [[Lahontan Speckled Dace]], [[Tahoe Sucker]] and [[Cui-ui]]. Most coprolite fish were small. The cui-ui and large chubs were probably transported from distant waters, and trout remains are absent despite their known use by the [[Northern Paiute]].

## Background & Description

**Study site.** Lovelock Cave lies in a limestone outcrop of the Humboldt Range at about 4200 feet elevation, roughly two miles east of the bed of [[Humboldt Lake]], the terminus of the [[Humboldt River]]. It is about 15 miles south-southwest of Lovelock.

**Sample and dating.** Coprolites were split into two lots:

| Lot | Coprolites | Context | Radiocarbon age (UCLA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interior | 16 | Dry cave interior | 1210 ± 60 years (756 A.D.) |
| Entrance | 14 | Dry, protected outer shelter | 145 ± 80 years (1821 A.D.) |

Midden material from the dry interior was estimated to date from about 1000 to 1500 A.D.

**Methods.** Identification relied mainly on [[Pharyngeal Teeth]], the most distinctive skeletal elements. Total length was taken as straight-line snout-to-caudal-tip length. Weights were approximations computed from skeletal length, and individuals of the same length could differ considerably in weight. Subspecies were distinguished partly by [[Gill Raker Counts]] and pharyngeal tooth formulae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat of the taxa.** The tui chub is known mainly from lakes of the [[Lake Lahontan]] system, with large individuals reaching 300 to 350 mm and occasionally 480 mm total length. Lahontan speckled dace inhabit streams and shallow lake margins. Tahoe suckers occur in streams and lakes. The cui-ui is restricted to remnants of ancient Lake Lahontan, principally [[Pyramid Lake]] and [[Winnemucca Lake]], with spawning migrations up the [[Truckee River]].

**Paleo-hydrology.** Radiocarbon dates on tufa at about 4,400 feet on Anaho Island, Pyramid Lake, place the last high stage of Lake Lahontan more than 11,000 years ago. Later dates and the Sequoia tree-ring curve indicate low lake levels during the last two millennia. Calculations cited by Broecker and Orr suggest the lake could return to near its present size within about 200 years if filled to maximum level. From 1000 to 1500 A.D., Humboldt Lake was probably a shallow, intermittent lake fringed by tules. Such a habitat would not have sustained cui-ui.

**Aboriginal fishing.** Ethnographic sources describe the Northern Paiute and neighbouring groups netting and hooking fish, including drying large quantities of cui-ui and trout during spawning runs. The author notes a bone-free preparation method for cui-ui, in which two boneless fillets were cut from the back.

## Key Findings

**1. Species composition.** Four taxa were identified: tui chub (*Gila bicolor*, with subspecies *obesa* and *pectinifer* recognised), Lahontan speckled dace (*Rhinichthys osculus robustus*), Tahoe sucker (*Catostomus tahoensis*) and cui-ui (*Chasmistes cujus*).

**2. Tui chub dominance in coprolites.** Coprolite material contained 474 pharyngeals (230 complete, 244 incomplete), 2.6 to 17.2 mm long. These represent at least 298 fish, about 38 to 239 mm long and about 0.2 to 164.0 g. Of 467 pharyngeals under 10 mm, the remains represent at least 292 fish under 142 mm and under 28 g.

**3. Small coprolite fish.** Of the 298 coprolite fish, 292 tui chubs ranged from about 38 to 139 mm. The 5 speckled dace were 68 to 79 mm long. The 3 Tahoe suckers were 150 to 210 mm long. Follett argues that the minnows were likely taken in Humboldt Lake or the lower Humboldt River.

**4. Large fish in midden deposits.** Midden material represents about 22 tui chubs (52 to 380 mm), seven Tahoe suckers (per table 3) and eight cui-ui (about 480 to 650 mm, about 1.2 to 3.2 kg). The large tui chubs and cui-ui probably came from Pyramid Lake, Winnemucca Lake or the lower Truckee River. Cui-ui were the largest remains found, and their presence is taken as evidence of transport from the [[Truckee River]] region, roughly 55 miles away at Wadsworth.

**5. Cooking and processing.** Of 224 pharyngeals from coprolites 10D and 56D, 140 are complete. Follett concludes that fish heads were swallowed whole, and some remains show signs of cooking, including a charred cleithrum from coprolite 48D.

**6. Absence of trout.** The collection contains no trout remains, despite the Northern Paiute's known use of trout and the abundance of Lahontan cutthroat trout remains at the [[Falcon Hill Sites]], where they nearly equalled all other fishes combined. Possible explanations include summer temperatures too high for cutthroat trout in shallow Humboldt Lake, and the likelihood that trout were eaten on site or not traded. The author describes the absence as "perplexing" and concludes that any explanation is conjectural.

**7. Conclusions on provenience.** Follett concludes that small minnows were probably captured locally, and that cui-ui were transported from the [[Lahontan Basin]] range of the species, most likely the lower Truckee River during spawning migration. Middle-sized chubs and suckers remain of indeterminate origin.
