# New Locality Records for Some Kansas Fishes, With Notes on the Habitat of the Arkansas Darter

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae #Cyprinidae #Sciaenidae

## Executive Summary
This 1981 paper by Matthews and McDaniel reports new locality records for four fish species in Kansas based on fieldwork conducted 12–16 June 1978. The most significant findings concern the [[Arkansas Darter]] (*Etheostoma cragini*), a species of special conservation concern (state-designated as threatened in Kansas in 1978), which was found at three localities exhibiting atypical, non-spring habitats — including turbid mainstream river conditions. These records challenge prior assumptions about the species' intolerance of turbid water and support the hypothesis that *E. cragini* can survive, at least temporarily, in sub-optimal environments. The paper also documents the first Kansas record of *Aplodinotus grunniens* (Freshwater Drum) from the Arkansas River mainstream, the first Jefferson County record of *Notropis umbratilis* (Redfin Shiner), and the first Walnut River drainage record of *Notropis buchanani* (Ghost Shiner).

## Background & Description
- **[[Arkansas Darter]]** (*Etheostoma cragini*): Small percid darter; specimens collected were juveniles/small adults ranging **25–27 mm Standard Length (S.L.)**.
  - Specimen 1: 25 mm S.L. (WJM 313; KU 18629)
  - Specimen 2: 26 mm S.L. (WJM 320; KU 18630)
  - Specimen 3: 27 mm S.L. (WJM 318; KU 18631)
- **[[Freshwater Drum]]** (*Aplodinotus grunniens*): Juvenile specimen collected, **15 mm S.L.** (KU 18632), notably small size indicating recent recruitment into the mainstream habitat.
- **[[Redfin Shiner]]** (*Notropis umbratilis*): One adult specimen collected (WJM 295; KU 18633); no additional morphometric data provided.
- **[[Ghost Shiner]]** (*Notropis buchanani*): Four specimens collected (WJM 322; KU 18634); no additional morphometric data provided.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
### Etheostoma cragini — Habitat Notes
- **Typical habitat**: Endemic to the [[Arkansas River]] basin; historically restricted to isolated colonies in small **spring runs overgrown with watercress** (per Cross 1967; Cloutman 1970).
- **Atypical habitat records (this study)**:
  1. **Rattlesnake Creek** (Stafford County): 10–15 m wide, ~1 m deep, mud bottom, **very turbid**, no flow at capture site; drains pastureland. Co-occurring fishes: *Notropis lutrensis* (112), *Notropis stramineus* (3), *Cyprinus carpio* (1).
  2. **Arkansas River mainstream at Oxford** (Sumner County): ~200 m wide, deep, swift, **highly turbid**; captured in moderate current over silty sand bottom near shore. Co-occurring fishes: *N. lutrensis* (7), *Pimephales vigilax* (1), *Notropis atherinoides* (2), *Dorosoma cepedianum* (1), *Aplodinotus grunniens* (1).
  3. **Chikaskia River** (Harper-Sumner county line): ~40 m wide, >1 m deep, **clear water**, unstable sand bottom, pastureland drainage, no nearby springs evident. Co-occurring fishes: *N. lutrensis* (94), *N. stramineus* (3), *Campostoma anomalum* (4), *Pimephales vigilax* (1), *Gambusia affinis* (1).
- **Ecological interpretation**: These records challenge the view (Cloutman 1970) that *E. cragini* is intolerant of turbid water. Authors argue specimens are unlikely to be recent "waifs" flushed from springs, since no significant rain events preceded collection. This suggests genuine, if temporary, tolerance for turbid, sluggish, or mainstream riverine conditions — an ability that could be **critical during drought conditions** when spring runs desiccate.
- Cross (1967) had previously noted a single *E. cragini* captured from the Arkansas River mainstream, corroborating occasional mainstream tolerance.

### Aplodinotus grunniens
- Collected as bycatch with *E. cragini* in the Arkansas River mainstream at Oxford, Sumner County — the **first record of this species from the Arkansas River mainstream** in Kansas (previously known only from tributaries within the drainage).

### Notropis umbratilis
- Collected from an unnamed channelized ditch tributary of the [[Kansas River]] near Williamstown, Jefferson County.
- Rarely reported from tributaries **north** of the Kansas River; this constitutes the **first record for Jefferson County**.

### Notropis buchanani
- Collected from the [[Walnut River]], 300 m downstream of a dam near Winfield, Cowley County.
- Not previously reported in this drainage despite a thorough prior survey (Metcalf 1959); previously known from nearby Grouse Creek and the Arkansas River (Sumner County).

## Key Findings
- **Three new atypical-habitat records** for the threatened [[Arkansas Darter]] (*Etheostoma cragini*) in Kansas, spanning turbid mainstream river, turbid creek, and clear-water river settings — none matching the species' typical spring-run/watercress habitat.
- Findings **contradict prior assumptions** (Cloutman 1970) that *E. cragini* cannot tolerate turbid water, suggesting instead a capacity for temporary survival in suboptimal, turbid, or sluggish environments.
- This tolerance may have **conservation significance**, potentially aiding survival during drought when preferred spring habitats become desiccated.
- **First record** of *Aplodinotus grunniens* from the Arkansas River mainstream in Kansas.
- **First Jefferson County record** of *Notropis umbratilis*, extending known range to tributaries north of the Kansas River.
- **First Walnut River drainage record** of *Notropis buchanani*, filling a gap despite earlier thorough surveys of the area.
- Study reinforces the value of targeted fieldwork in refining distribution maps established by foundational works such as Cross (1967) and Cross & Collins (1975).
