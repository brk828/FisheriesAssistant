# Twenty-Second Annual Desert Fishes Council Symposium Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #conservation #symposium

## Executive Summary

This document is the program for the 22nd Annual Symposium of the Desert Fishes Council (El Consejo de los Peces del Desierto), held November 14-17, 1990, at the Facultad de Ciencias, Universidad Autónoma de Baja California, Ensenada, Baja California, Mexico. The symposium featured 36 scientific presentations covering desert fish conservation, genetics, ecology, and management across Mexico and the United States, with a focus on endangered species and transboundary conservation efforts. The program included government agency reports, technical presentations, a banquet, business session, and a scientific field excursion to [[Arroyo San Rafael]] in the [[Sierra San Pedro Martir]].

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Council is an organization dedicated to the preservation of desert fishes of the Americas ("Dedicado a la Preservación de los Peces de los Desiertos de América"). This symposium brought together researchers, government representatives, and conservation professionals from both Mexico and the United States to address conservation challenges facing desert aquatic ecosystems.

The symposium was structured over four days:
- Wednesday, November 14: Social gathering (1830)
- Thursday, November 15: Registration, introduction, government reports, and presentations 2-13
- Friday, November 16: Presentations 14-36 and business session
- Saturday, November 17: Scientific field excursion to [[Arroyo San Rafael]]

Session chairs included Carlos Yruretagoyena (Patronato para la Protección y Reforestación de los Bosques de Baja California), Lloyd T. Findley (Tecnológico de Monterrey, Guaymas), Francisco Abarca (Arizona Game and Fish Department), and Dean A. Hendrickson (University of Texas, Austin). The business session was presided over by Jack E. Williams (President) and Phil Pister (Executive Secretary) of the Desert Fishes Council.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The presentations covered diverse desert aquatic ecosystems across the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, including:

**Geographic regions**: [[Colorado River]] basin, [[Rio Grande]], [[Dolores River]] (Colorado and Utah), [[Owens Valley]] (California), [[Gulf of California]], [[Mohave Desert]], [[Ash Meadows]] (Nevada), [[Aravaipa Creek]] (Arizona), [[Little Colorado River]], and various locations in [[Baja California]], [[Sonora]], [[Chihuahua]], [[Puebla]], and [[Oaxaca]].

**Habitat types**: Desert springs, intermittent streams, rivers, coastal waters, and isolated aquatic refugia. Many presentations addressed habitats supporting endangered or threatened species requiring specialized conservation management.

**Conservation threats**: Habitat degradation, introduced species (particularly hatchery trout and salmon), genetic impacts from non-native fish, flash floods, water resource management, and transboundary conservation challenges.

## Key Findings

**Major taxonomic groups addressed**:

1. **Endangered/threatened native fishes**: [[Totoaba]] (*Totoaba macdonaldi*), [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Rio Grande Shiner]] (*Notropis jemezanus*), [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Mohave Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor mohavensis*), [[Warner Sucker]], [[Owens Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)

2. **Gila species complex**: Multiple presentations on *Gila* species including *G. intermedia*, *G. purpurea*, *G. bicolor*, and morphometric variation in [[Colorado River]] *Gila* species

3. **Trout populations**: [[Nelson's Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss nelsoni*) from [[Sierra San Pedro Martir]], with studies on home range, sexual maturity, and parasites; genetic studies of [[Cutthroat Trout]]

4. **Livebearers**: *Gambusia* predation behavior studies

**Key research themes**:

- **Genetics and molecular biology**: Mitochondrial DNA restriction site mapping in cutthroat trout, PCR amplification and DNA sequencing techniques, DNA isolation from formalin-preserved specimens, conservation genetics of tui chubs, genetic diversity in small isolated populations, genetic interactions between wild and hatchery rainbow trout

- **Species status and distribution**: Rediscovery of *Gila intermedia* and *G. purpurea* in northern Sonora, status surveys of Chihuahua chubs across US-Mexico border, distribution and abundance surveys in [[Ash Meadows]], freshwater fishes of Sonora

- **Reproductive biology**: Reproductive behavior of [[Totoaba]] in the Gulf of California, age and size at first sexual maturity in Nelson's trout

- **Ecology and behavior**: Chemoreception and imprinting in Colorado squawfish recovery, intraspecific predation in *Gambusia*, potamodromous fish migration in [[Aravaipa Creek]], predation potential of aquatic insects (Libellulidae, Coenagrionidae, Hydrophilidae, Notonectidae, Corixidae)

- **Conservation management**: Pupfish habitat improvement at [[Quitobaquito]] (Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument), preserving Mohave tui chub at China Lake, water resources management for tui chub sites, refugium populations for Warner sucker

- **Environmental impacts**: Effects of flash floods on desert pupfish populations, ecological and genetic effects of hatchery trout and salmon introductions, habitat suitability assessments for endangered Colorado River fishes

- **Biogeography**: Probable origins of disjunct fish distributions in northern Mexico, fishes of tropical deserts, regional ichthyofaunas of Sierra Mixteca and Baja California Sur

- **Methodological advances**: Micromethod for culturing teleost fish leukocytes, morphometric variation analysis, observed growth measurements in humpback chub individuals

**Transboundary conservation**: The opening presentation "Futuro Sin Fronteras: México-E.U.A." emphasized international collaboration for desert fish conservation, reflected in numerous binational research teams and cross-border species management efforts.
