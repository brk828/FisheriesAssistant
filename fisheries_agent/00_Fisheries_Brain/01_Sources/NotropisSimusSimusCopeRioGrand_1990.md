## Executive Summary

This entry synthesizes taxonomic and conservation data for two imperiled cyprinid taxa native to New Mexico: the **Rio Grande bluntnose shiner** (*[[Notropis Simus Simus]]*) and the **beautiful shiner** (*[[Cyprinella Formosa]]*). Both species/subspecies are documented as extirpated from their native New Mexico ranges, driven primarily by anthropogenic hydrological alteration—specifically periodic dewatering, ephemeral stream flow, and water diversion for agriculture. *N. s. simus* is presumed extinct within the Rio Grande drainage, while *C. formosa* persists in Mexican drainage systems outside New Mexico, though it is federally listed as threatened.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological descriptions (meristics, coloration, body shape) are not detailed in this excerpt; the text is primarily taxonomic/nomenclatural and distributional. Historical taxonomic treatments indicate:

- ***Notropis simus simus***: Originally described as *Alburnellus simus* (Cope, 1876); later reclassified under *Notropis (Hudsonius)* and finally as a subspecies within *[[Notropis Simus]]* (Chernoff et al. 1982).
- ***Cyprinella formosa***: Originally described as *Moniana formosa* (Girard, 1857); subsequently treated as *Notropis formosus* and *Notropis lutrensis formosus* before consensus settled on species-level recognition under *[[Cyprinella]]*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Notropis Simus Simus]]** (Rio Grande bluntnose shiner):
  - Type locality: Rio Grande at San Ildefonso, Santa Fe County, New Mexico.
  - Historic range: Rio Grande drainage in New Mexico, specifically the reach between Otowi Bridge and Cochiti Lake.
  - Last confirmed collection: 1964, near Peña Blanca, Sandoval County (collected by R.D. Suttkus).
  - Habitat: Main-channel riverine habitat within the Rio Grande, susceptible to periodic dewatering.

- **[[Cyprinella Formosa]]** (beautiful shiner):
  - Type locality: Rio Mimbres, originally attributed to Chihuahua, later corrected to Mimbres River, Luna County, New Mexico (Gilbert 1978).
  - New Mexico range: Endemic to the [[Mimbres River]] system; now extirpated.
  - Broader range (extant): Mexican drainage systems including the Rio Casas Grandes, Rio Santa Maria, and Rio del Carmen (Guzman basin), as well as the Yaqui, Bavícora, and Sauz basins (excluding the extreme northern Yaqui basin).
  - Threats: Ephemeral stream flow (notably downstream of Dwyer, NM) linked to drought and agricultural water diversion.

## Key Findings

- ***Notropis simus simus*** is considered functionally **extinct** in the Rio Grande drainage of New Mexico, with no successful detections despite recent intensive surveys; last verified record dates to 1964.
- Listed as **State Endangered, Group I** in New Mexico (24 January 1975) and placed under federal "notice of review" status for potential threatened/endangered listing (18 September 1985).
- Primary driver of decline: periodic **dewatering** of Rio Grande habitat over the preceding ~40 years (pre-1990).
- ***Cyprinella formosa*** is **extirpated from New Mexico** (Mimbres River population), with decline attributed to drought-driven ephemeral flows and agricultural water diversion, particularly downstream of Dwyer.
- *C. formosa* was listed as **Federal Threatened** on 31 August 1984.
- Taxonomic note: Contreras-Balderas (1975) proposed that Guzman/Yaqui/Bavícora/Sauz basin populations represent a subspecies of *[[Cyprinella Lutrensis]]*, but subsequent authors—and this text—retain *C. formosa* as a distinct species for nomenclatural stability.
- Both taxa exemplify broader patterns of imperilment among Chihuahuan Desert and Rio Grande basin cyprinids linked to water extraction and habitat fragmentation; see also [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]] and other [[Chihuahuan Desert Fishes]] for comparative context.
