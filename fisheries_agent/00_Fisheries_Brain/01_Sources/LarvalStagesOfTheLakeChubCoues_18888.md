# Larval Stages of the Lake Chub (*Couesius plumbeus*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study describes the posthatching larval development of the [[Lake Chub]] (*Couesius plumbeus*), a freshwater cyprinid widely distributed across Canada and northern United States. Eggs collected from a New York stream were laboratory reared, and larvae were described mainly by external morphology, pigmentation, myomere counts, and morphometrics. The authors identify a combination of characters (high preanal myomere count, a diagonal breast melanophore row, and a caudal peduncle melanophore concentration) that is unique among known North American cyprinid larvae. They also compare *C. plumbeus* with its spawning associate [[Longnose Dace]] (*Rhinichthys cataractae*) and other sympatric cyprinids with high myomere counts.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The lake chub has had numerous generic assignments, reflecting uncertain classification. [[Wells 1978]] proposed that it is most closely related to [[Semotilus Margarita]]. The study aims were to describe gross morphological and pigmentary characteristics of posthatching ontogeny and to compare these with other sympatric cyprinids that have published larval descriptions.

**Collection and rearing.**
- Eggs were collected on 13 and 25 June 1977 and 18 May 1978 from [[Sucker Brook]], [[South Lake]], [[Herkimer County]], New York.
- Eggs were scattered in riffles for about 20 m upstream of the lake and collected by dip net after the gravel was churned.
- Water temperature at collection was 12 to 13°C. *C. plumbeus* was the only cyprinid reported from the system, and identification was confirmed by rearing eggs to juveniles.
- In 1977, eggs were held in an ambient water bath (13°C on 25 June, 20°C on 17 July, 17°C on 28 July) and then moved to laboratory conditions at 20°C. The 1978 eggs were reared entirely at 20°C.
- Specimens were preserved in 5% formalin buffered with sodium borate. A total of 26 eggs and 147 larvae and juveniles were preserved.
- Specimens were deposited in the [[Cornell University Ichthyological Collection]] (CU 55724, 56070, 64931).

**Eggs.** Eggs are demersal and non-adhesive, with dark golden yolk, no oil globules, and an unsculptured chorion. Diameter is 1.8 to 2.4 mm (N = 18, mean = 2.1) after fertilization. Brown et al. (1970) reported 2.30 mm.

**Larval diagnosis.** Larvae have 26 to 28 preanal myomeres and a melanophore row descending anteriorly across the lateral breast surface. Individuals over 10 mm usually show a concentration of melanophores on the distal hypaxial caudal peduncle. This combination is described as unique among North American cyprinid larvae currently known. (The abstract gives a broader range of 25 to 30 preanal myomeres.)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Developmental milestones.** Stage lengths are given as the smallest individual with a structure and the largest without it:
- **Newly hatched (5.8 to 6.4 mm TL):** small pectoral buds present, yolk sac bulbous anteriorly and tubular posteriorly, head deflected ventrally over the yolk sac until about 6.5 mm.
- **Yolk absorption:** 7.4 to 7.6 mm (about 7 days).
- **Functional mouth parts:** about 8.0 mm.
- **First caudal fin rays ossified:** 7.9 to 9.5 mm.
- **Caudal fin emarginate:** 9.7 to 10.1 mm.
- **Pelvic buds formed:** about 11.3 mm; first pelvic fin rays about 12.0 mm.
- **Scales:** first individual with scales at 29 mm (caudal peduncle only); squamation complete after 31 mm.
- **Fin fold absorption:** 17.5 to 19.1 mm.

Fin ray formation proceeds caudal, then dorsal, then anal, with paired fin rays developing last.

**Pigmentation sequence.** Pigment develops from sparse at hatching to dense occipital pigmentation and dorsal melanophore rows by 6.4 mm. The breast row joins across the midventral line by 7.6 mm. Snout and pericardial pigment appear around 7.4 to 8.7 mm (about 8 days). Hypaxial caudal peduncle pigment appears at 9.2 to 10.0 mm (about 20 days). By 15 mm, the caudal peduncle has two small spots, the more prominent one on hypaxial musculature. Juveniles (20 to 24 mm) show uniformly scattered dorsal pigment and a faded ventral pattern.

**Meristics.**
- Myomere number did not change after hatching.
- Preanal myomeres: 27.3 ± 1.0; postanal: 14.5 ± 1.0; total: 41.8 ± 1.1.
- Total vertebrae: 41 to 43 (N = 26, mean 42.3 ± 0.5), counted from radiographs of adults (50 to 100 mm SL) from Maine and northern New York.

**Morphometric allometry (log-regression slopes; values near 1.00 indicate isometry):**

| Relationship | Slope |
|---|---|
| Standard vs. total length | 0.88 |
| Preanal vs. total length | 0.83 |
| Head vs. total length (≤ 8.7 mm TL) | 1.33 |
| Head vs. total length (> 8.7 mm TL) | 1.14 |
| Eye diameter vs. total length | 1.02 |
| Body depth vs. total length | 1.20 |
| Eye diameter vs. head length | 0.79 |
| Body depth vs. head length | 0.93 |

Head length shows two growth stanzas, with the break at about 8.7 mm. Eye diameter relative to total length is nearly isometric.

## Key Findings

1. **Diagnostic characters.** The combination of 25 to 30 (abstract) or 26 to 28 (diagnosis) preanal myomeres, a diagonal breast melanophore row, and a hypaxial caudal peduncle melanophore concentration distinguishes larval *C. plumbeus*.

2. **Comparison with [[Campostoma Anomalum]].** This species has fewer postanal myomeres (11 to 15, modally 12), lacks the diagonal breast row, and is larger at hatching (6.3 to 6.9 mm) and yolk absorption (9.2 mm). These differences contrast with *C. plumbeus*.

3. **Comparison with [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] and [[Semotilus Corporalis]].** These species share high preanal myomere counts but differ in pigmentation: head pigment is scattered in *S. atromaculatus*, and the hypaxial caudal spot does not develop. Both *Semotilus* species are larger than *C. plumbeus* at comparable stages. [[Kranz et al. 1979]] reported yolk absorption, first caudal ray formation, and pelvic bud formation at 9.6, 9.0 to 10.0, and 14 to 15 mm, all later than in *C. plumbeus*.

4. **Comparison with [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]].** This spawning associate has larger eggs (2.3 to 2.7 mm), with yolk absorption, first caudal rays, and pelvic buds at 9.4, 9.4, and 12.0 mm versus 7.5, 8.7, and 11.3 mm in *C. plumbeus*. Its modal preanal myomere count is 26, but ranges overlap (as few as 24 reported). It lacks the midventral breast series and the larger-individual caudal spot. Relative eye size is reversed compared with adults: *C. plumbeus* larvae have eye diameter near 7.3% TL, while *R. cataractae* larvae have about 8% TL.

5. **Predicted larval traits of [[Semotilus Margarita]].** Larvae of this species have not been described. The authors predict fewer preanal myomeres than *C. plumbeus*, based on vertebral counts (less than 41 in *S. margarita* versus 42 to 43 in related *Semotilus* species) and the assumption that vertebra counts reflect myomere distribution.

6. **Conclusion.** Myomere number is a powerful character for separating cyprinid groups. The study provides a diagnostic larval description and comparative framework for identifying *C. plumbeus* larvae in sympatry with other high-myomere cyprinids.
