# Spinal Deformity in Threadfin Shad in Concrete-Lined Canals, with Two Related Notes (1983)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This short "Notes" section from the *Southwestern Naturalist* (vol. 28, no. 1, February 1983) contains three independent items. The main note reports an unusually high incidence of spinal deformity (lordosis) in [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) from concrete-lined canals in California and Arizona, compared with fish from earthen canals and a reservoir. The first item, a note on plant populations at three grazed desert sites, reports populations out of equilibrium with reduced reproductive success. The last item reports range expansion of the cladoceran *Leptodora kindtii* in the southern United States, and the text is truncated.

## Background & Description

**Threadfin Shad** had spread since its introduction in the 1950s to inhabit most warmwater habitats in Arizona and California ([[Minckley Fishes of Arizona]]; [[Moyle Inland Fishes of California]]). No spinal deformities had previously been reported in the species, although such anomalies are known in other clupeids and in other fishes. Wild-population frequencies of deformity are generally a few percent or less.

**Methods (shad study):** Shad were collected by hand, dip net and seine from three concrete-lined canals: [[Coachella Canal]] (Imperial Co., California, 2 Nov. 1981) and the South and Tempe canals (Maricopa Co., Arizona, 10–11 Oct. 1981). Comparison samples came from the earthen Coachella and Eastern canals and [[Apache Lake]]. Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin, transferred to 70% ethanol, measured for total length (TL), weighed, examined externally for anomalies, and radiographed for vertebral examination. Collections were compared on deformity frequency, condition factor (K) and length–weight relationships (log WT = m·log TL + b), following [[Ricker 1975]]. No correction was made for preservation effects on TL or weight.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Canal ecology:** Concrete-lined canals in Maricopa County are drained annually and shad rarely survive the dewatering period. The [[Coachella Canal]] was first used in November 1980, so the shad sampled in 1981 must have immigrated from confluent waters. Most fish in the Maricopa canals therefore enter each year from upstream rivers or impoundments. Most shad from the Coachella Canal were estimated at roughly 1–2 years old from length-frequency analysis (Johnson, 1969 dissertation, Arizona State University).

**Plant populations:** The second note concerns three sites with populations that were clearly not in equilibrium. Reproductive success was lower than it once was. Because the sites occupy very different habitats, the authors considered climatic change or a general ecosystem process as causes. All three sites had been heavily grazed, and two (White Sands and Organ Mountains) lie near former ranch facilities. The authors suggested that the effects of grazing onset, previously documented in [[Southwestern Grazing History]] studies, may continue as subtle, unrecognized change or recovery. They called for a more precise correlation with actual plant ages.

## Key Findings

### 1. High Incidence of Spinal Deformity in Concrete-Lined Canals

- Spinal deformity (mild to relatively severe lordosis) occurred in **21.3%** of shad from the Tempe Canal, **26.7%** from the South Canal and **40.7%** from the Coachella Canal.
- These values were far higher than those from the earthen Coachella and Eastern canals and Apache Lake, which showed only a minor incidence of deformity.
- Deformed and normal fish from the same populations did not differ significantly in mean TL, weight, condition factor (K) or length–weight relationship. Differences among populations therefore could not be attributed to deformity incidence.
- The authors concluded that concrete lining alone could not explain the deformities, because shad in Maricopa canals enter annually from outside the canal system. The deformities must have resulted from a factor unrelated to canal lining, probably acting on egg or early embryonic stages.

### 2. Comparative Deformity Frequencies in Clupeids and Other Fishes

- Wild-population incidence in clupeids is typically low: about **1.0%** for [[Atlantic Menhaden]], and comparable low values for [[American Shad]] and [[Blueback Herring]].
- Higher wild frequencies occur in other fishes, ranging from about **10%** in adult salmonids to about **16%** in young [[Roundtail Chub]].
- Frequencies above **70%** have been recorded in laboratory or hatchery stocks.

### 3. Plant Population Patterns at Grazed Desert Sites

- Populations at three sites were out of equilibrium, with reduced reproductive success compared with earlier conditions.
- Grazing history and possible ecosystem-wide or climatic drivers were proposed as causes, pending more precise plant-age data.

### 4. Range Expansion of *Leptodora kindtii* in the Southern United States

- Recent reports had documented the large predatory cladoceran *Leptodora kindtii* in Arkansas, Kansas, Oklahoma and Texas, and the note extends this to southern Louisiana. The text ends before the full findings are given, so the details are incomplete.
