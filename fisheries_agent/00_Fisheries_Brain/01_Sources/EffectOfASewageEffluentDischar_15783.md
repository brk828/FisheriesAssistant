# Effect of Sewage Effluent on Calcium Carbonate Deposition on Shells of Potamopyrgus Jenkinsi

#malacology #freshwater-ecology #potamopyrgus-jenkinsi

## Executive Summary

During an ecological survey of the [[River Ivel]], a [[Chalk Stream]] in Bedfordshire, researchers found that shells of the freshwater snail [[Potamopyrgus Jenkinsi]] were heavily encrusted with calcareous nodules upstream of a [[Sewage Effluent]] discharge but clean downstream. Field data, caging experiments and laboratory trials indicate that constituents of sewage effluent inhibit the deposition and aggregation of [[Calcium Carbonate Deposition|calcium carbonate]] under conditions resembling the River Ivel. The authors suggest that condensed phosphates and organic suspenders, such as [[Sodium Carboxymethyl Cellulose]], are likely contributors, and that other, unidentified inhibitors are probably also present.

## Background & Description

The study is a short reprinted research note (from *Nature*, 1960) by R. W. Edwards of the Water Pollution Research Laboratory, Stevenage, and J. Heywood of the Zoology Department, University of Reading.

The focus organism is the snail *Potamopyrgus jenkinsi*. Its shells were used as a substrate on which calcareous encrustations formed. The note also refers to earlier observations of similar encrustations on this snail in a limestone district of Derbyshire, reported by Warwick, where no habitat effect was found.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Field conditions (River Ivel):**
- Snails above the effluent were more than 70 per cent heavier than those below it, as stated in the source.
- Table 1 (average of 12 determinations):

| Location | Shell aperture width (mm) | Dry weight (mg) | Calcium carbonate (mg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Above effluent | 1.50 | 2.98 | 2.44 |
| Below effluent | 1.46 | 1.78 | 1.42 |

- Snails taken from below the discharge and caged above it developed encrustations within three weeks.
- Glass slides immersed upstream of the effluent also received deposits.
- River pH ranged from 7.9 to 8.3 across several measurements. Total hardness was about 290 ppm as calcium carbonate.
- The algal flora on snail shells was dominated by *Cocconeis* and *Chantransia* both upstream and downstream.
- The effluent was described as of good quality and made up 5 to 10 per cent of dry summer flow.

**Effluent chemistry (Table 2):** The analysis covered organic carbon, ammonia, nitrite, oxidized nitrogen, phosphate, synthetic detergent ([[Manoxol O.T.]]), permanganate value, 8-day [[Biochemical Oxygen Demand]] (BOD) and suspended solids. Several values are hard to read in the scanned table, so consult the original before quoting them. The clearer figures in the text are noted under Key Findings.

**Laboratory experiments:** Glass slides were immersed in five treatments:
- (a) river water
- (b) river water plus sewage effluent (5% by volume)
- (c) river water plus synthetic sewage effluent without synthetic detergents (5% by volume)
- (d) river water plus sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (5 ppm)
- (e) river water plus [[Sodium Hexametaphosphate]] (1 ppm as phosphorus), at pH 8.2 and 8.5

## Key Findings

- **Encrustation is inhibited by effluent.** Snail shells were encrusted with calcareous nodules above the discharge but clean below it. Snails from below the effluent developed encrustations within three weeks when caged above it.
- **Snails above the effluent were heavier.** Table 1 gives dry weights of 2.98 mg above and 1.78 mg below the discharge. Calcium carbonate content was 2.44 mg and 1.42 mg, respectively. The source describes the upstream snails as more than 70 per cent heavier. The tabulated means differ by about 67 per cent, so the reported figure should be checked against the raw data.
- **Deposition occurred only in the absence of additives.** In the laboratory, deposition occurred only on slides in plain river water.
- **Effluent composition.** The effluent sample contained 52 ppm total organic carbon, 4.5 ppm synthetic anionic detergent (as Manoxol O.T.) and 5.2 ppm phosphate (as P). The synthetic effluent contained 24 ppm total organic carbon and 2.6 ppm phosphate (as P).
- **Likely inhibitors.** Condensed phosphates, which sequester calcium and magnesium and act as dispersants, and organic soil suspenders such as [[Sodium Carboxymethyl Cellulose]] are probably important. Because the synthetic effluent without detergents also inhibited deposition, other agents are likely present as well.
- **Parallel observations.** Alekin and Moritschewa, working on the Don and Volga, reached similar conclusions. They proposed that certain organic substances, particularly [[Humus]] of plant origin, inhibit calcium carbonate deposition.
- **Scope.** The study is a short observational and experimental note. It identifies a plausible link between sewage effluent constituents and reduced calcareous encrustation on a freshwater snail, but it does not identify the specific inhibitors.
