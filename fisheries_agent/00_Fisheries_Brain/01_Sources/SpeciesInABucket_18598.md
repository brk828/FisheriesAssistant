# Species in a Bucket: Saving the Owens Pupfish from Extinction

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This essay, written by retired [[California Department of Fish and Game]] district fishery biologist Edwin Philip "Phil" Pister, recounts his career managing fisheries and desert aquatic habitats in eastern California and reflects on the conservation of endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] species. The centerpiece is an August 18, 1969 rescue at [[Fish Slough]], where Pister and colleagues moved roughly 800 [[Owens Pupfish]] from a drying pond into cages and buckets, narrowly averting extinction. The piece also covers the [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] and its 1976 U.S. Supreme Court case, and argues that conservation values should guide public policy and that scientists must engage with species preservation.

The article is a personal essay and reflection, not a research study. It contains no new quantitative dataset.

## Background & Description

- **Author:** Edwin Philip Pister, Executive Secretary of the [[Desert Fishes Council]] in Bishop, California; career began in 1951 with the California Department of Fish and Game, after studying wildlife conservation at Berkeley in 1948 under [[A. Starker Leopold]].
- **Scope of career:** responsibility for nearly 1,000 bodies of water from the Sierra Nevada crest to the Nevada state line, including a legendary reservoir trout fishery and a twenty-seven-year project to restore the [[California Golden Trout]] within the Golden Trout Wilderness.
- **Pupfish family:** Pupfishes belong to the killifish family (Cyprinodontidae), are named for their playful behavior, and are popular in the aquarium trade. The Death Valley system contains nine pupfish species.
- **Owens pupfish (*Cyprinodon radiosus*):** the largest Death Valley pupfish, occasionally reaching two inches. It was believed extinct when scientifically described in 1948.
- **Devil's Hole pupfish (*Cyprinodon diabolis*):** rarely exceeds one inch. It has been isolated for approximately 44,000 years in a pool roughly 10 by 50 feet, within a cavern at a constant 92°F, likely the most confined habitat of any vertebrate.
- **Feeding and behavior:** all pupfishes are opportunistic feeders on immature insects and algae and are highly territorial.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pleistocene origins:** During Pleistocene pluvial periods, much of the [[Great Basin]] was covered by freshwater lakes. As the Holocene arrived, these shrank into isolated permanent habitats, leaving fishes confined to "islands of water in a sea of sand."
- **Comparative diversity:** Pister notes that only Cuatro Ciénegas, Coahuila, Mexico, matches the number of well-defined local populations in North America; the Death Valley drainage is compared to Darwin's [[Galapagos Islands]].
- **Extreme tolerances:** some pupfishes live in water above 100°F and tolerate up to 113°F briefly, with daily fluctuations of as much as 36°. Others inhabit pools with several times seawater salinity. The Owens pupfish survives winter ice covering of an inch or two when air temperatures fall below zero.
- **Threats:** habitat alteration and introduced predatory gamefish (notably [[Largemouth Bass]], which Pister calls "chainsaws with fins") eliminated the Owens pupfish from marshes that once covered vast areas. Groundwater pumping for ranching threatened Devil's Hole, an aquifer undisturbed since the Pleistocene.
- **Mechanism of the 1969 crisis:** after heavy winter 1968–69 rains, high summer evapotranspiration and an unexplained reduction in spring flow dried the Fish Slough pond. Caged fish in eddies with low water velocity suffered low dissolved oxygen, overcrowding, and handling stress.

## Key Findings

- **The 1969 Fish Slough rescue:** about 800 remaining Owens pupfish were removed from the drying pond and held in three wire mesh cages. About 400 were later moved to a spring-fed site. Dead and dying fish were found because the cages sat in low-flow eddies; the cages and fish were moved upstream. Pister carried two buckets of more than thirty pounds each across marsh terrain. The fish population survived, and recovery continues.
- **Recovery history:** researchers found a remnant Owens pupfish population at Fish Slough in 1964. Early reintroductions preceded the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973.
- **Devil's Hole victory:** the 1976 Supreme Court decision protected Devil's Hole and its dependent life from a nearby ranching operation's groundwater pumping.
- **Research value:** Pister argues pupfishes could inform [[Vertebrate Physiology]], including kidney function and temperature tolerance, and that the emphasis should shift from what they can do for humans to what humans can do for them.
- **Scientific complacency:** at the 1992 meeting of the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists, only a small percentage of 385 research papers related to conservation.
- **Guiding principles:** Pister cites [[Aldo Leopold]]'s "Land Ethic" and its definition of right and wrong in terms of the biotic community's integrity, stability, and beauty, arguing for a sustainable society.
