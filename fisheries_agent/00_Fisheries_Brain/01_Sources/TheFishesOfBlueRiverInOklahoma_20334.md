# The Fishes of Blue River in Oklahoma with Descriptions of Two New Percid Hybrid Combinations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 study by Allan D. Linder, prepared under the direction of [[George A. Moore]], provides the first annotated checklist of the fishes of the [[Blue River]] in south-central Oklahoma and describes two percid hybrid combinations. The annotated list records 49 species in 9 families and 32 genera. Two hybrids are described: a [[Blue River Orangebelly Darter]] × [[Orangethroat Darter]] hybrid from Blue River, and an [[Orangethroat Darter]] (pulchellum) × [[Redfin Darter]] hybrid from Sand Creek. Both were analysed with the [[Hybrid Index]] and compared with their parental species.

## Background & Description

The Blue River rises in Pontotoc County, Oklahoma, about six miles west of Fittstown. It is about 100 miles long, drains approximately 660 square miles and flows southeast through Pontotoc, Johnston and Bryan counties to join the [[Red River]] south and east of Durant. The watershed is entirely gently rolling hill country, about 48 per cent timbered. Headwater elevations are about 1250 feet, falling to roughly 500 feet near the mouth. Average annual rainfall ranges from 37 inches at the headwaters to 40 inches at the lower basin. In the lower 60 miles, about 12,000 acres flood annually. The river is clear and moderately swift, with an average gradient of about 7.5 feet per mile, and the watershed vegetation is a typical oak-woods association.

Prior work on the river was sparse. [[Paden 1948]] reported [[Least Darter]] from Thomas Bricken Spring, [[Hubbs and Bonham 1951]] reported [[River Shiner]] from the same locality, and [[Moore and Rigney 1952]] described the Blue River subspecies of [[Blue River Orangebelly Darter]] and noted percid associations.

The study used 8 collecting stations (1a to 8) sampled between 1946 and 1949. Station 1b, Blue River 24 miles south of Ada, is the type locality of the Blue River Orangebelly Darter. Station 8 (JN Creek, Bryan County) yielded 147 specimens representing 18 species. Station 5 (Blue River near Wapanucka) yielded 34 specimens of 21 species, with seining hampered by boulder-strewn bottoms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitats varied from fast riffles and small falls on Cedar Creek, to fine sand and mud near the river source, to spring-fed creeks with [[Water Cress]], algae, *Spirogyra* and *Vaucheria*. Aquatic vegetation was absent at several sites. The annotated list includes:

- **Cyprinids (largest group):** about 17 taxa, including [[Plains Shiner]], [[Redfin Shiner]], [[Bigeye Shiner]] and [[Southern Sand Shiner]].
- **Centrarchids:** 11 taxa, including [[Northern Spotted Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]] and [[Green Sunfish]].
- **Percids:** 10 taxa, including [[Logperch]], [[River Darter]] and [[Northern Dusky Darter]].
- **Catostomids:** 4 taxa, including [[Golden Redhorse]].
- **Ictalurids:** 5 taxa, including [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Flathead Catfish]].

Several records are notable. A Ribbon Shiner (*Notropis f. fumeus*) record is believed to be the westernmost for the species. A [[Dionda nubila]] (Ozark Minnow) specimen is interpreted as a bait-bucket introduction. A single [[River Shiner]] was probably an escapee from a holding pond. Gizzard Shad, Blue Catfish and Crappie were reported by a local resident but not collected.

The hybrids were interpreted in terms of the ecological context. The authors suggested that hybridization may follow from a breakdown of ecological barriers: [[Orangethroat Darter]] is most common in quieter water, while [[Blue River Orangebelly Darter]] is most often taken in breeding colour from riffles. Milt from a riffle spawner could therefore fertilise eggs laid in pools below. Unbalanced parental abundance and sex ratios were also considered.

## Key Findings

- **Checklist:** The Blue River system supports 49 known fish species in 9 families and 32 genera, most reported here as first records for the river and its tributaries.
- **Hybrid 1 (*E. spectabile pulchellum* × *E. w. whipplei*):** One adult male (61.5 mm standard length), collected 25 March 1950 from Sand Creek near Foraker, with a second male (52 mm) found at Osage Hills State Park on 4 May 1952. Twenty-five characters were intermediate, giving an average hybrid index of 51.7. Genital papilla shape was intermediate.
- **Hybrid 2 (*E. radiosum cyanorum* × *E. spectabile* subsp.):** One male (61.0 mm) and one female (54.5 mm) from Blue River, 14 April 1949. Average hybrid index values were 57.5 for the male, 45.2 for the female, 51.4 overall, 46.7 for combined sexes, 40.7 for the cross with female *cyanorum* and male *spectabile*, and 61.6 for the reciprocal cross.
- **Sex-dependent characters:** Squamation of the cheeks and opercles differed between the two hybrid sexes, and the humeral scale appeared in the male only. The authors considered these possibly sex-linked or sex-influenced.
- **Sex ratio and dominance:** At Foraker, 82.3% of 175 specimens were *E. spectabile* subsp. At Blue River, 62.5% of 347 specimens were *E. r. cyanorum*, with a roughly 1:1 sex ratio.
- **Origin:** The hybrids probably arose by accident, reflecting interfertility and close relationship between sympatric parent species that nonetheless remain distinct.
- **Future work:** Experimental breeding and habitat studies are needed to confirm inheritance and the causes of hybridization.
