# USFWS Endangered Fish Reintroduction Program Arizona 1987-1988

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This March 1987 correspondence from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director to the Arizona Game and Fish Department outlines a comprehensive reintroduction program for threatened and endangered native fishes in Arizona waters. The program focuses on [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), utilizing National Wildlife Refuge ponds as intermediate rearing facilities to grow fish to sizes less vulnerable to predation before final stocking into historic habitats. The document requests state concurrence for stocking 500,000 razorback sucker fry into [[Buenos Aires NWR]] and provides a detailed stocking schedule for fiscal year 1987-1988 involving multiple species and locations across Arizona.

## Background & Description

The recovery effort addresses multiple threatened and endangered fish species native to Arizona river systems. Previous year activities included stocking [[Colorado Squawfish]] into the [[Upper Verde River]] and [[Salt River]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] into approximately six different locations. The program identified that larger size fish produce better survival results, but hatchery space limitations constrained production capacity.

To address this constraint, USFWS initiated a novel approach using [[National Wildlife Refuge]] waters as intermediate rearing facilities. The strategy involves stocking fry-size fish into selected refuge ponds, growing them to predation-resistant sizes, then transferring them to waters within their historic range. Suitable refuge ponds are characterized as isolated, fishless, with abundant food supply, and located within historic species ranges.

Razorback suckers were being spawned at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] at the time of correspondence, with fry ready for immediate stocking. [[Bonytail Chub]] spawning was planned for later in spring 1987.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The reintroduction program targets restoration of native fish populations to their historic ranges across Arizona's river systems. Target habitats include major river systems ([[Verde River]], [[Salt River]], [[Gila River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Blue River]], [[Colorado River]]) and smaller tributaries (Canyon Creek, Cibecue Creek, Carrizo Creek, Cedar Creek, Cherry Creek, Eagle Creek, Bonito Creek).

The program recognizes predation as a critical limiting factor for reintroduction success, particularly for small fish. The intermediate rearing strategy in refuge ponds addresses this by allowing fish to reach larger, less vulnerable sizes before release into natural systems. Refuge ponds provide controlled environments with abundant food resources and absence of predators or competing fish species.

The stocking plan encompasses both riverine and refuge pond habitats, with refuge ponds serving dual purposes: intermediate rearing facilities for eventual river stocking, and potential refuge populations for species like [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*).

## Key Findings

**Proposed Stocking Schedule FY 1987-1988:**

**[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*):
- [[Page Springs SFH]]: 100,000 fry
- [[Buenos Aires NWR]]: 500,000 fry
- [[Imperial NWR]]: 300,000 fry
- [[Havasu NWR]]: 250,000 fry
- [[Cibola NWR]]: 150,000 fry
- [[Colorado River Indian Reservation]]: 250,000 fry
- Upper Canyon Creek: 50,000 at 2 inches
- Cibecue Creek: 100,000 at 2 inches
- Carrizo Creek: 125,000 at 2 inches
- Cedar Creek: 25,000 at 2 inches
- Cherry Creek: 100,000 at 2 inches
- Eagle Creek: 75,000 at 2 inches
- Bonito Creek: 25,000 at 2 inches
- Lower Eagle Creek: 1,000 at 12 inches
- [[San Francisco River]]: 3,000 at 12 inches
- [[Gila River]]: 3,000 at 12 inches

**[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*):
- [[Buenos Aires NWR]]: 500,000 fry
- [[Gila River]]: 55,000 at 4 inches
- [[San Francisco River]]: 25,000 at 4 inches
- [[Blue River]]: 20,000 at 4 inches
- [[Gila River]]: 1,300 at 15 inches
- Note: River/stream stocking dependent upon publication of final rule determining populations as experimental nonessential

**[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*):
- [[Willow Beach NFH]]: 200,000 fry
- [[Page Springs SFH]]: 100,000 fry
- [[Verde River]]: 100,000 at 3 inches
- [[Salt River]]: 100,000 at 3 inches

**[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*):
- No stocking planned; all available fish reserved for broodstock development

**[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*):
- Various tanks: 5,000-10,000 mixed sizes

**[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*):
- [[Buenos Aires NWR]]: 2,500-5,000 mixed sizes

All stockings conducted according to existing MOUs, agreements, or joint recovery efforts coordinated between USFWS and state agencies.
