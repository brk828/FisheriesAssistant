# Headwater Chub (Gila nigra): Petition for Listing Priority Restoration and Emergency Listing

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a petition dated November 7, 2009, submitted to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) by four biologists: Sally E. Stefferud, Jerome A. Stefferud, [[Paul C. Marsh]] and [[Thomas E. Dowling]]. It requests two actions for the [[Headwater Chub]], an endemic [[Cyprinidae]] fish of the [[Gila River Basin]]: (1) restoration of its [[Listing Priority]] from 8 to 2, and (2) [[Emergency Listing]] as an endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. A cover letter from the FWS (Albuquerque Regional Office) acknowledges receipt on November 9, 2009, and states the Service's review, including new information, is expected by the end of 2010.

The petitioners argue that the species' status has declined since the 2006 FWS finding, that threats have grown more imminent, and that the 2008 downgrade rested on erroneous data, unsupported conclusions and speculative future conservation. Their key claim is that only 10 populations remain extant, none secure.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Headwater chub (*Gila nigra*), family Cyprinidae, endemic to the Gila River basin. It was formerly treated as a subspecies, *Gila robusta grahami*, and is closely related to [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*).
- **Listing history:** Candidate status since the late 1970s; listing priority 2 from 1982 through 2008, when it was lowered to 8. The FWS 2006 Petition Finding (71 FR 26007-26017) found listing warranted but precluded, assigning priority 2.
- **Petitioners:** Four scientists with extensive experience, including former FWS and Forest Service staff and academic researchers at [[Arizona State University]]. Appendix I summarizes their careers, roughly 25 to 30+ years each.
- **Legal basis:** Citizen petition under section 4(b) of the ESA and 50 CFR 424, invoking the [[Emergency Listing Provisions]] when a species faces a significant and imminent threat.

Note on the 2008 candidate notice: the 2008 Candidate Notice of Review (73 FR 75176-75224) and the November 26, 2008 Species Assessment and Listing Priority Assignment Form are the documents the petition challenges.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Population status (petitioners' assessment).** The petition argues 16 populations cited in 2006 were overstated. Ten are extant:
- 5 (50%) unstable or imperiled with significant, increasing threats
- 3 (30%) stable but threatened
- 2 (20%) of uncertain species identity

The petition identifies the [[Gila River Forks]] (East Fork and Middle/West Forks), [[Tonto Creek]], [[Spring Creek]], [[Haigler Creek]], [[East Verde River]], [[Fossil Creek]] and [[Deadman Creek]] as the main population complexes. The San Carlos River and Ash Creek populations are considered extirpated. West Clear Creek is excluded because its chub are [[Roundtail Chub]].

**Major threats cited:**
- [[Nonnative Fish]] predators, including [[Green Sunfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Flathead Catfish]] and [[Red Shiner]].
- Drought and [[Wildfire]]. The 2003 Picture Fire reduced chub numbers by 89% in affected sites (Carter and Rinne 2005).
- Water diversion: a Payson diversion from the [[Interbasin Water Transfer]] will reduce East Verde River flow by about 6 to 10 cubic feet per second during spring to autumn.
- Recreation at Fossil Creek, with average summer weekend use rising from 175 people (2006) to 539 (2009), a reported increase of over 300%.

**Conservation frameworks challenged:**
- The [[New Mexico Recovery Plan]] (2006) fails 11 of 15 [[PECE Criteria]] standards (73%).
- The [[Arizona Statewide Conservation Agreement]] (ASCA, 2006) is non-binding, unfunded, and the petitioners claim it fails to meet PECE standards.

## Key Findings

1. **Population decline.** The petition contends that the East Fork Gila River population declined from 2002 onward, with no chub in 2002 and 2003 monitoring, and that the Middle and West Forks population has also declined. Both were reclassified by FWS as improving; the petitioners say the data contradict this.
2. **Fossil Creek.** The population is described as "in flux" and in "a downward trend in abundance," with 2008 showing low reproduction. The 2008 FWS categorization as "stable, secure" is judged unsupported. Genetic analysis found 92% headwater in the upper reach (n = 131 fish).
3. **Fishing opening.** The Arizona Game and Fish Commission reopened Fossil Creek to recreational fishing in late 2008 (sport fishing opened in the 2008 decision). The petitioners estimate mortality of at least 10% for hooked fish, citing AGFD estimates of 3 to 7% and roundtail data of up to 12%.
4. **Taxonomic error.** The FWS claim of a "new population" is attributed to misidentified [[Roundtail Chub]] from West Clear Creek, based on Schwemm (2006). The FWS acknowledged this error in a July 2009 revised finding for roundtail chub.
5. **Regulatory inadequacy.** The petition argues neither the New Mexico plan nor the ASCA meets PECE standards for certainty of implementation and effectiveness. It notes the New Mexico plan quantifies only extant populations and lacks adaptive management.
6. **Requested actions.** Restore listing priority to 2, emergency-list the species as endangered, and publish a proposed rule with critical habitat designation.
