## Executive Summary

This paper is a taxonomic/nomenclatural submission to the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN) concerning the stabilization of scientific names for four species of needlefish (family [[Belonidae]]). Author Bruce B. Collette addresses ongoing disputes (with G.F. Mees and others) over which historical names should take priority for four species: *Strongylura marina*, *Platybelone argalus*, *Tylosurus crocodilus*, and *Tylosurus acus*. The core issue is resolving competing senior synonyms under Article 23b (nomina oblita/forgotten names) versus the Law of Priority, with direct implications for both ichthyologists and parasitologists (who rely on stable host-species names for describing associated copepods, trematodes, and cestodes). Collette recommends the ICZN use its plenary powers to suppress two problematic senior names (*houttuyni* and *maris rubri*) while formally validating the commonly used names *marina* and *crocodila*.

## Background & Description

Key diagnostic characters distinguishing species-complexes within Belonidae, as cited in the case for taxonomic separation:

- **Gonad Structure** (used to separate *Strongylura marina* from congeners):
  - *[[Strongylura marina]]*: Only the **right gonad** is developed (asymmetrical).
  - *[[Strongylura senegalensis]]* & *[[Strongylura timucu]]*: Possess the **usual paired gonads** (symmetrical).

- **Predorsal Scale Counts** (meristic differentiation, per Collette and Parin, unpublished manuscript):
  - *S. senegalensis*: 110–130 scales (mean 122)
  - *S. timucu*: 129–185 scales (mean 156)
  - *S. marina*: 213–304 scales (mean 255)

- **Fin Ray Counts** (relevant to the *[[Platybelone argalus]]* "composite species" debate):
  - Original LeSueur (1821) description reported Dorsal (D) 16, Anal (A) 19.
  - Collette confirmed via direct examination of two specimens that these counts are valid and consistent for the species, refuting Mees' claim that the name represents a taxonomic composite.

- **General Diagnostic Traits** (genus-level, per *Tylosurus acus* discussion):
  - Long snout.
  - High number of dorsal and anal fin rays.
  - High vertebral counts (distinguishing *[[Tylosurus acus]]* from other Atlantic needlefish).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Members of Belonidae discussed here are surface-dwelling, epipelagic marine predators (needlefishes), found in coastal and open-ocean tropical/subtropical waters.
- **Geographic Distribution**:
  - *Strongylura marina*: Western Atlantic (common species).
  - *Strongylura senegalensis*: Eastern Atlantic.
  - *Strongylura timucu*: Caribbean.
  - *Tylosurus crocodilus*: Cosmopolitan — Indo-Pacific and Eastern Atlantic populations noted, though possibly conflated with the newly clarified *[[Tylosurus choram]]* (Rüppell) in some historical records.
  - *Tylosurus acus*: Western Atlantic and (per Parin, 1967) Western Pacific and Indian Oceans.
  - *Platybelone argalus*: Western Atlantic, Eastern Atlantic, Western Pacific, and Indian Ocean.
- **Host-Parasite Interactions**: These needlefish species serve as critical hosts for several parasite taxa studied extensively in fisheries parasitology:
  - **Copepods**: Documented on *T. crocodilus* by Capart (1953, 1959), Delamare-Deboutteville & Nunes-Ruivo (1954), and Pillai (1961, Indian Ocean).
  - **Trematodes & Cestodes**: Documented on *S. marina* by Linton (1901–1940) in a long-running series of parasitological papers from Woods Hole.
  - The stability of host fish nomenclature was explicitly flagged as important to parasitologists, not just ichthyologists.

## Key Findings

1. **Species Complex Resolution**: What was historically lumped under "*Belone maris rubri*" (Mees) was demonstrated by Parin (1967) to actually comprise **two distinct species**: *[[Tylosurus crocodilus]]* and *[[Tylosurus choram]]*.

2. **Rejection of Genus Lumping**: Collette explicitly rejects Mees' (1962, 1964) proposal to synonymize *[[Strongylura]]*, *[[Tylosurus]]*, and five other genera under a single genus *[[Belone]]*, maintaining these as valid, distinct genera.

3. **Formal Nomenclatural Recommendations submitted to ICZN**:
   - **Suppress** (via plenary powers, for priority only, not homonymy):
     - *maris rubri* Bloch & Schneider, 1801 (as in *Esox belone* var. *maris rubri*)
     - *houttuyni* Walbaum, 1792 (as in *Esox houttuyni*)
   - **Place on Official List of Specific Names**:
     - *marina* Walbaum, 1792 (as *Esox marinus*)
     - *crocodila* Péron & LeSueur, 1821 (as *Belone crocodila*)
   - **Place on Index of Rejected and Invalid Names**:
     - *maris rubri* Bloch & Schneider, 1801
     - *houttuyni* Walbaum, 1792

4. **Priority Upheld for *Tylosurus acus***: Despite potential for confusion with the unrelated *Belone acus* Risso, 1826 (a junior synonym of *[[Esox belone]]* Linnaeus, later replaced by *gracilis* Lowe, 1839), Collette argues *Sphyraena acus* Lacépède, 1803 should stand under the Law of Priority for the Atlantic *Tylosurus* species, rejecting Mees' alternative proposal of *imperialis* Rafinesque, 1810.

5. **Criterion for Name Selection**: The paper reinforces reliance on **ICZN Recommendation 24A** — prioritizing the name that ensures the greatest **stability and universality of nomenclature** across both ichthyological and parasitological literature, rather than strict chronological priority alone (except where plenary suppression is formally invoked).

6. **Broader Context**: This correspondence is part of a multi-year nomenclatural dispute (Case Z.N.(S.) 1723) involving multiple authors (Mees, Tortonese, Berry, Collette) attempting to resolve naming instability ahead of major taxonomic works, including the *Atlantide Report* and *Fishes of the Western North Atlantic*.
