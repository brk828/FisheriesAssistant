# Petition to Reclassify Loach Minnow and Spikedace from Threatened to Endangered

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

The [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]] and [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] formally petitioned the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] on September 17, 1993, to reclassify two southwestern fish species—the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)—from Threatened to Endangered status under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Both species were originally listed as Threatened in 1986, with promises of Critical Habitat designation within one year. Seven years later, Critical Habitat remains undesignated, and both species have declined precipitously toward extinction. The [[Loach Minnow]] now occupies less than 15% of its former range, while the [[Spikedace]] persists in less than 6% of its historical distribution. The 50-page petition details population declines and proposes remedies for recovery, citing continued habitat degradation from groundwater pumping, stream diversions, sedimentation from logging and grazing, urban sprawl, and riparian cattle grazing.

## Background & Description

The [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) is a small stream-dwelling member of the minnow family ([[Cyprinidae]]) that inhabits turbulent, cobbled riffles of mainstem rivers and tributaries up to 7,200 feet elevation. It is found almost exclusively in stream-bottom habitats, swimming above substrates in short darts from place to place. During high water events, the species has recently been documented inhabiting slower-moving waters to avoid being swept downstream. Breeding males (late March to early June) display bright red-orange coloration around the mouth, gill openings, and underside fins, while breeding females become yellowish around fins and underbody.

The [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), also a member of [[Cyprinidae]], is a sleek fish that mainly inhabits the lower end of stream riffles, sometimes congregating along gravel-sand bars, broad shallow areas, and quiet eddies. The species breeds from April through June, with males taking on a brassy-golden coloration during breeding season.

Historically, the [[Loach Minnow]] was found in appropriate habitat throughout the [[Gila River]] basin above present-day [[Phoenix]]. Currently, it inhabits portions of the [[Gila River]] and its three forks, the [[San Francisco River]], and [[Tularosa River]] in [[New Mexico]]. In [[Arizona]], the species is found only in [[Aravaipa Creek]], the [[Blue River]], and a short stretch of the East Fork and mainstem of the [[White River]].

The [[Spikedace]] was once widely distributed in moderate-sized, intermediate elevation streams in the [[Gila River]] basin upstream from present-day [[Phoenix]]. Current populations are found in the [[Gila River]] and very short stretches of its three forks in [[New Mexico]], and in [[Aravaipa Creek]] in [[Arizona]]. Populations found in the mid-to-late 1980s in [[Eagle Creek]] appear to be extirpated, and populations in the [[Verde River]] are currently being described as a new subspecies or distinct species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both species serve as indicators for the health of southwestern rivers within their range. More than 90% of original desert river riparian areas have been destroyed. Of 102 native fish species in [[Arizona]] and [[New Mexico]], 11 are already extinct, and 51 of the surviving species are in trouble.

Primary threats to both species include:

- **Water impoundment**: Dams have contributed to changes in water chemistry and temperature affecting both upstream and downstream populations
- **Dewatering**: Dams and diversions (irrigation ditches and aqueducts) have contributed to the dewatering of nearly all southwestern creeks, streams, and rivers
- **Mining impacts**: Mines are responsible for changes in water chemistry and silt load in most southwestern watercourses
- **Logging**: Contributes to stream sedimentation and habitat degradation
- **Non-native species invasion**: The invasion of non-native fish species such as the [[Red Shiner]] has had serious effects on natives not evolved for competition with generalist species
- **Grazing impacts**: Both the dewatering of streams through vegetation loss and direct destruction of habitat through creation of unnatural flooding and siltation continue with very few changes since the 1986 listing. These impacts have been totally ignored by both the [[Forest Service]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]]

## Key Findings

1. **Population Declines**: The [[Loach Minnow]] has been reduced to less than 15% of its former range; the [[Spikedace]] has been reduced to less than 6% of its former range.

2. **Regulatory Failure**: Seven years after the 1986 Threatened listing, the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] has failed to designate Critical Habitat as promised, despite legal obligations under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

3. **Continued Habitat Degradation**: Primary threats including excessive groundwater pumping, stream diversions, stream sedimentation from upland logging and cattle grazing, urban sprawl, and riparian cattle grazing have continued unabated since the 1986 listing.

4. **Inadequate Protection**: Only projects proposing the largest, most obvious impacts since the 1986 listing have been scaled back, with much more destructive, insidious long-term impacts such as grazing virtually ignored. Excepting a few efforts such as monitoring or surveying populations, no federal, state, or private agency (excepting [[The Nature Conservancy]]) has taken steps needed to prevent further habitat destruction or facilitate species recovery.

5. **Expert Recommendations**: Calls for uplisting both species have come from several biologists intimately involved with research and recovery, including the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], [[Arizona Department of Game and Fish]], and the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] itself.

6. **Legal Action Threatened**: If the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] does not guarantee fulfillment of their acknowledged legal obligation, legal proceedings will be undertaken in Federal Court to compel designation of Critical Habitat.

7. **Petitioners**: The petition was filed by David Hogan, Peter Galvin, the [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]], the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]], and the [[Biodiversity Legal Foundation]] pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act and [[Endangered Species Act]].
