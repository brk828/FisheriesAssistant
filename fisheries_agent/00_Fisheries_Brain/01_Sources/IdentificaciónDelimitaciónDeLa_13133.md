# Identificación, Delimitación de la Distribución y Abundancia Relativa de Peces en los Ríos Santa Cruz, San Pedro y Norte del Río Yaqui (Sonora, México)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinodontidae #Poeciliidae #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This document is a **research project proposal** submitted by the Instituto del Medio Ambiente y el Desarrollo Sustentable del Estado de Sonora (IMADES) to identify, delimit the distribution, and determine the relative abundance of freshwater fish species inhabiting the [[Rio Santa Cruz]], [[Rio San Pedro]], and the northern portion of the [[Rio Yaqui]] (near [[Cascada Basaseachic]]) in Sonora, Mexico. The proposal is framed within a broader 11-year regional research effort (since 1986) on native freshwater fish, conducted in partnership with the **Arizona Game and Fish Department**, given that these river basins are shared between Sonora and Arizona. The project's central conservation concern is the ongoing decline of native fish populations — most notably the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — due to habitat degradation and introduction of exotic species. No field results are presented in this document; it outlines objectives, methodology, and a proposed 16-month field/mapping campaign (13 months of sampling).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historic range of *Cyprinodon macularius*:** Lower Colorado River basin, Gila River basin, and Río Sonoyta basin, spanning Mexico (Sonora and Baja California Norte) and the United States (Arizona and California).
- **Current status:** *Cyprinodon macularius* is reported as **extirpated** from the Río Gila drainage in Sonora (i.e., from both the Río Santa Cruz and Río San Pedro), a dramatic decline attributed to:
  - Habitat destruction and modification from poor water-body management.
  - Introduction of exotic/invasive species competing with native pupfish populations.
- This same threat pattern (habitat loss + invasive species) is described as affecting **all native fish populations** across the Río San Pedro, Santa Cruz, and Yaqui basins, several of which are listed under some risk category in the **Norma Oficial Mexicana (NOM) 1994**.
- **Regional threat drivers** (citing Nabhan & Holdsworth, 1998, on the Sonoran Desert): among the ten most significant anthropogenic threats to desert biodiversity, inadequate use and deterioration of aquifer/water resources is highlighted; increasing threats to riparian zones include urbanization, livestock grazing, water extraction/impoundment, and introduction of exotic animals.
- **Habitat loss** is broadly cited (Flores, 1993) as one of the most important threats to Mexican freshwater aquatic organisms generally.
- Prior IMADES work (1986–1997) with Arizona Game & Fish Department produced: habitat characterization, identification/localization of native fish populations, external morphological characterization, habitat condition assessments, and analysis of causes of population decline — informing recovery potential and management recommendations.

## Key Findings

Note: As this document is a **project proposal**, it contains no original field data, catch records, or abundance results. The "findings" below represent the proposal's planned methodology, deliverables, and success indicators rather than empirical outcomes.

**Planned Field Methodology:**
- A pilot trip to finalize sampling site locations, equipment checks, and community contacts.
- 13 months of field sampling across all four seasons, prioritizing sites with perennial water (pozas) and higher precipitation, focused on the northernmost portion of each shared sub-basin.
- Field data sheet variables: date, season, sampling area, latitude/longitude, species observed, total number of individuals, and qualitative observations (human impact, ecological notes).
- **Capture methods:**
  - In sufficient water flow: river "enclosure" (encierro) technique followed by dip-net/cuchara retrieval.
  - In insufficient water/low flow: direct "cuchareo" (scooping) collection.
  - Sedation protocol: xylocaine solution at 0.0075 cc concentration used sparingly to calm specimens (per Robles B., 1991 thesis); treated water not returned to the water body.
  - Sampling repeated a minimum of five times per site per month across the 13-month period.
  - Only **one voucher specimen per species** collected per site, to be confirmed taxonomically with assistance from an external advisor at the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
- Data management: field data digitized into Excel 98/Access 98 databases, housed at the **Centro de Datos para la Conservación (CDC-IMADES)**; spatial data collected via GPS and mapped onto INEGI 1:50,000 topographic maps, analyzed via GIS.

**Planned Deliverables/Products:**
1. Final project report updating fish population data for the Santa Cruz, San Pedro, and Yaqui basins.
2. A species checklist for the three river systems.

6. Seasonal abundance maps (one map per season, submitted to CONABIO).
