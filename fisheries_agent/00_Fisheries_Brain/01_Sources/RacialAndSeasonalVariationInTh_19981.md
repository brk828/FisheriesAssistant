# Racial and Seasonal Variation in the Pacific Herring, California Sardine and California Anchovy

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae #engraulidae

## Executive Summary

This California Fish Bulletin (No. 8, February 10, 1925), by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], examines meristic variation in three commercially important clupeoid fishes of western North America: the [[Pacific Herring]], the [[California Sardine]] and the [[California Anchovy]]. The central question is whether differences in average vertebral and fin-ray counts reflect distinct races or individual, seasonal and annual variation linked to water temperature during development. The study extends earlier work by [[William F. Thompson]] on herring and applies a method Hubbs had used on freshwater fishes. The author concludes that herring and anchovy show measurable environmental and developmental effects, that the anchovy splits into two sharply defined forms, and that the sardine evidence for racial distinctness is inconclusive.

## Background & Description

The study belongs to a series aimed at relating varying fish characters to physical features of the environment, with particular focus on the correlation between average vertebral number and water temperature. Surface temperature curves for San Francisco Bay, Monterey Bay and San Diego (Plate I) show a sharp contrast between cold central Californian waters and warmer southern waters, with [[Point Conception]] marking the boundary.

Study material and taxa:

- **Pacific Herring** (*Clupea pallasii*): 208 young from the Straits of Georgia, British Columbia; San Francisco Bay series collected 1922–1923 (fall, December 21 adults, April and June samples); 89 adults from Monterey Bay; four mature specimens from Point Loma. Ages were estimated mainly by size-frequency analysis, supplemented by scale annuli.
- **California Sardine** (*Sardinops caerulea*): vertebrae counted in series from four areas (San Francisco Bay, Monterey Bay, San Pedro/Redondo Beach, San Diego), totaling 1,910 fish across the Table 5 series.
- **California Anchovy** (*Engraulis mordax*): ocean subspecies sampled from five localities (San Francisco Bay, Monterey Bay, Redondo Beach, Anaheim Bay, Coronado); bay form from San Francisco Bay.

Methods: meristic counts (vertebrae, dorsal and anal rays), averages with probable errors, and comparisons of age or size groups within localities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature and vertebrae:** Fish hatched earliest in the season, at the coldest temperatures, showed the highest average vertebral numbers. The author links the pattern to temperature during development, since vertebral number is fixed early in life.
- **Spawning and migration:** Anchovies spawn chiefly in bays, roughly April to June. The bay anchovy enters San Francisco Bay before breeding, with ripe fish taken mid-April to early June at Sausalito, and disperses into lower-salinity San Pablo Bay in spring. Ocean anchovies are largely unseen in fall and winter, probably offshore.
- **Salinity and development:** The bay anchovy develops in warmer, less saline water, which accelerates early development and yields smaller adult size.
- **Herring migration:** Young herring leave San Francisco Bay seaward. Yearling fish appear in large numbers in April, suggesting a pre-spawning inshore migration.
- **Local variation:** Temperature in each region varies with protection, depth and upwelling of deep cold water (cited to McEwen, 1912, 1916).

## Key Findings

**Pacific Herring**
- Vertebral numbers decrease southward. The White Sea sample (Arctic Europe) averaged 53.56, British Columbia samples 52.69–52.83 (mode 53), and California samples 51.53–52.03 (mode 52). The overall California total was 51.81 (n = 913).
- Dorsal ray counts were slightly higher in San Francisco Bay than in British Columbia, but differences were near the limit of statistical significance. Anal ray counts were distinctly lower in San Francisco, confirming Thompson.
- The 1923 brood averaged 51.83 vertebrae (n = 504), not significantly different from the parent stock (51.77, n = 231). Larger young from earlier in the season averaged 51.99, higher than smaller young (51.73).
- Fluctuation of averages within the San Francisco race exceeded that among British Columbia samples, so the author cannot confirm racial meaning for vertebral differences.
- San Francisco Bay herring appear to mature partly at age two.

**California Sardine**
- Central California (n = 800, mean 51.78) averaged 0.09 vertebrae higher than southern California (n = 1,110, mean 51.69). This difference is statistically significant but smaller than differences between samples from the same port, which the author attributes mainly to annual variation.
- No proof of racial distinction between central and southern sardines was established.
- European sardine (*Sardina sardina*, per Fage, 1920) shows a regular northward increase in vertebrae, for comparison.

**California Anchovy**
- Two sharply differentiated races are recognized. The ocean subspecies *Engraulis mordax mordax* ranges widely along the coast, while the San Francisco Bay subspecies *Engraulis mordax nanus* (Girard) is restricted to the bay.
- The bay form averages 43.80 vertebrae (n = 172) versus 45.73 (n = 127) for the ocean form in the same region, a difference of about two vertebrae in both mean and mode.
- Ocean anchovies vary from 44 to 47 vertebrae (averages 45.48–45.89), with central California slightly higher (difference 0.10, about five times its probable error). The southward decrease is irregular.
- Anaheim Bay and Redondo Beach young differ in vertebral number in line with size and developmental temperature. The largest October fish were hatched in the coldest water and had the highest counts.
- Coronado males averaged 45.77 vertebrae (n = 240) and females 45.65 (n = 362), a difference of probable but not certain significance.
- The bay form is smaller (largest 99 mm to caudal fin, versus 144.5 mm for ocean fish), has a longer, deeper, less terete body, and is paler on the back. Head length averaged 31.90% of total length in the bay form versus 28.98% in adult ocean fish.
- Interbreeding between the two forms probably occurs at Sausalito, where some stragglers showed intermediate traits.

**Overall conclusions**
- Hubbs states that herring, anchovy and freshwater fish show higher vertebral counts in cooler developmental conditions, supporting a temperature-based interpretation.
- Racial status is only partly resolved. The anchovy subspecies distinction is strongly supported. Herring and sardine differences remain inconclusive and require further study.
