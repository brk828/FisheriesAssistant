# Lactate Dehydrogenase, Malate Dehydrogenase and Tetrazolium Oxidase Isozymes of Darters (Etheostomatini)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 study by [[L. M. Page]] and [[G. S. Whitt]] (Illinois Natural History Survey and University of Illinois) applies starch-gel electrophoresis of three enzyme systems to darters of the tribe Etheostomatini. Almost 500 specimens of 68 species were examined: tetrazolium oxidase (TO) mobilities for 68 species, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) patterns for 67 species and malate dehydrogenase (MDH) patterns for 57 species. The data are used to test the monophyly of the genera [[Percina]] and [[Etheostoma]] and to clarify relationships among subgenera, which are compared with the classification of [[Bailey and Gosline 1955]]. The authors conclude that enzyme data support monophyletic origins for Percina and Etheostoma, and they flag [[Percina copelandi]] as possibly a composite of two or more taxonomic forms.

## Background & Description

Darters are small percid fishes of North American freshwater systems. The authors note several unresolved systematic questions: the status of the three nominate-subgenus Percina species relative to the other described Percina; the relationship of [[Ammocrypta]] (including *A. asprella*) to other Ammocrypta; the limits of large Etheostoma subgenera; and the origin of Ammocrypta relative to the "primitive" Percina and "advanced" Etheostoma. Morphological evidence for generic monophyly was described as weak, apart from modified midventer scales in Percina and the translucency and terete body of Ammocrypta.

Methods were as follows:
- Head tissue was homogenized in Tris-HCl buffer (0.1 M, pH 7.0) and centrifuged to obtain a supernatant for electrophoresis.
- Vertical starch-gel electrophoresis (14% gel, 8 V/cm, 16–20 h, 4°C) used two buffer systems: Tris-citrate (pH 6.8) and EBT (pH 8.7).
- LDH patterns resolved into more species-specific identities in EBT buffer. MDH patterns were discernible only in Tris-citrate buffer.
- Specimens represented 25 of the 27 darter subgenera. Localities are documented in the source's material-examined list (summarized here, not reproduced).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Specimens came from a wide set of drainages, including the Middle Fork (Vermilion River), Embarras River, Maries River, Gasconade River, Big South Fork (Cumberland River), Conasauga River, Tar River, Roanoke River and Sugar Creek, among many others. Several sites were the source of multiple species, which indicates considerable sympatry within these drainages.

Geographic variation in enzyme patterns was limited. The main exceptions were:
- **P. copelandi**: a western population (Shoal Creek, Arkansas River, Kansas) and an eastern population (Big South Fork, Tennessee) showed different LDH patterns. The species has a highly disjunct distribution.
- **P. caprodes**: the Conasauga River (Alabama River drainage) specimen showed higher mobility of upper LDH bands than Mississippi system specimens.
- **P. maculata**: higher TO mobility in Sugar River, Middle Fork and Big South Fork specimens than in Kaskaskia River, Gibbons Creek and Clarks Fork specimens.
- **E. flabellare**: the Roanoke River (Atlantic drainage) population had higher uppermost LDH band mobility than Mississippi system populations.
- **E. stigmaeum** (Clarks Fork, Kentucky) had lower TO mobility than eastern Tennessee and Gulf Coast specimens.
- **E. spectabile**: Stony Creek (Wabash) had higher TO mobility than Buffalo River (Tennessee) specimens.

## Key Findings

**Enzyme pattern counts**
- 16 LDH patterns (EBT buffer) across 68 species; only three were shared by more than two species.
- 10 MDH phenotypes among 57 species; six were unique to single species, and the remainder were shared by 3, 10, 18 and 20 species.
- 4 TO mobilities; two (A and B) were very common.

**Monophyly of Percina**
- Identical LDH B4 isozyme mobility in all species examined.
- MDH pattern (A) in all species except *P. caprodes* and *P. evides*.
- TO mobility (A) in all species except *P. uranidea* and some *P. maculata* populations. About 89% of Percina had mobility A.
- Fifteen of nineteen Percina species are "generalized" in enzyme pattern.

**Monophyly of Etheostoma**
- Common LDH pattern (E) in 66% of species, present in 73% of subgenera.
- Common MDH pattern (C) in 53% of species, present in 47% of subgenera.
- Common TO mobility (B) in 72% of species, present in 80% of subgenera. About 67% of Etheostoma had mobility B.
- The only subgenus lacking at least one of the three common characteristics was Villora (one species examined, no MDH data).

**Genetic discontinuity and Ammocrypta**
- The common LDH pattern of Etheostoma was absent from Percina, and the common MDH pattern was absent from both Percina and Ammocrypta.
- The two Ammocrypta species shared LDH and MDH patterns but differed in TO mobility.
- Ammocrypta shares its MDH pattern with most Percina, but its LDH pattern with Etheostoma. The authors describe this as conflicting evidence.

**Copelandi complex**
- *P. copelandi* showed a dichotomous LDH pattern, with western and eastern populations resembling different Percina groups. Together with morphological differences, this suggests it may comprise two or more taxonomically recognizable forms.

**Subgenus evolution**
- Generalized Percina subgenera: Hadropterus, Swainia, Alvordius, Hypohomus, Cottogaster.
- Specialized Percina subgenera: Percina, Ericosma, Imostoma, and the questionable *P. cymatotaenia*.
- Generalized Etheostoma subgenera: Boleosoma, Etheostoma, Allohistium, Nothonotus.
- Most specialized Etheostoma subgenera: Ulocentra, Oligocephalus, Catonotus, Hololepis.
- Subgenera whose enzyme order departs from Bailey & Gosline (1955): Cottogaster and *P. cymatotaenia* (Percina); Litocara/Ioa, Vaillantia, Doration and Ulocentra (Etheostoma).

**Species relationships**
- Enzyme identity supports known groupings: Boleosoma–Etheostoma–Ulocentra lineage, Nothonotus (five of six species), Hadropterus (three species), Alvordius (four of six species), and *E. asprigene* with *E. swaini*.
- New suggested affinities: *A. pellucida* with *E. chlorosomum* (possibly indicating Ammocrypta and Etheostoma share a common origin), and *E. proeliare* (Microperca) with the Boleosoma–Etheostoma–Ulocentra lineage.
- The authors judge that *E. barbouri* and *E. tetrazonum* share a pattern, despite morphological dissimilarity, probably by chance.
- The most specialized species were *E. kennicotti*, *E. squamiceps*, *E.* (Catonotus) species and *E. punctulatum*.

**Interpretation caveats**
- Identical patterns may reflect recent divergence or genetic conservatism. The authors argue that conservatism is the more likely explanation for most cases.
- Intraspecific variation was small, so single specimens were considered adequate for interspecific comparisons, except for *P. copelandi* LDH.
