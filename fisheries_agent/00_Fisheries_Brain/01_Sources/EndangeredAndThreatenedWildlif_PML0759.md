# Animal Candidate Review for Listing as Endangered or Threatened Species (1994 Notice of Review)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fishes #invertebrates

## Executive Summary

This document comprises a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) letter and the Federal Register notice of review (50 CFR Part 17, Vol. 59, No. 219, November 15, 1994) for the [[Animal Candidate Review]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The notice compiles native U.S. animal taxa, and a set of plants, considered for possible listing as endangered or threatened, assigns them to status categories, and solicits information from the public. The compilation is organized by taxonomic group and lists roughly 124 animal and 104 plant candidates in the Arizona-focused cover list. Freshwater fishes, mussels, snails and cave-dwelling invertebrates feature prominently in the full table.

## Background & Description

The Service must determine whether species are endangered or threatened based on the best available scientific and commercial data. Under regulations at 50 CFR 424.75 it publishes comprehensive notices of review naming candidate taxa and indicating whether enough information exists to propose listing. Candidate taxa receive no substantive legal protection under the Act until a proposed rule is issued, although the Act requires monitoring of certain candidates to prevent extinction while listing decisions are pending.

The January 1994 letter to interested parties and agencies, signed by the State Supervisor in Arizona, requests status information, raw data and anecdotal observations on listed candidate species in Arizona, and notes that the Arizona candidate list (Category 1 and Category 2) was updated.

The Federal Register notice revises the previous animal notice of review (November 21, 1991, 56 FR 58804). Changes include new candidate taxa, changes in category, additions and deletions, corrections of historic distributions, and updated status trends.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Status categories** defined in the notice:
- **Category 1 (FE/PE/PT candidates):** Sufficient biological data on vulnerability and threats exist to support listing proposals; development of proposed rules anticipated.
- **Category 2:** Information suggests listing may be appropriate, but persuasive data are not currently available.
- **Category 3:** Taxa no longer under consideration, subdivided into 3A (presumed extinct), 3B (not distinct taxa) and 3C (more abundant or widespread than believed, or not subject to identifiable threat).

**Status trends** are coded I (Improving), S (Stable), D (Declining), U (Unknown) or N (Not applicable).

**Service Regions** (R1 to R7) hold lead responsibility for each taxon. Region 2 covers Arizona, New Mexico, Oklahoma and Texas; Region 1 covers the Pacific states and Hawaii; Region 4 covers the Southeast.

**Threat context:** The notice highlights habitat loss, groundwater extraction, and springs and cave modification as recurring concerns for aquatic and subterranean taxa, though the text documents threats only in general terms.

## Key Findings

**Scope of the 1994 candidate list (Arizona cover list, January 1994):**
- Animals: 124; Plants: 104 (per the document totals).
- Category 1 Arizona candidates include the Huachuca springsnail, Canyon talussnail, Gila chub and several leopard frogs (Chiricahua, Canyon), and the Tiger salamander (Sonoran).
- Category 2 includes numerous plants (e.g., Pima Indian-mallow, Hohokam agave) and invertebrates.

**Fish and aquatic vertebrates in the full table (selected entries):**
- Flannelmouth sucker (lower Colorado River basin population), Gila chub, Roundtail chub, Speckled dace (Gila and Bill Williams basins).
- Longfin dace, Mexican stoneroller, Little Colorado sucker, Desert sucker, Sonora sucker, Zuni Mountain sucker.
- Lake sturgeon and Green sturgeon (Category 2), plus numerous trouts, chubs, darters, minnows, madtoms and sculpins from eastern and western North America.
- Freshwater taxa extensively listed include the Bonneville cutthroat trout, Shoshone pupfish, Devils River minnow, and Pecos pupfish (Category 1 or 2 depending on population).

**Invertebrates:**
- Large groups include springsnails (Hydrobiidae), pleurocerid snails (elimia), freshwater mussels (Unionidae, roughly 90 entries across Southeast rivers), cave beetles (Pseudanophthalmus, dozens of entries in Kentucky, Tennessee and Virginia), and crayfishes.
- Notable Arizona aquatic invertebrates include Stygobromus amphipods, Sabino Canyon damselfly, Parkeri riffle beetle, Montezuma Well springsnail and Gila tryonia.

**Other taxa:**
- Amphibians and reptiles (e.g., Chiricahua leopard frog, Canyon leopard frog, Tarahumara frog, Sonoran desert tortoise, Texas horned lizard).
- Mammals (about 100+ entries, including Yuma puma, Mexican long-tongued bat and several bat and pocket mouse subspecies), and birds (e.g., Black rail, Mountain plover, Western snowy plover).

**Process and deadlines:**
- Comments on the notice were requested until an update notice (anticipated in 1996).
- Comments should go to the Regional Director with lead responsibility for each taxon; general comments go to the Chief, Division of Endangered Species, Washington, D.C.
- The Service plans substantive changes on a two-year cycle.
- Category 3 counts in the notice: 424 taxa in Category 3 (including 3A-156, 3B-61 and 3C-207 per the text summary), and 1,979 Category 2 taxa representing 2,001 taxa (as stated in the document).

**Notes on data quality:** The notice itself states that many invertebrates lack standardized common names and that many Category 3A taxa were found to have gone extinct well before 1973.
