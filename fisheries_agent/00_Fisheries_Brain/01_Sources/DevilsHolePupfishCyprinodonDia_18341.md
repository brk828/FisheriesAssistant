## Background & Description

The [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*) is a member of the family [[Cyprinodontidae]]. This species represents one of the most geographically restricted vertebrates on Earth, confined entirely to [[Devil's Hole]], a geothermal pool in the Mojave Desert of Nevada.

The species is adapted to the unique conditions of its isolated desert spring habitat, which features warm water temperatures and limited space. The photograph by Edwin Phil Pister provides visual documentation of this rare species in its natural environment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Devil's Hole]] represents an extreme example of habitat specialization and isolation. The pupfish population exists in a single small pool, making it exceptionally vulnerable to environmental changes and disturbances. The thermal spring environment provides stable water temperatures year-round, which has allowed this species to persist in an otherwise inhospitable desert landscape.

The restricted range and small population size of *Cyprinodon diabolis* make it one of the most endangered fish species in North America, requiring intensive conservation management and protection.
