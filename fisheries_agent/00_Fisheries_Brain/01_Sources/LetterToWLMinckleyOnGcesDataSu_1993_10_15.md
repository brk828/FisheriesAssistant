# Letter to W.L. Minckley on GCES Data Sub-Sets for Archiving

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #humpback-chub #conservation-biology #data-management

## Executive Summary
This 1993 letter from Paul C. Marsh and Michael E. Douglas (Arizona State University) to W.L. Minckley describes the structure, content, and status of data collected under the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) contract regarding [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) ecology in the [[Little Colorado River]]. The correspondence outlines primary (raw field capture) and secondary (derived/analytical) data sets, details study objectives centered on distribution, abundance, movement, and reproduction of humpback chub, and reports on the status of related manuscripts and conference presentations. The study is part of broader efforts to inform conservation management of this federally endangered species in the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem.

## Background & Description
While this document is primarily administrative/data-descriptive rather than a morphological study, it specifies key biometric variables recorded for each captured individual, which are relevant to characterizing the study population:

- **Total Length (TL)**: measured in mm for all captured fish
- **Weight**: measured in grams
- **Sex** and **reproductive condition** (e.g., ripe, spent) recorded for applicable individuals
- **Size-based handling protocol**:
  - Native fish **< 150 mm TL**: marked via fin clip
  - Native fish **> 150 mm TL**: tagged (applies to [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and [[Razorback Sucker]], including hybrids)
- Sampling gear (hoop nets, trammel nets, etc.) is noted as **size-selective**, biased toward capturing larger individuals (**>~100 mm TL**), which constrains the demographic representativeness of the primary dataset toward sub-adults and adults.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study data pertain to fish inhabiting the **Little Colorado River**, specifically the reach from its confluence with the [[Colorado River]] upstream to approximately **Blue Spring**. Key ecological data dimensions captured include:

- **Spatial distribution**: recorded at three nested location scales (general location, FWS transect code, and river meter upstream from confluence)
- **Temporal distribution**: captures dated (mm/dd/yy) allow analysis by month, season, and year
- **Species composition**: includes native species ([[Humpback Chub]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and hybrids) alongside **non-native fishes**
- **Trophic interactions**: non-native fishes were sacrificed for **food habits analysis**, revealing that humpback chub have been documented as **prey of introduced fishes** in this system (per Marsh & Douglas, AFS Transactions, submitted 09/93)
- **Habitat linkage**: capture data are noted as complementary to physical habitat datasets (thermal regime, discharge magnitude/pattern) and structural habitat data being collected independently by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), though no formal data merger was planned at time of writing
- **Reproductive ecology**: spawning behavior could not be directly observed due to poor water clarity; no spawning activity was observed even during a clear-water period in summer 1993; attempts to collect ova were unsuccessful; ripe females (especially humpback chub) were rare in collections

## Key Findings
- **Data structure**: GCES fish data are divided into a **primary set** (raw field capture data, updated monthly on the ASU mainframe, covering June 1991–September 1993) and a **secondary set** (derived data via SAS routines, CAPTURE software for population estimation, etc.), with only the primary set considered stable enough for archiving.
- **Study Objective 1 (Distribution, abundance, movement)**:
  - Primary data enabled direct estimates of spatial/temporal distribution of [[Humpback Chub]] and associated fishes.
  - Mark-recapture data processed via the **CAPTURE** program provided **population abundance estimates** with confidence intervals, groupable by location, time frame, and size class.
  - Movement pattern analysis remained preliminary, relying on visualization/summarization rather than fully developed statistical routines.
- **Study Objective 2 (Reproductive potential and spawning)**: **Unsuccessful** — no spawning activity observed, ova collection attempts failed, and ripe females were too rare for robust sexual condition analysis.
- **Trophic/food habits data**: Non-native fish food habits data were collected but **not digitized**, rendering them unavailable for archiving at the time; findings were being prepared for peer-reviewed publication.
- **Endangered species interaction**: A key emergent finding, reported in a manuscript submitted to *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*, identifies **humpback chub as prey of introduced fishes** in the Little Colorado River — a significant conservation concern.
- **Dissemination**: Findings were presented at multiple professional venues, including the **Desert Fishes Council** (1991, 1992), the **Society for Conservation Biology** (1993), and the **American Fisheries Society, Western Division** (1991), reflecting active but still-developing analytical progress under the GCES contract.
