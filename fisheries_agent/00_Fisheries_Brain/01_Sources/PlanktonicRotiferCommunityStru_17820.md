# Planktonic Rotifer Community Structure in Alto Cavado Reservoir in Relation to Trophic Status

#ichthyology #marine-biology #rotifera

## Executive Summary

This 1992 study by M. Conceição Guimarães, published by the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), examines the seasonal dynamics of planktonic Rotifera in [[Alto Cavado Reservoir]], northern Portugal. Monthly sampling from November 1988 to September 1989 at one station, alongside water temperature and dissolved oxygen profiles, showed that rotifer abundance peaked during the warmest months and the stratified period. The author identified 31 species and classified the reservoir as oligomesotrophic based on density, species richness and indicator-based criteria. The work forms part of the [[NATO PO-RIVERS Programme]].

Note: this document is a research article, not a fish study. Fish are not addressed.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** Alto Cavado is a reservoir about 25 years old, the first of four on the River Cavado. It lies in an isolated highland region with low population density and no industry, with cold snowy winters and hot summers. Its water is conveyed by gravity through a 4.9 km tunnel to [[Alto Rabagão Reservoir]], a downstream reservoir on a tributary of the Cavado.

**Physical characteristics:**
- Mean depth: 10 m; maximum depth: 11.5 m
- Capacity: given in the source as "2 x 10° m³" (OCR-affected; likely 2 × 10⁶ m³)
- Stratified June to September, with anoxic conditions below 2 m depth
- Water temperature: 3.3 °C at the bottom in winter to 22.5 °C at the surface in summer

**Rotifers as indicators.** The author notes that rotifer assemblages are highly characteristic of water types and can indicate limnological character (Ruttner-Kolisko, 1974). This makes them useful for trophic assessment.

**Objectives.** The study aimed to (1) identify the rotifer species present, (2) describe the annual density fluctuation of predominant species, and (3) relate these patterns to the reservoir's trophic level.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.**
- Frequency: monthly, November 1988 to September 1989
- Station: one, mid-reservoir, about 100 m from the dam
- Profiles: water temperature and dissolved oxygen measured with a YSI 58 meter
- Collection: vertical hauls from bottom to surface with an Apstein net (17 cm diameter, 55 µm mesh)
- Preservation: 4% [[Formalin]] for density counts; some live samples kept for identification
- Density: counted under an inverted microscope, expressed as individuals per m³
- Species richness: number of species per sample (following [[Pielou Diversity Index]] approach as cited, Pielou 1975)

**Seasonal pattern.** The study divides the year into periods:
- **Stratified period (June to September):** higher rotifer density, with species such as [[Keratella cochlearis tecta]], [[Asplanchna priodonta]], [[Conochiloides dossuarius]], [[Hexarthra mira]] and [[Polyarthra spp.]]
- **Mixed period (November to April):** lower density, dominated by [[Keratella cochlearis cochlearis]], [[Polyarthra spp.]] and [[Synchaeta spp.]]

**Dissolved oxygen tolerance.** Several taxa appear tolerant of low oxygen. Hexarthra mira was present in September, when mean dissolved oxygen in the water column was only 3.5 mg O₂/l. Polyarthra spp. also persisted under low-oxygen conditions.

**Trophic indicators used:**
- Density and composition assessed against criteria attributed to Weber (in Lander, 1976) and Ruttner-Kolisko (1974)
- Sládeček (1983): 64% of recorded species are oligosaprobic, generally considered oligotrophic
- The Brachionus:Trichocerca quotient (Q B/T) never exceeded 1, reflecting oligotrophic conditions

## Key Findings

**1. Species diversity and frequency**
- 31 rotifer species were identified.
- The abstract reports 35% of species as constant in the reservoir; the discussion reports 50%. The two figures are inconsistent in the source.
- Species richness ranged from 14 species (July and August) to 5 (February). It fell from November to February, probably with falling temperature, rose from March to August, and dropped in September, probably with high temperature and low oxygen.

**2. Dominant species and peak densities**
- **[[Keratella cochlearis cochlearis]]**: the most constant species, with the highest recorded density of 139,375 ind/m³. It was dominant in November, April and August. Density declined from September to April and rose from June to August.
- **[[Keratella cochlearis tecta]]**: stratified-period species, maximum 34,200 ind/m³ in June.
- **[[Ascomorpha ecaudis]]** (spelt "eucadis" in source): sporadic but very abundant, 86,210 ind/m³ in June, when it was dominant.
- **[[Asplanchna priodonta]]**: June to September, peaks of 61,046 ind/m³ (June) and 70,500 ind/m³ (August).
- **[[Conochiloides dossuarius]]**: maximum 39,000 ind/m³ in August; dominant in July.
- **[[Collotheca mutabilis]]**: maximum 35,250 ind/m³ in August.
- **[[Conochilus hippocrepis]]**: April to August, maximum 23,126 ind/m³ in June.
- **[[Polyarthra spp.]]**: June to January, maximum 61,072 ind/m³ in September; dominant in December, January and September.
- **[[Synchaeta spp.]]**: mixed-period species, maximum 4,293 ind/m³ in February.
- **[[Lecane elongata]]**: sporadic, 16,200 ind/m³ in July.
- **[[Lepadella patella]]**: November to April, maximum 432 ind/m³ in November.
- **[[Hexarthra mira]]**: stratified period, maximum 13,104 ind/m³ in September.

The source lists over 30 taxa in Table 2. Most of the remainder occurred sporadically at low densities and were considered unrepresentative of the fauna.

**3. Seasonal conclusions**
- Stratified-period increase, with taxa including [[Filinia longiseta]], [[Hexarthra mira]], [[Ascomorpha ecaudis]], [[Asplanchna priodonta]], [[Brachionus quadridentatus]], [[Collotheca mutabilis]] and [[Keratella cochlearis tecta]].
- Mixed-period decline in density, with [[Keratella cochlearis cochlearis]], [[Polyarthra spp.]] and [[Synchaeta spp.]] as principal species.

**4. Trophic status**
- The author concludes that [[Alto Cavado Reservoir]] is oligomesotrophic, based on density, composition, species richness and indicator criteria.
- The Q B/T index supports oligotrophic conditions.

**5. Limitations evident in the source**
- A single sampling station and one annual cycle (November 1988 to September 1989).
- The reported constancy percentages conflict (35% vs 50%).
- Several figures are affected by OCR or print errors (e.g., reservoir capacity, some species epithets), so exact values should be checked against the original.
