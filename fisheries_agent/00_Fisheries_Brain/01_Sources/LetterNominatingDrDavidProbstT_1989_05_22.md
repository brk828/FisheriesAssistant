## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6, Denver) nominating Dr. David Probst (New Mexico Department of Game and Fish) to the **Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team**. The letter itself does not present primary morphological or ecological data but is significant as institutional metadata: it confirms the regulatory and organizational history of endangered fish conservation on the [[Colorado River]]. It identifies the three flagship endangered fish species of the basin, the statutory basis for their protection (the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973), and the administrative structure (a formal Recovery Team, established 1975) tasked with guiding their recovery planning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat/Range**: All three species are endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system, implying large-river, warm-water, historically turbid and hydrologically dynamic habitat conditions typical of the pre-dam Colorado River Basin.
- **Conservation Status**: All three species are formally listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]], placing them under mandatory federal recovery planning.
- **Institutional Ecology**: The management context reflects a multi-agency, multi-jurisdictional recovery framework — federal ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) and state (New Mexico Department of Game and Fish) cooperation — reflecting the reality that recovery of these species requires coordination across state lines and stakeholder groups due to the fishes' large geographic range within the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Threats (Implied)**: The rationale cited for team formation — "complexity of their recovery program, the species range, or the need for coordination" — implicitly acknowledges the multi-state, multi-stakeholder threats affecting these species (e.g., flow alteration, habitat fragmentation, non-native species interactions), consistent with well-documented Colorado River Basin conservation issues, though not explicitly detailed in this letter.

## Key Findings
- The **Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team** was formally established in **December 1975** under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to assist in developing and implementing recovery plans for three endangered Colorado River fish species.
- The three listed species central to this recovery effort are:
  1. [[Humpback Chub]]
  2. [[Bonytail Chub]]
  3. [[Colorado Squawfish]] (syn. [[Colorado Pikeminnow]])
- As of the date of this letter (May 1989), the Team had **recently completed its final review of revised recovery plans** for these three species, indicating an active, ongoing, iterative recovery planning process spanning over a decade (1975–1989).
- Dr. David Probst, representing the **State of New Mexico Department of Game and Fish**, was nominated to join the Team, reflecting the cross-jurisdictional governance model used for wide-ranging endangered [[Freshwater Fish Conservation|freshwater fish]] species.
- The letter references enclosed **Service recovery planning guidelines**, indicating standardized federal protocols governing recovery plan development across listed species nationally, not just for the Colorado River fishes.
- This document serves as a primary historical record confirming the administrative lineage and multi-agency governance structure underpinning modern [[Colorado River Basin]] endangered fish recovery programs (which continue today under the Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program).
