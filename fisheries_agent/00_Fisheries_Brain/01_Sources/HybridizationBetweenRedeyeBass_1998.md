# Hybridization between Redeye Bass and Smallmouth Bass in Tennessee Streams

#ichthyology #Centrarchidae #black_bass #hybridization #genetic_conservation

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: [[Redeye Bass]] (*Micropterus coosae*) is one of four black bass species with limited ranges, naturally occurring in the Black Warrior, Cahaba, Coosa, and Tallapoosa portions of the Mobile River basin and tributaries of the Alabama River above the fall line in Alabama. In Georgia, redeye bass are indigenous to the Alabama River system and the Chattahoochee River above Columbus, and to headwaters of the Savannah River upstream of Hartwell Reservoir. [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*) was endemic to interior North America west of the Appalachians but has been widely introduced. Growth rates for redeye bass are slow, requiring 6 years to reach 200 mm. Age, maturity, and fecundity parameters not reported in this document.

**Habitat Associations**: The 11 streams containing redeye bass or hybrids were third-order or smaller mountain streams measuring 2.3–27.6 m wide and up to 1.5 m deep. Channel gradients ranged from 0.5–19.7 m/km, canopy coverage from 33–93%, and substrates were characterized by bedrock, boulder, cobble, and sand. Introduced redeye bass populations appear adaptable to various water quality and habitat conditions; heavy canopy coverage, low alkalinity and conductivity, and high gradients are not prerequisites for establishment.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Not reported. However, the presence of redeye bass in streams beyond documented stocking locations (e.g., Falling Water River and Post Oak Creek, Obed River and Otter Creek) suggests migration from stocked tributaries or undocumented stocking events.

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: Not reported.

**Environmental Drivers**: Hybridization between species is usually the result of unequal abundance of two parental species, competition for limited spawning habitat, natural and human-induced changes in environmental conditions, or previously allopatric species being brought together. The condition of previously allopatric species being brought together is the most probable cause of hybridization between [[Redeye Bass]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]]. Limited spawning habitat and natural or human-induced changes were not evaluated but may contribute.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: Between 1953 and 1960, 16 upper Cumberland region streams were stocked with redeye bass at 15–20 fish per stream. Poststocking assessment in 1962 and 1964 found only Spring Creek, Roaring River, and Calfkiller River still contained redeye bass populations. Current sampling (1994–1995) revealed more widespread occurrence in 11 streams within three river drainages, presumably from migration, undocumented stocking, and more sensitive detection criteria. Redeye bass populations are self-sustaining but provide only minimal catch-and-release fishery due to slow growth rates.

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: Extensive hybridization threatens genetic integrity of both parental species. Electrophoretic analysis identified hybrid genotypes in 66% of *Micropterus* spp. collected from 9 of 31 streams sampled. Meristic criteria identified hybrids in 20% of *Micropterus* spp. sampled in 6 streams within two river drainages. Introgression is highly probable in the [[Ocoee River]], [[Obed River]], and [[Roaring River]] systems. Because initial stocking occurred over 40 years ago, introgression of genes from one species into the gene pool of the other is highly probable and continuing. Methods to eliminate these hybridizing populations are impractical; populations will persist in various streams in north-central and southeastern Tennessee.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: Three key prestocking evaluation assumptions explain why introduction of redeye bass should not have occurred: (1) introductions are best suited for bodies of water sufficiently isolated so that introduced species does not spread uncontrollably; (2) the system to be stocked should have its biota thoroughly inventoried to determine species that might be influenced by introduction; (3) life history characteristics of species proposed for introduction must be thoroughly understood, and the ideal introduced species should have coevolved with many members of the fish assemblage to which it is being added. These hybridizing populations serve as constant reminders of the importance of genetic conservation as a goal in fisheries management decisions, especially when bringing together formerly allopatric congenerics is considered. No further introductions of redeye bass into streams containing smallmouth bass should occur.

**Biological Reference Points**: Not reported. The document notes that redeye bass populations do not provide a harvestable fishery because growth rates are slow and 6 years may be required for fish to reach 200 mm.
