## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific research paper but the legislative text of **Senate Bill 371** (New Mexico Laws 1995, Chapter 145), which amends the **Wildlife Conservation Act** (NMSA 1978, §17-2-37 through §17-2-46). The Act establishes the legal and procedural framework by which the New Mexico State Game Commission and the Department of Game and Fish identify, list, delist, and manage **threatened and endangered species**, including fish, mollusks, crustaceans, and other wildlife. Critically for aquatic science, the Act formally defines "fish" within the broader "wildlife" category, mandates biologically-driven listing criteria, and creates a structured process for scientific peer review, investigation, and recovery planning. While it contains no species-specific population data, it is a foundational regulatory document governing how ichthyological and aquatic conservation science is translated into state policy and legal protections in New Mexico.

## Background & Description

Senate Bill 371 amends the original 1974 **Wildlife Conservation Act** (Laws 1974, Chapter 83) to modernize procedures for the scientific assessment and legal listing/delisting of species. Key structural/definitional elements relevant to aquatic and fish science include:

- **"Wildlife"** (§17-2-38.N) is explicitly defined to include "any nondomestic mammal, bird, reptile, amphibian, **fish, mollusk or crustacean** or any part, egg or offspring, or the dead body or parts thereof" — establishing that fish and aquatic invertebrates fall under the same conservation and listing apparatus as terrestrial vertebrates.
- **"Endangered species"** and **"threatened species"** definitions incorporate cross-referencing to the federal [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]] (Section 4 lists of endangered/threatened native and foreign fish and wildlife), allowing the state to adopt federal listings in whole or in part.
- **"Ecosystem"** is defined broadly as "a system of living organisms and their environment," underpinning the Act's habitat-based approach to species management.
- **"Land or aquatic habitat interests"** are explicitly defined (§17-2-38.F) to include water rights, fee simple title, easements, and leases — indicating statutory recognition that aquatic habitat acquisition (e.g., stream reaches, riparian corridors, spring systems) is a legitimate conservation tool distinct from terrestrial land acquisition.
- The Act establishes a **peer review panel** requirement (§17-2-38.I; §17-2-40.B–C) mandating that a wildlife biologist and university-affiliated scientists review research methodology for any listing/delisting investigation, reflecting an attempt to formalize scientific rigor in species status determinations.
- A **biennial review** process (§17-2-40.A) requires the Director to systematically reassess all listed species using data on "population, distribution, habitat needs, limiting factors and other biological and ecological data."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although the Act is procedural rather than empirical, it encodes several ecologically relevant principles applicable to New Mexico's aquatic systems (home to numerous endemic and imperiled fish taxa such as [[Gila Trout]], [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]], and [[Gila Chub]]):

- **Habitat-based listing criteria**: Species may be listed as endangered or threatened due to "present or threatened destruction, modification or curtailment of its habitat" (§17-2-38.D(1)), directly applicable to stream dewatering, channelization, and riparian degradation common threats to New Mexico's native fish fauna.
- **Multi-factor threat recognition**: The definitions explicitly recognize overutilization, disease, predation, and combined anthropogenic/natural factors as legitimate bases for listing (§17-2-38.D(2–5)), aligning with modern multi-stressor frameworks used in aquatic conservation biology.
- **Multiple-species recovery plans** (§17-2-40.1.B) are explicitly authorized for species that "utilize similar habitats or share a common threat," a provision highly relevant to watershed-level recovery planning for co-occurring native fish assemblages (e.g., shared recovery plans across a single river drainage).
- **Emergency listing provisions** (§17-2-40.M) allow rapid listing action when "risk is likely to jeopardize the continued survival or recruitment of the species," relevant to acute events such as drought-driven stream desiccation or catastrophic fish kills.
- The Act's acquisition authority for "land or aquatic habitat interests" (§17-2-44.A) provides the legal mechanism for securing instream flows, spring habitats, and riparian buffers necessary for aquatic species recovery.

## Key Findings

- **Legal definition of "fish" as wildlife**: Fish, mollusks, and crustaceans are statutorily grouped with terrestrial vertebrates under "wildlife," subjecting them to identical listing, take-prohibition, and recovery-planning procedures (§17-2-38.N).
- **Formalized peer review requirement**: All state investigations into species status must undergo review by a panel including at least one wildlife biologist, formally institutionalizing scientific quality control in listing decisions (§17-2-38.I; §17-2-40.B).
- **Data-driven listing/delisting standard**: Commission decisions to list, delist, or maintain listing status must be based on "relevant and reliable evidence" and specifically "biological and ecological evidence" in the public repository file and hearing record (§17-2-40.L) — explicitly excluding socioeconomic data from the listing decision itself (§17-2-40.G).
- **Recovery plan objectives**: Recovery plans must aim for "restoration and maintenance of a viable population of the threatened or endangered species and its habitat reasonably expected to lead to the delisting of the species" (§17-2-40.1.E(1)), codifying a habitat-and-population-based recovery standard.
- **Timeframes for action**: Recovery plans must be finalized within two years of listing (§17-2-40.1.G); public information meetings must occur within one year of listing (§17-2-40.1.C); biennial reviews are mandatory (§17-2-41.B).
- **Habitat acquisition authority**: The Director is granted explicit authority to acquire "land or aquatic habitat interests" — including water rights — for conservation, restoration, and propagation of listed species (§17-2-44.A), a legally significant tool for freshwater/aquatic habitat protection in an arid state.
- **Federal-state list integration**: New Mexico may adopt some or all of the federal Endangered Species Act Section 4 lists of endangered/threatened native and foreign fish and wildlife, creating a mechanism for state-federal regulatory alignment (§17-2-38.D, M; §17-2-41.C(2)).
- **Judicial review mechanism**: A new section establishes formal appeal rights to the Court of Appeals for administrative listing actions, with a defined "arbitrary, capricious, or abuse of discretion" / "substantial evidence" standard of review (Section 8), providing a check on the scientific-regulatory process.
- **Contingent effective date**: Implementation of these amendments was made contingent upon appropriation of at least $450,000 total ($350,000 for prior conservation services obligations plus $100,000 for new amendment implementation) (Section 9), underscoring that funding — not just science — determines the practical application of these conservation procedures.
