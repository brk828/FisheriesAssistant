## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative and technical package from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (BR), announcing a May 1995 meeting to develop a **Fish Monitoring Plan for the Gila River Basin**. The plan is a required Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) stemming from a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) [[Biological Opinion]] on the transport and delivery of [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water. The core ecological concern is that the CAP aqueduct system — which transfers Colorado River water into the Gila River Basin — acts as a biological conduit, risking the introduction of non-indigenous fishes and other aquatic organisms into critical habitat for four federally protected native species: the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loachminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The draft plan includes an introductory framework and a statistical power analysis section intended to guide the design of a scientifically defensible, long-term monitoring program distinguishing true "monitoring" (with defined management thresholds and actions) from mere "surveillance."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Basin context:** The [[Gila River Basin]] spans Arizona and New Mexico and includes the Hassayampa, Agua Fria, Salt, Verde, San Pedro, and Middle/Upper Gila rivers and their tributaries.
- **Threat vector:** The [[Central Arizona Project]] aqueduct is identified as a novel conduit connecting the Lower Colorado River to Gila Basin drainages, facilitating potential invasion by non-native fishes and other aquatic organisms.
- **Critical habitat:** FWS (1994) determined the CAP water transport would adversely modify designated critical habitat for [[Spikedace]], [[Loachminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Target monitoring reaches** identified for the protocol:
  1. CAP Aqueduct
  2. Salt River Project (SRP) canals
  3. Florence-Casa Grande Canal
  4. [[Salt River]] between Stewart Mountain Dam and Granite Reef Dam
  5. Salt River between Granite Reef Dam and the electrical barriers
  6. [[Gila River]] below Coolidge Dam
  7. [[San Pedro River]] downstream of the U.S.-Mexico border
- **Management relevance:** Determining distribution, status, and trends of both non-native and native fishes across these reaches is framed as essential for informing management actions such as eradication, construction of fish exclusion structures ("EFES"), or habitat manipulation.

## Key Findings

- The FWS 1994 [[Biological Opinion]] concluded that CAP water delivery would **jeopardize the continued existence** of four native Gila Basin fishes: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loachminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- A Reasonable and Prudent Alternative requires BR, AGFD, and FWS to jointly develop and implement a **baseline study and long-term monitoring program** for non-native fish presence and distribution in the CAP aqueduct and selected river reaches.
- The proposed monitoring plan aims to: (1) establish baseline presence/distribution data on non-native fishes, and (2) detect changes in species composition or distribution over time.
- A true "monitoring" plan (as opposed to mere "surveillance," per [[Cairns 1980]]) must include: threshold criteria for triggering management action, and a defined management action plan — elements this draft explicitly builds toward.
- **Statistical Power Analysis** is treated as central to plan design:
  - Monitoring is framed as hypothesis testing — the null hypothesis being "no change" in a monitored parameter.
  - **Type II error (β):** failing to detect a real change (false sense of security); statistical **power = 1−β**.
  - **Type I error (α):** falsely concluding a change occurred when it did not.
  - Power increases with larger effect size and sample size, and decreases with higher data variability.
  - Convention cited: power should be ≥ 0.80, or α and β should be set equal (per [[Cohen 1988]], [[Peterman 1990]], [[Fairweather 1991]]).
  - Historical precedent cited: inadequate statistical power in data collection has contributed to **mismanagement and collapse of fisheries and marine mammal populations** (e.g., whale stock catch-per-effort analyses, Oregon coho salmon, sockeye salmon hatchery cost-benefit case).
- The proposed agenda for the May 22, 1995 meeting covers: monitoring goals (presence/absence, distribution, composition), statistical power analysis, repeatable field methodologies for streams/rivers/canals, database design, statistical testing and effect size vs. criteria, scheduling of data review, threshold criteria development, and formation of a management action "team."
- The overarching aspiration noted in the agenda is movement "**towards standardization of Gila River Basin aquatic monitoring**," implying this plan could serve as a template beyond the immediate CAP-related mandate.
