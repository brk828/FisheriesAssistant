# Virgin River Fish Recovery Team Meeting Minutes and Data (1992-1993)

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #recovery-team #virgin-river

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting minutes, agendas, and population monitoring data from the [[Virgin River]] Fish Recovery Team spanning September 1992 through April 1993. The Recovery Team coordinates multi-agency efforts to recover three federally listed native cyprinids: [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*), and [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*). Key activities include population monitoring at established stations, development of propagation protocols at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], implementation of [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) eradication programs, instream flow studies, and coordination of water development projects affecting critical habitat. The team represents federal agencies ([[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]]), state wildlife agencies (Utah, Nevada, Arizona), universities, and water districts.

## Background & Description

The [[Virgin River]] system in southwestern Utah, southeastern Nevada, and northwestern Arizona supports three endemic cyprinid species listed under the Endangered Species Act. The Recovery Team was established to coordinate conservation actions across jurisdictional boundaries.

**Target Species:**
- **[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*): Small-bodied cyprinid endemic to Virgin River
- **[[Virgin River Chub]]** (*Gila seminuda*): Roundtail chub subspecies/species endemic to Virgin and [[Moapa River]]
- **[[Virgin Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*): Petitioned for listing; inhabits perennial tributaries

**Non-native Species Threats:**
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*): Invasive cyprinid targeted for eradication in [[Fort Pierce Wash]] and canal systems
- Other introduced species documented in ponds and canal systems

**Key Facilities:**
- [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico): Primary propagation facility; 12 ponds lined for native fish culture
- [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] fish farm: Local holding facility
- [[UNLV]] Living Stream: University-based holding and research facility

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Virgin River ecosystem faces multiple anthropogenic stressors affecting native fish populations:

**Habitat Alterations:**
- [[Quail Creek Reservoir]] operations affecting flow and temperature regimes
- [[Washington Fields Diversion]] and [[Johnson Diversion]] fragmenting habitat
- [[State Route 9]] construction impacting river corridor
- Proposed [[Las Vegas Valley Water District]] surface water diversion (moved to ~1 mile north of [[Halfway Wash]])

**Water Development Projects:**
- [[North Creek]] reservoir proposals under environmental review with BLM
- [[Beaver Dam Wash]] deep well evaluations
- East Fork and North Fork development considerations

**Flow and Water Quality:**
- Instream flow studies conducted by BLM and at Washington Fields Diversion
- Temperature monitoring above and below Quail Creek
- USGS gaging stations providing continuous data
- Water quality/contaminant sampling proposed for [[Washington Fields Diversion]], [[Atkinville Wash]], and Halfway Wash sites

**Channel Dynamics:**
- Historical channel changes documented through aerial imagery
- Three-band false color composite photographs referenced to 7.5 minute quadrangle maps

## Key Findings

**Population Monitoring Results:**

*Spring 1992 Collections (April 20 - May 1):*
- Station-specific data collected across 32 monitoring stations
- Species codes: WF (Woundfin), SPKD (Spinedace), FS (Flannelmouth Sucker), DS (Desert Sucker), SPD (Speckled Dace), RC (Roundtail Chub), SH (Red Shiner), OT (Other)
- Red shiners dominated catches at several stations (e.g., Station 15: up to 305 individuals per sample)
- Native species present but in lower numbers at most stations

*Summer Collections (June 23-24, 1992):*
- Woundfin captured at multiple stations with highest counts at Station 11 (55 individuals) and Station 13 (10 individuals)
- Station 10 yielded 21 woundfin in one sample
- Red shiner continued to dominate many stations (e.g., Station 24: 678 individuals)

*Fall Collections (September 23-24, 1992):*
- Continued monitoring at established stations
- Data tables provided for Stations 10, 14, 15, 17, and 23

**Propagation and Refugia:**

[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] Status:
- 95 adult woundfin, 107 juveniles, 256 young-of-year maintained as of September 1992
- 120 [[Virgin River Chub]] held at facility
- Three ponds dedicated to woundfin; no specific target production for chubs established
- Temperature manipulation (70°F to 90°F over one week) successfully controlled ick (*Ichthyophthirius*) parasitism
- Post-treatment fish delayed spawning ~2 months but exhibited longer spawning period, suggesting potential for greater production
- Young-of-year chub production destroyed in 1992
- Target: ~200 fish for 1993 propagation, preferably collected November
- Contract awarded to study woundfin spawning behavior

Off-stream Facilities:
- [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] fish farm: 100 spinedace introduced mid-June 1992, lost to ick within one month (possible overfeeding); facility upgraded with water quality monitoring and lighting; re-introduction targeted for fall
- [[UNLV Living Stream]]: Rehabilitated and operational for 2 months; no native species present as of September 1992; 50 woundfin requested from salvage operations

**Red Shiner Eradication Program:**

Distribution Findings:
- Red shiners found in [[Fort Pierce Wash]] above previous treatment drip stations
- Red shiners present in [[Washington Fields Canal System]], creating bypass routes around Johnson and Washington Fields diversions
- Immediate threat to native populations upstream of Washington Fields Diversion
- Virgin River chubs found in Fort Pierce Wash up to ~1 mile above golf course
- Unpermitted introductions documented in private ponds; some permitted ponds contain unauthorized species

Proposed Actions:
- Barrier construction on Fort Pierce Wash
- Barrier in canal system above [[Sigmiller Marsh]]
- Treatment of Fort Pierce Wash and canal system
- Treatment of ponds with unauthorized species
- Optimal treatment window: October 15 - November 15 (based on flow conditions)
- Maintain all flow in main channel during treatment
- [[Utah State University]] providing technical assistance for drainage options to eliminate Sigmiller Marsh and "Duck Pond"
- Video imagery and three-band false color composite photographs obtained for treatment area
- Ground surveys essentially complete

**Recovery Plan Implementation:**

- Recovery Plan at printers as of September 1992; first public/agency review anticipated within 2-3 weeks
- Implementation Schedule requires careful Team review
- USFWS working on critical habitat designation for woundfin and Virgin River roundtail chub
- Propagation/Refugia Plan development delayed but anticipated by next meeting

**Taxonomic Studies:**
- Chub taxonomy report on Virgin River and Moapa River published (Paul Barrett)
- Report on treatment effects on Virgin River fishes near completion

**Virgin Spinedace Status:**
- USFWS petitioned to list spinedace; determination that listing is warranted to be published soon
- One-year timeline for final determination
- All perennial tributaries to mainstem Virgin River sampled
- Data entered and compiled with population estimates and length frequencies
- GIS map showing spinedace sampling locations and distribution 90% complete
- Draft report anticipated in 1-2 months

**Database Development:**
- Historical database translated to DBase format
- Data verified through current 1992 collections (excluding BLM Arizona and Nevada June collections)
- Data input routine completed; summary report procedures in development
- Draft standard report format to be distributed to Team

**Instream Flow Studies:**
- [[Washington Fields Diversion]] study report near finalization (Thom Hardy)
- Report covers habitat-flow relations above and below diversion
- BLM instream flow studies ongoing (Mike Herder); data availability and interagency coordination emphasized

**Water Rights and Development:**

[[Las Vegas Valley Water District]] Project:
- Surface diversion point moved to ~1 mile north of Halfway Wash
- Environmental report at printers (no public revision; comments allowed during water rights hearings)
- NEPA process required (state and federal)
- Storage reservoir planned at Halfway Wash
- Diversion structure to span entire river width
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] evaluating three alternatives for surface water diversion
- BOR conducting feasibility study and EIS
- Target date for Nevada State Engineer review: July 1993/1994
- Informal ESA consultation to be initiated by LVVWD

**Biological Opinions:**
- USFWS formally re-initiated consultation on [[Quail Creek Reservoir]]
- BLM and USFWS correspondence ongoing

**Monitoring and Research Priorities:**

Habitat Monitoring:
- Sub-committee appointed to formulate habitat monitoring plan addressing short and long-term needs
- Methodologies range from simple habitat availability estimates to formal ground-based measurements
- One-day working session planned

Genetic Monitoring:
- Focus on brood stocks and offspring targeted for re-introduction rather than wild populations
- Examination of existing specimens for genetic baseline work
- Historical data to establish baseline (Tom Burke coordinating; delivery December-March)

Re-introduction Protocols:
- Jim Heinrich leading development of draft protocol
- Henry Maddux assisting
- George Divine providing breeding protocol elements
- Issues to address: numbers, timing, frequency, genetic material infusion to brood stock
- Consideration of consistency with Colorado River Basin protocols while meeting Virgin River-specific needs

Ecological Studies:
- University research proposals solicited
- Top 5 Recovery Plan priorities emphasized
- Parasite questions inadequately addressed
- Community interaction studies critical for red shiner eradication efforts
- Life stage studies and flow relationships important

Habitat Improvements:
- Recovery Plan specifies feasibility assessments for habitat improvements favoring natives over non-natives
- Opportunities may involve increased water, improved quality, and appropriate timing
- Historical channel change analysis recommended (Leo Lentsch coordinating)

**Land Acquisition:**
- BLM identified high priority areas from [[Ash Creek]] and [[LaVerkin Creek]] downstream to Washington Diversion
- Additional Utah sites to Arizona border considered
- Draft plan anticipated within months
- Washington County Water Conservancy District interested in protective easements
- Arizona BLM Resource Management Plan identifies priority sites near Beaver Dam Wash
- Nevada draft RMP in progress

**Information and Education:**
- Video and resource materials on Habitat Conservation Plan and endangered species distributed to school districts (Nicole Young)
- Annual public meetings suggested for information exchange and public input
- Federal agency specialists available but lack viable delivery vehicle
- Independent organizations (The Nature Conservancy, Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance) may have better outreach opportunities
- BLM implementing community outreach through volunteer involvement in instream flow studies
- Simple newspaper articles on native fish life history recommended
- Brochure with common questions and answers proposed

**Meetings and Coordination:**
- Spring 1993 meeting scheduled April 26-29 at Thunderbird Hotel, St. George, Utah
- Field sampling April 28-29 (conditions permitting)
- Mid-winter meetings suggested to separate policy discussions from field monitoring activities
- Bonneville American Fisheries Society meeting in St. George 1993; special session on Virgin River water resources proposed

**Monitoring Station Adjustments:**
- Station locations and timing of collections to be reviewed winter prior to field sampling
- Addition of station 6 miles below [[Riverside]] suggested
- Hoop nets tested for chub monitoring during sampling period
