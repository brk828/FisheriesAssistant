# Colorado River Biologists First Annual Meeting (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #colorado-river-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This document is a November 30, 1993 circular from [[Wayne Gustaveson]] to all [[Colorado River]] biologists announcing the first annual meeting of a new biologists' group, held January 12-13, 1994, at the Flamingo Hilton in [[Laughlin Nevada]]. The meeting replaces the [[Sport Fish Committee]] of the CRWC, and the group becomes independent with no formal ties to the CRWC. The letter also includes a tentative agenda of fishery status reports, a list of suggested topics, and an organizational session for forming the new group.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Meeting announcement letter with an attached agenda (non-research correspondence).
- **Author:** Wayne Gustaveson, who was organizing meetings for the CRWC subcommittees.
- **Audience:** Researchers and managers at the biologist level. Administrators and other interested parties are welcome, but discussion is to focus on fish from a biologist's point of view.
- **Scope:** The needs of the fish and their habitat come first. The author acknowledges that politics cannot be fully separated from wildlife issues.
- **Logistics:** Registration fee of $15 to cover meeting costs, with any surplus to be managed by a newly elected treasurer during 1994. Rooms could be reserved by phone.
- **Response:** The author reported more responses in the month before the letter than in the previous ten years of subcommittee meetings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The meeting covers fisheries across the full Colorado River system, from the Lower Basin reservoirs to the Upper Basin waters. Agenda segments include:

- [[Lake Havasu]] and the tailwater above it
- [[Central Arizona Project Canal]] and inland waters
- [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Mead]]
- [[Lee's Ferry Tailwater]] and [[Lake Powell]]
- Upper Basin waters, including the [[San Juan River]], [[Elephant Butte]], and New Mexico, Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming waters

Suggested topics point to several management concerns: sport versus native fish conflicts, critical habitat status of "Big River Fishes," tailwater fisheries, and the expansion of species introductions and stocking. Stocking interest is linked to increased involvement from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

## Key Findings

- **New independent group:** The biologists' group replaces the Sport Fish Committee and operates without formal CRWC ties, continuing past practice.
- **Meeting structure:** Day one covers welcome, guidelines, and reports on each river segment (about 15 minutes per presentation). Day two covers organization of the new group: name, scope, bylaws, officers and their duties, meeting time and place, election of officials, oversight, and new business.
- **Presentation guidelines:** Reports should summarize recent knowledge and management implications rather than present raw data.
- **Priority topics suggested by members:**
  - Lake Powell's response to removal of the striped bass limit ([[Striped Bass]])
  - Status and impact of Big River Fishes critical habitat
  - [[Native Fish Reintroduction]] efforts
  - Utah's new species introduction efforts and future plans
  - Sport/native fish conflicts and management opportunities
  - The striped bass population increase in Lake Mohave and the work of the Native Fish work group
  - Implications of increased USFWS interest in stocking all fish in the Colorado River
- **Open question:** The group's name was left for members to recommend and decide at the January meeting; the author suggested "Colorado River Biologists."
- **Attendance intent:** The author called for broad participation, saying anyone who can contribute or benefit is invited.
