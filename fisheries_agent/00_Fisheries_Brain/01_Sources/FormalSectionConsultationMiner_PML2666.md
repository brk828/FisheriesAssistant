# Miner Flat Dam Project: Section 7 Consultation on Loach Minnow

#ichthyology #marine-biology #loach-minnow

## Executive Summary

This 1988 memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Phoenix, Arizona) to the [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]] Phoenix Area Office addresses a formal [[Section 7 Consultation]] on the proposed [[Miner Flat Dam Project]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The Service expects to concur that the project will have no effect on the [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Bald Eagle]], and [[Apache Trout]] (the OCR reads "tlachs trout," interpreted here as Apache trout). It expects that the [[Loach Minnow]] will be adversely affected. The project's [[Biological Assessment]] (April 1988) did not discuss effects on the loach minnow, and the Service states that the information needed to evaluate those effects is missing.

## Background & Description

The memo is a request for more information and an outline of the Service's position, not a final biological opinion. It is signed by the Field Supervisor and addressed to the Area Director.

**Species status.** The loach minnow population in the [[White River]] system is described as the last remnant of the species' historic upper-basin range. The memo calls it the only known population remaining in the lower [[Gila River]] basin. Other populations are in the upper Gila basin and in the [[Blue River]], [[San Francisco River]], and Gila River systems in New Mexico. The [[Black River]] population is presumed extirpated, and the [[Salt River]] tributaries sampled in 1983 contained no loach minnows.

**Distribution in the project area.** A population occurs in the [[East Fork White River]] and mainstem [[White River]] from above [[Nash Canyon]] downstream to the mainstem White River, covering at least 2.5 miles. The total occupied stretch is reported as "about ?.5 miles," a value the OCR does not render clearly. Sampling above and below this stretch has been very limited. Loach minnows probably do not extend more than a few miles further upstream in the East Fork because of habitat unsuitability. Biologists believe they may extend downstream a significant distance from the last known downstream collection near [[Canyon Day Diversion]], but access there is limited and documenting the downstream distribution will be difficult.

**North Fork White River.** Loach minnows have not been confirmed in the [[North Fork White River]]. Earlier sampling there found none, but much of it was done under adverse conditions. The Service states that more extensive sampling, targeted at suitable habitat, is needed before it can conclude that loach minnows are absent. Until then, the Service must assume they are present, based on their occurrence at the mouth of the North Fork and on large areas of apparently suitable habitat below Miner Flat.

**Surveys.** The 1983 White River sampling found the relict population and also sampled several other tributaries of the Salt River, but it found no loach minnows in any of them. A June 1988 survey by the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] is cited as a source of data. The memo says the lack of detailed information on project specifications, operations, and related facilities limits the analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo links the loach minnow's survival to the genetic and ecological condition of its remaining populations:

- **Limited populations.** The remaining populations are small and isolated, and none is considered safe from extirpation.
- **Genetic diversity.** The White River population is considered genetically distinct, though it may share some genetic material with other populations through the connecting Gila River system. The memo notes that maintaining [[Genetic Diversity]] among separate populations is important for long-term survival and for avoiding [[Stochastic Events]] that could wipe out a single population.
- **Recovery value.** The White River population is considered critical to the species' survival and recovery, even though it is subject to determination of its status.

The Service identifies the following potential project effects on the loach minnow and its habitat. Several are linked to changes in the flow regime at and below the dam:

1. Changes in [[Water Temperature]] from dam releases, including lowering of summer temperatures and changes in the annual thermal range.
2. Deprivation of substrate and gravel sources in the North Fork and mainstem White River.
3. Changes in minimum and peak flows in the North Fork and mainstem White River.
4. Introduction, either legal or illegal, of [[Non-Native Fish]] from the North and East Forks and from existing reservoirs.
5. Changes in water quality, including temperature, dissolved oxygen, and related parameters, below Canyon Day Diversion.
6. Changes in sediment transport and channel morphology in the North Fork and mainstem.
7. Dewatering of stream reaches below Canyon Day Diversion.
8. Increased sedimentation and reduced water quality below the diversion.
9. Mortality or injury of loach minnows in the Canyon Day Diversion area.
10. Changes in suitable habitat in the North Fork and mainstem.
11. Sedimentation and turbidity from construction.

The memo states that the diversion's effects are expected to be significant, although they were not discussed in the Biological Assessment.

## Key Findings

- **Preliminary opinion:** The Service likely will concur that the project has no effect on the peregrine falcon, bald eagle, and Apache trout. It expects the loach minnow to be adversely affected.
- **Data gaps:** The Biological Assessment did not address loach minnow distribution, habitat, or flow needs. The Service found that the detailed biological information needed to analyze effects was not available in time.
- **Distribution uncertainty:** The occupied reach is about 2.5 miles at minimum, and its downstream extent is unknown. The North Fork White River population cannot be ruled out, so the Service must assume it is present.
- **Significant diversion effects:** The Service expects the Canyon Day Diversion to have significant effects, and it considers the affected river miles, measured as occupied loach minnow habitat, an important parameter.
- **Information requests:** The Service asks for specific project details, including site specifications, operations, and effects on the loach minnow. It also asks for a meeting to discuss the biological assessment and possible measures to reduce impacts.
- **Conservation significance:** The White River population is the last remnant of an important part of the species' historic upper-basin range and the only known population in the lower Gila basin, so the Service treats its survival as central to recovery.
