# Biological Opinion on Proposed Introductions of Gila Topminnow in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service biological opinion (dated November 9, 1990) responds to a U.S. Forest Service request for formal [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] on the proposed introduction of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] into eight sites on the [[Tonto National Forest]] and [[Prescott National Forest]] in Arizona. The Service concludes that the proposed introductions, continued existing land uses, and certain anticipated management actions are **not likely to jeopardize the continued existence** of the species. Three sites are judged highly suitable, two are conditionally suitable, one should be deferred, and one should not be stocked until a fish barrier is in place. The opinion sets incidental take triggers, [[Reasonable and Prudent Measures]], terms and conditions, and conservation recommendations.

## Background & Description

**Species.** The Gila topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) was listed as endangered on March 11, 1967; no critical habitat has been designated. It is a small livebearing fish, one to two inches long, of the family Poeciliidae, known from the Gila, Sonora and de la Concepcion drainages of Arizona, New Mexico and Sonora, Mexico. It was once among the commonest fishes of the Gila River system. Habitat destruction (water diversion, downcutting, backwater draining, vegetation clearing, channelization, impoundment) and predation or competition from nonnative [[Mosquitofish]] caused its extirpation from most of its range. At the time of the opinion it was known from only 9 natural U.S. localities, about 30 introduced populations and several captive populations.

**Project.** The [[U.S. Forest Service]] proposed to stock at least 200 Gila topminnow of appropriate genetic stock into each of eight waters, with stocking in late spring or summer. Stock would come from natural populations, and removal could reach 50 percent of a donor population. The project also covered continuation of existing grazing, roads, recreation, fences and water developments, plus future foreseeable activities. Mining, new water diversions, new roads or facilities, and introduction of other species were excluded and would require separate consultation. Desert pupfish stocking was proposed by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] but was not part of this consultation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat condition.** Most sites have grazing under [[Allotment Management Plan]] systems, roads, recreation, fences and water developments. Riparian areas and springs are generally excluded from prescribed burns. Off-road-vehicle use is restricted to designated roads at all sites.
- **Nonnative species.** Sycamore Creek holds green sunfish and largemouth bass, and Middle Water Spring has an unidentified fish population that may be nonnative. Nonnative fish in the Verde River could move upstream into Sycamore Creek, so a barrier was proposed.
- **Vegetation.** [[Cattails]] are a rapidly growing problem at Artesian Well 3 and require periodic removal.
- **Hydrology and water quality.** Flows range from 0.33 gallons per minute at Artesian Well 3 to at least 10 gallons per minute at Long Gulch Artesian. Water temperature was 97° F at West Fork Pinto Creek and 84° F in the Artesian Well 3 pool (August 1989). Artesian Well 3 water was muddy.
- **Biological associates.** Longfin dace are abundant at West Fork Pinto Creek and present at Sycamore Creek. Lowland leopard frogs occur at Long Gulch Artesian. Beaver are present at Sycamore Creek.
- **Ownership and mitigation.** Private inholdings exist at West Fork Pinto Creek and Sycamore Creek. Long Gulch Artesian is a potential mitigation area for goose habitat lost to raising [[Roosevelt Lake]].

## Key Findings

**Overall conclusion.** Successful stocking would benefit recovery. Adverse effects are expected to be limited and are not expected to jeopardize the species.

**Site-by-site suitability (as judged by the Service):**
- **High likelihood of long-term suitability:** Reimer Spring, West Fork Pinto Creek and Cottonwood Creek. Each has relatively large perennial water, no nonnative fish, no major human impacts, and needs no intervention.
- **Conditionally suitable:** Artesian Well 3 (demonstrated 7-year persistence, but needs maintenance and cattail control). Mesquite Tank 2 (should be stocked if protected from unauthorized draining; the downstream drainage population supports suitability).
- **Moderate probability; not a high priority:** Middle Water Spring (nonnative fish present, artificial pool likely to fail without intervention).
- **Defer:** Long Gulch Artesian (high potential, minimal maintenance, but possible water diversion for goose habitat must be settled first).
- **Conditional on barrier:** Sycamore Creek (high-quality habitat and inaccessibility, but needs a functioning fish barrier before stocking; recreation and proximity to the Verde River raise bait-fish risk).

**Genetic management.** The Service stated that stock must follow the forthcoming Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan and interim guidance from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]. Random past introductions are considered obsolete. Proceeding out of accordance with that guidance would adversely affect recovery.

**Donor populations.** The proposed removal of up to 50 percent of a donor population was judged likely to cause unacceptably large adverse effects, because stochastic events could follow. Sharp Spring stock, obtainable from Dexter National Fish Hatchery, was judged acceptable.

**Ongoing activities.** Livestock grazing at the seven grazed sites appears compatible with survival. Fence maintenance, roads, trails, recreation and ORV restrictions are not expected to be substantially adverse. Maintenance of artificial facilities, sediment removal and cattail control have the highest potential for harm and incidental take.

**[[Incidental Take]] triggers.** Reinitiation of consultation is required if any of the following occurs:
1. Mortality exceeds an estimated 25 percent of fish captured, stocked, held or transported during an action.
2. Rapid abundance declines follow an action, or slower declines continue over the following year.
3. No detectable reproduction occurs in the next breeding season after an action.

**Terms and conditions (main points):**
- Captured holding stock must be at least 200 fish, including adult females, males and juveniles, held in suitable conditions until turbidity returns to near pre-project levels.
- Herbicides for cattails must be applied by wiping directly onto plants, not spraying.
- The cap on the Mesquite Tank 2 drain must be welded permanently.
- Permittees must be notified of the species and its legal protection, and this must be included in the AMP and annual operating plans.
- Baseline habitat data must be submitted before or within one month after stocking. Monitoring must occur within six months of stocking.
- Post-maintenance monitoring must occur at one week, one month, six months and one year.
- Stocking notifications and detailed action records (including before-and-after photographs) must be submitted in writing to the FWS.

**Conservation recommendations:**
- Repair the Reimer Spring exclosure fence.
- Acquire the private inholdings at West Fork Pinto Creek and Sycamore Creek.
- At Artesian Well 3, remove the collapsed shade structure, eradicate cattails before stocking, plant native shade trees, and stage cattail removal in halves a month apart.
- Survey Middle Water Spring fish before deciding on removal.
- Survey Sycamore Creek for a natural barrier and build an artificial one if none exists.
- Schedule maintenance during spring, summer or early fall, and minimize turbidity and sedimentation.
- Do not divert spring flows, prevent pollutant entry, and avoid actions that increase nonnative fish introduction risk.

**Reinitiation triggers.** Consultation must also be reinitiated if take limits are exceeded, new information reveals unconsidered effects, the action is modified, or a new species is listed. Operations must stop if additional take would cause irreversible harm (50 CFR 402.14(i)).
