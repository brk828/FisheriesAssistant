# Little Colorado Spinedace Proposed Listing as Threatened Species with Critical Habitat

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register proposed rule (May 22, 1985), circulated through the [[Arizona State Clearinghouse]] and accompanied by a cover letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], proposes listing the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*) as a threatened species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. It also proposes designating critical habitat in portions of East Clear, Chevelon and Nutrioso Creeks in Arizona, and special rules for take. The species is native to the upper Little Colorado River drainage but now survives in only a small part of the main river and parts of four tributaries. Decline is attributed to impoundment and diversion, habitat disturbance, and introduced exotic predatory and competitive fishes. Threatened rather than endangered status was selected because the species, though restricted in range, still occurs in several locations. The cover pages include a state review form with questions on agency goals, conflicts and legal consistency.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Lepidomeda vittata*, a cyprinid first described from specimens collected in 1874 by the [[Wheeler Survey]] (U.S. Geographical Surveys West of the 100th Meridian). The type locality was defined as the Little Colorado River near the mouth of the Rio Puerco-Little Colorado confluence area, in an area later defined as a canyon near Holbrook, Arizona.
- **Morphology/behavior:** Behaviorally and ecologically similar to a minnow-like fish; it persists in intermittent pools during drought and distributes itself across streams without apparent habitat preference during flows.
- **Spawning:** Primarily early summer, continuing at reduced levels into early fall (Minckley, 1978).
- **Status history:** Listed as a Category 1 candidate in the 1982 Federal Register review of vertebrate wildlife. A petition from the Desert Fishes Council was received April 12, 1983; a 90-day finding (June 14, 1984) and a 12-month finding (July 12, 1984) concluded listing was warranted.
- **Legal framework:** Section 4(a)(1) of the Act and implementing regulations (50 CFR Part 424) define the five listing factors applied in the proposal.
- **Geographic extent:** Historically throughout the upper Little Colorado River; now restricted to a small section of the main river and parts of East Clear, Chevelon, Nutrioso and Silver Creeks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat degradation (Factor A):**
- Approximately 150 impoundments exist in the Little Colorado basin, from small stock tanks to reservoirs of up to 1,400 surface acres. Reservoirs are uninhabitable for the species, inundate stream habitat, and cause dewatering downstream.
- Other disturbances include riparian destruction, urban growth, mining, timber and pinyon harvest, construction, livestock grazing, dewatering, erosion, channel downcutting, chemical and organic pollution, altered flow regimes, temperature change and sedimentation.
- The river above Grand Falls was once perennial with extensive riparian areas and marshes; it now flows perennially only in the uppermost 10 to 15 percent of its length.
- Proposed projects include Wilkin's Dam (Bureau of Reclamation, part of the Mogollon Mesa project), which would inundate about 5 miles of stream, and groundwater exploration in the Chevelon Creek drainage. Residential and urban growth near Snowflake, Taylor, Springerville and St. Johns are also potential threats.

**Overutilization (Factor B):** No evidence the species is overutilized for commercial, recreational, scientific or educational purposes.

**Predation (Factor C):** Introduced exotic predators, including black bullhead, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, green sunfish, largemouth bass, rainbow trout and brown trout, are a significant threat. Reservoirs favor predatory game fish, which encourage their spread.

**Inadequate regulatory mechanisms (Factor D):** State listing as a Group 3 Threatened Wildlife species regulates take only through collecting permits or licensed angling, and provides no habitat protection or management plan.

**Other factors (Factor E):** Introduced competitors, including golden shiner, red shiner and redside shiner, displace native spinedace. Piscicides (rotenone and toxaphene) were used in the drainage, including treatment of the Little Colorado River below Lyman Reservoir in 1951 and Chevelon Creek in 1965. These treatments likely reduced both population size and range.

**Population dynamics:** No population size estimates exist, but numbers fluctuate markedly. Threats must be assessed at low population levels, where habitat changes that are minor at higher levels could cause extirpation.

**Land status:** Much habitat is protected by remoteness or public ownership, including the Coconino and Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests and the Chevelon Creek Wildlife Area, though many populations lie on private land.

## Key Findings

- **Listing decision:** The Service proposes threatened status, not endangered, because the range is severely reduced but the species still occurs at several locations, and threats are generally localized.
- **Critical habitat proposed (three segments):**
  1. East Clear Creek, Coconino County: about 18 miles from the confluence with Leonard Canyon upstream to Blue Ridge Reservoir dam, plus about 13 miles above Blue Ridge Reservoir to Mormon Lake.
  2. Chevelon Creek, Navajo County: about 1 mile from the Little Colorado River confluence upstream to a spring source.
  3. Nutrioso Creek, Apache County: about 5 miles from the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest boundary upstream to Nelson Reservoir dam.
- **Constituent elements:** Clean, permanently flowing water with pools and a fine gravel or silt-mud substrate.
- **Activities that could adversely modify habitat:** Excessive groundwater pumping, impoundment, water diversion, chemical or biological pollutant releases, and introduction of exotic predatory or competitive fishes and their parasites.
- **Federal consultation:** No known Federal activities would be affected now. Section 7 consultation would be required if Wilkin's Dam is reactivated or if federally funded water projects are proposed.
- **Proposed special rule (50 CFR 17.44):** Prohibits take except for scientific, educational, enhancement or exhibition purposes; bars possession, sale or transport of illegally taken fish; and makes attempts to commit these acts unlawful.
- **Public process:** Comments were due by July 22, 1985, and public hearing requests by July 8, 1985, to the Albuquerque, New Mexico Regional Office (Gerald Burton, Endangered Species Biologist, Region 2).
- **Clearinghouse review:** Responses to the state review form were due within 17 working days of the date noted.
