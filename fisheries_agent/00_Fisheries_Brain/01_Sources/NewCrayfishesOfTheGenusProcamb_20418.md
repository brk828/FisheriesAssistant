# New Crayfishes of the Genus Procambarus from Alabama and Texas

#decapoda #astacidae #procambarus

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (Vol. 84, No. 11, pp. 81-94, 30 June 1971) by Horton H. Hobbs, Jr., of the [[Smithsonian Institution]], describes two new freshwater crayfish species of the genus *Procambarus*. [[Procambarus Capillatus]] is known from Alabama and belongs to the [[Barbatus Section]]. [[Procambarus Texanus]] is known from Texas and belongs to the [[Blandingii Section]]. The paper also questions a diagnostic trait of the Blandingii Section, namely asymmetrical male [[First Pleopod]]s, because *P. texanus* has nearly symmetrical pleopods. Both species are described from morphology of adults and types are deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]].

## Background & Description

The paper is a formal species description. Its stated context is a broader review of the infrageneric groupings of *Procambarus* that the author was undertaking, with revised diagnoses to follow in a separate study.

**Procambarus capillatus** (Figure 1)
- Diagnostic features include a rostrum without marginal spines or tubercles, an areola 31.2-33.7% of carapace length and 5.4-6.6 times longer than wide, and no cervical spines.
- Males have a hirsute mesial palm of the chela and asymmetrical first pleopods. Each pleopod bears four corneous terminal elements: a mesial process, a cephalic process, a caudal process, and a central projection.
- The holotype male (Form I) has a carapace length of 24.3 mm, height 12.4 mm, and width 12.4 mm. The allotypic female has a carapace length of 26.9 mm, and the morphotypic male (Form II) has a length of 25.0 mm (Table 1).
- The largest specimen is a female with a carapace length of 27.9 mm. The largest and smallest first-form males measure 27.7 and 24.3 mm.
- Colour phases are "spotted" (olive green with brown flecks and dark rectangular abdominal patches) and "striped" (a pinkish-tan dorsomedian stripe flanked by dark stripes).

**Procambarus texanus** (Figure 2)
- Diagnostic features include rostral marginal spines or tubercles delimiting the base of the acumen, and an areola 34.2-37.0% of carapace length and 8.5-16.4 times longer than wide.
- Males have nearly symmetrical first pleopods. Their distal extremity bears four corneous elements: a long mesial process, a cephalic process, a caudal process, and a central projection.
- The holotype male has a carapace length of 53.3 mm, height 24.6 mm, and width 26.4 mm. The allotype has a length of 45.4 mm, and the morphotype has a length of 45.0 mm (Table 2).
- The largest specimen is a first-form male with a carapace length of 54.6 mm. The smallest first-form male measures 45.0 mm.
- The preserved colour is dominated by reddish-brown carapace, scarlet uropods and telson, and a dark lateral band on the branchiostegites.

Both species share a generally similar gross morphology within the Barbatus Group. The main differences lie in the arrangement of the male [[First Pleopod]] terminal elements and in the [[Annulus Ventralis]] of the female.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and burrowing.** The type-locality of *P. capillatus* is a drainage ditch adjacent to Burnt Corn Creek (Escambia River drainage) on State Route 41, northwest of Brewton, Escambia County, Alabama. Adults were dug from complex burrows in sandy clay soil beside a wooded area. Trees recorded there include *Liquidambar styraciflua*, *Quercus* sp., and *Pinus* sp., with abundant *Salix* sp. along the ditch. Both the author and the paper describe the species as a primary or secondary [[Burrowing Behavior|burrower]].

**Geographic range.** *Procambarus capillatus* is, as far as is known, confined to the [[Escambia River Basin]] in Escambia and Conecuh Counties, Alabama, and perhaps Escambia County, Florida. Atypical specimens tentatively assigned to the species came from three further Alabama localities and from Bluff Springs, Escambia County, Florida. The paper notes that the localities extend the northwestern limit of the Barbatus Section range by less than 50 miles.

*Procambarus texanus* is known only from its type-locality, a fish hatchery near Smithville, Bastrop County, Texas. It was collected together with [[Procambarus Clarkii]] and a subspecies of [[Procambarus Acutus]].

**Ecological counterparts.** The paper describes *P. rathbunae* as the ecological counterpart of *P. capillatus* in the adjacent [[Yellow River Basin]] in Florida.

**Life history.** All specimens definitely assigned to *P. capillatus* were collected in April 1970, when first-form males were obtained. No ovigerous or young-carrying females were observed. The *P. texanus* specimens were collected in late spring and early summer. The author infers that adult males were in the first form through the winter and spring. No females with eggs or young were observed.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species are described.** *Procambarus capillatus* (Alabama, Barbatus Section) and *P. texanus* (Texas, Blandingii Section) are formally named, each with a holotype, allotype, and morphotype.
2. **Pleopod symmetry challenges a diagnostic trait.** The Blandingii Section has been diagnosed by asymmetrical male first pleopods. *P. texanus* has an almost completely symmetrical pair, which the author notes as an exception that a forthcoming review will address.
3. **Range extension.** The Alabama and Florida localities of *P. capillatus* extend the northwestern range of the Barbatus Section by less than 50 miles.
4. **Diagnostic traits of *P. capillatus*.** Key traits are the rostrum without marginal spines, the lack of cervical spines, the hirsute male chela palm, and the asymmetrical first pleopods with four corneous terminal elements.
5. **Diagnostic traits of *P. texanus*.** Key traits are the rostral marginal tubercles, the more elongate and less hirsute right chela, the multituberculate sternal prominences overhanging the annulus ventralis, and the nearly symmetrical first pleopods.
6. **Relationships.** *P. capillatus* is most closely related to *P. rathbunae* and more distantly to other Barbatus Group members. *P. texanus* is most similar to *P. acutus* and its subspecies and to [[Procambarus Lecontei]]. It is considered distinct from the typical *P. a. acutus* because of its shorter, less twisted male pleopod terminal elements.
7. **Type series and material.** The types are deposited in the National Museum of Natural History, along with paratypes. For *P. capillatus* the paratypes include 13 females and numerous juveniles. For *P. texanus* the paratypes are reported as five males and one female.
8. **Limited known distribution.** *P. texanus* is known from a single locality, and *P. capillatus* is known from the Escambia River basin, which points to restricted ranges for both species.
9. **Reproductive data gaps.** Neither species had ovigerous or young-carrying females observed in the material studied.
