## Executive Summary

This memorandum summarizes a two-day (July 12–13, 1995) fisheries survey of the [[Gila River]] conducted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department's Native Fish Program, in cooperation with BLM, USBR, USFWS, and San Carlos Recreation and Wildlife Department personnel. The primary purpose of the expedition was a BLM-led raft survey for [[Willow Flycatcher]] habitat, with fisheries sampling conducted opportunistically along the ~40+ mile reach from below Coolidge Dam to near Winkleman, Arizona. Raft-mounted electrofishing, seining, and overnight hoop-netting were used across multiple habitat types (runs, riffles, pools, backwaters). A total of 326 fish representing ten species were documented, including both native catostomids ([[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]]) and several nonnative species ([[Common Carp]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Green Sunfish]]), alongside the native [[Longfin Dace]]. Overall fish condition was reported as good, with no external parasites (e.g., *Lernaea*) observed.

## Background & Description

Detailed morphometric data were limited in this report, but the following observations were recorded:

- **Length and weight** measurements were taken for all captured suckers (*Pantosteus clarki* and *Catostomus insignis*), as well as for occasional [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] individuals.
- **Life stages**: Adult and juvenile fish were documented for all species except common carp and mosquitofish, for which only adults were noted. Young-of-the-year individuals were present among suckers and largemouth bass.
- **Larval fish** were not reported for any species during this survey.
- **Spawning condition** was not observed in any collected specimens at the time of sampling.
- **Health status**: Fish collected appeared to be in good overall health; no *Lernaea* (anchor worm) or other parasites were observed on any specimens.
- Voucher specimens were preserved from one seine haul, including [[Longfin Dace]], both sucker species ([[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]]), [[Red Shiner]], and [[Green Sunfish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat context**: The surveyed reach of the Gila River is canyon-bound but broad enough in places to support a moderately dense riparian overstory of cottonwood, willow (including large mature trees), and tamarisk, with a dense understory of cocklebur and grasses. Emergent aquatic vegetation included *Typha* and *Phragmites*, and the filamentous algae *Cladophora* was present in the channel. Macroinvertebrates were uncommon in seine samples, consisting primarily of belostomatid true bugs (giant water bugs). The river channel meanders considerably, with frequent strainers and debris piles despite the absence of rapids.

**Water quality** (measured via Hydrolab):
- River flow: ~1000 cfs (Coolidge Dam gauge)
- Temperature: 13.7–18.7°C
- Conductivity: 717–724 µS
- Dissolved oxygen: 9.0–9.1 ppm
- pH: 7.7–8.2
- Turbidity: moderate

**Habitat partitioning among species**:
- [[Common Carp]] were most abundant along river banks in areas with overhanging or emergent vegetation, and were more frequently encountered in the upper (coldwater) portion of the surveyed reach.
- [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] were more common in riffle and midchannel run habitats, and were more prevalent in the lower portion of the reach — though this pattern may partly reflect a sampling bias toward riffle/midchannel habitat later in the trip.
- Backwater and seine-suitable habitats were rare throughout the reach; where sampled, seining captured smaller-bodied native and nonnative cyprinids and poeciliids ([[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Mosquitofish]]) alongside [[Green Sunfish]].
- Overnight hoop-net sets (~36 hours total) in run habitats near campsites failed to capture any fish, though one set captured two large crayfish, indicating some macroinvertebrate presence not detected via seining.

**Associated wildlife**: The riparian corridor supported abundant birdlife, including bald eagles, black hawks, common mergansers, hooded and northern orioles, black phoebes, canyon wrens, cardinals, blue grosbeaks, summer tanagers, various warblers, and two pairs of [[Willow Flycatcher]] observed only at the launch-site campsite. Mammalian sightings included coati-mundi and javelina. Cattle and cattle sign were present but generally uncommon along the reach.

## Key Findings

- A total of **326 fish** across **10 species** were documented during **12,107 electrofishing seconds** (raft-mounted, AC and pulsed DC, 5–15 amperage) across 7 delineated survey stations.
- Electrofishing directly documented five species with retrieved specimens: [[Common Carp]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Channel Catfish]]; large carp and unidentified fast-swimming suckers were visually counted from the raft but not netted.
- Seining added five additional species not captured by electrofisher: [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Mosquitofish]], and [[Green Sunfish]] — though suitable seining sites (backwaters) were very rare in this canyon-bound reach.
- Spatial habitat segregation was evident: nonnative [[Common Carp]] favored vegetated bank habitat in the cooler upper reach, while native suckers favored riffle/midchannel habitats, more common in the lower reach.
- No spawning fish, larvae, or parasitized individuals were observed; overall fish health appeared good.
- Hoop-netting effort (36 hours) was ineffective for fish capture in this reach, suggesting run habitats adjacent to campsites may not be optimal for passive net gear, or that fish density/activity patterns limited capture success.
- This survey contributes baseline fisheries data for the Coolidge Dam–to–Winkleman reach of the [[Gila River]], useful for future monitoring of both native catostomid populations and nonnative species establishment in this Sonoran desert riverine ecosystem.
