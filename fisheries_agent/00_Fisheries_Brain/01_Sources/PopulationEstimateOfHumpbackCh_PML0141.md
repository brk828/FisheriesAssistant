# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Westwater Canyon, Colorado River: FY 2003 Annual Project Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 2003 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 132) documents the first of three years of work to estimate the population of endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in Westwater Canyon, [[Colorado River]], Utah. Population estimates are needed to meet recovery goals that depend in part on maintaining humpback chub in several locations. The study also tracks natural recruitment of subadults and the sympatric [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*). Three sampling passes were completed in September, October and November 2003 using several gear types. Catches totaled 298 adult humpback chub and 636 adult roundtail chub, and long-term recaptures of tagged fish were recorded for both species. The report also notes equipment failure that limited [[Electrofishing]] effort and a budget that was 80% spent by the reporting date.

## Background & Description

**Project identity.** Project 132 is titled "Population estimate of humpback chub in Westwater Canyon, Colorado River, Utah." Principal investigators were [[Paul Badame]] and [[J. Michael Hudson]], both of the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR) Moab Field Station.

**Objectives.**
1. Obtain a population estimate of adult humpback chub (≥200 mm total length) in Westwater Canyon.
2. Determine the mean estimated recruitment of naturally produced subadult humpback chub (150–199 mm) in Westwater Canyon.

**Study schedule.** The study runs from an initial year of 2003 to a final year of 2005. The work is tied to the Colorado River Action Plan (Mainstem, p. 36), under section V.C.2 (Westwater), which covers estimating humpback chub populations.

**Species.** Humpback chub is listed as endangered. Roundtail chub is a co-occurring native cyprinid. Both are caught together in the canyon, and subadult fish of the genus *Gila* that were too small for reliable identification to species were recorded separately as "Gila subadults."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Westwater Canyon is a reach of the Colorado River that spans river mile (RM) 124 to RM 116. Five sites were sampled:
- Miners Cabin (RM 124)
- Upper Cougar (RM 122.5)
- Lower Cougar (RM 121.5)
- Hades Bar (RM 120)
- Big Hole (RM 116), dropped after Pass 1 because no humpback chub were present below the rapids. Only one roundtail chub (442 mm) was caught there.

**Flow and temperature.** Flow and water temperature were recorded at [[USGS Gage 09163500]] (Colorado River near the Colorado–Utah State Line, provisional data).

| Pass | Dates (2003) | Mean flow (cfs), range | Mean temp (°C), range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sep 12–19 | 4,283 (3,550–5,510) | 16.2 (15.3–16.7) |
| 2 | Oct 9–15 | 3,234 (3,120–3,360) | 14.1 (11.6–16.5) |
| 3 | Nov 3–9 | 3,001 (2,870–3,090) | 8.2 (7.5–9.5) |

Across the study, daily mean flow ranged from 2,870 to 5,510 cfs and daily mean water temperature from 7.5 to 16.7 °C. Flow fell and water cooled steadily from September to November.

**Gear and effort.** Methods included [[Trammel Netting]], [[Electrofishing]], [[Hoop Netting]] and [[Minnow Trapping]]. Seven to twelve trammel nets were set per site depending on habitat. Reported effort was:
- Trammel netting: 1,729.34 hours
- Electrofishing: 2.04 hours (planned effort was not reached; see Key Findings)
- Hoop netting: 459.17 hours
- Minnow trapping: 254 hours

Every chub was identified to species, scanned for a [[PIT Tagging]] (PIT) tag, tagged if none was present, measured (total and standard length), weighed, and had its dorsal and anal fin rays counted before release.

## Key Findings

**Catch totals**
- Adult humpback chub: 298 captured.
- Adult roundtail chub: 636 captured.
- Subadults: 6 humpback chub, 25 roundtail chub, and 121 *Gila* subadults (too small to identify reliably to species). The report also cites an additional 120 subadult chub, with a mean total length of 131.8 mm (range 52–187 mm).

**Size**
- Humpback chub (all methods): mean total length 267.3 mm, range 190–392 mm.
- Roundtail chub (all methods): mean total length 261.1 mm, range 111–441 mm.

**Recaptures**
- Humpback chub: 50 adults had been PIT tagged in earlier years (long-term recaptures). 12 were recaptured within 2003.
- Roundtail chub: 43 adults had been PIT tagged in earlier years. 9 were recaptured within 2003.
- Long-term recaptures were observed for both species on every trip. However, their number was lower than in 2000 and similar to 1998. Within-year recapture rates were similar to 1998.

**Catch per unit effort (CPUE)**
- Electrofishing CPUE for chub in 2003 was among the highest ever recorded by UDWR since monitoring began in Westwater Canyon. Electrofishing CPUE was 8.824 for humpback chub, 34.844 for roundtail chub, and 40.196 for *Gila* subadults. Trammel net CPUE was 0.168 for humpback chub and 0.468 for roundtail chub.

**Operational issues**
- Electrofishing was planned for all three passes but was done only on Pass 2. Motor mechanical failure prevented it on Pass 1. The limited electrofishing that was done was highly productive and may point to a strong recruitment year.

**Unexpected movement**
- On the night of November 3, 2003, at Miners Cabin, 183 chub were captured in trammel nets during the night and the following morning. Only 24 were humpback chub; the rest were roundtail chub. About 75% were caught in the first net set. Very few were recaptures. The authors concluded that these fish had moved into the area from somewhere they had not previously been available for tagging.

**Recommendations**
1. Conduct electrofishing during the first two passes of 2004 and 2005.
2. Use [[Radiotelemetry]] to track humpback and roundtail chub and explain undocumented movement patterns.
3. Add a night of sampling at the Upper Cougar site.
4. Add a site between Miners Cabin and Upper Cougar to improve canyon coverage.

**Project status and budget**
- Status: ongoing. The first of three years is complete.
- Budget: $85,500 budgeted; $68,400 expended or obligated; $17,100 remaining.
- Work completed: 80% for FY 2003.
- Recovery Program funds spent on publication: $0.
- Data entry and transfer to USFWS were scheduled for January 15, 2004. The report was signed November 25, 2003.
