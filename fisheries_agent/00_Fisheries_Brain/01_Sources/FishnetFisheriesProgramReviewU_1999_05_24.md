# FISHNET Fisheries Program Review — USDA Forest Service Southwestern Region (1999)

## Executive Summary
This document is a five-year follow-up external program review conducted by the [[Fisheries Network (FISHNET)]] — a coalition of conservation and sport-fishing NGOs (Izaak Walton League of America, Trout Unlimited, American Rivers) — in partnership with USDA Forest Service national staff, evaluating the [[Southwestern Region Fisheries Program]] (Arizona and New Mexico National Forests). The review assessed budget allocation, staffing, watershed management, native fish restoration, recreational fishing compliance, and partnership effectiveness. The central finding is that the Region is undergoing a philosophical shift from a reactive, species-by-species and ESA-litigation-driven management style toward a proactive, ecosystem/watershed-level approach — but implementation on the ground still lags behind stated intentions. Chronic understaffing of fisheries biologists and hydrologists, inconsistent monitoring protocols, livestock grazing impacts on riparian zones, and an urgent opportunity to restore full flows to [[Fossil Creek]] via FERC relicensing were identified as top priorities.

## Background & Description
- **Review Purpose**: Periodic external audits by FISHNET member organizations of Forest Service Regional Fisheries Programs, intended to (1) evaluate spending of fisheries appropriations, (2) assess program effectiveness against Forest Service and Executive Order 12962 ([[Recreational Fisheries]]) objectives, (3) identify improvement opportunities, and (4) foster partnerships.
- **Prior Review**: A previous FISHNET review of the Southwestern Region occurred September 13–17, 1992.
- **Review Team**: Ron Scott (Izaak Walton League), David A. Nolte (Trout Unlimited — Bring Back the Natives Program), Matthew Sicchio (American Rivers); Forest Service participants Mark Hudy, Max Copenhagen, and Don Duff.
- **Scope of 1999 Review**: One-day regional briefing in Albuquerque (La Posada), one day of field visits to the Jemez Ranger District ([[Santa Fe National Forest]]), briefings from [[New Mexico Department of Game & Fish]] and [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]], and a closeout session with the Regional Forester.
- **Forests Covered**: Gila NF, Tonto NF, Apache-Sitgreaves NF, Santa Fe NF, Carson NF, Lincoln NF, Coconino NF, Prescott NF, Cibola NF, and Kaibab NF.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The Southwestern Region hosts a high diversity of **native warmwater and coldwater [[Threatened, Endangered, and Sensitive (TES) Species|TES]] fish**, including native sport fish such as [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], and [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]], alongside numerous non-game native desert fish assemblages.
- **Riparian zones and streams** are described as convergence points for fish, wildlife, livestock, and human recreational use — making them ecologically critical but also highly vulnerable multi-use zones.
- **Livestock grazing** was identified as the most significant and most-discussed source of ongoing habitat degradation, producing:
  - Destabilized stream banks and incised channels
  - Loss of riparian vegetation and large woody debris
  - Elevated water temperatures
  - Some progress has been made (e.g., reduced grazing on the Gila, Upper Verde, and Blue Rivers), largely driven by ESA §7 consultation requirements on grazing allotments.
- **Roads and recreational human use** (camping, fishing, hiking) were flagged as significant but under-addressed contributors to watershed degradation.
- **Groundwater–surface water connectivity**: Only the Tonto NF had proactively addressed groundwater withdrawal impacts on surface flows and aquatic/riparian ecosystems; this was highlighted as a likely Region-wide issue needing a formal policy.
- **Instream flow rights** (state law and federal reserved water rights) were noted as an underexplored but critically important issue, with the Gila NF's "white paper" cited as a potential starting model.
- **Fossil Creek** (Childs-Irving hydroelectric project, FERC relicensing) was singled out as a rare, time-sensitive opportunity to restore full natural flows to one of the Region's most unique aquatic ecosystems.
- **Recreational fishing pressure** was noted as the highest of any USFS Region nationally, compounded by rapid regional human population growth.

## Key Findings

### 1. Forest Planning & Program Effectiveness
- Native fish issues are **not fully integrated into Forest Management Plans**, risking non-compliance with the [[National Forest Management Act (NFMA)]].
- **Recommendation**: Rapidly incorporate fisheries/aquatic ecosystem strategies into plan revisions with measurable objectives, standards/guidelines, and monitoring provisions; involve fisheries and hydrology staff and external partners; leverage Forest Service Research staff (e.g., Dr. John Rinne) for TES species recovery.
- Lack of a **systematic watershed health assessment process** at the sub-basin level across all Forests.
- Inconsistent dissemination of effective protocols across the Region — e.g., the Tonto NF's "Protocol for Monitoring Riparian Areas" (Janet Johnson) was not shared Region-wide.
- No clear Region-wide **effectiveness monitoring system**; reporting tends to emphasize output metrics (e.g., trees placed in streams) rather than true ecological effectiveness.

### 2. Fisheries Program Capabilities
- **Budget allocation** across Forests uses an overly rigid formula (stream miles, surface acres, angler days); recommendation to adopt more flexible criteria reflecting initiative, time-sensitive opportunities, and native fish restoration priorities.
- **Accountability lapse**: Apache-Sitgreaves NF reportedly used Fisheries Program dollars (rather than grazing program funds) for ESA §7 consultation on a grazing allotment.
- **Staffing deficiencies**: Insufficient fisheries biologists and hydrologists (only 7 professional hydrologists Region-wide). Recommendations:
  - Create a new **Desert Fish Coordinator** position.
  - Hire at least **3 additional fisheries biologists** within the year, based in Supervisor's Offices (shareable across Forests).
  - Long-term goal: at least one fisheries biologist and one hydrologist per National Forest.

### 3. Watershed Management
- **Riparian/Aquatic Ecosystem Health**: Recommend closing/reducing grazing allotments, excluding cattle from stream banks, active rotational management, and watershed-scale (not allotment-by-allotment) permit review with cross-disciplinary stakeholder involvement.
- **Roads & Recreation Impacts**: Recommend a comprehensive roads management plan (closures, obliteration, maintenance) and curbing undesignated campsites/vehicle trails near streams.
- **Native Fish Restoration & Public Outreach**: Prioritize restoration of full native fish assemblages, not just single species. Named restoration opportunity streams:
  - [[Rio Cebolla]] (Santa Fe NF)
  - [[Comanche Creek]] (Carson NF)
  - [[Cedar Creek]] (Lincoln NF)
  - [[Las Animas Creek]] (Gila NF)
  - [[Oak Creek]] (Coconino NF)
  - [[Verde River]] (Prescott NF)
  - [[Dude Creek]] (Tonto NF)
  - [[Blue River]] (Apache-Sitgreaves NF)
  - Recommendation: each Forest should actively pursue at least one restoration project per TES aquatic species present or historically present.
- **Fossil Creek Restoration**: Urgent priority tied to Childs-Irving FERC relicensing; recommend seeking Washington Office/National Instream Flow Coordinator (Bob Deibel) support and building an administrative record for FPA §4(e) mandatory conditions to secure full flow restoration.
- **Water Policies**:
  - Recommend a Region-wide **groundwater withdrawal policy** modeled on Tonto NF's approach (recognizing groundwater–surface water connectivity, requiring proof of no off-Forest alternatives, burden of proof on permittees).
  - Recommend developing a comprehensive **instream flow rights strategy**, potentially using the Gila NF white paper as a template, adapted for AZ/NM legal differences.
- **Inventory & Monitoring (I&M)**: Region-wide I&M program is underdeveloped. Recommend a watershed-analysis-based I&M program (down to sub-basin level), leveraging Rocky Mountain Research Station, universities, state agencies, and volunteer partnerships for data collection (e.g., temperature monitoring, presence/absence surveys).

### 4. Recreational Fisheries
- Compliance with **[[Executive Order 12962]]** (Recreational Fisheries) is generally being met within budget/staffing constraints. Noted strong programs:
  - Warmwater fisheries: Tonto NF, Cibola NF
  - Coldwater fisheries: Santa Fe NF, Carson NF, Apache-Sitgreaves NF, Kaibab NF
- Recreational fishing pressure is the **highest in the National Forest System**, driven by regional population growth.
- Recommend expanding National Fishing Week-style outreach beyond a single week annually, and continued coordination with state agencies and USFWS.
- **Balanced Fisheries Management**: Recommend a "**zoned**" management approach — prioritizing certain streams for native fish restoration/conservation while designating others for non-native recreational sport fishing, guided by the USFWS policy on conserving listed species while enhancing recreational fisheries. Native sport fish emphasized for outreach: [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]].

### 5. Partnerships
- **State/Federal Agencies**: Strong relationships noted with NM Dept. of Game & Fish and AZ Game & Fish Dept.; weaker visible partnership with [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] despite its central role in listed species management. Recommend strengthening ties via the Southwest Strategy initiative.
- **NGO Partnerships**: Some Forests showed breakdowns in communication and partner engagement (e.g., Trout Unlimited's Old Pueblo Chapter had to proactively request project info from Apache-Sitgreaves NF). Recommend reviving an institutionalized "**Mini-FISHNET**"-style working group incorporating all relevant disciplines (fisheries, wildlife, hydrology, recreation, grazing, timber, engineering, GIS) to improve communication, prioritize projects, leverage funding, and highlight successes.
- Recommend broadening the definition of viable partners beyond traditional conservation/sport groups (cf. innovative partnerships already modeled by [[Gila National Forest]] with Phelps Dodge, and [[Tonto National Forest]] with the Maricopa Detention Center).

## Notable Program Successes Highlighted
- **Gila NF** & **Phelps Dodge** (mining company partnership) — innovative non-traditional partnership.
- **Tonto NF** & **Maricopa Detention Center** — innovative labor/partnership model.
- **Lincoln NF** — highly efficient use of limited fisheries funding for National Fishing Week programming.
- **Tonto NF** — leading Region in groundwater withdrawal policy development and riparian monitoring protocol design.
