## Executive Summary

This document is an interagency comment letter from the **Western Area Power Administration (Western)** to the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS)**, responding to a **Lower Colorado River Fish Recovery Prospectus**. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological field data, the letter provides **management, policy, and methodological critique** related to the recovery of native "**Big River**" fish species (collectively referring to large-bodied Colorado River natives such as [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]]) and their designated critical habitat in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]].

Western highlights its prior experience as a primary participant in the **Upper Colorado River Basin recovery program** and draws on that experience to inform comments on the lower basin's parallel efforts. The letter identifies multiple concurrent recovery initiatives and advocates for consolidating them into a **single coordinated management strategy** rather than pursuing fragmented, overlapping efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter implicitly addresses ecological and habitat considerations relevant to native fish recovery in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]:

- **Habitat type divergence between basins**: The lower basin is characterized by **reduced lotic (free-flowing river) habitat** and an **increased abundance of lacustrine (reservoir) habitat**, a fundamental ecological difference from the upper basin that the letter argues is under-addressed in the Prospectus.
- **Nonnative species interactions**: Identified as a critical, insufficiently emphasized factor in both basins. The letter calls for explicit objectives addressing competitive and predatory pressures from [[Nonnative Fish Species]] on native "Big River" fish.
- **Chemoreception and habitat imprinting**: Raised as a potential factor influencing the survival and homing behavior of stocked or reintroduced fish in novel environments, based on ongoing (but inconclusive) studies in the upper basin.
- **Hydrology and flow management**: Notes the tension between **altered water release regimes** (needed for ecological restoration/habitat management) and **hydropower generation requirements**, framing this as a central balancing challenge for the management plan.
- **Reservoir-based projects**: The [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Project]] is cited as an example of habitat-specific (lacustrine) management activity within the broader lower basin recovery framework.

## Key Findings

- Western identifies **four concurrent lower basin projects** relevant to "Big River" fish recovery:
  1. Bureau of Reclamation's **Lower Colorado River Operations' Biological Assessment**
  2. **Feasibility Assessment for a Multi-species Management Program** – Lower Colorado River
  3. **Lower Colorado River Ecosystem Plan Assessment**
  4. **[[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Project]]**

- Western recommends **consolidating these efforts** into a single coordinated recovery and habitat stabilization strategy for efficiency and effectiveness.

- **Specific critiques and recommendations on the Prospectus** include:
  - Add emphasis on the **lotic vs. lacustrine habitat disparity** between upper and lower basins.
  - Add an explicit **objective addressing nonnative fish species interactions**.
  - Incorporate **genetic fingerprinting** methodology to screen batches of artificially propagated fish prior to wild release, ensuring nonconflicting genetic composition.
  - Expand **Fish Health Diagnoses** protocols to include **contaminant screening of release sites** and **physiological conditioning** of hatchery-reared fish before release, based on upper basin lessons learned.
  - Consider **chemoreception effects** on stocked/reintroduced fish behavior and environmental response.
  - Address the **management dilemma of surplus fish** produced by captive propagation and refugia programs.
  - Conduct **habitat surveys** to establish a baseline for assessing population status and guiding habitat restoration; recommends use of a **GIS database** to maximize programmatic benefit.
  - Add a **nonnative species interaction objective** to support management actions tied to life history studies of competitors/predators.
  - Recognize that **altered water release schedules** for ecological purposes will materially affect **hydropower operations**, requiring balance in the final management plan.
  - Flag unclear wording in the Prospectus's habitat-related bullet point.

- Western requests formal inclusion of two named agency contacts (Greg Wold and Gary L. Burton) on the **Lower Basin Management Plan Coordination Team** mailing list, indicating a bid for direct agency participation in ongoing recovery governance.
