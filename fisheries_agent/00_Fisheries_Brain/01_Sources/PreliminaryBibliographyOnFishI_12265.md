# Preliminary Bibliography on Fish in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is a preliminary annotated-free bibliography compiled by Duncan McClusky on May 1, 1989, listing published and unpublished literature on [[Freshwater Fishes]] of [[Arizona]]. The author describes the list as a starting point: after a visit with [[C. O. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]], he recognized that the work had "just begun," and he planned to add the articles in Minckley's collection over the following summer. The author also states that he could not personally examine all listed items, and that some titles cite papers "in press" or with no date.

The bibliography is organized alphabetically by first author. It contains several hundred entries, spanning from [[Baird and Girard]] (1853) to late-1980s agency reports and conference abstracts. It covers taxonomy, life history, ecology, parasitology, genetics, hatchery and stocking programs, reservoir fisheries management, endangered species recovery, and the history of native fish decline.

## Background & Description

**Scope and compilation.** The bibliography draws on several sources:
- Computer search results provided by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in Fort Collins.
- Copies of all fish recovery plans for the Service's Region 2 (Albuquerque) office.
- A publication list from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
- Proceedings supplied by E. P. Pister, Secretary of the [[Desert Fishes Council]].
- Assistance from Dr. W. Linn Montgomery (Biology, [[Northern Arizona University]]) and Dr. Harlen Johnson (Library Services instruction).

**Entry types.** Citations include peer-reviewed journal articles (e.g., *Copeia*, *Southwestern Naturalist*, *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*), state agency publications (numbered Arizona Game and Fish Department reports), federal reports and recovery plans, unpublished theses and dissertations, conference abstracts (*Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council*), and historical field notes such as those of [[Chamberlain]] (1904), held at the Smithsonian Institution.

**Notable historical items.** The earliest entries are 19th-century taxonomic descriptions by [[Baird and Girard]] (1853), [[Girard]] (1857, 1859), [[Cope and Yarrow]] (1875), and [[Evermann]] (1895). Later foundational syntheses include [[Miller]]'s taxonomic works, [[Lowe]]'s checklists (1964, 1967), and [[Minckley]]'s *Fishes of Arizona* (1973).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The literature reflects several recurring themes in Arizona's freshwater ichthyology:

- **Desert stream and spring fishes.** Many entries concern species of [[Sonoran Desert]] streams, springs, and cienegas, including [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), and [[Gila Topminnow]]. Several papers address flooding effects, species replacement with [[Mosquitofish]], and loss of habitat along the [[San Pedro River]] and [[Sycamore Creek]].
- **Colorado River system.** A large group of entries covers the [[Colorado River]] and its tributaries, including [[Grand Canyon]] fishes, [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Powell]], the lower Colorado River, and the [[Virgin River]].
- **Reservoirs and stocked waters.** Dozens of state management reports document warmwater and coldwater fisheries in Arizona lakes, including [[Alamo Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[Canyon Lake]], [[Lake Pleasant]], and [[Apache Lake]]. Stocking programs for [[Rainbow Trout]] and the introduction of [[Threadfin Shad]] as forage are common topics.
- **Native trouts and salmonid management.** Papers on [[Apache Trout]] (*Salmo apache*), [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*), and related hybridization and competition with introduced salmonids form an extensive subgroup.
- **Parasites, pollution, and contaminants.** Entries include helminth surveys of Gila River catostomids, heavy metal and mercury concentrations in fish tissue, and pesticide residues in southern Arizona fishes.

## Key Findings

The bibliography itself makes several substantive points about the state of knowledge on Arizona fishes as of 1989:

1. **Endangered species dominate the literature.** Much of the list addresses recovery efforts for federally listed or sensitive species, including:
   - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): reintroduction, larval ecology, and age determination in [[Lake Mohave]].
   - [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): reintroduction and monitoring in the [[Gila River]] basin.
   - [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*): life history and Grand Canyon population studies.
   - [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*): habitat requirements, streamflow needs, and water management in the Virgin River.
   - Gila and Sonoran topminnows, Apache and Gila trout, and the desert pupfish.

2. **Foundational taxonomic and biogeographic work.** Key references include [[Minckley and Deacon]] (1968), "Southwestern fishes and the enigma of 'endangered species'" (*Science* 159: 1424–1432); [[Hubbs and Miller]] (1948) on fish distribution and desert basin hydrography; and [[Williams et al.]] (1985) on endangered aquatic ecosystems and a list of vanishing fishes of the region.

3. **Reservoir and fisheries management is heavily documented.** A large share of entries are Arizona Game and Fish Department management reports (publication numbers from roughly 67-1 through 88-59), covering individual lakes and stream reaches. These reports are the most common publication type in the list.

4. **Genetics and conservation.** [[Vrijenhoek et al.]] (1985, *Science* 229: 400–402) on conservation genetics of endangered Arizona fish populations, and [[Loudenslager et al.]] (1986) on biochemical genetics of native Arizona and New Mexico trout, represent the genetic subset.

5. **Known gaps and limitations.** The author notes that:
   - He had not personally examined all listed articles.
   - Some references are unpublished or "in press" and lack dates.
   - Several entries are abstracts only.
   - The bibliography was expected to expand with the collection of Dr. C. O. Minckley over summer 1989.

6. **Acknowledged contributors.** The author thanks Dr. C. O. Minckley (ASU), E. P. Pister (Desert Fishes Council), the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service offices in Fort Collins and Albuquerque, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, and faculty at Northern Arizona University.

7. **Date of compilation.** The document is dated May 1, 1989 and is a working draft rather than a finished review.
