# Four New Species of Subterranean Amphipod Crustaceans from Texas

#amphipoda #stygobiont #subterranean-crustacea #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by John R. Holsinger (Old Dominion University) describes four new stygobiont (groundwater-dwelling) amphipod crustaceans from south-central and western Texas: [[Artesia Welbourni]] (Artesiidae), [[Holsingerius Smaragdinus]] (Hadziidae), [[Mexiweckelia Hardeni]] (Hadziidae) and [[Seborgia Hershleri]] (Sebidae). It also reports new localities for the previously described [[Seborgia Relicta]]. The work adds to the known stygobiont amphipod fauna of Texas, which rises to 21 species, and it discusses phylogenetic and biogeographic relationships that support a marine-stranding origin for these inland crustaceans. The document is a species description and systematic/biogeographic paper, not a fish study.

## Background & Description

Texas holds the greatest diversity of subterranean amphipods in North America, concentrated in the [[Edwards Aquifer]] and the associated Balcones fault zone. The previous major Texas study (Holsinger and Longley, 1980) described one family, four genera and six species from the [[San Marcos Artesian Well]]. Subsequent collecting added many new localities and taxa. Descriptions of the four new species raise the Texas total to 21 stygobiont amphipods, spanning five families and nine genera.

Key taxonomic points:

- **Artesia welbourni** is a medium-sized species, closely allied to *A. subterranea*. It differs in more spinose and setose appendages, a larger size at maturity (largest female 10.5 mm; male unknown), and other minor features. Material is three female specimens (holotype USNM 239480 plus two paratypes), collected by W. C. Welbourn on 15 April 1976.
- **Mexiweckelia hardeni** is a small interstitial species. It is distinguished from *M. colei* and *M. mitchelli* by a very shallow telson notch and a heavily spined uropod 2 peduncle. Largest male is 3.2 mm and largest female 4.5 mm. Material is a holotype female and 4 paratype females (USNM 239477), collected 18 March 1988, plus additional paratypes from 1986 and 1988.
- **Holsingerius smaragdinus** is a medium-sized cavernicolous species, provisionally placed in *Holsingerius* although intermediate in some mouthpart characters with *Mexiweckelia*. It is distinguished from *H. samacos* by a vestigial right mandibular lacinia mobilis, fewer maxillary setae and a shorter maxilla 2 inner plate. Largest female is 8.5 mm.
- **Seborgia (Relictoseborgia) hershleri** is a very small stygobiont (largest male 1.3 mm; largest females 1.6 mm), closely allied to *S. relicta*. Of nine specimens collected to date (all in September), eight were female and five were ovigerous.

The paper also proposes subdividing *Seborgia* into two subgenera, *Seborgia* and *Relictoseborgia*, with *S. relicta* and *S. hershleri* in the latter. It also corrects two discrepancies in earlier descriptions of *M. colei* and *M. mitchelli*: certain flagellar structures are esthetascs rather than calceoli, and basofacial spines were omitted from some uropod 1 illustrations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The taxa inhabit several groundwater settings:

- **[[Border Cave]]** (Culberson County) is developed in Permian Castile Formation gypsum. Its deep phreatic lakes lie about 152 to 213 m from the entrance. The type species was known only from three specimens. Later visits in 1985 and 1986 by S. J. Harden and associates recovered 37 specimens of an undescribed *Stygobromus* (*hubbsi* group) and an undescribed asellid isopod, but no further *A. welbourni*. The nearby Parks Ranch-Resurgence caves yielded only occasional *Hyalella azteca*, because they are shallow and do not reach phreatic water.
- **[[Hondo Creek]]** (Medina County) hyporheic zone is a shallow gravel-bank interstitial habitat in Late Cretaceous shales and sandstones. Sampling there yielded flatworms, oligochaetes, hydrobiid snails, ostracods, the stenasellid isopod *Mexistenasellus coahuila*, and amphipods including *M. hardeni*, *Stygobromus russelli*, *S. relicta* and *Hyalella azteca*.
- **[[Emerald Sink]]** (Val Verde County) is a cave on the Stockton Plateau, about 91 m deep, with a deep phreatic lake in Cretaceous Buda and Devils River limestones. The lake also holds the cirolanid isopod *Speocirolana hardeni*.
- **Spring near the [[Devils River]]** (Val Verde County) emerges from Georgetown Limestone aquifers. Associated fauna includes *Stygobromus* (possible new species), an undescribed hadziid genus (more common), planarians, hydrobiid snails, copepods and the cirolanid isopod *Cirolanides texensis*.

## Key Findings

- **Four new species described** from Texas groundwater: *Artesia welbourni*, *Holsingerius smaragdinus*, *Mexiweckelia hardeni* and *Seborgia hershleri*. The Texas stygobiont amphipod total is now 21 species in five families and nine genera.
- **Range extensions:**
  - *Mexiweckelia* was previously known only from northern Mexico (Coahuila and Durango). *M. hardeni* is its first Texas record, extending the genus about 400 km northeast from Cuatro Ciénegas to Medina County and bringing the genus to three species.
  - *Holsingerius* was previously known only from the San Marcos artesian well (13 specimens recorded to that point). *H. smaragdinus* extends the genus about 350 km west, across the Pecos Valley to the Stockton Plateau.
  - *Seborgia relicta* was recorded from Hondo Creek, a range extension of about 120 km southwest and the first record outside the San Marcos well.
  - *Artesia* is now recorded about 650 km west of San Marcos, at Border Cave.
- **Phylogenetic relationships:** *M. hardeni* and the Mexican *M. colei* share many characters and are probably sister species. *H. smaragdinus* shares a suite of synapomorphies with *H. samacos*, supporting a closer relationship with *Holsingerius* than with *Mexiweckelia*. A cladistic analysis (Holsinger, in prep.) reportedly places *Mexiweckelia* and the Edwards Aquifer hadziid genera in a nested group with a relatively recent common ancestor.
- **Biogeography:** The distribution of *Mexiweckelia* overlaps the northern range of [[Mexistenasellus]] and the northern range of [[Speocirolana]]. The author interprets these overlaps as evidence of a shared north-central Mexico to south-central Texas crustacean track. The *Stygobromus* species at Border Cave is part of a *hubbsi* group otherwise restricted to the far western US and southwestern Canada.
- **Stranding hypothesis supported:** The author argues that most non-crangonyctid subterranean amphipods (artesiids, bogidiellids, hadziids, sebids), along with other stygobiont crustaceans, originated from marine ancestors stranded in Late Cretaceous or early Cenozoic times. The new taxa lie within areas once covered by Cretaceous marine embayments.
- **Taxonomic decision:** Because the *Seborgia* species have few morphological differences, the author proposes subgenera instead of full genera. The Val Verde *S. hershleri* is treated as a separate species from *S. relicta*, since the populations are geographically isolated and little is known about variation in the genus.
- **Conservation note:** Several records are from very small numbers of specimens (for example, three for *A. welbourni* and 9 for *S. hershleri*), so the species appear rare and poorly known.
