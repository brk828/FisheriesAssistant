# Spawning of Bluehead Sucker in Kanab Creek, Arizona, and White-tipped Dove Movements in Southern Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This two-article scan from *The Southwestern Naturalist* (vol. 33, no. 3, September 1988, Notes section) contains two unrelated short studies. The main item documents the spawning behavior, site selection and habitat conditions of the [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus*) in [[Kanab Creek]], a [[Colorado River]] tributary entering the [[Grand Canyon]]. The second item is a brief note on [[White-tipped Dove]] (*Leptotila verreauxi*) movements in southern Texas. The dove study is terrestrial and is recorded here for completeness only.

## Background & Description

The [[Bluehead Sucker]] is widely distributed in the Snake, Bear, Weber and upper Colorado river drainages of the western United States. Few life-history studies exist for the species. Earlier reproductive work estimated maturity and fecundity only, so direct observations of spawning were limited.

The authors observed spawning on 2 May 1985 from a rock shelf about 4.0 m above the water surface. Fish tolerated observers on the shelf but moved to deeper pools when approached from shore. Field methods included:

- A [[Hydrolab Sonde]] (Model 9000 Surveyor II) and a [[Marsh-McBirney Current Meter]] placed in the lower spawning area.
- Hourly logging of water temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity and velocity from 0730 h.
- Colorimetric [[Water Chemistry]] analysis on a water sample collected at 0930 h, using a Bausch and Lomb Mini-20 spectrophotometer.
- Gravel-bed measurements of spawning depressions to the nearest 1.0 mm, plus a gravel subsample.
- Larval/ova sampling with fine-mesh netting and adult collection with a 7.6-m bag seine.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Spawning took place over a loosely consolidated gravel bed in a canyon reach where the site was shaded by the canyon walls for much of the day. Direct sunlight reached the spawning area only during a midday window. During sunny periods, bluehead suckers moved to adjacent deeper pools and made occasional forays back to the spawning area. Males and females fed actively over gravel bottoms during the period of intense illumination.

Other fishes in the reach included [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*). No ova were found in the stomachs of carp or bluehead suckers collected in the area.

The authors compared their findings with other catostomids, which show similar spawning behavior ([[Minckley 1973]]). Observations from April 1984 and May 1985 suggest that bluehead sucker spawning may be prolonged, rather than confined to a single brief peak.

## Key Findings

### Spawning behavior
- Males in breeding coloration performed apparently random patrols of a 3.0 × 2.2 m gravel bed and showed no clear territorial defense.
- The largest group observed over the spawning area at one time was 28 fish (both sexes).
- A female entering the area was accosted by 1 to 8 males. Two to three males typically attempted to initiate spawning by nudging the female's sides and caudal peduncle.
- Successful spawning was apparent only when no more than two males closely tended a female. Mean males per spawning act was 2.3 ± 0.6.
- A spawning act occurred when a female settled to the bottom and a male positioned himself postero-laterally, with pelvic fins of both sexes overlapping. The male's tuberculate anal fin was near the female's urogenital papilla. Wrapping of the anal fin around the urogenital area was not clearly observed, though the authors judged it likely.
- Males that did not reach the lateral position swam side to side or settled on the female's dorsum or flank.
- Females and males cleared a depression in the substrate through rapid fanning and shuddering of the body and fins.

### Timing and intensity
- A total of 63 spawning acts were recorded during a 12-hour observation period, from the first act at 0700 h to the last at 1830 h.
- The major peak occurred between 1600 and 1800 h. Minor peaks occurred from 0700 to 0900 h and from 1300 to 1500 h.
- One female accounted for 28 acts (44% of all observed) within 1 h 23 min.

### Spawning substrate and depressions
- Females selected loosely consolidated gravel averaging 6.6 ± 6.2 mm in diameter (n = 95).
- Spawning depressions were 10 to 23 cm wide (mean 14.8 ± 5.7 cm) and 21 to 37 cm long.
- Ova recovered from depressions measured 2.4 to 3.1 mm in diameter and were slightly adhesive.
- Water depth over depressions was 9 to 29 cm (mean 16.1 ± 5.7 cm).

### Population sample
- 20 males and 9 females were seined after the final observation, a sex ratio of about 2:1 (male:female).
- The authors noted that this ratio was the mode observed during spawning and resembles that reported for other catostomids (Villa 1985).

### Study acknowledgments and support

### White-tipped dove movements (non-aquatic)
- The [[White-tipped Dove]] ranges from Mexico south to Brazil, Uruguay and eastern Argentina, and is common in Cameron, Hidalgo, Starr and Willacy counties, southern Texas.
- The study aimed to quantify movements on two areas: a [[Mesquite Woodland]] and a 130-ha [[Citrus Orchard]], about 1.6 km south of Citrus City, Hidalgo County.
- Reported climate: mild and semi-tropical, with mean annual precipitation of 59 cm.
- Doves appeared to migrate south in November and return in February.
- The author of the note criticized the American Ornithologists' Union checklist for classifying the species as resident throughout its range, arguing that the classification relied on subjective observation.
