# Cichlasoma minckleyi: A New Polymorphic Cichlid from Cuatro Ciénegas, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This 1983 paper in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (96(2), pp. 253-269) by Irv Kornfield and Jeffrey N. Taylor describes [[Cichlasoma Minckleyi]], a new species of polymorphic cichlid endemic to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in Coahuila, Mexico. The species combines two sympatric body forms (deep-bodied and slender-bodied), and within the deep-bodied form, two pharyngeal morphs (papilliform and molariform). Earlier morphology-based work had suggested three species, while biochemical data supported a single polymorphic species. Field observations of successful matings between morphs and between body forms resolved the question in favour of one species. The authors argue that phenetic (morphological) criteria alone may be insufficient to delimit biological species in the Cichlidae.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** The fish had been treated as *Cichlasoma* cf. *cyanoguttatum*, *Parapetenia* sp. (LaBounty 1974), and *Cichlasoma* sp. in a long list of references. [[Sage And Selander 1975]] used isozyme data to support one polymorphic species, and [[Kornfield Et Al 1982]] provided direct behavioural evidence of conspecificity. The name *minckleyi* honours Dr. W. L. Minckley of Arizona State University, who studied the Cuatro Ciénegas biota for many years.

**Diagnosis and morphology.** The species is placed tentatively in the [[Parapetenia Group]] of *Cichlasoma*. Diagnostic counts include dorsal fin elements XVI, 10-11; anal fin V, 8-9; 14 pectoral rays; 11-12 gill rakers; and 29 vertebrae (total count mode). The caudal peduncle is relatively slender, with length equal to or greater than least depth.

**Sexual dichromatism.** Breeding males are dark green to black with pale blue spots. Breeding females are snowy white with black flank markings. The authors describe this as uniquely diagnostic for the species.

**Type material.** The holotype is UMMZ 209434 (93.4 mm SL male, deep-bodied papilliform morph), collected 6 April 1961 at Posos de la Becerra. Paratypes are numerous lots at the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ) and the National Museum of Natural History (USNM), with lots ranging from 9 to 155 specimens.

**Morphometrics (Table 1).** Measurements are expressed in thousandths of standard length. Ranges are given for four groups: deep-bodied papilliform (n = 7), deep-bodied molariform (n = 7), slender-bodied papilliform (n = 1), and slender-bodied molariform (n = 4). Body depth was 2.2-2.5 in SL for deep-bodied fish and 2.6-2.8 in SL for slender-bodied fish (n = 15, 54.3-144.7 mm SL).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** The species is known only from the Cuatro Ciénegas basin. It is common and widely distributed in most streams, ponds and lagunas. At a few sites, including Laguna Santa Tecla in the southeast of the basin, it occurs sympatrically with the recent colonist [[Cichlasoma Cyanoguttatum]], which has gained access through newly constructed irrigation systems.

**Localities sampled.** Collections came from streams, springs, irrigation ditches and lagunas, including El Mojarral, Posos de la Becerra, Laguna Churince, Rio Mesquites, La Angostura canal and Puente Colorado.

**Relationships.** The authors consider *C. minckleyi* more closely related to endemic cichlids of the [[Rio Panuco Basin]] than to *C. cyanoguttatum*. The Panuco species cited include *C. labridens*, *C. bartoni* and *C. steindachneri*. The Parapetenia/Herichthys distinction is blurred by intermediate dentition, so supraspecific relationships remain unresolved.

**Trophic ecology.** The molariform morph has hypertrophied pharyngeal musculature and a massive pharyngobranchial apophysis. Its gut is significantly shorter than that of the papilliform morph. Gastropods occur in molariform guts but are usually absent from papilliform guts. Overall dietary overlap is large, particularly in juveniles and subadults. Slender-bodied forms mainly eat other fishes.

**Gene-based hypothesis.** Molariform frequency is substantially higher in males than in females at every locality (Table 3). The authors note this pattern is consistent with a single diallelic locus with a recessive molariform allele, and with sex linkage, but they state that formal inheritance studies are still needed.

## Key Findings

- **Species status:** *C. minckleyi* is described as a new, endemic, polymorphic species. Its variants are conspecific, based on direct observation of matings between morphs and between body forms.
- **Two-way polymorphism:** Two non-overlapping body forms (deep- and slender-bodied) co-occur. Deep-bodied fish carry either papilliform or molariform pharyngeal dentition, and each is maintained at high frequency.
- **Morphological dichotomy (Table 2):** In deep-bodied fish, the papilliform morph has a narrower mid-pharyngeal width, smaller horn width, and a longer gut than the molariform morph. The head width of molariform fish is greater, with a slope test that is significant at P < 0.001.
- **Gut length:** Gut length differs significantly between morphs (Fig. 7). Slender-bodied forms have shorter guts than deep-bodied forms (Table 1).
- **Sex-biased frequencies (Table 3):** Molariform frequency in males ranged from 0.375 to 0.583 across five localities, and in females from 0.111 to 0.400. Sample sizes ranged from 9 to 156.
- **Intermediates:** Fewer than 5% of deep-bodied fish show intermediate pharyngeal morphology and gut length. The authors say this cannot be explained by conventional hybridization.
- **Taxonomic implication:** The case shows that phenetic characters may not be enough to define species within the Cichlidae. Earlier morphological work had suggested three species.
- **Conservation status:** The paper does not discuss conservation status. It does note the threat posed by the recent introduction of *C. cyanoguttatum* into the basin.
