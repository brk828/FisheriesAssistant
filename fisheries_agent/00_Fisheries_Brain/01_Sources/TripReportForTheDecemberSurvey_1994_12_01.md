## Executive Summary
This trip report documents a one-night fish community survey conducted by Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies at the ASU Research Park ponds in Tempe, Arizona, on 30 November–01 December 1994. Using entanglement nets (experimental gill nets and trammel nets), researchers captured 75 individual fish representing 8 species across three artificial ponds. The survey's primary conservation focus was monitoring the status of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), five of which were captured, measured, and assessed for health and reproductive condition before release. No [[Colorado Squawfish]] or [[Bonytail Chub]] — two other Colorado River Basin endangered species historically documented at the site — were captured during this limited effort.

## Background & Description
- **Total catch:** 75 individuals, 8 species, all previously recorded in prior surveys of the research park.
- **Dominant species:**
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) — 40% of total catch.
  - [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) — 28% of total catch.
- **Subdominant species (≤18% each):** [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus* — note: source text lists scientific name as *Lepomis cyanellus*, likely an OCR/transcription error since this binomial belongs to Green Sunfish), [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and [[Blue Tilapia]] (*Tilapia aurea*).
- **Razorback Sucker biometrics (n=5):**
  - Males (n=2): Total Length (TL) = 510 mm and 565 mm.
  - Females (n=3): Total Length (TL) = 595 mm, 615 mm, and 612 mm.
  - Health status: All individuals appeared healthy — no external fungus, scarring, or tumors observed.
  - Notable anomaly: Two females each exhibited unilateral blindness (one blind eye).
  - Reproductive condition: Two males and one female were in active breeding condition (expressing gametes upon handling).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Three interconnected artificial ponds within the ASU Research Park, an urban lentic (still-water) environment in Tempe, Arizona.
- **Sampling gear:** Seven entanglement nets deployed across the three ponds, including combinations of 125 ft experimental gill nets, 150 ft trammel nets, and 300 ft trammel nets. Nets were set overnight (1830–2030 hrs) and retrieved the following morning (0700–0900 hrs), representing a single overnight soak period (~12 hr).
- **Community composition:** The pond system supports a mixed assemblage of native and non-native/introduced species, including sport fish ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Channel Catfish]]), invasive/introduced species ([[Grass Carp]], [[Blue Tilapia]], [[Threadfin Shad]]), and a remnant/stocked population of the endangered native [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Conservation context:** The presence of breeding-condition Razorback Suckers suggests the pond system may function as a refugium or holding habitat relevant to recovery efforts for this Colorado River Basin endemic. The absence of [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] in this sampling event was attributed to limited sampling effort rather than confirmed absence from the site.

## Key Findings
- Catch Per Unit Effort (CPE, expressed as number/100m²/12hr set) varied substantially across net sets, ranging from 0.00 to 9.81, with a cumulative CPE of approximately 9.81 for combined effort (based on Table 2 summary data).
- All 75 captured fish, including the 5 razorback suckers, were released unharmed, consistent with standard non-lethal survey protocols for handling endangered species.
- Two of three female Razorback Suckers captured exhibited unilateral blindness, a notable morphological anomaly warranting further monitoring in future surveys.
- Three of five Razorback Suckers were in active breeding condition at time of capture (late November/early December), suggesting an atypical or extended spawning window for this population compared to typical spring spawning behavior documented in riverine populations.
- No individuals of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) or [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) were captured; researchers attributed this to the relatively small scale of the single-night sampling effort rather than definitive absence.
- A follow-up survey of the ASU Research Park was tentatively planned for spring 1995 to continue monitoring population status and trends.
