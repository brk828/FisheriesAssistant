# Colorado River Recovery Program: 1989 Management Group Records, FY 1990 Work Plan and Water Rights Proposals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989 file of correspondence, meeting summaries, agendas, work plan tables and proposals for the [[Upper Colorado River Recovery Program]], a multi-agency effort to recover endangered big-river fishes. The fishes named are [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail]]. Most material concerns budgeting, the FY 90 work plan (about $2.76–2.83 million), instream flow protection and water rights acquisition, and the [[Section 7 Consultation]] process under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It is primarily administrative rather than research. Its scientific content is limited to flow-habitat figures for trout on the [[Fryingpan River]] and a Yampa River flow study plan.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program is coordinated by a [[Management Group]] and an [[Implementation Committee]], supported by a Technical Group. The program director is John Hamill of the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), Region 6. Participants include the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB), the [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]] (CRWCD), Utah and Wyoming agencies, the [[Western Area Power Administration]], the National Park Service, environmental groups, and [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC).

Meetings were held July 13–14, 1989 (Management Group) and were followed by an August 30, 1989 Implementation Committee meeting. The Management Group met again September 13, 1989.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Yampa River:** ranked the first-priority reach for water rights acquisition because of its importance to the endangered fishes. Phase I of the flow recommendation work was completed May 1, 1989.
- **Little Snake River:** averages 350,000–400,000 acre-feet (AF) per year contributed to the Yampa and Green rivers. Under Article IX of the 1948 Upper Colorado River Basin Compact, Colorado and Wyoming share this undeveloped supply equally.
- **Fifteen-Mile Reach:** the Ruedi Reservoir release program was designed to supply flows for the endangered fishes here.
- **Fryingpan River:** Ruedi releases are predicted to change trout habitat and angler catch rates, as summarized in Key Findings.
- **Flaming Gorge:** operations are governed by a biological opinion (draft scheduled for March 15, 1989).
- **Yampa River oil spill:** [[Amoco]] funded studies of residual impacts. No immediate impact had been detected.
- **Lake Powell:** Utah proposed introducing rainbow smelt. The Colorado Wildlife Council did not vote on the proposal and referred it to FWS and the National Park Service for review.

## Key Findings

**1. FY 90 Work Plan and Budget**

- The Technical Group's work plan (Table 3.1, July 6, 1989) totaled **$2,825.5K**, with 40 projects. The Management Group revised it (July 21 table) to **$2,757.4K**.
- Major Management Group changes included reducing the [[Fifteen-Mile Reach]] flow needs study to $140.3K, reducing propagation research to $207.7K, adding $37.4K for [[Kenney Reservoir]] sportfishery work, and adding $66.0K for a Flaming Gorge tailwater trout study.
- The Service's $200.0K in Section 6 funds was allocated as Utah $97.8K and Colorado $101.5K, across projects including standardized monitoring ($35.6K Utah, $18.2K Colorado) and game/endangered fish interactions ($72.3K).
- Utah lacked FY 90 funds beyond about $10,000 in matching money. It had requested $73.0K from its legislature, with action expected January 1990.
- Categories in the revised plan: habitat management $924.4K, research/monitoring/data management $503.5K, stocking $660.4K, program management $362.3K.

**2. Yampa Basin Reservoir and Phase II Flow Work**

- The CWCB requested $56.2K credit for its contribution to the [[Yampa Basin Reservoir Feasibility Study]], in addition to the $50.0K Recovery Program share. Concerns included the link to water rights purchase and defining fish flow needs first.
- Phase II steps: Steps A–C due October 1, 1989; Step D due November 15, 1989; Step E (detailed scopes of work) due February 1, 1990. Interim flow recommendations were targeted for fall 1989.

**3. Ruedi Reservoir Conservation Releases**

- Reclamation proposed 10,000 AF released over 62 days from August 15 to October 15, 1989.
- Projected Fryingpan flows: about 150 cfs early in the period, declining to as low as 130 cfs, then 90–110 cfs after October 15 for brown trout spawning.
- Catch per man-hour (CPMH) in lower sections fell from 0.702 to 0.601 when flows rose from 110 to 125–150 cfs. The first 2 miles fell from 1.308 to 1.184.
- Wadable area fell from 62,221 to 59,926 sq ft per 1,000 ft of river (about 3.5%).
- Juvenile rainbow trout habitat was projected to fall by up to 4.5%, and brown trout by about 3%. Reclamation judged these reductions insignificant.
- Reservoir level near Labor Day was projected at about 95,000 AF, above the 85,000 AF minimum the Forest Service considers adequate for recreation.
- The July 7, 1989 public meeting in Glenwood Springs drew about 25 attendees, mostly from Pitkin County. Concern centered on reservoir operating priorities rather than fish impacts.

**4. Water Rights Acquisition**

- **Little Snake:** The Pittsburgh and Midway Coal Mining Company (P&M) holds two Colorado conditional rights totaling 150,000 AF and a Wyoming permit for 22,865 AF. The CRWCD holds Powderwash and Pothook rights. The Management Group approved a study with a cap of $37,500, conditioned on staging the appraisal only if hydrology shows instream yield.
- **Cross Mountain Ranch (lower Yampa):** 27,000 acres with about 4,500 AF of senior agricultural rights. Estimated purchase cost was about $300,000, plus about $15,000 for an independent appraisal. The Management Group unanimously recommended TNC pursue the appraisal and purchase.
- **Ruedi/15-Mile lease:** a lease among Grand Valley water users, the Department of the Interior and CWCB is under review. Issues include the Green Mountain decree and legal basis, with a 40-year maximum lease length. CWCB is expected to act September 21–22, 1989. A preliminary notice was filed for a 168 cfs instream flow right as a fallback.
- **Section 7 funds:** the Management Group agreed that new and unconditioned existing Section 7 funds would go exclusively to water rights acquisition activities.

**5. Section 7 Consultations**

- The depletion table lists about 20 projects. Among pending items: Sandstone (30,600 AF, draft due July 1989), Muddy Creek (7,716 AF, MOU pending), and Price-San Rafael (22,951 AF, Bureau of Reclamation lead).
- **Sandstone:** Wyoming would forgo 20,000 AF of firm yield for six years, with a $10/AF charge if progress is judged insufficient.
- **Muddy Creek:** the draft opinion's language limited relief from the project's obligations to progress within the 15-Mile Reach. Concerns were raised about the State's incentive to acquire water in the Yampa.
- **Price-San Rafael:** the Soil Conservation Service and Utah were asked to consider four options, including crediting Uinta Basin accretions (20,200 AF) with instream flow protection, reducing the project size, or paying $10/AF while evaluating recovery progress. No agency committed to a specific option.

**6. Other Items**

- A Congressional request to add $324.0K to the Service budget for the Recovery Program passed the House Appropriations Committee.
- A fisheries biologist assistant for the Program Director was to be hired by October 1, 1989.
- The 5-year Information and Education plan, brochure and newsletter were in progress.
