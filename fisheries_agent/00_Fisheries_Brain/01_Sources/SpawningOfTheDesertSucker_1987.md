## Executive Summary
This short field note documents the spawning ecology of the [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarki*), a poorly studied catostomid native to the southwestern United States. Based on quarterly electrofishing surveys of [[Bass-Hotsprings Creek]] (a tributary of the [[San Pedro River]]) in 1987, the authors determined that spawning begins as early as March and is completed by June, following a single annual spring spawning period consistent with other *Catostomus* species. The study fills a data gap identified by earlier authors (Minckley 1973) who could only infer spawning timing by analogy to congeners.

## Background & Description
- **Sexual dimorphism during spawning condition:**
  - **Males:** Development of nuptial tubercles on the anal fin, most prominent in March, diminishing by June, and absent by August.
  - **Females:** Spawning condition identified by extrusion of eggs; by June, adult females were "spawned out" (post-spawning, depleted of eggs).
- **Juveniles (young of year):** Collected in June, ranging from **27–36 mm** in length.
- No detailed external morphometric data (fin ray counts, body proportions) provided in this note — focus is reproductive morphology only.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat preference:** [[Desert Sucker]] is more commonly associated with **riffle habitats** than quiet pool water, consistent with prior literature ([[Minckley 1973]], [[Schreiber 1978]], [[Fisher Et Al. 1981]]).
- **Diet:** Feeds primarily on **benthic algae** and **turbellarians** (flatworms), foraging behavior linked to riffle substrate.
- **Spawning habitat:** Riffle areas with **clean sand and pea-sized gravel**; shallow depressions (presumed redds) were observed, though active spawning itself was not directly witnessed.
- **Juvenile habitat:** Young of year (27–36 mm) were found in very shallow water (**<15 cm deep**), with a distribution skewed toward riffles (7 individuals) over pools (4 individuals), suggesting riffle-association begins early in life.
- **Sympatric species context:** Occurs alongside other Sonoran Desert stream fishes such as *Agosia chrysogaster* ([[Cyprinidae]]), with which diel feeding chronology has been compared (Fisher et al. 1981).
- **Regional distribution:** Found in the [[Gila River]] basin (Arizona, New Mexico) and the [[Colorado River]] below the [[Grand Canyon]] and associated tributaries.

## Key Findings
- **No spawning evidence in January** — fish showed no tubercle development or egg extrusion.
- **Peak spawning condition in mid-March** — over 80 individuals exhibited spawning characteristics (tuberculate males, gravid/spawning females).
- **Presumed redds** (shallow depressions of clean sand/fine gravel) were observed in riffle areas during the spawning period, though direct spawning behavior was not observed.
- **Young of year appeared by June** (27–36 mm), indicating spawning had already occurred and larvae/juveniles had developed to this size within roughly 2–3 months.
- **Spawning completed by June** — adult females were spent, and male tubercles were reduced.
- **No tuberculate fish observed in August**, confirming a **single, discrete spring spawning season** rather than multiple spawning bouts.
- This pattern — a **single spring spawning period** — aligns with the general reproductive strategy described for other [[Catostomus]] species (Moyle 1976), supporting Minckley's (1973) earlier hypothesis based on congeneric comparison.
- Study represents one of the first direct field confirmations of spawning timing and habitat for *C. clarki*, building on movement pattern data previously reported by [[Siebert 1980]] in a separate Arizona stream.
