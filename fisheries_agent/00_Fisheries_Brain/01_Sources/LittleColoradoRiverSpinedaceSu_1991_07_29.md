## Executive Summary

This intra-office memo from the Arizona Game and Fish Department summarizes fish survey results collected July 23–26, 1991, in tributaries of the Little Colorado River (Coconino National Forest, Arizona), including East Clear Creek, Chevelon Creek, and Barbershop Creek. The survey was intended to complete data collection for a [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]] status report, but notably **no spinedace were detected at any surveyed site**. The survey instead documented a diverse native and non-native fish assemblage, including [[Roundtail Chub]], two morphologically distinct forms of [[Little Colorado River Sucker]] (CASP), [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] (PADI), [[Speckled Dace]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Golden Shiner]], and reproducing populations of introduced [[Rainbow Trout]]. A key finding is the discovery of two non-hybrid morphotypes of Little Colorado River sucker at Lower Chevelon Creek that differ in coloration, lip morphology, and scale size, warranting further taxonomic investigation.

## Background & Description

### Little Colorado River Sucker (CASP) — Two Morphotypes Identified at Lower Chevelon Creek
Both types lack the diagnostic [[Pantosteus]] subgenus traits: modified upper lip, sharply indented lip junction, and Pantosteus-typical mouth shape. They are also distinct from previously documented **CASP × PADI hybrids** at the same location.

| Trait | Type 1 (typical CASP) | Type 2 (novel form) |
|---|---|---|
| Coloration | Sharply bicolored: tan/orange-brown dorsally, cream/white ventrally | Not sharply bicolored: grey dorsally, white ventrally |
| Lower lip | Standard fleshiness | Fleshier lower lips |
| Papillae arrangement | Honeycomb-like configuration (typical CASP pattern) | Arranged in medial rows |
| Scale size | Larger scales | Smaller scales |
| Distribution | Matches CASP from East Clear Creek & Upper Chevelon Creek | Novel/distinct from prior sampling |

Note: Specimens were preserved in formalin and slated for transfer to alcohol for further comparative examination.

### Roundtail Chub ([[Gila robusta]])
- Size range observed: 25–250 mm total length across sites.
- Juveniles and fry present at Chevelon Creek sites, indicating active reproduction.
- Breeding coloration observed in 3 of 5 chubs captured via gill net at the Points of Wildcat Gaging Station (225–250 mm TL).

### Little Colorado River Suckers — Size Data (Gill Net, Points of Wildcat Gaging Station)
- n = 53
- Average total length: 333.4 mm
- Range: 241–405 mm

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[East Clear Creek]] (Macks Crossing):** Dominated by [[Speckled Dace]] (n=251, "extremely abundant"), with fathead minnow (n=19), bluehead mountain sucker (n=3), rainbow trout (n=1), and LCR sucker (n=1). Overall fish abundance markedly higher than prior fall survey.

- **[[Barbershop Creek]] (near Dane Canyon confluence):** Supports a three-species community — speckled dace (abundant), rainbow trout (moderate), and bluehead mountain sucker (few) — with confirmed **in-stream reproduction of all three species**, including first documented rainbow trout reproduction in this reach. Notable microhabitat segregation: larger individuals occupied upstream reaches while juveniles were restricted to areas downstream of the Dane Canyon confluence.

- **[[Chevelon Creek]] (4.2 river miles downstream of Points of Wildcat Gaging Station):** Fathead minnow overwhelmingly abundant ("thousands," including fry); golden shiner rare. Two roundtail chubs (99, 128 mm TL) seined along with probable roundtail chub fry. One LCR sucker and one bluehead mountain sucker also captured. No salmonids observed. Water temperature 24°C during scattered showers.

- **[[Chevelon Creek]] (Points of Wildcat Gaging Station):** Most fish-diverse site sampled, using seine, experimental gill net, minnow trap, and backpack electroshocker. Notable for large-bodied LCR sucker population (avg. 333.4 mm TL) and multiple size classes of roundtail chub (25–250 mm TL), suggesting a self-sustaining, multi-cohort population. Fathead minnow and golden shiner present in high abundance (thousands and hundreds, respectively). One large rainbow trout gillnetted; another found dead on shore — possible evidence of trout mortality/stress at this site. Water temperature 24°C.

## Key Findings

- **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]] were absent from all surveyed sites**, a significant negative result for the species status report being prepared for Coconino National Forest.
- Discovery of a **second, previously undocumented morphotype of Little Colorado River sucker** at Lower Chevelon Creek, distinguishable by coloration, papillae arrangement, and scale size — distinct from both the typical CASP form and known CASP × PADI hybrids. Suggests possible undescribed diversity or cryptic population structure within *Catostomus* in this drainage.
- **Rainbow trout reproduction confirmed** in Barbershop Canyon for the first time noted in this survey series, indicating an established, self-sustaining non-native population with potential competitive/predatory implications for native cyprinids and catostomids.
- **Roundtail chub populations confirmed reproducing** at multiple Chevelon Creek sites, with evidence of multiple age/size classes and breeding coloration — a positive indicator for this native species of conservation concern.
- Clear **spatial segregation of size classes** (adults upstream, juveniles downstream of tributary confluences) observed in Barbershop Creek, possibly reflecting habitat preference differences between life stages or flow-related recruitment dynamics.
- High abundance of non-native/generalist cyprinids ([[Fathead Minnow]], [[Golden Shiner]]) at Chevelon Creek sites raises potential concern regarding competitive pressure on native fish assemblages, including [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]] recovery potential.
- Survey remains incomplete: **Lower East Clear Creek was not sampled**, representing a data gap for the final Coconino NF spinedace report.
