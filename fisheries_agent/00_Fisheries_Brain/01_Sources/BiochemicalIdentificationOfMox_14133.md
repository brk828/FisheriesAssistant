# Biochemical identification of Moxostoma rhothoecum and M. hamiltoni

## Executive Summary
This 1977 biochemical systematics study by Donald G. Buth used starch gel electrophoresis of four enzyme systems (acid phosphatase, glucosephosphate isomerase, glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase, and 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase) to resolve a long-standing taxonomic ambiguity between two morphologically similar [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] species: the torrent sucker (**[[Moxostoma rhothoecum]]**) and **[[Moxostoma hamiltoni]]**. The key finding confirmed [[Robert E. Jenkins|Jenkins']] (1970) morphological reassignment: the population inhabiting the **Roanoke River proper**, previously assigned to *M. hamiltoni*, is biochemically identical to *M. rhothoecum* populations from the James River drainage. *M. hamiltoni* was found to be biochemically distinct at all four enzyme loci examined, though the data alone could not resolve whether this distinction constitutes full species status or subspecific differentiation. This work is notable as an early application of allozyme electrophoresis to resolve species-level identity questions in polyploid [[Cypriniformes]].

## Background & Description
While this is primarily a biochemical/molecular paper rather than a morphological description, key background morphological/taxonomic points include:
- *M. rhothoecum* (the **torrent sucker**) has historically been considered a distinctive redhorse sucker, at one point elevated to its own genus (*Thoburnia*) and tribal status.
- *M. hamiltoni* was described by Raney and Lachner in the 1940s as a similar form inhabiting the Dan River system (Roanoke drainage).
- A detailed morphological study by [[Robert E. Jenkins]] (1970 Ph.D. thesis, Cornell University) reassigned the Roanoke River population from *M. hamiltoni* to *M. rhothoecum*, a finding directly corroborated by this biochemical study.
- Both species belong to the family [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), part of the ancient vertebrate **tetraploid** lineages within [[Cypriniformes]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Riverine/riffle freshwater habitats; *M. rhothoecum* is termed the "torrent sucker," suggesting adaptation to fast-flowing, high-gradient stream sections.
- **Geographic Distribution**:
  - *M. rhothoecum*: James River, Kanawha River, and Potomac River drainages in Virginia; confirmed also in the Roanoke River (Catawba Creek, Roanoke Co.; and three Roanoke River localities, Montgomery Co., Va.).
  - *M. hamiltoni*: Restricted to the Dan River system within the Roanoke drainage (collected from a tributary of Poorhouse Creek, Patrick Co., Va.).
- **Taxonomic Context**: Both species are members of the [[Moxostomatini]] tribe (redhorse suckers), a group noted for numerous unresolved distributional and phylogenetic problems.
- **Genome Context**: [[Catostomidae]] are one of the few vertebrate tetraploid groups, old enough to have undergone "functional diploidization" at many gene loci, but retaining sufficient gene duplication to make isozyme-based systematics informative.

## Key Findings

### Enzyme Systems Analyzed
| Enzyme | EC Number | Tissue Source | Loci | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acid phosphatase (Acp) | 3.1.3.2 | Liver | Single locus (Acp-A) | Dimer |
| Glucosephosphate isomerase (Gpi) | 5.3.1.9 | Brain | Duplicated (Gpi-A¹, Gpi-A²) | Dimer |
| Glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (G-3-pdh) | 1.1.1.8 | Muscle | Duplicated (G-3-pdh-A¹, G-3-pdh-A²) | Dimer |
| 6-Phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (6-pgdh) | 1.1.1.44 | Muscle | Duplicated (6-pgdh-A¹, 6-pgdh-A²) | Dimer |

### Allele Fixation Patterns
- **Acid Phosphatase**: Both *M. rhothoecum* populations (James River, Roanoke River) fixed for the slower **Acp-A^a** allele; *M. hamiltoni* fixed for the faster **Acp-A^b** allele.
- **Glucosephosphate Isomerase**:
  - Gpi-A¹ locus: James River *M. rhothoecum* polymorphic (A¹ᵃ = 0.192, A¹ᵇ = 0.808); Roanoke River *M. rhothoecum* fixed at A¹ᵇ; *M. hamiltoni* fixed at a distinct A¹ᶜ allele.
  - Gpi-A² locus: Both *M. rhothoecum* populations polymorphic (A²ᵃ/A²ᵇ); *M. hamiltoni* fixed at A²ᵃ.
- **Glycerol-3-Phosphate Dehydrogenase**: *M. hamiltoni* fixed at G-3-pdh-A¹ᵃ and A²ᵃ; *M. rhothoecum* populations fixed at A¹ᵇ but polymorphic at the A² locus (A²ᵃ/A²ᵇ, ~0.5/0.5 in Roanoke River).
- **6-Phosphogluconate Dehydrogenase**: Both *M. rhothoecum* populations fixed at A¹ᵃ/A²ᵃ; *M. hamiltoni* fixed at the alternate A¹ᵇ/A²ᵇ alleles.

### Genetic Distance Analysis
- Using [[Nei's Genetic Similarity Index|Nei's (1972) genetic similarity formula]] across 48 alleles (34 loci):
  - **I = 0.996** between the two *M. rhothoecum* populations (James River vs. Roanoke River) — indicating conspecific status, consistent with Jenkins' morphological reassignment.
  - **I = 0.807** between *M. rhothoecum* and *M. hamiltoni** — a substantially larger genetic distance, though below thresholds needed to definitively confirm full species status vs. subspecies.
- Comparative benchmark: geographic populations of the same species typically show I ≥ 0.95 ([[Avise & Ayala 1976]]); a comparable cyprinid study in California found I = 0.99 for conspecific populations, aligning with the *M. rhothoecum* result.

### Taxonomic Resolution
- **Confirmed**: The Roanoke River sucker population is *M. rhothoecum*, not *M. hamiltoni*, resolving prior conflicting assignments in the literature (Blair et al. 1968; Eddy 1969).
- **Unresolved**: The species-vs-subspecies status of *M. hamiltoni* relative to *M. rhothoecum* remains open, pending further data (noted as subject of ongoing investigation into [[Hypentelium]] and [[Moxostoma]] systematics by the author).
