# Minutes of the Desert Fishes Recovery Team, Tempe, AZ, 26-27 January 1989

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #desert-fishes #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #conservation-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper but the recorded minutes of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), a USFWS-affiliated advisory body coordinating recovery planning for imperiled desert and river fishes across Arizona, New Mexico, and adjacent basins (Region 2, with cross-boundary issues involving Region 6). The meeting addressed the stalled status of multiple federal Recovery Plans (for [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]], among others), proposed a reorganization of the Team into a nuclear/core group plus taxon-based working subgroups (Cyprinodontoid, Small-stream fishes, Big-river fishes, Yaqui fishes, Virgin River), and reviewed the conservation status, captive propagation, genetic identity, and habitat threats for over a dozen federally listed or candidate fish taxa. Key conservation issues discussed include hybridization/taxonomic uncertainty in the [[Gila Robusta]] complex, genetic contamination risks in the Virgin River fish fauna following a rotenone treatment, barrier construction to exclude invasive [[Red Shiner]] from Aravaipa Creek, and political/bureaucratic obstacles (Hooker Dam, Glen Canyon Dam operations, San Juan River study curtailment) affecting Endangered Species Act compliance.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] was established under USFWS Region 2 to formulate and complete Recovery Plans for a suite of imperiled fishes native to the arid Southwest, and to advise the Regional Director. By 1989, several plans were in varying states of completion:

- **Yaqui fishes Recovery Plan** — nearly finalized, pending concurrence with the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] Management Plan.
- **Sonoran Chub ([[Gila Ditaenia]]) Recovery Plan** — draft completed ~1.5 years prior; described by team members as unfairly criticized ("needed a lot of work") despite positive biological review.
- **Spikedace ([[Meda Fulgida]]) and Loach Minnow ([[Tiaroga Cobitis]]) draft Recovery Plans** — completed ~1 year prior, still awaiting agency review.
- **Desert Pupfish ([[Cyprinodon Macularius]]) and Gila Topminnow ([[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]]) Recovery Plans** — not yet completed; no dedicated funding available for contract drafting.

A chronic problem identified was lack of USFWS Regional Office response to DFRT correspondence and failure to advance plans through review, attributed generally to "problems of bureaucracy" following USFWS reorganization (recovery authority shifted to Ecological Services Field Offices in Phoenix and Albuquerque).

### Proposed Reorganization
The Team adopted a structure comprising:
1. **Nuclear/core group** (Minckley, Kepner, Hendrickson, Propst, Juarez) — correspondence and plan maintenance.
2. **Working subgroups**:
   - Virgin River fishes
   - Cyprinodontoid group (pupfish/topminnow)
   - Small-stream fishes (spikedace, loach minnow, Little Colorado spinedace)
   - Big-river fishes (Colorado River mainstem taxa)
   - Yaqui fishes

Responsibility for [[Chihuahua Chub]] was proposed for transfer to the Gila Trout Recovery Team; [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] ([[Lepidomeda Vittata]]) proposed for transfer to the Apache Trout Recovery Team.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic scope**: Colorado River Basin (Lower and Upper), Gila River Basin, Yaqui River Basin (Sonora, Mexico/Arizona border), Virgin River, San Pedro River, Little Colorado River, Aravaipa Creek.

**Cross-jurisdictional tensions**: USFWS Region 6 was assigned lead authority over big-river fishes of the Colorado River (including Virgin River populations), creating friction with Region 2's historical (since 1973) management role for Lower Basin big-river fishes. The DFRT sought formal recognition to ensure continued input into Region 6-led recovery actions — an early example of cross-regional coordination challenges in recovery efforts, especially since a listed subspecies in Region 2 may be considered part of a broader taxon managed elsewhere.

**Invasive species pressure**:
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) present in the San Pedro River since 1974, threatening to invade Aravaipa Creek via [[The Nature Conservancy]] property; a fish barrier was proposed, requiring engineering feasibility studies at 3–4 candidate sites.
- **Rainbow smelt** — proposed introduction into Lake Powell opposed by DFRT due to potential ecological risk to native Colorado River big-river fishes; concerns raised about the legality of the introduction under Section 7 and the Upper Basin Implementation Plan's prohibition on new species introductions.
- **Channel catfish-like hybrids** — historical introduction of an *Ictalurus lupus*-like catfish into the upper Gila River raised concern about persistent hybridization with the endangered [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*).
- **Centrarchids** — potential future introduction into a Virgin River holding pond cited as urgency for early fish rescue.

**Habitat modification impacts**:
- Rotenone treatment of the Virgin River caused a severe faunal crash; recolonization genetics are a concern because tributary and mainstem populations show distinct morphs, risking loss of original genomic diversity.
- Glen Canyon Dam operations subject to ongoing (non-jeopardy) biological opinion for [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*).
- Hooker Dam (Gila River) determined economically infeasible by the Bureau of Reclamation, weakening opposition to critical habitat designation for [[Spikedace]] and [[Meda Fulgida]].
- Miner Flat Dam proposal (White Mountain Apache Reservation) affecting [[Loach Minnow]] habitat appeared indefinitely postponed.
- Upper Verde–Prescott water withdrawal Section 7 consultation ongoing, with Prescott reportedly receptive to alternatives (e.g., groundwater pumping) to protect the Upper Verde River.

## Key Findings

### Species/Taxon-Specific Status Updates

- **[[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)**: Recovery Plan not commenced; no drafting funds. Hatchery stock derived from 150 individuals collected from the eastern Colorado River delta in 1975 (permit to R.R. Miller, University of Michigan), later supplemented with BOR fish (1978) and a 1983 USFWS-Sonora cooperative collection of 600 fish (distributed to Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Boyce Thompson Arboretum). Electrophoretic data held by A.A. Echelle (Oklahoma State University). Concerns raised over "Bog Hole" and "Research Ranch" populations of uncertain subspecific purity within the range of the typical subspecies, with Audubon Society raising concerns about subspecies validity, toxicant use, and non-target impacts of proposed renovation/restocking.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]] / [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)**: Downlisting proposal was pending; Arizona Game and Fish Department initially opposed but agreed to moderate its objection. DFRT recommended the Yaqui subspecies be excluded from a downlisting proposal intended only for the nominate subspecies, and called for a revised joint recovery plan for both subspecies.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*)**: 59 adults plus juveniles held at Dexter National Fish Hatchery. Morphological confirmation of identity pursued via R.R. Miller; electrophoretic/mtDNA analysis recommended to rule out introgression from introduced *I. lupus*-like catfish in the upper Gila.
- **[[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*)**: Discussion deferred (responsible team member absent).
- **[[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*)**: Dexter captive population remains infected with Asiatic tapeworm; another eradication treatment underway.
- **[[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*)**: DFRT unanimously urged expedited completion of the Recovery Plan, disputing an agency claim that the draft needed substantial rework.
- **[[Gila Robusta]] complex (Roundtail Chub)**: The name "*Gila robusta grahami*" determined invalid — type specimen not distinct from *G. r. robusta* (per R.R. Miller). Electrophoretic data show the "thick-bodied Gila" morph is not strongly distinct from *G. r. robusta*, but *Gila intermedia* is distinct. DFRT recommended *G. robusta* be proposed as a candidate species.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*)**: Extirpated from the entire Lower Colorado River Basin except the Virgin River. Grand Canyon populations are electrophoretically distinct from other samples, suggesting possible post-Glen Canyon Dam divergence; candidate status for Lower Basin populations left unresolved.
- **[[Zuni Bluehead Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus jarrovi*)**: Category 2 candidate species; New Mexico supports listing. DFRT recommended the Arizona-New Mexico Chapter of the American Fisheries Society formally petition for listing.
- **[[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*)**: Currently a candidate species; reported abundant at many localities including uppermost Beaver Dam Wash.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**: Basin-wide management plan reportedly being prepared by the Bureau of Reclamation. A proposal to use backwaters along the lower Colorado, Verde, and Gila rivers for razorback grow-out was circulated to AGFD, USFWS, and NMDGF.
- **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*)**: Up to $28,000 available for 1989 work; recommended actions include contracting a Recovery Plan and funding surveys of movement patterns, culvert impacts, and headwater habitat relationships.
- **[[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)**: Recovery Plan complete but stalled in the public comment process; ~50 comment letters received, including negative comments from Chino Mines (New Mexico), linked to opposition from the Hooker Dam Association. DFRT proposed drafting comment responses via the stream cyprinid subgroup. Habitat improvement recommendations included stream fencing to exclude cattle (Gila National Forest funds available) versus off-stream water development for livestock. An augmentation into the San Pedro River was recommended as an immediate action, since spikedace were locally abundant there and exotic species densities were low in target augmentation areas.
- **[[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)**: Recovery Plan status parallels spikedace; comments to be handled jointly since most letters addressed both species. Miner Flat Dam project (White Mountain Apache Reservation) apparently postponed indefinitely. Captive individuals maintained successfully in an artificial stream at Arizona State University (S. Vives), potentially limited by availability of spawning substrate (rocks).
- **[[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*)**: Region 6 assumed lead responsibility; listing considered likely to proceed. Severe population decline documented following a Virgin River rotenone treatment and associated poisoning event; almost no fish collected in the treated reach post-treatment. Recommended reconstruction of the Washington Fields diversion to restore its function as a fish barrier. Dexter National Fish Hatchery agreed to accept backup stock (100 fish) from the pond below Quail Creek Dam pending an AGFD permit.
- **[[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)**: Recovery Plan to be discussed at the upcoming Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team (CRFRT) meeting. Proposed introduction into Havasu Creek questioned, as only *Rhinichthys* (speckled dace) were found there in surveys. Critical habitat designation in the Little Colorado River discussed as biologically sound but politically contentious. Trace-element analysis of bone tissue proposed (contracting Winograd and Riggs, USGS Denver) as a tool to assess Little Colorado River residency patterns.
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)**: Recovery Plan essentially finished, in technical review. Dispute noted over inclusion of San Juan River data — an original 2-year NMGF study was shortened to 1 year, raising concerns (per D. Propst) about producing a scientifically defensible management plan; flagged as a possible Section 7 violation.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)**: Recovery Plan nearly finalized; Mohave Lake sampling planned for the second week of March 1989.
- **Glen Canyon Dam operations**: Biological opinion remains "non-jeopardy" with conservation measures attached; ongoing Region 2/Region 6 communication difficulties noted.

### Administrative/Regulatory Notes
- ESA amendments now require USFWS to monitor petitioned candidate species and delisted species for 5 years; Section 6 funding expanded to include candidate species. Arizona's Section 6 recovery budget increased from ~$193,000 to ~$703,000 year-over-year.
- New Recovery Plans must include site-specific management activities, measurable down/delisting criteria, cost and timeline estimates, and are now subject to mandatory public notice/review.
- Endangered species permitting (Form 3-200) is processed through the USFWS Regional Office in coordination with designated "lead" stations; permitting authority was slated to be decentralized to Regional Offices.

### Cross-Cutting Recommendations
- Formal DFRT reorganization into a core group plus five taxon-based working groups, approved by vote.
- Letter to the USFWS Regional Director urging prioritization of stalled Recovery Plan completions.
- Engineering and biological feasibility studies for an [[Aravaipa Creek]] fish barrier to exclude red shiner, using available Section 6 funds (~$10,000).
- DFRT opposition letter regarding the proposed rainbow smelt introduction into Lake Powell, requesting a legal review
