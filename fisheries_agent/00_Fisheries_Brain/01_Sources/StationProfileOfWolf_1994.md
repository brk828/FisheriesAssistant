# Station Profile: Wolf Creek National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
Wolf Creek National Fish Hatchery is a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] federal fish production facility located in Jamestown, Kentucky, established in 1975 under the authority of DOI & RA ACT '68 (81 Stat. 59–67). The station is sited on 21 acres of land granted by the U.S. Army in 1970, situated within a larger 923-acre tract managed by the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] below **Wolf Creek Dam**. The hatchery's primary mission is the production of **[[Rainbow Trout]]** and **[[Brown Trout]]** for stocking federal water development projects and U.S. Forest Service lands, supporting recreational and mitigation fisheries across Kentucky, Georgia, and Tennessee. The facility benefits from a multilevel dam intake structure enabling precise coldwater temperature management critical for [[Salmonidae|salmonid]] culture.

## Background & Description

- **Species Reared:** Rainbow Trout and Brown Trout (both members of [[Salmonidae]]).
- **Production Metric — Weight (lbs) vs. Number Stocked (FY1990–1992):**
  - FY1990: 620,122 fish / 177,460 lbs (Federal Mitigation Waters)
  - FY1991: 592,624 fish / 161,976 lbs (Federal Mitigation Waters)
  - FY1992: 640,053 fish / 158,912 lbs (Federal Mitigation Waters)
  - *Implied average unit weight has decreased slightly across years despite increased numbers stocked, suggesting a shift toward stocking at smaller (fingerling/sub-catchable) size classes in later distributions.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type:** Coldwater tailwater fishery immediately downstream of **[[Wolf Creek Dam]]**, part of the broader Cumberland River drainage in Kentucky.
- **Water Source Engineering:** A **multilevel intake** on Wolf Creek Dam allows water to be drawn from three distinct depth strata, supplying the hatchery at a rate of **10,000 GPM**. This engineering feature allows fine control of water temperature — essential for [[Coldwater Fisheries|coldwater species]] like trout, which require narrow thermal tolerances for optimal growth and survival.
- **Ecological Role:** The hatchery functions as a **mitigation and stocking hub**, compensating for lost or degraded natural spawning/rearing habitat caused by dam construction and reservoir operations. Stocked fish support put-and-take and put-grow-and-take recreational fisheries in reservoirs and rivers across three states.
- **Receiving Waters:** Distribution occurs into three water categories:
  - Federal Mitigation Waters
  - Other Federal Waters
  - State Waters
- **Geographic Range Serviced:** Kentucky, Georgia, and Tennessee — indicating a regional distribution network beyond the immediate Cumberland River basin.

## Key Findings
1. **Facility Establishment:** Constructed in 1975 following a 1970 land grant (21 of 923 acres) from the U.S. Army to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], enabling co-location with the Army Corps of Engineers' Wolf Creek Dam infrastructure.
2. **Infrastructure Capacity:** The station includes 64 raceways, two 20,000 lb feed bins, a sewer treatment plant, 3 residences, and a 6-bay garage/wood shop — indicating a mid-to-large-scale production hatchery.
3. **Water Supply Innovation:** The multilevel dam intake (10,000 GPM, three draw levels) is a key operational asset, allowing temperature-optimized water delivery for trout culture — a critical factor in [[Salmonid Aquaculture]] success.
4. **Production Trends (1990–1992):**
   - Numbers of fish distributed to **State Waters** nearly doubled from 95,155 (1990) to 151,845 (1992), indicating an expanding role in state-level stocking programs.
   - Federal Mitigation Waters remained the dominant distribution category by both number and weight throughout the reporting period.
   - Total weight distributed to "Other Federal Waters" declined slightly (25,286 lbs → 22,484 lbs) despite relatively stable fish numbers, suggesting smaller size-at-release in this category over time.
5. **Funding & Staffing:** Operations funding increased from $229.0K (1991 actual) to an estimated $287.0K (1994), while FTE staffing remained stable at 4–5 positions, reflecting a lean but consistent operational workforce.
6. **Regional Importance:** As one of the primary trout suppliers for federal and state waters in the Kentucky–Tennessee–Georgia region, Wolf Creek NFH plays a significant role in supporting the [[Southeastern U.S. Coldwater Fishery|regional coldwater sport fishery]] and mitigating hydropower/dam-related habitat impacts.
