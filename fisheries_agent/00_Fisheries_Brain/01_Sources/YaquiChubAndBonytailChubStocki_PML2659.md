# Yaqui Chub and Bonytail Chub Stockings from Dexter NFH (1986)

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #fish-stocking #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum, dated November 4, 1986, reports on stockings of two native southwestern fishes in Arizona by the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico. The memo was sent from the hatchery manager to the Regional Director of [[Region 2]] in Albuquerque. It lists stocking numbers by species and by release site, names the personnel involved, and describes the status of hatchery broodstock. The scan is partly garbled, so some numbers and column alignments below are uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interoffice memorandum (FWS letterhead, "Subject: Yaqui Chub and Bonytail Chub Stockings").
- **Sender:** Hatchery Manager, [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Dexter, New Mexico.
- **Recipient:** Regional Director, [[Region 2]], Albuquerque, New Mexico.
- **Species involved:**
  - [[Yaqui Chub]] (*[[Gila Purpurea]]*), abbreviated "YC" in the table. The memo states that the YC stocking was the station's total stock of this species and that Gila purpurea is no longer held at the station.
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*[[Gila Elegans]]*), abbreviated "BTC". The memo describes these fish as native southwestern species.
- **Distribution list (cc):** Eight FWS staff, including [[Steve Dobrott]] (biologist at Buenos Aires) and several other regional and station personnel.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Release sites:** Stockings were made at several Arizona locations, including tanks at the [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] (Buenos Aires NWR). The memo also names Big Bertha Tank, a King's Tank, and a pond/tank identified in the scan as a "Camp Tank." Some site names are unclear in the OCR.
- **Source of fish for [[Arizona State University]] (ASU):** Fish for ASU were collected at Buenos Aires by Dan Langhorst and Kirk Young, who also helped with stockings at the Buenos Aires NWR tanks.
- **Local fieldwork:** [[Steve Dobrott]], biologist at Buenos Aires, led the stocking effort and gave substantial help during the team's visit to the refuge.
- **Source of Bonytail fingerlings:** The memo states that the BTC fingerlings came from natural reproduction in two *Gila elegans* ponds at the hatchery.

## Key Findings

1. **Stocking totals:** The table lists the number of fish stocked for each species at each site. Counts such as 8,645 and 2,502 appear to be fish numbers for particular sites, but several cells are corrupted by the scan (for example "21966" and "2198A"), so exact per-site totals should be verified against the original.
2. **Yaqui Chub supply exhausted:** The YC stocking represented the station's entire stock. No *Gila purpurea* remain at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
3. **Bonytail Chub broodstock:** The hatchery was holding approximately 500 brood fish and 1,372 excess BTC adults. The scan gives the size as "about 15-16" and the density as "one fish to the pound," so the units are uncertain.
4. **Natural reproduction:** Bonytail fingerlings were produced through natural spawning in two *Gila elegans* ponds, a sign that the hatchery's pond system supported successful reproduction.
5. **Interagency cooperation:** The Arizona stockings depended on collaboration between the hatchery, the Buenos Aires NWR, and [[Arizona State University]] personnel, who together carried out collection, transport, and release.
