# Some Diplocentrid Scorpions from Baja California del Sur, Mexico

#arachnology #taxonomy #diplocentridae

*Note on scope: this source is a taxonomic revision of scorpions, not fish or aquatic research. The "Ecological & Environmental Context" section is limited accordingly.*

## Executive Summary

Stahnke (1968), published in the Proceedings of the [[California Academy of Sciences]], describes diplocentrid scorpions from Baja California del Sur, Mexico, a group not previously reported from the peninsula. The paper describes one new species of [[Didymocentrus]] (*D. caboensis*), one new genus ([[Bioculus]]) and fourteen further new species of *Bioculus*, and provides a dichotomous key to the genus. The work is based on a small number of specimens, mostly from the California Academy of Sciences and the Chris Parrish collection, and the author notes that some species may later be synonymized as more material becomes available.

## Background & Description

- **Prior records:** [[Ewing 1928]] and [[Hoffmann 1931]] did not record diplocentrids from Baja California; [[Gertsch 1958]] listed fourteen Baja California scorpion species in the families Buthidae and Vejovidae, with no diplocentrids. Hoffmann (1931) reported only *Diplocentrus* from the Mexican mainland.
- **Genus Didymocentrus:** Established by [[Kraepelin]] (1905) on the basis of a single character, the absence of lateral (terminal) lobes on tarsomere II. Members are generally small (seldom over 48 mm), have three pairs of lateral eyes, an exterior pedal spur, and 5 to 10 pectinal teeth. The genus centre of distribution is described as the West Indies.
- **Genus Bioculus (new):** Distinguished by having two pairs of lateral eyes rather than three. It is separated from [[Oeclus]] by a median carapacial notch, pedal spurs, tarsomere spine formulas, pectinal tooth counts and trichobothrial positions. It resembles Didymocentrus in lacking tarsomere lobes but differs from both related genera in several features, such as the consistent position of trichobothrium M1 on the manus.
- **Method:** Trichobothria (sensory macrochaetes) were mapped, and tarsomere II spine counts were given as fractional formulae (inner/outer margin, per leg pair). Measurements and ratios were taken from precise measurements. Species were separated mainly by pedipalp punctation, carapace and sternum ratios, pectine and third marginal lamella ratios, and trichobothrial patterns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a taxonomic study and contains no quantitative ecological data (no population counts, abundance, diet, behaviour or habitat measurements). The only ecological information given is collection geography:

- Most material comes from islands in the Gulf of California (Cerralvo, Santa Cruz, Danzante, Espiritu Santo, San Francisco), plus mainland localities near San Jose del Cabo, Comondu and Parras Canyon. One record gives an elevation of 1870 feet (Parras Canyon).
- The author notes a restricted specimen base and morphological homogeneity, which hampered species determination.
- Several species occur on the same island and some holotypes were taken on the same day, which the author uses to support distinctions among closely related forms.

## Key Findings

- Diplocentrid scorpions are reported from Baja California del Sur for the first time, a group absent from earlier regional works by Ewing, Hoffmann and Gertsch.
- One new species of *Didymocentrus* (*D. caboensis*) and one new genus (*Bioculus*) are described, with fourteen further new *Bioculus* species.
- *Bioculus* is separated from *Oeclus* and *Didymocentrus* using eye number, median carapacial notch, pedal spurs, pectinal tooth counts and trichobothrial positions.
- A dichotomous key to the genus *Bioculus* is provided, using pedipalp punctation, ratios and trichobothrial patterns.
- Holotypes are deposited at the California Academy of Sciences, with the Didymocentrus holotype deposited at the MCZ Harvard University.
- The author states that species designations are provisional and some may be synonymized once more specimens are available.
