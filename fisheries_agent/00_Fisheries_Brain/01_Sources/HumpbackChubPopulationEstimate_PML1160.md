# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Desolation/Gray Canyon, Green River, Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-spp

## Executive Summary

This FY2003 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] documents the third and final year of a three-year project to estimate the population of late juvenile/adult [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila* spp.) in Desolation/Gray Canyons on the [[Green River]], Utah. The population estimate is needed because recovery goals for this endangered species depend partly on maintaining populations at several sites. The project also tests whether [[ISMP]] catch rates correlate with population size. Three sampling passes were completed in fall 2003 at 12 sites, yielding 223 to 258 chub depending on the count used. The report is a preliminary summary of raw data, and the population estimate itself was not yet generated.

## Background & Description

**Project identity**
- Project number 129, prepared by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] Moab Field Station (principal investigators Julie A. Jackson and J. Michael Hudson).
- Study schedule: initial year 2001, final year 2003.
- Relationship to the 2003 RIPRAP: Green River Action Plan (Mainstem, p. 27), task V.B.1, "Conduct population estimate for humpback chub... Desolation/Gray."

**Objectives**
1. Obtain a population estimate of late juvenile/adult humpback chub in Desolation/Gray Canyons.
2. Determine whether a relationship exists between ISMP catch rates and population size.

**Rationale for fall sampling**
Earlier sampling was done in summer at flows below 8,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) to maximize catch. In 2003, sampling moved to fall to avoid capturing spawning [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and to reduce handling stress on humpback chub.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area and sites**
- Twelve sites were sampled, including the four long-term trend sites at river miles (RM) 185, 174.4, 160.4 and 145.7.
- Other sites: RM 202, 182, 174.2, 166.8, 158.7, 155, 153.5 and 147.5.
- Four "wild card" sites from earlier ISMP monitoring were included among the twelve.

**Conditions by pass**

| Pass | Dates (2003) | Mean water temp (°C) | Flow (cfs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aug 31 – Sep 8 | 23.0 | 1050–1250 |
| 2 | Sep 28 – Oct 4 | 19.0 | 1300–1520 |
| 3 | Oct 22–29 | 11.5 | 1430–1470 |

- Flows were taken from the [[USGS Gage 09315000]] (Green River at Green River).
- Low flows meant no inflatable sport boats were used, and some sites had reduced sampling areas.

**Gear and effort**
- [[Trammel Netting]]: six to eight 75-ft nets per site, fished from late afternoon to midnight and again pre-dawn to morning. One night was spent at each site. The gear was intended to target the adult component.
- [[Electrofishing]]: one pass at each of the 12 sites, intended to capture juveniles. Effort was 11 hours.
- [[Hoop Net]] and [[Minnow Trap]]: three of each per site, baited with cat food and set parallel to flow where possible.
- Fish were recaptured within the same pass and site at a rate of 15%. Holding pens were not used, which the report identifies as a source of repeat handling stress.

## Key Findings

**Catch summary**
- Total chub captured: 223 (Summary), 258 (Methods and Table 1), and n=224 (Figure 1). The report notes the counts differ because fish that escaped during measurement or were recaptured at the same site were excluded from some analyses.
- Trammel nets produced the highest catch: 258 chub from 2,970.2 net hours, for an overall CPUE of 0.088 fish/net hour.
- Trammel catch by pass: 44 (CPUE 0.444), 52 (0.054), and 162 (0.165). Catch rates rose across passes, with the most fish in the fall.
- Electrofishing yielded only one chub in 11 hours.
- Hoop nets and minnow traps yielded six chubs across the first two passes, two of them juveniles. Their CPUE was low and similar (0.001–0.003).

**Size structure**
- Mean total length (TL) was 256 mm (Methods; Figure 1 gives 254.9 mm) with a range of 114–434 mm.
- Trammel-net mean TL was 256.2 mm (range 188–434 mm).
- Hoop-net mean TL was 231 mm (n = 5).
- Five subadults were under 200 mm TL, and four of these were taggable (>150 mm TL).
- One unidentified chub from a minnow trap measured 114 mm.

**Movement and recapture**
- One fish first captured on pass 1 was recaptured on pass 3 after moving 14 miles downstream. No other movement was recorded.
- Fifteen long-term recaptures (fish [[PIT Tagging|PIT tagged]] in earlier years) were captured in 2003 and observed on all three trips.

**Recommendations**
1. If fall sampling continues, sample after mid-October or when water temperatures are near 10 °C, a window when catch rates may be higher and stress lower.
2. Revisit late spring/early summer sampling. Lower fall-2001 catch rates relative to spring/early summer sampling in prior years match historic [[Desolation Canyon]] and [[Gray Canyon]] data, and that season may support a more accurate estimate.
3. Use a holding pen at each site to prevent repeat captures and reduce handling stress.

**Status and budget**
- Project status: ongoing, with the third year of three completed.
- FY03 budget: $104,000 budgeted, $83,200 expended, and $20,800 difference (80% of funds spent). The report lists 90% of FY03 work completed, which does not match the expenditure ratio.
- Recovery Program funds for publication charges: $0.
- Data entry and transfer to [[USFWS]] are scheduled for January 15, 2004.

**Data caveats**
The report is a preliminary summary of raw data. Final population estimates depend on further verification and analysis. Some numbers in the text (such as effort totals and some OCR-affected values) were reported as printed and may need checking against the original data.
