# Listing Priority Memorandum: Three Catostomid Fishes for the Animal Candidate Review List

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

A memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 2 Director asks the national Ecological Services office to add three sucker species to the [[Animal Candidate Review]] List under 50 CFR Part 17: the [[Sonora Sucker]], the [[Desert Sucker]], and the [[Little Colorado Sucker]]. Priority Number Assignment Forms dated July 30, 1993 are attached for each species. All three are recommended for Category 2, based on historic and ongoing range and population declines, continuing threats, and the recommendation of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]. The forms rate each species' threat magnitude as high and its immediacy as imminent.

## Background & Description

- **Request:** Regional Director, Region 2, to the Director, Fish and Wildlife Service, Ecological Services, Washington, D.C. Sent from Albuquerque, New Mexico.
- **Species:**
  - *Catostomus insignis* (Sonora sucker), family [[Catostomidae]].
  - *Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarkii* (desert sucker).
  - *Catostomus* sp. (little Colorado sucker). The little Colorado sucker is described as relatively narrowly endemic, with a historic range limited to the upper reaches of the Little Colorado River basin.
- **Category:** Category 2 for all three species, as recommended by the Desert Fishes Recovery Team.
- **Contacts:** Sally Stefferud (Arizona Ecological Services Field Station, compiler) and George Divine (New Mexico Ecological Services). Copied to Dr. W.O. Minckley, Desert Fishes Recovery Team Leader, Arizona State University.
- **Supporting offices:** Arizona Ecological Services Field Station (lead), New Mexico Ecological Services Office (Dexter), National Fish Hatchery, Arizona Fisheries Resources Office, and Regions 6 and 7.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The threat analysis follows the five listing factors used under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Its shared findings are:

1. **Habitat present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment.** All three species have been seriously reduced in numbers and distribution within substantial portions of their historic ranges. Causes include dewatering, diversion, groundwater pumping, dam construction, channelization, and watershed erosion. Additional water development and ongoing watershed damage threaten large portions of the remaining range.
2. **Overutilization.** None known for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes.
3. **Disease or predation.** Historic and ongoing introduction and spread of [[Nonnative Fish Species]] are a serious threat through predation and competition. Exotic diseases and parasites introduced with nonnative fish are also a threat.
4. **Inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms.** Existing regulations are described as failing to prevent the continuing decline of these species.
5. **Other natural or manmade factors.** For the little Colorado sucker, the restricted historic range makes it particularly vulnerable to threats to its continued existence.

## Key Findings

- **Sonora sucker:** Habitat loss and nonnative fish threats are rated as high magnitude and imminent. The memo references a "Mexico" range component; the Sonora subspecies are already in Category 2.
- **Desert sucker:** Same threat profile, rated high magnitude and imminent. Threat sources include habitat modification and nonnative predators and competitors.
- **Little Colorado sucker:** Rated high magnitude and imminent. Its restricted endemic range in the upper Little Colorado River basin is identified as a specific vulnerability.
- **Expert basis:** The Desert Fishes Recovery Team reviewed the status of all three species. Its recommendation rests on personal knowledge and unpublished data, citing historic and ongoing declines and continuing and future threats.
- **Requested action:** Add all three species to the Animal Candidate Review List at Category 2, with attached Priority Number Assignment Forms.
