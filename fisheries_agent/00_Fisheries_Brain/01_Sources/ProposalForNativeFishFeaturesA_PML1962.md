# Native Fish Sanctuary Proposals and Management Plans, Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of correspondence, a site-development proposal, two sanctuary management plans, and a regional site inventory for conserving [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] on the lower [[Colorado River]]. The author proposes developing [[Three-Figures Lake]] (TFL), a historic dredged oxbow in [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]], as either an adult [[Adult Repository]] or a [[Native Fish Sanctuary]]. Two full management plans are included: one for [[Cibola High Levee Pond]] and one for [[Davis Cove]] in [[Lake Mohave]]. The material covers a site inventory, stocking needs, triggering thresholds, monitoring protocols, and research priorities.

## Background & Description

**Three-Figures Lake (TFL).** The lake is an oxbow that filled in and became overgrown after the closure of Hoover Dam. The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] dredged it in the early 1990s as part of a native fish program. It covers about 15 surface acres, with a dredged channel of nearly 2 miles in a horseshoe shape. The site is remote, with vehicle access limited to a boat launch at its southwestern corner. The document places the lake in California, although the refuge is elsewhere described as being in Arizona.

**Cibola High Levee Pond.** The [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) converted this pond into a rearing facility in 1993. Between 1993 and 1996, about 58,400 small bonytail and 14,000 razorback suckers were stocked. Fish reaching 30 cm were PIT-tagged and moved elsewhere. In 1998 both species were found to be reproducing, and stocking was suspended to study the population.

**Davis Cove.** This cove on Lake Mohave was selected in 1991 as a grow-out site. A net barrier was installed, isolating about 1.3 ha at a depth of 7 m. Survival of stocked razorbacks was extremely poor, at 0.1%. An earthen berm replaced the net in 1995, and the cove has since been managed as a grow-out facility.

**Program origin.** The approach traces to the [[Native Fish Work Group]], formed in 1989 by state, federal, and academic partners to save razorback suckers in Lake Mohave. Wild-born larvae were grown in isolated ponds and then stocked to augment the dwindling population of old adults.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Three-Figures Lake conditions.**
- Mid-channel depths averaged 5 to 10 feet.
- Conductivity was around 1200 µS/cm (printed as "Uohm/cm").
- The channel was heavily infested with [[Coontail]] (*Ceratophyllum demersum*). The author suspects that decaying biomass causes anoxic conditions, which may explain the recent absence of fish.
- Sampling in 2003 found a typical set of nonnative fishes and no natives. A 2005 trip found no fish, suggesting a possible fish kill.

**Cibola High Levee Pond.**
- The pond is a historical channel segment with varied substrates. Permeable levee and river allow groundwater exchange, which the plan says helps maintain temperature and [[Dissolved Oxygen]] (DO).
- Vegetation includes *Potamogeton* and *Najas*. Fish gather in the deepest water, where groundwater inflow is suspected.
- A [[Largemouth Bass]] population was found in 2004. Bass spawned in 2005, and by late 2005 they dominated the fish community, with more than 80% of the community and no sign of young natives.
- The community was estimated at roughly 6,000 bonytail (>15 cm) and 1,100 razorback suckers. In 2002 carrying capacity was measured at 4,350 fish/ha, with a biomass of 635 kg/ha.

**Davis Cove conditions.**
- Conductivity rose nearly fivefold over the past decade, to about 5,000 µS/cm. The author attributes this to limited exchange with the reservoir, with saline water moving through the subsurface beneath the berm.
- Vegetation shifted from pondweed and spiny naiad to sparse stands of stonewart (*Chara* spp.), which may affect the food web.

**Lake Mohave repatriation.** About 1,500 adult razorbacks remain from more than 100,000 large suckers stocked. Survival of adults in reservoirs and the mainstem is not occurring, so the author argues that off-site holding is needed to preserve genetic diversity. The author estimates TFL could support 2,000 to 3,000 adult suckers based on carrying capacity at Cibola.

## Key Findings

**Proposed TFL management options.**
- **Adult Repository:** Goal is to augment adult razorbacks grown from wild-born larvae. Actions include a management plan, a seasonal solar-powered [[Aeration System]] on pontoons deployed June through October, expanded [[Aquatic Weed Control]] for coontail, a floating boom to limit boat access, a [[Salvage Operation]] to remove large nonnative predators, stocking of 30 cm razorbacks at 250/acre (3,000 total as stated), and monitoring.
- **Native Fish Sanctuary:** Goal is natural recruitment of bonytail and razorback suckers. Actions include earthen berms isolating 1 to 4 sections of 1 to 5 acres each, ground-based aeration for each section, weed control, pumping or [[Chemical Renovation]], stocking of monocultures or mixed populations, and monitoring.
- The author cites five reasons for TFL's potential: the site was built for native fish, a recent natural fish kill removed most nonnatives, the Office Cove aeration system could be transferred, the refuge has an aquatic weed program, and the shallow constricted channel would allow effective predator removal.

**Triggering thresholds (Cibola High Levee Pond).**

| Trigger | Management action |
|---|---|
| >5% nonnative fish | Add to renovation list; initiate salvage |
| Poor recruitment | Initiate bullfrog, tadpole, and [[Crayfish]] control |
| Natural fish die-off | Restock with multiple year classes |
| Poor body condition (stunting) | Harvest and remove >20% |

The Davis Cove triggers are similar and add two more: spawning or recruitment failure (add spawning substrate) and salinity as a limiting factor (add fresh water by pumping).

**Monitoring program (both plans).** Key elements include:
- In-situ profiles with a Hydrolab, 24-hour DO and temperature monitoring with MiniSondes, and long-term temperature loggers.
- Water chemistry and contaminant analysis (Hg, Se, As, ClO4).
- Zooplankton tows, macroinvertebrate sampling at six locations, and aquatic vegetation mapping and health checks.
- Fish population surveys (snorkeling, electrofishing, trammel nets), larval light traps, and PIT-tag growth tracking.
- Data custodian: Jeanette Carpenter; annual reports to interested parties.

**Site inventory.** The regional table lists 15 existing or proposed sanctuary, repository, or rearing sites, ranked by status (complete, nearly complete, high prospect, interest, or medium/low). Examples:
- **Davis Cove:** renovated September 2005; 150 adult razorbacks and 1,500 mature bonytail stocked October–November.
- **Office Cove:** renovated and stocked with razorbacks; managed as a rearing facility.
- **Cibola High Levee Pond:** one salvage completed; FWS is pursuing permits for chemical renovation.
- **[[Hassayampa Preserve]]:** previously stocked but summer-killed; The Nature Conservancy is supportive.
- **Three Fingers Lake:** dredged for endangered species, now choked with watermilfoil; a proposed division into smaller reaches with dikes and herbicide treatment.
- **Iceberg Canyon, Stillman Lake, Sahara Estate Lakes, Cocopah, Beal Slough:** at various stages of planning.

Additional sites are flagged as low priority or requiring major modification, including [[Pittsburgh Point]] (shallow, low DO; deepening suggested), Achi Hanyo (9 to 12 ponds of 0.12 acre planned for 2006), and Palo Verde abandoned canal (about 300 yards, drained annually and renovatable without chemicals).

**Stocking needs (perceived).** The author lists these as proposed numbers, deferring to FWS on final decisions:
- 3-Finger: 2,000 razorbacks (30 cm), fall 2006.
- Bulkhead: 1,000 bonytail (10+ cm).
- High Levee: 3,000 razorbacks and 10,000 bonytail, fall 2006.
- BHC Nature Center: 5,700 razorbacks and 10,000 juvenile bonytail, spring 2007.
- Davis Cove: 500 razorbacks (30 cm), fall 2006.
- Demand for razorbacks may exceed 11,000 fish this year, and more than 20,000 bonytail could be stocked.
- The Augmentation Plan calls for 6,000 suckers over five years, to be grown to 45+ cm at the Nature Center before release.

**Research needs.** Priorities include PIT-tag technology at 134.2 kHz, efficient salvage methods such as broadcast feeders and pop nets, measuring hydraulic exchange and developing artificial recharge, bonytail rearing and growth, floating island aeration and shade, spawning and cover requirements, and salinity tolerances of native fish and their food web organisms.

**Immediate actions proposed.** The author proposes reviewing the TFL plan this year, along with a proposed freshening of Davis Cove by pumping reservoir water into the cove during a fall or winter period. The author also recommends giving High Levee Pond priority for Mohave suckers if supply is short.
