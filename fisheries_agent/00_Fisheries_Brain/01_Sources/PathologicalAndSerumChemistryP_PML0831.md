# Pathological and Serum Chemistry Profiles of Brown Bullheads from the Black River and Old Woman Creek, Ohio

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This study examined liver and external neoplasia, parasitism, and serum chemistry in [[Brown Bullhead]] (*[[Ameiurus Nebulosus]]*) collected in July 1992 from the [[Black River]] (a site with known [[Polynuclear Aromatic Hydrocarbons]] contamination) and from the reference site [[Old Woman Creek]], both in [[Ohio]] and tributary to [[Lake Erie]]. Liver neoplasia was common in Black River fish, dominated by [[Cholangiocellular Carcinoma]] and [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]]. Serum chemistry differed between sites, with significantly elevated liver enzymes and lipids at the Black River. However, no single serum measurement predicted any individual neoplasm or liver parasite, which the authors attribute mainly to small sample sizes.

## Background & Description

Numerous reports describe neoplasia in feral fish, and laboratory studies have linked chemical contaminants to fish tumors. Few studies, however, had tested whether serum chemistry changes accompany tumor development in wild fish. The authors aimed to determine whether serum chemistry could serve as an indicator of pathological change in feral brown bullheads and to characterize their liver lesions and parasites.

The brown bullhead is a North American freshwater catfish that has served as a sentinel species in [[Fish Tumor Prevalence]] studies. Previous work by Baumann and Harshbarger and colleagues (1982 to 1990) documented both hepatic and biliary tumors in Black River fish. The current study did not attempt to relate lesions or serum values to specific xenobiotic concentrations.

**Study sites:**
- **Black River, Ohio:** Heavily contaminated with PAHs, including [[Benzo(A)Pyrene]] (BaP).
- **Old Woman Creek:** Reference site, part of the [[Old Woman Creek National Estuarine Research Reserve]], with a small estuary and no major known contamination source in the text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Collection methods:** Fish were captured in July 1992 using overnight sets of nets. Only fish greater than 250 mm (age 2+) were retained for serum analysis and histopathology. Blood was drawn and analyzed on an automated clinical chemistry analyzer at 37 °C, with serum stored for analysis. Statistical comparisons between sites and between fish with and without lesions were made using analysis of variance and correlation analysis (SAS software).

**Sample sizes:**
- Black River: 46 fish examined for histopathology (45 for serum chemistry).
- Old Woman Creek: 29 tumor-free fish provided reference serum values.

**Parasites and environmental interactions:** Parasites were common at both sites. The authors note that pollutants may increase host susceptibility or provide food sources for intermediate hosts, though the interactions among parasites, natural factors, anthropogenic factors, and organisms remain poorly understood.

## Key Findings

### Neoplasia (Black River fish)

- **Overall cancer incidence:** The scanned text gives a cancer incidence figure that is garbled; the authors' later comparison reports a total tumor-bearing percentage of 58% in 1992 compared with 60% in 1982.
- **Biliary neoplasms:** 22 cases (48%), comprising 3 [[Cholangioma]] and 19 cholangiocellular carcinomas. Cholangiomas were well-differentiated and non-invasive. Carcinomas ranged from minute to over 1.0 cm in diameter, and larger lesions were often centrally necrotic.
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma:** 14 cases (30%), with lesions from 1.5 to 4.0 mm.
- **Combined lesions:** 11 fish (24%) had both biliary neoplasms and hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Foci of cellular alteration:** 8 fish (17%). Median diameter about 500 µm (about 50 cells). Whether these are preneoplastic or incipient neoplastic is unclear, but they appear to be part of the tumor sequence.
- **External tumors:** 5 fish had external tumors (squamous cell carcinomas, with one epidermal papilloma). One presumptive mesothelioma was also observed.

### Sex and age patterns

- Females had a higher percentage of hepatic neoplasms (79%) and biliary neoplasms (55%) than males. The authors highlight a significantly greater incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma in females, consistent with earlier work on 1981 to 1982 collections.
- Tumor prevalence rose with age: about 30% in Age 3 fish, 67% in Age 4, and 90% in Age 5. Only three Age 6 fish were collected, so that class is too small for firm conclusions.
- Tumor counts by age: 6 (Age 3), 7 (Age 4), 9 (Age 5), 2 (Age 6).
- The ratio of cholangiocellular carcinomas to hepatocellular carcinomas remained constant between the 1982 and 1992 samples.

### Parasitism

- **Helminth and cestode infections:** At least 29 Black River fish (about 65% as scanned) and 18 Old Woman Creek fish (62%) carried parasitic infections. Cestode infection rates did not differ between sites, suggesting contaminants neither affected cestode viability nor increased host susceptibility.
- **[[Myxidium Mellum]]:** Bile duct infection was documented in Black River and Old Woman Creek fish. Old Woman Creek fish were more heavily parasitized with *M. mellum* per equal section counts, possibly reflecting a contaminant effect on the intermediate host of this myxozoan.

### Serum chemistry

- **Site differences:** Black River fish showed significantly elevated serum [[Alanine Aminotransferase]] (ALT), [[Aspartate Aminotransferase]] (AST), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL), consistent with liver parenchymal damage. The text also reports significantly altered total protein, BUN, triglycerides, and calcium, with decreased phosphate.
- **Interpretation:** Elevated ALT, AST, and LDL indicate liver damage. Elevated triglycerides may reflect lipid sequestration of lipophilic xenobiotics. Elevated BUN may indicate kidney pathology, though kidneys were not examined histologically. Increased calcium and decreased phosphate may result from tumor-secreted estrogen stimulating [[Vitellogenin]] production.
- **Estrogen:** Total serum estrogen (estrone plus estradiol) was significantly elevated in both males and females with tumors.
- **Correlations:** Serum enzymes ALP, ALT, AST, and LDL were correlated in Black River fish. Several other significant correlations (Na with Cl and Albumin; Total Protein with Albumin; Ca, triglycerides, phosphate, and cholesterol) match those reported in other species such as [[Striped Mullet]] and [[Pinfish]].

### Predictive value

- No serum measurement predicted any single neoplasm (cholangioma, cholangiocellular carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma), focus of hepatocellular alteration, or liver parasite at the individual fish level.
- The authors attribute this mainly to small sample sizes (3 cholangiomas, 19 cholangiocellular carcinomas, 14 hepatocellular carcinomas), natural variability, and variable physiological responses to tumors. They suggest that continued serum monitoring of tumor-bearing fish may yield significant relationships.

### Conclusions

- Liver neoplasia, especially [[Cholangiocellular Carcinoma]], was common in Black River brown bullheads, with a sex-dependent pattern favoring females for hepatocellular carcinoma.
- Serum chemistry clearly distinguished the contaminated Black River population from the reference Old Woman Creek population, indicating liver abnormalities at the population level.
- Serum chemistry alone was not a reliable individual-level diagnostic for liver lesions or parasites in this study.
