# Trip 21 Report — AGFD Native Fish Studies (Colorado River, Grand Canyon)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Gila-Cypha #Freshwater-Ecology

## Executive Summary

This AGFD field report documents native and non-native fish sampling along the Colorado River in Grand Canyon from October 26 to November 15, 1993 (Trip 21). Sampling occurred via seining in backwaters, tributary mouths, and mainstem shorelines across river miles 0–225.7. A total of **322 fish were captured, including 59 [[Humpback Chub]]** — a substantial decline from the nearly 5,000 young-of-year (YOY) fish (including >2,000 humpback chub) captured during the September 1993 trip. The drop in catch is attributed to unusually **low turbidity (4–56 NTU)** and **cool mainstem temperatures (10.6–11.5°C)**, which may have driven fish to alternate habitats, downstream migration, or increased mortality. Notable findings include continued use of the **Eminence backwater (RM 44.27L)** as a key nursery site for [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] YOY, and discovery of a **warm spring-fed backwater (RM 181.15L, up to 23.4°C)** hypothesized as a potential spawning/thermal refuge site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat types sampled:** Backwaters, tributary mouths (Little Colorado River, Havasu Creek, Kanab Creek, Shinumo Creek, Crystal Creek, Chuar Creek), side channels, and mainstem shoreline/ledge habitats.

**Thermal regime:**
- Mainstem: 10.6–11.5°C (no downstream warming trend detected)
- Backwaters: 9.3–13.6°C (generally cooler than adjacent mainstem)
- Tributaries: Shinumo Creek 8.8°C, Kanab Creek 8.9°C, Havasu Creek 14.3°C
- Anomalous warm spring-fed backwater (RM 181.15L): up to **23.4°C**, pH 6.6, conductivity 1240 µS, dissolved oxygen 6.1 mg/L (72% saturation) — flagged as possible chub/sucker spawning habitat, consistent with prior YOY chub captures in lower canyon backwaters.

**Flow conditions:** Mainstem flows ranged from an extreme low of 5,000–6,000 cfs to a high of 15,000–16,000 cfs, driven by daily hydropower discharge fluctuations. Low flows caused **dewatering of productive backwaters** below the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence that had supported large numbers of fish in September, and caused the formerly productive LCR side channel to become nearly completely silted-in and exposed.

**Feeding behavior:** Four YOY [[Humpback Chub]] were observed feeding among *Cladophora* algal strands on rocks near the water surface in full sun at Lava Chuar campsite (RM 65.3R) — a direct behavioral observation of chub foraging ecology.

**Species distribution notes:**
- Eminence backwater (RM 44.27L) remains the **primary known nursery site** for both YOY [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]].
- Only a single chub (114 mm) was found below Nevills Rapid (RM 75.5), in a small backwater near Blacktail Canyon (RM 119.13R).
- **No chub were captured in the lower canyon below Lava Falls** (RM >179), despite favorable warm-spring habitat.
- Tributary hikes (Chuar Creek, Shinumo Creek) revealed dace and juvenile trout populations extending several miles upstream from the confluence.
- [[Brown Trout]] and [[Rainbow Trout]] were documented in Shinumo Creek, indicating salmonid use of tributary refugia.

## Key Findings

- **Total catch: 322 fish**, including 59 [[Humpback Chub]] — a sharp decline from ~5,000 fish (2,000+ chub) in September 1993, likely linked to lower turbidity, cooler temperatures, and backwater dewatering rather than a true population crash.
- **Night sampling** yielded only marginal catch improvements over daytime sampling, suggesting turbidity/visibility was not the sole limiting factor in catchability.
- **Habitat loss**: Low river and LCR flows caused significant dewatering and siltation of previously productive backwater/side-channel nursery habitat near the LCR confluence, with at least one dead YOY chub and one stranded (unrecovered) YOY chub documented.
- **Potential new thermal refuge/spawning habitat** identified: warm spring-fed backwater near RM 181.15L, with elevated temperature (23.4°C) relative to typical mainstem/backwater conditions — proposed as an explanation for prior lower-canyon YOY chub records.
- **No fish were tagged or recaptured** during this trip.
- **Seven humpback chub specimens were preserved** for otolith aging and stomach content analysis, spanning 24–93 mm TL.
- Findings support the value of proposed **Native Fish Research Flows** (stable flow regime) to evaluate backwater residency duration and habitat use by native YOY fish independent of confounding temperature/turbidity effects.
- Cross-reference with concurrent **Bio/West sampling** (different gear/habitats) was recommended to determine whether YOY fish had shifted to unsampled mainstem habitats rather than experiencing mortality or emigration.
