## Executive Summary
This study represents the first scientific contribution examining the feeding habits of the [[San Pedro Mártir Trout]] (*Salmo nelsoni*), an endemic salmonid restricted to the Sierra San Pedro Mártir mountain range in Baja California, Mexico. Researchers analyzed the stomach contents of 55 specimens (65–215 mm Standard Length) collected from multiple localities within the sierra. Diet composition was assessed both qualitatively and quantitatively using the [[Index of Relative Importance]] (IRI). The analysis identified 21 distinct food items, consolidated into 12 major food categories, revealing a diet dominated overwhelmingly by aquatic and terrestrial insects.

## Background & Description
*Note: The source text focuses on dietary/trophic ecology rather than detailed morphology. The following size data was extracted:*
- **Standard Length (SL) Range:** 65 mm – 215 mm
- **Sample Size (N):** 55 specimens
- **Size-based Diet Variation:** No statistically significant qualitative differences were found in diet composition across the different size classes (juveniles vs. adults) examined, suggesting a consistent trophic strategy throughout ontogeny (post-juvenile stages).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[San Pedro Mártir Trout]] occupies a specialized niche as an insectivorous predator within isolated, high-altitude stream ecosystems.
- **Habitat:** Endemic to the [[Sierra San Pedro Mártir]] river systems, B.C., Mexico.
- **Trophic Level:** Functions as a generalist insectivore, preying on both aquatic invertebrate larvae and terrestrial insects that fall into the water column.
- **Feeding Strategy:** The lack of significant dietary shift across size classes suggests the trout does not undergo a major niche shift (e.g., from invertivore to piscivore) within the sampled size range (up to 215 mm), relying consistently on macroinvertebrate prey.

## Key Findings
- **Diet Composition:** The diet is composed of **78% insects**, indicating a highly specialized insectivorous feeding habit.
- **Dominant Prey Taxa (Order Level):**
    - **[[Trichoptera]]** (Caddisflies): 27.5% (Most important prey item)
    - **[[Odonata]]** (Dragonflies/Damselflies): 16.2%
    - **[[Coleoptera]]** (Beetles): 15.8%
- **Diversity of Diet:** 21 specific food items were identified, grouped into 12 broader food categories, indicating a moderately diverse diet within the insect-dominated framework.
- **Methodology:** The study utilized the **[[Index of Relative Importance]]**, a standard ichthyological method combining frequency of occurrence, numerical abundance, and volumetric/gravimetric weight to rank prey significance.
- **Ontogenetic Consistency:** Statistical analysis revealed no significant qualitative differences in diet between different size groups of trout, suggesting size does not heavily dictate prey selection in this species.
