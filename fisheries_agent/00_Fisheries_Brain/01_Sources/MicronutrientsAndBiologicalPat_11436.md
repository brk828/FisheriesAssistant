# Micronutrients and Biological Patterns in Lake Mead

#limnology #reservoir-ecology #eutrophication #water-quality #primary-productivity

## Executive Summary

This final report (Contract 14-06-300-2210, 1971) presents an integrated study of chemistry, hydrobiology, and hydrodynamics in [[Lake Mead]], conducted by the University of Arizona for the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation. The investigation examined eight sampling stations across the reservoir's four major sections ([[Boulder Basin]], [[Virgin Basin]], [[Overton Arm]], and [[Gregg's Basin]]) to quantify biological productivity, plankton populations, trace metal concentrations, and water chemistry. The study revealed unexpectedly severe eutrophication in [[Boulder Basin]], with primary productivity rates placing the entire system above oligotrophic levels year-round and reaching polluted lake status during warmer months. The reservoir exhibits a warm monomictic thermal cycle with winter turnover at 11±1°C and summer stratification creating hypolimnetic stress conditions. Data acquisition spanned multiple seasonal surveys measuring temperature, dissolved oxygen, nutrients (phosphate, nitrate, bicarbonate), major ions, trace metals, light penetration, zooplankton populations, and primary productivity using C-14 techniques.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Mead]] was created by [[Hoover Dam]] on the [[Colorado River]] and represents this hemisphere's largest man-made reservoir when full, covering 157,900 acres with maximum depth of approximately 500 feet and capacity of 26.1 million acre-feet. The reservoir extends 110 miles and comprises four major sections. Water sources include the [[Colorado River]] (98% of inflow), [[Virgin River]] (1.5%), [[Muddy River]] (0.3%), and [[Las Vegas Wash]] (0.2%). Eight water-quality stations were established: three in [[Boulder Basin]] (Las Vegas Wash, Bureau of Reclamation Raft, Beacon Island), one in [[Virgin Basin]] (Bonelli Landing), three in [[Overton Arm]] (Miner's Cove, Echo Bay, Temple Bar), and one in [[Gregg's Basin]] (South Cove). All stations exceeded 30 meters depth and were positioned away from shore interference.

The study objectives were: (1) determine trace metal balance at different locations, (2) measure biological productivity and conduct plankton population counts, (3) derive functions relating biological productivity to trace metals, suspended sediments, and physical variables, and (4) develop a mathematical model for predicting spatial and temporal changes. Sampling employed PVC Van Dorn samplers to prevent ion exchange, with measurements including continuous temperature and dissolved oxygen profiles to 54 meters, pH, complete chemical analysis at 5 and 30 meters, phytoplankton and zooplankton samples at 11 depths (0-35m), and light transparency using photoelectric cells to determine compensation depth (1% of incoming solar radiation).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Mead]] exhibits a warm monomictic thermal regime with water temperatures never below 4°C, freely circulating during winter at or above 4°C. Temperature range was 10.1 to 28.4°C, with homogeneous winter temperatures of 11±1°C during November, January, and February turnover. By September, distinct epilimnion, thermocline (18-28m depth, 17-26°C), and hypolimnion layers developed. The dissolved oxygen pattern follows a negative heterograde scheme with minimum DO in the thermocline (as low as 1.8 ppm) and higher saturations in epilimnion and hypolimnion. September data showed hypolimnetic DO values of 2 ppm at thermocline depth, indicating high Biochemical Oxygen Demand as sinking algae accumulate at the density barrier and undergo bacterial decomposition, releasing ions and consuming oxygen.

Soluble salts increased approximately 100 ppm from South Cove to Bureau Raft across all seasons, ranging 600-840 ppm. Electrical conductivity increased 0.30 × 10³ at each station throughout the year, ranging 0.95-1.25 × 10³ micro mhos, placing [[Lake Mead]] deep into eutrophic lake range (>200 micro mhos at 18°C versus <200 for oligotrophic). Major ions (chloride, magnesium, potassium, sodium, calcium) increased across basins due to salt loading and concentration; chloride levels were five times higher than [[Lake Erie]]. Ortho-phosphate levels (0.1-0.3 ppm) fell in oligotrophic range but exceeded the 0.01 ppm minimum for excessive algae. Nitrate values were much higher in September and November, consistently exceeding the 0.3 ppm minimum for algae blooms. Bicarbonate showed excellent response to thermal stratification with higher values at 30m depth. Sulphate increased by more than 60 ppm across the lake, reaching levels 15 times higher than [[Lake Erie]], attributed to chemical action and hydrogen sulphide release under low oxygen/low redox conditions in basin bottoms.

Zooplankton communities included three dominant groups: [[Cladocerans]] ([[Daphnia]], [[Bosmina]]), [[Copepods]] ([[Calanoida]], [[Cyclopoida]], nauplii), and [[Rotifers]] ([[Keratella]]). [[Daphnia]] peaked April-June (maximum 3-10m depth) with minimum September-January. [[Bosmina]] peaked February-April (maximum 1-25m in February, 5-20m in April) with minimum June-September. [[Cyclopoida]] peaked April-June (1-25m depth). [[Calanoida]] showed maxima February-June at various stations. [[Keratella]], an indicator of eutrophic conditions, showed spring maximum (February-April, 0-30m) and fall maximum (September, 0-10m). The flagellate [[Ceratium]], capable of photosynthesis and associated with taste/odor problems, peaked in June (3-7m depth, photic zone limitation) with November minimum.

## Key Findings

**Primary Productivity and Eutrophication Status:**
Primary productivity (PPR) measurements using C-14 technique revealed [[Lake Mead]] exceeds oligotrophic levels at all times and locations. During winter months, the lake behaves as a natural eutrophic lake (25-75 g C/m²/growing season). In June, [[Gregg's Basin]] and [[Virgin Basin]] acted as natural eutrophic lakes, while [[Boulder Basin]] at all three locations fell into the polluted range (75-250 g C/m²/growing season). In September with high water temperatures, the entire system reached polluted range, with [[Boulder Basin]] showing most severe conditions. PPR consistently decreased from South Cove to Temple Bar across all five surveys, with Echo Bay showing comparable low values to Temple Bar. However, Miner's Cove showed increased PPR in each survey, and Bonelli Landing showed recovery and leveling. The highest PPR values occurred in [[Boulder Basin]], with maximum at Bureau of Reclamation Raft rather than Las Vegas Bay station.

**Boulder Basin Eutrophication Crisis:**
The study discovered unexpectedly severe eutrophication covering the whole of [[Boulder Basin]], attributed to accumulated effluent from [[Las Vegas Wash]]. This represents a more serious problem than the originally hypothesized limiting nutrient pattern. The basin acts as a nutrient trap, and without flushing, nutrients accumulate on the basin floor. During summer stratification, the mud surface becomes anaerobic, reducing ferric iron to ferrous iron and breaking down absorbing colloids, liberating nutrients from bottom mud reserves to supernatant water. Winter turnover allows all hypolimnetic nutrients released in summer to enrich surface waters, producing high PPR when temperature becomes optimal. The potential for severe eutrophication exists, with the current saving factor being species composition in the algae population. A species shift to problem organisms (as occurs in all eutrophic lakes) would make the high PPR rate very obvious.

**Water Chemistry Patterns:**
Specific conductance values place [[Lake Mead]] deep into eutrophic range throughout the year. pH ranged 7.8-8.4 with slight decrease across basins and lower values in hypolimnion during summer stratification. [[Las Vegas Wash]] station did not significantly affect pH levels in [[Boulder Basin]]. Phosphate levels, while in oligotrophic range (0.1-0.3 ppm), exceeded minimums for excessive algae growth (≥0.01 ppm), with [[Boulder Basin]] richer than [[Virgin Basin]]. Nitrate showed no consistent spatial trend, indicating [[Colorado River]] as the source of high levels; excessive nitrogen (>0.3 ppm) was available year-round. Trace metals (iron, manganese, zinc) showed no consistent trends; manganese was in optimal range, iron and zinc not limiting. Copper levels exceeded toxic threshold (0.05 ppm) many times but went into stress at certain times. Light penetration (1% compensation level) increased in warmer months, with Bonelli Landing and Temple Bar showing deepest penetration; [[Boulder Basin]] transparency was often as poor as South Cove despite sediment loss upstream.

**Seasonal and Spatial Patterns:**
The reservoir's warm monomictic cycle drives nutrient dynamics. Winter turnover (November, January, February) reaerated the system with homogeneous temperatures and close 5m-30m values for soluble salts. Summer stratification (September) created well-developed thermocline with highest soluble salt values at 30m in hypolimnion, approximately 100 ppm increase from South Cove to Bureau Raft. Dissolved oxygen reached stress levels (2 ppm) at thermocline depth due to high BOD from bacterial decomposition of sinking algae meeting the density barrier. Hypolimnetic DO levels in September fell considerably below recommended levels for cold-water fish regeneration. Bicarbonate values were 10-15 ppm less in summer than cool winter turnover values, showing excellent response to thermal stratification with higher values 30m beneath surface in September.

**Zooplankton Community Structure:**
Each basin appeared to act independently regarding zooplankton populations, with numbers differing significantly station-to-station. [[Daphnia]] distribution showed euryoecious pressure range (3-10m maximum), agreeing with Kuntze (1938) findings for Schleinsee. [[Bosmina]] showed prevernal maximum (February-April, 1-25m), consistent with Wesenberg-Lund (1904) and Kuntze (1938) work. [[Calanoida]] showed more even distribution throughout lake system with similar counts at different sites, suggesting they are not good water quality indicators; their February-June maxima in [[Lake Mead]] occurred later than boreal lake patterns (January-March). [[Keratella]] presence as eutrophic indicator confirmed enriched conditions. [[Ceratium]] reached maximum abundance in June (3-7m, photic zone), capable of rapid multiplication and surface blooms under favorable conditions; this flagellate is associated with taste and odor problems in water supplies.

**Nutrient Limitation Hypothesis:**
The original hypothesis of limiting nutrients across [[Lake Mead]] was not fully resolved. PPR from South Cove to Bonelli Landing behaved as predicted, with the drop potentially resulting from limiting element(s) since water transparency and temperature improved toward Bonelli Landing. The decrease in PPR despite improving physical conditions suggests nutrient limitation rather than light or temperature constraints. However, macro-chemical analysis showed excessive levels of major nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus, bicarbonate) throughout the system. Micro-elemental studies require expansion to identify potential limiting trace elements. The nutrient problem in [[Boulder Basin]] was characterized as making [[Lake Erie]]'s situation "appear trivial" by comparison.
