# Proasellus Vandeli n. sp.: A New Blind, Depigmented Hypogean Isopod from the French Basque Country

#crustacea #isopoda #proasellus #subterranean-biology

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Guy Magniez]] and [[Jean-Paul Henry]] describes a new blind and depigmented [[Asellota]] isopod, *[[Proasellus Vandeli]]* n. sp., from the French Basque Country. The species is named after Professor [[A. Vandel]]. The authors also re-evaluate the cave and spring Asellids of the western Pyrenees, separate them into three groups, and provide an updated checklist of the 15 French *[[Proasellus]]* species with a dichotomous key. They conclude that the Iberian-Aquitanian Proasellus fauna has a strong biogeographic affinity with Iberia and that the species occupies interstitial groundwater as well as caves.

## Background & Description

Before the twentieth century, only two Asellids were known from France: the eyed, cosmopolitan *[[Asellus Aquaticus]]* and the blind *Proasellus cavaticus*. Between 1905 and 1908, [[Jeannel]] found blind, depigmented Asellids in two Basque caves. [[Racovitza]] described them in 1922 as *Asellus spelaeus*, later *[[Proasellus Spelaeus]]*, and named a subspecies *aquae-calidae* from the Eaux-Chaudes cave. Later records from several other sites in the region prompted a detailed restudy.

**Morphology of *P. vandeli*:**
- Stocky, short and broad body. The elongation coefficient is about 3.5 in males, compared with about 4 in *P. spelaeus* and 5 in *[[Proasellus Albigensis]]*.
- Eyes and pigment absent. Yellow organs are conspicuous in living large individuals, and the gut contains yellowish silt or brownish plant debris.
- Antennules are short, reaching only two-thirds of the antennal peduncle. Males have three olfactory lamellae on the distal articles, females two.
- Male antennae have up to 45 articles, female antennae up to 40.
- Pereiopod IV retains a nuptial hook but is neither twisted nor shortened.
- Pleopod II endopodite has a vesicular structure with a large, nearly square tergal apophysis and a short, narrow neck set well back on it. The authors consider this a key character for species status.
- Pleopod V exopodite is extremely reduced in *P. vandeli*. In some adults from the [[Arbouet Underground Stream]], it is absent entirely, sometimes on one side only, with no sign of amputation.

**Morphology of *P. spelaeus*:**
- Males reach 5.8 mm and females 4.5 mm.
- Retained partial sexual dimorphism in pereiopod IV, less marked than in epigean relatives or in *[[Proasellus Chappuisi]]*.
- Pleopod V exopodite is as wide as the endopodite in the nominotypical subspecies, and much wider in *aquae-calidae*.
- The *aquae-calidae* subspecies has small rudimentary oostegites (under 0.1 mm) in pereiopods I to V of adult females in genital rest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors group the western Pyrenean Asellids into three sets:

- **a) *P. spelaeus spelaeus*:** [[Oxibar Cave]] and [[Campagnaga Leccia Cave]]. Individuals in both caves live in small pools fed by permanent seepage, not in large water bodies.
- **b) *P. spelaeus aquae-calidae*:** [[Eaux-Chaudes Cave]] and the [[Lescun Spring]]. In the Eaux-Chaudes cave, animals occur in pools fed by weak seepage, not in the underground river. [[Chodorowski]] (1962) also recorded them in a small temporary forest spring near the cave.
- **c) *P. vandeli* n. sp.:** [[Ainharp Underground Stream]], [[Lamina Chiloua Cave]] (Aussurucq) and [[Arbouet Underground Stream]]. The three populations are morphologically identical.

Key habitat observations:
- At Ainharp, Asellids occur in small lateral seepages and water-soaked surface fissures, not in the rock pools of the stream, which hold depigmented planarians.
- At Lamina Chiloua, a single male was found in a tiny seepage on a steeply inclined limestone slab.
- At Arbouet, Asellids are absent from open water, where [[Gammarus]], [[Niphargus]] and planarians live. They instead inhabit gravel masses penetrated by tree roots, through which the subterranean stream filters outward. Many individuals, including juveniles, were seen.

The authors conclude that cave stations are only singular, human-accessible points of a wider biotope. This includes interstitial and seepage waters upstream and downstream of the caves, within the karst massifs. They suggest that *P. vandeli* is a true interstitial species that enters caves wherever circulating groundwater passes through them. It probably also occupies alluvial valley aquifers, including those of the southern tributaries of the Gave d'Oloron.

**Biogeography:**
- *P. spelaeus* and *P. vandeli* occur near each other, but the authors judge the new species to have a wider range, which gives it greater biogeographic importance.
- The two Eaux-Chaudes and Lescun stations lie in parallel valleys (Gaves d'Ossau and d'Aspe) about 20 km apart. This supports the idea of a regional network of hypogean populations rather than isolated populations in single caves.
- The Iberian-Aquitanian Proasellus forms, including *P. lusitanicus*, *P. arthrodilus*, *P. cantabricus*, *P. chappuisi*, *P. spelaeus*, *P. albigensis* and the eyed *[[Proasellus Ibericus]]*, may form a western evolutionary branch of the genus. Possible links with North African forms (*P. hypogeus*, *P. gauthieri*, *P. thermonyctophilus*, *P. monsferratus*) are also suggested.
- The eyed *[[Proasellus Meridianus]]* occurs in epigean Basque waters. The Sare cave and Goueil di Her (Haute-Garonne) populations of *P. ibericus* are regarded as local variants of an epigean species common in northern Portugal.
- *[[Stenasellus Virei]]* occupies the same groundwater habitats in the Adour and Garonne basins. Both genera extend across the axial Pyrenees, suggesting the chain is not a biogeographic barrier.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic results**
- *Proasellus vandeli* n. sp. is described as a blind, depigmented species distinguished from *P. spelaeus* by pleopod II (endopodite neck structure), pleopod V (exopodite reduction or absence), and the male antennae, among other characters.
- *P. spelaeus* is split into two subspecies, *spelaeus* (Oxibar, Campagnaga Leccia) and *aquae-calidae* (Eaux-Chaudes, Lescun). The authors note that Racovitza would have done better to choose the Eaux-Chaudes form as type.
- The name *Asellus vandeli* Bresson (1955), a cave Asellid from Virginia, is a different taxon now placed in a Nearctic genus. The authors state that no confusion is possible.

**Station records**
- Seven Basque and Béarn stations are documented. Numbers are small: about a dozen adults at Ainharp, a single male at Lamina Chiloua, and several adults at Arbouet, with about 20 of the largest specimens collected there.
- Specimen sizes range from about 2.5 to 5.8 mm.

**Checklist of French *Proasellus***
The authors list 15 species. They are divided into eyed and blind forms:
- **Eyed (4):** *P. meridianus*, *P. coxalis coxalis*, *P. coxalis banyulensis*, *P. ibericus*.
- **Blind (11):** the *cavaticus* group (5 taxa), forms with Balkan affinity (*P. beroni*, *P. boui*), and forms with Iberian affinity (*P. spelaeus spelaeus*, *P. spelaeus aquae-calidae*, *P. albigensis*, *P. vandeli*).

**Identification key**
A four-step key separates the species. It uses the size of the pleopod II neck relative to the tergal apophysis, eye and pigment presence, body and uropod length relative to the pleotelson, and the length and width of the pleopod II and V exopodites.

**Conclusions**
- The homogeneity of French Iberian-Aquitanian *Proasellus* makes species determination difficult. The authors recommend using the key with the characters listed.
- The blind Proasellus fauna of France is arranged in provinces. The Aquitanian basin has species with clear Iberian affinities, the eastern regions are colonised by the *cavaticus* group, and the Mediterranean region and Corsica have forms with Balkan or obscuricolous affinities close to *P. coxalis*.
- The species is interstitial as well as cavernicolous, and its distribution is probably wider than the known caves suggest.
