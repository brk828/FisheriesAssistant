# Report on Luna Lake, Apache County, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Phytoplankton #Limnology #Eutrophication

## Executive Summary

This 1977 EPA National Eutrophication Survey (NES) working paper documents the trophic condition, nutrient loading, and biological/chemical characteristics of **Luna Lake**, a small reservoir in Apache County, Arizona, sampled during the 1975 open-water season. The lake is classified as **eutrophic**, exhibiting high chlorophyll *a* concentrations, elevated total phosphorus and orthophosphate levels (among the highest of 11 Arizona lakes surveyed in 1975), and recurrent algal blooms/floating algal mats. Algal bioassays identified **nitrogen**, not phosphorus, as the primary rate-limiting nutrient. Nonpoint sources — dominated by the San Francisco River inflow — contributed the majority (81.6%) of the annual phosphorus load, with the remainder from a single point source, the **Alpine Conservation Center** wastewater treatment plant. No fish species data are reported in this document; the study is limited to water chemistry, nutrient loading, and phytoplankton community composition.

## Background & Description

Luna Lake is a small, shallow impoundment in the headwaters of the **San Francisco River** drainage in east-central Arizona (Apache County), one of 11 Arizona lakes studied under the National Eutrophication Survey (NES), a program initiated in 1972 to assess nutrient sources, concentrations, and eutrophication impacts on U.S. freshwater lakes and reservoirs. The Survey was conducted with cooperation from the Arizona State Department of Health and the Arizona National Guard, which performed tributary sampling.

**Lake Morphometry:**
- Surface area: 0.30 km²
- Mean depth: 2.5 m
- Maximum depth: 6.3 m
- Volume: 0.742 × 10⁶ m³
- Mean hydraulic retention time: 107 days

**Hydrology:**
- Total drainage area: 93.2–93.5 km²
- Primary tributary: [[San Francisco River]] (A-2), drainage area 75.4 km², mean flow 0.10 m³/sec
- Minor tributaries/immediate drainage: 17.8 km², mean flow 0.01 m³/sec
- Outlet (A-1, San Francisco River): drainage area 93.5 km², mean flow 0.08 m³/sec
- Precipitation: 32.0 cm (sampling year); 28.8 cm (mean annual)

Sampling was conducted three times in 1975 (April 30, June 18, October 6) via helicopter, with physicochemical and biological samples collected at two lake stations across multiple depths.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Trophic Status:** Luna Lake is designated **eutrophic** — nutrient-rich and highly productive. Field observations recorded floating algal mats in April and algal blooms in June and September 1975.

**Water Chemistry (surface to max depth, 1975 sampling):**
- Chlorophyll *a*: range 1.7–4.2 µg/L, mean 3.4 µg/L
- Median total phosphorus: 0.182 mg/L (highest of 11 AZ lakes sampled in 1975)
- Median orthophosphorus: 0.131 mg/L (highest of 11 AZ lakes sampled)
- Median inorganic nitrogen: 0.050 mg/L (7 of 11 lakes ranked higher)
- Temperature, dissolved oxygen, conductivity, pH, alkalinity, and Secchi depth were recorded across three sampling dates (see Appendix C data); Secchi depth ranged approximately 1.2–4.0 m.

**Nutrient Limitation:**
Algal assay bioassays using *Selenastrum capricornutum* as the test organism demonstrated that **nitrogen** was the primary limiting nutrient in Luna Lake during both assay dates (04/30/75 and 10/06/75). Phosphorus-only spikes did not significantly stimulate growth beyond control yields, whereas nitrogen additions (alone or combined with phosphorus) produced significant growth increases. Lake inorganic N/orthophosphate (N/P) ratios were consistently <1 across all three sampling dates, well below the ~14:1 threshold that typically indicates phosphorus limitation — reinforcing the conclusion of nitrogen-limited primary production.

**Vollenweider Loading Comparison:**
- Estimated Luna Lake phosphorus loading: **1.90 g P/m²/yr**
- Vollenweider (1975) "eutrophic" threshold: 0.58 g P/m²/yr
- Vollenweider (1975) "oligotrophic" threshold: 0.29 g P/m²/yr

Luna Lake's phosphorus loading is **more than 3× the eutrophic threshold**, indicating that even complete elimination of the single known point source (Alpine Conservation Center) would not bring nutrient loading below eutrophic levels, since nonpoint sources alone (81.6% of load) exceed the threshold.

**Point/Nonpoint Source Details:**
- Known municipal waste source: [[Alpine Conservation Center]] (population served: 112; activated sludge treatment; mean flow 0.042 × 10³ m³/day), discharging to an unnamed creek/San Francisco River.
- No known industrial sources or septic tank contributions identified.
- Mean nonpoint nutrient export from San Francisco River subdrainage: 5 kg P/km²/yr; 42 kg N/km²/yr.

## Key Findings

1. **Trophic classification:** Luna Lake is eutrophic based on chlorophyll *a*, total phosphorus, orthophosphate, and algal bloom observations.
2. **Limiting nutrient:** Nitrogen, not phosphorus, limits primary productivity in Luna Lake, based on both algal bioassay results and lake N/P ratios (<1 on all sampling dates).
3. **Phosphorus loading:** Present loading (1.90 g P/m²/yr) is >3× Vollenweider's eutrophic threshold (0.58 g P/m²/yr); nonpoint sources (81.6%) dominate over the single point source (18.4%, entirely from the Alpine Conservation Center).
4. **Phytoplankton community composition** shifted seasonally:
   - **April 1975:** Dominated by *[[Cryptomonas]]* (2,990 units/mL) and *Chroomonas* (1,627 units/mL); total algal units 5,189/mL.
   - **June 1975:** Dominated by *[[Oscillatoria]]* (420 units/mL), *[[Anabaena]]* (262 units/mL), *Fragilaria* and *[[Aphanizomenon]]* (105 units/mL each); total 1,102 units/mL — coincides with reported algal bloom.
   - **October 1975:** Dominated by *Cryptomonas*, *Oscillatoria*, and *Nitzschia* (34 units/mL each); total 102 units/mL.
5. **Chlorophyll *a* values** ranged 1.7–4.2 µg/L across stations and dates, with the highest single value (4.2 µg/L) recorded in April 1975 at Station 01.
6. **Regional ranking:** Among 11 Arizona lakes sampled by NES in 1975, Luna Lake ranked highest in median total phosphorus and median dissolved orthophosphate, and mid-to-upper range in chlorophyll *a* and inorganic nitrogen — underscoring its status as one of the most nutrient-enriched lakes in the Arizona study set (comparison lakes include [[Big Lake]], [[Lake Havasu]], [[Lyman Lake]], [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Pleasant]], [[Lake Powell]], [[Rainbow Lake]], [[Theodore Roosevelt Lake]], [[San Carlos Reservoir]], and [[Lake Mead]]).
7. **No fish population or species data** are presented in this report; the study scope is restricted to water chemistry, nutrient budgets, and phytoplankton/chlorophyll monitoring under the National Eutrophication Survey framework.
