# Sexual Dimorphism in Tilapia aurea and Tilapia zillii from Dor and Lake Tiberias

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This short paper by J. Chervinski of the [[Dor Fish Culture Research Station]] compares body proportions of males and females of two [[Tilapia Aurea]] and [[Tilapia Zillii]] populations: fish from commercial ponds at Dor and wild fish from [[Lake Tiberias]]. Sex-related differences in body proportions were found in both species in Dor fish. In Lake Tiberias fish, the author reports no comparable differences for T. zillii and attributes the contrast mainly to environmental conditions, especially growth rate, rather than to genetic differences. The study is a brief morphometric comparison, not a full ecological survey.

## Background & Description

- **Sexual dimorphism in tilapiines:** In many [[Tilapia]] species, breeding colours develop mainly in males. Females are usually smaller, and males and females can differ in body shape even within the same spawning group.
- **Prior work cited:**
  - Whitehead (1962) found an enlarged upper jaw in males of *T. nigra* over 300 mm total length, absent in females. Lowe (cited without a reliable year in the scan) reported the same trait in *T. girigan*, *T. pangani* and *T. jipe*.
  - Chervinski (1965) reported differences in anal and dorsal fin length between sexes in *T. aurea* and *T. galilaea*, and in body proportions in *T. vulcani* (a subspecies of [[Tilapia Nilotica]]) from [[Lake Rudolf]] and in *T. aurea exul* from [[Ein Feshkha]] springs.
- **Morphometric characters measured:** Standard length (S.L.), body depth (D), head length (H), snout length (S), eye diameter (E), pectoral fin length (P), and lower jaw length (L.J.).

**Materials and methods:** *T. aurea* and *T. zillii* from Lake Tiberias were taken from ring-net catches. Fish of both species from the commercial ponds at Dor were also examined. Body proportions were compared between males and females of each population using t-tests.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Growth and age:** The author states that growth of both species is slower in Lake Tiberias than in the ponds, so fish reach a given size later. Dor *T. aurea* of 184–224 mm S.L. were in their first year of life. Lake Tiberias fish of the same size were already in their second year.
- **Maturity and sampling:** Both Dor and Lake Tiberias *T. aurea* were mature. Lake Tiberias fish were taken during their spawning period. Because of this, the author suggests the lake–pond difference could partly reflect the stage of spawning, not only sex.
- **Explanation offered:** The differences between Dor and Lake Tiberias fish are attributed to the interaction between genes and environment (pond versus natural lake conditions).

## Key Findings

1. **Dor fish showed sexual differences in body proportions.** Both *T. aurea* and *T. zillii* from Dor differed between males and females in several characters. The author reports these as significant in the t-tests.
2. **Lake Tiberias *T. zillii* showed no significant male–female differences.** Table 2 reports no significant sex differences for the Lake Tiberias group.
3. **Table 1 (*T. aurea*, Dor vs. Lake Tiberias) — sample and mean values:**
   - Dor males vs. females, body depth (D): mean 41.29 (n = 36) vs. 36.78 (n = 36).
   - Dor males vs. females, lower jaw (L.T.): mean 40.53 vs. 39.62.
   - Lake Tiberias males vs. females, body depth: mean 34.37 vs. 32.85.
   - The OCR-scanned table is hard to read, and some S.D., S.E. and t values are garbled. Recheck the original before quoting them.
4. **Table 2 (*T. zillii*, Dor vs. Lake Tiberias):**
   - Dor males (n = 25) vs. females (n = 18), a character with means of 44.26 vs. 42.19 (range 39.1–47.4 and 38.0–46.4).
   - Lake Tiberias males (n = 23–24) vs. females (n = 30) show similar means and no significant differences.
   - The t-test thresholds used are: 0.05 = 2.021, 0.01 = 2.704, 0.001 = 3.551 (n = 40 level as printed).
5. **Overall conclusion:** The author concludes that sexual differences in body proportions appear in *T. aurea* and *T. zillii* only in the Dor pond population. The Lake Tiberias fish show no comparable pattern in the body proportions the author emphasises.
6. **Note on Table 1:** Lake Tiberias *T. aurea* also show some male–female mean differences in Table 1. This partly conflicts with the Discussion, which says the differences appear only in Dor fish. The conflict should be checked against the original table.
7. **Interpretation:** The author suggests the pattern reflects environmental conditions, especially growth rate and maturity stage, rather than fixed genetic differences. The author recommends further work to separate these causes.

**Literature cited:** Six references in total. The most relevant are Whitehead (1962) on jaw enlargement in *T. nigra*, Chervinski (1965) on sexual dimorphism in *Tilapia* from Nature, and Ben-Tuvia (1960) on the cichlid fishes of Lake Tiberias and Hula, which covers the lake's fish fauna.
