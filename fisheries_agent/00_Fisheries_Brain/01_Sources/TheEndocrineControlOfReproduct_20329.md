# Endocrine Control of Reproductive Behaviour in the Female Guppy, with a Note on Hatching Synchronization in Quail

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This source is a scanned excerpt from the journal *Animal Behaviour* (1968, vol. 16). Its main part is N. R. Liley's experimental study of how [[Gonadectomy]] and [[Hypophysectomy]] affect the sexual behaviour of female [[Guppy]] (*[[Poecilia Reticulata]]*). The study asks whether the gonads and pituitary regulate female [[Receptivity]]. The central conclusion is that the ovary is not essential for complete sexual behaviour, but it shapes it, and the [[Pituitary Gland]] appears to be directly involved. Removing the pituitary causes an immediate and complete loss of receptivity.

A shorter second paper by Margaret A. Vince addresses [[Hatching Synchronization]] in [[Japanese Quail]] and [[Bobwhite Quail]]. Only its introduction and methods survive in the scan, so no results are reported here.

## Background & Description

**Guppy reproductive behaviour.** The guppy was chosen because it is hardy, easy to breed in the laboratory, and has a short, well-defined brood cycle. Receptive non-virgin females respond to the male's [[Sigmoid Display]] with a [[Glide Response]], an [[Arch and Wheel]] sequence, and, rarely, [[Copulation]]. Copulation requires active cooperation by the female. In non-virgin females, receptivity is highest in the 3 to 5 days after parturition. Virgin females stay receptive for longer and become less receptive as embryonic development begins.

**Prior work.** The author notes that few studies had examined hormonal control of female fish behaviour. Earlier work showed that testosterone and progestin treatment can induce male behaviour in genetically female poeciliids, and that [[Gonadectomy]] of male [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]] reduces some courtship movements. Ball (1960) reported that gonadectomized *Mollienesia* females were courted as readily as intact females.

**Methods.** Fish were held at 26 ± 1 °C under 12 h light per day and fed brine shrimp and dried food. Observations used 15-minute single-pair tests in 94 l tanks split by an opaque partition. Males were rejected if they gave fewer than one sigmoid display in the first 5 minutes or fewer than five displays in total. Female receptivity was expressed as the percentage of male sigmoid displays that elicited a female response. Male guppies were de-gonopodized, which removed the copulation problem and allowed repeated testing. Surgical survival was roughly half for gonadectomy and roughly two-fifths for hypophysectomy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study is entirely laboratory-based. Guppies came from commercial dealers in Vancouver, and the experiments used a range of selected strains. The source gives no field habitat, distribution, or population data. Environmental conditions were controlled: a constant temperature, a fixed light cycle, and fresh water with a 0.58 % Young's teleost saline used during and after surgery to reduce osmotic stress.

For the quail study, eggs were incubated at approximately 39 °C in a "Curfew" observation incubator. The biological context is the synchronization of hatching in nidifugous birds, whose young must leave the nest soon after hatching. Synchronization is thought to be achieved through embryo-to-embryo stimulation, including acoustic "clicking."

## Key Findings

**Experiment 1: Long-term effects of gonadectomy (non-virgin females)**
- Twelve non-virgin females were tested at 5, 10, 15 and 20 weeks after total gonadectomy, with three consecutive test days per session.
- Gonadectomy did not eliminate sexual behaviour. Eleven of twelve females showed some receptive behaviour, and several performed arch and wheel. Two females (7 and 8) showed few or no responses.
- Ten of twelve females scored higher at 10 weeks than at 5 weeks. The author interprets this as partial recovery of receptivity after an initial decline.
- Full responses (glide-arch-wheel) occurred in 10 of 144 tests (about 7 %). Pituitaries of all sacrificed females showed enlargement and degranulation of the mesoadenohypophysis. No ovarian regeneration was found.

**Experiment 2: Short-term effects of sham, partial and total gonadectomy (non-virgin females)**
- Groups: control (A, n = 10), partial gonadectomy at day 19 (B, n = 10), partial gonadectomy at day 11 (C, n = 8), total gonadectomy at day 19 (D, n = 9). The gestation cycle was 24 to 27 days.
- Group B (embryos removed at day 19) did not differ significantly from controls on any day (Mann-Whitney U test).
- Group C (embryos removed at day 11) showed low responsiveness. Only five females glided and three arched and wheeled, and no responses occurred before day 22.
- Group D (total gonadectomy) showed very few responses. Three females glided, and none arched or wheeled.
- The author concludes that partial removal of embryos at day 19 simulates normal parturition.

**Experiment 3: Partial and total gonadectomy (virgin females)**
- Groups: sham (E, n = 8), partial (F, n = 7), total (G, n = 14).
- Control and partial groups showed high levels of receptivity, with no significant difference between them.
- Total gonadectomy caused a marked decline. Eleven of fourteen females responded on days 1, 3 and 5, but by day 11 only four glided, and only one of those arched and wheeled.
- Total-gonadectomy females differed significantly from controls on day 3 (P < 0.05) and on days 7, 9 and 11 (P < 0.02).

**Experiment 4: Hypophysectomy (virgin females)**
- Eighteen of 44 hypophysectomized females and eight of nine sham controls survived.
- Histology classified the experimental fish as confirmed (n = 7), doubtful (n = 2) or incomplete (n = 5). Two further females had brain damage and were excluded, and two were rejected because histological preparations were lost.
- None of the confirmed group responded to males. Two incomplete-group females showed receptive behaviour.
- The author concludes that hypophysectomy causes an immediate and complete decline in receptivity, and that the effect is unlikely to be a surgical shock artefact.

**Interpretive findings**
- The corpora lutea are not directly correlated with receptivity. Few females in Experiments 2 and 3 carried recognizable corpora lutea.
- Receptive periods coincide with vitellogenesis, which the author links to pituitary gonadotrophin.
- The author hypothesizes that gonadotrophin acts directly on the central nervous system to increase receptivity, and that ovarian feedback modulates this pathway.

**Vince (Hatching Synchronization, Quail)**
- Ten bobwhite and ten Japanese quail clutches were incubated in contact. Groups of three or four eggs were chosen from each clutch and compared with isolated siblings.
- The aim was to test whether synchronization depends only on accelerating retarded embryos, or whether advanced embryos also slow hatching in others. The results are not included in the surviving text.
