# Growth and survivorship of Moapa dace (Osteichthyes: Cyprinidae: Moapa coriacea) in an isolated stream reach on Moapa National Wildlife Refuge

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This study documents a multi-year (1984–1987) mark-recapture investigation of growth, reproductive timing, and survivorship in the [[Moapa Dace]] (*Moapa coriacea*), a monotypic, federally endangered cyprinid endemic to the Warm Springs area of the Muddy River, Nevada. Researchers used an isolated headwater fork of a controlled stream channel at [[Moapa National Wildlife Refuge]] to track a founding cohort ("P1 generation") of 125 larvae stocked in March 1984 after piscicide treatment eliminated all other fish, including the invasive [[Shortfin Molly]] (*Poecilia mexicana*) implicated in the species' decline. The study provides foundational demographic data (growth curves, age/size at maturity, survivorship rates) critical for the conservation management of this narrowly distributed species, which naturally occupies only the upper 4 km of the 40-km Muddy River.

## Background & Description
- **Taxonomic status**: Monotypic genus; *Moapa coriacea*, family Cyprinidae.
- **Larval size at stocking**: 7–10 mm fork length (FL), judged 1–7 days old.
- **Growth trajectory**:
  - Year 1 (March 1984–March 1985): rapid growth, mean length increase of 35.9 mm.
  - Year 2 (June 1985–May 1986): markedly slower growth, mean increase of only 3.2 mm.
- **Size at first reproduction**: Adults measured in mid-March 1985 (near time of first reproduction) ranged from 41–54 mm FL, mean 47 mm.
- **Size at sexual maturity (females)**: ~44 mm FL was the smallest reproductive-sized individual observed; three of ten fish reached 40–42 mm FL by 6 months of age.
- **Sexual dimorphism**: External morphology could not be used to differentiate sex, but dissected specimens indicated females were generally larger than males (consistent with [[Hubbs and Miller 1948]]).
- **Progeny size**: First-observed offspring (2 April 1985) measured 8–10 mm total length; by June 1985 progeny ranged ~7–20 mm FL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Endemic range**: Restricted to the upper 4 km of the 40-km Muddy River, an area known as the [[Warm Springs Area]], fed by ~20 thermal springs with cumulative discharge of 1.1 m³/s.
- **Study site**: [[Moapa National Wildlife Refuge]] (established 1979) encompasses five small springs (70-m radius, cumulative discharge 0.09 m³/s). The isolated study reach was a headwater fork: 2 m average width, 0.3 m average depth, 85 m channel length, 108 m² surface area, ~60% shaded by fan palms (*Washingtonia filifera*); substrate predominantly silt/sandy silt with fan palm root structure.
- **Water chemistry**:
  - Temperature range: 30.0–31.2°C (study reach); juveniles in Warm Springs area generally experience 30–32°C.
  - Dissolved oxygen: 4.38–6.02 mg/L (study reach), generally low in the warm spring habitat.
  - Adults occupy a broader thermal range (26–32°C) than juveniles, extending further downstream from spring sources.
- **Threats**: Population decline attributed partly to introduction of the nonnative [[Shortfin Molly]] (*Poecilia mexicana*) into the Muddy River system in the early 1960s. Historical habitat degradation also linked to chlorination and flow manipulation of the Warm Springs area (former swimming resort).
- **Life history niche**: Juveniles restricted to warmer, low-oxygen spring-fed tributaries; adults more widely distributed with access to slightly cooler, more oxygenated waters.
- **Density-independence**: No evidence of density-dependent downstream emigration was detected among the P1 cohort; population counts (via snorkel surveys, 1985–1989) ranged from 31–52 individuals (mean 41) while drop trap was in place, and 37–62 (mean 51) after trap removal — suggesting the enclosed reach did not create artificially elevated densities.

## Key Findings
- **Reproductive timing**: The P1 generation reached sexual maturity and reproduced exactly ~1 year after stocking (first progeny observed 14 days after the 1-year anniversary of planting), coinciding with the deceleration of growth rate.
- **Survivorship**:
  - Year 1: 32% survival (40 of 125 initial larvae persisted to 14 months).
  - Year 2: 60% survival estimated using mark-recapture proportions (24 of 43 estimated P1 survivors from an adult population of 52, using a branding technique where 17 of 36 minnow-trapped fish showed detectable freeze brands, yielding a 47% brand-detection rate).
- **Growth-rate variability**: Individual growth rates varied substantially; this variability may relate to unconfirmed sexual dimorphism, as females are believed to grow larger than males.
- **Experimental controls**: The absence of older conspecifics and other fish species in the isolated study reach likely reduced interspecific/intraspecific competition, meaning growth and survivorship figures may not fully generalize to natural, multi-species/multi-age assemblages.
- **Conservation value**: This study represents rare baseline demographic data (growth, age/size at first reproduction, survivorship) for the endangered Moapa dace, intended to inform ongoing management and recovery efforts across its historic range.
- **Methodology innovation**: Use of freeze-branding to distinguish the original P1 cohort from co-occurring offspring allowed for accurate longitudinal tracking of growth and survival despite continuous in-stream reproduction.
