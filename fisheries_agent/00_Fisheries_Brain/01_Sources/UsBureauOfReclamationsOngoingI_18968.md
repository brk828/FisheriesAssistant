# Central Arizona Project: 1994 Notice of Intent to Sue under the Endangered Species Act

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a July 30, 1994 notice letter from the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] to the U.S. Secretary of the Interior, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR) Commissioner and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) Director. It alleges that the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP), a water-transfer aqueduct linking the [[Colorado River]] to the [[Gila River Basin]], is illegally jeopardizing at least seven (and possibly twelve) federally protected species and destroying Critical Habitat. The letter challenges the April 15, 1994 FWS [[Biological Opinion]] (BO) on CAP and its [[Reasonable and Prudent Alternative]] (RPA), argues that BR failed to consult on dewatering of the Colorado River, and gives 60 days' notice of intent to sue under Sections 2, 7 and 9 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The letter is an advocacy document from a conservation group; its claims are allegations, not findings of a court or agency.

## Background & Description

The letter is addressed to Secretary Bruce Babbitt, Commissioner Dan Beard and Director Mollie Beattie, and is signed by Robin D. Silver, M.D., Director of the Southwest Center. A copy goes to Mark Hughes, Esq., of Earthlaw.

CAP is described as the final and "open" link between the Colorado, Salt and Verde, and Gila River basins, connecting three of the four North American deserts (Mohave, Sonoran and Chihuahuan). The letter describes it as a potential conduit for terrestrial and aquatic species. Its key infrastructure and cost figures, as cited in the letter, include:

- 336 miles of aqueduct and a pumping system drawing 547,000 kilowatts from the Navajo Power Plant.
- Pumping of more than two million acre-feet per year, about 2,000 feet uphill and 300 miles across mountain ranges.
- Original late-1960s cost estimate of $932 million, rising to $3.5 billion (1983) and an unpaid bill of $4.7 billion as of December 10, 1993, per the [[U.S. General Accounting Office]] (GAO).
- Water first delivered to the Harquahala Valley Irrigation District after the aqueduct opened in 1985.

The letter places CAP in a longer history of water policy, citing [[John Wesley Powell]]'s warnings about the arid West's limits, the [[Colorado River Compact]] apportionments, and the [[California Guarantee]] of 4.4 million acre-feet.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Listed and at-risk species.** The letter identifies a group of species it considers jeopardized by CAP:

- FWS-acknowledged jeopardy from the transfer of exotics into the Gila Basin: [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The entire Gila River fish fauna is described as listed, candidate or proposed.
- Species the letter says desert experts believe may also be jeopardized: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Colorado River Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]], Apache trout and Gila trout.
- Species at risk from Colorado River dewatering: totoaba, bonytail, Colorado squawfish, California brown pelican, Yuma clapper rail and vaquita.

The letter states that 12 native species face extinction risk from BR's actions and that five more could be hastened toward extinction. It also notes that many plants, fish, mammals, reptiles and amphibians not yet federally protected would be affected, and cites a 1984 bird report listing five species of concern.

**Exotic species.** Exotic fish said to be transported by CAP and contributing to native declines include blue tilapia, channel catfish, mosquitofish, rainbow trout, red shiner, largemouth bass, striped bass and grass carp. The FWS BO is quoted on other risks, including the Asian clam, crayfish, Asian tapeworm (linked to woundfin declines in the Virgin River), zebra mussel and giant ramshorn snail. The letter cites [[Grass Carp]] found above a fish barrier on the Salt River Project's Arizona Canal as evidence that [[Electric Fish Barriers]] fail.

**Hydrology and habitat.** Increased discharges through Lake Mohave, Topcock Gorge and Lake Havasu are said to alter flow dynamics important to the bonytail and razorback sucker, and were never examined. A 1990s Lake Havasu beach closure due to sewage contamination is cited as evidence of system fragility.

**International dimension.** The letter notes that Mexico established the Upper Gulf of California and Colorado River Delta Biosphere Reserve to protect totoaba and vaquita fisheries. It states that Colorado River water is needed in the delta and upper Gulf as a nutrient carrier and dilution source, and that the upper Gulf is becoming hypersaline due to high evaporation and cessation of inflow.

## Key Findings

**Core allegations (per the letter):**

1. The April 15, 1994 CAP [[Biological Opinion]] is biologically and legally inadequate. The letter says the RPA does not remove jeopardy to at least seven species and does not stop destruction of Critical Habitat.
2. FWS allegedly violated the law by:
   - Failing to give "the benefit of the doubt" to listed species (citing H.R. Conf. Rep. No. 697 and 50 CFR 402.14).
   - Basing decisions on information other than the "best scientific and commercial data available" (ESA Section 7(a)(2)).
   - Preordaining uninterrupted CAP operation, foreclosing a "No Action" alternative (50 CFR 402.14(h)(3)).
3. BR allegedly failed to consult with the Secretary on the effects of increasing Colorado River dewatering on listed species and proposed Critical Habitat (ESA Section 7).
4. CAP allegedly has introduced exotic species into the Gila Basin since 1985, through the Harquahala Valley connection and roughly twenty other canal, reservoir and river connections.

**Deficiencies the letter identifies in the BO** (11 numbered points), including:

- No prevention of contaminant entry through the CAP intake pumps at Lake Havasu.
- No reduction of exotic biomass within the aqueduct and canals.
- No control of contamination through CAP turnouts.
- Drop dam complexes that fragment streams and block native fish migration.
- No ban on live bait fishing or agency introductions of exotics.
- No assessment of alternatives to CAP, including for Tucson.

**Required BO elements, as summarized in the letter:**

- Two future drop dam complexes.
- Continued operation of electric fish barriers.
- Continued monitoring.
- Transfer of $12.5 million from BR to FWS over 25 years ($500,000 per year) for recovery and protection programs.
- A future educational program on bait bucket transfers.

The letter calls the cash transfer "nothing more than a bribe" and says it does not remove jeopardy.

**Safeguards the letter proposes:**

1. Prevent exotics from entering the system (mechanical barriers and sterilization at the intake).
2. Keep exotics within the system at the lowest possible level (ozonation, ultrasonic, irradiation, or chemical sterilization stations).
3. Prevent exotics from leaving the system (barriers and sterilization at all turnouts, plus modification of [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]]).
4. Prevent further introductions (barriers on the Hassayampa, Agua Fria, Gila and San Pedro rivers, plus stronger monitoring and [[Section 7 Consultation]] for any new introduction).

**Options the letter says BR faces:**

1. Redesign CAP to prevent extinction and comply with law.
2. Seek an exemption from the [[Endangered Species Committee]] (the "God Committee"), which the letter says is unlikely to be granted.
3. Abandon CAP.

The letter argues that technical and economic infeasibility claims for safeguards are not valid, given the project's cost and historical overruns.

**Water-supply and allocation claims:**

- The 17.5 million acre-feet (maf) allocation ignores evaporative losses, which the USGS estimates at 1.3 maf in the Lower Basin.
- Using the Colorado River's mean flow of 13.5 maf, the letter calculates only 0.8 maf remains for Arizona, Nevada and federally reserved rights after the California Guarantee.
- Groundwater and Tucson supply analyses are cited to argue that CAP water was never necessary.

**Notice of intent:** The letter says the agencies have 60 days to remedy alleged violations of ESA Sections 2, 7 and 9, after which the Southwest Center intends to sue in U.S. District Court.
