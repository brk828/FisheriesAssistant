# Preliminary Guide to the Identification of Larval Fishes of the Tennessee Valley

#ichthyology #marine-biology #larval-fish #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) Technical Note (No. 819, December 1976) by [[Jacob Hogue]], [[Robert Wallus]] and [[Larry K. Kay]] is a preliminary illustrated guide for identifying fish larvae (prolarvae and postlarvae) collected in the [[Tennessee River]] reservoir system. It provides a dichotomous key to 14 Southeastern families, family-level and taxon-level keys, descriptive accounts with myomere counts and pigmentation patterns, glossary of morphological terms, plates of representative specimens, and a bibliography of 66 references. The guide was motivated by entrainment concerns at steam-electric power plants and by environmental legislation, and was built on the TVA larval fish monitoring program that began in 1974 at the [[Browns Ferry Nuclear Plant]].

## Background & Description

- **Origin of the program:** Conceptual development began in 1970; field sampling and larval identification began in 1974 as preoperational monitoring for the Browns Ferry Nuclear Steam Plant on Wheeler Reservoir, Alabama. By 1975–1976, 26 localities associated with existing, planned or potential steam-electric plants and other TVA projects had been sampled.
- **Motivation:** The authors note that early ecology of young fish, especially larvae, was largely unknown and represented perhaps the largest gap in understanding freshwater population biology. Relevant legislation included the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (1969) and the Federal Water Pollution Control Act Amendments (1972). Concern centered on [[Entrainment]] of larvae in power plant condenser cooling systems.
- **Scope:** Keys cover 14 families indigenous to the Southeast. Four families (Amiidae, Aphredoderidae, Cottidae, Esocidae) are discussed but excluded from the family key. Nomenclature follows American Fisheries Society Special Publication No. 6 (1970).
- **Methods:** Morphometric and meristic characters derive mainly from Tennessee River specimens, supplemented by reared developmental series and literature. The authors recommend [[Polarized Stereomicroscopy]], which greatly facilitated routine identification.
- **Terminology:** A glossary defines key terms, including prolarva (larva with yolk), postlarva (yolk absorbed, adult fin-ray complement not attained), larva (hatching to adult pelvic fin-ray complement), and myomere-based measures such as preanal and postanal myomere counts.
- **Status:** The authors describe the guide as preliminary and evolving, with keys expected to be revised as geographic and temporal variation is documented. They invite specimen verification and criticism.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Taxonomic challenge:** Larval fish taxonomy is described as dynamic because many taxa remain undescribed and ontogeny changes rapidly. Existing literature was meager and largely inapplicable to the warm-water fauna of southeastern reservoirs.
- **Identification aids:** Adult distribution, abundance and reproductive data from the collection area can help infer which larval species are present.
- **Limitations:** Because specimens derive mainly from the Tennessee River system, specimens from other drainages may key incorrectly.
- **Ecological notes on species:** Several taxa are restricted to the Tennessee system in terms of collection records, and some families (e.g., Cyprinodontidae) contain only three species likely to appear in TVA collections. Adult ecology is not otherwise quantified in the document.
- **Diversity of collections:** Clupeidae was the most abundant family in TVA collections, while Amiidae (bowfin) was not collected at all.

## Key Findings

**Overall guide structure**
- The family key separates 14 families using characters such as snout and mouth form, barbels, adhesive organs, myomere counts, yolk sac and oil globule position, fin placement and caudal fin shape.
- Each family section provides, where possible, a taxon key, descriptions, and plates. The document contains roughly 60 plates in total.

**Family and taxon highlights**
- **[[Paddlefish]] (Polyodontidae):** One species, *Polyodon spathula*. Prolarvae have 31–35 preanal and 20–22 postanal myomeres and range from 7.5–12 mm TL. Postlarvae are identified by small eyes, barbels and heterocercal tail.
- **[[Gars]] (Lepisosteidae):** One genus, *Lepisosteus*, represented by a few postlarvae. Adhesive organ present; two groups recognized, tentatively matched to Longnose Gar and Spotted or Shortnose Gar based on snout shape. Identification to species is considered impossible until more specimens are available.
- **[[Bowfin]] (Amiidae):** One species, *Amia calva*. No specimens were taken in TVA collections.
- **[[Herrings]] (Clupeidae):** Most abundant family in TVA collections. Prolarvae identified to genus and sometimes species; postlarvae separable only after anal ray anlages form (about 18–20 mm TL). Key separates [[Skipjack Herring]] (*Alosa chrysochloris*, 16–19 anal rays), [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*, 20–26 anal rays) and [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*, 29–34 anal rays); 27–28 rays are classed as *Dorosoma* sp.
- **[[Mooneyes]] (Hiodontidae):** Blunt snout, large eyes, 29–35 preanal myomeres. Two species, *Hiodon tergisus* and *Hiodon alosoides* (goldeye), have been identified from TVA collections. Separated from clupeids by postanal myomere count and dorsal fin placement.
- **[[Pikes]] (Esocidae):** One genus, *Esox*, with 32–35 preanal and 11–17 postanal myomeres. Newly hatched larvae (5–8 mm TL) are heavily pigmented; snout begins elongating by 10 mm TL.
- **[[Minnows and Carps]] (Cyprinidae):** The largest family treated, with several groups keyed by myomere counts, eye shape, fin size, and pigmentation. Taxa covered include Stoneroller (*Campostoma anomalum*), Rosyside Dace (*Clinostomus funduloides*), Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), Golden Shiner (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*), Emerald Shiner (*Notropis atherinoides*), Spotfin Shiner (*Notropis spilopterus*) and Fathead Minnow (*Pimephales promelas*), along with unidentified groups A–C.
- **[[Suckers]] (Catostomidae):** Only *Minytrema melanops* (Spotted Sucker) can be identified with confidence. Unidentified catostomids are grouped A–D by preanal myomere counts and pigmentation.
- **[[Catfishes]] (Ictaluridae):** No true postlarval stage; early juveniles are called alevins. Key uses anal ray counts and caudal fin shape to separate Channel Catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*, 24–29 anal rays), Blue Catfish (*Ictalurus furcatus*, 30–36), Flathead Catfish (*Pylodictis olivaris*, fewer than 17), and bullheads (*Ictalurus* spp., 17–27).
- **[[Pirate Perch]] (Aphredoderidae):** Only three postlarval specimens (*Aphredoderus sayanus*) were collected.
- **[[Killifishes]] (Cyprinodontidae):** One genus, *Fundulus*; 11 preanal and 22–24 postanal myomeres.
- **[[Mosquitofish]] (Poeciliidae):** One species, *Gambusia affinis*, with 9–10 preanal and 22 postanal myomeres.
- **[[Brook Silverside]] (Atherinidae):** One species, *Labidesthes sicculus*. Prolarvae have 6–7 preanal and 30–31 postanal myomeres.
- **[[Temperate Basses]] (Percichthyidae):** *Morone saxatilis* (Striped Bass), *M. chrysops* (White Bass) and *M. mississippiensis* (Yellow Bass). Prolarvae have 12–15 preanal and 8–12 postanal myomeres, with an anterior oil globule as the key character. White and yellow bass prolarvae cannot be separated.
- **[[Sunfishes]] (Centrarchidae):** Keyed to genera (*Lepomis*, *Micropterus*, *Ambloplites*, *Pomoxis*). Lepomis larvae have 11–13 preanal and 15–17 postanal myomeres; *Micropterus* species, with the exception of *M. dolomieu*, are not separable as larvae. Pomoxis has 18–23 postanal myomeres; *P. annularis* and *P. nigromaculatus* cannot be confidently separated.
- **[[Perches]] (Percidae):** Keyed to yellow perch (*Perca flavescens*), pikeperches (*Stizostedion* spp., which cannot be separated between sauger and walleye), and darter groups A and B. Group A is tentatively associated with *Etheostoma*; Group B with *Percina caprodes* (logperch).
- **[[Freshwater Drum]] (Sciaenidae):** *Aplodinotus grunniens* is the only freshwater sciaenid and is abundant in collections. Prolarvae have a large posteriorly placed oil globule.
- **[[Sculpins]] (Cottidae):** Few specimens from TVA collections. Descriptions derive from *Cottus carolinae* (Banded Sculpin) reared from eggs collected in January 1975 from a Tennessee River tributary in Lauderdale County, Alabama, with 13–15 preanal and 15–18 postanal myomeres.

**Practical conclusions**
- Identification to species is not feasible for many groups (e.g., Lepisosteus, Stizostedion, Pomoxis, most darters), and the authors emphasize that this is a preliminary tool.
- Polarized stereomicroscopy and comparison with adult collection data improve accuracy.
- The bibliography (66 references) spans developmental studies from 1829 onward and is the main source for literature-based descriptions.
