# Final Rule: Endangered Status for 159 Taxa of Animals

#endangered-species #wildlife-policy #vertebrates #invertebrates

## Executive Summary

This 1976 [[Federal Register]] final rule, issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (dated June 1, 1976; published June 14, 1976), determines 159 taxa of U.S. and foreign vertebrates and invertebrates to be Endangered under Section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. All 159 taxa were on Appendix I of the [[Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species]] (CITES) and not yet on the U.S. List. The rule narrows a September 26, 1975 proposal covering 216 taxa, withholding determinations on 56 taxa and resolving one other (the Glacier Bear) as not qualifying for listing. The document is a regulatory notice, not fish research, and contains no fish-specific data.

## Background & Description

- **Petitioner:** On May 22, 1975, the [[Fund for Animals]], Inc., requested listing of 216 plant and animal taxa from CITES Appendix I that were not on the U.S. list.
- **Convention:** CITES was drafted at a conference in Washington, D.C., February 12 to March 2, 1973. It is a treaty that regulates international trade in endangered wild flora and fauna. Appendix I covers species threatened with extinction that are or may be affected by trade; trade requires export permits, and imports require an import permit plus an export permit or re-export certificate.
- **Treaty status:** The United States signed March 3, 1973, the Senate consented August 3, 1973, and the President ratified September 13, 1973. By July 1, 1975 enough nations had ratified for entry into force; 23 nations had ratified at the time of the notice.
- **Legal basis:** The Act requires that a species be "in danger of extinction throughout all or a significant portion of its range" and that five statutory factors be considered.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The taxa span mammals, birds, reptiles and invertebrates, including freshwater mussels and marine mammals. Named examples include:

- [[Southern Sea Otter]] (*Enhydra lutris nereis*): the most-commented species, with 291 of 309 letters addressing it.
- [[Mexican Beaver]] (*Castor canadensis mesicanus*): data from New Mexico and Texas wildlife agencies opposed listing.
- [[Pearly Mussel]] (*Lampsilis satura* and *Epioblasma walkeri*): freshwater invertebrates whose resident states were not notified in time.
- [[Peregrine Falcon]] (*Falco peregrinus babylonicus*), and birds such as the Himalayan monal, Tibetan snowcock, Bengal florican, New Zealand parakeet and Principe parrot, which the [[International Council for Bird Preservation]] said may not be threatened.
- [[Marianas Mallard]] (*Anas oustaleti*), a Mariana Islands species whose governors had not been notified.
- [[Giant Sable Antelope]] (*Hippotragus niger variani*), whose range was corrected from "Southern Africa" to Angola.
- [[Bactrian Camel]] (*Camelus bactrianus*), the subject of a circus group's request.
- The so-called [[Glacier Bear]], judged to be an uncommon color variety of the black bear (*Ursus americanus emmonsii*).

Critical habitat was not designated; the notice states that any designation would require a separate rulemaking.

## Key Findings

- **Listing decision:** 159 of 216 proposed taxa are determined Endangered and added to the U.S. list (Section 17.11).
- **Deferred taxa:** No determination was made on 56 of the remaining 57 taxa, for four stated reasons:
  1. Substantial new data on the Mexican beaver and Southern sea otter were still being analyzed.
  2. The International Council for Bird Preservation said six birds may not be threatened or endangered; their status is in abeyance.
  3. Governors of the states where two pearly mussels and the Marianas mallard live were not notified as required; a final decision waits for their 90-day comment periods.
  4. Most of the remaining species are plants, and plant regulations were not yet finalized.
- **Glacier Bear:** After Alaska's data review, the Glacier Bear was found neither Endangered nor Threatened and is not listed.
- **Public comment:** 309 letters were received. Five opposed the overall listing, and three of those argued the Service had not shown the species were "in danger of extinction" or addressed the five factors. The Service responded in the rule's description section.
- **Southern Sea Otter:** Of 291 letters on this species, 289 favored listing and 2 opposed it. The State of California submitted several volumes of opposing data, so action was delayed for review.
- **Corrections:** The giant sable antelope's vernacular name and range were corrected.
- **Codification:** The list is added to 50 CFR Part 17, Section 17.11, and a footnote is added to the table.
