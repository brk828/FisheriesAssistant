# Changes in Fish Populations in the Green River Due to Inlet Modifications of Flaming Gorge Dam

#ichthyology #marine-biology #riverine-fishes

## Executive Summary

This report, by Paul B. Holden of BIO/WEST, Inc., evaluates how fish populations in the [[Green River]] changed after the [[Water And Power Resources Service]] installed inlet modifications on [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] in June 1978. The dam had been closed in November 1962. It produced a trout fishery below the dam that declined in 1967–1968, while [[Colorado Squawfish]] reproduction stopped in [[Dinosaur National Monument]] and resumed only after 1978. Cold tailwater temperatures were initially blamed. Holden's analysis of temperature and flow data suggests the cooling was driven mainly by high releases rather than by release temperatures alone. The inlet modifications allowed warm-water species to establish themselves above the [[Yampa River]] mouth, and young squawfish were found below it in 1980. The study was a 3-year effort (1978–1980).

## Background & Description

- **Dam history:** [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] closed in November 1962 after an extensive fish eradication program on the Green River and its tributaries in 1962. During its first several years, the tailwater trout fishery became one of the best "blue ribbon" fisheries in the country.
- **Native fish baseline (1963–1966):** Surveys found that native species, including Colorado squawfish, humpback chub, bonytail chub, and razorback sucker, had been nearly eliminated for 105 km below the dam, attributed to cold temperatures (Vanicek et al. 1970).
- **Yampa River role:** The [[Yampa River]] enters the Green River at a single point and appears to have moderated the cold dam flows enough for squawfish and most other native species to reproduce downstream of its mouth.
- **Decline:** Around 1967–1968 the trout fishery declined sharply, and squawfish reproduction below the Yampa mouth also dropped abruptly. No young squawfish were found for about 72 km below the Yampa mouth until 1978.
- **Study design:** Holden's 3-year study (1978–1980) was funded by the Water and Power Resources Service, with additional support from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and National Park Service. It focused on distribution and reproduction of Colorado squawfish and other native species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow and temperature.** Holden compared Table 1 (flows and temperatures at Greendale, Utah) and Table 2 (flows and temperatures at Jensen, Utah), covering 1951–1962, 1963–1966, and 1967–1977 (U.S. Geological Survey data). Release temperatures at the dam were only slightly lower during 1963–1966 than before. Temperatures at Jensen were also slightly lower after 1967. Flow from the dam, however, more than doubled after 1967 for nearly all months of the year. Higher flows would have moved water downstream faster, leaving it less time to warm. This meant the Green River below the trout area was affected by higher, and much colder, flows after 1962. The Yampa River's ability to moderate these flows was limited, especially after June when spring high flows receded. Holden suggested that reduced summer temperatures below the Yampa mouth (June, July, and August) were likely responsible for lower squawfish reproductive success.

**Sampling methods.** Sampling was conducted in March, June, August, and October of each year. Five main stations were sampled on the Green River:

- Above the Yampa mouth: [[Lodore Canyon]] (Lodore) and [[Alcove Brook]]
- Below the Yampa mouth: Island Park and Jensen
- At the Yampa mouth: [[Echo Park]]

Two stations on the Yampa River served as controls (Lower Yampa and Lily Park). Fish were collected by boat-mounted electrofishing and seines. All fish over 200 mm total length were tagged with numbered Floy tags, and rarer fish received small numbered dangler tags.

**Thermal regime shift.** The modifications allowed selection of a preferred release temperature near 11 °C. The section of the Green River above the Yampa mouth changed from a cool-water habitat to a warm-water habitat. This allowed warm-water fishes to invade and reproduce successfully.

## Key Findings

### 1. Changes above the Yampa River mouth
- Several warm-water species moved up to Alcove Brook and Lodore after the modifications. Reproduction of flannelmouth and bluehead suckers and speckled dace occurred at both stations in 1979.
- Roundtail chubs, redside shiners, and red shiners also reproduced at Alcove Brook in 1979.
- Cold-water species, primarily rainbow trout, declined in abundance except during March.
- Holden concluded that the Green River in Lodore Canyon changed from a cold-water habitat to a cool- or warm-water habitat in response to the modifications.
- The study noted a reduction in the number of species reproducing at Alcove Brook and lower numbers of young fish at Echo Park and Island Park in 1980.

### 2. Squawfish reproduction below the Yampa River mouth
- The Colorado Division of Wildlife found squawfish about 15 km up the Yampa River in August 1980.
- Young-of-the-year squawfish were found at Lower Yampa, Echo Park, and Island Park in 1980, but not in 1978 or 1979. This indicates successful reproduction.
- Adult squawfish collected by several researchers were relatively few (fewer than 100 in total) and small, compared with the numbers found in the 1960s.
- Holden and Crist (1981) hypothesized that the modifications caused the return of squawfish reproductive success to the Lower Yampa and Echo Park area, and that cold summer temperatures may have been responsible for the low success rate.

### 3. Growth and movement
- Growth of young fish above and below the Yampa mouth differed, probably due to daily flow fluctuations above the Yampa mouth.
- Recapture data on native and introduced fishes indicated movement patterns, though these were not summarized in detail.

### 4. Species presence and reproduction (Table 3)
Table 3 lists presence (X) and successful reproduction (R) for 21 species at Lodore and Alcove Brook, 1977–1980, before and after the modifications. Key entries include:
- Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker, and several warm-water species were newly recorded or reproducing after the modifications.
- Rainbow, cutthroat, and brown trout remained present in the Green River, though their abundance declined at Lodore and Alcove Brook.

### 5. Management implications and conclusions
- Flows from Flaming Gorge Dam likely reduced reproductive success of several species at Alcove Brook, Island Park, and Echo Park.
- The study demonstrated that inlet modifications can shift thermal conditions enough to change fish community composition and restore native reproduction, but also favor warm-water invaders.
