# Preimpoundment Fish Kill in Navarro Mills Reservoir

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #reservoir-management #chemical-treatment

## Executive Summary

This job completion report documents a preimpoundment chemical treatment of [[Navarro Mills Reservoir]] in western [[Navarro County]], Texas, conducted March 18-19, 1963. The treatment aimed to remove the existing native fish population, which consisted of 83% rough fish, prior to restocking with game fish. The main reservoir (670 acres, 3,400 acre feet) was treated with 80 gallons of toxaphene at 50 parts per billion, while tributaries received 55 gallons of rotenone. Post-treatment surveys indicated a successful fish kill, though not complete eradication. The project was conducted under Federal Aid Project F-14-D-7 (Statewide Rough Fish Control) with total expenditures of $1,661.85.

## Background & Description

[[Navarro Mills Reservoir]] is a [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] impoundment on [[Richland Creek]] in western [[Navarro County]], Texas. At full capacity, the reservoir would cover 11,700 acres with 143,200 acre feet of water. At the time of treatment (March 1963), the dam gates had just closed (March 15, 1963) and the lake covered approximately 670 acres with 3,400 acre feet of water.

Major tributaries entering the lake basin include [[Richland Creek]], [[Wolf Creek]], [[Ash Creek]], and [[White Rock Creek]]. Flow from these creeks was minimal during treatment.

Pre-treatment surveys conducted in December 1962 and February 1963 documented the existing fish community through gill netting (1,700 feet of nets) and seining. The surveys captured 197 fish representing multiple species, with rough fish dominating the assemblage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pre-Treatment Fish Community Composition:**

The combined netting results from December 11-12, 1962 and February 13-14, 1963 revealed:

- **Rough fish (83.3% by number, 77.1% by weight):**
  - [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*): 125 individuals (63.6%), 33.9 pounds, average 0.3 pounds
  - [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*): 29 individuals (14.7%), 15.7 pounds, average 0.5 pounds
  - [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*): 7 individuals (3.5%), 3.6 pounds, average 0.5 pounds
  - [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*): 3 individuals (1.5%), 2.8 pounds, average 0.9 pounds

- **Game fish (16.7% by number, 22.9% by weight):**
  - [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*): 26 individuals (13.2%), 7.1 pounds, average 0.3 pounds
  - [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*): 3 individuals (1.5%), 5.5 pounds, average 1.8 pounds
  - [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*): 2 individuals (1.0%), 0.5 pounds, average 0.2 pounds
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*): 1 individual (0.5%), 3.2 pounds
  - [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*): 1 individual (0.5%), 0.3 pounds

Seine collections additionally documented [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) and [[Red Shiners]] (*Notropis lutrensis*).

**Treatment Methodology:**

- **Tributaries (March 18, 1963):** 55 gallons of Noxfish (5% emulsifiable rotenone) applied via gravity flow drums mounted in three outboard motorboats over 3 hours. Dripper cans placed strategically upstream. Several ponds in the lake basin also treated.

- **Main Reservoir (March 19, 1963):** 80 gallons of toxaphene (6 pounds per gallon) applied at 50 parts per billion concentration. Water temperature: 62°F at surface. Chemical delivered via gravity flow drums into prop wash of outboard motors. Lake divided into four treatment areas, completed in approximately 3 hours.

## Key Findings

**Immediate Treatment Response:**

Fish began surfacing in tributaries approximately 30 minutes after rotenone application. Species observed included all those documented in pre-treatment surveys plus additional species:
- [[Tadpole Madtom]] (*Schilbeodes gyrinus*)
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus natalis*)
- [[Longear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis megalotis*)
- [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*)
- [[Freshwater Drum]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*)

Total number of fish killed in tributaries appeared relatively small. Most surfaced fish sank by late afternoon.

In the main reservoir, few [[Gizzard Shad]] surfaced on treatment day. Twenty-four hours later, many fish observed around shoreline, with shad and carp most abundant.

**Post-Treatment Assessment (April 22-23, 1963):**

Gill netting (900 feet) captured 19 fish:
- Rough fish: 14 individuals (73.7% by number, 88.3% by weight)
  - [[Black Bullhead]]: 12 individuals (63.3%), 9.0 pounds, average 0.7 pounds
  - [[River Carpsucker]]: 1 individual (5.2%), 1.3 pounds
  - [[Carp]]: 1 individual (5.2%), 1.0 pound
- Game fish: 5 [[Bluegill]] (26.3% by number, 11.7% by weight), 1.5 pounds total, average 0.3 pounds
- Seine collections added [[Mosquitofish]]

The findings indicated a successful fish eradication, though complete kill was not achieved.

**Management Recommendations:**

Restocking plan for [[Navarro Mills Reservoir]]:
- 200 [[Largemouth Bass]] fry per acre
- 50 [[White Crappie]] fingerlings per acre
- 50 [[Channel Catfish]] fingerlings per acre

Fish to be supplied by State fish hatcheries as available. Periodic monitoring recommended under Job B-21 (Fisheries Reconnaissance).

**Project Costs:**

Total expenditure: $1,661.85
- Salaries and wages: $779.78
- Travel expense: $394.07
- Operation of equipment: $50.00
- Materials and supplies: $438.00 (85 gallons toxaphene at $212.50; 55 gallons rotenone at $225.50)
