# Humpback Chub Recovery Plan Litigation: Order on Summary Judgment (D. Ariz. 2006)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This order from the [[United States District Court for the District of Arizona]] (Case No. 04-CV-636-PHX-FJM, dated January 2006) resolves cross-motions for summary judgment in a citizen suit by Grand Canyon Trust and others against the [[United States Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and the Secretary of the Interior. The dispute concerns the 2002 Recovery Goals for the [[Humpback Chub]], an endangered fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The court granted summary judgment for plaintiffs on one claim: that defendants failed to include time and cost estimates in the recovery plan as required by the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It granted summary judgment for defendants on the remaining claims, and ordered defendants to withdraw the 2002 Recovery Goals.

## Background & Description

The humpback chub ("chub") inhabits the Colorado River basin and is listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). Under 16 U.S.C. § 1533(f), the Secretary of the Interior must develop and implement recovery plans unless a plan will not promote species conservation. FWS prepared a recovery plan in 1979, revised it in 1990 (the "1990 Recovery Plan"), and supplemented and amended it in 2002 (the "2002 Recovery Goals").

Plaintiffs alleged that the 2002 Recovery Goals violate the ESA and the [[Administrative Procedure Act]] (APA). Their claims were narrowed over the litigation. The court previously dismissed the APA-based procedural challenges and the APA challenges to the specific recommendations of the 2002 plan. Plaintiffs' attempt to recast a claim as requiring use of "solely" the best scientific information was held untimely because the amendment deadline had passed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a legal order and contains no field data, population counts, or sample sizes. Its ecological relevance lies in the recovery framework for an endangered riverine fish. The ESA defines conservation broadly, covering a wide range of actions. The court noted that determining how to conserve the chub "requires the fusion of technical knowledge and skills with judgment," which it treated as a discretionary task.

## Key Findings

- **Non-discretionary duty:** The ESA requires the Secretary to develop and implement recovery plans, which is a non-discretionary duty. The substance of those plans is discretionary, so the court held that no citizen-suit cause of action exists to challenge whether the plan provides for the chub's conservation and survival. Defendants' motion on this claim was granted, and defendants' motion to strike related plaintiff declarations was granted.
- **Citizen-suit scope:** The ESA citizen-suit provision (16 U.S.C. § 1540(g)(1)(C)) permits "any person" to sue without the standing limits of other statutes. The court found it is not substantially broader than similar citizen-suit provisions regarding permissible subject matter. Waivers of sovereign immunity must be strictly construed, citing [[Kennecott Copper Corp. v. Costle]].
- **Time and cost estimates (successful claim):** Under 16 U.S.C. § 1533(f)(1)(B)(iii), recovery plans must include estimates of the time required and cost to carry out measures needed to achieve the goal. The 2002 Recovery Goals included none. The court held that the 1990 plan's estimates did not carry over, because the 2002 document was an amendment that modified measures and therefore required revised estimates.
- **Practicability defense rejected:** Defendants argued FWS had the necessary cost and time information but chose not to include it due to scarce resources. The court held that the ESA requires such estimates "where practicable," and defendants did not argue that including them was impracticable.
- **Procedural exhaustion:** The court rejected defendants' argument that plaintiffs were barred from raising the time-and-cost issue, since the issue was raised before FWS during the decision-making period.
- **Abandoned claims:** Plaintiffs' claims on objective, measurable delisting criteria and on critical habitat for all designated areas were treated as abandoned, since they were not addressed in briefing. Summary judgment was granted for defendants on these.
- **Remedy:** The 2002 Recovery Goals were declared of no force and effect, and defendants were ordered to withdraw them. The court denied plaintiffs' request for an injunction requiring new recovery goals, finding no basis was shown, but stated that any new goals must comply with the court's findings.
- **Final disposition:** Summary judgment was granted to plaintiffs on the time-and-cost claim (Doc. 41). Summary judgment was granted to defendants on all other claims (Doc. 49), and defendants' motion to strike (Doc. 52) was granted. The clerk was directed to enter judgment.
