# West Turkey Creek Fish Survey Results

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
On 29 November 1999, a fish survey was conducted along a series of pools in West Turkey Creek (Chiricahua Mountains, Arizona) using electro-shocking equipment. The survey targeted two native cyprinid species: **[[Gila Purpurea]]** (Yaqui chub) and **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]** (longfin dace). A total of 21 *Gila purpurea* (3 males, 12 females, remainder unsexed/juvenile) and 6 *Agosia chrysogaster* were documented across multiple discrete pool habitats. Notably, this survey recorded the **first confirmed occurrence of *Agosia chrysogaster* above the Lower Sycamore Waterfall Pool** since electro-shock surveys began in 1996, suggesting range expansion or a previously undetected upstream population. The population is also noted as a potentially **undescribed Yaqui form**, warranting further taxonomic investigation.

## Background & Description
- **[[Gila Purpurea]]** individuals ranged in total length from **60.5 mm to 101.0 mm**, with females generally larger than males (e.g., largest recorded female = 101.0 mm vs. largest male = 79.5 mm).
- One female *Gila purpurea* (101.0 mm, Upper Sycamore Waterfall pool) exhibited a **deformed/severely damaged right pectoral fin**, suggesting possible predation attempt, injury, or developmental anomaly.
- **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]** individuals were smaller, with recorded total lengths around **50.5–53.5 mm**; several were sex-undetermined or only observed (not captured/measured).
- Sex ratio for *Gila purpurea* captures was heavily skewed toward females (12 females : 3 males), which may reflect reproductive timing, habitat segregation by sex, or capture bias from electro-shocking technique.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Habitat consists of a series of **discrete pool microhabitats** along West Turkey Creek, including dispersed campsite pools, outcrop pools, waterfall plunge pools (Upper and Lower Sycamore Waterfall), downed-log pools, tributary confluence pools, and waterfall base pools.
- **[[Gila Purpurea]]** was the dominant and most widely distributed species across sampled pools, found from the dispersed campsite pool down through Upper and Lower Sycamore Waterfall pools.
- **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]** was more restricted in distribution, historically confined below the Lower Sycamore Waterfall — a likely natural barrier to upstream dispersal. Its detection above this barrier in this survey represents a significant range-limit shift.
- Some pools ("Downed Log," "SE Tributary," "Lower Waterfall Base") yielded **no captures or observations**, indicating patchy occupancy or unsuitable microhabitat conditions (depth, cover, flow, connectivity) at time of sampling.
- Species were sampled in the same drainage system, implying **sympatric co-occurrence** with potential microhabitat partitioning around waterfall barriers.

## Key Findings
- **21 *Gila purpurea*** captured/observed: 3 males, 12 females, with remaining individuals unsexed or juvenile; size range 60.5–101.0 mm TL.
- **6 *Agosia chrysogaster*** captured/observed; size range ~50.5–53.5 mm TL, some individuals observed only (not measured).
- **First confirmed record of *Agosia chrysogaster* above Lower Sycamore Waterfall Pool** since surveys began in 1996 — a notable biogeographic/range-expansion finding.
- The *Agosia chrysogaster* population in this drainage is suspected to represent an **undescribed Yaqui form**, highlighting potential cryptic diversity within the species complex.
- Electro-shocker output was increased mid-survey (200 V → 300 V) at the "Outcrop" pool, suggesting initial settings were insufficient for effective capture in certain habitat conditions.
- One *Gila purpurea* female displayed a **damaged right pectoral fin**, a morphological anomaly of note for individual health/predation-pressure monitoring.
- Multiple pools showed **zero captures**, underscoring spatial heterogeneity in fish distribution within even a small stream reach.
