# Annual Production of Stream Trout in Minnesota Streams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This paper estimates the annual production of stream-dwelling trout in Minnesota streams, with a focus on the production-to-biomass (P/B) ratio, a standard index of population turnover. Populations of [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]] and [[Brook Trout]] were sampled by [[Electrofishing]] in October 1977 in the [[Blackhoof River]] and the [[Caribou River]], with a third stream also contributing data. The authors found that annual P/B ratios were inversely related to the number of age-groups in a population, rather than to species identity, and they suggest this relationship as a hypothesis for further testing. The work is part of research on [[Secondary Production]] in streams and draws on the broader literature on [[Fish Production]].

## Background & Description

Stream fish production is usually estimated from abundance, standing stock and growth data. Annual production is normally expressed relative to standing biomass as the P/B ratio. The study used [[Population Estimation]] methods based on electrofishing, with variances for abundance, standing stock, production and P/B calculated using the approaches of [[Newman and Martin 1983]]. Earlier reviews, notably [[Mann and Penczak 1986]], reported that annual P/B ratios tend to decline with cohort age, which framed the analysis.

The study was published as paper 17,078 of the Scientific Journal Series of the [[Minnesota Agricultural Experiment Station]], conducted under project 72. Acknowledgments credit multiple volunteer field assistants and the [[National Science Foundation]] (grant DEB76 series, number partly illegible in the scan). Support also came from a University of Minnesota Doctoral Dissertation Fellowship. The manuscript was received May 8, 1989 and accepted December 14, 1989.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three study streams lie in Minnesota, including the Blackhoof River in Carlton County and the Caribou River, both within the north-shore region of [[Lake Superior]]. The stream-trout populations were sampled as age-structured groups, including age-group 0. Age-group composition differed among the three populations, and this structure was the main driver of the P/B patterns reported. Partially illegible sampling details in the scan mean that exact site sizes and sampling areas cannot be reported with confidence.

The paper places its trout data within a wider set of stream studies, including work on [[Macroinvertebrate Production]] in eastern Minnesota streams and comparisons of [[Fish Production]] with benthic production in trout streams (see [[Waters 1988]]).

## Key Findings

- **Annual P/B ratios by population:**
  - Blackhoof River rainbow trout: annual P/B = 2.2, with a small number of age-groups present.
  - Caribou River brook trout: four age-groups present, annual P/B = 1.7.
  - Shadow-area brown trout population (river name partly garbled in the scan): six age-groups present, annual P/B = 0.9.
- **Statistical comparison:** By analysis of variance, the P/B ratios for brown trout and rainbow trout differed significantly. The brook trout P/B ratio was intermediate between them.
- **Main pattern:** An inverse relationship appeared between annual P/B ratio and the number of age-groups in a population. The authors note this pattern was expected from earlier work showing that P/B falls with cohort age, and they suggest that P/B is better correlated with the number of age-groups than with species identity.
- **Limitation and next step:** The authors state that the correlation is only a postulated relationship. They call for fuller analysis including other fish populations before its validity can be assessed.
- **Key references cited:** Work on [[Fish Production in Rivers]] (Mann and Penczak 1986), [[Population Estimation]] by Newman and Martin (1983), statistical methods for fish populations by [[Ricker 1975]], and production studies on trout from [[Elliott 1984]] and [[Cooper and Scherer 1972]].
