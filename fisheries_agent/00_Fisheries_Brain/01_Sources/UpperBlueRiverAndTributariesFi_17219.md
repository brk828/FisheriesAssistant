# Upper Blue River and Tributaries Fisheries Survey (1998)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

A June–July 1998 fisheries survey by [[Arizona State University]] sampled about 50 named localities across the upper [[Blue River]] drainage in Arizona and New Mexico. The survey recorded 10 fish taxa: six native and four non-native. Native species were [[Apache Trout]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Desert Sucker]]. Non-native taxa were [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], and [[Apache-Rainbow Trout Hybrid]]. Two non-fish amphibians, tiger salamanders and leopard frogs, were also recorded.

Fish were confined largely to permanent water. Many sampled reaches were dry, intermittent, or separated from perennial flow by falls, so the survey documented a fragmented fish community. Pure Apache trout were found at only one site, and their genetic purity is uncertain. The authors flagged possible native fish reintroduction sites.

## Background & Description

**Survey scope and team.** The field crew comprised Brian Bagley, Brian Kesner, and Carol Secor of Arizona State University. Sampling ran from June 20 to July 26, 1998, covering Apache and Greenlee counties (AZ) and Catron County (NM). The survey report notes the source file as "blue98report."

**Methods.** Crews used a gas-powered Smith-Root backpack electrofisher, dip nets, gill nets (including a 125 m experimental net at Jackson Creek Reservoir), and seines (1/8 inch mesh in several reaches). Electrofishing effort was recorded in seconds. Table 1 reports catch per unit effort as fish caught per minute of shocking. Appendix A gives legal descriptions, elevations, habitat notes, and counts for each site.

**Taxa recorded.** Ten fish taxa were collected, along with tiger salamanders (*Ambystoma tigrinum*) and leopard frogs (*Rana* sp.).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat conditions.** Sampled reaches ranged from steep, boulder-cobble canyons to shallow meadow streams. Riffle-pool sequences predominated in productive reaches, with cottonwood, willow, alder, and ponderosa pine along banks. Many canyons held only isolated pools or no water at all during the survey period.

**Barriers.** Several sites had natural barriers to fish movement:
- A 50 ft falls on [[Buckalou Creek]] above [[Castle Creek]], with no fish above it.
- A 7 ft near-vertical drop on [[Mckittrick Creek]] below Grapevine Canyon, with trout and dace below but none above despite adequate habitat.
- A 40 ft falls on Turkey Creek above Campbell Blue Creek.
- A 20 ft falls on [[Pace Creek]] near Jackson Creek, and a 30 ft falls 100 m above the AZ-NM border.

The authors noted that many boulder falls and plunge pools could block upstream fish movement.

**Land use and water.** Private land, stock tanks, and gravel pits occurred in the drainage. Several canyons had springs with milky or murky water. Crayfish were common at several lower Blue River and Campbell Blue Creek sites but were absent from some others.

**Comparative data.** At Blue River @ Bobcat Flat, the 1998 counts were compared with an Arizona Game and Fish Department survey from August 1994. Counts were lower in 1998 for longfin dace (11 vs. 340), speckled dace (100 vs. 267), loach minnow (23 vs. 74), and desert sucker (5 vs. 69). Brown trout were recorded in 1998 (5) but not in 1994 (0). This comparison comes from a single site and does not establish a regional trend.

## Key Findings

**Apache trout and hybridization**
- [[Apache Trout]] were found in only one stream, upper [[Coleman Creek]]. Two individuals were collected at the pool below Rd 59, but rainbow trout were found downstream, so genetic purity may be questionable.
- Apache trout are known from [[KP Creek]] and Grant Creek, which were not sampled.
- Apache-rainbow hybrids were found in Castle Creek (43 fish at rd 58 to Buckalou Creek, plus 1 at the Campbell Blue confluence), Buckalou Creek (20), and Mckittrick Creek.

**Non-native salmonids**
- [[Rainbow Trout]] were most abundant in lower upper Blue River reaches. At Blue River @ Bobcat Flat, 1 rainbow trout was caught, with 5 brown trout (one about 20 inches).
- [[Brown Trout]] were widespread and mostly concentrated in upstream reaches of the Blue River and tributaries. The largest counts were 36 at Coleman Creek, 33 at Campbell Blue @ Cat Creek, and 32 at Campbell Blue @ Castle Creek.
- [[Brook Trout]] were found at one location only: Pace Creek, 100 m below the AZ-NM border, where 2 fish were recorded below a falls.

**Native minnows and suckers**
- [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Speckled Dace]] occurred in most permanent water in the upper Blue River drainage. The highest speckled dace counts were 340 at Foote Creek 0.75 mi above Blue River and 275 at Frieborn Canyon 2.3 mi above Dry Blue Creek.
- At Dry Blue Creek near Unnamed Spring, longfin dace (71) outnumbered speckled dace (6), which the authors found unusual given that the habitat appeared more suitable for speckled dace.
- [[Loach Minnow]] were found throughout the upper Blue River and in several tributaries, including Dry Blue Creek, Pace Creek, Frieborn Canyon, and Campbell Blue Creek. They were locally abundant in places, with 23 at Bobcat Flat.
- [[Sonora Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]] occurred throughout most permanent water except headwater reaches. A genetic-analysis sampling effort at Blue River @ Blue Camp on July 24 targeted desert suckers, and many juveniles were captured.

**Amphibians**
- Leopard frogs were found at two areas, lower Frieborn Canyon (5 frogs in 2/3 mi reach) and lower Dry Blue Creek. Private land separates them, but the authors judged the populations likely continuous.
- Tiger salamanders were noted at many stock tanks and lakes, including Dry Blue Creek headwaters, Hy Clark Canyon, WS Lake, Steeple Canyon, and Foote Creek headwaters (P Bar Lake).

**Dry and fishless sites**
- Roughly a third of the sampled localities yielded no fish, most often because of dry channels, intermittent flow, or barriers. Examples include Grapevine Canyon, Johnson Canyon, Largo Creek, Milligan Lake, and most Steeple Canyon forks.

**Management implications**
- The authors identified Jackson Creek above Pace Creek and the area above the Pace Creek falls as having potential for native fish reintroduction, given the lack of exotic fish downstream of the falls.
- Steeple Canyon (1.5 mi east of Blue River) was judged suitable for native fish reintroduction if its water were permanent.
- The authors recommended caution when interpreting Apache trout populations because of ongoing hybridization with rainbow trout and because the survey did not walk entire drainages, so physical barriers could not be fully assessed.
