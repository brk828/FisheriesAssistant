# Aquatic Collection Field Notes - Gila River Basin Survey 1973

#ichthyology #aquatic-ecology #field-survey #water-quality

## Executive Summary

This document comprises field collection notes from aquatic surveys conducted by James F. LeRousty throughout the [[Gila River]] Basin and [[San Pedro River]] system in Arizona and New Mexico during 1973. The notes document multiple sampling locations from April through December 1973, recording water quality parameters, physical habitat characteristics, and collection data at various sites along the [[Gila River]], its tributaries (including [[Mineral Creek]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Eagle Creek]], [[Bonita Creek]]), and the [[San Pedro River]]. The surveys employed standardized data collection protocols tracking temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, turbidity, and various chemical parameters across seasonal conditions.

## Background & Description

The field notes represent systematic aquatic surveys conducted across the [[Gila River]] watershed system in 1973. Collection sites spanned from the upper [[Gila River]] (junction of Middle and East Forks near [[Turkey Creek]]) downstream through various confluences and tributaries in [[Graham County]], [[Pinal County]], and [[Cochise County]], Arizona, extending into [[Grant County]], New Mexico. The collector utilized a standardized field data sheet format documenting locality information, water characteristics, habitat features, and collection methodology.

Key survey locations included:
- [[Gila River]] at multiple points from headwaters to lower reaches
- [[Mineral Creek]] at confluence with [[Gila River]]
- [[San Pedro River]] at [[Charleston]] and other locations
- [[Bonita Creek]], [[Eagle Creek]], [[San Francisco River]]
- Various tributary systems and springs

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water quality parameters documented across sites revealed substantial variation in aquatic conditions throughout the basin:

**Temperature Ranges**: Water temperatures ranged from 51°F to 85°F across seasons and locations, with air temperatures recorded from 59°F to 95°F.

**Dissolved Oxygen**: DO levels varied from low values (4 mg/L) to supersaturated conditions (14 mg/L), indicating diverse metabolic conditions across habitats.

**pH**: Recorded values ranged from pH 3.5 to 8.5, with some locations showing acidic conditions.

**Conductivity and Chemistry**: Specific conductivity measurements ranged from low values in headwater streams to over 1000 μmhos/cm in some locations. Chemical analyses included calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) ranging from 60-183 mg/L, chloride (Cl) from 5-130 mg/L, and sulfate (SO₄) measurements.

**Flow Conditions**: Discharge measurements documented flows from 5 cfs in small tributaries to 233 cfs in the main [[Gila River]] channel, with seasonal variation evident.

**Habitat Characteristics**: Notes reference turbidity levels (0-775 JTU), transparency measurements, current conditions (still to rapid), substrate types, vegetation, and cover features.

## Key Findings

1. **Seasonal Flow Variation**: The [[Gila River]] at [[Coolidge Dam]] showed substantial flow variation, with measurements of 14 cfs (June 1973), 233 cfs (July 1973), and 241 cfs, indicating significant seasonal or management-related discharge patterns.

2. **Water Quality Gradients**: Systematic differences in water chemistry were documented along the river continuum, with headwater tributaries generally showing lower conductivity and different chemical signatures compared to downstream main-stem locations.

3. **Temperature Regimes**: Water temperatures showed both spatial and temporal variation, with tributary streams often cooler than main-stem locations, and seasonal warming evident in summer collections (water temperatures reaching 85°F in August).

4. **Dissolved Oxygen Dynamics**: DO concentrations varied substantially, from hypoxic conditions (4 mg/L) to supersaturated levels (14 mg/L), suggesting diverse metabolic processes including photosynthetic production and respiratory demand across habitats.

5. **Tributary Contributions**: Multiple tributary systems ([[Mineral Creek]], [[Bonita Creek]], [[Eagle Creek]], [[San Francisco River]]) were documented with distinct water quality signatures, contributing to the overall aquatic diversity of the [[Gila River]] system.

6. **Chemical Composition**: Hardness values (as CaCO₃) ranged from 60-183 mg/L, indicating moderately hard to hard water conditions. Chloride and sulfate concentrations varied substantially among locations, reflecting geological and land-use influences.

7. **Habitat Diversity**: Field notes document diverse habitat types including pools, riffles, runs, and various substrate compositions (bedrock, cobble, sand), supporting varied aquatic communities.

8. **Survey Coverage**: The 1973 field season included intensive sampling from April through December, with collections at [[Cochran]] (multiple dates), [[Charleston]], [[Clifton]], and numerous other localities, providing comprehensive spatial and temporal coverage of the basin.

9. **Methodological Documentation**: Standardized data collection protocols were employed, including consistent measurement of physical, chemical, and biological parameters, enabling comparative analysis across sites and seasons.

10. **Geographic Scope**: The survey encompassed the [[Gila River]] from its headwaters in New Mexico through Arizona, including major confluences with the [[San Pedro River]] and multiple tributary systems, representing one of the most extensive aquatic surveys of this watershed system.
