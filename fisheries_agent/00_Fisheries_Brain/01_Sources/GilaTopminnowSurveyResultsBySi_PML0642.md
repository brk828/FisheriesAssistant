# Gila Topminnow Survey Results by Site (1996 Compilation)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation, dated January 1996 and prepared by Doug Duncan, tabulates survey records, stocking events and population status for the [[Gila Topminnow]] (recorded as POOC) across Arizona and New Mexico. The record spans from discovery records in the 1900s to surveys in early 1995. It covers natural sites (springs, creeks and rivers), reintroduced sites (stock tanks, ponds and artificial impoundments) and a large number of failed attempts. The main pattern is the replacement or decline of topminnow populations by the non-native [[Western Mosquitofish]] (GAAF), with the topminnow persisting mainly where physical barriers, renovation or isolation were used.

## Background & Description

- **Focal species:** [[Gila Topminnow]] (POOC; recorded in the source as "POOCOC" in many entries, likely an OCR variant of the same taxon). It is a small live-bearing poeciliid.
- **Co-occurring and comparison taxa:**
  - [[Western Mosquitofish]] (GAAF), an introduced competitor and predator, the most frequently recorded companion and invader.
  - [[Longfin Dace]] (AGCH), a native cyprinid, frequently recorded in creeks and springs.
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (CYMA, including CYMAMA and CYMAER strain labels), used in stocking at several sites.
  - Other taxa noted include green sunfish (LECY), a mosquitofish-type livebearer, and several small native and introduced fishes (e.g., CACL, PIPR, RHOS, PACL, NOLU, MISA).
- **Survey method and notation:** Records are mostly terse field notes with date, site, count and observer. Counts are reported as given (e.g., "61 POOC", "AGCH abundant"). Stocking sources are noted as "Mixed stocks", specific strains (e.g., [[Monkey Spring Strain]], [[Sharp Spring Strain]], [[Santa Clara Strain]]), or source localities such as [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]]. Dates are recorded as day/month/year.
- **Observers and agencies:** Named contributors include J. and S. Stefferud, D. Weedman, L. Simons, B. Bagley, M. Brown, J. Young, Brooks, Hines, Simons and Parker, and agencies including [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), [[U.S. Forest Service]] (USFS) and [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The record documents a consistent sequence of events at many sites:

1. **Discovery or historic presence** of the topminnow (often early 1900s to 1960s records, e.g., Hubbs, Chamberlain, Minckley).
2. **Invasion by mosquitofish (GAAF)**, often in the 1960s to 1980s, after which topminnow proportions fell sharply. Examples include Bylas Springs (GAAF present by 1978-79) and the Patagonia Lake spillway (GAAF invasion in 1969).
3. **Management intervention:** concrete or gabion barriers (e.g., at [[Bylas Springs]] in 1984 and 1989), piscicide renovation using [[Antimycin A]] and rotenone, followed by restocking.
4. **Reintroduction** into artificial and isolated habitats such as [[Stock Tank]] systems, ponds and troughs. Many of these failed due to desiccation, dredging or colonization by mosquitofish.

Several sites show the consequences of this pattern. Barriers could hold back mosquitofish, but water sometimes washed around them. Stock tanks were often dry or not perennial. Isolated spring and creek sites supported the best topminnow populations.

## Key Findings

**Natural sites**

- **[[Bylas Springs]]:** The barrier-and-renovation sequence did not keep mosquitofish out. In 1987, 98% of fish immediately above the barrier were GAAF. In 1989 there was 1 POOC and 47 GAAF, and by 1991 POOC made up 7%. The source also records 60 POOC moved to [[Arizona State University]] (ASU).
- **[[Fresno Canyon]]:** In 1992, 88 POOC were recorded with 1 GAAF, 47 longfin dace (LECY-associated sampling listed separately) and 1 MISA. In 1993, several upstream pools above the Sonoita Creek confluence yielded 82 POOC.
- **[[Monkey Spring]]:** A large population was reported in 1990, and 61 POOC with abundant AGCH in 1994.
- **[[Middle Sorrel Spring]] and [[Redrock Canyon]]:** Middle Sorrel was barrier-built in 1984 and found fishless by 1990-91. Redrock Canyon had repeated surveys with POOC present at falls and gate springs, and high GAAF proportions at some sites (e.g., 98% in one record).
- **[[Santa Cruz River]] (Lochiel gage):** GAAF rose from 3% (1981) to 81% (1987). In 1989, 208 GAAF and 30 POOC were recorded, and by 1991 the count was 247 GAAF and 2 POOC.
- **[[Sharp Spring]]:** In 1986, 40 POOC and 37 GAAF were recorded. By 1990-91, GAAF numbered 173-286 with 23-25 POOC.
- **[[Patagonia Lake Spillway]]:** GAAF invaded in 1969. POOC were absent 1978-81 and reappeared in 1982. By 1986 the record shows 100% GAAF below the dam.
- **[[Tonto Creek]]:** Historic records from 1904 to 1941 are followed by 1989-94 records with POOC present (e.g., 26 POOC and 150 AGCH in 1994).

**Reintroduced sites**

- Most reintroductions in the record failed. Stock tanks, ponds and troughs were frequently dry, dredged or invaded.
- **[[Mud Spring]] trough:** This is one of the more persistent reintroduced populations. POOC were abundant and breeding in 1995, with all age classes present in the upper trough, while the record also notes a dry overflow and a site with no fish in 1995.
- **[[Cave Creek]] (1992):** 2,269 POOC were recorded in multiple sampling and reported with 1,000s of AGCH, indicating a persistent population.
- **[[Tres Alamos]]:** Stocked with 1,000 POOC from Tule Creek in 1984 (outside historic range). Extant through 1986, then failed by 1991.
- Many individual site entries are recorded as "failed", "dry", "no fish" or "not stocked". The compilation therefore documents substantial reintroduction failure across the region.

**Overall outcomes**

- Mosquitofish dominance and drying were the two most frequently recorded causes of decline.
- Barriers and piscicide renovation provided temporary success but were rarely permanent.
- Source counts are approximate and inconsistent across observers, with many records based on single visits.
