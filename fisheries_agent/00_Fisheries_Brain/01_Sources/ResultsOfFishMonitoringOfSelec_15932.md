# Results of Fish Monitoring of Selected Waters of the Gila River Basin, 1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #centrarchidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This report presents the 1997 results of fish monitoring in the Gila River Basin, Arizona, prepared by the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Phoenix Area Office) for the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] and the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]]. The monitoring was required by a 1994 [[Biological Opinion]] on delivery of [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water. That opinion found the project could jeopardize four threatened or endangered fishes and required a baseline study and long-term monitoring of non-native fish in the CAP aqueduct and selected rivers and canals.

In 1997, 27 taxa (including two *Tilapia* species) were captured. Native fishes made up a small share of the catch, and red shiner was the only species found in every monitored stream and canal. The authors state that data quality and sampling design limit cross-year and cross-site comparisons, so only general conclusions are drawn.

## Background & Description

The [[Central Arizona Project]] is a system of aqueducts and pipelines that carries Colorado River water from Lake Havasu to central and southern Arizona. It was authorized by the Colorado River Basin Project Act of 1968 and largely completed in 1993. The 1994 [[Biological Opinion]] concluded that CAP water transfers could introduce non-native fishes and aquatic organisms from the lower Colorado River into Gila Basin drainages, where they could harm four listed species: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The opinion also found that the project would adversely modify critical habitat for the last three.

The monitoring plan targets six reaches:

1. CAP aqueduct
2. [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) canals
3. [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG)
4. Salt River between Stewart Mountain and Granite Reef dams
5. Gila River between Coolidge and Ashurst-Hayden dams
6. San Pedro River downstream from the U.S.-Mexico border

Monitoring began in 1994 and earlier data were reported in memoranda and by Clarkson (1998). Methods follow the [[Long-Term Monitoring Plan]] (Revision No. 2, Clarkson 1996). Stream sampling used replicate quantitative stations and mainly [[Electrofishing]], supplemented by nets, traps, seines, and angling. Canal sampling was opportunistic and occurred during low-flow or dry-up periods. Primary sampling was split among Reclamation, [[Arizona State University]] (for the SRP and FCG canals), and AZGFD (for the Gila, Salt, and San Pedro rivers).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampled waters differ widely in habitat. The CAP canal is deep, swift, and monotonous, so sampling concentrated on pumping plant forebays. The SRP and FCG canals are segmented by [[Electrical Fish Barrier]] installations, which concentrate fishes above the barrier and complicate comparisons. Some sites were dry or nearly dry at sampling, and some had unusual flows. In 1995 and 1996, lower Gila River discharges were higher than inflows and leakage could explain, which made backpack shocking inefficient. Flows in 1997 were normal and low.

Sampling conditions shaped the results in several ways. Flows on the San Pedro were high and muddy at the Aravaipa Creek site, which reduced sampling efficiency. Right-of-entry problems closed or moved several stations. The Hawk Spring Canyon and Gage Station sites were deleted, and four Gila River stations were relocated 1.6 to 3.3 km.

The authors note that fish assemblages were highly variable among streams and canals. Most species in the sampled waters are [[Non-Native Fish]], which is the concern underlying the monitoring program.

## Key Findings

**Overall richness and native fish**

- 27 taxa were captured in 1997, including the January 1998 SRP Arizona Canal samples.
- Only four native species were encountered: [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Sonora Sucker]].
- Native percentages ranged from 0% (CAP Canal) to 33% (FCG Canal above the barrier).
- No species new to the monitoring record were captured in 1997.

**Distribution patterns**

- [[Red Shiner]] was the only species found in every stream and canal, though not at every station.
- [[Common Carp]], green sunfish, and [[Largemouth Bass]] occurred in all waters except the FCG Canal.
- Several species were restricted to single systems, including [[Smallmouth Bass]] (Gila River only), striped bass (CAP only), and rainbow trout, walleye, and goldfish (SRP canals only).
- Roundtail chub was absent from the Salt River even though it was common in the downstream SRP canals.

**Water body summaries**

- **CAP Canal:** 12 species in total (maximum of 7 at any station). All captured fishes were non-native. Young-of-year [[Bluegill]] at the San Xavier pumping plant outnumbered all other species captured at other stations combined. Grass carp appeared only in the upper reach, which the authors attribute to the absence of electrofishing in lower reaches.
- **Gila River:** 15 species (maximum of 12 at one station). Native suckers dominated several samples, while red shiner, yellow bullhead, and mosquitofish were prominent elsewhere. Native fish made up 15 to 25% of the species at stations.
- **San Pedro River:** 11 species across eight stations, with 3 to 9 species per station. [[Longfin Dace]] was the only species present at every station. The lower reach was numerically dominated by red shiner, but very few fish were captured.
- **FCG Canal:** 7 species total. Mosquitofish dominated above the barrier and red shiner below it. Natives made up 33% (2 of 6) above the barrier and 20% (1 of 5) below.
- **Salt River:** 11 species (7, 9, and 6 at the upper, middle, and lower stations). Post-young-of-year native suckers and largemouth bass made up most of the catch. No young-of-year Sonora sucker were captured, and desert sucker captures dropped sharply at the lower station.
- **SRP South Canal:** 13 species total, with 10 above and 11 below the barrier. Natives made up 30% (3 of 10) above the barrier and 27% (3 of 11) below.
- **SRP Arizona Canal:** 18 species total, with 13 above and 16 below the barrier. Natives made up 23% (3 of 13) above and 25% (4 of 16) below.

**Caveats and planned work**

- Clarkson (1998) raised concerns about data quality, sampling design, and the suitability of the data for cross-year and cross-site comparisons. A statistical review was due in the first half of 2000, after which the monitoring plan would be revised.
- Because of these concerns, the report gives only very general conclusions about assemblage structure.
- Habitat data from the monitoring were to be published in a comprehensive report in 2000.
