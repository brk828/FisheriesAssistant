# Primary Productivity--Fish Harvest Relations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #Clupeidae #Centrarchidae #Poeciliidae #primary-productivity

## Executive Summary

This is a 1959 Federal Aid in Fish and Wildlife Restoration project proposal (Dingell-Johnson Act, Project F-8-R-2) submitted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department, led by **William J. McConnell**, to investigate the relationship between **gross primary productivity (G.P.P.)** and **game fish biomass production** at **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]**, Santa Cruz County, Arizona. The project comprises five interconnected jobs: (A) measurement of lake primary productivity via diurnal oxygen curves, (B) fish harvest/creel census, (C) data analysis from the prior project segment (F-8-R-1), (D) continued investigation of **[[Threadfin Shad]]** biology as a forage base, and (E) an experimental synthetic-community approach to establish G.P.P.-to-consumer biomass ratios using laboratory microcosms. The overarching goal is to develop a predictive model linking phytoplankton/benthic productivity to harvestable fish biomass, circumventing the previously abandoned organic carbon budget approach due to unmeasurable respiratory metabolism in the lake.

## Background & Description

This document is a management/methods proposal rather than a morphological study; only incidental biological descriptors are given for the study organisms:

- **[[Threadfin Shad]]** (*Dorosoma petenense*): Individuals available during the reporting period were uniformly **>4 inches**, with spawning anticipated in June. Age determination planned via **scale annulus readings** for large individuals, supplemented by length-frequency mode progression for growth rate estimation.
- **[[Gambusia]]** sp.: Used as small-bodied terminal consumers in synthetic laboratory communities; young individuals introduced as primary "harvestable" biomass proxy.
- **Largemouth Bass** fry (*Micropterus salmoides*): Introduced later in the experimental tank succession as a secondary, higher-order predator once the Gambusia-supporting community was established.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]** is differentiated into two principal habitat/production zones for sampling purposes:
  - **Deep-water (open) community**: Productivity dominated by **phytoplankton**; photosynthesis measured via diurnal oxygen variation in vertical water "cores" within the **photic zone** (defined as depth of 1% surface light penetration, measured with a submarine photometer).
  - **Cove/shallow-water community**: Combined productivity of **attached (benthic) plants** and **plankton**.
- **Trophic structure** modeled experimentally: primary producers (phytoplankton, algal slime, plastic-simulated "plant thickets") → micro-crustaceans/rotifers → **[[Gambusia]]** (primary consumers) → **Largemouth Bass** fry (secondary/terminal consumers) — an explicit attempt to construct a simplified synthetic food web mirroring lake trophic dynamics.
- **[[Threadfin Shad]]** serve as the key **forage fish** linking primary/secondary production to game fish (predator) biomass; their population dynamics (spawning timing/location, egg and larval density via Clarke-Bumpus tow nets and tube plummet samplers, size/age distribution, and food habits) are central to interpreting energy flow to game fish.
- Sampling gear reflects seasonal shad behavior: **cast and dip nets** effective when water <65°F slows shad movement; **surface seines and gill nets** (including fine-mesh Japanese gill nets) used when shad are more mobile and dispersed.
- **Fish harvest** data (creel census via personal interviews and roadblock traffic counts) is used to empirically anchor the productivity-to-harvest relationship in the natural lake system, complementing the controlled laboratory microcosm ratios.

## Key Findings

- The previously planned **organic carbon (organic matter) budget** approach for relating G.P.P. to fish production was **abandoned** because respiratory metabolism of Pena Blanca Lake could not be satisfactorily measured.
- An alternative approach was adopted: deriving **G.P.P.-to-consumer biomass ratios** from **synthetic laboratory communities** (8 five-gallon jugs; 8 125-gallon cattle tanks), enriched with inorganic nutrients and seeded with lake plankton/plant washings, to establish a transferable ratio for interpreting field G.P.P. data.
- G.P.P. was operationally defined as **daily oxygen production corrected for respiration and diffusion loss**, determined graphically from the difference between pre-sunset oxygen concentration and the extrapolated concentration expected from prior night's respiration rate; surface diffusion loss was found to be negligible in prior work (F-8-R-1).
- **Chlorophyll-to-G.P.P. ratios** (from filtered seston/phytoplankton, acetone-extracted and measured photometrically) were planned as a supplementary, more frequent proxy for productivity between direct oxygen-curve measurements.
- The **fish harvest census** methodology finalized after early-project iteration combines **personal interviews of departing anglers** at parking areas with a **roadblock traffic-counter** pressure survey, sampled on both a weekday and weekend day during peak season (May–September) and reduced frequency in other months.
- **[[Threadfin Shad]]** food habit analysis relied on conventional anterior gut examination (major food categories only) and an experimental **differential depletion of plankton organisms** offered to confined shad in polyethylene tanks — attempted only when small (<4") shad were available, which was not the case at time of writing.
- The project explicitly frames its ratio-based interpretation as an interim solution: *"If diverse communities... show similar G.P.P. to consumer relations it is reasonable to use these ratios in interpreting G.P.P. until better information is available."*
- Total estimated project cost: **$4,000.00**, funded 100% under the **Dingell-Johnson (D-J) Act**, covering salaries (150 man-days field/lab assistance), equipment (four 125-gallon stock tanks), vehicle operation (13,500 miles), and materials/supplies.
- Project timeline: December 1, 1959 – November 30, 1960, with completion report due November 30, 1960.
