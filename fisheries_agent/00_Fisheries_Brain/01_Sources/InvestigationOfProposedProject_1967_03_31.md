## Executive Summary

This 1966–1967 completion report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] documents interagency review of several water resource and land management projects and their impacts on fish and wildlife resources. Central to the report is the **[[Black River-Springerville-St. Johns Project]]**, a Bureau of Reclamation Colorado River diversion scheme involving three new dams/reservoirs on the [[Black River (Arizona)]], [[White River (Arizona)]], and [[Little Colorado River]]. The project would eliminate 11.5 miles of irreplaceable trout stream fishery while creating new trout lake fishing and waterfowl habitat. The report also evaluates the **Apache Junction-Gilbert Small Watershed Project**, the **Harquahala Valley Watershed Project**, and large-scale **phreatophyte (riparian vegetation) eradication programs** along the [[Gila River]], which threaten dove, quail, and upland game habitat, as well as an experimental introduction of the Afghan white-winged pheasant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Trout Stream/Reservoir System ([[Black River-Springerville-St. Johns Project]]):**
- Affected reaches: North Fork of the East Fork [[Black River (Arizona)]] (Crosby Crossing Dam site), [[White River (Arizona)]] below the confluence of Snake and Ord Creeks (Apache Dam site), and the [[Little Colorado River]] near Springerville (Bigelow Crossing Dam site).
- Three reservoirs proposed with variable capacity/surface area (see Key Findings table).
- Downstream flow releases identified as critical to sustaining stream fisheries and supporting operations of the **[[Alchesay National Fish Hatchery]]** below Apache Reservoir.
- Reservoirs expected to create new **lentic trout habitat**, offsetting loss of lotic (stream) trout habitat, though exact operational regimes were undetermined at time of reporting.
- Reservoirs would also serve as waterfowl nesting, feeding, and resting habitat (~850 additional man-days of waterfowl hunting estimated).

**Gila River Riparian Corridor (Phreatophyte Zone):**
- Dominant vegetation: salt cedar (*Tamarix* sp.) and mesquite (*Prosopis* sp.), forming a dense river-bottom habitat corridor from the [[San Carlos Indian Reservation]] to the Camelsback Dam site.
- Functions as critical nesting, roosting, and escape cover for [[Mourning Dove]] and [[White-Winged Dove]], quail, and rabbits; also frequented by deer, javelina, and occasional turkey and bear.
- Serves as a natural human-activity barrier, providing winter waterfowl refuge.
- Identified as key habitat for a newly introduced population of **Afghan white-winged pheasant**, which showed a habitat preference for river-bottom vegetation over adjacent agricultural land, using it as escape cover near feeding fields.

**Watershed Flood Control Structures (Apache Junction-Gilbert):**
- Ungated flood-control impoundments create only short-duration water retention (not permanent fish habitat).
- Cleared vegetation zones and periodic inundation expected to promote dense revegetation, enhancing upland game (not fish) habitat.

- **Fishery Impact:** ~11.5 miles of trout stream fishery would be inundated or severely degraded — described as an "irreplaceable" loss to White Mountain anglers.
- **Mitigation Recommendations** (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, concurred by AGFD):
  - Boating facilities/regulations planned for Bigelow and Crosby Crossing Reservoirs.
  - Minimum downstream flow releases required at all three dams to sustain stream fisheries and hatchery operations.
  - Portions of Crosby Crossing and Bigelow Crossing Reservoirs to be fenced as waterfowl production areas.
- **Waterfowl Benefit:** Estimated +850 man-days of hunting opportunity from new reservoir habitat.
- **Phreatophyte Eradication Impacts (Gila River):**
  - Ongoing U.S. Geological Survey study of water salvage from clearing 5,000 acres of phreatophytes on the San Carlos Indian Reservation (32% complete as of Feb. 1967).
  - Bureau of Indian Affairs to reseed cleared areas with Bermuda grass; controls resprouting via mowing.
  - Corps of Engineers Gila River Channel Improvement Project would clear vegetation from Camelsback Dam site to the San Carlos Indian Reservation, with clearing widths ranging from a few hundred feet (Safford) to 4,000 feet (near Ft. Thomas); mitigation plan proposes retaining a 100-ft vegetation buffer strip.
  - **Dove habitat loss quantified:** W-53-R nest transects (summer 1966) documented 6,607 acres of prime dove nesting habitat along the Gila River from Solomon to San Carlos Reservoir.
  - Clearing 3,232 acres of phreatophytic vegetation between Glenbar Center and the Highway 70 bridge alone projected to cause an annual loss of **140,000 mourning and white-winged doves**.
  - Full clearing project (Camelsback Dam site to San Carlos Lake) projected to cause a total annual loss of **193,000 doves**.
- **Species Introduction:** First release of Afghan white-winged pheasants into the Gila River Valley occurred in 1967; within five months, the population dispersed along 21 miles of river corridor, showing strong association with riparian vegetation over farmland — raising concern that phreatophyte removal would undermine establishment success.
- **Procedural Framework:** Standard interagency review process established between constructing agencies (Bureau of Reclamation, Corps of Engineers, Soil Conservation Service, Bureau of Indian Affairs), the [[U.S. Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]], and the Arizona Game and Fish Department for evaluating fish/wildlife impacts of proposed water and land development projects prior to Congressional authorization.
