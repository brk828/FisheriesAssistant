# Review of the Lanternfish Genus Lampadena with a Description of a New Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #myctophidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic review by [[Nafpaktitis]] and [[Paxton]] (Los Angeles County Museum of Natural History, *Contributions in Science* No. 138, 1968) revises the [[Lampadena]] genus of [[Myctophidae|Myctophid]] lanternfishes. The authors consolidate redescriptions of the genus's species, which had been scattered through the literature, and describe one new species from the southern hemisphere, *Lampadena notialis*. They synonymize *L. nitida* with *L. luminosa*, decline to recognize the subgenus *Lychnophora*, and show that otolith morphology differs among species and is useful for taxonomy. Species distributions are mapped from examined material.

## Background & Description

- **Genus status:** Prior to this work, *Lampadena* included nine described species assigned by [[Fraser-Brunner]] (1949) to two subgenera, *Lampadena* and *Lychnophora*. [[Bolin]] (1959) moved two species to *Taaningichthys* and synonymized *L. braueri* with *L. speculigera*. [[Paxton]] (1963) described *L. urophaos* from the eastern North Pacific.
- **Scope:** The authors aimed to gather detailed descriptions of all species in one place and survey the species occurring in southern oceans. The genus is described as uncommon.
- **Methods:** Specimens were mainly collected with a 10-ft [[Isaacs-Kidd Midwater Trawl]] (IKMT). Counts used the first gill arch, and measurements were expressed as percent of standard length. Vertebral counts came from X-rays. Otoliths were measured with an ocular micrometer, graphite-coated for sculpture detail, and photographed. Body photophore terminology follows Bolin (1939), and otolith terminology follows [[Frizzell and Dante]] (1965).
- **Morphology:** Species are relatively large myctophids with large, terminal to subterminal mouths and variable eye size. Key diagnostic traits include the elevation of PO1 (highly elevated only in *L. luminosa*), the number of gill rakers (total 13–16 in *L. luminosa* and *L. urophaos*, versus 17–26 in the other species), and the length and position of the supra- and infracaudal luminous glands. Dentition on the mesopterygoid and dentary also varies among species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution patterns:**
  - *L. luminosa*: mainly equatorial waters of all three oceans, between about 20°N and 20°S. Its occurrence in the Gulf of Mexico, Straits of Florida and high western North Atlantic latitudes is attributed to the [[Gulf Stream]]. A 150 mm specimen was reported from Japan.
  - *L. urophaos*: mostly southern California offshore waters; ranges 25°N–42°N in the central and eastern Pacific and 21°N–38°N in the western Atlantic.
  - *L. speculigera*: subtropical-temperate; 30°S–45°S in the Indian and Pacific Oceans, 35°N–45°N in the North Atlantic. Apparent absence from the South Atlantic is unconfirmed.
  - *L. dea*: southern parts of all three oceans, roughly 20°S–50°S.
  - *L. chavesi*: North Atlantic (about 30°N–45°N) and southern Indian and Pacific Oceans (about 30°S–40°S), indicating an antitropical distribution.
  - *L. notialis*: high southern latitudes.
  - *L. anomala*: insufficient data for horizontal distribution; apparently occurs deeper than congeners.
- **Depth and vertical migration:** Most species are among the deepest-dwelling myctophids. The two open-net captures of *L. anomala* were below 750 m. Shallow nighttime captures of large specimens indicating extensive vertical migration are known only for *L. luminosa* and *L. urophaos*. Small (20–35 mm) *L. speculigera*, *L. dea* and *L. chavesi* were taken at night in the upper 200 m.
- **Size:** *L. speculigera*, *L. notialis* and *L. luminosa* reach over 100 mm standard length.
- **Biology gaps:** Knowledge of lanternfish biology, including *Lampadena*, is described as meager.
- **Otolith-based predator record:** Otoliths of an undescribed *Lampadena* species were recovered from the stomach of a [[Pygmy Sperm Whale]] (*Kogia simus*) caught off Taiji, Japan.

## Key Findings

- **Synonymy:** *Lampadena nitida* (Taaning, 1928) is placed in the synonymy of *L. luminosa* (Garman, 1899). Variation in VO and AOa spacing was judged to be intraspecific, with no differences sufficient to justify even subspecies status.
- **New species:** *Lampadena notialis* sp. nov. is described from a holotype (LACM 11321-1, immature, 66.3 mm SL) collected at 42°00'S, 160°11'E, with two paratypes (105.0 mm male; 25.2 mm). The name derives from the Greek *notia* ("southern"). Diagnostic features include gill rakers 7–8+1+16–17 (total 24–26), VO 5(6), AOa 6, and a highly developed Vn.
- **Subgenus Lychnophora:** Not recognized. The authors found that the characters used to define it (eye diameter in head length; enlarged pterygoid teeth) are not diagnostic. They state that retaining or erecting subgenera adds little to understanding evolutionary trends.
- **Relationships (tentative):** *L. luminosa* and *L. urophaos* are clearly related and share low gill-raker counts and similar otolith shape. *L. speculigera* is most similar to *L. notialis*; *L. dea* is intermediate between these and *L. chavesi*. *L. chavesi* may be related to *Taaningichthys* through shared white iris tissue and infracaudal gland position. *L. anomala* appears to be evolving on a separate line.
- **Otoliths:** Otoliths show interspecific differences and are of taxonomic value. *L. luminosa* and *L. urophaos* have large, elongated otoliths (length:height 1.7:1–1.8:1), while *L. speculigera*, *L. notialis*, *L. dea* and *L. chavesi* have oval otoliths (1.3:1–1.5:1). *L. anomala* has a small otolith (1.2:1), and *Taaningichthys bathyphilus* is nearly round (1.0:1).
- **Standard length to otolith length ratios:** *L. luminosa* 13.1:1; *L. notialis* 16.9:1; *L. speculigera* and *L. dea* 17.4:1; *L. chavesi* 20.6:1; *L. anomala* 26.7:1.
- **Material examined:** Specimens came from numerous [[R/V Anton Bruun]], [[R/V Atlantis II]], [[R/V Chain]], [[USNS Eltanin]] and other cruise stations, with the largest series for *L. luminosa* (for example, 41 specimens from a single station, 18.0–26.0 mm).
- **Key taxon-level distinctions:**
  - *L. speculigera* shows two hemisphere trends: southern individuals have 6+4 AO and 7+1+13–14 gill rakers, while North Atlantic individuals have 7+3 AO and 6+1+12–13. The authors considered the populations conspecific pending larger samples, despite otolith differences in one 126 mm North Atlantic specimen.
  - *L. chavesi* has the longest caudal glands in the genus, white luminous-looking iris tissue, and a flat, ventrally restricted infracaudal gland.
  - *L. anomala* is known from only two specimens (about 48 mm); the description is based on the poorly preserved Woods Hole specimen.
- **Measurement summary (Table 1):** Ranges of percent SL for eye diameter, caudal peduncle length and depth, preanal distance, and caudal gland lengths are given per species. For example, *L. luminosa* eye diameter 7.3–9.4% SL, and *L. speculigera* infracaudal gland 13.2–15.4% SL.
