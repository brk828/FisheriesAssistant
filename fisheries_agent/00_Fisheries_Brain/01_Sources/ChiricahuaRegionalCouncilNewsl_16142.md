# Native Fish Restoration in West Turkey Creek

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary
This report details a multi-agency native fish restoration project in **West Turkey Creek**, on the west slope of the [[Chiricahua Mountains]], Arizona. The project, initiated in November 1998 by the Forest Service, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Arizona and New Mexico Game and Fish Departments, Arizona State University, and the El Coronado Ranch, aimed to eradicate the invasive, non-native **[[Fathead Minnow]]** to protect and restore populations of two native fishes: the **[[Yaqui Longfin Dace]]** and the endangered **[[Yaqui Chub]]**. The effort combined piscicide treatment, temporary translocation of native fish, and watershed sediment-control engineering to protect long-term habitat quality.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide detailed morphological descriptions (e.g., meristic counts, coloration, body shape) for the [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Longfin Dace]], or [[Fathead Minnow]]. These species are identified functionally by their conservation status and ecological role:

- **[[Yaqui Chub]]**: A federally endangered cyprinid native to the Rio Yaqui drainage system, historically found in [[West Turkey Creek]].
- **[[Yaqui Longfin Dace]]**: A native, currently non-listed but co-occurring cyprinid species sharing the same habitat and conservation concern.
- **[[Fathead Minnow]]**: A hardy, widely-introduced non-native cyprinid known for its resilience and invasive tendencies, likely introduced via bait-bucket release.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[West Turkey Creek]] and its tributaries, plus associated stock ponds on private land within the drainage, situated on the west slope of the [[Chiricahua Mountains]].
- **Historical Disturbance**: Flooding following the **1994 [[Rattlesnake Wildfire]]** naturally scoured non-native fish from the creek, allowing native [[Yaqui Chub]] and [[Yaqui Longfin Dace]] populations to persist unchallenged for several years.
- **Invasion Event**: In 1997, non-native **[[Fathead Minnow]]** populations were discovered both in West Turkey Creek and in nearby private stock tanks, posing a significant competitive/predatory threat capable of eliminating native fish populations.
- **Co-occurring Fauna**: Frogs, tadpoles, turtles, and aquatic invertebrates share this habitat; piscicide treatment protocols were specifically vetted to avoid harming these non-target taxa.
- **Anthropogenic Stressors**: Sediment runoff from an adjacent Forest Service road was identified as an additional, non-biological threat to native fish habitat quality.

## Key Findings
- **Translocation for Safekeeping**: In May 1999, native fish populations were relocated to stock ponds on private property for temporary protection prior to treatment.
- **Piscicide Treatment**: West Turkey Creek was treated twice (May and June 1999) with the piscicide **antimycin** to eliminate [[Fathead Minnow]].
- **Non-Target Safety Confirmed**: Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]], ichthyologist at Arizona State University, confirmed to the [[Chiricahua Regional Council]] that antimycin treatment would not harm frogs, tadpoles, or turtles. Post-treatment monitoring found no adverse effects on these taxa or on aquatic invertebrates.
- **Successful Captive Reproduction**: Sequestered native fish populations successfully reproduced in the stock ponds during the safekeeping period, indicating suitable habitat conditions and reproductive viability.
- **Sediment Mitigation Engineering**: A rapid-response Forest Service project was designed to reduce road-derived sediment threats along a 2.5-mile stretch of road paralleling the creek, including:
  - Paving approaches to three bridges
  - Replacing a vehicle stream crossing with a bridge
  - Surfacing the road with soil-stabilized crushed rock
  - Diverting road runoff and culvert outflow into sediment collection basins
  - Installing loose rock check dams in gullies
  - Adding culverts/drainage structures to redirect runoff away from the creek
  - Using curbing/ditches to route campground runoff into settling basins
  - Reseeding disturbed areas with native plant species
- **Contingency for Reinfestation**: A second antimycin/permanganate treatment was anticipated as possibly necessary if Fathead Minnow persistence was detected before native fish reintroduction.
- **Planned Reintroduction**: Yaqui Chub and Yaqui Longfin Dace were slated for release back into West Turkey Creek once fishery biologists confirmed complete Fathead Minnow eradication and the sediment reduction project was completed.
- **Related Watershed Restoration Context**: The success of loose rock check dams in the [[West Turkey Creek]] watershed was cited elsewhere in the newsletter as a model for broader watershed restoration practices under the forthcoming revised [[Coronado National Forest Plan]].
- **Complementary Fire Management**: The Forest Service planned approximately 4600 acres of low-intensity prescribed fire near West Turkey Creek over multiple years specifically to reduce silt and ash loading into the creek, supporting long-term native fish habitat quality.
