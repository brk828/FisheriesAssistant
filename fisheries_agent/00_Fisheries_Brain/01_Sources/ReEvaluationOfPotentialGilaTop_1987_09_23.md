## Executive Summary
This document is a 1987 interagency memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) to the Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Arizona, concerning the re-evaluation of potential reintroduction sites for the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) on BLM-administered lands. The memo does not present primary morphological or ecological field data; rather, it documents a decade-long interagency effort to reestablish populations of this endangered fish and advocates for reintroducing the species under full protection of the [[Endangered Species Act]] rather than as an "experimental nonessential" population, citing a proven track record on [[U.S. Forest Service]] lands over the preceding five years.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Aquatic habitats on lands managed by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] and, comparably, the [[U.S. Forest Service]] in Arizona — implying small desert springs, streams, or similar lentic/lotic freshwater systems typical of the historic range of the [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Geographic context**: Arizona waters, within the broader range associated with the [[Gila River]] drainage system in the American Southwest.
- **Conservation ecology**: The species is treated as highly sensitive to habitat conditions, with reintroduction site selection guided by a "tested set of site evaluation criteria" developed through prior stocking efforts, used to rank candidate sites by their potential for long-term population success.
- **Population strategy**: Multiple discrete reintroduced populations are being pursued as a hedge against localized failure, reflecting standard practice in small-fish conservation biology for distributing extinction risk across sites — relevant to concepts of [[Metapopulation Conservation]] and [[Endangered Species Reintroduction]].

## Key Findings
- The [[Gila Topminnow]] is classified as **endangered** under the Endangered Species Act at the time of writing (1987), with active efforts underway toward **downlisting to threatened status** and eventual delisting (full recovery).
- For approximately **10 years**, USFWS and BLM had attempted to establish a stocking/reintroduction mechanism for the species on BLM lands, paralleling an ongoing and successful [[U.S. Forest Service]] reintroduction program active for the preceding **5 years**.
- An amendment to the [[Endangered Species Act]] permitting designation of **"experimental nonessential" populations** was seen as a potential pathway to enable reintroduction on BLM lands; a proposed rule listing candidate sites under this designation was drafted and submitted to the Washington FWS office, then returned for revision.
- USFWS recommended an alternative approach: reintroducing topminnows under **full protection** of the Act (rather than experimental nonessential status), citing three main justifications:
  1. A validated **site evaluation criteria system** exists to rank reintroduction sites by likelihood of long-term success.
  2. Fully protected status would confer **additional legal protections** to reintroduction sites under the Act.
  3. Full-protection stockings could begin **immediately (fall of that year)**, giving populations at least a **one-year head start** over sites awaiting publication of a final experimental nonessential rule.
- Establishing additional populations was viewed as directly supporting the case for **downlisting** the species and providing **redundancy/security** against the risk of individual population failures.
- The memo indicates collaboration with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (led by Dr. W.L. Minckley) as key stakeholders in the reintroduction planning process.
- USFWS offered to assist BLM directly in **evaluating candidate reintroduction sites** and requested a decision on which regulatory pathway (full protection vs. experimental nonessential) BLM would prefer to pursue.
