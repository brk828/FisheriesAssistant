# I Congreso Nacional de Ictiología: Program and Abstract Index (La Paz, B.C.S., 1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #teleostei

## Executive Summary

This document is the program and abstract index of the **I Congreso Nacional de Ictiología**, held in La Paz, Baja California Sur, Mexico, from 8 to 11 November 1988. The congress was organized by the [[Sociedad Ictiologica Mexicana]] (SIMAC) with the [[Universidad Autonoma De Baja California Sur]] (UABCS). It includes the event schedule, sponsors, organizing committee, and an index of roughly 120 contributed talks and posters. The index covers taxonomy, reproductive biology, feeding ecology, population dynamics, fisheries, ichthyoplankton, freshwater fish, and ichthyological collections. It contains no full results, so the findings below are topic-level and drawn from titles and authors only.

## Background & Description

The congress ran over four days in two parallel auditoria, "A" (Marine Sciences Building) and "B" (Social Sciences Building). Registration and the opening were held in the Marine Sciences Building, and poster sessions in the UABCS library. Sessions were moderated by researchers from institutions including the [[Centro Interdisciplinario De Ciencias Marinas]] (CICIMAR), the [[Centro Regional De Investigacion Pesquera]] (CRIP-La Paz), and the [[Centro De Investigaciones Biologicas]] (CIB). The meeting closed with a round table and a closing banquet.

**Organizing committee**
- Honorary presidents: Pedro Ojeda Paullada (Secretary of Fisheries) and José Eduardo Ruiz Castro (Rector, UABCS).
- President: Salvador Contreras Balderas (SIMAC).
- Executive secretary: Enrique Gonzalez Navarro (Interdisciplinary Area of Marine Sciences, UABCS).

**Sponsors:** the Government of Baja California Sur, the UABCS computing program, a regional bottling company, and Cervecería Cuauhtémoc.

**Attendance guidelines:** speakers were asked to submit slides at least 30 minutes before their talk, confirm presence with the session moderator, and wear badges for event access.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The contributed works cover a wide range of Mexican aquatic systems:

- **Pacific and Gulf of California:** [[Bahia De La Paz]], [[Bahia Magdalena]], Bahia Almejas, the Ensenada de La Paz, Cerralvo, Bahia Vizcaino, the Punta Banda estuary, the coast of Baja California Sur, and deep-water fish records.
- **Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean:** Campeche-Tabasco, the Sonda de Campeche, Tecolutla estuary, Veracruz lagoons (Pueblo Viejo, El Farallon, Tamiahua-Tampamachoco), [[Cenotes Of Yucatan]], Isla Contoy, and Cabo Pulmo reef.
- **Freshwater:** Lake Chapala (Jalisco), Lake Patzcuaro (Michoacan), Lake Zempoala (Morelos), Lerma-Chapala basin, Rio Amacuzac, Rio Turbio-Silao (Guanajuato), Sonoran rivers, and reservoirs in Hidalgo and Nuevo Leon.
- **Lagoons and estuaries:** Huizache-Caimanero (Sinaloa), Gulf of Tehuantepec lagoons, Yavaros Bay (Sonora), and Celestun (Yucatan).

Several papers address environmental and anthropogenic factors. These include temperature effects on marine finfish production in Baja California and Oaxaca (1968-1985), lead levels in water and fish in the Gulf of California, and predation by introduced [[Largemouth Bass]] on Chirostoma (silversides) in Lake Patzcuaro. Competition from introduced rainbow trout on a native goodeid in Morelos is also covered.

## Key Findings

The index is organized by thematic area. Titles and authors are reported as listed; no quantitative results are given in the source.

### 1. Taxonomy, Systematics and Morphology
- Species delimitation within the thread herring genus *Opisthonema* in northwestern Mexico, using gill-raker characters (R. Rodriguez).
- Determination of anchovy species (Engraulidae) in the Gulf of Mexico (F.J. Abarca), and ontogeny of the chondrocranium in *Engraulis japonicus* (E.F. Balart).
- Recognition of *Codoma* as a monotypic cyprinid genus, with its geographic variation and evolution (S. Contreras and W.L. Minckley).
- Descriptions of new species: a Proserpinus-rutilus complex cyprinid from the Nazas River, Durango, and a new *Astyanax* characin from Chiapas (Contreras and Lozano).
- Taxonomic considerations on Otolithinae croakers (Sciaenidae) on the Pacific coast of Mexico (C. Rosales and P. Fuentes).
- Genetic variation in *Opisthonema* populations along the coast of Baja California Sur (A. Lagunez).
- Comparative swim bladder form and function in clupeoid fishes (P.J.P. Whitehead), and evolutionary trends within *Lile* (Clupeidae) (J.M. Vivero and J.L. Castro).
- Osteology and sexual dimorphism in *Astyanax fasciatus*: males show anal and pelvic fin spinules as a secondary sexual trait (H. Mejia).

### 2. Reproduction and Early Life History
- Reproductive cycles and fecundity of the northern anchovy (*Engraulis mordax*) in its central subpopulation, comparing 1981-1985 (C.A. Cotero et al.).
- Reproductive activity of the Pacific sardine (*Sardinops sagax*) over seven years in Bahia Magdalena, and spawning frequency in the Gulf of California (J.R. Torres et al.).
- Comparison of reproductive patterns of *Sardinops sagax* and *Opisthonema libertate* in Bahia Magdalena (Torres et al.), and egg abundance, larval survival and fecundity of *Opisthonema libertate* in the same bay (R. Vera et al.).
- Transport and early survival of Pacific sardine larvae in the Gulf of California (M.G. Hammann), and larval mortality of *S. sagax* and *Vinciguerria lucetia* (M.J. Haro).
- Allometric growth in early larvae of three subtropical marine species (J.L. Ortiz) and larval digestive tract structure in *Opisthonema* (M.S. Munaton and R.I. Ochoa).
- Reproduction in *Lutjanus synagris* off Veracruz in 1983 (R. Torres et al.), and in *Scarus* parrotfishes of Baja California Sur (M. Villarejo and R.I. Ochoa).
- Larval development of *Cichlasoma istlanum* (E. Contreras) and reproduction of *Gambusia affinis* in a Nuevo Leon reservoir (M. Mireles).

### 3. Age, Growth and Population Dynamics
- Age and growth of the red porgy-type *Calamus campechanus* and *C. bajonado* in the Campeche Sound (M. Garduno; A. Lopez).
- Age and growth of Pacific chub mackerel (*Scomber japonicus*) from Bahia Vizcaino (Ma.G. Gluyas).
- Growth patterns from scales, otoliths and dorsal spines of several species, using calcified rings (M. Kimberly et al.).
- Growth, mortality and exploitation rate of *Lutjanus synagris* off Yucatan, 1983-1985 (R. Torres and S. Salas), and growth and mortality of mullet and leather-jacket in Gulf of Tehuantepec lagoons (S. Ramos-Cruz).
- Population dynamics of *Chirostoma luctus* in Lake Chapala (J.A. Menchaca), and growth of tilapia (*Oreochromis aureus*) in Amela Lagoon, Colima (F. Ascencio and A.M. Zabala).
- Length-weight relationships and otolith length-body length relationships for several Baja California fishes, with applications to seabird and marine mammal diet studies (D. Aurioles).

### 4. Feeding Ecology and Trophic Relations
- Food habits of three commercially important species in Bahia Concepcion (A. Abitia et al.), and of yellowtail snapper and Pacific graysby in Bahia de La Paz (G. Garcia and J.M. Orozco).
- Diet of the bullseye puffer (*Sphoeroides annulatus*) in Bahia de La Paz (J.R. Turrubiates), and of Pacific rainbow trout in Lake Zempoala (J.A. Viana).
- Trophic analysis of *Chirostoma jordani* in the Requena Reservoir, Hidalgo (C. Escudero), and feeding of coastal fish communities in Michoacan (V.H. Aguirre et al.).
- Predator-prey relations between fishes and shrimp in the Huizache-Caimanero lagoon system (A.J. Taboada).

### 5. Community Ecology, Distribution and Abundance
- Ichthyofauna and community structure: Puerto Viejo lagoon (Veracruz), El Farallon lagoon (Veracruz), three estuaries of Bahia de La Paz, Contoy Island (Quintana Roo), the rocky intertidal zone of northwestern Baja California, Cabo Pulmo reef, and the coastal communities of Michoacan.
- Ecological parameters of Yucatan cenote fish communities, with a comparison to Contoy reef ichthyofauna (M. Navarro).
- Distribution and abundance of flatfish species (*Paralichthys californicus*, *Xystreurys*, *Hippoglossina*) on the west coast of Baja California Sur (M.A. Martinez and J.L. Castro-Aguirre).

### 6. Fisheries and Management
- Commercial catch of fish in Campeche-Tabasco, covering species, seasons, recruitment and reproduction.
- Captures of marine fish in Mexico from 1970-1982 (M. Vazquez and M. Siri), and a checklist of marine fish names from the northwestern Mexican coast (R. Escobar and M. Siri).
- Longline fisheries: billfish and other species landed at Manzanillo (H. Santana et al.), and effort and catch distribution of the longliner *Tiburon II* in the Mexican Pacific, 1966-1987 (R. Veleaz et al.).
- Spanish mackerel migration in the Gulf of Mexico (J. Vasconcelos et al.) and biology of Sierra (*Scomberomorus sierra*) and Spanish mackerel (*S. maculatus*) in Nayarit and the Gulf of Mexico.
- Fishery of Chapala Lake: characterization of the fisheries of tilapia, carp and whitefish, and an analysis of the commercial exploitation of catfish (J.J. Davila).
- A **proposal to modify the closed season for mullet** (*Mugil cephalus*) on the coasts of Oaxaca and Chiapas (S. Ramos-Cruz). This is the only regulatory item in the index.
- Efficiency of the Pacific sardine fleet in the Gulf of California, 1983-1986 (G. Ponce and R. Rodriguez).

### 7. Freshwater Aquaculture and Pathology
- Growth evaluation of three carp species in production ponds in Morelos, using the Polychou model (J.G. Granados et al.).
- Hematology of *Oreochromis urolepis hornorum* across growth stages (M. Villasenor), and aquaculture as a factor of social change for women (M.C. Regis).
- Parasites: a review of parasitic isopods of Mexican marine fishes (J.R. Campos), and a new definitive host record for a cestode in the skate *Raja binoculata* off Baja California Sur (C. Alarcon and J.L. Castro).

### 8. Collections and Ichthyological Infrastructure
- The ichthyological collections of the UNAM Institute of Biology (P. Fuentes and J. Luna) and CICIMAR in La Paz (F. Galvan et al.) are described, along with a general overview of Mexican ichthyology (H. Espinosa).

## Summary of Significance

The index shows the breadth of Mexican ichthyology in 1988, with strong representation of the Gulf of California, the Gulf of Mexico, and Yucatan cenotes. Two items deserve particular attention: the description of new freshwater species in Durango and Chiapas, and the regulatory proposal on mullet closed seasons. Several species appear repeatedly across multiple talks, including *Sardinops sagax*, *Opisthonema libertate*, *Lutjanus synagris* and *Mugil cephalus*, which reflects the importance of these taxa to Mexican fisheries and research at the time.
