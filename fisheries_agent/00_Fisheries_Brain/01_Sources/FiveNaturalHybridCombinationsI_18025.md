# Five Natural Hybrid Combinations in Minnows (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 University of Kansas Museum of Natural History paper by [[Frank B. Cross]] and W. L. Minckley describes five natural hybrid combinations among [[Cyprinidae]] minnows, with morphological comparisons to each parental species. Four of the five hybrids were taken during a severe drought (1953–1956) in Kansas and Arkansas, from small spring-fed streams with dense fish populations. The authors attribute most of these hybrids to crowding during spawning, which increased the chance of cross-fertilization. They also suggest that recent stream alteration, including overgrazing, contributed. The hybrids are generally intermediate between their parents in meristic, proportional, and pigmentation characters, though some show deformities (notably in pharyngeal teeth) or enlarged heads.

## Background & Description

The paper reports five hybrid combinations:

- [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]] × [[Notropis Cornutus Frontalis]] (three specimens)
- [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] (one specimen)
- [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] (three specimens, plus one from Nebraska)
- [[Gila Nigrescens]] × [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]] (one specimen)
- [[Notropis Venustus Venustus]] × [[Notropis Whipplei]] (one specimen)

Two of these combinations had been mentioned previously in the literature without descriptions. [[Hubbs]] (1955) graphically indicated hybridization within the same genera but did not name the species involved.

Specimens were recognized as unusual at capture and were saved as part of numerically selective samples, not found in random laboratory sorting. Measurements followed the methods of [[Hubbs And Lagler]] (1958) and are expressed as thousandths of the larger dimension (standard length or head length).

**Key morphological comparisons:**

- **Head size:** In the [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]] × [[Notropis Cornutus Frontalis]] and [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] hybrids, the head is larger than in parental fish of similar size, mainly from elongation of the postorbital region. Eye size, upper jaw length, and gape width are intermediate.
- **Intestine:** In [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]], the gut is long and coiled, crossing the mid-ventral line eight to nine times. In [[Notropis Cornutus Frontalis]], it forms a flat S-shaped loop that does not cross the midline. The largest hybrids cross four times, and the smallest crosses twice.
- **Pigmentation:** The hybrids are generally intermediate. The mid-lateral band is broader and fainter than in [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]] but better developed than in [[Notropis Cornutus Frontalis]].
- **Pharyngeal teeth:** Several hybrids show abnormal tooth arrangement, including supernumerary teeth and irregular alignment. One arch bore only three teeth plus a pit, and another bore eight.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hybrid collections came from a period of drought. [[Minckley]] and [[Cross]] (1959) documented its strong effects on Kansas stream habitats. Suitable spawning sites were probably scarce, but adult fish from earlier wet years remained abundant. The authors argue that this crowding increased cross-fertilization. Hybridization is also reported more often in areas with major climatic change over the past 20,000 years or fewer, and in streams recently altered by human activity. The streams with hybrids were probably affected by [[Overgrazing]] of their watersheds, which was unusually severe during the drought.

**Stream settings described:**

- **[[Deep Creek]] and [[Bluff Creek]]:** Clear, gravel-bottomed streams draining parts of the [[Flint Hills]] of Kansas. Earlier surveys of these streams are cited.
- **[[Mill Creek]] (Wabaunsee County, Kansas):** A clear stream similar to Deep and Bluff creeks but somewhat larger. It had an exceptionally large fish population when the hybrid was collected. Two other documented crosses were also found there.
- **[[Timber Creek]] (Scott County, Kansas):** A small, spring-fed, sandy-bottomed tributary of Scott County State Lake, less than 10 feet wide and six inches deep except in three pools. The hybrids were found in two of these pools, along with numerous [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] and one adult [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]].
- **[[Winters Creek]] (Arkansas):** Flowed about five cubic feet per second at collection. A landowner reported that it had been dry except for pools in the two previous summers. The habitat was mainly short riffles (average depth four inches) and pools to two feet deep. Twelve fish species were found, including [[Notropis Whipplei]] but not [[Notropis Lutrensis]] or [[Notropis Venustus Venustus]].
- **Rio Grande (Bernalillo County, New Mexico):** Source of the [[Gila Nigrescens]] × [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]] hybrid. The authors had no information on stream conditions at this site.

## Key Findings

1. **Five hybrid combinations are documented.** Specimen counts are one to three per combination, with the exception of the [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] cross, which includes three specimens from Kansas and one from Nebraska.

2. **Crowding during drought is the proposed mechanism.** Four hybrids (all except [[Gila Nigrescens]] × [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]]) are attributed to crowded spawning in drought-stressed, spring-fed streams.

3. **Hybrids are generally intermediate, with notable exceptions.** The [[Chrosomus Erythrogaster]] × [[Notropis Cornutus Frontalis]] hybrid has an enlarged head, and proportional measures shift toward one parent or the other depending on the divisor used.

4. **Pharyngeal dentition is abnormal in several hybrids.** The authors suggest tooth replacement failure as a cause, citing [[Evans And Deubler]] (1955), who found three rows of teeth in two of 150 [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] specimens.

5. **Hybrid index values quantify intermediacy.** For [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]], the hybrid index is 55.7, with [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]] set at 0. For [[Gila Nigrescens]] × [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]], the index is 49.7 per cent across six characters, with [[Gila Nigrescens]] set at 0.

6. **The [[Gila Nigrescens]] × [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]] hybrid is supported by snout and mouth features.** The frenum width is 8 mm in [[Rhinichthys Cataractae]] at 60 mm standard length, absent in [[Gila Nigrescens]], and 1.7 mm in the hybrid. The hybrid also has an intermediate lateral line, a dorsal fin height above the parental average, and a weak mid-dorsal dark streak.

7. **The [[Notropis Venustus Venustus]] × [[Notropis Whipplei]] hybrid has a mixed character set.** It has the 9-rayed anal fin of [[Notropis Whipplei]] and a diffuse basicaudal spot like [[Notropis Venustus Venustus]]. Its body depths are intermediate, and its pigmentation and snout shape suggest [[Notropis Whipplei]] more than [[Notropis Lutrensis]] × [[Notropis Venustus Venustus]] hybrids, which are deeper-bodied.

8. **The paper also reports a new Nebraska record.** A [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] specimen (KU 4841, 39.3 mm standard length) was taken in the [[North Platte River]] at Lisco, Garden County, Nebraska, on September 11, 1959. It has 7 anal rays and 52 lateral line scales.

9. **Some resemblances are coincidental.** The authors note that the [[Campostoma Anomalum Plumbeum]] × [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] hybrids partly resemble [[Hybopsis Biguttata]], but other characters rule out a relationship with that species.
