# Yampa River Channel Monitoring (FY-93)

## Executive Summary
This FY-93 Colorado River Recovery Program report documents a channel-morphology monitoring study on the [[Yampa River]] designed to validate and test the "Yampa Model" — a flow/sediment process model developed to describe formation and maintenance of [[Colorado Squawfish]] spawning bars. Fieldwork was conducted August 25–29, 1993 at two known spawning bar sites, Mathers Hole (RM 17.5) and Cleopatra's Couch (RM 16.5), during low-flow conditions (350–400 cfs) following an unusually high 1993 runoff year. The study successfully re-surveyed cross-sections established in 1983 and 1991, produced new topographic data for future HEC-2 hydraulic/sediment modeling, and generated preliminary evidence that high 1993 flows caused aggradation (bar-building) at Cleopatra's Couch. The project was a cooperative effort among the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the Colorado River Water Conservation District, the National Park Service, and Resource Consultants & Engineers.

## Background & Description
- **Species of concern:** [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), an endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River Basin, which relies on specific geomorphic sandbar/cobble bar configurations for spawning.
- **Study origin:** Builds on a previously developed "Yampa Model," a flow/sediment-transport model created to explain physical (micro-habitat) conditions at a known Colorado squawfish spawning bar at River Mile (RM) 16.5 in Yampa Canyon, referred to as **Cleopatra's Couch**.
- **Second site:** RM 17.5, known as **Mathers Hole**, where squawfish spawning congregations have also been documented; this site lacked baseline model validation prior to this study.
- **Historical monitoring lineage:** Cross-sections at these sites were originally established by O'Brian in 1983, resurveyed in 1991 under the Channel Monitoring Program, and resurveyed again in this 1993 study to detect physical changes.
- **Study design:** An interdisciplinary field team (many members carried over from the 1991 survey for methodological continuity) conducted ground surveys using an Electronic Distance Measurement (EDM) total station to build detailed topographic maps and cross-sectional profiles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Freshwater canyon river channel — specifically low-flow sand/gravel spawning bars in [[Yampa Canyon]], a critical spawning habitat type for the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]].
- **Hydrological context:** 1993 was a high-runoff year; the study specifically aimed to assess whether the elevated spring flows built up ("aggregated"/aggraded) the spawning bars and flushed fine sediment (sand/silt) known to impair spawning success.
- **Sediment dynamics:** The presence of excess sand/silt on spawning bars is understood to be detrimental to Colorado squawfish reproductive success, underscoring the ecological importance of high-flow "flushing" events for maintaining suitable spawning substrate.
- **Access constraints:** Extremely low flow conditions (350–400 cfs) during the August survey window necessitated helicopter access to the remote canyon sites (transport from Mantle Ranch to Mathers Hole), highlighting the logistical challenges of monitoring remote riverine spawning habitat.
- **Basin-wide relevance:** Findings tie into the broader [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] framework (RIPRAP sections I.B.1 and I.B.2.a) aimed at recovering endangered Colorado River Basin fishes through flow management.

## Key Findings
- **Cross-section surveys:** 13 cross-sections were established and measured at **Mathers Hole (RM 17.5)**; a full topographic map of the bar and river channel was created using EDM total station survey methods.
- **Model applicability confirmed:** The "Yampa Model" was shown capable of describing physical conditions at RM 17.5, extending its validated utility beyond the original RM 16.5 site — suggesting the model may help identify other potential Colorado squawfish spawning sites in the basin.
- **Cleopatra's Couch (RM 16.5) resurvey:** Previously established 1991 cross-sections were relocated and resurveyed using EDM; topographic data was collected at lesser resolution than at Mathers Hole.
- **Aggradation detected:** Preliminary analysis indicated the bar at Cleopatra's Couch experienced general aggradation (sediment build-up) following the high 1993 flows, with variable magnitude of change across different cross-sections.
- **Model validation:** The study helped validate assumptions underlying the original "Yampa Model" at RM 16.5 and demonstrated its transferability to RM 17.5.
- **Data application:** Cross-section and topographic data are intended as direct inputs into the **HEC-2 hydraulic model** to predict water surface elevations and sediment transport characteristics at both sites.
- **Budget/execution:** Of $31,000 in combined funds ($6,000 Recovery Program + $25,000 River District), $27,475 was expended; the effort was substantially supported through District funding and in-kind FWS/NPS personnel and equipment contributions (helicopter charter: $2,475).
- **Recommendation:** The "Yampa Model" should be tested at additional sites with differing geological controls to assess broader applicability across the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Programmatic note:** The work exemplifies a successful interdisciplinary, multi-agency cooperative approach (FWS, NPS, River District, and private engineering consultants) to endangered fish habitat monitoring.
