# Glen Canyon Dam Beach/Habitat Building Test Flow 1996 - Schedule and Research Proposals

#hydrology #dam-management #habitat-restoration #experimental-design

## Executive Summary

This document contains administrative correspondence and planning materials for the 1996 [[Glen Canyon Dam]] experimental high flow release, designed as a beach/habitat building test. The primary document is a schedule revision memo dated January 9, 1996, from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] group, providing detailed hourly flow schedules for the experimental release. The package includes a revised flow schedule showing a spike flow reaching 45,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) and peer review comments on associated research proposals covering sediment transport, fisheries, aquatic ecosystems, and riparian vegetation responses.

## Background & Description

The experimental flow was designed to test habitat-building and sediment redistribution effects in the [[Colorado River]] downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The flow schedule shows:

- Baseline flow: 8,000 cfs (March 22-26, 1996)
- Ramp-up phase: 4,000 cfs/hour increments from 8,000 to 45,000 cfs (March 26, starting at 0200 hours)
- Peak flow: 45,000 cfs maintained for approximately 168 hours (7 days, March 26 - April 2)
- Ramp-down phase: Initially 1,500 cfs/hour, then 1,000 cfs/hour, then 500 cfs/hour decrements
- Return to baseline: 8,000 cfs by April 4, maintained through April 8

The experimental design involved coordinated studies across multiple disciplines including sediment transport, debris fan dynamics, beach surveys, camping area assessments, fisheries displacement, aquatic drift, and riparian vegetation responses. Research teams included [[USGS]], [[Arizona Game and Fish]], [[National Park Service]], and university researchers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The experimental flow was conducted within the [[Glen Canyon]] reach of the Colorado River, a highly regulated system where dam operations control downstream hydrology, sediment dynamics, and aquatic/riparian habitat conditions. Key ecological contexts include:

**Sediment Dynamics**: The flow targeted sediment redistribution from the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) and [[Paria River]] tributaries, which provide sand inputs to the mainstem. Research focused on how aggraded debris fans would be reworked and whether sand deposits would be mobilized and redeposited as beaches.

**Fisheries**: Studies examined displacement effects on both native species (particularly [[Humpback Chub]], with 10 tagged individuals monitored near the LCR) and non-native species. Hypotheses tested whether displacement during the 45,000 cfs flood differed from displacement during typical high flows (25,000 cfs).

**Aquatic Food Web**: Drift studies at LCR and Hells Hollow sites examined changes in invertebrate drift and food availability, with sampling planned before, during, and after the flow event. Temperature effects on oxygen and CO2 measurements in [[Lake Powell]] tailwater were noted as experimental challenges.

**Riparian Vegetation**: Studies examined responses across variable sample units (3-10 per polygon), though reviewers noted need for clarification on statistical treatment of variable sample sizes.

## Key Findings

**Flow Schedule Details**:
- Total experimental period: 407 hours (approximately 17 days, March 22 - April 8, 1996)
- Peak discharge: 45,000 cfs
- Average daily flows: 8,000 cfs (baseline), 37,083 cfs (ramp-up day), 45,000 cfs (peak days), 39,021 cfs (first ramp-down day), 18,188 cfs and 8,583 cfs (subsequent ramp-down days)
- Ramp rates varied: 4,000 cfs/hour (up), 1,500 cfs/hour (initial down), 1,000 cfs/hour (mid down), 500 cfs/hour (final down)

**Research Design Issues Identified** (from peer review by Duncan Patten):

*Sediment Transport*: Questions raised about tributary sediment inflow gaging methodology and whether debris fan sampling would capture overtopping dynamics important to eddy studies.

*Beach Surveys*: Apparent conflict between pre/post flood measurements and stated goals to measure scour rates on rising limb; methodology for real-time measurements unclear.

*Camping Areas*: Need for standardized definitions of "sleeping area" and "kitchen area" sizes to ensure consistency.

*Fisheries*: Sample size concerns regarding 10 tagged [[Humpback Chub]] for statistical testing; confusion about whether same fish would be tagged at LCR and recaptured in eddy complexes; unclear time frames for "over time" analyses; stomach content sampling planned before and during but not after flood (when food sources would remain altered).

*Drift Studies*: Suggestion to use dye study data for more accurate time frame adjustments when analyzing drift samples from LCR and Hells Hollow.

*Lake Powell Studies*: Temperature effects on both O2 and CO2 measurements acknowledged as experimental challenges.

*Riparian Studies*: Variable sample unit ranges (3-10) required clarification for legitimate statistical treatment.

**Administrative Actions**: Schedule revision distributed January 9, 1996, to Beach/Habitat-Building Test Flow Planning Group with instructions to check logistical plans against corrected flow schedule.
