# Section 6 Amended Job Description: Razorback Sucker and Colorado Squawfish Reintroduction and Monitoring, Arizona (1991-1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an amended Section 6 project job description and related correspondence from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). It amends Project E5-3, Job 7, Segment 3, which covers survey, monitoring, and reintroduction of threatened and endangered fishes in Arizona. The amendment was prompted by the November listing of the [[Razorback Sucker]] as an endangered species. It adds federal-aid coverage for field work on razorback suckers alongside the existing [[Colorado Squawfish]] program. The job runs from July 1991 through June 1992, with a total cost of $90,160 ($67,620 federal, $22,540 state).

The document is administrative rather than research-based. It reports no population counts, survey results, or statistics.

## Background & Description

The project is funded under [[Section 6 Of The Endangered Species Act]] through the federal-aid framework of the [[Federal Aid In Sport Fish Restoration Act]] and related programs. The project fits within the [[Endangered Species Program]] and is linked to the [[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan]] (draft, USFWS 1988).

Two species are targeted:

- **Razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**: a catostomid native to the Colorado River system. Its federal listing as endangered triggered the amendment.
- **Colorado squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)**: a large cyprinid of the Colorado River system, listed under the existing recovery plan.

Stated objectives:

1. Propagate and rear razorback suckers and Colorado squawfish, then stock them into suitable waters of the Salt and Verde rivers.
2. Monitor populations and threats to sensitive or potential habitat of listed and rare species.
3. Reintroduce or explore options for other threatened, endangered, or rare species in Arizona.

Procedures include:

- Propagation and rearing at [[Arizona Game And Fish Department Hatcheries]].
- Expanded post-stocking monitoring to collect physical and biological data on habitat preference and seasonal use.
- Surveys of the Gila River, Colorado River, and their impoundments and canal systems for the presence of razorbacks and squawfish.
- Expansion of the [[Native Fish Database]], maintained with the [[Nongame Data Management System]].
- Collection of preserved specimens for museum and laboratory analysis.
- A performance report summarizing activity under the job.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study areas:**

- **Verde River**: from Perkinsville to about river mile 10 (RM10), Arizona.
- **Salt River**: from Cibeque Creek (RM48) to the Roosevelt Diversion Dam above Roosevelt Lake (RM0), Arizona.
- **Gila River, Colorado River, and their impoundments and associated canal systems**: surveyed for presence of target species.

**Recovery context:** The draft Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan (USFWS 1988) recognizes stocking and monitoring under Task 3.2.3. It states that stocking is planned to continue in the Salt and Verde rivers through 1995, with regular monitoring to evaluate whether the program is establishing the species.

**Regulatory context:** The newly listed razorback sucker population was not covered under the existing Section 6 agreement. Field activities on razorbacks needed explicit authorization. The amendment is meant to close that gap so that survey, monitoring, and other post-listing "take" activities are covered. The letter notes that a separate [[Endangered Species Permit]] (PRT-6 series) would also be requested for razorback and squawfish work, similar to the arrangement used for the [[Mohave Tortoise]] survey.

## Key Findings

- **Amendment purpose:** Extend federal-aid coverage to razorback sucker work after its endangered listing, so that existing squawfish monitoring can be expanded to cover both species.
- **Budget (July 1991 - June 1992):** Total $90,160; federal share $67,620; state share $22,540. The amendment lists "Other: Endangered Species" as the cost distribution, with no change to existing funding.
- **Project personnel:**
  - Terry B. Johnsen, Project Leader and Endangered Species Coordinator.
  - Robert W. Clarkson, Assistant Project Leader and Native Fish Research Biologist.
  - Francisco J. Abarca, Native Fish Biologist, along with contract and temporary employees.
- **Coordination:** The letter (November 18, 1991) was sent to Sam Spiller, Supervisor of the USFWS Ecological Services Field Office in Phoenix. It requests approval so that field work can begin before the next renewal of the endangered species permit (planned for December). Copies went to regional and state contacts.
- **Planned field work:** A Mohave razorback survey was scheduled for later that month under the same coverage logic.
- **Reporting:** A performance report will be prepared for the job. The document cites the Arizona Game and Fish Department's "Endangered Species" reference materials and a technical review draft of the recovery plan.
