# Fish Fauna of Lake Valencia, Venezuela, and Comparison with Neighboring Basins

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 paper by José A. Luengo, published in *Acta Biologica Venezuelica*, inventories the freshwater fishes of the [[Lake Valencia]] basin in north-central Venezuela. The author lists 37 species from material examined (26 species, 559 specimens), of which 8 are reported as new records for the basin, and compares the fauna with the [[Orinoco Basin]], [[Tuy River]], [[Lake Maracaibo]] and [[Trinidad]]. The author concludes that the lake fauna is not peculiar but a mixture of neighboring basin faunas, and that apparent endemisms reflect fragmentary collecting. The paper also documents deliberate introductions of marine, Llanos and other species between 1947 and 1953.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Lake Valencia (Lago de Tacarigua) lies between the Ramal de la Costa and Ramal del Interior of the Cordillera Central, roughly 67°36'–67°53' W and 10°06'–10°16' N, with a basin of about 2,646 km² spanning Aragua and Carabobo states.
- **Hydrological history:** Until 1727 the lake drained via the El Paito sill (418.15 m a.s.l.) into the Pao River and thus the [[Orinoco Basin]]. Its maximum former surface was estimated at about 678 km², against a present surface of 874 km² as cited by the author. Since then the lake has been drying, with the level falling by about 4 m, attributed to population growth, water consumption, and deforestation and burning in headwaters.
- **Water balance:** Evaporation losses are about 22 m³/s (≈700,000,000 m³/year); tributary inflow averages 18.2 m³/s and direct precipitation 8.7 m³/s, giving an annual average deficit reported as 278,860 m³/s (as printed in the source).
- **Capture event:** The Guayas River and other streams that once fed the Aragua River were captured by the [[Tuy River]] through tectonic and erosional processes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Faunal origin:** The author judges the ichthyofauna to be relatively recent and of mixed geographic origin, derived from invasion by upper tributaries of adjacent basins. It includes lowland species and upland taxa such as *Astroblepus* sp., and semi-upland forms such as *Piabucina pleurotaenia*.
- **Affinities:** 16 species are shared with the Tuy basin, 15 with the Orinoco basin, and 8 with all three. Trinidad shares 14 species with Lake Valencia (6 previously recorded by [[Eigenmann]] in 1920, plus 8 added here), 7 of which also occur in the Orinoco. About 10 species are shared with [[Lake Maracaibo]].
- **Endemism:** Seven species are described as endemic to the basin in the source's diagram, but the author attributes these apparent endemisms to uneven, incomplete collecting.
- **Introductions:** Marine species (grouper, snapper, mullet) were stocked under the government of J. V. Gómez, reportedly on the assumption that lake salinity would allow survival. Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) followed with greater success. In 1947–1948, Fulvio Benedetti and Agustín Fernández-Yépez introduced 66 *Mylossoma duriventris* and 2 *Pseudoplatystoma fasciatum* from the Llanos. Manuel Vicente Ramírez introduced many more Llanos species from 1948 to 1953.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** The author reports 37 species for the basin in this work. The bibliography cites 30 earlier, and 8 new records are added (the paper's text states a 25.8% increase; the abstract gives 7 new records and a total of 37 or 87 in different places, as printed).
- **Principal taxa documented:** Characidae (e.g., *Astyanax bimaculatus*, *Astyanax metae*, *Moenkhausia pittieri*, *Gephyrocharax valencia*), Loricariidae (*Chaetostoma* spp., *Hypostomus plecostomus*), Callichthyidae (*Corydoras aeneus*), Cichlidae (*Crenicichla geayi*, *Aequidens pulcher*), Poeciliidae (*Poecilia reticulata*) and Synbranchidae (*Synbranchus marmoratus*).
- **Largest sample sizes:** *Hemibrycon dentatus metae* (48 specimens, Rio San Diego), *Chaetostoma guairense* (84 specimens, Castaño tributaries) and *Creagrutus beni* (up to 46 specimens at one site).
- **Introduced species:** The checklist of introduced taxa (1947–1953) includes about 30 entries across Characidae, Cyprinidae, Pimelodidae, Loricariidae, Poeciliidae and Cichlidae, with local names such as cachama, palometa, payara, carpa, pavón and vieja.
- **Conclusion:** The author maintains that the lake fauna is a mixture of neighbouring basin faunas rather than a distinct endemic assemblage, and stresses that records of introductions are needed to avoid future distributional confusion.
