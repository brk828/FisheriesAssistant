## Executive Summary
This 1995 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services State Office) to a private citizen outlines the regulatory and scientific status of the [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), a cyprinid fish native to the Gila River drainage of Arizona and Mexico. The document describes the Service's efforts—initiated following a 1992 recommendation by the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]—to compile scattered biological, taxonomic, and distributional data on the species through a contracted **Status Survey** conducted by [[The Nature Conservancy]] and subcontracted to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), with taxonomic support from Gila-genus expert Bruce DeMarais. The letter details the Endangered Species Act (ESA) listing process, current conservation actions (transplant proposals, nonnative fish removal), and the species' precarious but data-deficient status.

## Background & Description
The source document is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not provide direct morphological, meristic, or anatomical data for *Gila intermedia*. However, the associated **Statement of Work** (Attachment, July 1992) mandates that any resulting status survey report include:
- A brief taxonomic description of the species
- Citation of the original published species description
- Location and data on **type specimens**
- Summary of the taxonomic history of the genus *Gila*, noted as taxonomically complex

This indicates that, as of 1995, formal morphological synthesis for *Gila intermedia* was still pending compilation, reflecting the broader taxonomic uncertainty within the genus [[Gila (genus)]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Historically occupied streams, springs, and cienega (wetland) habitats within the [[Gila River]] drainage, including areas in Arizona and Mexico.
- **Historic vs. current range**: AGFD had, by early 1995, mapped all historic and presently known populations, though comprehensive field verification was incomplete.
- **Associated threats identified in the Statement of Work**:
  - Habitat loss, alteration, and degradation from land and water use
  - Competition/predation from nonnative fish species
  - Anthropogenic and natural threats affecting survival at both rangewide and site-specific scales
- **Conservation-relevant sites**:
  - Several [[The Nature Conservancy]] preserves provide partial habitat protection.
  - A proposed **transplant of Gila chub** into tributaries of the [[Agua Fria River]] (within historic range) was being developed by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] and AGFD, though it faced opposition from local landowners concerned about land/water use restrictions.
  - A joint effort involving the Service, AGFD, and the [[Coronado National Forest]] (Forest Service) aimed to remove nonnative fishes from a site in preparation for restocking with native species, including Gila chub — an explicit example of [[Nonnative Fish Removal]] as a conservation strategy.
- **Associated species**: The Statement of Work required collection data on associated fish species during field surveys, implying co-occurrence with other native and nonnative fish taxa in these desert stream systems.

## Key Findings
- **Listing recommendation**: In February 1992, the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] recommended *Gila intermedia* be listed as threatened or endangered under the ESA, based on collective expert knowledge rather than a formal compiled dataset.
- **Data deficiency**: As of 1995, information on status and threats remained fragmented across journals, agency files, and personal knowledge, necessitating a dedicated status survey contract.
- **Status survey contract**: The Service contracted [[The Nature Conservancy]] (subcontracted to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) to:
  1. Compile all existing biological, ecological, life history, habitat, distribution, and taxonomic data.
  2. Identify historic declines and their causes, plus existing/potential future threats.
  3. Map land ownership at all known occurrence sites.
  4. Compile all known collection records (museum, literature, personal communication).
  5. Conduct field surveys (U.S. only) to fill data gaps, including voucher specimen deposition at [[Arizona State University]].
  6. Produce a comprehensive final report with taxonomic history, distribution maps, status conclusions, and management recommendations.
- **Timeline slippage**: The draft report, originally due November 30, 1993, was delayed to fall 1995 due to contracting issues.
- **Regulatory status (1995)**:
  - Federal status: Candidate species, **Category 2** — possible but unconfirmed need for listing.
  - Potential outcomes: proposal for threatened/endangered listing (~1 year to Federal Register publication, plus ~1 year for final determination), retention in Category 2, or reclassification to **Category 3C** (species proven more abundant/widespread than believed, or without identifiable threats).
  - State status: Listed as **threatened** by the State of Arizona.
- **Interim protective actions**: Ongoing efforts included the Agua Fria transplant proposal and nonnative fish removal on the Coronado National Forest to safeguard the species while the federal listing decision remained pending.
- **Taxonomic complexity**: The genus *Gila* was noted as taxonomically challenging, requiring specialist involvement (Bruce DeMarais) to properly resolve species-level identifications during the survey.
