# The Fishes of the Jordan River Drainage Basin and Azraq Oasis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cichlidae #balitoridae #clariidae #cyprinodontidae #blenniidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic account by [[F. Krupp]] and [[W. Schneider]] (Fauna of Saudi Arabia, vol. 10, 1989) provides the first comprehensive treatment of the freshwater fishes of the Jordan River drainage basin and [[Azraq Oasis]]. It covers 25 native species from the Jordan-Dead Sea drainage plus one native species from Azraq (Aphanius sirhani). The authors give identification keys, brief descriptions, figures, lectotype designations and synonymies. Twelve species are endemic to the area, and 27 species have been introduced by humans. The paper also resolves numerous nomenclatural problems in genera such as [[Acanthobrama]], [[Barbus]], [[Garra]], [[Nemacheilus]] and [[Aphanius]].

## Background & Description

**Scope and history.** Knowledge of the region's fishes dates to the 18th and 19th centuries, when naturalists including Hasselquist, Valenciennes, Heckel, Günther, Lortet and Tristram described species. [[Trewavas]] (1942) revised the cichlids. [[Steinitz]] (1952–1954) and Krupp (1987) analysed the zoogeography. [[Lortet]] (1883) remained the most comprehensive regional work before this study.

**Study area.** The Jordan River rises on Mount Hermon and historically fed the [[Lake Hula]] swamp, which was drained in the 1950s. It enters [[Lake Tiberias]] (170 km², maximum depth 60 m) and flows about 105 km south to the [[Dead Sea]]. The Dead Sea (salinity 28–33%) supports no higher organisms. Lake Muzairib, in the Nahr Yarmuk headwaters, is the third major lake and was poorly studied before this work. Azraq Oasis had about 5 km² of perennial water, down from a Pleistocene lake of 4,000 km².

**Methods.** Fieldwork was conducted in Jordan from January to December 1980, supplemented by examination of type material from multiple museums and large recent collections. Counts and measurements follow Krupp (1983). Morphometric ranges are given with means in parentheses.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geology and drainage history.** According to [[Horowitz]] (1979), the Jordan-Dead Sea graben developed through four faulting phases. The Dead Sea and Hula basins are early Pleistocene, while Lake Tiberias is younger. During Pleistocene glacials, the graben held successive lakes, including Lake Samra and Lake Lisan, which had lower salinity than the present Dead Sea. The modern drainage pattern formed in the Würm Glacial.

**Habitats.** Species occupy a wide range of environments: spring-fed wadis such as Wadi Kafrain and Wadi Zarqa, fast-flowing streams, shallow lake margins, vegetated pools and hot springs. Some species, such as [[Garra ghorensis]], are restricted to springs and wadis in the southern Dead Sea area with temperatures from about 15°C to over 40°C.

**Zoogeographic patterns.** The Levantine fish fauna is a transition zone between Mediterranean, Tigris-Euphrates and African elements. For example, Garra ghorensis is considered an African element. Pseudophoxinus is disjunct, occurring in the Levant, the Quwaiq River and southern Iran.

**Human impacts.** Lake Hula was drained, which likely caused the decline or local extinction of Acanthobrama hulensis and possibly A. terraesanctae there. Azraq's Aphanius sirhani is endangered by a recent drop in water level. Exotic species may affect native stocks, and Gophen et al. (1983) linked introductions to a decline in [[Sarotherodon galilaeus]].

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic outcomes**

- **Acanthobrama:** [[Acanthobrama hulensis]] is raised to species rank from A. terraesanctae, based on body shape, pharyngeal teeth, and lateral line and gill raker counts. [[Acanthobrama lissneri]] is restored as valid. Records attributed to "Alburnus sellal" in Lortet and Tristram are A. lissneri, not A. terraesanctae.
- **Barbus:** Barbus canis and B. longiceps are retained in *Barbus* despite doubts about generic placement. B. beddomii is a synonym of B. canis.
- **Capoeta damascina:** Only one species occurs in the Jordan drainage. Karaman's proposed subspecies is rejected.
- **Garra:** [[Garra ghorensis]] is a valid species distinct from G. tibanica. It is separated from G. rufa by scale counts, gill rakers and distribution.
- **Hemigrammocapoeta nana:** Records from outside the Damascus and Jordan basins and the Qishon River are misidentifications. H. steinitziorum is a synonym.
- **Pseudophoxinus:** Records of P. zeregi from the Jordan basin largely refer to misidentified Acanthobrama lissneri. P. drusensis and P. kervillei are both valid.
- **Nemacheilus:** Three species occur: N. galilaeus (scaleless, endemic to Tiberias, Hula and Muzairib), N. insignis and N. leontinae. N. leontinae is valid and distinct from N. panthera. Many Orthrias and Oxynoemacheilus names are synonyms of N. insignis.
- **Aphanius:** Aphanius sirhani is endemic to Azraq. A. mento striptus is a colour morph, not a subspecies. A. dispar richardsoni occurs in the Dead Sea area.
- **Cichlidae:** Six Levantine cichlids are recognised: [[Astatotilapia flaviijosephi]], [[Oreochromis aureus]], [[Sarotherodon galilaeus]], [[Tilapia zillii]], [[Tristramella sacra]] and [[Tristramella simonis]]. Tristramella simonis intermedia is noted as a possible distinct species.
- **Salaria:** [[Salaria fluviatilis]] is the only freshwater member of a mainly marine genus, known in the basin from Lake Tiberias and its tributaries.

**Species facts (selected)**

- **Barbus longiceps:** Feeds mainly on chironomid larvae and oligochaetes in Lake Tiberias. Only 8% of 231 fish had mollusc shells in their guts (Spataru & Gophen 1987). Males can reach 500 mm and females 700 mm.
- **Acanthobrama terraesanctae:** The most common species in Lake Tiberias. It is pelagic and feeds mainly on zooplankton, with Cladocera and Copepoda the most important. Spawning is from late November to mid-March.
- **Clarias gariepinus:** Introduced to the Levant. About 80% of its diet is fish. It has a suprabranchial respiratory organ.
- **Capoeta damascina:** Ubiquitous in the Jordan basin and sometimes the only fish species in Dead Sea tributaries. It grazes aufwuchs with a horny lower lip.
- **Garra ghorensis:** Feeds almost entirely on algae and is restricted to a few micro-populations in the southern Dead Sea area.

**Endemism, introductions and hybrids**

- Twelve species are endemic to the area; the paper identifies Aphanius sirhani as endemic to Azraq.
- Of 52 species in the Jordan-Dead Sea catchment, 24 are native and 28 have been introduced intentionally or accidentally. Two species that also occur naturally in the basin, Sarotherodon galilaeus and Oreochromis aureus, were introduced to other localities. Table 1 lists introduced species; examples include Cyprinus carpio, Gambusia affinis and Oncorhynchus mykiss.
- Natural hybrids include Barbus longiceps × B. canis and hybrids involving Capoeta damascina (Mir et al. 1988). Artificial hybrids of Aphanius dispar and A. mento were produced in captivity, but no natural hybrids have been found.

**Conservation concerns**

- Acanthobrama hulensis is probably on the verge of extinction after the drainage of Lake Hula.
- Aphanius sirhani is endangered by water-level decline at Azraq.
- Lake Muzairib's fauna warrants more study.
