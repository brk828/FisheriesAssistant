# Lake Mohave: Recreation, Fisheries and History of the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 *Desert Magazine* travel feature promotes [[Lake Mohave]], a 56-mile reach of the lower [[Colorado River]] between Arizona and southern Nevada. It covers recreational fishing, boating, and camping, and describes four resorts. It also recounts the lake's prehistoric and historic background, including Native American rock art, [[Basketmaker II People]] occupation, 19th-century steamship and gold-mining activity, and a recent sighting of the rare [[Colorado Squawfish]]. The piece is not a research study. It contains no sampling data, and its fish and water-quality observations are descriptive.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Lake Mohave lies within a few hours' drive of many crowded Southern California communities, yet boaters often have it nearly to themselves. The surrounding terrain includes abandoned mines, geological formations, [[Petroglyph]] sites, and desert scenery.
- **Managing body:** Several resorts operate within the [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]].
- **Fishing:** Anglers commonly fish for bass, catfish, and trout at the same time, often with two poles. The article also mentions crappie and bluegill.
- **Colorado Squawfish:** The article calls this species "nearly extinct" and a member of the minnow family. It may weigh up to 100 pounds. A large specimen was caught in November at [[Willow Beach]], and [[National Park Service]] naturalist Ted Whitmoyer identified it as related to a fish taken in November 1952. The source says the species was once common and was a food source for prehistoric Indians.
- **Resorts (four in total):**
  - [[Lake Mohave Resort]], near [[Davis Dam]] on the Arizona side, with a 82-unit motel (the source says "B2-unit"; OCR uncertain) and a restaurant and lounge planned.
  - [[Willow Beach Resort]], about four miles south of U.S. 93, close to the Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery.
  - [[Crazy Canoe Cove]], near Nelson, Nevada, which has cabins for rent.
  - [[Cottonwood Cove]], near Searchlight, Nevada, with a marina of about 125 boat slips and trailer sites.
- **Advertising:** The page includes a Datsun Patrol four-wheel-drive advertisement. It is commercial content, not part of the scientific or travel narrative.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal structure:** Water released from [[Lake Mead]] at [[Hoover Dam]] enters Lake Mohave cold enough to form a cold-water layer. The source says this supports trout in the deep original river channel, while bass occupy warmer upper waters. [[Lake Havasu]], to the south, is described as bass-dominated.
- **Water level and clarity:** Lake Mohave holds a nearly constant level, unlike Lake Mead's large fluctuations. Its water is clearer than other Colorado River lakes because silt settles out. Visibility is described as exceptional, and depths reach several hundred feet.
- **Wind and shoreline:** Strong winds can rise quickly off the steep mountains. The long, narrow lake still has sheltered, easily reached shores.
- **Paleontology and wildlife:** Excavators have uncovered vertebrae of extinct fish. Grizzly bear evidence was also found, and the article notes bears were observed in the nearby Cerbat Mountains as recently as 1960.
- **Historical hydrology:** The article describes 1858–1859 government steamboat trials on the Colorado and the placement of ringbolts in [[Black Canyon]] to help ships pass [[Ringbolt Rapids]] near Hoover Dam. These are historical navigation accounts, not ecological data.

## Key Findings

- **Recreation:** The lake offers year-round fishing, water skiing, boat racing, swimming, and beach camping. It is a fishing and boating destination within easy reach of Southern California.
- **Fisheries:** Lake Mohave supports channel catfish, bass, trout, crappie, bluegill, and the rare Colorado Squawfish. The article reports the fish's persistence without population estimates.
- **Squawfish recovery:** The sighting suggests more individuals may survive, but the article says a team of [[Scuba Diving]] surveyors might be needed to find them.
- **Archaeology:** Excavations by the National Park Service in 1936 and 1947–1950 found occupancy dating from 250 B.C. by a western variant of the Basketmaker II people. Petroglyphs remain visible above the waterline near Monkey Rock.
- **Mining history:** The Techatticup Mine in [[Eldorado Canyon]] produced about $3.5 million in gold. A dredge costing about $185,000 was launched around 1909 and swamped by a flood about a year later. Spanish or Mexican prospectors may have worked the canyon earlier.
- **Searchlight origin:** The town's name, according to the article, came from a matchbox brand that a prospector used while signaling near a gold outcrop in the 1890s.
- **Safety:** The article advises 4-wheel drivers to report their destinations before entering the backcountry.
- **Resort development:** All four resorts are expanding, with new motels, cabins, and marina facilities planned or under construction. Rates are described as nominal.
