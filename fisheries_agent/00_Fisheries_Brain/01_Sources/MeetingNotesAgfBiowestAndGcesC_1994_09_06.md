## Executive Summary

This document is a set of interagency meeting notes (Arizona Game and Fish Department [AGF], Bio/West, and Glen Canyon Environmental Studies [GCES]) dated September 6, 1994, consolidating Fiscal Year 1995 (FY95) work plans for aquatic monitoring in the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River. The core scientific objective is to evaluate population densities and trends of native and non-native fish species — particularly the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] — in relation to the operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The notes outline a coordinated, multi-agency field program to test and standardize sampling gear (electrofishing, trammel nets, hoop nets, seines, minnow traps) toward development of a formal **Long-Term Monitoring Plan for Native Fishes in Grand Canyon**, drawing on historical data dating back to 1977.

## Background & Description

While this is primarily a program-planning document rather than a morphometric study, it references life-stage classifications used to structure monitoring of the [[Humpback Chub]] population:

- **Young-of-the-Year (YOY):** Fish in their first year of life; targeted for production and survival surveys in backwaters and tributary habitats.
- **"Super Cohort":** Juvenile fish encompassing ages 0, I, and II, up to approximately **200 mm total length (TL)**. This size/age class is treated as a distinct demographic unit for monitoring purposes.
- **Adults:** Fish of age III+ (age class three and older), the reproductively mature component of the population targeted via tributary mouth sampling during spawning season.

No detailed external morphology, meristic counts, or coloration data are provided in this source; the focus is on age/size-class stratification for demographic monitoring.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary Habitat:** Mainstem Colorado River within [[Grand Canyon]], including backwaters, main channel runs, and tributary confluences.
- **Key Tributary:** The **Little Colorado River (LCR)**, identified as a critical spawning and rearing area — particularly the reach from the LCR confluence to Red Creek — for adult and juvenile [[Humpback Chub]].
- **Other Tributaries Sampled:** Bright Angel Creek, Shinumo Creek, Kanab Creek, Havasu Creek, Clear Creek — indicating tributary mouths broadly serve as spawning/nursery habitat distinct from the mainstem.
- **Habitat Types Monitored:** Backwaters (critical YOY/juvenile rearing habitat), main channel (adult/subadult habitat), and warm springs (e.g., near RM 30), reflecting thermal habitat heterogeneity in the regulated river.
- **Anthropogenic Driver:** [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations are explicitly identified as the primary anthropogenic factor influencing population demographics, density, and trends of native fish — likely through altered flow regimes, temperature, and sediment dynamics affecting backwater formation (nursery habitat).
- **Community Context:** The monitoring explicitly considers both **native fish** (e.g., Humpback Chub) and **non-native fish**, implying an interactive or competitive ecological dynamic between introduced and endemic species within the same reach.
- **Trophic/Ecosystem Linkages:** Continued sampling of benthos, sediment, and plankton alongside fish sampling indicates the monitoring program treats fish population dynamics as embedded within broader food-web and habitat (backwater mapping) contexts.

## Key Findings

- **Multi-agency coordination** (AGF, Bio/West, GCES) was established to consolidate FY95 mainstem Colorado River aquatic sampling efforts and unify fragmented historical databases.
- **Historical data sources** identified for retrospective analysis include: Carothers and Minckley (1977), Kaeding and Zimmerman (1980), Maddux et al. (1984) through present-day GCES Phase I and II data; older data (e.g., Suttkus) still needed. Full "metadata" development for all historical studies was flagged as a priority task.
- **Gear evaluation** was a central FY95 objective: trammel nets, electrofishing, and hoop nets were identified as preferred gear for adults and smaller juveniles; backwater seining/trapping was considered standard/effective for YOY and juveniles. Catch efficiency and gear bias (by species and size class) were to be assessed via depletion sampling.
- **Seasonal sampling matrix (per Rich Valdez's Bio/West framework)** for [[Humpback Chub]] life stages:
  - **Spring (Apr):** LCR seine sampling (YOY).
  - **Winter/Spring (Feb):** LCR seine sampling.
  - **Spring–Summer (Feb, May):** LCR confluence to Red Creek — seine, electrofish, minnow trap.
  - **Fall (Oct):** LCR confluence to Red Creek — same gear suite.
  - **Fall, every 2–3 years:** Trammel netting at River Mile (RM) segments 30–35, 61.3–76.5, and 117–128.
- **FY95 field trip schedule** comprised approximately five coordinated river trips spanning late March through September, covering spawning/yearling survival (spring), LCR monitoring (May), YOY production/yearling survival (June), tributary habitat and early YOY survival (July–August), and YOY survival assessment (September), with sampling sites spanning from Reach 1 (put-in) to Diamond Creek (take-out), including RM 30, RM 44, LCR, Tanner, Clear Creek, Cremation, Bass, Blacktail, Randy's Rock, Kanab Creek, Havasu Creek, National, below Lava Falls, and RM 190–204.
- **Continued ancillary sampling** of benthos, sediment, plankton, and backwater habitat mapping was mandated alongside fish sampling, underscoring an ecosystem-level (not solely fish-centric) monitoring approach.
- **Deliverables proposed:** A Fish Monitoring Database compatible with the GCES Integrated Database, standardized field data collection forms, joint-authored trip reports, and an eventual formal Long-Term Monitoring Plan for Native Fishes of Grand Canyon.
- **Institutional logistics:** Proposal to designate Hoffnagle or Tuegle as Trip Leader, with potential subcontracting of Bio/West through AGF to streamline field logistics decision-making.
