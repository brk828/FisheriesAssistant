## Executive Summary
This 1960 study by W. E. Ricker describes a dwarfed, deep-water-restricted population of the [[Coastrange Sculpin]] (*Cottus aleuticus*) in Cultus Lake, British Columbia. Unlike typical populations of this species elsewhere, which spawn in gravel-bottomed tributary streams in early spring, the Cultus Lake population appears to complete its entire life cycle within the lake's deep water, growing slowly to a maximum length of only ~50 mm over 3–4 years, and spawning throughout the summer rather than in a discrete spring pulse. The species was documented almost exclusively through analysis of [[Dolly Varden]] (*Salvelinus malma*) stomach contents, since standard shore seining and outlet fingerling traps failed to capture any specimens, indicating strict habitat segregation from the shallow/littoral zone dominated by the congener *[[Cottus asper]]*. The author interprets this as a population that is ecologically, though not geographically, "relict," drawing comparison to deep-water benthic sculpins of the Great Lakes such as *[[Myoxocephalus quadricornis]]* and *[[Cottus ricei]]*.

## Background & Description
- **Maximum size**: Largest specimen examined = 49 mm to end of caudal fin — substantially dwarfed compared to regional conspecifics.
  - Lake Washington (Seattle) population: ~100 mm maximum (L. P. Schultz, pers. comm.)
  - British Columbia collections (McAllister, MS 1957, n=41 collections): max 115 mm; 80–100 mm specimens common.
- **Egg size**: Formalin-hardened ripe eggs averaged only 0.8 mm diameter.
- **Sexual maturity size**:
  - Ripe/near-ripe females (orange-colored eggs): 37–45 mm (n=6)
  - Males with enlarged testes: 29–41 mm (n=3)
- **Age structure** (from 219 measured specimens, June 14–Sept 19, 1934):
  - Age I (previous year's spawning): modal length ~17.5 mm (July)
  - Age II and III: dominant size classes taken by char predators
  - Age IV: rare, few individuals
  - Age 0 fish essentially absent from samples (too small/cryptic given tiny egg size and prolonged spawning).
- Growth is notably slow, with most individuals not reaching maturity until their third calendar year of life.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Depth stratification**: Restricted to deep water of Cultus Lake; never captured by extensive shore seining (19 mm stretched mesh, 1935–1936) or by outlet salmon fingerling traps, both of which regularly caught *[[Cottus asper]]*.
- **Predation exposure**: Found abundantly in stomachs of [[Dolly Varden]] char (up to 100 specimens per fish; 25–30 commonly in char 300–700 mm fork length), which forage at depth. Rarely found in [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki*) or "residual" [[Coho Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*) stomachs, and never in [[Northern Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus oregonensis*) — species that forage at higher levels or closer inshore, except in winter.
- **Seasonal occurrence in predator stomachs**: March 7 to December 10 (extreme dates); regular occurrence late March to late October. Absence during coldest months suggests reduced activity/accessibility in winter.
- **Diet** (16 specimens, 30–45 mm, examined June 26 & July 10, 1934), in decreasing volumetric order:
  1. *Daphnia*
  2. Chironomid midges (17 larvae, 1 pupa)
  3. *Epischura*
  4. Ostracoda
  5. *Bosmina*
  6. *Cyclops*
  - One case of cannibalism: a 37 mm specimen had eaten a 14 mm juvenile *C. aleuticus*.
  - Diet indicates reliance on both zooplankton and microbenthos.
- **Spawning habitat/timing**: Unlike typical stream-spawning populations (e.g., Lake Washington, spawning March–April in tributary creeks), no stream-spawning individuals were observed at Cultus Lake — likely because tributary streams largely dry up in summer. Spawning instead appears to occur within the lake itself, beginning late May/early June, peaking through July, continuing into August, with the latest gravid female recorded September 11.

## Key Findings
- The Cultus Lake *Cottus aleuticus* population represents a **dwarfed, deep-water ecotype** distinct from neighboring populations in both maximum body size (~50 mm vs. up to 115 mm elsewhere) and reproductive timing (prolonged summer spawning vs. discrete spring stream-spawning).
- The population shows strong **habitat segregation** from the congeneric *[[Cottus asper]]*, which occupies shallow/littoral zones and is captured by standard shore-based sampling methods.
- Life history is inferred almost entirely from **predator stomach contents** (particularly [[Dolly Varden]] char), highlighting the ecological importance of piscivore diet analysis for detecting cryptic, deep-dwelling prey populations that evade conventional sampling gear.
- Age-frequency analysis suggests the population contains **4–5 discernible age classes** despite the very small maximum size, with most individuals maturing in their third year.
- The author proposes that this population may be considered an **ecological "relict"** — not geographically isolated, but ecologically confined to a novel deep-lake niche, drawing parallels with deep-water sculpins of the Great Lakes (*[[Myoxocephalus quadricornis]]*, *[[Cottus ricei]]*).
- Findings suggest that lack of viable stream-spawning habitat (due to seasonal drying of tributaries) may have driven the shift to lake-based, prolonged summer spawning and a fully lacustrine life cycle.
