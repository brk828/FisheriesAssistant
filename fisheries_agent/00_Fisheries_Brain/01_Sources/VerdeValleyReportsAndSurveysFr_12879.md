# Verde Valley Survey on Attitudes Toward Scenic, Natural and Cultural/Historic Resources

#survey #water-management #riparian-ecology #conservation-planning #land-use #public-opinion

## Executive Summary

This document presents results from two public opinion surveys conducted in the [[Verde Valley]], Arizona between Fall 1990 and 1991. The first survey was administered at Verde River Days 1991, while the second was a comprehensive mail survey of community residents (n=94, 36% response rate) and telephone census of land-use decision makers (n=12, 70% response rate). The research, conducted by Dr. Donovan C. Wilkin and Catherine Bradley of the [[University of Arizona]] School of Renewable Natural Resources, assessed community attitudes toward growth, water resources, scenic/natural/cultural resources, and quality of life in the [[Verde Valley]]. Results demonstrate strong public support for growth controls to protect water resources (93% agreement), concerns about diminishing quality of life with population growth (86% agreement), and preferences for low-impact recreational uses of the [[Verde River]] corridor. The surveys provide critical input for Yavapai County's 20-year comprehensive planning process and [[National Park Service]] land management decisions.

## Background & Description

The [[Verde Valley]] encompasses the [[Verde River]] corridor from the TAPCO historic power plant above Clarkdale downstream approximately 60 river miles to Beasley Flats below Camp Verde, including areas within the 100-year floodplain. The valley contains multiple protected areas including [[Tuzigoot National Monument]], [[Montezuma Castle National Monument]], [[Montezuma Well]], [[Dead Horse Ranch State Park]], [[Fort Verde State Historic Park]], and [[Jerome State Historic Park]].

The mail survey was subdivided into five general topics: (1) general attitudes about growth, (2) attitudes about Verde Valley resources, (3) management of growth resources, (4) satisfaction with quality of living, and (5) personal information. Survey responses were categorized as positive, neutral, or negative, with results compared between residents and community leaders. The Verde River Days survey focused specifically on water resource management and growth control attitudes among 121 respondents (72 property/business owners, 23 resident non-property owners).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Verde River]] is a perennial river supporting riparian habitat in an otherwise arid landscape. The river corridor provides critical habitat for wildlife and natural communities, with survey respondents identifying protection of these resources as a high priority (86.2% of residents, 58.4% of leaders favored development only with adequate protection of wildlife and natural communities).

Water resources represent the most limiting factor for valley development. Survey results indicate strong public concern about water allocation, with 85.1% of residents and 100% of leaders favoring blocking all transfers, trades, or sales of water out of the valley. Both groups supported designation of a water management authority (66.7% of residents, 73.4% of leaders) to allocate water resources, promote conservation, and protect water quality.

Preferred recreational uses of the [[Verde River]] corridor emphasized low-impact activities: walking access (84.0%), picnicking (80.9%), hiking (78.3%), photography (74.5%), bank fishing (70.2%), swimming (65.9%), and birdwatching/nature study (62.8%). Equipment-intensive activities received low support: paramilitary uses (71.3% opposed), trapping (71.3% opposed), off-road vehicle use (62.8% opposed).

## Key Findings

**Verde River Days Survey (1991):**
- 93% agreed with controlling growth as necessary to sustain and protect water resources (57% strongly)
- 86% agreed that quality of life is very likely to diminish as population grows over the next 20 years (36% strongly)
- 83% favored controlling growth to maintain present quality of life (48% strongly)
- 84% agreed all interested parties should have opportunity to democratically influence water use (46% strongly)
- Only 19% believed there is enough water to maintain quality of life while accommodating reasonable development over 20 years
- 42% agreed private property rights should take precedence over public interest, while 41% disagreed; property owners (50% agreed, 40% disagreed) differed significantly from non-owners (22% agreed, 43% disagreed) only on this question

**Mail Survey Growth Attitudes:**
- Tourism growth received strong support: winter tourism (73.4% residents, 100% leaders), summer tourism (78.7% residents, 100% leaders)
- Stable residential community favored: working permanent residents (77.7% residents, 100% leaders), retired permanent residents (77.7% residents, 100% leaders), single-family residences (80.8% residents, 100% leaders)
- Leaders demonstrated consistently more favorable attitudes toward growth than residents across most categories
- Shopping malls rated lowest among development types (53.2% residents, 58.3% leaders)

**Resource Protection Priorities:**
- 76.6% of residents (50.0% of leaders) favored development only with adequate protection of scenic views
- 86.2% of residents (58.4% of leaders) favored protection of wildlife and natural communities
- 78.7% of residents (58.4% of leaders) favored protection of significant historic and archaeological resources
- 55.3% of residents (41.6% of leaders) believed costs of protection would be compensated by increased property values and quality of living

**Water Management:**
- 85.1% of residents and 100% of leaders favored blocking water transfers out of valley
- 72.3% of residents (50.0% of leaders) favored limiting development to protect river quality and quantity
- 52.1% of residents (66.7% of leaders) favored limiting grazing to protect water
- Only 46.8% of residents (66.7% of leaders) favored limiting irrigated farming to protect water

**Public Investment and Management:**
- 61.7% of residents (91.7% of leaders) favored using public funds to purchase rights along [[Verde River]] corridor
- 63.8% of residents (75.0% of leaders) favored purchasing land adjacent to state and national parks as buffer
- Neither group favored increased property taxes (33.0% residents, 16.6% leaders) or sales taxes (40.4% residents, 33.3% leaders)
- 62.8% of residents (50.0% of leaders) favored increased restaurant/hotel/motel taxes for improvements
- 72.4% of residents (58.4% of leaders) favored vesting valley-wide authority with power to protect resources through public planning and review

**Recreation Management (in event of chronic overuse):**
- 68.1% of residents (83.3% of leaders) favored fees for selected uses or areas
- 66.0% of residents (91.7% of leaders) favored reservations for overused areas
- 66.0% of residents (75.0% of leaders) favored limiting parking at frequently used areas
- Only 13.9% of residents (66.6% of leaders) favored not restricting overuse in any way

**Quality of Life:**
- 82% of residents expressed substantial satisfaction with overall quality of living
- Highest positive contributions: access to outdoor recreation (87.3%), present scenic quality (89.3%), present air quality (89.4%), present water quality (83.0%)
- Lowest ratings: access to public transportation (18.1% positive), police/sheriff protection (76.6% positive), access to public schools (43.6% positive)
- Seven of top eight factors contributing to high quality of life were natural, scenic, and/or cultural historic resources

**Demographics:**
- 89.4% permanent valley residents, 87.2% voted in last election
- 51.1% had attended public meeting concerning local development in past two years
- 50.0% registered Republican, 36.2% registered Democrat
- 26.6% lived in valley 0-5 years, 29.8% lived 6-10 years, 32.9% lived >11 years
- 33.0% fully employed locally, 37.2% not employed in valley
- Majority of household purchases made in [[Cottonwood]] (66.0%)
- Leaders achieved higher income levels (83.3% between $30,000-$74,999) and education (41.7% completed at least some college) compared to residents (53.2% income $10,000-$29,999)
