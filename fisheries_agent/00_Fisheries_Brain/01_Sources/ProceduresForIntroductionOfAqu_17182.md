## Executive Summary
This document, issued as **Appendix K** by the **[[Colorado River Wildlife Council]]**, establishes the formal procedural and evaluative framework governing proposals to introduce aquatic organisms (fish and other aquatic species) into the **[[Colorado River Basin]]**. Rather than presenting original species-level biological data, the text functions as a **regulatory protocol** intended to prevent ecological harm from non-native or translocated aquatic species. It outlines mandatory criteria for introduction proposals, defines categories of prohibited organisms, and specifies administrative timelines for review by the Council's Technical Committee. The policy reflects a precautionary, multi-agency approach to safeguarding indigenous aquatic biodiversity across a seven-state jurisdictional basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no single species' niche is detailed, the document implicitly addresses basin-wide ecological considerations relevant to any candidate introduction:

- **Indigenous species displacement**: Proposals must evaluate the potential for a new organism to displace or outcompete native aquatic species within the basin's [[Freshwater Ecosystems]].
- **Species ecology assessment**: Applicants must characterize the ecological role, requirements, and interactions of the proposed organism prior to release.
- **Disease potential**: Introductions must be screened for pathogen or parasite risk to existing aquatic communities, connecting to broader concerns in [[Fish Disease Ecology]].
- **Hybridization risk**: Evaluation of genetic compatibility with native taxa is required, relevant to conservation of genetically distinct populations such as basin [[Native Cyprinids]] or other endemic fishes.
- **Habitat degradation potential**: Organisms that could alter physical or chemical habitat conditions (e.g., benthic disturbance, vegetation changes) are explicitly restricted.
- **Sport and aesthetic value conflicts**: The policy recognizes recreational fisheries value as an ecological/socio-economic factor, protecting species "esteemed for their sporting or aesthetic attributes" — a nod to managed sport fisheries coexisting with native fish conservation in the [[Colorado River Basin]].

## Key Findings
- Introduction proposals are reviewed **case-by-case** by the Council, requiring a written submission addressing eight specific criteria: need, expected benefits, negative impacts, indigenous species displacement, species ecology, disease potential, hybridization potential, and post-introduction monitoring.
- **Prohibited introductions** are those aquatic organisms that may:
  1. Cause or accelerate decline of indigenous species status.
  2. Jeopardize species valued for sport or aesthetic reasons.
  3. Pose a threat to human health or safety.
  4. Cause habitat degradation.
- Proposals must be submitted to the **Council Secretary at least 60 days prior** to the annual **Technical Committee meeting in February**, allowing distribution to relevant subcommittees for review.
- Exceptions to submission timelines may be requested under **Council By-laws Article V, Paragraph F**.
- The Council's authority spans **seven state wildlife/game agencies** ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[California Department of Fish and Game]], [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], [[Wyoming Game and Fish Department]]) and **eight cooperating federal agencies**, reflecting an integrated, multi-jurisdictional management model for the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- The document was formally accepted by the Council in **Las Vegas**, underscoring its role as an official interstate/federal management instrument rather than a primary research publication.
- This policy framework is foundational to broader [[Invasive Species Management]] and [[Native Fish Conservation]] efforts within the Colorado River system, complementing species-specific recovery programs for basin endemics such as [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]].
