## Executive Summary

The [[Lees Ferry Trophy Trout Fishery]] on the Colorado River below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] has undergone a turbulent management history driven by variable dam-controlled flows, water quality shifts, reservoir filling in [[Lake Powell]], and changing angler demands. A drastic population decline occurred from 1990-1993, especially among older age classes, attributed largely to the [[GCES Phase II]] research flows of 1990-1991. These flows caused food base scouring and desiccation, compounding a pre-existing nematode infestation in trout pyloric caeca, which together drove poor body condition and increased mortality. In response, Arizona Game and Fish Department proposed a "jump start" stocking of 20,000 catchable-sized (10"-12") Belaire rainbow trout to bolster the depleted 1992 year class and accelerate recovery of the adult population, alongside continued fingerling stocking and enhanced monitoring.

## Background & Description

- **Mean length and weight of harvested fish** declined substantially over the survey period (Table 1):
  - 1977: 398 mm / 735 g
  - 1984: 370 mm / 555 g (start of condition decline)
  - 1990: 385 mm / 514 g
  - 1993: 363 mm / 476 g (lowest recorded)
- **Relative weight (condition factor)**: A key metric of "fatness" tracked quarterly from 1984-1993; began declining in 1984, reaching critically poor levels by 1992, with recovery beginning in late 1991 as interim dam operations started.
- **Growth rates**:
  - Naturally spawned fish (1992-1993): ~4 inches/year, down from ~6 inches/year estimated for 1984-1988, but improved from near-zero growth during 1990-1991 research flows.
  - Hatchery-stocked (wire-tagged) fish showed somewhat better growth than naturally spawned fish over the same period.
- **Parasitism**: Heavy infestation of nematodes in the pyloric caeca observed in all examined fish (December 1990 health exam); retrospective analysis of preserved specimens confirmed nematode presence as early as 1980, indicating a longstanding but exacerbated condition rather than a novel pathogen.
- **Survival/growth to trophy size**: Wire-tagged cohorts (electrofished 8/92-9/93) showed 60% survival for stocked fingerlings and 48% for stocked catchables; projected 22% of fingerlings and 23% of catchables would exceed 16" within three and two years respectively, assuming constant rates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System type**: A dam-regulated tailwater [[Trout Fishery]] created by cold, clear, nutrient-influenced outflows from Glen Canyon Dam beginning in 1964, atypical for a naturally warm, sediment-laden Colorado River reach.
- **Food base**: Dependent on algae and [[Gammarus]] (amphipod) production, both of which were severely reduced by scouring and desiccation from fluctuating/low research flows in 1990-1991, and which recovered under more stable interim operations post-1991.
- **Flow regime as ecological driver**: Peaking hydropower operations caused fluctuating flows implicated in stranding of spawning adults and desiccation of redds; specific damaging periods identified as June-July 1990, September 1990-January 1991, and May-June 1991, linked to high fluctuation with low minimum flows or sustained periods below 5,100 cfs.
- **Species community history**: Managed trout assemblage shifted between rainbow trout ([[Oncorhynchus mykiss]]), brook trout, and cutthroat trout depending on stocking strategy (1978-1981), reflecting attempts to balance natural recruitment evaluation against angler harvest pressures.
- **Reservoir influence**: The "filling" of Lake Powell is cited as a source of increased nutrient input benefiting the downstream food web and trout growth potential.
- **Host-parasite dynamics**: Nematode infestation of the pyloric caeca acts synergistically with reduced food availability and physiological stress (variable flows, spawning, angling/hooking stress) to elevate mortality — an example of multiple interacting ecological stressors.

## Key Findings

- The 1990-1993 population crash in older/larger trout is linked directly to [[GCES Phase II]] research flow experiments (1990-1991), which scoured and desiccated the invertebrate/algal food base.
- A previously unrecognized but long-present nematode infestation of the pyloric caeca was identified as a major synergistic contributor to poor body condition and mortality, distinct from and compounding food-base reduction.
- Regulatory history reflects an evolving management philosophy: catchable-to-fingerling stocking shift (1976), bag limit reductions (1978, 1990), immediate-kill regulation to prevent high-grading (1980), artificials-only regulation (1986), and a protective 16-22 inch slot limit (1990) — all aimed at sustaining a "Blue Ribbon" trophy fishery.
- Creel census data (Table 1) show angler catch rates and estimated harvest collapsing from a peak of 53,780 fish harvested in 1983 to just 2,051 in 1993, while percent catch-and-release rose from 30% (1980) to 92-94% (1990-1993), reflecting both fishery decline and shifting angler ethics.
- Naturally spawned recruitment first appeared in 1991 and became significant by 1992-1993, but growth rates (~4 in/yr) remain insufficient alone to rapidly rebuild the adult population (3-4 year recovery estimated).
- Hatchery (wire-tagged) fish grow at least as well as naturally spawned fish under current conditions; in 1993 stocked fish comprised ~30% of trout under 300 mm.
- Proposed management intervention: stocking of 20,000 catchable (10"-12") Belaire rainbow trout, individually wire-tagged in the adipose fin, to distinguish them from naturally spawned and fingerling-origin fish for independent growth/survival monitoring, with a potential follow-up catchable stocking in 1995 contingent on evaluation results.
- Public/stakeholder recommendations (March 1994 meeting) included catchable stocking, a 16-inch maximum size limit, single barbless hook regulations, and evaluation of alternative trout strains for growth and nematode resistance.
