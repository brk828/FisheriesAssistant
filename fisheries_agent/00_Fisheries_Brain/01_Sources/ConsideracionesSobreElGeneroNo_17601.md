# Notropis imeldae sp. nov.: Barbel Variation and the Generic Limits of Notropis in Southern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1966 taxonomic paper by M. T. Cortés (Departamento de Zoología, Escuela Nacional de Ciencias Biológicas, IPN, Mexico) addresses whether the presence of small maxillary barbels can separate the genera [[Notropis]] and [[Hybopsis]]. The author shows that barbels occur variably within [[Notropis Boucardi]] and [[Notropis Moralesi]], which invalidates barbels as a generic or specific character. The paper then describes a new species, [[Notropis Imeldae]], from the [[Atoyac River]] at Juchatengo, Oaxaca, and reassigns the barbelled forms to Notropis. The work is primarily morphological, based on meristics, morphometrics and [[Pharyngeal Teeth]] counts.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic problem.** Barbels had been the key distinction between Notropis (barbels absent) and Hybopsis (barbels present) since [[Jordan and Gilbert]] (1883). In 1966 a cyprinid collected at Juchatengo had small barbels yet otherwise resembled Notropis, so its generic placement was uncertain.

**Collection.** On 16 April 1965, Imelda Martínez and Lauro González collected 193 specimens of 19.7 to 74.5 mm standard length from the Atoyac River, which is crossed by the Oaxaca–Mixtepec–Puerto Ángel highway. The material was deposited in the Laboratorio de Cordados, ENCB-IPN, as P-915.

**Description of Notropis imeldae sp. nov.**
- Holotype: one specimen of 59.9 mm standard length, collected 16 April 1965.
- Paratypes: 193 specimens; 25 (40.0 to 74.5 mm) were measured for the variation table.
- Diagnosis: slender, slightly compressed body; blunt snout; subterminal mouth; head 3.2 to 3.6 in standard length; maximum depth 3.5 to 4.5; D 10, A 10 (each preceded by a small ray); lateral line 45 to 49 scales (usually 45); 22 to 28 predorsal scales; 3 to 5 gill rakers on the first arch; one small barbel at the posterior end of each maxilla.
- Coloration (preserved): cream-yellow venter, dark brown dorsum, a dark lateral band from behind the operculum to the caudal peduncle, and a conspicuous occipital blotch.
- Type locality: Juchatengo, Río Atoyac, Oaxaca. The species is named after the collector, Imelda Martínez.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper treats the fish as a freshwater cyprinid in river systems of the Balsas and Santo Domingo drainages and the Atoyac in Oaxaca. It contains no data on habitat use, water chemistry, diet, reproduction or the wider aquatic community. The comparative table (Table II) places the new species among Mexican Notropis with blunt snouts and subterminal mouths, which provides the regional taxonomic context.

## Key Findings

- **Barbels are variable within species.** Of 100 [[Notropis Boucardi]] from the Balsas basin, 24 had a barbel on each side, 18 had one on only one maxilla, and 48 lacked barbels (about 50% with at least one barbel). Of 20 paratypes of [[Notropis Moralesi]] (De Buen, 1956), 12 had two barbels, 6 had one and 2 had none, although the original description stated that the species lacks barbels.
- **Pharyngeal teeth do not separate the groups.** Specimens with and without barbels all had 4-4 pharyngeal teeth, and the dentary forms showed no differences. Meristic and morphometric characters also failed to split the Balsas or Santo Domingo samples into two species, so both nominal species are treated as single taxa.
- **Generic conclusion.** Because barbels are not a reliable generic character, the author retains the three barbelled forms in Notropis (which has priority over Hybopsis), with the caveat that some Notropis species may have one barbel or none.
- **Diagnosis of N. imeldae.** The species is distinguished from its nearest relatives by:
  - Lateral line scales (42 to 49, mostly 45), more than the less than 40 in N. boucardi, and more than 50 in N. aztecus.
  - Predorsal scales (22 to 28), against 19 to 20 in [[Notropis Celayensis]].
  - Gill rakers (mostly 4), against 5 to 6 in N. celayensis.
  - Pectoral fins with rounded tips (pointed in N. celayensis).
  - Dorsal fin rays (mostly 10), against 7 in N. orca, 8 in N. nazas, and 7 to 8 in N. moralesi.
  - Lateral line to pelvic fin base (8 scales), against 5 to 6 in N. celayensis.
- **Only the presence of barbels separates N. imeldae from all other Notropis.** Within the comparison group, the species is closest in morphology to N. boucardi, N. moralesi, N. celayensis and N. lermae, but the author notes that its barbels, if not diagnostic, are the only feature setting it apart from several of them.
- **Measurement summary (Table I, 25 paratypes).** Head length 27.8 to 30.8 per mil of standard length (mean 29.2); maximum depth 22.8 to 28.8 (mean 26.1); predorsal distance 51.6 to 55.9 (mean 54.1).
