# Trip Report: September 1996 Survey of the ASU Research Park

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae #Catostomidae #Cichlidae

## Executive Summary
This trip report documents a fish community survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel on 12–13 September 1996 at three artificial ponds within the [[ASU Research Park]], Tempe, Arizona. Using entanglement gear (experimental gill nets and trammel nets), researchers sampled the ponds overnight to assess fish population status. A total of 31 individuals representing 7 species were captured, all previously documented in past surveys. [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Yellow Bass]] dominated the catch, while a notable recapture of an endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] provided valuable long-term growth data. Native big-river species such as [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] were absent from the catch, continuing a multi-year trend of scarcity or absence for these species in the research park ponds.

## Background & Description
- **[[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**: One male recaptured, Total Length (TL) = 580 mm, in good health. Originally tagged 30 November 1994 at TL = 565 mm — indicating a growth of 15 mm over approximately 22 months in this pond environment. Individual identified via PIT tag # 1F0B7A3725.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)**: Most abundant species captured; specific length/weight data not itemized beyond catch proportions.
- **[[Yellow Bass]] (*Morone mississippiensis*)**: Second most abundant species in the catch.
- **[[Blue Tilapia]] (*Tilapia aurea*)**: Comprised 16% of total catch.
- Minor contributors (≤6% each): [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Zilli's Tilapia]] (*Tilapia zilli*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and the aforementioned Razorback Sucker.
- No morphometric data were reported for [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), as none were captured despite visual observation of surface-schooling individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Three artificial, interconnected ponds (labeled A, B, C) within the ASU Research Park, an urban impounded freshwater system in Tempe, Arizona — a non-native, human-modified habitat supporting a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fishes.
- **Community composition**: The pond system supports a mix of introduced sport/forage fishes (largemouth bass, yellow bass, bluegill, green sunfish, blue tilapia, Zilli's tilapia) alongside remnant/stocked populations of imperiled native Colorado River basin fishes ([[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]]), suggesting the ponds may serve as a refugial or monitoring site for these native taxa.
- **Trophic/behavioral notes**: [[Threadfin Shad]] were observed schooling near the surface but evaded capture, indicating an avoidance behavior toward entanglement gear or a spatial distribution not well-sampled by net placement.
- **Sampling methodology**: Gear consisted of experimental gill nets (125 ft) and trammel nets (150–300 ft) set across the three ponds between 1930–2145 hrs and retrieved between 0500–0730 hrs the following morning. Catch per unit effort (CPE) was calculated as number of fish per 100 m²/12-hr net set. Confounding factor: two nets in Pond B (fountain trammel net and waterfall gill net) had already been pulled by anglers prior to the research team's arrival, introducing unknown effort/bias into those catch data.

## Key Findings
- Total catch (31 individuals, 7 species) was **lower than typical**, even after accounting for the two prematurely-pulled nets, signaling a possible decline in overall pond fish abundance or catchability during this survey period.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (39%) and **[[Yellow Bass]]** (23%) were the dominant species, together comprising nearly two-thirds of the total catch.
- **[[Threadfin Shad]]**, normally a major component of past catches, was conspicuously absent from the net catch despite being visually observed — highlighting a discrepancy between visual survey and net-based catch data.
- A single **[[Razorback Sucker]]** recapture provided direct evidence of long-term growth (565 mm → 580 mm TL over ~22 months) and confirmed continued survival of tagged individuals of this federally endangered species within the research park pond system.
- **No [[Bonytail Chub]]** were captured, despite this species typically being encountered in small numbers (1–2 individuals) during past sampling efforts.
- **No [[Colorado Squawfish]]** were captured, consistent with their "conspicuous absence" over "the last several years," suggesting a possible local extirpation or severe decline of this species within the ASU Research Park ponds.
- All captured fish were released alive following data collection (tagging, measurement).
- A follow-up survey was tentatively planned for Spring 1997, indicating this report is part of an ongoing long-term monitoring program for both native and non-native fish species within this artificial pond ecosystem.
