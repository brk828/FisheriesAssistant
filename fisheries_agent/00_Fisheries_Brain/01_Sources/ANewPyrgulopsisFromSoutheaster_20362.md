# *Pyrgulopsis giulianii*: A New Springsnail from Southeastern California

#malacology #freshwater-biology #hydrobiidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, the third and final in a series on springsnails (Hydrobiidae) of the [[Death Valley System]], describes the new species [[Pyrgulopsis Giulianii]] from six small to moderate-sized streams in the southern [[Sierra Nevada]]. The species is separated from its closest relative, [[Pyrgulopsis Stearnsiana]], and from related forms in [[Owens Valley]] by shell form and penial morphology. The authors also use the distributions of 13 [[Pyrgulopsis]] species to propose a drainage-evolution model for the region. The model supports a highly integrated pluvial drainage in the late Quaternary and identifies several historic connections, some of them pre-Quaternary, with adjacent basins.

## Background & Description

**Scope and methods.** The paper continues earlier work on the [[Ash Meadows]] fauna (Hershler and Sada 1987) and the [[Owens Valley]] and [[Amargosa Basin]] fauna (Hershler 1989). It reports a survey of the remaining low- to mid-elevation springs and perennial streams. Shell measurements follow Hershler (1989). Collections are cited by institutional codes, including USNM, LACM, UNM and the personal collection of W. B. Miller ("WBM"). Distribution maps show one or several closely spaced localities per dot.

**Taxonomy.** The new species is placed in the family Hydrobiidae and the genus *Pyrgulopsis* Call and Pilsbry, 1886. Hershler (1989) had tentatively assigned these populations to *P. stearnsiana*. Later comparison with near-topotypical preserved material showed clear penial differences, which led to recognition of a separate species. The holotype is USNM 860444, from Sand Canyon.

**Morphology of *P. giulianii*:**
- **Shell:** small to moderate, ovate-conic, 2.0–4.0 mm high. Height/width ratio 130–170%. Four to four and a half whorls with indented sutures. Shell surface usually encrusted with brown-black deposits.
- **Operculum:** paucispiral, with 3–4 whorls and a thickened amber callus.
- **Radula:** formula 5(6)-1-5(6), 2(3,4)-1-3(4,5), 23–29, 23–35, from two populations. About 20 ctenidial filaments.
- **Penis:** small relative to the head/foot, with a reduced lobe and an elongate filament. Penial glandular ridges number 1–4, and the ventral ridge is sometimes borne on a low swelling.
- **Reproductive tract:** small seminal receptacle and bursa copulatrix, with an albumen gland roughly equal to the capsule gland.

**Etymology.** The name honours Derham Giuliani, a collector of Great Basin biota who found many new *Pyrgulopsis* localities in the Owens Valley area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality and habitat.** The type locality is a moderate-sized stream in the floor of [[Sand Canyon]], Kern County, at about 1068 m elevation. Snails were common in shallow water (< 0.5 m) on watercress.

**Distribution.** *P. giulianii* is known from six streams: five in western [[Indian Wells Valley]] (Sage, Sand, Ninemile, Grapevine and Cow canyons, plus a small streamlet in South Fork Short Canyon) and one in the adjacent [[Kern River Valley]]. Its occurrence in the Kern River Valley is linked to the historic South Fork Kern River connection to the Owens Basin.

**Pyrgulopsis in the Death Valley System.** *Pyrgulopsis* is the most diverse genus (13 species) and one of the most widespread freshwater mollusc genera in the system. Its distributions were used as a proxy for past aquatic continuity, on the assumption that non-aquatic dispersal is insignificant at large scales. Other perennial-water prosobranchs, *Tryonia* and *Assiminea*, are less diverse and more localized.

**Zoogeographic evidence:**
- [[Pyrgulopsis Wongi]] ranges from Mono Valley through Adobe and Long valleys into Owens Valley. This supports pluvial integration of the Owens River drainage.
- [[Pyrgulopsis Micrococcus]] occurs along the modern Amargosa River, at Ash Meadows, in northern Death Valley, in Panamint Valley and in the San Bernardino Mountains. This supports pluvial integration of the Amargosa and related drainages.
- Wisconsin pluvial sediments from Chicago Valley, a dry tributary of the Amargosa Basin, contain *P. micrococcus* along with *Tryonia variegata* and an undescribed *Assiminea*.

**Geological timeline (proposed model):**
- **Early Miocene (> 16 Ma):** the Mohave and region east of the Sierra drained to the Pacific, and the White-Virgin system drained east to the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Late Miocene (< 15 Ma):** [[Basin And Range Uplift]] severed the Amargosa Basin and transferred it to the Death Valley System. The Colorado was reversed and ponded as [[Lake Hualapai]].
- **Pliocene:** tilting of the Sierra Nevada beheaded trans-montane drainage and formed the Owens River. Owens Valley was forming by about 6 Ma. The San Joaquin headwaters were severed at about 3.2 Ma.
- **Late Pliocene:** uplift of the Transverse Ranges reversed the Mohave River. The Death Valley graben formed, and the South Fork Kern River was transferred to the Kern system.

**Relict populations.** *P. micrococcus* in the headwaters of Willow Creek (Spring Range) is interpreted as a pre-Wisconsin relict. The species is absent from 26 well-studied late Wisconsin sediment samples from the valley floor.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Pyrgulopsis giulianii* is distinguished from *P. stearnsiana* and from Owens Valley relatives by its combination of large size, moderately elongate shell, and small penis with a small lobe and very reduced ventral swelling.
- **Range extensions:** *P. owensensis* extends into the Walker Basin (Lahontan System), and *P. wongi* extends into Fish Lake Valley and Mono Valley. Mono Valley had previously been considered free of perennial-water mollusc species.
- **Micrococcus status:** *P. micrococcus* is very similar to *P. stearnsiana* in penial form. The main difference is the larger, longer penial lobe of *P. micrococcus*. The authors say more *P. stearnsiana* material is needed to confirm this.
- **Pluvial integration:** the distributions of *P. wongi* and *P. micrococcus* support integration of the Owens, Amargosa and Mohave drainages during the pluvial period (about 10,000–100,000 B.P.).
- **Historic connections:** the paper suggests connections between the Owens Basin and Lahontan System, Deep Springs and Fish Lake valleys, and Kern River Valley. It also suggests links between the Mohave and Los Angeles basins, between the Amargosa Basin and Frenchman Flat and Indian Springs Valley, and between the system and Saline Valley. Some of these are Quaternary and others are Neogene.
- **Affinities:** the Owens Basin *P. owensensis* group appears to be an endemic species flock. Ash Meadows contains at least two species flocks. Some Ash Meadows fauna may have been derived from the White River drainage.
- **Review:** a 1989 editorial letter (S. Cairns, Proc. Biol. Soc. Wash.) asked W. L. Minckley to review the manuscript. The review criteria covered organization, clarity, figures, literature and tables.
