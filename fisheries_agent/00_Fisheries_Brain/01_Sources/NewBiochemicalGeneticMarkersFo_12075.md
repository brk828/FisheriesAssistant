# New Biochemical Genetic Markers for Largemouth Bass

#ichthyology #marine-biology #micropterus

## Executive Summary

This 1986 study by Williamson, Carmichael, Schmidt and Morizot (*Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* 115:460-465) used starch gel [[Electrophoresis]] to find new biochemical genetic markers in [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). The authors compared four hatchery stocks: two "pure" subspecies, [[Northern Largemouth Bass]] and [[Florida Largemouth Bass]], and two domestic [[Intergrade Stocks]]. More than 60 presumptive loci were screened, at least 11 were polymorphic, and several showed allele frequency differences useful for identifying subspecies ancestry and tracking [[Hybridization And Introgression]] in hatchery and wild populations. The paper's main contribution is a panel of diagnostic markers for [[Genetic Stock Identification]] and hybrid monitoring in largemouth bass management.

## Background & Description

The largemouth bass is among the most popular sport fishes in North America and the principal warmwater predator in the United States. Its native range extends from northern Mexico east to Florida, Georgia and South Carolina, and north through the central and eastern Mississippi River drainage to the Great Lakes and southeastern Canada. Over the last century it has been widely transplanted across the contiguous United States, southern Canada and other regions.

Transplants often succeeded for sport fishing, but some populations failed to thrive in apparently suitable waters. The authors suggest this may reflect genetic maladaptation. Two latitudinally separated subspecies are recognized: the northern *M. s. salmoides* and the Florida *M. s. floridanus*. Stocking both subspecies together, often unknowingly, raised concern that local adaptation could be diluted through genetic introgression.

Electrophoresis and histochemical enzyme staining were the most practical screening tools. [[Philipp Et Al 1983]] and earlier work by Philipp and colleagues had already mapped allele clines for six enzymes, identifying "marker" alleles distinctive enough to assign subspecies even for morphologically ambiguous fish. The present study aimed to add more markers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Genetic data of this kind can measure gene flow among populations and can be correlated with environmental conditions. The authors argue that such data help determine systematic relationships, stock interactions, and which stocks are likely to perform well under specific environmental conditions. More markers also improve the accuracy of assigning individual fish to a population of origin or to a specific hatchery stock.

The study sampled fish from hatchery and impoundment settings rather than natural waters. The stocks were:

- **Northern stock (West Lake, NE Texas):** one generation removed from a wild population, reported isolated for over 50 years with no subsequent stocking. Offspring used were from the 1981 year-class (N = 30).
- **Florida stock:** a captive population from an impoundment at the [[Texas A&M University Aquaculture Research Center]] near College Station. The ancestral stock was obtained from Florida in the early 1970s; the 1981 year-class (N = 30) was used.
- **Inks Dam stock:** a domesticated intergrade used as production broodstock at the [[Inks Dam National Fish Hatchery]], near Burnet, Texas. Early analysis estimated that 5-10% of its alleles came from the Florida subspecies. The 1983 year-class (N = 30) was used.
- **Marion stock:** a domestic intergrade maintained at the [[San Marcos National Fish Hatchery And Technology Center]] since 1976, descended from a single broodfish pair selected in 1966 at the Marion (Alabama) hatchery. Its mean lateral line scale count of 61 was lower than typical for either subspecies. Harvey et al. (1980) found 15 of 21 individuals carried allozymes of both subspecies. The 1981 year-class (N = 30) was used.

## Key Findings

**Methods and scope**

- Tissue samples (liver and skeletal muscle) were taken from anesthetized fish (0.2% tricaine) and stored at -90°C.
- Vertical starch gel electrophoresis followed established protocols, using TVB (pH 8.0) and TC (pH 7.0) buffers.
- 44 enzyme systems coded by more than 60 loci were assessed for polymorphism.
- Gene nomenclature followed [[Human Gene Nomenclature]] conventions, with alleles designated "fast" (f) or "slow" (s) by anodal mobility.

**Polymorphism results**

- At least 11 of the 60+ loci scored were polymorphic (Table 1).
- Six of these were previously described by Philipp et al. (1983). Two showed fixed subspecific differences, and two or three showed known allele frequency differences.
- Five polymorphic loci were reported for largemouth bass for the first time: [[Adenosine Deaminase]], [[Alcohol Dehydrogenase]], [[Galactose-1-Phosphate Uridyltransferase]], [[Mannosephosphate Isomerase]], and [[Peptidase-3]]. [[Triosephosphate Isomerase-2]] was also newly characterized in this paper.
- Previously known variable loci reported here include [[Superoxide Dismutase-2]], [[Alpha-Glycerophosphate Dehydrogenase-A]], [[Malate Dehydrogenase-1]], [[Glutamate-Oxaloacetate Transaminase-B]], and [[Isocitrate Dehydrogenase-B]].

**Key diagnostic markers**

- Alleles restricted to Florida or intergrade populations were found at superoxide dismutase-2, alpha-glycerophosphate dehydrogenase-A, adenosine deaminase, alcohol dehydrogenase, galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase, and mannosephosphate isomerase.
- Variants restricted to northern and intergrade populations were found at malate dehydrogenase-1, peptidase-3, and triosephosphate isomerase-2.
- Some examples from Table 1:
  - Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase fast allele: 0.958 in the Florida stock and absent in the northern stock. The northern stock was fixed for the slow allele.
  - Mannosephosphate isomerase fast allele: 0.500 in the Florida stock and absent in all other stocks.
  - Adenosine deaminase slow allele: 0.283 in the Florida stock and 0.017 in Marion, with no occurrence in the northern or Inks Dam stocks.
  - Alcohol dehydrogenase slow allele: 0.383 in the Florida stock and 0.017 in Marion.
  - Peptidase-3 slow allele: 0.567 in the northern stock and 0.667 in Marion, with a markedly lower frequency in Inks Dam (0.225) and a fast allele in the Florida stock absent (0.000).

**Recommendations for management**

- The authors suggest fixing eight Florida-specific alleles in a hatchery stock to monitor survival and admixture of Florida genomes in northern populations. The eight loci are ADA slow, ADH fast, GALT fast, GOTB alleles 3 and 4, aGPDH fast, IDHB allele 3, MPI fast, and SOD2 slow.
- Under this scheme, after two generations of possible introgression, at least four of the eight loci are expected to be heterozygous for Florida alleles if the loci are unlinked.
- Hybrids should remain detectable after three generations of backcrossing to northern parents, since on average two of the eight loci should still be heterozygous.
- Three polymorphisms restricted to northern populations further improve the chance of correct hybrid identification.
- A preponderance of highly homozygous or highly heterozygous individuals carrying both Florida and northern allozymes would indicate further generations of hybridization.

**Limitations noted by the authors**

- Larger samples from northern populations are needed to confirm whether certain variants are restricted to Florida and intergrade populations.
- Heterozygotes for GALT are difficult to resolve because allozymes have similar mobilities.
- ADH variation was not detected by Philipp et al. (1983), probably because gel and buffer conditions were unsuitable for cathodal migration.
- PEP3 variation requires high-quality starch and long run times.
- Variation in triosephosphate isomerase-2 across more populations still needs to be assessed.
