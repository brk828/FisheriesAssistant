# Fish Remains from Deer Creek Cave, Elko County, Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #zooarchaeology #prehistoric-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This archaeological report documents fish remains recovered from [[Deer Creek Cave]] in [[Elko County]], Nevada, near the confluence of [[Deer Creek]] and the [[Jarbidge River]], at approximately 5,800 feet elevation. The collection consists of 24 identifiable skeletal elements representing [[Chinook Salmon]] (*[[Oncorhynchus tshawytscha]]*), radiocarbon dated to 2,585±150 years before present. The remains represent at least two individuals, one approximately 28 inches long and 8 pounds in weight. The paucity of fish remains at this site contrasts sharply with the extensive fish utilization documented elsewhere in the [[Columbia River Basin]], suggesting the [[Deer Creek Cave]] people relied primarily on non-fish food sources despite the historical availability of salmon and resident fish species in the [[Jarbidge River]].

## Background & Description

[[Chinook Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*), also known as king salmon, quinnat salmon, spring salmon, and tyee salmon, is a highly regarded anadromous food fish. Under primitive conditions, chinook salmon entered the [[Columbia River]] in vast numbers during spawning migrations, with the Columbia regarded as the world's most productive salmon river until at least 1875. A portion of the spawning run ascended the [[Snake River]] and tributaries including the [[Bruneau River]], with some fish ascending the [[Jarbidge River]] into Nevada and spawning in headwaters during October.

The skeletal elements recovered include: opercles (left and right), centra (15 complete specimens), articular (left proximal fragment), dentary (left distal fragment), parasphenoid (right side fragment), epiotic (left fragment), and frontal (left fragment). The right opercle measures 47 mm long and 28 mm wide with a glenoid fossa 10 mm in greatest diameter. Centra range from 8 to 10 mm in greatest diameter, with the largest measuring 9.6 mm.

The opercle of *O. tshawytscha* is distinguishable from other American salmonids by the extensive network of oblique pits with sharply delineated arcuate rims radiating from the glenoid fossa region. The centra display characteristic trabecular structure with relatively large interspaces adjacent to centrum rims.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Deer Creek Cave]] is located approximately 4 miles north of Jarbidge, Nevada, and 4 miles south of the Idaho boundary. Fish remains were recovered from depths of 30-54 inches, with charcoal from 30-32 inches yielding a radiocarbon date of 2,585±150 years BP.

Under primitive conditions, the [[Jarbidge River]] almost certainly supported multiple edible nonmigratory fish species including: [[Prosopium williamsoni]] (mountain whitefish), [[Salmo gairdnerii irideus]] (resident rainbow trout), [[Salmo clarkii lewisi]] (cutthroat trout), [[Salvelinus malma]] (Dolly Varden), [[Catostomus columbianus]] (sucker), and [[Ptychocheilus oregonensis]] (northern squawfish). These species were extensively utilized by aborigines elsewhere in western North America.

The lower [[Bruneau River]] during the early 19th century was within the territory of the [[Northern Paiute]] band known as Koa'aga'itka ("salmon eaters"). The [[Nevada Shoshoni]], whose territory included the [[Deer Creek Cave]] region, are documented to have made some use of fish resources.

## Key Findings

**Species Identification**: All 24 fish remains represent a single species, *[[Oncorhynchus tshawytscha]]* (chinook salmon). The collection represents two or more individuals, with one specimen approximately 28 inches in length and perhaps 8 pounds in weight.

**Skeletal Material Distribution**:
- Pit 1, Trench B (30-36 inches): 1 left opercle proximal fragment (Catalog No. 377)
- Pit 2, Trench B (42-48 inches): 1 left articular, 15 complete centra, 1 left dentary, 1 left opercle, 1 right opercle, 1 parasphenoid fragment (Catalog No. 360)
- Pit 2, Trench B (48-54 inches): 1 left epiotic, 1 left frontal, 1 left opercle (Catalog No. 361)

**Subsistence Pattern Anomaly**: The paucity of fish remains at [[Deer Creek Cave]] contrasts dramatically with extensive fish utilization documented elsewhere in the [[Columbia River Basin]], where salmon was "the chief single factor in the diet of the Indians of the Columbia Basin in their native state." The absence of remains from nonmigratory resident fish species that were certainly available in the [[Jarbidge River]] further emphasizes this pattern.

**Hypotheses for Low Fish Utilization**: Two potential explanations are proposed: (1) downstream populations (possibly predecessors of the Koa'aga'itka "salmon eaters") may have intercepted salmon runs to such an extent that few fish reached upper tributaries like the [[Jarbidge River]]; (2) the [[Deer Creek Cave]] people relied principally on food sources other than fish, despite the availability of both anadromous and resident fish species.

**Archaeological Significance**: The small number of salmon remains and complete absence of other fish species indicates that fish played a minimal role in the subsistence economy of the [[Deer Creek Cave]] occupants circa 2,585 years BP, representing a significant departure from typical [[Columbia River Basin]] subsistence patterns.
