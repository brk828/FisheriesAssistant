# Gila Trout Reclassification from Endangered to Threatened with a Special (4(d)) Rule

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register final rule (71 FR 40657, July 18, 2006), issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service), reclassifies the [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) from endangered to threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The Service finds the species no longer in danger of extinction throughout a significant portion of its range, while concluding it is likely to become endangered in the foreseeable future. The rule adds a special 4(d) regulation that allows [[Recreational Fishing]] for Gila trout in specified waters of New Mexico and Arizona, excluding four relict populations. The document also responds to 27 public comments and summarizes the species' recovery history, threats and conservation measures.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Gila trout, a native salmonid of the upper [[Gila River]] system in southwestern New Mexico and east-central Arizona. Its ancestry is shared with [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Apache Trout]] (Behnke 1992, 2002; Dowling and Childs 1992; Utter and Allendorf 1994).
- **Listing history:** Listed as endangered in 1967 under the Federal Endangered Species Preservation Act (32 FR 4001); critical habitat was not designated because the species was listed before the 1978 amendments to the Act.
- **Recovery plans:** Original plan (1979), revised in 1984, 1993 and 2003. Downlisting criteria tightened over time; the 2003 plan requires four non-hybridized indigenous lineages replicated in at least 85 km (55 mi) of streams, geographic separation of replicates, and an [[Emergency Evacuation Plan]].
- **Earlier attempt:** A 1987 proposal to downlist (52 FR 37424) was withdrawn in 1997 (56 FR 46400) after 1988 flooding, 1989 wildfires, hatchery shortfalls and brown trout predation in Iron Creek.
- **Lineages:** Four original pure ("relict") populations: Main Diamond, South Diamond, Spruce and Whiskey Creeks. Table 1 of the rule lists their replicates (roughly 12 populations in total).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Population status**
- Wild population was estimated at fewer than 10,000 fish older than age 1 in 1992; New Mexico's population was estimated at 37,000 fish in 2001. No current estimate was available for the newly replicated Arizona populations.
- Replicated populations in New Mexico are reproducing; Raspberry Creek (Arizona) showed successful recruitment in 2004 but no documented spawning in 2005; Dude Creek is no longer considered viable.
- Whiskey Creek was replicated into Langstroth Canyon on June 21, 2006.

**Threats and stressors**
- **Wildfire and post-fire flooding:** High-severity fires extirpated populations (Main Diamond Creek 1989; South Diamond including Burnt Canyon 1995; Upper Little Creek 2003), with further losses at Trail and Woodrow Canyons and Sacaton Creek (1996).
- **Nonnative salmonids and hybridization:** Iron and McKenna Creek populations hybridized with rainbow trout and are not considered pure. Nonnative trout are absent from streams holding pure or replicated populations, protected by barriers.
- **Livestock grazing:** Historically severe; now managed, with grazing suspended or cattle excluded on the creeks occupied by Gila trout. Raspberry Creek's grazing was reduced to 46 cattle.
- **Disease:** [[Bacterial Kidney Disease]] was found in low levels in some brown trout and hybrid populations but not in Gila trout; [[Whirling Disease]] has no documented cases in the Gila drainage, and Gila trout are considered vulnerable if exposed.
- **Overutilization:** Streams have been closed to angling since listing; illegal fishing evidence is limited (tackle found on a few occasions).
- **Habitat (water quality):** Sensitive to warm temperatures; in clear cold streams *Tubifex* worm (secondary host of whirling disease parasite) is seldom abundant, and infection rates are low below 10 °C (50 °F).

**Protected areas:** Ten of eleven New Mexico populations lie within Gila or Aldo Leopold Wilderness; Raspberry Creek lies in the Blue Range Primitive Area.

## Key Findings

1. **Reclassification:** Gila trout is downlisted to threatened with a special 4(d) rule, effective on publication. The species is not considered in danger of extinction but is still likely to become endangered without continued management.
2. **Population growth:** Populations increased from 4 original pure populations (with replicates bringing the total to 12 in the rule's summary, or 13 with Langstroth Canyon) and wild numbers rose markedly between 1992 and 2001.
3. **Special rule scope:** Recreational fishing will be permitted only in waters designated by New Mexico and Arizona state agencies, working with the Service. The four relict populations (Main Diamond, South Diamond, Spruce and Whiskey Creeks) remain closed.
4. **Justification:** Excess hatchery fish from the [[Broodstock Management]] program can be placed in non-recovery streams, avoiding overcrowding or euthanasia of surplus fish. Angling revenue through the [[Sport Fish Restoration Program]] is expected to increase recovery funding (NMDGF received $3,258,275 and AGFD $3,556,697 in fiscal year 2004; Service spending on Gila trout was estimated at $137,500 in 2004).
5. **Sustainability:** Modeling by Brown et al. (2001) found that up to 15 percent annual angling mortality of adult Gila trout had no effect on population viability.
6. **No critical habitat:** Not required, because the species was listed before 1978 and most populations are already protected in designated wilderness.
7. **Public response:** The Service addressed 27 comments. Concerns about illegal take, pesticide use (Antimycin), grazing and prescribed fire were answered, with the Service citing [[Section 7 Consultation]] and state regulatory processes as safeguards.
8. **Ongoing obligations:** The [[Emergency Evacuation Plan]] was invoked in 2002, 2003 and 2004; state regulations will govern any angling, and monitoring is planned to detect negative effects.
9. **Prohibitions retained:** Take, possession and trade of Gila trout remain prohibited except as authorized by state law, scientific permit, or the special rule; habitat destruction and introduction of nonnative trout are also prohibited.
