# Analysis of the Gammarus-pecos Complex (Crustacea: Amphipoda) in Texas and New Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

*Note: this is a crustacean study. The tags above are the required template set; the taxon-specific tag is #gammaridae, and the document covers amphipods rather than fish.*

## Executive Summary

Cole (1985, *Journal of the Arizona-Nevada Academy of Science* 20(2): 93–103) compares seven populations of the [[Gammarus-Pecos Complex]], amphipod crustaceans from spring and pool waters in areas once overlain by Permian seas in West Texas and southeastern New Mexico. Three species were already described: [[Gammarus pecos]], [[Gammarus hyalelloides]] and [[Gammarus desperatus]]. Using 20 [[Mann-Whitney U Test]] comparisons of morphological characters, the author concludes that the San Solomon population is conspecific with *G. pecos*, that the Phantom Lake canal form (C) is probably a new species, that the Carlsbad population (E) probably represents another new species, and that the M and H animals appear conspecific. The author estimates that at least two undescribed species remain to be named from the saline springs of the region.

## Background & Description

- **Geological setting:** The populations occur in sulfatochloride waters derived ultimately from Permian marine sediments, across the Roswell Artesian Basin, the West Texas Permian Basin (including the Toyah Basin) and the Delaware Basin. All known populations lie within 90 km of the [[Pecos River]].
- **Sampling:** Material was examined from field collections (1965–1979) and from loans of the [[Smithsonian Institution]] National Museum of Natural History (USNM). Populations E and M were studied only from loaned material.
- **Morphology:** The animals are banded, have elongate reniform eyes, non-calceolate second antennae, C-setae on the mandibular palps and narrow oostegites (brood lamellae). Coxal plates 1–4 and pereopod bases are armed with long setae in the larger individuals.
- **Systematic context:** The group is compared with [[Gammarus Fasciatus]], [[Gammarus Tigrinus]] and the genus *Lagunogammarus*, proposed by Sket (1971) and later synonymized with *Gammarus* by Barnard and Barnard (1983).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Salinity:** The author treats the complex as euryhaline. Using Remane's (1971) scheme (fresh <0.5 ‰, oligohaline 0.5–3 ‰, miohaline 3–8 ‰), the habitats range from fresh to miohaline. Estimated salinities from filtrable residues:

| Site | Salinity (‰), as reported |
|---|---|
| Lander Spring Brook (Noel, 1954) | 3.87 |
| North Spring, Roswell (Cole, 1981) | 5.02 |
| Diamond Y Spring (Cole & Bousfield, 1970) | 5.85 |
| San Solomon Spring (Cole, 1981) | 1.85 |
| Phantom Lake Spring (White et al., 1941) | 2.03 |

The author also reports Phantom Lake Spring values of 0.123–2.03 ‰ across studies (lowest to highest salinity). North Spring had a specific conductance of 17.6 mS/cm at 25 °C. The author suggests that poikilohalinity may apply to these athalassohaline habitats.

**Habitat notes:** Populations occur in springs, spring outflows, canals and a pool. The author states that the complex is not restricted to springs along the Pecos River. Searches at Villanueva and Santa Rosalía, which lie outside the former Permian sea area, found no gammarids.

**Distribution:** The group represents the southernmost inland *Gammarus* species in North America. *G. desperatus* in Roswell is the northernmost known member.

## Key Findings

**Population relationships (from the 20 Mann-Whitney U tests):**
- *P* (*G. pecos*, Diamond Y Spring) and *S* (San Solomon Spring) are conspecific. *S* shows some affinity to the larger canal form *C*.
- *C* (Phantom Lake canal system) is probably a new species, most closely related to *M* and *H*.
- *D* (*G. desperatus*) is unique but shows some relationship to *E*.
- *E* (Carlsbad pool) probably represents a new species.
- *M* and *H* seem to be conspecific, and *M* may be a population of Phantom Lake Spring itself.

**Diagnostic characters:**
- All members lack calceoli and bear C-setae on the mandibular palps. Only *G. fasciatus*, *G. tigrinus* and the complex share these C-setae among North American *Gammarus*.
- The first antennule peduncle carries at least one posterodistal spine. This is shared with only a few other inland species.
- Mandibular palp setation, pereopod 7 basis armament and short coxal plate 1 setae (0.13–0.18 mm) distinguish the complex from *G. fasciatus* and *G. tigrinus*. Setae on coxal plate 1 are longer in those two species (up to 0.54 mm and 0.43 mm).
- The *H*, *M* and *C* group has fewer and shorter setae overall, with the smallest individuals in *H* and *M*. The *D* and *E* group has the highest counts of many setal characters.

**Size and morphology:**
- Mean male total lengths (mm): P 13.52, D 12.98, S 12.76, C 10.71, E 9.85, M 8.79, H 7.06.
- The first brood lamellae are strap-like, wider than in *G. zaddachi* but narrow in comparison with other North American species.
- Uropod 3 endopod/exopod ratios exceed 0.6 in all complex members, with *G. zaddachi* from Norway averaging 0.62.

**Taxonomic conclusions:**
- Adding the described and undescribed species, the author estimates about 14 freshwater and euryhaline North American *Gammarus* species, under stated synonymy assumptions.
- A sixth species of the complex may have existed in Lander Spring Brook, but it is now extinct. Noel's 1950 USNM specimens were not re-examined for this report.

**Limitations noted by the author:** The population count is "seven (possibly only six)." Some populations were studied only from museum loans. The author says that intraspecific variation in calceoli and some characters is not fully known.
