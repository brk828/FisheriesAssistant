## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Craig Brook National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 5) facility located in East Orland, Maine, established in 1880 (leased site) and formally purchased in 1889 under 25 Statute 954. The hatchery's core mission is the propagation of [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*) to restore self-sustaining, sea-run populations in rivers of the northeastern United States, specifically the **Penobscot River**, **Machias River**, and **Dennys River** drainages. During the reporting fiscal year, the station transitioned its production strategy from smolt-based to fry-based stocking, and completed infrastructure renovations enabling it to hold seven distinct genetic strains of [[Atlantic Salmon]] broodstock.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species Focus:** [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*), an anadromous salmonid.
- **Life History Strategy:** The hatchery manages multiple life-history components of the species, including **sea-run adult returns**, **kelt** (post-spawning adults), **eggs**, and **fry**, indicating active management of the full anadromous life cycle.
- **Watershed Management:** The station supports three genetically/geographically distinct river populations:
  - [[Penobscot River]] drainage (primary production focus, largest egg/fry output)
  - [[Machias River]] drainage
  - [[Dennys River]] drainage (downeast Maine river system)
- **Genetic Stock Diversity:** The facility currently maintains and holds **seven different strains** of [[Atlantic Salmon]], reflecting a strategy to preserve river-specific genetic diversity among sea-run populations.
- **Broodstock Source:** Adult sea-run returns are collected directly from the [[Dennys River]] and [[Machias River]] for use as broodstock, linking hatchery operations directly to wild population restoration.
- **Program Shift:** A documented shift from **smolt production** to **fry production** reflects a change in stocking ecology strategy, targeting downeast river systems and the Penobscot River basin specifically, likely to allow salmon to imprint and rear naturally in-river from an earlier life stage.

- **Egg Production (Sea-run):**
  - Penobscot River: 1,881,872 eggs
  - Machias River: 50,078 eggs
  - Dennys River: 19,339 sea-run eggs + 8,590 kelt eggs = **27,929 total eggs**
- **Fry Stocking (Spring):**
  - Penobscot drainage: 1,174,670 fry
  - Dennys River: 32,700 fry
- **Fish Distribution to Federal Waters (excludes eggs/fry):**
  - FY91: 274,512
  - FY92: 1,616,785
  - FY93: 1,174,670
- **Funding Trends (Operations, $000):** Increased steadily from $201,200 (1990) to an estimated $215,000 (1995).
- **Maintenance Funding:** Sharp increase projected from $500,418 (1990 actual) to an estimated $6 million (1995), suggesting major planned capital/infrastructure investment.
- **Staffing (FTE):** Stable at 4.25 FTE from 1990–1993, increasing to 5.0 FTE by 1995.
- **Physical Infrastructure:** Hatchery building (office/garage/rearing room/incubator/conference room/feed prep), water treatment system, two residences, shop/garage, two pump houses, two bath houses, a visitor center, **20 raceways**, **4 Burrows-type pools**, and **4 Swedish pots** (rearing/holding units).
- **Program Milestone:** Completion of renovations enabling simultaneous holding of seven distinct Atlantic salmon strains represents a significant operational and conservation genetics achievement for the station.
- **Strategic Shift:** Transition from smolt to fry stocking programs marks a notable management change aimed at restoring [[Atlantic Salmon]] populations across all downeast Maine river systems and the Penobscot River basin.
