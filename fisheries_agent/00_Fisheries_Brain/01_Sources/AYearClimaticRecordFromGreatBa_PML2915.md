# A 250,000-Year Climatic Record from Great Basin Vein Calcite: Implications for Milankovitch Theory

#aquatic-ecology #paleoclimatology #groundwater #hydrogeology

## Executive Summary

This paper reports a continuous oxygen-isotope (δ18O) record of about 250,000 years, measured in dated calcite veins from the groundwater system at [[Devils Hole]], [[Ash Meadows]], Nevada. The calcite precipitated from groundwater, so its isotopic composition tracks the temperature and precipitation of recharge areas in the Spring Mountains and Sheep Range. The authors use [[Uranium-Series Dating]] to build the chronology, and they compare the record with the [[Marine Oxygen Isotope Record]], an [[Antarctic Ice Core]] record and [[Insolation Forcing]] curves. They conclude that two rapid warming transitions, at about 272 ka and 147 ka, are Great Basin equivalents of marine Terminations III and II. The 147 ka date for Termination II is about 17,000 years earlier than the conventional marine age of about 130 ka. This challenges the strict [[Milankovitch Theory]] timing of glacial terminations.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Ash Meadows is the principal discharge area of a regional carbonate aquifer. Groundwater flows through fractured carbonate rocks from recharge areas more than 1,000 m above sea level, and it emerges at the surface in springs, including the seven largest springs and Devils Hole, a fault-controlled spring pool.
- **Sample:** The analysed vein calcite, designated DH 2, was collected from a fault zone near the centre of the modern spring lineament, at a depth below the water table that is garbled in the scanned text.
- **Geochemical rationale:** Dissolved uranium and thorium were effectively absent from the water (<0.01 ppb), so the authors treated the calcite as a closed system. Their estimate of Ash Meadows groundwater temperature agreed closely with measured spring temperatures.
- **Chronology:** Ages are determined by [[Uranium-Series Dating]] (230Th-234U-238U). The authors used a regression equation to convert ages for the sampled calcite.
- **Interpretation of δ18O:** Heavier isotopic values indicate relatively warm conditions and lighter values indicate relatively cold conditions, in line with the temperature and precipitation signal of recharge.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Groundwater residence time** is the main uncertainty. The authors estimate it at a few thousand years during glacial climates and up to about 16,000 years during and after the transition to interglacial conditions. Because the residence time is poorly known, the calcite ages are likely to be older than the climate events they record. The authors state that this limitation makes their interpretations conservative.

**Regional signals:** The [[Great Basin]] record shows a sawtooth pattern of warm interglacial or interpluvial periods, each followed by progressively cooler stadial and interstadial episodes. The authors describe this as the Great Basin counterpart of the marine δ18O pattern.

**Continental comparison:** Records from [[Searles Lake]] (about 140 km from Devils Hole, extending over 3 million years) and speleothem records from high-latitude caves are cited as independent evidence. Searles Lake deepening at the marine Termination II boundary, and its desiccation at Termination I, indicate that the continental records do not simply mirror the marine record.

**Sea-level evidence:** Several sea-level studies indicate a high stand at or above present level at 135–140 ka and a higher one at 120–125 ka. The authors argue that these data imply that deglaciation was well under way by about 140 ka, which is consistent with the DH 2 timing.

## Key Findings

1. **Two rapid warming transitions:** The δ18O record indicates rapid warming beginning at about 272 ka and 147 ka (Termination III and Termination II equivalents). The authors suggest that the true dates may be up to about 16,000 years earlier because of groundwater residence time.
2. **Termination II timing:** The DH 2 record places Termination II at about 147 ka, which conflicts with the widely accepted marine age of about 130 ka. The authors argue that a 147 ka age is consistent with the marine record only if the marine chronology is revised.
3. **Milankovitch pattern:** The intervals between warm-period peaks and cold-period troughs (about 60–120, 160–250 and 280–310 ka) suggest a relation to the obliquity and precession cycles. Spectral analysis of the full data set shows periods of about 126.5, 62.x, 32.2, 42.5, 27.6, 34.5, 50.6 and 23 thousand years. Terminations II and III are separated by about 135,000 years, close to the 123 and 131 ka eccentricity cycles.
4. **Time offset:** Compared with the marine δ18O and [[Antarctic Ice Core]] records, the DH 2 record appears to lead by about 17,000 years. The authors conclude that the DH 2 timing is not simply a reflection of the marine chronology.
5. **Insolation link uncertain:** The authors note that ice volume, temperature, storm tracks and precipitation source may also influence the δ18O record. They propose that DH 2 may reflect a groundwater or regional climate signal that is not directly tied to insolation.
6. **Independent support for an older Termination II:** Searles Lake, speleothem records and sea-level data together provide what the authors call strong independent support for a pre-147 ka age, rather than the conventional 130 ka age.
7. **Unresolved discrepancies:** The authors acknowledge several mismatches. These include the 135 ka sea-level high, which is not clearly reflected in DH 2, and high sea levels at 40–100 ka, when the marine δ18O record suggests low sea level. They conclude that much remains to be learned.

**Overall conclusion:** The Devils Hole record is a continental, independently dated proxy that challenges the strict timing of the marine δ18O chronology and supports a Termination II age of about 147 ka. The authors conclude that the timing of some glacial terminations may need to be revised, and that continental and marine records must be reconciled before Milankovitch predictions can be fully tested.
