# Survival of F1 Hybrids Between Cyprinodont Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1959 study by Hubbs and Drewry (University of Texas Institute of Marine Science) examines the survival of F1 hybrids produced by artificially crossing five species of cyprinodont fishes native to the Port Aransas, Texas area: **[[Fundulus grandis]]**, **[[Fundulus similis]]**, **[[Lucania parva]]**, **[[Adinia xenica]]**, and **[[Cyprinodon variegatus]]**. The authors demonstrate that hybrid survival is strongly asymmetric between reciprocal crosses (i.e., dependent on which species supplies the eggs vs. sperm) and, more broadly, that the degree of hybrid developmental success correlates with the phylogenetic closeness of the parental species. Notably, a **Fundulus × Cyprinodon** hybrid — representing a cross previously classified between different subfamilies — hatched and fed, challenging existing subfamily-level taxonomy within the Cyprinodontidae and supporting a proposed reclassification at the tribal level.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic scope**: All parental species belong to the family Cyprinodontidae, historically split into subfamilies Fundulinae (*Fundulus*, *Adinia*, *Lucania*) and Cyprinodontinae (*Cyprinodon*).
- **Study system**: Gametes were manually stripped from wild-caught adults collected near Port Aransas, Texas, and zygotes were cultured in glass dishes following [[Strawn and Hubbs 1956]] protocols.
- **Rearing conditions**: Water temperature 26–31°C; salinity ~30 ppt (matching natural estuarine/marine conditions for these species).
- **Developmental scoring criterion**: Following [[Hubbs and Strawn 1957a]], development was only counted as valid hybridization once eye pigmentation appeared, to avoid misattributing gynogenetic (maternal-only) development to true hybridization.
- **Historical context**: Builds on early 20th-century hybridization work by Newman (1908, 1914) at Woods Hole and Moenkhaus (1910), as well as Öztan's (1954) work on Turkish cyprinodontids, extending these studies with a taxonomically richer Gulf Coast fauna.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Marine vs. freshwater hybrid frequency**: The authors invoke [[Hubbs 1955]]'s observation that natural hybridization is much rarer among marine/estuarine fishes than freshwater fishes, attributing this partly to the greater environmental stability of marine habitats reducing opportunities/pressures for hybridization, and partly to intrinsic hybrid inviability documented here.
- **Niche availability hypothesis**: The authors speculate that even where hybrids survive to some degree, they may fail to persist in nature due to an absence of a suitable ecological niche, serving as an additional post-zygotic isolating mechanism beyond simple viability.
- **Comparative subfamily patterns**: Contrasts sharply with freshwater darters (subfamily Etheostomatinae), where nearly any hybrid combination succeeds and hybrid survival often equals or exceeds parental controls — unlike the Fundulinae/Cyprinodontinae complex studied here, where most hybrids show reduced survival relative to controls.
- **Egg size and yolk assimilation**: The paper evaluates (and partially refutes) Newman's (1908, 1914) hypothesis that reciprocal hybrid hatching failure is driven by inability of small paternal genomes to metabolize large maternal yolk reserves — noting several contradictory cases in the current dataset (e.g., [[Fundulus grandis]] eggs hatched successfully with much smaller-egged [[Lucania parva]] and [[Cyprinodon variegatus]] sperm).

## Key Findings

### Hybrid Survival by Cross (Table 1 summary)
| Maternal × Paternal | Outcome |
|---|---|
| *F. grandis* × *F. similis* | Successful hatching and larval survival; fastest-growing lab fish was this hybrid (possible heterosis) |
| *F. similis* × *F. grandis* | Did **not** survive hatching period (near-total failure) |
| *F. grandis* × *L. parva* | Reduced survival vs. controls, but larvae survived |
| *L. parva* × *F. grandis* | Lower survival; no fish survived larval stages |
| *F. grandis* × *A. xenica* | Did not hatch |
| *A. xenica* × *F. grandis* | Hatched, reduced survival vs. control |
| *F. grandis* × *C. variegatus* | Fertilization successful; hatching failed, but one hybrid larva fed for ~1 week before dying — deformed but phenotypically intermediate |
| *C. variegatus* × *F. grandis* | No fertilization achieved (tried 3×) |
| *A. xenica* × *C. variegatus* | Fertilized but failed to hatch |

### Reciprocal Asymmetry
- Hybrid outcomes were **highly dependent on maternal parent identity**, mirroring classic results from *F. heteroclitus* × *F. majalis* crosses (sibling species to *F. grandis*/*F. similis*) reported by Newman and Moenkhaus.
- This asymmetry is attributed to incompatibility between maternal cytoplasm/enzymes and paternal chromatin, becoming critical later in ontogeny as paternal gene expression increases.

### Fundulus × Cyprinodon Hybrid — Key Result
- One larva from the *F. grandis* (♀) × *C. variegatus* (♂) cross hatched, fed, and survived about one week.
- Displayed intermediate phenotypic traits (head shape, peritoneal and opercular melanophore patterns, mid-dorsal band width) confirming true biparental hybrid status rather than gynogenesis.
- Also displayed deformities: postoccipital hump, ventral bend at the anus, sharp caudal peduncle bend — likely reflecting incompatibility late in development.
- This is a substantially greater developmental success than Newman's (1914) attempts at the same cross, which mostly died at or before gastrulation — suggesting Newman's failures may have been gynogenetic artifacts rather than true fertilization failures.

### Timing of Hatching
- Hybrids hatched over a **much wider time window** than either parental control.
- Earliest hybrids hatched at times intermediate between parental rates (supporting Newman 1914), while the latest hybrids hatched much later than either control (supporting Moenkhaus 1910) — suggesting both prior models are partially correct depending on developmental stage examined.

### Hybridization–Phylogeny Correlation Framework
The authors propose four tentative hierarchical levels linking developmental milestones to taxonomic rank of parental relationship:

| Developmental Milestone | Taxonomic Boundary |
|---|---|
| Hatching | Intraordinal : Interfamilial |
| Feeding | Intrafamilial : Intertribal |
| Rearing to size | Intratribal : Intergeneric |
| Fertility | Intrageneric : Interspecific |

- Many purported "interfamilial" or "interordinal" hybrids from older literature (Moenkhaus, Newman) are reinterpreted as likely **gynogenetic false hybrids** (Hertwig's *Falsche Bastarde*) rather than true hybrids, since paternal chromatin is often eliminated or inactivated during early cleavage in distantly related crosses.
- The successful **Fundulus × Cyprinodon** hybrid supports [[Miller 1956]]'s morphology-based argument that Fundulinae and Cyprinodontinae should be merged, or at minimum that the taxonomic split should be reduced from subfamily to tribal rank.
- Comparable interfamilial successes are discussed for other fish groups: *Tautogolabrus* × *Stenotomus* (Labridae × Sparidae) and *Fundulus* × *Menidia* (Cyprinodontidae × Atherinidae) hybrids, the latter suggesting cyprinodonts and atherinids may be more closely related than traditional classification (different orders) implies — consistent with views of [[Regan 1911]], [[Hubbs 1924]], and others linking cyprinodonts to spiny-rayed fish lineages.
- Hybrid fertility (not just viability) is argued to be a stronger diagnostic of congeneric relationship, following patterns noted across salmonids, sunfishes, and loaches in the broader comparative literature cited.

### Broader Taxonomic Implication
- The data support reclassifying the *Fundulus*/*Adinia*/*Lucania* vs. *Cyprinodon* split from the subfamily level down to the **tribal level**, given documented hybrid viability across this boundary — an explicit challenge to classifications by [[Hubbs 1924]], [[Myers 1931]], and [[Berg 1947]].
