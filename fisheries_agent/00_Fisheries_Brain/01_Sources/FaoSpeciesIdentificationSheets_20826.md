# FAO Species Identification Sheets: Holocentridae of the Western Central Atlantic

#ichthyology #marine-biology #holocentridae #squirrelfishes #soldierfishes

## Executive Summary

This FAO species identification guide covers the family [[Holocentridae]] (squirrelfishes and soldierfishes) in Western Central Atlantic fishing area 31. The family comprises two subfamilies: [[Holocentrinae]] and [[Myripristinae]], with 11 species documented in the region. Detailed identification sheets are provided for three species: [[Holocentrus ascensionis]] (Squirrelfish), [[Holocentrus rufus]] (Longspine squirrelfish), and [[Myripristis jacobus]] (Blackbar soldierfish). These predominantly nocturnal, reef-associated fishes are characterized by elongate compressed bodies, large eyes, serrated head bones, and distinctive fin ray counts. While never very abundant, larger species occur regularly in local markets, particularly in artisanal fisheries along the Venezuelan coast.

## Background & Description

### Family Characteristics

The [[Holocentridae]] are distinguished by a suite of morphological features: elongate, laterally compressed bodies with slender caudal peduncles; rounded upper head profile with moderately developed mucous channels; serrate or spiny edges on membrane bones; large eyes; nearly horizontal mouth with protractile upper jaw and two supramaxillae present. Dentition consists of minute teeth in bands on jaws, enlarged near symphysis, with teeth present on vomer and palatines in all species, and additionally on ectopterygoids in [[Holocentrus]] and [[Adioryx]].

The dorsal fin base extends over most of the back, with the spiny portion 3-4 times longer than the soft portion. Diagnostic fin ray counts include 11 or 12 spines in the dorsal fin, pectoral fins with a weak short spine, pelvic fins with 1 spine and 7 soft rays (distinctive for the family), and always 4 anal-fin spines (the third enlarged in [[Holocentrinae]]). The caudal fin is forked with 17 branched rays. The lateral line is complete; scales may be smooth or ridged with spines on posterior margins, but lateral-line scales are not enlarged. Body coloration is generally reddish.

### Subfamily and Generic Distinctions

**Subfamily [[Holocentrinae]]**: Preopercle with sharp angle and strong spine much longer than broad; 11 spines in anterior dorsal fin (10 in [[Flammeo]]); 7-10 soft anal-fin rays. Three genera occur in the area:

- **[[Holocentrus]]**: Upper caudal lobe notably longer than lower; anterior soft dorsal-fin rays elongate; 15-18 gill rakers on lower limb of first gill arch
- **[[Flammeo]]**: Last dorsal spine close to first dorsal ray and membrane-connected; lower jaw projecting beyond upper, entering profile; 10-14 gill rakers
- **[[Adioryx]]**: Last dorsal spine separated from first dorsal ray; lower jaw equal to or shorter than upper; 10-14 gill rakers

**Subfamily [[Myripristinae]]**: Preopercle crescentric, usually without spine (except short triangular projection in [[Ostichthys]], 1-2 strong spines in [[Corniger]]); 10-11 spines in anterior dorsal fin plus always 1 spine in posterior portion; more than 10 anal-fin rays. Four genera occur in the area:

- **[[Myripristis]]**: 10 anterior dorsal spines; no suborbital or preopercular spines
- **[[Ostichthys]]**: 11 anterior dorsal spines; no suborbital spines
- **[[Plectrypops]]**: 11 anterior dorsal spines; slender curved partly antrorse suborbital spines; last dorsal spine shorter than penultimate; 32-42 lateral-line scales
- **[[Corniger]]**: 11 anterior dorsal spines; long strong retrorse suborbital spines; 2 preopercular spines; last dorsal spine equal or longer than penultimate; 28-30 lateral-line scales

### Species Accounts

**[[Holocentrus ascensionis]]** (Squirrelfish): Large species reaching at least 34.5 cm standard length (reported to 60.8 cm total length). Moderately compressed, relatively slender oblong body with long slender caudal peduncle. Upper jaw long (13.6-15.8% of standard length), extending to or beyond posterior pupil margin; 23-24 total gill rakers on first arch. Dorsal fin with 11 spines and 14-16 soft rays; anal fin with 4 spines and 10 soft rays; anterior soft dorsal and upper caudal rays elongate. Lateral-line scales with pores 46-50 (rarely 51).

Coloration: back and upper sides reddish with gold reflections; 2 dark greenish stripes following scale intersections on upper sides, fading posteriorly; stripes broader, less distinct, pink to white on lower scale rows; lower sides, belly and breast white. Snout and top of head dark red; upper maxilla white; white streak diagonally across cheek; lower jaw white; iris bright red near pupil with blackish distal margin. Dorsal spines yellowish or yellowish green; interspinal membranes green to greenish brown adjacent to spines and basally with red margin; soft dorsal rays pink; outer caudal rays white, rest pink; first three anal spines white, fourth spine and soft rays pink; pectoral fin pink with darker red upper edge on first two rays; pelvic spine and anterior margin of first ray white, other rays pink.

**[[Holocentrus rufus]]** (Longspine squirrelfish): Large species reaching at least 32 cm standard length. Similar body form to H. ascensionis. Upper jaw shorter (11.4-13.4% of standard length), extending at most to below center of pupil; lower jaw also shorter (14.7-17.1% vs 17.2-19.8% in H. ascensionis); 24-26 total gill rakers. Dorsal fin with 11 spines and 14-16 soft rays; anal fin with 4 spines and 10 soft rays (rarely 9 or 11); anterior soft dorsal and upper caudal rays elongate. Lateral-line scales with pores 50-57, usually 51-54.

Coloration: top of head and suborbital brick red; iris red; lips and branchiostegal membranes pink; cheeks and opercular bones shining white. Lines on sides above lateral line red, diffuse, about equal in width to light interspaces; lines very indistinct, pink below lateral line. Dorsal spines and membranes red or pink basally with distinctive white triangular or round spot distally just posterior to spine tip; soft rays of dorsal, anal, caudal, and pelvic fins pink; third anal and pelvic spines white at least on anterior portion; pectoral fins pale with faint red spot on basal portion of upper rays.

**[[Myripristis jacobus]]** (Blackbar soldierfish): Large species reaching 21.6 cm standard length. Moderately compressed, relatively deep oblong body. Anterior profile triangular; eyes very large; head bones smooth except finely serrate margins; no spine on preopercle or preorbital; 29-33 total gill rakers (usually 31). Dorsal fin with 10 spines in anterior portion, 1 spine plus 12-15 soft rays (usually 14) in posterior portion; anal fin with 4 spines and 12-14 soft rays (usually 13). Scales present on membranes between soft dorsal and anal fin rays, covering over half the fin length; perforated lateral-line scales 33-37 (usually 35).

Coloration: head and back reddish orange above, shading to more silverish with pink tinge below; reddish black pigment on post-temporal, supracleithrum, cleithrum, posterior opercle margin, and opercular flap, forming appearance of broad rectangular bar from upper gill opening to pectoral base. Central portion of membranes between first two dorsal spines reddish orange with white on distal and basal portions; membranes of remaining dorsal spines reddish orange only distally; anterior border of pelvic, soft dorsal, anal, and caudal fins white with darker reddish orange band directly posterior to white edge.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The majority of squirrelfishes live in association with [[Coral Reefs]] in shallow tropical waters. [[Holocentrus ascensionis]] also occurs on rocky reefs at greater depths to about 120 m, while other species are deep-water forms (to 500 m) found mainly on sandy or muddy bottoms.

**[[Holocentrus ascensionis]]** inhabits shore and shallow waters from the surface to more than 90 m depth, found from [[Bermuda]], Cape Fear (North Carolina), South Carolina, Georgia, [[Florida]], northwestern, northeastern and southern [[Gulf of Mexico]], throughout the [[West Indies]] and [[Caribbean]] shores south to Santos, [[Brazil]]. Also occurs at tropical Atlantic islands including south Trinidad, St. Helena, [[Ascension Island]], and Angola (West Africa). This nocturnal species hides by day in deep crevices or under coral ledges; at night feeds away from the reef over sand and grass beds, taking mainly crabs, shrimps and other small crustaceans.

**[[Holocentrus rufus]]** occurs off [[Bermuda]], the [[Bahamas]] and throughout the [[West Indies]]; [[Caribbean Sea]] from Misteriosa Bank to Quita Sueño Bank; Panama; southeast [[Florida]]; northwestern [[Gulf of Mexico]]. More abundant in the northern part of its range. Inhabits shallow reefs from the surface to 32 m in the Gulf of Mexico. Like H. ascensionis, this nocturnal species hides by day in deep crevices or under coral ledges, feeding at night away from the reef over sand and grass beds on crabs, shrimps, gastropods and brittle stars.

**[[Myripristis jacobus]]** ranges from Georgia, [[Florida]], northwestern and northeastern [[Gulf of Mexico]], [[Bermuda]], throughout the [[West Indies]], [[Caribbean Sea]], Central American coast, [[Campeche Bank]], [[Venezuela]], and offshore [[Guianas]]. Outside fishing area 31 it occurs in northern [[Brazil]], [[Cape Verde Islands]] and [[Ascension Island]]. Found from shallow coral reefs to offshore deeper waters to depths of about 90 m. This nocturnal species aggregates around coral reefs and deeper rocky reefs, feeding at night predominantly on planktonic organisms.

### Diagnostic Features for Field Identification

The family is readily distinguished from other fishes in the area by the combination of: bony edges and spines on head, large eyes, very long spinous dorsal portion compared to soft portion, presence of 4 anal-fin spines, and 7 soft rays in pelvic fins.

Key distinguishing features among the three detailed species:

- **H. ascensionis vs H. rufus**: Jaw length (upper jaw 13.6-15.8% vs 11.4-13.4% SL; lower jaw 17.2-19.8% vs 14.7-17.1% SL); lateral-line scale counts (46-50 vs 51-54); dorsal interspinal membrane coloration (green to greenish brown vs white spot near tip)

- **H. ascensionis and H. rufus vs M. jacobus**: Preopercular spine (strong spine present vs absent); anal-fin ray count (10 vs 12-14); dorsal spine count (11 vs 10 in anterior portion)

- **H. ascensionis and H. rufus vs Flammeo marianus**: Gill raker count (23-26 vs 12-14 on lower limb); caudal lobe length (upper notably longer vs nearly equal); soft dorsal ray elongation (elongate vs not elongate); jaw position (upper longer or equal vs lower projecting)

### Fisheries Information

Although apparently never very abundant, some larger squirrelfishes occur regularly in local markets. All three detailed species are caught incidentally throughout their ranges. [[Holocentrus ascensionis]] is regularly taken in some artisanal fisheries on the Venezuelan coast (Los Roques, La Orchila, Los Frailes). [[Myripristis jacobus]] is more regularly taken in some local artisanal fisheries in [[Venezuela]].

Separate statistics are not reported for any of these species. Primary fishing gear is traps, with occasional capture by trammelnets. All are marketed mostly fresh.

### Size Data

- [[Holocentrus ascensionis]]: Maximum at least 34.5 cm standard length; reported to reach 60.8 cm total length
- [[Holocentrus rufus]]: Maximum at least 32 cm standard length
- [[Myripristis jacobus]]: Maximum 21.6 cm standard length

### Behavioral Ecology

All three species are nocturnal, exhibiting similar diel activity patterns: hiding by day in deep crevices or under coral ledges, then emerging at night to feed. [[Holocentrus ascensionis]] and [[Holocentrus rufus]] typically feed away from the reef over sand and grass beds on benthic crustaceans (crabs, shrimps) and other invertebrates (gastropods, brittle stars in H. rufus). In contrast, [[Myripristis jacobus]] feeds predominantly on planktonic organisms and aggregates around coral and rocky reefs.
