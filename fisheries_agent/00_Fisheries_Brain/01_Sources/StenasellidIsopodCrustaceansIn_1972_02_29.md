# Mexistenasellus coahuila (New Genus and Species)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Stenasellidae #Isopoda #Crustacea #Stygobiont #Freshwater-Isopods

## Executive Summary

This paper describes **Mexistenasellus coahuila**, a new genus and species of stenasellid isopod crustacean, representing the **first record of the family Stenasellidae in the Western Hemisphere**. The species was discovered in thermal springs of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico, an isolated desert wetland system renowned for high aquatic endemism. Prior to this discovery, Stenasellidae (formerly the subfamily Stenasellinae within Asellidae) was known only from the Old World — Europe (especially the peri-Mediterranean region), Turkmenia, and tropical Africa. The discovery of a stenasellid in North America has significant biogeographic implications, suggesting either an ancient trans-Atlantic dispersal or a pre-Tertiary (Mesozoic) origin for the group, predating continental drift separation of Africa/Europe from the Americas. The paper also provides a broader taxonomic review of North American freshwater isopod genera (Sphaeromatidae, Cirolanidae, Asellidae).

## Background & Description

**Diagnostic features of genus *Mexistenasellus*:**
- Bright red pigmentation in life; completely **eyeless** (a hallmark of [[Stygobiont]] adaptation)
- Pleonites 1 and 2 well developed and free
- Exopod of antenna 2 absent (or reduced to a stout seta)
- Maxilla 1 inner plate bears four setae
- Dactyls of pereopods 2–4 armed with **one spine**; pereopods 5–7 with **two spines**
- Male pleopod 1: protopodite lacks coupling spines
- Male pleopod 2: protopodite with produced, setose distolateral corner; exopod narrow, subcylindrical, single distal seta; **endopod two-segmented, enclosing a sharp terminal spike** (unique feature)
- Pleopods 3–5: endopods non-bifid and respiratory; exopods non-bifid, non-respiratory
- No plumose setation on any pleopod
- Uropods slightly shorter than pleotelson

**Type species:** *Mexistenasellus coahuila* Cole and Minckley, by monotypy.

**Body plan (M. coahuila):**
- Slender, elongate body; length ~5× width
- Cephalon length ~0.85× width; anterior margin slightly convex, rostral angles rounded
- Pereonites 1–2 shortest; pereonites 6–7 longest
- Pleotelson 1.3× longer than broad
- Antenna 1: peduncle of 4 segments; flagellum of 10–14 articles with 6–9 distal articles bearing lamellae olfactoriae
- Antenna 2: ~0.6× body length; peduncle of 6 articles, flagellum of 33–44 articles
- Mandible with 4-toothed incisor; asymmetric right/left spine rows and molar lobes
- Maxilla 1 outer ramus with 12 pectinate apical teeth
- Maxilliped with two coupling spines on masticatory lobe
- Pereopod 1 prehensile (both sexes); pereopods 2–7 ambulatory, increasing in length posteriorly (pereopod 7 ~1.6× length of pereopod 1)
- Sexual dimorphism: females (based on allotype) largely similar to males but with fewer setae on mandible/maxilla 2 and a sub-circular, crenulate-margined pleopod 2

**Size range:** Specimens measured 4.2–8.2 mm (holotype male 8.2 mm; allotype female 7.4 mm, non-ovigerous).

**Material examined:** 10 specimens total, collected 1967–1970 from multiple springs within the Cuatro Ciénegas basin. Type material deposited in the Colección Nacional de México, the U.S. National Museum of Natural History, and with Prof. Guy Magniez.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat type:** Phreatic/interstitial freshwater habitats associated with **desert thermal springs (limnocrenes)** — specifically:
- Crevices and tube-like channels within soft, porous [[Travertine]] substrate
- Copropelic (organic detrital) sediments at depth (~3 m)
- Detritus beneath floating vegetation mats at spring outlets
- Small, nearly-filled seep/spring detrital material

**Environmental conditions:**
- Water temperatures ranged **30.5–34.2°C** (thermal spring conditions)
- Filtrable residue (TDS) at Laguna Juan Santos: **2.1 g/L**
- Depth of collection ranged from near-surface (25–50 cm) to ~3 m

**Sympatric fauna:** Laguna Juan Santos supports a large, diversified fish fauna (per Minckley 1969), plus other crustaceans including the amphipod *Hyalella azteca* and the decapod shrimp *Palaemonetes suttkusi*.

**Distribution:** Currently known only from a handful of springs within the Cuatro Ciénegas basin (Laguna Juan Santos, Laguna Escobeda, "Pozo Barbado," and small unnamed springs/seeps) — potentially a basin-endemic, similar to the cirolanid isopod *[[Speocirolana thermydronis]]*.

## Key Findings

1. **First Western Hemisphere stenasellid record**: *Mexistenasellus coahuila* is the first stenasellid isopod (family [[Stenasellidae]]) described from the Americas, all prior known taxa being restricted to Europe, the peri-Mediterranean, Turkmenia, and tropical Africa.

2. **Novel morphology**: The unique two-segmented endopod of male pleopod 2 enclosing a protruding sharp styliform spike is unlike any other known stenasellid, though it shows some resemblance to the African genus *[[Parastenasellus]]* (distolateral process) and the genus *[[Synasellus]]* (lack of plumose pleopod setae, single distal exopod seta, oval pleopod 1).

3. **Biogeographic significance**: The presence of this taxon in North America challenges prior hypotheses (e.g., Birstein 1964) that stenasellids were relicts of an Equatorial Africa–Central Asia Tertiary tropical fauna. The authors argue this instead supports an **origin predating the Tertiary**, i.e., prior to the breakup of Pangaea, linking African/Mediterranean and American faunas via ancient continental connections (cf. [[Continental Drift]], Dietz and Holden 1970).

4. **Parallel biogeographic patterns**: Comparable transatlantic affinities are noted for other ancient groundwater taxa, including the thermosbaenacean *Monodella texana* (Edward's Plateau, Texas) and the hydrobiid snail tribe Horatiini (also documented in Cuatro Ciénegas by Taylor 1966), reinforcing a broader pattern of Mediterranean–African–American subterranean faunal connections.

5. **Regional endemism**: *Mexistenasellus coahuila*, like the cirolanid *Speocirolana thermydronis* and the genus *[[Sphaerolana]]*, appears to be endemic to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], underscoring this basin's status as a refugium of extraordinary aquatic endemism.

6. **Broader North American isopod fauna review**: The paper contextualizes *Mexistenasellus* within a wider survey of North/Middle American freshwater isopods:
   - **Sphaeromatidae**: e.g., *Exosphaeroma thermophilum* (New Mexico) and *E. dugesi* (Aguascalientes, Mexico), both derived from marine/estuarine ancestors and associated with thermal waters.
   - **Cirolanidae**: eight genera in the Western Hemisphere, including *[[Cirolanides]]*, *[[Speocirolana]]*, *[[Sphaerolana]]*, *Creaseriella*, *Troglocirolana*, and *Haptolana*, forming a biogeographic arc around the Gulf of Mexico.
   - **Asellidae**: North America's most widespread freshwater isopod family, including genera *[[Lirceus]]*, *[[Asellus]]*, *[[Conasellus]]*, *[[Nipponasellus]]*, and *[[Pseudobaicalasellus]]*, several with proposed Asian (Bering Strait) or Mediterranean biogeographic origins.

7. **Taxonomic reclassification context**: Confirms elevation of former subfamilies Asellinae and Stenasellinae to full family rank ([[Asellidae]] and [[Stenasellidae]]) following Henry and Magniez (1968, 1970), with further genus-level splitting of the former broad genus *Stenasellus* into *Stenasellus* (s.s.), *[[Parastenasellus]]*, *[[Metastenasellus]]*, and *[[Magniezia]]*.
