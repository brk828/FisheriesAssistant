# The Fate of Biogenic Suspensoids in a Desert Reservoir

#limnology #reservoir-ecology #phytoplankton #biogeochemistry

## Executive Summary

This 1980 symposium paper examines what happens to biogenic suspensoids (particulate organic matter produced by primary production) in [[Lake Mohave]], a [[Multipurpose Reservoir]] on the [[Colorado River]] in the desert southwest. Using vertical light extinction, hypolimnetic nutrient accumulation and hypolimnetic areal oxygen deficits, the authors show that suspended material and dissolved inorganic nitrogen accumulate in the hypolimnion near the impounding dam. Hypolimnetic oxygen depletion accounted for about 39% of the oxygen produced by phytoplankton during spring and summer, with over 63% of this occurring near [[Davis Dam]]. The authors argue that in a through-flow reservoir, organic matter is transported downstream rather than settling into a basin, so standard lake trophic indices are misleading and release depth at the dam matters for downstream water quality.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Lake Mohave lies on the lower Colorado River between [[Hoover Dam]] and Davis Dam, filled in 1953. It is one of six reservoirs between the Grand Canyon and Mexico, created mainly to reduce flow variability caused by irregular power demands at Hoover Dam, and it also stores water for hydroelectric production at Davis Dam.
- **Morphometry (Table 1, at maximum elevation):**
  - Elevation: 197 m (MSL)
  - Surface area: 115.3 km²
  - Volume: 2.25 × 10⁹ m³
  - Mean depth: 19 m
  - Mean depth to maximum depth ratio: 0.48
  - Theoretical renewal time: 79 days
  - Shoreline length: 322 km
- **Study design:** Five permanent stations were sampled monthly or semi-monthly from October 1976 to September 1977. Stations M1 to M4 were in the lake proper, and M5 was on the Colorado River below Hoover Dam.
- **Methods (summary):**
  - Light profiles with a submarine photometer, with suspensoid dry weights from 0, 5 and 10 m composites (February to April 1977), filtered through 0.45 µm membranes.
  - An extinction coefficient per unit of suspensoids of 0.07 m² g⁻¹, derived with a modified [[Beer-Lambert Law]] (Verduin's method).
  - Nitrate-plus-nitrite and ammonium nitrogen measured at 10 m intervals, with a sensitivity of 20 µg N L⁻¹.
  - Hypolimnetic oxygen deficits from a Hydrolab analyzer (April to July), compared with [[Primary Productivity]] measured by the ¹⁴C method.
  - Horizontal currents measured with an electromagnetic current meter.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Inflow:** Water enters from Lake Mead, drawn from a depth of 61 m, giving 11–14 °C inflows year-round. During summer stratification this cold water underflows the warmer lake water, stays in the hypolimnion and exits through deep penstocks at Davis Dam.
- **Retention:** The hypolimnion was about half the lake volume (thermocline averaged 10 m). Estimated hypolimnetic retention was about 40 days, roughly half the theoretical whole-lake value.
- **Hydraulics:** Mean current velocity fell from 35.5 cm s⁻¹ to 5.4 cm s⁻¹ on entering the lake proper, as the basin widened. Hypolimnetic currents of about 6.0 cm s⁻¹ were recorded in summer.
- **Nutrients:** Nitrate-N in the river (M5) stayed high all year, reflecting nutrient-rich hypolimnetic water from Lake Mead, and stayed below 40 µg L⁻¹ at M5 in the sampling period as reported. Within the lake, nitrate fell from about 350 to 150 µg L⁻¹ downstream during isothermal months, and was depleted to under 20 µg L⁻¹ in the epilimnion through summer.
- **Phytoplankton:** A bloom of blue-green algae occurred in June. Surface ammonium rose to 169 µg L⁻¹ at M3 in July, possibly from decomposition of that bloom. Chlorophyll *a* reached 49.6 µg L⁻¹ at M4 in May.

## Key Findings

1. **Hypolimnetic accumulation of suspensoids:** After stratification in May, hypolimnetic suspensoid levels rose toward the dam and surpassed epilimnetic levels at M1. In July, hypolimnetic concentrations were over twice the epilimnetic values from M3 to the dam, peaking at 12.71 mg L⁻¹ at the dam.
2. **Source of the hypolimnetic load:** Station M5, with low turbidity in the river underflow, ruled out Lake Mead inflow as the source of downstream hypolimnetic suspensoids.
3. **Nitrogen regeneration:** A decline of about 330 µg L⁻¹ NO₃-N in the epilimnion was followed by the reappearance of about 180 µg L⁻¹ dissolved inorganic N at M1. Because no other DIN inputs were found, the authors estimate that about 50% of the depleted nitrate is regenerated in the hypolimnion.
4. **Oxygen deficits (Table 2):** Hypolimnetic areal oxygen deficits (mg O₂ cm⁻² day⁻¹) increased downstream:

   | Station | Distance below Hoover Dam (km) | Deficit |
   |---|---|---|
   | M4 | 40 | 0.002 |
   | M3 | 61 | 0.019 |
   | M2 | 73 | 0.050 |
   | M1 | 108 | 0.126 |

5. **Oxygen budget:** Hypolimnetic depletion equalled about 39% of mean phytoplankton oxygen production (April to July), with over 63% occurring at the impounding dam. Upstream stations contributed 25% (M2) to 1% (M4) of the total depletion, with the lowest deficit tied to the highest epilimnetic production at M4.
6. **Trophic classification caveat:** Hutchinson's index would classify the upper reaches as extremely oligotrophic and the dam area as highly eutrophic. The authors note this index was designed for natural lakes, not high-throughput reservoirs, so the deficits instead indicate zones of organic buildup and degradation.
7. **Conceptual sequence:** The authors propose a sequence in which (1) epilimnetic phytoplankton production depletes DIN, (2) cells sink through the thermocline, and (3) they are mineralized and nitrified during downstream hypolimnetic transport. Nitrification consumes two moles of O₂ per mole of NH₄⁺, which accounts for the oxygen loss.
8. **Management implications:** Accumulated organic matter at discharge points can cause taste and odour problems, reduce the oxygen available for downstream BOD, and affect reservoir water quality. The authors call for more study of the depth of release at the dam, especially where downstream water allocations depend on upstream release quality.
