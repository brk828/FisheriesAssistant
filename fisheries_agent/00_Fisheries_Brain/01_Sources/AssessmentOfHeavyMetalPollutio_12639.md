# Assessment of Heavy Metals Pollution in the Upper Arkansas River of Colorado

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 Bureau of Reclamation report assesses heavy metal contamination of the upper [[Arkansas River]] in Colorado, using monthly water, sediment, benthic invertebrate and fish sampling at 11 stations from April through November 1974. Three main impact zones were identified: the [[Leadville Drain]] with a trailer park sewage outflow, the [[California Gulch]] outflow, and diffuse sources between stations AR-5 and AR-7. California Gulch is the most damaging input to aquatic life. Dilution by [[Lake Fork]], [[Halfmoon Creek]] and [[Lake Creek]] flows currently mitigates the damage, so planned transmountain diversions that reduce these flows could extend the contamination downstream.

## Background & Description

The study area lies within the [[Colorado Mineral Belt]], which has been mined since 1859 when placer gold was discovered in California Gulch. The [[Leadville Mining District]] boom of 1874–1893 produced large volumes of tailings and smelter waste, and mine drainage tunnels such as [[Yak Tunnel]] and [[Leadville Drain]] continue to discharge metals. An estimated 64 miles of stream in the upper Arkansas basin were polluted by heavy metals (Wentz, 1974). The main contaminants are manganese, iron, copper, zinc and cadmium, with locally high sulfate. California Gulch and Yak Tunnel water is acidic, while other sources are neutral to slightly alkaline.

The study was part of the [[Fryingpan-Arkansas Project]], a transmountain diversion that will move Colorado River Basin water into the Arkansas basin. Its planned works (Boustead Tunnel, Mt. Elbert Canal, Otero Canal, Clear Creek Reservoir and Pueblo Dam) would reduce flows in the reach studied.

Stations were sampled from upstream to downstream (EF-3, EF-5, AR-1, AR-3, AR-4, AR-5, AR-6, AR-9, AR-10, AR-7, AR-8). Sampling used [[Artificial Substrate Samplers]] (bark-filled baskets) and [[Surber Sampling]], and fish were collected by electrofishing on 18 September 1974.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water chemistry:** Dissolved oxygen was always sufficient for aquatic life (lowest 6.4 ppm). Conductivity ranged from 66 µmho (AR-5, July 18) to 355 µmho (AR-4, September 16). A strong negative correlation of −0.89 (±0.05) was found between flow and conductivity (May–October 1974).
- **Sediments:** Metals accumulated in sediments at AR-4 (iron 30,450 ppm, copper 381 ppm, lead 2,452 ppm, zinc 6,540 ppm) and from AR-6 to AR-7. Molybdenum was not detected. The authors judged that pH and dissolved oxygen were high enough to prevent metal release from sediments.
- **Calcium and hardness:** Calcium reduces metal toxicity to fish. Concentrations in the Arkansas were mostly below 30 ppm.
- **Benthic invertebrates:** Insects made up over 99% of organisms, with Trichoptera (caddisflies) dominant. Invertebrate metal content was high, consistent with bioconcentration.
- **Fish:** Fish numbers were low where metal pollution was present. The only native species collected was the White Sucker, and the trout were introduced.

## Key Findings

- **Three impact areas:** (1) Leadville Drain and trailer park sewage; (2) California Gulch outflow; (3) diffuse sources between AR-5 and AR-7.
- **Most damaging source:** California Gulch raises metal loads sharply. Station AR-4 had the highest average concentrations (iron 2.62 ppm, zinc 2.83 ppm, manganese 1.59 ppm, lead ≥0.2 ppm, copper 0.055 ppm, April–November 1974 averages).
- **Mitigation:** Lake Fork and Halfmoon Creek dilute California Gulch inflows at AR-5, and Lake Creek dilutes the diffuse sources at AR-8. Reduced flows would extend the metal effect downstream.
- **Drinking water standards:** Iron, zinc and manganese exceeded the 1962 USPHS recommended limits at most stations. Only EF-3 met the manganese standard (0.05 ppm), and only EF-3 and AR-1 were of high enough quality to be fully safe for aquatic life.
- **Aquatic life thresholds:** Literature cited indicates that zinc at 0.1–1.0 ppm is toxic to some aquatic life in soft water. Lead above 0.1 ppm is deleterious to fish, especially in soft water. Manganese above 1.0 ppm may have deleterious effects.
- **Invertebrate diversity:** The mean d index fell from 3.01 (EF-5) to 1.65 (AR-4) below California Gulch, recovering to 2.63 at AR-8. Diversity was generally "clean" to "moderately polluted" by Wilhm and Dorris criteria.
- **Fish populations:** Only 1 brook trout was found in 400 yards at AR-4. Numbers dropped from 72 fish at EF-1 to 12 at AR-7. The authors concluded the section was not a good fishery, but could become excellent if sources were cleaned up.
- **Fish tissue:** Metal concentrations in trout were abnormally high but not high enough to make the fish unfit for human consumption. Brown trout had more zinc, manganese and cadmium than brook trout; brook trout had more iron and copper.
- **Management recommendations:** Treating California Gulch effluent should come before treating Leadville Drain, because California Gulch is the principal point source. Operational hydrology data are needed to quantify effects of project flows.
