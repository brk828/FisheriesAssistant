# Station Profile: Quinault National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

The **Quinault National Fish Hatchery** is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) facility located in Neilton, Washington, established in 1968 under the Appropriation Act of July 7, 1964 (78 Stat. 23). The station operates cooperatively with the **[[Quinault Indian Nation]]** to produce and distribute **[[Coho Salmon]]**, **[[Steelhead]]**, **[[Fall Chinook Salmon]]**, and **[[Chum Salmon]]**, with an explicit mission to reduce negative impacts on the genetic integrity of wild fish populations while maintaining a cooperative, teamwork-based operational culture. The hatchery falls under USFWS Region 1 and Congressional District 6, with oversight from Senators Slade Gorton and Patty Murray, and Representative Norman Dicks.

## Background & Description

This document is a hatchery administrative/operations profile rather than a biological or morphological study. Species identity is inferred solely from stocking and distribution records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Context**: The hatchery functions within the freshwater portion of the **[[Quinault River]]** system in coastal Washington State, supporting anadromous **[[Pacific Salmon]]** and **[[Steelhead]]** life cycles (freshwater rearing/egg incubation to smolt release, prior to ocean migration).
- **Cooperative Management**: Operations are conducted jointly with the **[[Quinault Indian Nation]]**, reflecting a co-management model for tribal and federal fisheries resources in the Pacific Northwest.
- **Conservation Emphasis**: The stated mission prioritizes minimizing genetic impacts of hatchery-origin fish on wild population integrity — an important consideration in **[[Hatchery-Wild Fish Interactions]]** and **[[Salmonid Genetic Conservation]]**.
- **Species present**: Multi-species facility rearing four anadromous salmonids common to Pacific Northwest coastal drainages: Coho, Steelhead, Fall Chinook, and Chum.
