# Arizona Game and Fish Department Endangered Species Activity Reports under Blanket Permit PRT-676811 (1991–1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover letter and two annual reports from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Region 2. The Department acted as a subpermittee under [[Blanket Permit PRT-676811]] for Endangered or Threatened species. The cover letter is dated December 13, 1993, and the reports cover December 1992 to November 1993 and December 1991 to November 1992. They document captures, releases, stockings, preserved voucher specimens, salvage operations, hatchery work, and nest monitoring of raptors. Most entries concern fishes, with additional records for birds, a reptile, mammals, and one pronghorn study conducted in Mexico. The data are described by the author as preliminary and abbreviated, with full records held in Department databases available to Service offices on request.

## Background & Description

The 1993 letter, signed by Director Duane L. Shroufe, transmits the Department's 1992 report, which was prepared by Endangered Species Coordinator Terry B. Johnson. The annual reports are organized by species, following the list of species covered under the blanket permit. Each entry gives the activity (take, stocking, capture-release, preservation) and the number of animals handled, by site.

Several categories appear repeatedly:
- **Fishes:** Native and listed desert and mountain fishes, including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Apache Trout]].
- **Birds:** Raptors including [[Bald Eagle]], [[Golden Eagle]], and [[Peregrine Falcon]], plus [[Brown Pelican]] and [[Thick-billed Parrot]] records from the [[Arizona Wildlife Rehabilitation Center]] (AMWC).
- **Other vertebrates:** [[Desert Tortoise]], [[Sonoran Pronghorn]], [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], and several mammals, including the lesser long-nosed bat and Mexican vole.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most fish records come from perennial desert streams and springs in central and southern Arizona (for example [[Aravaipa Creek]], Sonoita Creek, and the [[Verde River]]), mainstem reaches of the Colorado River such as [[Lake Mohave]] and the Grand Canyon, the [[Little Colorado River]], and reservoir or pond habitats such as [[Palm Lake]] at The Nature Conservancy's Hassayampa River Preserve. Hatchery and rearing work was conducted at facilities including [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery]].

Raptor records relate to nest sites in Arizona's national forests and urban and rural areas, including the Phoenix metropolitan area. Nest failures, storm-blown nestlings, shooting, and predation by great horned owls are noted as causes of loss. Several records describe the physical hazards nests presented, including fallen branches requiring tree climbing or chainsaw removal.

## Key Findings

### Fishes

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]:** In 1993, 1,120 were stocked into the upper Verde River (630 on March 17 and 490 on March 31). In June 1993, 66 were handled in Lake Mohave, 12 of which were blind in one eye and one blind in both eyes. Mean length was 597.9 mm (range 528–680 mm). At Willow Beach, 118 were captured by gill and trammel nets and 9 by electrofishing; all were released alive. In 1992, 894 brood stock and roughly 100,000 larvae from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] were received; 138 mortalities occurred. Larvae and juveniles were also collected from Lake Mohave for research at Arizona State University (ASU).
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]:** Approximately 500 were stocked into AD Wash in March 1993. Capture-release and preservation records were kept for about 20 sites, including Hidden Water Spring, Redrock Canyon, Monkey Spring, and Sonoita Creek. In 1992, 663 specimens were preserved as vouchers.
- **[[Yaqui Topminnow]]:** Captured and released at about 14 pond and spring sites near the San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge, with counts ranging from 2 to 200 per site.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]:** Eight were preserved from Cold Spring. Approximately 500 were stocked at AD Wash in March 1993. A possible hybrid (C.m. macularius × C.m. eremus) was captured at Finley Tank.
- **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]:** In 1993, 5 were released at Chevelon Creek, 9 at East Clear Creek, 12 at Leonard Canyon Creek, 44 from the Little Colorado River, and 50 from Nutrioso Creek. In 1992, 50 were released at Rudd Creek and 53 from Nutrioso Creek were removed for research.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]:** In 1992, 20 adults were salvaged from Palm Lake, 9 natural mortalities were collected, 18 surviving fish were moved to ASU's Research Park, and 32 were trammel netted and released on October 27. In 1993, 18 were released in Palm Lake.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]:** In 1993, 2,483 chubs over 29 mm were handled in the Little Colorado River, including 5 incidental mortalities and 5 sacrificed specimens. In the mainstem Colorado River, the count was reported as "3?56" (OCR-garbled, likely 3,756), with 17 sacrificed and 70 found dead. In 1992, 1,724 were captured in the Little Colorado River, and 68 (including 7 net mortalities) were preserved for otolith and diet studies.
- **[[Spikedace]]:** In 1992, 2,881 were captured and released in Aravaipa Creek, and 5 were preserved from the Verde River. The 1993 total for Aravaipa Creek is OCR-garbled, with one reading of 10,685.
- **[[Apache Trout]]:** In 1992, 33,883 eggs from Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery were received at Sterling Springs State Hatchery. About 7,240 fingerlings were moved to Page Springs. At Tonto Creek State Hatchery, an estimated 15% hatch (about 14,000 eggs) and 62.4% fry mortality were reported. 5,264 fish survived, averaging about 2.0 inches in early November.
- **No take or possession (1992–1993):** [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], Sonoran chub, Yaqui chub, [[Gila Trout]], and woundfin were not taken in either year, and [[Colorado Squawfish]] had no take in 1993.

### Birds

- **[[Bald Eagle]]:** In 1992, two dead birds were recovered: a nestling blown from the Orme nest and an adult from Horseshoe Lake, which x-rays showed was killed by shotgun pellets. Fourteen nestlings were banded in 1992. In 1993, 22 nestlings were banded, and carcasses, eggs, and prey remains were sent to USFWS. At the Alamo nest, two eggs were removed when threatened by rising water. One hatched at the Phoenix Zoo and was fostered into the Ive's Wash nest in March 1993.
- **[[Peregrine Falcon]]:** Two peregrines were radio-tailed in Phoenix in winter 1991–92. Both left the metropolitan area by May 1992, and one returned by November 1992. Eyrie remains were collected from Bill Williams Mountain and West Beaver Creek in 1993.
- **[[Golden Eagle]]:** Four golden eagles were handled in 1992 and released or transferred. No take occurred in 1993.
- **[[Brown Pelican]]:** Roughly ten were received at AMWC in 1992, and about nine in 1993. Most were transferred to Sea World, San Diego.
- **[[Thick-billed Parrot]]:** In the Chiricahua Mountains, captive-bred birds were released (16 on 12/12/92, 1 on 2/20/93, and some on 4/1/93). A 1993 release of several birds is OCR-garbled.
- **[[Mexican Spotted Owl]]:** No take in 1993, but owls responded at 7 breeding sites in the Apache-Sitgreaves, Tonto, and Coronado Forests. Pellets were collected from 7 nests and one egg from Largo Canyon.

### Other Species

- **[[Desert Tortoise]]:** In 1993, blood samples were taken from 10 tortoises at City Creek (Utah), 1 at Paradise Canyon (Utah), and 15 at a third site in Arizona. There were no mortalities, and all were released immediately. The 1992 figures were similar (15, 1, and 15 tortoises).
- **[[Sonoran Pronghorn]]:** No take in the United States. In 1993, 15 previously radio-collared animals were monitored in northwestern Sonora under Mexican permits, with 2 mortalities. In 1992, one adult male was captured, collared, and released with no mortality. AGFD flew two telemetry flights to locate animals.
- **Other mammals:** Three Mexican voles were live-trapped and released in the Hualapai Mountains in 1993, and 4 lesser long-nosed bats were captured and released at a mine in Coronado National Memorial. No take occurred for Mount Graham red squirrel or black-footed ferret, though extensive surveys were conducted.
