# Virgin Spinedace Proposed Listing: Public Hearing and Comment Period Extension

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepidomeda

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover letter dated July 6, 1994, from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) Utah Field Office, signed by Assistant Field Supervisor Robert D. Williams. It encloses a [[Federal Register]] notice concerning the proposed listing of the [[Virgin Spinedace]] as a threatened species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The letter announces a public hearing in St. George, Utah, on July 13, 1994, and extends the public comment period by 30 days, to August 17, 1994.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Virgin spinedace, *Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis* (genus [[Lepidomeda]]). The letter gives no morphological, life-history or family information.
- **Regulatory action:** A proposed listing as a threatened species under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The FWS, under the Department of the Interior, is the responsible agency.
- **Enclosure:** A copy of the Federal Register publication (Thursday, June 30, 1994, based on the partially legible page header), which announces the hearing and the comment extension.

## Key Findings

1. **Public hearing:** Wednesday, July 13, 1994, at the [[St. George Hilton Inn]], 1450 South Hilton Drive, St. George, Utah (Garden Room).
   - Session times: 4:00–6:00 p.m. and 7:00–9:00 p.m.
   - Registration begins at 3:30 p.m.
   - An adjacent room provides information on the proposed listing and answers questions.
2. **Comment period extension:** The original period closed July 18, 1994. It was extended by 30 days, so written comments are due by **August 17, 1994**.
3. **Submission address:** Field Supervisor, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Suite 4M, Lincoln Plaza, 145 East 1300 South, Salt Lake City, Utah 84115.
4. **Public access:** Comments and materials received are available for public inspection by appointment during normal business hours at the same address.
5. **Information requested:** Biological and ecological data, additional information on the species, the location of other populations, and current or planned activities and their possible impacts on the species' habitat.
6. **Contact:** Robert D. Williams, Assistant Field Supervisor, via the Salt Lake City office. The Federal Register notice names additional staff, including the Denver Regional office, but that text is largely illegible.
