## Executive Summary

This document is a 1990 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Phoenix Field Office) to the Albuquerque Regional Director, providing recommended revisions to the federal **Animal Notice of Review (NOR)** — the precursor list to the Endangered Species Act candidate species list. Rather than a primary scientific study, this is a **regulatory status compilation** synthesizing survey data, recovery team input, and status reviews across a taxonomically diverse set of Arizona/Southwest species: two fishes, one amphibian, two caddisflies, seven land snails, and one freshwater mussel. The core content of ichthyological relevance concerns two candidate fish species — the [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*, lower Colorado River basin population) — both recommended for **Category 2** candidacy status due to declining population trends across their historic range in the Colorado River basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*, Cyprinidae)**: A native cyprinid of the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically distributed across Arizona, California, Colorado, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, Wyoming, and Mexico. Listed with a **Declining (D)** population status and **Priority 5**, spanning FWS Regions 1, 2, and 6 — indicating a wide-ranging, multi-jurisdictional riverine fish under range-wide pressure (likely from habitat alteration, water diversion, and nonnative species interactions, though not stated explicitly in text).

- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*, Catostomidae)**: Specifically referenced as the **lower Colorado River basin** population, occupying AZ, CA, NV, and UT. Status **Declining (D)**, **Priority 3** (higher urgency than the chub), spanning Regions 1, 2, 6. This regional-population-level candidacy reflects concern for a distinct segment of the species rather than the entire taxon.

- Both fish species share the [[Colorado River Basin]] as core habitat, suggesting co-occurring riverine community stressors — a pattern consistent with broader declines in southwestern native fish assemblages tied to impoundment, flow regulation, and habitat fragmentation.

- **Broader ecosystem context**: The memo situates these fish within a wider suite of imperiled Arizona endemics, including the [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] (*Rana chiricahuensis*, riparian/aquatic amphibian, Regions 5 shared), two rare spring/stream-dwelling caddisflies (*Protoptila balmorhea*, *Metrichia volada* — the latter endemic to [[Page Spring]], AZ), a suite of montane talus-dwelling land snails (genus *Sonorella*, family Helminthoglyptidae) restricted to isolated "sky island" mountain ranges, and the [[California Floater]] mussel (*Anodonta californiensis*, Unionidae), a freshwater bivalve also in decline (Priority 11) across Regions 1, 2, 6.
- This mix underscores that aquatic/riparian imperilment in the Southwest cuts across trophic levels and taxa — fish, amphibians, mollusks, and aquatic insects are jointly affected by shared habitat degradation in desert stream and spring systems.

## Key Findings

- **Two fish species were recommended for Category 2 candidate status** in this 1990 review:
  - *Gila robusta* ([[Roundtail Chub]]) — Status: Declining; Priority 5; Regions 1, 2, 6.
  - *Catostomus latipinnis* ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]], lower Colorado River basin population) — Status: Declining; Priority 3; Regions 1, 2, 6.
- Recommendations were based on **status reviews, field surveys, and recovery team input**, coordinated informally (via telephone) with the Arizona Game and Fish Department and other regional offices due to time constraints.
- The memo reflects a **multi-taxa Southwestern biodiversity crisis** pattern: of 13 species recommended for NOR addition, only 2 were fish, while the majority (7) were narrow-range endemic land snails (*Sonorella* spp. and *Oreohelix grahamensis*) restricted to isolated mountain "talus" habitats — highlighting the endemism-rich, fragmented nature of Arizona's sky-island and desert-stream ecosystems.
- Status codes used: **I** = increasing, **S** = stable, **D** = declining, **U** = unknown — both fish entries were coded **D (declining)**, in contrast to several snail taxa coded **S** or **U**, suggesting comparatively higher documented population stress for the fish species relative to some co-listed invertebrates.
- Category 3C designations (e.g., *Oreohelix grahamensis*, *Sonorella imitator*, and *Sonorella todseni* — [[Santa Catalina Talussnail]]) indicate taxa considered **more abundant/secure than previously believed** or **taxonomically invalid**, per NOR classification conventions — a useful contrast to the declining-status fishes.
- This document is valuable primarily as a **historical regulatory snapshot** (1990) of candidate species prioritization preceding formal ESA listing actions for southwestern native fishes, useful for tracing the regulatory history of [[Gila robusta]] and [[Catostomus latipinnis]] conservation status over time.
