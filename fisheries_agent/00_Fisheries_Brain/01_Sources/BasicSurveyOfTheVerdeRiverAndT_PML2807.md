# Basic Survey of the Verde River and Tributaries

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 Arizona Game and Fish Wildlife Restoration Division completion report (Project F-22-R-2, Job 1, by Richard A. Wagner) surveys the [[Verde River]] and its tributaries in central Arizona. It documents seasonal flow, water temperature, turbidity, and reservoir fluctuation during 1954, and assesses fish food production and fishery conditions. The report concludes that silt loads and water-level fluctuation limit fish habitat and food, and recommends aquatic plant and crayfish introductions, a forage fish stocking in [[Bartlett Reservoir]], quarterly limnological checks, and a desilting dam at Perkinsville.

## Background & Description

The survey was undertaken to gather chemical, biological and hydrological data on the Verde River system, which drains rugged canyons and high-country tributaries and supports recreational fishing. The stated objectives were to evaluate fishing sites, examine fish-food and game-fish relationships, record the effects of silting and flooding on spawning and survival, and gather flow and elevation data for management. Procedures included stream sampling, reservoir gauge readings, and recording of spawning season and nest conditions.

The main river runs roughly 50 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit in winter and spring and warms in summer. Upstream high-country streams, such as Oak Creek, were cooler and supported trout fisheries that were reported as satisfactory.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow and sources.** Water reaching [[Horseshoe Dam]] came from several sources, with the main Verde River above Perkinsville contributing the largest share. Other contributors included Sycamore Creek, Oak Creek, Beaver Creek, Clear Creek and [[Fossil Creek]].

**Flow regime.** Monthly flows measured below Horseshoe Reservoir in 1954 showed a mean of 260 cubic feet per second. Peak flows occurred in the spring and summer months, while the lowest flows fell in late fall. Flow volume below [[Bartlett Dam]] was reported at 199,300 acre-feet, with a mean of 254 cubic feet per second.

**Turbidity and silt.** Snowmelt and summer rains carried most of the silt, which peaked in March, July and August. March silt destroyed and limited habitat, while July and August silt destroyed fry and reduced fish food production. Turbid water also made feeding difficult for fish.

**Reservoirs.** Horseshoe Reservoir fluctuated greatly in depth and volume (gauge readings ranged roughly from 26 to 97 feet), so no fisheries management had been practical beyond establishing a monthly elevation record. Bartlett Reservoir also fluctuated widely (gauge readings from about 72 to 171 feet), making stable habitat for game fish difficult.

**Fish food.** Although some side areas of the Verde supported fish food organisms, most fish food was made up of insects, and many side areas had little food. The report noted a shortage of fish food and limited fish populations.

## Key Findings

- **Silt and turbidity** are the primary limiting factors for the Verde River fishery, reducing habitat, fry survival and food production.
- **Flow patterns** are dominated by seasonal snowmelt and rain; water temperature and volume vary strongly across the year.
- **Horseshoe Reservoir** water levels fluctuated too much for practical fisheries management; monthly elevation records were kept for 1954.
- **Bartlett Reservoir** fluctuated markedly, and a bass fishery did well during a fall draw-down, when fish concentrated.
- **Recommended actions:**
  1. Introduce [[Pondweed]], spiny naiad and chara into the Verde River below Horseshoe Reservoir.
  2. Introduce warm-water burrowing crayfish below Horseshoe and Bartlett Reservoirs.
  3. Introduce [[Threadfin Shad]] into Bartlett Reservoir as a forage fish suited to fluctuating water levels.
  4. Conduct quarterly [[Limnology]] spot checks to track changing conditions.
- **Desilting dam:** The best site for a desilting dam was judged to be Perkinsville. It would reduce turbidity on the main Verde, though it would not affect lower Oak Creek.
- **Red plains shiner** was introduced as a forage species in the Verde system to supplement fish food, though the degree of its success could not be determined in this survey.
- **Lake rehabilitation:** Several lakes on the Verde drainage (such as Upper and Lower Goldwater, Watson, Lynx, Fool's Hollow and Stoneman Lakes) were identified for future improvement and recreational fisheries work.
