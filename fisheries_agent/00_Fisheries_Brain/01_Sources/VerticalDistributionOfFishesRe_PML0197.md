# Vertical Distribution of Fishes Relative to Physical, Chemical and Biological Features in Two Central Arizona Reservoirs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 Arizona State University master's thesis by Peter Olof Bersell examines how fish are distributed with depth in two [[Salt River Reservoirs]], [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[Apache Lake]]. Vertical gill nets were fished three times a year across transects in each reservoir, and multiple linear regression (MLRP) was used to identify which abiotic and biotic variables best explained fish depth. Dissolved oxygen, chlorophyll *a* and net plankton emerged as the most consistent factors, with temperature playing a lesser role. Vertical light penetration (depth of the [[Euphotic Zone]]) appeared to act indirectly, mainly through its control of [[Threadfin Shad]] distribution, which in turn shaped the depth use of predators. Most fish netted in both reservoirs were within the upper 10 m of the water column.

## Background & Description

The study formed part of a broader survey of the Salt River system, funded through the National Marine Fisheries Service under P.L. 88-309 to the Arizona Game and Fish Department and Arizona State University. Four contiguous reservoirs on the Salt River (Roosevelt, Apache, Canyon and Saguaro) were built in the early 1900s for irrigation and municipal water storage, and later became heavily used for recreation near the [[Phoenix Metropolitan Area]].

The two study lakes differed markedly in morphology (values as reported by Rinne, 1973):

| Parameter | Roosevelt | Apache |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum depth (m) | 24.3 | 28.8 (reported as 24.3/28.8 in source table) |
| Surface area (km²) | 114.4 | 10.8 |
| Basin slope (%) | 7.0 | 32.0 |

Roosevelt is broad and open, while Apache is a narrow, fjord-like canyon reservoir. Mean width of Roosevelt was about five times that of Apache, and volume nearly six times greater. Roosevelt was never completely full during the study; Apache ranged from full to nearly empty.

The methods relied on [[Vertical Gill Nets]] built after Lackey (1968), with three mesh sizes (2.54, 3.81 and 4.62 cm bar measure) fished as a "gang." Nets were typically set for a minimum of three nights. Water quality was measured with a [[Hydrolab]] instrument at 1 m intervals in the top 20 m, and plankton was assessed through chlorophyll extraction and [[Clarke-Bumpus Plankton Sampler]] net collections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Thermal structure.** Both reservoirs are warm monomictic lakes with circulation in winter and summer stratification. Apache was drawn down through deep penstocks during the study, which removed most hypolimnetic water and produced near-isothermal conditions in run #4. Metalimnion depth differed: about 10–15 m in Roosevelt versus 5–7 m in Apache, reflecting Roosevelt's greater exposure to wind. Metalimnion temperatures were similar in both lakes (21.0–23.4 °C).

**Dissolved oxygen.** Hypolimnetic waters in both reservoirs dropped below 1.0 mg/L in summer. Winter overturn restored oxygen throughout the column. Roosevelt had a slightly thicker zone of reduced oxygen. The study used 2.0 mg/L as a lower tolerance limit for fish distribution, following [[Eley et al. (1967)]].

**Light and productivity.** Apache was clearer, with a mean euphotic zone depth of 9.2 m versus 5.1 m in Roosevelt. Specific conductance ranged from 1,031 to 3,380 µmhos/cm (about 500 to 1,800 mg/L total dissolved solids). pH ranged from 5.7 to 9.4, within the range reported as tolerable for adult freshwater fishes. Turbidity was generally higher in Roosevelt because it received direct inflow from the Salt River and Tonto Creek.

**Fish community.** [[Minckley and Johnson (1968)]] recorded 10 species from the four Salt River reservoirs. This study captured 13 taxa, dominated by Threadfin Shad, which made up more than two-thirds of all fish netted. [[Largemouth Bass]] was the most important game fish at the time.

## Key Findings

- **Overall distribution:** The majority of fishes netted in both reservoirs (1,410 total in the source abstract, recorded as 1,410–1,4xx across runs) occurred in the top 10 m of the water column.
- **Threadfin Shad:** Over 1,100 were taken from Roosevelt and about 800 from Apache. In Roosevelt, 99% of shad in run #1 were above the thermocline, versus 30% in Apache. Mean capture depth was 4.2 m in Roosevelt and 4.1 m in Apache. Shad distribution was the strongest driver of predator depth use.
- **Largemouth Bass:** 222 were netted in Roosevelt (mean depth 5.8 m) and 37 in Apache (mean depth 6.7 m). Most bass in both lakes were in the upper 10 m. About half of bass were in the [[Euphotic Zone]] in both reservoirs.
- **Carp:** 61 were caught in Roosevelt (mean depth 7.7 m) and 77 in Apache (mean depth 11.5 m). Carp were the only species consistently found deeper than the euphotic zone limit in both lakes, consistent with bottom-feeding habits.
- **Channel Catfish:** 46 from Roosevelt (mean depth 7.7 m) and 35 from Apache (mean depth 6.7 m). None from Apache were caught below 2.0 mg/L dissolved oxygen.
- **Sunfish (Lepomis):** 23 from Roosevelt (mean depth 4.8 m) and 73 from Apache (mean depth 5.4 m). Sunfish were largely shallow-water, insectivorous fish associated with lake margins.
- **Black Crappie:** 110 netted in Roosevelt (mean depth 3.4 m); only two in Apache. Black crappie inhabited the shallowest waters.
- **Buffalofishes:** Too few were caught for statistical conclusions (27 bigmouth buffalo in Roosevelt, 11 in Apache, according to the summary). Their mean capture depths were 6.2 m (Roosevelt) and 7.8 m (Apache).
- **Thermocline:** In Roosevelt, nearly all species were above the thermocline. In Apache, most species were below it. Because the fish depth distribution was nearly identical between the two lakes despite different thermocline depths, the author concluded temperature was not limiting.
- **Dissolved oxygen:** The author concluded that oxygen set outer limits on distribution, with other factors limiting fish within the adequately oxygenated zone. Oxygen alone did not explain depth distribution.
- **Euphotic zone:** About 60% of fishes in Roosevelt and 68% in Apache were captured within the euphotic zone. The author proposed that light penetration indirectly controlled fish depth by determining phytoplankton and zooplankton distribution, and thus shad distribution, with predators following shad.
- **Multiple linear regression:** Explained variability was generally low, reflecting the highly variable nature of these desert impoundments. Still, in nine of 21 cases where a biological correlation was established at level 1 or 2, a food-chain relationship was indicated.
- **Inter-lake comparison:** Correlation of fish depth distributions between lakes was low in run #1 (r = 0.36) and highest in run #4 (r = 0.88; r² = 0.78), suggesting similar vertical distribution patterns between reservoirs in most periods.
