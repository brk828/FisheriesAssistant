# Arizona-Nevada Cooperative Fisheries Survey – 1962

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries #Salmonidae #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae #Colorado-River

## Executive Summary

This report documents the 1962 joint fisheries management program between the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and the [[Nevada Fish and Game Department]] on the [[Colorado River]], focusing on the reservoirs [[Lake Mead]] and [[Lake Mohave]] and the riverine reach below [[Davis Dam]]. The survey combines creel census data, stocking records, and length-frequency analyses for a mixed warmwater/coldwater fishery. Key management themes include a coordinated interstate [[Rainbow Trout]] stocking program (with Nevada shifting plants to Lake Mohave and Arizona compensating below Davis Dam to reduce hauling costs), continued study of [[Threadfin Shad]] life history, and formal stocking recommendations submitted to the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] based on multi-year data regarding optimal stocking timing, fish size, and distribution methods from the [[Willow Beach National Fish and Wildlife Hatchery]].

## Background & Description

- **Rainbow Trout (Nevada stocked)**: Averaged **8 inches** in length at time of planting; 16,815 fin-clipped for identification/tracking.
- **Rainbow Trout (Arizona stocked)**: Averaged **8–10 inches** at time of planting.
- **Rainbow Trout (Lake Mohave, angler-caught, Katherine Wash)**: Averaged **17.30 inches** total length — indicating substantial post-stocking growth.
- **Largemouth Bass (Lake Mead, shocker barge sample, Jan–Feb 1962)**: Length-frequency distribution generated from **644 specimens**, spanning approximately 2–21 inches size classes.
- **Largemouth Bass (angler-caught, 1962)**:
  - Lake Mead: n = 3,879; **average length = 15.13 inches**
  - Lake Mohave: n = 2,095; **average length = 14.32 inches**
- Size differences between the two lakes' bass populations suggest differing growth conditions, forage availability, or fishing pressure/selectivity between [[Lake Mead]] and [[Lake Mohave]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake Mead**: A warmwater fishery ecosystem dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] (80.16% of catch composition at combined boat landings), with [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Black Crappie]] as secondary components. [[Bluegill]] and [[Rainbow Trout]] present in minor proportions. Angling success has been roughly stable since 1959 with a slight decline in catch-per-hour through 1961–1962.
- **Lake Mohave**: Increasingly a coldwater/put-and-grow trout fishery — [[Rainbow Trout]] comprised **80.11%** of the creel, with [[Largemouth Bass]] second at **16.59%**. Minor catches of [[Bluegill]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Common Carp]], [[Cutthroat Trout]], and the native [[Bonytail]] were also recorded — notable as one of the few explicit mentions of a native Colorado River cyprinid amid an otherwise introduced-species-dominated system.
- **Below Davis Dam**: A tailwater "put-and-take" trout fishery, with stocking practices being shifted toward wider distribution to improve poundage return and reduce dependence on immediate short-term harvest.
- **Threadfin Shad**: Functioning as a forage base species within Lake Mead/Lake Mohave; life history and summer sampling studies continued in 1962, though no specific numeric results are reported in this excerpt.
- **Stocking ecology**: Interstate cooperative management altered the spatial distribution of hatchery inputs — Nevada concentrated trout stocking in the [[Eldorado Canyon]] area of Lake Mohave (heaviest in late winter/spring), while Arizona increased plants below Davis Dam, reducing hauling distance/time for both agencies.

### Management & Policy Outcomes
- A cooperative interstate agreement was reached allowing **Nevada to stock its full Colorado River allotment directly into Lake Mohave**, while **Arizona correspondingly shifted plants to the river below Davis Dam**, reducing hauling costs/distance for both states.
- Formal **stocking recommendations** (timing, optimal size, and method) were submitted jointly to the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]], representing a multi-year data-driven management milestone.
- Reduced Nevada budget for creel census staff led to **discontinuation of census operations at Vegas Wash and Overton Dock after July 1, 1962**, creating a data gap for the latter half of the year at those stations.
- Continued interest in evaluating **[[Threadfin Shad]]** as a forage species, with sampling emphasis placed on summer months.
- Future research directions identified: continued evaluation of trout stocked from [[Willow Beach National Fish and Wildlife Hatchery]] to refine planting procedures.
