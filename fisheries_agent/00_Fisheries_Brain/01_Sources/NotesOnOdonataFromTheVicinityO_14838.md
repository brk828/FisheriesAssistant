## Executive Summary

This 1938 paper by [[E. M. Walker]] and [[W. E. Ricker]] documents the [[Odonata]] fauna (dragonflies and damselflies) of the region surrounding [[Cultus Lake]], British Columbia, based on collections made largely by Ricker between the 1920s and 1937. The study catalogs 40 species across nine aquatic and semi-aquatic habitat types — including a deep glacial lake, warm and cold outlet streams, boggy sloughs, golf-course ponds, and high-elevation alpine sloughs and tarns — situated within the [[Vancouveran Faunal Zone]] of the Fraser Valley. Three records are of particular biogeographic significance: [[Macromia rickeri]] (a newly described endemic species), and first Canadian records of [[Tanypteryx hageni]] and [[Octogomphus specularis]]. The paper integrates habitat descriptions, phenology, morphometric variation, and zoogeographic analysis relating local Odonata distributions to broader faunal zones (Vancouveran, Okanagan, Sitkan, Upper Sonoran, and boreal/Transition Zone elements).

## Background & Description

While the paper is primarily faunistic/distributional, it includes several morphometric notes used to assess geographic variation and subspecific identity:

- **[[Lestes disjunctus]]**: British Columbian specimens noted as unusually large; six males from Chilliwack golf course ranged 36.5–39.5 mm total length, hind wing 19–21 mm.
- **[[Somatochlora albicincta]]**: Liumchin Mountain specimens described as larger and darker than typical boreal populations, but smaller than Vancouver Island (sea-level) specimens. Measurements (4 males, 2 females): total length ♂ 49–52 mm, ♀ 50–51 mm; abdomen ♂ 35–37 mm, ♀ 37 mm; hind wing ♂ 31–32.5 mm, ♀ 32.5 mm.
- **[[Sympetrum pallipes]]**: Comparative measurements show Indian Reserve Sloughs specimens (total length 37.5 mm, abdomen 24.5 mm, hind wing 27.5 mm) slightly smaller than Washington State (Anacortes) specimens (38.0/26.0/28.5 mm); Sweltzer Creek individuals noted as distinctly smaller still.
- **[[Sympetrum decisum]]**: Marked size variation noted; Chilliwack (Aug. 1936) specimens exceptionally large, exceeding any eastern Canadian material examined.
- **[[Sympetrum semicinctum]]**: All local specimens described as large relative to eastern Canadian populations; smallest/largest males from Hope Slough measured total length 31.0–35.5 mm, abdomen 18.5–23.0 mm, hind wing 22.5–26.5 mm.
- **[[Leucorrhinia hudsonica]]**: Specimens uniformly large — total length 32.5–33.0 mm, abdomen 22.0 mm, hind wing 23.5–25.0 mm.
- Thoracic stripe pattern used diagnostically in **[[Aeschna interrupta nevadensis]]** — broader, divided stripes distinguishing coastal *nevadensis* from more typical eastern *interrupta*, with intergradation toward the race *interna* noted in the Kootenay district.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper details habitat partitioning among species across a gradient of lentic and lotic freshwater systems:

- **[[Cultus Lake]]**: Deep, clear glaciated U-valley lake (160 ft elevation, 3 mi long), epilimnion warm in summer (20–22°C), stony/bedrock shores with sparse *Scirpus validus*, *Typha latifolia*, *Nymphaea polysepala*. Core resident species: *Argia emma*, *Enallagma cyathigerum*, *Macromia rickeri*, *Aeschna umbrosa*.
- **[[Sweltzer Creek]]**: Warm, moderate-current outlet stream (~1 ft³/s summer flow) with muddy/*Salix*-choked upper reach and stony lower reach. Dominant species *Argia emma* extremely abundant; breeding residents include *E. cyathigerum*, *Ophiogomphus occidentis*, *Octogomphus specularis*.
- **[[Liumchin Creek]]**: Cold, swift, stony mountain stream fed by snowmelt; unusual habitat record for *Tanypteryx hageni* (found on a log over flowing water at 800 ft), contradicting prior assumption (Kennedy 1917) that this genus is a bog/swamp specialist.
- **[[Chilliwack River]]**: Large, cold, flood-prone river with shifting channel; likely not a primary breeding habitat for collected specimens.
- **[[Lost Slough]]**: ~5-acre boggy slough with *Scirpus*, *Typha*, *Carex*, *Sphagnum*; highest species richness among boggy habitats (11 species), including rare *Ischnura erratica*.
- **[[Indian Reserve Sloughs]]**, **[[Hope Slough]]**, **[[Chilliwack Golf Course]] slough**, **[[Sumas Canal Sloughs]]**: Varying degrees of stagnant, emergent-vegetation-choked lowland waters dominated by *Sympetrum* spp. and *Libellula* spp.
- **[[Liumchin Mountain Sloughs]]** and **[[Liumchin Mountain Lake]] (Dipper Lake)**: High-elevation (4700–5000 ft) cold habitats that likely freeze solid in winter; dominated almost exclusively by *Somatochlora albicincta*, a species apparently limited in adult stage by high summer temperatures — reaching sea level only on Vancouver Island where climate is cooler.

Zoogeographic patterning links species to faunal zones: [[Vancouveran Faunal Zone]] endemics/residents (e.g., *Macromia rickeri*, *Octogomphus specularis*, *Tanypteryx hageni*), boreal/transcontinental species tied to higher latitude or altitude (*Aeschna juncea*, *Somatochlora albicincta*), Upper Sonoran-affiliated western species (*Argia emma*, *Ischnura cervula*, *Amphiagrion abbreviatum*), and southern-limited transcontinental forms (*Enallagma carunculatum*, *Anax junius*, *Libellula lydia*, *Leucorrhinia intacta*).

## Key Findings

- **New/first records**: *[[Macromia rickeri]]* described as a new species (also found at Salmon Arm, B.C.), interpreted as a probable northern derivative of *M. magnifica* (ranging Vernon, B.C. to Arizona/California); *[[Tanypteryx hageni]]* and *[[Octogomphus specularis]]* recorded for the first time in Canada.
- **Habitat surprise**: *Tanypteryx hageni* found in a swift mountain stream habitat, contrary to Kennedy's (1917) prediction that related species would prove to be bog/swamp inhabitants.
- **Endemism**: *Ischnura erratica* restricted to extreme southwestern B.C. (including southern Vancouver Island), ranging south to California; rare in B.C.
- **Coastwise wide-ranging species**: *Cordulegaster dorsalis* ranges California to Alaska, spanning the entire Sitkan faunal area.
- **Cordilleran-restricted boreal species**: *Aeschna palmata* and *Somatochlora semicircularis* confined to the western Cordillera but likely province-wide within it.
- **Taxonomic ambiguity flagged**: *Aeschna interrupta* races (*nevadensis* vs. *interna*) grade imperceptibly into one another across B.C.; authors call for taxonomic revision based on broader geographic sampling.
- **Phenology**: *Argia emma* observed on the wing until late September at Sweltzer Creek/Cultus Lake; *Enallagma cyathigerum* recorded flying at Cultus Lake until early November.
- **Mass mortality event**: Several dozen dead *Somatochlora albicincta* of both sexes found floating on Liumchin Mountain slough surfaces (16.viii.36), alongside active oviposition by live females — suggestive of a synchronized emergence/die-off event.
- **Overall faunal list**: 40 species total (9 Zygoptera, 31 Anisoptera) enumerated with precise collection dates, localities, and collector attributions, forming a baseline faunistic record for the Cultus Lake region within the broader context of British Columbian and Pacific Northwest odonate biogeography.
