# Federal Register Notices, April 19, 1983: Endangered Species Listing Priorities and Public Land Actions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This scanned page from the [[Federal Register]] (Vol. 48, No. 76, Tuesday, April 19, 1983) contains several unrelated notices rather than a single research study. The most relevant to aquatic conservation is a draft set of guidelines from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] for ranking species for listing or reclassification under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The page also includes a [[Bureau of Land Management]] land sale notice for public tracts near the [[Blackfoot River]] in Idaho, an [[Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act]] land-conveyance notice, a permit application for endangered species activity, and a [[National Park Service]] advisory council meeting notice for the [[Pioneer National Historic Trail]]. The text is OCR-degraded, and some figures and table contents are only partly legible.

## Background & Description

The page covers five topics:

- **Public land sales (BLM, Idaho):** Public-auction notice for tracts in the Idaho Falls District, including the Soda Springs Resource Area. The notice describes about 1,025 acres, with tracts identified by number (Tracts No. 4, 5, and 6 are referenced). Tract 5 adjoins the ordinary high-water line of the Blackfoot River. Sales were scheduled at the Soda Springs Resource Area office and at the Idaho Falls office, with bidding terms available from the district manager.
- **Alaska Native Claims (BLM, Alaska):** Notice of a decision to convey land under the [[Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act]] (ANCSA) to a Native corporation, with protest and appeal procedures and contact addresses for the Anchorage state office and the Alaska State Office.
- **Endangered species permit application:** Notice of an application for a permit to conduct activity involving endangered species, with comments invited from the public within a set period. The specific species and applicant details are partly illegible in the scan.
- **Pioneer National Historic Trail Advisory Council:** Meeting notice for April 28–29, 1983, at the Nauvoo Visitor Center, Nauvoo, Illinois, held under the Federal Advisory Committee Act, to discuss trail management and resource issues.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The most ecologically significant material is the Fish and Wildlife Service draft guidance on listing priority. It states that the Service has limited resources to implement the Act and that the priority system must be based on an assessment of threat magnitude, immediacy of threat, and taxonomic distinctiveness. The draft explains that candidate species and species that are already listed are ranked together, and that species with a higher degree of threat (including conflicts with construction or economic activity) should receive priority for recovery planning and funding. The guidance covers fishes, reptiles, amphibians, insects, mollusks, crustaceans, and other taxa.

The Blackfoot River tract is the only element that touches a freshwater habitat directly. The public-land disposal, by transferring land adjacent to the river, has potential implications for shoreline and riparian habitat, but the notice does not assess those effects.

## Key Findings

- **Draft listing priority guidelines (Fish and Wildlife Service):**
  - Comments on the draft guidelines were due by June 20, 1983 (OCR reading "June zo, 1983"). Comments were to be sent to the Director of the Fish and Wildlife Service in Washington, D.C.
  - The system ranks species for listing, delisting, and reclassification (for example, endangered to threatened) using a priority scale. The guidance says it establishes 12 priority categories, with Table 1 and Table 2 summarizing criteria. Scale numbers are not directly comparable across different categories.
  - Highest priority goes to species facing high-magnitude, imminent threats. Those with a conflict with construction or development projects are to be considered within the system.
  - The guidance is intended to satisfy the requirements of the amended Act and to direct resources toward the most critically endangered species.
- **Public land (Idaho):** About 1,025 acres offered at public auction. Tract 5 abuts the Blackfoot River high-water line. Bidding and terms were to be obtained from the BLM Idaho Falls District Office or the Soda Springs office.
- **Alaska (ANCSA):** A conveyance decision was issued, with a 30-day window for protests and appeals. Parties with an interest were advised of the appeal process.
- **Endangered species permit:** A permit application was published for public comment, with comments to be submitted to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regional office. Details are partly illegible.
- **Pioneer National Historic Trail Advisory Council:** Public meeting, April 28–29, 1983, Nauvoo, Illinois, open to the public, with public comment opportunities.
