- **Mesethmoid and ethmoid region:** The text describes ossified or cartilaginous structures in the oblique and median region, including paired processes that surround the vomerine region.

## Key Findings

1. **Protrusion of the upper jaw** is produced partly by the outward and forward movement of the entire upper jaw and suspensorium. The [[Protractor Hyomandibularis Muscle]] drives this movement, and the mandible moves with it.
2. **Premaxillary release:** The ascending rami of the opposing dentaries push the lower ends of the maxillae forward, which rocks the upper ends backward and releases the premaxillae. This is the mechanism for forward thrust of the premaxilla.
3. **Feeding by buccal pumping:** Food is drawn in by alternate expansion and constriction of the oral cavity. Expansion comes from the protractor hyomandibularis and the levator arcus palatini, together with the ventral mandibular muscles. Constriction comes from the adductor mandibulae and adductor arcus palatini.
4. **Experimental evidence (after Edwards):** Stimulating the hyoid musculature in live fish produced forward, then backward, thrusting of the premaxilla. Stimulating the adductors produced the reverse effect (OCR-garbled; exact sequence uncertain).
5. **Ligamentous control:** Ligaments and paired tracker-like structures constrain and guide the premaxillary and maxillary movements, which is a key part of the jaw's functional integration.
6. **Comparative significance:** The two figures contrast the upper jaw arrangement of *Carpiodes* and *Hydrocyon*, illustrating how the premaxilla–maxilla–mandible linkage varies across these fish groups.
