# Gila Topminnow Reintroduction Memo - Bylas Spring

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gambusia

## Executive Summary

This 1996 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum concerns the [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction at [[Bylas Spring]], Arizona. Surrogate [[Gambusia]] collected from the spring were examined for parasites earlier in July 1996. Only two parasites were found, [[Cleidodiscus]] and [[Ambiphrya]]. The Center Director recommends stocking only fish free of parasites other than those already identified. Where needed, fish should come from [[Controlled Propagation]] rather than ponds or open environments.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal memorandum, dated 7/25/96.
- **From:** Ronald D. Major, Center Director, Fish Health Center (FHC), Pinetop, AZ.
- **To:** Project Leader, Arizona Fishery Resources Office (AZFRO), Pinetop, AZ.
- **Subject:** Gila topminnow reintroduction at Bylas Spring.
- **Gila topminnow:** The native fish targeted for restocking the spring area.
- **Surrogate fish:** [[Gambusia]] were used as stand-ins to test the spring for parasites before any topminnow were introduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The spring is the target site for restocking. Before introduction, the health of the receiving environment was assessed through parasite screening of surrogate fish. The memo does not report water chemistry, habitat conditions, invertebrate communities, population sizes, or other aquatic ecosystem data.

Parasite screening is the main health safeguard in the document. The FHC expresses concern about introducing pathogens or parasites from unscreened sources, particularly ponds or open water that may carry unknown infections.

## Key Findings

1. **Parasites identified:** Internal and external examination of surrogate Gambusia from Bylas Spring found only *Cleidodiscus* and *Ambiphrya*. The memo does not give sample sizes, prevalence, or intensity.
2. **Stocking criteria:** Only Gila topminnow free of parasites, apart from the two already identified, should be used for restocking.
3. **Source preference:** Fish spawned and reared in a controlled environment are preferred. Fish from ponds or open environments are not recommended.
4. **Ongoing support:** The FHC will examine any potential source populations for parasites before they are used.
