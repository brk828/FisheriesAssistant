# Creel Census (Lakes) — Fishery Investigations in Region I, Arizona (1966)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #creel-census #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1966 completion report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] presents creel census data for five lentic fisheries in Region I: [[Big Lake]], [[Lyman Lake]], [[Rainbow Lake]], [[Show Low Lake]], and [[Woods Canyon Lake]]. Data were gathered through scheduled spot-check creel surveys (three full-day visits per month — two weekend, one weekday) to assess fishing success, species/size composition of the catch, and relative fishing pressure. The dominant harvested species was [[Rainbow Trout]], supplemented by minor catches of [[Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Largemouth Bass]] at Lyman Lake. Catch-per-man-hour (CPMH) ranged from 0.31 (Woods Canyon Lake) to 0.95 (Big Lake). Prepared baits were the most popular fishing method across all sites, while fly fishing — though least popular — was consistently the most successful method. Maricopa County anglers dominated angler-trip origin at most sites. A key management concern raised was the poor return on a massive stocking effort (>600,000 trout) at Lyman Lake, prompting a recommendation to shift toward warm-water species management there.

## Background & Description

Size composition of creeled fish is reported as total length (inches) by month, reflecting the population structure available to anglers rather than a laboratory morphometric study.

### Rainbow Trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)
- **Big Lake**: n = 1,983 measured fish; average total length ≈ **9.3 in** (seasonal monthly averages 8.85–9.76 in); 116 fish unmeasured.
- **Lyman Lake**: n = 895 measured; average total length **10.81 in** (highest average length of all census areas); 71 fish unmeasured.
- **Rainbow Lake**: n = 499 measured; average total length ≈ **7.62 in** (bimodal distribution with two size peaks around 10 in and 15–18 in, suggesting mixed stocked cohorts).
- **Show Low Lake**: n = 760 measured; average total length **8.91 in**.
- **Woods Canyon Lake**: n = 346 measured (14 unmeasured); average total length **10.04 in**.

### Brook Trout (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) — Big Lake only
- n = 35 measured fish; average total length **10.54 in** (May–September).

### Channel Catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — Lyman Lake only
- n = 5 measured fish; average total length **7.20 in** (August–October).

### Largemouth Bass (*Micropterus salmoides*) — Lyman Lake only
- n = 2 measured fish; average total length **9.50 in** (September).

### Brown Trout (*Salmo trutta*) — Lyman Lake only
- n = 2 measured fish; average total length **8.00 in** (May).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: All five census areas are managed put-and-take or mixed-fishery impoundments in Region I, Arizona, spanning montane trout waters (Big Lake, Rainbow Lake, Woods Canyon Lake) and a lower-elevation, warmer reservoir (Lyman Lake) more suited to warm-water species.
- **Stocking pressure**: Lyman Lake received extremely heavy trout stocking (>600,000 fish) during the study period, yet yielded comparatively low returns to anglers, suggesting the habitat may not support trout well — likely due to temperature, productivity, or competition unsuitable for salmonids, and better suited to warm-water fish assemblages ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]]).
- **Species assemblage shift**: The presence of incidental catches of Channel Catfish, Largemouth Bass, and Brown Trout at Lyman Lake (alongside dominant Rainbow Trout) indicates an already-transitioning warm-water/cold-water mixed community.
- **Seasonal survey coverage**: Census periods were tailored to each lake's ice-free season or peak-use season (e.g., Big Lake: May–Sept; Lyman Lake: ice-free period; Woods Canyon Lake: May–Aug), reflecting differing elevation/climate regimes across the region.
- **Angler-use patterns as ecological pressure indicator**: Fishing pressure (angler-days, hours fished) varied substantially by lake and month, providing an index of harvest pressure on each fish population.

## Key Findings

**Catch Rates (Catch Per Man-Hour, CPMH)**
| Lake | CPMH | Avg. Rainbow Trout Length (in) |
|---|---|---|
| [[Big Lake]] | 0.95 | 9.3 |
| [[Lyman Lake]] | 0.51 | 10.8 |
| [[Rainbow Lake]] | 0.44 | 9.6 |
| [[Show Low Lake]] | 0.53 | 8.9 |
| [[Woods Canyon Lake]] | 0.31 | 10.0 |

**Fishing Method Effectiveness (Table II)**
- **Prepared baits** were the most-used method at all five lakes (58–79% of anglers).
- **Fly fishing** was used by only 1–14% of anglers yet had the highest success rate at every lake (48–100% of fly anglers caught ≥1 fish), confirming it as the most effective but least popular method.
- Lure fishing showed moderate popularity and success; worm fishing was generally least successful relative to its popularity (e.g., only 29–38% success rate despite substantial use).

**Angler Origin (Table III)**
- [[Maricopa County]] residents comprised the largest angler group at four of five lakes (Big Lake 46%, Rainbow Lake 42%, Show Low Lake 32%, Woods Canyon Lake 88%).
- **Lyman Lake** was the exception — dominated by local [[Apache County]] residents (30%) combined with regional counties, totaling 63% local-county fishermen.
- **Big Lake** had the highest out-of-state angler proportion at 29%.

**Juvenile (Unlicensed) Anglers (Table IV)**
| Lake | % Juvenile Anglers |
|---|---|
| Big Lake | 12% |
| Lyman Lake | 12% |
| Rainbow Lake | 7% |
| Show Low Lake | 10% |
| Woods Canyon Lake | 20% |

- At Woods Canyon Lake, juveniles made up 20% of fishermen but accounted for only **8%** of the total catch, indicating lower per-capita catch efficiency among juvenile anglers.

**Management Recommendations**
1. Eliminate creel census activity during winter months regionwide due to low activity/return on effort.
2. Stock [[Lyman Lake]] with multiple warm-water fish species given poor trout returns despite massive stocking (>600,000 individuals).
3. Substantially reduce future trout plantings at Lyman Lake until warm-water fish introductions are evaluated, or until the lake undergoes renovation.
