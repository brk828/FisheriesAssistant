# Middle Spring (S2) and the Endangered Gila Topminnow: Habitat Restoration and Reintroduction Project

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This draft proposal, dated 6 May 1996, sets out a plan to restore the [[Middle Spring]] (S2) system at the [[Bylas Springs]] complex on the [[San Carlos Apache Reservation]] and to reintroduce [[Gila Topminnow]] of S2 lineage. The authors are staff of the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Fishery Resources Offices and [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]) and the [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] Recreation and Wildlife Department. The S2 population disappeared in 1990 after surface water depletion, so the project combines spring renovation, invasive plant removal, native plant translocation, fish restocking and one year of monitoring. The renovation budget is $4,000.00.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, family Poeciliidae) is endemic to the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona and New Mexico and to the Rio Gila, Concepcion and Sonora basins of Sonora, Mexico. It was once among the abundant fishes of the lower Colorado River watershed. The species was listed as endangered in 1967. Mid-1960s declines were attributed to predation by the introduced [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) and to habitat degradation.

The Bylas Springs complex consists of three small springs beside the Gila River, labeled S1, S2 and S3 from west to east. All three historically held Gila topminnow. S1 is one of eight remaining populations. The site history is as follows:

- **1968:** Topminnow discovered at the springs.
- **1980:** A Gila River flood allowed mosquitofish to invade S1 and S3.
- **1983-1984:** [[Fish Barrier]] structures and fences were built on all three springs to exclude mosquitofish and livestock. Later renovations did not permanently remove mosquitofish from S1 and S3.
- **1968-1989:** Gila topminnow were the only fish found in S2. Mosquitofish never infested it.
- **November 1987 and September 1988:** Two samples of S2 fish were stocked into Upper and Lower Headquarters ponds at [[Roper Lake State Park]] as refugia.
- **1990:** Gila topminnow disappeared from S2 after surface water depletion. Causes cited were vegetation increase after livestock exclusion, higher evapotranspiration, and sediment and debris build-up (Marsh and Minckley 1990).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Elders' historical account.** The [[Elders' Cultural Advisory Council]] described S2 (*ya bilnagoswaud*, "Mesquite Round") as having large mesquites, more surface flow, little underbrush, and stream banks lined with [[Yerba Mansa]] (*Anemopsis californica*). Rushes were absent or scarce at the spring and along the stream. The elders reported that [[Salt Cedar]] (*Tamarix chinensis*) was absent until about 1934-1935, and that the spring was once completely dry on the surface for an unknown period.

**Habitat use.** Recent studies at S1 and S3 (Jacks and Leon 1992) and of the related Yaqui topminnow (Maes 1995) found Gila topminnow along the margins of a few shallow pools and in deeply incised spring heads. Relatively high disturbance regimes maintained each habitat type. The proposal argues that the elders' description of a more open system matches this habitat data.

**Threats identified.** Invasive vegetation, especially salt cedar and dense rushes, is the main habitat problem. Salt cedar is reported to use large amounts of water. Mosquitofish invasion and livestock disturbance are also identified.

## Key Findings

**Project objectives.** The proposal lists eight objectives:

1. Remove fencing around the existing fish barrier.
2. Remove emergent vegetation and silt upstream of the barrier.
3. Deepen and reinforce the footing on the downstream side of the barrier.
4. Remove introduced plant species, such as salt cedar.
5. Install a series of four cement troughs (6 ft long) below the spring head, with the most downstream trough serving as a secondary fish barrier.
6. Transplant Yerba Mansa from disturbed areas to other sections of the stream course.
7. Reintroduce S2-lineage Gila topminnow from the [[Hassayampa Preserve]].
8. Monitor the S2 system's response to treatment.

**Methods.**

- Fencing will be removed manually. A backhoe will clear vegetation and sediment upstream of the barrier. Trough installation uses hand tools for site preparation, with a backhoe for placement. The fourth trough includes a drop structure and splash pad to act as an additional barrier. It will also serve as a cattle drinker.
- Salt cedar will be cut, debris removed, and stumps treated with the herbicide [[Tordon RTU]] immediately after cutting. The active ingredients are 5.4% picloram and 20.9% 2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid. The product is described as low in toxicity to mammals and birds and moderate in toxicity to fishes.
- Arizona Game and Fish Department personnel will collect more than 130 S2-lineage Gila topminnow from existing refugia, mainly the Hassayampa Preserve. Fish will be held in aquaria for inspection by State and USFWS biologists before release.
- Monitoring will occur monthly for the first year at three permanent sites: the spring head, the trough area, and the area immediately upstream of the original barrier. Continuous-recording thermographs will log hourly stream temperatures. Dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity and discharge will be measured before work begins and monthly afterward. Data will be held by the San Carlos Apache Recreation and Wildlife Department and treated as proprietary.

**Elders' recommendations (memo of 29 April 1996).** The elders advised clearing rushes (backhoe use is acceptable) and removing salt cedar, but gave no opinion on fencing. They asked to be notified before streambank clearing so medicinal plants can be dug out first. They also asked that Yerba Mansa be replaced on the banks and that mesquites and other trees be left undisturbed.

**Budget (renovation only).**

| Category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Salaries and per diem | $1,000.00 |
| Equipment and supplies | $3,000.00 |
| **Total** | **$4,000.00** |

Equipment includes four cement troughs at $300.00 each ($1,200.00), backhoe transport and operation for three days ($1,200.00), and cement mixer rental, cement and rebar ($250.00). Arizona FRO-Pinetop will cover monitoring and fence costs outside this budget.

**Deliverables.** The Principal Investigators will provide a synopsis of the completed work to the SCWD Director, the Arizona FRO Project Leader and the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] Central Arizona Project Biologist. One year after reintroduction, a status report will go to the SCWD Director. The Director decides whether to release it in full or redacted form.

**Timeline and contingencies.** Work is expected to start in the first half of June 1996 and finish within five working days. Reintroduction this year depends on findings from the Hassayampa Preserve and Arizona State University. Some remaining S2-lineage fish may have to be sent to Dexter for propagation before enough individuals exist to stock S2.
