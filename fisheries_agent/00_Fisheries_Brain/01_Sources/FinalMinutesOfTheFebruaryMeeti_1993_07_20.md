# Final Minutes of the February 24, 1993 Meeting of the Desert Fishes Recovery Team

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #desert-fishes #Cyprinidae #conservation-management

## Executive Summary

This document records the proceedings of the February 24, 1993 meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), convened at Tonto National Forest, Phoenix, Arizona, under U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) auspices. The meeting addressed recovery planning, taxonomy, genetics, and management status for a suite of imperiled southwestern desert fishes, including the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Desert Pupfish]], the [[Gila Robusta]]/[[Gila Intermedia]] (roundtail/Gila chub) species complex, [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]]. Major topics included: reassignment of team responsibilities (e.g., Little Colorado spinedace moving to the Apache Trout Recovery Team), genetic evidence supporting the separation of upper- and lower-basin [[Roundtail Chub]] lineages, a proposal to add four additional Gila basin native fishes as Category 2 candidate species, ongoing habitat and population monitoring in [[Aravaipa Creek]], a contested taxonomic reclassification of loach minnow (*Tiaroga* → *Rhinichthys*), and plans to repropose critical habitat for spikedace and loach minnow. The tone throughout is one of urgency regarding rapid, unexplained population collapses (e.g., roundtail chub) juxtaposed against limited agency resources and political sensitivities around candidate species listings.

## Background & Description

The DFRT is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service-convened recovery team responsible for developing and overseeing recovery plans for federally listed and candidate desert fish species in the American Southwest, primarily within the [[Gila River Basin]], [[Yaqui River Basin]], [[Colorado River Basin]], and associated desert spring/stream systems (e.g., [[Rio Sonoyta]], [[Quitobaquito Springs]]).

Attendees included representatives from Arizona State University, U.S. Forest Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Bureau of Reclamation (BR), New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (NMDGF), the University of Arizona, Dexter National Fish Hatchery, San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge (NWR), and multiple FWS field offices.

Species under DFRT purview discussed at this meeting span five families:
- **Poeciliidae**: [[Gila Topminnow]]
- **Cyprinidae**: [[Sonora Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Virgin Chub]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Red Shiner]] (non-native)
- **Catostomidae**: [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], undescribed [[Little Colorado River Sucker]]
- **Ictaluridae**: [[Yaqui Catfish]]
- **Cyprinodontidae**: [[Desert Pupfish]] and its subspecies/forms ([[Quitobaquito Pupfish]], proposed [[Colorado Pupfish]], proposed [[Sonoyta Pupfish]])

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Aravaipa Creek]]**: A key native fish refugium in Arizona, monitored monthly with quarterly full canyon walk-throughs. Winter flooding homogenized in-stream habitat into uniform "riffle" structure. Post-flood sampling recorded healthy numbers of [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Speckled Dace]] at the Nature Conservancy guest house site; loach minnow were found approximately 14 miles downstream of prior known range. Notably, **no red shiner** (an invasive competitor) had been detected in Aravaipa Creek since February 1991, though red shiner were newly collected in the nearby San Pedro River near the Aravaipa confluence — raising reinvasion concern. A fish barrier project for Aravaipa Creek has seen zero progress, stalled pending completion of the Central Arizona Project (CAP) Section 7 consultation.

- **[[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] / [[Rio Yaqui]] system**: Threatened by groundwater pumping for agricultural development in Sonora, Mexico (crop shift from alfalfa to chiles, land clearing for buffelgrass). The refuge is installing well-monitoring systems to track pumping impacts. FWS is pursuing instream water rights on Leslie Creek and San Bernardino Creek. The North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) was flagged as a potential driver of increased Mexican agricultural pressure on Yaqui basin water resources.

- **[[Sycamore Canyon]] (Sonora chub habitat)**: Concerns raised over cattle grazing effects on Sonora chub habitat in the absence of formal Section 7 consultation on the grazing allotment management plan.

- **[[Gila River]] / [[San Francisco River]] / [[Verde River]] systems**: Discussed as focal areas for critical habitat reproposal and reintroduction planning for spikedace and loach minnow. The Alma Valley reach of the San Francisco River, a candidate reintroduction site, seasonally dries due to irrigation withdrawal.

- **[[Agua Fria]] drainage**: Proposed BLM-led Gila chub translocation program using stock from Silver Creek (large, monitored population) into small tributary streams; Sycamore Creek stock recommended for upper reintroduction sites.

- **Regional policy context**: Increasing county-level involvement in recovery planning (e.g., Catron County, NM; Coalition of Arizona/New Mexico Counties for Stable Economic Growth) was noted as a potential constraint on FWS recovery authority via the National Federal Lands Conference framework.

## Key Findings

### Taxonomy & Genetics — Roundtail/Gila Chub Complex

A genetic briefing (mtDNA and allozyme data) presented at the meeting differentiated *Gila* lineages as follows:

| Taxon | mtDNA Lineage | Allozyme Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| *G. elegans* ([[Bonytail Chub]]) | MtDNA1 | Fixed allozymic difference |
| *G. seminuda* ([[Virgin Chub]]) | MtDNA1 | Intermediate between elegans/cypha |
| *G.* "Moapa form" | MtDNA1 | Intermediate between elegans/cypha |
| *G. cypha* ([[Humpback Chub]]) | MtDNA2 | Fixed allozymic difference (upper & lower basin) |
| *G. r. jordani* | MtDNA2 | Cypha-influenced allozymes |
| *G. robusta* (upper basin) | MtDNA2 | Cypha/robusta allozyme mix |
| *G. robusta* (lower basin) | MtDNA3 | Fixed allozymic difference; low within-species variation |
| *G. intermedia* ([[Gila Chub]]) | MtDNA3 | No fixed allozyme difference vs. robusta; high between-population variation |
| *G. r. "grahami"* | MtDNA3 | Allozymes same as robusta; high between-population variation |

- This data supports treating the upper-basin (*robusta/elegans/cypha* complex) and lower-basin (*robusta/intermedia/"grahami"* complex) roundtail chub populations as genetically and potentially taxonomically distinct.
- A single Mexican genetic sample suggested Mexican roundtail chub populations may represent yet another distinct lineage.
- In New Mexico's Gila basin, [[Turkey Creek]] populations are classified as *G. intermedia*; all other New Mexico populations are *G. r. "grahami"*.

### Population Status & Decline

- **[[Roundtail Chub]]**: Rapid, unexplained population collapse across Arizona over the past decade. In the San Juan River (New Mexico), populations collapsed over 15–20 years despite no obvious predator pressure or dewatering. W.L. Minckley estimated the species could face **extinction within approximately 10 years** absent intervention.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]**: Identified in the Biennial Report to Congress as the only Arizona-listed fish species showing an **increasing** status trend.
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (non-native, Gila River NM): Abundant at Redrock and Virden in 1981 but severely reduced by 1983 flooding above the Middle Box; populations have not recovered.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (Verde River, AGFD October 1992 survey, Morgan Ranch downstream): Only 6 individuals captured during heavy seining effort, indicating very low relative abundance.

### Candidate Species Recommendations

The team recommended **Category 2 candidate status** for:
- [[Longfin Dace]]
- [[Desert Sucker]]
- [[Sonora Sucker]]
- [[Little Colorado River Sucker]] (undescribed *Catostomus* sp., genetics under study by a student of Tom Dowling)
- An as-yet-undefined taxonomic unit of [[Speckled Dace]]

(Dissent noted from D. Propst, who supported Category 2 for desert, Sonora, and Little Colorado suckers but opposed Category 2 status for longfin dace and could not support candidate status for an undefined speckled dace unit.)

### Taxonomic Controversy — Loach Minnow

- A proposed reclassification would subordinate *Tiaroga* (loach minnow) into the genus *Rhinichthys*.
- Accepted by the American Fisheries Society in their 1991 common names list (AFS Special Publication 20); presented as a poster at the 1989 ASIH meeting; slated for publication in an upcoming book by Mayden.
- W.L. Minckley and D. Propst both disputed the change, citing contrary data, and predicted it would not withstand scientific scrutiny.
- **Team recommendation**: FWS should continue using the name *Tiaroga* until the change is formally published and evaluated by the academic community.

### Desert Pupfish Nomenclature

- Public review draft of the Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan was published in the Federal Register on January 29, 1993, with a signed final plan targeted for September 30, 1993.
- Proposed common names (following *Battle Against Extinction*): **[[Colorado Pupfish]]** for *C. m. macularius*; **[[Sonoyta Pupfish]]** for the undescribed Rio Sonoyta form; **[[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]** for *C. m. eremus*.

### Yaqui Basin Fishes

- A [[Yaqui Chub]] manuscript was in press in *Biological Conservation*.
- New genetic data indicate the Yaqui River form of *Agosia* (longfin dace relative) is **distinct** from the Gila basin form.
- Dexter National Fish Hatchery maintained 15–20 mature [[Yaqui Catfish]] ("pure" stock from Rio Sirupa) with planned breeding in 1993; a Yaqui catfish exchange program with Monterrey, Mexico is exploring aquaculture potential. Concerns were raised about the confused state of Mexican catfish taxonomy.
- Historic range question raised regarding whether Willcox Playa basin qualifies as native range for Yaqui basin fishes — determined **not** to be within historic range (W.L. Minckley).

### Critical Habitat Reproposal (Spikedace & Loach Minnow)

Recommended areas for inclusion:
- **Spikedace & Loach Minnow**: Verde River above Camp Verde; lower Wet Beaver Creek; lower West Clear Creek; Gila River (AZ/NM border to Middle Box); Redfield Canyon; Hot Springs Canyon; San Pedro River; Bonita Creek; Gila Box (AZ)
- **Spikedace only**: East Fork Gila River; San Francisco River (up to above Alma)
- **Loach Minnow only**: Verde River

### Administrative/Policy Outcomes

- Little Colorado spinedace recovery planning responsibility transferred to the Apache Trout Recovery Team (three new members added).
- Recommendation to consider forming a new team or subgroup for [[Rio Yaqui]] fishes, partly in response to anticipated NAFTA-driven development pressures.
- Team purpose under review; original mandate (writing recovery plans) nearing completion, prompting discussion of an expanded advisory role — though most members declined a formal Section 7 consultation review function.
- Meeting participation to remain open to non-members unless problems arise, with attendance lists to be maintained for potential closed-session use.
