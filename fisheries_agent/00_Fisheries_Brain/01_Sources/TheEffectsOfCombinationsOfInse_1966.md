# The Effects of Combinations of Insecticides on Susceptible and Resistant Mosquito Fish

## Executive Summary
This 1966 study by Ferguson and Bingham investigated the combined toxicological effects of paired insecticides on two populations of [[Mosquito Fish]] (*Gambusia affinis*): a **resistant population** from an agriculturally contaminated ditch near Belzoni, Mississippi, and a **susceptible population** from an uncontaminated pond near State College, Mississippi. The research addressed a gap in [[Ecotoxicology]] literature regarding the interactive (rather than isolated) effects of pesticides on non-target aquatic organisms in intensively sprayed cotton-growing regions. Fish were exposed to all pairwise combinations of four insecticides commonly used in the Mississippi Delta — [[Endrin]], [[DDT]], [[Toxaphene]], and [[Methyl Parathion]] — to determine whether combined exposure produced additive, synergistic, or antagonistic mortality effects relative to single-compound exposure.

## Background & Description
This paper is a toxicology/ecology study and does not describe detailed morphological traits of *Gambusia affinis*. Relevant biological notes include:
- **Species**: *Gambusia affinis*, a small live-bearing freshwater fish (family [[Poeciliidae]]) widely used as a bioindicator and mosquito-control species.
- **Population differentiation**: Two physiologically distinct populations were compared — a pesticide-**resistant** population (chronic exposure history) versus a **susceptible** population (no known pesticide exposure) — representing an example of rapid, localized [[Physiological Adaptation]] / [[Insecticide Resistance]] rather than gross morphological divergence.
- No standard length, weight, or anatomical measurements were reported; sample groups consisted of uniform batches of 50 individuals per test aquarium.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Mosquito fish were sourced from two contrasting freshwater habitats:
  - **Resistant population**: A drainage ditch bisecting cotton fields near Belzoni, Mississippi — a site subject to routine agrochemical drift and runoff.
  - **Susceptible population**: A pond on non-agricultural land near State College, Mississippi — considered a pesticide-free reference site.
- **Anthropogenic pressure**: Cotton fields in the Mississippi Delta received 10–15 insecticide applications per growing season (June 1–September 10), primarily via aerial application. Spray drift and agricultural runoff introduce endrin, DDT, toxaphene, and methyl parathion into adjacent waterways, exerting strong selective pressure on aquatic non-target species.
- **Trophic/ecosystem role**: *Gambusia affinis* functions as a key mosquito-larvae predator in these freshwater systems, linking [[Insecticide Runoff]] exposure directly to [[Mosquito Control]] ecosystem services.
- **Water chemistry of test medium**: Tapwater used for dilution had pH 7.8 and hardness of 28 ppm; test temperature maintained at 72 ± 4°F.
- **Evolutionary context**: The existence of a highly resistant wild population illustrates in situ selection for pesticide tolerance, linking this study to broader findings on resistance in [[Gambusia Affinis]] and other fish species in heavily sprayed agricultural landscapes (see cited works by Vinson, Boyd, and Ferguson, 1963–1964).

## Key Findings
- **No additive toxicity observed**: In all paired insecticide combinations tested (endrin-DDT, endrin-toxaphene, endrin-methyl parathion, DDT-toxaphene, DDT-methyl parathion, toxaphene-methyl parathion), the mortality from the **combination never exceeded the sum of mortalities from the individual insecticides** — ruling out simple additive toxicological effects.
- **Differential resistance profile**:
  - Endrin and toxaphene were **relatively more toxic to susceptible fish**.
  - DDT and methyl parathion were **relatively more toxic to resistant fish**.
  - This reversal pattern held across nearly all combinations except the DDT–methyl parathion pairing.
- **Concentration disparity**: Because resistant fish tolerated much higher concentrations, tests required substantially elevated insecticide doses (especially for endrin and toxaphene) to produce comparable mortality curves relative to susceptible fish.
- **Combination effect stronger in resistant fish**: For resistant fish, insecticide combinations produced markedly higher mortality than either compound alone; in susceptible fish, the combination barely exceeded the mortality caused by toxaphene or endrin individually.
- **Methyl parathion decay artifact**: The mortality curve for methyl parathion consistently showed a deflection after 12 hours, attributed to **rapid hydrolysis** of the compound reducing its bioavailability/toxicity over time.
- **Jar test corroboration**: Independent jar bioassays confirmed the aquarium test results — combined insecticide treatments consistently produced mortality **less than the sum** of individual compound mortalities (no additive interaction), though the mixture often approached the total single-compound mortality in resistant-fish trials (endrin-toxaphene, endrin-methyl parathion, toxaphene-methyl parathion).
- **Mechanistic interpretation**: Elevated (but sub-additive) mortality from combined exposures is proposed to result from **differences in the modes of action** of the insecticide toxicants rather than simple concentration summation, suggesting complex interactive toxicodynamics relevant to [[Pesticide Interaction]] research.
- **Control mortality**: Negligible baseline mortality (≤1/50 fish) in tapwater controls validated the reliability of the aquarium bioassay methodology.
