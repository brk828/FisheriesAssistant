# Characters and Distribution of the Atlantic Coast Fishes Referred to the Genus Hypsoblennius

#ichthyology #marine-biology #blenniidae

## Executive Summary

This 1939 reprint by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] revises the separation and geographic ranges of the two eastern North American blennies then placed in the genus [[Hypsoblennius]]: [[Hypsoblennius Hentz]] and [[Hypsoblennius Ionthas]]. Hubbs argues that the dichotomous key in [[Jordan and Evermann]] (1898) relies on characters that are not useful, because supraorbital cirrus differences are obscured by sexual dimorphism and individual variation. He instead proposes several more reliable morphological distinctions and substantially expands the known range of *H. hentz*, which was previously shortened in manuals and checklists. The paper closes with a catalogued list of specimens examined in the [[United States National Museum]] and the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]].

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic context:** The genus was treated in [[Jordan and Evermann]] (1898), whose synonymy and descriptions were judged largely correct, although their key was considered unsatisfactory.
- **Key diagnostic differences** (summarized from Table I, comparing the two species):
  - **Upper lip:** *H. hentz* has an almost linear lip, widely separated by a smooth area; *H. ionthas* has a semicircular lip separated by a broad, shield-shaped lobe.
  - **Gill membrane:** thin in *H. hentz*; thick in *H. ionthas*. Both extend downward in profile, but *H. hentz* extends slightly forward and *H. ionthas* slightly backward.
  - **Fold across isthmus:** a slight fold is present in *H. hentz* and absent in *H. ionthas*.
  - **Supraorbital cirrus:** longer in *H. hentz* (length contained less than 3.5 times in standard length) and shorter in *H. ionthas* (more than 3.5 times).
  - **Anal spines in breeding males:** in *H. hentz* the first spine is shield-shaped and the second is baglike; in *H. ionthas* both spines are lanceolate.
  - **Head markings:** *H. hentz* has an angled light bar behind and below the eye, usually indistinct, and conspicuous dark spots over head and trunk in males. *H. ionthas* usually has a conspicuous light bar, with dark spots absent or scarcely developed except in the suborbital region in males.
  - **Size:** the largest *H. hentz* examined measured 104 mm standard length; the largest *H. ionthas* measured 66 mm.
- **Note on anal spines:** In [[Chasmodes]] and [[Hypleurochilus]], padlike anal spines bear conspicuous longitudinal striae, a feature absent in *Hypsoblennius*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range of *H. hentz*:** Previously restricted in manuals to the coasts of North and South Carolina south to Indian River, Florida. Hubbs documents a much wider range from New Jersey and Chesapeake Bay south to Yucatan, including valid records from Cape Charles, Virginia, Corpus Christi, Texas, Cameron, Louisiana, and Champotén, Campeche, Mexico. A Gulf of Mexico record (Goode and Bean, 1882) could not be confirmed.
- **Range of *H. ionthas*:** Limited, as then known, to the coastline from South Carolina to Texas.
- **Habitats noted in specimen records:** Oyster beds (Rockport, Texas; Matanzas River, Florida), reef areas (Palacios Reef, Matagorda Bay; Sand Point Reef, Lavaca Bay), creek mouths and bays (Hampton Creek, Little Bay, Lemon Bay, Sawmill Creek), and Chesapeake Bay light stations.
- **Sampling context:** Material came largely from U.S. Fish Hawk stations, Schroeder's Chesapeake Bay collections, and Michigan collections by Albert Collier, Gordon Gunter, and Stewart Springer.

## Key Findings

- The two eastern North American species are readily separable by several characters, most of which had previously been overlooked. The key characters in earlier literature (especially supraorbital cirrus length) are unreliable due to sexual dimorphism and individual variation.
- *Hypsoblennius hentz* has a far wider distribution than generally assumed, ranging from New Jersey and Chesapeake Bay to Yucatan.
- *Hypsoblennius ionthas* is restricted to the Atlantic and Gulf coast from South Carolina to Texas.
- Hubbs notes that some differences between the two species are significant enough to question their generic unity, but deferred that decision until the whole group is reviewed.
- The museum catalogue lists about 20 U.S. National Museum lots and about 10 Michigan lots for *H. hentz*, and about 12 National Museum lots and 5 Michigan lots for *H. ionthas*, with specimen counts ranging from one to 18 per lot. Records include the earliest material from 1880 and 1886 and collections extending into 1936.
- Two records are considered doubtful: a West Indies specimen whose data are "hardly to be trusted," and an unconfirmed Gulf of Mexico record.
