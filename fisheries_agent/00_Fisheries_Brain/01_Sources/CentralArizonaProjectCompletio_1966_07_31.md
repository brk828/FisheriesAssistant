## Executive Summary

This completion report documents the fifth work segment (1965–1966) of the [[Central Arizona Project]] fish and wildlife investigations conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The report is primarily administrative and socio-ecological rather than taxonomic, tracking (1) the legislative progress of the Colorado River Basin Bill (HR 4671) in the U.S. Congress, which would authorize the Central Arizona Project and its associated water allocations for fish and wildlife, and (2) visitor-use surveys at five Arizona reservoirs — [[Bartlett Lake]], [[Parker Canyon Lake]], [[Pena Blanca Lake]], [[Black Canyon Lake]], and [[Lyman Lake]] — conducted to guide future reservoir-based sport fisheries development. No specific fish species, morphological data, or population data are presented; the focus is on reservoir recreational fisheries planning tied to proposed dam and aqueduct construction (e.g., [[Bridge Canyon Dam]], [[Marble Canyon Dam]], [[Orme Dam]], [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]], [[Salt-Gila Aqueduct]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Artificial reservoirs ("fishing lakes") constructed or planned along the [[Mogollon Rim]] and in southeastern Arizona, fed by Colorado River water transferred via the Central Arizona Project.
- **Water allocation for fish/wildlife:** The Arizona Game and Fish Department requested **84,000 acre-feet** of mainstream [[Colorado River]] water annually (from Arizona's 2.8 million acre-foot allotment) be reserved for fish and wildlife purposes:
  - 57,000 acre-feet consumptive use
    - 17,000 acre-feet for fish and wildlife facilities along the Lower Colorado River
    - 40,000 acre-feet for constructing fishing lakes along the Mogollon Rim and southeastern Arizona
  - 27,000 acre-feet returned to the system
- **Habitat creation via infrastructure:** Reservoirs behind proposed [[Bridge Canyon Dam]] and [[Marble Canyon Dam]] were identified as having major sport-fishing habitat potential; proposed aqueducts ([[Granite Reef Aqueduct]], [[Salt-Gila Aqueduct]]) were expected to provide secondary fishing opportunity and wildlife watering habitat in desert terrain.
- **Conservation/land-use conflict:** Proposed expansion of [[Grand Canyon National Park]] boundaries (HR 14176) to include the [[North Kaibab National Forest]] was opposed by the Department because reclassification would eliminate deer hunting, disrupting a then-successful deer population management program — illustrating the interplay between game/fish management and competing conservation land designations.
- **Regulatory framework:** Reservoir/recreation management tied to the [[Federal Water Projects Recreation Act]] (P.L. 89-72) and the [[Land and Water Conservation Fund Act]] (P.L. 88-578), which allow non-federal agencies (like Arizona Game and Fish) to administer recreational aspects of federal water projects by covering half of separable recreation development costs.

## Key Findings

- **Legislative status:** By the end of the reporting segment, the Colorado River Basin Bill (HR 4671), which encompasses the Central Arizona Project, had cleared the House Committee on Interior and Insular Affairs and awaited scheduling by the House Rules Committee. Passage was delayed by controversies over (a) Columbia River water importation into the Colorado River Basin, (b) construction of Bridge and Marble Canyon Dams, and (c) proposed Grand Canyon National Park boundary expansion (HR 14176).
- **Letter of intent:** In February 1966, the Arizona Game and Fish Department submitted a formal "letter of intent" to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City, NV) to administer fish and wildlife aspects of the Central Arizona Project under P.L. 89-72.
- **Visitor-use survey results (June 1–September 15, 1965)** at five reservoirs, using a roadside-check interview method (a competing "spot check" method was abandoned as inferior):
  - **Bartlett Lake** and **Lyman Lake** received the highest total visitor numbers.
  - **Bartlett Lake** and **Black Canyon Lake** had the highest proportion of visitors engaged primarily in fishing.
  - **Pena Blanca Lake** had the highest recreational use per surface acre among the five lakes.
- **Estimated recreational value per surface acre** (per Ad Hoc Water Resources Council 1964 Evaluation Standards):
  | Lake | Value/Surface Acre |
  |---|---|
  | [[Pena Blanca Lake]] | $747 |
  | [[Black Canyon Lake]] | $252 |
  | [[Lyman Lake]] | $184 |
  | [[Parker Canyon Lake]] | $115 |
  | [[Bartlett Lake]] | $53 |
- **Planning implications:** Visitor-use data are intended to support benefit-cost justification for constructing additional reservoirs and to guide fish and wildlife facility planning under the Central Arizona Project, contingent on Congressional authorization.
- **Future work:** Surveys planned to continue in summer 1966 at the same five lakes, with new surveys initiated at [[Riggs Flat Lake]] and [[Lee Valley Lake]], and additional winter surveys at lower-elevation lakes as staffing permits.
