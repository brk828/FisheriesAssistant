# Sampling at ASU Research Park, April 1989

#ichthyology #native-fish-conservation #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This report documents fish sampling conducted 14-17 April 1989 at [[ASU Research Park]] lakes in Tempe, Arizona, to assess the status of native fishes. Sampling employed trammel nets (12-hour overnight sets) and seining, with supplementary larval sampling initiated in February 1989. The survey captured 108 individuals across eight taxa via trammel netting, dominated by [[Bluegill]] (50%) and [[Largemouth Bass]] (16%). Native species including [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), and [[Gila Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*) were documented. Despite evidence of spawning condition in bonytail and razorback suckers, no larvae of these endangered species were collected. Larval sampling primarily captured [[Threadfin Shad]] and unidentified cyprinids.

## Background & Description

The [[ASU Research Park]] lakes serve as a research facility for native fish conservation in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The facility maintains populations of endangered native cyprinids and catostomids alongside introduced sport fish and canal system invaders. Previous sampling in April 1988 established baseline data for comparison.

**Bonytail** (*Gila elegans*): Eight specimens captured, ranging 315-346 mm TL (mean 332 ± 11 mm), weighing 220-358 gm (mean 282 ± 44 gm). Compared to April 1988 (mean 248 mm, 153 gm), fish showed substantial growth. Both sexes collected (4 males, 2 females, 2 undetermined), including two ripe males. Males exhibited tuberculation and spawning coloration. Most fish infected with parasitic copepod *Lernaea cyprinacea*.

**Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): Nine specimens captured, 454 ± 41 mm TL (range 397-525 mm), weighing 932 ± 296 gm (range 290-1500 gm). Growth evident since April 1988 (mean 433 mm, 844 gm). One of four males ripe, another tuberculated. Three females showed no reproductive signs. Fish in excellent health with minimal parasite infection.

**Roundtail Chub** (*Gila robusta*): One ripe male captured. Species uncommon at ASURP but present in [[Salt River Project]] canal system. Known to hybridize with bonytail, complicating larval identification.

**Gila Sucker** (*Catostomus insignis*): Eleven specimens captured (10% of total). Common in SRP canals. Hybridization potential with razorback sucker noted, requiring careful scrutiny of larval Catostomus specimens.

**Colorado Squawfish** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): Not captured despite presence in facility. Young stocked from [[ASU Genetics Collection]]. An angler reported capturing "several" Colorado squawfish (estimated 22-30 cm TL) from Middle Lake. Report recommends experimental gill netting and angling with artificial baits for future sampling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The ASURP lakes exist within the broader [[Salt River Project]] canal system, which serves as both a source and sink for fish species. The facility experiences continuous invasion pressure from canal species including [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), various sunfish species, and tilapia. Unauthorized fish releases by anglers (e.g., [[Fathead Minnow]] as bait, [[Rainbow Trout]] reportedly from Black River) complicate species management.

Water temperature increased from 18.0°C (22 March) to 25.0°C (19 April), corresponding with onset of larval fish activity. Spawning observations by local anglers described bonytail aggregations of 6-10 fish in shallow water (< 1 m) near shore along Middle Lake margin, exhibiting bottom-oriented milling behavior and sediment disturbance consistent with known spawning activities.

Hybridization concerns exist between:
- [[Bonytail]] and [[Roundtail Chub]] (both *Gila* spp.)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Gila Sucker]] (both catostomids)

These sympatric congeners require careful morphological and genetic analysis of larvae for accurate species determination.

## Key Findings

**Trammel Net Results (Table 1):**
- Total catch: 108 individuals, 8 taxa
- Species composition: Bluegill 50%, Largemouth Bass 16%, Gila Sucker 10%, Bonytail 7%, Razorback Sucker 8%, Threadfin Shad 1%, Roundtail Chub 1%, Blue Tilapia 1%
- North Lake: 78 fish, 5 species
- Middle Lake: 23 fish, 7 species  
- South Lake: 7 fish, 4 species

**Seine Results (Table 2):**
- North Lake: 3,348 fish in 34 hauls (CPE 98.47), dominated by [[Red Shiner]] (78.9%) and Bluegill (18.2%)
- Middle Lake: 2 fish in 2 hauls (CPE 1.00), only Blue Tilapia captured
- South Lake: 44 fish in 21 hauls (CPE 2.10), dominated by Bluegill (47.7%) and Largemouth Bass (45.5%)
- First records: [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[Redear Sunfish]] at ASURP

**Larval Sampling Results:**
- First larvae collected 22 March 1989
- 22 March: Unidentified cyprinid (n=4, mean 2.40 mm TL), Threadfin Shad (n=13, mean 5.89 mm TL)
- 5 April: 284 Threadfin Shad in 60 min (mean 6.47 mm TL, n=100), plus two unidentified cyprinid taxa
- 12 April: 384 Threadfin Shad (mean 6.47 mm TL, n=100), 48 unidentified cyprinid larvae (mean 5.689 mm TL)
- 19 April: 10 Threadfin Shad (mean 10.93 mm TL), 18 unidentified cyprinid (mean 5.51 mm TL)
- **No bonytail or razorback sucker larvae detected despite spawning condition of adults**

**Conservation Implications:**
- Bonytail and razorback sucker populations show good growth and health
- Evidence of spawning readiness but no documented reproduction
- Possible explanations: (1) spawning occurred before larval sampling initiated in February, (2) larvae produced but not surviving, (3) spawning unsuccessful
- Comparison with [[Lower Colorado River]] reservoirs where razorback sucker spawning documented suggests ASURP conditions may support reproduction
- Hybridization risks require enhanced larval identification protocols
- Unauthorized fish introductions threaten native species management

**Methodological Recommendations:**
- Continue weekly larval sampling through spawning season
- Implement experimental gill netting for Colorado Squawfish
- Deploy angling with artificial baits
- Enhance larval rearing protocols for species identification
- Next comprehensive sampling scheduled for autumn 1989
