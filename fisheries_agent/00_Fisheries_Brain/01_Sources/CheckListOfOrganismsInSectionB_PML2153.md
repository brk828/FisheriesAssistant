# Check List of Organisms in Tenth Avenue Backwater

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This March 7, 1973 memorandum from the Department of Fish and Game (Assistant Fisheries Biologist Gary Ponder, to Fritz Walgenbach, Paul C. Marsh, and others at The Resources Agency) lists the organisms found in the [[Tenth Avenue Backwater]], locally called "Big Hole," and in the channels of Section 36. The memo gives a checklist of fish, mollusks, crustaceans, and insects. Each listed species was recorded from at least one specimen. The author states that the listed taxa make up at least 95% of the specimens taken, so the other organisms recorded were not worth separate attention.

## Background & Description

The memo is an internal correspondence and checklist, not a research report. It does not describe methods, sampling dates, sample sizes, or abundance counts. Taxa are grouped by major category, and the scientific names are partly garbled in the source scan. The fish list includes both native-range sportfish and introduced or pest-control species such as [[Mosquitofish]]. The invertebrate lists include [[Asian Clam]] and [[Mosquito]] larvae, which are often associated with disturbed or stagnant water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The backwater is a shallow, slow-moving or still water body connected to channels. The species assemblage suggests warm, low-flow conditions typical of Central Valley-type lowland waters. The fish group contains open-water planktivores such as [[Threadfin Shad]], which are prey for predatory centrarchids and catfishes. The group also includes littoral sunfish and basses that use vegetated or structured edges. The invertebrates include filter-feeding and grazing taxa (cladocerans, copepods, snails, clams) and predatory or detritus-feeding insects (water beetles, mayfly and stonefly nymphs). Together these groups point to a food web of plankton, benthic grazers, and vertebrate predators. The memo does not discuss water chemistry, temperature, or habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

**Fish (approximately 11 entries, two partly illegible in the scan):**
- [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*)
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*)
- [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*)
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus natalis*)
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)
- [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*)
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)
- [[Warmouth]] (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*)
- [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*)
- [[Black Crappie]]
- One sunfish entry, name unreadable in the scan

**Mollusks:**
- Lymnaeidae, a pond snail (*Lymnaea stagnalis*)
- Physidae, physid snails
- [[Asian Clam]] (*Corbicula fluminea*), family Corbiculidae

**Crustaceans:**
- [[Grass Shrimp]] (*Palaemonetes paludosus*), decapod
- Cladocerans: *Daphnia pulex*
- Copepods: *Cyclops bicuspidatus*
- One additional crustacean entry, name illegible in the scan

**Insects:**
- Plecoptera (stoneflies): *Capnia spinulosa*
- Ephemeroptera (mayflies): *Callibaetis californicus*
- Odonata (dragonflies and damselflies): listed as a group
- Coleoptera (water beetles): *Cybister explanatus*, a second *Cybister* species (name partly garbled), and *Thermonectus marmoratus*
- Diptera (flies): [[Mosquito]] (*Culex quinquefasciatus*)

**Summary statement:** The memo's author reports that the listed taxa together account for at least 95% of all specimens taken, and that the remaining organisms were minor and not worth further effort. No counts, densities, or per-taxon abundances are given.
