## Executive Summary
This document is an interim field protocol memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), coordinated with Arizona State University (ASU) and Grand Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES), outlining standardized methodology for hoopnet-based fish habitat sampling along the **[[Little Colorado River]]** during an October 15–22 field trip. Rather than reporting species-specific morphological or ecological data, the text specifies a sampling design intended to balance habitat representation ("positive" vs. "negative" net sets), standardize net pairing distances, and capture diel (24-hour) patterns of fish activity via scheduled net checks. The protocol reflects methodological continuation from a prior late-August sampling trip and aims to generate a dataset of approximately 50 net sets for **[[Habitat Use Assessment]]** of fish populations in the LCR, a critical tributary of the Colorado River system in **[[Grand Canyon]]**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **System**: **[[Little Colorado River]]**, a major spawning and rearing tributary within the **[[Colorado River]]** basin, Grand Canyon region — historically significant habitat for native fishes such as **[[Humpback Chub]]**.
- **Sampling approach reflects habitat heterogeneity**: Nets are deployed in "neighborhoods" (localized reaches) with paired sets sampling contrasting microhabitats — described as "positive" (fish-favorable) and "negative" (fish-unfavorable) net sets — to characterize **[[Habitat Selection]]** patterns.
- **Spatial design**: Paired or multiple net sets are placed within 100 m of each other to control for localized environmental variability while capturing habitat heterogeneity at a fine spatial scale.
- **Temporal/diel considerations**: The protocol explicitly incorporates a **[[Diel Activity Pattern]]** component for the first 24 hours of each deployment — nets set pre-10:00 AM, checked in early evening (5:00–6:30 PM), and rechecked the following morning (6:00–10:00 AM) — indicating an interest in day/night differences in fish catchability or habitat use, consistent with known crepuscular/nocturnal activity patterns in many riverine cyprinids and catostomids of the Colorado River system.
- **Field logistics**: Sampling teams were split between two base camps — Powell Camp and Salt Camp — suggesting spatial coverage of distinct LCR reaches with differing habitat/geomorphic characteristics.

## Key Findings
- Establishes a **standardized dual-purpose sampling framework**: approximately two-thirds of net sets deployed in paired/multiple "neighborhood" configurations (half of these as "negative" habitat controls), with the remaining one-third as solitary sets — designed to support robust **[[Habitat Use Assessment]]** comparisons.
- Introduces a **diel sampling protocol** for the initial 24-hour period of the trip only, to evaluate time-of-day effects on fish capture, after which standard ASU protocols resume.
- Consistent **mesh size** should be used within net pairs to avoid confounding gear selectivity with habitat effects.
- Precise recording of **net set/check times** is emphasized as essential for data quality and later diel analysis.
- Target sample size: **~50 net sets** over the October 15–22, 1993 field trip.
- This protocol builds directly on methodology first implemented during a **late August field trip**, indicating an iterative, multi-season monitoring program for LCR fish habitat use, likely tied to endangered native fish conservation efforts (e.g., **[[Humpback Chub]]** recovery monitoring) under GCES/USFWS collaboration.
