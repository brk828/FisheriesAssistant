## Executive Summary

This document is a 1997 interagency memorandum from the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Yuma Field Office to the Bureau of Reclamation, providing comments on the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Final Biological Opinion (BO) governing [[Lower Colorado River]] operations and maintenance from Lake Mead to the Southerly International Boundary. The memo is a regulatory/management document rather than a primary field study, and contains no direct morphological, taxonomic, or population data on specific fish species. Its scientific relevance lies in habitat-based conservation planning: it addresses riparian revegetation (cottonwood-willow galleries), backwater/off-channel habitat management for both native and non-native fish, and mitigation strategy prioritization (on-site/in-kind vs. off-site) within the Lower Colorado River ecosystem. The [[Imperial Division]] restoration project is explicitly identified as a dual-purpose effort to enhance both native and non-native fish populations, which the memo argues should be formally reconciled with the BO's restrictions on new sportfishing backwaters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although fish-specific ecological data are not detailed, the memo describes key habitat elements relevant to the aquatic and riparian ecosystem of the Lower Colorado River corridor:

- **Riparian vegetation structure**: Naturally regenerated stands of [[Fremont Cottonwood]] (*Populus fremontii*) and [[Goodding's Willow]] (*Salix gooddingii*) along the [[Gila River]] and the Colorado River below the Gila confluence, which established following the 1993 Gila River flood. These riparian corridors are tied to habitat requirements for the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), a federally listed species co-occurring with river/backwater fish habitat.
- **Backwater habitats**: The Biological Opinion restricts the design of "additional backwaters... for sportfishing," directly implicating shared aquatic habitat used by both listed native fish and recreational non-native fish populations.
- **Imperial Division**: Identified as an active restoration and enhancement planning effort intended to benefit both native and non-native fish assemblages in the Lower Colorado River, raising a management tension with the BO's sportfishing-backwater restriction.
- **Minimum habitat mitigation footprint**: 1,400 acres of riparian/floodplain habitat along the Gila River and lower Colorado River (below the Gila confluence) identified as available for protection under the Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA), supporting the broader river-floodplain ecosystem in which fish habitat is embedded.
- **Co-managed species context**: The memo also references the [[Flat-tailed Horned Lizard]] Rangewide Management Strategy, indicating that Lower Colorado River management operates within a multi-species conservation framework spanning aquatic and terrestrial desert taxa.

## Key Findings

- The BLM supports the overall scope of the USFWS Final [[Biological Opinion]] and the associated [[Reasonable and Prudent Alternative]] for Lower Colorado River operations, viewing it as beneficial to listed species and habitat.
- **Mitigation priority**: On-site, in-kind mitigation is favored over off-site mitigation; this principle is recommended for extension to all species covered under the BO, implicitly including native fish species.
- **Habitat preservation over replanting**: Existing naturally-regenerated cottonwood-willow stands (post-1993 Gila River flood) along the Gila River and lower Colorado River are recommended for protection via managed river flows and supplemental water rather than costly conventional revegetation — an approach with direct implications for maintaining riparian-fish habitat linkages.
- **Policy inconsistency flagged**: The BO's statement that "no additional backwaters will be designed for sportfishing" is identified as contradictory to the accepted [[Imperial Division]] restoration project, which is explicitly designed to enhance both native and non-native fish. The memo recommends the BO clarify that the Imperial Division project is an accepted component of the RPA.
- **Interagency coordination**: BLM recommends continued Bureau of Reclamation participation in the Flat-tailed Horned Lizard Rangewide Management Strategy, underscoring the multi-species, cross-taxon nature of Lower Colorado River conservation planning that encompasses both aquatic (fish) and terrestrial listed species.
- **Data limitation**: No species-level fish population estimates, survey data, or biological metrics are included in this document; all fish-related content is administrative/habitat-policy in nature, centering on the [[Lower Colorado River]] and [[Imperial Division]] as focal management units.
