# Records of Three Cyprinid Fishes From The Tallapoosa River System

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae #notropis #hybognathus

## Executive Summary

This 1967 paper by James D. Williams reports on an intensive ichthyofaunal survey of the [[Tallapoosa River System]] (eastern-most drainage of the [[Mobile Basin]]) conducted between fall 1963 and winter 1965. Across 148 stations and 162 collections, 114 species and subspecies within 19 families were documented. Of particular scientific significance are three [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] species representing new distributional records: **[[Notropis uranoscopus]]** (Stargazing Shiner), previously known only from the [[Cahaba River]] drainage; **[[Notropis zonistius]]** (Bandfin Shiner), extending the known range from the Chattahoochee/Flint/Apalachicola/Savannah systems into the Tallapoosa; and **[[Hybognathus hayi]]** (Cypress Minnow), extending known Alabama records from the Escambia drainage into the lower Tallapoosa. The paper provides detailed meristic (scale and fin-ray) counts and habitat descriptions for each species, along with a detailed account of nuptial tuberculation in male *N. zonistius*.

## Background & Description

### Notropis uranoscopus (Stargazing Shiner)
- Based on meristic counts of 15 specimens:
  - Anal rays: 7 (consistent)
  - Caudal rays: 19 (one specimen with 17)
  - Pectoral rays: 13–15 (mode 14, mean 14.3)
  - Caudal peduncle scales: 5-2-5
  - Lateral line scales: 34–36 (mode 36, mean 35.2)
  - Predorsal scales: 13–15 (mode 14, mean 14.1)
  - Circumferential scales (before dorsal/pelvic fins): 20–23 (mode 22, mean 21.8)
- Counts were consistent with the original description by [[Suttkus 1959]].

### Notropis zonistius (Bandfin Shiner)
- Anal rays (n=100): range 8–10, mode 9, mean 9.2 — supports a north-south latitudinal gradient noted by [[Gilbert 1964]] (mode 10 in south, 9 in north populations)
- Pectoral rays (n=25): 13–16, mean 15.2
- Lateral line scales (n=25): 38–42, mean 39.5
- Scales above lateral line: 7–8, mean 7.6
- Scales below lateral line: 3–4, mean 3.8
- Predorsal scales: 15–17, mean 16.1
- Circumferential scales: 28–32, mean 29.3
- **Nuptial tuberculation (male, 61.1 mm SL, collected 5 July 1964):**
  - Well-developed tubercles on lower jaw and snout tip
  - Single tubercle row from upper jaw below nasal opening, around upper anterior orbit margin to upper posterior orbit margin
  - Scattered well-developed tubercles in interorbital and supratemporal regions
  - Granular tubercles on sides of head and gill membranes
  - Tubercles on posterior scale margins along lateral line and all scale rows above it
  - Tubercles on dorsal surface of pectoral fin, restricted to fin rays
  - Granular tubercles on dorsal and anal fins
  - **Females**: tubercles best developed only on lower jaw/snout tip; granular elsewhere; absent on fins and body scales
  - No significant morphological differences found between tuberculate specimens from the Tallapoosa and Chattahoochee systems

### Hybognathus hayi (Cypress Minnow)
- Based on 3 specimens:
  - Lateral line scales: 37 (one specimen), 38 (two specimens)
  - Predorsal scales: 14 (two specimens), 15 (one specimen)
  - Caudal peduncle scales: 7-2-5 (all three)
  - Dorsal and anal rays: 8 (all three)
  - Pectoral rays: 15 (one specimen), 14 (two specimens)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Notropis uranoscopus
- Habitat: Moderate-to-large streams and main river channel, below the Fall Line
- Substrate: Sand and gravel bottoms
- Water conditions: Moderate current, 2–3 feet deep, clear water, with some algae present on gravel
- Habitat matches that reported for the species in its type locality, the [[Cahaba River]]

### Notropis zonistius
- Habitat preference: Pools with sand or gravel bottom at the ends of riffles
- Also collected in midstream areas with moderate to swift current (e.g., Wedowee Creek)
- Possible mechanisms for presence in Tallapoosa system: natural stream capture or anthropogenic bait-bucket introduction

### Hybognathus hayi
- Habitat: Still, murky water over mud bottom in lower Tallapoosa tributaries
- Stream dimensions: 15–25 feet wide, 2–3 feet deep
- Some filamentous algae present at one collection site (Coleman Creek)
- Represents a lowland/Gulf Coastal Plain habitat specialist, consistent with known range in the lower Mississippi Valley and Gulf Coast drainages

## Key Findings

- **First record of *[[Notropis uranoscopus]]* outside the Cahaba River drainage**, documented at three localities below the Fall Line in the Tallapoosa system; habitat and meristic counts closely match the original Cahaba River description.
- **Range extension for *[[Notropis zonistius]]*** into the Tallapoosa River system (Alabama and Georgia), previously known from the Chattahoochee, Flint, Apalachicola, and Savannah systems; anal ray counts (mode 9) fit expected geographic cline described by Gilbert (1964).
- **Detailed sexual dimorphism in tuberculation documented** for *N. zonistius*, with males showing extensive body/fin tuberculation absent in females — a useful diagnostic and breeding-condition indicator.
- **New Tallapoosa system record for *[[Hybognathus hayi]]***, extending known Alabama distribution from the Escambia drainage; specimens collected from still, mud-bottomed lowland streams typical of the species' broader Gulf Coastal range.
- Overall survey establishes a baseline faunal inventory (114 species/subspecies, 19 families) for the [[Tallapoosa River System]], supporting future biogeographic and conservation assessments of the [[Mobile Basin]] ichthyofauna.
- Findings suggest the Tallapoosa system, though historically less studied than neighboring drainages, hosts species with affinities to both the Alabama/Cahaba faunal province and the Apalachicola/Chattahoochee faunal province, hinting at complex historical connectivity (e.g., stream capture events) among Southeastern U.S. river systems.
