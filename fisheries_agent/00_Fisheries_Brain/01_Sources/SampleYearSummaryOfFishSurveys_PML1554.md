# Sample Year 2010 Summary of Fish Surveys in Arizona Canals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes Sample Year (SY) 2010 fish surveys in four canal systems of the [[Gila River Basin]], Arizona: the [[Salt River Project]] Arizona (North) Canal (SRPN), the SRP South Canal (SRPS), the [[San Carlos Irrigation Project]] Florence-Casa Grande (FCG) Canal, and the [[Central Arizona Project Canal]] (CAP). Sampling was conducted by [[Marsh & Associates]] (M & A) with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Reclamation) under a long-term monitoring plan, between 12 July 2010 and 24 January 2011. The monitoring has two goals: detecting new taxa and assessing community status through species richness, relative abundance and distribution.

A total of 20 taxa was captured. No species new to the Gila River basin was found, but [[Desert Sucker]] was recorded for the first time from the Pima Lateral Canal. Canal sampling is opportunistic and typically depends on dewatering outages, so the results are largely qualitative and not directly comparable among sites.

## Background & Description

Canal surveys follow protocols described in Clarkson (1995/1996). Sampling is opportunistic, usually during low-flow or "dry-up" conditions. Only CAP canal stations are fixed. Survey methods differed by system:

- **SRP canals above the [[Electrical Fish Barrier]]:** repeated seining, which captures nearly all fishes in these short, confined reaches.
- **Below the barriers (SRP and FCG):** seines, dip nets, backpack [[Electrofishing]] and entanglement gear, used during flow outages.
- **CAP Canal (at pumping plant forebays):** [[Trammel Netting]], minnow traps, boat-mounted electrofishing and trot lines.

Recent changes in SRP and CAP operations have reduced the frequency and spatial extent of outages. Construction at FCG may also alter service schedules. As a result, sampling sometimes had to occur during low-flow, bank-full conditions, which the authors describe as less effective. Sampling during normal operational flows was judged inefficient and impractical.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Community structure:** SRP and FCG canal systems held 17 fish species in total. Native [[Roundtail Chub]] is typically found in SRP canals but was absent in 2010.
- **Changes since SY 2009:** Goldfish, yellow bullhead and black crappie, recorded in SY 2008, were not found in 2009 or 2010. Two cichlids, [[Blue Tilapia]] and [[Mozambique Mouthbrooder]], appeared in 2010 but not 2009. [[Red Shiner]] was taken in 2010 but not 2009, while [[Green Sunfish]] was found in 2009 only. [[Redear Sunfish]] was stocked by SRP for mollusk control in 2007 and was not encountered in SY 2009 or 2010 at the SRPN Indian Bend site.
- **Native fishes:** [[Sonora Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]] were the only native species captured in the SRP canals. Sonora sucker was common in SRPN and SRPS, while desert sucker was in both SRP canals and in one FCG specimen.
- **Crustaceans:** Native [[Freshwater Shrimp]] (*Palaemonetes paludosus*) was collected in the CAP Canal for the first time by these authors. [[Northern Crayfish]] (*Orconectes virilis*), a non-native species, occurred in all three canal systems.
- **CAP Canal habitat:** The authors describe the canal as inhospitable to most fishes because of high current velocity, lack of structure, and apparently low primary and secondary production.
- **Non-native dominance:** Most taxa were non-native. The authors report 16 non-native taxa from SRPN, 13 from SRPS and six from FCG.

## Key Findings

### Salt River Project Arizona (North) Canal
- **Reach 1 Site 1, above the electrical barrier (24 Jan 2011):** 727 individuals of 12 species in six hauls. Channel catfish was predominant. Sonora sucker was second, and largemouth bass (about 13%) and flathead catfish (about 9%) were also notable. The remaining six species each made up 2% or less.
- **Reach 2 Site 1, Indian Bend Siphon:** 182 individuals of 13 species (one native, as stated in the text) by boat electrofishing and two trammel nets. Red shiner was most abundant (139 individuals, about 78%). Redear sunfish was not encountered.
- **Totals:** Table 1 gives 913 individuals across both sites.
- The canal was largely dewatered; discharge at [[Granite Reef Diversion Dam]] was reported as 0 cfs when sampled.

### Salt River Project South Canal
- **Taxa:** 14 taxa (two native) were captured or observed.
- **Reach 1 Site 1 (22 Nov 2010):** 278 individuals of 12 taxa in 5 bag seine and 5 straight seine hauls. Sonora sucker was predominant (age-1 about 35%). Channel catfish (23%) and largemouth bass (14%) followed.
- **Reach 2:** Sonora sucker and channel catfish were collected at the barrier, and five adult largemouth bass at River Road Siphon. Dip netting in the dewatered Tempe Canal at "Triple Junction" captured 79 individuals of 8 species, most of them age-0 fish. Only large adult grass carp were seen between the dam and Lehi Road.

### San Carlos Irrigation Project Florence-Casa Grande Canal
- **Taxa:** One native and six non-native species were captured.
- **Total catch:** Reported as 156 fish above the barrier and 1,392 downstream. The overall total is printed in garbled form in the source; the components sum to 1,548.
- **Pima Lateral Canal:** One age-1+ desert sucker (about 10 cm TL) was captured. This is the first monitoring record of desert sucker in the canal, and the specimen was kept as a voucher. The Pima Lateral Feeder Canal was drained and contained no fish.
- **Reach 1 Site 1 (below Ashurst-Hayden Diversion Dam):** 160 individuals of five species. Channel catfish, mostly age-0, made up 78%. Mosquitofish (11%) and red shiner (9%) followed.
- **Reach 2 Site 1 (China Wash barrier):** 122 individuals of four species. Red shiner was 43% and mosquitofish 34%.
- **Reach 2 Site 3 (Pima Lateral Turnout):** 1,266 individuals of three species, including 1,264 age-0 channel catfish (about 99.8%).

### Central Arizona Project Canal
- **Sampling:** Reach 1 (12–15 July 2010), Reach 2 (28–29 Oct 2010) and Reach 3 (17–20 Oct 2010). Sampling is possible only when flow is reduced to below about 500 cfs.
- **Taxa:** Nine non-native species were collected, with four to six per site. Bouse, the upstream-most site, had the highest richness (six taxa). No species occurred at all seven sites.
- **Totals:** The text gives 154 fish, but the site totals sum to 164 (Bouse 39, Little Harquahala 7, Hassayampa 24, Salt-Gila 20, Brady 32, Redrock 17, San Xavier 25). Most fish (83%) were age-1+.
- **Dominant species:** [[Striped Bass]] (19% of catch), bluegill (14%), common carp, grass carp and channel catfish (13% each), largemouth bass (11%) and redear sunfish (10%).
- **Notable records:**
  - [[Smallmouth Bass]] was rare. The single specimen at Bouse was the first age-0 individual recorded in the CAP Canal.
  - At Brady (Reach 3), 15 age-0 striped bass (47% of the site catch) were captured. The origin is unknown. The authors list possibilities including in-canal reproduction, Lake Havasu and Lake Pleasant.
  - At Salt-Gila, native freshwater shrimp was collected for the first time in the canal.
- **Site-specific counts:** Bouse had 17 bluegill (44%); Redrock had 10 largemouth bass (59%); San Xavier had 17 redear sunfish (68%).

### Deviations and Recommendations
- **Deviations:** Leaking radial gates at FCG (1–2 cfs) made sampling less efficient near China Wash. SRPN was dewatered only above the barrier. Coordination with SCIP and SRP was generally good.
- **Recommendations:**
  1. Salvage and release any state-listed Roundtail Chub encountered during SRP monitoring, by moving them above Granite Reef Diversion Dam.
  2. Continue monitoring in years without dry-ups using boat-mounted equipment and hoop or entanglement nets when flows are safe to work.
  3. Maintain open communication between canal operators (SRP, SCIP, CAWCD) and monitoring crews so sampling can coincide with outages.
  4. Arrange keys to canal gate locks with Reclamation, or issue a key to the contractor.
