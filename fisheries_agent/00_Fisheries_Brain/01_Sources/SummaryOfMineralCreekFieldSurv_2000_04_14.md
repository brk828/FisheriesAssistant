## Executive Summary
This report summarizes a single-day electrofishing survey (April 14, 2000) of [[Mineral Creek]], a tributary to the [[Gila River]] in Pinal County, Arizona, conducted by Arizona Game and Fish Department personnel. The survey covered nearly the entire stream reach from Big Box Dam upstream to the confluence with Tillmans Wash. Three fish species were documented: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*, a non-native species). Species distribution was strongly correlated with surface flow availability and pool habitat, with chub and sunfish restricted to perennial pool-rich reaches upstream of a dry channel segment. Voucher specimens and genetic samples were collected to support ongoing taxonomic work distinguishing *Gila intermedia* from the sympatric [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and newly described [[Headwater Chub]] (*Gila nigra*).

## Background & Description
- **Longfin Dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*)**: Small cyprinid; tuberculate adult males observed during survey (breeding condition indicator). Some individuals infected with black grub parasites (metacercariae, likely trematode).
- **Green Sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*)**: Non-native centrarchid; several vouchered specimens approached 240 mm total length, indicating a well-established, mature population.
- **Gila Chub (*Gila intermedia*)**: Species identification was based on a morphometric ratio — **head length divided by least caudal peduncle depth**:
  - Ratio **< 3.0** → *Gila intermedia* ([[Gila Chub]])
  - Ratio **3.0–3.3** → intermediate form / *Gila nigra* ([[Headwater Chub]])
  - Ratio **> 3.3** → *Gila robusta* ([[Roundtail Chub]])
- Sixteen chub specimens were preserved as voucher material; all sixteen were confirmed as *Gila intermedia* by Dr. W.L. Minckley (ASU).
- Ten fin-clipped chub specimens ranged **130–223 mm total length**, averaging **150 mm TL**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Stream System**: [[Mineral Creek]] flows through a narrowing bedrock/boulder canyon with deep plunge and lateral scour pools (largest recorded: ~6 m × 3 m × >2 m deep), interspersed with intermittent dry and low-flow reaches.
- **Flow Gradient**: Surface flow near Big Box Dam was minimal, sustained largely by inflow from [[Devils Canyon]]. A ~¼ mile reach above Devils Canyon was completely dry, consistent with channel downcutting and sand/gravel/cobble substrate instability.
- **Species Zonation**:
  - *Agosia chrysogaster* was distributed throughout the entire surveyed reach but was **most abundant in the lower half** near perennial flow from Devils Canyon.
  - *Gila intermedia* and *Lepomis cyanellus* were **absent from the lower, drier reaches** and first appeared near the Section 26/35 boundary (~¼ mile above Big Box Dam), becoming abundant in the deeper pools of the narrow upper canyon.
- **Riparian Habitat**: Mature gallery forest of cottonwood, willow, and sycamore; additional woody cover from ash, mesquite, and seep willow. Deer grass dominated understory in the narrow canyon reach. Cattle grazing sign was noted despite dense riparian vegetation and rocky substrate.
- **Co-occurring Vertebrates**: [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (*Rana yavapaiensis*), [[Sonora Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense*), Sonoran whipsnake (*Masticophis bilineatus*), and ornate tree lizard (*Urosaurus ornatus*) were observed, indicating a diverse riparian/aquatic vertebrate assemblage.

## Key Findings
- Only **three fish species** were detected in the entire surveyed reach: *Agosia chrysogaster* (native), *Gila intermedia* (native, conservation concern), and *Lepomis cyanellus* (non-native, invasive).
- Presence of an established **non-native Green Sunfish** population (individuals up to 240 mm) in the same pools as native Gila Chub raises predation/competition concerns for this imperiled native cyprinid.
- **Habitat-driven zonation**: dry/intermittent lower reaches supported only longfin dace, while perennial pool habitat in the narrow canyon upstream supported the full three-species assemblage, underscoring the importance of permanent pool habitat for chub and sunfish persistence.
- Parasitism (black grub) observed in longfin dace below the Devils Canyon confluence.
- Sixty-six longfin dace were preserved for **tissue contaminant analysis** by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, suggesting concurrent water/sediment contamination concerns possibly linked to nearby ASARCO Ray Mine Complex operations.
- Genetic samples (fin clips) from ten Gila Chub were provided to Dr. Mike Douglas (ASU) to support ongoing systematics/genetics research distinguishing *Gila intermedia*, *Gila nigra*, and *Gila robusta*.
- Morphometric identification (head length : caudal peduncle depth ratio) combined with expert taxonomic confirmation (Dr. W.L. Minckley) affirmed all 16 chub vouchers as *Gila intermedia*, with no *Gila nigra* or *Gila robusta* detected in this survey.
