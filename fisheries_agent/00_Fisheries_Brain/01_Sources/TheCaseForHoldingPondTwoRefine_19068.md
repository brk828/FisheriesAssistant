# The Case for Holding Ponds: Refinery Wastewater Treatment Effectiveness

#ichthyology #environmental-toxicology #Cyprinidae #water-quality #industrial-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study evaluated the effectiveness of holding pond systems for treating refinery wastewater at two facilities operated by Apco Oil Corp. in [[Arkansas City]], Kansas and [[Cyril]], Oklahoma. Using [[Fathead Minnow]] ([[Pimephales promelas]]) bioassays and chemical analyses, researchers demonstrated that approximately 40 days of retention time reduced most toxic components to minimum levels. The study documented systematic reductions in pH, alkalinity, ammonia nitrogen (80-90%), phenols (>90%), sulfides (>90%), biochemical oxygen demand (BOD, ~80%), and chemical oxygen demand (COD, >50%). After 25 days, effluents were non-toxic to test fish in 48-hour bioassays. The research provides quantitative evidence for holding ponds as an effective wastewater treatment method for petroleum refineries.

## Background & Description

The [[Arkansas City]] refinery processed 20,000 barrels per day using atmospheric and vacuum distillation, catalytic cracking, HF alkylation, propane deasphalting, and catalytic reforming. The [[Cyril]] refinery processed 10,000 barrels per day with crude distillation, vacuum distillation, catalytic cracking, polymerization, and catalytic reforming.

Raw waste characteristics varied between facilities. At Arkansas City (after 1.9 days holding): average flow 781,000 gallons/day, pH 9.1, total alkalinity 255 ppm, ammonia nitrogen 25 ppm, phenols 3.0 ppm, sulfides 1.0 ppm, COD 284 ppm, BOD 70 ppm, oil 20 ppm, total dissolved solids 2,232 ppm, median tolerance limit (TLm) 21.0%. At Cyril: average flow 216,000 gallons/day, pH 9.7, total alkalinity 243 ppm, ammonia nitrogen 51 ppm, phenols 1.2 ppm, sulfides 1.7 ppm, COD 426 ppm, TLm 10.4%.

The [[Arkansas City]] system consisted of three oil-settling ponds (~14 ft deep, 1.86 days retention) followed by four oxidation ponds (~5 ft deep) with spray aeration, totaling 60.36 days retention. The [[Cyril]] system included three oil-settling ponds (9 days retention) and six oxidation ponds with underwater pipe connections and flume aeration between ponds, totaling 36.90 days retention.

[[Fathead Minnow]] were selected as the test organism for toxicity bioassays using 48-hour exposure periods. The median tolerance limit (TLm) was defined as the effluent concentration at which exactly 50% of test fish survived.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study identified three distinct patterns of chemical reduction correlated with dissolved oxygen conditions. In anaerobic phases (first 9-16 days), no free oxygen was present, and sharp reductions occurred in phenols, sulfides, and some other components. The deeper ponds at [[Arkansas City]] (14 ft vs 5-6 ft at [[Cyril]]) showed delayed oxygen distribution and slower initial reduction rates.

Aerobic phases began after oxygen became available through atmospheric diffusion and algal photosynthesis. Blue-green algae played a dual role: fixing atmospheric nitrogen as ammonia and producing oxygen through photosynthesis. Algal activity caused pH increases and shifts in alkalinity ratios in later pond stages. The study suggested that algae maintained ammonia nitrogen at approximately 5 ppm, representing a minimum concentration below which ponding treatment could not further reduce this parameter.

Spray aeration at [[Arkansas City]] (after 16 days retention) sharply accelerated reduction of several components by enhancing oxygen transfer. The shallow pond design at [[Cyril]] allowed more effective oxygen distribution from atmospheric sources.

Seasonal differences were documented, with winter conditions showing reduced biological activity and lower 5-day BOD values after 25 days, attributed to smaller biological populations in colder temperatures.

## Key Findings

**Optimal Retention Time**: Approximately 40 days holding time reduced nearly all components to minimum levels, though many components achieved maximum reduction in less than 40 days.

**pH and Alkalinity**: Peak reduction occurred at about 25 days. The phenolphthalein/total alkalinity ratio declined initially (normal stabilization), followed by increases due to algal photosynthesis withdrawing CO₂ from bicarbonates. Regression at [[Cyril]] was -0.06 per day.

**Ammonia Nitrogen**: Reduced at uniform rate of -0.57 ppm/day (combined summer data) for 37 days, removing 80-90% of initial concentration. After 37 days, concentration stabilized at ~5 ppm, apparently maintained by nitrogen-fixing blue-green algae. This represents the minimum achievable through ponding treatment.

**Phenols**: Reduced >90% in approximately 5 weeks. Greatest reduction occurred during anaerobic phase (85% at [[Cyril]], 50% at [[Arkansas City]] by end of anaerobic period). Aerobic phase regression rates: -0.058 ppm/day ([[Arkansas City]] summer), -0.040 ppm/day ([[Arkansas City]] winter), -0.039 ppm/day ([[Cyril]] summer). Reduction was practically complete at 60 days under summer conditions.

**Sulfides**: At [[Cyril]], reduced >90% following same pattern as phenols. Aerobic phase regression was -0.070 ppm/day, approximately twice the phenol reduction rate.

**Toxicity**: After approximately 25 days (3.5 weeks), effluents were non-toxic to [[Fathead Minnow]] in 48-hour bioassays—fish survived in 100% effluent concentration. Toxicity reduction showed uniform regression rates: +3.70% per day ([[Cyril]] summer), +2.05% per day ([[Arkansas City]] summer), +2.87% per day ([[Arkansas City]] winter). Delayed reduction at [[Arkansas City]] correlated with extended anaerobic phase.

**Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)**: Reduced slightly more than 50% in approximately 5 weeks. [[Cyril]] showed higher initial COD (426 ppm vs 284 ppm) and greater regression rate (-5.44 ppm/day vs -2.12 ppm/day summer at [[Arkansas City]]). No further change occurred between 37 and 60 days at [[Arkansas City]].

**Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)**: Reduced approximately 80% in 25 days under both summer and winter conditions at [[Arkansas City]]. Regression rate: -1.98 ppm/day (summer), -1.263 ppm/day (winter). Five-day BOD was practically satisfied by 25 days. Residual BOD after 25 days likely resulted from decomposition of algae and other organisms, supported by lower winter values when biological populations were smaller.

**Oxygen Production**: Regression rates varied: -2.59 lb/acre/day ([[Arkansas City]] summer, days 16-37), +0.46 lb/acre/day ([[Arkansas City]] winter, days 16-60), -1.76 lb/acre/day ([[Cyril]], days 22-37), +1.27 lb/acre/day (winter, days 23-37).

**Design Factors**: Shallow ponds (5 ft) at [[Cyril]] showed more effective early reduction than deeper ponds (14 ft) at [[Arkansas City]] due to better oxygen distribution. Spray aeration at [[Arkansas City]] (after 16 days) sharply accelerated component reduction. Underwater pipes and flumes between ponds at [[Cyril]] provided additional aeration.

**Seasonal Effects**: Winter conditions showed reduced biological activity but maintained effective BOD reduction. Phenol reduction was slower in winter aerobic phase (-0.04 ppm/day vs -0.06 ppm/day summer).
