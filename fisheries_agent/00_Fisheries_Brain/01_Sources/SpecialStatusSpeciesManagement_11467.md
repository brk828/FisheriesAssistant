# BLM Manual 6840 - Special Status Species Management

#conservation-policy #endangered-species-act #habitat-management #federal-land-management

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Manual Section 6840, dated September 16, 1988, establishes comprehensive policy and guidance for the conservation of Special Status Species (plants and animals) and their habitats on BLM-administered lands. Special Status Species include those proposed for listing, officially listed as threatened or endangered (T/E) under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA), candidates for listing, State-listed species, and BLM-designated sensitive species. The manual implements Section 7 of the ESA, requiring BLM to ensure that authorized actions do not jeopardize listed species, destroy [[Critical Habitat]], or contribute to the need for additional listings. It establishes consultation procedures with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NMFS), defines responsibilities from Director to Area Manager level, and mandates conservation programs aimed at species recovery rather than mere survival.

## Background & Description

The manual supersedes Release 6-58 and provides the regulatory framework for BLM's implementation of the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.), which became law December 28, 1973. The ESA represented the first comprehensive Federal effort to conserve plants and wildlife, marking a significant departure from traditional state authority over native species. The Act applies to species listed as endangered or threatened by the Secretary of the Interior, with implementation responsibility assigned to Federal agencies.

**Special Status Species Categories:**

1. **Proposed Species**: Officially proposed for listing via Federal Register publication
2. **Threatened/Endangered (T/E) Species**: Officially listed under ESA
3. **Candidate Species**: Categories 1 and 2 species under consideration for listing
4. **State Listed Species**: Species listed by states as potentially endangered or extinct
5. **Sensitive Species**: Designated by State Directors, typically in cooperation with state wildlife agencies

The manual operates under authority of the ESA, [[Sikes Act]] Title II (16 U.S.C. 670 et seq.), and the [[Federal Land Policy and Management Act]] (FLPMA) of October 21, 1976 (43 U.S.C. 1701).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The manual addresses species management across four major habitat divisions: terrestrial, freshwater, estuarine, and marine environments on BLM-administered public lands. [[Critical Habitat]] is defined as areas containing physical and biological features essential to species conservation, including physical structure, topography, biota, climate, human activity, and chemical content of land, water, and air.

**Habitat Management Requirements:**

- BLM must determine occurrence and distribution of all T/E species on administered lands
- Essential habitat and designated [[Critical Habitat]] must be identified and prescribed in land use plans
- Management plans must ensure conservation of T/E species and their habitats
- Actions must be consistent with [[Multiple Use]] principles while ensuring species conservation

The manual recognizes that habitat encompasses the complete environment where organisms live, requiring integrated management of ecosystem components. For candidate species, BLM must determine distribution, abundance, current status reasons, and habitat needs, evaluating the significance of BLM lands and actions in maintaining those species.

## Key Findings

**Section 7 Requirements and Consultation Procedures:**

The manual establishes mandatory compliance with ESA Section 7 for all BLM actions that may affect listed or proposed species, regardless of when planning began, if BLM authority remains discretionary. Key requirements include:

- **Section 7(a)(1)**: BLM must use existing authorities to plan and implement conservation programs for T/E species, directing maximum effort toward habitat and population restoration until ESA provisions are no longer necessary
- **Section 7(a)(2)**: BLM shall not jeopardize continued existence of T/E species or destroy/adversely modify [[Critical Habitat]]
- **Jeopardy Standard**: Defined as actions that would "appreciably reduce the likelihood of survival and recovery of a species"
- **Recovery Emphasis**: BLM must ensure actions avoid any appreciable reduction in likelihood of recovery, meeting or not interfering with recovery objectives

**Consultation Process Hierarchy:**

1. **Informal Consultation**: Required for all actions that may affect listed species unless formal consultation is clearly necessary; includes discussions to determine if formal consultation required
2. **Formal Consultation**: Commences with written request after determining action may affect listed species or [[Critical Habitat]]; results in biological opinion from FWS/NMFS
3. **Conference**: Required for proposed species or proposed [[Critical Habitat]]; provides advisory recommendations
4. **Technical Assistance**: Required for candidate species actions that may contribute to listing need

**Cumulative Effects Analysis:**

The manual distinguishes between FWS/NMFS cumulative effects analysis (which excludes future Federal actions) and BLM's responsibility under Section 7(a)(1). BLM must complete separate cumulative impact analysis including reasonably foreseeable future Federal actions (per 40 CFR 1508.7) to ensure compliance with recovery responsibilities. This analysis may occur during NEPA process (EA/EIS) or as separate action.

**Compensation vs. Mitigation:**

The manual clarifies that compensation (neutralization of effects through habitat purchase, improvements, reclamation outside affected area) is appropriate for eliminating jeopardy. However, mitigation that merely reduces but does not eliminate adverse impacts and jeopardy is not appropriate and will not satisfy BLM's recovery responsibility. This distinction was established by Interior Department Solicitor opinion.

**Applicability to Non-Federal Lands:**

ESA provisions apply regardless of surface ownership on split estate lands where BLM authorizes actions. For projects on non-Federal land requiring BLM right-of-way authorization on Federal land, ESA applies to entire project regardless of surface ownership.

**Candidate Species Management:**

For Category 1 and 2 candidate species, BLM must carry out management consistent with [[Multiple Use]] principles to ensure actions do not contribute to listing need. Specific requirements include:

- Determine distribution, abundance, status reasons, and habitat needs
- Include candidate species as priority species in land use plans (per BLM MS 1622)
- Develop rangewide and/or site-specific management plans with specific habitat and population objectives designed for recovery
- Monitor populations and habitats to determine if management objectives are met
- Request technical assistance from FWS/NMFS on any action that may contribute to listing need

**Recovery Plans and Teams:**

BLM employees are encouraged to serve on recovery teams as technical experts. Recovery plans establish recovery objectives, list necessary tasks, and recommend agency assignments. When BLM plans are prepared under planning regulations and an approved recovery plan exists, BLM management objectives must be consistent with recovery plan objectives. Agency review of recovery plan drafts requires analysis of: measurable objectives, conflicts with laws/regulations/policies, constraints on BLM programs, effects of other cooperators' actions, needed modifications to BLM plans, and cost estimates.

**Experimental Populations:**

The Secretary may designate experimental populations (per 50 CFR 17.80) outside current species range as either "essential" (essential to continued existence) or "nonessential" (not essential). Nonessential experimental populations are treated as proposed species for Section 7 purposes (except 7(a)(1)), meaning formal consultation is not required but conferences are required on habitat-affecting actions. [[Critical Habitat]] will not be determined for nonessential populations.

**State Director Responsibilities:**

State Directors must develop and implement conservation programs, coordinate with state and Federal agencies, establish programs to determine which Special Status Species occur on public lands, ensure conservation provisions in land use plans, ensure ESA compliance, ensure appropriate FWS/NMFS consultations, and designate a Special Status Species Program Coordinator for each state.

**Wilderness Considerations:**

Transplanting T/E species into historic range within designated wilderness and [[Wilderness Study Areas]] is appropriate, using minimum necessary actions and methods most appropriate for wilderness areas (per BLM Handbook H-8560-1).
