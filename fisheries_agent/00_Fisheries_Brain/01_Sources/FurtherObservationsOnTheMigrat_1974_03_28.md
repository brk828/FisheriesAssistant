## Executive Summary

This study, a follow-up to [[Dennert Et Al 1969]], investigates the migratory behavior of the amphipod *[[Gammarus Zaddachi]]* in La Slack, a small tidal stream on the French Channel coast (Pas-de-Calais). The authors combined field observations (dip-net and combination-net sampling across 12 stations plus 3 dedicated migration stations) with laboratory survival/reproduction experiments manipulating chloride (Cl⁻) and calcium (Ca²⁺) concentrations. Field data confirmed a strong seasonal migration pattern: a catadromous (downstream/seaward) migration in autumn linked to equinoctial spring tides and high population density, and depopulation of upriver limnic zones by *G. zaddachi* during winter, with replacement by *[[Echinogammarus Berilloni]]*. Laboratory experiments demonstrated that raising Cl⁻ concentration significantly improved survival rate (highly significant, P < 0.001), while raising Ca²⁺ concentration produced a positive but statistically non-significant improvement. The combination of elevated Cl⁻ and Ca²⁺ yielded the highest survival rates. These results support earlier hypotheses (Sutcliffe 1968, 1971a, 1971b) regarding sodium regulation physiology in gammarid crustaceans and confirm that migration is an actively regulated, physiologically-driven process tied to the reproductive cycle rather than a passive response to physico-chemical drift.

## Background & Description

*No new morphological data on body structure were reported in this paper.* The study is physiological and ecological in scope, focusing on:
- **Survival rate** under varying ionic conditions (Cl⁻, Ca²⁺)
- **Reproductive status**: distinction between ovigerous (egg-bearing) and non-ovigerous females
- **Longevity metric**: "d50" — number of days for a population to be reduced to 50% of its original number, used as a proxy for physiological robustness across experimental media
- Comparative note: *G. pulex* was found to have disappeared entirely from the study stretches of river, persisting only in the most inland reaches, indicating niche displacement/competitive exclusion dynamics with *G. zaddachi* and *E. berilloni*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat structure (La Slack river system):**
- **Station I**: Strong tidal influence; sharp/strong salinity variation; current reverses direction at high tide.
- **Station II**: Current reverses at high tide; only occasional slight salinity rise.
- **Station III**: Current reverses at high tide, but water remains fresh (limnic) throughout.
- Stations 1–12 (dip-net, monthly): stations 1–7 remained limnic (Cl⁻ ≤ 40 mg/l) year-round; stations 11–12 showed extreme chlorinity spikes at spring tide (up to 17,700 mg/l Cl⁻; Na⁺ up to 6,000 mg/l; Ca²⁺ up to 280 mg/l).

**Physico-chemical parameters:**
- Cl⁻: ≤40 mg/l in limnic reaches; up to ~160 mg/l in upper mixohaline reaches; dramatic spikes at seaward stations during spring tide.
- Ca²⁺: relatively stable (95–110 mg/l) except at seaward stations during spring tide (up to 280 mg/l).
- Na⁺: 10–90 mg/l typically, fluctuating in parallel with Cl⁻; up to 6,000 mg/l at stations 11/12.
- pH: stable, ranging 7–8 throughout the year and across stations.
- Temperature: winter bottom temperatures dropped below 6°C at all stations — concluded to be **not** a limiting factor for reproduction in limnic zones (consistent with [[Kinne 1952]]).

**Migration dynamics:**
- Year-round downstream ("drift") migration observed, overlaid by pronounced **catadromous** (seaward) migration pulses at equinoctial spring tides (notably October, with a lesser pulse in February) at Station I.
- **Anadromous** (upstream) migration also documented, correlated with increased population density upriver rather than being an independent process.
- Station III showed almost no catadromous migration at any sampling period — reflecting its stable freshwater character.
- Seasonal zonation: upriver limnic stations become depopulated by *G. zaddachi* in winter; the niche is occupied by *[[Echinogammarus Berilloni]]*, which itself shows no comparable migratory rhythm. Repopulation of upriver zones occurs the following autumn via catadromous-origin migrants.
- *G. pulex* has been displaced entirely from the studied river reaches, now found only in the furthest inland portions — suggesting long-term community-level shifts since [[Stock Et Al 1966]].

**Species interactions referenced:**
- Related migration study of *G. zaddachi* and *[[Gammarus Chevreuxi]]* in a Brittany stream (Girisch et al., unpubl.).
- Comparable salinity/ion tolerance work in *[[Gammarus Tigrinus]]* (Schmitz et al. 1967) and *[[Asellus Aquaticus]]*.

## Key Findings

1. **Confirmation of prior migration model**: Field data corroborate [[Dennert Et Al 1969]]'s finding of a diurnal activity rhythm (peak at night), continuous downstream drift, and equinoctial spring-tide-driven upstream/downstream migration pulses tied to the reproductive cycle.
2. **Chloride is the dominant survival factor**: Raising Cl⁻ concentration (from ~40 mg/l to ~160 mg/l) more than doubled survival percentage in laboratory trials — a highly significant effect (P < 0.001).
3. **Calcium has a secondary, non-significant positive effect**: Raising Ca²⁺ (from ~10 mg/l to ~100 mg/l) improved survival but did not reach statistical significance (P > 0.05) at either Cl⁻ level tested.
4. **Combined ion effect**: Highest survival rates occurred in the medium combining high Cl⁻ (160 mg/l) and high Ca²⁺ (100 mg/l).
5. **Population origin matters**: Animals collected from limnic (freshwater) stations were more sensitive to low-ion conditions than animals from upper mixohaline stations, implying local physiological acclimation or population-level differences in osmoregulatory tolerance.
6. **Reproduction tracks ion concentration**: In media with elevated Cl⁻ and/or Ca²⁺, 50–62.5% of females produced offspring, versus poor reproductive output in the low-ion control medium (medium I).
7. **Winter temperature is not limiting**: Despite dropping below 6°C in winter, temperature alone does not prevent reproductive activity in limnic-zone *G. zaddachi*, aligning with [[Kinne 1952]].
8. **No single physico-chemical trigger for migration**: Variation in Cl⁻, Ca²⁺, Na⁺, and pH in the limnic river zone was too small and incoherent to serve as a migratory trigger factor — supporting the hypothesis that migration is an internally (physiologically/reproductively) regulated behavior rather than a direct environmental response.
9. **Physiological basis supported by prior work**: Results align with [[Sutcliffe 1968]], [[Sutcliffe 1971a]], and [[Sutcliffe 1971b]] on sodium regulation in gammarid crustaceans, showing *G. zaddachi* can maintain sodium balance briefly at very low external Cl⁻ (~12 mg/l) but saturates its sodium-transport system around 350 mg/l Cl⁻, with Ca²⁺ enhancing sodium influx up to 400 mg/l.
10. **Community-level consequence**: The seasonal replacement dynamic between *G. zaddachi* and *[[Echinogammarus Berilloni]]* in upriver limnic zones represents a temporally partitioned niche occupation pattern, while *G. pulex* has been effectively excluded from the mid-to-lower river reaches.
