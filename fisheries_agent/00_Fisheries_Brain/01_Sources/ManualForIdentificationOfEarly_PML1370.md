# Identification Guide for Developmental Stages of Fishes of the Potomac River Estuary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ichthyoplankton #early-life-history

## Executive Summary

This December 1974 identification manual, prepared by Alice J. HipPson and R. Lynn Moran of the Martin Marietta Corporation Environmental Technology Center and sponsored by the Power Plant Siting Program of the Maryland Department of Natural Resources, provides illustrated keys and life-history descriptions for the eggs, larvae, juveniles and adults of fishes in the [[Potomac River Estuary]]. Its purpose is to support identification of early developmental stages in plankton and ichthyoplankton collections, which are often difficult to identify to species. Chapters by several contributors cover sturgeons, gars, herrings, minnows, catfishes, temperate basses, sunfishes and perches.

## Background & Description

The manual opens with a glossary of morphological and embryological terms, including standard length, total length, myomere counts, [[Yolk Sac Larva]] stages, [[Perivitelline Space]] and [[Egg Capsule]] characteristics. Standard measurement conventions are defined, such as preanal and postanal myomere counts, which are used throughout as diagnostic characters.

Each species account generally follows a common structure:

- **Spawning**: habitat, dates and temperature
- **Eggs**: demersal or pelagic, adhesive or buoyant, size and appearance
- **Larvae**: hatching size and diagnostic characters
- **Juveniles and adults**: meristic counts (dorsal, anal, pectoral and ventral fin rays, vertebrae, gill rakers, lateral line scales)
- **References and illustrations**

Chapter authors include John E. Cooper and Michael Fahay (Ophichthidae), James Loos (Cyprinidae key), Neal R. Foster (Poeciliidae), Charles A. Angler (Centrarchidae) and Fred C. Rohde (Percidae).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Potomac estuary is a transition zone between [[Tidal Freshwater]] and brackish water. Species differ in how far into tidal waters they spawn or occur:

- **Anadromous or estuarine spawners** such as [[Striped Bass]], [[White Perch]], [[Gizzard Shad]] and [[American Shad]] spawn in tidal fresh and slightly brackish areas, often close to the salt/fresh interface.
- **Mostly upriver spawners**: [[Threadfin Shad]] probably spawn well upstream, with only juveniles and adults entering tidal waters. Yellow perch spawn in tributaries and tidal freshwater in late winter and early spring.
- **Freshwater nest builders**: most [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes) build nests in shallow, vegetated or sandy areas. Their demersal, adhesive eggs and guarded prolarvae are rarely caught in plankton nets.

The manual notes that only [[Pumpkinseed]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] have been found spawning in tidal waters among the centrarchids, while [[Smallmouth Bass]] and white crappie have no recent tidal records.

## Key Findings

- **Sturgeon (Acipenser)**: Spawns April–May in tidal freshwater to brackish water. Eggs are opaque, adhesive and heavy, with a two-layered capsule. Hatching size is 11 mm TL.
- **Longnose Gar (Lepisosteus osseus)**: Spawns May–June in shallow, grassy freshwater. Eggs are 3.3–5.0 mm, demersal and adhesive. Larvae hatch at 8–10 mm TL and have a distinctive suctorial disc, which distinguishes them from other species. Larval occurrence in Potomac tidewaters is described as highly unlikely.
- **Gizzard Shad (Dorosoma cepedianum)**: Spawns early summer to late June, possibly into July, at 10–23 °C. Eggs are about 0.75 mm, with one large and up to five smaller oil globules. Hatching size is 3.25 mm TL. Late seasonal appearance and oil globules help separate them from other clupeids, including menhaden.
- **Threadfin Shad (Dorosoma petenense)**: Rare in the Potomac, with little regional spawning data. Eggs are about 1 mm and slightly adhesive. Hatching size is about 4.0 mm TL. Larvae are separated from gizzard shad by anal fin ray counts (17–27 vs. 23–35).
- **Cyprinidae (minnows and carps)**: James Loos's key separates larval and juvenile cyprinids, including carp, goldfish, golden shiner, spottail shiner, satinfin shiner and silvery minnow, using melanophore patterns, fin ray counts and the urostyle marking. Silvery minnow is excluded from the larval key because its characters were uncertain.
- **Goldfish (Carassius auratus)**: Infrequent in the mainstream Potomac below Washington. Spawns late April–May at 16–23 °C in shallow weedy areas. Eggs are 1.6 mm unfertilized and 1.0–1.2 mm fertilized. Hatching size is 3–5 mm TL. Larvae are distinctively yellow when alive.
- **Carp (Cyprinus carpio)**: Spawns in Potomac tributaries and tidal freshwater in clusters of 500–200 eggs among vegetation. Hatching size is 3.0–6.4 mm TL. Carp have barbels by about 12 mm, which separates juveniles from goldfish.
- **Golden Shiner (Notemigonus crysoleucas)**: Spawns March to August, possibly October. Hatching size is about 4.5 mm TL or less. Eggs have no oil globule. Juveniles are identified by a dark banding pattern at the urostyle.
- **Catfishes**: Brown bullhead (Ictalurus nebulosus) spawns May–June at about 21–25 °C in nests. Eggs are about 3.0 mm, pale cream and in clusters. Hatching size is about 6.0 mm TL. Channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) spawn probably May–June at 21–29 °C, with 3.5 mm eggs and a hatching size of about 6.4 mm TL.
- **Temperate Basses (Percichthyidae)**: White perch and striped bass dominate Potomac collections. White perch eggs are spherical with thick capsules and a single large oil globule. Striped bass eggs are about 3.5 mm, with a thin capsule and a single distinctive oil globule. Striped bass hatch at 2.0–3.2 mm TL and reach about 25–30 mm as adults. Striped bass larvae become difficult to catch in plankton tows between 20 and 40 mm.
- **Yellow Perch (Perca flavescens)**: Spawns late February to early April in tidal freshwater and tributaries. Eggs are laid in gelatinous strands. Hatching size is 5.5–6.0 mm TL. Yellow perch have 34–40 myomeres, compared with 23–26 in white perch and striped bass.
- **Sunfishes (Centrarchidae)**: Nine species are listed, and pumpkinseed is expected to be the most common in samples. Pumpkinseed (Lepomis gibbosus) spawns May–August at 16–27 °C, with eggs of 0.8–1.2 mm and hatching size of 2.6–3.1 mm TL. Bluegill (L. macrochirus) spawns May–August at 16–27 °C. Largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) eggs are 1.4–1.8 mm, with hatching size averaging 3.5 mm TL.
- **Percidae (perches and darters)**: Four percids occur in the lower Potomac region: swamp darter, tessellated darter, glassy darter and johnny darter. Only yellow perch regularly occur in brackish waters, tolerating salinities up to 12 ppt. Darter eggs are ovoid, about 1.5 mm, and adhered to substrate.
- **Key diagnostic traits**: Myomere counts, the presence of a single large oil globule, egg capsule thickness, adhesiveness and the position of the vent or anus are repeatedly used to separate species at early stages.
