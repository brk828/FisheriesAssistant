# Mosquito Control in Tucson's Wetlands: Atturbury Design and Sweetwater 1998 Monitoring

#ichthyology #marine-biology #culicidae

## Executive Summary

This newsletter issue (Mosquito Connection, Volume II) covers two linked topics. First, mosquito control is being built into the design of the [[Atturbury Wetlands And Recharge Project]], a 3.5-acre pilot wetland in southeast Tucson scheduled for completion in spring 2000. Second, it reports a 1998 University of Arizona study of mosquitoes at the [[Sweetwater Wetlands]], which tested [[Bacillus Thuringiensis Israelensis]] (Bti) as a biological larvicide and detected [[Western Equine Encephalitis Virus]] in *Culex tarsalis*. The issue also includes submission guidelines, mosquito trivia, and a mailing notice. Because the source is a newsletter rather than a fish study, the fish content is limited to one proposed native species.

## Background & Description

The Atturbury project is the City of Tucson's second wetland effort after Sweetwater. It will treat about 500 acre-feet of reclaimed wastewater per year for recharge through a well rather than surface spreading basins, and it will be studied to test whether well recharge suits urban areas with limited space. Features include four wetland cells in two treatment trains, a half-mile stream feature flowing to Atturbury Wash, and a filtration system and recharge well about one-quarter mile downstream. The site lies near neighborhoods, schools and a community college, so the design treats mosquito control as a requirement from the start.

Sweetwater Wetlands, operated by the Tucson Water Department on the west side of Tucson, combines water treatment and recreation. It has two 7.5-acre polishing basins with deep and shallow zones, which drain into 14 acres of recharge basins that replenish the local aquifer. Dense large water reeds in the shallow zones act as the main filtering agents for treated effluent, and they also create mosquito habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Mosquito breeding is linked to standing water and vegetation structure. Dense, tall emergent plants such as giant bulrush, American three-square bulrush and cattail are efficient at treating water, but their rapid spread and heavy thatching have complicated mosquito control at Sweetwater. Adult mosquitoes are not water-dependent and congregate in tall grasses and weedy vegetation.

Birds are the normal host for encephalitis virus. The large bird populations attracted to Sweetwater are considered the likely virus source, and horses and humans become hosts mainly when bird and mosquito populations are both high. *Culex tarsalis* is an important carrier between birds, horses and occasionally humans, while *Culex pipiens quinquefasciatus* and *Culex erythrothorax* are minor carriers.

Background sampling in 1997, before the wetlands were built, found nightly trap counts up to 289 mosquitoes at a wastewater treatment plant in July and at a city park in August, which suggests that high mosquito levels already existed in the area.

The Atturbury design also considers native fish. The Long-finned Dace, found in the San Pedro River, is being assessed with the Arizona Game and Fish Department as a possible biological control candidate. No fish data were collected in the document.

## Key Findings

**Atturbury Wetlands design features for mosquito control**
- Four wetland cells in two fairly equal treatment trains, so cells can be drained alternately if needed.
- Access to wetland cells for larvicide dispersal if needed.
- Wide, concrete-lined deep zones up to four feet deep, with four deep zones per cell, to limit breeding and plant encroachment. Dimensions were developed with input from [[Arizona Department Of Health Services]].
- Shorter wetland plants with fewer stems per meter to reduce thatching. Research support came from Martin Karpiscak (University of Arizona Arid Lands Studies).
- Tall species (American three-square bulrush, giant bulrush, cattail) were advised against. Candidate plants include bountiful sedge, blister sedge, rush, arrowhead, salt marsh bulrush and giant bureed, with final selection depending on availability.
- Pre-project mosquito monitoring to set baseline conditions, and a backup plan including periodic de-thatching and biological larvicide.
- The stated goal is to minimize mosquito numbers while providing a safe, educational recreational site. The document acknowledges that no strategy can fully eliminate mosquitoes.

**Sweetwater 1998 study (University of Arizona and Tucson Water)**
- Objective: determine whether Bti is a viable means of killing mosquito larvae.
- Bti was applied by hand from May 8 and 15 around the east and west polishing ponds, weekly from June 4 to July 7 around islands and deepwater edges of the west basin, and from the air by remote-controlled helicopter over both basins from July 2 through mid-October, with a final application in November.
- Adult collections ran weekly from May 1998 to late November at four sites, using CO2 traps (two to four per night). Larval and pupal collections from 17 sites in the westernmost basin began May 27; these samples were still being examined.
- Virus testing covered 57 pools (average 20 mosquitoes per pool) containing 1,073 mosquitoes.
- Eight species in four genera were identified among adults. *Culex tarsalis* dominated, with *Culex erythrothorax* next in number.
- Western equine encephalitis virus was detected in two *Culex tarsalis* samples collected in September and confirmed by the Arizona Department of Health Services in November.
- Population was bimodal, with the largest peak in May and June and a smaller peak in September and October. The authors consider this a normal seasonal response to rising temperatures, as Robert MacDonald reported similar curves for Tucson in 1980.
- Trap counts were 7,500 to 10,000 mosquitoes per trap in May and June, fell to 250 to 800 in July and August, and rose to 900 to 2,000 per trap later.
- The Arizona Department of Health Services considers more than 20 mosquitoes per trap unacceptable. The authors acknowledge that Sweetwater is far above this benchmark.
