# Results of identification of collections of larval fish made in the upper Salt and Gila Rivers, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Gila-River-Basin #larval-fish-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1985 technical report by Kevin R. Bestgen (Colorado State University), submitted to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, presents identification results from 16 larval fish collections taken across the upper [[Salt River]] and [[Gila River]] drainages in Arizona between 19–24 May 1985. A total of **7 species and 7,952 larval specimens** were identified, dominated numerically by two native catostomids, the [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*, 45.6%) and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*, 42.5%). No non-native species were detected in any collection, despite documented introductions at some of the same localities in prior literature. A significant biogeographic finding was the detection of larval [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) in **Eagle Creek**, a stream not previously documented to support the species — expanding its known range within the Gila River Basin. The report also addresses taxonomic ambiguity in distinguishing larval *Gila robusta* from *Gila intermedia*, using pigmentation and myomere counts as diagnostic criteria.

## Background & Description

Morphological data in this report are limited to larval size ranges (total length, TL) and diagnostic larval characters used to resolve taxonomic ambiguity within the genus *Gila*:

- **[[Gila Robusta]] (roundtail chub) larvae** (Eagle Creek): lightly pigmented; **28–29 pre-vent myomeres** — consistent with *G. robusta* populations from the upper Colorado River system ([[Snyder 1981]]) and the Gila River of New Mexico.
- **[[Gila Intermedia]] (Gila chub) larvae** (Blue River, a tributary confirmed to support *G. intermedia*): heavily pigmented; **24–25 pre-vent myomeres**.
- Size ranges (TL) observed across collections:
  - *Gila robusta*: 12–30.5 mm
  - *Gila intermedia*: 8–22.5 mm (varying by site)
  - *Meda fulgida* (spikedace): 13–25 mm
  - *Agosia chrysogaster* (longfin dace): 7–40 mm
  - *Rhinichthys osculus* (speckled dace): 10–31 mm
  - *Catostomus insignis* (Sonora sucker): 12–41 mm
  - *Pantosteus clarki* (desert sucker): 12–33 mm

Because of persistent taxonomic confusion surrounding the genus *Gila* in the lower Colorado River basin, larval identifications were treated as **tentative**, relying on co-occurrence with sympatric identified adults as corroborating evidence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Drainage systems sampled**: [[Salt River Drainage]] (Cedar Creek, Carrizo Creek, Cibicue Creek, White River–East Fork) and [[Gila River Drainage]] (Blue River, Aravaipa Creek, Eagle Creek).
- **Habitat context**: Collections were made in perennial montane/desert stream reaches within the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] and surrounding Gila Basin tributaries — habitats characterized by mixed riffle-pool geomorphology supporting native cyprinid-catostomid assemblages.
- **Community composition**: The catostomids (*C. insignis*, *P. clarki*) overwhelmingly dominated larval assemblages (88.1% combined), reflecting either high spawning output, differential larval retention/drift dynamics, or sampling bias toward sucker spawning habitat during the survey window.
- **Rare/imperiled native cyprinids**: *Gila robusta*, *Gila intermedia*, and *Meda fulgida* were rare in the samples (combined <0.6% of total specimens), consistent with their status as species of conservation concern within the Gila Basin.
- **Absence of non-natives**: No introduced species were found in any of the 16 larval collections, despite known non-native presence at some sites from earlier adult-based surveys ([[Minckley 1973]]; [[Minckley and Sommerfield 1979]]; [[Propst et al. 1985]]), suggesting possible differences in spawning phenology, larval habitat use, or sampling timing between native and non-native taxa.
- **Range extension**: *Meda fulgida* larvae collected from **two Eagle Creek localities** (Bat Cave/above diversion and San Carlos Reservoir boundary sites) represent a **new documented occurrence** for this stream, not previously reported in Minckley (1973), Minckley and Sommerfield (1979), or Propst et al. (1985).

## Key Findings

- **7 species / 7,952 specimens** identified from 16 collections across the Salt and Gila River drainages, AZ (19–24 May 1985).
- Dominant taxa: **[[Sonora Sucker]]** (45.6%) and **[[Desert Sucker]]** (42.5%) together comprised ~88% of all larvae collected.
- Subdominant native cyprinid: **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*, 7.3%), followed by **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*, 1.4%).
- Rare imperiled cyprinids: **[[Gila Chub]]** (0.25%, n=21), **[[Spikedace]]** (0.15%, n=12), **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (0.14%, n=11).
- **No non-native larval fishes** detected in any of the 16 collections, despite documented adult introductions at some sites in earlier literature.
- **New locality record**: *Meda fulgida* (spikedace) larvae documented for the first time in **Eagle Creek**, extending its known distribution within the Gila River drainage.
- Larval *Gila* taxonomy resolved using **pre-vent myomere counts and pigmentation**: Eagle Creek larvae (28–29 myomeres, lightly pigmented) assigned to *Gila robusta*; Blue River larvae (24–25 myomeres, heavily pigmented) assigned to *Gila intermedia*.
- Heaviest larval catostomid abundances recorded from **Eagle Creek** sites (e.g., 2,203 *C. insignis* and 1,353 *P. clarki* at the Bat Cave/diversion site), indicating this reach as a significant spawning/nursery area.
- All voucher specimens are archived at the **Arizona State University Collection of Fishes**.
