# A Comparison of the Nymphs of the British Species of the Genus Ephemera

#entomology #freshwater-biology #ephemeroptera

## Executive Summary

This 1958 taxonomic paper by [[Thomas Macan]] (Freshwater Biological Association, Ambleside) compares the nymphs of the three British mayflies of the genus Ephemera recorded by [[Kimmins Key|Kimmins (1954)]]: [[Ephemera Vulgata]], [[Ephemera Danica]] and [[Ephemera Lineata]]. Macan tests the nymphal characters used by earlier workers (Lestage, Schoenemund, Percival and Whitehead, Petersen) and finds that several are unreliable. He identifies the abdominal dorsal pattern as the most dependable distinction and adds a new character, the shape of the fore tibia. He ends with a revised identification key to the nymphs.

## Background & Description

Earlier descriptions of the [[Mayfly Nymph]] of the two common British species were based largely on the work of [[Lestage Description|Lestage (1916)]], which was confirmed by [[Schoenemund Key|Schoenemund (1930)]] and [[Percival and Whitehead Study|Percival and Whitehead (1926)]]. The nymph of E. lineata had never been described. Macan's aim was to check the existing differences against a range of material and to add characters where possible.

The characters compared were:
- **Head markings:** joined together in E. danica, not joined in E. vulgata.
- **Sides of head:** less convex in E. danica; more convex (round) in E. vulgata.
- **[[Clypeal Process]] shape:** frontal bay and lateral projections differ between species.
- **[[Dorsal Abdominal Markings]]:** differ in pattern and location on the abdominal terga.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The material came from a mix of lotic and lentic habitats:
- **Canals:** [[Kennet-Avon Canal]] near Reading (both E. vulgata and E. danica), and the Kendal-Lancaster canal.
- **Lakes:** [[Windermere]] and Bassenthwaite (E. danica), and L. Sheelin (Ireland).
- **Streams and rivers:** Dodnash Stream (Suffolk), R. Darent (Kent), Tillingbourne (Surrey), and Bottisham Lode (Cambridgeshire, E. vulgata).
- **Lowland Thames:** E. lineata was first taken by Eaton near Reading. Later records came from three sites further down the Thames, the most recent dated 1902.

Nymphs were collected across seasons. Some specimens were taken in December and May, and Macan notes that the eye size of nymphs appears to increase as metamorphosis approaches. He also notes that the dorsal abdominal pattern fades in preservative.

## Key Findings

- **Character reliability:**
  - The head-marking character (joined vs. not joined) is judged worthless because the pattern varies considerably.
  - The head-side character is also worthless. Eye size differs between sexes and increases near metamorphosis, so it cannot separate species.
  - The clypeal process is more reliable than earlier figures suggested, but its shape varies, with some E. danica resembling E. vulgata.
  - The [[Dorsal Abdominal Markings]] are described as an "excellent distinction." Percival and Whitehead could separate nymphs down to 12.5 mm; Macan found the E. danica pattern present in nymphs as small as 5 cm.
- **New character:** the [[Fore Tibia]] of E. danica is broad with an evenly rounded convex side. In E. vulgata it is narrower with straighter sides. In E. lineata the tibia resembles E. danica, but the [[Fore Femur]] is broader than in either other species. The mid-tibia shows the same difference more clearly.
- **E. lineata markings:** Schoenemund's prediction is confirmed. E. lineata has six narrow dark lines on the tergum, matching the adult pattern, though the E. danica triangular mark can split, giving six dark marks in total.
- **Revised key (summary):**
  1. Distinct dark marks on all terga except the first and last, with segments 7–9 only about twice the size of other segments; fore tibia narrow with two straight inner edges joined by a curve; clypeal process with slightly convex sides and a deep, wide frontal bay → **E. vulgata**.
  2. Large triangular mark on terga 7–9 (may be split, with a narrow inner line); femora relatively narrow; clypeal process sides may be more convex, with a smaller frontal bay and more obtuse projections → **E. danica**.
  3. Three dark lines on terga 7–9; femora broader → **E. lineata**.
- **Material examined (lots as listed in the paper):**
  - E. vulgata: 1 cast skin and 15 whole nymphs.
  - E. danica: 17 cast skins and 31 whole nymphs in total across 9 lots (including Windermere, Dodnash Stream, R. Darent and Tillingbourne).
  - E. lineata: 10 whole nymphs from Poland, provided by Dr. Maria Keffermüller.
