# Turtox News: Field Collecting Equipment, Algal Identification Key, and Biology Supplies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #algae #biological-supply-catalog

## Executive Summary

This is a multi-item issue of the [[Turtox]] trade newsletter (catalog pages 103 to 108) aimed at teachers, field collectors and laboratory biologists. It contains product advertisements for sweeping nets, dissectible eye models, skeleton sets and wood-cell slides; a notice soliciting specimens for a North American [[Fairy Shrimp]] and [[Freshwater Jellyfish]] study; and a field identification key to genera of [[Algae]] by Clarence E. Taft of The Ohio State University. No fish species, fish habitats or aquatic ecosystem measurements are reported. The aquatic content is limited to the algal key, which classifies algae by habitat and gross morphology.

## Background & Description

The newsletter is a commercial and educational publication. Its main items are:

- **Sweeping nets (Turtox 105A17 and 105A165):** Insect and field collecting nets. The 105A17 Utility Net has a 24-inch varnished hardwood handle and a 12-inch ring. The 105A165 Heavy-Duty Net, improved at the request of [[Franz Schrader]] of Columbia University, has a 1⅜-inch handle, a 12-inch non-rusting metal ring and a reinforced muslin bag 24 inches deep. It is priced at $10.50.
- **Algal key:** A field key by Clarence E. Taft (Department of Botany, The Ohio State University) for identifying macroscopic and some microscopic algal genera, intended for collectors who need clean unialgal samples.
- **Cell mounts of timber woods:** Macerated wood-cell microscope slides representing more than sixty-five timber tree species, referenced to Turtox Biology Catalog No. 58, pages 400 and 401.
- **Fairy shrimp and freshwater jellyfish call:** Ralph W. Dexter (Kent State University) requests specimens from anywhere in North America for taxonomy, ecology and distribution studies, with full collecting data.
- **Dissectible eye model (TM-412):** An enlarged right-eye model, 8 × 7 inches, with removable lenses and a numbered key.
- **Nylon netting:** Durable fine-mesh nylon used in insect net bags.
- **Human skeletal products:** A Survey Set (catalog 14860, $48.50), a complete articulated adult skeleton (catalog 14810105, $175), and skeleton cleaning and repair services.
- **Turtox Biology Catalog No. 58:** A 768-page handbook published August 1956.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The algal key organizes taxa by habitat, which provides the main ecological content of the issue. Key divisions include:

- **Association with animals:** Algae on snapping turtle shells, on snail shells, in old egg masses, or inside green [[Hydra]] or green [[Planaria]] (e.g., *Zoochlorella*).
- **Terrestrial and aerial substrates:** Algae on soil, rocks, bark, old buildings, rotting logs, and dry cliff or quarry faces (e.g., *Trentepohlia*).
- **Running water versus standing water:** Taxa separated by cold (early spring or spring-fed) and warm (late spring to summer) conditions, with examples such as *Cladophora*, *Batrachospermum* and *Draparnaldia* in flowing water.
- **Temporary versus permanent waters:** Bird baths, puddles, cow tracks and pigpen ponds contrasted with ponds, lakes and lake margins, including *Euglena*, *Chlamydomonas*, *Spirogyra*, *Zygnema*, *Mougeotia* and *Chara*.
- **Laboratory cultures:** Aquaria and culture dishes, with *Chlorella*, *Scenedesmus* and *Ankistrodesmus*.

The key is calibrated for Central Ohio but is described as applicable to most of Ohio and surrounding states. It assumes that the collector's judgment will be used in place of detailed habitat descriptions.

## Key Findings

- The issue contains no fish-specific data. It covers general biological supplies, with the algal key as the only aquatic-ecology content.
- The algal key contains roughly 50 numbered couplets. It separates genera by habitat (animal-associated, terrestrial, running or standing water, temporary or permanent water, laboratory) and by gross morphology (filamentous or not, branched or not, slippery or not, gelatinous or net-like).
- The key is intended for field use, and a hand lens is helpful but not essential. It includes only macroscopic or distinctive algae.
- Taft notes that closely related forms may key out together, which simplifies the key without much loss of accuracy because such forms are often interchangeable for lab use.
- Dexter's fairy shrimp and freshwater jellyfish study requests specimens from anywhere in North America. Specimens are returned on request after determinations and measurements, provided collecting data are included.
- The Heavy-Duty Sweeping Net was built to Professor Schrader's specification after he reported it was "really excellent" in heavy grass and underbrush, especially in tropical collecting.
- The Turtox Biology Catalog No. 58 (768 pages, August 1956) is cross-referenced for wood-slide and other product pricing.
