# Letter to Desert Fishes Council on Status of Endangered Fish Recovery Plans

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #conservation-policy #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1990 administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) to the [[Desert Fishes Council]], responding to Council Resolution 89-2. The resolution urged prioritization of recovery plan completion/revision for 16 native North American desert fish species. The letter is not a primary scientific study but a **regulatory status update**, providing a timeline for federal recovery plan development for multiple endangered and threatened fish taxa native to the American Southwest, including the [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Sonoran Chub]], Yaqui basin fauna, [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Apache Trout]], and [[Gila Trout]]. It documents the administrative/regulatory pipeline (drafting, technical review, public review, Federal Register publication) rather than biological or ecological data per se.

## Background & Description

The letter was issued under the authority of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in response to advocacy from the [[Desert Fishes Council]], a scientific/conservation body concerned with fishes endemic to arid and desert aquatic habitats of the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. The correspondence references coordination with **W.L. Minckley**, Leader of the Desert Fishes Recovery Team at Arizona State University, and multiple Fish and Wildlife Service field offices (Dexter National Fish Hatchery, Phoenix Ecological Services, Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office, San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge, and Albuquerque Ecological Services).

Species referenced fall under the U.S. Endangered Species Act recovery planning process, which requires:
1. Draft plan preparation
2. Technical review
3. Agency review
4. Public review
5. Federal Register publication
6. Final approval and distribution

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All species discussed are native to isolated desert aquatic ecosystems of the [[American Southwest]] and the [[Sonoran Desert]], spanning several major drainage basins:

- **[[Gila River Basin]]** — historic range for [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Gila Trout]]
- **[[Yaqui River Basin]]** (Arizona/Sonora, Mexico) — home to the Yaqui fauna: [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and [[Beautiful Shiner]]
- **[[Little Colorado River]]** — habitat of the [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]
- **Sonoran border streams** — habitat of the [[Sonoran Chub]]
- **Desert springs and washes of the Colorado Desert** — habitat of the [[Desert Pupfish]]
- **Apache and White Mountains streams (Arizona)** — habitat of the [[Apache Trout]]

These species share common ecological threats typical of desert fish conservation: habitat fragmentation, water diversion, introduction of non-native competitors/predators, and small isolated population sizes — all of which necessitate species-specific recovery planning rather than a single unified management approach.

## Key Findings

- **16 desert fish species** were identified in Desert Fishes Council Resolution 89-2 as needing prioritized recovery plan action; the Service's **Region 2** was responsible for recovery planning of **4** of these species directly, with additional related taxa also addressed.
- **[[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]**: Recovery plans proceeding simultaneously; technical, agency, and public review completed; anticipated Federal Register publication and plan approval/distribution **before September 30, 1990**.
- **[[Sonoran Chub]]**: Recovery plan in draft form, undergoing revision; no firm completion date; anticipated ready for technical/agency/public review by **late summer 1990**.
- **Yaqui fauna** ([[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Beautiful Shiner]]): Single combined recovery plan in preparation; expected ready for review within **calendar year 1990**.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]**: Draft recovery plan **completed**, soon to enter formal review process.
- **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]** and **[[Gila Topminnow]]**: Recovery plans in preparation; draft plans due within **calendar year 1990**.
- **[[Apache Trout]]** and **[[Gila Trout]]**: Existing recovery plans undergoing **revision**; expected ready for review **before December 31, 1990**.
- The letter emphasizes ongoing interagency coordination between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Arizona State University's Desert Fishes Recovery Team (led by **W.L. Minckley**), and multiple regional field offices to support recovery implementation.
- No new biological, morphological, or population data are presented; the document's primary scientific value lies in establishing a **timeline record for endangered desert fish species recovery planning** as of mid-1990.
