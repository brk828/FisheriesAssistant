# FWS Field Study Continuation Proposal for GCES Phase II (FY94)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-cypha

## Executive Summary

This internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum, dated 22 February 1994 and written by GCES Project Coordinator Owen Gorman, transmits a revised draft proposal to continue Service field studies under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Phase II program for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. The studies address the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fishes of the [[Grand Canyon]], focusing on [[Conservation Measures]] 5 and 7. The Service argues that its contracted field work was cut short by permitting delays, flooding, and late program changes, so it requests roughly $141,000 in additional FY94 funding on top of the existing $261,549 budget, for a total of $402,549.

## Background & Description

The proposal responds to the reasonable and prudent alternatives set by the Service for GCES Phase II. Among them are establishing a second humpback chub spawning aggregation in the Grand Canyon, assessing the effects of a [[Multi-Level Intake Structure]] on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] for endangered and native fishes, and developing an [[Adaptive Management Plan]]. Draft proposals were circulated in August, September, October 1993, and February 1994, and a formal proposal was signed by Regional Director John Rogers on 6 December 1993.

The study has two components:

1. **Habitat use in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR):** describing habitat use by post-larval humpback chub, including spawning habitat. The LCR supports the largest concentration of successfully reproducing humpback chub in its native range.
2. **Tributary habitat studies:** evaluating smaller Colorado River tributaries in the Grand Canyon for their potential to support secondary reproducing humpback chub populations.

The memo also names [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]] as native species whose strong year classes are of interest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo describes the habitat and biological conditions that shaped the study:

- **Spawning window:** humpback chub spawn over a short period, about 2 to 3 weeks in spring, when the river is usually flooding and muddy. This made Objective 3 (spawning habitat) difficult to sample.
- **Flooding and permits:** permits from Grand Canyon National Park were received in 1992, and prolonged flooding throughout 1992 limited access. During the summer 1992 and early winter 1993 surveys, all tributaries were at flood stage.
- **Tributary role:** surveys suggested the lower 1 km before each confluence may be the most critical zone for humpback chub and other native species. The Service's focus was the confluence areas and the lower 1000 m, because these are more likely to be used by mainstem adult and young-of-year (YOY) chub. Chub captures from the confluences of [[Shinumo Creek]] and [[Kanab Creek]] support this view.
- **Tributary differences:** cursory examination suggests most tributaries, except [[Havasu Creek]], differ strongly from the LCR and may be poor candidates for new chub populations. The Service argues that quantitative data are needed before that conclusion can be accepted.

## Key Findings

**Achievement of study objectives**

- **LCR:** Objectives 1 (seasonal habitat availability) and 2 (seasonal distribution and habitat use by YOY, juvenile, and adult fishes) were met. Objective 3 (spawning habitat) was not achieved, with only one spawning opportunity because of project start delays. Objective 4 (river modeling of high-discharge effects) was officially abandoned, though data were collected that could support future instream flow models. Mapping of the LCR confluence and other Grand Canyon confluences was completed for preliminary hydraulic and habitat models.
- **Tributaries:** Objectives were addressed, but the Service states that the database is inadequate for a firm assessment. Three of six downstream tributaries (Shinumo, Kanab, Havasu) were resurveyed at base flow in summer 1993 using the same methods and crews as the LCR work.

**Justification for continuation**

1. **Strong 1993 year class:** the exceptionally strong 1993 humpback chub year class offers a rare chance to track YOY chub through their first year. The Service says this is needed to implement conservation measures 2 and 5.
2. **Tributary surveys:** the Service asks to complete base-flow surveys of Bright Angel Creek, Tapeats Creek, and the Paria River, which were not sampled in 1993. It states that without a complete, common-method survey, the tributary assessment will be "incomplete and perhaps inconclusive," and that denying the work would deny a role for tributaries in humpback chub recovery.

**Proposed FY94 field studies**

| Study area | Trips | Days | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Little Colorado River | 4 (2 spring, 2 summer) | 40 | $28,000 |
| Grand Canyon tributaries (Bright Angel, Tapeats) | 1 | 12 | $8,000 |
| Paria River | 1 | 5 | $3,000 |

The Service notes it made 10 LCR trips in FY93. The Grand Canyon trip would focus on confluences and lower reaches within Grand Canyon National Park. The Paria survey would be done by automobile.

**Staffing and reporting**

- Continued employment of a 4-person field crew, including one GS-5 and one GS-7 technician, is budgeted at $58,000.
- A GS-9/11 fishery biologist ($36,000) would help write the final report.
- Equipment, supplies, and repairs are budgeted at $8,000.

**Schedule and funding history**

- The contract began 1 October 1990, but field operations did not start until July 1991. The Service returned $100,000 to the Bureau of Reclamation in August 1991.
- The Service says it has had only about 15 months of effective field work against a planned three-year field period plus a fourth analysis year.
- Based on September 1993 and September 1993 (Albuquerque) meetings, the Service understood that interim drafts would be due 1 October 1994 and final reports 1 October 1995. At the 15 February 1994 GCES-ACT meeting, it was instead announced that contractors would be held to the original deadlines. The Service asks for resources to meet the schedule or for an extension.

**Cost summary**

- Additional FY94 costs: $141,000 (field studies $39,000; equipment $8,000; technicians $58,000; biologist $36,000). These figures come from actual FY93 costs and include a 15% USFWS overhead.
- Present FY94 budget: $261,549.
- Total estimated FY94 need: $402,549.
