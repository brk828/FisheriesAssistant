# Eulimnadia antlei: A New Conchostracan Phyllopod from Oklahoma

#conchostraca #phyllopoda #crustacean-taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1940 taxonomic paper by [[J. G. Mackin]], reprinted from *The American Midland Naturalist* (Vol. 23, No. 1, pp. 219–221), describes a new conchostracan branchiopod, *Eulimnadia antlei* sp. nov., from small ponds in Oklahoma. The species is named for [[Herbert Antle]], who collected the first specimens. The author separates it from its closest relatives, [[Eulimnadia Diversa]], [[Eulimnadia Antillarum]] and [[Eulimnadia Minuta]], mainly on shell growth lines, rostrum shape and telson spine counts. The paper is a morphological description with locality records, not a study of ecology or population dynamics.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The genus *Eulimnadia* belongs to the conchostracans, bivalved "clam shrimps" commonly called fairy shrimp. The author notes that the Antle collections were first set aside as a new species but were too immature to describe. Later collections included mature specimens, which allowed a full description.

**Sexual dimorphism.**
- *Male:* The shell has 4 or 5 indistinct growth lines. The head's lower angle projects into a sharp point. The first antennae are slightly longer than in the female, but the dimorphism is less marked than in [[Eulimnadia Diversa]]. The largest specimen measures 5 × 3 mm.
- *Female:* The shell has 5 or 6 growth lines and a highly vaulted dorsal hinge line. The largest specimen measures 6.5 × 4 mm. The rostrum ends in a right-angled lower point. The first foot has a short flabellum ending at the base of the sixth endite.

**Body and appendages.**
- Eighteen pairs of feet is the most common count, though some small specimens have 16 or 17.
- Nine of the posterior body segments are spined dorsally, with some variation.
- In two representative females, spine formulae (given in inverse order in the source) were 1-3-5-5-5-3-3-1-1 and 3-5-5-7-5-5-3-3-1.
- Each posterior dorsal ridge of the telson carries 7–9 long, slightly curved, usually smooth spines.
- The claw bears 12 to 18 long plumose setae. The small terminal segment is finely subspined.
- The forked telson filament lies between the second and fourth telson spines.
- Spination of the second antennae varies considerably. The author suspects this is true of all species in the genus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper records three collection sites, all small ponds in Oklahoma:

| Locality | Depth | Description | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near Altus, Jackson County | 2 in. | Small pond, about 3 ft diameter | 6 Sept 1932 |
| ½ mile west of Southard, Blaine County (type locality) | 4 in. | Clear, grassy pond | 13 June 1936 |
| 3 miles west of Ringling, Jefferson County | 6 in. | Clear pond choked with grasses | 17 July 1935 |

These habitats are shallow, clear, vegetated freshwater ponds. The paper gives no water chemistry, temperature, hydroperiod, or associated fauna. Ecological observations are limited to the pond descriptions.

**Type material.** Cotypes are held in the [[U.S. National Museum]] (catalog no. 78481) and in the author's collection.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Eulimnadia antlei* sp. nov., from Oklahoma, named for [[Herbert Antle]].
- **Diagnostic features:** The species is distinguished from [[Eulimnadia Diversa]] by shell growth lines (5 vs. 2), male rostrum shape (pointed vs. right-angled), and telson spine count (9 vs. 12 on the posterior ridge).
- **Similar species:** [[Eulimnadia Antillarum]] and [[Eulimnadia Minuta]] also have few telson spines, but they have only two growth lines on the shell, so they differ from *E. antlei* in that character. *E. antillarum* has a long female flabellum, and *E. minuta* has 12 dorsally spined segments and a different female rostral point.
- **Variable characters:** Second antennal spination varies widely in several *Eulimnadia* species, so it is excluded from the diagnosis. The author suspects this applies across the genus.
- **Size range:** Males reach 5 × 3 mm and females 6.5 × 4 mm.
- **Locality records:** Three Oklahoma pond sites, with the Blaine County pond designated as the type locality.
- **Note on the text:** The paper gives the male shell growth lines as "4 or 5" but later cites "4" for *E. antlei* in the comparison. The source is reported here as written.
