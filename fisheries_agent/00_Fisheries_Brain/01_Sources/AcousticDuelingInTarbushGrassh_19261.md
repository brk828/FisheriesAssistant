# Acoustic Dueling in Tarbush Grasshoppers: Settlement of Territorial Contests via Alternation of Reliable Signals

#entomology #orthoptera #acoustic-signaling

## Executive Summary

This Ethology paper (Greenfield & Minckley, 1993) reports a field study of male [[Ligurotettix Planum]] contesting ownership of mating territories on [[Flourensia Cernua]] shrubs in the [[Chihuahuan Desert]]. Most contests were settled ritually through antiphonal exchange of acoustic signals, with a special aggressive "shuck" call central to the outcome. Winners were predicted best by the rate of shuck production (SPR), and contests between similarly matched males lasted longer and were more likely to escalate. Playback experiments indicated that males hear less well while calling, which the authors argue favors the mutual call alternation seen in these contests. The document also contains unrelated University Faculty Grievance Committee minutes (November 11, 1993), which are summarized under Key Findings.

## Background & Description

Contests over resources are expected to be settled by restrained, ritualized displays when resources are plentiful and losers can find alternatives later. Displays in such contests should be reliable indicators of either resource holding potential (RHP) or an uncorrelated asymmetry such as residency (see [[Resource Holding Potential]]). The authors tested five predictions: (1) most contests are settled without physical contact; (2) winners are distinguished by call parameters reflecting RHP; (3) contests are prolonged when males are matched in call parameters; (4) escalation is more likely when males are matched; and (5) males compare call parameters, and alternation of calls enables this comparison.

*L. planum* adults are monophagous and univoltine, with males showing strong site fidelity to individual host plants or clusters of plants used as [[Territorial Contest|mating territories]]. Males advertise with a 350-ms "rasp" call (about 150 complete leg strokes per second; principal frequencies 3–9 kHz). When a rival arrives, residents typically answer with a complex call of 1–60 "shucks" (25–35 ms each, 10–15 shucks per second) preceding and sometimes following the rasp. Interactions are antiphonal, with calling males approaching to within about 20 cm, and can last from 30 s to 40 min.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site.** Fieldwork was conducted at K-Bar, Brewster County, Texas, during July and August 1991 and 1992, at an elevation of 1100 m. *Flourensia cernua* was the dominant perennial plant. Two 30 × 40 m plots, separated by 100 m, were gridded with stake flags at 5-m intervals. Host plant density was about 1000 ha⁻¹, with approximately 25 adult males per plot. The shrub-to-male ratio was about 4:1. Shrub quality varies considerably and is linked to value as [[Female Rendezvous Site|female rendezvous sites]], which the authors use to explain why males defend shrubs so vigorously.

**Acoustic context.** Interactions were loud (65–70 dB at 1 m) and audible beyond 30 m, so the observers assumed they recorded nearly all contests. Background sounds were filtered with a high-pass filter at 2.7 kHz. Both frequency and amplitude degrade over distance, whereas temporal parameters degrade little, which the authors propose makes temporal cues more reliable for receivers (see [[Signal Reliability]]).

**Behavioral constraints.** Calling impairs hearing in several acridid grasshoppers, so the authors suggest that timing rules preventing call overlap may have evolved to ensure accurate assessment of rivals.

## Key Findings

**Settlement mode (Prediction 1 supported)**
- 86 interactions were recorded over 7 days of systematic observation (30 July–5 August).
- In 34 interactions, only one male produced shuck calls; that male always won, and the rival departed within 200 s.
- In 52 [[Antiphonal Encounter|antiphonal encounters]] (54 males), both males produced shuck calls. In 36, one male fell silent and departed (mean 58.2 s, SE = 10.5). The remaining 16 escalated to kicking, pushing, or grappling.
- Overall, 81% of interactions were settled without escalation. Several males took part in more than one contest, which reduces statistical power.
- Males that never produced shuck calls invariably lost.

**Size and residency (Prediction 2 partly supported)**
- In the 36 non-escalated antiphonal encounters, winners were larger than losers only 59% of the time (p = 0.21). Size did not predict winning overall.
- The hind femur length ratio of winners to losers was 1.02, and the coefficient of variation in femur length was just 5.3%, so size variation was low.
- The only size effect was in escalated contests where the larger male's length ratio exceeded 1.06: the larger male won 6 of 6 times (p = 0.02).
- Residents won 26 of 39 distinguishable antiphonal encounters (p = 0.02). Residency was therefore a better predictor than size.

**Call parameters as predictors (Prediction 2 supported)**
- In non-escalated contests, SPR (shuck production rate, the product of shuck call rate and shucks per call) predicted the winner in 83% of contests (p < 0.01, Holm-adjusted across 16 parameters). It was the only parameter significant after adjustment.
- The shucks-per-call component (SN) was the key driver of SPR, predicting outcomes with p = 0.02 unadjusted. Shuck call rate (SCR) was marginal (p = 0.07), and LASTSH was marginal (p = 0.06).
- Call amplitude (SA) did not predict outcome (p = 0.11), and weighting by SA did not improve SPR's predictive value.
- In escalated contests, SN predicted the winner in 12 of 16 contests (p = 0.04), and 75% of escalated outcomes were predictable from shuck number.
- Residents showed higher SPR than intruders in 70% of cases (p < 0.05).

**Reliability and RHP**
- SN and SCR were negatively correlated across all individuals, winners and losers alike (regression r = −0.43, df = 66; the text also reports r = −0.57, df = 32). The authors interpret this tradeoff as evidence that shuck production is energetically limited, so high SPR reflects energy reserves or motivation.
- Shuck output was not inversely related to SA.

**Matching and contest duration (Predictions 3 and 4 supported)**
- Contests with SPR, SN, or SA ratios below 1.25 lasted significantly longer than 200 s (p < 0.05).
- Escalation was rare when the SPR ratio exceeded 1.50 (1 of 16 contests; p < 0.01).
- Of contests lasting longer than 400 s, 8 of 10 escalated (p < 0.05).

**Assessment of signals (Prediction 5 supported)**
- Among contests with matched calling rates (ratio < 1.25), those with matched SN (ratio < 1.4) persisted beyond 500 s more often.
- Winners tended to have higher LASTSH values than losers, so a sudden drop in a loser's shuck number may signal its inability to keep up.
- The authors propose simple mechanisms: males tally rival calls and depart when the tally becomes lopsided, and they may phonorespond by matching rival shuck numbers.

**Playback experiments**
- *Call type* (12 males, five stimuli): calls containing shucks evoked significantly stronger responses than rasp-only calls. A synthetic 9.8-kHz buzz evoked responses comparable to natural rasps, suggesting limited discrimination of rasp characteristics.
- *Call overlap* (17 males, playback at 62 dB at 0.5 m): males responded more strongly to stimuli delivered 400 ms after their own calls than to stimuli delivered during calls (p < 0.05, G-test). A supplementary trial (8 males, undelayed stimuli for 4 min) showed no increase after 2 min, arguing against response latency as the explanation. Males therefore appear to perceive rivals poorly while calling.

**Implications and open questions**
- Ritualized acoustic signaling allows males to avoid costly escalation. The authors argue that bluffing is unlikely, because males seldom depart before hearing six or more rival shuck calls.
- Escalation is rarer than expected under injury-risk models. Only one injury (to a hindleg) was observed, which the authors find puzzling. Future work on fighting costs and territory benefits is needed.
- Reliance on temporal rather than amplitude or frequency cues is attributed to signal degradation over distance.
