# Middle-American Poeciliid Fishes of the Genus Xiphophorus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 revision by Donn Eric Rosen, published in the [[Bulletin of the Florida State Museum]], reappraises the live-bearing genus [[Xiphophorus]] (platyfishes and swordtails) by combining comparative morphology, distribution, ecology, genetics, cytology, embryology, endocrinology and ethology. The author recognizes 8 species and 11 subspecies, five of which are described as new (three subspecies, one species and one further subspecies). The genus is grouped into three phyletic lines: the [[Maculatus Species Group]] (platyfishes), the [[Montezumae Species Group]] and the [[Helleri Species Group]]. The paper proposes that the genus radiated from the Rio Panuco basin in northern Veracruz and eastern San Luis Potosi, and it reverses earlier generic splits by treating platyfishes and swordtails as one genus.

## Background & Description

The genus was historically split between *Xiphophorus* (swordtails) and *Platypoecilus* (platyfishes), largely on fin structure and dentition. [[Regan Classification Of Poeciliidae|Regan (1913)]] showed that both groups share biserial dentition and are closely related. [[Gordon And Rosen Genetic Studies|Gordon and Rosen (1951)]] found no reproductive barrier sufficient to separate them and recommended one generic name, which Rosen now extends to remove even subgeneric separation.

Diagnostic features used for the genus include a moderately deep, compressed body with large cycloid scales; a caudal "sword" in many males; a gonopodium with a distinctive claw on ray 5a, serrae on ray 4p and hooks on ray 3; 26 to 31 vertebrae; and 9 to 17 dorsal fin rays depending on species. The author notes that the tail patterns of [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]] and the gonopodial claw types distinguish the taxa.

Specimens were studied from UMMZ, LSU, USNM, SU, CNHM and ANSP collections, plus laboratory stocks descended from nine expeditions to Mexico and Central America (1932–1957) maintained at the [[New York Aquarium Genetics Laboratory]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat partition:** Platyfishes (*maculatus*, *variatus*, *couchianus*) are primarily lentic, living in sluggish or still clear to turbid water over mud among dense vegetation. Swordtails (*helleri*, *clemenciae*, *montezumae*, *pygmaeus*, *milleri*) are lotic, living in swiftly flowing streams and undercut banks.
- **Sympatry:** Where *maculatus* and *helleri* co-occur, they are often ecologically stratified. In the Rio Coatzacoalcos, sympatry was 62 percent of localities collected, compared with 40 percent in the Rio Papaloapan and 1 percent in the Rio Jamapa. Most sympatric records are considered transient, from swordtails washed downstream.
- **Rainfall and climate:** Annual rainfall is about 500 mm in the north and may reach 4500 mm in the Grijalva and Usumacinta region.
- **Dispersal limits:** Species showed little or no tolerance for salt or brackish water; in lab tests, no group survived more than one hour in the lowest seawater concentrations. Dispersal occurred mainly through coastal confluences, headwater transfer and flood-plain overflow.
- **Altitude and temperature:** Metameric counts (dorsal rays, lateral scales) co-vary with latitude and altitude, which the author links provisionally to temperature. Laboratory stocks retained their counts, suggesting a partly genetic basis.
- **Isolation barriers:** Swift waters of the Maya Mountains appear to have limited southward spread of *maculatus*. A cataract series near Necaxa at about 4000 feet isolates the subspecies *evelynae*.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic reorganization.** The genus is recognized as 8 species and 11 subspecies, compared with 7 species and 6 informal subspecies in previous classifications. The author states that the classification rests on 124 natural populations.
2. **New taxa described.** *Xiphophorus variatus evelynae* (Rio Tecolutla system, Puebla), *X. milleri* (Catemaco livebearer, Laguna Catemaco basin, Veracruz), *X. montezumae cortezi* (Rio Moctezuma, San Luis Potosi), *X. pygmaeus nigrensis* (Rio Choy, San Luis Potosi) and *X. helleri alvarezi* (Rio Santo Domingo, Chiapas).
3. **Regrouping of platyfish taxa.** *X. variatus xiphidium* is treated as a subspecies of *X. variatus*, not a separate species, because hybrids between them showed no significant gonopodial anomalies in 26 adult male hybrids.
4. **Green swordtail geography.** *X. helleri* forms a north-south morphocline. The author recognizes four subspecies: *helleri*, *strigatus*, *guentheri* and *alvarezi*. Dorsal fin ray means rise from 12.67 to 14.00 in the upper Rio Usumacinta and Peten populations.
5. **Maculatus tail patterns.** Seven basic micromelanophore tail patterns in *X. maculatus* are controlled by autosomal dominant epistatic multiple alleles (Gordon, 1947a, as cited).
6. **Gonopodial claw.** The claw on ray 5a is largest in *helleri*, intermediate in *pygmaeus* and smallest in *montezumae*. Two claw types are recognized in platyfishes, with type II (swordtail-type) claws in *maculatus* from the Rio Coatzacoalcos.
7. **Hybridization.** About 47 qualitatively different laboratory hybrids are reported across 6 of the 8 species, but none has been found in the field. Platyfish-swordtail hybrids show sterility, sex-ratio distortion and melanotic tumors linked to the *Sp* spotting gene of *maculatus*.
8. **Species groups.** The *maculatus* group (*couchianus*, *maculatus*, *variatus*) is treated as a superspecies pair with *couchianus* as an earlier-diverging platyfish; the *montezumae* group (*milleri*, *montezumae*, *pygmaeus*) is described as diffuse; the *helleri* group (*clemenciae*, *helleri*) is the most specialized.
9. **Proposed origin.** The author proposes a center of adaptive radiation in the Rio Panuco basin, with swordtails derived from a headwater stream prototype and platyfishes from a lowland lentic form.
10. **Allometry caveat.** Body proportion indices vary with size and growth, and the author notes that samples from the same locality collected on different days differed in proportions within a week, so only adult male indices are advised for comparisons.
