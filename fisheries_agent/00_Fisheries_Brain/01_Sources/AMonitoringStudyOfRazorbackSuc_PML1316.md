# Razorback Sucker Stocking and Monitoring, Lower Colorado River, June 1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This California Department of Fish and Game monthly report (June 1987) documents the stocking and monitoring of juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) into backwaters of the lower [[Colorado River]]. Juveniles reared at the [[Niland Fish Hatchery]] and at a [[Blythe Municipal Golf Course Pond]] were stocked at several sites in June 1987, totaling 151,904 fish. Post-release monitoring used minnow traps, a trammel-type net, seines, and an Oneida trap to track dispersal, predation risk, and survival. Some stocking sites were checked for the species only once, with no razorbacks captured. Individual recaptures and a larger unmarked fish were recorded. The report also covers the effects of a barrier-style structure in one backwater and nocturnal movement patterns.

## Background & Description

The study is a monitoring program for the reintroduction of the razorback sucker, a native catostomid of the Colorado River system, into its lower reaches. The report covers the June 1987 field season and the monitoring work of the consulting biologist, Dan Langhorst, Razorback Sucker Consultant.

Juvenile razorbacks were reared at the Niland facility and in local ponds. Stocked fish averaged about 140 mm (5.5 in) at release, with a range of 110–175 mm (4.4–6.9 in). Condition of the fish was reported as good. Transport mortality was reported as low, under 1%. The report lists a separate fish-handling event in which a left pelvic fin was clipped on a group of 165 mm fish for future identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling and monitoring.** The team used several methods to assess stocked fish and the risk from predators:

- **Minnow traps:** Twelve small and six medium [[Minnow Trap]]s were set randomly in brushy shoreline, cattail, and open-water habitats. Traps were set overnight and checked each morning.
- **Oneida trap:** One [[Oneida Trap]] was set overnight in the mid-backwater habitat (depth about 2 m / 6 ft).
- **Seining:** About 25 m (75 ft) of sandy shoreline were seined with a 15 m (50 ft) bag seine. Two razorbacks were caught.
- **Trammel net:** A [[Trammel Net]] (Net 1 and others) was set in backwater areas June 17–18, 1987.

**Predator assessment.** The Oneida trap catch in mid-backwater included eleven species, among them seven of an unclear sucker group, channel catfish, and largemouth bass. Stomach examination of channel catfish and largemouth bass (average 295 mm) found no razorback suckers. The report notes that predators examined were large enough to consume juvenile razorbacks, which were about 140 mm at stocking.

**Barrier structure and movement.** Before release in one backwater (the Arizona/California side), a series of barrier structures (about 44 in. tall, 150 ft. wide) was set to deter movement out of the backwater. The procedure replicated that used in May. Nocturnal movement was generally concentrated between 0400 and 0600. The movement pattern on the California side resembled that on the Arizona side, though catches were slightly lower.

**Other sites checked.**

- **Paz Lake** (about 5 mi north of Ehrenberg, AZ, on the Colorado River Indian Reservation): Five small and twelve medium minnow traps were set for two nights. No razorbacks were caught; only young-of-the-year carp were taken. This site had earlier stocking on March 11.
- **Oxbow Ponds:** One Oneida trap was set for two nights at a site stocked earlier, both in March and as juveniles. No razorbacks were caught. Catches included bluegill, threadfin shad, and largemouth bass. The report states it is unclear why no razorbacks were caught, noting that none of the fish captured were large enough to eat juvenile razorbacks.

## Key Findings

1. **Stocking scale:** A total of 151,904 juvenile razorback suckers were stocked in June 1987 at multiple backwater and canal sites along the lower Colorado River.
2. **Survival in transit:** Transport losses were low. One note in the report gives survival of 99% (5 mortalities) for a transported group.
3. **Recaptures:** One stocked fish was recaptured in Net 2 near the backwater–river confluence on substrate of sand and gravel. A second, unmarked, substantially larger fish was taken in Net 4 between 1000 and 1200. It was possibly a survivor of the previous year's stocking. The scanned length measurement is garbled.
4. **Seine and trap results:** Two razorbacks were caught by seine. No razorbacks were caught by minnow traps or the Oneida trap at the Paz Lake and Oxbow Pond sites.
5. **Predation risk:** Predatory fish captured in the mid-backwater habitat (channel catfish, largemouth bass) did not contain razorback suckers in their stomachs. Larger fish that could prey on juveniles were present.
6. **Movement behavior:** Released fish showed nocturnal movement, concentrated between 0400 and 0600, and movement patterns on the California side resembled those on the Arizona side.
7. **Monitoring effort:** Field work was reported at 40 hours per week during the June work weeks (June 1–5, 8–12, 15–19, and 22–26).
