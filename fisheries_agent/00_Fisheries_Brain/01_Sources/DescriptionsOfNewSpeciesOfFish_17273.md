# Descriptions of New Fishes from Texas, New Mexico and Sonora (1854), with Conrad's Rectification of Tertiary Fossil Shell Genera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1854 paper by [[Spencer Baird]] and [[Charles Girard]] (Second Part, Percoid and related groups) describes approximately 19 numbered new species of freshwater fishes collected in Texas, New Mexico and Sonora. Specimens were gathered by [[John H. Clark]] and [[Van Vliet|Captain Stewart Van Vliet]] during the [[United States-Mexico Boundary Survey]] and under Major W. H. Emory. The paper covers sunfishes ([[Pomotis]]), a cichlid ([[Herichthys]]), catfishes, characins, minnows, suckers and a Gila-group cyprinid assemblage. The document also contains a short appendix by [[Thomas A. Conrad]] that rectifies the nomenclature of several Tertiary fossil bivalve genera. The OCR text contains some garbled species names and counts, which are reported below only where legible.

## Background & Description

The fish descriptions follow a consistent 19th-century format. Each entry gives:

- A diagnostic description of body shape, head proportions, eye diameter, mouth position, and fin placement.
- A meristic formula covering dorsal (D), anal (A), caudal (C), ventral (V) and pectoral (P) fin rays, plus lateral line scale counts.
- Preserved colour in alcohol.
- The collection locality and collector.

Key diagnostic traits used across the paper include:

- **Head length** as a fraction of total length (e.g., one-fourth, one-fifth, two-sevenths).
- **Eye diameter** relative to head length (e.g., contained four to six times).
- **Position of the posterior maxillary tip** relative to the orbit, used to separate closely allied *Pomotis* species.
- **Fin tip reach**, such as how far pectoral or ventral fins extend toward the anal or vent.
- **Opercular flap colour and size**, a diagnostic feature in the sunfishes.

### Key Taxa Described

**Sunfishes ([[Centrarchidae]]; genus [[Pomotis]])**
- *Pomotis speciosus* (Brownsville, Texas; lateral line 43 scales). Body ovate, with a large subcircular black spot on the posterior soft dorsal.
- *Pomotis fallax* (Elm Creek, Texas; lateral line 39 scales). Body deeper, with a very large black opercular flap and bluish dots on the head.
- *Pomotis eros* (Rio Cibolo, Texas). Elevated spiny dorsal and very long pectorals. Uniform blackish brown with a black opercular flap bordered in white.
- A third *Pomotis* species from Rio Cibolo (name illegible in the scan), distinguished by its short tail peduncle and prolonged ventral ray.
- A further species from Rio Cibolo and Rio Salado, Texas, uniformly reddish brown with a small black posterior dorsal spot.

**Cichlid ([[Cichlidae]]; genus [[Herichthys]])**
- *Herichthys cyanoguttatus* (Brownsville, Texas; fresh water). Scales large, with 19–20 rows across the depth and 18 in the lateral line. Brownish with small bluish spots. The authors separated the genus from *Heros* of [[Heckel]] mainly by the simple, non-hooked anterior maxillary teeth and scale size.

**Catfishes ([[Ariidae]]; genus [[Ailurichthys]])**
- A species recorded at Indianola, Texas, confirmed as *Galeichthys* marinus (earlier described as *Silurus marinus* by Mitchill). The authors recommended retaining several *Galeichthys* species under this genus.
- *Arius* species from Indianola and the Rio Grande, with long maxillary barbels reaching the middle of the pectoral fins and a concave anal fin.
- A *Galeichthys*-like form from the Rio Frio and Rio Nueces, with a deeply cleft mouth and a black-clouded back.

**Characins ([[Characidae]]; genus [[Astyanax]])**
- *Astyanax arcexrarus* (upper tributaries of the Rio Nueces). Very compressed body, deeply forked caudal fin longer than the head, and a black basal caudal spot. Back deep reddish brown, sides silvery.

**Minnows ([[Cyprinidae]]; genus [[Carpiodes]] and related genera)**
- *Carpiodes concestes* (Rio Salado, Texas; 14 rows of scales across the body and about 46 in the lateral line).
- *Carpiodes crassus* (Rio Santa Cruz, Gila; about 20 rows of scales and 68–70 lateral line scales).
- A cyprinid from Rio Mimbres, tributary of the Gila, with about 80 lateral line scales and mottled colouration.
- A cyprinid from the San Pedro River (Rio San Pedro) of the Gila, with dark dots at each scale base.
- Several *Gila* species from the Gila basin, including a species from the Rio Santa Cruz and a species from the Rio Mimbres. These are distinguished by body depth and the length of the caudal fin.

**Suckers ([[Catostomidae]])**
- A species near Port Brown, Texas, with 13–14 rows of scales across the body depth and light white underside.
- A species from the Rio Grande in Texas, with a large eye and a low posterior caudal lobe.

**Rio Grande catfish ([[Ariidae]])**
- A catfish from Indianola is listed as a new record, not a new species.

### Conrad's Appendix: Tertiary Fossil Bivalve Genera

[[Thomas A. Conrad]] presents a short nomenclatural review of Tertiary fossil bivalves. Using the earliest valid authority, he rectified genus names for groups such as *Glycimeris* (Lamarck), *Leda* (Schumacher), *Mercenaria* (Schumacher) and others. He lists the species assigned to each genus, including many Conrad species such as *Glycimeris americana*, *Leda acuta*, and *Mercenaria staminea*. The appendix is taxonomic in nature and contains no ecological or collection data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fishes come from the Rio Grande and Gulf-slope drainages of Texas, the Rio Mimbres, and Gila basin rivers of New Mexico and Sonora. These habitats are fresh or nearly fresh rivers and creeks. The Indianola record represents a coastal, possibly brackish, environment. The text gives no quantitative habitat measurements (water chemistry, temperature, or flow). Several species were collected from tributaries of the same river (e.g., Rio Cibolo, Rio San Antonio), suggesting strong local endemism among the sunfishes and minnows.

Colour descriptions, such as dark dorsal surfaces and pale bellies, are based on alcohol-preserved specimens and are not field observations. The specimens reflect a survey effort in the 1850s, when the [[United States-Mexico Boundary Survey]] traversed the region.

## Key Findings

- **Approximately 19 numbered new fish species** are described from Texas, New Mexico and Sonora across several families, with the sunfishes ([[Pomotis]]) most fully treated.
- **Diagnostic traits** rely on head and eye proportions, the position of the maxillary tip, fin reach, lateral line scale counts, and opercular flap colour.
- ***Herichthys cyanoguttatus*** is distinguished from *Heros* by simple, non-hooked anterior maxillary teeth and by scale size.
- **Catfish nomenclature** was clarified: the Indianola species is retained in *Galeichthys* (or *Ailurichthys* as used by the authors), and several *Galeichthys* species were recommended for retention.
- **Gila basin cyprinids** (*Carpiodes*, *Gila*) were described from several rivers, with lateral line scale counts ranging from about 46 to about 80.
- **Specimens were collected** by [[John H. Clark]], [[Van Vliet|Captain Stewart Van Vliet]], Col. J. D. Graham and Major W. H. Emory during boundary survey work.
- **Conrad's appendix** provides a corrected nomenclature for multiple Tertiary fossil bivalve genera, including *Glycimeris*, *Leda* and *Mercenaria*, based on the earliest valid authorities.
- **Data limitations:** The OCR contains several garbled species names and lateral line counts (e.g., one *Pomotis* count rendered as "87"). These are not reported as definitive values, and some names are left unidentified.
