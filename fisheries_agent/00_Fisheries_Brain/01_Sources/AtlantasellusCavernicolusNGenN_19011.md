# Atlantasellus cavernicolus n. gen., n. sp. (Isopoda Asellota, Atlantasellidae n. fam.) from Bermuda

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atlantasellidae

*Note: this document describes a crustacean (isopod), not a fish. The tag line above follows the required format but the taxon is non-fish; the isopod family tag is the more accurate taxonomic marker.*

## Executive Summary

The paper by B. Sket (1979, *Biol. vestn.* Ljubljana 27(2): 175–183) describes *Atlantasellus cavernicolus*, a new genus and species of asellote isopod, and erects the new family [[Atlantasellidae]] for it. The animal was found as single specimens in the seawater anchialine pool of [[Walsingham Sink Cave]] on Bermuda. It is the only known marine member of the superfamily [[Aselloidea]], whose other families, [[Asellidae]] and [[Stenasellidae]], are almost entirely freshwater. The species combines a sphaeromatoid (volvating, roll-up) body form with aselloid pleopods, and lacks female pleopods I–II. The author concludes it is an independent phyletic line that retreated into a cave refuge, where conservative forms can persist.

## Background & Description

- **Discovery:** Found during cave fauna research on Bermuda with Dr. [[Thomas Iliffe]]; Iliffe supplied most samples. The first samples contained only females, and a single male later allowed the systematic placement to be settled.
- **Material:** Holotype male (1.1 mm) plus 5 females (one postmanca), collected September 1978 to April 1979.
- **Morphology:**
  - Body elongated, semi-cylindrical, unpigmented, translucent, and eyeless; able to roll into a ball ("volvation").
  - Antennae I flattened, short, and very broad, with four long aesthetascs; antennae II about twice as long, eight articles.
  - Pleonites I–II free and well developed; pleotelson elongated semicircular.
  - Pereiopods I prehensile; walking legs with two claws on the dactyl.
  - Female pleopods I–II absent; male pleopods II with a 1-articulate copulatory endopodite; pleopods III operculate but not fused; uropods vestigial.
- **Diagnosis of the family:** Aselloidea with sphaeromatoid habitus; male pleopods I 2-articulate; male pleopods II with sympodite, small exopodite and 1-articulate endopodite; female pleopods I–II missing; pleopods III operculate, not fused, without articulation lines; pleonites I–II free. Type genus: *Atlantasellus*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Steep bottom of the anchialine pool in Walsingham Sink Cave, connected to the sea. Salinity is almost or entirely marine.
- **Accompanying fauna** (listed as recorded): gastropods *Caecum* spp.; polychaetes *Laonice cirrata* and *Mesonerilla* sp.; copepods including *Pseudocyclops* sp., *Paramphiascella robinsoni* and Laophontidae; ostracod *Bairdia* sp.; isopod *Limnoria* sp.; amphipods *Ingolfiella* sp., *Eriopisa* sp. and *Bogidiella martini* ssp. Some are planktonic and some interstitial.
- **Origin:** The author suggests the species may be a cave-adapted relict or a direct immigrant from abyssal or interstitial waters.
- **Evolutionary context:** Most aselloids are freshwater. Continental waters, caves and other extreme habitats act as refuges for conservative types that cannot compete with newer marine lineages.
- **Adaptive features:** Volvation (rolling) is linked to the reshaped first antennae, the reduced uropods, and the non-specialised pereiopods. Volvation is common among marine isopods (e.g., Sphaeromatidae, Haploniscidae) but had not previously been recorded in Aselloidea.

## Key Findings

- *Atlantasellus cavernicolus* is the first marine aselloid known and is placed in a new family, Atlantasellidae, within [[Aselloidea]].
- The family is diagnosed by the absence of female pleopods I–II and by unfused, inarticulate pleopods III, distinguishing it from [[Asellidae]] and [[Stenasellidae]].
- Volvation and the sphaeromatoid habitus are adaptive, probably related to movement and body protection; they are not conclusive for phylogeny.
- Well-developed, free pleonites I–II are a primitive trait shared with Stenasellidae.
- The absence of female pleopods II is an apomorphic character of high phylogenetic weight.
- Pleopods III are unique within the superfamily and represent another route toward covering breathing structures.
- The author concludes that *Atlantasellus* is an independent phyletic line developed towards a sphaeromatoid "Lebensform," and that its cave occurrence is explained by refuge theory.
