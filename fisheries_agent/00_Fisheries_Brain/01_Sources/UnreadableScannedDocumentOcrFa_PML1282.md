# Notes on a Collection of Fishes Obtained in the Gila River, at Fort Thomas, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic note, written by [[Philip H. Kirsch]] and published in the Proceedings of the [[United States National Museum]] (dated [[Indiana University]], April 16, 1889), reports a collection of fishes from the [[Gila River]] at [[Fort Thomas]], [[Arizona Territory]]. The specimens were gathered by [[Lieut. W. L. Carpenter]], U.S. Army, and sent to the museum of the University of Indiana on June 4, 1887. The collection covers seven taxa: four [[Catostomus]] suckers, one [[Syrauchen cypho]] (described as a new genus), [[Ptychochilus lucius]], and [[Gila emorii]]. The note supplies morphological descriptions, meristic counts, colour notes, and Carpenter's field observations on feeding and growth. It also proposes one new species and one new genus.

## Background & Description

The paper is a list of fishes collected by Carpenter and sent with his notes on habits and local names. Carpenter held that only these seven species occur in the Gila River at Fort Thomas. He also judged that several species previously described from the stream are not really distinct. He noted that [[Gila emorii]] and [[Ptychochilus lucius]] change in appearance with growth stage, so specimens at different ages may have been mistaken for separate species.

Taxa and specimen notes:

- **[[Catostomus latipinnis]]**: one specimen, 19 inches long. It matches the [[Jordan & Gilbert Synopsis]] description except for a longer preorbital bone (more than twice as long as deep).
- **[[Catostomus gila]]** (new species, described by Kirsch): three specimens, 12 to 14 inches. Closely allied to *C. ardens*. Differs in body compression, shorter and more compressed caudal peduncle, a shorter and higher dorsal fin, and a lower lip divided into two fleshy lobes. Meristics recorded include D. 11–12 and A. 7. Colour is dark brown above, yellow below. One type was deposited in the [[U.S. National Museum]].
- **[[Catostomus insignis]]**: one specimen, about 12 inches (OCR-garbled in source). Notes on large back scales and very small belly scales.
- **[[Catostomus clarki]]**: one specimen, 12 inches. Scale arrangement resembles *C. insignis*; 68 scales in the lateral line.
- **[[Syrauchen cypho]]** (originally *Catostomus cypho* Lockington; "Buffalo Fish"): Kirsch and Eigenmann erected the genus *Syrauchen* for it. The defining feature is a large, sharp-edged hump behind the occiput, formed by enlarged anterior [[Interneural Bones]]. The paper gives a detailed osteological account and external description, including a single row of more than 35 pharyngeal teeth, an intestine about 5.5 times body length, and a black peritoneum.
- **[[Ptychochilus lucius]]** ("Gila Salmon"): one specimen, 15 inches. Agrees broadly with the Jordan & Gilbert description. Peritoneum silvery; vertebrae counted at about 45–46.
- **[[Gila emorii]]** ("Gila Trout; Bony Tail"): six specimens of small size. Differences from the published description include a maxillary that does not reach the front of the orbit in most individuals. Colour is dark back, light sides, silvery belly. Pharyngeal teeth are falciform with grinding surfaces. Vertebrae 42–45.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Carpenter's field notes describe the feeding ecology and life history of the Gila fishes:

- **Diet**: *Gila emorii* feeds almost entirely on gastropods and caddisfly larvae, which it crushes with strong pharyngeal teeth.
- **Seasonal change**: the pharyngeal grinding surface wears down in winter because of reduced feeding, so the species "might thus have been described as without grinding surface." Large specimens in November show little grinding surface.
- **Growth-stage variation**: young fish differ strongly from adults. Cranial depression appears only around the second year. A 4-inch specimen had an undepressed cranium and a different eye-to-head proportion.
- **Hardiness**: Carpenter reports that *G. emorii* survived several hours out of water and revived after drying and stiffening. The species takes hooks readily, so it is angler-accessible.
- **Habitat**: the fishes come from the Gila River, a desert-region freshwater system in Arizona Territory. The paper gives no water-chemistry, flow, or habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

1. **Seven taxa** were recorded from the Gila River at Fort Thomas, according to Carpenter. He considered several previously described species to be synonyms.
2. **One new species** is proposed: [[Catostomus gila]] (three specimens, 12–14 inches). It is distinguished from *C. ardens* by body shape, fin proportions, and lip structure.
3. **One new genus** is established: [[Syrauchen]] (type species *S. cypho*), based on an enlarged interneural hump and related skeletal features.
4. **Growth-related variation** in [[Gila emorii]] and [[Ptychochilus lucius]] is documented. It is the stated reason for Carpenter's doubt about several species names.
5. **Trophic specialization**: *Gila emorii* is a molluscivore and larval-insect feeder whose pharyngeal grinding surface depends on season.
6. **Museum deposition**: a typical specimen of *Catostomus gila* was sent to the [[U.S. National Museum]], and the rest of the collection went to the [[Indiana University]] museum.
