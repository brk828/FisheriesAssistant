# Glen Canyon Flood Experiment Planning Memo and Research/Monitoring Outline (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #riverine-ecosystem

## Executive Summary

This planning memo, issued by Duncan Patten of the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies on March 24, 1994, transmits the outputs of a planning workshop for a controlled high-flow release from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] into the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. The team's goal is a rough draft of the research and monitoring plan within two weeks. The material covers the proposal outline, writing assignments, working hypotheses, flood design, subhypotheses, and field logistics for nine study areas.

The main research questions concern sediment deposition and storage in eddies, riparian and wetland vegetation response, export of organic matter, reach-scale nutrient changes, and effects on young fish distribution and [[Lake Powell]]. The design centres on a roughly 45,000 cfs flood, with the central prediction that it will cause measurable, predictable changes in eddy sand storage, sandbar morphology, and mainstem sediment transport, but no long-term effect on the riverine ecosystem.

The document is a planning draft rather than a research report. It contains no measured results.

## Background & Description

**Document type and purpose.** The memo is an internal correspondence from the planning team coordinator. It transmits flip-chart notes and a draft structure for a "Flood Flow Research/Monitoring" plan. Patten notes that the material has no obvious order, and asks participants to coordinate overlapping writing assignments by phone.

**Proposal outline.** The outline is organised around process response measurements and hypotheses:

- **A. Sediment deposition** in relation to sediment supply across four river segments, including counts and sizes of eddy bars, beaches and debris fans; detailed dynamics in selected eddies; and net sediment flux by segment.
- **B. Riparian/wetland vegetation dynamics** (disturbance and recovery), including marsh and beach vegetation before and after the flood, vegetation–hydraulics interactions at eddy sites, and changes in plant community character with geomorphic change.
- **C. Reach-scale export of organic matter**, including composition, quantity and profiles in [[Lake Powell]].
- **D. Reach-scale changes in nutrients** at three sites (Dam, Grand Canyon, Diamond), covering nutrients, phosphorus, dissolved organic matter and hydrolab measurements.

**Writing assignments.** Assignments are split among team members: Patten (introduction, riparian/wetland), Schmidt (sediment, flood design), Jackson (general working hypothesis), Stanford (organic matter), Stevens (riparian/wetland), Minckley ("young" fish distribution), Marzolf ([[Lake Powell]]), Andrews (sediment), and Wegner (logistics).

**Flood design (as printed).** The preferred period is April 1–15. The planned sequence comprises:

- 4 pre-flood days at about 8,000 cfs;
- 7–8 flood days at about 45,000 cfs (one entry lists 52,000 cfs);
- 5 post-flood days at about 8,000 cfs;
- an up-ramp of 2,500 cfs and a down-ramp of 1,500 cfs.

The text reports a total volume of "794,227 ac-ft", alongside a figure of 600,000 af for a related period. Scan quality makes these figures ambiguous, so they should be checked against the original before citing. See [[Flood Design]].

**Survey design.** Measurements are classified as before, during, or after the flood. The "during" category is set out as a planning matrix for [[Eddy Bars]], [[Return Channels]], [[Riparian Vegetation]], [[Drift]], and [[Nutrient Dynamics]]. The matrix also covers [[Young Fish Distribution]] (seine trips) and Lake Powell forebay profiles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sediment and geomorphology.** The eddy, beach and return-channel scale is central to the design. Measurements include deposition surveys at seven eddies (30 topographic surveys), beach photo mapping, bed deposition on 2–4 mile reaches with cross-sections, sediment transport at five gages, and a water-travel dye study. The team sought to test whether sediment supply is sufficient to build bars and return channels in critical reaches. Stated uncertainties include [[Hydraulic Roughness]] and the magnitude and duration of floods needed to build a significant number of bars.

**Vegetation.** Marsh changes are to be tracked at one intensive site using aerial photographs, with detailed sites at eddies. The team considered whether the flood would scour marshes and whether changes to the vegetation would be favourable.

**Organic matter, nutrients and drift.** Organic matter export is assessed at reach scale. Drift is sampled with drift nets at five gages and with plankton nets at several sites above and below the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence. Water chemistry (P, N, DOM, hydrolab) is measured at three sites.

**Biota.** The planning team included fish, snail, chub, trout and flycatcher groups in the measurement scheme. Young fish distribution is assessed by seining, with trips scheduled during the study months. The document gives no species-level detail, and no species is named in the text beyond these general groups.

**Study areas.** The memo describes nine areas:

- **Area 1:** Lees Ferry gage to Badger Rapids (USGS-trained crew, cableway sediment transport, flow pattern maps, NAU beach survey with bathymetry).
- **Area 2:** South Canyon to 36 Mile Rapid (eddy dynamics crew, Nautiloid site).
- **Area 3:** Eminence to Saddle, including Anasazi Bridge and Triple Alcoves, with vegetation characterisation at Eminence.
- **Area 4:** [[Fifty-Five Mile Marsh]] (vegetation stability and hydraulics interactions, daily hydrographic measurements, acoustic Doppler studies).
- **Area 5:** Above the [[Little Colorado River]] to Chuar Rapid (carbon site, USGS bathymetry at monumental cross-sections, RM 61–65).
- **Areas 6, 8 and 9:** Grand Canyon gage, National gage and [[Diamond Creek]] gage (USGS-trained crew, water quality, drift, beach and bathymetry surveys at about 25 river miles).
- **Area 7 (Blacktail to Forster):** eddy dynamics crew at the 122-Mile Creek site.

Several survey sites are assigned to NAU beach surveys, and some are marked as overlapping with USGS work.

## Key Findings

The document presents working hypotheses, assumptions and expected outcomes rather than measured results.

1. **Main hypothesis.** The 45,000 cfs flood will have no long-term effect on the Grand Canyon riverine ecosystem. It will cause measurable and predictable changes in (a) sand storage in eddy zones, (b) sandbar morphology, and (c) mainstem sediment transport and storage. These changes are expected to affect other parameters.

2. **Expected null effects.** The team predicted no flood effect on riparian vegetation cover, species composition, recruitment, marsh extent, weedy species, woody vegetation, or the quantity and quality of drift.

3. **Subhypotheses.** These include no change in the number and size of eddy bars or backwater return channels at 8,000 cfs and at 20,000–25,000 cfs, and no change in debris fan shape and size. Other subhypotheses predict that sediment mass-balance calculations, empirical mapping and eddy deposition models will successfully predict deposition and erosion, and that seepage-stress and groundwater models will predict post-flood erosion rates and stable profiles.

4. **Working assumptions.** The team assumed that there is sufficient sediment in storage to be elevated by the flood. It also asked whether scouring marshes is desirable. Two stated goals were not to adversely affect native fish or other biota, and to learn more about sediment transport.

5. **Long-term objective.** The flip-chart notes list additional data needs for a global model (hydraulic roughness) and an eddy model (mainstem concentration and bed aggradation, return-channel characteristics), plus testing of mapping validity.

6. **Open questions.** The team left several items open: the magnitude and duration of floods needed to construct a significant number of bars and return channels in critical reaches; the antecedent sediment storage required; whether gage sites and crews can be used; and what measures to use for primary productivity and recreation.

7. **Next steps.** The team agreed on a rough draft within two weeks, to be distributed on disk and hard copy. Writing groups are to coordinate by phone. The document has no conclusions beyond these planning steps.
