# Draft Final Rule to Designate a Reintroduced Population of Colorado Squawfish as Nonessential Experimental

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #conservation-policy #ColoradoSquawfish

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal 1993 [[USFWS]] Region 2 memorandum, not a primary biological research paper, addressing the regulatory status of a proposed reintroduction of [[Colorado Squawfish]] into the [[Colorado River]] below Headgate Rock Dam near Parker, Arizona. The core issue is whether to finalize or withdraw a draft rule designating this reintroduced population as "nonessential experimental" under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The memo outlines administrative, legal, and biological obstacles that stalled the program, including conflicts with refuge management requirements, overlap with newly-listed [[Razorback Sucker]] populations, pending critical habitat designations for "four species of big river fishes," and insufficient hatchery production of sub-adult squawfish for stocking. No direct morphological, physiological, or ecological field data on the species is presented in the source text; the content is policy/administrative in nature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historic/Target Range**: The proposed reintroduction site is the [[Colorado River]] downstream of Headgate Rock Dam (Parker, Arizona), part of the lower Colorado River basin.
- **Comparable Precedent**: The action was modeled on similar nonessential experimental population designations previously implemented on the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]].
- **Sympatric Species of Concern**: Isolated populations of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus) occur throughout the lower basin in the same reintroduction area, creating regulatory overlap since these existing populations retain full ESA protection.
- **Habitat Designation Context**: Critical habitat was concurrently being proposed for "four species of big river fishes" (likely including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]]), indicating the species' niche within a guild of large-river endemic fishes native to the Colorado River system.
- **Management Complication**: Where reintroduced populations occur on National Wildlife Refuges or National Parks, the ESA requires retained "threatened" status protections, complicating any state-led sport fishery management goals, since a formal management plan would be required to allow the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to manage the population for angling.

## Key Findings
- The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] had initiated, but not completed, a rule to establish a **nonessential experimental population** of [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the lower Colorado River basin near Parker, Arizona.
- The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) supported the reintroduction, motivated by the potential to establish a **trophy sport fishery** for Colorado squawfish in the lower basin; AGFD's Fisheries Division continued to favor this program as of the memo's writing.
- Four primary complications stalled the rule's finalization:
  1. **ESA protection conflict**: Populations on refuges/parks must retain threatened-species protections, requiring a management plan before state-run sport fishing could occur.
  2. **Razorback Sucker listing**: The endangered listing of [[Razorback Sucker]] created overlapping protected populations in the same target area.
  3. **Pending critical habitat**: Critical habitat proposals for four big-river fish species added regulatory complexity to the reintroduction site.
  4. **Hatchery production shortfall**: Insufficient quantities and sizes of sub-adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] had been produced to support a viable reintroduction effort.
- The memorandum (dated 1993, signed by James A. Young, Regional Director) requested a formal recommendation from the Arizona State Supervisor of Ecological Services on whether to **proceed with the rule** or **initiate its withdrawal**, with a response deadline of August 6, 1993.
- This document illustrates the **administrative and interagency friction** common in multi-species endangered fish recovery efforts within shared river systems, particularly when sport fishery interests intersect with strict ESA mandates for co-occurring listed species.
