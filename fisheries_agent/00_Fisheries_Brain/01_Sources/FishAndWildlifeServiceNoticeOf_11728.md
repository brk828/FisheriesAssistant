# Federal Register Notice of Endangered Species Permit Applications August 1995

#crocodilian #conservation #endangered-species-act #Crocodylidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice announces receipt of multiple permit applications under Section 10(c) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 for activities involving endangered species. Among the applications, the [[University of California]] La Jolla Department of Biology requested permits for genetic research on several species, including [[Siamese Crocodile]] (*Crocodylus siamensis*), [[Saltwater Crocodile]] (*Crocodylus porosus*), and their hybrids from ranched populations in [[Thailand]]. Additional applications covered various endangered species including primates, elephants, tortoises, and plants for purposes of research, propagation, and conservation.

## Background & Description

The notice was published in the Federal Register Vol. 60, No. 169 on Thursday, August 31, 1995, by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Office of Management Authority. The document lists permit applications from various institutions and individuals seeking authorization to import, export, or conduct activities with endangered species under federal law.

Application PRT-805772 specifically addressed crocodilian research: the [[University of California]] La Jolla Department of Biology requested a permit to import blood samples from ranched populations of [[Siamese Crocodile]] (*Crocodylus siamensis*), [[Saltwater Crocodile]] (*Crocodylus porosus*), and Siamese/saltwater crocodile hybrids (*C. porosus* X *C. siamensis*) from Dr. Ratanakorn at [[Kasetsart University]], Bangkok, [[Thailand]]. The stated purpose was genetic research in support of international conservation efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The applications reflect conservation efforts for endangered species through various approaches including genetic research, captive breeding programs, and scientific study. The crocodile research specifically targeted ranched populations, representing a managed conservation approach where crocodilians are raised in controlled conditions. [[Ranching]] operations can support both conservation and sustainable use objectives for endangered crocodilian species.

The [[Siamese Crocodile]] is critically endangered in the wild, while [[Saltwater Crocodile]] populations vary by region. Genetic research on these species and their hybrids can provide important information for conservation management, population genetics, and breeding program guidance.
