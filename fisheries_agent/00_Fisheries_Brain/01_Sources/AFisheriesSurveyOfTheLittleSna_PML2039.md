# Fisheries Survey of the Little Snake River Drainage, Carbon County, Wyoming (1986–1987)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Wyoming Game and Fish Department administrative report (Oberholtzer, May 1987) revises a 1954–1955 inventory of the [[Little Snake River]] Drainage in southwestern Carbon County. It consolidates 1986 field work with data from water-development evaluations to cover 126 streams, compared with 46 in the original survey. The drainage is about 725 stream miles, of which 342 miles are judged capable of supporting trout. Management priorities centre on the declining [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]], threatened by [[Brook Trout]] competition, sedimentation, livestock grazing, and proposed [[Stage II and Stage III Water Development]] projects.

## Background & Description

- **Scope and authorship:** Data were collected by Wyoming Game and Fish Department staff (with U.S. Forest Service, Bureau of Land Management and University of Wyoming contributors) from about 1976 onward. Project number 5086-01-8501.
- **Setting:** The Little Snake River originates near the Wyoming–Colorado state line, flows about 55 miles west, and enters the [[Yampa River]] in Colorado. The drainage covers roughly 3,102 square miles, about 2,420 in Wyoming. Elevations range from 6,515 feet at the stateline to 10,515 feet on the [[Continental Divide]].
- **Land use:** Livestock grazing is widespread, with heavy overgrazing along many streams. Timber harvest is at a low level, and coal, oil and gas exploration in the [[Muddy Creek]] area is currently slowed by a depressed energy market. Most of the river corridor is private land.
- **Management context:** A 1985 request for a Colorado Squawfish study was conducted in August–September 1986. Its results are reported separately.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Main-stem sections show extensive bank erosion, debris accumulation after the 1984 flood (peak flow about 13,000 cfs), and summer flows that become marginal for trout. Muddy Creek is deeply headcut, with turbidity from clay soils, and has 10% (about 7 miles) of its length suitable for trout. Savery Creek shows heavy siltation of spawning gravels.
- **Water quality:** Generally good, with pH mostly 7.0–8.9 (exceptions: pH 5.0 in a small intermittent stream and 6.6–6.9 in the upper [[Roaring Fork]] and parts of the [[North Fork Little Snake River]]). Alkalinity ranged from 17.1 to 459 ppm. Copper pollution from the [[Ferris-Haggerty Mine]] is a major concern in Haggerty Creek, where copper concentrations were reduced from about 3.0 to 0.5 µg/l, still above the 0.15 mg/l state limit.
- **Native and competing species:** Colorado River cutthroat trout remain in isolated populations. Brook trout are displacing them in Big Sandstone Creek and Douglas Creek, where cutthroat fell to zero in a 1986 sample.
- **Species present:** 18 species are recorded in Appendix A, including the trout species above and nongame fishes such as flannelmouth sucker, white sucker, bluehead sucker, roundtail chub, bonneville redside shiner, speckled dace, creek chub, and carp.
- **Fishing:** Pressure is about 3,000 angler days per year, concentrated near campgrounds in the Battle Creek Drainage. The drainage is open year-round except the North Fork closure. Creel limit is six trout per day, with one over 20 inches.

## Key Findings

- **Upper main stem (Section I and II):** Rainbow trout density was 9 fish/mile in Section I and 115 fish/mile in Section II. Mountain whitefish were 242 fish/mile in Section I and 1,215 fish/mile in Section II, with 90% under 9 inches, suggesting poor recruitment or spawning upstream. Roundtail chub density was 536 fish/mile in Section I.
- **Rainbow trout recruitment:** Length-frequency data indicate no natural recruitment; populations are maintained by Colorado Division of Wildlife stocking upstream. Stocking in Wyoming was discontinued due to low flows.
- **Colorado River cutthroat trout:** The North Fork Little Snake River holds the largest known essentially pure population in its native range. Densities ranged from 121 to 840 fish/mile across 25+ estimates. A barrier built in 1977 is apparently working.
- **Stage II construction impacts:** Poor stream-crossing practices and erosion control caused sediment washouts. Deadman Creek lost 92% of standing stock after a 1986 washout. Nearly one-third of the Stage II project remained to be built in 1987.
- **Recommendations (summary):**
  1. Implement the Colorado River Cutthroat Trout Management and Enhancement Plan on schedule.
  2. Support BLM Habitat Management Plans for Baggs and Savery Creek (including Dirtyman and Hell Canyon creeks).
  3. Continue annual stocking of 2,900 catchable rainbow trout and 15,000 fingerlings (Little Robbers Gulch Reservoir), at specified sites.
  4. Stock about 30,000 fingerling channel catfish annually in the Little Snake River from Dixon to the stateline, to be evaluated after 3–4 years.
  5. Stock surplus brook and/or rainbow trout in Mackarty Creek, Little Savery Creek, Savery Creek, North Fork Savery Creek and upper Muddy Creek.
  6. Evaluate timber sales and monitor road construction for erosion control, and review [[Wyoming Water Development Commission]] proposals so fishery concerns are included.
