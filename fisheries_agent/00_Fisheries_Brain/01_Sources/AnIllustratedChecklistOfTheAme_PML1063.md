# An Illustrated Checklist of the American Crayfishes (Decapoda: Astacidae, Cambaridae, and Parastacidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #crayfish #decapoda #cambaridae

## Executive Summary

This document is a taxonomic checklist, not a fish study. It updates Hobbs's 1974 checklist of North and Middle American crayfishes and extends it to the South American Parastacidae. The revision incorporates about seventy species described since 1974, updates synonymies to 1 October 1987, and adds species described up to 1 January 1989 in Appendix 3. Each taxon is given a synonymy, type data, type locality, range and habitat. The checklist covers three families, with Cambaridae comprising most of the taxa. It also lists nomina nuda, suppressed names, species inquirenda, and provides distribution appendices by country and state or province.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Revise the 1974 checklist after the revisions of *Cambarellus* (Fitzpatrick, 1983a) and *Orconectes* (in progress by Fitzpatrick), and the South American work of Buckup and Rossi (1980).
- **Scope:** Three families: Astacidae (Holarctic, with Pacifastacus as the main American genus), Cambaridae (the largest group, with many subfamilies and genera), and Parastacidae (South American, with Parastacus and Samastacus).
- **Format:** Each species has a synonymy, type designations (holotype, allotype, morphotype, syntypes), type locality, range and habitat. Burrowing behaviour follows Hobbs (1942): primary, secondary and tertiary burrowers.
- **Type repositories:** Type collections are listed in institutional abbreviations (e.g., USNM, MCZ, BMNH, ANSP), reflecting holdings in more than 60 museums.
- **Scope of the checklist:** The author notes that the Chilean review by Buckup remained unpublished at the time, and that about 70 new species were described after 1974.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Burrowing ecology:** Species are classed as primary (largely restricted to burrows), secondary (usually in burrows but move into open water during wet periods), or tertiary (burrow only in part of the life cycle).
- **Habitats recorded:** Streams, lakes, ponds, swamps, ditches, sloughs, springs, seepage areas, caves and subterranean waters, and fossil lake deposits (*Procambarus rodriguezi*; *Procambarus* from Eocene Green River beds).
- **Distribution:** Range entries cover states and provinces of the United States, Canada and Mexico, plus Cuba, Central American nations, Chile, Argentina, Brazil and Uruguay. Several species have been introduced (for example, *Procambarus clarkii* now occurs in more than 20 countries, and *Pacifastacus leniusculus* has been introduced into Japan and Europe).
- **Cave fauna:** Many troglobitic species (e.g., *Cambarus* spp., *Procambarus* spp. in Florida, Oaxaca, Veracruz) are known from single caves or small karst areas.
- **Conservation notes:** Several species are known from only one locality (e.g., *Cambarellus ceotlascos*-type localities; *Orconectes* from the Lower Ohio), and the status of several *Pacifastacus* taxa is considered likely extinct (*P. nigrescens*) or uncertain.

## Key Findings

- **Checklist size:** The revised checklist includes the three families with species listed in alphabetical Appendix 1 (about 380 entries, including subspecies and new species listed in Appendix 3). Appendix 2 lists the countries and states/provinces and their crayfish faunas. The Cambaridae are the dominant family.
- **Nomenclatural changes:** Several names were newly synonymized or validated. Two nomina nuda (*Cambarus scotti* Pearse, 1941 and *Cambarus kanthus* [sic] Pearse, 1945) were associated with established taxa. *Astacus fossor* Rafinesque and *Astacus oregonus* Randall were suppressed by the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature.
- **Species inquirenda:** *Procambarus wiegmanni* (Erichson, 1846) from Mexico is known only from the original description; the type was lost from BMNH and the identity remains uncertain.
- **Fossil taxa:** Two fossil species are included (*Procambarus primaevus* from the Miocene of Wyoming; *Pacifastacus chenoderma* from Idaho and Oregon deposits).
- **South American parastacids:** The genus *Parastacus* is reviewed with species such as *P. brasiliensis*, *P. defossus*, *P. laevigatus* (described by Buckup and Rossi, 1980), *P. pugnax* and *P. varicosa*. The genus *Samastacus* (Riek, 1971) is included.
- **Additional descriptions:** Appendix 3 lists species described in 1987 and 1988, including *Cambarus (Lacunicambarus) aculabrum* Hobbs and Brown, *Cambarus (Erebicambarus) maculatus* Hobbs and Pflieger, *Fallicambarus (Fallicambarus) devastator* Hobbs and Whiteman, and *Procambarus (Austrocambarus) olmecorum* Hobbs.
- **Author's acknowledgments:** Contributions from museum curators (e.g., Douglas G. Smith, Jacques Forest) and Fitzpatrick were central to the revision.
