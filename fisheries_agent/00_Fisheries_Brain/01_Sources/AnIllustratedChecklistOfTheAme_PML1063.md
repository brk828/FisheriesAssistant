# An Illustrated Checklist of the American Crayfishes (Decapoda: Astacidae, Cambaridae, and Parastacidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #crayfish #decapoda #cambaridae

*Note on scope: the source is a crustacean taxonomic checklist, not a fish study. The tags above use the taxon-appropriate crayfish/decapod labels in place of a fish family.*

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic work by Horton H. Hobbs, Jr. (Smithsonian Institution, National Museum of Natural History) updates the 1974 checklist of North and Middle American crayfishes and extends it to South American parastacids. It covers all American crayfishes, including two fossil species, described before 1 January 1987, with synonymies updated to 1 October 1987 and new taxa through 1 January 1989 listed in Appendix 3. The checklist records type collections, type localities, ranges and habitats for each taxon, and documents nomenclatural changes at family, subfamily, genus and subgenus level. The main outcomes are the reorganization of the family structure, the proposed [[Cambaridae]] family-level arrangement, and many resolved synonymies, including several [[Nomina Nuda]] tied to established taxa.

## Background & Description

**Scope and rationale.** The revision was prompted by roughly seventy species described since the 1974 checklist, by a revision of [[Cambarellus]] (Fitzpatrick, 1983a), and by ongoing work on [[Orconectes]] (Fitzpatrick, 1987a). South American crayfishes had been incompletely summarized since Faxon (1914); Riek (1971) and Buckup and Rossi (1980) provided the main recent treatments.

**Format.** For each taxon the checklist gives a synonymy (including erroneous spellings), citations to recent descriptions and keys, type series and depository, type locality, range and habitat. Burrowing behaviour follows Hobbs (1942b): primary burrowers are largely confined to burrows, secondary burrowers wander into open water during breeding, and tertiary burrowers use burrows during drought.

**Institutional abbreviations.** The source lists about 45 museum and collection codes (e.g., [[United States National Museum]] (USNM), Museum of Comparative Zoology (MCZ), Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia (ANSP), Museum National d'Histoire Naturelle (MHNP)).

**Taxonomic structure adopted (summary).**
- Superfamily Nephropoidea (lobsters and marine allies) and Superfamily Astacoidea: Family Astacidae (genus [[Pacifastacus]], subgenera Hobbsastacus and Pacifastacus).
- Family Cambaridae, with subfamilies Cambarellinae (genus [[Cambarellus]], 3 subgenera) and Cambarinae (genera Barbicambarus, Bouchardina, [[Cambarus]], Distocambarus, Fallicambarus, Faxonella, Hobbseus, [[Orconectes]], [[Procambarus]], Troglocambarus).
- Superfamily Parastacoidea: Family Parastacidae (genera Parastacus and Samastacus).
- Cambarus has the most subgenera (about 11 in the synopsis); Orconectes has 10 and Procambarus has 16 subgenera recognized here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitats.** Taxa occupy streams (often riffle areas), lakes, ponds, swamps, roadside ditches, springs, seepage areas and subterranean waters. Many are restricted to caves or cave streams, such as [[Cambarus]] (Jugicambarus) setosus and C. (J.) tartarus.
- **Burrowing.** Numerous species are primary burrowers (e.g., [[Cambarus]] (Depressicambarus) species and [[Fallicambarus]] species), often living in roadside ditches and seepage areas.
- **Range and endemism.** Many species are known from a single locality or a few localities (e.g., [[Cambarellus]] zempoalensis at Lagunas de Zempoala, Morelos, Mexico; [[Cambarellus]] prolixus at Lago de Chapala, Jalisco). Several are described as possibly extinct, such as [[Pacifastacus]] nigrescens, reportedly destroyed in the Chicago fire of 1871 (type not extant).
- **Introductions.** Recorded introductions include [[Pacifastacus]] leniusculus to Sweden and Japan, [[Cambarellus]] shufeldtii into parts of Louisiana and Georgia, [[Orconectes]] virilis into California, Utah and other areas, and [[Orconectes]] (Gremicambarus) causeyi-like populations in several states.
- **Fossils.** Two fossil species are included, including [[Pacifastacus]] chenoderma from Miocene and Pliocene(?) deposits in Idaho and Oregon.
- **Species inquirenda.** [[Procambarus]] wiegmanni is known only from a lost type from Mexico; its range and habitat are unknown.

## Key Findings

- **Checklist scale.** The 1974 checklist recognized 294 species and subspecies; this revision reconciles them with later descriptions and synonymies, and the body covers families Astacidae, Cambaridae and Parastacidae.
- **Genus-level history.** Cambarus (Erichson, 1846) was the standard genus from Girard (1852) and Hagen (1870) until Hobbs (1942a) elevated Ortmann's subgenera to genera, recognizing Orconectes (Cope, 1872) as senior to Faxonius, which was later restored as a subgenus.
- **Family reorganisation.** Hobbs (1974a) elevated Cambarinae to family rank as Cambaridae, including Cambaroidinae and Cambarellinae within it.
- **Parastacid genera.** South American parastacids are placed in Parastacus and Samastacus (Riek, 1971), and the most recent additions by Buckup and Rossi (1980) resurrected several previously synonymized species.
- **Nomina nuda resolved.** Through J.F. Fitzpatrick, Jr., many Lyle (1938) nomina nuda were associated with established taxa. Two remain unresolved: Cambarus schmittii Penn, 1941 (questionably synonymized with [[Cambarellus]] puer) and Cambarus lucifugus Packard, 1888 (a lapsus for an amphipod, Crangonyx lucifugus).
- **Suppressed names.** Astacus fossor Rafinesque, 1817 (suppressed by ICZN Opinion 522) and Astacus oreganus Randall, 1840 (suppressed by Opinion 855). Astacus leniusculus was placed on the Official List of Specific Names in Opinion 855.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty.** The author flags Cambarus (Lacunicambarus) diogenes as a species complex needing attention, Cambarus (Puncticambarus) acuminatus as highly variable, and the [[Orconectes]] (Gremicambarus) virilis complex (including O. causeyi and O. nais) as needing further study.
- **Scope of data.** Type series, localities and ranges are given for each taxon, supporting future conservation and distributional work, particularly for range-restricted and possibly extinct species.
- **Closing note.** The author states that the compilation has been improved by many specialist contributions, and that further survey work is needed in parts of the Missouri, Arkansas and Oklahoma range of [[Faxonella]].
