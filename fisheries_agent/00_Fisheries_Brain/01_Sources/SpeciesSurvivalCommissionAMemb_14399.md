# IUCN Species Survival Commission Member's Guide 1994-1996

#conservation #biodiversity #organizational-policy #IUCN-SSC

## Executive Summary

This document is a comprehensive member's guide for the [[IUCN]] [[Species Survival Commission]] (SSC) covering the 1994-1996 triennium. The SSC is described as the largest global network of volunteer experts working for species conservation, comprising approximately 5,000 members in 170 countries. The guide outlines the Commission's structure, mandate, operational procedures, and member responsibilities. It explains SSC's role within the broader IUCN framework and details how the network functions through approximately 102 Specialist Groups organized primarily by taxonomy, plus disciplinary groups addressing thematic issues such as [[Captive Breeding]], [[Invasive Species]], [[Re-introductions]], [[Sustainable Use]], and veterinary concerns. The document serves as an operational manual for both new and existing members, clarifying governance structures, funding mechanisms, communication protocols, and the relationship between SSC and other conservation organizations.

## Background & Description

The Species Survival Commission was established in 1949 and has grown into a network of approximately 5,000 professionals including taxonomists, conservation biologists, and wildlife managers. The Commission operates as one of six voluntary networks within IUCN - The World Conservation Union. SSC's guiding philosophy derives from the [[World Conservation Strategy]] (1980) and its successor document "Caring for the Earth" (1991), emphasizing three principles: conservation of biological diversity, preservation of life-support systems, and sustainable use of living natural resources.

The Commission's organizational structure includes: a Chair (elected by IUCN General Assembly), Deputy Chair, Steering Committee (approximately 35 members), Regional Members serving as focal points within countries or regions, approximately 102 Specialist Groups (mostly taxonomic), Task Forces (for [[Invertebrates]] and [[Declining Amphibian Populations]]), Cooperating Organizations, Members Emeritus, and a Roll of Honour. A Plant Conservation Sub-Committee oversees plant-related issues. The SSC Programme is managed from four offices: Gland, Switzerland (headquarters); Cambridge, UK (wildlife trade focus); Chicago, USA (Chair's office); and Minnesota, USA ([[Captive Breeding Specialist Group]]).

Specialist Groups are organized primarily by taxonomy, covering mammals (31 groups including [[African Elephant]], [[Primates]], [[Cetacean]], [[Canid]]), birds (working with [[BirdLife International]], IWRB, and World Pheasant Association), reptiles and amphibians (10 groups including [[Crocodile]], [[Marine Turtles]]), fish (6 groups including [[Coral Reef Fish]], [[Sharks]], [[Sturgeon]]), invertebrates (8 groups), and plants (19 groups). Five disciplinary groups address cross-cutting themes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document emphasizes that SSC operates within the broader context of global biodiversity conservation, addressing threats to species across all taxonomic groups and geographic regions. The Commission's work encompasses both terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, though specific ecological details are not provided in this administrative document.

The guide notes that SSC collaborates closely with key partner organizations including the [[TRAFFIC Network]] (monitoring wildlife trade), [[World Conservation Monitoring Centre]] (WCMC - managing global biodiversity information), [[BirdLife International]], International Waterfowl and Wetlands Research Bureau (IWRB), and World Pheasant Association. These partnerships extend SSC's capacity to address conservation challenges across diverse habitats and taxa.

Regional and national offices provide localized support in countries including Bangladesh, Botswana, Burkina Faso, Cambodia, Cameroon, Congo, Ethiopia, Ghana, Guatemala, Kenya, Laos, Mali, Nepal, Nicaragua, Niger, Nigeria, Pakistan, Panama, Senegal, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Uganda, Vietnam, Zambia, and Zimbabwe, among others. This geographic distribution enables SSC to address conservation issues at multiple scales.

## Key Findings

**Mission and Goals**: SSC's mission is "to conserve biological diversity by developing and executing programmes to save, restore and manage wisely species and their habitats." The purpose is "to mobilize action by the world conservation community on behalf of species, in particular those threatened with extinction and those of importance for human welfare."

**Core Objectives**: The Commission pursues ten primary objectives including: implementing principles of "Caring for the Earth"; assessing species conservation priorities through Action Planning; promoting implementation of Action Plans; developing policies and guidelines for species conservation; providing technical advisory services; maintaining the global expert network; communicating SSC work broadly; and managing species data in standardized databases.

**Action Planning Programme**: Launched in 1986 with support from [[WWF]] and the Sultanate of Oman, the Action Planning programme represents one of SSC's most important activities. As of March 1994, 26 SSC Action Plans had been published with many more in preparation. Action Plans review conservation needs, identify priorities, and recommend actions required for species' long-term survival.

**Membership Benefits**: Members receive the newsletter "Species" (published twice yearly), the SSC Membership Directory (produced once per triennium), specialist group newsletters, free copies of Action Plans to which they contribute, invitations to Commission meetings and IUCN General Assemblies, and opportunities to participate in a respected global conservation network.

**Specialist Group Activities**: Groups engage in diverse activities including Action Planning and implementation, field project promotion (though not direct implementation), newsletter production (at least 30 groups publish regular newsletters), meetings and workshops (including [[Conservation Assessment and Management Plan]] (CAMP) Workshops), formal interventions to governments, assignment of [[IUCN Red List]] categories, support to international treaties (especially [[CITES]]), policy development, and project review.

**Financial Resources and Fundraising**: Neither SSC nor IUCN are well-resourced donor organizations. Specialist Groups are encouraged to raise funds following specific guidelines: contact the SSC Financial Development Officer before approaching donors, obtain approval for proposals requiring IUCN Project Review Group submission, and have final proposals approved by both the Financial Development Officer and Programme Officer. Groups should not maintain bank accounts in their own names; funds should be managed by IUCN member organizations or IUCN offices, with annual audits and quarterly financial reporting required.

**Membership Appointment Process**: The Commission Chair is elected by IUCN General Assembly for up to two consecutive three-year terms. The Chair nominates Deputy and Steering Committee members. Specialist Group and Task Force Chairs are reviewed by the Steering Committee. Group Chairs have delegated authority to appoint their own members at any time during the triennium using standardized appointment letters. All appointments are valid until the next IUCN General Assembly, with reappointment normally automatic except for inactive or resigned members.

**Member Responsibilities**: Specialist Group Chairs must establish effective membership networks, maintain communication mechanisms, establish goals and objectives for each triennium, develop systems for assessing and monitoring species status, create response mechanisms for information requests, report on activities, promote projects (with appropriate review), and coordinate interventions with SSC leadership. Group members must maintain regular contact with Chairs, respond promptly to requests, contribute to agreed programmes, and share expertise through newsletters and meetings. Regional Members serve as focal points promoting SSC priorities and policies within their countries or regions.

**Support Infrastructure**: Staff support is limited, with only seven staff in Gland, two in Chicago, two in Cambridge, plus staff at the Captive Breeding Specialist Group office in Minneapolis and sustainable use expertise in Washington, D.C. Members are advised to use their Group Chair as the primary link to staff except in emergencies. The Programme Office in Gland handles Action Planning, membership appointments, policy matters, meetings, and information management. The Chair's Office manages direct business with the SSC Chair, fundraising, finance, and the Species newsletter. The Cambridge office addresses CITES and wildlife trade issues.

**Disciplinary Group Terms of Reference**: The five disciplinary groups have specific mandates: the [[Captive Breeding Specialist Group]] focuses on establishing viable populations through captive propagation and intensive management; the [[Invasive Species Specialist Group]] works to reduce threats from invasive species; the [[Re-introduction Specialist Group]] develops expertise on reintroductions and translocations; the [[Specialist Group on Sustainable Use]] promotes sustainable use as a conservation tool; and the [[Veterinary Specialist Group]] ensures specialist veterinary advice is available for wild species populations.

**Communication and Reporting**: Groups are expected to produce triennial reports for presentation at SSC meetings held prior to each IUCN General Assembly. Thirty copies of each specialist group newsletter should be sent to the SSC Programme Office for broader distribution. Members should contribute articles to "Species" and group newsletters, coordinating with their Chairs. Correspondence on group letterhead (which must include IUCN and SSC logos) should only address matters relating to that group's work.

**Intervention Protocols**: Specialist Group Chairs may make interventions on behalf of their groups but not in the name of IUCN or SSC as a whole. Before intervening with governments or others, Chairs must contact the SSC Chair's Office and Programme Office in Gland to ensure wide review and policy alignment. Potentially controversial issues (sustainable utilization, captive breeding, animal capture, wildlife trade, reintroductions, alien species eradication, disease control) require review by the Chair's Office, Programme Office(s), and appropriate disciplinary groups before submission to implementing agencies.

**Database and Information Management**: SSC maintains a membership database updated through membership forms returned to the Programme Office. The Commission is developing standardized, distributed, computerized databases for species data. The [[World Conservation Monitoring Centre]] (WCMC) cooperates in producing IUCN Red Lists and can provide data services, database development assistance, and geographic information systems support to Specialist Groups.

**National Biodiversity Groups Initiative**: A major initiative for the 1994-1996 triennium involves developing National Biodiversity Groups in association with the [[Commission on National Parks and Protected Areas]] (CNPPA). These groups will bring together SSC, CNPPA, and other IUCN network members in selected countries to assist development and implementation of biodiversity conservation initiatives at national and regional levels.
