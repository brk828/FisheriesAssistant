# Letter to D. A. Duff on Salmo and Parasalmo Classification

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1988 letter from W. J. Berg (UC Davis) to D. A. Duff (AFS Fisheries Management Section) argues against the American Fisheries Society's decision to retain [[Parasalmo]] as a subgenus of [[Salmo]] in the forthcoming 1990 edition of *Common and Scientific Names of Fishes*. Berg contends this decision represents a taxonomic "punt" driven by unresolved phylogenetic relationships between the fossil genus [[Rhabdofario]] and [[Parasalmo]]. Drawing on morphological, genetic, biogeographic, and paleontological evidence, Berg advocates for full generic status of *Parasalmo*, positioning it as the sister-taxon to [[Oncorhynchus]] rather than a subgenus of *Salmo*. The letter includes a speculative phylogeny of western North American salmonids and a proposed subspecies nomenclature for rainbow trout under the genus *Parasalmo*.

## Background & Description

- **Salmo (sensu lato)** is characterized as a **polyphyletic assemblage** (per [[Cavender and Miller 1982]]), rendering it an artificial and taxonomically inadmissible construct.
- **Character-state overlap** between *Rhabdofario* and *Parasalmo* is attributed to an **ancestor-descendant relationship** and **canalization**, not convergence.
- Some character-state distribution problems noted in the phylogeny are flagged as potentially **symplesiomorphic** within [[Salmonini]] or as **ontogenetic (non-convergent) adaptations to anadromy**, complicating cladistic interpretation.
- Diagnostic biogeographic split proposed for rainbow trout subspecies: *Parasalmo mykiss mykiss* (Kamchatkan) vs. *P. m. gairdnerii* (coastal), distinguished by spawning distribution west/east of 170°W and oceanic distribution west/east of 165°E respectively.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The salmonids under discussion occupy North Pacific freshwater and anadromous marine systems, including lacustrine (lake), riverine, and coastal marine environments.
- **Evolutionary/ecological history** (per attached speculative phylogeny):
  1. **Miocene radiation** of large lacustrine and anadromous taxa (e.g., [[Smilodonichthys]]).
  2. **Subsequent diminution** of riverine forms through the Pliocene ([[Rhabdofario]]) into the Pleistocene-Recent ([[Parasalmo]]).
  3. **Environmentally and competitively induced extinctions** shaping the modern distribution of surviving lineages.
- Proposed subspecies occupy distinct freshwater habitats across western North America, including:
  - [[Eagle Lake Rainbow Trout]] (*P. m. aquilarum*)
  - [[San Pedro Martir Rainbow Trout]] (*P. m. nelsoni*)
  - [[Kern River Rainbow Trout]] (*P. m. gilberti*)
  - [[Volcano Creek Golden Trout]] (*P. m. aguabonita*)
  - [[Little Kern River Golden Trout]] (*P. m. whitei*)
  - [[Inland Redband Trout]] (*P. m. gibbsii*)
  - [[McCloud River Redband Trout]] and [[Goose Lake Redband Trout]] (subspecies pending description)

## Key Findings

1. **Polyphyly of Salmo (sensu lato)**: Established by [[Cavender and Miller 1982]], undermining the validity of a broadly inclusive *Salmo*.
2. **Sister-group relationship**: Among extant taxa, *Parasalmo* is identified as the sister-group to *Oncorhynchus*, supported by three independent lines of evidence — [[Berg and Ferris 1984]] (mitochondrial DNA restriction analysis), [[Johnson 1984]] (protein/allozyme electrophoresis), and [[Kendall and Behnke 1984]] (developmental/ontogenetic characters).
3. **Paleontological precedent**: Prior taxonomic work ([[Miller and Smith 1981]], [[Smith 1981]]) supports distinguishing Miocene-Pliocene *Rhabdofario* from Pleistocene-Recent *Parasalmo* as separate, phylogenetically informative taxa — despite the caveat that zoological nomenclature lacks formal "case law" (ICZN 1985).
4. **Proposed phylogenetic hypothesis**: *Parasalmo* is hypothesized to be an **anagenic derivative** of *Rhabdofario* initially, later becoming **cladogenic**, implying a direct ancestor-descendant lineage distinct from true *Salmo*.
5. **Three nomenclatural alternatives** presented for resolving the *Salmo*/*Parasalmo*/*Oncorhynchus* relationship:
   - (1) Elevate *Salmo* to tribe-level equivalence ([[Salmonini]]), demoting all included genera (*Oncorhynchus*, *Parasalmo*, *Smilodonichthys*) to subgenera of *Salmo*.
   - (2) Demote *Parasalmo* and *Rhabdofario* to subgenera of *Oncorhynchus*.
   - (3) Grant *Parasalmo* full generic rank, per [[Vladykov 1963]]'s original taxonomic intent.
6. **Author's recommendation**: Berg supports **Option 3** — full generic status for *Parasalmo* — citing conceptual alignment with classification principles attributed to Darwin, Hennig, and Mayr, and warning that failure to do so risks long-term (10+ year) confusion for non-specialists (e.g., biochemists working across *Oncorhynchus*, *Salmo*, and *Parasalmo* physiological data).
7. **Proposed subspecies nomenclature for rainbow trout** (under *Parasalmo mykiss*), including newly justified subspecific distinction between **Kamchatkan** and **coastal** rainbow trout based on biogeographic and management relevance.
8. **Outstanding taxonomic uncertainty**: The nomen *P. m. newberrii* ([[Girard]]) for upper Klamath basin redband trout remains provisional, pending electrophoretic analysis.
