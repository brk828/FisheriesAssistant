# Catalogue of Type Specimens of Fossil Fishes in the Field Museum of Natural History

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoichthyology #type-specimens

## Executive Summary

This Field Museum of Natural History catalogue (Fieldiana: Geology, New Series No. 23, published 31 January 1992) by [[John Clay Bruner]] lists every fossil fish type held by the museum's Geology Department. It was the first complete inventory of these holdings, correcting earlier citation errors and identifying types that had been unmarked or unpublished. The collection contains 119 holotypes, 36 cotypes, 1,503 paratypes, 37 casts of holotypes and 2 casts of paratypes (abstract figures). The catalogue gives, for each type, its catalogue number, current classification, description, type locality, full citation and figure references. It also serves to advertise where the types are housed, including former [[Walker Museum]] material now held by the Field Museum.

## Background & Description

The catalogue arose from the lack of an accurate count of types. Some lots were never marked as types, and some types were never published. Many original authors had cited the wrong prefix or specimen number. The author also notes that the University of Chicago still received requests for Walker Museum specimens despite a 1964 notice (Weller, 1964).

Catalogue numbers follow a fixed citation format of "FMNH" followed by a collection prefix and the number. The prefixes are:

- **FMNH**: Geology Department Slide Collection
- **P**: Paleontology Collection
- **PE**: Fossil Invertebrates Collection
- **PF**: Fossil Fishes Collection
- **UC**: University of Chicago, Walker Museum Paleontology Collection
- **UF**: University of Chicago, Walker Museum Fossil Fishes Collection

For example, the holotype of [[Mayomyzon Pieckoensis]] is cited as FMNH PF 5687.

Classification follows a composite scheme drawn from Carroll (1988), Denison (1978, 1979), Moy-Thomas and Miles (1971), Nelson (1984), Obruchev (1967) and Zangerl (1981). Most material is from the Paleozoic, with a small number of Mesozoic and Cenozoic types. The most prolific describer in the holdings is [[Robert H. Denison]], the former curator of fossil fishes, who described 31 holotypes and 931 paratypes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The catalogue covers fishes and fish-like vertebrates from a wide span of time and environments. Many type localities are famous fossil lagerstätten, including:

- The [[Mazon Creek Fauna]] (Middle Pennsylvanian, Illinois), source of many concretion-preserved chondrichthyans and dipnoans.
- The [[Holland Quarry Shale]] (Early Devonian, Ohio).
- The [[Water Canyon Formation]] and [[Beartooth Butte Formation]] (Early Devonian, Utah and Wyoming).
- The [[Green River Formation]] (Eocene, Wyoming), which yielded hiodontids, catostomids and catfish types.
- The [[Selma Group]] (Late Cretaceous, Alabama), with chimaeroid, elopid, albulid and acipenserid material.

Several lots are described as concretion faunas or freshwater deposits. Taxa include jawless ostracoderms, placoderms, [[Acanthodii]], early sharks, lungfishes, actinopterygians and teleosts.

## Key Findings

- **Type inventory:** 119 holotypes, 36 cotypes, 1,503 paratypes, 37 casts of holotypes and 2 casts of paratypes are catalogued. The body text gives slightly different counts (35 cotypes, 36 casts of holotypes), which the author does not reconcile.
- **Provenance:** 77 holotypes and 1,447 paratypes belong to the original Field Museum collections. 42 holotypes, 32 cotypes and 56 paratypes came from the former Walker Museum.
- **Major contributors:** Denison (31 holotypes, 931 paratypes); [[Rainer Zangerl]] (16 holotypes, 28 paratypes, plus 7 holotypes and 224 paratypes with Case); St. John and Worthen (14 holotypes, 24 cotypes); Cope (11 holotypes); Williams (3 holotypes, 224 paratypes).
- **Major groups:** The Agnatha section covers Cephalaspidomorphi and Pteraspidomorphi, including the first fossil lamprey, [[Mayomyzon Pieckoensis]]. Chondrichthyan types are numerous, with many Pennsylvanian holotypes from the Mazon Creek and Logan Quarry faunas. The Actinopterygii and Dipnoi sections also contain many important type series.
- **Notable taxa:** Holotypes include [[Mayomyzon Pieckoensis]] (first fossil lamprey, Illinois), [[Iniopteryx Rushlaui]] and related iniopterygians (Zangerl & Case, 1973), [[Dunkleosteus Maraisi]] (Morocco), and [[Eohiodon Woodruffi]] (Wyoming, Eocene).
- **Casts and foreign material:** The collection holds casts of types from the Princeton University Geological Museum, Royal Scottish Museum, National Museum of Canada and Oslo Paleontological Museum. These are useful when originals are on loan-restricted or distant.
- **Taxonomic notes:** Several entries record reassignments. Examples include the Amyzon case (A. gosiutensis is a junior synonym of A. aggregatum, see Bruner 1991), a chimaeroid-like jaw identity for some Tanaodus material (per Lund), and the Sagenodus synonymies. The author also flags a possible mix-up between UF 617 and UC 27323, and a Tamiobatus that is more likely a xenacanthid.
- **Catalogue purpose:** The author aims to improve citation accuracy, prompt discovery of unidentified types and direct researchers to the correct repository.
