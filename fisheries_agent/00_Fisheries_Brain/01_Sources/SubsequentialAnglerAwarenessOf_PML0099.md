# Alamo Lake Slot Size Limit: Subsequent Angler Awareness and Compliance, August 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #largemouth-bass

## Executive Summary

A one-week intensive enforcement study by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Region 4) was conducted at [[Alamo Lake]], La Paz County, Arizona, from August 6 to 13, 1989. It re-tested angler awareness of and compliance with the [[Slot Size Limit]] on [[Largemouth Bass]] (12 to 16 inches, in effect since January 1, 1989) and repeated the design of an earlier March 1989 study.

Eight patrols by four officers (44 hours of active patrol) documented 164 angler contacts, about 55% of the estimated 297 anglers on the lake that week. Anglers caught 440 bass (2.7 per angler). Of these, 340 (77%) were released and 100 (23%) were possessed. Unlawfully possessed bass made up 7% of the bass kept and 2% of all bass caught.

Awareness of the slot limit was documented at 100%. Overall compliance with Title 5 and Title 17 rules was 94%, and slot-limit non-compliance was low (3%). Compliance rates were essentially unchanged from the March study, but the share of bass kept rose sharply. The authors recommended that the existing [[Information and Education]] materials remain available and that enforcement continue at routine levels.

## Background & Description

**Study species and lake.** Largemouth bass were the focus of the study. [[Alamo Lake]] was formed in 1968 by the construction of Alamo Dam. It became one of Arizona's premier bass fishing lakes, hosting the most bass tournaments in the state from 1983 through 1987. Its surface area exceeded 10,000 acres after above-normal rainfall in the 1970s. The lake was held at about 3,000 surface acres from 1985 onward, while angler pressure remained high.

**Reason for the regulation.** [[Creel Census]] data and population surveys from 1985 onward showed a decline in bass size and age. By 1987, largemouth bass over four years old were rare in both the harvest and the population. Bass under 12 inches made up just under 80% of the population but under 17% of the harvest. Bass of 12 to 16 inches made up over 80% of the harvest but about 19% of the population. Bass over 16 inches were about 1% of the population and about 2% of the harvest.

**The slot limit.** The regulation made it unlawful to possess largemouth bass between 12 and 16 inches. It was designed to protect 3- and 4-year-old fish and redistribute the population toward larger size classes. Because slot limits were uncommon in Arizona, the expected level of angler compliance was uncertain. A 1984 [[Oklahoma Slot Limit Study]] had found non-compliance as high as 35% where anglers did not understand the limit and enforcement was weak.

**Prior study.** An initial enforcement and [[Information and Education]] (I&E) program ran from January through March 15, 1989. It combined news releases, posters, pamphlets, and high-profile patrols. A follow-up study was then planned to measure whether awareness and compliance persisted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addressed angler behavior and fishery harvest rather than the broader aquatic ecosystem. No water chemistry, plankton, invertebrate, or habitat measurements were reported. The document notes that the reduction in fish habitat and the decline in lake levels occurred alongside high angler pressure. This suggests that the bass fishery was affected by both habitat change and harvest.

Seasonal fishing pressure was also relevant. August was chosen because creel records showed most anglers targeted bass then, and because summer visitation was high. Total angler use was estimated from [[Alamo State Park]] visitor records, which were adjusted for visits that likely included more than one angler-day. Adjustments included an upward expansion of about 33% for multi-day trips and a downward reduction of about 27% for visitors not engaged in consumptive use.

Monthly angler use fluctuated between January and July of 1988 and 1989. This suggested that the slot limit may have affected some anglers' use of the lake, but the study could not separate that effect from weather or other factors.

## Key Findings

**1. Angler awareness was complete.**
- All contacted anglers (100%) were aware of the slot limit, up from about 95% in the March study.
- The authors suggested that [[Information and Education]] efforts, [[Enforcement Patrols]], and word of mouth were effective. Many anglers were probably returning users or [[Catch and Release]] anglers who came to the lake because of the limit.

**2. Overall compliance remained high, and slot-limit compliance was unchanged.**
- Overall compliance with Title 5 and Title 17 regulations was 94%.
- Slot-limit non-compliance was 3% in August, the same as in March. Contact-level compliance with all rules was 94%; about 6% of anglers were in violation of some rule.
- The authors judged that changes in the compliance rate were not statistically meaningful and that the percentages of individual citation types should be read cautiously because the sample was small.

**3. Citations.**
- Ten citations were issued: 2 under Title 5 (watercraft, 20%), 4 under other Title 17 rules such as licensing and fishing-method violations (40%), and 4 for slot-limit violations (40%).
- In the two weeks after the enforcement phase, 16 citations were issued, 7 of them (43%) for slot-limit violations.

**4. Harvest rose, though the share of anglers violating the limit did not.**
- Bass caught per angler increased from 2.0 in March to 2.6 in August.
- The percentage of bass kept rose from about 6% to 23%.
- Unlawfully possessed bass rose from 2% to 7% of the bass kept.
- Those who violated the limit kept more bass in August than in March. The authors attributed this to better fishing success rather than to a change in rule-breaking behavior.
- Bass lawfully possessed over 16 inches were 3% of the bass kept in August and were considered consistent with the 1987 creel data.

**5. Release rates.**
- Anglers released 31% of their bass from January through April 1987, before the slot limit took effect.
- Release rates were 93% during the 1989 study period.
- In the combined data from both 1989 phases, 315 anglers caught 621 bass and released 93% (578 fish).

**6. Recommendations.**
- Existing I&E material should remain available at Alamo Lake and at local license dealers.
- Law enforcement should continue at the level normally required for enforcement and creel census needs.
- Future population surveys will show whether the higher release rate affects the size-class distribution of the bass population.
- The Department is distributing 18-inch [[Slot Size Limit]] rulers with peel-off adhesive backings, funded through [[Sport Fish Restoration]]. The rulers are for use at both [[Alamo Lake]] and [[Choila Lake]], which have the same slot limit.
