# Systematics of the Etheostoma jordani Species Group

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This systematic revision examines the [[Etheostoma Jordani]] species group, a set of darters in the subgenus [[Nothonotus]] that had long been treated as a single species. Using meristic counts, morphometric and truss measurements, and colour pattern data, the authors recognise four allopatric species endemic to the [[Mobile Basin]] largely above the [[Fall Line]]. The paper redescribes [[Etheostoma Jordani]] and formally describes three new species: [[Etheostoma Douglasi]], [[Etheostoma Etowahae]], and [[Etheostoma Chuckwachatte]]. The authors recommend state and federal protection for three of the four species and highlight the group's value as indicators of stream quality.

## Background & Description

- **Regional context:** Rivers of the Mobile Basin hold one of the most distinctive ichthyofaunas in North America, with at least 40 endemic species. Diversification is attributed to drainage exchange, gene pool isolation, and limited impact from Pleistocene glaciation.
- **Study scope:** Variation was assessed across 22 standard and truss measurements, 18 meristic characters, and coloration. Colour was verified on live and freshly preserved breeding and non-breeding adults over a four-year period.
- **Methods:** Morphometric data were analysed by t-tests (P<0.05) for sexual dimorphism and by sheared principal component analysis (PCA). Meristic data used a correlation matrix, and morphometric data used a covariance matrix.
- **Group diagnosis:** Members lack dark horizontal lines between scale rows, have a partially scaled nape (otherwise found only in [[Etheostoma Bellum]]), show 3 to 11 weak vertical flank bars, and males have a broad subdistal red band in the caudal fin.

The name *chuckwachatte* derives from Creek words for mouth (*chuckwe*) and red (*chattee*), referring to the red lips of breeding males. The name *douglasi* honours Dr. Neil H. Douglas, and *etowahae* refers to the Etowah River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Adults of the group typically inhabit riffles with moderate to strong current over gravel or cobble substrate. The authors note a general preference for high-gradient, clear streams with silt-free substrate.
- **Diet:** Orr (1989) found that dipteran, ephemeropteran, and trichopteran larvae made up most of the diet of *E. jordani* in Opintlocco Creek. For *E. chuckwachatte* in Hillabee Creek, dipteran, ephemeropteran, and plecopteran larvae dominated. Nothing has been reported on the diet of *E. etowahae*.
- **Reproduction (*E. jordani*, Opintlocco Creek):** Orr and Ramsey (1990) recorded peak reproductive activity in the third week of April 1986. Ripe females occurred from 22 April to 3 June at 18.0–29.4 °C. The smallest mature female was 23.0 mm SL. The species buries its eggs in sand at a site chosen by the female. O'Neil (1980) recorded a reproductive peak in mid to late May 1977 in Barbaree Creek.
- **Reproduction (*E. chuckwachatte*, Hillabee Creek):** Peak activity occurred in the first week of May. Ripe females were found from 7 April to 30 June at 20.0–25.6 °C. The smallest mature female was 29.0 mm SL. Spawning has not been observed, but the species is assumed to be an egg burier.
- **Distribution by species:**
  - *E. jordani* is distributed through the Coosa system (including Conasauga and Coosawattee rivers), the [[Cahaba River]], and the [[Tallapoosa River]] below the Fall Line.
  - *E. douglasi* is known only from the upper [[Black Warrior River System]] in Alabama.
  - *E. etowahae* is restricted to the [[Etowah River]] system of Georgia above Lake Allatoona.
  - *E. chuckwachatte* occurs throughout the Tallapoosa system above the Fall Line in Alabama and Georgia.
- **Biogeographic parallels:** Several other Mobile Basin taxa show similar disjunct and endemic patterns, including *Cyprinella gibbsi* and *C. trichroistia*, *Fundulus bifax* and *F. stellifer*, and *Cottus carolinae zopherus*. The authors also note similar patterns in several snubnose darter species.
- **Threats:** Habitat degradation, impoundments, and water withdrawals for urban use threaten the fragmented ranges. The type locality of *E. jordani* appears to have been lost. Repeated searches near Oxford, Alabama, failed, and no fishes were collected from the heavily polluted [[Choccoloco Creek]].

## Key Findings

1. **Four species, not one.** Morphometric, meristic, and colour evidence support recognising four allopatric species within the former *E. jordani* concept.

2. **Sexual dimorphism is limited.** Males are more brightly coloured year-round, peaking in spring. Significant sex differences in measurements were few and varied among species. For example, males of *E. jordani* had greater head depth, gape width, and caudal peduncle depth, while females had larger eyes.

3. **Diagnostic characters.** Table 3 separates species by transverse scale rows, caudal peduncle scale rows, flank red spots, anal fin red, lip red, and opercle scales. For example, *E. etowahae* has 11–14 transverse scale rows versus 14–16 in the other species, and 16–18 caudal peduncle scale rows versus 17–20 in *E. chuckwachatte*.

4. **Meristic separation of *E. etowahae*.** Principal component analysis of meristic data separates *E. etowahae* almost completely along PC I. Its mean transverse scale rows (12.7) fall below the minimum mean of 14.5 for the other species. Its mean lateral line scales (45.5) fall below the minimum mean of 48.3, and its mean caudal peduncle scale rows (17.1) fall below the minimum mean of 18.8.

5. **Morphometric separation.** Along sheared PC I, *E. chuckwachatte* is separable from nearby *E. jordani* populations in the lower Tallapoosa. *E. etowahae* is separated from both *E. chuckwachatte* and most *E. douglasi* along sheared PC III. *E. etowahae* has a shorter head, snout, and pectoral fins, and a longer anal fin base.

6. **Coloration of *E. chuckwachatte*.** Males differ from *E. jordani* by having bright red lips and a broad red band through the blue-turquoise anal fin.

7. **Conservation recommendations.**
   - *E. etowahae* merits **endangered** status. Burkhead (1992) recommended federal endangered listing because of its extremely restricted range and ongoing habitat degradation, and the authors concur.
   - *E. douglasi* and *E. chuckwachatte* should be treated as **threatened** at minimum. Both have fragmented, restricted ranges in watersheds facing impoundment and water removal proposals.
   - *E. jordani* has a wider range and locally abundant populations. The authors do not recommend protection until a thorough status survey is done.

8. **Indicator value.** Because these darters require clear, silt-free, high-gradient streams, they are considered sensitive indicators of aquatic ecosystem quality in the upper Mobile Basin. Their fragmented ranges and impending threats warrant concern for their survival.
