# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin - FY 1997 Work Plan

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #endangered-species #program-management #habitat-restoration

## Executive Summary

This document presents the Fiscal Year 1997 work plan and budget for the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Approved by the Colorado River Implementation Committee on October 2, 1996, the plan encompasses over 90 scopes of work prepared by state and federal agencies, universities, private contractors, and Recovery Program staff. The FY 97 recommended annual budget is $3.08 million (2.8% increase over FY 96), with total program funding reaching $13.41 million when including additional agency contributions, FY 88 appropriation funds, Section 7 funds ($28,500), and Reclamation capital projects ($7.47 million). The work plan implements recovery activities identified in the Program's Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP) dated March 11, 1996, targeting four endangered fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]].

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program operates through a collaborative partnership involving the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, Western Area Power Administration, environmental groups, and water users. The Implementation Committee, comprising representatives from these entities, oversees program direction and annual budget priorities. The program addresses recovery through seven major elements: instream flow identification and protection, habitat restoration, nonnative fish management, propagation and genetics management, research and monitoring, information and education, and program management.

The work plan development follows a structured annual process beginning in December with project reports and culminating in October with Implementation Committee approval. Technical committees review and recommend projects, while the Management Committee provides oversight. Projects are designed to implement priority recovery actions for the endangered fishes, with emphasis on restoring natural river functions while accommodating water development needs consistent with the Endangered Species Act.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] encompasses critical habitat for four endangered fish species adapted to the dynamic flow regimes and geomorphology of large southwestern rivers. The basin includes major tributaries: the [[Colorado River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Green River]], [[Gunnison River]], [[White River]], and [[Little Snake River]]. Historical alterations through dam construction (particularly [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and the [[Aspinall Unit]]), water diversions, and introduction of nonnative species have severely impacted native fish populations.

The endangered fishes require specific flow regimes for reproduction and recruitment, including spring peak flows for spawning cues and floodplain inundation, stable summer flows for nursery habitat, and appropriate winter flows. Floodplain habitats provide critical nursery areas for larval and juvenile fish. The [[15-Mile Reach]] of the Colorado River between the Grand Valley diversions and the Gunnison River confluence represents particularly important habitat. Nonnative predators and competitors (centrarchids, catfish, northern pike) pose significant threats to native fish populations, particularly to early life stages.

## Key Findings

**Instream Flow Protection and Identification ($2,011,100 total):**
- Grand Valley water management activities will provide up to 30,000 acre-feet of additional water through canal improvements and Green Mountain Reservoir storage
- Ruedi Reservoir short-term contract provides 21,600 acre-feet annually for endangered fish
- Steamboat Lake lease provides 2,000-3,300 acre-feet for late summer Yampa River flows ($32,000)
- [[Yampa River Basin Recovery and Water Management Plan]] (CAP-9, $318,000) will integrate flow protection with water development needs
- Colorado Water Conservation Board continues instream flow appropriation process for Colorado, Yampa, White, and Little Snake rivers ($187,000)
- [[Flaming Gorge]] studies synthesis ($363,600) will refine flow recommendations and support revised Biological Opinion
- [[Aspinall Unit]] studies synthesis ($410,500) completing 5-year evaluation of Gunnison River flow effects
- Flow recommendations being finalized for White River ($7,000) and upper Colorado River ($17,000)

**Habitat Restoration ($3,467,500 capital funds):**
- Fish passage construction/planning at multiple barriers: [[Price-Stubb Diversion]] ($575,000), [[Grand Valley Irrigation Company]] ($600,000), [[Hartland Diversion]] ($175,000), [[Grand Valley Project Diversion]] ($50,000)
- [[Redlands]] fish passage evaluation continuing ($111,800)
- Floodplain Restoration Program ($1,933,800) targeting levee removal at 10 Green River sites and 5 Colorado/Gunnison sites
- Land acquisition pursuing 1,000 acres of Green River floodplain easements and 200 acres along Gunnison/Colorado rivers
- [[Old Charlie Wash]] serving as controlled facility for razorback sucker floodplain habitat testing
- Selenium tolerance studies at [[Walter Walker]] and [[Adobe Creek]] to determine Gunnison River restoration potential
- Gravel pit evaluation as surrogate floodplain habitat at 29 5/8 Road site

**Nonnative Fish Management ($326,600):**
- Gunnison River nonnative removal study completion ($7,000)
- Green River pilot removal program in [[Gray Canyon]] (catfish) and Ouray reach (centrarchids and catfish) ($164,400)
- Floodplain pond reclamation targeting up to 25 ponds in FY 97 to eliminate centrarchid sources ($155,200)
- Strategic plan developed for basin-wide nonnative fish control

**Propagation and Genetics ($3,670,500):**
- Hatchery facility operations at [[Grand Valley]] ($118,100), [[Wahweap]] ($35,000), and [[Ouray]] ($250,000)
- Major construction at Ouray: 10 0.5-acre production ponds, ozone system, hatchery building completion ($2,857,000)
- [[Gila]] taxonomy studies completing mitochondrial DNA analysis, allozyme work, and synthesis ($94,200) to resolve taxonomic uncertainty among [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Roundtail Chub]]
- Experimental bonytail stocking in Green River (2,000 fish, $35,000)
- Five-year razorback sucker stocking evaluation testing three size classes (4, 8, 12 inches) for optimal survival ($10,000)
- Chemoreception and imprinting studies ($43,200) investigating homing behavior and potential for synthetic chemical imprinting

**Research and Monitoring ($482,600):**
- [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP) continuing for post-larval and adult squawfish, humpback chub, and sympatric species ($131,100)
- New basin-wide razorback monitoring program established ($131,200) to assess population responses to restoration
- Larval fish identification supporting multiple studies ($92,100)
- Database management maintaining comprehensive program data ($28,200)
- Interim management objectives development for all four species ($40,000) to define self-sustaining population parameters
- [[Price River]] assessment evaluating importance to Green River system ($60,000)

**Program Management and Coordination ($1,175,300):**
- Program Director's office coordination ($704,600)
- State and federal agency participation: Reclamation ($130,000), Utah ($65,000), Colorado ($119,700), Wyoming ($12,000)
- Capital projects coordination ($108,500)
- Approximately 10% of total program budget allocated to management

**Public Involvement Plans ($159,000):**
- Targeted plans for Colorado instream flow appropriations, Grand Valley capital projects, coordinated reservoir operations, Flaming Gorge releases, floodplain restoration, and nonnative fish control
- Information and education coordination including newsletter, news releases, Congressional briefing materials, and web page development ($26,700)

**Implementation Committee Recommendations:**
- Biology Committee should review continued need for Steamboat Lake water lease
- Bonytail stocking should proceed unless Gila studies suggest otherwise
- Duchesne River biological studies approved (up to $150,000 from Section 7 or FY 88 funds)
- Flaming Gorge winter flow study approved subject to scope development
- Nonnative fish control strategy should be reviewed for acceleration opportunities
- $12,000 committed for Vernal visitor center cost-share in FY 98

**Financial Structure:**
- Annual contributions: FWS ($2,774,000), Reclamation ($2,011,100), Colorado ($823,700), Utah ($97,900), Wyoming ($31,100)
- Section 7 depletion charge: $13.41/acre-foot (adjusted for inflation from $10 base)
- Section 7 fund balance: approximately $267,000 available October 1, 1996
- FY 88 appropriation balance: $185,200 projected after FY 97 expenditures
- Total FY 97 budget: $13.41 million including capital projects
