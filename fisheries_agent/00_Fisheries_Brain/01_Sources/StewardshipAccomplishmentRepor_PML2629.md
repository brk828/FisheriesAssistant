# Stewardship Accomplishment Report: Native Fish Rearing and Stocking on the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by the Parker Fishery Resource Office (scan partially garbled), documents stewardship work on the lower [[Colorado River]] during 1993–1994 to recover two federally listed native fishes, the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail Chub]]. The work combined rearing-pond and cove development, stocking of hatchery-reared juveniles, removal of non-native predators, and a [[Hatchery Propagation]] program at the Achii Hanyo facility. A stocking list from 2002–2003 is appended. Much of the source text is OCR-degraded, so some counts and dates are uncertain and are flagged below.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, family Catostomidae) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*, family Cyprinidae). Both are native large-river fishes whose populations declined in the Colorado River system.
- **Program goal:** Establish protected, predator-free growout habitat so stocked juveniles can reach larger sizes before release into [[Lake Havasu]].
- **Partners:** Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State University, Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge, Bureau of Land Management, Bureau of Reclamation, California Division of Wildlife, Cibola National Wildlife Refuge, Palo Verde Rod and Gun Club, and the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]] (CRIT).
- **Source hatchery:** Juveniles were produced by [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (DNFHTC).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Facility types.** Six isolated facilities were developed on or adjacent to Lake Havasu and included:
- Pittsburgh Point backwater
- Bulkhead Cove
- Office Cove (Bill Williams River NWR)
- No Entry Cove
- North and South Coves
- Davis Cove

Additional ponds were built at Cibola NWR (High Levee Pond), at Parker golf course ponds (Emerald Canyon Golf Course, Ponds 1A–2B, 12A/12B, 18A/18B), and at CRIT's Achii Hanyo Hatchery.

**Predator control.** Facilities were repeatedly rotenone-treated to remove non-native fish before stocking. Species removed or recorded included [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Common Carp]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], green sunfish, warmouth, redear sunfish, black crappie, goldfish, yellow bullhead, and mosquitofish. Netting and electrofishing were used in between treatments.

**Pathogen survey.** Only No Entry Cove was surveyed for pathogens; the scan reads as reporting that no pathogens were present. Other coves could not be surveyed because of funding and scheduling constraints with the [[Fish Health Center]].

## Key Findings

### 1. Pittsburgh Point Backwater
- Rotenone-treated on 1 June 1993 (the report also gives 8 June in one place; the date is uncertain). Removed fish included carp, goldfish, yellow bullheads, and mosquitofish.
- 199 PIT-tagged razorback suckers released 1 June 1993 (count uncertain in scan). Mean release size ~179 mm and ~69 g.
- Recapture on 4 August 1993 yielded 16 fish, which had grown (reported increase of 69 mm). The backwater became anoxic for several weeks after sampling.
- On 18–19 October 1993, about 6,000 razorback suckers and 100 bonytail chubs were stocked. Mean lengths were 98 mm (razorback) and 71 mm (bonytail).
- Netting on 1 December 1993 recovered razorbacks averaging 201 mm and 93 g.

### 2. Bulkhead Cove
- Developed August 1994. An estimated 200–1,000 fish (OCR-uncertain) were stocked, and the cove was monitored for water quality.

### 3. Office Cove, Bill Williams River NWR
- Developed October 1993 and stocked with about 17,552 bonytail chubs (OCR-uncertain) on 21 October 1993, the 1993 DNFHTC cohort (mean length 111 mm).
- Wind-driven wave action breached the net and damaged the cove's structure, so repairs and weekly dive inspections were required.
- Netting for 54 net-days removed 97 largemouth bass, 16 bluegill, 13 black crappie, 2 carp, 6 channel catfish, 3 green sunfish, and 1 flathead catfish.
- Stomach analysis: 7 largemouth bass (of the examined predators) contained bonytail chubs; 1 channel catfish did as well. Mean total lengths were 233 mm (largemouth bass) and 534 mm (channel catfish). Most stomachs were empty, and threadfin shad were the next most common food item.

### 4. High Levee Pond, Cibola NWR
- Rotenoned on 28 September 1993, removing carp, threadfin shad, largemouth bass, redear sunfish, green sunfish, warmouth, channel catfish, and flathead catfish.
- Stocked on 18 and 21 October 1993 with razorback suckers (count garbled in scan; mean 98 mm, n=150) and 21,503 bonytail chubs (mean 66 mm, n=100).
- On 2 December 1993, 16 razorbacks (201 mm TL, 89 g, a reported 103 mm gain in 70 days) and one bonytail chub were recovered. The bonytail was 300 mm TL, 220 g, PIT-tagged, and about two years old.

### 5. Hatchery Propagation at Achii Hanyo (CRIT)
- During March–April 1994, 20 pairs of razorback suckers were spawned. Eggs were water-hardened at the lake and transported within 12 hours to hatching trays.
- The egg count is garbled in the scan. Genetic management was a stated objective.

### 6. Stocking List, 2002–2003
- Table of bonytail chub stocking events (Dexter, Willow Beach, Achii Hanyo, Partners Point, BW Refuge) with fish at least 250–300 mm.
- The table total reads 21,573 (OCR-uncertain). Individual lots range from roughly 250 to 2,400 fish.

### 7. Planned Activities for 1994
- Continue monitoring existing facilities.
- Develop one or two growout facilities on Imperial NWR, one on Cibola NWR, and three on Havasu NWR.
- Negotiate with CRIT for a series of ponds to grow out larvae collected or produced from [[Lake Mohave]] fish.

### 8. Coordination
- The Arizona and CRIT tribes were contacted about their ponds for native fish. Their reply was not received before the report was written.
