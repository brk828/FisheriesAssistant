# Field Columbian Museum Zoology Vol. V: Systematic Accounts of Mexican and Central American Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This is a scanned, partly legible page set from the *Field Columbian Museum – Zoology, Vol. V*, a taxonomic catalogue of freshwater fishes from Mexico and Central America. Most pages are heavily degraded OCR output, so only the species accounts that could be read with reasonable confidence are summarised here. The legible material covers six families and about ten species, including one new species description, with morphometric counts, colour descriptions, sizes and geographic ranges. Plate captions identify several specimens held in the [[Field Columbian Museum]]. The front matter also contains a short glossary of fin, scale and body-measurement terms.

## Background & Description

The volume follows the standard museum systematic format for each taxon:

- **Heading:** family and subfamily (for example, Subfamily Characinae), genus with authority, and sequential catalogue numbers (e.g. 37 for *Roeboides*, 83 for *R. guatemalensis*, 84 for *Symbranchus marmoratus*, 136 for *Platypoecilus variatus*, and 206–208 for three *Cichlasoma* species).
- **Diagnosis:** generic characters, such as body shape, mouth and tooth arrangement, gill openings, fins and vertebral formula.
- **Meristics:** head length, body depth, dorsal (D.), anal (A.) and scale counts, given as ratios of head length or body length.
- **Colouration and size:** live or preserved colour, banding and spotting, and total length in inches or feet.
- **Distribution:** drainage basins and localities, often with named collection sites.

The front matter defines morphological terms used across the catalogue. Fin spines are described as stiff, unbranched structures, and some fins are preceded by a spine formed from modified soft rays. Scales are classed as cycloid (smooth posterior margin, common in soft-rayed fishes) or ctenoid (rough or toothed margin, characteristic of spiny-rayed fishes). Standard length is measured from the tip of the upper jaw to the base of the caudal fin, and the caudal peduncle is measured at its narrowest point.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The legible accounts place the species in a range of freshwater settings:

- **Pacific slope and Central American rivers:** *[[Roeboides Guatemalensis]]* is recorded from rivers on the Pacific slope of the Isthmus of Tehuantepec and in Central America, including San Gerónimo and the Río Chagres.
- **Tropical streams:** *[[Symbranchus Marmoratus]]* occurs in tropical streams from Vera Cruz to the Amazon.
- **Lowland Gulf drainages:** *[[Platypoecilus Variatus]]* is known from the basins of the Río Pánuco and Río Soto la Marina, in lowland streams. Localities include Valles, Victoria and Rascón.
- **Large Gulf rivers:** *[[Cichlasoma Parma]]* inhabits large rivers of Mexico that drain to the Gulf south of Vera Cruz. The author describes it as one of the most abundant cichlids in southern Mexico.
- **Tropical American rivers:** *[[Cichlasoma Melanurum]]* is recorded from large rivers of tropical America south of Vera Cruz and Tehuantepec, including Lago de Petén in Guatemala.
- **Southern Mexico to Honduras:** *[[Cichlasoma Octofasciatum]]* is reported from southern Mexico to Honduras.

The volume also notes the importance of museum collecting. Specimens are listed by locality and catalogue number, and several plates depict specimens from the [[Field Columbian Museum]] collection.

## Key Findings

1. **New species description.** *Platypoecilus variatus* sp. nov. is described from a type specimen (No. 4501, 1.35 in, from Valles, San Luis Potosí). Diagnostic features include a head about one-third of body length, depth 2.4–2.67 times in length, 10 dorsal rays, 6 or 7 anal rays, 9–25 scales, 15 gill rakers, and vertebrae 14+14 = 28. In males the anal fin is modified into an intromittent organ. Largest male: 1.87 in.

2. **Colour variation.** Colour markings in *P. variatus* are described as the most variable of any species the author examined. Some males have four black vertical bars, and the black caudal spot and crescent may be present, reduced or absent.

3. **Characin morphology.** *Roeboides guatemalensis* (Günther) has 9 dorsal rays, about 50 anal rays (OCR-uncertain), 86 scales, and a deeply forked caudal fin. The largest specimen recorded is 1.75 in, and a 3 in specimen was collected at San Gerónimo. The genus is characterised by conical teeth, adipose fin presence and 34 vertebrae (12+22).

4. **Symbranchid eel.** *Symbranchus marmoratus* is eel-shaped and lacks paired fins. Its gill openings are confluent in a single slit, and it has no air bladder. Vertebrae are counted as 79+57 = 136. The species grows to 3–5 feet and is brownish and variously marbled.

5. **Cichlid diagnostics.** The three *Cichlasoma* accounts give diagnostic counts:
   - *C. octofasciatum* (length 5 in): seven obscure dark bands, a spot on the third band, and light blue spots on the head and scales.
   - *C. parma* (about 1.25 in): seven dark vertical bars and an indistinct longitudinal band. It is deeper than *C. melanurum* and lacks a prominent lateral band. Scales are recorded as 7-33-12.
   - *C. melanurum*: about seven black vertical bars, a thick lip frenum in adults, and scales recorded as 6-34-11 (OCR-uncertain).

6. **Plate evidence.** Plates depict *Cichlasoma eigenmanni* (No. 3412), *Xiphophorus helleri* (No. 4668), *X. jalapensis* (No. 124), *C. parma*, *Cichlasoma* sp., and a *Pomadasys* species, among others. Plate references confirm the holdings of the [[Field Columbian Museum]], but the OCR does not allow each plate to be matched to a specific account with confidence.

7. **Limitations of the scan.** Most of the document is garbled, and large portions of text and plates cannot be read. Some numerical values are uncertain and are marked above. The figures should be checked against the original volume before being used in quantitative work.
