# Biological Opinion on Repair and Maintenance of a 4-Wheel Drive Road on the Lower San Francisco River, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) biological opinion, dated April 15, 1997, evaluates the Forest Service's proposed repair and maintenance of seven of 26 low-water ford crossings on an unnumbered high-clearance 4-wheel drive road along the lower [[San Francisco River]] upstream from Clifton, Greenlee County, Arizona. The Forest Service is lead agency, with the [[Bureau of Land Management]] as a joint agency for a small portion of the work. Two listed species are addressed: the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and the endangered [[American Peregrine Falcon]] (*Falco peregrinus anatum*).

The Service concluded that the project, as proposed, is **not likely to jeopardize** either species. The Service did not concur with the Forest Service's earlier "not likely to adversely affect" finding for the peregrine falcon, because the work would now occur during the breeding season. An [[Incidental Take Statement]] was issued with quantified triggers for reinitiating consultation.

## Background & Description

**Project purpose.** The road gives access to the privately owned RU Ranch, an inholding within the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]]. The access standard is high-clearance 4-wheel drive, and the project does not intend to increase traffic or allow other vehicle types.

**Scope.** The track runs roughly 8.7 miles along the river (Township 35 North, Range 30 East). Work is limited to the approaches of seven crossings, mostly within the lower 5.5 miles. It is described as a one-time event lasting about one week. Work includes:

- Removing blow sand from ascension and descension points and uncovering a hardened gravel base.
- Adding gravel to the roadway to replace blow sand and prevent water-collecting depressions.
- Obtaining fill from side drainages (Hackberry and Santa Cruz canyons) rather than importing it from outside the basin.
- Placing large rocks and boulders at key access points to discourage secondary travelways.
- Removing some large rocks from one crossing's wetted channel, with heavy equipment driven across the first 15 crossings.

The work is performed by Greenlee County under the supervision of the County roads supervisor and a Forest Service district biologist.

**Consultation history.** Informal consultation began in winter 1995–96. Formal consultation was initiated on September 27, 1996. The Service agreed in November 1996 to prioritize this project alongside the [[Blue River Road]] consultation, with biological opinions targeted for May 1, 1997 and on-the-ground work completed by September 30, 1997. State emergency funding supported the timeline.

**Species background: Loach Minnow.** A small, slender, benthic fish with upwardly directed eyes. It was listed as threatened on October 28, 1986. Its range has declined by about 85 percent through non-native fish competition and predation and habitat destruction. Key biology:

- Lives about 2 years and feeds exclusively on aquatic insects.
- Spawns March through May, with reported autumn spawning under some conditions.
- Eggs are attached to the underside of rocks; males may guard the nest.
- Genetic work (Tibbets, 1992) recommended managing genetically distinct units separately.
- Its status is declining rangewide. A petition to uplist to endangered was found warranted, but reclassification is precluded by higher-priority work.

**Species background: American Peregrine Falcon.** Listed as endangered on October 13, 1970. No critical habitat has been designated. The subspecies is recovering and a delisting proposal was advanced in 1995. Falcons feed almost entirely on birds, succeed in only 10–40 percent of hunts, and may hunt up to 10 miles from the nest during breeding. The breeding season in the Southwest runs from March 1 to late June or early July. The [[Peregrine Falcon Recovery Plan]] recommends protecting habitat within 10 miles of an eyrie and within 1 mile of the nesting cliff.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Environmental baseline.** The lower San Francisco River has been substantially modified over more than 150 years by beaver trapping, agriculture, livestock grazing, roads, mining, timber harvest and fuelwood cutting. Large floods between 1890 and 1906 accelerated erosion, producing the current channel. The river is now a wide, unvegetated, braided channel of cobble, gravel, boulder and sand. Riparian vegetation is sparse and lacks herbaceous cover. Non-native species include red shiner, channel catfish, flathead catfish, mosquitofish, carp, fathead minnow, smallmouth bass and [[Red Shiner]].

**Fish community.** The lower river is depauperate. Chamberlain (1904) found no fish in surveys from the river mouth to the Blue River. Surveys in 1979 found "few individual fishes and little biomass," and 1994–96 surveys found low numbers. Four other native fishes remain: speckled dace, longfin dace, desert sucker and Sonora sucker. Gila chub persists only in two tributaries. The Service estimates eight native species have been extirpated in the past century, which it characterizes as over 60 percent of the native fauna.

**Peregrine habitat.** No active eyries are known within 10 miles of the project, and no falcons have been observed in the project area. The nearest known sighting was along the Blue River about 6 air-miles away. Of the 5.5 river miles affected, only about 1 mile of suitable nesting habitat is estimated. Rocky outcrops are mostly low and small.

**Cumulative effects.** Most land in the action area is federal, so future grazing and timber actions would require section 7 consultation and are not counted as cumulative. Private land downstream contributes to the loss of loach minnow populations, but does not affect the population within the project area directly.

## Key Findings

**Conclusion.** Implementation of the project as proposed is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the Loach Minnow or the American Peregrine Falcon.

**Informal concurrences.** The Service concurred that the project is not likely to adversely affect the razorback sucker, Mexican gray wolf, southwestern willow flycatcher, bald eagle, Mexican spotted owl and jaguar. It did not concur for the peregrine falcon.

**Direct and indirect effects on Loach Minnow:**

- Crushing of fish and eggs by vehicles on crossings and during boulder removal. Only about 1 percent of loach minnow habitat in the action area lies within the 15 crossings.
- Sedimentation of riffles, including the roughly 23 percent of habitat directly downstream of crossings, which can smother eggs.
- Loss of riparian buffering. The road sits within the floodplain and often within the stream.
- Risk of toxic spills, including petroleum products.
- Increased use after improvement could raise mortality, sediment, unauthorized routes and channel instability. The Service notes that increased use could outweigh the erosion benefits of the project.

**Effects on Peregrine Falcon:** Disturbance from heavy machinery during breeding could cause nest abandonment. The Service notes the work would likely occur late in the breeding season, when falcons are less likely to abandon nests, and that the use of heavy machinery would occur within half a mile of the cliffs.

**Incidental Take Statement (Loach Minnow).** Take cannot be quantified directly, so it is indexed to the fish community and project parameters. Take is exceeded if:

1. More than 20 dead or dying fish of any species are found at or within 500 yards downstream of the project.
2. Equipment enters the wetted channel more than 30 feet from the centerline of any low-water crossing.
3. Any toxic material spill occurs in the Blue River or its floodplain during the project.

**Incidental Take Statement (Peregrine Falcon).** Take is expected as harm and harassment from disturbance. It is limited to no more than **two adult and four young** peregrine falcons for one year, covering reduced fledging success and abandonment of breeding sites within one-half mile of the work during the 1997 season.

**Key terms and conditions:**

- No on-site activity during loach minnow spawning seasons (March 1 to June 1 and September 1 to October 31).
- No removal of more than 5 gallons of water from the river during the project.
- Vehicle and heavy equipment use within the wetted channel limited to 30 linear feet upstream and downstream of each crossing centerline.
- Gravel borrowing from tributary alluvial fans must retain natural fan contours.
- Monitoring for dead or dying fish, with immediate telephone notification to the Service if more than 20 are found.
- A biologist must be present during project activities.
- Peregrine falcon surveys using Arizona Game and Fish Department protocol (Ward, 1994).
- A written report within 60 days of project completion.

**Conservation recommendations (discretionary).** The Service recommends exploring ways to restrict or eliminate road use along the river, such as buying out the private inholding, providing an alternative access route, or restricting use to the landowner. The Service acknowledges that non-biological factors may prevent this.

**Reinitiation triggers.** Consultation must be reinitiated if take is exceeded, if new information reveals unanticipated effects, if the action is modified, or if a new species is listed or critical habitat is designated.

**Limitations of the opinion.** The opinion does not exempt the project from the Migratory Bird Treaty Act.
