# Mexican Scientific Research Permit Requirements

#ichthyology #marine-biology #research-regulation

## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative correspondence and regulatory packet, not a fish study. A U.S. Embassy letter dated January 10, 1989 (which refers to a researcher's inquiry dated January 3, 1990) sends [[W.L. Minckley]], Professor of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]], the [[Mexican Government]]'s requirements for conducting scientific research in Mexican territory. The letter places his proposed work under the category of [[Marine Scientific Research]] not involving sea-going vessels. The packet sets out how requests must be filed, what each research type must contain, and what special conditions and suggestions apply. The text contains no species data, sampling results or ecological findings.

## Background & Description

**Scope of the regulations.** The Mexican government requires that all scientific and archaeological research conducted in Mexico, whether by Mexican or foreign researchers, be processed through diplomatic channels. Requests go to the [[Secretariat of Foreign Relations]] (SRE) in Mexico City. The rules cover individuals, institutions, organizations and government agencies, including joint ventures and invitations from Mexican bodies.

**Procedure for U.S. scientists.** Requests travel from the [[U.S. State Department]] in Washington, D.C., to the [[U.S. Embassy Mexico City]], which presents them to the SRE by [[Diplomatic Note]]. Research without this approval is considered illegal. Approval is granted only in writing, through a diplomatic note from the SRE, and may carry special conditions. Activity outside the approved scope is also treated as illegal. Compliance with the requirements does not guarantee approval, and Mexico reserves the right to deny requests for any reason it considers sufficient.

**Research categories.** The packet defines four categories, each with its own content requirements:

- **A. Marine research involving sea-going vessels** (from rubber rafts to large ships): 15 required items.
- **B. Marine research not involving vessels** (from a beach, island or shoreline): 15 required items.
- **II. Land scientific and archaeological research**, including freshwater streams, lakes, marshes and inland lagoons: 15 required items.
- **III. Aircraft overflight research** (meteorology, ornithology, topography, geology, archaeology): 14 required items.

Every marine, land and overflight request must reach the SRE at least 180 days before the first day of research.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The packet addresses the environment only through its scope and sensitivities:

- The marine category covers "all research of the flora, fauna, geophysics or oceanography of any point within Mexican waters."
- The land category covers flora, fauna, geophysics and archaeology, including freshwater habitats.
- The Mexican government is "highly sensitive" to research with resource-exploitation implications, especially minerals, metals and oil, and to [[Endangered Species Research]]. It favors work that benefits Mexico and Mexicans, and a research prospectus should "allay the fears" by emphasizing those benefits.

No species, habitat measurements, water chemistry or population data are reported.

## Key Findings

**Required content for each request type** (summarized from the 15-item marine vessel list, which is the most detailed):

1. **Dates:** specific, inclusive dates for each research period.
2. **Vessel and personnel:** vessel name, registration, captain, crew count, chief scientist and institution, and principal scientists' CVs.
3. **Research prospectus:** target organisms with common and scientific names, a detailed area chart, objectives, [[Scientific Methodology]], intended use of data, links to past or future phases, and the physical form and storage of data.
4. **Data commitment:** preliminary and final reports, with all findings, sent in four copies through the State Department.
5. **Equipment:** a complete list of scientific, film and data-recording equipment.
6. **Logistics:** port calls, itineraries, transport details, and a research coordinator.
7. **Funding:** sources of research funding and any support offered to Mexican participants.

Land and overflight requests follow the same pattern, adding lodging arrangements, local laborers and aircraft or pilot details. The overflight category also requires a flight plan and passport details for pilots and crew.

**Special notes:**
- Overland transport into Mexico requires vehicle identification, driver license details and an exact itinerary with border entry and exit points.
- Mexican-chartered vessels need only a statement of use, though researchers' travel to the vessel must still be detailed.
- Land research must specify lodging (even tents) and any local assistants and their tasks.

**Suggestions:**
- **Lead time:** submit as early as possible. Recent approvals have often arrived in the first days of the planned research, so more than the required 180 days helps avoid disruption.
- **Amendments:** any substantive change must be requested and approved by [[Diplomatic Note]] before it is put into effect.
- **Specimens:** collection of specimens, dead or alive, must be declared with collection, preservation and export-packing details. Collecting an extra specimen of each for the Mexican government is suggested.
- **Collaboration:** joint US-Mexico work is actively encouraged, and any Mexican collaborators should be named in the request ([[Mexican Collaboration]]).
- **Mexican participation:** space should be offered on research trips so the government can send deserving Mexican individuals.
- **Data return:** all data and findings must be returned to the Mexican government. Failure to do so could lead to future denial of requests.
- **Language:** requests need not be in Spanish, but a Spanish translation of the prospectus may speed processing.

**Additional requirements** the Mexican government has added:
- a letter from the director of the applicant's institution approving the research;
- a letter of support from a Mexican researcher or agency;
- a letter committing the applicant to pay expenses for a Mexican technician;
- if any collection is planned, a **collecting fee of 931,000 Mexican pesos** (nine hundred thirty-one thousand pesos), paid by international funds transfer to "La Tesoreria de la Federacion" through a bank in Mexico City, referencing the Unidad de Asuntos Internacionales of the Secretaria de Desarrollo Urbano y Ecologia.
