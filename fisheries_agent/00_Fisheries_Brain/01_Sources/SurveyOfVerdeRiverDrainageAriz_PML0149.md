# Verde River Tributary Survey for Loach Minnow (2000–2002)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is Appendix A of a final report on a survey of tributaries of the [[Verde River]] in Arizona, conducted for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to assess habitat for the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). Sampling ran from September 2000 to April 2002 at roughly 130 numbered sites across more than 30 named drainages. For each site, the appendix gives location, habitat types, substrate, current velocity, bank and riparian vegetation, sampling gear and effort, and species captured by age class.

The main result is that the survey section records no loach minnow captures. Most sites were rated poor or fair for the species, mainly because water was intermittent or absent and because [[Non-Native Fishes]] were common. A single historical record from [[Beaver Creek]] (collected by C. Hubbs in 1938) is noted, and [[Cougar Canyon]] on the Lime Creek system was judged excellent habitat and recommended for native fish reintroduction.

## Background & Description

**Target species.** The loach minnow is a small cyprinid of riffle-dominated, clear-flowing desert streams. Habitat suitability in this report is judged by riffle area, substrate size (cobble and gravel favoured over sand, silt, or large boulders), and steady, moderate flow.

**Survey design.** The contract specified sampling locations for several drainages (for example, Sycamore Creek, three sites; Alder Creek, one site; Deadman Creek, two sites; East Verde River, five sites; Wet Beaver Creek, three sites). Additional sites were sampled beyond the contract and are marked as such.

**Methods.** Standard gear in the appendix includes:
- [[Electrofishing]] (backpack "shocker" units, recorded in seconds of effort; typical runs were 60 to 4,200 seconds)
- [[Seine Netting]] (3 mm mesh, typically 1 × 1 m to 3.3 × 2 m seines, recorded as numbers of hauls)
- [[Gill Netting]] (10 m nets, 1–3 cm mesh, set for 0.5 to 14 hours)
- Dip netting and visual inspection for dry or very small waters

Fish counts are reported as young-of-year and total, with notes on parasites (black spot), genetic sampling, and gametes where relevant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most of the surveyed tributaries are intermittent. Many are dry in their lower reaches, and some are dry throughout the survey area. Habitat is often dominated by pools, with riffles in only a minority of sites. Substrates range from cobble and boulder in canyon reaches to sand, silt, and mud in lower reaches and stock tanks.

Several recurring pressures are noted:
- **Non-native fishes.** Green sunfish, smallmouth and largemouth bass, yellow and black bullhead, red shiner, fathead minnow, mosquitofish, and catfish were reported at many sites. Reports on the East Verde River, Oak Creek, Beaver Creek, and Deadman Creek describe them as limiting loach minnow potential.
- **Hydrologic disturbance.** Flooding, shifting sand, and seasonal drying are cited at several sites, including Sycamore Creek, Gap Creek, and Tangle Creek.
- **Land use.** Cattle use, human use (including a trailer and bathing supplies on Spring Creek), and fire history (Dude Creek and Moore Creek, burned about 10 years earlier) are noted.
- **Man-made habitats.** Stock tanks, troughs, and cement-lined springs (for example, [[Walnut Spring]], Alder Creek Spring, Lockwood Spring) typically hold isolated populations or are fishless.

Other native fishes recorded in the survey include [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Roundtail Chub]], and [[Gila Trout]]. [[Gila Topminnow]] persists in reintroduced populations at Walnut Spring and Lime Creek.

## Key Findings

- **No loach minnow captures.** None of the surveyed sites in the appendix lists loach minnow in its species capture data.
- **Historical record.** A 1938 collection by C. Hubbs from [[Beaver Creek]] is the only historical record mentioned. The site still has suitable habitat, but high numbers of non-native fishes limit its potential.
- **Best habitat noted.** Sites described as "good" or "excellent" for the species include:
  - [[Dude Creek]] above Control Road (steady, clear, riffle-pool habitat with cobble and gravel; the site was fishless)
  - [[West Clear Creek]] near the campground (riffle-run habitat, cobble-dominated substrate)
  - [[Oak Creek]] near Cave Springs Campground (good habitat with a large non-native population)
  - [[Lime Creek]] (good habitat; gila topminnow reintroduced in 1982)
  - [[Cougar Canyon]] (excellent habitat for chubs and loach minnow; a 4 m waterfall may limit upstream fish movement)
- **Reintroduction recommendation.** The Cougar Canyon site is judged a good candidate for native species reintroduction because it is remote, on federal land, lacks non-native fishes, and has a falls that could act as a barrier.
- **Common gamefish and nuisance species.** Green sunfish, smallmouth bass, and red shiner were among the most widely recorded species, often in high numbers.
- **Native fish at selected sites.**
  - [[Gila Chub]] were captured at Wet Bottom Creek (five fish retained for genetic analysis) and at other sites on the Verde system.
  - [[Gila Trout]] (nine total, 4–10 inches) were recorded at Dude Creek above the Highline Trail.
  - [[Roundtail Chub]] were captured at Deadman Creek and West Clear Creek.
- **Dry or fishless sites.** Numerous sites were dry or fishless, including most of the upper reaches of the drainage and several springs.
- **Species notes.** Some sites report black spot parasite infection in fishes (Sycamore Creek and Sunflower sites). Walnut Spring had an established [[Gila Topminnow]] population numbering in the thousands.
