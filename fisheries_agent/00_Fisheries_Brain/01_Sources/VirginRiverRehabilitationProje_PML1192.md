# Virgin River Rotenone Escapement Memorandum (October 1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1988 internal memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] documents the escape of the piscicide [[Rotenone]] past its intended [[Detoxification Barrier]] during the treatment of the [[Virgin River]] under the [[Native Fishes Rehabilitation Project]]. Field observations made on October 5 and 6, 1988, confirmed that the toxicant had moved downstream of the barrier near the Utah-Arizona border and was killing fish at multiple sites. The memo was addressed from the [[Research Biologist]] to the [[Hatchery Manager]] at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and was copied to regional and hatchery staff. It is a preliminary account; a full project report was to follow from Don Archer and Denise Knight.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose of memo:** Regional Director (Region 2, Albuquerque, New Mexico) requested observations on the passage of fish toxicant past the detoxification barrier during the Virgin River rehabilitation project, which the memo describes as occurring the prior week.
- **Project:** The [[Native Fishes Rehabilitation Project]] treated the Virgin River in Utah with rotenone to remove non-native fish, with a detoxification station intended to neutralize the toxicant before it reached Arizona waters.
- **Author:** Don Archer, Research Biologist, reporting to Buddy Jensen, Hatchery Manager, Dexter National Fish Hatchery, New Mexico.
- **Field party:** Don Archer, Terry Mickman (named "Terry Illcknan" in the scan), Denise Knight, and Chris Taylor, with Dr. Paul Marsh and his student crew working in the area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo records a sequence of fish kills and stressed fish moving downstream over roughly 24 hours, indicating a progressive downstream wave of toxicant:

- **Round Valley (downstream of Bloomington, Utah), October 5:** Around noon, the field team placed a cage of live fish in the river to determine when rotenone reached that point. Dr. Marsh's crew reported no dead fish on arrival the previous evening, but by about 8:00 AM on October 5 they observed dead fish along the banks.
- **Utah-Arizona border barrier:** Dr. Marsh observed dead fish downstream of the barrier, the point where detoxification was supposed to occur. This was the first evidence that the toxicant had passed the barrier. Fish in this section had been dead for some time.
- **Black Rock Campground (rest stop), October 5, about 3:00 PM:** Fish were stressed but few were dead; the material had only just arrived. Over 10 minutes at a sandbar about 10 feet wide, the observer counted:
  - 5 suckers
  - 17 red shiners
  - 1 chub
  - 1 dace
  
  The count was acknowledged as a partial sample, since fish moved off the far edge of the sandbar into deeper water.
- **Beaver Dam (about 10 highway miles below Black Rock), October 5:** No stressed or dead fish were observed.
- **Black Rock Campground, October 6, about 9:30 AM:** Fish were obviously stressed. The only live fish seen were a couple of bullheads.
- **Beaver Dam, October 6, about 10:15 AM:** Many stressed fish were passing in the current, and some were at the stream edge. Most were still alive, but all were stressed.
- **Mesquite-Bunkerville Bridge, October 6, about 10:30 AM:** The team checked whether rotenone had reached this site and telephoned Don Archer at St. George to report. The scan is partly illegible on the outcome of the fish observations at this site.

## Key Findings

1. **Barrier failure:** Rotenone passed the detoxification barrier near the Utah-Arizona border, and dead fish were found downstream of it. The memo notes this as the first indication of breach.
2. **Downstream progression:** The toxicant was observed moving through Round Valley (October 5, morning), Black Rock Campground (October 5, afternoon), Beaver Dam (October 5 without effects, then stressed fish October 6), and toward the Mesquite-Bunkerville Bridge.
3. **Species affected:** Observed fish included [[Sucker]]s, [[Red Shiner]]s, a [[Chub]], a [[Dace]], and [[Bullhead]]s. Stressed or dead fish were recorded at multiple sites, and only a few bullheads were noted as live at Black Rock on October 6.
4. **Limited quantitative data:** The sandbar count at Black Rock (5 suckers, 17 red shiners, 1 chub, 1 dace in 10 minutes) is the only numeric fish census in the memo, and it is explicitly partial.
5. **Recommendations and next steps:** The memo recommends that a full project report be prepared by Don Archer and Denise Knight, and it includes the field observations as the preliminary record. The author expresses confidence in that report.
