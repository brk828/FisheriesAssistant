# Diversity, Distribution, and Conservation Status of Native Freshwater Fishes of the Southern United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This review, prepared by the [[Southeastern Fishes Council]] (SFC) Technical Advisory Committee and published in *Fisheries* (Vol. 25, No. 10, October 2000), synthesizes the diversity, distribution, and conservation status of the native fish fauna of the southern United States. The authors tallied 662 native freshwater and diadromous fishes and 24 marine fishes that are significant components of freshwater ecosystems. Of these, 560 freshwater species are described and 49 undescribed species are included provisionally.

Roughly 28% (187 taxa) of southern freshwater and diadromous fishes are classed as extinct, endangered, threatened, or vulnerable. The authors report that jeopardized fishes have increased sharply since earlier assessments, and they argue that conservation must operate at the scale of watersheds and landscapes rather than one species at a time.

## Background & Description

The region is the richest freshwater fish area in North America north of Mexico. Its diversity is concentrated in families including [[Darters]] (Percidae), [[Minnows and Shiners]] (Cyprinidae), [[Suckers]] (Catostomidae), [[Sunfishes]] (Centrarchidae), [[Madtom Catfishes]] (Ictaluridae), and [[Sturgeons]] (Acipenseridae). Sizable groups of cavefishes, sculpins, and topminnows are also represented.

The authors defined the study area as the southern states listed above, plus the Delmarva Peninsula, southern West Virginia, southern Missouri, southeastern Kansas, eastern Oklahoma, and the Texas Gulf Slope (excluding the Rio Grande drainage). They divided the region into 51 hydrologic units, delimited by fish faunal similarity, historical biogeography, drainage proximity, and shared outlets such as [[Chesapeake Bay]], [[Albemarle and Pamlico Sounds]], and [[Lake Pontchartrain]].

Inclusion followed the taxonomy of Robins et al. (1991), with additions from later publications. Undescribed taxa were included provisionally "in the spirit of tallying potential diversity." Conservation categories follow [[American Fisheries Society]] (AFS) criteria: Extinct (X), Endangered (E), Threatened (T), Vulnerable (V), and Candidate or Special Concern (CS), with XS denoting extinction within the study area.

The review was motivated by a recognized gap: the taxonomic and distributional information needed by resource managers and policymakers was scattered or unpublished. The SFC was modeled on the [[Desert Fishes Council]] and was created in 1997 to bridge scientific discovery and management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Threats.** The authors identify pervasive habitat degradation as the main driver of decline. Major stressors include:

- **Impoundment and flow alteration:** Dams and channel modifications have fragmented rivers and blocked spawning runs of many anadromous species, including [[Gulf Sturgeon]] and [[Atlantic Sturgeon]].
- **Urbanization and point-source pollution:** These affect rivers such as the Etowah, and many small to medium-sized streams.
- **Groundwater and spring degradation:** Springs and caves face water withdrawals and pollution, with cave populations described as "extremely sensitive to groundwater pollution."
- **Land ownership:** Nearly 20% of forested land in the region is held by nonindustrial private owners, many absentee, in parcels of one to several hundred acres. Only a small share of forested watersheds is in public ownership.

**Range restriction.** Many species have small ranges and specialized habitat needs. Endemic and spring-associated fishes are especially vulnerable to fragmentation and isolation.

**Related taxa.** Freshwater mussels are also heavily affected: of 269 species in the southeastern United States, 12% are extinct and 60% jeopardized. Gastropods and aquatic reptiles show similar patterns.

## Key Findings

### Diversity and taxonomy
- **Total native fauna:** 662 native freshwater and diadromous fishes, plus 24 marine fishes that regularly enter freshwater.
- **Described species:** 560 freshwater species, with 49 undescribed species included provisionally.
- **Subspecies:** 36 described subspecies are recognized within 43 freshwater species. Six fishes contain undescribed subspecies, and nine are recognized as complexes of undescribed taxa.
- **Hidden diversity:** The authors state their accounting is conservative and that many undescribed taxa will likely be validated through phylogenetic and biotechnological analyses.

### Distribution
- **Endemism:** 28% of southern fishes (183 taxa) are restricted to a single drainage unit.
- **Unique taxa:** 37 of 51 drainage units contain at least one unique fish taxon.

### Conservation status
- **Overall:** 187 taxa (28%) are extinct, endangered, threatened, or vulnerable.
- **Extant species:** 41 (6%) endangered, 46 (7%) threatened, and 101 (15%) vulnerable. Five marine fishes that frequent freshwater are regarded as vulnerable.
- **Extinct throughout range:** [[White-Line Topminnow]] (*Fundulus albolineatus*), [[San Marcos Gambusia]] (*Gambusia georgei*), and [[Harelip Sucker]] (*Moxostoma lacerum*).
- **Extirpated from the study region:** Greater redhorse (*Moxostoma valenciennesi*) and [[Headwater Catfish]] (*Ictalurus lupus*).
- **Possibly extinct but classed as endangered:** Maryland darter (*Etheostoma sellare*) and smalleye shiner (*Notropis buccula*).
- **Federally listed example:** The [[Cape Fear Shiner]] (*Notropis mekistocholas*) is federally endangered and has experienced recent population declines.

### Trends
- **Increase since earlier assessments:** The 1979 assessment (Deacon et al.) and the 1989 assessment (Williams et al.), which placed 109 southern fishes in jeopardized categories, are both surpassed. The authors report a 75% increase since 1989 and a 125% increase over about 20 years.
- **Severity shift:** Endangered-category fishes have more than doubled in this period. Jeopardized species are "successively being moved from the vulnerable category to that of imminent threat of extinction."
- **Projection:** Based on a future extinction rate of 2.4% per decade for freshwater fish in the United States (Ricciardi and Rasmussen 1999), about 10% of the continental freshwater fish fauna could be extinct by 2050.

### Conservation recommendations and conclusions
- The SFC intends the list as a **planning tool** to set priorities for status surveys, taxonomic work, and recovery planning for jeopardized species.
- The authors call for the ichthyological community to resolve undescribed taxa and examine subspecies and complexes.
- Conservation of southern fishes requires **landscape-scale management** of land and water, not only single-species recovery.
- The authors urge that the biological warnings of degradation be communicated to the public, politicians, and policymakers "amid the noisy chaos of short-term economic gain and unsustainable development."
- Private landowners and urban residents must be involved in protecting and restoring southern aquatic resources.

### Key cited sources
- Deacon et al. (1979) and Williams et al. (1989, 1993): earlier AFS conservation assessments.
- Ricciardi and Rasmussen (1999): extinction-rate projections.
- Etnier (1997): assessment of jeopardized southeastern fishes.
