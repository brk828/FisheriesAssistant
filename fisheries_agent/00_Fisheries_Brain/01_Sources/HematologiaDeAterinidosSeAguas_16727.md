# Hematología de Aterínidos de Aguas Dulces: Género Chirostoma spp. del Lago de Pátzcuaro, Mich.

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This study establishes baseline haematological parameters for the native freshwater silversides of the genus [[Chirostoma]] in [[Lake Pátzcuaro]], Michoacán, Mexico. Blood from 50 seine-caught fish (standard length 130–286 mm) was sampled by cardiac puncture and analysed for red cell, white cell and thrombocyte counts, haemoglobin, haematocrit, leucocyte differential counts and enzyme cytochemistry. Because individual variation within a single species was too large for species-specific reference tables, results are reported at genus level. Most abnormal findings (anaemia, immature and polychromatophilic erythrocytes, microcytosis, hypochromia) are attributed to haematophagous [[Leech Infestation]] (Hirudinea), and lymphopenia with neutrophilia is linked to parasitism. The authors argue that haematology can serve as an indicator of the clinical state of fish in degrading aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description

- **Taxon:** Genus [[Chirostoma]] (family Atherinidae), known locally as *pescado blanco* (larger species) and *charales* (smaller species). The lake's native ichthyofauna comprises 10 species in three families (Goodeidae, Cyprinidae, Atherinidae); six introduced species belong to Centrarchidae, Cyprinidae and Cichlidae.
- **Rationale:** Comparative haematology, developed from human clinical methods, had been applied mainly to freshwater Cyprinidae and Salmonidae and marine teleosts. No haematological data existed for native Atherinidae, so the study was intended as a reference point for assessing health and disease.
- **Context in fish health:** The authors cite trout studies in which blood changes signal viral diseases (e.g. [[Viral Haemorrhagic Septicaemia]]), toxicant exposure (lead, cadmium, copper) and stress, showing the diagnostic value of blood profiles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study lake:** [[Lake Pátzcuaro]] is an endorheic basin on the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt (Cordillera Neovolcánica). Maximum surface area is 11,775 ha (including about 300 ha of islands and 500 ha of marshes); the catchment covers about 66,000 ha. Maximum recorded depth is 12 m (November 1984) and maximum recorded volume is 285 × 10⁶ m³ (October 1987).
- **Climate:** Temperate, mean annual temperature 16 °C, mean annual precipitation about 1000 mm, with rains from June to November (Köppen C(W)).
- **Vegetation:** Pine and oak forest in the catchment; aquatic macrophytes (emergent, floating-leaved and free-floating) colonise the shallows, where leeches nest and the fish spawn.
- **Degradation:** Limnological studies describe increasing [[Eutrophication]] and loss of volume, driven by catchment deforestation, water extraction for irrigation and domestic use, aquatic weed invasion and contaminant input into a closed basin.
- **Fishery pressure:** Chirostoma supports a major tourist and economic resource, under pressure from destructive seine nets (*chinchorro*) and rising numbers of fishers.
- **Parasites:** Chirostoma carries heavy internal and external infections by trematodes (notably *Posthodiplostomum*), nematodes, cestodes and hirudineans, which further reduce condition and growth.

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- 50 Chirostoma were seine-caught from commercial catches in the north basin (off Ichupio, San Jerónimo, San Andrés, Oponguio) and south basin (Uranden, Janitzio to the Erongarícuaro shore).
- Blood was drawn by cardiac puncture (1 ml syringe, 23 G needle) into Wintrobe anticoagulant; smears were made in the field and air-dried.
- Erythrocytes and leucocytes were counted in a Neubauer chamber with Rees-Eckert diluent; thrombocytes were estimated indirectly from smears; haemoglobin was measured as cyanmethaemoglobin (Drabkin's solution).
- Cytochemistry used Wright stain, PAS, myeloperoxidase (pH 6.3), and acid and alkaline phosphatase.
- Each fish was recorded for site, date, standard and total length, weight and external parasites (leeches on mouth and gills).

Thrombocytes ranged from 27 to 63 × 10³/mm³ (mean 47.5 × 10³/mm³). Correlations among red-series parameters were computed, with the strongest relationship between erythrocyte count and haemoglobin.

**Erythrocytes**
- Smears from anaemic fish showed immature red series cells, polychromatophilic erythroblasts (up to 480 per 100 leucocytes in some cases), nearly empty erythrocytes (up to 400 per 100 leucocytes), microcytosis and hypochromia.
- The authors interpret this as erythroid regeneration or hyperplasia compensating for blood loss to leeches, with a defect in haemoglobinisation.

**Leucocyte differential**
- [[Lymphocytes]] averaged 83% (range 75–92%), about 5% of them large lymphocytes.
- Neutrophilic granulocytes: G1b 3.6% (1–6%), G2 2.5% (1–4%), G3 4.2% (1–9%; up to 16–28% in heavy parasitism). The three types show strong PAS and peroxidase reactions; only G2 is alkaline-phosphatase positive. Their morphology resembles phagocytes described in elasmobranchs.
- [[Monocytes]] averaged 5.3% (1–10%).
- [[Eosinophils]] were found in only 2 of 50 fish, both heavily infected with leeches (one with 13 leeches and neutrophilia).
- No basophils were found.
- Thrombocytes appear as spindle, teardrop or round forms, or as naked nuclei in clumps that can be mistaken for lymphocytes.
- Unidentified immature-looking mononuclear cells were also present and may be blast forms released from haematopoietic organs under stress or disease.

**Lymphopenia and neutrophilia (Table 5)**
- Ten fish with lymphopenia (56–80% lymphocytes) showed frequent rises in one neutrophil type, anaemia and erythrocyte abnormalities. Lymphopenia did not track leech numbers closely.
- The authors discuss stress (acute versus chronic, via the hypothalamic-pituitary-interrenal axis and cortisol) as a possible driver, but conclude that the short interval between capture and sampling argues against stress alone, so the changes likely reflect clinical state.

**Parasite epidemiology**
- Earlier surveys (Sabanero et al.) reported 77% of fish infected in the north (mean 2.5 leeches per fish) and 67% in the south (3 per fish).
- The present study found 50% infection between Erongarícuaro and Janitzio, away from emergent vegetation where leeches nest and fish spawn.
- Leeches were found in the mouth in almost all cases and on the gills in one.

**Conclusions**
1. Normal and pathological ranges of haematological parameters are given for Chirostoma.
2. Marked anaemia, microcytosis and immature erythrocytes indicate active erythropoiesis compensating for haematophagous leech infestation.
3. White cell series varies qualitatively and quantitatively, probably in response to internal and external parasites, stress and pollution.
4. Lymphocytes make up 83% of leucocytes; monocytes tend to rise alongside neutrophils.
5. Neutrophil increases are associated with external parasitism.
6. Eosinophils and basophils are rare or absent.
7. Haematology can provide valuable indicators of clinical status in species confined to degraded water bodies.

**Recommendations for future work:** histological, cytological and cytochemical studies of blood and haematopoietic organs, and investigation of whether the fish eat leeches or ingest them while feeding.
