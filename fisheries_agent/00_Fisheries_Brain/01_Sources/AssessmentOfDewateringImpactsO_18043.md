# Assessment of Dewatering Impacts on Stream Fisheries in the Arkansas and Cimarron Rivers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report assesses the status of fishes in the [[Arkansas River]] and the streams south of its mainstream in southwestern Kansas, including the [[Cimarron River]]. Fieldwork on 16–23 July 1983 visited 35 sites; 23 had water and 12 were dry. Combined with historical collections (1853 to present), the records cover 46 localities and 42 species. The central finding is that altered stream flow, driven mainly by irrigation diversion, [[John Martin Reservoir]] regulation, and groundwater withdrawal, has dewatered the western basin and depleted the native prairie fauna. Several flood-dependent species of primary concern have declined sharply or disappeared from the study area.

## Background & Description

The study was funded by a Nongame Wildlife Contract from the Kansas Fish and Game Commission and conducted by the [[University of Kansas Museum of Natural History]]. Its objectives were to:

- locate populations of species characteristic of the western Plains segment of the Arkansas basin;
- assess threat by comparing current and historical distributions; and
- test relationships between changes in stream discharge and changes in species status.

Species of primary concern were:

- [[Speckled Chub]] (Arkansas River form, *Hybopsis aestivalis tetranemus*)
- [[Flathead Chub]] (*Hybopsis gracilis*)
- [[Emerald Shiner]] (*Notropis atherinoides*)
- [[River Shiner]] (*Notropis blennius*)
- [[Arkansas River Shiner]] (*Notropis girardi*)
- [[Plains Minnow]] (*Hybognathus placitus*)
- [[Plains Killifish]] (*Fundulus zebrinus*, with *F. kansae* considered conspecific)
- [[Arkansas Darter]] (*Etheostoma cragini*)

Baseline historical data come from Cragin (1885) and Gilbert (1885, 1886) at Garden City and in the Medicine Lodge drainage. Most comparative collections (1951–67) used similar haul seines. From about 1962 onward, quantitative counts accompany most collections. Discharge records came from USGS gaging stations, grouped into flow-duration and seasonal periods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology.** Gaging records show that the western Arkansas and Cimarron have lost most of their flow:

- **Syracuse (Arkansas):** flows exceeded 10% of the time fell from 980 cfs (1904–42) to 450 cfs (1943–65) and to roughly 165 cfs (1977–82). Minimum flows (95% exceedance) dropped from 5.7 cfs (1966–76) to about 1 cfs. The channel has been dry or below 1 cfs more than 10% of the time from September through February.
- **Dodge City:** 10%-exceedance flows were 330 cfs (1945–65) and 240 cfs (1966–76). The report states flows were 1.3 cfs in subsequent years, and flows exceeded 1 cfs only 10% of the time after 1976.
- **Great Bend:** high flows (10%) fell from 820 to 440 to 136 cfs across three periods.
- **Wichita:** flows changed little. High flows fell from 2400 cfs (1935–65) to 1850 cfs (1977–82), while low flows rose from 53 to 140 cfs, likely from treated groundwater and municipal discharge.
- **Cimarron at K-23:** 10%-exceedance flows fell from 110 to 76 cfs. Peak flows over 4000 cfs occurred in 16 of 21 years before 1960 but in only 3 of 18 later years.
- **Crooked Creek:** mean annual peak discharge was about 6000 cfs in the 1950s and about 1400 cfs in the 1970s.

Flood-flows that trigger spawning have declined markedly. [[Crooked Creek]], the [[Medicine Lodge River]], and the Cimarron show few or no May–August flows above 2000 cfs after about 1964, while the [[Ninnescah River]] shows little change in peak flows but altered mid-range flows after [[Cheney Reservoir]] was built in 1964.

**Habitat.** Historic channels were wide, braided, and sandy. The Arkansas at Lakin was about 1200 ft wide originally, and was 97 ft in 1983. Channels are now dry for long reaches, with isolated pools colonized by [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Mosquitofish]], and [[Red Shiner]].

**Major drivers.** The report identifies diversion of surface and groundwater for irrigation as the most important factor, along with impoundments, altered water sources, municipal discharge, cultivation, and introductions. Channel and water-quality changes are intertwined with these.

## Key Findings

- **Arkansas River Shiner:** This endemic species declined severely. It was abundant at Oxford (484 specimens in 1955; 281 in 1967), but was absent from Oxford in 1983. It was found in 1983 only at two Cimarron sites: Clark County (4 specimens, <1% of catch) and Meade County (38 specimens, 8%). Western populations collapsed first. The decline also coincides with the spread of [[Red River Shiner]], a competitor first recorded in 1972.
- **Plains Minnow:** Distribution has receded. It was last recorded in Kearny County in 1972 and was rare at Oxford in 1983 (4 specimens per sample). The best 1983 population was on the Cimarron at K-23, where it made up 23% of the catch.
- **Plains Killifish:** It persists in the western basin, occurring at 17 of 22 sites sampled in July 1983. Its abundance is strongly correlated with salinity, and it declined from west to east. It comprised 84% of the Kinsley collection in 1926, but was absent from Cimarron, Ford, and Kinsley in 1983.
- **Speckled Chub and Flathead Chub:** The Speckled Chub was last reported from the lower mainstream near Derby in 1975 and has not been found at the 1979–83 sites. The Flathead Chub was last captured in Hamilton and Kearny counties in 1977 and has not been found in the Cimarron since 1968.
- **Arkansas Darter:** Its type locality at Garden City is no longer occupied, and mainstream records are limited to vagrants at Oxford in 1955 and 1978. It persists in the Cimarron, Salt Fork, Medicine Lodge, and Ninnescah drainages. It had not been recorded in the Cimarron before 1980, which suggests altered flows favor it.
- **Mosquitofish and Red Shiner:** These introduced or opportunistic species dominate the intermittent reaches. At Ford, Mosquitofish comprised 74% of the 1983 catch. Red Shiners increased in relative abundance and now outnumber Sand Shiners at 27 of 40 stations where both occurred (1979–83).
- **Community composition:** Of 70 kinds of fishes reported from the Arkansas and Cimarron systems, 12 are introduced, 4 may owe their presence to introductions, and 54 are probably native. Eighteen species were not in the earliest collections. The 1883–86 western fauna included 19 species, but recent surveys show a shift toward eastern and forested-watershed fishes.
- **Medicine Lodge drainage:** Similarity to the 1886 species list was 78% in 1958 and 57% in 1979 and 1983. Peak flows in the most recent decade are generally under half those of the first decade of record.
- **Conclusions and conservation:** The authors conclude that the native prairie fauna has been "caught in a crunch" by dewatering, while eastern species have expanded westward. Conserving species such as the Arkansas River Shiner and Speckled Chub may conflict with water-management goals. Two ranch properties, the Anchor D (Robbins) Ranch in Kiowa County and the XIT (Adams) Ranch in southwestern Meade County, retain the best representation of the original Plains fauna because of conservative land use and favorable aquifer locations.
