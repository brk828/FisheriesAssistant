# Survey of Salt River Project Canals in the Phoenix Metropolitan Area, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1996 summary report by Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]] documents fish occurrences in the [[Arizona Canal]] and [[South Canal]] of the [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) irrigation system in the [[Phoenix Metropolitan Area]]. The work was submitted to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under federal grant 6-FG-32-00480. Surveys were conducted in autumn-winter 1995, when canal dewatering allowed access. Twenty-two fish species were collected or observed across the two canals. The Arizona Canal held 20 species and the South Canal held 18. Native species were a minority of the fauna, and no substantive change in the fish community was detected since the last published account, apart from the stocking of [[Grass Carp]].

## Background & Description

The canal system is operated by SRP to deliver water for agricultural, domestic, and industrial use. Water is diverted at [[Granite Reef Dam]] from the Salt and Verde rivers into two main concrete canals:

- **Arizona Canal (No. 1):** serves the region north of the Salt River channel.
- **South Canal (No. 3):** serves the region south of the river bed.

The canals are colonized by fishes that move in from upstream natural watercourses. Others are stocked by SRP, or released by the public, either deliberately or inadvertently. Sampling during water delivery is difficult and hazardous, so the study relied on periodic dewaterings for inspection, maintenance, and repair, usually scheduled in autumn-winter. Data from these windows are largely qualitative.

Surveyed reaches:

- **Arizona Canal:** inspected in full from the [[Electrical Fish Barrier]] below Granite Reef Dam about 24 km downstream to the Indian Bend Wash siphon. Collection sites included immediately below the barrier, several points between Greenfield Road and Vale Vista Drive, sites 0.5 km downstream of Beeline Highway and Gilbert Road, and above the Indian Bend Wash siphon.
- **South Canal:** inspected in full from the electrical barrier about 16 km downstream to its junction with the Tempe (No. 6) and Consolidated (No. 5) canals. The [[Tempe Canal]] was examined from its origin about 7 km downstream to University Drive. Sites included the [[Central Arizona Project]] interconnect, River Road siphon, the Roosevelt Water Conservation District pumping plant, Lindsay Road, and the Chandler Falls reach.

Sampling involved personnel from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the Bureau of Reclamation, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and SRP, plus interested parties.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling dates and methods**

- The Arizona Canal was sampled on 25-26 November 1995. The South Canal was sampled on 21-22 October 1995.
- Canals were viewed from a slow-moving vehicle along the bank, with frequent stops for closer inspection and collection.
- Gear included [[Seine Sampling]] nets (1.8, 3.0, or 4.6 m long; 1.2 or 1.8 m deep; 5.2 or 6.4 mm mesh), [[Trammel Net]]s (23 or 26 m long, 1.8 m deep, 25 mm inner and 305 mm outer mesh), dip nets (3.2 mm mesh), and hand collection, selected by habitat, depth, species, size, and expected capture efficiency.
- All fish were identified to species and counted. Subsamples were measured for total length (TL, nearest mm). Selected specimens were photographed or fixed in 10% formalin and deposited as vouchers in the ASU Collection of Fishes (Department of Zoology).

**Relative abundance scale** (percentage of total fishes, as reported): rare = <2; uncommon = 3-5; common = 6-20; abundant = >21. The scale was based on collection counts plus visual assessment.

**Regional species context**

The most recent published account of canal fishes (Marsh and Minckley 1982) recorded 23 species. The total number of fish species recorded from the Phoenix metropolitan area now stands at 38.

## Key Findings

**1. Community richness**

- 22 species were collected or reliably observed across both canals in 1995.
- The Arizona Canal had 20 species; the South Canal had 18.
- Among the 22 species, four were not listed in the 1982 account: bigmouth buffalo, smallmouth bass, walleye, and grass carp. The first three occur upstream, and grass carp was stocked. Their appearance was therefore considered expected.
- Six species from the 1982 account were not found in 1995: longfin dace (native, considered rare then), fathead minnow, black bullhead, yellow bullhead, redear sunfish, and Mozambique tilapia. The authors attribute these absences to rarity, since they occur upstream or elsewhere in the area.

**2. Native species**

Three native species were encountered in both canals:

- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*): rare in both canals.
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarkii*): abundant in both canals.
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*): abundant in both canals.

Juvenile and adult life stages of all native species were collected.

**3. Dominant and common species**

- [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) was the only abundant introduced species, abundant in both canals.
- [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), stocked by SRP, was common in both canals.
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) and [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) were common in both canals.
- Most other species were uncommon or rare.

**4. Canal-specific patterns**

- Bigmouth buffalo (rare) and largemouth bass were the only species in the Arizona Canal beyond those noted above. Black crappie, green sunfish, blue tilapia, and bigmouth buffalo were not found in the South Canal, while redbelly tilapia was rare there and not found in the Arizona Canal.
- [[Smallmouth Bass]] and [[Walleye]] were not encountered downstream of the electrical barrier in either canal, though they may be present.

**5. Comparative assessment**

- No distinctive differences in species richness or relative abundance were found between the Arizona and South canals.
- Apart from the stocking of grass carp, there is no evidence of substantive change in the canal ichthyofauna since the last published report.

**6. Data limitations and recommendations**

- Data are qualitative because sampling was restricted to dewatering periods.
- Canal sampling during water delivery is hazardous and difficult, so the autumn-winter dewatering windows remain the main access route.
- Appendices A and B contain field notes, fish measurements, and data summaries for each canal. Field collection forms in the source are largely illegible in the scanned copy and are not summarized here.
