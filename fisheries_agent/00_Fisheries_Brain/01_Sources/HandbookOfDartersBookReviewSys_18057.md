# Handbook of Darters — Review Synthesis

## Executive Summary
This entry synthesizes a 1983 book review (Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences, Vol. 40) of Lawrence M. Page's *Handbook of Darters* (TFH Publications), a comprehensive taxonomic and ecological reference on the [[Darters]], a diverse group of small freshwater fishes endemic to North America, classified within the subfamily Etheostomatinae of the family [[Percidae]]. The reviewer, E. J. Crossman of the Royal Ontario Museum, praises the book as a major systematic contribution, consolidating information on morphology, distribution, ecology, and taxonomy for all 131 recognized darter species at time of publication (with three additional species — *Etheostoma baileyi*, *E. rafinesquei*, and *E. barrenense* — noted post-completion). The review highlights taxonomic revisions relative to the standard reference of the era, the *Atlas of North American Freshwater Fishes* (Lee et al. 1980).

## Background & Description
- Darters are small-bodied freshwater fishes noted for **striking and sometimes bizarre coloration**, especially pronounced during spawning periods.
- The genus *[[Etheostoma]]* comprises 91 closely related species/forms at the time of the review, necessitating careful subgeneric organization.
- Page's handbook provides **quantitative morphological tables** (counts and measurements from hundreds of specimens) summarizing characteristics across darter subgenera — considered a substantial improvement in accuracy and clarity over prior classifications.
- Notable taxonomic revision: the subgenus **Oligocephalus**, historically regarded as a poorly defined "catch-all" group of possibly unrelated forms, is given a clearer morphological diagnosis in this work.
- A tested, illustrated **taxonomic key** covering all species treated is included, designed for practical field/lab identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Darters occupy **freshwater stream and river habitats exclusively within North America**, including Mexican representatives (*Etheostoma australe*, *E. pottsi*) that extend beyond the geographic scope of the Lee et al. (1980) Atlas.
- The handbook includes dedicated chapters on **Ecology**, **Morphological Evolution**, and **Zoogeography**, addressing habitat use, evolutionary morphological trends, and biogeographic distribution patterns across the group.
- Species-level accounts include **Range** and **Natural History** sections, implying documentation of habitat associations, behavior, and reproductive ecology (notably spawning-related coloration) for each species.
- Appendices map species distributions to political/geographic areas, providing a biogeographic framework analogous to species richness by region.

## Key Findings
- **Taxonomic reconciliation**: Differences from Lee et al.'s (1980) *Atlas of North American Freshwater Fishes* include:
  - Inclusion of *[[Percina gymnocephalus]]* (undescribed at time of Atlas publication).
  - Separation of *[[Percina carbonaria]]* from *[[Percina caprodes]]* (previously lumped in the Atlas).
  - Synonymization of *Etheostoma meadiae* (Atlas) into *[[Etheostoma stigmaeum]]* under Page's treatment.
  - Addition of two Mexican species (*E. australe*, *E. pottsi*) outside the Atlas's covered territory.
- The work draws together an extensive literature base of **~360 citations**, representing a thorough consolidation of darter research to date.
- Visual documentation includes **308 color illustrations** and **89 distribution maps**, covering all 131 recognized species — a near-complete pictorial and geographic reference for the group.
- Editorial/production critiques (unrelated to scientific content) include: distribution maps and color plates being difficult to cross-reference (keyed by map/plate number rather than page number), inconsistent color fidelity in some plates, and disruptive placement of advertising within the text.
- Despite minor editorial flaws, the review concludes the handbook is an essential reference for anyone studying [[Darters]] or North American freshwater fish diversity, and a significant advance in the systematics of subgenera within *[[Etheostoma]]* and *[[Percina]]*.
