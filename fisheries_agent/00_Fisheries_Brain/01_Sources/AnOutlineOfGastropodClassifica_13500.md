# An Outline of Gastropod Classification

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gastropoda

## Executive Summary

This 1962 outline by D. W. Taylor and N. F. Sohl (U.S. Geological Survey) compiles a family- and superfamily-level classification of the class [[Gastropoda]] (snails, limpets and slugs). It draws primarily on the *Handbuch der Paläozoologie* (Wenz and Zilch) and the *Treatise on Invertebrate Paleontology* (Knight and others), supplemented by other works and reconciled through extensive annotations. The class is listed as 7,324 genera and subgenera. The document is not a fish study; it covers molluscs, and the classification is explicitly acknowledged to be partly out of date.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** The outline extends only to the family level. Families and superfamilies from sources beyond the core works were recognised conservatively. Genera described after the principal sources were rarely added, so group sizes are indicative only.
- **Main sources:** [[Treatise On Invertebrate Paleontology]] (Knight and others, 1960) for Archaeogastropoda and Paleozoic gastropods; Zilch (1959-60) for shelled Euthyneura; Wenz (1938-44) for post-Paleozoic Mesogastropoda and Neogastropoda. Additional works include Korobkov (1955), Pchelintsev and Korobkov (1960), and Termier and Termier (1952).
- **Format:** Each family carries a genus/subgenus count. Asterisks mark groups known only as fossils. Figures 1 and 2 compare relative order sizes in this classification (living and fossil) and in Thiele's (1929-35) living-only classification.
- **Multilingual abstract:** The paper includes German (Umriss einer Klassifikation der Gastropoden), French (Résumé) and Spanish (Reseña) summaries, plus a Russian abstract.
- **Index:** A scientific name index follows the classification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses taxonomy rather than habitat or ecology, so no habitat, ocean basin or location data are reported. Some notes do touch on biology relevant to classification:

- Larval shell types in Nudibranchia (Thompson, 1961): type 1 (spiral) and type 2 (egg-shaped), which never co-occur within the same family in this scheme.
- Shell reduction and the loss of shell and operculum are used as characters in cephalaspid lineages (Boettger).
- Some limpet-like freshwater snails (e.g. [[Acroloxus Lacustris]]) are treated as a separate family on egg capsule and spermatogenesis evidence.
- Several Vermetidae genera are reassigned, and four genera are thought to be annelids rather than molluscs.

## Key Findings

**Overall structure**
- Class Gastropoda: **7,324** genera and subgenera.
- Subclass [[Streptoneura]]: **4,218** genera and subgenera, divided into **3 orders**.
- Subclass [[Euthyneura]]: **3,106** genera and subgenera, divided into **14 orders**.
- The subclass names replace the traditional Prosobranchia, Opisthobranchia and Pulmonata, following Boettger (1955) and Zilch (1959-60).

**Largest orders (genera and subgenera, from the outline)**
- [[Archaeogastropoda]]: 1,130 (many families with few genera; more finely divided than other orders).
- [[Mesogastropoda]]: 1,969, the largest streptoneuran order in the count.
- [[Neogastropoda]]: 1,119 (suborders Stenoglossa and Toxoglossa).
- [[Stylommatophora]]: 2,184, the largest euthyneuran order.
- [[Basommatophora]]: 517.
- [[Nudibranchia]]: 1,631 (in the listing).

Figure 2 shows that Stylommatophora grow relatively larger in the living-only Thiele classification, mainly at the expense of Archaeogastropoda.

**Major taxonomic decisions**
- **Pyramidellidae** are moved from Mesogastropoda to Euthyneura and grouped with the Nerineacea in the order Entomotaeniata, following Cossmann's inferred phylogeny.
- **Viviparidae and Ampullariidae** are separated from Cyclophoracea into their own superfamily, Viviparacea.
- **Acroloxidae, Lancidae, Ancylidae, Planorbidae** and **Payettiidae** are reorganised out of the old composite Ancylidae and Lymnaeacea, reflecting the work of Baker (1956) and Bondesen (1950).
- **Seguenziidae** are placed in Mesogastropoda "without conviction," because no new anatomical data exist.
- **Vitrinellidae** are used in place of Adeorbidae and Tornidae, with many genera transferred from Wenz's Skeneidae and Cyclostrematidae.
- **Mitridae** are moved from Stenoglossa to Toxoglossa.
- **Epitoniidae** and **Eulimidae** replace the names Scalidae and Melanellidae, based on nomenclatural priority.
- **Bithyniidae** replace Bulimidae, and **Ciionellidae** replace Cochlicopidae, on nomenclatural grounds.

**Notes on uncertainty**
- Wenz's neogastropod families were retained because no recent monograph exists, with common alternatives given in parentheses.
- The authors describe the classification as partly out of date and including the weaknesses of its sources.
