# Letter from Irv Kornfield to Wendell Minckley on Cuatro Cienegas Cichlids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cichlidae #freshwater-biology #Cuatro-Cienegas

## Executive Summary
This 1983 letter from Irv Kornfield (University of Maine at Orono) to Wendell L. Minckley (Arizona State University) documents an early stage in the taxonomic and genetic characterization of endemic cichlid fishes from [[Cuatro Ciénegas]], a desert spring ecosystem in Coahuila, Mexico. Kornfield reports that the local cichlid population had recently received a formal taxonomic name, facilitating future literature discussion. The letter also describes an active captive-breeding program using specimens collected in June 1983, with controlled single-pair crosses established at a commercial cichlid farm in Florida to generate F1 (and eventually F2) progeny for further analysis, presumably to investigate the population's complex genetic and morphological variation. The correspondence acknowledges Minckley's earlier logistical support for Kornfield's graduate research conducted under Richard Koehn.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological descriptions, measurements, or meristic data are provided in this letter.
- The letter references ongoing morphological/genetic complexity within the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Cichlids]] species complex, implying variability that had not yet been fully resolved taxonomically prior to naming.
- Analysis of F1 and later F2 offspring was planned to help clarify inherited versus environmentally influenced traits — a classic approach to disentangling phenotypic plasticity from genetic differentiation in [[Cichlidae]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The cichlids originate from [[Cuatro Ciénegas]], an isolated desert wetland basin in the Chihuahuan Desert of Coahuila, Mexico, renowned for high levels of endemism across multiple taxa (fish, invertebrates, and microbialites).
- **System complexity**: Kornfield explicitly describes the study system as "very complex, but very rewarding," consistent with the well-documented radiation and micro-endemism of aquatic fauna within the isolated springs, pools, and channels of this basin.
- **Human intervention**: Specimens were translocated from their natural habitat to an artificial captive setting (a commercial cichlid farm in Florida) for controlled breeding experiments — a common technique for studying heritability and speciation in [[Desert Fishes]] research during this era.
- Relevant broader context: This work sits within the tradition of desert fish conservation and evolutionary biology pioneered by [[Wendell Minckley]], a leading authority on Cuatro Ciénegas and North American desert aquatic ecosystems.

## Key Findings
- As of August 1983, the Cuatro Ciénegas cichlid population had been formally assigned a scientific name, resolving prior taxonomic ambiguity referenced in the correspondence.
- Specimens collected in June 1983 were transferred to a commercial breeding facility in Florida to establish controlled **single-pair crosses**.
- First-generation (**F1**) offspring were expected by Fall 1983, with plans to analyze both parental and F1/F2 individuals — indicating an experimental breeding design aimed at assessing genetic inheritance patterns within the species complex.
- The letter credits [[Wendell Minckley]] with enabling Kornfield's earlier graduate fieldwork in Cuatro Ciénegas (conducted under advisor Richard Koehn), underscoring collaborative networks in early desert fish systematics research.
- The correspondence reflects the broader scientific effort during the early 1980s to formally describe and genetically characterize the unique, isolated cichlid lineage(s) of [[Cuatro Ciénegas]], later associated with taxa such as [[Herichthys minckleyi]].
