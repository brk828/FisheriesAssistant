# Notes on the Early Life History of Lepisosteus platostomus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Lepisosteidae #early-life-history

## Executive Summary

Field observations and laboratory study documenting eggs and newly hatched fry of [[Shortnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus platostomus*) collected from the [[Marias des Cygnes River]] in Miami County, Kansas on May 25, 1956. The study describes collection habitat, early developmental stages, and morphometric relationships between snout length and body length across various size classes. Eggs and fry were found in swift riffle habitat with flat rock and clay substrate in 2-8 inches of water depth.

## Background & Description

[[Lepisosteus platostomus]] (Shortnose Gar) is a member of the family [[Lepisosteidae]]. This study focuses on the earliest life stages, from eggs through early fry development. The species exhibits characteristic gar morphology with elongated body, distinctive snout, and well-developed fins including dorsal, caudal, and anal fins.

Collection occurred in a pool-riffle section of stream characterized by extremely swift current compared to other river portions. Water conditions were muddy with slightly elevated flow above normal levels. Bottom substrate consisted of flat rocks and solid clay.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Characteristics:**
- Swift riffle areas in pool-riffle stream sections
- Water depth: 2-8 inches at collection point
- Substrate: Flat rocks and solid clay
- Water clarity: Muddy
- Flow conditions: Slightly above normal, extremely swift current

**Microhabitat Use:**
Fry were observed beneath flat rocks along edges of swift water, present in large numbers. Eggs were found in slower water along the bank, presumably carried by current. No attachment of eggs to bottom substrate or rocks was observed.

**Behavioral Observations:**
Fry were quite active, exhibiting violent swimming movements when disturbed, indicating strong locomotory capability even at early developmental stages.

## Key Findings

**Developmental Sequence (Laboratory Observations):**

Specimens measured 1.3 mm total length showed:
- Dorsal fin: No rays evident
- Anal fin: No rays evident  
- Opercle: Not developed
- Jaws: Upper very short, lower with no trace

Specimens at 1.8 mm total length showed:
- Dorsal fin: Rays evident
- Anal fin: Rays evident
- Opercle: Developed
- Jaws: Upper developed with teeth, lower developing

Specimens at 2.4 mm total length showed:
- Dorsal fin: Rays evident
- Anal fin: Rays evident
- Opercle: Fully developed
- Jaws: Upper fully developed with teeth present, lower as long as upper

**Snout/Body Length Relationships:**

Analysis of specimens from multiple Kansas locations (Marias des Cygnes River, [[Walnut River]], [[Caney River]]) collected May-July 1956 revealed:

- 5.0 mm total length: Snout = 16.42% of body length
- 6.5 mm: Snout = 16.18%
- 9.0 mm: Snout = 19.44%
- 52.0 mm: Snout = 18.3%
- 58.0 mm: Snout = 20.7%
- 95.0 mm: Snout = 21.1%
- 108.0 mm: Snout = 20.8%
- 112.0 mm: Snout = 22.3%
- 114.0 mm: Snout = 21.9%
- 215.0 mm: Snout = 21%

Snout length as proportion of body length increases from approximately 16% in smallest fry (5-6.5 mm) to stabilize around 19-22% in larger juveniles, indicating allometric growth patterns during early development.
