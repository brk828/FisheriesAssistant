# Lower Colorado River "Big River Fishes" Management Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1995 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Fisheries and Federal Aid) directs the Arizona Fishery Resources Office to take the lead on a draft [[Lower Colorado River]] "Big River Fishes" management plan. The plan, drafted by Chuck, was mailed to 104 individuals and organizations for comment. The memorandum sets out the purposes of the plan, a schedule for consolidating comments and holding workshops, and a target completion date of May 1, 1995. The document contains no species-level biological data; its content concerns management planning, coordination, and funding.

## Background & Description

The document is a two-page memorandum dated February 17, 1995, issued from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Albuquerque office (Post Office Box 1306, Albuquerque, N.M. 87103). It is addressed to the Project Leader of the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] and concerns the "Big River Fishes" program on the lower basin of the [[Colorado River]]. The memorandum refers to the mailing list being sent electronically and to the plan being distributed to stakeholders.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memorandum identifies several contextual factors:

- **Endangered fish management:** Planning and managing for endangered fish in the lower basin is described as a high priority.
- **Basin differences:** The plan is intended to establish differences in management needs between the upper and lower basins.
- **Stakeholder conflicts:** The river has many conflicts and interests, which the memorandum notes create better funding opportunities than elsewhere in Arizona.
- **Related processes:** Comments are to be addressed in the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] biological assessment, considered in the FY 1996 Congressional appropriations process, and considered in the Service's FY 1997 budget deliberations.

The memorandum does not report species lists, population data, water chemistry, or habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

- **Plan purposes:** Serve as a starting point for a State, Federal, and private cooperative fisheries management plan; aid recovery of the fish; distinguish management needs between upper and lower basins; demonstrate Service leadership in endangered fish management; and justify Service funding needs for the lower basin.
- **Stakeholder outreach:** The draft plan was mailed to 104 individuals and organizations.
- **Comment process:** The Regional Office will forward comments to the Arizona office for consolidation and analysis. Workshops are proposed for Yuma and Lake Havasu City, with locations still to be selected.
- **Timeline:** A completed document is required no later than May 1, 1995.
- **Lead assignment:** The Project Leader is asked to assume lead responsibility. If unable to do so, or if commitments outside the White Mountains cannot be met, the Project Leader is asked to advise so that other arrangements or a review of AZFRO priorities against station funding can be made.
