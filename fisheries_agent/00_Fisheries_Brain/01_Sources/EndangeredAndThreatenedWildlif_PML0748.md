# Critical Habitat Designation for Four Endangered Colorado River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This [[Federal Register]] notice, published September 15, 1993 (Vol. 58, No. 177), is issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service). It announces the availability of a [[Biological Support Document]] for the proposed designation of [[Critical Habitat]] for four endangered Colorado River fishes: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. It also reopens the public comment period on that designation. The notice is procedural. It does not present new biological data on the fishes themselves.

## Background & Description

The four species are listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.). The proposed rule would amend 50 CFR 17.95, the section of the regulations that delineates critical habitat. It includes maps and descriptions of the areas proposed for each species.

- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family Catostomidae
- **Colorado Squawfish** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), family Cyprinidae
- **Humpback Chub** (*Gila cypha*), family Cyprinidae
- **Bonytail Chub** (*Gila elegans*), family Cyprinidae

Key procedural background:

- The Service published the proposed rule in the Federal Register in January 1993. The text gives the date as both January 20 and January 29, 1993. The comment period is stated as running from January 29 through April 15, 1993.
- The Service held public hearings and received written and oral comments on the proposal.
- The Service then prepared a Biological Support Document detailing the biological analysis used to determine critical habitat.

The notice is signed by Ralph Morgenweck, Regional Director, dated September 9, 1993. The contact is Robert D. Williams, Utah State Supervisor. The author is Harold M. Tyus of the Denver Regional Office.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document gives no ecological data for the fishes. It does not report population sizes, sample sizes, habitat measurements, water chemistry, or life-history information. The only environmental context is the geographic scope: the Colorado River system, with proposed habitat in the Colorado River Basin. The Service's critical habitat process considers the biological basis for designation, which is contained in the separate Biological Support Document. That document is not reproduced here.

The economic analysis is also described as in progress. It will examine the economic effects of the critical habitat designation.

## Key Findings

- **Reopened comment period:** The Service is reopening public comment on the Biological Support Document. The period will remain open until further notice. The original comment period ran from January 29 to April 15, 1993.
- **Document availability:** The Biological Support Document is available on request from the Service's Utah State Supervisor office in Salt Lake City, Utah. Comments and materials are available for public inspection by appointment.
- **Pending economic analysis:** An [[Economic Analysis]] and an overview document are in preparation. The Service expected them to be available to interested parties in about 60 days.
- **Planned public hearings:** After the economic analysis is complete, the Service plans eight public hearings. At least one will be held in each of Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah, and Wyoming. Hearings are meant to receive comments on the biological and economic analyses.
- **Second comment window:** Once hearings are announced, the public comment period will stay open an additional 60 days after the notice of the hearings and the economic documents is published in the Federal Register.
- **Legal authority:** The action is authorized under the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.).
