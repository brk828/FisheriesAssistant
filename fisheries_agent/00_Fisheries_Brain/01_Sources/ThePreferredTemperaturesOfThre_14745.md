# The Preferred Temperatures of Three *Semotilus* Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This study experimentally determined the **final preferred temperatures** for three sympatric/syntopic species of the minnow genus *[[Semotilus]]*: *[[Semotilus atromaculatus]]* (Creek Chub), *[[Semotilus corporalis]]* (Fallfish), and *[[Semotilus margarita]]* (Pearl Dace). Using a thermal gradient trough methodology, the researchers established that each species exhibits a statistically distinct (P < .05) thermal preference curve. These behavioral preferences correlate strongly with the known geographic distribution limits of each species, suggesting that **[[Temperature Preference]]** acts as a proximate mechanism for niche partitioning among these closely related, co-occurring fishes.

## Background & Description
*Note: This paper is primarily behavioral/physiological rather than morphological. No morphometric data (length, weight, fin ray counts) were reported. The following taxonomic distinctions from the genus are noted:*

- **Genus [[Semotilus]]**: Comprises four species — *S. atromaculatus*, *S. corporalis*, *S. lumbee*, and *S. margarita*.
- Only the three most widely distributed species (*atromaculatus*, *corporalis*, *margarita*) were tested; *S. lumbee* was excluded due to its highly restricted range (Carolina sandhills).
- Specimens were wild-caught juveniles/adults collected via seine net (3.2 m) from a single syntopic population, eliminating confounding morphological/geographic variation between species comparisons.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat & Distribution
| Species | Common Name | Distribution | Thermal Habitat Preference |
|---|---|---|---|
| *[[Semotilus atromaculatus]]* | Creek Chub | Southern Canada to Alabama; east of continental divide (absent from FL/Coastal Plain) | Ubiquitous; warmest tolerance |
| *[[Semotilus corporalis]]* | Fallfish | Atlantic slope drainages: James River (VA) to Miramichi drainage (Canada) | Larger, warmer stream reaches |
| *[[Semotilus margarita]]* | Pearl Dace | Northern US and Canada, south to northern Virginia | Cold, spring-fed headwaters and northern lakes |

### Thermal Niche Segregation
- The three species co-occur **syntopically** in the Raystown Branch of the Juniata River (Susquehanna drainage), providing a natural experiment free of confounding climatic/geographic variables.
- Field distribution data (Matthews et al. 1978; Barila et al. 1981) suggest:
  - *S. margarita* → small, spring-fed headwater areas
  - *S. corporalis* → larger, warmer stream reaches
  - *S. atromaculatus* → ubiquitous across stream types
- Authors hypothesize this thermal segregation minimizes **interspecific competition**, analogous to findings in sympatric *[[Ambystoma]]* salamander larvae (Stauffer et al. 1983).
- *S. margarita*'s Canadian distribution has been previously linked to the 12.98–15.6°C July isotherm (Ryder et al. 1964; Loch 1969), which aligns closely with this study's experimental result.

## Key Findings

### Experimental Design
- Fish acclimated at capture temperature for 5 days, then gradually acclimated (≤1°C/day) to test temperatures: **6, 12, 18, 24, 30, and 33°C**.
- *S. margarita* could **not** be acclimated to 33°C — repeated attempts resulted in mortality, indicating a lower thermal tolerance ceiling for this species.
- Preference tested in a thermal gradient trough (after Meldrim & Gift 1971); acute preferred temperature = mean of position-derived temperature readings over 20 minutes (recorded every 15 sec) following a 40-minute orientation period.
- n = 8 fish tested individually per species per acclimation temperature.

### Final Preferred Temperatures (Fry 1947 method)
| Species | Regression Model (P = preference, A = acclimation) | R² | **Final Preferred Temp (°C)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| *S. atromaculatus* | P = −0.00736A² + 0.7506A + 11.72 | 0.78 | **26.4** |
| *S. corporalis* | P = −0.00536A² + 0.6551A + 10.34 | 0.75 | **22.3** |
| *S. margarita* | P = 0.012A² + 0.024A + 12.59 | 0.64 | **16.0** |

### Statistical Significance
- A nested ANOVA on residuals (removing acclimation temperature effects) confirmed species-level differences were highly significant:
  - Among groups (species): F = 25.8, P < .05
  - Among subgroups within groups (individual fish): F = 49.2, P < .05

### Ecological Correlation
- **Rank order of preferred temperature directly mirrors southern range limits**: *S. atromaculatus* (warmest preference, southernmost range to Alabama) > *S. corporalis* (intermediate, south to Virginia) > *S. margarita* (coolest preference, restricted to cold headwaters despite reaching northern VA).
- Supports the broader hypothesis that **behavioral thermal preference** is a reliable proximate predictor of species distribution limits in closely related, syntopic freshwater fishes — a rare direct behavioral-distributional comparison using standardized laboratory methodology across congeners.
- Reinforces call for combined field and laboratory approaches to link [[Thermal Tolerance]] behavior with realized fish distributions.
