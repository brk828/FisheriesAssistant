# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program Management Group Meeting Summary - April 25, 1990

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes #recovery-program #water-rights

## Executive Summary

This document is a meeting summary from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] Management Group meeting held April 25, 1990, in Lakewood, Colorado. The meeting addressed organizational restructuring, budget allocations, water rights acquisition, Section 7 consultations, and coordination among federal and state agencies working to recover endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The FY-91 budget was confirmed at $624,000 (same as FY-90), with no inflation adjustment. Major discussions focused on reorganizing the Technical Group structure and improving input from biological researchers through the [[Biological Researchers Advisory Group]] (Bio/RAG).

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] is a collaborative effort involving the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], state agencies from Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, water users, environmental groups, and power authorities. The program aims to recover endangered fish species in the Upper Colorado River Basin while allowing water development to proceed. The Management Group provides oversight and coordination, with support from Technical Groups, subcommittees (Water Acquisition, Information & Education), and the Bio/RAG. Key geographic focus areas include the [[Yampa River]], [[Little Snake River]], the 15-mile reach of the Colorado River, and [[Flaming Gorge]] reservoir operations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The program operates within the complex hydrology of the Upper Colorado River Basin, where water depletions from mining, oil and gas development, municipal use, and agricultural projects require Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act. Water acquisition and instream flow protection are critical for maintaining habitat for endangered fish species. The [[Yampa River Projects Alternatives Feasibility Study]] is examining reservoir options, while water rights appraisals are underway for the [[Little Snake River]]. The 15-mile reach requires operational scenarios to identify when water is available for fish. Drought conditions in 1990 prompted consideration of additional water releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] (10,000 acre-feet for August-October) to augment flows. The [[Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion]] addresses reservoir operations' impacts on downstream fish habitat.

## Key Findings

**Budget and Program Status:**
- FY-91 Recovery Program budget confirmed at $624,000 (same as FY-90, no inflation increase)
- Service will provide in-kind services (technical reviews) on [[Yampa River]] feasibility study to offset shortfall
- As of March 31, 1990, depletion charge funds totaled $44,433.68 (not including interest)
- Recent completed reports: Five Year Plan for Information and Education, Status Report on Water Acquisition Activities, Fiscal Year 1989 Annual Report

**Organizational Restructuring:**
- Major discussion on reorganizing Technical Group structure to improve biological input
- Concerns identified: inadequate input from technical experts, insufficient planning guidance, redundancy between Technical and Management Groups, inadequate time for members
- John Hamill proposed abolishing Technical Group and establishing standing Biology, Water Rights, and I&E Committees reporting directly to Management Group
- Special meeting scheduled June 8, 1990, to finalize reorganization recommendations for August 1990 Implementation Committee meeting

**Water Rights Acquisition:**
- [[Yampa River Projects Alternatives Feasibility Study]]: CH2MHill selected as contractor, 4-6 weeks behind schedule but summer fieldwork on track
- [[Little Snake River]] water rights appraisal in progress, recommendations expected for July Management Group meeting
- [[Cross Mountain Ranch]] negotiations slowed due to upper range allocation issues
- [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] and [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]] developing scope of work for water availability study on 15-mile reach ($50,000 proposal for FY-91)
- Bureau of Reclamation study on water sources for 15-mile reach approximately 6 months behind schedule
- Proposed water appraiser/engineer position for FY-91 ($25,000-35,000 budget)

**Section 7 Consultations:**
- [[Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion]] draft extended to approximately August 1, 1990, due to concurrent [[Animas-La Plata]] project consultation
- Bureau of Reclamation considering recommending opinion be held in abeyance for further study (estimated $100,000 cost)
- [[Price-San Rafael Biological Opinion]] pending, with water rights retention question for [[Desert Lake Waterfowl Management Area]]
- [[Sandstone Biological Opinion]] progressing, expected conservation measures similar to [[Muddy Creek Opinion]], final draft expected May-June 1990
- Status report shows 33 consultations initiated since January 1988, with depletions ranging from 0.7 to 30,600 acre-feet
- Total depletion charges collected: approximately $680,000 from completed consultations

**Information & Education:**
- Second newsletter in production, mailing list grown to ~1,500 individuals (~4,000 total copies)
- Future newsletters will emphasize material suitable for general public rather than technical audience
- Unresolved issue regarding Management Group review of newsletter before publication
- I&E coordinator will attend Management Group meetings
- Potential display at State Fair in Pueblo under consideration

**Environmental Group Participation:**
- Concern expressed about waning environmental group interest
- Carse Pustmueller expected to resign; Bob Weaver may replace but already stretched thin
- Discussion of potential funding support for environmental group participation through [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]]
- Bob Weaver invited to June 8 special meeting to discuss participation

**Planned Activities:**
- August 23-25, 1990: Colorado River trip through [[Westwater Canyon]] and [[Black Rocks]], 23 Recovery Program attendees plus 11 invited guests including Phil Pister ([[Desert Fishes Council]])
- Week of August 6, 1990: Washington D.C. briefing trip (not budget-focused)
- May 9-11, 1990: Professionally facilitated Road Maps meeting
- Road Maps to be finalized and sent to Management Group in late June for fatal flaw review and July approval
