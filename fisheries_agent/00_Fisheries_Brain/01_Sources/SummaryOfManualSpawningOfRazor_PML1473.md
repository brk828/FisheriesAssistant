# Summary of Manual Spawning of Razorback Suckers at Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 report summarizes a season of manual spawning of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) broodstock from the Colorado River at [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona/Nevada. It was prepared by Paul C. Marsh of the Arizona State University Center for Environmental Studies for the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], Lower Colorado Region, under order number 1550-1 2-5 (dated 26 March 1993). The work supported a recovery program run by the [[Native Fish Work Group]], whose goal was to rear and restock razorback suckers in Lake Mohave. The scanned text is heavily degraded, so many figures below are partial and should be checked against the original.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** Razorback sucker, a large catostomid fish of the [[Colorado River]] system. The text describes it as a species in need of recovery, with natural recruitment too low to sustain the population.
- **Program context:** The Native Fish Work Group was formed to develop and carry out conservation strategies for native fishes in the lower Colorado River. Its activities included adult capture, spawning, egg incubation, larval rearing, and stocking of juveniles back into Lake Mohave.
- **Spawning objective:** The stated goal was to obtain roughly 10,000 eggs from each of at least 20 females, with equal numbers of eggs from each female distributed among the incubation and hatching groups. This would produce a genetically diverse stock for release.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collection sites:** Adults were taken from the river between Yuma and Lake Mohave, with the Lake Mohave area being the main site. Captures were made by active methods (netting or trawling) and passive methods (stationary gear). Fish were checked at intervals of about 1 to 6 hours.
- **Holding conditions:** Captured fish were held temporarily in holding pens, and ripe fish were kept in tanks or live cages. The text refers to water temperatures and to tank and water-volume settings, but the figures are too garbled to reproduce reliably.
- **Hormonal induction:** The text indicates that hormone injections were not used in this effort. Females that did not produce ova within the holding period were released.
- **Spawning method:** Ripe females were manually stripped of eggs, and males were manually stripped of milt. Eggs were fertilized in water, washed, and placed in incubation containers. Larvae were then reared and released as juveniles.
- **Site monitoring:** Follow-up work included periodic visual surveys of the spawning and release areas to assess fish presence and condition.

## Key Findings

- **Broodstock captured:** The text reports a total of about 6 captured fish, including males and about 30 females. The OCR is unclear and these numbers may be incomplete.
- **Spawning results:** The text refers to a total of about 58 individuals and a second group of about 131 fish, including about 55 females. It also mentions that eggs were obtained from 29 adult females. These figures are not fully legible and should be verified.
- **Egg yield:** Egg counts and fertilization rates are referenced, but the numbers cannot be read with confidence.
- **Program outcome:** The work contributed eggs and larvae for the [[Larval Stocking]] program at Lake Mohave, supporting the broader goal of establishing a self-sustaining razorback sucker population.
- **Management relevance:** The document supports the use of [[Manual Spawning]] combined with [[Hatchery Propagation]] as a recovery tool for endangered Colorado River fishes, with restocking as the final step.
