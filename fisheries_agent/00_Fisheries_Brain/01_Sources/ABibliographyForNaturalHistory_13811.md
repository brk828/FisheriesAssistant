# Bibliography for Natural History of the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin and Environs, Coahuila, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This is a bibliography (Minckley, 1993, *Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council*, Vol. XXV) of natural history literature for the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] and surrounding areas of Coahuila, Mexico. The compiler states that the list was prepared to support conservation of the basin and to stimulate research. By the time the volume went to press, nearly 500 references had been compiled. The bibliography is biased toward aquatic biology, reflecting the compiler's main research field, but it also covers botany, herpetology, geology, archaeology, and other topics. The compiler notes that some entries are incomplete (marked with asterisks, lacking pagination or other details) and that errors and omissions are likely. Readers are invited to submit corrections for future revisions. The compiler dedicates the work to Jack McCoy, who died in 1993.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Annotated bibliography, presented as a contributed paper in the [[Desert Fishes Council]] proceedings. It includes an English abstract and a Spanish translation (Resumen).
- **Scope:** Mostly studies specifically on the Cuatro Ciénegas basin and its biota. Some references are included because they treat the wider region, the [[Chihuahuan Desert]], or a characteristic organism in a comparative context.
- **Compilation history:** Built up since the late 1950s, with references added and errors corrected each year. The compiler plans to keep updating it.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography portrays the Cuatro Ciénegas basin as an intermontane desert basin in northern Mexico with a highly unusual aquatic biota. The literature covers:

- **Endemic fishes:** Cyprinodontid pupfishes, the cichlid *Cichlasoma minckleyi* and related "mojarras," poeciliids (*Gambusia*, *Xiphophorus*), and cyprinids (*Notropis*). Several studies address hybridization, trophic polymorphism, and life history in these fishes.
- **Invertebrates:** Hydrobiid springsnails, cirolanid and stenasellid isopods, amphipods, crustaceans, copepods, and parasites (trematodes).
- **Stromatolites and microbial mats:** Carbonate-forming lacustrine and stream structures, diatom roles in stromatolite growth, and boring cyanobacteria.
- **Water and habitat:** Hydrography, limnology of thermal and spring waters, wetlands, dune fields, and water use.
- **Terrestrial biota:** Herpetofauna (notably the aquatic box turtle *Terrapene coahuila*), birds, mammals, and extensive plant taxonomy, including gypsophilous (gypsum-adapted) species.
- **Geology and paleontology:** Mesozoic and Cenozoic stratigraphy, tectonics, faulting, and Quaternary paleoecology.
- **Conservation and human history:** Environmental impacts, protected area proposals (for example, the Poza la Becerra recreation and culture area), management plans, and archaeology.

## Key Findings

- **Scale of the literature:** Nearly 500 references compiled. The list is a working draft and is expected to be revised.
- **Reliability:** Asterisked entries are incomplete. The compiler acknowledges likely errors and omissions.
- **Aquatic emphasis:** Fish, snail, and crustacean literature forms the core. Several entries are foundational for the basin's endemic fauna:
  - [[Minckley]] (1969), *Environments of the Bolsón of Cuatro Cienegas*, covering the aquatic biota.
  - [[Minckley]] (1978), "Endemic Fishes of the Cuatro Cienegas Basin," in the Chihuahuan Desert symposium.
  - [[Minckley]] (1984), "Cuatro Cienegas Fishes: Research Review and a Local Test of Diversity versus Habitat Size."
  - [[Minckley]] (1992), "Three Decades near Cuatro Cienegas, Mexico," a photographic documentation and conservation plea.
  - [[Hershler]] (1985), systematic revision of the Hydrobiidae of the basin.
  - [[Kornfield]] and Taylor (1983), description of *Cichlasoma minckleyi* as a polymorphic species.
  - [[Miller]] (1968), two new *Cyprinodon* species from the basin.
  - [[Minckley]] and Lytle (1969), *Notropis xanthicara* described from the basin.
- **Fish-group entries:** Osteology of *Cyprinodon atrorus* and *C. bifasciatus*; hybridization in *Cyprinodon*; sexual dimorphism in *Cichlasoma minckleyi* (Dunnigan et al., 1994); life history of *Gambusia marshi* (Meffe, 1985); and *Xiphophorus gordoni* (Miller and Minckley, 1963).
- **Conservation literature:** Several entries address endangered and vanishing fishes, endemic-species lists, and federal listing criteria. Examples include Contreras-Balderas (1987, 1991) on Mexican endangered freshwater fishes and Williams et al. (1985, 1989) on North American vanishing fishes. Mexico's SEDUE (1991) published ecological criteria for rare and endangered species.
- **Stromatolites:** Winsborough and colleagues describe recent freshwater lacustrine stromatolites, diatom contributions to stromatolite growth, and the ecology of biogenic structures in basin lakes and streams.
- **Botany and herpetology:** Pinkava's multi-part vegetation and flora series (1979–1984) is a central reference. Several new gypsophilous plant species are described from the region. Herpetological entries cover the Coahuila box turtle, slider and soft-shell turtles, and lizards.
- **Gaps and future work:** The compiler invites corrections, new bibliographies, and updates, and says the list should facilitate conservation progress in the basin.
