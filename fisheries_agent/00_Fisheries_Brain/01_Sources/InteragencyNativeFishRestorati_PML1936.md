# Interagency Native Fish Restoration Proposal: Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fish-management

## Executive Summary

This informal letter from a [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] staff member to two colleagues outlines an idea for Reclamation to shift from a construction agency toward a resource manager role. The main proposal is a low-cost, cooperative native fish restoration project at [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]], developed with the [[National Park Service]] and the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]]. The writer wants to pair Reclamation's facilities and engineering with other agencies' expertise and volunteer labor. The letter is an early concept, not a research report, and it contains no data, sample sizes or measured results.

## Background & Description

- **Agency context:** Reclamation is seeking to be recognized as a resource manager, and the writer sees environmental projects as a way to build that image and secure the agency's future.
- **NPS initiative:** The writer has worked for about 18 months on an NPS fisheries management plan for Lake Mohave. A proposed "pong" (likely a fish-rearing or holding) concept was expected to be cut from that plan, possibly for a couple of years.
- **Species of concern:** The letter refers to native fish that could be saved through rearing and stocking. The OCR text is unclear, but the reference to a "roundup" in Lake Mohave suggests the [[Razorback Sucker]], though the species is not named with confidence in the source.
- **Other fish:** Possible sportfish for release in the lakes are also mentioned, but no species are named.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The project targets specific bays and backwaters in Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu, which are used for rearing and release.
- A preliminary data point is noted: a roundup on Lake Mohave produced only data, with no reported population counts or survey figures.
- The writer notes that Reclamation may not face the same bureaucratic constraints as other agencies because the environmental role is new for the agency.
- The letter indicates that the work is meant to maintain existing native populations or restore extirpated ones, but it provides no population estimates or habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

1. **Proposal structure:** The writer recommends a written proposal that includes:
   - A technical description of the facility, management plan and proposed research
   - Labor commitments and volunteer contributions
   - Engineering designs for the facility
   - Maintenance commitments
   - A prioritized list of facility options that can accommodate support from other agencies
2. **Funding:** Reclamation cannot fund the entire effort. The writer proposes an initial contribution of about $10,000 to start cost-sharing, with a possible long-term commitment of a few thousand dollars per year for miscellaneous costs, contingent on other agencies' participation.
3. **Partners to identify:** The proposal should specify what expertise is needed and which agencies could contribute money, staff or other resources.
4. **Outreach:** The writer suggests a short audiovisual package on the problem and the proposed solution, similar to a program the recipients had produced, to be sent to other agencies and presented at professional meetings. The aim is to invite broad participation.
5. **Objective:** A low-cost, cooperative project that stocks and rears native fish to maintain or re-establish populations at Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu.
6. **Limits:** The writer states the project would not replace the need for further research and is meant to complement it.
7. **Next steps:** The letter asks recipients to respond on whether they are interested, and suggests an interagency work group to take the idea forward. It also proposes a possible joint approach to the NPS initiative and to bringing a portion of the Fish and Wildlife Service into the effort.
