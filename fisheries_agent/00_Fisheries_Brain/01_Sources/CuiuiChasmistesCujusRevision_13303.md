# Cui-ui Recovery Plan Revision (1983)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #conservation-planning

## Executive Summary

This document is the second revision of the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) Recovery Plan, approved November 22, 1983, providing direction through FY 1986. The [[Cui-ui]] is an endangered sucker endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]] and the [[Truckee River]] system in Nevada. The plan's goal remains unchanged from the original 1978 version: restore the species to non-endangered status and reclassify it from endangered to threatened. The strategy involves protecting existing cui-ui, augmenting the population through artificial culture, and rehabilitating essential habitat. Critical concerns include the lack of significant recruitment since 1969, with the spawning population dominated by a single age class (14 years old), indicating the species may face extinction within decades without intervention. The plan outlines a comprehensive approach integrating biological research, habitat rehabilitation, population management, and public education.

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus* Cope) is a large sucker fish endemic to the [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] ecosystem in Nevada. Since the turn of the 19th century, water diversions from the [[Truckee River]] for agricultural, municipal, and industrial uses have severely altered the natural flow regime and reduced inflow to [[Pyramid Lake]]. These diversions have created a delta across the river mouth that prevents cui-ui from reaching historic spawning grounds except during rare high-water years.

The species was listed as endangered, and the original recovery plan was approved January 23, 1978. An updated plan in 1980 directed efforts through FY 1983, focusing on propagation techniques and establishing spawning runs through the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]. This 1983 revision continues those priorities while incorporating new knowledge about the species' precarious status.

Recent aging studies reveal that no significant recruitment has occurred since 1969, with the spawning population dominated by 14-year-old fish. The construction of [[Marble Bluff Dam]] and Fish Facility in 1975, intended to provide passage around the delta, has paradoxically eliminated access during high-water years while never functioning properly, exacerbating the recruitment crisis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] ecosystem has been severely degraded by multiple anthropogenic stressors:

**Water Quantity**: Diversions have reduced both volume and timing of natural flows in the lower [[Truckee River]], potentially making optimum flow regimes for cui-ui reproduction unattainable without augmentation from [[Stampede Reservoir]].

**Water Quality**: Inadequately treated sewage effluent and nonpoint source pollution from municipal, agricultural, and industrial sources have created excessive nutrient loading (nitrogen and phosphorous) and toxic conditions. Intergravel studies indicate nutrients may be decreasing intergravel water quality by increasing biostimulation, which reduces dissolved oxygen concentration and may eliminate spawning habitat.

**Physical Habitat Degradation**: Channelization, grazing, and timber harvesting along the lower river have reduced riparian canopy and increased bank erosion, leading to elevated water temperatures, increased sedimentation, reduced wetted perimeter, and lowered dissolved oxygen. These conditions have deteriorated or eliminated cui-ui spawning and nursery areas.

**Lake Conditions**: Increasing total dissolved solids (TDS) levels in [[Pyramid Lake]] may affect the food chain by reducing survival of zoo- and phytoplankton. The lake is thought to be nitrogen-deficient and phosphorous-rich; large nitrogen loading may alter this ratio, disrupting the ecosystem.

**Barriers to Migration**: The delta formation and inadequate fish passage facilities prevent adult cui-ui from accessing spawning areas. Unscreened water diversions reduce recruitment of juveniles to the lake population.

**Competition and Predation**: Native and exotic aquatic animals may compete with cui-ui for space and food, though the extent and mechanism of this competition remain unknown.

## Key Findings

**Population Status**: The cui-ui population faces imminent extinction risk. No significant recruitment has occurred since 1969, with possible exceptions in 1982 (14,000 spawners) and 1983 (6,000 spawners) at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], though adequacy of these numbers is unknown. The spawning population is dominated by a single age class (14 years old), indicating the species may become reproductively extinct before the turn of the century without intervention.

**Recovery Goal**: Restore and maintain an optimum, self-sustaining population of cui-ui in the [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] system, enabling downlisting from endangered to threatened status, with eventual delisting possible if sufficient species security is achieved within historical habitat.

**Three-Component Strategy**:
1. Identification, rehabilitation, and maintenance of sufficient habitat in [[Truckee River]] and [[Pyramid Lake]] to maintain optimum cui-ui population through natural reproduction
2. Protection and management of the optimum self-sustaining population
3. Public education about the recovery effort

**Critical Research Needs**:
- **Life History**: Fecundity variations, population dynamics, food preferences, pre-spawning aggregate dynamics
- **Population Dynamics**: Mortality, longevity, recruitment, and age composition estimates to verify population status
- **Habitat Requirements**: Environmental stimuli influencing migration, spawning habitat specifications, water quality parameters for adults, dissolved oxygen and pH requirements for egg incubation, larval tolerance to diurnal temperature fluctuations and water chemistry, lake habitat requirements for all life stages

**Habitat Rehabilitation Priorities**:
- Stabilize lower [[Truckee River]] in natural course to reduce bank erosion and velocity
- Reduce water pollution through enforcement of regulations and development of cooperative approaches among agencies
- Install fish ladders and screening devices on barriers and diversions
- Restore river canopy to reduce water temperatures
- Control illegal dredge and fill activities under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act
- Secure and maintain flow regime identified for optimum population, potentially through [[Stampede Reservoir]] releases and negotiated settlement of Truckee/Carson River water use conflicts

**Population Management Actions**:
- Determine optimum self-sustaining population size by integrating life history, population dynamics, and habitat carrying capacity data
- Determine river flow regime required to maintain habitat for optimum population
- Develop and implement cui-ui management plan with strategies for achieving and maintaining target population
- Annually monitor abundance, age composition, spawning run, and spawning success
- Improve and operate [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and [[Numana Dam Fishway]], including construction of permanent weirs, modified building bypass ladder, refined handling procedures, and increased operations and maintenance funding

**Hatchery Program**: Continue augmenting population with hatchery-reared cui-ui until natural reproduction becomes self-sustaining. The [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]] operates a cui-ui hatchery targeting production of 10 million larvae annually. Priority research includes assessing survival of hatchery-reared fish in [[Pyramid Lake]] and developing culture techniques to rear larvae to 20 mm (larger size expected to improve survival). Eggs are obtained from wild fish in [[Pyramid Lake]].

**Protection Measures**: Enforce Federal (Endangered Species Act), State (Nevada Statutes), and Tribal fishing regulations through cooperative interagency effort. Protect spawning cui-ui from harassment to allow reproduction.

**Public Information**: Provide news media with current information on recovery progress and develop supplemental informational leaflet about cui-ui plight, recovery effort, and life history.

**Implementation Priorities**: The plan categorizes tasks by priority (Priority 1: prevent extinction; Priority 2: prevent significant decline; Priority 3: full recovery actions) and provides estimated costs through FY 1986. Total estimated costs exceed $1 million across multiple agencies including U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, Bureau of Indian Affairs, Nevada Department of Wildlife, Pyramid Lake Indian Tribal Enterprises, and others.

**Critical Constraint**: Without proper management of [[Stampede Reservoir]] water, the cui-ui face greater extinction risk due to insufficient habitat for reproduction. Technical assistance for reservoir management and resolution of ongoing water use conflict negotiations are essential to recovery success.
