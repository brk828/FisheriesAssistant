## Executive Summary
This short technical note describes two pieces of field equipment developed to improve efficiency and cost-effectiveness of winter fishery survey operations: (1) a gasoline-engine-powered "power pack" mounted on a toboggan to supply continuous electrical power to battery-operated ice augers, and (2) an inexpensive, modular utility anchor constructed from scrap iron for use by fishery workers and sport anglers. Both innovations were designed and field-tested by Louis H. Carufel of the North Dakota Game and Fish Department, aimed at reducing the time and labor costs associated with cutting large numbers of holes through lake ice during biological surveys.

## Background & Description
*Not applicable in the biological sense — this note concerns fishery survey equipment rather than an organism. Equipment "morphology" (physical design) is summarized below:*

**Power Pack Assembly:**
- Power source: 2.5-horsepower gasoline engine
- Connected to a 12-volt generator, voltage regulator, and battery
- Mounted on a toboggan for mobility across ice
- Powered ice auger connected to the battery via 6-foot cables
- Compatible with standard battery-operated (6- or 12-volt) starter-motor-type ice augers

**Utility Anchor:**
- Constructed from scrap iron components
- Shanks made from scrap rod pieces
- Flukes fashioned from repurposed car and truck leaf springs
- Modular/dismantlable design for ease of transport and storage
- Weight adjustable by modifying shank or fluke dimensions
- Treated with rust-inhibiting paint to extend usable lifespan
- Approximate construction cost: $1 (1963 USD)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*Not directly applicable, as this note addresses survey methodology rather than species ecology.* However, contextual relevance includes:
- Equipment designed for use on ice-covered lakes during winter, a season when standard netting and survey techniques are constrained by ice cover.
- Supports [[Winter Fish Sampling]] efforts including lake mapping and biological surveys conducted through the ice.
- Facilitates test-netting operations across multiple survey seasons, indirectly supporting population and habitat assessments of lake fish communities.

## Key Findings
- The **power pack** significantly reduces the time required to cut hundreds to thousands of holes through lake ice, a major bottleneck in winter [[Fishery Survey Methods]].
- Pairing a 2.5-hp gas engine with a 12-volt generator and battery system allows continuous power supply to [[Powered Ice Augers]], eliminating dependence on limited battery charge alone.
- The toboggan-mounted design improves mobility across frozen lake surfaces, enabling efficient movement between survey sites.
- Time and cost savings from the power pack are considered justified for programs surveying multiple lakes each winter.
- The companion **utility anchor** offers a low-cost ($1), durable, and field-tested alternative to commercial anchors for both fishery researchers and recreational anglers.
- Use of repurposed materials (scrap rod, leaf springs) demonstrates a practical, low-budget engineering solution adaptable to available scrap materials.
- Both innovations reflect broader efforts within 1960s fisheries management to improve efficiency of [[Ice Fishing Survey Techniques]] and reduce field labor costs.
