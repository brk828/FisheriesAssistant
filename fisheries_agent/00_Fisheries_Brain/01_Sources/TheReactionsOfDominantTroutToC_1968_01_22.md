# The Reactions of Dominant Trout to Changes in Overhead Artificial Cover

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #freshwater-ecology #fish-behavior

## Executive Summary
This 1968 field study conducted at the [[Sagehen Creek Project]] (University of California) examined how dominant individuals of three trout species — [[Brook Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], and [[Brown Trout]] — responded to changes in artificial overhead shade cover. Using a submerged observation tank with plate-glass windows, researchers permuted three sizes of black-painted plywood covers (large 3×3 ft, medium 2×2 ft, small 1×1 ft) across three positions in a 9×30 ft test area over 286 total hours of observation. All three species preferred shade from the large cover over the medium cover (Chi-square P<0.005), with negligible use of the small cover. Species differed markedly in activity level and shade dependency: [[Rainbow Trout]] showed the lowest shade use and highest movement activity, [[Brown Trout]] showed the highest shade use and lowest activity, and [[Brook Trout]] was intermediate. The study also revealed patterns of [[Dominance Hierarchy|social dominance]] tied to spatial control of shaded feeding positions, with subordinate trout tolerated only in specific downstream zones.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat setting**: A concrete tank with underwater viewing windows built into a side channel of Sagehen Creek, Sierra Nevada Mountains. Water depth ~12 inches behind a weir; current velocity reduced from ~0.70 ft/s in the riffle to 0.27–0.33 ft/s in the observation area ([[Stream Hydraulics]]).
- **Thermal regime**: Water temperature tracked the main stream; ranged 18–8°C during the brook trout study and 0–3°C during rainbow/brown trout studies.
- **Cover ecology**: Artificial overhead covers (marine plywood, black underside) simulated natural [[Overhead Cover]] features such as boulders, overhanging banks, floating logs, and turbulence — well-documented components of trout habitat structure.
- **Spatial resource use**: [[Brown Trout]] dominant occupied the most distant row (Row 3) from the observation window nearly continuously, coinciding with natural shade cast by a concrete block wall during low solar declination — illustrating flexible substitution of natural vs. artificial shade sources.
- **Social structure / niche partitioning**: Dominant trout tolerated subordinates (other brook, rainbow, and even different-species trout) sharing shaded cover, but only in downstream/side positions; aggression was reserved for subordinates attempting to feed upstream of the covered site, suggesting dominance functions primarily to secure a **superior feeding position** rather than exclusive shelter.
- **Co-occurring species**: Multiple brook and rainbow trout individuals occupied the test area simultaneously with each single-species dominant, allowing observation of interspecific and intraspecific tolerance.

## Key Findings
- **Cover size preference**: All three species significantly preferred shade from the **large (3×3 ft) cover** over the **medium (2×2 ft) cover** (Chi-square, P<0.005); the **small (1×1 ft) cover** was used minimally by all species.
- **Species-specific shade dependency and activity**:
  - [[Rainbow Trout]]: lowest shade utilization (36.2% of observed time), highest activity/movement (one departure per 4.4 minutes), least site attachment.
  - [[Brown Trout]]: highest shade utilization (up to 84% excluding a low-cover-availability period), lowest activity (as low as one departure per 51 minutes), strong site attachment.
  - [[Brook Trout]]: intermediate in both shade use (72.4%) and activity (one departure per 24.7 minutes).
- **Site attachment / behavioral flexibility**: [[Rainbow Trout]] adapted readily to cover relocation with little site fidelity; [[Brook Trout]] and [[Brown Trout]] showed strong preference for particular site + cover combinations and were slower to abandon an accepted site once covered by the smallest cover — suggesting a form of **operant reinforcement/conditioning** to specific locations.
- **Natural shade substitution**: When artificial covers were removed from the brown trout's preferred row (14–18 December), the fish shifted to natural shade at the base of a concrete block wall, indicating functional equivalence between artificial and natural shade sources.
- **Dominance and feeding position**: Findings support the idea that [[Dominance Hierarchy|dominance]] in trout is partly an expression of securing superior feeding position rather than sheer aggression or exclusive shelter access; subordinates were tolerated if restricted to downstream/side feeding zones.
- **Interspecific tolerance**: Dominant brown trout shared cover with up to 4 rainbow trout; dominant brook trout shared with up to 6 brook trout and 1 rainbow trout; dominant rainbow trout shared with up to 5 brook trout — indicating loose interspecific cover-sharing under low-aggression conditions.
- **Methodological caveat**: Authors note that the sequential presentation of large-then-medium cover at an already-preferred site may have reinforced site preference in the brook trout, confounding pure "response to cover" with **conditioning effects** — a design limitation flagged for future studies.
- **Call for further research**: The authors recommend future comparative behavioral studies run concurrently across seasons and with larger, multi-habitat samples of each species to validate these preliminary findings, referencing foundational work on [[Trout Population Dynamics]] and stream cover (Boussu 1954; Saunders & Smith 1962).
