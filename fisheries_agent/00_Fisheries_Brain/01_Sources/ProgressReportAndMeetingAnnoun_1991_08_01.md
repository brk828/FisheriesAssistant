## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Bureau of Reclamation memorandum announcing an August 19, 1991 meeting of the **[[Native Fish Work Group]]**, convened to review conservation efforts for native fish populations in backwater habitats along the **[[Lower Colorado River]]**. The report describes a disappointing spawning season and poor juvenile recruitment in a managed backwater (**[[Yuma Cove]]**), despite high adult survival attributed to a newly constructed berm. A barrier net intended to isolate a second habitat, **[[Davis Cove]]**, had been built but not yet deployed due to insufficient juvenile fish available for translocation. The memo is primarily administrative/programmatic rather than a data-rich scientific paper, but it provides insight into active habitat-management and species-recovery efforts for imperiled native fishes in this river system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Engineered/managed backwater coves off the main channel of the Lower Colorado River, used as refugia and nursery habitat for native fish life stages.
- **Key sites discussed:**
  - **[[Yuma Cove]]** — site of spawning attempts, berm construction, and adult fish holding; experienced poor recruitment to the juvenile stage in the reported season.
  - **[[Davis Cove]]** — a secondary backwater habitat undergoing environmental assessment (EA) and fitted with a barrier net (not yet installed) intended to exclude non-target or predatory fish and/or contain translocated natives.
  - **[[Davis Dam Spillway]]** — identified as a future action site requiring net construction and stocking.
- **Habitat engineering:** A "new berm" at Yuma Cove is credited with improving adult fish survival, indicating its role in stabilizing water levels, reducing predator access, or improving retention of managed populations within the backwater.
- **Population management interventions referenced:** adult removal, fish eradication (non-native species control implied), barrier net installation, restocking, and monitoring — all classic native fish recovery tools used in the **[[Colorado River Basin]]** where native cyprinids and catostomids face predation and competition from introduced sportfish.

## Key Findings

- Spawning success in the Yuma Cove backwater was poor during the reporting period ("not a very productive spawn").
- Recruitment of spawned fish to the juvenile life stage was likewise poor, suggesting high early-life mortality or unsuitable nursery conditions.
- The newly constructed berm at Yuma Cove performed well structurally and is linked to high adult fish survival, decoupling adult survivorship from reproductive/recruitment success.
- The barrier net built for Davis Cove remained uninstalled because there were not enough juvenile fish available to transfer — a logistical bottleneck directly caused by the recruitment failure at Yuma Cove.
- Planned future actions span both cove systems and include:
  - Habitat/water treatment and non-native **[[Fish Eradication]]** efforts at Davis Cove prior to restocking.
  - Restocking decisions (species and size classes) pending at Yuma Cove, Davis Cove, and Davis Dam Spillway.
  - Development of fish removal techniques at Yuma Cove (likely targeting undesirable/competing species).
  - Expansion of barrier net infrastructure to the Davis Dam Spillway site.
- The overall program reflects an adaptive-management approach typical of **[[Endangered Fish Recovery Programs]]** on the Lower Colorado River, where backwater habitat creation, predator/competitor exclusion, and staged stocking are used to bolster native fish populations against recruitment bottlenecks.
