# Aquatic Survey of the Gila and Salt Rivers Below the Phoenix 91st Avenue Wastewater Treatment Plant

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines two reconnaissance surveys of the [[Gila River]] and [[Salt River]] downstream of the [[Phoenix 91st Avenue Wastewater Treatment Plant]], Phoenix, Arizona. The first is a fish inventory at three sites (near [[Gillespie Dam]], [[Gila River at 115th Avenue Crossing|115th Avenue]], and the [[Highway 85]] crossing). The second, by Nancy B. Grimm (Arizona State University), is a qualitative survey of macroinvertebrates and algae at the 91st Avenue, 115th Avenue, and Highway 85 sites. The fish were abundant and dominated by [[Tilapia]], while the invertebrate fauna was exceedingly poor in taxa and dominated by midges and snails. Fish were absent from the site nearest the treatment plant. Survey dates and sample counts are not stated in the source.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** The sites lie along the Salt and Gila Rivers at roughly 0, 15, and 35 km downstream of the 91st Avenue wastewater treatment plant. The fish survey sampled three sites, and searches near the plant outfall at 35th and 43rd Avenues found no fish.

**Fish survey methods.** Fish were collected by two methods: [[Electrofishing]] and [[Seine Netting]]. Seining was the more successful method.

**Invertebrate and algae methods.** Each site was sampled for about one hour using several techniques:
- [[Kick Sampling]] in riffles with a 1.0 m net, disturbing substrate over about 1.0 m² to 10–20 cm depth.
- Hand collection of invertebrates from larger riffle rocks.
- Elutriation of sand, gravel, and silt from backwaters and shorelines through a 0.25 mm sieve.
- Algae collected from kick nets and by scraping rocks.

Invertebrates were preserved in 70% ethanol. Algae were preserved in formalin-ethanol solution.

**Site characteristics (Grimm survey):**

| Site | Width | Mean Depth | Current Velocity | Features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 91st Avenue | ca. 15 m | 34 cm | 1.2 m/s | Rapid riffle, cobble-boulder substrate, few backwaters; no fish |
| 115th Avenue | ca. 25 m | 31 cm | ca. 1.0 m/s in riffles | Cobble riffle in sand matrix, large sandy-silty backwaters |
| Highway 85 | ca. 40 m | 25 cm | 0.5–1.0 m/s in riffles | Three shallow riffles, sand/silt backwaters, slow side channels; warmer water |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish habitat (fish survey).** At [[Gillespie Dam]], about one mile north of the dam, the substrate was mainly fine sand. Water was one to four feet deep, mostly about one foot, with slow current and visibility to about three feet. The Highway 85 site had shallow pools with sand bottoms and riffles with cobble-rubble substrate, to about three feet deep. The 115th Avenue site had higher currents than the downstream sites, with a silty backwater (four to five feet deep, static water) and long riffles (two to three feet deep, hard rubble bottom).

**Water quality and substrate.** Fine sediments at all three Grimm sites were black, foul-smelling, and probably anoxic below the surface. Water temperature at Highway 85 was noticeably higher than at the upstream sites.

**Algal food web.** Dark green fish feces and green-filled midge guts suggested that fish and invertebrates fed on abundant algae. The snails at Highway 85 are known algal grazers.

## Key Findings

### Fish Inventory

- **Gillespie Dam:** Five genera collected, with relative numbers of 100, 10, 10, 5, and 1. Tilapia made up almost 90% of the fish collected. Hundreds of fish were taken in each seine haul, and most were young-of-the-year.
- **Highway 85:** Five genera collected, with relative numbers of 40, 30, 2, 2, and 1. Tilapia again dominated, with several adults also collected.
- **115th Avenue:** Four genera collected (tilapia, shiners, goldfish, and carp), with relative numbers of 100, 90, 5, and 1. Two habitats were sampled: a large backwater (dominated by tilapia and goldfish) and long riffles (where shiners were most common). Carp occurred only in the backwater.
- **Species overall:** Tilapia was the most abundant fish at all locations and ranked second in biomass. Shiners were the second most common. Other genera were rarer in numbers but often contributed more biomass because of their larger size.
- **Conclusions from the fish survey:**
  1. Fish were very abundant at all sampling stations.
  2. Tilapia and shiners were the dominant species at all sites.
  3. Young-of-the-year fish at all sites indicated suitable spawning conditions.
  4. Conditions in the river were considered suitable for the fish present, which are adapted to high temperatures and low flows.

### Macroinvertebrates (Grimm survey)

- **91st Avenue:** No fish were present. Invertebrate taxa were extremely scarce. Dominated by [[Chironomus]] midges (ca. 90 individuals) and only three lumbricid earthworms. Densities were severely depressed.
- **115th Avenue:** Taxa increased. Chironomus abundance was about 100 times that at 91st Avenue. Adult damselflies (*Enallagma* sp.) were ovipositing, and nymphs were collected from a shallow riffle.
- **Highway 85:** Chironomus remained abundant but was no longer dominant. The snail [[Physa]] was by far the most abundant invertebrate, covering riffle rocks along with egg masses. Other taxa included riffle beetles (Dryopidae), the midge *Rheotanytarsus*, oligochaetes, and two Naucoridae (true bugs). Backwaters were dominated by Diptera and Oligochaeta.
- **Overall:** Taxonomic richness rose from 2–3 taxa at the upstream site to about 9 taxa at the downstream site. Mayflies (Ephemeroptera) and caddisflies (Trichoptera) were completely absent. The survey concluded the stream is unsuitable for many common aquatic insect orders.

### Algae (Grimm survey)

- **91st Avenue:** [[Oocystis]] was especially abundant. Diatoms were rare, and the substrate lacked the slickness typical of dense diatom cover.
- **115th Avenue:** Diatoms were abundant, along with two species of the bluegreen alga *Oscillatoria*.
- **Highway 85:** The filamentous green alga [[Stigeoclonium]] formed dense mats on most riffle rocks.
- Algal abundance was high at the two downstream sites and was probably an important food source for fish and invertebrates.
