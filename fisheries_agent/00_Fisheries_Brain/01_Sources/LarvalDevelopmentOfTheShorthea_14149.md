#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 study by Buynak and Mohr describes the larval development of the [[Shorthead Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma macrolepidotum*), a catostomid fish, using artificially spawned eggs collected from the Susquehanna River in 1974. The paper traces development from egg through protolarval, mesolarval, and metalarval phases into the juvenile stage, documenting morphometric and meristic changes at each stage. A central goal of the paper is to establish diagnostic characteristics that distinguish shorthead redhorse larvae from the co-occurring and morphologically similar [[Northern Hog Sucker]] (*Hypentelium nigricans*), which had previously been difficult to separate in early life stages. The authors conclude that no single character reliably separates the two species at all life stages; rather, a combination of pigmentation patterns, myomere counts, and select morphometric ratios must be used together.

## Background & Description

**Eggs**
- Pale yellow, demersal, nonadhesive
- Mean preserved egg diameter: 3.3 mm
- Hatched 8 days post-fertilization at mean incubation temperature of 15.6°C (spawning water temp: 12.0°C)

**Protolarvae (newly hatched to ~12.4 mm TL)**
- Mean total length (TL) at hatching: 10.0 mm (range 9.3–10.4 mm)
- Mean standard (notochord) length: 9.4 mm (range 8.7–9.9 mm)
- Cylindrical body, melanophore-free at hatching
- Slightly upturned urostyle
- Head decurved over granular yolk; mouth incomplete
- Pectoral fin buds present
- Dorsal fin fold originates ~30% of TL behind snout, continuous with caudal and ventral fin folds
- Vent located well beyond two-thirds of TL from snout
- Eyes pigmented by 10.2 mm TL; first melanophores appear on occipital head region at this size
- Most larvae free-swimming ~5 days post-hatching
- Standard length: 93–94% TL
- Postanal length: 22–25% TL
- Preanal myomeres: 34–35 (mode)
- Total myomeres: 40–41

**Mesolarvae (begins at 12.4 mm TL)**
- Mouth open; dorsal head pigmentation evident
- Caudal fin rays visible by 12.9 mm TL
- Melanophore patch forms between eyes (occipital region) — notably WITHOUT a melanophore-free interocular area (key distinguishing trait from *H. nigricans*)
- Swim bladder begins inflating; dorsal pigmentation increases posteriorly by 13.8 mm TL
- Developing dorsal fin location apparent at 15.1 mm TL
- Three pigment bands appear: middorsal (intense on head/caudal peduncle), midlateral (along lateral line), midventral (intense behind anus)
- Dorsal fin rays visible by 15.8 mm TL; dorsal fin fold separates from caudal fin fold
- Pectoral fin rays visible by 16.0 mm TL
- Anal fin rays and pelvic fin buds appear by 16.7 mm TL
- Standard length: 87–91% TL
- Postanal length: 23–31% TL
- Preanal myomeres: 33–35 (mode 34)
- Total myomeres: 40–41

**Metalarvae (begins ~16.2 mm TL, most >16.8 mm)**
- Fin development nearly complete by 18.8 mm TL
- Midlateral melanophores more dispersed/smaller than mesolarval stage
- Dorsal/ventral caudal peduncle pigmentation less intense than earlier stages
- Sparse pigmentation appears on pectoral fins
- Leading edge of dorsal fin usually unpigmented (per Fuiman 1978)
- Total myomeres: 38–41 (mode 38)
- Body depth and eye diameter ranges overlap with congener but trend smaller eye diameter than *H. nigricans*

**Juvenile (~29.3 mm TL)**
- Head naked (no scales); body covered in cycloid scales
- Head length: 21% TL
- Greatest body depth: 18% TL
- Mouth small, inferior, protrusible
- Dense small melanophores across body and fins
- Pigmentation denser on caudal and dorsal fins than on anal, pelvic, and pectoral fins

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Large river system (Susquehanna River), demersal egg deposition
- **Reproductive strategy**: Broadcast spawning of nonadhesive demersal eggs; captured gravid adults via AC electrofisher and artificially spawned
- **Co-occurring species**: Part of a four-species catostomid assemblage in the Susquehanna River near Berwick, PA, alongside [[Northern Hog Sucker]], [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*), and [[Quillback]] (*Carpiodes cyprinus*)
- **Larval drift**: Referenced connection to riverine larval fish drift dynamics (see [[Larval Fish Drift]], Gale and Mohr 1978)
- **Niche differentiation**: Despite morphological and developmental similarity to *H. nigricans*, subtle distinctions in pigmentation and myomere counts suggest fine-scale niche/microhabitat partitioning during early life history, though this is not directly addressed by the authors beyond taxonomic separation

## Key Findings

1. **Hatching biology**: Shorthead redhorse eggs hatch in 8 days at 15.6°C mean temperature; larvae average 10.0 mm TL at hatching.
2. **Developmental transitions**: Transformation to mesolarval phase occurs by 12.4 mm TL; to metalarval phase by 16.2 mm TL.
3. **Fin ray sequence**: Fin rays appear sequentially — caudal (12.9 mm) → dorsal (15.8 mm) → pectoral (16.0 mm) → anal (16.7 mm) → pelvic (18.8 mm).
4. **Protolarval species separation**: Shorthead redhorse protolarvae differ from [[Northern Hog Sucker]] protolarvae by:
   - Smaller standard length (93–94% vs. 95–97% TL)
   - Greater postanal length (22–25% vs. 17–22% TL)
   - Fewer preanal myomeres (34–35 vs. 36–38)
5. **Mesolarval species separation**: Shorthead redhorse mesolarvae lack the melanophore-free interocular area present in *H. nigricans*, show sparser dorsal pigmentation but denser lateral-line pigmentation, and have slightly fewer preanal myomeres (33–35 vs. 33–38).
6. **Metalarval species separation**: Distinguished by lower total myomere counts (38–41 vs. 40–42), absence of five saddle-like pigment bands (present in *H. nigricans* >20.0 mm), less dense dorsal pigmentation, and absent interocular melanophore-free zone.
7. **No single diagnostic character**: The authors emphasize that species separation across all larval phases requires a **combination** of morphometric, meristic, and pigmentation traits rather than any single feature — highlighting the taxonomic challenge posed by convergent catostomid larval morphology.
8. **Geographic variability caveat**: The authors suggest that pooling morphometric/meristic data across multiple drainage systems (as done by [[Fuiman 1978]]) may obscure fine-scale distinguishing characters that are detectable within a single drainage system (e.g., Susquehanna River).
