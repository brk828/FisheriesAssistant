# Introduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish into Selected Waters in Phoenix District

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #endangered-species #reintroduction

## Executive Summary

This supplemental environmental assessment, prepared by the [[U.S. Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Phoenix District Office in December 1983, authorizes the introduction of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) into selected public waters. The action represents interagency cooperation between BLM, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) to reestablish self-sustaining populations within the species' historic range and work toward their recovery and eventual delisting. The proposal serves as mitigation for the loss of the [[Cocio Wash]] topminnow population, which was extirpated by copper mine spills from the ASARCO Silverbell Mine in August-September 1981. More than 30 potential sites were evaluated, with 10 selected for introduction. The action is authorized under BLM Manual 6820 and consistent with the [[Endangered Species Act]] as amended by Public Law 97-304, Section 10j (Experimental Populations), October 13, 1982.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, family [[Poeciliidae]]) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*, family [[Cyprinodontidae]]) are threatened/endangered native fish species historically distributed across desert aquatic habitats in [[Arizona]]. Both species have experienced significant population declines and range contractions, necessitating active recovery efforts.

In 1981, AGFD and USFWS requested BLM assistance in introducing these species into suitable waters on the Phoenix District. An initial Environmental Assessment (AZ-020-1-246) was prepared in July 1981 for proposed introductions at two localities: [[South Peoples Spring]] (T12N R10W SE SW Sec.14) and [[Grapevine Spring]] (Yerba Mansa, T11N R11W NW NW Sec.21). A separate Memorandum of Understanding (August 10, 1982) and Reintroduction Plan (October 20, 1982) were signed with AGFD specifically for desert pupfish introductions. Desert pupfish were introduced into two Phoenix District sites in August 1982: South Peoples Spring and [[Tres Alamos Falls]] (T10N R0W NE NW Sec.13).

The critical event precipitating this supplemental assessment was the local extinction of the Cocio Wash topminnow population on public lands northwest of [[Tucson]]. In August and September 1981, recurrent copper mine spills from the [[ASARCO Silverbell Mine]] resulted in complete loss of this natural population. At the request of the [[Arizona Department of Health Services]] (ADHS, April 29, 1983) and AGFD (April 20 and May 10, 1983), the Phoenix District Office evaluated potential introduction sites to serve as mitigation. More than 30 sites were jointly evaluated by BLM and AGFD (documented in "Recommended Cocio Wash Mitigation," Kepner 1983), with 10 selected for introduction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposed introductions target desert spring and aquatic habitats on public lands within the Phoenix District. These sites represent suitable habitat within the historic range of both species. The reintroduction strategy follows management plans written for both species regarding introduction and monitoring protocols.

Introductions are designated as experimental populations under Section 10j of the Endangered Species Act (as amended 1982). This designation means these populations will have no declared critical habitat and will not be designated with protected status, allowing the action to proceed without interrupting existing land uses or restricting future management options.

The environmental assessment identified no adverse impacts to threatened/endangered species, floodplains and wetlands, wilderness areas, Areas of Critical Environmental Concern (ACECs), visual resources, water quality and quantity, cultural and historical resources, paleontological artifacts, air quality, or prime and unique farmland.

A public meeting was held November 21, 1983, at the Phoenix District Office, with no adverse public comment received. Extensive consultation occurred with federal agencies (BLM, USFWS, USDA Soil Conservation Service), state agencies (AGFD, ADHS, Arizona State Land Department), elected officials, grazing allotment holders, conservation organizations (Sierra Club, Desert Fishes Council, Arizona Wildlife Federation, Southern Arizona Environmental Council, The Wildlife Society Arizona Chapter), and other stakeholders.

## Key Findings

**Decision and Authorization**: AGFD and USFWS were authorized to introduce Gila topminnow and desert pupfish into selected public waters (10 sites listed in Appendix 2) in the Phoenix District. Fish introductions were scheduled for spring 1984 following a 180-day public comment period in the Federal Register.

**Mitigation Objective**: The introductions serve as mitigation for the loss of the Cocio Wash topminnow population, which was extirpated by copper mine spills from the ASARCO Silverbell Mine in August-September 1981. This loss occurred on public lands and required compensatory action.

**Recovery Goals**: The action is designed to reestablish self-sustaining populations of both species within a significant portion of their historic range and result in their eventual recovery and delisting, as outlined in the Gila and Yaqui Topminnow Recovery Plan (USFWS Region 2, 1983).

**Site Selection**: More than 30 potential sites were evaluated jointly by BLM and AGFD, with 10 selected for introduction based on habitat suitability and management considerations.

**Experimental Population Status**: Introduced populations are designated as experimental under ESA Section 10j (as amended 1982), meaning they have no declared critical habitat and no protected status, allowing flexibility in land management.

**Finding of No Significant Impact**: No adverse impacts were identified. The proposed action will not interrupt existing multiple-use management or restrict future management options. No adverse public or agency reaction was received. The action does not require an Environmental Impact Statement under NEPA Section 102(2)c.

**Alternative Considered**: The only alternative evaluated was no action, which would deny AGFD and USFWS authorization to introduce native fishes into selected public waters. This alternative would eliminate interagency mitigation attempts for the Cocio Wash loss and may effectively prevent recovery and delisting of both species.

**Previous Introductions**: Desert pupfish were successfully introduced into two Phoenix District sites in August 1982: South Peoples Spring and Tres Alamos Falls, demonstrating feasibility of the reintroduction approach.

**Legal Authorization**: Introduction of native animals on public lands is authorized under BLM Manual 6820 and is consistent with the Endangered Species Act as amended by Public Law 97-304, Section 10j (Experimental Populations), October 13, 1982.
