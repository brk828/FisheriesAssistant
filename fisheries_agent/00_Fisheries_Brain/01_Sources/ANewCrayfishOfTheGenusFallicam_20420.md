# A New Crayfish of the Genus Fallicambarus from Tennessee (Decapoda, Astacidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #astacidae #decapoda #crayfish #taxonomy #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic description presents *[[Fallicambarus hortoni]]*, a new species of burrowing crayfish discovered in [[McNairy County]], [[Tennessee]]. The species was collected from shallow burrows in sandy soil near a tributary of [[Cypress Creek]] in the [[Hatchie River]] drainage system. Based on 24 specimens collected in May 1969, this represents the first species originally assigned to the genus *[[Fallicambarus]]* (all eight previously recognized species were transferred from *Cambarus*). The new species is most closely related to *F. byersi* and *F. oryktes* from the Gulf coastal plain, but is distinguished by unique morphological features, particularly the structure of the first pleopod in form I males.

## Background & Description

*[[Fallicambarus hortoni]]* is a pigmented, burrowing crayfish with well-developed eyes. Key diagnostic features include:

**Carapace and Rostrum**: Areola obliterated or linear, comprising 36.0-38.2% of total carapace length. Rostrum depressed, acuminate, lacking marginal spines or tubercles. Postorbital ridges terminate cephalically with or without very small tubercles. Cervical spines absent. Suborbital angle weak and obtuse.

**Appendages**: Antennal scale 2.3-2.5 times longer than broad, broadest distal to midlength. Chela bears two rows of tubercles on mesial palm surface; lateral margin costate; both fingers with well-defined longitudinal ridges on upper surface; dactyl with distinct emargination.

**Sexual Dimorphism**: First pleopod of form I male exhibits corneous central projection recurved at approximately 90 degrees and strongly deflected dextrally, broadly truncate with shallow or absent subapical notch. Non-corneous mesial process slightly tapering distally with eminence overlapping central projection laterally. Annulus ventralis of female immovable, approximately 1.6 times broader than long, deeply excavate dextrally with conspicuous tongue sloping cephalosinistrally.

**Coloration**: Olive brown with irregular dark and light markings. Some specimens exhibit median longitudinal pale olive-tan stripe. Carapace and abdomen drab olive-brown with olive black and pale olive tan mottlings. Distal podomeres of chelae very dark dorsally, paler ventrally with reddish orange finger tips.

**Measurements** (holotype male, form I): Carapace length 24.5 mm, width 12.6 mm, height 10.8 mm. Rostrum length 6.4 mm. Right chela outer margin 19.0 mm, dactyl length 13.1 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat**: The type locality consists of a low area along a roadside ditch leading into a small tributary of [[Cypress Creek]], 7.5 miles east of the [[Hardeman County]] line on State Route 57. Burrows were relatively simple and shallow, constructed in sandy soil. Some burrows featured chimneys similar to those of *[[Cambarus diogenes diogenes]]*.

**Vegetation**: The collection site supported several grass and Compositae species, *Viola* sp., with *[[Salix nigra]]* (black willow) along the ditch. More distant trees included *Acer* (maple), *[[Liquidambar]]* (sweetgum), and *[[Liriodendron]]* (tulip tree).

**Crayfish Associates**: No other primary burrowing species were found at the immediate type locality. However, nearby collections yielded:
- *[[Cambarus striatus]]* from adjacent creek
- *[[Procambarus ablusus]]* from adjacent creek
- Undescribed *[[Orconectes]]* species from adjacent creek
- *[[Cambarus diogenes diogenes]]* from nearby localities
- Apparent *[[Cambarus diogenes ludovicianus]]* from nearby localities

The authors note that further collections are needed to determine the precise ecological and geographical overlap between these primary burrowing crayfishes.

**Geographic Range**: Currently known only from the type locality in [[McNairy County]], [[Tennessee]]. The range lies along the edge of the genus boundary, which extends from southern Ontario, Michigan, and Illinois southward to Texas and across western Tennessee to southwestern Georgia; east of the Appalachians from Maryland to South Carolina.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Significance**: *[[Fallicambarus hortoni]]* represents the first species originally described within the genus *Fallicambarus* (established by Hobbs in 1969). All eight previously recognized species were transferred from *Cambarus*.

**Phylogenetic Relationships**: The species is most closely allied to *[[Fallicambarus byersi]]* and *[[Fallicambarus oryktes]]*, both occurring in the lower coastal plain between the [[Choctawhatchee River]] (Florida) and the [[Pontchartrain Basin]] (Louisiana). Similarities are most evident in first pleopod and annulus ventralis morphology. More distantly related to *F. fodiens*, *F. hedgpethi*, and *F. uhleri*, which occupy northwestern, southwestern, and eastern limits of the genus range.

**Distinguishing Characters**: The peculiar structure of the distal portion of the first pleopod in form I males readily distinguishes *F. hortoni* from all other crayfishes. The central projection is recurved at approximately 90 degrees, strongly deflected dextrally, and broadly truncate.

**Type Material**: Holotypic male form I (USNM 129895), morphotypic male form II (USNM 129896), and allotypic female (USNM 129897) deposited in the [[United States National Museum]]. Additional paratypes distributed between USNM and the junior author's collection. All specimens collected 20 May 1969.

**Morphological Variation**: Little variation observed beyond typical population variability. One form I male exhibited sharply arced branchiocardiac grooves creating non-linear (though obliterated) areola with one punctation visible anterior and posterior to obliterated portion.

**Sexual Forms**: Form II males differ from form I in lacking corneous elements on first pleopods; central projection broadly rounded with faint apical emarginations; mesial process also broadly rounded and directed somewhat laterally; basal "segment" delimited by suture.

**Etymology**: Species named in honor of [[Horton H. Hobbs III]], whose work on entocytherid ostracods associated with primary burrowing crayfishes has significantly advanced knowledge of burrowing species ranges.
