# Morphology and Development of the Sex Organs in the Snail *Pomatiopsis cincinnatiensis* (Lea)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pomatiopsidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 paper by [[Henry Van Der Schalie]] and [[Lowell L. Getz]] (University of Michigan and University of Connecticut) examines the reproductive anatomy of the amphibious prosobranch snail *Pomatiopsis cincinnatiensis* using serially sectioned specimens from natural populations. The authors trace male and female organ development against shell size, clarify the path eggs take through the female genital tract, and correct long-standing misidentification of the accessory gland as a "pallial oviduct." They also report two females with poorly developed male organs (verges). The work is relevant to the closely related [[Oncomelania]] genus, the intermediate hosts of [[Schistosoma japonicum]].

## Background & Description

- **Taxon:** *Pomatiopsis cincinnatiensis* (Lea), an operculate, amphibious snail in the family Pomatiopsidae. The authors state that *Pomatiopsis* is nearly identical in morphology, ecology and life history to the Oriental genus *Oncomelania*, and that hybrids between the two genera had been produced.
- **Prior work:** [[E. G. Berry]] (1943) described American Pomatiopsis broadly; [[H. van der Schalie]] and [[D. S. Dundee]] (1956) described internal anatomy of *P. cincinnatiensis*; Dundee (1957) reported on *P. lapidaria*. Multiple studies addressed *Oncomelania* anatomy, including work by Robson (1921), Nakamoto (1923), Itagaki (1955), Roth and Wagner (1957, 1960), Pesigan et al. (1956-1957), Li (1934) and Roth (1960, on *O. formosana*).
- **Methods:**
  - Collection 1: 74 specimens taken hourly from 11:30 A.M., 2 August, to 1:30 A.M., 3 August 1960. Each was relaxed, killed, fixed in Bouin's fixative, measured for shell length, serially sectioned and stained.
  - Collection 2: 238 snails collected 20 October 1959, containing all growth stages.
  - Sex organ maturity was compared with body size.
- **Funding:** U.S. Army Medical Research and Development Command (contract DA-49-007-MD-604), sponsored by the Commission on Parasitic Diseases, Armed Forces Epidemiological Board.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The genus *Oncomelania* is the snail intermediate host of [[Schistosoma japonicum]], the cause of oriental schistosomiasis, which motivated much of the comparative anatomy literature.
- The paper notes that *Pomatiopsis* and *Oncomelania* share ecology and life histories, and that both groups can strongly control egg-laying. Oviposition did not occur in a vivarium lacking soil to serve as husk for the egg, and the animals withheld egg-laying under various unfavorable conditions.
- Males' verges appear soon after hatching, and gonads and prostates are typically fully developed and functional before the animals [[Hibernation]] in the fall. Copulation was observed among young-of-the-year snails.

## Key Findings

1. **Male development:** The gonad of a half-grown male already shows considerable development, but sperm production and packing of the gonad and seminal vesicles occur only from the three-quarter to full-grown stages. The prostate gland is poorly developed until the three-quarter-grown stage and becomes relatively large in full-grown adults.
2. **Verge timing:** The verge is present almost immediately after hatching, making sex determination of very young snails relatively easy.
3. **Female development:** Females had clearly defined eggs in the ovary at only one-fourth grown. Eggs in full-grown females were several times larger than in half-grown specimens and surrounded by a large yolk mass. The accessory gland is conspicuous even at half-grown size and becomes massive in adults.
4. **Accessory gland is not an oviduct:** The accessory gland contains two tissues, an upper portion that may produce albumen and a lower portion (capsule gland) that probably secretes the mucus forming the egg husk. Serial sections show it does not convey eggs, correcting the "pallial oviduct" label used by van der Schalie and Dundee (1956), Roth and Wagner (1957) and Roth (1960).
5. **Egg path:** Eggs line up in the oviduct above its junction with the spermathecal duct, but are rarely found in the lower tract because they pass through quickly. One section showed an egg far down the common tract, confirmed by capsule-gland tissue in the same section.
6. **Dual role of the spermathecal duct:** This tube serves both as the route for sperm to reach the bursa copulatrix and seminal receptacle and as the passage for eggs laid. Connections among the convoluted tubules, bursa and accessory gland were confirmed, and some sperm invaded the upper accessory gland.
7. **Copulation:** The 80 specimens sectioned showed that copulation occurs among fully developed young-of-the-year snails. Sperm in the seminal receptacle lie perpendicular to its wall, with heads embedded en masse in the wall tissue.
8. **Anomalous females:** Two females, recognizably female by size and appearance, had poorly developed verges. Sectioning of one showed a normal female gonad with no ovotestis or hermaphroditic arrangement. Neither specimen had a vas deferens, and neither showed evidence of mating, so the male organ appeared non-functional.
9. **Corrections to prior literature:** The authors identify inconsistencies in earlier reports on *Oncomelania* and *Pomatiopsis* female tract anatomy, attributing them mainly to limited understanding of egg passage. They note that Roth (1960) failed to account for the bursa and seminal receptacle relationship described earlier by van der Schalie and Dundee (1956).
