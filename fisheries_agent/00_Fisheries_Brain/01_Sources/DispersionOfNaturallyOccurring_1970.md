## Executive Summary

This study examines an undisturbed alluvial groundwater basin in the upper [[San Pedro River]] Valley (southern Arizona) to determine how the mineralogy of surrounding fault-block mountains controls the natural ionic composition of groundwater. Seventy-nine groundwater samples were analyzed for **Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Na⁺, and Cl⁻** concentrations, mapped as isogram (isoconcentration) maps across sedimentary, igneous, volcanic, and alluvial source terrains. The authors demonstrate that ion concentrations act as natural geochemical tracers, revealing subsurface flow barriers, recharge zones, and structural controls (faults, intrusions) on the regional flow net — most notably the [[Dragoon Mountains]] limestone/quartz-monzonite complex as a Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ source, and the [[Tombstone Hills]] granitic intrusions as both a Cl⁻ source and a hydraulic barrier.

## Background & Description
*(Reframed: Geological & Hydrological Characteristics of the Study System)*

- **Basin structure**: Basin-and-Range fault-block topography; alluvium-filled trough flanked by mountain ranges, with the [[Dragoon Mountains]] forming the eastern boundary and the [[Tombstone Hills]] disrupting the pediment surface to the south/west.
- **Alluvial fill depth**: Ranges from less than 30 feet near rock outcrops to a maximum of 3300–3400 feet (determined gravimetrically).
- **Aquifer type**: Regionally unconfined; only 2 of 82 well logs showed artesian conditions (maximum 58-foot head rise in a conglomerate layer).
- **Water table behavior**: Essentially static over 8 years of record; minor recharge observed beneath ephemeral streambeds via transmission losses.
- **Rock-type classification**: Sedimentary (limestone, dolomite), igneous (granodiorite, quartz monzonite, quartz-latite porphyry), volcanic (andesite flow breccia, rhyolite), and alluvium.
- **Key structural features**:
  - Large displacement fault east of [[Tombstone, Arizona]] combined with a granitic intrusion northwest of it, deflecting groundwater contours from normal downslope trend.
  - A structural divide (limestone + andesite) south and east of the Tombstone Hills splits drainage toward Walnut Gulch (north) versus a separate southern drainage — both ultimately reaching the San Pedro River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*(Reframed: Hydrogeochemical Niche — Source-Rock to Groundwater Ion Pathways)*

- **Calcium (Ca²⁺)**: Sourced primarily from limestones of the Dragoon Mountains and fractured/jointed limestone near igneous contacts in the Tombstone Hills; concentration decreases with distance from source due to mixing in the deep alluvium; resistant andesite-rhyolite volcanics contribute little Ca²⁺.
- **Magnesium (Mg²⁺)**: Parallels Ca²⁺ patterns but at lower concentration; highest where limestone coexists with granitic intrusives (biotite-augite quartz monzonite in the Dragoons; hornblende-biotite granodiorite in the Tombstone Hills) due to hydrothermal alteration of mafic minerals (biotite/olivine → serpentine).
- **Sodium (Na⁺)**: Three source zones — (1) coarse-grained quartz monzonites of the lower Dragoon Mountains (feldspar decomposition via carbonated water → sodium carbonate + clays); (2) altered andesitic volcanics north and west of Tombstone; (3) volcanics south of the Tombstone Hills restricting river recharge and confining flow.
- **Chloride (Cl⁻)**: Concentrated (up to 40 ppm) in an area of the Tombstone Hills coincident with granodiorite and quartz-latite porphyry intrusions, attributed to hydrothermal breakdown of mafic minerals (chlorite/epidote alteration); an unrelated high-Cl⁻ zone in the southwest is linked to evaporites and underlying volcanics.
- **Base-exchange dynamics**: Sodium-Absorption-Ratio (SAR) analysis along two cross-sections showed SAR values of only 0.08–2.5, indicating minimal Ca²⁺/Na⁺ base-exchange reactions in the alluvium — confirming Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ as reliable, largely unaltered tracers of flow.

## Key Findings

- **Rock-specific ionic "fingerprints"**: Each isolated source-rock terrain (Dragoon Mountains limestones, Tombstone Hills granitics, andesitic volcanics) produces a distinguishable and mappable ionic signature in groundwater, enabling use of Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Na⁺, and Cl⁻ as **natural geochemical tracers** of groundwater provenance and flow direction.
- **Dragoon Mountains** = major contributor of Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ to the deep alluvial basin via carbonate dissolution.
- **Tombstone Hills granitic intrusives** (granodiorite, quartz-latite porphyry) act as both:
  1. A **hydraulic barrier**, deflecting the normal downslope flow pattern northward/westward, and
  2. A **primary Cl⁻ source**, via hydrothermal alteration of hornblende/biotite/augite-bearing rock.
- **Structural/geochemical barrier** identified south and east of the Tombstone Hills, where shallow limestone and volcanic formations disconnect the basin into separate flow compartments (draining respectively toward Walnut Gulch and a southern drainage network).
- **Minimal base-exchange activity** in the alluvium (SAR 0.08–2.5) validates the use of Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ concentration gradients as unmodified tracers of recharge-source contribution.
- **Practical implication**: In undisturbed, low-permeability alluvial basins of the [[Basin and Range Province]], naturally occurring major-ion chemistry can substitute for costly artificial tracer studies in delineating recharge zones, subsurface flow barriers, and basin compartmentalization — a technique broadly relevant to groundwater resource management in arid southwestern watersheds such as the [[San Pedro River Basin]].
