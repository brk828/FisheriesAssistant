# Subterranean Harpacticoid Copepods from Greek Groundwater

#ichthyology #marine-biology #harpacticoida

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Giuseppe Lucio Pesce]] (Istituto di Zoologia, Università di L'Aquila, 1981) describes three new harpacticoid copepod species from phreatic groundwater in Greece and reports new localities for several previously known species. Specimens were collected from freshwater and brackish wells between 1976 and 1980 on the Northern Sporades, the Peloponnesus, Zante and Attica. The paper also briefly discusses the systematic status of several [[Elaphoidella]] species. The author notes that the samples contain no fish; the work concerns groundwater crustaceans.

## Background & Description

Harpacticoid copepods are small crustaceans that inhabit groundwater. The study was conducted by the Zoological Institute of the University of L'Aquila as part of research on underground waters of Greece. Sampling covered wells on Skiros, Skiathos, Alonissos, Skopelos, the Peloponnesus and Zante. Research was supported by a CNR grant (No. CT 79.00988.04). Acknowledgements credit [[Rouch]] for advice on the genus *Elaphoidella* and collectors G. Silveri and D. Maggi.

The new species are:
- **Elaphoidella moreae n. sp.**: female body length 0.75–0.85 mm; anal operculum with 12–15 stout spines; caudal rami about 1.4–1.5 times longer than wide; belongs to "group IV" of [[Elaphoidella]] after Lang (1948).
- **Parapseudoleptomesochra hellenica n. sp.**: female body length 0.42–0.49 mm; unpigmented and eyeless; holotype from a well at Aspous, Skiros. Male length 0.39–0.44 mm.
- **Nitocrella achaiae n. sp.**: female body length 0.45–0.53 mm; caudal rami about twice as long as wide (L/W 1.7–1.8); type locality Kavasilas, Peloponnesus. Male length 0.39–0.43 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All newly described species are groundwater (stygobiont) forms known from single areas:
- *E. moreae* is endemic to the Peloponnesus.
- *P. hellenica* is endemic to Skiros, Northern Sporades.
- *N. achaiae* is endemic to the Peloponnesus.

Other taxa have wider ranges. *Elaphoidella elaphoides* is a stygophil species found in northern Europe, Italy and the Balkan Peninsula, in both subterranean and surface waters. *Nitocrella stammeri* is known from phreatic and cave waters in Italy (including Sardinia), Turkey and Greece. According to some authors, it may be a recent immigrant that colonized groundwater during Pliocene regressions of the [[Mediterranean Sea]].

Several sampled wells were brackish (for example at Linaria, Molos and Zante), and some sites lay within about 5–150 m of the coast. The paper gives no water chemistry measurements.

## Key Findings

- **Three new species** are described: *Elaphoidella moreae*, *Parapseudoleptomesochra hellenica* and *Nitocrella achaiae*.
- **New localities** are reported for eight known species: *Elaphoidella karamani*, *Elaphoidella elaphoides*, *Attheyella (Brehmiella) dentata*, *Attheyella (Attheyella) crassa*, *Nitocrella skiroensis*, *Nitocra spinipes*, *Bryocamptus (Bryocamptus) minutus* and *Canthocamptus staphylinus*.
- **Systematic status:** *E. karamani* and *E. eucharis* are considered possibly synonymous ("*E. eucharis* (= karamani?)"). The author notes *E. elaphoides* shows wide morphological variability, as described by [[Petkovski]], and may form a "Rassenkreis" with related Balkan forms.
- **Diagnostic separation of *E. moreae*:** the distal exopod segment of P3 and P5 carries 6 armature elements (5 in *karamani* and *eucharis*), its caudal rami are shorter, and its furcal dorsal seta is longer than the rami.
- **Nitocra specimens from Zante** are assigned as *Nitocra cfr. spinipes*. They differ from the type description in leg and anal operculum armature (9–10 spines versus 12–14). The author considers the material too limited to define the status with certainty.
- **Nitocrella achaiae** belongs to the "chappuisi" group of *Nitocrella s. str.*, and is closest to *N. stammeri*.
- Material is deposited in several collections, including the Zoological Institute of the University of L'Aquila, the Muséum d'Histoire Naturelle (Genève), the Prirodonaucen Muzej (Skopje) and the Zoologisch Museum (Amsterdam).
