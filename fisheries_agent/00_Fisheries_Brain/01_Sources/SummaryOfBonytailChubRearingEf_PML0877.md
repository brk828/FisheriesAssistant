# Bonytail Chub Rearing Efforts at Niland Native Fish Ponds, 1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This California Department of Fish and Game interoffice memorandum (March 21, 1988) summarizes the 1987 attempt to rear [[Bonytail Chub]] fingerlings at the [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]]. About 1,000 fingerlings from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] were stocked in a quarter-acre pond in July 1987 to test whether the fish could tolerate the facility's high summer water temperatures. The fish survived the summer and grew well. A [[Lernaea]] (anchor worm) outbreak, first seen in [[Razorback Sucker]] at the same site, then reached the chubs. By February 1988 all of the fish were dead. The memo states that rearing will be tried again in 1988 with preventive measures against parasites.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), a Colorado River cyprinid. The memo gives no morphological or life-history detail.
- **Source stock:** Approximately 1,000 fingerlings from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Transport:** Fish arrived at El Centro Airport on July 15, 1987, and were moved to the Niland facility.
- **Rearing site:** A quarter-acre pond at the [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]] (NNFP), a facility chosen for its high summer water temperatures.
- **Purpose:** To determine whether bonytail could be reared under those conditions.
- **Author and distribution:** Written by the Area Fishery Biologist, Region 5 (Blythe), with copies to the Region 5 management staff and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] office in Albuquerque, NM.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal conditions:** The Niland site has high summer water temperatures. The memo treats these as the variable under test, and the study reports no measured temperature values.
- **Parasite threat:** [[Lernaea]], a parasitic copepod, infested the [[Razorback Sucker]] population at NNFP in October 1987 and then affected the bonytail.
- **Relocation:** In January 1988, after further chub deaths, the fish were to be moved to a backwater of the [[Colorado River]] within the [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]]. Agreements were required before the move.
- **Disease management gap:** The memo indicates that parasite control was not in place during the 1987 rearing period, which is the main reason stated for the failure.

## Key Findings

1. **Survival and growth (summer 1987):** The bonytail fingerlings survived the summer months at Niland and appeared to have good growth rates. This is the main positive result, showing the species could be held under high summer temperatures.
2. **Initial mortality (October 1987):** A Lernaea outbreak that affected [[Razorback Sucker]] at NNFP was presumed to have also infected the bonytail. Hatchery personnel reported a few chub deaths, but not many.
3. **Continued losses (January 1988):** Further reports of chub deaths from Lernaea prompted a decision to relocate the remaining fish to the Colorado River backwater at the Imperial National Wildlife Refuge.
4. **Total loss (February 16, 1988):** When the pond was drained to collect the fish, all of them were found dead. No bonytail remained in the pond.
5. **Plan for 1988:** The agency will attempt bonytail rearing again and will take all possible preventive measures to keep Lernaea from infesting the fish. The memo does not list the specific measures.

**Overall conclusion:** Bonytail can survive and grow at the Niland site in summer, but the program failed because of parasite-related mortality. Effective parasite prevention is the key requirement for any future rearing.
