# Arnett Creek Fish Barrier Design Narrative

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This USDA Forest Service design narrative, prepared for the Globe Ranger District of the [[Tonto National Forest]] (Southwestern Region), specifies the purpose, site, hydrology, design standards, and funding for a fish barrier on [[Arnett Creek]], a tributary of [[Queen Creek]] in Gila County, Arizona. The barrier would block upstream invasion of perennial reaches of Arnett Creek by non-native [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Mosquitofish]], which are known to negatively affect native Arizona fishes. Downstream movement would not be restricted. The document also contains a meeting agenda for a design review and a record of stream flow, rainfall, and recreation data that inform the design.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Block upstream movement of non-native fish from Queen Creek into perennial portions of Arnett Creek above the barrier site, protecting native aquatic habitat from reinvasion.
- **Setting:** The site lies in an ephemeral reach (T2S R11E, Section 12) in an alluvial valley near the border of the proposed [[Proposed Research Natural Area]] (named "Pickeeost Mountain" in the scan). The valley is about 300 ft wide, with a floodprone width of about 100 ft. Much of the valley is terrace. Vegetation is dominated by Baccharis along the channel and mesquite on the terrace.
- **Site selection criteria:**
  1. Maximize aquatic habitat protected from reinvasion.
  2. Road access (overland access to other candidate sites was unavailable).
  3. Narrow floodprone width, since a wider section would spread flood flow and reduce barrier stability.
  4. Anchoring substrate: exposed bedrock on the east bank, with terrace soils on the west.
- **Cultural resources:** A historic (1930) livestock well and water storage structure lies about 30 ft downstream of the barrier on the south terrace, and a drinking trough about 100 ft downstream. Mitigation measures were planned to protect them.
- **Agenda (July 14, 1995, 1 p.m., Tonto NF Supervisor's Office):** Review design considerations and engineering specifications, including structure depth and height to withstand and contain a 50-year flood.

The watershed above the barrier is reported as 1004 (units garbled in the scan), which the document says is 231 acres less than a comparison area. Basin mean annual rainfall ranges from 14 to 22 inches depending on elevation, mainly from winter frontal systems and summer thunderstorms.

**Geology and vegetation.** The upper watershed is composed of volcanic and granitic rock, with more exposed bedrock and less vegetation in the steeper terrain.

**Stream flow (August 1991 onward).** Flow is measured near the middle of the instream flow reach with a USGS pygmy meter, galvanized and fiberglass Parshall flumes, and since October 1992 a Marsh-McBirney flow meter, which performs well at all flows observed.

- Average flow: 3.0 cfs (29 instantaneous measurements).
- Median annual flow: 0.42 cfs.
- Flow is perennial, never below 0.1 cfs, and exceeds 1 cfs about 25% of the time.
- Monthly means ranged from 0.16 cfs (July) to 17.0 cfs (January), with the lowest flows in summer and early fall.

**Rainfall.** At Superior, annual totals against a normal of 17.84 in were 24.16 in (1991, +6.32), 35.68 in (1992, +17.84), and 29.66 in (1993, +11.82). Because the monitoring period was wetter than normal, the authors concluded that long-term January, February, and possibly March flows would be lower than recorded, while September and October flows would be slightly higher.

**Recreation.** Arnett Creek is a desert oasis. Its perennial water and riparian vegetation support picnicking, hiking, birding, wildlife viewing, and wading, all of which depend on perennial flow.

**Fish and flow requirements.** Arnett Creek lies within the historic range of eight native desert fishes: [[Longfin Dace]], [[Spikedace]], Desert Sucker, Speckled Dace, Loach Minnow, [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Gila Chub]], and Sonora Sucker. The Tonto NF, in cooperation with the Arizona Game and Fish Department, proposes reintroducing at least some of these species.

## Key Findings

**Design standards** (developed with the Tonto NF hydrologist, a Bureau of Reclamation biologist, a Globe District archaeologist, and a Tonto NF civil engineer, drawing on [[Bagley et al. 1995]]):

1. **Flood capacity:** Construct to withstand and contain a 50-year flood of 9,207 cfs. The barrier will most likely be built of concrete.
2. **Drop height:** Provide a vertical drop of 4 to 5 ft on the downstream side, creating a waterfall. The barrier is asymmetric, with a lower center, to funnel most flow to the north half of the channel and away from the historic livestock structure on the south bank.
3. **Keying:** Key the barrier into bedrock on the north bank and 10 to 20 ft into the terrace on the south bank.
4. **No pools:** Avoid pool formation upstream and downstream, and design to discourage future pool development.
5. **Lifetime:** Design for 50 to 100 years.
6. **Apron:** Angle the barrier downstream and install a downstream apron sloped away from it, with no eddies, backwaters, or low-velocity zones where fish could rest while attempting to pass. The apron serves two purposes: it acts as a splash pad to prevent undercutting, and it increases velocity and reduces depth over the barrier to discourage fish from jumping or holding there.

**Engineering and schedule:**

- The design and contract package is prepared by Tonto NF engineers Steve Wendland and LeAnne Olmstead, who will also serve as Contracting Officer's Representatives. The design was scheduled for completion by August 1994.
- Construction will be contracted to the private sector, with standard Best Management Practices (BMPs) included in the contract to avoid or minimize impacts on forest resources.
