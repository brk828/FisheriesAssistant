# Comments on the Draft Environmental Impact Statement for Glen Canyon Dam Operations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This is a formal public comment letter, dated 12 March 1993, from [[Paul C. Marsh]], Research Professor at the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies, on the draft [[Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS) prepared by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] for the operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The letter is written from the perspective of an aquatic biologist. It praises the EIS team's effort but argues that the document has a bias toward sediment and hydropower concerns and away from federally listed native fishes. The main requests are a native-fish alternative, a fuller justification for the elimination of a [[Run-of-the-River Alternative]], a re-evaluation of the role of [[Cladophora Glomerata]] in the food base, better treatment of [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], and a clearer account of how the alternatives and flow numbers were chosen.

## Background & Description

The letter reviews the draft EIS at a general level and in detail, focusing on the aquatic biology sections (fishes, Cladophora, and food base) and noting that hydrology, sediment, terrestrial, cultural and human-use sections were left to other reviewers. Marsh asks who participated in developing the 10 alternatives, which stakeholders were involved in the "brainstorming" sessions, and how specific flow values (daily, seasonal and monthly volumes) were derived. He also requests that the [[Desert Fishes Council]] be added to the mailing list.

The comments cover:

- **Process and transparency:** the scoping section placement, the missing Adaptive Management section, an unclear "Grand Canyon Protection Act" attachment, and undocumented sources for alternatives.
- **Flow tables and definitions:** Table II-2 (minimum releases, footnotes, units in cfs/hr versus cfs/day), the Monthly Release Volume text (page 31), beach/habitat-building flow frequency (2 in 5 years), and the definition of fluctuating flow.
- **Biology:** Cladophora as the food base, humpback chub population structure, trout versus native-fish treatment, razorback sucker age claims, and sampling bias.
- **Editorial corrections:** terminology and nomenclature errors.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter frames the Colorado River below Glen Canyon Dam as a regulated, cold tailwater system. Several points recur:

- **Humpback chub:** The letter notes that humpback chub spawn in the [[Little Colorado River]] and that mainstem reproduction is likely precluded by water temperature. The Little Colorado River may therefore be the only place in Grand Canyon where the species reproduces and recruits successfully. The draft's account of a single population versus separate resident and migratory groups is described as unsettled, citing [[Angradi et al. 1992]] and related work by Douglas and Marsh.
- **Food base:** The EIS claims that Cladophora forms the basis of the aquatic food web below the dam. Marsh argues that this is unsupported without an organic matter budget that accounts for allochthonous inputs, dissolved organic matter from [[Lake Powell]], and other autochthonous production.
- **Trout and non-native fishes:** The letter points out that [[Rainbow Trout]] and other coldwater species receive far more text than native fishes, and that some non-native species (such as the fathead minnow) are small-bodied and rarely considered.
- **Sampling bias:** Seines, traps and electrofishing sample only shallow, quiet margins. Most of the swift main channel is therefore effectively unsampled, which means that fish absence cannot be assumed.
- **Hydrology:** Backwaters are said to increase threefold as flows decline from 15,000 to 5,000 cfs. Sediment transport capacity and flow fluctuation thresholds are also questioned.

## Key Findings

**Alternatives and process**
- The EIS lacks an alternative designed to optimize conservation of native fishes, including State- and Federally-listed threatened and endangered species, even though federal agencies have a legal mandate to protect them.
- The Run-of-the-River Alternative was dismissed on the basis that sediment augmentation would harm the aquatic ecosystem below Lees Ferry. Marsh considers this inadequately justified because a run-of-the-river regime would restore some pre-dam conditions. He recommends that it, and a native-fish alternative, be considered in detail.
- The EIS should document who participated in developing the alternatives, how flow values such as 10 alternatives were derived, and how stakeholder preferences were applied.
- The Adaptive Management section was missing from the reviewed copy. The letter says it is critical to a full review and asks for it to be released before public distribution.
- The Scoping section should be moved from Chapter V to the front of the document (for example, Chapter I).

**Flows and tables**
- Table II-2 has inconsistent units (cfs/hr versus cfs/day) and footnote-dependent minimum flows. Marsh asks that minimum releases during high-volume months be added to the body of the table.
- The Monthly Release Volume text should be revised to match the seasonally adjusted table. Monthly values listed are Aug–Sep 9,000 cfs, Oct–Nov 8,000 cfs, and Dec 8,500 cfs.
- The beach/habitat-building flow frequency of 2 in 5 years is questioned. The letter asks which value was determined first and how it was combined with projected low reservoir elevations.
- The threshold defining fluctuating flow (more than 2,000 cfs in 24 hours) is unexplained, and the letter asks what impacts would occur at 1,000, 1,500 or 1,750 cfs.
- The statement that sediment transport capacity has been reduced is questioned, because stream competence generally increases when sediment load is lower.

**Species and populations**
- The statement that two populations of humpback chub spawn in the Little Colorado River is described as unfounded. Marsh notes that the EIS does not cite its sources, which makes independent verification difficult.
- Plans to establish a second population of humpback chub in tributaries below Glen Canyon Dam are questioned, along with the vague commitment to "every effort" to achieve this and the absence of thermal modification in the operational alternatives.
- Critical habitat for humpback chub was proposed in the *Federal Register* (58(181): 6578–6597) and should be documented in the EIS. The proposal includes razorback sucker, Colorado squawfish and bonytail, but only humpback chub critical habitat within Grand Canyon.
- The claim that small razorback suckers in Grand Canyon are old individuals is unfounded, because total length does not reliably indicate age in cold mainstream habitats.
- The claim that flannelmouth sucker use lower reaches for reproduction and nursery, then disperse upstream, requires quantitative data.

**Biology and terminology**
- Cladophora's role in the food base is overstated without an organic matter budget. The letter recommends an outside expert with broader knowledge of Cladophora in other systems, and notes that the cited technical reports are unpublished and not peer-reviewed.
- The statement that phosphorus is limiting has not been experimentally tested. The letter asks for the evidence behind this conclusion.
- Corrections requested: "fry" should be "larva"; "production" should be "primary production" or "productivity" where photosynthesis is meant; the Little Colorado River is mislabeled "Lower" in Figure III-2; Chaenobryttus should be Lepomis (green sunfish) and Notropis should be Cyprinella (red shiner) in the list on page III-82.
- The fish list (Table FSH-2, 13 species, only three of them salmonids) should be ordered systematically, not alphabetically, and native and non-native species should be separated.
- The EIS should state that the fish list is not comprehensive. For example, ameiurid catfishes are omitted.

**Overall judgments**
- The section on trout (about 3 pages) is disproportionately long compared with the section on humpback chub (under 1 page), which suggests either missing information or a bias toward sport fishes.
- The EIS impact assessments for humpback chub and razorback sucker (Table IV-1) are considered unreliable because the species accounts are weak.
- The letter asks the EIS team to consult recognized species experts from outside the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies group.
