# Toxicity of Carbaryl and Malathion to Colorado Squawfish and Bonytail

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This doctoral dissertation by Daniel H. Beyers (Colorado State University, Spring 1993) estimates the toxicity of two grasshopper-control insecticides, [[Carbaryl]] and [[Malathion]], to the federally endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail]]. The work combines a 4-d renewal-acute test, a 32-d early life-stage (ELS) test, and a 1-d in vivo brain [[Acetylcholinesterase]] (AChE) inhibition test. It also compares hypothesis-testing (ANOVA) with [[Linear-Plateau Regression]] for deriving effect concentrations. The dissertation is organized into three manuscript-form chapters plus a conclusion, and it is framed around the risk that accidental pesticide drift or runoff poses to fish in aquatic habitats adjacent to rangeland spraying.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Colorado squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and bonytail (*Gila elegans*) are large riverine minnows that historically occurred throughout the Colorado River Basin. Their populations declined after reservoir construction and other basin management practices. They were listed as federally endangered in 1967 and 1980, respectively.
- **Surrogate species:** [[Colorado Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta robusta*) remains abundant in the upper basin and was used to optimize the brain AChE assay.
- **Insecticides tested:** technical carbaryl (1-naphthyl methylcarbamate, 99%); Sevin-4-Oil (49% carbaryl plus petroleum distillates); and technical malathion (93%).
- **Chapter structure:**
  - Chapter 1: [[Solid-Phase Extraction]] of carbaryl and malathion from pond and well water (published in *Environmental Toxicology and Chemistry*, 10:1425-1429).
  - Chapter 2: optimal assay conditions for brain AChE in roundtail chub.
  - Chapter 3: toxicity of carbaryl and malathion to the two endangered fishes, analyzed by regression and ANOVA.
- **Funding:** USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service, Grasshopper Integrated Pest Management Project.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Grasshopper outbreaks in western states are controlled by aerial and ground insecticide applications on rangeland and cropland. No-spray buffers exist around aquatic habitats, but pesticide can still reach them through drift or runoff.
- Larvae of both endangered species occupy shallow, low-velocity, near-shore habitats from June through October. Low water exchange in these habitats can keep pesticides present long enough to cause toxic effects. Life stages less than 30 days old overlap temporally with grasshopper control programs, so they are at risk of exposure.
- Carbaryl and malathion hydrolyze rapidly at pH above 7. Test solutions at pH 7.9-8.5 lost 58% (carbaryl) and 37% (malathion) of their concentration in 24 h. Breakdown products can be more toxic than the parent compounds, so the net effect of hydrolysis is uncertain.
- Grasshopper control risk has declined in recent years due to natural population cycling, bran-bait application practices, and integrated pest management. The author notes that it is unknown whether these measures will suffice during peak grasshopper years.
- Laboratory tests use dilution water from a campus well (about 22 °C, pH about 8.0-8.6, hardness roughly 212-378 mg/L as CaCO₃).

## Key Findings

**Analytical method (Chapter 1)**
- A solid-phase extraction method on C18 columns gave mean recoveries of 80-100% over 1 to 10,000 µg/L in well and pond water.
- Fortified samples stored on columns at -4 °C for 30 d showed no significant decomposition.

**Assay optimization (Chapter 2)**
- Optimal pH-stat AChE conditions were 10 mg brain tissue per reaction vessel, 30 °C, 11 mM substrate, and pH 7.5. Enzyme inhibition confirmed that acetylcholine hydrolysis was primarily by AChE.

**Median lethal concentrations (4-d renewal-acute, probit analysis)**

| Toxicant | Colorado Squawfish | Bonytail |
|---|---|---|
| Technical carbaryl | 1.31 mg/L (1.23-1.40) | 2.02 mg/L (1.78-2.25) |
| Sevin-4-Oil | 3.18 mg/L (2.87-3.52) | 3.31 mg/L (3.06-3.55) |
| Technical malathion | 9.14 mg/L (8.36-10.0) | 15.3 mg/L (14.4-16.4) |

- Sevin-4-Oil was about half as toxic as carbaryl. Ratios between 0.5 and 1.5 were considered normal variation, so no synergism or antagonism was found.
- Malathion was about 7 times less toxic than carbaryl.

**Early life-stage tests (32 d)**
- NOECs for growth, carbaryl / malathion: Colorado squawfish 445 / 1680 µg/L; bonytail 650 / 990 µg/L.
- Linear-plateau threshold concentrations, carbaryl / malathion: Colorado squawfish 364 / 455 µg/L; bonytail 217 / 521 µg/L.

**Brain AChE inhibition (1 d, Colorado squawfish)**
- NOECs: 29.3 µg/L carbaryl and 371 µg/L malathion.
- Linear-plateau thresholds: 7.40 µg/L carbaryl (95% CI 4.93-11.1) and 150 µg/L malathion (83.8-270).
- AChE inhibition was the most sensitive endpoint; survival was the least sensitive.

**Statistical comparison**
- Linear-plateau regression consistently gave lower effect concentrations than hypothesis testing. Six of ten thresholds were within a factor of two of the NOECs, and the rest were within a factor of four.
- The author attributes much of the discrepancy to the use of only two replicates per concentration in ELS tests.

**Comparative sensitivity**
- Both endangered fishes were about as sensitive to carbaryl as cutthroat and rainbow trout, and two to ten times more sensitive than fathead minnow, channel catfish, and bluegill.
- They were one to two orders of magnitude less sensitive to malathion than the trouts and bluegill, and about as sensitive as fathead minnow and channel catfish.
- Their ELS effect concentrations for carbaryl overlapped those of fathead minnow (NOEC 210 µg/L, LOEC 680 µg/L). Their malathion effect concentrations were five to eight times higher.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Laboratory results indicate that carbaryl and malathion concentrations equivalent to those from large-scale aerial applications have toxic effects on young Colorado squawfish.
- Ecological risk assessment is recommended. It would integrate laboratory and field data and needs only the distribution of vulnerable life stages and species to be completed.
- A blanket moratorium on synthetic pesticides near basin rivers is considered unjustified. Risk-based guidelines are preferred so that insect control can proceed without significantly affecting listed fishes.
