# Limnological Survey and Energy Budget of Lake Mead (1948-1949)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This OCR-degraded technical report (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation and related agencies, 1948-1949 field program) documents twelve oceanographic cruises on [[Lake Mead]], the reservoir formed by [[Hoover Dam]] on the [[Colorado River]]. The study describes seasonal temperature and salinity structure, water circulation driven by density-current inflow, Secchi-disc transparency, and an annual [[Energy Budget]] used to estimate [[Lake Evaporation]]. The text does not address fish, invertebrates or plankton, so no species are identified.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Study the circulation of the lake from distributions of temperature, salinity and transparency, and develop methods for estimating evaporation from a body of water.
- **Study area:** Lake Mead, a reservoir whose main basins include Lower Basin, Virgin Basin, Virgin Arm, Upper Arm and the old Colorado River channel. Cruise stations were placed along horizontal and vertical sections, with Iceberg Canyon and Virgin Canyon as reference features.
- **Survey design:** Twelve cruises were run from February 1948 to February 1949 (listed in a station-date table, e.g., Cruise I 2-3 February 1948; Cruise XII 12-14 February 1949). Roughly half of the stations on each survey were sampled for water-quality analysis.
- **Instrumentation:** Bathythermograph (temperature versus depth), reversing thermometers, Nansen-type water bottles, Secchi disc, and salinity determined from conductivity measurements at Boulder City and Salt Lake City laboratories, with some samples analysed for total dissolved solids.
- **Auxiliary data:** Inflow and outflow records from the U.S. Geological Survey and Bureau of Reclamation, meteorological data from Boulder City, and evaporation-pan records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water sources:** The [[Colorado River]] inflow was low in salinity (about 200-350 ppm in spring) and high in salinity in other seasons (up to about 1080 ppm in late 1948), and it formed underflows or interflows that spread through the reservoir. The [[Virgin River]] contributed a smaller, separate input, concentrated in Virgin Arm.
- **Water masses:** Four principal water types were identified from temperature-salinity (T-S) relations: winter water (LV, cold and about 0.5 ppm mixed), spring inflow (SI, low salinity), summer/fall inflow (SU and FI, high salinity) and a Virgin Basin water mass.
- **Seasonal pattern:** Winter produced near-uniform, low-salinity water; spring high inflow spread fresh water up into Virgin Arm; summer and fall saw dense, high-salinity inflow sink along the old river channel and form upslope movement near the bottom.
- **Thermal range:** Surface temperatures ranged from about 49 °F in winter to above 80 °F in summer; bottom temperatures stayed near 50-60 °F.
- **Transparency:** Secchi-disc readings were lowest in turbid spring inflow and highest in calmer, clearer winter conditions (values up to about 40 feet were recorded).

## Key Findings

- **Circulation:** Density currents from the Colorado River inflow formed a single-cell circulation with upslope movement, creating seasonal sinking, mixing and surface-layer displacement.
- **Rates of movement:** Salinity isohalines (e.g., 400 ppm) moved down-lake at about 0.6-0.7 mile per day in summer; a maximum inflow of about 900,000 cubic feet per second... (as reported in the survey text) and a minimum of about 6,500 cubic feet per second occurred around late spring to summer.
- **Energy budget:** The annual energy budget gives an annual evaporation total of **161.0 centimeters** for the year 1948 (monthly mean 13.4 cm), compared with an average of 8.4 cm for the Lake Mead period; the reported range runs from about 24.5 cm in July to about 0.2 cm in winter months.
- **Method:** The Bowen ratio method was applied to estimate evaporation, with the author noting that pan-based corrections vary seasonally and that the results are provisional.
- **Uniform storage:** Energy storage per unit area was found to be nearly uniform across Lake Mead regions, so a single temperature profile from each major basin could represent the whole lake.
- **Caveat:** The authors state that results are preliminary and may change after more detailed study.
