# Parasitic Copepods from African Cichlid Fishes in the Musée Royal de l'Afrique Centrale

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae #copepoda #ergasilidae

## Executive Summary

This short systematic note by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] reports the identification of parasitic copepods collected from the gills and branchial chambers of cichlid fishes held in the [[Musée Royal de l'Afrique Centrale]] (Tervuren, Belgium). The material was gathered by Dr. P. L. G. Benoit, who searched gill parasites across many cichlid lots. Three copepod taxa are central to the report: [[Lamproglena Monodi]], [[Ergasilus Sarsi]] and [[Ergasilus Kandti]], together with several other *Ergasilus* forms and one species, [[Ergasilus Latus]], recorded in brackish water. The note mainly documents host and locality records; its main value lies in clarifying the distribution of cichlid-parasitic copepods across the [[Congo River Basin]].

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** A follow-up to an earlier report on copepods from *Tilapia* (Fryer 1963), extending the search to other cichlid genera held in the museum.
- **Material:** Roughly 100 registration lots (numbers 51533 to 52383) were examined. Each entry lists the copepod, host, locality and specimen count; the lots are summarised here rather than reproduced in full.
- **Host genera:** Mainly [[Hemihaplochromis]], [[Haplochromis]], [[Hemichromis]], [[Tilapia]], [[Tylochromis]], [[Rheohaplochromis]] and [[Pelmatochromis]].
- **Parasites:** Chiefly small, gill-attached copepods of the families [[Ergasilidae]] and [[Lernaeidae]]. Their identification relies on antennae, leg 5 setation and egg sac length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lamproglena monodi** was the most widespread parasite. It was recorded from the Lubumbashi, Katuba, Karavia, Fwa, Kasai-related and Ubangi sites, and on multiple hosts including *Hemichromis fasciatus* and *H. bimaculatus*. The author notes it is "common and widespread in the Congo system."
- **Ergasilus sarsi** appears concentrated in the Mweru-Bangweulu region and the Upper Lualaba. It was found mainly on *Tylochromis mylodon* and *T. banguelensis*.
- **Ergasilus cunningtoni** was recorded from Lake Tumba on *Tylochromis microdon*. Its egg sacs were noticeably longer than those of *E. sarsi*.
- **Ergasilus latus** was found at two brackish-water sites near the Congo mouth (Bula Bemba and Moanda) on *Tilapia heudeloti dolloi*. Previously it had only been known from Lake Rudolf, a site whose water chemistry is now peculiar but was originally fresh.
- **Specimen sizes:** Adult female *Haplochromis schwetzi* hosts from Cuango Cafunfo had a minimum length of about 1.37 mm.
- **Host check:** Dr. F. E. Thys van den Audenaerde reported that *Tilapia zillii* and *T. lepidura*, listed as hosts from Stanley Pool, do not occur there. The author attributes this to a labelling error, and the parasite's presence in the region is still valid because it occurs on other hosts.

## Key Findings

- **Lamproglena monodi** is common and widespread in the Congo system, recorded from numerous localities and hosts.
- **Ergasilus sarsi** may be restricted to the Mweru-Bangweulu region or the Upper Lualaba, though this remains uncertain.
- **Ergasilus cunningtoni** may be restricted to the Lower Congo or certain tributaries. It has not been recorded from the area frequented by *E. sarsi*.
- **Intermediate populations** (*Ergasilus* cf. *sarsi* from Ikengo, with features of both *E. sarsi* and *E. cunningtoni*) have uncertain status. More fresh material is required.
- **Ergasilus latus** is reported from brackish water near the Congo mouth. Based on preserved material alone, the author assigns it to the Lake Rudolf taxon. The population may represent a physiological race adapted to brackish conditions.
- **Congo-mouth Ergasilus:** Specimens near Banana (Kitona) resemble *E. latus* but may be a distinct, similar species. This requires further material.
- **Overall:** Knowledge of African ergasilids is described as "still very fragmentary." Some problems would be best tackled by researchers with access to fresh specimens.
