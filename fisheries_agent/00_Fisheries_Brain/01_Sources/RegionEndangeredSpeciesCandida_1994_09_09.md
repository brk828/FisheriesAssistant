# Region 2 Endangered Species Candidate Animals Table (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #cyprinodontidae #freshwater-conservation

## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Region 2 memorandum (dated September 9, 1994) transmitting an updated **Candidate Animals Table** under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It is not a research paper but a regulatory/administrative record used to track species being considered for federal listing as **endangered** or **threatened**. The attached table (heavily degraded by OCR) enumerates candidate animals — dominated by **southwestern desert fishes** (suckers, pupfish, chubs, topminnows, darters, shiners) alongside numerous **aquatic and terrestrial invertebrates** (freshwater mussels, snails, amphipods, isopods, crayfish, stoneflies, grasshoppers, and lepidopterans) — with fields for taxonomic category (1, 2, 3A, 3B, 3C), population trend, distribution by state, and lead/support Field Offices. The core scientific value lies in the **candidate species categorization framework** and the **taxonomic composition of imperiled aquatic fauna in Region 2** (Arizona, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Texas, and portions of neighboring states), rather than in original field data.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose**: Update Region 2's official roster of candidate species to align with the forthcoming Animal Notice of Review (superseding the November 21, 1991 Notice, 56 FR 58804).
- **Administrative mechanism**: Changes to a species' candidate status (category, priority, distribution, nomenclature, population trend) require submission of a standardized **"Category and Listing Priority Assignment Form"** by the lead Field/State Office.
- **Category definitions** (as reconstructed from the form):
  - **Category 1**: Species for which the Service has sufficient biological information to support listing as endangered or threatened; requires a **Listing Priority Number** and both Regional Office and Washington Office concurrence for assignment or priority changes.
  - **Category 2**: Species for which listing may be appropriate but for which data are insufficient to support a proposed rule; requires Regional Office concurrence to add.
  - **Category 3 (3A, 3B, 3C)**: Former candidates removed from consideration — 3A (taxon extinct), 3B (taxon does not meet the ESA's definition of a "species"), 3C (taxon found to be more abundant/widespread than previously believed, or not facing significant threats).
- **Listing Priority Number system**: Derived from a matrix crossing **threat magnitude** (Imminent vs. Non-imminent) with **taxonomic distinctiveness** (Monotypic genus > Species > Subspecies), producing priority ranks used to sequence Category 1 rulemaking.
- **Status Trend categories**: Improving, Stable, Declining, Possibly Extinct, Unknown, Not Applicable.
- **Scope of taxa covered**: The table spans vertebrates (fishes prominently) and invertebrates (mollusks, crustaceans, insects), reflecting Region 2's exceptionally high concentration of endemic, isolated desert aquatic species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Region 2 (the Southwest) contains some of the most range-restricted freshwater fish and invertebrate faunas in North America, largely because of:

- **Isolated spring and desert stream habitats**: Many candidate fishes (e.g., pupfish, chubs, topminnows) persist in single springs, spring-fed pools, or short stream reaches, making them acutely vulnerable to habitat loss.
- **Habitat modification pressures** (Factor A of ESA Section 4 criteria explicitly referenced in the form): groundwater pumping, stream dewatering, channelization, and impoundment are recurring, implicit threats to taxa such as [[Gila Chub]], [[Desert Pupfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]-type catostomids.
- **Invasive species and competition**: Although not detailed in the legible OCR text, the taxa listed (topminnows, pupfish) are classically imperiled regionally by nonnative predatory and competing fishes (e.g., mosquitofish, sport fishes).
- **Co-occurring invertebrate endemism**: The presence of numerous candidate **amphipods, isopods, and freshwater mussels (Unionidae)** alongside fishes underscores that these desert spring/river systems function as biodiversity refugia for entire aquatic communities, not fish alone.
- **Multi-state jurisdictional distribution**: Species ranges cross state lines (AZ, NM, TX, OK, CO, UT, NV, CA), reflecting the transboundary nature of desert river basins such as the [[Colorado River Basin]], [[Rio Grande]], and [[Gila River]] systems.

## Key Findings

- **Document function**: Serves as the official 1994 tracking mechanism for Region 2's ESA candidate species list, feeding into the national Animal Notice of Review process.
- **Taxonomic breadth identified in the table** (as recoverable from OCR):
  - **Fishes** — Family-level groups strongly represented include:
    - [[Catostomidae]] (suckers) — genera resembling *Chasmistes*, *Xyrauchen*, and related sucker taxa tied to Great Basin and Colorado River drainages.
    - [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows/chubs/shiners) — genera consistent with *Gila*, *Notropis*, *Moxostoma*, *Meda*, and *Plagopterus*-type desert cyprinids.
    - [[Cyprinodontidae]] (pupfish) — multiple *Cyprinodon* sp. entries tied to isolated desert spring systems (e.g., Death Valley/lower Colorado region pupfishes).
    - [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers) — entries consistent with *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* (Gila topminnow) and allied topminnow taxa.
    - [[Percidae]] (darters/logperches) — a smaller number of candidate entries, likely representing eastern Region 2 (Oklahoma/Texas) darter species.
  - **Freshwater mollusks** — numerous Unionidae (freshwater mussel) genera (e.g., forms resembling *Truncilla*, *Quadrula*, *Lampsilis*) concentrated in Texas/Oklahoma river systems.
  - **Crustaceans** — multiple Amphipoda and Isopoda entries (spring-dwelling amphipods), plus crayfish (Cambaridae/Decapoda), reflecting groundwater/spring endemism parallel to that seen in fishes.
  - **Insects** — candidate Orthoptera (grasshoppers/crickets), Plecoptera (stoneflies), and Lepidoptera (butterflies/moths), indicating the table's scope extended well beyond aquatic vertebrates.
- **Regulatory rigor embedded in the process**: Any reclassification of a species into or within Category 1 required **dual concurrence** (Regional Office + Washington Office), reflecting the heightened evidentiary standard for species nearing formal listing.
- **Data limitation**: Due to significant OCR degradation of the tabular data (columns for scientific name, common name, distribution, and status trend are fragmented across pages), individual species-level population estimates, precise ranges, and threat narratives could not be reliably reconstructed from this scan and would require consultation of the original 1994 Notice of Review or the source table for verification.
- **Historical significance**: This table is a direct administrative precursor to numerous species that were later formally listed under the ESA (e.g., several [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]] populations, and multiple Texas/Oklahoma freshwater mussels), making it a useful historical checkpoint for tracking the regulatory trajectory of Region 2's imperiled aquatic fauna.
