# Bait Culturists Guide (Ohio Division of Conservation Bulletin 137, 1937)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1937 Ohio Department of Agriculture, Division of Conservation bulletin, written by T. H. Langlois at the [[Franz Theodore Stone Laboratory]], is a practical guide to producing and handling live bait minnows and crayfish in small ponds. Demand for live bait had depleted wild stocks in streams around reservoirs, raising costs and harming [[Game Fish]] populations, which depend on forage minnows. The bulletin covers pond site selection, species-specific breeding habits, single- and multi-species pond production, holding-tank husbandry, disease prevention, crayfish culture and the [[Hellgrammite]] as a bait insect. Appended county tables list minnow catches reported by licensed dealers in 1931 and 1936.

## Background & Description

The bulletin responds to the widespread use of live bait by hook-and-line anglers, which created a commercial bait trade drawing almost entirely on streams in counties surrounding reservoirs. Langlois argues that rebuilding forage populations would improve both angling and bait sales. Protected areas and closed seasons were considered helpful, but length and bag limits were judged impractical because minnow species are hard to identify.

The bait species profiled are:

- [[Horned Dace]] (*Semotilus atromaculatus*)
- [[Black-Nosed Dace]] (*Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris*)
- [[Stone-Roller Minnow]] (*Campostoma anomalum*)
- [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*)
- [[Blunt-Nosed Minnow]] (*Hybopsis notatus*)
- [[Blackhead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*)
- [[Mud Minnow]] (*Umbra limi*)
- [[Crayfish]] (*Cambarus rusticus*), a shallow-burrowing species suited to pond culture

Males of most minnow species develop temporary breeding "pearl organs" (small white head tubercles), which aid identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Pond productivity depends on bottom soil: warm-water ponds with rich soil bottoms yield the most fish, while cold-water ponds with sterile bottoms do not. In Michigan experiments, barren ponds yielded 42,900 golden shiners per acre versus 235,000 in fertile ponds. Rich bottoms can be limed and poor ones manured. Sky ponds (rain-filled surface basins) suit heavy clay regions; creek valleys suit other sites.

Holding tanks are vulnerable to chlorine in city water (allowed to dissipate in a reserve tank for about three days), fungus in July and August, and post-spawning mortality in blackhead minnows. Crayfish are threatened by the [[Anchor-Worm]], a crustacean parasite on Lake Erie carp; only frozen carp is recommended as feed.

Environmental settings noted include spring-fed versus cold ponds and warm southern versus cooler northern Ohio, which affect growth rates and spawning timing.

## Key Findings

- **Multi-species culture:** Combining golden shiners with blunt-nosed minnows gave an average of 185,000 minnows per acre at the Ohio State fish farms in 1936.
- **Single-species yields (Ohio State fish farms):** Redbelly dace, 68,000 per acre; golden shiners, 118,495 per acre; blunt-nosed minnows, 473,350 per acre (710,026 fish from 1.5 acres). Blunt-nosed minnow was judged the best pond species, reaching about 1¼ inches by fall (the text states "14 inches," likely an OCR error for 1¼ inch).
- **Breeder density:** One adult per two square feet of pond surface area is recommended.
- **Golden shiners:** 1935 yielded 39,430 two-inch fish per acre from 36 two-year-old breeders; 1936 yielded 118,495 per acre from 8,000 one-year-old breeders.
- **Holding-tank practice:** Tanks should be shallow, cleaned at least twice weekly, fed cereals or bread crumbs, and disinfected with potassium permanganate for fungus. Minnows are dipped in mercurochrome (two tablespoons per gallon of water) to prevent fungus or tailrot. A fair allowance is one cubic foot of water per fifty minnows.
- **Crayfish growth:** Average length rose from 6 mm (May 15) to 60 mm (October 30) in central Ohio. Crayfish are best sold at two to three inches. Females of 102 mm carried 574 eggs. Draining ponds in late October yields mostly males, and draining in late August or early September can eliminate crayfish.
- **Crayfish production records:** Nine Ohio ponds totaling 32 acres produced 809,747 crayfish (average 25,308 per acre-unit, 358.5 lb per acre), reported as secondary crops in bass-rearing ponds.
- **Hellgrammite:** A larval Dobson fly, with a complete life cycle of three years and two years and eleven months spent in water. Adults emerge in June and July, and females lay 2,000 to 3,000 eggs.
- **Dealer catches (1931 and 1936):** Appendix tables list licensed dealer minnow catches by county. Statewide totals were about 5.4 million minnows in 1931 and about 5.9 million in 1936, with peak catches in April through June.
