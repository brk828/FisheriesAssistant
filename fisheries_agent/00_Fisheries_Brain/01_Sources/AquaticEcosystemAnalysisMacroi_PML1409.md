# Aquatic Ecosystem Analysis for Selected Streams on the Tonto National Forest: Christopher Creek Macroinvertebrate Progress Report (1987)

#macroinvertebrates #freshwater-ecology #benthos #bioassessment

## Executive Summary

This USDA Forest Service Intermountain Region progress report, prepared by the [[Aquatic Ecosystem Analysis Laboratory]] at [[Brigham Young University]] (Provo, Utah) and dated December 28, 1987, assesses benthic macroinvertebrate communities in [[Christopher Creek]] on the [[Tonto National Forest]], Arizona. The report is based on six macroinvertebrate samples, together with water chemistry and physical habitat data, collected at a single station below [[Christopher Creek Campground]] in July and November. The community was dominated by sediment-tolerant taxa with signs of organic enrichment. Both [[Biotic Condition Index]] (BCI) values (67 and 65) indicate severe stress. The stream's potential for a resident fishery is judged fair to poor, and the authors identify opportunities to improve instream habitat, riparian habitat, and possibly water quality.

## Background & Description

The report describes why macroinvertebrates are used as monitors of land management. Aquatic invertebrates respond to their total environment, so their community structure shifts when water quality declines or habitat changes. Analysis is based on several linked measures:

- **Macroinvertebrate community data:** dry-weight biomass (standing crop, g/m²), number of taxa, taxa composition, [[Diversity Index]] (DAT), habitat and feeding preferences (for example, [[Shredders]]), and the BCI, which expresses as a percentage how close an ecosystem is to its potential.
- **Physical parameter data:** habitat characteristics such as instream substrate, velocity, depth, and canopy.
- **Water chemistry data:** including total [[Alkalinity]] (mg/L) and sulfate (mg/L).

The authors argue that at least three samples per station are needed to represent the community accurately. Random samples taken from similar rubble substrate, accounting for flow velocity and depth, also allow statistical analysis. Among sampling devices, the [[Winget-Modified Surber Net]] was found to give the highest coefficient of correlation (similarity among samples). Sampling and analysis follow [[FSH R-4 2609.23 Fisheries Habitat Surveys Handbook]] (March 1985).

The cover letter, from the Fisheries and Wildlife office of the Intermountain Region, transmits four copies of the report to the forest supervisor. It notes that the data give "excellent indicators of water quality" as it relates to aquatic biota, and that the results can help assess the effects of land use activities on stream ecosystems. Contacts were listed as Fred Hangum (Aquatic Ecologist and laboratory coordinator) and MI Duff (Aquatic Ecologist, Forest Service office).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site:** The station is located below the Christopher Creek Campground and was chosen to monitor the effects of multiple management activities in the drainage. The stream lies in Arizona, within the Tonto National Forest.

**Stressors identified in the drainage:**
- Grazing, which has apparently weakened the ecosystem and caused impacts that would be slow to repair.
- Intense recreation use.
- Land development and road development.
- A barrow pit within the drainage.

The authors describe these as having had severe impacts on the ecosystem.

**Water chemistry:** Alkalinity was 130 mg/L, which the authors describe as near the optimum concentration for productivity.

**Riparian vegetation:** The number of shredders collected in the November sample indicated some deciduous plants along the streambank. The shredder count in July suggested riparian habitat in fair to poor condition.

**Assessment scales cited:** The report gives reference categories for the DAT and BCI, but the scale table is partly garbled in the scan. Based on the readable portions, BCI values above 90 are excellent, 80 to 90 are good, 72 to 79 are fair, and below 72 are poor. Standing crop categories are also given, with the lowest category at 0.0 to 0.5 g/m².

## Key Findings

**Sampling design and sites**
- One station on Christopher Creek, below the campground.
- Six macroinvertebrate samples in total, with associated water and habitat data.
- Sampling dates were 11-07-86 (November 1986) and 7-06-87 (July 1987).

**Macroinvertebrate community**
- The community was dominated by taxa tolerant of sedimentation, with some evidence of organic enrichment.
- Clean-water and moderately tolerant species were present but extremely limited in numbers.
- The Diversity Index (DAT) values were fairly good, at 13.9 and 12.6 in the table.
- BCI values were 67 (November) and 65 (July), indicating severe stress.
- The benthic community was made up almost entirely of tolerant species, indicating stress.

**Biomass and productivity**
- Standing crop was low for a stream with 130 mg/L alkalinity. The narrative reports 0.5 and 0.2 g/m², while the table gives 0.5 and 0.7 g/m². The source is inconsistent, and the discrepancy should be checked against the original data.
- The authors state that a healthy stream with this alkalinity should have a dry-weight biomass of about 4 g/m² (the scanned value is partly illegible).
- Biomass of 0.5 to 0.7 g/m² would limit the number and size of fish the stream could support.

**Fishery potential**
- Potential for a resident fishery was judged fair to poor.
- Dominance of sediment-tolerant taxa and scarcity of clean-water taxa suggest there is very little, if any, suitable spawning substrate.

**Management recommendations**
- Opportunities exist to improve instream habitat quality, riparian habitat quality, and possibly water quality.
- Management should address the sedimentation linked to grazing, recreation, road and land development, and the barrow pit.
