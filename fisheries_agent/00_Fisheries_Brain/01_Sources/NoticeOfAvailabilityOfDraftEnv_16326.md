# Childs Irving Hydroelectric Project Draft Environmental Assessment (FERC Project No. 2069-003)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Draft Environmental Assessment (DEA), jointly prepared by the [[Federal Energy Regulatory Commission]] (FERC) and the [[Coconino National Forest]] (USDA Forest Service), evaluates the relicense application of Arizona Public Service Company (Public Service) for the [[Childs Irving Hydroelectric Project]] on [[Fossil Creek]] in central Arizona. The project diverts water from about 14 miles of stream and occupies 326.8 acres in the Coconino National Forest and 17.2 acres in the Tonto National Forest. Staff concludes that relicensing with environmental enhancements would not be a major federal action significantly affecting the quality of the human environment.

The staff's preferred alternative adopts Public Service's proposed minimum flows of 10 cfs in the Irving bypassed reach and 5 cfs in the Childs bypassed reach, with monitoring of streamflow, travertine, fish, and riparian vegetation, a recreation management plan, an erosion and sediment control plan, a visual resource management plan, stream gaging, and a cultural resources Programmatic Agreement. Staff does not recommend the proposed [[Stehr Lake]] enhancements.

## Background & Description

**Project setting.** The application was filed December 18, 1992. The project is located 7 miles west of Strawberry on Fossil Creek and consists of two developments:

- **Irving Development:** a 25-foot diversion structure diverting up to 43 cfs, a 16,578-foot flume, a 3,278-foot penstock, and a powerhouse with one 1,600 kW unit. Under existing conditions 0.2 cfs flows in the 4-mile Irving bypassed reach.
- **Childs Development:** a 5-foot diversion, a 23,190-foot conduit discharging to [[Stehr Lake]] (23-acre lake formed by 12-foot and 20-foot dams), a 6,281-foot pressure tunnel, a 4,800-foot penstock, and a powerhouse with three units totaling 5,400 kW. Public Service releases 2 cfs in the 10-mile Childs bypassed reach.

The current and proposed generating capacity is 7 MW, operated in run-of-river mode.

**Power need.** Public Service projected an average annual peak load growth of 1.9 percent to 2011. The entire project output serves local loads. WSCC projected about 9,120 MW of generation additions regionally through 2005, including 567 MW in the Arizona-New Mexico power area.

**Process.** The analysis is a cooperative effort under a Memorandum of Understanding dated September 18, 1991. Pre-filing and scoping comments came from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]], the [[Arizona Riparian Council]], [[American Rivers]], the Department of the Interior, and others.

**Water quality certification.** The Arizona Department of Environmental Quality issued a [[Section 401 Water Quality Certification]] on December 14, 1993, after the 1-year period had lapsed. Conditions include maintaining Irving flows at current levels or reduced by at most 2 cfs, a constructed wetland at Stehr Lake, and annual management plans due by October 31.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Watershed.** The Verde River flows 190 miles and drains about 6,188 square miles. Fossil Creek originates on the Mogollon Rim, flows about 17 miles southwest, and enters the Verde River five miles below Childs. Elevations range from 7,260 feet to 2,550 feet.

**Hydrology.** Perennial flow begins at [[Fossil Springs]], which provides a constant baseflow of about 43 cfs. Staff estimates 77 percent of annual flow above Fossil Springs dam comes from springs and 23 percent from runoff. The 2-year peak flow is 510 cfs, more than ten times the full 43 cfs baseflow.

**Travertine.** Spring water is saturated with calcium carbonate and dissolved carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide release and rising pH cause [[Travertine]] precipitation, which forms dams, terraces, and stairstep pools. Historical accounts by Lummis (1891) and Chamberlain (1904) describe large travertine dams and pools.

**Fish community.** Of 31 native freshwater fish species in Arizona, 25 are identified by Game and Fish as "wildlife of special concern." Five native species persist in Fossil Creek:

- [[Speckled Dace]]: abundant only at Fossil Springs, uncommon between the dam and Irving.
- [[Roundtail Chub]]: highest age and size diversity at Fossil Springs and the 5-mile reach below Irving; uncommon in the Irving bypassed reach.
- [[Sonora Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]]: variable abundance, present throughout the stream.
- [[Longfin Dace]]: rare and limited in extent.

[[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] no longer occur in Fossil Creek. Three nonnative fishes are present: green sunfish, smallmouth bass, and yellow bullhead. Gila topminnow stocked in 1967 and 1969 did not survive.

**Riparian and terrestrial.** The canyon supports 205.0 acres of riparian vegetation from Fossil Springs to the Verde River (117.6 acres mixed broadleaf forest, 78.8 acres mixed broadleaf shrub-scrub, 8.7 acres palustrine forest). Cobble/boulder substrates show 88.1 percent of woody vegetation as seedlings. Sensitive species include [[Common Black-Hawk]], [[Belted Kingfisher]], [[Southwestern River Otter]], [[Lowland Leopard Frog]], and the [[Fossil Springsnail]].

**Threatened and endangered species.** Species noted by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] include [[Bald Eagle]] (threatened), [[American Peregrine Falcon]] (endangered), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (endangered), [[Razorback Sucker]] (endangered), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (experimental nonessential population in Salt and Verde drainages), and [[Arizona Agave]] (endangered). Staff found the project would not be likely to adversely affect the willow flycatcher or its critical habitat, provided a recreation management plan and maintenance plan are required.

**Grazing and recreation.** Livestock grazing from about 1875 to the mid-1920s reduced ground cover. Current grazing has declined, and Fossil Springs received up to 150 people on one weekend in summer 1993. Recreation zones include the Fossil Creek Wilderness (10,433 acres), Fossil Springs (6 acres), Stehr Lake, Childs, and the Verde Wild and Scenic River.

**Cultural resources.** The Childs Irving Hydroelectric Facilities were listed on the [[National Register of Historic Places]] (August 9, 1991), and designated a National Historical Mechanical Engineering Landmark in 1976. The survey identified 54 sites, including 48 eligible archeological and historic sites.

## Key Findings

**Alternatives analyzed**

- Staff's preferred alternative: Public Service's flows (10 cfs Irving / 5 cfs Childs), monitoring, recreation management plan, erosion and sediment control plan, visual resource management plan, stream gaging, and cultural PA. Stehr Lake enhancements are not included.
- Flow Alternative #1: 10 cfs / 10 cfs.
- Flow Alternative #2: 15 cfs / 5 cfs.
- Flow Alternative #3: 20-25 cfs / 20-25 cfs.
- Decommission #1 (Irving): 43 cfs Irving / 5 cfs Childs, Irving flume removed.
- Decommission #2 (entire project): 43 cfs both reaches.
- No-action: existing license; 0.2 cfs / 2 cfs.

**Habitat (IFIM and wetted perimeter)**

- The three IFIM-evaluated species (roundtail chub, Sonora sucker, desert sucker) are primarily pool dwelling and pool feeding. Roundtail chub and Sonora sucker WUA is maximized at flows below 10 cfs in Irving and decreases with flow.
- At 10 cfs Irving, WUA decreases 38% for adult roundtail chub, 62% for adult Sonora sucker, and 61% for desert sucker fry, while adult and juvenile desert sucker WUA increases 180%.
- At 10 cfs Irving, the average wetted perimeter is about 92% of that at 16 cfs. At 5 cfs Childs it is about 79% of that at 16 cfs.
- At 43 cfs Irving, adult roundtail chub and Sonora sucker WUA is 68% and 87% less than existing flow.
- The wetted perimeter analysis shows the most rapid gains up to 16 cfs.

**Staff conclusions**

- The best habitat balance for native species is 10 cfs Irving and 5 cfs Childs.
- Public Service's proposal benefits flow-dependent resources most among alternatives considered. Its generation drops from 36.52 GWh to 29.87 GWh per year.
- Flow Alternatives #1, #2, and #3 are not preferred due to reduced pool habitat and added economic losses.
- Decommissioning Irving and decommissioning the entire project are not recommended because effects on travertine, native fish, and riparian vegetation cannot be predicted with assurance and because of adverse cultural and developmental impacts.

**Travertine**

- Public Service's modeling predicted more than 1,375 tons per year at 43 cfs, about 0.29 inch per year over the reach. At 2 cfs, about 0.02 inch per year.
- A one-month full-flow release in March 1996 accumulated travertine to depths of 1 foot in the Irving reach.
- [[Game and Fish]] is concerned travertine could cement the substrate, restrict fish movement, and reduce spawning gravel.

**Monitoring and gaging**

- Travertine and aquatic monitoring: about $9,500 initially and $2,500 annually for five years (30-year net annual cost about $2,700).
- Riparian monitoring: about $4,500 initially and $2,500 in years one, three, and five (30-year net annual cost about $1,400).
- Two stream gages (one per bypassed reach): about $2,000 with $1,000 annual O&M (30-year net annual cost about $1,300).

**Stehr Lake**

- Public Service's proposal (about $800,000; 30-year net annual cost about $135,300) would dredge the lake from about 3 acres to 14 acres of open water and create a 1-acre island, boulder/riprap areas, and recreational facilities.
- Staff does not recommend the proposal because the cost is not justified by the expected benefits.
- Dredging would remove about 9 acres of cattails and 1 acre of woody riparian vegetation. Staff recommends that any modification proceed only with a Habitat Evaluation Procedure study to ensure no net loss of habitat value.

**Recreation**

- Staff recommends a recreation management plan for Fossil Springs, Fossil Creek, Stehr Lake, and the Childs areas, due within one year of license issuance (estimated cost $40,000; 30-year net annual cost $6,800).
- Staff recommends delaying flow releases until the plan is approved.
- Fossil Springs received 155 visitors on one summer Saturday (per a recent wilderness ranger report); visitation rose more than 400% in 1988 after an Arizona Highways article.

**Economics (30-year analysis, 11.85% discount rate)**

- Public Service's proposal: annual cost $1,053,500 (35.3 mills/kWh), power value $1,350,400 (45.2 mills/kWh), net annual benefit $296,900 (9.9 mills/kWh).
- Staff's preferred alternative: annual cost $960,900 (32.2 mills/kWh), power value $1,350,400, net annual benefit $389,500 (13.0 mills/kWh).
- Decommission #1: total cost $1,255,700 annually.
- Decommission #2: total cost $2,201,400 annually.
- Gross investment: net investment cost of $2,028,000; O&M $568,900 annually.

**Air quality (replacement generation)**

- Replacing 1 GWh with coal generation would emit about 8.18 tons of sulfur oxides, 3.78 tons of nitrogen oxides, and 965.2 tons of carbon dioxide.
- Replacing 1 GWh with gas generation would emit about 0.003 ton of sulfur oxides, 2.13 tons of nitrogen oxides, and 614 tons of carbon dioxide.

**Cumulative effects**

- Fossil Creek watershed is the geographic scope for fish and riparian vegetation, with a 30-year temporal scope.
- Higher flows would have positive cumulative effects on aquatic resources at 10 cfs Irving and 5 cfs Childs. Higher flows may limit pool and riffle habitat, creating negative cumulative effects.

**Conclusion**

- The Finding of No Significant Impact states that issuing a new license with the recommended measures would not be a major federal action significantly affecting the quality of the human environment.
