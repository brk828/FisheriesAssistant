# Composición y Distribución de Peces Continentales en el Sur de Quintana Roo, Península de Yucatán

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae #poeciliidae #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This study by Schmitter-Soto and Gamboa-Pérez (1996, *Revista de Biología Tropical* 44(1): 199–212) presents a preliminary inventory and distribution analysis of continental fishes in southern [[Quintana Roo]], drawing on 69 sampled localities in the [[Río Hondo]] basin, published records and the [[Zoology Museum at CIQRO]] collection. The state inventory now includes 85 species, of which 55 occur in the study area. Twelve species are reported for the first time in inland waters of Quintana Roo. Cluster analyses delineate four local ichthyofaunas that follow a gradient of distance to the sea, temperature and salinity. The authors conclude that fish distribution is mainly ecological, while the decline of neotropical and primary fishes from Belize to [[Sian Ka'an Biosphere Reserve]] reflects historical, biogeographic factors at a slightly larger scale.

## Background & Description

The southeastern [[Yucatan Peninsula]] was poorly explored ichthyologically despite offering freshwater, brackish and transitional habitats, including cenotes, coastal lagoons, wetlands, interior lakes and the Río Hondo. The study area lies entirely within the Río Hondo basin and is a transitional zone where karstic terrain begins. Its objectives were to provide a preliminary list of southern fishes, analyse their distribution within the study area, and compare them with provisional lists from [[Belize]] (Thomerson and Greenfield 1972) and Sian Ka'an (Navarro-Mendoza 1988; Gamboa-Pérez 1992; Ramo and Busto 1992). Earlier work was sparse: Castro-Aguirre (1978) noted peripheral species from Bacalar, and Navarro-Mendoza (1988) worked mainly in northern Sian Ka'an.

**Study area and zones.** The area was divided a priori into 12 zones:
- **ZOH** – Zohlaguna Plateau (up to ~250 m a.s.l.), within [[Calakmul Biosphere Reserve]]; 17 lentic samples and one stream.
- **NBR** – Nicolás Bravo, permanent freshwater lagoons.
- **MOR** – Morocoy, wetlands, temporary pools and the Aguadulce creek.
- **BAC** – [[Bacalar Lagoon]] (near sea level; salinity about 3 ppm, Xul-Há creek freshwater).
- **SNF** – San Felipe, eutrophic freshwater lagoons.
- **NOH** – Noh-Bec, mesotrophic lagoons.
- **BAN / BAS** – Northwest and Southeast Chetumal Bay, brackish and freshwater wetlands, mangroves and cenotes.
- **RHA / RHB** – Upper and Lower Río Hondo.
- **CAN / CAS** – Northern and Southern Caribbean coastal lagoons; the north exchanges water with the sea mainly subterraneously, whereas the south has deep permanent channels.

**Sampling.** Collections totalled 232 samples from 117 localities, reduced to 69 by merging geographically close or hydrologically connected sites. About 21% of localities were lotic and 79% lentic; 35% were sampled in both wet and dry seasons. Gear included seines (2 and 20 m), throw nets (1 and 3 m), hooks, spears, hand nets and traps, with mesh sizes from 2 to 20 mm. Visual censuses used snorkelling or polarized glasses, and local fishers' catches were consulted. Water parameters measured included temperature, salinity, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, pH, Secchi transparency, depth and GPS position.

**Regional setting.** Rainfall ranges from about 1000 to 1500 mm per year, increasing north to south and inland to coast. Mean annual temperature is 26 °C (range 21–28 °C). Water is hard because of the calcareous substrate. Permanent lagoons expand by up to 150% in area between dry and rainy seasons. Native vegetation is mostly semi-evergreen medium forest, now largely replaced by secondary growth, pasture and crops, with mangroves along coastal lagoons.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Environmental gradients.** Mean temperature was significantly different among zones: W 25.6 °C, NE 27.8 °C, RH 29.3 °C, CAS 30.7 °C. Conductivity rose from W 449 µmho/cm through RH 1664 and NE 3467 to CAS 13618 µmho/cm. Dissolved oxygen was lower in NE (4.50 mg/l) than in RH (6.30) and CAS (6.50). pH in RH (6.63) differed from other zones (about 7.52). Transparency was greater in CAS (1.80 m) than elsewhere (0.87 m). Lotic environments had lower oxygen (4.96 mg/l) than lentic (6.26 mg/l).

**Marine influence.** The Caribbean coast carries a Mesoamerican barrier reef shared with Belize. Currents flowing north from the South Caribbean are partly deflected by headlands and bays, creating southward counter-currents. Marine fishes enter inland waters through lagoons, wetlands and the Río Hondo, occupying niches left vacant by the scarcity of freshwater forms in Central America (about 500 freshwater species, of which 146 are marine invaders; Nelson 1984).

**Biogeography.** The Usumacinta Province has very few primary fishes (Characoidei and Siluriformes). All species present belong to the "Old Southern Element" of Bussing (1985), which arrived with the South American invasion in the Paleocene. Most cichlid and poeciliid endemism in Central America is comparatively recent and limited, unlike the African lake radiations.

## Key Findings

- **Inventory:** 85 continental species are known from Quintana Roo; 55 (in 40 genera and 22 families) occur in the study area. The species accumulation curve did not reach an asymptote, so true richness is probably underestimated. Most small localities, however, were poor, with only one to three ubiquitous species.
- **New state records (12):** *Anchoa colonensis*, *A. parva*, *Rhamdia laticauda*, *Atherinella* sp., *Strongylura timucu*, *Diapterus auratus*, *Eugerres plumieri*, *Stegastes leucostictus*, *Rivulus tenuis*, *Phallichthys fairweatheri*, *Xiphophorus helleri* and *Stephanolepis hispidus*. Many are juvenile marine fishes captured in the Southern Caribbean zone.
- **Species groups:** Ubiquitous (for example *Gambusia yucatana*, *Astyanax fasciatus*, *Poecilia mexicana*, *Cichlasoma meeki*); freshwater (primary species such as *Rhamdia guatemalensis*, *R. laticauda*, *Hyphessobrycon compressus*); and coastal/brackish (peripheral species such as *Arius assimilis*, *Anchoa* spp., *Gobiomorus dormitor*, many Gerreidae and Lutjanidae). Freshwater species were absent from the Southern Caribbean zone, and brackish species from the Western zone.
- **Four ichthyofaunas:** (1) Western / Zohlaguna Plateau, with the exclusive *X. helleri* and *Rhamdia guatemalensis*; (2) Río Hondo and riparian waters, with the least distinctive fauna and only one exclusive species, *Ophisternon aenigmaticum*; (3) Center-Northeast, rich in peripheral species; (4) Southern Caribbean coastal lagoons, significantly richest (mean richness higher than other zones, F = 8.79, p < 0.05), with no primary fishes and many marine species.
- **Drivers:** Distance to sea, temperature and salinity explained the zone gradient W > RH > NE > CAS. Historical factors dominate at larger scales: the Río Hondo acts as a dispersal corridor for ubiquitous fishes, and the decline of primary and neotropical species from Belize to Sian Ka'an reflects dispersal limits and salinity tolerance (for example, *H. compressus* is absent from Sian Ka'an and northern Yucatán because of low salinity tolerance, while *R. laticauda* is absent there for ecological reasons, lacking freshwater lotic habitat).
- **Regional similarity:** The local fauna shared more species with Sian Ka'an (Jaccard 0.55) than with Belize (0.52). The Belizean list and the southern Quintana Roo–Sian Ka'an list were only 0.13 similar.
- **Taxonomic issues:** Several earlier records were removed as misidentifications, including *Cichlasoma aureum*, *C. melanurum*, *Ariopsis felis*, *Elacatinus picta*, *Synbranchus marmoratus* and *Poecilia sphenops*. The *Thorichthys*-like cichlid from Bacalar may be an undescribed species, and the *Atherinella* species may also be new to science, which would make southern Quintana Roo a notable area of endemism.
- **Management relevance:** The authors note the potential for aquaculture of native fishes, and the paper is linked to a companion study on aquaculture potential (Schmitter-Soto and Gamboa-Pérez, in press).
