# Age and Growth of the Apache Lake Walleye

#ichthyology #marine-biology #stizostedion

## Executive Summary

This student report estimates the [[Age Determination|age]] and growth history of [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*) in [[Apache Lake]], Arizona, using the [[Scale Method]]. Twenty fish were sampled in March 1976. Scale-derived back-calculations show that females grew faster than males in every year of life, that males reached sexual maturity in their second year and females in their third, and that first-year growth declined between the 1972 introduction cohorts and 1976. The author concludes that the sample is too small for firm conclusions and recommends at least 30–40 fish for future studies.

## Background & Description

**Study species.** The Walleye is a percid whose scales are [[Ctenoid Scales]]: the exposed posterior field bears spines (ctenii), while the embedded anterior margin carries the concentric growth lines used for aging.

**Scale structure.** The anterior margin consists of [[Circuli]], concentric growth lines of hyalodentine. Seasonal growth produces closely spaced circuli (slow growth) and widely spaced circuli (fast growth), forming light and dark bands. A complete [[Annulus]] (year mark) is identified by:
- one or more discontinuous circuli sandwiched between continuous ones;
- a change in circuli spacing, which is tight just before the annulus and wider just after;
- vacant circular openings on the radii, beginning at the annulus line.

Scales with an unusually large focus were judged to be regenerated and discarded.

**Justification.** The method was supported by two observations from [[Priegel 1969]]: young-of-the-year fish had no annuli, and annulus count increased with fish size.

**Field methods.** Fish were collected on 17–18 March 1976, mostly with [[Trap Net]] and the remainder with an experimental [[Gill Net]]. Scales were taken above the lateral line and anterior to the dorsal fin on the left side.

**Lab methods.** Scales were cleaned in 20% KOH and mounted. Six scales per fish were dry temporary mounts; one scale per difficult fish was permanently mounted in cellulose acetate using a roller press after [[Smith 1954]]. Measurements were made with a micro-projector.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Population context.** [[Walleye Introduction]] to Apache Lake occurred in 1972. The author interprets the 1972 cohort's first-year growth as unusually high, and suggests that later declines reflect a more restricted population limited by food and space. The author notes that the population may not have reached equilibrium.

**Sex-linked growth.** Growth differed between sexes. The author attributes part of the female advantage in later years to [[Energy Allocation to Reproduction]] in males, which is not paid by females until their third year.

**Caveats.** The author notes that a single male in age group IV appeared growth-inhibited, and that single-fish age groups distort the curves.

## Key Findings

**Sample and body-scale relationship**
- Sample: 20 Walleyes, with 5 scales measured per fish for back-calculation.
- Body–scale regression was linear. Three functions (linear, exponential, power) were compared, and the [[Anterior Scale Radius]] correlated best with standard length.

**Age composition (Table 4)**
- Males (n = 8): age I = 1, II = 2, III = 4, IV = 1.
- Females (n = 12): age I = 1, II = 1, III = 8, IV = 2.
- Maturity: males matured in year 2; females in year 3. The reported percentage mature was 100% for all age groups III and IV.

**Growth patterns**
- The greatest growth for both sexes occurred in year 1.
- Growth slowed in years 2–4, most markedly in males. The author links the year-4 male value to the single fish in that group.
- Females were longer than males at every age and the gap widened with age.

**Trends**
- First-year growth declined across cohorts from 1972 to 1976 (Figures 6–7). Age group II for females was the one exception, attributed to small sample size.
- The author concludes that growth trends in Apache Lake Walleye "seem to be on the decline."

**Recommendations**
- Use at least 30–40 fish for future age and growth studies with scales.

**Summary of conclusions**
1. Age and growth were estimated from 20 Walleyes using scale analysis.
2. Anterior scale measurements best predicted body length.
3. Males matured in year 2; females in year 3.
4. Females held a size advantage, and first-year growth decreased between 1972 and 1976.
