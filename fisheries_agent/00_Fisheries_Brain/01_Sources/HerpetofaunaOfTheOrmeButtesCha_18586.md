# Herpetofauna of the Orme, Buttes, Charleston, and Hooker Reservoir Sites, Arizona-New Mexico

#herpetology #amphibians #reptiles #environmental-impact #reservoir-assessment

## Executive Summary

This 1972 report by W.L. Minckley documents the herpetofauna (reptiles and amphibians) of four proposed reservoir sites in Arizona and New Mexico: [[Orme Reservoir]], [[Buttes Reservoir]], [[Charleston Reservoir]], and [[Hooker Reservoir]]. A total of 87 species (102 named forms including subspecies) occur across all four sites combined. The assessment predicts that impoundment will decimate and displace most species from flooded areas, with only two introduced turtle species ([[Common Snapping Turtle]] and [[Texas Softshell]]) expected to benefit. No officially listed rare or endangered species were identified, though some peripheral species may be affected at Charleston. The report relies on literature records, museum specimens, field observations, and ecological inference to compile species lists for each site.

## Background & Description

The report addresses amphibians and reptiles at four reservoir sites in the American Southwest. The [[Orme Reservoir]] site, located near Arizona State University, received the most intensive study and has 53 listed species. [[Buttes Reservoir]] has 41 species, [[Charleston Reservoir]] 66 species, and [[Hooker Reservoir]] 62 species. The Charleston site shows recent vegetational change and receives faunal influence from Mexican highlands and Great Plains elements. The Hooker site exhibits complexity due to proximity to high-elevation areas, allowing invasion by mesic-adapted and montane species alongside desert and grassland forms.

Species occurrence is categorized as definite, probable, or hypothetical based on: published literature records, specimens in Arizona State University collections, personal field observations, and ecological associations with vegetation, topography, and climate. Taxonomy follows Stebbins (1966) with subsequent modifications. The herpetofauna includes representatives from multiple families: [[Ambystomatidae]] (mole salamanders), [[Pelobatidae]] (spadefoot toads), [[Bufonidae]] (true toads), [[Hylidae]] (treefrogs), [[Ranidae]] (true frogs), various turtle families, [[Iguanidae]] (iguanids), [[Scincidae]] (skinks), [[Teiidae]] (whiptail lizards), and numerous snake families including [[Colubridae]] and [[Viperidae]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The four reservoir sites span diverse ecological zones from Sonoran Desert to montane habitats. Flowing streams allow mesic or higher-elevation species to occur far below typical altitudinal ranges, exemplified by hypothetical presence of [[Southern Plateau Lizard]], [[Arizona Alligator Lizard]], and [[Arizona Black Rattlesnake]] at Orme far below their normal Upper Sonoran or Transition zone habitats. Conversely, open well-drained terraces permit desert and grassland species to penetrate higher than normal elevations, including burrowing snakes, [[Spadefoot Toads]], whiptail lizards, and scaly lizards ([[Sceloporus]]).

Steep-walled canyons at sites like Buttes introduce habitat heterogeneity, with southerly slopes supporting desert species while north-facing walls enhance mesic-adapted forms. The Orme site reflects southwestern desert influence with 11.9% unique species (including 3 introduced forms). Charleston shows 11.3% unique species, mostly Mexican or grassland forms. Hooker exhibits 16.1% unique species, demonstrating proximity to montane and grassland faunas. Faunal similarity between sites decreases with geographic distance: Orme-Buttes share 59.3% of species, while Orme-Hooker share only 45.6%.

Ecological relationships show 29 species associated with riparian/aquatic habitats, 23 with desert environments, 15 with grasslands, 11 with rocky/cliff habitats, and smaller numbers with montane, arboreal, and fossorial niches. Many species occupy multiple habitat types.

## Key Findings

**Species Diversity and Distribution:**
- 87 species total across all four sites (102 named forms including subspecies)
- [[Orme Reservoir]]: 53 species; [[Buttes Reservoir]]: 41 species; [[Charleston Reservoir]]: 66 species; [[Hooker Reservoir]]: 62 species
- Only two species (both introduced turtles) expected to benefit from impoundment
- No officially listed rare or endangered species identified, though peripheral species may occur at Charleston

**Predicted Impoundment Effects:**
- Immediate displacement of animals upslope or upstream into already-occupied habitats
- Brief adjustment period involving elimination of displaced individuals
- Complete loss of riparian, terrace, and cliff-side habitats within flood pools
- Post-impoundment fauna dependent on habitats above flood-pool margin
- Potential enhancement of certain riparian species ([[True Frogs]], [[Garter Snakes]]) only if stable water levels maintained and lakeside riparian vegetation develops
- Access roads will cause direct mortality from vehicular traffic but may create beneficial roadcut habitats for rock-dwelling lizards
- Recreation areas will significantly alter or obliterate herpetofauna through human predation, soil compaction, and habitat modification

**Taxonomic and Research Challenges:**
- Literature widely scattered and voluminous; few current compendia available
- Many taxonomic problems persist, particularly in whiptail lizards ([[Cnemidophorus]])
- Biological information ranges from extensive to nearly absent for different species
- Some species rarely surface (worm snakes), are secretive (alligator lizards, skinks), rare (hook-nosed snakes), or appear only sporadically
- Years of on-site study required for complete assessment; certain species will evade detection
- Confidence in species lists varies by site, highest for Orme due to proximity to Arizona State University
