# Fish Distribution in the Gila River Basin, American Southwest

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This is an update to the first annual report of a joint project between [[American Rivers]] and [[Arizona State University]] on the distribution of fishes in the [[Gila River Basin]] of the American Southwest. The report covers 1 April 1994 through 30 June 1995 and was prepared by [[W. L. Minckley]] of the ASU Department of Zoology. The project is supported by the Fish & Wildlife Foundation, the [[US Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[US Forest Service]].

The first project year was devoted to compiling historical fish records into a computer-compatible, GIS-based database, acquiring hardware and software, and building base maps. The bulk of the input work used museum specimen records from the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ) and ASU. The document is an interim progress report, and its section on projections for the coming year is listed in the contents but is not present in the source text.

## Background & Description

The project aims to map where native fishes of the Gila River basin occur, using museum specimens and published records. The basin spans Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico. Records were gathered from several sources:

- **Level I records:** museum specimen data. UMMZ and ASU holdings from the basin are the largest in existence and make up more than 75% of all available Level I records. Other specimens are housed at institutions including the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia, the California Academy of Sciences, Cornell University, the Field Museum of Natural History (Chicago), the Los Angeles County Museum, the Museum of Comparative Zoology (Harvard University), the University of Arizona, UCLA, the University of New Mexico, and the Texas Memorial Museum.
- **Level II records:** secondary records from peer-reviewed publications, from the 1840s onward. More than 1,000 specimen/locality records from these sources had been entered by the reporting date.
- **Mexican data:** reworked for mapping and entered into the system.

Historical museum records from the early twentieth century are hard to evaluate because reliable maps were lacking. The team therefore confirmed the travel routes of early collectors so that localities could be plotted accurately.

Each specimen record in the database stores catalog number, genus, species, subspecies (if applicable), number of specimens collected, collectors, locality, and comments. Records can be mapped individually or together, and queries may be based on any attribute, such as collector, date, genus, or river.

### Data Processing and Tools

- Museum data from UMMZ were stored on a dedicated Hewlett-Packard storage and computing system running [[Arc/Info]] software.
- ASU records, originally in an incompatible locally programmed format, were converted to ASCII files and transferred to the standardized HP format in early April 1994. Entry of these records was under way.
- Mapping and database queries were handled in ArcView 2.
- Hydrologic base maps for Arizona and New Mexico had been procured. Background layers include county boundaries, towns, major transportation routes, and perennial rivers.
- A Mexican hydrologic base map was to be provided at no cost by its Mexican source and was expected at ASU that month.

### Personnel and Participation

Jana Fry served as computer operator and data specialist. Three summer months of work were contributed at no cost by high school students and teachers through the ASU Office of Research Affairs. The report notes that this participation benefits the students' education and may influence their later career choices.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Gila basin fish checklist covers the indigenous fishes of the basin, broken down by sub-basin. The table lists roughly 20 taxa, including subspecies and an unidentified *Cyprinodon*, against sub-basin codes and status markers. The sub-basin codes include the Agua Fria, Salt, Verde, San Pedro, San Carlos, and Santa Cruz basins, among others. Status markers include "E" (endangered) and "P" and "X" entries, which the OCR does not fully resolve. Entries marked "E" include [[Gila elegans]] and [[Gila robusta]].

Several species have a restricted or threatened range:

- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): In the US, its natural distribution is restricted to the Gila and [[Bill Williams River]]. Its southern populations hold candidate 2 status with the US Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): Endemic to the Gila River basin and listed as threatened by the US Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*): In the US, its natural distribution is restricted to the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]. Its southern populations hold candidate 2 status. A northwestern subspecies in Nevada (*P. c. intermedius*) is listed as threatened.

The Arizona distribution maps show collection sites with museum specimens and the state's perennial streams, stream segments, and natural lakes.

## Key Findings

- **Data coverage:** UMMZ and ASU museum holdings are the largest source of Level I records for the basin, comprising more than 75% of available records.
- **Database status:** Museum records were converted to the standardized HP/Arc-Info format. ASU record entry was under way, and more than 1,000 Level II specimen/locality records had been entered.
- **Geographic scope:** Records cover Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico. Mexican data were reworked and entered into the system.
- **Checklist:** A checklist of indigenous Gila basin fishes was compiled with sub-basin breakdowns. It lists roughly 20 taxa and their status markers.
- **Conservation status:** Longfin dace, loach minnow, and desert sucker are highlighted for range restrictions and conservation listings, including candidate 2 status and threatened status.
- **Base maps:** Hydrologic base maps for Arizona and New Mexico are in use, and a Mexican base map was expected to be available at no cost.
- **Community involvement:** High school students and teachers contributed three months of unpaid summer work through the ASU Office of Research Affairs.
