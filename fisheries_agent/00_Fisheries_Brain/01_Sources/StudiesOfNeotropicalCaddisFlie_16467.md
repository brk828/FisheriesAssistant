# Studies of Neotropical Caddis Flies XI: The Genus Rhyacopsyche in Central America

#trichoptera #hydroptilidae #rhyacopsyche

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Oliver S. Flint Jr.]] (Smithsonian Institution) revises the poorly known hydroptilid caddisfly genus [[Rhyacopsyche]], originally named by [[Fritz Müller]] from Brazil. The author argues that [[Rhyacopsyche Mexicana]] (originally described as [[Metrichia]] mexicana) is congeneric with [[Rhyacopsyche Hageni]], providing a line of evidence that links larva, case and adult. The paper gives a generic diagnosis and describes three new Central American species, [[Rhyacopsyche Obliqua]], [[Rhyacopsyche Torulosa]] and [[Rhyacopsyche Turrialbae]], and divides the Central American species into two groups. The paper is a taxonomic revision rather than a population or ecosystem study. Its ecological content is limited to one field observation of larvae and pupae at a boulder cascade in Guatemala.

## Background & Description

- **Historical background:** Müller named the genus in 1879a without species or figures. He later described the larval cases of *R. hageni* in 1879b, 1880 and 1881, but gave no description of larvae or adults. [[Thienemann]] (1905) gave the first description of larval and adult morphology. [[Ulmer]] (1913) tentatively assigned Paraguayan material to the species, but this material was apparently a species of [[Neotrichia]].
- **Basis for synonymy:** Flint (1967) described *Metrichia mexicana* from Mexico. Later he found larvae, pupae and metamorphotypes from [[Finca Moca]], Guatemala. The pupal cases matched Müller's figures for *R. hageni*, and the larval tarsi and claw matched those shown by Müller and Thienemann. The author therefore concluded that the species belongs in the same genus.
- **Generic diagnosis (adults):** Ocelli 3. Spurs 1,3,4. Male ninth segment produced into dorsolateral lobes. Female eighth tergum bears a setate lobe. Claspers paired and distinct.
- **Generic diagnosis (larvae):** Legs short and similar. Tarsus with one (or possibly two) enlarged, platelike ventral seta. Claw angled ventrad and inflated basally.
- **Generic diagnosis (cases):** Elongate and basically tubular. Pupal case attached by a short silken stalk to a silken holdfast.
- **Separation from related genera:** Adults are separated from [[Metrichia]] and [[Ochrotrichia]] by genitalic structure. Larvae are separated from [[Ochrotrichia]] and [[Hydroptila]] by the platelike tarsal seta, the hook claw, and the lack of anterolateral expansion on the metanotum. Cases of [[Ochrotrichia]] and [[Hydroptila]] are compressed and purse-like, whereas *Rhyacopsyche* cases are distinctive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat of immatures (*R. mexicana*):** The immature stages were collected only once. The [[Rio Bravo]] above Finca Moca is a few yards wide, with an average depth of about a foot. The bottom is mostly inorganic, ranging from small sand grains to large boulders. The author infers from the general rarity of caddisfly larvae that the river is subject to frequent scouring floods.
- **Microhabitat:** Cases were attached to a large boulder in a cascade. Water poured between two boulders at shoulder height, so most of the inner and under surfaces stayed out of the water. These surfaces were exposed to splash and to a film of seeping water. Pupae were attached by their holdfasts to this moist but not submerged zone and projected at right angles from the boulder.
- **Attachment behaviour:** Of two larval cases, one had a little silk at its anterior end and was apparently being attached for pupation. The other had no silk. The author could not determine whether the species lacks the long silken attachment thread seen in *R. hageni*, or whether the specimen had cut its thread and not yet begun pupal attachment.
- **Geographic and altitudinal records:** Material came from Mexico, Guatemala and Costa Rica. One Escuintla locality (Río Metapa) is recorded at 275 m elevation.

## Key Findings

- **Synonymy:** *Metrichia mexicana* Flint is transferred to *Rhyacopsyche* as *R. mexicana* (Flint), new combination. The author considers it congeneric with *R. hageni* Müller, based on correlations among larva, case and adult.
- **Three new species** are described from the mexicana group and the turrialbae group:
  - *R. obliqua* n. sp. (holotype USNM Type 71116, Veracruz, Mexico)
  - *R. torulosa* n. sp. (holotype USNM Type 71115, Río Metapa, Guatemala)
  - *R. turrialbae* n. sp. (holotype USNM Type 71117, Costa Rica)
- **Species groups:**
  - *mexicana* group: *R. mexicana*, *R. obliqua*, *R. torulosa*. Elongate claspers, ribbonlike subgenital sclerites, simple female eighth sternum.
  - *turrialbae* group: *R. turrialbae* only. Dorsoventrally developed claspers, large heavily sclerotized subgenital plate, modified female eighth sternum.
- **Key morphological separators:**
  - *R. mexicana*: evenly rounded ninth tergal lobes; tongue-shaped, lightly sclerotized female ventral lobe.
  - *R. torulosa*: small apicomesal lobe on the ninth tergal lobes; heavily sclerotized, narrowed female ventral lobe.
  - *R. obliqua*: obliquely truncate or pointed ninth tergal lobes; female has a pair of setate lobes from the eighth tergum.
- **Body size (adult forewing length):** *R. mexicana* 2.5 mm; *R. torulosa* 3 mm; *R. obliqua* 2.5 mm; *R. turrialbae* 3–3.5 mm.
- **Immature size (*R. mexicana*):** Larvae 3–3.5 mm long. Larval case 4.5 mm long and 1 mm wide, silken, fusiform, covered in small sand grains. Pupal case 5–6 mm long and 1 mm wide, with a silken stalk 0.5–1 mm long.
- **Material examined (as recorded; OCR counts are approximate):**
  - *R. mexicana*: holotype male plus paratypes from Veracruz (Río Tacolapan, 25–26 July 1966; Cuitlahuac, 10–12 Aug 1964), Guatemala (Finca Moca, 12 June 1966: 2 larvae, 4 prepupae, 19 pupae, metamorphotypes and 20 pupal cases), and Escuintla (Río Metapa, 5–6 March 1970, E. J. Fee, one male).
  - *R. torulosa*: holotype male and 16 male and 22 female paratypes, Río Metapa, 5–6 March 1970.
  - *R. obliqua*: holotype male from Fortín de las Flores, Veracruz, 17 May 1964 (Blanton et al.), with paratypes from the same data.
  - *R. turrialbae*: holotype male from Costa Rica, 19 June 1967 (Flint & Ortiz), with paratypes from the same date and from 3 miles west of Turrialba, 18–21 June 1967.
- **Taxonomic status of *R. hageni*:** The author states that the lack of specimens prevents placing *R. hageni* in either species group.
