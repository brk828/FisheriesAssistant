# Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement: FY1997 Annual Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This draft annual assessment reports progress in fiscal year 1997 (FY1997) under the [[Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement]] (VSCA) for the [[Virgin Spinedace]], a small cyprinid of the [[Virgin River Basin]]. The agreement has two objectives: reducing or eliminating significant threats, and enhancing or stabilizing occupied and unoccupied historic habitat. Progress was organized under the three threats identified by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) under Section 4(a)(1) of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The report concludes that all actions are on track and consistent with the VSCA. Main activities included maintaining population maintenance flows, reintroducing fish to [[Beaver Dam Wash]], eradicating [[Red Shiner]] from the mainstem Virgin River, monitoring populations at 12 standard sites, and securing new fishing regulations. Reintroduced fish showed no apparent recruitment by the end of the year.

## Background & Description

The Virgin spinedace is the focal species of a multi-agency conservation effort. The document does not give a morphological description or distribution data beyond the sampling reaches and sites it names.

The VSCA rests on four general administrative actions:
- Coordination of conservation activities
- Implementation of conservation schedules
- Funding of conservation actions
- Assessment of conservation progress

Quarterly reports and annual assessments form the assessment process. Many actions began in FY1996 and were expected to continue into FY1998.

Participating organizations include:
- [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR) and the [[Utah Department of Natural Resources]]
- [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]] (NDOW)
- FWS
- [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM)
- [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] (WCWCD)
- [[Utah State University]] (USU)
- Environmental representatives

These partners are developing the [[Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program]] (Program), which in FY1997 focused on drafting its Strategy, mitigation components, and an Implementation Agreement. The Strategy and Implementation Agreement were expected to be finalized in early FY1998.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Threats addressed under the VSCA:**
1. **Habitat destruction, modification, or curtailment** of habitat or range, including water diversions and depletions, stream alterations, and off-highway vehicle (OHV) use.
2. **Predation, competition, and disease**, mainly from non-indigenous fishes such as red shiner, green sunfish, and rainbow trout.
3. **Other natural or manmade factors** affecting continued existence. No actions were taken under this threat in any quarter.

**Flow management:**
- The [[Quail Creek Diversion]] reach receives 3 cfs of instream flow maintained by WCWCD, described as a [[Population Maintenance Flow]].
- No new diversions or depletions occurred in any quarter, and existing natural and reestablished flows were maintained.
- Sampling in January and February, and again in March, collected spinedace in this reach for the first time after flows were re-established.

**Habitat and regulatory review:**
- The Division of Water Resources and UDWR reviewed approximately 24 stream alteration applications. Where potential negative impacts were found, protocols were changed to reduce them.
- The FWS reviewed 23 stream alteration applications received in 1997, and also commented on or consulted on projects including the Utah National Recreational Trail Fund Projects and the [[Questar Mountain Fuel Supply Pipeline]] crossing of the Virgin River.

**Co-occurring species:** The report names [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Chub]], flannelmouth suckers, and desert suckers as native fishes that were salvaged and relocated during the [[Ft. Pearce Wash]] treatment.

## Key Findings

**1. Reintroduction to Beaver Dam Wash**
- In February 1997, NDOW and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] collected spinedace from Mormon Well, Arizona, and released 274 fish into a small tributary arm of Beaver Dam Wash.
- Per the quarterly report, the release was in two clusters of 30 fish in the upper fishless reach, two clusters of 30 between waterfall barriers, and five clusters in the lower reach below the barriers.
- NDOW sampled in September and observed many adults, but found no apparent reproduction or recruitment.
- A second reintroduction in December used fish collected by UDWR near [[Lytle Ranch]]. The summary reports 402 fish released. Attachment D reports 408 collected, with 50 released at eight locations along the Beaver Dam Creek tributary. The two figures differ and the report does not reconcile them.

**2. Red shiner eradication**
- In December 1997, the mainstem Virgin River was chemically treated from the Washington Fields Diversion to the Johnson Diversion, including Ft. Pearce Wash, beaver ponds, and side channels.
- Two detoxification stations were established below the Johnson Diversion.
- The Ft. Pearce Wash treatment covered 4.5 miles and required 29 personnel over 3 days, with salvage of native fish the day before.
- Nearly all red shiners were expected to be eradicated, with a follow-up effort planned for FY1998.
- Survey effort: UDWR conducted two surveys in June, about six in the third quarter, and two in the fourth quarter.

**3. Population monitoring**
- The annual monitoring by UDWR and USU was conducted in September and October at 12 standard sites using electrofishing, consistent with FY1995 and FY1996.
- "Numerous" spinedace were sampled, but the document gives no counts.
- NDOW's September monitoring of the reestablished Beaver Dam Wash population recorded numerous adults and no recruitment.

**4. Habitat protection and enhancement**
- The BLM completed the Santa Clara River Reserve Cooperative Agreement with the City of Santa Clara, restricting camping and OHV use to protect riparian and aquatic habitat.
- The BLM also completed the Virgin Falls Park Management Agreement and built a fence to keep OHVs out of riparian habitat.
- In September, BLM bought fencing materials for an exclosure on the Santa Clara River below Gunlock Reservoir.
- BLM habitat monitoring ran in June and July on the Santa Clara River, West Fork of Beaver Dam Wash, and segments of the mainstem Virgin River.

**5. Fish stocking and regulation**
- The Nevada Wildlife Board accepted NDOW's proposal to restrict sport fishing on Beaver Dam Wash and [[Schroeder Reservoir]] to artificial lures, effective March 1998, to prevent introduction of undesirable aquatic species.
- Protocols for non-indigenous fish introductions were maintained.
- The [[Ft. Pearce Wash Fish Barrier]] was monitored for stability in every quarter. Modifications were made in January 1997.

**6. Negotiations and water supply**
- WCWCD was still negotiating with the Shivwitz Indian tribe over construction of the Santa Clara Pipeline during the first quarter.

**7. Assessment and 1998 outlook**
- The report judges all actions to be on track and consistent with the VSCA.
- Many actions are expected to take multiple years and will be carried out through the Program, consistent with the [[Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan]] and the Virgin River Management Plan.
- Planned 1998 actions include additional red shiner treatments, annual spinedace monitoring, response monitoring of the 3 cfs flow, reestablishment and monitoring of spinedace on Beaver Dam Wash, selective removal of [[Rainbow Trout]] on lower Beaver Dam Wash, control of [[Green Sunfish]] by UDWR, and continued maintenance of population flows and habitat.

**Document quality note:** The draft contains several internal inconsistencies. Attachment A names the source of the first release as "Mormon Wash" while the summary says "Mormon Well." Attachment A also refers to "Beaver Dam Creek" while the summary says "Beaver Dam Wash." The December fish count differs between sections (402 vs. 408), and Attachment A dates the sampling to March, while the summary gives January and February.
