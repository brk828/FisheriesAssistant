# Section 7 Compliance Letter: Cottonwood Artesian Cienega Improvement Project

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #freshwater-conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1991 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Section 7 compliance letter concurring with a Tonto National Forest biological evaluation regarding the **Cottonwood Artesian Cienega Improvement Project**. The project, located in Connor Wash on the Tonto Basin Ranger District, was designed to restore and protect cienega (desert spring/marsh) habitat for the endangered **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*). FWS concluded the project would have a net beneficial effect on the species and its habitat, contingent on mitigation measures to protect individual fish during construction and habitat modification activities. The letter emphasizes habitat engineering, salvage/relocation of fish, permitting compliance, and long-term monitoring recommendations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: [[Cienega]] (desert wetland/spring ecosystem) — a rare and imperiled aquatic habitat type in the arid Southwestern United States.
- **Specific site features**:
  - Artesian well/standpipe discharge supplying water to the system.
  - Existing concrete drinker (livestock water trough) used as refuge habitat for salvaged fish.
  - Stocktank (existing, with cattail removal planned).
  - Newly constructed shallow cienega pools (2–3) and connecting channels intended to expand shallow, low-flow habitat characteristic of natural cienegas.
- **Threats/pressures**:
  - Historic and ongoing livestock grazing degrading riparian/aquatic vegetation and water quality.
  - Emergent vegetation encroachment (e.g., cattails) reducing open water habitat.
  - Construction activity posing direct mortality risk to resident fish.
- **Habitat management interventions**:
  - Exclosure fencing to eliminate general livestock grazing from the stocktank and cienega pools.
  - Potential prescribed (controlled) livestock grazing reintroduced strategically for emergent vegetation control, requiring FWS notification protocols.
  - Pipe rail fence to protect the concrete drinker structure.
  - Fish salvage operations to relocate Gila topminnow from construction zones to a protected concrete drinker before groundbreaking.
- **Regulatory context**: Activities involving capture, handling, and incidental mortality of the endangered species were authorized under an existing FWS **[[Endangered Species Subpermit]]** held by Jerome A. Stefferud (Tonto National Forest).

## Key Findings
- FWS **concurred** with the Tonto National Forest's determination that the Cottonwood Artesian Cienega Improvement Project would have a **beneficial effect** on the Gila topminnow, based on document review and an on-site visit (December 5, 1990).
- The project constitutes an active **recovery effort** for a federally endangered species, combining habitat restoration (new cienega pools, channel construction) with protective infrastructure (fencing, exclosures).
- **Key FWS recommendations** for project implementation:
  1. Quantify well discharge (gallons per minute) prior to project initiation to inform water allocation decisions.
  2. Require an on-site fisheries biologist with Gila topminnow habitat expertise during excavation of cienega pools/channels, with authority over final habitat design (size/shape).
  3. Any future prescribed livestock grazing within the exclosure (for vegetation control) requires prior FWS telephone notification and subsequent written reporting (numbers of livestock, grazing dates).
  4. Long-term project monitoring should include before/after photography, established long-term photo points, and assessment of effects on riparian/aquatic habitat and topminnow populations, with monitoring data shared with FWS.
- The **subpermit holder must be physically present** during all handling and taking (including incidental mortality) of Gila topminnow during the project.
- This case illustrates a collaborative interagency model between the **[[Tonto National Forest]]** and **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]** for **[[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7** consultation on small-scale habitat restoration projects benefiting a federally listed fish species in desert spring ecosystems.
