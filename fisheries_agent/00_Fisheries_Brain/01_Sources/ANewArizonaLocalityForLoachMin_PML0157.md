## Background & Description

*Tiaroga cobitis* is a small [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] endemic to the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona and New Mexico, USA, and Sonora, Mexico. Historic populations have declined because of habitat loss and interactions with [[Nonnative Fishes]]. The species was federally listed as threatened in 1986.

The [[Biogeography]] abstract describes northwestern Mexico as a connecting link between continental freshwater ichthyofaunas of the Western Hemisphere. The region lies at the transition between the [[Nearctic Region]] to the north and the [[Neotropical Region]] to the south. The abstract states that three levels of specimen records were used:

- **Level I:** museum specimens
- **Level II:** peer-reviewed literature
- **Level III:** "gray" literature

It poses two questions, the first asking whether within-region distributions are more a function of ecologic or geologic factors. The second question is cut off in the source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The loach minnow abstract places the species in a fragmented landscape. Its historic range has contracted under habitat loss and competition or predation from nonnative fishes. The newly documented [[Black River]] reach lies within the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]], where the discovery was made during routine fish collections. The closest known population within the same watershed is more than 200 stream km (130 mi) downstream in the [[White River]], so the Black River site is isolated from the nearest known population.

The biogeography abstract frames regional fish distributions within a broader pattern of transition between Nearctic and Neotropical faunas, linking local distributions to ecological and geological history.

## Key Findings

**1. New locality for loach minnow in the Black River, Arizona**
- Loach minnow were collected throughout a 3.6 km (2.25 mi) reach of the Black River, near [[Three Forks]], Arizona.
- All size (age) classes were present, including mature individuals of both sexes.
- The authors conclude this presumably represents a reproducing population.
- The authors are aware of no other occurrences of loach minnow in the Black River system.
- The closest population within the watershed is more than 200 stream km (130 mi) downstream in the White River.

**2. Biogeographic framework for northwestern Mexico**
- The region is a transition zone between Nearctic and Neotropical freshwater faunas.
