# Age and Growth of Atherinops affinis oregonia and Other Baysmelt Subspecies Along the Pacific Coast of the United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinopsidae

## Executive Summary

This 1933 study by [[Leonard P. Schultz]] (University of Washington Publications in Biology, Vol. 2, No. 3) examines the age, growth, sexual maturity and life history of baysmelt (*Atherinops*) collected between Coos Bay, Oregon, and San Diego Bay, California. Age was estimated primarily from length frequencies, supported by [[Scale Annuli]] counts and [[Scale Analysis]]. The author concludes that scale-based ageing is reliable for the northern subspecies *A. affinis oregonia* but unreliable for the southern subspecies *A. affinis littoralis*, whose first annulus is often absent. Baysmelt grow faster and reach larger sizes in the north, and spawn later in life there.

## Background & Description

- **Study scope:** 11,260 specimens measured for length; 1,208 weighed (*A. a. oregonia*); 5,035 scale slides prepared.
- **Taxonomy:** Following [[Hubbs]] (1917, 1918) and [[Jordan and Hubbs]] (1919), the genus includes three species and four subspecies with largely exclusive ranges from Mexico to northern Oregon. *A. a. oregonia* ranges from Oregon to Humboldt Bay; *A. a. affinis* from San Francisco to Monterey; *A. a. littoralis* from Monterey south to San Diego Bay, intergrading with *A. a. affinis* between Monterey and Santa Barbara.
- **Related forms** (not analysed in detail): *A. insularum cedroscensis*, *A. insularum insularum*, *A. affinis guadalupe*, *A. affinis magdalenae* and *A. regis* (Gulf of California).
- **Collection methods:** Gill nets, circle nets, round haul nets and beach seines. Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin and stored in 70% alcohol.
- **Measurement:** Total length from snout tip to base of caudal fin, measured with dividers and rule.
- **Sexing:** Gonads examined by gross inspection; a compound microscope was used for specimens under about three inches.
- **Scale method:** One scale per fish from a standardized position, plus two to four random scales, mounted in glycerine-waterglass and projected at 65x magnification. About 5% of mounts were discarded for irregularities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Seasonal movements and spawning (*A. a. oregonia*, Coos Bay):**
- Schools form in March–October, near the bay entrance in March–April.
- Pre-spawning fish move into sloughs and mud flats with eelgrass in May.
- Spawning occurs from late May to early July, with eggs attached to eelgrass under about four feet of water at mean low tide.
- Fish return to the open bay in August–September. The author notes they are absent from commercial catches October–February, possibly because they enter the ocean, though this may reflect fishing methods.

**Regional contrast:** Baysmelt in southern California are caught in bays in every month of the year. The northern form's habits therefore differ.

**Spawning grounds** are similar across localities: shallow sloughs and mud flats with eelgrass.

**Sex ratio:** Females predominate throughout:
- *A. a. oregonia*: 58.9% female, 41.1% male
- *A. a. affinis*: 76.4% / 23.6%
- *A. a. littoralis*, Anaheim Bay: 51.7% / 48.3%
- *A. a. littoralis*, San Diego Bay: 79.8% / 20.2%
- *A. i. cedroscensis*: 62.8% / 37.2%

## Key Findings

**1. Age groups and annulus formation (*A. a. oregonia*)**
- Age group 0 was not represented in the collections.
- The first annulus forms soon after 26 March in age group I, which is about the second year of life.
- The second annulus forms in late spring, May–June, in age group II (third year of life).
- The third annulus forms later in the year (after 8 July, possibly September) in age group III. The author states that ages beyond the third year cannot be determined with certainty.

**2. Growth increments (*A. a. oregonia*)**
- First year: about 100 mm
- Between first and second annuli: about 86 mm
- Between second and third: about 32 mm
- Between third and fourth: about 15 mm

**3. Maximum sizes and ages**
- Largest specimen: a 299 mm female (March 26, 1930), with five annuli.
- Largest male: 272 mm, with five annuli (July 8, 1929).
- Six annuli were found on a 249 mm female, and the oldest fish were estimated at seven or eight years.

**4. Growth compensation**
- Correlation coefficients (r) were: first vs. second year, −0.759 ± 0.016; second vs. third year, −0.582 ± 0.040; first vs. third year, +0.418 ± 0.050.
- The author concludes that significant [[Growth Compensation]] occurs over the first three years.

**5. Sexual maturity**
- *A. a. oregonia*: about 5% of fish mature in their second year, the rest in their third. Spawning occurs May–early July.
- *A. a. affinis* and *A. a. littoralis*: at least two-thirds mature in their second year.
- *A. a. littoralis*: spawning March–June, possibly later.
- Northern baysmelt spawn at an older age than southern ones.

**6. Scale readability**
- *A. a. oregonia* annuli are clear and distinct, making scales suitable for ageing.
- *A. a. littoralis*: only 72% of adult scales showed clearly defined annuli, and the first annulus formed on only 85% of scales. The author concludes scales are unreliable for this subspecies.
- Among ageing errors, the study identifies two overlapping age groups with the same annulus count, and annuli appearing progressively later in older fish.

**7. Growth comparison among subspecies**
- Mean length at the end of the first year: *A. a. littoralis* about 65 mm; *A. i. cedroscensis* about 60 mm; *A. a. affinis* about 70 mm; *A. a. oregonia* (Humboldt Bay) about 87 mm; *A. a. oregonia* (Coos Bay) about 100 mm.
- The author concludes that growth is slower in southern California, and the Coos Bay form reaches an average length nearly twice that of the Anaheim Bay form.

**8. Length–weight relationship (*A. a. oregonia*)**
- Formula W = F·L^x, with F = 0.0000992 and x = 2.59, for specimens over 135 mm.
- Young fish are slender, while adults gain weight rapidly relative to length.
- Mean female F values rose from 0.0000960 (March) to 0.0001097 (7 May), then fell during spawning (0.0000980 on 21 June), which the author interprets as gain and loss of weight from gonad maturation and egg release.

**9. Conclusions**
- Scale methods suit *A. a. oregonia* in Oregon but not *A. a. littoralis* in southern California.
- Baysmelt show a marked northward increase in size and growth rate.
- Females grow larger than males at the same age.
