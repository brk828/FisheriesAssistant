# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants: 1982 Republication of the U.S. Lists (50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1982 republication by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (with the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] for marine species) of the federal lists of wildlife and plants determined to be Endangered or Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, codified at 50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12. The lists are regulatory in name and status but the accompanying data (historic range, footnotes, synonyms) are informational. The republication makes no substantive changes to listings; it consolidates prior Federal Register rules and updates nomenclature. The bulk of the document is the species tables themselves, covering mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, snails, clams, insects, crustaceans, and plants.

## Background & Description

The document is a regulatory compilation, not a research study. Key structural features of the lists:

- **§ 17.11 (wildlife):** Lists species with columns for common name, scientific name, historic range, vertebrate population where Endangered or Threatened, status (E = Endangered, T = Threatened, E or T (S/A) = similarity of appearance), listing footnote, special rules, and critical habitat.
- **§ 17.12 (plants):** Same structure for plant taxa, using scientific names only as the regulatory identifier.
- **Regulatory logic:** Common names are not reliable for identifying specimens; scientific names govern. Listing a genus (for example, *Hylobates* gibbons in China, India and Southeast Asia) applies to all lower taxa. Where a population is listed separately (for example, the bald eagle in five U.S. states), only those populations carry protection.
- **Notation:** "NA" in the Special Rules or Critical Habitat columns means no special rules or critical habitat apply, though other parts of 50 CFR 17 may still apply.
- **Authority:** Pub. L. 93-205, 87 Stat. 884; Pub. L. 94-359; Pub. L. 95-632; Pub. L. 97-304, as cited in the rule.
- **Effective date:** July 27, 1983 for the rule text, with the republication itself dated 1982.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The lists document a broad range of threatened taxa across biomes. Representative examples include:

- **Mammals:** Asian and African elephants, black rhinoceros, Sumatran rhinoceros, Asiatic lion, snow leopard, giant panda-adjacent Asian species, and several marine mammals such as the [[Humpback Whale]], [[Right Whale]], and [[Hawaiian Monk Seal]] (Monachus schauinslandi). Many Asian and African ungulates, primates (gibbons, langurs, lemurs, tamarins, howler monkeys) and bats are listed.
- **Birds:** Numerous island endemics (Hawaiian honeycreepers, Seychelles and Mascarene species, Pacific ground-doves and rails) and raptors such as the [[Bald Eagle]] and Philippine eagle.
- **Reptiles and amphibians:** Sea turtles (including the [[Hawksbill Sea Turtle]], [[Leatherback Sea Turtle]], [[Green Sea Turtle]], Kemp's ridley and olive ridley), crocodilians (including the [[American Alligator]] and Chinese alligator), Komodo monitor, and several island and salamander species.
- **Fishes:** Freshwater and desert taxa dominate, including the [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], Devils Hole pupfish, Tecopa pupfish, Colorado squawfish, Alabama cavefish, several darters and madtoms, and totoaba (*Cynoscion macdonaldi*) in the Gulf of California. Marine fishes are scarce in the list; the Gulf of California totoaba and Asian arapaima-type freshwater species are the notable examples.
- **Mollusks:** Many freshwater pearly mussels (Unionidae) in the southeastern and midwestern United States, and land snails including Oahu tree snails (*Achatinella* spp.) and Chittenango ovate amber snail.
- **Insects and crustaceans:** Butterflies (Lange's metalmark, El Segundo blue, Palos Verdes blue), the Delta green ground beetle, and a freshwater crustacean, *Thermosphaeroma* (*Sphaeromatidae*).
- **Plants:** Cacti (for example, Brady pincushion, Knowlton cactus), Santa Barbara Island liveforever, and other endemics across the U.S. Southwest and Pacific islands.

The historic-range column denotes general known distribution from the scientific literature, while the present distribution may be greatly reduced. This is a critical caveat for management and for interpreting absence from current ranges.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** The document is a republication of U.S. Endangered and Threatened lists under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. It contains no new listings or delistings beyond cumulative Federal Register amendments, which are referenced by footnote (for example, 35 FR 8495, June 2, 1970; 44 FR 61911 et seq.).
- **Scale:** The wildlife list (§ 17.11) comprises several hundred entries across mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, snails, clams, insects and crustaceans. The plant list (§ 17.12) lists cacti, orchids, mints and other vascular plants as a separate table.
- **Marine and aquatic entries:** Marine mammals (whales, monk seals, dugong, manatees), sea turtles, and the totoaba are listed. Freshwater fishes and mussels form a large share of aquatic entries, many restricted to single river basins or springs.
- **Regulatory logic:** Scientific names govern identification. Vertebrate populations may be listed separately by geographic boundary, and a genus-level listing covers all species within it.
- **Status codes:** E (Endangered), T (Threatened), and E or T (S/A) for species similar in appearance to listed species.
- **Administrative notes:** The Service intended annual updates to nomenclature and range data without public notice, a mechanism that reduces Federal Register costs.
- **Caveat:** The OCR text contains substantial scan artifacts; species names, ranges and footnote numbers may contain transcription errors and should be checked against the official 50 CFR text before use.
