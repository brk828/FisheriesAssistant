# Request for Razorback Sucker Tissues and Gametes for Genetic Analysis

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #genetic-diversity

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal request from Arizona State University's Department of Zoology to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service for tissue samples and gametes of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) from two facilities: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH) and [[Ouray Endangered Fish Culture Experiment Station]] (OES). The request supports genetic analysis via mtDNA studies and protein electrophoresis to assess genetic diversity in wild and hatchery populations. Materials are requested from fish captured from the [[Colorado River]] mainstream near [[Grand Junction]], [[Lake Powell]], and from hatchery-produced juveniles.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is an endangered catostomid fish native to the Colorado River basin. This request involves collaboration between Arizona State University (Dr. Hinckley, Professor of Zoology) and Dr. Donald Buth at University of California, Los Angeles. The study aims to characterize genetic diversity using mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis and protein electrophoresis across different source populations. The razorback sucker was noted as "yet-to-be-listed" at the time of this 1991 correspondence, indicating imminent endangered species listing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Source populations represent distinct ecological contexts:
- **Colorado River mainstream and backwaters near Grand Junction**: Wild-caught fish held at DNFH
- **Lake Powell**: Wild-caught fish held at OES
- **Hatchery production**: 1990 year-class juveniles produced at DNFH

The request emphasizes that fish from certain localities, especially the [[Colorado River]] near [[Grand Junction]], are in shortest supply, making tissue collection from these populations particularly critical for conservation genetics.

## Key Findings

**Sample Requests:**
- 7 gamete samples (5.0 ml each) from DNFH from [[Colorado River]] mainstream and backwater fish
- 50 juveniles from 1990 DNFH year-class: 10 for mtDNA analysis, 40 for protein electrophoresis (Dr. Buth)
- 15 gamete samples (5 ml each) from OES from [[Lake Powell]] fish
- Additional materials from 1991 field season as they become available

**Technical Requirements:**
- Frozen muscle plugs (0.5 grams wet weight, -80°C) for mtDNA analysis when gametes unavailable
- For mortality events: 1+ gram each of liver and/or gonad frozen immediately
- mtDNA purification from muscle is "far more difficult" than from liver or gametes
- 6-base restriction enzymes reliably assayed from muscle; 4-base enzymes produce "far more information relative to the levels of genetic diversity" but require liver/gonad tissue

**Coordination:**
- Specimens for mtDNA will be carefully labeled and carcasses sent to Dr. Buth for protein electrophoresis
- ASU cannot take responsibility for tissue harvest for Dr. Buth's project from live fish during gamete collection
- Request coordinated with U.S. Bureau of Reclamation mtDNA contract
- Endangered species subpermit to be obtained when required
