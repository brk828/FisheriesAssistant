# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes and Cover Memo (June 1983)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover memorandum and draft minutes from the [[Recovery Team]] for endangered Colorado River fishes. The team met in Las Vegas, Nevada, on June 21–23, 1983, at the offices of the Nevada Department of Wildlife. The memo is from Team Leader J. A. St. Amant to team members and asks for comments on the draft minutes by August 22, 1983.

The meeting reviewed and revised the final draft recovery plans for the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]]. It also covered the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) [[Upper Colorado River Conservation Plan]], the [[Lower Colorado River Conservation Plan]] and [[Lower Colorado River Subcommittee]] activities, the team's role, and proposed dams. Main issues were the status of the bonytail in the Upper Basin, the handling of consultant appointments, the [[Tusher Wash Hydropower Project]], and the need for an [[Environmental Impact Statement]].

## Background & Description

The Recovery Team is responsible for writing and revising recovery plans for four large river fishes of the Colorado River: the Colorado squawfish, humpback chub, bonytail chub and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Under team guidelines, the FWS regional director decides which consultants serve the team. The memo notes that the team can either ask the regional director to add consultants or appoint "special consultants," a power the team leader has already used for three individuals.

The memo raises a dispute over consultant numbers. The team had agreed on a list of 13 consultants. In recent correspondence, the regional director (Buterbaugh) proposed reducing this to three. The team leader's view is that the team did not recommend this cut.

Several new reports were cited during the meeting:
- "Fishes of the Upper Colorado River System: Present and Future" (Miller, Tyus and Carlson 1982)
- "Colorado Squawfish Investigations – White River, Utah" (Radant, Cranney,Ruesink and Rose 1983)
- "Colorado River Fishes Monitoring Project: First Annual Report" (Miller, Tyus and Kaeding 1983)
- "Windy Gap Fishes Study: First Annual Report" (Miller, Kaeding and Tyus 1983)
- "Movement, Migration and Habitat Preference of Radiotelemetered Colorado Squawfish" (Miller, Tyus and McAda 1983)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The meeting discussed how well the recovery plans reflect current science and where the fish live. Participants noted that new information is produced continuously, so the team agreed to add only information that is significant and that would change recovery efforts.

Bonytail status in the Upper Basin was a central concern. Tyus reported no "pure" bonytail collected in the Upper Basin, but at least one specimen was thought to show Gila elegans characteristics. The team questioned type specimens and whether young fish share the diagnostic traits of adults. FWS no longer lists the bonytail in the Upper Basin under Section 7 species lists, which suggests it may no longer exist in those reaches. The team concluded the plan should state that a viable Upper Basin population may not exist, but individuals may still be present. The species should still be recovered because suitable habitat may remain.

Dam and water-development proposals were also reviewed. The Bureau of Reclamation (USBR) reported no new water-development proposals, but several low-head hydropower projects were proposed, such as [[Tusher Wash Hydropower Project]]. These would not deplete river flow but could act as a serious barrier to fish passage. Tusher Wash could divert the entire flow during low-flow periods. The developers asked for an exemption from the environmental statement process. The FWS denied that request and will require an environmental statement to assess effects on endangered fishes.

## Key Findings

**Recovery plan revisions**
- **Bonytail Chub:** Plan revised with several clarifications. All references to the Upper Basin Conservation Plan were deleted, because that plan covers only the Upper Basin and the draft was not final. The team also recommended immediate collection of additional bonytail for [[Hatchery Brood Stock]].
- **Humpback Chub:** Minor changes made. Recovery recommendations kept. All references to the Conservation Plan were deleted again.
- **Colorado Squawfish:** Sections were moved within the plan (for example, economic importance). Narrative objectives were revised. Maps were upgraded to show critical habitat and the most recent distribution data. The plan was revised further pending the 1983 report results.
- The team agreed that the FWS regional director's June 16, 1983 letter would be answered with a formal response letter from the team.

**Consultant and procedural issues**
- The dispute over reducing the consultant list from 13 to three was unresolved. Options were to request all 13 (or some of them) from the regional director, or to appoint special consultants, as was done for Tom Lytle, Linda Ulmer and Dick Weisbrod.
- A motion by Tyus was adopted: team comments on the Upper Basin Conservation Plan would not be sent unless a formal request came from the regional director.
- Individual comments on the Upper Basin Conservation Plan were due to St. Amant by July 18, 1983. He would then decide whether a team meeting was needed. Tentative dates for that meeting were September 6 and 7.

**Tagging and research**
- Some tags meant for endangered fish were found on other species. Team members agreed to remind researchers under their jurisdiction to follow the agreed-upon [[Tagging Scheme]].
- The team also reported [[Radiotelemetry]] research on squawfish movement and habitat preference.

**Section 7 consultation and dams**
- The team suggested that the FWS reopen [[Section 7 Consultation]] on the [[Taylor Draw Project]] on the [[White River]]. Reasons: new information had emerged, and construction had been indefinitely postponed.
- The team agreed the step-down plan was adequate because it calls first for a determination of species status.

**Lower Basin activities**
- The Lower Basin Subcommittee will focus on the four large river fishes. Seventeen species were or will be proposed for listing on the Endangered Species List.
- Bonytail fry were stocked into backwaters of [[Lake Mohave]] to test whether those areas could be used for rearing.
- Monitoring of humpback chub in the [[Little Colorado River]] continues. Fifty subadults will be collected and moved to [[Page Springs]].
- The USBR captured razorbacks in the [[Dirty Devil River]] and is considering funding a tracking program.
- A Lower Basin Management Plan will be developed. It will use methods other than a formal conservation plan, such as state plans, and will address reintroduction of three of the endangered species.

**Budget and scheduling**
- About $3,000 remained in the team's 1983 budget. Because printing of the recovery plans is unlikely to happen this fiscal year, the team may meet again before the end of the year to review agency comments on the revised drafts.
- The Upper Colorado River Fishes planning meeting (held December 1982) will be held at least once a year. It gives a preliminary overview of the prior year's research and coordinates the current year's efforts.
- The meeting began at approximately 1:30 p.m. on June 21, 1983, and adjourned at approximately 10:00 a.m. on June 23, 1983.
