# The Nationwide Rivers Inventory: National System Components and Study Rivers (1981 Appendix)

#freshwater-ecology #riverine-habitat #river-conservation

## Executive Summary

This National Park Service appendix, dated January 1982, lists the rivers in the [[National Wild and Scenic Rivers System]] as of 1981 and the rivers designated for study under Section 5(a) of the [[Wild and Scenic Rivers Act]]. The document is an administrative inventory, not a research study. It gives, for each river, the counties, physiographic section, segment reach, length in miles, designation date, managing agency and agency contact. It contains no fish, invertebrate, water-chemistry or habitat measurements, and it does not describe any species.

## Background & Description

The appendix has four parts:

- **Section III, Existing Components:** rivers already designated into the National System. These span roughly 23 states, with Alaska, California and Idaho the best represented. Alaska alone accounts for roughly 25 segments.
- **Section IV, Section 5(a) Study Rivers:** rivers listed for possible future designation. They span about 29 states and territories, and the list includes both Alaskan and Southeastern segments.
- **Physiographic maps:** national and regional maps of [[Physiographic Provinces]] and sections, drawn from N. Fenneman (USGS 1946) and adapted by NPS regional offices (Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, Rocky Mountain, Northwest, Western).
- **Table layout:** each entry gives the river name, state, county, congressional district, physiographic section, segment reach description, length, designation date, managing agency and contact.

The text includes a note on Alaska: the state is organized by boroughs, has only one congressional district, and the management agencies had not yet delineated headwater boundaries, so mileages for those segments are only estimates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes rivers by their land and management setting, not by their biota. The relevant context it gives is:

- **Land management:** most components lie within or adjacent to [[National Park Service]] units (for example Gates of the Arctic, Yukon-Charley Rivers, Lake Clark, Kobuk Valley), [[US Forest Service]] lands (for example Tahoe, Plumas and Mark Twain National Forests), [[Bureau of Land Management]] districts, and [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] refuges (for example Yukon Delta, Arctic, Nowitna and Selawik refuges).
- **Physiographic setting:** segments are classed by province, such as Brooks Range (BR), Interior & Western (I&W), Pacific Mountain (PM) and Arctic Lowland (AL) in Alaska, and Blue Ridge, Piedmont, Ridge & Valley and Appalachian Plateaus farther east.
- **Wilderness and protected areas:** many segments run through designated wilderness areas, such as the Marble Mountain and Yolla Bolly Wildernesses in California and the Glacier Peak Wilderness in Washington.
- **Federal review:** for state-administered segments, [[Section 7 Determinations]] under the Act are made by the Director, National Park Service.

The rivers named for salmon, such as the [[Salmon River]] (Idaho and California) and the North and South Fork Salmon in California, are listed only by name and location. The document does not describe any fish populations, habitat condition or water quality for them.

## Key Findings

**Existing National System components (Section III), selected examples:**

- **Alaska (about 25 segments, designated 12/2/80 for most):** Alagnak (67 mi), Alatna, Andreafsky, Aniakchak, Beaver Creek, Birch Creek, Charley (251 mi), Chilikadrotna, [[Delta River]] (designation details listed), [[Forty-mile River]] (396 mi), [[Gulkana River]] (152 mi), Ivishak, John, [[Kobuk River]] (110 mi), [[Koyukuk River]] (102 mi), Mulchatna, [[Noatak River]] (330 mi), Nowitna (225 mi), Salmon, Selawik (160 mi), Sheenjek (160 mi), Tinayguk, Tlikakila, Unalakleet (66 mi) and Wind (140 mi).
- **California (designations mostly 1/19/81):** the Lower American and North Fork American; the Eel system (Eel, Middle Fork, South Fork and North Fork Eel); the Middle Fork Feather (93 mi, designated 10/2/68); the Van Duzen; the [[Klamath River]] system (Klamath, Salmon, North Fork and South Fork Salmon, Scott, Wooley Creek) including the Klamath main stem at 190 mi; the [[Smith River]] system (main stem and four forks plus a combined tributary entry of 217 mi); and the [[Trinity River]] system (Trinity main stem at 111 mi, North and South Fork Trinity, New River).
- **Other states:** the Chattooga River and West Fork (Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina); the Middle Fork Clearwater, Rapid River, Middle Fork Salmon, Salmon River and St. Joe River (Idaho); the [[Snake River]] (Idaho/Oregon, Hells Canyon, 67 mi); the Flathead, Missouri (Montana, 149 mi) and Pere Marquette (Michigan) rivers; the [[St. Croix River]] (Minnesota and Wisconsin, 52 mi lower and 200 mi upper); the Upper Delaware, Middle Delaware (Pennsylvania, New Jersey and New York, 35 mi) and Allagash (Maine, designated 7/19/70); the Eleven Point (Missouri, 44 mi); the Rio Grande in Texas (191 mi); and the Obed River (Tennessee, 45 mi).

**Section 5(a) Study Rivers (Section IV), selected examples:**

- **Alaska:** the Colville (428 mi), Yukon (Rampart section, 128 mi), Porcupine (200 mi), Melozitna (270 mi), Utukok (250 mi) and Koyuk (160 mi) rivers, among 12 Alaskan rivers listed.
- **Arizona:** the Salt, San Francisco and Verde rivers.
- **California:** the North Fork Kern and Tuolumne rivers.
- **Colorado:** the Cache la Poudre, Colorado, Conejos, Dolores (202 mi), Green, Gunnison, Los Pinos, Piedra and Yampa rivers.
- **Southeast:** the Suwannee (Georgia and Florida, 277 mi), Myakka, Loxahatchee, Ogeechee (246 mi), Escatawpa and Sipsey Fork rivers.
- **Other regions:** the Penobscot (Maine), Cacapon, Gauley, Bluestone and Greenbrier (West Virginia), Manistee-Pine and Au Sable (Michigan), Wisconsin River, Nolichucky, Buffalo and Obed (Tennessee), and Illinois River (Oklahoma, 115 mi).

**Limitations of the document:**

- It contains no species, sample sizes or biological statistics.
- The mileages for Alaska headwaters are estimates, because management agencies had not yet delineated those boundaries.
- Several entries are marked as having no determined boundaries, and some OCR text is garbled, so individual values should be checked against the original scan before citation.
- The document records designation and study status, not ecological condition or the reasons for designation.
