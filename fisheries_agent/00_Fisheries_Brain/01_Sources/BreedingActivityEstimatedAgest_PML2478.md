# Breeding Activity, Age Structure, and Growth in Sonoran Desert Anurans

#herpetology #anura #bufonidae #scaphiopodidae

## Executive Summary

This study documents the breeding activity, age structure, and growth of four desert anurans at two flood control sites in north-central [[Phoenix Arizona]], [[Maricopa County]]. The sites are [[Adobe Dam]] (534 ha) and [[Cave Buttes]] (737 ha). The four species are [[Bufo Alvarius]], [[Bufo Cognatus]], [[Bufo Punctatus]], and [[Scaphiopus Couchii]]. Breeding was tied to heavy [[Summer Monsoon]] rainfall, occurring mainly in 1990 and 1992. Age was estimated by [[Skeletochronology]] using [[Lines of Arrested Growth]] (LAGs). The authors conclude that these anurans are young, fast-growing, and short-lived, not long-lived species with extended reproductive lifespans. The work provides baseline data as urbanization reaches these sites.

## Background & Description

- **Study taxa:** Four anuran species were studied: the Colorado River toad (*Bufo alvarius*), the Great Plains toad (*B. cognatus*), the red-spotted toad (*B. punctatus*), and Couch's spadefoot toad (*Scaphiopus couchii*).
- **Rationale:** Population declines have been recorded for many temperate and tropical anurans, but the population biology of most desert amphibians of southwestern North America is largely unknown.
- **Study sites:** Both sites are flood control lands maintained by Maricopa County along Skunk Creek and Cave Creek. They were just beginning to experience urban expansion northward.
- **Skeletochronology background:** Amphibian bone cross-sections show concentric layers separated by LAGs, which reflect winter growth depression. Bone resorption can erase early LAGs, so age estimates can be conservative. Earlier work on [[Bufo Bufo]] (Hemelaar, 1981) corrected for resorption losses.
- **Objectives:** The study aimed to (1) document species occurrence and breeding over 1990–1995, (2) estimate age structure and growth from bone sections, and (3) assess population persistence under variable rainfall.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Monsoon-driven breeding:** Breeding was tied to significant rain events, defined as at least 25 mm of rainfall within 24 h. Breeding occurred mainly in 1990 and 1992. No oviposition was recorded in 1991 or 1993. *S. couchii* bred on a single night in 1994, and *B. alvarius* bred on a single night in 1995.
- **Habitat use:** The four species typically use rain-formed pools in low-lying areas or along low-gradient floodplains. Dike-created habitat may resemble their historical breeding habitat in the region.
- **Urbanization and dams:** The effects of constructing [[Dams and Dikes]] on Skunk Creek and Cave Creek on anuran breeding are unclear. Some workers suggest impoundments may benefit desert anurans, but the authors had no supporting study.
- **Additional species:** One further anuran species was noted at Adobe Dam in 1994. It is common in temporary pools and probably breeds early in spring in more permanent water. It was not captured at either site in other years.
- **Road surveys:** Surveys were run by driving slowly along roads at night after rain. Breeding activity was recorded only at a coarse level, so counts were approximate.

## Key Findings

**1. Breeding is rainfall-dependent and episodic.**
- Breeding was restricted mainly to 1990 and 1992, both years with repeated significant rain events.
- Breeding was absent in 1991 and 1993. Only single-night breeding occurred in 1994 (*S. couchii*) and 1995 (*B. alvarius*).

**2. Populations are young.**
- Conservative LAG-based age estimates suggest average ages of about two to four years for all four taxa at both sites in 1994 and 1995.

**3. Growth is fastest early in life for some species.**
- Growth, measured as bone deposited between LAGs, was greatest in the second year for *B. alvarius* and *B. cognatus*.
- This pattern was not found for *B. punctatus* or *S. couchii*.
- Results indicate rapid growth to maturity, which does not support long adult lifespans.

**4. Sample sizes were small and site differences varied.**
- In 1995, *B. alvarius* had 10 males from Adobe Dam and 14 from Cave Buttes. Samples did not differ significantly and were pooled.
- *B. cognatus* had 15 males from Adobe Dam and 6 from Cave Buttes. Males differed significantly in LAG number and SVL, so they were analyzed separately. Cave Buttes males were apparently younger (about 2.5 LAGs versus about 4.2 at Adobe Dam), though small samples limit this interpretation.
- *S. couchii* had 8 males from Adobe Dam and 13 from Cave Buttes, with 10 females collected in total. Samples were pooled.
- *B. punctatus* had 36 individuals in 1995. Three from Adobe Dam were excluded because of sample size, leaving 25 males and 8 females from Cave Buttes. This species was apparently younger than the others, averaging about two LAGs.

**5. Body size relationships are limited.**
- SVL was significantly positively correlated with body mass in *B. alvarius*.
- Most other size and age correlations were weak or not significant.

**6. Persistence without annual breeding.**
- Populations at both sites appear able to persist for many seasons without successful breeding, across at least a 6-year span.

**7. Methodological caveats.**
- The authors regard the age estimates as conservative, because resorption may erase early LAGs.
- The increase in age of samples from 1994 to 1995 is consistent with the estimates being accurate.
- Validation would require skeletochronology of known-age individuals reared under natural or laboratory conditions, which had not been done.

**8. Conclusions.**
- The authors conclude that these anurans reach maturity rapidly and that the evidence does not support long-lived, extended-breeding strategies in this harsh desert environment.
- The data provide baseline information before urbanization affects these populations.
