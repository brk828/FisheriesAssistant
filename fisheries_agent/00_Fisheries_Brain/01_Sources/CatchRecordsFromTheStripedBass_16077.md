# Catch Records from the Striped Bass Sportfishery in California, 1949–1959

#ichthyology #marine-biology #moronidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 report by Harold K. Chadwick of the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] analyzes [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*) catch records for 1949–1959 to assess population status. It draws on two sources: mandatory [[Party Boat Catch Records]] and [[Postal Card Surveys]] of licensed anglers. The study supplements Calhoun's 1938–1948 analysis. Both sources show a gradual decline in fishing success through the late 1940s and early 1950s, a low point in the mid-1950s, and a partial recovery after 1956. Average fish size also fell over the period, which the author attributes most likely to increased harvest rate on a declining population.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Striped bass (*Roccus saxatilis*), a migratory sportfish. In California, party boat striped bass fishing is seasonal, following fish between localities.
- **Party boat records:** Since 1936, State law has required skippers of party boats (anglers pay to be taken out fishing) to record catches for each trip and submit them monthly to the [[California Department of Fish and Game]]. Calhoun (1949) analyzed 1938–1948; this report covers 1949–1959.
- **Postal card surveys:** Questionnaires sent to a random sample of angling license buyers asking for estimated catches the previous year. Surveys were conducted in 16 years between 1936 and 1959.
- The author notes these are the only available means of determining trends in population size and age structure over the full period, making them essential for managing the fishery.
- The work was part of Dingell-Johnson Project California F-9-R, "A Study of Sturgeon and Striped Bass," funded by Federal Aid to Fish Restoration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fishing areas:** Party boats fished primarily in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] and [[San Francisco Bay]]. Six fishing blocks comprise the main area (Figure 1). Blocks analyzed include 301 ([[San Pablo Bay]]), 302 ([[Suisun Bay]]), 303 (the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]), 308, 488 (Upper San Francisco Bay) and 489 (Lower San Francisco Bay).
- **Migration:** Bass are migratory. Tag returns (Chadwick, unpublished) indicated a tendency since 1953 to move farther downstream in summer, which would reduce summer fishing success in Blocks 301 and 308.
- **Prey and new fishing methods:** In 1958–1959, [[Pacific Herring]] (*Clupea pallasi*) spawning in the Bay drew bass that fishermen caught by deep and surface trolling. Deep trolling with a three-pound sinker and sinker release was introduced in the mid-1950s and widely used from summer 1957.
- **Hydrology:** Calhoun (1949) noted a positive correlation between fishing quality in Block 488 and Sacramento-San Joaquin River runoff. This relationship was not evident in 1949–1959. Runoff to the Delta is reported as a percent of normal, ranging from 62 (1955) to 171 (1956).
- **Regulation:** A minimum size limit was increased in 1956. It removed the smallest fish from the catch, and 12- to 16-inch fish had previously formed important components of the catch in Blocks 301 and 308.

## Key Findings

**Party boat effort**
- Reported party boat effort was roughly constant. The mean angler days per year was 15,913 for eight non-war years (1938–1948) and 15,625 for 1949–1959 (Table 1). Operators reporting ranged from 102 to 161 per year.
- Because successful anglers more than doubled since 1938, the party boat share of total effort likely declined.

**Block-level trends**
- **Block 301 (San Pablo Bay):** Angler days from 1950–1955 were about one-third to one-half of the 1938–1948 average. In 1958 and 1959, more angler days were recorded than in any other year of the 22-year period. Fishing shifted from spring and fall (1938–1948) to fall only (1953–1958). The best fall fishing of the period occurred in 1958, with a November catch of 2.8 bass per angler day despite a three-fish limit.
- **Block 303 (Delta):** Effort peaked at almost 7,000 angler days in 1952, then declined to below the 1945 level by 1959. Mean catch per angler day fell from 1.11–1.55 (1945–1952) to 0.61–1.02 (1953–1959). Causes included growing private trailer-boat use and declining success, and some boats moved to improved Bay fishing.
- **Block 488 (Upper San Francisco Bay):** Effort was under 1,000 angler days annually from 1944 to 1951. Effort and success rose sharply from 1957–1959 following new deep-trolling methods. The author concludes party boat catch in this block cannot measure abundance changes due to changed techniques.
- **Block 302 (Suisun Bay):** Little party boat fishing was reported since 1948 (annual mean 105 angler days and 190 bass). The author states this does not indicate an absence of bass.
- **Block 489 (Lower San Francisco Bay):** Very little fishing occurred (annual mean 252 angler days and 146 bass), and catches per angler day were very low (0.20–0.93 in the months with more than 30 boat days).

**Population trends**
- Three-year moving averages of September–October catch per unit of effort in Blocks 301, 303 and 308 (Figures 14–15) show a low point in the mid-1950s. Blocks 301 and 308 declined gradually from 1944 to 1956, while Block 303 was stable from 1946 to 1952 and then fell sharply until 1956.
- Postal card survey trends (Figure 16) closely match party boat trends.
- The author concludes the population declined gradually through the late 1940s and early 1950s to a mid-1950s low. Since 1956, population size appears to have increased substantially, though it is unclear whether this reverses the long-term trend. The increase appeared one year after the regulation change, so the regulation could not have caused it.

**Size trends**
- Mean reported weight was 4.6 lb for 1938–1948 and 4.0 lb since 1949. In Block 301, mean annual weight ranged 4.4–8.3 lb (1938–1948) versus 3.1–4.9 lb (1949–1959).
- Mean weights in Blocks 301, 303 and 308 all show low points around 1942 and 1952 and an overall downward trend. The author's most probable explanation is increased fishing pressure on a declining population, raising harvest rate and lowering average age of fish caught.

**Data limitations**
- Party boat records cover only part of the fishery. Boats are a substantial share only in northern San Francisco Bay, San Pablo Bay and the Delta. Records from two-trip and evening troll boats were excluded from most tabulations.
- Postal card surveys include only successful licensed anglers, which biases catch-per-effort estimates. Their accuracy has not been fully evaluated for striped bass. Salmon and Idaho studies showed exaggerations of about 3× and 100% respectively. The author recommends using postal card data only to indicate trends.
