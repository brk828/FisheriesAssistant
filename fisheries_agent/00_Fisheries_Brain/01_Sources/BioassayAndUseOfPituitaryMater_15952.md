# Bioassay and Use of Pituitary Materials to Spawn Warm-Water Fishes

#ichthyology #aquaculture #reproductive-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 research report by Howard P. Clemens and Kermit E. Sneed documents comprehensive investigations into the use of pituitary materials to induce spawning in warm-water fishes. The study evaluated pituitary activity and potency through bioassay methods, tested phylogenetic specificity across 29 interspecific/intergeneric and 25 interfamilial injections, and developed practical spawning protocols for twelve species. Results demonstrated that phylogenetic specificity is negligible for fish-cultural applications, with pituitaries from various donor species successfully inducing spawning across taxonomic boundaries. The research established standardized dosages, injection techniques, and timing protocols that enabled predictable spawning within 12-20 hours. Over 200 [[Channel Catfish]] spawns and thousands of [[Goldfish]] spawns showed no detrimental effects on fertility or offspring development, validating the method for large-scale aquaculture operations.

## Background & Description

The pituitary injection method offers significant advantages for fish culture by bypassing environmental variables (temperature, light, rainfall) and enabling predictable spawning schedules. The technique allows hand-stripping of both sexes, uniform age-class stocking, reduced disease transmission, elimination of parental predation, and facilitation of hybridization. While South American and Russian fish-culturists had applied the method to large-scale operations, United States applications remained largely experimental prior to this work.

**Pituitary Collection and Preparation**: Pituitaries were collected by removing fish heads, exposing the brain case, and extracting the pituitary gland with forceps. Collection teams of 9 workers processed approximately 1,000 pituitaries per hour. Preservation methods included immediate freezing followed by freeze-drying, or dehydration in four changes of cold acetone at 12-hour intervals. Properly stored acetone-dried pituitaries retained potency for at least 2 years (Hasler et al. reported 10-year viability). Fresh pituitaries were injected whole; dried pituitaries were ground, mixed, and suspended in distilled water at 2-3 milligrams per pound of recipient body weight.

**Injection Protocols**: Intraperitoneal injections at the pelvic fin axil proved most effective. Small fish (2-3 inches) received 0.1-0.2 milligrams in 0.1-0.2 milliliters; larger fish received 2+ milligrams per pound in 1+ milliliter volumes. Crystalline penicillin (10,000 units per injection) minimized peritoneal inflammation from foreign proteins, particularly important for species requiring multiple injections like [[Channel Catfish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research addressed fundamental questions about seasonal pituitary activity, donor fish characteristics, and environmental requirements for induced spawning. Collections throughout the year from [[Carp]] (9 months tested), buffalo (8 months), and other species revealed no clear seasonal inactivity patterns, though individual physiological variation in recipient fish exceeded detectable monthly differences in pituitary potency.

**Temperature Effects**: [[Goldfish]] latent period (injection to spawning) varied from 12 hours at 85°F to 25 hours at 52°F, with optimal spawning at 60-80°F. The 14-16 hour latent period at 72°F for 90% of females enabled precise scheduling—afternoon injections produced early morning spawns coinciding with natural spawning times.

**Phylogenetic Relationships**: The study tested donor-recipient combinations across taxonomic levels. Of 61 heteroplastic injections, only 7 failed: 4 involved [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*) and [[Freshwater Drum]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*) pituitaries at standard dosages, and 3 were unsuccessful [[Carp]] spawning attempts with non-carp pituitaries. [[Carp]] pituitaries successfully spawned 9 species, establishing them as effective universal donors. Carpsucker pituitaries showed activity only at extremely high dosages (23-98 milligrams vs. standard 1-5 milligrams), suggesting either low gonadotropin content or collection from immature fish.

**Pituitary Characteristics**: Regression analysis established linear relationships between pituitary weight and fish dimensions. For [[Goldfish]], the equation Y=1.30x-6.381 (r=0.892) related pituitary weight to length. [[Channel Catfish]] showed sex-specific relationships: males Y=1.55x-15.55 (r=0.887), females Y=3.40x-44.44 (r=0.817) for length; stronger correlations existed with body weight (males r=0.911, females r=0.952). Fresh to acetone-dried weight ratio was 3.13:1 (24% dry weight). Pituitary-to-body-weight ratios: [[Goldfish]] 1mg:34.9g (18mg/pound), [[Channel Catfish]] females 6.2mg/pound, males 5.4mg/pound.

## Key Findings

**Bioassay Methods**: [[Goldfish]], [[Zebra Fish]], and [[Channel Catfish]] served as primary test species. Positive response indicators included ovulation in females and increased seminal plasma volume in males. Male [[Carp]] injected with pituitary produced 100-fold increases in milt volume (0.1 to 10 milliliters), with seminal plasma osmotic pressure changes measurable via sperm immobilization tests—a potential quantitative bioassay method.

**Pituitary Activity Variables**:
- **Donor sex**: No practical differences between male and female pituitaries for fish-cultural applications, though Barannikova (1949) found male sevruga pituitaries half as potent as females in some tests
- **Pituitary region**: Anterior lobe contained all gonadotropic activity; posterior (meta-adenohypophysis) showed no activity unless contaminated with anterior tissue
- **Size relationships**: Activity per unit weight remained constant across pituitary sizes (1-10 milligrams for [[Carp]]), with total activity directly proportional to weight

**Species-Specific Protocols**:

*[[Goldfish]] (Carassius auratus)*: Standard protocol for 8-12 inch females during March-June spawning season: single injection of 1-5 milligrams (2-3 mg/pound) acetone-dried pituitary induced spawning within 12-20 hours at 60-80°F. Responded to pituitaries from 14 donor species. Hand-stripping method: eggs remained viable 1 hour outside body, stored in sealed plastic bags in refrigerator without fertility loss. Spanish-moss mats collected adhesive eggs for transfer to rearing ponds treated with 2.5 ppm Lexone to eliminate predators and parasites. Commercial operations at Sulphur Fish Hatchery and Tishomingo National Fish Hatchery produced 675,000 fish (2,925 pounds) in 0.7-acre pond over 5 months.

*[[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio)*: Technique identical to [[Goldfish]] except demonstrated specificity—only homoplastic (carp) pituitaries effective; 20 [[Goldfish]] pituitaries produced no response. Successfully spawned February through July 14, extending beyond natural season.

*[[Channel Catfish]] (Ictalurus punctatus)*: Most complex protocol—74 pairs spawned in 10-gallon aquariums (larger fish required proportionally larger containers). Only females injected. Total dosage 3-32 mg/pound (average 3 injections at 2 mg/pound). Injection-to-spawning interval 2.5-72 hours (typically 16-24 hours). Number of injections ranged 1-28 (average 3). Penicillin inclusion controlled peritoneal inflammation from multiple injections. Successfully spawned refractory females that failed in natural habitat or hatchery ponds.

*[[Flathead Catfish]] (Pylodictis olivaris)*: Four pairs spawned in 55-gallon aquariums using buffalo, [[Carp]], and flathead pituitaries at 2.5-4 mg/pound every 24 hours. Two females responded to single injection. Selection criteria: darker coloration, reddish raised genital papillae with dilated opening. Brood stock overwintering in hatchery ponds showed poor gonadal development by June, suggesting temperature and feeding regime limitations.

*[[Golden Shiner]] (Notemigonus crysoleucas)*: Gravid females 3-5 inches received one-tenth carp pituitary (approximately 0.1-0.2 mg). Spawning occurred 15 hours post-injection at 68°F on Spanish-moss mats. Large-scale application at Sulphur Fish Hatchery: 200 injected brood females in earthen pond produced superior uniformity and production compared to traditional stocking methods.

*Notropis species*: [[Emerald Shiner]] (*N. atherinoides*), [[Mimic Shiner]] (*N. volucellus*), and [[Redfin Shiner]] (*N. umbratilis*) spawned with 0.1 ml injections containing 0.1-0.2 mg pituitary. Fish anesthetized in 1:10,000 MS-222, injected through netting. Spawning occurred 20-24 hours post-injection. [[Redfin Shiner]] exhibited dominant male behavior with intensified breeding coloration during spawning. Eggs scattered on aquarium bottom; nylon mesh prevented parental consumption.

*[[White Bass]] (Roccus chrysops)*: Two females with unspawned eggs (post-season collection) received 0.5 mg carp pituitary, then 2 mg second injection 17 hours later. Spawning occurred within 3 hours of second injection on Spanish-moss mats at 61°F. Approximately 60% fertility observed.

*[[White Crappie]] (Pomoxis annularis)*: Four pairs spawned in cement trough with flowing water. One female received cholesterol pellet with 114 mg chum salmon extract, showed genital pore enlargement at 15 hours, hand-strippable at 48 hours, then spawned following 4 mg carp pituitary injection. Three other pairs spawned within 30 hours of single carp pituitary injection.

*[[Rock Bass]] (Ambloplites rupestris)*: Two pairs spawned in 5-gallon aquariums with coarse gravel bottom and slight current at 76°F. Both sexes received two injections (total ~2 mg buffalo pituitary) 24 hours apart. Environmental specificity critical—gravel substrate and flowing water both necessary. Eggs adhesive, scattered on gravel, hatched in 3 days. Male nest-building behavior appeared essential trigger; male injection may be necessary.

*[[Zebra Fish]] (Brachydanio rerio)*: Twenty-four females injected with 0.6 mg carp pituitary in 0.2 ml water. Individual spawning containers with plastic screen 2 inches above bottom (2 males per female). Four of six surviving individually-housed females spawned 340 total eggs (average 57 eggs/female, range 17-148). One control female also spawned, demonstrating this species' readiness to spawn in captivity complicates assessment of pituitary effect. Fertility 51-96% across treatments. Cull fish showed high mortality (11 of 33 died immediately post-injection), suggesting dosage excessive for poorly-developed individuals.

**Fertility and Development**: No detrimental effects detected in over 200 [[Channel Catfish]] spawns and thousands of [[Goldfish]] spawns. Hand-stripped [[Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] showed 80-99% fertility (Table 11). Decreased fertility (1-5%) observed only with repeated male use or early/late season spawning, attributable to incomplete development or natural egg deterioration rather than hormone effects. Russian literature reports of "inferior sex products" from improper application (excessive doses) not replicated in this study. Eggs, fry, and fingerlings developed normally through all life stages.

**Carrier and Delivery Systems**: Comparison of distilled water, sesame oil, and cholesterol carriers showed distilled water most practical despite theoretical advantages of slower absorption with oil/cholesterol. Glycerine extracts up to 10% concentration proved safe; higher concentrations (>10%) caused peritoneal inflammation and hemorrhage. Equivalent dosages as aqueous suspension vs. 75-84% glycerine extract showed similar effectiveness in [[Goldfish]], though one [[Channel Catfish]] experiment suggested reduced effectiveness (possibly confounded by transport stress and oxygen deprivation).

**Toxicity in Immature Fish**: Chronic injections at spawning-level dosages proved harmful to fish with immature gonads. One-year-old [[Largemouth Bass]] with small eggs developed nervousness, mucus loss, whitish spots, violent jerking, muscle rigidity, and sigmoid flexure after 5-6 days of repeated injections. Similar responses observed in [[White Crappie]], [[Goldfish]], [[Carp]], and [[Zebra Fish]]. Possible mechanisms: (1) pharmacological overdose disrupting metabolism in sexually immature fish, (2) toxic compounds in pituitary preparations, (3) diuretic effects causing dehydration (evidenced by increased urination and apparent dehydration). ACTH content suggested as potential toxicity factor in immature fish.
