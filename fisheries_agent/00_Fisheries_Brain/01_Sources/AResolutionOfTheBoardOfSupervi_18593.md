# Pima County Sonoran Desert Conservation Plan Resolution and Species List

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document comprises Pima County, Arizona's Resolution No. 1998-250 establishing the [[Sonoran Desert Conservation Plan]] (SDCP), correspondence soliciting expert input on species selection, and a preliminary species list for multi-species habitat conservation planning. Passed December 3, 1998, the resolution commits Pima County to working with the U.S. Department of the Interior to develop a regional conservation plan addressing 18 federally listed threatened and endangered species. The plan aims to achieve species recovery, habitat conservation throughout the regional ecosystem, and regulatory assurances while meeting community economic needs. Aquatic species specifically identified include [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]], and [[Sonoyta Mud Turtle]].

## Background & Description

The [[Sonoran Desert Conservation Plan]] represents a cooperative, federally recognized planning process initiated by the [[Pima County Board of Supervisors]] to address conservation needs under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The Board's October 27, 1998 action established a public stakeholder process and invited participation from Native American Tribes, federal and state land managers, and all municipalities within Pima County.

The March 12, 1999 correspondence from Brooks A. Keenan (Director, Department of Transportation and Flood Control District) to W. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]] requests expert assistance in developing the biological component of the SDCP. The letter specifically solicits identification of species or subspecies in danger of extirpation in Pima County, with requested response by March 24, 1999.

The preliminary species list categorizes taxa into three groups: (1) federally listed threatened and endangered species (18 species total), (2) federal candidate species (5 species), and (3) additional species likely to be listed (2 species identified in the excerpt).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Pima County encompasses portions of the [[Sonoran Desert]] ecosystem, supporting diverse aquatic and terrestrial habitats. The region's 18 federally listed species represent multiple taxonomic groups including mammals, birds, fish, amphibians, reptiles, and plants, indicating ecosystem-wide conservation challenges.

**Aquatic Species Context:**

The listed and candidate aquatic species represent critical components of desert aquatic ecosystems:

- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (Cyprinodontidae): Federally listed threatened/endangered species dependent on isolated desert springs and streams
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (Poeciliidae): Federally listed species inhabiting small streams and springs
- **[[Gila Chub]]** (Cyprinidae): Federal candidate species requiring stream habitat
- **[[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]** (Ranidae): Federal candidate species dependent on permanent and semi-permanent aquatic habitats
- **[[Sonoyta Mud Turtle]]** (Kinosternidae): Federal candidate species requiring wetland habitats

Additional aquatic-associated species include [[Huachuca Water Umbel]] (plant) and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (riparian-dependent bird).

The [[San Xavier Talusnail]] appears on the additional species list, representing endemic invertebrate fauna potentially requiring conservation attention.

## Key Findings

**Resolution Commitments:**

1. Pima County formally commits to upholding the [[Endangered Species Act]] through regional multi-species habitat conservation planning
2. The county will work with the [[U.S. Department of the Interior]] to develop the [[Sonoran Desert Conservation Plan]]
3. Conservation goals include: (a) recovery of listed species, (b) conservation of habitat and species throughout the regional ecosystem, and (c) obtaining broad and predictable regulatory assurances
4. The plan must balance conservation goals with community economic needs

**Species Coverage:**

- 18 federally listed threatened or endangered species currently documented in Pima County
- 5 federal candidate species under consideration
- Minimum 2 additional species identified as likely future listings
- Aquatic taxa represent significant proportion of conservation concern, with 5 aquatic/semi-aquatic species among listed and candidate categories

**Planning Process:**

- Public stakeholder process established October 27, 1998
- Expert consultation initiated March 1999, targeting specialists like W. Minckley (noted ichthyologist and desert fish expert)
- Biological evaluation development underway for long-term multi-species habitat conservation plan
- Cooperative agreement framework invites participation from tribal, federal, state, and municipal entities

**Administrative Structure:**

The SDCP is coordinated through Pima County Department of Transportation and Flood Control District, with technical development involving the Floodplain Management Division, indicating recognition of aquatic habitat conservation as integral to the planning effort.
