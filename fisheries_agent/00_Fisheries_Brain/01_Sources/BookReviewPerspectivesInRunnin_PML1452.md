# Book Review: Perspectives in Running Water Ecology

#stream-ecology #limnology #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This review evaluates [[Perspectives In Running Water Ecology]], an edited volume by Maurice A. Lock and David D. Williams (Plenum Press, New York, 430 pages). The volume is dedicated to [[H. B. Noel Hynes]], a renowned stream ecologist whose work shaped the field. The reviewer describes it as a useful compendium of specialized literature reviews for graduate students, postdoctoral researchers, and other specialists, but notes that it is neither comprehensive nor well integrated. Its four sections are largely unrelated, cross-referencing between papers is limited, and the typography and organization are uneven. The reviewer recommends selective purchase or access through institutional libraries rather than personal purchase, because the list price is high.

The first part of the source is a separate review of a revised edition of a book on African lakes, including [[Lake Malawi]], [[Lake Chad]], and [[Lake Turkana]]. Its content is only partly legible in the scan and is summarized briefly below.

## Background & Description

- **Structure:** Four sections, described by the reviewer as "largely unrelated":
  1. Energy inputs and nutrient cycling
  2. [[Benthos]] in time and space
  3. Perturbations of the aquatic environment, including impacts and applications
  4. Regional running water ecology
- **Nature of content:** Each paper is a literature review with few new data. Most are reasonably comprehensive through the late 1970s, and together they give a substantial information base for specific areas of stream ecology.
- **Editorial critique:** The reviewer argues that synthesis would have improved the volume if editors had written introductions and summaries for each section. The reviewer also notes organizational inconsistencies, topical headings and compartmentalization that vary between chapters, typographical errors, and citation problems. Numerical data are described as the most frustrating omission, since several chapters present results without clear sample sizes or methods.
- **Price and audience:** The reviewer calls the list price high for a volume of this scope and suggests it suits libraries and specialists more than general readers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Section I: Energy inputs and nutrient cycling.** Papers cover:
- [[River Epilithon]] (M. A. Lock)
- Organic matter processing (G. A. Bird and a second author, name partly garbled in scan)
- Seasonality of organic matter (K. W. Dance)
- [[Dissolved Organic Matter]] (P. M. Wallis)
- [[Nitrogen Transport And Transformation]] (N. K. Kaushik and a co-author)
- Inorganic carbon compounds as process drivers (D. L. Lush)

The reviewer notes that major current topics are missing. These include primary production by plants and by invertebrates and fishes, nutrient budgets, and inorganic nutrients beyond nitrogen.

**Section II: Benthos.** Contributions address:
- Movement and drift of benthic invertebrates (D. Williams)
- [[Lotic Ciliate Ecology]] (W. D. Williams)
- Lotic benthic ecology (R. Marchant)
- Hydrodynamic influences on lentic benthos (Barton)

The reviewer considers the lentic paper out of place in a volume on streams.

**Section III: Perturbations and applications.** Papers include:
- [[Impounded Tropical Rivers]] (L. T. Obeng), covering reservoirs that are disturbed but not strictly lotic
- [[Aquatic Invertebrate Paleoecology]] (N. E. Williams), described as a known topic that is largely outside the volume's scope
- Research management problems (R. R. Wallace), which the reviewer judges unlikely to reach planners or politicians

This section is the most uneven. It is most useful for comparing perturbations based on case studies and [[Environmental Impact Assessment]] reports.

**Section IV: Regional ecology.** Chapters cover several regions, including Africa and Australia (W. D. Williams). The reviewer notes that the regional chapters vary in scope: some are truly regional, while others cover broad continental or global themes. Together they give a comparison framework for other regions, such as Europe, Asia, and the tropics. Editorial effort appears uneven across chapters, which weakens the volume's overall sense of continuity.

**Companion volume.** The source also briefly reviews a second book on aquatic organic matter. The reviewer finds its outstanding contributions to be in the first chapters, with only partial coverage of the later material. A further volume on the functions of aquatic environments (salinity, pH, redox potential, dissolved oxygen, surface tension, hydrostatic pressure, and nutrients) is described as mostly tangential. The reviewer says it reads as an introduction to marine physiology and does not take a clearly mechanistic or synthetic approach.

## Key Findings

- The volume is a collection of literature reviews, not a research report, and offers few new data.
- Its four sections are weakly linked, and the volume lacks synthesis, partly because the editors provided no section introductions or summaries.
- Coverage is strongest in stream benthos, organic matter processing, and regional summaries. It is weakest in primary production, nutrient budgets, and general theory.
- The reviewer says the volume rarely moves beyond description to mechanistic explanation of processes and patterns.
- Editorial and production problems include inconsistent chapter organization, typographical errors, citation issues, and missing numerical detail.
- The reviewer recommends the book mainly as a literature resource for specialists. Most readers should consult it through libraries or shared resources rather than buy it.
- The volume is dedicated to Hynes and shows his influence through the work of researchers who trained with him and the ecologists he influenced.
