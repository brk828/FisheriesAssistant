# California Fish and Game Commission Memorandum on Exotic Fish Import Request

#ichthyology #aquaculture #Cyprinidae #Cichlidae #exotic-species #regulatory-policy

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the California Department of Fish and Game to the Fish and Game Commission addresses a request by [[United Energy Corporation]] (UEC) to import and possess eight exotic fish species for aquaculture and research at a facility near [[Yermo]], [[San Bernardino County]]. The species include [[Grass Carp]], [[Bighead Carp]], [[Silver Carp]], and five [[Tilapia]] species (T. aurea, T. nilotica, T. mossambica, T. hornorum, and T. zillii). While the Department has serious reservations about all eight species due to invasive potential, they support the proposal conditionally based on the facility's remote location and stringent security measures. The Department emphasizes this approval should not set precedent for general aquaculture use of these species in California.

## Background & Description

The [[United Energy Corporation]] submitted letters and reports describing an aquaculture and research operation under construction near Yermo in San Bernardino County. The facility plans to utilize eight exotic fish species:

**Chinese Carps:**
- [[Grass Carp]] (Ctenopharyngodon idella) - prohibited under Section 671(e)(9)(B)
- [[Bighead Carp]] (Hypophthalmichthys nobilis) - not prohibited
- [[Silver Carp]] (Hypophthalmichthys molitrix) - not prohibited

**Tilapia Species:**
- Tilapia aurea (now [[Coptodon aureus]]) - not confirmed present in California
- T. nilotica ([[Oreochromis niloticus]]) - not confirmed present in California
- T. mossambica ([[Oreochromis mossambicus]]) - present in southern California waters
- T. hornorum - present in southern California waters
- T. zillii ([[Coptodon zillii]]) - prohibited

The Department notes that California's official prohibited species list (Section 671 of Title 14) was never intended to be all-inclusive. Species are typically added in response to import pressures from aquaculturists and aquarium dealers. Many unlisted species pose threats equal to listed species but have not been evaluated due to limited time and manpower.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Department expresses serious concerns about all eight proposed exotic species, not just the prohibited [[Grass Carp]]. Should these species escape into California waters, their survival chances are good, with particularly severe consequences for [[Tilapia]] species.

**Invasive Potential:**
- [[Tilapia]] species would most likely reproduce and spread if escaped
- Negative impacts expected on game fish and native nongame species
- T. aurea and T. nilotica present greater threats than T. mossambica and T. hornorum because they are more cold-tolerant
- The absence of confirmed populations of T. aurea and T. nilotica in California increases risk

**Comparative Threats:**
The memorandum provides examples of regulatory gaps:
- [[Crocodiles]] are prohibited but [[Alligators]] and [[Caimans]] are not, despite similar threats
- All [[Cambaridae]] (crayfish family) are prohibited, but [[Australian Marron]] (different family) is not, despite being a serious threat
- [[Grass Carp]] is prohibited while [[Silver Carp]] and [[Bighead Carp]] are not, despite similar risks
- T. zillii is prohibited while T. aurea and T. nilotica are not, despite comparable threats

The Department's preference would be to restrict use to T. mossambica and T. hornorum alone, as these are already established in southern California waters and would not set precedent for introducing new species.

## Key Findings

**Department Position:**
The Department supports UEC's proposal solely because the facility location and stringent security measures virtually eliminate escape threat. This approval explicitly should NOT be considered precedent for general aquaculture use of these species in California. Any subsequent proposals would be evaluated case-by-case and must meet or exceed Yermo facility safeguards.

**Required Conditions for Permit Approval:**

1. Continuous facility patrol by UEC employee at all times
2. All facility waters must be contained in on-site ponds; no discharge to State waters
3. Eight-foot high chain link fence surrounding all aquaculture, research, settling and clarifying ponds
4. Access only through locked gates; entry restricted to authorized individuals on official business
5. All broodstock confined within locked greenhouse
6. Fish held only in above-ground tanks
7. All outdoor tanks covered with bird netting or similar material to prevent predation and bird removal
8. Outdoor [[Tilapia]] must be 100% male hybrids
9. [[Grass Carp]] use limited to confirmed sterile, triploid fish
10. No live fish may leave facility; only dead fish or parts may be removed
11. Fish shipments into California governed by appropriate importation regulations (license, permits, disease and parasite inspections)
12. Before [[Tilapia]] importation, acknowledged expert must provide positive species identification
13. Any permits become null and void if conditions not adhered to
14. Department will meet annually with UEC to review program prior to issuing new aquaculture registration

**Administrative Notes:**
- Most conditions were already suggested by UEC in their proposal
- Conditions should not present special hardship to operation
- Memorandum dated July 26, 1984, for August 3, 1984 Commission meeting
- Signed by Jack C. Parnell, Director
