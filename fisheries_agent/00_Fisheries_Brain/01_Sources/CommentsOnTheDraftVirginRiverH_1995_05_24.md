# Comments on the Draft Virgin River Habitat Conservation and Management Plan

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #desert-fishes #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal commentary letter (dated 24 May 1995) from Dr. Walter R. Courtenay, Jr., Chairman of the ASIH Environmental Quality Committee, addressed to the Washington County Water Conservancy District regarding the draft **Virgin River Habitat Conservation and Management Plan**. The letter, submitted jointly on behalf of the [[American Society Of Ichthyologists And Herpetologists]] (ASIH) and the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC), critiques the plan's narrow focus on a single indicator species — the [[Virgin River Spinedace]] — arguing that this approach risks neglecting the broader aquatic community of the [[Virgin River]] ecosystem, including other imperiled native fishes such as the [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin River Chub]]. The letter raises concerns that the plan may function primarily as a mechanism to avoid formal Endangered Species Act listing rather than a genuine ecosystem-level conservation strategy.

## Background & Description

While this is a policy/conservation letter rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study, it identifies three key native fish taxa central to the Virgin River aquatic community:

- **[[Virgin River Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinus*) — subspecies-level taxon, focal species of the draft conservation plan.
- **[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) — federally listed native cyprinid; status noted as continuing to decline despite existing protections.
- **[[Virgin River Chub]]** (*Gila seminuda*) — federally listed native cyprinid; status also noted as declining despite "protection."

No morphometric, meristic, or anatomical descriptions are provided in this source; the document is exclusively focused on conservation policy and habitat management concerns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat system**: The [[Virgin River]] watershed, a desert riverine system in the southwestern U.S. (Utah/Arizona/Nevada), including its floodplain and riparian corridor.
- **Thermal/chemical inputs**: [[La Verkin Springs]] (Pah Tempe Springs) contributes high total dissolved solids (TDS) and heated water into the Virgin River system. The letter argues native fishes have coexisted with this natural thermal/chemical outflow for "centuries and probably many millennia," suggesting it is not a primary threat.
- **Anthropogenic pressures identified**:
  - Habitat modification (over ~100 years), including water diversions downstream of La Verkin Springs, identified as likely more ecologically damaging than the springs' natural outflow itself.
  - Introduction of nonindigenous (non-native) fish species, compounding habitat degradation.
  - Floodplain development pressure, partially addressed in the draft plan via proposed land purchases and floodplain development restrictions.
- **Community-level framing**: The letter emphasizes that the Virgin River supports an interconnected native fish community (spinedace, woundfin, chub, and others), and that conservation actions targeting only the spinedace could have negative cascading effects on co-occurring threatened/endangered species sharing the same habitat.

## Key Findings

- The draft plan is criticized as effectively a **single-species conservation approach** (focused on Virgin River spinedace) that may be insufficient — and potentially counterproductive — for protecting the full native aquatic community of the Virgin River, which includes other already-listed species ([[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Chub]]).
- The apparent motivation behind the plan is questioned: ASIH/DFC suggest it may function as a strategy to **avoid formal ESA listing** of additional fish species rather than as a genuine ecosystem conservation measure.
- Historical framing is challenged: the claim that native fishes have "survived 100 years of habitat modification" is reframed — the authors argue the aquatic community *prior* to intensive study period likely represented something closer to a "natural state," and that recent documented declines reflect real, accelerating jeopardy, not stability.
- **Habitat modification** and **introduction of nonindigenous species** (facilitated by habitat modification) are identified as the primary drivers of decline for Virgin River native fishes.
- The proposal to **divert flow from La Verkin (Pah Tempe) Springs** is characterized as "biologically questionable," since native fishes have long persisted with this natural high-TDS, heated water input; downstream water diversions are considered a greater threat.
- **Flow modeling** cited in the draft plan is criticized as potentially flawed, given the poorly understood biological "needs" of the full suite of Virgin River native fishes — likened to "trying to stop a major brushfire with a garden hose."
- Positive aspects of the draft are acknowledged: efforts to halt floodplain development and to purchase adjacent riparian lands are seen as evidence of an ecosystem-level (not purely single-species) conservation intent.
- A **strong recommendation** is made for a **total prohibition** on further introductions of nonindigenous fish species into the Virgin River watershed.
- The letter defers detailed technical/status assessments to regional specialists with direct field experience in Virgin River fish populations, while reserving policy-level critique for the ASIH/DFC committees.
