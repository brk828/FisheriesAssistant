# A Procedure for Evaluating Management Options for Cui-ui Recovery

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #catostomidae #endangered-species #population-dynamics

## Executive Summary

This 1990 memorandum and attached proposal outline a quantitative framework for evaluating management options in the [[Cui-ui]] ([[Chasmistes cujus]]) Recovery Plan. The approach uses hydrological modeling and population dynamics simulations to assess extinction risk over 200 years and population growth potential over 50 years under various water diversion and harvest scenarios. The methodology integrates the Buchanen-Strekel [[Cui-ui]] population model with the Frevert-Lane hydrological model to generate probabilistic projections based on 80 years of historical data from the [[Truckee River]] system. The framework aims to inform the Secretary's Negotiated Settlement Task Force regarding Senate Bill 1554 and provide recommendations by mid-July 1990, ahead of the January 30, 1991 deadline.

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] is an endemic sucker species (Family [[Catostomidae]]) restricted to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. The recovery effort focuses on two primary management levers: (1) control of water diversion from the [[Truckee River]] to the [[Newlands Project]], and (2) regulation of future [[Cui-ui]] harvesting. The proposal was prepared by John Emlen of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Seattle Fisheries Research Center following meetings with recovery team members in Seattle and Reno.

The computational framework employs two linked models:

1. **Buchanen-Strekel Population Model**: A state-of-knowledge model developed by Chester Buchanen (USFWS) and Tom Strekel (Bureau of Reclamation) that uses hydrologic input to generate age-structured population densities over time based on age-specific birth and death rates.

2. **Frevert-Lane Hydrological Model**: A "Spatial Disaggregation Procedure" developed by D.K. Frevert and W.L. Lane (Bureau of Reclamation) employing a two-year autocorrelation lag model that constructs joint probability density functions for eight hydrologic variables and generates month-by-month projections of [[Pyramid Lake]] water levels.

The approach defines "in perpetuity" as 200 years and sets a 50-year timeframe for population restoration to pre-agreed levels.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Pyramid Lake]] hydrology is driven by flows from the [[Truckee River]] system, with significant water diversion to the Truckee Division of the [[Newlands Project]] affecting lake levels and [[Cui-ui]] reproductive success. The model is based on 80 years of historical hydrological data, though concerns exist that this period may represent unusually wet conditions. Long-term weather cycles and climatic changes of greater periodicity than captured by the third-order autocorrelation process may result in extended periods without successful [[Cui-ui]] reproduction.

The population model omits density feedback mechanisms due to insufficient data, meaning density compensation effects that might prevent population declines in reality are not reflected. However, this conservative approach is justified because populations reaching extreme lows will ultimately face extinction given sufficient time.

Potential growth of the Reno-Sparks urban area and legally mandated changes in water management (e.g., [[Stampede Reservoir]] operations) are not incorporated in current models, likely causing underestimation of extinction risk.

## Key Findings

**Decision Criteria Established:**

- **Extinction threshold**: Population growth multiple R₂₀₀ < 1.0 over 200 years indicates eventual extinction
- **Acceptable risk level**: Maximum tolerable extinction probability set at 0.01 (99% persistence probability required)
- **Recovery criterion**: Minimum doubling of population over 50 years, requiring R₅₀ ≥ 2.0

**Analytical Framework:**

The proposal evaluates 15 scenarios combining five water management options (purchase 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% of water currently diverted to Truckee Division) with three harvest levels (0%, 10%, or 20% annual adult harvest). Each scenario runs 200 replicate simulations of 200-year hydrological projections.

**Hypothetical Results Illustrated:**

- With no harvesting, achieving 99% persistence probability requires purchasing approximately 50% of diverted water
- With 10% annual harvest, approximately 90% of diverted water must be purchased for 99% persistence
- For population doubling over 50 years with no fishing, approximately 70% of water must be saved for [[Pyramid Lake]]
- With 10% harvest, even complete water buy-out may be insufficient for recovery

**Sources of Error Identified:**

1. Two-year lag autocorrelation may underrepresent long-term wet/dry cycles, underestimating extinction risk
2. The 80-year historical record may represent unusually wet conditions; tree ring analysis suggested for correction
3. Reno-Sparks urban growth not modeled, likely underestimating extinction risk
4. Future water management changes (e.g., [[Stampede Reservoir]]) not anticipated

**Management Applications:**

The framework can evaluate alternative water management strategies, such as withholding water to ensure regular annual fish runs versus percentage buy-outs of [[Newlands Project]] diversions. The approach serves as a mechanism for exploring compromise solutions between [[Cui-ui]] recovery needs and regional water demands, providing quantitative support for policy decisions presented to the Secretary's Negotiated Settlement Task Force.

**Timeline and Coordination:**

Recovery team deadline remains January 30, 1991, with interim recommendations for the Task Force targeted for mid-July 1990. Technical staff from various agencies may be assigned to assist with alternative analysis. Mr. Plenert expressed interest in attending the next recovery team meeting.
