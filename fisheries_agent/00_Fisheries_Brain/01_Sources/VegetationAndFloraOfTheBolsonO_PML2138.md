# Vegetation and Flora of the Bolsón of Cuatro Ciénegas Region, Coahuila, Mexico: Summary, Endemism and Corrected Catalogue

#botany #vascular-plants #chihuahuan-desert

## Executive Summary

This paper, Part IV of a series by [[Donald J. Pinkava]] (Department of Botany & Microbiology, [[Arizona State University]]), summarizes the vegetation and vascular flora of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] (Bolsón de Cuatro Ciénegas) in [[Chihuahuan Desert]] Coahuila, Mexico. The study area covers more than 2,000 km² and ranges from about 740 m on the basin floor to over 3,000 m atop [[Sierra de la Madera]]. The known flora comprises 879 taxa, distributed among 860 species in 456 genera from 114 families. The region is a type locality for 49 taxa, of which four are no longer recognized and 23 are endemic. The author concludes that botanical and zoological evidence justifies protection of the basin's unique habitats and biota, which face grazing, farming, gypsum harvest, logging, mining and water salinization.

## Background & Description

The Bolsón is an intermontane valley bordered by limestone sierras capped by conifer forests. It contains playas, rivers, gypsum dunes, subterranean tubes, artesian wells, pozos (springs) and [[Cienegas]] (wetlands). Bordering ranges include [[Sierra de San Marcos]], Sierra San Vicente, Sierra de la Purísima, Sierra de la Fragua, Sierra de la Madera and Sierra de la Menchaca. The town of Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza lies in the north-central basin.

The author divides the vegetation into eight zones:

1. Basin sacaton grasslands (see [[Sacaton Grassland]])
2. Aquatic and semi-aquatic habitats
3. Gypsum dunes
4. Transition zone between grasslands and bajadas
5. Desert scrub, rich in cacti
6. Chaparral
7. Oak-pine and oak woodlands
8. Montane conifer forests

Earlier floristic and aquatic work cited includes Gilmore (1947), Meyer (1975), Minckley (1969, 1977), Minckley and Cole (1968), Pinkava (1979–1981) and Wood (1975).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Endemism.** The author describes the basin as rich in both plant and animal endemics. The flora's endemism is concentrated in three areas:

- **Laguna Grande–Laguna Churince complex**, extending north to Río Mesquites and east onto the north tip of Sierra de San Marcos: about 12 endemic taxa.
- **Canyons and bajadas of Sierra de la Madera**, from Agua to Hacienda to Charretera canyons, on east-facing slopes and partly down west-facing slopes: about 10 endemic taxa.
- **South-facing bajadas and canyons above Pozo Anteojo**: an additional 2 endemic taxa.

The author argues these areas should be correlated with the endemic fauna and protected, while respecting the rights of local residents.

**Threats.** Endangerment to endemic plant habitats includes goat and cattle grazing, farming, harvesting of gypsum from dunes, and logging of conifers. The once-isolated basin now has a town, with associated farming, logging, gypsum removal, mining and salinization of water.

**Collection history.** The first significant collections were made by [[Marshall C. Johnston]] on 24–26 August 1938, followed by specimens from Leroy H. Harvey, Ernest M. Marsh Jr., Cornelius H. Muller and Stephen S. White in 1939, and by Johnston again in 1941. About 25 years later, an intensive survey was led by teams from [[Arizona State University]], the [[University of Texas at Austin]] and the Desert Botanical Garden in Phoenix. The nomenclature follows Cronquist et al. (1966), Crabbe et al. (1975) for pteridophytes, and Cronquist (1981) for angiosperms.

## Key Findings

**Flora size and composition**
- 879 taxa total: 860 species, 456 genera, 114 families.
- Cultivated plants away from habitation add 7 species, 7 genera and 3 families.
- Table 2 breaks the flora into divisions, including Lycopodiophyta, Polypodiophyta, Pinophyta (Gymnospermae) and Magnoliophyta (Angiospermae), with dicots and monocots treated separately. Exact subgroup counts are garbled in the source scan and are not reproduced here.
- The catalogue includes many genera with notable species richness, including [[Asteraceae]], [[Fabaceae]], [[Poaceae]], [[Cactaceae]], [[Euphorbiaceae]] and [[Acanthaceae]].

**Type localities and endemism**
- The basin is the type locality for 49 taxa. The abstract reports four as no longer recognized and 23 as endemic. The introduction states that 45 of the 49 are currently recognized and 23 are endemic. The source does not fully reconcile these counts.
- Examples of type-locality taxa include *Abies durangensis* var. *coahuilensis*, *Ancistrocactus brevihamatus*, *Atriplex prosopidum*, *Nerisyrenia incana*, *Sabatia tuberculosa* and *Satureja maderensis*.
- Gypsum endemics include *Machaeranthera gypsophila*, *Dyssodia gypsophila* and *Gaillardia gypsophila*.

**Conservation**
- Protection is urgently needed "before this important part of man's heritage is lost." The author stresses the combined importance of endemic taxa, unique aquatic habitats and scenic value.
- The author cites botanical and zoological evidence as sufficient to justify protection of the basin's habitats and biota.

**Catalogue**
- The annotated catalogue lists vascular plants by zone code (A = aquatic, B = basin grasslands, C = chaparral, D = desert scrub, G = gypsum dunes, M = montane forest, O = oak-pine woodland, T = transition zone). It includes new records, nomenclatural changes and specimen citations.
- Specimens are deposited in herbaria including ASU and TEX-LL, with several collectors identified by code letters.
