# Survival of Age-0 Colorado Squawfish in the Green and Colorado Rivers

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This 1993 interim report by [[Richard A. Valdez]] and [[Bryan R. Cowdell]] (BIO/WEST, Inc.), submitted to the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR Contract No. 90-2558), presents preliminary survival data for the **1992 year class** of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in three standardized monitoring reaches: the upper Green River (Reach 4), lower Green River (Reach 3), and lower Colorado River (Reach 1). The study is part of the long-term [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP, USFWS 1987), tracking age-0 (young-of-year) squawfish from fall to the following spring to assess overwinter survival and growth. Data show substantial declines in raw abundance between fall and spring across all three reaches, though catch-per-effort (CPE) trends varied, and mean total length increased consistently from fall to spring, indicating differential growth and mortality dynamics in nursery backwater habitats.

## Background & Description

- **Age-0 vs. Juvenile size thresholds** (established via length-frequency histograms and field observation):
  - 1987, 1988, 1990, 1991 year classes: max age-0 size = 60 mm TL (fall), 70 mm TL (spring)
  - 1989 year class: max age-0 size = 70 mm TL (fall), 75 mm TL (spring)
  - 1992 year class: max age-0 size = 60 mm TL (fall), 65 mm TL (spring)
- **Reach 3 (Lower Green River), 1992 year class:**
  - Fall 1992 mean TL: 37 mm (range 16-59 mm)
  - Spring 1993 mean TL: 45 mm (range 30-65 mm) — an increase of 8 mm
  - Juveniles: Fall 1992 = 61-93 mm TL (n=11); Spring 1993 = 67-90 mm TL (n=17)
- **Reach 1 (Lower Colorado River), 1992 year class:**
  - Fall 1992 mean TL: 34 mm (range 21-59 mm)
  - Spring 1993 mean TL: 40 mm (range 29-60 mm) — an increase of 6 mm
  - Juveniles: Fall 1992 = 65-68 mm TL (n=5); Spring 1993 = 67-79 mm TL (n=10)
- One unusually large individual (82 mm TL) captured at RM 70.5 in Reach 3 exhibited morphological characteristics suggestive of *Gila cypha* ([[Humpback Chub]]) but was not positively identified — highlighting field identification challenges among sympatric [[Gila]] species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use:** Age-0 Colorado Squawfish primarily inhabit warm, low-velocity **backwater habitats** along the river margins; these serve as critical nursery habitat during their first year of life.
- **Sampling design:** Backwaters sampled every 5 river miles within each reach using 1/8-inch mesh beach seines (15 ft x 4 ft), two hauls per backwater, in fall (post-hatch aggregation) and spring (pre-runoff, before backwater inundation).
- **Thermal regime:** Backwater temperatures in Reach 3 ranged from as low as 5°C (spring) to 32°C (fall); main channel temperatures ranged 9-24°C (fall) and as low as 1°C (spring 1993). Backwaters consistently ran warmer than main channel, supporting their role as thermal refugia for young fish.
- **Flow regime:** Flows during spring 1993 sampling were notably elevated (4100-6900 cfs in Reach 3; 7400-7700 cfs in Reach 1) compared to prior years, reflecting high spring runoff which can inundate/eliminate backwater nursery habitat.
- **Sympatric species (Reach 3, Spring 1993, primary backwaters):** Colorado Squawfish were the **4th most abundant species**, dominated by non-native [[Red Shiner]] (mean CPE 69.30/10m²) and [[Sand Shiner]] (13.70/10m²), followed by [[Fathead Minnow]] (1.88/10m²), then Colorado Squawfish (0.31/10m²), [[Speckled Dace]] (0.60/10m²), [[Channel Catfish]] (0.02/10m²), and [[Black Bullhead]] (0.01/10m²). This underscores heavy numerical dominance of non-native cyprinids in nursery backwaters, a likely competitive/predatory pressure on native [[Colorado Squawfish]] recruitment.
- **Spatial distribution:** No significant downstream transport of age-0 fish was observed in Reach 3 between fall 1992 and spring 1993 sampling.

## Key Findings

**Reach 3 - Lower Green River (RM 120-0), 1992 year class:**
- Fall 1992: 119 age-0 squawfish captured; CPE = 0.77/100 m²
- Spring 1993: 88 age-0 squawfish captured (26% decline in raw numbers); CPE = 1.03/100 m² (not significantly different from fall, Student's t, P>0.05)
- Mean TL increased from 37 mm (fall) to 45 mm (spring)

**Reach 4 - Upper Green River (RM 320-215), 1992 year class:**
- Fall 1992: 180 age-0 squawfish captured; CPE = 1.29/100 m²
- Spring 1993: 71 age-0 squawfish captured (61% decline); CPE = 0.39/100 m² (70% decline)
- This reach showed the steepest CPE decline of the three reaches, suggesting substantially lower overwinter survival in the upper Green River relative to Reach 3.

**Reach 1 - Lower Colorado River (RM 50-0), 1992 year class:**
- Fall 1992: 103 age-0 squawfish captured; CPE = 0.84/100 m²
- Spring 1993: 18 age-0 squawfish captured (83% decline in raw numbers)
- Despite the sharp drop in total numbers, CPE actually *increased* by 9% to 0.92/100 m², likely reflecting reduced sampling effort/habitat availability (nursery/backwater area reduction) rather than improved survival — Spring 1993 data restricted to RM 35-0 only.
- Mean TL increased from 34 mm (fall) to 40 mm (spring).

**Cross-reach comparison:**
- All three reaches showed declining raw catch numbers from fall to spring, consistent with expected overwinter mortality in age-0 cohorts, though the magnitude varied considerably (26% in Reach 3, 61% in Reach 4, 83% in Reach 1).
- Growth in total length was consistent across reaches (6-8 mm mean TL increase fall-to-spring), indicating surviving individuals continued to grow through the winter period despite population declines.
- Methodology relies on **geometric mean CPE** (log-transformed, +1 correction for zero catches) per [[Sokal and Rohlf 1987]] to account for non-normal distribution of catch data in backwater seining, following protocols developed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's ISMP database management (C. McAda, pers. comm. 1992).
- Findings are preliminary; full integration with the broader ISMP dataset and Service-conducted overwinter survival analyses (including sympatric species catch rates for Reach 1) were slated for the Annual Report, January 1994.
