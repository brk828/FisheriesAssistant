# Development of the Chondrocranium in Mystus and Rita (Bagridae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bagridae

## Executive Summary

This 1957 morphological study by H. R. Srinivasachar describes the fully formed [[Chondrocranium]] (embryonic cartilaginous skull) of two catfishes of the family [[Bagridae]]: [[Mystus Vittatus]] (20 mm stage) and [[Rita]] sp. (12 mm stage). It is the second part of a series, following a study of [[Silonia]], [[Pangasius]] and [[Ailia]] (Schilbeidae). The author shows that the two bagrid genera differ in several cranial features and concludes that they should remain distinct genera. The paper argues against placing them in the genus *Pimelodus*, as [[Hamilton]] (1822) had proposed.

## Background & Description

**Scope and sample material.** The study is based on developing fry, not adults. Mystus vittatus was collected near Bangalore and Rita sp. came from the [[Mahanadi River]]. Earlier developmental stages could not be obtained.

**Specimen stages examined:**
- *Mystus vittatus*: a stage with head length 4.6 mm (described as a [[Wax Model Reconstruction]]), and a later stage described from [[Transverse Sections]]. The 20 mm stage is the main description, and a 22 mm stage is also mentioned.
- *Rita* sp.: Stage 1 (body length 9.0 mm, head length 1.5 mm, transverse sections) and Stage 2 (body length 12.0 mm, head length 2.0 mm, wax model).

**Methods.**
- Fixation in [[Bouin's Fluid]] and 4% formalin, then storage in 70% alcohol.
- Some older stages were decalcified.
- Sections were cut at 10 µm and stained with Mallory's triple stain, iron haematoxylin and Delafield haematoxylin.
- Cartilage was stained by a modified [[Van Wijhe Staining]] method, so the results could be compared with wax models.

**Description of the cranium (summary).** The chondrocranium is described by region:
- **Occipital region:** an occipital arch joined to the auditory capsules by a [[Tectum Posterius]] and [[Tectum Synoticum]]. The [[Vagus Nerve]] passes through the [[Fissura Metotica]].
- **Basal plate and notochord:** a massive basal plate with recesses for the sacculus and lagena, and a [[Basicapsular Fenestra]].
- **Auditory region:** large otic capsules with semicircular canal enlargements. A medial wall is absent except at the ends.
- **Orbitotemporal region:** dorsal orbital cartilages, trabeculae, a [[Sphenoid Fissure]], a [[Hypophysial Fenestra]] and an [[Epiphysial Bar]].
- **Ethmoid region:** an [[Ethmoid Plate]] with cornua, a solum nasi, a nasal septum and a [[Lamina Orbitonasalis]].
- **Visceral arches:** a [[Pterygoid Process]], Meckel's cartilage, a [[Hyomandibula]] with an [[Interhyal]], and five branchial arches. The fifth arch is reduced to ceratobranchials.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not study ecology, behaviour, population dynamics or water chemistry. Its only environmental information is the collection localities: [[Bangalore]] for Mystus and the Mahanadi River for Rita. Both are freshwater settings. The study treats the chondrocranium as a taxonomic and phylogenetic character. It compares the bagrids with other siluroids (catfishes), including [[Amiurus]], and with teleosts such as [[Salmo]], [[Gambusia]] and [[Ophicephalus]].

## Key Findings

1. **Mystus vittatus** has a highly modified ethmoid region. It has an extensive tectum (tegmen cranii) over the olfactory tract, a distinct nasal septum that extends back to about two-thirds of the ethmoid region, and a much-reduced olfactory foramen. The ophthalmic trigeminal branch passes through a groove and a foramen in the lamina orbitonasalis.
2. **Rita sp.** has a lamina precerebralis that forms the anterior cranial boundary. It lacks a distinct nasal septum and has a large olfactory foramen. It also has a small anterior [[Myodome]] and a small [[Taenia Tecti Medialis]].
3. **Cranial nerves:** in Mystus the glossopharyngeal nerve passes through its own foramen behind the anterior basicapsular fenestra. In Rita the glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves share a common [[Metotic Fissure]].
4. **Shared traits of both bagrids:**
   - A sphenoseptal commissure is absent.
   - No preoptic fontanelle is present.
   - The gap between the anterior and posterior pterygoid process is small.
   - An independent interhyal links the hyomandibula to the ceratohyal.
   - The hyomandibular branch of the facial nerve passes through a gap in front of the hyomandibula.
   - The foramen for the otic branch of the facial nerve (the lateral line nerve) is absent.
5. **Differences between the two genera:** Mystus has the extended tectum, the distinct nasal septum, the reduced olfactory foramen and the separate glossopharyngeal foramen. Rita has the lamina precerebralis, the absence of a nasal septum, the large olfactory foramen and the shared metotic fissure.
6. **Taxonomic conclusion:** the author concludes that Mystus and Rita should be kept as distinct genera within Bagridae. Placing them in [[Pimelodus]] is "questionable" until more species are studied.
7. **Comparative notes:** the lamina orbitonasalis, solum nasi and orbitonasal foramen are reduced in both bagrids compared with Silonia, Pangasius and Ailia. A basicapsular fenestra is present in both. The skull is platytrabic, meaning it has no interorbital septum. This is typical of many siluroids but differs from the tropitrabic condition in some teleosts.
