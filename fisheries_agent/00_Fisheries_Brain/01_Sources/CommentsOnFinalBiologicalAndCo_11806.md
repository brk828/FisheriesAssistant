# ESA Violation Notice: Lower Colorado River Operations and Endangered Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-law #cyprinidae #catostomidae #cyprinodontidae #rallidae #emydidae #phocoenidae #sciaenidae

## Executive Summary

This legal notice, dated July 3, 1997, from the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] serves dual purposes: (1) providing formal comment on the Final Biological Opinion for Lower Colorado River operations, and (2) notifying federal agencies of [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) violations relating to ongoing river operations and maintenance activities. The notice alleges that Reclamation's activities jeopardize multiple threatened and endangered species, that the Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) fails to remove jeopardy, and that agencies have failed to formally consult on effects to numerous listed species. The document serves as required 60-day notice under ESA Section 1540(g) for potential citizen suit litigation.

## Background & Description

The [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] requested formal ESA consultation on August 23, 1996, regarding effects of Lower Colorado River operations and maintenance on federally listed species including [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and critical habitat, [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and critical habitat, [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (*Rallus longirostris yumanensis*), and proposed [[Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard]] (*Phrynosoma mcallii*). The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] issued a Final Biological Opinion on April 30, 1997, determining that Reclamation's activities jeopardized the continued existence of bonytail, razorback, and flycatcher, and destroyed or adversely modified critical habitat for bonytail and razorback, but were not likely to adversely affect flat-tailed horned lizard or Yuma clapper rail.

The notice identifies additional species allegedly affected but not adequately addressed: [[Jaguar]], [[Jaguarundi]], [[Ocelot]], [[Vaquita]] (*Phocoena sinus*), [[Bald Eagle]], [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Desert Tortoise]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Totoaba]] (*Totoaba macdonaldi*), and [[Woundfin]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Lower Colorado River ecosystem has been fundamentally altered by dam operations, water diversions, and maintenance activities managed by Reclamation. These activities affect aquatic and riparian habitats from [[Hoover Dam]] and [[Lake Mead]] downstream through the [[Colorado River Delta]] in Mexico. Key ecological concerns include:

**Native Fish Habitat Loss**: Historic Lower Colorado River aquatic ecosystems supporting native fish have been nearly completely lost. According to Fish and Wildlife biologist Owen Goreman (May 8, 1997 memorandum): "the solution to removal of jeopardy and maintaining opportunity for future recovery rests in dam operations and river regulation. Fundamental changes in the operation of LCR dams and diversions are needed to avert extinction of these species."

**Non-Native Species Conflicts**: Sport fish stocking and maintenance of aquatic habitat conditions favor non-native fish over natives, creating ongoing conflicts with imperiled native species.

**Delta Dessication**: Diversion of the United States' entire Colorado River water entitlement has resulted in dessication of the [[Colorado River Delta]], affecting [[Gulf of California]] species including vaquita and totoaba. The Biological Assessment acknowledges "reduced Colorado River flows into the Gulf" as an actual or potential impact to vaquita, and that "reduced flow into the upper gulf may have contributed to an alteration of the vaquita's habitat."

**Cienega de Santa Clara Threats**: Operation of the [[Yuma Desalting Plant]] (YDP) at 1/3 capacity could result in loss of approximately 2,360 acres of [[Cienega de Santa Clara]] habitat occupied by Yuma clapper rail and desert pupfish.

**Cumulative Effects**: The notice emphasizes that "diversion and distribution of entitlement waters... (are) contemporaneous and future cumulative actions" requiring comprehensive analysis, including effects through [[Central Arizona Project]] water delivery facilitating non-native fish introduction to [[Gila River Basin]], and urban growth (e.g., Las Vegas expansion) affecting desert tortoise habitat.

## Key Findings

**RPA Inadequacy for Fish Species**: The notice alleges the RPA completely fails to remove jeopardy for bonytail chub, razorback sucker, and southwestern willow flycatcher. Specific deficiencies include:

1. No specific timeline or extent for bonytail augmentation efforts
2. Non-substantive requirements leaving conservation determinations to Reclamation's discretion
3. Construction of only three impoundments for native fish habitat insufficient to address continuing population declines
4. Funding for habitat preference studies rather than substantive action to reduce non-native fish prevalence
5. Studies where action implementation is left to Reclamation's "limited discretion"
6. Participation in habitat conservation planning (MSCP) that may remove jeopardy at unspecified future date
7. Provision of discretionary authority account described as "the most hollow and insincere stipulation"

**Consultation Scope Violations**: The notice identifies multiple instances where Reclamation allegedly failed to formally consult or adequately consider effects:

- **Yuma Clapper Rail**: "Not likely to adversely affect" determination based on illegally limited temporal scope (5 years), YDP analysis at only 1/3 capacity, and disregard of delta dessication
- **Gila River Basin Species**: Failed to consult on indirect and cumulative effects of [[Central Arizona Project]] operation and resulting delivery of non-native fish affecting Apache trout, desert pupfish, Gila topminnow, Gila trout, loach minnow (and critical habitat), razorback sucker (and critical habitat), spikedace (and critical habitat), woundfin
- **Gulf of California Species**: Failed to adequately consider effects of delta dessication on vaquita and totoaba, despite Biological Assessment acknowledging potential impacts. Reclamation claimed "no authority (or discretion) regarding the flow of water to the Colorado River delta," which Interior Solicitor Dave Gayer contradicted, stating consultation is required if species "may be affected, directly or indirectly"
- **Desert Pupfish in Salton Sink**: Failed to consider effects of [[All American Canal]] operation and non-native organism delivery
- **Desert Tortoise**: Failed to adequately consider indirect effects of quarry operations and cumulative impacts of agricultural and municipal development
- **Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard**: Illegally limited analysis to 5-year period, avoiding consideration of future well construction in 5-mile zone and extensive cumulative effects

**Legal Violations Alleged**: The notice asserts violations of:

- ESA Section 2 and 7 conservation mandates
- ESA Section 7 consultation requirements
- ESA Section 7 prohibition on irreversible or irretrievable commitment of resources during consultation
- ESA Section 9 prohibition on "take" of listed species, including habitat modification

**Agency Awareness of Inadequacy**: Internal documents reveal agencies understood RPA deficiencies. Fish and Wildlife note by Leslie Fitzpatrick (December 31, 1996) states: "Mike Young of SOL wants to take it to the God Squad and not fuss with an RPA," suggesting Interior Solicitors wanted to avoid controversy rather than require substantive management changes.

**Incidental Take Authorization Issues**: Because jeopardy and critical habitat destruction/adverse modification were not removed by the RPA, and because Reclamation had not formally agreed to implement the RPA, the notice argues formal consultation was not properly concluded and incidental take cannot be legally granted.

**Intent to Litigate**: The Southwest Center explicitly states intent to pursue litigation against Interior, Reclamation, Fish and Wildlife, and Marine Fisheries for these violations, with this notice serving as required 60-day notice under ESA citizen suit provisions.
