# A New Echinogammarus of the Berilloni Group: *E. aquilifer* nov. sp. from the Pyrenees (Crustacea, Amphipoda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 taxonomic note from the [[Zoological Museum Amsterdam]] (Bulletin Vol. 1 No. 11) by S. Pinkster describes a new freshwater amphipod, *Echinogammarus aquilifer* nov. sp., from the western Pyrenees. Based on topotypical material, the author provides a detailed redescription of [[Echinogammarus Berilloni]] (Catta, 1878) and a comparative description of the new species. The two species were collected in the same drainage, the Petite Nive, on the French and Spanish slopes. The paper argues that the new species differs from *E. berilloni* in a large number of characters, most notably in the setation of the abdomen, the second antenna, the mandibular palp, several gnathopod and pereopod features, the uropod and telson.

Note: the document is an amphipod taxonomy paper, not a fish study. The ichthyology tags reflect the required template; the organisms are crustaceans.

## Background & Description

- **Motivation:** Considerable morphological variability in the *E. berilloni* group had been noted by [[Spandl, 1926]] and [[Margalef, 1956]]. No adequate description or reliable figures of *E. berilloni* existed, and the type material collected by Berillon near Mount Mondarrain (750 m) could not be located.
- **Approach:** The author used specimens from the same drainage as the type locality. Published figures from Catta (1878), Chevreux (1896), Spandl (1926), Margalef (1956) and Hoffmann (1963) were consulted as recognition aids.
- **Topotypes of *E. berilloni*:** 54 specimens (males and females) from the Petite Nive, 1 km south of St. Jean-Pied-de-Port, France. Maximum length 12 mm in this material, up to 18 mm elsewhere.
- **Diagnostic features of *E. berilloni*:** dense clusters of long, curved setae covering the last 6 or 7 body segments; a dorsal hump on urosome segments 1 and 2; a second antenna lacking calceoli; a mandibular palp with 5 terminal setae on segment 3; and 7 spines plus a medial palmar spine on the first gnathopod hand.
- **Type material of *E. aquilifer*:** 29 specimens (males and females) from the Petite Nive about 5 km south of Valcarlos, Navarra, Spain, including one holotype male, one allotype female and 27 paratypes, deposited at ZMA Amph. 102.126–102.128. *E. berilloni* occurred alongside it.
- **Etymology:** *aquilifer* (Latin, "standard-bearer"), referring to the flag-like brush of long setae on the second antenna flagellum.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** present in both species. In females, setation on P5–P7 is strongly reduced, and gnathopod palms are almost transverse.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Habitat is small, fast-flowing montane streams of the Petite Nive drainage in the western Pyrenees.
- Both species were collected together, so sympatry is documented at the Spanish site.
- The paper notes that earlier distribution work (e.g., [[Vandel, 1926]]; [[Pacaud, 1952]]) addressed the regional distribution of *E. berilloni* and [[Gammarus Pulex]] in France. Ecological parameters such as water chemistry or flow are not reported.

## Key Findings

1. **A new species is recognized.** *E. aquilifer* differs from *E. berilloni* in many characters and is described as new.
2. **Abdomen and second antenna:** *E. aquilifer* has an almost naked abdomen (only small tufts of setae), whereas *E. berilloni* is densely setose. The second antenna of *E. aquilifer* bears a flag-like brush of long setae; *E. berilloni* lacks this.
3. **Mandibular palp:** segment 3 has 4 terminal setae (2 plumose) in *E. aquilifer* versus 5 (3 plumose) in *E. berilloni*. The spinule row is regular and comb-like in the new species.
4. **Gnathopods:** in *E. aquilifer* the first gnathopod medial palmar spine is not separated from the angle spines, and the second gnathopod lacks a medial palmar spine (a group of 6 spines instead). The second gnathopod hand is about 1.5 times as long as wide, versus more than twice in *E. berilloni*.
5. **Pereopods and uropods:** the 5th and 6th legs have reduced setal groups in *E. aquilifer*; the 7th leg lacks the long setae characteristic of *E. berilloni*. The inner ramus of uropod 3 is about 2.5 times as long as wide in the new species, versus 1.5–2 times in *E. berilloni*.
6. **Telson and urosome:** the telson in *E. aquilifer* carries more setae around the terminal spines, and urosome segments 1 and 2 lack the dorsal hump.
7. **Female telson:** a female *E. aquilifer* telson resembles a male *E. berilloni* telson.
8. **Conclusion:** the author states that at least one form, *E. aquilifer*, must be considered a new species, and that further data were still being collected.
