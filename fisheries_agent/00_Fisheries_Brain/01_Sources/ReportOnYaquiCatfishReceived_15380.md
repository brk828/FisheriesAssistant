# Identification of Ictalurus Specimens from Dexter National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[University of Michigan]] [[Museum of Zoology]] Division of Fishes provides expert identification of five catfish specimens submitted by the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in [[New Mexico]]. The analysis confirms hybridization between [[Yaqui Catfish]] ([[Ictalurus pricei]]) and [[Channel Catfish]] ([[Ictalurus punctatus]]) in the hatchery stock, based on detailed morphometric and meristic characters including vertebral counts, anal ray counts, gill raker counts, and skeletal features.

## Background & Description

Five specimens were submitted for identification on January 20, 1989, including one decomposed specimen. The specimens were examined using standard ichthyological techniques including vertebral counts, anal fin ray counts, gill raker counts, pectoral fin spine characteristics, and skeletal morphology. One adult was preserved and four were prepared as skeletons for detailed osteological analysis.

[[Ictalurus pricei]] (Yaqui Catfish) typically exhibits 46-48 vertebrae and 23-26 anal rays. [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (Channel Catfish) in Mexico typically has 48-51 vertebrae and 26-32 anal rays. These meristic characters serve as diagnostic features for species identification and hybrid detection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimens originated from hatchery stock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], raising concerns about genetic integrity of the [[Yaqui Catfish]] population. The presence of hybrids indicates contamination of the pure [[Ictalurus pricei]] breeding stock with [[Channel Catfish]] genetics. The [[Yaqui Basin]] was surveyed in 1978, providing reference skeletal material for comparison with the submitted specimens.

## Key Findings

**Confirmed Pure Yaqui Catfish (1 specimen):**
- The decomposed specimen was confidently identified as [[Ictalurus pricei]]
- 26 anal rays
- 47 total vertebrae
- Small pectoral fin-ray serrations (many bifurcate)
- Coronoid process of dentary posterior in position
- Skeletal morphology matches reference specimens from 1978 [[Yaqui Basin]] survey

**Confirmed Channel Catfish (1 specimen):**
- 51 vertebrae
- Very long dorsal and pectoral spines
- Only 16 gill rakers
- Supraoccipital notched and strongly joined to supraneural spine
- 27 anal rays
- Many small spots over sides of body
- Identified as [[Ictalurus punctatus]], possibly not 100% pure but functionally a Channel Catfish

**Hybrid Specimens (3 specimens):**
- Display various combinations of [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Yaqui Catfish]] characters
- Cannot be confidently identified as either pure species
- Show evidence of F₂ hybridization and backcrossing
- Meristic counts: 27-28 anal rays, 48 (possibly one 47) vertebrae, 18-22 gill rakers
- One specimen closer to [[Ictalurus punctatus]], one closer to [[Ictalurus pricei]], one nearly intermediate

**Management Implications:**
The hatchery manager's concern about stock contamination is confirmed. The presence of hybrids and one apparent pure [[Channel Catfish]] indicates a significant problem with the genetic integrity of the [[Yaqui Catfish]] breeding program.
