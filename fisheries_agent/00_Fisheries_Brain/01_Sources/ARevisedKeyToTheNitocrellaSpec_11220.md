# A Revised Key to the Nitocrella Species of the Hirta-Group, with Description of Nitocrella maggii n.sp. from Lesbos, Greece

#stygobiology #copepoda #harpacticoida #ameiridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by Giuseppe Lucio Pesce (Bulletin Zoologisch Museum Universiteit van Amsterdam, Vol. 9 No. 12, 1983) describes a new harpacticoid copepod, [[Nitocrella Maggii]], from phreatic groundwater on the island of [[Lesbos]], Greece. The species is assigned to the [[Nitocrella Hirta-Group]] (sensu Petkovski, 1976), and the paper provides a revised dichotomous key to the group's females. The find extends the known eastward range of the genus *Nitocrella* in Greece and suggests the genus may be more widespread in Greek groundwater than previously recorded.

## Background & Description

The material came from a stygobiological survey of Lesbos carried out in July 1982 by the Zoological Institute of the University of L'Aquila (Italy). Besides harpacticoids, the survey recorded cyclopid copepods, asellid and microparasellid isopods, ostracods and amphipods.

The hirta-group is characterised by small body size, reduced setation on the endopod of the fourth and fifth legs, and a distinctive armature on the terminal exopod segment of a leg. Since Petkovski's 1976 key, two further species have been described: [[Nitocrella Juturna]] (Italy) and [[Nitocrella Skyrensis]] (Greece). The revised key therefore covers the group's expanded membership.

**Morphology of N. maggii (females; male unknown)**

- Body length 0.40-0.48 mm, excluding antennae, antennules and furcal setae.
- Body slender, with long spinules along the posterior margin of each abdominal segment and minute spinules on the genital, post-genital and terminal somites.
- Anal operculum armed with 8-9 stout spines.
- Caudal rami longer than wide (L/W = 1.50-1.56), each with two principal terminal setae, one inner distal seta and one long dorsal seta.
- Antennule 8-segmented; antenna exopod 1-segmented with 3 setae.
- Fifth leg: basiendopod fused and scarcely protruding, armed with 2 long barbed spines; exopod longer than wide (L/W = 2.0-2.1) with 4 slender setae.
- Fourth leg: endopod very short, with one apical seta on the second segment.

**Type material**

- Holotype female, Zoological Institute, University of L'Aquila, No. HLB.28.1.
- Three paratypes, same collection, Nos. HLB.28.2-4.
- Two paratypes dissected and mounted on coverslips in Faure's medium, in the Zoologisch Museum Amsterdam.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality:** a fresh-water well at Moria, Mytilene, eastern Lesbos, collected 28 July 1982 by Pesce, Maggi and Silverii.

**Physico-chemical conditions recorded at the well:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Well depth | 2.5 m |
| Water level | 0.5 m |
| Water temperature | 14.1 °C |
| pH | 6.9 |
| Bottom sediment | Organogenic sandstone |

**Associated fauna:** other harpacticoids ([[Elaphoidella]] sp. and [[Attheyella Crassa]]), the cyclopid [[Diacyclops Antrincola]], microparasellid isopods of the genera [[Microcharon]] and *Microparasellus*, amphipods, ostracods, water mites, gastropods and oligochaetes. This assemblage is typical of [[Phreatic Groundwater]] and [[Stygobiont Fauna]].

**Distribution:** the hirta-group shows a continuous geographical distribution across Italy and the Balkan area. Its member species occur in phreatic, cave, spring and interstitial waters of Italy, Yugoslavia, Hungary, Bulgaria, Austria, Romania, Greece and Turkey. ## Key Findings

1. **New species described.** *Nitocrella maggii* n.sp. is distinguished from its relatives by the fused basiendopod of the fifth leg, the shortness of all setae on the fifth-leg exopod, the small fourth-leg endopod, and the leg-four endopod armature. Its terminal exopod segment bears two setae and two spines, placing it in the hirta-group.
2. **Closest relatives** within the group are *N. juturna* (central Italy), *N. hirta* (Balkans, Central Europe) and *N. calcaripes* (Romania).
3. **Range extension.** The find extends the genus's known occurrence in Greece eastward and suggests *Nitocrella* may be more widespread in Greek phreatic waters than previously thought.
4. **Revised key.** The key separates eleven taxa of the hirta-group (including *N. hirta tirolensis*) using the setation of the fourth and fifth-leg exopods and endopods, the anal operculum, and the furcal ramus proportions. Furcal rami about as long as wide separate *N. skyrensis*, and rami about twice as long as wide separate *N. psammophila*.
5. **Phylogeny unresolved.** Males are unknown, so the phylogenetic relationships of the new species remain provisional until males are collected.
6. **Etymology.** The species is named after Dr. Domenico Maggi, who collected it.
