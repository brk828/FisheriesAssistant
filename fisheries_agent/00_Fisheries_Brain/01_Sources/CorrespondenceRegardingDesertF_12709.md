## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque, NM) to Dr. Samuel Ocaña García, Director General of the [[Centro Ecológico de Sonora]], regarding a personnel vacancy on the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]. The letter is not a primary scientific data source but provides important institutional and conservation-management context for binational efforts to recover imperiled desert fish species along the U.S.–Mexico border. It requests appointment of a new Mexican representative (Alejandro Varela-Romero) to fill a seat vacated by Lourdes Juárez-Romero, formerly of the Centro's Área de Ecología Acuática. No morphological, taxonomic, or ecological data on specific fish species are presented in this text.

While no specific species or habitat data are detailed, the letter references a broader ecological and conservation context:

## Key Findings

- The **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** requires a Mexican fisheries biologist representative from the Centro Ecológico de Sonora, historically filling this seat since at least 1986.
- **Lourdes Juárez-Romero** resigned her position with the Centro's Área de Ecología Acuática, creating the vacancy.
- **Alejandro Varela-Romero** is recommended as her replacement, based on his prior collaborative work with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Arizona Game and Fish Department on the biology and management of species under Team jurisdiction.
- The Team meets annually (2-day meetings) in Phoenix, Arizona, with travel and per diem costs covered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service; the next meeting was planned for **January 1992**.
- **Dr. W.L. Minckley** (Department of Zoology, Arizona State University) is identified as the Desert Fishes Recovery Team Leader, a notable figure in [[Desert Fish Conservation]] and [[Sonoran Desert]] ichthyofauna research.
- This correspondence exemplifies the binational, institutional cooperation model necessary for managing and recovering desert fish species whose ranges cross the U.S.–Mexico border, particularly within the [[Sonoran Desert]] ecoregion.
