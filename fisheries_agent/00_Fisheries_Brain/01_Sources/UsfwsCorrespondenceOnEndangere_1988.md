# 19778 — USFWS Correspondence on Endangered Fish Rearing Program

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1988 administrative letter from James E. Johnson (Chief, Division of Endangered Species, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region 2) to Dr. Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University), responding to a proposal to use ASU golf course ponds as auxiliary rearing habitat for three federally endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish|Colorado squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker|razorback sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and [[Bonytail Chub|bonytail chub]] (*Gila elegans*). The letter approves the concept, coordinates logistics with [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and outlines stocking numbers and timing constraints. While not a primary scientific research paper, it provides valuable historical documentation of early conservation aquaculture strategies for these critically endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] endemics.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body plan) are presented in this source. The document is purely administrative/logistical in nature. For morphological detail on these taxa, refer to dedicated species accounts:
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] — largest North American cyprinid, elongated body, piscivorous adult morphology.
- [[Razorback Sucker]] — distinctive dorsal keel ("razorback") predorsal ridge, adaptation debated (predator avoidance/hydrodynamic hypothesis).
- [[Bonytail Chub]] — slender, streamlined body adapted to swift-water riverine habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native habitat**: All three species are native to the mainstem and tributaries of the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically occupying big-river riverine habitats with variable flow, turbidity, and temperature regimes.
- **Conservation context**: By 1988, wild populations of all three species were in steep decline due to habitat alteration (damming, flow regulation, thermal changes) and competition/predation from introduced non-native fishes — driving their listing under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Artificial rearing niche**: The letter documents a strategy of using **golf course ponds** at Arizona State University as a non-traditional, human-made "refugial" habitat to grow out hatchery-produced fish before release, supplementing capacity at the federal [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, NM) — an early example of *ex situ* conservation aquaculture and habitat surrogacy for endangered riverine fish.
- **Distribution logistics**: Fish were part of an established interstate hatchery-to-field distribution pipeline, normally transported from Dexter, NM to Arizona via truck runs in September–October.

## Key Findings
- **Interagency coordination**: USFWS Division of Endangered Species approved and coordinated a collaborative rearing arrangement between [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], ASU, and the Phoenix Ecological Services Field Office.
- **Stocking targets**: Approximately **1,500 individuals each** of Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker, and bonytail chub were planned for stocking into the ASU ponds, with numbers subject to variation based on survival and hatchery production rates.
- **Timing constraint**: Due to competing operational needs at Dexter NFH, fish for the ASU ponds needed to be delivered earlier than the standard November distribution schedule, aligning instead with the normal September–October Arizona distribution run.
- **Logistical requirement**: ASU was responsible for arranging pickup/meeting the distribution truck along its established route, coordinated directly with hatchery staff ("Buddy" Jensen at Dexter).
- **Historical significance**: This correspondence documents an early (1988) example of using non-traditional infrastructure (golf course ponds) as supplemental grow-out habitat in the recovery programs for three of the Colorado River's most imperiled endemic fishes, reflecting the resource constraints and adaptive management approaches used in early endangered fish recovery efforts.
