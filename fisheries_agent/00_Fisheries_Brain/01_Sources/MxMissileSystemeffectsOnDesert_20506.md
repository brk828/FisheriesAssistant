# MX Missile System Threat to Nevada and Utah Desert Fishes

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #endemic-species #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1981 correspondence from the Desert Fishes Council Chairman alerts members to grave threats posed by the proposed MX Missile System to aquatic ecosystems throughout central and southern Nevada and west-central Utah. The $33 billion Department of Defense project would construct 200 racetrack loops with 23 missile launch sites each, 5,000-8,000 miles of highways, extensive railroad infrastructure, and communities for 10,000-15,000 workers across 30-40 desert valleys. The project threatens numerous endemic fish species through groundwater removal, habitat destruction, and increased human population impacts. With construction planned for early 1982 and the Air Force considering exemption from environmental laws, immediate congressional action is urgently requested.

## Background & Description

The MX Missile System represents what has been called the largest public works project ever attempted by mankind. Each of the 200 racetrack loops would span 20-30 miles with missile launch sites spaced every 7,000 feet. Supporting infrastructure includes extensive highway and railroad networks, plus three worker communities with schools, warehouses, churches, and businesses typical of western towns. The entire system has a 20-year life expectancy, after which all facilities would be completely abandoned.

Primary construction and municipal water sources would draw from groundwater systems in valley floors throughout the project area. Test facility construction at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]] has been authorized and is proceeding, though Congress has not completely authorized funding for the full project.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The affected region contains extraordinary endemic fish diversity concentrated in isolated desert spring and valley systems. These groundwater-dependent habitats support numerous subspecies of [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) and [[Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor*), along with rare springfish (*Crenichthys* spp.), the [[Moapa Dace]] (*Moapa coriacea*), multiple spinedace species (*Lepidomeda* spp.), and the [[Relict Dace]] (*Relictus solitarius*).

The [[Great Basin Desert]] aquatic systems face multiple threat vectors: direct habitat destruction from construction, groundwater depletion, nuclear waste disposal site impacts, and secondary effects from increased human population including off-road vehicle recreation and other recreational abuses. Many affected species are already rare or possibly extinct (e.g., [[Grass Valley Speckled Dace]], [[Pahranagat Spinedace]]).

The [[Death Valley System]] species, including the critically endangered [[Devils Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), face secondary impacts despite being more distantly removed from direct construction zones.

## Key Findings

**Fish Directly Influenced by MX (Nevada):**

- **[[Big Smoky Valley]]**: *Rhinichthys osculus lariversi*, two undescribed *Gila bicolor* subspecies
- **[[Grass Valley]]**: *R. osculus reliquus* (possibly extinct)
- **[[Diamond Valley]]**: aberrant forms of [[Lahontan Tui Chub]] and [[Lahontan Speckled Dace]]
- **[[Monitor Valley]]**: aberrant form of Monitor Valley speckled dace
- **[[Newark Valley]]**: *Gila bicolor newarkensis*
- **[[Fish Creek Springs]]**: *Gila bicolor euchilla*
- **[[Clover Valley]]**: *Rhinichthys osculus oligoporus*
- **[[Independence Valley]]**: *R. osculus lethoporus*, *Gila bicolor isolata*
- **[[Railroad Valley]]**: Locke's Ranch springfish, [[Duckwater Springfish]], eight undescribed *Gila bicolor* subspecies
- **Steptoe, Goshute, Butte, and Ruby valleys**: *Relictus solitarius*
- **[[White River Valley]]**: Preston springfish, White River speckled dace, [[White River Mountainsucker]] (*Pantosteus intermedius*), [[White River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda albivalis*)
- **[[Pahranagat Valley]]**: Pahranagat roundtail chub (*Gila robusta jordani*), Pahranagat spinedace (possibly extinct), southern springfish
- **[[Muddy Valley]]**: [[Moapa Dace]] (*Moapa coriacea*), *Gila robusta* ssp.
- **[[Reese River Valley]]**: [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*)
- **[[Meadow Valley Wash]]**: Big Spring spinedace (*Lepidomeda mollispinis pratensis*)
- **[[Spring Valley]]**: refuge populations of [[Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos latos*) and relict dace

**Fish Directly Influenced by MX (Utah):**

- Throughout mid-west portion: [[Least Chub]] (*Iotichthys phlegethontis*)

**Species Secondarily Influenced:**

- **[[Death Valley System]]**: [[Devils Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), multiple [[Amargosa Pupfish]] subspecies (*C. nevadensis* ssp.), [[Amargosa Speckled Dace]]
- **[[Black Rock Desert]]**: [[Desert Dace]] (*Eremichthys acros*)
- **[[Summit Lake Area]]**: Lahontan cutthroat trout

**Conservation Actions Requested:**

1. Write Congress and the President questioning selection of this environmentally unsound defense alternative
2. Demand Congress ignore Air Force petition to exempt MX from environmental laws

**Critical Timeline Concerns:**

- Inadequate time allotted for appropriate biological data collection
- Construction planned for early 1982
- Air Force considering exemption from all environmental laws
- Test facility construction already proceeding at Vandenberg AFB
