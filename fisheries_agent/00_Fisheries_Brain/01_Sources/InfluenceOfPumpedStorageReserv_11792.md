# Influence of Pumped Storage Reservoir Operation on Primary Productivity and Water Quality in the Salt River Reservoirs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #phytoplankton

## Executive Summary

This research proposal, submitted to the [[Salt River Project]] by Arizona State University's Department of Botany and Microbiology, requests a 24-month study of four [[Salt River Lakes]] (Roosevelt, Apache, Canyon and Saguaro) to document the phytoplankton, primary productivity, and trace element chemistry of reservoirs before and during [[Pumped Storage]] operation. The study is designed to run alongside an on-going [[Commercial Fisheries Study]] led by W. L. Minckley, sharing sampling schedules, equipment and data. The proposal was revised after the Salt River Project requested additional data, adding study areas and sampling sites. The proposed start date is September 1, 1970.

## Background & Description

- **Principal Investigator:** Milton R. Sommerfeld, Assistant Professor, Department of Botany and Microbiology, Arizona State University, Tempe.
- **Department head:** James E. Canright. **Institutional administrative official:** W. J. Burke, Vice President.
- **Proposed starting date:** September 1, 1970; **duration:** 24 months.
- **Revision history:** An earlier version was submitted July 31, 1970. The revision followed Salt River Project requests for more data and tighter coordination with the fisheries work.
- **Objectives:**
  1. Determine the phytoplankton composition of the biomass.
  2. Measure primary productivity.
  3. Obtain field data on trace element composition and behavior.
  4. Establish reliable biological (phytoplankton) indicators of productivity and water quality.
  5. Collect baseline field data with the Commercial Fisheries Study so that pumped-storage effects can be evaluated.

The document is a proposal, so it contains no measured results. Its rationale is that field data on the existing aquatic environment are scarce, and must exist before ecological effects of the pumped-storage system can be judged.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stratification and the pump-back mechanism.** Earlier evidence (refs 2, 4, 5) indicates the Salt River lakes stratify in summer. Apache and Roosevelt lakes have thermoclines reaching 10–15 m. The authors reason that water pumped back into Canyon and Apache lakes will enter below the thermocline and may cause destratification. In temperate and subtropical lakes, summer destratification could considerably increase phytoplankton productivity by making bottom nutrients available throughout the water column.

**Trace elements.** The authors note that relatively minute changes in specific trace elements may drastically alter the type and number of phytoplankton (refs 7, 8). Possible outcomes include enhanced overall productivity and fish production, or unfavorable effects such as large noxious algal blooms and increased turbidity.

**Knowledge gap.** Few studies (refs 9, 11) address pump-back effects on water quality and productivity. Existing results may not transfer to the Salt River system because climate, water chemistry and biota differ from previously studied areas.

**Input sources.** Roosevelt Lake receives inflow from the Salt River and Tonto Creek. These inputs shape trace element levels and phytoplankton flora in Roosevelt Lake and, downstream, in the lower lakes. The authors state that the flora of the individual lakes differs considerably.

**Monitoring rationale.** Chemical monitoring only detects what is sought, whereas the aquatic ecosystem responds to all changes. Reliable biological indicators could therefore anticipate and evaluate change and predict its rate.

## Key Findings

The proposal makes the following main points and plans (no new data are reported):

1. **Proposed trace element and gas analysis:** Dissolved trace elements (Al, B, Co, Cr, Cu, F, Fe, Mn, Mo, N as NH3/NO3, Ni, P, Si, V, Zn) and dissolved gases (CO2, H2S), not analyzed by the fisheries project, will be measured photometrically and/or by atomic absorption spectrometry.
2. **Primary productivity methods:** Phytoplankton standing crop, chlorophyll per unit water volume, and photosynthesis measured by oxygen evolution or ¹⁴C uptake of natural populations.
3. **Taxonomic work:** Phytoplankton species and relative abundance will be correlated with chemical, physical and biological parameters to identify indicator organisms. Prior work (Olsen and Sommerfeld) documented planktonic diatoms of central Arizona.
4. **Study scope:** Although Canyon, Apache and Saguaro lakes will be affected most, all four lakes are to be studied for full coordination with the fisheries work.
5. **Sampling plan:** Dates, sites and depths will match the fisheries study. Fisheries staff sample intensively for two weeks every two to three months; this proposal plans at least monthly sampling, and more often during periods of intense biological activity.
6. **Shared resources and duration:** The project should run concurrently with the remaining two years of the fisheries study (which runs through 30 June 1972). Equipment and data will be shared, and reporting is to be joint where possible to avoid duplication.
7. **Comparative coverage (Table I):** The proposed project covers trace elements, dissolved gases, primary production and physical parameters. The fisheries project covers macroelements, zooplankton, benthic algae, and fishes, clams and crayfish (the latter marked as studied for vertical, horizontal and seasonal distribution). Together they cover the full food web.
8. **Data policy:** Data will be exchanged as acquired and published in the open literature after granting-agency approval, with appropriate acknowledgments.
9. **Cost rationale:** The two projects together are expected to yield "a vast amount of information for a far lower outlay of project monies."
