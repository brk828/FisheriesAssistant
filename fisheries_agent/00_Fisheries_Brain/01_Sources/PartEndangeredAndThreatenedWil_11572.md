# Title 50 CFR Part 17—Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#endangered-species #threatened-species #wildlife-regulation #plant-regulation #federal-register

## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory compilation from Title 50 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), specifically Part 17, which establishes the official lists of endangered and threatened wildlife and plants under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Published as a special reprint current to the date shown on the cover, it provides comprehensive listings maintained by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]]. The document includes detailed taxonomic information, historic ranges, listing status (Endangered, Threatened, or similarity of appearance), critical habitat designations, and special rules for hundreds of species across multiple taxonomic groups including mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, invertebrates, and plants. It serves as the authoritative reference for species protection under federal law.

## Background & Description

The regulatory framework establishes standardized listing procedures under §17.11 (wildlife) and §17.12 (plants). Species are identified by scientific name (the legally binding identifier) and common name, with status indicated as "E" (Endangered), "T" (Threatened), "E(S/A)" or "T(S/A)" (similarity of appearance), "XE" (essential experimental population), or "XN" (nonessential experimental population). The document relies on the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] for animals and [[International Code of Botanical Nomenclature]] for plants.

Historic range indicates known general distribution as reported in current scientific literature, though present distribution may be greatly reduced. The listing of a taxon includes all lower taxonomic units (e.g., listing a genus includes all species and subspecies). Geographic populations of vertebrate species may be listed separately with differing status based on defined boundaries.

Critical habitat and special rules columns cross-reference other sections in Parts 17, 222, 226, or 227. "NA" (not applicable) indicates no special rules or critical habitat for that species, though all other appropriate regulations still apply. The document includes port-of-entry requirements and other federal, state, and local regulations that may apply.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listings span diverse ecosystems and taxonomic groups across U.S. territories and possessions:

**Mammals**: Includes marine mammals ([[Hawaiian Monk Seal]], [[West Indian Manatee]], multiple whale species), terrestrial carnivores ([[Florida Panther]], [[Red Wolf]], [[Grizzly Bear]]), ungulates ([[Sonoran Pronghorn]], [[Key Deer]]), bats ([[Indiana Bat]], [[Mexican Long-nosed Bat]]), and rodents (multiple kangaroo rat species).

**Birds**: Encompasses seabirds ([[Short-tailed Albatross]], [[California Least Tern]]), raptors ([[Bald Eagle]], [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[California Condor]]), waterfowl ([[Hawaiian Goose]], [[Laysan Duck]]), and Hawaiian honeycreepers (multiple species).

**Reptiles**: Includes crocodilians ([[American Crocodile]], [[American Alligator]]), sea turtles, and terrestrial species ([[San Francisco Garter Snake]], multiple iguana species).

**Fishes**: Covers freshwater species ([[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], multiple darters and pupfish), anadromous salmonids ([[Chinook Salmon]], [[Sockeye Salmon]]), and marine species.

**Invertebrates**: Lists freshwater mussels (multiple pearly mussel species), snails, insects (butterflies, beetles), arachnids, and crustaceans.

**Plants**: Encompasses diverse families including cacti, orchids, palms, ferns, and flowering plants across terrestrial and aquatic habitats.

## Key Findings

**Regulatory Structure**: The document establishes that species may be listed based on five factors: (1) habitat destruction/modification, (2) overutilization, (3) disease or predation, (4) inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms, or (5) other natural or manmade factors. Delisting may occur due to extinction, recovery, or original data error.

**Geographic Distribution**: Listings include species from all 50 states, U.S. territories ([[Puerto Rico]], [[U.S. Virgin Islands]], [[Guam]], [[American Samoa]]), and Pacific islands ([[Hawaii]], [[Palau]]). Many species have highly restricted ranges (e.g., single islands, specific river systems, isolated springs).

**Taxonomic Representation**: The wildlife list includes extensive coverage of vertebrates (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes) and selected invertebrates. The plant list covers multiple families with particular emphasis on Hawaiian endemics, desert species, and southeastern U.S. flora.

**Status Designations**: Species are designated as Endangered (facing extinction throughout all or significant portion of range) or Threatened (likely to become endangered in foreseeable future). Some populations of wide-ranging species receive different status designations based on geography (e.g., [[Gray Wolf]] endangered in lower 48 states except Minnesota, where threatened).

**Critical Habitat**: Designated for selected species with specific geographic coordinates and habitat descriptions provided in cross-referenced sections. Many species lack designated critical habitat (indicated by "NA").

**Experimental Populations**: Certain reintroduced populations are designated as experimental (essential or nonessential) with modified regulatory protections to facilitate recovery efforts (e.g., [[Red Wolf]] in North Carolina/Tennessee, [[Black-footed Ferret]] in Wyoming).

**Removed Species**: The document includes a list of species removed from protection, with reasons including extinction ([[Tecopa Pupfish]], [[Longjaw Cisco]]), recovery, or original data error ([[Mexican Duck]], [[Pine Barrens Treefrog]]).

**Federal Register Citations**: Each listing includes "When listed" citations to Federal Register publications documenting the scientific basis and effective dates, with footnote numbers running sequentially across both wildlife and plant lists since 1973.
