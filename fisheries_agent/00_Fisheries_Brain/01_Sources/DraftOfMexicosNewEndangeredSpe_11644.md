# Mexican Official Standard NOM-CRN-001-ECOL/1993 on Endangered, Threatened, Rare and Specially Protected Species

#conservation #endangered-species #Mexican-biodiversity #regulatory-document #flora-fauna-lists

## Executive Summary

This document is a draft Mexican Official Standard (Norma Oficial Mexicana NOM-CRN-001-ECOL/1993) issued by the National Institute of Ecology (Instituto Nacional de Ecología) establishing comprehensive lists of terrestrial and aquatic flora and fauna species and subspecies classified as endangered (P), threatened (A), rare (R), or subject to special protection (Pr). The standard was developed following public consultation procedures and includes specifications for the protection, use, and management of listed species and their habitats. Endemic species to [[Mexico]] and federal jurisdiction waters are specifically identified with asterisks (*). The document represents a foundational regulatory framework for [[Mexican Wildlife Conservation]] and biodiversity protection.

## Background & Description

The standard was developed under the authority of the [[Ley General del Equilibrio Ecológico y la Protección al Ambiente]] (General Law of Ecological Equilibrium and Environmental Protection) and related federal legislation. The project was published for public comment on August 2, 1993, in the Diario Oficial de la Federación, with a 90-day comment period. Numerous Mexican and international institutions participated in its development, including UNAM institutes, conservation organizations, government agencies (SARH, SEDESOL, SEMIP, Secretaría de Pesca), universities, and NGOs such as [[Ducks Unlimited de Mexico]], [[Grupo de los 100]], and international bodies.

The standard applies four conservation categories based on population status, distribution, and threats: (1) **Endangered (P)**: species whose distribution or population size has been drastically reduced, threatening biological viability; (2) **Threatened (A)**: species that could become endangered if deteriorating factors continue; (3) **Rare (R)**: naturally scarce species with viable populations but restricted distributions or specialized habitat requirements; (4) **Special Protection (Pr)**: species subject to permanent or temporary harvest bans or limitations to promote recovery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The standard addresses both terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems across [[Mexico]], recognizing the country's exceptional biodiversity. Listed taxa span multiple kingdoms and phyla, including vascular plants, fungi, mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, and invertebrates (particularly mollusks and crustaceans). Marine species include sea turtles ([[Chelonia mydas]], [[Eretmochelys imbricata]], [[Caretta caretta]], [[Lepidochelys kempi]]), marine mammals ([[Balaenoptera]], [[Megaptera novaeangliae]]), sharks ([[Carcharodon carcharias]], [[Rhincodon typus]]), and reef-building corals ([[Acropora cervicornis]], [[Acropora palmata]]).

Freshwater ecosystems are represented by endemic fish genera including [[Chirostoma]] (silversides), [[Cyprinodon]] (pupfish), [[Notropis]] (shiners), and [[Xiphophorus]] (swordtails and platys), many restricted to single springs or lake systems. Terrestrial habitats include tropical forests (supporting [[Tapirus bairdii]], [[Panthera onca]], numerous parrots and toucans), temperate forests ([[Ursus arctos]], [[Canis lupus baileyi]], endemic salamanders), deserts ([[Gopherus agassizii]], [[Antilocapra americana]], numerous cacti), and coastal zones.

## Key Findings

**Flora**: The standard lists hundreds of plant species across major families. [[Cactaceae]] dominates with extensive listings of [[Mammillaria]], [[Coryphantha]], [[Echinocereus]], [[Ferocactus]], and [[Turbinicarpus]] species, most endemic and rare (R*). [[Zamiaceae]] (cycads) includes all [[Ceratozamia]], [[Dioon]], and [[Zamia]] species, predominantly endangered or threatened. [[Orchidaceae]] lists numerous genera including [[Laelia]], [[Encyclia]], [[Epidendrum]], [[Barkeria]], and [[Cattleya]]. [[Pinaceae]] includes multiple [[Pinus]] and [[Abies]] species under special protection. [[Palmae]] lists many [[Chamaedorea]] species and [[Brahea]] palms. Edible fungi ([[Agaricaceae]], [[Boletaceae]], [[Morchelaceae]]) are included under special protection (Pr).

**Mammals**: 186 mammalian taxa are listed. Primates include [[Alouatta palliata]] (P*), [[Alouatta pigra]] (P), and [[Ateles geoffroyi]] subspecies (A to P). Large carnivores: [[Panthera onca]] (P), [[Panthera onca veraecrucis]] (P*), [[Ursus arctos]] (P), [[Canis lupus baileyi]] (P). Marine mammals: [[Balaenoptera musculus]] (P), [[Megaptera novaeangliae]] (P), multiple dolphin species, [[Arctocephalus townsendi]] (P), [[Mirounga angustirostris]] (Pr). Ungulates: [[Tapirus bairdii]] (P), [[Antilocapra americana]] subspecies (P to A*), [[Odocoileus]] subspecies. Numerous endemic rodents, particularly island endemics (many R*), and bats including [[Leptonycteris]] species (A to P).

**Birds**: Over 350 avian taxa listed. Parrots: [[Ara militaris]] (P), [[Ara macao]] (P), [[Amazona]] species (P to A), [[Rhynchopsitta]] species (P*). Raptors: [[Harpia harpyja]] (P), [[Aquila chrysaetos]] (Pr), [[Gymnogyps californianus]] (P), [[Falco peregrinus]] (Pr). Waterbirds: [[Jabiru mycteria]] (P), [[Mycteria americana]] (P), [[Grus]] species (P). Endemic species: [[Dendrortyx]] (wood-partridges, A* to P*), [[Campylorhynchus]] wrens, [[Toxostoma]] thrashers, numerous hummingbirds. Seabirds: [[Puffinus]] species, [[Sula]] boobies, [[Sterna]] terns.

**Reptiles**: Extensive listings across families. Crocodilians: [[Crocodylus acutus]] (P), [[Crocodylus moreletii]] (P), [[Caiman crocodilus]] (P). Sea turtles: all species endangered (P) including [[Dermochelys coriacea]], [[Chelonia mydas]], [[Eretmochelys imbricata]], [[Caretta caretta]], [[Lepidochelys kempi]], [[Lepidochelys olivacea]]. Freshwater turtles: [[Dermatemys mawii]] (P*), numerous [[Kinosternon]] species. Lizards: [[Heloderma]] species (A), [[Ctenosaura]] iguanas, [[Phrynosoma]] horned lizards, numerous endemic [[Sceloporus]], [[Anolis]], and [[Phyllodactylus]] species. Snakes: [[Boa constrictor]] (A), numerous rattlesnakes ([[Crotalus]]), coral snakes ([[Micrurus]]), many endemic and rare species.

**Amphibians**: 180+ taxa, predominantly endemic. Salamanders: [[Ambystoma]] species including [[Ambystoma mexicanum]] (P*, axolotl), [[Ambystoma dumerilii]] (P*), numerous [[Pseudoeurycea]], [[Chiropterotriton]], and [[Bolitoglossa]] species (mostly A* to R*). Frogs: extensive [[Eleutherodactylus]] listings (direct-developing frogs), [[Hyla]] and related genera, [[Rana]] species, many cloud forest specialists.

**Fish**: 150+ taxa listed. Freshwater endemics dominate: [[Cyprinodon]] pupfish species (many P* or A* from single springs), [[Chirostoma]] silversides from Mexican lakes, [[Xiphophorus]] livebearers, [[Notropis]] and [[Gila]] minnows, [[Ictalurus]] catfish. Marine species: [[Carcharodon carcharias]] (Pr), [[Rhincodon typus]] (Pr), [[Cetorhinus maximus]] (Pr), [[Totoaba macdonaldi]] (P*), groupers and snappers (Pr).

**Invertebrates**: Focus on commercially important mollusks and crustaceans. Mollusks: [[Strombus gigas]] (Pr, queen conch), [[Haliotis]] abalone species (Pr), [[Atrina]] pen shells (Pr), numerous oysters and clams. Crustaceans: [[Panulirus]] spiny lobsters (Pr), [[Penaeus]] shrimp species (Pr), land crabs. Cave-adapted species: [[Typhlatya]] shrimp (P to A), [[Procambarus]] crayfish. Corals: [[Acropora cervicornis]] (P), [[Acropora palmata]] (P).

**Regulatory Specifications**: Section 7 establishes that (7.1) endangered, threatened, rare and specially protected species may be extracted for scientific research in quantities authorized by SEDESOL, but cannot be commercialized; (7.2) endangered and threatened species under permanent harvest bans may be collected to establish breeding populations for species recovery, but offspring cannot be sold; (7.3) commercial use of rare and specially protected species should primarily utilize captive-bred specimens; (7.4) wild harvest of rare and specially protected species requires population dynamics studies and authorized harvest rates; (7.5) habitat use and transformation where listed species occur must ensure conservation. Enforcement is assigned to SARH, Secretaría de Pesca, and SEDESOL through the Federal Environmental Protection Agency (Procuraduría Federal de Protección al Ambiente).

The standard represents a comprehensive baseline for [[Mexican Biodiversity Conservation]], identifying priority species across all major taxonomic groups and establishing a legal framework for their protection and sustainable use. Endemic species receive particular emphasis, reflecting [[Mexico]]'s role as a megadiverse country and center of endemism.
