# Limnological Characterization of the Reference State of River Vizela

#limnology #freshwater-ecology #phytoplankton

## Executive Summary

This 1990 limnological survey, published by the Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre" (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), describes the baseline (reference) condition of a stretch of [[River Vizela]] in northern Portugal before the construction of [[Queimadela Dam]]. Monthly sampling at two stations from July 1988 to June 1989 measured physical, chemical, biological and sediment variables. The author concludes that the reach is a low-nutrient, well-oxygenated, very soft-water lotic system with low biological productivity and no evident organic pollution. Bacterial indicators, however, exceeded drinking-water guideline values, which the author attributes to influence from margin soils. The study is framed as a baseline for monitoring the ecological evolution of the future reservoir.

## Background & Description

The paper argues that impoundment transforms a lotic river into a lake upstream of a dam and a strongly modified river downstream. Cited literature (Wollman 1971; Pettis 1984, both via García de Jalón 1986) is used to argue that flow regulation causes deeper and more irreversible change to fluvial ecosystems than pollution, and that it is among the most widespread deliberate human impacts on natural environments. The author accepts the need for hydraulic works for water supply and energy, but argues that baseline limnological data are required to manage the reservoir and its biological exploitation.

**Study setting**
- The [[River Vizela]] is a tributary of the River Ave, about 47 km long, with a catchment of about 342 km².
- The study stretch lies between Travassés and Queimadela, where no contamination had been detected. Lower reaches near Fafe and Vila das Aves are polluted.
- Human population density is below 2 inhabitants/km², and adjacent land is used for agriculture and forestry.

**Sampling design**
- Two stations: V1, about 3 km downstream of the dam (width about 14 m, depth about 0.6 m); V2, about 2.56 km upstream (width about 7 m, depth about 0.8 m).
- Sampled monthly from July 1988, except December. Sampling dates were n = 11 (V1) and n = 10 (V2) for most variables.
- Methods included the [[Winkler Method]] for dissolved oxygen and BOD5, [[Carlson Trophic State Index]] calculations, Utermöhl phytoplankton counts, membrane-filter and multiple-tube bacterial tests, and [[Sediment Analysis]] using an Eckman dredge.
- Water was sampled about 0.2 m below the surface because of shallow depth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The author expects that inundation of riparian and farmland soils will initially raise nutrient levels and productivity, followed by a decline as nutrients are assimilated. Stratification and oxygen depletion in deep reservoir zones are also anticipated. Because [[Phytoplankton]] sit at the base of the food web, changes there are expected to propagate to periphyton, benthic macroinvertebrates, [[Zooplankton]] and fish.

The reach is classed as a [[Lotic Ecosystems]] habitat with high current speed. Phytoplankton and zooplankton are therefore expected to be sparse, and benthic organisms are expected to dominate. The author notes that fish and benthic macroinvertebrate data were collected but are to be reported separately by other researchers.

## Key Findings

### Physical factors
- **Temperature:** Air temperature ranged from 5.0 °C (January) to 25.5 °C (June) at V1. Water temperature ranged from 7.0 °C to 17.5 °C at V1 and from 7.0 °C to 16.5 °C at V2. Water was generally cooler than air except in November and January.
- **Current:** Measured with floating orange (an approximate method). Mean velocity was 0.52 ± 0.15 m/s at V1 and 1.24 ± 1.40 m/s at V2, with a maximum of 4.00 m/s at V2 in February. V2 was faster, consistent with its steeper slope and narrower channel.
- **Suspended solids:** Irregular, from 0.2 mg/l (January) to 9.8 mg/l (February) at V1. Usually below 6 mg/l.

### Chemical factors
- **[[Dissolved Oxygen]]:** 9.86–11.44 mg/l at V1 and 9.86–11.83 mg/l at V2. Saturation was always above 88%, with means of 98.3 ± 4.3% (V1) and 99.5 ± 3.5% (V2).
- **BOD5:** Means of 1.95 ± 0.61 mg O₂/l (V1) and 1.55 ± 0.64 mg O₂/l (V2). Most values were below 2 mg O₂/l.
- **pH:** Range 5.6–7.9; means 7.0 ± 0.6 (V1) and 6.9 ± 0.6 (V2).
- **Hardness:** Means of 7 ± 2 (V1) and 6.7 ± 1.9 mg CaCO₃/l (V2), classed as very soft water.
- **Alkalinity:** 4–12 mg CaCO₃/l; annual means 7.2 ± 2.2 (V1) and 6.9 ± 1.8 (V2).
- **Conductivity:** Means of 30 ± 5 µS/cm (V1) and 26 ± 5 µS/cm (V2), indicating very poorly mineralized water.
- **Chloride:** Means of 5.22 ± 2.33 (V1) and 4.96 ± 0.82 mg Cl/l (V2). The author states values were below 12 mg/l, typical of unpolluted waters.
- **Nitrogen compounds:**
  - Ammonium: means of 1.6 ± 4.3 µg N/l (V1) and 0.4 ± 1.1 µg N/l (V2). Over 80% of results were undetectable.
  - Nitrite: means of 1.1 ± 1.3 and 0.6 ± 0.5 µg N/l.
  - Nitrate: 281–597 µg N/l at V1 and 222–765 µg N/l at V2, with the peaks in April and September.
- **Phosphorus:**
  - Phosphate: means of 3.2 ± 4.1 (V1) and 4.2 ± 5.3 µg P/l (V2). Maxima of 13.3 and 15.0 µg P/l in July.
  - Total phosphorus: means of 8.2 ± 7.1 (V1) and 8.8 ± 6.8 µg P/l (V2). Maxima of 21.4 (V1) and 20.5 µg P/l (V2) in June.
- **Silicate:** Means of 2.27 ± 0.84 and 2.29 ± 0.72 mg Si/l.
- **NO₃:PO₄ ratio:** Highly variable, with nitrate dominance and a September peak. Means of 156 ± 119 (V1) and 155 ± 161 (V2).

### Biological factors
- **Chlorophyll *a*:** Maximum 2.27 mg/m³ (July, V1). Undetectable on several dates at both stations.
- **Phaeopigments:** Maximum 1.39 mg/m³ (February, V1).
- **Phytoplankton density:** 13–93 cells/ml at V1 and 7–47 cells/ml at V2. Diatoms were usually the most abundant group.
- **Phytoplankton taxa:** About 30 forms were identified across Bacillariophyceae (e.g., *Asterionella formosa*, *Melosira ambigua*, *Surirella robusta*, *Tabellaria fenestrata*), Chlorophyceae (e.g., *Ankistrodesmus angustus*, *Scenedesmus* sp.) and other groups (e.g., *Cryptomonas*, *Synura uvella*).
- **[[Zooplankton]]:** No cladocerans or copepods were found in either the quantitative (250 ml) or qualitative (30 µm net) samples.
- **[[Heterotrophic Bacteria]]:** Means of 1744 ± 1183 colonies/ml (V1) and 1463 ± 947 colonies/ml (V2). The text gives 1463 for V2, while Table 4 gives 1436. Peaks were 5100 (V1) and 4000 colonies/ml (V2) in February.
- **Fecal streptococci:** Peaks of 109/100 ml (November, V1) and 142/100 ml (June, V2). Means of 56 ± 31 and 52 ± 43 per 100 ml.
- **Total coliforms:** Means of 861 ± 784 (V1) and 413 ± 331 MPN/100 ml (V2).
- **[[Fecal Coliforms]]:** Means of 81 ± 54 (V1) and 56 ± 46 MPN/100 ml (V2).

### Sediment
- **Organic matter:** Means of 1.50 ± 0.37% (V1) and 1.01 ± 0.48% (V2).
- **Total phosphorus:** Means of 0.64 ± 0.08 and 0.68 ± 0.08 mg P/g dry sediment.
- **Total nitrogen:** Means of 0.18 ± 0.10 (V1) and 0.18 ± 0.11 mg N/g (V2).

### Trophic state
- [[Carlson Trophic State Index]] means: TSI(chl *a*) 20 ± 10 (V1) and 18 ± 5 (V2); TSI(TP) 29 ± 14 (V1) and 33 ± 12 (V2).
- The author classifies the reach as oligotrophic.

### Correlations
- Few significant correlations were found. At V2, water temperature correlated with fecal coliforms, suspended solids with heterotrophic bacteria, and phytoplankton density with heterotrophic bacteria. At V1, only current speed versus heterotrophic bacteria was significant.
- Chlorophyll *a* did not correlate with phytoplankton density, which the author attributes to changing dominant species.

### Conclusions and management implications
- **Water quality:** The author judges the reach to have good water quality and low biological productivity. Dissolved oxygen saturation above 88% is described as suitable even for trout (per Klein 1959, cited by Nisbet & Verneaux 1970).
- **Microbial indicators:** Mean heterotrophic bacteria exceeded the maximum guideline for drinking water (Directive 80/778/EEC). Fecal streptococci, total coliform and fecal coliform means exceeded drinking-water values but were within bathing-water limits. The author attributes this to margin-soil runoff. The fecal streptococci test probably could not distinguish *S. faecalis* from *S. faecalis* var. *liquefaciens*, which is not restricted to human or warm-blooded animal intestines.
- **Sampling frequency:** Monthly sampling is too coarse for bacteria with short generation times.
- **Impoundment outlook:** The author expects higher nutrients, altered thermal and oxygen profiles, changed water renewal, and shifts in phytoplankton and zooplankton after closure. Soil clearing before first filling and catchment control are recommended. Where the reservoir supplies drinking water or recreation, multi-depth intake is advised to manage oxygen depletion in the hypolimnion and suspended-solids excess in surface layers.
- **Future work:** Periphyton, benthic macroinvertebrate and fish data were collected and were to be reported separately.
