# Razorback Sucker Movements and Habitat Use in the Lower San Juan River Inflow Area, Utah (1996 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This annual report from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] documents the first year (fall 1995 to October 1996) of a sonic telemetry study of [[Razorback Sucker]] movements in the lower [[San Juan River]], Utah. The study tracked 34 hatchery-reared, three-year-old fish released in November 1995 at two sites in the river's inflow area influenced by [[Lake Powell]]. The goals were to learn how the fish move and to identify habitats that may support reintroduced or wild populations. Contact was made with about half of the tagged fish. Most contacts were in shallow, cobble-bordered and vegetated habitats near [[Neskahi Bay]], and many fish showed one-way or bidirectional movements. Lake-level fluctuations of about 20 feet and dewatering of side canyons were proposed as explanations for lost contacts. The authors plan additional surveys in FY97.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is native to the [[Colorado River Basin]] and is listed as endangered (USFWS 1991). It was once widespread across the basin, but remnant populations now persist only in isolated reaches of the Green, Colorado, and San Juan Rivers and in several Arizona lakes. The report attributes its decline partly to construction of mainstream dams and the deep, cold reservoirs they created.

The study aimed to:
- Determine razorback sucker movements in the inflow area of the San Juan River, the lower 50-55 miles strongly influenced by Lake Powell.
- Identify habitats that may support reintroduced and/or wild fish.

The work was coordinated with the [[Lake Powell Native Fish Work Group]].

**Study fish:** The 34 fish were three-year-old progeny from the San Juan River propagation program, held in ponds at Wahweap and Ouray, Utah. They were surplus hatchery fish being stocked in the San Juan River during summer/fall 1995. Fish were transported at 58°F and received on November 1, 1995, at the Piute Farms Wash area.

**Tags and equipment:** Two transmitter types were purchased from [[Sonotronics]] (Tucson, Arizona), each uniquely coded and transmitting in the 72-79 kHz range:
- 14-month tags (about 2 g in air, 65 mm x 16 mm), implanted internally.
- 50-month tags (about 5 g in air, 70 mm x 16.5 mm), attached externally because the smallest fish were too small for surgery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[San Juan River]] inflow area is lake-like, with depths reaching 100 meters, which made [[Sonic Telemetry]] the chosen method. Fish were released at two sites selected for protection from wave action and for shallow, vegetated shoreline:
- [[Neskahi Wash]] (river mile 24): 16 fish, including those in the upper inflow area where wild fish had been captured in earlier years.
- [[Zahn Bay]] (river mile 42.8): 18 fish, with the number inferred from the total of 34 and the report's "16 at Neskahi Wash" figure.

River temperature at release was 61°F. The majority of the lower San Juan is incised canyon habitat with little vegetated or cobble shoreline. Key habitats used by fish included:
- [[Neskahi Bay]] (RM 21-26), including [[Piute Canyon]], Neskahi Wash, and the northeastern shoreline. Neskahi Bay is the largest open water body in the river and appears to mark the downstream limit of graveled shorelines.
- [[Nokai Canyon]] (RM 43.5), a short side canyon with broad, shallow vegetated areas dominated by [[Salt Cedar]] and muddy banks.
- The [[Piute Farms Wash]] reach (RM 51-54), a broad, relatively shallow river reach where the lake's influence ends. Dense salt cedar and mud flats made access difficult at low lake levels.

Fish were typically found in areas with a mixture of cobble shorelines and flooded stands of salt cedar and other vegetation. Only three fish were contacted in the deeper channels of the inflow area.

Lake Powell levels fluctuated about 20 feet during the first study year. The lake filled toward the end of summer, which may have buried tags in sediments or left them out of the water after side canyons dewatered.

## Key Findings

**Methods and procedures**
- Fish were examined for sex, weighed, measured, and scanned for a PIT tag before tagging.
- Internal tags were implanted through the abdominal wall using [[MS-222]] anesthetic (80-100 mg). External tags were sutured to the nuchal hump without anesthesia.
- Tagging took 2-5 minutes per fish, followed by about 30 minutes of recovery.
- Tags were checked before and after release.
- Manual tracking and an automatic [[Remote Listening Station]] were used. The station was installed upstream of the Zahn Bay release site to detect upstream movement.

**Size data**
- Internally tagged fish (14-month tags): 18 fish, mean total length 456.16 mm.
- Externally tagged fish (50-month tags): 16 fish, mean total length 435.25 mm.

**Tracking effort**
- Tracking focused on the lower 50-55 miles of the San Juan River (Piute Farms Wash to the mouth).
- Fish were searched for in the first two days after release. The lower river was surveyed 10 times through October 1996.
- Lake Powell was searched from mile marker 30 (near Friendship Cove) upstream to mile marker 111, including the [[Escalante River]] and all side canyons. About 500 locations were examined in April 1996.
- The upper San Juan River was surveyed from [[Mexican Hat]], Utah, to Clayhills Crossing in June 1996, and from Bluff, Utah, to Piute Farms Wash in October 1996.

**Results**
- Of the 34 fish released, one Zahn Bay fish was found dead the next morning and removed from the study.
- All contacts with tagged fish occurred near or upstream of Neskahi Bay (RM 24).
- Of the 16 Neskahi Wash releases, all but two left the release site soon after release. Fish #2336 and #465 were presumed dead or to have expelled their tags, since their positions did not change.
- Two Zahn Bay fish (#2237, #2426) moved into Neskahi Wash over winter and spring. Fish #2237 was active in spring but has not been contacted since. Fish #2426 moved upstream to the Piute Farms area.
- Three fish (#3335, #2354, #2453) were located in Piute Canyon during winter. Fish #2354 and #2453 later left the area. Fish #3335 may be dead or have expelled its tag.
- Six fish from Zahn Bay (#267, #276, #249, #366, #2435, #2255) moved into Nokai Canyon over winter. Fish #2327 was found there in October 1996.
- Four fish (#2327, #2426, #2633, #2345) were located in the Piute Farms area in late spring, coinciding with rising lake levels.
- Some fish made long-distance movements. Fish #2237 and #2264 moved downstream 19 and 13 miles before contact was lost. Fish #2345 and #2633 moved upstream about 10 miles and remained in Piute Farms Wash. Fish #2354 moved upstream about 43 miles in late spring to early summer.
- Only one fish (#2354) and possibly five others were located in the San Juan River between Mexican Hat and Clayhills Crossing during the June surveys. High flows made the five unidentifiable.
- No fish were contacted during the April Lake Powell survey, so few or none appear to have moved into the lake.
- No fish were contacted upstream of Piute Farms in October 1996. The river was turbid from recent flash flooding, and reception was poor.

**Remote station**
- The [[Remote Listening Station]] (river mile 45) recorded data from November 1995 to February 1996. It was successful, indicating that five fish moved upstream in the first few months.
- The station was vandalized in February 1996 and has not been replaced. Replacement is undecided because of security problems.

**Conclusions**
1. Contact was made with about half of the tagged fish. Internal implants and the 50-month external tags may have been lost, either snagged in salt cedar or because the fish died.
2. Lake-level fluctuations, which may have left tags out of the water or buried in sediment, along with the large area surveyed, probably explain much of the lost contact.
3. No obvious downstream movement into Lake Powell was observed. Fish appeared to make one-way or bidirectional movements, seeking suitable habitat.
4. Fish moved into canyons in the upper inflow area (Neskahi Bay and above), which have shallow and rocky shorelines.
5. Several fish are believed alive, including #2633, #2345, #2327, #2237, #2426, #276, #2543, #2363, and #2246. Fish #2525 and #465 are presumed dead or tag-expelled.
6. The movement patterns suggest that fish seek specific habitat types and may be related to spawning behavior.

**Future plans (FY97)**
- Survey the inflow area in late fall to early winter.
- Conduct extensive surveys of Lake Powell and the San Juan River from February to April, from Bluff to the confluence.
- Determine the extent of additional sampling after spring findings.
