## Executive Summary
This memo reports results of a five-day fish sampling effort (March 15–20, 1998) conducted by Arizona State University on [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada, targeting the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Six 91-meter [[Trammel Net|trammel nets]] were deployed at depths of 1–10 m across shoreline and embayment habitats between the Cottonwood East area and Carp Cove on the Arizona side. Total sampling effort was 41.75 units (1 unit = 100 m² of netting fished for 24 hours). Seven fish species were recorded, and 39 razorback suckers were captured, tagged/scanned, and sampled for muscle tissue to support genetic analysis. A substantial proportion of captured razorbacks were recaptures, including confirmed repatriated (hatchery-stocked) individuals and at least one historically tagged wild fish from a 1994 sonic telemetry study.

## Background & Description
- **Razorback Sucker size range:** 362–700 mm total length (TL) across 39 captured individuals.
- Species is identifiable by tag data (PIT tags) and prior sonic telemetry tagging (external/internal sonic tag #276 on one individual, PIT ID 7F7D78316D).
- No morphological anomalies or condition notes reported beyond size range; note that the previously sonic-tagged individual showed **no visible indication of a retained sonic tag** upon recapture and workup in 1998, suggesting tag loss, expulsion, or degradation over the ~4-year interval since original tagging (1994).
- Other species captured (co-occurring in net catch) included:
  - [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
  - [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)
  - [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*)
  - [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*)
  - [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*)
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat sampled:** Reservoir shoreline, embayments, and open-water points at depths of 1–10 m within the Cottonwood East to Carp Cove reach of [[Lake Mohave]], part of the Lower [[Colorado River]] system on the Arizona-Nevada border.
- **Community structure:** The seven species collected reflect a mixed native–nonnative reservoir fish assemblage typical of the Lower Colorado River mainstem impoundments, combining a federally listed endemic catostomid ([[Razorback Sucker]]) with introduced sport and forage fishes (carp, rainbow trout, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, striped bass, largemouth bass).
- **Population management context:** The recapture of repatriated (previously stocked) razorback suckers indicates ongoing use of Lake Mohave as a recovery/stocking site for this endangered species, with survival of stocked cohorts from at least 1995, 1996, and late 1997 documented in this survey.
- **Long-term monitoring value:** Recapture of a wild-origin razorback sucker originally tagged during a 1994 sonic telemetry study demonstrates multi-year individual longevity and site fidelity/detectability within the reservoir, supporting long-term demographic tracking of this species.

## Key Findings
- **Total effort:** 41.75 trammel net units (100 m² per unit per 24 hr) over 5 days.
- **Species richness:** 7 fish species recorded in trammel net catches.
- **Razorback sucker catch:** 39 individuals captured, size range 362–700 mm TL.
- **Recapture rate:** 18 of 39 razorback suckers (46%) were recaptures from prior sampling/stocking events.
- **Repatriation confirmation:** At least 6 of the 18 recaptures (33%) were confirmed hatchery-repatriated fish:
  - 3 stocked in 1995
  - 1 stocked in 1996
  - Remainder presumed stocked in late 1997
- **Historical tag recovery:** One recaptured individual (PIT tag 7F7D78316D) was originally tagged with sonic tag #276 during a 1994 sonic telemetry study; no physical evidence of the sonic tag remained at time of 1998 recapture.
- **Genetic sampling:** Muscle tissue plugs were collected from all captured razorback suckers for genetic analysis, supporting future population genetics/stock identification work.
