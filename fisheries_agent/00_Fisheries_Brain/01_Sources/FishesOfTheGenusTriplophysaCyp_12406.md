# Fishes of the Genus Triplophysa in the Yuanjiang Basin of Yunnan, China, with Description of a New Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #balitoridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Zhou Wei]] and [[Cui Gui-Hua]] (*Ichthyological Exploration of Freshwaters*, 1997) reviews [[Triplophysa]] loaches of the [[Yuanjiang River]] (upper [[Red River]]) basin in Yunnan and formally describes *Triplophysa laticeps* as a new species. Two species had previously been recorded from the basin, *T. grahami* and *T. gejiuensis*. The new species was collected from the Luzhijiang, a tributary near Lufeng, and is most similar to *T. venusta* and *T. xichangensis* from the [[Jinshajiang River]] (upper Yangtze) basin. The authors describe the work as a preliminary revision.

## Background & Description

- **Genus:** *Triplophysa* Rendahl, 1933 (type species *Noemacheilus huijertiuensis*). Members are distinguished from other Nemacheilinae by close-set nares, an inferior arched mouth, no processus dentiformis, no median notch in the lower jaw, and small or absent scales. Males have breeding tubercles in the suborbital area and tuberculated pectoral pads.
- **Distribution of genus:** Mainly the [[Qinghai-Xizang Plateau]] and adjacent central Asian and Himalayan regions. The Yuanjiang basin lies at the southeastern margin of its range, and *T. gejiuensis* and *T. laticeps* are the southernmost Chinese records.
- **Methods:** Specimens are held at the [[Kunming Institute of Zoology]] (KIZ), the [[Institute of Hydrobiology]] (IH) and the [[Nanjing Institute of Geography and Limnology]] (NIGL). Counts and measurements follow [[Kottelat]] (1984). Data on *T. grahami* and *T. xichangensis* were supplied by Zhu Songquan (NIGL).

**Species treated:**
- *Triplophysa gejiuensis* (Chu & Chen, 1979): a blind, subterranean species known only from Bajiaoqing near Qiafang, Gejiu, Yunnan. Diagnosis includes no eyes, absent posterior airbladder chamber, and a forked caudal fin. The material examined comprised a holotype (KIZ 7803001, 47.5 mm SL) and 7 paratypes (40–52 mm SL).
- *Triplophysa grahami* (Regan, 1906): eyes present, 9–10 branched dorsal rays, brown stripes and saddles, absent posterior airbladder chamber. Material included 17 specimens (60–91 mm SL) from Midu (Yuanjiang basin) and 2 from Fumin (Jinshajiang basin).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Type habitat of *T. laticeps*:** A small stream with clear water, moderate to swift current and a gravel substrate, at about 1600 m elevation near Lufeng city (25°15'N, 102°03'E).
- **Associated fauna:** The only other fish collected there was [[Schistura macrotaenia]].
- **Diet:** Stomachs of 4 dissected specimens (34–39 mm SL) contained mainly aquatic plants, the cyanobacteria *Dichothrix* and *Cylindrospermum*, the diatom *Cymbella*, and a few aquatic insects.
- **Biogeography:** The Yuanjiang basin has its own endemic-rich loach fauna, with species closely related to taxa in the neighbouring Jinshajiang basin. The authors note they are unsure whether *T. laticeps*, *T. venusta* and *T. xichangensis* form a natural lineage, since no other supporting evidence was found.

## Key Findings

**1. A new species, *Triplophysa laticeps*, is described.**
- Holotype: KIZ 914077, 53 mm TL, 42 mm SL, collected 24 April 1991 by Zhou Wei, Liu Yincong et al.
- Paratypes: 21 specimens (KIZ series), 46–53 mm TL, 34–42 mm SL.
- Meristics: D 3,8; A 3,5; C 8+8; P 1,11; V 1,7; 9–10 gill rakers.
- Diagnostic features: pelvic fin origin under the first to second branched dorsal rays, pelvic fin not reaching the anus, 5 branched anal rays, deeply emarginate caudal fin with rounded tips, and 9–10 gill rakers.
- Colour: light yellow body with brown blotches and saddles, and a dissociated black basal caudal bar.
- Etymology: from Latin *latus* (flat) and *ceps* (head), referring to the flat head.
- Known only from the type locality.

**2. Close relatives and separating characters.**
- Versus *T. venusta*: fewer gill rakers (9–10 vs 10–13), always 8 branched dorsal rays (vs 8–9), deeply emarginate caudal fin (vs slightly concave), and a deeper caudal peduncle (73.5–100.0% vs 52.6–76.9% of its length).
- Versus *T. xichangensis*: pelvic origin under dorsal rays 1–2 (vs in front of dorsal origin), fewer gill rakers (9–10 vs 11–13), more branched dorsal rays (8 vs 6–7), and a deeper caudal peduncle (73.5–100.0% vs 51.6–64.0%).
- Versus *T. grahami*: well-developed posterior airbladder chamber (vs reduced or absent), 5 branched anal rays (vs 6), 8 branched dorsal rays (vs 9–10), and a deeply emarginate caudal fin (vs slightly forked).
- Versus *T. gejiuensis*: eyes present (vs absent), well-developed posterior airbladder chamber (vs absent), 11 pectoral rays (vs 9–10), and 7 pelvic rays (vs 5).

**3. Sexual dimorphism was not detected.** The authors suggest the samples were immature or collected outside the breeding season.

**4. Conclusion.** The Yuanjiang basin now has three recorded *Triplophysa* species of its own, and this is described as a preliminary revision, with further work needed on the lineage relationships.
