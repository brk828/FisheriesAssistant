# Distribution and Status of the Chihuahua Chub (*Gila nigrescens*)

## Executive Summary
This preliminary report, submitted by David L. Propst (NMGF) in April 1990, documents a multi-agency field survey (NMGF, USFS, USFWS, SEDUE) conducted from 5–30 March 1990 across three endorheic basins in Chihuahua, Mexico: the **Bustillos**, **Bavicora**, and **Guzman** basins. The primary objective was to inventory lotic (stream-dwelling) fish faunas, with specific attention to the distribution and status of the [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), alongside associated native and introduced fish species. A total of 55 locations were sampled across the three basins, revealing that the [[Guzman Basin]] harbors the highest fish diversity (at least 13 species) and is the primary stronghold for *G. nigrescens*, which was absent from the [[Bustillos Basin]] and [[Bavicora Basin]] where an undifferentiated "*Gila* sp." was recorded instead.

## Background & Description
While this report is primarily a distributional survey rather than a taxonomic description, standardized morphometric data were collected for all captured *Gila* specimens to aid in future taxonomic/status assessments. Measurements taken for each *Gila* specimen included:
- **Standard Length (SL)**
- **Dorsal fin insertion-to-snout length**
- **Dorsal fin insertion-to-hypural plate length**
- **Body depth**
- **Body width**

Specimens were weighed and measured in the field before being returned live to their habitat. Representative native species, including *G. nigrescens*, were photographed for voucher documentation. Note: In the Bustillos and Bavicora basins, chub specimens were only identified to genus level (*Gila* sp.), suggesting potential ambiguity or hybridization/undescribed distinction from the nominal *G. nigrescens* found in the Guzman Basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*Gila nigrescens* and its congeners inhabit desert lotic (stream/river) systems, as well as associated spring pools, runs, and cienegas (wetland marshes).

- **Guzman Basin Distribution:** *G. nigrescens* was a dominant and widespread species specifically within the **Santa Clara** and **Casas Grandes** drainages, found at 8 of 9 sites and 11 of 17 sites, respectively. It was comparatively less prevalent in the **Santa Maria** drainage (found at only 4 of 12 sites), where the habitat appeared more heavily dominated by introduced [[Gambusia affinis]] (mosquitofish) and native *Pantosteus plebeius* (Rio Grande sucker).
- **Sympatric Species:** In its core range (Santa Clara/Casas Grandes), *G. nigrescens* co-occurs frequently with [[Campostoma ornatum]] (Mexican stoneroller), [[Cyprinella formosus]] (shiner), and [[Pantosteus plebeius]] (Rio Grande sucker).
- **Basin Exclusivity:** True *G. nigrescens* was not confirmed in the Bustillos or Bavicora basins; these basins instead contained an unidentified "*Gila* sp." alongside *Cyprinella formosus* and *Campostoma ornatum*.
- **Threats/Competition:** The survey noted the presence of introduced species (Status "I") throughout the range, including [[Cyprinus carpio]] (common carp), *Ictalurus melas* (black bullhead), *Lepomis macrochirus* (bluegill), and *Micropterus salmoides* (largemouth bass), which represent potential competitive or predatory threats to native cyprinids in shared habitats.

## Key Findings
- **Survey Scope:** 55 total locations sampled across three basins (9 in Bustillos, 5 in Bavicora, 41 in Guzman) between 5–30 March 1990.
- **Guzman Basin Diversity:** The Guzman Basin was the most speciose, yielding at least 13 fish species across the Santa Maria, Santa Clara, and Casas Grandes drainages.
- **Chub Abundance:** In the Guzman Basin, *G. nigrescens* numbers totaled 202 (Santa Maria), 226 (Santa Clara), and 254 (Casas Grandes) individuals.
- **Frequency Ranking:** *G. nigrescens* ranked as the 2nd most frequently collected species in the Santa Clara drainage (8/9 sites) and 4th in the Casas Grandes drainage (11/17 sites).
- **Species Absence:** *Gila nigrescens* was **not recorded** in the Bustillos or Bavicora basins; only an unidentified *Gila* sp. was found there, suggesting these populations may be distinct or warrant further taxonomic investigation.
- **Dominant Basin Species:** [[Pantosteus plebeius]] was the most numerically dominant and widely distributed species in the Santa Clara (found at 9/9 sites) and Casas Grandes drainages, while [[Gambusia affinis]] dominated the Santa Maria drainage.
- **Native vs. Introduced Status:** The vast majority of species recorded were native (N), though introduced (I) species such as common carp, black bullhead, mosquitofish, bluegill, and largemouth bass were present in the more downstream/accessible sites of the Casas Grandes and Santa Maria drainages.
- **Methodology:** All specimens were sampled via electrofishing, seining, and dipnetting, measured/photographed, and released live back into their habitat of origin.
