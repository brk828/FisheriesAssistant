# Aquatic Inventory of the Upper Bill Williams Drainage, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 baseline inventory, prepared for the [[Bureau of Land Management]] Phoenix District Office, surveyed the aquatic habitat, water quality, [[Macroinvertebrates]], and [[Ichthyofauna]] of the perennial drainages of the upper [[Bill Williams River]] basin upstream from [[Alamo Lake]], Arizona. Sampling took place at 64 stations on three watersheds ([[Santa Maria River]], [[Burro Creek]], and [[Big Sandy River]]) between December 1978 and February 1979. The data were intended to support management plans for terrestrial and aquatic resources, and to address NEPA requirements after a 1973 court ruling on livestock grazing.

The inventory found a native-dominated fish fauna, with [[Longfin Dace]] the most abundant species in every watershed. The main threats identified were [[Cyprus-Bagdad Mine]] effluent in Burro Creek, the proposed [[Anderson Uranium Mine]] near the Santa Maria River, livestock and feral burro grazing of riparian zones, and water withdrawals.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and regulatory context.** The [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) must address resource impacts of land use in an Environmental Statement under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) of 1970. Following an October 1973 lawsuit by the [[Natural Resources Defense Council]], wildlife inventories were started on public lands because general grazing evaluations were judged too broad for localized impacts. The document estimates that BLM administers 310 stream miles and over 11,800 acres of riparian woodland in Arizona (Hoeft, 1978).

**Study area.** The survey covered perennial drainages of the BLM Hualapai and Aquarius Planning Units in Mohave and Yavapai counties. The area is characterized by low-order desert streams with riffles, pools, and runs, plus intermittent reaches during low flow. Annual precipitation is 11 to 18 in., and rainfall is bimodal (summer monsoon and winter Pacific storms). Snow usually falls from mid-November above 5,000 ft.

**Methods.**
- 64 stations sampled on 3 major watersheds; more than 168 stream-miles surveyed at roughly 2.5-mile intervals.
- Each station was a 60-ft reach, classified as pool, run, or riffle.
- Discharge estimated by the [[Embody Cork-Float Method]] (Embody, 1927); substrate by Hynes (1970); gradient by Abney level.
- Water quality measured with Hach field test kits (DO, CO2, pH, NO3-N) and, at selected stations, analyzed by the Arizona Game and Fish Department for chloride, hardness, ammonia, phosphate, sulfate, turbidity, and TDS.
- Benthic macroinvertebrates sampled qualitatively to identify distribution trends, not for statistical quantification.
- Fish collected by 115 V AC [[Backpack Electrofishing]] and 1/8-inch mesh seines; identifications followed Minckley (1973). Specimens were deposited in the Collection of Fishes, Arizona State University, Tempe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Santa Maria River.** Originates at 2,650 ft and flows about 41 miles to Alamo Lake (1,170 ft). It is a broad, shallow, sandy-bottomed system with few riffles. Mean width was 132.5 ft and mean velocity 2.5 ft/sec. Surface flow is not sustained in summer. Riparian vegetation is sparse (mesquite bosques, seep willow, Goodding willow, cottonwood). Physical habitat is considered marginal, and fish depauperacy is attributed to lack of habitat diversity rather than chemistry.

**Burro Creek.** Runs about 57 miles from 4,380 ft to the Big Sandy River, draining 687 square miles of the Mohon and Aquarius mountains. It is described as an extremely heterogeneous aquatic system with riffle, run, and pool habitats, and contains many springs and seeps. Francis Creek hot springs measured 85°F at a springhead on January 4, while the creek below was 53°F. Burro Creek is the most biologically diverse watershed in the study.

**Big Sandy River and Trout Creek.** The Big Sandy originates near Wikieup and flows about 37.8 miles to Alamo Lake. Its drainage area excluding Burro Creek is about 2,123 square miles. It is a broad, sandy, essentially pool-less run. Trout Creek, its major upper tributary, is narrower, deeper, and more heterogeneous, with riffles, runs, and pools.

**Alamo Lake.** Alamo Dam (constructed 1968 by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for flood control) stores up to 1,043,000 acre-feet. The reservoir was about 1,800 surface acres before the 1978 and 1979 winter storms and now exceeds 7,000 acres. The Bill Williams River's average annual flow fell from 66,760 acre-feet (pre-dam) to 30,909 acre-feet, with 76 zero-flow days per year after dam completion.

**Water quality highlights.** Water was alkaline and generally met state (AWQCC, 1979) and federal (EPA, 1977a) standards, with exceptions noted below. Total phosphate exceeded EPA standards in all three watersheds. Burro Creek water below the mine showed the greatest degradation.

**Macroinvertebrates.** The Santa Maria had only 21 taxa, with 4 to 15 per sample. Burro Creek had the highest diversity, with 41 of 62 total taxa and members of 9 of 10 aquatic orders. Dominant taxa included *Progomphus borealis*, *Tropisternus ellipticus*, *Hydropsyche* sp., and two stoneflies (*Mesocapnia frisoni* and *M. arizonensis*). Burro Creek below Boulder Creek was essentially abiotic, with only the most tolerant taxa (belostomatids and dytiscids) recovering in Boulder Creek.

**Mining and water withdrawal.**
- *Cyprus-Bagdad open-pit copper mine:* Identified as a point source of toxic effluent and sediment, entering Burro Creek via Boulder Creek (90 sq mi). Sulfate in mine effluent was 640 mg/l, versus a Arizona standard of 250 mg/l.
- *Anderson Uranium Mine:* Reopening planned near the Santa Maria River, expected to employ 350 permanent workers in a round-the-clock mill and mine operation. Concerns include sedimentation, heavy metals, radionuclides, mill tailings, and water withdrawals.

**Multiple use.** Livestock grazing (cow-calf operations) and feral burros (500 to 600 in the Alamo Lake vicinity) cause riparian damage. Irrigation diverts about 5,300 acres in the Santa Maria watershed and 3,800 acres of alfalfa, grain, and pasture in the Big Sandy Valley. Recreation at the BLM campground below the U.S. Highway 93 bridge was found to exceed bacterial standards.

## Key Findings

**Fish fauna**
- **Santa Maria River:** 7 species in 4 families, 1,364 fish. Longfin dace made up 97.1% (1,324 individuals). Two native species (Gila robusta robusta and Pantosteus clarki) were not previously in museum records for the drainage. No listed threatened, endangered, or sensitive species.
- **Burro Creek:** 9 species in 4 families, 3,594 fish. Red shiner was most abundant at 43.7% (1,570), followed by roundtail chub at 15.0% (540). Speckled dace was most common in upper reaches. A Catostomus insignis × Pantosteus clarki hybrid (0.03%) was found.
- **Boulder Creek:** 228 fish across 5 species. Burro, Francis, and Conger Creeks were almost exclusively native-dominated.
- **Trout Creek:** 7 species, 480 fish. Longfin dace was 65.6% (315); roundtail chub 12.9% (62).
- **Big Sandy River:** 8 species in 4 families, 1,094 fish. Longfin dace was 50.5% (553) and red shiner 33.0% (361). Only 3 of the 8 species are native.
- **Alamo Lake:** Carp were 48.5% of gill-net catch (1.5-inch mesh, two-day survey). Bluegill has replaced redear sunfish and is the main forage for the bass fishery. Fishermen from Los Angeles were noted, but more than 80% of fishing pressure comes from Maricopa County.

**Water quality**
- **Santa Maria:** TDS 180–240 mg/l (mean 214); pH 7.5–8.5; DO 9–12 mg/l; NO3-N 1.5–3.0 mg/l (below the EPA standard of 5.0); total phosphate 0.18–0.53 mg/l (above the EPA standard of 0.06); sulfate 24.8–35.6 mg/l (mean 29.7); chloride 3.55–5.32 mg/l (mean 3.9); turbidity 8–24 JTU (below the Arizona warm-water standard of 50 JTU).
- **Burro Creek (Table 3):** Mean discharge 25.5 ft³/sec (range 0.02–195); mean sulfate 82.5 mg/l; mean hardness 516 mg/l; pH 8.0–9.5; NO3-N declined downstream.
- **Burro Creek below mine:** Sulfate averaged 20.1 mg/l above the Boulder Creek confluence and 113.8 mg/l below. Chloride rose from 4.1 to 23.6 mg/l. Total hardness rose from 343 to 602 mg/l. Copper reached 8.0 mg/l in spring 1977 (Arizona State Department of Health Services), exceeding state and federal limits.
- **Big Sandy River:** Hardness mean 880 mg/l; pH 8.5; DO 9–11 mg/l; total phosphate 2.68 mg/l in Table 9 (above EPA standard). Table 9 lists TDS as 413 mg/l, while the text states a mean of 592 mg/l; the discrepancy is in the source. Fluoride mean 1.2 mg/l. Fecal coliform mean 120/100 ml in 1977–78 sampling.

**Toxicity and bioaccumulation**
- Lethal copper concentration for 50% mortality (LC50) for longfin dace in Pinto Creek was 0.86 mg/l; zinc LC50 was 0.79 mg/l; the copper-zinc mixture (0.21 mg/l Cu + 0.28 mg/l Zn) was more toxic than either alone (Lewis and Burraychak, 1979).
- Fish kills in Pinto Creek involved over 300,000 individuals.
- Downstream of the mine, Gila mountain-sucker declined sharply, possibly due to loss of perilithic algae.

**Water and habitat management**
- Cyprus-Bagdad pumps more than 1,100 gallons/minute from Francis Creek. Its Application for Permit to Appropriate Water No. A3195 would raise total permissible allocations to 490,628,250 gallons/year (an increase of 131,400 gallons/year).
- Refining one ton of copper requires approximately 100,396 gallons of water (Lewis and Burraychak, 1979).
- Burro Creek Allotment (6,026 acres) is licensed for 75 head of cattle; fecal coliform counts reached 200/100 ml.
- Fecal coliform averaged 275/100 ml below the BLM campground (state full-body-contact standard: 200/100 ml). Fecal streptococci averaged 224/100 ml.
- Riparian areas are less than 1% of Arizona's surface area but support more than 70% of its wildlife species (USDA Forest Service, 1977).

**Management recommendations (six)**
1. Limit or eliminate livestock grazing in riparian areas to restore bank stability and stream cover.
2. Reduce or adjust the feral burro population to protect aquatic and riparian habitats.
3. Maintain instream flows and discourage diversions, impoundments, and groundwater withdrawals.
4. Continue monthly water chemistry and biological monitoring, including Santa Maria River monitoring downstream of the Anderson Mine and heavy-metal monitoring near the Cyprus-Bagdad mine, with analysis of fish and raptor nest products.
5. Construct a permanent streamflow gauge on Burro Creek, preferably at the old U.S. Highway 93 bridge.
6. Manage the upper Bill Williams drainage as a native non-game fishery; sport fishery potential is considered marginal due to intermittent flow and limited access.

**Other notable findings**
- The upper Bill Williams drainage is described as one of the few remaining Arizona watersheds that flows relatively undisturbed by human activity.
- Black spot (*Uvulifer ambloplitis*) infection was extremely high in native fishes of upper Burro Creek, associated with snail habitat (*Physa virgata*) and belted kingfishers, with no evident debilitating effects.
- Tilapia (200 individuals, probably *Tilapia zillii*) stocked in 1968 probably did not survive the winter of 1968/1969.
- Smallmouth bass (two specimens, April 1977) were not established; their absence in the 1979 collections indicates no reproduction.
