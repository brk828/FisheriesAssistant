# Effects of Operating the Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant on Twin Lakes, Colorado: 1981 Report of Findings

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation report (REC-ERC-83-2, December 1982) presents the 1981 limnological findings from a long-term study of [[Twin Lakes]], Colorado, before and during early operation of the [[Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant]]. The powerplant began testing in July 1981 and operated sporadically from September 1981, so the report states that ecological effects are "still speculative." The study combines physical, chemical, plankton, benthos, creel and fishery data, with appendices on [[Entrainment Studies]] (Colorado Cooperative Fishery Research Unit) and [[Creel Survey]] results (Colorado Division of Wildlife). The authors conclude that both lakes remained oligotrophic and that 1981, a drought year, produced notable changes in the upper lake, the lower lake's chironomid populations, and the [[Mt. Elbert Forebay]] biota.

## Background & Description

- **Study setting:** Twin Lakes lie at 2802 m above mean sea level on Lake Creek, at the eastern front of the Sawatch Range. The upper lake has a maximum surface area of 263.4 ha and the lower lake 736.5 ha, with maximum depths of about 28 m and 27 m. The lower lake is described as "the largest natural mountain lake in Colorado" (Pennak, 1966).
- **Research history:** Ecological studies began in 1971. Quarterly reports date from 1976. The study was planned to continue through at least 1984, when a second 100-megawatt unit and the new Twin Lakes Dam were expected to begin operation.
- **Management context:** Outlet control works, channel dredging, human activity, and the introduction of [[Rainbow Trout]] (Salmo gairdneri), [[Lake Trout]] (Salvelinus namaycush), and [[Mysis Shrimp]] (Mysis relicta) have altered the original ecology since the turn of the century. Use of the new Twin Lakes Dam was attempted in late 1980 but terminated in early 1981 pending seepage control modifications.
- **Mysis introduction:** Mysis shrimp were introduced in 1958 as a food source for lake trout. The introduction was "extremely successful"; [[Nesler]] (1981) reported densities up to 510 per square meter with a benthic sled trawl.
- **Methods overview:** Five Hydrolab meteorological-limnological stations, Van Dorn water sampling, 14C primary productivity, chlorophyll sampling at 0.1, 1, 3, 5, 9 and 15 m, plankton hauls with a No. 20 (0.076 mm) net, and Ekman dredge benthos sampling sieved at 0.589 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical and chemical conditions**

- Both lakes were "relatively cool, well oxygenated, poorly buffered, and well into an oxidized state" in 1981.
- Average temperature of the upper lake was 1 to 2 °C cooler than the lower lake. Peak average temperatures were 10.7 °C (upper) and 12.8 °C (lower) in late August.
- The lakes were ice-free for 244 days (April 21 to December 23), compared with 208 days in 1980. They were thermally stratified from about April 30 to October 16 (about 169 days).
- The hypolimnion stayed at or below 10 °C all year. The authors state that because the optimum temperature for lake trout is below 10 °C, Twin Lakes "remain as an excellent lake trout habitat."
- Dissolved oxygen averaged more than 90 percent saturation in the top 13 m from May 1 to November 10, 1981. The lowest concentration measured was 1.7 mg/L (about 18 percent saturation) on October 1 at the bottom of the lower lake.
- Average pH was lower at 13 m than at 1 m in both lakes. Conductivity was below 90 µS/cm, and the water is poorly buffered.
- Light transmissivity was commonly between 50 and 80 percent.

**Hydrology**

- 1981 was a drought year. June inflow was 63 percent of the 1971-81 average, and July inflow was 52 percent. Total inflow was 70 percent of the 10-year average.
- Inflow from Mt. Elbert Conduit, carrying water from Turquoise Reservoir, substantially raised October through December flows to 660, 907, and 1,171 percent of average.
- Total outflow for 1981 was 118 percent of normal, indicating a loss of water from storage.

**Fishery and benthos**

- The [[Mysis Shrimp]] population in Twin Lakes is clumped and was estimated by [[Bergersen and Maiolie]] using phototrawling at a geometric mean of 102 shrimp/m² (upper lake) and 112 shrimp/m² (lower lake). The forebay estimate was 32 shrimp/m².
- Chironomids (nonbiting midges), oligochaetes, [[Pea Clams]] (Pisidium spp.), and Mysis were the four benthic groups examined.
- Lake trout gill-net samples in November 1981 gave a mean of 8.3 lake trout per net in the lower lake (deep stations 11.3, shallow 3.6), up from earlier years.
- Winter creel survey results show a rainbow trout–dominated harvest, with lake trout second in relative abundance.

**Heavy metals**

- Metals derive mainly from natural sources above 3600 m, plus human influences such as road building, mining, and forest harvest. Metals accumulate in the upper lake sediments.
- In the late winter of 1974–75, bottom hypolimnetic anaerobic conditions released toxic metals. The upper lake's benthos was "almost totally destroyed," with almost 5 years needed for recovery. The authors note that this condition has not recurred but that the potential for a major release "is always present."

## Key Findings

**Physical-chemical**

- Mt. Elbert operation began testing in July 1981 and sporadic operation in September 1981.
- The inflow decrease during drought (see above) produced greater water clarity and higher phytoplankton density in the upper lake.
- Conductivity ranged from 69 to 102 µS/cm (upper) and 73 to 92 µS/cm (lower). Average conductivity was 87 (upper) and 81 (lower) µS/cm, about 10 percent above 1980, though not statistically significant.
- Redox potential ranged 266–439 mV (upper) and 296–465 mV (lower). Both lakes remained in an oxidizing state all year.
- The lakes turned over in spring for about 10 days and in fall for about 69 days.

**Nutrients**

- Average TKN in 1981 was about 200 µg/L, nearly double the level for 1979 and 1980.
- Nitrate nitrogen was under 10 µg/L until late June, with net retention in the lakes.
- Orthophosphate reached up to 20 µg/L in mid-1981, and total phosphorus reached 50 µg/L in the first set of samples. The authors doubt these values, which were not matched by productivity increases, and consider the second set (lower values) more realistic.
- The authors maintain that Twin Lakes are phosphorus-poor, and biota respond dramatically to any phosphorus input.

**Plankton and productivity**

- Mean areal primary productivity averaged about 12 g C/(m²·a) (upper) and 26 g C/(m²·a) (lower). The lower lake has been more than twice as productive on average.
- Phytoplankton ranged from about 700/L (upper, January and October) to nearly 5000/L (upper, September). The September bloom was 99 percent Dinobryon. Lower lake counts ranged from less than 200/L to over 18,000/L in March.
- Zooplankton densities were 22/L (upper) and 53/L (lower) in 1981 (Table 9 averages), compared with 13 and 47 in 1979.
- Cladocerans (Bosmina) were nearly eliminated after the introduction of mysis in 1958, consistent with predation described by Goldman et al. (1979) for Lake Tahoe.

**Benthos**

- Pisidium clams were the most abundant benthos in the lower lake in 1981, about 18 percent more abundant than chironomids.
- Chironomid abundance in the lower lake dropped significantly after powerplant operation began in July. The authors state that this may be due to powerplant effects but note the cause remains unclear.

**Mt. Elbert Forebay**

- The forebay was drained, lined with chlorinated polyethylene, and refilled with Turquoise Lake water between fall 1979 and spring 1981.
- Chlorophyll a ranged from 2.0 to 5.2 mg/m³ in late 1981, versus 0.3 to 1.9 mg/m³ in 1978–79.
- Mysis shrimp were estimated at 35 million in late 1981, with 32 shrimp/m² (95% CI 19–52).
- The forebay is described as having a "hybrid nature," with features of both Turquoise Lake and Twin Lakes.

**Trophic status**

- Twin Lakes were categorized as oligotrophic on seven of seven limnological parameters, with some individual values in 1981 approaching meso- or eutrophic levels. The authors state that these were random in time and location and that the mean values place the lakes in the oligotrophic category.

**Fishery and entrainment**

- Creel data for 1981 show lower-lake open-water harvest of 28,362 fish and upper-lake harvest of 7,171 fish.
- Rainbow trout made up 82 to 99 percent of harvest in all categories.
- Forebay gill nets captured 52 fish in mid-November, including 15 rainbow trout, 12 of which were "literally gorged with mysis shrimp."
- Three certain mortalities were observed in the forebay (two small lake trout and a longnose sucker).
- Under-ice current measurements on February 20, 1982 showed a large counterclockwise gyre across the western two-thirds of the lower lake, with velocities 40 to 474 cm/min, while ice thickness ranged 34 to 58 cm.

**Recommendations and planned work (1982–83)**

- Continue the stratified random creel survey and standardized lake trout gill-net sampling.
- Resume fin-clip marking of all stocked rainbow trout; reduce rainbow stocking from 87,000 to 70,000 for 1982–83.
- Continue monitoring of entrainment with the tailrace barge and forebay net, and of mysis populations in Twin Lakes and the forebay.
- Phase II monitoring depends on establishing a routine operating schedule at the powerplant.
