# Razorback Sucker Collection and Transport from Lake Mohave (January 1981)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This field report documents the January 1981 collection of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) broodstock at [[Cottonwood Cove]] on [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona, and their transport to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. A total of 134 fish (76 females, 58 males) was loaded and delivered alive in good condition after a roughly 900 lb, one-day haul. The report also includes a fragmentary OCR table of trammel-net catch rates for Lake Mohave from 1975 to 1980, in which [[Carp]], [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Bluegill]] are the most identifiable species.

## Background & Description

The collection crew assembled on the east side of Cottonwood Cove on January 12, 1981. It included personnel from the Southwest region (Jim Johnson and Jerry Burton, SE), Mike Donahoo (Fishery Services), [[Dr. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]], and other service staff. Dean Elliot and Troy Winham arrived on January 14 with the distribution truck.

Fish were taken by several methods: [[Gill Netting]], [[Electrofishing]] (electro-shocking) and [[Trammel Netting]]. Razorbacks were captured in shallow coves in two to three feet of water. Observed spawning areas had been cleaned of silt, leaving smooth gravel (the exact particle size range is illegible in the scan, but the gravel is described as fractional-inch material).

Handling and holding procedures:
- Fish were brought to a base camp, treated with [[Furacin]] and held in holding nets before shipment.
- On January 15, fish were injected with 10 mg of [[Terramycin]] per pound of body weight as a precaution against bacterial infection.
- Males averaged about 5 lb and females about 8 lb.
- Fish were loaded in wet burlap sacks to reduce rough handling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water quality was a central concern during transport:
- Lake water dissolved oxygen (DO) was about 12–13 ppm.
- Oxygen treatment with two aerators, using the supplied O2 volume, gave roughly the same DO on the truck. The crew therefore carried the fish in untreated lake water with no added chemicals.
- Transport DO held at 10–13 ppm, with water temperatures of 49–52°F during the trip.

On arrival, fish were at 51°F and were gradually tempered to 64°F in the holding house. The report notes that the fish tolerated this change well.

The Lake Mohave trammel-net survey table covers catch per 100 m of netting for 1975–1980 across nine sampling dates (sampled effort ranged from 1,561 to 5,202 net-meters, as legible). Species columns include carp, green sunfish and bluegill. The scan is heavily corrupted, so species-level values and totals cannot be reliably reconstructed and are not reported here.

## Key Findings

- **Collection success:** 134 Razorback Suckers (76 females, 58 males) were collected at Cottonwood Cove on Lake Mohave and loaded for transport (approx. 900 lb total).
- **Transport outcome:** The fish arrived alive at Dexter National Fish Hatchery on January 16, 1981, at 8 a.m. They were in excellent condition given the trapping and handling.
- **Water quality management:** Lake water at 10–13 ppm DO was judged sufficient, so no chemicals were added to the transport water.
- **Thermal acclimation:** Fish arrived at 51°F and were tempered to 64°F without apparent problems.
- **Health observations:** Some hemorrhaging, attributed to electro-shocking, and fungal problems from handling were noted. Terramycin was used prophylactically.
- **Spawning habitat:** Razorbacks were found spawning in shallow coves over clean smooth gravel, a habitat condition relevant to their reproductive ecology.
- **Operational assessment:** The author judged the operation "very professional," with collection efforts "excellent" and fish arriving "in better shape than we had hoped."
- **Survey context:** The 1975–1980 Lake Mohave trammel-net table documents fish catch per effort over several years, but the scan is too degraded for quantitative use.
