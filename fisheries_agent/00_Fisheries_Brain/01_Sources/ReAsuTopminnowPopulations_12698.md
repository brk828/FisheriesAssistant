# Re: ASU topminnow populations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #Ancyrocephalidae #fish-health #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary
This 1996 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service documents a parasitological health survey of four captive [[Gila topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) populations maintained at Arizona State University (ASU) for conservation purposes. The populations originated from four distinct source localities: **Bylas Spring**, **Monkey Spring**, **Cienega Creek**, and **Sheehy Spring**. Comprehensive external and internal examinations revealed a single ectoparasite — a monogenetic trematode of the family **[[Ancyrocephalidae]]** — present on the gills of approximately 50% of sampled fish from three of the four populations. The Sheehy Spring population was found to be parasite-free. Based on the apparent host-specificity and benign nature of the infection, the examining fish health specialist recommended that the ASU stocks be cleared for reintroduction/translocation **without prophylactic treatment**.

## Background & Description
This document is a health/parasitology report rather than a morphological study of the host fish; however, key parasite-related morphological notes include:

- **Parasite taxon:** Monogenetic trematode (Monogenea), family **[[Ancyrocephalidae]]**
- **Taxonomic affinity:** Provisionally identified as consistent with **[[Cleidodiscus]] sp.**, based on prior identification from the Bylas Spring population (reference specimen S-1)
- **Site of infection:** Exclusively the **gills** — no parasites were detected on skin, eyes, brain, gastrointestinal tract, or within the body cavity
- **Infection prevalence:** ~50% of examined fish (in three of four populations)
- **Host condition:** Fish were assessed to be in **excellent physical condition** despite parasite presence, suggesting a low-pathogenicity or co-evolved host-parasite relationship

No morphometric, meristic, or external body-shape data on the topminnows themselves are provided in this source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Host species:** [[Gila topminnow]], a small-bodied, live-bearing freshwater fish endemic to the Gila River basin of Arizona and Sonora, Mexico — federally listed as endangered and a focal species for reintroduction efforts.
- **Habitat type:** Isolated desert spring and creek ecosystems, characterized by small, often disconnected freshwater refugia critical to the species' survival.
- **Source populations sampled:**
  - [[Bylas Spring]]
  - [[Monkey Spring]]
  - [[Cienega Creek]]
  - [[Sheehy Spring]] (parasite-negative)
- **Host-parasite ecology:** The consistent presence of the same Ancyrocephalid trematode across geographically separate populations (except Sheehy Spring) raises the hypothesis of a **long-term co-evolved association** between the topminnow host and this monogenean, potentially representing an **undescribed species** restricted to this host lineage.
- **Conservation context:** ASU-held populations function as a captive refuge/broodstock resource supporting reintroduction efforts for this endangered species under USFWS's Arizona Fish and Wildlife Resource Office (AZFRO) recovery program.

## Key Findings
1. **No systemic parasitism detected** — external (gill, skin) and internal (eyes, brain, GI tract, body cavity) examinations found parasites confined solely to the gills.
2. **Monogenetic trematode identified** in the family Ancyrocephalidae, likely conspecific with a previously noted *Cleidodiscus*-type organism from Bylas Spring (voucher S-1).
3. **Prevalence ~50%** in Bylas Spring, Monkey Spring, and Cienega Creek populations; **absent** in the Sheehy Spring population.
4. **Overall fish health rated excellent**, with no clinical signs of disease burden despite gill parasitism.
5. **Management recommendation:** ASU topminnow stocks were cleared for **introduction/translocation without treatment**, indicating low perceived risk of pathogen spread or host harm.
6. **Scientific hypothesis raised:** The trematode may represent a **novel, undescribed species** that co-evolved with *Poeciliopsis occidentalis*, warranting further taxonomic investigation.
7. Distribution addressed to **Dr. W.L. Minckley**, a leading authority on Southwestern U.S. desert fishes, underscoring the report's relevance to regional [[endangered species management]] and [[desert fish conservation]] efforts.
