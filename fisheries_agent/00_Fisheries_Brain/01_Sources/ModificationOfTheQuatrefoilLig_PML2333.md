# Modification of the Quatrefoil Light Trap for Use in Hatchery Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #morone-saxatilis

## Executive Summary

This technical note describes modifications to the [[Quatrefoil Light Trap]], a light-attracted larval fish sampler, so it can collect [[Striped Bass]] fry in earthen [[Hatchery Pond Management|hatchery production ponds]]. The modifications replace the electric lamp with [[Chemical Light Sticks]], add a floating body, and add a funnel-based collection assembly that can be sampled like a plankton net. The authors also loosened construction tolerances, cutting the unit cost by 65%. In 1-h night deployments, the modified traps captured an average of 58 fry per trap across 89 deployments, with performance strongly tied to darkness and fry age. The authors argue the design can quantify early growth, dispersal, and abundance, which conventional single-beam light sampling cannot.

## Background & Description

Striped bass larvae and juveniles are hard to sample in pond production because they do not recruit to [[Seine Gear]] until about 25 mm standard length (SL). Standard practice evaluates fry at night using a light beam shone into the water, but that beam illuminates less than 0.5 m² and therefore yields little information on growth, survival, or dispersal.

The original [[Quatrefoil Light Trap]], designed by [[Floyd Et Al 1984]], consists of four clear Plexiglas tubes cut longitudinally around a central electric lamp. Slots admit larvae from four directions, and a cloverleaf-shaped interior limits their exit. The authors found it effective but cumbersome to deploy and awkward to sample.

The modified trap is built from:
- Four clear acrylic tubes (30.5 cm long, 7.6 cm inside diameter, 0.32 cm walls), each with two longitudinal cuts 90° apart, arranged in a cloverleaf between 22.9 cm acrylic plates.
- Gaps of 0.76 to 1.27 cm for fry passage, wider than the 0.15 cm gaps in the original design, which excluded larger larvae and juveniles. Gap size can be adjusted to select for particular fry sizes.
- A 2.54 cm top hole with a sealed small tube for adjustment, and a 30.5 × 30.5 × 2.54 cm Styrofoam float for flexible placement.
- A funnel collection assembly (22.9 cm plastic funnel on PVC neck and removable cod end, with three 3.8 cm water-passage holes and 1 mm mesh screen).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fry sampling matters because growth, survival, and dispersal are central to judging [[Hatchery Pond Management|pond production]]. The modified trap samples the water column and attracts larvae from all directions in its illumination field, giving a more representative picture than a single light beam. Performance depends on environmental conditions. Captures were strongly affected by night darkness and water clarity, and the illuminated radius reached about 3 m depending on those conditions. Predators and other organisms were also a consideration in the cod end, though the document gives limited detail on them.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling performance:** The modified trap captured fry (7–35 mm SL) with an average of 58 fry per trap (SD = 91) in 89 one-hour deployments. The authors note that catches were highly variable.
- **Effect of darkness:** Traps worked best in the darkest conditions:
  - Dusk (N = 11): average 18 fry per trap (SD = 19; fry age 12 d).
  - New moon (N = 13): average 121 fry per trap (SD = 123; fry age 16 d).
  - Cloudy night (N = 8): average 201 fry per trap (SD = 156; fry age 23 d).
- **Controls:** Traps without light sticks (N = 8) averaged less than one fry per trap (mode 0).
- **Fry age:** Age influenced catch (ANOVA, N = 89, F = 4.8, P = 0.003) but showed no consistent trend.
- **No effect of light color or pond depth:** White versus green light and pond depth did not affect trap performance (reported as P = 0.22 for light color; the abstract states neither factor had an effect).
- **Enclosure test:** A separate test in 2.5 m³ enclosures with older larvae was used to estimate sampling efficiency. The OCR text for this result is garbled, so the exact figure (reported as roughly 30% of fry) should be checked against the original.
- **Cost:** Acrylic construction cost US $25.00, and the Styrofoam float and funnel assembly added less than $15.00 for supplies and labor. Relaxing construction precision reduced the price per unit by 65%.
- **Light source:** [[Chemical Light Sticks]] (15 cm long, 1.8 cm diameter) burn for 6–24 h, are durable, waterproof, and inexpensive, and fit the original trap dimensions.
- **Deployment:** A float permits flexible placement, and the funnel lets the trap be lifted vertically like a plankton net.
- **Management application:** The authors conclude the modified trap can improve [[Hatchery Pond Management]] by yielding fry samples for quantifying early growth, dispersal, and abundance, with the caveat that sampling is highly sensitive to night conditions.
