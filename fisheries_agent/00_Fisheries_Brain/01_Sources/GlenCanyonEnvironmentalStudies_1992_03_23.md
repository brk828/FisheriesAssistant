## Executive Summary

This document is a **1992 administrative memorandum and meeting agenda** from the Bureau of Reclamation's [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, coordinating a joint meeting with the [[Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS) team. It is **not a primary scientific data report**, but rather a logistical planning document outlining which researchers should attend, meeting agenda topics, and a distribution list of subject-matter experts across disciplines (sediment/hydrology, aquatic biology, terrestrial biology, recreation, economics, cultural resources). No direct morphological, physiological, or population data on fish species are presented. However, the document is valuable as a record of the **institutional and research structure** underpinning post-dam Colorado River ecosystem studies, and it identifies fish and an aquatic snail (likely the [[Kanab Ambersnail]]) as key Threatened & Endangered (T&E) species topics slated for discussion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no direct ecological data are given, the agenda structure implies the ecological framework used by GCES to assess dam impacts on the Colorado River ecosystem in [[Glen Canyon]] and [[Grand Canyon]]:

- **Aquatic Biology Research Team**: Includes Dr. Richard Valdez ([[Bio/West Inc.]]), Dennis Kubly and James DeVos ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), Dr. Wendell Minckley ([[Arizona State University]]) — a noted expert on Southwestern native fishes — and Dr. Richard Lechleitner ([[Bureau of Reclamation]]).
- **Agenda Topic – Fish (Larry Riley)**: A dedicated 15-minute impact analysis presentation on fish resources tied to the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, indicating fish population/habitat impacts were a core EIS resource category.
- **T&E and Special Status Species session**: Explicitly separates **Fish (Frank Baucom)** and **Snail (Gordy Lind)** as distinct special-status taxa under review — the snail almost certainly referring to the endangered [[Kanab Ambersnail]], a species historically associated with Glen Canyon springs and known to co-occur in studies of dam-regulated riparian/aquatic habitats.
- The overall EIS framework links fish resources to **Water Quality**, **Sediment**, **Vegetation**, and **Wildlife and Habitat** sessions — reflecting the interconnected niche factors (flow regime, temperature, turbidity, sediment transport) that GCES research linked to native fish persistence in the regulated Colorado River below [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

## Key Findings

- The meeting (April 16–17, 1992, Phoenix, AZ) was convened to reconcile EIS team questions with GCES researcher findings from **December 1991 reports**, not to present new data.
- Fish-related impacts were treated as a **standalone resource category** in the EIS impact analysis, separate from broader "Wildlife and Habitat," underscoring the significance of native and endangered fish (e.g., presumably [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and other Colorado River native fishes historically central to GCES research) in dam-relicensing decisions.
- A **Threatened & Endangered (T&E) species subsession** explicitly separated fish and snail issues, suggesting parallel regulatory concern under the Endangered Species Act for both taxa in the Glen Canyon/Grand Canyon reach.
- The distribution list confirms **Dr. W.L. Minckley**, a leading ichthyologist specializing in desert fishes of the American Southwest, was a designated aquatic biology contact — reinforcing that native Colorado River fish conservation was a major scientific thread of the GCES program.
- The document establishes the **administrative and interdisciplinary linkage** between hydrology/sediment transport, water quality, and fish resources — a foundational structure later used in quantifying [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operational impacts on the [[Colorado River]] aquatic ecosystem.
