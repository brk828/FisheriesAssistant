# Colorado River Fish Recovery Plan Update Correspondence and Format Guidance

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a February 3, 1987 letter from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) Colorado/Platte River Endangered Species Recovery Coordinator to Dr. W. L. Minckley of Arizona State University. It requests literature review for the planned 1987 updates of three [[Recovery Plan]]s for the endangered Colorado River fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. It also asks for a reference list on the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The attached material is followed by a set of FWS guidelines on how recovery plans should be structured, with emphasis on the [[Implementation Schedule]] and its [[Priority System]].

## Background & Description

The letter states that recovery plans for the three Colorado River fishes were drafted in 1983 but placed on hold pending the outcome of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee]] effort. Since then, substantial new information has become available, especially on the Colorado Squawfish and Humpback Chub. The FWS requested:

- Review of an attached list of published and unpublished reports available since 1980, with needed changes, additions, or deletions identified, including page and report numbers.
- Copies of any newly identified reports and of reports highlighted on the current list.
- Review of a separate list of razorback sucker references, to maintain a current library for recovery work on that species.

Reports were being abstracted for the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]], with plan updates scheduled for March–April 1987. The letter was signed by John Hamill, with Richard Wydoski as an additional contact. The attached report lists themselves are not reproduced in the source text.

The remainder of the document is a guidance text on recovery plan preparation, with the following components:

- **Part I, Introduction:** defines the problems facing the species, including habitat requirements, limiting factors, distribution, status, commercial significance, and conservation efforts. It also includes a problem analysis (environmental, biological, and other influences) and a set of recovery strategies.
- **Part II, Recovery:** states quantitative objectives for delisting or reclassification, a [[Stepdown Outline]] of tasks, and a narrative for each task, plus literature cited.
- **Part III, Implementation Schedule:** assigns tasks, agencies, priorities, schedules, and funding over a three-year planning horizon.
- **Appendix:** lists reviewers and supporting documents.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The source does not provide ecological data on the four fishes (no population counts, life history data, or water chemistry). Its environmental content is limited to general recovery concerns:

- **Habitat protection:** essential habitat should be identified as specifically as possible, with specific locations and sizes included when they can be given without increasing the risk of take.
- **Physical and biological threats:** the guidance lists natural disasters, human activities, pollution, competition, hybridization, loss of food supply and habitat, predation, and disease as categories to be analyzed.
- **Over-utilization and law enforcement:** the guidance notes that increased law enforcement is often needed for recovery and should be coordinated with FWS law enforcement offices during plan preparation.
- **Funding link:** the FWS will not request Land and Water Conservation Funds for listed-species projects without an approved recovery plan. A draft plan is sufficient if the threat to the habitat is high.

## Key Findings

- **Update status:** Colorado River fish recovery plans were drafted in 1983 but held pending the Upper Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee process. New data, particularly on the [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]], prompted the 1987 update.
- **Literature review request:** Dr. Minckley was asked to review report lists (reports since 1980), suggest corrections, and supply copies of new or highlighted reports. A separate [[Razorback Sucker]] reference library is being maintained.
- **Timeline:** Abstracts are to go to the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team for plan updates scheduled for March–April 1987.
- **Implementation Schedule importance:** the schedule is the central working component. It tracks task completion and funding, and it guides review of permits, Section 7 consultations, and funding requests. Tasks should be specific, with agency assignments and manpower estimates where known.
- **Priority definitions:**
  - Priority 1: must be taken to prevent extinction or irreversible decline in the foreseeable future. Requires strong justification in the narrative. The guidance warns that inflated priorities reduce the credibility of the ranking system.
  - Priority 2: must be taken to prevent significant decline in population or habitat quality, or other significant negative impact short of extinction.
  - Priority 3: all other actions needed for full recovery or reclassification.
- **Plan objectives:** the primary objective should be quantitative and tied to the factors that caused listing. Where data are insufficient, interim objectives must be stated with a reassessment point. Objectives should be reviewed at least annually.
- **Planning horizon:** the Implementation Schedule should cover the next three years and be revised as tasks are completed. The plan must be reviewed at least annually.
- **Format requirements:** plans include a Title page, Disclaimer, optional Acknowledgements and Executive Summary, Table of Contents, Parts I–III, and an Appendix. Narrative task numbers must match the Stepdown Outline.
- **Scope limits:** plans should recommend essential habitat but not specify land-protection strategies (e.g., fee purchase or easement). Experimental population status is determined by regulation, not in the plan.
