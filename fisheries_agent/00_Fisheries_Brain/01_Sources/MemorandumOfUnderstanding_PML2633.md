# Draft Memorandum of Understanding on Endangered Colorado River Fishes (1984)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This draft Memorandum of Understanding (MOU), dated May 17, 1984, sets out a cooperative framework among the [[United States Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), the [[United States Bureau of Reclamation]], and the states of [[Colorado]], [[Utah]], and [[Wyoming]]. The parties aim to conserve threatened and endangered fishes in the Upper Basin of the Colorado River while fully maintaining beneficial uses of water under state water rights systems and interstate compacts. The MOU establishes a Coordinating Committee, a Steering Committee, and a Drafting Committee, defines their duties, and provides for staffing and decision-making safeguards. Its stated term expires March 31, 1985 unless extended. Much of the text is marked "to be rewritten," so the document is a working draft.

## Background & Description

The parties' authority rests on two statutes:

- **[[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA):** The Secretary of the Interior may list and delist species, determine whether federal actions might jeopardize them, and prepare and approve recovery and conservation plans.
- **Title 43, Chapter 12, U.S. Code:** The Bureau of Reclamation may construct and operate water projects.

The states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming manage fish and game resources and oversee water resource development under their own authority. The document names no specific fish species; it refers only to "endangered Colorado River fishes."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses freshwater, not marine, systems. Its concern is the balance between species conservation and water use in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The parties recognize that water apportioned under the [[Colorado River Compact]] and related compacts must be honored. Threats and management tools listed for consideration include:

- Competition with non-native species
- Hatchery and stocking programs
- Fish passage facilities
- Establishment of preserves
- Instream flows, water delivery, and reregulation
- Responses to [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the ESA

No population counts, habitat measurements, or water chemistry data are reported.

## Key Findings

**Committee structure**

- **[[Coordinating Committee]]:** Includes the USFWS Region 6 Regional Director, two Bureau of Reclamation regional directors (Upper Colorado and Lower Missouri), the executive directors of the Colorado and Utah Departments of Natural Resources, and a representative of Wyoming's governor. It may add members.
- **Executive Director:** Frank Dunkle, a USFWS representative, was selected to serve. Duties include circulating draft recommendations, keeping minutes and records, running a public information program, coordinating reports from other committees, and serving as the link between committees and agencies.
- **[[Steering Committee]]:** Chaired by the Coordinating Committee's Executive Director. Each party may appoint a biologist and a water expert. It develops a work plan (subject to Coordinating Committee approval), and it creates hydrological and biological subcommittees.
- **[[Drafting Committee]]:** Convened as needed to review draft plans and findings. Its membership definition is still "to be rewritten."

**Stated purposes of the Coordinating Committee**

- Build shared understanding of scientific data on factors affecting the presence and future of endangered fishes.
- Evaluate conservation strategies, including hatcheries, non-native species control, fish passage, preserves, instream flows, and water delivery, while preserving water uses.
- Prepare a conservation plan that defines federal, state, and private responsibilities and reasonable and prudent alternatives.
- Develop strategies for responding to Section 7 consultations during the MOU term.

**Process and transparency**

- Draft recommendations must be presented at a public roundtable meeting before final recommendations are submitted.
- Parties will share all necessary data, though litigation-related material may be withheld.
- Steering Committee findings go out for peer review.

**Staffing and decision-making**

- Each agency contributes staff time within its resources and priorities. Any additional funds would support an operating plan designed by the Coordinating Committee.
- The MOU does not delegate or abrogate any party's statutory decision-making authority. Parties agree to seek mutually acceptable solutions.

**Term and membership changes**

- The MOU expires March 31, 1985, unless a majority of Coordinating Committee members extend it.
- Parties may withdraw with written notice. The committee continues as long as a majority of the remaining members wish.
- Possible future members include the Western Area Power Administration, the National Forest Service, the National Park Service, the Bureau of Land Management, New Mexico, and the Corps of Engineers. User groups may attend as observers.
