## Executive Summary

This document is a **Call for Papers** announcement — not a primary research article — for a symposium and workshop titled "Restoring Native Fish to the Lower Colorado River: Interactions of Native and Non-native Fishes," held July 13–14, 1999, in Las Vegas, Nevada. It was jointly sponsored by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]]. The stated purpose is to disseminate state-of-the-art methods and concepts for restoring native fish populations in the [[Lower Colorado River]], with particular emphasis on **native versus non-native fish interactions** and management strategies for recovering threatened and endangered species. No species-specific data, quantitative findings, or ecological results are presented in this text; it functions solely as a logistical and administrative announcement soliciting speakers and attendees.

## Background & Description

- **Nature of document**: Administrative call for speakers/papers, not a scientific study. Contains no direct data on fish biology, morphology, or population metrics.
- **Context**: The symposium responds to **Provision #4 of the Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPA)** in the [[Biological Opinion on Lower Colorado River Operations and Maintenance]], which mandates research into "habitat use and habitat preference of native and non-native fishes in order to reduce conflicts."
- **Institutional framework**: Findings and recommendations from the event were intended to feed into the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (LCR MSCP), a major interagency conservation initiative.
- **Publication plan**: Selected speakers were required to submit manuscripts (max. 5,000 words, WordPerfect 5.1+) for a proceedings volume to be published by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
- **Audience**: Open to federal, state, local, and tribal officials, user groups, NGOs (especially LCR MSCP partners), scientists, educators, students, and the public. Attendance capped at 150 registrants, no registration fee.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System of concern**: The [[Lower Colorado River]] is characterized in the text as a **"severely modified"** riverine ecosystem — implying substantial anthropogenic alteration (e.g., damming, flow regulation, habitat fragmentation) that underlies the need for native fish restoration.
- **Core ecological issue framed by the symposium**: Interactions between **native fish species** (implicitly including federally threatened/endangered Colorado River fishes such as species in genera like *Gila*, *Ptychocheilus*, and *Xyrauchen*, though not explicitly named in this text) and **non-native/introduced fish species**, which are broadly recognized in Colorado River management literature as a major driver of native fish decline via competition, predation, and habitat alteration.
- **Management driver**: The explicit regulatory hook is habitat use/preference research to "reduce conflicts" between native and non-native fishes — signaling that non-native species presence and niche overlap with natives is the central environmental problem motivating this workshop.
- **Geographic scope**: Restoration efforts are situated within the broader [[Colorado River Basin]] and specifically its lower reach, an area subject to multi-agency, multi-species conservation planning.

## Key Findings

**No empirical, quantitative, or species-specific scientific findings are contained in this source.** This document is purely a symposium/workshop announcement. The "findings" it conveys are procedural/organizational rather than ecological:

- The event's **stated goal** was to generate "useful information, stimulating ideas, and recommendations" for future native fish restoration in the Lower Colorado River — but the announcement itself does not present any of these outcomes.
- It identifies a **regulatory research gap**: habitat use and habitat preference of native vs. non-native fishes, needed to inform Bureau of Reclamation management decisions.
