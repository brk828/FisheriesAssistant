# A distributed list of the species of freshwater fishes in California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the introduction, bibliography and distributional checklist of the freshwater fishes of California, compiled by Paul C. Marsh with a senior author who began locality records in 1914. It traces the scientific history from the first descriptions (Gibbons, 1854) through the Pacific Railroad Surveys, the work of [[David Starr Jordan]] and [[Charles Henry Gilbert]], and later Snyder, Rutter, Evermann and others. The core product is a systematic list, arranged after the 1920 [[Check-List of North and Middle American Fishes]] (Jordan, Evermann & Clark), giving localities, authorities and dates for each species. The list records 77 freshwater species from California, and 90 nominal freshwater species described from California localities, of which 34 are now synonyms, leaving 56 valid species. An annotated list of introduced species is also included.

## Background & Description

The authors set out to compile a distributional catalogue of species recorded from definite localities in California. Work required critical review of all faunal literature, a chronological bibliography with summaries, and a reconciliation of duplicate names created when the same species was described under different names by different authors.

Key historical milestones:
- **Pre-scientific period:** Alzate y Ramirez (1772) letter on Cyprinodonts; Thornton (1849) book reference. No recognizable species descriptions.
- **Birthday of the field (May 18, 1854):** Dr. [[W. P. Gibbons]] described [[Hysterocarpus Traski]], the only freshwater member of the viviparous perch family, from the lower Sacramento River.
- **Early work (1854–1862):** Ayres, Girard and Gibbons described many new species, largely from San Francisco markets and [[Pacific Railroad Surveys]] collections.
- **Stanford era (from 1891):** [[David Starr Jordan]] and [[Charles Henry Gilbert]] led intensive coastal and inland studies for more than twenty years, with Rutter, Snyder and Chamberlain.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes the fauna across several drainage systems:
- **[[Sacramento-San Joaquin Basin]]:** The richest fauna, with the largest number of native species. Rutter (1908) reported 86 species for the basin, including many tributary collection sites.
- **[[Klamath River]] and Lakes:** Distinct fauna, with species such as Klamath roach and Klamath sucker.
- **Russian River:** Shares some fluvial species with the Sacramento, but the drainages were isolated. Snyder (1908) argued the transfer direction was from the Sacramento to the Russian River, opposing Holway (1907).
- **[[Lahontan System]] and Great Basin:** Isolated endorheic basins (Eagle Lake, Honey Lake, Lake Tahoe, Truckee, Carson, Walker rivers) with endemic trout and suckers.
- **[[Owens Valley]]:** Four native species (Catostomus arenarius, Siphateles obesus, Agosia robusta, Cyprinodon macularius); fauna partly derived from the Lahontan System.
- **[[Salton Sea]] and desert springs:** Desert minnow (Cyprinodon macularius) and introduced Gambusia.

Coastal streams from Monterey Bay to the Oregon border host several salt-tolerant or anadromous species (sculpins, stickleback, lamprey, sturgeon, salmon).

## Key Findings

- **Species count:** The distributional list reports 77 freshwater species from California, across 12 families: Petromyzonidae (2), Acipenseridae (2), Salmonidae (22), Catostomidae (9), Cyprinidae (27), Cyprinodontidae (2), Empetrichthyidae (1), Gasterosteidae (1), Centrarchidae (1), Cottidae (8), Embiotocidae (1), Gobiidae (1).
- **Nomenclature:** 90 nominal freshwater species were described from California localities; 34 are now synonyms and 56 remain valid.
- **Key early descriptions:** [[Hysterocarpus Traski]] (Gibbons 1854), [[Archoplites interruptus]] (Sacramento Perch), [[Lavinia exilicauda]] (Sacramento Hitch), [[Ptychocheilus grandis]] (Sacramento Pikeminnow).
- **Trout diversity:** Numerous endemic trout forms were described by Jordan and co-workers in the 1890s, including [[Salmo Stonei]], [[Salmo Gilberti]], [[Salmo Agua-Bonita]] (Golden Trout), and [[Salmo Roosevelti]]. Many were later reassessed as subspecies or variants.
- **Historic claims:** Girard (1856–1859) described species from Pacific Railroad Survey collections, often with misattributed localities (e.g., Monterey Presidio, Fort Reading).
- **Native sturgeon:** Green Sturgeon (Acipenser acutirostris) was considered abundant in the Bay and rivers but regarded as poisonous by fishermen (Lockington 1879).
- **Introduced species:** The annotated list includes about 30 introductions. Notable successes were the [[American Shad]] (Sacramento River, 1871), [[Striped Bass]] (Suisun Bay, 1879), largemouth and smallmouth black bass ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]]), and catfish. Failures included Atlantic salmon, Montana grayling and Atlantic eel. Brown trout and Loch Leven trout were established and used in state hatcheries.
- **Conservation signal:** Snyder (1905) recorded Sacramento sucker and others disappearing in drought conditions, and Snyder (1908) documented the ecological and biogeographic relationships of Russian River and Sacramento fauna.
- **Taxonomic revisions:** Jordan (1885) and Snyder (1917, 1919) compared types and synonymized several names (e.g., Siphateles obesus and S. oliváceus), reinforcing the value of the synthesis.
