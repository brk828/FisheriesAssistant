# Draft-- Spawning and movements of razorback sucker, Xyrauchen texanus, in the Green River Basin of Colorado and Utah

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This study by Tyus and Karp examines spawning behavior, movement patterns, and habitat use of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the Green and Yampa rivers of the upper [[Colorado River Basin]] during 1987-1989, using radiotelemetry (n=14) and capture-recapture methods (n=231 adults, 106 recaptures). Key findings include the identification of two primary spawning aggregation sites, spawning tied to ascending spring flows and water temperatures near 14°C, significant migratory movements (up to 105.6 km one-way), and evidence of hybridization between razorback sucker and native [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]. The study underscores that this Green River basin population represents the largest extant riverine population of razorback sucker, yet faces continued extinction risk due to low recruitment, hybridization, and habitat loss from dam-altered flow regimes.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a monotypic catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically abundant enough to support commercial harvest (Jordan, 1891). Natural populations persist only in:
- Lakes Mohave and Havasu (lower Colorado River)
- A short reach of the upper Colorado River near Grand Junction, CO
- Sporadically in the lower San Juan River
- The [[Green River]] and [[Yampa River]] (Utah/Colorado) — the focus of this study, and the **largest extant riverine population**

Populations are characterized by aging individuals with minimal to nonexistent recruitment (McCarthy and Minckley, 1987; Tyus, 1987; Marsh and Minckley, 1989). At the time of this study, the species was under consideration for federal listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and was already protected by five western states.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Study Area
Encompasses the Green, Yampa, and White rivers of the upper Colorado River basin, including:
- Upper Green River (RK 396.8-552)
- Lower Yampa River (RK 0-72, Yampa Canyon)
- Duchesne River and Ashley Creek confluences
- Stewart Lake Drain and Old Charley Wash (flooded bottomlands)
- Proximity to [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and [[Dinosaur National Monument]]

### Two Key Spawning Aggregation Sites
1. **Jensen Site**: Mouth of Ashley Creek to lower boundary of Dinosaur National Monument (RK 476.8-504), including Stewart Lake Drain — **91% of breeding captures (n=182)**
2. **Yampa Site**: Lower 5.0 km of Yampa River — **7% of breeding captures (n=14)**

### Spawning Microhabitat
- Substrate: cobble, gravel, sand (submerged shoreline riffles)
- Average depth: ~0.61 m
- Average velocity: ~0.64 m/s
- Specific identified spawning locations:
  - Yampa River: left channel at mouth (RK 0.8); left shoreline above Echo Park Campground (RK 0.16)
  - Green River: right shoreline/channel below Green River Bus Campground (RK 491.2-500.8)

### Flow & Temperature Regime
- Spawning coincided with **ascending and highest spring flows**
- Average water temperature: **~14°C** (range 9-17°C)
  - Yampa site: mean 13.8°C (range 10.5-16°C)
  - Jensen site: mean 14.1°C (range 9-17°C)
- This is notably **below the 20°C optimal hatch temperature** identified by Marsh (1985), raising concern that Flaming Gorge Dam-altered thermal/flow regimes may impair larval hatch success.

### Associated Species & Hybridization
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]] co-occurred in breeding condition on the same spawning riffles.
  - Flannelmouth sucker: 2x more abundant than razorback sucker at Jensen site
  - Bluehead and flannelmouth suckers ripe for longer periods (42-45 days) than razorback sucker
- **17 suspected Flannelmouth x Razorback hybrids** captured in 1989 (14 ripe males, 3 ripe females) in Yampa Canyon and upper Green River, identified via skull/mouth/nape morphology — furthest upstream detection 67.2 km up Yampa Canyon.
- Hybridization/introgression risk linked to flannelmouth abundance, razorback scarcity, and spawning overlap.

### Flooded Lowland Habitats
- ~18% of ripe razorback suckers (n=56, combined with historic data) captured in low-velocity bottomlands (Old Charley Wash, Stewart Lake Drain) and flooded tributary mouths (Duchesne River, Ashley Creek) — suspected **resting/feeding habitat** during breeding season, now threatened by impoundment and flow control.

## Key Findings

### Spawning Phenology
- Ripe fish (n=195) captured over a **4-5 week window**, mid-April to early June (1987-1989)
- **Males**: ripe for up to 28 days (avg capture window 33 days, range 25-40)
- **Females**: ripe for only ~5 days (range 2-8 days; avg capture window 29 days, range 27-33)
- Spawning overlapped temporally among razorback, flannelmouth, and bluehead suckers (mid-April to early June, 1989)

### Historical Capture Data (1981-1989 combined)
| Category | n | % of breeding captures |
|---|---|---|
| Breeding males | 185 | 60% |
| Breeding females | 74 | 24% |
| Tuberculated (immature/sex undetermined) | 48 | 16% |
| **Total breeding-condition fish** | 307 | 55% of 560 total adult captures |

### Movement & Migration Patterns
- **80% of tracked/recaptured fish (n=68) were sedentary** — remaining within Jensen site (91%, n=60) or between Island Park and Yampa confluence (8%, n=5)
- **Migratory fish**: movements ranged 52.8-190.4 km
- Two major migration corridors identified:
  1. **Lower Yampa River ↔ Jensen site**: n=4 fish, avg one-way 49.6 km (range 30.4-60.8 km)
  2. **Jensen site ↔ Old Charley Wash/Duchesne River**: n=12 fish, avg one-way 94.4 km (range 54-106 km)
- One radiotagged fish completed a **156 km round trip** (Jensen ↔ Old Charley Wash) between May 4 and August 2, 1988
- Round-trip migrants (Jensen ↔ Old Charley Wash) averaged **177.6 km**
- 16 long-distance migratory fish (7 radiotagged, 9 recaptures over 1-7 years) moved specifically in association with spawning; 10 of these were captured ripe (8 males, 2 females)

### Spawning Site Fidelity
- 31 razorback suckers recaptured in breeding condition at the **same spawning reach in different years** (1 Yampa fish, 30 Jensen fish; 5 Jensen fish recaptured across multiple years)
- Notable case: a male captured ripe at the same lower Yampa River location (RK 0.16) in both **1981 and 1988**, after overwintering in the Jensen area and bypassing the Jensen spawning site to return to its original site — strong evidence of **spawning fidelity**
- Some fish (n=6; 5 males, 1 female) were recaptured at *different* spawning sites within/between years, suggesting either downstream transport during sampling, use of multiple spawning areas, or transit through one site en route to another

### Conservation Threats Identified
1. **Paucity of spawning adults** — aging populations, minimal recruitment
2. **Hybridization** with flannelmouth sucker, potentially exacerbated by spatial/temporal spawning overlap and flannelmouth abundance
3. **Habitat loss** — curtailment of spring flooding, loss of nursery bottomlands, altered thermal regime from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]
4. **Predation by non-native fishes** (per Minckley 1983; Brooks et al. 1985; Marsh and Brooks 1989)
5. **Stream passage blockage** — implicated in decline at other locations where razorback sucker has been extirpated

### Author Recommendations
- Recovery efforts should prioritize restoring seasonal flooding and connectivity to bottomland/tributary habitats
- Further study needed on hybridization dynamics and effects on species decline
- Caution warranted when interpreting spawning site data in turbid systems, given the short "ripe" window in females and potential for now-lost historic spawning sites
