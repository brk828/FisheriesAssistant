# Letter Regarding Cyprinodon macularius Sample from Bog Hole for Genetic Analysis

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1988 letter from Dean A. Hendrickson (Native Fish Biologist, Arizona Game & Fish Department) to Dr. Jeff Mitton (University of Colorado), accompanying a shipment of 32 frozen [[Cyprinodon macularius]] (Desert Pupfish) specimens for electrophoretic genetic analysis. The specimens were collected from **Bog Hole**, an artificial impoundment in the headwaters of the Santa Cruz River (Gila River tributary) in Santa Cruz County, Arizona. The letter raises a taxonomic conservation concern: this non-native population, likely introduced circa 1979 by graduate students associated with Dr. Boyd Kinard, is morphologically suspected to be [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*C. m. eremus*) rather than the native Gila basin subspecies (*C. m. macularius*), which was previously extirpated from this basin. The letter requests genetic clarification to support a planned habitat renovation and reintroduction of the native subspecies, while mitigating hybridization risk.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Cyprinodon macularius* (Desert Pupfish complex)
- **Sample size:** 32 individuals, frozen whole for electrophoretic (allozyme) analysis
- **Key diagnostic observation:** Absence of **yellow caudal fins**, a trait typical of *C. m. macularius* (nominal subspecies) — this absence supports the field identification as [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*C. m. eremus*)
- **Supporting evidence:** Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) reportedly conducted **meristic character analysis** supporting the *eremus* subspecies identification
- **Taxonomic ambiguity:** Exact genetic constitution unclear due to history of mixed pupfish stocks (including hybrid crosses) held by the source researcher (Dr. Kinard) prior to introduction

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Type locality of sample:** Bog Hole — a small artificial impoundment in the headwaters of the [[Santa Cruz River]], a tributary of the [[Gila River]], Santa Cruz County, Arizona
  - Coordinates: ~31°28'N, 110°37'35"W (T22S; R17E; SE1/4, NE1/4, Sec. 32)
- **Population origin:** Non-native to this locality; believed **stocked circa 1979** by graduate students of Dr. Boyd Kinard (formerly University of Arizona), who maintained multiple pupfish stocks including other species and hybrid crosses
- **Native context:** The true native subspecies, *C. m. macularius*, was **extirpated from the Gila River basin** long before this population's appearance; the site had been well-sampled prior to 1979 with no prior pupfish detections
- **Conservation risk:** Potential for escape of this non-native/hybrid population from Bog Hole into the broader [[Gila River]] basin, risking **hybridization** with reintroduced native stock
- **Management plan:** Arizona Game & Fish Department intended to **renovate Bog Hole** and remove this population to reintroduce native *C. m. macularius* as part of a recovery program for the taxon
- **Related population of concern:** [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] source population itself was also being targeted for separate sampling by Mark Stromberg, referenced in the letter's closing

## Key Findings
- A population of pupfish in Bog Hole, presumed introduced (~1979), is morphologically and meristically consistent with [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*C. m. eremus*) rather than the native Gila basin *C. m. macularius*.
- This finding is based on **absence of yellow caudal fin coloration** and meristic analysis by Dr. W. L. Minckley, though full genetic confirmation was pending via electrophoretic analysis at the time of writing.
- The population's presence poses a **hybridization risk** for planned reintroduction efforts of native [[Cyprinodon macularius]] into the Gila River basin.
- This letter documents an early instance of applying **genetic/electrophoretic techniques** to resolve subspecies-level taxonomic uncertainty in imperiled pupfish populations, directly informing a conservation and habitat renovation decision.
- Illustrates broader historical issue of **uncontrolled interagency/academic stocking** of pupfish taxa (including hybrids) creating downstream taxonomic and conservation complications.
- Connects to concurrent efforts to obtain specimens directly from the [[Quitobaquito Springs]] population (via Mark Stromberg) for comparative genetic work.
