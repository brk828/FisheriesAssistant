# A New Chaetodont Fish, Holacanthus limbaughi, from the Eastern Pacific

#ichthyology #marine-biology #chaetodontidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, published in [[Los Angeles County Museum]] *Contributions in Science* No. 74 (December 30, 1963) by Wayne J. Baldwin, describes **Holacanthus limbaughi**, a new species of chaetodontid fish (subfamily [[Pomacanthinae]]) from [[Clipperton Island]] in the [[Eastern Pacific]]. The species is distinguished from its eastern Pacific congeners, [[Holacanthus passer]] and [[Holacanthus clarionensis]], by fin shape (notably concave caudal fin with an elongate upper lobe), uniform dark brown coloration, and the absence of a black caudal margin. The material came from two expeditions in 1956 and 1958 organized by the [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]]. The species is named for [[Conrad Limbaugh]].

## Background & Description

**Study context.** The author collected fishes during two exploratory expeditions to Clipperton Island, a remote coral atoll, in the fall of 1956 and summer of 1958. The expeditions gave the team four weeks of total elapsed time on the island. The author made 30 collections, and the inshore ichthyofauna is considered reasonably well collected. The full collection is to be reported later as a unit; this paper reports the new species in advance.

**Type material.**
- **Holotype:** USNM 178998, adult male, 171.4 mm standard length (SL), collected with explosives on the northwest shore of Clipperton Island, 22 to 29 October 1956.
- **Paratypes:** 9 specimens (70.5 to 214.5 mm SL) in the UCLA and LACM collections, including UCLA 56-236 (2 specimens, 176.1 to 214.5 mm), UCLA 58-287 (3 specimens, 70.5 to 96.6 mm), UCLA 58-289 (1, 198.6 mm), LACM 3308 (1, 187.2 mm), LACM 3309 (1, 200.0 mm), and LACM 3307 (1, 79.0 mm).
- **Specimen distribution:** Of 11 specimens collected, the holotype went to the [[United States National Museum]], six paratypes and one 24.0 mm juvenile went to the [[University of California Los Angeles]] fish collection, and three paratypes went to the Los Angeles County Museum.

**Diagnosis.** A *Holacanthus* with a uniform dark brown body, one light oval spot below the spinous dorsal, a light brown caudal fin without a narrow black margin, a concave caudal fin with the upper rays prolonged, a rounded soft dorsal, and a moderately pointed anal fin.

**Key measurements and counts (holotype; paratype range in parentheses; proportions in thousandths of SL unless noted):**
- Body depth 583 (524–601); head length 272 (262–321); snout length 115 (103–130)
- Length of dorsal fin 793 (737–805); length of upper caudal lobe 353 (250–340)
- Dorsal fin XIV, 17 (XIV, 17–18); anal fin III, 18 (II, 17–18); pectoral rays 18 (17–18)
- Longitudinal scale rows 47 (45–48); scales below lateral line 25 (26–27); gill rakers 12 (11–12)

**Morphology.** The body is oblong and compressed, with greatest depth 1.8 (1.7–1.9) in SL. The head profile is unevenly convex. The soft dorsal is usually rounded, and the anal is pointed to rounded. The caudal fin is convex in small individuals and becomes notably concave to slightly truncate in large specimens. The body and fins are covered with rough, ctenoid scales.

**Coloration.** In alcohol, the body is uniform dark brown, with one light oval spot below the seventh or eighth dorsal spine that may be indistinct or absent. Freshly preserved specimens show a purplish cast, a bright blue margin on the dorsal and anal fins, and brownish orange membranes between the anterior dorsal spines.

**Juvenile.** A 24.0 mm juvenile (UCLA 58-370) has seven narrow vertical crossbars on the head and body, a pattern similar to the barred phase of *H. passer* and *H. clarionensis*.

**Etymology.** The name honors Conrad Limbaugh, who collected the specimens and led the Clipperton Island shore parties. He is now deceased.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and abundance.** The species appears fairly common close to shore, in waters 20 to 30 feet deep and deeper. Expedition members observed it while diving just outside the reef "breaker line." Collection methods were varied: eight specimens (including the holotype) were taken with explosives in areas a short distance from shore, two were speared, and one juvenile was captured in a hand net while diving.

**Geographic setting.** Clipperton Island is a remote coral atoll about 600 miles west of Costa Rica. The paper describes only the island's inshore reef environment and does not report water chemistry or other environmental variables.

**Comparison with congeners.** Two earlier-described eastern Pacific members of the genus are relevant:
- *H. passer* has notably prolonged dorsal and anal fins, often reaching the caudal tip, with an evenly convex caudal fin. In life it is dark purple with a white bar below the spinous dorsal and yellow caudal, pectoral, and pelvic fins.
- *H. clarionensis* has a slightly truncate caudal fin and, in life, is bright orange with an olive brown posterior body half.

Counts and proportions of *H. limbaughi* are close to those of both species, so the author relies on fin prolongation, caudal fin shape, and coloration as diagnostic characters.

**Genus context.** The genus, as defined by [[Fraser-Brunner]] (1933), has large scales (fewer than 50 in the longitudinal series), an incomplete lateral line, and a large interopercle. Three other *Holacanthus* species had been described since that revision: *H. africanus*, *H. xanthotis*, and *H. armitagei*. None of them corresponds to *H. limbaughi*.

## Key Findings

- **A new species is described.** *Holacanthus limbaughi* is a new chaetodontid from Clipperton Island, eastern Pacific, based on one holotype and nine paratypes plus a juvenile.
- **Diagnostic features** are the concave caudal fin with an elongate upper lobe, uniform dark brown body, a single light oval spot below the spinous dorsal, and the absence of a black caudal margin.
- **Relationships:** It is closest in counts and proportions to *H. passer* and *H. clarionensis*, but differs in fin prolongation, caudal shape, and coloration.
- **Ecology:** The species is fairly common in shallow nearshore reef waters (20 to 30 feet and deeper) outside the breaker line.
- **Coloration is drab** compared with most chaetodontids, which is unusual for the family.
- **Collections:** Specimens are deposited in the USNM, UCLA, and LACM collections. The author states that the inshore fish fauna of Clipperton Island is reasonably well sampled, with a full faunal report to follow.
- **Publication administration:** The front matter also includes the journal's manuscript instructions, which cover format, illustration standards, proof procedures, and the editor's name (David K. Caldwell). These are administrative and carry no scientific data.
