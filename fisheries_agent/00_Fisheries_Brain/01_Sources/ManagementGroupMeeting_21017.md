# Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program: 1989 Management Group Documents

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This bundle of 1989 correspondence covers the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]]. It includes the draft summary of the September 13, 1989 [[Management Group]] meeting, the meeting agenda, the November 9, 1989 agenda, a memo on the [[15-Mile Reach]] gage, the adopted motion on [[Cross Mountain Ranch]] water rights, and a press clipping on flow protection for the [[Black Canyon of the Gunnison]]. The main threads are instream flow protection, water rights acquisition, [[Section 7 Consultation]] and [[Biological Opinion]] progress, and funding for monitoring and reservoir projects. No fish survey data, population counts or physiological measurements are reported. The documents are administrative and planning records.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program is coordinated by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service), with participation from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB), the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), Utah and Wyoming agencies, water developers, environmental groups and power interests. Its work is organized through a Management Group, a Technical Group and an [[Implementation Committee]]. Hamill (Service) was the program's recovery coordinator in 1989. The Management Group's 1989 agenda covered recovery element "road maps," annual project reports, information and education planning, and possible reorganization of the Technical and Management Groups.

The meeting summary lists about 18 attendees from federal agencies, state agencies, water developers, TNC, environmental groups and the Colorado River Water Conservation District (CRWCD). Among them were Hamill, Shields, Pitts, Saunders, Palmer, Harris, Wigington, Smith, Weaver, Jencsok, Zallen, Kuhn, Nelson, Williamson, Shanks, Uppendahl, Bovaird and Jencsok (the list repeats Jencsok).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The documents focus on flow regimes for endangered fishes in three river reaches:

- **[[Yampa River]], Colorado.** The Phase I report, "Habitat Use and Streamflow Needs of Rare and Endangered Fishes, Yampa River, Colorado," was circulated in September 1989. Phase II interim flow recommendations were due by October 1, 1989. The Service wanted to protect a relatively natural flow regime on the Yampa, and the Cross Mountain and Lily Park pumps were targeted for this.
- **[[15-Mile Reach]], Colorado River.** Water released from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] supplements flows here. The reach is a focus for [[PHABSIM]]-based habitat studies and for the gage installation.
- **[[Black Canyon of the Gunnison]], Gunnison River.** The National Park Service is seeking enough flow to protect waterfalls, silt-flushing floods and insect hatches in the canyon. The 12-mile canyon drops 95 feet per mile.

Non-native fish stocking was a discussion item. The agenda names [[Rainbow Smelt]] and the Dawson Draw proposal. The Service agreed to review stocking proposals from Utah and Wyoming.

## Key Findings

**1. Ruedi Reservoir releases.** Releases were being made to supplement flows in the 15-mile reach, and no adverse public comments had been received. The final technical issue concerned whether Green Mountain Reservoir could make substitute releases. Its decree does not permit releases for instream flow purposes. A public negotiating session was planned for about October 1989.

**2. Yampa River reports and flow studies.**
- The Phase I habitat and streamflow report was mailed in early September 1989.
- Phase II interim flow recommendations were due October 1, 1989.
- Shanks agreed to ensure coordination of FY 90 instream flow studies on the Yampa and the 15-mile reach, aiming for one basinwide methodology.
- A [[Yampa Basin Feasibility Study]] scope of work and contract were being negotiated.

**3. Biological Opinions.**
- The Muddy Creek opinion was on hold pending comments from the River District. A redraft was on the November agenda.
- The Sandstone opinion was tied to a meeting between Wyoming and the Service on October 2, 1989. The Service and Wyoming might approach the CWCB about instream protection for Sandstone releases into the Little Snake River.
- The draft Price-San Rafael opinion (due September 15, 1989) would credit accretions from the Uintah Basin Unit, if they can be legally protected instream.

**4. Cross Mountain Ranch water rights.** The [[Implementation Committee]] adopted a conditional motion supporting appraisal and acquisition of two decreed irrigation rights on the Yampa River:
- Lily Park Pumps: 36.67 cfs, 1886 appropriation date, upstream of the Little Snake confluence.
- Cross Mountain Pumps: 17.78 cfs, 1900 appropriation date, about 5 miles upstream of the head of Cross Mountain Canyon.

Conditions included:
1. Yampa instream flows remain a priority and the rights remain necessary.
2. The Service, CWCB and BLM (or TNC) agree on ownership, change to instream use under Colorado law, and enforcement.
3. No Recovery Program funds are paid until the Colorado water courts approve the change to instream purposes.
4. A Service officer approves TNC's fair-market appraisal during FY 1990.
5. The Management Group and Service approve any re-purchase.

BLM was preparing a letter of intent to buy the property from TNC.

**5. Other water rights.**
- [[Hinman Park Water Rights]]: Public Service Company had asked TNC to donate conditional rights on the Elk River (Yampa basin). Wigington was to evaluate the donation.
- Roberts Ditch (Maybell): The CWCB was evaluating senior Yampa rights near Maybell that were for sale.
- An ad hoc subcommittee (Pitts, chair) was formed to find ways to speed water rights acquisition and instream flow protection.

**6. Funding and monitoring.**
- The 1990 budget includes $20,000 from Reclamation for a [[15-Mile Reach Gage]].
- Colorado could not fund its $18,800 share of [[Kenney Reservoir]] in FY 90. Bennett was to report on the project, and the Group would consider using Section 7 funds.
- Utah was to pursue funding for its FY 90 work plan commitments.
- The CWCB could no longer rely on Reclamation to estimate water available for appropriation in the 15-mile reach, because of funding limits.

**7. Gage site selection.** Four candidate sites were studied on an August 29, 1989 field trip:
- Near the beginning of the reach (RM 185), below the power plant return. This is the best site for measuring program-acquired water, with good hydraulics, but it misses significant reach inflows and may be costly to install. It was the first choice in a telephone poll, and the Service concurred.
- Near the 32 Road Bridge (RM 177–78), two sites. These are near the PHABSIM study site and the best habitat, but they are poor for measuring acquired water.
- Above the Highway 50 Bridge. This captures all bypasses and gains but cannot separate sources and gives no Ruedi information.

The Management Group approved the upstream site immediately below the Orchard Mesa Power Plant. The Survey would provide installation and maintenance cost estimates.

**8. Black Canyon flow dispute (press clipping).** The National Park Service wants Gunnison flows to re-create natural conditions in the Black Canyon. The park's hydrologist, Owen Williams, leads a study, which federal hydrologists and lawyers say needs at least a year. Current informal "fish flows" are 300 cfs. Park officials say 1,000 cfs would fill the channel enough for waterfalls, 2,000 cfs would make conversation difficult, 5,000 cfs would produce a roar, and 20,000 cfs would approach a natural flood. The AB Lateral project would divert 544,000 acre-feet per year for Public Service Co. power generation, which is almost twice the annual use of Denver Water's 910,000 customers. The Colorado Supreme Court has ruled that the monument has a legal right to water for its founding purpose (1933). Aurora and Arapahoe County plans for transmountain diversions could also be affected.

**9. Procedural actions.**
- Bennett and Hamill will consult key researchers about the Recovery Program and the work plan process.
- The Service will develop "road maps" for each recovery element, identifying limiting factors and threats by species and reach.
- Hamill will issue FY 89 annual project report guidance.
- Shanks will ensure a review process for non-native stocking proposals.
- The next Management Group meeting was set for November 9, 1989, 9:00 am to 3:30 pm, at the Service's Regional Office.
