# Etheostoma Rubrum: A New Darter of the Subgenus Nothonotus from Bayou Pierre, Mississippi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

The paper describes [[Etheostoma Rubrum]], the Bayou Darter, a new percid species of the subgenus [[Nothonotus Subgenus]]. The description is based on 209 type specimens from seven series, all taken from [[Bayou Pierre]] or its tributary [[White Oak Creek]] in [[Copiah County]], Mississippi. The species is small, ranking as the smallest in its subgenus except for [[Etheostoma Tippecanoe]]. Its closest relative is [[Etheostoma Moorei]], and the two form a species group with an undescribed species from the Cumberland and Tennessee rivers. The species is known only from its type locality and nearby habitat, which the authors attribute to a restricted range tied to limited riffle habitat.

## Background & Description

The species was first noticed when a single specimen, collected by Ralph W. Yerger and students of Florida State University, was examined during a study of [[Etheostoma Camurum]] by Edward C. Raney and Timothy Zorach. The authors then requested collections at the type locality, which John S. Ramsey and Michael D. Dahlberg of Tulane University made in December 1963, obtaining 107 specimens. Five further series were collected later, bringing the total to 209 specimens. Measurements were made by Raney, and most meristic counts followed the methods of [[Hubbs and Lagler Methods]]. Support came from [[National Science Foundation]] grants and a [[National Institutes of Health]] grant.

**Diagnosis and morphology**
- Diminutive, compressed, moderately streamlined body with a nearly horizontal ventral contour.
- Snout moderately sharp; frenum broad; lower jaw included; caudal fin truncate.
- Cheek naked except for a patch of six to nine exposed scales behind the eye; opercle scaled; lateral line complete.
- Pronounced double basicaudal spot; the only complete vertical body bar encircles the caudal peduncle.
- Holotype: Cornell University no. 48232, adult male, 34.9 mm standard length, collected 7 December 1963 at the Highway 18 crossing, 8.6 mi SW of Utica.
- Largest specimen recorded: a 46 mm male.

**Sexual dimorphism**
- Males are larger than females. Males show few red spots on the body sides; females have many bright crimson dots (26 to 56) and spotted fins.
- Horizontal dark lines are poorly developed in males and absent in females.
- Unusually for darters, the bright red coloration is found in females.

**Meristic comparisons**
- Lateral line scales: *rubrum* usually 50 to 53; *moorei* 53 to 57.
- Vertebrae: *rubrum* 36 to 37; *moorei* 36.
- Total dorsal rays: *rubrum* 21 to 23; *moorei* 22 to 23.
- Differentiation from *moorei* in most meristic characters is low, but sharpest in lateral line scale number.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat**
- The type locality is a long riffle, 60 to 100 feet wide, with moderate flow. Riffle depth approached three feet and pool depth exceeded five feet. The water was slightly turbid and no vegetation was present.
- The bottom was bedrock partly covered by loose gravel, with occasional sand bars.
- Adults were most abundant near the heads of riffles in water under one foot deep, though some occurred in more turbulent areas.
- Specimens were usually taken over gravel near, but not in, the swiftest current.

**Distribution**
- The species is known only from the type locality in Bayou Pierre and its major tributary, White Oak Creek. Repeated attempts to find it elsewhere in the same and nearby stream systems failed.

**Associated fauna**
- About 30 other fish species were recorded from the same riffles or nearby pools. Examples include [[Zonale Darter]], [[Hildebrand Madtom]], [[Percina Uranidea]], [[Ictalurus Punctatus]], [[Lepomis Megalotis]], and several [[Notropis Shiners]].

**Collection method**
- Sampling used a 12 by 6 foot [[Nylon Seine]] with 1/4 inch mesh.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** [[Etheostoma Rubrum]] is described from 209 specimens taken in seven series from Bayou Pierre and White Oak Creek.
- **Size:** It is the smallest species of its subgenus except for Etheostoma tippecanoe.
- **Relationships:** It is closely related to [[Etheostoma Moorei]]. Together with an undescribed species from the Cumberland and Tennessee rivers, it forms a species group in [[Nothonotus Subgenus]]. The authors correct an earlier statement that it was not closely related to moorei.
- **Diagnostic traits:** Its clearest separation from moorei is in lateral line scale count. Color pattern is also a key species character, with a double basicaudal spot, scattered dark fin spots in females, and a suborbital bar that is complete but has a lighter section below the eye.
- **Comparison with camurum:** Unlike [[Etheostoma Camurum]], *rubrum* has the double basicaudal spot, a sharp oblique suborbital bar, and exposed cheek scales. Camurum is described as a complex of subspecies and species, which makes generalized comparisons difficult.
- **Range:** The species is restricted to the type locality and White Oak Creek. The authors link this to the limited availability of preferred riffle habitat.
- **Life history (inferred):** Yearlings reached 21 to 22 mm standard length. The species probably spawns at two years and may live about three years, based on length-frequency distributions.
- **Conservation implication:** Because of its restricted range and dependence on a narrow riffle habitat, the species is vulnerable to localized habitat change, though the paper does not assess conservation status directly.
