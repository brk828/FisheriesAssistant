# Allotoca maculata: A New Goodeid Fish from Western México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *[[Allotoca Maculata]]*, a new viviparous goodeid fish from an endorheic basin in west-central Mexico, the second known species of the genus *[[Allotoca]]*. The new species is distinguished from its only congener, *[[Allotoca Dugesi]]*, by the position of the dorsal fin and dorsal ray count, the structure of the embryonic trophotaeniae, diploid chromosome number, and coloration. The authors also synonymize *Allotoca vivipara* with *A. dugesi* and argue, from fish distributions, that the [[Laguna De Santa Magdalena]] basin was once hydrographically connected to the [[Río Lerma]] system.

## Background & Description

The Goodeidae comprises about 35 species of viviparous cyprinodontoid fishes, generally confined to the [[Mesa Central]] of Mexico and its periphery. Despite their small size, goodeids have diversified into nearly all permanent aquatic habitats accessible to fishes in the region. The Mesa Central has experienced intense volcanic and tectonic activity since the Miocene, and repeated disruption of hydrographic systems likely provided many opportunities for allopatric speciation, which may explain much of the endemism of the central Mexican fish fauna.

**Holotype and type series.** The holotype (UMMZ 200250) is a mature male, 27.2 mm SL, collected 22 February 1970 by R. R. Miller and J. M. Fitzsimons at Laguna de Santa Magdalena, Jalisco, about 82 km west of Guadalajara, at roughly 1210 m elevation. The paratype series includes 48 specimens (UMMZ 178322, 10–37 mm SL), 51 newborn to adult specimens (UMMZ 203851), 21 to 38 mm specimens (USNM 219779), and 15 specimens from Hacienda San Sebastián near Etzatlán (UMMZ 173553), 5 of which were cleared and stained. Most material was maintained or reared in Ann Arbor.

**Diagnosis of *A. maculata*.** A small species (maximum SL about 35 mm) with the dorsal and anal fins set far back on the body, less posteriorly than in *A. dugesi*. Key traits:
- Predorsal length 64–68% SL
- Dorsal rays usually 13 or 14; anal rays usually 11–13
- 28–32 lateral scales; mandibular pores absent
- Fins dimorphic in adults, longer in males
- Diploid chromosome number 48, with no metacentrics
- Females with three to six lateral blotches of iridescent blue; males with a lateral series of fine dark spots
- One or more irregular dark spots on the caudal peduncle

**Morphology and reproduction.** Meristic counts (Table 2) and morphometrics (Table 1) were taken from the holotype and paratypes. The ovary is a single median structure formed by fusion of paired organs, and in both species the embryonic trophotaeniae are aligned against the ovary wall. In *A. maculata* the trophotaeniae form a rosette of eight blunt processes, four well developed and four small, bud-like. Brood size ranged from 2 to 20 (mean 7.3, n = 23) in an aquarium stock. Males bear the modified anal fin used for intromission, typical of goodeids.

**Coloration.** Live adults are gray-green dorsally and silvery below. Females show 3–6 vertical bars and 4–7 blotches of iridescent blue; males show a lateral stripe that ends in a dense spot at the caudal base. A dark basicaudal spot is present in both sexes and persists after preservation. Preserved specimens lose the blue and gray-green colors, leaving a yellow-brown pattern.

**Karyotype.** *A. maculata* has 2N = 48 (44 telocentrics and 4 subtelocentrics). *A. dugesi* has 2N = 26, comprising 22 metacentrics, 2 large subtelocentrics, and 2 small subtelocentrics. Karyotypes were determined by [[Teruya Uyeno]] from gill epithelial cells.

**Comparison with *A. dugesi*.** Table 3 contrasts the two species. *A. dugesi* is larger (SL to about 63 mm), with more dorsal rays (14–17), a longer predorsal distance (69–73% SL), more lateral scales (30–35), and a complex trophotaeniae arrangement with two pairs of processes. *A. dugesi* also has a prominent continuous lateral stripe and yellow pigmentation in both sexes. A. vivipara (De Buen, 1940) was found to show no significant meristic or morphometric differences from *A. dugesi*, and the authors treat it as a synonym.

**Etymology.** *Maculata* comes from the Latin *macula* ("spot"), referring to the black basicaudal spot.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*A. maculata* inhabits shallow lake margins and adjacent marshes, usually near shore in water generally under 0.5 m deep. It has been taken over soft mud substrates in clear to muddy water, which can be easily roiled. During the long dry season it survives in the very shallow, well-vegetated water of shrinking lakes and can be collected by dipnet. It has also been taken in areas without vegetation. At the type locality, the lake margin was choked with *Eichhornia* in 1957 and 1970, with some *Hydrocotyle* present. Late-afternoon water temperatures were 15.5 °C (27 February 1957) and 16.2 °C (22 February 1970).

**Associated fishes.** At Laguna de Santa Magdalena the species co-occurred with *[[Algansea Tincella]]*, *[[Poeciliopsis Infans]]*, *[[Xenotoca Eiseni]]*, and *[[Xenotoca Melanosoma]]*. At Etzatlán, associates included *[[Moxostoma Mascotae]]* and *[[Goodea Atripinnis]]* (cf.). Commercial fishing for an atherinid (*[[Chirostoma Jordani]]*) was reported in 1900, and local people still knew "charal" and "pescado blanco" (Chirostoma spp.) in 1957.

**Known range.** *A. maculata* is known from only two localities within the isolated Magdalena basin of western Jalisco: Laguna de Santa Magdalena and a reservoir near Etzatlán. *A. dugesi* is known from many lakes and streams across the Mesa Central, including Michoacán, Guanajuato, Jalisco, and Aguascalientes.

**Basin history.** The Magdalena basin is one of a series of endorheic basins spanning central Mexico near 20°N, coinciding with the Neovolcanic Axis. Tamayo and West (1964) proposed that the isolation resulted from volcanic disruption of normal drainage, though increasing aridity and stream capture may also have contributed in the western basins. Laguna de Santa Magdalena was "several miles wide and about twenty miles long" in June 1892 (Goldman, 1951), but little surface water remains today.

## Key Findings

- **New species.** *A. maculata* is described as a second species of *Allotoca*, separated from *A. dugesi* by fin position, ray counts, trophotaeniae structure, karyotype (2N = 48 vs. 26), and coloration.
- **Karyotype evolution.** *A. maculata* retains the basic goodeid diploid number (48, no metacentrics) and is interpreted as the more primitive species. *A. dugesi* has a derived karyotype with 22 metacentrics. The genus shows extensive chromosomal change with relatively little morphological evolution, unlike *Xenotoca*, where morphologically distinct species show only small chromosomal differences.
- **Synonymy.** *Allotoca vivipara* is synonymized with *A. dugesi* based on the lack of significant meristic or morphometric differences.
- **Historical connection.** Fish distributions in the Magdalena basin, including *Allotoca*, suggest a former hydrographic connection with the Lerma system. Stream-deposited sediments at Jocotepec Quarry indicate a westward outlet of Lake Chapala, which may have drained the Lerma basin through the present Ameca system. The authors hypothesize that Laguna de Santa Magdalena was drained by the Río Ameca when the latter served as the Lerma outlet. Table 4 shows the Lerma shares more fish taxa with the Ameca than with the Río Grande de Santiago.
- **Distribution of *A. dugesi*.** *A. dugesi* occurs across lakes and streams of the Mesa Central that are now or formerly connected to the Río Lerma system.
- **Conservation note.** The Laguna de Santa Magdalena, the type locality, held little surface water by the late 20th century, which bears on the restricted range of *A. maculata*.
