# Escape of Hybrid Grass x Bighead Carp into Central Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This comment updates an earlier report on the experimental introduction of triploid [[Hybrid Grass Carp X Bighead Carp]] into the Phoenix Metropolitan Area of central Arizona. The fish, stocked in August 1982 to test [[Aquatic Vegetation Control]] in [[Salt River Project]] canals, were largely unaccounted for when recovery was attempted in January 1983. Only 13 of 1,100 stocked fish were recovered. Inspection revealed that the fish had most likely escaped through a gap in a pond outflow grate and through enlarged grate openings in the canal. The authors conclude that the consequences are unknown but that the incident sets a dangerous precedent. The comment also corrects earlier literature errors about the occurrence of [[Grass Carp]] and [[Silver Carp]] in Arizona.

## Background & Description

The fish were produced by spawning in 1981 at Malone's Chinese Fish Farms in Lonoke, Arkansas. The cooperating agencies were [[Salt River Project]] (SRP), [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AZGFD), and [[University Of Arizona]] (UA). The project aimed to determine the efficacy of the hybrid for aquatic vegetation control in SRP-operated canals.

At stocking, the fish ranged from 193 to 299 mm total length, had head widths of 25.4 to 38.2 mm, and weighed 67 to 206 g (N = 36 measured). All were reported as [[Triploidy|Triploid]] and therefore presumed sterile.

Stocking sites were:
- A 1.2-km reach of Lateral 9.5 of the Consolidated Canal in Mesa, Arizona (N = 500).
- A 0.01-hectare pond on SRP property in Phoenix, adjacent to the Grand Canal (N = 600).

Both sites were closed with vertical bar grates spaced 25.4 mm apart.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recovery attempt.** In January 1983 the canal was drained to depths of a few centimeters to about 50 cm, and the pond was pumped down. Both were seined thoroughly. Twelve fish were recovered from the canal and one from the pond. An SRP spokesman proposed theft, poisoning, or escape as explanations, with theft and poisoning considered most likely at first. However, a gap at the base of the pond outflow was found. [[Centrarchid Fishes]] were observed escaping through this hole during the recovery attempt.

**Physical failure of containment.** The downstream canal grate showed erosion around its concrete support apron and bent bars. These had enlarged the original 25.4-mm openings to about 32 mm, which would allow all but the largest fish to pass. High runoff had also raised canal water levels to near or above the grate tops.

**Pathways to open water.** The canal system is interconnected. During high water, storm drains link it to the [[Salt River]], which flows to the [[Gila River]] and the lower [[Colorado River]]. Although normally dry, the Salt and Gila channels were inundated by runoff events in August and October 1982 and from January through March 1983. This flooding reached [[Painted Rock Reservoir]], about 150 km downstream. The Gila River below the reservoir was largely dry until February, when reservoir releases flowed to the Colorado River at Yuma, Arizona. Upstream access to the Salt and Verde rivers was likely blocked by [[Granite Reef Diversion]] and other dams. The consequences of the release remain unknown.

**Ploidy of the source brood.** Subsamples of the 1981 brood included both triploid and diploid fish. Up to 400 fish per sample were examined independently by R. Wattendorf (Florida Game and Fish Department), A. Mitchell (USFWS, Stuttgart Laboratory, Arkansas), and D. Phillip (Illinois Natural History Survey) using cell-diameter or electrophoretic methods. Results indicated 92 to 98% triploid hybrids. In at least one sample, the diploid fish (about 2%) were gynogenetic grass carp and androgenetic bighead carp. Fish stocked in 1982 were considered 100% triploid, because they had been separated from diploids by size owing to differential growth rates (J. Malone, personal communication).

## Key Findings

- **Stocking and loss:** 1,100 triploid hybrid carp were stocked in August 1982. Only 13 were recovered in January 1983 (12 from the canal, 1 from the pond).
- **Containment failure:** Escape most likely occurred through a gap at the base of the pond outflow grate and through bent grate bars that enlarged the 25.4-mm openings to about 32 mm. The authors judged theft and poisoning less likely.
- **Dispersal risk:** Canal water could reach the Salt, Gila, and lower Colorado rivers through storm drains during high water. Floods between August 1982 and March 1983 inundated these channels as far as Painted Rock Reservoir, about 150 km downstream.
- **Ploidy:** Brood sampling showed 92 to 98% triploids, with about 2% diploids (gynogenetic grass carp and androgenetic bighead carp in at least one sample). The 1982 stock was considered 100% triploid.
- **Policy implication:** The authors stress that the mishap sets a dangerous precedent. Future releases require greater care, since a similar escape of a fish capable of serious environmental disturbance could be far more damaging.
- **Corrections to the record:** Earlier reports of [[Silver Carp]] (*Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*) in Arizona were likely contamination of stocks of [[Grass Carp]] imported from Hong Kong in 1965 through Page Springs Hatchery. Unauthorized stocking of grass carp before 1976 may still persist in some Arizona waters. Two localities mapped by Guillory and Gasaway (1978) and Guillory (1980) were judged invalid. These included a site on the north side of the Gila River near the San Pedro River inflow and a site on the Salt River arm of Roosevelt Lake, which was an aquarium record. Valid Arizona sites were listed as Page Springs Hatchery stock (NAU), Sun Lakes, and the experimental UA stock.
