# The Fishes of Lake Valencia, Caracas, and the Rio Tuy at El Concejo, Venezuela

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This Indiana University Studies publication (Study No. 44, Contribution No. 170 from the Zoological Laboratory of Indiana University) by [[Carl H. Eigenmann]] reports a freshwater fish collection from [[Lake Valencia]], the [[Maracay]] area, [[El Concejo]] on the [[Rio Tuy]], and near [[Caracas]], Venezuela. The material was collected by Dr. A. S. Pearse of the University of Wisconsin in July and August 1918. The paper lists 31 species, describes several new species, and discusses the faunal affinities of the region. It also reviews the historical drainage changes of Lake Valencia, which once flowed into the [[Orinoco Basin]].

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Little was known about the fishes around Caracas, less about Lake Valencia (1,421 feet elevation), and least about the Tuy, which flows eastward into an indentation of the Caribbean.
- **Collector:** Dr. A. S. Pearse, University of Wisconsin, July and August 1918.
- **Collecting stations and elevations:**
  - Isla del Buro, Lake Valencia (July 9–12)
  - Maracay, 1,530 feet (lake collection July 25; Rio Tapa Tapa July 15; Rio Castaño July 16, 27; Rio Bue July 19, 20, 29, 30)
  - El Concejo, 2,040 feet (Rio Tiquirito, a Tuy tributary, August 1; Rio Tuy August 1)
  - Guaire basin near Caracas (August 4)
- **Taxonomy:** The paper describes new species, including *Pimelodella tapatapae*, *Rhamdia guairensis*, *Ancistrus brevifilis*, *Chaetostomus pearsei*, [[Moenkhausia Pittieri]], *Gephyrocharax valencia*, *Menidia venezuelae*, and *Bryconamericus beta* (listed as an existing name in the enumeration). Detailed morphometric and meristic descriptions are given for each species.
- **Specimen numbering:** Collections are catalogued under Indiana University numbers (for example, 15077 to 15149).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake Valencia history:** The lake was formerly considerably larger and drained regularly through the Rio Paito and Rio Pao into the [[Orinoco Basin]]. It later became landlocked, with occasional overflows. Together with its tributaries, it formed the northernmost sources of the Orinoco basin.
- **Historical drainage changes:** Accounts quoted from Sievers (*Cordillere von Merida*, p. 119) and other sources describe the lake outflow through the Cano Camburi. Around 1873, the hacendado Amarado Muñoz diverted the Rio Paito to prevent annual flooding of his fields. The Rio Paito now rises at the Loma de la Sabana de San Pablo and reaches the lake via the Cano Camburi. Artificial interventions had restored an endorheic (closed) basin about 15 years before the account, although a connection with the Rio Pao was said to persist in the rainy season.
- **Faunal character:** The fauna is described as poor. Lowland fauna is represented only by widely distributed species plus *Roeboides*, *Characidium*, and *Moenkhausia*. The highland fauna (*Pygidium* and *Astroblepus*) is not represented in the collection.
- **Biogeographic groups:** Species were grouped by distribution:
  - **A (6 species):** universally distributed
  - **B (6 species):** also found on the Island of Trinidad
  - **C (16 species):** peculiar to Venezuela around Valencia and the upper Rio Meta, belonging to widely distributed genera
  - **D (1 species):** also found in the Rio Magdalena
  - **E (1 species):** found in the lowlands of British Guiana (the document does not name this species)
  - **Unassigned:** 2 species
  - **V:** only 10 species were taken in Lake Valencia itself

## Key Findings

- **Species total:** 31 species were collected. The document gives family counts as Siluridae 4, Loricariidae 5, Callichthyidae 1, Characidae 14, Gymnotidae 1, Atherinidae 1, Poeciliidae 1, Symbranchidae 1, and Cichlidae 2, which sum to 30. The enumerated species list also includes *Hoplias malabaricus* (Erythrinidae), which accounts for the 31st species. The enumerated list is not fully consistent with the family tallies (for example, the list includes eight loricariids), so the counts should be checked against the original.
- **New species described (as named in the text):**
  - *Pimelodella tapatapae* sp. nov. (Valencia basin; type 156 mm, mouth of Rio Tapa Tapa, July 15, 1919)
  - *Rhamdia guairensis* sp. nov. (near Caracas; type 132 mm, Rio Guaire, August 4, with 5 paratypes; the author notes it resembles *Pimelodus humilis* Günther except for pectoral spine structure)
  - *Ancistrus brevifilis* sp. nov. (Tuy basin; type 150 mm, Rio Tiquirito, August 2; paratypes 136 mm male and 100 mm female; 23+1 plates)
  - *Chaetostomus pearsei* sp. nov. (Valencia and Tuy basins; type 146 mm, Rio Castaño at Maracay, July 16; 24+1 plates)
  - *Moenkhausia pittieri* sp. nov. (Valencia and Tuy basins; type 58 mm, Rio Tiquirito, August 1; D. 11; A. 26 to 29; scales 7-35-6; 5+8 gill rakers)
  - *Gephyrocharax valencia* sp. nov. (Valencia basin; reaches about 45 mm; D. 9; A. 30 to 32; scales 5 to 6.5-40 to 42-5)
  - *Menidia venezuelae* sp. nov. (Valencia basin; largest 61 mm, Rio Tapa Tapa, July 15, 1918)
- **Lake Valencia fauna:** Only 10 species were taken in Lake Valencia itself, and the document notes a comparatively poor fish fauna.
- **Trinidad connection:** Six species shared with Trinidad indicate that the Trinidad fauna is linked to the Valencia and Rio Meta faunas.
- **Diet notes:** *Moenkhausia pittieri* had an alimentary canal containing insect fragments.
- **Hydrographic conclusion:** Lake Valencia and its tributaries formerly belonged to the Orinoco drainage, and artificial diversions created the endorheic basin observed in the collection period.
