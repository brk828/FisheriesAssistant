# Canyon Lake - LaBarge Cove Fish Habitat Project

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This implementation report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), Region 6, documents the Canyon Lake - LaBarge Cove Fish Habitat Project (CLLCFHP), carried out from November 2 to November 22, 1988. The project placed three types of artificial underwater cover in LaBarge Cove of [[Canyon Lake]], a reservoir with very little submerged vegetation. The structures were [[Crappie Condos]], [[Fish N' Trees]] (arranged as "Fish Forests"), and [[Catfish Houses]]. The work was a joint effort among AGFD, the [[US Forest Service]] (USFS), the nonprofit [[Anglers United]], volunteers, and private businesses. The report covers design, placement, participation, costs, public outreach, and a monitoring plan for 1989. Some figures differ between sources; these are noted below.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** Canyon Lake lies about 42 miles east of Phoenix in Maricopa County. It was formed in 1925 by Mormon Flat Dam on the [[Salt River]]. Water levels average 1665 feet above mean sea level, and the lake covers an average of 850 surface acres. LaBarge Cove (also called Boulder Creek Recreation Area) is in the southeast part of the lake, directly south of the Canyon Lake Resort.

**Problem.** Previous surveys found that Canyon Lake was nearly devoid of submerged vegetation in its littoral zone. The report identifies escape cover as a major limiting factor for [[Largemouth Bass]] and forage fish, especially [[Threadfin Shad]]. An earlier trial in 1985 stocked 2,000 Berkley "FISH HAB" modules in Tortilla and LaBarge Coves. That trial covered less than 4 acres in total, but 1986 gillnetting showed largemouth bass regularly oriented to the covered areas.

**Objectives.** The project aimed to improve the angling experience and increase resting, nesting, and escape cover for sport fish. It was an extension of the USFS Boulder Creek Recreation Area and Fish Pier Project.

**Structure designs.**
- **Crappie Condos** (designed by AGFD Region 6 and USFS staff): plastic snow fencing about 5 ft tall, two 16" x 16" cinder blocks (42 lbs each), and an opaque or gray plastic cap, tied with plastic tie-raps. They were meant to shelter young sportfish and threadfin shad and to provide surfaces for periphyton and zooplankton production.
- **Fish N' Trees** (commercial product designed by Dr. Loren Hill): palm-like buoyant leaves on eight-sided hollow stems, filled with foam for vertical buoyancy. The leaves rotate so lures and hooked fish can pass without snagging. Each section has twelve perpendicular leaves on a three-foot stem.
- **Catfish Houses**: six 3-ft PVC pipes bound in a pyramid, with one end of each pipe capped with 4 inches of cement to create one-way entry. They were designed as nesting and shelter cover for [[Channel Catfish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The habitat was matched to target species and water-level fluctuation. Crappie Condo complexes were set at 13 to 20 ft, within the usable light and temperature zones for the target species, and allowed for annual lake fluctuation. Fish Forests were set at 12 to 29 ft, and the tops of the artificial trees were planned to stay at least 3 ft below the surface at all times. Catfish Houses were placed directly on the bottom at 10 to 15 ft.

Target species were:
- Bluegill and green sunfish, plus young sportfish and threadfin shad (Crappie Condos)
- Largemouth bass, bluegill, and crappie (Fish N' Trees)
- Channel catfish (Catfish Houses)

Canyon Lake has relatively few crappie. The report expects the condos to attract bass and bluegill for the protection they offer.

Water clarity limited observations. Divers reported visibility of only one to six feet. Pre-placement gillnetting in LaBarge Cove (set November 1) caught walleye, rainbow trout, and channel catfish, which indicates the cove was already a productive fishing site. The site is also about 150 yards from where a 15-lb largemouth bass was illegally killed with dynamite a few years earlier.

## Key Findings

**Placement and quantities**
- Crappie Condos: each unit was built from snow fencing, two cinder blocks, and a cap. Twenty-one units were joined in a 3 x 7 "Complex" with polypropylene rope so the complex sank as one unit. The report states that 27 Complexes made up of 580 individual Condos were installed in a seven-day period. One Complex covered about 4,000 cubic feet (113 m3). The unit count does not match the Complex count (27 x 21 = 567), so the figures are reported as given.
- Fish N' Trees: 70 "Forests" were placed over three days. They were built in four types by number of sections per tree: #4 (1 section, 12 ft deep), #3 (2 sections, 19 ft), #2 (5 sections, 23 ft), and #1 (7–8 sections, 29 ft). Type #1 Forests (seven units) were scheduled for spring 1989, alongside the fishing pier. Each Forest base carried about 80 lbs of rebar in a 5 x 10 ft frame of 2-inch black PVC. A separate account in the 1989 article says 68 Forest frames holding 475 individual "trees" were provided.
- Catfish Houses: 19 units were assembled and placed in one day.

**Placement quality.** About 75% of the structures were placed within casting distance of shore or the fishing pier. Divers checked placement one week after installation. Of the structures they found (nine in total), all were in good position and in use by small fish.

**Coverage.** The article in *The Arizona Republic* (11-25-88) said about 40% of the cove was now covered with habitat. The *Arizona Wildlife Views* article (January 1989) said about 70% of the new habitat was within casting range of shore, and that about 75% of LaBarge Cove shoreline is accessible on foot.

**Participation and labor.** The USFS Mesa Ranger District reported 65 people working 183 mandays:
- 60% (110 mandays) by volunteers
- 27% (50 mandays) by AGFD personnel
- 13% (23 mandays) by USFS personnel

The report's activity section lists 45 AGFD mandays and over 30 hours of boat operation. AGFD staff drove 3,159 miles. Anglers United volunteers included members, Boy Scouts, and retirees from the Apache Junction area. The project used a 24-ft pontoon boat donated by Anglers United, and Plastic Research and Development Corp. (PRADCO) supplied materials and input.

**Costs and value**
- AGFD estimated cost: $7,034
- Labor contributions: $9,500
- Anglers United paid for materials, with about $68,000 of total project cost
- Total project value, including tools, labor, supplies, materials, and PRADCO input: $82,000

**Public outreach.** Channel 3 (Phoenix ABC) and Channel 12 (Phoenix NBC) covered the project. Anglers United included it in its Summer 1988 *FISHLINE* publication. Statewide press releases and AGFD articles also covered it. A training video (28 min, VHS) was produced by AGFD Region 6.

**Recommendations (manpower perspective)**
1. *Team responsibility:* USFS and AGFD split habitat placement coordination, with one designated foreman per team. This kept logistics consolidated and limited equipment and time-frame problems common in multi-agency and volunteer projects.
2. *Volunteer management:* Key individuals recruited volunteers for each weekend. Volunteers signed up for specific work crews, and one person coordinated transport, food, and camping for each weekend.

**Monitoring plan (1989–1990)**
- *Fish utilization:* Surveys in spring/summer 1989 using electrofishing, gillnetting, seining, sonar, SCUBA, and hook and line. The Procedures section specifies May 1989.
- *Angler use:* Random [[Creel Survey]] four times monthly (two weekdays and two weekend days), plus questionnaires, to measure angling pressure, catch rates, and angler acceptance of the structures.
- *Water quality:* Quarterly oxygen/temperature profiles and SCUBA inspections.
- *Controls:* Survey control areas each year to compare covered and uncovered sites.
- *Reporting:* An initial evaluation report due January 1990, with the option to continue monitoring.

**Future work**
- A 100-ft fishing dock for handicapped anglers, to be built among the habitat, after the lake stabilizes (late 1991 or early 1992 per the article).
- A similar, roughly ten-times-larger habitat project at [[Saguaro Lake]] in 1989, when the lake is drawn down 45 ft for Stewart Mountain Dam repairs. Planned elements include real hardwood trees, tire reefs, and rye grass seeding to recycle lake-bed nutrients.
