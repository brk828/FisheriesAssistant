## Executive Summary
This short faunistic note reports the first documented occurrence of the planorbid snail **[[Gyraulus arizonensis]]** in Texas, far outside its previously known range centered on the San Pedro River in Arizona. The specimens were recovered from drift debris in a brackish-influenced habitat locally called the "mare trap" within the **[[Welder Wildlife Refuge]]**, located in the coastal plains near Sinton, San Patricio County, Texas. The finding suggests that *G. arizonensis* is more widely distributed than previously believed. The collection also yielded several other notable mollusk species, contributing to a broader survey of the refuge's malacofauna.

## Background & Description
- **Diagnostic sculpture:** Shells display conspicuous **spiral striae**, a distinguishing surface feature used to identify the species.
- **Shell size range:** 1.1 mm to 3.2 mm in diameter across the sample of 91 specimens.
- **Whorl count:** Ranges from 2 to 3-2/3 whorls; the initial specimen found had approximately 2¼ turns.
- **Comparative identification:** Texas specimens were morphologically indistinguishable from topotypic material of *G. arizonensis* collected from the San Pedro River, Arizona, supporting conspecific identification rather than description of a new taxon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Found in drift debris within a coastal plain wetland subject to **periodic increased salinity**, indicating tolerance for brackish or fluctuating-salinity conditions atypical for many freshwater planorbids.
- **Associated vegetation:** The "mare trap" locality supports brackish-water plant species such as **Spartina**, situating the snail within an estuarine-influenced freshwater/brackish ecotone.
- **Biogeographic context:** The refuge sits at the transition between the **Texan** and **Tamaulipan biotic provinces**, with rainfall only marginally above physiological requirements — a marginal, ecologically transitional environment.
- **Proximity to marine influence:** The site lies roughly five miles from **Copano Bay** (Gulf of Mexico), and the refuge's northern border is formed by the Arkansas River, situating the locality at a freshwater-estuarine interface.
- **Co-occurring molluscan fauna:** The same drift-debris sample contained abundant populations of other gastropods and a bivalve, indicating a diverse molluscan assemblage in this brackish-freshwater habitat:
  - ~1000 *Drepanotrema cultratum labrosum*
  - ~500 *Planorbina (Obstructio) obstructa*
  - ~500 *Planorbina (Tropicorbis) orbiculus*
  - 1 *Pyrgophorus spinosus*
  - 1 immature *Cincinnatia peracuta*
  - 1 *Texadina sphinctostoma*
  - 9 *Mytilopsis leucophaeta* (a brackish-water bivalve, "false mussel")

## Key Findings
- **Range extension:** This represents a substantial **geographic range extension** for [[Gyraulus arizonensis]], previously known only from Arizona (San Pedro River drainage), now documented in coastal Texas — implying a much wider distribution than originally supposed.
- **Sample size:** A total of **91 specimens** of *G. arizonensis* were recovered upon further searching, following the discovery of a single immature individual in initial drift debris sorting.
- **Voucher deposition:** 12 specimens were deposited in the **U.S. National Museum** (catalog #622469); remaining specimens were retained by the author for further study.
- **Broader survey implications:** These records are intended for incorporation into a larger, comprehensive report on the **Mollusca of the Welder Wildlife Refuge**, indicating this note is a preliminary contribution to a more extensive faunal survey.
- **Taxonomic method:** Identification relied on direct morphological comparison with topotypic material rather than molecular or novel diagnostic techniques, reflecting standard mid-20th-century malacological practice.
- **Ecological tolerance implication:** The presence of *G. arizonensis* and associated taxa in a habitat with periodic salinity increases suggests underappreciated euryhaline tolerance in freshwater planorbid snails.
