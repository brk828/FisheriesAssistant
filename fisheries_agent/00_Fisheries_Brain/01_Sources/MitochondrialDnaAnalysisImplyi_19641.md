# Three Nature Letters on Lion Coalitions, Red Wolf Hybridization and Retinogeniculate Segregation (1991)

#mammalogy #marine-biology #felidae #canidae #mustelidae

*Note: this compilation contains no aquatic or marine material. The three letters are unrelated terrestrial studies, and the marine-biology tag is included only because the template requires it.*

## Executive Summary

This document compiles three letters published in *Nature* in June 1991 (vol. 351, 13 June; vol. 354, 13 June). It contains no fish, aquatic, or marine data. The three studies are:

1. **Male cooperation in lions.** Analysis of [[Kinship]] and paternity in [[African Lion]] coalitions in the Serengeti. Large coalitions are maintained by related males, because reproduction is highly skewed among coalition members.
2. **Hybridization in the red wolf.** Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis of [[Red Wolf]] samples, showing that the red wolf's genotypes are coyote-like or [[Grey Wolf]]-like, consistent with extensive [[Hybridization]] with [[Coyote]] and grey wolves.
3. **NMDA receptors and visual wiring in ferrets.** Pharmacological blockade of [[NMDA Receptor]]s in the [[Lateral Geniculate Nucleus]] during development prevents retinal axons from segregating into "On" and "Off" sublaminae.

Each study is summarized below. The lion and red wolf letters address conservation and population questions. The ferret letter is a developmental neuroscience study.

## Background & Description

**Lion coalitions (Serengeti).** Male [[African Lion]]s form coalitions of varying size. Within a coalition, only one or a few males may breed, while others act as non-breeding helpers that increase coalition size and so the reproductive success of companions. The authors argue that such helpers can be favoured by selection only if their presence increases the reproductive success of close relatives. The study used the [[DNA Band Sharing]] technique to measure relatedness among companions, neighbours and mating partners in the [[Serengeti National Park]] population. The authors caution that band-sharing values differ between the Serengeti and [[Ngorongoro Crater]], so they should be calibrated against an independent measure of kinship.

**Red wolf taxonomy.** The [[Red Wolf]] (*Canis rufus*) was previously endemic to the southeastern United States. Its numbers declined sharply after 1900 because of habitat destruction, predator control and hybridization with coyotes. By 1970 wild red wolves survived only in extreme southeastern Texas and southwestern Louisiana. The species was listed as endangered in 1967, and a captive breeding programme began in 1974 under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Earlier identification relied on protein electrophoresis and morphometrics. The authors instead used [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA), which evolves faster in mammals than nuclear genes and therefore discriminates closely related taxa more effectively.

**Ferret visual system.** In the ferret, retinal ganglion cell axons from each eye segregate early into eye-specific laminae of the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN). The dorsal laminae then divide into inner (On-centre) and outer (Off-centre) sublaminae. The study asks whether the activity of postsynaptic LGN cells shapes this afferent patterning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lion social system.** The letter describes lion coalitions in terms of mating competition. A male attempts to monopolize a female during her 2–4-day oestrus. Breeding within each pride is typically synchronous, so several females may be in oestrus at once. Larger coalitions do not preferentially occupy larger prides, so the number of males in a large coalition more often exceeds the number of oestrous females. Mating success is more uneven in coalitions whose partners differ more in age or vigour.

**Red wolf range and hybrid zones.** The red wolf's historical range was centred on the southeastern United States. A grey wolf–coyote hybrid zone already existed in the Great Lakes region and in Minnesota and Canada. The authors note that hybrid zone width can reach 50 times the average dispersal distance, and that grey wolves and coyotes can move more than 100 km in a single generation. Gene flow from neighbouring grey wolf populations is considered likely to have obliterated the distinctive red wolf morphology.

**Developmental neural activity.** The study places the ferret experiments in the context of activity-dependent wiring. NMDA receptor antagonists are known to reduce retinogeniculate transmission and to lower LGN cell activity. The authors suggest that these antagonists disrupt a mechanism by which LGN neurons detect correlated afferent and target activity.

## Key Findings

### Lion coalitions

- **Paternity is concentrated.** Mixed paternity occurred in only one of 24 litters containing two or more cubs. Consorting males therefore usually father an entire litter.
- **Reproductive skew increases with coalition size.** Within-coalition variance in the number of litters fathered per male increased significantly with coalition size (P = 0.0084, n = 44 litters).
- **Kinship maintains larger coalitions.** Previous models assumed each male fathered an equal share of offspring, so cooperation did not require kinship. The authors argue that this holds only for small coalitions. In larger coalitions, where reproduction is strongly skewed, kinship is essential to maintain cooperation.
- **Trio partners are closer in age.** The age difference between unrelated partners is significantly smaller in trios (0.75 years) than in pairs (2.5 years, P < 0.05). Males forming unrelated trios appear more sensitive to potential disparities in mating success.
- **Non-breeders are often related.** Among trios, a relatively low proportion of males lack any kinship with companions. In one trio with a non-relative, the non-breeder was closely related to one of the breeders.
- **Methodological caveat.** Band-sharing relatedness differed between the Serengeti and Ngorongoro Crater populations, so band-sharing estimates should be calibrated against an independent kinship measure.

### Red wolf hybridization

- **Captive population.** The four matrilines of the captive red wolf population displayed a single mtDNA genotype. This genotype grouped phylogenetically within a coyote-only clade and was indistinguishable from coyote genotype 32 found in two recent Louisiana coyotes.
- **Wild canids sampled 1974–76 (n = 77).** Morphological classification gave 58% coyotes, 31% red wolf/coyote hybrids and 11% red wolves. mtDNA genotyping (one to four restriction enzymes) gave 84% coyote-type (65 individuals), 7% northern grey wolf-type (5) and 9% Mexican grey wolf-type (7).
- **Captive-type genotype is common in the wild.** Of 30 canids typed with HincII, 23 (77%) carried the restriction-fragment pattern diagnostic of captive red wolves.
- **Morphology and genetics disagree.** Of 12 canids with a grey wolf genotype, one was classed as a red wolf, four as coyotes, six as hybrids and one as unknown. The authors infer that hybridization between coyotes and grey wolves occurred in the source population before 1974.
- **Historical pelts (1905–1930).** Six museum pelts from five southeastern states yielded 398 bp of cytochrome b sequence. Grey wolves and coyotes differed by 21–28 nucleotides (5.3–7.0%). Within each species no more than seven substitutions separated genotypes. All six historical red wolf sequences matched either grey wolf or coyote sequences.
- **Phylogenetic result.** Two monophyletic clades were supported in all 1,000 bootstrap replicates: one with grey wolves and three red wolf genotypes, the other with coyotes and two red wolf genotypes.
- **Interpretation.** The authors consider two scenarios. The red wolf was either a distinct species with unique mtDNA lineages that were missed or lost, or its phenotype arose entirely from coyote–grey wolf hybridization. Either way, extensive hybridization occurred among red wolves, coyotes and grey wolves.
- **Conservation conclusion.** The authors argue that the red wolf's current hybrid zone may be the largest yet described for a terrestrial vertebrate relative to its past range. They recommend continued protection, since the red wolf was a top predator and an integral part of the former ecosystem. They also note that US Fish and Wildlife Service policy does not protect hybrids. Protection should continue unless loss of the hybrid form is judged reversible.

### Retinogeniculate segregation in ferrets

- **Sample.** The study used 32 ferret kits.
- **Normal development.** At birth (embryonic day 41), axons from the two eyes overlap extensively in the LGN. Eye-specific laminae are essentially complete by two weeks (n = 2). Sublaminae are clearly identifiable by three weeks (n = 3).
- **D-APV blocks sublamination.** Blocking NMDA receptors with D-APV between two and three weeks of age prevents formation of On and Off sublaminae. The effect is dose-dependent: 0.8 mM blocks it (n = 3), while 0.08 mM does not (n = 2). The inactive isomer L-APV at 0.8 mM has no effect (n = 2).
- **MK-801 blocks sublamination.** The NMDA antagonist [[MK-801]] blocks sublamina formation at a reported concentration of 4.75 mM (n = 2) but not at 1.2 mM (n = 2). Saline vehicle has no effect.
- **Single-axon arbors (Table 1).**

- **Interpretation.** Arbors in D-APV-treated animals are not restricted appropriately, or are restricted in size but misplaced within the eye-specific laminae. The authors conclude that postsynaptic activity can significantly influence the patterning of presynaptic afferents during development.
