# 1987 Fish Rearing Program at Niland Native Fish Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1986 interoffice memorandum from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]], Region 5 Fisheries Management Supervisor Keith R. Anderson, sets out the operating plan for the [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]] facility during 1987. The plan covers clearing and dewatering the ponds, growing [[Bonytail Chub]] fry, and rearing [[Razorback Sucker]] from fry to fingerling size. All production fish are to be planted in approved [[Colorado River Basin]] waters. The memo assigns program responsibility to Jim St. Amant and Mike Giusti.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal management memorandum, dated December 30, 1986, following a December 10, 1986 coordination meeting.
- **From:** Keith R. Anderson, Fisheries Management Supervisor, Region 5.
- **To:** Jim St. Amant, Mike Giusti, Glenn Black, and Wayne Parker (with a cc to Northley, RM).
- **Purpose:** Summarize the [[Fish Rearing Program]] plan for the facility for the 1987 season.
- **Species referenced:** [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]] (abbreviated RBS in the memo), and [[Grass Carp]], which must be removed from the facility.

The memo is an operational plan, not a research report. It contains no field data, sample sizes, or survival results.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not describe the natural habitat, water chemistry, or ecological condition of the ponds or the receiving waters. Its environmental content is limited to:

- **Facility use:** The ponds are managed as a hatchery system. All ponds are dewatered and prepared in February and March.
- **Release destination:** Fish are to be planted in "approved Colorado River basin waters." The memo does not name specific release sites.
- **Stocking densities:** Razorback sucker are stocked at 25,000 fry per pond in early April, then at 10,000 fingerlings per acre in early June for summer growout.
- **Pond infrastructure:** Ponds 7 and 8 are described as equipped with collection kettles.

## Key Findings

The memo lays out a six-step schedule:

1. **Fish removal (by February 1, 1987):** All fish must be removed from the facility. Randy Stocker is the liaison for scheduling grass carp removal.
2. **Pond preparation (February–March):** All ponds are dewatered and prepared for use.
3. **Bonytail chub growout:** One pond is set aside for experimental bonytail growout. Fry are stocked about mid-May and inventoried periodically to estimate survival and growth rates. Fish are removed in the fall for planting in approved Colorado River basin waters.
4. **Razorback sucker production:**
   - Fry are stocked in early April into Ponds 7 and 8 at 25,000 fry per pond.
   - By early June, at about 2 inches, the fish are inventoried and spread to all available ponds at 10,000 fingerlings per acre.
   - The production goal is a minimum size of "I inches" in the scanned text, likely 4 inches, by fall. Fish are then planted in approved Colorado River basin waters. Verify this figure against the original.
5. **Optional two-month razorback growout (April–May):** Additional razorback fry may be reared in available ponds to about 2-inch fingerlings for early June planting in the Colorado River. Ponds must be cleared by early June for production fish. This segment runs only if adequate temporary help is available.
6. **Final removal (by December 15, 1987):** All fish are removed from the facility. The ponds are then dewatered, maintained, and prepared for the 1988 season.

**Responsibilities:** Jim St. Amant and Mike Giusti are responsible for implementing and operating the program until further notice.
