## Executive Summary

This document is a 1997 news release from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the Colorado Water Conservation Board (CWCB) announcing a landmark legal and hydrological milestone: the first-ever water rights decrees issued specifically to protect **instream flows** for endangered fish species in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The Division 3 Water Court granted two decrees (Case 82-CW256 filed 1992; Case 94-CW330 filed 1994) securing minimum flow volumes through the ecologically critical **"15-Mile Reach"** of the Colorado River. While not a biological morphology study, this record is foundational to the conservation status and habitat management of four endemic, endangered [[Colorado River Basin Fish]] species: the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Endemism:** All four species are described as occurring nowhere outside the [[Colorado River Basin]], indicating high habitat specificity and evolutionary isolation within this river system.
- **Habitat Segment:** The **"15-Mile Reach"** — the stretch of the mainstem Colorado River between the Grand Valley Irrigation Company diversion near Palisade, CO, and the confluence with the [[Gunnison River]] in Grand Junction, CO — is identified as critical habitat requiring protected flow regimes.
- **Flow Dependency:** Adequate instream flow (measured in cubic feet per second, cfs) is identified as an essential ecological requirement:
  - **561 cfs** of return flows from the Orchard Mesa hydroelectric facility maintained during **July, August, and September**, ensuring the river remains "wetted bank to bank" through the 15-Mile Reach.
  - **300 cfs** of protected "accretions" (additional inflows from irrigation return flows, storm water, and other tributary sources) within the same reach.
- **Threats Implied:** The need for legally decreed minimum flows implies that water diversion (e.g., irrigation, hydroelectric operations) had historically reduced streamflow to levels threatening [[Native Fish Species]] survival, likely via loss of spawning habitat, temperature regulation, and channel connectivity.
- **Multi-jurisdictional Management:** The species' recovery is managed under the **Recovery Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado**, a partnership (est. 1988) among Colorado, Wyoming, Utah, federal agencies, environmental groups, and water development interests — reflecting the broad, basin-scale ecological management required for wide-ranging riverine fish.

## Key Findings

1. **Legal Precedent:** On September 2, 1997, the Colorado Division 3 Water Court issued the **first-ever water rights decrees** explicitly designed to benefit endangered fish species, marking a significant precedent in U.S. water law and conservation policy.
2. **Instream Flow Program Origins:** Colorado's Instream Flow Program was created by the state legislature in 1973, empowering the CWCB to appropriate water rights to "preserve the natural environment to a reasonable degree." By 1997, this program had resulted in instream flow decrees covering over **7,900 miles of stream** and minimum lake level decrees on **435 natural lakes** statewide.
3. **Two Key Decrees for Endangered Fish:**
   - **Case 82-CW256** (filed 1992): Secures 561 cfs of Orchard Mesa hydroelectric return flows during summer months (Jul–Sep) in the 15-Mile Reach.
   - **Case 94-CW330** (filed 1994): Protects 300 cfs of accretion flows within the same reach.
4. **Resolution Without Litigation:** CWCB staff spent approximately five years negotiating with objecting parties, ultimately securing the decrees without formal litigation — a notable achievement in contentious Western water rights law.
5. **Target Species:** The decrees directly support recovery efforts for four endangered, endemic fish of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically "Colorado squawfish"), [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] — all listed under the federal [[Endangered Species Act]].
6. **Institutional Framework:** The [[Recovery Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado]] represents a multi-state (Colorado, Wyoming, Utah), multi-stakeholder collaborative model for basin-wide endangered species recovery, integrating water law, hydrology, and conservation biology.
