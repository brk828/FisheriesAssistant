# Razorback Sucker Critical Habitat Designation Process - U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Letter

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species #critical-habitat

## Executive Summary

This January 14, 1992 letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Utah-Colorado Field Office to the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] summarizes a December 17th meeting and formally requests review of information for establishing critical habitat for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The document outlines constituent habitat elements, identifies areas critical for survival versus recovery, and presents tentative recovery goals including downlisting criteria (self-sustaining populations in 3 of 5 basins) and delisting criteria (populations in all 5 basins). The Service requests completion of information forms by February 10, 1992, with a target completion date of December 1992 for the critical habitat package.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is an endangered fish species requiring critical habitat designation under Section 3 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Critical habitat is defined as specific areas containing physical or biological features essential to species conservation and recovery. The document identifies five primary constituent habitat elements for [[Razorback Sucker]]:

1. **Spawning areas**: Coarse clean substrates in depths <6 feet with flow velocities <1.5 feet/second
2. **Nursery habitats**: Slow moving or standing water where temperatures can warm above adjacent main river areas
3. **Drift corridors**: Unimpeded shorelines between spawning and nursery areas
4. **Feeding and rearing areas**: Mainstream portions of moderate and large waters, low velocity shorelines, pools and slow runs up to 10 foot depth, shallow to deep channels adjacent to or over midstream sandbars, eddies, backwaters, oxbow lakes, flooded bottomlands, and submerged sand bars
5. **Movement corridors**: Connections between all habitat types listed above

The species historically occupied multiple river basins across the [[Colorado River Basin]] system and [[Lower Colorado River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Recovery Team]] identified limiting factors on recruitment that make it difficult to determine which areas may support species recovery. The document emphasizes that establishment of populations in all proposed areas is not necessary for delisting, but multiple areas are being considered due to uncertainty about where recovery may be possible.

Five major basin systems are identified for recovery planning:
- [[Green River Basin]]
- [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]
- [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]
- [[San Juan River Basin]]
- [[Gila River Basin]]

The critical habitat designation process must consider water quality, flow regimes (quantity and hydrograph), predator loads, and the potential to restore areas that once supported viable populations. The document notes that economic impacts will be incorporated in later stages of the designation process.

## Key Findings

**Areas Critical for Survival:**
1. [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] from Lily Park downstream to Sand Wash
2. [[Colorado River]] from Rifle, Colorado to Westwater Canyon
3. [[Lower Colorado River]] from Hoover Dam to Palo Verde Dam

**Additional Areas Important for Recovery:**
1. [[Green River]] from Sand Wash to confluence with [[Colorado River]]
2. [[Gunnison River]] from Delta, Colorado to confluence with [[Colorado River]]
3. [[Colorado River]] from Westwater Canyon to and including Dirty Devil Arm of [[Lake Powell]]
4. [[Animas River]] from Durango, Colorado to Farmington, New Mexico
5. [[San Juan River]] from Farmington, New Mexico to and including San Juan Arm of [[Lake Powell]]
6. [[Verde River]] from Sullivan Lake to Horseshoe Reservoir
7. [[Salt River]] from Apache Falls to Roosevelt Lake
8. [[Gila River]] from San Carlos Reservoir to New Mexico border
9. [[Colorado River]] below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] downstream to Grand Wash on [[Lake Mead]]
10. [[Colorado River]] from Palo Verde Dam to Imperial Dam

**Tentative Recovery Goals:**

*Downlisting criteria:*
- Habitats are legally protected AND
- Self-sustaining populations (to be defined) established in 3 of 5 basins

*Delisting criteria:*
- Habitats are legally protected AND
- At least one self-sustaining population established in each of the 5 basins

**Process Timeline:**
- Information forms due: February 10, 1992
- Critical habitat package completion target: December 1992
- Initial assessment based solely on species biology and constituent elements
- Economic analysis to be incorporated in later stages
