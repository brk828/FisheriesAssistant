#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the role of larval starvation as a driver of chronic recruitment failure in the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) population of Lake Mohave, Arizona-Nevada. Field observations show wild-caught larvae disappear from the reservoir at small sizes (~10.3–10.6 mm TL), with absorbed yolk sacs, empty stomachs, and estimated ages of ~19 days — consistent with the transition from endogenous (yolk-dependent) to exogenous (prey-dependent) feeding, a vulnerable developmental window known as the "[[Critical Period]]" (May 1974). Three controlled laboratory experiments at Dexter National Fish Hatchery tested the effects of (1) complete food deprivation, (2) delayed food presentation, and (3) varying food ration size on larval survival and growth. Results demonstrate that larvae denied adequate food consistently die between 20 and 30 days post-hatching, and that a minimum threshold of ~30–60 nauplii fish⁻¹ d⁻¹ is required to survive this critical window. These findings support the hypothesis that food limitation, likely driven by low and variable zooplankton densities in Lake Mohave, is a plausible mechanism contributing to the population's chronic year-class failure.

## Background & Description
- **Initial larval size at stocking:** 9.6–10.7 mm TL (mean 9.96 ± 0.04 mm), 2 days post swim-up.
- **Yolk absorption:** Complete at ~8 days post-hatching at 18°C (consistent with prior data from Minckley & Gustafson 1982 at 15°C).
- **Size at death (starved, Experiment 1):** Mean 9.6 ± 0.2 mm TL.
- **Size at death (delayed feeding, Experiment 2):** Mean 9.3 ± 0.1 mm TL (n=35), death concentrated 20–30 d post-hatch.
- **Size at death (ration-limited, Experiment 3):** Mean 10.1 ± 0.2 mm TL (n=25).
- **Growth outcome — Experiment 2 (delayed feeding):** Larvae fed continuously from 7 d reached 23.2 mm TL by day 50; larvae first fed at day 31 (after prolonged starvation) reached only 15.8 mm TL (n=2), showing stunted but non-zero growth in starvation survivors.
- **Growth outcome — Experiment 3 (ration size):** Larvae on low rations (5, 10, 50 nauplii L⁻¹) reached 14.9–16.1 mm TL; larvae on high rations (500, 1000 nauplii L⁻¹) reached 23.7–25.0 mm TL. Larvae on 100 nauplii L⁻¹ were intermediate (19.4 mm TL).
- **Wild larvae (Lake Mohave):** Disappear at ~10.3–10.6 mm TL, matching laboratory sizes at death under food stress, supporting a shared mechanism.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Lake Mohave, a reservoir on the lower Colorado River (Arizona-Nevada), representing a lentic, thermally distinct environment (9–15°C during larval incubation) compared to the 18°C hatchery conditions used experimentally.
- **Trophic ecology:** Larvae transition from endogenous yolk reserves to exogenous planktivory (feeding on zooplankton, modeled here with *Artemia salina* nauplii) during the critical period.
- **Prey landscape:** Natural zooplankton densities in Lake Mohave are described as "low and variable" ([[Paulson et al. 1980]]; [[Langhorst & Marsh 1986]]), a condition mirrored by the low-ration treatments in this study that produced high mortality.
- **Population status:** Old adults persist and reproduce annually in Lake Mohave, but no recruitment to the adult population has been documented across extensive sampling efforts, indicating a bottleneck localized to the larval stage — directly implicating the [[Critical Period]] as the demographic failure point.
- **Comparative life history:** The critical period concept, first proposed by [[Hjort 1914]] and refined by [[May 1974]], is broadly applicable across larval marine and freshwater fishes (e.g., herring, anchovy, lake whitefish), situating razorback sucker findings within a wider framework of larval fish recruitment ecology.

## Key Findings
- **Experiment 1 (Total Starvation):** 54% of unfed larvae died by 25 d post-hatching; mean time to death 26.0 ± 1.4 d (range 10–50 d); mean size at death 9.6 ± 0.2 mm TL.
- **Experiment 2 (Delayed Feeding):**
  - No mortality when food was provided starting at 7 d post-hatch.
  - Mortality remained low and statistically indistinguishable for feeding onset at 7, 11, 15, and 19 d (0–26.7%).
  - Mortality rose sharply for feeding onset at 23 d (56.7%), 27 d (86.7%), and 31 d (93.3%).
  - Bulk of mortality occurred between 20–30 d post-hatching regardless of treatment.
- **Experiment 3 (Ration Size):**
  - High mortality (not significantly different) at 5 nauplii L⁻¹ (86.7%) and 10 nauplii L⁻¹ (73.3%).
  - Mortality dropped to <20% at 50, 100, 500, and 1000 nauplii L⁻¹, with no significant differences among these higher rations.
  - Threshold food density for maximal survival identified at ~50 nauplii L⁻¹ (equivalent to ~20 nauplii per larva per feeding, 3x daily).
  - Minimum sufficient ration estimated at 30–60 nauplii fish⁻¹ d⁻¹ to survive the critical period.
- **Defined Developmental Timeline (at 18°C):**
  - Yolk absorption complete: ~8 d post-hatching.
  - [[Critical Period]] (must find/consume food or die): ~8–19 d post-hatching.
  - Point of irreversible starvation: 19–23 d post-hatching.
  - Peak mortality window across all experiments: 20–30 d post-hatching.
- **Field-Lab Concordance:** Wild larvae collected from Lake Mohave are estimated at no older than ~19 days, with absorbed yolk and empty stomachs — placing them squarely within the laboratory-defined critical period, and supporting food limitation as a plausible driver of observed recruitment failure.
- **Temperature Caveat:** Lake Mohave's cooler ambient temperatures (9–15°C) versus experimental conditions (18°C) may extend the critical period duration in the wild, per temperature-dependent yolk utilization efficiency effects described in [[Houde 1974]] and [[Laurence 1973]].
- **Management Implication:** Chronic absence of larvae surviving past the critical period in Lake Mohave is consistent with food-related mortality contributing to sustained year-class failure in this endangered species.
