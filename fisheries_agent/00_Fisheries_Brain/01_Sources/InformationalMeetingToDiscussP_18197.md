# Interagency Meeting on San Bernardino Ranch Endangered Species Habitat and Geothermal Development

#ichthyology #conservation-policy #habitat-management

## Executive Summary

This 1980 memorandum from the Arizona Office of Economic Planning and Development convenes an interagency meeting to address potential conflicts between maintaining endangered species habitat at the [[San Bernardino Ranch]] and geothermal exploration/development in the vicinity. The meeting involves federal agencies (Bureau of Land Management, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service), state agencies (Arizona Game and Fish, State Land Department), academic institutions (Bureau of Geology & Mineral Technology), and conservation organizations (The Nature Conservancy). Primary concerns include impacts to water table-dependent fish populations and riparian habitat, Clean Air Act implications, and federal refuge establishment procedures.

## Background & Description

The [[San Bernardino Ranch]] in Arizona supports endangered species habitat, including fish populations dependent on the local water table and associated riparian habitat. The area has geothermal potential that may be explored and developed. The meeting was scheduled for February 19, 1980, in Phoenix, bringing together multiple stakeholders to discuss regulatory and ecological concerns before proceeding with either refuge establishment or geothermal operations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The habitat at [[San Bernardino Ranch]] is characterized by water table-dependent ecosystems supporting fish populations and riparian vegetation. The maintenance of these endangered species habitats requires stable groundwater levels. Geothermal exploration and potential development pose risks to the water table, which could directly impact aquatic species survival and riparian ecosystem integrity. The establishment of a national wildlife refuge was under consideration, which would impose additional regulatory constraints including Clean Air Act provisions on any industrial operations in the vicinity.

## Key Findings

The memorandum identifies four major discussion points for the interagency meeting:

1. **Clean Air Act Impacts**: Examination of how Clean Air Act regulations would affect geothermal exploration and operations if a national wildlife refuge is established at the site.

2. **Water Table Impacts**: Assessment of potential impacts to the water table from geothermal exploration and development, specifically concerning effects on fish populations and riparian habitat currently supported by groundwater.

3. **Federal/State Regulatory Framework**: Review of additional federal and state regulations applicable to establishing a federal refuge on the [[San Bernardino Ranch]].

4. **Interagency Communication**: Establishment of continued communication protocols between interested parties to coordinate management and development decisions.

Each participating agency was requested to prepare discussion of their specific regulatory role and jurisdiction related to these issues.
