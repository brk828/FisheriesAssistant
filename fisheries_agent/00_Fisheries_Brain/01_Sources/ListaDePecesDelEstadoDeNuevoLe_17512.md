# Checklist of the Fishes of the State of Nuevo León, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1967 checklist, published by the Universidad de Nuevo León's Instituto de Investigaciones Científicas, compiles the fish species recorded in the state of [[Nuevo Leon]]. It is intended as a baseline for fisheries management and for ongoing distribution studies of Mexican fishes. The author reports **54 taxa** in total, an update of the only earlier list, [[Alvarez 1952]], which recorded 48. Ten forms were removed, ten new records were added (one by introduction), and two species were confirmed with authentic records. The fauna is drawn from the [[Rio Bravo]] and its tributaries plus rivers that drain independently to the [[Gulf of Mexico]].

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Species knowledge is described as essential for studying fish biology and habits, and for regulating and administering fishery resources. The list precedes a planned illustrated descriptive catalogue of the state ichthyofauna.
- **Literature status:** State records were described as fragmentary but abundant. The list merges literature records with recent specimens collected by the author.
- **Drainages (north to south):** [[Rio Bravo]] (with the Rio Colombia confluence), [[Rio Salado]], [[Rio Alamo]] and [[Rio San Juan]] (tributaries of the Bravo), [[Rio San Fernando]], [[Rio Soto la Marina]], and [[Rio Tamesi]]. The last two (and to a degree the San Fernando) barely enter the state, so their fauna is poorly known within it.
- **Taxonomic arrangement:** Species are grouped by family and each entry gives common names (Spanish) and known localities within the state. A separate section lists 10 excluded names.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Reservoir effects:** The [[Presa Marte R. Gomez]], built on the [[Rio San Juan]] at the Nuevo León–Tamaulipas border, altered local fish records. Specimens taken there from Tamaulipas beaches are included for Nuevo León. The source states that [[American Eel]] (*Anguilla rostrata*) has probably been eliminated from the state because of the dam.
- **Introductions:** [[Common Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] are described as widely introduced. [[Warmouth]] (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*) was a new introduction to the reservoir, and [[Threadfin Shad]]-like silversides and other forms are recorded by new local records.
- **Endemic or restricted populations:** [[Xiphophorus couchianus]] was noted as probably disappeared from its type locality (Cañón de la Huasteca, Santa Catarina), with new localities at Ojo de Agua (Apodaca) and La Pastora (Villa de Guadalupe).
- **Rare or doubtful species:** Several species are rare or probably absent from the state, including [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus platyrhynchus*), [[Blue Sucker]] (*Cycleptus elongatus*), and [[Spotted Gar]]-related *Lepisosteus platostomus*, whose occurrence is considered doubtful.
- **Habitat context:** Most species are riverine or lowland freshwater taxa of the [[Rio Bravo]] and [[Gulf of Mexico]] drainages, with no marine species recorded as residents.

## Key Findings

**Checklist totals**
- 54 taxa are recorded for Nuevo León (author's total).
- The main list contains about 53 named entries across 15 families. The Cyprinidae are the most diverse family, with about 17 entries, mainly [[Notropis]] (7 entries).
- Largest families by named forms: Cyprinidae (about 17), then Ictaluridae (4), Poeciliidae (5), Cichlidae (2), and Lepisosteidae (3).

**Changes from the 1952 list**
- 10 forms were removed from the earlier list.
- 10 new records were added, one of which is an introduction.
- 2 species were confirmed with authentic records.

**Notable records**
- [[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*): first authentic record from the Lower San Juan River.
- [[Sheepshead Minnow]]-like *Poecilia latipinna* (Sailfin Molly): first authentic record from the Lower San Juan River.
- [[Dorosoma petenense]] (Threadfin Shad) and [[Notropis stramineus]] are new records from the Lower San Juan.
- [[Membras vagrans]] (Silverside): new record from the [[Presa Marte R. Gomez]].
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) is recorded from the Salado, San Juan, and San Fernando rivers.

**Uncertain or likely absent**
- *Anguilla rostrata*: rare, probably eliminated from the state.
- *Ictalurus natalis*: unverified records.
- *Lepisosteus platostomus*: doubtful occurrence.
- *Notemigonus crysoleucas* and *Lepomis cyanellus*: no recent captures.

**Excluded names (10)**
- Removed as belonging to other regions or as invalid: *Phenacobius mirabilis* (central Texas), *Hybopsis adustus* (Chihuahua, possibly not a valid species), *Gila pulchella* (northwest Mexico), *Notropis lutrensis garmani* (Nazas River only), *Moxostoma parvidens* (based on wrong locality), *Pantosteus plebeius* (northwest Mexico), *Ictalurus meeki* (Chihuahua and Durango), *Cyprinodon elegans* (Pecos system), *Cyprinodon latifasciatus* (endemic to Parras, Coahuila), and *Etheostoma laterale* (based on a goby, *Gobiomorus dormitor*).

**Stated purpose and outcome**
- The list is a base for distribution studies and for a future illustrated catalogue of the state's fishes, and it provides the basis for fishery regulation and management.
