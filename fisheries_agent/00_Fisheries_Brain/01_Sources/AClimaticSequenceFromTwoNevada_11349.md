# A Climatic Sequence from Two Nevada Caves

#palynology #paleoecology #pleistocene-climate

## Executive Summary

Sears and Roosma reconstruct a climatic sequence for roughly the last 15,000 years from pollen preserved in floor sediments of two granite caves, [[Guano Cave]] and [[Fishbone Cave]], in Pershing County, Nevada. The caves were cut by [[Pluvial Lake Lahontan]] and were occupied by people and animals from about the time of the [[Two Creeks Interstadial]]. Modern disturbance and sampling constraints prevented a continuous stratigraphic record, so the authors combined [[Radiocarbon Dating]], sediment characteristics, and statistical treatment of pollen spectra. The resulting profile shows a climax of aridity around 6,000–7,000 years B.P., shortly after the last evidence of Pleistocene horse and camel, and a lesser arid phase near 3,200 B.P. The authors argue that earlier climatic change must await coring of [[Lake Winnemucca]] sediments.

## Background & Description

**Study sites.** Both caves are cut in granite at about 4,060 ft elevation, roughly 270 ft above the present level of [[Pyramid Lake]], at the north end of dry Lake Winnemucca. Guano Cave may have persisted longer as a depositional site, possibly because a barrier formed across the mouth of Fishbone. Earlier archaeological and geological work was published by [[P. C. Orr]] (1956) and [[Broecker and Orr]] (1958).

**Stratigraphy.** Orr designated six levels in Fishbone and seven in Guano. Level descriptions range from windblown dust and rat and bat guano at the top, to habitation layers containing artifacts, ash, charcoal and human burials, to lake sand and gravel at the base. Horse and camel bones occur in the Fishbone habitation levels, though some may have been dug up from below.

**Samples.** The first set comprised seven floor-sediment samples (four from Guano, three from Fishbone). A second, more extensive set was then requested, including:
- Guano, left side: 9 samples (7GN–15GN), 0 to 100 inches.
- Guano, right side: 6 samples (16GN–21GN), 12 to 54 inches.
- Fishbone, from the top of Level 6: 6 samples (1FB–6FB), 4 to 60 inches.

Pollen was counted in the first-set tables (12 columns, Guano and Fishbone levels) and in the second-set tables (Tables 2–4). Total pollen counts per sample ranged from roughly 35 to about 1,600 grains in the reported spectra.

**Method.** Pollen percentages were grouped into four altitudinal zones: Arboreal (AP), Artemisia, Grassland, and Desert. The authors assumed these zones shift up and down the slopes with climate, so the arboreal fraction served as the main climatic indicator. Sediment character (mineral content, ostracod and gastropod shells, bone, charcoal, flint) was used to order undated samples.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The vegetation record traces movement between montane forest (pine, fir, spruce, juniper, oak) and desert communities (Chenopodiaceae, Cactaceae, Ephedra, with Artemisia and grasses as intermediate zones). High arboreal pollen indicates moister conditions; high Chenopodiaceae and Cactaceae indicate aridity. The authors also note that Searles Lake, California, shows an organic layer with higher arboreal pollen beneath 90 ft of salt, dated to 10,500 ± 560 B.P. at the top and 21,200 ± 2,170 B.P. at the base. This layer is contemporaneous with active Wisconsin glaciation, which the authors read as evidence that glacial-period climate reached far from the ice margin.

Tufa samples indicate standing or shallow calcareous water at the cave entrances at certain times. Owl pellets preserved on the floor around 15,130 B.P. suggest the floor was exposed or only shallowly covered then.

**Main conclusions:**
1. The caves became habitable at about 11,500 B.P., near the Two Creeks interval, and remained so until abandonment sometime after 2,000 years ago despite intermittent moist episodes.
2. The climax of aridity occurred at about 6,250 B.P. (abstract gives 6,000–7,000 B.P.), shortly after the latest evidence of Pleistocene mammals (last dated at 7,380 B.P.).
3. A less extreme arid phase developed about 3,200 B.P. (sample 18GN, the desert maximum).
4. Undated spectra were placed by inference: G3 with a moister trend beginning around the Christian era; F3 at about 5,000 B.P. (following the humid episode after 6,000 B.P.); G5 with the [[Valders Glaciation]]; 14GN with the [[Port Huron Glaciation]]. The oldest and most arboreal sample, 1FB, is placed at about 15,760 B.P. and lies between two more arid intervals.
5. Sequence of the lowest undated spectra is reasonably secure, but their timing is not.

**Limitations and recommendations:**
- Discontinuous stratigraphy (vandalism and original site conditions) prevented a continuous record; the authors describe the problem as reconstructing from "fragments of physical evidence, despite serious gaps."
- Some dates are reported inconsistently across the text, tables, and figures (10GN, 1FB), which should be checked against the original data.
- The authors recommend coring and pollen analysis of [[Lake Winnemucca]] bed sediments to test and refine the reconstruction, citing its climatic and archaeological importance.
