## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 interagency review memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Arizona Ecological Services Office (AESO), commenting on a draft **Lower Colorado River Native Fish Management Plan**. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research, the memo provides regulatory and programmatic feedback on recovery planning for four federally listed endangered big-river fishes native to the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrachen texanus*). The AESO broadly supports the plan's intent but raises concerns regarding sequencing of reintroduction efforts, taxonomic/nomenclatural consistency, critical habitat designation, coordination structure, and the geographic scope of recovery actions (particularly the exclusion of interior Arizona tributaries such as the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], and [[Verde River]]).

## Background & Description

The source text contains no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data. It is a management/policy document rather than a taxonomic or morphological study. The only taxonomic note of substance is a nomenclatural clarification:

- *Gila elegans* — commonly recognized as "bonytail," but formally listed as endangered in 1980 under the common name **"bonytail chub."** The AESO recommends consistent use of "bonytail chub" to match the legal listing terminology.

No other morphological descriptors (body form, coloration, fin counts, size classes) are provided in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not a primary ecological study, the memo does convey key habitat and population context for these four species:

- **Core habitat**: Mainstem [[Lower Colorado River]], including managed impoundments [[Lake Havasu]] and [[Lake Mohave]], identified as focal areas for early-stage ("Level III") recovery tasks and grow-out/rearing facility operations.
- **Historic/peripheral habitat**: [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], and [[Verde River]] — sites of earlier razorback sucker stocking efforts. Though not highly successful, surviving individuals became fully protected under the Endangered Species Act upon the species' 1991 listing.
- **Tributary ecoregion question**: The plan separately designates the [[Virgin River]] and [[Little Colorado River]] as their own ecoregion, distinct from the mainstem Colorado River ecoregion — a distinction the AESO questions, suggesting these tributaries might be more appropriately grouped with their Colorado River confluence zone (Ecoregion I).
- **Special adaptations**: The draft plan references unspecified "special adaptations" of these species, which the AESO requests be elaborated upon — implying these fish possess distinctive physiological or behavioral traits adapted to big-river conditions (e.g., turbidity, flow, temperature regimes typical of the Colorado River system), though specifics are not detailed in this memo.
- **Population status considerations**: Emphasis is placed on maintaining genetically sound broodstock and captive/grow-out populations as a prerequisite for reintroduction, reflecting a niche currently constrained by small, fragmented wild populations dependent on augmentation programs.

## Key Findings

- **Species prioritization discrepancy**: The draft plan emphasizes the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] as primary recovery targets, with the [[Colorado Squawfish]] as secondary, while recovery actions for the [[Humpback Chub]] are deferred to future plan iterations — creating inconsistency when all four species are later discussed together.
- **Uncertainty in rearing programs**: No large-scale rearing program currently exists for Colorado squawfish; the AESO questions whether one is planned and whether sufficient genetically sound broodstock is available.
- **Reintroduction sequencing concern**: AESO objects to language suggesting habitat and reintroduction work outside Lake Havasu, Lake Mohave, and the lower Colorado River mainstem would be deferred until mainstem populations stabilize (i.e., pausing progress until completion of "Level III" tasks). AESO argues habitat protection/enhancement and population augmentation (e.g., in the Gila, Salt, Verde Rivers) should proceed **concurrently**, not sequentially.
- **Regulatory/administrative gaps**: 
  - Recommendation to revisit the "experimental non-essential" population designation for Colorado squawfish in the Salt and Verde Rivers.
  - Clarification needed on recovery team formation authority — only the Regional Director can formally establish a recovery team; the Lower Basin Coordinator can only recommend team formation and membership.
  - Suggestion that the Lower Basin Coordinator and Coordination Team Leader roles be merged to reduce redundancy; recovery team leader need not be a Service employee.
- **Critical habitat**: Critical habitat has been designated for these four species; AESO recommends explicitly listing or mapping these areas within the plan.
- **Geographic scope**: AESO advocates expanding recovery efforts beyond the lower Colorado River mainstem to include interior Arizona rivers (Gila, Salt, Verde), citing existing grow-out infrastructure and multi-agency interest beyond the Colorado River corridor.
- **Legal/administrative note**: Any range expansion for [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] into new population areas will likely require new proposed and final regulatory rules, and coordination should begin promptly to avoid delaying recovery.
