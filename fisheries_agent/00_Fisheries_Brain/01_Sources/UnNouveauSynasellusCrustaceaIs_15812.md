# Synasellus mariae favaiensis ssp. n.

#Isopoda #Asellidae #Crustacea #stygofauna

## Executive Summary
This paper by Jorge Eiras (1974), published under the "Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre" series (Porto, Portugal), describes a new stygobiotic (subterranean freshwater) isopod subspecies, **[[Synasellus mariae favaiensis]]**, within the genus **[[Synasellus]]** (family [[Asellidae]]). The taxon was collected from groundwater wells in the locality of Favaios, Trás-os-Montes province, Portugal, in July 1973. The description is based on 17 males and 37 females collected from wells on the Borralheira and Arufe farms. The new subspecies is differentiated from the nominotypical **[[Synasellus mariae mariae]]** (Braga, 1942) primarily by body size, antennal proportions, and fine details of mouthpart and pleopod chaetotaxy (setae/spine counts). This work is part of a broader taxonomic lineage of Portuguese asellid isopod research initiated by J. M. Braga, documenting the rich groundwater-adapted crustacean fauna of Iberia.

## Background & Description
**General body form:**
- Body with parallel sides, ~5.3× longer than wide; maximum width at pereionite VI in both sexes.
- Completely depigmented; no trace of eyes or ocular pigment — consistent with troglobitic/stygobitic adaptation.
- Head slightly longer than wide (1.7× longer, slightly narrower than pereionite I).

**Size (largest specimens measured):**
| Measurement | Male (♂) | Female (♀) |
|---|---|---|
| Body length (excl. antennae/uropods) | 6.2 mm | 7.4 mm |
| Width | 1.15 mm | 1.3 mm |
| Antennules | 1.05 mm | 1.25 mm |
| Antennae | 4.4 mm | 5.5 mm |
| Pleotelson | 1.6 mm | 2.15 mm |
| Uropods | 1.3 mm | 1.7 mm |

- Average length: ♂ 6.3–5 mm; ♀ 9.5–5.8 mm (as reported).
- Holotype: adult male, 5.5 mm long (excl. antennae/uropods), deposited at the Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre," Faculty of Sciences, University of Porto.

**Appendages:**
- **Antennules**: In males slightly shorter than antennal peduncle; in females shorter than half the peduncle. Flagellum with 6–7 articles (♂) and 5–7 (♀). Olfactory setae (aesthetascs): 3–4 in females, 4–8 in males.
- **Antennae**: Long, nearly reaching pleotelson; 35–40 articles (♂), 32–40 (♀).
- **Maxillae I & II**: Detailed spine/seta counts on external and internal lobes (12 strong distal spines on Maxilla I outer lamella; 11–23 setae across lobes of Maxilla II).
- **Mandibles**: Left mandible with 4-toothed apical/medial apophysis plus 3 strong semipectinate setae; right mandible apophysis with 2 strong semipectinate setae; molar processes cylindrical and voluminous.
- **Maxillipede**: Body 2.6× longer than wide; masticatory lobe with 2–4 retinacles; epipodite trapezoidal with 10–14 unequal smooth setae.
- **Pereiopods I–VII**: Detailed relative length ratios by sex; dactyls bear a single long spine near base of terminal claw. Pereiopod I in males non-chelate but robust (ovoid propodus, 1.8× longer than wide); Pereiopod IV in males NOT specialized into a nuptial hook (distinguishing feature).
- **Pleopods I–V**: Detailed sympodite/exopodite/endopodite proportions and setal counts; pleopod II male sympodite with **rectilinear external border** (key diagnostic trait vs. nominotypical subspecies); sternal fissure narrow, positioned in distal quarter of endopodite.
- **Pleotelson**: Subrectangular, 1.2× longer than wide, slightly convex borders, telson point well marked.
- **Uropods**: Shorter (1/5 less) than the pleotelson — a key diagnostic feature distinguishing this subspecies from *S. mariae mariae*, whose uropods are much longer than the pleotelson.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Subterranean freshwater — specifically groundwater accessed via wells on agricultural land (Borralheira and Arufe farms), rather than caves per se.
- **Geographic range**: Localized to Favaios, Trás-os-Montes province, northern Portugal — part of the broader Iberian [[Stygofauna]] radiation of the genus [[Synasellus]].
- **Adaptations to subterranean life**: Total depigmentation and complete absence of eyes/ocular structures, typical of stygobiotic Asellidae.
- **Collection context**: Specimens collected in July 1973 (10th and 11th), reflecting standard groundwater faunal survey methodology (well sampling) used extensively in Portuguese subterranean biology research.
- This taxon belongs to a broader guild of Portuguese groundwater Asellidae including other described species/subspecies such as *Asellus seabrai*, *Asellus pauloae*, *Asellus frontellum*, *Synasellus albicastrensis*, *Synasellus serranus*, *Synasellus nobrei*, and *Synasellus minutus* — indicating a diverse and highly endemic Iberian subterranean crustacean fauna.

## Key Findings
- **New subspecies established**: *Synasellus mariae favaiensis* ssp. n., named for its type locality, Favaios.
- **Taxonomic placement**: Assigned to *Synasellus mariae* (Braga) based on male pleopod II morphology, which the author considers diagnostic at the species level; however, sufficient morphological differences warranted subspecific separation from the nominotypical form, now designated *Synasellus mariae mariae*.
- **Principal differentiating characters from *S. mariae mariae*** (per comparative table):
  - Larger body length (♂ 6.2 mm vs 6–6.5 mm status quo comparison; ♀ 7.4 mm vs 4–6.5 mm range in nominotypical form).
  - Relatively longer antennae relative to body length (nearly reaching pleotelson vs. only slightly longer than half body length in nominotypical form).
  - Fewer retinacles in the maxillipede's masticatory lobe (2–4 vs. 2–5).
  - Fewer setae on the maxillipede epipodite (10–14 vs. 19–20).
  - Rectilinear (vs. concave) external border of the pleopod II sympodite in males.
  - Fewer setae on pleopod II exopodite in males.
  - Uropods shorter than the pleotelson (vs. much longer in nominotypical form) — one of the most conspicuous distinguishing traits.
- **Methodology**: Standard isopod taxonomic protocol — dissection, mounting in polyvinyl-lactophenol, camera lucida illustrations, comparative meristic tabulation against the type subspecies.
- **Broader context**: This paper is one contribution within a long, continuous Portuguese research tradition (J. M. Braga → Jorge Eiras) on the systematics of subterranean Asellidae (*Synasellus*, *Stenasellus*, *Asellus*) in Portugal, reflecting significant endemic radiation of stygobiotic isopods across the Iberian Peninsula.
