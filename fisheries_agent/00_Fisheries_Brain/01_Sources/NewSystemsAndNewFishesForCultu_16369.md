# New Systems and New Fishes for Culture in the United States and Europe

#aquaculture #pond-culture #commercial-fish-farming #warm-water-species

## Executive Summary

This document presents two comprehensive reviews of commercial warm-water pond fish culture developments: one covering the United States (by James R. Fielding) and one covering Europe (by Elek Woynarovich). The U.S. section describes four new systems of commercial pond culture that emerged in the 20th century, with [[Channel Catfish]] (Ictalurus punctatus) identified as the most desirable species due to efficient feed conversion, high production (2,423 kg/ha in 252 days reported), and strong consumer acceptance. The European section details advances in [[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio) pond design, water supply systems using pumping, and the introduction of Chinese herbivorous species. Both reviews emphasize the economic viability of commercial food fish production in ponds and the importance of polyculture systems that utilize multiple trophic levels.

## Background & Description

**United States Context:**
Commercial pond culture for food fish production is described as a recent development in the U.S., emerging primarily in the last 20 years (as of 1966). Initial development was closely tied to rice production in the Mississippi Delta, where farmers discovered that flooding fields for weed control could simultaneously support fish crops. By the time of publication, approximately 28,300 ha were in commercial warm-water food fish production. The document covers culture systems for buffalo fishes, catfishes, crayfish, brackish-water species, and miscellaneous species.

**European Context:**
European carp culture has a history spanning several hundred years, with modern methods developed since the late 19th century. The European section focuses on innovations in pond construction and water management, particularly the use of electric and diesel pumps to supply water to ponds distant from natural water sources. In Hungary, more than half of the 100,000 ha of ponds use pumped water continuously or periodically. The document describes new pond designs featuring external fishbeds and the introduction of predatory and herbivorous species to complement carp production.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pond Water Requirements (Hungary):**
Normal water requirements for operating fish ponds vary by soil porosity:
- Non-porous soil: 10,000 m³/ha for filling, 4,000 m³/ha for maintenance
- Moderately porous soil: 13,000 m³/ha for filling, 6,000 m³/ha for maintenance
- Very porous soil: 16,000 m³/ha for filling, 10,000 m³/ha for maintenance

**Temperature Requirements:**
- [[Channel Catfish]]: feeding response best above 21°C, comparatively little growth below 16°C
- [[Grass Carp]]: optimum feeding temperature over 20°C, hardly eat at 14°C
- [[Pike-perch]]: spawning occurs at 18-21°C
- [[Sheatfish]]: spawning at 20°C

**Polyculture Rationale:**
Both reviews emphasize that natural food production in ponds is complex, and single-species culture fails to utilize all available resources. Polyculture systems are designed to:
1. Utilize different food sources (predators consume small fish, herbivores consume plants, filter-feeders consume plankton)
2. Control unwanted species (predators eliminate wild fish that compete with cultured species)
3. Provide "sanitary service" (predators consume weak or diseased fish)
4. Increase overall pond productivity through complementary feeding habits

**Brackish-Water Culture:**
The U.S. section describes preliminary experiments in brackish-water pond culture, noting that while systems are well-developed in Asian countries, commercial operations had not yet been initiated in the U.S. Salinity ranges of 7.6-35.7 ppt were tested with various species.

## Key Findings

**United States - Buffalo Fish Culture:**

Production rates varied significantly with stocking density and management:
- Stocking at 1,250/ha in unfertilized ponds: 567 kg/ha
- Stocking at 593/ha in fertilized ponds: fish averaged 0.58 kg in 6 months, 0.92 kg in 1 year, 1.09 kg in 18 months
- Stocking at 297/ha: fish averaged 0.64 kg in 6 months, 1.63 kg in 18 months
- Feeding with soybean meal gave higher production than fertilization alone, but costs exceeded product value

Buffalo fish contain many small bones that consumers find objectionable; minimum marketable size is 2.7 kg for most inland markets. Cross-breeding experiments showed promise: black × bigmouth cross was 33% heavier than parent species after one year.

**United States - Channel Catfish Culture:**

[[Channel Catfish]] emerged as the most economical species for commercial pond culture:
- Maximum production: 2,423 kg/ha in 252 days (stocking 4,942 fingerlings/ha with supplemental pelleted feed)
- Production range: 1,070-2,624 kg/ha depending on stocking rate and feeding method
- Most efficient feed conversion: 1.2:1, average conversion 1.87:1 for seven experiments
- Survival rates: 79.6-99.2% with effective parasite and disease treatment (average 94.6% for 13 populations)
- Feeding rate: 2-5% of estimated fish weight when water temperature above 16°C, maximum 33.6 kg/ha/day to prevent oxygen depletion

Pelleted fish feed proved twice as efficient as dry meal mix. Spring stocking (March-April) resulted in lower conversion rates, lower cost per unit, and higher average gains than autumn stocking due to winter temperature effects.

**United States - Crayfish Culture:**

[[Louisiana Red Crayfish]] culture developed as a rotation crop with rice:
- Production cycle: rice planted in May, flooded June-August, drained and harvested in August, reflooded September-June for crayfish
- Harvest period: December through early June
- Yield: 448-1,120 kg/ha in well-managed fields
- Stocking rate: 6-12 kg adults/ha if natural population insufficient
- Growth to minimum edible size (15-20 g): approximately 210 days

By 1963-64 season, approximately 1,900 ha of rice fields were in crayfish rotation, with an equal area of swamp-land also in cultivation.

**United States - Brackish-Water Experiments:**

[[White Shrimp]] showed high production potential:
- 1953 experiment: stocked 118,000 shrimp/ha (341.6 kg/ha), recovered 80,099 shrimp (1,147.3 kg/ha) after 4.5 months, gain of 806.7 kg/ha
- 1954 experiment: stocked 180,000 shrimp/ha (214 kg), recovered 93,225 shrimp (1,601.8 kg/ha) after 5 months
- Individual weight increase: 1.19 g to 17.19 g (over 1,300% increase)
- Feed conversion: 7.05:1
- Winter growth experiment: 20% mortality, gain of 207 kg/ha over winter period

[[Common Pompano]] experiments:
- Production: 270-438 kg/ha over 65-133 days
- Maximum production: 438 kg/ha from ponds stocked with 4,942 pompano/ha for 65 days
- Average daily production: 4.03 kg/ha (range 2.35-6.38 kg/ha)
- Average feed conversion: 6:1
- Mortality: 13-23.9% (average 18.7%)
- Largest fish: averaged 268.0 g after 108 days

**Europe - Pond Design Innovations:**

The central external fishbed system offers multiple advantages:
- Eliminates need for individual fishbeds in each pond (which fill with mud)
- Allows up to 9 ponds to drain successively into one external fishbed
- Facilitates mechanization of fishing operations
- Dimensions: 15-25 m wide, 100-500 m long (exceptionally 1,000 m)
- Enables year-round maintenance and concrete construction for easier fish handling

**Europe - Predatory Fish Culture:**

[[Pike-perch]] (Lucioperca lucioperca):
- Growth: one-summer-old 12-25 cm (30-100 g), two-summer-old 30-40 cm (200-500 g), three-summer-old 37-55 cm (500-1,500 g)
- Stocking rates: 1,000-2,000 eggs or 50-100 one-summer-old or 30-60 two-summer-old fish per hectare
- Feed conversion: 5-10 kg small fish to produce 1 kg pike-perch flesh
- Spawning: induced in concrete tanks using hypophysation (4 pituitary hypophyses for 2-2.5 kg females)
- Egg production: 20,000-200,000 eggs per nest

[[Sheatfish]] (Silurus glanis):
- Growth: one-summer-old 50-100 g, two-summer-old 500-1,400 g, three-summer-old 1,500-2,500 g
- Stocking rates: 1,500-2,000 fingerlings/ha (4-6 cm size), 80-150 one-summer-old, 35-60 two-summer-old per hectare
- Spawning: occurs naturally in storage basins at 20°C using "tent-nest" structures
- Hatching: 30,000-50,000 eggs per crate, hatch in 2.5 days at 20-22°C
- Cannibalism: develops at 10-20 days old, requiring size grading

**Europe - Herbivorous Fish Introduction:**

[[Grass Carp]] (Ctenopharyngodon idella):
- Growth rates vary by region: Hungary two-summer-old 550-1,200 g, three-summer-old 1,500-4,000 g
- Food consumption: 1 kg fish consumes 0.8-1.5 kg green food daily; 18-22 kg green food needed for 1 kg weight gain
- Stocking rates: 500-800/ha for weed control, 200-500/ha for maintenance, 30-150 three-to-four-year-old fish/ha
- Plant preferences: eagerly consumed Potamogeton natans, P. lucens, Myriophyllum spicatum, Ceratophyllum demersum, Elodea canadensis
- Artificial reproduction: successful using hypophysation (1 hypophysis per kg body weight for females), spawning at 23-25°C
- Fingerling production: 40,000-50,000/ha when fed artificial foods and vegetable feeds

[[Silver Carp]] (Hypophthalmichthys molitrix):
- Feeds primarily on planktonic algae through branchial filter
- Growth: Hungary one-summer-old 57-66 g; Rumania two-summer-old 2,030 g, three-summer-old 2,500-4,000 g
- Stocking: 250-500/ha yielded 250-500 kg increase in fish crop
- More sensitive than grass carp; requires careful handling and transport in water 1-6°C
- Intestines 5.3-7.9 times body length

[[Bighead Carp]] (Aristichthys nobilis):
- Fastest growing of Chinese carps, especially after third summer
- Rumania three-summer-old: 4,500-7,500 g
- Feeds on zooplankton, algae, and floating detritus
- Intestines 3.2-5 times body length
- Successfully hybridized with [[Silver Carp]]

**Europe - Tench Culture:**

[[Tench]] (Tinca tinca):
- Growth: one-summer-old 2-10 g, two-summer-old 25-150 g, three-summer-old 170-450 g (females grow 50-60% faster than males)
- Stocking: 20-30 one-summer-old tench per 100 carp of same age; 20 two-summer-old tench per 100 two-summer-old carp
- Production: 50-80 kg/ha, yield typically 2-3 times original stock weight
- Market preference: 120-300 g fish for portion sales
- Advantages: grub deeper than carp, utilize food carp leave, consume carp excrement, 20% higher market price than carp
- Spawning: naturally in ponds at 20°C, females produce ~300,000 eggs

**Economic Considerations:**

Cost of pumped water in Hungary: 0.3-0.45 U.S. cents/m³, considered negligible in large-scale operations. The document emphasizes that proper species selection and polyculture management are essential for economic viability. [[Channel Catfish]] in the U.S. and [[Common Carp]] with complementary species in Europe represent the most economically successful systems described.
