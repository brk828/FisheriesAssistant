# Age and Growth of Xyrauchen texanus (Razorback Sucker)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen

## Executive Summary

This report, by Mark Fredlake, estimates the age and growth of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) from [[Scale Analysis]] of fish collected from [[Lake Mohave]], a mainstream reservoir in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]. Sixty-three fish were captured in November 1975, and scales from 20 preserved specimens (15 females, 5 males) were analyzed. Lateral scale radii showed the strongest relationship with standard length, and [[Annuli]] counts were used with the [[Dahl-Lea Method]] to back-calculate growth. Growth was nearly linear, females grew faster than males, and females reached maturity at about years eight and nine. A supplementary analysis found the length–scale relationship weaker than first reported, which casts doubt on scale-based growth estimates for this species. The author proposes a [[Tagging And Recapture]] program as a more reliable approach.

## Background & Description

*Xyrauchen texanus* is a large fish of the Colorado River system. The document describes adults that were fully mature, with females carrying distended ovaries and males bearing breeding tubercles on the anal and caudal fins. The species was regarded as a "trashfish" in the past. Little growth research had been done on it, and populations had declined sharply by the time of the study. The report cites [[Minkley]] (1973), who gives a potential maximum size of about 1 meter.

**Sampling:**
- **Collection:** 63 fish captured by a team from the [[Lower Colorado River Basin Research Station]], [[Arizona State University]], in late November 1975.
- **Scale study:** 20 preserved specimens (15 females, 5 males) sampled in April 1976.
- **Scale site selection:** Four body regions were tested. Region 3 (midway between the dorsal fin and lateral line) was chosen because its scales were the most symmetrical and least variable. This choice followed [[Lagler]] (1956).
- **Scale preparation:** Scales were soaked in potassium hydroxide, rinsed, and dry-mounted between slides. Impressions made with a scale press lacked detail.
- **Measurement:** Anterior, posterior, and lateral radii were measured with a [[Scale Projection Apparatus]] and compared with [[Standard Length]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The author argues that Lake Mohave is not natural habitat for the species, and that this affects its growth and survival:

- **Predators:** Razorbacks face many predators at every life stage, including the [[Green Sunfish]] and the [[Striped Bass]].
- **Competition:** The planktivorous and highly prolific [[Threadfin Shad]] may compete with razorbacks for food.
- **Altered conditions:** [[Reservoir Impoundment]] has changed turbidity, temperature, and flow rate.
- **Consequences:** Growth and survival of young fish may be suppressed until they reach a certain age or size. Unusual growth curves and a predominance of older fish could signal a declining population and possible future disappearance.

The sample also lacked young fish. The 1975 collection contained only large individuals, so a full size distribution was not available. Environmental stress or unusually mild winters could also produce false or missing annuli, which would distort age estimates.

## Key Findings

**1. Scale measurements and length relationships**
- Lateral radii gave the strongest association with standard length, so they were used for all later annuli measurements.
- Reported linear relationships (main analysis):
  - Posterior radii vs. standard length: r ≈ 0.55
  - Anterior radii vs. standard length: value unreadable in scan
  - Lateral radii vs. standard length: r ≈ 0.6 (partly garbled in scan), with the equation V = 2.8903 + 0.1263X
- Power and exponential functions were tested but did not fit better than the linear model.

**2. Scale selection and sample size**
- Some scales were regenerative and were discarded, so each fish had between 2 and 5 usable scales.
- When annuli counts varied within a fish, the majority count was used. Counts never differed by more than one.

**3. Age and growth model**
- Scale formation was assumed to begin at about 11 months of age, based on personal correspondence with W. L. Minckley (1976).
- The regression intercept (2.89 cm) was taken as the length at which scale growth begins.
- The first annulus was set to 0.5 cm, giving year-1 mean growth of about 1.6 to 2.9 cm, which agreed with the intercept.
- Growth was calculated with the Dahl-Lea direct proportion formula (Ricker, 1949). Means, standard errors, and 95% confidence limits were tabulated for annuli 1 through 11 (Tables 2.1–2.11).

**4. Growth patterns**
- Growth was described as nearly linear. This departs from most fishes, which grow rapidly until maturity and then slow.
- For nine females of the 1965 year class, mean annual growth rose to a peak of 5.7 cm per year in 1975.
- Two males of the 1965 year class grew more slowly until 1975, when they grew almost 6 cm.
- Females showed a slight growth peak in 1972–1973 followed by a slight decline. Five males showed a sharp increase in 1975.
- Most females reached peak growth in their seventh or eighth year.
- Females were estimated to reach maturity at years eight and nine, at about 39 cm (the upper bound is illegible in the scan). The male sample was too small for estimates of age or size at maturity.
- The author concluded that males probably grow more slowly than females, but noted that the sample sizes were too small for statistical tests.

**5. Validity concerns**
- The author questions whether scale annuli are true annual marks, since spawning, high temperature, or mild winters could create false or missing annuli.
- Agreement among year classes on relative good or poor growth in a given year suggested the annuli have some validity.

**6. Supplementary analysis (revised conclusions)**
- When unsuitable scales were removed, the length–scale correlation fell to r = 0.4834 (equation Y = 4.44 + 0.097X, where Y is lateral radii mean).
- The association was much weaker than first reported. The author questions whether scale growth tracks actual somatic growth at all.
- Proposed next step: a [[Tagging And Recapture]] program with live fish at the [[Phoenix Zoo]]. This would record actual growth, allowing the scale-based estimates to be checked against measured growth under near-natural conditions.
