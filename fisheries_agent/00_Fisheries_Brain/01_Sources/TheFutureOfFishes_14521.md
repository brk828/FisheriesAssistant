# The future of fishes

## Executive Summary
This bulletin, published by the [[Aquatic Conservation Network]] (ACN), is a conservation advocacy essay rather than a primary data paper. It addresses the global decline of freshwater fish biodiversity and argues for greater involvement of amateur aquarists in species conservation efforts. The author, [[Rob Huntley]], frames the crisis using the obscure European [[Bleak]] (a small silvery cyprinid) as a symbol of overlooked, "unglamorous" species facing extinction. The piece cites contemporary (1996) estimates of global fish diversity and extinction risk, and highlights case studies such as the [[Lake Victoria Cichlids]] collapse, Mexican desert [[Pupfish]], and threatened rainforest fish fauna. It also discusses the ethical and practical role of hobbyist aquarists in ex-situ conservation, drawing on Colin Tudge's *Last Animals at the Zoo* (1991).

## Background & Description
- **[[Bleak]]**: Described as a small, silvery, freshwater fish native to Northern European rivers, approximately 6 inches (15 cm) in length. Likely referring to *Alburnus alburnus* (family Cyprinidae), though the source text does not provide the scientific name.
- No detailed morphological data is provided for other taxa mentioned (e.g., [[Lake Victoria Cichlids]], [[Pupfish]]); these are referenced only in a conservation context.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Bleak**: Inhabits N. European river systems; ecological role not detailed beyond general freshwater cyprinid life history.
- **[[Lake Victoria Cichlids]] (Haplochromines)**: Referenced as a group suffering severe population collapse, historically maintained in captivity by Dr. Gordon Reid (Horniman Museum, later Chester Zoo) for ex-situ breeding and conservation.
- **[[Pupfish]] (Desert Fishes Council)**: Represents endemic, highly restricted desert spring/pool habitats in the southwestern USA and Mexico, emblematic of extreme habitat specialization and vulnerability to water extraction and habitat loss.
- **Rainforest fishes**: Broadly cited as threatened by habitat destruction, though no specific species or family is detailed.
- General ecosystem context: freshwater fish populations globally are described as facing collapse due to habitat degradation, human exploitation, and lack of conservation prioritization for "non-charismatic" species.

## Key Findings
- **Species diversity estimates (1996)**: ~24,000 known fish species, with projections suggesting the true total may be as high as 35,000 species, many undiscovered.
- **Extinction projection**: At least **one-third of freshwater fish species** were projected to become extinct within a few decades from the time of writing — approximately **10,000 species**.
- **Case studies of decline**:
  - [[Lake Victoria Cichlids]] — dramatic population crashes noted as a flagship example of freshwater fish conservation crisis.
  - Mexican desert fishes (e.g., [[Pupfish]]) — endangered due to habitat specialization and water resource conflicts; referenced via the [[Desert Fishes Council]] and Phil Pister's famous rebuttal: *"What use are you?"* in response to *"What use is a pupfish?"*.
  - Rainforest fish species — generally noted as threatened, without specific taxonomic detail.
- **Role of amateur aquarists**: The article argues aquarists possess specialized knowledge and resources (equipment, breeding expertise) that could meaningfully contribute to conservation breeding programs, citing successful amateur-assisted conservation of Lake Victoria haplochromines and rare parrot species as models (per Tudge, 1991).
- **Institutional context**: References the [[IUCN Freshwater Fish Specialist Group]] (chaired by Chris Andrews, 1990) as confirming the absence of a unified amateur conservation body — a gap the ACN was founded to fill.
- **Founding rationale of ACN**: Established in response to a series of 1990–1991 hobbyist magazine editorials (FAMA, TFH, AFM) highlighting freshwater habitat loss and the aquarium trade's potential conservation role.
