# Mineral Report: Introduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish into Selected Waters, Phoenix District

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontiformes

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Land Management (BLM) mineral report, dated June 25, 1984, assesses the potential for mineral development around three spring sites proposed for introducing the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]]. The [[Gila Topminnow]] is federally endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and the [[Desert Pupfish]] is State listed. The report was requested by Bill Kepner, Wildlife Biologist (Fisheries). Each site is evaluated separately because the sites are not close to one another. All three sites were rated as having low mineral potential, and the report concludes that the proposed introductions may be approved.

## Background & Description

The report covers federal surface and mineral estate. Access to the sites is limited to primitive roads and trails because of rugged terrain, which was one factor in site selection. The report states that it should not be used for purposes other than those for which it was intended.

The three sites are:

- **Grapevine Springs (Yerba Manza), Site 1:** T. 11 N., R. 11 W., Section 22 (NW¼ NW¼). A manmade spring several feet above the wash, approximately 0.18 acres (197' x 40'), at 1360' MSL. Its area of influence is about 275 acres, mostly south of the site.
- **Tres Alamos Falls, Site 2:** T. 10 N., R. 9 W., Section 13 (NE¼ NW¼). A natural spring of less than 0.75 acres, roughly a circle 205' in diameter, at 2420' MSL. Its area of influence is about 680 acres, mostly north of the site.
- **Peoples Canyon, Site 3:** T. 12 N., R. 10 W., Section 14 (SE¼ SW¼). A natural spring of less than 0.75 acres, roughly a circle 205' in diameter, at 2440' MSL. Its area of influence is about 805 acres, mostly northwest of the site.

"Area of influence" is defined as the area where surface-disturbing activities would likely be viewed critically for their effects on these fish species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geology**

- **Grapevine Springs:** Mafic volcanic rocks, sedimentary rocks (possibly the [[Chapin Wash Formation]]), and alluvium lie within the [[Date Creek Basin]]. The Chapin Wash Formation is middle Miocene in age and is the host rock for uranium at the [[Anderson Mine]], about five miles east.
- **Tres Alamos Falls:** Miocene andesites and rhyolites, with inferred northwest-trending faults.
- **Peoples Canyon:** Miocene intrusive and volcanic rocks of generally intermediate composition. Steeper terrain is probably volcanic. Topographic features suggest a northwest structural trend.

**Hydrologic setting:** The report describes the Grapevine Springs spring as a closed system above the drainage. Tres Alamos Falls and Peoples Canyon are also described as enclosed or closed systems, which limits how far surface disturbance could reach the spring water.

**Relief and drainage:** Grapevine Springs has about 170' of relief, with drainage running northwest to north. Tres Alamos Falls has about 380' of relief, with drainage to the south. Peoples Canyon has about 1260' of relief, with drainage to the southeast.

**Mining and land status**

- **Grapevine Springs:** No current activity was observed. The Spring Mine, apparently an old mill site, once occupied the site. A mining claim search dated May 2, 1984, found no claims in the area. The area is under oil and gas lease.
- **Tres Alamos Falls:** No activity has been observed recently or historically. No claims lie within the area of influence, but three association placer claims are less than one mile south in the Date Creek drainage, and a few lode claims are less than one mile west. The area is under oil and gas lease.
- **Peoples Canyon:** No activity has been observed. No claims lie within the area of influence, but several lode claims are less than one mile west. Most of the area is not under oil and gas lease.

## Key Findings

- **Mineral potential is low at all three sites.** Grapevine Springs has low potential for mineral development. Tres Alamos Falls has low potential for discovery of lithophile elements. Peoples Canyon has low potential for valuable minerals.
- **Future activity would have little effect on the springs.** Each spring is a closed or enclosed system, so surface activity in the area of influence would have limited impact on the fish habitat.
- **Mining activity in the areas of influence is expected to be low.** No production is known from any of the areas.
- **Recommendation:** The proposed use of all three sites for introduction of the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] may be approved.
- **Data limitations:** The report is a mineral-potential assessment. It does not include population, biological, or water-quality data for the fish or the springs.
