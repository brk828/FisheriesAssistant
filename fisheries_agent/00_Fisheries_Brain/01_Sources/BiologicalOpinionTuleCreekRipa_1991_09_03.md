# Biological Opinion: Tule Creek Riparian Exclosure Pipeline

## Executive Summary
This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Biological Opinion (September 3, 1991) evaluates a proposed pipeline and livestock watering trough construction project at [[Tule Creek]], a tributary of the [[Agua Fria River]] in Yavapai County, Arizona, under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The species of concern is the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), a small livebearing fish listed as endangered on March 11, 1967. The FWS concluded that the project is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the species, while authorizing limited incidental take during construction. The pipeline is designed to divert livestock from a fenced riparian exclosure protecting critical cienega habitat, resolving a conflict between livestock water needs and habitat protection following an earlier (February 1991) exclosure construction opinion.

## Background & Description
- **Common name:** Gila topminnow
- **Scientific name:** *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*
- **Family:** [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers)
- **Body size/form:** Small-bodied fish (characteristic of the genus *Poeciliopsis*)
- **Reproductive strategy:** Livebearing (ovoviviparous), noted as a **short-lived species with high fecundity**, contributing to rapid population fluctuations
- No detailed meristic or morphometric data provided in this document (regulatory/management focus rather than taxonomic description)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historic range:** [[Gila River]], [[Sonora River]], and de la Concepción River drainages across Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico
- **Historic abundance:** Once among the commonest fish species in the Gila River and tributaries (Hubbs and Miller 1941)
- **Current status:** Extirpated from most of historic range due to:
  - Water diversion
  - Stream downcutting/channel incision
  - Backwater draining
  - Riparian vegetation clearing
  - Channelization
  - Water impoundment
  - Competition/predation by nonnative fish, especially [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)
- **Habitat type:** Cienega (desert spring-marsh) deposits with perennial flow; associated riparian vegetation includes willow (*Salix* sp.), seepwillow (*Baccharis salicifolia*), salt cedar (*Tamarix chinensis*), mesquite (*Prosopis juliflora*), cottonwood (*Populus fremontii*), bulrush (*Scirpus* sp.), cattail (*Typha* sp.), and arrow-weed (*Pluchea sericea*)
- **Tule Creek population specifics:**
  - Stocked in 1968 from [[Monkey Springs]] stock via [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]] as part of recovery efforts
  - Extirpated by a severe **1978 flood** that incised the channel and removed cienega deposits
  - Restocked in 1981 (same source lineage)
  - Since 1981, considered **one of the most successful reintroduced populations** of Gila topminnow
  - Best habitat: ~0.5 mile perennial reach in T.8N., R.1E., Sections 28–29
- **Threats at project site:** Livestock and feral burro grazing degrading cienega hydrology and vegetation; hydrological disruption from exclosure fencing altering surface water distribution; sedimentation from pipeline trenching

## Key Findings
1. **Regulatory conclusion:** The proposed pipeline/trough project is **not likely to jeopardize** the Gila topminnow's continued existence.
2. **Habitat linkage:** The pipeline is a critical component enabling long-term effectiveness of the **riparian livestock exclosure**, since it allows watering of livestock outside protected habitat, preventing exclosure fence reopening.
3. **Hydrological feedback loop:** Removal of grazing altered cienega hydrology, causing water retention in the upper cienega and unexpected drying of downstream pools relied upon for livestock water — necessitating this pipeline solution.
4. **Short-term impacts:** Minor risks from trenching-related sedimentation, bank disturbance, and potential erosion/stream diversion if heavy rain occurs during construction; mitigated by scheduling work in September (outside monsoon season) and replacing vegetative mat post-trenching.
5. **Long-term benefit:** Supports sustained exclusion of livestock/feral burros from the cienega, reinforcing habitat recovery begun under the February 21, 1991 Biological Opinion.
6. **Incidental take statement:** Take cannot be numerically quantified due to sampling difficulty and the species' rapid population turnover (short lifespan, high fecundity). A threshold of **>10 dead fish of any species** observed in the project area triggers reinitiation of consultation.
7. **Reasonable and prudent measures:**
   - Minimize take of Gila topminnow and habitat during all actions
   - Maintain accurate records of actions that may result in take
8. **Terms and conditions:**
   - Minimize disturbance within wetted channel/cienega
   - Regular inspection/repair of pipeline, drinker, and float valve
   - Written report with maps and before/after photos due to FWS within two months of project completion
9. **Conservation recommendations:**
   - Prevent pollutant entry during construction
   - Retain the pipeline/trough permanently (even if natural pools recover) to provide a secure alternative water source and avoid future exclosure reopening
10. **Reinitiation triggers:** Standard ESA reinitiation clauses apply (take threshold exceeded, new information, project modification, or new listed species/critical habitat).
