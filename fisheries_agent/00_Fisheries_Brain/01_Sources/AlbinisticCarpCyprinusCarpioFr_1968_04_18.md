# Albinistic Carp, *Cyprinus carpio*, from Roosevelt Lake, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-biology #pigment-anomaly

## Executive Summary
This short paper documents the occurrence of partial albinism (amelanism) in common carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) collected from [[Roosevelt Lake]], Arizona, during a broader 1966 survey of central Arizona fishes conducted by Arizona State University. Of approximately 1,100 carp captured in the first year of study, three individuals (0.27%) exhibited a partially albinistic trait. The specimens were not true albinos, as they retained dark, wine-red eye pigmentation, but displayed a marked reduction in body pigmentation caused by a lack of pigment beneath the scales and operculum. The findings are compared to prior reports of pigment deficiency in carp and channel catfish populations elsewhere in North America.

## Background & Description
- **Specimens examined:**
  - **ASU 2697** — 275 mm SL male; body color milky/unpigmented except for dark red eyes; unpigmented operculum (resembled a wild-type operculum with pigment scraped off).
  - **ASU 2698** — 335 mm SL female; milky body color; two darkened patches on either side of the nape (4 scales on right side, 10 on left side); two small darkened areas near the vent; carrot-orange coloration at bases of paired and anal fins, extending onto the body beneath the paired fins (faded in preservative).
  - **ASU 2699** — 255 mm SL male; more silvery coloration than the other two; pigmented scales persisted along the left dorso-lateral area for the length of the dorsal fin; opercles darkened; carrot-orange fin base coloration as in ASU 2698.
- **Eye color:** All three specimens retained dark, wine-red eyes in life — confirming these were **not true albinos**, but exhibited **partial albinism/amelanism**.
- **Darkened/pigmented patches:** Interpreted as residual wild-type coloration persisting despite overall depigmentation.
- **Scale characteristics:** Albinistic individuals had scales that were:
  - Thinner
  - More delicate
  - More highly imbricate (overlapping)
  - Somewhat larger than wild-type carp scales
- **Meristic/morphological comparison:** No significant differences from wild-type carp in meristic counts or general morphology aside from pigmentation and scale texture.
- **Underlying cause of coloration:** Lack of pigment beneath the scales and operculum, consistent with findings by [[Hansen and Shoemaker]] (1943) for Illinois River carp and carpsuckers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Roosevelt Lake]] is an artificial impoundment created in 1911 at the confluence of the Salt and Tonto Rivers in Arizona. It is described as a "V"-shaped desert lake, approximately 23 miles long, with a surface area of 17,300 acres and maximum depth of 234 feet when full (as in 1966).
- **Community composition:** The dominant fish species in the lake are all exotic (non-native) introductions, including:
  - *Cyprinus carpio* (common carp)
  - *Micropterus salmoides* (largemouth bass)
  - *Lepomis cyanellus* (green sunfish)
  - *Lepomis microlophus* (redear sunfish)
  - *Lepomis macrochirus* (bluegill)
  - *Ictiobus cyprinellus* (bigmouth buffalo)
  - *Ictalurus punctatus* (channel catfish)
  - *Dorosoma petenense* (threadfin shad)
- **Spatial distribution of albinistic individuals:** The three albinistic carp were collected from **both ends of the western (Tonto) arm** of the lake on separate dates (7 June 1966, 10 October 1966, and 6 June 1967), indicating that the trait was **not confined to a single isolated population** but was distributed across the study area over time.

## Key Findings
- **Incidence rate:** 3 of ~1,100 carp (0.27%) captured during the first year of the survey exhibited partial albinism — a rate that falls between previously reported values for related pigment anomalies in other cyprinid/catfish populations.
- **Comparative incidence in literature:**
  - [[Hansen and Shoemaker]] (1943): 2–5% of carp in the Illinois River were depigmented (possibly albinistic).
  - [[Menzel]] (1959): Channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*) in Virginia's Pamunkey and Mattaponi Rivers showed 0.10% true albinism in 1940, declining to 0.001% by 1958.
  - [[Dawson]] (1964, 1966) compiled 80 references on fish anomalies, with only one prior reference specifically addressing albinism in carp — underscoring the rarity of documented cases in this species.
- **Nature of the anomaly:** The Roosevelt Lake carp were classified as **partial albinos** (not true albinos) due to retained ocular pigmentation (wine-red eyes), distinguishing this case from complete albinism.
- **Pigmentation mechanism:** Confirmed that depigmentation resulted from an absence of pigment beneath the scales and operculum, replicating the mechanism described for Illinois River carp by [[Hansen and Shoemaker]].
- **Taxonomic/morphological conclusion:** Aside from pigmentation and scale texture (thinner, more delicate, more imbricate, larger), the albinistic carp were morphologically and meristically indistinguishable from wild-type *Cyprinus carpio*, suggesting the trait is a localized pigmentary anomaly rather than indicative of broader phenotypic divergence.
- **Significance:** This represents one of very few documented records of albinism/partial albinism in *Cyprinus carpio* in scientific literature at the time of publication, contributing a rare data point to the study of [[Pigment Anomalies in Fishes]].
