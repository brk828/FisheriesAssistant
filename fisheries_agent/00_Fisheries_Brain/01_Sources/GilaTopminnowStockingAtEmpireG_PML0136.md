# Gila Topminnow Stocking at Empire Gulch

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum, dated April 13, 2006 and addressed to interested parties, reports a translocation of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from [[Cienega Creek]] to a spring at [[Empire Gulch]] in Arizona. Fish were collected on April 11, 2006 on the [[Bureau Of Land Management]]'s [[Las Cienegas National Conservation Area]] and stocked by students from [[Doolen Middle School]]. The team had aimed for at least 200 fish but collected only 52, and a follow-up augmentation trip was planned with the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] later that summer.

## Background & Description

The memo was written by Jeremy Voeltz, Native Fish Specialist, and concerns a single stocking event coordinated with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD). The document does not give morphological, life-history, or genetic details about the species. Its focus is operational: who collected the fish, where they came from, where they went, and what was planned next.

Personnel and partners named in the memo:
- Jeremy Voeltz, Native Fish Specialist (author)
- Bonnie Tighe, AGFD intern (co-collector)
- Students from Doolen Middle School (stocking participants)
- Bureau of Land Management (land manager at the source site)
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (partner in planned follow-up)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species is native to the desert streams and springs of the Southwest, and the document places it in two linked habitats: the cienega (wetland) system of Cienega Creek and the spring at Empire Gulch. The memo does not report water chemistry, habitat quality, population size at either site, or the presence of other aquatic organisms, so these aspects cannot be characterized from this source.

Conservation context is implied by the involvement of AGFD's native fish program, the federal land manager, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The document frames the release as augmentation (adding fish to an existing or restored population), not as the first introduction.

## Key Findings

- **Collection:** 52 Gila topminnows were collected from Cienega Creek on April 11, 2006 (the memo's date appears as "April Il" in the scan, read here as April 11).
- **Target not met:** The goal was at least 200 fish. The shortfall was attributed to short time frames and "uncooperative fish."
- **Release site:** All 52 fish were transported to Empire Gulch and stocked into the spring.
- **Public involvement:** Students from Doolen Middle School carried out the stocking, making this an educational as well as a conservation activity.
- **Next steps:** A follow-up augmentation trip was planned with the Bureau of Land Management and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service later in summer 2006 to make up the shortfall.
- **Data limitations:** The memo contains no monitoring results, survival data, or post-release assessment. The second page of the scan is largely illegible handwritten or OCR-damaged text and could not be reliably transcribed, so any content there is not reflected above.
