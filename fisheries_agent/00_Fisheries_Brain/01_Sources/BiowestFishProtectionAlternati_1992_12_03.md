## Executive Summary

This 1992 BIO/WEST memorandum (authored by Rich Valdez) presents the **Seasonally-Adjusted Steady/Fluctuating Flow Alternative**, a proposed dam operations strategy for **Glen Canyon Dam** developed for the Glen Canyon Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) process. The alternative was designed in response to unresolved threats identified across seven other flow alternatives to the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], as well as native [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[Speckled Dace]]. The proposal combines seasonally variable flow volumes, a **Multi-Level Intake Structure (MLIS)** for selective thermal releases, and **low-level sediment augmentation** to mimic a natural hydrograph — favoring native fish reproduction and recruitment while destabilizing habitat conditions favorable to non-native warmwater fishes (e.g., [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Fathead Minnow]]) and reducing salmonid predation pressure on native juveniles downstream of the Paria River.

## Background & Description

This document does not describe morphological traits directly but references key **life-stage size data** relevant to habitat use:

- Young **Humpback Chub** entering the mainstem from the Little Colorado River were observed at **50–75 mm** total length during July–September, based on GCES investigations (Maddux et al. 1986) and BIO/WEST field studies (Valdez et al. 1992).
- Juvenile native fishes (chub, suckers, dace) rely on shallow, warm **ephemeral backwaters** and **talus shorelines** during their first 1–2 months of life, transitioning to broader shoreline/tributary habitat use through their first year.

**Habitat use patterns:**
- Native species (chub, suckers) aggregate at **tributary inflows** for prespawning behavior during high, warming spring flows (April–June).
- **Mainstem spawning** by Humpback Chub and Razorback Sucker may become possible under stable 10,000 cfs summer flows at 15°C (16–18°C at the Little Colorado River confluence), potentially establishing a **second mainstem population** of Humpback Chub — addressing **Conservation Measure 7** of the Biological Opinion.
- Flannelmouth Sucker, Bluehead Sucker, and Speckled Dace require ≥18°C and likely continue tributary-dependent spawning.
- **Ephemeral backwaters** are critical nursery habitat, running 2–5°C warmer than the main channel; their seasonal permanence depends on flow stability.
- **Non-native warmwater fish** (carp, striped bass) may exploit warmed summer releases for spawning, but fluctuating cold fall/winter flows are designed to stress juveniles and displace adults from the mainstem.
- Elevated turbidity from sediment augmentation is linked to increased **near-surface activity in adult Humpback Chub** (Valdez et al. 1992), supporting the hypothesis that turbidity functions as evolved predator-avoidance cover.
- Warmed but capped release temperatures (≤15°C) are calibrated to support [[Rainbow Trout]] (13–21°C optimum) and [[Brown Trout]] (12–19°C optimum) growth near Lees Ferry while remaining unsuitable for non-native spawners like catfish and fathead minnow.

## Key Findings

- **Flow structure:** High stable spring flows (~20,000 cfs, April–June) → moderate stable summer flows (~10,000 cfs, July–October) → fluctuating fall/winter flows (5,000–20,000 cfs, ≤6 hrs/24 hr at minimum, November–March).
- **Ramping limits:** ≤2,500 cfs/hour increase; ≤1,500 cfs/hour decrease within any 24-hour period.
- **Temperature targets via MLIS:** Base hypolimnetic release ~8°C (Nov–Apr); warmed to 11°C in May, 15°C June–September, 12°C in October.
- **Sediment augmentation:** Added at the Paria River confluence, June–October, at low levels to enhance cover and turbidity-driven foraging without excessive habitat degradation.
- **Dual-purpose design:** The alternative simultaneously (1) promotes native fish spawning, growth, and recruitment via warm stable summer conditions and turbidity cover, and (2) suppresses non-native warmwater fish reproduction via cold fluctuating fall/winter flows.
- **Predator control side-effect:** Warm, turbid summer conditions may reduce downstream trout abundance below the Paria River, indirectly benefiting native fish by reducing sight-feeding predation pressure.
- **Policy linkage:** The proposal directly responds to jeopardy findings for Humpback Chub and Razorback Sucker under the Endangered Species Act framework and was intended to inform the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Impact Statement]] process regarding dam operations.
- **Data provenance:** Field data draw on GCES 1984–86 studies (Maddux et al. 1986) and BIO/WEST 1991 investigations (Valdez et al. 1992), particularly regarding juvenile Humpback Chub backwater use and adult turbidity association.
