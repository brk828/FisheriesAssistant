# Lake Mead Razorback Sucker Studies, 2000-2001 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This fifth-year annual report from the [[Lake Mead Razorback Sucker Study]] documents monitoring of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mead]], conducted July 2000 to June 2001 by BIO-WEST, Inc. for the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]], with cooperation from the [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[National Park Service]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Work focused on two known spawning areas, [[Echo Bay]] and [[Las Vegas Bay]], and on a possible new spawning area near the [[Colorado River Inflow]] and [[Grand Wash Bay]].

Trammel netting captured 20 razorback suckers during BIO-WEST sampling, 14 of them recaptures. USFWS spring netting in March-April 2001 captured 33 adults and juveniles. Larval catches at Echo Bay rose sharply, while Las Vegas Bay catches stayed near previous levels. Lake level fell about 16 feet during the spawning season, and the report links this decline to changing habitat conditions. The authors also propose that post-[[Lake Powell]] lake management, with reduced fluctuations, allowed shoreline vegetation to establish and aided recruitment.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endemic large-river fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically widespread but now endangered. Population declines are attributed mainly to [[Mainstem Dams]], which replaced warm riverine habitat with cold tailwaters and reservoirs, and to predation and competition from [[Non-Native Fishes]]. Lower basin reservoir populations are typically made up of adults that recruited during the first few years after impoundment, and they tend to decline 40 to 50 years later.

Lake Mead, formed in 1935 when [[Hoover Dam]] closed, followed this pattern. Razorback suckers were common in the 1950s and 1960s, declined in the 1970s, and were largely absent from agency collections from 1980 to 1989. Local reports in 1990 led to renewed sampling. From 1990 to 1996, NDOW collected 61 razorback suckers, 34 from Blackbird Point in Las Vegas Bay and 27 from Echo Bay. Stocked fish (26 in Las Vegas Bay in 1994, 14 in Echo Bay in 1995) were PIT-tagged and came from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

The study objectives were to (1) estimate population size, (2) describe habitat use and life history, and (3) document use of known spawning sites. Later objectives added a search for new spawning concentrations via larval light-trapping and enhanced juvenile sampling, under provision #10 of the USFWS Reasonable and Prudent Alternative for [[Lower Colorado River Operations]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake level.** Lake elevation fell from about 1,200 feet amsl in June 2000 to about 1,183 feet by June 2001, the lowest level of the study. At Echo Bay, water over the spawning area dropped from about 17 feet (March 1999 and 2000) to about 1 foot at its shallowest point by March 2001. Falling levels also extended the [[Las Vegas Wash]] delta into Las Vegas Bay, covered vegetation, and eliminated the flowing portion of the bay.

**Habitat and sedimentation.** Sediment from the wash filled much of the bulrush (*Scirpus* sp.) and tamarisk (*Tamarix chinensis*) area that was present at the start of the study in 1996. Turbidity from silt loads and green algae blooms (*Cladophora* sp.) limited underwater video and reduced larval sampling efficiency in Las Vegas Bay.

**Non-native fishes.** At Echo Bay, fyke netting caught 1,005 fish of 9 species. Red shiner was most abundant (50.9%), followed by threadfin shad (23.1%) and largemouth bass (12.6%). Predation by non-native species is identified as a major threat to recruitment.

**Sampling calendar.** Trammel netting was avoided from July to September because warm water stresses fish brought to the surface, and during the spawning season (March to May) except in the Grand Wash area.

## Key Findings

**Adult capture and recaptures**
- BIO-WEST trammel netting used 186 net nights: 49 in Las Vegas Bay/Boulder Basin, 39 in Echo Bay/Overton Arm, and 98 in the Grand Wash/Colorado River inflow area.
- Catch rate was 0.14 fish per net night at Las Vegas Bay and 0.33 at Echo Bay.
- Of 20 fish captured, 14 were recaptures (70% overall). Four of ten recaptures at Echo Bay were NDOW-stocked fish.
- USFWS netting in March-April 2001 captured 33 fish (18 Las Vegas Bay, 15 Echo Bay). Of these, 16 (48.5%) were new captures. Four Las Vegas Bay fish and one Echo Bay fish were NDOW-stocked.
- The lower recapture rate in BIO-WEST's pre-spawning samples suggests that spawning-season sampling captures more of the population.
- Cumulative totals after five years: 120 individual razorback suckers handled, 40 PIT-tagged at Echo Bay and 46 at Las Vegas Bay.

**Growth**
- Mean annual growth was 12.8 mm for the 20 recaptured fish in 2000-2001. Las Vegas Bay averaged 9.1 mm and Echo Bay 16.5 mm (the discussion reports 20.1 mm for Echo Bay).
- Smaller fish grew fastest. One 351 mm juvenile captured in 1998 grew to 570 mm by 2001, an annual rate of 75.2 mm.
- Fish recaptured within 60 days of a previous capture often showed negative growth, which the authors attribute to netting and handling stress and possibly spawning stress.
- Mean growth per year was higher for Las Vegas Bay females (18.1 mm) than males (13.6 mm). Echo Bay males grew 11.9 mm and females 13.6 mm per year.
- Compared with other populations, growth was much faster. Lake Mohave averages about 2.0 mm per year.

**Size and sex**
- Las Vegas Bay females averaged 605 mm and reached 735 mm. Echo Bay females averaged 645 mm and reached 730 mm. Echo Bay males averaged 570 mm.
- About 78% of males were 551-650 mm. No males exceeded 700 mm, while 24% of females did.
- The share of fish 601 mm or longer rose from 28.1% to 50.0% at Echo Bay, and from 53.2% to 65.1% at Las Vegas Bay, comparing 1996-1998 with 1999-2001.

**Larvae**
- Echo Bay produced 987 larvae in 2001, compared with 73 in 2000. The catch per minute (CPM) was 0.89 in 2001, compared with 0.09 in 2000.
- Las Vegas Bay produced 40 larvae in 2001, compared with 32 in 2000 (CPM 0.02 vs. 0.0).
- Larvae were first collected on March 11 at Las Vegas Bay and March 12 at Echo Bay. The last larva was collected April 25 at Echo Bay.
- 22 larvae were found in the Grand Wash Basin and Colorado River inflow area, from April 29 to May 15. Water temperatures there ranged from 64 to 74°F, compared with 57 to 71°F at Echo and Las Vegas Bays.

**Sonic telemetry**
- Fish 247 (Las Vegas Bay, 532 mm male, 1998) was located 28 times in 2001, always between Blackbird Point and the Las Vegas Wash inflow.
- Fish 79 (Echo Bay, 630 mm male, 1998) was located 14 times in 2001, mostly in Echo Bay and the western shore of Overton Arm.
- External tag 3434 (687 mm female) became stationary soon after release, so external attachment was judged inadequate and will be revised. Internal tagging will be used in future work.
- Sonic-tagged fish from the two bays did not overlap in distribution in any study year.

**Aging**
- Fin-ray sections were taken from eight fish, bringing the total aged to 20. Ages ranged from 6 to 18+ years.
- Fish spawned between 1978 and 1995. The greatest recruitment was 1985-1987.
- Fish aged at 5 to 8 years indicate recent recruitment in the 1990s, which the authors note is unusual among razorback sucker populations.
- Fin-clipping was judged not to reduce survival, since recapture rates for fin-clipped fish were high (68.4% in 2001 vs. 7.1% for non-clipped).

**Population estimates**
- Model Mo and Chao's Mh estimates for Echo Bay ranged from about 65 to 90 fish (1996-2001). Estimates for Las Vegas Bay ranged from about 55 to 130 fish.
- The 1999-2001 estimate was 55 fish at Las Vegas Bay (Model Mo, 95% CI 41-91) and 58 at Echo Bay (Model Mo, 95% CI 50-77).
- The authors consider the estimates likely low because BIO-WEST sampled only before and early in the spawning season.

**Hypothesis on recruitment**
- Recruitment is linked to lake management after [[Lake Powell]] filled. Lake Mead was drawn down about 100 feet in the mid-1960s and then operated with small annual fluctuations. This may have allowed shoreline vegetation to establish and then remain intact when inundated, providing cover for young fish.

**Recommendations for year six (2001-2002)**
1. Continue trammel netting, electrofishing, and fyke netting at Echo Bay and Las Vegas Bay, extending sampling into the spawning period.
2. Keep at least two sonic-tagged fish at each bay, and replace tags with dead batteries.
3. Monitor larval production at the two known spawning areas.
4. Search for a new spawning population in Grand Wash Basin and the Colorado River inflow via larval light-trapping and trammel netting. Release four to ten sonic-tagged fish there.
5. Collect fin-ray sections from all trammel-netted fish for aging, and collect fin rays and whole larvae for genetic analysis.
6. Sample the flowing portion of Las Vegas Bay and [[Las Vegas Wash]] for adults and larvae, and use weekly video to locate spawning.
