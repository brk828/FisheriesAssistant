# Occurrence of a South American Armored Catfish in the Hillsborough River, Florida

## Executive Summary
This note (Ludlow & Walsh, 1991) documents the first confirmed occurrence of the exotic South American armored catfish **[[Pterygoplichthys cf. multiradiatus]]** (Loricariidae) in the **[[Hillsborough River]]** proper, Florida — a range expansion from a previously established population in **[[Six-Mile Creek]]**, an independent Tampa Bay tributary first documented by Burgess (1958). The authors hypothesize that the completion of the **[[Tampa Bypass Canal]]** in December 1982 created a corridor allowing the fish to disperse from Six-Mile Creek into the Hillsborough drainage. Three voucher specimens (UF 79623), collected in May 1989 at least 10 river miles upstream of the presumed entry point, indicate rapid upstream dispersal. The species appears to be well established and abundant in **[[Hillsborough River State Park]]**, likely benefiting from anthropogenic eutrophication that has increased benthic algal growth — its primary food source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Trophic role:** Algivore/detritivore — feeds on algae, other plant material, and detritus (per Courtenay et al., 1974), consistent with the [[Loricariidae]] family's benthic grazing habit.
- **Habitat association:** Found in shallow river-bottom habitats; presence indicated indirectly by conspicuous worm-like fecal casts on the substrate.
- **Environmental facilitation:** Eutrophication of the Hillsborough River, driving heavy benthic algal growth, is suspected to have been beneficial to this species' establishment and abundance.
- **Invasion pathway:** Likely spread via a constructed waterway ([[Tampa Bypass Canal]]) connecting Six-Mile Creek to the Hillsborough River, rather than natural range expansion.
- **Population status at time of study:** Abundant and apparently well-established within [[Hillsborough River State Park]]; first ranger observations date to November 1986.
- **Broader introduction context:** Other congeneric/confamilial loricariids (identified variously as *Plecostomus* sp. and *Hypostomus* sp. in earlier literature) had previously been reported as exotic introductions in Nevada, Texas, and Hillsborough and Dade Counties, Florida (Courtenay & Hensley, 1979).

## Key Findings
1. First documented occurrence of an armored catfish (Loricariidae) within the Hillsborough River proper, extending known range from the adjacent Six-Mile Creek population.
2. Anthropogenic infrastructure (the Tampa Bypass Canal, completed 1982) is implicated as the likely dispersal corridor connecting previously isolated exotic catfish populations.
3. Specimens collected 10+ river miles upstream of the presumed point of entry demonstrate rapid dispersal capability of this species within a few years of access to the new drainage.
4. Species-level identification remains provisionally *Pterygoplichthys* cf. *multiradiatus*, reflecting persistent taxonomic uncertainty in hypostomine loricariids and unclear South American source populations.
5. The species' algivorous/detritivorous feeding habits, combined with river eutrophication promoting algal growth, may facilitate its establishment success.
6. Authors recommend future systematic surveys to (a) determine whether multiple loricariid species co-occur in the drainage, and (b) assess ecological/community impacts of this and other exotic fish introductions in the Hillsborough River system.
