# Gila elegans Stocking Records for Lake Mohave, Arizona-Nevada

#ichthyology #aquatic-ecology #gila-elegans

## Executive Summary

This 28 January 1997 letter from [[Paul C. Marsh]] of [[Arizona State University]] to [[Tom Burke]] at the [[U.S. Bureau Of Reclamation]] revises an earlier summary of [[Bonytail]] ([[Gila Elegans]]) stocking records for [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona and Nevada. The revision adds a November 1994 stocking that was previously unrecorded. In total, 202,622 bonytail were stocked on 27 dates. All fish were raised at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico. The letter is a records update, not a research study. It notes that the records may be incomplete and that new information will be added to a comprehensive Lake Mohave database maintained for native fish management, including [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] programs.

## Background & Description

The letter responds to a federal contract (order number 1425-96-PC-30-07870). It replaces records presented at the [[Native Fish Work Group]] meeting and in an earlier letter from late 1996. Marsh states that the summary was compiled from field notes, stocking records, memoranda, and reports from several agencies and institutions:

- [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]]
- [[Arizona State University]]
- [[U.S. Bureau Of Reclamation]]
- [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]]
- The National Biological Service, listed in the scan as "USGS Biological Services Division" (the OCR is partly garbled)

Marsh accepts responsibility for any errors or omissions.

Stocked fish fell into three life-stage categories:

- **Larvae:** 6–10 mm total length (TL)
- **Juveniles:** up to 150 mm TL
- **Adults:** greater than 150 mm TL, up to a maximum size that is illegible in the scan

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Stocking was not limited to the lake itself. Some fish were routed to other facilities and to backwaters adjacent to [[Lake Mohave]] before release. The letter states that all stocked adults were released into Lake Mohave backwaters from 1993 onward. This suggests a management shift toward adult releases in backwater habitats in the later years of the program.

Marking methods varied. The letter lists the following:

- [[Oxytetracycline Marking]]
- [[Fin Clip Marking]]
- [[Coded Wire Tags]]
- [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tags]] (PIT tags)
- [[Acoustic Transmitters]]

Marsh states that most stocked fish were not marked. Hatchery-reared fish that were released as adults carry PIT tags. Because of this, the marked-fish data cannot support a full estimate of survival or recapture.

## Key Findings

**Total stocking and life stages**

- A total of **202,622** bonytail were stocked into Lake Mohave, Arizona and Nevada on **27 dates**. The exact date range is partly illegible in the scan, but the record extends at least to November 1994.
- **Larvae:** The letter gives 14% of the total as larvae, stocked on one occasion. The exact count is partly illegible in the scan.
- **Juveniles:** The letter reports 125,414 fish stocked on **11 dates** (the scanned figure is partly garbled). The percentage is also illegible.
- **Adults:** **438** fish were stocked on **15 dates**, less than 1% of the total.

**Data quality**

- The November 1994 stocking was not in earlier records and was added in this revision.
- Other unrecorded stockings may exist. Marsh asks that any new information be sent to him so it can be added to the Lake Mohave database.
- Records are not complete for marking, and most fish were unmarked.

**Program context**

- The data support ongoing management of [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in Lake Mohave.
- The record notes that stocking at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] was the source for all fish.
