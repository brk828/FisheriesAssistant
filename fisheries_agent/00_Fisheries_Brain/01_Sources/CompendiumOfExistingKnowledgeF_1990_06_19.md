# Compendium of Existing Knowledge for Habitat Management Recommendations — Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #upper-colorado-river-basin #conservation-management

## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6) memorandum, dated June 19, 1990, introducing and circulating a **compendium of existing knowledge** compiled to support habitat management decisions for rare/endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The project began in 1989 with the goal of synthesizing "key" scientific literature describing the physical, chemical, and biological habitat requirements of these species. The memo itself contains **no primary morphological, taxonomic, or ecological data**; rather, it describes the purpose, methodology, and intended use of the larger compendium document to which it is attached. It was circulated to management, technical, and recovery team stakeholders for review and comment, with a deadline of November 30, 1990.

## Background & Description

No morphological data are presented in this source. The document is a cover memorandum for a broader compendium and does not include species-specific anatomical, meristic, or physical descriptions. Presumably, the full compendium (not included here) would address species such as those historically associated with [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] recovery programs (e.g., [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]]), though none are explicitly named in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While specific ecological data are not detailed in this memo, the document establishes the **conceptual framework** for habitat assessment used in the compendium:

- **Rare fish species** in the Upper Colorado River Basin are described as having specific **physical, chemical, and biological habitat needs** that are "apparently not being met in certain areas" — indicating recognized habitat degradation or limitation.
- The compendium organizes information by **categories of habitat variables** considered important to riverine fishes (e.g., presumably flow, temperature, substrate, water chemistry, though not itemized in this excerpt).
- For each variable, the intent was to define **ranges of values** that are beneficial versus detrimental to the species, enabling habitat suitability assessments.
- The approach acknowledges that some **important variables and interactions among variables** may have been overlooked, reflecting the acknowledged incompleteness of ecological understanding at the time.

This reflects a broader systems-based approach to [[Riverine Habitat Management]] and endangered species recovery planning, situating individual species' needs within the context of the basin's altered hydrology and habitat conditions (implied but not detailed).

## Key Findings

- The compendium project was initiated in **1989** by the USFWS to compile "key" information on rare fish species' habitat needs relative to past, present, and future environmental conditions in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].
- "Key" information was operationally defined as data translatable into **recovery actions** or usable to **assess impacts** of existing/proposed actions on the fishes.
- The stated goal was to enable users to:
  1. Assess the relative suitability of habitat at a given locale.
  2. Assess or predict impacts of proposed projects.
  3. Identify research needs where information gaps exist.
- The compendium's methodology emphasized restating **facts, relationships, hypotheses, theories, and opinions** of cited researchers as originally stated, rather than the compilers' own interpretations — intended to reveal areas of **scientific consensus vs. areas needing further study**.
- Authors acknowledge the compendium was **already out of date** at the time of circulation (1990) due to rapidly accumulating new research, and explicitly invited additions of overlooked pre-1989 literature.
- The document was distributed to multiple stakeholder groups (Management Group, Technical Group, Biology/Rag Group, Recovery Team, and named individual consultants: Tom Chart, Lee Carlson, Ned Andrews, Jim O'Brien, Wayne Hubert), reflecting a **multi-stakeholder, iterative review process** for endangered species habitat management guidance.
- A comment deadline of **November 30, 1990** was set for stakeholder feedback on format, content, accuracy, and utility of the compendium.
