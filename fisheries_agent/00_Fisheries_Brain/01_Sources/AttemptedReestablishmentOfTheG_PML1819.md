# Attempted Re-establishment of the Gila Topminnow Within Its Former Range

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This short note by [[Paul C. Marsh]] (Copeia, 1969, No. 1) reports attempts to re-establish the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) in semi-isolated habitats within its former range in the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona. The species, once one of the most common fishes in the southern [[Colorado River Drainage]], survived in only one small stream system by 1968. Since 1964, stocks derived from a single source population were introduced at multiple localities in Maricopa, Gila, Yavapai, Pinal, and Graham counties. Outcomes were mixed: several introductions failed through flooding, pesticide exposure, or predation by [[Gambusia Affinis]] (mosquitofish), while others persisted or reproduced. The author asks for information on suitable isolated waters and recent collections.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The Gila Topminnow, *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* (Baird and Girard), a small live-bearing poeciliid fish.
- **Historical status:** Described in earlier work by [[Hubbs and Miller]] (1941) as one of the commonest fishes of the southern Colorado River system.
- **Current status:** Extirpated from all but one small stream system by 1968, according to [[Minckley and Deacon]] (1968).
- **Source stock:** All introduced fish came from individuals seined about seven miles northeast of Patagonia, Santa Cruz County, Arizona, from a pond fed by [[Monkey Spring]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The note identifies several threats and habitat conditions affecting re-establishment:

- **Flooding:** Scouring floods in 1965–66 and 1966–67 eliminated or threatened several populations, notably in the [[Salt River]] bed, [[Cave Creek]], [[Camp Creek]], and [[Fish Creek]].
- **Pesticide exposure:** A population at [[Papago Park]] Golf Course was possibly eliminated by pesticide blowover (question mark in original).
- **Competition and predation:** Introduced [[Gambusia Affinis]] eliminated topminnow populations at Papago Park (1966) and in the Salt River pools (autumn 1966).
- **Habitat type:** Successful sites included springs, marshes adjacent to channels, and small streams, such as the [[Aravaipa Creek]] marsh and the [[East Verde River]] headspring.

The author frames re-establishment as a way to create re-dispersal centers and as insurance against extinction of the species. Research on native Gila basin fishes was supported by [[Arizona State University]], the [[Sport Fishing Institute]], and [[The Nature Conservancy]].

## Key Findings

Introductions were made at roughly ten localities. Status as reported in the note:

| Locality | Result (as reported) |
|---|---|
| Papago Golf Course and Papago Park, Maricopa Co. | Eliminated by pesticide blowover(?) in 1965 and by mosquitofish in 1966 |
| Salt River bed (Tempe), Maricopa Co. | Reproducing in 1966; eliminated by mosquitofish in autumn 1966 |
| Cave Creek, Maricopa Co. | Probably eliminated by flooding, 1965–66 |
| Camp Creek, Maricopa Co. | Established 1966; probably eliminated by flooding, 1966–67 |
| Fish Creek, Maricopa Co. | Stock eliminated immediately by flooding, 1965 |
| East Verde River headspring, Gila Co. | Reproducing in 1965; not checked subsequently |
| Fossil Creek, Gila and Yavapai Cos. | Reproducing in 1968; population very small and localized |
| Aravaipa Creek marsh, Pinal Co. | Established 1968; population large, with a few individuals in the channel |
| Aravaipa Creek, Graham Co. | Reproducing (established?) in 1968 |

Key conclusions:

- Populations were established at several sites, but flooding and introduced mosquitofish were the main causes of failure.
- The [[Aravaipa Creek]] marsh population was the largest reported success.
- Established populations may serve as re-dispersal sources or as insurance against extinction.
- The author requests information on isolated waters suitable for introduction and on recent collections of the species.
