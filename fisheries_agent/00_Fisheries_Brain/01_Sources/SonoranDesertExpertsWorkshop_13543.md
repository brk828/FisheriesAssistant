# Sonoran Desert Ecoregion Experts Workshop

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fish #conservation-planning #ecoregional-assessment

## Executive Summary

This document presents the agenda, background materials, and participant information for the [[Sonoran Desert]] Ecoregion Experts Workshop held May 20-21, 1998, at the University of Arizona Student Union. Co-sponsored by [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[IMADES]] (Instituto del Medio Ambiente y el Desarrollo Sustentable del Estado de Sonora), and the [[Sonoran Institute]], the workshop aimed to identify priority conservation sites and develop a comprehensive ecoregional plan for the [[Sonoran Desert]]. The effort was funded by the Department of Defense Legacy Program and represented a binational collaboration covering the ecoregion across Arizona, California, Sonora, and Baja California. Expert panels were organized by taxonomic groups including fish and aquatic invertebrates, birds, mammals, herpetofauna, terrestrial invertebrates, rare plants, and communities/landscapes.

## Background & Description

The [[Sonoran Desert]] ecoregion extends from uplands to the littoral zone (mean high tide elevation), with boundaries adapted jointly by TNC, [[CONABIO]] (Mexico's National Biodiversity Institute), and the [[World Wildlife Fund]]. The boundary excludes some areas of Baja California Norte and Baja California Sur typically considered part of the Sonoran Desert, which would be addressed in separate planning efforts. The ecoregional planning approach combines three information sources: (1) GAP products using remote sensing of vegetation patterns, (2) species and natural community occurrence data from state heritage/natural diversity programs, and (3) expert knowledge of landscape, species, and natural communities.

Conservation targets included federally threatened and endangered species, globally rare species (ranked G1-G3 and T1-T3), natural community types at the Series level classification from Brown, Lowe, and Pase (1979), species of special concern, endemics, area-sensitive species (e.g., [[Sonoran Pronghorn]], [[Desert Bighorn Sheep]]), species with disjunct distributions, and special biological features (migratory corridors, bat roosts, rookeries, tinajas, snake dens). The preliminary target list contained over 500 species and natural communities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The workshop addressed biodiversity across multiple habitat types including [[Sonoran Savanna Grassland]], [[Sonoran Desertscrub]] (creosotebush-bursage series), [[Southwest Riparian Deciduous Forest]] and woodlands (cottonwood-willow series), [[Sonoran Interior Marshland]], and [[Sonoran Maritime Strand]]. Aquatic systems included both riverine and spring habitats supporting endemic fish species. The ecoregion faces multiple threats as outlined in Nabhan and Holdsworth (1998), "State of the Desert Biome: Uniqueness, Biodiversity, Threats, and the Adequacy of Protection in the Sonoran Bioregion."

The fish and aquatic invertebrates panel included experts Jorge Alaniz, Dave Gori, [[Dean Hendrickson]], Gene Maughn, [[Wendell Minckley]], Gorgonio Ruiz, Jerry Stefferud, Sally Stefferud, Carlos Valdes, and Carlos Yruretagoyena. This group was tasked with compiling maps of sites designed to conserve aquatic targets.

## Key Findings

**Conservation Targets - Fish and Aquatic Invertebrates:**

The draft target list identified multiple federally listed and candidate fish species:

- [[Cyprinodon macularius]] (Desert Pupfish) - G1, Endangered, with subspecies including [[Cyprinodon macularius eremus]] (Quitobaquito Desert Pupfish) and [[Cyprinodon macularius macularius]]
- [[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail) - G1, S1, Endangered
- [[Xyrauchen texanus]] (Razorback Sucker) - G1, Endangered
- [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado Squawfish) - G1, Endangered
- [[Meda fulgida]] (Spikedace) - Threatened
- [[Tiaroga cobitis]] (Loach Minnow) - Threatened
- [[Gila intermedia]] (Gila Chub) - Candidate

Aquatic invertebrate targets included:
- [[Tryonia gilae]] (Gila Tryonia) - G1, S1, Candidate
- [[Tryonia quitobaquitae]] (Quitobaquito Tryonia) - G1, S1, Candidate
- [[Pyrgulopsis arizonae]] (Bylas Springsnail) - G2, S2, Candidate
- [[Anodonta californiensis]] (California Floater)

**Workshop Structure:**

Day 1 focused on concurrent expert panel sessions for identifying conservation sites by taxonomic group. Day 2 included regional breakout groups for stress/threat assessment, data gap identification, and conservation goal development. Each taxonomic group had facilitators, translators, and recorders to document site nominations with supporting information (target species, location, ownership, threats, management challenges).

**Next Steps:**

The workshop would produce annotated maps synthesizing biological and ecological information, illustrating the network of sites of greatest importance for maintaining biodiversity. A protected areas assessment would determine correspondence between existing protection efforts and identified priority sites. The effort aimed to result in increased management and protection, filling of critical data gaps, widespread environmental awareness, strengthened institutional capacity, and innovative policy reforms.
