# Pike (Esox lucius L.) on the edge: consistent individual movement patterns in transitional waters of the western Baltic

#ichthyology #Esocidae #northern_pike #brackish_water_ecology

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius* L.) in [[Stege Lagoon]], western [[Baltic Sea]], inhabit brackish waters at 8–10 ppt salinity, near the upper physiological tolerance limit for the species. Twenty-two adult pike were studied (14 females: mean TL 87.0 cm ± 9.7 SD, mean weight 5.1 kg ± 1.7 SD; 8 males: mean TL 58.8 cm ± 5.9 SD, mean weight 1.5 kg ± 0.4 SD). Spawning occurred in late April to early May in both 2003 and 2004, with all pike spawning within the brackish lagoon despite salinity levels. On 23–24 April, 1/3 of captured pike were spent; by 5–6 May, all were spent. Previous experimental work showed successful fertilization and egg development at 8.5 ppt, with pike fry exhibiting normal behavior up to 13.5 ppt. Mass mortality has been reported at salinities exceeding 18 ppt. The lagoon experiences salinity fluctuations, with occasional extreme inflows reaching 17–20 ppt (January 2003) and 12–16 ppt (January 2005), suggesting pike live on the edge of physiological tolerance.

**Habitat Associations**: [[Stege Lagoon]] covers 5.69 km² with mean depth 1.3 m (maximum 3.7 m). The lagoon connects to [[Stege Bay]] through a narrow outlet (~60 m width) and receives freshwater input from [[Lendemark Brook]] in the northeastern part. Salinity normally ranges 8–10 ppt but varies with wind-driven water exchange; horizontal mixing is good, with maximum salinity differences of 0–1.5 ppt between harbour and remote receivers. Habitat includes shallow vegetated areas with bladder weed (*Fucus vesiculosus*), *Potamogeton pectinatus*, and reed belts (*Phragmites australis*). The fish community includes [[Perch]] (*Perca fluviatilis*), [[Roach]] (*Rutilus rutilus*), [[European Eel]] (*Anguilla anguilla*), [[Black Goby]] (*Gobius niger*), [[Three-spined Stickleback]] (*Gasterosteus aculeatus*), and [[Sea Stickleback]] (*Spinachia spinachia*).

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Two distinct, individually consistent movement patterns were observed across two consecutive years. Approximately two-thirds of tagged pike (14 of 22; 10 females, 4 males) left the lagoon for extended periods after spawning, while one-third (7 fish: 3 females, 4 males) remained resident year-round. Pike leaving the lagoon departed between late April and mid-May (females) or late May to early August (males) in 2003. Of the 14 that left, 10 returned between September–November 2003 or February–March 2004, spending 57–335 days outside the lagoon during one year. Maximum single excursion duration ranged 42–254 days; minimum duration 1–60 days. Pike made 2–5 excursions annually. By April 2004, 16 fish (73%) were recorded in the lagoon. Nine fish (6 females, 3 males) again left the lagoon April–June 2004, demonstrating individual behavioral consistency. Movements out of the lagoon occurred primarily during dawn and dusk; returns showed no diel pattern. Within the lagoon, resident pike averaged 420 registration days per year (± 34.2 SD), with individuals recorded on more than one receiver per day (mean 1.2), indicating extensive movement throughout the lagoon. All but one pike were registered in all four lagoon areas during the annual cycle.

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: Not reported in detail. The study notes no obvious predation risk from other fish except cannibalism from larger conspecifics. Potential predation from [[Harbour Seal]] (*Phoca vitulina*) was mentioned but not documented. High pike density in the lagoon could impose predation risk on smaller individuals.

**Environmental Drivers**: Salinity tolerance appears to be the primary environmental constraint. Pike in this population exhibit higher salinity tolerance (larvae tolerate up to 13.5 ppt) compared to freshwater populations, suggesting local adaptation. Water temperature was monitored but specific temperature effects on movement were not reported. The lagoon may provide more stable salinity during spawning compared to exposed coastal locations. Movement out of the lagoon after spawning may reflect exploitation of alternative food resources following [[Ideal Free Distribution]] principles, with autumn returns corresponding to decreasing temperatures and lower food demands.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: Commercial catches of pike have declined considerably throughout the [[Baltic Sea]] including Danish straits since the 1970s–1980s, suggesting drastic population decreases. Reasons for declines are not well determined but may include poor recruitment, habitat deterioration, or eutrophication.

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: Pike in [[Stege Lagoon]] live at the edge of their salinity tolerance, experiencing periodic severe salt stress or potential local population crashes during high-salinity inflow events (e.g., 17–20 ppt recorded in harbour area). The population is vulnerable to exploitation at lagoon mouths during movement periods. Despite historical stocking with 550,000 fry from genetically differentiated non-native freshwater strains (1993–2006), genetic analyses using 12 microsatellite markers showed all tagged fish belonged to the same reproductive population unit as the native Stege Lagoon population (all membership probabilities >0.9). No genetic differentiation was detected between resident pike and those leaving the lagoon, indicating a single panmictic population. This suggests the population is self-sustaining with successful recruitment occurring at regular levels.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: Protected brackish areas like [[Stege Lagoon]] may be more important for pike recruitment than previously recognized. Successful recruitment hinges on unhindered access to lagoons and protected areas. Intense fisheries exploitation at lagoon mouths, particularly during movement periods, renders populations vulnerable. Temporal and spatial fisheries regulations are recommended as valuable measures to protect and increase these populations. Management should recognize that besides freshwater inlets, brackish spawning grounds are critical habitat. Increasing socioeconomic potential from pike angling demands accomplished management and identification of appropriate management units.

**Biological Reference Points**: Spawning occurs late April to early May (consistent across years). Successful egg development occurs at ≤8.5 ppt salinity. Pike larvae exhibit normal development and behavior at salinities up to 13.5 ppt. Mass mortality occurs at salinities >18 ppt. Post-spawning emigration occurs late April through August. Return migration to spawning grounds occurs September–March. No significant effect of condition factor (Fulton's K) or total length on probability of leaving the lagoon was detected (P = 0.34 for both). However, among pike that moved out and returned, a significant positive relationship existed between total length and total days outside the lagoon (N = 10; R² = 0.41, P < 0.05), with larger individuals staying longer outside.
