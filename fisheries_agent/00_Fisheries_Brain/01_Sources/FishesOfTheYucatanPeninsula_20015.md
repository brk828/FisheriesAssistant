# Fishes of the Yucatan Peninsula

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae #ariidae #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This is an excerpt (pages 1–9, partly garbled by OCR) from Chapter XVII of *The Cenotes of Yucatan*, a Carnegie Institution of Washington publication by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] of the [[Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan]]. The chapter reports fish collections from the peninsula, mainly from a 1932 expedition led by Dr. A. S. Pearse of Duke University, alongside older [[Gaumer and Cole Collections]] (1906) re-examined at the U.S. National Museum and the Museum of Comparative Zoology. The author proposes a large number of new taxa and gives a detailed taxonomic description of [[Astyanax Fasciatus Altior]], a deep-bodied characin from Yucatan. The excerpt also includes a nomenclatural discussion of the catfish genus *Bagre* and the synonymy of *Silurus felis*.

## Background & Description

**Scope and sources**

- Core material: the 1932 expedition of the [[Carnegie Institution of Washington]], [[Duke University]] and the Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan.
- Older material: [[Gaumer and Cole Collections]] (1906), re-examined.
- Imported aquarium fishes were also studied, including the only known specimens of *Pseudoxiphophorus bimaculatus peninsulae*.
- A five-species collection by C. L. Lundell from an aguada at Tuxpeña, Campeche, is reported for completeness.
- Comparative work covers Middle American relatives, including the [[Cichlasoma Urophthalmus]] complex, and a new form of [[Gambusia Nicaraguensis]] from southern Mexico.

**Taxonomic framework**

- Fishes are arranged by family (e.g., Clupeidae, Engraulidae, Characidae, Ariidae, Pimelodidae, Belonidae, Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae, Cichlidae, Gobiidae, Soleidae).
- The author uses meristic and morphometric characters (fin-ray counts, scale counts, gill rakers, tooth counts, body proportions) as the basis for distinguishing subspecies.
- Colour in preservative and in fresh specimens is recorded, since it aids identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cenotes and aguadas:** The fishes come from sinkhole pools (cenotes), aguadas and caves. The [[Chapultepec Cenote]] in Merida was artificial and reportedly stocked from cienagas (marshes). Its characins were seen but not captured.
- **Cave populations:** Several *Rhamdia* forms come from caves: *R. guatemalensis stygea* from San Isidro Cave near Merida, and *R. g. decolor* from San Bulha Cave, Motul. Their names and descriptions point to cave adaptation, though the excerpt does not discuss this in detail.
- **Coastal and estuarine waters:** Shore and bay fishes were collected, including *Bagre marinus* from the Rio Champoton about 18 km from its mouth.
- **Biogeographic position:** The Yucatan fauna is compared with that of adjacent Middle America (northern Guatemala and southern Mexico). The Yucatan inshore and freshwater faunas are treated as distinct sets with different levels of novelty.
- **Locality data:** Stations are numbered and described, e.g. Sta. 22 (Chapultepec Cenote, Merida), Sta. 28 (Xlaka Cenote), Sta. 33 (roadside pool 3 km south of Progreso), Sta. 42 (Rio Champoton) and Sta. 45 (aguada at Tuxpeña).

## Key Findings

**1. High rate of novelty in the collections**

- Shore and estuarine fishes: 27 genera, 29 species and 4 subspecies recognised. Of these, 1 genus, 3 species and 3 subspecies are new. Novelty: 4% of genera, 10% of species, 75% of subspecies.
- Freshwater fishes: 11 genera, 18 species and 19 subspecies recognised. Of these, 1 genus, 4 species and 12 subspecies are described for the first time. Novelty: 9% of genera, 22% of species, 63% of subspecies.

**2. New names proposed**

- Total: 2 new genera, 8 new species and 18 new subspecies.
- New genera: *Garmanella* and *Allophallus*.
- New species include *Fundulus grandissimus*, *Rivulus myersi*, *Garmanella pulchra*, *Allophallus kidderi*, *Menidia colei*, *Cichlasoma heterospilum*, *Cichlasoma champotonis* and *Herichthys pearsei*.
- New subspecies include 8 of [[Cichlasoma Urophthalmus]] (e.g., *stenozonum*, *alborum*, *aguada*, *cienega*, *amarum*, *conchita*, *zebra*, *mayorum*), and others in [[Astyanax Fasciatus]], [[Rhamdia Guatemalensis]], [[Floridichthys Carpio]], *Cyprinodon variegatus artifrons*, *Gambusia nicaraguensis sexradiatus*, *Belonesox belizanus maxillosus*, *Mollienisia sphenops altissima* and others.

**3. *Astyanax fasciatus altior* is a distinct Yucatan subspecies**

- Holotype: 53 mm standard length, pool 3 km south of Progreso (UMMZ 102144). Paratypes: one adult (44 mm) and two young (15 and 17.5 mm) (UMMZ 102145).
- The body is extremely deep: depth enters standard length 2.2 times. No specimens of *eneus* or *mexicanus* exceeded two-fifths of standard length.
- Anal fin rays average fewer than in *eneus* (Tuxpeña mean 24.07) and are intermediate between *eneus* and *mexicanus*. Altior means: 22.75 (Progreso pool) and 23.00 (Xlaka Cenote).
- Other counts: dorsal 9, anal 22 (24), caudal 19, pelvic 7–8, pectoral 13–15, 35 lateral-line scales, gill rakers 9+13.
- The Rio Champoton series, identified as *eneus*, shares the low anal-ray count, which the author notes approaches *altior*.
- Anal-ray count is described as the most valuable single character for separating these forms.

**4. Cave *Rhamdia* forms**

- *R. guatemalensis stygea*: holotype 109 mm SL, San Isidro Cave, near Merida.
- *R. g. decolor*: holotype 139 mm SL, San Bulha Cave, Motul.

**5. Nomenclature of *Bagre* and *Silurus felis***

- The author argues that the generic name *Bagre* Oken should be used for the gaff-topsail catfishes, following Jordan (1917) and Article 30 of the International Rules of Zoological Nomenclature. On this view *Bagre* takes precedence over *Felichthys* and *Ailurichthys*.
- *Bagre marinus* material from the Rio Champoton: two adults, about 350 and 365 mm total length.
- The author rejects Günther's identification of *Silurus felis* Linnaeus, because Linnaeus explicitly described six barbels (four on the chin). Misidentification or label transposition is judged more likely.

**6. Colour and variation**

- Fresh *altior*-like Rio Champoton specimens had olive bodies, cream cheeks and orange-blotched caudal lobes separated by lemon-yellow from a median black band.
- Tuxpeña specimens showed lemon-yellow caudal, anal and pelvic fins, becoming bright red toward the front rays.
