# Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #habitat-restoration #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This document is a memorandum and program proposal from the Recovery Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin, dated September 23, 1994. It announces a meeting scheduled for November 8-10, 1994, to discuss goals, strategies, and issues of the [[Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program]]. The program's short-term goal is to restore flood plain habitats to assist recovery of the [[Green River]] [[Razorback Sucker]] population and reestablish one self-sustaining population in the [[Colorado River]] subbasin. The long-term goal is to restore flood plain habitats for all endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin. The document includes detailed FY 1995 scope-of-work proposals covering site selection, screening, design, restoration, and evaluation, with a total budget of $6.6M over three years.

## Background & Description

The [[Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program]] operates under the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The program addresses multiple endangered species including [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). The program is coordinated by Pat Nelson (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) and involves multiple federal and state agencies including U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Colorado Division of Wildlife, Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, Wyoming State Engineer's Office, and The Nature Conservancy.

The program operates on several key assumptions: (1) restoration of bottom land habitats for razorbacks may benefit other native fish species; (2) razorback suckers and possibly bonytail require flooded bottom land habitats to complete their life cycle; (3) lack of suitable habitat is a limiting factor contributing to razorback decline; and (4) razorback sucker and bonytail are highest priority among endangered fishes based on status and trends.

The study area encompasses the Upper Colorado River Basin excluding the San Juan River, with focus on the [[Green River]] (river mile 0-398), [[Colorado River]] (river mile 0-241), and [[Gunnison River]] (river mile 0-75). A pilot study site at [[Old Charley Wash]] (Wood's Bottom) on the Green River has been prepared with water inlet/outlet control structures and screens installed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Historical flood plain dynamics in the Upper Colorado River Basin have been severely altered by water management, reducing flood frequency and duration. Many flood plain habitats are in low-lying areas where municipal, industrial, and agricultural runoff collects, and high spring flows that historically provided annual flushing have been reduced. The program defines restoration as "reconnecting flood plain habitats to the main channel of the river in such a way that flooding occurs at near-historical frequency, timing, and duration."

The program recognizes that production in flooded bottom lands is orders of magnitude greater than in the main river channel. As bottom lands are reconnected, they will become seasonal sources of allochthonous materials, nutrients, and food for aquatic biota, thereby influencing production. However, whether these increases in system productivity will benefit native fishes more or less than nonnative fishes remains unknown.

Key ecological concerns include predation by larger fish, birds, mammals, and invertebrates on larval razorbacks. The program plans to screen most sites to prevent access by larger fish predators. Other factors affecting survival include winter kill, summer kill, food supply, and contaminants (particularly selenium, which has been identified as a potential problem at many sites).

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Phases:**
The program follows a sequential approach: (1) Site selection from 57 inventoried bottom land sites (25 for Green River, 32 for Colorado/Gunnison); (2) Screening for contaminants, hydrology, geomorphology, and biology; (3) Design and restoration; (4) Evaluation of results.

**Site Selection Criteria:**
For [[Green River]] sites, ranking criteria include distance downstream from razorback spawning area (RM 311.5), floodability during June 1993, whether site is part of a network versus isolated, and property ownership (public versus private). For [[Colorado River]] subbasin sites, criteria include proximity to recent razorback captures (since 1974), floodability, network connectivity, and ownership.

**Initial Priority Sites:**
Green River sites include Escalante Ranch (RM 304-306), Escalante to Jensen (RM 302-303), Little Stewart Lake (RM 296-297), Baeser Bend (RM 271-273), Brennan Bottom (RM 262-265), Leota Bottom (RM 254-261), Sheppard Bottom (RM 252-254), [[Old Charley Wash]] (RM 250-252, being evaluated), and others. Colorado River sites include Battlement Mesa locations, Pike's, Clifton Sanitation, Griffith's, Gravel Pit at 29 5/8 Road, Golf Course West, and [[Moab Slough]] (RM 63). Gunnison River sites include Confluence Park, Johnson Boy's Slough, and Escalante State Wildlife Area.

**Restoration Strategy:**
In the [[Green River]], razorback larvae will be allowed to drift into sites naturally, with captured wild larvae also placed into sites. In the [[Colorado River]] or [[Gunnison River]], hatchery-produced razorback larvae must be placed in restored sites since wild larvae are few or nonexistent. Some sites will be restored to allow access by adult razorback suckers for pre- and post-spawning conditioning.

**Program Components and Budgets:**
The FY 1995 budget totals $1.6M, distributed as follows: General Coordination ($78K), Site Coordination ($50K), Technical Advisors ($25K), Contaminants ($150K), Hydrology ($100K), Geomorphology ($100K), Biology ($200K including screening and predator-control experiments), Environmental Compliance ($50K), Property Acquisition Activities ($50K), Design/Acquisition/Restoration ($650K), Evaluation ($127K), and Miscellaneous ($20K). Total three-year budget (FY 1995-1997) is $6.6M.

**Contaminants Assessment:**
Level I and Level II surveys will be conducted for each site using standard protocols. Research will determine contaminant levels that adversely affect razorback suckers. Selenium has been identified as a potential problem at many sites. FY 1994 contaminants budget is $100K (including $50K from National Biological Service), with FY 1995 budget at $150K.

**Hydrologic Restoration:**
Goals include restoring historical frequency, duration, timing, and extent of flooding. Stage-discharge relationships will be developed for each site. USGS flow data will be analyzed to compare recent with historical flood flow parameters. Sites requiring minimal excavation or flow augmentation will be considered more suitable. Some sites will flood and drain naturally; others will have inlet/outlet control structures.

**Geomorphologic Considerations:**
Sites will be evaluated to ensure main channel stability is not adversely affected and that site topography will endure over time and during flood events. Sites requiring less excavation, construction, and stabilization, expected to last with low maintenance, and not expected to adversely affect channel stability will be considered more suitable. Topographic contour maps will be developed as baselines to detect changes over time.

**Biological Screening:**
Reconnaissance-level biological characterization will determine relative suitability of sites for razorback suckers. Work includes: (1) fish species composition and relative abundance surveys; (2) plankton sampling to develop food abundance indices; (3) water quality monitoring (temperature, conductivity, pH, dissolved oxygen); (4) identification of potential avian, mammalian, and invertebrate predators; (5) assessment of vegetative cover for predator avoidance. FY 1994-95 biology screening budget is $60K ($30K per subbasin).

**Governance and Review:**
The program includes a Work Group of component experts and Recovery Program participant representatives, and a Technical Advisory Group (peer review panel) including Ned Andrews (USGS), Peter Bayley (Illinois Natural History Survey), Larry Hesse (Great Plains River Research), and Wendell Minckley (Arizona State University).

**Meeting Agenda:**
The November 8-10, 1994 meeting will address program short-term and long-term goals, habitat management approaches, site selection criteria, site evaluation approaches, Lower Green River sites, and other issues. Site visits are scheduled for November 9 and 10.
