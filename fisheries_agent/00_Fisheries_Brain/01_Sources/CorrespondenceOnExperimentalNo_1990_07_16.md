# Correspondence on Experimental Nonessential Population Designation for Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish

#ichthyology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #ConservationPolicy

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 interagency letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Ecological Services office in Phoenix, Arizona, to the Arizona Game and Fish Department. It addresses the status of a proposal to designate 44 sites on Bureau of Land Management (BLM) lands (Safford and Phoenix Districts) as locations for establishing "experimental nonessential populations" of two federally listed fish species, [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), under Section 10(j) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The FWS Regional Office concluded that further action on this designation should be paused pending reanalysis of the recovery program for both species. The letter proposes that reintroduction efforts continue but be managed through a comprehensive [[Section 7 Consultation]] process rather than the Section 10(j) experimental population mechanism.

This is not a primary scientific/morphological source; it is a regulatory and administrative record relevant to conservation management and legal status of these two imperiled southwestern U.S. fish species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Both species are associated with desert aquatic habitats on [[Bureau of Land Management]] lands within the [[Safford District]] and [[Phoenix District]] in Arizona — indicative of isolated desert springs, streams, and riparian corridors typical of the [[Gila River Basin]] and related drainages.
- **Conservation status**: Both *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* and *Cyprinodon macularius* are listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], necessitating formal regulatory consultation (Section 7) for actions affecting them or their habitat.
- **Management mechanism discussed**: [[Section 10(j)]] of the ESA allows for designation of "experimental nonessential populations," a regulatory tool intended to facilitate species reintroduction with reduced regulatory constraints compared to core populations — proposed here across 44 BLM sites.
- **Reintroduction strategy**: The FWS advocates continuing reintroduction efforts for both species but transitioning to a comprehensive Section 7 consultation framework instead of the Section 10(j) experimental designation, as previously outlined in an October 19, 1989 FWS communication regarding reintroduction of endangered and threatened species onto federal lands.

## Key Findings
- The proposed designation of 44 BLM sites (Safford and Phoenix Districts) as experimental nonessential population sites for [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] was **not approved** to proceed at the time of writing.
- The FWS Regional Office determined that the **recovery program** for both species required **reanalysis** before further action on the Section 10(j) designation.
- FWS recommended that reintroduction of these species **continue**, but via a **comprehensive Section 7 consultation** approach rather than the experimental nonessential population framework.
- This reflects broader early-1990s regulatory and interagency coordination challenges between FWS, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and [[Bureau of Land Management]] in managing recovery actions for two critically imperiled Sonoran Desert fish species.
- Document serves as a historical record of the administrative and legal process surrounding endangered fish reintroduction policy in Arizona, illustrating the interplay between the [[Endangered Species Act]]'s Section 7 and Section 10(j) provisions.
