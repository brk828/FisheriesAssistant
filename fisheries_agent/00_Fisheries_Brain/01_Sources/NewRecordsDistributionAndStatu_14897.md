# New Records, Distribution, and Status of Ten Rare Fishes in the Tradewater and Lower Green Rivers, Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Percidae #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #Catostomidae #Lepisosteidae #Aphredoderidae

## Executive Summary

This 1982 survey by the Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission (KNPC) documents the distribution and conservation status of ten rare or previously unrecorded fish species in the [[Tradewater River]] and [[Lower Green River]] drainages of the [[Shawnee Hills Section]] (Western Kentucky Coal Field), Kentucky. Conducted 1979–1980 across 30 collection sites as part of a regional environmental impact assessment, the survey substantially expanded known ranges for several taxa with primary affinities to the [[Gulf Coastal Plain Province]], indicating a much stronger Gulf Coastal Plain biogeographic influence on the lower Ohio River valley ichthyofauna than previously recognized. The study also catalogs 83 total fish taxa (Table 1) across six sub-basins/segments (Tradewater, Lower Green, Middle Green, Pond River, Rough River, Mud River) and highlights severe anthropogenic threats — acid mine drainage, siltation from surface coal mining, oil field brine effluent, and wetland destruction — that jeopardize the persistence of these disjunct, relictual populations.

## Background & Description

The paper is primarily distributional/ecological rather than morphometric, but notes several habitat-linked and taxonomic distinctions relevant to identification and niche differentiation:

- **[[Spotted Gar]]** (*Lepisosteus oculatus*) — not readily captured with small seines, implying deep-bodied, evasive morphology suited to dense vegetated wetland habitat.
- **[[Lake Chubsucker]]** (*Erimyzon sucetta*) vs. its congener *E. oblongus* ([[Creek Chubsucker]]) — sympatric but ecologically segregated; most specimens collected were juveniles, with adults/sub-adults uncommon in samples.
- **[[Blue Sucker]]** (*Cycleptus elongatus*) — a strongly migratory, deep-water, fast-current specialist, morphologically and behaviorally adapted to riverine habitats below navigation dams.
- **[[Banded Pygmy Sunfish]]** (*Elassoma zonatum*) — one of the smallest North American centrarchids (family Elassomatidae in modern taxonomy), restricted to densely vegetated wetland microhabitats.
- Darter species (*Etheostoma*, *Percina*) show habitat-correlated morphological/ecological specialization: e.g., **[[Mud Darter]]** (*Etheostoma asprigene*) and **[[Bluntnose Darter]]** (*Etheostoma chlorosomum*) associated with sluggish, soft-substrate habitats, contrasting with typical riffle-dwelling darters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area spans two contrasting physiographic habitat regimes within the [[Shawnee Hills Section]]:

- **Escarpment/upland streams**: high gradient, rocky substrate, narrow undeveloped floodplains (associated with the [[Dripping Springs Escarpment]]).
- **Interior lowland streams**: low gradient, soft substrates, wide alluviated floodplains with riparian wetlands and oxbows — the primary habitat for most of the rare Gulf Coastal Plain-affiliated species discussed.

Species-specific niche notes:

- **[[Spotted Gar]]**: shallow (0.3–0.6 m) wetlands with dense *Saururus cernuus* (lizard's tail) and *Nuphar* sp. (spatterdock) beds.
- **[[Cypress Minnow]]** (*Hybognathus hayi*): isolated oxbow habitat (0.3–2.3 m deep) in the Pond River, representing a 173 km eastward range extension and the easternmost Ohio River valley occurrence of this southern species.
- **[[Blue Sucker]]**: deep, fast-current habitat below navigation dams (e.g., below US Lock and Dam No. 2) and gravel/clay riverbed reaches; strongly migratory.
- **[[Lake Chubsucker]]**: well-vegetated backwaters and sloughs (vs. flowing-water habitat of *E. oblongus*).
- **[[Banded Pygmy Sunfish]]**: relictual populations in remnant cypress wetlands (Cypress Creek, Long Pond — Pond River system) and newly discovered in the Tradewater River, geographically intermediate between Green River and Gulf Coastal Plain populations.
- **[[Spotted Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis punctatus*): relatively undisturbed Green River tributaries, representing the eastern range limit in the Ohio River valley.
- **[[Mud Darter]]**: sluggish streams and sloughs with soft substrates.
- **[[Bluntnose Darter]]**: standing/gently flowing water over silt or mud bottoms littered with logs, twigs, detritus.
- **[[Harlequin Darter]]** (*Etheostoma histrio*): sporadic populations in the Rough River, Mud River, and Green River mainstem.
- **[[Saddleback Darter]]** (*Percina ouachitae*): extends into the Mud River and a 32 km Green River segment between Lock and Dam Nos. 4 and 5.

## Key Findings

- **New drainage records**: First substantiated records of *Lepisosteus oculatus*, *Erimyzon sucetta*, and *Elassoma zonatum* in the [[Tradewater River]]; first Green River drainage records for *Hybognathus hayi*, *Cycleptus elongatus*, and *Lepomis punctatus*.
- **Range extensions**: *Hybognathus hayi* extended 173 km eastward from its previously known Gulf Coastal Plain range — the easternmost record of the species in the Ohio River valley (shared distinction with SW Indiana populations).
- **Rediscoveries**: *Etheostoma histrio* and *Percina ouachitae*, both previously known in the Green River drainage only from single 1892 Woolman collections, were relocated at multiple new sites (Rough River, Mud River, Green River mainstem).
- **Biogeographic significance**: The cumulative presence of ten Gulf Coastal Plain-affiliated species in the Shawnee Hills/Green River system indicates far stronger historical Gulf Coastal Plain faunal influence on the lower Ohio River valley than previously documented, with present-day distributions being discontinuous relicts of a once more continuous range.
- **Conservation status (per Branson et al. 1981)**:
  - Threatened: *Lepisosteus oculatus*, *Hybognathus hayi*, *Cycleptus elongatus*, *Etheostoma histrio*
  - Special concern: *Elassoma zonatum*, *Lepomis punctatus*
  - Undetermined/uncertain: *Erimyzon sucetta*, *Percina ouachitae*
  - Not officially listed but locally tenuous: *Etheostoma asprigene*, *Etheostoma chlorosomum*
- **Primary threats identified**:
  1. **Acid mine drainage** from active/abandoned coal mines (notably degrading [[Clear Creek]] and the Tradewater River; documented since Grubb & Ryder 1972).
  2. **Siltation** from area-wide surface mining destroying stream channels and wetlands.
  3. **Oil field effluent** (brines and waste oil) — ~20% of swamp vegetation in Henderson County damaged or killed (Converse & Cox 1967); some oil-impacted wetlands showed water quality/algal flora resembling brackish rather than freshwater systems.
  4. **Wetland drainage/clearing** and unplanned agricultural/urban expansion reducing habitat availability across the [[Shawnee Hills Section]].
- **Outlook**: Authors describe the prognosis for Shawnee Hills aquatic ecosystems as poor, citing accelerating coal/oil extraction and relaxed environmental regulation as compounding threats to already fragmented, relictual fish populations.
- **Data structure**: Table 1 documents 83 fish taxa across six sub-basin/segment categories (Tradewater, Lower Green, Middle Green, Pond River, Rough River, Mud River), providing a comprehensive baseline checklist for the region.
