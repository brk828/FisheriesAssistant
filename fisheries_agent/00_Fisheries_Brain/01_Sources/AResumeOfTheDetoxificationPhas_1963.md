## Executive Summary

This 1963 report summarizes the detoxification phase of the **[[Green River Rehabilitation Project]]**, a joint effort by Utah, Wyoming, and the Fish and Wildlife Service to neutralize rotenone toxicant used in a large-scale fish eradication program. The operation aimed to prevent unintended mortality of indigenous fish species downstream of the treatment zone, including within **[[Dinosaur National Monument]]**. Potassium permanganate (KMnO4) was used as the detoxifying agent, dispensed at a calculated ratio to residual rotenone concentrations. Despite operational challenges (unexpected drops in river flow, cooler water temperatures, and longer-than-anticipated rotenone persistence), the detoxification station operated for 82 hours and was judged largely successful, though some fish distress/mortality was reported downstream. Post-treatment sampling confirmed persistence of all native fish species except **[[Gila cypha]]** (humpback chub), which was not detected but considered likely still present given its historic rarity and difficulty of capture.

## Background & Description

While this report is not primarily morphological, it includes a notable taxonomic observation:

- **[[Gila cypha]]** (humpback chub) exhibits variable expression of its characteristic nuchal "hump," with "various gradations in the size of the hump" observed across individuals.
- This morphological variability led some fisheries personnel at the time to question whether *Gila cypha* was a valid distinct species or simply a morphotype of **[[Gila robusta]]** (Colorado River / roundtail chub).
- No detailed morphometric data, meristics, or diagnostic characters are provided in this text beyond this qualitative note on hump variation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The Green River detoxification site was located approximately 35 miles downstream of the last toxicant distribution station and approximately 25 miles below the dam, within a river system supporting native Colorado River Basin fish fauna.
- **Indicator/bioassay species**: Rainbow trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and goldfish (*Carassius auratus*) were used as sentinel organisms in live cages placed 1 and 2 miles downstream of the detoxification station to monitor water safety in real time.
- **Target conservation species**: The primary ecological concern was protecting indigenous fish species — particularly rare Colorado River Basin endemics such as **[[Gila cypha]]** — from unintended rotenone exposure during the broader rehabilitation (eradication) project upstream.
- **Rotenone decay dynamics**: Field and laboratory data (Utah State University hydraulic lab) suggested rotenone decayed at an estimated rate of approximately 1 ppm per 16 miles of stream flow, informing predictions of toxicant concentration reaching the detoxification site.
- **Post-project fish community**: Subsequent sampling within **[[Dinosaur National Monument]]** found all native fish species still present except *Gila cypha*, which was not collected but presumed extant due to its historically low abundance and sampling difficulty.

## Key Findings

- **Detoxification chemistry**: 1 ppm of rotenone (regular commercial carrier) could be effectively neutralized with approximately 2.3 ppm of potassium permanganate (KMnO4); operationally, ratios of 1.5–3 ppm KMnO4 per 1 ppm rotenone were used, with satisfactory results maintained as long as the ratio did not fall below 1:1.7.
- **Operational scale**: At maximum anticipated river flow (1,400 cfs) and 1 ppm rotenone, detoxification required dispersal of 827 lbs/hour of KMnO4. The site had 14,000 lbs available, sufficient for 24 hours at 0.7 ppm average rotenone concentration.
- **Equipment design**: Four KMnO4 dispensers were mounted on a bridge, using gravity-fed sheet metal containers with adjustable openings and motorized rotating discs to disperse detoxifier over a 50–75 foot diameter area.
- **Unexpected complications**: An undetected drop in river flow, a possibly overestimated rotenone decay rate, and falling water temperatures caused the operation to run far longer than planned — ultimately 82 hours, with an emergency shipment of 3,000 lbs of additional KMnO4 required.
- **Monitoring methods**: Rotenone concentrations were tracked via colorimetric analysis (Post method) and U.S. Public Health Service bio-assays, supplemented by live-cage sentinel fish monitoring.
- **Downstream impact**: Some fish mortality/distress was reported in Dinosaur National Monument after the station ceased operation, possibly due to residual rotenone passing through after KMnO4 supplies were exhausted, though some mortality likely originated from upstream treatment zones.
- **Conservation outcome**: All native fish species were confirmed present in post-project sampling except *Gila cypha*, supporting cautious optimism regarding the program's success in preserving indigenous fish diversity.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty**: The report notes ongoing scientific debate over whether *Gila cypha* constitutes a valid species distinct from *Gila robusta*, based on variable hump morphology.
- **Recommendation**: The authors conclude that KMnO4 is an effective agent for rotenone detoxification in fish rehabilitation projects, but recommend development of faster, more accurate field methods for rotenone concentration testing to improve future operations.
