# Distribution of Menhaden in the Gulf of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This 1958 paper by [[Royal D. Suttkus]] (Tulane University), reprinted from the Transactions of the Twenty-third North American Wildlife Conference, revises the distribution of [[Menhaden]] (genus [[Brevoortia]]) in the Gulf of Mexico. Using specimens, meristic counts and proportional measurements, the author reidentifies several previously reported records and concludes that three species occur in the region: the large-scaled [[Brevoortia Patronus]] (Gulf menhaden) and two fine-scaled forms, [[Brevoortia Gunteri]] (western Gulf) and [[Brevoortia Smithi]] (eastern Gulf and Atlantic). The paper also documents the first Gulf record of B. smithi on Florida's west coast and notes major gaps in knowledge of the fishery composition and spawning biology.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic history:** [[Samuel F. Hildebrand]] (1948) reviewed the American menhaden genus, recognizing seven species. Hildebrand fixed the name *B. patronus* Goode, 1878 to the large-scaled Gulf form. Goode's original description combined two forms; one, described by Hildebrand as *B. gunteri*, was described in Goode's diagnosis (smooth or delicately striated operculum), while the description (numerous fine opercular striations) matched *B. patronus*.
- **Diagnostic features discussed:** Opercular striation pattern, fin ray counts (dorsal, anal, pectoral, pelvic), predorsal modified scales, transverse body rows, gill rakers on the lower limb, total vertebrae (typically 45–46) and ventral scutes (about 30–33), and proportional body measurements (% of standard length).
- **Study material:** Specimens from Cedar Keys, Placida (Gasparilla Sound), Englewood and Florida's west coast; reference specimens from Lake Pontchartrain and Mississippi, Alabama and west Florida coasts; Atlantic comparisons from Hildebrand (1948).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Salinity and habitat:** [[Gunter]] (1945) had described *B. gunteri* from brackish water in Texas. The author's several years of study in Lake Pontchartrain and adjoining brackish areas found only *B. patronus*, suggesting *B. gunteri* does not extend east of Grand Isle, Louisiana.
- **Spawning and early life:** The author noted that the spawning behavior, timing and locations of all Gulf menhaden species were essentially unknown. Juveniles of the fine-scaled forms were absent from estuarine areas, leading to the assumption that they occur in deeper offshore waters, where they are considered more prolific.
- **Fishery context:** The major commercial center is in Louisiana, where *B. patronus* dominates; a Pensacola, Florida fishery is probably based entirely on *B. patronus*. [[Brevoortia Smithi]] does not enter the commercial catch.
- **Transformation stage:** Small specimens of *B. smithi* (Placida, 15 May 1957) were judged to represent a transformation stage (Suttkus, 1956) and were not directly comparable with adult material.

## Key Findings

- **Reidentification of records:** A Tampa, Florida record of *B. patronus* (Henshall, 1894) could not be verified and should be accepted with caution. A specimen attributed to *B. gunteri* by [[David K. Caldwell]] (1954) from the Cedar Key area (University of Florida catalog UF 692) was reidentified as *B. smithi*. A head from Escambia Bay, Florida (Bailey, Winn & Smith, 1954) was confirmed as *B. patronus* based on well-developed opercular striae.
- **Range of *B. patronus*:** From Brazos Santiago, Texas (including the type locality) to Cedar Keys, Florida, overlapping *B. gunteri* from Brazos Santiago to Grand Isle, Louisiana, and *B. smithi* at Cedar Keys.
- **First Gulf record of *B. smithi*:** Discovered in November 1957 among fine-scaled menhaden from the Cape Haze Marine Laboratory at Placida, Florida, extending the known range (Beaufort, NC to Indian River, FL) into the Gulf.
- **Meristic and morphometric data:** 23 Gulf specimens of *B. smithi* were counted for meristic characters (no north–south cline evident); 14 specimens were measured (Table 1 range) and 9 small Placida specimens (Table 2) were summarized. Vertebral counts show a possible north–south trend when Atlantic and Gulf specimens are compared (Table 4).
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** The Gulf population of *B. smithi* is not intermediate between the Atlantic *B. smithi* and *B. gunteri*; it is best regarded as a sub-population of *B. smithi*.
- **Management implication:** During discussion, the author and discussion leader Frank (Leader) agreed that management policy should not assume *B. patronus* is the only menhaden species in the Gulf fishery, since the other two species may be ecologically or economically significant.
- **Research gaps:** The composition of the Gulf catch, spawning location and timing, and the schooling behavior of the fine-scaled species remain largely unknown. A U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service vessel operating off the Mississippi River captured a large catch of apparent *B. patronus*, the only recent spawning-related information noted; several federal contracts were then in place to investigate Gulf menhaden.
