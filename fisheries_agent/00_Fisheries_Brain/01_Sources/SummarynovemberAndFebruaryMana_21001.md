# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program Management Group Meeting Summaries (November 1988 - February 1989)

#endangered-species #recovery-program #policy #Colorado-River #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting summaries, correspondence, and administrative materials from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] Management Group meetings held November 22-23, 1988 (Grand Junction, Colorado) and February 9, 1989 (Lakewood, Colorado). The Recovery Program addresses four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The program coordinates federal, state, and stakeholder efforts to recover these species while accommodating water development under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Key participants include the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], and the states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming. The document also includes a March 1989 petition from environmental groups requesting federal listing of the [[Razorback Sucker]] as endangered throughout its range.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] was formalized through a cooperative agreement signed in January 1988 by the Governors of Colorado, Wyoming, and Utah, the Secretary of the Interior, and the Administrator of [[Western Area Power Administration]]. The program establishes a framework for recovering endangered Colorado River fishes while maintaining state water rights systems and interstate compacts. An Implementation Committee oversees program execution, supported by Management and Technical Groups.

The program addresses habitat protection through instream flow identification and legal protection under state law, modification of major [[Bureau of Reclamation]] dam operations (particularly [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]), habitat management and enhancement, propagation and stocking, exotic species control, and research/monitoring. Funding mechanisms include a $10 per acre-foot depletion charge on new water projects, annual state contributions ($100,000 total, redistributed with Wyoming paying $23,000), Section 7 contributed funds, and [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s $1.5 million annual contribution from [[Colorado River Storage Project]] revenues.

The [[San Juan River]] was initially excluded from the Recovery Program due to New Mexico's reluctance to participate and historically low recovery potential. However, 1987-1988 field studies documented a small reproducing population of [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the San Juan from Shiprock, New Mexico to Lake Powell, prompting development of a separate management plan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The four target species evolved in the dynamic, turbid, warm-water conditions of the pre-development Colorado River system. Dams and water diversions have fundamentally altered river hydrology, temperature regimes, sediment transport, and channel morphology. [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] (closed 1962) releases cold water that depresses downstream temperatures, potentially affecting fish reproduction and survival. Flow regulation has eliminated natural flood pulses important for spawning cues and habitat maintenance.

Exotic species introduced throughout the basin prey on and compete with native fishes. Species of concern include [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*). These exotics thrive in altered reservoir and tailwater habitats.

Critical habitats include the 15-mile reach of the [[Colorado River]] (from [[Gunnison River]] confluence to Colorado-Utah border), the [[Yampa River]] (particularly spawning areas), and the [[Green River]]. The [[Yampa River]] remains the only major unregulated tributary in the upper basin, providing essential spawning habitat for [[Colorado Squawfish]]. Sediment transport from tributaries, particularly the [[Little Snake River]] to the Yampa, may be critical for maintaining downstream channel and spawning bar characteristics.

## Key Findings

**Program Management and Coordination:**
- October 12, 1988 Management Group meeting summary approved without modification
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] continuing to pursue mechanisms to transfer Section 7 and donated funds to [[Fish and Wildlife Foundation]]
- [[Uintah Mountain Club]] filed Freedom of Information Act requests regarding endangered fishes and Section 7 consultations; environmental groups discouraged litigation
- [[Environmental Defense Fund]] and [[Colorado Wildlife Federation]] signed supporting resolutions for the Recovery Program
- Public hearings held in Meeker and Rangely, Colorado (October 20, 1988) with small attendance; local [[Water User Association]] opposed further [[Colorado Squawfish]] stocking at [[Kenney Reservoir]]
- Wyoming scheduled public hearing in Rock Springs (December 20, 1988) as required under state law

**Technical and Scientific Activities:**
- Technical Group met November 14-15, 1988; report and recommendations accepted without modification
- Genetics/electrophoretic assessment of [[Colorado Squawfish]] shows "good variability" between and among upper basin populations, greater than in [[Northern Squawfish]]
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in final stages of selecting position to head implementation of Propagation Plan
- FY-89 Information and Education work plan approved with modifications: Utah travel budget increased to $1,200, all parties to review materials before finalization, [[Western Area Power Administration]] to provide design support at no cost
- Work on 15-mile Reach and [[Yampa River]] Biology Reports on schedule; final draft expected December 1988
- Difficulty developing acceptable work plan for quantifying instream flows on [[Yampa River]]; Service's approach criticized as vague, proposing to try multiple methods and only use those agreeing with field observations

**Water Rights and Habitat Protection:**
- State of Colorado continuing negotiations with local government and [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]] regarding [[Juniper-Cross Mountain]] water rights acquisition
- State agreed to conduct site feasibility study for offstream reservoir as alternative to Juniper-Cross Mountain
- Meetings held regarding protection of [[Ruedi Reservoir]] releases; follow-up meetings scheduled for December 1988
- [[The Nature Conservancy]] developed draft work statement for survey of available water rights on White, Colorado, Yampa, and [[Little Snake Rivers]]
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] willing to develop scope of work for evaluating alternative means of providing flows to 15-mile reach, pending approval
- Concern expressed that requesting additional Section 8 funds for water rights acquisition may impact other [[Colorado River Storage Project]] activities
- Colorado, environmental groups, and water developers considering spring 1989 Washington DC briefings to report progress and seek Congressional support without requesting additional appropriations

**Sediment Issues (February 9, 1989 Meeting):**
- Critical policy discussion on whether sediment reduction from water projects constitutes "depletion" or "nondepletion" impact under Recovery Program Section 4.1.5
- Most Management Group members believed sediment reductions from depletions outside occupied habitat should be considered depletion impacts
- Consensus that significant impacts identified in Section 7 consultation must be addressed for legally defensible biological opinions
- Agreement that once depletion charge paid, project proponents should proceed; offsetting measures are Recovery Program responsibility, not individual project responsibility
- Tom Pitts proposed language: "sediment transport, deposition, movement, reduction, etc., will be considered a depletion impact" with Service determining sufficient progress case-by-case
- Issue of "sufficient progress" raised: has Recovery Program progress been sufficient to offset impacts of proposed projects (Sandstone, Muddy Creek, Price-San Rafael Salinity Control)?
- Questions about whether progress in one basin area can offset impacts in another area
- Recommended language to be presented to Implementation Committee February 21, 1989

**Grand Canyon/Lower Basin Issues:**
- [[Western Area Power Administration]] and [[Bureau of Reclamation]] proposed funding [[Humpback Chub]] studies in [[Grand Canyon]]/[[Little Colorado River]] from Reclamation's $1.5 million annual contribution
- Concerns raised: (1) Grand Canyon outside Recovery Program scope; may require amendment of Cooperative Agreement, (2) would dilute upper basin recovery effort, (3) Arizona not a participant in Recovery Program
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] argued [[Glen Canyon Dam]] is upper basin [[Colorado River Storage Project]] feature; studies should receive same treatment as [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Aspinall Unit]], or [[Navajo Dam]]
- Some members indicated potential support if studies contributed directly to upper basin recovery and received high Technical Group priority
- Issue deferred for further Management Group discussion; Reclamation and Western requested to document pros and cons

**San Juan River Development:**
- Service sent letter to New Mexico (November 21, 1988) inviting participation in developing endangered fish management plan/recommendations for [[San Juan River]]
- 1987-1988 field studies documented small reproducing [[Colorado Squawfish]] population from Shiprock, New Mexico to Lake Powell confluence
- Strategy adopted August 30, 1988: complete initial recovery potential evaluation in FY-89 (budget not to exceed $50,000)
- Field studies to determine abundance/distribution of rare fishes, assess habitat potential, identify limiting factors (New Mexico and Utah lead; final report due January 31, 1989)
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] to assess present/future hydrology, model environmental baseline, determine operational flexibility and uncommitted water at [[Navajo Reservoir]] (final report due January 31, 1989)
- Draft recovery/management plan for San Juan due March 1, 1989
- Section 7 approach: implement depletion charge on small projects using San Juan-specific formula; evaluate large projects case-by-case
- New Mexico invited to nominate representative to [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]]

**Razorback Sucker Listing Petition:**
- Sierra Club, National Audubon Society, Wilderness Society, Colorado Environmental Coalition, Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance, and Northwest Rivers Alliance filed petition March 14, 1989 to list [[Razorback Sucker]] as endangered throughout its range
- Petition documents dramatic population decline and range contraction; species once abundant in "wagon loads" in Phoenix area canals and throughout lower Colorado River system
- Current populations extremely rare: Green River sampling (1979-1981) yielded only 5 razorbacks vs. 1,776 [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]s and 278 [[Colorado Squawfish]]; upper Colorado surveys (1979-1985) yielded only 70 razorbacks
- Only mature fish found in lower basin reservoirs ([[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]]), indicating recruitment failure
- Threats include habitat modification from dams (altered flows, temperatures, sediment), predation by exotic fishes, and inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms
- Petition argues [[Recovery Implementation Program]] inadequate: (1) only covers upper basin, not entire range, (2) lacks species-specific biological recovery plan, (3) provides no definite ongoing actions
- Petitioners cite *Northern Spotted Owl v. Hodel* precedent: listing decisions must be based exclusively on biological risk evaluation
- Administrative process: Service has 30 days to acknowledge receipt, 90 days to determine if substantial information presented, 12 months to determine if listing warranted

**Wyoming Participation Issues:**
- October 15, 1987 letter from Regional Director to Wyoming State Engineer summarized resolution of Wyoming's May 1, 1987 proposals
- State-funded projects above [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] not exempt from depletion fee (found unworkable and inequitable)
- Water sales from [[Fontenelle Reservoir]] considered existing [[Bureau of Reclamation]] project; no depletion charge if consultation completed after Recovery Program implementation
- Credit provision added for successful water conservation by existing projects
- Wyoming's annual contribution reduced from $100,000 to $23,000; may provide in-kind services reflecting Implementation Committee priorities
- Wyoming to pay $10/acre-foot for [[Little Snake River]] development with conservation credits
- May 29, 1987 internal memo proposed alternative: drop Wyoming's $100,000 annual contribution (no fish/habitat in state), Wyoming fund sediment/hydrology studies on Little Snake, all Wyoming projects pay $10/AF depletion charge

**Next Steps and Scheduling:**

- January 12-13, 1989 meeting scheduled (joint with Technical Group January 12) at Service Regional Office, Lakewood, Colorado to discuss FY-88 work status, FY-89 scopes of work, and FY-90 work advises
- Technical Group to provide guidance for FY-90 work in form of RFPs by January 1989
- Implementation Committee meeting scheduled February 1989
- Annual cycle established: January (guidance/RFPs), February (Implementation Committee), May (evaluate RFP responses), August (approve budget/work plans), December (annual reports and detailed scopes of work)
