# Further Studies on the Parasitic Crustacea of African Freshwater Fishes

#crustacea #parasitology #copepoda #branchiura #lernaeidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 taxonomic paper by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] of the Freshwater Biological Association reports new crustacean ectoparasites of African freshwater fishes. It describes two new copepods, [[Lamproglenoides Vermiformis]] (new genus and species) and [[Lamproglena Intercedens]], and a new branchiuran, [[Chonopeltis Meridionalis]]. It provides identification keys for African [[Lamproglena]] and [[Chonopeltis]], reports hatching behaviour and the first description of the larva of [[Dolops Ranarum]], and gives a set of new distribution records. The paper also reassigns [[Afrolernaea]] to the family Lernaeidae and synonymises [[Delamarina]] with it.

## Background & Description

The paper gathers miscellaneous observations on parasites of African freshwater fishes, many collected while the author was on staff of the East African Fisheries Research Organization. Material was also received from several collectors, including Dr J. D. Thomas, Mr R. A. Jubb and Dr H. Matthes. The main taxa treated are:

- **Copepoda**: [[Lamproglenoides Vermiformis]] (new genus and species), [[Lamproglena Intercedens]] (new species), [[Afrolernaea]], [[Ergasilus Nodosus]] and other [[Ergasilus]] species, and several [[Lernaea]] species.
- **Branchiura**: [[Chonopeltis Meridionalis]] (new species), [[Dolops Ranarum]], [[Argulus Africanus]] and [[Argulus Schoutedeni]], and [[Chonopeltis Brevis]].

### Lamproglenoides vermiformis gen. et sp. nov.

- Adult female up to about 4.2 mm, elongate and irregularly swollen, with very indistinct segmentation.
- Cephalothorax comprises a little over 10% of total length.
- Antennule two-segmented; antenna minute; maxilla has one fleshy ramus and one spine-armed ramus; maxilliped is wide and fleshy with three terminal hooks.
- Legs 1 to 4 are reduced and unsegmented; leg 5 is absent.
- Furcal rami are reduced to small papillae.
- Eggs are carried in uniseriate strings of up to at least 26 eggs.
- Known from three specimens (holotype plus paratypes), collected from the gills of [[Labeo Cylindricus]] in the [[Tana River]] at Merilla and in the Ragati River at Sagana, Kenya.

### Lamproglena intercedens sp. nov.

- Adult female 4.5 to 5.8 mm (most over 5.0 mm), with a conspicuous swelling at thoracic somites 3 and 4.
- Cephalothorax about 9% of total length. Neck length varies between individuals.
- Thoracic legs 1 and 2 are large, unjointed, and linked on each side by a transverse sclerotised plate.
- Legs 3 and 4 are very small; leg 5 is a tiny plate with three spiniform setae.
- Collected from the gill filaments of *Citharinus* sp. (Citharinidae) in the Sielo Tuni Stream near Wa, a tributary of the Black Volta, Ghana. Heavy infestations were noted.

### Chonopeltis meridionalis sp. nov.

- Adult female up to about 6.2 mm, with a trifoliate carapace comprising about 56% of body length and an abdomen about 28%.
- Maxillae are not prehensile.
- Adult male at least 3.63 mm; legs 2, 3 and 4 are modified for clasping.
- Six specimens (one male, five females) collected from *Labeo rosae* in the Nuanetsi River, Southern Rhodesia (Limpopo System). This is the southernmost river system from which the genus is recorded in Africa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Attachment and gill tissue**: In [[Lamproglena Intercedens]], attachment is by the first two pairs of legs. Host gill tissue proliferated around the body, enveloping it as far back as the second pair of legs. In [[Lamproglenoides Vermiformis]], the maxillae are the main attachment organs.
- **Position on host**: In [[Lamproglena Intercedens]], specimens lay in the sheltered space between the inner and outer rows of gill filaments.
- **Parasite-host relationships**: [[Chonopeltis]] species are bottom-dwelling host associates. Larval [[Chonopeltis]] from the Mokombe River, Congo System, were found on *Synodontis nigriventris*, which is likely an intermediate rather than definitive host.
- **Pigmentation**: In [[Chonopeltis Meridionalis]] and [[Chonopeltis Brevis]], paired dark pigment bands overlie the uteri. The author suggests these shield developing eggs from intense light. Of the four [[Chonopeltis]] species lacking such bands, three live in the buccal cavity of the host, which is in semi-darkness.
- **Host-specificity**: [[Afrolernaea]] and related forms show host-specific attachment patterns. Keys use host family as a diagnostic (e.g. parasitic on *Bagrus*, *Lates*, clariid fishes, cichlids, characids). See the Key Findings section for details.
- **Distribution**: Many records extend known ranges. [[Dolops Ranarum]] is recorded from West Africa for the first time and is considered pan-African, previously known from the [[Nile]], [[Congo System]] and [[Zambezi]] systems. A specimen from a closed drainage basin in Angola, about 3,500 ft above sea level, is discussed in relation to the [[Kunene River]] and [[Okavango River]] and to possible past swamp connections.

## Key Findings

1. **New taxa**: [[Lamproglenoides Vermiformis]] is a new genus allied to [[Lamproglena]] but distinct in body form, maxillae and maxillipeds. The author assigns it to Lernaeidae. [[Lamproglena Intercedens]] and [[Chonopeltis Meridionalis]] are new species.

2. **Afrolernaea**: The enlarged first two thoracic legs of [[Lamproglena Intercedens]] are interpreted as a precursor to the anchoring hooks of [[Afrolernaea]]. The hooks are therefore considered modified thoracic appendages. The author places [[Afrolernaea]] in Lernaeidae. Lamproglena is also assigned to Lernaeidae, not Dichelesthiidae.

3. **Synonymy**: [[Delamarina]] Dollfus (1960) is a synonym of [[Afrolernaea]] Fryer (1956). Its West African species is renamed *Afrolernaea nigeriensis* (Dollfus), which is distinct from *A. longicollis*.

4. **Lamproglena synonymy**: *L. aubentoni* Dollfus (1960) is a synonym of *L. hemprichii* Nordmann (1832). The differences cited by Dollfus fall within known variation.

5. **Keys**: An identification key covers the ten African species of [[Lamproglena]], up from six listed by Capart (1956). Keys to both sexes of the African [[Chonopeltis]] species are also given, using characters such as maxillary prehensility, abdominal lobe length, thoracic pigment bands and leg proportions.

6. **Egg hatching in Dolops ranarum**: At a mean temperature of about 75°F, eyes became visible after 13 days, and hatching began after 27 days. Some eggs took up to 35 days.
   - In a group of 131 contiguous eggs (112 fertile), the first 23 to hatch were nearly all peripheral.
   - Hatching then proceeded centripetally over about eight days, with the last eggs hatching four days after the last peripheral egg.
   - The author suggests reduced oxygen access or a build-up of metabolites in central eggs explains the delay.
   - Hatching was not simultaneous, which the author argues means the gregarious adult behaviour is not due to newly hatched young staying together.

7. **Larva of Dolops ranarum**: The newly hatched larva is essentially a juvenile adult, which the author interprets as simple retardation of hatching by one larval instar relative to the modified nauplius of some [[Argulus]] species. Its maxillule ends in a single simple hook, unlike the two-hooked maxillule of *Argulus*. It is able to swim at hatching and is parasitic from the outset.

8. **Ergasilus nodosus**: The fifth legs, which Wilson (1924) reported absent, are present but very small in West African material from a [[Bagrus]] in the Sielo Tuni Stream. Wilson's "feathering" on the furcal setae was not seen. A record by Monod (1932) is considered a different, unidentified species.

9. **Lernaea barilii**: A West African record from *Barilius steindachneri* in Guinea extends the range of this species, previously known only from Lake Nyasa. The author notes that a Chinese record on *Carassius auratus* is morphologically identical, which raises either a very stable species or a case of homeomorphism between separate parasites.

10. **Additional records**: Several [[Lernaea]], [[Ergasilus]] and [[Argulus]] species are recorded from new localities, including [[Lake Albert]], Lake Tumba, Lake Mweru, Lake George, the Zambezi tributaries and the Limpopo System. Notable host records include *Lamproglena elongata* on *Citharinus latus* in Lake Albert, and *Argulus schoutedeni* on *Distichodus fasciolatus* in the Congo System.
