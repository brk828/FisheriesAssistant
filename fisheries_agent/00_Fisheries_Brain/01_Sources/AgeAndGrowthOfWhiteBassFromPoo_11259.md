# Age and Growth of White Bass from Pool 19 of the Mississippi River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-biology #Moronidae

## Executive Summary

This 1970 study examined the age and growth of 106 [[White Bass]] (*Morone chrysops*) collected from **Pool 19** of the **Mississippi River** near Fort Madison, Iowa, during 1966–1967 as part of a broader environmental assessment evaluating the impacts of a proposed harbor dredging operation on fish and waterfowl food organisms. The research established a [[Length-Weight Relationship]], calculated [[Coefficient of Condition|coefficients of condition (k)]], derived a [[Body-Scale Relationship]] for back-calculating growth, and compared annual growth increments to those from other populations in the Upper Mississippi River system. Overall, growth of Pool 19 white bass was found to be notably **slower** than in comparable regional populations (Lake Pepin, Pool 5A, and Illinois-Missouri waters) and slower than the average reported across 13 lakes and streams in Carlander's (1953) handbook.

## Background & Description

- **Total Length Measurement**: Defined as the distance from the anterior tip of the snout to the posterior margin of the caudal fin (dorsal/ventral lobes squeezed together); recorded in inches, converted to millimeters.
- **Length-Weight Relationship**:
  - Equation: `Log W = -5.036 + 3.063 Log L` (W = weight in g; L = total length in mm)
  - The growth exponent (slope = 3.063) was slightly above isometric (3.00), suggesting longer fish were marginally more robust, but a t-test showed **no statistically significant difference** from isometric growth (3.00) at the 95% confidence level.
- **Body-Scale Relationship**:
  - Equation: `Y = 37.6 mm + 1.35X` (Y = calculated total length; X = scale radius at 40X magnification)
  - An origin value of ~38 mm was used on the [[Nomograph|Carlander-Smith Nomograph]] for back-calculating length at each annulus.
- **Scale Reading Methodology**: Scales taken posterior to the pectoral fin, ventral to the lateral line; plastic impressions made and read twice blind (without length data) to reduce bias; 16 fish over age I showed disagreement upon a third reading, requiring cross-reference with measured lengths to resolve false/indefinite annuli.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Pool 19 of the Mississippi River, a large impounded river reach; fish were collected via trawls, experimental gill nets, electric shockers, and seines from May–December in both 1966 and 1967.
- **Context of Study**: Data were gathered incidentally to a broader Iowa Cooperative Fishery Unit investigation into the ecological effects of a proposed dredging operation in the **Fort Madison harbor**, which threatened to alter or destroy food organisms relied upon by fish and waterfowl.
- **Population Structure**: Age classes 0 through V were represented, though the sample was heavily skewed toward young fish — only 19 of 106 individuals (~18%) had two or more annuli, indicating a population sample dominated by age-0 and age-I fish.
- **Comparative Populations**: Growth was benchmarked against three other Upper Mississippi River populations:
  - [[Illinois-Missouri Waters]] (120 fish, collected 1944)
  - [[Pool 5A]] backwater near Fountain City, Wisconsin (11 fish, 1948–49)
  - [[Lake Pepin]] (83 fish, October 1965)

## Key Findings

- **Slow Growth**: Pool 19 white bass exhibited slower growth than all three comparison populations and slower than the range reported across 13 lakes/streams summarized by Carlander (1953). The cause of this below-average growth rate was not determined.
- **First Annulus Similarity**: Length at the first annulus in Pool 19 fish was comparable to [[Lake Pepin]] and [[Pool 5A]] fish, but by the **second annulus**, Pool 19 fish were noticeably smaller — indicating a growth deceleration specific to this population after year one.
- **Oldest Fish**: The single oldest specimen collected was age V, and it matched the size of a single age-V fish reported from Lake Pepin, suggesting comparable maximum growth potential despite slower early trajectories.
- **Combined Mean Calculated Lengths at Annuli** (mm): Annulus 1 = 139, Annulus 2 = 210, Annulus 3 = 244, Annulus 4 = 272, Annulus 5 = 382 (n=106 combined across year classes).
- **Condition Factor (k) Trends**:
  - Coefficients of condition were **lower in December** compared to summer months.
  - Condition was generally **higher in 1966 than in 1967**.
  - No significant variation in condition factor was detected across different size classes.
- **Seasonal Growth Pattern**: Very little growth occurred by the May collection period, with growth resuming and continuing through the summer months (per monthly increment data in Table 4).
- **Age/Length Variability**: Considerable individual variation was noted — e.g., two age-II fish exceeded 300 mm, while seven fish in age groups III and IV measured only 249–285 mm, highlighting inconsistent growth trajectories within cohorts.
- **Methodological Note**: [[Seriation Tables]] (Carlander 1968) were used to analyze length-weight and body-scale relationships, and the [[Carlander-Smith Nomograph]] method (1944) was applied for back-calculating lengths at each annulus.
