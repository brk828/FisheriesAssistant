# Pre-Impoundment Study of the Flaming Gorge Reservoir

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Wyoming Game and Fish Commission technical report (Fisheries Technical Report No. 9, June 1960, by [[Clifford E. Bosley]], edited by Fred Elserman) documents the pre-impoundment condition of the lower [[Green River]] in Wyoming and Utah before the construction of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] (Ashley Dam). The study ran from July 1, 1958 to April 30, 1960 under [[Dingell-Johnson Program]] project F-25-R-1.

Gill netting, electrofishing and toxicant applications showed that game fish populations inside the future reservoir area were negligible, while rough fish (suckers, bonytail, squawfish and carp) dominated. The report concludes that rough fish removal is feasible, that the reservoir will provide salmonid habitat in the canyon and main body and warm-water habitat in its arms and bays, and that the planned [[Seedskadee Project]] will degrade downstream conditions. It sets out a stocking policy (rainbow trout, kokanee salmon, with largemouth bass and walleye considered), egg-planting experiments, and recommendations for chemical rough fish removal.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Evaluate present fishery status and predict habitat changes from the Flaming Gorge Reservoir and Seedskadee Project, as part of an interstate cooperative program with Utah.
- **Study objectives (7):** species composition; water chemistry; plankton, invertebrates and algae; feasibility of resident sport fish; mapping of rough fish spawning areas; sport fish spawning potential (natural and artificial); and species best suited to reservoir conditions.
- **Methods:** Experimental gill nets (150 ft, 1 x 1 to 2.25 x 2.25 in mesh), modified wide-mouth fyke nets (50 ft wings), emulsified rotenone, and three shockers (115 V 60 A AC, 230 V 10.9 A DC, 115 V 1.3 A AC). An air boat was built for shallow-water access. Water chemistry followed standard methods; invertebrates were sampled with a Surber sampler, zooplankton with a Wisconsin plankton sampler, and phytoplankton by scraping and a modified diatometer. Turbidity was measured with a Jackson turbidimeter.
- **Study area:** Seven sampling stations, from near LaBarge, Wyoming (Station 1, Names Hill) to the Wyoming–Utah state line (Station 7, Linwood Bridge). Only Stations 2, 3 and 4 would remain available for future comparison.
- **Project context:** Ashley Dam (495 ft high) was to impound about 3,930,000 acre-feet and form a reservoir of about 43,000 surface acres, with an estimated 300,000 annual recreational users.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Upper river (LaBarge to Big Island Bridge):** Excellent trout habitat with moderate-to-good bottom fauna. The main limiting factor was the lack of attached algae and aquatic plants.

**Lower river (Big Island Bridge to state line):** Shifting silt and sand beds, high turbidity from tributaries (Big Sandy, Sage and Currant creeks), and high summer temperatures approaching the tolerance limit of trout (peak 79 °F at Green River). Water temperatures ranged from freezing to about 78 °F in July.

**Water quality:** Total dissolved solids rose at each downstream station. Fontenelle Creek TDS was more than double the Green River value. The Big Sandy was very poor for fisheries, with TDS of 3,240 ppm and a large perennial delta. Coliform counts were highest at Stations 4 and 5, downstream of Green River town and the Rock Springs sewage outfall on Bitter Creek (counts of 115–134 per cc).

**Pollution sources:** Raw sewage from Green River (population about 4,500); oil discharges from the U.P. Railroad (reduced by an oil separator plant); and effluent from Rock Springs, which had no chlorination (BOD reduction 85–90%, outfall equivalent to a town of 11,300–11,950).

**Tributaries:** Fontenelle Creek (sustained flow, wild rainbow), Big Sandy (poor, sediment-laden), Sage and Currant creeks (silt, poor spawning), Blacks Fork (bonytail reproduction recorded), Henrys Fork (trout headwaters, reservoir arm extends about 5 miles), Sheep Creek (trout, brown trout stocked by Utah), and Carter Creek (natural rainbow × native hybrids found).

**Biological data:** Zooplankton averaged 0.42–7.2 organisms per liter across stations. Bottom fauna was dominated by Ephemeroptera and Diptera, with Annelida and Gastropoda indicating organic waste at Station 5.

## Key Findings

### Fish populations
- Game fish were negligible inside the reservoir area. Rough fish dominated.
- **Bigmouth sucker** (*Catostomus latipinnis*): the most widely distributed large sucker, 48.5% of fish by number and 51.4% by weight in gill net sets.
- **Bonytail** (*Gila robusta*): widespread in the Green River; a humpback form was over 66% of bonytails in one net set. Fingerlings found in the Blacks Fork (about 35 river miles from the mouth) indicate successful reproduction.
- **Brown trout** made up 90.7% of fish planted below LaBarge since 1939. Natural reproduction was suspected at Sage Creek.
- **Rainbow trout** were limited to the upper Fontenelle Creek and Sheep/Carter creek systems.
- **Channel catfish** were found only below the Flaming Gorge Canyon. Pollution was believed to have caused decline. Only 2 fish were taken in the study.
- **Squawfish:** eight captured, largest 24 in. and 3 lb 8 oz; range ended near Kinkaid Ranch.
- **Yellow perch** had escaped from Eden Valley Reservoir into the Little Sandy River. Toxicant was applied in May and September 1959 to remove them.
- **Hybrid trout** (rainbow × native) recorded at Carter Creek in September 1959, aged at two years.

### Spawning experiments
- **Brown trout eyed eggs:** 21,800 planted December 19, 1958 at Green River (above Big Island Bridge); survival of recovered eggs was 91.8%.
- **Kokanee eyed eggs:** 11,500 planted January 1959 at Big Island Bridge, with 80–90% incubation completed at planting. Losses were attributed to alevin escape and rust from wire baskets.
- **Green kokanee eggs (Dec 1960):** planted at 40 Rod Creek (above 43 °F) had high survival, while plants in Pine Creek and the Green River (near 32 °F) suffered 100% mortality.
- **Green rainbow eggs (1960):** a 50% hatch in experimental groups versus an 83% hatch in the control.

### Reservoir and planning
- Reservoir fluctuation: average annual maximum of 34 ft, with a practical minimum fluctuation of 90 ft.
- **Access sites:** seven proposed (four in Utah, three in Wyoming). Holmes Ferry and Marsh Creek were recommended for development. Estimated access costs were $32,600.
- **Stocking policy:** rainbow trout at 200 fish per surface acre (6,000 fish from each state in the first year); kokanee at state discretion; walleye deferred; largemouth bass and channel catfish considered.
- **Seedskadee Project** (Fontenelle Dam): about 7,100 surface acres, 285,000 acre-feet capacity, irrigation of 59,620 acres. Predicted impacts include de-watering of about 10 miles of river in fall, rising temperatures and TDS, and increased turbidity.

### Recommendations
- Chemically treat the Green River above the reservoir (and the New Fork, East Fork and Horse Creek systems) to reduce trash fish populations, with cooperation between Wyoming and Utah.
- Stock rainbow trout (2–4 in) after rotenone dissipates, with fish planted by boat.
- Defer walleye stocking until other species are tested.
- Study largemouth bass suitability after initial rainbow plantings.
- Require an adequate disposal system at Green River before planting fish below the town.
- Request Rock Springs to improve its sewage disposal to reduce coliform counts if the Green River lagoon does not resolve them.
