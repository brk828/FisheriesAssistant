## Executive Summary

This document is a **cooperative Private Lands Agreement** (FWS Agreement No. 1448-00002-95-0597) between the San Carlos Apache Tribe, the San Carlos Recreation and Wildlife Department, the Ash Creek Cattle Association, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), executed under the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] (16 U.S.C. §§ 661–666c). The agreement does not present primary fish species data; rather, it establishes a **riparian habitat restoration project** along a six-mile reach of [[Bronco Gulch]], a tributary drainage on the San Carlos Apache Reservation in Graham County, Arizona. The stated goals include restoring riparian vegetation integrity, increasing native woody plant (cottonwood) cover, improving habitat structure for neotropical migratory birds and other wildlife, and — of direct relevance to aquatic ecology — **extending seasonal surface flow and potentially establishing perennial flow** in Bronco Gulch through livestock exclusion and vegetation recovery. This hydrological objective has downstream implications for any native fish or aquatic macroinvertebrate populations dependent on intermittent desert stream flow regimes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although fish-specific ecological data are absent, the agreement provides substantial detail on the **riparian and hydrological habitat context** that would structure any aquatic community present in [[Bronco Gulch]]:

- **Stream type**: Currently an intermittent/seasonal-flow desert drainage; the project explicitly aims to "extend the period of seasonal flow" and "potentially establish perennial flow," implying the current hydrology may be insufficient to support permanent aquatic fish populations.
- **Riparian vegetation baseline**: Existing riparian cover is dominated by **Emory oak** (*Quercus emoryi*) with only "widely scattered cottonwood and other woody plants," indicating a degraded riparian buffer likely linked to historical livestock grazing pressure.
- **Livestock exclusion as habitat management tool**: Cattle grazing by the [[Ash Creek Cattle Association]] will be excluded from the six-mile project reach for a minimum of five years to allow revegetation, a common strategy for restoring streambank stability, shade, and organic input relevant to aquatic/riparian food webs.
- **Adjacent protected area**: An existing ~200-acre wildlife habitat improvement area upstream at [[Boni Tank]] will remain permanently excluded from grazing, functioning as a long-term riparian refugium.
- **Water source management**: A livestock trap near Boni Tank will remain accessible as an alternative watering point, redirecting grazing pressure away from the restoration reach while maintaining Boni Tank itself as a livestock water source — a dual-use water management approach balancing agricultural and habitat needs.
- **Fencing infrastructure**: 12 miles of new fencing (9.5 miles funded by the Service, 2.5 miles by Cooperator B) will physically exclude livestock from the six-mile riparian corridor, the primary mechanism for enabling vegetative and hydrological recovery.
- **Broader ecosystem services**: The project also aims to improve acorn availability for Tribal harvest (Emory oak mast) and benefit neotropical migratory bird communities, situating the aquatic/riparian restoration within a multi-use, multi-species wildlife management framework rather than a fish-specific conservation plan.

## Key Findings

- No direct fish species, population, or morphological data are contained in this document; it is a **legal/administrative cooperative agreement**, not a scientific study.
- The project's central aquatic-relevant hypothesis is that **excluding livestock grazing for a minimum of five years** will allow riparian vegetation (especially cottonwood) recovery sufficient to **extend seasonal flow duration and potentially restore perennial flow** in Bronco Gulch — a hydrological outcome with likely indirect benefits for native desert fish and aquatic invertebrates dependent on sustained flow.
- A **Wildlife Management Plan**, to be developed cooperatively before project implementation, will include monitoring protocols to measure "changes in the riparian community and the influence of changes in the water flow regimes," suggesting flow/hydrology monitoring was anticipated as a key restoration success metric.
- Grazing exclusion is structured as an **adaptive management** process: after five years, all parties (Service, Tribe, Recreation and Wildlife Department, and Cattle Association) will jointly evaluate vegetation recovery criteria before considering reintroduction of limited, controlled grazing.
- The **Boni Tank area** functions as an existing reference/control site for habitat recovery, having already been under livestock exclusion, and will remain a long-term water source management site independent of the new restoration reach.
- Funding structure: USFWS committed **$13,749** toward fencing materials (9.5 miles), while Cooperator B (San Carlos Recreation and Wildlife Department) committed **$27,814** toward materials, labor, fence maintenance, and annual vegetation/wildlife monitoring — indicating a substantial long-term Tribal investment in monitoring outcomes relevant to riparian and potentially aquatic habitat recovery.
- The agreement's 10-year term and requirement for a cultural resource survey prior to fence installation reflect procedural safeguards but do not include any explicit fish survey, stocking, or aquatic species monitoring requirement — highlighting a **data gap** regarding baseline or target fish/aquatic fauna in Bronco Gulch.
