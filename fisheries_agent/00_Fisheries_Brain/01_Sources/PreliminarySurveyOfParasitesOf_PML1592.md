# Preliminary Survey of Parasites of Vertebrates from Cuatro Cienegas, Coahuila, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This preliminary survey documents helminth parasites of selected vertebrates from the [[Cuatro Cienegas]] valley in [[Coahuila]], Mexico. It is based on specimens collected in 1973–1974 and 1983. The study focuses on the endemic [[Cichlasoma]] species complex, whose three cryptic, sympatric species differ in diet (molluscivorous, detritivorous, or piscivorous). Four digenean trematodes were found in these cichlids, and parasites were also recorded from other fishes, a lizard, an aquatic turtle, a frog, and an endemic snail. A total of 499 digenean trematodes were counted from the cichlid sample. Parasite occurrence differed between two habitats, which the author attributes to differences in water chemistry and temperature.

## Background & Description

The Valley of Cuatro Cienegas is an area of high [[Endemism]], with unique plants, fishes, snails, turtles, and scorpions. Three endemic, cryptic, sympatric [[Cichlasoma]] species are distinguished by pharyngeal teeth and diet. A fourth endemic cichlid is piscivorous. Later work described these cichlids as a single polymorphic species, [[Cichlasoma minchleyi]]. Earlier work recorded 18 gastropod species from the valley, 12 of them endemic, representing three endemic subfamilies and two endemic genera.

Because of this gastropod endemism and cichlid speciation, the valley offers an opportunity to study relationships among fish species, their diets, and their digenean parasites. For the 1973–1974 portion of the study, 62 cichlid specimens covering all three diet types were collected from April 1973 to March 1974, dissected, and examined internally.

Further collections in early 1983 included amphibians, reptiles, and birds as well as fishes. This broadened the survey beyond cichlids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Diet and feeding:** Stomach contents of fishes from all three diet types almost always included small crustaceans, insects, and detritus alongside their main food items. The author suggests these secondary foods may reflect opportunism, chance, or ecological stress.

**Habitat differences:** Two habitats were compared:
- **Poza la Becerra:** fishes contained only [[Quadripaludis luistoddi]] and [[Saccocoelioides]] sp., with Saccocoelioides sp. found in a large number of host individuals.
- **Rio Mezquites:** fishes hosted all four trematode species, with [[Crassicutis bravae]] the most frequent.

The author proposes that differences in parasite presence, frequency, and infestation level between the habitats probably reflect differences in [[Water Chemistry]] and/or water temperature. These factors could affect both the parasites and their intermediate hosts.

**Intermediate hosts:** Of two potential snail intermediate hosts examined, [[Nymphophilus minchleyi]] and [[Mexithauma quadripaludium]], only the latter yielded digenean cercariae. These were found in the Rio Mezquites.

## Key Findings

**Parasite community in cichlids (Cichlasoma spp.)**
- 499 digenean trematodes were recovered, representing four species:
  - [[Quadripaludis luistoddi]] (Hemiuridae)
  - an undescribed haploporid, [[Saccocoelioides]] sp. (Haploporidae)
  - [[Crassicutis bravae]] (Lepocreadidae)
  - metacercariae of [[Maritreminoides]] sp. (Microphallidae)
- There was no parasite specificity for a particular host, although distribution differed between habitats.

**Parasites of other fishes (1983 collections)**
- Dactylogyrid monogeneans were collected from Cichlasoma spp. and Etheostoma sp.
- Encysted and intestinal nematodes occurred in [[Gambusia marshi]], Etheostoma sp., and [[Cyprinodon bifasciatus]].
- A piscicolid leech ([[Piscicolidae]], Annelida) was hosted by [[Notropis xanthicara]].

**Reptiles and amphibians**
- [[Sceloporus merriami]] carried acanthocephalan larvae beneath the skin.
- [[Terrapene coahuila]] (aquatic box turtle) hosted the monogenean [[Polystomoides]] sp. in the pharyngeal region, the digenean [[Telorchis]] sp. in the intestine, and two species of oxyuroid nematodes, one in the intestine and one in the stomach.
- [[Rana pipiens]] (leopard frog, cf.) contained two intestinal lecithodendriid trematodes, [[Pleurogenoides]] sp. and Limatulum (?) sp.

**Summary of the survey**
- The parasite fauna includes representatives of Digenea, Monogenea, Nematoda, Acanthocephala, and Hirudinea (leeches).
- Two species of digenean trematodes were recorded from the frog, and the endemic snail *Mexithauma quadripaludium* was identified as an intermediate host.
- Differences in parasite communities between the two habitats point to water chemistry and temperature as possible drivers, a hypothesis the author leaves for future testing.
