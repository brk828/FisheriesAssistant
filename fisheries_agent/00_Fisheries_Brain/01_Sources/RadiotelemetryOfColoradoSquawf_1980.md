# Radiotelemetry of Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker, Green River System of Utah, 1980

## Executive Summary
This 1980 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service study represents an early application of radiotelemetry to track two endangered fish species — the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — in the [[Green River]] system of Utah. Six Colorado squawfish and one razorback sucker were surgically implanted with radio transmitters and monitored via boat-based and aerial tracking for periods ranging from 14 days to over 4 months. The study revealed two distinct movement patterns in Colorado squawfish potentially linked to sexual maturity, documented long-distance riverine migrations (>300 km), and found the razorback sucker to be comparatively sedentary. High water conductivity in the [[Green River]] and [[Duchesne River]] posed technical challenges to radio signal transmission, partially mitigated by the shallow depth of the study reach. This work laid groundwork for an expanded 1981 telemetry program and contributed early insight into the spawning-season movement ecology of the [[Colorado River Endangered Fishes|Colorado River's endangered big-river fishes]].

## Background & Description
- **Colorado Squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)**
  - Total length (TL) range of captured specimens: 508–707 mm
  - Largest member of the North American minnow family ([[Cyprinidae]]); apex predatory cyprinid of the Colorado River Basin
- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**
  - Single specimen captured: TL 510 mm
  - Member of [[Catostomidae]]; named for the distinctive dorsal keel/hump anterior to the dorsal fin (not explicitly described in this text but characteristic of the species)
- **Surgical/Implant Details**
  - Radiotransmitters: AVM Sm-1 "padlock"-shaped units, ~11.0 g, powered by Hg-630 mercury battery, theoretical lifespan ~215 days
  - Transmission frequency range: 40.660–40.700 MHz
  - Implantation site: lateral incision immediately anterior and slightly dorsal to the right pelvic fin insertion
  - Sutures: individual (typically five), knots sealed with cyanoacrylate ("Krazy Glue")
  - Post-mortem inspection of two recaptured squawfish showed no adverse tissue effects; transmitters were walled off by adhesions with no organ irritation, attributed to small module size and absence of sharp edges

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Large, turbid, free-flowing desert river system; study area centered on the [[Green River]] near Ouray, Utah, with fish movements extending into the [[White River (Utah)|White River]] and [[Duchesne River]] tributaries
- **Movement Ecology**:
  - Colorado squawfish exhibited two behavioral phenotypes:
    1. **Mobile/migratory group**: Long-distance upstream movement (~80 km) peaking in late June–early July, followed by downstream return past release points; one fish tracked >300 km downstream into [[Labyrinth Canyon]]; another ascended the White River 56 km
    2. **Stationary group**: Remained within 5–16 km of release site over the same period
  - Sex/maturity linkage: A recaptured stationary fish was an immature male; a recaptured mobile fish was a sexually mature female, observed orienting to riffle habitats in both the Green and White Rivers during the presumed spawning season — suggesting mobile behavior is tied to reproductive migration in mature individuals
  - Razorback sucker showed comparatively sedentary behavior: moved ~6 km downstream into the Duchesne River, remained within <1 km until early June flooding, then relocated to the Green-Duchesne confluence and moved ~11 km upstream in the Green River before contact was lost
- **Water Chemistry Context**:
  - Green River conductivity varied seasonally: lowest in June (mean 202 µmho, n=9), highest in September (mean 809 µmho, n=6)
  - Duchesne River conductivity markedly higher: up to 1950 µmho recorded April 18, with readings of 1600, 1000, and 980 µmho within 1.5 km downstream of its mouth
  - High conductivity combined with water depths >2 m degraded radio signal detection range; the predominantly shallow nature of the study reach helped offset this limitation

## Key Findings
- Successful field validation of radiotelemetry techniques for two imperiled big-river fish species despite prior theoretical concerns (Sinning 1979) about high-conductivity interference with radio signal transmission in the [[Green River]]
- Documented **riffle-orientation behavior** in a mature female Colorado squawfish coinciding with the expected spawning season, including a rapid ~161 km movement in 5 days between riffle sites in the Green and White Rivers
- Identified an apparent behavioral dichotomy in Colorado squawfish — **long-distance migratory movement in sexually mature individuals** versus **sedentary behavior in immature fish** — providing early evidence linking movement ecology to reproductive status
- Surgical implantation technique (modified from Bidgood 1980) proved minimally invasive, with no observed internal organ damage or irritation upon necropsy
- Razorback sucker movement was limited and tied to tributary/mainstem transitions potentially influenced by seasonal flooding events
- Established seasonal conductivity and temperature baselines for the Green and Duchesne Rivers relevant to future telemetry equipment performance and habitat characterization
- Findings directly informed the design of an **expanded 1981 radiotelemetry program** as part of the broader [[Colorado River Fisheries Investigations]] effort (Miller et al. 1980)
