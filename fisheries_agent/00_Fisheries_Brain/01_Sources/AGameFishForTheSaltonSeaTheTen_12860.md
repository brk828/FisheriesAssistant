# A Game Fish for the Salton Sea, the Ten-Pounder, Elops affinis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Elopidae #game-fish #salton-sea

## Executive Summary

This 1942 report documents the first confirmed occurrence of ten-pounders ([[Elops affinis]]) in the [[Salton Sea]], California, representing a potential new sport fishery for this previously barren water body. Between May 14-24, 1942, commercial fishermen and biologists captured 244 specimens ranging from 17.1 to 19.7 inches in length and 1.75 to 2.5 pounds in weight. All examined fish were sexually mature and in spawning condition. The species likely migrated from the [[Gulf of California]] through the [[Colorado River]] and irrigation canal system. Management recommendations include minimum 5-inch stretched mesh nets to protect ten-pounders while allowing [[Mullet]] harvest.

## Background & Description

The [[Salton Sea]] lies 240 feet below sea level in southern California, measuring 40 miles long and 12 miles wide with saline waters. Fed primarily by the [[Alamo River]] and [[New River]]—drainage channels from the [[Imperial Valley]] irrigation system—the Sea has no outlet and connects to the [[Colorado River]] through canals, allowing fish migration.

Prior to 1942, the Sea's fish fauna was limited and provided minimal sport fishing opportunities. Known species included:
- [[Mullet]] ([[Mugil cephalus]])
- [[Desert Minnow]] ([[Cyprinodon macularius]]), native to the Salton Sink
- [[Mosquito Fish]] ([[Gambusia affinis affinis]])
- [[Carp]], catfishes, and sunfishes (near river mouths only)

Previous introduction attempts failed: [[Striped Bass]] ([[Roccus lineatus]]) in 1929 and 1930, and [[Silver Salmon]] ([[Oncorhynchus kisutch]]) in 1934.

[[Elops affinis]] is common in the [[Gulf of California]] and ranges from California to Ecuador, typically inhabiting bays, estuaries, sloughs, and muddy tidal streams. Glidden (1941) first reported the species in the [[Colorado River]] at [[Laguna Dam]], 12 miles above Yuma, Arizona, where two specimens (11 and 14 inches) were collected in August 1941.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Characteristics:**
The [[Salton Sea]] environment is characterized by:
- Saline water chemistry
- Desert climate with high temperatures
- Muddy substrate from irrigation drainage
- Connection to [[Colorado River]] via canal system with barriers (drops) preventing fish exodus

**Food Web:**
Stomach content analysis of 20 ten-pounders revealed:
- 12 empty stomachs
- 5 fish contained 1-9 [[Desert Minnow]]s each (from [[Bombay Beach]])
- 3 fish contained 2-34 [[Desert Minnow]]s each (from [[Mullet Island]])
- Desert minnows ranged 1-2 inches in length
- One hydrophilid beetle found

The authors note that [[Desert Minnow]]s and [[Mosquito Fish]] appear plentiful as prey, though desert minnow populations might not sustain a large ten-pounder population. Other fishes and invertebrates may also serve as food sources.

**Reproductive Status:**
All fish examined on May 24, 1942 (3 females from [[Mullet Island]], 3 females and 7 males from mouth of [[Alamo River]], 1 male from [[Bombay Beach]]) were sexually mature and in spawning condition. Males produced large quantities of milt readily.

**Migration Pathway:**
The species likely entered from the [[Gulf of California]] through the [[Colorado River]] and irrigation canal system. The timing and mechanism of colonization remain uncertain—residents near Yuma had previously reported fish called "anchovies" and "gars" or "pike" that may have been ten-pounders.

## Key Findings

**Collection Data (May 1942):**
- May 14: 10 ten-pounders (18-20 inches) caught at [[Bombay Beach]] in beach seine hauls
- May 24: 20 ten-pounders caught off [[Mullet Island]] in gill nets (3¼ inch stretched mesh)
- May 24: 11 additional ten-pounders caught by authors (1 at [[Bombay Beach]], 10 near mouth of [[Alamo River]])
- Total documented: 41+ specimens
- Nets with 4-inch stretched mesh caught only mullet; 3 and 3¼ inch mesh caught ten-pounders

**Morphometrics:**
10 fish from [[Alamo River]] mouth:
- Length (to caudal fork): 17.1-19.7 inches
- Weight: 1.75-2.5 pounds
- All other specimens within or close to this size range

**Distribution:**
Fish appear well distributed throughout the [[Salton Sea]], captured at multiple locations including [[Bombay Beach]], [[Mullet Island]], and [[Alamo River]] mouth.

**Sport Fishing Potential:**
[[Elops affinis]] is considered equal to its Atlantic relative [[Elops saurus]] as a game fish:
- Takes bait and artificial lures
- Responds to No. 3 or 4 spoons with triple hooks
- Provides excellent sport on light tackle
- Known for long leaps
- Authors suggest streamer flies with or without spinners, given feeding on desert minnows
- Flesh quality reported as excellent by multiple consumers
- Caution: meat spoils rapidly in hot climate; immediate icing recommended

**Management Recommendations:**
1. California Division of Fish and Game ordered permit holders to use minimum 5-inch stretched mesh nets
2. This mesh size allows [[Mullet]] harvest while permitting ten-pounder escapement
3. Proposal to classify as game fish in forthcoming report on lower [[Colorado River]] and [[Salton Sea]] fishery

**Establishment Uncertainty:**
Success depends on:
- Continued ingress from [[Colorado River]] (barriers prevent return migration)
- Successful completion of life cycle in the Sea
- Unknown whether physical, chemical, and biological conditions support egg and larval development
- Food availability (primarily [[Desert Minnow]]s and [[Mosquito Fish]])

**Historical Context:**
Commercial fishing for [[Mullet]] operated 1915-1921, was voluntarily abandoned, prohibited by law in 1931, and resumed experimentally in 1942 under close supervision. Ten-pounders were apparently absent during 1915-1921 commercial operations, suggesting recent colonization.
