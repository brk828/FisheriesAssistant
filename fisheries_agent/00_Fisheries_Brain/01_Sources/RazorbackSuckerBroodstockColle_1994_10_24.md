# 1994 Razorback Sucker Broodstock Collection

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) interoffice memorandum detailing the collection and transfer of **287 [[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) fingerlings. The fish were harvested as wild larvae from **[[Lake Mohave]]** during the spring spawning season (Feb-Mar 1994) and reared at the Parker National Fish Hatchery (Parker FRO) before being transported to the **Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center** to supplement the species' captive [[Broodstock Management|broodstock]] program. This effort represents a standard operational component of the [[Razorback Sucker Recovery Program]] aimed at preventing the extinction of this federally endangered catostomid.

## Background & Description
Biometric data was collected on a subsample of the transported cohort to characterize growth post-rearing.

* **Life Stage:** Fingerlings (originally collected as wild larvae).
* **Sample Size for Measurement:** n = 50 individuals (out of 287 total transported).
* **Total Length (TL) Range:** 78 mm – 132 mm.
* **Mean Total Length:** 105 mm.
* **Weight:** Not determined (no mass data recorded for this cohort).

> [!note] Growth Context
> The range in total length (54 mm spread) among fish collected within the same general spawning window (Feb-Mar) suggests differential growth rates or staggered hatching/spawning events among the source coves prior to capture and standardized hatchery rearing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The data highlights the specific spawning and larval-drift habitat utilized by the species within a large reservoir ecosystem.

* **Habitat Utilized:** Wild larvae were harvested exclusively from **littoral coves**, indicating these serve as critical nursery/nursery-drift habitats for newly hatched [[Larval Fish Ecology|razorback sucker larvae]].
* **Geographic Distribution:** Collections spanned a specific range of the reservoir, primarily concentrated on the **east side of Lake Mohave** (River Miles 25–40).
* **Collection Sites (Coves):**
    * Burro Cove
    * Eagle Cove
    * Red-tail Cove
    * Great-west Cove
    * Thirty Mile Cove
    * Owl Cove
    * Owl Point Cove
    * Yuma Cove
    * **Tequila Cove** (Notable outlier: located on the *west* side of the lake, ~River Mile 20).
* **Collection Timing:** February to March 1994, corresponding to the species' natural spring spawning and larval emergence period.
* **Stakeholders:** Collection was conducted by the **[[Lake Mohave Native Fish Group]]**, indicating a collaborative, multi-agency monitoring effort.

## Key Findings
1.  **Successful Recruitment Capture:** 287 wild-spawned larvae were successfully collected, reared, and survived to the fingerling stage, demonstrating effective hatchery intervention techniques for the species.
2.  **Multi-Cove Sourcing:** Because collections from the nine distinct coves were pooled ("mixed") prior to transport, the exact genetic/geographic contribution of each specific cove to the final 287 fish is **unknown**. This has implications for future genetic diversity tracking in the [[Dexter Broodstock]] program.
3.  **Growth Benchmark:** The mean TL of 105 mm provides a benchmark growth metric for wild-origin razorback sucker larvae reared in a hatchery setting (Parker FRO) prior to long-distance transport.
4.  **Institutional Role:** This memo documents the standard "feeder" relationship between the Parker FRO (rearing/holding wild-caught fish from Lake Mohave) and the Dexter NFHTC (long-term genetic refuge and broodstock development), a key logistical pathway in the [[Colorado River Fish Recovery Program]].
