# Multiple Genetic Forms of Blind Cavefish in *Astyanax mexicanus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This study analyzes sequence variation in the mitochondrial [[Ndh2 Gene|NADH Dehydrogenase 2 (ND2)]] gene across surface (epigean) and cave (hypogean) populations of [[Astyanax Mexicanus]], a characid fish with a surface form and numerous independently derived eyeless cave populations. The authors identify at least two genetically distinct cavefish lineages (A and B) that share similar eyeless, depigmented phenotypes. The lineages differ in the number of rib-bearing thoracic vertebrae, supporting the hypothesis that cave-adapted phenotypes evolved through multiple founder events and/or introgressive hybridization rather than a single invasion.

## Background & Description

*Astyanax mexicanus* is a teleost with an eyed, pigmented surface form and at least 29 hypogean populations. Cave forms show reduced eyes, loss of melanin pigmentation, and enhanced gustatory and lateral-line systems, collectively termed [[Troglomorphism]]. Earlier work, including [[Allozyme Analysis]] by Avise and Selander (1972), attributed low within-cave variation to genetic drift in small populations and concluded the forms were conspecific. Earlier crossing experiments by Wilkens (1971) suggested that cavefish populations from different caves carried different mutations, supporting independent origins.

Cave populations discussed include:
- [[Chica Cavefish]] (La Cueva Chica), which contains putative hybrids
- [[Pachón Cavefish]] (La Cueva de El Pachón), albinistic and fully regressed
- [[Los Sabinos Cavefish]] (Cueva Los Sabinos)
- [[Tinaja Cavefish]] (El Sótano de la Tinaja), the largest hypogean population studied
- [[Curva Cavefish]] (La Cueva de la Curva)
- [[Subterráneo Cavefish]] (La Cueva del Río Subterráneo), with a mixed-morphotype sample

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Cave populations occur in the limestone escarpment of the [[Sierra de El Abra]] and the [[Micos Region]] in Tamaulipas and San Luis Potosí. Connections with surface drainage vary: La Cueva Chica is linked to the Río Tampaón, which allows periodic introgression with epigean fish; La Cueva de El Pachón is a former spring resurgence with no obvious access for surface fish; the Subterráneo cave receives an intermittent stream during the rainy season. The authors also note a Guerrero population (Gruta de las Granadas) derived from the epigean *Astyanax aeneus*, reflecting additional independent cave colonization.

Sampling included surface populations from Texas, Coahuila, Tabasco, Veracruz, and Costa Rica, plus *A. bimaculatus* as an outgroup. Collection methods were cast nets, hand-held nets, and seines. Tail-fin clips were preserved in 95% ethanol saturated with EDTA.

## Key Findings

**Genetic structure**
- ND2 sequences were analyzed from 364 individuals representing 6 hypogean and 11 epigean populations.
- Single-strand conformation polymorphism (SSCP) analysis of a 306-bp ND2 fragment identified 26 variable positions and 20 haplotypes, five unique to cavefish. Most changes were transitions.
- Phylogenetic analysis identified two distinct lineages:
  - **Lineage A** (16 haplotypes): includes Chica, Pachón, and Subterráneo cavefish plus closely related epigean populations.
  - **Lineage B** (4 haplotypes): includes Los Sabinos, Tinaja, and Curva cavefish, with no closely related epigean counterparts. Lineage B is about 3.5% divergent from lineage A.
- All 11 epigean populations carried only lineage A haplotypes, with haplotype A1 found in all but one sample.
- Lineage A and B divergence exceeded divergence between *A. mexicanus* and *A. aeneus*.

**Diversity**
- Epigean populations averaged 3.6 haplotypes (range 2–6), gene diversity 0.45, and nucleotide diversity 0.0020.
- Hypogean populations had lower diversity; only the Subterráneo and Tinaja cave populations were not fixed for a single haplotype.
- Epigean vs. hypogean variability differed significantly (Mann-Whitney U tests, p = 0.006 and 0.006 as reported).

**Geographic structure**
- AMOVA showed significant structure among populations (FST = 0.88, p < 0.001), mainly between northern and southern drainages (FCT = 0.77, p = 0.018), which indicates restricted gene exchange between them.
- Nested clade analysis found that some haplotypes were associated with geography and that isolation by distance was the likely explanation.

**Axial skeleton**
- Lineage A cavefish, like surface fish, typically have 12 rib-bearing thoracic vertebrae.
- Lineage B cavefish have 11 and are body-compressed along the anteroposterior axis.
- The difference was statistically significant (χ² test, p < 0.001), linking mtDNA lineage to vertebral number. This is the first quantitative link between axial skeleton changes and cave-associated body shape.

**Interpretation**
- Two scenarios are proposed: (1) multiple independent origins, with lineage B and the Subterráneo haplotype supporting this; (2) introgressive hybridization from surface populations, particularly for lineage A caves such as Chica and Subterráneo.
- Within the Subterráneo cave, 6 of 9 intermediate-morphotype individuals carried the diagnostic A15 haplotype, suggesting hybridization mainly between hypogean females and epigean males.
- Pachón cavefish show a conflict: their extreme regression is inconsistent with a recent origin implied by their lineage A haplotype. The authors suggest that hybridization may have affected earlier results, or that Pachón evolved more rapidly.
- The authors conclude that *A. mexicanus* is a strong model for studying the genetic basis of eye and pigment degeneration, and recommend nuclear-gene analysis and detailed phylogeographic work to resolve the origins.
