## Executive Summary
This document is a regulatory and management record concerning the reinitiation of formal Endangered Species Act consultation between the Tonto National Forest and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regarding the **Dos S Unit Sunflower Allotment Management Plan**. The core biological subject is the **Mud Springs Gila Topminnow Habitat Improvement Project**, aimed at protecting a population of the federally listed [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from livestock grazing impacts. The amendment proposes expanding a habitat exclosure, removing livestock access to a critical drinker containing the fish population, and establishing a maintenance protocol for the water delivery infrastructure (springbox, pipe, drinker) to ensure the species' long-term survival in an isolated, artificial pothole/drinker ecosystem.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Gila Topminnow]] population discussed occupies a highly specific and vulnerable **anthropogenic/spring-fed microhabitat** rather than a natural stream reach.

- **Primary Habitat:** The population resides within **potholes** and an **existing drinker** system fed by a natural spring (Mud Springs) via a springbox and pipe infrastructure.
- **Habitat Type:** Desert spring/wetland characterized by cattails, indicating a slow-moving or standing water riparian ecosystem typical of the [[Sonoran Desert]] aquatic refugia.
- **Water Dependency:** The species is entirely dependent on continuous water flow from the spring; failure of the "drinker" (an artificial water catchment) poses a direct extinction risk to this isolated population.
- **Threats/Interactions:**
  - **Livestock Grazing:** Cattle access to the drinker and springs poses a risk of habitat degradation (trampling, water fouling) and direct conflict with the fish habitat.
  - **Habitat Fragmentation:** The population is isolated within a small pothole/drinker system, making it susceptible to stochastic events (e.g., mechanical failure of water delivery).
- **Conservation Strategy:** Management involves creating a livestock exclosure to eliminate grazing pressure while providing an alternative water source (a new drinker outside the exclosure) to redirect cattle, thereby protecting the [[Aquatic Refugia]] status of the spring.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Action:** The Tonto National Forest requested a reinitiation of formal consultation under the Endangered Species Act to amend a 1994 Biological Opinion (File No. 2-21-92-F-213) due to changes in the habitat improvement project design.
- **Exclosure Expansion:** The management plan calls for significantly enlarging the livestock exclosure to fully encompass the drinkers containing the Gila Topminnow population, eliminating livestock access entirely to this critical habitat.
- **Infrastructure Mitigation:** A new springbox and drinker will be installed *outside* the exclosure to provide livestock with an alternative water source, explicitly excluding the introduction of Gila Topminnow into this new, unprotected drinker.
- **Maintenance Protocol:** A four-year maintenance window is established for the existing drinker following initial fish stocking. Maintenance (e.g., clearing pipes, cleaning springboxes) is permitted to ensure water supply continuity for the fish, despite potential stress to the population.
- **Adaptive Management Trigger:** After four years, if the population is *stable and reproducing*, intensive maintenance will be scaled back (pending USFWS/AGFD concurrence). If the population is *unstable or unreproducing*, major reconstruction of the water delivery system will be triggered to ensure long-term water supply.
- **Effects Determination:**
  - The exclosure expansion and removal of livestock access were determined to have **"No Effect"** on the Gila Topminnow population.
  - Drinker maintenance activities were determined to **"May Adversely Affect"** the species due to potential "take" of individuals during infrastructure work, though the *net effect* of maintenance is deemed positive for population persistence.
- **Monitoring Recommendation:** The Forest Service requested that long-term fish population monitoring (beyond the initial 6 years) be shifted from mandatory "Terms and Conditions" to a voluntary "Conservation Recommendation," citing that fish population management falls outside the agency's primary jurisdiction (deferring to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and USFWS).
- **Interagency Consultation:** The project involved consultation with fisheries biologists from USFWS and the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), alongside Forest Service hydrologists, soil scientists, and riparian specialists, highlighting a multi-disciplinary approach to managing this single-species refugium.
