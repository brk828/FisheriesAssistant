## Executive Summary

This 1984 inter-office memo from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Nongame Branch) is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper but a **conceptual management framework** for the entire native ichthyofauna of Arizona. Biologist Jim Brooks outlines species-specific conservation, monitoring, and reintroduction goals for approximately 30 native fish taxa spanning multiple families (trout, chubs, dace, suckers, pupfish, topminnow, catfish, and several marine-derived/estuarine strays). The document also codifies a **Priority Ranking System (A1, A2, B1, B2, B3, C, D, X)** used to triage management attention based on taxonomic distinctness, distribution, degree of protection, and vulnerability to extirpation. The source is valuable primarily as a historical snapshot of pre-ESA-era (and early-ESA) conservation strategy for the [[Gila River]] and [[Colorado River]] native fish faunas, rather than as a source of anatomical or physiological data.

## Background & Description

The memo contains **no detailed morphological descriptions** (no meristics, coloration, body plan, or anatomical data) for any species, consistent with its nature as a management planning document rather than a taxonomic or descriptive study. However, several taxonomic notes relevant to species delineation are embedded in the management goals:

- **[[Gila Chub]]**: Management action explicitly contingent on ongoing taxonomic research determining whether it constitutes a "good" (valid) species.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]**: Introduction potential is likewise dependent on taxonomic research (conducted by Arizona State University) clarifying species/subspecies boundaries within the *Gila robusta* complex, including the [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]] population under consideration for federal listing.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Flagged as taxonomically unresolved — the memo anticipates that future work will split populations into "several new subspecies of *Rhinichthys*," which would substantially increase management obligations.
- Priority Code criteria (Appendix, Pages 5–6) formally incorporate taxonomic distinctness ("full species or clearly defined subspecies") as a primary axis for ranking conservation concern (see A1 and A2 codes).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Species are distributed across several major Arizona drainage basins, each with distinct management contexts:

- **Colorado River mainstem & tributaries**: [[Humpback Chub]] (Colorado & Little Colorado River), [[Bonytail Chub]] (Lake Mohave), [[Razorback Sucker]] (Lake Mohave; targeted for Gila River drainage reintroduction), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (extirpated, targeted for reintroduction with radio telemetry study of adult movement and microhabitat use), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]].
- **Little Colorado River drainage**: [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], [[Little Colorado River Sucker]].
- **Virgin River drainage**: [[Virgin River Spinedace]], [[Woundfin]], and the [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]] population.
- **Gila River drainage (upper basin/tributaries)**: [[Arizona Trout]] (barrier construction and reintroduction), [[Gila Trout]] (Gap Creek population), [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Sucker]], [[Gila Mountain Sucker]], [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] (Aravaipa Creek, subject of water-rights acquisition), [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] (Nutrioso Creek), [[Rio Grande Mountain Sucker]].
- **Rio Yaqui drainage (Sonoran border streams)**: [[Yaqui Chub]] (Leslie Creek), [[Sonoran Chub]] (Sycamore Canyon; threatened by introduced green sunfish and 1983 flood impacts), [[Yaqui Shiner]] (Leslie Creek, introduced population; brood stock from Mexico planned for San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge), [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (Rucker Canyon), [[Yaqui Sucker]] and [[Yaqui Catfish]] (San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge broodstock program).
- **Desert springs/relict habitats**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (Boyce Thompson Arboretum refuge stock), [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, coordinated with National Park Service), [[Gila Topminnow]] (ongoing introduction program on private and public lands).
- **Widespread/generalist species**: [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Speckled Dace]] are noted as broadly distributed and resilient, requiring only general distributional monitoring rather than intensive management.
- **Euryhaline/estuarine strays**: [[Pacific Tenpounder]], [[Striped Mullet]], and [[Spotted Sleeper]] are listed with "none recommended," indicating peripheral or non-priority status in Arizona's freshwater/estuarine transition zones (likely lower Colorado River delta influence).

Threats identified include **exotic warmwater species introductions** (smallmouth bass, sunfish, catfish) impacting [[Roundtail Chub]] populations, and **green sunfish predation/competition** threatening [[Sonoran Chub]] in Sycamore Canyon.

## Key Findings

- **Reintroduction**: Multiple taxa targeted for reintroduction into historic range (Arizona Trout, Gila Trout, Razorback Sucker, Colorado Squawfish, Yaqui Shiner); dependent on establishment of broodstock programs and removal/control of competing/predatory exotics.
- **Water acquisition**: Aravaipa Creek identified as critical habitat requiring land/water-rights acquisition to secure base flow for Spikedace and Loach Minnow populations.
- **Taxonomic contingencies**: Management of Gila Chub, Roundtail Chub, and Speckled Dace tied to resolution of ongoing taxonomic research, with anticipated designation of new subspecies or revision of species boundaries.
- **Refuge populations**: Desert Pupfish, Quitobaquito Pupfish, and Gila Topminnow depend on maintenance of refuge populations (captive or semi-natural) as insurance against field extirpation.
