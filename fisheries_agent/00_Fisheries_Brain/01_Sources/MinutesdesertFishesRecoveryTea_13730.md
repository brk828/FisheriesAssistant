# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes April 1986

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

Minutes from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting held April 17-18, 1986 in Tucson, Arizona. The team, led by W.L. Minckley of [[Arizona State University]], reviewed recovery efforts for multiple endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River Basin]] and surrounding drainages. Key discussions included hatchery production at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], reintroduction programs for [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Desert Pupfish]], and coordination between federal and state agencies. The meeting addressed both upper and lower basin recovery efforts, listing status of various species, and development of experimental population introductions on [[Bureau of Land Management]] lands.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Team comprised representatives from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], and academic institutions. The meeting addressed recovery efforts for multiple endangered fish species native to desert aquatic systems in the southwestern United States.

**Species Status Discussed:**

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): 9 million eggs collected spring 1986 with 55% viability; 1 million fry distributed to California and 100,000 to [[Page Springs Hatchery]]. Channel catfish and flathead catfish identified as major predators of stocked fingerlings.

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): 440 subadults stocked in [[Verde River]] late summer/early fall 1985 with successful recaptures documented.

- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*): One specimen captured from [[Lake Mohave]], possibly from 1984-85 stocking. [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] identified as suitable for rearing and refugium populations. Upper basin populations believed genetically swamped with potential extinction listing.

- **[[Woundfin]]**: 589 collected February 1986 from [[Virgin River]] but all died from ich infection. Approximately 1,400 produced in 1985 using gravel-filled boxes; 200 survived winter. Discovery of [[Red Shiner]]s above [[Virgin River Gorge]] adds urgency to recovery efforts.

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*): 33-34 reintroduction sites occupied during 1985, though many sites marginal. Additional wild stock to be obtained from Sharp Spring. Model being developed for stocking site selection.

- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*): Good numbers available at Dexter (Santa Clara Slough stock). No wild California stocks remain, only refugium populations. [[Quitobaquito]] and [[Boyce Thompson]] populations doing well. [[Bog Hole]] stock to be eliminated and replaced with Santa Clara Slough stock.

- **[[Beautiful Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella formosa*): Hatchery stock cleared of tapeworms. Western form needed for restocking [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]].

- **[[Sonoran Chub]]**: Habitat fairly secure but extremely limited, may never be delisted. Additional inventory work needed in [[Sycamore Creek]] drainage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recovery efforts occur within context of multiple threats including habitat loss, exotic species introductions, predation, and water management. [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed recently acquired by federal ownership through land exchange; stock tanks identified as source of exotic species (green sunfish, fathead minnows, gambusia) requiring chemical treatment.

Upper [[Colorado River Basin]] research on juvenile fish use of backwaters may provide valuable information for lower basin recovery. Region 6 Recovery Implementation Plan designed to effect recovery of [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] within 10 years at estimated cost of $36 million.

Genetic work by Buth indicates *Gila intermedia* is distinct from *Gila robusta*. US/Mexico cooperative agreement drafted between [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[University of Nuevo Leon]] to share fish for research and recovery purposes.

## Key Findings

**Hatchery Operations and Production:**
- [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] produced 9 million [[Razorback Sucker]] eggs (55% viable) in spring 1986
- 1 million [[Razorback Sucker]] fry distributed to California, 100,000 to Arizona
- Approximately 1,400 [[Woundfin]] produced in 1985; 200 survived winter
- Culture techniques for [[Woundfin]] require further development
- Team recommended committee be appointed to oversee Dexter NFH operations and set goals/objectives

**Reintroduction and Stocking Programs:**
- 440 [[Colorado Squawfish]] subadults successfully holding in [[Verde River]] with documented recaptures
- 33-34 [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction sites occupied, though many marginal
- Successful [[Gila Topminnow]] introductions at People Canyon and Tres Alamos
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to prepare experimental population rule for stocking topminnows into approximately 30 sites on [[Safford BLM District]] lands
- First [[Razorback Sucker]] stocking into [[San Juan River]] planned for summer 1986

**Species Listing Status:**
- *Lepidomedia vittata* listing package in Washington
- *Tiaroga cobitis* listing package in Washington; 1-year delay requested for critical habitat designation
- *Meda fulgida* listing package in Washington; 1-year delay requested for critical habitat designation
- No official movement to list [[Razorback Sucker]] in upper basin
- Upper basin [[Bonytail Chub]] may be listed as extinct due to genetic swamping

**Threats and Management Issues:**
- Channel catfish and flathead catfish identified as major predators of stocked [[Razorback Sucker]] fingerlings
- [[Red Shiner]]s discovered above [[Virgin River Gorge]], threatening [[Woundfin]] populations
- Exotic species (green sunfish, fathead minnows, gambusia) found in [[Aravaipa Creek]] originating from stock tanks
- All stock tanks in [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed require survey and chemical treatment for exotic removal

**Action Items Assigned:**
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to define fish culture and production priorities for Dexter NFH
- [[U.S. Forest Service]] to initiate paperwork for draining and chemical treatment of [[Bog Hole]]
- Team to request Region 2 FWS be given lead for [[Desert Pupfish]] recovery
- Draft team letter concerning California plans for [[Razorback Sucker]] introductions
- Coordinate moving stonerollers from Rucker Canyon to [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]
- Draft biology section of [[Sonoran Chub]] recovery plan
- Review technical draft of [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]] (comments due May 15)
- Assist in drafting biological opinion for [[Glen Canyon]] study

**Interagency Coordination:**
- Land exchange proposal involving [[Chihuahua Chub]] habitat on [[Mimbres River]]
- [[Bureau of Land Management]] land exchange completed for [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed
- Cooperative agreement drafted between US and Mexico for fish research and recovery
- Arizona/New Mexico [[Spikedace]]-[[Red Shiner]] study proposal to be presented to Arizona Game and Fish Commission April 25-26

Next meeting scheduled for last week of July at either Pinetop, Arizona or Silver City, New Mexico.
