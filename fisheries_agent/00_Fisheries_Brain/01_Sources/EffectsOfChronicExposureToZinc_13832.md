# Chronic Zinc Exposure and the Toxicity of a Detergent–Zinc Mixture to Rainbow Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 laboratory study by Brown, Mitrovic and Stark examined how 100 days of chronic exposure to [[Zinc]] (0.8 mg Zn/l) alters the acute toxicity of a soft [[Alkyl Benzene Sulphonate]] (ABS) detergent in [[Rainbow Trout]]. Zinc-exposed fish were more sensitive to the detergent–zinc mixture than controls, and the two poisons acted approximately additively. Zinc exposure did not induce the acquired resistance to zinc that had been observed in acute zinc poisoning. Gill histopathology showed that detergent progressively destroyed gill tissue, from swelling and thickening of filaments to loss of the secondary lamellar epithelium and fusion of pillar cells.

## Background & Description

Poisons rarely occur singly in polluted waters. Synthetic detergents and metals such as zinc are both widespread in polluted rivers, which motivates studies of combined toxicity. The authors built on earlier work: [[Marchetti Review (1965)]] reported lethal ABS concentrations of 0.25 to 26.0 mg/l for freshwater fish, with toxicity rising with alkyl chain length, and [[Lloyd (1960)]] described zinc toxicity to rainbow trout.

The study used rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdnerii*), a species whose gill anatomy is well characterised. The gill consists of four arched bars per side, bearing primary filaments with secondary lamellae that form the respiratory surfaces. Each secondary lamella is supported by [[Pillar Cells]] and is covered by a single-cell-thick epithelium in trout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Experimental setup**
- 240 rainbow trout (5.0–6.5 cm fork length) randomly divided into two groups of 120: zinc-exposed (0.8 mg Zn/l for 100 days) and controls (zinc below 0.05 mg/l).
- Fish held at 40 per tank in glass-fibre tanks with continuous flow (1 l/min). Test volume was reduced to 40 l to limit retention time and detergent biodegradation.
- Dilution water was hard bore-hole water (total hardness 290 mg/l as CaCO3; alkalinity 230 mg/l as CaCO3). Oxygen was kept near air saturation and pH was controlled at 7.5 during tests.
- Water temperature was uncontrolled, ranging from 11.8 to 16.8 °C.
- Detergent (a new ABS, 55% active ingredient) was tested at 0.18, 0.30, 0.42 and 0.65 mg active material/l as [[Manoxol OT]]. Ten fish per concentration. Detergent was measured by the [[Methylene-Blue Method]] and zinc by adsorption spectrometry.

**Fish condition**
- Fish roughly doubled in length during the 100-day pre-test period.
- Mean condition factors (K) were 1.37 for zinc-exposed fish and 1.32 for controls; the difference in weight was not significant, so groups were treated as comparable.

## Key Findings

**Toxicity results**
- Zinc-exposed fish were more sensitive to the mixture. Their LC50 values were about 37% lower than controls at all times.
- For the mixture, zinc-exposed fish showed 86% (48 h) and 95% (72 h) additive fractions; controls showed 116% and 119%. The poisons were therefore approximately additive.
- Zinc exposure did not change sensitivity to detergent alone. Two-day and three-day LC50 values were similar between groups.

**LC50 values (mg detergent/l as Manoxol OT)**

| Pretreatment | Test | 2-day | 3-day | 5-day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zinc-exposed | Detergent + zinc | 0.34 | 0.33 | 0.25 |
| Control | Detergent alone | 0.56 | 0.50 | 0.40 |
| Zinc-exposed | Detergent alone | 0.59 | 0.50 | – |
| Control | Detergent + zinc | 0.49 | 0.45 | – |

**Gill histopathology (47 fish examined)**
- Normal and control gills showed intact secondary lamellae. Zinc-exposed gills had thinner lamellae and some displaced epithelium before testing.
- At 0.18 and 0.30 mg/l, controls and zinc-exposed fish both showed thickened epithelium and cell proliferation at the lamellar bases. Damage was more severe in zinc-exposed fish at 0.30 mg/l, where the interlamellar space was occluded in places.
- At 0.42 mg/l, epithelial walls were disrupted, and zinc-exposed fish showed larger areas of destruction.
- At 0.65 mg/l, both groups showed extensive epithelial loss, fused pillar cells and blood clots. All zinc-exposed fish died within 48 h, compared with 60% of controls.

**Mechanism and interpretation**
- Damage followed a consistent sequence: swelling, then thickening, then epithelial destruction, pillar-cell fusion and loss of mucous cells. This matches [[Dodecylbenzene Sulphonate]] effects described by [[Schmid and Mann (1961)]].
- Zinc enhanced lower-concentration damage, mainly through greater cell proliferation at the lamellar bases. At higher concentrations, detergent damage masked the zinc effect.
- The authors concluded that histological severity broadly matched toxicity. They also cautioned that histopathology is only informative when large gill areas are examined, and that gill abnormalities can occur in apparently normal fish.
- Detergent in rivers often derives from treated effluents, whose degradation products may differ in toxicity from the parent compound.
