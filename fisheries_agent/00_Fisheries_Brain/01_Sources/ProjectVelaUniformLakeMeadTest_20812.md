# Lake Mead Test Explosions - Project Vela Uniform June 1962

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Centrarchidae #impact-assessment #fish-mortality

## Executive Summary

In June 1962, the [[United States Geological Survey]] conducted nine underwater test explosions in [[Lake Mead]] as part of [[Project Vela Uniform]], a continuation of November 1961 tests. The explosions occurred approximately one mile north of [[Saddle Island]] in the [[Vegas Wash Arm]], in 200 feet of water. Two explosive types were tested: ammonium nitrate (3 shots) and U.S. Navy Composition B (6 shots), with Composition B yielding approximately 25% more force. The [[Nevada Fish and Game Department]] and [[Arizona Fish and Game Department]], assisted by the [[National Park Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and sportsmen, documented fish mortality. The most significant impact was mortality of young-of-year fish, with an estimated 1,000,583 juvenile gamefish killed (985,648 [[Crappie]], 14,935 [[Largemouth Bass]], 492,824 [[Threadfin Shad]]). Mature gamefish mortality was relatively small (411 fish, 287.28 pounds total). [[Channel Catfish]] showed increased mortality in later explosions due to their tendency to aggregate in blast areas to feed on dead fish.

## Background & Description

The test series consisted of nine underwater explosions conducted during June 1962 in [[Lake Mead]], Nevada-Arizona. All charges were detonated at the same location in approximately 200 feet of water depth, 1,800 yards from the southwest shore in the [[Vegas Wash Arm]].

**Explosive Types and Charges:**
- Shots 1-3 (June 8, 11, 12): Ammonium nitrate (6,000, 6,000, 4,000 lbs respectively)
- Shots 4-9 (June 14, 19, 20, 21, 22, 25): Composition B (4,000, 2,000, 2,000, 4,000, 6,000, 6,000 lbs respectively)

Composition B produced approximately 25% more force per unit weight than ammonium nitrate and generated a different shock wave type.

**Species Documented:**
- Gamefish: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Crappie]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Rainbow Trout]]
- Non-gamefish: [[Common Carp]], [[Threadfin Shad]]
- Incidental: 1 [[Eared Grebe]]

All mature gamefish were collected, weighed, and measured. Fish from seven of nine shots received individual length and weight measurements. Samples of [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Crappie]] were examined for maturity and spawning progress; many mature bass had not yet spawned.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The explosions occurred during the spawning and early recruitment season for warmwater gamefish in [[Lake Mead]]. Young [[Crappie]] ranged 1.0-2.0 inches in length, young [[Largemouth Bass]] 1.0-1.5 inches, and young [[Threadfin Shad]] were also present in significant numbers.

**Spatial Distribution Patterns:**
- Young [[Crappie]] exhibited pelagic behavior, moving freely in open water, making them more vulnerable to blast effects
- Young [[Largemouth Bass]] remained near shoreline at greater distances from blast area, resulting in lower mortality
- [[Channel Catfish]] actively moved into blast areas post-explosion to feed on dead fish on the bottom

**Temporal Patterns:**
Larger kills of [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Common Carp]] occurred when 2-3 days elapsed between shots, allowing time for fish to aggregate in the blast zone. The affected area diameter varied from 900 to 1,800 yards depending on charge size and type.

Only one mature [[Threadfin Shad]] was observed, suggesting adults were not concentrated in the blast area during this period.

**Other Mortality:**
- [[Common Carp]]: 1,810 individuals
- [[Eared Grebe]]: 1 individual

**Recommendations for Future Tests:**
1. Conduct all explosions at the same location
2. Concentrate explosions in shortest time period possible (multiple shots per day or consecutive days) to prevent [[Channel Catfish]] aggregation in blast zones
3. Schedule test explosions during late fall or winter months to:
   - Minimize young-of-year mortality
   - Avoid potential shock wave effects on warmwater gamefish eggs deposited along shorelines (shock waves may kill eggs even if insufficient to kill adult fish)
