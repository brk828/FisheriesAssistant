# Lake Mohave Razorback Sucker Monitoring Studies: Willow Beach NFH, March 1996

#ichthyology #freshwater-conservation #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This DRAFT report documents a multi-agency effort (March 9–16, 1996) to monitor, sample, and propagate the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in upper Lake Mohave, Colorado River. Objectives spanned three linked programs: (1) population monitoring and gamete collection from wild fish, (2) sperm cryopreservation research to refine freezing/thawing protocols and improve fertilization outcomes, and (3) hatchery propagation to augment captive broodstock and supply ~100,000 fry to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD). Electrofishing at putative spawning sites (debris-fan gravel bars near side canyons) captured 105 fish, revealing strong sex- and time-of-day-based capture biases suggestive of nocturnal spawning behavior. Cryopreservation trials achieved a 10-fold improvement in post-thaw sperm motility (up to 10–40%) using fast-freezing rates and caffeine additives, which also significantly boosted fertilization rates. Propagation efforts met the AGFD production quota and provided new genetic stock for the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] captive broodstock. The report raises concern that cold, fluctuating tailwater conditions below [[Hoover Dam]] may be impairing reproductive success of razorback sucker in this reach.

## Background & Description
While this report is primarily behavioral/reproductive and methodological rather than morphometric, several reproductive and physiological characteristics are documented:

- **Sex ratio in catch**: Males comprised 74% (n=78) of 105 fish captured; females comprised the remainder (n=27).
- **Male reproductive condition**: Of 78 males, 49 yielded sperm ("ripe") and 29 were spent. Of the 49 ripe males, 41 yielded ≥2 ml sperm, 31 yielded ≥5 ml, and 18 yielded ≥10 ml. Typical fully ripe males yield 10–30 ml sperm; only 23% of males met the ≥10 ml "fully ripe" threshold.
- **Female reproductive condition**: Of 27 females, 70% (n=19) were gravid, 1 was ripe, and 5 were spent — an unusually high proportion of gravid females late in the spawning season.
- **Sperm quality metrics**: High-quality sperm defined as >80% initial motility. Post-thaw motility varied by cryoprotectant, freezing rate, and additive (see Key Findings).
- **Recapture data**: 21% (n=22) of the 105 fish were recaptures; 3 within-season, 20 (19%) from previous years, indicating repeat use of specific spawning aggregation sites across years.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[Razorback Sucker]] aggregations were sampled at four discrete sites (river miles 61.5, 59.5, 58.5, 56.5) in upper [[Lake Mohave]], where small side canyons intersect the main river channel, forming debris-fan gravel bars. These sites are the only known locations between [[Hoover Dam]] and Willow Beach NFH where razorback sucker aggregations occur and are considered putative spawning sites, confirmed by direct spawning observations in 1994 and 1995.
- **Physical conditions**: Depths of 1–2 m with moderate to fast currents (0.3–1.0 m/s) characterize these spawning habitats.
- **Thermal regime**: Tailwater temperatures below Hoover Dam remain cold (~10°C), potentially suppressing oogenesis and delaying full reproductive readiness relative to warmer reaches, possibly selecting for cold-tolerant spawning phenotypes in upper Lake Mohave.
- **Diel behavior**: Capture data indicate males remain on/near spawning areas continuously (day and night), while females move onto spawning grounds predominantly at night (78% of female captures were nocturnal, vs. 55% of male captures), suggesting most spawning activity occurs at night.
- **Anthropogenic stressors**: Water level fluctuations from [[Hoover Dam]] operations were observed to dewater gravel bars used for spawning, representing a potential negative impact on reproductive success. The report recommends discharging warmer surface waters from Lake Mead and applying ramping rate limits during January–March to mitigate these impacts.
- **Comparative note**: The pattern of near-spent males/high gravid female proportion late in the season contrasts with patterns observed in [[Humpback Chub]] in Grand Canyon, where both sexes are typically spent by this stage.

## Key Findings

### Population Monitoring & Capture
- 105 razorback suckers captured over 10 electrofishing sessions (5 day, 5 night) using a Smith-Root SR-18 electrofishing boat (low range 50–500V DC, 30 pulses/s, 30% pulse width).
- 60% of total captures occurred at night, driven largely by female capture bias (78% female captures at night vs. 55% male).
- Arizona Bay support crew captured 103 razorback suckers at Owl Cove Reef (27% recaptures), yielding 18 sperm and 7 egg samples; assisted AGFD in processing 35 additional fish (16 sperm, 3 egg samples).

### Sperm Cryopreservation Research
- **Cryoprotectants**: 5% DMSO and 10% methanol had lowest toxicity and highest pre-freeze motility (75–90%); DMA and glycerin were more toxic (0–40% pre-freeze motility). Propylene glycol and ethylene glycol (5–10%) were ineffective (<1% post-thaw motility).
- **Equilibration time**: Soak times (1:30 to 30:00 min) had no effect on pre- or post-thaw motility across four cryoprotectants — no benefit to extended soaking.
- **Stimulants**: Addition of 0.005 M caffeine increased pre-freeze and post-thaw motility by 5–50%.
- **Extracellular protectants**: Sucrose, glucose, mannitol (5%, 10%) with 10% methanol showed no effect on post-thaw motility.
- **Freezing rate**: Standard protocol (~1°C/s) yielded 1–5% post-thaw motility; faster freezing (>1.6°C/s, via direct dewar drop) nearly doubled the rate and yielded 10–30% post-thaw motility. Direct immersion in liquid nitrogen was too fast, causing straws to explode.
- **Thawing rate**: Highest motility achieved with 30–40°C water bath thawing; optimal protocol was 40°C water bath swirl for 7 seconds (vs. slower room-temperature thawing at ~18°C).

### Fertilization Trials
- **Experiment 1 (sperm dosage)**: Using slow-frozen sperm (low motility, <10%), fertilization rates increased with sperm dose, up to 5% with five 0.5 ml straws.
- **Experiment 2 (caffeine additive)**: Fast-frozen sperm (10–40% motility, mean 24.5%) treated with caffeine achieved 14.5% mean fertilization vs. 3.5% untreated (60% of the 24.8% control rate) — caffeine improved fertilization despite no motility effect.
- **Experiment 3 (fast vs. slow freezing)**: Fast-frozen sperm (15–25% post-thaw motility) achieved 27% mean fertilization vs. 3.8% for slow-frozen sperm (≤5% motility); controls averaged 41.5%.
- **Conclusion**: Fertilization rate correlates with post-thaw motility; caffeine addition can independently boost fertilization success. Further experimental refinement recommended.

### Propagation
- 37 total crosses performed following [[Gorman and Carmichael 1996]] protocols.
- **Broodstock augmentation**: 16 unique crosses using fresh gametes from wild fish at upper Lake Mohave (6 pairs), Yuma Cove (4 pairs), and Jeff Davis Cove (4 pairs) produced several thousand progeny destined for Dexter NFH.
- **Hormone induction**: 16 gravid females held at WBNFH were injected intramuscularly with 675 IU/kg HCG every 24 hrs over 3 days (per Hamman 1981) to induce ovulation; many produced immature ("green") infertile eggs, and some females shed eggs spontaneously 48–72 hrs post-treatment.
- **Cryopreserved sperm progeny**: Crosses using cryopreserved vs. fresh (control) sperm were incubated in Zip-Lock bag microhatcheries; resulting fish retained at WBNFH for future fitness evaluation.
- **Production quota**: ~100,000 fry hatched (primarily in Heath trays) and delivered to Arizona Game and Fish Department, meeting the study's production target.
- Additionally, WBNFH reared >60,000 wild-caught larvae for stocking into Lake Mohave via the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (reported separately).

### Management Implications
- High proportion of gravid females (70%) late in spawning season alongside largely spent males suggests reproductive timing mismatch, potentially linked to cold Hoover Dam tailwater temperatures (~10°C) suppressing oogenesis.
- Water level fluctuations dewater spawning gravel bars, posing a physical threat to spawning success.
- Recommended dam operation changes: discharge warmer surface water from Lake Mead, limit daily fluctuations, and apply ramping rates during January–March spawning season.
- Facility upgrades (new dewars, microscopes, warm-water raceways) position WBNFH for expanded cryopreservation and propagation programs, including establishment of a cryo-repository and rearing capacity for [[Bonytail Chub]] alongside razorback sucker.
