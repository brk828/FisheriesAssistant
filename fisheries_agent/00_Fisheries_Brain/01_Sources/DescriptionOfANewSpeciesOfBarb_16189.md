# Barbus (Enteromius) guildi sp. nov. from the Hedjo River, West Africa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 taxonomic paper by [[Paul V. Loiselle]] describes *Barbus (Enteromius) guildi* sp. nov., a small cyprinid from the headwaters of the [[Hedjo River]] in Ghana and Togo. The species was first set aside as possible hybrids between two sympatric congeners, [[Barbus Macrops]] and [[Barbus Parablabes]]. Later collections showed sizeable populations with evidence of behaving as a biological species, which justified formal description. The species is named after [[Paul D. Guild]], a fellow [[Peace Corps]] volunteer. The author discusses its affinities within the West African subgenus [[Enteromius]] and tests three hypotheses for its origin, one of which is a hybrid origin involving [[Barbus Callipterus]].

## Background & Description

**Context.** The fish were collected during a faunal survey of Togo's freshwaters (1966–1969) and further collected in Ghanaian headwaters in 1969–1970. All early specimens were juveniles under 30.0 mm standard length. The author worked as Staff Ichthyologist for the [[Volta Lake Research Project]] at Akosombo, Ghana, where most of the laboratory work was done.

**Diagnosis.** A small *Barbus* in the subgenus *Enteromius* (sensu Greenwood 1970) with:
- Moderately developed barbels and a complete lateral line.
- A longitudinal row of dark bow-shaped markings along the midlateral line, combined with a narrow midlateral stripe on the posterior flanks.
- A diffuse dusky area in the distal dorsal fin.
- A bright red crescentic marking in the upper iris.

**Type material.**
- Type series: 12 fish, 41.0–56.0 mm standard length, from the Hedjo headwaters at Gbota, about 20 km northeast of Ho.
- Holotype: adult male, 49.0 mm SL, U.S.N.M. 205-635, collected 3 October 1970 by Loiselle, Goertz and Hackett.
- Allotype: adult female, 48.0 mm SL, U.S.N.M. 205-636.
- Paratypes: deposited at U.S.N.M. 205-637 and the British Museum (Natural History), 1971.9.13.

**Morphology.**
- Body moderately compressed. Maximum depth 3.0–3.6 times in SL; head length 3.2–3.7 times in SL.
- Subterminal mouth with two oral barbels. The posterior barbel reaches just past the vertical diameter of the orbit.
- Fin formulae: dorsal III, 8; anal III, 5.
- Lateral line scales: 22–25 (mode 24).
- Ten scale rows around the caudal peduncle.
- Enteromius-type pit-lines fully developed on the snout, cheek, operculum and dorsal interorbital region.

**Coloration.** In living fish the back is light, the flanks silver and the venter white. A dusky median stripe is more evident in males. The first and second dorsal rays are black, and the rest of the dorsal and caudal fins are dull rosy orange. The iris has a bright red crescent. Formalin-fixed specimens show dense dorsal pigment and very dark bow-shaped markings on the lateral-line scales.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** *B. guildi* appears restricted to the upper Hedjo River flowing under intact Chlorophora-Antiaris forest. This covers the eastern Volta Region of Ghana and the Palime Circumscription of Togo. Extensive collecting failed to find it in:
- Comparable habitats in the coastal drainages of Togo (Mono, Haho and Zio basins).
- The Volta basin in Ghana or Togo.
- Rivers west of the Volta basin in Ghana.

**Habitat preference.** The species occurs in relatively quiet water over fine gravel or organic-rich substrata. In swifter water over coarser substrata it is replaced by [[Barbus Parablabes]] and [[Barbus Sublineatus]].

**Ecology and feeding.** Fish live in aggregations of 10–30 individuals of similar size. It is a predominantly bottom-feeding omnivore. Gut contents of adults included fragmentary insect remains, leafy tissue, empty diatom shells, filamentous green algae, silica granules and undifferentiated organic matter. It occasionally takes terrestrial insects.

**Reproduction.** Ripe females and juveniles under 30.0 mm were captured from August to early October, late in the rainy season. Spawning therefore appears limited to this period. Congeners such as [[Barbus Parablabes]], riverine [[Barbus Macrops]] and [[Barbus Callipterus]] appear to spawn earlier, in the first part of the rainy season. Females grow somewhat larger than males and are deeper-bodied anteriorly. No other sexual dimorphism was noted.

## Key Findings

1. **New species.** *Barbus (Enteromius) guildi* is formally described from 12 type specimens, with a holotype of 49.0 mm SL.
2. **Meristic overlap.** Five West African *Enteromius* species (excluding the highly modified *B. sylvaticus*) are so similar in meristic characters that these cannot separate them reliably. Morphometrics and general facies are more useful.
3. **Closest affinities.** Based on morphometrics, facies, barbel development and color pattern, *B. guildi* is most closely related to [[Barbus Ablabes]], [[Barbus Macrops]] and [[Barbus Callipterus]]. All share silvery flanks, rosy-orange fins and a red iris crescent.
4. **Diagnostic character.** *B. guildi* has ten scale rows around the caudal peduncle, compared with twelve in *B. macrops* and *B. callipterus*. Its spawning season also differs.
5. **Restricted range.** The species is endemic to the upper Hedjo and its forest-gallery habitat. This is a puzzling distribution given the basin is under 150 km long.
6. **Origin hypotheses.** Three explanations were considered:
   - A relict *B. callipterus* population that survived in forest refugia after the formation of the [[Togo-Dahomey Gap]].
   - Introgressive hybridization between *B. callipterus* and *B. macrops*, triggered by recent deforestation.
   - Other scenarios, including dispersal routes.
   
   The author judged the second hypothesis to pose the fewest obstacles, applying [[Occam's Razor]]. However, none fully explained the endemic distinct phenotype.
7. **Evidence against recent hybrid origin.**
   - Reproductive syntopy and synchrony are not demonstrated. Forest species like *B. callipterus* appear to spawn in headwaters and permanent creeks, while savannah species like *B. macrops* use flooded oxbows.
   - No hybrid zone or hybrid-swarm variability is present in the Hedjo basin.
   - Parental populations show no detectable introgression.
   - Usual hybrid-zone signatures, as described by [[Hybridization]] studies, are absent.
8. **Future work.** The author suggested [[Electrophoresis]] of West African enteromiid barbs as a way to resolve the origin question with additional genetic data.
