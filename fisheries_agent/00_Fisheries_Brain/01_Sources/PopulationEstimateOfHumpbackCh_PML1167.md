# Population Estimate of Humpback Chub in Westwater Canyon, Colorado River, Utah (FY 2005 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This FY 2005 annual project report from the [[Upper Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 132) describes the third and final year of a three-year population estimate for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Westwater Canyon]], Colorado River, Utah. The study was run by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (Moab Field Station) and supports recovery goals that call for maintaining humpback chub populations in several locations. Three sampling trips in late September through October 2005 used [[Trammel Netting]] and [[Electrofishing]] at four sites. Trammel nets caught the most adult humpback chub, and electrofishing proved productive for juveniles. Recaptures were used to support population estimation. The project is marked as ongoing, with the third year completed and 80% of the FY 2005 budget work done.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program recently set recovery goals for the endangered humpback chub. These goals depend partly on keeping populations in several locations, including the Westwater Canyon population on the [[Colorado River]]. Identifying, maintaining, and monitoring these populations requires accurate population estimates.

The report lists two objectives:
1. Estimate the population of adult humpback chub (>200 mm) in Westwater Canyon.
2. Determine the mean estimated recruitment of naturally produced subadult humpback chub (150–199 mm).

The study ran from 2003 to 2005 and was tied to the Colorado River Action Plan (Mainstem, sections V.C and V.C.2 on estimating humpback chub populations). Three native Gila species were handled: humpback chub, [[Roundtail Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]. Fish identified only as "Gila" were either too small to identify reliably in the field or showed characteristics of more than one species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling sites** (river mile, RM):
- Miners Cabin (RM 124)
- Upper Cougar (RM 122.5)
- Little Hole (RM 121.5)
- Hades Bar (RM 120)

**Flow and temperature:** Sampling was done under variable river conditions. Mean daily flow by pass, from the USGS gage near the Colorado–Utah state line, was:
- Pass 1: 5,136 cfs (range 3,740–6,400 cfs); mean temperature 17 °C (16–18 °C)
- Pass 2: 4,743 cfs (4,280–5,300 cfs); mean temperature 13 °C (12.5–14 °C)
- Pass 3: 4,461 cfs (4,220–4,900 cfs); mean temperature 12 °C (13–15 °C per source)

**Debris:** A storm during the first pass left the river heavily debris-laden. Trammel netting was suspended for several days, and electrofishing alone was used until the river cleared on day four. The source notes that the sampling methods were adjusted to conditions in the field.

**Sampling protocol:** Two nights were sampled at each site on each pass, for seven nights in total. Eight to nine trammel nets were set per site, depending on habitat and how quickly fish could be removed. Fish were identified to species, scanned for PIT tags, PIT tagged if needed, measured (total and standard length, mm), weighed, and had their dorsal and anal fin rays counted before release. Hoop nets were not used in 2005 because of poor past catch rates.

## Key Findings

**Catch totals (2005):**
- Adult humpback chub: 292
- Adult roundtail chub: 763
- Adult Gila with intermediate characteristics: 12
- Bonytail: 8
- Subadult humpback chub: 8
- Subadult roundtail chub: 138
- Subadult Gila: 55
- Gila less than 150 mm TL: 203

**Size ranges:**
- Humpback chub, trammel nets: mean total length 273.6 mm (range 182–386 mm)
- Humpback chub, electrofishing: mean 254.5 mm (range 165–350 mm)
- Roundtail chub, trammel nets: mean 265 mm (range 177–476 mm)
- Roundtail chub, electrofishing: range 179–379 mm (mean illegible in the source)
- Most chub under 150 mm TL were collected by electrofishing.

**Effort:** Electrofishing was conducted on all three passes in 2005, compared with one pass in 2003 and 2004. Electrofishing totaled 30.1 hours. Trammel netting effort is reported as "1,142.4 hours" in the source, but the figure is garbled by OCR, so the reading is uncertain.

**Catch efficiency:**
- Trammel netting produced twice as many adult humpback chub as electrofishing over the three passes.
- Trammel-net CPUE for humpback chub was 0.168 (2003), 0.164 (2004), and 0.176 (2005). Trammel-net CPUE was similar across years.
- Electrofishing CPUE in 2005 was about half that of 2003 and 2004, which the source describes as some of the highest ever recorded by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources.
- Electrofishing was productive in the afternoon for collecting juvenile chub, and the authors say it is the most productive method for those fish.

**Recaptures and tagging:**
- Humpback chub: 38 long-term recaptures (previously PIT-tagged fish) and 24 within-year recaptures in 2005.
- Roundtail chub: 40 long-term recaptures and 44 within-year recaptures in 2005.
- Long-term recapture rates in 2005 were similar to 1998–2004. Within-year recaptures of humpback chub were about double the 1998–2004 level, which the authors attribute possibly to increased sampling effort.
- Long-term recaptures have declined slightly since 2000 but remain similar to 1998 levels.

**Conclusions and recommendations:**
1. Electrofishing should be conducted on every pass.
2. Electrofishing should be done in the afternoon before nets are set and in the evening after nets are pulled.
3. Electrofishing should be used when the river is too debris-laden for trammel netting.
4. Radio telemetry of humpback and roundtail chub may help explain movement patterns that have not been documented.

**Project status and budget:**
- Status: Ongoing. Third year of three-year project completed.
- FY 2005 budget: $83,490 budgeted; $66,792 expended/obligated; $16,698 difference; 80% of FY 2005 work completed.
- Recovery Program funds spent on publication charges: $0.
- Data entry and transfer to USFWS were scheduled for "January 15, 2005" (as written in the source, which likely should read 2006).
- Report signed by Julie A. Jackson, dated 11/18/2005.
