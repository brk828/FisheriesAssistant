# Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program: Technical Committee Meeting Minutes (2 June 1999)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

These minutes record the June 2, 1999 meeting of the Technical Committee for the [[Lake Havasu]] Fisheries Improvement Program, held in Lake Havasu City, Arizona. Seven partner representatives from federal, state, local, and angler organizations attended. The committee reviewed habitat work, the proposed reintroduction of wakeless zones, a [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) stocking plan, the development of a native fish production facility, and long-term funding options. The main output was a plan to produce and stock [[Bonytail]] in time for release by summer 2000, with a draft plan document to be completed for review.

## Background & Description

The program is a partnership coordinated by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Lake Havasu office. Participants included representatives of the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AZG&F), the California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG), the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), and local angler groups. Kirk Koch of BLM chaired the meeting. The committee was guided by a task order from the Executive Committee to develop a conservation action plan leading to a recovery goal.

The meeting focused on three main workstreams:

- **Habitat enhancement** in the lake's coves, using structure designs and GIS-based mapping.
- **Wakeless zone restoration**, a permit-dependent effort run by CDFG.
- **Native fish recovery**, centered on [[Bonytail]] production at [[Dexter Hatchery]], [[Achii Hanyo]], and [[Niland]] facilities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat status**

- Habitat work now covers more than 560 acres in the lake.
- Work on over 100 acres has been ongoing for the fifth consecutive year.
- A dive team was scheduled to survey six coves on June 9–11, 1999, with a [[Geographic Information System]] (GIS) used to produce habitat maps.
- Structure designs were shown to the group.

**Wakeless zones**

- Wakeless buoys in coves had been installed previously (in 1995 or earlier, per the minutes) but did not last.
- Replacing them requires a permit from CDFG, and Coast Guard approval is also needed. Buoys were noted as a way to reduce navigation conflicts.
- Estimated cost per buoy was reported as under $1,000.

**Bonytail status**

- The minutes describe [[Bonytail]] as a species in need of recovery effort in the Colorado River system.
- Growth projections from the hatchery ponds guided stocking timing.
- The fish are kept in ponds with water temperatures monitored. Stocking is timed to avoid high summer water temperatures that increase mortality risk.

**Willow Beach**

- The committee discussed [[Willow Beach]] as a rearing or stocking option. Participants noted the site is dedicated to [[Mohave Tribe]] fisheries interests, which complicates use.

## Key Findings

### 1. Program structure and attendees

- Attendees: Dale Webster (AU), Bob Koeppe (AU), Brad Jacobson (AZG&F), Al Doelker (BLM), Kirk Koch (BLM), Chris Hayes (CDFG), Chuck Minckley (USFWS).
- Kirk Koch explained that the program is funded and managed through federal and state partnerships.
- Participants agreed that more research is needed. An academic partner could be brought in for a graduate thesis.

### 2. Wakeless zone reinstallation

- Bob Koeppe proposed reinstalling wakeless buoys in coves to protect riparian habitat and quality fishing.
- The program has no current funding for this work. The permit is under CDFG's name.
- Chris Hayes and Brad Jacobson noted that wakeless zones require Coast Guard and CDFG permits and could affect navigation. Moorings were suggested to reduce conflicts.
- Chris Hayes pledged to check permit status and operations budget, and cost effectiveness was questioned.

### 3. Habitat accomplishments and planning

- Structure designs were presented, and GIS-based mapping was planned.
- Work continued on more than 100 acres for a fifth consecutive year.
- A cultural resources planning effort (CA) was introduced, with BLM researching issues of importance to tribes and the Refuge Manager.
- Black Rock progress was reported, including local efforts toward a sponsorship and land acquisition program.
- The Bluebird proposal includes easements across city land, with an access road and a work camp planned. Construction support was expected to start in early July.

### 4. Bonytail stocking plan

- Chuck Minckley reported that he had requested 30,000 [[Bonytail]] from [[Dexter Hatchery]] for Havasu. The request had not been answered after a year.
- He argued that [[Achii Hanyo]] could be adapted to produce fish successfully, but vegetation needs to be treated and water run through the ponds first.
- Plan timeline:
  - Hatchery fish stocked from [[Willow Beach]] and [[Dexter Hatchery]] once water drops below 20°C in late winter or spring.
  - Fish grown out through the year to reach target size by summer 2000.
  - Tagging and release into the lake before summer water temperatures raise mortality risk.
- Hatchery operations at [[Achii Hanyo]] were estimated to cost about $100,000 per year.
- Chuck Minckley estimated that at least 10,000 fish should be produced at [[Niland]] and [[Achii Hanyo]]. He noted the Dexter manager was reluctant to grow more [[Bonytail]] because of inadequate facility infrastructure.
- Chuck Minckley advertised for volunteer support with little response.
- Fish for [[Niland]] stocking are to be transferred from the Dexter shipment. A native fish specialist is being hired, with the position expected to be filled around June 24.
- Early October shipment from Dexter was the target for preparing facilities.

### 5. Funding and sustainability

- Brad Jacobson said short-term and long-term efforts are both needed. Kirk Koch noted the Havasu goal currently has a four-year time frame.
- Bob Koeppe urged inclusion of all production costs in native fish projections and proposed a maintenance fund to protect improvements over time.
- He suggested raising the river stamp fee by one dollar to fund this purpose, arguing anglers would see it as well spent. Brad Jacobson considered the idea feasible but noted such fee rules rarely change.
- Chris Hayes asked why anyone would expect the improvements to go unmaintained.

### 6. Outreach and partnerships

- Bob Koeppe said the issue needs marketing to build public support.
- Tribal and MSF (?) participation was discussed. The committee agreed that the tribe and other interested parties could attend future meetings.
- A possible funding partner for wakeless buoys was to be identified by Bob Koeppe.

### 7. Regulatory update

- Brad Jacobson reported that AZG&F no longer requires a permit to hold a fishing tournament.
- California still requires a tournament permit.

### 8. Action items and next steps

- Al Doelker to finalize a draft plan document.
- Chuck Minckley to review the draft.
- Chris Hayes to check wakeless zone permit status and budget.
- Bob Koeppe to contact Rob Beltaeo (or similar contact) for details from a Tucson meeting, and to identify a funding partner.
- Dexter to be contacted for a response on the 30,000-fish request.
- Next meeting scheduled for October 6, 1999, possibly at [[Achii Hanyo]].
