# Marine Fish Fauna from Otoliths of a Lower Pleistocene Deposit, San Pedro, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoichthyology

## Executive Summary

This Los Angeles County Museum of Natural History contribution (No. 128, May 31, 1967) by John E. Fitch describes the marine fish fauna recovered from the Miraflores Street deposit at San Pedro, California ([[San Pedro Sand]], LACMIP 332), a Lower Pleistocene outcrop. About 300 pounds of fossiliferous sediment yielded 2,746 otoliths, 584 vertebrae, 324 teeth and 105 miscellaneous remains, representing at least 10 elasmobranch and 30 teleost taxa. The assemblage resembles the fish fauna of central and northern California today, which led the author to classify the deposit as "coldwater." The outcrop was slated for destruction by [[Harbor Freeway]] extension before 1970.

## Background & Description

- **Discovery and sampling:** Roger D. Reimer first found the otoliths in 1963. Museum staff under George P. Kanakoff sampled the site, and the author processed the field sample using methods described in Fitch (1966b). Permission came from the property owner, Jack Bell.
- **Setting:** The layer lies at the base of a north-facing cliff, possibly 8 to 10 feet above present sea level. It measures about 50 feet east-west by 18 to 24 inches vertically, and is composed mainly of friable molluscan remains in a fine, clean sandy matrix.
- **Stratigraphy:** Per [[Woodring Et Al 1946]], San Pedro sand lies unconformably below the Palos Verdes sand. Mollusk data suggested depths of 25 to 50 fathoms and colder local ocean temperatures than today.
- **Assemblage composition:** 10 elasmobranch taxa (for example, [[Notorynchus Maculatus]], [[Isurus Oxyrinchus]], [[Squalus Acanthias]], [[Myliobatis Californicus]]) and about 30 teleost taxa. Shiner perch ([[Cymatogaster Aggregata]]) otoliths (1,129) and speckled sanddab ([[Citharichthys Stigmaeus]]) otoliths (191) are the most numerous identified species, while about 795 sanddab otoliths could only be identified to genus.
- **Methods:** Identification relied on otolith morphology (sagittae), compared with modern reference material. Figures 1 to 30 show inner faces of sagittae, photographed by Jack W. Schott.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature signal:** Many taxa range north of San Pedro today. [[Fish Faunal Analysis]] led the author to call the deposit coldwater, and Figure 31 maps modern distributions of the 28 non-bathypelagic bony fishes against that interpretation.
- **Northern and southern elements:** Of about 40 species identified, roughly 5 were northern forms, 1 a southern form, and 3 offshore or pelagic. Species such as [[Spirinchus Starksi]], [[Microgadus Proximus]] and [[Enophrys Taurina]] have northern range limits south of San Pedro. Carcharhinus species, which are warm-water taxa, are absent from the northern range of this deposit's fauna.
- **Comparison with other deposits:** The fauna is similar to the Timms Point silt and [[Lomita Marl]], both coldwater deposits. Genyonemus lineatus and Seriphus politus were scarce here (Genyonemus: 0, 8 and 2 otoliths across the three coldwater deposits), but very important in five non-coldwater deposits (up to 55 percent of yield).
- **Sheltered-environment signal:** Shiner perch made up about 41 percent of the total otolith yield, against under 10 percent in any other deposit the author examined. The author suggests this reflects deposition in a sheltered environment at moderate depth, supported by a Ship Rock sediment sample from 125 feet.
- **Absent taxa:** Otophidium taylori otoliths were entirely missing, though important in other deposits. The author attributes this to real absence rather than sampling bias.
- **Temperature interpretation:** A deposit like this may span 1 to 1,000 or more years, so temperature fluctuations could have occurred. The lack of southern fishes suggests stable conditions with no significant warm-water intrusion.

## Key Findings

- **Assemblage size:** 2,746 otoliths, 584 vertebrae (9 elasmobranch), 324 teeth (99 elasmobranch) and 105 miscellaneous items, from at least 10 elasmobranch and 30 teleost taxa.
- **Dominant taxa:** [[Cymatogaster Aggregata]] (1,129 otoliths), [[Citharichthys Stigmaeus]] (191), [[Engraulis Mordax]] (80), [[Trachurus Symmetricus]] (3 otoliths, longer than 6 mm), and many sanddab otoliths identifiable only to genus (795).
- **Interpretation:** The fauna reflects a coldwater, northern-leaning, probably sheltered, moderate-depth environment, consistent with colder local ocean temperatures than today.
- **Unique or rare records:** [[Cabezon]] and [[Gopher Rockfish]] otoliths are not known from other fossil deposits. Notably, [[Otophidium Taylori]] is absent.
- **Conservation concern:** The deposit is one of the few surface outcrops of San Pedro sand and was to be destroyed by freeway construction before 1970.
- **Future work:** The author expects additional sampling to reveal more northern species, such as Atheresthes stomias and Malacocottus zonurus, which occur at Timms Point and Lomita marl.
