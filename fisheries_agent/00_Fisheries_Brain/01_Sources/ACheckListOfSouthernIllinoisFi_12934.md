# A check-list of southern Illinois fishes

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae #centrarchidae #ichthyofauna-survey

## Executive Summary

This document is a **regional faunal checklist**, "A Checklist of Southern Illinois Fishes," compiled by W.R. Bridges for the Cooperative Fishery Research Laboratory at Southern Illinois University. Rather than a species-specific morphological study, the source catalogs the ichthyofauna of southern Illinois (defined as all counties south of, and including, the tier containing Randolph, Perry, Jefferson, Hamilton, and White counties). The two nominal focal taxa, **[[Golden Shiner]]** (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) and **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus dolomieui*, family [[Centrarchidae]]), appear as individual entries within a much larger taxonomic inventory spanning roughly 25 families and well over 100 species. Nomenclature follows Bailey's treatment in Harlan and Speaker's *Iowa Fish and Fishing*. Each species is annotated with a relative abundance code (Uncertain, Rare, Common, Abundant) and, where absent from the laboratory's permanent collection, flagged with an asterisk for targeted future collection.

## Background & Description

The source is a taxonomic/distributional checklist and does **not** provide morphological descriptions, meristic counts, or diagnostic features for individual species. The only structural information present is taxonomic placement (genus, species, family) and common names. Notable entries relevant to the title species:

- **[[Golden Shiner]]** (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*) — listed under family [[Cyprinidae]], among ~40 cyprinid genera/species cataloged (including [[Carp]], [[Goldfish]], [[Creek Chub]], and numerous *Notropis* shiners).
- **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus dolomieui*) — listed under family [[Centrarchidae]], grouped with [[Bluegill]], [[Rock Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[White Crappie]], [[Pigmy Sunfish]], and [[Flier]].

No further physical characteristics (body form, coloration, fin ray counts) are given in the extracted text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Geographic scope**: Southern Illinois, defined by county boundaries south of and including Randolph, Perry, Jefferson, Hamilton, and White counties — part of the Mississippi River drainage system.
- **Habitat types implied by family composition**: large river channels (e.g., [[Paddlefish]], [[Blue Catfish]], [[Freshwater Drum]]), small headwater/spring streams (e.g., [[Spring Cavefish]], [[Southern Redbelly Dace]]), sluggish backwaters and swamps (e.g., [[Pigmy Sunfish]], [[Mudminnow]], [[Swamp Darter]]), and lakes/ponds (e.g., [[Golden Shiner]], [[Black Bullhead]]).
- **Abundance classification system**: Four categories used to describe species' relative frequency in southern Illinois:
  - **U** = Uncertain (expected based on known range but not yet documented in literature for the region)
  - **R** = Rare (e.g., [[Pigmy Sunfish]] *Elassoma zonatum*, [[Spring Cavefish]] *Chologaster papilliferus* — noted as regionally rare despite being locally common at specific sites)
  - **C** = Common
  - **A** = Abundant
- The author explicitly cautions that this classification is **subjective and potentially misleading**, since regional rarity may mask high local abundance at specific collection sites.
- Species marked with an asterisk (*) were **absent from the laboratory's permanent reference collection** at time of writing, flagging them as collection priorities for field personnel.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic breadth**: The checklist spans 25 families, from primitive taxa like [[Petromyzonidae|Lampreys]] (*Ichthyomyzon* — American brook lamprey) and [[Polyodontidae|Paddlefish]], through [[Lepisosteidae|Gars]] and [[Amiidae|Bowfin]] (*Amia calva*), to advanced teleosts including [[Cyprinidae]], [[Ictaluridae|Catfishes]], [[Centrarchidae]], [[Percidae|Darters and Perches]], and [[Cottidae|Sculpins]].
- **Cyprinidae dominance**: The minnow family represents the single largest taxonomic group in the checklist, including *Notemigonus crysoleucas* (Golden Shiner), *Semotilus atromaculatus* (Creek Chub), numerous *Notropis* shiners (Emerald, Redfin, Common, Blackchin, Spottail, Bigeye, River, Red, Sand, Mimic), and *Campostoma anomalum* (Stoneroller).
- **Centrarchidae diversity**: Includes *Micropterus dolomieui* (Smallmouth Bass) alongside [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Rock Bass]] (*Ambloplites rupestris*), [[Orangespotted Sunfish]], [[Flier]] (*Centrarchus macropterus*), [[White Crappie]] and [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis* spp.), and the regionally rare [[Pigmy Sunfish]] (*Elassoma zonatum*).
- **Darter richness (Percidae)**: An unusually high number of *Etheostoma* species documented — Mud, Greenside, Rainbow, Bluebreast, Bluntnose, Iowa, Fantail, Swamp, Least, Johnny, Stripetail, and Spottail darters — indicating southern Illinois as a regional hotspot for darter diversity.
- **Notable rarities**: *Elassoma zonatum* (Pigmy Sunfish) and *Chologaster papilliferus* (Spring Cavefish) are singled out as biologically significant rare species tied to specialized spring/cave habitats.
- **Bibliography**: The reference list cites multiple prior regional ichthyological surveys (Lewis & Elder on Muddy River fish populations; O'Donnell's *Annotated List of the Fishes of Illinois*, Illinois Natural History Survey Bulletin, 1935; Forbes & Richardson foundational works), situating this checklist within a lineage of Illinois freshwater fish distribution studies.
- **Methodological caveat**: The compiler explicitly notes the abundance rankings are "quite arbitrary," resting on personal field experience rather than systematic quantitative sampling — an important limitation for any downstream ecological interpretation of this dataset.
