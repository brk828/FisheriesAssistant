## Executive Summary

This 1962 paper by Branley A. Branson (Kansas State College, Pittsburg) presents distributional records for freshwater and terrestrial gastropods (snails) collected across Louisiana, Missouri, and Arkansas. The author frames the work as a response to the paucity of comprehensive malacological surveys in these states — noting that no major taxonomic work on Arkansas/Louisiana mollusks had been published in nearly 50 years, and that Missouri lacked thorough taxonomic/ecological surveying altogether. The paper is a faunistic checklist documenting collection localities, dates, specimen counts (live vs. dead shells), and taxonomic identifications for numerous [[Gastropoda]] species across freshwater (lakes, ponds, swamps, rivers) and terrestrial (woodland leaf litter) habitats. Notably, it records *Mesodon magazinensis* in Arkansas — the first collection of this species since 1903 — and notes *Mesodon perigraptus* near its western range limit.

## Background & Description

While the paper does not provide detailed morphological descriptions, it distinguishes taxa at the species and subspecies level, implying reliance on shell morphology (whorl count, aperture shape, size, coloration) typical of gastropod taxonomy of the era. Key taxa mentioned include:

- **Aquatic/freshwater gastropods**: [[Campeloma Decisum]], [[Viviparus Intertextus]], [[Lymnaea Columella]], [[Physa Anatina]], [[Helisoma Anceps]], [[Oxytrema Alveare]] (noted as "typical downstream forms")
- **Terrestrial land snails**: [[Stenotrema Leai Aliciae]], [[Mesodon Thyroidus]], [[Mesodon Inflectus]], [[Mesodon Zaletus]], [[Mesodon Perigraptus]], [[Mesodon Magazinensis]], [[Triodopsis Vultuosa Cragini]], [[Triodopsis Albolabris]], [[Euglandina Rosea Bullata]], [[Retinella Indentata]], [[Retinella Wheatleyi]], [[Ventridens Demissus]], [[Ventridens Demissus Brittsi]], [[Zonitoides Arboreus]], [[Anguispira Alternata]], [[Succinea Concordialis]], [[Strobilops Labyrinthica]], [[Polygyra Jacksoni]], [[Polygyra Deltoidea]], [[Stenotrema Labrosum]], [[Mesomphix Cupreus]], [[Paravitrea Petrophila]], [[Discus Patulus]]

Specimens are differentiated as "live adults," "live immatures," or "dead shells," indicating shell condition and completeness (e.g., aperture and lip development) was used to assess maturity in the field.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The records span two broad ecological categories:

1. **Freshwater habitats**: Species such as *Campeloma decisum*, *Viviparus intertextus*, *Lymnaea columella*, *Physa anatina*, *Helisoma anceps*, and *Oxytrema alveare* were collected from lake spillways, ponds, swamps, and river systems (e.g., [[Chatham Lake]] spillway, [[White River]], [[D'Arbonne Swamp]]). *Oxytrema alveare* specimens from the White River were specifically noted as "typical downstream forms," suggesting a longitudinal (upstream-downstream) ecological/morphological gradient within river systems — a pattern relevant to [[Habitat Gradient Ecology]] in fluvial mollusk populations.

2. **Terrestrial habitats**: The majority of land snail taxa (e.g., *Stenotrema*, *Mesodon*, *Triodopsis*, *Retinella*, *Zonitoides*, *Discus patulus*) were collected from forested/woodland sites such as Cove Creek Area (Franklin County, Arkansas), South Mountain in Hot Springs National Park, and sites near the Sabine River — consistent with leaf-litter and understory microhabitats typical of terrestrial gastropod ecology.

Geographic distribution notes are ecologically significant: *Mesodon perigraptus* was found "near the western limits" of its range in Franklin County, Arkansas, indicating a biogeographic boundary relevant to [[Range Limit Distribution]] studies.

## Key Findings

- **Rediscovery record**: *Mesodon magazinensis* (Pilsbry and Ferriss) was collected in Franklin County, Arkansas (3 live specimens) — the first record of this species since **1903**, a nearly 60-year gap.
- **Range limit documentation**: *Mesodon perigraptus* collected in Franklin County represents a locality "near the western limits" of the species' known range.
- **New locality records**: Numerous species (e.g., *Campeloma decisum*, *Euglandina rosea bullata*, *Ventridens demissus brittsi*) are documented at specific new localities across Jackson, LaFourche, Union, Grant, and Sabine Parishes (Louisiana), Jasper County (Missouri), and Franklin, Washington, and Garland Counties (Arkansas).
- **Downstream morphotype note**: *Oxytrema alveare* from the White River, Arkansas, exhibited "typical downstream forms," hinting at intraspecific morphological variation correlated with stream position — relevant for future work on [[Intraspecific Variation In Freshwater Snails]].
- **Survey gap identified**: The author explicitly states that Arkansas and Louisiana mollusk fauna had not been comprehensively surveyed in ~50 years, and Missouri had never received thorough taxonomic/ecological survey — establishing this paper as a foundational contribution to filling regional [[Malacological Survey Gaps]].
- **Collection methodology**: All Louisiana specimens were collected by Dr. Walter Harman and students; Missouri specimens from a single collecting date (14 October 1961) at Saginaw, Jasper County; Arkansas White River specimens collected by Dr. Kirk Strawn (University of Arkansas).
- **Cross-reference to broader regional literature**: The paper appears alongside continued reference material on the Mollusca of Alabama, situating this study within a broader Southeastern U.S. malacological literature effort including works by Goodrich, van der Schalie, and Walker on [[Naiades]] (freshwater mussels) and terrestrial shell-bearing mollusks of Alabama.
