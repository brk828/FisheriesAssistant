## Executive Summary
This Arizona Game and Fish Department inter-office memo (dated January 15, 1988) documents a translocation and stocking event for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large-bodied catostomid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. On December 17, 1987, Nongame Branch personnel seined 255 individuals from Mormon Lake on the Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge. The following day, 225 fish were transported and released into Cherry Creek, a tributary of the Salt River in Gila County, Arizona, as part of a native fish recovery/reintroduction effort. Health screening by a USFWS fish pathologist found no evidence of parasitic infection, and transport mortality was minimal (one individual).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Source habitat:** Mormon Lake, situated on the Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge — a lentic (standing water) environment used as a rearing/holding site for [[Razorback Sucker]] stock.
- **Recipient habitat:** Cherry Creek, Gila County, Arizona — a lotic desert stream system.
  - Release site: a **run** habitat, 1/2 mile upstream of Dagger Ranch
  - Located 2¼ miles above the confluence of Cherry Creek with the **Salt River**
  - Precise legal location: T1N, R15E, Sec 10, SW¼ (Gila and Salt River Meridian)
- This stocking reflects broader efforts to reestablish [[Razorback Sucker]] populations within historic range in the [[Salt River]] drainage, part of the larger [[Colorado River Basin]] native fish recovery program.
- The species is associated with warm, low-to-moderate gradient desert streams and reservoirs, often facing competition/predation pressure from introduced non-native fishes — a major driver of range-wide population decline for this now-endangered catostomid.

## Key Findings
- **255 razorback suckers** were collected via seining from Mormon Lake on December 17, 1987.
- **225 individuals** were successfully transported and released into Cherry Creek the following day (December 18, 1987).
- Stocking operation was completed by **4:45 P.M.**, with only **one transport mortality** recorded — indicating a low-stress, well-managed translocation protocol.
- A **USFWS fish pathologist (Ron Major)** examined a subset of fish for external/internal parasites; **no parasites were detected**, suggesting a healthy source population suitable for reintroduction.
- **30 specimens** were retained/preserved, presumably for scientific reference (morphometrics, genetics, or pathology archives).
- This record serves as a historical data point for tracking **[[Razorback Sucker]] reintroduction efforts** in Arizona and contributes to long-term population monitoring in the [[Cherry Creek (Arizona)]] and [[Salt River]] systems.
