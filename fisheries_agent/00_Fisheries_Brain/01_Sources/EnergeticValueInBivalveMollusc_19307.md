# Energetic Value in Bivalve Molluscs I: Interest as Food for Higher Trophic Levels

#malacology #marine-biology #bivalvia #mytilidae #unionidae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 study by M. Manuela Q. Machado (Faculty of Sciences, University of Porto) measures the energetic (calorific) value of three bivalve species from northern Portugal: the marine [[Mytilus Galloprovincialis]], the riverine [[Unio Litoralis]] and the lagoon-associated [[Anodonta Cygnea]]. Calorific values were obtained both by direct bomb combustion and by calculation from chemical composition. The author reports no appreciable differences between marine and freshwater bivalves and concludes that freshwater mussels, especially Anodonta, may be a useful food source for higher trophic levels, particularly fishes.

## Background & Description

The paper is framed within bioenergetics. Knowing the energy content of animal populations, alongside physiological data and population dynamics, helps characterise [[Energy Flow]] through populations. Without a full energy budget (ingestion, assimilation, egestion), calorific content still indicates the [[Nutritive Value]] of organisms.

Mytilus is heavily studied for industrial use, whereas freshwater mussels are not. The author notes that population density data were not yet available, but the collected material suggested that the freshwater species occur in abundance. This raised the question of whether bivalve culture could also be considered in continental waters.

The study was part of a research programme of the Centre of Ecology and was supported by the Instituto Nacional de Investigação Científica (INIC).

**Species examined**

- *Mytilus galloprovincialis*: collected in the intertidal zone at low tide, about 2.06 km north of the Douro River mouth.
- *Unio litoralis*: collected at Melres on the Douro, 25 km from the mouth, in an area still tidally influenced, at about 50 cm depth.
- *Anodonta cygnea*: collected from [[Mira Lagoon]], 4.1 km from the shore and indirectly linked to the sea, at about 50 cm depth.

**Methods**

- Collection in summer 1978 (Mytilus and Anodonta in August, Unio in September). Searches were not quantitative.
- Animals were starved for 48 hours to remove gut contents.
- Animals were sorted into size classes (I to VI), with the percentage per class calculated. Shells were removed before weighing.
- Wet weight was measured, and dry weight was determined after oven drying at 60 °C.
- Calorific value was measured by combustion in a [[Phillipson Micro-Bomb Calorimeter]], calibrated with benzoic acid. Ash remaining after combustion was weighed to give mineral content.
- Proteins were estimated by the biuret method, lipids by warm ethyl ether extraction, and carbohydrates by difference.
- Calorific values were also calculated from composition using [[Calorific Equivalents]]: 9.45 cal/mg dry weight for lipids, 5.65 for proteins and 4.10 for carbohydrates.
- Each reported calorific value is the average of five determinations.

## Key Findings

**Overall energy comparison**

- No appreciable differences were found between the energy values of marine (Mytilus) and freshwater (Anodonta and Unio) bivalves. Student's t test found no statistically significant differences among species' mean calorific values (P > 0.05).

**Ash-free calorific value (cal/mg)**

- *Mytilus galloprovincialis*: 5.3762 (highest)
- *Anodonta cygnea*: 5.2520
- *Unio litoralis*: 5.1593 (lowest)

**Calorific value including ash, and value from chemical composition (cal/mg dry weight)**

- *Anodonta cygnea*: 4.7138 (combustion, including ash) and 4.3841 (calculated), the highest in both cases.
- *Mytilus galloprovincialis*: 4.5613 (combustion) and 4.3224 (calculated).
- *Unio litoralis*: 4.4432 (combustion) and 4.2117 (calculated), the lowest in both cases.
- Calculated values were slightly lower than combustion values but did not differ considerably.

**Size and weight**

- Calorific value decreased as dry weight increased. This agrees with [[Griffiths 1977]], who reported that calorific value rises in molluscs up to about 10 mg dry weight and falls above that.
- Mean dry weight was highest in Anodonta (9.45 g) and lowest in Mytilus (0.20 g), consistent with Anodonta's longer average length.
- Wet-to-dry weight ratios were 4.33 (Anodonta), 5.39 (Unio) and 5.55 (Mytilus). Water content was 76.8% in Anodonta, 81.2% in Unio and 81.6% in Mytilus. Mytilus kept a constant water percentage across size classes.
- The lowest calorific values in size classes III and V were found in Mytilus and Anodonta, not Unio, but these differences were not clearly evident.

**Biochemical composition (dry-weight percentages)**

| Component | [[Anodonta Cygnea]] | [[Unio Litoralis]] | [[Mytilus Galloprovincialis]] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ash | 9.48–23.77 | 9.03–19.84 | 9.80–22.85 |
| Proteins | 15.4–42.33 (mean 35.51) | 10.50–13.95 (mean 11.97) | 17.17–25.33 |
| Lipids | 3.38–7.98 | 6.26–14.07 | 7.40–10.33 |
| Carbohydrates | 37.08–74.1 | 54.59–73.94 (mean 66.26) | 49.65–61.45 |

- Carbohydrates were the dominant component in all three species.
- Anodonta had the highest protein content. In its largest size class (VI), carbohydrate content was highest and protein content was lowest.
- Anodonta had the greatest organic component percentage.
- Lipids and ash were more uniform across the three species.

**Sampling limitations**

- In Mytilus, size classes I and II occurred at very low percentages (2.9% and 0.7%), too few for accurate determination.

**Conclusions and implications**

- The author concludes that these bivalves, and especially Anodonta, may be a valuable food source for higher trophic levels, specifically [[Fish]].
- The author suggests that freshwater bivalve culture should be considered, given their nutritive value and apparent abundance.
