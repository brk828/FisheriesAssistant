# Lethal Oxygen Concentrations for the Northern Common Shiner

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #water-quality #dissolved-oxygen

## Executive Summary

This experimental study determined mean lethal oxygen concentrations for the [[Northern Common Shiner]] (*Notropis cornutus frontalis*) across a temperature range of 55° to 80° F. Mean lethal oxygen levels ranged from 0.54 to 1.23 p.p.m., with values increasing linearly with temperature when plotted on semi-logarithmic paper. The research was conducted between 1952 and 1956 using fish collected from [[Flint Creek]] in [[Ontario County]], New York. The study provides critical data for investigating fish kills caused by oxygen depletion from organic pollution in streams.

## Background & Description

The [[Northern Common Shiner]] (*Notropis cornutus frontalis*) is a minnow species common to New York streams. This study was initiated because minnow fish kills resulting from dissolved oxygen depletion due to organic wastes required lethal oxygen data for pollution investigations, yet very little information existed for species common to New York streams. The northern common shiner was selected as a test species because it appeared moderately sensitive to reductions in dissolved oxygen.

Test fish were collected from a short section of [[Flint Creek]] north of Gorham in [[Ontario County]], which was free from pollution except for minimal household wastes. Nearly all fish were in age group I, with some young-of-the-year and possibly a few age group II individuals. Fish ranged from 38 to 95 millimeters total length (mean 62 mm) and 0.35 to 5.70 grams weight (mean 1.8 g based on 224 fish). Some fish showed [[Black Spot]] (*Neascus* sp.) parasitism, but noticeably parasitized individuals were excluded from testing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Tests were conducted at five temperatures: 55°, 65°, 70°, 75°, and 80° F using [[Rochester]] tap water from [[Hemlock Lake]]. Fish used for 80° F tests and some 75° F tests were collected during mid-April or early May when stream temperatures were rising. Fish for remaining 75° F tests and all 70°, 65°, and 55° F tests were collected in late October, November, or December when stream temperatures were falling.

The experimental water had dissolved oxygen concentrations slightly above 7 p.p.m. in holding aquaria. For most tests, water chemistry was consistent, though tests at 65° F and 55° F conducted in 1956 at [[Scottsville]] showed elevated pH (8.92) and decreased total alkalinity (36 p.p.m., including 5.0 p.p.m. phenolphthalein alkalinity) due to new transite piping. This variation did not appear to affect results.

Fish were acclimated to assay water for 8 to 30 days (minimum holding time) and to test temperature for 4 to 26 days (most exceeding 7 days). Mortality during holding never exceeded 10% and was usually less than 5%. No mortality occurred in 55° F stock; only three fish died in 80° F stocks. Greatest mortality occurred in 70° and 75° F stocks.

## Key Findings

**Lethal Oxygen Concentrations by Temperature:**

- **55° F**: Mean 0.54 p.p.m. (range 0.38-1.40 p.p.m., median 0.52 p.p.m., n=28)
- **65° F**: Mean 0.77 p.p.m. (range 0.46-3.10 p.p.m., median 0.74 p.p.m., n=61)
- **70° F**: Mean 0.85 p.p.m. (range 0.43-4.34 p.p.m., median 0.74 p.p.m., n=47)
- **75° F**: Mean 1.05 p.p.m. (range 0.46-6.00 p.p.m., median 0.96 p.p.m., n=39)
- **80° F**: Mean 1.23 p.p.m. (range 0.56-6.20 p.p.m., median 1.04 p.p.m., n=56)

**Temperature-Oxygen Relationship:**
The relationship between temperature and lethal oxygen concentration follows the equation: log Y = 0.0140X - 1.0361, where Y = dissolved oxygen in p.p.m. and X = temperature in degrees Fahrenheit. This produces a straight-line curve on semi-logarithmic paper.

**Experimental Methodology:**
Each fish was placed in a sealed glass jar (1 pint to 2 quarts capacity depending on fish size and temperature) and oxygen was gradually depleted through the fish's respiratory activities. The end-point was defined as the fish lying completely on its side with barely perceptible gill movements. Residual oxygen was measured using the [[Winkler Method]] (sulfamic acid modification). Mean test duration was 13 hours 50 minutes, ranging from 4 hours 27 minutes (80° F) to 28 hours 52 minutes (55° F).

**Validation:**
Field observations and stream bio-assays showed close agreement with mean lethal values at several temperatures. The mean lethal oxygen value at 66.4° F (0.73 p.p.m.) computed from data by [[Black et al.]] (1954) for low carbon dioxide tension was only 0.05 p.p.m. lower than the curve value, suggesting good agreement between methodologies.

**Standard Deviations and Ranges:**
Standard deviations (calculated using logarithms) showed wide ranges at higher temperatures, indicating individual variation in tolerance. The range of values for logarithmic means increased with temperature, from 0.412-0.714 p.p.m. at 55° F to 0.687-2.195 p.p.m. at 80° F.
