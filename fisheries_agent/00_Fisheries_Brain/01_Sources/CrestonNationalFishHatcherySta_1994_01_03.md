## Executive Summary
The Creston National Fish Hatchery (NFH) 1993 Station Profile documents the operations of a federal salmonid production facility located near Kalispell, Montana, established in 1936 under Special Act (58 Stat. 801). The station's core mission is producing fish to support tribal fishery management programs on Montana's seven Indian Reservations, as well as mitigation obligations tied to the construction of [[Hungry Horse Dam]] and, prospectively, [[Kerr Dam]]. The facility also conducts early-stage rearing studies on [[Bull Trout]] and supplies fish under a Memorandum of Agreement with State management programs. In FY1993, the hatchery distributed a total of 778,188 fish (excluding eggs and fry) across federal, state, and private waters in six states, though production of [[Kokanee Salmon]] fell short of targets due to recurring gill disease outbreaks linked to inadequate early rearing space.

## Background & Description
This station profile is administrative/operational in nature and does not report morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data on the reared species. The four primary production species — [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Westslope Cutthroat Trout]], [[Lake Trout]], and [[Kokanee Salmon]] — are all members of family Salmonidae, but no size, weight, or growth-stage measurements are provided in this source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Facility Role**: Creston NFH functions as a freshwater salmonid production and stocking station rather than a natural habitat study site, supplying hatchery-reared fish into regulated and mitigation waters.
- **Mitigation Context**: Fish production is directly tied to compensating for anadromous/resident fish habitat losses caused by hydroelectric dam construction ([[Hungry Horse Dam]], future [[Kerr Dam]] mitigation), reflecting the ecological displacement caused by impoundment of Montana river systems.
- **Bull Trout Conservation**: A newly installed bull trout isolation facility (funded by Montana Power Company) in the feed building, with dedicated water supply and drain systems, signals early conservation-oriented rearing efforts for this sensitive, cold-water adapted species — relevant given [[Bull Trout]]'s status as an indicator species for high-quality, cold montane stream habitats.
- **Disease Ecology**: Recurrent gill disease outbreaks in [[Kokanee Salmon]] populations highlight density-dependent pathogen pressure in raceway environments, particularly during early juvenile rearing stages when space limitations increase pathogen transmission risk.
- **Distribution Network**: Fish were distributed to federal, state, and private waters across Montana, Idaho, Colorado, Washington, Michigan, and Wyoming, indicating the hatchery's role in a broader interstate stocking and tribal fisheries network.

## Key Findings
- **Total FY1993 Distribution**: 778,188 fish distributed (excluding eggs/fry), comprising:
  - Federal waters: 7,913
  - State waters (other): 229,519
  - Private waters: 520,756
- **Species Distributed**: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Westslope Cutthroat Trout]], [[Lake Trout]], and [[Kokanee Salmon]].
- **Production Shortfall**: High and repetitive gill disease infections in [[Kokanee Salmon]] reduced output below projected numbers, attributed to insufficient early-stage rearing space; Bonneville Power Administration (BPA) committed to funding additional rearing capacity in FY1994.
- **Infrastructure Upgrades (FY1993)**:
  - Bird netting installed on raceways 1–24 (station personnel).
  - Packed columns and oxygen delivery systems installed on raceways 25–48 (funded/contracted by BPA).
  - Bull trout isolation facility installed with dedicated water supply/drain systems (funded/contracted by Montana Power Company).
- **Environmental Compliance**: Testing for ground contamination from underground storage tanks was completed with all results negative.
- **Operational Pressure**: High visitor traffic (~25,000 estimated in FY93) and fish transportation demands from the station's large distribution unit created labor shortages during the distribution season.
- **Budget Trends**: Operations funding remained relatively stable (~$135–158K, FY91–95), while maintenance funding dropped to $0 by FY1993–95 in listed figures; cost recoverables rose sharply from $10K (FY91) to a projected $305K (FY95), reflecting increased reliance on external funding partners (BPA, Montana Power Company).
- **Staffing**: Full-time equivalent (FTE) staffing increased modestly from 6.0 (FY91) to a projected 8.0 (FY94–95).
- **Contributing Partners**: Bonneville Power Administration (BPA) and Montana Power Company (MPC) were key funding contributors for infrastructure and mitigation-related upgrades.
