# Bureau of Reclamation Response to Reasonable and Prudent Alternative Provision 13(a) - Lower Colorado River Operations

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #river-management #ESA-compliance

## Executive Summary

This memorandum and attached analysis document the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s response to provision 13(a) of the Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) in the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]'s Final Biological and Conference Opinion (Consultation No. 2-21-95-F-216) on [[Lower Colorado River]] Operations and Maintenance from [[Lake Mead]] to the Southerly International Boundary with Mexico. The document addresses discretionary action flexibility available to Reclamation under existing legal and contractual obligations for managing endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*). The analysis distinguishes between unilateral discretion (independent decision-making authority) and cooperative discretion (actions requiring coordination with other parties). Reclamation identifies that most operational flexibility resides in cooperative undertakings with stakeholders rather than unilateral authority, due to extensive legal frameworks governing Colorado River water apportionment, delivery contracts, flood control, and power generation.

## Background & Description

The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] operates the [[Lower Colorado River]] system on behalf of the Secretary of the Interior under three primary roles: (1) Watermaster, originating from the Boulder Canyon Project Act of 1928 and the Colorado River Compact of 1922, which apportioned 7.5 million acre-feet to Lower Division States ([[Arizona]], [[California]], [[Nevada]]); (2) manager of power operations at mainstream facilities; and (3) manager of operation and maintenance of dams, main river channel, and levee systems covering approximately 250 miles between [[Davis Dam]] and [[Morelos Dam]].

The Biological Opinion addresses jeopardy and adverse modification of critical habitat for endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], plus jeopardy for endangered [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]. The RPA includes seventeen provisions, with provision 13 specifically addressing discretionary action flexibility. Provision 13(a) required Reclamation to provide a detailed account of discretionary flexibility within 120 days of the final BO completion (transmitted April 30, 1997, received May 1, 1997).

Discretion is defined as actions Reclamation can initiate either unilaterally and independently, or through cooperative/collaborative efforts with other parties holding rights under legally binding arrangements comprising the "Law of the River." The Federal Government manages and distributes Colorado River waters but has no ownership of water rights except where established by the Supreme Court Decree of 1964, rights held in trust for Native Americans, or legally acquired rights.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Lower Colorado River]] system encompasses multiple reservoir and riverine habitats critical for endangered species recovery. [[Lake Mohave]] operations maintain target elevations to accommodate [[Razorback Sucker]] preservation programs, including spawning and rearing activities. The reservoir system includes [[Lake Mead]] (created by [[Hoover Dam]]), [[Lake Mohave]] (created by [[Davis Dam]]), [[Lake Havasu]] (created by [[Parker Dam]]), [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], and [[Mittry Lake]].

Water elevation management directly affects aquatic habitat quality and availability for endangered fish species. [[Lake Mohave]] target elevations are depicted with and without constraints desired for [[Razorback Sucker]] programs. Statistical analysis shows mean historic elevations closely match target elevations, with variations primarily caused by water supply fluctuations and revised water orders. [[Lake Havasu]] serves as the last major control point for water releases, with target elevations more difficult to meet consistently due to added constraints on upstream [[Lake Mohave]] during spawning/rearing activities.

[[Senator Wash Reservoir]] operates between elevations 210-240 feet and serves as a re-regulating reservoir to manage unanticipated flows and excess water, while also providing recreation and fishery benefits. [[Mittry Lake]] is managed cooperatively with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] for fish, wildlife, and recreation purposes.

The 1992 [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] includes requirements to manage releases to protect river sediment storage downstream, reshape river topography, redeposit sediment, and enhance aquatic habitat. These objectives constrain flexibility to manage releases on annual, monthly, daily, and hourly bases.

## Key Findings

**Discretionary Action Framework:**
- Unilateral discretion is limited to short-term refinements of operations timing and approaches to maintenance activities in the mainstream
- Most operational decisions require consultation or modification of existing contracts with diverse stakeholders
- Cooperative discretion offers greatest potential for long-term endangered species management and recovery

**Specific Activity Discretion Analysis (Table 1 Activities):**

1. **[[Lake Mead]] Water Elevation:** No unilateral flexibility; releases governed by Supreme Court Decree and Boulder Canyon Project Act section 6. Limited discretion over timing of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] releases constrained by avoiding spills, equalizing [[Lake Powell]]/[[Lake Mead]] contents, and [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] requirements.

2. **[[Lake Mohave]] Water Elevation:** Limited discretion comprises minor short-term refinements. System requirements include physical/safety constraints, leveling hourly fluctuations from [[Hoover Dam]], and maintaining elevation constraints for [[Razorback Sucker]] program. Unilateral decisions for dam safety/flood control; cooperative interactions for contractual impacts.

3. **[[Lake Havasu]] Water Elevation:** Extremely limited flexibility due to contractual minimum elevations for [[Metropolitan Water District]] and [[Central Arizona Project]] diversions, downstream water orders, and Mexico's requirements. Daily/weekly decisions essentially transparent to end users but directly result from treaty/decree/law/contract requirements.

4. **[[Senator Wash Reservoir]] Water Elevation:** Day-to-day decisions made independently considering mainstream conditions; long-term elevation management discretionary but limited by short-term flow changes. Currently operated between 210-240 feet elevation.

5. **[[Mittry Lake]] Water Elevation:** Limited discretion collaborative with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] under management agreement for fish, wildlife, and recreation.

6. **Flow-Rate Below [[Hoover Dam]]:** No unilateral discretion; narrow flexibility requiring close cooperation with power contractors and [[Western Area Power Administration]]. Excellent cooperation achieved for short-term refinements accommodating endangered fish rearing programs on [[Lake Mohave]].

7-8. **Water Releases from [[Davis Dam]] and [[Parker Dam]]:** Little/no unilateral flexibility on annual, seasonal, or monthly basis. Short-term flexibility exercised cooperatively with power contractors for legal, contractual, or emergency needs.

9. **Flow-Rate Below [[Imperial Dam]]:** Limited discretion in routing water subject to power contract provisions. Cooperative routing through [[Laguna Division]] and [[Yuma Division]] achieved for short-term events/emergencies.

10. **Declaring Surplus/Normal/Shortage Years:** Secretary determines conditions through consultation with Seven Basin States and other parties via [[Colorado River Management Work Group]]. Consultation includes tribes, [[National Park Service]], [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], water districts, environmental groups, power contractors. Secretary has discretion to release unused apportionment and develop specific criteria.

11-13. **Channel Maintenance, Levee Operations, Quarry Management:** Broad unilateral discretion over conveyance methods, navigation needs, and flood control manner, limited by environmental/regulatory compliance (Clean Water Act section 404). Quarry system provides rock riprap for bankline stabilization and emergency levee repairs. Past accommodation of environmental enhancement techniques while meeting project purposes.

14. **[[Yuma Desalting Plant]] Operation:** Authorized under Colorado River Salinity Control Act of 1974 to meet quality obligations to Mexico under Minute 242. Reclamation considering alternatives; may have discretion regarding plant operation if alternatives identified.

15. **Water Delivery to Mexico:** Quantities established by treaty, not subject to unilateral modification. Delivery schedules negotiated through [[International Boundary and Water Commission]]. Timing/operation of 242 wellfield discretionary within narrow limits.

16. **Dredging at [[Imperial Dam]] and [[Laguna Settling Basin]]:** Full discretion over timing and methods, so long as scheduled delivery orders can be met.

**Stakeholder Engagement:**
- Reclamation committed to consensus-based approach with full stakeholder participation
- Document distributed for public comment after submission to [[Fish and Wildlife Service]]
- Invitation extended to stakeholders to supply additional information on discretion discussion
- Potential revisions to information pursuant to RPA provision 13 based on public input
- [[MSCP Steering Committee]] meetings indicate significant stakeholder interest in discretion provision

**Legal/Contractual Framework:**
- Water contracts are for permanent service; changes require willingness to renegotiate by appropriate parties
- Contracting process for Colorado River water within 7.5 million acre-feet apportionment essentially complete
- Small portion of Arizona's basic apportionment not yet under contract
- Secretary consults with affected State regarding use of water within its apportionment (section 16 BCPA)
- [[Army Corps of Engineers]] established Flood Control Criteria for [[Hoover Dam]], reviewed at five-year intervals

**Operational Constraints:**
- Annual Operating Plan preparation requires consultation per section 602 Colorado River Basin Project Act
- Section 1803 [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] expanded consultation to include Interior agencies, Department of Energy, tribes, public (academic/scientific communities, environmental organizations, recreation industry, power contractors)
- Flood control operations explicit with no alternate actions when hydrologic projections fall within Criteria
- Power generation schedules coordinated between Reclamation and [[Western Area Power Administration]]
