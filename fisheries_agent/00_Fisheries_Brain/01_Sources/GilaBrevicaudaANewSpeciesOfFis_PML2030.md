# Gila brevicauda, a New Species of Fish from the Sierra Madre Occidental of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper formally describes *Gila brevicauda* (Teleostei: Cyprinidae), a new stout-bodied chub known only from the Rio Basaseachic, a headwater tributary of the [[Rio Mayo]] drainage in western Chihuahua, Mexico. Its defining trait is an unusually short caudal fin, which is nearly always shorter than the caudal peduncle. The species is distinguished from its closest relatives, [[Gila Pulchra]] and *G. cf. pulchra*, by fin-ray counts, pharyngeal dentition, pigmentation and morphometrics. The authors also review the taxonomy of *Gila* in northern Mexico and the southwestern USA, the biogeography of the transdivide region, and the conservation threats facing the species.

## Background & Description

The cyprinid genus [[Gila]] is widespread in arid and semi-arid western North America, and its taxonomy has long been unstable. Earlier work relied mainly on external meristic and morphological characters, with molecular studies only recently contributing. The holotype is an adult female of 93.9 mm standard length (SL), collected by [[Findley]] and colleagues in November 1975. Paratypes were drawn from several museum series, and the authors also reference a 1979 collection of 112 specimens.

Key diagnostic and descriptive features:
- **Body form:** deep, stout and rounded, with a deep caudal peduncle and blunt snout; jaws terminal.
- **Caudal fin:** unusually short, nearly always shorter than the caudal peduncle. In all other examined *Gila* species the caudal fin is longer than the peduncle. It is more strongly forked in juveniles.
- **Pharyngeal dentition:** 1,4-4,1 (a single tooth in the minor rows).
- **Scales:** relatively fine scales with well-developed basal radii.
- **Pigmentation:** darker than congeners in adjacent basins, with dark-brown dorsum and mottled flanks, a dark lateral band in juveniles, and a light tan venter in larger fish. A dark caudal spot becomes an ill-defined blotch in larger individuals.
- **Breeding:** tuberculate males as small as about 75 mm SL; sexual dichromatism was not observed.
- **Etymology:** from Latin *brevis* (short) and *cauda* (tail). Suggested common names are "carpita cola corta" (Spanish) and "shorttail chub" (English).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat:** The type locality is a bedrock-controlled headwater stream with clear water, hard substrate (about 90% bedrock, boulders and coarse gravel), long deep pools, high-gradient riffles and low waterfalls. Aquatic vegetation was limited to green algae in quiet water. The surrounding terrain is steep cliffs and pine-oak-juniper forest.

**Diet:** Very little is known. Of four guts examined, three were empty; the fourth contained aquatic insect fragments (odonate nymphs, a belostomatid, a corixid, and dipteran larvae).

**Biogeography:** The upper Rio Mayo is surrounded by other Pacific-slope drainages, including tributaries of the [[Rio Papigochic]] and the [[Rio Fuerte]]. East of the continental divide lies the [[Rio Conchos]]. The authors hypothesize that *G. brevicauda* either descended from an ancestor that crossed basins through stream capture or tectonic displacement, or from a *G. pulchra*-like ancestor isolated by the [[Cascada de Basaseachic]] waterfall.

**Conservation:** Abundance data are lacking beyond the 1979 collection. The Rio Basaseachic watershed has suffered from overgrazing, deforestation, timber harvest and increasing tourism. Part of the Rio Mayo watershed is protected as a national park and a flora and fauna protection area. Introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) pose a potential threat, as does the possibility of further non-native trout stocking.

## Key Findings

- *Gila brevicauda* is a new species, distinguished chiefly by its abbreviated caudal fin, a feature consistent across all specimens and not an artifact of preservation or disease.
- The species is known only from the Rio Basaseachic and its tributaries in the upper Rio Mayo. It has not been recorded in adjacent basins.
- It most resembles *G. pulchra* and *G. cf. pulchra* but differs in pigmentation, fin-ray counts (8 dorsal and anal rays, 9 pelvic rays in congeners), and morphometrics. Principal component analysis separated it mainly on caudal-fin length, with eye diameter and caudal-peduncle depth contributing. Caudal-fin and caudal-peduncle length differed significantly from *G. pulchra* (two-tailed t-test, P < 0.0001).
- Molecular data (Dowling, unpublished) show considerable genetic divergence from the Rio Papigochic populations. Populations from the Rio Conchos and Rio Fuerte remain genetically uncharacterized.
- The paper proposes two options: transfer from another basin, or isolation by the waterfall. Both are untested.
- The authors recommend a regional survey to determine status and to search for other mountain isolates, and note that systematic resolution is needed for effective conservation.
- The paper also revises *Gila minacae* to include Pacific-slope populations from the Rio Yaqui to the Rio Culiacán, which had previously been assigned to *G. robusta*.
- The authors propose "shorttail chub" as the English common name, and "carpita cola corta" as the Spanish name.
