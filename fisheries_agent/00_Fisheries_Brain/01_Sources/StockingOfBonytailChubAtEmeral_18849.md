## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum, not a primary research paper, detailing a **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) stocking event conducted on 27 October 1994. The memo, originating from the Parker Fish and Recovery Office (FRO), records the release of hatchery-reared bonytail chub into four constructed pond habitats at the Emerald Canyon Golf Course near Parker, Arizona. This action represents a component of the broader **[[Endangered Species Recovery Program]]** for native Colorado River fishes, utilizing off-channel, human-made aquatic habitats as refugia or grow-out ponds for this critically endangered cyprinid.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Habitat:** *Gila elegans* is historically native to the mainstem [[Colorado River]] and its major tributaries, adapted to warm, turbid, fast-flowing riverine conditions.
- **Habitat Used in This Record:** The fish were introduced into **artificial lentic (pond) environments** on a golf course — a marked departure from natural lotic river habitat. This reflects a common conservation strategy wherein isolated, predator-controlled ponds serve as refuge habitats to protect endangered cyprinids from non-native predatory and competitive fish species present in the mainstem river.
- **Site Distribution:** Fish were distributed across four discrete ponds:
  - Pond 1
  - Upper Pond 12
  - Lower Pond 12
  - Pond 18
- This multi-pond stocking strategy likely aims to reduce density-dependent mortality/competition risk and hedge against localized environmental failures (e.g., disease, oxygen depletion) by spreading the population across separate water bodies.
