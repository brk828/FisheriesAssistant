# Artificial Hybridization of *Crenichthys baileyi* With Related Cyprinodont Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This study by Hubbs and Drewry investigates the phylogenetic placement of [[Crenichthys baileyi]] (White River Springfish) within the Cyprinodontidae family using artificial cross-fertilization experiments. Historically, *C. baileyi* has been ambiguously classified—originally described as a subspecies of [[Cyprinodon macularius]] due to superficial morphological similarity, yet suspected to be more closely allied with [[Fundulus]] based on generic relationships within its sister genus [[Empetrichthys]]. By fertilizing eggs from various cyprinodontid genera ([[Fundulus]], [[Lucania]], [[Adinia]], [[Cyprinodon]]) with *C. baileyi* sperm, the authors demonstrate significantly higher developmental success in crosses with fundulines (Fundulus, Lucania) versus cyprinodontines (Cyprinodon), supporting a taxonomic placement of *Crenichthys* closer to [[Fundulus]] than to [[Cyprinodon]]. The findings also challenge tooth-cusp morphology (bicuspid vs. tricuspid vs. conical) as a sole valid criterion for subfamily separation between Fundulinae and Cyprinodontinae.

## Background & Description
- **Dentition**: *Crenichthys baileyi* possesses **bicuspid teeth**, an intermediate condition between:
  - *Cyprinodon*: tricuspid teeth
  - *Fundulus*: conical teeth
- This dental morphology was historically used by Myers (1931) to separate the subfamilies **Fundulinae** and **Cyprinodontinae**; Miller (1956) noted that the *Crenichthys* condition weakens this subfamilial distinction.
- **Superficial body form**: *C. baileyi* was originally described (Gilbert, 1893) as *Cyprinodon macularius baileyi*, indicating gross morphological resemblance to [[Cyprinodon]] rather than [[Fundulus]].
- No detailed external morphometric data (fin counts, scale counts, etc.) provided in this text; study relies on embryological/developmental comparison as a proxy for phylogenetic affinity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: *Crenichthys* and the related genus [[Empetrichthys]] are endemic to **warm springs and associated outflow waters** in southern Nevada and southeastern California — isolated desert spring systems.
- **Collection locality**: Stocks of *C. baileyi* were collected from **Crystal Springs**, 4 miles south of Hiko, Nevada (collections in July 1959 and July 1961).
- Comparator species stocks were derived from southern Texas freshwater/brackish habitats:
  - *Fundulus pulvereus* — Live Oak Peninsula, TX
  - *Fundulus zebrinus* — Live Oak Creek, TX
  - *Fundulus kansae* — Sandy Creek, TX
  - *Fundulus grandis* — Lula Sams State Fish Hatchery stock, TX
  - *Lucania parva* — Live Oak Peninsula, TX
  - *Adinia xenica* — Live Oak Peninsula, TX
  - *Cyprinodon variegatus* — Live Oak Peninsula, TX
- This juxtaposition of desert spring endemics against Gulf Coast estuarine/freshwater species highlights the broad geographic and ecological distribution of the Cyprinodontidae family, despite disparate habitats.

## Key Findings

|---|---|---|---|---|

| *F. pulvereus* | *C. baileyi* | 4 | 3 | 1 hatched (day 12), died day 14 post-yolk absorption |
| *F. zebrinus* | *C. baileyi* | 5 | 1 | Hatched day 7, died day 8 |
| *F. kansae* | *C. baileyi* | 10 | 3 | Died from fungus, day 10 |
| *F. grandis* (trial 1) | *C. baileyi* | 240 | 15 | Abnormal development, no hatching, died by day 17 |
| *F. grandis* (trial 2) | *C. baileyi* | 212 | 25 | Abnormal development (weak heartbeat, poor vasculature), no hatching |
| *L. parva* | *C. baileyi* | 60 | 8 (from 19 blastodiscs) | 1 hatched (day 12), died day 18; largely normal development |
| *A. xenica* | *C. baileyi* | 22 | 1 (blastodisc only) | Failed to survive gastrulation |
| *C. variegatus* (trial 1) | *C. baileyi* | 27 | 9 (blastodiscs) | Died in late gastrulation |
| *C. variegatus* (trial 2) | *C. baileyi* | 14 | 6 (blastodiscs) | Died in late gastrulation |

### Interpretation
- **Fundulus and Lucania crosses** with *C. baileyi* sperm consistently reached **late embryonic or hatching stages**, indicating relatively compatible genetic/developmental machinery.
- **Cyprinodon variegatus crosses** died uniformly in **late gastrulation** — well past the stage at which purely gynogenetic (sperm-inviable) eggs would normally arrest, indicating genuine **hybrid inviability** rather than fertilization failure.
- The *F. grandis* crosses, while achieving late embryonic stages, showed marked abnormalities (absent heartbeat, underdeveloped blood islands, spinal curvature), suggesting reduced but nonzero compatibility — intermediate between the *Fundulus*/*Lucania* successes and the *Cyprinodon* failures.
- The *Adinia xenica* result is inconclusive due to small sample size (n=1 developing blastodisc).

### Taxonomic Conclusion
- The gradient of hybridization success (**Fundulus/Lucania > Cyprinodon**) parallels the pattern previously established for natural and artificial hybrids among [[Fundulus]], [[Adinia]], and [[Lucania]] versus [[Cyprinodon]] (Newman 1914; Hubbs & Drewry 1960), reinforcing the use of **hybridization compatibility as a proxy for phylogenetic relatedness** (per [[C. L. Hubbs 1955]] correlation hypothesis).
- Findings support placing *Crenichthys* — and by extension its close relative [[Empetrichthys]] — within or near the **Fundulinae**, contradicting classifications (Eigenmann 1920; Jordan 1923) that allied *Empetrichthys* with the South American genus [[Orestias]].
- The bicuspid tooth condition of *Crenichthys*, intermediate between Fundulinae (conical) and Cyprinodontinae (tricuspid), is argued to be **insufficient as a sole diagnostic character** for subfamily separation, since genetic/developmental compatibility data align *Crenichthys* with Fundulinae despite its divergent dentition.

### Methodological Notes
- Gamete-stripping technique followed [[Strawn and Hubbs 1956]] protocol.
- Eggs/larvae reared in 200 mm glass finger bowls at temperatures ranging ~24–29°C.
- Field-to-lab transport mortality (likely cold-related) limited the availability of female *C. baileyi*, restricting experiments to **C. baileyi sperm × heterospecific eggs** rather than reciprocal crosses.
