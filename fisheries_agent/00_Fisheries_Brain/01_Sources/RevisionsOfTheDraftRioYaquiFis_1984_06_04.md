# Revisions of the draft 'Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan'

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1984 internal correspondence from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (Arizona State University), authored by W.L. Minckley, Team Leader, addressed to Jerry Burton of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of Endangered Species. The letter concerns the technical review and revision process for the draft **"Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan."** Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, it documents the collaborative scientific contributions of multiple specialists on three focal taxa — the [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], and [[Yaqui Catfish]] — and outlines editorial, mapping, and coordination requirements needed to finalize the plan. It also flags the need for consistency between this recovery plan and the management scheme for [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]].

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (meristics, coloration, body proportions) are presented in this letter. The document instead references specialist contributions where such data likely reside:
- **[[Yaqui Chub]]** — species account contributed by Hendrickson, incorporating additional data from the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
- **[[Beautiful Shiner]]** — species account contributed by Probst.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** — species account contributed by Kepner, with subsequent additions by the Recovery Team.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The plan's geographic scope is explicitly the **Rio Yaqui drainage** of northwestern México and adjacent southeastern Arizona (a Gulf of California endorheic/exorheic system), not restricted to the mainstem river alone.
- Minckley requests that distribution maps be expanded to cover **all of northwestern México**, enabling complete range delineation for the covered species, and that a base map be supplied if needed.
- Distribution records are to be temporally partitioned into **pre-1978 and post-1978 occurrence data**, implying documented range contraction or shifting population status over time — a common signal of habitat degradation or extirpation in desert fish taxa.
- A forthcoming **Table 1**, to be compiled from [[Hendrickson et al. 1981]] and [[Minckley et al. 1986]], will additionally catalog **marine fish species entering the mouth of the Rio Yaqui**, indicating an estuarine/freshwater interface relevant to the basin's fish fauna.
- The plan is intended to be ecologically and administratively coherent with the **Master Management Scheme for San Bernardino NWR**, underscoring the refuge's role as critical habitat for these species.

## Key Findings
- The **Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan** draft (as of June 1984) integrates species accounts for the [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], and [[Yaqui Catfish]], each contributed by different specialists (Hendrickson, Probst, Kepner respectively).
- Additional technical review was recommended from Dr. David Galat (Arizona State University) and Dr. R.R. Miller (University of Michigan) prior to finalization.
- The Recovery Team intended to finalize the draft at their **July 1984 meeting**, with typed submission targeted for **August 1984**.
- Emphasis was placed on ensuring **spatial and temporal resolution** in distribution mapping (pre-/post-1978 records; full northwestern México coverage) to support accurate status assessment.
- A cross-referenced **marine fish table** was planned to document species entering the Rio Yaqui mouth, broadening the plan's taxonomic scope beyond strictly freshwater/endangered species.
- Coordination was explicitly sought between this plan and the **San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge** management documents to avoid conflicting management directives.
- Internal review assignments were proposed for team members (Stetterud, Hamblin — noted as being geographically remote from San Bernardino) to provide independent perspective on document alignment.
