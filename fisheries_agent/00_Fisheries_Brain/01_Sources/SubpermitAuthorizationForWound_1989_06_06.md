# Subpermit 89-9: Authorization for Woundfin Research (W. L. Minckley)

## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific research paper but a **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regulatory permit** (Subpermit 89-9, under master permit PRT-704930) authorizing Dr. W. L. Minckley and cooperators Dr. M. E. Douglas and Dr. P. C. Marsh to collect the federally endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) from the [[Virgin River]] in Utah. The stated scientific purpose is to assess **genetic diversity** in Virgin River woundfin populations using **protein electrophoresis** and **mitochondrial DNA restriction endonuclease analysis**. While largely administrative/legal in content, the permit provides valuable metadata on approved field sampling protocols, population handling limits, and the institutional/regulatory context for endangered fish research in the American Southwest during the late 1980s.

## Background & Description

The permit itself contains no direct morphometric or anatomical descriptions of *Plagopterus argentissimus*. However, contextual/taxonomic notes can be extracted:

- **Scientific name**: *Plagopterus argentissimus*
- **Common name**: [[Woundfin]]
- **Taxonomic affinity**: Historically placed in Cyprinidae, distinguished from other North American minnows by unique spinous dorsal ray and lack of scales — traits associated with the genus *Plagopterus*, part of the endemic western North American [[Plagopterinae]] lineage.
- Downstream genetic/morphological work proposed in this permit (electrophoretic and mtDNA analysis) implies the research aimed to resolve **population-level morphological and genetic diversity**, though results are not reported in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: [[Virgin River]], Utah — a warm, turbid, sand/silt-bottomed desert stream in the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Life stage targeted**: Young-of-year (YOY) woundfin specifically targeted for collection, suggesting a focus on recruitment-stage genetic sampling.
- **Sampling design**: Collections mandated to be spatially distributed — at least **3 sampling sites**, each separated by **≥1 mile**, reflecting an ecological sampling strategy to capture spatial genetic structure across the river reach rather than a single localized population sample.
- **Co-occurring endangered fish taxa** (per PRT-704930 species list, same general Southwest ichthyofauna context):
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
  - [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
  - [[Kendall Warm Springs Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus thermalis*)
  - [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
  - [[June Sucker]] (*Chasmistes liorus*)
  - [[Greenback Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki stomias*)
  - [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*)
- This co-listing underscores the Virgin River and broader Colorado River Basin as a hotspot of endemic, critically imperiled freshwater fish diversity.

## Key Findings

Because this document is a regulatory permit rather than a study report, "key findings" pertain to **authorized research parameters and legal/administrative conditions** rather than empirical ichthyological results:

1. **Collection limits**: A maximum of **30 young-of-year woundfin** total, with **no more than 10 individuals per sampling site**, could be legally collected under this subpermit.
2. **Excess bycatch protocol**: Any woundfin captured beyond authorized numbers had to be **immediately released at the point of capture**.
3. **Research methods authorized**:
   - **Protein electrophoretic analysis** (assessing allozyme variation)
   - **Mitochondrial DNA restriction endonuclease analysis**
   - Combined, these methods aimed to characterize **genetic diversity and population structure** of woundfin in the Virgin River — a conservation genetics approach standard for the era.
4. **Regulatory chain of custody**: All woundfin specimens, even if incidentally taken, legally remained **property of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service**, with final disposition authority resting with the Assistant Regional Director of Law Enforcement.
5. **Institutional coordination requirement**: All field activities required prior coordination with the Utah Fish and Wildlife Enhancement office, reflecting **strict multi-agency oversight** typical of Endangered Species Act Section 10(a) research permits (statutory basis: **16 USC 1539(a)**, **16 USC 1533(d)**; **50 CFR 17.22, 17.32**).
6. **Reporting obligations**: A full activity report was due by **December 31, 1989**, with mortality/escape events required to be reported within 24 hours — indicating tight regulatory monitoring of impacts to this endangered species during research.
7. **Permit duration**: Subpermit valid only through **December 15, 1989**, under master permit PRT-704930 (effective 5/19/88–5/31/90), situating this study within a specific, time-bound conservation genetics research effort on [[Endangered Freshwater Fish of the Southwest United States]].
