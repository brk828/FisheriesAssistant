## Executive Summary

This document is a 1993 administrative reminder notice from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services State Office) addressed to researchers, including Dr. W.L. Minckley, working under Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811 on [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] populations in the [[Grand Canyon]] section of the [[Colorado River]]. Rather than presenting novel biological or morphological data, the letter outlines mandatory **reporting requirements** tied to incidental take provisions in biological opinions issued for scientific collecting permits granted by the National Park Service. The notice underscores administrative and regulatory coordination among federal, state, and tribal agencies overseeing endangered fish conservation research in this critical habitat reach.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source document. The text is strictly regulatory/administrative in nature. For species-level morphological characteristics, refer to primary research literature on [[Humpback Chub]] (family [[Cyprinidae]]) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (family [[Catostomidae]]), both of which are endemic, ESA-listed big-river fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]] noted for distinctive body forms (a pronounced dorsal hump in *Gila cypha* and a bony nuchal keel in *Xyrauchen texanus*) adapted to swift, turbulent riverine flow.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Both species inhabit the mainstem and tributary reaches of the [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon National Park]], a designated critical habitat and focal research area for endangered fish conservation.
- **Conservation Status**: Both [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act, necessitating incidental take permits (Subpermit PRT-676811) for any research-related handling, injury, or mortality.
- **Regulatory Ecology Context**: The document references the involvement of multiple stakeholder agencies managing this shared aquatic habitat, including the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Hualapai Tribe]], [[Navajo Nation]], and the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program — reflecting the multi-jurisdictional ecological management of the Colorado River ecosystem, likely influenced by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affecting downstream habitat conditions.
- **Research Network**: The letter identifies an interagency Adaptive Management/Advisory Coordination Team (ACT) receiving trip reports on adverse effects and unusual conditions observed in humpback chub, suggesting ongoing monitoring of population health and river conditions as they relate to endangered species persistence.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory Framework**: All deaths or injuries of [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] incurred during authorized research must be reported to the Service's Region 6 office in Denver, with dead specimens salvageable for scientific study only by employees of federal land management agencies, state conservation agencies, or the Service.
- **Reporting Timeline**: Mortality/injury events must be reported within **five days** to the Service in Washington, D.C., which determines specimen disposition; all such activities must be coordinated through the State Supervisor, Ecological Services, Phoenix.
- **Trip Reporting Requirement**: Biological opinions mandate that permit holders submit **trip reports every one to two months**, documenting any adverse effects or unusual conditions observed in [[Humpback Chub]] populations, distributed to the Service and other Advisory Coordination Team (ACT) members.
- **Multi-Agency Permitting**: Researchers are reminded that additional endangered species permits may be required from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Hualapai Tribe]], [[Navajo Nation]], or National Recreation Area authorities, reflecting overlapping jurisdictional authority over the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River.
- **Named Research Stakeholders**: Key researchers and institutions identified in distribution include Dr. W.L. Minckley and Dr. Paul Marsh ([[Arizona State University]]), Dr. Eugene Maughan ([[University of Arizona]] Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit), Dr. Rich Valdez ([[BIO/WEST]]), and Dr. Owen Gorman and Dr. Stuart Leon (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service satellite offices), indicating an active, coordinated research consortium studying these species circa 1993.
- **Administrative Intent**: The overarching purpose is compliance assurance — ensuring all research entities maintain current reporting status to support the Service's broader Endangered Species Act obligations regarding [[Incidental Take]] provisions for these two federally protected Colorado River fish species.
