# Gravitational Potential Energy and Active Deformation in the Southwestern US; Crystal Structure of Rab GDP-dissociation Inhibitor

#geophysics #structural-biology #lithosphere #protein-structure

## Executive Summary

This document combines two unrelated journal articles, both from *Nature* (1996). Neither addresses fish, aquatic organisms, or aquatic ecosystems. The first article is a geophysical study of lithospheric gravitational potential energy (PE) and its relation to active deformation in the western United States. The second is a structural biology paper on the crystal structure of bovine Rab GDP-dissociation inhibitor (GDI). The content is therefore outside the scope of an ichthyology or aquatic ecology note, and the aquatic fields are recorded as N/A.

## Background & Description

**Article 1: Gravitational PE in the Cordillera.** The 800–1,600 km wide Cordilleran orogen of the western US shows several active tectonic styles: strike-slip motion in California, extension in the [[Basin and Range]], contraction in the Pacific Northwest, and relatively undeformed uplifted crust in the [[Sierra Nevada]] and [[Colorado Plateau]]. The interior plateau averages 1.5–2.2 km in elevation, and some ranges reach 3.0–4.0 km. The authors compare two classes of explanation: plate-margin or basal tractions, and buoyancy from spatial differences in [[Gravitational Potential Energy]]. They calculate PE using crustal density structures from seismic refraction profiles and the assumption of local [[Isostasy]].

**Article 2: Rab GDI structure.** [[Rab GDP-dissociation Inhibitor]] (GDI) regulates the membrane cycling of [[Rab GTPases]] in vesicle transport. GDI is related by sequence-conserved regions to the [[Choroideraemia Gene Product]] (CHM), which is linked to late-onset retinal degeneration when defective. The crystal structure was solved at 1.81 Å resolution by multiple isomorphous replacement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The first article treats environmental and geological context only in terms of lithospheric and crustal processes. It discusses:

- Contrasting PE sources: the Basin and Range is linked to a buoyant mantle, while the Rocky Mountains store much of their PE in the crust, probably from the [[Laramide Orogeny]].
- The [[Rio Grande Rift]] as the only part of the southern Rockies with significant late Cenozoic deformation, probably linked to higher mantle buoyancy and heating.
- Mid-ocean ridges appear as small positive APE values under the chosen reference state, consistent with their weak lithosphere.

The second article is a molecular study with no environmental or ecological context.

## Key Findings

**Article 1: Potential energy and deformation**

- Buoyancy forces from PE variations can produce nearly all active deformation in the southwestern US, apart from strike-slip motion at the Pacific–North America boundary.
- The reference state is an asthenospheric column with its top 2.4 km below sea level. Positive APE implies deviatoric tension, and negative APE implies compression.
- The most positive APE values lie in the [[Great Basin]] and the Rio Grande rift, not in the highest topography. Near-zero values in the southern Basin and Range match low extensional strain rates. Negative values in the Great Plains and coastal California match observed compression.
- Predicted Great Basin extensional strain rates are compatible with geodetically observed rates across the province. The [[Colorado Plateau]] and [[Rocky Mountains]] are too strong to deform significantly under these PE stresses, except along the Rio Grande rift.
- Independent validation: APE estimated from the seismic data matches APE derived from the [[Geoid]] when averaged over physiographic provinces.
- Stated uncertainties include mantle-density assumptions (about 50 kg m⁻³), with APE errors reaching roughly 7.5 × 10¹¹ N m⁻¹ in the Rockies and Plains. Elevation errors are less than about 200 m in the Basin and Range, rising to about 500 m in the Rockies and Plains.
- The conclusion is that external forces, such as [[Plate Kinematics]] or basal tractions, are not required to explain active deformation in the Cordillera interior.

**Article 2: Rab GDI structure**

- GDI has two domains: a large, mostly β-sheet domain I (about 340 residues) and a smaller α-helical domain II (residues 119–219, about 100 residues).
- Domain I is structurally similar to [[Flavoproteins]] that act as monooxygenases and oxidases. DALI searched 642 Protein Data Bank structures. The top three alignments were p-hydroxybenzoate hydroxylase (z = 15.6), cholesterol oxidase (z = 12.2), and glucose oxidase (z = 11.2).
- Two sequence-conserved regions form the GDI choroideraemia consensus domain (GCD), which lies at the apex of the molecule. Site-directed mutagenesis shows that its conserved residues are critical for binding Rab proteins.
