## Executive Summary

This 1984 report to the Water and Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Coordinating Committee (WUCRECC) delineates "sensitive areas" — river reaches critical to the survival of three endemic, endangered big-river fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], the [[Humpback Chub]], and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. Using electrofishing catch rates, larval/YOY seine data, and radio-telemetry of tagged adults, the Biological Sub-Committee mapped life-stage-specific distributions, concentration areas, nursery habitats, and spawning grounds across the Colorado, Green, Yampa, White, and Duchesne Rivers. The report establishes quantitative catch-rate thresholds as classification criteria for each habitat category, forming a baseline for conservation and water-management decisions (e.g., minimum instream flow requirements) in the basin.

## Background & Description

Life-stage size classes are used as primary classification boundaries rather than detailed anatomical description, reflecting the report's management-oriented focus:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — North America's largest native cyprinid, a pikeminnow-like piscivore. Life stages defined by total length (TL):
  - Larvae: <15–25 mm TL
  - Young-of-Year (YOY, post-larval): 25–60 mm TL
  - Juvenile: 60–450 mm TL
  - Adult: >450 mm TL

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Distinguished by a pronounced nuchal hump, adapted to swift, turbulent canyon waters.
  - Adult: >200 mm TL
  - Larval/juvenile stages noted as morphologically indistinguishable in the field from the closely related [[Roundtail Chub]], complicating stage-specific habitat classification.

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Named for its sharp-edged dorsal keel; large-bodied catostomid.
  - Adult: >450 mm TL
  - A further concentration-area classification uses a >475 mm TL threshold.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All three species are large-river, warmwater endemics restricted to the mainstem Colorado River system and require specific mesohabitats at each life stage:

- **Adult habitat**: Deep pools interspersed with cobble/riffle sequences (spawning-associated) and deep canyon reaches (e.g., Westwater Canyon, Black Rocks, Gray Canyon) serving as core adult habitat, especially for [[Humpback Chub]].
- **Spawning habitat**: Gravel/cobble substrates in canyon-bound reaches; confirmed via collection of ripe (strippable egg/milt) individuals co-occurring with radio-tagged adults during the spawning season.
- **Nursery/YOY habitat**: Low-velocity, shallow **backwater embayments** ("within-channel ephemeral embayments with water velocity near 0 fps and depth <3 feet") serve as critical [[Colorado Squawfish]] nursery habitat — a key finding linking river geomorphology to recruitment.
- **Migration corridors**: Long-distance spawning migrations documented via radio telemetry, with adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] traversing corridors up to ~200+ river miles (e.g., Green River confluence to Gates of Lodore, 0–364 mi) up to two months before/after spawning.
- **Interconnected tributary use**: The Yampa, White, Gunnison, and Duchesne Rivers all provide distribution, concentration, or spawning habitat, underscoring the necessity of maintaining connectivity across the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] mainstem-tributary network.
- **Known barrier**: Taylor Draw Dam identified as an upstream-movement barrier for [[Colorado Squawfish]] on the White River.

## Key Findings

### Colorado Squawfish
- **Adult distribution** documented across Colorado River (Lake Powell–Palisade, RM -16 to 185), Green River (mouth–Lodore, RM 0–364), Gunnison River (RM 0–33), White River (RM 0–156), and Yampa River (RM 0–140).
- **High concentration areas** (catch rate >0.70 fish/hr): Westwater–Loma (Colorado R., RM 125–154); Sand Wash–Yampa confluence (Green R., RM 211–345); White River confluence–Mt. Fuel Bridge (RM 0–21).
- **Concentration areas** (catch rate >0.30 fish/hr): Big Bend–Onion Creek; Ruby Ranch–Gunnison Butte; Little Snake–Juniper Canyon and Juniper Canyon–Round Bottom on the Yampa.
- **Confirmed spawning area**: Green River, Three Fords (RM 148–157); Yampa Canyon (RM 4–31) — verified by co-occurrence of ripe females, telemetered adults, and downstream larvae (<15 mm TL) within 20 miles.
- **Suspected spawning areas**: Cataract Canyon, Loma–Black Rocks, Clifton–Grand Junction (Colorado R.); multiple Green River reaches (Labyrinth Canyon, Tusher Wash, Desolation Canyon, Split Mountain); Yampa River mouth–Warm Springs.
- **Larval distribution** (<25 mm TL): Cataract Canyon–Clifton (Colorado R., RM -18 to 180); entire lower Green River (RM 0–345); Yampa Canyon (RM 0–21).
- **YOY high-density nursery areas** (seine CPE >0.9/10m²): Upper Professor Valley (Colorado R.); Lower Green River–Gray Canyon and Ouray/Jensen reaches.
- **Juvenile high concentration area** (catch rate >0.30/hr): Green River confluence–Gunnison Butte (RM 0–131).

### Humpback Chub
- **Adult distribution** concentrated in canyon-bound reaches: Black Rocks, Westwater Canyon, Cataract Canyon, Moab Canyon (Colorado R.); Gray Canyon, Whirlpool Canyon (Green R.); Yampa Canyon–Cross Mountain (Yampa R.).
- **Concentration criterion**: ≥5 adult captures in one year within deep canyon habitat — met at Black Rocks, Westwater Canyon, Gray Canyon, and Yampa Canyon.
- **Confirmed spawning area**: Black Rocks (Colorado R., RM 135–136) — the only site meeting the strict criterion of ripe females plus deep canyon habitat.
- **Suspected spawning area**: Westwater Canyon (RM 111–125).
- **Larval/juvenile identification challenge**: Morphological similarity to [[Roundtail Chub]] prevents independent life-stage habitat classification; juveniles assumed to co-occur with adult habitat.

### Razorback Sucker
- **Adult distribution**: Gypsum–Confluence and Moab (Colorado R.); Black Rocks–Debeque; Green River confluence–Green River, UT and Sand Wash–Echo Park; Duchesne River mouth–Ouray.
- **Concentration areas** (≥10 individuals/20-mi reach/year): Grand Junction–Clifton (Colorado R., RM 163–180); Green River near Duchesne confluence (RM 247) and Ashley Creek–Island Park (RM 298–308).
- **Confirmed spawning areas**: Clifton (Colorado R., RM 179); Ashley Creek–Split Mountain and Echo Park (Green R.); Ashley Creek mouth; Duchesne River mouth; Yampa River mouth — all verified by collection of ripe, strippable-egg females.
- **Suspected spawning areas** (≥2 ripe males on gravel/cobble substrate): Labyrinth Canyon, Split Mountain, and Island Park reaches.

### Cross-Cutting Management Implications
- Quantitative electrofishing/seine catch-rate thresholds (e.g., 0.70, 0.30, 0.10 fish/hr; 0.9 and 0.09 per 10 m²) standardize habitat tier classification across species and life stages, enabling reproducible reach-by-reach delineation.
- Findings directly informed protection of backwater nursery habitats and canyon spawning reaches under the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] framework, and support flow-regime and dam-operation decisions affecting migration corridors (e.g., Taylor Draw Dam barrier).
