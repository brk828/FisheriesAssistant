# Vejovis pseudopumilis: A New Scorpion from the Pumilis Group of Baja California

#arachnology #scorpiones #vejovidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, reprinted from the *Pan-Pacific Entomologist* (Vol. 46, No. 3, July 1970, pp. 181–183), by [[Stanley C. Williams]] of San Francisco State College, formally describes *Vejovis pseudopumilis* as a new species of scorpion in the family [[Vejovidae]]. The species was collected during a 1968 entomological expedition through Baja California, Mexico, led by Williams and [[M. A. Cazier]]. It is a tiny, pale yellow scorpion placed in the *pumilis* group of the genus [[Vejovis]]. Its only known close relative is [[Vejovis Pumilis]], which was described from the same expedition. The author names the new species for its strong resemblance to that relative and proposes it as an ecological counterpart of *V. pumilis* in the [[Vizcaíno Desert]]. The paper presents a diagnosis, a detailed morphological description, and a table of holotype measurements. The holotype is deposited in the [[California Academy of Sciences]].

## Background & Description

**Context of discovery.** The 1968 expedition through [[Baja California Sur]] and Baja California collected several new scorpion species. *V. pumilis* was of particular interest for three reasons:
- It is one of the smallest species in the genus.
- It appears to be endemic to the [[Magdalena Plain]], a terrestrial habitat of relatively recent formation.
- It seems largely unrelated to other members of *Vejovis*.

A second, much less abundant species from the same collections was not identified until later. It was judged to be the only close known relative of *V. pumilis* and a possible ecological counterpart in the Vizcaíno Desert.

**Diagnosis.** *V. pseudopumilis* differs from *V. pumilis* in the following ways:
- Pectines are distinctly larger, with more numerous teeth.
- The vesicle (bulb) of the telson is proportionally shorter, about twice the aculeus length versus three times in *V. pumilis*.
- The telson is light amber, not contrasting orange.
- The pedipalp fingers are not scalloped and leave a tiny gap when closed. In *V. pumilis* they are scalloped and show a large gap.

**Morphology of the holotype male.**
- **Coloration:** Uniform pale yellow, with pink pedipalp fingers, light amber telson, and whitish pectines.
- **Carapace:** Anterior border convex. Each group of lateral eyes has two large anterior eyes and a third that is obsolescent.
- **Mesosoma:** Stigmata are tiny and long-oval. Keels on the last sternite and tergite are irregularly granular.
- **Metasoma:** Keel crenulation and serration vary by segment. Inferior median keels carry stout red hairs in counts of 3/4/4/5 pairs on segments I–IV. Segment II is definitely longer than wide.
- **Telson:** Elongate, with a long slender vesicle. The ventral vesicle surface bears several large rounded tubercles, including a large subaculear tubercle, and about 10 pairs of long stout hairs.
- **Chelicerae:** The inferior border of the movable finger lacks denticles.
- **Pedipalps:** Small, slightly swollen chelae with short fingers. The movable finger is 2/3 of carapace length and shorter than carapace width at the median eyes.

**Type material.** The holotype male was collected at San Angel, 13 miles west of San Ignacio, Baja California Sur, Mexico, on 28 June 1968 by S. C. Williams and M. A. Cazier and party. It is permanently deposited in the California Academy of Sciences.

**Etymology.** The name *pseudopumilis* ("false *pumilis*") reflects its close resemblance to *V. pumilis*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper gives little ecological detail beyond collection locality and habitat associations:
- *V. pumilis* is associated with the Magdalena Plain, a relatively young terrestrial landscape.
- *V. pseudopumilis* is proposed as an ecological counterpart of *V. pumilis* in the Vizcaíno Desert. The paper does not give habitat measurements, abundance data, or microhabitat notes for either species.
- The new species was described as "much less abundant" than *V. pumilis* in the collections.

The paper gives no data on prey, behavior, reproduction, or climate.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Vejovis pseudopumilis* Williams, new species, from a single holotype male.
- **Closest relative:** [[Vejovis Pumilis]], from which it is separated by pectine size and tooth count, vesicle proportions, telson colour, and pedipalp finger structure.
- **Biogeographic significance:** The species may be an ecological counterpart of *V. pumilis* in the Vizcaíno Desert, and its relationship suggests a close phylogenetic link within the *pumilis* group.
