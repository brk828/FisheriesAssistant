## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the operations of the **North Attleboro National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1950 under Congressional Act (Public Law 732-79th Congress, 64 Statute 1053). The hatchery's primary mission is to produce [[Atlantic Salmon]] for the restoration of New England river systems, particularly under the **Merrimack River Restoration Program**. The facility also supported limited stocking of [[Rainbow Trout]] in FY93. Data presented spans FY1991–FY1995, covering egg production, fry stocking, funding, staffing (FTE), and fish distribution across federal, state, and private waters in New Hampshire, Rhode Island, and Massachusetts.

## Background & Description

The source document is an administrative/operational station profile rather than a biological or morphological study. No direct morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data on [[Atlantic Salmon]] or [[Rainbow Trout]] are provided. Life-stage terminology referenced includes:

- **Green eggs**: newly fertilized eggs received from broodstock sources (domestic and sea-run lineages).
- **Unfed fry**: early post-hatch juvenile salmon prior to first feeding, the primary life stage stocked into rivers.
- **Broodstock**: 266 three- and four-year-old Atlantic salmon used for egg-taking operations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target Species**: [[Atlantic Salmon]] (primary), [[Rainbow Trout]] (secondary, FY93 only).
- **Habitat Type**: Anadromous freshwater river systems within the [[North Atlantic]] basin drainage, specifically the **Merrimack River** (New Hampshire/Massachusetts) and **Pawcatuck River** (Rhode Island) drainages.
- **Restoration Context**: The hatchery functions within the broader **Merrimack River Restoration Program**, an interstate/interagency effort to reestablish self-sustaining anadromous Atlantic salmon runs in New England rivers that had been extirpated or severely diminished.
- **Broodstock Source Diversity**: Eggs were sourced from two genetic/life-history lineages — domestic (hatchery-reared) salmon and sea-run (wild-origin, ocean-migratory) salmon — received from the **Nashua National Fish Hatchery**, reflecting a genetic diversification strategy typical of anadromous salmonid restoration programs.
- **Distribution Waters**: Stocking occurred across a mix of federal, state, and private waters, indicating cooperative multi-jurisdictional management typical of [[Anadromous Fish Restoration]] efforts.

## Key Findings

### Egg & Fry Production (FY period reported)
- **Total eggs taken**: 1.13 million, from 266 three- and four-year-old Atlantic salmon.
- **Green egg sourcing** (from Nashua NFH):
  - Domestic-origin salmon: 921,000 eggs
  - Sea-run salmon: 143,000 eggs
- **Unfed fry stocked**: 743,257 total (stocked in May)
  - Lower Merrimack River basin (NH): 666,014 fry
  - Pawcatuck River drainage (RI): 77,263 fry

### Notable Operational Context
- The hatchery operates under a decades-long Congressional mandate (since 1950) specifically for [[Atlantic Salmon]] restoration in New England, reflecting sustained federal investment in reversing historical population declines caused by damming, habitat loss, and overfishing.
- Introduction of [[Rainbow Trout]] stocking in FY93 (10,361 fish to private waters) represents a secondary, non-native put-and-take fisheries component distinct from the core native species restoration mission.
- Interagency collaboration with **Nashua National Fish Hatchery** for egg supply underscores a networked hatchery system supporting the [[Merrimack River Restoration Program]].
