# Collecting Permit Application for Endangered Colorado River Fishes

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from Dr. Royal D. Suttkus of [[Tulane University]] to the [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]] requests a collecting permit for endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River]] system between [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and [[Hoover Dam]]. The application is part of a collaborative study with [[Grand Canyon National Park]] to document vertebrate populations and obtain baseline data. Dr. Suttkus requests permission to collect all fish species including endangered taxa such as [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The letter also reports significant findings regarding hybridization, population declines, and historical distribution records.

## Background & Description

Dr. Suttkus is Professor of Biology and Curator-in-charge of Vertebrate Collections at Tulane University's Systematic and Environmental Biology Laboratory in Belle Chasse, Louisiana. He has conducted six float trips down the [[Colorado River]] between [[Lees Ferry]] (River Mile 0) and Temple Bar on [[Lake Mead]] (River Mile 312) from September 1970 to April 1975. His research interests include the status of native versus introduced fishes in Arizona and systematic studies of suckers (Catostomidae) and minnows (Cyprinidae).

The collecting permit application requests authorization to take up to 25 specimens (inclusive of all age groups and both sexes) of each endangered species at any one collecting site at any one time, with provisions for additional seasonal sampling to obtain life history data. Specimens would be transported to [[Tulane University]] for study, with subsamples deposited in the [[U.S. National Museum of Natural History]] and Arizona collections as designated by [[Grand Canyon National Park]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] system has undergone significant alterations due to dam construction, particularly [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and [[Hoover Dam]]. Dr. Suttkus notes evidence of increased hybridization resulting from river impoundments, a pattern he has documented in southeastern rivers. The [[Virgin River]] population of [[Woundfin]] shows alarming decline: in 1967, the ratio of *Plagopterus argentissimus* to *Notropis lutrensis* (introduced [[Red Shiner]]) was approximately 1:3 (127 woundfin to 393 red shiners), but by 1975 the ratio had deteriorated to approximately 1:50-100. This contrasts sharply with earlier reports by Minckley stating woundfin comprised more than 90% of the total fish population in the Virgin River mainstream during the early 1960s.

The [[Virgin River]] habitat is described as swift shallows over stone, sand, or mud bottom, with woundfin historically the only fish capable of tolerating "swift mud" environments (dirty-orange liquid mud with swift current). An irrigation diversion dam on the Virgin River appears to have created a barrier, with only *Notropis lutrensis* found below the dam approximately 8 airline miles southwest of Mesquite, Nevada.

## Key Findings

**Hybridization Documentation**: Dr. Suttkus identified a hybrid between *Xyrauchen texanus* ([[Razorback Sucker]]) and *Catostomus latipinnis* ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]]) collected at [[Lees Ferry]], an expected locality below the barrier created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. This represents evidence of increased hybridization resulting from impoundment.

**Woundfin Population Decline**: Dramatic population shift in the [[Virgin River]] near Bunkerville, Nevada:
- June 17, 1967: 127 *Plagopterus argentissimus* and 393 *Notropis lutrensis* (ratio ~1:3)
- March 1975: Estimated ratio of 1:50-100 woundfin to red shiners at upper site
- Lower crossing site (below irrigation diversion dam): Only *Notropis lutrensis* found, no woundfin

**Historical Distribution Record**: Discovery of a [[British Museum]] specimen of *Plagopterus argentissimus* cataloged December 14, 1883 (former U.S.N.M. number 15776) with locality listed as "Colorado Chiquito River, New Mexico" (now [[Little Colorado River]]). This contradicts Miller and Hubbs (1960) who discredited the Little Colorado River as former woundfin habitat. Dr. Suttkus maintains that *Plagopterus* did occur in the Little Colorado and possibly still does, recommending helicopter reconnaissance of upper sections and consideration of the system as a potential reintroduction site if Virgin River alterations proceed.

**Conservation Recommendations**: 
- Monitor sampling program proposed for entire free-flowing portion of [[Virgin River]] in Utah, Arizona, and Nevada (minimum one series per year)
- Request to accompany Arizona Game & Fish personnel during [[Lake Mead]] netting operations for razorback sucker brood stock
- Documentation of natural spawning areas and collection of young specimens for life history records
- Consideration of [[Little Colorado River]] system as potential introduction site for woundfin if Virgin River dams proceed

**Research Collaboration**: Cooperative investigation with Dr. Roy Johnson, Research Scientist for [[Grand Canyon National Park]], to obtain baseline data on animal status in the inner gorge of the Colorado Canyon, with initial boat trip scheduled April-May 1975.
