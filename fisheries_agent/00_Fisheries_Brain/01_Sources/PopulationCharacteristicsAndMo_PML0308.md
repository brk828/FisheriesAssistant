# Population Characteristics and Movement of Roundtail Chub in the Lower Salt and Verde Rivers, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report (October 2000) from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD), funded by the [[United States Bureau Of Reclamation]] under Cooperative Agreement No. 98-FG-32-0240, documents a two-year study of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) in the [[Lower Salt River]] and [[Lower Verde River]]. The study was prompted by a proposal to raise winter minimum flows from [[Stewart Mountain Dam]] to support a blue-ribbon [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery, and by concern for native fishes in both rivers.

Using [[PIT Tagging]], [[Radio Telemetry]] and [[Mark-Recapture Estimation]], the authors estimated the population at over 6,400 individuals, found the chub to be larger than in most Arizona populations, and found the fish to be largely sedentary. The length-frequency data showed a shortage of juveniles and several apparently missing year-classes, which the authors link to a lack of flood and spring runoff events. The authors judged the population larger and more stable than previously reported, but concluded that two years were not enough to establish stability.

## Background & Description

The study originated in 1998 as a response to a public request for a year-round trout fishery below [[Stewart Mountain Dam]]. Winter minimum discharge (October to May) was proposed to rise from as low as 8 cubic feet per second (cfs) to roughly 50 to 100 cfs. The first phase (Bryan et al. 2000) examined spawning and rearing habitat of native fishes and found populations to be healthy and relatively stable. It also reported successful, though minimal, reproduction and evidence that chub might move between the two rivers to spawn.

Roundtail Chub are designated Wildlife of Special Concern in Arizona (Girmendonk and Young 1997), yet the status of many Arizona populations was poorly known. The second-year objectives were to:

1. Determine the population status of adult roundtail chub.
2. Track individual movements and record spawning habitats.
3. Locate juvenile chub and determine their habitat preferences.
4. Identify potential competitors through species associations.

The study area comprised the lower Salt River (between Stewart Mountain Dam and [[Granite Reef Dam]]) and the lower Verde River (between [[Bartlett Dam]] and the Salt River confluence). Both rivers are regulated by [[Salt River Project]] for hydroelectric power and irrigation serving the Phoenix metropolitan area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling and methods.** The lower Verde River was sampled quarterly from April to August in 1999 and 2000. The lower Salt River was sampled only from April to February because high flows (over 1200 cfs) prevented sampling in spring and summer 2000. Sites were sampled by [[Electrofishing]] from a canoe and by [[Gill Netting]] (up to three nets, 2.4 m x 45 m, mesh sizes 25.4 to 76.2 mm). Fish over 90 mm were PIT tagged in the body cavity, and each capture recorded length, weight, sexual maturity and parasites.

**Habitat.** Chub were most abundant in the upper reaches of the lower Verde, where steep gradients and canyon walls produced numerous [[Pool-Glide Complexes]] next to swift riffles. In the lower Salt River, winter flows as low as 8 cfs confined fish to deep pools. Water conductivity of about 1350 µS/cm and high spring and summer flows made capture difficult, so the authors caution that their distribution and abundance estimates may be biased by gear limitations. Snorkel checks after electrofishing showed that only a fraction of fish at a site were being captured.

**Juvenile sampling.** Only three juvenile chub under 200 mm were collected, by canoe electrofishing, in May to August 2000. Juvenile habitat preferences and relative abundance could not be determined.

**Species associations.** Adult fish were analysed with chi-square tests and the phi-squared coefficient to identify potential competitors and predators.

## Key Findings

### Population and Size Structure

- **Population estimate:** The [[Joint Hypergeometric Estimator]] gave a population of 6,424 individuals (95% CI 5,048 to an upper bound that is illegible in the scan; the abstract gives "over 6,400"). The population was treated as closed, geographically and demographically, with the two rivers combined.
- **Sample totals:** 877 chub were PIT tagged (833 in the lower Verde, 44 in the lower Salt). Another 27 were tagged in the Arizona Canal below Granite Reef Dam and excluded from analyses. Lower Verde sampling covered 42 sites and lower Salt sampling six sites.
- **Recaptures:** 53 of 877 tagged fish were recaptured (6%), and five of these were recaptured twice. The highest recapture count came in August 2000 (20).
- **Size:** Chub attained lengths up to 502 mm and weights up to 1,406 g. The largest fish was a female from the Salt River.
- **Sex-specific size:** Females averaged 409.3 mm and 644.6 g; males averaged 376.1 mm and 464.1 g (both P = 0.000). Males outnumbered females nearly 2:1 where sex could be determined, but sex could not be determined for most fish.
- **Length-frequency:** There was a sizeable population of large fish (over 35 cm) but few small fish (under 34 cm). The authors identified a disparity of young fish and probably at least three missing year-classes.

### Growth

- **Walford growth line:** Based on 20 fish recaptured at least a year after tagging, the asymptotic length (L∞) is 562 mm, and the growth coefficient (k) is 0.90.
- **Monthly growth:** Mean monthly growth from PIT recaptures was 9.6, 1.9, 1.6 and 1.1 mm for 50 mm length groups from 250–300 mm up to over 400 mm (the OCR values are partly garbled).
- **Comparison:** Growth was similar to upper Verde River chub (Brouder et al. 2000), but lower Salt and Verde fish reached much larger sizes. The upper Verde asymptotic length from unpublished mark-recapture data was 525 mm.

### Condition and Health

- **Length-weight:** Salt River chub were more robust than Verde River chub. The Salt River slope was significantly greater (ANCOVA; the scanned p-value is partly illegible). Both slopes were near 3.0, indicating isometric growth.
- **Spawning condition:** Chub began to show spawning colours in February. By late April or early May about 20% of adults were ripe. Few fish were in reproductive condition in fall.
- **Parasites:** Nearly 57% of Verde chub and 32% of Salt chub were infected by *Lernaea cyprinacea* (anchor worm). Yellow grub (*Clinostomum marginatum*), black grub and an unidentified fungus were also recorded. Overall, nearly 60% of chub carried some parasite, yet growth was not reduced relative to other studies.

### Habitat Use

- **Capture habitats:** Verde chub were mainly collected in glides, main-channel pools and lateral scour pools. Salt chub were mainly collected in main-channel pools.
- **Radio-tagged fish:** In the Salt River, glides (44.7%) and main-channel pools (36.5%) were used most. In the Verde River, lateral scour pools (46.8%) and glides (45.5%) dominated. Riffles were rarely used.

### Movement

- **Radio telemetry:** Twenty chub (10 males, 10 females) were tagged in February 2000 and tracked through roughly six months. Only 10 of 20 moved from their release sites. Seven of the 10 movers were originally released in the Verde River. There was one confirmed mortality and three suspected mortalities.
- **Distances:** The furthest Verde movement was 3.5 km upstream, shortly after Bartlett Dam discharge rose from 125 to 700 cfs. The furthest Salt movement was 4.5 km upstream in late May, not linked to discharge changes. Radio-tagged males and females did not differ (P = 0.06).
- **No inter-river spawning movement:** No fish moved from the Salt to the Verde, so the earlier hypothesis of inter-river spawning migration was not supported. No spawning behaviour was observed, even though fish showed spawning colour and expressed gametes.
- **PIT recaptures:** 23 fish (43%) did not move, 18 (34%) moved downstream and 12 (22.6%) moved upstream. Overall mean distance was 2.3 km (SD 2.2): upstream 2.8 km (SD 1.9) and downstream 1.5 km (SD 1.8). The longest movement was 7.5 km downstream in the Verde between spring 1999 and spring 2000. No sex difference was found (P = 0.346).
- **Overall conclusion:** Chub were "somewhat sedentary" with a maximum distance of about 7.5 km and a mean below 2.5 km, and showed no apparent movement tied to spawning.

### Species Associations

- Thirteen additional species were collected where chub occurred. Desert sucker (*Catostomus clarki*) and Sonora sucker (*Catostomus insignis*) were grouped as catostomids.
- Most associations were neutral. The only significant association was a **positive** one between roundtail chub and catostomids (phi² = 0.51; chi-square = 14.54, P < 0.05).
- Gear may have missed small species such as red shiner and mosquitofish, which may prey on or compete with early life stages.

### Conclusions and Recommendations

- The population is larger than previously reported and its baseline data are valuable. However, the authors state that the two-year study was not sufficient to determine stability.
- Flood events and dam discharge appear important to reproduction and recruitment. Reduced recruitment after the April 1998 flood is consistent with the missing year-classes.
- Winter flow increases of only 50 cfs are unlikely to affect chub populations directly. The strongest potential threat is competition from large [[Rainbow Trout]], which needs further study.
- Because a large PIT-tagged population remains in place, continued monitoring is relatively inexpensive and recommended for several more years.
