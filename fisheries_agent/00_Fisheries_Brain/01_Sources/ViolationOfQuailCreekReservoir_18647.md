# Legal Notice Regarding Quail Creek Reservoir Project Violations and Endangered Fish Species Protection

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #ESA-section-7

## Executive Summary

This legal notice from the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund, dated June 22, 1994, addresses violations of the Endangered Species Act (ESA) related to operation of the [[Quail Creek Reservoir Project]] in the [[Virgin River Basin]], Utah. The document notifies the Secretary of the Interior and Bureau of Land Management (BLM) that the project is violating the December 7, 1982 Biological Opinion by failing to maintain required minimum flows of 86 cubic feet per second (cfs) for protection of three endangered cyprinid species: [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*), and [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*). USGS gage data indicate the 86 cfs requirement has been violated approximately 50% of the time.

## Background & Description

The [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) was the only endangered fish species in the project area at the time of initial consultation in the early 1980s. The [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*) was subsequently listed as endangered in 1989 (54 Fed. Reg. 35305, August 24, 1989). The [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) was proposed for listing as threatened on May 18, 1994 (59 Fed. Reg. 25875).

All three species are members of the family [[Cyprinidae]] and inhabit the [[Virgin River Basin]] in southern Utah. The Biological Opinion identifies habitat loss and modification as primary threats to the woundfin's imperiled status, specifically noting that "reservoirs, physical barriers to movement, water diversions, and pollution are the main reasons the woundfin are endangered."

The [[Quail Creek Reservoir Project]] was designed by the Washington County Water Conservancy District (WCWCD) to store water from the [[Virgin River]] and [[Quail Creek]] for human uses. The project required a perpetual right-of-way to approximately 682 acres of public land administered by BLM. The diversion structure was constructed approximately 1,500 feet upstream from the existing Hurricane Canal Company diversion, located approximately 12 miles upstream from the reservoir.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Biological Opinion describes four sections of the [[Virgin River]], with Section One (LaVerkin Springs to Washington Fields Diversion) representing approximately 12 miles of the uppermost portion of known [[Woundfin]] habitat. This critical habitat section was designated to maintain minimum flows of 86 cfs unless natural flows were less than this threshold.

The Environmental Assessment for the project indicates that flows of 86-120 cfs are required to maintain a viable [[Woundfin]] population. The reservoir stores water from natural drainage of [[Quail Creek]] and high water from the [[Virgin River]], diverted through a 60 to 70-inch pipeline from the diversion structure.

The right-of-way grant explicitly incorporated conditions and stipulations from the Biological Opinion, requiring the reservoir to be operated according to proposals submitted to the University of Nevada, Las Vegas for analysis of impacts on the [[Woundfin]] and its habitat.

## Key Findings

**Flow Requirement Violations**: Available USGS gage data demonstrate that the mandatory 86 cfs bypass requirement has been violated approximately 50% of the time, with excessive water diversion to the [[Quail Creek Reservoir Project]].

**ESA Compliance Failures**: The violations encompass multiple ESA provisions including:
- Section 7(a)(1) duty to conserve (16 U.S.C. § 1536(a)(1))
- Section 7(a)(2) prohibition against jeopardizing listed species (16 U.S.C. § 1536(a)(2))
- Section 7(a) procedural requirements for reconsultation following new species listings (50 C.F.R. § 402.16 and § 402.10)

**Missing Biological Opinions**: No Biological Opinion exists discussing project impacts on the [[Virgin River Chub]], nor is there a conference opinion addressing impacts on the proposed [[Virgin Spinedace]].

**Reconsultation Requirements**: The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has been requesting BLM reconsultation since at least October 21, 1991. BLM's failure to reconsult constitutes agency action "unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed" under the Administrative Procedure Act (5 U.S.C. § 706(1)).

**Remediation Requirements**: Any reconsultation must ensure the 86 cfs bypass requirement is met during consultation pendency. The resulting biological opinion must make project operation contingent upon installation, operation, and monitoring of bypass structures and gages that ensure and demonstrate required flows are present in the river.

**Legal Action Timeline**: The notice provides 60 days for remedy before the Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance files suit for declaratory and injunctive relief under the ESA citizen suit provision (16 U.S.C. § 1540(g)(2)).
