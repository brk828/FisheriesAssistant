# Crustacean Parasites of African Freshwater Fishes, Mostly from Lake Tanganyika and the Kivu, Edward and Albert Expeditions

#parasitology #copepoda #branchiura #ergasilidae #argulidae

## Executive Summary

This systematic paper by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] reports parasitic crustaceans (Copepoda and Branchiura) recovered from African freshwater fishes. The material comes mainly from the Belgian expeditions to [[Lake Tanganyika]] (1945-1947) and to [[Lake Kivu]], [[Lake Edward]] and [[Lake Albert]] (1952-1954), held by the [[Institut Royal des Sciences Naturelles de Belgique]]. Fryer also examined museum fishes from elsewhere in Africa. The paper lists at least ten copepod and eight branchiuran species. One copepod, *Ergasilus flaccidus*, is described as new. The author also suppresses the genus *Ergasiloides* and redefines [[Ergasilus]] to include its species. Several records are first reports for the lakes concerned.

## Background & Description

The paper compiles scattered collections into one work, arranged in systematic order, with biological notes added where useful. Specimen numbers are those assigned during the expeditions or in museum catalogues. Host identifications sometimes use local names, such as "Sangala" (*Lates angustifrons*) and "Nonzi" (*Lates microlepis*).

The taxa are grouped into two orders:

- **Copepoda:** Parasitic cyclopoid copepods. Species include [[Lamproglena Monodi]], [[Lamproglena Hemprichii]], [[Opistholernaea Laterobrachialis]], [[Opistholernaea Longa]], [[Lernaea Barnimiana]], [[Lernaea Cyprinacea]] (queried), [[Lernaea Bistricornis]] (queried), [[Ergasilus Kandti]], [[Ergasilus Flaccidus]] and [[Ergasilus Megacheir]], plus an unnamed *Ergasilus* near *E. megacheir* on *Synodontis*.
- **Branchiura (fish lice):** Eight species: [[Argulus Striatus]], *Argulus personatus*, [[Argulus Rubropunctatus]], [[Argulus Schoutedeni]], [[Argulus Rhipidiophorus]], [[Argulus Africanus]], *Dolops ranarum* and *Chonopeltis schoutedeni*.

**Taxonomic actions:**
- The genus *Ergasiloides* Sars (1909) is suppressed. Its species are transferred to *Ergasilus* Nordmann, and the genus is redefined. The distinguishing feature, two versus three abdominal somites, is judged insufficient for separate genera.
- *Ergasiloides* Yamaguti (1939) is a homonym. Its replacement name *Nipergasilus* Yin (1949) must be used, and Yamaguti's date of 1956 is an error. *Nipergasilus* is left provisionally outside *Ergasilus*.
- *Opistholernaea* Yin (1960) is followed rather than placing these species in *Lernaea*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Host associations:** Parasites were found on the gills, in the mouth or buccal region, under the operculum, and attached to the palate or fin bases. Most hosts are cichlids (*Tilapia*, *Haplochromis*, *Boulengerochromis*, *Limnotilapia*, *Cyphotilapia*, *Bathybates*, *Plecodus*), with Latid (*Lates*), catfish (*Bagrus*, *Clarias*, *Synodontis*, *Heterobranchus*) and characin (*Hydrocyon*, *Citharinus*) hosts also represented.
- **Site selection:** *E. kandti* sits low on the gill filament and never near the tip. *E. megacheir* sits near the tip, so its egg sacs protrude beyond the filament. *E. kandti* causes marked gill proliferation, whereas *E. megacheir* only indents the filament.
- **Host tissue:** Some *Lamproglena* and *Opistholernaea* specimens were embedded in host flesh or bone, making intact removal difficult.
- **Lake history:** Fryer notes that [[Argulus Schoutedeni]] may be a recent invader of Lake Tanganyika, possibly arriving after the Lukuga River reconnected the lake to the [[Congo River]] system. Its host, *Citharinus gibbosus*, is non-endemic and is considered a recent invader too.
- **Endemism:** *Argulus striatus* is considered endemic to Lake Tanganyika, and *Argulus personatus* is an endemic Tanganyikan species. *Ergasilus flaccidus* may also be confined to Lake Tanganyika.

## Key Findings

**Records and new localities**
- *Lamproglena hemprichii* is first recorded from Lake Tanganyika and extends its known distribution to the Lower Congo (Banana Creek).
- *Opistholernaea laterobrachialis* was previously known only from Lake Bangweulu. This paper records it from Lake Mweru, on *Tilapia macrochir*, with a single specimen. Its anchor was longer and more pointed than in Bangweulu material, and it lacked the fibrous capsule typical of that material.
- *Opistholernaea longa* is first recorded from Lake Tanganyika, on *Lates* (three specimens).
- *Lernaea barnimiana* had not previously been found on *Haplochromis* in Lake Edward.
- *Argulus schoutedeni* is first recorded from Lake Tanganyika, where previously only *A. africanus* was known among non-endemic *Argulus* species.
- *Argulus rhipidiophorus* is first recorded from Lake Kivu. It is the only parasitic crustacean found in that lake: only two of more than fifty *Tilapia nilotica regani* examined were infected.

**Ergasilus flaccidus (new species)**
- Described from many specimens on *Tilapia tanganicae* in Lake Tanganyika.
- Distinguished by a 5-segmented antennule, a thin-walled distal antennal segment (the basis of the specific name), and a leg-4 armature that is unusual for the genus.
- Its spine formula matches that of [[Ergasilus Megacheir]], which Fryer interprets as independent convergence rather than common ancestry.
- Syntypes are held in the Institut royal collection, with further material in the British Museum (Natural History).

**Ergasilus kandti**
- Recorded from Lake Albert (*Lates niloticus albertianus*, two samples), Lake Tanganyika (many cichlid and other hosts) and elsewhere.
- Shows wide host tolerance.
- Heavy infestations reached 15 specimens on one gill filament of a Lake Albert *Lates*.
- Gill damage is severe in cichlids but minimal in *Lates*.
- Lake Tanganyika specimens show minor antennal denticle differences, which Fryer suggests may indicate incipient allopatric speciation.

**Ergasilus megacheir**
- Redescribed from several Lake Tanganyika hosts, including *Cyphotilapia frontosa*, *Bathybates* spp. and *Plecodus paradoxus*.
- Sars's genus *Ergasiloides* was based on immature specimens, and its generic distinction is reconsidered.
- Fryer notes a *Synodontis*-parasitising form that differs slightly. It may be a separate sibling species, but this cannot be resolved without infection experiments.

**Argulus**
- *Argulus rubropunctatus*: Of all specimens known, 107 are males and 37 are females. Males bear maxillary claspers, an unusual trait within *Argulus*.
- *Argulus personatus*: Only five specimens were known before this collection, all from *Bathybates*. Fryer suggests host preference has influenced argulid speciation in Lake Tanganyika.
- *Argulus africanus*: Two Lake Tanganyika specimens from *Hydrocyon lineatus* show abnormal spines. Fryer warns that such variation could lead to misidentification.

**Other notes**
- Pronounced variation in *Lamproglena hemprichii* abdomen length was seen among four Lake Albert specimens from the same host.
- *Lernaea cyprinacea* may occur in Lake Edward. Large numbers of its copepodid larvae were found on the gills of *Bagrus docmac*, as in Lake Victoria, but this remains unconfirmed.
- *Lernaea bistricornis* is a tentative identification from a single incompletely developed female on *Boulengerochromis microlepis*.

**Summary**
- The collection includes at least ten copepod species and eight branchiuran species, all mostly from Lakes Tanganyika, Kivu, Edward and Albert.
- The author calls for further collection in Lake Edward and for males of *Argulus schoutedeni* to confirm determinations.
