# New Records and Descriptions of Plecoptera (Class Insecta)

#entomology #freshwater-ecology #plecoptera #stoneflies

## Executive Summary

This systematic paper by W. E. Ricker, published in the *Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada* (1965), describes new stonefly (Plecoptera) species and subgenera from North America and revises several known taxa. The paper is dedicated to Dr A. G. Huntsman and documents gaps in the distribution and diversity of North American stoneflies. It describes 15 new species, one new subgenus ([[Bolshecapnia]]), a new combination ([[Isocapnia]] vedderensis), and a new synonymy (*Isoperla minuta* = *Isoperla nana*). Most material is from [[British Columbia]], with additional records from Alberta, the Yukon, Alaska, Quebec, and several US states.

## Background & Description

The paper is a taxonomic study of Plecoptera, a group of freshwater insects that is important in running-water ecosystems. The author notes that stoneflies serve as food for fish, may prey on fish eggs and larvae, and indicate pollution or its absence. Knowledge of their North American distribution was incomplete at the time.

Type specimens are deposited mainly in the [[Canadian National Collection of Insects]] (CNC), Ottawa, and the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]] (INHS), Urbana. Additional material is held by S. G. Jewett, Jr., and by the author.

Morphological descriptions focus on male and female terminalia, which are the key diagnostic structures:

- **Epiproct** (supra-anal process): shape, sclerotization, and the presence of internal probe-like filaments (the "anterior" and "posterior" filaments, which the author names in a footnote).
- **Subgenital plate** (8th sternite): shape, notch depth, and projection beyond the segment margin.
- **Ninth sternite**: presence or absence of a ventral lobe (vesicle), which is widespread in filipalpous stoneflies and considered ancestral.
- **Wing length and venation**: brachyptery (reduced wings) and crossvein patterns, which vary within genera.

Species are named after collectors, localities, or cultural references. For example, *Capnia sasquatchi* is named after the sasquatch legends of the Fraser Valley Salish people.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most described species live in cold, high-elevation or spring-fed habitats:

- **Alpine lakes and cirque lakes**: *Capnia gregsoni* males were collected from snow and eroding ice at the edge of a cirque lake of about 2 hectares.
- **Snowmelt and avalanche margins**: *Capnia spenceri* was found on snow at Consolation Lake (1900 m, Banff National Park), where nymphs emerged through melt holes in 3–6 m of avalanche snow.
- **Cold spring creeks**: *Leuctra sibleyi* was recorded from cool spring streams across the eastern and midwestern US and Ontario.
- **Northern and arctic waters**: *Capnia beringi* ranges from the Bering Sea coast to the Yukon boundary, and *Capnia sugluka* was collected on the shores of Ungava Bay, Quebec.
- **Slow, clear creeks and sloughs**: the wingless *Capnia bergi* was collected in a slow-flowing creek on the lower Yukon River, using a plankton net.

The author proposes that *Capnia beringi* survived the glacial periods in the unglaciated Yukon Valley, while its relative *Capnia oenone* more likely persisted south of the Continental ice sheet.

Some species appear to be aquatic as adults. *Capnia bergi* nymphs and adults were found together, which suggests that adults may remain aquatic, as reported for a wingless *Capnia* from Lake Tahoe.

## Key Findings

**New species and subgenus**

- **Fifteen new species** are described: *Brachyptera fosketti*, *B. zelona*, *Capnia gregsoni*, *C. spenceri*, *C. sasquatchi*, *C. rogozera*, *C. bergi*, *C. cheama*, *C. beringi*, *C. sugluka*, *Nemoura wenatchee*, *Leuctra baddecka*, *Paraleuctra dusha*, *Paraperla wilsoni*, and *Chloroperla ovibovis*.
- **New subgenus** *Bolshecapnia* (type species *Capnia gregsoni*). Its members are large-bodied western North American *Capnia* with a ventral lobe at the base of the male 9th sternite and a female subgenital plate produced beyond the 8th sternite margin. The author places *maculata* Jewett, *gregsoni*, *spenceri*, *sasquatchi*, and *rogozera* in the subgenus. Two Japanese species (*C. bituberculata* and *C. shirahatae*) may belong to it if their ventral lobes were overlooked.

**Brachyptera**

- *Brachyptera fosketti* and *B. zelona* are separated from *B. glacialis* by male epiproct and forewing structure. The author notes that western records previously attributed to *glacialis* do not match eastern specimens. He also suggests that *fosketti* and *zelona* could eventually prove to be variants of one species if collecting reveals intergradation.
- A revised male key separates *glacialis*, *zelona*, and *fosketti*.

**Capnia and Bolshecapnia**

- *Capnia gregsoni* and *C. spenceri* are closely related. Their boundary, or zone of intergradation, is unresolved. *C. spenceri* shows an unusual asymmetric female subgenital plate, which the author states has no parallel among North American stoneflies.
- *Capnia bergi* is wingless in both sexes and has no ventral lobe on the male 9th sternite.
- *Capnia cheama*, described from the Fraser River bridge near Agassiz, is one of the smallest North American stoneflies.
- *Capnia sugluka* is a small species known only from females (Payne Bay, Quebec), with 43 female paratypes.

**Revisions and new combinations**

- *Capnia coloradensis* Claassen: the author shows that Claassen's figure of the 8th tergite illustrated a relaxed specimen, and clarifies male and female characters.
- *Isocapnia vedderensis* (Ricker 1943) is transferred from *Eucapnopsis* to *Isocapnia*, based on thoracic sternal structure (Hanson, 1946).
- *Isoperla minuta* (Banks) is treated as the same species as *Chloroperla nana* Walsh (1862). The author concludes that the diagnostic features of Walsh's description (small size and striped pronotum) match only *minuta* among described stoneflies.

**Nemoura (Ostrocerca) and related taxa**

- The female of *Nemoura truncata* is described for the first time, and new records extend its known range.
- The author provides a new key to the six *Ostrocerca* females and notes that the female of *N. albidipennis* has a sternal nipple that earlier descriptions overlooked.
- *Nemoura wenatchee* is one of the largest members of the subgenus *Malenka*. It is distinguished from *N. cornuta* by size and the margin of the female 8th sternite notch.

**Leuctra and Paraleuctra**

- *Leuctra baddecka* is described from a female (Baddeck Forks, Nova Scotia). The author notes that it may be a *Leuctra*, but the male is needed to confirm its placement.
- *Paraleuctra dusha* is based on a single male holotype from Barkerville, British Columbia. Its asymmetric subanal lobes are unusual, and the author considers that it may be an undescribed species rather than an abnormal specimen.

**Paraperla and Chloroperla**

- *Paraperla wilsoni* is separated from *P. frontalis* by smaller size, less angular head corners, a more sclerotized and straighter male epiproct tip, and absence of lateral stylets. The two type specimens come from near sea level (Vedder Crossing, B.C.).
- *Chloroperla ovibovis* is the only American *Chloroperla* with the described hind wing anal reduction, and it is similar to European *C. burmeisteri*.

**Overall**

The paper extends known stonefly distributions across western and northern North America, improves identification keys for several difficult genera, and resolves a long-standing synonymy. Its main conclusion is that North American stonefly diversity is greater than previously recognized, and that several taxa need further study, especially males of *Leuctra baddecka* and females of some *Capnia* species.
