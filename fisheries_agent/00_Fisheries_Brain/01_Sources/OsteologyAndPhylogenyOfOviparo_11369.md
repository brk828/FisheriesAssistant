# Osteology and Phylogeny of Oviparous Cyprinodont Fishes (Order Cyprinodontiformes)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 doctoral dissertation from the University of Florida by Ram Prakash Sethi uses comparative skeletal anatomy to assess the classification and evolutionary relationships of the oviparous (egg-laying) cyprinodonts of the [[Cyprinodontiformes]]. The author argues that existing classifications, which relied on nutritional and dental traits, were unreliable, and that [[Osteology]] offers a more dependable basis for phylogeny. Using cleared-and-stained specimens of roughly two dozen species from about 14 genera, the study recognises eight osteologically distinct groups, tests earlier subfamily arrangements, and proposes several taxonomic changes, including a new family for [[Aplocheilichthyidae]] and a separate family for [[Oryzias]].

## Background & Description

The order Cyprinodontiformes includes more than 500 species, mostly tropical, and is divided into the blind cave-dwelling suborder [[Amblyopsidae]] (Amblyopsoidea) and the suborder Poecilioidea. Poecilioidea contains a viviparous superfamily, whose classification rests on the male [[Gonopodium]], and an oviparous superfamily that lacks an equivalent diagnostic structure. The oviparous family [[Cyprinodontidae]] is the largest and most widespread group, with more than 300 species.

Earlier workers placed these fishes among the cyprinids and later among the Haplomi. [[Charles Tate Regan]] (1909, 1911) separated them as the Microcyprini, closer to the acanthopterygians. Classifications by [[Gunther Classification]], [[Garman Classification]] and [[Myers Classification]] relied on intestinal length, dentition and external features. The author notes that such adaptive characters have been altered repeatedly and independently. Revisionary work by [[Hubbs Classification]] and Myers, together with later proposals by Hoedeman and Miller, left the subfamily arrangement unresolved.

**Methods.** Specimens were cleared, stained and kept in glycerine, following Davis and Gore (1936) and Evans (1948). Structures were dissected under a binocular microscope and drawn with a camera lucida. Series of juveniles and adults of each sex were prepared where material allowed. Collections cited include the [[Universidade Da Florida]] (UF), the [[United States National Museum]], the [[British Museum of Natural History]] and the Zoologisches Museum, Amsterdam.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution by group.** Group I (cyprinodontids) ranges from the Colorado River system and the eastern and southern United States to the Caribbean coast of South America. Group II (aphanids) occupies the Mediterranean, Red Sea and Arabian Sea margins, extending to the Gulf of Kutch. Group III (fundulids) is New World, mainly North and Central America. Group V (aplocheilids) is nearly worldwide in the tropics, spanning tropical America, Africa, Madagascar and South and Southeast Asia.
- **Habitat preferences.** Cyprinodontids occupy quiet shallows of fresh, brackish and salt water, including desert warm springs, and are seldom found in swift water. Fundulids range from upland streams to marine littoral settings. Many aplocheilids inhabit pools and mudholes that dry up annually.
- **Highland and lake endemics.** *Orestias* is confined to lakes and stagnant river sections of the high Andes, from 7,000 to 16,000 feet, with peak abundance near 12,000 feet in [[Lake Titicaca]]. *Lamprichthys tanganicanus* is restricted to [[Lake Tanganyika]], where the author links its specialised form to the lake's deep, open habitat.
- **Biogeographic significance.** The author notes that the cyprinodonts are chiefly freshwater fishes and should yield useful biogeographic data once their interrelationships are understood. He suggests that the southerly, peripheral distribution of aplocheilids resembles that of primitive freshwater vertebrates.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic groups identified.** The study recognises eight osteological groups:

| Group | Assemblage | Main genera |
|---|---|---|
| I | Cyprinodontids (New World) | [[Cyprinodon]], [[Floridichthys]], [[Jordanella]], [[Garmanella]], [[Cualac]] |
| II | Aphanids (Old World) | [[Aphanius]], [[Aphaniops]], [[Anatolichthys]], [[Kosswigichthys]] |
| III | Fundulids (New World) | [[Fundulus]], [[Lucania]], [[Adinia]], [[Profundulus]], [[Empetrichthys]], [[Crenichthys]] and others |
| IV | Valencids | [[Valencia]] (single species, *V. hispanica*) |
| V | Aplocheilids | [[Rivulus]], [[Aplocheilus]], [[Aphyosemion]], [[Nothobranchius]], [[Cynolebias]] and others |
| VI | Aplocheilichthyids (African) | [[Aplocheilichthys]], [[Micropanchax]], [[Procatopus]] and others |
| VII | Oryziatids | [[Oryzias]] (single genus) |
| VIII | Orestiids | [[Orestias]] (single genus) |

**Major shared characters of oviparous cyprinodonts.** The brain case is incompletely ossified, the maxillae are edentulous, and the orbitosphenoid, basisphenoid and opisthotic are absent. Branchiostegals occur in two groups on each side, and the frontals are large.

**Key corrections to earlier work.**
- Parietals are **absent** in *Cyprinodon* and *Floridichthys*, contrary to Hubbs (1926) and Myers (1931). The basisphenoid is absent in all cyprinodonts examined.
- The **metapterygoid is present** in aplocheilids (Group V), which contradicts Regan's (1911) diagnosis of Poecilioidea. The presence of this bone in seven species confirms that the suborder diagnosis needs revision.
- Regan's claim that New World cyprinodontids possess exoccipital condyles is incorrect. Those condyles are absent in Groups I and III in the way Regan described, but present in Old World genera.
- The lower pharyngeals are never completely fused in any cyprinodont examined. *Empetrichthys latos* lacks the enlarged, fused pharyngeals used in earlier generic diagnoses, so that diagnosis is untenable.

**Independent derivation of Old and New World tricuspid-toothed groups.** Groups I and II share tricuspid teeth but differ in exoccipital condyles, mesethmoid ossification, first-vertebra articulation and other traits. The author therefore supports Hoedeman (1951) and Miller (1955, 1956) that these groups derived independently. He proposes four natural groupings instead of the current two subfamilies: New World tricuspid, Old World tricuspid or unicuspid, New World conical or bicuspid, and *Valencia*.

**Kosswigichthys and Aphanius.** Although *Kosswigichthys* has unicuspid teeth, its osteology (absent parietals, cartilaginous mesethmoid) places it with *Aphanius* and *Anatolichthys*. This agrees with the hybridisation and cytological evidence of Aksiray (1952) and Oztan (1954).

**Empetrichthys and Orestias are unrelated.** The author rejects earlier claims of close affinity. *Empetrichthys* has parietals, a vomer and a posterior myodome, whereas *Orestias* lacks all three. The author adds that Myers' epipleural distinction does not apply, because *Orestias agassizii* has simple epipleurals.

**Aplocheilids as a basal stock.** The author concludes that the aplocheilids are the most generalised cyprinodont group. They share characters with Orestias, fundulids, aphanids, aplocheilichthyids, *Valencia* and *Oryzias*, and have lost or modified traits in other lines. The author proposes that a widespread aplocheilid-like ancestor produced the other groups.

**Proposed taxonomic changes.**
- Create the family **Aplocheilichthyidae** to hold the tribe Aplocheilichthyini and *Lamprichthys tanganicanus*, which the author considers close to *Procatopus*. Two subfamilies are proposed: Aplocheilichthyinae and Procatopinae.
- Create the family **Oryziatidae** for *Oryzias*, based on its non-protractile jaws and absence of a vomer.
- Consider *Orestias* a group of more than generic significance, possibly divisible into several genera.
- Recognise the Fundulinae and Cyprinodontinae as heterogeneous assemblages in their current form.

**Evolutionary trends within fundulids.** Fundulids (Group III) show three skull trends. The *Profundulus–Empetrichthys–Crenichthys* line forms a phyletic unit with a large mesethmoid, a forked posttemporal and 28 to 56 vertebrae. A middle *Fundulus–Adinia* line has constricted epiotics. A *Lucania–Chriopeops–Leptolucania* line has reduced supraorbital processes. The author suggests a *Profundulus*-like stock reached Death Valley by the middle Pliocene.

**Limits of the study.** The author states that the fossil record is incomplete, Group III needs fuller sampling (especially *Fundulus* and *Profundulus*), and soft-anatomy, embryological and genetic data are required before relationships can be settled.
