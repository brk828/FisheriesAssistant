# Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program: Bonytail Plan Conference Call (September 14, 1999)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the minutes-style record of a partner conference call on the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) stocking and rearing plan under the Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program. Federal, state, and tribal-linked partners reviewed the plan's five recommendations and agreed on a near-term approach: two facilities would be in production, two would be held in contingency, and a task force would examine private fish farming. The main constraints were hatchery infrastructure, operations funding, and fish parasites. The call ran from about 9:00 AM to 9:50 AM, and Kirk Koch said he would circulate minutes afterward.

## Background & Description

- **Focal species:** [[Bonytail]] (the document calls it "bonytail" or "chub"), a native fish whose stocking to [[Lake Havasu]] was the subject of the call. [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is also produced at one facility. [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) appears only as a stock raised at the Niland facility, which was concerned about parasites.
- **Purpose:** Track bonytail stocking to date, identify remaining stocking needs over coming years, and confirm roles for the rearing sites in the five-part plan.
- **Participants (named in the record):**
  - Arizona Department of Game & Fish: Duane Schroufe, Larry Riley
  - Bureau of Land Management: Kirk Koch (who led the call), Don Ellsworth, Al Doelker
  - [[Bureau of Reclamation]]: Mike Walker
  - California Department of Fish & Game: Terry Foreman
  - [[Fish and Wildlife Service]]: Chuck Minckley, Pete Stine, Stuart Jacks
  - Marty Meisler, who joined late, around 9:45 AM
- **Recognized partners:** Renne Lohoefener (Stuart Jacks's supervisor), Laura Crum (CF&G coordinator for Niland), Leslie Fitzpatrick (to be involved in private aquaculture), George Brooks, and the Gila Indians (interest in private aquaculture).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The discussion focused on rearing and supply logistics, not field ecology. The points that bear on the species' status are:

- **Juvenile supply:** [[Dexter Hatchery]] can deliver at least 70,000 bonytail fingerlings per year. Pete Stine said a second facility should double that supply. The Service committed to meeting supply needs, referring to a recent Las Vegas conference on native fish.
- **Priority conflicts:** Larry Riley asked how stocking priorities would be set between Mohave and Havasu. The record does not give an answer.
- **Parasites:** Tapeworms are present in the river and in the hatcheries. Pete Stine said infected fish can be treated and that they are not a problem in the lower Colorado River. Chuck Minckley said he treats bonytail with a simple worm medicine before shipping from Dexter. The Niland facility had been free of parasites for some time and was worried about reinfection, possibly from canal water.
- **Site limitations:** At [[Achii Hanyo Facility]], summer heat, parasites, and long-term funding are the stated limits. Pete Stine said production may suffer until the funding problem is solved.

## Key Findings

**1. Bubbling Ponds moved to long-term contingency.** Duane Schroufe said the facility would need significant improvements and that the cost/benefit did not justify use given other options. [[Bubbling Ponds]] was removed as a short-term bonytail facility and reserved as a later contingency for the partnership.

**2. Niland pilot program (one year).**
- Terry Foreman's CF&G office will coordinate, with Laura Crum as the contact.
- About 1,500 fish will be picked up from Achii Hanyo in early October and moved to Niland to start the pilot.
- A contract glitch between CF&G and Imperial remained, but assurance was given that it would be fixed in the short term.
- Minor improvements are needed, including aerators and pumps. Al Doelker said he might have these items. Mike Walker said BR would cover the cost of aerators.
- Terry Foreman said the lab at Niland could have five larger ponds divided for better management. If the pilot succeeds, the program can be extended.

**3. Dexter Hatchery not ready for bonytail growout.**
- The well system needs renovation, which could take up to three years.
- Pete Stine plans to move part of the bonytail brood stock to a second facility to protect the species and expand young production.
- Chuck Minckley confirmed that the October delivery date was firm.

**4. Achii Hanyo production this year.**
- Renne Lohoefener decided that bonytail and razorback production will continue there this year.
- Stuart Jacks wants to set production goals and secure dedicated operations funding from partners for five years. The production goal figure is garbled in the scan and is not reproduced here.
- Don Ellsworth asked that funding be raised at the next executive meeting. Pete Stine recommended developing a [[Five-Year Funding Projection]] for the site to present to executive partners.

**5. Private aquaculture task force.**
- Larry Riley, Stuart Jacks, and Pete Stine agreed that a task force should explore open questions, including permits, contracting, and take.
- Duane Schroufe offered staff support. Terry Foreman said an outside source should not be a barrier, and the partnership could pay according to fish delivered.
- Pete Stine said the [[Private Aquaculture]] concept should be judged on whether it helps recover the species, and that Leslie Fitzpatrick should be involved from the beginning.

**Summary of status (as stated by Kirk Koch):** Niland and Achii Hanyo will be in production this year. The other two facilities are in contingency status. A team will be formed to refine private fish farming options.

**Next steps:**
- Kirk Koch will circulate minutes to all participants.
- Partners will develop the five-year funding projection for Achii Hanyo and take it to the executive meeting.
- The aerator and pump needs for Niland will be coordinated with Al Doelker and Laura Crum.
