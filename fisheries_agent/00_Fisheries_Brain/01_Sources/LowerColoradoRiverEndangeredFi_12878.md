# Lower Colorado River Endangered Fish Project — Phase II Progress Report

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This progress report details Phase II of a cooperative study between the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation and the Arizona Game and Fish Department investigating habitat use of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the Imperial Division of the Lower [[Colorado River]]. Historically abundant throughout the basin, the species has been virtually extirpated from the lower river due to flow regulation, reservoir construction, and introduction of non-native fishes, with extant populations now largely restricted to [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Mead]], and [[Senator Wash Reservoir]]. The species was federally listed as endangered in 1991, with critical habitat designated in 1994 covering the 100-year floodplain of the Imperial Division.

Researchers surgically implanted ultra-sonic transmitters into 26 (Group 1, Nov 1995) and 38 (Group 2, Nov 1996) hatchery-reared razorback suckers of [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]/[[Lake Mohave]] broodstock origin, released them into a 12-km study reach above Imperial Dam, and tracked their movements via boat-based reconnaissance and fixed remote sensing stations. Preliminary Year 1 analysis of Group 1 fish demonstrated statistically significant non-random habitat selection: study fish disproportionately favored the [[Imperial Dam Impoundment]] and [[Backwater Habitat|backwaters]], underused the [[Main Channel Habitat|main channel]], and used [[Side Channel Habitat|side channels]] and [[Squaw Lake]] roughly in proportion to availability. These findings support the hypothesis that razorback suckers, despite being often characterized as habitat generalists, exhibit selective habitat preferences even within a novel, previously understudied lotic reach of the lower basin.

## Background & Description

The report does not provide direct anatomical/morphometric measurements of individual fish, but includes relevant husbandry and tagging details:

- **Species**: *Xyrauchen texanus*, distinguished taxonomically by its characteristic dorsal keel ("razorback"), though this feature is not explicitly described in this report.
- **Study Group 1 (n=26)**: Implanted 14 Nov 1995; sourced from a 1984 year class held at [[Floyd Lamb State Park]] (Nevada) pond; F1 generation of broodstock collected from [[Lake Mohave]] in 1981; raised on natural forage.
- **Study Group 2 (n=38)**: Implanted 24–25 Nov 1996; also of Dexter National Fish Hatchery stock; held ~1 year on natural foods at [[Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery]] prior to surgery; generally smaller body size, resulting in smaller body cavity relative to transmitter size and higher post-surgical mortality (5 of 38 died in captivity).
- **Tagging equipment**: Ultra-sonic transmitters (Sonotronics CT-82-2, 73–80 kHz) and passive integrated transponder (PIT) tags (AVID, extended-read 125 kHz) surgically implanted using sterile techniques adapted from [[Clarkson Et Al 1993]]; incisions sealed with waterproof skin bandage compound.
- **Post-surgical care**: 3-week observation in circulating freshwater tanks; treated with external bactericide (100 ppm formalin) and systemic antibiotic (oxytetracycline)-laced feed.
- **Transmitter signal characteristics**: Frequency output (70–80 kHz) shown to vary with water temperature, complicating individual identification at remote sensing stations.
- **Expected transmitter longevity**: ~14 months (i.e., through end of January 1997 for Group 1).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat types assessed within the Imperial Division study area (12 km reach, bounded by Imperial Dam):**

| Habitat Type | Code | % of Total Available Habitat |
|---|---|---|
| [[Backwater Habitat]] | B | 32% |
| [[Imperial Dam Impoundment\|Impoundment]] | IMP | 5% |
| [[Main Channel Habitat]] | M | 48% |
| [[Side Channel Habitat]] | S | 5% |
| [[Squaw Lake]] | SQL | 10% |

- Habitat was digitized in a two-dimensional AutoCAD database from aerial imagery; mainstem partitioned into twelve 0.8-km reaches.
- Microhabitat variables recorded at sampling stations: depth, velocity, substrate type, temperature, conductivity, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, and percent submerged aquatic vegetation (measured at bottom and mid-depth).
- Site-level descriptors: accessibility to razorback suckers, shoreline emergent vegetation composition, percent shoreline development, and site geomorphic shape.
- **Escape routes** from study area: upstream via the Colorado River main channel, and downstream via the [[Gila Gravity Canal]] and [[All-American Canal]] (both fitted with debris racks generally excluding fish passage, except during periodic rack-cleaning operations).
- **Ecological context**: The Imperial Division habitat assemblage — a long, uninterrupted mainstem reach with numerous backwaters, side channels, and wetland-vegetated borders — differs substantially from upper-basin riverine habitats and lower-basin reservoir habitats (e.g., [[Lake Mohave]]), necessitating site-specific in situ study.
- Extensive search (135 km upstream to [[Palo Verde Diversion Dam]], Aug 1996) failed to detect any emigrated study fish, though elevated background noise may have limited detection capability rather than confirming absence.

## Key Findings

- **Non-random habitat use confirmed**: Chi-square test on Group 1 relocation data (n=154 observations) showed highly significant deviation from expected use-by-availability (χ² = 180.625, df = 4, *P* = 0.0000).
- **Habitat use vs. availability (Bonferroni CI analysis)**:

| Habitat | Observed Use (%) | 95% CI (Bonferroni) | Expected Use (%) | Jacob's D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Backwaters (B) | 42 | 0.33–0.51 | 32 | +0.21 |
| Impoundment (IMP) | 24 | 0.16–0.32 | 5 | +0.71 |
| Main Channel (M) | 10 | 0.04–0.15 | 48 | −0.79 |
| Side Channels (S) | 7 | 0.02–0.12 | 5 | +0.18 |
| Squaw Lake (SQL) | 17 | 0.10–0.24 | 10 | +0.30 |

- **Interpretation**: Razorback suckers strongly preferred the [[Imperial Dam Impoundment|impoundment]] (Jacob's D = +0.71) and moderately preferred [[Backwater Habitat|backwaters]] (+0.21) and [[Squaw Lake]] (+0.30), while strongly avoiding the [[Main Channel Habitat|main channel]] (−0.79). [[Side Channel Habitat|Side channels]] were used approximately proportional to availability (+0.18).
- **Tracking outcomes (Group 1, Dec 1995–Dec 1996)**: 154 total relocation events; only 10 of 26 original fish located in final three surveys; 1 confirmed mortality (visual), 5 suspected mortalities (stationary signal across 3+ surveys); remainder unaccounted for — likely emigrated from study area or lost to transmitter expiration.
- **Group 2 outcomes**: Two post-release surveys located 27 of 33 newly released fish plus 7 of the original Group 1 fish; all Group 2 relocations occurred either within release sites or in a distinctly different habitat site, suggesting active movement/redistribution shortly after release.
- **Methodological limitations**: Remote sensing stations at potential escape routes (Colorado River channel, [[Gila Gravity Canal]], [[All-American Canal]]) were compromised by vandalism, unreliable power supply, and temperature-dependent frequency drift, limiting their utility for confirming emigration.
- **Return of "missing" fish**: The reappearance of some previously unlocated fish after extended absences suggests emigration (rather than mortality) explains many "unaccounted for" individuals, and indicates reduced ultrasonic detection efficacy in faster, noisier upper reaches of the Imperial Division.
- **Management implication**: Preliminary results (Year 1 of a 2-year study) support refining the razorback sucker [[Habitat Assessment Indices]] developed in Phase I ([[Bradford And Vlach 1995]]), with data suggesting that impoundment and backwater habitats are disproportionately important for conservation and restoration planning in the Lower Colorado River.
- **Caveat**: Findings are explicitly preliminary and pertain only to the first year of a two-year study; author (R.H. Bradford) notes a supplementary analysis (post-report) combined [[Squaw Lake]] with backwaters and incorporated data through May 1997 for logistic regression purposes, though those detailed results are not fully reproduced in this report.
