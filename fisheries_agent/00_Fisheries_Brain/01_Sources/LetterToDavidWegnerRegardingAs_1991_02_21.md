## Executive Summary
This document is a 1991 administrative letter from ASU/NNHP co-investigators [[Paul C. Marsh]] and [[Michael E. Douglas]] to [[David Wegner]] of [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES). It does not present primary field data, but instead outlines critical logistical and methodological requirements for an ongoing field research program studying the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and associated native fishes in the [[Little Colorado River]]. Key issues raised include the status of Passive Integrated Transponder (PIT) tag acquisition, the scientific rationale for tagging non-target native species, communications infrastructure, off-campus logistics support, and contractual delays affecting research timelines. The letter is valuable as a primary source documenting the applied research infrastructure and multi-species tagging strategy underpinning early 1990s [[Colorado River Basin]] native fish conservation studies.

## Background & Description
No morphological data are presented in this correspondence. The letter references four native fish taxa relevant to the [[Little Colorado River]] aquatic ecosystem, without physical descriptions:
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — primary target species of the ASU/NNHP research program.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) — secondary tagging target.
- **[[Bluehead Sucker]]** (*Catostomus discobolus*) — secondary tagging target.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*) — secondary tagging target.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- All four species co-occur within the **[[Little Colorado River]]** system, a tributary ecosystem of the greater **[[Colorado River]]** in the Grand Canyon region, functioning as critical spawning and refuge habitat for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]].
- The researchers emphasize that understanding population dynamics of co-occurring native fishes (dace and suckers) is considered **critical to the "ultimate management of the Little Colorado River aquatic ecosystem"**, indicating a community-level, multi-species approach to conservation rather than a single-species focus.
- Logistical constraints (helicopter-supported base camps, remote field access, off-site personnel coordination) reflect the **remote, canyon-bound riverine habitat** and the operational difficulty of conducting mark-recapture studies in this environment.
- Coordination with agencies including the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Navajo Native Heritage Program]], and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] underscores the multi-jurisdictional ecological management context of the [[Little Colorado River]] as tribal, state, and federal interests intersect.

## Key Findings
- **PIT Tagging Program Scale:** The ASU/NNHP proposal specifies a requirement for **six (6) PIT tag scanners** and a minimum of **~10,000 PIT tags** (2,500 per field-year) for [[Humpback Chub]] research alone.
- **Multi-species Tagging Rationale:** The technical proposal identifies the importance of implanting PIT tags in non-target native fishes — [[Speckled Dace]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] — despite internal debate within the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] (ACT) about whether this expanded tagging effort was warranted.
- **Additional Tag Requirement:** If approved, the multi-species tagging component would require an additional **2,000 PIT tags** (500 per field-year), with annual quantities subject to adjustment based on incoming data.
- **Infrastructure Needs:** The research program required a **radio communications system** for field researchers and at least **800 sq ft of secure, dry off-campus storage** in Flagstaff, Arizona, for equipment and supply staging.
- **Personnel Logistics Gap:** The letter identifies an unbudgeted but "mandatory" need for a dedicated Flagstaff-based logistics coordinator (~half-time during field season) to manage supply chains, waste disposal, helicopter transport coordination, and inter-agency liaison — revealing a gap between the original technical/cost proposals and actual field operation needs.
- **Contractual Delay:** As of the letter's writing, no signed contract or formal notice to proceed had been received from the **U.S. Bureau of Reclamation**, delaying both fieldwork and expenditure schedules on the [[Little Colorado River]] project.
- **Institutional Collaboration:** The research was conducted under the joint banner of [[Arizona State University]]'s Center for Environmental Studies and the [[Native Nations Heritage Program]] (NNHP), with oversight/coordination through [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES).
