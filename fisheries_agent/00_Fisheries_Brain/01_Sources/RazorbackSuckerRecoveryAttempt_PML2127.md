# Razorback Sucker Broodstock Capture Attempt, Lake Mohave (1973)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (dated January 31, 1974) reports a failed attempt to collect [[Razorback Sucker]] broodstock from [[Lake Mohave]] on October 6, 1973. Twenty-nine suckers were captured with [[Gill Net]]s, held in a [[Live Car]] in the lake, and transferred to a hatchery truck. All captured suckers died within about four days, despite appearing healthy at transfer. The author concludes that the capture and holding methods, not the fish's condition at capture, were the likely problem, and recommends testing [[Electrofishing]] and a larger, better-designed live car.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]], a large catostomid sucker. The document refers to it only by common name.
- **Purpose:** Collection of broodstock for the [[Chino Fish Hatchery]] and related hatchery operations, as stated in the memo.
- **Participants:** Biologists from the California Department of Fish and Game, the Nevada Fish and Game Department, and the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife (BSFW), as listed in the memo.
- **Reporting chain:** The results were distributed to recovery team members on November 12, 1973, at a recovery team meeting in Tops, and the memo was written as a follow-up.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Capture site:** [[Lake Mohave]], Nevada.
- **Capture method:** Gill nets set in the late afternoon and not checked until the following morning. The memo describes this as the initial method.
- **Holding conditions:** Fish were held alive in a live car in the lake. The car was built of material that would not injure the fish. The fish appeared to be in good condition at transfer and showed no signs of stress.
- **Transport:** Fish were moved from the live car to a hatchery truck equipped with a lot of water and an aeration system.
- **Stressors noted:** The memo mentions no stress at transfer. The author notes that the fish were held overnight in the live car, which may have contributed to mortality.

## Key Findings

1. **Catch:** 29 [[Razorback Sucker]]s were collected on October 6, 1973.
2. **Biometric data:** 24 suckers were weighed, measured, and placed into the hatchery truck. The table in the attachment lists total length (cm) and weight (g) for each fish. Lengths range from roughly 45 to 61 cm, and weights from 1,350 g to 3,680 g. Some OCR values are uncertain, so these ranges are approximate.
3. **Mortality:** By October 10, 1973, all suckers that had been transferred to the hatchery truck had died.
4. **Necropsy and sampling:**
   - Five dead fish were taken to Mel Willis at the river hatchery for pathological study. The specimens had deteriorated too much for any study.
   - Scales were removed from 12 of the suckers, and otoliths were taken from one fish. The author did not feel confident in reading the scales or otoliths accurately.
   - Live suckers taken to Mel Willis on November 23, 1973 had also decomposed.
   - Some remaining fish were preserved for [[Skeletal Analysis]].
5. **Author's assessment:** The fish were healthy at transfer. The memo points to the capture and holding process as the probable cause of death.
6. **Recommendations:**
   - Test [[Electrofishing]] as a possibly better capture method than gill netting.
   - Do not leave captured fish in the gill net overnight.
   - Use a larger live car that would let fish forage on the bottom, possibly with a floating design.
   - Hold captured fish in the live car for a set period to determine whether the capture methods have lasting effects.

**Signed:** Junior Aquatic Biologist (Alan Pickard, Blythe office, per OCR).
