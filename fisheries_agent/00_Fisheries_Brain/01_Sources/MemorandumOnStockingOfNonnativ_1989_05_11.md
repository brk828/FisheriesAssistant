# Memorandum on Stocking of Nonnative Fishes (Colorado River Recovery Program, 1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #conservation-policy #Ictaluridae #Osmeridae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This 1989 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] addresses management concerns over proposed introductions of nonnative fish species into the Upper [[Colorado River Basin]]. The document does not present primary morphological or ecological field data, but instead codifies a regulatory and evaluative framework for assessing risk posed by nonnative fish stocking to rare and endangered native fishes. Two specific stocking proposals prompted the review: (1) introduction of [[Channel Catfish]] into [[Kenney Reservoir]], and (2) introduction of [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Powell]]. The memo invokes Section 4.4.3(a) of the Recovery Program, which restricts nonnative stocking to areas where absence of conflict with rare/endangered species can be demonstrated, and calls for a joint state-federal review process to resolve disputed introductions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Recipient ecosystem**: Upper [[Colorado River Basin]], specifically two water bodies:
  - [[Kenney Reservoir]] — proposed recipient of stocked [[Channel Catfish]] (Colorado Division of Wildlife draft lake management plan).
  - [[Lake Powell]] — proposed recipient of stocked [[Rainbow Smelt]] (per a January 7, 1988 draft proposal, later revised).
- **Conservation concern**: Both proposed nonnative species are flagged as potential ecological threats to native, rare, or federally endangered [[Colorado River Fishes]] through mechanisms of **competition** and/or **predation**.
- **Regulatory niche framework**: Section 4.4.3(a) of the Recovery Program establishes that nonnative stocking (including augmentation of existing nonsalmonid populations and new introductions of any nonnative species that competes with or preys upon rare species) is only permissible in habitats where absence of conflict with rare/endangered native species can be affirmatively demonstrated.
- **Stakeholders defining niche interactions**: The [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] (CDOW) proposes lake management plans; the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service review these plans for ecological risk to native species assemblages.

## Key Findings
- Concerns were formally raised at a Management Group meeting regarding nonnative fish stocking proposals that could compete with or prey upon rare [[Colorado River Fishes]].
- Two specific proposals were flagged for review:
  1. Stocking of [[Channel Catfish]] into [[Kenney Reservoir]] (CDOW draft lake management plan).
  2. Stocking of [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Powell]] (draft proposal dated January 7, 1988; reportedly later revised).
- The Recovery Program's Section 4.4.3(a) policy mandates that nonnative stocking be confined to locations where no conflict with rare/endangered species can be demonstrated, applying to both augmentation of existing nonsalmonid populations and new nonnative introductions.
- The [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] submitted formal comments (February 1, 1989) opposing/evaluating the rainbow smelt proposal for Lake Powell.
- The Technical Group was tasked with reviewing both site-specific stocking proposals and the Service's general evaluation procedures, with a report and recommendations due at the Management Group meeting scheduled for June 14, 1989.
- Supporting materials compiled for review included prior CDOW lake management plans previously evaluated by the Service, and a proposed set of standardized factors/criteria for evaluating future nonnative stocking proposals — indicating movement toward a more formalized risk-assessment protocol.
- This memorandum illustrates an early institutional mechanism for balancing state fisheries management (stocking for sport/recreational fisheries) against federal endangered species conservation mandates within a shared river basin.
