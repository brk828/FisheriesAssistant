## Executive Summary
This technical note describes a method developed at the U.S. Fish-Cultural Station in Tishomingo, Oklahoma, for tattooing individual identification numbers onto [[Channel Catfish]] brood stock. The study addresses the practical need for long-term, year-to-year individual tracking of hatchery broodfish. Researchers tested tattoo placement at three body sites (caudal peduncle, belly, pelvic girdle) and found that the skin overlying the **pelvic girdle** provided the optimal combination of firm underlying support (for needle penetration) and light pigmentation (for ink contrast), yielding tattoos that remained legible after one year. The paper also outlines equipment specifications and infection-prevention protocols relevant to captive brood stock management.

## Background & Description
- **Body regions tested for tattoo application:**
  - **Caudal peduncle** – dark pigmentation; insufficient contrast with black ink.
  - **Belly (ventral surface)** – white/light skin gave good contrast, but the tissue lacked firm underlying support, so the needle did not penetrate deeply enough to deposit ink permanently; tattoos faded over time (e.g., a "9" applied here faded within a year).
  - **Pelvic girdle region** – skin overlying this area combines light pigmentation (for contrast) with firm skeletal support beneath, allowing adequate needle penetration and durable ink retention.
- **Tattoo durability observations:**
  - A tattoo number "2" on the pelvic girdle remained clear after 1 year.
  - A tattoo number "9" on the belly faded significantly over the same period.
  - A 6-week-old tattoo on the pelvic girdle skin showed proper, dense application.
- **Tattoo quality criteria:** Numbers should appear uniformly black and very dense; ink penetration is verified by wiping the area with a cloth after application.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
This note is methodological rather than ecological in focus, pertaining to captive [[Channel Catfish]] brood stock maintained in hatchery troughs at an aquaculture research station rather than a natural aquatic habitat. Relevant husbandry context includes:
- Fish held in troughs are prone to skin/tissue infections and erosion at tattoo sites due to prolonged captivity and handling stress.
- Infection risk was mitigated through **antibiotic prophylaxis** (penicillin injection) or **topical disinfection** of the tattoo site, indicating attention to fish health management within a controlled brood stock program (see [[Fish Hatchery Management]]).

## Key Findings
- The **pelvic girdle region** is the optimal tattoo site for channel catfish due to the combination of light skin coloration (ink contrast) and firm underlying tissue (needle penetration/ink retention).
- Tattoos on the **caudal peduncle** are unsuitable due to poor contrast from dark pigmentation.
- Tattoos on the **belly** provide good contrast but fail structurally because the soft tissue does not allow deep enough needle penetration for permanent marking.
- Proper tattoo application requires:
  1. Removing mucus from the pelvic area prior to tattooing.
  2. Using a pricking (not writing) motion to trace numbers multiple times.
  3. Verifying ink penetration by wiping the area.
- Fish can be immobilized via **anesthesia** or manual restraint by an assistant during the procedure.
- **Infection prevention** for trough-held brood stock can be achieved via:
  - Injection of 10,000 units of penicillin, or
  - Swabbing the tattoo site with disinfectant.
- **Equipment used:**
  - Combination electric tattoo machine with broad-line needle
  - Foot switch
  - Switchboard with rheostat
  - Four 6-volt dry batteries
  - 1/2-ounce bottle of Pelican concentrated black ink
- This tattooing method enables reliable **individual identification of brood stock across multiple years**, supporting selective breeding and long-term monitoring programs in channel catfish aquaculture (see [[Broodstock Identification Methods]]).
