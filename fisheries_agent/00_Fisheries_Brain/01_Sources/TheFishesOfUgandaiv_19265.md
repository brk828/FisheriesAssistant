# The Fishes of Uganda IV: Cyprinodontidae, Centropomidae, Cichlidae, Anabantidae and Mastacembelidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #cichlidae #centropomidae

## Executive Summary

This chapter of the *Uganda Journal* series *The Fishes of Uganda* provides identification keys, species descriptions, ecological notes and distribution records for five freshwater fish families: the killifishes ([[Cyprinodontidae]]), the [[Lates]] (Nile Perch) family ([[Centropomidae]]), the cichlids ([[Cichlidae]]), the climbing gouramis ([[Anabantidae]]) and the spiny eels ([[Mastacembelidae]]). Coverage is taxonomic and descriptive. It identifies the number of species recorded for Uganda, their diagnostic characters, habitat and food where known, and their distribution across the major lakes and rivers. It also notes gaps in knowledge, including unresolved taxonomy and unknown breeding biology for several species. The chapter is an early regional reference rather than a quantitative study, and it contains no sample sizes or survey statistics.

## Background & Description

**Cyprinodontidae (killifishes).** Small, often brightly coloured fishes with scales covering the body and head, a protractile mouth directed upwards, no lateral line, and no spines in the fins. African members are oviparous and carnivorous. Four genera are treated:

- **[[Nothobranchius]]**: one species in Uganda, *N. taeniopygus*. Fishes are up to 5 cm long, with a dorsal fin originating at or slightly in advance of the anal fin.
- **[[Aplocheilichthys]]**: four species, and possibly a fifth, recorded from Uganda. Species are separated by scale counts, fin ray counts and caudal fin length. The genus includes *A. pelagicus*, *A. kassenjiensis*, *A. pumilus* and *A. eduardensis*, with *A. mahagiensis* discussed as a close relative of *A. pumilus*.
- **[[Hypsopanchax]]**: one species, *H. deprimozi*, distinguished by a deep body and 19–21 anal fin rays.
- **[[Cynopanchax]]**: one apparently rare species, *C. bukobanus*, known only from its type series and the Bukoba area.

**[[Lates]] (Centropomidae).** Deep-bodied, compressed fishes with ctenoid scales and a dorsal fin almost completely divided by a deep notch. Only one genus, *Lates*, occurs in African freshwaters. Two subspecies of *Lates niloticus* were formerly recognised in Lake Albert, *L. n. albertianus* and *L. n. macrophthalmus*. The author regarded these as possibly only subspecifically distinct, and the chapter follows that view.

**Cichlidae.** A widely distributed family with variable body form and a single nostril on each side. Cichlids are the predominant freshwater percomorphs in Africa and support major East African fisheries. In Uganda, the family divides into the *Tilapia* species and the *Haplochromis* generic complex, which contains seven genera. Six of these are each represented by a single species.

**Anabantidae.** Fishes with an accessory breathing organ above the gills. *Ctenopoma* is the only genus in Uganda, with two species, *C. murei* and *C. damasi*. The accessory organ allows these fishes to tolerate foul and stagnant water.

**Mastacembelidae.** Eel-like fishes with minute cycloid scales, an anterior nostril in the form of a short tentacle, and confluent dorsal, caudal and anal fins. The family is not closely related to true eels. The genus *Mastacembelus* has one Ugandan species, *M. victoriae*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The chapter links the fishes to several lake- and river-scale ecological patterns:

- **Habitat partitioning.** [[Aplocheilichthys Pelagicus]] is unusual among East African members of its genus because it inhabits the surface layers of deep open water. Other species are generally confined to shallow inshore water and rivers.
- **Drought-resistant eggs.** *Nothobranchius taeniopygus* lives in temporary streams. Its eggs are deposited in mud, survive the dry season and hatch when the streams refill. The author notes that few African fishes are known to produce drought-resistant eggs.
- **Diet.** Diets range from phytoplankton-feeding cichlids to molluscivores and piscivores. [[Astatoreochromis Alluaudi]] feeds almost entirely on molluscs, and the author suggests it may help control snail vectors of Bilharzia and liver fluke. Most [[Haplochromis]] species are predators on other fishes.
- **Reproduction.** Most cichlids are [[Mouth Brooding]] females, and the female may guard the shoal after the young are free-swimming. *Tilapia zillii* is a non-mouth-brooder that guards a nest.
- **Lake-level ecology of Lates.** Young *Lates* feed mainly on prawns and insect larvae, and larger individuals (75–170 cm) feed almost exclusively on other fishes.
- **Periodic mortality.** [[Periodic Mortality]] of *Lates* in Lake Albert has been recorded for years, and the cause is still unresolved. A proposed mechanism involves cold, dense Semliki River inflow flowing along the lake bottom and becoming deoxygenated. *Lates* is intolerant of low oxygen, so it is affected more severely than other fishes. Heavy gill parasitisation and fish condition are also considered contributory.
- **Introductions.** The [[Uganda Game and Fisheries Department]] introduced *L. n. albertianus* into the Victoria Nile below the Owen Falls dam and into Lake Kyoga. Several *Tilapia* species have been introduced into lakes, dams and ponds, and the author warns that misidentification has led to failed stocking.
- **Biogeography.** *Lates* was present in the Lake Victoria basin during the Miocene (about 25,000,000 years ago) and persisted in Lake Edward until comparatively recent times. Its absence from Lakes Edward, George, Victoria and Kyoga today is attributed to barriers formed by the Semliki rapids and Murchison Falls, which prevented recolonisation.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic and identification points**

- *Nothobranchius taeniopygus* is the only Ugandan member of its genus and is rarely encountered in the Protectorate.
- Four *Aplocheilichthys* species are recorded from Uganda, plus a possible fifth. *A. pelagicus* is restricted to Lake Edward, *A. kassenjiensis* to Lake Albert, *A. pumilus* to Lake Victoria and affluent streams of Lakes George and Nabugabo, and *A. eduardensis* to Lake Edward and its rivers.
- *Hypsopanchax deprimozi* is confined to rivers in the Lake Edward basin and the Semliki River. *Haplochilichthys analis* is treated as a synonym.
- *Cynopanchax bukobanus* is known only from its original description, with no subsequent collections.
- *Lates niloticus albertianus* is distinguished by its caudal peduncle proportions and is recorded only from Lake Albert and the Albert and Murchison Niles.
- The chapter lists two *Tilapia* groups: the *T. zillii* group (fewer than 18 gill rakers on the first arch) and the group with 18 or more gill rakers.
- Of the Ugandan *Tilapia*, *T. nilotica* is considered the most widely distributed species. *T. esculenta* and *T. variabilis* are indigenous to Lakes Victoria, Kyoga and Nabugabo.

**Fisheries and economic importance**

- *Lates niloticus albertianus* is of considerable importance in the Lake Albert fishery. The largest recorded specimen weighed 360 lb and was caught in a seine net. The heaviest angled fish weighed 226 lb and was six feet long.
- *Tilapia esculenta* is the principal catch of commercial fishermen in most parts of Lake Victoria. The modal adult size is 30–32 cm total length.
- *Tilapia nilotica* is the most important element in the catches of African fishermen in Lakes Edward and George.
- *Tilapia galilaea* is of slight economic importance.
- *Haplochromis* is not of direct economic importance, but it is the principal food of economically important fishes such as *Bagrus* and *Clarias*.

**Ecological and biological findings**

- *Tilapia zillii* feeds on leaves and stems of rooted aquatic plants and their epiphytic algae, which makes it useful for pond culture.
- The *Haplochromis* species flocks of Lakes Victoria and Edward are highly diverse. Lake Victoria has about seventy species, Lake Edward about twenty-three, Lake Albert five, Lake Nabugabo four and the Koki lakes two.
- Female mouth-brooding is typical of most Ugandan cichlids, and breeding biology is unknown for many *Haplochromis* species.
- The species of *Ctenopoma* tolerate poor water quality through their accessory breathing organ.
- *Mastacembelus victoriae* is known only from Lake Victoria and the Victoria Nile, and its breeding biology is unknown.

**Research gaps and recommendations**

- Further studies of *Nothobranchius taeniopygus* are of particular interest.
- A thorough taxonomic revision and ecological study of *Haplochromis* is described as urgent.
- Whether *L. n. albertianus* and *L. n. macrophthalmus* warrant separate subspecies status remains unresolved.
- Specimens of *Aplocheilichthys* from Lake Victoria at Bugungu were awaited to confirm their identity as *A. eduardensis*.
- Expert identification is recommended before fish are introduced into new waters.
