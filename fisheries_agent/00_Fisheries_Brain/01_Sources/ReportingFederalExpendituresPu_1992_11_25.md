## Executive Summary

This document is an **administrative memorandum**, not a primary scientific research paper. It was issued by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's Lower Colorado Regional Office in November 1992, reporting fiscal year 1992 expenditures related to compliance with the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**. The memo lists federal conservation and consultation costs for a range of threatened, endangered, and candidate species within the Lower Colorado Region, including several imperiled freshwater fish taxa native to the Colorado River Basin. While it contains no morphological, physiological, or ecological data in the traditional scientific sense, it provides valuable **conservation-status and funding-allocation metadata** for these species during a critical period of Endangered Species Act implementation in the American Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

No direct habitat or ecological niche descriptions are given beyond implicit associations with the **Lower Colorado River Basin** and its tributary systems. Based on known species accounts referenced:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** – large-river specialist historically found throughout the mainstem [[Colorado River]] and its major tributaries.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** and **[[Virgin River Chub]]** – pelagic cyprinids adapted to swift, turbid desert river conditions in the Colorado River and Virgin River systems.
- **[[Woundfin]]** and **[[Loach Minnow]]** – small-bodied cyprinids inhabiting shallow, sandy-bottomed desert streams (Virgin River and Gila River basins, respectively).
- **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]** – highly restricted endemic confined to a single geothermal spring pool (Devil's Hole, Nevada), representing one of the most narrowly distributed vertebrate habitats known.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** – isolated desert springs, marshes, and irrigation drains in the Sonoran Desert region.
- **[[Spikedace]]** – riffle habitats of mid-elevation Gila River basin streams in Arizona and New Mexico.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** – shallow, vegetated margins of desert springs and streams in the Gila River basin.

These species collectively represent the endemic ichthyofauna of the arid **[[Colorado River Basin]]** and adjacent desert spring ecosystems, many of which have experienced severe habitat fragmentation due to damming, water diversion, and introduction of non-native species.

## Key Findings

- The **Bureau of Reclamation's Lower Colorado Regional Office** reported FY1992 species-specific expenditures for endangered species conservation and Endangered Species Act consultation.
- Fish species received substantial allocated funding, reflecting significant conservation investment in Colorado River Basin ichthyofauna:
  - **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: $200,000 (highest funding among listed fish)
  - **[[Virgin River Chub]]** and **[[Woundfin]]**: $75,000 each
  - **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: $15,000
  - **[[Loach Minnow]]**: $10,000
  - **[[Spikedace]]**: $9,000
  - **[[Gila Topminnow]]**: $22,000
  - **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]**: $8,000
  - **[[Desert Pupfish]]**: $3,000
- For comparison, non-fish species also tracked included the **[[Desert Tortoise]]** ($10,000), **[[Bald Eagle]]** ($251,000), **[[American Peregrine Falcon]]** ($89,000), and **[[Yuma Clapper Rail]]** ($75,000), indicating relative regional funding priorities across taxa.
- Numbers in parentheses in the original memo correspond to **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service species account numbers**, used for tracking Endangered Species Act-related federal spending.
- This record illustrates the administrative and budgetary infrastructure supporting on-the-ground conservation of imperiled Southwestern desert fishes during the early 1990s, a period of intensifying recovery efforts for Colorado River Basin native fish assemblages.
