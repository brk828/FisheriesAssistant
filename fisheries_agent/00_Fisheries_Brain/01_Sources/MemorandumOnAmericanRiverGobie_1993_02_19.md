## Executive Summary

This 1993 taxonomic memorandum by Reeve M. Bailey re-examines the American "river gobies" of genus *Awaous*, prompted by Ronald E. Watson's 1992 revision of the genus in the insular Pacific. Bailey's analysis focuses specifically on the subgenus **[[Chonophorus]]**, addressing generic nomenclature, subgeneric classification, and — most substantively — a reassessment of species boundaries among American *Awaous* based on scale-count meristics. The core finding is that the widely used name *A. tajasica* has been misapplied to most North and Central American populations; the correct name for the small-scaled Caribbean/Middle American form should be *A. banana* (Valenciennes, 1837), with true *A. tajasica* restricted to large-scaled Brazilian populations. A probable third, undescribed-in-scope large-scaled species (*A. transandeanus*) is proposed for Pacific drainages from Peru/Ecuador to Panama.

## Background & Description

- **Diagnostic generic trait:** *Awaous* is distinguished from other gobiids by one to five (usually two or three) fleshy lappets projecting under the operculum from the edge of the [[Cleithrum]].
- **Sensory structures:** Watson (1992) documented distinctive, extensive development of sensory papillae over much of the gill structure — an unusual (though not unique) gobiid feature.
- **Subgenus Chonophorus vs. other subgenera:** In adult *Chonophorus*, the oculoscapular canal is continuous, unlike in subgenera *Awaous* and *Euctenogobius*.
- **Body size:** Several species attain relatively large size for gobiids.

### Scale Count Data (Key Diagnostic Meristics)

| Population / Taxon | Lateral scales (pectoral base–caudal base) | Horizontal scale rows (2nd dorsal–anal) | Caudal peduncle circumferential scales |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large-scaled Brazilian form (*A. tajasica*, Ceará & Paraná states) | 53–59 | 14–18 | 18–21 |
| Small-scaled form (*A. banana*) — Haiti, Puerto Rico, Cuba, Venezuela coast, Middle America (Panama–eastern Mexico), Florida | 60–80 (usually 62–72) | 20–28 | 24–31 |
| Pacific slope material, Mexico–Honduras (n=23) | 60–76 | 20–28 | 24–31 |
| Single Panama specimen (Río Bayano) | 60 | 18 | 21 |

- Günther's original description of *G. transandeanus*: 15 scale rows between second dorsal and anal fin.
- Günther's original description of *G. mexicanus*: 24 horizontal scale rows (now synonymized with *A. banana*).

These meristic differences underpin Bailey's argument for a "marked dichotomy" between large-scaled and small-scaled Atlantic-drainage populations, interpreted as separate species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Life history:** *Awaous* exhibits an amphidromous-type pattern — larvae develop in marine or estuarine nursery areas, while most of the life cycle (juvenile through adult) occurs in freshwater river and stream habitats. This is described as an "ungobylike" (atypical for gobies generally) characteristic.
- **Habitat range within subgenus Chonophorus:** Restricted to tropical and subtropical streams and rivers of both the eastern and western Atlantic and the west coast of the Americas, occupying both fresh and brackish water.
- **Geographic distribution by taxon:**
  - *A. laterostriga* (= *A. guineensis*): endemic to western Africa, Senegal to Angola.
  - *A. tajasica*: coastwise Brazil (large-scaled form).
  - *A. banana*: Caribbean (Haiti, Puerto Rico, Cuba), Florida, and coastwise from Venezuela to Vera Cruz, Mexico (Atlantic); also Pacific drainages from the Gulf of Fonseca (Honduras) to Río Yaqui, Sonora, Mexico.
  - *A. transandeanus* (provisional): Pacific drainages from Peru and Ecuador to Panama, possibly Costa Rica and Nicaragua.
  - *A. badius* (subgenus Euctenogobius, monotypic): mouth of the Amazon to Río Magdalena, Colombia.
  - Subgenus *Awaous* (nominal): ~15 species, tropical Indo-Pacific freshwaters — outside scope of this memorandum.
- **Nomenclatural note on habitat linkage:** The dichotomy in scale counts correlates geographically with distinct drainage systems (Brazilian Atlantic coast vs. Caribbean/Middle American Atlantic and Pacific coasts), suggesting habitat-linked speciation within [[Chonophorus]].

## Key Findings

1. **Generic nomenclature resolved:** *Awaous* Valenciennes, 1837, is confirmed as the valid genus name over *Chonophorus* Poey, 1860 (still used by P.J. Miller as late as 1990), per rules of nomenclature detailed by Watson (1992).
2. **Type species priority correction:** Ginsburg (1933) showed that Bleeker (1874) — not Koumans (1931), as cited by Watson and by Eschmeyer & Bailey (1990) — made the earliest valid subsequent designation of *Gobius ocellaris* Broussonet, 1782 as type species of *Awaous*.
3. **Three subgenera recognized** (Watson 1992): subgenus *Awaous* (Indo-Pacific, ~15 spp.), *Euctenogobius* (monotypic, *A. badius*), and *Chonophorus* (Atlantic/Pacific Americas + West Africa, ~3 species).
4. **Species-level reclassification for American Chonophorus:**
   - The name *A. tajasica* (Lichtenstein, 1822) should be restricted to the **large-scaled Brazilian species**, its original type locality.
   - The widespread small-scaled Caribbean/Middle American/Florida form should be renamed **[[Awaous banana]]** (Valenciennes, 1837), per Günther's (1861) synonymy and first-reviser selection — replacing the erroneous use of *A. tajasica* on North American species lists.
   - *Gobius mexicanus* Günther, 1861, is a synonym of *A. banana*.
5. **Trans-isthmian distribution of *A. banana*:** Scale-count data from 23 Pacific-slope specimens (Mexico to Honduras) match the small-scaled Atlantic *A. banana*, indicating this species occurs on **both Middle American versants**, at least as far south on the Pacific side as the Gulf of Fonseca, Honduras.
6. **Probable distinct Pacific South/Central American species:** A single large-scaled specimen from the Río Bayano, Panama, suggests a second, large-scaled species (tentatively *A. transandeanus*) may range from Peru and Ecuador to Panama, and possibly into Costa Rica and Nicaragua — distinct from both *A. tajasica* and *A. banana*, though diagnostic characters remain unresolved.
7. **Data limitations acknowledged:** The University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ) lacks Pacific-drainage *Awaous* specimens from Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Costa Rica, and Nicaragua, limiting firm conclusions about the southern Pacific species.
8. **Related regional taxonomic work:** Gilmore and Yerger (in Gilbert 1992, "Rare and Endangered Fishes of Florida") independently anticipated Watson's taxonomic conclusions in discussion, though not in nomenclature.
9. **Future work anticipated:** Ronald Watson was reported (via Carter Gilbert) to be preparing a full revision of subgenus [[Chonophorus]] following his relocation from Florida to Germany.
