## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (Dean A. Hendrickson, Native Fish Biologist, March 6, 1989) reports results of quarterly winter fish monitoring on the [[Verde River]] targeting [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (squawfish). Three reaches were sampled via canoe-mounted electrofishing (VVP shocker) and seining between December 1988 and February 1989. A striking longitudinal gradient in community composition was documented: the upstream Perkinsville-to-Tapco "canyon reach" retained a predominantly **native fish assemblage (92% of catch)**, while the two downstream reaches (Beasley Flat to Sheep Bridge) were dominated by **non-native species (up to 68% of catch)** and showed markedly lower overall fish abundance. No squawfish were captured in any reach, and previously tagged individuals were not recaptured.

## Background & Description

While this is primarily a population survey rather than a morphological study, the memo notes life-stage distinctions relevant to identification and stocking history:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: All individuals captured (n=20) were **juveniles**, suggesting a discrete recent stocking cohort (likely Fall 1988) rather than natural recruitment.
- **[[Smallmouth Bass]]**: Both adult and juvenile size classes were represented in the Beasley Flat area catch.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]**: Juveniles noted in seine catches in the lower reaches (Beasley Flat to Sheep Bridge), alongside juvenile smallmouth bass and bluegill.
- Native fishes (Sonora sucker, roundtail chub) were tagged with **AGFD floy tags** (color-coded by reach: yellow/blue in the upper canyon reach, red in the Beasley Flat reach), enabling future recapture-based growth and movement studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study reaches** (River Miles calculated from Horseshoe Dam):
- **Perkinsville (RM 121.6) to Tapco (RM 101.5)** — Jan 12–17: upper "canyon reach," clear water, native-dominated.
- **Beasley Flat (RM 59.4) to Childs (RM 41.3)** — Feb 14–17: sampling limited to ~7 river miles (RM 59.4–52.5) due to equipment failure; turbid water.
- **Childs (RM 41.3) to Sheep Bridge (RM 12.0)** — Dec 27–31: excellent visibility (~3 m), non-native dominated.

**Habitat associations and spatial patterns:**
- [[Roundtail Chub]] were most abundant **above RM 120**, within the canyon reach near Perkinsville, suggesting a preference for higher-gradient, confined canyon habitat.
- [[Razorback Sucker]] juveniles were confined to reaches **below the confluence with Sycamore Canyon**, consistent with a localized stocking event.
- [[Flathead Catfish]] were first detected approximately **two river miles below Sycamore Canyon**, marking a possible invasion front or habitat threshold.
- Other non-natives (carp, smallmouth bass, channel catfish) were **broadly distributed** throughout the 20-mile Perkinsville–Tapco reach.
- [[Spikedace]] were found specifically near **Perkinsville (RM 120)** and **Mormon Pocket (RM 114)**, indicating localized occurrence of this small-bodied native cyprinid within the upper canyon.
- Downstream reaches (Beasley Flat to Sheep Bridge) showed **large visible schools of carp** and scattered channel catfish, reflecting non-native numerical dominance in slower, more turbid, lower-gradient river segments.
- Water clarity varied strongly by reach (turbid at Beasley Flat vs. ~3 m visibility at Childs–Sheep Bridge), affecting both visual survey efficacy and possibly habitat suitability for sight-feeding predators.

## Key Findings

- **Native fish stronghold**: The Perkinsville-to-Tapco canyon reach hosted a native-dominated assemblage (92%), composed of [[Sonora Sucker]] (63%, n=516), [[Desert Mountain Sucker]] (26%, n=217), [[Roundtail Chub]] (8%, n=67), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (2%, n=20), collected over 5346 seconds of electrofishing effort.
- **No squawfish detected** in any of the three reaches despite dedicated monitoring effort.
- **Extensive tagging effort**: 88% (n=712) of native fish captured in the upper reach were tagged with floy tags; no previously tagged fish were recaptured, indicating either high mortality/emigration or a still-developing recapture dataset.
- **Non-native fish complement (upper reach)**: [[Common Carp]] (n=42), [[Smallmouth Bass]] (n=15), [[Channel Catfish]] (n=9), [[Flathead Catfish]] (n=3), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (n=2), [[Green Sunfish]] (n=1).
- **Seine catch composition**: Dominated (>90%) by [[Red Shiner]] across all reaches; additional taxa included [[Spikedace]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], roundtail chub, Sonora sucker, [[Western Mosquitofish]], and green sunfish.
- **Downstream decline in native fish dominance**: 
  - Beasley Flat area (RM 59.4–52.5): only 47 fish collected in 2727 shocking-seconds plus gill-net effort; natives comprised just 32% (Sonora sucker n=13, roundtail chub n=2), while non-natives made up 68% (smallmouth bass n=9, largemouth bass n=1, green sunfish n=18, carp n=2, flathead catfish n=2).
  - Childs-to-Sheep Bridge reach: 3301 shocking-seconds yielded **exclusively non-native fish** — carp (n=30), largemouth bass (n=3), channel catfish (n=2), smallmouth bass (n=1) — despite excellent water clarity permitting visual confirmation of additional carp schools and channel catfish.
- **Overall pattern**: A strong upstream-to-downstream shift from native-fish dominance (canyon reach) to non-native dominance and reduced overall fish abundance (lower gradient reaches), highlighting the [[Verde River]] canyon as a critical refuge for native Sonoran Desert fish fauna, including candidate/listed species such as [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], and [[Spikedace]].
