# Subterranean Animals in Thermal Waters

#ichthyology #marine-biology #stenasellidae

Note on scope: the source is a speleobiological and invertebrate study. It reports no fish, so the taxa below are crustaceans and molluscs.

## Executive Summary

Sket and Velkovrh (1981) surveyed the fauna of about 26 groups of thermal springs in Slovenia (NW Yugoslavia) with temperatures of 15-28°C. Among 19 troglobiontic taxa found, only [[Hadziella]] spp. (Gastropoda) and the isopod family [[Stenasellidae]] appear characteristic of thermal water. The authors compare these with published records from other continents and argue that thermal groundwaters act as Pleistocene refugia for thermophilic subterranean species.

## Background & Description

- Thermal springs in Slovenia occur almost entirely in limestone and dolomite, which are karstified. The exception is some Prekmurje wells drilled into sandy strata.
- Warm water rises from depth after heating along faults. Where it emerges through bedrock it links to cave systems. Where it rises through gravel it has an interstitial character.
- Waters above roughly 20°C are considered "warm" or "alien" to the local karst. Normal Slovene karst groundwater is 8-12°C.
- About 30 spas (toplice) are known. Ten have been modified so that the natural spring habitats are destroyed. The authors investigated 26 spring groups, mostly small natural springs and some warm water from deep boreholes.
- The team was funded by the Raziskovalna skupnost Slovenije under the project "Speleobiološke raziskave" ([[Speleobiology]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Physiological constraints:** Poikilotherms in warm water face a mismatch between higher metabolism and lower oxygen solubility. Food supply from the surface is also scarce in deep thermal water.
- **Oxygen:** Dissolved O2 in most Slovene thermal springs was 5.5-7.5 mg/l (55-70% saturation). In the Klevevž cave it reached 8.6-9.9 mg/l, with a maximum of 10.6 mg/l. Fauna composition did not appear to depend on oxygen within the measured range.
- **Klevevž cave (Spodnja Klevevška jama):** Three water streams at different temperatures (about 20-22°C, 15-18°C, and about 12°C). Snail and oligochaete populations were densest where food input was higher. Parabathynella sp. was abundant in the interstitial water next to the warm stream.
- **Glaciation and climate:** Stenasellids live in regions with present mean temperatures of about 10°C or more. They are absent from the zones next to large formerly glaciated areas (for example the Alps), which the authors suggest explains their restriction to thermal water in Slovenia.
- **Species-level data:** Thermal-water stenasellids include [[Stenasellus hungaricus]] thermalis (optimum 20°C, field range 18.2-24.4°C, experimental tolerance 13-28°C) and [[Balkanostenasellus]] skopljensis ssp. (field range 19-28°C in Slovenia).

## Key Findings

1. **Characteristic thermal taxa:** Of 19 troglobiontic taxa, only Hadziella spp. and Stenasellidae are regularly found in thermal water. Hadziella demitnuta (Bole) occurs in Hotavlje, Tuhinj and Vikrče, and an undescribed Hadziella sp. occurs at Bušečke toplice and Podsused.
2. **New taxa:** Monolistra (Microlistra) sp. n. and Paladilhiopsis sp. n. were found only in thermal water (16-18°C) at Kronovo. The authors note these two are still considered restricted to thermal water.
3. **Sampling outcomes:** Some sites were negative. Ten spas, including Bled, Besnica, Vrhnika and Dolenjske toplice, yielded no fauna. At Vasenje, 30,000 litres filtered from a 29.5-29.8°C borehole yielded no animals.
4. **Global comparison:** Thermophilic subterranean fauna elsewhere includes [[Thermosbaena Mirabilis]] at about 45°C in Tunisia, Thermobathynella adami at 55°C in Congo, and a cluster of stenasellids in Mexico (Cuatro Ciénegas) at 29.5-34°C. The highest recorded stenasellid temperature is 33°C for Metastenasellus wikkiensis (Nigeria), and 30-34°C for Mexistenasellus coahuila.
5. **Thermal refugium hypothesis:** The authors propose three routes for colonising thermal habitats: relict populations surviving in deep thermal water, survival at the cave-surface boundary during cold periods, and later invasion of thermal water by cold-water forms. For Slovenia they favour a combined model in which thermophilic forms survived glaciations in thermal groundwater.
6. **Thermal character threshold:** For Slovenia, groundwater at 13°C or more (16°C or more in the warmest parts of Yugoslavia) is effectively thermal, because mean annual air temperatures do not exceed these values.
7. **Taxonomic limits:** Most taxa could only be identified to genus level. A thorough taxonomic revision of several genera is needed, at least for Slovenia.
