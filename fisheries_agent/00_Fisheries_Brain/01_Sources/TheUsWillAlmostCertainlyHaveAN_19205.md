# Nuclear Power Reactors in the United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #nuclear-energy-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a political pamphlet by U.S. Senator [[Mike Gravel]] (D.-Alaska) arguing that the nation's [[Nuclear Power Plant|nuclear power reactors]] are unsafe and should be halted. It does not address fish, invertebrates, plankton, water chemistry, or aquatic habitat. Its subject is reactor safety, liability law, sabotage risk, and energy policy. Gravel contends that existing safety systems have not been shown to work, that a serious accident could cause mass casualties and widespread contamination, and that the [[Atomic Energy Commission]] (AEC), Congress's [[Joint Committee on Atomic Energy]], and utility companies have understated these risks. He calls for repealing the [[Price-Anderson Act]], halting reactor construction and operation until safety is assured, and funding alternatives such as [[Solar Energy]].

## Background & Description

The pamphlet is a policy statement rather than a research report. It draws on figures and assertions attributed to several sources:

- **[[Ralph Lapp]]**, described as an internationally renowned scientist who worked on the first atomic bomb, is quoted as saying the U.S. "will almost certainly have a major nuclear disaster within the next 10 years."
- An unnamed article in the AEC's own house organ is cited as saying the only reason no major disaster has occurred is luck.
- Internationally prominent nuclear scientists are described as saying an accident is "only a matter of time."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document's environmental concerns concern radiation and geology, not aquatic ecosystems. Its main points are:

- **Radiation contamination:** The area around a destroyed plant "would not be inhabitable for a hundred years or more."
- **Release threshold:** "Scientists all agree that if a nuclear reactor leaks out as little as 10 percent of its radioactive material, it will be a disaster."
- **Siting:** Gravel says most plants are near major cities, and lists New York, Minneapolis, Detroit, San Diego, and Los Angeles among areas "on the danger list." He also says some reactors are sited near earthquake faults that have shown more ground motion than predicted, and that the plants are not adequately designed to withstand it.
- **Scale of potential harm:** A serious accident could "take out a city the size of Chicago — or even an entire state," with losses of "hundreds of thousands or even millions of American lives" and property damage in "hundreds of billions of dollars." These are the author's projections, not results of a cited study.
- **Sabotage:** He argues that terrorist sabotage of reactors is a credible threat in an era of skyjacking, bombing, and assassinations.

## Key Findings

The pamphlet's main claims and recommendations are:

1. **Safety systems are inadequate.** The "safety cooling system," described as the last line of defense, is said to have failed six out of six tests. Gravel argues the AEC approves construction anyway. He also says the view that reactor failure is "extremely remote or negligible" rests on "conservative assumptions" rather than experimental evidence.
2. **Liability is shifted.** Gravel says utility companies promoted the [[Price-Anderson Act]] in 1957, which relieves them of nearly all financial liability for radioactive releases. He reads this as an implicit admission of risk.
3. **Prior incidents.** A worker at the [[Indian Point Nuclear Power Plant]] in Peekskill, N.Y., allegedly tried to set it on fire, causing "$5 to $10 million" in damage. In March of that year, a subcontractor had asked the AEC to withhold licensing because of "inadequate, incomplete and questionable design" of parts of the reactor.
4. **Recommended actions:**
   - Repeal the Price-Anderson Act so utilities bear full liability.
   - Halt reactor construction and operation until accident-prevention systems are proven to work.
   - Increase federal funding for alternative energy. The author cites solar energy as "apparently pollution-free" and "almost completely neglected" in federal research, and says he proposed legislation providing $15 million for solar research.
   - Encourage citizens to organize and use legal tools to object.
5. **Framing.** The author concludes that the choice is between "gamble[ing] our lives and our planet's future on a radioactive source of energy" and developing safer sources of power.

**Data limitations:** Most figures are unsourced or attributed to unnamed articles. The text gives no peer-reviewed data, sample sizes, or test details for the failed safety-system tests.
