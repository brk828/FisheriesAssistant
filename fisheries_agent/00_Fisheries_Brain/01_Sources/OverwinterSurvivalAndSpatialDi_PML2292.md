# Overwinter Survival and Spatial Distribution of Wild Razorback Sucker in the San Juan River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cypriniformes

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) project, led by Principal Investigator [[Benjamin Schleicher]] (Grand Junction Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office), documents overwinter survival and spatial distribution of wild-spawned [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[San Juan River]]. The work followed a 2018 water year with very low runoff, which produced low, warm and very clear summer base flows and a record number of early juvenile Razorback Suckers. A single electrofishing pass in spring 2019 captured 45 wild age-1 Razorback Suckers, confirming that the 2018 cohort survived its first winter. The trip also captured 274 [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], a catch rate well above that of the 2015 Adult Monitoring pass. Funding came from contracts R13PG40052 and R13PG4005L (USFWS) and R13AC40007 (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources).

## Background & Description

Razorback Sucker are endangered large-bodied suckers, and early juvenile life stages are rarely documented in riverine systems. The document reports the following context:

- **2018 water year:** One of the lowest run-off years in recent history, producing low, warm and very clear summer base flows. Reviewers cautioned that these conditions were "sufficient" but not ideal.
- **2018 early juvenile catch:** A record n = 154 early juvenile (larger than larval) Razorback Suckers were captured by American Southwest Ichthyological Researchers ([[Larval Fish Survey]]; Farrington et al., 2019).
- **Fall 2018 young-of-year:** Six young-of-year Razorback Suckers were collected during a longitudinally truncated small-bodied fish monitoring trip by the [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]] (Zeigler and Wick, 2019).
- **Rationale for spring sampling:** Spring sampling was intended to capture wild-spawned fish before they grew substantially. Later sampling might not separate wild age-1 recruits from the smallest [[Hatchery Stocking|hatchery-reared]] Razorback Suckers, which are stocked at a minimum of 300 mm total length (TL) and may have lost their [[PIT Tag]]s.

The primary goals, as summarized in reviewer responses, were to answer three questions: (1) Did wild-spawned age-0 Razorback Suckers successfully overwinter? (2) What is the size variation of wild age-1 Razorback Suckers in spring, and what is the likelihood they reach 300 mm TL by fall? (3) Does the spatial distribution of wild age-1 Razorback Suckers indicate spatial variation in [[Recruitment]] success?

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study reach and methods.** Sampling occurred 25 March to 4 April 2019 from Shiprock Bridge, NM (RM 147.9) downstream to [[Clay Hills]], UT (RM 2.9), covering 145 river miles. Crews used electrofishing rafts equipped with an ETS MBS unit, with peak output kept around 9 amps. Each raft had one netter and one rower, and each bank was worked independently each day. Fish were netted with a 10-foot dip net and held in a live well.

Razorback Suckers under 300 mm TL and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] under 300 mm or over 400 mm TL were targeted. At the end of each river mile, the crew stopped to measure fish to the nearest mm TL and standard length, weigh them to the nearest gram, and record date, start and end river mile, and electrofishing effort (minutes and seconds). Effort was measured through [[Electrofishing]] catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) calculations.

**Water temperature and handling.** The spring trip occurred during cooler water temperatures. The authors noted that handling smaller Colorado Pikeminnow was less stressful than in summer, when small size classes are normally captured. Handling stress for juveniles was a concern based on [[Clark et al. 2018]], which reported deleterious capture effects and low survival in stocked juvenile Colorado Pikeminnow.

**Monitoring context.** The 2015 [[Adult Monitoring]] pass was the last time the whole river was sampled in a single pass. Fall [[Demographic Monitoring]] occurs in the same reach where passive stocking of [[Hatchery Stocking|NAPI pond]] fish takes place. New protocols prohibit stocking fish under 300 mm TL, but they were not in effect before the last fall monitoring trip, so tag loss was proposed as a possible explanation for small unmarked fish upstream.

## Key Findings

### Razorback Sucker

- **Total captured:** 45 wild age-1 Razorback Suckers were identified in the single spring pass, which the authors considered encouraging evidence of [[Overwinter Survival]].
- **Spatial range:** Captures extended from RM 119 at [[Four Corners Bridge]] to RM 17.0, just below [[Slickhorn Canyon]], a span of about 102 river miles.
- **Size:** Fish ranged from 100 mm to 197 mm TL. About 71% (reported as "7|o/o" in the scan) were 140–179 mm TL, with the highest frequency at 150–159 mm TL.
- **Tagging:** 43 of the 45 fish received PIT tags. Two were too small to tag.
- **CPUE:** Average catch rate was 0.47 fish per hour, with 95% confidence intervals of 0.33 and 0.62 fish per hour.
- **Growth and recaptures:** Two spring-tagged fish were recaptured in fall Demographic Monitoring:
  - A fish tagged at 155 mm TL grew 90 mm to 245 mm TL between 29 March and 20 September 2019 (175 days).
  - A fish tagged at 160 mm TL reached 275 mm TL by 4 October 2019 (188 days), a growth of 115 mm over 188 days. This individual was later identified as a [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] × Razorback Sucker [[Hybridization|hybrid]].
- **Size-by-fall question:** Only one usable fish remained after excluding the hybrid, and it did not reach 300 mm TL. The authors stated there was insufficient data to estimate the likelihood that an age-1 fish reaches 300 mm TL by fall.
- **Additional detections:** Of the 43 tagged fish, six had later detections. Four were detected at the McElmo Creek antenna at RM 0.1, and two of those were also detected upstream at the RM 0.2 antenna. The remaining 37 tags had not been detected since spring 2019 (see the [[STReaMS]] query in Appendix A).
- **Spatial interpretation:** The first age-1 fish was found at Four Corners Bridge. Possible gaps include 32.7 river miles between the Animas River confluence (RM 180.6, where a Razorback Sucker larva was collected in 2018) and Shiprock Bridge, and 28.9 river miles from Shiprock Bridge to the first age-1 capture. The authors concluded that the data could not clearly separate successful spawns by location.

### Colorado Pikeminnow

- **Total captured:** 274 individuals were captured. The document also gives 271 in one place; the 274 figure is used here because it matches the PIT tag arithmetic.
- **Size distribution:** Fish ranged from 152 mm to 751 mm TL and weighed 23 g to 4000 g. The highest frequency was 210–299 mm TL, with 219 fish reported at 76.6%. The peak was at 280–289 mm TL.
- **Size classes:** Two recruiting sub-adults (400–449 mm TL) and eight adults (over 450 mm TL) were captured.
- **Tagging:** 21 fish (7.66%) already had PIT tags. The other 253 were implanted with tags before release. Among the tagged fish, one was a recruiting sub-adult (429 mm TL) and eight were adults.
- **Recaptures:** No within-trip recaptures occurred.
- **CPUE:** Average catch rate was 2.87 fish per hour, with a 95% confidence interval of 2.23–3.52.
- **Comparison with 2015:** The 2015 Adult Monitoring pass yielded a CPUE of 1.00 fish per hour (95% CI 0.77–1.24) and captured 10 adults from the Animas confluence to Clay Hills, covering only three of every four river miles. The 2019 trip captured eight adults over every river mile.
- **Interpretation:** Most fish were 2–3 years old, likely from the 2015 or 2017 cohorts, and the authors suggested these smaller cohorts drove the higher 2019 CPUE. Length-at-age analysis in spring could separate these cohorts, but it was outside the scope of this fiscal year.

### Reviewer Feedback and Program Decisions

- Reviewers recommended adding context on the rarity of juvenile Razorback Suckers and clarifying terms such as "juvenile."
- Reviewers judged that [[Demographic Monitoring]] was not effective for tracking age-1 recruitment. Only a few Razorback Suckers under 300 mm TL were captured in three fall passes (the scanned figure is unclear).
- The authors noted that a more thorough study of Colorado Pikeminnow is planned for FY2021 under the project's Statement of Work (SOW), and that Colorado Pikeminnow handling will be reduced once the large non-native removal effort ends.
- Reviewers asked for a capture-location map. The authors deferred this to next year's report.
