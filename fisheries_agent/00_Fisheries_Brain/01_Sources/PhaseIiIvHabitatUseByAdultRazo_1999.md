## Executive Summary

This report details Phase II-IV (1998-1999) findings of a long-term telemetry study assessing habitat use by adult [[Razorback Sucker|razorback suckers]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the lower Imperial Division of the [[Colorado River]]. The primary objective was to correlate specific habitat characteristics (surface area, temperature, turbidity, conductivity, pH, velocity, depth) with habitat selection to refine a [[Habitat Assessment Index (HAI)]] developed in Phase I. A total of 57 fish were released with ultrasonic transmitters (26 in Jan 1998, 31 in Dec 1998), yielding 374 contacts over 19 surveys in the current phase (872 contacts over 51 surveys cumulatively since Phase II). Data strongly indicate a disproportionate reliance on [[Backwater Habitat|backwaters]] relative to their availability. Evidence of active reproduction was documented, including the collection of larval razorback suckers from Squaw Lake and Senator Wash Reservoir, pending taxonomic confirmation via Arizona State University. Restoration/enhancement activities by the [[USBR]] were postponed during this reporting period, though baseline and habitat-use data collection continued uninterrupted.

## Background & Description

*Note: The source text is a habitat-use and population management report; it does not provide direct morphological or anatomical data on the study specimens.* Key methodological notes relevant to specimen handling include:

- **Tagging Method:** Adult fish surgically implanted with ultrasonic transmitters for long-term tracking.
- **Life Stage Sampled:** Adults (tagged/tracked) and larvae (collected via floating fishing light traps for reproductive confirmation).
- **Larval Preservation:** Larval specimens preserved in 10% formalin for lab-based taxonomic confirmation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study characterizes the [[Razorback Sucker|razorback sucker]]'s habitat preferences within a regulated large-river system, highlighting strong selectivity for off-channel environments.

- **Primary Habitat:** [[Backwater Habitat|Backwaters]] are overwhelmingly preferred, accounting for 79% of observed use despite representing only 42% of available habitat.
- **Secondary Habitat:** [[Impoundment Habitat|Impoundments]] were used moderately more than expected (11% observed vs. 5% expected).
- **Underutilized Habitats:** [[Main Channel Habitat|Main channel]] (7% observed) and [[Side Channel Habitat|side channels]] (3% observed) were used less than their availability would predict, though the December 1998 release specifically targeted these underutilized zones to test dispersal patterns.
- **Key Environmental Drivers (Backwater Use Model):** Logistic regression identified backwater **perimeter, connectivity, entrance length, temperature, and turbidity** as statistically significant predictors of use.
- **Reproductive Habitat:** Evidence suggests spawning activity occurs in proximity to reservoir pumping stations and coves (e.g., Senator Wash Reservoir), raising questions about larval drift/origin between riverine adults and reservoir populations.
- **Threats to Habitat Connectivity:** Siltation and encroachment by dense aquatic emergent vegetation are actively isolating backwaters from the main river channel, threatening access to this critical habitat type.

## Key Findings

- **Habitat Use Distribution (Table 1, Jan 1998–Feb 1999):**
  | Habitat Type | Number Observed | % Observed | % Expected (Availability) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Backwaters | 295 | 79% | 42% |
  | Impoundment | 42 | 11% | 5% |
  | Main Channel | 26 | 7% | (not specified) |
  | Side Channel | 11 | 3% | (not specified) |

- **Predictive Model Performance:** The logistic regression model achieved ~84% overall predictive accuracy for habitat use, with a notably strong **97% accuracy in predicting non-use** of specific backwater sites — a valuable tool for prioritizing restoration exclusion/inclusion criteria.

- **Reproductive Evidence:** Three pairs of tagged adults were observed exhibiting probable spawning behavior in late February/early March 1999. A single larval fish (tentatively identified as *Xyrauchen texanus*) was collected in Squaw Lake; five additional larvae were collected near a pumping station cove in Senator Wash Reservoir. Confirmation pending genetic/morphological analysis at Arizona State University.

- **Management Implication:** Maintaining hydrological connectivity between the main river and backwaters should be a conservation priority, as siltation and vegetative encroachment are causing natural isolation of these preferred habitats.

- **Project Trajectory:** Differential habitat use was even more pronounced in Phase III than in prior phases, possibly linked to anomalous weather conditions during spring/summer 1998. Restoration/enhancement fieldwork by USBR was postponed, but the monitoring and modeling framework remains intact for future application to restored sites via the adjusted [[Habitat Assessment Index (HAI)]].

- **Related Prior Work:** Builds directly upon [[Bradford & Vlach 1995]] (original HAI development) and [[Bradford, Gurtin & Vlach 1998]] (initial ultrasonic telemetry release data).
