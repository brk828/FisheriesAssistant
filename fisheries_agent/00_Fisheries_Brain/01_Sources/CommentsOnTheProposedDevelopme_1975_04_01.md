## Executive Summary

This 1975 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife) to the BLM Safford District addresses the ecological impact of proposed **geothermal development** on the riparian habitat of the **Gila River** in Arizona. Despite the paper's fisheries-agency origin and title, **the substantive content is almost entirely ornithological** — it catalogs bird species (with emphasis on raptors and obligate riparian species) observed along the Gila River corridor and argues for habitat protection prior to any development. Critically, the author explicitly notes that **no fish surveys have been conducted** in the Arizona portion of the Gila River riparian system, alongside herpetological and mammalian data gaps. This note preserves that scope faithfully: it is a call for baseline ecological survey work (including fish) rather than a fisheries dataset itself.

## Background & Description

No morphological data on fish or other aquatic organisms are presented in this source. The document contains no ichthyological descriptions, meristic counts, or species-level fish taxonomy. The only "characteristics" discussed are behavioral/nesting traits of birds, e.g.:

- **[[Black Phoebe]]**: nests on overhanging canyon walls and dirt banks; found at 100–200 yard intervals in the Gila narrows — among the highest densities recorded for the species.
- **[[Peregrine Falcon]]** and **[[Prairie Falcon]]**: cliff-nesting (ledges, potholes) raptors requiring high canyon walls.
- **[[Black Hawk]]**, **[[Zone-tailed Hawk]]**, **[[Gray Hawk]]**, **[[Mississippi Kite]]**: riparian-tree-dependent nesters, largely restricted to the Gila River corridor.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Gila River riparian corridor (dominated by **[[Cottonwood]]** (*Populus* spp.), **[[Willow]]** (*Salix* spp.), and **[[Sycamore]]** (*Platanus* sp.)) functions as an aquatic-terrestrial ecotone providing:

- Shelter, food, and water resources for a "unique and complex assortment of animals" in an otherwise arid desert-shrub landscape.
- Nesting substrate stratified by guild:
  - **Obligate riparian species** (herons, ducks, rails, Yellow-billed Cuckoo, marsh wrens, vireos, warblers, blackbirds, orioles, tanagers) — dependent entirely on riparian vegetation/wetland margins for reproduction.
  - **Facultative riparian species** (Turkey Vulture, Cooper's Hawk, quail, doves, owls, woodpeckers, etc.) — use riparian habitat but are not restricted to it.
  - **Cliff-nesting raptors** (Peregrine Falcon, Prairie Falcon, Golden Eagle, Bald Eagle, Red-tailed Hawk) — use canyon walls adjacent to the river, exploiting the river's avian prey base (both aquatic and terrestrial species).
- The **[[Gillard Hot Springs]]** area and adjoining box canyon represent a largely unaltered stretch of river, cited as prime habitat for the endangered **[[Peregrine Falcon]]** and a potential reintroduction site.
- Anthropogenic threats identified: irrigation, dam building, channelization, phreatophyte control, grazing, and agricultural conversion — all of which degrade riparian structure and the aquatic-adjacent food web that supports the avifauna.
- **Data gap explicitly acknowledged by the author**: "the birds of the Gila Valley in Arizona have not been investigated, nor have the fish, herps, or mammals" — indicating that fish community composition, distribution, and status in this reach of the Gila River were **entirely unstudied** at the time of writing.

## Key Findings

- **No fish-specific data exist in this source.** The paper is fundamentally an avifaunal risk assessment for geothermal development, produced by a fisheries-and-wildlife agency biologist but focused on birds.
- The Gila River (Graham/Greenlee Counties, AZ) is identified as one of the least-altered river systems remaining in the Southwest, supporting high vertebrate diversity, but comprehensive baseline data (fish, herpetofauna, mammals) are **absent**.
- Approximately 80 species of breeding birds occur along ~50 miles of the Gila River in New Mexico (Johnson et al. 1973); similar diversity is inferred, but unconfirmed, for the Arizona reach.
- Threatened/endangered/status-undetermined raptors documented or inferred: **[[Peregrine Falcon]]**, **[[Prairie Falcon]]**, **[[Bald Eagle]]**, **[[Black Hawk]]**, **[[Gray Hawk]]**, **[[Zone-tailed Hawk]]**, **[[Mississippi Kite]]**.
- **Primary recommendation**: an in-depth ecological investigation — explicitly including fish along with herps, mammals, and birds — should precede any geothermal, dam, or other major development in the Gila River area, to (1) establish species inventories and interrelationships, (2) evaluate designation as a Research Natural Area/Wilderness Area, and (3) assess socioeconomic impacts of such protective status.
- A secondary recommendation calls for reassessing grazing leases in the riparian zone due to likely habitat degradation effects, which would presumably also affect any (unstudied) aquatic/fish communities dependent on riparian bank stability and water quality.
- **Implication for future ichthyological work**: this document functions as a historical baseline demonstrating that Gila River (Arizona reach) fish community data were nonexistent as of 1975, underscoring the need for cross-referencing with later studies (e.g., **[[Gila Chub]]**, **[[Gila Topminnow]]**, **[[Loach Minnow]]**, and other Gila River basin native fishes) to assess subsequent status changes potentially linked to geothermal or other development activity in this watershed.
