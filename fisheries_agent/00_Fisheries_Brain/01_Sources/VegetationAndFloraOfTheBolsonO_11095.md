# Vegetation and Flora of the Bolson of Cuatro Ciénegas, Coahuila, Part II

#botany #angiosperms #dicotyledonae

## Executive Summary

This is the second installment of a floristic survey of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] region of [[Coahuila]], Mexico, by [[Donald J. Pinkava]] (Department of Botany and Microbiology, Arizona State University). It covers the dicotyledonous families from Casuarinaceae through Loasaceae and was published in the *Boletín de la Sociedad Botánica de México* (No. 39, 1980). The first part appeared in Volume 38 (1979). The document is an annotated checklist: each taxon is followed by its habitat, relative abundance, and locality, with specimen citations keyed to collector numbers and herbaria. It contains no ichthyological, aquatic faunal, or water-chemistry data. Its aquatic content is limited to the spring, marsh and stream vegetation of the basin.

## Background & Description

The survey is organized by family and covers 54 families, from Casuarinaceae and Saururaceae to Loasaceae. Entries are mostly native shrubs, herbs, trees and vines, with a small number of cultivated and introduced species. The checklist does not describe morphology in general terms. Its descriptive content is limited to the habitat, abundance and locality notes attached to each taxon.

Locality notation is abbreviated throughout. "Basin" marks sites on the valley floor, and "CC" refers to the town of Cuatro Ciénegas. The codes SSM, SM and SSV denote named mountain or range localities, and "P." and "C." mark places and canyons (cañones). Specimen numbers follow several collector series, including Pinkava (P), Minckley, Meyer, Marsh, Arnold, Engard, Turner, White, Wendt, Johnston (IMJ) and Hoult. Herbarium abbreviations include GH, TEX, DES, NY, UC and MCJ. Most records are cited as "not seen" and attributed to a collector or specialist when the specimen was not examined directly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist separates vegetation by landscape position:

- **Basin floor and wetlands:** Alkaline marshes and flats support [[Anemopsis Californica]], [[Allenrolfea Occidentalis]] and [[Sesuvium Verrucosum]], which is described as abundant on gypsum flats and dunes. Riparian and spring-margin species include [[Salix Nigra]], [[Nymphaea Amplia]] (abundant in streams and pools), [[Polygala Turgida]] (abundant along waterways), and *Polygonum hydropiperoides*, which is uncommon near water.
- **Gypsum and saline flats and dunes:** Taxa such as [[Atriplex Canescens]], [[Nerisyrenia Incana]], *Drymaria lyropetala* var. *coahuilana* (very common), *Petalonyx crenatus* and *Mentzelia mexicana* are characteristic of this substrate.
- **Bajadas and lower slopes:** [[Larrea Tridentata]] is dominant. Other common taxa are [[Prosopis Glandulosa]] var. *torreyana*, which forms bosques in less saline areas, *Thamnosma texana* (very common), *Euphorbia antisyphilitica* and *Acleisanthes longiflora*.
- **Canyons, chaparral and oak-pine forest:** The upper elevations hold most of the woody diversity. [[Quercus]] species are common, and *Q. glaucoides*, *Q. gravesii*, *Q. greggii* and *Q. invaginata* are among the most frequently recorded. Conifer-associated and montane taxa include [[Cercocarpus Montanus]], *Fendlera linearis*, *Lindleya mespiloides* and *Vitis riparia*.

The checklist also notes a set of introduced or cultivated plants, including *Casuarina cunninghamiana*, *Ficus carica*, *Eruca sativa*, *Salsola iberica*, *Melilotus albus*, *Parkinsonia aculeata*, *Tamarix aphylla*, and *Carya illinoensis*. *Medicago sativa* is listed as an escaped cultigen.

## Key Findings

- **Scope and size:** The document lists dicotyledonous taxa across 54 families. Entries are grouped by family and include collection and specimen data. The checklist is arranged for floristic reference rather than as a summary count.
- **Introduced and cultivated species:** These are recorded, with notes on status. Examples include the cultivated tree *Casuarina cunninghamiana* and the cultivated fig *Ficus carica*, the uncommon introduced weed *Salsola iberica*, and *Tamarix aphylla*, a cultivated tree. The checklist also records *Euphorbia antisyphilitica* as an abundant bajada species whose cuticle is used as a wax source for candles.
- **New and unpublished taxa:** *Clematis coahuilensis* is described as a new species, marked "sp. nov. ined." (unpublished), with the type collected at Cañón Hacienda (H11880). The checklist also cites an unpublished name, *Selinocarpus undulatus*, based on a collection from 6.4 km W of CC, which differs from *S. angustifolius* in leaf-margin undulation and perianth pubescence.
- **Hybridization and introgression:** *Quercus greggii* × *Q. pringlei* is recorded from Cañón Agua. At Cañón Barrica, two *Q. gravesii* collections are noted as introgressed by *Q. hypoxantha*.
- **Range notes:** *Oxalis alpina* is recorded from a lower canyon near Laguna Grande, described as the second known Coahuila collection. Records of *Atriplex prosopidum* and *Tidestromia suffruticosa* var. *coahuilana* are documented with localities in and around the basin.
- **Type and reference specimens:** Several taxa are based on type material, including *Euphorbia pinkavana* (TEX type, cited from Cañón Hacienda), *Tidestromia suffruticosa* var. *coahuilana* and *Suaeda suffruticosa* var. *detonsa* (GH types, with the latter listed as several type collections). Many other records are cited as "not seen" and attributed to other workers.
- **Aquatic relevance:** The aquatic-associated flora is limited to marsh, spring and stream margins. This includes *Anemopsis californica*, *Nymphaea amplia*, *Salix nigra*, *Polygonum hydropiperoides*, *Nasturtium officinale* (uncommon in streams at Río Cañón) and *Acacia smallii* (locally common along waterways). The document does not address aquatic animals.
