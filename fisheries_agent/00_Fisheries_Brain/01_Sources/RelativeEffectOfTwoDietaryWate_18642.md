# Dietary Menaquinone Analogues and Blood Coagulation in Lake Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[H. A. Poston]] (1976), published in the *Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada*, compared two water-soluble synthetic forms of [[Vitamin K]], [[Menadione Sodium Bisulfite]] (MSB) and [[Menadione Dimethylpyrimidinol Bisulfite]] (MPB), in the diet of fingerling [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*). Over 18 weeks at 14 °C, MPB at 1 or 2 ppm hastened blood coagulation as effectively as the higher doses, and 1 ppm MSB produced the highest packed cell volume (PCV). Neither vitamin K form affected growth, feed conversion or body composition. The author concludes that 1 ppm MPB, supplying about 0.5 mg menaquinone per kg of diet, is sufficient for normal coagulation and adequate PCV.

## Background & Description

- **Subject species:** [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*), a salmonid fish. Fingerlings with an initial mean weight of 5.3 g were used.
- **Vitamin K context:** [[2-Methyl-3-Phytyl-1,4-Naphthoquinone]] (vitamin K1) was previously shown to reduce whole-blood clotting time and raise PCV in [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*). Menaquinone (menadione) prevented hemorrhage and death in [[Channel Catfish]] and anemia in [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*).
- **Feed industry practice:** Manufacturers predominantly used synthetic water-soluble vitamin K derivatives. MPB was approved as a feed additive in poultry and swine feeds in the USA, but data on its relative effectiveness in fish diets were scarce.
- **Study aim:** To compare two dietary levels of MPB and MSB in reducing clotting time and raising PCV in fingerling lake trout on a semipurified diet.

### Basal Diet Composition

The semipurified basal diet (Table 1) contained casein (vitamin-free) 40%, gelatin 10%, soy oil 10%, sucrose 10%, dextrin 10%, cellulose 12%, minerals 6.9%, vitamins 0.9%, L-tryptophan 0.04% and DL-methionine 0.20%. Mineral and vitamin premixes were specified in the table footnotes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Rearing conditions:** Triplicate lots of 50 fish were held in 7-liter Plexiglas jars supplied with aerated well water at 14 °C and a flow of 2 liters/min.
- **Feeding regime:** Fish were fed 3% body weight daily for the first 6 weeks, then progressively reduced to a minimum of 2.3% by the end of the study, adjusted from body weights taken every 2 weeks.
- **Diet processing:** Diets were extruded without steam in a laboratory pellet mill and sieved into two granule sizes: 1.08–2.83 mm until fish reached about 15.0 g, then 2.84–3.17 mm.
- **Vitamin K sources:** MSB contained 49% menaquinone; MPB contained 45.5% menaquinone. Five diets were tested: basal, plus 1.0 or 2.0 mg/kg of each source.

## Key Findings

**Blood coagulation and PCV (Table 2)**

| Treatment | Coagulation time (s) | PCV (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Basal diet | 100 (A) | 28.8 (A) |
| + 1 ppm MPB | 75 (B) | 34.5 (B, C) |
| + 2 ppm MPB | 76 (B) | 31.9 (A, B) |
| + 1 ppm MSB | 92 (C) | 37.1 (C) |
| + 2 ppm MSB | 95 (C) | 33.1 (B) |

Pooled SE: 1.52 (coagulation), 1.02 (PCV). Each value is the mean of five determinations per replicate lot, across three lots per treatment.

- **Coagulation:** MPB at 1 or 2 ppm reduced coagulation time significantly (P < 0.01) compared with the basal diet and MSB. MSB also shortened clotting time significantly (P < 0.01) but less effectively than MPB. The 1 ppm MPB dose was as effective as 2 ppm.
- **PCV:** Fish fed 1 ppm MSB had the highest mean PCV (37.1%). This did not differ significantly from 1 ppm MPB but was significantly higher than either analogue at 2 ppm. Fish fed 2 ppm MPB showed an intermediate PCV, not significantly different from the basal diet or 2 ppm MSB.

**Growth and feed efficiency (Table 3)**

- Mean body weight after 18 weeks ranged from 32.4 to 33.4 g across treatments, against a starting weight of 5.3 g.
- Feed:gain ratios ranged from 1.90 to 1.96.
- No significant differences (P < 0.05) occurred in growth or feed conversion, and the vitamin K source and level had no significant effect on body composition.

**Conclusions**

- MPB was the more effective source of vitamin K (menaquinone) for hastening coagulation in fingerling lake trout.
- A dietary level of 1 ppm MPB, supplying about 0.5 mg menaquinone per kg of diet, is apparently sufficient for normal coagulation and adequate PCV.
- This requirement is similar to the 0.53 mg/kg vitamin K1 requirement reported for young chickens (National Research Council 1971).
- The lack of growth or survival effect agrees with earlier brook trout work by Poston (1964).

**Methods note:** Coagulation time was measured by inserting a non-heparinized capillary tube into the heart and breaking segments at 15-second intervals until a fibrous strand formed. PCV was measured from heparinized capillary samples centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 5 minutes. Data were analyzed by [[Analysis of Variance]] and [[Duncan's Multiple Range Test]].
