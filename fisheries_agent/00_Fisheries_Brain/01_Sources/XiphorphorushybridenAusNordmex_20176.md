# Xiphophorus Hybrids from North Mexico and a Revision of X. kosszanderi and X. roseni

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1983 taxonomic paper by Manfred K. Meyer (Staatliches Museum für Tierkunde Dresden, Zoologische Abhandlungen, Bd. 38, Nr. 18) documents three natural hybrid populations of [[Xiphophorus]] (Poeciliidae) from northern Mexico. It also revises two previously described taxa, *X. kosszanderi* and *X. roseni*, which are reclassified as hybrids and synonymized. The paper includes an annotated letter from Dr. S. [[Contreras-Balderas]] on the hybridization problem in the Monterrey area, with a distribution map of local populations and their conservation status.

## Background & Description

The genus *Xiphophorus* Heckel, 1848 was recently revised by [[Rosen 1979]], and new taxa were described by Meyer and colleagues (Meyer & Schartl 1980; Meyer & Wischnath 1981). Natural hybridization within the genus was considered extremely rare. The only published example before this study was *X. alvarezi* × *X. helleri* from the Río Usumacinta system in Guatemala (Rosen 1979).

Three hybrid populations were examined, based on material from four collecting trips in 1980 and 1981:

- **[[Xiphophorus Variatus]] × [[Xiphophorus Xiphidium]] (El Limón, Tamaulipas):** Collected from a pond about 2 km south of El Limón near Cd. Mante. Specimens are catalogued as MTD-F 1891–1893 (wild-caught) and MTD-F 1894–1895 (F lab-bred, collected by M. Schartl). Described as intermediate between the parent species. Gonopodium structure is intermediate and shows no detectable anomalies. Adult males lack a sword and macromelanophore patterns; the body is grey-brown with a broad median stripe.
- **X. variatus × X. xiphidium (Río Purificación, Tamaulipas):** Sampled from a probable pond near the river (SMF 16200–16203). Habitat data are unknown. Identified as the same taxon as *X. kosszanderi* (Meyer & Wischnath 1981).
- **[[Xiphophorus Couchianus]] × X. variatus (Arroyo Chapultepec, Nuevo León):** Specimens include MTD-F 1896–1907 and SMF 16204–16207, collected in October 1981, November 1981 and February–March 1981 by E. Hnilicka, H. P. Weil and others. Identified as the same taxon as *X. roseni* (Meyer & Wischnath 1981).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**El Limón pond:** About 8 m² in area, with water depths of 30–80 cm in March 1980. The water was turbid, densely covered with water hyacinth, and had a thick mud substrate. Shoreline vegetation consisted of scrub and grassland. Associated fishes were [[Gambusia Vittata]] and [[Gambusia Affinis]] × [[Gambusia Aurata]] hybrids. The site lies directly beside the Federal Highway 85, north of Cd. Mante.

**Arroyo Chapultepec (Monterrey region):** Habitat details are referenced to Meyer & Wischnath (1981). Karte 2 (Monterrey) lists nine numbered localities for *X. couchianus*-like populations. Status of these localities:

- Localities (1), (3) and (7) were eliminated by drought.
- Localities (4) Ojo la Pastora and (5) Apodaca are threatened.
- Locality (6) was a temporary habitat, with individuals collected 20 m downstream and 1 m deeper than (4) in 1961/62.
- Locality (2) Cadereyta could not be verified after the original description.
- Locality (4) is currently secured and may be preserved.
- Localities (7) and (8) were the most recent sites where hybrids appeared.

Drought and habitat modification, along with aquarium releases, are the main threats. Ornamental Xiphophorus stocks released around Monterrey interbreed with each other and hybridize with *X. couchianus* forms.

## Key Findings

1. **Three natural hybrid populations** of Xiphophorus were documented from northern Mexico, which the author states is a new finding for this genus.
2. **Reclassification of two taxa:** *X. kosszanderi* Meyer & Wischnath, 1981 is synonymized with the *X. variatus* × *X. xiphidium* hybrid, and *X. roseni* Meyer & Wischnath, 1981 is synonymized with the *X. couchianus* × *X. variatus* hybrid.
3. **Morphology of hybrids:**
   - El Limón hybrids: intermediate gonopodium with no anomalies; grey-brown adults; no black flank spotting.
   - Arroyo Chapultepec hybrids: variatus-type dorsal fins in many adults; gonopodial claw anomalies; inner teeth of ray 4p often couchianus-like with double elements; split spines on ray 3; some grey individuals alongside yellow males and females; two individuals with black flank spots (macromelanophores).
4. **Sympatry without hybridization** is documented elsewhere in the genus, including [[Río Axtla]] (*X. pygmaeus*, *X. cortezi*, *X. variatus*), [[Río Nautla]] (*X. helleri*, *X. variatus*), [[Río Papaloapan]] system (*X. helleri*, *X. maculatus*), and [[Río Atoyac]] (*X. helleri*, *X. andersi*).
5. **Novel finding:** After geographic isolation was removed, the formerly valid species *X. variatus*, *X. xiphidium* and *X. couchianus* began interbreeding in the wild. Causes may include flooding or human introduction.
6. **Unresolved question:** It is unclear whether *X. variatus* and *X. xiphidium* periodically move between habitats through the upper [[Río Guayalejo]], possibly during floods or through human introduction.
7. **Monterrey taxonomy:** The original *X. couchianus* (Girard, 1859) was described from females collected at Monterrey and Cadereyta. Dr. Contreras-Balderas suggests that the La Huasteca form may be distinct, and that several *couchianus*-like local forms may have differentiated. Hybridization with aquarium stocks complicates this picture.
8. **Conservation concern:** Several natural *Xiphophorus* populations in the Monterrey area have been lost or are at risk, mainly from drought and habitat change, and hybridization with introduced stocks is a further threat.
