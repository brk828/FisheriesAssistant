# Geographic Variation, Taxonomic Status, and Biogeography of Two Widely Distributed African Fishes: Ctenopoma petherici and C. kingsleyae (Anabantidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #anabantidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines [[Ctenopoma Petherici]] and [[Ctenopoma Kingsleyae]], two closely related, allopatrically distributed anabantid fishes of central and west Africa whose taxonomic status has long been confused, with synonymy proposed in the literature. The authors used meristic and morphometric data from large museum series to map geographic variation and test whether the two forms are distinct species. They conclude that the two are separate entities that can be reliably allocated using three key meristic counts and a classification function. The paper also revises several synonymies, describes both species, and discusses the biogeographic history that produced their current distributions.

## Background & Description

The two species belong to the deep-bodied clade of the genus [[Ctenopoma]] within the family [[Anabantidae]], a group of air-breathing fishes possessing a suprabranchial [[Labyrinth Organ]]. Most species inhabit waters with low dissolved oxygen, such as swamps, marshes and inundation zones. The genus is broadly distributed across Africa, with at least 21 species, which fall into several groups (deep-bodied, shallow-bodied and dwarfed).

Earlier workers recognized and described multiple names for these forms. Several authors placed additional species in synonymy with one of the two taxa, while others proposed that the two be synonymized, which created longstanding confusion. The authors examined large numbers of museum specimens across the known range of both forms.

**Morphological diagnosis (summarized):**
- Both species have a multiply folded labyrinth organ, relatively nonprotrusible jaws, a spot at the base of the caudal fin, and large ctenoid scales covering the body and head.
- *C. petherici* is unpatterned except for the caudal spot; body colour is brown, tan or grey. Maximum observed size is 140 mm.
- Adult male *C. petherici* and *C. kingsleyae* both bear spined scale patches (scale ctenii) near the eye and caudal peduncle; males are about 50 mm and larger at maturity.
- Juvenile colour patterns are indistinguishable between the two species, which supports a close phylogenetic relationship.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat preferences.** The authors describe *C. petherici* as largely associated with savannah habitats and *C. kingsleyae* with forest habitats. In the Zio Marshes of western Togo, Loiselle (1971) reported that the fish are generally found in permanent oxbows during most of the year, and that ripe adults move into floodpools with the arrival of the rains to spawn. The same account describes local practice of holding live fish in earthen pots until the fishing season, a practice thought to appease river spirits and aid the catch.

**Distribution of *C. petherici*.** Found in the White Nile below Lake Albert, the Lake Chad basin, the Benue River, the upper and middle Niger, and the Senegal, Gambia and Casamance systems. It is also the only anabantid verified from the Oueme River (Benin) to the Sassandra River (western Ivory Coast).

**Distribution of *C. kingsleyae*.** Found in the Zaire basin, the lower Guinean drainages (Gabon, Cameroun, Nigeria, Niger Delta and Cross River), and the upper Guinean drainages (Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea), extending from the Cavally River to the Geba River.

**Biogeographic setting.** The distributions are largely parapatric, especially in west Africa. Several features shape them:
- The [[Dahomey Gap]] (Togo-Benin Gap), an intrusion of savannah habitat that reaches the coast and separates the forests of west and central Africa.
- A second savannah intrusion, the Baoulé V, which separates the coastal forest belt from the upper Guinean forest.
- The coastal forest belt, which remains only as a fragmented relic along lower river reaches (e.g., Sassandra, Bandama, Comoe), where forest fishes have been largely replaced by Nilo-Sudanic forms.
- [[Stream Capture]] events, such as elements of the Niger system captured by the upper Volta, which gave Nilo-Sudanic fishes access to the coastal region.

**Related taxa.** [[Ctenopoma Nebulosum]] was recently described from southeastern Nigeria (eastern Niger River Delta and lower Cross River). It is confined to running water in forests, lives sympatrically but apparently not syntopically with *C. kingsleyae*, and has a dramatically reduced labyrinth organ and a strikingly different pigmentation pattern. [[Ctenopoma Muriei]], from eastern Nilo-Sudanic waters, resembles *C. petherici* in general form and meristic counts but is much smaller and has different adult and juvenile colour patterns.

## Key Findings

**1. Two separable species.** The authors conclude the two forms are distinct species that can be allocated with high accuracy using meristic counts and a classification function, and that they should be recognized as separate entities.

**2. Meristic discrimination.** Six meristic variables were recorded from 204 *C. kingsleyae* and 172 *C. petherici*. [[Stepwise Multiple Discriminant Analysis]] (SMDA) identified three key counts that together accounted for 95% of total discrimination:
- Square root of lower vertical series scales (VSL), the most discriminating variable
- Square root of opercular scales (OPSR)
- Square root of upper vertical series scales (VSU)

A classification function was derived, with canonical mean scores of +1.444 for *C. kingsleyae* and a negative mean (about −1.7) for *C. petherici*. Using three (or six) meristic characters, the analysis assigned specimens correctly with 95% accuracy, based on jackknife evaluation.

**3. Scale and fin differences.**
- *C. petherici* has smaller scales, with 14 or more opercle scales in most individuals, compared with fewer than 14 in *C. kingsleyae*.
- Most *C. petherici* populations have anal-fin spine counts one or two higher than *C. kingsleyae*.
- An anal-spine count of 10 is rare in all *C. kingsleyae* populations, but nearly universal in Nile River *C. petherici*, found in nearly half of Lake Chad basin specimens, and common in other western Nilo-Sudanic populations.

**4. Morphometric confirmation.** Morphometric data (32–33 variables, size-corrected by [[Sheared Principal Component Analysis]] and log-ratio transformation) were consistent with the meristic results. Nine of 35 characters (body depth, preisthmus length, P2IST, GLCFTPI, horizontal eye diameter, pelvic-fin length, pectoral-fin length, longest soft anal ray and caudal-fin length) together correctly discriminated 84.7% of the combined individuals (70 *C. kingsleyae*, 67 *C. petherici*). Canonical means were +1.25 for *C. kingsleyae* and −1.19 for *C. petherici*. No single morphometric character was diagnostic, but body depth, eye diameter and interorbital distance showed modal differences.

**5. Pigmentation.** Pelvic-fin membranes of adult *C. kingsleyae* (40 mm and larger) are diffusely pigmented, giving a grey appearance, especially when folded. Pelvic fins of adult *C. petherici* are always unpigmented and clear. Zairean populations are more strongly pigmented than the upper or lower Guinean ones.

**6. Geographic variation.**
- *C. petherici* shows more pronounced intraspecific geographic meristic variation. Nile and Lake Chad populations are essentially identical and represent the typical morph (holotype from the White Nile). Lake Chad fish have slightly lower anal-spine counts, and counts decline further westward.
- The Oueme to Sassandra populations show the lowest fin counts (fewer than 10% of specimens have 10 anal spines), but scale size and pigmentation clearly place them in *C. petherici*. The authors identify these as "low fin meristic morph" *C. petherici*, recognized for the first time.
- *C. kingsleyae* is less meristically differentiated. Upper and lower Guinean populations have slightly higher scale counts than the homogeneous Zaire basin population, and morphometric variation is more apparent in this species.

**7. Synonymies revised.**
- *Anabas caudomaculatus* Ahl, 1927, *A. riggenbachi* Ahl, 1927, *A. togoensis* Ahl, 1928 and *A. petherici chadensis* Fowler, 1936 are placed in synonymy with *C. petherici*. *A. togoensis* had previously been placed with *C. kingsleyae*.
- *A. brevicentralis* Pellegrin, 1938 is placed in synonymy with *C. kingsleyae*.
- The authors decline to recognize subspecies within *C. petherici*, since Lake Chad and Nile River fish are nearly identical.

**8. Biogeographic conclusions.** The intra- and interspecific variation fits the hypothesized historical biogeography of central and west Africa, supporting the existence of two distinct evolutionary lineages. The authors note that the two species show parapatric patterns not fully explained by habitat. Historical factors, including savannah expansion and stream capture, probably shaped the modern distributions. Divergence has been significant in some disjunct populations (e.g., Nile vs. the low-fin morph), but slight in others (e.g., Nile vs. Lake Chad). Genetic study is needed to clarify these relationships.

**9. Specimen basis.** The specimen list is extensive, comprising hundreds of catalogued lots from museums including MRAC, BMNH, MNHN, USNM, CAS, ZMB and ANSP. Codes indicate which lots were used in the six-character meristic set (A), other meristic data (B) and morphometric data (C). Holotypes and paratypes are included for the taxa discussed.
