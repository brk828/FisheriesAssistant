# Desert Fishes Council Letter Opposing Gila River Dam Construction

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1980s-era correspondence from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation opposes proposed dam construction on the [[Gila River]] in New Mexico. The letter focuses on threats to the [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda fulgida]]) and [[Loach Minnow]] ([[Tiaroga cobitis]]), both endemic desert fishes facing extinction due to habitat loss and introduced species. The proposed Conner Damsite reservoir in the [[Cliff-Gila Valley]] would inundate critical habitat for these species, which are listed as endangered by Arizona and New Mexico and proposed for federal threatened species listing.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Council]] is an international organization founded in 1970 with over 400 members, including federal and state scientists, university researchers, resource specialists, and conservation advocates. The organization's mission is to halt habitat degradation in the Southwest and preserve desert aquatic ecosystems throughout the [[Basin and Range Province]] and other North American desert regions.

The [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda fulgida]]) is endemic to the [[Gila River]] basin and has experienced precipitous decline over 50 years in both range and abundance. At the time of writing, the species survived only in restricted portions of the [[Verde River]] (near [[Sycamore Creek]] confluence), [[Aravaipa Creek]] in Arizona, and limited reaches of the [[Gila River]] mainstream in the [[Cliff-Gila Valley]] of New Mexico. The [[Loach Minnow]] ([[Tiaroga cobitis]]) occupies similar habitat in the [[Cliff-Gila Valley]] area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The primary causes of [[Spikedace]] decline are human activities in the basin, specifically loss of suitable habitat and interactions with introduced fishes. Both [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] were listed as endangered by Arizona and New Mexico, with federal threatened species listing proposed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

The [[Gila River]] in the affected area represents one of the last relatively unmodified desert rivers in the nation. The ecosystem supports numerous species of special concern beyond the target fish species, including raptors, reptiles, and amphibians at or near the limits of their ranges.

The proposed dam would create multiple detrimental effects: inundation of primary habitat range in New Mexico, alteration of natural flow regimes, and facilitation of non-native fish species spread. Any combination of these impacts could result in species demise in New Mexico.

## Key Findings

- The [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda fulgida]]) has declined dramatically over 50 years and survives in only three restricted locations: [[Verde River]] confluence area, [[Aravaipa Creek]], and [[Cliff-Gila Valley]]
- The proposed Conner Damsite reservoir would inundate much of the species' primary range in New Mexico
- Both [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] ([[Tiaroga cobitis]]) are state-listed as endangered and proposed for federal threatened species listing
- The [[Desert Fishes Council]] petitioned the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to expedite federal listing
- The organization strongly opposes construction of any mainstream dam on the [[Gila River]] of New Mexico
- The affected river segment represents one of the last relatively unmodified desert rivers in the nation
- The Council questions the justification for expensive reservoir construction that provides only local benefits while threatening ecosystem survival during an era of budget constraints
- Alternative methods for providing New Mexico water authorized under [[Central Arizona Project]] provisions should be considered
