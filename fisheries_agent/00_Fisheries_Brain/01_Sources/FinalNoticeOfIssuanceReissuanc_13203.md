# U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Nationwide Permits - Final Notice of Issuance, Reissuance, and Modification

#regulatory #wetlands #aquatic-habitat #environmental-protection

## Executive Summary

This document is the final Federal Register notice (December 13, 1996) from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers announcing the issuance, reissuance, and modification of Nationwide Permits (NWPs) under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act and Section 10 of the Rivers and Harbors Act of 1899. The notice establishes standardized conditions for activities affecting waters of the United States, including specific protections for fish spawning areas, waterfowl breeding habitats, and aquatic life movements. The document addresses public comments on proposed permit conditions and establishes regional coordination procedures with District Engineers across all U.S. states and territories.

## Background & Description

The Corps of Engineers administers a system of Nationwide Permits that authorize certain activities in [[Waters of the United States]] with minimal environmental impact. This notice finalizes 40 NWPs (numbered 1-40, with #39 reserved) covering activities ranging from aids to navigation and scientific measurement devices to wetland restoration and single-family housing construction. Each permit includes general conditions applicable to all authorized activities and specific Section 404-only conditions for discharges of dredged or fill material.

The document responds to public comments on proposed conditions, particularly those affecting aquatic ecosystems. Key permit categories relevant to aquatic ecology include: NWP 4 (Fish and Wildlife Harvesting, Enhancement, and Attraction Devices), NWP 13 (Bank Stabilization), NWP 19 (Minor Dredging), NWP 27 (Wetland and Riparian Restoration), and NWP 30 (Moist Soil Management for Wildlife). The permits establish acreage limits, notification requirements, and mitigation standards to ensure minimal adverse effects on aquatic resources.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The permit conditions explicitly protect critical aquatic habitats and ecological processes. **Spawning Areas** (Condition 5) require that discharges in spawning areas during spawning seasons be avoided to the maximum extent practicable. One commenter suggested banning all discharges during spawning season, while another recommended extending protection through incubation periods. The Corps determined that regional conditions applied by District and Division Engineers provide more appropriate site-specific protections, as impacts vary by waterbody and activity type.

**Waterfowl Breeding Areas** (Condition 8) mandate avoidance of discharges in breeding areas for migratory waterfowl to the maximum extent practicable. Commenters suggested expanding protection to include shorebirds and neotropical migratory songbirds, but the Corps concluded this would impose unreasonable restrictions while the existing condition provides sufficient protection.

**Aquatic Life Movements** (General Condition 4) prohibits activities that substantially disrupt movement of indigenous aquatic species, including those that normally migrate through the area, unless the activity's primary purpose is water impoundment. This condition protects fish passage and connectivity of aquatic habitats.

The document addresses [[Submerged Aquatic Vegetation]] (SAV) protection, prohibiting certain activities in sites supporting SAV, including sites where SAV is documented to exist but may not be present in a given year. NWP 19 specifically prohibits dredging that would degrade coral reefs, SAV sites, anadromous fish spawning areas, or wetlands.

## Key Findings

1. **Spawning Area Protection Adopted Without Change**: The Corps retained the existing condition requiring avoidance of discharges in spawning areas during spawning seasons to the maximum extent practicable, rejecting suggestions for absolute bans. Regional conditions by District Engineers address site-specific spawning protection needs.

2. **Waterfowl Breeding Area Protections Maintained**: Condition 8 requires avoidance of discharges in migratory waterfowl breeding areas to the maximum extent practicable. The Corps declined to expand coverage to shorebirds and neotropical songbirds, determining existing protections are sufficient.

3. **Aquatic Life Movement Requirements**: General Condition 4 prohibits substantial disruption of indigenous aquatic species movements, including migratory species, protecting fish passage and habitat connectivity throughout project areas.

4. **Special Aquatic Sites Receive Enhanced Protection**: Multiple NWPs require notification and delineation when activities affect special aquatic sites including wetlands, and establish lower impact thresholds (e.g., NWP 18 limits impacts to 1/10 acre in special aquatic sites versus 25 cubic yards in other waters).

5. **Anadromous Fish Spawning Areas Explicitly Protected**: NWP 19 (Minor Dredging) specifically prohibits dredging in anadromous fish spawning areas, recognizing the critical importance of these habitats for fish populations.

6. **Submerged Aquatic Vegetation Sites Protected**: Activities are prohibited in sites supporting SAV, including documented historical SAV sites, even when vegetation is not present in a given year, protecting these critical habitats from degradation.

7. **High Flow Obstruction Minimized**: Section 404 Condition 6 requires that discharges not permanently restrict or impede passage of normal or expected high flows, protecting natural hydrologic regimes essential for aquatic ecosystems.

8. **Impoundment Effects Must Be Minimized**: Condition 7 requires minimization of adverse effects on aquatic systems caused by accelerated water passage or flow restriction when discharges create impoundments.

9. **Temporary Fill Removal Required**: Condition 9 mandates complete removal of temporary fills and restoration of areas to preexisting elevation, allowing natural revegetation with native species.

10. **Regional Coordination Established**: District Engineers across all 50 states and territories will issue local public notices establishing regional conditions or revocations based on local aquatic resource concerns, with contact information provided for each district.
