## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 Bureau of Reclamation news release announcing the initiation of an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) process for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. The EIS is intended to evaluate how dam operations affect downstream natural resources in [[Grand Canyon National Park]] and the [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]], including explicit consideration of "wildlife and fishery needs" alongside power generation, recreation, and legal/regulatory mandates. The release contains no direct morphological, taxonomic, or population data on fish species; its relevance to ichthyology is entirely as a management and regulatory action affecting [[Colorado River]] fish habitat downstream of a major hydroelectric dam.

## Background & Description
No morphological, anatomical, or species-specific biological data are presented in this source. The document is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not describe any fish taxa in physical detail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **System affected**: Regulated tailwater reach of the [[Colorado River]] downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], spanning [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]] and [[Grand Canyon National Park]].
- **Ecological driver of concern**: Dam operations (flow regime, discharge patterns) are recognized as a major determinant of downstream aquatic and riparian conditions, implicating temperature, sediment transport, and flow variability — all key habitat factors for native and endangered Colorado River fish species.
- **Regulatory/ecological frameworks referenced**:
  - [[Endangered Species Act]] — explicitly named as a legal consideration, implying presence of federally listed fish species in the affected reach (though none are named in this text).
  - [[Colorado River Compact]] and [[Colorado River Storage Act]] — legal frameworks governing water allocation and dam operation that intersect with ecological management.
  - National Park Service mandates — implying habitat protection obligations within [[Grand Canyon National Park]].
- **Cooperating agencies**: [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Park Service]] are named as cooperating agencies specifically for wildlife/fishery and park resource expertise, alongside the [[Western Area Power Administration]] representing power interests.

## Key Findings
- The Bureau of Reclamation filed a Notice of Intent (published in the Federal Register, October 30, 1989) to prepare a draft EIS examining [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and downstream impacts.
- The EIS was directed by Interior Secretary Manuel Lujan and is coordinated by Upper Colorado Regional Director Roland Robison.
- A proposed schedule targeted completion of a **draft EIS by the end of 1991**.
- The EIS scope explicitly includes **wildlife and fishery needs** as a category of downstream natural resources to be evaluated, alongside recreation and power needs.
- The process will examine both **existing operational criteria** and **alternative environmental criteria** for future dam operating plans, aimed at identifying options to minimize ecological impacts of dam operations consistent with existing law.
- Formal public involvement (scoping meetings) was scheduled to begin in **January 1990**, though specific meeting times/locations were not yet determined at the time of release.
- **Significance for ichthyology**: This document represents an early institutional acknowledgment that dam operation regimes (flow, temperature, sediment) directly affect downstream fish populations, foreshadowing later research and management actions concerning native Colorado River fishes (e.g., species protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]]) in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach — though no specific species data are provided in this release itself.
