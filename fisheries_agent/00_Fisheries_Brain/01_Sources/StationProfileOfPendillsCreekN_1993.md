## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Pendills Creek National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1951 under Public Law 249. Located near Brimley, Michigan, the hatchery's primary mission is to rear broodstock [[Lake Trout]] to supply eggs and yearlings for the **Great Lakes Restoration Program**. As of the end of FY-1993, the station maintained a broodstock population of 2,045 adult lake trout across two strains — the **Superior Marquette** and **Seneca Lake** strains — with a phased transition underway to retire the Superior Marquette legacy year classes. The facility is notable for its dual-purpose infrastructure (broodstock rearing and egg incubation) and for an experimental cold-water incubation technique using creek water rather than well water to delay development timing for interstate egg shipment.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphometric or anatomical data (e.g., length, weight, meristics) for individual [[Lake Trout]] specimens. Available biological data pertain to population structure and reproductive output rather than individual morphology:

- **Broodstock composition (end of FY-93):** 2,045 total adults — 1,193 females and 852 males.
- **Strain identity:** Superior Marquette strain (year classes 1974, 1975, 1977, and 1981) and Seneca Lake strain.
- **Juvenile cohort:** 425,000 fish of the 1993 year class (Superior Marquette strain) held on station for the spring 1994 distribution program.
- **Egg size/viability proxy:** Eye-up rate of eggs (a standard hatchery viability metric) was 77%, suggesting normal-to-healthy embryonic development under the station's incubation regime.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Pendills Creek NFH functions as a controlled propagation environment supporting the restoration of [[Lake Trout]] populations within the [[Great Lakes]] ecosystem, a species whose wild populations were historically decimated by overfishing and invasive [[Sea Lamprey]] predation.

- **Habitat management:** The hatchery utilizes both well water and natural creek water (Pendills Creek) for incubation, with creek water intentionally employed in FY-93 for its cooler temperature profile to slow embryonic development by approximately four weeks prior to shipment.
- **Target stocking waters:** Distributed fish are released into federal waters (via the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) supporting Lake Michigan and Lake Huron fisheries in the states of **Michigan** and **Wisconsin**.
- **Restoration role:** The station operates as a node within the broader [[Great Lakes Restoration Program]], producing broodstock-derived eggs and yearlings rather than solely stocking fish directly, indicating its role as a genetic/reproductive resource hub feeding other hatcheries (notably [[Jordan River National Fish Hatchery]]).
- **Physical infrastructure supporting the niche:** Sixteen concrete raceways, an effluent abatement tank, a sludge holding pond, and a water diversion box with a degasser — reflecting active water quality management for coldwater salmonid rearing.

## Key Findings

- **Egg production (FY-93):** 2,678,950 eggs spawned from 620 females (Superior Marquette and Seneca Lake strains combined); 2,064,850 eyed eggs resulted, an overall eye-up rate of **77%**.
- **Cold-water incubation trial:** All eggs incubated at Pendills Creek in FY-93 used creek water instead of the typical well water, deliberately slowing development by ~4 weeks to align with shipping schedules to [[Jordan River National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Operational transition:** The hatchery is scheduled to revert to a **100% production facility** in summer 1994, phasing out older Superior Marquette year classes (1974–1981) after the fall 1993 spawning season.
- **Distribution (FY-93):** 295,900 lake trout distributed via federal (FWS) waters supporting Michigan and Wisconsin fisheries in Lakes Michigan and Huron; no state or private waters were stocked that year.
- **Visitor engagement:** Over 22,000 visitors in FY-93, a >50% increase attributed to the resurfacing and designation of Lakeshore Drive as a **National Scenic Byway**.
- **Funding trends:** Operations budget rose from $381,382 (FY-92) to $396,596 (FY-93), with projected increases to $442,741 (FY-94) and $456,026 (FY-95); FTE staffing remained stable around 7.0–8.0 over the period, down from 10.5 in FY-91.
- **Physical plant:** Facility includes a hatchery building (office, lab, tank room, visitor lobby), a service building with workshop and apartment, multiple storage buildings, sixteen concrete raceways, and staff housing (three six-room wood-frame houses).
