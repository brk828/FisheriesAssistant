# Late Pleistocene and Recent Erosion and Alluviation in Parts of the Colorado River System, Arizona and Utah

#geology #quaternary #pleistocene #erosion #alluviation #colorado-river

## Executive Summary

This geological survey documents five cycles of downcutting and alluviation during the Pleistocene in the [[Colorado River]] basin, with total Quaternary cutting between 800 and 1,500 feet and late Pleistocene to Recent cutting between 100 and 300 feet. The study identifies five Recent alluvial units and multiple erosional cycles, with deposits ranging from 25 to 200 feet thick throughout valleys and canyons on the [[Colorado Plateau]]. Cultural material and fossil evidence provide chronological control from approximately 5000-6000 B.C. to present (A.D. 1925).

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River]] system on the [[Colorado Plateau]] contains extensive alluvial deposits recording multiple cycles of erosion and deposition. Late Pleistocene deposits are identified by fossils of large mammals found near the [[Little Colorado River]] and by relationships to glaciofluvial deposits in nearby mountains. The [[Jeddito Formation]] and terrace deposits contain these diagnostic fossils.

Streams with headwater areas above 7,500 feet (including the [[Colorado River]], [[San Juan River]], [[Little Colorado River]], [[Escalante River]], and [[Deadman Wash]]) deposited gravelly sediments during Pleistocene time that were coarser than Recent deposits. Streams heading below 7,500 feet (such as [[Jeddito Wash]] and [[Castle Creek]]) deposited relatively less gravel, with locally finer material than Recent alluvium.

Recent time is represented by five alluvial units (Units 1-5) and four erosional cycles. Unit 4 forms the bulk of Recent alluvium in arroyo walls, containing cross-bedded sand and gravel beds that intertongue with and grade upward into sandy silt and silty sand layers. Buried soils with carbonaceous material and thin mud beds occur throughout. Unit 3 contains relatively large amounts of carbonaceous material and consists mainly of thin-bedded sediments ranging from mud to silty sand.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The depositional environment varied significantly between units. Unit 3 sediments were laid down by sluggish streams in swamps with runoff spread across flood plains, while Units 1, 2, and 4 gravels were deposited by more vigorous streams with concentrated runoff in channels and sheet flooding. These differences reflect changing fluvial conditions and climate throughout the late Quaternary.

Periglacial and glacial activity within drainage areas strongly influenced deposit types. Glaciofluvial sediments from [[San Francisco Mountain]] extend downstream and grade into terrace deposits along [[Deadman Wash]] and the [[Little Colorado River]]. Lower gravel terraces along the [[San Juan River]] and [[Animas River]] trace into glacial-outwash deposits in the [[San Juan Mountains]].

The study area encompasses major drainages including [[Glen Canyon]], [[Marsh Pass]], [[Lake Canyon]], [[Harris Wash]], [[Laguna Creek]], and [[Moenkopi Wash]]. Work was conducted in cooperation with the Bureau of Indian Affairs.

## Key Findings

**Quaternary Erosion and Alluviation:**
- At least five cycles of downcutting and alluviation occurred during Pleistocene age
- Total Quaternary cutting: 800 to 1,500 feet
- Late Pleistocene and Recent cutting: 100 to 300 feet
- Alluvium thickness ranges from 25 to 200 feet in most valleys and canyons

**Late Pleistocene Deposits:**
- Consist predominantly of sand and gravel
- Form terraces along major rivers ([[Colorado River]], [[Little Colorado River]], [[San Juan River]])
- Possibly preserved beneath Recent alluvium
- Age confirmed by large mammal fossils and relationships to glaciofluvial deposits

**Recent Alluvial Units (in descending order):**
- Unit 1: Most recent deposits
- Unit 2: Includes early deposits of sand and gravel, followed by late deposits forming low alluvial terraces
- Unit 3 ([[Naha Formation]]): Carbonaceous material, thin-bedded mud to silty sand, prominent soil zones
- Unit 4 (upper [[Tsegi Formation]]): Bulk of Recent alluvium, cross-bedded sand and gravel with buried soils
- Unit 5 (lower [[Tsegi Formation]]): Oldest Recent unit, lithologically similar to Unit 4 but more cemented

**Erosional Episodes:**
- Major erosion possibly starting A.D. 1100, predominating in A.D. 1200s
- Channels cut 10 to 20 feet deep, two-thirds as deep and one-fifth as wide as present arroyos
- Present arroyo cutting episode: main cutting between approximately A.D. 1850 and 1900
- Early Unit 2 deposition preceded main arroyo cutting
- Some arroyos currently aggrading in [[Defiance Uplift]]-[[Chuska Mountains]] area

**Cultural Chronology:**
- Associated cultural material ranges from Basketmaker III to present (approximately A.D. 500 to A.D. 1925)
- Pueblo II and Pueblo IV periods represented
- Navajo and Paiute utilization documented
- Anglo-American farm and ranch utilization by A.D. 1925
- Chipped stone tools found in older deposits (approximately 2000 B.C.)

**Stream Behavior Patterns:**
- Streams with headwaters above 7,500 feet deposited coarser Pleistocene gravels than Recent sediments
- Streams heading below 7,500 feet deposited finer, less abundant gravel
- Depositional conditions varied: Unit 3 by slow-moving water and swamps; Units 1, 2, 4 by faster-moving water with concentrated runoff
