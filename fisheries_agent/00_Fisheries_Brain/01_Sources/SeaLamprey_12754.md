# Sea Lamprey Control in the Great Lakes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #petromyzontidae #invasive-species #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1965 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service leaflet documents the devastating impact of the invasive [[Sea Lamprey]] (*Petromyzon marinus*) on [[Great Lakes]] fisheries and describes cooperative U.S.-Canadian control efforts using selective chemical larvicides. The lamprey invasion caused near-extinction of [[Lake Trout]] populations, particularly in [[Lake Michigan]]. Chemical treatment programs using [[TFM]] (3-trifluormethyl-4-nitrophenol) began in 1958 on tributary streams, achieving an 82% reduction in adult lamprey populations in [[Lake Superior]] by 1962. The document covers lamprey natural history, control methods including electric barriers and chemical treatments, and lake trout restoration efforts through hatchery stocking programs.

## Background & Description

The [[Sea Lamprey]] is a parasitic jawless fish that invaded the [[Great Lakes]] system, causing catastrophic damage to commercial and sport fisheries. Adult lampreys are parasitic, attaching to fish with their sucker-like mouths and feeding on body fluids. The species has a complex life cycle involving freshwater spawning in tributary streams, a prolonged larval (ammocete) stage in stream sediments, metamorphosis to parasitic adults, and migration to lakes for the parasitic feeding phase.

The lamprey's invasion resulted in near-extinction of native [[Lake Trout]] populations, particularly devastating in [[Lake Michigan]] where commercial catches collapsed. The species became established throughout the upper Great Lakes system, with breeding populations in numerous tributary streams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Requirements:**
- Spawning occurs in tributary streams with suitable gravel substrates
- Larval lampreys (ammocetes) burrow in stream sediments for 3-7 years
- Adult parasitic phase occurs in open lake waters
- Lampreys require specific stream conditions for successful reproduction

**Life Cycle Stages:**
- **Spawning**: Adults migrate upstream to spawn in gravel nests
- **Larval Stage**: Ammocetes remain burrowed in stream sediments, filter-feeding on organic matter
- **Metamorphosis**: Larvae transform to parasitic adults
- **Parasitic Phase**: Adults migrate to lakes, attach to host fish, feed on body fluids

**Impact on Fisheries:**
- Caused near-extinction of [[Lake Trout]] in [[Lake Michigan]]
- Severely depleted lake trout populations in [[Lake Superior]] and [[Lake Huron]]
- Each lamprey can kill multiple fish during its parasitic phase
- Devastated commercial fishing industry in the Great Lakes

## Key Findings

**Control Program Results (1958-1965):**
- Chemical treatments using [[TFM]] began on [[Lake Superior]] tributaries in 1958
- By 1960, 72 streams were treated; 53 additional streams treated by 1965
- Adult lamprey population in [[Lake Superior]] declined 82% by 1962 compared to 1957-61 levels
- Population remained 77% below historical levels in 1963-1964
- By 1964, 78 streams tributary to [[Lake Michigan]] successfully treated
- Remaining 21 [[Lake Michigan]] streams anticipated for treatment by late 1966

**Chemical Control Methods:**
- Primary larvicide: [[TFM]] (3-trifluormethyl-4-nitrophenol)
- Selective toxicity: kills lamprey larvae while sparing most fish species
- Application: metered into streams using portable feeders
- Monitoring: post-treatment surveys to verify larval mortality
- Re-treatment conducted if live larvae found in significant numbers
- Synergistic combinations with other compounds (5,2'-dichloro-4'-nitrosalicylanilide) developed

**Alternative Control Methods:**
- Electric barriers (weirs) tested on streams like [[Pere Marquette River]]
- Alternating current electrodes prevent upstream passage
- Direct current arrays guide fish into traps
- Mechanical barriers less practical than chemical treatment for large-scale control

**Lake Trout Restoration:**
- Since 1961, lake trout populations responded favorably to reduced lamprey predation
- Older and larger trout more abundant
- Decline in incidence of fresh lamprey wounds on trout
- 10.7 million hatchery-reared yearling trout planted in [[Lake Superior]] since 1958
- 3.4 million trout planned for stocking in Lakes Superior and Michigan in spring 1965
- Hatchery fish comprise bulk of younger trout in [[Lake Superior]]

**Research Infrastructure:**
- Bio-assay laboratory at [[Hammond Bay]], Michigan screens selective chemicals
- Cooperative research between U.S. and Canadian teams
- State, Federal, and Provincial hatcheries produce stocking fish
- Ongoing monitoring of lamprey populations and treatment effectiveness

**Future Plans:**
- Complete treatment of remaining [[Lake Michigan]] tributaries by 1966
- Commence work in [[Lake Huron]] area after Lake Michigan completion
- Re-treat any reinfested streams before larvae reach metamorphosis
- Continue monitoring to maintain lamprey populations at suppressed levels
- Uncertainty remains whether lamprey reduction sufficient for complete lake trout recovery
