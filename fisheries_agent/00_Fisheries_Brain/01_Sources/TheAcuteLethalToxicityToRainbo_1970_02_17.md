## Executive Summary

This 1970 study by Brown & Dalton, conducted at the Water Pollution Research Laboratory (Stevenage, England), investigated the acute (48-hour) lethal toxicity of binary and ternary mixtures of [[Copper]], [[Phenol]], [[Zinc]], and [[Nickel]] to [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*). The core hypothesis tested was the principle of **concentration addition** (simple additivity), whereby the toxicity of a mixture can be predicted by summing the fractional toxicities (relative to each poison's individual 48-h LC50) of its components. Three mixtures were tested: (1) copper + phenol, (2) copper + zinc + phenol, and (3) copper + zinc + nickel. Results confirmed that additive toxicity models adequately predicted observed mortality for all three mixtures, reinforcing findings from prior related studies on ammonia-phenol-zinc mixtures, though with a noted caveat regarding zinc-dominant high-hardness mixtures from earlier work.

## Background & Description

- **Test organism:** Approximately 1-year-old, hatchery-reared [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri* Richardson).
- **Cohort 1 (Copper + Phenol trials):**
  - Average length: 138 ± 16 mm
  - Average weight: 28.5 ± 8.6 g
  - Condition factor (K): 1.05
- **Cohort 2 (Three-poison mixture trials):**
  - Average length: 143 ± 19 mm
  - Average weight: 33.5 ± 13.6 g
  - Condition factor (K): 1.14
- Condition factor calculated as K = 10⁵·W/L³ (W = weight in g, L = length in cm).
- Fish were held in flowing water matching dilution water quality for ≥1 month prior to testing; not fed during the test or in the preceding 24 h.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat medium:** Hard, untreated ground water (total hardness 240 mg/l as CaCO₃; broader laboratory range 240–320 mg/l).
- **Water chemistry controls:**
  - pH maintained between 7.3–7.5 (broader relevant range 7.4–7.8 in related lab work).
  - Dissolved oxygen maintained above 90% air saturation via diffused compressed air (2 l/min).
  - CO₂ added at 65 ml/min to buffer pH.
  - Temperature range across the test series: 15.3°C–18.4°C, held within ±0.5°C per individual test (except the copper-zinc-nickel test, which varied by 2.9°C).
- **Exposure regime:** Static renewal toxicity test; solutions renewed every 6 h in 600 × 300 × 300 mm Perspex aquaria (40 l volume) to maintain stable poison concentrations (±10% analytical error).
- **Relevance:** This work sits within a broader ecological concern regarding [[Freshwater Pollution]] and the survival of fish populations in rivers receiving industrial effluents containing heavy metals and phenolic compounds — directly relevant to interpreting fish absence in polluted English rivers (cf. Brown et al. 1970, in press).
- Nickel toxicity was notably higher under these **static** test conditions compared to prior **continuous-flow** exposure studies (Jackson & Brown, 1970), suggesting static/renewal test design may elevate apparent nickel toxicity — an important consideration for [[Toxicity Testing Methodology]].

## Key Findings

### Individual poison toxicity (48-h LC50 to Rainbow Trout)
| Poison | 48-h LC50 (mg/l) | 95% CI | Slope Function (S) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Copper]] | 0.75 | 0.67–0.84 | 1.29 (1.16–1.43) |
| [[Phenol]] | 9.4 | 9.09–9.8 | 1.08 (limits not estimable) |
| [[Zinc]] | 4.0 | 3.63–4.46 | 1.3 (1.07–1.51) |
| [[Nickel]] | 32.0 | 28.6–35.8 | 1.32 (1.2–1.45) |

### Mixture toxicity (48-h LC50 expressed as proportion of predicted additive LC50)
| Mixture | 48-h LC50 (proportion of predicted) | 95% CI | Slope Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper + Phenol | 1.17 | 1.07–1.29 | 1.175 |
| Copper + Zinc + Phenol | 1.07 | 1.02–1.13 | 1.11 |
| Copper + Zinc + Nickel | 1.01 | 0.9–1.14 | 1.31 (1.20–1.43) |

- A predicted value of 1.0 represents perfect agreement with the **simple additivity model**; all three observed mixture LC50 values (1.01–1.17) closely approximated 1.0, confirming that acute lethal toxicity of these mixtures is well-predicted by **summation of fractional toxicities**.
- This supports and extends earlier findings on additive toxicity models for other poison combinations (e.g., ammonia + phenol + zinc; Brown, Jordan & Tiller, 1969).
- **Exception/caveat:** In prior related studies, mixtures where zinc contributed >75% of predicted toxicity were found to be **significantly less toxic than predicted**, indicating a potential **antagonistic effect** at high zinc-dominance ratios — a boundary condition for the additivity principle not violated in the present study's tested ratios.
- Nickel was not directly chemically analyzed in solution; reported nickel concentrations are **nominal** (based on quantities added), a methodological limitation.
- Analytical methods used: [[Oczynski Method]] for phenol, [[Sandell Method]] for zinc, and [[Jonassen Method]] for copper; all concentrations remained stable within ±10% between the 6-hourly solution renewals.
- Statistical determination of LC50, slope function, and confidence limits followed the [[Litchfield-Wilcoxon Method]] (1949); median survival times were calculated via geometric mean or Bliss (1937) graphical analysis depending on mortality proportion.
- **Broader implication:** The findings support using additive toxicity models as a practical tool for predicting acute lethal effects of multi-pollutant discharges in freshwater ecosystems, aiding water quality standards and effluent management, while cautioning that extreme compositional ratios (e.g., zinc-dominant mixtures) may require independent verification due to possible synergism or antagonism.
