# Roundtail Chub Taxonomic Status: Moapa River Gila robusta vs. Virgin River Gila robusta seminuda

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document reviews the taxonomic status of the roundtail chub (*Gila robusta*) from the [[Moapa River]] in Nevada and compares it with the Virgin roundtail chub (*Gila robusta seminuda*) from the [[Virgin River]]. The Moapa population has received little systematic study, and no published systematic work on it existed when the paper was written. Meristic and morphometric comparisons suggest the two forms differ, and the author concludes that the Moapa form may warrant subspecific recognition. Formal systematic analyses were not yet available.

## Background & Description

- **Taxa compared:** *Gila robusta* (Moapa River) and *Gila robusta seminuda* (Virgin River). The Virgin form was originally described as *Gila seminuda* by [[Cope and Yarrow 1875]].
- **Status history:** The Moapa form has been tentatively considered an undescribed subspecies by some authors (Deacon and Williams 1985). Distinctiveness of *G. r. seminuda* from related taxa in the [[Colorado River Basin]] has been widely accepted since its original description (Holden and Stahlnaker 1970; Minckley 1973; Smith et al. 1979; Deacon and Williams 1985).
- **Data limitations:** Published and unpublished data came from different sources, sample sizes were small, and collection methods varied, which made comparisons difficult. Only characters that could be compared objectively were used.
- **Original description (Virgin form):** Cope and Yarrow (1875) reported fin ray counts, scale counts, head length of about one-fifth of total length, and a maxilla that does not reach the orbit's front rim, among other features.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area and habitat alteration**
- Both the Moapa and Virgin river basins have been substantially altered by human development, including the construction of [[Hoover Dam]] and the filling of [[Lake Mead]].
- Before Lake Mead formed, the Moapa River entered the Virgin River about 35 km upstream of its confluence with the Colorado River. Lower reaches of both rivers are now inundated by Lake Mead.
- The Moapa River is spring-fed and has cooler temperatures and more stable flows downstream of its springs. Its upper reaches have been modified by water withdrawals for domestic and agricultural use.
- The Virgin River is a larger (about 370 km long), highly fluctuating system with intermittent reaches. Flash floods from summer thunderstorms are common.

**Distribution and habitat use**
- *G. r. seminuda* was once widely distributed across the Virgin River system but is now restricted to the mainstem and some tributaries.
- Warm, saline discharges from springs in the Virgin basin were suggested as a factor supporting the form's survival.
- *G. r. seminuda* is most abundant in deep runs and pools with gravel and sand substrates, and it feeds on macroinvertebrates and algae in riffles.
- Little ecological information exists for the Moapa form. It is most common in middle and upper reaches, away from springs.

**Threats:** Both populations have declined because of habitat alteration, pollution, water withdrawals, and the introduction of exotic species.

**Native fish community of the Moapa system:** The Moapa River supports a small endemic assemblage that includes [[Crenichthys]] species and the monotypic *Moapa coriacea*. All three native fishes are endemic to the Moapa system, and their relationships suggest a history of connection with Pleistocene or Holocene drainages.

## Key Findings

1. **Morphology (meristics):** The Moapa form has more gill rakers than Virgin River specimens, with counts taken on the left anterior gill arch. Fin ray counts differ only slightly between the two forms (Tables 1–2).
2. **Morphometrics:** Head length to caudal peduncle depth shows some differentiation. The Moapa form has a thicker caudal peduncle and a deeper, longer head (Table 3).
3. **Intermediate character states:** *G. r. seminuda* is morphologically intermediate between *G. robusta* (Baird and Girard) and *G. cypha* (Baird and Girard). [[Miller 1945]] suggested this intermediacy may indicate hybrid origin, and this idea was later explored by Smith et al. (1979).
4. **Biogeographic relationships:** The Moapa River is a direct tributary of the Virgin River, but its fish fauna has closer affinities with the Pahranagat Valley than with the Virgin system. Its nearest relatives may be *Gila cypha* from the Pahranagat Valley. That form differs from the Pluvial/Pahranagat chub only in having a slightly thicker body and smaller scales.
5. **Taxonomic conclusion:** The Moapa River roundtail chub may warrant subspecific recognition. Formal systematic analyses were not indicated at the time of writing.
