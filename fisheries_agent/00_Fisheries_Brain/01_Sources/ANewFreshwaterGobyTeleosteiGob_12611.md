# A New Freshwater Goby from the Pongolo Floodplain, Zululand, South Africa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gobiidae

## Executive Summary

This 1977 taxonomic paper by H. M. Kok and S. J. M. Blaber, published in *Zoologica Africana*, describes [[Mugilogobius Pongolensis]] sp. n., a freshwater goby from the [[Pongolo Floodplain]] in northern Zululand. It is the first record of the genus [[Mugilogobius]] from southern and eastern Africa. The record extends the known range of the genus from the Indonesian archipelago and South Australia to the Indian Ocean and the eastern African coast. The species is known only from a small group of floodplain pans and is considered probably endemic to the Pongolo system.

## Background & Description

The Pongolo floodplain comprises the [[Pongolo River]] and 25 major associated pans. It lies east of the [[Lebombo Mountains]], between latitudes 26°51'S and 27°25'S, near longitude 32°15'E. The first specimens were collected from [[Nsimbu Pan]] in August 1974, and later from [[Tete Pan]].

The authors checked South African gobioid literature (Barnard, Smith, Jubb) and Indo-Pacific literature before concluding the fish was new. The male holotype was examined by the [[J. L. B. Smith Institute of Ichthyology]], which confirmed it as new to southern and eastern Africa. Initially the fish was keyed to [[Vaimosa]], but Jordan, Tanaka & Snyder (1913) and Koumans (1940) regarded Vaimosa as a synonym of Mugilogobius, so the species was described within that genus.

**Holotype:** RUSI 807, male, 27 mm SL, collected 30 August 1974 by H. Kok from Nsimbu Pan using a fry net.

**Paratypes:** Ten specimens, 14.5–25 mm SL, including males, females and juveniles, collected from Nsimbu Pan between September and October 1974.

**Diagnostic characters (from the description):**
- Dorsal fin formula D VI + 8; anal fin A I 8.
- Lateral scale series 29 (range 28–30, mean 29.1); all scales ctenoid, with equal size.
- Body cylindrical; head 3.39 times in SL (mean; range 3.2–3.6).
- Eyes large, subdorsal, 3.29 times in head (mean).
- Ventral fins fully united, with a strong frenum.
- Males have a narrow, acutely conical genital papilla; females have a short, broadly conical one.
- Eggs round, 0.25–0.425 mm, heavily yolked.
- Teeth are simple recurved spikes in about four rows, with canines in males only.

**Colour (live fish):** Semi-transparent, faintly golden-green, with five or six broken dark-brown diagonal bands and two dark spots at the caudal base. The first dorsal fin has a dark blue iridescent spot and a red margin. The second dorsal has three bands, and the anal fin is reddish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occurs in floodplain pans, which are disconnected from the main river during part of the year. The authors treat it as truly freshwater because ovigerous females and fry were found in these isolated pans. [[Glossogobius Giuris]] is also present and is widespread across the floodplain, while [[Mugilogobius Pongolensis]] was recorded only from two adjacent pans, the Nsimbu/Mtigi complex and Tete.

Two other southern African freshwater gobies were noted: [[Platygobius Aeneofuscus]], an Indo-Pacific species from east-coast rivers, and [[Silhouettea Sibayi]], which is restricted to [[Lake Sibaya]]. Jubb (1967) recorded the first two species in the Pongolo system, but the authors collected only *G. giuris* and *M. pongolensis* from the floodplain pans.

The genus shows a discontinuous Indo-Pacific distribution, similar to that of [[Pandaka]], which is found in the Philippines and southern Africa. The authors suggest the species is probably endemic to the Pongolo floodplain because no other records existed at publication.

## Key Findings

- **New species and genus record:** *Mugilogobius pongolensis* is described as new to science. It is the first record of *Mugilogobius* from southern and eastern Africa and from the Indian Ocean proper.
- **Distinguishing from *Vaimosa*:** The species differs from the type species of Vaimosa, *V. fontinalis* (Samoa), in having no cycloid scales and in having 28–30 lateral scales rather than 37.
- **Comparison with congeners:** The species differs from the type species [[Mugilogobius Abei]] (Japan) in scale size and in opercle scaling. It differs from [[Mugilogobius Galwayi]] (South Australia) in the shape of the snout, eye size and maxilla length. It differs from [[Mugilogobius Luzonensis]] (Philippines) in fin ray counts and maxilla extent.
- **Restricted range:** Specimens were collected only from Nsimbu Pan and Tete Pan at first. The addendum reports later captures from Namanini, Nhlolo, Nhlanjane, Tete and Nyamiti pans, the Pongolo River, and the [[Lake St. Lucia]] system after heavy flooding.
- **Freshwater status:** Ovigerous females and fry in disconnected pans indicate that the species completes its life cycle in freshwater.
- **Probable endemism:** Given the lack of other records, the authors consider the species probably endemic to the Pongolo River floodplain.
