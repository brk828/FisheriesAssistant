# Toxaphene Toxicity and Detoxification in Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 study by [[Workman and Neuhold]] at [[Utah State University]] tested the acute toxicity of the insecticide [[Toxaphene]] to [[Goldfish]], [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Rainbow Trout]], and documented how long the chemical persists in water and in the field. Two formulations were compared: a "floating" emulsion and a "sinking" type. Tests were run in three natural waters of differing chemistry. Lethal concentrations (LC50 values) were estimated with [[Probit Analysis]]. The authors concluded that toxaphene is a viable piscicide for eradication work, especially in spring-fed ponds, because it kills fish without the irritation that drives them to escape, but that its slow detoxification limits its use in rivers and streams.

## Background & Description

- **Toxaphene** is a synthetic insecticide made of polychloro bicyclic terpenes, with chlorinated camphene predominating. Its approximate empirical formula is given as C10H10Cl8. It is derived from camphene, which is obtained synthetically from many essential oils.
- The main drawback in the field is its **detoxification time**. The authors argued this problem may be less serious than some investigators believed if the proper amounts are used.
- Earlier fish-toxicity work was hard to compare because test designs varied widely. Cope, Gjullin and Storm (1949) found rainbow trout disabled within 15 minutes at 8 p.p.m. toxaphene.
- Several analytical methods exist for toxaphene (infrared, pyridine-alkali reactions, chlorine extraction, sodium reduction), but none was then considered specific to toxaphene.

**Test waters** (all natural, obtained for the experiments):

| Source | pH | Total dissolved solids (p.p.m.) |
|---|---|---|
| Logan municipal supply, Utah | ~8.3 | 166 |
| Strawberry River, Wasatch County | ~7.8 | 238 |
| Blacks Fork, Summit County | ~7.0 | 46 |

**Toxaphene formulations:**
- **Floating type** (formulation 5622-1): mixes spontaneously with water; oil particles rise to the surface and decompose faster. Contains exactly 0.835 lb toxaphene per gallon.
- **Sinking type**: about 62.6% technical toxaphene, 6.0% NPX emulsifier and 31.4% kerosene carrier. It sinks to the bottom and can be applied by the same methods used for emulsified [[Rotenone]].

Fish were held about one week before tests (goldfish up to one month) and fed trout food until shortly before experiments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water conditions:** Dissolved oxygen, ammonia-nitrogen and carbon dioxide were measured during and after experiments and stayed within recognized tolerance limits. Test temperatures were 68–75 °F for goldfish and mosquitofish, 55 °F for trout, 47 °F for reduced-temperature goldfish, and 70–80 °F for the detoxification experiment.
- **Symptoms:** At mild concentrations (below 0.2 p.p.m.) fish lost balance gradually and could not move fins or navigate; death was marked by extended fins and closed gill covers, with internal rupturing noted in mosquitofish. At strong concentrations (above 3.0 p.p.m.) fish swam erratically, sank, bumped the aquarium walls and died soon after. Revival attempts kept poisoned fish alive for no more than 2–3 days.
- **Prior literature cited in the paper:** Earlier reports gave a wide range of values, such as a 9-hour LC100 of 0.1–1.0 p.p.m. for brown trout (Stock) and an LC100 of 0.01–0.005 p.p.m. for 1-inch rainbow and brown trout (Surber, 1948). Lawrence (1950) found 0.05 p.p.m. killed goldfish, largemouth bass and bluegill in aquaria but not in an earthen pond.
- **Field detoxification:** Two spring-fed ponds in [[Cache Valley]] (connected to the [[Bear River]] by an irrigation ditch) were treated at 0.1 p.p.m. Carp, [[Utah Chub]], [[Leatherside Chub]] and dace were drifting or dying within 24 hours, and all fish were dead after one week. About 30 days later, carp were spawning in the shallows, indicating recolonization from the river once toxaphene had broken down. At two weeks, these fish were still present.

## Key Findings

**Lethal concentrations (LC50, 95% confidence, p.p.m.)**

| Species | Water | Temp. (°F) | Sinking | Floating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goldfish | Logan | 68 | 0.009 | 0.004 |
| Goldfish | Logan | 47 | 0.044 | 0.026 |
| Goldfish | Strawberry | 68 | 0.016 | 0.028 |
| Goldfish | Blacks Fork | 68 | 0.009 | 0.007 |
| Mosquitofish | Logan | 68 | 0.048 | 0.024 |
| Mosquitofish | Strawberry | 68 | 0.006 (considered unexplained) | 0.052 |
| Mosquitofish | Blacks Fork | 68 | 0.009 | 0.009 |
| Rainbow trout | Logan | 55 | 0.023 | 0.028 |

**Statistical results**
- Mortality differed significantly among the three water types, attributed to water chemistry.
- Floating versus sinking toxaphene was significant at the 95% level.
- The fish × water interaction was significant at the 99% level; the authors attributed this to water chemistry rather than fish identity.
- At optimum temperatures, species differences were significant at the 99% level, while toxaphene type had no detectable effect.
- Temperature was significant at the 99% level for goldfish (47 vs. 68 °F).
- Strawberry water, with the highest dissolved solids, required the most toxin. Blacks Fork water, with the lowest, gave the lowest LC50 values.

**Detoxification**
- In the laboratory, 72 units (2 L of each water type with silt, sand or marl bottoms, floating and sinking toxaphene at 0.1 p.p.m., with broken and unbroken surface tension, in duplicate) reached almost 100% detoxification within 23 days at 70–80 °F. Earlier investigators had expected weeks or months.

**Practical conclusions**
- A treated lake may stay toxic longer than one treated with rotenone products, but total kill is more assured and costs less.
- Sinking toxaphene costs about one-fifth to one-sixth as much as the floating type, per active ingredient. The floating type would only be justified by a shorter detoxification time.
- Toxaphene can be applied with an ordinary water pump. In water deeper than about 10 feet, floating toxaphene should be introduced from the bottom.
- Toxaphene acts on the central nervous system and is non-irritating at lethal levels, so fish may accumulate a lethal dose without fleeing. This makes it promising for eradication in spring-fed ponds, unlike rotenone, which drives fish into springs for relief.
- Use in rivers and streams is very limited, because kill at low concentrations takes too long, and raising concentrations to kill fish in flowing water removes the economic advantage.
- Natural water movements such as the [[Fall Overturn]] are recommended to speed detoxification.
