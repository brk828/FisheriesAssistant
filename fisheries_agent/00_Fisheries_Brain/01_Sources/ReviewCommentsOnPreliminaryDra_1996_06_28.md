# Review Comments on Preliminary Draft Environmental Assessment for Red Shiner Eradication Project, Virgin River, Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology #conservation-management

## Executive Summary
This document is a formal scientific review letter authored by Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) critiquing a 1996 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) preliminary draft environmental assessment (EA) proposing eradication of the non-native [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) from the [[Virgin River]] in Utah using piscicide treatment. Rather than presenting novel morphological or ecological data, the letter functions as an expert critique identifying deficiencies in documentation, methodology, taxonomy, and impact assessment. Key concerns include inadequate literature support, absence of re-invasion prevention measures, insufficient treatment of problem habitats (e.g., Seegmiller Marsh), lack of provisions for scientific salvage collection, unaddressed genetic consequences of repeated piscicide treatments on native fish populations, undocumented impacts of diversion dam modifications, and several taxonomic/nomenclatural errors in the draft.

## Background & Description
No morphological data on target or non-target species are presented in this review letter. However, several taxonomic corrections relevant to species identification in the draft EA are noted:
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*lutrensis*) was reclassified from genus *Notropis* to genus **[[Cyprinella]]**.
- **Bullhead catfishes** are now correctly assigned to genus **[[Ameiurus]]**, not *Ictalurus*.
- Genus spelling correction: ***[[Lepomis]]***, not "Lepomos" (sunfishes, family Centrarchidae).
- **Astacidae** is a crayfish *family* (not a genus) and should not be italicized in scientific text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The letter addresses the aquatic habitat mosaic of the Virgin River drainage subject to piscicide treatment, including:
- **Mainstem river reaches** treated progressively downstream.
- **Associated habitats**: irrigation drains, ponds, marshes, washes, springs, and tributary inflows.
- **Seegmiller Marsh**: identified as a problem area with dense emergent macrophyte stands requiring aggressive treatment (cutting/herbicide application, burning, aerial piscicide application) to expose spring sources and water-retaining depressions that could harbor surviving target fish after drainage.
- **Washington Fields Diversion Dam** and **Johnson Diversion Dam**: structural modifications (rebuilding/raising) implicated in cessation of perennial flow and desiccation of river reaches immediately downstream, representing unassessed hydrological/habitat impacts.
- Non-native species potentially present in the watershed but omitted from the draft:
  - **Tiger guapote / Jaguar cichlid** (*Cichlasoma managuense*) — reported from Boiler Spring, a tributary to the Virgin River.
  - **Grass carp / White Amur** (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) — possible watershed stockings.
- Potential parasite concern: **Asian tapeworm** — risk of introduction/spread via transplanted or transferred fish stocks moved between treatment areas or from federal hatchery facilities to treatment reaches.

## Key Findings
- **Documentation deficiency**: The draft EA lacks sufficient citation of peer-reviewed literature to substantiate factual claims; reliance on unreviewed agency reports undermines scientific credibility.
- **Re-invasion risk unaddressed**: No mechanism proposed to prevent non-native fish re-invasion post-treatment; barrier dams only block unassisted upstream movement and do not address other introduction pathways (e.g., human-mediated transport/release). Historical renovation projects have consistently failed on this point.
- **Regulatory recommendation**: Suggests local/county/state/federal ordinances prohibiting possession, transport, or release of non-native species (e.g., [[Red Shiner]]) within the Virgin River drainage, paired with public information/education programs.
- **Inadequate marsh treatment protocol**: Seegmiller Marsh treatment plan needs vegetation removal (cutting, herbicide, burning) prior to piscicide application to improve aerial detection of refugia and piscicide penetration; recommends repeated aerial and ground piscicide applications.
- **No scientific salvage provision**: Draft fails to arrange for scientific collection of fishes, aquatic invertebrates, and amphibians during salvage, poisoning, and post-poisoning phases; recommends USFWS facilitate collecting permits for interested researchers.
- **Genetic impact concern**: No assessment of genetic consequences to native fish populations from piscicide treatment. Cites existing quantitative evidence that prior red shiner eradication attempts in the Virgin River mainstem measurably reduced genetic variability in native fish populations — a critical omission requiring expert consultation and species-by-species evaluation.
- **Undocumented infrastructure impacts**: Renovation of Washington Fields diversion dam and repair/raising of Johnson diversion dam lack documentation of dates, engineering specifications, and permitting authority (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, EPA, USFWS); both projects caused loss of perennial flow and habitat desiccation downstream.
- **Insufficient treatment-area specificity**: Draft does not adequately define treatment reach boundaries, sequencing, or problem areas despite stating treatment will proceed through "progressive downstream reaches" over a multi-year project. Recommends detailed maps specifying location, extent, ownership, land use, discharge, and biota for each treatment area before implementation.
- **Chemical treatment query**: Raises unresolved technical question regarding whether potassium permanganate chemically degrades rhodamine dye, implying uncertainty in proposed neutralization/tracking methodology for piscicide application.
- **Nomenclatural/taxonomic errors** flagged throughout draft: genus assignments for red shiner ([[Cyprinella]]) and bullhead catfishes ([[Ameiurus]]), spelling of *Lepomis*, and correction of Astacidae as a family rather than genus.
- **Administrative/personnel errors**: Corrections requested for misspelled names and appropriate use of titles for team members. Questions raised about whether all addressees are formally part of the Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team.
- **Distribution notes for additional non-natives**: Tiger guapote (*Cichlasoma managuense*) reported from Boiler Spring; possible grass carp (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) stocking in watershed — neither addressed in draft despite relevance to eradication planning.
