## Executive Summary
This 1957 paper by H.S. Swingle reports preliminary results from a 1955–56 experiment at Auburn, Alabama, evaluating the commercial viability of raising [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) in fertilized, fed ponds. Two 0.1-acre ponds were stocked at 1,000 and 2,000 fingerlings per acre and reared for 11 months under a fertilization and supplemental feeding regime. The study demonstrated high survival rates (95–98%) and substantial per-acre production (773–1,242 lbs/acre), supporting the conclusion that channel catfish were a promising candidate for commercial [[Pond Aquaculture]]. The paper also provides early economic data on dressing yields, labor costs, and profit margins, though it notes that affordable fingerling supply remained a limiting factor for broader commercial adoption.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Ictalurus punctatus* (Channel Catfish), family [[Ictaluridae]].
- **Dressing/processing yield data** (n = 293 fish):
  - Live weight: 201.5 lbs
  - Dressed weight (head, skin, viscera removed): 121.5 lbs (**60.3%** dress-out rate)
  - Total loss: 80.0 lbs
- **Proportional loss composition** (% of live weight):
  - Head: 23.6%
  - Skin: 4.2%
  - Viscera: 9.0%
  - Body fluids: 2.8%
- No detailed external morphological descriptors (fin counts, coloration, etc.) are provided; the paper is production-focused rather than taxonomic.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Small commercial fertilized/fed [[Freshwater Pond]] systems (0.1-acre experimental ponds), representative of pond-based [[Aquaculture]] rather than natural riverine or lake habitats.
- **Trophic ecology**: Fish were sustained via a combination of pond [[Fertilization]] (8-8-2 fertilizer, 100 lbs/acre per application) to stimulate natural productivity, and supplemental feeding with a formulated diet ("Auburn No. 1 fish feed": 35% soybean oil meal, 35% peanut oil meal, 15% fish meal, 15% distillers dried solubles).
- **Stocking density effects**: Comparison of two densities (1,000 vs. 2,000 fish/acre) reveals density-dependent production scaling with only modest survival differences, suggesting the species tolerates moderate-to-high stocking densities in pond conditions.
- **Health management**: Pre-stocking prophylactic treatments (potassium permanganate, formalin, acriflavine) and anti-parasitic treatment (dibutyl tin oxide for intestinal parasites, referencing early [[Fish Disease Management]] practices) were used to prepare fingerlings, indicating parasite and pathogen pressures were a known concern in pond-reared stock.
- **Life stage**: Study used fingerlings sourced from Osage Springs Minnow Farm (Rogers, Arkansas), reared to marketable size over an 11-month grow-out period (October 1955–August 1956).

## Key Findings
- **Stocking & Survival**:
  | Stocking Rate (per acre) | Number Stocked | Number Recovered | Survival (%) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1,000 | 1,000 | 950 | 95.0% |
  | 2,000 | 2,000 | 1,960 | 98.0% |

- **Production Yields**:
  | Stocking Rate | Pounds Stocked | Pounds Recovered | Net Production/Acre | Cost/lb (¢) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1,000/acre | 60.0 lbs | 773 lbs | 773 lbs | 12.7¢ |
  | 2,000/acre | 120.0 lbs | 1,242 lbs | 1,242 lbs | 15.0¢ |

- Doubling stocking density (1,000 → 2,000/acre) increased net production by ~61% (773 → 1,242 lbs/acre), though at a higher per-pound production cost (12.7¢ vs. 15.0¢), indicating diminishing efficiency at higher densities — a foundational data point for [[Stocking Density Optimization]] in catfish farming.
- **Feed inputs** scaled with stocking density: total feed applied was 1,765 lbs/acre (1,000 fish) vs. 3,165 lbs/acre (2,000 fish) over the production period.
- **Economic analysis**:
  - Live-weight production cost: 15¢/lb
  - Dressed-weight cost (accounting for 60.3% yield): 24.9¢/lb
  - Labor cost for dressing: 6.3¢/lb (~3.3 man-minutes/fish; 18 fish/man-hour at $0.50/hr)
  - Total cost: 31.2¢/lb dressed fish
  - Sale price: 50¢/lb → **Profit margin: 18.8¢/lb** above feed/fertilizer costs
  - Estimated return per acre (2,000 fish stocking): **$140.79**, before accounting for fingerling procurement and other labor/capital costs.
- **Conclusion**: Channel catfish showed strong promise as a commercial pond species due to high survival and production, but the study explicitly flags **fingerling availability and cost** as the primary bottleneck limiting commercial-scale adoption — an early articulation of the [[Hatchery Bottleneck]] problem in aquaculture development.
- This work is positioned as a follow-up/expansion of Swingle's earlier 1954 study on commercial fish production in ponds, and contrasts with earlier fertilization-only trials yielding only 71–92 lbs/acre, versus up to 238.6 lbs/acre with soybean cake feeding — underscoring the substantial yield gains from the improved [[Formulated Fish Feed]] regime used in this study.
