# Redrock Canyon Renovation Project: Decision Notice, FONSI and Final Environmental Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the Decision Notice and Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) for the Redrock Canyon Renovation Project, together with the Final Environmental Assessment (EA) that supports it. The Coronado National Forest Supervisor approved, on November 5, 2010, the Proposed Action alternative. It authorizes the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) to use [[Piscicide]] treatments (rotenone and/or antimycin-A) to eradicate non-native fish and bullfrogs from the Redrock Canyon watershed, plus two earthen stock tanks in Meadow Valley. The project aims to recover the federally endangered [[Gila Topminnow]], the threatened [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] and the endangered [[Sonora Tiger Salamander]], and to restore a native fish community. The EA considered two alternatives: the Proposed Action and [[No Action]]. A fish barrier and stock tank breaching were analyzed and then eliminated.

The decision was subject to a 45-day appeal under 36 CFR 215, and the Forest Service finding is that no environmental impact statement is needed.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** The project is in the Redrock Canyon watershed, about 20,000 acres, mostly National Forest land, with two private inholdings. Elevations range from about 5,900 ft at the Canelo Hills crest to 4,075 ft near the canyon mouth. Redrock Canyon is a major headwater tributary of [[Sonoita Creek]], comprising about 15 percent of the drainage area above the Sonoita Creek gauge.

**Native aquatic fauna.** Four native fish are known from the canyon: longfin dace, speckled dace, desert sucker and Gila topminnow. Native amphibians include the Chiricahua leopard frog and Sonora tiger salamander. Native reptiles include the Sonora mud turtle, black-necked gartersnake and Northern Mexican gartersnake.

**Non-native species.** Five non-native fish and the American bullfrog are established. Mosquitofish and bullfrogs are widespread. Largemouth bass, green sunfish and bluegill have declined and may now be absent. The document identifies predation on early life stages as the main mechanism by which non-natives suppress natives, and notes that Gila topminnow has been "all but eliminated" from the canyon.

**Piscicides.** Rotenone is a naturally occurring compound from legumes, used in U.S. fisheries since the 1930s. Antimycin-A is an organic compound from *Streptomyces*, used since 1964. Both block cellular respiration in gill-breathing animals. Recommended doses are 0.005 to 0.250 mg/L for rotenone and 5 to 25 µg/L for antimycin-A.

**Project history.** A related 2007–2008 project, Native Fish Restoration in Redrock Canyon, included a fish barrier on Forest land. The Forest Supervisor withdrew the 2008 decision on October 14, 2008, citing new information on human health and groundwater. The Proposed Action was listed on the Forest schedule of proposed actions on April 1, 2009. The revised EA was released for a 30-day comment period from February 2, 2010 to March 8, 2010. Twelve respondents commented. Public comments were judged to have resulted in minor changes and did not identify substantive issues requiring new alternatives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Drought and habitat.** The drought that began in 1995 substantially reduced surface water. Drought concentrates non-native fish and bullfrogs into small pools, which the document describes as favorable conditions for removal.

**Water budget.** An average year intercepts about 3 percent of surface flow with stock ponds and spring developments. The driest year on record (1996) was modeled as a worst case, giving about 6 percent of surface flow intercepted.

**Water quality.** ADEQ classifies uses as aquatic life, warm water, full body contact and fish consumption. The 2004 assessment found the surface water in attainment for all designated uses.

**Groundwater.** About 45 registered wells lie within the watershed. Most are on private land or more than 0.5 miles from the Forest boundary. The analysis concludes that contamination of domestic wells is unlikely. Rotenone moves about 2 cm in most soils.

**Impacts on native fauna.** The EA predicts mortality among gill-breathing non-target species. Sonora mud turtles could be affected by both piscicides. Tadpoles and zooplankton are sensitive. Adult frogs, gartersnakes and birds are predicted to be largely unaffected. Aquatic invertebrates are expected to recover within about two years, based on the cited literature.

**Cultural and social context.** The project is within Management Area 7 (Riparian) of the Coronado Forest Plan. Four livestock allotments overlap the watershed. The area is lightly used by the public. Patagonia and nearby residents were concerned about piscicide safety.

**Consultation.** The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service issued a Biological Opinion on June 13, 2008. It concluded that the project "may affect, likely to adversely affect" Gila topminnow, Chiricahua leopard frog and Sonora tiger salamander, but that the benefits outweigh a small loss of individuals. Forest-sensitive species are unlikely to trend toward listing.

## Key Findings

- **Decision:** The Forest Supervisor authorized AGFD to use rotenone and/or antimycin-A on all surface waters in Redrock Canyon and on two stock tanks in Meadow Valley. Field work is limited to the low-flow period, typically May–June.
- **Native species salvage:** Gila topminnow is **not** salvaged, because it closely resembles mosquitofish and sorting would increase the risk of project failure. Other natives are salvaged and held.
- **Treatment protocol:** Two piscicide applications are planned, about two weeks apart. Further applications may be needed for up to four years. Neutralization with potassium or sodium permanganate is available.
- **Restoration:** Gila topminnow is restocked from captive populations first. Up to three more natives (longfin dace, desert sucker, speckled dace, Chiricahua leopard frog, Sonora tiger salamander) may be repatriated later, after evaluation.
- **Fish barrier:** Eliminated. The CAP Technical Committee preferred a barrier on Sonoita Creek. AGFD and the Forest Service did not support one in Redrock Canyon.
- **Stock tanks:** Not breached. The watershed contains 15 earthen tanks and two concrete dams, and tanks are needed for livestock distribution and as habitat for listed species.
- **Human health:** The EA concludes that exposure risk is very low at label concentrations. Mitigation includes bilingual signage, temporary closure orders and PPE for applicators.
- **Regulatory compliance:** ESA Section 7 consultation is complete. The project is consistent with the Forest Plan. An AZPDES permit is required for treatments after April 2011, following the Sixth Circuit ruling on NPDES exemptions for pesticides.
- **Significance:** The Forest Supervisor found effects "short term, localized, minor and predictable" and not highly controversial. The finding is that no environmental impact statement is needed.
- **Appeal:** A 45-day appeal period follows publication of the legal notice in the *Arizona Daily Star*, with the expected date of publication November 15, 2010. The publication date alone governs timing.

**Principal public concerns recorded in the EA:**

- The EA responds to a 2010 comment from [[Paul C. Marsh]] (Marsh & Associates) urging that the barrier be reinstated, that all five native fish be explicitly included, and that recreational angling be closed. The Forest Service responded that these are outside its jurisdiction or are already covered.
- Opposition from a public comment that the pesticides are not registered in Arizona. The response is that the project involves application only, not distribution.
