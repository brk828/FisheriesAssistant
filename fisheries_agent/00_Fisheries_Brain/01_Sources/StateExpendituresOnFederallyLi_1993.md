# State Expenditures on Federally Listed Threatened and Endangered Species

## Executive Summary
This 1993 report by the International Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies ([[IAFWA]]) presents the third annual Threatened and Endangered Species (T/E) State Expenditure Report, mandated under a 1988 amendment to the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It quantifies non-federal (state-level) spending on the conservation of federally listed T/E species. Of the $131 million total reported for FY 1991/1992 across 633 listed species, fish received only **$1,239,050 (0.9%)** — one of the lowest-funded taxonomic groups, dwarfed by expenditures on birds ($73.7 million, dominated by the [[Red-Cockaded Woodpecker]]) and mammals ($39.3 million).

## Background & Description
- The report was compiled pursuant to a Congressional mandate requiring states receiving Section 6 grants under the Endangered Species Act to report "reasonably identifiable expenditures" made for the conservation of federally listed endangered and threatened species.
- At the time of reporting, **775 federally listed T/E species** existed in the U.S., though the FY 1991/1992 expenditure data covered the then-current **633 listed species**.
- Total non-federal expenditures reported: **$131 million**, more than double the prior year's **$66.6 million**, attributed partly to more thorough reporting (e.g., new outreach to state departments of transportation) and anticipatory budget requests.
- Several states (Hawaii, Louisiana, Texas, Wisconsin) reported gross budget estimates divided evenly across all listed species within the state rather than itemized per-species figures, introducing a methodological caveat to cross-taxon comparisons.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Expenditure allocation by taxonomic group (FY 1991/1992), as shown in the report's funding breakdown:
  - Birds: $73,672,459 (~53% of total; dominated by land acquisition for the [[Red-Cockaded Woodpecker]] in Florida, accounting for 89% of bird-related spending)
  - Mammals: $39,265,361
  - Reptiles & Amphibians: $13,441,481 (reptiles ~10%; amphibians ~0.02%)
  - Invertebrates: $2,287,559 (1.7%)
  - Plants: $1,428,422
  - **Fish: $1,239,050 (0.9%)**
- This distribution highlights a strong funding skew toward charismatic terrestrial and avian species, with **aquatic vertebrates (fish) receiving disproportionately minimal state conservation investment** relative to the number of federally listed fish species.
- The disparity raises questions relevant to [[Conservation Funding Equity]] and the adequacy of Section 6 state grant allocations for freshwater and aquatic ecosystem stewardship compared to terrestrial habitat acquisition programs.

## Key Findings
- **Total fish-specific expenditure**: $1,239,050, representing just 0.9% of the $131 million total non-federal T/E spending for FY 1991/1992.
- Fish ranked among the **lowest-funded taxonomic groups**, alongside amphibians (0.02%) and invertebrates (1.7%), despite fish being a substantial component of the 633 total listed species.
- The overall funding increase (from $66.6M to $131M year-over-year) was largely **not** driven by fish conservation but by land acquisition spending for a single bird species (the [[Red-Cockaded Woodpecker]]), underscoring that increases in aggregate T/E funding do not necessarily translate to proportional gains for aquatic/fish conservation.
- The report's methodology (per-species reporting vs. evenly divided gross budgets in states like Hawaii, Louisiana, Texas, and Wisconsin) may introduce inconsistencies in group-level totals, including for fish, warranting caution when using this data for cross-state or cross-taxon funding comparisons.
- No individual fish species is named or singled out in this excerpt as receiving disproportionate funding, in contrast to birds, where one species accounted for the vast majority of group expenditures — suggesting a more distributed (or under-documented) pattern of state spending across listed fish species.
