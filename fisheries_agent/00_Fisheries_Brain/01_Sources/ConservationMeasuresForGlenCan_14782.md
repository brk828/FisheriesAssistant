# Glen Canyon Dam Conservation Measures for the Humpback Chub

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989–1990 interagency package from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Upper Colorado Region) and the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) on seven conservation measures for the operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The measures are attached to the Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (consultation no. 2-21-87-F-23, BR-AZ). They cover fish taxonomy, hatchery stock maintenance, flood release probability, a [[Little Colorado River]] management plan, impact research, long-term monitoring, and an attempt to establish a second spawning aggregation of [[Humpback Chub]]. The package totals $1,580,000 and is linked to the [[Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS) and the eventual [[Biological Opinion]].

## Background & Description

The humpback chub (*Gila cypha*) is listed as endangered. The Glen Canyon package also treats related native fishes of the Colorado River: [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The document describes the humpback chub as a fish that congregates seasonally at the confluence of the Little Colorado River and the mainstem Colorado River. Fish move between the tributary and mainstem, and the Little Colorado River is needed for spawning because mainstem water is too cold (Kaeding and Zimmerman, 1983).

Key concerns in the document:
- **Taxonomy:** The genetic and taxonomic relationships within *Gila* are disputed. Stocking and refugia decisions depend on them.
- **Isolation:** The Little Colorado River aggregation is isolated from other humpback chub stocks and has apparent phenotypic uniqueness.
- **Flow:** Flood releases above powerplant capacity have mixed effects. They add staging and rearing habitat at the Little Colorado River mouth but are detrimental to downstream resources overall.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use:** Backwaters, side channels, and cobble bars in the mainstem and the Little Colorado River are considered critical for young fish. Backwaters are few, widely spaced, and strongly affected by fluctuating flows.
- **Essential habitat:** The Little Colorado River habitat of primary importance runs from its mouth to Blue Springs, about 14 miles. Upstream land and water management affects this reach.
- **Reproduction:** Spawning depends on temperature and other environmental cues in the Little Colorado River and confluence area. The document asks how dam operation alters these cues.
- **Threats named:** Dam operations, non-native fish competition and predation, parasitism, habitat change, oil and hazardous material spills in the Little Colorado River basin, and angling injuries.
- **Flow regime:** Reclamation's 1988 criteria aim to reduce the probability of bypass releases to less than one in twenty years and to limit powerplant releases to less than 31,500 cubic feet per second (cfs). The document states that the probability was previously about one in four and that the justification for the change had not been fully explained to the public.

### Measure Details

- **Measure 1 (Taxonomy):** Goals include genetic relationships among *Gila* populations (mitochondrial DNA, electrophoresis), field characters for discrimination, low-risk genetic sampling, and comparison of wild and hatchery stocks. Expected products include an accurate taxonomy, identification of hybrids, and a genetic baseline. Initial literature review was due October 1, 1989, and a work plan was due March 30, 1990. A three- or four-year program was anticipated beginning FY 1991.
- **Measure 2 (Hatchery stocks):** A basin-wide propagation and genetic management plan covers four species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. It also covers the Little Colorado River humpback chub and evaluates the need for a separate Grand Canyon refugium. Budget: FY 1990 $215,000; FY 1991 $10,000; FY 1992 $10,000. The stated total of $35,000 does not match this breakdown. Costs will rise if humpback chub are placed in a hatchery.
- **Measure 3 (Flood releases):** Reclamation will build a Monte Carlo simulation of historic forecasts and runoff. Variables include the January 1 and July 31 Lake Powell storage targets and monthly release patterns. Budget: FY 1990 $5,000; FY 1991 $10,000; FY 1992 $5,000. A draft report is due by the end of FY 1990 and the final report by the end of FY 1992.
- **Measure 4 (Little Colorado River plan):** Six objectives include an ad hoc basin management group, a consolidated data base, identification of threats and management actions, inter-agency agreements under ESA Sections 2 and 7, and a spill contingency plan for the Region IX Mainland Regional Response Team (RRT) plan. Tasks run 42 months overall. Budget: FY 1989 $5,000; FY 1990 $30,000; FY 1991 $15,000; FY 1992 $15,000.
- **Measure 5 (Impact research):** Objectives include a literature review (complete by December 1, 1989, with Arizona Game and Fish), habitat use, food limitation, reproductive cues, early-life survival, distribution and movement, biotic interactions and parasitism, and a life-history population model. Methods include electrofishing with safeguards, non-entanglement nets (hoop nets, minnow traps, seines), and gill and trammel nets checked every 2–3 hours. Budget: FY 1989 pilot $45,000; FY 1990 $150,000 (LCR) plus $250,000 (mainstem); FY 1991 and FY 1992 $400,000 each. Total $1,245,000. Annual reports are due January 30 each year, and final reports 180 days after research ends.
- **Measure 6 (Monitoring):** A monitoring program will be developed to track population and habitat responses to operations. No new monitoring should start until GCES Phase II research ends. A draft plan is due in FY 1992, with implementation in FY 1993. Base funding is $50,000 each for FY 1993 and FY 1994, with outyears unknown.
- **Measure 7 (Second aggregation):** Surveys of tributaries with confluence-area collections (including Bright Angel, Shinumo, Kanab, Havasu, and others) would precede any augmentation or introduction. Budget: FY 1990 $60,000 (baseline survey); FY 1991 $45,000 (follow-up). The measure will be dropped if the species cannot establish itself after reasonable reintroduction efforts.

### Agency Positions and Decisions

- Reclamation notified FWS on December 13, 1989, that it would proceed with the conservation measures so data collection could start. Reclamation would lead the Biological Assessment, expected by April 1991, and then request a Biological Opinion for the EIS.
- In a February 16, 1990 memo, FWS concurred with Measures 1–7 but raised a conflict with Measure 5. Reclamation viewed Measure 5 as a 4–5 year program, while GCES needs short-term (14 months or less) hypothesis-testing research using controlled flows. FWS recommended revising the Request for Proposal to allow controlled flows as an experimental variable.
- FWS said the existing consultation could be ended by replacing the proposed action with the EIS preferred alternative. It did not request a new biological assessment, noting that a draft jeopardy biological opinion already existed for this consultation.
