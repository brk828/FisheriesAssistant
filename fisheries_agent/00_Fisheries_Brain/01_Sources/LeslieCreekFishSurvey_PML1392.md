# Leslie Creek Fish Survey, Cochise County, Arizona (August 1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A single-day fish survey of [[Leslie Creek]] in [[Cochise County]], Arizona, was conducted on August 7, 1997, by researchers from [[Arizona State University]]. Fish were collected only downstream of a railroad track and boulder dam, where three seine hauls yielded 8 [[Gila purpurea]] and 8 [[Agosia]] (identified in the field notes as [[Longfin Dace]]). No fish were recorded at two other sites: upstream of the railroad track and boulder dam, and at a road crossing. All longfin dace collected were infected with a "black spot" parasite.

## Background & Description

The document is a brief field record rather than a full research report. It lists the survey date, location, participants, sampling method, catch by site, and one field observation. The participants were Brian Bagley, Patti Sowko, and Travis Tuchak, all affiliated with Arizona State University. The record does not include water chemistry, habitat measurements, or body size data.

The two species recorded are:

- **[[Gila purpurea]]:** 8 individuals collected below the railroad track/boulder dam.
- **[[Agosia]] (Longfin Dace):** 8 individuals collected below the railroad track/boulder dam. All of these were infected with the "black spot" parasite.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey was spatially limited. All fish came from one reach, immediately downstream of a [[Railroad Track and Boulder Dam]] structure. The upstream reach above the same barrier and the reach at the road crossing both yielded no fish. The record does not explain this pattern. The barrier may be relevant, but the document does not test or state that relationship.

The "black spot" infection is recorded as a field observation. The document does not identify the parasite taxon, its prevalence beyond the statement that all longfin dace were infected, or its effect on host condition. Black spot disease is a commonly reported trematode condition in fishes, but the document itself does not confirm that identification.

## Key Findings

- **Fish occurred only below the barrier:** Three seine hauls below the railroad track/boulder dam produced 8 *Gila purpurea* and 8 *Agosia*.
- **No fish upstream or at the road crossing:** The sites above the railroad track/boulder dam and at the road crossing produced no fish.
- **Universal parasite infection in longfin dace:** All *Agosia* (longfin dace) collected were infected with "black spot" parasite. The document reports no uninfected individuals, so the infection status of *Gila purpurea* is not recorded.
- **Limited sampling effort:** The survey covered one date and four sampling locations, with three seine hauls at the only productive site. The sample sizes are small, so the results describe only this survey.
