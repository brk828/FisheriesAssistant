# Parasites of Selected Fishes of the Southwestern United States that are Endangered, Threatened, or of Special Concern

#ichthyology #marine-biology #parasitology #cyprinidae #salmonidae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a **research grant proposal** (submitted February 1980 by E. James Landers, Department of Zoology, Arizona State University) rather than a completed empirical study. It outlines a plan to systematically survey the parasite fauna of 21 fish species/subspecies from the Southwestern United States that were listed as [[Endangered Species]], [[Threatened Species]], or [[Species of Special Concern]] in [[Deacon et al. 1979 Fish Status List|Deacon et al. (1979)]]. The rationale is to document host-parasite relationships and broader community ecology **before potential extinction** of these imperiled taxa. The proposal requests $10,456.00 to fund an 8-month field and laboratory effort (Oct 1980–May 1981), employing research assistant Mbida Mpoame, with cooperation from the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, NM).

## Background & Description

No original morphological data on the fish hosts themselves is presented in this proposal (it is a funding narrative, not a results paper). However, morphological/taxonomic identification is referenced for **parasite** taxa already recovered or targeted:

- **[[Crepidostomum farionis]]** — a digenetic trematode identified by Mpoame (1979) from both [[Arizona Trout]] (*Salmo apache*) and Arizona *S. gairdneri* ([[Rainbow Trout]]).
- **[[Gyrodactylus cyprinodontis]]**, **[[Gyrodactylus nevadensis]]**, and **[[Gyrodactylus saratogensis]]** — three monogenetic trematode species described by Mizelle and Kritsky (1967) from the skin of the Saratoga Nevada pupfish, *Cyprinodon nevadensis nevadensis*.

Planned parasitological screening categories (methodological, not descriptive of host morphology):
- **Ectoparasites:** protozoans (e.g., *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*), monogenetic trematodes (adult *Gyrodactylus* sp.), metacercariae of digenetic trematodes, and crustaceans (*Argulus* sp., *Lernaea* sp.)
- **Endoparasites:** larval and adult digenetic trematodes, cestodes, nematodes, and acanthocephalans

Proposed laboratory techniques include fixation, staining, and mounting of specimens, followed by photomacrography, photomicrography, and bright-field microscopy to produce measured illustrative drawings of recovered parasites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal targets host fishes distributed across distinct freshwater basins of the Southwestern US, reflecting a range of lotic, spring, and hatchery-refuge habitats:

- **[[Colorado River Basin]]**: *Gila cypha*, *G. elegans*, *G. robusta grahami*, *Ptychocheilus lucius*, *Xyrauchen texanus*, *Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*, *L. vittata*, *Meda fulgida*, *Tiaroga cobitis*
- **[[Gila River Basin]]**: *Gila intermedia*, *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* (Gila topminnow)
- **Isolated desert springs/closed basins**: *Cyprinodon macularius macularius* (Gila/Sonoran desert pupfish), *Cyprinodon macularius* ssp. (Quitobaquito form), maintained "from local stocks or in nature"
- **[[Pecos River]] / Trans-Pecos springs (Texas/New Mexico)**: *Cyprinodon bovinus*, *Cyprinodon elegans*, *Gambusia gaigei*, *Gambusia nobilis*, *Gambusia amistadensis* — several maintained ex situ at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]
- **High-elevation coldwater streams (Apache/Gila trout range)**: *Salmo apache*, *Salmo gilae*

Host-parasite ecological interest centers on:
- Shared trematode infections between native [[Arizona Trout]] and introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] (*S. gairdneri*), suggesting potential cross-species parasite transmission in sympatric salmonid populations.
- Reliance on captive/hatchery refuge populations (Dexter, Willow Beach) as sources for parasitological sampling of species now rare or extirpated in the wild, raising questions about how captivity may alter parasite community composition relative to wild populations.
- The stated goal of relating fish parasites to "other inhabitants of communities in which these fish of special concern occur," framing parasites as an ecological indicator of habitat and community integrity.

## Key Findings

- Prior to this proposal, **no parasite records existed** for *Salmo gilae* (Gila trout) in the authors' literature search, highlighting a significant knowledge gap for this endangered salmonid.
- *Crepidostomum farionis* was confirmed in both *Salmo apache* and Arizona populations of *S. gairdneri*, the first documented digenean record for Arizona trout.
- Three *Gyrodactylus* species (*G. cyprinodontis*, *G. nevadensis*, *G. saratogensis*) were previously described from *Cyprinodon nevadensis nevadensis*, representing the only pupfish monogenean records cited.
- Ongoing cooperative work with [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] had already begun identifying parasites of the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), per a 1979 progress report (Landers and Mpoame).
- The proposal identifies **21 target fish species/subspecies** across 5 families (Salmonidae, Cyprinidae, Catostomidae, Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae) as the full scope of the intended survey (see Appendix A list).
- Literature search relied on the *Index-Catalogue of Medical and Veterinary Zoology* (from Supplement 15 onward) and planned a computerized search of *Biological Abstracts* to fill remaining knowledge gaps — underscoring that, as of 1980, parasite data for most listed endangered/threatened southwestern fishes were largely absent from the literature.
- Budget and logistics (~$10,456 total) reflect a modest field-and-hatchery sampling design: 8 field trips within Arizona/New Mexico, one trip each to Dexter, NM and Willow Beach, AZ hatcheries.
