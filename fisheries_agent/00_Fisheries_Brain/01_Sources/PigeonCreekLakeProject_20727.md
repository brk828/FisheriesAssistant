# Pigeon Creek Lake Project: Status Correspondence and Water Rights Impasse (1992-1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #water-rights

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of agency and county correspondence (1992-1993) on the proposed [[Pigeon Creek Lake]] on the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]]. The main obstacle was [[Water Rights Severance and Transfer]]. Under [[Arizona Revised Statutes 45-172]], downstream irrigation districts hold an effective veto, and the [[Gila Valley Irrigation District]] opposed the transfer. A County-proposed workaround (transferring the rights to [[Greenlee County]]) was also rejected. In May 1993 District Ranger Frank A. Hayes recommended against further spending on project analysis. The letters also cover an [[Environmental Impact Statement]] cost estimate of $200,000-$400,000 and a related water-rights issue involving [[Phelps Dodge]] and the [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]].

## Background & Description

- **Project:** A proposed lake on Pigeon Creek, with a water-based recreation facility as its main goal. The Forest Service originally proposed it, and Greenlee County sponsored it.
- **County contribution:** The County provided $25,000 and managed an engineering feasibility study. The study gave the Forest Hydrologist data on potential downstream flow impacts if the dam were built.
- **Stakeholder meetings:** In December 1991 the Forest Service met with the Gila Valley, Duncan, and San Carlos Irrigation Districts, the [[Gila River Indian Tribe]], and the Bureau of Indian Affairs.
- **Statutory process:** In March 1992 the Forest Service sent a severance and transfer proposal to downstream users under [[Arizona Revised Statutes 45-172]]. Any district rejection, "regardless of merit," ends the procedure.
- **Attempted alternatives:** The County proposed on June 5, 1992 that the water be transferred to Greenlee County. The Forest Service declined on June 25, 1992. It preferred the rights stay in its own name, citing [[Forest Service Water Rights Policy]] and the [[Organic Act]] and [[Multiple Use Sustained Yield Act]]. The Gila Valley Irrigation District's president said the holder of the rights was not material to the denial.
- **Legal challenge:** On April 12, 1992 the Forest questioned the fairness of the statute giving districts veto power. A request for a legal opinion from the [[Office of the General Counsel]] received no formal response.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letters name the following environmental concerns raised during scoping:

- Water quality
- Loss of [[Riparian Vegetation]]
- Direct impacts to stream flow
- Impacts to native fish populations, especially candidate or federally listed species
- Likely introduction of non-native fish species
- Soil disturbance from road relocation
- Increased human activity not analyzed in the [[Forest Plan]]
- Loss of area solitude and overuse of the [[Blue Range Primitive Area]]
- Consistency with Forest Plan direction
- Highway safety along Highways 191 and 666

Under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]], these issues had to be resolved or mitigated. The letters do not report any fish survey, population count, or water-quality measurement. They state that analysis and discussions with the irrigation districts "did not reveal any quantifiable or significant effect downstream."

The [[Phelps Dodge]] correspondence addresses groundwater and surface flow:
- A [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] Memorandum of Understanding allows groundwater production of up to 30,000 acre-feet per year over a 100-square-mile area, while current pumping is far lower.
- Item 10 of that MOU requires bypass flows to replace reduced [[Eagle Creek]] flow if Phelps Dodge pumping is shown to reduce it.
- The Forest objected to an agreement not to contest the flow of about 11,800 acre-feet per year from New Mexico down the [[Gila River]] for later diversion, because the diversion points and amounts were unknown.

## Key Findings

1. **Water rights were the controlling constraint.** The issue should have been resolved before the County and Forest committed time and money. Without transfer approval, the project could not proceed.
2. **Downstream veto.** Under the state statute, the Gila Valley Irrigation District's written opposition effectively blocked the transfer. The County's alternative did not change the outcome.
3. **Cost of analysis.** The estimated [[Environmental Impact Statement]] cost was $200,000-$400,000. For comparison, Forest Service timber-sale EISs ran $25,000-$100,000, and a San Carlos Irrigation District EIS was about $25,000.
4. **Recommendation.** On the District Ranger's recommendation, continued expenditure on analysis was not supported. The goal of a water-based recreation facility was still described as worthwhile, and the project could be pursued by the County outside Forest lands.
5. **Low priority.** The project was not a high priority in the recent Rural Development visioning exercise.
6. **Litigation risk.** Litigation was considered likely, especially from tribes, if water rights stayed unresolved. Any state or federal funding would also require resolving the water-rights issue.
7. **Pending items.** The Forest will continue to seek a legal opinion on the state statute, but this is not a current priority. The Forest also had no formal OGC response on the Phelps Dodge settlement questions.
