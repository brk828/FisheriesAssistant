# Southwest Strategy: Interagency Collaboration on Natural Resource Management in Arizona and New Mexico

#natural-resource-management #interagency-collaboration #southwest-strategy

## Executive Summary

This document is a policy package rather than research. It contains a November 12, 1997 memorandum from the Secretaries of the Interior and Agriculture and the Deputy Under Secretary of Defense, and a December 16, 1997 briefing paper. Together they direct federal agencies to build a collaborative [[Southwest Strategy]] for natural resource conservation and management on federal lands in [[Arizona]] and [[New Mexico]]. The effort is driven by growing management complexity, court injunctions, agency backlogs, and a [[House Appropriations Committee]] directive to avoid shutdowns of activities on public lands tied to an endangered species. The memo sets staged deadlines for priority issue lists, agency collaboration proposals, stakeholder engagement, and a full strategy. The documents do not name any fish or other organisms, habitats, or measured ecological data.

## Background & Description

**Origin and authority.** The memorandum was addressed to agency and regional directors and signed by [[Bruce Babbitt]] (Secretary of the Interior), [[Dan Glickman]] (Secretary of Agriculture), and the Deputy Under Secretary of Defense. It followed a meeting in Phoenix on natural resources and endangered species issues in the Southwest.

**Stated principles.** The strategy must:
- respect individual agency mandates and professional perspectives;
- include private sector, non-governmental organizations, and state, local, and tribal governments;
- maintain and restore the region's cultural, economic, and environmental quality of life;
- be scientifically based, legally defensible, and implementable within current budget and staff constraints;
- be developed at field and regional levels with national-office support and oversight.

**Participating agencies.** The collaborative effort involves the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Forest Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[National Park Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[US Geological Survey]], [[Natural Resources Conservation Service]], and the [[Department of Defense]]. The Department of Defense is interested because of its significant land holdings in both states and its training and readiness mission.

**Leadership.** Regional co-chairs are [[Nancy Kaufman]] (Regional Director, Fish and Wildlife Service) and [[John Kirkpatrick]] (Acting Regional Forester, Forest Service). DoD regional environmental coordinators are RADM Veronica Froman (Region 9) and Ed Lopez (Region 6). Washington coordination is provided by Jamie Rappaport Clark (Fish and Wildlife Service Director), Mike Dombeck (USDA Forest Service Chief), and Tom Tuchmann (Western Director and Special Assistant to the Secretary of Agriculture), who facilitates decision-making.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The briefing paper describes the region's natural and cultural resources as a "complex, fragile, and unique balance" with substantial potential in natural, cultural, and recreational values. Management of land, water, and cultural resources on federal lands in Arizona and New Mexico is described as increasingly complex. The only biological reference is an unnamed endangered species that prompted the House Appropriations Committee's FY 1998 directive. No species lists, population data, water-quality measurements, habitat descriptions, or site-level ecological results are provided.

## Key Findings

The documents set out the following required actions and timelines:

1. **Priority issue list (due November 14, 1997):** a final prioritized list of issues needing immediate attention by federal natural resource agencies, with a management plan.
2. **Agency collaboration proposal (due November 21, 1997):** must specify (1) participating agencies, (2) decision-making representatives for each agency, (3) staff for each decision maker, and (4) how split jurisdictions will be managed.
3. **Non-federal collaboration proposal:** must involve private sector, NGO, and other government representatives. The deadline was set in December 1997 (the exact day is unclear in the scanned copy).
4. **Comprehensive southwest strategy (deadline set by December 10, 1997):** must list issues in short-, mid-, and long-term horizons; describe resolution approaches; assign responsible agencies; define issue scope; outline public involvement; and include a timeline.

**Status as of the December 16, 1997 briefing paper:**
- Agencies have identified immediate issues and developed a collaboration process.
- Non-federal stakeholders will be contacted starting January 1998 to help develop the collaborative process and the strategy.
- A timeline for developing the strategy has been drafted.
- Some agency changes have already been implemented, and more will be needed.

**Stated goals:** effective communication and collaboration among stakeholders, streamlined regulatory processes, identification and resolution of resource-use conflicts, and improved resource conditions.
