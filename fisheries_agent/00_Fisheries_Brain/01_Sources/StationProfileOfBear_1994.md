## Executive Summary

This document is a **station profile** for the **Bears Bluff National Fish Hatchery (NFH)**, a satellite/substation facility of the [[Orangeburg National Fish Hatchery]], operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Located on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina (~20 miles south of Charleston), the 31-acre facility was transferred from the Environmental Protection Agency in 1981. Its designated future mission is the propagation and restoration of three key Atlantic coastal species: the [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], [[Red Drum]], and [[Striped Bass]]. As of the reporting period, the station remains in a pre-operational/transitional state, holding fish in circular tanks pending renovation of its brackish-water tidal basin ponds. This profile is primarily an administrative and infrastructural record rather than a biological or ecological research paper.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: The facility is designed around a **brackish water** tidal basin pond system, indicating that the target species occupy **estuarine/coastal transitional habitats** consistent with:
  - [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] — anadromous species inhabiting large coastal rivers and estuaries along the Atlantic coast; a species of conservation concern subject to restoration stocking.
  - [[Red Drum]] — euryhaline sciaenid inhabiting estuaries, tidal creeks, and nearshore coastal waters.
  - [[Striped Bass]] — anadromous species utilizing estuarine nursery habitats and coastal migration corridors.
- **Current interim habitat**: Until pond renovation is complete, all species are held in **8 circular tanks**, a controlled artificial environment rather than natural brackish habitat.
- **Geographic context**: Positioned within the Charleston Harbor/Wadmalaw Island estuarine complex on the South Carolina coast, an important nursery and migratory zone for anadromous and estuarine-dependent fishes along the [[Atlantic Coast Fisheries Restoration]] network.

## Key Findings

- **Facility purpose**: Bears Bluff NFH is designated to eventually produce [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], [[Red Drum]], and [[Striped Bass]] specifically for **restoration stocking efforts along the Atlantic coast**.
- **Operational status**: As of the report, the station operates only as a **saltwater substation** of Orangeburg NFH; full brackish-pond operations are pending renovation of the tidal basin ponds.
- **Infrastructure**: Physical assets include 2 laboratory houses, an administration/office building, a dormitory, a shop house, a storage house, a boat dock, a sea wall, and 8 circular holding tanks.
- **Administrative history**: The site (31 acres) was transferred from the **Environmental Protection Agency** to USFWS in **1981**, with an Advanced Planning Document for full development approved in **1985**.
- **Staffing/Funding**: Minimal dedicated staffing (1 FTE), with operations budget folded into the [[Orangeburg National Fish Hatchery]] budget rather than tracked independently.
- **Distribution record**: Fish distribution (Striped Bass and Shortnose Sturgeon) to South Carolina is reported jointly with Orangeburg NFH distribution figures, indicating Bears Bluff does not yet independently track stocking output.
- **Conservation relevance**: The explicit inclusion of the [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] — a federally protected anadromous species — signals this station's intended role in **endangered species restoration** alongside recreationally/commercially important species like [[Red Drum]] and [[Striped Bass]].
