# Property Rights Group Rails Against Rare Species Law

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 newspaper article from the *Bisbee Daily Review* (Herald/Review), transmitted by fax on May 23, 1995. It reports a meeting of the [[Coalition of Arizona and New Mexico Counties]] in Sierra Vista, Arizona, where speakers criticized the federal [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) and argued that land-use authority should rest with local people rather than federal scientists. The article contains no species data, no aquatic ecology, and no field or research results. Its relevance to aquatic science is indirect, limited to the policy context in which endangered-species protections are debated.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The article touches on environmental governance rather than ecology.

- **ESA criticism:** Christensen said the ESA shows more concern for "moss, spiders and ticks" than for humans. The article does not identify any specific listed species, so no taxa can be extracted.
- **National Biological Survey:** Christensen claimed that some people in the United States want to manage all land through a [[National Biological Survey]] that is "designed to deny humans use of the land." The article gives no details of the survey's design or scope.
- **Land-use control:** Pratt agreed that some federal and state land-use control is necessary but argued it should rest with people who understand "husbandry," defined as the control or judicious use of resources, rather than "a group of scientists who sit behind desks."
- **Public mood:** Pratt said the attendees were apparently mostly part of the growing [[Property Rights Movement]]. He also said he disagreed with some of what was said there.

No aquatic habitats, water bodies, fish populations, invertebrates, plankton, or water-chemistry data are mentioned.

## Key Findings

1. **Opposition to ESA implementation:** Coalition speakers argued that the ESA has gone too far and places disproportionate emphasis on minor organisms relative to human land use.
2. **Local versus federal control:** Speakers argued that resource and land decisions should be made by people with practical knowledge of land use rather than by federal scientists.
3. **Industry-linked coalition:** The coalition's board and funding are tied to timber, mining and ranching interests, and the article notes this composition.
4. **Internal divisions:** Jerry Pratt, while criticizing ESA administration, distanced himself from parts of the property rights movement, indicating divergent views among attendees.
