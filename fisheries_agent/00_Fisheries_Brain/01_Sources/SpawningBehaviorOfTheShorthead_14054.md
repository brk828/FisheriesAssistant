#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1977 field study by Burr and Morris documents the first detailed description of spawning behavior in the [[Shorthead Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma macrolepidotum*), observed in [[Big Rock Creek]], Illinois, on 17–18 May 1976. Contrary to prior conjecture that redhorse spawning occurs during evening/early morning hours, this population spawned in direct sunlight during midday and afternoon hours (1100–1600 h) at a water temperature of 16°C. The study characterizes spawning site morphology, group spawning mechanics, tuberculation patterns, and interspecific spawning-site sharing with the [[Northern Hog Sucker]] (*Hypentelium nigricans*) and other catostomids, contributing baseline reproductive ecology data for the [[Catostomidae]] (sucker family).

## Background & Description
- **Size**: Sampled individuals (n=25, captured via bag seine) were largely uniform in size, standard length 26–30 cm; smaller unmeasured individuals were also observed spawning.
- **Age**: Annuli readings from scales of three males indicated they were in their fifth year of life, consistent with size-at-age data reported for *M. m. macrolepidotum* in the Des Moines River, Iowa (Meyer 1962).
- **Sex ratio of captured sample**: Strongly male-biased — 23 of 25 individuals captured were male, only 2 were female.
- **Tuberculation (secondary sexual characteristic)**:
  - All captured males were fully tuberculate.
  - A single file of large, white tubercles present on the **lower lobe of the caudal fin**.
  - Large, white tubercles in a single file on **all elements of the anal fin**.
  - Smaller, more granular tubercles on the **upper lobe of the caudal fin**, and on **pelvic and pectoral fins**.
  - One of two captured females showed tubercles on anal and caudal fins, though less developed than males.
  - Tubercle placement/size suggests a dual function: maintaining spawning position (mechanical clasping) and possibly serving as a tactile/stimulus cue during spawning contact.
- **Coloration**: Both sexes displayed blood-red caudal fins; other ventral fins brilliant orange-red. Dorsal fin gray basally, suffused with red distally. Males appeared slightly more intensely colored than females.
- **Reproductive behavior note**: Both sexes readily released sex products (milt/eggs) when handled prior to release, indicating peak spawning readiness.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[Big Rock Creek]] is described as a high-gradient, rocky stream (Fox River drainage), located on the east edge of Plano, Kendall County, Illinois.
- **Spawning microhabitat**: Shallow riffle (15–21 cm deep) near the edge of a sandbar; substrate consisted of fine sand at depression edges and coarse stones/rubble (2.5–8.0 cm diameter) at depression centers.
- **Spawning structures**: Circular "nests" (40–55 cm diameter) and troughs (~90 cm long × 50 cm wide) — likely disturbance depressions rather than constructed nests. Approximately 30 separate depressions present at the site.
- **Population density**: Over 100 individuals estimated on the spawning riffle itself, with at least as many in adjacent deeper water (30–60 cm).
- **Interspecific associations**:
  - Tuberculate [[Northern Hog Sucker]] (*Hypentelium nigricans*) and [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*) were both captured at the site on 17 May; hog suckers occurred in groups of 3–9 sharing the riffle with shorthead redhorse.
  - By 18 May, only shorthead redhorse were observed on the riffles — suggesting temporally shifting multi-species use of shared spawning riffles.
  - No other *Moxostoma* species were seen at this site, though Pflieger (1975) reported *M. macrolepidotum* co-occurring on riffles with [[Golden Redhorse]] (*M. erythrurum*) in Missouri, spawning separately.
  - A concurrent observation of *M. erythrurum* migration/spawning was made the same day at Kilbuck Creek (Rock River drainage), Winnebago County, Illinois — also over rocky substrate, suggesting regional synchrony among redhorse species in timing, temperature, and substrate preference.
- **Disturbance response**: When frightened, spawning individuals retreated downstream or to deeper adjacent water, returning to resume spawning after approximately 25–30 minutes.

## Key Findings
1. **Spawning timing**: Observed spawning and migration occurred from **1100–1300 h on 17 May** and **1400–1600 h on 18 May**, in direct sunlight under clear skies — contradicting earlier literature (Eddy and Surber 1943; Scott and Crossman 1973) suggesting evening/early-morning spawning.
2. **Water temperature**: Spawning occurred at **16°C**, higher than the 11.1°C reported for Des Moines River, Iowa populations (Meyer 1962).
3. **Geographic/temporal consistency**: Spawning dates in northeastern Illinois align closely with those reported for central Illinois (Luce 1933), Ontario (Dymond 1926), and Iowa (Harlan and Speaker 1956), though spawning may occur as early as late April in Iowa (Meyer 1962) or as late as June in Canada (Richardson 1823).
4. **Spawning group mechanics**: Breeding adults typically formed groups of 3 (occasionally 4, 5, or 7). The core spawning act involved **two males flanking a female**, pressing bodies tightly together with caudal fins clasping the female's caudal region, followed by a few seconds of vigorous quivering before the trio dispersed and rejoined the group. This "trio" spawning strategy is characteristic of other catostomid species (Bowman 1970; McSwain and Gennings 1972; Reighard 1920).
5. **No territoriality**: Unlike the [[Black Redhorse]] (*M. duquesnei*), which reportedly exhibits territorial/aggressive spawning displays (Bowman 1970), no such behaviors were observed in *M. macrolepidotum* at this site.
6. **No eggs recovered**: Despite clear evidence of spawning activity (spent females, vigorous trembling), a search of the riffle substrate did not reveal any deposited eggs.
7. **Multi-species riffle sharing**: The study documents apparent temporal partitioning of a shared spawning riffle between shorthead redhorse and northern hog suckers, an ecologically notable observation of interspecific spawning habitat overlap among [[Catostomidae]].
8. **First behavioral description**: This paper represents the first published detailed description of spawning behavior for *M. macrolepidotum*, previously only documented via spawning dates/sites in the literature.
