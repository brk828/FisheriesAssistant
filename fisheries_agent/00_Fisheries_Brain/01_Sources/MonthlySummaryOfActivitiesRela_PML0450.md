# Spikedace Propagation Progress Report, 2002

#ichthyology #marine-biology #meda-fulgida

## Executive Summary

This monthly progress report, submitted on 10 December 2002 by [[American Southwest Ichthyological Research Foundation]] staff ([[Heather L. Parmeter]] and [[Steven P. Platania]]) to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] Phoenix Area Office, summarizes 2002 work to develop captive propagation techniques for [[Spikedace]] (*[[Meda Fulgida]]*), a threatened fish. The project tested whether water temperature, photoperiod, and feeding regime trigger reproduction in captive fish. The first wild-spawned eggs were recovered on 26 February 2002, and the first hatching occurred on 4–5 March 2002. Larvae were reared to about 10 mm standard length by the end of March. Later in 2002, the work shifted to handling and disease control, particularly an outbreak of [[Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis]] ("Ich"), and to new collections from the [[Gila River]]. Captive adults had not produced milt or typical spawning coloration by November 2002.

## Background & Description

[[Spikedace]] is a small minnow-like fish native to the [[Gila River]] system of the American Southwest. The report refers to it throughout as *Meda fulgida*. The species lacks scales, which the authors note makes it particularly susceptible to fungal and bacterial agents, and it is sensitive to handling stress.

The project was organized in phases. Phase one, running from October 2001 to May 2002, manipulated environmental conditions in captive adults. Phase two began in late May 2002 after the rearing system was moved to a larger facility. Captive stocks came from several wild collections:

- **October 2001 collection** (population #1), held for about 14 months by November 2002.
- **May 2002 collection** (population #2), taken from the Gila River at the [[Bird Area Site]] on 30 May 2002 (about 75 fish).
- **October 2002 collection** (population #3), taken from the same Gila River site (47 fish).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Captive conditions.** Early in 2002, fish were kept at ambient temperature (about 19 °C) under an 11 h light:13 h dark photoperiod, with about 30% water changes every fifth day. From 4 February 2002, seven tanks (A–G) were subjected to combinations of:

- Heat: 21–24 °C
- Extended light: 14 h light:10 h dark
- Feeding increased from 3 to 5 times per day
- Gravel, cobble, or artificial plant cover

Stocking in these seven tanks ranged from 5 to 29 fish per tank. No ill effects were observed during the trials. After the May 2002 move, fish were held at ambient temperature (about 24 °C) on a 12 h photoperiod using fluorescent light, supplemented by indirect natural sunlight. They were fed a mixed diet of algae and zooplankton three times daily.

**Wild habitat.** On 30 May 2002, discharge in the Gila River at the Bird Area site was very low (about 35–40 cfs), with water at 28 °C and air at 37 °C. Spikedace were most abundant at the interface of deep riffles and mid-stream runs, where seine hauls yielded about 7–12 fish. Shallow riffles, pools, and banks yielded 0–3 fish per haul. Other fish captured at the site included [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*). Young-of-year fish of these species were also collected, most estimated to be 3–4 weeks old.

**Transport.** Fish were moved to Albuquerque in oxygenated river water with salt and stress coat, held in ice chests for about four hours. Water temperature shifted by roughly 8 °C during transport. No mortalities occurred, and the fish were acclimated before being placed in the aquaria.

## Key Findings

**1. Spawning and egg recovery (February 2002).**
- At about 20:00 on 26 February 2002, 1–3 males chased a large female in Tank D. This is the first recorded spawning behavior. No simultaneous gamete release was witnessed.
- Eggs were recovered by vacuuming the substrate and straining the water. A total of 49 eggs were collected from Tank D: 12 were preserved and 37 were placed in a hatching chamber. Another 33 eggs came from Tank F and were held separately.
- Eggs measured about 1.5 mm in diameter and were opaque, demersal, and adhesive.

**2. Hatching and larval rearing (March 2002).**
- Hatching occurred on 4–5 March 2002. Larvae were first fed a commercial powdered diet (Fry Feed Kyowa A, 250 µm maximum particle size), then commercial flake food and frozen foods such as chironomids, Artemia, and algae.
- Larvae were preserved at roughly seven-day intervals in March for a developmental series. By the end of March, they had a mean standard length of about 10 mm and no dorsal or pectoral fins. Ten larvae were preserved in March.
- A second spawning event occurred 1–3 weeks after the February spawn. About 50 larvae, estimated at 2–5 weeks old, were observed in Tank D on 9 March 2002. Larvae that stayed with adults in treatment tanks appeared to survive better than those in hatching chambers.

**3. Environmental manipulation and its end (January–May 2002).**
- Experimental manipulation was discontinued on 27 May 2002 when the system was moved. No mortalities resulted from the move.
- Juveniles (about 200) were separated from adults and placed in 20-gallon tanks.
- The April 2002 monthly report is missing; April data will be included in the final report.

**4. Disease outbreak and treatment (June–August 2002).**
- Fish collected on 30 May were subjected to a bath of 25 ppm formalin and 0.5–1.0 ppt salt, with repeated tank transfers every 3–4 days to break the parasite life cycle. Fish were observed rubbing against the substrate, a sign of ectoparasite irritation.
- Approximately 40 juvenile spikedace died during the Ich outbreak in several rearing tanks. The outbreak was controlled by June–July, and the authors believe the infection likely originated in fish from wild collections, with a stress-related event possibly allowing the parasite to proliferate.
- Daily chemical treatment of the May 2002 adults was discontinued on 1 August 2002. Preventative treatments were applied on 13 and 1? August, and juveniles were treated on 14 August. No further signs of infection were reported afterward.

**5. Induced spawning attempts (June–September 2002).**
- Two induced spawning attempts were made in June 2002, using [[Carp Pituitary Extract]] (CPE) injections into fish held in three aquaria. Tank substrates included gravel and cobble, gravel alone, and gravel with plant cover. Sex ratios were about one male to one female. Sex characteristics were not apparent in either sex. No spawning occurred, and the attempt was repeated with an increased CPE concentration in July with the same negative result.
- The September 2002 experiments tested several temperatures (ambient, 16 °C, and 26 °C), photoperiods (10 h light:14 h dark, and others), substrates, and cover types across two populations. Densities ranged from 10 to 47 fish per 20-gallon tank. No spawning was recorded.

**6. Status of captive adults (October–November 2002).**
- No spawning occurred in October. There was one mortality during September 2002.
- On 25 October 2002, 47 spikedace were collected from the Gila River in 26 seine hauls to establish population #3. Catch rates differed from the October 2001 collection: most fish were taken in mid-stream runs over cobble, while deep and shallow habitats yielded only four and five fish, respectively. Ich prophylaxis for population #3 began on 26 October 2002.
- By November 2002, no male had produced milt or developed spawning coloration. Some females showed slight abdominal distension, possibly from egg development, but no reproductive behavior was observed.
- Four mortalities occurred in population #1, which was at least one year old and held for 14 months. One mortality occurred in population #2. No disease signs were evident in these deaths. Five primarily undersized juveniles also died in November, and all mortalities were preserved in 10% formalin for accession into the [[Museum Of Southwestern Biology]] fish collection.
- Juveniles averaged about 41 mm standard length.
- Population #3 showed Ich symptoms beginning 4 November 2002 (day 11 of holding). One mortality occurred on 15 November 2002 (day 22) but did not appear linked to the parasite or the treatment. Treatment was planned to continue through December 2002.

**Overall conclusion.** By the end of 2002, the project had achieved larval rearing and recovered eggs from a captive spawning event. Consistent reproduction in captive adults had not been achieved. The authors identified disease control and juvenile growth as priorities for the 2003 spawning season.
