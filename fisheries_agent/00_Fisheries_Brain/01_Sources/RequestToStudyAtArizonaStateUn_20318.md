# Graduate Study Application Letters from Gao Lianyong (1992)

#marine-biology #carcinology #penaeus-orientalis

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of three near-identical graduate application letters, all dated August 18, 1992, from [[Gao Lianyong]] of the [[Fisheries Research Institute]] of Hebei Province, Qinhuangdao, P.R. China. Each letter is addressed to a different group of faculty in the Department of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]] (Tempe, Arizona). The author seeks encouragement to apply for fall 1993 admission as a graduate student and research assistant, requests a scholarship, and presents his test scores and publication list as evidence of his background. The letters are research-career and admissions correspondence rather than primary research, but they summarize the author's work on [[Penaeus Orientalis]] (prawn) genetics, [[Sex Control]], [[Triploid Induction]], and aquaculture.

## Background & Description

**Applicant profile**
- Born 1962.
- Bachelor's degree in Fisheries Biology and Aquaculture (first grade in class), [[Ocean University of Qingdao]], 1983.
- Position at the [[Fisheries Research Institute]] of Hebei Province; now an engineer in aquatic zoology and aquaculture.
- Specialties listed: genetics and breeding, developmental and reproductive biology, nutrition, and aquaculture.
- Early work: genetic improvement of earthworm under Prof. Yin Zuofen (junior year); graduate thesis on induced polyploidy of fish under Prof. Ge Guochang (senior year).

**Test scores (as reported)**
- TOEFL: 587 (taken January 11, 1992).
- GRE General Test: 1720 total; verbal and quantitative scores of 1210 (taken April 11, 1992).
- Planned: TSE and TOEFL retake in October 1992, with expected scores "over 250" and "620" respectively.

**Research history (as reported)**
- 1983: Participated in the Nationwide Coastzone Biological Resources Investigation (accepted by the government in 1986); the Coastzone Fisheries Division won a provincial second prize.
- 1985–1987: Worked with Senior Engineer [[Chen Huibin]] on artificial propagation of mullet in freshwater, described as a significant breakthrough in China and abroad; won the national first prize in 1988.
- Since 1987: Entire artificial cultivation of prawn, including breeding, feeding, overwintering, and diet processing.
- 1989 to 1992: Sexual control and chromosome manipulation of aquatic animals, funded under the "National Eighth Five-Year Research Programs," emphasizing induced polyploidy and feminization, artificial insemination, tissue culture, and gonad ultrastructure.

**Stated research interest areas**
1. Genetics and cellular biology (genome study, chromosome manipulation, sex control).
2. Organismal biology and population study (reproduction, nutrition, aquatic ecology for aquaculture).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The author's field work centers on the [[Hebei Intertidal Zone]], where a 1988 study documented biological resources and a 1992 study addressed species composition, distribution, and utilization of clam worm (polychaete).
- Prawn work is framed within aquaculture (breeding, overwintering, diet processing) rather than wild population ecology.
- Mullet work concerns freshwater artificial propagation.
- No environmental measurements (water chemistry, habitat parameters, population sizes) are reported.

## Key Findings

**Reported research results**
- Prawn ([[Penaeus Orientalis]]) induced triploid rate: over 87%.
- Prawn feminization rate: over 93%, described by the author as "the most successful report up to now."
- Mullet artificial propagation in freshwater: national first prize, 1988.

**Requests and plans stated in the letters**
- Seeks faculty encouragement to apply for fall 1993 admission.
- Seeks a scholarship from the research programs, department, or Graduate School.
- Will submit application forms, documents, and official test scores once encouraged to do so.
- Will send publication reprints on request.
- If unable to accept him, asks each recipient to recommend other appropriate faculty.

**Document note:** The three letters (to Drs. Rasmussen, Chandler, and Capco; Drs. Hazel and Moore; Drs. Minckley, Douglas, and Dowling) share identical body text. Differences are limited to addressees and a few minor wording and typographical variations.
