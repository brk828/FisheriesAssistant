# Growing Live Bait for Bream and Panfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #aquaculture #bait-production

## Executive Summary

This Alabama Cooperative Extension Service leaflet provides detailed instructions for home production of four live bait organisms commonly used for [[Bream]] and other [[Panfish]] that feed primarily on worms and insects. The document covers cultivation methods for [[Mealworms]] (beetle larvae), [[Soldierfly]] grubs, [[Earthworms]], and [[Crickets]], including container specifications, feeding regimens, environmental requirements, pest management, and harvesting techniques. Production methods are designed for small-scale operations suitable for personal use or cash market sales, with specific guidance for 4-H youth projects.

## Background & Description

The document addresses the common problem of bait scarcity when fishing for bream and panfish. Four invertebrate species are recommended for home cultivation:

**[[Mealworms]]** (also called "golden grubs"): The larval stage of a black beetle used as bait. Adults are black beetles; larvae are yellowish-brown grubs.

**[[Soldierfly]] grubs**: Inch-long grayish-white larvae of a harmless black fly, historically common in privies and now found in decaying organic matter. The soldierfly does not carry human disease organisms.

**[[Earthworms]]**: Specifically [[English Red Worms]], cultivated in outdoor beds with organic bedding material.

**[[Crickets]]**: Gray or black crickets grown in controlled containers. Females identifiable by long ovipositor tube extending from abdomen.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document reflects mid-20th century (1959) agricultural extension practices in Alabama, focusing on organisms that naturally occur in the diet of freshwater panfish. Bream and related species feed primarily on terrestrial and aquatic invertebrates, making these cultured baits ecologically appropriate.

**Habitat requirements vary by species:**
- [[Mealworms]]: Dry, ventilated environments; containers kept at room temperature
- [[Soldierfly]]: Dark, warm conditions during spring and summer; require decaying organic matter
- [[Earthworms]]: Shaded, well-drained soil near water; beds maintained at consistent moisture
- [[Crickets]]: Temperature-sensitive; optimal range 80-90°F; require protection from temperature extremes below 70°F or above 95°F

## Key Findings

**Mealworm Production:**
- Stocking rate: 50-100 beetles or larvae per 5-gallon container
- Substrate: 6 inches wheat bran
- Moisture source: 2-3 whole unpeeled cull potatoes
- Reproduction: Each adult female lays 250-1,000 eggs
- Timeline: Hundreds of grubs produced within a few weeks
- Storage: Refrigerate in ventilated jars with bran to prevent premature metamorphosis

**Soldierfly Production:**
- Container: 5-gallon drum in dark shed distant from dwellings
- Medium: 6-inch layer of laying mash mixed with water to soupy consistency
- Key requirement: Darkness prevents housefly contamination
- Timeline: Inch-long grubs appear within a few weeks after eggs hatch
- Management: Keep mash fairly dry to prevent premature pupation

**Earthworm Production:**
- Bed dimensions: 6-12 inches deep, 3 feet wide, any length
- Stocking rate: 25 worms per square foot
- Bedding options: (1) cottonseed meal flue bran, (2) cotton motes, or (3) 2 parts ground peanut hay with molasses plus 1 part corn and cob meal
- Feed: ¼ to ½-inch layer cottonseed meal weekly
- Production: 700-1,500 worms per square foot in 6-8 months
- Harvest timeline: Worms ready for bait within 6 months
- Maintenance: Clean and restart beds every 6-10 months

**Cricket Production:**
- Container: 18-24 inches deep metal containers or wax-polished wooden boxes
- Stocking: 5 females and 5 males per square foot for breeding
- Egg substrate: Baked, moistened sand in bread pans; females require damp sand for oviposition
- Timeline: Remove sand pans 15 days after placement; remove 25 days after first hatchlings appear
- Production: 130-170 crickets per square foot every 3 months
- Feed requirement: 2 pounds laying mash per 100 crickets produced
- Temperature management: Light bulb heating for winter production; thermostat recommended

**Pest Management:**
- [[Earthworms]]: Red mites controlled with parathion spray (1 pint 25% emulsifiable concentrate or 2 pounds 15% wettable powder per 100 gallons water); houseflies managed by reducing cottonseed meal and increasing watering; moles trapped or excluded with hardware cloth
- [[Crickets]]: Daily removal of dead individuals; equipment sterilization with parathion followed by soap washing; feed fumigation with 3:1 ethylene dichloride:carbon tetrachloride mixture (6 gallons per 1,000 cubic feet)
- Safety warning: Parathion identified as extremely dangerous; requires immediate washing, avoidance of fumes and skin contact

**Marketing and Use:**
- [[Mealworms]]: Packaged in pint ice cream containers with bran and ventilation holes
- [[Soldierfly]]: Small cardboard containers with dry mash and ventilation holes
- [[Earthworms]]: Waxed cartons two-thirds full of bed material, topped with moistened peat moss
- [[Crickets]]: Screen wire cages for customer sales; measuring jar system for counting (25, 50, 100 cricket marks)
- Fishing technique: Place two grubs/worms on hook so ends wiggle

**4-H Project Requirements:**
Produce one or more bream baits as recommended; maintain records of costs, personal bait use, and profits from sales.
