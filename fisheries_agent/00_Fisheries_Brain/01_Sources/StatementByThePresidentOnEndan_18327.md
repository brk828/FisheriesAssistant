## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 White House press release and accompanying Department of the Interior / U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) fact sheets announcing regulatory reforms to the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) and [[Clean Water Act]] wetlands programs. The reforms are aimed at reducing regulatory burden on small landowners and homeowners with five acres or fewer of land, while attempting to preserve conservation protections for species listed as "threatened." ## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although no specific habitat-use data for individual fish taxa is presented, the document provides regulatory context relevant to the **habitat protection status** of threatened aquatic and terrestrial species:

- **Regulatory habitat classification:** Threatened species (as distinct from [[Endangered Species]]) are protected under ESA Section 4(d), which grants the Secretary of the Interior discretionary authority to craft species-specific protective regulations — unlike the fixed statutory prohibitions applied to endangered species.
- **Small-landowner habitat interactions:** The policy presumes that "residential property has limited habitat value for threatened species," implying that small, disturbed, or developed parcels (≤5 acres) are assumed to contribute negligibly to the survival or recovery needs of most threatened species' habitat.
- **Wetlands habitat provision:** The [[Clean Water Act]] wetlands permitting reform allows homeowners to fill up to a half-acre of **non-tidal wetland** without individual permitting, directly reducing regulatory oversight of wetland habitats — ecosystems that can serve as spawning, nursery, or foraging habitat for various fish and aquatic wildlife species.
- **Precedent case cited:** An 80-acre small-landowner exemption was already proposed for the **northern spotted owl** (a non-fish terrestrial species), justified by the existence of a comprehensive Federal Forest Plan — illustrating that exemption acreage thresholds are adjustable on a species-by-species basis depending on existing conservation infrastructure.

## Key Findings

- **Scope of species affected:** The proposed small-landowner exemption regulatory presumption would apply to a pool of **111 currently listed threatened fish and wildlife species**, with FWS conducting case-by-case assessments within 90 days of final rule adoption to determine applicability.
- **Three exemption categories** proposed under 50 CFR 17.31:
  1. **Residential Exemption** — activities on ≤5-acre parcels with a single-household dwelling used principally for non-commercial purposes.
  2. **5-Acre Exemption** — any activity (including commercial) limited to 5 contiguous acres of cumulative disturbance, applicable regardless of total parcel size.
  3. **Negligible Effects Exemption** — a catch-all category for activities identified by FWS as having negligible adverse effects on a given threatened species.
- **Non-additive exemptions:** Landowners may use either the Residential or 5-Acre exemption, but not both; multiple-property owners are limited to one exemption across all holdings.
- **Anti-subdivision safeguard:** Exemptions cannot be applied to parcels created by subdividing larger tracts after (a) the date of proposed listing (future species) or (b) March 6, 1995 (currently listed species) — preventing developers from circumventing acreage limits.
- **Exclusion of endangered species and plants:** The exemption framework applies **only to threatened species**, not endangered species, since ESA Section 4(d) regulatory discretion does not extend to endangered listings. Threatened **plants** are also excluded, since the ESA generally does not prohibit "taking" of plants on private land.
- **Cumulative effects safeguard:** Exemptions would not apply, or would be modified via a "special rule," if the **cumulative effects** of many landowners' activities were determined likely to significantly harm a threatened species' survival or recovery.
- **Wetlands parallel reform:** The [[Army Corps of Engineers]] would issue a new nationwide permit allowing homeowners to fill up to 0.5 acres of non-tidal wetland for residential construction/expansion without individual permitting review.
- **Stated policy rationale:** Interior Secretary [[Bruce Babbitt]] noted that approximately 95% of Americans live on parcels of five acres or less, framing the reform as producing "fairness and certainty" for landowners while purportedly maintaining conservation outcomes for species such as the bald eagle, gray whale, and peregrine falcon (cited as ESA success stories, though not directly linked to the new exemption policy).
