# Introduction to Caves Studied During the First Italian Zoological Mission to Mexico (1969)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This introductory note from the [[Accademia Nazionale Dei Lincei]] reports the first Italian biospeleological expedition to Mexico (10 October to 9 December 1969), led by Valerio Sbordoni and Roberto Argano. The mission aimed to compare cave fauna of tropical and temperate karst by sampling caves of contrasting climate and altitude within the same country. It surveyed 17 cavities, of which 16 yielded isopods and 9 yielded [[Ptomaphagus]] beetles. The note describes each cave's locality, elevation, temperature, humidity, and collected taxa, and gives preliminary faunal lists. It is primarily a site and inventory report rather than a fish study, although it records cave-adapted [[Astyanax Cavefish]] populations.

## Background & Description

- **Rationale:** Troglobitic (cave-obligate) terrestrial fauna is scarce in tropical caves, where troglobites are mainly aquatic. Earlier comparisons were hampered by heterogeneous data and distant study areas, so Mexico was chosen for its climatic gradient within circumscribed areas.
- **Focus groups:** Triconiscid isopods and catopid beetles of the genus *Ptomaphagus*, which occur in both temperate and tropical caves.
- **Sampling:** About 300 *Ptomaphagus* from three populations were brought alive to Rome for physiological study. Interstitial and epigean collections were also made, with results to be published separately.
- **Team:** The authors, with Prof. Vittorio Parisi (Milan) until 13 November.
- **Sponsorship:** Fully funded by the Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei, planned by Prof. Pasquale Pasquini.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study areas were organised from north to south into four carsic regions:

- **[[Sierra Madre Oriental]]:** Sierra de Guatemala (Tamaulipas), Sierra de El Abra (San Luis Potosí), and the Xilitla region (San Luis Potosí/Querétaro). Elevations range from temperate highlands to hot lowlands with mean annual temperatures near 25 °C.
- **[[Cacahuamilpa Karst]]:** A carsic plateau with immense dolinas, centred on Tonatico (Mexico State/Guerrero).
- **[[Veracruz Karst]]:** Tlilapan (near Orizaba, about 3000 ft) and Córdoba (about 1700 ft, mean annual rainfall above 80 in).

Cave conditions varied widely. Internal temperatures ranged from about 11.5 °C in high-altitude Cueva de la Perra to about 24–26 °C in tropical sites such as Grutas de Quintero and Cueva Chica. Guano accumulations, which provide the main trophic base in many caves, support dense invertebrate communities and bat colonies. The note also flags [[Histoplasmosis]] risk, since *Histoplasma capsulatum* was recorded in guano-rich areas of Cacahuamilpa.

## Key Findings

**Cave inventory:** 17 cavities were visited. Key sites include:

- **Cueva de la Perra** (Gómez Farías, Tamaulipas, 7200 ft, ~11.5 °C): rich troglobite fauna including *Ptomaphagus troglomexicanus*, carabids of *Mexaphaenops*, and pool-dwelling planarians and a depigmented dytiscid.
- **Grutas de Quintero** (Ciudad Mante, Tamaulipas): troglobitic Mysidacea (*Typhlolepidomysis quinterensis*), cirolanid isopods, and a population of ricinulei (*Cryptocellus osorioi*).
- **Sótano de Pichijumo** (Sierra de El Abra): depigmented, anophthalmic *Astyanax* living alongside normally pigmented, eyed *Astyanax*. No intermediate forms were found, which the authors interpret as evidence of reproductive isolation in nature.
- **Cueva Chica** (El Pujal, San Luis Potosí): a topotypical population of *Astyanax jordani* in the lake, with a large bat colony. Its temperature was 24.2 °C, and it had been studied by Breder (1942). The authors describe the population ~20 km away as showing continuous variability from hybridisation between epigean and hypogean forms.
- **Cueva del Taninul No. 1** (Ciudad Valles): abundant oniscoid isopods and ricinulei, with a tunnel temperature of 24.3 °C.
- **Cueva del Madroño** (Querétaro): exclusively terrestrial fauna, with about 100 bats at the time of visit.
- **Cueva de la Estrella** (Tonatico): very numerous white thysanurans of the genus *Vecoletia*, and tarantulid amblypygids.
- **Cueva de Agua Brava** and **El Resuello** (Hoya Coralleyo, Cacahuamilpa): El Resuello, a vauclusian source, held a lake with small *Astyanax* of normal eye and pigment development.
- **Gruta de Cacahuamilpa** (Guerrero): a tourist cave with about 100 specimens of the endemic *Ptomaphagus spelaeus*. Bilimek (1867) had earlier listed 11 species here.
- **Cueva de Ojo de Agua Grande** (Córdoba, Veracruz): an abundant isopod fauna, including *Typhlotricholigioides aquaticus* (Rioja 1952) and a new *Cyathura* species.

**Fish records:** Cave-adapted and epigean *Astyanax* were the only fish reported. Records come from Sótano de Pichijumo, Cueva Chica, El Resuello, and the Cacahuamilpa area. The document gives no fish counts or formal species descriptions.

**Sampling and status:** The study was preliminary. Material was still being processed by specialists, and Catopid results were to follow. The authors state that comparison between temperate and tropical cave populations remained only indicative until taxonomic work is complete.

**Summary:** Mexico offers a close-range climatic gradient suitable for testing cave adaptation. The expedition documented 17 caves, several notable troglobite and fish populations, and the outline of a long-term biospeleological programme.
