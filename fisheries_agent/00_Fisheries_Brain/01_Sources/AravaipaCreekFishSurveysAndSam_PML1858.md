# Aravaipa Creek Fish Surveys and Sampling Coordination, 1999

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence thread documents coordinated fish sampling in [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona, during 1999. It covers three field efforts: an April 9–11 survey by Arizona State University (ASU) researchers, an October 18–20 survey of nine stations led by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) with [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC) and volunteers, and an October 22–23 ASU survey of canyon sites. The thread also records permit and access arrangements and the data-sharing plan. Native cyprinids and catostomids dominate the catches. Non-native species were recorded only at a few sites.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Aravaipa Creek is a desert stream in southeastern Arizona. Access is split among private owners, TNC preserves and BLM/Forest Service land. The creek is managed as a wilderness area.
- **Principal taxa named in the correspondence:** *Agosia* (longfin dace), *Rhinichthys* (speckled dace), *Tiaroga* (loach minnow), *Meda* (spikedace), *Gila* (chub), *Pantosteus* (sucker), *Catostomus* (sucker), and the hybrid suckers noted at several sites. Non-natives recorded were *Ameiurus*, *Cyprinella*, *Gambusia* and *Lepomis*.
- **Coordinators:** [[Peter Unmack]] (ASU) compiled the ASU catches. [[David A. Weedman]] (AGFD Native Fish Biologist) led the October AGFD survey and its data summary. [[Paul C. Marsh]] was the recipient of the field data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Permits and access:** Sampling required written approval from the AGFD Fisheries Supervisor, BLM Tucson fisheries staff and the Safford Field Office (wilderness permit). Landowner and TNC access agreements covered the upper, middle and lower reaches, and the thread lists on-site contacts for gate keys and emergencies. Sampling was to be scheduled at least one month in advance. Data were to be shared with AGFD, BLM and TNC, and a thank-you was to go to the private landowner who cooperated.
- **Survey methods (October 18–20):** At each of nine stations, a 200 m reach was seined. The lowest 100 m was then electrofished. Fish were released upstream of the seined area, so shocking may have recaptured some fish. The AGFD advised against pooling shocking and seining data. This method matched earlier efforts.
- **Efforts recorded:** Shocking durations ranged from 334 to 898 seconds across stations. Seined areas were roughly 1,100–2,400 m² per station.
- **Non-native species:** [[Gambusia]] was abundant at Sycamore Tree in October (268 individuals). [[Lepomis]] and [[Cyprinella]] appeared at a few sites. No exotics were collected at the TNC house site below the barrier in April.

## Key Findings

**Spring survey, April 9–11, 1999 (ASU):**
- **Upper TNC house:** [[Agosia]] 597, [[Tiaroga]] 170, [[Meda]] 43, [[Pantosteus]] 29, [[Rhinichthys]] 12, [[Catostomus]] 1, [[Gila]] 12 (count per source), plus 2 hybrid suckers. All seven native taxa were caught below the barrier, though numbers were not recorded there.
- **Chimney Rock:** [[Agosia]] 176, [[Meda]] 155, [[Tiaroga]] 46, [[Pantosteus]] 31, [[Catostomus]] 12, [[Gila]] 15, [[Rhinichthys]] 7, 1 hybrid sucker.
- **Turkey Creek confluence:** [[Meda]] 134, [[Agosia]] 11 (count per source), [[Pantosteus]] 33, [[Gila]] 21, [[Tiaroga]] 32, [[Catostomus]] 19, [[Rhinichthys]] 8.
- **Parsons Canyon confluence:** [[Meda]] 41, [[Agosia]] 2, [[Pantosteus]] 6.
- **Booger Canyon confluence:** [[Agosia]] 27, [[Pantosteus]] 19, [[Tiaroga]] 4, [[Gila]] 4.
- **Sycamore Tree:** [[Agosia]] 40, [[Cyprinella]] 10, [[Gambusia]] 2 plus juveniles, [[Ameiurus]] 4, [[Gila]] 1, [[Lepomis]] 1.
- Larval and juvenile fish were common at several sites. Sampling was not exhaustive at the tributary sites.

**Fall survey, October 18–20, 1999 (AGFD, nine stations):**
- Stations included Hell Hole, Horse Camp, Painted Cave, Wagner Ranch, White's Ranch and Sycamore Tree, among others.
- The source summary table is partly garbled by scanning, so station-level counts are not reproduced here. The reported total row is incomplete.
- Native taxa dominated the catches. Non-natives were recorded at a few stations.

**Fall survey, October 22–23, 1999 (ASU):**
- **Upper end TNC house:** [[Agosia]] 57, [[Catostomus]] 10, [[Meda]] 70, [[Rhinichthys]] 22, [[Tiaroga]] 23, 2 hybrid suckers.
- **Chimney Rock:** [[Agosia]] 130, [[Meda]] 126, [[Pantosteus]] 54, [[Tiaroga]] 37, [[Rhinichthys]] 15, [[Catostomus]] 16, [[Gila]] 6.
- **Turkey Creek confluence:** [[Meda]] 78, [[Pantosteus]] 56, [[Catostomus]] 20, [[Tiaroga]] 49, [[Gila]] 4, [[Rhinichthys]] 6, 2 hybrid suckers.
- **Middle canyon sites:** [[Agosia]] and [[Ameiurus]] were recorded, along with [[Catostomus]] 30. The thread noted the catch "seemed like mostly suckers."
- **White's Ranch:** [[Agosia]] 67, [[Pantosteus]] 9, [[Catostomus]] 6, [[Ameiurus]] 1, [[Cyprinella]] 1.
- **Sycamore Tree:** [[Agosia]] 371, [[Gambusia]] 268, [[Cyprinella]] 19, [[Ameiurus]] 2, [[Catostomus]] 2, [[Pantosteus]] 2, [[Tiaroga]] 13.

**Management implications stated in the thread:**
- Consistency with previous sampling designs is a stated priority, so that fall results can be compared across years.
- Data are to be sent to landowners, agencies and TNC once compiled, and the thread records the planned distribution list.
