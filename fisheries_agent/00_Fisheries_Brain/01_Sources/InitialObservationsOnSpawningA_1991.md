# Initial Observations on Spawning and Conservation Status of Redfinned Blue-eye

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Pseudomugilidae

## Executive Summary
This 1991 preliminary study by Unmack and Brumley documents the reproductive biology and dire conservation situation of the [[Redfinned Blue-eye]] (*Scaturiginichthys vermeilipinnis*), a newly discovered micro-endemic fish restricted to a small cluster of artesian springs at Edgbaston Station, central Queensland, within the [[Lake Eyre Drainage Division]]. The species is described as hardy and relatively easy to breed in captivity under specific water chemistry and feeding conditions. Critically, the paper documents the invasion of the springs by the introduced [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia holbrooki*), which is actively displacing and predating on the blue-eye via habitat exclusion and fin-nipping. The authors predict imminent local extinction in at least two of five known spring populations, prompting a call for endangered species listing and captive breeding efforts.

## Background & Description
- **Size at sexual maturity/spawning onset:** ~15mm total length (TL).
- **Sexing (from ~12-15mm TL):** Primarily distinguished by colouration rather than morphology.
  - **Males:** Bright red colouration on dorsal, ventral, anal, and caudal fins; develop a dark vertical stripe through the eye during display/spawning; overall body darkens during spawning; fins reportedly slightly longer than females.
  - **Females:** Minimal colour change; body becomes only slightly darker during spawning; occasionally show a faint red tint on the outer edge of dorsal, ventral, and anal fins (no caudal colouration).
- **Egg size:** Approximately 1.5mm diameter, often laid in clusters of 2-6.
- **Larval size at hatching:** 4-5mm TL.
- **Fin damage as ecological indicator:** Reduction in caudal fin length observed in wild blue-eyes due to fin-nipping by juvenile/sub-adult mosquitofish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Extremely shallow (max depth 8cm, often 1-4cm), fully sun-exposed artesian spring pools fed by the [[Great Artesian Basin]].
- **Water chemistry (type locality, Nov 11 1990):**
  - Water temperature: 36°C
  - pH: ~8
  - Calcium/Magnesium hardness: 20ppm
  - Conductivity: 100 EC
  - Salinity: <1%
- **Thermal extremes:** Air temperatures ~40°C (up to 48°C in shade) in summer; sub-freezing air temperatures possible on winter nights. Water temperature in winter unknown. Recorded water temp of 23-25°C near dusk on April 2, 1991.
- **Distribution:** Known only from five spring pools ("Pool 1" through "Pool 5") near Edgbaston homestead, with Pool 1 serving as the type locality.
- **Sympatric species:** Co-occurs with an undescribed species of [[Desert Goby]] and the invasive [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia holbrooki*). Novel endemic crustaceans and molluscs have also recently been discovered in the same spring system, indicating a broader undocumented endemic fauna.
- **Interspecific interactions:**
  - Historically, blue-eyes co-existed with mosquitofish throughout spring pools when mosquitofish numbers were low.
  - By April 1991, mosquitofish (up to 51mm TL) had proliferated in deeper zones (6-8cm) of Pools 3 and 4, forcing blue-eyes into marginal shallow refugia (1-3cm depth) alongside juvenile/sub-adult mosquitofish (up to 30mm TL).
  - Pool 5 (max depth 4cm) lacks deep-water habitat preferred by mosquitofish; blue-eyes remain more abundant than mosquitofish there, suggesting habitat depth is a key determinant of competitive/predatory pressure.
  - Pool 1 remained free of mosquitofish at time of writing; Pool 2 status unconfirmed pending resurvey.

## Key Findings
- **Breeding methodology:** Successful captive spawning achieved via group spawning (8-12 fish) at an approximate sex ratio of 2:1 (female:male), using Melbourne tap water (soft, slightly alkaline) buffered with sodium bicarbonate.
- **Spawning temperature range:** 20-32°C (not necessarily true physiological limits).
- **Key spawning drivers:** Food availability (twice-daily live food, e.g., mosquito larvae) identified as the most critical factor; pH may also play a role.
- **Egg-laying behavior:** Eggs deposited on spawning mops in clusters of 2-6; daily mop checks required as adults consume eggs despite available food. Best single-day yield: 24 eggs from a group of 5 females and 3 males; average ~12 eggs/day from this group size.
- **Development timeline:** Eggs hatch in 1-2 weeks depending on temperature. Fry raised initially on vinegar eels, then newly-hatched brine shrimp.
- **Conservation crisis:** Mosquitofish detected in 4 of 5 known blue-eye springs as of December 18, 1990 survey. By April 3, 1991 resurvey, mosquitofish had become dominant in Pools 3 and 4, with blue-eyes predicted to face local extinction within one year in these two pools.
- **Conservation recommendation:** Authors argue *S. vermeilipinnis* is likely Australia's most endangered fish species due to extremely restricted range (five small spring pools) and advocate for formal endangered listing. Contact was made with the [[Queensland Fisheries Branch]] to develop a management plan, alongside establishment of a captive breeding population as an insurance measure.
- **Research gaps identified:** Need for a comprehensive spring survey given recent (likely anthropogenic) mosquitofish introduction; further study needed on interactions between blue-eye, mosquitofish, and the undescribed desert goby species; winter water temperatures in the springs remain unrecorded.
