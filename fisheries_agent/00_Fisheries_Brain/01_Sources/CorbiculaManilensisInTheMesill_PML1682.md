# Corbicula manilensis in the Mesilla Valley of Texas and New Mexico

#malacology #marine-biology #bivalvia

## Executive Summary

This compiled section of *Nautilus* Vol. 80 (1) contains two short bivalve (clam) studies and a bibliographic note. The first is a distributional note on the genus [[Mya]] in Alaska, which reports [[Mya Truncata]] from the Anchorage area and other Alaskan localities and suggests that Anchorage specimens are reworked fossils. The second, by Artie L. Metcalf, documents the establishment of the introduced [[Asiatic Clam]] (*[[Corbicula Manilensis]]*) in [[West Drain]] of the [[Mesilla Valley]], Texas and New Mexico, and discusses its probable age, habitat, dispersal limits and economic effects. A third note reviews the life and publications of [[Charles Moore Wheatley]]. The text also includes a brief mention of unionid mussels in Kansas.

## Background & Description

**Mya (Mya truncata and Mya arenaria).** The genus *Mya* includes two Recent species discussed here. [[Mya Arenaria]] does not range as far north as the Alaska Peninsula and does not occur in peninsular collections. *Mya truncata* is illustrated by MacNeil (1965) from Kodiak Island and St. Lawrence Island, so it occurs both north and south of the Alaska Peninsula. However, it is absent from the 44 collections the author examined. Soot-Ryan (in MacNeil, 1965, p. 40) describes *M. truncata* as essentially circumboreal and states it is absent from the East Siberian Sea.

**Corbicula manilensis.** Specimens are referred by Sinclair and Isom (1963) to *Corbicula (Corbiculina) manilensis* (Philippi). Shells from West Drain are subtrigonal to ovate, with low umbones, fine sculpture and an intensely purple nacreous layer. They most closely resemble figures 31–36 in Sinclair and Isom (1963), which show clams from Phoenix, Arizona.

**Study system.** The drains of the Mesilla Valley were built mainly between 1917 and 1930 to lower a rising water table that harmed agriculture. Channels are generally 6–10 feet deep and 8–20 feet wide at the bottom. Irrigation ditches, by contrast, carry water to fields and are dry in winter. Between 1962 and 1965, the lower parts of the major drains flowed permanently, while the upper reaches were periodically dry. Water is usually clear, and bottom sediments are mixed silt and sand, with gravels up to six inches in diameter in places.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Anchorage beaches.** In the Anchorage region, at the start of the [[Alaska Peninsula]], *M. truncata* is locally the dominant mollusk on modern beaches. These occurrences are limited to shorelines formed by the [[Bootlegger Cove Clay]], a Pleistocene unit, where the species is also the most common shell in the fossiliferous clay. The author considers all Anchorage specimens examined to be reworked fossils, so the species may not be part of the modern Anchorage fauna. MacNeil (in Schmidt, 1963; in Miller and Dobrovolny, 1959) had earlier listed *M. truncata* from this clay.

**West Drain habitat.** The drain carries a narrow fringe of phreatophytic shrubs and small trees, with cottonwoods rare. Grasses, rushes and cattails grow along the bottom. The main aquatic plants are watercress, duckweeds and algae. Chemical analyses from October 1964 to January 1965 compared West Drain with its tributary Nemexas Drain, a short distance above their confluence. The main difference was hardness, with West Drain yielding higher values. Metcalf found no evidence that clams had established in Nemexas or Montoya drains, and suggested differences in environmental conditions as a possible explanation.

**Dispersal barriers.** Metcalf inspected many sites along the [[Rio Grande]] between El Paso, Texas, and Hatch, New Mexico, and found no established *C. manilensis* in the main channel, except possibly just below the mouths of major drains. The river may act as a barrier, either as an unsuitable habitat for larvae or because it is a difficult dispersal route.

## Key Findings

- **Mya truncata:** Recorded from Kodiak Island and St. Lawrence Island, so it occurs north and south of the Alaska Peninsula. It was not found in the 44 collections examined. Its absence from the East Siberian Sea is reported by Soot-Ryan.
- **Mya arenaria:** Does not extend north to the Alaska Peninsula and is absent from peninsular collections.
- **Anchorage specimens:** Probably reworked from the Pleistocene Bootlegger Cove Clay. The modern presence of *M. truncata* in the Anchorage area is uncertain.
- **Corbicula establishment:** The clam was first observed in West Drain in November 1964. Individuals had reached 25 mm by then, and specimens collected in November 1965 reached 34 mm. Sinclair and Isom (1963) estimated that a 28 mm specimen from Tennessee was four years old. Bank sediments contained no *C. manilensis* shells, so the population is probably recent.
- **Limited spread:** The species was absent from other drains, the main Rio Grande channel and the tributaries of West Drain, which points to dispersal limits or differences in environmental conditions.
- **Economic impact:** Ingram et al. (1964) reported that Asiatic clams in concrete-lined irrigation canals near Parker, Arizona, created nuisance conditions in canals, ditches, pumps and cooling systems. Mesilla Valley sediments are not used in concrete making, so this issue does not apply here.
- **Positive uses of Corbicula:** Villadolid and Del Rosario (1930) and Sinclair and Isom (1963) list human food, animal food and fish bait.
- **Wheatley note:** The author's bibliography covers his conchological works, including a privately printed *Catalogue of the Shells of the United States with their Localities* (1842; second edition 1845) and "Revision of M. Petit's Catalogue of the Genus Monocondylaea D'Orb." (1865). The text gives his death year as 1889, which is probably a misprint because it precedes his listed publications, so the date should be checked against Johnson (1959).
- **Kansas unionids:** Murray and Leonard (1962) reported that clear-water streams were absent in their survey of Kansas unionid mussels. The surrounding text is incomplete in this excerpt.
