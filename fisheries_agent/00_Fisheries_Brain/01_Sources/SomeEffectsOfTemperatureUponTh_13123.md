# Some Effects of Temperature upon the Development of Tilapia macrocephala

#ichthyology #marine-biology #tilapia

## Executive Summary

This item is a 1962 issue of the [[Turtox News]] (Vol. 40, No. 6, June). Its main scientific content is a short research note by [[Sanford H. Vernick]], who tested whether water temperature during embryonic development changes the vertebral count of [[Tilapia Macrocephala]]. Cold rearing appeared to increase vertebral number and high temperature appeared to reduce it. The altered spines produced [[Lordosis]] or [[Scoliosis]] in adults, although the statistical significance of the count differences was not fully established. The rest of the issue is supplier catalogue material (frog embryo stages, insect pins and nets, slide sets, a herbarium specimen, and charts), with no further research data.

## Background & Description

- **Research question:** Temperature is known to influence animal growth and development. In fishes, meristic characters such as vertebral number were traditionally treated as fixed early in development and used in taxonomy. [[Carl Hubbs]] (1922) challenged this by reporting that vertebral number in some fishes varied inversely with the temperature during development. Later workers disagreed about whether Hubbs was correct.
- **Study species:** *Tilapia macrocephala*, the African Black Chinned Mouth Breeder.
- **Design:** Eggs were divided into two groups, one collected very early in development and one very late. One batch from each group was reared to hatching at abnormally high temperatures and another at abnormally low temperatures. Separate short-exposure experiments exposed eggs from both groups to brief high or low temperatures.
- **Assessment:** Adult fish were X-rayed to detect skeletal abnormalities.
- **Author affiliations:** Marymount College, Tarrytown, New York, and Fordham University, Bronx, New York (Dept. of Biology). The author's summer address was the [[University of Oregon Biological Station]], Charleston, Oregon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The note deals with the thermal environment of embryos, not with wild habitat. The source does not describe the natural habitat, distribution, or ecology of *Tilapia macrocephala*, and it gives no field data. The temperature treatments are described as "abnormal" relative to the species' normal developmental conditions, but the document does not state the normal range.

The author notes that the genetic and embryological basis of meristic variation in fishes is poorly understood. This leaves the mechanism behind the temperature effect open.

## Key Findings

1. **Cold increases vertebral number.** Embryonic exposure to cold temperature appeared to increase vertebral count above the normal number. The study reports this as a trend and does not state a sample size or statistical test.
2. **Heat reduces vertebral number.** Exposure to abnormally high temperatures appeared to have the opposite effect. The statistical significance of both trends "has not been completely established."
3. **Skeletal deformities in adults.** Changes in vertebral number were associated with [[Lordosis]] (spinal curvature in the sagittal plane) or [[Scoliosis]] (lateral curvature). The X-ray plate shows a fish from an egg kept at 20 °C from fertilization to hatching, which displays both conditions. A normal fish is shown for comparison.
4. **Swimming was unaffected.** Affected fish swam normally despite the spinal abnormalities.
5. **Misleading external appearance.** Altered body form made an inexperienced observer likely to classify the fish as a different species. This has taxonomic implications, because vertebral count is used to identify species.
6. **Conclusion.** The author states that more knowledge of fish embryology and genetics, especially for *Tilapia macrocephala*, is needed before the observed phenomena can be fully understood.
