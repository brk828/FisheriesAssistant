# Observations on Spawning and Breeding Coloration of Moxostoma lachneri

## Executive Summary
This field note by Burr (1978) documents the first detailed observations of spawning behavior and breeding coloration in the [[Greater Jumprock]] (*Moxostoma lachneri*), a poorly studied catostomid endemic to the Apalachicola River drainage. Spawning was observed on 10–11 June 1976 in the Chattahoochee River, Georgia, at 19°C, in a gravel/rubble-bottomed trough with clear, rapid current. The spawning behavior—a female flanked by two males in a quivering trio—closely mirrors that of other non-nest-building *Moxostoma* species. Breeding males displayed distinctive bluish-gray fin coloration and tuberculation on the anal fin and lower caudal lobe, a color pattern notably divergent from the red/orange breeding hues typical of other *Moxostoma* subgenera.

## Background & Description
- **Standard length**: One preserved adult male measured 205 mm SL.
- **Breeding coloration**:
  - Anal and caudal fins: rich blue-gray (matching color 15 nc, Jacobson's 1945 color guide), more blue than gray.
  - Remaining fins: slate-gray with a slight orange wash (especially [[Pelvic Fins]]) and minor blue pigmentation.
  - Body: iridescent silvery-black dorsally, fading ventrally.
  - Post-preservation: blue pigment faded to gray except in the anal fin and lower caudal lobe, which retained some blue.
- **Tuberculation** (breeding tubercles):
  - Single file of small white tubercles on rays of the lower lobe of the caudal fin.
  - Large white tubercles in a single file on all rays of the anal fin.
  - No tubercles on the upper caudal lobe, head, or body.
  - R. E. Jenkins (pers. comm.) reported more extensive tuberculation in other individuals, suggesting variability.
- Tubercles are hypothesized to function in maintaining sexual contact during spawning and as a tactile stimulus for females, consistent with broader catostomid patterns (see [[Collette 1977]] review of epidermal breeding tubercles).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution**: Restricted to the Apalachicola River drainage in Alabama and Georgia (Smith-Vaniz 1968; Dahlberg and Scott 1971).
- **Habitat**: Rubble-bottomed chutes and gravel-bottomed troughs (~0.5–1 m deep, ~10 m² area) in clear, rapid water; site located under the Route 17 bridge near Nacoochee, White County, Georgia (N34°42′, W83°44′).
- **Water temperature during spawning**: 19°C, within the broader 11.1–22.2°C range reported for other *Moxostoma* species ([[Jenkins 1970]]; [[Scott and Crossman 1973]]).
- **Sympatric species**: A ripe female [[Alabama Hog Sucker]] (*Hypentelium etowanum*) was captured from the same spawning trough, with hog suckers observed in groups of three to five, likely spawning or preparing to spawn concurrently. This co-occurrence is interpreted as coincidental rather than a true interspecific spawning association, paralleling prior observations of *Hypentelium nigricans* on *Moxostoma macrolepidotum* spawning grounds ([[Burr and Morris 1977]]).
- **Social structure during spawning**: Adults congregated in groups of three to four (commonly five to seven), oriented upstream with slow upstream/downstream drift; startled individuals retreated downstream to deeper cover. No territorial or aggressive behavior was observed among individuals.

## Key Findings
1. **Spawning behavior**: Classic non-nest-building catostomid spawning act observed — two males flank a female, pressing bodies together with caudal fins contacting the female's caudal region, followed by rapid quivering and dispersal, then trio reformation. This matches patterns described for other *Moxostoma* and catostomids ([[Reighard 1920]]; [[Bowman 1970]]; [[Burr and Morris 1977]]).
2. **Reproductive timing**: Confirmed spawning condition via a ripe male that readily released milt; observed spawning dates (10–11 June) align with known late-spring/early-summer spawning timing of other moxostomatins ([[Jenkins 1970]]).
3. **Taxonomic/phylogenetic context**: *M. lachneri* belongs to subgenus [[Scartomyzon]] (Robins and Raney 1956); no prior reproductive behavior data existed for other members of this subgenus, limiting direct comparative analysis.
4. **Distinctive breeding coloration**: The bluish-gray breeding coloration of *M. lachneri* contrasts sharply with the red/orange breeding hues typical of subgenera [[Moxostoma (subgenus)]] and [[Megapharynx]], suggesting distinct evolutionary divergence in coloration signaling within the tribe Moxostomatini.
5. **Nest-building status**: *M. lachneri* is evidently a non-nest-building catostomid, aligning it behaviorally with most species in subgenus *Moxostoma*, with the notable exception of the nest-building [[River Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma carinatum*) ([[Hackney et al. 1967]]).
6. **Novelty of observations**: This note represents the first substantive report on breeding ecology, coloration, and reproductive behavior for this range-restricted species since its original description by [[Robins and Raney 1956]].
