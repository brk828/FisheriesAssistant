# USFS Livestock Grazing Management and ESA Section 7 Consultation: Questions and Answers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) document, issued from the Phoenix Ecological Services Office in October 1991, is a question-and-answer guide on how the [[Forest Service]] (USFS) must handle [[Livestock Grazing Management]] under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). It covers [[Land and Resource Management Plan]] (LMP) consultations, [[Allotment Management Plan]] (AMP) changes, [[Biological Evaluation]] (BE) procedures, [[Formal Consultation]] and [[Informal Consultation]], timelines, the binding status of [[Biological Opinion]] (BO) measures, and special rules for proposed and [[Experimental Populations]]. The guide is procedural and contains no population data. Its central message is that grazing, its management, and its physical developments must be analyzed together, because splitting them ("piecemealing") risks invalid conclusions and ESA violations.

## Background & Description

The document is a regulatory guidance text, not a research report. It is organized as 19 questions and answers addressed to USFS and FWS staff. It describes the consultation process, the three possible BE conclusions ("no effect," "may affect," "beneficial effect"), and the roles of the two agencies.

The source names only two fish species, both in a single passage on experimental populations:
- [[Woundfin]]
- [[Colorado Squawfish]]

The text states that these are the only two species with experimental populations in FWS Region 2 (AZ, NM, OK, TX). No scientific data, sample sizes, population counts, or habitat descriptions are given for either species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The ecological relevance is indirect. Livestock grazing on USFS allotments can affect listed species and [[Critical Habitat]] through several pathways:
- Livestock type, numbers, seasons of use, and pasture locations
- Physical developments such as fences, springboxes, pipelines, and troughs
- Grazing systems, such as a four-pasture rest rotation
- Cumulative, interrelated, and interdependent effects

The document stresses that open-ended management flexibility cannot be analyzed for effects on listed species. Flexibility must be bounded by specified date ranges, maximum grazing lengths, or defined alternatives. For example, a pasture might be grazed April 15 to June 15 in most years, with a cap of 75 days and no more often than every third year. The guide also notes that a three-pasture system cannot exist without fences, so a decision to implement such a system is a de facto decision to build them.

## Key Findings

**Scope of existing consultations**
- Programmatic LMP consultations assess the overall concept of grazing. Allotment-level specifics (livestock type, numbers, pastures, seasons, structures) are analyzed only if stated in the LMP and BO.

**When reinitiation or new consultation is required**
- Reinitiation is required if: (1) the incidental take amount is exceeded; (2) new information reveals unanticipated effects; (3) the LMP is modified in an unconsidered way; or (4) a new species is listed or critical habitat designated.
- Additional formal consultation is required for unmentioned actions or for specifics of actions previously proposed only in concept (for example, an AMP the LMP recommended).

**Consultation process and BE conclusions**
- The USFS prepares a BE and, if it finds "may affect," sends a request for formal consultation through its Regional Office to the FWS field office in the relevant state.
- "May affect" is triggered by any likely effect, from very minor to extirpation. Only "beneficial effect" (all effects beneficial, no residual adverse effects, no incidental take) allows formal consultation to be skipped, and it still requires written FWS concurrence.
- For USFS sensitive and candidate species, no specific ESA conclusion is required; the BE should still address impacts and mitigation.

**Documentation**
- A "may affect" request must include the BE, the draft AMP, draft NEPA documents, and maps.
- BEs for "no effect" findings need not be sent, but the FWS requests copies of all BEs.

**Timelines**
- Formal consultation is concluded within 90 days of FWS receiving the request. Region 2 normally delivers the BO at day 90; an extra 45 days is allowed when needed.
- The FWS responds to beneficial-effect concurrence requests within 30 days.

**Binding status of BO measures**
- Non-jeopardy BOs contain non-binding Conservation Recommendations, plus binding terms in the [[Incidental Take Statement]].
- Jeopardy BOs add binding Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives.
- The Reasonable and Prudent Measures and Terms and Conditions in any incidental take statement are binding.

**Disagreements and flexibility**
- If the FWS disputes a "no effect" finding, the USFS may initiate formal consultation, modify or drop the action, or proceed without consultation, though the last option risks an ESA violation notice.
- Flexibility is allowed when the boundaries of possible actions are specified and analyzed.

**Grazing systems and physical developments**
- Grazing systems should not be consulted on separately from the developments they require. Doing so creates piecemealing and may lead to reconsultation and cumulative-effect problems.
- Signing an AMP and NEPA decision notice that requires fencing and water developments precludes a true no-action alternative and may violate NEPA.

**Term permits**
- Issuing term grazing permits is a discretionary federal action, so Section 7 applies. AMP and permit consultations may be combined if all aspects are specified.

**Protection status**
- Listed populations are generally fully protected.
- Proposed species receive limited protection: the USFS must ask whether the action is likely to jeopardize them and request a [[Conference with FWS]] if so.
- Experimental non-essential populations also require a conference if jeopardy is likely. Experimental essential populations follow formal consultation rules. Status is checked in 50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12 (codes X or XN).

**Conference**
- A conference is advisory, not binding, and results in a conference report with recommendations to minimize adverse effects.
