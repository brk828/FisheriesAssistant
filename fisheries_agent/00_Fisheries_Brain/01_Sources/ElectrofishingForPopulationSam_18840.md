# Electrofishing for Population Sampling: A Selected Bibliography

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Teleostei

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[Selected Bibliography]] on [[Electrofishing]] for fish population sampling, issued as Bibliography Series No. 31 in November 1974. It was compiled by Ruth Friedman of the Research Services Branch, Office of Library Services, U.S. Department of the Interior, with cooperation from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]]. It lists 115 numbered entries grouped into five subject sections, intended as a practical reference for researchers designing their own electrofishing studies.

## Background & Description

The bibliography was compiled at the [[Natural Resources Library]] to serve people who would use electrofishing gear to sample fish populations. Its stated scope covers:

- Bibliographies on electrofishing
- Effects of electricity on organisms
- Descriptions of electrofishing equipment
- Evaluations of the technique in specific sampling situations
- Population studies using the technique

Selection rules: works primarily about commercial electrofishing were excluded, as were articles giving only slight mention of the method, very brief notes on a single application or device, and popular rather than scientific works. Only English-language literature is included, except foreign titles listed in the Bibliographies section. Books, reports and articles are integrated within each category. The compiler states that evaluation of the facts or theories presented is left to individual specialists.

**Sources consulted** include major abstracting services such as [[Biological Abstracts]], [[Commercial Fisheries Abstracts]], [[Marine Fisheries Abstracts]], [[Sport Fishery Abstracts]], the Monthly Catalog of U.S. Government Publications, and the Monthly Checklist of State Publications, plus Besterman's world bibliography of bibliographies.

**Acknowledgements** go to William H. Massmann, Joseph A. Boccardy and Robert E. Lennon of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and to state game, fish and conservation departments that supplied citations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Electrofishing is used to collect fish for population estimates, and the bibliography shows that its effects extend beyond the target fish. The Effects section covers:

- **Fish responses:** galvanotaxis and electrotaxis, including [[Electrotaxis]] thresholds for twelve coastal pelagic and bottom fishes, and responses of [[Tuna]] and tropical fish to electrical stimuli.
- **Physical injury and mortality:** mortality in salmon fingerlings exposed to pulsating current, internal injuries in [[Largemouth Bass]] and bluegill, and harmful effects on large [[Rainbow Trout]].
- **Non-target organisms:** effects of electrofishing on stream invertebrates.
- **Reproduction:** effects of shock on eggs and sperm of [[Northern Pike]].
- **Sampling bias:** effects of size selectivity and fish irritation on population estimates.
- **Environment:** the response of fish in marine and fresh water, and of trout in both settings.

Equipment development spans AC, DC and pulsed-DC systems, from [[Backpack Shocker]] units to [[Boom Shocker]] and boat-mounted designs, lake shockers, [[Electric Seine]] techniques and alternate-polarity electrodes.

## Key Findings

- **Scope and size:** 115 numbered entries, from early work such as Haskell's 1939 paper on an electrical method of collecting fish, to 1972–1973 studies.
- **Foundational references:** Haskell (1939) introduced the electrical collecting method. The 1957 Iowa State College symposium on warm water stream evaluation (Carlander, ed.) includes an electric shockers section (pp. 64–93). The 1967 symposium *Fishing with Electricity* (276 p.) is a major multidisciplinary volume.
- **Key bibliographies:** Applegate et al. (1954), Carlander (1957), Halsband and Halsband (1970), Meyer-Waarden et al. (1960), and Rawstron (1966) are among the primary bibliographic sources.
- **Effects research:** Notable entries include Klima (1972) on voltage and pulse rates for electrotaxis in twelve fishes, Elliott and Bagenal (1972) on effects on stream invertebrates, Libosvarsky (1967) on fish irritation and population estimates, and Junge and Libosvarsky (1965) on size selectivity in successive removal estimates.
- **Equipment:** Entries describe backpack shockers (Blair 1958; Haskell et al. 1954; Lowry 1964), boat and lake shockers (Loeb 1955; McCann 1966; Stubbs 1965), pulsed-DC and AC/DC designs (Moore 1968; Northrop 1962; Patten and Gillaspie 1966), and electrode designs (Petty 1955; Jones 1959; Lennon 1961).
- **Applications:** Studies include Larimore (1961) on fish populations and electrofishing success in a warm-water stream, Lewis et al. (1962) on combining shocking with mark-and-recovery to estimate largemouth bass populations, Von Geldern (1971) on fingerling largemouth bass at Lake Nacimiento, California, and Larsen (1955) on Danish trout streams.
- **Language and inclusion:** Only English titles are included, except in the Bibliographies section, which also lists foreign works, notably German and French titles by Halsband and Meyer-Waarden.
- **Purpose:** The bibliography functions as a design resource for fisheries researchers choosing gear and methods. It does not evaluate the cited work.
