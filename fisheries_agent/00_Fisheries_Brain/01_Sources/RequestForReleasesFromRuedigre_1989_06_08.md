## Executive Summary

This 1989 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) to the Bureau of Reclamation documents a water management action taken under the Endangered Species Act to benefit the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the [[15-Mile Reach]] of the [[Colorado River]]. As a condition of a 1987 biological opinion on water sales from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] and [[Green Mountain Reservoir]], Reclamation agreed to release 10,000 acre-feet of water to augment flows during the critical low-flow period (mid-August to mid-October) when habitat for adult and larval Colorado squawfish is most constrained. The document is not a primary morphological or life-history study but rather a flow-management directive informed by prior Service research on minimum instream flow requirements for the species.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide direct morphological data on the [[Colorado Squawfish]]. It references distinct **adult** and **larval** life stages, noting that both are radio-tracked/sampled and are differentially affected by flow conditions in the 15-mile reach, implying stage-specific habitat requirements (e.g., larvae are dependent on low-velocity nursery habitats, while adults use radio-telemetry-monitored mainstem habitats).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat:** The [[15-Mile Reach]] of the [[Colorado River]], a critical habitat segment for the endangered Colorado squawfish, situated between the Grand Valley diversion and the confluence with the [[Gunnison River]].
- **Flow dependency:** Habitat availability for both adult and larval Colorado squawfish is strongly tied to discharge volume, with **mid-August to mid-October** identified as the period of lowest natural flows and most limited habitat, particularly in low-water years such as 1989.
- **Baseline vs. augmented flows:** Natural flows at the upper end of the 15-mile reach were projected at ~250 cfs during the critical period; the addition of released water was expected to raise flows to ~325 cfs.
- **Minimum flow recommendation:** A prior Service report recommended a minimum flow of **700 cfs** for the 15-mile reach under normal conditions, and **600 cfs** in low-water years — figures well above even the augmented projected flow of 325 cfs, indicating a persistent habitat deficit.
- **Interconnected water management:** Flow augmentation for the squawfish in the Colorado River mainstem is linked to reservoir operations at Ruedi Reservoir on the [[Fryingpan River]], creating a trade-off with the Fryingpan's regulated trout fishery.
- **Co-occurring managed fishery:** The [[Fryingpan River]] supports a trout fishery managed by the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], which indicated that Fryingpan flows below 200 cfs would not adversely affect trout fishability, setting an operational ceiling on release rates from Ruedi Reservoir.

## Key Findings

- Reclamation committed to releasing **10,000 acre-feet (af)** of water from Ruedi and/or Green Mountain reservoirs in 1989 to benefit Colorado squawfish habitat in the 15-mile reach.
- Releases were planned in **equal daily amounts over a 62-day period** (August 15–October 15, 1989), with flexibility to adjust timing based on weather and water-use conditions.
- Requested Service releases were **capped at 100 cfs** to keep Fryingpan River flows below **200 cfs**, protecting the trout fishery per Colorado Division of Wildlife guidance.
- The planned release was expected to **augment 15-mile reach flows by approximately 75 cfs**, raising projected flows from ~250 cfs (natural) to ~325 cfs (augmented) — still substantially below the recommended minimum of 600–700 cfs.
- During the augmentation period, Service personnel planned to:
  - Monitor habitat use of **radio-tagged adult Colorado squawfish**
  - Record general habitat availability
  - Sample **larval fish community abundance and composition**, including larval Colorado squawfish
- These monitoring activities were intended to generate data to refine future-year recommendations for Ruedi/Green Mountain release strategies under the adaptive management framework established by the 1987 biological opinion.
- The memo illustrates an early example of **flow-based endangered species management** in a multi-use reservoir system, balancing squawfish habitat conservation against recreational trout fishery interests in a regulated tributary.
