# Tropisms and Instinctive Activities

#animal-behavior #invertebrate-zoology #tropisms

## Executive Summary

This is a general review by [[Wallace Craig]] (University of Maine), published in the *Psychological Bulletin* (Vol. 17, No. 6, June 1920). It summarizes recent literature on [[Tropisms]], taxes, [[Instinctive Behavior]], reflexes, locomotion, migration, rhythms, defense, immobility, fear, physiology, and neurology. Most studies concern invertebrates, with some work on birds, rodents, reptiles, and other vertebrates. Fish content is limited to a few references: flying fishes ([[Flying Fishes]]), embryonic sharks ([[Scyllium]]), and fish kept in darkness ([[Oltramare]]). The review is not focused on aquatic ecology.

## Background & Description

The review is organized by topic and cites the work of many authors. Each entry gives a brief critical comment, and the reviewer sometimes disagrees with the cited authors. Key themes include:

- **Comprehensive studies:** [[Jones]] (*Arboreal Man*, 1916) on the arboreal origin of human ancestors. [[Szymanski]]'s twelve-article series (*Pflüger's Archiv*) on innate actions, attention, and daily activity cycles.
- **Parasitism:** [[Wheeler]] (1919) on how parasitic wasps, bees, and ants evolve from their hosts.
- **Tropisms and taxes:** responses of [[Onchidium]], limpets, ascidians, myriapods, plants, and horned lizards to light, gravity, touch, and temperature.
- **Locomotion and righting reflexes:** movement mechanisms in gastropods, crustaceans, and insects, and how beetles right themselves.
- **Migration and rhythm:** bird-banding, bird migration and weather, firefly synchrony, and rhythmic behavior.
- **Defense, immobility, and fear:** color change, death feigning, and responses to cat odor in rats.
- **Physiology, neurology, and philosophy:** gonadal effects on behavior, anesthesia and temperature, brain chemistry in ataxic pigeons, and organismal theories of behavior.

Reference list: 50 items, largely from 1916–1920 journals (*Biological Bulletin*, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, *Journal of Experimental Zoology*, *Journal of Comparative Neurology*, *Bulletin biologique*, *Pflüger's Archiv*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Intertidal and shore species:** Limpets rely on rock surface relief, inclination (weight), and light direction for homing. Their reliance depends on rock roughness: rough rocks favor tactile cues, smooth rocks favor the other two. [[Onchidium]] is photonegative in the laboratory but diurnal in nature, and its heliotropism is "centrally inhibited by other stimulations" in its natural habitat.
- **Ascidian larvae:** Free-swimming period lasts "ten minutes to two hours." Larvae are briefly positively heliotropic, then become negatively heliotropic and probably positively geotropic, descending to attach.
- **Freshwater and terrestrial insects:** Apterous [[Water Strider]]s (*Gerris remigis*) that lose their pools may travel overland, but many die. Rain isolates insects on "tiny islands" in meadows, and they escape by flying, jumping, swimming, or crawling underwater.
- **Flying fishes:** [[Ahlborn]] (1916) examined their flight mechanism and concluded they "cannot fly, except by momentum."
- **Environmental cues for migration:** [[Smith]] (1917) found migration records correlated with barometric conditions and favorable winds, contrary to [[Bretscher]]'s thesis that migration is independent of weather.
- **Darkness and vertebrates:** [[Oltramare]] (1919) reported that rabbits, guinea pigs, cocks, pigeons, tortoises, frogs, tritons, and fish kept in a dark room, some for more than three months, showed no profound effect on vital condition.

## Key Findings

**Comprehensive and theoretical findings**
- [[Szymanski]] proposes that animals are divided into optical, osmatic, and tactile types. [[Tubifex]] is an extreme tactile example.
- Szymanski states that in most animals, perhaps all (including man), the "action sphere" is restricted to inborn actions, while the "receptory sphere" is comparatively unlimited. Craig does not agree.
- [[Sumner]] (1919) extends the principle of trial and error to explain adaptation and "organic purposefulness," including regeneration.
- [[Hooker]] (1919) proposes that organisms show two fundamental processes: behavior (following Le Chatelier's theorem) and assimilation (autocatalysis).

**Parasitism**
- [[Wheeler]] concludes that in wasps, bees, and ants, a parasitic species evolves from its host through a "circular process," eventually exterminating the host and shifting to a related host.

**Tropisms and taxes**
- [[Crozier]] and [[Arey]]: [[Onchidium]] is always photonegative in the laboratory, but diurnal in nature. The authors consider its heliotropism to be of no use.
- [[Piéron]]: limpets rely on rock relief most on rough rocks and on inclination and light direction on smooth rocks.
- [[Rabaud]]: the myriapod [[Schizophyllum]] is phototactic but not phototropic, and is "stenophote" (dark-living).
- [[Small]]: in [[Vicia Faba]], root-tip geotropism obeys Weber's law and depends on changes in protoplasm permeability to ions rather than statoliths.
- [[Weese]]: the horned lizard (*Phrynosoma*) responds more definitely to soil temperature gradients than to air temperature.

**Locomotion**
- [[Copeland]]: locomotion in [[Alectrion]] is due to ciliary movement, controlled by the nervous system through the tentacles.
- [[Crozier]]: *Xenophora* moves by advancing the foot, attaching by suction, and drawing the shell forward, a new gastropod locomotion type.
- [[Miller]]: the swimming-foot rate in a *Branchipus*-like crustacean increases with temperature and light, decreases with viscosity and age.
- [[Stellwaag]]: insects do not steer by moving the abdomen, and the two wings, though synchronous, may move in different planes and amplitudes.

**Righting and other reflexes**
- [[Szymanski]] found twelve righting methods across sixty beetle species, with several variations tried in turn.
- [[Ahlborn]]: flying fishes can glide only by momentum.
- [[Grave]]: ascidian larvae swim with constant rotation on their long axis, less similar to vertebrates than assumed.

**Migration**
- [[Baldwin]] (bird-banding): individuals winter in the same quarters in successive years, yearlings return to their hatching locality, and a pair of wrens "divorced" after one brood to rear new broods with new mates.
- [[Bretscher]]: bird migration occurs when the time has come, independent of temperature.

**Rhythm and cycles**
- [[Snyder and Snyder]] (1920): firefly flashing rate increases with temperature, and similar rates among individuals make synchrony possible. Shull found similar results for crickets.
- [[Szymanski]]: the ringed snake was active regularly at midday, not due to hunger or temperature.
- Adult man is "monophasic" (one activity and one rest period per nychthemeron). The human infant (1 to 10 days old) is "polyphasic," with 5 or 6 activity periods.
- [[Swindle]]: a cockatoo can be trained to beat four different rhythms with 5 to 11 beats in the measure (the source OCR reads "§ to 11").

**Defense, immobility, and fear**
- [[Crozier and Arey]]: [[Onchidium floridanum]] has two coloration types, neither clearly concealing nor warning, and repugnatorial glands that discharge irritants toward stimuli.
- [[Cowles]]: hermit crabs transfer sea anemones from old shells to new ones.
- [[Rabaud]]: "death feigning" is a special case of reflex immobility found in all arthropods, driven by tactile stimuli.
- [[Szymanski]]: in beetles, "hypnotized" immobility is passive, whereas death feigning is actively assumed.
- [[Griffith]] (1919): white rats show extreme fear on first encountering a cat. [[Kunkel]] (1919) found fear on first perceiving cat odor, but not rabbit odor, suggesting the fear is specific to the feline odor.

**Physiology and neurology**
- [[Moore]]: castrated rats with implanted ovaries show maternal behavior, and spayed females with implanted testes show male behavior.
- [[Oltramare]]: darkness reduced waste products and muscular activity and increased stored nourishment, attributed to a retinal reflex.
- [[Pantel and Sinéty]]: experimentally blinded phasmid larvae showed changes in molt number and transformation rate.
- [[Moral]]: some anesthetics act more strongly at high temperature, likely due to differential solubility in lipoids versus water.
- [[Koch and Riddle]]: brains of ataxic pigeons are chemically underdeveloped. Chemical differentiation follows the same course in pigeons, rats, and humans.
- [[Wintrebert]]: the shark [[Scyllium]] begins automatic rhythmic movements at an early embryonic stage, independent of the nervous system.
- [[Meltzer and Githens]]: in dogs, nose-licking persists after cranial blows that abolish voluntary movement and pain, indicating it is a reflex.
