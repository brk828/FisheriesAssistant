# Down with the God Squad

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1990 essay by Ted Gup argues against proposed amendments to the [[Endangered Species Act]] that would expand the powers of the [[God Squad]], a committee of political appointees that can exempt species from the act's protections when economic interests dictate. The author contends that species-by-species protection is insufficient, that anthropocentric justifications for conservation undermine the intrinsic value of species, and that decisions of such gravity should reflect society's broadest interests rather than political expediency. The essay is not a research study and does not report original field data on fish or aquatic ecosystems. Aquatic taxa appear only as examples within a policy argument.

## Background & Description

The essay opens with a hypothetical revision of Genesis in which [[Noah]] selects species for the ark on economic grounds. It then frames the real-world debate over the Endangered Species Act, which the author describes as having served for 17 years as a "911" distress line for imperiled life forms. The act is characterized as unique because it "sets no test for survival" and gives the [[Humpback Whale]] and [[Black Rhinoceros]] no more protection than the [[Noonday Snail]] or the [[Lakeside Daisy]]. An inch-long, unpigmented, eyeless shrimp found in a sinkhole near Gainesville, Florida, is cited as a recent addition to the list of imperiled species.

Officials named in the essay include Agriculture Secretary [[Clayton Yeutter]] Jr. and Interior Secretary [[Manuel Lujan]], who suggested amending the law to let the God Squad make the toughest decisions. The essay also notes that the Senate recently defeated a measure that would have empowered the God Squad to settle the dispute over timbering ancient forests.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The essay presents several concrete conflicts between economic development and species protection:

- **Pacific Northwest:** Logging jobs are pitted against the need to protect ancient forests, the habitat of the [[Spotted Owl]].
- **Southwest:** A $582 million water project was delayed because it threatened the [[Squawfish]].
- **Arizona:** A $200 million observatory was held up on behalf of roughly 150 rare [[Mount Graham Red Squirrel]]s.

Broader threats cited include logging, mining, dams, and habitat fragmentation. The author attributes much of the loss of biodiversity to underlying causes rather than individual species crises. Edward O. Wilson, a Harvard entomologist, is quoted as estimating that habitat loss in tropical rain forests is pushing at least 20,000 species a year toward extinction.

The essay also notes that fewer than 1.4 million of the earth's tens of millions of species have been named, and that many remain unexamined for their ecological role.

## Key Findings

- **Scale of listing:** The essay reports 1,116 imperiled species on the endangered list and an additional 3,600 candidate species awaiting listing, and warns that some will go extinct before being listed.
- **Extinction rate:** Estimated loss of at least 20,000 species per year in tropical rain forests, per Edward O. Wilson.
- **Policy critique:** The author argues that expanding the God Squad would effectively end the Endangered Species Act's protections, and that political appointees' decisions "dim beyond the next election."
- **Ecosystem approach:** The essay argues for anticipating the consequences of human activity and focusing on entire ecosystems, since single-species rescues do little to address causes of biodiversity loss.
- **Intrinsic value:** Justifying species only by their usefulness to humans "leaves the vast majority of species defenseless" and undermines their intrinsic worth.
- **Inclusive decision-making:** Biologists, environmentalists, theologians, historians, and industry representatives should participate in decisions about species' fates.
- **Example for other nations:** The author argues that wealthy nations must restrain their own consumption before demanding sacrifices from poorer nations, and that U.S. influence overseas will come through example rather than rhetoric.
- **Core conclusion:** The act's "final blessing" is that it preserves the elements that stir human wonder, a benefit "too precious for the God Squad to barter away."
