# San Pedro River Groundwater Decline and Growth Policy Debate

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-hydrology

## Executive Summary

This news article by [[Bill Hess]] reports on a public policy talk at the University of Arizona's Sierra Vista campus, given during the 13th Southwest Wings Birding & Nature Festival. The speaker, [[Glennon]], argues that the [[San Pedro River]] is likely to die because of continued growth in [[Sierra Vista]] and [[Cochise County]], which draws groundwater that normally feeds the river. A representative of the [[San Pedro Partnership]] and the [[National Audubon Society]] responds that the partnership has done substantial work but has not yet taken enough action. The article also warns that the water conflict could affect [[Fort Huachuca]] during the next round of [[Base Realignment and Closure]]. The document does not describe any fish, invertebrates, or other aquatic organisms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Core problem:** A serious four-year drought is compounding the impact of growth. Glennon says that when it has not rained for a while, river flow comes from [[Groundwater]], so pumping near the river directly reduces streamflow.
- **Wells:** Glennon says new wells being dug in the Sierra Vista subwatershed are halting the natural flow of water to the stream. He argues that wells close to the river harm the waterway and should be moved away from it.
- **Mitigation under way:** The partnership's representative says that conservation easements have taken some wells near the river out of production.
- **Aquifer analogy:** Glennon describes the river's problem as too many "straws" in a milkshake glass, where the glass stands for the area's [[Aquifer]]. A new user's straw would require removing an existing one.
- **Regional supply:** He says there will be no more [[Central Arizona Project]] water, which carries Colorado River water to communities such as Phoenix and Tucson, and that no more large dams will be built.
- **Legal gap:** Glennon argues that Arizona's laws are outdated because they treat groundwater and surface water as separate issues when they are one connected system. He sums this up as "a disconnect between science and the law."

## Key Findings

1. **Prognosis:** Glennon's main conclusion is that the San Pedro River is likely to die under current conditions.
2. **Drivers of decline:** Continued growth in Sierra Vista and Cochise County, combined with a four-year drought, is reducing groundwater that feeds the river.
3. **Well impacts:** Wells near the river are said to disrupt natural flow to the stream. Relocating them is proposed as a solution.
4. **Partnership progress:** The partnership reports nearly (figure illegible in scan) projects, either started or proposed, and some conservation easements already in place. Its representative accepts that "we have not done enough."
5. **Central criticism:** The partnership has spent too much effort studying the river and too little acting on it. Gerrodette agrees that it has "done a lot of talking and not enough action."
6. **Key unresolved issue:** The partnership has not dealt with growth as part of water management, which Gerrodette identifies as its main failure.
7. **Military stakes:** If local officials do not act, Gerrodette warns that [[Fort Huachuca]] could be affected in the next base closure and realignment round. The installation could be closed, reduced in size, or kept open with added missions.
8. **Policy recommendations:** Glennon calls for no new Central Arizona Project supply, no new large dams, and legal reform that joins groundwater and surface water management.
