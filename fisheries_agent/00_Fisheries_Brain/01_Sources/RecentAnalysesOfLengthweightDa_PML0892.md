# Humpback Chub Length-Weight Trends: 1996 USFWS Memorandum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A 26 September 1996 memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Flagstaff), prepared by Owen T. Gorman and Vicky J. Meretsky for [[GCES Phase II]] researchers, reports preliminary analyses of length-weight data for adult [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The longest single-site, single-season series, collected in May at the confluence of the [[Little Colorado River]] and the [[Colorado River]], indicates that adult humpback chub weights at a given length declined over the study period (1978–1996). The authors estimate a decline of roughly 13–14% at a given length. The memo asks for comments and suggestions for additional analyses, and it lists caveats about the data. Parts of the source are degraded by scan quality, so some values and sample details are unreadable and are noted as such below.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** Adult humpback chub, a native [[Colorado River Fishes]] species of the [[Grand Canyon]] region.
- **Purpose:** The analysis was being prepared as part of an integrated paper on length-weight relationships in adult humpback chub.
- **Standardized length:** Weights were modelled at a standard total length (TL) of 300 mm, with comparisons also shown for 200 mm and above.
- **Sex categories:** Analyses separated fish by sex. Figures and predicted trends refer to **nonfemale** humpback chub. The memo notes that some fish were classified as female, but the scan does not allow exact counts.
- **Data source:** The primary dataset is the May collections from the Little Colorado–Colorado confluence, the longest series from one year at one location. The memo also notes related data from several research programs, and that earlier data show the same pattern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling and data handling**
- The memo describes a dataset of several thousand fish, but the sample-size figures are illegible in the scan. The estimated number of fish captured in May is not readable.
- Length and weight were log-transformed before analysis, with length and weight recorded in millimetres (TL) and grams.
- The authors caution that the model's slope may vary from year to year, and that adding this complexity could change the output substantially. They do not believe this variation is large.

**Environmental covariate: drought**
- The [[Palmer Drought Severity Index]] (PDSI) was used as a drought measure for the Little Colorado drainage region north to the top of Arizona. Positive values indicate wet years and negative values indicate dry years.
- The memo uses a **Modified PDSI (PMDI)** from the [[National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration]] (NOAA). It is calculated as a probability-weighted average of wet and dry indices, following Heddinghaus and Sabol (1991).
- The authors had just obtained [[U.S. Geological Survey]] gage data for Cameron and Lee's Ferry, and planned to overlay these patterns on the weight trends.

**Other pressures**
- The [[Asian Fish Tapeworm]] (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*) entered the system in the early part of the decade (partly illegible in the scan). The memo treats this as a factor in the trend analysis, and the analysis was also run excluding the tapeworm years. This excluded-years trend was nearly identical to the full trend.

## Key Findings

1. **Statistical model:** Estimated log(weight) was modelled as a multiple regression of year and log(length) on log(weight).
   - Adjusted R² = 0.9388
   - Linear trend in year: t = −7.396, df = 1511, p < 0.001
2. **Weight decline:** The model indicates a decline of about 13–14% in weight at a given length (above 200 mm) over the period of record, 1978–1996. The first page's percentage is only partly legible.
3. **Robustness:** Excluding Asian fish tapeworm years gives a trend virtually identical to the trend using all years.
4. **Consistency with earlier data:** Earlier data show the same pattern.
5. **Drought:** The memo presents PMDI-weighted weight estimates to test whether drought severity explains the trend, but no quantitative result is given in the scanned text.
6. **Data caveats:** The authors note several limitations, including the single-site, single-season basis of the longest series and the uncertainty in the year-to-year slope. Comments and additional analysis suggestions were requested.
