# Western Lake Survey Phase I: Data Compendium for Selected Physical and Chemical Variables

#limnology #aquatic-ecology #water-chemistry #acid-deposition #western-lake-survey

## Executive Summary

This document is Volume II of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's [[Western Lake Survey]] - Phase I (WLS-I), part of the [[National Surface Water Survey]] under the [[National Acid Precipitation Assessment Program]]. It is a data compendium: population estimates, cumulative distributions and individual lake records for physical and chemical variables measured in lakes of the western United States, mainly those potentially sensitive to acidic deposition. Sampling was completed in fall 1985. The volume supplements Volume I, which holds the interpretation. The document contains no fish or species-level data; its focus is water chemistry and lake physical characteristics.

## Background & Description

- **Program context:** The NSWS, begun in 1983, aims to (1) determine present chemical conditions in surface waters of sensitive regions, (2) evaluate temporal variability and biological status, and (3) develop long-term monitoring. The WLS-I belongs to the first goal and is one of five projects under the [[Aquatic Effects Research Program]] (the others being the [[Direct/Delayed Response Project]], [[Watershed Manipulation Project]], [[Episodic Response Project]] and [[Long-Term Monitoring Project]]).
- **Objectives:** The WLS-I was designed to (1) determine the percentage (by number and area) and location of acidic lakes, (2) determine the percentage and location of lakes with low [[Acid Neutralizing Capacity]], and (3) describe lake-population chemistry and provide a database for selecting lakes for future study.
- **Study region:** Lakes were drawn statistically from five western mountainous subregions: California (4A), Pacific Northwest (4B), Northern Rockies (4C), Central Rockies (4D) and Southern Rockies (4E). Ten states were sampled, including California, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Oregon, Utah, Washington and Wyoming.
- **Sampling design:** One sample was collected from each lake, during fall turnover, to represent the whole lake. Weighted estimators are required because sampling intensity varied among strata; a single sampled lake may represent 3 lakes in one stratum and 36 in another.
- **Volume structure:** Section 1 (Introduction), Section 2 (population estimates and distributions for about 27 variables), Section 3 (USGS maps used), Section 4 (state location maps) and Section 5 (lake lists and individual lake data).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target population:** Lakes under 2000 ha within the target population. Lake size, watershed area and [[Water Chemistry Variables]] were measured to characterize each lake.
- **Acidity and buffering:** The primary variables are [[Acid Neutralizing Capacity]] (ANC), pH, sulfate, calcium, extractable aluminum and dissolved organic carbon. Reference values for these were the basis for risk comparisons. Physical variables and secondary variables have no reference values.
- **Aluminum speciation:** Extractable aluminum is an estimate of labile, monomeric aluminum, a form relevant to aquatic toxicity at low pH. Total aluminum was also measured.
- **Lake physical setting:** Variables include lake surface area, site depth, elevation, watershed-to-lake area ratio (WA/LA), [[Secchi Disk Transparency]], true colour and turbidity.
- **Hydrologic classes:** Lakes were classed as seepage, drainage, closed or reservoir, and stratification as mixed, weakly or strongly stratified.
- **Data-quality notes:** Negative values (other than ANC) were set to zero; 2 missing values and 54 suspected analytical errors were replaced with synthetic values; 215 duplicate samples were averaged. Users seeking unmodified data are directed to the validated data set (Data Set 3).

## Key Findings

- **Document scope and size:** The compendium is 425 pages. Section 2 presents 162 figures (2-1 to 2-162) covering about 27 variables, each broken down into region 4 and subregions 4A to 4E. Each figure gives population size (N), lake area (A), sample size, cumulative frequency and areal distribution curves, quintiles, medians and confidence limits.
- **Population size:** For lakes under 2000 ha in region 4, the estimated target population is 10,393 lakes (SE 219.4) with a total area of 181,851 ha. The subregional estimates are 2,401 (4A), 1,706 (4B), 2,379 (4C), 2,299 (4D) and 1,609 (4E).
- **Sample size:** The overall sample is 719 lakes. Subregional samples are 149 (4A), 159 (4B), 143 (4C), 129 (4D) and 139 (4E).
- **Worked example (sulfate):** At sulfate = 50 µeq L⁻¹, the estimated proportion of lakes above this value is 0.13, about 1,351 lakes, with an upper 95% confidence limit of about 1,767. The area-based proportion is 0.20 (about 36,370 ha). The document states that reading values from the curves gives only rough estimates.
- **Acidity:** Region-wide median closed-system pH is 7.16 (mean 7.28). The 4A median is 6.94 and the 4D median is 7.18. The proportion of lakes with pH below 5.0 is about 0.001 region-wide.
- **ANC:** The region-wide median is 119.35 µeq L⁻¹ (mean 527.75), and about 0.168 of lakes fall below 50 µeq L⁻¹ (roughly 1,745 lakes). The subregion 4A median is 62.63 µeq L⁻¹, reflecting lower buffering.
- **Aluminum:** The region-wide median total aluminum is 23.27 µg L⁻¹ and the median extractable aluminum is 2.80 µg L⁻¹. Extractable aluminum exceeds 50 µg L⁻¹ in very few lakes (estimated 16 lakes region-wide).
- **Dissolved organic carbon:** The region-wide median is 1.21 mg L⁻¹. Subregion 4A, with the largest area of low-DOC lakes, has a median of 0.97 mg L⁻¹.
- **Calcium and other ions:** The region-wide median calcium is 92.35 µeq L⁻¹. Median sodium is 23.85 µeq L⁻¹, median chloride 4.11 µeq L⁻¹, and median nitrate 0.38 µeq L⁻¹.
- **Physical characteristics:** The median lake area is 4.60 ha. Median site depth is 7.83 m, median elevation about 2,613 m, and median Secchi transparency 4.83 m.
- **Sample inventory:** Section 5 lists the probability-sample lakes and special-interest lakes. Lake IDs follow a seven-character code (region, subregion, alkalinity class, dash, lake number). The special-interest lakes are not part of the probability sample and cannot be used for population inference.
- **Maps:** Section 3 lists about 80 small-scale USGS maps (1:100,000 and 1:250,000) and about 200 large-scale topographic maps (1:24,000 and 1:62,500). Section 4 maps sampled lakes in ten states.
- **Stated limitation:** The WLS-I data alone cannot establish cause-and-effect between acidic deposition and surface water quality. Such relationships are the goal of later Aquatic Effects Research Program work.
