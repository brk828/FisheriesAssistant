## Executive Summary

This document is a 1993 **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS)** station profile for the **[[Fort Morgan Fish Health Center]]**, located in Fort Morgan, Colorado. Rather than describing a single species or ecosystem, the report characterizes an inland fish health support facility established in 1974 under the **[[Fish and Wildlife Restoration Act]]** and the **[[National Aquaculture Act]]**. The center's mission centers on fish disease diagnostics, inspection, and regulatory compliance across USFWS **[[Region 6]]**, supporting national fish hatcheries, tribal fisheries, military installations, and private aquaculture operations across seven states. As no biological specimen data (morphology, ecological niche of a fish taxon) is present in the source, this note documents the operational, regulatory, and institutional scope of the facility instead.

## Key Findings

- **Mandate:** Fish health inspections to ensure compliance with USFWS and multi-state/foreign fish disease policies, laws, and statutes.
- **Services Provided:** Diagnostic, therapeutic, extension, **ELISA**, and histology services for fish disease detection and management.
- **Workload:** Over 400 yearly contacts, generating approximately 300 separate case histories annually.
- **Advisory Role:** Staff serve as advisors to **ARD-FFA** on all fish health matters within Region 6.
- **Institutional Reach:** Supports national hatcheries, tribal fisheries, military fish stocks, state wildlife agencies, and ~70 private aquaculture sources across 7 states.
