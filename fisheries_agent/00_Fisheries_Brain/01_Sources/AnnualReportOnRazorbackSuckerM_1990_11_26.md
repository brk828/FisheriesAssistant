## Executive Summary

This annual report (Year 1 progress letter) documents a mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) survey of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) across multiple Colorado River Basin locations, conducted under a U.S. Bureau of Reclamation contract. The study aims to characterize genetic diversity and population structure to inform conservation management. Analysis is well advanced for the [[Lake Mohave]] population (21 fish, essentially complete) and partially advanced for the [[Green River]] (two subsamples, 28 individuals total) and [[Lake Powell]] (10 of 13 samples processed) populations. Preliminary findings reveal exceptionally high mtDNA nucleotide diversity in Lake Mohave fish, shared haplotypes between Green River and Lake Mohave individuals, and early indications of geographic differentiation in haplotype frequencies. No samples had yet been obtained from the Colorado River population near Grand Junction, other native sucker species (apart from a [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] gamete sample), or the full complement of hatchery stocks planned for comparison.

## Background & Description

This report is a genetics/management progress letter and does not describe external morphological traits of the Razorback Sucker. Note: the species is broadly recognized in the literature for its distinctive dorsal keel ("razorback") — a diagnostic character of the [[Razorback Sucker]] not elaborated upon in this text. All morphological references in the source are limited to reproductive/gonadal condition (see Ecological Niche below) rather than body-form description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range sampled:** [[Lake Mohave]], [[Green River]], [[Lake Powell]], and [[Lake Mead]] — reservoir and riverine habitats within the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Reproductive biology/timing:** Lake Mohave razorbacks were observed forming turgid ova and males yielding milt under moderate pressure as of late November 1990, indicating an early onset of breeding condition. Upstream populations (Lake Mead, parts of Lake Powell) were expected to enter breeding condition roughly 30–60 days later, suggesting a latitudinal/thermal gradient in spawning phenology across the basin.
- **Hatchery stocks referenced:** [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (1989 year class already sampled; 1990 year class pending) and [[Page Springs Hatchery]] (Arizona Game and Fish, 1989 year class pending) — used as captive-reared comparison populations for genetic characterization.
- **Sympatric species:** A single gamete sample from [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (Green River) was noted as the only non-Razorback Sucker material obtained during this reporting period.
- **Sample tissue types:** Analyses relied on eggs (≥2 ml) and concentrated milt (0.5–1 ml); many field-collected sperm samples were high-volume (~5 ml) but too dilute for full restriction enzyme analysis, constraining resolution of population-level questions.

## Key Findings

- **Enzyme panel established:** Three six-base-pair (6 bp) restriction enzymes — [[BanI]], [[BstEII]], and [[NheI]] — were identified as sufficiently polymorphic for population-level characterization, to be used alongside five four-base-pair (4 bp) enzymes ([[HinfI]], [[HinPI]], [[MboI]], [[ScrFI]], [[TaqI]]).
- **Lake Mohave population:** Analysis of 21 individuals is virtually complete. Nucleotide diversity was calculated at **0.97** — among the highest values recorded for any vertebrate — suggesting a large effective population size (consistent with mark-recapture estimates) and no recent genetic bottleneck.
- **Green River population:** Two subsamples analyzed:
  - Wild-fish progeny sample (18 individuals from ≥7 females) yielded **7 distinct haplotypes**, though pseudoreplication from shared maternal parentage limits firm population-genetic conclusions.
  - A second sample of 10 mtDNAs was of variable quality; 6 bp enzyme patterns were obtainable for nearly all, but 4 bp enzyme resolution was unreliable due to low tissue/DNA quantity.
- **Lake Powell population:** 13 samples collected, 10 processed. Reliable 6 bp enzyme scoring achieved for 9 of 10 individuals; only 2 were fully characterized with 4 bp enzymes due to sample limitations.
- **Cross-population comparison:** Considerable mtDNA similarity was observed across all sampled locations, with some Green River haplotypes identical to Lake Mohave haplotypes. However, preliminary geographic patterning in 6 bp haplotype distribution suggests some degree of population differentiation — though sample sizes remain too small for definitive conclusions.
- **Technical/logistical constraints:** Successful mtDNA isolation requires a minimum of 2 ml eggs or 0.5–1 ml concentrated milt; many field sperm samples, while high in volume, were too dilute for 4 bp enzyme analysis, necessitating resampling efforts.
- **Sample inventory (as of 12 Nov 1990):**
  - Lake Mohave: 25 samples (23 egg-derived, 2 sperm-derived) — 21 complete, 4 unprepped.
  - Lake Powell: 13 samples — 1 complete, 8 requiring 4 bp rerun, 1 requiring full rerun, 1 not surveyed (insufficient sample), 2 unprepped.
  - Green River (non-hatchery): 10 samples, all requiring 4 bp enzyme reruns (one requiring full rerun).
  - Lake Mead: 3 samples (2 milt, 1 egg), all unprepped; one sperm sample contaminated with eggs.
- **Downstream data integration:** Tissue samples will subsequently be forwarded to Don Buth for complementary allozyme analysis (target: 50 fish), enabling combined mtDNA/allozyme datasets for management-relevant population structure assessment of the [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Outstanding sampling gaps:** No specimens yet obtained from the Colorado River population near [[Grand Junction]]; additional hatchery year classes (1990 Dexter, 1989 Page Springs) still pending authorization and shipment.
