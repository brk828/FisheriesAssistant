## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper but the **minutes of the August 2, 1995 meeting of the Southeast Arizona Native Fish Team**, an interagency working group (AGFD, USFWS, USFS, BLM, Bureau of Reclamation, University of Arizona, Arizona State University) coordinating native fish conservation projects on public lands in southeastern Arizona. The meeting focused on prioritizing nine candidate conservation/restoration projects targeting imperiled native fishes—principally the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and [[Gila Chub]]—threatened largely by competition and predation from non-native species such as the [[Green Sunfish]]. Two projects were selected for action in the 1996 fiscal year: (1) enhancement of [[Gila Chub]] in [[Sabino Creek]] via green sunfish removal, and (2) initiation of a Habitat Conservation Plan for [[Rio Yaqui Fish Recovery Plan|Rio Yaqui fishes]] in [[West Turkey Creek]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** and **[[Yaqui Topminnow]]**: Small-bodied livebearing fishes native to desert spring/stream systems of the Gila and Rio Yaqui basins; historically widespread but now restricted to isolated refugia due to habitat degradation and invasive species pressure.
  - A discrete population occurs in the **[[Red Rock Canyon]]** watershed south of Sonoita, AZ, targeted for enhancement.
  - Yaqui Topminnow populations occur on private land in **[[West Turkey Creek]]**, Chiricahua Mountains, part of the Rio Yaqui drainage.

- **[[Gila Chub]]**: A cyprinid native to the Gila River basin, occupying pools and low-velocity stream reaches. The population in **[[Sabino Creek]]**, Santa Catalina Mountains, is imperiled by the presence of the non-native **[[Green Sunfish]]**, a predatory/competitive centrarchid; removal of green sunfish was proposed as the primary enhancement strategy.

- **Non-native fish species** (unspecified assemblage) inhabit the **[[Gila River]]**, **[[San Pedro River]]**, and **Central Arizona Project (CAP) canals**, prompting a dedicated Bureau of Reclamation monitoring program set to begin September 1, 1995.

- Broader habitat-scale conservation efforts were proposed for:
  - **[[Sycamore Canyon]]** (Atascosa Mountains, Coronado National Forest, Nogales Ranger District) — watershed management plan.
  - **[[Cienega Creek]]** (BLM Tucson Resource Area) — stream modification/rehabilitation project.
  - **[[Mescal Creek]]** (Mescal Mountains, BLM Phoenix Resource Area) — stream renovation.

These sites collectively represent isolated headwater and canyon-stream refugia characteristic of southeastern Arizona's fragmented native fish habitat, where small population sizes and non-native species introductions are the dominant ecological stressors.

## Key Findings

- The **Southeast Arizona Native Fish Team** was established to coordinate cross-agency (AGFD, USFWS, USFS, BLM, BOR, universities) native fish conservation planning, budgeting, and administration on public lands.
- Nine candidate projects were identified for the FY1996 budget cycle, spanning recovery plan revisions, habitat rehabilitation, non-native species removal, and non-native species monitoring.
- **Two projects were prioritized** for FY1996 implementation:
  1. **[[Gila Chub]] enhancement in [[Sabino Creek]]** via removal of invasive **[[Green Sunfish]]**.
  2. **Initiation of the [[Rio Yaqui Fish Recovery Plan]]**, beginning with a Habitat Conservation Plan for the [[West Turkey Creek]] population on private land in the Chiricahua Mountains.
- A parallel non-native fish monitoring program (Bureau of Reclamation) was slated to begin September 1, 1995, covering the **[[Gila River]]**, **[[San Pedro River]]**, and **CAP canals**.
- A proposal was raised (Project 9) to develop a shared interagency computer database for native fish data, modeled after the "Run Wild" database, reflecting recognition of fragmented data management across agencies.
- The team agreed to reconvene in **March 1996** at the Casa Grande Public Library to continue annual project planning, establishing an annual cycle: broad project brainstorming in Q1 (Jan–Mar), followed by prioritization meetings later in the year (as with this August meeting).
- Key participating agencies/individuals included AGFD (Will Hayes, Kirk Young, Rob Bettaso), USFWS (Doug Duncan), USFS (Jerry Stefferud, Deborah Bieber), University of Arizona (Bill Matter, O. Eugene Maughn), Arizona State University ([[W.L. Minckley]]), Bureau of Reclamation (Rob Clarkson), and BLM (Dan Langhorst, Jeff Simms) — indicating strong multi-institutional collaboration typical of Southwestern native fish conservation efforts in the mid-1990s.
