# Personal Letter on Manuscript Review, Aravaipa Food-Habit Data, and Research Plans (1972)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #blenniidae

## Executive Summary

This is a personal letter dated 1 August 1972, written from a research post at [[CSIRO]] in Australia to a former mentor addressed as "Mink." The author asks the recipient to review a short manuscript of about nine pages, proposes working up old [[Aravaipa Creek]] food-habit data, describes the marine invertebrates and small fishes under study, reports a leadership change at the division, and outlines personal plans to extend the stay in Australia and possibly travel via Russia. The letter contains no new quantitative data, and its scientific content is limited to general remarks about the organisms and the research environment.

## Background & Description

- **Format:** Informal correspondence, partly OCR-degraded. The author describes a chronic difficulty with writing, which delayed a holiday-season note.
- **Author's work:** The author was hired to work on shrimp rather than fish, though the author states a preference for fish. Shrimp are described as plentiful, which the author links to a practical benefit in lab meals.
- **Organisms mentioned:**
  - **Shrimp:** The main study group. The letter praises their diversity and "fantastic" range of adaptations.
  - **Blenny:** A small fish the author particularly likes, described colloquially as "an aquatic going sabre-tooth tiger without legs." No species name is given.
  - **Aravaipa fishes:** Food-habit data from [[Aravaipa Creek]] collected earlier, outside the author's current division.
- **Institutional setting:** The author works within a division of [[CSIRO]] and refers to a head office with computer facilities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Food habits:** The letter proposes reanalysing older [[Aravaipa Creek]] food-habit data, which would be a useful source of dietary information for stream fishes if the author lacks time for new fieldwork.
- **Adaptations:** The author notes that the organisms show a striking variety of adaptations, though no specific adaptations are described.
- **Division priorities:** The author states that the division's long-standing emphasis was on physical and chemical oceanography, which left the biological side comparatively weak. The author hopes that the new leadership will redirect the division toward biology.
- **Visiting researchers:** CSIRO is said to encourage visiting professors, at least in other divisions, which the author suggests the recipient consider for a sabbatical year.

## Key Findings

- **Manuscript review:** A roughly nine-page manuscript, worked on intermittently since the author's arrival, is submitted for the recipient's review.
- **Data reuse is encouraged:** Informal consensus among division researchers is that working up older data obtained outside the division is acceptable, provided it does not interfere with current duties. Head office computing facilities are available for this work.
- **Research focus:** The author works mainly on [[Shrimp]], though fish, especially the [[Blenny]], are a preferred interest.
- **Leadership change:** A new chief, Allen, is in place. The author hopes he will give the division "new direction and vitality." The author also describes a group of unproductive staff and an outdated emphasis on physical and chemical oceanography.
- **Personal plans:**
  - The author will stay at least one extra year to produce publications.
  - If the family returns home, the author plans to travel via Russia to work or study with a colleague for three to six months, depending on funding.
  - Return depends on job availability at the time.
- **Invitation:** The recipient is invited to visit Australia during a future sabbatical.
