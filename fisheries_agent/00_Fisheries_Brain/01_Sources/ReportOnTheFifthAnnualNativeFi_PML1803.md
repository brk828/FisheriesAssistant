# Fifth Annual Native Fish Roundup on Lake Havasu, AZ-CA (14-18 February 2005)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report documents the fifth annual [[Native Fish Roundup]] on [[Lake Havasu]], coordinated by the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] (AZFRO) in Parker, Arizona, from 14 to 18 February 2005. Multiple agencies surveyed the reservoir for native fishes, especially [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] team sampled the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] arm, and other agencies surveyed the remaining sections. No native fish were taken in the AZFRO nets. The [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR) and [[U.S. Geological Survey]] (USGS) crew caught 588 fish of 13 species, including 5 bonytail chub and 2 razorback suckers. An aerial survey also found a sucker aggregation near [[Park Moabi]].

## Background & Description

The roundup began in 2000 and has become an annual event. Participating agencies include the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), the California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG), the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), USBR, USGS, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The reservoir was divided into three survey areas:

- **USBR and USGS:** from Castle Rock Cove to Park Moabi.
- **AGFD:** from Contact Point to the northern end of the Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge (BWRNWR).
- **AZFRO (MFRO):** the Bill Williams River arm, within BWRNWR.

The report covers AZFRO's collections for 14-18 February 2005 and summary data from the other cooperating agencies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Lake Havasu is a Colorado River reservoir shared by Arizona and California. The survey targets native species, several of which are federally listed, and tracks their movements and aggregations, including spawning sites. Non-native and sportfish species made up most of the catch in every net survey.

## Key Findings

**Methods**

- **Aerial survey (16 February, 1000-1400 hrs):** A [[USBR]] helicopter searched for spawning razorback sucker aggregations. The flight ran from Lake Havasu City down the Arizona shore to Parker Dam, then up the California shoreline to just above the entrance to Park Moabi.
- **Net sampling (AZFRO):** Six [[Trammel Net]]s (100 m long, as scanned from the OCR text) were set at sites in and next to BWRNWR. Nets were set in the early evening and run in the early morning. GPS coordinates were recorded for each net.
- **Fish processing:** Native fish were measured for total length (mm), weighed (g), and scanned for a [[PIT Tag]] or [[Coded-Wire Tag]] (CWT). If no PIT tag was found, one was inserted. Fish with a CWT were noted and PIT-tagged. Sportfish and non-native fish were counted only. All fish were released at the capture site.

**Results: AZFRO net surveys (Bill Williams River arm)**

- Eleven species were captured, listed in decreasing order of abundance: [[Common Carp]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], and [[Green Sunfish]].
- **No native fish were captured in 2005.** Daily and weekly totals are in the report's Table 1. Parts of the scanned table are illegible, so exact totals should be checked against the original.

**Results: Helicopter survey**

- An aggregation of either flannelmouth sucker or razorback sucker was seen just above Park Moabi in the upper river. The location was passed to the USBR crew working that area.

**Results: AGFD, CDFG, and BLM**

- No native fish were taken by this group in 2005. CDFG and AGFD ran the camp, and BLM could not participate that year.

**Results: USBR-USGS (Castle Rock Cove to Park Moabi)**

- 39 trammel net nights produced a total catch of **588 fish representing 13 species**.
- Catch by species, in decreasing order: redear sunfish (159), bluegill (108), carp (108), largemouth bass (91), yellow bullhead (71), channel catfish (20), striped bass (17), bonytail chub (5), razorback sucker (2), threadfin shad (2), goldfish (2), black crappie (2), and one smallmouth bass.
- Native species in this catch: **5 bonytail chub and 2 razorback suckers.** This conflicts with the AZFRO finding of no native fish, which the report does not reconcile.
