# Notropis rafinesquei: A New Shiner from the Yazoo River System, Mississippi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This issue of the [[Bulletin Alabama Museum of Natural History]] (Number 10, June 1, 1991) contains three papers. The main and most detailed paper, by [[Royal D. Suttkus]], describes *Notropis rafinesquei* (Yazoo Shiner), a new cyprinid from the Yazoo River system of Mississippi. The species is endemic to that drainage and belongs to the [[Longirostris Species Complex]], alongside [[Notropis Ammophilus]], [[Notropis Longirostris]], and [[Notropis Sabinae]]. The paper combines morphometric, meristic, pigmentation, distribution, and habitat data, and provides a dichotomous key to the complex.

The other two papers are listed only by title and authors in the supplied text: "Reproductive Behavior of *Exoglossum* Species" (Maurakis, Woolcott, and Sabaj) and "*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*, a New Sturgeon from the Mobile Basin of Alabama and Mississippi" (Williams and Clemmer). Their contents are not present in this document, so they are not summarized further.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The paper follows the 1980 common and scientific names list for generic groupings in Cyprinidae. *Notropis ammophilus* had been described shortly before (Suttkus and Boschung, 1990), and the new species was an undescribed form within the same complex.

**Type material.** The holotype is TU 157264, an adult tuberculate male (38.2 mm SL) collected 12 May 1985 from Teoc Creek, a tributary of the [[Yalobusha River]] at Mississippi Highway 35, Carroll County. Type material totals 1838 specimens measuring 18 to 43 mm SL. Other examined material includes 7931 specimens in 92 collections from 75 additional sites in the Yazoo system. Of the paratypes, 429 were collected with the holotype.

**Diagnosis.** The species has 4-4 pharyngeal teeth and 7 anal fin rays. It is distinguished mainly by coloration: xanthic (yellowish) pigment is concentrated at the base of each fin, and it has a unique xanthic cleithral bar running from the pectoral fin base to the upper end of the opercular opening. In nuptial males this bar extends forward to the posterior rim of the orbit. A distinct dark basal-caudal spot is separated from the lateral stripe by a pale area. Meristically, it has the lowest mean pectoral ray count (13.2), the highest mean lateral line scale count (35.3), and the highest mean predorsal diagonal scale count (15.7) in the complex.

**Morphology.** The body is moderately slim and short, with an arched dorsal profile and a slightly curved venter, which the author links to a benthic habitat. The head is nearly conical, and its width and depth are less than in other complex members. Sexual dimorphism is marked: males show well-developed head and pectoral tuberculation and a longer anal fin anterior ray structure, and nuptial females have flexed anterior anal rays. Lateral line completeness is variable. In a sample of 20 specimens, 6 had a complete lateral line, while the rest had one to six unpored scales.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Range.** *N. rafinesquei* is endemic to the Yazoo River system. It occurs in the upper tributaries, from the Loess or Bluff Hills physiographic region eastward and northeastward into the North Central Plateau. It has not been found west of the Bluff Hills in the Yazoo Delta.

**Habitat.** The species usually occupies small to medium streams with moderate current and sand or fine gravel substrate. Type locality Teoc Creek is 3 to 9 m or more wide, with clear water ranging from riffles a few centimeters deep to pools several meters deep. A plunge pool below the Hwy 35 culvert reaches just over 1 m deep. The species is gregarious, and spawning aggregations were observed in Teoc Creek on 12 May 1985.

**Co-occurrence.** *N. rafinesquei* and *N. sabinae* co-occur at numerous sites. *N. rafinesquei* was taken at all 15 sites where *N. sabinae* was found in the Yazoo system. The two differ in stream-size preference: *N. rafinesquei* is a small-stream species, while *N. sabinae* prefers larger streams. At Sand Creek near Carrollton, one low-water collection yielded 10 *N. rafinesquei* and 73 *N. sabinae*. At Cane Creek, the upper site yielded 116 *N. rafinesquei* and 4 *N. sabinae*, and the lower site yielded 63 and 109, respectively.

**Associated fauna.** Five type-locality collections contained 3430 specimens. Of these, 1788 (52.1%) were *N. rafinesquei*. Associated species included *Erimyzon oblongus*, *Notropis camurus*, *Pimephales notatus*, *Micropterus salmoides*, *Lepomis macrochirus*, and *Etheostoma whipplei*, among others.

**Isolated populations.** *N. longirostris* occurs in the lower Yazoo, and *N. ammophilus* occurs in the upper Skuna River system, both isolated from the rest of the complex.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *N. rafinesquei* is a new, endemic shiner of the Yazoo River system, named in honor of [[Constantine Samuel Rafinesque]] (1783–1840).
- **Closest relative:** It most resembles *N. ammophilus* in body proportions and melanophore pigmentation. It differs in xanthic pattern, fin-ray and scale counts, and the presence of a basal-caudal spot. The submarginal lateral line pigment seen in *N. ammophilus* is absent or poorly developed in *N. rafinesquei*.
- **Meristic differences (Table 3 means):** Pectoral rays 13.18 (n = 110) versus 14.16 in Yazoo *N. ammophilus*. Lateral line scales 35.33 versus 33.72. Predorsal diagonal scale rows 15.74 versus 13.50. Body circumferential scale rows 23.49 versus 21.40 in *N. ammophilus*.
- **Vertebrae:** *N. rafinesquei* averages 35.43 (n = 131). *N. ammophilus* from the Alabama River averages 34.54 (n = 109). The paper notes that *N. rafinesquei* usually has 35 or 36 vertebrae, while *N. longirostris* usually has 36 or 37.
- **Body shape:** *N. rafinesquei*, *N. ammophilus*, and *N. sabinae* are short-bodied forms, while *N. longirostris* is elongate. *N. rafinesquei* has smaller head, body, and peduncle proportions than the other two short-bodied species.
- **Geographic variation:** No significant variation was found among tributary population samples.
- **Range records:** The paper gives the first published records of *N. longirostris* and *N. sabinae* from the Yazoo River system. It also gives the first record of *N. sabinae* east of the Mississippi River.
- **Identification key:** The paper provides a key to the complex separating *N. ammophilus*, *N. rafinesquei*, *N. sabinae*, and *N. longirostris* by pigmentation, pharyngeal tooth formula, breeding tubercle pattern, mental bib presence, and body depth (under or over 20% of SL).
- **Pharyngeal teeth:** *N. rafinesquei*, *N. ammophilus*, and *N. sabinae* invariably have 4-4 teeth. *N. longirostris* is variable, with 50 Yazoo specimens showing several formulae.
