# Mezquital Pupfish and the Cyprinodon eximius Complex: Correspondence and Taxonomic Notes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a bundle of correspondence from [[Robert R. Miller]] (Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan) to [[Paul C. Marsh]] (Arizona State University), followed by a taxonomic paper, "Four New Pupfishes of the Cyprinodon eximius Complex" (1976). The letters concern captive culture, conservation status, and a planned joint paper on the [[Mezquital Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon meeki*). The 1976 paper revises the *C. eximius* complex of the Chihuahuan Desert, describes four new species, and provides a diagnostic key, species accounts, and morphometric data. Most of the text is taxonomic and the correspondence is informal, so the note emphasizes the most important entries.

## Background & Description

The letters document personal communications and work on pupfishes between 1989 and 1993. Miller notes that he had worked on the genus *Cyprinodon* since the 1940s, when [[Carl L. Hubbs]] turned over his notes on the group. In the 1976 paper, Miller and Marsh state that the seven species in the complex (four new) are closely related to *C. eximius*, the most widely distributed species in the group. The complex is distinguished by a broad black terminal caudal bar on nuptial males, a yellow to orange dorsal fin in nuptial males, and lacking mandibular pores.

Species accounts in the paper include:
- *C. eximius* (Conchos Pupfish), Rio Conchos basin, Chihuahua and Texas, with synonyms and historical misidentifications listed.
- *C. atrorus* (Bolsón Pupfish), Cuatro Ciénegas basin, Coahuila.
- *C. lutilusciatus* (Parras Pupfish), Parras basin, Coahuila, now extinct.
- *C. nazas* (Nazas Pupfish), Nazas and Aguanaval drainages, Durango and Zacatecas.
- *C. alvarezi* (Potosí Pupfish), spring at El Potosí, Nuevo León, reduced by introduced [[Largemouth Bass]].
- *C. macrolepis*, Ojo de la Hacienda Dolores, Chihuahua.
- *C. meeki* (Mezquital Pupfish), Río Mezquital basin, Durango.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The *C. meeki* habitat includes streams, springs, ponds, and spring-fed ditches at depths to 1.3 m (generally no more than 0.5 m), over clay, mud, sand, gravel, rock, and silt. Water temperatures in warm springs reach 29.5 °C. Vegetation includes algae, *Potamogeton*, and *Nasturtium*. Because the species lives at high elevation, its reproductive season may be later and more restricted than usual for pupfishes. In a pond at 1,845 m, water was 8 °C on 15 April. Captive spawning in Tempe, Arizona, occurred mainly from October to December.

Threats are habitat destruction, pollution, predation by largemouth bass, and competition with exotic species. The species was found at only three localities in 1983 and is considered threatened. The 1989 letter states that it should be listed as endangered by the IUCN and the US federal list.

Pupfish populations in the *C. eximius* complex are restricted and mostly of limited distribution. One species is extinct and another may be.

## Key Findings

- **Captive culture and reproduction:** Miller's captive *C. meeki* stock, held from 1983 onward, produced F1 young in the outdoor pond. Adults reproduced in autumn, and by spring 1984 there were 15–40 young. The stock declined after a June 1987 pesticide treatment of the pond, which killed all aquatic vertebrates and invertebrates. The letter reports that the outdoor pond produced its F1 young in late summer and autumn.
- **Field observation gap:** The October–December spawning of captive fish is unique to this species, according to Miller. No one had observed spawning in its natural habitat.
- **Conservation status:** The species is threatened and warrants endangered status. A 1990 EPA-sponsored symposium on early life biology of threatened species was proposed as a venue for a joint paper on adult reproductive behavior.
- **Taxonomic revision:** The 1976 paper provides a key to seven species of the complex, based on terminal caudal bar width, gill raker count, scale counts, vertebrae, mandibular and lacrimal pores, and dorsal fin color. Four species are new.
- **Nomenclatural note:** Miller corrected a claimed first North American record of *Gambusia* (cichlid) *managuense*, noting that it had occurred in Mexico before the Utah collection. He also suggested the giant guapotes may belong in their own genus.
- **Holotype data:** *C. meeki* holotype is UMMZ 197420, an adult male 37.6 mm SL, from a hot-spring-fed pond tributary to Río del Tunal near Durango, collected 3 August 1952.
