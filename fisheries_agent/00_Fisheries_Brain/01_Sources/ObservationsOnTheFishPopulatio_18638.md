# Observations on the Fish Population of Willow Creek Reservoir, Montana

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 study by James A. Posewitz, published in the *Proceedings of the Montana Academy of Sciences*, examines the trout fishery of [[Willow Creek Reservoir]], a cold-water impoundment in Madison County, Montana. Field work ran from March through November 1960. The study covers the life history, distribution, and harvest of [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) and [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), with supporting data on [[Brook Trout]] and native suckers. The authors report that natural reproduction occurred only in Willow Creek, that hatchery-origin fish made up a large share of the catch, and that trout growth and size were declining. The reservoir supported a harvest estimated at 14,868 fish in 1960.

## Background & Description

The study was motivated by a shortage of published work on reservoirs with cold-water fish populations; the author cites prior Colorado studies by Nelson (1955) and Sharpe (1958). The project was funded under the Federal Aid to Fish Restoration Act (Project F-9-R-9) through the [[Montana Fish and Game Department]].

The reservoir was formed by an earth-filled dam across Willow Creek, a tributary of the Jefferson River, which is one of three headwater tributaries of the [[Missouri River]]. Filled first in 1938, it covers 868 acres at full pool, holds 17,760 acre-feet, and reaches a maximum depth of 74 feet. Summer irrigation drawdown lowered the water level by about 15 feet during the study. The main inflows are Willow Creek (88.4 sq mi drainage, mean flow 43.1 c.f.s.) and Norwegian Creek (34.6 sq mi, 7.5 c.f.s.), plus the intermittent Dry Hollow Creek.

Fish species recorded in the reservoir were rainbow trout, brown trout, brook trout, mountain whitefish, white sucker, longnose sucker, longnose dace, and mottled sculpin. Only the three trout species were non-native to the drainage. Stocking records of rainbow trout from 1953 to 1960 are documented, and an egg-taking trap on Willow Creek has operated since 1950.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry and temperature.** The reservoir water was relatively clear, even during runoff. Surface temperatures ranged from 78°F (July 22) to 32°F under ice cover (December through March). No marked thermal stratification was observed. Alkalinity measured 13 ppm (phenolphthalein) and 127 ppm (methyl orange), with pH 7.3. Tributary chemistry (Table I) showed total hardness of 250 ppm for Willow Creek and 281 ppm for Norwegian Creek, with total dissolved solids of 370 and 385 ppm, respectively.

**Vegetation.** Rooted aquatic vegetation occurred at depths under 10 feet but was largely exposed by drawdown.

**Spawning habitat.** Tributaries provided spawning areas for most reservoir fishes. Willow Creek was blocked by a trap, while Norwegian and Dry Hollow Creeks were sampled by electrofishing over 300-foot reaches.

**Temperature and distribution.** Depth-profile temperatures (Table XII) recorded surface values of 63.7°F (June 14), 76.3°F (July 22), and 63.7°F (September 12), with bottom values falling to about 54–59°F at depth. Brown trout were most abundant below 30 feet, but no clear temperature relationship was found for them in June or September.

## Key Findings

**Reproduction and spawning**
- Rainbow trout ran into Norwegian and Dry Hollow Creeks in spring 1960. Electrofishing produced 207 male and 124 female rainbow trout in Norwegian Creek and 71 male and 31 female in Dry Hollow Creek.
- The Willow Creek trap captured 474 rainbow trout (235 males, 239 females) between April 1 and May 8, 1960, yielding 279,800 eggs.
- Egg yields declined from 3,585,600 eggs (1953) to 279,800 (1960), and females stripped fell from 1,830 (1955) to 239 (1960). Norwegian Creek contributed an additional 935,088 eggs in 1959.
- Only Willow Creek showed successful reproduction. Fry trapping in Norwegian Creek (June 3–August 17) captured no trout, and Dry Hollow Creek was dewatered on May 28.
- Brown trout spawned in Willow Creek from October 4 to November 2, with 10 redds observed in about two miles of stream.

**Hatchery influence**
- Of rainbow trout in the spawning run, 32 percent (192 fish) were identified as hatchery fish from fin deformities. Among 12- and 13-inch fish, 55 percent and 52 percent respectively had deformed fins.
- In gill nets, 488 of 609 rainbow trout (80.1 percent) were presumed hatchery fish from the 1959 and 1960 plantings.
- Of rainbow trout examined in the creel census, 71.4 percent had deformed fins.

**Size and growth**
- Average spawning-run rainbow trout length declined from 20.42 inches and 3.08 pounds (1954) to 16.19 inches and 1.38 pounds (1960).
- Calculated rainbow trout length at annulus III was 12.93 inches in 1960, compared with 15.6 inches in 1954 and 14.9 inches in 1949. Growth in 1960 was lower than in earlier years.
- Brown trout reached 24.40 inches at annulus VIII in the reservoir. The largest specimen was 25.0 inches and 7.50 pounds.
- Growth of brown trout in Willow Creek Reservoir exceeded that reported by Bishop (1955) for ages II–VII.

**Movement and distribution**
- Of 3,210 fish marked (2,500 hatchery and 710 wild), 31 of the 710 tributary-marked fish were recovered. One fish moved about 43 miles, the longest movement recorded.
- Hatchery fish planted in Willow Arm (2,500 fin-clipped, May 16) yielded only 23 recoveries, mostly by gill netting.
- Rainbow trout distribution correlated with temperature: they were evenly spread when warm and concentrated near the surface when cool.

**Gill netting (1960)**
- Three series of 20 overnight sets (June, July, September) captured 5,456 fish. Suckers made up 83.83 percent (4,574), rainbow trout 69.05 percent (609), brown trout 30.04 percent (265), brook trout 0.68 percent (six), and mountain whitefish 0.23 percent (two).
- Brown trout were most abundant in bottom sets below about 24–30 feet. Rainbow trout were concentrated in surface sets during the September series (79 percent).

**Angler harvest**
- The fishery opened May 22 to July 15, 1960, and was limited to Norwegian Arm. An estimated 14,868 fish were taken (90 percent confidence interval 11,231–18,505).
- 2,076 anglers were interviewed. Boat anglers averaged 4.31 fish per person (0.850 per hour) and shore anglers 2.67 (0.455 per hour). Overall success was 4.03 fish per angler (0.775 per hour).
- The catch was 97.27 percent rainbow trout, 2.47 percent brown trout, and 0.26 percent brook trout.

**Overall conclusions**
- Hatchery stocking supplied much of the trout harvest, but natural reproduction was limited to Willow Creek.
- Declining egg yields, smaller spawning-run fish, and slower growth suggest a fishery under pressure.
