# Population Trends and Distributions of Flannelmouth Sucker and Bluehead Sucker in the Lower Green River, 2001–2003

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR) final report (Publication Number 05-08, June 2004) documents three years of spring electrofishing (2001–2003) on the [[Lower Green River]] to estimate adult populations, distribution, and life history of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*). Both species occupy about 50% of their former range in the Upper Colorado River Basin. Sampling was run alongside [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] monitoring for the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]].

Flannelmouth sucker population estimates were 35,232 (2001), 30,495 (2002) and 18,624 (2003). The short-term trend was decreasing but not statistically significant. Too few recaptures were obtained for a bluehead sucker population estimate. Recapture rates were insufficient to detect changes of even 50% in abundance, so the report recommends separate, sucker-focused mark-recapture work.

## Background & Description

- **Conservation context:** The two species were assessed under a range-wide [[Conservation Agreement]] signed in early 2004 by AZ, NV, UT, WY, CO and NM. A follow-on [[Conservation Strategy]] was due by December 2004. The aim is to maintain and expand populations and avoid listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Objectives:** (1) determine distribution in the lower Green River; (2) estimate adult population size; (3) examine movement, reproduction and other life history traits.
- **Study area:** The reach runs from RM 120 to RM 0, passing through [[Labyrinth Canyon]] and [[Stillwater Canyon]]. Habitat is mainly homogeneous runs with gravel riffles and large eddies. Sediment from the [[San Rafael River]] (RM 97) creates silt/sand substrate for about 10–15 river miles. The Tusher Diversion area (RM 120–128) was not sampled for logistical reasons.
- **Sampling:** Three or four electrofishing passes per year, using two boats (one per shoreline) fitted with pulsed DC Coffelt or Smith-Root 5.0 GPP units. Fish were measured, weighed, scanned for PIT tags, and tagged if larger than 150 mm. Sampling occurred before and during spring runoff.
- **Analysis:** Closed-population estimates were made in [[Program CAPTURE]] with the M0 (null) estimator selected for all years. CPUE was analysed with two-way ANOVA and Holm-Sidak comparisons across three reach segments (RM 0–39, 40–79, 80–120). Movement was tested with one-way ANOVA across size classes, and unit comparisons used paired t-tests.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat preferences:** Both species were captured far more often in deep, low-to-moderate flow habitats (large pools, mid-channel runs). Bluehead suckers are also associated with rocky substrate and moderate-to-fast current. The authors state that [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] sampling, which focuses on shorelines, backwaters and flooded tributaries, under-samples these preferred habitats. This is the main reason recaptures were scarce.
- **Substrate:** From RM 120 to RM 97 the substrate is mainly cobble. Below the San Rafael mouth it is mainly sand. The authors link this change to forage and spawning habitat, and to the concentration of catch and spawning in the upper reach.
- **Regional comparison:** Distribution, length frequency, growth, reproduction and movement were similar to earlier reports for mainstem sections of the Upper Colorado River Basin, including [[Holden and Stalnaker 1975]] and [[McAda and Wydoski 1985]].

## Key Findings

**Flannelmouth Sucker**
- **Population estimates (Table 1, Mo estimator):**
  - 2001: N = 35,232 (profile likelihood interval 18,420–81,693; CV 0.37; p-hat 0.006)
  - 2002: N = 30,495 (interval 20,152–50,158; CV 0.23; p-hat 0.014)
  - 2003: N = 18,624 (interval 11,149–34,128; CV 0.28; p-hat 0.015)
- The trend slope did not differ significantly from zero (p = 0.67). Error levels prevent detection of abundance changes as large as 50%.
- **Catch and CPUE (Table 2):** Total captures were 855 (2001), 1,290 (2002) and 832 (2003). CPUE was 2.45, 5.16 and 3.29 fish/hr. CPUE in 2002 was significantly higher than in 2001 and 2003 (p < 0.05). No correlation was found between CPUE and population estimates.
- **Distribution:** Found throughout the reach, with CPUE significantly higher in the upper section (RM 120–90). The species made up 17%, 34% and 17% of the total electrofishing catch in 2001–2003 (the conclusions cite 17–34% overall).
- **Length and growth:** Total lengths ranged 61–591 mm (mean 335 mm). Cohorts were distinguishable for the first three to four years. Maximum annual growth occurred from age two to three, averaging 130 mm. Growth rates declined steadily after age three.
- **Reproduction:** Earliest tuberculated fish and ripe males were recorded on March 12, 2001 (water temperature 10 °C). Earliest gravid female was April 3, 2001 (11 °C). Mean length of gravid males was 460 mm (range 401–495 mm); ripe females averaged 509 mm (range 473–538 mm). Tuberculated adults peaked between RM 90–110 and were fewest in RM 70–89 and 5–12.
- **Movement:** Of 25 recaptured fish, 13 moved upstream (mean 19.4 miles, maximum 59 miles) and 12 moved downstream (mean 17.2 miles, maximum 59 miles). Mean movement by size class was 9.1 RM (140–299 mm), 33.8 RM (300–399 mm) and 15.7 RM (400–538 mm). These differences were not statistically significant.
- **Gear comparison:** Catch rates with the Coffelt unit were 35% lower in 2001 (p < 0.01) and 19% lower in 2002 (p = 0.09) than with Smith-Root units.

**Bluehead Sucker**
- **Population estimate:** Not calculated, because too few recaptures were obtained.
- **Catch and CPUE (Table 3):** Total captures were 171 (2001), 180 (2002) and 107 (2003). CPUE was 0.49, 0.72 and 0.42 fish/hr. No significant differences were found among years.
- **Distribution:** Present in all sections of the reach. Over 60% of captures came from the upper 30 miles. The species made up 3%, 5% and 4% of the catch in 2001–2003.
- **Length and growth:** Total lengths ranged 60–520 mm (mean 232 mm). Two 520 mm fish were noted as the largest bluehead on record for the lower Green River. Growth was estimated from length-frequency modes at 80–110 mm/yr for two- to three-year-olds, slowing to about 30–40 mm for older fish.
- **Reproduction:** Only two ripe males were captured. No ripe or tuberculated females were observed.
- **Movement:** One fish moved upstream more than 71 miles after a year at large (anecdotal).

**Management Recommendations**
- For a precise baseline estimate, run mark-recapture for both species separately from Colorado Pikeminnow sampling.
- Expand the study area upstream to Tusher Diversion (RM 128). Increase effort in concentration areas (RM 90–120). Focus on off-shoreline riffle habitat.
- Use [[Open Population Models]] to use longer recapture histories for estimating abundance, survival and recruitment.
- Account for geomorphic reach boundaries when scaling sample areas, to capture [[Metapopulation]] structure.

**Conclusions**
- Mark-recapture did not yield enough recaptures to detect trends statistically.
- Flannelmouth sucker is common to abundant and makes up a considerable share of the fish community.
- Flannelmouth sucker spawning is concentrated in the upper 40 miles of the reach, which also holds the largest share of both species.
- Bluehead sucker is present but rare, making up a small share of the community.
