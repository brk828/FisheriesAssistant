# Razorback Sucker Reintroduction and Monitoring Study: February 1987 Monthly Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This February 1987 monthly report from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] covers the [[Razorback Sucker]] reintroduction and monitoring study on the [[Colorado River]]. Work in February focused on three areas: recovering juvenile razorbacks from hatchery ponds, surveying the river and isolated ponds for potential stocking sites, and netting ponds to measure exotic predator composition and abundance. Roughly 60 juvenile razorbacks were recovered and moved to [[Senator Wash Reservoir]]. Several backwater habitats and ponds were identified as candidate stocking sites, and final site selection was deferred until predator netting results were available. The report is a field progress summary, not a formal research paper.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large catostomid sucker native to the [[Colorado River]] system. In this study it is the focal species for reintroduction and monitoring. The report is a monthly progress document prepared by the Razorback Sucker Consultant, [[Daniel R Langhorst]], for the [[California Department of Fish and Game]]. Its field work was run from the Blythe office and coordinated with [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] staff, the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]], and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

Work was reported as 40 hours per week for the weeks of February 9–13, 16–20, and 23–27, 1987.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Stocking-site selection was based on:

- Cover
- Water depth
- Substrate type
- The likelihood that stocked fry would remain in the site for further study
- Possible predator interaction

Predator risk was a central concern. Exotic predators were to be netted at each candidate site in early March to determine species composition and abundance before final site selection. Sites were assessed in several areas:

- **[[Imperial Division]]:** Downstream of [[Oxbow Lake]], four backwater habitats were identified as potential stocking sites.
- **[[Parker Division]]:** Below the [[Agnes Wilson Bridge]], two sites were identified as potential stocking sites.
- **[[La Paz Lake]]:** A 1986 stocking site on the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]] reservation, with a chemical treatment proposed pending Tribal Council approval. Razorback fry were to be stocked after arrival from Dexter and studied in a predator-free environment.
- **Levee pond:** An isolated pond next to the levee road north of Oxbow Lake was netted to determine predator composition and abundance.
- **Backwater site:** One backwater about 1 mile upstream of the Parker Bridge, the site of a 1986 stocking, was judged potentially suitable for 1987 reintroduction. An inspection of the Imperial Division was cut short by a boat motor failure.

Predator netting used trap nets, gill nets, and minnow traps set for approximately 24 hours. Netting at [[La Paz Lake]] and two other reservation ponds was conducted on February 18–19, 1987. Netting at the levee pond was conducted on February 25–26, 1987.

The captured species were:

- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
- [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*)
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*)
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)
- [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucus*)
- Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

Catch counts in the report tables are partly illegible in the scanned copy and are not reproduced here. Measurements are given in millimeters for length and grams for weight.

## Key Findings

- **Juvenile recovery:** On February 10, about 60 juvenile razorbacks were removed from two ponds at the [[Niland Reservoir Fish Hatchery]]. The fish came from an estimated 51,000 fry stocked in March 1986. Total lengths ranged from roughly 4 to 12 inches (the scan is partly illegible), and most fish were in good physical condition. All recovered fish were transported and stocked in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]].
- **Site survey:** Candidate stocking sites were identified in the Imperial Division (four backwater habitats) and the Parker Division (two sites). One additional backwater was judged potentially suitable.
- **Predator risk:** Exotic predators were present in the netted ponds, including carp, sunfish, bullhead, bass, and shiner. Predator composition and abundance will determine which sites are finally selected.
- **Pending actions:** Chemical treatment of [[La Paz Lake]] awaits Tribal Council approval. Razorback fry from Dexter National Fish Hatchery are to be stocked on arrival. Final site selection depends on the early-March predator netting results.
- **Operational issue:** An inspection of the Imperial Division was stopped by a boat motor failure.
