# Endangered Fish Research Program Summary - Humpback Chub Mainstem Studies

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #dam-operations #radiotelemetry

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from BIO/WEST, Inc. (October 18, 1991) summarizes preliminary findings from the Endangered Fish Research Program studying [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub) in the mainstem [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]]. The research program, conducted in cooperation with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[Arizona Department of Game and Fish]], investigates the relationship between [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and the ecology and life history of the endangered humpback chub population. Between October 1990 and October 1991, researchers completed thirteen trips (seven 12-day and six 20-day expeditions) covering river miles 56-226, employing multiple sampling methods including electrofishing, trammel nets, gill nets, and radiotelemetry of 48 adult fish.

## Background & Description

The research program titled "Characterization of the Life History and Ecology of the Humpback Chub in the Grand Canyon - Mainstem Studies" was led by Dr. Richard A. Valdez as Principal Investigator. The program's primary purpose was to determine how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affect the ecology and life history requirements of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] population in [[Grand Canyon]].

The study had two main objectives: (1) determine ecological and limiting factors for all life stages of humpback chub in the mainstem [[Colorado River]] and effects of dam operations, including resource availability and use, reproductive capacity and success, survivorship of early stages, distribution and abundance, and biotic interactions; (2) determine the life history for the [[Grand Canyon]] humpback chub population and develop or modify a population model from empirical data for analyses of reproductive success, recruitment, and survivorship.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area extended from river mile 56 to 226 in the [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]], with particular focus on the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) confluence area (RM 57-65). The mainstem environment is heavily influenced by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, which affect flow magnitudes, ramping rates, water temperature, and turbidity.

Water quality monitoring was conducted using Hydrolab Surveyor and Datasonde instruments at camps and the [[Little Colorado River]] inflow, measuring dissolved oxygen, conductivity, pH, and temperature. The fish community composition varied by reach: Reach 1 (RM 56-77) was dominated by [[Rainbow Trout]] (57%), humpback chub (21%), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (14%), and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (2%); Reach 2 (RM 77-160) by rainbow trout (53%), [[Carp]] (14%), and flannelmouth sucker (11%); Reach 3 (RM 160-226) by flannelmouth sucker (30%), carp (29%), and rainbow trout (17%).

The presence of [[Striped Bass]] (five caught in May, twelve in July, with upstream-most capture at RM 156.4 near [[Havasu Creek]]) represented a potential predatory threat to the endangered species.

## Key Findings

**Population Distribution and Abundance:**
- 95% of humpback chub (848 total captured) were found between RM 57.0 ([[Malagosa Crest]]) and RM 65.9 (Below [[Lava Chuar Rapid]]), concentrated near the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence
- In Reach 1, 45% were caught above the LCR confluence and 55% below
- Only 39 humpback chub were captured in Reaches 2 and 3, at tributary confluences including [[Bright Angel Creek]] (1 fish), [[Shinumo Creek]] (20 fish), [[119 Mile Creek]] (2 fish), [[127 Mile Creek]] (8 fish), [[Havasu Creek]] (4 fish), and [[Pumpkin Spring]] to [[Granite Spring]] (4 fish)
- Upstream-most capture was at RM 57.0; downstream-most at RM 221.8

**Sampling Effort (approximate, RM 56-226):**
- Electrofishing: 350 efforts, 100 hours
- Trammel nets: 2,200 sets, 4,500 hours
- Gill nets: 1,000 sets, 2,000 hours
- Hoop nets: 100 sets, 800 hours
- Minnow traps: 100 sets, 1,000 hours
- Seines: 30 hauls
- Drift samples: 75 sets (15-30 minutes each)

**Tagging and Recapture:**
- 538 humpback chub were PIT tagged (>175 mm TL)
- 44 BIO/WEST PIT tagged chub were recaptured (8.2% recapture rate)
- 188 chub marked by all investigators were recaptured (34.97% overall recapture rate)

**Radiotelemetry Results:**
- 48 adult humpback chub were radiotagged
- 250 surveillance runs conducted (RM 57-65)
- 62 monitoring maps showing fish movement with hydrograph fluctuations
- Remote station data collected February-September 1991
- Radiotagged adults primarily occupied eddies and return channels behind reattachment sand bars, with most-used sites including [[Awatubi]] (RM 58.7-58.8), [[60-Mile]] (RM 60.0-60.1), [[ESPN]] (RM 60.7-60.8), and [[Carbon Creek]] (RM 64.6-64.7)
- Majority of radiotagged fish remained within 0.5 miles of capture/release point, with maximum movement of 3.0 miles downstream during spawning
- One radiotagged fish moved 62.0 miles downstream from RM 65.0 to RM 127.0 between October 1990 and recapture

**Reproductive Behavior and Recruitment:**
- Pre-spawning aggregations of radiotagged adults noted March 5, 1991, at the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence
- Radiotagged adults observed entering the LCR on April 5, 1991
- Catch rates of adults in mainstem decreased dramatically in May 1991, concurrent with movement to LCR inflow
- Gravid female humpback chub caught in LCR inflow in May, suggesting attempted reproduction in mainstem affected by LCR flow
- Larval fish (Catostomids and Cyprinids) first observed in lower LCR on May 20, 1991
- Age-0 and juvenile humpback chub first captured in mainstem in early May 1991, with highest catches in September; these fish presumably originated from the LCR
- Age-0 and juvenile fish captured along shorelines with talus and emergent boulders below the LCR; three young fish captured above LCR

**Behavioral Patterns:**
- Greatest near-surface activity of radiotagged adults occurred during crepuscular periods (morning and evening)
- Activity extended into daytime during turbid water conditions and was nearly absent during low turbidity
- Activity and habitat use affected by flow magnitudes and ramping rates

**Other Species Observations:**
- Aggregations of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]] noted in [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], [[Deer Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]] in April and May 1991

**Habitat Mapping:**
- 21 macrohabitat maps completed for RM 60-65
- 2 maps for RM 108.0-108.6 and RM 118.0-188.7
- 15 temporary bench marks established at RM 60-65

**Concerns Identified:**
1. Degree of handling of humpback chub in summer months (May-July); fish potentially handled multiple times by different research teams; overall recapture rate in mainstem = 35%
2. Health of radiotagged adults; one fish died following recapture, five others appeared healthy; all radiotagged fish observed at LCR aggregation exhibited normal spawning behavior
3. Difficulty ascertaining survival of age-0 and small juvenile chubs due to lack of reliable permanent mark to follow cohorts
4. Lack of accurate ongoing turbidity measurements to correlate with fish activity

**Recommendations for Future Research:**
1. Expand sampling intensity in Reach 1 to better define boundary of LCR inflow population in 1992
2. Quantify adult humpback chub habitat and relate to flows in 1992
3. Quantify juvenile and age-0 humpback chub habitat and relate to flows in 1992
4. Evaluate food habits of humpback chub in 1992
5. Conduct radiotelemetry in Reaches 2 and 3 in 1993
6. Continue operating directional remote telemetry stations at LCR mouth January-August and nondirectional station September-December 1992
7. Intensify drift sampling in conjunction with food habits analysis in 1992
