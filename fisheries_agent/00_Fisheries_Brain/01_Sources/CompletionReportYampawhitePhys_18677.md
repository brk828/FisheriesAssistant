# Yampa-White Physical Habitat Study Completion Report

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #habitat-modeling #Colorado-River-Basin

## Executive Summary

This completion report documents physical habitat and streamflow analysis conducted on the [[Yampa River]] and [[White River]] in Colorado and Utah during 1981 as part of the [[Colorado River Fisheries Project]] (CRFP). The study applied the [[PHABSIM]] (Physical Habitat Simulation) model to eight river reaches—five on the Yampa and three on the White—to assess habitat suitability for endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Hydraulic simulation models (WSP and IFG-4) predicted depth, velocity, and substrate conditions at varying flows, which were then compared against habitat preference curves for young-of-year (YOY), adult, and spawning life stages. Weighted Usable Area (WUA) calculations provided quantitative estimates of habitat quality across discharge ranges. The study identified critical spawning reaches, optimal flow regimes for different life stages, and reach-specific habitat characteristics that support endangered fish populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Fisheries Project]] was conducted by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Salt Lake City Area Office with funding from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], and [[National Park Service]]. The overall objective was to acquire knowledge of life history, movements, and habitat requirements of endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. This phase focused on collecting physical habitat data using the [[PHABSIM]] system, part of the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM).

**Study Sites:**

*Yampa River (5 sites):*
- [[Maybell Reach]] (river mile 72): Representative of cobble-sand substrate reach, wide and shallow with low habitat diversity
- [[Lily Park Reach]] (river mile 50): Highly diverse substrate with riffles, pools, backwaters, and boulders; known for numerous adult squawfish captures
- [[Cross Mountain Reach]] (river mile 53): Unique cliff-bound gorge habitat with numerous adult squawfish
- [[Box Elder Campground]] (river mile 2): Representative of lower Yampa Canyon with sand bars, deep pools, cobble riffles, and backwaters; larval squawfish documented
- [[Cleopatra's Couch]] (river mile 16.5): Critical spawning reach utilized during spawning period

*White River (3 sites):*
- [[Bonanza Reach]] (river mile 45): Diverse substrate representing upper White River cobble-gravel reaches
- [[Mountain Fuel Reach]] (river mile 20): Representative of incised canyon reaches with diverse substrate
- [[Ouray Reach]] (river mile 1): Sand-bed segment with straight channel representing lower 15 miles to White-Green confluence

**Methodology:**

Depth, velocity, and substrate were measured at multiple transects using standard surveying techniques (accuracy 0.01 ft), wading rods and boat sounding equipment (depth to 0.1 ft), Marsh-McBirney electromagnetic current meters (velocity to 0.1 fps), and modified Wentworth substrate classification (silt=3, sand=4, gravel=5, cobble=6, boulders=7, bedrock=8). Two hydraulic simulation models were employed: WSP (step-backwater calculation, single measurement, extrapolation range 0.4-2.5× measured flow) used at Bonanza and Cleopatra's Couch; and IFG-4 (regression technique, minimum three measurements, greater predictive capability) used at remaining six sites with 3-4 flow measurements per site.

**Habitat Suitability Curves:**

- **YOY squawfish:** Derived from ~1,900 fish captures (Miller 1982), considered most reliable
- **Adult squawfish:** Based on 200 captures from Green River sand-bed reaches only; may reflect available habitat more than true preferences (least reliable)
- **Spawning squawfish:** Constructed from northern squawfish (*Ptychocheilus oregonensis*) data (Beamesderfer and Congleton 1981) compared with conditions at Cleopatra's Couch; moderately reliable with consistent velocity and substrate information

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Yampa River]] and [[White River]] are critical tributaries of the [[Green River]] system within the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. These rivers provide essential habitat for two federally endangered cyprinids: [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. The study area encompasses diverse aquatic habitats ranging from wide, shallow cobble-sand reaches to deep canyon pools and sand-bed segments.

**Hydrologic Context:**

Spawning flows on the Yampa River typically occur during late July-early August. Above the [[Little Snake River]] confluence, optimal spawning flows range 500-1,200 cfs; below the confluence, 800-2,000 cfs. On the White River, spawning occurs during early-to-mid July at flows of 300-800 cfs. At the [[Mountain Fuel Reach]], WUA declined sharply below 250-300 cfs, representing a critical lower flow limit.

**Habitat Characteristics:**

The study documented significant habitat diversity across reaches. [[Lily Park Reach]] displayed the highest substrate diversity with riffle-pool sequences, while [[Cross Mountain Reach]] was characterized by deep, swift, cliff-bound conditions. [[Box Elder Campground]] provided complex channel characteristics with secondary shelves offering slow, shallow water at intermediate flows. [[Ouray Reach]] on the White River consisted exclusively of sand substrate.

**Life History Considerations:**

Adult squawfish were captured at all study sites, with [[Cross Mountain Reach]] yielding the most captures during CRFP sampling. YOY squawfish have not been documented in the Yampa River, and no YOY were found in the White River during 1981. Spawning was documented only at [[Cleopatra's Couch]] and suspected near [[Box Elder Campground]] on the Yampa. A suspected spawning site was identified within 5 miles of the [[Bonanza Reach]] on the White River. Radiotelemetry and larval collections confirmed successful spawning at Cleopatra's Couch during 1981.

## Key Findings

**Yampa River Results:**

1. **Maybell Reach:** YOY habitat limited due to restricted sand-silt substrate distribution. Adult WUA highest at 200-1,200 cfs but reach not considered excellent habitat. Spawning WUA peaked at 800-900 cfs with suitable range 300-1,500 cfs, encompassing expected late July flows when temperature reaches spawning threshold. Very few squawfish captures by CRFP.

2. **Lily Park Reach:** YOY habitat much higher than Maybell due to high substrate diversity; WUA highest at low flows but remained high through 600-700 cfs range. Adult WUA dramatically higher than Maybell, comprising high percentage of total surface area at 100-2,000 cfs. Numerous historical adult captures confirmed high WUA values, though CRFP collected no squawfish during 1981. Spawning WUA increased with discharge even to highest modeled flow (12,000 cfs), increasing 10-fold between 150-1,200 cfs. Total spawning WUA less than Maybell at all flows due to less gravel-cobble substrate, but riffle-pool sequences may make this reach more suitable than WUA values suggest.

3. **Cross Mountain Reach:** YOY habitat limited and available primarily at low flows due to deep, swift, cliff-bound nature. Adult WUA very extensive across broad flow range; approximately half of maximum WUA retained even at 10,000 cfs. CRFP captured most adult squawfish in this stratum. No spawning habitat demonstrated at any flow due to lack of gravel-cobble substrate within gorge.

4. **Box Elder Campground:** Provided highest available habitat of all Yampa sites for all life stages. YOY WUA showed two peaks: 100-400 cfs and 1,000-1,200 cfs, due to complex channel and secondary shelf providing slow, shallow water at intermediate flows. Adult WUA highest of all Yampa reaches at 100-1,700 cfs. Significant adult concentrations documented prior to and during spawning season. Spawning habitat highest at 400-4,000 cfs with significant peak at 700-2,000 cfs; highest among reaches displaying pool-riffle sequences.

5. **Cleopatra's Couch:** Results available only for 250-1,200 cfs range. Spawning WUA relatively high and increasing with discharge; highest attained on either Yampa or White Rivers considering pool interspersion. CRFP radiotelemetry and larval collections clearly demonstrated successful spawning in 1981. YOY habitat quite limited due to lack of fine sediment with shallow water. Adult WUA relatively high due to pool area, though post-spawning capture efforts were futile, suggesting not an important resident area.

**White River Results:**

1. **Bonanza Reach:** Simulations ranged 75-400 cfs (single measurement limitation). Relatively diverse substrate resulted in significant WUA for all life stages. YOY habitat quite high above 100 cfs, peaking 200-300 cfs. Adult WUA slightly higher than YOY, increasing with flow. Spawning WUA higher than any other White River site, increasing to very high percentage of total surface area at 400 cfs.

2. **Mountain Fuel Reach:** Offered highest combined WUA of three White River sites across all life stages and broad flow range. YOY WUA relatively high and stable across entire flow range with minor peak at 500 cfs. Adult habitat quite high and very stable; differences among WUA values not significant enough to regard any flow as superior. Spawning WUA quite high but within restricted range of 300-800 cfs. Rapid WUA loss below 300 cfs probably due to depth limitations; considered significant given large size of spawning squawfish and body-depth water requirements.

3. **Ouray Reach:** Substrate exclusively sand; predictably high WUA for YOY and adults, no spawning WUA. YOY WUA highest of all White River sites with high values at 100-700 cfs. Adult habitat high at most flows with broad peak at 500-1,200 cfs.

**General Conclusions:**

- Spawning WUA values were highest at sites suspected as best spawning areas, especially considering riffle-pool proximity. Spawning WUA was low or non-existent at reaches not identified as prime spawning areas.
- Little can be concluded from YOY analysis as no site on either river has been associated with significant YOY captures. Results included for future impact analysis or transplantation studies.
- Spawning WUA on both rivers was at peak or high values at flows expected during normal spawning periods, consistent with known hydrologic events.
- Adult WUA was highest at quite low flows (<400 cfs) at all Yampa sites, possibly reflecting preference curves developed without accounting for available habitat. Flows did not appear critical for fish with these preferences on either river.
- WUA evaluations for adult squawfish at representative stations did not reflect relative numbers collected by CRFP investigators, possibly due to inadequacies in sampling program, curve generation, or representative site selection.
- Results considered reliable within limits of hydraulic simulation models and habitat preference curves. YOY curves probably most reliable; spawning curves less reliable but consistent; adult curves least reliable due to potential influence of available habitat.
- Results did not contradict known or suspected life history phenomena or associated hydrologic events.
