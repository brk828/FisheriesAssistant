# Revised USFWS-Pinetop FAO General Study Plan: Microhabitat Use by Fishes of the Little Colorado River

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1991 field study plan (third revision) coordinated by [[Owen T. Gorman]] for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Pinetop office, designed to characterize seasonal microhabitat availability and microhabitat use by fishes—particularly the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)—in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), a major tributary of the [[Colorado River]] within the [[Grand Canyon]] system. The plan establishes multiple study reaches from the LCR/Colorado River confluence (km 0) upstream to Blue Springs (km ~21), with standardized grid-based habitat mapping and low-impact fish sampling protocols (mini-hoopnets, minnow traps, seining, and trammel netting) to be coordinated with Arizona Game and Fish (AGF), Arizona State University (ASU), and BioWest research teams. The overarching goal is to assess how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] discharge regimes affect physicochemical conditions and fish microhabitat selection, informing management of humpback chub populations.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide morphometric or anatomical descriptions of *Gila cypha* or other LCR fish species; it is a methodological field study plan rather than a taxonomic or morphological account. Notable species-specific ecological detail mentioned:
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is treated as an **endangered species** with strict incidental mortality limits under sampling permits.
- The distribution terminus of humpback chub in the LCR is identified at the **Atomizer Falls reach (km 14–15.5)**, which is viewed as a natural upstream barrier to chub movement.
- Chub exhibit a **spring migration and spawning period**, explicitly targeted for additional sampling effort near Atomizer Falls.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study area spans a longitudinal gradient of the LCR from its confluence with the Colorado River (km 0) to Blue Springs (km ~21), encompassing a diversity of mesohabitats relevant to niche partitioning:

- **Confluence area (km 0–1):** Influenced directly by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] release regimes; mapped via transect grids (1–2 m × 20 m) under both low and high discharge conditions, including nighttime mapping during high flow and peak LCR spring snowmelt discharge.
- **Lower Base Camp / Powell Canyon Camp (km 2.5–4.0):** Two rotating 500 m² exclusive study reaches containing pools, raceways-tailwaters, riffles, and travertine reefs/dams — representative of the heterogeneous mesohabitat mosaic characteristic of the LCR.
- **Upper Base Camp / Salt Trail Canyon (km 10–12):** Parallel design to LBC.
- **Atomizer Falls Reach (km 14–15.5):** Marks the upstream distributional limit of humpback chub; habitat assessed both below and above falls.
- **Blue Springs Reach (km 20–21):** Represents the upper extent of the LCR study range, likely influenced by spring-fed water chemistry distinct from mainstem/dam-influenced reaches.

Physical habitat types repeatedly referenced as structuring fish microhabitat use include **pools, riffles, raceways, tailwaters, and travertine dams/reefs** — geomorphic features typical of a [[Travertine-Influenced River]] system. High water conductivity in the LCR is noted as rendering electroshocking ineffective, an important abiotic constraint on sampling ecology and likely reflective of the mineral-rich, travertine-depositing water chemistry of the system.

Fish community structure is assessed through complementary gear types capturing different habitat/size niches:
- **Mini-hoopnets (20" × 4', 1/4" mesh):** primary gear, sampling a wide range of habitats and size classes.
- **Minnow traps (Gee's standard, 1/4" mesh):** shallow edge-habitat specialists.
- **Seines:** effective only in narrow, homogeneous, boulder/travertine-free shallow edge habitats; best for small and young-of-year fish.
- **Trammel nets:** size-selective toward larger fish; provide point-of-capture data but cause higher physical trauma and occasional incidental mortality of humpback chub.

## Key Findings
- By **August 1991**, habitat mapping had been completed for the lower 4 km and km 9.75–12.00 on a fine (1 × 20 m) grid, and the entire LCR from confluence to Blue Springs (km 0–21) had been mapped at coarser (1 × 100 m) resolution.
- Habitat measurements at the confluence area had been taken during **summer low-flow conditions** (4,000 cfs Colorado River; 200 cfs LCR base flow).
- Fish habitat-use data from trammel net sets were collected **21 July–24 August 1991**.
- Sampling design uses an **"exclusive" study area** concept: 500 m reaches (with 100 m buffers) sampled by only one research team (USFWS) per month to minimize cumulative sampling disturbance, with data sharing (mark/recapture) across ASU, AGF, and BioWest teams.
- Standardized protocol: nets/traps soaked and run every **12 hours**, moved to new grid locations every **24 hours**, with no single location sampled more than **1 week per month**, minimizing sampling-induced behavioral bias.
- **Electroshocking excluded** as a viable method due to high water conductivity in the LCR — a distinctive water-chemistry constraint for this system.
- Trammel nets identified as **occasionally lethal to humpback chub**, a critical consideration given the species' endangered status and strict incidental take limits.
- Drift trammel netting through pools proposed as an alternative to stationary sets, allowing recording of lateral/vertical position and directional movement data without the confusion of capture-location data caused by dragging nets to shore.
- The study explicitly links physicochemical/habitat monitoring to **Glen Canyon Dam discharge regime changes**, positioning this work within the broader [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Phase II research program.
