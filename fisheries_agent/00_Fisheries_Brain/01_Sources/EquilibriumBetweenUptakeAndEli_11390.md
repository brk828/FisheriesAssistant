# Equilibrium between uptake and elimination of dieldrin by channel catfish, Ictalurus punctatus

## Executive Summary
This study examined whether [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) reach an equilibrium between uptake and elimination of the organochlorine pesticide [[Dieldrin]] in dorsal muscle tissue under continuous laboratory exposure. Juvenile catfish (150 ± 5 mm) were exposed to three sublethal, environmentally relevant concentrations of dieldrin (13, 27, and 49 parts per trillion) for 70 days. Results demonstrated a **dose-dependent time-to-equilibrium relationship**: fish exposed to the lowest concentration (13 pptr) reached equilibrium fastest (56 days), fish at the intermediate level (27 pptr) approached equilibrium by day 56, and fish at the highest level (49 pptr) showed continued linear bioaccumulation through day 70 without reaching a steady state. These findings support the hypothesis that dieldrin accumulation in fish tissue has a dose-dependent upper limit, consistent with agricultural runoff patterns observed in Iowa's [[Des Moines River]] system, where seasonal aldrin/dieldrin application drives fluctuating contaminant burdens in wild catfish populations.

## Background & Description
- **Test organism size:** Channel catfish standardized to 150 mm (± 5 mm) total length, sourced from a commercial fish culturist.
- **Tissue sampled:** Dorsal muscle (skeletal muscle), 10 g samples per analysis — chosen as the primary edible/sport-fish tissue of regulatory and human health concern.
- **Sampling regimen:** Three samples of two fish each drawn from each tank at 2-week intervals (days 0, 14, 28, 42, 56, 70).
- No morphometric or anatomical abnormalities were reported; the study focused exclusively on tissue-level pesticide residue kinetics rather than gross morphology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** *Ictalurus punctatus* is one of [[Iowa]]'s most economically and recreationally important sport fish, inhabiting rivers and streams subject to heavy agricultural runoff.
- **Environmental exposure pathway:** [[Aldrin]], applied at 2.0–6.5 million lbs/year on Iowa cropland (primarily for corn rootworm control), degrades to its epoxide metabolite dieldrin, which enters surface waters via agricultural runoff.
- **Seasonal contamination dynamics:** Field studies (Bulkley et al. 1974) showed dieldrin levels in wild catfish muscle rise sharply within weeks of spring corn planting, then decline — indicating a direct link between agrochemical application timing and bioaccumulation in the aquatic food web.
- **Trophic/water chemistry context:** Experimental water was dechlorinated well water (pH 8.6–8.9), with a 50% proportional diluter system simulating continuous flow-through exposure (complete water turnover every 6 hr), mimicking riverine conditions more realistically than static bioassays.
- **Interspecies comparison:** Related work on [[Rainbow Trout]] (Macek et al. 1970) found skeletal muscle equilibrium after only 28 days at much higher dietary exposure (1 ppm/week dieldrin in food), reaching whole-body equilibrium by 140 days — highlighting species- and exposure-route-dependent differences in bioaccumulation kinetics.

## Key Findings
- **13 pptr dieldrin exposure:** Muscle residues reached equilibrium by day 56 (29 ppb) and remained statistically unchanged at day 70 (31 ppb).
- **27 pptr dieldrin exposure:** Residues approached but did not statistically confirm equilibrium — 39 ppb at day 56 vs. 48 ppb at day 70 (difference not significant).
- **49 pptr dieldrin exposure:** Residues continued to increase **linearly** throughout the 70-day trial (7→45→57→84→130→164 ppb), with day 70 levels significantly higher than day 56 (p = 0.01) — indicating no equilibrium was reached at this exposure level.
- **Dose-dependent equilibrium timing:** Lower exposure concentrations reached steady-state faster than higher concentrations, supporting the concept of a concentration-dependent bioaccumulation ceiling for cyclodiene insecticides (per [[Brooks 1971]]; [[Macek et al. 1970]]).
- **Field relevance:** Concentrations used in this study (13–49 pptr) are environmentally realistic, corroborating field-measured seasonal dieldrin spikes in the Des Moines River following aldrin application during corn planting season.
- **Method validation:** Aldrin recovery efficiency during extraction procedures averaged 85% (range 83–90%), supporting reliability of the GC-based (electron capture detector) quantification method used for dieldrin residues.
- **Study limitation:** Two lower-exposure treatment tanks (8.5 and 4.0 pptr) were lost due to unexplained fish mortality, limiting the lower end of the dose-response curve.
- **Broader implication:** Findings imply that real-world dieldrin burdens in sport fish like channel catfish are highly sensitive to fluctuating agricultural contamination levels, with human health implications given that residues up to 1600 ppb have been recorded in Iowa catfish (Morris & Johnson 1971) — over 5× the FDA permissible limit at the time.
