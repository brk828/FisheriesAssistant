# Four New Pupfishes of the Genus Cyprinodon from Mexico, with a Key to the C. eximius Complex

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1976 taxonomic paper by [[Robert Rush Miller]] revises the *Cyprinodon eximius* complex, a group of seven pupfish species restricted to, or originating in, the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] region of northern Mexico and adjacent parts of Texas and New Mexico. Four species are described as new: *C. nazas*, *C. alvarezi*, *C. meeki* and *C. macrolepis*. The paper provides an identification key, diagnoses, ranges and illustrations for every species, and emphasizes that life colors and color patterns are key specific traits. Most species have restricted distributions; one (*C. latifasciatus*) is extinct and another (*C. alvarezi*) is rare.

## Background & Description

The author inherited the genus revision project from [[Carl L. Hubbs]], who had gathered data on *Cyprinodon* since his time at the Field Museum of Natural History, and who had identified colour and colour pattern as important specific traits. The complex includes species closely related to the widely distributed *C. eximius* [[Conchos Pupfish]]. Diagnostic characters include:

- Broad black terminal caudal-fin bar in nuptial males (wider than the pupil in most species).
- Gill rakers typically fewer than 20 on the first arch (exceptions in *atrorus*, *latifasciatus*, and *macrolepis*).
- Dorsal fin of nuptial male yellow, orange or amber (exceptions *latifasciatus*, *meeki*, *alvarezi*).
- Mandibular pores generally lacking (exceptions *alvarezi*, with 2, and *nazas*, with 0–2).
- Enlarged cleithral process.
- Modally six pelvic rays.

Courtship behaviour differences between this complex and other *Cyprinodon* were noted by [[Liu]] (1970) from a thesis, but their significance was not established.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitats range from desert springs and hot springs to pools, endorheic lagoons and river systems. Specific localities described include:

- **Rio Nazas** at San Miguel, Coahuila, near Laguna Mayrán, at 1,093 m elevation with a salinity of 8.2 ppt.
- **El Potosí**, a spring-fed pond at about 1,900 m elevation, where the species is now rare owing to the introduction of [[Largemouth Bass]] in 1974.
- **Hot-spring pond** near Durango City, with water at 20.5 °C and spring water at 29.5 °C.
- **Hacienda Dolores hot spring**, Chihuahua, with winter–summer temperatures of 29–33 °C and salinity of 0.5 ppt. The outflow once connected with the Rio Florido of the Rio Conchos basin.

Several *Cyprinodon* species and subspecies occur in isolated desert basins, and this isolation supports the restricted distributions reported. The paper cites [[Minckley]] (1969) for the environments of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas]] basin, and the [[Sauz Basin]] as an endorheic system. The paper notes that *C. latifasciatus* is extinct and the Parras basin population is no longer present.

## Key Findings

- The *C. eximius* complex consists of **seven species**, four of which are new to science.
- **New species** described: *C. nazas* (Rio Nazas, Coahuila), *C. alvarezi* (El Potosí, Nuevo León), *C. meeki* (Durango), and *C. macrolepis* (Chihuahua).
- *C. latifasciatus* is **extinct**, known only from the Parras basin; the author notes *C. alvarezi* is rare because of predation by introduced bass.
- Colour and colour pattern are critical for species identification, as Hubbs had recognised.
- Separation of *C. nazas* from *C. eximius* relies on gill rakers (12–17 versus 12–18 in *eximius*), the absence of caudal spots in *nazas*, and a range of morphometric characters.
- A key is provided for all seven species, based on caudal bar width, gill raker counts, scale counts, dorsal-fin colour, head pore counts and pelvic fin size.
- The Santiaguillo basin population of *C. nazas* may warrant subspecific recognition, based on fewer caudal peduncle scales (usually 16) and fewer body scales (usually 30–32).
- Holotype and paratype series are documented for each new species, with Universidad de Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ) specimens as the primary types. Sample sizes include 207 juvenile to adult paratopotypes of *C. nazas* and 314 young to adult paratopotypes of *C. alvarezi*.
- Morphometric measurements (expressed in thousandths of standard length) and meristic ranges are reported for each new species.
