# An Assessment of Diet-Overlap Indexes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinopsidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This methodological study tests how the choice of diet measure and overlap index changes the estimated diet overlap between two co-occurring fishes: [[Mississippi Silverside]] (*Menidia audens*) and juvenile [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) from [[False River]], Louisiana. Five diet measures and several overlap indexes were applied to the same stomach-content data set. The authors found that each index gave different values for different diet measures, so overlap estimates depend heavily on analytical choices. Among the options tested, the [[Schoener Index]] based on mean volume percentages is favored, and researchers are urged to justify their choice of index against the diet items and food availability in their system.

## Background & Description

- **Study organisms:** Mississippi silversides and juvenile largemouth bass, both collected together in the littoral zone of a Louisiana lake.
- **Core problem:** Diet overlap indexes are widely used to infer competition, but the literature applies different diet measures and indexes inconsistently. The same data can yield quite different conclusions depending on the combination used.
- **Objective:** To examine combinations of common overlap indexes and diet measures and comment on their appropriateness.
- **Diet measures compared:**
  - Percentage of occurrence
  - Percentage of total number
  - Percentage of total volume
  - Mean of volume percentages
  - [[Relative Importance Index]] (George and Hadley 1979), which combines occurrence, number and volume into one value
- **Overlap indexes compared:** [[Schoener Index]], [[Horn Index]], [[Levins Index]], and [[Spearman Rank Correlation]] (used as an overlap measure).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** False River, Louisiana, a freshwater lake sampled in May 1979.
- **Sampling:** Fish were collected by [[Seine Netting]] at approximately regular intervals over 24 hours. Specimens were preserved in 10% formalin and later transferred to 40% isopropyl alcohol.
- **Sample size:** Stomachs from 30 Mississippi silversides and juvenile largemouth bass were examined. The size range of the largemouth bass is partly illegible in the scan.
- **Stomach analysis:** Each food item was measured to the nearest 0.1 mm along three dimensions to estimate volume. Contents were tabulated by occurrence, number and volume across all stomachs containing food (see [[Stomach Content Analysis]]).
- **Funding and affiliation:** The research was supported by the Alabama Game and Fish Division through a research assistantship. The authors were affiliated with the Auburn University Cooperative Fishery Research Unit.

## Key Findings

1. **Index values depend on the diet measure.** Each overlap index gave different values for different diet measures, so the choice of measure can change the apparent degree of overlap between the two species.
2. **Schoener index on mean volume percentages is preferred.** Among the combinations tested, the Schoener index based on the mean of volume percentages is presented as the most appropriate option.
3. **Overlap values vary widely.** Reported overlap values span a broad range, with the lowest value about 0.06. The scan of the results section is partly illegible, so the full range should be checked against the original.
4. **Correlation indexes are sensitive to data changes.** The worked examples show that the [[Spearman Rank Correlation]] result is strongly affected by small changes in diet composition:
   - Example 1 vs. Example 2: the Schoener index falls from 0.81 to 0.74 when one food category's proportions are altered, while the major overlapping category remains unchanged.
   - The Spearman coefficient falls from 0.80 (p < 0.05) to a non-significant value (p > 0.10).
5. **Overlap is only as good as the measure of food availability.** Measures of diet based on numbers or volumes do not necessarily reflect what fish actually use. Abundant but rarely eaten items, or items that are hard to catch, may inflate or deflate overlap estimates.
6. **Ambiguities in the [[Levins Index]] and [[Horn Index]].** The authors report that overlap values from these indexes can be unstable when a food category is dominant for one species but absent for the other, and they question their use for this reason.
7. **Recommendation for researchers.** Investigators should state which diet measure and overlap index they use, and carefully justify that choice based on the particular diet items and the availability of those items in their study system.
