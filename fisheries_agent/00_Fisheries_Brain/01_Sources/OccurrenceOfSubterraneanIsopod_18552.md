# Occurrence of Subterranean Isopods in Epigean Waters

#carcinology #freshwater-ecology #asellidae

## Executive Summary

This study reports the frequent occurrence of the troglobitic isopod [[Asellus Stygius]] in surface (epigean) waters of Meade and Jefferson counties, [[Kentucky]]. The species was collected at 20 localities, most of them in [[Doe Run]]. There it persisted as a consistent but minor element of the riffle fauna, with populations reaching as many as 43 individuals on a single stone. The author argues that these surface populations are maintaining themselves outside the cave environment, which challenges the common view that cave organisms in surface habitats are merely accidental displacements.

## Background & Description

Troglobitic organisms found at the surface are usually attributed to accidental displacement from caves. This is especially true of highly modified cave asellids. Two cave species, [[Asellus Tridentatus]] and [[Asellus Conestogensis]], had previously been recorded from surface habitats. This paper extends the record to [[Asellus Stygius]], which was first collected in surface waters near the source of Doe Run in February 1959.

Four streams contrast sharply in physical ecology:

- **Doe Run:** A large spring-fed creek with an average flow exceeding 50 cubic feet per second. Its temperature extremes are moderated, and it flows mainly over rock for about 10 miles before entering the [[Ohio River]] about four miles upstream from Brandenburg.
- **Morgan's Creek:** A small spring (about 30 gallons per minute) emerging from [[Morgan's Cave]] in Otter Creek Park, Meade County. It runs about one mile to the Ohio River near Rock Haven, through a heavily shaded, vegetated ravine with relatively stable conditions.
- **Otter Creek:** A larger stream near [[Fort Knox]], fed by surface runoff and small springs. It experiences radical and frequent fluctuations in flow, temperature, and turbidity.
- **Harrod's Creek:** Similar to Otter Creek but somewhat larger. It enters the Ohio River upstream from [[Louisville]] in eastern Jefferson County.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat use.** In caves, the species is usually found under flat stones and debris. It is most abundant in small solution pools high above the existing water level. In surface waters, it occupies several microhabitats:

- Beneath larger stones on riffles, and under loosely associated rocks less than six inches in diameter in swift water. These resemble the cave environment and are comparable to surface sites of other species.
- Loose gravel bars, especially in the lower section of Doe Run. Clinging to the underside of stones or in crevices, the isopods were found to a depth of about six inches.
- Beneath marl-cemented conglomerate slabs in the middle section of Doe Run. These slabs form a "cave" habitat within the epigean waters through [[Marl Deposition]].

**Current and siltation.** In every Doe Run collection, the isopods occurred under moderate to rapid current with little siltation. Specimens placed in silty water quickly became incapacitated. The author suggests that suspended particles entangle the dense setation, so silt may restrict the species to areas of strong current.

**Associated fauna.** Three other asellids occur in Doe Run alongside the study species: [[Lirceus Fontinalis]], [[Asellus Brevicaudus]], and an unidentified Asellus under study by Barbara A. Walker. The unidentified form is the most abundant in the creek and was often found under the same rocks as the study species.

**Sampling methods.** Sampling included rubble examination, hand digging of gravel, and a square-foot [[Surber Sampler]] used on the marl habitat. The author reports no quantitative per-area data for most sites and notes that many gravel specimens may have been overlooked.

## Key Findings

- **Geographic occurrence:** [[Asellus Stygius]] was collected at 20 localities in epigean waters of Meade and Jefferson counties. By February 1961, it had been recorded at 12 localities on Doe Run, excluding three cave collections.
- **Population persistence:** Within two months of intensive sampling beginning in October 1959, the species was found at all five permanent Doe Run stations plus four additional localities. Repeated collections at the same stations indicate established surface populations.
- **Independence from caves:** Little correlation was found between collection sites and cave locations. The author considers the possibility of undetected fissures or springs in the creek bed, but states that the basin has been thoroughly explored.
- **Records from other streams:** Morgan's Creek yielded surface specimens on three occasions, with two collections about 200 yards above the Ohio River confluence and three within 300 yards of the cave. Otter Creek produced four small specimens beneath one stone in June 1960. Harrod's Creek produced two adults in October 1960.
- **Density:** The maximum was 43 individuals on a single stone (about 10 by 14 inches). The average from three Doe Run collections was about four per square foot, ranging from none to 43, based on 60 stones larger than six inches. Loose gravel averaged about eight per square foot, though some specimens may have been missed.
- **Size:** Specimens measured 4 to 10 mm in length (excluding antennae and uropods). Gravel-dwelling individuals were generally smaller.
- **Abundance in gravel:** Thirty specimens were collected in under half an hour on one gravel bar in Doe Run.
- **Marl habitat:** A single square-foot Surber sample under a marl slab yielded eight individuals, and hand examination revealed a large population beneath this and similar slabs.
- **Data caveat:** The abstract describes populations "as high as 43 individuals per square foot," whereas the body text reports 43 as the maximum on a single stone. The text states that no quantitative per-area data exist for the species in Doe Run.
- **Conclusion:** The author concludes that some [[Asellus Stygius]] populations maintain themselves in the surface environment, and that epigean colonization of non-cave habitats is more than an accidental occurrence.
