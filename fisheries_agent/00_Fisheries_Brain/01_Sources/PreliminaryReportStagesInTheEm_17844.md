# Preliminary Report: Stages in the Embryonic Development of the Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen-texanus

## Executive Summary

This 1975 preliminary report, prepared by [[Eric S Gustafson]] and submitted by [[W L Minckley]] of the [[Lower Colorado River Basin Research Laboratory]] at [[Arizona State University]], documents early embryonic and larval development of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The work was addressed to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Regional Office in Albuquerque. Embryos were obtained from [[Lake Mohave]] suckers and reared at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. The report gives a day-by-day staging sequence from hatching to juvenile pigmentation, with length and fin measurements, and sets out a Phase I plan for a complete morphological atlas of embryonic, prolarval, and postlarval stages. The authors state that the available hatchery specimens were insufficient for accurate staging.

## Background & Description

*Xyrauchen texanus* is a sucker native to the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]. The report describes its development as a sequence of morphological stages, with particular attention to:

- Increases in total length and pigmentation from hatching onward.
- Oral and propulsive structures (mouth, pectoral fins, caudal fin).
- Pigment lines (dorsal, lateral, ventro-visceral), tracked as [[Melanophore Distribution]] changes.
- Feeding and swimming behaviour, drawn from a hatchery observational record kept while the fish matured.

The study followed the terminology of earlier work, including [[Hubbs Terminology of Early Fish Stages]] (1943), [[Winn and Miller Postlarval Key]] (1954), and [[Balinski Cyprinid Development]] (1948) for pigment-line descriptions.

The project had several parts:

1. **Embryological research**, begun November 1974, centred on gross morphology.
2. **Photographic record** of razorback development, in preparation.
3. **Population survey** of razorback suckers in Lake Mohave, planned to start within the month.
4. **Juvenile husbandry**: live juveniles from the hatchery were held in an outdoor pond near Arizona State University, and observed a few days each week.

Phase I aims to document the gross morphology of all embryonic, prolarval, and postlarval stages across the species' life history.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Source of broodstock and eggs.** Breeding adults were captured at [[Arizona Cove]], [[Lake Mohave]], on 29 March 1974, and transferred to Willow Beach Hatchery. Eggs and sperm were stripped into buckets. The report gives a total of about 140 ml of eggs, collected from two females. Milt from a male was used for fertilization.

**Incubation conditions.** The egg batch was divided into three lots:

- About 100 ml was placed in a jar at 32°C (listed in the source as 53°F, which does not match this conversion and may be a transcription error). Growth was retarded and fungal infection set in. These eggs were discarded, though some early stages were preserved.
- The remaining 40 ml was placed on screens in an indoor raceway at 14°C (58°F).
- Between days 17 and 19 after fertilization, the eggs were moved to an outdoor raceway. The source gives temperatures of "10–19°C (58–66°F)"; the Celsius and Fahrenheit ranges do not fully agree, so the figures should be checked against the original.

**Sampling constraints.** Long gaps between samples limited the staging inventory. Day-by-day preservation, as practised by the hatchery, was judged too coarse to capture rapid early development. The authors argue that accurate staging requires direct observation of living embryos and hourly preservation. Some freshly preserved specimens were not obtained by ASU until eight months after preservation, so length data for them came from the hatchery's journal.

## Key Findings

**Developmental staging sequence (select stages, as reported):**

| Day | Length (mm) | Key developmental events |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 7.6 | Olfactory bulbs and pectoral fin buds present; yolk reduced; eye pigmentation increasing |
| 10 | 9.3 | Gills apparent; dorsal and ventral fin folds; pectoral fins 0.38 mm; larvae begin swimming off screens; eye pigmentation complete |
| 11 | 9.5 | Pectoral fins 0.56 mm; stomata open; larvae actively swimming and surfacing to feed on strained baby liver |
| 13 | 9.7 | Heart formed and visible; pigment spreading onto cerebellum |
| 16 | 9.8 | Lips forming, still terminal; pectoral fins 0.79 mm |
| 21 | 10.1 | Yolk completely absorbed; caudal and pectoral fins with rays; slight mouth movements |
| 28 | 10.4 | Pectoral fins 1.3 mm; fry feeding on natural foods in raceway; faecal material in intestine |
| 63 | 25.1 | Sucker-type mouth evident but still terminal; typical juvenile pigmentation |
| 73 | 30.8 | Fins fully formed; mouth still terminal |
| 103 | 45.5 | Gut contents include scuds, cladocerans, and algae; hump formation beginning; mouth deflecting ventrally; juvenile pigmentation complete; pectoral fins 4.2 mm |

**Main conclusions and plans:**

- Hatchery-provided specimens were inadequate for complete staging, although they yielded valuable information.
- Accurate staging needs live observation and hourly preservation of embryos, which the authors planned for a spawn expected that spring.
- The transition from yolk-dependent embryo to feeding fry occurred roughly between days 16 and 28, with swimming and surface feeding beginning around day 11 and natural foods appearing in the gut by day 28.
- Juvenile diet by day 103 included scuds, cladocerans, and algae.
- Phase I of the programme will produce a full morphological atlas, supported by a photographic record, and a population survey of razorback suckers in [[Lake Mohave]] was about to begin.
