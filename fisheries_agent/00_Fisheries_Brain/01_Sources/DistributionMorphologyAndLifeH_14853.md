# Distribution, Morphology and Life History of the Least Brook Lamprey (*Lampetra aepyptera*) in Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #petromyzontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1981 study by [[Walsh and Burr]] (Southern Illinois University at Carbondale), published in *Brimleyana* No. 6, maps the Kentucky distribution of the [[Least Brook Lamprey]], morphometrically compares over 220 adult specimens, and documents a neotenic population in Terrapin Creek. The authors conclude that the recently described *[[Lethenteron Meridionale]]* is a synonym of *L. aepyptera*, based on variable posterior circumoral teeth. The species is the most common and widespread lamprey in Kentucky. The Terrapin Creek population fails to complete nuptial transformation, which the authors tentatively attribute to limited food during the larval stage.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic context:** *L. aepyptera* (originally described by Abbott) is a nonparasitic lamprey. Earlier life-history studies were carried out in Maryland ([[Seversmith 1953]]) and Delaware ([[Rohde et al. 1976]]). [[Vladykov et al. 1975]] described *Lethenteron meridionale* from Tennessee, Alabama and Georgia, reportedly differing from *L. aepyptera* mainly in dentition.
- **Taxonomic outcome:** The authors found small, unicuspid posterior circumoral teeth in Kentucky specimens from several drainages. The mean number was 7.6 (range 1–22, N = 15). Their dentition data overlapped with those of *L. meridionale*. The authors therefore treated *L. meridionale* as a synonym. [[Bailey 1980]] had downgraded *Okkelbergia* and *Lethenteron* to subgenera of *Lampetra*, which the authors accepted.
- **Morphometrics:** Measurements were taken to the nearest 0.01 mm with dial calipers. Dentition followed [[Hubbs and Potter 1971]] and [[Potter 1980]]. Oral discs were stained with alizarin red S to aid tooth counts.
- **Adult dentition means (N = 17):**
  - Oral papillae: 16.3 (range 12–20)
  - Infraoral cusps: 9.8 (range 5–13)
  - Intermediate teeth in anterior field: 14.3 (range 0–32)
  - Inner lateral teeth: 6.2 (range 4–9)
- **Myomeres:** Adults from Kentucky ranged from 52 to 62, with drainage means between 55 and 59, except the lower Cumberland and Tennessee rivers. A one-way ANOVA found no significant differences among drainages (α = 0.05). Terrapin Creek ammocoetes (N = 105) ranged from 51 to 59 (x̄ = 55.0).
- **Body proportions:** Males averaged TL 134.6 mm (N = 67, range 80–165) and females 132.0 mm (N = 44, range 115–158). Males had greater disc length, second dorsal fin height and prebranchial length, and longer tails and shorter trunks than females. The mean male genital papilla length was 4.4 mm (range 2.4–7.5, N = 57).
- **Breeding coloration:** Nest-building adults at Peter Creek, Barren County, on 18 March 1980 were mottled gray-brown dorsally, with silvery-yellow venters, black lateral bands, and dark-speckled dorsal fin margins. A gold band crossed the caudal fin base.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** The species is the most common lamprey in Kentucky. It occurs in all major state drainages except the Tradewater River and lower Ohio and Mississippi tributaries in the extreme west (Burr 1980). It is collected less often in the [[Kentucky River]] system than in other eastern drainages. Its occurrence in sandy tributaries of the [[Obion River]] is unusual because that area is typically inhabited by Coastal Plain fishes.
- **Habitat preference:** Adults were most often collected in riffles and raceways of small to medium sand-gravel creeks during March and April. The authors suggest that few Kentucky records result from scarce early-spring collections in small streams.
- **Terrapin Creek:** This is a clear, low-to-moderate-gradient tributary of the Obion River, 2 to 5 m wide and 0.2 to 1.0 m deep at normal flow, bordered by deciduous forest. Ammocoetes were found in mud banks and moderate-current pools. Transformers and neotenes were found in debris-ridden riffles.
- **Beaver slough:** A backwater slough 0.8 km south of Bell City, inundated by beaver activity, had watercress and other macrophytes. A 2.5 m × 1.5 m pool with silt yielded many ammocoetes.
- **Fish community:** Terrapin Creek supports at least 30 fish species ([[Table 3]]). Relative abundance ranked *L. aepyptera* highest at 16.6%, followed by [[Etheostoma zonale]] (15.4%) and [[Etheostoma nigrum]] (13.6%). Other taxa included [[Phenacobius mirabilis]], [[Umbra limi]] and [[Semotilus atromaculatus]].
- **Nest and spawning sites:** Nests were found at a riffle crest in 15 cm of water, about 1.8 m from shore, over sand and loose gravel, at 12 °C. Reported spawning temperatures range from 10 to 16 °C ([[Brigham 1973]]; [[Rohde et al. 1976]]; [[Seversmith 1953]]).

## Key Findings

**1. Distribution and taxonomy**
- Over 220 adults were examined from the drainages listed in the collection records.
- The authors recognize *[[Lampetra Aepyptera]]* as the valid name and *Lethenteron meridionale* as a junior synonym.
- Morphometric variation among Kentucky drainages falls within the normal range for the species in the Ohio Valley.

**2. Terrapin Creek sampling**
- Intermittent sampling from 23 March 1978 to 4 June 1980 yielded 138 specimens, all from the Obion River drainage.
- Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin and transferred to 70% ethanol.

**3. Growth and larval life**
- Length-frequency analysis of 45 ammocoetes (4 June 1980) indicated at least five, and possibly six, age classes.
- Mean TL by age: age 0 = 16 mm; age I = 38.6 mm; age II = 65.0 mm; age III = 88.8 mm.
- Estimated larval duration was 4.5 years, or 5.5 years if arrested growth occurs.
- Transformers averaged 112 mm TL (range 95–125, N = 14). Neotenes averaged 103 mm (range 75–129, N = 15).
- Condition factor K averaged 0.15 for ammocoetes (N = 107), 0.17 for transformers (N = 14), and 0.13 for neotenes (N = 15).

**4. Length-weight relationship**
- For ammocoetes, the authors reported Log W = 1.95 + 0.352 Log L (r = 0.99). The regression for transformers and neotenes combined was Log W = 1.96 + 0.248 Log L (r = 0.92).
- **Erratum (1981):** These equations were corrected to Log W = −5.48 + 2.82 Log L (r = 0.99) for ammocoetes, and Log W = −6.72 + 3.43 Log L (r = 0.87) for transformers and neotenes. [[Rohde et al. 1976]] reported a weight increase proportional to the 2.73 power of length in Delaware.

**5. Reproduction and fecundity**
- Reported adult sex ratios range from 1:3 ([[Seversmith 1953]]) to 2.7:1 ([[Rohde et al. 1976]]). The Knob Creek sample from the [[Salt River]] was 2.3:1 (N = 108).
- Spawning in Kentucky was recorded between 22 February and 1 June, with a peak from mid-March to early April.
- Literature fecundity estimates range from 610 to 2154 eggs per female.
- Three Kentucky females (115, 142 and 142 mm TL) had 1802, 2596 and 3816 mature ova, with egg diameters of 0.70–1.00 mm (x̄ = 0.86 mm, N = 43).
- Five neotenic Terrapin Creek females (105–119 mm TL) had 572 to 1541 mature ova, with egg diameters of 0.66–1.36 mm (x̄ = 0.99 mm, N = 100). This matches the inverse fecundity–egg size trend reported by Rohde et al.

**6. Neoteny in Terrapin Creek**
- Seven neotenic males and six neotenic females were collected. Partially transformed individuals lacked normal nuptial traits, such as enlarged eyes, melanistic pigmentation, enlarged dorsal fins and an enlarged disc. Some females were fully mature, with ripe ova visible through the body wall.
- Numerous collections contained no nuptial adults, which the authors regard as the normal condition for this population.
- Temperature was ruled out as a cause, because Terrapin Creek temperatures resemble those of streams with normal adults.
- The authors tentatively attribute the failure to transform to limited food during the ammocoete stage, supported by low K values and low fish abundance.
- Only two other nonparasitic lampreys are documented as neotenic: [[Lampetra Zanandreai]] (Europe) and [[Lampetra Lethophaga]] (northern California).

**7. Other observations**
- A Cumberland River population above Cumberland Falls had a relatively low second dorsal fin height, which the authors suggest may reflect reproductive isolation.
- Kentucky specimens are longer on average than Atlantic Coast populations.
- Spawning in the upper Cumberland drainage (Taylor Branch, 16 April 1977) occurred over bedrock without nest construction.

**Limitations noted by the authors:** Data were limited for fall months, so arrested growth could not be tested. A thorough range-wide study is needed to test possible morphological variation in the species.
