# West American Freshwater Mollusca, 1: Bibliography of Pleistocene and Recent Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-mollusca

## Executive Summary

This document is the front matter and bibliography of Memoir 4 of the [[San Diego Society Of Natural History]] (1970), compiled by [[Dwight W Taylor]]. It is the first installment of a planned review of the freshwater and brackish-water Mollusca of western North America, covering both Pleistocene fossils and living forms. The compilation includes a dedication, a preface on the purpose of scientific bibliography, an introduction defining scope, a list of sources and acknowledgments, statistical analyses of publication serials, geographic and topical indices, and an alphabetical bibliography of roughly 1,777 titles. Part 2 is announced as a compendium of nominal species. The work does not present original field data; it is a reference tool for locating literature.

## Background & Description

The memoir was issued June 30, 1970, and is dedicated to [[Stillman Berry]], the author's long-time mentor, who helped him publish his first paper while Taylor was seventeen. The preface argues that descriptive science depends on periodic bibliographies, noting that the previous comprehensive summary of this subject dated back more than a century, to the [[Smithsonian Institution]] volumes on land and freshwater shells of North America (Binney, 1865) and British Association reviews by Carpenter (1857, 1864).

The bibliography is designed for direct library use: citations are written out in full, Library of Congress and Superintendent of Documents call numbers are supplied, and subject and geographic indices are included. Acknowledgments credit libraries including the [[California Academy Of Sciences]], [[Scripps Institution Of Oceanography]], [[Stanford University]], and the [[United States National Museum]], along with several individual librarians and malacologists.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Scope.** Geographically, the area runs from the eastern edge of the Rocky Mountains and the Plateau of Mexico to the Pacific, from Alaska to the southern end of the Plateau of Mexico and to Bandera Bay on Mexico's west coast, plus the Rio Grande drainage and much of southern and central Texas. The core focus is characteristically freshwater fauna. Included brackish-water and marginal groups are Assimineidae, Corbiculidae, Ellobiidae, [[Cerithidea]] (Potamididae), Truncatellidae, high intertidal Littorinidae, Rangia (Mactridae), Neritina and Theodoxus (Neritidae), and brackish-water Hydrobiidae. Both native and introduced species are covered.

**Geologic scope.** Geologic papers are included when they record post-Blancan Pleistocene fossils or pre-Pleistocene occurrences of species still living in western North America.

**Archaeological and parasitological links.** Many mollusc records come from archaeological sites (e.g., shellmounds and rockshelters), and many molluscs serve as intermediate hosts for trematodes. Topics of medical relevance include [[Fascioliasis]], [[Salmon Poisoning]], [[Schistosome Dermatitis]], and [[Schistosomiasis]], though the memoir notes that human schistosomiasis is unknown in western North America.

## Key Findings

- **Bibliographic content:** Approximately 1,777 titles are listed, of which 1,583 (89.4%) are serial and 194 (10.6%) nonserial. For 1950–1969 alone, 657 titles were recorded (610 serial, 47 nonserial).
- **Literature concentration:** Of 304 serials cited overall, 64 have five or more titles and account for about 76% of serial literature; only 14 serials have 20 or more titles, accounting for about 48%. Over 1950–1969, the leading serials were the [[Journal Of Parasitology]] (62 titles), the [[Nautilus]] (53), and the [[Veliger]] (24).
- **Shifting research fields:** The most-cited list over the full period is led by the Nautilus (317 titles) and the Journal of Parasitology (84). Taylor concludes that the decline of short species-naming papers has been accompanied by growth in archaeology, parasitology, geology, and ecology as sources of molluscan literature.
- **Limitations of completeness:** The author judges the bibliography possibly complete for new species descriptions, Pleistocene fossil records, and trematode parasite works, but probably least complete for ecology, limnology, and fisheries literature.
- **Recommendation:** Future papers on the western fauna should be placed in the dominant journals where length, illustration, and cost permit, and the author argues that standard indices are inadequate outside specialized fields such as parasitology.
- **Index structure:** Geographic indices cover Alaska, Alberta, Arizona, Baja California, British Columbia, California (northern and southern), Chihuahua, Coahuila, Colorado, Mexican states, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Oregon, Texas, Utah, Washington, Wyoming, Yukon, and others, with asterisks marking fossil or archaeological papers.
- **Bibliographies of zoologists:** The memoir lists about 40 malacologist bibliographies, including those of [[Frank Collins Baker]], [[Henry Augustus Pilsbry]], and [[Junius Henderson]].
- **Planned Part 2:** A compendium of nominal species with original descriptions, types, and type localities.
