## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Colorado River Fishery Project) letter from Frank K. Pfeifer to Dr. Thomas Dowling (Arizona State University), accompanied by extensive data tables tracking genetic sample chain-of-custody for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally endangered catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The correspondence coordinates a multi-institutional genetic analysis effort — mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) restriction fragment analysis by Dowling, RAPD analysis by Marek Kaliszewski (Brigham Young University), allozyme analysis by Dr. Buth (UCLA), and otolith aging by Dr. Minckley — on 31 razorback or razorback-like fish collected from the upper Colorado River. Of these, 21 were presumed pure razorback suckers and 10 were suspected [[Razorback Sucker × Flannelmouth Sucker Hybrids]]. The associated haplotype tables present mtDNA restriction site data across multiple wild and hatchery populations, including [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Powell]], the [[Green River|Green/Yampa River]], the [[Little Colorado River]], and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] broodstock, providing a snapshot of range-wide mtDNA haplotype diversity and evidence of introgressive hybridization with the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*).

## Background & Description

Although this is primarily a genetic sample-tracking document, biometric data were recorded at time of tissue sampling for individual fish:

- **Total length**: ranged roughly from 285 mm (a small suspected hybrid, HYB-007) up to 592 mm among presumed pure razorback suckers.
- **Weight**: ranged from ~184 g (small hybrid) to ~2,921 g (large pure razorback sucker, RSB-002).
- **Sex determination**: recorded for several fish via expressed gametes (milt = male, eggs = female); many specimens listed as sex unknown ("?").
- **Tissue sampling protocol**: fin clips and muscle plugs (FIN, MUS) collected from live/pond fish; whole-body samples preserved frozen for dead specimens; gamete samples (milt, eggs) collected opportunistically during spawning condition.
- **Aging methodology**: otolith extraction and reading planned for the 16 dead specimens held at ASU, to be conducted by Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]]. Field notes reference otolith condition issues (e.g., "broken," "little shining"), indicating some samples were compromised for aging purposes.

No morphometric ratios, meristic counts, or diagnostic external characters distinguishing pure razorback suckers from hybrids are provided in this text; identification as "razorback-like" appears field/phenotype-based pending genetic confirmation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range sampled**: Upper Colorado River mainstem sites (e.g., River Mile 146.7, 174.4, 175.2, 235.0), off-channel/floodplain habitats ([[Highline Lake]], [[Clough Pond]], [[Etter Pond]]), and reservoir populations ([[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Powell]]).
- **Additional basin coverage**: [[Green River]]/[[Yampa River]] confluence area, [[Little Colorado River]] tributary system, and [[Colorado River Indian Tribe]] irrigation canals — indicating the species' (and hybrid swarm's) persistence in both lotic mainstem and lentic/impounded habitats across the basin.
- **Hatchery/refugial context**: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] maintained captive broodstock lineages traced to wild-caught founders from "Buenos Aires" (likely referring to a specific source population/lake), with distinct 1987, 1989, and 1990 year-classes — reflecting ex situ conservation propagation efforts for this endangered species.
- **Sympatry and hybridization risk**: The presence of suspected hybrids carrying mtDNA haplotypes matching *Catostomus latipinnis* (flannelmouth sucker, annotated "LATIPINNIS MTDNA") at Highline Lake indicates ecological overlap and interbreeding between [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] in shared spawning habitat, a significant conservation concern for genetic integrity of the endangered species.
- **Disposition of specimens**: Live fish were largely released back to source waters (e.g., "RELEASED ALIVE" at Highline); mortalities were transferred to Dexter NFH ("NFH-MORT") or frozen for museum/genetic archiving at ASU.

## Key Findings

- **Sample scope**: 31 total razorback/razorback-like fish sampled from the upper Colorado River for tissue analysis; 21 presumed pure [[Razorback Sucker]], 10 suspected hybrids.
- **Multi-lab genetic workflow established**: mtDNA restriction analysis (Dowling, ASU) → RAPD analysis (Kaliszewski, BYU) → allozyme analysis (Buth, UCLA) → otolith aging (Minckley) — an integrated approach combining nuclear, mitochondrial, and hard-part aging data for the same specimens.
- **Sample custody discrepancy noted**: Of 22 fish expected to have usable mtDNA material (15 pure, 7 hybrids) at ASU, only 16 dead specimens (11 pure, 5 hybrids) were physically confirmed in Dowling's possession, with the remainder represented by gamete samples sent separately — prompting the letter's clarification effort to the Biology Committee.
- **Live pure razorback rarity**: Of the 21 pure razorback suckers collected since 1988, only 3 remained alive at time of writing (1992), and none had yet contributed gametes for genetic analysis — underscoring the critically endangered status and small extant wild population size.
- **mtDNA haplotype diversity documented**: Restriction fragment patterns across 8 enzymes (MboI, HinPI, HinfI, TaqI, ScrfI, NheI, BstEII, BanI) were cataloged for numerous individuals across [[Lake Mohave]] (25 haplotypes listed), [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Powell]], [[Green River]]/[[Yampa River]] (1990–1991 cohorts), and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] broodstock (1987, 1989, 1990 year-classes) — forming a range-wide baseline for population genetic structure in *Xyrauchen texanus*.
- **Evidence of introgression**: Multiple Highline Lake specimens flagged as hybrids (HY) carried mtDNA haplotypes scored as "LATIPINNIS MTDNA," directly implicating maternal-line hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and confirming field suspicions of hybrid status via molecular data.
- **Data quality caveats**: Several samples across tables were flagged as "old samples of poor quality" (excluded from analysis) or having "too little tissue for accurate scoring," and the 1989 Green River hatchery-produced upper basin samples (UBXT series) had not yet been rescored against updated haplotype designations — highlighting ongoing data standardization challenges in the genetic monitoring program.
- **Institutional/regulatory note**: Despite interest in accessioning dead specimens into the ASU museum collection, carcasses remained U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service property and were required to stay in ultra-cold storage pending further agency authorization.
