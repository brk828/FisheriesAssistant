## Executive Summary
This document is a 1995 interagency review memorandum from the Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Yuma District, responding to a draft "Fisheries Management Prospectus" for indigenous "big river" fishes of the Lower Colorado River Basin, prepared for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research, the memo critiques the draft plan's lack of measurable objectives, insufficient detail on habitat refugia development, and omission of existing conservation infrastructure. The two indigenous species explicitly named are the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail]], both imperiled "big river" fishes native to the Colorado River system. The memo underscores the need for a more rigorous, recovery-plan-style framework with defined delisting/downlisting criteria and a prioritized geographic strategy for habitat restoration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Geographic Scope:** Lower Colorado River Basin, spanning multiple jurisdictions including BLM-managed lands, national wildlife refuges, and the **Imperial Division** of the Colorado River.
- **Habitat Types Referenced:**
  - **Riverine Cove Refugia** — constructed or managed cove habitats intended to support recovery populations; existing examples cited near **[[Lake Havasu]]**.
  - **Grow-out Facilities** — used cooperatively by BLM and USFWS to rear [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] to larger, more predator-resistant size classes before release, located on Lake Havasu.
  - **Large Backwater Habitats** — proposed as dedicated native fish refugia, discussed by the **Backwaters Subcommittee of the Lower Colorado River Work Group**, with potential development sites in the Imperial Division and elsewhere along the river.
- **Land Tenure Context:** The memo notes that refugia planning in the draft prospectus disproportionately emphasizes **national wildlife refuge** lands, while overlooking existing or potential efforts on **non-refuge (e.g., BLM-managed) lands**.
- **Threats Implied:** The plan's stated goal of "expanding habitat and populations" and precluding "losses due to catastrophes" implies ongoing risks to population viability from stochastic events (e.g., drought, flow alteration, habitat loss) affecting these Colorado River endemics.

## Key Findings
- The draft prospectus deviates from the **traditional threatened/endangered species recovery plan format**, and the reviewing agency (BLM) requests justification for this structural change if the document is intended to function as a recovery plan.
- **Objectives are not measurable** — the BLM recommends that stated objectives include quantifiable criteria, particularly explicit **downlisting/delisting criteria** for the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]].
- **Existing management techniques are inadequately documented**, notably the collaborative razorback sucker and bonytail grow-out program at **[[Lake Havasu]]**, which is omitted from the "Riverine Cove Refugia" discussion in the draft.
- The plan lacks **monitoring evaluation**, including assessment of past refugia/cove management successes and failures — the BLM requests this analysis be incorporated.
- **Large-scale backwater refugia** require a clearer overall development strategy: it is unresolved whether these habitats will be **distributed basin-wide** or **concentrated on national wildlife refuges**.
- The BLM recommends the final plan include **specific target areas**, a **development schedule**, and a **priority listing** for habitat restoration sites to meet stated population expansion and catastrophe-mitigation goals.
- The memorandum reflects active **interagency coordination** among BLM, USFWS, and the **Lower Colorado River Work Group** (via its Backwaters Subcommittee) in shaping conservation strategy for these indigenous big-river fishes.
