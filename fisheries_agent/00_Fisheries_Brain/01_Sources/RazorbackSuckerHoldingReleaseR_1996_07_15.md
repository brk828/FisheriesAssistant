## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service correspondence from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery detailing a short-term holding event for 16 [[Razorback Sucker]] individuals transferred to the hatchery on behalf of the Hualapai Tribe (Hualapai Game and Fish). Over a ~4-week holding period in 56°F flow-through tanks, four fish died, with mortality attributed in part to ectoparasitic infestation (*[[Costia]]* and *[[Chilodonella]]*). The Pinetop Fish Health Center and Bozeman Fish Health Center were consulted for diagnostics. The surviving 12 fish were PIT-tagged, sexed, and released into [[Lake Mohave]] at River Mile 41 on July 12, 1996. This record serves as a primary-source case study in captive holding stress, parasitic disease management, and reintroduction protocol for an endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] catostomid.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native range context**: *[[Xyrauchen texanus]]* is endemic to the [[Colorado River]] drainage; this event involved a temporary translocation from field collection (presumably Colorado River/Lake Mohave stock) into hatchery care and subsequent return to the wild.
- **Release habitat**: [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the Colorado River between Hoover and Davis Dams, at River Mile 41 — a known refuge/stocking site for endangered razorback sucker recovery programs.
- **Holding conditions**: Fish held in a flow-through system at 56°F (13.3°C), reflecting cold-water hatchery conditions atypical of the species' warmer natural riverine/reservoir habitat, which may have contributed to physiological stress and susceptibility to parasitic infection.
- **Disease ecology**: Ectoparasites *[[Costia]]* (*Ichthyobodo* sp.) and *[[Chilodonella]]* are common opportunistic protozoan parasites in stressed or crowded captive freshwater fish, exploiting compromised host immune status during confinement.

## Key Findings

- **Initial stock**: 16 razorback suckers delivered June 11, 1996, held in 56°F flow-through tankhouse system for the [[Hualapai Tribe]] under coordination with [[Hualapai Game and Fish]].
- **Mortality Event 1 (June 29)**: Fish #7F7B1D351B found dead; cause undetermined; carcass frozen for later analysis.
- **Mortality Event 2 (July 8)**: Fish #7F7E1E380F found dead; post-mortem gill/skin exam revealed heavy co-infestation of *[[Costia]]* and *[[Chilodonella]]*, presumed contributing mortality factor.
  - **Treatment response**: Remaining fish treated with 167 ppm formalin for 1 hour (administered twice: July 8 and July 9) plus a salt block to mitigate osmotic/handling stress.
  - Consultation initiated with [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]]; fish pathologist Jerry Landye scheduled for on-site visit July 13.
- **Mortality Event 3 (July 11)**: Fish #7F7E2D695D found dead, frozen immediately. A second compromised fish (#7F7E1F2D0F) was euthanized upon Landye's arrival for full post-mortem exam.
  - No external parasites found on this specimen (contrasting Event 2), suggesting mortality may have had a distinct or systemic cause.
  - Tissue samples collected and shipped to [[Bozeman Fish Health Center]] for further laboratory analysis.
- **Total mortality**: 4 of 16 fish died during the ~1-month holding period (25% mortality rate).
- **Release**: Remaining 12 razorback suckers (6 female, 6 male) released into [[Lake Mohave]] at River Mile 41 on July 12, 1996, each individually identifiable via PIT tag:
