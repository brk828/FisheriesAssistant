# Key to the British Freshwater Cladocera (Second Edition, 1958)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cladocera

*Note on scope:* This document is a taxonomic key to freshwater Cladocera (water fleas), which are crustaceans, not fish. The tags above follow the requested format and reflect the aquatic-ecology context of the source.

## Executive Summary

This is the second edition (1958) of the Freshwater Biological Association's *Scientific Publication No. 5*, a key to the British freshwater [[Cladocera]]. The original author, D. J. Scourfield, died in 1949, so J. P. Harding of the [[British Museum (Natural History)]] revised the work. The key replaces the earlier dichotomous key to families with illustrated family drawings and explanatory notes, and it adds ecological notes and Lake District distribution records, largely supplied by W. J. P. Smyly. The key covers roughly 100 species and varieties across eight families, with a Lake District record for each where known.

## Background & Description

- **Order and affinities:** Cladocera are an order of the Branchiopoda, closest to the bivalved Conchostraca and more distantly related to Copepoda, Ostracoda and Cirripedia.
- **Feeding types:** Calyptomera feed on small particles (algae, detritus) by filtering or rasping. Gymnomera are predatory and eat other small crustaceans.
- **Habitat:** Almost entirely freshwater. Only three genera ([[Podon]], [[Evadne]] and [[Penilia]]) are marine. Limnetic species are transparent; pond and margin species are often coloured.
- **Reproduction:** Mostly parthenogenetic females for much of the year. Males appear in autumn or under stress, producing fertilised resting ("winter") eggs protected by an [[Ephippium]]. Resting eggs survive drying and freezing, and some have hatched from mud kept dry for over twenty years.
- **Dispersal:** Ephippia can float and be carried by wind or birds, which likely explains the sporadic appearance and wide distribution of many species.
- **Structure:** Cladocera are defined by two-branched second antennae used for swimming. In Calyptomera the body is enclosed in a bivalve carapace. *[[Daphnia]] hyalina* var. *galeata*, the common Daphnia of Windermere, is used as the model for the group.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake District survey:** Records cover a representative selection of tarns and lakes, with a much larger set of smaller tarns held at The Ferry House. Abbreviations are used for about 50 named waters, including [[Windermere]], [[Esthwaite Water]], [[Derwentwater]], [[Bassenthwaite Lake]], [[Wastwater]], [[Grasmere]], [[Rydal Water]] and [[Haweswater]].
- **Habitat associations:** Many species live among [[Potamogeton]] weeds, in [[Sphagnum]] swamps, or on mud. Some are planktonic in open water. Holopedium is restricted to clear, soft-water lakes. Some Daphnia occur in warm, shallow "duck ponds".
- **Acidity:** *[[Acantholeberis curvirostris]]* is recorded from shallow acid waters, especially over Sphagnum. *[[Alona quadrangularis]]* favours acid waters among weeds.
- **Distribution limits:** Several species are recorded only from southern or eastern England. Some are northern forms (for example *[[Ophryoxus gracilis]]*, recorded from Loch Ness). Others are known only from a few uncertain records.
- **Seasonal plankton:** *[[Bythotrephes longimanus]]* and *[[Leptodora kindti]]* occur in Windermere plankton from May to October, and Leptodora occasionally in November. *[[Holopedium gibberum]]* occurs in Windermere only occasionally in summer and autumn, probably washed down from Grasmere and Rydal.

## Key Findings

- **Structure of the key:** The family key uses drawings and notes rather than a conventional dichotomous key, because families are hard to separate by strictly conventional features. The genus and species keys remain dichotomous.
- **Family count:** Eight families are covered: Sididae (3 species in the key), Holopedidae (1), Bosminidae (1 genus, 8 taxa), Daphniidae (about 33 taxa in 4 genera), Macrothricidae, Chydoridae, Polyphemidae (1 species) and Leptodoridae (1 species).
- **Taxonomic revisions:** Dr Harding brought *Daphnia* and *Bosmina* into line with modern ideas. Daphnia is separated into groups (magna, pulex, longispina) on claw combs, head shape and crest form.
- **Most widespread species:** *Chydorus sphaericus* is described as the commonest and most widely distributed of all Cladocera. *Alona affinis* is "very common and widely distributed", and *Bosmina longirostris* is common in S and E England.
- **Lake District sample:** For most species the Lake District records list many waters. *Daphnia hyalina* var. *galeata* is planktonic in Windermere all year. Males and ephippial females of this variety have been seen only once or twice.
- **Rare or unrecorded in the Lake District:** Many species have "not recorded" entries for the Lake District, including *[[Daphnia magna]]*, *[[Daphnia curvirostris]]*, *[[Scapholeberis aurita]]* and *[[Moina brachiata]]*.
- **Introduced species caveat:** *[[Daphnia ambigua]]* was found in Kew Gardens and Regent's Park, and may be introduced.
- **Taxonomic sources:** The key follows [[Lilljeborg]]'s *Cladocera Suecia* (1900) for most names. Other key references include Birge (1918) and Keilhack (1909). The reference list contains about 40 items, including [[Scourfield]] (1903–1946) and [[Gurney]] (1903–1929).
