# Two New Species of the Crayfish Genus Procambarus (Decapoda, Astacidae) with Keys to the Members of the Spiculifer Group

#decapoda #astacidae #procambarus

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes two new crayfish species of the genus [[Procambarus]] and provides dichotomous keys to the [[Spiculifer Group]]. [[Procambarus Elegans]] is known from five localities in the [[Ouachita River]] system of Louisiana. [[Procambarus Gibbus]] is known from seven localities in the middle [[Flint River]] drainage, chiefly the [[Muckalee Creek]] watershed, in Georgia. Both species have restricted ranges, and both are rare in the material examined. The paper also gives a diagnosis, morphological comparisons and range summaries for the group's species, which are defined by the presence of two or more cervical spines on each side of the carapace.

## Background & Description

The paper is a formal species description. Its main morphological basis is the first pleopod of the male, with additional characters from the chelae, pereiopods, annulus ventralis (female) and carapace. Measurements are given in tables for the holotype, allotype and morphotype of each species.

**Procambarus elegans** (named for its coloration and apparent rarity):
- Carapace: two cervical spines per side; areola 5.19 to 5.9 times longer than wide; areola 26.6 to 28.6 percent of carapace length.
- Holotype: carapace length 53.2 mm, the largest specimen available. Only two first form males were available.
- First pleopod: asymmetrical, with a slender mesial process, a short cephalic process, a truncate caudal knob bearing a toothlike caudal process, and a short corneous central projection.
- Colour: straw-brown carapace with dark brown longitudinal stripes; pinkish tan branchiostegites flecked with dark blotches; dark banded fingers with orange tips.

**Procambarus gibbus** (named for the swollen caudodistal part of the male first pleopod):
- Carapace: two strong cervical spines per side; areola 2.8 to 3.5 times longer than wide; areola 23.8 to 27.2 percent of carapace length.
- Holotype: carapace length 37.0 mm. The largest first form male measured 42.2 mm and the smallest 29.8 mm. The largest specimen overall was a second form male at 46.9 mm.
- First pleopod: lacks a cephalic process; has a curved mesial process, a simple bladelike caudal process, and a conspicuous beaklike central projection. The annulus ventralis has a deep median groove.
- Colour: pale tan carapace with dark brown rostral and postorbital margins; abdominal terga tan to olive green with scarlet spots; chela brownish black with scarlet finger tips.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Procambarus elegans** occurs in the Ouachita River system in Claiborne, Ouachita and Union parishes, Louisiana. The type locality is the spillway of [[Corey Lake]], an impounded tributary of the Ouachita, with "coffee-tinted" water, sluggish to moderately swift current, and an eroded clay substrate. Aquatic plants there include *Myriophyllum*, *Potamogeton* and *Ludwigia*. The confluence of channels forms a stream about 40 feet wide and more than five feet deep. A 1965 collecting trip of about four hours yielded at least 100 crayfish (47 retained), of which only two were *P. elegans*. The holotype was found under a large log lodged against the bank of a larger channel. Associated crayfish included *Procambarus acutus acutus*, *Procambarus clarkii*, [[Procambarus Vioscai]], [[Cambarellus Puer]], [[Fallicambarus Hedgpethi]], [[Faxonella Clypeata]] and *Orconectes palmeri longimanus*.

**Procambarus gibbus** is known from the middle Flint River drainage in Crawford, Lee, Schley and Sumter counties, Georgia. The type locality is a comparatively clear tributary to Muckalee Creek, 3.2 miles north of Americus. It is 10 to 15 feet wide and about two feet deep, with a moderate current over a sandy, organic bed and a stand of *Vallisneria* in the unshaded area near the bridge. Shoreline trees include willow, tulip poplar, oak, maple and magnolia. No other crayfish were collected there. Associated species at other localities included *Procambarus paeninsulanus*, Faxonella clypeata, *Cambarus diogenes diogenes* and *Cambarus latimanus*. First form males were collected in April and August, and no egg-bearing or juvenile-carrying females were found.

**Group-level ecology:** The Spiculifer Group comprises 14 stream-dwelling species in the Coastal Plain and Piedmont provinces. Its range extends from the Trinity River, Texas, east to the Edisto River, South Carolina, and the Suwannee River, Florida. Members occasionally occur in areas without obvious current but are not known from lentic habitats. Five species (*P. ablusus*, *P. echinatus*, *P. lagniappe*, *P. ouachitae* and *P. penni*) have allopatric ranges. Only two co-occurring species combinations have been recorded at a single locality: *P. elegans* with *P. vioscai* in Louisiana, and *P. spiculifer* with *P. versutus* in Alabama and Florida.

## Key Findings

- **Two new species described:** *Procambarus elegans* (Louisiana) and *Procambarus gibbus* (Georgia), both with limited known ranges.
- **Closest relatives of *P. elegans*:** [[Procambarus Natchitochae]] and [[Procambarus Ablusus]]. It differs from *P. natchitochae* by a much shorter central projection and a truncate caudal knob, and from *P. ablusus* in the caudal aspect and in the non-recurved central projection.
- **Closest relative of *P. gibbus*:** [[Procambarus Spiculifer]], whose range completely surrounds that of *P. gibbus*. *P. gibbus* is distinguished by the broad, subtruncate caudolateral portion of the male first pleopod, whereas in *P. spiculifer* this margin tapers sharply toward the caudal process.
- **Type material:** The *P. elegans* holotype, allotype, morphotype and one paratype are deposited at the [[Smithsonian Institution]]. The *P. gibbus* types and paratypes (including 4 first form males, 7 second form males, 16 females and numerous juveniles) are also deposited there.
- **Key structure:** The keys separate first form males by the position and form of the cephalic process, the caudal knob, and the central projection. Females are separated by the annulus ventralis and the sternal tubercles or lobes. The keys include range summaries for each species.
- **Rarity:** Both species were uncommon in the collections studied. This fits the paper's emphasis on restricted ranges and the low number of specimens available.
- **Naming:** *P. elegans* was named for its coloration and apparent rarity. *P. gibbus* was named for the swollen caudodistal portion of the male first pleopod.
