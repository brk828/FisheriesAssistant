# Review of Draft Bonytail and Humpback Chub Recovery Plans

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is a formal review letter from Paul E. Marsh, Associate Research Scientist at the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies (Tempe, Arizona), to Dr. Larry Shanks of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Denver, Colorado). It responds to the Service's request for comments on draft [[Recovery Plan]]s for the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The reviewer finds the plans generally comprehensive but raises specific objections on references, hatchery suitability, broodstock collection, budget allocation, and schedule consistency. The letter is dated 13 September (year partly garbled in OCR).

## Background & Description

- **Subject species:** Bonytail (*Gila elegans*) and Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*), both Cyprinidae, with the draft plans also referencing [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Catostomidae).
- **Document type:** Single-author peer review letter with page-keyed comments and enclosed, annotated copies of the draft plans.
- **Purpose:** To assist the Service in revising the bonytail and humpback chub recovery plans before finalization.
- **Context:** The bonytail and humpback chub are rare, and the reviewer notes that wild individuals are scarcely reproducing or recruiting in the wild, so captive propagation and wild-fish collection are central management issues.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Wild bonytail status:** A bonytail captured in [[Lake Mohave]] in March 1988 was aged by otolith examination at about 13 years, which the reviewer reads as evidence of natural reproduction in that reservoir. Several additional bonytail were collected from Lake Mohave in early 1989, including multiple individuals in May 1989, described as a remarkable catch.
- **Lake Mohave significance:** The reviewer objects to wording that makes Lake Mohave appear to be a lower-priority recovery area than other habitat for bonytail.
- **Green River:** The reviewer requests preliminary results of experimental stocking or monitoring of bonytail in the Green River.
- **Hatchery concerns:** [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] facilities, which the reviewer describes as artificial, insecure watering tanks, have experienced water-quality, infrastructure, and thermal problems that caused significant losses of native razorback suckers. The reviewer advises against designating the facility as a refugium without expert evaluation.
- **Thermal regime at Willow Beach:** [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] is a cold-water trout hatchery supplied with perennially cold water (about 11–13 °C) released from [[Hoover Dam]]. It may suit thermal-effects research on humpback chub, but it is unsuitable as a refugium unless a reliable supply of uncontaminated warm water is assured.
- **Non-native fish:** The reviewer supports a halt to non-native warmwater fish stocking but asks that the plan specify where restrictions would apply throughout the Colorado River basin.

## Key Findings

**Reference and citation corrections**
- Tyus and Karp (1989) was listed as "in preparation" and is not citable; the reviewer asks that it be updated.
- Tyus and Minckley (1988), Tyus and Karp (1989), and related citations should reflect published versions.
- Huth (1988) should not be cited unless it is a finalized doctoral dissertation.
- Valdes and Williams (1999) should not be cited unless finalized.
- Some fish-source references were mis-attributed, and a sentence on gastropods and caddisflies was miscited (gastropods, not "caddisflies," in the reviewer's correction).
- Funding for facilities used for bonytail, including the Arizona State University research farm, should be clarified in the text.

**Broodstock and genetics**
- The reviewer "strongly disagrees" with the proposal to acquire wild bonytail broodstock before genetic studies are complete.
- Wild fish are rare, and those remaining may be very old; each individual should be secured wherever encountered because it can contribute to genetic studies and may have a longer lifespan in a captive hatchery than in the wild.

**Refugia and hatchery suitability**
- Buenos Aires NWR and Fish Springs hatchery should not be proposed as bonytail refugia until managers and experienced personnel are interviewed about their suitability.

**Recovery priorities and population classifications**
- The sentence making Lake Mohave a secondary recovery area should be revised.
- The reviewer calls it "biological (and political) nonsense" to suggest a new bonytail population could be reclassified as nonessential, since that decision should wait until planned recovery is achieved.

**Budget and schedule**
- The reviewer questions the justification for collecting food and feeding-habit data on bonytail and asks how it would inform management.
- Dollar allocations in the implementation schedule are unclear. For example, one entry appears to give the state of Utah a large sum for wild-fish acquisition while other entities receive nothing. The reviewer says the table requires clarification.
- Costs and responsibilities shared with other recovery plans (humpback chub and Colorado squawfish) are not separated, making it impossible to see which tasks belong to each species. The reviewer requests either a statement that costs are independent of other species' schedules or a detailed analysis of shared tasks.
- Task 51 allows two years for establishing and maintaining humpback chub refugia; this may be realistic for the former but is inadequate for the latter.

**Overall assessment**
- The plans are generally comprehensive, but the reviewer requests revisions in the areas above before finalization. The letter closes by offering further assistance.
