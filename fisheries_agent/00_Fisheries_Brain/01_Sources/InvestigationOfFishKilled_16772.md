# Arizona Game & Fish Investigation of a 1969 Mine-Effluent Fish Kill on the San Francisco River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

In late June 1969, a field investigation by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] documented a major fish kill on the [[San Francisco River]] below [[Chase Creek]] at Clifton, Arizona. The kill followed a release of red-orange leaching water from [[Phelps Dodge Corporation]] operations at the [[Morenci Mine]]. Survey estimates put fish mortality at 58%, aquatic insect mortality at 98%, and other aquatic organism mortality at 100%. A follow-up visit on July 9, 1969, found the river largely recovering, with the dead fish gone and the surviving minnows back in the main channel. The investigators concluded that repopulation would depend on upstream migration and drift.

## Background & Description

The memo, written by the Water Quality Analyst Ned L. Rathbun and Project Ecologist Howard McDonald, is a field report and correspondence file. It covers:

- Initial notification on June 30, 1969, and the survey of the river four miles below Chase Creek.
- Reconstruction of the spill source at the mine's leaching ponds.
- Water sampling, with analysis performed at Arizona State University's Hydrology Building on July 3, 1969.
- A re-survey on July 9, 1969.

Water analysis results are referenced as attachments. The two-page "Water Quality Inventory" forms (chemical and physical determinations) are blank in the source copy, so no chemical values are reported here.

Species affected included:

- **[[Channel Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus punctatus*): counted in three length classes (1–3 in, 6–8 in, and 18–20 in).
- **[[Common Carp]]** (*Cyprinus carpio*): counted in two length classes (6–8 in and 12–14 in).
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): the most numerous fish, with 3,200 counted in 700 ft.
- **[[Flathead Catfish]]**: large individuals found dead in shallow water below pools in the lower three miles.
- **[[Leopard Frog]]** adults and tadpoles, with only three live frogs seen.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spill sequence.** A leak began about midnight on Saturday, June 28, 1969, and ran for about 24 hours. Mud from a [[Mud Slide]] below the [[Leaching Ponds]] plugged the well pond and burned out three pumps. Water then overflowed through a pipe into three check ponds. The walls of the lower check pond failed, releasing leaching water into Chase Creek and then the San Francisco River. The mine's branch manager attributed the release to the mud slide, not to pond-wall failure.

**Volume.** The leaching ponds were reported as about 100 × 100 × 5 ft and usually held 2–4 ft of water. Assuming 3 ft of water in each pond, the minimum release was 60,000 cubic feet, or at least 448,800 gallons.

**Water chemistry.** The residue was identified as probably iron oxide. It was about 24 inches deep and 4 feet wide along the west bank below Chase Creek. No residue was seen above the confluence. Field readings of dissolved oxygen, pH, CO₂, and temperature were taken at 7:00 p.m. on June 30, but the values are not included in the source text.

**Invertebrates.** Dead aquatic insects were widespread, including beetle larvae, mayfly larvae, bloodworms, stonefly larvae, and dragonfly larvae. Bottom samples taken with a [[Surber Sampler]] produced no living organisms. The only live insects were three giant water bugs (Belostomatidae) and numerous water boatmen ([[Corixidae]]).

**Downstream context.** Investigators noted that the Gila River at San Jose showed no sign of the leaching water as of July 1, 1969.

## Key Findings

**Kill estimates (June 30, 1969 survey)**

- Fish mortality: 58%
- Insect mortality: 98%
- Other aquatic organism mortality: 100%
- Visual count in 700 ft of stream: 125 dead minnows in one isolated pool, with the investigators estimating about twice as many live minnows there.
- Survey extent: 1,100 ft of river, with fish counts extrapolated over four miles from Chase Creek to the lower end of the picnic area below Plansite Hill.

**Extrapolated fish counts (per mile, as reported)**

| Species | Size class | Counted in 700 ft | Per mile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channel Catfish | 1–3 in | 28 | 211 |
| Channel Catfish | 6–8 in | 80 | 692 |
| Channel Catfish | 18–20 in | 4 | 32 |
| Common Carp | 6–8 in | 26 | 195 (780 as reported) |
| Common Carp | 12–14 in | 2 | 16 (64 as reported) |
| Longfin Dace | 1–3 in | 3,200 | 24,135 (96,540 as reported) |

The source gives per-mile values inconsistently, and some entries are illegible. Length–weight conversions for biomass came from Carlander (1950) and a federal hatchery manual. Carp and catfish weights used were 0.19 lb (6–8 in carp), 0.80 lb (12–14 in carp), 0.11 lb (6–8 in catfish), and 2.26 lb (18–20 in catfish). The 1–3 in catfish weight was 304 per lb.

**Cause**

- Mud from a slide plugged the well-pond pumps, causing bearings to burn out and motors to overload.
- A leak released leaching water into Chase Creek for about 24 hours starting June 28, 1969.
- Mine management stated that the cause was the mud slide and that a [[Settling Pond]] with baffling would be built to prevent mud from reaching the well pond.

**Recovery (July 9, 1969 re-survey)**

- The river bottom was free of residue, and the moss was green again.
- Longfin dace had returned from isolated pools to the main river; two schools of about 20–24 fish each were seen.
- Two one-foot bottom samples yielded only one midge larva.
- Dead fish carcasses had disappeared.
- Flathead catfish were found dead in shallow water below deep pools in the lower three miles, and the fish had decomposed badly because of the heat.

**Investigator conclusions**

- The stream was judged to be back to its pre-spill condition, apart from the lack of aquatic life.
- Minnows were expected to repopulate rapidly.
- Other fish would depend on upstream populations and upstream migration.
- Aquatic insects were expected to recolonize through [[Downstream Drift]], with fair numbers by late 1970 or possibly summer 1970.
- If the upper San Francisco and Blue River catfish populations match the lower-river population killed, re-establishing catchable-size catfish would take "between 3 and [illegible] years."
