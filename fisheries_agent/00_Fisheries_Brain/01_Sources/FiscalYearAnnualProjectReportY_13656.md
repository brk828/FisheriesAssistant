# Survival of Age-0 Colorado Squawfish in the Green and Colorado Rivers: FY 93 Interim Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 93 annual report and attached interim report, prepared for the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program]] and the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]], evaluate overwinter survival of age-0 [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), an endangered species. The work was conducted under Project 18-5, "5-Year Flaming Gorge Recommendations: Overwinter Survival on Green River," with principal investigators Richard Valdez, Brian Cowdell (BIO/WEST, Inc.) and Leo Lentsch (UDWR). The study runs from 1990 to 1996. Field sampling was completed in 1993 and data were analyzed. FY 92 funding was $25,000, fully expended.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado Squawfish]] is a large cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River system. Failed recruitment of age-0 fish into juvenile and adult size classes is cited as a major reason for the species' poor status in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The initial overwintering period is identified as a critical stage in early life history.

The study's goals were to:
- Relate river discharge and other environmental variables to reproductive success and early-life survival of endangered fishes.
- Continue spring sampling of age-0 squawfish on the Green River and assess whether it should join the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP).
- Identify relationships between relative abundance of key species, discharge regime, and apparent overwinter survival.

Sampling followed the ISMP protocol: two backwaters sampled every 5 miles per reach, using 1/8-inch mesh beach seines, with two seine hauls per backwater. Fall sampling (late September) targeted fish aggregated in warmer backwaters; spring sampling (late March to early April) occurred before spring runoff inundated backwaters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study reaches:**
- Reach 4, upper Green River (RM 320 to 215), sampled April 5-7, 1993.
- Reach 3, lower Green River (RM 120 to 0), the focus of this report, sampled March 29 to April 1, 1993.
- Reach 1, lower Colorado River (RM 50 to 0), analyzed separately by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Grand Junction CREP Field Station.

**Flow and temperature:** Spring 1993 flows in Reach 3 (4100-6900 cfs) were higher than in the previous spring (2900-3400 cfs in 1992). Backwater temperatures in spring 1993 ranged from 9-20°C in Reach 3 and 7-16°C in Reach 4.

**Length criteria:** Maximum total lengths for age-0 fish were 60 mm (fall) and 70 mm (spring) for most year classes, and 70 and 75 mm for the 1989 year class. The 1992 year class maxima were 60 and 65 mm.

**Sympatric species:** In Reach 3 backwaters in spring 1993 (N = 21 first seine hauls), the most abundant fish were [[Red Shiner]] (4,876 collected; mean CPE 69.30/10 m²), [[Sand Shiner]] (1,048; 13.70/10 m²), and [[Fathead Minnow]] (253; 1.88/10 m²). Age-0 Colorado squawfish ranked fourth in mean CPE. One unidentified fish (82 mm TL) at RM 70.5 had characters suggesting [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) but was not positively identified.

**Density metric:** Geometric mean CPE was used, with log transformation (ln(CPE+1)) because catch data were not normally distributed (Sokal and Rohlf 1987). This approach weights high CPE values less heavily.

## Key Findings

**Reach 3, lower Green River (RM 120-0):**
- Fall 1992: 119 age-0 squawfish, CPE 0.77/100 m² (geometric mean), mean TL 37 mm (range 16-59 mm).
- Spring 1993: 88 age-0 squawfish, a 26% decline in numbers, with CPE 1.03/100 m² (geometric mean), mean TL 45 mm (range 30-65 mm).
- The spring CPE was not significantly different from other 1992 spring sampling (Student's t, P>0.05).
- Mean TL increased by 8 mm from fall to spring. No significant downstream transport was detected.
- Juveniles: 11 captured in fall 1992 (61-93 mm TL) and 17 in spring 1993 (67-90 mm TL).

**Reach 4, upper Green River (RM 320-200):**
- Fall 1992: 180 age-0 squawfish, CPE 1.29/100 m².
- Spring 1993: 71 fish, CPE 0.39/100 m² (a 70% drop in CPE, 61% drop in numbers), mean TL 45 mm.

**Reach 1, lower Colorado River (RM 50-0):**
- Fall 1992: 103 age-0 squawfish, CPE 0.84/100 m², mean TL 34 mm (range 21-59 mm).
- Spring 1993: 18 fish (an 83% decline in numbers), CPE 0.92/100 m², mean TL 40 mm (range 29-60 mm).
- Juveniles: 5 in fall 1992 (65-68 mm TL) and 10 in spring 1993 (67-79 mm TL).

**Historical context:** Reach 3 data for the 1987-1992 year classes show high fall densities in some years (e.g., 1988 fall CPE 48.40/100 m²; 1989 fall CPE 19.91/100 m²), with recent years far lower (1991 fall CPE 2.23; 1992 fall CPE 0.77). Reach 1 CPE values for the same period ranged from 0.42 to 4.92/100 m².

**Conclusions and recommendations:**
- The overwinter survival work provides data on the relationship between winter flow patterns and survival of juvenile squawfish.
- Recommended: incorporate age-0 overwinter survival investigations into the ISMP.
- Recommended: correlate all early-life-history sampling with measures of habitat availability, such as aerial video imagery.
- Interim results (Appendix) are preliminary. Full analysis is scheduled for the Annual Report in January 1994.
