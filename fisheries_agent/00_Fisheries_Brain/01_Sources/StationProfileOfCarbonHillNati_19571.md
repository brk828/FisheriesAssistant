## Executive Summary

The **Carbon Hill National Fish Hatchery** (established 1938, operational 1939) is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service freshwater production facility located in Carbon Hill, Alabama. Originally constructed under P.L. 52-1127 to rear [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and [[Crappie]] for stocking private farm ponds across 24 northwest Alabama and 3 east-central Mississippi counties, the station's mission has evolved substantially over its operational history. Following the closure of Corning, Cohutta, and Frankfort National Fish Hatcheries, Carbon Hill absorbed Tennessee distribution responsibilities (68 counties) until the state assumed its own farm pond program in 1987. The hatchery's water supply is uniquely sourced from **two wells tapping flooded underground coal mines**, a notable geohydrological feature for a fish culture facility. Current production emphasizes [[Walleye]] for reservoir mitigation, [[Striped Bass]] for anadromous stock restoration in Gulf of Mexico river systems, and [[Channel Catfish]] for federal and state agency distribution.

## Background & Description

*Note: The source document is an administrative station profile and does not provide morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data for the species reared. The species cultured at this facility belong to four distinct families spanning divergent body plans:*

- **[[Percidae]]** ([[Walleye]]) — elongate percid body form; reared specifically for reservoir stocking.
- **[[Ictaluridae]]** ([[Channel Catfish]]) — scaleless, barbeled catfish morphology.
- **[[Moronidae]]** ([[Striped Bass]]) — anadromous temperate bass; cultured through "Phase I" and "Phase II" life stages prior to release, indicating staged rearing to specific size/age classes before distribution.
- **[[Centrarchidae]]** ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Crappie]]) — pond-adapted sunfishes historically central to the station's original farm-pond stocking mandate.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water Source:** Two wells drawing from flooded, abandoned underground coal mines — an unusual anthropogenic groundwater source repurposed for aquaculture, supplying 12–14 ponds totaling 17 surface acres at full utilization.
- **Production-to-Release Pathway:**
  - [[Walleye]] are reared specifically for **mitigation stocking** of the Tennessee Valley Authority's **Cherokee Reservoir** in northeastern Tennessee, compensating for habitat/ecological impacts of impoundment.
  - [[Striped Bass]] (Phase I and Phase II) are distributed to **major river systems of the Gulf of Mexico** to restore depleted anadromous stocks, linking this inland hatchery to broader coastal/estuarine fishery restoration efforts.
  - [[Channel Catfish]] are distributed to the U.S. Forest Service, the Veterans Administration, and the State of Tennessee, suggesting stocking of public recreational and institutional waters.
  - [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Redear Sunfish]] now serve a supportive/backup role for the farm pond programs of [[Meridian National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Pvt. John Allen National Fish Hatchery]], rather than direct primary distribution.
- **Geographic Range of Influence:** Historically spanned Alabama, Mississippi, and Tennessee; contracted after 1987 when Tennessee assumed its own farm pond stocking program.
- **Habitat Engineering:** A 1964 rehabilitation project modified pond structure (dividing two ponds, rebuilding seven "kettles") and added infrastructure (seven hatchery buildings, a residence), reflecting adaptive management of the captive-rearing habitat over time.

## Key Findings

- **Mission Shift:** The hatchery transitioned from a farm-pond-focused sunfish/bass supplier (pre-1965) to a multi-species facility supporting reservoir mitigation ([[Walleye]]) and interjurisdictional anadromous stock restoration ([[Striped Bass]]) by the early 1990s.
- **Distribution Volume Growth:** Total annual distribution rose sharply across the reporting period:
  - FY1990: 475,603 total fish (114,092 to federal waters; 205,699 to other waters; 155,840 to state waters)
  - FY1991: 747,390 total fish (193,000 federal; 551,690 other; 2,700 state)
  - FY1992: 941,047 total fish (597,290 federal; 253,731 other; 90,026 state)
  - This represents nearly a **100% increase** in total distribution from 1990 to 1992, driven largely by growth in federal-waters allocations.
- **Budgetary Trends (1991–1994):** Operations funding increased from $160.0K (1991 actual) to an estimated $218.4K (1994), while maintenance funding dropped to $0.0 in the 1994 estimate despite a spike to $44.0K in 1992. Cost-recoverable funds remained stable (~$19–21K), and staffing (FTEs) was projected to increase from 3.6–4.0 to 5.0 by 1994.
- **Institutional Interdependency:** Carbon Hill NFH functions within a network of federal hatcheries (backing up [[Meridian National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Pvt. John Allen National Fish Hatchery]]) and inherited distribution duties from three closed hatcheries (Corning, Cohutta, Frankfort), illustrating the consolidation of federal fish culture infrastructure in the southeastern U.S. during this period.
- **Community Engagement:** An 8-volunteer crew improved public use facilities under the Challenge Grant Program, indicating incorporation of citizen-engagement/volunteer initiatives into hatchery operations.
- **Unique Water Source:** The reliance on flooded coal mine groundwater is an atypical hatchery water supply that merits consideration for water chemistry (potential metal/mineral loading) in any future ecological or fish health assessment of the station.
