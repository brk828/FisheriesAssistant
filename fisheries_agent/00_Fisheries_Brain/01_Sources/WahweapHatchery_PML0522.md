# Wahweap Hatchery Update: Native Colorado River Fish Propagation, 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1998 email from a [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] field contact reports on the [[Wahweap Hatchery]] near [[Lake Powell]]. The hatchery propagates endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] for recovery stocking in the Colorado River system. The update covers facility improvements, creek stabilization, stock sources, growth and tagging status, and stocking and staffing plans. Much of the text is degraded by scanning errors, so several figures are incomplete and are reported only where legible.

## Background & Description

The hatchery holds native fish from several sources:

- **Razorback Sucker:** Brood stock from the [[Valley City National Fish Hatchery]] source, fish from the 1995 year class, and a lot received in 1998 from a [[Green River]] spawn.
- **Bonytail:** Lots from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and an older lot from the Colorado River, including fish from the 1995 year class.

A video on the native fish hatchery and its plight aired on public television. The hatchery also reports a new utility building, which included a wet lab for water chemistry analysis, and a [[Stream Bank Revegetation]] effort along [[Wahweap Creek]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flash flood protection:** Stabilization of Wahweap Creek, including [[Gabion Baskets]] placed to add stability, continues to protect the hatchery from flash flood events. Stream bank revegetation with cottonwood was planned.
- **Predator and small-fish protection:** Netting was installed over three ponds to protect small fish. It was to be extended to a fourth pond that fall.
- **Stocking sites:** Year-class fish were stocked at [[Dewey Bridge]] on the [[Colorado River]].
- **Water chemistry:** The wet lab allows on-site water chemistry analysis, saving about 2 hours of travel per day.

## Key Findings

**Facilities and operations**
- The utility building was completed and its wet lab is in use for water chemistry analysis, saving about 2 hours per day in travel time.
- The building is also used for daily feeding and [[PIT Tagging]] during inclement weather.
- Netting protects small fish in 3 ponds, with a fourth pond to be covered.

**Razorback Sucker**
- A lot received in 1998 from a Green River spawn (lot 623X268) was placed in ponds for maximum growth. Growth was reported as good, with an average length of 88.4 mm on Aug 5. These fish were expected to reach the target length of 300 mm.
- Fish from the 1995 year class were scheduled for transfer to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in October 1998. Some males expressed milt when stroked.
- Brood stock from the Valley City source were PIT tagged, with an average total length of 410 mm and weight of 910 g. Some developed tubercles.
- Limited natural reproduction was noted in lined ponds from this lot in spring 1998.

**Bonytail**
- Fish from the Dexter source were received in 1998, held in 3 ponds, and averaged 72 mm. PIT tagging was planned for September, with 15,000 tags on hand.
- The author reports that fish tagged at about 72 mm showed no more mortality or tag loss than fish over 100 mm tagged with needles.
- A mid-July sample averaged 153 mm and 34 g. These fish were held in 4 ponds and must be tagged before stocking that fall.
- An older lot averaged 399 mm and 350 g. About 216 fish remained from this lot, which was kept as brood stock.

**Stocking completed**
- 1995 year-class radio-tagged Bonytail were stocked at Dewey Bridge in March 1998.
- 1995 year-class PIT-tagged Bonytail were stocked at Dewey Bridge in April 1998.

**Plans**
- A full-time hatchery technician was to be hired in September.
- Operating funds were to come equally from the Sport Fish and Native Fish programs.
- Other planned work included radio tagging and stocking of smaller Bonytail that fall and transfer of Razorback Sucker to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

**Data limitations**
- Several lot counts and stocking numbers, including a line about roughly 7,000 fish and a 40,000-fish reference, are illegible in the scan and are not reported here.
- One sucker lot is named only as "ACK SUCKER" in the scan and could not be identified reliably.
