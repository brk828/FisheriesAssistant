## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service **Station Profile** for the **Bozeman Fish Technology Center (FTC)**, located in Bozeman, Montana. Established in 1892 under the Congressional Appropriation Act (27 Stat. 361), the Center operates as a research, culture, and technical-support facility rather than a production hatchery. Its core mission centers on applied fish science supporting **[[Threatened And Endangered Species]]** and species of special concern, with a particular institutional focus on **[[Fluvial Arctic Grayling]]** broodstock management and **[[Rainbow Trout]]** culture research. The Center integrates disciplines including histopathology, nutrition, reproductive physiology, and fish health to improve hatchery efficiency and conservation outcomes across USFWS Region 6.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: The Center operates coldwater and coolwater fish culture systems, including a dedicated **Coolwater Building**, **Reuse Building**, and **Water Treatment Facility**, indicating management of species with differing thermal tolerances.
- **Native range emphasis**: The **[[Fluvial Arctic Grayling]]** program is explicitly tied to native Montana stream systems, supporting the **Montana Fluvial Arctic Grayling Workgroup** by supplying fry and fingerlings for reintroduction/supplementation in native streams — indicating a conservation-focused ecological role for a fluvial (river-dwelling) salmonid population considered a species of special concern.
- **Water resource management**: Ongoing infrastructure work (replacement of deteriorated water lines, development of new warm-water sources, installation of a water reuse system) reflects active management of water quality/quantity to support multiple thermal habitat regimes (cold and warm water) within the same facility.
- **Distribution footprint (FY1994)**: Fish distribution data show **zero fish distributed** in FY 1992–1994 to federal, state, or private waters (excluding eggs and fry), suggesting the Center's outputs during this period were primarily in the form of eggs/fry (tracked separately) rather than stocked fingerlings/adults, consistent with its research/broodstock mission rather than a production stocking role.

## Key Findings

- **Institutional Mission**: The Bozeman FTC's six-part mission spans diet testing, feed development, histopathology, reproductive physiology, broodstock management, and fish culture enhancement for **[[Threatened And Endangered Species]]**, plus support for hatchery effluent/pollution reduction and fisheries management technique evaluation (e.g., electroshocking, strain comparisons for growth/survival).
- **Eight Core Activity Areas**: (1) development investigations, (2) fish husbandry, (3) fish health, (4) feed quality control, (5) nutrition, (6) broodstock management, (7) water quality management, and (8) teaching in fish husbandry/health — positioning the Center as a multidisciplinary technical hub rather than solely a production hatchery.
- **Regional Support Role**: The Center provides histopathology diagnostic services not only for its own programs but for the broader **Fishery Resource Program in Region 6** and external federal/state/private partners.
