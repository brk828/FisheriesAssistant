# CPSU/UA Field Projects and Activities Update - April 1994

#desert-ecology #herpetology #conservation-biology #monitoring #national-parks

## Executive Summary

This newsletter from the National Biological Survey Cooperative Park Studies Unit at The University of Arizona (CPSU/UA) documents field research activities, monitoring projects, and administrative developments during early 1994. Staff conducted diverse ecological studies across Arizona and California, including cactus monitoring at [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]], desert tortoise research near Kingman, amphibian and reptile surveys, and Channel Islands restoration projects. The unit also coordinated The Fourth California Islands Symposium and contributed to NBS Status and Trends reports on introduced species impacts.

## Background & Description

The CPSU/UA serves as a research and technical support unit for the National Park Service and other land management agencies in warm desert regions. Staff includes research ecologists, biological technicians, cartographers, and administrative personnel who conduct field studies, develop monitoring protocols, and provide GIS and GPS technical support. The unit publishes Bajada newsletter three times annually to facilitate communication between researchers and park managers.

Key personnel mentioned include [[Bill Halvorson]] (unit leader), [[Cecil Schwalbe]] (research ecologist specializing in herpetology), [[Peter Bennett]] (research ecologist), [[Mike Kunzmann]] (ecologist/GIS specialist), and [[Kathy Hiett]] (biological technician).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Research activities spanned multiple desert ecosystems and habitat types:

- **Desert uplands**: Acuña cactus monitoring at [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]] in 6 plots of 1,000 m² each
- **Sand dune systems**: [[Mohawk Dunes]] surveys for [[Colorado Desert Fringe-Toed Lizard]] and sand-adapted reptiles
- **Riparian zones**: Monitoring protocols developed for amphibians, birds, mammals, and reptiles at [[Tonto National Monument]]
- **Island ecosystems**: Restoration and monitoring on [[San Nicolas Island]] and [[Santa Cruz Island]]
- **Desert tortoise habitat**: Hibernation studies using fiber-optic probes near Kingman, Arizona

The unit addressed conservation concerns including invasive species (bullfrogs, nonnative plants), disappearing native species ([[Tarahumara Frog]]), and threatened taxa ([[Pima Pineapple Cactus]]).

## Key Findings

**Acuña Cactus Monitoring**: At [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]], monitoring was completed in record time with 23 plants discovered that had not been previously documented in the plots, including 13 seedlings.

**Desert Tortoise Research**: [[Ann McLuckie]] (UA graduate student) conducted ecology, genetics, and morphometric studies of desert tortoises near Kingman using fiber-optic probes to observe hibernating animals. The study was cooperatively funded by BLM, Arizona Game and Fish Heritage Program, Transwestern Pipeline, CPSU, and UA.

**Reptile and Small Mammal Trapping**: At [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], graduate student [[Dixie Bounds]] doubled the number of lizards caught, marked, and released during the first 2 weeks of spring trapping, indicating seasonal activity increases.

**Pima Pineapple Cactus Conservation**: Staff salvaged 77 [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] individuals from a land-development project 15 miles south of Tucson. Plants were transplanted to UA/Arid Lands Resource Sciences greenhouses for growth and fecundity studies and future recovery efforts.

**Channel Islands Symposium**: The Fourth California Islands Symposium in Santa Barbara attracted more than 300 attendees. CPSU/UA staff contributed to symposium organization and proceedings publication.

**NBS Status and Trends Contributions**: CPSU/UA staff coauthored 3 papers covering: (1) predation by introduced [[American Bullfrog]] in southwest US wetlands, (2) disappearance of [[Leopard Frog]] (Tarahumara frog) in the United States, and (3) [[Africanized Honey Bees]].

**Saguaro-Vulture Interaction**: A note by [[Peter Bennett]] and [[Mike Kunzmann]] titled "Suppression of Saguaro Cactus Flower Bud Formation by Roosting Vultures in Arizona" was accepted for publication in Southwestern Naturalist.

**GPS/GIS Development**: [[Mike Kunzmann]] established a GPS base station at CPSU/Advanced Resources Technology (ART) with files to be made available for the Tucson area via modem. He conducted seminars and provided technical support for GIS implementation at multiple park units including [[Point Mugu Naval Base]], [[Saguaro National Monument]], [[Petrified Forest National Park]], and [[Chiricahua National Monument]].

**Bat Monitoring**: Approximately 30 [[California Leaf-Nosed Bat]]s were counted in the Copper Mountain mine shaft at [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]].
