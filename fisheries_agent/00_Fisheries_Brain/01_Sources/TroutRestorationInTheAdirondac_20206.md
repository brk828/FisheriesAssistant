# Adirondack Trout Restoration Project Progress Report (1951-1952)

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #Salmonidae #brook-trout #reclamation

## Executive Summary

This progress report documents the second year (1951) of the [[Adirondack Trout Restoration Project]], an experimental fisheries management program aimed at reclaiming former trout waters now inhabited by less desirable species. The reclamation procedure involves chemical eradication using [[Rotenone]] followed by restocking with [[Brook Trout]] (Salvelinus fontinalis). During 1951, eleven ponds were reclaimed across five Adirondack counties, nearly tripling the acreage of restored trout water compared to 1950. The report evaluates stocking strategies, detoxification timelines, fishing success, and measures to prevent reintroduction of undesirable species.

## Background & Description

The [[Adirondack Trout Restoration Project]] began in 1950 as an experimental fisheries reclamation program targeting ponds that formerly supported trout populations but had been colonized by less desirable species including [[Perch]], [[Pike]], [[Bass]], minnows, and suckers. The restoration methodology consists of:

1. Chemical treatment with rotenone to eradicate existing fish populations
2. Monitoring for detoxification using live fish in cages
3. Restocking with [[Brook Trout]] using various strategies
4. Installation of barrier dams on outlets to prevent reintroduction

By 1951, the program had expanded to include ponds in five counties: [[Essex County]], [[Franklin County]], [[Fulton County]], [[Hamilton County]], and [[St. Lawrence County]]. The 1951 season treated eleven ponds compared to seven in 1950, with significantly larger pond sizes resulting in nearly triple the acreage reclaimed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Detoxification periods following rotenone treatment varied considerably, ranging from 39 days to 302 days depending on multiple environmental factors including normal outflow, precipitation, water temperature at treatment, and other physical pond conditions. Live fish cage tests provided the most reliable method for determining when water was no longer toxic.

[[Mountain Pond]] in southern [[Franklin County]] exemplified successful reclamation outcomes. This 50-acre pond was treated on August 30, 1950, and stocked with six-inch yearling [[Brook Trout]] on April 26, 1951 at a rate of 200 fish per acre. Post-season test netting revealed trout ranging up to 9.5 inches despite heavy fishing pressure.

The primary threat to reclamation success was reintroduction of undesirable species through tributaries or human activity (minnow buckets). To address this, barrier dams were constructed on outlets of eight of eighteen reclaimed ponds. Legislative measures enacted January 1, 1952 prohibited use of minnows or fish (dead or alive) as bait in reclaimed ponds and remaining natural brook trout ponds in [[St. Lawrence County]], [[Franklin County]], [[Clinton County]], and [[Essex County]], with expansion to [[Hamilton County]], [[Warren County]], and the Town of Webb in [[Herkimer County]] effective January 1, 1953.

## Key Findings

**Reclamation Scale and Efficiency:**
- 1951: 11 ponds reclaimed (vs. 7 in 1950)
- Acreage reclaimed nearly tripled compared to previous year due to larger pond sizes
- Total program coverage: 18 ponds across five Adirondack counties by end of 1951

**Detoxification and Stocking:**
- Detoxification period range: 39 to 302 days post-treatment
- Five of seven ponds reclaimed in 1950 provided fishing during 1951 season
- Stocking strategies tested: four-inch fingerlings (fall stocking) and six-inch yearlings (spring stocking)

**Fishing Success - Mountain Pond Case Study:**
- Stocking rate: 200 yearling [[Brook Trout]] per acre (six inches average length)
- Stocking date: April 26, 1951
- Legal-size trout caught by late May 1951
- Post-season: trout up to 9.5 inches present despite heavy fishing pressure
- Second year (April 19, 1952): 97 anglers caught 220 [[Brook Trout]] averaging 8.5 inches in single day

**Species Eradication Success:**
- No survival of non-native species ([[Perch]], [[Pike]], [[Bass]]) detected to date
- One pond experienced recolonization by minnows and suckers from tributaries
- Complete eradication effectiveness remains under evaluation

**Reintroduction Prevention Measures:**
- Barrier dams constructed on 8 of 18 reclaimed pond outlets
- 10 ponds relied on natural barriers
- Legislative bait restrictions implemented (effective January 1, 1952, expanded January 1, 1953)
- Signage program initiated on reclaimed ponds

**Outstanding Research Questions:**
1. Can complete fish population extermination be achieved with current methods?
2. If incomplete eradication occurs, can ponds be managed for trout long enough to justify reclamation costs before undesirable species re-dominate?
3. Is reclamation practical in ponds with marginal food and chemical conditions for trout?

**Future Program Direction:**
- Additional pond reclamations planned
- Emphasis shifting to answering outstanding research questions
- Focus on monitoring completeness of undesirable fish destruction
- Evaluation of [[Brook Trout]] survival and growth rates
- Assessment of pond reclamation as practical fish management method
