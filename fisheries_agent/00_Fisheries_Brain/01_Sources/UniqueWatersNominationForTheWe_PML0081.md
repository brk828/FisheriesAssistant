# West Fork of the Little Colorado River: Unique Waters Nomination

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

The Bureau of Water Quality Control, Arizona Department of Health Services, nominated the West Fork of the [[Little Colorado River]] upstream of Government Springs (south of Greer, Arizona) as a Unique Water of Exceptional [[Recreational Significance]] and [[Ecological Significance]]. The nomination was submitted to the [[Arizona Water Quality Control Council]] on April 13, 1983. The Bureau concluded that all three primary and five secondary designation criteria under Regulation R9-21-102.C are fulfilled. It recommends upgraded numerical water quality standards, no additional management practices beyond the antidegradation commitment, and reliance on the existing practices of the [[Apache National Forest]]. The Council adopted a resolution designating the stream on April 13, 1983.

## Background & Description

The nominated segment runs from the Government Springs Forest Camp (about 33°59'N, 109°27'W) to the stream's sources on the northeast slopes of [[Mt. Baldy]]. The entire reach lies within the Apache National Forest, and the upper watershed above Sheep Crossing lies within the [[Mt. Baldy Wilderness]]. The headwaters are rugged, the Sheep Crossing area has rolling mountain meadows, and the stream enters a narrow canyon before reaching Greer. Typical summer flows in the Sheep Crossing to Government Springs area are 7 to 10 cubic feet per second.

The nomination is prepared under the Council's Unique Waters Policy (AWQCC, 1981), which covers waters of exceptional recreational or ecological significance or those constituting an outstanding public resource. The report contains a description of the segment, criteria responses, a compliance summary, proposed standards revisions, implementation procedures, and agency comment letters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Vegetation and habitat.** Three vegetation types occur in the drainage: alpine spruce-fir forest on the Mt. Baldy slopes (of limited distribution in Arizona), mountain meadow, and montane conifer forest of ponderosa pine and Douglas fir. Recently reforested areas support monospecific stands of quaking aspen. Riparian vegetation includes alders and willows above Sheep Crossing, with maples, chokecherry and elderberry added near Greer. The shaded canyon supports a diverse assemblage of lower plants and fungi.

**Fauna and fish.** Deer, bear, turkey and elk are present. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] stocks [[Rainbow Trout]] at Sheep Crossing and Government Springs, and the headwaters support a self-sustaining fishery of wild rainbow-Arizona trout hybrids. Fish are introduced regularly in summer.

**Water quality baseline.** Monitoring by the [[Ambient Water Quality Unit]] at five sites (summer and fall 1981) and by [[USGS]] at Greer (monthly since 1976) showed most allowable limits for trace elements, bacteria and nutrients were not exceeded. Temperature, dissolved oxygen and pH were unaltered. Turbidity rarely exceeded 10 NTU, apparently due to spring flooding. Pesticides and organic compounds were not detected. Phenolics exceeded the standard continuously, which ADHS attributed to natural decay of litter from coniferous forest.

**Land management.** Grazing and recreation occur without degrading water quality. The river and adjacent lands between Sheep Crossing and Government Springs are managed as a "Water Influence Zone," which restricts activities such as logging. A timber sale is planned in the watershed; the Bureau found it compatible with the designation.

## Key Findings

- **Criterion A (water quality):** Water quality is consistently better than the standards. The Bureau found the West Fork "easily meets" the limits. Phenolic exceedances are considered natural and not violations.
- **Criterion B (compatibility with uses):** The [[Northern Arizona Council of Governments]] (NACOG) supports designation, and it is named in the NACOG 208 plan. The [[Apache National Forest]] "heartily endorses" the nomination. [[Apache County]] has no opposition, and the [[Arizona State Land Department]] concurs.
- **Criterion C (manageability):** The watershed can be managed to maintain high quality through existing ANF practices in consultation with the Bureau.
- **Secondary Criterion 3.A (public recreation):** Use is dispersed and accessible (campground, trails, parking, hunting, winter skiing). It has not conflicted with instream water quality. No national-average use comparison figures were available.
- **Secondary Criterion 3.B (unique attributes):** Pristine, virgin forest, unimpounded and undiverted stream, and frequent use in Arizona Highways photo essays.
- **Secondary Criterion 3.C (national land system):** The upper segment lies within the Mt. Baldy Wilderness and is served by a maintained trail.
- **Secondary Criterion 3.F (ecosystem function):** The West Fork is the largest tributary of the Little Colorado upstream of [[Lyman Lake]] and supplies downstream impoundments (Greer Lakes, Becker Lake, Lyman Lake). It contributes to groundwater recharge and drinking water.
- **Secondary Criterion 3.I (unique ecology):** The virgin conifer canyon forest supports fungi, mosses, ferns and wildflowers of limited Arizona distribution.

**Proposed standards revisions (Table 2):**
- Bacteria: single-sample maximum of 200 colony forming units/100 ml, simplified and made more stringent.
- Arsenic, chromium, lead, mercury, selenium, silver and zinc limits lowered to reflect ambient conditions.
- pH unchanged, except no human-caused alteration.
- No human-caused alteration of dissolved oxygen (not below 6.0 mg/l), temperature (no increase above natural ambient, with a 1.5 °C limit noted in the table), or TDS.
- Phenolics standard waived under R9-21-111.H because no anthropogenic source exists.
- Natural exceedances, such as floods, are not violations.

**Implementation:**
- Standards are to be incorporated into Articles 2 and 3 of state surface water regulations during the triennial review scheduled for 1983-84.
- No specific management practices are imposed. The Council is to state that discharges and land use practices that permanently lower water quality are forbidden, while temporary degradation from maintenance activities (e.g., selective timber harvest, road improvement) is allowed.
- A cooperative quarterly monitoring program is recommended at Sheep Crossing and Government Springs, covering nutrients and turbidity, at nominal cost.

**Review and comments:** The draft (January 31, 1983) received no adverse comments. Three agency letters were received (State Land Department, NACOG, Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests). The Forest Service endorsed the nomination but objected to the wording forbidding all lowering of water quality, noting that temporary degradation could result from projects such as upgrading State Highway 273 or timber sale roads.

**Council resolution:** On April 13, 1983, the Council designated the West Fork of the Little Colorado River as a Unique Water of Recreational and Ecological Significance and directed staff to prepare proposed numerical standards revisions.
