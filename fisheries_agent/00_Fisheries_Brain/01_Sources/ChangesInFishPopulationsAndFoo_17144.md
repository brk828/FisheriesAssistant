# Changes in Fish Populations and Food Habits of Yellow Bass in North Twin Lake, 1956-1958

#ichthyology #marine-biology #moronidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Jon E. Cottier]] (Iowa Cooperative Fishery Research Unit, Iowa State University) reports fish population trends and the food habits of [[Yellow Bass]] in [[North Twin Lake]], a shallow, partly dredged prairie lake in Calhoun County, Iowa, over 1953 to 1958. Populations were high until a severe [[Winter Kill]] in 1955, were depressed in 1956, rose in 1957 and fell again in 1958. Before 1956, adult yellow bass relied heavily on forage fish (young yellow bass and [[Gizzard Shad]]) plus insects. In the summers of 1956 to 1958, forage fish were of minor importance; the bass fed mainly on immature insects and minute crustaceans.

## Background & Description

North Twin Lake lies about five miles north of Rockwell City. It is roughly 2.5 miles long and 0.25 mile wide, with a full-pool surface area of 569 acres. In 1939-1940, about 135 acres at the southern end were dredged by the State Conservation Commission to improve boating and fishing (see [[Owen Erosion-Littoral Study]]). During the summers of 1956-1958, low water reduced the lake to about two-thirds of its normal size, with a maximum depth of about eight feet in the dredged area.

The [[Iowa Cooperative Fishery Research Unit]] began studying the lake in 1951, covering bottom fauna, fish populations and food habits. Blue-green algae were a major problem in 1956-1958 but not in 1954-1955, when gizzard shad were abundant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Winter kill (1955):** The State Conservation Commission estimated about 15,000 pounds of fish killed, including 24,000 yellow bass, 4,000 pounds of gizzard shad, 3,200 walleye, 875 crappie, 490 yellow perch, 375 largemouth bass and 220 white suckers, plus carp, buffalo and bullheads. Gizzard shad were nearly eliminated.
- **Population recovery:** In the winter of 1957 and spring of 1958 no evidence of low oxygen or winter kill was found. Carp was the only population that recovered to any extent after 1955.
- **Stocking:** About 6,200 young largemouth bass were released in September 1956, and 20,000 yearling walleye in June 1957.
- **Food web:** Yellow bass diets shifted from forage fish and insects (1953-1954) to insects and [[Cladocera]] and [[Copepoda]] (1956-1958), with algae-related organisms (Cyclops, Daphnia) prominent in the plankton.

## Key Findings

**Population trends (Rough Fish Removal Crew seining, 2-inch mesh, about 3,200 ft of net per year):**
- Catch per net foot: 2.53 (1954) and 2.32 (1955) before the winter kill; 0.34 (1956), 0.82 (1957) and 0.40 (1958) afterward.
- Total fish taken: 139,396 (1954), 154,536 (1955), 8,517 (1956), 13,990 (1957), 28,458 (1958).
- Yellow bass catches: 36,935 (1954), 12,042 (1955), 149 (1956), 307 (1957), 379 (1958).
- Gizzard shad fell from 77,050 (1955) to 2 (1956).

**Gill-net catches (per gill-net hour, 1953-1958):** Total catch per hour declined after the winter kill; yellow bass catch was 3.1 (1953), 4.1 (1954), 2.7 (1955), 6 (1956), 2.2 (1957) and 17 (1958), with 36 hours of effort in 1958. Gill-net data were collected from 1953 to 1955 by Kutkuhn and Owen.

**Reproduction:** Shoreline minnow seining (1956-1959) showed good reproduction of yellow bass. No small gizzard shad were taken in the summers of 1956 or 1957.

**Food habits, pre-kill (1953-1954):** Young-of-the-year yellow bass fed on microcrustacea and insects; yearlings began eating forage fish, and adults depended mainly on forage fish. Young yellow bass were the main forage in 1953, young gizzard shad in 1954.

**Food habits, post-kill (summers 1956 and 1957):**
- Stomachs of 120 yellow bass were examined: 72 fish in 1956 (8 empty) and 48 in 1957 (4 empty).
- Insects occurred in 54 of 72 stomachs (1956) and 36 of 48 (1957). Diptera, mainly Chironomidae and Chaoborus, were the dominant food for all sizes.
- Crustaceans, mainly Cladocera, occurred in 24 (1956) and 31 (1957) stomachs. Notonectidae (backswimmers) appeared in 19 stomachs in 1957, about 40 percent.
- Fish remains were found in only 5 stomachs in 1956 and none in 1957; fish eaten could not be identified.
- In 1958, 20 stomachs examined in the field contained Daphnia, Cyclops, Chaoborus and chironomids, but no fish.

**Conclusions:** The 1955 winter kill sharply reduced the fish community. Yellow bass shifted from fish-dominated to insect- and plankton-dominated diets once forage fish became scarce, while the population was still in a low state.
