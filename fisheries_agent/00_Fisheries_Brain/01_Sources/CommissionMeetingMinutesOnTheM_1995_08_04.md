## Executive Summary
This document details the minutes of an Arizona Game and Fish Commission meeting regarding the conservation status of two federally endangered fish species: the [[Gila Topminnow]] and the [[Desert Pupfish]]. The primary agenda item was the approval of a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Commission, the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) to facilitate the reintroduction and stocking of self-sustaining populations of both species on public lands. The motion was passed unanimously, contingent upon the reintroduced populations being classified as "nonessential experimental populations" under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) to mitigate conflicts with sportfishing and land management activities.

- **[[Desert Pupfish]]:** Implied small-bodied fish adapted to harsh, isolated desert water conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Preference:** Both species are characterized as inhabitants of small, specific sites rather than large, complex river systems. The text specifically notes they occupy "springheads and local waters."
- **Geographic Range:** Currently restricted in Arizona to less than a dozen natural sites for the Gila Topminnow, with approximately 22 reintroduced sites.
- **Interspecies Conflict:** A critical ecological threat identified is interaction with [[Nonnative Fishes]], specifically the [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). The proposed MOU explicitly prohibits the introduction of mosquitofish into areas stocked with these two native species due to competitive/predatory interactions.
- **Human Interaction:** Sites occupied by these species are generally distinct from "sportfish sites," minimizing direct competition management issues, though the Commission expressed concern regarding the potential for reintroduction sites to overlap with game fish population areas within the [[Gila River]] system.

## Key Findings
1. **Endangerment Status:** Both the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] are listed as federally endangered due to a combination of habitat loss, pollution, and biotic interactions with nonnative fish species.
2. **Recovery Metrics:**
   - The *Draft Revised Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan* requires **55 successful reintroductions** for downlisting.
   - The *Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan* similarly requires **55 re-established populations** for downlisting.
3. **Management Strategy:** The proposed MOU aims to establish **20-30 or more self-sustaining populations** of both species by the year 2004 on BLM-administered lands.
4. **Regulatory Mechanism:** To balance conservation with land-use and sportfishing interests, all reintroduced populations must be designated as **"Nonessential Experimental Populations"** under the ESA. This designation allows for more flexible management prescriptions specific to each site, alleviating strict regulatory burdens on surrounding land uses (e.g., ranching permittees).
5. **Interagency Cooperation:** The MOU facilitates the sharing of personnel, data, and equipment among the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], BLM, USFWS, and the U.S. Forest Service (USFS) to execute recovery plan actions.
6. **Commission Decision:** The motion to approve the MOU passed with a **unanimous vote**, provided the "nonessential experimental" safeguard was included to protect the Department's ability to manage conflicts with game fish populations on a case-by-case basis.
