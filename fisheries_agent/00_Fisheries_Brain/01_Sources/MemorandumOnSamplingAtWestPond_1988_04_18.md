# Memorandum on Sampling at West Pond

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1988 memorandum from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) reports on a field survey conducted 14–15 April 1988 at West Pond, Imperial County, California, aimed at determining the status of stocked [[Razorback Sucker]] populations. The pond had been stocked in 1986 by the California Department of Fish and Game (CADFG) with hatchery-reared and wild adult razorback suckers, and earlier (1983) with juveniles inside a Bureau of Reclamation (BR)-constructed enclosure. Despite targeted sampling effort using electroshocking and trammel netting, no razorback suckers were detected in the 1988 survey. The authors conclude that this does not constitute definitive evidence of extirpation, given the structural complexity of the habitat and limited sampling effort.

## Background & Description
Data on stocked razorback sucker cohorts provide size-class documentation relevant to survival and vulnerability assessments:

- **1986 hatchery-produced adults**: n = 17, average total length (TL) = 32.5 cm
- **1986 wild Lake Mohave adults**: n = 3, average TL = 56.6 cm (larger size class, sourced from [[Lake Mohave]] wild broodstock)
- **1983 hatchery-produced juveniles**: n = 457, average TL = 9.5 cm (stocked into BR enclosure)

Size disparities among stocked cohorts are ecologically significant: larger individuals (i.e., the 1986 wild adults at 56.6 cm) are hypothesized to be relatively immune to predation, whereas smaller juveniles (9.5 cm) stocked in 1983 are considered vulnerable to predatory fish mortality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
West Pond is a small (~8 ha) isolated aquatic habitat in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]], characterized by:

- **Habitat complexity**: Extensive marginal cattail growth, creating structurally complex, low-visibility zones that are "extremely difficult to sample."
- **Community composition (1988 total catch, n = 77 fish)**:
  - [[Threadfin Shad]]
  - [[Common Carp]] — most abundant species in electroshocker samples
  - [[Largemouth Bass]]
  - [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and hybrid sunfishes
  - [[Flathead Catfish]]
  - [[Blue Tilapia]] and hybrid tilapias — predominant in trammel net catch
- **Predator-prey dynamics**: The presence of predatory fishes (e.g., largemouth bass, flathead catfish) is implicated as a likely source of mortality for smaller stocked razorback sucker juveniles.
- **Anthropogenic habitat degradation**: The BR-constructed protective enclosure was found in disrepair and no longer functions as a predator exclusion structure. An accompanying "Native Fish Project" educational sign (placed by CADFG) was weathered and no longer effective for public outreach or vandalism deterrence.

## Key Findings
1. **No razorback suckers detected** in April 1988 sampling despite documented stocking events in 1980/1981–1983 and 1986.
2. **Sampling effort**: Boat-mounted electroshocker (0.82 hr real time) and trammel nets (1.67 net-units) were used; this is characterized by the authors as a "minimal effort."
3. **Non-detection ≠ extirpation**: Given habitat complexity (cattail margins) and limited gear diversity/effort, researchers state results do not conclusively demonstrate that [[Razorback Sucker]] has been extirpated from West Pond.
4. **Differential survivorship hypothesis**: Smaller (9.5 cm) juveniles from 1983 stocking are more likely to have suffered predation losses; larger (56.6 cm) 1986 adult stock should be comparatively predation-resistant.
5. **Infrastructure failure**: The protective enclosure has not provided effective security for stocked razorback suckers for "several years," compromising the original conservation stocking strategy.
6. **Management recommendations**:
   - Consider removal of the deteriorated enclosure debris and outdated signage.
   - Recommend additional, more diverse sampling gear/effort to improve detection probability of surviving razorbacks.
   - Authors offer to assist resource agencies in future monitoring efforts, though no specific return visit is currently planned.
7. **Historical stocking record inconsistency**: Note of "additional, undocumented stockings" possibly occurring 1981–1983, indicating incomplete historical stocking records for this site.
