# Experimental Breeding of the Amazon Molly

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #reproductive-biology #experimental-aquaculture

## Executive Summary

This experimental study documents 12 years (1932-1944) of breeding experiments with [[Mollienisia formosa]], the Amazon Molly, a viviparous fish species in which no males have ever been found. Approximately 8,000 individuals representing 20 generations were bred at the Museum of Zoology, [[University of Michigan]]. The research demonstrates that M. formosa reproduces exclusively matroclinously (offspring identical to mother) regardless of which male species is used for mating, with no paternal characteristics inherited. Fertility rates were approximately 10 times higher when mated with males of genus [[Mollienisia]] compared to males of other genera.

## Background & Description

[[Mollienisia formosa]] is a viviparous fish collected from northeastern Mexico and the Mexican border region in [[Texas]]. Among thousands of specimens collected, not a single male has been found, representing a remarkable reproductive anomaly. The species has a dorsal ray count of 11 (occasionally 10 or 12).

The experimental aquarium maintained breeding stocks of multiple [[Mollienisia]] species including M. velifera, M. petenensis, M. latipinna, and M. sphenops (14 different stocks of the latter), along with M. formosa. Live specimens were first received in 1932, initiating intensive breeding experiments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Amazon Molly inhabits freshwater environments in northeastern Mexico and southern Texas. The species exists in sympatry with other [[Mollienisia]] species, which serve as sperm donors for reproduction despite producing no hybrid offspring.

The experimental work was conducted in controlled aquarium conditions at the [[University of Michigan]] Museum of Zoology, with specimens representing natural populations from locations including [[Olmita, Texas]], [[Forlon, Mexico]], [[Acapulco, Mexico]], [[Lake Peten, Guatemala]], [[Yucatan, Mexico]], and [[Biloxi, Mississippi]].

## Key Findings

**Reproductive Pattern**: All 8,000 individuals bred over 20 generations were female and consistently resembled their mother, inheriting no characteristics from the father. This matroclinous reproduction occurred regardless of male species used.

**Mating Experiments with Mollienisia Males**: 
- 213 matings with 56 different types of male [[Mollienisia]] (including hybrids) produced approximately 7,500 young
- Average of 34.4 young per mating, 14.5 young per brood
- Males ranged from giant M. velifera (dorsal ray count >17) to smaller M. sphenops from [[Acapulco]] (dorsal ray count 8.5)
- [[Black Molly]] males produced no black coloration in offspring, despite black being dominant in normal M. latipinna crosses

**Cross-Genus Mating Experiments**:
- 89 matings with males from 16 other genera: [[Gambusia]], Quitana, [[Platypoecilus]], [[Xiphophorus]], Pseudoxiphophorus, Heterandria, Glaridichthys, Poecilistes, Micropoecilia, [[Allopoecilia]], [[Limia]] (three species), and [[Lebistes]]
- Only four genera produced offspring: Gambusia, Allopoecilia, Limia, and Lebistes (approximately 400 young total)
- Average of 4.55 young per mating, 12.66 young per brood

**Fertility Comparison (Single-Pair Matings)**:
- Non-Mollienisia males: 5,483 days produced 8 broods (98 individuals) = 0.15 broods or 1.80 young per 100 days
- [[Allopoecilia]] male: 561 days, 1 brood (10 individuals) = 0.18 broods, 1.78 young per 100 days
- [[Limia]] males: 1,628 days, 6 broods (61 individuals) = 0.87 broods, 8.75 young per 100 days
- [[Lebistes]] males: 2,077 days, 1 brood (27 individuals) = 0.05 broods, 0.13 young per 100 days
- [[Mollienisia sphenops]] male (Acapulco): 21 matings over 5,196 days produced 67 broods (1,067 young) = 1.29 broods, 20.54 young per 100 days

**Key Conclusion**: M. formosa fertility with [[Mollienisia]] males was approximately 10 times higher than with males of other genera, though all offspring remained matroclinous regardless of paternal species.
