# A Contribution Toward a Bibliography on Game Kill and Creel Census Procedures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-statistics

## Executive Summary

This is an annotated-style reference listing compiled by [[Vincent Schultz]], agricultural biometrician at the Agricultural Experiment Station, University of Maryland (Miscellaneous Publication No. 359, 1959). It catalogues literature on two harvest-estimation traditions: [[Game-Kill Survey]]s, which estimate wildlife harvest, and [[Creel Census]]es, which estimate angling catch. The emphasis is on statistical procedures and planning details rather than on harvest totals themselves. The list is organized into two main sections (game-kill pages 3 to 20, creel census pages 21 to 38) plus a short appended group of three items on licence-holder counts. No summary totals are given in the document.

## Background & Description

The compiler received repeated requests for statistical advice on planning game-kill surveys and creel censuses, which prompted the collection. The bibliography deliberately:

- Excludes most studies reporting actual harvest figures, keeping mainly methodological or planning material.
- Includes a few commercial fisheries papers because of their "statistical nature."
- Contains items that were read by the compiler, including quarterly reports and obscure releases, which often hold the most useful planning details.
- Is not exhaustive for any individual state project.

Users are advised to consult both the game-kill and creel census listings when planning a harvest study. Acknowledgments go to Daniel R. Embody, W. S. Overton, Jr., and others who supplied reports.

The references are overwhelmingly agency reports, proceedings of the [[Western Association of State Game and Fish Commissioners]] and [[Southeastern Association of Game and Fish Commissioners]], and papers in the *Journal of Wildlife Management*, *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* and *California Fish and Game*. Many are undated or are mimeographed departmental bulletins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography spans a wide range of taxa and settings:

- **Game birds and mammals:** [[Waterfowl Harvest]] (flyway plans, [[Mississippi Flyway]], [[Pacific Flyway]], [[Central Flyway]]), [[Mourning Dove]] kill, [[Ring-Necked Pheasant]] surveys, [[White-Tailed Deer]] and elk harvests, cottontail and Danish hare populations, squirrels, and furbearers such as trappers' records.
- **Freshwater fisheries:** lake and stream creel surveys, [[Trout]] exploitation and plantings, [[Largemouth Bass]] angler harvest, and reservoir studies (for example [[Tennessee Valley Authority]] impoundments, [[Clear Lake]] in Iowa, [[Pend Oreille Lake]] in Idaho, [[Clearwater Lake]] in Missouri).
- **Marine and coastal fisheries:** [[Saltwater Sport Fishing]] in California, Florida, Texas, Long Island and Virginia, and commercial catch statistics such as the [[Striped Bass]] fishery.
- **Management concepts:** regulation effects (size and bag limits), season length, and the relationship between catch per unit effort and stock abundance.

## Key Findings

The listing's most useful threads for a biologist planning a harvest estimate are methodological:

1. **Mail and questionnaire surveys.** [[Atwood]] (1956, *Journal of Wildlife Management* 20(1):1–16) examined the validity of mail survey data on bagged waterfowl, and several Atwood manuscripts address response bias and identification bias adjustments. [[Hayne]] and [[Eberhardt]] (1958) discuss the bias of estimates from voluntary reports.
2. **Field bag checks and checking stations.** [[Crawford]] (1951) describes the field bag-check method for hunting success, pressure and kill. [[Rogers]] (1953) and [[Lawson]] (1951) examine checking stations in Colorado and elsewhere.
3. **Statistical analysis of game records.** [[Bellrose]] (1947) analyzes methods used to determine game kill. [[Moran]] (1952, 1953) addresses statistical analysis of game-bird records. [[Petrides]] (1953) estimates percentage kill in ringnecked pheasants.
4. **Creel census design.** [[Carlander]] (1956) edited a symposium on sampling problems in creel census. [[Carlander]], [[Di Costanzo]] and [[Jessen]] (1958) reviewed sampling problems. [[Mottley]] (1946) gives a statistical analysis of creel-census data. [[Mottley]] and [[Embody]] (1940) describe an efficient method for taking a creel census.
5. **Catch per unit effort and exploitation.** [[Ricker]] (1940) relates catch per unit effort to abundance and exploitation rate. [[Ricker]] (1942) reports creel census and population estimates for Shoe Lake, Indiana.
6. **Regulation effects.** [[Allen]] (1955a, 1955b) analyzes the efficiency of size limits and bag limits in New Zealand fisheries management.
7. **Variance and statistical adjustment.** [[Embody]] (1954) applies [[Analysis of Variance]] to creel census data, and [[Embody]] (1955) describes statistical adjustments for changes in census methods on Pend Oreille Lake. [[Rose]] (1956) examines catch record variance in completed and uncompleted fishing trips.
8. **Sampling theory for fisheries.** [[Gulland]] (1957, FAO) reviews sampling problems and methods in fisheries research. [[Sukhatme]] and colleagues (1958) present a sampling technique for estimating sea fish catch in India.
9. **Long-running programs.** Overton's Florida "Wildlife Inventory, Harvest and Economic Survey" progress reports (1951–1956) recur many times, and Iowa's quantitative creel census series by [[Rose]] (1956, 1958) and [[Moen]] reports sampling levels and sub-sampling.
10. **Acknowledged foundational sources.** The compiler notes that many useful results sit in quarterly or departmental reports, not journal articles, so readers should check agency publications as well as the formal literature.
