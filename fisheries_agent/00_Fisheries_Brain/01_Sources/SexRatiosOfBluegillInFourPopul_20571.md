# Sex Ratios of Bluegill in Four Populations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepomis-macrochirus

## Executive Summary

This short paper by H. R. Schmittou (Auburn University Agricultural Experiment Station, 1967) reports the sex ratios of [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) in four populations in Alabama and Georgia. Two were experimental ponds stocked with [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) and bluegill, one was a 3.3-acre farm pond, and two were reservoirs. Across 4,456 fish, males made up 31.0% and females 69.0%. A [[Chi-Square Test]] showed that the sex ratio in each population differed very significantly from 1:1 (P = 0.01). Because male bluegill grow faster and larger, a skewed ratio can affect the balance of [[Bass-Bluegill Populations]] and the reliability of stocking formulas.

## Background & Description

- **Rationale:** Male bluegill grow faster and reach a larger size than females (Schmittou, 1965). A population with more males is therefore considered more desirable, and an unequal sex ratio in either direction could disrupt bass-bluegill balance, especially where stocking follows a fixed formula.
- **Species:** [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) is the focal species. [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) was co-stocked in the experimental ponds.
- **Study sites:**
  - **Ponds E-7 and E-8** (Agricultural Experiment Station, Auburn University): two rectangular 1-acre ponds. In March 1963 each was stocked with 2,000 2-inch bluegill from the [[Marion National Fish Hatchery]], Alabama. In April 1963 they received 150 1-inch largemouth bass. Ponds were sampled until drained in October 1964 and were not fished.
  - **FP-1** (Auburn University): a 3.3-acre unmanaged mixed-fish pond, drained in October 1962 and repopulated from its watershed. The origin of its bluegill is unknown.
  - **[[Lake Martin]]:** a 40,000-acre reservoir on the Tallapoosa River (Alabama River System), impounded in 1926, 58 miles above the Tallapoosa–Coosa junction.
  - **[[Lake Eufaula]]:** a 45,200-acre reservoir on the Chattahoochee River between Alabama and Georgia, impounded in 1962.
- **Methods:**
  - Ponds E-7 and E-8 were sampled during the study and sexed by dissecting fish and examining gonads. Fish recovered at draining were sexed by secondary sex characteristics. A check of 50 fish of each sex found one misidentification.
  - Bluegill in the reservoirs and FP-1 were collected by [[Rotenone Sampling]] (Lake Martin, 26–29 July 1966; Lake Eufaula and FP-1, 12 August 1966). Samples were preserved in 10% formalin and sexed by dissection.
  - Age groups were separated by length-frequency, with scale aging of 10–30 fish per inch group as support.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sex-biased growth:** [[Sexual Dimorphism]] in growth rate was significant (P = 0.05) in three pond populations from about the start of the 5-inch group (about 4.5 inches). Male bluegill grow faster and reach larger sizes than females.
- **Size-related sex skew:** In every population, the number of bluegill fell with increasing size class, but the percentage of males rose. The author attributes this mainly to faster male growth. Whether males also live longer than females is unknown.
- **Sampling bias:** Because larger males are more likely to be captured in larger inch groups, the author limited the analysis to 3- and 4-inch groups. This correction increased the estimated sex-ratio inequality.
- **Unresolved cause:** The author notes that further work is needed to determine whether the unequal ratios are genetically or environmentally determined.

## Key Findings

**Table 1: Sex ratio by population (3- and 4-inch groups used for corrected analysis)**

| Population | Total | Males | Females | % Male | % Female |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pond E-7 (Marion Hatchery) | 1,738 | 553 | 1,185 | 31.8 | 68.2 |
| Pond E-8 (Marion Hatchery) | 1,688 | 539 | 1,149 | 31.9 | 68.1 |
| Lake Martin | 237 | 91 | 146 | 38.4 | 61.6 |
| Lake Eufaula | 523 | 144 | 379 | 27.5 | 72.5 |
| FP-1 | 270 | 56 | 214 | 20.7 | 79.3 |
| **All populations** | **4,456** | **1,383** | **3,073** | **31.0** | **69.0** |

- Marion Hatchery ponds combined: 3,426 fish, 31.9% male and 68.1% female.
- Each population departed significantly from 1:1 (chi-square, P = 0.01).

**Table 2: Sex by inch group, natural populations (Lake Martin, Lake Eufaula, FP-1)**

- Total of 1,381 fish in 3- to 8+-inch groups: 35.1% male and 64.9% female.
- 3-inch group: 506 fish, 28.5% male.
- 4-inch group: 524 fish, 28.1% male.
- 5-inch group: 231 fish, 48.9% male.
- 6-inch group: 70 fish, 51.4% male.
- 7-inch group: 32 fish, 84.4% male.
- 8+-inch group: 18 fish, 100% male.
- Male share rises with size even as the total number of fish declines.

**Population-level conclusions**

- [[Lake Martin]]: no significant difference in sex distribution across 3- to 8+-inch groups.
- [[Lake Eufaula]] and FP-1: each had significantly more females than males.
- Using corrected 3- and 4-inch data, all four populations showed unequal sex ratios.

**Overall conclusion:** Unequal sex ratios in bluegill are common across these populations, and the sampling method itself can inflate apparent male dominance in larger size classes. The cause of the skew remains open.
