# Upper Basin Endangered Fishes: Email Assessment of Population Decline and Recovery Efficacy

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a March 13, 2009 email from [[Harold Tyus]], a senior research scientist at the University of Colorado at Boulder, to Robin Silver of the Native Fish Lab. Tyus argues that the endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin are in worse shape than before, and that the [[Upper Colorado River Recovery Program]] has not produced the declines' reversal. He contends that the system is unstable, that non-native species are proliferating, and that standard [[Conservation Biology]] tools are being misapplied. He asks that he not be quoted, since the statements could affect his working relationships on endangered-species efforts. The email is an informal expert opinion, not a peer-reviewed study, and it contains no new sampling data.

## Background & Description

The email concerns four federally listed species of the Colorado River system:

- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): the largest native cyprinid of the basin.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): a catostomid with known spawning populations confined to the Green and Lower Yampa rivers.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*): a cyprinid associated with canyon habitats.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*): a cyprinid with remnant wild populations in the Lower Basin.

The author frames the discussion around the question of whether species should be downlisted under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. He also raises the use of [[Franklin's 50/500 Rule]] and the use of hatchery sex ratios in estimating effective population size (Ne), a method he considers inappropriate for this system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Tyus describes the Upper Basin as "in a state of flux." He attributes instability to several non-native taxa:

- **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus dolomieu*): described as proliferating in the Yampa River, which he calls "the #1 habitat" for the endangered fishes.
- **[[Northern Pike]]** (*Esox lucius*): cited as an ongoing problem.
- Other non-native fishes, abundant [[Crayfish]], and [[Sticklebacks]].

He also notes that abundance shifts in Upper Mainstem Colorado and Green River pikeminnow populations appear erratic, citing work by Osmundson and Best (the email gives these names in garbled form). The author states that the Green River pikeminnow population, the largest in the basin, declined in a three-year period. The reported percentage is garbled in the scanned text ("488"), so the exact figure cannot be confirmed; it may be 48%.

## Key Findings

1. **Overall decline:** Tyus states that the abundance of all four species has "surely declined," and that the endangered fishes have declined under the present recovery program. He describes this as an opinion, not a measured trend.
2. **Colorado Pikeminnow:** Populations show erratic abundance shifts. The Green River population (described as the largest) reportedly declined in three years (figure garbled; see above).
3. **Razorback Sucker:** Wild fish in the Green and Lower Yampa rivers, the only known spawning populations, are "functionally extinct." Stocking hatchery fish is occurring, though the author questions its value.
4. **Humpback Chub:** Populations in the Green basin and Cataract Canyon are "just about gone."
5. **Bonytail:** Populations have not improved. Lower Basin remnant populations of bonytail, razorback, and humpback chub have also declined, which undercuts the earlier view that these offered "comfort."
6. **Downlisting:** Tyus considers downlisting "ridiculous" given the proliferation of smallmouth bass, northern pike, and other non-natives.
7. **Methodology:** He criticizes the use of Franklin's 50/500 rule and hatchery sex ratios in place of field collections to estimate Ne, arguing that these tools fail in unstable systems.
8. **Recovery metric:** He proposes that recovery should be judged only by increasing or stable populations in a natural environment that is not undergoing changes in species composition.
9. **Caveat on attribution:** Tyus says he cannot judge whether the system would be worse without the program, since that would be conjecture. He asks that he not be quoted.
