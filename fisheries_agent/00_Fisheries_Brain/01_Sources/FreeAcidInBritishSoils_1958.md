## Executive Summary
This 1958 communication by Evelyn Gorham investigates whether **sulphuric acid** is the principal driver of low pH in acidic humus systems of northern and western Britain — specifically **[[Sphagnum Bog Peats]]** and **[[Mor Humus]]** layers in woodland soils. By sampling bog pool waters across seven British bog systems and pressing soil solution from Lake District oakwood mors, the author demonstrates strong statistical correlations between free hydrogen ion concentration and sulphate concentration (corrected for sea-spray-derived sulphate). The findings implicate both **[[Industrial Air Pollution]]** (via sulphur dioxide oxidation) and natural **decomposition of organic sulphur compounds** as sources of soil and bog-water acidity, with implications for soil leaching, podzolization, and acid deposition ecology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat types studied:**
  - **[[Ombrogenous Bogs]]** — deep, extensive peatlands (Cooran Lane, Druimbasbie, Strathy, Rannoch Moor, Tregaron, Dergoals, Kilkahockadale, Moor House, Coom Rigg) that derive nearly all ion input from atmospheric deposition rather than groundwater or mineral substrate.
  - **[[Mor Humus]]** layers in Lake District oakwoods on Silurian substrata, overlying leached mineral soils transitional between brown earths and podzols.
  - Comparative freshwater system: Nova Scotian lakes draining acid peaty soils on lime-poor substrata.
- **Indicator species:** *Sphagnum* moss cover used to confirm natural, water-saturated bog conditions.
- **Anthropogenic influence on habitat chemistry:**
  - Proximity to industrial regions (e.g., **Ringinglow Bog**, near Sheffield) sharply elevates hydrogen ion and sulphate concentrations (0.57 and 0.96 m.equiv./l., respectively) relative to remote, unpolluted bogs.
  - **English Lake District** rainfall pH averages below 4.5 (occasionally below 4.0), reflecting regional **[[Acid Rain]]** influence from air pollution.
- **Soil-water interaction:** Mor humus solutions show lower correlation between H⁺ and sulphate than bog waters, attributed to partial neutralization of acid by mineral base cations embedded in humus — linking soil mineralogy to the buffering capacity of organic horizons.

## Key Findings
- **Bog waters (7 sites):**
  - H⁺ concentration: 0.03–0.13 m.equiv./l. (mean 0.066 m.equiv./l.)
  - Sulphate: 0.06–0.24 m.equiv./l. (mean 0.133 m.equiv./l.)
  - Correlation (H⁺ vs. total sulphate): r = 0.964
  - Correlation after subtracting **[[Sea Spray]]**-derived sulphate: r = 0.985
  - Regression: y = 0.556x + 0.006
  - H⁺ accounts for ~61% of corrected sulphate equivalence.

- **Mor humus solutions (16 samples, Lake District oakwoods):**
  - H⁺ concentration: 0.02–0.36 m.equiv./l. (mean 0.109 m.equiv./l.)
  - Sulphate: 0.26–1.01 m.equiv./l. (mean 0.508 m.equiv./l.; corrected mean ~0.46 m.equiv./l.)
  - Correlation (H⁺ vs. corrected sulphate): r = 0.872
  - Regression: y = 0.307x − 0.033
  - H⁺ accounts for only ~24% of corrected sulphate equivalence — lower than in bogs, suggesting neutralization by mineral soil bases.

- **Comparative pH discrepancy:** Direct glass-electrode pH measurements on fresh soils/peats are consistently **lower** than pH inferred from pressed/pooled water solutions:
  - Moor House: pool water pH 3.7–4.1 vs. fresh peat pH 3.3–3.9
  - Lake District mors: solution pH 3.8–4.6 vs. fresh soil pH 3.2–3.5
  - Attributed to a **"suspension effect"** on the glass electrode caused by exchangeable H⁺ adsorbed onto soil colloids.

- **Sources of acidity identified:**
  1. **Atmospheric sulphur dioxide oxidation** from industrial fuel combustion → sulphuric acid deposition (dominant near industrial zones).
  2. **Natural decomposition of organic sulphur compounds** in peat/humus, relevant even in non-industrial areas.
  3. Minor but non-negligible contribution of **sea spray sulphate**, corrected for via chloride:sulphate ratio (9.7) in seawater.

- **Broader ecological implication:** Sulphuric acid appears to be the principal chemical driver of low pH in both bog peat and woodland mor systems, with direct consequences for **[[Soil Leaching]]** processes and **[[Podzolization]]** of underlying mineral soils — establishing a mechanistic link between atmospheric pollution, soil acidification, and long-term pedogenesis in humid, high-rainfall regions of Britain.

- **Supporting cross-regional evidence:** Similar inter-correlations between H⁺, sulphate, and dissolved organic carbon were found in acid, peat-draining lakes of **[[Nova Scotia]]**, reinforcing the generality of the sulphuric acid mechanism beyond the British sites studied.
