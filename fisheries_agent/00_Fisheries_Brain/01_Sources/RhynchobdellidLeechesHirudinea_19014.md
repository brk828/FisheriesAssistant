# Rhynchobdellid Leeches (Hirudinea, Rhynchobdellae) in the Relict Ohrid Lake Region

#hirudinea #rhynchobdellae #glossiphoniidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic study by [[Sket B]] reviews the rhynchobdellid leeches (Hirudinea, Rhynchobdellae) of the [[Ohrid Lake]] basin, an ancient lake in the southern Balkans. The lake itself is inhabited almost exclusively by three endemic taxa: *Cystobranchus pawlowskii*, *Glossiphonia complanata maculosa* and *Glossiphonia pulchella*. Widespread species occur mainly in springs and eutrophic ponds around the lake. The paper redescribes both endemic glossiphoniids, raises *G. pulchella* to species rank, and proposes that these taxa arose during a low-water lake phase and were re-connected with spring populations when lake levels rose.

## Background & Description

The Ohrid basin has a rich endemic fauna. For example, its gastropods include 77 species, 59 of them endemic (after [[Hadzisce S]], 1974), and its tricladid flatworms include 26 species, about 20 of them endemic (after [[Kenk R]], 1978). Earlier leech studies by [[Augener H]], [[Pawlowski L K]] and [[Sket B]] (1968) had recorded some endemic forms. Sket (1968) described *G. complanata maculosa*, *G. complanata f. pulchella*, *Cystobranchus pawlowskii* and *Dina lineata ohridana* as new. Some of these descriptions were uncertain because samples were small, and [[Minelli A]] (1979) accepted them with scepticism.

The present study is based mainly on collections made by the author with F. Velkovrh in September 1979 and September 1981. More than 600 Glossiphoniidae specimens were collected. Methods included individual sampling in the littoral and in springs, dredging, and skin diving. Some animals were photographed alive, narcotised in ethanol, and preserved in 70% ethanol or 4% formalin. Some specimens were dissected and others sectioned for histology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake versus springs:** Karstic springs around the lake contain fauna of similar type and origin, unlike stagnant ponds and marshes. Rheocrene springs around the lake mostly lack Rhynchobdellae but are rich in [[Erpobdellidae]].
- **Cystobranchus pawlowskii:** Sparse across the lake, found from the shore to 60 m. It was also recorded in the Sveti Naum springlakelet and in other nearby springs. Its host is unknown, though it is probably a fish. Only egg cocoons were found on shores, suggesting it avoids shore habitats in summer.
- **Glossiphonia complanata maculosa:** Strongly tied to lacustrine habitats. It is most abundant on hard, wave-exposed shores, often on the undersides of stones, and reaches 50 m. It is sparse on muddy bottoms, which may limit its spread into the shallows.
- **Glossiphonia pulchella:** Co-occurs with *G. c. maculosa* on hard shores but in much smaller numbers. It becomes dominant on soft bottoms and at depth, reaching at least 160 m.
- **Glossiphonia complanata complanata:** Restricted to a few flowing-water springs with constantly low temperatures around 10 °C, including springs at the Sveti Naum lakelet and Studeničište. It has not been found in the lake proper.
- **Substrate and burrowing:** Below about 40 m the bottom is silty, but the glossiphoniids do not burrow into it, unlike the co-occurring erpobdellids.
- **Other species:** *Helobdella stagnalis* and *Hemiclepsis marginata* occur only sparsely in the lake. *Glossiphonia heteroclita* and *Haementeria costata* are very local. *Batracobdella paludosa* and *G. cf. concolor* were found in ditches or eutrophic ditches.

## Key Findings

**Species inventory**
- The Ohrid basin yields 10 rhynchobdellid taxa (Summary section). Three are endemic to the lake: *C. pawlowskii*, *G. c. maculosa* and *G. pulchella*.
- The remaining taxa are widespread species: *G. complanata complanata*, *G. heteroclita*, *G. cf. concolor*, *Helobdella stagnalis*, *Batracobdella paludosa*, *Hemiclepsis marginata* and *Haementeria costata*.
- *Piscicola geometra* records are considered very doubtful. Augener's (1937) material was actually *C. pawlowskii*.

**Taxonomic decisions**
- *Glossiphonia pulchella* (stat. nov.) is elevated from a form of *G. complanata* to a species. It has a whitish, pinkish-grey body, large dorsal tubercles, a small posterior sucker, and does not interbreed with *G. c. maculosa*.
- *G. c. maculosa* is regarded as a subspecies on the basis of the low percentage of the *complanata* phenotype in the composite population, following Mayr's (1969) criteria. Its taxonomic independence is considered secure, although a few apparent hybrids were noted.
- *Cystobranchus pawlowskii* is probably descended from *C. respirans*, but its genital organs have also changed in shape.

**Morphological and colour variation**
- *G. c. maculosa* around the Sveti Naum lakelet: about 10% of the population has the *complanata*-type dorsal pigment.
- *G. pulchella* has 11 pairs of testicles, compared with an apparent 10 in *G. complanata*. The author notes this count is difficult to make in *G. complanata*.
- Deep-water *G. pulchella* are very thin, transparent, and have irregularly shaped eyes.
- Colour in *G. c. maculosa* varies greatly across populations, with a distinctive "Gradište-type" pattern and three brown girdles in deeper animals.

**Evolutionary interpretation**
- Both endemic *Glossiphonia* taxa are closely related to *G. c. complanata*, which is considered their ancestor. They differ mainly in surface features such as pigmentation and tubercles, a pattern the author considers common in Ohrid speciation.
- The taxa probably diverged during a lower lake-level stage. The lake then rose rapidly; submerged solution runnels at depths of up to 6 m near Gradište support this. The rise brought *G. c. maculosa* back into contact with spring populations of *G. c. complanata*.
- Some differentiation is in progress. For example, Gradište *G. c. maculosa* show distinct pigmentation. Depth adaptation has reduced pigmentation and sucker size in *G. pulchella*. The author considers depth variation probably clinal, with gene flow maintained, and treats all depth animals as one taxon provisionally.

**Open problems**
- Very large, softbodied, poorly pigmented animals from depth may be *G. c. maculosa* in a special physiological state, analogous to adult *Theromyzon tessulatum*, or a separate species.
- A few deep specimens with large dorsal tubercles could be hybrids of *G. c. maculosa* and *G. pulchella*, but they are too rare to alter the overall picture.
- Deep-water *G. pulchella* may represent an independent subspecies or a closely related species, and this remains unresolved.

**Gaps and future work**
- Reproduction was not investigated, and feeding experiments were unsuccessful.
- The northernmost shores near Struga are not well studied and may hold more cosmopolitan species.
- Host identities for *Cystobranchus pawlowskii* are unknown.
