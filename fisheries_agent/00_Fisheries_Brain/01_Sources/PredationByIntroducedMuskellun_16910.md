# Predation by Introduced Muskellunge on Perch and Bass

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae #centrarchidae #percidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Gammon and Hasler]] tracks a five-year whole-lake experiment in two small northern Wisconsin bog lakes, [[George Lake]] and [[Corrine Lake]], where young [[Muskellunge]] were stocked into systems with stunted [[Yellow Perch]] populations and small numbers of [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]]. The authors asked whether muskellunge predation would reduce perch density, improve bass survival, and produce a better balance between the species. Perch declined sharply in both lakes, largemouth bass recruitment failed after stocking, and smallmouth bass numbers rose. The authors attribute these divergent responses mainly to differences in early schooling behavior and habitat preference. Muskellunge survival and growth were also measured, with a high early loss in spring-stocked fish.

## Background & Description

- **Origin of the study:** [[Johnson]] (1954) described unbalanced perch and bass populations in these lakes. He found many young bass were eaten by perch and proposed that reducing perch density would increase bass survival.
- **Failed earlier attempts (1953–1955):** Stocking of largemouth bass and walleye, netting, and local [[Rotenone Treatment]] did not reduce perch numbers.
- **Stocking:**
  - George Lake: 400 yearling muskellunge, obtained from the Wisconsin Department of Conservation (Woodruff, Wisconsin), stocked in May 1956.
  - Corrine Lake: 395 large young-of-the-year muskellunge, introduced in October 1956.
- **Objectives:** (1) predation efficiency of muskellunge, (2) growth rates of perch and bass, and (3) mortality of stocked muskellunge.

### Study Lakes

| Feature | George Lake | Corrine Lake |
|---|---|---|
| Area | 17.3 ha | 14.6 ha |
| Bog shoreline | ~5% | ~80% |
| Littoral substrate | Mostly sand and rubble | Mostly muck |
| Midsummer anoxia | Below 5.5 m | Below 3 m |

Aquatic vegetation was sparse in both lakes. Morphometry is described in [[Gammon]] (1961).

### Methods Overview

- **Population estimates:** Fish were captured by [[Fyke Netting]], electrofishing and angling. Annual estimates used [[Schnabel Estimate]] or [[Schumacher-Eschmeyer Estimate]] methods, supplemented by [[Petersen Estimate]] calculations. Most sample sizes exceeded the recommendations of [[Robson and Regier]] (1964).
- **Age and growth:** Scales from 1958–1960 were used for [[Scale-Based Growth Back-Calculation]]. Only smallmouth bass yielded an acceptable regression (Y = 38.01 + 1.290X). Correction intercepts were borrowed for largemouth bass (22.8 mm, from nearby Flora Lake) and perch (25 mm). Growth was compared before and after stocking with a *t* test.
- **Condition:** Length-weight regressions for each year were compared by analysis of covariance.
- **Diet:** Stomach contents were collected by [[Stomach Flushing]], which allowed fish to be released alive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Perch cycles:** The authors draw on [[Alm]] (1946), who documented regular abundance cycles in Swedish bog lakes dominated by European perch. A strong year class suppresses recruitment by cannibalism until it declines. The authors suggest similar cycles may occur in [[Yellow Perch]] populations in lakes with few other species.
- **Species-specific behavior:**
  - Young largemouth bass formed dense schools from hatching into the second summer, closely associated with vegetation and brush piles.
  - Young smallmouth bass schooled initially but dispersed by mid- to late July and then occurred singly or in small groups in shallow, rocky or sandy water near shore.
  - Largemouth bass in both lakes congregated in the same scattered areas preferred by muskellunge.
- **Muskellunge diet:** Other studies cited describe muskellunge as almost exclusively piscivorous ([[Hourston]], 1952; [[Parsons]], 1959; [[Anderson]], 1948).

## Key Findings

### Yellow Perch
- **George Lake:** The perch population was already declining before stocking. Perch abundance fell from roughly 31,000 individuals (Abstract) to a level too low to estimate by 1957. In 1957, 104 fyke-net-days captured only 21 perch. In May 1958, ten days of netting and electrofishing produced only 18 small adult perch. Netting removed 19,600 perch in 1953–1954, which may have contributed to the early decline.
- **Corrine Lake:** Perch stabilized at roughly 10,000 individuals after stocking (estimates of 10,030, 11,200 and 9,480 in 1956, 1957 and 1958), then declined sharply in 1959 (1,900 estimated).
- **Growth:** In George Lake, growth increased in the first two years of life after the predator was introduced, with no increase in older fish. In Corrine Lake, average growth remained about the same. Perch growth in both lakes was similar to that in Weber and Silver Lakes (Schneberger, 1935) and Flora Lake (Parker, 1958).
- **Recruitment:** Excellent annual hatches of perch occurred through 1960 in George Lake despite very few mature adults.

### Largemouth Bass
- **George Lake:** Numbers of bass aged II and older were stable before stocking. Young-of-the-year were largely absent after stocking, except for 1958. The population steadily declined. The only significant growth change was a reduced growth rate in young-of-the-year.
- **Corrine Lake:** The population declined steadily from 1954 to 1960. Recruitment was poor after the 1957 hatch, with fair numbers again seen in 1960. A significant decrease in growth occurred at age five.
- **Abstract conclusion:** Declines resulted from the virtual absence of small bass surviving to their third summer, despite several strong year classes.

### Smallmouth Bass
- **George Lake:** Numbers rose to more than twice the former abundance and remained high, sustained by small but steady annual production, although a decline was measured in 1960. No exceptionally strong year classes were recorded.
- **Growth:** Growth increased significantly only at age three.
- **Interpretation:** The authors suggest reduced perch density aided fry survival and that smallmouth habits reduced contact with muskellunge.

### Muskellunge
- **Survival:** About 25% of the spring-stocked fish (George Lake) were unaccounted for after 49 days. No similar loss occurred in the fall-stocked Corrine Lake fish. A weighted estimate gave an annual survival rate of 0.76 for George Lake and 0.87 for Corrine Lake (excluding 1960). After the initial loss, annual mortality of 20% to 25% was observed in both populations.
- **Diet:** Of 220 muskellunge stomachs examined in 1958–1959, about one-third contained food. Nearly all food was fish. Perch formed the bulk of the diet, and the ratio of small to large perch eaten corresponded closely to their ratio in the lakes. Bass fry and fingerlings were also prominent.
- **Growth:** Growth in George Lake declined considerably from 1959 to 1960, probably because small perch were scarce after the first year. Growth in Corrine Lake was slow in the first summer and increased each year. Fish from both lakes reached the same length in the fourth year. Growth in both lakes was below the Wisconsin average ([[Schloemer]], 1936).
- **Tagging effects:** Annual tag losses were about 20%. [[Muir]] (1960) found the tag deleterious to growth. Growth was unaffected in George Lake but reduced in Corrine Lake.

### Overall Community Effects
- Length-weight relationships of all species remained unchanged after muskellunge introduction.
- The authors conclude that the timing of stocking relative to the perch abundance cycle largely determined the course of events.
