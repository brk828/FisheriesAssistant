## Executive Summary
This 1948 note by Moore and Poole documents the first confirmed record of the **[[Pigmy Sunfish]]** (*Elassoma zonatum*) in Oklahoma. Twenty-one specimens (13 males, 8 females) were collected on April 19, 1947, from a small, turbid roadside pool near a swamp approximately ten miles east of Broken Bow, McCurtain County, Oklahoma. The specimens showed several morphological deviations from the original species description by Jordan and Evermann (1896), prompting a detailed re-comparison. A follow-up survey on June 4, 1947, at the same site failed to recover any additional *Elassoma zonatum* specimens, despite the presence of many previously co-occurring species, suggesting possible seasonal, ephemeral, or migratory population dynamics tied to the connectivity between the roadside pool and the adjacent swamp.

## Background & Description
Comparison of Oklahoma specimens against the Jordan and Evermann (1896) type description revealed the following distinctions:

| Character | Jordan & Evermann (1896) | Oklahoma Specimens (Moore & Poole, 1947) |
|---|---|---|
| Eye size (in head lengths) | 3.0 | Average 3.9 |
| Anal fin rays | Always III, 5 | III, 6 (n=16); III, 5 (n=5) |
| Vertical bars (count) | 11 | Average 10 |
| Vertical bar width vs. interspace | Equal | Slightly wider than interspaces |
| Lateral black spot length (vs. eye) | Equal to eye | ~2x length of eye; divided by 1–2 light lines |

**Sexual Dimorphism:**
- Males are distinguished from females by **blackened posterior edges of the dorsal and anal fins**.
- Males observed in breeding-associated coloration during the April collection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Small, shallow (~2 ft depth), highly turbid roadside pool (~15 ft x 8 ft), seasonally or storm-connected to an adjacent clearer, shallower (~1 ft) swamp.
- **Hydrological connectivity:** The pool and swamp appear to be linked during heavy rainfall events, suggesting *E. zonatum* may utilize both habitats depending on water conditions, or disperse from the swamp into the pool during high-water periods.
- **Co-occurring species (April 19, 1947 collection):**
  - *Amia calva* (395 mm)
  - *Notemigonus crysoleucas*
  - *Notropis percobromus*
  - *Ameiurus melas*
  - *Ameiurus natalis*
  - *Fundulus notatus*
  - *Gambusia affinis*
  - *Aphredoderus sayanus* (observed spawning)
  - *Hololepis gracilis* (in breeding color)
  - *Chaenobryttus coronarius*
  - *Lepomis cyanellus*
  - *Lepomis megalotis*
  - *Lepomis macrochirus*
- **Follow-up survey (June 4, 1947):** No *E. zonatum* recovered despite repeated hauls. Notably absent taxa previously present: *Notropis percobromus*, *Ameiurus melas*, *A. natalis*, *Fundulus notatus*, *Hololepis gracilis*, *Chaenobryttus coronarius*.
- **New species recorded in June (not seen in April):**
  - *Esox vermiculatus*
  - *Lepomis humilis*
  - *Centrarchus macropterus* (large numbers of juveniles, from the nearby swamp)
- This turnover in species composition between April and June suggests strong seasonal habitat partitioning or pool-swamp exchange dynamics affecting the local fish assemblage.

## Key Findings
- **First documented occurrence** of *Elassoma zonatum* in the state of Oklahoma, collected under the direction of Dr. W. H. Irwin.
- Specimens exhibited **morphometric divergence** from the original type description in eye size, anal fin ray counts, vertical bar count/width, and lateral spot size — potentially indicating geographic variation at the edge of the species' range.
- Sexual dimorphism identified via **fin pigmentation** (blackened dorsal/anal fin margins in males).
- The species was **not recollected** two months later at the same site despite intensive sampling effort, implying a transient, cryptic, or highly habitat-specific population possibly tied to swamp/pool hydrological connectivity.
- The collection site — a small roadside pool near U.S. Highway 10, McCurtain County — represents a biodiversity-rich microhabitat supporting at least 16 fish species across the two sampling events, underscoring the ecological value of small ephemeral wetlands in the [[Red River Drainage]] system.
- This record contributes to the broader understanding of the distribution of the family **[[Elassomatidae]]**, a group of small, cryptic freshwater fishes endemic to the southeastern United States.
