# Virgin Chub (Gila robusta seminuda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Gila #ConservationLaw #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary
This document is an official notification letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Ecological Services, Phoenix, Arizona office), dated September 7, 1989, announcing the final rule listing the **Virgin chub** ([[Gila robusta seminuda]]), a subspecies of the [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), as an **endangered species** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The rule was published in the Federal Register on August 24, 1989. Notably, the **final designation of critical habitat** was deferred pending completion of a full economic analysis. The letter is administrative/regulatory in nature rather than a primary scientific research paper, and thus contains no direct morphological or ecological field data — only status, jurisdiction, and regulatory implications.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (body form, coloration, meristic counts, or diagnostic taxonomic features) are provided in this source. The species is identified taxonomically as:
- **Genus/species**: *Gila robusta* (Roundtail Chub complex)
- **Subspecies**: *Gila robusta seminuda* — the Virgin chub, a geographically isolated subspecies associated with the [[Virgin River]] drainage.

*(Note: For detailed morphological descriptions, refer to primary ichthyological literature on the Gila robusta species complex, as this regulatory letter does not include such data.)*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Endemic to the [[Virgin River]] drainage system, spanning portions of Arizona, Nevada, and Utah.
- **Ecosystem type**: Freshwater riverine habitat within the [[Colorado River Basin]] (the Virgin River is a tributary of the Colorado River system via Lake Mead).
- **Geographic range**: Restricted to three U.S. states — [[Arizona]], [[Nevada]], and [[Utah]] — indicating a narrow, localized distribution typical of many endemic southwestern cyprinids.
- **Conservation context**: The narrow endemism and small range are consistent with common threats faced by southwestern desert fishes, including habitat alteration, water diversion, non-native species introduction, and flow regulation (though these are not explicitly detailed in this letter).

## Key Findings
- **Legal status**: The Virgin chub was formally listed as an **endangered species** effective with the August 24, 1989 Federal Register final rule.
- **Critical habitat**: Designation of critical habitat was **deferred**, pending a full economic analysis — a procedural step under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Regulatory implications**:
  - Federal agencies must consult with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] under **Section 7(a)(4)** of the Act for actions that may affect the species.
  - **Section 6** (State Cooperative Agreements) allows Arizona, Nevada, and Utah to receive federal funding for species management.
  - Broad prohibitions apply, and **permits** may now be required for biologists and others conducting activities involving the species.
- **Administrative contacts**:
  - Arizona/Section 7 inquiries: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Phoenix, AZ office.
  - Recovery-related inquiries: Don Archer, Fishery Biologist, Salt Lake City, UT office.
- **Agency call to action**: The Service explicitly requested additional data on the species' status and potential recovery actions from the scientific and conservation community, indicating that knowledge of the subspecies was still developing at the time of listing.
