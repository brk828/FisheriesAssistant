# Blue River Road Bush Creek Realignment Biological Opinion Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) on October 14, 1997, addresses a formal [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] on the realignment of about 1,400 feet of [[Forest Road 281]] near the mouth of [[Bush Creek]] on the upper [[Blue River]], Greenlee County, Arizona. The [[U.S. Forest Service]] (Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests) is the lead agency, with the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] (COE) participating for its [[Clean Water Act]] Section 404 authority. Greenlee County would implement the project.

The Service concluded the project is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] or the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]]. The opinion includes an [[Incidental Take Statement]] with reasonable and prudent measures, mandatory terms and conditions, and three discretionary [[Conservation Recommendations]]. Before the consultation was completed, unauthorized pre-permit work had already been done on private land, and the COE authorized that completed work under [[Nationwide Permit No. 32]].

## Background & Description

**Consultation history.** The Forest Service requested formal consultation on September 8, 1997, and the Service received the request on September 10, 1997. The project is tiered to a June 16, 1997, biological opinion covering normal maintenance and repair of FR 281 under [[Best Management Practices]]. Greenlee County raised the realignment to its top priority on September 9, 1997, because state emergency funding was due to expire September 30, 1997. The COE was initially not part of the consultation because it had received no permit application. It joined as a consulting agency on September 17–18, 1997.

**Unauthorized work.** Greenlee County reported on September 18, 1997, that work had already occurred on the alignment, including:
- Placement of a culvert from the hatchery settling pond.
- Placement of fill on about 120 feet of the new alignment, entirely on private land.
- Disturbance of about 3,200 square feet of Bush Creek and 1,000 square feet of Blue River floodplain.
- A temporary diversion of hatchery tailwater directly into the river, bypassing the settling pond.

Work stopped immediately. The COE issued a Notice of Violation on September 30 and then authorized the completed work on October 7, 1997, with special conditions for aerial photography and hardening of the new embankment.

**Project design.** The existing alignment is about 1,200 feet long and runs as a dike of pushed-up river gravel that washes out in floods. The new alignment is about 1,400 feet long, lies along the toe of the hill on the southwest side of Bush Creek, and does not involve hillside excavation. Key features:
- Construction is expected to take about one week.
- Material comes from the old roadbed and from the Bush Creek ford, so no additional fill is needed.
- A new ford crossing on Bush Creek would be built without hardening.
- A culvert would carry hatchery settling-pond drainage into Bush Creek.
- The west lane of the old roadbed would remain as a dike during construction.
- Equipment would stay within 25 feet of the alignments. Staging would occur on private land outside the 50-year floodplain.
- No sediment controls are planned, since work is expected on dry surfaces, except possibly a silt fence during the Bush Creek ford work if the creek is flowing.

**Species status.**
- *Loach Minnow* was listed as threatened on October 28, 1986. Critical habitat was designated on March 8, 1994, but was enjoined by the New Mexico District Court in 1997, so no critical-habitat consultation is required. The species' range has declined about 85% from historic levels. It is a benthic fish of shallow, swift gravel and cobble habitat, feeds exclusively on aquatic insects, lives about 2 years, and spawns mainly March–May. A petition to uplist it to endangered status was found warranted but precluded.
- *Razorback Sucker* was listed as endangered on October 23, 1991, and critical habitat was designated on March 21, 1994. The species can exceed two feet in length and may live up to about 50 years. It spawns January–May over gravel, cobble, or coarse sand. It was reintroduced to the Blue River between 1986 and 1989 using hatchery stock, but no individuals were found in recent surveys.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Baseline condition.** The Blue River aquatic and riparian system is described as badly degraded. Nine native fish species, about 65% of the native fauna, have been extirpated, and floodplain soils and riparian vegetation have been largely lost. Base flows become critically low in dry periods. Roads in the floodplain are identified as a significant contributing factor to degradation and may prevent recovery.

**Fish community.** Four other native fishes persist: [[Speckled Dace]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Sonora Sucker]]. Loach minnow is the least abundant of the five native species. Nonnative species that compete with and prey on natives include [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Western Mosquitofish]], [[Red Shiner]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. Channel catfish may be abundant in the lower river.

**Direct and indirect effects.** The opinion characterizes the existing road as one of the largest federal impacts on the river. The realignment is expected to:
- Cause a short-term increase in sediment, which would be limited because work occurs on dry ground unless precipitation falls. Completion by late September is planned, but an expected El Niño winter could bring above-normal fall rain.
- Remove only a small amount of [[Riparian Vegetation]], with disposal of cleared vegetation in the strip between alignments expected to add woody debris to the aquatic system.
- Halve the old dike's width while leaving the other half in place, so channelization is only partly removed. Over time, floods are expected to breach the old dike and allow the river to form more natural channels.
- Still expose the new road to erosion as the river migrates, which could require future repair and armoring.

The opinion expects the long-term sediment reduction from removing the old alignment to outweigh the short-term increase. Other risks include toxic spills (for example petroleum products) that could reach the river, and possible increased recreational use of the improved road.

**Cumulative effects.** The opinion treats cumulative effects as the principal concern. Most watershed land is federal, but the main cumulative sources are private valley-bottom activities, including livestock grazing, cropping, residential development, and an aquaculture operation that stocks predatory nonnative fish and diverts water. The American Fisheries Society (1991) warned that accumulated small impacts are a serious threat. The opinion concludes that the realignment only partially mitigates past damage. It describes the project as part of a watershed-wide effort to avoid "pushing the trouble down the creek."

## Key Findings

- **Jeopardy determination:** The Service found the realignment **not likely to jeopardize** loach minnow or razorback sucker.
- **Concurrences:** The Service concurred with the Forest Service's "not likely to adversely affect" findings for [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Bald Eagle]], [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], and [[American Peregrine Falcon]]. Those concurrences apply only if work finishes before March 1 and outside the bald eagle wintering season. The Service did not concur on razorback sucker, which triggered the formal opinion.
- **Incidental take threshold:** Take is considered exceeded if any of the following occurs during project work:
  1. More than 20 dead fish of any species are found in the project area or within 500 yards downstream.
  2. Machinery or vehicles operate outside a 70-foot-wide band (35 feet on each side of the alignment centerlines).
  3. Any toxic spill enters the Blue River or its floodplain.
  Take cannot be quantified directly for either species, so it is indexed to the fish community and habitat.
- **Mandatory terms:** These include:
  - Minimizing work in the wetted channel.
  - No vehicle or equipment entry into surface water during loach minnow spawning seasons (March 1–June 1 and September 1–October 31).
  - No water removal from the Blue River.
  - Measures to prevent pollutants from entering surface waters.
  - Application of Best Management Practices.
  - Salvage and replanting of true willow poles.
  - Monitoring for dead or dying fish, with immediate telephone notice to the Service if thresholds are exceeded.
  - A written report within 60 days of project completion, with before-and-after photographs and all monitoring records.
- **Reinitiation:** Consultation must be reinitiated if take limits are exceeded, if new information reveals unconsidered effects, if the action is modified in unconsidered ways, or if a new species is listed or critical habitat designated. Operations must stop if a new consultation determines the additional take would cause irreversible, adverse impacts.
- **Conservation recommendations (discretionary):**
  1. Explore a further realignment to remove FR 281 from the floodplain.
  2. Conduct standard commercial aerial photography of FR 281 within six months, covering the Blue River from its confluence with Turkey Creek to its confluence with Raspberry Creek, as a planning baseline.
  3. Allow the west-lane dike on the old alignment to erode during natural floods without reconstruction, and riprap the riverside embankment of the new alignment.
- **Scope note:** The Incidental Take Statement under the ESA does not exempt take of listed migratory birds under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act.
