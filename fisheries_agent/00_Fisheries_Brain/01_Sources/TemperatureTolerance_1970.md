#ichthyology #marine-biology #Clariidae

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the lower lethal temperature tolerance of [[Clarias batrachus]] (Walking Catfish), an introduced exotic species established in Southeast Florida, USA. Conducted at Auburn University via laboratory trough experiments and pond trials, the research aimed to determine cold tolerance limits to estimate the species' potential range expansion within the United States. Findings indicate that rate of temperature decline and seasonal thermal history significantly influence survival outcomes, with rapid cooling being far more lethal than gradual cooling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range:** Southeast Asia (implied, as an introduced/exotic species in the study region).
- **Introduced Range:** Established populations documented in Southeast Florida, United States.
- **Habitat Type:** Freshwater ponds and still/slow-moving water bodies; capable of surviving in laboratory recirculating water systems.
- **Invasive Potential:** As an exotic species, its northward range expansion in the U.S. is constrained by its lower lethal [[Thermal Tolerance]] threshold, making cold winter temperatures a potential natural barrier to further establishment.
- **Pathogen Susceptibility:** Prolonged exposure to sub-optimal (though non-lethal) cold temperatures increases vulnerability to opportunistic infections, notably bacteria of the genus *[[Aeromonas]]* and fungal pathogens.

## Key Findings
- **Rapid Cooling Sensitivity:** *Clarias batrachus* experiences rapid mortality when water temperature decreases quickly (up to 2°C per hour) below **7.2°C**.
- **Gradual Cooling Tolerance:** When temperature was lowered gradually (5°C every 4 days), lethal temperatures ranged between **9.4°C and 12.8°C**.
- **Seasonal/Thermal History Effect:**
  - Fish tested in **July** died at a higher lethal temperature (**12.8°C**).
  - Fish tested in **October** survived to a lower lethal temperature (**9.4°C**).
  - This suggests that prior thermal acclimation (seasonal thermal history) affects an individual's cold tolerance threshold — a phenomenon relevant to [[Thermal Acclimation]] studies in ectotherms.
- **Chronic Sub-lethal Exposure:** Constant water temperatures below **18.3°C**, if sustained for longer than one month, resulted in death — not from direct thermal shock, but due to secondary infections (*Aeromonas* bacteria and fungus) facilitated by weakened immune resistance under thermal stress.
- **Management/Range Implications:** These lethal temperature thresholds provide a basis for predicting the northern limits of *Clarias batrachus* range expansion in the continental United States, informing invasive species monitoring and control efforts related to [[Exotic Species Introduction]].
