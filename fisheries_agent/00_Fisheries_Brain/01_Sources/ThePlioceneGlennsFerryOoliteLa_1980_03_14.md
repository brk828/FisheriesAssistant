# The Pliocene Glenns Ferry Oolite: Lake-Margin Carbonate Deposition — Fish Faunal Analog Discussion

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoichthyology #Catostomidae #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae #Cottidae #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary
This discussion paper is a scientific reply defending the interpretation of the Pliocene **[[Shoofly Oolite]]** (Glenns Ferry Formation, southwestern Snake River Plain) as a lake-margin carbonate deposit formed in **[[Lake Idaho]]**, a Miocene–Pliocene ensialic rift lake. Central to the argument is a paleoecological and paleolimnological comparison between the extinct **[[Lake Idaho]]** fish fauna and the extant fauna of **[[Pyramid Lake]]**, Nevada (a Basin and Range remnant of Pleistocene **[[Lake Lahontan]]**). The authors argue that shared genera — particularly the co-occurrence of **[[Chasmistes]]** (suckers) and **[[Archoplites]]** (sunfish) — found nowhere else, past or present, support Pyramid Lake as a valid limnological and water-chemistry analog for Lake Idaho, reinforcing a biogenic (algal-mediated) rather than purely physicochemical origin for the oolite crenulations. The paper also addresses ooid mineralogy and aragonite-to-calcite inversion fabrics, though these are geological rather than biological arguments.

## Background & Description
- **Ooid-algal association**: Crenulated ooid cortices from low-salinity Lake Idaho frequently preserve remains of cyanophytic (blue-green) algal filaments/tubes, often coating molluscan debris (e.g., **[[Goniobasis]]** gastropod shells) with "oncolitic" textures — contrasted with the purely inorganic "cerebroid ooids" of hypersaline **[[Great Salt Lake]]**.
- **Fish morphological/taxonomic notes**:
  - **[[Rhabdofario lacustris]]** (Lake Idaho) is identified as an extinct relative — probably the direct ancestor — of **[[Salmo]]** (cutthroat/rainbow trout lineage) found in Pyramid Lake today.
  - Sucker genus **[[Chasmistes]]** is represented by *Chasmistes spatulifer* (Lake Idaho, Shoofly Oolite Stage) versus *Chasmistes cujus* (Pyramid Lake) — the cui-ui.
  - Sunfish genus **[[Archoplites]]** occurs as *Archoplites taylori* (fossil, Lake Idaho) versus *Archoplites interruptus* (extant, Pyramid Lake) — the Sacramento perch.
  - Minnow (Cyprinidae) diversity in Lake Idaho includes several extinct genera without direct Pyramid Lake counterparts: *Ptychocheilus arciferus*, *Acrocheilus latus*, *Idadon hibbardi*, *Mylocheilus robustus*, *Orthodon hadrognathus* — versus extant Pyramid Lake cyprinids *Gila bicolor*, *Richardsonius egregius*, *Rhinichthys osculus*.

\* = artificially introduced species (not native, excluded from paleoecological comparison)

- **Habitat/tectonic setting**: Both **[[Pyramid Lake]]** and **[[Lake Idaho]]** formed in ensialic rift/block-faulted basins (Basin and Range Province vs. western Snake River Plain), with subsidence beginning in the Miocene and continuing through post-Miocene time.
- **Sedimentary facies parallel**: Both systems show basin-center diatomaceous ashy silts grading to nearshore algal carbonates and oolitic carbonates — supporting a genetic (not just taxonomic) lake-margin analogy.
- **Water chemistry inference**: Pyramid Lake is well-oxygenated to depth (~100 m), weakly stratified with a single annual overturn, bottom water slightly >4°C, pH ~9, and total dissolved solids ~4.7‰ today (3.5‰ pre-1906 before the Truckee Canal diversion). Because Lake Idaho supported *more* fish species than Pyramid Lake, the authors infer Lake Idaho was **not more saline nor more alkaline** than Pyramid Lake.
- **Comparative salinity tolerance control (Walker Lake)**: **[[Walker Lake]]**, Nevada — with nearly double the dissolved solids and greater alkalinity than Pyramid Lake — cannot support reproduction of some Pyramid Lake fish species, indicating those species are near their physiological salinity/alkalinity tolerance limits at Pyramid Lake's current chemistry. This calibrates the inferred upper bound for Lake Idaho's water chemistry.
- **Thermal niche differentiation**: **[[Prosopium]]** (whitefish) and **[[Cottus]]** (sculpins) occur in the broader Lahontan basin with lake access but are functionally absent from Pyramid Lake itself, suggesting Pyramid Lake water is too warm for these cold-adapted taxa — implying Lake Idaho (which supported both a whitefish, *Prosopium prolixus*, and a sculpin, *Kerocottus divaricatus*) was likely slightly **cooler** than Pyramid Lake.
- **Productivity inference**: The large size and abundance of fish in both lakes indicate comparably high nutrient levels and productivity, a trait the authors describe as unique among western U.S. Cenozoic lakes.

## Key Findings
1. **[[Chasmistes]]** and **[[Archoplites]]** co-occurrence is unique to Pyramid Lake and Lake Idaho among all known past or present lakes — the single strongest line of evidence for treating Pyramid Lake as a valid ecological/limnological analog for Lake Idaho.
2. Fish faunal similarity, combined with matching tectonic setting (ensialic rift basins) and sedimentary facies patterns (nearshore algal/oolitic carbonates vs. basin-center diatomites), supports the original interpretation that the **[[Shoofly Oolite]]** formed via lake-margin carbonate deposition rather than through an alternative (e.g., water-treatment-plant-analog or purely evaporative hypersaline) mechanism proposed by Sandberg.
3. Postulated water chemistry for Pliocene Lake Idaho (near-neutral to mildly alkaline, moderate dissolved solids, well-oxygenated, cooler than Pyramid Lake) is derived **from** the faunal comparison rather than being an independent a priori assumption — i.e., paleoichthyology is used as primary paleolimnological proxy evidence.
4. Preservation of cyanophytic algal remains within crenulated ooid cortices in Lake Idaho (absent in hypersaline Great Salt Lake ooids) supports a biogenic contribution to the Shoofly ooid crenulation, consistent with a low-salinity, biologically productive lake-margin environment rather than a purely abiotic hypersaline analog.
5. Petrographic argument (secondary to the biological data): aragonite-to-calcite inversion fabric retention scales inversely with original aragonite crystal size — fine tangential ooid-cortex aragonite (~0.3 μm diameter) is obliterated upon inversion, whereas coarser radial aragonite cements (1–2 μm diameter, hundreds of μm long) better preserve fabric as oriented calcite laths — used to argue that Shoofly ooid fabrics are consistent with inverted aragonite rather than primary calcite.
