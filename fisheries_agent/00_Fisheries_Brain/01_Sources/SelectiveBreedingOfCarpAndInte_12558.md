# Selective Breeding of Carp and Intensification of Fish Breeding in Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is an English translation (Israel Program for Scientific Translations, 1970) of Volume 61 of the Bulletin of the State Scientific Research Institute of Lake and River Fisheries (Leningrad, 1966). The volume compiles Soviet and allied work on [[Carp Selection]] and pond pisciculture. Part One (about 170 pages of articles) addresses genetics, breeding methods, [[Heterosis]], scale-cover genetics, hybridization with [[Eastern Carp]] and tench, and the organization of breeding farms in the northwestern RSFSR. Part Two addresses an artificial method for producing carp larvae, nutrition, stocking density, disease, pond productivity and pond culture in Belorussia and Western Siberia. The editors frame the volume as a corrective to decades of stagnation in carp genetics after 1948, when the [[Lysenko Doctrine]] suppressed fish genetics in the USSR.

## Background & Description

**Scope of the volume.** The preface notes that Soviet work on carp selection continued under difficult conditions in the Ukraine, Moscow Region, northwestern RSFSR and Belorussia. A Ukrainian breed was completed by A.I. Kuzema (UkrNIIRKh), and a northern hybrid breed, "Ropsha" carp, was developed by a team under V.S. Kirpichnikov (GosNIORKh).

**Structure.** The volume contains 18 papers in two parts, with a bibliography and list of abbreviations. Articles were translated by E. Hoz and M. Yariv and edited by A. Isseroff.

**Origins of carp culture.** The article "Goals and Methods in Carp Selection" reviews carp domestication, from the Danube wild carp ancestor in Europe (about 6–7 centuries of pisciculture) to Chinese culture (about 2000 years), where carp are secondary to grass and silver carp in polyculture.

**Breed groups (USSR).** Four genetic scale-cover groups are recognized: scaly, sparse-scale (mirror), linear and bare. Scale cover is governed by two non-linked factor pairs (Ss/Nn), with linear carp being eliminated under regulation. Ukrainian frame-scale and scaly carp and "Ropsha" northern hybrids are the principal named breeds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal and oxygen tolerance.** Physiological studies on northern carp, southern carp, eastern carp and hybrids found heat-shock temperatures of about 32.5–36°C depending on oxygen and group; oxygen thresholds were generally below 0.5 mg O2/l.
- **Cold tolerance.** Muscle excitability disappeared after 15–20 s (northern carp), 18–23 s (F1 C×K hybrid), 30–33 s (eastern carp) and 30–35 s (F1 K×C hybrid) at −1.5°C, indicating matrilinear inheritance of tissue cold tolerance.
- **Food competition and density.** Stocking density strongly influenced growth, variability and survival of larvae and fingerlings; plankton, benthos (midges and oligochaetes) and artificial feeds were the main food sources.
- **Scale reduction.** Scale loss progresses from the median line to the fin bases, interpreted as an adaptive pattern shaped by partial domestication, reduced mechanical contact, and selection.
- **Pond conditions in the northwest.** Water temperatures, overgrowth, peat beds, and waterborne parasites (Chilodonella, Dactylogyrus, Diplostomum, ichthyophthiriasis) strongly affected outcomes.

## Key Findings

**Selection theory (Kirpichnikov, "Goals and Methods").**
- Main goal: breeds with high food conversion and resistance to environmental stress and disease, to lift pond yields toward 10–12 centners/ha (planned 7–8 million centners by 1980).
- Heritability of carp body weight was reported as 0.20–0.30 (Kirpichnikov) and 0.09–0.18 (Moav and Wohlfarth).
- Illustrative calculation: mean 600 g, SD 90 g, selection intensity 0.1%, h²=0.1; after 10 generations about 25% growth gain, a gain the authors note has never been achieved historically (pond carp only 30–40% heavier than wild carp).
- Mass selection is most effective for hybrid groups; combined mass and family selection is recommended.
- Competitive "aggressiveness" can bias mass selection toward fast feeders; progeny testing in replicated, uniform conditions is needed.
- Inbreeding to high coefficients takes 40–50 years in temperate zones, so the paper advises parallel strains with periodic crossing rather than closed inbred lines.

**Progeny testing (Kirpichnikov, "Methods of Progeny Testing").**
- Males can be evaluated by larval and fingerling growth; females show a strong maternal effect (egg size correlated with embryo survival, r = +0.78 in one test) that persists until larvae reach about 200–300 mg.
- Large females produced better progeny (correlation above +0.6 in 1960 and 1961 tests).
- Heritability of weight in "Ropsha" carp was estimated at 0.3–0.5, higher than the expected 0.1–0.2.
- Heterozygous spawners for scale factor S grew somewhat faster (stated as probable, not statistically proven).

**Heterosis (Andriyasheva).**
- First-generation carp × eastern carp hybrids showed heterosis in growth and survival, most evident in early development and under cold, starvation and low-calcium conditions.
- Crossing 3rd-generation strains produced smaller heterosis; the author concludes large-scale four-way crossing is promising for northern commercial carp breeding.

**Scale cover and genetics (Shpet; Kirpichnikov).**
- Study of 708 reduced-scale carp showed a consistent pattern of scale loss, and the author argues that this is adaptive; no completely scaleless carp were found among several thousand "bare" individuals.
- Sparse-scale and scaly carp are the only purebreeding groups; linear and bare crosses produce segregating and less fertile progeny.
- Radiation-induced diploid gynogenesis yielded up to 6% developing eggs, offering a route to homozygosity.

**Heritability of morphological traits (Nenashev).** Heritability estimates (analysis of variance, both sexes) were 0.634 for dorsal-fin branched rays, 0.648 for total vertebrae, 0.578 for trunk vertebrae, and 0.371 for lateral-line scales (lowest). Regression estimates were unreliable with 11 families.

**Carp × tench hybrids (Viktorovskii).** Viable hybrids were produced from Ropsha carp females and tench males (581 of 1,000 eggs hatched; 92 hybrids recovered from a 400 m² pond). Hybrids were generally close to carp, with intermediate pharyngeal tooth and head-length traits; hybrid mean weight reached 46.4 g in a 20-fish sample.

**Organization of breeding in the northwestern RSFSR (Kirpichnikov; Zonova and Shatrova; Petrov and Romanova).**
- A three-tier system is proposed: a few selective breeding farms, breeding farms that supply spawners, and commercial farms that do not keep spawners.
- The breeding farms "Yazhelbitsy" (Novgorod) and "Opochka" (Pskov) received Ropsha spawners; by 1964 Opochka had 987 backcross 2+ fish and 843 backcross 4+ fish (including about 400 mature males), and 433 four-way 3+ fish.
- Spawner stock was sold to commercial farms in mixed-strain nests, with the aim of replacing all commercial stocks by 1966–1967. Lack of ponds, trained selective breeders and management attention were named as the main obstacles.
- Hybrid fecundity was about 120,000–150,000 eggs per kg of female weight; recovered larvae averaged 90,000–100,000 per female, versus 70,000–90,000 for local mongrels.
- Hybrid underyearlings grew about 15–25% faster than carp (Pskov tests), survived summer and winter better (for example, 3% versus 65% mortality in one yearling pond), and ate more benthos, while carp relied more on artificial feed.

**Artificial larval production (Konradt and Sakharov; Dmitrenko).**
- Enzymatic removal of the mucous envelope with hyaluronidase (10% pig-testicle extract, or acetone-treated powder) reduced cleaning time to 20–60 minutes (versus 5–7 hours for the earlier Voinarovich method).
- In 1963 about 970,000 carp eggs were cleaned, yielding 600,000 larvae (63.4% of incubated eggs); incubation mortality at Ropsha was 33.2%.
- The method was proposed to protect young from parasites and spawner contact, and to support selective breeding through controlled crosses.

**Nutrition and density (Kryazheva; Sergeeva).**
- Higher stocking density reduced growth sharply and increased weight variability; at highest densities mortality exceeded 90% in several aquarium series, with chilodonellosis epidemics.
- A small group of exceptional fish grew rapidly at high density, suggesting intraspecific competition raises the natural-selection gradient.
- Conversion ratios of about 1–2 were recorded for larvae of 25–100 mg, low enough that the authors suggest additional nutrient sources (phosphorus, calcium, or wall growths).

**Pond productivity and disease.** Papers by Lyakhnovich (Belorussia), Zlokazov and Ignat'ev (Western Siberia), and the dropsy and coccidiosis studies (Konradt and Faktorovich; Zmerzlaya) describe pond classification by biological productivity, state of carp culture in Siberia, and disease control. Data from the Siberian article were not included in the translation's abstract.

**Overall conclusion.** The editors and authors conclude that carp selection has been neglected, that breeding should be organized on a division-of-labor basis, and that pond culture in the north can benefit from hybrid carp with higher winter tolerance and faster growth.
