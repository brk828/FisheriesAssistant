# A Guide for the Identification of British Aquatic Oligochaeta

#ichthyology #marine-biology #oligochaeta #tubificidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1963 identification guide (Freshwater Biological Association Scientific Publication No. 22) by [[Brinkhurst]] for all truly aquatic oligochaete worms recorded from Britain. It provides a [[Key To Families]], then species keys for the [[Aeolosomatidae]], [[Naididae]] and [[Tubificidae]], plus brief descriptions for the remaining families. Identification relies on microscopic study of chaetae (bristles) in prepared whole mounts. The guide also notes that the group has long been neglected by ecologists, so distribution data are sparse. Its foreword asks for faunistic records to be sent to the Ferry House or to the author, with maps promised for a future edition.

Note: this is a taxonomic reference rather than a fish study. Its subject is the aquatic oligochaete fauna, which is ecologically relevant to fish habitat and water quality.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and scope.** The guide addresses a long-standing difficulty in freshwater faunistic surveys. Tubificidae are recognised as important indicators in streams and rivers affected by organic pollution. Recent systematic reviews of the Naididae (Sperber 1948, 1950), Enchytraeidae (Nielsen & Christensen 1959) and Tubificidae (Brinkhurst 1963a) made it possible to compile new keys. A Russian work by Chekanovskaya (1962) covering USSR species is cited as a valuable supplement.

**Coverage.**
- Full keys: Aeolosomatidae, Naididae and Tubificidae, including all known British species.
- Enchytraeidae: referred to Nielsen & Christensen (1959), since most species are not aquatic.
- Lumbriculidae: brief species descriptions, because the British members are few and poorly known.
- Glossoscolecidae, Haplotaxidae, Lumbricidae and Branchiobdellidae: one aquatic species described in each case, or none for Branchiobdellidae.

**Preparation.**
- Worms are preserved in 70% alcohol. *Branchiura sowerbyi* is narcotised in magnesium chloride first, because it fragments easily. It is recognisable in the field.
- For identification, specimens are cleared in [[Amman's Lactophenol]] (or polyvinyl lactophenol for permanent slides). Gentle coverslip pressure helps reveal features.
- Fine chaetal details may need an oil immersion lens.

**Anatomical features.**
- Segment I is devoid of chaetae. Other segments carry four bundles: dorsal and ventral pairs.
- Chaetae types include hair chaetae, bifid crotchets, pectinate chaetae, genital chaetae, and simple-pointed or broad chaetae.
- Tubificid genera are defined mainly by male efferent ducts, but the key proceeds to species using chaetae alone.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Knowledge gaps.** The author states that these worms have been "virtually ignored by ecologists." Field data are limited. Most information on Tubificidae comes from the author's own collections. Aeolosomatidae have almost no field information. Naididae ecology is mostly restricted to being freshwater or brackish.
- **Distribution terms.** "Common," "rare" and "local" refer to the number of localities. "Abundant," "frequent" and "scarce" refer to numbers within localities. Single records do not imply restricted range. Many species are considered cosmopolitan.
- **Habitats noted.**
  - [[Chaetogaster Limnaei]] is commensal in the mantle cavity and kidney of gastropod snails.
  - Several Naididae and Tubificidae are tube-dwellers (e.g., *Dero* spp., *Aulophorus furcatus*, *Ripistes parasita*).
  - [[Stylodrilus Heringianus]] lives in sand and gravel of stony streams, rivers and oligotrophic lakes.
  - [[Sparganophilus Tamesis]] occurs in black mud among roots of *Sparganium* and *Phragmites*.
  - [[Haplotaxis Gordioides]] is a cold-water stenothermous species from stony riffles.
  - [[Lamprodrilus Mrazeki]] is found in ponds among leaves of *Salix* and *Quercus*, and encysts from June to October.
- **Pollution.** [[Tubifex Tubifex]] and [[Limnodrilus Hoffmeisteri]] are common in organically polluted waters and may reach very large numbers where leeches are absent.
- **Brackish and marine species.** Several Tubificidae are brackish or marine littoral, including *Tubifex costatus* (local and abundant in brackish water) and *Aktedrilus monospermathecus*, known in Britain only from Hale (Lancashire).

## Key Findings

**Scope of the British fauna**
- Enchytraeidae: about forty species recorded from Britain, most not strictly aquatic.
- Lumbriculidae: only two firmly established British species, [[Lumbriculus Variegatus]] (common) and [[Stylodrilus Heringianus]] (common). A new *Stylodrilus* from Llyn Tegid (Bala Lake) was described for future publication.
- Branchiobdellidae: no certain British record. Parasites of crayfish, with chaetae absent and toothed jaws.
- Glossoscolecidae: [[Sparganophilus Tamesis]] recorded from the Thames at Goring and Windermere. *S. elongatus* is retained as a species dubium.
- Haplotaxidae: [[Haplotaxis Gordioides]], up to 300 mm long, recorded from the Severn at Montford Bridge, the R. Alun, and Three Bridges (Sussex).
- Lumbricidae: [[Eiseniella Tetraedra]] is the only earthworm commonly found in freshwater, recognised by its square posterior cross-section.

**Aeolosomatidae (6 species keyed)**
- [[Aeolosoma Tenebrarum]] (5–10 mm; yellow-olive green droplets) and [[Aeolosoma Beddardi]] (1–2.5 mm; colourless droplets) are the commonest key entries.
- Others keyed: *A. hemprichi*, *A. quaternarium*, *A. variegatum*, *A. headleyi*.
- The droplets are described as skin-gland secretions, not oils (Marcus 1944).

**Naididae**
- Keyed species include *Chaetogaster* (5 species, e.g., *C. limnaei*, *C. diaphanus*), *Pristina* (several), *Dero* (*D. digitata*, *D. obtusa*), *Nais* (about 9 species), and *Stylaria lacustris*, common in lakes.
- Noted Britain records: [[Nais Elinguis]] is common and abundant in polluted water. [[Ophidonais Serpentina]] is recorded from several sites, including Oxwich Marsh and the Shropshire Union Canal.

**Tubificidae**
- [[Branchiura Sowerbyi]] is found in warm-water tanks and warm effluents (e.g., R. Thames at Reading), and occasionally in normal-temperature rivers.
- [[Peloscolex Ferox]] is scarce in rivers and more abundant in lakes.
- [[Aulodrilus Pluriseta]] is common in lakes, rivers, canals and ponds, sometimes abundant.
- [[Psammoryctes Barbatus]] is common and frequent in rivers and ponds, occasionally where polluted.
- [[Tubifex Tubifex]] is common and abundant everywhere, especially in organically polluted waters.
- [[Rhyacodrilus Coccineus]] is common and abundant, often in sandy river reaches.
- [[Limnodrilus Hoffmeisteri]] is abundant in fresh, rarely brackish, water and is often found with *T. tubifex* in polluted water.
- Newly described or named forms: *Tubifex templetoni* var. *walshi* (three specimens from Rostherne Mere, Cheshire).

**Identification notes**
- Lumbriculids: *Stylodrilus parvus* is probably immature *S. heringianus*.
- *Nais pardalis* and *N. variabilis* are hard to separate without thickened chaetae. Hair chaetae length differs (100–200 µm vs 300–500 µm).
- *Tubifex templetoni* forms have tub-shaped or elongate penis sheaths that aid separation.

**Future work and conclusions**
- Distribution maps were not included because records are inadequate. The author will welcome specimens and records.
- Several European species not yet recorded in Britain are keyed in parentheses so that new British finds can be named.
