# Bonytail Chub Collection at Lake Mohave, May 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum, dated 17 May 1993 and addressed to the [[Parker Fish and Wildlife Office]] project leader, reports a netting effort for [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) in [[Lake Mohave]] from 10 to 13 May 1993. Fifteen bonytail were taken. Fourteen were judged wild and were transported to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. One fin-clipped fish was pit-tagged and released. Several other species were also captured, including fewer than 20 [[Razorback Sucker]]. The memo concludes that the captured bonytail were likely spawning in the netting area.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** The memo documents a cooperative netting operation and the disposition of captured bonytail chub.
- **Participating agencies:** The [[Arizona Fishery Resource Office]] (AZFRO, Parker), the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR), and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD). Named participants were Paul Marsh (AZFRO), Tom Burke, Mike Horn (USBR), and Kirk Young (AGFD).
- **Author:** A fishery biologist from the Parker FRO, who assisted with the netting.
- **Study species:** Bonytail chub, a large cyprinid. The wild fish captured measured about 450–480 mm.
- **Recipients at the hatchery:** Lyle Miller and Glen Green received the fish at Willow Beach.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Lake Mohave is a reservoir on the Colorado River. The netting took place in the lake.
- **Reproductive condition:** Captured bonytail showed signs of spawning activity. Males had tubercles, and some displayed red coloration at the bases of their paired fins. One female was distended and gravid, and the other was flaccid and spent. The author presumed that the fish were spawning in the netting area.
- **Bycatch:** Other species taken included fewer than 20 [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Largemouth Bass]]. Untagged razorback suckers were pit-tagged and released.
- **Handling and survival:** Wild bonytail were transferred to the hatchery within three hours of capture. They were tempered briefly and placed in a covered raceway in the hatchery building. The author judged all fish to be in good shape, as they were active after removal from the nets.

## Key Findings

- **Total catch:** 15 bonytail chub were taken at Lake Mohave between 10 and 13 May 1993.
- **Wild status:** 14 fish were classified as wild based on large size (about 450–480 mm) and were moved to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery.
- **Tagged fish:** One bonytail with a pectoral fin clip was pit-tagged and released.
- **Sex and maturity:** Several fish were tuberculate males. Of the females, one was gravid (distended) and one was spent (flaccid).
- **Spawning evidence:** Red coloration at the fin bases of males, together with gravid and spent females, indicated that spawning was likely occurring in the netting area.
- **Fish condition:** All fish were active after capture and considered to be in good shape.
- **Bycatch and tagging:** Fewer than 20 razorback suckers and several other fish species were captured. Untagged razorback suckers were pit-tagged and released.
- **Management implication:** The capture of spawning wild bonytail at Lake Mohave, moved to a hatchery for holding, supports ongoing recovery efforts for this species. (This interpretation is the note's inference; the memo itself does not state a management plan.)
