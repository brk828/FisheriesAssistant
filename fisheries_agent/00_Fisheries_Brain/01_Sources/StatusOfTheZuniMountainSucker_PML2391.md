# Status of the Zuni Mountain Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 report, prepared by G. R. Smith and R. K. Koehn with assistance from Paul C. Marsh, John Hall, Ellen Dawley and Robert Dawley for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Division of Ecological Services, evaluates the taxonomic status, distribution and population stability of the Zuni Mountain Sucker (*Catostomus discobolus yarrowi*). The range of this extreme form lies in an area that could be inundated by a proposed dam on Nutria Creek and Rio Pescado, McKinley County, New Mexico. The authors conclude that the subspecies is a stable, true-breeding hybrid-derived population of intermediate character between *C. discobolus* and *C. plebeius*, originating from a stream capture near the head of Nutria Creek, and recommend it be treated as a distinct subspecies of conservation significance.

## Background & Description

- **Historical taxonomy:** Cope's original type description (specimens with nine dorsal rays and a sharp cartilaginous edge of the commissure) was assigned to the Zuni River, New Mexico, a locality later corrected from Provo, Utah. The type specimens (USNM 15793) were collected by H. W. Henshaw in 1873.
- **Rediscovery:** The unusual populations were redetected in the Rio Pescado in 1949 by W. J. Koster, who also collected specimens from Nutria Creek in 1960.
- **Prior work:** [[Smith (1963, 1965)]] found the New Mexico populations resembled *C. discobolus* in gill raker counts but resembled *C. plebeius* in vertebrae, dorsal rays, jaw size and pigmentation.
- **Diagnosis:** *C. d. yarrowi* is distinguished from [[Creek Chub Sucker|*C. plebeius*]] by usually 25 or more first-arch gill rakers, 35 or more back-row rakers, more than 50 predorsal scales and more than 40 post-Weberian vertebrae. Versus other *C. discobolus*, it usually has fewer than 42 vertebrae and a caudal peduncle depth usually above 0.08 of standard length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Threat:** The range in Nutria Creek and Rio Pescado could be flooded by a dam. Alternative sites (Coal Mine Dam and Burned Dam) were also considered.
- **Habitat:** Most fish were found in late-summer pools of small, permanent, high-gradient streams, usually with hard substrate (larger than sand) present or nearby. Riffles are likely required for spawning, probably in spring.
- **Sampling survey:** Surveys covered tributaries of the [[Little Colorado River]] drainage, including the Little Colorado in Navajo, Apache and Coconino counties (Arizona), Kinnikinnick Creek, East Clear Creek, and Nutria Creek and Rio Pescado in McKinley and Valencia counties (New Mexico).
- **Diet:** Stomach analysis of 54 individuals taken in October showed sparse particulate organic matter, detritus and diatoms, abundant filamentous algae, and numerous small midge larvae, caddisfly and mayfly larvae, flatworms, and occasional terrestrial insects, fish scales, gastropods and insect eggs.
- **Growth:** Growth appeared good in Rio Pescado, intermediate in Nutria Creek and poor in Radosevich Creek. Scale-annulus uncertainty prevented firm age and growth conclusions.

## Key Findings

1. **Morphometrics and meristics (247 specimens):** Principal component analysis showed Component I explained 56% of variance and Component II an additional 18%. Nutria Creek was most similar to *C. plebeius*; Kin-li-Chee was most similar to *C. discobolus*; Rio Pescado was relatively distinct but intermediate.
2. **Clinal variation:** Plebeius-like characters decline progressively downstream, correlated with distance from Nutria Creek. Kin-li-Chee Creek, about 250 miles by water from Nutria Creek, shows only a trace of introgression.
3. **Biochemistry:** Of about 35 loci surveyed, 18 were polymorphic. Three loci provided exclusive evidence linking *yarrowi* to *discobolus*. Five loci showed hybrid influence from *plebeius* concentrated in Nutria Creek. Four loci were equivocal, and four showed local differentiation unrelated to the stream capture history.
4. **Conclusions:** The population is intermediate between *discobolus* and *plebeius*, appears stable and true-breeding, and likely arose by introgression following stream capture. Nutria Creek is the probable site of capture and initial hybridization.
5. **Dam impact:** Any dam should maintain a permanent discharge of at least 1 cfs, with normal seasonal water temperature and dissolved oxygen, to protect populations. Introduced flowing surface water and protection from exotic competitors are recommended.
6. **Conservation significance:** Although a product of hybridization, the taxon is scientifically unique and reflects several thousand years of natural hybridization experiments; the authors recommend protection and cautious management.
