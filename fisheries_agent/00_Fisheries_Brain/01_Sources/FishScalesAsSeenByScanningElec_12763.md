# Fish Scales as Seen by Scanning Electron Microscopy

#ichthyology #marine-biology #scale-morphology

## Executive Summary

This 1974 paper by [[Edward D. DeLamater]] and [[Walter R. Courtenay Jr.]], published in the *Florida Scientist*, uses the [[Scanning Electron Microscope]] (SEM) to characterize fish scale structure for taxonomic use. It describes circuli (ridges), the dentition on circuli crests, the [[Ctenoid Scales|cteni]] and the adjacent subctenoid area, with particular attention to lateral-line scales. The authors argue that SEM reveals scale features beyond the reach of the light microscope and may support identification, especially within the difficult genus [[Tilapia]]. The study examines nine species and is presented as a demonstration rather than a full revision.

## Background & Description

Scale sculpture and shape have been used inconsistently in fish taxonomy, while scale counts and distribution have received greater weight (Goodrich, 1908; Norman, 1951; Van Oosten, 1957; Lagler et al., 1962). Earlier detailed imaging by Cockerell (1912), Chu (1935), Kobayashi (1951–1955) and McCully (1961) was limited by the resolving power and shallow depth of field of the light microscope. The SEM is presented as overcoming these limits.

**Methods overview:**
- Two SEM systems (ISI Mini-Sem and AMR 1000) were used, alongside a Reichert photonic microscope and a B&L research microscope with camera systems.
- SEM specimens were cleaned, bleached in 10% H₂O₂, cleared in borax-trypsin solution, sonicated, dehydrated, mounted on metal pegs with aluminum paint contacts, and gold or gold-rhodium coated.
- Light microscopy specimens were cleared and stained with Alizarin Red S, then mounted in glycerin.

**Species examined (nine):** [[Tilapia melanotheron]], [[Tilapia aurea]], [[Kyphosus sectatrix]], [[Synodus foetens]], [[Haemulon plumieri]], [[Pomacentrus leucostictus]], [[Equetus acuminatus]], [[Diplectrum formosum]] and [[Myripristis jacobus]].

**Scale terminology:** The authors divide a lateral-line scale into four quadrants (dorsoanterior, dorsoposterior, ventroanterior, ventroposterior) using anteroposterior and dorsoventral axes. The focus is the initial formation point of the scale, and the lateral-line canal lies along the A-P axis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper is a laboratory morphology study and does not report habitat, depth, ocean basin or population data for the species. The geographic remarks are limited to the taxonomic context of [[Tilapia]]: *T. aurea* occurs in Israel and surrounding countries, while its counterpart *T. nilotica* occurs in Africa. The authors note that some 200+ Tilapia species have been described, largely on overlapping meristic values and geographic distribution, and that unidentified specimens cannot be assigned without knowing their origin. The paper also notes that Haemulon populations in widely separated areas may be distinguishable by scale patterns, and that scale morphology differs between the eyed and blind sides of [[Flatfishes]].

## Key Findings

1. **Circuli patterns:** Circuli form in the outer calcified layer and resemble fingerprint ridges, with insertions, bifurcations and endings. In *T. melanotheron*, a concentrated band of insertions and bifurcations lies just dorsal to the most dorsal radius. Circuli between radii show few such changes.
2. **Pseudotubercles and tubercles:** In *T. melanotheron*, widely spaced circuli appear beneath epithelium as "pseudotubercles." In *T. aurea*, two true tuberculate knobs occur near the focus, possibly representing early circuli.
3. **Crest dentition:** Teeth on circuli crests differ among taxa. *T. melanotheron* has pointed teeth apparently set in sockets. *T. aurea* has conical, somewhat recurved teeth without sockets. *Kyphosus sectatrix* has irregular, blunted, conical teeth. *Synodus foetens* has widely spaced, highly irregular blunted teeth. The authors suggest these features may characterize genera.
4. **Cteni and mosaics:** Cteni are resorbed to form species-specific mosaics. *Haemulon plumieri* shows matching cteni on lateral-line and body scales. *Kyphosus sectatrix* has tangential cteni, *Equetus acuminatus* has cteni parallel to the scale plate producing a "brick wall" pattern, *Diplectrum formosum* has two rows of newest cteni, and *Myripristis jacobus* has a single row with growth by layer addition and no resorption.
5. **Taxonomic potential:** The authors suggest SEM scale analysis may separate Tilapia species into groups, distinguish geographic populations of Haemulon, and separate Holocentridae genera (though not species, which are defined by other means). They also suggest it may help define relationships among flatfishes.
6. **Limitations:** The paper states that extensive analysis of scale form without body-position reference is not diagnostically significant. Lateral-line scales are generally more consistent than body scales and carry additional canal-based features. The authors contest McCully (1961) on this point.
7. **Conclusion:** The authors describe the research as a limited demonstration. They conclude that thorough SEM characterization could supplement optical methods as a useful identification system and note that the field has been relatively under-studied.
