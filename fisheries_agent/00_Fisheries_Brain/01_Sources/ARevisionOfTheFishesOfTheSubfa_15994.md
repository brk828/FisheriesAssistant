# A Revision of the Fishes of the Subfamily Argentininae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #argentinidae

## Executive Summary

Cohen (1958), published in the [[Bulletin of the Florida State Museum]], revises the subfamily Argentininae, a worldwide group of argentinid fishes whose adults are restricted to continental shelf edges and slopes. The revision recognizes two genera: [[Argentina]] with seven species and subspecies, and [[Glossanodon]] with five species. The paper presents one new subgenus, two new species, and one new subspecies, and offers comments on habits, distribution, the swimbladder, and the so-called spiral valve. It concludes that the presence of a spiral valve cannot be used to define the subfamily or any other argentinoid group.

## Background & Description

The Argentininae were formerly placed in *Argentina*. They differ from other argentinoids, which are bathypelagic, in living close to the bottom on the shelf and slope. Key diagnostic traits include supratemporals fused to the pterotics, a mesocoracoid, pectoral fins inserted on the ventrolateral body contours, adipose eyelids, and large scales that mold the body into an angular, polygonal box of 6 to 10 sides. Measurements followed standardized methods (standard length, preanal, prepectoral and head lengths, among others), and counts used modal values with ranges.

The author treats the taxonomic history in detail, tracing the genus from [[Rondelet]]'s 1554 figure through [[Linnaeus]]'s shifting concepts of *Argentina sphyraena* and the confusion caused by pike and anchovy records. Synonymies for the main species run to many pages and are summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat and depth:** *A. sphyraena* is generally taken in trawls on or near the bottom, commonly 90 to 200 m off Bergen, and prefers the coastal shelf. *A. silus* is probably the deepest-living member of the genus, mostly between 75 and 200 fathoms, with a maximum of 320 fathoms. *A. sialis* ranges from 11 to 250 m, most often 50 to 80 m. *A. striata* is most often taken at 100 to 200 fathoms over mud. *G. pygmaeus* ranges from 52 to 163 fathoms.
- **Diet:** *A. sphyraena* feeds mainly on bottom mollusks and crustaceans, with some pelagic prey such as the tunicate *Pyrosoma elegans* and *Clupea pilchardus*. *A. silus* eats shrimplike crustaceans and was found with *Cyclothone braueri*. In New Zealand, *A. elongata elongata* feeds on crabs, isopods and worms, and is preyed upon by red cod and groper.
- **Life history:** Larvae and postlarvae are pelagic. Schmidt (1906, 1918) placed *A. sphyraena* spawning in winter in the Mediterranean and later in northern populations. Fridriksson (1937) reported that *A. silus* off Iceland matures at 8 to 15 years, with spawning probably in April and May.
- **Distribution:** The group is worldwide along continental margins, although no species have been recorded from the west coast of South America, the southern coasts of Asia, or Antarctica. Fossil otoliths attributed to *Argentina* occur from the lower Tertiary of England to the Pliocene of Italy.
- **Population behavior:** Several catches show aggregation. Firth (1931) recorded a 15,000 lb catch on Georges Bank, and Wilson reported 1,000 individuals south of Nova Scotia.

## Key Findings

- **Generic revision:** The subfamily is split into *Argentina* (diagnosed by maxillaries separated medially by at least half the width of the mesethmoid, and continuous palatine/vomerine dentition) and *Glossanodon* (maxillaries touching or nearly so, with vomerine teeth set back and tongue teeth at the front only).
- **Spiral valve:** Cohen found Ringfalten, not a true spiral valve, in *A. sphyraena* and *A. silus*, and concluded that "spiral valve" is not a usable character for defining the subfamily or any argentinoid group. The term "vestigial spiral valve" is advised for use with great caution.
- **Swimbladder:** The bladder is unconnected to the gut and lacks a rete, which places the Argentininae with Stomiatoidei-like isospondyls and agrees with Salmonidae, Cyprinidae and the osmerid *Spirinchus starksi*. Silvery pigment is present in *A. sphyraena*, *A. silus*, *A. striata* and *A. sialis* in some form (absent in *A. striata*) and treated as genetically controlled, though used only alongside other characters.
- **Geographic variation:** In *A. sphyraena*, northern North Atlantic specimens show higher anal counts and smaller head and snout measures than Mediterranean specimens. Schmidt (1918) recorded higher vertebral numbers north of Scotland. *A. silus* populations on each side of the Atlantic are nearly identical and not recognized as subspecies.
- **Nomenclature resolved:** *Goniosoma argentinum*, *Argentina cuvieri* and *A. yarrelli* are nomina nova for *A. sphyraena*. *A. silus junior*, *Osmerus hebredicus* and *A. hebredica* were lumped with *A. sphyraena* by Collett (1875) and Day (1881). *Argentina syrtensium* is a synonym of *A. silus*. *Salmo immaculatus* Linnaeus is judged not conspecific with *A. silus*.
- **New taxa:** *Prosoarchus* (new subgenus of *Glossanodon*, distinguished by an anus at least as far forward as the depth of the caudal peduncle, 12 to 14 pectoral rays, 43 to 46 lateral line scales, and no adult dentary teeth); *Glossanodon (Glossanodon) polli* (new species from West African and Brazilian waters); *Glossanodon (Prosoarchus) pygmaeus* (new species from the northern Gulf of Mexico, Caribbean and Brazil); *Argentina elongata australiae* (new subspecies from Australia, with 13 pectoral rays and 5.4% maxillary length).
- **Subspecies of *A. elongata*:** Three subspecies are recognized: *A. e. elongata* (New Zealand), *A. e. australiae* (southeastern Australia), and *A. e. kagoshimae* (Japan), separated by gill raker counts, pectoral rays and maxillary length.
- **Specimen base:** The revision is based on dissections and counts from many museum collections. Examples include 73 specimens for *A. sphyraena*, 59 for *A. silus*, 50 for *A. sialis*, and 42 for *A. striata*.
- **Glossanodon subgenera:** *Glossanodon* (anus close to the anal fin base, 48 to 56 lateral line scales, 18 to 22 pectoral rays) is separated from *Prosoarchus* as described above.
- **Distribution gaps:** *A. sphyraena* records from the Indian Ocean (Gilchrist and von Bonde, 1924) were not accepted; any argentinid found there would likely be a *Glossanodon*.
