# Ultrasonic Tracking of Striped Bass Movements in Lake Mohave, Arizona-Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Moronidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 study, prepared by W.L. Pelle and Larry J. Paulson of the University of Nevada, Las Vegas for the [[Nevada Department Of Wildlife]], used [[Ultrasonic Telemetry]] to test whether [[Striped Bass]] movements and habitat use in [[Lake Mohave]] are influenced by [[Trout Stocking]] of [[Rainbow Trout]]. Anglers and managers were concerned that trophy stripers were preying heavily on stocked trout, and the authors note that predation is difficult to measure directly. Seventeen tagged fish were released near [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] on November 16, 1992 and tracked for 172 days. Mortality and tag loss were high, leaving four fish alive at the end of the study. The striped bass did not show the seasonal migrations reported elsewhere. They were, however, detected in [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] stocking areas more often than expected, though this preference was not related to stocking frequency. Fish were never found following the stocking barge.

## Background & Description

**Striped Bass (*Morone saxatilis*)** were first discovered in Lake Mohave in 1981. They were never stocked there and are thought to have entered from [[Lake Mead]] as unidentified life stages, passing through intake towers and/or over the spillway. Their share of the total catch has increased since the early 1980s, and large individuals have been caught in the river reach between [[Hoover Dam]] and Eldorado Canyon. The lake is not actively managed for this fishery.

**Rainbow Trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)** have been stocked below Hoover Dam since 1935. There is no natural reproduction. Lake Mohave was once known for its trophy trout fishery, and the Arizona state record of 9.2 kg was caught near Willow Beach in 1966. Trout catch rates declined after 1980, with estimates of 42% (Bushman 1991) and 55% (Burrell 1991). Striped bass predation was proposed as one cause, and a similar predation-linked crash had been suspected in Lake Mead.

**Stocking program:** The USFWS stocks seven regular locations by barge, and the [[Nevada Department Of Wildlife]] (NDOW) stocks four locations by truck. The [[Black Canyon]] section, including Willow Beach, is stocked year-round because of constant, optimal low water temperatures. Other areas are stocked January to May and November to December.

**Study objective:** To determine the extent to which striped bass movements and habitat preference, monitored by telemetry, are influenced by trout stocking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area:** Lake Mohave is the third mainstream impoundment on the [[Colorado River]], about 108 km long, formed by the closing of [[Davis Dam]] in 1950. It consists of two hydrologically distinct systems:

- **Black Canyon (upper 32 km):** Cold water (11.0-12.5°C) and relatively fast-moving, with a maximum velocity of 2.1 m/s depending on releases from Hoover Dam. Pool and backwater habitat is limited, especially in the 18 km reach above Willow Beach.
- **Lake Mohave proper (from El Dorado Canyon, river mile 43):** Slow-moving, with several large basins up to 6.4 km wide. Current velocity at mile 24.5 was reported at a fairly constant 0.5 m/s.

**Water quality (from earlier studies):** Dissolved oxygen was generally high (about 7 mg/l). It dropped below 4 mg/l only in late summer near the bottom above Davis Dam and never fell below 3 mg/l. The maximum surface temperature was 29°C in Little Basin, but hypolimnetic temperatures never exceeded 17°C. The entire lake becomes isothermal in winter. The authors concluded that no part of the lake was thermally or oxygen-limiting for striped bass during the study period.

## Key Findings

### Methods

- **Capture and handling:** Fish were caught by local guide Rick Francis and other anglers within 1 km of the hatchery. They were held for up to a week in raceways.
- **Transmitters:** Sonotronics CT-8 2-2 transmitters were surgically implanted mid-ventrally by a veterinarian, with a 3 cm incision closed by a surgical skin stapler. Surgery took about 5 minutes per fish.
- **Release:** On November 16, 1992, 17 tagged fish were released at six locations within 1 km of Willow Beach.
- **Tracking:** Fish were tracked daily for the first week, then weekly until May 7, 1993 (172 days, 25 weeks), using a Sonotronics USR-4D receiver and omni- and uni-directional hydrophones. Tracking intervals were about 0.75 km. Detections were recorded to the nearest 0.25 river mile.
- **Movement index:** Minimum speed between sightings, following Farquhar and Gutreuter (1989).
- **Stocking areas:** Each "stocking area" was a 1.25 km reach centered on the recorded stocking river mile. Records were only kept to the nearest mile, and USFWS spreads trout over a longer area than NDOW.
- **Statistics:** [[Chi-Square Test]] goodness-of-fit for detection proportions and [[Spearman Rank Correlation]] for stocking frequency and performance measures.

### Mortality and Tag Loss

- Mortality was estimated at about 41% and lost tags at about 20% during the first three weeks. The source text is an OCR scan, so these values should be checked against the original.
- By January 12, 1993, only four fish were considered alive and carrying transmitters.
- The authors considered tag shedding, sensor failure under cover, and unreported captures as possible explanations for missing fish.

### Movement Patterns

- All fish moved downstream rapidly in the first two days after release. Net movement is recorded as negative for downstream movement.
- Average movement between sessions dropped below 1 km/day through winter, with a slight increase in early spring. Both movement indices varied widely.
- Of the four surviving fish:
  - **#258 and #456** stayed between river mile 25 (Arizona Bay) and mile 35 (Aztec Wash) in winter. In spring they moved upstream. #456 traveled at least 37 miles in a month to river mile 63, below Hoover Dam.
  - **#276 and #97** stayed near Willow Beach, directly in front of the hatchery outflow at river mile 52.75, within 3 miles all winter. #276 also reached the Hoover Dam area by May 7. #97 moved downstream to river mile 26.75 (Castle Cliff Light) in spring.
- The movement index (average distance per day divided by longevity) ranged from 0.5 to 31.8 (as reported in the source).

### Habitat Preference

- USFWS regular stocking areas cover about 22% of the river/lake, NDOW areas about 13%, and about 5% overlap (as OCR-read).
- Pooled across 115 total detections, 41% were in USFWS regular stocking areas. This was significantly higher than expected (X² = 28.96, p < .005, df = 1).
- Detections in NDOW areas matched the expected 13%.
- USFWS areas stocked within three days prior held 27% of detections, not significantly above expectation (X² = 1.64, p > .05).
- Only 6% of detections were in recently stocked NDOW areas, significantly below expectation (X² = 4.91, 0.025 < p < 0.05).
- About 55% of USFWS-area detections were near Willow Beach Hatchery. That area held 23% of all detections, mostly from fish #276 and #97.

### Stocking Frequency

- Combined stocking frequency was highest at area 2 (172) and lowest at area 6 (2). Area 7 showed 177 stockings, but these reflect the Willow Beach hatchery outflow, where trout remains are present and escapement could not be quantified. It was treated as stocked daily.
- Area 7 also had the highest number of tagged striped bass occurrences (38), followed by area 4 (31) and area 5 (23).
- Spearman's rank correlation showed no significant association between stocking frequency and striped bass occurrence (rs = 0.643, alpha = 0.05).

### Barge Tracking

- The USFWS stocking barge was tracked on five occasions. It carried nine tagged fish en route to stocking areas, but no tagged striped bass were ever detected following the barge.

### Physiology and Performance

- Weights ranged from 3,180 g to 9,530 g (mean 6,082 g, SD 1,787). Mean total length was 78 cm (SD 9). Mean condition factor (K) was 1.2 (SD 0.1).
- Longevity ranged from 1 to 172 days.
- Size and condition were not significantly correlated with longevity or average distance traveled. The strongest correlation was between longevity and condition (r = 0.195).

### Discussion and Conclusions

- **Migration:** The striped bass did not follow the seasonal migratory patterns reported in other reservoirs (for example, [[Seasonal Migration]] studies in Keystone Reservoir, Lake Whitney, and the Ohio River). The authors suggest this is because no part of Lake Mohave was physiologically limiting during the study period.
- **Spring upstream movement:** This may reflect pre-spawning behavior, but it does not explain the downstream movement of another fish at the same time. Prey availability may account for some of the variation.
- **Stocking association:** There is some association between striped bass habitat use and trout stocking areas, but it is not related to stocking frequency. The Willow Beach "preference" may partly reflect a homing effect of two fish, or the fact that these fish were caught there initially.
- **Predation efficiency:** Trout stocking probably concentrates striped bass locally, but only near the stocking point and while trout remain abundant. Long-term concentration would occur only where stocking is near-continuous, as at Willow Beach.
- **Management recommendations:**
  - Avoid restocking areas where trout densities are likely still high, such as Willow Beach.
  - If striped bass augmentation is the goal, Willow Beach may be the best place for trout stocking, given warm water and high predator concentration.
  - Use anesthesia during handling and apply an iodine-based disinfectant afterward.
  - Size does not appear to be an important criterion for selecting fish.
- **Mortality and transmitter placement:** Earlier work by [[Transmitter Implantation]] studies reported high mortality from intracavity implants under heavy anesthesia. The authors note that tag shedding is documented in other fish, but several days to a week may not be enough for encapsulation. No reports of surgical staple failure were found.
- **Caution:** Conclusions rest on relatively few fish, and early movements may reflect handling stress.
