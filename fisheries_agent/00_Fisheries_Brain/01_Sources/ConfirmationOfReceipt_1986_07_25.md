## Executive Summary
This document is a 1986 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Albuquerque, NM regional office) to Bob Preble of the University of Arizona's Physical Resources Department, addressing the legal implications of stocking and maintaining a self-sustaining population of the endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) in artificial ranch ponds on the Coronado Ranch. The correspondence clarifies obligations under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 regarding federal consultation requirements and prohibitions against willful take, and expresses FWS support for private landowner participation in endangered species recovery efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Man-made stock/ranch ponds on the Coronado Ranch, used as a refugial habitat for establishing a self-sustaining population outside the species' native range.
- **Native range context**: The Yaqui chub is native to the [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] drainage system spanning Arizona (USA) and Sonora (Mexico).
- **Conservation strategy**: Stocking of ranch ponds represents a common recovery technique for imperiled desert fishes — establishing refuge populations in isolated, low-threat waters to buffer against extinction risk in the native range.
- **Anthropogenic interaction**: The ponds are not open to fishing, minimizing risk of illegal or incidental take of the species.
- No federally funded or authorized activities were identified at the time as likely to adversely affect the stocked population, indicating minimal anticipated regulatory conflict under Section 7 consultation requirements of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Key Findings
- The [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) is legally protected as an endangered species under the U.S. Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended.
- Establishing the species in the Coronado Ranch ponds would trigger Section 7 consultation requirements only for federally funded/authorized activities affecting the population; no such conflicting activities were identified based on past pond use.
- Willful take (knowing capture or destruction) of the species is prohibited under the Act; since recreational fishing is not permitted at the ranch, this risk was considered negligible.
- The FWS explicitly endorsed and encouraged private landowner cooperation (Coronado Ranch) in supporting endangered species recovery, citing this partnership as a meaningful step toward the Yaqui chub's recovery.

Fjetland ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]), and W.L. Minckley (cc'd, noted ichthyologist associated with Sonoran desert fish conservation), with copy to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- This document illustrates a real-world case study of a **Safe Harbor-style / refuge population strategy** for freshwater desert fish conservation prior to formalized Safe Harbor Agreement policies.
