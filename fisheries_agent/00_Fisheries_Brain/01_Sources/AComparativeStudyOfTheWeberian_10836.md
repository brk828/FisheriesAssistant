# Comparative Osteology of the Weberian Apparatus in the Genus Pimephales (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 Master of Science thesis in Zoology, submitted to [[Oklahoma State University]] by Anwar David Niazi, presents a comparative osteological study of the [[Weberian Apparatus]] in four North American minnow species of the genus [[Pimephales]]. Using alizarin-stained and dissected specimens, along with serial sections of one fish, the author describes the paired [[Weberian Ossicles]] (claustrum, scaphium, intercalarium, tripus) and the supporting vertebral structures. The study concludes that the four species do not differ enough to warrant separate genera and should be treated as one genus. Within that genus, [[Pimephales Tenellus]] and [[Pimephales Notatus]] are proposed to be more closely related to each other than either is to [[Pimephales Vigilax]] or [[Pimephales Promelas]].

## Background & Description

**Scope and taxonomy.** Prior work had not examined the osteology of the subfamily Pimephalinae. The subfamily had been split into the nominal genera Hyborhynchus, Pimephales and Ceratichthys. Bailey (1951) merged these into Pimephales without comment, and Cross (1953) reduced *P. perspicuus* to a subspecies of *P. vigilax*. The thesis treats *Ceratichthys callarchus* as a dubious species and excludes it.

**Diagnostic characters (after Moore, 1957).**
- *P. promelas*: stout body (greatest depth 3.2-4.0 times in standard length), three rows of snout tubercles in breeding males, indistinct or absent caudal spot.
- *P. notatus*: black peritoneum, fleshy overhanging snout, variable intestine with at least one transverse loop.
- *P. vigilax* and *P. tenellus*: never a black peritoneum, short gut without a transverse loop. *P. tenellus* has more distinct cross-hatching and a more slender body than *P. vigilax*.

**Methods.** Specimens came from the [[Oklahoma State University Museum of Zoology]] and field collections. Fish were fixed in formalin, preserved in alcohol, and cleared and stained with [[Alizarin Staining]] in 2% [[Potassium Hydroxide]]. Dissections were made under a binocular microscope and drawn with a camera lucida. Table III lists 45 dissected specimens: 10 *P. tenellus*, 11 *P. notatus*, 10 *P. vigilax* and 10 *P. promelas*, with standard lengths of roughly 32 to 77 mm. Serial cross-sections were made of one *P. tenellus*.

**Historical context.** The Weberian apparatus was first described by [[E. H. Weber]] (1820). Later work by Bridge and Haddon (1889, 1892), Krumholz (1943), Nelson (1948) and Watson (1939) established the nomenclature and developmental origins used here. Ostariophysi were defined by Jordan (1929) as fishes with modified anterior vertebrae connected to the air bladder and enclosing an organ of hearing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Weberian apparatus links the [[Air Bladder]] to the inner ear. Vibrations pick up pressure waves across the body wall and transmit them to the sacculus and lagena. The tripus is held in place by the tensor tripodis, so vibrations are transmitted by changes in tension rather than by impact, as in mammals. Pressure in the anterior air chamber is kept uniform by a vagus-innervated sphincter at the ductus communicans (Evans, 1924-1925). Kuiper (1915) found that destroying the apparatus abolished responses to vibration but not to other stimuli.

The thesis also proposes an ecological split. The two proposed species groups each contain one carnivorous and one herbivorous form, judged by intestinal length and peritoneal colour. The author suggests the pimephaline ancestor may have arisen alongside the Notropinae as a herbivore.

## Key Findings

- **Structure:** The Weberian apparatus consists of the first four modified vertebrae, none fused to the others. It comprises the *Pars auditum* (the four ossicles) and the *Pars sustentaculum* (the supporting vertebrae, pleural ribs, ossa suspensoria, and air bladder).
- **Homology:** The processes of the first and second vertebrae are interpreted as modified ventral ribs fused laterally to the centra. Homologies between the scaphium and intercalarium and the third neural pedicle and fourth neural arch are proposed. The neural complex is interpreted as the neural spine of the third vertebra plus any interneural bones.
- **Scaphium:** Species differ only in ascending process length. Order from longest to shortest: *P. notatus*, *P. tenellus*, *P. vigilax*, *P. promelas*.
- **Average rib length ratios** (first modified rib to second): *P. notatus* and *P. promelas* 1/4.5; *P. tenellus* 1/3.48; *P. vigilax* 1/2.4.
- **Intercalarium:** Shape differs among species. *P. notatus* is inverted L-shaped, *P. vigilax* triangular with a needle-like ascending process, *P. tenellus* T-shaped, and *P. promelas* crescentic.
- **Tripus:** *P. vigilax* has short, wide rami and an unfenestrated body. The body is fenestrated in *P. tenellus* and frequently in *P. notatus*, rarely in *P. promelas*, and never in *P. vigilax*.
- **Neural complex:** Highest in *P. promelas* and *P. notatus*, low in *P. tenellus*, and moderately high with a long stem in *P. vigilax*.
- **Second neural arch:** Carries a rudimentary neural spine in *P. promelas* and usually in *P. notatus*.
- **Pits in first centrum (dorsal/ventral):** *P. promelas* 3/1; *P. notatus* usually 2 (rarely 3)/1; *P. tenellus* 2/1; *P. vigilax* 2/1.
- **Genus-level conclusion:** The four species show no generic-level differences and are treated as one genus, *Pimephales*.
- **Species relationships:** *P. tenellus* and *P. notatus* share a swollen manubrium incudis terminus, long rami, a fenestrated tripus body and a small boat blade. *P. promelas* and *P. vigilax* share short ascending scaphium processes, unfenestrated tripodes, and larger neural complex blades. The author proposes two species groups that arose separately within Pimephalinae.
- **Systematic implication:** The pale-peritoneum, short-gut character used to separate *Ceratichthys* breaks down in *Hyborhynchus* (*P. notatus*), which varies in both pigmentation and gut length. The thesis therefore argues against the earlier generic separation.
- **Future work:** The author notes an unpublished companion study on *Hybognathus placita* and *H. nuchalis* (Niazi and Moore, n.d.).
