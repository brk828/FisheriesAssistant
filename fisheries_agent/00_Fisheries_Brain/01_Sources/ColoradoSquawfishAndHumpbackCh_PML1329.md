# Colorado Squawfish and Humpback Chub Population and Habitat Monitoring (Colorado, 1979)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This Colorado Division of Wildlife Job Progress Report (Project SE-3-2, Work Plan 1, Job 1; period October 1, 1978 to September 30, 1979) documents the second year of monitoring for endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Colorado River]] system. Fishes were sampled by seines, dip nets and boat electrofishing from July through August 1978 across five [[Trend Zones]] and special sites on the [[Yampa River]], [[Colorado River]], [[Gunnison River]] and [[White River]]. A total of 19,042 fish were processed from 269 collections at 57 sites. Eighteen endangered fishes were collected, and both endangered species were documented, apparently sympatric in parts of the Colorado River. The report also describes native and introduced fish trends and ends with recommendations for future work.

## Background & Description

- **Sponsor and program:** State of Colorado, Department of Natural Resources, Division of Wildlife, Nongame Program Management. Coordinated by David Langlois and Tom Lytle; fish collection and identification by Edmund J. Wick and Darrel E. Snyder (graduate students at [[Colorado State University]]).
- **Objectives:** Monitor reproductive success and year-class abundance of Colorado squawfish and humpback chubs; develop an identification key for larval fishes (sponsored by the [[Bureau of Land Management]]); and monitor habitat status in trend zones known to contain these species.
- **Study criteria for trend zones:** (1) the species must have occurred in the area within the last 10 years; (2) the area must be accessible to a field crew; and (3) the area must be representative of other river portions.
- **Gear:** A rubber raft and a flat-bottomed electrofishing boat provided access. Seines (fine, under 6 mm mesh; coarse, over 6 mm mesh) and dip nets were used. Fish were grouped into five length classes (0–20, 21–50, 51–150, 151–300 and 301 mm TL and over).
- **Habitat types defined:** [[Backwater]], [[Pool]], [[Shoreline]], [[Chute Channel]], [[Riffle]] and [[Run]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Native fishes were better represented across waters than in 1977, with suckers forming a greater share of size categories. Higher, colder water and runoff in 1979 shaped species composition; spawning was delayed and no non-native fishes had successfully spawned by the July 22 sampling date.
- Red shiners, common in 1977, were not collected in the White River in 1979, and high runoff probably displaced intermediate-sized fishes downstream.
- Seven introduced species are firmly established in the Yampa River: common carp, white sucker, fathead minnow, redside shiner and sand shiner. Hybridization between white sucker and native suckers is common above Maybell.
- A suspected redside shiner × speckled dace hybrid was captured (two additional specimens in 1979; the largest, 130 mm, from station D). A plains killifish was documented in 1977 and again at station D in 1978, and a smallmouth bass was captured near Redlands Dam (the first documented capture in the Gunnison basin since 1973 per the report).
- Endangered fish captures were associated with swift water near sandy shorelines, sandbars or eddies near boulders; water depths ranged from 0.5 to 3 m over sand and silt substrates.

## Key Findings

- **Totals:** 19,042 fish processed from 269 collections at 57 sites across five trend zones and five special sites.
- **Endangered fishes:** 18 collected in 1978: four Colorado squawfish and seven humpback chubs from the lower Colorado River trend zone; one Colorado squawfish just below Redlands Dam; six Colorado squawfish from Yampa canyons (two at Cross Mountain, four at Juniper Canyon).
- **Sympatry:** The Black Rocks area (Moore Bottom) was the only site where both species were captured together, suggesting co-existence of two populations; a ripe male squawfish (560 mm) and a possibly spent female (540 mm) raise the possibility of spawning there.
- **Size and age:** Colorado squawfish ranged from 500 to 920 mm; the largest, from Juniper Canyon, was estimated at 12 pounds. Scale-based ages ranged from eight to 12 years. Humpback chub lengths (131, 258, 300, 330, 331 and 335 mm) represented about four year classes, and the 131 mm fish was estimated at two years, spawned in 1976.
- **Range:** A humpback chub about 4 km above Black Rocks indicates the population is more spread out than previously thought.
- **Cross Mountain Canyon survey (October 1978):** No humpback chubs were captured despite apparently favorable habitat; a 2-hour (approximately) pool yielded three roundtail chubs and one channel catfish.
- **Recommendations:** (1) continue earlier recommendations with date updates; (2) sample the Yampa and lower Colorado trend zones by electrofishing before spring runoff and in fall, with fall seining for young-of-year fish; (3) institute an endangered fish tagging program and coordinate upper Colorado tagging; (4) study habitat preferences and requirements experimentally; (5) include [[Razorback Sucker]] in investigations; (6) remove the White River and Gunnison River (above Redlands Dam) from the annual program and survey them every three years.
