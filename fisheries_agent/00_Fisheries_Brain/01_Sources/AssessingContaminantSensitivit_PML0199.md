# Chronic Toxicity of Copper and Pentachlorophenol to Two Listed and Two Surrogate Fish Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ecotoxicology #Percidae #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This study compares early life-stage chronic toxicity of [[Copper]] and [[Pentachlorophenol]] (PCP) in two federally listed fishes, the endangered [[Fountain Darter]] and the threatened [[Spotfin Chub]], with two commonly tested surrogate species, the [[Fathead Minnow]] and the [[Rainbow Trout]]. The authors asked whether listed species are adequately protected by current chronic [[Water Quality Criteria]] (WQC). Effect concentrations were derived three ways: [[Analysis of Variance]]-based [[Lowest Observed Effect Concentration]] (LOEC) values, [[Linear Interpolation]] inhibition concentrations (IC), and [[Logistic Regression]] effect concentrations (EC).

The fountain darter was the most sensitive species for both chemicals. Its chronic effect concentrations for copper and PCP were lower than current chronic WQC values. The spotfin chub was no more sensitive than the commonly tested species. Biomass was the most sensitive and least variable end point. The authors recommend linear interpolation over ANOVA for chronic effect estimation and propose [[Safety Factor]] approaches as an interim measure for listed species.

## Background & Description

The document is a peer-reviewed research article in an environmental toxicology journal. Authors are from the Columbia Environmental Research Center of the [[United States Geological Survey]], Columbia Missouri, AScI Corporation, the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, and the United States Environmental Protection Agency.

**Regulatory context.** The [[Endangered Species Act]] requires federal agencies to ensure that authorized actions do not jeopardize listed species or adversely modify critical habitat. Short-term tests are used to assess risk to listed fishes, but they cannot establish safe long-term exposure levels. Few chronic data exist for listed species. Chronic WQC are usually derived from acute data using average [[Acute-Chronic Ratio]] (ACR) values.

**Prior work.** Earlier acute tests from the same laboratory found that rainbow trout were generally as sensitive as the listed species tested. Across species and chemicals, LC50 values for listed species differed from those for rainbow trout by no more than a factor of 3. The fathead minnow was similar in sensitivity to listed species in tests with effluents.

**Study objectives.** The authors had two aims: (1) to compare the toxicity of two chemicals to two listed and two commonly tested fish species using the most appropriate end points and effect concentrations; and (2) to determine whether current chronic WQC for copper and PCP protect the species tested.

**Test chemicals.** Copper and PCP were chosen because they are widespread in contaminated waters and represent two broad contaminant classes: cationic metals and water-soluble organic compounds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Test organisms.** Fountain darters came from the National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center (San Marcos, TX). Spotfin chubs came from Conservation Fisheries (Knoxville, TN). Fathead minnows came from Aquatic BioSystems (Fort Collins, CO). Rainbow trout were obtained from a national fish hatchery. Trout tests began with eyed embryos or swim-up fry.

**Test design.** Tests used [[Early Life-Stage Toxicity Test]] methods based on ASTM guidelines. Key conditions:
- Intermittent-flow proportional diluter with five exposure concentrations (dilution factor 0.5) plus a control
- Four replicate chambers per concentration, with 10 or 15 larvae per chamber (15 or 25 for trout)
- Test duration of 30 days (trout eggs tested for 30 days after swim-up)
- Temperature of 25 ± 1°C for darters, chubs, and minnows; 23°C for one fountain darter copper test; 10°C for trout
- Moderately hard reconstituted water (hardness about 170 mg/L as CaCO3, alkalinity about 115 mg/L, pH 8.3)
- Fish fed live brine shrimp nauplii three times daily

**Water quality.** Measured concentrations generally matched nominal values. Mean pH was 8.30 ± 0.13. Dissolved oxygen stayed above 70% of saturation. Un-ionized ammonia did not exceed 0.002 mg/L.

**Quality control.** In test Cu1, background copper contamination (about 5 µg/L in controls) came from a brass pump impeller, which was replaced with plastic. PCP measured about 7–10% above nominal in the three highest treatments of test PCP1; the source was not determined, but later PCP tests were close to nominal.

## Key Findings

**Sensitivity ranking.**
- For copper, the fountain darter was the most sensitive species. Survival LOECs for two fountain darter tests were 9.3 and 11 µg/L, compared with 22–42 µg/L for the other species.
- For PCP, fountain darter growth and biomass LOECs were 39 µg/L. The spotfin chub and rainbow trout had LOECs of 68–72 µg/L.
- Fountain darter PCP effect concentrations were about three times lower than those for spotfin chubs or rainbow trout and about six times lower than those for fathead minnows.

**Copper effect levels.**
- Fountain darter biomass IC25 values were about 8–9.3 µg/L. Chronic values for survival, biomass, and growth were between 8 and 9 µg/L.
- Spotfin chub biomass IC25 was about 33 µg/L, and its other end points were about 30–73% greater than those of the other species.
- Rainbow trout had chronic values of 9–16 µg/L. Fathead minnow sublethal IC25 values were consistent across tests (11–24 µg/L).

**PCP effect levels.**
- Rainbow trout biomass IC25 was 69 µg/L. Fathead minnow biomass IC25 was 142 µg/L.
- The geometric mean chronic value for fathead minnow biomass was 47 µg/L.
- Fountain darter biomass ICL10 was below 15 µg/L.

**End point comparison.**
- Growth was generally more sensitive than survival.
- Biomass was at least as sensitive as growth or survival for fountain darters and spotfin chubs in copper tests, and for all four species in PCP tests.
- Biomass gave the most sensitive IC25 in 15 of 16 tests.
- Among-test variation of IC25 values was 10–40% RSD, equal to or lower than that for LOECs or IC10 values for all end points.
- Biomass integrates reduced survival and reduced growth, and it appears less affected by density-dependent effects than growth.

**Measurement method.**
- Approximately half (48%) of LOECs fell within the 10–25% effect range.
- LOECs corresponded to effects ranging from 9% to 76% relative to controls.
- IC10 values averaged 32% (range 8–74%), and they were lower than the corresponding IC25 values.
- The IC approach identified aberrant LOECs in fathead minnow tests.
- Logistic regression EC25 values agreed with linear interpolation IC25 values, with an average RPD of 6.6%. Logistic models fit the low-effect range for more than 20% of end points, while IC25 could be calculated for nearly 80%.

**Listed species versus surrogates.**
- In acute tests, the fountain darter was the most sensitive of 14 species to copper. Its 96-hour LC50 was 57 µg/L, compared with 80 µg/L for rainbow trout and 470 µg/L for fathead minnow.
- Listed species were not consistently more sensitive than the commonly tested species overall.

**Protectiveness of current criteria.**
- Fountain darter chronic values and IC25 values for copper were below the national criterion of 14.7 µg/L at a hardness of 170 mg/L. The authors conclude that the chronic WQC does not adequately protect this species.
- The chronic copper WQC was derived from the acute criterion using an ACR of 2.1–3 (value as reported in the source). The fountain darter's chronic ACR was much larger, so the chronic WQC is less protective for this species.
- Current PCP chronic WQC (24 µg/L at pH 8.3) protects the spotfin chub and both surrogate species, but may not protect the fountain darter. Fountain darter biomass ICL10 was below this value.
- Proposed revisions (for example, a draft copper criterion of 9.4 µg/L at the test hardness) would protect the species tested, but a full revision is a long-term process.

**Recommendations.**
- Use linear interpolation (IC) or regression to derive effect concentrations rather than relying only on ANOVA. ANOVA should still confirm statistical significance before IC calculations.
- Apply safety factors to chronic WQC in areas where listed species occur or in designated critical habitat. The authors found that a factor of 0.5 gave adequate margins for the two listed species tested.
- Safety factors of 0.5, 0.33, and 0.2 applied to IC25 values would protect against 10% inhibition in 84%, 95%, and 100% of cases, respectively.
- Additional chronic testing is most valuable for listed species that are known or suspected to be exposed to specific toxicants in their native habitats.
- The authors note that roughly 100 listed species have never been tested, so safety factors derived from four species and two chemicals have limited reach.
