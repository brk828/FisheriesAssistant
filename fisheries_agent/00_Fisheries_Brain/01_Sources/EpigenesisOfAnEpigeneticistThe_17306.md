# Epigenesis of an Epigeneticist: Alternative Concepts on the Early Ontogeny and Evolution of Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-ontogeny

## Executive Summary

This is the inaugural review in the periodical [[Guelph Ichthyology Reviews]] (Number 1, September 1990), written by [[Eugene K. Balon]] of the [[Institute of Ichthyology]], University of Guelph. A foreword by David L.G. Noakes introduces the new Institute, founded in June 1990 on the [[Axelrod Fossil Collection]] donated by Dr. Herbert R. Axelrod. The main review compiles Balon's papers from 1959 to 1989 into a case for a developmental, epigenetic view of fish life histories. Its central claims are that ontogeny proceeds in saltatory steps separated by thresholds, that early ontogeny determines the definitive phenotype, and that larvae are not universal in fishes. It also proposes a classification of reproductive styles and an "alprehost" (altricial-precocial homeorhetic states) mechanism for evolutionary divergence. The author describes the work as unconventional and contentious.

## Background & Description

**Foreword and institutional context.** The foreword compares the Institute to a cathedral spire whose foundations must support its ambitions. It notes that the Institute draws on a fossil donation announced in December 1989 and on the accumulated work of the Guelph ichthyologists. The founding Core Members are Balon, F. William H. Beamish, Moira M. Ferguson, John F. Leatherland, David L.G. Noakes, E. Donald Stevens and Glen J. Van Der Kraak. Their interests span endocrinology, ethology, genetics, embryology, physiology, evolution and fisheries management. The periodical is intended for longer, synthetic, refereed reviews, and it follows two existing Guelph journals, [[Environmental Biology of Fishes]] and [[Fish Physiology and Biochemistry]].

**Scope of the review.** Balon argues that the life-history model, not reproductive timing or fecundity alone, should guide the study of fish development. He stresses that fish ontogeny should be compared with that of other vertebrates and invertebrates, and that terms such as "fry," "larva" and "embryo" are used loosely. The review sets out the following terms:
- **Epigenesis:** the interaction of developmental elements that builds a phenotype.
- **Homeorhesis:** [[Waddington]]'s concept of stabilized flow along a trajectory of change.
- **Saltatory ontogeny:** development as a sequence of stabilized steps separated by thresholds.

**Life-history model.** The model includes embryo, larval, juvenile, adult and senescent periods. Periods are divided into phases, and phases into steps. Stage denotes an instantaneous state. The embryonic period runs from activation to the start of exogenous feeding, and includes a free-embryo (eleutheroembryo) interval. The author proposes that activation, not fertilization or hatching, marks the precise start of ontogeny in bisexual organisms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Feeding transitions:** The shift from endogenous (yolk) to exogenous feeding is treated as the decisive threshold. Larvae are described as nutrient-gathering devices that exist to supply nutrition the yolk cannot provide.
- **Planktonic habitat:** The author argues that the planktonic "soup" favours retaining larvae in marine fishes, and that the common belief that all fishes have larvae is a bias from marine ichthyoplankton studies.
- **Predation and dispersal:** Larval vulnerability is offset by high egg numbers. Larval dispersal may bring benefits, but larvae also face high mortality.
- **Freshwater invasion:** The walleye (*Stizostedion vitreum*) example shows that freshwater invasion reduced invertebrate predation on eggs and embryos. Free embryos drift downstream to lacustrine habitats where food becomes available at first feeding.
- **Hiding and guarding:** Salmonids hide eggs in gravel, and their dense yolk and carotenoid-based respiration compensate for low oxygen in those hiding places.
- **Bearing and brooding:** Parental carriage reduces exposure to predators and to water-level fluctuations.
- **Temperature and oxygen:** Hatching timing depends on oxygen tension and other environmental cues, and is not a fixed developmental event.

## Key Findings

1. **Saltatory ontogeny.** Ontogeny consists of long stabilized steps separated by rapid, far-from-stable thresholds. Figure 1 uses the Danubian cyprinid *Abramis ballerus* to illustrate this. The author argues that arbitrary "normal stages" and gradual-accumulation models obscure these thresholds, and that sampling designs must account for them.

2. **Timing of ontogeny begins at activation.** Fertilization is a process that can last more than 20 hours in salmonids. Activation by water is the defensible starting point, because it occurs even in uninseminated eggs. Thermal-sum, mitotic-cycle and other timing methods are reviewed, but the author questions their value without an understanding of developmental events.

3. **Hatching is not a threshold.** Hatching is gradual, varies between individuals, and is environmentally influenced. The transition to exogenous feeding is the more meaningful ontogenetic boundary.

4. **Larvae are not universal.** Larvae are present in some fishes but absent in others. Many fishes develop directly into juveniles. The lamprey *Petromyzon marinus* and *Lampetra planeri* develop indirectly, while the hagfish *Myxine glutinosa* (with 42 chromosomes, against 168 in the lamprey) develops directly. The author treats direct development as the more specialized condition.

5. **Reproductive classification.** The review presents a classification of reproductive guilds grouped into A. nonguarders, B. guarders and C. bearers. The main trend runs from egg-scattering to hiding, guarding and bearing, with progressively larger yolk and more protected young. The table lists 30+ guilds with examples.

6. **Cichlid mouthbrooding.** Balon argues that mouthbrooding evolved polyphyletically, and that developmental timing at hatching is less important than the size and state of young at first exogenous feeding. In *Cyphotilapia frontosa*, exogenous feeding begins inside the mother's buccal cavity, and the young are released as "yolksac juveniles" after 54 days.

7. **Latimeria chalumnae.** One female contained 19 eggs of about 9 cm diameter. A 163 cm female bore a single embryo of 42 cm and 800 g at release, after an estimated gestation of 10–13 months. The author places this species in a guild combining lecithotrophy and matrotrophy.

8. **Alprehost mechanism.** Altricial (generalist) and precocial (specialist) forms are alternative homeorhetic states. Epigenetic bifurcations at thresholds generate both forms within a single clutch. The author suggests that this mechanism underlies alternative life histories and evolutionary divergence. Paedomorphosis is discussed as a route to juvenilization, which can postpone extinction.

9. **Chromosome-based evolutionary patterns.** The salmonin cladogram (Fig. 24a) suggests paedomorphosis at each bifurcation. Chromosome numbers fall along the precocial trajectory, from 2n = 92 in *Brachymystax lenok* to 52 in *Oncorhynchus gorbuscha*. Among equids, chromosome numbers fall from 66 in Przewalski's horse to 32 in mountain zebra. Balon presents these as correlations, not as proof.

10. **Critique of neo-Darwinism.** The review argues that neo-Darwinian mechanisms are incomplete rather than wrong, and that equilibrium-based population genetics has constrained ecological theory. The author states that his confidence in adaptation and natural selection has weakened.
