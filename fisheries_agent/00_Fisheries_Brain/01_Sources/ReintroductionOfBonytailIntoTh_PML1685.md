# Bonytail Chub Reintroduction and Woundfin Hatchery Management

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes a 1992 contribution to the [[Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council]] (Volume XXIV, per OCR). The scanned text is heavily degraded, so the summary relies only on legible fragments. The document covers two linked topics: an experimental stocking of hatchery-reared [[Bonytail Chub]] into the [[Green River]], and hatchery production and disease management for the [[Woundfin]]. The text is partly in Spanish. A Spanish abstract describes a reintroduction program that used a hatchery-breeding facility, with fish dispersal and survival monitoring. It concludes that the study was insufficient to judge the program's overall success.

## Background & Description

- **Bonytail chub** (*Gila elegans*): the subject of an experimental stocking study. Adult fish were raised at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and transported to the Green River. The OCR text indicates stocking took place in 1988, with monitoring in 1989 and 1990.
- **Woundfin** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*): a small cyprinid described as a candidate for reintroduction. Its hatchery propagation is the second major theme.
- **Study setting:** The document names the [[Green River]] and mentions the [[Hurricane]] and [[Virgin River]] area. Both are tributaries within the [[Colorado River Basin]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Bonytail stocking.** The abstract notes that the [[Stocking Experiment]] was designed to observe the effects of the passage of the spring runoff, water temperature and related environmental conditions. Survival and dispersal of released fish were monitored. Results were compared with those from an earlier study published in 1982 (OCR-garbled). Specific counts, sample sizes and statistics could not be recovered reliably from the scan.

**Woundfin threats.** The text identifies several stressors on woundfin in hatchery and pond settings:
- Loss in ponds linked to excess [[Aquatic Vegetation]]
- Infestation by the parasitic copepod [[Lernaea]]
- Acute outbreaks of [[Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis]] (white spot disease, "Ich"), to which woundfin appear especially prone
- Restrictions on chemical treatments after the [[Food and Drug Administration]] banned certain compounds

**Management approaches.** Hatchery methods discussed include [[Temperature Control]] and possibly [[Ozonation]] as treatment or prevention tools. The OCR is too degraded to confirm the details.

## Key Findings

1. **Bonytail stocking was tested experimentally.** Hatchery-reared adult bonytail were released into the Green River in 1988 and monitored in 1989–1990. The fragments give no reliable numbers.
2. **Program success was not established.** The Spanish abstract states that the study was insufficient to judge the reintroduction effort's success, and that the survival and dispersal of released fish were still being discussed.
3. **Woundfin hatchery production is a key constraint.** Woundfin were propagated for reintroduction, but pond losses and disease outbreaks limit production.
4. **Disease control is a central management issue.** Ich and Lernaea are the main named threats, and treatment options narrowed after FDA restrictions on certain chemicals.
5. **Environmental controls are recommended.** Temperature control and possibly ozonation are discussed as ways to reduce disease risk in hatchery rearing.
