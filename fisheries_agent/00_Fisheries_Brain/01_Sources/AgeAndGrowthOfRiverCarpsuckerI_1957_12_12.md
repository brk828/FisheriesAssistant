# Age and Growth of River Carpsucker in Des Moines River, Iowa

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1957 life-history study by Buchholz examines the [[Northern River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio carpio*) population in a ~4-mile stretch of the [[Des Moines River]] below the Frazer Dam, Boone County, Iowa. Despite being one of the most abundant fish species in the river, the carpsucker was poorly studied and commercially undervalued, classified largely as a "rough fish." The study characterizes length-weight relationships, age and growth via scale analysis, fecundity, and food habits, using 1,441 specimens collected primarily via electric shocker. Key findings include a negative allometric growth pattern (weight increases slower than the cube of length), evidence of [[Lee's Phenomenon]] in back-calculated growth, an intermittent spawning strategy from May through July, and a diet dominated by diatoms and algae rather than the "bottom ooze" traditionally ascribed to the species.

## Background & Description

- **Length measurement conventions:**
  - Total Length (TL): snout tip to compressed caudal fin tip
  - Fork Length (FL): snout tip to caudal fin fork
  - Standard Length (SL): snout tip to posterior hypural plate edge
  - Conversion factors (n=53 fish, 3.4–13.7 in.):
    - SL = 0.754 × TL
    - FL = 0.890 × TL
    - SL = 0.848 × FL

- **Length-Weight Relationship** (n=209 carpsuckers):
  - `Log W = -2.966 + 2.809(log L)` where W = weight (hundredths of lbs), L = TL (inches)
  - Regression coefficient (2.809) significantly differs from isometric value of 3.0 (p<0.01) — indicates fish become relatively less plump ("less deep-bodied") as length increases.
  - No significant sex-based difference in length-weight relationship (17 males vs. 20 females, July sample, analysis of covariance, p>0.05).

- **Condition Factor (C):**
  - Formula: `C = W(10^5/L^3)`
  - Range: 84 (1.5–1.9 in. fish) down to 41 (17.5–17.9 in. fish)
  - Spent females: average C = 42; Egg-bearing females: average C = 47 (significant difference, p<0.01)
  - No seasonal variation in condition factor noted April–August.

- **Largest specimen:** 17.5 inch male (also oldest fish recorded).

- **Scale/Body relationship:** Linear regression: `L = 0.57 + 0.393(S)`, where L = TL (inches), S = postero-lateral scale radius × 49mm magnification factor. Focal intercept of 0.57 inches used in nomograph construction (per [[Carlander And Smith Nomograph Method]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat preference:** Adults concentrated in deeper, quieter river sections, under brushpiles, and narrow bank-associated channels; rarely in shallow/fast water. Young-of-year (YOY) almost exclusively in shallow water.
- **Substrate:** Predominantly sand, with bedrock/boulder patches and sheltered muck-bottom areas.
- **Sympatric Catostomids:** Co-occurring with [[Highfin Carpsucker]], [[Quillback Carpsucker]], and [[Plains Carpsucker]] in same river reach, though in far lower abundance (103, 15, and 15 individuals respectively vs. 1,441 river carpsuckers).
- **Diet (Food Habits, n=104 stomachs, 28 empty):**
  - Contrary to reputation as a "bottom ooze" feeder, diet dominated by:
    - Diatoms (71% occurrence)
    - Green algae (69%)
    - Blue-green algae (55%)
    - Desmids (54%)
    - Dipterous larvae/pupae (41%)
    - *Difflugia* sp. (37%)
    - Rotifera (24%)
    - Copepoda (22%)
  - Fine sand grains and mucous nearly universal in stomach contents (likely incidental to substrate feeding).
  - Diet composition largely consistent across fish size/age, though plant material (stems, roots) and larger prey (e.g., *Physa* sp. snails) only found in larger fish.
  - No clear seasonal variation detected in food habit frequency.
  - Potential dietary competition with [[Des Moines River Minnows]] and juvenile [[Channel Catfish]] over dipterous larvae (per Starrett 1950; Bailey & Harrison 1948).
- **Parasites documented:**
  - Cestodes: *Glaridacris* sp. (unsegmented), *Biacetabulum infrequens*
  - External parasitic copepods: *Argulus* sp., *Lernaea* sp. (commonly attached at fin base)

## Key Findings

### Age & Growth
- **Annulus formation:** Occurs in May, coinciding with transition from slow winter growth to faster growth. Confirmed via bracketing capture dates (pre-annulus fish through May 22; all post-annulus by June 13).
- **Growth pattern:** Average annual length increments rise through year 2, then progressively decrease (Table 2) — except final year class (single specimen, 1947 year class, anomalous).
- **Year-class growth variation:** 1952 and 1954 classes show above-average growth; 1953 class below average.
- **[[Lee's Phenomenon]]:** Older fish show smaller calculated lengths at given annuli than younger fish at the same annuli — attributed primarily to differential mortality (faster-growing fish die younger; per Hile 1936), consistent with prior work by Van Oosten (1928).
- **Additional growth discrepancies** (calculated length at annulus < average length at capture for preceding age class) attributed to three compounding factors:
  1. Inter-annual variation in year-class growth rates
  2. Above-average growth conditions in 1956 (paralleling channel catfish growth patterns, per Muncy 1957)
  3. Potential gear selectivity of electric shocker toward larger individuals (per Fisher 1950 — larger fish stunned at lower voltage thresholds)

### Fecundity & Reproduction
- **Spawning period:** May–July 1956 (based on appearance of 1.0–1.4 inch fingerlings June–August); supports intermittent spawner classification (per Starrett 1948) rather than single discrete spawning event.
- **Spent female ratios:** 2/6 (May), 4/19 (June), 15/30 (July) — increasing proportion spent over time supports prolonged spawning window.
- **Egg size distribution:** Bimodal — modes at 2.9–3.4mm and 5.4–5.9mm (49X magnification); plus a third, smaller transparent egg group (~1/3 size of smallest opaque mode), excluded from fecundity counts.
- **Fecundity range:** 4,828 eggs (9.6 in., age 2 female) to 149,744 eggs (14.1 in., age 5 female).
- **Mean fecundity:** 102,766 eggs at mean length 13.5 inches.
- **Fecundity-length regressions:**
  - Linear: `N = 141,539 + 18,137(L)` (r = 0.62)
  - Log-log (better fit): `Log N = -2.435 + 6.549(Log L)` (r = 0.83)
- **Sexual maturity:** Youngest mature female = age 2 (4,828 eggs); mature females recorded ages IV–VII. Five age-class II males recorded as immature via testis size/texture.

### Comparative Note
- Brezner (1956, [[Lake Of The Ozarks]] population) reported females grow faster/larger than males — this study's sample size insufficient to test sex-based growth differences directly, though largest/oldest fish captured was male.
