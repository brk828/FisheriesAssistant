# Supplement to the List of References on Control of Aquatic Plants, Including Algae

#aquatic-botany #phycology #aquatic-weed-control

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1957 supplement to an earlier bibliography on [[Aquatic Weed Control]] and [[Algae Control]], compiled by [[Margaret Greenwald]] of the [[Chipman Chemical Company]] Research Division. It contains (1) corrections to errors in the 1956 reference list and (2) additional references that reached the author during 1956, including a few on aquatic fungi. The list is explicitly neither complete nor selected. It contains no original experimental data of its own, but many annotations summarize the findings of the cited works. The author states that no further supplements are planned and encourages the [[Weed Society of America]] or another group to develop a critical review of the world literature.

The document contains no fish-specific research, although several entries concern fish toxicity and fish-pond management.

## Background & Description

The supplement is a bibliographic finding aid. Its entries are alphabetical by first author and follow a standard citation style: author, year, title (often in capitals), journal or report, volume, pages and, in many cases, a short annotation. Sources include:

- Peer-reviewed journals (for example *Weeds*, *Botanical Review*, *Journal of Wildlife Management*, *Journal of the American Water Works Association*)
- Conference proceedings, notably the [[Northeastern Weed Control Conference]], the [[Western Weed Control Conference]] and the [[Southern Weed Conference]]
- Government reports from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, the U.S. Department of Agriculture and several Australian and British agencies
- Trade literature, booklets, patents and newspaper or magazine articles
- Foreign-language items, including German, French, Hebrew, Italian and Portuguese titles, several of which the list notes are available only in those languages

The **Corrections** section lists about ten fixes, including corrected author spellings, page numbers, volume numbers and misfiled entries.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The references cover a broad range of aquatic nuisance problems:

- **Invasive floating and emergent plants:** [[Water Hyacinth]] in Africa, New Zealand, Florida, Louisiana and Pakistan; [[Water Chestnut]] in New York and the Chesapeake Bay; [[Cattail]] and [[Phragmites]] in ditches and marshes; and [[Salvinia Auriculata]] in Ceylon.
- **Submersed plants and algae:** *Anacharis densa*, *Najas* and *Chara* in fish ponds, canals and alkaline waters, and filamentous and blue-green algae in ponds, reservoirs and water supplies. [[Prymnesium Parvum]], a toxic phytoflagellate, is treated in several Israeli fish-pond studies.
- **Water quality and taste and odor:** Chlorination, copper sulfate treatment and actinomycetes as causes of taste and odor in drinking water.
- **Wildlife and habitat effects:** Impacts of ditching tidewater marshes, duck marsh management, and drawdowns on fish populations. One 1950 report concludes that ecological changes from ditching were often undesirable for wildlife and that useless drainage should be halted.
- **Industrial systems:** Algae and slime in cooling towers, condensers and sanitary sewers.

Several entries also address the non-target effects of control agents, including toxicity to fish, to livestock and to mosquito-control organisms.

## Key Findings

**Corrections to the 1956 list**
- Corrected spellings and citation details for authors including Balcom, Flentje, Moran, Sharif el Din, Wilson-Jones and Timmons.
- Deleted a duplicate entry (Glund, J.W., 1954), which was already listed as Lund, J.W.G., 1954.
- Reassigned several entries that had been misfiled, including Sharif el Din and Wilson-Jones.

**Notable findings reported in annotations**
- **Water chestnut control:** Martin (1955) reported good control with 2,4-D at 8 lb acid equivalent in 50 gallons of spray per acre. Oil and water carriers were nearly equally effective. Two to three years of spraying with follow-up patrols were needed to eliminate stands and prevent reinfestation.
- **Anacharis densa:** Jordan's 1952 Oregon State thesis evaluated about 150 chemicals against *A. densa* and listed the 10 most toxic. It also tested dried plants as mulching media.
- **Algicide screening:** Maloney and Palmer (1956) tested six compounds on thirty algae cultures. The compounds were copper sulfate, 2,3-dichloronaphthoquinone, dodecylacetamido dimethyl benzyl ammonium chloride, rosin amine D acetate, rosin amine D sulfate and zinc dimethyl dithiocarbamate.
- **Salvinia control:** Chow et al. (1955) found pentachlorophenol effective against *Salvinia*, which hosts *Mansonia* mosquitoes.
- **Herbicide toxicity to fish:** Nietzke (1952–53) found that high concentrations of 2,4-D plus 2,4,5-T, and of 2,4-D esters and salts, were lethal to fish within 10 to 20 minutes. The sodium salt was the least toxic. The author concluded that normal spraying near ponds should not injure fish.
- **Mosquito larvae and algae:** Gerhardt (1955) observed that mosquito larvae were generally absent from test plots with abundant blue-green algae of the genus *Anabaena*.
- **Fish-pond management:** Several sources address pond weed control with [[Copper Sulfate]] (Shelubsky, Shilo and colleagues in Israel), fertilizer, sodium arsenite and herbicide-related fish losses.
- **Water quality criteria:** The California State Water Pollution Control Board's *Water Quality Criteria* (512 pages, plus a 191-page addendum) is described as a useful compendium even though it is not about plant control.
- **Literature reviews:** Prescott (1956) reviewed the ecology and life histories of algae and cites over 1,000 references. Mortimer and Hickling (1954) published a 155-page review of fertilizers in fish ponds.
- **Historical items:** Early references include [[Copper Sulfate]] use at Hartford, Connecticut (Garratt, 1922) and [[Water Chestnut]] studies (Winne, 1935).

**Stated recommendations and plans**
- The author hopes that the [[Weed Society of America]] or another body will expand the list into a critical review and reference manual.
- Several 1957 conference papers were scheduled for the Northeastern Weed Control Conference Proceedings, including sessions on public relations, equipment, and control in potable water and watersheds.
