# Movement and Harvest of Crappie and Other Game Fishes in Kentucky Lake and its Tailwaters

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Centrarchidae #Moronidae #Percidae #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1953 tagging study examined movement patterns and fisherman harvest rates of game and pan fishes in [[Kentucky Lake]] and its tailwaters along the [[Tennessee River]]. Fish were captured using eight net types (hoop, fiddler, wing, gill, trammel, wooden basket, lead, and heart-lead nets) in the lake, and hoop and wing nets in the river. A total of 2,170 fish (seven species) were tagged in the lake proper, while 5,125 fish (nine species) were tagged in the tailwaters. The study measured overall harvest rate, direction/extent of movement, and the degree to which fish used the navigation locks at [[Kentucky Dam]] to move between the lake and tailwater habitats. Recapture rates were modest (6.6% in the lake, 2.8% in the tailwaters), and most recaptured fish were caught close to their release point, though a minority exhibited long-distance movement of tens of miles, including lock passage.

## Background & Description

This paper is primarily a population movement/harvest study rather than a morphological analysis; morphological data are limited to measurements taken at tagging:

- **Total length**: recorded in inches and tenths of inches at time of tagging.
- **Weight**: recorded in pounds and tenths of pounds at time of tagging.
- **Tagging method**: metal strap jaw tags inscribed "Game and Fish, Frankfort," bearing a unique identification number.

Species tagged spanned multiple families and forms, including elongate ambush predators ([[Northern Pike]]), schooling basses ([[White Bass]], [[Yellow Bass]]), a percid ([[Sauger]]), and centrarchids ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Kentucky Bass]], [[White Crappie]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Bluegill]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat description:**
- **Kentucky Lake**: A 260,000-acre eutrophic impoundment, 184 miles long, averaging 23 feet deep at conservation pool, with 2,600 miles of shoreline.
- **Tailwaters**: The Tennessee River section running 22 miles north from [[Kentucky Dam]] at Gilbertsville, Kentucky, to its confluence with the [[Ohio River]]. Approximately 1/4 mile wide, up to 35 feet deep, with rapid flow — a habitat distinct from the lentic lake environment.

**Netting/tagging zones (Kentucky Lake)** — all located in and around Big Bear Embayment, ~7 miles above Kentucky Dam:
1. Malcolm Creek (shallow cove) — 248 net days
2. Mouth of Big Bear (large embayment) — 382 net days
3. Scout Cove (shallow cove) — 198 net days
4. Middle of Main Lake (channel/shelf) — 81 net days
5. Main Lake Channel (deep water) — 35 net days

**Netting/tagging zones (Tailwaters):**
1. Kentucky Dam (1-mile stretch, both banks) — 462 net days
2. Three miles below dam — 35 net days
3. Ten miles below dam — 173 net days
4. Ohio River, 1 mile below Tennessee River mouth (south shore) — 59 net days
5. Ohio River, 1 mile above Tennessee River mouth (south shore) — 72 net days

**Interconnectivity**: The navigation locks at [[Kentucky Dam]] serve as a key ecological corridor allowing interchange of fish populations between the lentic lake and the riverine tailwater/[[Ohio River]] system, functioning bidirectionally (downstream lake-to-river movement and upstream river-to-lake movement documented).

## Key Findings

### Kentucky Lake Population
- **2,170 fish** of 7 species tagged; only **4 species recaptured**: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Yellow Bass]], [[White Crappie]], [[Black Crappie]].
- **142 fish (6.6%)** recaptured by fishermen — interpreted as a proxy for total harvest rate.
- **2 fish (0.1%)** recovered dead (both White Crappie).
- **Largemouth Bass** showed the highest recapture rate at **14.3%**, though sample size was small.
- **5 White Crappie (3.5% of recaptured fish)** moved downstream through the Kentucky Dam lock chambers.
- **50 fish (35.2% of recaptures, 2.3% of total tagged)** moved more than 1 mile, averaging **5.1 miles** per fish.
- Of 48 crappie moving >1 mile: 34 moved downstream, and 5 moved ≥7 miles downstream through the locks.
- Remaining recaptured fish showed limited movement: 56 fish moved <0.5 mile; 36 fish moved 0.5–1 mile.
- **Longest recorded movement**: a [[Yellow Bass]] recovered 29.3 miles upstream (up-lake) from release.
- A [[White Crappie]] traveled 19.8 miles in 42 days.

### Tailwater Population
- **5,125 fish** of 9 species tagged; **6 species recaptured**: [[Kentucky Bass]], [[White Bass]], [[Yellow Bass]], [[Sauger]], [[White Crappie]], [[Black Crappie]].
- **144 fish (2.8%)** recaptured by fishermen.
- **5 fish (0.1%)** recovered dead (4 White Crappie, 1 Black Crappie).
- **86.2%** of tailwater fish were tagged within 1 mile of the dam, despite a 22-mile study area.
- **Kentucky Bass** had the highest recapture rate (**10.5%**), though sample size was small and inconclusive.
- **82 of 144 (56.9%)** recaptured fish were caught <0.5 mile from release.
- **29 fish (21.4%)** moved more than 1 mile, averaging **17.1 miles** per fish — over 3x the average distance of lake fish movers.
- **22 of these 29 fish (75.9%)** moved upstream; **11 fish (7.6% of total recaptures)** passed upstream through the Kentucky Dam lock chambers into the lake.
- **Longest recorded movements**:
  - [[Sauger]]: 70 miles downstream (greatest distance recorded in the entire study).
  - [[White Bass]]: 67 miles — 22 miles down the Tennessee River to its mouth, then 45 miles up the Ohio River.
  - [[Yellow Bass]]: 22 miles downstream in 20 days.
  - [[White Crappie]]: 35 miles downstream.
  - [[Black Crappie]]: 30 miles (maximum for species).
