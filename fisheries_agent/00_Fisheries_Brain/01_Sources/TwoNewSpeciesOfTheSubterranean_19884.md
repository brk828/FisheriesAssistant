# Two New Species of the Subterranean Amphipod Genus Mexiweckelia (Gammaridae) from Mexico and Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

*Note: The source document concerns subterranean amphipod crustaceans, not fish. The tag line follows the system instruction for non-fish taxa.*

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[John R. Holsinger]] (Smithsonian Institution) describes two new species of the subterranean amphipod genus [[Mexiweckelia]] (family [[Gammaridae]]). The first, [[Mexiweckelia Mitchelli]], comes from [[Cueva de la Siquita]] in Durango, Mexico, and the second, [[Mexiweckelia Texensis]], from an artesian well at a fish hatchery in San Marcos, Texas. With these additions the genus holds four species and extends into the southern United States. The paper also discusses the genus's probable origin from Hadzia-group marine relicts and its disjunct, insular distribution.

## Background & Description

- **Prior knowledge:** [[Mexiweckelia]] was described by [[Holsinger and Minckley (1971)]] with two species, [[Mexiweckelia colei]] and [[Mexiweckelia particeps]], from groundwater outlets in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila. Only one other subterranean amphipod, [[Bogidiella tabascensis]] (Villalobos, 1960, Tabasco), was previously known from Mexico.
- **Collection context:** In June 1972, members of the Association for Mexican Cave Studies collected blind, white amphipods from two pools at Cueva de la Siquita. Specimens from the artesian well were collected by [[Leslie Hubricht]] in 1940.
- **Family status note:** The author now regards Bogidiellidae as invalid and places Bogidiella in Gammaridae.
- **Mexiweckelia mitchelli:** Holotype female and 84 paratypes (4.5 mm largest female; 3.5 mm largest male). Holotype (USNM 142355) and six paratypes (USNM 142356) are at the Smithsonian; 78 paratypes are in the author's collection. Eyeless and depigmented; separated from M. colei by more setae on maxillary inner plates, narrower pereopod bases 5–7 lacking distoposterior lobes, and fewer telson and uropod 3 spines.
- **Mexiweckelia texensis:** Four syntypes (4.0–5.0 mm; largest 5.0 mm) from the San Marcos artesian well, collected 14 May 1940. Described from damaged material lacking the third uropods, so sex and some structures are undetermined. Distinguished by long, attenuated appendages, absence of a lacinia mobilis, a greatly expanded coxal plate 1, and produced distoposterior corners of the abdominal side plates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cueva de la Siquita (M. mitchelli):** Type series came from two apparently interconnected, probably spring-fed pools, 0.3–1.5 m deep, at 24.5 °C. Amphipods were abundant and associated with small aquatic microdrile oligochaetes. No females had setae-fringed brood plates, although the larger females appeared mature.
- **Cuatro Ciénegas (M. colei, M. particeps):** Inhabit groundwater outlets fed by percolating water, at 30–33 °C.
- **San Marcos (M. texensis):** Probably an interstitial form living in minute spaces below the groundwater table, at about 20 °C. Its occurrence in the artesian well was probably fortuitous. Nearby Ezell's Cave and Johnson's Well were searched repeatedly without finding it.
- **Biogeography:** Ranges are highly insular and disjunct. Cueva de la Siquita lies 240 km southwest of the Cuatro Ciénegas species. San Marcos lies 550 km northeast of Cuatro Ciénegas and 790 km northeast of Cueva de la Siquita. Barriers include the Sierra del Tlahualilo, the Sierra Madre Oriental, and coastal plain. San Marcos sits on the [[Balcones Fault Zone]] at the edge of the [[Edwards Plateau]].
- **Hadzia group:** The group comprises eight predominantly interstitial-subterranean genera in marine, brackish and fresh waters, with a curious circumtropical distribution. Five of the eight genera are freshwater only. Mexiweckelia is closely allied to Caribbean genera Weckelia, Paraweckelia and Alloweckelia, and more distantly to [[Hadzia]] (synonym Metaniphargus).
- **Paleogeography:** The author proposes that Mexiweckelia descends from brackish-water ancestors that colonized new freshwater niches as a marine embayment regressed at the end of the Cretaceous or beginning of the Tertiary. Later Laramide mountain building would have isolated populations and driven speciation.
- **Associated relicts:** Parallels exist with stenasellid isopods (Mexistenasellus coahuila, one of only three Western Hemisphere representatives of the subfamily) and with thermosbaenaceans. The sole Western Hemisphere thermosbaenacean is from Ezell's Cave. Monodella texana is a separate, probably distinct, genus from the five Old World species.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species described:** [[Mexiweckelia Mitchelli]] (Mexico) and [[Mexiweckelia Texensis]] (Texas). The genus now has four species.
2. **Range extension:** M. texensis marks a significant extension of Mexiweckelia into the southern United States.
3. **Regional diversity:** Mexico's subterranean amphipod total rises to four species; cavernicolous species total rises to two.
4. **Diagnostic differences:** M. mitchelli differs from M. colei in setation, pereopod base shape and spination. M. texensis is diagnosed by elongated appendages and lack of a lacinia mobilis.
5. **Generic diagnosis amended:** An amendment to the genus diagnosis (upper lip margin, mandibular incisor and molar) is proposed to accommodate M. texensis. The generic placement is somewhat uncertain because the third uropod is missing.
6. **Insular distributions:** Each species is known only from its type locality, and ranges are separated by large distances and mountain barriers.
7. **Temperature-linked habitat differences:** Cave pools (24–25 °C), groundwater outlets (30–33 °C) and deep interstitial groundwater (~20 °C) appear to be distinct habitats.
8. **Marine relict origin:** Mexiweckelia is interpreted as a marine relict lineage linked to Caribbean and peri-Mediterranean subterranean faunas along the old Tethyan sea area.
9. **Future work:** Additional specimens of M. texensis are needed to describe the third uropod and sex. Continued biospeleological survey of the Caribbean region is expected to reveal more marine relict crustaceans.
