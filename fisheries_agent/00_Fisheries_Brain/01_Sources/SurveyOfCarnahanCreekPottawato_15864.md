# Carnahan Creek Fish Survey, Stockdale, Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #centrarchidae #stream-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document describes a fish collection survey conducted on [[Carnahan Creek]], a small prairie stream tributary to the [[Big Blue River]] near Stockdale, Kansas. Six collection stations were established along the stream from its mouth to approximately 2 miles upstream. The survey documented 17 fish species using seine collections, with [[Notropis cornutus]] (Common Shiner), [[Lepomis humilis]] (Orangespotted Sunfish), and [[Lepomis cyanellus]] (Green Sunfish) being most common. The stream is characterized by wooded banks, clay and gravel substrate, spring-fed upper reaches, and pollution from agricultural runoff and farm lots.

## Background & Description

[[Carnahan Creek]] flows north to south into the [[Big Blue River]] approximately 1 mile north of Stockdale, Kansas, emptying 100 yards east of the Stockdale bridge on the north side of the river. The stream is subject to backwater conditions when the Blue River floods and experiences severe flooding based on cut bank evidence. Water sources include numerous small springs, seeps, and surface runoff from grassland and farmland.

The stream banks average 6 feet high and are composed of clay and silt in the lower half, with clay, gravel, and limestone in the upper reaches. Banks are almost entirely wooded except for small areas grazed to the top. The stream is polluted mainly by farm lots and soil erosion from surrounding farmland.

Collection methods employed a seine 15 feet long, 3 feet deep, with 1/4 inch mesh. Two hauls were made at each station area: one along shore and one adjacent to shore in deeper water. Additional hauls were taken if initial drags yielded no fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Station 1** (Stream mouth): Located where the creek enters the [[Big Blue River]]. The area consisted of the river proper adjacent to the stream mouth and the mouth itself (12 feet long, 4 feet wide). Bottom composed of very soft silt. Water depth 0-4 feet. Banks bare of vegetation with many leaves, twigs, and decay debris.

**Station 2** (100 yards upstream): Pool 25 feet long, 3 feet wide, 6 inches to 1 foot deep. Water very clear with no flow in or out. Much filamentous algae present. Bottom of soft silt/clay.

**Station 3** (300 yards east of steel bridge): Pool 150 feet long, 30 feet wide, 5 feet deep at deepest point. Banks 6-8 feet high on north side, 8-20 feet high on south side, composed of clay and gravel. Banks covered with grasses, forbs, and trees (5-15 feet back from water edge). Water light brown with logs, forbs, and leaves present. Bottom of gravel, sand, and silt. Standing water with no flow.

**Station 4** (150 yards west of steel bridge): Pool approximately 175 feet long, 40 feet wide, 5 feet deep. Formed by stream bend and gravel/sand bar at upper end. South, east, and west banks covered with trees, grass, brush, and forbs. Water light brown with leaves and twigs. Standing water with slight trickle coming in. Bottom of gravel, sand, and bed-rock.

**Station 5** (100 yards above low water bridge, ~2 miles above Station 4): Pool 100 feet long, 25 feet wide, estimated 6 feet deep. East side consists of high shale, limestone, and clay bank (heavily wooded). West side has 8-10 foot cut clay bank topped by cornfield with few trees. Water very clear with 3-4 foot visibility. Bottom completely covered with leaves, twigs, and debris over loose rock, gravel, sand, and silt. Running water present.

**Station 6** (350 yards above low water bridge): Last large, deep hole in stream. Pool 70 feet long, 70 feet wide, 4.5-5 feet deep. Formed by high gravel bar. North bank covered with brush, forbs, and fallen trees. Water brown due to cattle and livestock watering. Bottom of gravel, sand, silt. Water running into pool and seeping out through gravel bar.

The stream character changes at the second steel bridge on [[Carnahan Road]]. Below this point, the stream occupies flood plain with silt and clay banks and is dry or nearly dry except during floods. Above the bridge, the stream becomes spring and seep fed with clear water, gravel and loose rock bottom, and banks of clay and rock including shale and bed-rock.

## Key Findings

**Species Composition** (ranked most to least common):
1. [[Notropis cornutus]] (Common Shiner)
2. [[Lepomis humilis]] (Orangespotted Sunfish)
3. [[Lepomis cyanellus]] (Green Sunfish)
4. [[Pimephales notatus]] (Bluntnose Minnow)
5. [[Semotilus atromaculatus]] (Creek Chub)
6. [[Pimephales promelas]] (Fathead Minnow)
7. [[Campostoma anomalum]] (Central Stoneroller)
8. [[Carpiodes carpio]] (River Carpsucker)
9. [[Ictalurus lacustris]] (Channel Catfish)
10. [[Chrosomus erythrogaster]] (Southern Redbelly Dace)
11. [[Catostomus commersonii]] (White Sucker)
12. [[Etheostoma spectabile]] (Orangethroat Darter)
13. [[Ameiurus natalis]] (Yellow Bullhead)
14. [[Cyprinus carpio]] (Common Carp)
15. [[Pomoxis annularis]] (White Crappie)
16. [[Boleosoma nigrum]] (Johnny Darter)

Station-specific collections documented varying fish assemblages, with no fish taken at Station 1 (stream mouth). The fish community reflects typical prairie stream fauna dominated by cyprinids and centrarchids, with agricultural impacts evident in water quality degradation and livestock access affecting habitat conditions, particularly in the upper reaches.
