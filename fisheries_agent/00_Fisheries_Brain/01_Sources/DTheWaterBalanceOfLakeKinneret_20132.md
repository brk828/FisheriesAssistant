# Water Balance of Lake Kinneret

#limnology #hydrology #water-balance

## Executive Summary

This section of a study on [[Lake Kinneret]] establishes the lake's generalized water balance for 1959–1974 and estimates how much each geological formation of the surrounding [[Kinneret Basin]] contributes to lake inflow. Average net inflow from the [[Jordan River]] (558 MCM/yr) almost equals average lake outflow (546 MCM/yr). Evaporation (about 294–295 MCM/yr) is balanced mainly by inflow from the immediate basin (282 MCM/yr). Groundwater supplies roughly three-quarters of watershed runoff. The formation-level estimate of basin influx (216.6 MCM/yr) closely matches the basin contribution from the overall balance (212 MCM/yr).

## Background & Description

The Kinneret watershed receives annual precipitation ranging from about 400 mm in the lake area to 1,200 mm on [[Mount Hermon]]. The total average precipitation volume is 2,160 MCM/yr. Annual totals vary widely: the text, which is partly garbled by OCR, gives the share received in very wet and very dry years as "160 to 30%" of the average.

The watershed has two main components:

- **Jordan drainage:** the upper Jordan sub-basins (Hermon River, [[Dan Springs]], Snir River, Ayun River), the [[Hula Basin]] and the Jordan–Korazim Valleys. Together they supply 66% of total lake inflow. Total Jordan discharge at the Pardes Hurih gauging station is 653.3 MCM/yr.
- **Kinneret Basin:** eastern catchments (southern [[Golan Heights]]), western basins and springs, and direct rain on the lake surface.

The authors also divided the Kinneret Basin into four geological areas:

| Formation | Area (km²) | Hydrological character |
|---|---|---|
| Basalts (northern and western sectors) | 125 | Minor aquifer units |
| [[Eocene Limestone]] | 85 | Confined aquifers of medium size |
| [[Turonian-Cenomanian Formations]] (karst) | 180 | Mixes with saline water; shapes the chemistry of mineral springs |
| Eastern shore basalts | 205 | Minor aquifer giving rise to numerous springs |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Runoff partitioning:** About 75% of total superficial runoff is underground in origin, forming a fairly uniform [[Baseflow]] from rivers and springs. The remaining 25% is storm runoff and surface flow during and after the rainy season.
- **Interannual variability:** Discharge varies considerably between years because surface flow responds almost directly to rainfall amount and timing.
- **Evaporation:** Lake evaporation (about 294 MCM/yr, calculated by energy balance) must be offset by inflow from the basin. This is why the basin's contribution matters so much to the lake's water budget.
- **Saline inputs:** The saline flux is estimated at 90 × 10⁶ m³/yr. Subtracting it from the 212 MCM/yr of surface flow and springs leaves about 122 MCM/yr of freshwater from the basin close to the lake.
- **Groundwater and salinity:** Water from the [[Turonian-Cenomanian Formations]] mixes with saline water and affects the chemistry of the mineral springs. The basalts also transmit large volumes of water to underlying formations, even though they are not strictly basaltic aquifers.
- **Sublacustrine springs:** The [[Ma'agan Sublacustrine Springs]] are part of the investigated hydrology and help explain the origin of saline water and seepage on the lake floor.

## Key Findings

1. **Water balance (1959–1974):** Average precipitation on the watershed is 2,160 MCM/yr. Total net lake inflow is 840 MCM/yr, of which the Jordan contributes 558 MCM/yr (net, after about 110 MCM/yr upstream use) and the Kinneret Basin 282 MCM/yr. Outflow is about 546 MCM/yr.
2. **Basin contribution:** The four-formation analysis gives an average total influx of 216.6 MCM/yr (about 217 MCM), in close agreement with the 212 MCM/yr basin contribution from the overall balance.
3. **Average influx by formation (MCM/yr):**
   - Basalts (west and north): 15.0 (ground water 9.1, surface 5.9)
   - [[Eocene Limestone]]: 23.7 (ground water 20.3, surface 3.4)
   - Turonian–Cenomanian formations: 56.7 (ground water 51.3, surface 5.4)
   - [[Golan Heights]]: 121.2 (ground water 74.7, surface 46.5)

   The Golan Heights therefore contributes the largest share, about 56% of average influx in the table. The text states a figure of "60%" for seepages, but that sentence is truncated in the source.
4. **Interannual range:** Annual total influx in Table 15 ranges from a low of 105.0 MCM (1972/73) to a high of 499.7 MCM (1968/69). Average groundwater flow is 155.4 MCM/yr and average surface flow is 61.2 MCM/yr.
5. **Freshwater versus saline supply:** Of the 212 MCM/yr surface and spring flow, 90 × 10⁶ m³/yr is saline, leaving about 122 MCM/yr of freshwater from the close basin.
6. **Purpose:** The formation-level water balance supports detailed investigation of the origin of saline water and the groundwater–lake interaction, including the sublacustrine springs at the lake bottom.
