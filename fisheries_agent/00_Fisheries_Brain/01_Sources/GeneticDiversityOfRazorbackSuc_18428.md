# Genetic Diversity of Razorback Sucker (Xyrauchen Texanus) as Determined by Restriction Endonuclease Analysis of Mitochondrial DNA

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #conservation-genetics

## Executive Summary

This is a research proposal submitted by [[Arizona State University]] to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] in January 1990 to study genetic diversity in [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations using [[Mitochondrial DNA]] analysis. Principal investigator Professor [[Thomas E. Dowling]] of the Department of Zoology proposed to employ [[Restriction Endonuclease Analysis]] to assess genetic variation in this species. The proposal followed telephone conversations and a meeting in Albuquerque between Dr. Dowling and Bureau representatives. Co-investigator Professor [[W. L. Minckley]] also participated in the project design.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]] inhabiting freshwater systems of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. This proposal represents an application of molecular genetic techniques to conservation biology, specifically using [[Restriction Endonuclease Analysis]] of [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) to characterize genetic diversity within the species. The methodology involves analyzing mtDNA variation patterns through restriction enzyme digestion, a standard approach in population genetics during this period. The research was designed to provide baseline genetic data relevant to conservation management decisions for this species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal addresses conservation concerns for [[Razorback Sucker]] populations in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Understanding genetic diversity through [[Mitochondrial DNA]] analysis provides critical information for species management, particularly for populations that may be fragmented or declining. The [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]]'s interest in funding this research reflects institutional recognition of the species' conservation status and the need for genetic data to inform management strategies. The collaboration between academic researchers at [[Arizona State University]] and federal resource managers demonstrates an integrated approach to conservation science.
