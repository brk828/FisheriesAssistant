## Executive Summary

This document is a **Station Profile** for the [[Fish Health Unit]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located near Lamar, Pennsylvania, within Region 5. Established in 1984 through a regional decision to centralize fish health activities, the Unit evolved from the Region 5 Hatchery Biologist Program into an independent project by 1987, under the direction of John C. Thoesen. Rather than focusing on a single fish species or taxon, the Unit functions as a **centralized diagnostic and regulatory laboratory** serving federal, state, and commercial aquaculture facilities across a thirteen-state area. Its core mandate includes fish health inspection, pathogen diagnostics, extension services, and broodstock/hatchery stock monitoring, operating under multiple regional and national fish health policy frameworks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Fish Health Unit's "niche" is functional/administrative rather than biological, situated within the broader fisheries management ecosystem of [[Region 5]] (a thirteen-state area of the northeastern and midwestern United States). Its operational scope includes:

- **Client base**: 16 federal facilities, 47 state facilities, and a limited number of commercial aquaculture operations.
- **Regulatory frameworks governing activity**:
  - U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service [[Fish Health Policy and Implementation Guidelines]]
  - [[New England Salmonid Health Guidelines]]
  - [[St. Croix Fish Health Policy]]
  - [[Great Lakes Fish Disease Control Policy and Model Program]]
  - [[Canadian Manual of Compliance]]
- **Target populations monitored**: Wild broodstock and various hatchery-reared stocks, with particular relevance to salmonid health in New England and Great Lakes basin fisheries.
- **Stakeholder network**: Department of Interior, other federal agencies, state and foreign fish health professionals, professional fishery societies, and the public — positioning the Unit as a node connecting local hatchery operations to national and international fish health management strategy.

## Key Findings

- The Fish Health Unit was established in **1984** as part of a regional decision to centralize fish health services, replacing a decentralized biologist program structure.
- It became a fully **independent project in June FY-87**, with John C. Thoesen promoted from Project Leader to Director.
- Core services include: fish health **inspections** (annual/semi-annual), **diagnostics** with treatment recommendations compliant with FDA/EPA/state law, **monitoring** of wild and hatchery fish health, and **extension/training** services for culturists, biologists, administrators, and educators.
- Budget trends (Operations, in $000) show growth from **$337.4K (1991)** to a projected **$422.0K (1995)**, with Maintenance costs rising sharply from **$0 (1991)** to **$50.2K (1995 est.)**.
- Staffing (FTE) remained stable at **7** from 1991–1993, increasing to **8** for 1994–1995 estimates.
- No cost recoverables from contributing agencies were reported across all listed years (1991–1995).
- The Unit's impact extends beyond Region 5, influencing **national and international fish health management strategies**, reflecting its role as a model for centralized fish disease diagnostic programs within the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
