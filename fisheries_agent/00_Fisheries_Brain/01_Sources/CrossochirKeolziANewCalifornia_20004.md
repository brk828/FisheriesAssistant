# Crossochir koelzi: A New Californian Surf-Fish of the Family Embiotocidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #embiotocidae

## Executive Summary

This 1933 Proceedings of the [[United States National Museum]] paper by Carl L. Hubbs describes a new genus and species of [[Embiotocidae]] (viviparous perches of the North Pacific), [[Crossochir Koelzi]]. The species had been misidentified for decades as [[Holconotus Rhodoterus]]. Its distinctiveness rests on a combination of dorsal spine, interneural, pectoral fin and gill raker characters, and on its coloration. The new species is known from central and southern California, and the name honours explorer [[Walter Koelz]].

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** Hubbs had prepared a monographic review of the Embiotocidae. In his 1918 revision, the species was keyed as *Holconotus rhodoterus*, a misidentification made because only *Crossochir* specimens, and not true *Holconotus*, were available. Earlier authors (e.g., [[Jordan and Gilbert]], [[Eigenmann]]) recorded *H. rhodoterus* from California localities, many of which, south of San Francisco, were in fact based on *C. koelzi*.

**Key diagnostic traits** (from the key to the Amphistichinae):
- Subfamily Amphistichinae; teeth unicuspid in two series per jaw.
- Dorsal spines extremely robust and shorter than the dorsal rays; anterior interneurals very strong with sharply expanded lateral keels.
- Pectoral fin shorter, with upper margin strongly curved downward; lower rays frayed and silky.
- Lower lip groove interrupted by a broad frenum (distinguishes it from *Crossochir*'s group versus *Hypocritichthys*-type lips in the key).
- Gill rakers 14 to 17 below angle of the first arch in the key diagnosis; the description gives the first-arch count as 9 (6 to 8) + 16 (14 to 17) = 25 (21 to 23).
- Body deep (depth about half standard length); anal fin rather long with 25 to 31 soft rays.
- Color: body speckled with brown flecks, trace of bars; fins without black markings. Life colour from San Diego specimens (Eigenmann and Eigenmann, 1890) suggests a brassy, scarlet-and-black-dotted body with reddish fins.

**Meristic and morphometric data** (holotype and paratypes, ranges in parentheses):
- Scales in lateral line: 66 (61 to 68).
- Dorsal rays XI (X or XI, usually X), 26 (24 to 28); anal rays III, 29 (25 to 31); pectoral rays 26 (25 to 29).
- Depth 1.8 (1.8 to 2.1) in standard length; head 3.4 (3.2 to 3.5) in standard length.
- Anal fin of males bears a modified ray (tenth to fourteenth) forming a triangular, serrated plate, as in the subfamily.

**Type material.** The holotype is USNM No. 26901 (198 mm to caudal), collected by Jordan at Santa Barbara in 1880. Paratypes are distributed among several institutions, including the [[United States National Museum]], [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]], [[Field Museum of Natural History]], and the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Like its close relatives *Holconotus rhodoterus* and *Amphistichus argenteus*, *C. koelzi* is essentially a surf-zone inhabitant, penetrating bays only to their more open portions.
- **Range:** Central and southern California, from Drakes Bay to San Diego. Its range approximately coincides with *A. argenteus* and overlaps *H. rhodoterus* near San Francisco.
- **Range correction:** The misidentification had produced erroneous range statements. *H. rhodoterus* is abundant along the surf of Oregon and Washington, not limited to San Francisco, and *A. argenteus* has no authentic records north of San Francisco.
- **Collection localities cited:** Santa Barbara, Monterey, Santa Cruz Island, Drakes Bay, San Diego, La Jolla (Scripps Institution Pier and surf), and San Simeon Bay.

## Key Findings

1. *Crossochir koelzi* is a new genus and species, the type and only known species of *Crossochir* (name meaning "fringed hand," referring to the frayed, silky lower pectoral rays).
2. The species was previously misidentified as *Holconotus rhodoterus* from 1878 onward, and the error persisted until Hubbs's 1928 correction.
3. Its closest relatives are *Holconotus* and *Amphistichus*; it differs from *Amphistichus* by lacking the frenum and having a more oblique mouth and deeper body, and from *Holconotus* by stronger, lower dorsal spines, more rounded spinous dorsal and keeled interneurals.
4. The range is central and southern California (Drakes Bay to San Diego). Previously reported northern records for *H. rhodoterus* and *A. argenteus* were corrected.
5. Eight paratype lots and the holotype were examined across multiple museum collections, with paratype lengths of 107 to 210 mm.
6. The name honours Walter Koelz for his studies on American coregonid fishes.
