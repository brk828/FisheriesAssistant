# Embryonic and Early Larval Development of the Tui Chub, Siphateles bicolor (Girard), from Eagle Lake, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #embryology #larval_development

## Executive Summary

This study documents the embryonic and early larval development of the [[Tui Chub]] (*Siphateles bicolor*) from [[Eagle Lake]], [[Lassen County]], California. Conducted in 1949 by Robert R. Harry at Stanford University while employed by the California Division of Fish and Game, this represents pioneering work as the complete early development had not been described for any native western North American cyprinid at that time. Approximately 20,000 fertilized eggs were obtained through artificial insemination and studied from 70 hours after fertilization to 12 hours after hatching. Development was tracked under suboptimal temperature conditions (45°F, approximately 20°F below normal), which affected normal progression but still yielded valuable developmental data.

## Background & Description

The [[Tui Chub]] is a cyprinid fish native to western North America. This study was conducted as part of a broader investigation into the entire life cycle of the species in [[Eagle Lake]], led by J. Bruce Kimsey of the California Division of Fish and Game. Prior to this work, only the postembryonic development of one western cyprinid species, the [[Venus Roach]] (*Hesperoleucus venustus*), had been described in detail by Fry (1936), with its embryonic development remaining completely unknown.

**Egg Characteristics**: Freshly stripped eggs are translucent, yellowish, spherical, and adhesive, measuring 1.8-2.0 mm in diameter—rather large for cyprinid eggs. The membrane is thick, tough, and fairly smooth with minute crenulations on the surface. The yolk contains numerous oil globules and has specific gravity considerably greater than fresh water, causing free eggs to sink quickly. Eggs are emitted as a sticky, fluid mass and adhere to objects upon contact with fresh water, though this adhesive character is soon lost.

**Collection Methods**: Ripe adults were caught in gill nets on the western side of [[Eagle Lake]] near Webb's resort over spawning grounds at approximately six feet depth. Females were stripped into a bowl containing plants, and eggs were artificially inseminated at 8 a.m. on June 24, 1949. Approximately 20,000 eggs were obtained and immediately transported to the [[Lake Almanor State Fish Hatchery]] on [[Clear Creek]], [[Lassen County]], arriving at noon the same day.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The natural spawning habitat for [[Tui Chub]] in [[Eagle Lake]] occurs at approximately six feet depth. The study notes that normal development temperature in [[Eagle Lake]] would be approximately 65°F, but experimental conditions maintained eggs at 45°F—approximately 20°F below normal. This temperature differential significantly affected developmental timing and outcomes.

Eggs were maintained in highly mineralized [[Eagle Lake]] water throughout the study, with water partially changed at frequent intervals. The suboptimal temperature conditions resulted in developmental abnormalities: after 100 hours, eggs at 45°F either remained dormant or continued growth with irregular cell development. Some eggs clumped together in large masses, forming the blastodermal cap but developing abnormally thereafter, with yolk becoming granular and shrunken.

A subset of 100 eggs removed three days after stripping and kept at room temperature (varying from 54°F to 84°F) developed rapidly, appearing almost fully formed within six days, though most died from fungus at that time. The surviving embryos developed the pigment pattern characteristic of the species but may have been too weakened by abnormal conditions to break out of eggs at the proper time.

## Key Findings

**Early Embryonic Development (70-100 hours)**:
- At 70 hours: Fully developed blastodermal cap forms a symmetrical dome, very large with outer surface forming gentle curve almost continuous with yolk outline; cells too small to distinguish; perivitelline space remains small throughout all developmental stages
- At 85-100 hours: Segmentation cavity develops beneath central area of blastodermal cap through thinning of central blastodermal wall; cavity distinguishable by looking down on upper surface of blastoderm
- At 100 hours: Segmentation cavity becomes narrower and slightly eccentric, marking first indication of main embryonic axis with thicker area marking posterior pole

**Mid-Embryonic Development (100-140 hours)**:
- Continued growth causes thinning of central blastodermal cap and thickening of peripheral germ ring, appearing as broad, slightly darker ring surrounding translucent area
- Embryonic shield is fairly narrow and somewhat wedge-shaped
- At 120 hours: Blastoderm covers half of yolk sphere; [[Kupffer's Vesicle]] appears imbedded in yolk at posterior end; metameric segmentation detected at middle of body; notochord becomes apparent
- At 136-140 hours: Blastopore closure occurs; eyes become visible; metameric segmentation well developed caudally; heart begins to pulsate below middle of left eye (dorsal view); auditory capsules barely visible as small oval vesicles behind eyes; [[Kupffer's Vesicle]] appears obsolete

**Late Embryonic Development (140-216 hours)**:
- At 170 hours: Blastopore closed; head markedly increased in size; pupils and lenses visible in eyes; otoliths formed; tail twitches side to side at frequent intervals, passing over snout and eyes; continuous fin fold clearly visible
- Day 9: Pigment begins appearing as several rows of conspicuous melanophores on dorsal side, particularly on head; eyes heavily pigmented; pigmentation extent increases during remainder of embryonic development; yolk reduced to less than half original size

**Early Larval Development (Day 12, post-hatching)**:
- Hatching occurred during night of twelfth day
- Larva exceptionally active, able to swim rapidly and effectively
- Yolk almost completely absorbed
- Auditory capsules enlarged; notochord cells visible along entire length
- Tail developing rays (ceratotrichia) and changing shape
- **Pigmentation Pattern**: Chromatophores concentrated along ventral midline of body (exclusive of gut); two irregular longitudinal rows of large melanophores along each side of dorsal midline; large triangular blotch of giant melanophores on nape; eye and lens solid black
- Pectoral fins very small with rays only slightly developed
- Anus positioned far posteriorly
- No special structures observed on head for breaking egg membrane
- Single hatched larva died approximately 12 hours after emergence

**Developmental Constraints**: The study acknowledges that circumstances caused egg development under conditions that did not lead to unquestionably normal results, with no opportunity to repeat work under more normal conditions. However, given the scarcity of recorded data on early fish development, particularly for western North American cyprinids, publication was deemed worthwhile despite incompleteness.
