# Norma Oficial Mexicana NOM-059-ECOL-1994: Species at Risk in Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is the official Mexican standard NOM-059-ECOL-1994, published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación on 16 May 1994 by the [[Secretaría de Desarrollo Social]] through the [[Instituto Nacional de Ecología]]. It determines the terrestrial and aquatic flora and fauna species and subspecies that are in danger of extinction (P), threatened (A), rare (R), or subject to special protection (Pr), with particular attention to those endemic to the Mexican Republic and waters under federal jurisdiction. It also sets out the rules for protecting, collecting, breeding and using these taxa and their habitats. The standard is mandatory, supports the General Law of Ecological Equilibrium and Environmental Protection, and was developed with a broad consultation process involving government agencies, universities, research centres and NGOs.

## Background & Description

The standard was drafted as a revision of the project NOM-PA-CRN-001/93, published 2 August 1993. After a 90-day public comment period, and analysis by the Comité Consultivo Nacional de Normalización para la Protección Ambiental, it was renamed NOM-059-ECOL-1994 and approved by the Comisión Nacional de Normalización on 9 March 1994. It was signed on 28 April 1994 by the president of the Instituto Nacional de Ecología, [[Julia Carabias Lillo]].

Key definitions (Section 3) include:
- **Species and subspecies endemic**: distribution confined to the Mexican Republic and federal waters.
- **Habitat**: the specific physical environment and biotic community occupied by an organism or species.
- **Endangered (en peligro de extinción)**: populations or range drastically reduced, with biological viability at risk.
- **Threatened (amenazada)**: likely to become endangered if threatening factors continue; treated as equivalent to "vulnerable".
- **Rare (rara)**: biologically viable but naturally very scarce or restricted in range or habitat.
- **Subject to special protection (sujeta a protección especial)**: limited harvesting or closed seasons to allow recovery or protect associated species.

Scope (Section 2) covers possession and use of specimens from captive breeding or the wild, scientific collection from the natural environment, and conservation or use of habitats where listed taxa occur.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listed taxa span plants and fungi, fauna (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, and invertebrates), and reflect a high level of endemism and habitat diversity across Mexico, including arid and semi-arid zones, tropical forests, coral reefs, lagoons and freshwater systems. The aquatic component relevant to aquatic ecology includes:

- **Fishes**: a long list of endemic freshwater species such as [[Gila Ditaenia]] (charalito sonorense), [[Totoaba]] (*Totoaba macdonaldi*), [[Shiffia Bilineata]], [[Xiphophorus Clemenciae]] and [[Cyprinodon Alvarezi]]-type pupfishes, Mexcalpique and charal silversides (*Chirostoma* spp.), and the Yaqui and Conchos cyprinids.
- **Marine and estuarine invertebrates**: coral species (*Acropora cervicornis*, *Acropora palmata*), sea fans and gorgonians, pearl oysters, the Pismo clam (*Tivella stultorum*), and cave-dwelling crustaceans such as *Typhlatya* spp. and *Mexipyrgus* snails.
- **Marine reptiles and mammals**: sea turtles (green turtle, hawksbill, Kemp's ridley, loggerhead, leatherback), and cetaceans such as the vaquita (*Phocoena sinus*) and the blue whale.

The standard also addresses coastal habitats such as mangroves (*Rhizophora mangle*, *Avicennia germinans*, *Laguncularia racemosa*, *Conocarpus erecta*) and lagoon ecosystems.

## Key Findings

- **Scope of listings**: The standard lists several hundred taxa across flora, fauna and aquatic invertebrates, each assigned a category (P, A, R or Pr). Full lists are summarized here rather than reproduced; the entries were not individually counted in the source text.
- **Aquatic highlights**: Freshwater and marine fish, crustaceans, molluscs and corals feature heavily. Notable endangered fishes include the totoaba, the Yaqui and Conchos species, and several *Xiphophorus* and *Cyprinodon* endemics.
- **Marine turtles**: Green, hawksbill, Kemp's ridley, loggerhead and leatherback turtles are all listed, reflecting threats from fisheries and nest exploitation.
- **Permitted uses**:
  - Scientific collection from the wild is allowed in quantities authorized by the competent authority, but specimens may not be commercialized (6.1).
  - Specimens may be taken from the wild for breeding stock or propagules in registered reproduction units, aimed at recovering species in nature, but may not be sold (6.2).
  - Commercial use of rare and special-protection species under closed or open seasons must primarily come from authorized reproduction units where captive breeding exists (6.3).
  - Harvesting in the wild without closed season is permitted only at rates set by the authority on the basis of population studies (6.4).
  - Habitat use must ensure conservation of listed taxa (6.5).
- **Authority**: Authorizations for extraction or commercialization of terrestrial taxa fall under the Secretaría de Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos; aquatic taxa are authorized by the Secretaría de Pesca. Enforcement is shared among the Procuraduría Federal de Protección al Ambiente and these secretariats (Sections 6.6 and 7).
- **Penalties**: Non-compliance is sanctioned under the General Law of Ecological Equilibrium, the Fisheries Law, the Forestry Law, the Federal Hunting Law, and the Penal Code (Section 8).
- **International alignment**: The standard partially coincides with the United States Endangered Species Act (1973) and Canada's Species at Risk Act (1992) (Section 10).
- **Effective date**: The standard enters into force the day after publication in the Diario Oficial (Section 11).

The same issue of the Diario Oficial includes unrelated items: a Secretaría de Energía, Minas e Industria Paraestatal list of mining land-freedom declarations (dated 13 May 1994) and a draft health standard (PROY-NOM-SSA1-1993) on packaged water, which are outside the scope of this note.
