# Loss of Lake Mohave Bonytail Chub

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum, dated 1993, reports the loss of most endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) captured during a May 1993 netting survey of [[Lake Mohave]]. Of 16 fish netted, 14 were held at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and shipped to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]]. Losses came from handling trauma, a fungal outbreak, and a suspected bacterial infection. The memo attributes the failure mainly to the absence of a written protocol for capturing and handling endangered species, and to poor communication during a weekend treatment gap. A new handling protocol was scheduled for submission by June 18, 1993.

## Background & Description

- **Species status:** The bonytail is listed as endangered. Individuals are collected annually from Lake Mohave as potential broodstock for Dexter NFH&TC. Historical catches were very small, four or fewer per year.
- **Broodstock origin:** The original Dexter stock derived from 10 fish. Around 1987, it was proposed that any bonytail caught in Lake Mohave netting surveys be sent to Dexter, since these wild survivors could increase the genetic variation of the broodstock.
- **Reproductive status:** None of the fish captured since 1987 had spawned. All had died either at Dexter or at Willow Beach.
- **Survey timing:** The netting was an [[Arizona State University]] project, conducted around the end of the school year.
- **Personnel:** Chuck Minckley (USFWS), Paul Marsh (Arizona State University), and Kirk Young ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) led the netting. Glenn Green of Willow Beach Hatchery held and treated the fish. Roger Harman and Philip Gesa transported them to Dexter. George Divine coordinated the effort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Capture and holding sequence (May 1993):**

- **Netting:** Trammel nets were set on selected points in Lake Mohave on the evening of May 10 and pulled for the last time on May 15. Nets were checked every morning and evening.
- **Field handling:** Captured fish were moved in a live well to camp, measured, weighed, and photographed, then placed in a Nalgene tank for transport. No stress-reduction chemicals were used during transport.
- **Temperature acclimation:** Fish were tempered to hatchery water over one hour. The source's stated temperature values are garbled in the scan, so the exact range cannot be confirmed.
- **Condition on arrival:** The collector reported the fish looked healthy. Hatchery staff reported that the net had badly marked them.
- **Treatment:** Formalin flow-through treatments at 1:6000 were given May 11 to 14 to reduce fungal risk. A planned treatment gap followed (see Key Findings).
- **Disease signs:** Eight fish were heavily fungused by May 16. Seven were painted with potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) before treatment was halted as over-stressing them. Dr. Carmichael supplied a stress-reduction formula for tank holding.

## Key Findings

**Survey results**

- 16 bonytail were netted.
- One fin-marked fish was PIT-tagged and released back into the lake.
- The other 14 were taken to Willow Beach for holding and treatment.

**Mortality and losses**

- 2 fish died during roughly two weeks of holding at Willow Beach.
- 12 fish were shipped to Dexter on May 24 to 27. One was lost en route.
- 7 more died after arrival at Dexter, leaving 4 fish in the raceway at the time of writing.
- Lost fish did not show fungus. They sloughed necrotic tissue from the dorsal portion of the caudal peduncle, which suggests either a serious bacterial infection or damage to nerves and blood vessels from handling.

**Causes identified**

- **Protocol gap:** No written protocol existed for capture and handling of endangered species. The one existing rule, that no fish be moved to a hatchery without examination by a hatchery biologist, was ignored.
- **Communication failure:** The formalin treatment was not continued on May 15, 16, or 17. The treating technician took the weekend off and did not brief the weekend duty staff, so the treatment lapsed.
- **Handling stress:** The memo states that overhandling, poor handling, and lack of communication together created the problem.

**Decisions and plans**

- On May 24 the hatchery crew judged the fish fit enough to ship. Dexter staff doubted their condition but judged they had a better chance of survival than at Willow Beach, so the fish were shipped.
- A protocol for handling all endangered species was to be submitted to the Regional Office by June 18, 1993, and distributed by June 25.
