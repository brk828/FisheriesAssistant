#ichthyology #marine-biology #Petromyzontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a **taxonomic controversy exchange** (Comment and Reply) between Vladykov & Kott and Reeve M. Bailey, published in *Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences* (1982), concerning the classification and nomenclature of holarctic lampreys (family [[Petromyzontidae]]). The dispute centers on: (1) the generic placement of [[Lampetra zanandreai]] (a northern Italian brook lamprey), (2) the validity of numerous newly described [[Lethenteron]] and [[Entosphenus]] species, (3) the taxonomic status of the subgenus/genus [[Okkelbergia]], (4) a broader "splitting versus lumping" debate over genus-level classification within [[Petromyzontidae]], and (5) the correct scientific names for the American brook lamprey and least brook lamprey. No new field or laboratory data are presented; the paper is a synthesis and critique of dental, myomere, and urogenital-papilla characters used in lamprey systematics.

## Background & Description

Key diagnostic characters debated in this exchange, primarily related to oral disc dentition and body metrics used to delimit lamprey genera/species:

- **Posterior circumoral teeth ("posterials"/mandibular labials):**
  - Presence in the posterior field of the oral disk is a key character separating [[Lampetra (sensu stricto)]] (posterials absent) from [[Lethenteron]] (posterials present, typically forming a full row).
  - Vladykov & Kott maintain that *L. zanandreai* consistently lacks posterials; Bailey argues, citing Zanandrea's (1958, 1961) stained material, that posterials are present but often embedded in disk mucosa and easily overlooked in unstained, formalin-preserved specimens.
  - Horny lamprey teeth reportedly do not dissolve in formalin or alcohol, making claimed "disappearance" of teeth after preservation biologically implausible — a key point of contention.

- **Endolateral (inner lateral circumoral) tooth formula:**
  - *Lampetra* (s.s.): middle inner lateral typically **tricuspid** (formula 2–3–2).
  - *Lethenteron*: all three inner laterals typically **bicuspid** (formula 2–2–2).
  - This character is variable and considered unreliable at genus level by Bailey; in *L. zanandreai*, Zanandrea's larger sample (232 specimens) found 89% with 2 cusps on at least one side — fitting the *Lethenteron* pattern rather than the original *Lampetra* description.

- **Trunk myomere counts:**
  - American brook lamprey ([[Lethenteron wilderi]] / [[Lampetra appendix]]): 65–74 myomeres.
  - Least brook lamprey ([[Lampetra aepyptera]]): 50–59 myomeres.

- **Urogenital papilla length (spawning males), as % of branchial length:**
  - American brook lamprey: short, mean 9.4% (range 4.5–16.7%).
  - Least brook lamprey: long, mean 38.8% (range 25.0–57.7%).
  - Bailey's independent measurements on Michigan specimens gave 20.7–29.4% (mean 23.5%) — intermediate and substantially longer than Vladykov & Kott's figures, illustrating measurement/interpretive disagreement central to the naming dispute.

- **Velar tentacle morphology:** used by Vladykov & Kott (1976c) as an additional taxonomic character supporting new species descriptions within [[Entosphenus]] and [[Lethenteron]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Parasitic vs. non-parasitic ("satellite") life histories:** Petromyzontidae comprises polyspecific genera containing both anadromous/parasitic (blood/tissue-feeding) stem species and derived, non-parasitic "satellite" species that lack a feeding phase after metamorphosis.
- **Satellite species concept** (Vladykov & Kott 1979b): non-parasitic forms morphologically resemble their presumed parasitic ancestor but arose independently multiple times from different parasitic stem lineages rather than from one another — implying a reticulate, non-treelike phylogeny best represented by "circles and connecting lines" rather than a standard cladogram.
- **Reduced selection pressure hypothesis** (Hardisty & Potter 1971; Potter 1980): satellite (non-parasitic) species show more variable dentition than parasitic stem species because the functional/selective constraints on feeding dentition are relaxed after loss of the parasitic phase.
- **Geographic/habitat distributions cited:**
  - *L. zanandreai*: endemic to the Po River basin and northern Italian plain streams (Europe only).
  - *Lethenteron meridionale*: freshwater streams draining to the Gulf of Mexico.
  - *L. matsubarai*: Hokkaido, Japan.
  - *L. alaskense*: northwestern North America.
  - *Entosphenus folletti*, *E. hubbsi*: Klamath River system and south-central California.
  - *E. similis*: Klamath River, California/Oregon.
  - *Lampetra aepyptera*: eastern United States, no closer to southeastern Pennsylvania (Chesapeake Bay drainage) than southwestern Pennsylvania (Ohio River drainage).
  - American brook lamprey: sympatric historically with *Petromyzon marinus* in the Providence, RI, and lower Hudson River, NY region — used as key biogeographic evidence for identifying DeKay's (1842) type description.
  - Bailey's critique of Vladykov & Kott's broad *Lampetra* (sensu lato) notes it would span three continents (North America, Asia, Europe), which he considers biogeographically implausible since true *Lampetra* (s.s.) is absent from Asia — an argument Vladykov & Kott actually use *against* Bailey's lumped classification.

## Key Findings

- **Disputed genus placement of *L. zanandreai***: Vladykov (1955) and Vladykov & Kott (1982) retain it in *Lampetra* (s.s.); Hubbs & Potter (1971), Potter (1980), and Bailey (1980, 1982) place it in [[Lethenteron]] based on Zanandrea's (1958, 1961) dentition data. Bailey argues Vladykov & Kott's rejection of Zanandrea's findings amounts to an unsubstantiated denial of his staining-based observations, given they did not attempt comparable histological/staining techniques.
- **New species validity contested**: Bailey treats numerous Vladykov & Kott–described species (*L. folletti*, *L. hubbsi*, *L. similis*, *Lethenteron meridionale*, *L. alaskense*, *L. matsubarai*) as merely "nominal species," while Potter (1980) accepts their validity. Later authors were split: Hubbs et al. (1979) flagged the validity of localized California forms as needing critical appraisal; Robins et al. (1980) recognized *hubbsi* and *pacifica* but not *folletti*, *alaskensis*, or *similis*.
- **Okkelbergia status**: Vladykov & Kott (1976a) synonymize [[Okkelbergia]] with [[Lampetra]], finding no morphological/anatomical distinction; Potter (1980) agrees. Bailey retains *Okkelbergia* as a valid subgenus (applied solely to *L. aepyptera*), regarding it as the most degenerate/specialized lamprey lineage with uncertain phylogenetic affinities — possibly convergent with, rather than derived from, the *Lampetra* (s.s.) line.
- **Splitting vs. lumping**: Vladykov & Kott recognize **8 genera** in Petromyzontidae; Bailey recognizes only **4 genera**, subsuming *Lampetra*, *Okkelbergia*, *Lethenteron*, *Entosphenus*, *Tetrapleurodon*, and *Eudontomyzon* as six subgenera under a broad *Lampetra* (sensu lato). Each side accuses the other's scheme of obscuring true relationships (heterogeneous grouping vs. unresolved subgeneric interrelationships).
- **Nomenclature resolution for brook lampreys** (per Vladykov & Kott 1982):
  - American brook lamprey → recommended as **[[Lethenteron wilderi]]** (Gage, 1896), rejecting *Lampetra lamottenii* (Lesueur) as unidentifiable and inapplicable.
  - Least brook lamprey → recommended as **[[Lampetra aepyptera]]** (Abbott, 1860), by convention despite technical priority of *L. lamottenii* (Lesueur, 1827), to avoid nomenclatural confusion; supported by Fowler's (1901, 1908) redescription of the aepyptera type with matching myomere counts.
  - Bailey (Reply) instead concludes, based on re-examination of urogenital papilla length and DeKay's (1842) original figure/description, that the American brook lamprey should be called **[[Lampetra appendix]]** (DeKay, 1842), disputing suppression of this name.
- **Procedural/nomenclatural note**: The International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature ruled the correct family-group stem is *Petromyzont-*, formalizing **[[Petromyzontidae]]** as the family name — a point both parties came to accept, resolving a prior disagreement from the SLIS symposium volume.
- **Underlying methodological critique** (Vladykov & Kott's closing point): taxonomic conclusions drawn solely from literature review without direct specimen examination risk error — a criticism explicitly leveled at Bailey (1980) regarding his reliance on Zanandrea (1958) alone rather than firsthand study of *L. zanandreai* material.
