# Trip Report: April 1995 Fish Survey of the ASU Research Park

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from [[Arizona State University]]'s Center for Environmental Studies reports a fish survey of the [[ASU Research Park]] in [[Tempe Arizona]], conducted 6–7 April 1995 by Brian Bagley, Terry Inman and Patti Sowka. Seven entanglement nets sampled the park's three ponds overnight. The catch comprised 277 fish of 10 species, all previously recorded at the park. The survey captured three endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and one previously tagged [[Bonytail Chub]]. All fish were released. A follow-up survey was tentatively planned for autumn 1995.

## Background & Description

- **Survey dates:** 06–07 April 1995 (nets set 1900–2100 hrs on 6 April; retrieved 0500–1000 hrs on 7 April).
- **Gear:** Seven [[Entanglement Net]]s deployed across the park's three ponds.
- **Personnel:** [[Brian Bagley]], Terry Inman, Patti Sowka.
- **Document type:** Interoffice trip report addressed "To: Interested Parties," dated 3 May 1995.
- **Note on scan quality:** The source is OCR-scanned. Several values (tag number, one female razorback length, one bonytail length, and the origin of the bonytail) are partly illegible and are flagged below. The second page consists almost entirely of scan debris and contains no usable content.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The ASU Research Park ponds support a mixed assemblage dominated by introduced and common warmwater species. The native or conservation-priority fishes in the catch, [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], are federally endangered Colorado River basin natives. Their presence in urban retention ponds makes the park's population notable for monitoring. The report does not describe water chemistry, habitat structure, or invertebrate communities.

The non-native or non-native-origin species in the catch include [[Blue Tilapia]], [[Common Carp]] and [[Green Sunfish]]. The report does not discuss their ecological effects.

## Key Findings

**Catch composition (277 individuals, 10 species)**
- [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) was the most abundant species, at 60% of the total catch.
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) was second, at 24%.
- Eight remaining species each made up 9% or less of the catch: [[Bluegill]], [[Blue Tilapia]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], Razorback Sucker, Bonytail Chub, [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Bass]].
- The report does not give exact per-species counts for the eight minor species.
- All previously recorded species were captured again, so the park's known fish assemblage was confirmed.

**Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**
- Three individuals were captured: two males (TL 500 mm and 530 mm) and one female (TL partly illegible in the scan).
- All appeared healthy, with no fungus, scars or tumors. One male had a cloudy eye.
- Both males and the female were in breeding condition (tuberculate and releasing gametes).
- None had been tagged previously.

**Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*)**
- One female was captured, with a TL reported as "a21 mm" (partly illegible, possibly 421 mm).
- The fish carried a previous tag (number partly illegible in the scan).
- It was last caught on 31 January 1994 at [[Palm Lake]], when it measured 378 mm. Its origin is partly illegible in the scan but appears to be a hatchery.

**Absent species**
- No [[Colorado Squawfish]] were captured. The authors attributed this to the small sampling effort and the type of gear used, not to confirmed absence.

**Management actions and plans**
- All captured fish were released.
- A follow-up survey of the ASU Research Park is tentatively scheduled for autumn 1995. Interested parties were invited to participate.
