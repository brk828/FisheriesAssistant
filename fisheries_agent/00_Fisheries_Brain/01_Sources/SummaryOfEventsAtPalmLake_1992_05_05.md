## Executive Summary
This memo documents an emergency fish salvage operation conducted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) at **Palm Lake**, Hassayampa River Preserve, from April 20–24, 1992, in response to a fish kill event. The kill was attributed to severe **hypoxia** (critically low dissolved oxygen, DO), described by AGFD water quality staff as a "traditional summer fish kill." The primary conservation concern was the salvage of the federally endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), with secondary observations of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*). Over four days, staff used trammel nets, seines, cast nets, and electroshocking to recover surviving individuals for transport to the Page Springs Hatchery.

## Background & Description
Direct biometric data was limited to **Total Length (TL) measurements** of 20 salvaged [[Bonytail Chub]] individuals transported to Page Springs Hatchery on April 21, 1992 (in mm):

> 316, 335, 474, 328, 351, 306, 273, 382, 396, 284, 252, 295, 301, 263, 275, 322, 423, 360, 270, 345

- **Size range:** 252 mm – 474 mm TL
- **Approximate mean:** ~327 mm TL
- This range suggests a population of sub-adult to adult bonytail chub, with the 474 mm individual representing a notably large specimen for the species.

No morphological data was recorded for [[Razorback Sucker]] (single visual observation only) or for the 83 [[Gila Topminnow]] collected, beyond species-level identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat structure:** Palm Lake is a shallow artificial impoundment with a **maximum depth of only 8.5 feet**, densely lined with cattails along most of the shoreline — a structural factor that likely allowed fish to evade seine nets by escaping beneath them.
- **Connectivity:** Palm Lake is connected to upper and lower **spring-fed pools**, separated by a **fish barrier** that was confirmed to be intact and functioning to exclude invasive [[Mosquitofish]] from the spring pools.
- **Refugia:** The spring pools served as the sole habitat for [[Gila Topminnow]], with 83 individuals collected exclusively from the lower pool (192 seconds of backpack electroshocking); the upper pool yielded no fish after 210 seconds of shocking.
- **Water chemistry conditions during the kill:**
  - Dissolved oxygen (DO) fluctuated between **0.2–3.1 ppm** across the event, with most readings well below levels required to support fish (typically <5 ppm is stressful for most species).
  - pH ranged from 7.7–8.0.
  - Conductivity: 1190 µS.
  - Water temperature ranged from 15°C (early morning) to 21.5°C (afternoon).
- **Contributing factor:** A water sample revealed **dense, unidentified zooplankton** blooms, hypothesized to be contributing to oxygen depletion (likely via high biological oxygen demand or diel respiration dynamics), consistent with a [[Hypoxia|hypoxic]] eutrophication-type event.
- **Species interactions:** The intact fish barrier highlights the ecological importance of physical barriers in protecting sensitive native species like [[Gila Topminnow]] from invasive [[Mosquitofish]] predation/competition.

## Key Findings
- **Cause of fish kill:** Diagnosed as a hypoxic event ("traditional summer fish kill"), confirmed by AGFD water quality specialist Marc Dahlberg, who validated the calibration and accuracy of the DO meter.
- **DO extremes:** Readings as low as **0.2–0.3 ppm** were recorded in mid-lake waters, both at the surface and at depth (4 ft), indicating lake-wide oxygen collapse rather than localized stratification alone.
- **Fish behavior:** Surface "piping" behavior (gulping air at the surface) was observed in 50–80 fish during early morning hours (0615), a classic hypoxia stress response; this behavior subsided by 0900, likely due to increased surface agitation, sunlight, and temperature affecting surface oxygen microlayers.
- **Salvage results (cumulative):**
  - Total live [[Bonytail Chub]] salvaged and transported to Page Springs Hatchery: **20**
  - Additional bonytail chub captured post-transport (April 24): **2** (one revived after being found floating but alive)
  - Bonytail chub mortalities recovered: **≥10** (documented across multiple net checks and floating specimens)
  - [[Gila Topminnow]] collected from spring pool refugia: **83**
  - Single [[Razorback Sucker]] observed (not captured)
  - Crayfish were the only organisms captured in minnow traps set for topminnow/pupfish in the spring pool.
- **Gear efficacy:** Electroshocking, cast nets, and bag seines were largely ineffective in the open lake due to dense cattail cover and shallow depth; the **trammel net used in a seining arc** by canoe was the most effective method for capturing bonytail chub.
- **Conservation significance:** This event underscores the vulnerability of small, isolated lake populations of endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] to acute water quality crashes, and highlights the critical role of barrier-protected spring refugia in sustaining populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] amid the threat of invasive [[Mosquitofish]].
