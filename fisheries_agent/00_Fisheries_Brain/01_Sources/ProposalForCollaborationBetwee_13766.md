# Collaborative Research Proposal for Cuatro Cienegas Cichlids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cichlidae #endemic-species #conservation #research-proposal

## Executive Summary

This 1988 letter from W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) to Salvador Contreras Balderas (Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon) proposes collaborative research on the endemic cichlid fishes of [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico. The proposal outlines a comprehensive study combining genetic, morphometric, and ecological approaches to investigate these unique fishes, which represent either a polymorphic species or a species flock. The research is deemed urgent due to reports of African cichlid introductions that could endanger the endemic fauna. Field studies are proposed for July-August 1988, with participation from four ASU faculty members and UANL collaborators.

## Background & Description

The endemic cichlids of [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] consist of at least three morphological types in addition to the common [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] (Rio Grande cichlid). These fishes were discovered in the 1940s and announced by D. W. Taylor and Minckley in 1946 (Pacific Discovery 19: 19-22). James F. LaBounty analyzed the group's morphology in his 1974 Ph.D. dissertation (Arizona State University) but did not publish formal descriptions.

Subsequent studies by Sage and Selander (1975, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 72: 4669-4673) and Kornfield and collaborators developed the thesis that [[Cuatro Cienegas]] endemic cichlids represent a single polymorphic species. Kornfield and Taylor (1983, Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington 96: 253-269) named this species [[Cichlasoma minckleyi]]. However, Minckley (1978, 1984) considered the [[Cuatro Cienegas]] cichlids a [[Species Flock]] rather than a single polymorphic species, a view supported by Williams et al. (1985).

The warm, crystalline waters of [[Cuatro Cienegas]] springs allow year-round field study, providing exceptional research opportunities. The co-occurrence of diverse morphotypes with pronounced resource and space partitioning makes this system important for understanding broad evolutionary problems.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] contains multiple aquatic habitats including:

- **Laguna Santa Tecla**: inhabited by [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] alone
- **Laguna San Pablo**: where endemic forms co-occur with [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]]
- **El Mojarral, Pozos de la Becerra, and Laguna Anteojo systems**: where all three endemic morphs are syntopic and [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] is absent
- Additional localities where one or another endemic morph is absent

The basin's isolation and unique spring systems have facilitated the evolution of endemic forms. However, the reported introduction of African cichlids poses an alarming conservation threat. If African species spread, the endemic cichlids and potentially the entire fauna may become endangered.

Minckley has worked in the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] since 1958 and co-authored or authored more than 15 papers on its biota. Paul C. Marsh edited a symposium on the biota (Arizona-Nevada Academy of Science 19: 1-90).

## Key Findings

### Proposed Research Framework

The proposal outlines three major research areas:

**I. Genetic Studies**: Using advanced biochemical techniques to determine divergences and similarities among the various kinds and their relationships to other Mexican cichlids. Preliminary sampling planned for summer 1988 from 10 localities:

- [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] samples from [[Rio Sabinas System]] (remote baseline/control)
- Samples from [[Rio Salado de los Nadadores]] near Sacramento, Coahuila (to assess gene flow from [[Cuatro Cienegas]] endemics into [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] populations)
- Samples within [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] from multiple lagoon systems
- At least three additional localities where one or another endemic morph is absent

At least 10 adult, readily identifiable fish to be sampled at each locality. Muscle, liver, brain, and eyeball tissues to be frozen immediately with dry ice for protein electrophoresis and mitochondrial DNA analyses. Any intermediate specimens to be sampled additionally.

**II. Biometric Studies**: Re-assessment and re-analysis of data collected by Dr. James F. LaBounty already underway. Careful tissue removal will allow preservation of specimen remains for additional biometric studies, enabling direct comparisons between biochemical and morphological data. Preparation of skeletal material anticipated to facilitate osteological studies. All preserved specimens to be deposited at UANL in Monterrey for distribution on loan.

**III. Ecological Studies**: Detailed observations including assessment of breeding status, food habits, parasites, growth trends, and other biological parameters for all collected specimens. Underwater observations planned in all feasible habitats. Review of published and unpublished ecological information underway, to result in published report with complete bibliography.

### Proposed ASU Research Team

Four faculty and professional staff from Arizona State University:

- **Michael E. Douglas, Ph.D.** (Curator of Collections, Department of Zoology): morphometric, biometric, and computer expertise; specialist in cryptic or difficult taxonomic groups
- **Thomas E. Dowling, Ph.D.** (Assistant Professor, Department of Zoology): expert in protein electrophoresis and mitochondrial DNA technology for systematic and population genetic problems in fishes
- **Paul C. Marsh, Ph.D.** (Center for Environmental Studies): ecological and basic biological studies; editor of [[Cuatro Cienegas]] biota symposium; desert fish ecology specialist
- **W. L. Minckley, Ph.D.** (Professor, Department of Zoology): worked in [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] since 1958; ecological and morphological studies

### Collaborative Framework

Proposed work as research team under UANL auspices with:

- Joint publication in Spanish and English
- Collaborative analysis, interpretation, and presentation of results
- ASU to provide field assistance, collection/preservation equipment, biochemistry facilities and chemicals, advanced computer capabilities and support personnel

Target field period: July 29 (Friday) through August 3 (Friday), 1988, utilizing local motel housing. At least one day reserved for detailed planning of cooperative research program.

### Conservation Assessment

The field survey will allow assessment of African cichlid distribution, population sizes, and potential impacts on endemic species, addressing the urgent conservation concern raised in the proposal's introduction.
