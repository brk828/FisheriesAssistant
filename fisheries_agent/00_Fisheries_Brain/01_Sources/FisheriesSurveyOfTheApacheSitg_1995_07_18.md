# Fisheries Survey of the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest — Eagle Creek (Seventh Trip Report)

## Executive Summary
This report documents the seventh fisheries survey trip conducted by Arizona State University researchers on **[[Eagle Creek]]**, within the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest, Arizona, on July 18-19, 1995. Four localities were sampled via backpack electrofishing to assess distribution and abundance of native and non-native fish species. Eleven species were collected total, with native cyprinids and catostomids—particularly **[[Speckled Dace]]**—dominating catches. The imperiled **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) population appeared stable and consistent with prior-year survey results. Non-native **[[Rainbow Trout]]** were present but small (indicating in-stream reproduction), and crayfish (non-native, likely *Orconectes* or *Procambarus* sp.) were noted in high abundance at all sites, raising concern over predation pressure on native fishes.

## Background & Description
Specific morphometric data were not reported; however, qualitative field notes include:
- **Loach Minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and **Speckled Dace** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) collected at the FS Road 217 crossing were noted to be predominantly **large individuals displaying bright breeding coloration**, suggesting active spawning condition at time of sampling.
- **Rainbow Trout** collected were described as **small**, interpreted as evidence of natural in-stream reproduction rather than solely stocking-derived individuals.
- No length/weight data tables were preserved in the extracted OCR; only specimen abundance counts by locality are available (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**[[Eagle Creek]]** is a perennial desert/montane stream within the Gila River drainage, characterized by:
- **Substrate**: predominantly cobble, with gradients from small rocks/sand (upper reaches) to gravel/silt (lower reaches near Phelps Dodge Pump Station).
- **Channel morphology**: mix of riffle (30-60%), run, and pool habitats; stream widths ranged ~6-10 m, depths ~0.2-0.8 m depending on site and habitat unit (riffle vs. pool).
- **Riparian vegetation**: cottonwoods, alders, willows, and sycamores providing bank stabilization and shade, more developed at Honeymoon Campground than at Sheep Wash Gauge.
- **Elevation range sampled**: 4680 ft (Sheep Wash Gauge) to 5240 ft (FS Road 217 crossing).

**Community structure**: Native fish (**[[Longfin Dace]]**, **[[Speckled Dace]]**, **[[Roundtail Chub]]**, **[[Sonora Sucker]]**, **[[Desert Sucker]]**, **[[Loach Minnow]]**) dominated the assemblage numerically and were "found predominantly in the streams sampled." Non-native species present included **[[Rainbow Trout]]**, **[[Smallmouth Bass]]**, **[[Common Carp]]**, and **[[Red Shiner]]**, along with introduced crayfish observed at all four localities—most abundant at Honeymoon Campground.

**Species co-occurrence by locality**:
- *Honeymoon Campground*: 5 species, dace-dominated.
- *FS Road 217 Crossing*: Loach Minnow, Speckled Dace, Rainbow Trout co-occurring in fast riffle habitat with breeding-condition individuals.
- *Sheep Wash Gauge*: Longfin Dace, Speckled Dace, Sonora Sucker, Desert Sucker; fewer crayfish than upper sites.
- *Below Phelps Dodge Pump Station*: 7 species collected, Speckled Dace most numerous; includes non-natives Smallmouth Bass, Carp, Red Shiner, Yellow Bullhead.

## Key Findings
- **Species richness**: 11 total fish species documented across the drainage.
- **Dominant native species**: **Speckled Dace** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) was "by far the most abundant" species overall, with counts of 249 (Honeymoon Campground), 447 (FS Rd 217), and 111 (Sheep Wash) individuals encountered during electrofishing passes.
- **Loach Minnow status**: Population at FS Road 217 crossing (12 individuals, 10 preserved) considered stable and consistent with prior year's capture of ten specimens at the same site — a positive indicator for this federally listed species.
- **Desert Sucker & Speckled Dace populations**: Both assessed as **stable** based on catch consistency across sites.
- **Non-native trout reproduction**: Small-sized **Rainbow Trout** captured suggest active reproduction within Eagle Creek, a concern given potential predation/competition with native fishes.
- **Crayfish infestation**: Non-native crayfish were "far too numerous," especially in upper Eagle Creek near Honeymoon Campground, flagged as a management concern due to possible predation on native fish (eggs/larvae) and competition for invertebrate prey resources.
- **Sampling effort**: Electrofishing effort ranged from 886 seconds (Sheep Wash Gauge) to 2180 seconds (FS Road 217 Crossing) using a Smith-Root Model 15-A backpack electrofisher.
- **Preservation protocol**: Up to 30 specimens per species per locality were preserved as vouchers; only 10 **Loach Minnow** were preserved from a single locality, reflecting conservation-sensitive handling of this species.
- **Management implication**: Authors explicitly recommend that the crayfish and non-native fish issues "should be addressed" due to risk to native fish assemblages, reinforcing this survey's role in ongoing monitoring of the **[[Gila River Basin]]** native fish community.
