## Executive Summary

This study, conducted by Dean Blinn (NAU) and NPS/GRCA researchers, investigates the effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on aquatic trophic relations in the [[Colorado River]] corridor through [[Grand Canyon National Park]], Arizona. While the paper is framed around fisheries management (via the Environmental Impact Statement, or EIS, process for dam operations), the primary empirical focus is on **aquatic macroinvertebrates** — chironomids, simuliids, gastropods, oligochaetes, and amphipods — as the base of the food web supporting trout and native fish populations. The research characterizes standing crop, species composition, phenology, and habitat requirements of macroinvertebrates across ten sites from Lees Ferry to Diamond Creek, and experimentally tests the impacts of fluctuating hydropower discharges on primary producers (*Cladophora*, periphyton, *Oscillatoria*) and primary consumers. Findings reveal a strong downstream gradient in productivity, tributary-driven "step-down" effects, and substantial desiccation-related losses of algal and invertebrate standing crop under simulated fluctuating flow regimes — with direct implications for [[Rainbow Trout]] and native fish forage availability.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study Design**: Ten sites sampled bimonthly across [[Colorado River]] miles 0–220, spanning wide reaches (Lees Ferry, Nankoweap, Little Colorado River confluence, Tanner Canyon, Spring Canyon) and narrow reaches (South Canyon, Grand Canyon gauge/Bright Angel Creek, Kanab Creek, Havasu Creek/National Canyon, Diamond Creek). Three habitat types were sampled: low-velocity fluvial, high-velocity fluvial, and tributary habitats.
- **Sampling Methods**: Hess sampler (≥3 replicates each for taxonomy and standing crop per habitat), Peterson dredge (≥6 replicates each in low-velocity zones), plus littoral zone sampling, sweep netting, black-and-white light trapping, and surface sampling for adult Diptera. Water quality (temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, specific conductance) recorded at each site.
- **Trophic Structure**: A longitudinal "stair-step" decline in algal standing crop and macroinvertebrate abundance occurs downstream from Lees Ferry, driven by tributary confluences that act as productivity "steps down."
- **Community Gradient**: Tailwater reaches near the dam are dominated by a *Cladophora*/*Diatoma*/*[[Gammarus]]*/*Cricotopus*/*Physa*/lumbricid assemblage, transitioning downstream to an *Oscillatoria*/*Simulium*/mixed chironomid assemblage in the lower Grand Canyon.
- **Habitat Patterns**:
  - Benthic macroinvertebrate diversity is lower in the mainstem Colorado River than in tributaries.
  - Chironomids and oligochaetes inhabit silty fine-sand deposits in wide reaches but are rare in narrow reaches.
  - Benthic macroinvertebrates are rare in mid- and upper-littoral river zones but abundant in comparable tributary settings.
- **Trophic Role of Simuliids**: *Simulium arcticum* is an important, though patchily distributed, food resource for trout, concentrated on driftwood substrate in cobble bars — linking [[Simuliidae]] production directly to [[Trout]] forage ecology.
- **Fish-Related Data Gaps**: Trout stomach samples were collected and under analysis; however, promised native fish gut-content samples had not been obtained by the time of reporting, leaving a significant gap in understanding [[Native Fish]] trophic dependence on the invertebrate community.
- **Flow Regime Impacts**: Desiccation experiments (12-hour submersion/exposure cycles over 5 days) demonstrated that fluctuating discharge patterns associated with dam operations substantially reduce standing crop of primary producers and consumers, directly affecting habitat quality and food availability in littoral zones.

## Key Findings

1. A pronounced downstream "stair-step" decline in benthic/attached algae and macroinvertebrate standing crop occurs below [[Lees Ferry]], attributable to productivity resets at major tributary confluences.
2. Macroinvertebrate species composition shifts predictably with distance from the dam, from a *Cladophora*-based tailwater assemblage to an *Oscillatoria*/*Simulium*-based lower canyon assemblage.
3. Benthic macroinvertebrate diversity and abundance patterns differ markedly between river and tributary habitats, and between wide versus narrow river reaches.
4. *[[Simulium arcticum]]* is a key trophic link to trout diets but exhibits patchy distribution tied to driftwood substrate availability in cobble bars.
5. Experimental desiccation trials simulating fluctuating dam discharges caused losses exceeding 50% of *Cladophora* standing crop, over 50% of periphyton standing crop and caloric value, and more than 70% of chironomid standing crop/abundance — demonstrating a direct mechanistic pathway by which [[Dam Operations]] and flow fluctuation degrade the aquatic food base supporting fish populations.
6. Native fish dietary data remain absent despite repeated efforts to obtain gut-content samples, representing a critical unresolved gap in trophic linkage assessment between macroinvertebrates and the native fish community of the Colorado River.
7. Caloric (microbomb calorimetry) analyses of *Cladophora*, periphyton, *Oscillatoria*, *Gammarus*, and Chironomidae are underway to quantify energy flow through lower trophic levels, intended to inform NPS monitoring protocols and EIS alternatives regarding dam operation impacts on the aquatic food base.
