# Fauna and Flora in the Waters Adjacent to the Sado Marine Biological Station: Supplement 2

#ichthyology #marine-biology #faunal-checklist

## Executive Summary

This short communication by [[Yoshiharu Honma]] and [[Takehiko Kitami]] extends the faunal inventory of the waters around [[Sado Island]] in the [[Sea of Japan]]. It was published in the [[Report of the Sado Marine Biological Station]] of [[Niigata University]] (No. 25, March 1995, pp. 13–30) and forms the second supplement to the station's earlier catalogues. The paper is essentially an annotated taxonomic checklist of newly secured specimens, followed by a long list of selected references. It also acknowledges [[Ju-shey Ho]], whose work on parasitic copepods added a dozen new species from the area.

## Background & Description

The [[Sado Marine Biological Station]] (Faculty of Science, Niigata University) marked its 25th anniversary of foundation when its research-museum specimen catalogues were first compiled (Honma and Kitami 1978, 1979). Earlier work included plankton surveys of [[Tassha Bay]], a small inlet near the station (Abe, Honma and Kitami 1984), and a freshwater biota list for Niigata Prefecture based on a twenty-year limnological survey by the Niigata Freshwater Ecology Research Group (Honma et al. 1988).

The authors state that continued specimen collection has added many species to the reference collection and that ongoing taxonomic revision has produced several name changes. This justified a new supplement, although some individual fishes and cetaceans had already been reported separately (Honma 1990, 1991; Honma and Kitami 1980).

The checklist is arranged by phylum and class, with Japanese vernacular names given alongside scientific names in the original. Taxa are grouped as follows:

- Porifera (sponges): Demospongiae, two species.
- Cnidaria: Hydrozoa (Anthomedusae, Leptomedusae, Siphonophorae), Scyphozoa, and Anthozoa (Octocorallia, Hexacorallia).
- Kinorhyncha: a single family and species.
- Mollusca: Gastropoda, Opisthobranchia, Pulmonata, Scaphopoda, Bivalvia (Palaeotaxodonta, Pteriomorphia, Heterodonta, Anomalodesmacea), and Cephalopoda.
- Annelida: Polychaeta.
- Arthropoda: Thecostraca, Isopoda, Amphipoda, Decapoda, and Stomatopoda.
- Bryozoa, Echinodermata (Crinoidea, Asteroidea, Ophiuroidea, Echinoidea), and Prochordata (Ascidiacea).
- Vertebrata: Chondrichthyes, Osteichthyes, and Mammalia (Cetacea).
- Tracheophyta: two seagrasses.

A separate section lists parasitic animals compiled by Ju-shey Ho, covering Monogenea, Nematoda and many Copepoda.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Sado Island waters include both temperate coastal habitats and the nearshore zone of the Sea of Japan. Several taxa are typical of rocky or sandy shores, seagrass beds, and drifting or floating seaweed. The document notes, for example, that caprellid amphipods were found attached to floating seaweeds (Ito, Ikehara and Honma 1988). Parasitic relationships are a major theme: copepods were recorded on molluscs, echinoderms, sponges, cnidarians, tunicates, stingrays and surfperches, and the gnathiid isopod larva was found in the branchial chamber of a stingray.

The paper also records large marine animals stranded on the Niigata and Sado coasts, including the cetaceans *Mesoplodon stejnegeri* and *Ziphius cavirostris*, and the pelagic squids recorded from stranded specimens (Honma, Kitami and Mizusawa 1983).

Freshwater biota are treated only in the cited reference list (Honma et al. 1988; Honma, Inouye and Matsumoto 1981 on freshwater fishes of Sado Island). The supplement itself concerns marine and brackish-zone species.

## Key Findings

**Scope and provenance**
- The checklist extends earlier catalogues (Honma and Kitami 1978, 1979) with newly secured specimens and revised names.
- The authors say the work is a short communication, not a full ecological survey, and it reports no sample sizes, abundance data or statistics.

**Fishes (Chondrichthyes and Osteichthyes)**
- Cartilaginous fishes include *Cephaloscyllium umbratile*, *Rhizoprionodon acutus*, and *Dasyatis matsubarai*.
- Bony fishes span a wide range of orders, including Elopiformes (*Megalops cyprinoides*), Clupeiformes (*Clupea pallasi*), Salmoniformes (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*), Anguilliformes (*Anago anago*), Lampriformes (*Velifer hypselopterus*, *Lophotus capellei*), and Perciformes with many carangids, gobiids and stichaeids.
- Notable records include *Stereolepis doederleini*, *Makaira indica*, *Icosteus aenigmaticus*, and the sebastids *Sebastes taczanowskii* and *Sebastes zonatus*.
- Pleuronectiformes (e.g., *Heteromycteris japonica*) and Tetraodontiformes (e.g., *Balistoides conspicillum*, *Lactoria cornuta*) are also included.

**Invertebrates**
- Cnidaria include the scyphozoan *Rhopilema esculenta* and the gorgonian *Ellisella rudva*.
- Molluscs form the largest group in the text, with many nudibranchs and opisthobranchs. Several species are named after Baba's work on Dendrodoris, including *Dendrodoris sadoensis*.
- Arthropods include many isopods (e.g., *Cirolana sadoensis*, *Holotelson decorata*), decapods (e.g., *Charybdis feriata*, *Hemigrapsus sinensis*) and stomatopods (*Harpiosquilla raphidea*).
- Echinoderms include the sea urchins *Strongylocentrotus sachalinicus* and *Temnopleurus reevesi*, and the brittle star *Amphiura syntayacha*.
- Ascidians include *Ciona intestinalis* and *Halocynthia igaguri*.

**Marine mammals and plants**
- Cetacea are represented by *Mesoplodon stejnegeri* and *Ziphius cavirostris*.
- Seagrasses recorded are *Zostera asiatica* and *Phyllospadix japonica* (Kitami 1986 on the seagrasses of Sado Island).

**Parasites (compiled by Ju-shey Ho)**
- Ho's contribution covers many parasitic copepods in the orders Harpacticoida, Cyclopoida, Poecilostomatoida and Siphonostomatoida, plus Monogenea and a philometrid nematode on *Pleuronectes yokohamae*.
- Ho's reports of 12 new species from waters adjacent to Sado Island are cited in the Acknowledgements.
- Notable taxa include *Coelotrophus nudus* (described from *Phascolosoma scolops*, causing sterility) and *Lernaeolophus aceratus* (on a rainbowfish).

**Taxonomic revisions**
- The authors note that several names have changed, but the text does not give a count of revised names.

**Key references cited**
- Honma (1991): a list of fishes VII near the station.
- Honma (1992): colored illustrations of marine fishes of Niigata Prefecture (358 pp.).
- Honma and Kitami (1981): records of marine mammals based partly on old documents.
- Ho and Kim (1995): chondracanthid copepods and *Acanthochondria* on Sado Island fishes.
