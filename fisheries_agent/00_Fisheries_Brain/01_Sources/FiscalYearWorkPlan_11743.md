# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin - FY 1998 Work Plan

#ichthyology #endangered-species #recovery-program #upper-colorado-river #program-management

## Executive Summary

This document presents the Fiscal Year 1998 work plan and budget for the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The program's established annual budget for FY 98 is $3.18 million (a 3.0% increase over FY 97), with total funding including additional contributions reaching $10.37 million when capital projects are included. The work plan was approved by the Colorado River Implementation Committee on September 17, 1997, and encompasses more than 80 individual scopes of work prepared by state and federal agencies, universities, private contractors, and Recovery Program staff. The plan implements recovery activities identified in the Program's Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP) dated March 6, 1997, focusing on seven major recovery elements: instream flow identification and protection, habitat restoration, nonnative fish control, propagation and genetics management, research and monitoring, information and education, and program management.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program operates under the direction of the Colorado River Implementation Committee, which includes representatives from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Colorado Department of Natural Resources, Utah Department of Natural Resources, Wyoming State Engineer, Western Area Power Administration, environmental groups, and Upper Basin water users. The program addresses recovery of endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], including the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Bonytail]].

The FY 98 budget structure includes: Fish and Wildlife Service ($1,120,000), Bureau of Reclamation ($2,071,400 annual + $6,013,000 capital), State of Colorado ($948,600), State of Utah ($102,300), State of Wyoming ($32,000), Water Users Section 7 Funds ($79,300), and FY 88 Water Acquisition Appropriation ($9,400). Lead agencies implement activities through contracts and fund allocations, with the goal of having all contracts issued by the end of the first quarter (December) of FY 98.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] encompasses multiple river systems critical to endangered fish recovery, including the [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Gunnison River]], and [[Duchesne River]]. The program recognizes the [[Yampa River]] as "the most important river in the Upper Colorado River Basin to the maintenance and recovery of the endangered fishes." Recovery efforts address multiple ecological factors including instream flow requirements, floodplain habitat function, nonnative fish competition and predation, channel morphology, sediment transport, water temperature, and spawning habitat availability.

Key ecological concerns include: impacts of reservoir operations on flow regimes and water temperature; degradation of floodplain habitats through levee construction and altered hydrology; barriers to fish passage at diversion structures; predation and competition from nonnative species including centrarchids (bass and sunfish), channel catfish, and small cyprinids; and loss of natural flow variability needed to maintain channel morphology and backwater habitats. The program employs adaptive management approaches, including experimental flow releases from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and the [[Aspinall Unit]], coordinated reservoir operations to enhance spring peak flows, and systematic monitoring to evaluate population status and trends.

## Key Findings

**Budget Allocation by Recovery Element (FY 98):**
- Instream Flow Identification and Protection: 32% ($3.3 million, including $1.78 million for [[Grand Valley]] water management)
- Habitat Restoration: 20% ($2.1 million for fish passage and floodplain restoration)
- Propagation and Genetics Management: 20% ($2.4 million for hatchery operations and facility expansion)
- Program Management: 12.4% ($1.3 million)
- Research, Monitoring, and Data Management: 7.6% ($787,000)
- Nonnative Fish Control: 6.6% ($687,000)
- Information, Education, and Public Involvement: <1% ($103,000)

**Major Capital Projects:**
- [[Grand Valley Water Management]]: $1.78 million for canal improvements to provide up to 30,000 acre-feet of water for endangered fish through reduced demand and storage in [[Green Mountain Reservoir]]
- Floodplain Restoration Program: $1.49 million for levee breaching at 7 sites (5 on Green River, 2 on Colorado River), land acquisition of willing seller properties (targeting ~1,000 acres on Green River, ~200 acres on Colorado/Gunnison rivers), and experimental habitat manipulation
- Propagation Facility Expansion: $1.83 million for construction at [[Ouray Endangered Fish Hatchery]], growout ponds on Green River, and Grand Valley modifications
- Fish Passage Construction: $370,000 for completion of [[Grand Valley Irrigation Company]] passage structure on Colorado River

**Instream Flow Protection Activities:**
- Negotiation of contract for 21,650 acre-feet/year from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] for 15-Mile Reach
- Lease of 2,000 acre-feet from [[Steamboat Lake]] for late summer Yampa River flows ($32,000)
- Development of [[Yampa River Basin Recovery and Water Management Plan]] ($398,000) to balance endangered fish needs with existing and future water development
- Coordinated reservoir operations in upper Colorado River to enhance spring peak flows ($78,000)
- Colorado Water Conservation Board adjudication of instream flow filings on Colorado and Yampa rivers and review of Gunnison River flow recommendations ($125,000)

**Habitat Restoration Accomplishments and Plans:**
- [[Redlands Fish Passage]] structure (completed FY 96) restored access to 57 miles of Gunnison River; used by 19 Colorado squawfish and >19,000 other native fishes
- Completion of fish passage at Grand Valley Irrigation Company diversion will restore access to 3 additional miles of Colorado River habitat
- Future passage construction at Hartland, Price Stubb, and Roller Dam will restore access to 67.4 additional miles
- Floodplain restoration employs 8-site block design comparing fish response to various habitat types
- [[Old Charley Wash]] operated as controlled facility to remove nonnative fishes

**Nonnative Fish Control Strategy:**
- Removal of centrarchids and channel catfish from 20-mile reach of Green River near Ouray and 10-mile reach in Gray Canyon ($164,400)
- Reclamation of up to 150 floodplain ponds by 2003 (minimum 10 in FY 98) along Colorado and Gunnison rivers to eliminate centrarchid sources ($258,100)
- Removal of small nonnative cyprinids from backwaters in Colorado, Gunnison, and Green rivers ($152,400 combined)
- Channel catfish removal from Yampa River ($96,800)
- Centrarchid removal from Colorado River integrated with abundance monitoring ($37,000)

**Propagation and Stocking Programs:**
- Approximately 2,200 bonytail stocked in Professor Valley area of Colorado River in late FY 97; additional 2,800 to be stocked spring 1998; up to 15,000 from 1997-98 year classes planned for early FY 99 ($65,000)
- Experimental stocking and monitoring of razorback suckers in Green and Gunnison rivers
- Operation and maintenance of facilities: [[Grand Valley Endangered Fish Facilities]] ($151,000), Wahweap ($50,000), [[Ouray Endangered Fish Hatchery]] ($296,000), razorback sucker broodstock collection ($25,000)
- Study to determine if hatchery-reared razorback suckers will locate wild spawning aggregations, comparing direct release versus acclimation approaches ($31,000)

**Monitoring and Research:**
- Expanded Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program (ISMP) for population estimates of Colorado squawfish in Yampa, Green, and upper Colorado reaches; humpback chub in five river reaches; and razorback sucker in lower Green River ($623,900)
- Larval fish identification supporting ISMP and other studies ($61,200)
- Database management maintaining computerized records and PIT-tag registry ($29,000)
- Completion of [[Flaming Gorge]] and [[Aspinall Unit]] flow recommendation synthesis reports ($122,000 combined)
- Channel monitoring to evaluate geomorphic changes from coordinated reservoir operations ($44,800)
- Peer review panel established for geomorphological studies ($10,000)

**Water User Section 7 Funds:**
- Depletion charge adjusted to $13.81/acre-foot for FY 98
- Estimated $140,000 available October 1, 1997; $79,300 in planned expenditures
- Funds held by National Fish and Wildlife Foundation in interest-bearing account
- Primary use reserved for water rights acquisition and directly related activities

**FY 88 Appropriation Status:**
- Original $1 million appropriation for water rights acquisition nearly depleted
- Expenditures to date: $988,600 including overhead, water leases, Grand Valley water management, hydrology support, and Duchesne River studies
- Only $9,400 planned for FY 98 (hydrology support); up to $80,000 may remain depending on FY 97 final accounting

**Contingency Project List:**
- Annual funds contingencies include: additional Yampa catfish removal ($3,200), selenium studies ($100,000), Redlands passage evaluation ($29,410), Gila hybridization ($32,000), spawning site identification ($42,000), chemoreception ($77,200)
- Capital funds contingencies include: additional hatchery/growout pond construction, acceleration of Price-Stubb fish passage, additional pond reclamation, Yampa low-flow data collection ($50,000-$75,000), and additional floodplain land acquisition

**Public Involvement:**
- Comprehensive public involvement plans for 10 major program elements including Duchesne River flow protection, Colorado River coordinated reservoir operations, capital improvement projects, Ruedi and Flaming Gorge releases, floodplain restoration, and Yampa River Management Plan
- Information and education coordination ($28,500) including newsletter production, news releases, Congressional briefing packets, and web page development
- Educational exhibit on endangered fish at [[Browns Park National Wildlife Refuge]] ($9,000 matching funds)
- Improved coordination of public involvement for nonnative fish control, land acquisition, and propagation across three geographical areas ($10,000)

**Program Management Structure:**
- Multi-agency coordination: Utah ($68,300), Bureau of Reclamation ($150,000), Fish and Wildlife Service ($707,000), Colorado ($120,000), Wyoming ($12,000)
- Capital projects coordination by Bureau of Reclamation ($119,000)
- Service administrative overhead charges (17.65% on transferred funds, approximately $100,000) subject to potential Congressional waiver
- Utah convening task group to evaluate relationship between program activities and mosquito production ($5,000)

**Mid-Year Review Requirements:**
- Grand Valley water management project scheduled for mid-year Management Committee review due to continuing negotiations and schedule delays
- Floodplain restoration program scheduled for mid-year review to determine if scope or budget modifications warranted
- Facility expansion project (CAP-7) scheduled for mid-year review
