# Native Fish Needs in the Little Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD) correspondence file documents how an expert group set population and habitat objectives for native fish in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) basin. The group met on 17 November 1998 in Phoenix. Its decisions were circulated in a memo dated 18 December 1998, then revised in a 26 May 1999 memo, which checked each recommendation against the draft [[Watershed Fisheries Management Plan]]. The statements cover seven taxa: [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Little Colorado Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Zuni Bluehead Sucker]] and [[Apache Trout]]. The needs will guide management prescriptions and will be balanced against [[Sportfish Management]].

## Background & Description

The project concerns native fish conservation in the LCR basin, with [[Recovery Plan]] documents (notably the 1997 Little Colorado spinedace plan and a draft Apache trout revision) as the main reference points. The meeting objective was to decide, for each native species except razorback sucker and humpback chub, how many populations to aim for in each sub-basin. Listing status, genetic integrity and barriers to dispersal were to be considered.

The expert group included staff from AGFD (research, nongame, fisheries and regional offices), the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the U.S. Forest Service, the Bureau of Reclamation, the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests and Arizona State University. Twelve people attended. The memo distribution list was broader.

The 1999 revision clarified an ambiguity in the spinedace plan. Refugia need not be in the same drainage as their source population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historical range:** Little Colorado spinedace probably occurred throughout the LCR above Grand Falls, at least down to St. Johns.
- **Refugia:** Spring and pond refugia were debated. Participants agreed they offer a managed, intermediate level of security between wild and captive populations. Roger Sorensen noted that refugia are often not secure. Spinedace persisted in intermittent pool systems during droughts.
- **Dispersal and competition:** Speckled dace should not be repatriated into areas where they might compete with repatriated spinedace. Little Colorado spinedace was kept separate from the sucker-dace assemblage because it is not known what competes with it.
- **Elevation:** At higher elevations, the native assemblage should be Apache trout and bluehead suckers. The other species depend on appropriate elevational range.
- **Extinction:** Monkey Springs pupfish are definitely extinct, and the final document should reflect this.
- **Data gaps:** Speckled dace are scarce in Silver Creek, but the group was not certain they are absent. Distribution maps have gaps. The group also noted that perennial stream mileage is of limited value, because many species use water not classed as perennial.

## Key Findings

**1. Little Colorado Spinedace**
- Maintain the five existing known populations: East Clear Creek above and below Blue Ridge Reservoir, Chevelon Creek near the LCR confluence, portions of Nutrioso and Rudd Creeks, and Silver Creek below the Canoncito gauging station.
- Establish refugia in each drainage with current populations. Candidate sites include [[Potato Lake]], Hart Canyon (on AGFD's [[Vincent Ranch]]), [[Chevelon Canyon Wildlife Area]] (lower Chevelon), [[Mineral Creek]] (proposed for Chevelon populations, although outside that drainage), the Silver Creek Hatchery, [[Wenima Wildlife Area]], the Pinetop State Hatchery, the Flagstaff Arboretum and the [[Mineral Ditch Complex]]. The plan does not consider Wenima because it floods during high flows.
- Repatriation sites include the mainstem LCR above Becker Lake, above and below [[Lyman Lake]], and portions of Show Low, Walnut and Billy Creeks. The 1999 review evaluated specific reaches, such as Show Low Creek from Fool Hollow Lake to Show Low Lake and Walnut Creek from Rainbow Lake to Woodland Reservoir.
- Chevelon Creek has only one population, so all of its refugia will be replicates of that population.

**2. Speckled Dace**
- Manage for no net loss of any population.
- Repatriate into drainages where poisoning is suspected, mainly [[Show Low Creek]] and its tributaries.
- Manage as part of an assemblage with bluehead and Little Colorado suckers.
- Extend the range in Silver Creek from the LCR confluence to the headwaters, mirroring the sucker distributions.

**3. Bluehead Sucker**
- Manage for no net loss. Repatriate into suspected poisoning areas, primarily Show Low Creek and its tributaries.
- Manage within a native assemblage that includes speckled dace and Little Colorado suckers.

**4. Little Colorado Sucker**
- Manage for no net loss. Repatriate into the same suspected poisoning areas.
- Manage within the same assemblage as bluehead suckers and speckled dace.

**5. Roundtail Chub**
- Maintain and enhance the two existing populations, in Chevelon Canyon and Clear Creek.
- Repatriate to reach two populations in each of four sub-basins: the upper LCR, Silver Creek, Chevelon Creek and East Clear Creek. Repatriation should include the reach above Lyman Lake.
- Broodstock and repatriation fish must come from within the LCR basin.
- The 1999 review noted that the two East Clear Creek units are adjacent and probably cannot count as two populations. It recommended evaluating Leonard Canyon for translocation, probably after spinedace is delisted.

**6. Zuni Bluehead Sucker**
- Surveys are needed to determine whether the species has been extirpated from Arizona before any specific recommendations are made. The main population is in New Mexico on the Zuni River. Reports of water and fish near the New Mexico border justify survey work there.

**7. Apache Trout**
- Management follows the draft Recovery Plan revision. Nearly all LCR headwater streams with appropriate conditions should be repatriated.
- Introductions outside the known range are not proposed.

**Plan concurrence (May 1999):** The memo reports that the draft Watershed Fisheries Management Plan agrees with all recommendations, with the exception of the revised refugia and repatriation site details. Final wording was to appear as a Native Fish Needs subheading in the Results section. It will pair the expert group's statements with an assessment of whether the plan meets them.
