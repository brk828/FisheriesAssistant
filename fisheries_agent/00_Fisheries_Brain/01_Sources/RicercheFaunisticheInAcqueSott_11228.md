# Phreatic Subterranean Fauna of Northwestern and Central Iran

#copepoda #groundwater-fauna #phreatobiology

## Executive Summary

This paper, by G. L. Pesce, R. Pace and D. Maggi (Istituto di Zoologia, Università dell'Aquila), reports the first partial results of two phreatobiological field campaigns in Iran in 1977 and 1978, totalling 38 days in the country. The survey sampled groundwater wells along the [[Caspian Sea]] littoral, around Tehran, and on the [[Esfahan Plateau]] (Shahr-e-Kord, Khvonsar, Cialestore). The authors identified 22 species and subspecies of micro-crustaceans, oligochaetes and other groups, the majority of them new for the Iranian fauna. Eight taxa are new to Iran or to science. The fauna shows a mixed biogeographic origin: Palaearctic, Oriental, Indo-Pacific, and endemic elements. The campaigns were halted by political events, and the southern and eastern regions were never sampled.

## Background & Description

The authors describe Iran as a meeting point of faunas of different origins because of its geomorphological, climatic and geological diversity. Three climatic zones are recognised (Caspian, interior plateau, southern), following the Köppen system. Elevations range from about −26 m in the Caspian depressions to about 2000 m on the plateaus. Geologically, the country spans Cambrian–Triassic volcanic rocks to Pleistocene deposits.

Earlier subterranean data for the region were scarce, mainly from [[Löffler]] (1959, 1961). The study aimed at the systematic, ecological and biogeographic study of phreatic biocoenoses.

**Methods (as reported):**
- Physicochemical parameters were measured in the field: water temperature and pH with portable YSI and Fisher monitors, air temperature with thermometers, and salinity with a YSI monitor (0–40‰).
- Samples were fixed in 5% formalin, then transferred to 70–80° alcohol or acetic solution.
- Most material was identified to species level (Cyclopidae, Harpacticoida, Asellidae, Microparasellidae, Amphipoda, Oligochaeta). Other groups were identified only to order or family, pending study by specialists.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling sites:**
- 1977 survey: 21 stations (wells of fresh or slightly brackish water, plus two rainwater cisterns) on the Caspian littoral (Kelar-Abad, Kargash, Motel Orkide, Nowshahr, Chalus mosque wells) and around Tehran (Karaj cisterns), Esfahan and Shahr-e-Kord.
- 1978 survey: 20 stations (freshwater wells) around Shahr-e-Kord, Farrokhshahr and Cialestore on the plateau, plus re-sampling of promising 1977 stations.
- Overall, 41 stations are reported, with station-by-station data on depth, water level, temperature, pH, sediment and biocoenosis.

**Physical conditions:**
- Well depths range from about 2 m to 35 m.
- Water temperatures range from about 14.5 °C to 27 °C.
- pH is mostly 7–8.5.
- Sediments are mostly sands and silts of Pleistocene (Caspian), Pliocene (plateau) or Upper Miocene (Khvonsar) age.
- Elevations are about 1550 m (Shahr-e-Kord), 2000 m (Cialestore) and 2350 m (Khvonsar).

**Ecological structure:** Stygobionts and stygophiles were more abundant in protected, covered wells with little external organic input, used mainly for drinking water. Stygoxenes (surface species) dominated in large, open wells that were heavily pumped for irrigation and exposed to organic and inorganic contamination. Oligochaetes were absent from protected wells and common in open ones. The authors suggest that the ratio of epigean to hypogean organisms in wells could serve as a water-quality index, following [[Ronneberger]] (1975) and [[Danielopol]] (1981).

## Key Findings

**Species inventory:** 22 species and subspecies were identified: 16 Cyclopidae and 6 Harpacticoida. Eight are new to Iran or to science. Taxa marked as new are:
- [[Acanthocyclops Dussarti Orientalis]] (new subspecies, described from Cialestore wells)
- [[Acanthocyclops Robustus]]
- [[Diacyclops Ivanicus]] (described by Pesce and Maggi, 1982; endemic to Iran)
- [[Diacyclops Cfr. Languidus]] (doubtful record)
- [[Diacyclops Languidoides Zschokkei]]
- [[Bryocyclops Neuter]]
- [[Nitocrella Paceae]] and [[Nitocrella Petkovskii]]

**Key taxonomic results:**
- **[[Microcharon Raffaellae]]** (Microparasellidae) is the first record of this family for Iran. It is described as a new species from the Shahr-e-Kord and Cialestore wells.
- **[[Phreatomelita Paceae]]** is a new genus and species of blind amphipod (Gammaridae), described by Ruffo (1979). It is the first stygobiotic amphipod recorded from the study area. Its closest relatives are Indo-Pacific and Hadziid genera.
- **[[Bryocyclops Neuter]]** was previously known only from Madagascar. Its presence in Iran suggests an unexpected biogeographic link.
- **[[Lycodrilus Phreodriloides]]** (Oligochaeta) was known only from Lake Baikal. The Iranian specimens come from sand and silt, whereas the Baikal form lives on rocky substrates, so the record also extends its known ecology.
- **Nitocrella:** the Iranian *N. paceae* and *N. petkovskii* are known only from the Esfahan Plateau phreatic systems. Iran now has four known *Nitocrella* s.l. species, including two from southwestern Iran (*N. mara* and *Parapseudoleptomesocra iranica*).
- **Nomenclature:** *Megacyclops viridis deserticola* is doubtful. It was known from one brackish pond south of the Dasht-e-Kavir desert, and its intermediate characters raise questions about keeping *Megacyclops* and [[Acanthocyclops]] separate.

**Ecological classification (four groups):**
1. Epigean, eurivalent, accidental visitors (e.g. *Eucyclops speratus*, *Megacyclops viridis*, *Cyclops furcifer*, *Asellus aquaticus*, most oligochaetes).
2. Eurytopic, widely distributed, with some pre-adaptation to groundwater (e.g. *Eucyclops serrulatus*, *Paracyclops fimbriatus*, *Tropocyclops prasinus*, *Attheyella crassa*).
3. Eustygophile forms, mostly in groundwater (e.g. *Diacyclops languidoides zschokkei*, *Nais elinguis*).
4. Stygobionts, highly specialised and restricted to subterranean habitats (e.g. *Acanthocyclops dussarti orientalis*, *Nitocrella paceae*, *N. petkovskii*, *Microcharon raffaellae*, *Phreatomelita paceae*).

**Biogeographic groups:**
- Cosmopolitan species (most cyclopids, *Nitocra lacustris*, oligochaetes).
- Palaearctic species with northern distributions (e.g. *Diacyclops languidoides zschokkei*, *Cyclops furcifer*, *Attheyella crassa*).
- Euro-Asiatic or eastern affinities (*Asellus aquaticus*, *Lycodrilus phreodriloides*).
- Indo-Pacific affinities (*Bryocyclops neuter*, *Phreatomelita paceae*).
- Endemic or isolated forms (*Diacyclops ivanicus*, *Acanthocyclops dussarti orientalis*, *Nitocrella* spp., *Microcharon raffaellae*).

**Origin hypothesis:** Several *Nitocrella* and microparasellid genera are thought to have derived from marine ancestors (e.g. *Nitocra*) that colonised freshwater groundwater during Mio-Pliocene regressions, following the "regression model" of [[Stock]] (1977).

**Conclusions and outlook:** The authors conclude that Iranian phreatic faunas are distinctive, dominated by crustaceans, oligochaetes, gastropods and mites, and that their origin is partly Palaearctic and partly composite. Ostracods, gastropods and hydracarinae were still under study. Further sampling in eastern and southern Iran was recommended but never completed because of political events.
