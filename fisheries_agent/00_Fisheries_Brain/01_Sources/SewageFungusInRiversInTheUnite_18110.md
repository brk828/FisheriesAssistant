# Sewage Fungus in Rivers in the United Kingdom: The Slime Community and Its Constituent Organisms

#freshwater-ecology #pollution-biology #microbiology #protozoa #sewage-fungus

## Executive Summary

This 1971 study by Curtis and Curps, published in *Water Research* (Pergamon Press), describes the biological composition of sewage fungus slimes sampled at 178 outbreak sites in the United Kingdom during 1968–1969. Slimes were most often dominated by [[Sphaerotilus Natans]] or zoogloeal bacteria. The study compares these communities with those of other polluted habitats, such as [[Activated Sludge]] and percolating filters, and uses cluster analysis and diversity indices to explore species relationships. The document covers slime-forming bacteria, fungi, algae, diatoms and protozoa only. No fish or other macrofauna are addressed.

## Background & Description

Sewage fungus is a slime growth in rivers linked to organic effluent. The survey had two aims: to relate slime growth to water quality and effluent type (see Curtis and Harrington, 1971), and to define the organisms forming the slime and their relationship to other aquatic microbiota.

Sites were located with help from River Authorities and Conservancies in England and Wales and River Purification Boards in Scotland. Samples were held in sterile screw-capped bottles and examined by phase-contrast microscopy within 24 h of collection. Fungi were isolated in pure culture for identification. Abundance was scored on a logarithmic scale:

- Small: fewer than 10 cells per ml
- Medium: tens per ml
- Large: hundreds per ml
- Very large: thousands per ml

Fungi and small zoogloeal bacteria were scored by biomass equivalent to [[Sphaerotilus Natans]].

The taxonomic background is summarised as follows:

- [[Sphaerotilus Natans]] is a sheathed bacterium (Chlamydobacteriaceae), regarded as the typical sewage fungus organism since Kützing (1833). The variety *S. natans* var. *typica* was most frequent.
- Zoogloeal bacteria are a heterogeneous group of small bacteria that extrude a mucilaginous layer. Their taxonomy is poorly understood, and the type organism, *Zoogloea ramigera*, has uncertain status.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Slime formers:** [[Sphaerotilus Natans]] and zoogloeal bacteria were the dominant organisms, forming most of the biomass. Other slime organisms were infrequent. The fungi [[Leptomitus Lacteus]] and [[Geotrichum Candidum]] and the gliding bacterium [[Beggiatoa Alba]] were the most common dominant non-bacterial forms.
- **Algae:** Filamentous algae were rare in slimes. Algal outbreaks are typically linked to inorganic pollution and often form a zone downstream of the sewage fungus zone. The alga *Stigeoclonium tenue* was the most frequent filamentous alga.
- **Protozoa:** Flagellates and ciliates were present in most samples. Ciliates are the main consumers of bacteria in the slime, and some filamentous material may be grazed as well.
- **Comparison with treatment plants:** Of the ciliate species, 72.7% were also found in aerobic treatment processes. Polluted rivers host a wider ciliate range, mainly because of more holotrich species.
- **Pollution tolerance:** Most protozoan species are poly- or meso-saprobic, indicating grossly to moderately polluted water. The exception is [[Carchesium Polypinum]].

## Key Findings

- **Survey scope:** 178 outbreak sites in the UK, 1968–1969.
- **Dominant slime formers:** *Sphaerotilus natans* was found at 88.9% of sites and zoogloeal bacteria at 93.4%. They were dominant (formed the major biomass) at 50.3% and 59.3% of sites, respectively.
- **Other bacteria:** [[Flavobacterium Sp X]] was found at 36.9% of sites, *Flexibacterium* sp. at 25.2%, and [[Beggiatoa Alba]] at 29.6%.
- **Fungi:** Rare. *Leptomitus lacteus* occurred at 3.9% of sites and *Geotrichum candidum* at 7.3%.
- **Diatoms:** Mainly *Navicula* (17.9%), *Fragilaria* (11.3%) and *Synedra* (11.8%).
- **Protozoa:** Flagellates were found at 97.5% of sites and ciliates at 93.8%. Seventy-seven ciliate species were recorded: 40 holotrichs, 15 peritrichs, 19 spirotrichs (15 hypotrichs, 4 heterotrichs) and 3 suctorians.
- **Abundance of ciliates:** 11 species were recorded in very large numbers, 29 in large numbers, 46 in medium numbers and 70 in small numbers.
- **Key ciliates:** Apart from the attached [[Carchesium Polypinum]], the most important ciliates are holotrichs. [[Colpidium Colpoda]] and [[Chilodonella Cucullulus]] were the most common dominant protozoa.
- **Diet:** Most ciliates feed mainly on bacteria. Two *Chilodonella* species also ingest filamentous organisms, which may help control slime growth. Only *Trachelophyllum pusillum* and *Hemiophrys fusidens* are carnivorous.
- **Species associations (Jaccard):** The strongest link is between *Sphaerotilus* and zoogloeal bacteria (0.850), which is attributed to a shared habitat. Other notable associations include *Chilodonella cucullulus*/*C. uncinata* (0.450) and *Uronema*/*Tachysoma* (0.410, possibly predator–prey). Bacteria and ciliates form a single association group, while fungi and algae are only loosely linked.
- **Diversity:** Slimes with more than one dominant species had a higher species diversity slope (0.0404) than *Sphaerotilus*-dominated (0.0287) or zoogloeal-dominated (0.0307) slimes. The two single-dominant groups did not differ significantly.
- **Historical comparison:** Butcher (1932) described a similar typical community. Zoogloeal bacteria were more widespread in this survey than *Zoogloea ramigera* was in Butcher's work.
- **Conclusion:** Sewage fungus slimes are primarily bacterial, with a ciliate grazing community, and fungi and algae play only minor roles.
