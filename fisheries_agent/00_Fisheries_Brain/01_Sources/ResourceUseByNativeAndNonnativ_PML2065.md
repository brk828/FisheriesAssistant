# Cited Literature Index: Fish Predation, Competition, Habitat Use and Introduced Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research article. It is the back matter of a review, consisting of a coded key to fish and study-type abbreviations followed by two long lists of cited literature (one numbered, one alphabetical). Its scientific content is therefore limited to the bibliography and the coding scheme that classifies each reference by species and study setting. The cited works, as far as titles reveal, centre on predator-prey interactions among freshwater fishes, competition between native and introduced species, microhabitat and instream-flow use, and the ecological impacts of fish introductions, with a strong emphasis on the warmwater and desert fishes of the western United States.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The cited titles suggest several recurring themes:

- **Predation and species interactions**: studies of largemouth bass feeding behaviour, predation on larval and juvenile fishes, and predator-induced habitat shifts (e.g., Harvey 1991; Brown and Moyle 1991; Brown and Brasher 1995).
- **Introduced species impacts**: documented effects of [[Brown Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Common Carp]], [[Tilapia]] species and [[Striped Bass]] on native fishes (e.g., Crowl et al. 1992; Minckley 1982; Moyle and Light 1996).
- **Habitat and instream flow**: use of the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] and [[Habitat Suitability Index Models]] to relate flow and microhabitat to fish distribution (e.g., Bovee 1986; Lee and Terrell 1987).
- **Endangered Colorado River fishes**: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]] (e.g., Tyus 1987; Tyus 1991; Marsh and Douglas 1997).

## Key Findings

The document itself reports no quantitative results. Its main contributions are:

1. **Coded taxonomy of about 70 fish species and genera**, organised by family, allowing cross-referencing of the review's tables.
2. **A classification of study settings** into field and laboratory categories, enabling the authors to weight evidence by study type.
3. **A bibliography of roughly 420 numbered references**, the bulk concerning predator-prey interactions, competition, introductions and habitat selection in freshwater fishes.
4. **Several flagged references** (marked with asterisks or noted as not reviewed), indicating that part of the literature base was excluded from the analysis.
