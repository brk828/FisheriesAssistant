# How to Throw a Castnet

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fishing-gear

## Executive Summary

Fishery Leaflet 609, issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] through its [[Bureau of Commercial Fisheries]] in March 1968, is a pictorial, step-by-step guide to throwing a [[Castnet]]. Written by fishery methods and equipment specialist Hilton M. Floyd at the Exploratory Fishing and Gear Research Base in Pascagoula, Mississippi, it responds to frequent public requests following the earlier Fishery Leaflet 579 on machine-made castnet construction. The document is an operational gear-handling manual rather than a research study; it contains no biological, habitat, or catch data.

## Background & Description

The leaflet presents one of several possible castnet-throwing techniques, chosen for being relatively simple and conventional. The author notes that small nets (3 to 5 feet long) are easier to master than larger ones, and that the skill mainly requires practice and patience.

Equipment terminology covered in the leaflet includes the net's parts, the [[Handline]], and the [[Leadline]]. Figure plates illustrate a Spanish castnet and an English castnet as reference types.

## Key Findings

The leaflet's main content is a nine-step procedure, illustrated with eleven figures:

1. **Learn the parts of a castnet** (Figures 1 and 2 show Spanish and English designs).
2. **Loop the handline around the right wrist.**
3. **Coil the handline clockwise** and hold the coil in the right hand.
4. **Coil the netting clockwise** to within about 2.5 feet of the leadline, holding it in the right hand.
5. **Separate about one-third of the loose netting** into the left hand.
6. **Place the leadline in the mouth**, on the vertical line between the hands and nearest the body. The net is then ready to throw.
7. **Lean the body to the right** with arms extended, keeping the hands apart.
8. **Swing the net about 180 degrees and release it.** The author stresses that release from the hands occurs a moment before release from the mouth, so this step requires practice.
9. **Haul in the net**, keeping the handline coiled in the right hand for the next cast.

Figure 11 covers preparation for throwing a small castnet: hold the net clear of the ground, hold the leadline nearest the body in the mouth, and hold the leadline with the left hand at arm's length.

The leaflet's central conclusion is that a clear pictorial sequence, combined with practice and patience, is enough to teach castnet throwing.
