# Mitochondrial DNA Variation Within and Among Populations of the Little Colorado Spinedace

## Executive Summary
This report presents a mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) restriction site analysis of the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), a federally threatened [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid fish]] endemic to the Little Colorado River drainage in Arizona. Using restriction endonuclease digestion of mtDNA from 134 individuals across five collection sites, researchers identified six closely related haplotypes distributed unevenly among three genetically distinct population groups: (1) [[Chevelon Creek]], (2) [[East Clear Creek]], and (3) the [[Upper Little Colorado River]] complex (mainstem at St. John, Nutrioso Creek, and Rudd Creek). Despite low overall sequence divergence (<0.7%) among haplotypes, a high proportion of genetic diversity (~65%) was partitioned among localities rather than within them, indicating substantial population subdivision (overall θ/F<sub>ST</sub> = 0.5542) driven by restricted gene flow rather than deep evolutionary divergence. These findings support managing the three regions as distinct conservation units.

## Background & Description
*Note: This study is a molecular genetics survey and does not report external morphological data. Morphological and ecological descriptions of L. vittata are referenced from Miller (1963) and Minckley and Carufel (1967).*

- **Species**: *Lepidomeda vittata* — [[Little Colorado Spinedace]]
- **Family**: [[Cyprinidae]]
- **Genetic marker studied**: Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), estimated genome length ~16.5 kb
- **Restriction enzyme survey**: 15 six-base recognition endonucleases (BamHI, BclI, BglII, BstEII, EcoRI, HindII, MluI, NcoI, NdeI, NheI, PvuII, SacI, SacII, XbaI, XhoI) yielding 44 total restriction sites
- **Haplotype diversity**: Six distinct composite haplotypes identified (Haplotypes 1–6), differing from one another only by single restriction site changes
- **Sequence divergence among haplotypes**: <0.7% (Table 1), indicating very recent common ancestry among all sampled populations

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historic range**: Throughout the Little Colorado River drainage, Arizona
- **Current range**: Restricted to three regions due to habitat degradation and interactions with non-native fishes (Minckley and Carufel 1967):
  1. [[Chevelon Creek]] (Coconino County, AZ)
  2. [[East Clear Creek]] (Coconino County, AZ)
  3. [[Upper Little Colorado River]] system — mainstem at St. John, [[Nutrioso Creek]], and [[Rudd Creek]] (Apache County, AZ)
- **Habitat type**: High-elevation streams with a wide variety of microhabitats (Miller 1963)
- **Conservation status**: Federally listed as **Threatened** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (USFWS 1987), with designated critical habitat
- **Threats**: Habitat degradation and competition/predation from introduced non-native fish species
- **Population connectivity**:
  - [[Nutrioso Creek]] and [[Rudd Creek]] populations are nearly genetically identical (θ ≈ 0), suggesting a single interbreeding population with high gene flow
  - The mainstem [[Little Colorado River]] at St. John is closely related to the Nutrioso/Rudd cluster (~0.05% sequence divergence)
  - [[Chevelon Creek]] and [[East Clear Creek]] each harbor a single unique, fixed haplotype, indicating isolation from the upper LCR cluster and from one another
  - Geographic distance and habitat quality likely restrict dispersal between lower Little Colorado tributaries (Chevelon, East Clear) and upstream populations

### Haplotype Distribution
- **Chevelon Creek**: single unique haplotype (fixed; Haplotype 6)
- **East Clear Creek**: single unique haplotype (fixed; Haplotype 2)
- **LCR at St. John**: two haplotypes (Haplotype 4 ~75%, Haplotype 5 ~25%)
- **Rudd Creek**: two haplotypes (Haplotypes 3 and 4, near-equal frequency)
- **Nutrioso Creek**: three haplotypes (Haplotypes 3 and 4 near-equal, Haplotype 1 in 3/30 individuals)

### Nucleotide Diversity (Table 3)
- Chevelon Creek: 0.000000 (monomorphic)
- East Clear Creek: 0.000000 (monomorphic)
- Nutrioso Creek: 0.002101
- Little Colorado River: 0.002482
- Rudd Creek: 0.002089
- **Average within-population diversity**: 0.001335
- **Average divergence among localities**: 0.002508
- **Total nucleotide diversity**: 0.003842
- **Proportion of diversity among localities**: 65.28% — the majority of genetic variation is distributed *between* populations, not within them

### Population Subdivision (θ / F<sub>ST</sub>, Table 4)
- **Overall θ (all localities)**: 0.5542 (significantly different from zero via jackknifing) — indicates substantial population structure
- Haplotypes 2 and 6: θ = 1.0 (completely fixed/private to East Clear and Chevelon, respectively)
- Haplotypes 3, 4, 5: moderate-high θ values (0.2219–0.3466), reflecting divergence of the upper LCR/Nutrioso/Rudd group from Chevelon and East Clear
- Excluding Chevelon and East Clear Creek sequentially: overall θ drops to ~0.41, then ~0.18
- **Nutrioso vs. Rudd Creek only**: θ ≈ 0.0001 (not significantly different from zero) — essentially one panmictic population

### Sequence Divergence Between Population Clusters (Figure 2)
- Nutrioso Creek ↔ Rudd Creek: ~identical (0.00 divergence)
- Upper LCR cluster ↔ Chevelon Creek: 0.25% divergence
- Chevelon Creek ↔ East Clear Creek: 0.202% divergence

### Conservation/Management Implications
- Data support recognition of **three distinct management units**: [[Chevelon Creek]], [[East Clear Creek]], and the [[Upper Little Colorado River]] complex (St. John mainstem + Nutrioso + Rudd creeks)
- Though genetic differences among regions are small in absolute terms and may not be adaptively significant, they reflect restricted historical gene flow
- Recommendation: conserve populations independently within each of the three regions to preserve total existing genetic variation in *Lepidomeda vittata*, regardless of adaptive significance
- Findings are attributed to historical isolation of stocks into geographic refugia followed by stochastic genetic differentiation, rather than necessarily reflecting only recent anthropogenic fragmentation
