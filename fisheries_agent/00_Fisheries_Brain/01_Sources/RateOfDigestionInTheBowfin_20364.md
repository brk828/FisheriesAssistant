# Rate of Digestion in the Bowfin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #amiidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 study by [[Gerald E. Herting]] and [[Arthur Witt Jr.]] (Missouri Cooperative Fishery Unit, University of Missouri) measured the rate of digestion in young-of-the-year [[Bowfin]] (*Amia calva*) held at 21°C and fed mosquitofish. The authors compare these results with previously published data for the [[Longnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus osseus*). Bowfin digested a meal equal to 4.9 percent of body weight in 28 to 32 hours, whereas longnose gar digested a larger ration (6.7 percent of body weight) in 24 hours. The authors conclude that the bowfin has a smaller daily ration and a slower digestion rate than the longnose gar, both within the Holostei.

## Background & Description

- **Study subject:** Young-of-the-year [[Bowfin]] (*Amia calva*), a holostean fish.
- **Sample:** 22 fish, mean weight 18 g (range 11 to 33 g), mean total length 134 mm (range 115 to 161 mm).
- **Comparison species:** [[Longnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus osseus*), with data from [[Netsch and Witt 1962]].
- **Rationale:** Digestion rates help interpret [[Food Habits Studies]] by indexing feeding frequency, amount eaten, and feeding periodicity, and may explain growth-rate differences among fishes.
- **Funding and setting:** Contribution from the [[Missouri Cooperative Fishery Unit]] (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Missouri Department of Conservation, Edward K. Love Foundation, University of Missouri), conducted at the [[Gaylord Memorial Laboratory]] under NSF Grant GB-699.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Experimental conditions:** Fish were held in aquaria at a nearly constant 21°C. Food was withheld for 48 hours before feeding.
- **Feeding protocol:** Each bowfin was force-fed three [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), with ration equal to 4.9 percent of body weight. A preliminary test allowing unlimited feeding set the ration at 5.0 percent.
- **Sampling design:** At selected intervals, three bowfin were sacrificed and stomach contents were weighed and examined.
- **Comparative temperature:** Longnose gar were held at 26.4°C in the earlier study. [[Dillard 1965]] reported bowfin at 26.2°C with an average daily ration of 6.48 percent of body weight.
- **Taxonomic context:** The paper contrasts the Holostei (bowfin, gars) with the Teleostei, including [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Warmouth]], bullheads, [[Rock Bass]], and [[Pumpkinseed]], citing [[Hunt 1960]] on Florida gar and [[Pearse 1924]] on food consumption.

## Key Findings

- **Digestion onset:** Digestion of mosquitofish began within 2 hours of feeding. Prey weight initially increased through water absorption in the stomach.
- **Digestion duration:** Complete digestion of the 4.9 percent ration required 28 to 32 hours in bowfin, compared with 24 hours for longnose gar digesting 6.7 percent of body weight.
- **Digestion stages (Table 1, bowfin at 21°C):**

| Time (h) | Percent of meal digested | Condition summary |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | Digestion begins in posterior stomach; mucus and some scales gone |
| 8 | 15.6 | Caudal fins and most scales gone; fish identifiable to genus |
| 12 | 24.5 | Fish fused but recognizable as three individuals |
| 16 | 46.7 | Scales and skin gone; fish fused into a mass |
| 24 | 62.3 | Pulpy mass; some fish identifiable only as remains of 2 or 3 fish |
| 28 | 99.7 | Small pulpy mass; digestion complete in one test animal |
| 32 | 100.0 | Fish completely digested |

- **Interspecific comparison:** Bowfin digest more slowly than longnose gar. The authors attribute part of the gar's rapid digestion to higher temperature (26.4°C), though the larger gar ration may have offset this effect.
- **Ration and growth:** Dillard reported lower growth rates and daily rations for bowfin than for longnose gar, consistent with slower digestion.
- **Ration size effect:** [[Hunt 1960]] showed that larger rations of Florida gar increased time to complete digestion.
- **Holostei versus Teleostei:** Largemouth bass and warmouth consumed more food and digested it faster than Florida gar. Several Teleostei consumed more food than longnose gar. The authors conclude that daily ration and digestion rate are generally lower in the Holostei than in some Teleostei.
