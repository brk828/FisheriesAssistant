## Executive Summary

This document is a management/regulatory record detailing candidate and approved introduction (translocation) sites for two federally endangered small fishes native to the American Southwest: the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*). The text catalogs specific legal-survey locations (Township/Range/Section) across Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) regions, organized by their regulatory protection status under the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]]. Sites are divided into three tiers: (1) sites under full ESA protection, (2) sites pending U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service designation as "Non-essential Experimental" populations, and (3) additional experimental nonessential stocking sites. The document also lays out six ecological/hydrological criteria used by biologists to select suitable introduction (refuge) sites for the Gila Topminnow.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] are small-bodied, live-bearing/egg-laying fishes endemic to desert aquatic habitats of the Gila River basin and lower Colorado River drainage in Arizona and Sonora, Mexico. Both species have suffered severe range contraction due to habitat loss, water diversion, and introduction of non-native predatory and competitive fishes (e.g., mosquitofish, other poeciliids). As a conservation strategy, wildlife agencies (Arizona Game and Fish Department, in coordination with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) have pursued a strategy of establishing multiple isolated "refuge" or "introduction" populations in protected, fishless perennial water sources to reduce extinction risk from any single catastrophic event (e.g., flash flood, drought, predator invasion).

The document reflects a legal/administrative framework distinguishing:
- **Fully protected populations** — established under full ESA Section 9 protections.
- **"Non-essential Experimental Population" (NEP) sites** — a regulatory designation under ESA Section 10(j) allowing more flexible management (e.g., easier removal/relocation, reduced permitting burden) while still supporting recovery goals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Introduction Site Selection Criteria (for [[Gila Topminnow]])
The paper specifies six critical habitat criteria for selecting suitable introduction sites, reflecting the species' ecological tolerances and vulnerabilities:

| # | Criterion | Detail |
|---|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Perennial water source | Site must not dry out seasonally |
| 2 | Absence of non-native fish species | Prevents predation/competition |
| 3 | Low probability of flash flood | Minimizes catastrophic population loss |
| 4 | Size, depth, and flow constraints | Ponds < 1 m depth; streams < 4 cfs flow |
| 5 | Elevation | Below 5,000 ft. |
| 6 | Water quality | Must meet suitable water quality standards |

These criteria highlight the species' adaptation to small, shallow, low-elevation desert waters typical of the [[Sonoran Desert]] and [[Gila River Basin]] ecosystem, and underscore the primary threats to their persistence: [[Non-native Fish Introductions]], habitat desiccation, and flash-flood disturbance regimes characteristic of arid-land hydrology.

### Habitat Types Represented
Introduction sites span a range of small desert water bodies:
- Natural springs (e.g., Mescal Warm Springs, Cold Spring, Bear Skin Spring)
- Man-made wells and tanks (e.g., Howard Well, Construction Well, San Simon Tank, Mucho Tank)
- Canyon streams and washes (e.g., Hot Spring Canyon, Spring Canyon, Watson Wash)
- Ponds (e.g., Porter Wash Ponds)

This diversity reflects the flexible habitat use of both species within the strict physical constraints outlined above.

### Regional Distribution
Sites are distributed across multiple AGFD administrative regions (I, III, IV, V, VI) and resource areas, including:
- **SSRA** (San Simon Resource Area)
- **GRA** (Gila Resource Area)
- **PRA** (Prescott Resource Area, inferred)
- **LGRA** (Lower Gila Resource Area, inferred)

This wide geographic spread across Arizona is consistent with a "distributed refuge" conservation strategy to buffer against localized extinction events for both the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]].

## Key Findings

- **Six-criterion habitat screening protocol** established for Gila Topminnow introductions, emphasizing perenniality, absence of competitors/predators, flood risk, shallow/low-flow physical dimensions, low elevation (<5,000 ft.), and water quality.
- **Five sites** (Howard Well, Mescal Warm Springs, Big Spring, Cold Spring, Green Tanks) identified as under **full ESA protection** for combined Gila Topminnow/Desert Pupfish introductions.
- **~28 additional sites** listed pending USFWS preparation of a "Non-essential Experimental" population package, spanning AGFD Regions I and V, predominantly in GRA and SSRA resource areas.
- A **cross-stocking strategy** is documented: sites historically holding one species (e.g., Desert Pupfish at Mesquite Spring) are being evaluated for co-introduction of the other species (Gila Topminnow), and vice versa (e.g., Peoples Canyon, Tres Alamos Falls, Grapevine Springs/Yerba Mansa for Gila Topminnow sites receiving Desert Pupfish).
- A **third stocking category** lists nine additional sites (e.g., Dandrea Spring, Sooz Spring, Doe Peak Spring, Hidden Spring, Chalky Spring, Larry Creek, Hackberry Spring) designated for stocking as "Experimental Nonessential" populations under Section 10(j) of the ESA, spanning Resource Areas PRA and LGRA in Regions II, III, IV, and VI.
- The tiered regulatory structure (full protection vs. experimental nonessential designation) illustrates an adaptive management approach balancing **species recovery goals** with **landowner and management flexibility**, a strategy relevant to other imperiled desert fishes such as the [[Gila Chub]] and [[Sonoran Desert Fish Conservation]] programs generally.
- Legal descriptions (Township/Range/Section) provided for all sites enable precise GIS-based habitat tracking, indicating this document served as an operational field-management and regulatory-compliance reference rather than a primary research study.
