## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary research paper but a formal denial letter issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Division of Law Enforcement) in response to an application by Mr. Ken Harper and Dr. Boyd Kynard (University of Arizona, Department of Watershed Management) for an Endangered Species Act permit. The applicants sought to "take" (collect/sacrifice) specimens of the [[Apache Trout]] (*Salmo apache*) for a scientific study examining food habits and the effects of logging on trout stream habitat. The Service denied the permit, citing sufficient existing knowledge from analogous species, non-lethal alternative methodologies, and insufficient literature review by the applicants.

## Key Findings
1. **Permit Denial**: FWS declined to issue an endangered species take permit (FWS/LE PRT 8-36-C) for lethal sampling of Apache Trout for a proposed food habits/logging impact study.
2. **Redundancy Argument**: The Service determined that sufficient food habits and food preference data already exist for ecologically similar trout species, making the sacrifice of an endangered species scientifically unnecessary.
3. **Non-Lethal Alternative Methodology Recommended**: Effects of logging on the species can be assessed indirectly through habitat change monitoring (e.g., changes in food organism quality/quantity) rather than direct collection of the endangered fish.
4. **Literature Review Deficiency**: The applicants' submitted literature review was judged inadequate regarding the existing body of research on the effects of logging on trout stream ecosystems, contributing to the permit's rejection.
5. **Regulatory Precedent**: This case illustrates early implementation of Endangered Species Act permitting standards (post-1973 ESA) balancing scientific research needs against conservation of a threatened/endangered salmonid, and reflects the precautionary principle in permitting decisions for the [[Apache Trout]].
