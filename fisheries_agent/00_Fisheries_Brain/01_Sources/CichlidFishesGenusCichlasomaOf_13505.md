# Cichlid Fishes (Genus Cichlasoma) of the Rio Panuco Basin, Eastern Mexico, with Description of a New Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic revision from the [[University of Kansas Museum of Natural History]] (Occasional Papers No. 104, 1983) by Taylor and Miller clarifies the status of the cichlid genus *Cichlasoma* in the [[Rio Panuco Basin]], the second largest Atlantic-slope drainage of Mexico. The authors resolve confusion over [[Cichlasoma Steindachneri]], a name that had been applied to three distinct species, provide a dichotomous key to the native Panuco cichlids, and describe a new species, [[Cichlasoma Pantostictum]]. The basin's cichlids comprise two widespread species, [[Cichlasoma Labridens]] and [[Cichlasoma Cyanoguttatum]], and three more restricted forms, plus one exotic tilapia.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** The Rio Panuco enters the Gulf of Mexico at Tampico. Its northern arm is the Rio Tamesi. Of about 75 known fish species in the basin, 25 are endemic. The 25 families represented are divided into four primary and five secondary freshwater families, and cichlids are among the secondary group.
- **Cichlid diversity:** Five native *Cichlasoma* are recognized in the basin: two widespread species (C. cyanoguttatum, C. labridens), and three restricted species (C. bartoni, C. steindachneri, and the new C. pantostictum).
- **Exotic species:** [[Tilapia Aurea]] has become established in the upper Rio Verde drainage (San Luis Potosi) and is reportedly undergoing a population explosion (pers. comm., 1981). It is distinguished from native cichlids by having only three anal spines.
- **Methods:** Counts and measurements follow [[Taylor and Miller 1980]]. Museum abbreviations include UMMZ (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology), KU, TU, CAS (SU), FMNH, and USNM.
- **Key characters:** The native species are separated mainly by the shape of the outer jaw teeth (incisor-like in the [[Herichthys Group]]), anal spine count (IV-VII), head shape, mouth angle, and the presence of molariform pharyngeal teeth. The [[Parapetenia Group]] contains conical, canine-enlarged jaw teeth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat preferences.** Most Panuco cichlids favor clear rivers and streams 3-40 m wide with moderate to strong current, rocky riffles and pools, and sand, gravel, rock, or boulder bottoms. Depths were generally under 1 m. Water temperatures ranged from 18 to 30.5 °C, and visibility was rarely below 0.6 m. [[Cichlasoma Labridens]] occurs from about 50 to 460 m commonly and has been collected up to 1100 m at Puerta del Rio.

**La Media Luna.** This warm-spring marsh-lagoon system south-southwest of Rioverde is an exception to the riffle preference. It is very clear, bluish, and sulfurous, with temperatures of about 27-30 °C, flocculent silt and marl bottoms, and dense submerged vegetation. A salinity of 1.3 ppt was recorded on 17 May 1978.

**Coastal lagoons.** [[Laguna de Chairel]] near Tampico is a brackish, muddy lagoon with salinity of 0.5 to 4 ppt (0.5 ppt measured in 1979), vegetation including *Eichhornia*, *Azolla*, *Vallisneria*, and *Chara*, and a water temperature of 16.6 °C.

**Associated fauna.** The basin fish fauna spans 10 families, including Clupeidae, Characidae, Cyprinidae, Catostomidae, Ictaluridae, Cyprinodontidae, Goodeidae, Poeciliidae, Mugilidae, Cichlidae, and Eleotridae. Sympatric species at key sites included *Dionda* spp., *Notropis* spp., *Gambusia* spp., *Poecilia* spp., and *Gobiomorus dormitor*.

**Trophic link.** Development of molariform pharyngeal teeth in *C. labridens* appears strongly correlated with mollusk consumption. Individuals from La Media Luna commonly had crushed snails in the gut, along with enlarged molars and massive pharyngeal plates.

## Key Findings

**1. Redefinition of *Cichlasoma steindachneri*.** The species is slender-bodied, with a long, pointed head and a lower jaw that projects. It has conical pharyngeal teeth and a black blotch in the pectoral axil. It is known only from the montane Rio Tamasopo-Gallinas and Rio Ojo Frio drainages of San Luis Potosi. The original description was based on 10 specimens, the largest only 61 mm TL. Earlier records, including Meek (1904), combined this species with *C. labridens*. Meek's Rascon collection contained both species.

**2. Key to native Panuco species.** The key separates species by outer jaw tooth shape, dorsal and anal spine counts, pelvic fin length, head length relative to body depth, mouth angle, pharyngeal dentition, and speckling pattern.

**3. *Cichlasoma labridens*.** This moderately elongate species is the most widespread native cichlid in the basin. Key features are a small to moderate mouth angled downward, molariform pharyngeal teeth, and a magenta pectoral-axil spot in life. Breeding adults show a contrasting black and white pattern. Meristics include anal spines most often V (311 of 480 counts) and VI (73), and dorsal spines most often XVI (172). Morphometric variation is extensive, and the species is recorded from Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosi, Veracruz, Queretaro, and Hidalgo.

**4. *Cichlasoma pantostictum* sp. nov.** The holotype is UMMZ 207699, a non-gravid female of 79.5 mm SL from Laguna de Chairel near Tampico, collected 29 December 1952. Paratypes come from Laguna de Chairel, Laguna de la Puerta, and the Rio Sabinas. The species is diagnosed by a body entirely covered with small dark brown spots (except the abdomen and ventral head surface), longer dorsal and anal fin bases, and a shorter caudal peduncle. Anal spines are predominantly VI (20 of 23), and the new species has more gill rakers than most *C. labridens*. Spots fade in individuals over 70 mm SL. No ripe gonads were found, so breeding coloration is unknown. The specific name refers to the spotting ("spotted all over").

**5. *Cichlasoma bartoni*.** It usually has four anal spines and shorter pelvic fins than *C. labridens*. In breeding condition it has a uniformly black lower head and body, and it lacks the pectoral magenta spot. Electrophoretic data from La Media Luna give Nei's I of 0.887 (21 loci) between *C. bartoni* and *C. labridens*, a level similar to that reported for Sea of Galilee cichlids.

**6. *Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum*.** This species is distinguished by incisor-like outer jaw teeth in the Herichthys group. It has 5 or 6 lateral blotches and a caudal spot. Its distribution extends from southwestern Texas through northeastern Mexico to the Rio Panuco basin. It was recently introduced into the upper Rio Verde (La Media Luna). The authors did not revise this widely distributed species.

**7. Tilapia.** [[Tilapia Aurea]] is an exotic that is spreading in the upper Rio Verde drainage. It is easily separated from native cichlids by its three anal spines.

**Overall significance.** The paper removes long-standing synonymy errors, adds one new species from the lower Panuco-Tamesi system, and documents close phylogenetic relationships within the Parapetenia group, particularly between *C. labridens* and *C. pantostictum*. Their known sympatry is restricted to the Rio Sabinas, so further collecting in the lower Rio Tamesi may reveal more overlap.
