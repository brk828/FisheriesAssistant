# Memorandum on Fall Meeting and Sampling for Virgin River Recovery Team

## Executive Summary
This document is a compilation of two administrative/scientific communications related to the [[Virgin River Recovery Team]], a multi-agency effort coordinated by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources to manage and protect native fishes of the Virgin River system. The first is a logistical memorandum (Sept. 2, 1993) from Leo Lentsch scheduling a fall meeting and seine sampling event. The second is a taxonomic status letter (Aug. 20, 1993) from the Chair of the AFS/ASIH Fish Names Committee, addressing the valid species status of **[[Gila seminuda]]** (Virgin River chub) and an undescribed **[[Gila]] sp. (Moapa River)** population. The taxonomic letter represents a key piece of evidence supporting formal recognition and conservation protection of these taxa.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphometric data are presented in this document.
- **Gila seminuda** is described qualitatively as maintaining a **"stable morphology"** across its range, supporting its recognition as a distinct, valid species rather than a hybrid or intergrade form.
- The Moapa River *Gila* population (**Gila sp.**) is treated as morphologically and taxonomically distinct, though not described in detail here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Gila seminuda]]** occupies the **Virgin River**, a tributary within the greater Colorado River drainage system, characterized by warm, turbid desert stream conditions typical of southwestern U.S. cyprinid habitat.
- The related **Gila sp.** population occupies the **Moapa River**, a spring-fed desert stream system in Nevada, ecologically distinct from the Virgin River but within a similar arid Southwest ichthyofaunal province.
- Both populations are described as **"distinct, self-perpetuating taxa"** — implying independent reproduction and population maintenance without requiring introgression from other *Gila* species, supporting their ecological and evolutionary independence.
- Management activities referenced (seine sampling, electrofishing, monitoring stations) reflect ongoing field-based population assessment within the Virgin River ecosystem.

## Key Findings
- The **[[AFS/ASIH Fish Names Committee]]**, following review by Dr. R.D. Williams (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) and consultation with ichthyologists **D.G. Buth, C.R. Gilbert, and R.L. Mayden**, unanimously concluded that:
  - **[[Gila seminuda]]** and **Gila sp. (Moapa River)** should be regarded as **valid taxa**, irrespective of their evolutionary mode of origin (e.g., potential hybrid origin was not disqualifying).
  - *G. seminuda* fulfills recognized criteria for species-level taxonomic status due to its **stable, self-perpetuating morphology and range**.
  - Both taxa were recommended for **full protection** under fisheries management and biodiversity conservation frameworks.
  - The Committee expressed intent to reflect this taxonomic recognition in the **next official AFS/ASIH names list revision**.
- The **[[Virgin River Recovery Team]]** planned a fall 1993 meeting (Sept. 27) at the Thunderbird Inn, followed by **seine sampling** (Sept. 28–29), with agenda items including:
  1. Monitoring station standardization
  2. Standardized data input format
  3. Fall electrofishing surveys
  4. Taxonomic nomenclature updates (linked to the *Gila seminuda* status letter)
  5. Recovery Plan development
  6. Propagation and stocking policy discussions
- This correspondence illustrates the direct link between **taxonomic determination** and **conservation policy implementation** for endangered desert fish species in the American Southwest.
