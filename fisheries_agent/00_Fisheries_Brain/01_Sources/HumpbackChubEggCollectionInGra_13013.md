# Grand Canyon National Park Research Permit Correspondence for Humpback Chub Egg Collection

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #research-permits

## Executive Summary

This administrative correspondence from April 25, 1968, documents the [[National Park Service]] response to Mr. William Minkley of [[Arizona State University]] regarding a research permit application to collect [[Humpback Chub]] (Gila cypha) eggs within [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The letter provides an information packet detailing requirements for conducting research within the park, including proposal format, permit procedures, and compliance with federal and state regulations for collecting endangered species specimens.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] is an endangered cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system. This correspondence addresses a multi-park research project being conducted in both [[Grand Canyon National Park]] and [[Dinosaur National Monument]]. The National Park Service encourages research that provides scientific data for resources management objectives while ensuring studies comply with NPS Management Policies and cannot be performed outside park boundaries.

The research involves egg collection, requiring coordination between multiple agencies including the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Commission]], and the NPS Regional Director for Western Region.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] ecosystem within [[Grand Canyon National Park]] provides critical habitat for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]]. Research on reproductive biology through egg collection contributes to understanding population dynamics and informing conservation management strategies. The park's unique riverine environment makes it essential for studies that cannot be replicated elsewhere.

Collection activities must consider timing, methods, locations, and specimen numbers to minimize impacts on the endangered population while gathering necessary scientific data. All specimens remain federal property and must be catalogued into the National Park Service system for future research access.

## Key Findings

**Permit Requirements:**
- Research proposals must be submitted to the Superintendent for staff review of scientific validity and potential conflicts with park uses
- Processing time: approximately three months from receipt
- Collection permits required for obtaining study specimens of animals, plants, rocks, and natural objects
- All specimens must be catalogued into NPS catalogue system and remain federal property

**Multi-Agency Coordination Required:**
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] approval necessary for endangered species collection
- [[Arizona Game and Fish Commission]] permit required for vertebrate specimen collection (2222 West Greenway Road, Phoenix, Arizona 85020)
- Regional Director, Western Region, NPS authorization required for endangered species

**Proposal Format Requirements:**
- Title, abstract, introduction with literature review
- Clear study objectives
- Detailed methodology including field, laboratory, and analytical procedures
- Specimen type, amount, and collection methods
- Study duration and termination date
- Management implications for park use of results
- Literature cited and appendices

**Accompanying Documentation:**
- Brief study description
- Justification for why study must be performed in Grand Canyon
- Principal investigator resume/curriculum vitae
- Institutional affiliation details
- Personnel and field assistant qualifications

**Restrictions:**
- Approval may include restrictions on collection locations, timing, methods, and specimen numbers
- Specimens must be available for other researchers
- Compliance with all applicable state and federal laws required
