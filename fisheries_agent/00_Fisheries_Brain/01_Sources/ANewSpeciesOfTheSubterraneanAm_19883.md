# A New Species of Subterranean Amphipod Genus *Allocrangonyx* (Gammaridae), with a Redescription of the Genus and Remarks on its Zoogeography

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

*Note: this document concerns subterranean amphipod crustaceans, not fish. The taxon tag above reflects the actual subject.*

## Executive Summary

This 1960s-era systematic paper by J. R. Holsinger revises the North American subterranean amphipod genus [[Allocrangonyx]], a group originally erected by [[Schellenberg]] (1936) for [[Allocrangonyx pellucidus]] (Mackin, 1935). The author recognizes two species: *A. pellucidus* (Mackin), restricted to the Arbuckle Mountains of Oklahoma, and *A. hubrichti* n. sp., described from two caves in central Missouri. The paper provides a diagnostic redescription of the genus, removes it from the [[Crangonyx]] group, and places it in a new *Allocrangonyx* group. It also discusses the genus's close relationship to the European genus [[Niphargus]], its cave-restricted ecology, and a possible Cretaceous or late Cenozoic colonization history.

## Background & Description

- **Genus history:** [[Schellenberg]] (1936) established *Allocrangonyx* for a single species previously described by Mackin (1935) as *Niphargus pellucidus*. [[Hubricht]] (1943) later accepted the genus; earlier Hubricht and Mackin (1940) had retained the species in *Niphargus*.
- **Genus diagnosis:** Eyeless and unpigmented; known only from subterranean waters, mostly caves. Adults are about 14.00–22.00 mm long. Antenna 1 is 45–85% of body length, with a short, two-segmented accessory flagellum. Gnathopod 2 has a large, bi-lobed coxal gill. Uropod 3 has a two-segmented outer ramus, and the telson is broader than long and deeply cleft.
- **Morphological affinities:** Although assigned to the *Crangonyx* group, the genus shares few features with it. It is more closely allied with the European *Niphargus*, sharing mouthpart structure, propod shape, sternal process absence, and coxal gill structure.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** In males, the outer ramus of uropod 3 becomes elongate and differentiates into secondary segments with growth. This shows strong positive allometry. Females show little or no allometry in this structure.

### *Allocrangonyx pellucidus* (Mackin)
- Largest male 21.75 mm; largest female 18.00 mm.
- Outer ramus of male uropod 3 has usually 5 secondary segments (rarely 6).
- Material examined includes the type locality Bird's Mill Spring (Pontotoc Co., Oklahoma; 15 paratypes), Coal Cave, and several springs and caves near Turner Falls, Wildwoman Cave, and Bitter Enders Cave.

### *Allocrangonyx hubrichti* n. sp.
- Named in honor of Leslie Hubricht.
- Holotype male and allotype female from Saltpeter Cave (Pulaski Co., Missouri), with additional material from Maxey Cave (Pulaski Co.) and Phelps County.
- Distinguished from *A. pellucidus* by smaller adult size, longer antenna 1 and seventh pereopod (65–70% of body length), middorsal spines on dactyls of pereopods 3–7, and up to 9 secondary segments on the male uropod 3 outer ramus.
- Holotype male: 15.00 mm. Ovigerous female: 14.50 mm, carrying 5 embryos averaging 1.15 mm in diameter.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** *A. pellucidus* is known from three caves, two springs, and one seep in the Arbuckle Mountains, in Ordovician limestones, over a range of about 80 km. The largest collection came from a seep near Turner Falls (177 specimens). *A. hubrichti* is known from two caves in the Salem Plateau of the Ozark Plateau region, developed in Ordovician dolomites in the Gasconade River drainage, about 38.4 km apart.
- **Habitat:** Both species are largely cavernicolous. In Bitter Enders Cave, four *A. pellucidus* were found on submerged wood in rock basin pools 15–30 cm deep, alongside troglobitic isopods (*Asellus tridentatus*). Their large size would likely exclude them from interstitial habitats.
- **Isolation:** The two species are separated by about 520 km. Barriers include differences in limestone stratigraphy and intervening non-cavernous Permian bedrock. Species such as [[Stygonectes]] and *Asellus tridentatus* can disperse through superficial groundwater, unlike *Allocrangonyx*.
- **Reproduction:** Ovigerous females of *A. pellucidus* were recorded from February and January collections. Collections from April, May, June, and November had none.
- **Historical biogeography:** The author proposes that the ancestor colonized freshwater during the Cretaceous, when a shallow sea reached within a few kilometers of the Arbuckles. Later, late Cenozoic drying may have pushed populations into permanent cave streams, which act as refugia during droughts.

## Key Findings

1. *Allocrangonyx* is a distinct genus that should not be placed in the *Crangonyx* group. The author placed it in the new *Allocrangonyx* group, defined by a unique combination of six characters.
2. Two species are recognized: *A. pellucidus* (Oklahoma) and *A. hubrichti* n. sp. (Missouri). Earlier records of *A. pellucidus* from central Missouri were shown to belong to *A. hubrichti*.
3. The genus is more closely related to the European *Niphargus* than to any North American genus, although it differs from it in several mouthpart, gnathopod, and uropod characters.
4. Both species are restricted to cave waters and solution channels rather than superficial groundwater, which the author attributes to their large size and lack of pigmentation and eyes.
5. The author favors a subterranean origin for the genus, and suggests that drier late Cenozoic climates drove populations into cave refugia, leading to isolation and speciation.
6. Sexual dimorphism in the male uropod 3, with strong allometric growth of the outer ramus and development of secondary segments, is a key diagnostic feature.
