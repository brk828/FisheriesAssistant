# Aberrant Variants of the Agonid Fish *Odontopyxis trispinosus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #agonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1942 morphological study by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] examines two aberrant specimens of the agonid [[Odontopyxis Trispinosus]] collected in [[British Columbia]]. Both fish show fusion of the dorsal shields and keels before the dorsal fin, a feature that otherwise defines the related [[Bothragonus Swanii]]. In most other respects the two variants match typical *O. trispinosus*. On re-examination, the author concluded that both are aberrant examples of *O. trispinosus* rather than members of a new species or genus, though the author noted that this possibility could not be fully excluded.

## Background & Description

- **Origin of the specimens:** Mr. G. V. Wilby of the [[Pacific Biological Station]] at Nanaimo sent the first specimen to Hubbs, which initially appeared to represent a new genus and species. A second aberrant specimen was collected by Wilby in 1934. No further similar fish had been found.
- **Reference species:** *O. trispinosus* was described by Lockington. The author relied heavily on the description of [[Jordan and Evermann]] (1898) for the species' diagnostic characters.
- **Variant 1:** Dredged by J. L. Hart in [[Nanoose Bay]], British Columbia, on June 5, 1931. Standard length 63 mm.
- **Variant 2:** Collected by G. V. Wilby in [[Burrard Inlet]], at the Second Narrows bridge, in 10 to 20 fathoms (18 to 36 m), on February 1, 1934. Standard length 67 mm.
- **Comparison specimens:** *Bothragonus swanii*, 53 mm standard length, was poisoned in a tidal reef pool near [[La Push]], Washington, on June 25, 1926. A normal *O. trispinosus* from British Columbia (63 mm) and specimens from [[California]] were also examined.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document reports only collection localities and depths. It provides no data on water chemistry, habitat structure, prey, or population abundance. The two variants came from different inlets and years, and the author noted that several characters appear to vary geographically. Specifically, the lower pectoral rays were less exserted in California specimens than in British Columbia specimens. The author suggested this could reflect geographical variation, and that the dorsal and anal ray counts tend to increase toward the north. This latitudinal trend may explain the variant ray counts.

## Key Findings

**1. Anterior fusion of the dorsal plates and keels (main diagnostic feature)**
- The two variants share fusion of the predorsal dorsal ridges and bony shields. This makes the normally concave anterior back convex at the point of fusion.
- The fusion is diagnostic of *Bothragonus swanii*, which lends some support to the idea that the change arose from a single mutation.
- Variant 1: Fusion occurs at the fifth of six predorsal plate pairs. The median plate is slightly movable, and the body appears hinged over the anus, so the trunk and tail move somewhat independently.
- Variant 2: Ridges fuse behind their origins, with a median second plate and a median third plate. The body is not hinged at the fusion point.
- A similar anomaly was found in one of the Japanese agonids, which supports the interpretation of the fusion as an anomaly.

**2. Exserted pectoral rays**
- Jordan and Evermann (1898) describe *O. trispinosus* as having 14 pectoral rays, with the lower 5 to 7 exserted.
- Variant 1 has the most strongly exserted lower rays. On one side the fifth ray extends about one-third of the orbital length beyond the sixth. None of the seven other specimens at hand showed comparable exsertion.
- Variant 2 has the next most pronounced exsertion.
- A normal British Columbia specimen had almost as deeply incised membranes as the variants.

**3. Osseous plate counts**
- Variant 1 has 12 pairs of ventral plates before the anal fin, compared with 13 in all other specimens. It has 6 pairs along the anal fin, a count shared by only one of the seven other specimens (the rest have 4½ to 5½).
- Variant 1 has reduced head and breast scutes. The lower head total is 12 plates, which is 4 to 8 fewer than in any other specimen. The gular patch has 3 scales versus 6 or 7 in the others, and the branchiostegal patch has 2 scutes versus 3 to 5.
- Variant 2 shows no such head reduction and is closer to normal in ventral counts.
- Breast plate series vary considerably among specimens. The first series is the only constant one.

**4. Fin ray counts (Table I)**
- The two variants show a tendency toward increased dorsal and anal ray counts, and possibly reduced pectoral ray counts.
- One California specimen lacked a left pectoral fin, leaving only a socket-like depression. The right pectoral was normal.

**5. Taxonomic conclusion**
- The variant features differ between the two specimens and are less consistent than would be expected for a new species.
- Except for the dorsal fusion and related traits, the variants agree closely with [[Jordan and Evermann]]'s description of *O. trispinosus*. The proportions are about the same.
- The author concluded both fish are aberrant *O. trispinosus*. The possibility that they represent two new species in a new genus was considered remote.
