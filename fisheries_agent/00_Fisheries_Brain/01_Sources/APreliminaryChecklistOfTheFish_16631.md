# Preliminary Checklist of the Fishes of the Youghiogheny River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This preliminary checklist, published in the [[Natural History Miscellanea]] series by the Chicago Academy of Sciences, updates the known fish fauna of the [[Youghiogheny River]], a major tributary of the [[Monongahela River]]. The drainage now holds **82 known species**: 59 native, 20 introduced and 3 of undetermined status. Six species are new records for the drainage, one species is a new record for the state of Maryland, and several range extensions are documented. The authors suggest that [[Ohiopyle Falls]] and dams on the main stem limit upstream dispersal of fishes.

## Background & Description

The Youghiogheny drains about 4585 km² across Pennsylvania, Maryland and a small part of West Virginia, joining the Monongahela at McKeesport, Pennsylvania. [[Acid Mine Drainage]] pollution limited fishes from about 1890, but water quality improved considerably after 1950, and the river now supports productive fish communities.

Earlier faunal work included Fowler (1919), who produced the first comprehensive list, Mansueti (1962) for Maryland, and Jenkins et al. (1972) for the Monongahela. Some earlier lists included species that were expected but never collected in the Youghiogheny, such as Lepisosteus osseus and Hybopsis storeriana, and the authors corrected these records. Percina phoxocephala, cited by Jenkins et al. (1972), actually refers to P. oxyrhycha.

Fishes were collected by seine, electroshocker, gill net and trap net, using a qualitative sampling method ([[Qualitative Fish Sampling]]). Of 110 localities sampled, 33 are listed in Table 1 as significant range extensions or otherwise noteworthy. Voucher specimens are held at the [[University of Maryland Appalachian Environmental Laboratory]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Barriers to dispersal:** Ohiopyle Falls (about 4 m high), dams at Connellsville and Confluence (near the Maryland border), and the Mill Run Reservoir dam appear to block upstream movement. The authors argue these barriers prevented repopulation of the upper drainage after pollution declined.
- **Historical gap:** No comprehensive fish collections were made in the upper drainage (Maryland and West Virginia) before acid mine pollution in the late 1800s, so the original fauna of that area is unknown.
- **Refugia and recolonization:** Silverjaw Minnow's widespread presence suggests it survived pollution in clean tributaries and then recolonized the main stem from those refugia.
- **Stream capture:** Several species, including [[Greenside Darter]] and Silverjaw Minnow, may have entered the Potomac drainage via stream capture across the [[Savage River]] route.
- **Bait and stocking:** Several non-native records (Redside Dace, Fathead Minnow, Banded Killifish) may reflect [[Bait Bucket Introductions]] or stocking by the Pennsylvania Fish Commission.

## Key Findings

### Six New Records for the Drainage

- [[Bowfin]] (*Amia calva*): one specimen (65.7 mm SL) from [[Somerset Lake]]. Bowfin were introduced there in 1961 and 1962 and now reproduce.
- [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*): first records from Stations 30 to 33, with 12 specimens in total (for example, 5 at Station 33, 162–170 mm SL).
- [[Silverjaw Minnow]] (*Ericymba buccata*): over 300 specimens from 13 stations, from young of the year to adults. Considered native to the drainage.
- [[Rosyface Shiner]] (*Notropis rubellus*): 47 specimens at Station 11, plus records from Stations 12, 14, 24 and 25.
- [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*): 14 specimens from Stations 30, 31 and 33. Considered an introduced population.
- [[Banded Killifish]] (*Fundulus diaphanus*): 92 specimens from Stations 8, 9, 13, 15 and one collected by E. L. Cooper. The specimens are intergrades of *F. d. diaphanus* and *F. d. menona*, possibly introduced from the Lake Ontario drainage.

### New State Record

- [[Redside Dace]] (*Clinostomus elongatus*): a single 50.3 mm SL specimen at Station 3 (Bear Creek) is the first Maryland record. Its presence is possibly due to bait bucket introduction, since the same collection also yielded a [[Fathead Minnow]] and a second visit found neither species.

### Range Extensions

- [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*): a new Maryland portion record (57.7 mm SL, Station 3), plus four other locations.
- [[Longnose Sucker]] (*Catostomus catostomus*): only the fifth Monongahela-drainage record. Collections were made at Whitehorn Lake, Maryland, in 1956 and 1957 after the lake was drained. A 1977 collection after drainage yielded none.
- [[Greenside Darter]] (*Etheostoma blennioides*): collected at 16 widely distributed stations. Considered native, though the Savage River route is not ruled out.
- Hybrid *Nocomis micropogon* × *Rhinichthys cataractae*: new Youghiogheny locations at Stations 1, 2 and 23.

### Barrier Effects

Thirteen large-river species (including *Dorosoma cepedianum*, *Carassius auratus*, *Ictalurus catus* and *Ictalurus punctatus*) were collected below the barriers but not above them. The authors conclude these species were either never present above the falls or were extirpated there and cannot recolonize.

### Checklist Summary

Table 2 lists all 82 species with status codes (N = native, I = introduced, ? = unknown, UI = unsuccessful introduction) and marks species collected in 1977. Of the 82, the authors collected 56 species in this study. Notable unresolved records include *Perca flavescens* (status unknown) and *Semotilus* records requiring confirmation.
