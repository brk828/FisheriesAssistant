# E.S. George Reserve Aquatic Habitat and Amphibian Breeding Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #amphibians #pond-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is a scanned, heavily OCR-degraded research report on the aquatic habitats of the [[E.S. George Reserve]], an University of Michigan field reserve. Its core content is a numbered survey of ponds and wetlands, a species-by-habitat record of amphibian breeding, and statistical tests of which amphibian species co-occur in the same breeding habitats. A smaller part of the record lists fish taxa found in the reserve's waters. Much of the body text, along with some numbers, is unreadable, so the summary below relies on table structure, headings and legible fragments. Values are not reproduced where OCR makes them uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** A grid map of the E.S. George Reserve (Figure 1) was used to locate aquatic habitats. A second map (Figure 2) numbers each individual habitat. Permanent and temporary water are distinguished on the map, and the crosshatching marks temporary water only.
- **Habitat inventory:** Table 1 is a 1963 habitat register that gives details for each numbered habitat. The numbering appears to run to roughly 49 sites in the amphibian tables. The exact count and site characteristics are not legible.
- **Amphibian record:** A "species-by-habitat" matrix (the main table on pages 5 to 6) marks which amphibians bred in each habitat. Life-stage codes distinguish adults, subadults and juveniles. Footnotes flag habitats where no amphibian breeding populations were recorded. The matrix also flags a marsh area excluded from some analyses.
- **Fish record:** A short list of fish taxa appears against the habitat numbers, with X marks showing presence: *Phoxinus eos* (northern redbelly dace), *Notemigonus crysoleucas* (golden shiner), *Umbra limi* (central mudminnow), *Ameiurus melas* (black bullhead), *Culaea inconstans* (brook stickleback), *Esox* sp. (pike) and *Moxostoma* sp. (redhorse). Fish appear in the record as occupants of the reserve's waters, but the document is mainly an amphibian study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The habitat survey ties amphibian breeding to a mix of permanent and temporary waters. Because the reserve contains both, the breeding assemblage depends on hydroperiod. Temporary ponds generally lack fish, which is an ecologically important difference, since fish predation strongly affects amphibian larval survival. Table 2 is a coefficient-of-association matrix that compares pairs of amphibian species across habitats. It includes a count of the habitats where each pair bred together. Values marked with an asterisk are significantly different from zero at P < 0.05. Table 3 gives the probabilities that species associations arose at random, with [[Chi-Square Test]], [[Chi-Square Test With Continuity Correction]] and [[Fisher's Exact Test]] used as indicated in the footnotes.

Pond use by the amphibian group is concentrated in several key taxa, including [[Tiger Salamander]], [[Spotted Salamander]], [[Wood Frog]], [[Spring Peeper]], [[Gray Treefrog]] and [[American Toad]]. Fishless ponds are particularly important for [[Ambystoma Salamanders]]. Fish are recorded from the reserve's waters, and they probably shape where amphibians can breed successfully.

## Key Findings

- **Habitat-level breeding record:** The species-by-habitat matrix documents amphibian breeding across the reserve's numbered ponds and marshes. Some habitats show no amphibian breeding at all, and these are explicitly noted.
- **Species association analysis:** Table 2 uses association coefficients and co-breeding counts to test whether pairs of amphibian species occur together more or less often than chance would predict. Significant pairs are marked with asterisks. Several pairs of frogs and salamanders show significant values, but the OCR makes exact coefficients unreliable, so they are not quoted here.
- **Statistical methods:** Table 3 uses probability tests that include Fisher's exact test for small samples. Footnotes label which test produced each probability. This shows the authors were careful to match tests to sample sizes.
- **Fish occurrence:** Seven fish taxa are listed across reserve habitats, including [[Golden Shiner]], [[Central Mudminnow]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Brook Stickleback]] and [[Northern Pike]]. Their co-occurrence with amphibian breeding sites is a key ecological question for the survey.
- **Data quality caveat:** The document is a poor-quality scan. Most body text, several table values and part of the legend are not legible. The conclusions above rest on table structure and legible fragments. Verifying exact figures requires the original publication.
