## Executive Summary
This document is a formal comment letter (dated December 13, 1994) from Rob Bettaso (Arizona Game and Fish Department, Native Fish Field Projects Coordinator) addressed to Leo Lentsch (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources) regarding the draft **Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement and Strategy**. Rather than presenting new morphological or ecological research, the letter critiques a multi-agency conservation planning document for the [[Virgin Spinedace]], a cyprinid fish endemic to the Virgin River basin. The comments address gaps in habitat mapping, ambiguous terminology (e.g., "occupies," "effective flow"), unsupported claims lacking citations, inadequate mitigation ratios, insufficient nonnative species control measures, and concerns about the adequacy and timeline of the interagency review process. The letter provides indirect but valuable insight into the ecological threats and management priorities affecting this species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The [[Virgin Spinedace]] occupies stream reaches within the **Virgin River basin**, spanning Utah and Arizona. The letter references at least 15 identified stream reaches containing "minimum low-flows," though only a subset are confirmed as currently "occupied" by the species.
- **Flow Dependency**: The species' habitat requirements are tied to specific hydrological conditions, including:
  - Minimum low-flow maintenance
  - "Effective flow" / "effective discharge"
  - "Bankfull stage"
  - "Channel maintenance processes"
  
  These terms are flagged in the letter as needing formal definitions, indicating their centrality to the species' [[Habitat Requirements]] but lack of empirical grounding in the draft strategy.
- **Reproductive/Nursery Habitat**: The letter notes a significant data gap — the draft Strategy fails to address reproductive or nursery habitat use, raising the question of whether such data exist but were omitted, or are simply unavailable.
- **Biotic Threats**:
  - **Nonnative crayfish predation**: Crayfish are identified as likely predators of spinedace larvae and young-of-the-year (YOY) life stages. The letter recommends prohibiting all nonnative crayfish introductions.
  - **Nonnative fish species**: Concerns are raised about stocking of nonnative species (already present in the basin) and their potential undocumented movement and negative impacts. The negative impact of salmonids, **particularly brown trout (*Salmo trutta*)**, is specifically flagged as understated in the draft.
- **Habitat Modification**: Water quality degradation and hydrological alteration are cited as ongoing/threatened impacts under the Endangered Species Act-style "Present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment of habitat or range" criterion.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Status**: Contrary to a claim in the draft Strategy, Arizona **does** provide protection for the Virgin Spinedace — it was listed as a "Threatened Native Wildlife" species in Arizona's 1988 listing, with prohibitions on unpermitted take. This directly corrects a factual error in the conservation document.
- **Mitigation Ratios**: The letter criticizes the use of arbitrary mitigation ratios (e.g., suggesting a more equitable ratio such as 1:4) for impacts to occupied habitat, arguing that occupied habitat holds greater intrinsic conservation value than unoccupied historic habitat.
- **Occupancy Metrics**: A cited "80%" occupancy figure in the draft lacks methodological transparency — the letter questions how this was derived and what criteria define "occupation."
- **Nonnative Species Management**: Recommends stronger, more assertive language: eradication of nonnative fishes "where feasible," with maximal control where eradication is not possible. Also invokes the **Nonindigenous Aquatic Nuisance Prevention and Control Act of 1990** (P.L. 101-646, 16 U.S.C. 4701 et seq.) as a compliance requirement for any new species introductions.
- **Monitoring Protocol Gaps**: The letter calls for clearer articulation of seining protocols (number of sites per station) and stresses that analysis of existing monitoring data is essential before the value of a new monitoring protocol can be assessed.
- **Process/Governance Concerns**:
  - Lack of defined criteria for measuring management success and unclear adaptive management recourse.
  - Ambiguity in decision-making processes (e.g., consensus vs. majority vote among the management team).
  - Absence of a comprehensive basin map showing "core" priority protection areas versus "secondary" re-establishment/enhancement areas.
  - Insufficient legal detail regarding the [[Endangered Species Act]] petition/listing timeline (90-day initial ruling; 1-year status review and Federal Register publication) and how a Conservation Agreement might serve as an alternative to formal listing.
- **Documentation Weaknesses**: Multiple unsupported/conjectural statements throughout the Strategy (e.g., regarding flow ecology and nonnative species stocking effects) are flagged as requiring citations or explicit acknowledgment as hypotheses rather than established fact.
- **Terminological Ambiguity**: Recurrent concern over vague terms such as "occupies," "and/or," "effective flow," and "channel maintenance processes" — the letter recommends the Agreement and Strategy include a formal glossary.
