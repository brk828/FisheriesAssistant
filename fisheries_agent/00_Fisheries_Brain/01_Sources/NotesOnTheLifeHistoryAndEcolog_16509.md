# Life History and Ecology of Isoperla clio and Isogenus decisus (Plecoptera: Perlodidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #perlodidae

## Executive Summary

This 1966 study by [[G. Wayne Minshall]] and [[Judy Norman Minshall]] examines the life history, feeding and distribution of two carnivorous stoneflies, *Isoperla clio* and *Isogenus decisus*, in [[Morgan's Creek]], a relatively cool, spring-fed limestone brook in Meade County, Kentucky. Both are the only carnivorous plecopterans collected in the stream. Both are univoltine, and temperature is identified as a key factor regulating their longitudinal distribution. Note: the source is an entomological paper on aquatic insects; it contains no fish data, so the "species" field refers to stoneflies, not fishes.

## Background & Description

- **Taxa:** *Isoperla clio* (Newman) and *Isogenus decisus* Walker, family Perlodidae, order Plecoptera (stoneflies).
- **Similarity:** The two species share similar general appearance, distribution within the stream and timing of life cycles.
- **Other stream Plecoptera:** Four additional species occur in the creek: *Nemoura delosa*, *Nemoura vallicularia*, *Leuctra sibleyi* and *Allocapnia rickeri*.
- **Prior work:** Earlier North American studies cited include [[Smith (1913)]], [[Wu (1923)]] and [[Holdsworth (1941)]]; European comparisons include [[Hynes (1941, 1962)]], [[Aubert (1946)]] and [[Brinck (1949)]].
- **Context:** The data come from a community-dynamics study of [[Benthic Macroinvertebrates]] in [[Morgan's Creek]] (Minshall, 1965).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site**
- [[Morgan's Creek]] is a spring-fed brook in Otter Creek Park, about 30 mi SW of Louisville, flowing about 0.8 mi (1335 m) through a wooded valley into the [[Ohio River]].
- The stream is characterized as relatively cool, well-oxygenated and hard-water.
- Habitat is mainly a rubble-bottomed riffle-reach complex with few pools; average width at modal discharge is about 1.68 m and depth about 5 cm.

**Physical and chemical conditions (Table 1, 1 Feb 1963 to 1 Feb 1964)**
- Temperature: station means ranged roughly 10.8–12.5 °C; maxima reached 14.0–20.8 °C; minima of 0 °C occurred at lower stations.
- Discharge (Station IV): maximum 0.352 m³/s; minimum 0.005 m³/s; mode 0.007 m³/s.
- pH: generally 7.2–8.4.
- Free CO₂: means 4–8 mg/L.
- Alkalinity: means about 185–205 mg/L as CaCO₃.
- Dissolved oxygen: means about 10.5–11.6 mg/L, with saturation of 82–121%.

**Stations**
- Eight stations (I–VIII) were sampled along the creek. Station II is a steep cascade (45°), Station V a rare permanent pool, and Station VIII a sandy reach at the stream mouth. Stations I and II had no sustained populations of either species.

## Key Findings

**Abundance and sampling**
- Quantitative samples were collected at bimonthly, then biweekly, intervals, using 5-minute standardized netting. Mesh changed from 8 to 24 threads/cm on 1 February 1963.
- A total of 454 *I. clio* and 56 *I. decisus* naiads were collected in quantitative samples.
- *I. clio* was about eight times as numerous as *I. decisus*.

**Life cycle: *Isoperla clio***
- Univoltine. Hatching occurs in late August into September (first small naiads, 1.0–1.5 mm, taken 17 August 1964).
- Growth is slow in autumn and early winter, accelerates from about late February, and naiads were all ≥10 mm by mid-April.
- Qualitative collections showed the autumn 1964 cohort averaged 14.0 mm by 28 March 1965 (range 10.0–17.0 mm).
- Emergence runs April through June; the peak is in April. Maximum counts at Morgan's Creek were five adults per day, versus 20–30 observed at Falling Spring on 15 May 1964.
- Eggs are laid in packets during a skipping glide over the water; egg deposition runs April to June.

**Life cycle: *Isogenus decisus***
- Similar sequence to *I. clio*, but lagging by about 1–2 weeks. A 6.5 mm naiad from November 1962 suggests hatching at about the same time as *I. clio*.
- Growth picks up around early March; mature nymphs appear in late April, and emergence is essentially over by late May.

**Feeding**
- Animal material made up 75–100% of gut contents of both species.
- Known prey: Tendipedidae, Ephemeroptera (especially Baetidae), *Asellus* and *Phagocata*.
- Despite being the most abundant invertebrate, [[Gammarus minus]] was rarely taken. *I. clio* preferred mayflies and captured prey from behind, usually tail-first.

**Distribution**
- Both species mainly occupy riffles, in decaying leaf packs and under loose stones.
- *I. decisus*: 52% of specimens at Station IV; restricted mainly to Stations III–IV.
- *I. clio*: 48% at Station III; a wider range, extending to Station VI and, notably, the Station V pool.

**Temperature as limiting factor**
- Both species appear to need temperatures below 12–13 °C for growth and survival, especially in later nymphal stages.
- Most growth occurs in the cool January–April period. Downstream stations warm earlier, and nymphs disappear there sooner.
- No physical barriers were found; colonization of Stations I and II appears possible, so suboptimal temperature is the more likely cause of absence.

**Population dynamics**
- High discharge and adult emergence are major causes of seasonal numerical decline; warm temperatures further restrict nymphs at lower stations.
- The apparent build-up after September likely reflects increased capture susceptibility, not real population growth.

**Conclusions**
- Both species are univoltine in Morgan's Creek. Temperature is a major factor shaping their longitudinal distribution, and the two species share similar but slightly offset life cycles.
