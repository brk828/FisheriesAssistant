## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory compliance report submitted by a Forest Service fisheries biologist to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, detailing 1992 collection activities for federally listed endangered fish species under Endangered Species subpermit PRT-676811. The core activity documented is the collection of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) at two Arizona sites, one of which was conducted in coordination with a Biological Opinion for a prescribed burn operation. The report also includes an application for subpermit renewal covering voucher specimen collection of five additional endangered fish taxa native to the [[Gila River Basin]] and [[Colorado River Basin]]. The stated purpose of all collection activity is habitat/population monitoring and species recovery support on or near the Coronado, Prescott, and Tonto National Forests.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*): Collected from two distinct Arizona habitats — [[Redrock Canyon]] (Santa Cruz County) and [[Cave Creek]] (Maricopa County) — indicating a distribution across small desert stream systems associated with the [[Sonoran Desert]] riparian corridors within or adjacent to National Forest lands.
- The Cave Creek population was subject to habitat disturbance risk from the **Quien Sabe Prescribed Burn**, necessitating collection under a Biological Opinion (2-21-91-F-299) issued by the Phoenix Field Office — indicating overlap between fire management practices and endangered [[Poeciliidae]] habitat conservation.
- The renewal application targets a broader endangered fish community spanning multiple watershed types: montane cold-water systems ([[Gila Trout]] in headwater streams), desert cienega/stream systems ([[Yaqui Chub]], [[Desert Pupfish]]), and large mainstem river systems ([[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Colorado River]] system).
- Collection methods specified for future permitted work include electrofishing, seining, and handnetting — standard non-lethal survey techniques for small-bodied freshwater fishes.
- All live specimens were intended to be returned to their collection site, minimizing population-level impact; only unavoidable mortalities and designated voucher specimens were retained.

## Key Findings

- **373 Gila Topminnow** collected at Redrock Canyon, Santa Cruz County, AZ.
- **634 Gila Topminnow** collected at Cave Creek, Maricopa County, AZ, under the auspices of a Biological Opinion tied to a prescribed burn mitigation effort.
- Subpermit renewal requested voucher collection allowances for six endangered species: 10 [[Gila Trout]], 10 [[Yaqui Chub]], 10 [[Colorado Squawfish]], 10 [[Razorback Sucker]], 50 [[Desert Pupfish]], and 1,000 [[Gila Topminnow]].
- Voucher specimens and unavoidable mortalities were designated for accession to the **Museum of Fishes, Arizona State University**, supporting long-term scientific reference collections.
- Monitoring activity was explicitly tied to habitat and population assessment on/near three Arizona National Forests: [[Coronado National Forest]], [[Prescott National Forest]], and [[Tonto National Forest]].
- The report illustrates an operational linkage between land management actions (prescribed fire) and endangered aquatic species protection protocols, reflecting interagency coordination between the U.S. Forest Service and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service under the Endangered Species Act.
