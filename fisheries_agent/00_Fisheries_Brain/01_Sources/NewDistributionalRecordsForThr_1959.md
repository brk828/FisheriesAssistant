# New Distributional Records for Three Species of Kansas Crayfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cambaridae #Crayfish #FreshwaterEcology

## Executive Summary
This 1959 note by Minckley and Deacon reports new locality records for three species of Kansas crayfish—[[Procambarus gracilis]], [[Orconectes neglectus]], and [[Orconectes luteus]]—that extend previously documented range boundaries established by Williams and Leonard (1952). The collections, made between 1957 and 1958 across several northeastern and southeastern Kansas drainages, expand known distributions westward, northward, and southward, respectively, refining understanding of crayfish biogeography within the state's river basins.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide morphological descriptions of the three species; it is a faunal distribution note rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study. Species identifications were verified by Dr. A. B. Leonard (University of Kansas Department of Zoology), and voucher specimens were deposited in the University of Kansas Department of Zoology collections. For detailed morphological diagnostics, reference is made to [[Williams and Leonard 1952]], the foundational taxonomic work for Kansas [[Crayfish]] (Decapoda: Cambaridae).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Procambarus gracilis]]**: Collected from Bluff Creek, Pottawatomie Co. An adult female was seined near a muddy bank in quiet water, suggesting a preference for slow-moving, turbid stream margins.
- **[[Orconectes neglectus]]**: Found in multiple northeastern Kansas creeks (Carnahan Creek, Bluff Creek, Fancy Creek, Mission Creek). In this region, the species is restricted to clear, rocky creeks of the [[Flint Hills]], indicating a preference for well-oxygenated, hard-substrate lotic habitats. Its Kansas distribution is disjunct, with a separate population occurring in extreme southeastern Kansas.
- **[[Orconectes luteus]]**: Collected from the Little Osage River and Marmaton River, Bourbon Co. This species is confined to the [[Marais des Cygnes River Basin|Marais des Cygnes (Osage) River Basin]] in Kansas, occupying tributary rivers within that drainage system.

## Key Findings
- **Procambarus gracilis**: The Bluff Creek, Pottawatomie Co. record (6 October 1957) is the second-westernmost known occurrence in Kansas, extending the species' known range 65 miles westward in the northern third of the state.
- **Orconectes neglectus**: The Mission Creek, Marshall Co. record (30 November 1957) extends the known range 58 miles north along the [[Big Blue River]]. This confirms a discontinuous statewide distribution, with populations in the Flint Hills region of northeastern Kansas and a separate population in extreme southeastern Kansas.
- **Orconectes luteus**: The Marmaton River, Bourbon Co. record (10 June 1958) extends the known range 25 miles south. This finding was anticipated given the species' known confinement to the Marais des Cygnes (Osage) River Basin, as both the Little Osage and Marmaton rivers are tributaries of the Osage River.
- All new records collectively refine the biogeographic boundaries first outlined by Williams and Leonard (1952), demonstrating the value of continued regional faunal surveys for updating species range maps.
