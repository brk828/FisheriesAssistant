# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin - FY 1999 Work Plan

#ichthyology #endangered-species #recovery-program #upper-colorado-river-basin #habitat-management

## Executive Summary

This document presents the Fiscal Year 1999 Work Plan for the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin, approved by the Implementation Committee on September 10, 1998. The program addresses recovery of four endangered fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]. The FY 99 budget totals $12.06 million, including $4.44 million in annual funds and $7.628 million in capital project funds from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. The work plan encompasses over 90 individual projects organized into seven recovery elements: instream flow identification and protection, habitat restoration, nonnative fish control, propagation and genetics management, research and monitoring, information and education, and program management. Major funding partners include the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], and the states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program operates under a cooperative framework involving federal agencies, states, water users, environmental groups, and power interests. The program's operational plan (RIPRAP - Recovery Action Plan) identifies priority recovery actions for the endangered fishes. The FY 99 established annual budget of $3.2 million represents a 2.3% increase over FY 98, with additional contributions from various sources bringing total annual funding to $4.44 million. Capital project funds add $7.628 million for major construction activities.

The program addresses four endangered fish species native to the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (now [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]), [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]. These species face threats from habitat loss, altered flow regimes, nonnative fish predation and competition, and barriers to migration. The work plan development follows a structured annual process involving technical committees, management committees, and the Implementation Committee, with activities scheduled from November through December of each fiscal year.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] encompasses the [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Gunnison River]], [[Duchesne River]], and their tributaries. The endangered fishes evolved in a dynamic riverine environment characterized by seasonal flow variations, warm water temperatures, and turbid conditions. Human alterations including dams, diversions, water depletions, and introduction of nonnative species have severely degraded habitat conditions.

Key habitat features for recovery include: spring peak flows for spawning habitat maintenance, adequate summer base flows, floodplain connectivity for nursery habitat, unobstructed fish passage, and reduced nonnative fish populations. The [[15-Mile Reach]] of the Colorado River and [[Yampa Canyon]] represent critical habitats. Water quality concerns include elevated selenium levels in some areas affecting fish reproduction and survival. The program recognizes that tributary streams play important but not fully understood roles in endangered fish life cycles.

## Key Findings

**Instream Flow Protection (30% of budget, $3.6 million):**
- [[Grand Valley Water Management]] project ($2.015 million) will provide up to 30,000 acre-feet through canal improvements and storage in [[Green Mountain Reservoir]]
- Coordinated reservoir operations continue to enhance spring peak flows in the [[15-Mile Reach]]
- [[Yampa River Basin Recovery and Water Management Plan]] ($205,600) developing comprehensive approach to balance water needs and fish recovery
- Steamboat Lake lease provides 2,000 acre-feet for late summer flows in [[Yampa River]] ($32,000)
- [[Colorado River Decision Support System]] (CRDSS) ($424,000) assessing water availability for instream flows
- [[Duchesne River]] flow studies and coordinated reservoir operations ongoing ($50,000)

**Habitat Restoration (34% of budget, $4.1 million):**
- [[Redlands Fish Passage]] operational, providing access to 57 miles of [[Gunnison River]]; 43 [[Colorado Squawfish]] and 20,000+ native fish have used structure
- [[Grand Valley Irrigation Company]] fish passage completed, evaluation continuing ($28,300)
- [[Price-Stubb Fish Passage]] pre-construction activities ($675,000); will restore access to 5.4 miles
- [[Floodplain Restoration Program]] ($2.74 million) includes land acquisition (14 owners, 3,000+ acres on [[Green River]]), levee removal evaluation, and experimental habitat manipulation
- [[Old Charley Wash]] managed for razorback rearing or nonnative removal depending on flow conditions
- Gravel pits at 29-5/8 Road and Jarvis site reconfigured to minimize nonnative benefits while providing surrogate floodplain habitat

**Nonnative Fish Control (6.4% of budget, $770,900):**
- Centrarchid and channel catfish removal from [[Green River]] near Ouray (20-mile reach) and [[Gray Canyon]] (10-mile reach) ($20,000)
- Small cyprinid removal from lower [[Colorado River]] and [[Green River]] in Utah ($87,500) and Colorado ($54,000)
- Channel catfish removal from [[Yampa Canyon]] ($90,800)
- Centrarchid removal from [[Colorado River]] backwaters ($56,200)
- Northern pike translocation from [[Yampa River]] ($93,700)
- Pond reclamation program targeting 10 ponds in FY 99, up to 150 through 2002 ($272,000)
- [[Highline Reservoir]] screening to prevent nonnative fish escapement ($88,000)

**Propagation and Genetics (9.4% of budget, $1.13 million):**
- Operation and maintenance of [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] ($308,000), [[Wahweap]] ($83,000), and [[Grand Valley]] facilities ($210,800)
- [[Bonytail]] experimental stocking in [[Colorado River]] (Professor Valley) and [[Green River]] ($96,000)
- Razorback sucker stocking evaluation in [[Gunnison River]] and upper [[Colorado River]] ($52,800)
- Propagation facility expansion at [[Grand Valley]] and [[Ouray]] ($390,000)
- Development of larval [[Colorado Squawfish]] diet for intensive culture ($16,900)

**Research and Monitoring (6.7% of budget, $806,300):**
- Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program (ISMP) expanded ($623,100)
- Population estimates for [[Colorado Squawfish]] in [[Green River]] and upper [[Colorado River]] reaches
- [[Humpback Chub]] monitoring at [[Yampa Canyon]], Black Rocks, Westwater, Desolation/Gray Canyon, and Cataract Canyon
- Razorback sucker basinwide monitoring ($216,000)
- Larval fish identification supporting ISMP ($50,000)
- Database management for recovery program data ($34,500)
- Selenium effects studies on larval razorback sucker ($43,400 plus $35,000 from NIWQP)

**Water User Section 7 Funds:**
- Depletion charge set at $14.13/acre-foot for FY 99 (adjusted for inflation from original $10/acre-foot)
- Approximately $180,000 available as of October 1, 1998
- Seven projects totaling $95,900 funded, including gage operations, water rights evaluation, and Steamboat Lake lease
- Funds held by [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] in interest-bearing account

**Program Management (12.4% of budget, $1.49 million):**
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] program management ($762,300)
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] program management ($150,000) plus capital projects coordination ($381,000)
- State contributions: Colorado ($110,000), Utah ($71,100), Wyoming ($12,000)

**Contingency Projects Identified:**
- Additional [[Green River]] young-of-year squawfish monitoring site ($39,400)
- Hatchery-reared razorback sucker distribution study ($21,000)
- Razorback broodstock collection from [[Lake Mohave]] if recommended ($10,000)
- Baseline population estimates for [[15-Mile Reach]] programmatic biological opinion (cost unknown)
