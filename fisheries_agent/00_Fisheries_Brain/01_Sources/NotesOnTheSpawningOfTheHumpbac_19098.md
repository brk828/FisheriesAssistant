# Notes on the Spawning of the Humpback Sucker, Xyrauchen texanus (Abbott)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #spawning-behavior #Colorado-River

## Executive Summary

This 1950 field study documents the first published observations of spawning behavior in [[Xyrauchen texanus]] (humpback sucker), a native fish of the [[Lower Colorado River]] drainage. Observations were conducted at [[Lake Havasu]], an impoundment created by [[Parker Dam]] in 1938. The author observed spawning activities during March 1950, documenting courtship behavior, spawning acts, breeding coloration, and physical characteristics of ripe males. Nine ripe males were captured (4.3-5.6 pounds, 19.0-23.1 inches fork length), but no gravid females were obtained. One larval specimen (0.4 inch total length) was collected via plankton tow. This represents the first detailed account of spawning activities for this species, though previous literature had recorded sexually mature specimens.

## Background & Description

[[Xyrauchen texanus]] is one of the few native fishes remaining in the lower [[Colorado River]] drainage. The species was originally described by Abbott (1861). Prior publications (Jordan 1891, Gilbert and Scofield 1898, Jordan and Evermann 1896, Ellis 1914, Snyder 1915, Evermann 1916, Coleman 1929, Grinnell 1914, Dill 1944, Moffett 1942, 1943) documented the species but none included information on spawning activities.

Physical characteristics of a representative male specimen (5.3 pounds, 21.5 inches):
- Pronounced breeding tubercles (approximately 3/32 inch diameter and height) on lower nine rays of caudal fin and extending anteriorly on lateral portions of caudal peduncle for approximately 2.5 inches
- Tubercles present on all seven anal fin rays
- Pelvic fins covered with smaller tubercles (approximately 1/8 inch diameter)
- S-shaped pattern of tubercles over frontal bone
- Mouth sub-inferior
- Predorsal bone plate height: 1.6 inches (posterior edge of opercle) to 1.2 inches (base of first dorsal ray)
- Two separations in bone plate between dorsal spine base and nuchal hump apex, dividing plate into thirds for lateral flexibility
- Heavy plate (1.0 inch high) extending entire body length below dorsal fin
- Fin ray counts constant across four specimens: D-15, A-7, C-18

Breeding coloration in males: dorsal and lateral integument black to approximately one inch below lateral line, with brilliant orange coloration extending ventrally from this point. Females appeared lighter in color, more brown than black on dorsal side.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Havasu]] characteristics (1950):
- Created October 1938 following completion of [[Parker Dam]]
- Total capacity: 770,000 acre-feet
- Maximum area: 20,000 surface acres
- Maximum depth: 82 feet
- Average depth: approximately 30 feet
- Mean air temperature: 74°F (range 29-127°F)
- Mean water temperature: 70°F (range 42-85°F)
- Mean rainfall: 6.0 inches
- Life zone: Lower Sonoran
- No thermal stratification due to constant overturn in shallow lake
- Current velocities practically zero
- High winds generally prevalent during spring months

Water chemistry (October 10, 1950):
- pH: 8.3 at 23°C
- Phenolphthalein alkalinity: 3 ppm
- Methyl orange alkalinity: 125 ppm
- Hardness: 311 ppm CaCO₃
- CaCO₃: 125 ppm
- Ca(HCO₃)₂: 186 ppm
- Total alkalinity: 618 ppm CaCO₃
- Free CO₂: 1.0 ppm
- Electrical conductivity: 994 reciprocal megohms

Spawning habitat at [[Needles Boat Landing]]:
- Small bay in northwest corner of Lake Havasu
- Bottom composition: silt over sand with scattered boulders and gravel in shallow areas
- Numerous depressions over one foot in diameter, appearing to be [[Micropterus salmoides]] (largemouth black bass) nests
- Aquatic vegetation: small clumps of blue-green algae on boulders and bottom; [[Scirpus]] sp. (bullrush) patches along marginal areas at bay head
- [[Tamarix gallica]] (tamarisk) providing considerable shade to littoral zone in bay apex
- Adjacent point: three-foot wide silt strip approximately 25 feet offshore, bordered by rubble both lakeward and shoreward; no aquatic vegetation; open beach

Associated species: [[Cyprinus carpio]] (carp) intermingling with suckers (10-15 inches length); [[Lepomis macrochirus]] (bluegill) present.

## Key Findings

**Spawning Period and Conditions:**
Spawning observed March 2, 15, 16, and 17, 1950. Surface-shore water temperatures during observations: 63-71°F. Air temperatures: 63-70°F. Spawning occurred in littoral areas, approximately 15-25 feet from shore, in water depths of 10 inches to 2 feet, over silt bottom.

**Spawning Behavior - March 15, 1950:**

Group 1 (six fish total):
- Fish observed revolving clockwise at slow rate in four-foot diameter circles
- Five fish (presumably males) exerted pressure on one fish (presumably female)
- Three males crowding on female's right side, two on left
- Two most proximate males pressing against female's sides with heads just behind her hump
- Caudal fins of all fish began vibrating violently; one fish jumped clear of water
- Violent motion raised bottom silt, obscuring further observation
- Occasional water agitation noted during 1.5-minute activity period
- Location easily determined by cloud of suspended matter
- Following spawning act, fish separated and moved away singly (two to deeper water, four parallel to shore)

Group 2 (three fish):
- Fish lying in depressed pocket (possibly [[Largemouth Bass]] nest) about three feet from shore in 10 inches of water
- Caudal fins of two outer suckers (presumably males) began vibrating
- Column of water approximately five inches high by seven inches in diameter thrown into air
- Bottom silt occluded actual spawning act
- After approximately 1.75 minutes of activity, fish separated
- Marine plankton net towed through immediate area recovered no spawn

Within 0.5 hour, 48 suckers (20-30 inches long) counted passing through area. Slight wave action in bay due to easterly breeze.

**Spawning Behavior - March 17, 1950:**

Group 1 (three fish: two males, one female presumably):
- Moving counter-clockwise in circle approximately three feet in diameter
- Males appeared to be "herding" female with heads and humps, nudging female in genital region
- After approximately 30 seconds of circular movement, all three lay on bottom on ventral surfaces
- Males' caudal fins vibrated rapidly, appearing to slap female in genital region
- Activity occurred in two feet of water approximately 25 feet from shore, over silt bottom
- Silt erupted with activity, impairing observation
- Silt cloud remained in suspension for three minutes before fish separated and moved away
- Action observed 10:05-10:13 a.m.

Group 2 (four fish: three males, one female presumably):
- Observed moving along silt-bottomed strip 10:15-10:17 a.m.
- Female escaped to deeper water; no spawning activity noted
- Female appeared lighter in color (more brown than black on dorsal side) but same size as males

Additional observations:
- By 10:45 a.m., only seven suckers in shallow area
- Several silt "eruptions" noted 50 feet offshore in 4.5-6 feet of water, indicating spawning activities
- 11:09 a.m.: six males chased one female (presumably) along shoreline over silt strip; three males left, entire group dispersed with no spawning
- 11:10 a.m.: group of four males and one female (presumably) went into violent action following short courtship; visibility impaired; by 11:13 a.m. all fish dispersed

**Collections:**
- March 2: Two ripe males seined, 21.2 and 21.3 inches, 4.8 and 5.5 pounds, both exuding milt
- March 16: Four ripe males captured in gill net (10:15 a.m.-12:00 p.m.), 21.2-23.1 inches, 4.8-5.6 pounds, all extruded milt
- March 16 afternoon: Five ripe males captured in gill net, 19.0-21.3 inches, 4.3-5.5 pounds
- March 17: Overnight gill net set captured seven ripe [[Cyprinus carpio]] and three [[Lepomis macrochirus]]; no suckers
- Total: Nine ripe male [[Xyrauchen texanus]] captured; no gravid females obtained

**Larval Collection:**
One larval humpback sucker, 0.4 inch total length, captured in marine plankton net tow on March 16 at 3:05 p.m. Tow consisted of 40-foot drag each way, covering area 15 feet by 40 feet over spawning grounds, sampling all depths. Identification confirmed by Dr. Carl L. Hubbs. No spawn recovered in plankton tows.

**Behavioral Summary:**
Spawning involved groups of 2-5 males attending single female. Courtship included circular swimming, "herding" behavior with males nudging female in genital region using heads and humps, followed by fish lying on bottom with males' caudal fins vibrating rapidly and slapping female's genital region. Spawning acts lasted 1.5-3 minutes. Violent activity raised bottom silt, creating suspended sediment clouds and obscuring direct observation of gamete release. Fish separated and dispersed following spawning. Wave action from wind or boat traffic disrupted spawning groups.
