# Fishes from the Gulf of California: Mearns Collection from Tributaries of the Gulf

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #salmonidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by John Otterbein Snyder reports on a freshwater fish collection made by [[Edgar A. Mearns]], surgeon and naturalist with the United States Army, during the survey of the Mexican boundary between January 1892 and September 1894. The specimens came from rivers draining to the [[Gulf of California]], including the [[Colorado River]], the [[Gila River]], and the [[Yaqui River]] and its tributaries. Snyder's main goal was to clarify the identity and relationships of the sucker genera *Pantosteus*, *Notolepidomyzon* and *Catostomus*, and of several cyprinid and other species. He also describes one new species, *Notropis mearnsi*.

## Background & Description

The collection was made while Mearns was in charge of the biological section of the boundary survey. Snyder notes that the faunas of the Colorado and Yaqui systems were then poorly known, and that the relationships and distribution of their fishes remained open questions. The specimens were examined against type material held at the [[United States National Museum]].

Much of the paper is a detailed morphological comparison, centred on:

- **Lip structure**: *Pantosteus* and *Notolepidomyzon* have a horny sheath on the lips, which distinguishes them from *Catostomus*.
- **Cranial structure**: the presence, shape and closure of the fontanelle between the parietal and frontal bones.
- **Scalation**: counts of lateral-line scales, scales above and below the line, and scales before the dorsal fin.
- **Proportional measurements**: body, head, fin and snout dimensions, expressed as ratios.
- **Vertebral and dorsal-crest anatomy** in [[Xyrauchen Texanus]], including the [[Weberian Ossicles]] complex.

Snyder also records colour in life, habitat notes, and some remarks from Mearns on flavour and feeding.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fishes come from arid to semi-arid drainages of the US Southwest and northwest Mexico. Localities include:

- **Lower Colorado system**: Colorado River at Yuma, where [[Xyrauchen Texanus]] and [[Ptychocheilus Lucius]] were taken.
- **Gila system**: Gila River at Gila City, Verde River near Fort Verde, Santa Cruz River near Tucson, and Clear Creek.
- **Mountain streams**: Oak Creek, where trout were found in a canyon with walls over 1,000 feet high. The canyon floor carried Douglas spruce, willow, aspen and walnut.
- **Border and Mexican tributaries**: San Bernardino River, Sonoyta River, Altar River, Bear Creek and Cajon Bonito Creek.

Snyder places the Colorado and Yaqui faunas in a historical context. The two drainages were once connected through a channel that is now separated by salt water. Colorado forms also reached the larger streams of southern California, where they now survive as [[Notolepidomyzon]] *santa-anae* and [[Richardsonius Gibbosus]]. Snyder states that the [[Bonneville Basin]] is the centre of distribution of both *Pantosteus* and *Notolepidomyzon*.

The notes mention several behavioural and ecological details: suckers were caught by snaring in deep rock holes, *Gila robusta* was taken on baited hooks, and a stomach of one *G. robusta* contained a frog. The trout from Oak Creek had stomachs containing sand, pebbles and insects.

## Key Findings

**1. Revised generic and specific limits in the Pantosteus group**
- The paper separates two natural groups. *Notolepidomyzon* has a thick cranium with a closed suture between parietals and frontals. *Pantosteus* has a thin cranium with an open fontanelle.
- *Notolepidomyzon* is recognised as available for the thick-skulled group, and *Pantosteus* is retained for the thin-skulled group.
- Snyder concludes that the Colorado system holds only two species of this group. [[Notolepidomyzon Clarki]] includes *Pantosteus arizonae* and the cotypes of *Catostomus clarki*. [[Pantosteus Delphinus]] includes *P. virescens* and *P. bardus*.

**2. Synonymies**
- *Catostomus gila* Kirsch is synonymous with *Catostomus insignis*.
- *Richardsonius gibbosus*, *Gila intermedia* (Girard) and *Gila nigra* Cope represent a single species.
- *Gila seminuda* is treated as a synonym of [[Gila Robusta]].
- *Pantosteus columbianus* is provisionally identified with *P. jordani*.

**3. Scale and measurement data**
- [[Catostomus Insignis]]: four cotypes have 54 to 58 lateral-series scales, 11 or 12 dorsal rays, and 26 to 30 scales between the occiput and dorsal fin.
- *Notolepidomyzon clarki* from Beaver Creek have 62 to 75 scales in the lateral series. The smallest specimen examined measured 70 mm and lacked a fontanelle.
- Very large *Pantosteus* specimens measured 230 to 395 mm, and the largest weighed 14 pounds. The type of *P. virescens* (370 mm) is the largest *Pantosteus* the author had seen.
- The type of *Gila gibbosa* (170 mm) has 74 lateral scales and 8 dorsal and anal rays.

**4. New species**
- [[Notropis Mearnsi]] is described as new from five specimens collected in the Yaqui Basin. The type came from the San Bernardino River near monument 77 of the international boundary, collected by Mearns on 6 October 1893 (USNM No. 76163). Diagnostic features include a rounded, elongate body, a posterior dorsal fin, teeth in a 4-5 formula, and no dark bars or spots.

**5. Other taxa**
- [[Salmo Irideus]]: a non-native trout introduced from a California stream. Clear Creek specimens had 116 to 120 lateral-line scales, and a 140-scale specimen was also noted.
- [[Agosia Chrysogaster]]: teeth had a well-developed grinding surface, and ripe females were recorded.
- [[Plagopterus Argentissimus]]: three specimens showed rudimentary scales and papillae on the throat.
- [[Poecilia Occidentalis]]: a female of 2.5 inches from the Santa Cruz River near Tucson carried 15 eyed embryos.
- [[Xyrauchen Texanus]]: the dorsal hump is formed by a fused occipital crest and spine complex, and its skull is structurally distinct from *Catostomus*. Mearns found the flesh excellent.

**6. Research outlook**
- Snyder expects that undescribed forms may still be found, and that the ranges of known species may be extended. The status of *P. platyrhynchus*, *P. jordani* and *P. columbianus* needs further study, and *N. generosus* may occur in the upper Columbia Basin.
