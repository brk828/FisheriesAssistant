## Executive Summary
This document is an interagency administrative letter from the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] to the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], dated January 13, 1989. It serves as a stocking-recovery notification rather than a primary research paper. The core scientific content reports the recapture of a single [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), a federally endangered [[Cyprinidae]] species native to the [[Colorado River System]]. The recaptured individual is believed to be a survivor from a large-scale conservation stocking event conducted by the hatchery less than two weeks prior to capture, providing an early data point on short-term post-stocking survival and dispersal in a reservoir environment.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Gila elegans* (Bonytail Chub)
- **Recaptured Size:** 119 mm (total/standard length, unspecified in source)
- **Stocked Size (reference cohort):** ~5 inches (~127 mm) at time of release
- **Growth/Size Note:** The recaptured fish's size (119 mm) is broadly consistent with the stocked cohort's release size (~5 in), suggesting minimal growth or slight measurement variance over the ~13-day interval between stocking and capture, consistent with a recently released hatchery fish rather than a wild-recruited individual.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range:** [[Colorado River System]], historically occurring in main-channel riverine habitat with swift currents; the species is now largely restricted to reservoir/impounded habitats.
- **Habitat at Capture:** Shallow water along the western shoreline of [[Davis Basin]] (part of [[Lake Mohave]]), specifically near "Two Dollar Cove" (site #123).
- **Capture Method:** [[Electroshocking]] survey, conducted at night (October 25, 1988), a standard non-lethal sampling technique for freshwater fish population monitoring.
- **Conservation Context:** *Gila elegans* is one of the four "big-river" [[Colorado River endemic fish]] species (alongside razorback sucker, Colorado pikeminnow, and humpback chub) subject to intensive federal and state recovery efforts due to endangered status. The species is functionally extirpated from much of its historic range and maintained largely through hatchery propagation and stocking programs such as those at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

## Key Findings
- **Stocking Event:** On October 12, 1988, Dexter National Fish Hatchery released approximately 20,000 five-inch bonytail chub into the Colorado River System bordering Nevada.
- **Recapture Event:** A single 119 mm bonytail was recovered via electroshocking on October 25, 1988 — just 13 days post-stocking — in Davis Basin near Two Dollar Cove, strongly suggesting direct traceability to the October 12 release.
- **Interagency Coordination:** The letter formally requests that Dexter National Fish Hatchery notify the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (Fisheries Division) of any future native fish stocking events within the shared Colorado River System, indicating an active interstate/interagency monitoring protocol for endangered native fish reintroduction.
- **Significance:** This record represents a documented short-term survival and localized dispersal data point for hatchery-reared bonytail chub, useful for evaluating early post-release survivorship in reservoir stocking programs — a key metric for [[endangered species recovery programs]] targeting Colorado River native fishes.
- **Administrative Note:** Document stamped as received by Dexter National Fish Hatchery on January 19, 1989, confirming successful transmittal of the recovery data.
