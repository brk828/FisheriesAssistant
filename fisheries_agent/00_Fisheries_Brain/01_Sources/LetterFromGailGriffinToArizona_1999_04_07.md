## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific study but a **1999 letter from Arizona State Representative Gail Griffin to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD)**, challenging a proposed piscicide (fish poison) treatment program. The plan, developed in partnership with the **Coronado National Forest** and reportedly involving **The Nature Conservancy**, sought to apply chemical piscicides — specifically **[[Rotenone]]** and **[[Antimycin]]** — to several water sites in southeastern Arizona, including **O'Donnell Canyon** and the **Bog Hole Wildlife Area**. The stated management rationale was the removal of non-native predatory/competitive fish (bass and sunfish) suspected of contributing to the decline of the native **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*). The letter raises regulatory, ecological, and public-safety objections and requests that the eradication effort be halted pending further justification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: Isolated desert aquatic habitats in the Coronado National Forest (southeastern Arizona), including canyon stream systems (O'Donnell Canyon) and constructed/managed wetland sites (Bog Hole Wildlife Area).
- **Native vs. introduced dynamic**: The letter explicitly disputes the causal link between exotic centrarchids (bass/sunfish) and the decline of the native Gila Chub population, stating: *"It has not been proven that bass and sunfish are the reason for decline of the Gila Chub."*
- **Site-specific note**: At Bog Hole Wildlife Area specifically, the letter asserts **no native fish species are known to exist**, raising the ecological question of management justification for poisoning non-native fish at a site lacking a native species to protect.
- **Piscicide ecological risk**: Concerns are raised regarding off-target impacts of [[Rotenone]] and [[Antimycin]] on:
  - Non-target aquatic organisms downstream
  - Water quality (potential violation of Arizona Department of Environmental Quality [[ADEQ]] water and air quality discharge rules)
  - Human residents near release sites (inhalation/downwind exposure risk)
  - Recreational fisheries (loss of angling opportunity)

## Key Findings

- The **Arizona Game and Fish Department**, in coordination with the **Coronado National Forest** (and possibly **The Nature Conservancy**), proposed piscicide treatment (rotenone and/or antimycin) at multiple southeastern Arizona water sites to eliminate exotic bass and sunfish, ostensibly to benefit the native **[[Gila Chub]]**.
- The scientific justification for the eradication — that exotic centrarchids are driving Gila Chub decline — is characterized in the letter as **unproven**, highlighting a gap between the proposed management action and confirmed causal ecological evidence.
- At the **Bog Hole Wildlife Area**, no native fish are documented to currently exist, undermining the conservation rationale for treatment at that specific site.
- Regulatory concerns are raised regarding compliance with **ADEQ discharge permitting requirements** under state water and air quality law.
- A precedent is cited: in **California**, criminal charges (12 alleged violations of the California water code) were filed against the California Department of Fish and Game for comparable piscicide actions, suggesting legal/regulatory risk associated with such programs.
- Public opposition is documented via constituent complaints (calls, letters, faxes) regarding potential impacts on **human health, water resources, and recreational fishing**.
- The letter formally requests AGFD provide:
  - Data on negative impacts of rotenone/antimycin
  - Confirmation of ADEQ permitting compliance
  - Copies of any Memorandum of Understanding among AGFD, U.S. Forest Service, and The Nature Conservancy
  - Clarification on potential access/fishing restrictions if endangered species are present at treatment sites
- The representative's ultimate request: **halt the proposed poisoning operations** at O'Donnell Canyon, Bog Hole Wildlife Area, and any other Arizona waters pending further review.
