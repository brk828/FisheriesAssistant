# Selected Bibliography of Wildlife and Habitats for the Southwest

#wildlife-biology #bibliography #mammalogy #ornithology

## Executive Summary

This document is USDA Forest Service General Technical Report RM-16 (September 1975) by [[David R. Patton]] and [[Peter F. Ffolliott]]. It is an annotated-by-subject bibliography of 390 references on wildlife and habitat management in Arizona and New Mexico, covering publications from 1913 to early 1975. It is a literature compilation, not a field study, and it contains no original data.

The document has no fish, aquatic-invertebrate, plankton, or water-chemistry content, and it does not address marine or freshwater ecosystems. Its only water-related material concerns terrestrial wildlife water use, such as desert waterholes, guzzlers, and watershed yield. The fish-oriented fields in this note are therefore marked N/A.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Respond to field biologists' requests for literature, because the volume of wildlife papers in professional journals had made it hard to maintain a reference library.
- **Audience:** Students, research and management biologists, and professors working on Southwestern wildlife and habitats.
- **Structure:** A numbered reference list (1–390) in alphabetical order by author, plus a [[Subject Index]] keyed to those reference numbers. Users locate a subject, then find the author entry. A supplement lists publications not included in the subject index.
- **Scope exclusions:** Small rodents, amphibians, reptiles, and bats; articles that were hard to obtain; popular publications; most agency federal-aid job completion reports (final reports were kept); and periodicals devoted to a single species, such as the *Transactions of the Desert Bighorn Council*.
- **Authors:** Patton was a principal wildlife biologist with the [[Rocky Mountain Forest and Range Experiment Station]], assigned to the Forest Hydrology Laboratory in Tempe. Ffolliott was an associate professor at the University of Arizona's School of Renewable Natural Resources.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography is organized around these ecological and management themes:

- **Target game and wildlife species:** [[Mule Deer]] and [[White-Tailed Deer]] are the most heavily indexed mammals. Other species include [[Elk]], [[Pronghorn Antelope]], [[Desert Bighorn Sheep]], [[Barbary Sheep]], [[Collared Peccary]] (javelina), and [[Abert's Squirrel]] (also [[Kaibab Squirrel]]). Among birds, the index covers [[Gambel's Quail]], [[Mearns' Quail]], [[Scaled Quail]], [[Mourning Dove]], [[White-Winged Dove]], [[Merriam's Wild Turkey]], and raptors such as the [[Golden Eagle]].
- **Vegetation types:** [[Chaparral]], [[Sonoran Desert]] and desert shrub, [[Grassland]] and meadow, [[Oak Woodland]], [[Pinyon-Juniper Woodland]], [[Ponderosa Pine Forest]], and [[Spruce-Fir Forest]] with aspen.
- **Habitat and forage:** Food and forage (browse, shrubs, mast), habitat improvement, vegetation overstory and understory relationships, and the effects of fire, logging, and pinyon-juniper removal.
- **Livestock and land use:** Cattle-wildlife competition, range improvement, and watershed management alternatives.
- **Water:** Water supply for desert animals, waterhole use, and water requirements of quail and deer. These are terrestrial water-use topics only.
- **Population and health:** Reproduction, population decline, hunting and harvest, and wildlife diseases and parasites.

## Key Findings

- **Scale:** 390 references spanning 1913 to early 1975, with a supplement of additional publications not in the subject index.
- **Most-indexed subjects:**
  - *Deer (mule):* the largest subject entry, with many dozens of reference numbers.
  - *Food (forage):* a major cross-cutting topic linking wildlife to browse and vegetation.
  - *Quail (Gambel's, Mearns', scaled)* and *Dove (mourning, white-winged)*: heavily studied upland game birds.
  - *Ponderosa pine* and *Chaparral, brushland, sagebrush*: the most frequently indexed vegetation types.
- **Authorship and form:** A mix of journal articles, master's and doctoral theses, agency research notes, Arizona and New Mexico Game and Fish reports, and conference proceedings. Many sources are regional, such as the Arizona Watershed Symposium proceedings and the Western Association of State Game and Fish Commissioners conference proceedings.
- **Notable topics:**
  - Effects of logging, thinning, and fire on deer and elk use of ponderosa pine forest (e.g., [[Patton]] 1969; [[Pearson]] 1968; [[Reynolds]] 1962, 1966).
  - Chaparral conversion and its effects on water yield and wildlife (e.g., [[Hibbert]] et al. 1974).
  - Pinyon-juniper removal and cottontail, deer, and natural resource responses (e.g., [[Clary]] et al. 1974; [[Kundaeli]] and [[Reynolds]] 1972).
  - Diet and nutrition of deer, including rumen and fecal analysis (e.g., [[Anthony]] and [[Smith]] 1974; [[Urness]] 1969).
  - Wildlife disease, including quail malaria and elk blindness from a blood parasite (e.g., [[Hungerford]] 1955; [[Smith]] and [[Davis]] 1968).
- **Exclusions limit coverage:** Small mammals, reptiles, amphibians, and bats are absent by design. Fish and aquatic ecosystems are not covered at all.
- **Intended use:** A starting reference for finding core literature, with the note that the cited articles' own bibliographies can reveal additional sources.
