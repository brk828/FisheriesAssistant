# Electrofishing Survey at Three Fingers Lake and Fish Salvage at High Levee Pond, Cibola National Wildlife Refuge

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Parker, AZ, dated 1 June 2006) reports two field operations on the lower [[Colorado River]] system. On 30 May 2006, a multi-agency crew electrofished [[Three Fingers Lake]] and recorded no fish captures. On the following night, the crew salvaged [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] from [[High Levee Pond]] on the [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]]. Sixty razorback suckers were captured, tagged with PIT tags, and released into the Colorado River. No [[Bonytail]] were observed.

## Background & Description

The memo was written by Chuck Minckley, Project Coordinator, to the LCRFISH group and interested persons. It covers two linked activities:

- **Electrofishing survey** of Three Fingers Lake, a waterbody in Imperial County, California.
- **Fish salvage** at High Levee Pond, a pond within the Cibola National Wildlife Refuge. Salvage removes fish from a site so they can be relocated or released elsewhere.

Participating agencies included the Arizona Fishery Resources Office (AZFRO), the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR), the National Park Service (NPS), and the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (CDFG). Named staff included Jeff Lantow, Trish Delrose, Melissa, Joe Millosovich, Phil Gebelhouse, Rick Frances, and Dave Baker. The source text is partly garbled by OCR, so some details (including the coordinates given for the Three Fingers Lake boat ramp and the exact evening start time) could not be reliably recovered.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Three Fingers Lake (30 May 2006)**
- Survey window: 1530 to 2100 hrs.
- Effort: two boats, 5,279 seconds (88 minutes) of generator time.
- Results: no fish were seen or captured by [[Electrofishing]]. The only fish observed was [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). A large number of [[Bullfrog]] tadpoles were also present.

**High Levee Pond (31 May 2006)**
- The CDFG electrofishing boat was used for salvage, with the same crew plus Dave Baker.
- Two 300-foot trammel nets (2-inch mesh) were set. They captured 2 razorback suckers. All other razorback suckers were collected by electrofishing.
- Largemouth bass were also removed.

The site context matters for the findings. The razorback sucker is a native Colorado River species, and the presence of non-native predators and competitors in these waters is a recurring management concern. The memo does not discuss these issues directly, so this note does not draw conclusions about them.

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker salvage results**
- **Sixty** razorback suckers were captured.
- Mean total length was **53 cm**. Mean weight was reported as **1,897 g**.
- All fish were scanned for existing PIT tags. **Twenty-one** were recaptures, or **35%** of the sample.
- Untagged fish received **125 kHz [[PIT Tag]]s**.
- All fish were in good condition. They were released by about 2200 hrs into the [[Colorado River]] at the [[Oxbow Campground]] Recreational Area, managed by the [[Bureau of Land Management]]. The release site is 1 mile north (upriver) of High Levee Pond.

**Other species**
- Approximately forty-four **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (about 15 cm TL) were relocated to other waters by CDFG. The OCR text is garbled at this count, so the exact figure should be checked against the original.
- **No Bonytail** were seen during either operation.

**Three Fingers Lake**
- No fish were captured. Only mosquitofish and bullfrog tadpoles were observed.

**Monitoring implications**
- The recapture rate of 35% is a key result. It indicates that a portion of the population was already PIT-tagged and has been previously encountered, which supports [[Mark-Recapture]] analysis of population status.
