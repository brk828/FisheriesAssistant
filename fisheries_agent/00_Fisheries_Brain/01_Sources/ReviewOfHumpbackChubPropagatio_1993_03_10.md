# Review of Humpback Chub Propagation Plan

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Gila-cypha #conservation-genetics

## Executive Summary
This document is a peer-review memorandum from Rich Valdez (BIO/WEST, Inc., Grand Canyon Studies) critiquing a 1993 [[Humpback Chub]] propagation plan authored by Diann M. Smith for the Arizona Game and Fish Department. The memo does not present primary field data but provides expert commentary on the experimental design, logistics, and conservation risk of an egg-taking and captive propagation program intended to establish a captive broodstock of [[Gila Cypha]] from the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), a key spawning tributary in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. Key concerns include an overly complex egg-treatment matrix that could yield statistically and logistically unworkable sample sizes, risks to wild population estimates from removing PIT-tagged adults, and physiological/logistical risks during egg transport and hatchery incubation.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphometric data presented; however, the memo references standard field protocols for adult humpback chub handling:
  - Screening captured adults for **PIT tags** to identify recaptures.
  - Measuring and weighing all captured fish.
  - Checking each fish for **radiotag surgery scars** and photographing any surgical scars observed, indicating this population is subject to ongoing telemetry studies.
- Reproductive biology note: expected fecundity per female humpback chub is approximately **2,500 eggs** (citing Hamman 1982), a key figure used to evaluate the feasibility of the proposed egg-treatment design.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Primary habitat discussed: the **[[Little Colorado River]]**, a major spawning tributary for the mainstem Grand Canyon population of humpback chub, and **Bubbling Ponds Hatchery**, used as an off-site incubation/rearing facility.
- The species exists as an isolated aggregation in the [[Colorado River]] mainstem through [[Grand Canyon]], with the LCR serving as critical spawning habitat.
- Recapture rate of PIT-tagged fish in the LCR cited at **80%**, indicating a well-monitored, intensively studied population with mark-recapture infrastructure already in place (population estimates, survival indices, individual growth tracking).
- Ecological/management interdependencies noted: proposed removal of 20 adult chub for broodstock could compromise ongoing demographic studies (population estimation, survival rate assessments) conducted by BIO/WEST, [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS).
- Physiological stress considerations: recommendation to add **uniodized salt** to transport containers to mitigate electrolyte loss in stressed fish during transit to Bubbling Pond Hatchery — reflects concern for handling stress in this sensitive, endangered cyprinid.

## Key Findings
1. **Experimental Design Flaw**: The propagation plan's egg-taking protocol proposed 3 treatments (Bubbling Pond vs. LCR water; sperm diluent vs. none; saran egg-trays vs. moist/double bags) producing **8 potential egg lots**, risking confusion in tracking and inadequate replication.
2. **Sample Size Problem**: Applying nested 10% treatment splits to an average clutch of 2,500 eggs yields progressively smaller sub-samples — 250 eggs (LCR water) → 25 eggs (no diluent) → **as few as 2 eggs** in the moist/double-bag treatment — rendering some treatment groups statistically meaningless.
3. **Genetic Tracking Risk**: Pooling eggs from multiple females would increase treatment sample sizes but could compromise parentage tracking, which is critical if progeny are retained as **F1 broodstock** for a basin-wide recovery effort.
4. **Recommendation**: Consult a propagation/genetics specialist (Dr. Holt Williamson, USFWS Region 6) to resolve whether egg batches should be kept separate by female; simplify the treatment design to reduce the number of egg lots while still preserving maternal lineage tracking.
5. **Logistical/Equipment Gaps**: On-site LCR incubation equipment (only four 5-gallon buckets) is insufficient to support the full 3-treatment/8-lot design; either equipment must be increased or the design simplified.
6. **Thermal Shock Risk**: Fertilizing eggs in tempered Bubbling Pond water then transferring to on-site LCR incubation (or vice versa) could cause thermal shock; a failed incubation attempt could require an additional multi-day spawning trip.
7. **Handling Protocols**: Recommends comprehensive PIT-tag screening, biometric measurement, and documentation (including photographing radiotag surgical scars) for all captured adults to support recapture and health-monitoring records.
8. **Hormone Injection Risk**: Warns that carp pituitary extract injections used to induce "green" (non-ripe) females to spawn can be lethal, and requests clarification on maximum injection dosage and cessation criteria.
9. **Population Impact of Broodstock Removal**: Given an **80% recapture rate** in the LCR, removing 20 adult chub (contingency plan) for broodstock is likely to disproportionately affect PIT-tagged individuals already integrated into demographic monitoring datasets — recommends inter-agency coordination (ASU, USFWS) before proceeding, and prefers non-PIT-tagged fish be used if removal is necessary.
10. **Transport Welfare Recommendation**: Suggests use of uniodized salt in transport water to reduce electrolyte loss and handling stress in captured broodstock candidates.
