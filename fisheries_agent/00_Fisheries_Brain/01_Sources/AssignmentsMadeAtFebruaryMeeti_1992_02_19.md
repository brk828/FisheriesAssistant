# Assignments Made at February 1992 Meeting — Desert Fishes Recovery Team

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #ConservationBiology #FreshwaterEcology

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1992 internal memorandum from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (coordinated under the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], Ecological Services, Phoenix, AZ) rather than a primary morphological or ecological research paper. It records task assignments given to team members following a February 5–6, 1992 meeting, primarily concerning drafting advocacy letters for [[W.L. Minckley]]'s signature to the FWS Regional Director. The core issues addressed are transboundary groundwater impacts on the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]], barrier construction and monitoring on [[Aravaipa Creek]], native fish reintroduction planning in the [[Gila Box National Riparian Conservation Area]], the conservation status of the [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], and wilderness/land-use planning for the Middle Box of the [[Gila River]]. While no direct morphological or population data are presented, the memo provides valuable context on early-1990s desert fish conservation policy, threats, and recovery strategy.

## Background & Description
No morphological data are provided in this source. The document is administrative in nature. For species-level morphological detail, see linked concept notes:
- [[Spikedace]] (Meda fulgida) — small benthic cyprinid native to the Gila River basin, imperiled by habitat degradation and nonnative species.
- [[Loach Minnow]] (Tiaroga cobitis) — small benthic cyprinid adapted to swift, rocky desert stream riffles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although not a species-account paper, the memo identifies several key habitats and threats relevant to desert fish ecology:

- **[[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]** (Arizona/Sonora border): Aquatic habitats threatened by **groundwater pumping in Sonora, Mexico**, highlighting the transboundary hydrological connectivity critical to desert spring and wetland ecosystems supporting native fishes.
- **[[Aravaipa Creek]]** (Arizona): A perennial desert stream supporting native fish assemblages; management involves fish barrier construction (upper and lower barriers) to exclude nonnative species, alongside ongoing monthly biological monitoring.
- **[[Gila Box National Riparian Conservation Area]]**: Riparian corridor managed by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), identified as a candidate site for native fish reintroduction.
- **[[Gila River]] – Middle Box**: Subject of a BLM wilderness proposal; recovery team advocated for active management provisions for native fishes within any wilderness planning process.
- **Sonoran groundwater–surface water linkages**: Emphasizes the ecological dependency of U.S. refuge wetlands on cross-border aquifer conditions, an important abiotic driver for desert fish habitat persistence.

## Key Findings
- The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] assigned multiple members (Cobble, Abarca, Varela-Romero) to co-draft a letter urging FWS to address Sonoran groundwater pumping impacts on [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] waters, including a proposed FWS-to-[[SEDUE]] (Mexican environmental secretariat) communication to engage the responsible landowner. Deadline: submission to Minckley by March 1, 1992.
- Team member Simms was tasked with recommending:
  1. Proceeding with construction of the **lower Aravaipa barrier**.
  2. Holding construction of the **upper barrier** in abeyance pending monitoring results.
  3. Continuing **one more year of monthly monitoring** of [[Aravaipa Creek]], with a goal of establishing a long-term monitoring plan within a year.
  4. Communicating these recommendations to **[[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC)** and the **[[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM)**.
- Simms was also assigned to draft a letter expressing the team's support for including **native fish reintroduction** in BLM's [[Gila Box National Riparian Conservation Area]] management plans.
- Stefferud (S.) was assigned to recommend **uplisting of [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] to Endangered status**, and to recommend development of a **captive propagation program** for these species — indicating heightened conservation concern for these cyprinids by 1992.
- Burke was tasked with recommending **implementation of recovery actions** (species/plan unspecified in excerpt, likely tied to spikedace/loach minnow recovery planning).
- Propst was assigned to draft a letter to BLM regarding the **wilderness proposal for the Middle Box** of the [[Gila River]], advocating that BLM planning incorporate **active management for native fishes** rather than passive wilderness designation alone.
- All draft letters were to be routed through team lead **[[W.L. Minckley]]** for signature, underscoring his central coordinating role in desert fish conservation advocacy during this period.
