# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Phase II Native Fish Studies Trip Report and Annual Summary

#ichthyology #native-fish-conservation #cyprinidae #salmonidae #dam-operations #flow-regime

## Executive Summary

This document comprises two reports from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] covering native fish and trout studies in the [[Colorado River]] system below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] during 1991. The March 26-April 14, 1991 trip report details field sampling in mainstream and [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) habitats, while the June 28, 1991 annual summary provides comprehensive progress updates on Phase II research under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES). Research addresses Conservation Measure 5 objectives under Section 7 Consultation on Glen Canyon Dam operations, focusing on three major areas: ecosystem processes and lower trophic levels, [[Rainbow Trout]] populations in the [[Lee's Ferry]] tailwater, and native fish studies emphasizing [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Studies examine effects of fluctuating flows on spawning, rearing habitats, fish behavior, growth, and survival across multiple life stages.

## Background & Description

The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] Phase II represents ongoing research to determine effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on aquatic resources in Glen and Grand canyons. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] conducts research under Cooperative Agreement with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], coordinating with [[Arizona State University]], [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]], BIO/WEST Inc., and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

**Study Areas:**
- **Mainstream [[Colorado River]]**: Glen Canyon Dam to [[Lee's Ferry]] (16 miles) and downstream through Grand Canyon
- **[[Little Colorado River]]**: Lower 22 km perennial reach to confluence with Colorado River
- **Tributaries**: [[Kanab Creek]] and other Grand Canyon tributaries

**Target Species:**
- Native fishes: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus*)
- Introduced species: [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)

**Flow Regimes Studied:**
- Normal operations: fluctuating flows
- Research flows: 5,000 cfs constant, 15,000 cfs constant, 8,000 cfs, 11,000 cfs
- Discharge range at Cameron gauge: 228-474 cfs (LCR)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Tailwater Ecosystem (Lee's Ferry):**
The [[Glen Canyon Dam]] tailwater supports a productive [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery but experiences dramatic daily flow fluctuations affecting spawning and rearing habitats. Water temperatures remain perennially cold (12-16°C), precluding formation of annual growth rings in fish scales and otoliths. The amphipod *[[Gammarus lacustris]]* constitutes an important trout food resource. Primary production depends on light availability, which varies with turbidity and flow regime. Spawning bars at River Miles 4.0, 6.1, 8.9, and 14.0 experience varying degrees of dewatering and redd exposure under different flow regimes.

**Little Colorado River:**
The LCR provides critical warm-water breeding and rearing habitat for [[Humpback Chub]], the only Grand Canyon tributary where this endangered species reproduces. Water chemistry differs markedly from the mainstem:
- Conductivity: 2,180-3,630 µS/cm (varying with discharge vs. ~5,000 µS/cm at base flow)
- Temperature: 12.1-15.9°C (warmer than mainstem)
- Dissolved oxygen: 8.5-9.1 mg/L
- pH: 7.7-8.0
- Alkalinity: 256-484 mg/L as CaCO₃
- CO₂: 210-256 mg/L

Upper reaches above Atomizer Falls (15 km) show elevated CO₂ from Blue Springs inflow. [[Speckled Dace]] dominates upper perennial reaches; [[Humpback Chub]] concentrates in lower 2 km and confluence zone.

**Confluence Zone Dynamics:**
Mainstream stage fluctuations impound tributary flows, creating variable temperature and velocity conditions in confluence zones. These areas may serve as staging sites for adult native fishes and rearing habitats for young-of-year. Backwaters provide low-velocity rearing habitat but experience dewatering at low flows and conversion to large eddies at high flows.

## Key Findings

**March-April 1991 Field Trip:**

*Little Colorado River Sampling (March 28-April 1):*
- Collected approximately 50 [[Humpback Chub]], including 14 recaptures
- Total lengths: 69-446 mm; weights: <10-1,037 g
- Transported 10 adult chub (5 males 255-358 mm TL, 5 females 328-437 mm TL) to [[Page Springs Hatchery]] for propagation
- Only one female extrudable in field; small proportion of males ripe
- No larval fishes observed in drift samples
- Two radio-tagged chub recaptured in trammel nets at mouth
- Additional transmitting chub detected at frequencies 40.670, 40.680, 40.730 MHz
- Other species collected: [[Speckled Dace]], [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], single [[Common Carp]] (650 mm TL), [[Rainbow Trout]]

*LCR Hydrologic Conditions:*
- Cameron gauge flows: 228 cfs baseline, peaked at 474 cfs March 29
- Estimated lower LCR discharge: 450-700 cfs (including 210-230 cfs Blue Springs base flow)
- Conductivity decreased from 3,630 µS/cm (12.5 km) to 2,180 µS/cm (1.8 km) during high flow
- Water temperatures: 12.1-15.9°C (air temperatures 6.0-22.7°C)

*Mainstream Sampling Methods:*
Three sampling intensity levels established:
- **Level I**: Fish collections, water temperature, basic habitat mapping (≤1 hour)
- **Level II**: Added plankton, benthos, sediment, full hydrolab suite, detailed mapping (≥2 hours)
- **Level III**: 24-48 hour studies with Datasondes, topographic surveys, minnow traps, measurements tied to flow cycle

**Annual Summary Progress (through June 1991):**

*Ecosystem Processes (Work Items 1.1-1.4):*
- Eleven input-output sampling efforts completed at Glen Canyon Dam and Lee's Ferry
- Quarterly Lake Powell sampling for biomass, chlorophyll, nutrients, zooplankton
- Primary productivity measurements limited by lack of instrumentation and manpower
- *Gammarus lacustris* studies: benthic samples collected with mini-Ponar dredge; two 24-hour trout diet sampling efforts completed
- Colonization experiments show more algal biomass on sand substrates than previously suspected
- Desiccation experiments in dam sluiceways evaluate effects on algal appearance, biomass, chlorophyll

*Rainbow Trout Studies (Work Items 2.1-2.4):*

**Spawning Habitat (2.1):**
- Four spawning bars surveyed (RM 4.0, 6.1, 8.9, 14.0) for sediment size and embeddedness
- Wetted perimeter measured at 3,000, 5,000, 8,000, 11,000 cfs
- 430 redds located at 4.0 mile bar, 178 at 8.9 mile bar
- Minimal spawning at 6.1 mile bar, none at 14.0 mile bar
- Spawning delayed until late December 1990 (depressed levels)

**Stranding and Mortality (2.2):**
- Ten major stranding areas identified (RM -0.5R to 14.8R)
- Surveyed 4x/month March-August 1990, 2x/month September 1990-February 1991
- Summer 5,000 cfs flows caused three mortality events, likely from temperature increases
- Spawning season mortalities from dewatering and predation
- Tetracycline marking insufficient to discriminate hatchery vs. wild trout (antibiotic level changes)

**Trout Strain Studies (2.2 Addendum):**
- Literature review subcontract awarded April 30, 1991 (Davis and Ruffner)
- Genetic analysis initiated: samples from 10 tributaries, 9 mainstream sites, hatchery stocks
- Tissue samples sent to Illinois Natural History Survey for gel electrophoresis
- Draft report due September 30, 1991

**Age and Growth (2.3):**
- Quarterly electrofishing collections from 4-8 random sites
- Growth rates calculated from floy tag recaptures
- Fluorescent pigment retention: 77% (1989 fish), 58% (1990 fish) as of December 1990
- Binary coded wire tags being explored as permanent marking method
- Sluiceway growth experiments delayed by water surge issues

**Behavioral Responses (2.4):**
- Ten adult trout (40-50 cm TL) radio-tagged November-December 1990
- Triangulations 3x/day for 2 days during each study flow
- Habitat measurements: depth, substrate, cover, velocity, distance to shore
- 24-hour tracking during normal winter/spring flows and 5,000/15,000 cfs steady flows

*Native Fish Studies (Work Items 3.1-3.9):*

**Monitoring Program (3.1):**
- LCR: 13 hoop nets run continuously in lower 1,200 m since early May
- Six additional sites between RK 9.1-11.6 since mid-May
- Trammel nets discontinued due to [[Humpback Chub]] mortalities (deaths in <2 hours)
- Larval drift nets run every other day from two camps
- Extensive habitat measurements: current velocity, substrate, depth, cover
- Helicopter shuttles used due to Salt Trail closure

**Early Life Stage Distribution (3.2):**
- Larval drift nets at Salt Trail Canyon mouth and RK 1.8
- Current velocities measured at net mouths to quantify filtered volume
- Nets run during crepuscular, daylight, darkness hours every other day
- Post-larval sampling with fine-meshed seines and minnow traps
- Mainstream: 27 [[Humpback Chub]] and ~2,300 other species collected
- Preliminary sorting of all larval samples completed

**Propagation (3.3):**
- ~300 fertilized eggs from one female died shortly after hatchery arrival
- Carp pituitary injections failed to induce egg expression (up to 72 hours, 3 injections maximum)
- Four adult chub died jumping from circular raceway at Bubbling Ponds
- Additional adult transfer scheduled for autumn

**Rearing Habitat Conditions (3.4):**
- Topographic surveys completed on 12 backwater sites using Leitz equipment
- Computer-generated maps to be produced in Flagstaff GCES office
- Plane table mapping to begin July (20 additional sites per trip)
- Data linked with fish densities, algal/invertebrate standing crops, physicochemical characteristics
- Integration with GIS and flow-routing model planned

**Food Resources (3.5):**
- LCR longitudinal sampling May 14-18: transects at 20, 15, 10, 5, 0.07 km above mouth
- Algal and invertebrate abundance/diversity decreased downstream with light penetration
- Mainstream: coincident fish and invertebrate collections from backwaters
- Chlorophyll a analysis and zooplankton exchange studies to begin August

**Behavioral Responses (3.6):**
- Larval drift nets measure early life stage passage to mainstream
- Fin-clip marking unique to capture location
- Immersion dyes investigated as alternative to fin-clips
- Limited progress on confluence zone drift/behavior due to monitoring responsibilities

**Age Structure and Growth (3.7):**
- 50+ [[Humpback Chub]] otoliths sent to Dr. Dean Hendrickson (University of Texas)
- Daily age determinations on young-of-year, annual determinations on older individuals
- Dr. Ed Brothers (Cornell) to verify analyses
- Lapillus confirmed as appropriate otolith (asterisci unreliable)
- Bonytail chub grown at three temperatures at Dexter National Fish Hatchery for verification

**Otolith Chemistry (3.8):**
- Single analysis at UC Davis showed Sr/Ca ratio variation across asteriscus
- Lapillus recommended for chemistry analyses
- Field experiments designed but not yet implemented
- Expensive analyses require sophisticated equipment

**Limnological Factors (3.9):**
- June 1991 LCR sampling: CO₂, alkalinity, pH, temperature, dissolved solids at 5 km intervals
- Preliminary evaluation: CO₂ and alkalinity do not solely explain [[Humpback Chub]] absence above Atomizer Falls (15 km)
- [[Speckled Dace]] and larvae collected from upper reach
- Quarterly assessments and substrate colonization experiments planned

**Major Problems Identified:**
- Particulate organic matter nutrient analysis delayed (USGS lab detection limits inadequate)
- Meteorological stations needed for primary productivity modeling
- Lack of permanent habitats/algal resources above 5,000 cfs
- Manpower shortages limiting multiple study components
- ASU funding delays affected upper LCR sampling
- USGS map generation delays
- Compass coordinates insufficient for precise redd identification
- Low flows needed during daylight for redd mapping
- Marking techniques for larval fishes require development
- Algal taxonomy expertise lacking
- Otolith analysis expensive and time-consuming
- Some electronic water quality gear malfunctioned
