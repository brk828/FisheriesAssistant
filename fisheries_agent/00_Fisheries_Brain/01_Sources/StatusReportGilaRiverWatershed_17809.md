## Executive Summary

This document is a **project status report** (April–September 1994) submitted by American Rivers to the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation, detailing administrative and funding progress for Phase I of the "[[Gila River Watershed Habitat Study]]." The report does **not contain primary morphological, physiological, or ecological data** on fish species; rather, it documents the logistical, financial, and institutional groundwork for a research program studying fish habitat in the [[Gila River]] drainage of Arizona and New Mexico. The lead scientist is [[Wendell L. Minckley]], a prominent ichthyologist affiliated with Arizona State University, known for extensive work on native [[Desert Fishes]] of the American Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no direct ecological data are reported, the document establishes important **habitat and geographic context**:

- **System studied:** [[Gila River]] drainage, spanning portions of **Arizona** and **New Mexico**, a major tributary system of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Conservation designation:** Segments of the Gila River system are recognized as "**biosphere reserve**" projects by [[The Nature Conservancy]], indicating high conservation value, likely due to endemic and imperiled native fish fauna.
- **Habitat assessment scope:** The project's deliverables include:
  - Basemap with **first- and second-order locality data** (headwater/small tributary habitat mapping)
  - Basemap with **third-order locality data** (larger stream habitat mapping)
  - A **preliminary assessment of needs for additional field surveys**
- **Institutional stakeholders:** Coordination with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[New Mexico Game and Fish Department]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], and the [[U.S. Forest Service]] indicates the study's relevance to multi-agency native fish habitat management and regulatory compliance in the desert Southwest.
- **Relevant scientific forum:** The project's objectives were slated for presentation at the annual meeting of the [[Desert Fish Council]] in Nevada (November 1994), a professional society focused on the conservation and biology of fishes in arid Southwestern watersheds — implying the study's focal taxa are native desert fish species (e.g., [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], and other Gila River basin endemics commonly studied by Minckley).

## Key Findings

- **Project initiation and funding secured:** Phase I of the Gila River Watershed Habitat Study was formally launched in 1994 after securing funding from multiple sources, including:
  - $25,000 from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]
  - $20,000 from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]
  - Equipment funding from the [[Robidoux Foundation]] (GIS/computer systems)
  - Matching in-kind contributions from [[Arizona State University]] (documented check for $3,760.87)
- **Institutional partnerships:** A Memorandum of Understanding was signed with federal agencies; an agreement was also reached with [[The Nature Conservancy]] for matching funds tied to Gila River "biosphere reserve" segments in Arizona and New Mexico.
- **Staffing:** Research assistant Patti Snull and GIS specialist Jana Fry were hired to support literature review, data compilation, and geospatial mapping using [[U.S. Geological Survey]] maps.
- **Deliverables planned:** The study aimed to produce tiered basemaps of fish locality data (1st/2nd order and 3rd order stream reaches) and a needs assessment for future survey work — foundational steps toward a comprehensive habitat assessment for native Gila River fishes.
- **Dissemination:** Findings and objectives were to be presented at the [[Desert Fish Council]] annual meeting, situating this work within the broader scientific community studying imperiled desert fish species of the American Southwest.
