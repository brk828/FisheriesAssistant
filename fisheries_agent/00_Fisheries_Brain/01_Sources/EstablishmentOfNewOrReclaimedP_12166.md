# West Virginia Small Impoundment Investigations Project F-3-R-4 (1955-1956)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Centrarchidae #pond-management #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This multi-job completion report documents experimental pond management studies conducted by the West Virginia Conservation Commission during 1955-1956 (Project F-3-R-4). The investigation focused on establishing experimental ponds, conducting creel censuses, sampling fish populations, and testing corrective procedures for common pond management problems. Ten privately owned ponds were selected and stocked with various [[Largemouth Bass]]-sunfish combinations at ratios of 1:1, 1:5, and 1:10. Population inventories were conducted through draining and mark-and-recovery seining methods. The project also tested algae control methods using Delrad 70 and copper sulphate. All experimental ponds were located within 50 miles of [[Palestine State Fish Hatchery]] to reduce travel time and improve data collection efficiency.

## Background & Description

The project operated under multiple job designations addressing different aspects of small impoundment management in [[West Virginia]]. Job A focused on establishing new or reclaimed ponds for experimental use. Job G conducted voluntary creel censuses on study and experimental ponds. Job D sampled fish populations using reproduction checks, draining inventories, and mark-and-recovery seining. Job E tested corrective procedures for algae control.

The experimental design compared three primary stocking techniques: (1) the Kentucky system (100 [[Largemouth Bass]] fingerlings and 30 adult [[Bluegill Sunfish]] per acre), (2) 1:5 bass:sunfish ratio (100 bass per acre with 500 sunfish per acre), and (3) 1:10 bass:sunfish ratio (100 bass per acre with 1,000 sunfish per acre). Sunfish species included [[Bluegill Sunfish]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*), and [[Yellowbelly Sunfish]] (*Lepomis auritus*).

Population balance was assessed using Swingle's ratios: F/C (frequency of catch), Y/C (yield per catch), and A₁ (age distribution index). Optimum values indicating balanced populations were established based on Alabama research by H.S. Swingle.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All experimental ponds were constructed by [[Soil Conservation Service]] technicians and represented typical farm pond environments in West Virginia's physiographic subdivisions. Pond sizes ranged from 0.29 to 2.2 acres, with most under 1 acre. Water chemistry analyses showed alkaline conditions typical of the region, with pH values ranging from 7.2 to 8.2 and total alkalinity between 25-35 p.p.m.

Summer water temperatures in bass-bluegill ponds ranged between 65-75°F during the growing season. Two [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) ponds were established at elevations of 2,000 and 3,000 feet in [[Randolph County]], though summer temperatures likely exceeded the 73°F tolerance threshold for trout.

Algae problems were common, with filamentous and net-type algae (*Hydrodycton*) covering 30-85% of pond surfaces in affected impoundments. Dense patches of submerged algae attached to bottom substrates in shallow areas and pond margins.

## Key Findings

**Job A - Pond Establishment:**

- Ten privately owned ponds were selected and stocked during 1955-1956
- Nine ponds were new; one (Ernestine Haymond pond, 0.29 acres) was reclaimed after being overcrowded with bluegills
- Stocking combinations: 1 pond at 1:10 bass:redear ratio, 3 ponds at 1:5 bass:redear, 3 ponds at 1:5 bass:yellowbelly, 3 ponds at 1:1 bass:bluegill
- Total of 25 ponds established since project inception in 1953
- Two additional ponds at [[Palestine State Fish Hatchery]] were set aside for experimental use
- Recommendation: establish approximately 30 ponds within 50-mile radius of headquarters; discontinue ponds more than 100 miles distant

**Job G - Creel Census Results:**

Study Ponds (1:10 bass:bluegill, stocked 1953 or earlier):
- Only 3 of 10 study pond owners maintained accurate harvest records
- Mean annual yield: 96.5 pounds per acre (compared to 63 pounds per acre in 1954)
- Average fishing pressure: 110 hours per acre
- Catch rate: 5.2 fish per man-hour
- Harvest composition: 35% largemouth bass, 65% bluegills and other sunfish

Experimental Ponds:
- [[Palestine Hatchery]] Reservoir (2.2 acres): balanced population stocked November 1954 yielded 178 pounds total in 556 angler hours (1.9 fish per hour); harvest rate increased from 0.7 to 1.9 fish per man-hour compared to 1954
- Kentucky system pond (1 year old): 67.6 pounds per acre annual harvest, 10.4 fish per man-hour
- Bass size differences: Kentucky system pond bass averaged 0.70 pounds vs. 0.42 pounds in 1:10 ratio pond after one year
- Rainbow trout ponds were underfished with poor catches

**Job D - Population Sampling:**

Reproduction Checks:
- Seven study ponds showed bluegill reproduction in all ponds, bass reproduction in four
- Marginal seining with 15-foot minnow seine sometimes failed to detect bass reproduction when young-of-year bass exceeded 6 inches total length

Palestine Hatchery Reservoir (2.2 acres):
- Stocked with balanced population (14,340 fish, 351 pounds) in November 1954
- Recovered 25,272 fish (298 pounds) in April 1956 inventory
- Standing crop 53 pounds less than original stocking
- Including 178 pounds harvested, produced approximately 56 pounds per acre in 1955
- F/C ratio: 11.4, Y/C ratio: 1.47, A₁ ratio: 21.8 (outside balanced range)
- **Critical finding:** Heavy fishing pressure (556 hours) threw balanced population out of balance through excessive harvest of large bass, allowing intermediate bluegill overcrowding
- Harvestable fish declined from 99 pounds per acre to 25 pounds per acre

Kentucky System vs. 1:10 Ratio Comparison (stocked summer 1954, inventoried April 1956):
- Both ponds contained balanced populations after two years
- Kentucky pond (1.5 acres): 138 pounds total, 62 pounds per acre harvestable fish
- 1:10 pond (1.9 acres): 468 pounds total, 177 pounds per acre harvestable fish
- **1:10 pond produced 4 times more harvestable fish per acre than Kentucky pond**
- First-year bass growth superior in Kentucky pond: 39% reached 9.5+ inches vs. only 5% in 1:10 pond
- Second-year pattern reversed: young-of-year bass in 1:10 pond averaged 6.0 inches vs. 3.5 inches in Kentucky pond
- Age 1+ bass averaged 12.6 inches (1.10 pounds) in Kentucky pond vs. 11.0 inches (0.71 pounds) in 1:10 pond
- Bass survival to harvestable size: 41% (Kentucky) vs. 63% (1:10 ratio)
- Greater bass growth in Kentucky pond attributed to heavy bluegill spawning in 1954 providing abundant forage

1:5 Ratio Ponds (stocked summer/fall 1954, inventoried fall 1955):
- Three ponds (Allman 0.33 acres, Sears 0.40 acres, Smith 0.22 acres) sampled
- Two ponds achieved balance within one year; third nearly balanced
- Harvestable fish production: 20.9, 74.5, and 145.4 pounds per acre respectively
- In two balanced ponds, all surviving bluegills from original stock reached harvestable size (5.5+ inches) first summer
- Bass of age 1+ averaged 9.1-10.1 inches total length
- Bass survival: 88% (Allman), 25% (Sears), 82% (Smith)
- Bass reproduced at age 1+ in two ponds stocked in September 1954, but not in pond stocked in July 1954

Rainbow Trout Ponds:
- Two ponds stocked at 300 fingerlings per acre in November 1954
- Prison pond (1.25 acres, 2,000 ft elevation): 26% survival, 15 pounds per acre
- Wilson pond (0.28 acres, 3,000 ft elevation): 30% survival, 17 pounds per acre
- Average growth: 3.2 inches annually, reaching 8.4-8.6 inches
- Total weight of surviving trout less than weight stocked one year earlier
- High summer temperatures (likely exceeding 75°F) believed responsible for heavy mortality
- **Conclusion:** Trout stocking not recommended in small West Virginia impoundments without constant cold water supply (65°F or less)

**Job E - Algae Control:**

Delrad 70 (dehydroabietylamine acetate) Testing:
- 1.87-acre pond treated three times (May-June 1955)
- 0.24 p.p.m. concentration: 40% kill within 4 days, regrowth within one week
- 0.3 p.p.m. concentration: 75% effective on submerged algae, 50% on surface growth, regrowth began within one week
- **0.5 p.p.m. concentration: complete control within one week, no regrowth during remainder of growing season**
- Floating box application method provided even distribution
- No apparent harm to bass-bluegill population
- Caution: 0.7 p.p.m. concentration toxic to bluegills, goldfish, and young largemouth bass (Alabama research)
- Delrad brochure concentration tables found to be in error

Copper Sulphate (Cu SO₄) Testing:
- Three ponds treated at 1.5-2.0 p.p.m. concentrations
- Ralph Sears pond: 1.5 p.p.m. by drag method, complete control within one week
- Hatchery Pond #11 (pH 7.2, alkalinity 25 p.p.m.): 1.5 p.p.m., complete control within one week
- Hatchery Pond #9 (pH 8.2, alkalinity 30 p.p.m.): 2.0 p.p.m., complete control within five days
- No harmful effects observed on bass-bluegill populations
- **Recommendation:** 2.0 p.p.m. concentration safe and effective for alkaline West Virginia waters

**General Management Implications:**

- Reducing travel distance to experimental ponds (50-mile radius) improved data collection and owner cooperation
- Population estimates by mark-and-recovery seining limited to September 1 - November 20 window (water temperatures below 75°F, before freezing)
- With these constraints, approximately 30 ponds can be managed successfully
- Ponds exceeding 10 feet depth or containing debris unsuitable for seining operations
- At least 3-4 replications of each species combination and stocking rate needed for reliable results
- Voluntary creel census difficult to maintain beyond second year; frequent visits and informative discussions improve cooperation
- Stocking balanced populations not practical for small impoundments unless harvest can be regulated
- Selective angling for bass can throw populations out of balance even when initially stocked in balanced ratios
