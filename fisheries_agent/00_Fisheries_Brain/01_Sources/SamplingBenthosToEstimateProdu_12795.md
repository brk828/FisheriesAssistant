## Executive Summary

This paper, presented by Jack S. Dendy of the Auburn University Agricultural Experiment Station, describes the development and application of the **multiple-plate sampler**, an artificial substrate device for collecting benthic macroinvertebrates to estimate aquatic **productivity**. While the primary organisms of interest are [[Benthic Macroinvertebrates]] (chironomids, ostracods, bryozoans, mayflies, etc.), the study is fundamentally tied to fisheries science: the sampler's efficacy was validated by correlating dry-weight biomass of colonized organisms with **fish yield data** obtained from draining experimentally fertilized ponds stocked sequentially with goldfish, carp, and channel catfish. The paper also documents significant effects of **fish predation** on benthic standing crop, comparing results across several centrarchid, ictalurid, and cichlid species.

## Background & Description

The note centers on sampling apparatus and colonizing invertebrate taxa rather than fish morphology per se. Key structural/methodological details:

- **Multiple-plate sampler**: constructed of tempered hardboard (found most practical material after experimentation), assembled as a multi-space unit (compared against an 8-space vs. 1-space configuration).
- Sampler design allows for protected surfaces (scraped during processing) simulating substrate free from fish cropping pressure — central to the study's "uncropped population" hypothesis.
- **Fish species involved in productivity correlation** (by stocking year in experimental ponds):
  - Year 1: [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*) and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
  - Year 2: Common Carp only
  - Year 3: [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), 150 fingerlings
  - Year 4: Channel Catfish, 850 fingerlings
- Predation-effect comparisons involved: [[Largemouth Bass]] and crowded [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], Channel Catfish, [[Tilapia Mossambica]], and [[Green Sunfish]] — species selected to represent varying predation pressure/foraging behavior on benthic organisms colonizing the plates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Quarter-acre experimental fertilized ponds (fertility treatments: 0-0-0, 0-8-2, and 8-8-2 N-P-K, four replicates each) and natural streams used for comparative and pollution-related studies.
- **Trophic structure examined**:
  - Basic productivity (light/dark bottle methods, chlorophyll content, carbon fixation)
  - Standing crop across trophic levels: phytoplankton → zooplankton → benthos → fish → fisherman yield
- **Predator-prey dynamics**: Fish predation substantially reduced benthic standing crop on samplers, with effects varying by species and stocking density (e.g., "Bass and crowded [[Bluegill]]" produced different depletion patterns than [[Redear Sunfish]] or [[Tilapia Mossambica]]). This directly demonstrates top-down control of macroinvertebrate productivity by fish communities — a foundation of the "uncropped population" sampling hypothesis (samplers protect colonizing organisms from fish predation, allowing measurement of the environment's inherent productive capacity).
- **Seasonal & spatial variation**:
  - Correlation between sampler biomass and pond fish yield was stronger in late summer/early fall than early summer, attributed to pond age, organism life cycles, and temperature.
  - Distance from shore and from surface affected quantity of organisms colonizing samplers.
  - Comparison with [[Ekman Dredge]] sampling showed Ekman samples yielded more organisms per unit area, with cold water producing smaller multiple-plate samples (seasonal effect).
- **Community composition on samplers (ponds)**: Protozoa (amoeboid forms, *Stentor*, colonial vorticellids, creeping ciliates), Hydra (abundant in cold water), turbellarians, rotifers (abundant), few nematodes/annelids, ostracods (abundant), copepods, cladocerans, chironomid larvae (dominant insect group), mayflies, damselflies, microcaddisflies, beetle larvae/adults, water mites (frequently abundant), *Plumatella* (Bryozoa, second most abundant after chironomids in fertile water), and rare snails. Egg masses (ostracod, mite, insect) irregularly abundant.
- **Stream fauna**: Typical lotic macroinvertebrate assemblage plus some uncommon taxa such as nemertines (ribbon worms).

## Key Findings

- The **multiple-plate sampler** provides a practical, inexpensive, and easily operated method for estimating benthic productivity via colonization of artificial substrate, complementing traditional [[Ekman Dredge]] sampling.
- Dry-weight biomass from sampler colonization **correlated with fish yield** at pond draining, particularly in ponds stocked with [[Common Carp]], [[Goldfish]], and [[Channel Catfish]] — validating the sampler as a productivity proxy across different fertility treatments (0-0-0, 0-8-2, 8-8-2 N-P-K).
- Correlation strength was **season-dependent** (better in late summer/early fall), likely driven by pond age, organismal life-history timing, and water temperature.
- **Fish predation** measurably reduced standing crop on samplers, demonstrated across bass, crowded bluegill, redear sunfish, channel catfish, tilapia, and green sunfish — supporting the sampler's use in isolating "uncropped" productivity estimates protected from fish grazing.
- Sampler catch is **selective**: many benthic forms (e.g., sponges, most mollusks) were absent or underrepresented, so results should be considered a partial, not comprehensive, community sample.
- Hardboard, while practical, is **not necessarily the optimal substrate material**; further refinement of sampler design/materials is recommended.
- Beyond productivity estimation, the sampler has secondary applications: supplying live food for laboratory animals (**fish**, salamanders, beetle larvae), studying aquatic insect emergence, bryozoan growth, and bioassay work in both polluted and clean streams and experimental ponds.
- Overall, the multiple-plate sampler is characterized as "no panacea" but a valuable **additional tool** in the productivity-estimation toolkit, particularly useful where precise zonal sampling and low-effort replication are needed.
