## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Pinetop Fishery Assistance Office (FAO), dated September 26, 1991, addressed to David L. Wegner of the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program. The letter explains the rationale behind USFWS-Pinetop FAO's acquisition of an endangered species subpermittee collection permit (PRT-676811) to legally collect [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Little Colorado River]] and other Colorado River tributaries within [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. While not a primary scientific data source, the letter provides critical context regarding permitting logistics, interagency collaboration, and the specific tributary habitats targeted for fish population and habitat research under the GCES program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although no direct ecological data (diet, water chemistry, population density) is presented, the letter identifies the specific riverine habitats targeted for research and collection, implying these are critical habitats for the two endangered species:

- **Primary Focal Habitat:** [[Little Colorado River]] (both inside and outside Grand Canyon National Park boundaries, the latter requiring Navajo Fish and Wildlife Department permitting).
- **Additional Tributaries Surveyed:** [[Paria River]], [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Tapeats Creek]], [[Deer Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]] — all tributaries of the [[Colorado River]] within the Grand Canyon corridor.
- **Research Activities:** Habitat measurement and fish collection/sampling, conducted under the broader [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) research contract.
- **Jurisdictional Ecology Note:** The need for multiple permits (USFWS, USNPS, Navajo Fish and Wildlife Department, AZGF) underscores the fragmented jurisdictional management of contiguous [[Colorado River Basin]] habitat critical to endangered fish species survival.

## Key Findings
- USFWS-Pinetop FAO, in affiliation with the [[University of Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit]], obtained subpermittee status under USFWS Permit **PRT-676811** to collect two federally listed endangered species: **[[Humpback Chub]]** and **[[Razorback Sucker]]**.
- This federal permit was a legal precondition for securing a corresponding **U.S. National Park Service (USNPS)** collection permit, which had been issued by the time of writing.
- A USFWS permit was likewise necessary before applying for a research/collection permit from the **Navajo Fish and Wildlife Department**, required for work in the Little Colorado River outside Grand Canyon National Park boundaries.
- The organization also held a separate **Arizona Game and Fish (AZGF)** collection permit (**#600-7-91-0000227**) for listed species in the Grand Canyon and Little Colorado River.
- As of the letter's date, USFWS-Pinetop FAO was **not** a subpermittee on permits held by other GCES contractors, namely **Arizona State University (ASU)** and **AZGF**.
- Logistical challenges were reported: verbal agreements for **ASU** to provide fish collection support for USFWS sampling in the Little Colorado River had proven unreliable, as ASU personnel could not consistently accompany USFWS staff for required sampling.
- The independent acquisition of permits by USFWS-Pinetop FAO was framed as a strategy to increase field sampling flexibility and efficient allocation of personnel resources among **GCES** contractors, without displacing collaborative research relationships with **ASU** and **AZGF**.
- The permitting developments were formally announced at the **3 September 1991 ad hoc Adaptive Cooperative Team (ACT) meeting** at AZGF offices in Phoenix, indicating coordinated multi-agency oversight of Grand Canyon endangered fish research under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program.
- The letter signals an explicit institutional intent to deepen future research integration between USFWS-Pinetop FAO, ASU, and AZGF concerning endangered fish conservation in the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem.
