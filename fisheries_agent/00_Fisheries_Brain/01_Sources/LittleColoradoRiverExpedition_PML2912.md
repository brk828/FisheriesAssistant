# 1951 Boat Expedition on the Lower Little Colorado and Colorado Rivers, Grand Canyon

#aquatic-ecology #freshwater-ecology #grand-canyon #river-expedition

## Executive Summary

This is a short field report by two temporary [[National Park Service]] employees describing the first recorded boat expedition on the lower [[Little Colorado River]] and the [[Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon National Park]], run from April 22 to 27, 1951. The party used a Navy-surplus rubber boat, carried supplies and water down a steep route from [[Blue Springs]], and concluded that the lower river is unlikely to become a popular boating destination because of the difficult descent. The document is primarily a logistics and exploration narrative. It does not report fish, invertebrates, water chemistry measurements, or other ecological data.

## Background & Description

- **Authors:** K. C. [name partly illegible in scan], Ranger-Naturalist, and Lester Womack, both temporary employees of the National Park Service at Grand Canyon.
- **Date and route:** The expedition ran April 22–27, 1951. Boating covered 13 miles on the Little Colorado River and 10 miles on the Colorado River. Three days were spent on the rivers and three days on packing in and out.
- **Planning:** The original plan was to boat the Little Colorado from Cameron, Arizona, during a flood, but drought conditions led the party to launch from Blue Springs, about 13 miles above the river junction. April was chosen for the best expected weather.
- **Equipment:** A Navy-surplus 4-man rubber boat performed well on both the small stream and the Colorado. Standard rucksacks carried the folded boat and supplies. Drinking water was carried in four 1-gallon cans because the Blue Springs water was described as unpalatable and potentially dangerous. Waterproof plastic bags protected food, matches, and film. Each man had one blanket. A 120-ft climbing rope was used for a 59-ft rappel near the top of the Blue Springs trail. Total equipment weighed over 200 pounds, of which 60–70 pounds was water or canned fruit and juices.
- **Photography:** The party took 35 mm and black-and-white pictures, which the authors said were available to anyone interested in studying the canyon more fully.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Drought:** Drought conditions prevented the originally planned flood-season trip from Cameron.
- **Water quality:** The Blue Springs water was described as unpalatable and "even dangerous" to drink, which is why drinking water was carried in.
- **Geology and terrain:** The descent passed mostly through the [[Coconino Sandstone]], which the authors described as containing many precipitous spots, with an obscure route marked only by rock cairns.
- **Cultural and scenic notes:** The authors noted broken and tumbled sculpture-like rock formations resembling fragmented statuary from ruined Greek temples. The text does not describe biological communities.

## Key Findings

- **Expedition outcome:** The party completed the first recorded boat trip on the lower Little Colorado and Colorado Rivers in April 1951. Equipment failures were not reported.
- **Descent difficulty:** The first day's descent from the top of the Blue Springs trail took from 6:10 a.m. until about 5 p.m., with two trips needed to carry loads to the rappel point. The second day's start from the springs was at 8 a.m., reaching the boat by 11:30 a.m. and the riverside by 4:30 p.m.
- **Boat trial:** The party inflated the boat at the foot of the first pool on day two. The trial built confidence, and the boat caused no trouble afterward.
- **Conclusion:** The authors concluded that the steep descent to Blue Springs is a sufficient deterrent to ensure that the lower river "will never become a boaters' paradise." The river remained accessible on foot or by boat, but the descent was the main constraint.
