# Ictiofauna del Río Pánuco, Noreste de México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #Poeciliidae #Cyprinidae #Cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This 1972 undergraduate thesis from the Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León (Facultad de Ciencias Biológicas) by Jorge Armando Verduzco Martínez documents the fish fauna (ictiofauna) of the [[Río Pánuco]] basin in northeastern Mexico. Based on 32 collections at 24 localities made between 1964 and 1970, the author examined 12,799 specimens, of which 714 were used for counts and meristic data. The work covers morphology, taxonomy and distribution, and lists 42 species treated in detail (29 described in the text), including two new forms and a new species, with several species reported for the first time from the basin. Its stated purpose is to provide the taxonomic baseline needed for biology, fisheries, aquaculture and sport-fishery regulation.

## Background & Description

The study was motivated by a lack of prior taxonomic work on the region and by abundant unidentified material in the [[Laboratorio de Vertebrados]] collections at UANL. Fieldwork ran from February 1970 to February 1972, and the thesis was defended in March 1972 under the supervision of Biól. M.Sc. Salvador Contreras B.

Historical study of the basin dates to the early twentieth century, with contributions from [[Meek]] (1904, who recorded 29 species for the basin), [[Hubbs]] (who described several *Gambusia* and *Xiphophorus* taxa), [[Rosen]] (revision of *Xiphophorus*), [[Miller]] (description of *Cualac tessellatus*), [[Alvarez]] (1959 preliminary checklist of 41 species for San Luis Potosí) and De Buen (regional fish lists). Contreras (unpublished) listed 54 species and localities for San Luis Potosí.

Methods: seine nets (chinchorro, 3 m × 1.8 m, mesh 1/8 to 1/4 inch) and occasional gill nets. Specimens are deposited under the acronym UANL, with Tulane (T.U.) material also consulted. Counts and measurements follow [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1970). Taxonomy follows Greenwood et al. (1966) for families and Rosen and Bailey (1963) for Poeciliidae. The author explicitly states that new taxon names are not valid nomenclaturally, as the thesis is a private, non-published academic document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The basin is divided hydrologically into the Tamuín and Moctezuma branches and spans three physiographic zones: the altiplano, the montane region and the coastal plain. [[Tamayo]] (1962) is cited for the drainage description (catchment area recorded as 66,300 km²; the exact value is partly illegible in the scan).

The thesis proposes three ichthyofaunal sections: **Tampico** (lower Pánuco and coastal lagoons), **Valles** (Verde, Frío, Tampaón rivers and middle-upper Tamesí) and **Moctezuma** (upper Tula and middle Moctezuma, plus Santa María tributaries). The basin is described as a zone of overlap between Nearctic and Neotropical faunas.

Fishes were classified by apparent salinity tolerance:
- **Freshwater stenohaline** species, including many endemics.
- **Invaders** of freshwater, from brackish-tolerant forms to occasional marine visitors.
- **Euryhaline** species (e.g., *Lepisosteus* species, *Dorosoma*, *Eleotris pisonis*), which move between fresh and marine waters.
- **Catadromous or marine/brackish species** entering lower reaches (e.g., *Centropomus parallelus*, *Menidia beryllina*).

Habitat notes repeatedly link species to clear versus turbid water, current strength, bottom type (mud, gravel, rock) and shoreline vegetation, with temperatures recorded from roughly 20 to 29 °C.

## Key Findings

- **Survey scope:** 32 collections, 24 localities, 12,799 specimens examined (714 for counts), 1964–1970.
- **Taxonomic coverage:** 14–15 families, about 25 genera, 42 species and 10 subspecies treated; 18 genera are represented by a single species.
- **New taxa proposed:** *Dionda rasconis rioverdensis* (Río Verde, Media Luna system), *Notropis medialunae* (canals of Laguna de la Media Luna; holotype and paratypes given), and an undescribed *Cichlasoma* sp. with coloration unlike *C. steindachneri*.
- **Species pending description by others:** *Dionda erimyzonops* and *Notropis tropicus* (Hubbs manuscript).
- **Problematic population:** *Xiphophorus pygmaeus* from the Río Coy, intermediate between subspecies *pygmaeus* and *nigrensis*. The author considers either hybridization or ontogenetic variation and could not resolve which.
- **New records for the basin:** *Heterandria bimaculata bimaculata* and *Poeciliopsis infans* (the latter previously known only from the Lerma–Santiago system).
- **Endemism:** The author highlights a high endemism index. Examples include *Dionda rasconis rasconis*, *Notropis lutrensis forlonensis*, *Xiphophorus montezumae* subspecies, *Gambusia regani*, *Gambusia atrora*, and *Cualac tessellatus* (endemic to the Laguna de la Media Luna).
- **Intestinal morphology:** *Dionda* species all show a double-S gut; *Notropis* shows single or double-S forms, possibly ontogenetically variable, suggesting a diagnostic character worth further study.
- **Most abundant taxa:** *Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus*, *Poecilia mexicana*, *Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum* and *Cichlasoma steindachneri*.
- **Conservation/management implications:** The author stresses that taxonomic knowledge underpins fisheries management, aquaculture and sport-fishery regulation, and that further collecting is needed; the thesis is explicitly not a final resolution of the basin's unknowns.
- **Salinity-based groups (summary):** Roughly 33 freshwater-stenohaline species, a set of euryhaline species, and several marine invaders, with the largest list of frequent brackish-water invaders among the Atherinidae, Engraulidae and Cyprinodontidae.
