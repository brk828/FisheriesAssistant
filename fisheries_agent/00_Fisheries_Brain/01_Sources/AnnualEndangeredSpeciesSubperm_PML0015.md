# 1993 Annual Endangered Species Subpermit Report and 1994 Renewal Request

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum from the Area Manager of the [[Phoenix Resource Area]] to the Regional Director, Region 2, reports 1993 inventory and monitoring of three endangered or sensitive species under [[Endangered Species Permit]] PRT-676811: the [[Gila Topminnow]], the [[Desert Pupfish]] and the [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]]. It also requests renewal of the permit for 1994 with only minor changes. The main story is flood damage to [[Tule Creek]] in early 1993, followed by recovery of the topminnow population by May, and stable topminnow occupancy at [[Humbug Creek]] and [[Cow Creek]]. No bats were captured.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Annual subpermit report and renewal request (memo with two attachments: a 1994 Inventory and Monitoring Plan and the permit).
- **Authors and permittees:** Area Manager Gail Acheson, with field work by sub-permittees Timothy J. Hughes and Daniel R. Langhorst, both Wildlife Biologists, Phoenix Resource Area.
- **Target species:**
  - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), the focal fish species.
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*).
  - [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]] (*Leptonycteris curasoae yerbabuenae*), a mammal surveyed by mist-netting.
- **Survey effort:** Field work covered several Arizona sites, including [[Tule Creek]] (Yavapai County), [[Humbug Creek]] and [[Cow Creek]], [[Mescal Warm Spring]] (Gila County), [[Lousy Canyon]] (Yavapai County) and the [[Gila River]] (Pinal County).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Tule Creek flood damage:** An inspection in early January 1993 (exact dates partly illegible in the scan) found the channel greatly incised. Dense bulrush (*Scirpus* sp.) along the margins was scoured away, and much of the creek bed was eroded to bedrock and rubble. Flow was about 50 ft (15 m) wide and up to 8 ft (2.5 m) deep. Riparian exclosure fencing, a water pipeline and a livestock water trough structure were badly damaged. Only the stock trough outside the downstream fence stayed intact.
- **Recovery trend:** By March 1993 bulrush was recovering along the margins. By May, topminnows were locally abundant and widely distributed, which the authors interpreted as a post-flood recovery.
- **Mescal Warm Spring:** The site was heavily overgrown with [[Bermuda Grass]] and had no open water. Topminnows had been stocked there in earlier efforts, but the report describes the habitat as unsuitable for them in its current state.
- **Desert pupfish habitat:** The spring outflow pool held ample water, but nearly all of it was covered by a dense mat of [[Duckweed]] (*Lemna* sp.), with [[Cattail]] (*Typha* sp.) emerging from the middle of the pool.
- **Water quality at the pupfish spring (single measurement set):**
  - pH 7.5
  - Conductivity 1140 (units partly illegible; likely µS/cm)
  - Dissolved oxygen 1.1 ppm
  - Temperature 59 °F

## Key Findings

1. **Tule Creek (Yavapai County):**
   - Before the flood, the exclosure held an abundant topminnow population.
   - On the early January 1993 inspection, about 100 topminnows (sex ratio partly illegible) were found in one small pool at the base of a large boulder on a bench at the upper end of the stream. Only about a dozen unidentified individuals were seen elsewhere in the channel.
   - On March 17, 1993, 20 topminnows were counted (13 female, 7 male). They were in the isolated pool and had moved into the largest pool along the creek.
   - On May 20, 1993, topminnows were locally abundant. The authors concluded they were "very post-flood" and had recovered well. A total of 90 were captured (sex not determined) from the isolated pool at the base of a small waterfall in the middle of the exclosure.
2. **Humbug/Cow Creek:**
   - Sites were visited on March 25, May 5, July 15 and July 29, 1993. Topminnows were found on all visits and throughout the permanent stream reach, indicating stable occupancy.
3. **Mescal Warm Spring:** Two 1993 site visits found the habitat overgrown and without open water. No topminnow results are reported beyond the earlier stocking history.
4. **Desert pupfish:** The spring pool was dominated by duckweed and cattails. The report implies conditions similar to 1992, but counts are not clearly legible in the scan.
5. **Lesser long-nosed bat:** Mist-net sampling at Lousy Canyon (June 7) and the Gila River (June 16) captured no bats.
6. **Renewal request:** The authors request that permit PRT-676811 be renewed for 1994 with only minor changes. Contacts for questions are Daniel Langhorst and Timothy Hughes, Phoenix Resource Area.
