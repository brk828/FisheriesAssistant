# Exotic Fishes of Southern California: Correspondence from Peter B. Moyle to Walter R. Courtenay Jr. (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #Cichlidae #Cobitidae #Gobiidae

## Executive Summary

This 1991 letter from [[Peter B. Moyle]] (Department of Wildlife and Fisheries Biology, University of California, Davis) to [[Walter R. Courtenay Jr.]] (Florida Atlantic University) asks for information to help decide which exotic fishes from Southern California belong in a revised edition of *Inland Fishes of California*. It lists nine candidate species, raises taxonomic questions about tilapias, requests life-history notes and illustrations, and reports that the [[Chameleon Goby]] has expanded into freshwater in California. The letter contains no original data.

## Background & Description

- **Author and purpose:** Moyle is revising [[Inland Fishes of California]] and needs to confirm which introduced species to include and how to describe them.
- **Context:** The letter says Moyle cancelled travel to a World Fisheries Conference in Greece because of the [[Gulf War]]. He argues that the war diverts attention and money from environmental problems and calls for more advocacy by professionals.
- **Copies:** The letter was copied to J. St. Amant, M. Horn and W. Minckley for additional information.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species under discussion are non-native fishes established or reported in California freshwater systems. The letter notes the following situations:

- **Canals and drains:** Shortfin molly occurs in a drainage canal south of Mecca, Riverside County. Oriental weatherfish occurs in [[Orange County Flood Control Channels]].
- **Irrigation ditches:** Redbelly tilapia occurs in ditches in the Coachella, Imperial and Palo Verde Valleys. Blue tilapia is inferred to occur in the [[Colorado River]] from Arizona collections.
- **Native fish impacts:** The Mozambique tilapia introduction into [[High Rock Spring]], Lassen County, is associated with the extinction of the native [[Tui Chub]].
- **Mosquito abatement:** Wami tilapia may have been used for mosquito abatement at [[Bolsa Chica]], which the letter flags as the only reasonably good record.
- **Estuary spread:** Over the past five years the chameleon goby moved into fresh water. It is now among the most abundant species in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Estuary]] and has spread through the canal system.

## Key Findings

**Candidate exotic species for the revised book (Moyle's list):**

1. Shortfin molly (*Poecilia mexicana*): drainage canal south of Mecca, Riverside County.
2. Blue tilapia (*Tilapia aurea*): Colorado River, by inference from Arizona collections.
3. Mozambique tilapia (*Oreochromis mossambicus*): widespread; linked to the loss of native tui chub.
4. Redbelly tilapia (*Tilapia zilli*): irrigation ditches in three Imperial-region valleys; Moyle asks about other extant populations.
5. Oriental weatherfish (*Misgurnus anguillicaudatus*): Orange County flood control channels.
6. Porthole livebearer (*Poeciliopsis gracilis*): distribution same as shortfin molly.
7. Green swordtail (*Xiphophorus helleri*): probably not established; Moyle asks about recent records.
8. Guppy (*Poecilia reticulata*): asks whether any Southern California records exist.
9. Wami tilapia (*Tilapia hornorum*): asks whether it is established, whether Bolsa Chica is the only good record, and whether it is still used for mosquito abatement.

**Open questions and requests:**

- **Tilapia identification:** If all four tilapias are present, Moyle asks for a key to separate them. His collection contains only *T. zilli* and *O. mossambicus*.
- **Nomenclature:** He asks for the official [[American Fisheries Society]] position on tilapia generic names.
- **Life-history data:** He asks for field notes or life-history information on these species in California to support short species accounts.
- **Illustrations:** He asks for usable illustrations of the species, or sources for them.

**Other conclusions:**

- The letter reports that the chameleon goby has moved into fresh water and is among the most abundant species in the Sacramento-San Joaquin estuary.
- The letter states that the chameleon goby has spread through the canal system "as far south as Pyramid Reservoir in southern California." Pyramid Reservoir is generally located in Nevada, so this location may be an error in the original letter and should be checked against other sources.
