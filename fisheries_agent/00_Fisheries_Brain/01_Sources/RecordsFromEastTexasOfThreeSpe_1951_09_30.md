## Executive Summary

This 1951 note by Clark Hubbs documents new East Texas distribution records for three small freshwater fish species — **Semotilus atromaculatus atromaculatus** (creek chub), **Notropis cornuta isolepis** (a shiner subspecies), and **Microperca proelearis** (a darter) — none of which were included in Baughman's earlier survey, "Random Notes on Texas Fishes" (1950). The records extend the known ranges of these taxa into multiple East Texas river systems, including the Neches, Sabine, Lake Caddo (Red River drainage), Trinity, San Jacinto, and Sulphur River systems, establishing these species as more widely distributed in the region than previously recognized.

## Background & Description

The note reports on collections made in East Texas over a two-year period preceding publication. Three species/subspecies collected during this survey work were notably absent from [[Baughman's Random Notes on Texas Fishes]] (1950), making these new distributional records of taxonomic and biogeographic interest:

- **[[Semotilus Atromaculatus]]** (creek chub) — a widespread cyprinid known for tolerance of small-stream habitats.
- **[[Notropis Cornuta Isolepis]]** — a shiner subspecies originally described from the Red River drainage.
- **[[Microperca Proelearis]]** — a small darter (Percidae), with specimens from different river systems showing slight morphological differences, suggesting possible undescribed variation or a closely related species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All three species were collected from small lotic freshwater habitats — creeks, bayous, and one lake — situated across several distinct river drainages in the northeastern corner of Texas. This distributional pattern connects several major Gulf-draining systems:

- **[[Neches River System]]**
- **[[Sabine River System]]**
- **[[Lake Caddo Drainage]]** (draining into the [[Red River]])
- **[[Trinity River System]]**
- **[[San Jacinto River System]]**
- **[[Sulphur River Drainage]]** (Red River tributary)

The presence of *Notropis cornuta isolepis* exclusively within the Lake Caddo drainage is ecologically consistent, since this drainage connects to the Red River, the type locality for the subspecies — a "range extension" that the author notes is unsurprising given this hydrological connection.

For *Microperca proelearis*, specimens from the Red River system (Sulphur River drainage) reportedly differ slightly in morphology from those collected in the Neches, Trinity, and San Jacinto systems, hinting at intraspecific geographic variation possibly linked to drainage isolation.

## Key Findings

**Semotilus atromaculatus atromaculatus** — collected at four East Texas localities:
1. Venado Bayou, 4.5 mi west of San Augustine, San Augustine County ([[Neches River System]])
2. Harris Creek, 7.5 mi east of Tyler, Smith County ([[Sabine River System]])
3. Creek, 1 mi east of Gilmer, Upshur County ([[Lake Caddo Drainage]])
4. Haggerty Creek, 7 mi east of Marshall, Harrison County ([[Lake Caddo Drainage]])
- Conclusion: fairly widely distributed across northeastern Texas.

**Notropis cornuta isolepis** — collected at four localities, all within the Lake Caddo drainage:
1. Haggerty Creek, 7 mi east of Marshall, Harrison County
2. Lawrence Creek, 6 mi northwest of Marshall, Harrison County
3. Eagle Creek, 4.5 mi west of Harleton, Henderson County
4. Creek, 1 mi west of Harleton, Harrison County
- Conclusion: common within the Lake Caddo area; consistent with its original description from the connected Red River drainage.

**Microperca proelearis** (or a closely related species) — the most frequently collected of the three, from six localities:
1. Peach Creek, 14 mi east of Conroe, Montgomery County ([[San Jacinto River System]])
2. Creek, 6 mi east of Livingston, Polk County ([[Trinity River System]])
3. Creek, 1 mi west of Saratoga, Hardin County ([[Neches River System]])
4. Lake Caddo, 5 mi northeast of Karnack, Harrison County (collected 3 times)
5. Haggerty Creek, 3 mi northwest of Karnack, Harrison County, Lake Caddo drainage (collected 2 times)
6. Rice's Creek, 4 mi west of Maud, Bowie County ([[Sulphur River Drainage]], Red River)
- Notable finding: specimens from the Red River system show slight morphological differences compared to those from the Neches, Trinity, and San Jacinto systems, suggesting possible geographic variation.
