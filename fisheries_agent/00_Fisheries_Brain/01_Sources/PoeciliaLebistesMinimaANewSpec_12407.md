# Poecilia Minima: A New Neotropical Poeciliid from the Brazilian Amazon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes [[Poecilia Minima]], a new species of [[Poeciliinae]] livebearer from a stream (igarapé) in Ourém, in the [[Rio Guamá Basin]] of eastern Brazilian Amazonia, Pará State. The species is placed in the subgenus [[Lebistes]] and is hypothesized to be closely related to [[Poecilia Branneri]], with which it shares five synapomorphies. It is distinguished mainly by a distinct colour pattern, a more slender body, and a markedly smaller adult size: males do not exceed 16 mm standard length (SL). The species is known only from its type locality.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The Poeciliinae is a diversified clade of cyprinodontiform fishes found across North, Middle and South America. Its defining feature is the [[Gonopodium]], a modified male anal fin used for internal fertilization. Most members are [[Viviparity|viviparous]]; the exception noted is *Tomeurus*. Twenty-one genera were recognized in the last major revision by [[Rosen & Bailey (1963)]], but few have been formally shown to be monophyletic. The authors note that many taxa in the subfamily lack objectively defined apomorphies.

**Lebistes.** Originally a genus, [[Lebistes]] is now treated as a subgenus of *Poecilia*. It comprises small poeciliins endemic to northern South America (Venezuela, the Guianas, Brazil and the Lesser Antilles). Males usually reach about 25 mm SL and females about 40 mm SL. The validity of several nominal species similar to [[Poecilia Parae]] remains doubtful.

**Diagnosis.** The new species shares these five synapomorphies with *P. branneri*:
- Dorsal fin of males with an elongated posterior tip, formed mainly by the penultimate ray.
- A black blotch bordered by a yellow-to-red spot just anterior to the caudal fin.
- Males with more dorsal-fin rays than females.
- Last segments of gonopodial rays 3 and 4a ankylosed (fused).
- Subdistal segments of ray 4p bearing pointed processes that overlap ray 5.

**Distinguishing characters.** Compared with *P. branneri*, *P. minima* has:
- Males with two to four black vertically transverse stripes on the abdomen, versus eight to ten pale grey transverse stripes on the body sides in *P. branneri*.
- A purplish silver longitudinal stripe above the lateral midline of the male caudal peduncle, absent in other congeners.
- A black blotch on the female abdomen, absent in other congeners.
- A more slender body: body depth 18.6–19.8% SL in males and 19.3–20.4% in females, versus 25.0–25.9% and 21.9–23.2% in *P. branneri*.
- A longer dorsal-fin base in males (9.8–12.1% SL, versus 13.3–14.5%).

**Morphology.** Key meristic and osteological features include:
- Dorsal-fin rays 8 (i, 5, i) in males and 7 (i, 5, i) in females.
- Anal-fin rays 9 (iii, 5, i); pectoral-fin rays 13–14; pelvic-fin rays 6.
- Vertebrae 29, with two gonapophyses on vertebrae 13–15 of males.
- Longitudinal scale series 26–27; 16 scale rows around the caudal peduncle.
- Conical teeth in males; outer-row teeth spatula-like in females.
- Male pelvic fin elongate, reaching a vertical through the posterior half of the dorsal fin.

**Coloration.** Males have light grey sides with the features listed above, a triangular black caudal blotch with a red dorsoposterior border, and hyaline fins with pale yellow transverse zones. Females have a rounded black caudal blotch with an orange dorsoposterior spot and a transverse black blotch below the lateral midline at the pelvic-fin base.

**Etymology.** The name derives from the Latin *minima* ("minimal"), referring to the small size of the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The holotype and paratypes were collected from a stream (igarapé) in Ourém, rio Guamá basin, Pará, Brazil. The paper gives no water chemistry, habitat structure or community data.
- **Distribution:** The species is known only from the type locality.
- **Sympatry:** *P. minima* occurs sympatrically, but not syntopically, with *P. parae* and *P. branneri* in Pará. Syntopic means occupying the same specific microhabitat, so the paper implies separation at a finer scale.
- **Reproduction:** Two cleared and stained females (18.8 mm and 21.0 mm SL) carried three or four fully developed embryos (about 5 mm SL) and three further embryos (about 2 mm SL) showing only the caudal fin. This confirms viviparity.
- **Size:** Maximum recorded size is 15.7 mm SL for males and 23.6 mm SL for females. Sexual dimorphism is visible in specimens longer than 12.0 mm SL.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Poecilia minima* is described from Ourém, rio Guamá basin, Pará, Brazil.
- **Type series:** The holotype is MPEG 3393, a male of 15.7 mm SL, collected 7–8 August 1996 by A. Sarraf, J. R. Carvalho Jr., E. Nogueira and L. Gomes. The paratypes total roughly 60 specimens across eight institutional collections, including UFRJ, MZUSP, MCP, MNHN, USNM and UMMZ. The series includes males of 12.2–16.3 mm SL, females of 13.8–23.6 mm SL, and seven juveniles of 10.6–12.0 mm SL. The paratype counts were not totalled in the paper.
- **Closest relative:** *P. branneri* is the sister taxon, based on five synapomorphies.
- **Size as diagnostic:** The males are much smaller than those of congeners (about 25 mm SL in other species).
- **Taxonomic placement:** The species is assigned to [[Lebistes]] sensu [[Rosen & Bailey (1963)]]. The authors argue that the monophyly of Lebistes is supported by the much smaller size of males, an apomorphy earlier noted for *Acanthophacelus*.
- **Synonymy history:** The paper traces the genus-level history from [[Lebistes]] (de Filippi, 1861) through [[Acanthophacelus]] (Eigenmann, 1907), [[Micropoecilia]] (Hubbs, 1926) and the subsequent revisions by [[Meyer (1993)]] and Rosen & Bailey (1963). The authors conclude that Meyer's redefinition of *Micropoecilia* is probably not monophyletic.
- **Future work:** The authors' broader phylogenetic study of *Poecilia* sensu Rosen & Bailey is in progress. Relationships with the other Lebistes species were beyond the scope of this paper.
- **Publication:** The paper appeared in *Ichthyological Exploration of Freshwaters* (Vol. 8, No. 2, pp. 185–191, October 1997). It was received 3 September 1996, revised 2 December 1996, and accepted 19 January 1997.
