# Australian Desert Springs/Great Artesian Basin Bibliography

#hydrogeology #desert-ecology #artesian-springs #Great-Artesian-Basin #Australian-fauna

## Executive Summary

This document is an extensive unpublished bibliography compiled by Peter J. Unmack in 1995, cataloging scientific and technical literature on the [[Great Artesian Basin]] and its associated [[Mound Springs]] in Australia. The bibliography encompasses over 300 references spanning from the 1870s to the early 1990s, covering geological, hydrological, biological, archaeological, and conservation aspects of Australia's desert spring ecosystems. The references document the discovery, exploitation, and scientific investigation of artesian water resources and the unique endemic biota dependent on these isolated aquatic habitats in arid central Australia.

## Background & Description

The [[Great Artesian Basin]] is one of the world's largest groundwater systems, underlying portions of Queensland, New South Wales, South Australia, and the Northern Territory. The basin's natural discharge points create [[Mound Springs]]—isolated wetland oases in the Australian desert that support endemic aquatic species and have cultural significance to Aboriginal peoples.

The bibliography documents multiple research domains:
- **Hydrogeology and hydrology**: Groundwater movement, recharge, discharge, water chemistry, isotope studies, and basin structure
- **Ichthyology**: Endemic fish species including [[Craterocephalus]] (hardyheads), [[Chlamydogobius]] (desert gobies), and the [[Redfinned Blue-eye]] ([[Scaturiginichthys vermeilipinnis]])
- **Invertebrate fauna**: Molluscs (particularly hydrobiid snails), crustaceans (amphipods, isopods, yabbies), and other aquatic invertebrates showing high endemism
- **Botany**: Aquatic and semi-aquatic vegetation, including rare species like [[Eriocaulon carsonii]]
- **Geology**: Basin stratigraphy, Cretaceous and Tertiary formations, spring deposits
- **Conservation**: Threats from water extraction, bore rehabilitation programs, heritage assessments
- **Aboriginal significance**: Cultural sites, mythology, archaeological evidence

Key locations referenced include [[Dalhousie Springs]], [[Coward Springs]], [[Paralana Hot Spring]], [[Lake Eyre]], and numerous other spring complexes throughout the basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The mound springs represent relict aquatic ecosystems isolated since the Pleistocene, harboring species that evolved in isolation for thousands to millions of years. The springs discharge ancient groundwater (dated using chlorine-36 and other isotopes to hundreds of thousands of years old) that rises through geological structures, creating carbonate mound formations.

The aquatic fauna shows remarkable endemism at the species and genus level, with many taxa representing ancient lineages (e.g., phreatoicidean isopods, endemic hydrobiid snails). Fish species like [[Craterocephalus dalhousiensis]] and [[Chlamydogobius eremius]] demonstrate physiological adaptations to the stable thermal conditions and isolated nature of spring habitats.

Environmental threats documented include:
- Declining artesian pressure from uncontrolled flowing bores
- Water extraction for mining operations (e.g., [[Olympic Dam Project]])
- Habitat degradation from pastoral activities
- Climate change impacts on recharge

The bibliography documents extensive government and scientific efforts to understand basin hydrology, rehabilitate uncontrolled bores, and assess conservation priorities for spring ecosystems.

## Key Findings

**Historical Development**: References trace the discovery and exploitation of artesian water from the 1870s through systematic boring programs in the 1880s-1890s, with early scientific investigations by David, Jack, Gregory, and others establishing the basin's geological framework.

**Hydrogeological Research**: Major advances documented include isotope hydrology studies (Airey, Bentley, Habermehl), digital modeling of groundwater systems (GABHYD model), geothermal gradient mapping, and water chemistry analyses establishing recharge areas, flow paths, and residence times.

**Biological Discoveries**: The bibliography catalogs the description of numerous endemic species, particularly:
- Fish: [[Craterocephalus dalhousiensis]] (Ivantsoff & Glover 1974), [[Craterocephalus gloveri]] (Crowley & Ivantsoff 1990), [[Scaturiginichthys vermeilipinnis]] (Ivantsoff et al. 1991)
- Molluscs: Radiation of hydrobiid snails (Ponder & Clark 1990, Ponder et al. 1989)
- Crustaceans: Endemic amphipods, isopods, and ostracods (De Decker 1979, Zeidler 1991)

**Conservation Assessments**: Multiple studies from the 1980s-1990s document biological surveys, heritage assessments (Aboriginal, European, natural), and management recommendations, particularly related to [[Witjira National Park]] and the [[Olympic Dam Project]] environmental impact assessments.

**Dalhousie Springs Focus**: A major synthesis volume (Zeidler & Ponder 1989) provides comprehensive natural history coverage of this key spring complex, documenting geology, hydrology, flora, fauna, archaeology, and European history.

**Management Issues**: References document bore rehabilitation programs, well headworks standards, monitoring of aquifer response to extraction, and development of management strategies for sustainable use of basin water resources.

**Quaternary Studies**: Palynological and sedimentological work (Boyd 1990, Callen 1977) provides evidence for late Holocene environmental changes and spring activity patterns.
