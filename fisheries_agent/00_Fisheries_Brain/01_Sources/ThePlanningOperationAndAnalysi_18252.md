# The Planning, Operation, and Analysis of the Green River Fish Control Project

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This joint report from the Wyoming Game and Fish Department and the Utah State Department of Fish and Game describes the planning, field operation, and evaluation of a 1962 rotenone treatment of about 445 miles of the [[Green River]] and its tributaries above [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. The stated objective was to reduce non-desirable fish, chiefly [[Common Carp]] and suckers, so that a [[Sport Fishery]] could be established in the new [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]]. The project was financed under the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] at a total cost of $106,000 and coordinated by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Treatment ran from September 4 to 7, 1962, using [[Rotenone]] formulation (Chem-Fish Regular). A detoxification station at [[Brown's Park Bridge]] used [[Potassium Permanganate]] to neutralize toxicant before it reached [[Dinosaur National Monument]]. Detoxification was largely effective but was strained by under-supplied neutralizer, flow overestimates and inadequate toxicant testing methods. Aquatic invertebrates were almost totally destroyed, while native fishes were later found to be adequately represented.

## Background & Description

- **Project setting:** [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], completed as a unit of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]], began impounding the Green River in November 1962 from about 15,000 square miles of drainage. The planned dam is 495 feet high and will impound 3,930,000 acre-feet, forming a reservoir of about 42,000 surface acres.
- **Environment:** Flows ranged from about 12,000 cfs at spring runoff to about 450 cfs in low water. Air temperatures reached about -50°F in winter and about 100°F in summer; water temperatures ranged from 32°F to 75°F. Erosive Bridger and Wasatch formations dominate the lower river.
- **Rationale:** Earlier reservoir impoundments, such as [[Boysen Reservoir]] on the Wind River, saw carp explosions that spread into trout water. Two North Platte River rotenone projects ([[Glendo Reservoir]] and [[Pathfinder Reservoir]]) were cited as successful precedents.
- **Anticipated demand:** Estimated fishing use of Flaming Gorge Reservoir was 120,000 to 150,000 fisherman-days initially, rising to 185,000 to 228,000 by 1975.
- **Opposition:** Some groups argued that rotenone threatened native species such as the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Sucker]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. The agencies argued that impoundment, not rotenone, posed the main threat to swift-water species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fish community:** Non-game species (suckers, chubs and carp) formed most of the fish populations in the study area. Good trout habitat extended from the upper project area to the town of Green River; below it, siltation and temperature limited game fish.
- **Water chemistry (Big Sandy River, fall 1961):** pH 8.1 to 8.2, total alkalinity 170 to 240 ppm, temperature 41 to 56°F. Rotenone products showed little loss of efficiency under these alkaline conditions.
- **Water chemistry during treatment:** Methyl orange alkalinity rose from 91 to 100 ppm (minimum) and 135 to 142 ppm (maximum) over three weeks. pH ranged from 7.8 to 8.6. The agencies found no evidence that treatment affected water chemistry.
- **Detoxification reach:** The lower river was turbid and moved at about 2 feet per second, with a bed over 300 feet wide. Natural rotenone decay was estimated at about 1 ppm per 16 river miles.
- **Municipal supplies:** Fontenelle, Green River (serving Rock Springs), Dutch John and other communities drew water from the treated reach.

## Key Findings

**Treatment design**
- Rotenone was introduced at about 5 ppm at 10-mile station intervals, starting stations 3 hours apart. Introduction ran 7 hours at main-river stations and 6 hours on tributaries.
- Design flows were based on average September flows, with contingencies at 1.36 and 1.6 times average flow.
- A one-inch hose-and-yoke dispersal system replaced the earlier float-valve design and was adopted as the standard after a 1961 pilot study on the Green River.
- The project used 38 station sites, 3 airboats, a helicopter for isolated waters (including [[Blacks Fork River]]) and 2 commissary trailers. Over 100 men took part.

**Toxicant use and public relations**
- A total of 21,495 gallons of Chem-Fish Regular, purchased from the Chemical Insecticide Corporation for $76,307.25, was used over roughly three days.
- Landowners were notified by letter, and bridge closures were arranged. The report recorded no dissent.

**Flow errors**
- Gauge-based final flow estimates were still above actual flows. Flows on September 6 were 19 to 24 percent lower than calculated, which raised rotenone concentrations.

**Efficacy and rotenone movement**
- Field bio-assay three-fourths mile below Green River Station 1 on September 4 gave concentrations ranging from about 3.9 to 7.3 ppm across eight samples.
- Rotenone arrived on schedule above Station 9 and up to 3.75 hours early below it. The New Fork front lagged, leaving a possible untreated zone.
- Live cage survival ranged from 1 to 4 hours after initial contact.
- Fish kill was described as nearly complete on the treated reach.

**Fish kill and species**
- The fish checklist (17 species, in order of apparent abundance) was led by [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Mountain Whitefish]], followed by [[Bonytail Chub]], redside shiner and speckled dace.
- Trout were extremely infrequent above the dam and absent below it. Five squawfish were collected; no humpback chub were collected during the work.

**Post-treatment**
- Gill net and dynamite sampling afterward found very few fish, mostly whitefish from downstream migration.
- A seismographic crew detonated 275 underwater charges between September 20 and November 28, 1962, and saw no fish.
- Pre-treatment invertebrate fauna were almost totally destroyed; as of March 1, 1963, none of the groups had regained their former distribution.

**Detoxification at Brown's Park Bridge**
- Station located 31 river miles below the dam, about 16 miles above Dinosaur National Monument. It used four remodeled hatchery pellet feeders (with a fifth emergency unit) to spread coarse [[Potassium Permanganate]] crystals across the river at a planned 1:2.3 permanganate-to-rotenone ratio.
- Crystals arrived with about 20 percent passing a 30-mesh screen, forming clods. Permanganate supply was inadequate. A total of 13,860 pounds was ordered, with about 3,200 pounds added later, for a total of 17,160 pounds expended.
- Operation ran about 83 hours. The team cut the ratio to lower levels at times, which allowed brief rotenone escapes that killed caged trout.
- Rotenone was detected at the station at 0410 hours on September 8. Chemical and bio-assay tests indicated no rotenone was present below the bridge except during brief fish disturbances.
- Rotenone concentrations were eliminated for over 95 percent of the operation.

**Decay rates**
- Natural decay was non-constant. Toxicant from drip station #10 decayed at a mean of 7.2 miles per ppm over the first 37 miles, and 28 miles per ppm after 164 miles of flow.

**Dinosaur National Monument**
- Fish distress was observed at Echo Park on September 13 and at Split Mountain Campground on September 14, mostly among channel catfish. Travel-time analysis suggested the agent passed the detoxification site 12 to 36 hours after work ended. A later bio-assay was negative.
- Post-treatment surveys (September 1962 and April 1963) found native species adequately represented. Bonytail chub and possibly bluehead sucker may have declined slightly.

**Water supply and pollution monitoring (U.S. Public Health Service addendum)**
- Carbon dosing of about 150 ppm removed 5 ppm of toxicant formulation; about 60 ppm removed 2 ppm.
- Few consumer complaints were received. Coliform, B.O.D. and manganese changes were minor, with some increases attributed in part to a heavy rainstorm.
- Bottom macroinvertebrate populations were almost completely destroyed, and follow-up studies were recommended.

**Recommendations**
- Improve rotenone determination methods, since colorimetric tests took about 1.5 hours and bio-assay was preferred but slow.
- Calculate relatively exact flows each day of treatment where detoxification is planned.
- Use worm-gear or controlled feed on dispensers, and dry, moisture-protected neutralizer.
- Refine aerial application to avoid excessive concentrations on streams such as the Blacks Fork.
