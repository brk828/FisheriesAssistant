# Neolissochilus: A New Genus of South Asian Cyprinid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

Walter J. Rainboth (University of California, Los Angeles) proposes the genus [[Neolissochilus]] ("new Lissochilus") in *Beaufortia* (Vol. 35, no. 3, June 29, 1985) for large-scaled barbels of southern and southeastern Asia. The name is needed because the earlier genus name *Lissochilus* Weber & de Beaufort, 1916 is a homonym of *Lissochilus* Zittel, 1882, a fossil gastropod genus. The paper provides a generic diagnosis, reviews the nominal taxa through their type material, and separates the genus from its closest relative, [[Tor]] Gray, 1834. The genus is said to contain twenty-three nominal taxa. The paper does not attempt a full species revision.

## Background & Description

**Nomenclatural history.** The group was placed in [[Barbus]] as a subgenus by [[Hora 1941]] and was later assigned to [[Acrossocheilus]] Oshima, 1919 by [[Myers 1941]] as a replacement for *Lissochilus*. Subsequent authors distributed its species among *Acrossocheilus*, *Tor* and *Puntius* (Smith, 1945), so single publications sometimes placed members of the group in two or more genera. The type species is [[Neolissochilus Stracheyi]] (Day, 1871), originally *Barbus stracheyi*.

**Diagnostic features (from the generic description).**
- Medium to large barbels, sometimes exceeding 1 m and 10 kg, though 60 cm and 3 kg is more common.
- Dorsal fin iv/9 (rarely iv/8); pectoral i/13–17; pelvic i/8 (rarely i/7); anal iii/5; caudal fin deeply forked.
- Lateral line scales 20 to 29, plus 2 (occasionally 3) on the caudal base; circumpeduncular scales always 12.
- Scales large and heavy with numerous radii; circuli 25–28/mm on lateral fields.
- Mouth ranges from oblique and near-terminal to horizontal and inferior; long maxillary and rostral barbel pairs always present.
- Pharyngeal teeth in three rows (5.3.2) with hooked, grinding surfaces.
- Living fish dark green dorsally, silvery white ventrally, often with a bluish lateral stripe from eye to caudal base.

**Scope of material.** Counts (20) and measurements (45) were taken from more than 250 specimens across the geographic range. The author notes that considerably more material is needed to define variability within each species.

**Type material.** Most nominal taxa were examined through type specimens (all but two instances) or original figures. Notable designations include:
- [[Neolissochilus Soroides]] (Duncker, 1904): ZIM 8441 treated as lectotype by Ladiges et al. (1958) is accepted, and ZIM 7735 is excluded because it was described as a different species.
- *Lissochilus sumatranus* Weber & de Beaufort, 1916: ZMA 112606 designated lectotype; the dark caudal-tip coloration is attributed to stain rather than melanin.
- [[Neolissochilus Dukai]] (Day, 1878): a lectotype was deferred because the closest match to Day's figure is not yet certain.
- Several taxa are of doubtful status or lost. *Barbus stevensoni* Day, 1870 is known from a single juvenile specimen that is now lost or destroyed. *Barbus innominatus* Day, 1869 has not been collected for over a century and may be extinct.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and migration.** Members of the genus are described as often migratory. They move upstream into clear, rocky headwater reaches, remain in deep, slowly moving pools during the wet season, and return downstream at the onset of the dry season.

**Distribution.** The genus occurs over much of the range of [[Tor]], from the Indus River basin to Borneo. In the eastern part of the range there are two species per drainage. Both species illustrated in the paper come from the [[Chao Phraya Drainage]] and northern coastal drainages of the [[Gulf Of Thailand]]. Neolissochilus and Tor are both described as the most important indigenous game fishes of southern Asia.

**Ploidy.** Tetraploid genomes have been reported in Tor (Khuda-Bukhsh, 1980) and in *Acrossocheilus sumatranus* (Suzuki & Taki, 1981), now assigned to Neolissochilus. Other tetraploid barbels and carps are listed, including *Barbus barbus*, *Cyprinus carpio* and *Carassius auratus*. The author infers that [[Polyploidy]] arose in at least two lineages besides the Tor–Neolissochilus line.

**Ecophenotypic variability.** Lip and jaw structure, and squamation, vary greatly within these fishes. The sharp, horny lower jaw is ecophenotypically variable, so it cannot serve as the main diagnostic character.

## Key Findings

1. **New genus proposed.** [[Neolissochilus]] is erected to replace *Lissochilus* Weber & de Beaufort, 1916, which is a homonym of *Lissochilus* Zittel, 1882. The genus is said to contain twenty-three nominal taxa and is most closely related to [[Tor]].

2. **Type species.** [[Neolissochilus Stracheyi]] (Day, 1871) is the type species. Its lectotype is ZSI 2175 from Moulemein.

3. **Sharp jaw is not diagnostic.** The sharp, horny mandible varies with ecophenotype, as in many barbins, so it cannot be the primary character for the group.

4. **Key separation from Tor.** The most useful character across the full range is the lower lip. In Tor the median lobe always shows notches at the mandible margins, while Neolissochilus shows these notches only in Java and Sumatra.

5. **Gill raker counts.** Gill raker counts on the lower arm of the first arch separate the genera in most regions:
   - Ganges and tributaries: Neolissochilus 6–9 vs. Tor 10–16 (except *Tor progeneius*, 8–10).
   - Peninsular India and Sri Lanka: Neolissochilus 6–9 vs. Tor 10–14.
   - Burma (44 Neolissochilus examined): 7–11 vs. Tor 12–16.
   - Thailand and Malaya: Neolissochilus 7–12 vs. Tor 12–17.

6. **Pharyngeal arch differences.** Neolissochilus arches are shorter and more massive. The articular margin forms an angle of about 75° with the tooth-bearing arm, compared with about 45° in Tor.

7. **Tor progeneius.** This species has intermediate traits. It is assigned to Tor following Hora (1941), and the author states that it is not a hybrid.

8. **Nominal taxa.** The paper reviews the nominal taxa chronologically, from *Barbus hexastichus* M'Clelland, 1839 onward, and documents the type material for each. Problems include mixed M'Clelland specimens at the British Museum and several lost or doubtful types.

9. **Species richness and diversity.** Two species occur per drainage in the eastern range. A full species revision is needed, and the paper expects sampling and allometry problems to complicate it.

10. **Polyploidy.** Tetraploidy is documented in at least one Neolissochilus species and in Tor, suggesting polyploidization in at least two other barbel and carp lineages.

11. **Acknowledgments.** The study drew on specimens from many museums, including ZSI, BMNH, FMNH, USNM and ZMA. It was supported by NSF grant BSR 83-07102 and the UCLA Fisheries Program.
