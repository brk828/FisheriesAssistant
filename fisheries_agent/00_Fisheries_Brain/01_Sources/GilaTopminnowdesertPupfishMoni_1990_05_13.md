## Executive Summary
This internal memo (dated May 13, 1990) from Brian Bagley documents field monitoring of native fish refugia and wild populations of two federally protected Arizona desert fishes: the [[Gila Topminnow]] and the [[Desert Pupfish]]. Sampling occurred at artificial refugia ponds at Roper Lake State Park and at natural spring/creek systems near Bylas, Arizona. Key findings include an unexplained species turnover between topminnow and pupfish in stocked refugia ponds, near-extirpation of the native Middle Spring population due to drought, and continued dominance of invasive [[Western Mosquitofish]] at most natural sites. The report also notes an ongoing habitat renovation effort (gabion/dam construction) at Bylas Spring intended to support population recovery.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide direct morphological descriptions (e.g., meristics, coloration, body measurements) of the species involved. Species were identified qualitatively during seining/visual survey as:
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — small live-bearing poeciliid, native to the Gila River basin.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — small-bodied cyprinodontid adapted to extreme, fluctuating desert aquatic conditions.
- **[[Western Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*) — non-native poeciliid, morphologically and ecologically similar to Gila topminnow, a key factor in competitive displacement.
- **Bullhead** (*Ameiurus* sp.) — non-native ictalurid, present in low numbers in Lower Headquarters Pond.

No quantitative morphometric data were reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Small, isolated spring-fed ponds and desert spring/creek systems characteristic of the arid Southwest; critical refugia for endemic, imperiled desert fishes.
- **Site descriptions**:
  - **Lower Headquarters Pond** (Roper Lake State Park): Topminnow abundant and healthy; low numbers of non-native bullhead present; no pupfish observed; encroaching vegetation identified as an emerging habitat concern.
  - **Upper Headquarters Pond**: Located above Lower Pond, separated by a ~4-foot near-vertical waterfall. Stocked exclusively with desert pupfish in 1989; subsequent sampling found only [[Gila Topminnow]], with no clear mechanism for topminnow colonization or pupfish disappearance — mirroring an analogous species reversal seen previously in the lower pond.
  - **Bylas Spring** (below barrier): Dominated by [[Western Mosquitofish]] (100 individuals), topminnow absent — suggesting competitive exclusion below the barrier.
  - **Bylas Spring** (above barrier, incl. spring near corral): Coexistence of mosquitofish (68) and topminnow (40), indicating barriers to mosquitofish invasion provide partial refuge for topminnow.
  - **Spring next to corral only**: Comparable co-occurrence (28 mosquitofish, 43 topminnow) — best relative topminnow representation among natural sites.
  - **Middle Spring** (near Bylas): Native source spring for Roper Lake Park's topminnow stock; nearly desiccated in 1989, resulting in extirpation of the natural population; only a native snail species persisted at time of survey.
  - **Salt Creek** (below barrier): Heavily mosquitofish-dominated (99 mosquitofish : 1 topminnow).
  - **Salt Creek** (above barrier): Improved but still mosquitofish-dominated (76 mosquitofish : 24 topminnow).
- **Species interactions**: Strong evidence of competitive/ecological displacement between native [[Gila Topminnow]] and invasive [[Western Mosquitofish]], with physical barriers (e.g., natural or constructed barriers) apparently mitigating mosquitofish encroachment and supporting topminnow persistence.
- **Conservation infrastructure**: A newly constructed gabion and cement dam at Bylas Spring represent active habitat renovation to exclude non-native fish and support the reintroduction of Bylas-origin [[Gila Topminnow]]. Some engineering issues were noted (seepage through mounded dirt near the dam, water welling up beneath the dam).

## Key Findings
- **Refugia significance**: The native fish ponds at Roper Lake State Park constitute the only substantial refugium for the Middle Spring topminnow lineage, aside from a few small aquarium populations, following the near-total loss of the natural Middle Spring population to drought in 1989.
- **Unexplained species reversal**: In both the Upper and Lower Headquarters Ponds, stocked cohorts of [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]] showed anomalous shifts — ponds stocked predominantly with one species were later found to contain almost exclusively the other. The mechanism (e.g., differential survival, predation, unnoticed movement over/around the waterfall barrier, sampling bias) remains unresolved.
- **Non-native species pressure**: [[Western Mosquitofish]] heavily dominate natural habitats at Bylas Spring (below barrier) and Salt Creek, both above and below barriers, underscoring the ongoing threat of invasive poeciliids to native topminnow persistence.
- **Barrier effectiveness**: Sites located "above barrier" consistently showed higher relative proportions of topminnow to mosquitofish, supporting the conservation strategy of using physical barriers to exclude invasives.
- **Propagation effort**: 70 [[Gila Topminnow]] from the spring near the corral at Bylas were transferred to Arizona State University (ASU) for captive propagation, intended for future reintroduction to Bylas Spring after habitat renovation.
- **Habitat threats**: Encroaching vegetation at Lower Headquarters Pond and structural seepage issues at the newly built Bylas Spring gabion/dam were flagged as needing attention.
- **Personnel/agencies involved**: Field work and reporting involved Brian Bagley, Jim Wallace, [[Paul Marsh]], Jason Dunham, Steve Vives, Stewart Jacks, Jim Higgs, Sally Stefferud, and Jim Brooks — indicating a multi-agency (state park, university, and possibly USFWS) collaborative monitoring effort for these two Endangered/Threatened desert fish species.
