> **Note on Source Scope:** This paper ("Butterflies from Coahuila, Mexico," Clench 1968) is a lepidopteran faunal survey and is **not** a fish-focused study. It contains only a single, brief comparative reference to freshwater fish endemism in the Cuatro Ciénegas region, cited in support of a biogeographic argument about butterfly distribution. This note extracts and contextualizes that reference and the relevant aquatic habitat data, rather than fabricating fish-specific content absent from the source.

## Executive Summary
The source documents butterfly collections from three localities near [[Cuatro Ciénegas De Carranza]], Coahuila, Mexico (Rio Cañon, Rio Salado de los Nadadores, and El Carrizo), made in June 1966. While the paper's primary taxonomic focus is Lepidoptera, it makes a notable ecological aside: unlike the butterfly fauna, which shows **no trace of regional endemism**, the region's **freshwater fish and especially freshwater mollusks** exhibit a "truly remarkable degree" of endemism, citing [[Taylor 1966]] ("A remarkable snail fauna from Coahuila, Mexico"). This statement situates the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] as a globally significant hotspot of aquatic endemism, contrasting sharply with the highly mobile, widespread, opportunistic terrestrial insect fauna occupying the same physical landscape.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although no fish-specific ecological data are given, the paper describes the aquatic and riparian habitats within which the (unstudied) fish populations presumably occur:

- **Rio Cañon** (~1000 m elevation, 3 mi N of Cuatro Ciénegas): A spring-fed, permanent stream within a deep, narrow canyon (walls up to 500 ft high), flanked by willows and narrow-leaf cottonwoods. Classified as Lower Sonoran zone / [[Chihuahuan Desert Scrub]] (per Muller 1947). This represents a well-watered, perennial refugial habitat type — the kind of isolated spring/stream system associated with high aquatic endemism in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]].
- **Rio Salado de los Nadadores** (~650 m elevation, 7.3 mi W of Sacramento): A heavily farmed desert basin river with man-made channels, hedgerows, and scattered cottonwoods. Classified as Subtropical zone / [[Tamaulipan Thorn Scrub]].
- **El Carrizo** (~550 m elevation, 2.4 mi E of Sacramento): Rocky, hilly, goat-grazed mesquite grassland, not directly aquatic; likely Subtropical zone.

The author explicitly attributes the differences between the Rio Cañon and Rio Salado faunas/habitats to a ~350 m elevation gradient and a westward decrease in precipitation, with Rio Cañon being comparatively more "well-watered." This hydrological contrast is ecologically relevant to understanding aquatic habitat heterogeneity in the broader [[Chihuahuan Desert]] freshwater ecosystem, even though it is applied here to explain butterfly zonation rather than fish distribution.

## Key Findings
- **No fish taxa are named, counted, or otherwise scientifically treated** in this source; all quantitative data (specimen counts, sex ratios, collection dates) pertain to butterfly species.
- The single aquatic-ecology-relevant statement: butterfly fauna of the region shows **no regional endemism**, in contrast to reptiles (some endemism) and, most strikingly, **freshwater fish and freshwater mollusks**, which show a "truly remarkable degree" of endemism (citing [[Taylor 1966]]).
- This supports the broader recognition of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] as an isolated desert wetland/spring system harboring highly localized aquatic biodiversity — a pattern well documented independently in ichthyological and malacological literature on the basin's endemic pupfish (*Cyprinodon* spp.) and other aquatic taxa, though such species are **not named or discussed** in this particular paper.
- Researchers seeking primary fish data on the Cuatro Ciénegas system should consult [[Taylor 1966]] (Veliger 9:152–228) and dedicated ichthyological literature on the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Pupfish Complex]], as this butterfly-focused source provides only indirect, citational acknowledgment of that endemism.
