# Erythrocyte Antigens of California Trouts

#ichthyology #salmonidae #immunogenetics #population-genetics #trout-management

## Executive Summary

This 1964 study by [[John R. Calaprice]] and [[John E. Cushing]] investigated erythrocyte (red blood cell) antigens in California trout species to determine if immunogenetic techniques could identify relationships and variation useful for fisheries management. The research examined [[Golden Trout]] (*Salmo aquabonita*), [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*), [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*), [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), and [[Eastern Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) from multiple California locations. Using agglutination tests with normal and immune sera, the authors demonstrated that California trouts display high antigenic diversity at species, strain, and individual levels, providing genetic markers applicable to population studies, hybridization detection, and management of distinctive physiological strains.

## Background & Description

The study was conducted at the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Convict Creek Experiment Station]] in [[Mono County]] and the [[University of California Santa Barbara]] from July through November 1962. Specimens were supplied by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] from diverse sources:

- **[[Golden Trout]]** (*Salmo aquabonita*): [[Laurel Lakes]] (Mono County), South Fork [[Kern River]] and [[Golden Trout Creek]] (Tulare County), [[Cottonwood Lakes]] (Inyo County)
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Salmo gairdnerii*): Fall-spawning strain from [[Hot Creek Hatchery]] broodstock; spring-spawning strain from [[Mt. Whitney Hatchery]] broodstock
- **[[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Salmo clarki henshawi*): [[Heenan Lake]], [[Alpine County]]
- **[[Brown Trout]]** (*Salmo trutta*): [[Mt. Whitney Hatchery]] broodstock
- **[[Eastern Brook Trout]]** (*Salvelinus fontinalis*): [[Mt. Whitney Hatchery]] broodstock

Blood samples were obtained by caudal bleeding, and erythrocytes were washed in [[Alsever's Physiological Saline Solution]]. The cells remained viable for several days under refrigeration. Two types of sera were used: (i) normal sera from uninjected animals, and (ii) immune sera prepared by injecting rabbits with trout erythrocytes. Antigenic differences were detected using slide and capillary tube agglutination tests, with reactions scored on a scale from 4 (strong agglutination) to negative. Absorption techniques removed antibodies with unwanted specificities, leaving antibodies reactive only with particular antigens useful for distinguishing individuals, strains, or species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research addressed practical fisheries management needs in California, where understanding genetic relationships and population structure is essential for:

- Managing endemic [[Golden Trout]] populations across isolated high-elevation waters
- Distinguishing between spring-spawning and fall-spawning [[Rainbow Trout]] strains with different physiological characteristics
- Detecting hybridization between species in wild populations
- Evaluating survival of planted trout stocks
- Assessing relative fitness of distinctive physiological strains in hatchery culture

The study built upon extensive prior work on salmonid erythrocyte antigens by Ridgway (Tenth Pacific Science Congress, 1962), [[Cushing and Sprague]] (1952), Balakhnin (1961), [[Sanders and Wright]] (1962), and Bingham (1963). The authors noted that antigenic characters offer advantages as genetic markers: uncomplicated genetics, independence from environmental influences, and detection by relatively simple techniques.

## Key Findings

**Species-Level Antigenic Differences:**

Normal sera from various animal species (human, cow, goat, horse, sheep, chicken, goose, pigeon, turkey, finback whale, spiny lobster) distinguished trout species. [[Brown Trout]] was serologically very distinctive, while [[Brook Trout]], [[Cutthroat Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], and [[Golden Trout]] formed a closely related serological group. Each reaction was essentially identical for six individuals of each species tested.

**Immune Sera Reactions:**

Unabsorbed anti-golden serum (Laurel-27) prepared from [[Laurel Lakes]] [[Golden Trout]] agglutinated golden trout erythrocytes at very dilute concentrations (up to 1:2048 dilution). At the same concentration, [[Brown Trout]] erythrocytes showed no agglutination, while [[Rainbow Trout]] showed intermediate reactions.

**Strain and Population Distinctions:**

Absorbed immune sera demonstrated antigenic differences between:
- [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Golden Trout]]
- Rainbow spring-spawning and fall-spawning stocks
- [[Golden Trout]] populations from [[Laurel Lakes]] versus [[Cottonwood Lakes]]

Anti-rainbow (spring spawn-26) serum absorbed with pooled [[Laurel Lakes]] golden trout cells detected individual antigenic differences in [[Cottonwood Lakes]] golden trout. [[Golden Trout Creek]] fish failed to react or reacted slightly, similar to [[Laurel Lakes]] fish.

**Individual Variation in Rainbow Trout:**

Detailed analysis of individual [[Rainbow Trout]] revealed:
- Both anti-rainbow (spring spawn-26) and anti-rainbow (fall spawn-21) sera recognized individual differences
- Unabsorbed fall-spawning antiserum readily distinguished between spring- and fall-spawning strains
- At least two antigenic types occur in rainbow spring-spawning fish
- Individual variations exist in rainbow fall-spawning fish
- One or more antigens occur at high frequency in spring-spawning strain but are absent or at low frequency in fall-spawning fish

**Brown Trout Individual Variation:**

Serum from rabbits immunized with [[White Croaker]] (*Genyonemus lineatus*) erythrocytes agglutinated some [[Brown Trout]] erythrocytes but not others, suggesting these species share an antigen potentially useful in population studies.

**Management Applications:**

The authors concluded that erythrocyte antigenic diversity provides valuable genetic markers for:
- Studying ecology and evolution of endemic [[Golden Trout]]
- Detecting hybridization of species in the wild
- Evaluating survival of planted trout
- Assessing culture and relative fitness of distinctive physiological strains (e.g., spring- vs. fall-spawning [[Rainbow Trout]])
- Various experimental situations where biology or management techniques are not fully understood

The study demonstrated that California trouts display sufficient antigenic diversity to characterize species, strains, and individuals, forming recognizable markers advantageous for biology and management research.
