# A Synopsis of the Characeae

#phycology #characeae #stoneworts #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This synopsis presents a revised classification of the green algal family [[Characeae]] (stoneworts), as delimited in an unpublished manuscript for the forthcoming [[Monograph of the Characeae]]. The author recognizes **81 species**, down from the 314 taxa listed in earlier work ([[Wood and Imahori 1959]]). The reduction reflects a deliberate shift to broad, "sensu latissimo" species concepts, with narrower earlier taxa demoted to varieties, subspecies, or forms. The synopsis is designed to reveal the classification system, not to serve as an identification tool. Keys and fuller treatments are reserved for the forthcoming [[Taxonomic Revision]]. One nomenclatural change is made: the illegitimate name *Nitella gelatinosa* is replaced by *Nitella tasmanica*, and the new subspecies *N. tasmanica* subsp. *gelatinifera* is established.

## Background & Description

**Scope and history.** The most recent worldwide synopsis was [[Nordstedt Synopsis]] (1882), later translated and expanded for North American taxa by [[Allen 1888]]. The present treatment is a slightly modified extract from the manuscript for the "Revision of the Characeae" (Part I, monograph by R. D. Wood; Part II, iconograph by R. D. Wood and K. Imahori). The monograph manuscript was completed in June 1959, but publication was repeatedly delayed. To release key findings early, parts are being issued in advance. The first, an outline of the classification and nomenclature, appeared in 1962 ([[Nomenclature]]). This article is the second. The paper was received October 30, 1963, and was supported in part by a grant from the [[National Science Foundation]].

**Species concept.** The author addresses the problem of excessive taxon proliferation in phycology, where ever-finer subdivision has inflated the Characeae. Earlier authors such as Braun and Groves & Groves used broad species on a limited scale. The author adopts [[Broad Species Concept]] throughout the revision. Narrower earlier taxa are placed at lower ranks:
- **Varieties** where taxa are consistent over particular areas.
- **Subspecies** in complex cases with several infraspecific taxa.
- **Forms** where taxa differ mainly by single traits or trait recombinations. The author expects formae will not be retained in future.

**Synopsis structure.** Roman numerals mark taxa at or above genus rank, and Arabic numerals mark infrageneric taxa. Each taxon carries two numbers: its position within the next higher category, and a sequential number in parentheses across the whole synopsis. Distribution codes are: cosm (cosmopolitan), Eu (Europe), Af (Africa), As (Asia), Aust (Australia), Oc (Pacific Oceania), NA (North and Central America), and SA (South America).

**Major taxonomic units.**
- **Tribe I Chareae**, with **Genus I [[Chara]]** (subgenera Chara and Charopsis, divided into sections such as Chara, Drepanophora, Grovesia, Agardhia, and Willdenowia).
- **Tribe I(2) Nitelleae**, including **Genus [[Nitella]]** (subgenera Nitella, Hyella, and Tieffallenia, containing numerous sections).
- Smaller genera: [[Lamprothamnium]], [[Lychnothamnus]], [[Nitellopsis]], and [[Tolypella]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a taxonomic synopsis and does not describe habitats, water chemistry, or ecological associations. Its ecological relevance lies in its **distribution data**, which are expressed as regional codes attached to each taxon. Examples include:
- [[Chara vulgaris]] and [[Chara hispida]], which are recorded from Europe, Africa, and Asia, and in the case of *C. vulgaris* cosmopolitan.
- [[Chara braunii]], described as nearly cosmopolitan.
- [[Nitella flexilis]], recorded from Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia, North America, and South America.
- [[Nitella gracilis]], cosmopolitan.
- Several taxa restricted to single regions, such as *Nitella tasmanica* (Australia, Oceania) and *Nitella hookeri* (Australia, Oceania).

Some taxa are noted as uncertain in distribution, such as *Nitella flexilis* (Australia marked with "?") and *Nitella tasmanica* (marked with "?").

## Key Findings

1. **Reduced species count.** The revised classification recognizes 81 species in place of 314 earlier taxa, a consequence of adopting broad species concepts.

2. **Hierarchical reassignment.** Narrower earlier taxa are retained as infraspecific ranks (varieties, subspecies, forms), with the author expecting formae to be dropped in future.

3. **Nomenclatural change.** *Nitella gelatinosa* A. Br. (nomen illegitimum, non-Roth 1806 homonym) is replaced by *Nitella tasmanica* Mill. ex A. Br. The taxon is construed to include two subspecies:
   - *N. tasmanica* subsp. *tasmanica*
   - *N. tasmanica* subsp. *gelatinifera* R.D.W., nom. et stat. nov. (basionym *Chara gelatinosa* Braun, Linnaea 17: 115, 1843)

4. **Structural basis of classification.** Key diagnostic characters used in the synopsis include:
   - Number of corticate layers in the axis (1-, 2-, or 3-corticate)
   - Arrangement of stipulodes (one or two tiers)
   - Presence or absence of cortication on the basal branchlet segment (phloeopodous, gymnopodous)
   - Whether gametangia occur at the base of whorls
   - Dactyl cell number and shape, mucus presence, and oospore membrane reticulation in *Nitella*
   - Monoecious versus dioecious condition

5. **Sectional treatment of *Nitella*.** Numerous sections are recognized within *Nitella* subgenus *Tieffallenia*, including mucronate and non-mucronate groupings, with several taxa placed as "incertae sedis" because they do not fit cleanly into existing sections.

6. **Taxa with uncertain placement.** The synopsis flags several difficult taxa, including *Chara socotrensis* f. *nuda*, two imperfectly corticated varieties of *C. vulgaris*, and *C. globularis* f. *capensis*, a two-corticated form resembling a three-corticated one.

7. **Publication status.** The monograph is still unpublished as of this synopsis. Its full treatment, including keys and the iconograph, is scheduled for later release.
