# Long-term Limnological and Water Quality Monitoring in Lake Powell and Glen Canyon Tailwater

#limnology #water-quality #lake-monitoring #reservoir-ecology

## Executive Summary

This FY1996 proposal from the [[U.S. Geological Survey]], [[National Park Service]], and [[Arizona Department of Game and Fish]] outlines a collaborative lake-wide monitoring program for [[Lake Powell]] and method development in the [[Glen Canyon]] tailwater. The proposal seeks $262,801 to conduct monthly forebay profiling, three lake-wide sampling trips covering 14 stations from [[Wahweap Bay]] to [[Hite]], and experimental work measuring primary production via diel pH and oxygen oscillations in the Glen Canyon reach. The work is designed as a transition step toward long-term monitoring mandated by the [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]], recognizing that water quality in the [[Grand Canyon]] is controlled primarily by processes in Lake Powell. Principal investigators include Richard Marzolf (USGS), Doyle Stephens (USGS Utah), Robert Hart (USGS Arizona), Norman Henderson, Susan Dodson, and Kevin Berghoff (NPS), and Andrew Ayers (Arizona Game and Fish).

## Background & Description

[[Lake Powell]] was created by the closure of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] in 1963. The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] has maintained monitoring responsibility since closure, driven primarily by salinity documentation needs. Recent concerns include endangered species, selenium contamination, and potential construction of a selective withdrawal device on the dam. An inter-agency work group has collaborated since 1992 to develop a comprehensive monitoring plan serving multiple agency needs.

The proposal identifies 14 sampling stations along the reservoir mainstem from Palisades (mile 139 from dam) to Wahweap Bay (mile 1.6), including key locations at [[Hite Basin]], [[Bullfrog Bay]], [[Escalante Canyon]], mouth of [[San Juan River]], and [[Crossing of the Fathers]]. Tributary stations are excluded from this design. The monitoring employs CTD profiling (conductivity-temperature-depth with pH and oxygen sensors) at approximately 30 cm intervals, with grab samples collected from epilimnion, metalimnion, and hypolimnion based on real-time profile interpretation.

Water samples undergo ultra-clean protocols developed by USGS for analysis of nutrients, alkalinity (Gran titration), major cations and anions, trace elements and metals, and dissolved organic carbon. Zooplankton samples are collected via three vertical closing net tows (epilimnion, metalimnion, hypolimnion). All laboratory analyses are performed at USGS National Laboratories in Denver within days of sample return.

The [[Glen Canyon]] tailwater component focuses on the benthic alga [[Cladophora]], its epiphytes and grazers, which form the photosynthetic trophic base for the Glen Canyon trout fishery and potentially support trophic structure throughout the upper Grand Canyon. No previous estimates exist for total primary production of the reach or its spatial and seasonal variation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water quality of the [[Colorado River]] in the [[Grand Canyon]] is controlled primarily by processes in [[Lake Powell]]. The river flows so rapidly and voluminously through the canyon to [[Lake Mead]] that in-canyon processes rarely alter water quality appreciably. This makes lake-wide measurements essential rather than just forebay monitoring, as forebay data alone can document variation but cannot provide interpretive information about causes and effects needed for management decisions.

The hydrologic regime of the Colorado River, moderated by reservoir processes in Lake Powell before passing through the dam, drives the variation in water quality. The proposal schedules three lake-wide trips keyed to hydrologic patterns recorded at gages on the Colorado River at Cisco and Green River at Green River, Utah: Trip 1 in April (beginning of snowmelt inflow), Trip 2 in late July (end of snowmelt period), and Trip 3 in late September (end of stratified period that received annual snowmelt).

Multiple agencies require this monitoring data for distinct management purposes: [[National Park Service]] (Glen Canyon National Recreation Area) needs information for natural resource preservation and public health/safety; [[Bureau of Reclamation]] requires data for salinity management (specifically selenium effects on endangered fish) and effects of selective withdrawal to warm the river per [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] biological opinion on [[humpback chub]]; [[Arizona Game and Fish]] and [[Utah Division of Wildlife]] need data for sport fishery management in Lake Powell and Glen Canyon tailwater.

The Glen Canyon reach presents unique opportunities for reach-scale primary production measurement. If photosynthetic processes mediate measurable changes in water chemistry (CO₂/pH or O₂), tracking these changes on daily or seasonal time scales could provide integrated quantitative estimates of photosynthesis for the entire reach, avoiding expensive and potentially intractable spatial sampling problems inherent in chamber-based methods.

## Key Findings

**Monitoring Program Objectives:**
1. Document limnological status of Lake Powell and identify water quality trends by continuing Reclamation's monitoring program
2. Interpret trends and spatial variation to serve management needs of NPS, Reclamation, Arizona Game and Fish, and Utah Division of Wildlife
3. Provide long-term basis for research program understanding mechanisms of stability and change
4. Phase from present methods to improved program proposed by Lake Powell inter-agency work group (2 March 1994)

**Proposed Tasks and Schedule:**
- Monthly (April-September) forebay vertical profiling of temperature, oxygen, conductivity, and pH to document water quality available for release to Grand Canyon
- Monthly 24-30 hour measurements at 5-minute intervals near dam at penstock depth to document short-term variability from internal waves
- Two one-day quality assurance comparisons of chemical constituents between forebay at penstock depth and tailwater at USGS cableway
- Three lake-wide data collection trips (April, late July, late September) covering 14 stations with full water chemistry and zooplankton sampling
- One measurement period (November) of diel pH and oxygen oscillation in Glen Canyon reach as "dry run" before experimental flood in April 1996

**Glen Canyon Tailwater Methods Development:**
The proposal includes innovative approach to measuring reach-scale primary production by tracking photosynthetic effects on water chemistry. Preliminary observations on 3 May 1995 by Marzolf, Hart, and Ayers confirmed daytime pH hysteresis patterns consistent with photosynthetic CO₂ uptake. The proposed method involves four teams making synoptic measurements between dam and [[Lees Ferry]] for 24 hours at 15-minute intervals, monitoring CO₂ flux, pH, and oxygen concentration.

If successful, this approach would provide rapid, potentially automated method for estimating primary production at reach spatial scale on daily time scale, enabling experimental work on nutrient enrichment, seasonal evaluation, flow variation responses, and coupling of flow models to chemical change models incorporating biological processes.

Total project cost: $262,801 (approximately $7,000 additional if using the Leopold research vessel)
- Personnel (USGS, NPS, Arizona Game and Fish): $58,500

**Deliverables:**
1. Written protocols on monitoring strategy and methods for long-term monitoring program
2. Data files from all tasks with established data management protocols, integrated with previous monitoring records
3. Published analytical and interpretive articles as appropriate

**Collaborative Framework:**
Field personnel provided by USGS (NRP-Boulder, Arizona District-Flagstaff, Utah District-Salt Lake City) and NPS (Glen Canyon Natural Resources staff). Logistic support (boats, field safety, data logger retrieval) provided by NPS Glen Canyon NRA. Laboratory analyses by USGS Denver. Data management by USGS Flagstaff, with integration into previous monitoring records and development of methods documentation.
