# Gila Chub Proposed Endangered Listing and Critical Habitat Designation (Federal Register, August 9, 2002)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a compilation of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) proposed rule, published 9 August 2002 in the *Federal Register* (67 FR 51948), to list the [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] with designated [[Critical Habitat]]. It also contains the FWS peer-review request (2005), internal correspondence, and several public comment letters from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], the [[Center for Biological Diversity]], [[Arizona State University]] researchers, and a former FWS employee. The Service proposes about 333.6 km (207.8 mi) of headwater, spring-fed and perennial stream reaches across seven river units as critical habitat. The commenters largely support listing but challenge the narrow critical habitat design.

## Background & Description

- **Morphology:** Small-finned, deep-bodied, chunky, darkly colored fish. Adult males average about 150 mm total length; females can exceed 200 mm. Lateral-line scales usually number more than 61 and fewer than 80. Dorsal and anal fin-rays typically eight.
- **Habitat preference:** Pools in small streams, springs and [[Cienega]] wetlands; also survives in small artificial impoundments. Highly secretive, preferring quiet deep water or cover such as vegetation, boulders and fallen logs.
- **Taxonomy:** Described by Baird and Girard (1854) as *Gila gibbosa*, renamed *Tigoma intermedia* (Girard 1856). Recognized as a distinct species since the 1850s, except briefly as a subspecies of *Gila robusta* in the mid-1900s. Minckley (2000) described *Gila nigra* as a hybrid of *G. robusta* and *G. intermedia*. Commenters objected that the rule misused this taxonomy.
- **Range:** Historically recorded in about 30 rivers, streams and spring-fed tributaries across the Gila basin. Currently occupies about 10–15% of its historical range. Extant populations are scattered across Yavapai, Maricopa, Gila, Coconino, Pinal, Graham, Pima, Santa Cruz, Cochise and Greenlee counties.
- **Status survey (Weedman 1996):** Of 23 extant U.S. populations, all but one were considered small, isolated and threatened. Categories: stable-secure, stable-threatened, unstable-threatened and extirpated.
- **Regulatory listing history:** Candidate status since 1982 (Category 1, then Category 2). Petition finding in 1996 (warranted but precluded).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Threats (FWS assessment):**
- **Habitat loss:** Approximately 85–90% of habitat degraded or destroyed, from groundwater pumping, surface diversions, channelization, livestock grazing, mining, roads, recreation and urbanization. Excessive sedimentation is the primary water-quality threat.
- **Nonnative species:** Predation and competition from Centrarchidae (*Micropterus* and *Lepomis* spp.), other fishes, bullfrogs and crayfish (*Orconectes virilis*). The rule characterizes this as a major, increasing threat.
- **Disease and parasites:** *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* ("Ich") and anchor worm (*Lernaea cyprinacea*), which was found heavily infesting Gila chub in 1998 by the BLM.
- **Inadequate regulation:** Illegal fish introductions, live-bait use (nine species allowed in Arizona), and "bait-bucket" transfers via the CAP aqueduct. Protections under state, federal and Mexican law are partial.
- **Natural factors:** Drought, flood disruption, wildfire intensity and small population size.

**Food and behavior:** Omnivorous. Adults are principally carnivorous, eating invertebrates and small fishes. Juveniles feed on debris, filamentous algae and diatoms. Spawning probably occurs over submerged vegetation or root wads, with warmer temperatures (20–24 °C) apparently increasing breeding color intensity.

**Primary constituent elements (PCEs):** The rule lists seven PCEs: perennial pools and areas of higher velocity in small headwater segments; spawning temperatures of 20–26.5 °C; water quality with low contaminant levels; a food base of invertebrates, filamentous algae and insects; cover such as downed logs, submerged vegetation, root wads and undercut banks; habitat free of detrimental nonnatives; and natural, unregulated flow patterns with periodic flooding.

**Conservation actions:**
- Upper San Pedro Partnership strategies, including irrigation non-expansion areas and groundwater pumping limits.
- Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) removal of green sunfish from Sabino Canyon (1999) and O'Donnell Canyon (2002), and planned work at Bog Hole.
- Gila chub repatriation to Larry and Lousy Canyons (1995) and Gardner Canyon (1988, not confirmed).

## Key Findings

**Proposed action**
- List the Gila chub as **endangered** (not threatened). Without protection, the species will become extinct in the foreseeable future, based on: (1) 85–90% of habitat degraded; (2) small, fragmented populations; (3) nonnative competition and predation.
- Propose about **333.6 km (207.8 mi)** of critical habitat across seven river units (Upper Gila, Middle Gila, Babocomari, Lower San Pedro, Lower Santa Cruz, Verde, Agua Fria). Units include Turkey Creek (77.8 km), Eagle and East Eagle Creeks (35.2 km), Mineral Creek (14.4 km), Bonita Creek (63.5 km), Sabino Canyon (about 17.7 km) and Little Sycamore Creek.
- Critical habitat extends **300 feet** laterally on each side of the bankfull width, a method the Service admits is arbitrary. The 100-year floodplain was rejected because it is not mapped on standard topographic maps.
- Exclusions from critical habitat: Muleshoe Preserve (Double R Canyon and Wildcat Creek) and Sheehy Spring (conservation easement). The San Carlos Apache Tribe's draft fisheries plan may also lead to exclusion.

**Regulatory context**
- Section 7 consultation applies to federally funded, permitted or authorized actions. Section 9 take prohibitions apply to all endangered wildlife, including unauthorized collection and intentional introductions of nonnative predators.
- The FWS invites comments on threats, additional populations, economic and other relevant impacts and nonnative fish status.

**Public and peer comments (summary)**
- **Robert W. Clarkson (2002):** Supports endangered status but argues the critical habitat is contradictory, since the rule says stabilization at current levels will not achieve conservation while limiting habitat to currently occupied sites. Lists many internal reach-length discrepancies.
- **Desert Fishes Recovery Team (2002):** Strongly supports the listing. Recommends adding about 20 extirpated or unoccupied areas (including the Salt River and San Simon basins), and using the 100-year floodplain instead of the 300-foot buffer.
- **Center for Biological Diversity (2002):** Argues that watershed-level protection is required, citing [[Muleshoe Ranch]] case study evidence.
- **Paul C. Marsh, ASU (2002):** Notes numerous technical errors and says the rule must address nonnative species and streambank stability.
- **Former FWS employee (Stefferud, 2002):** Argues that the 19-year delay in listing contributed to the decline and that AGFD's claimed management was overstated.
- **FWS memo (August 2002):** Solicits comments until 8 October 2002. Public hearing requests were due 23 September 2002.
- **FWS peer-review request (2005):** Letter to Paul C. Marsh requesting independent expert review, under the 1994 peer-review policy, of the proposed rule. Comments were requested during a 30-day reopened comment period.

**Document status**
- The proposed rule remains open for comment and has not been finalized in this compilation. The economic analysis and environmental assessment were still pending.
