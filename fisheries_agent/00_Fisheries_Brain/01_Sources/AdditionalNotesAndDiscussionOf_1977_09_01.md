# Gila esmeralda (La Rivers 1966)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #paleoichthyology

## Executive Summary
This paper by Thomas Lugaski (1977) re-examines the taxonomic status of the fossil cyprinid **[[Gila Esmeralda]]**, originally described by La Rivers (1966) from the "Esmeralda" Formation of Nevada. The study addresses a long-standing question of whether *G. esmeralda* is a valid, distinct species or should be synonymized with **"Leuciscus" Turneri** (=*Gila turneri*), a fossil fish described earlier by Lucas (1900) from the same broad geologic formation. Using six newly examined topotype specimens (BSN #57-1 through 6, collected 1966), combined with radiometric dating evidence (Evernden & James 1964) and revised regional geology (Albers & Stewart 1972), Lugaski concludes that *G. esmeralda* is a distinct, valid **Late Miocene** taxon (~12.7 Ma), taxonomically and stratigraphically separate from the Pliocene-aged *"Leuciscus" turneri*, and that the two fishes likely inhabited separate lake basins rather than a single "Lake Esmeralda."

## Background & Description
Comparative meristic and morphometric data were drawn from topotype **BSN #57-1** (a complete specimen; standard length 95 mm, total length 126 mm) and compared against *"Leuciscus" turneri* and the extant **[[Gila Bicolor Obesus]]** (Pyramid Lake, Nevada).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Freshwater lacustrine environment; part of a fossil lake system within the broader, informally-used "**[[Esmeralda Formation]]**" of west-central Nevada (Silver Peak Range, Big Smoky Valley, Monte Cristo Range).
- **Paleoenvironmental context**: Historically treated (Turner 1900) as deposits of a single large paleolake ("Lake Esmeralda"), but revised geological work (**[[Albers And Stewart 1972]]**) demonstrates the "formation" comprises multiple, lithologically distinct sedimentary units interspersed with volcanic units — evidence for **multiple smaller lake basins** rather than one continuous lake system during the late Miocene–early Pliocene.
- **Taxonomic/ecological implication**: Because *G. esmeralda* and *"L." turneri* occur in different lithologic units of different ages, they likely represent populations from **separate, non-contemporaneous lake basins**, rather than co-occurring populations of a single variable species.
- **Modern comparative analog**: **[[Gila Bicolor Obesus]]** from Pyramid Lake, Nevada, used as a living relative for comparative meristic/morphometric benchmarking, situating *G. esmeralda* within the extant western North American **[[Cyprinidae]]** (genus *Gila*) radiation of the Great Basin.

## Key Findings
1. **Age revision**: Radiometric K-Ar dating (Evernden & James 1964) of the sedimentary unit near the type locality yields an age of **12.7 million years**, placing *G. esmeralda* in the **Late Miocene**, contradicting the original Pliocene assignment by La Rivers (1966).
2. **Species validity supported**: Multiple independent meristic differences (anal fin rays: 8 vs. 10; caudal fin rays: 19–21 vs. 23; pectoral fin rays: 14 vs. 11) and morphometric differences (head length ratio: 5.25 vs. 3.5) strongly support *G. esmeralda* as **taxonomically distinct** from *"Leuciscus" turneri*.
3. **Fin placement diagnostic**: The anal fin origin lies well behind the dorsal fin origin in *G. esmeralda* (80° angle between fin origins), whereas in *turneri* the anal fin origin is slightly anterior to the dorsal fin origin — a robust taxonomic diagnostic character.
4. **Stratigraphic/geographic separation**: *G. esmeralda* and *"L." turneri* occur in **different lithologic units** and were likely deposited in **different lake basins**, undermining Turner's (1900) single-lake "Lake Esmeralda" hypothesis.
5. **Formation nomenclature caution**: Following Albers and Stewart (1972), the term "**Esmeralda Formation**" should be applied **locally and restrictively**, not broadly across the entire Silver Peak/Big Smoky Valley region, due to significant lithologic heterogeneity (interspersed volcanic and sedimentary units).
6. **Conclusion**: Based on combined morphological, stratigraphic, and radiometric evidence, *G. esmeralda* is confirmed as a **valid, separate Miocene-age species**, distinct from the Pliocene *"Leuciscus" turneri*.
