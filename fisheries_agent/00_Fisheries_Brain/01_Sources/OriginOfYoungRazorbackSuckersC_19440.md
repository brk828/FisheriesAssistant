## Executive Summary

This document is a clarifying memorandum, not a primary research paper, authored by Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) on 30 May 1990. It addresses an apparent anomaly noted in an earlier report (18 May 1990) concerning two distinct size classes ("large" and "small") of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) collected from lakes at the ASU Research Park in Tempe, Arizona. Initial hypotheses considered the possibility of *in-situ* natural reproduction to explain the smaller size cohort, since ASU records indicated only a single authorized stocking event (November 1986). Subsequent records obtained from the Arizona Game and Fish Department revealed two additional, previously undocumented stocking events (March 1988 and September 1989) that fully account for the "small" size group. The memo formally retracts the natural reproduction hypothesis and requests correction of institutional records.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data. However, it implicitly references a **bimodal size-class structure** within the sampled population:

- **"Large" size group**: Presumed to correspond to fish stocked in November 1986, representing older, larger individuals (~3.5 years old at time of May 1990 sampling).
- **"Small" size group**: Corresponds to two more recent stockings:
  - Larvae stocked 14 March 1988 (~26 months old at sampling)
  - Juveniles stocked 29 September 1989 (~8 months old at sampling)

No length, weight, coloration, or fin-ray count data are reported in this correspondence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Artificial/constructed lentic lakes at the ASU Research Park, Tempe, Arizona — an anthropogenic, non-riverine habitat atypical of the historic range of [[Razorback Sucker]], which is native to large, turbid rivers of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Population origin**: Entirely stock-derived (hatchery-origin), not self-sustaining or naturally recruiting at this site, based on the corrected stocking record.
- **Hatchery sources**:
  - [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) — source of the 1988 larval stocking.
  - [[Bubbling Pond State Fish Hatchery]] (Arizona) — source of the 1989 juvenile stocking.
- **Management context**: This case is relevant to broader conservation and recovery efforts for the [[Razorback Sucker]], a federally listed endangered species, where stocked populations in refugial habitats (like research park lakes) are used to supplement wild, declining populations in the Colorado River system.

## Key Findings

- **No evidence of natural reproduction**: The presence of a "small" size class of razorback sucker at the ASU Research Park lakes is **not** attributable to *in-situ* spawning and recruitment, contrary to initial speculation.
- **Incomplete stocking records were the source of confusion**: ASU's internal files reflected only one stocking event (November 1986, presumably of larger fish), omitting two subsequent stockings documented independently by the Arizona Game and Fish Department:
  - 14 March 1988: 5,130 larvae from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]
  - 29 September 1989: 10,000 juveniles from [[Bubbling Pond State Fish Hatchery]]
- **Corrective action**: The memo formally requests that recipient institutions correct their records to reflect that the "small" size group originated from documented hatchery stockings, not wild recruitment.
- **Broader implication**: This case underscores the importance of comprehensive, cross-agency record-keeping in evaluating apparent recruitment or reproductive events in stocked or refugial populations of endangered fishes such as the [[Razorback Sucker]], to avoid erroneous conclusions about self-sustaining wild populations.
