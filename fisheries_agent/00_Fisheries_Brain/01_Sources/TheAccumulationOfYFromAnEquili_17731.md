# The Accumulation of Y-90 from an Equilibrium Mixture of Sr-90/Y-90 by Artemia salina (L.)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #artemiidae

## Executive Summary

This 1958 laboratory study examined how adult [[Brine Shrimp]] (*Artemia salina*), a planktonic crustacean representing the second trophic level, take up radionuclides from sea water containing an equilibrium mixture of [[Strontium-90]] and its daughter [[Yttrium-90]]. Animals were unfed and exposed for 92 hours, after which their activity was counted with an end-window Geiger-Müller tube. At 16 hours the shrimp held about 40 times the activity of an equal weight of sea water. The decay of the accumulated activity matched the half-life of Y-90, indicating that the biological uptake was mainly yttrium, with only about 5 per cent of equilibrium activity attributed to Sr-90. The authors conclude that this biological fractionation may alter the transfer of these hazardous nuclides up human food chains.

## Background & Description

The study was motivated by the need to quantify the biological hazard of radioisotopes from reactor wastes and fallout, specifically their rates of accumulation and maximum concentrations in ecosystems. Earlier work by [[Boroughs et al. (1956)]] showed that strontium and yttrium fed to fish are rapidly excreted, while relatively little is accumulated from water; retention depends on species, age, food habits and possibly sex. [[Rice (1956)]] reported that some organisms discriminate between isotopes present in the same medium.

Planktonic crustacea make up a large share of food at lower marine trophic levels, so they may transfer isotopes from phytoplankton to smaller carnivores. The experiment aimed to measure the Sr-90 and Y-90 accumulated by brine shrimp when both are present in sea water.

Key physical properties noted:
- **Sr-90:** half-life about 28 years; maximum beta energy 0.61 MeV.
- **Y-90:** daughter of Sr-90; half-life about 2.5 days; maximum beta energy 2.18 MeV.
- Secular equilibrium is re-established after about 10 half-lives of Y-90 (about 25 days).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Adult brine shrimp averaged 10.3 mg. Sea water was filtered through glass fiber filters retaining particles of bacterial dimensions (0.1 micron). Experimental solutions were prepared from about 61.2 µCi of Sr-90/Y-90 from [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] diluted to 100 ml. Glassware was washed with concentrated H2SO4 and detergent, then rinsed in distilled water.

Two processes shaped the results:
- **Biological fractionation:** Artemia preferentially retained the short-lived Y-90 over the long-lived Sr-90, which may reduce Sr-90 at higher trophic levels while concentrating it elsewhere.
- **Surface adsorption:** Y-90 was lost from solution to glassware, most likely by adsorption, and more was adsorbed from distilled water than from sea water ([[Adsorption on Glassware]]).

Comparative work cited in the paper included [[Chipman et al. (1955, 1956)]] on Artemia nauplii, and [[Rice (1956)]] on marine planktonic algae, where 90 per cent or more of activity in ten algal species was yttrium, but *Carteria* accumulated only strontium.

## Key Findings

- **Rapid, large uptake:** Shrimp held about 40 times the activity of sea water at 16 hours (about 10^5 cpm/g versus about 25,000 cpm/g). Shrimp that were only dipped in the water took up little activity (about 55 cpm per animal at zero time, roughly 5,335 cpm/g).
- **Y-90 dominance:** Activity fell by half in 2.5–3.0 days, matching the half-life of Y-90. The high initial activity was therefore mainly Y-90, with the residual due to Sr-90.
- **Concentration ratio:** Adult shrimp reached a mean concentration ratio of 0.13 (range 0.10–0.16) across 16, 40, 64 and 92 hours. Table 1 lists sea water at 36,000 cpm/g and shrimp at 3,500–5,600 cpm/g. The authors estimate adults accumulate about 13 per cent of available nuclides on a weight basis.
- **Sr-90 share:** Only about 5 per cent of equilibrium activity was attributed to Sr-90, after correcting for the higher counting efficiency of Y-90's more energetic beta particles.
- **Age effect:** Nauplii data from Chipman et al. (Table 2, 0.5–24 hours, concentration ratios 0.43–0.77) suggest young Artemia accumulate more Sr than adults, so life-long exposure could give a higher Sr-90 share than the 5 per cent measured.
- **Food pathway:** Sr-90 uptake may rise if it is present in phytoplankton eaten by Artemia.
- **Sea water activity shift:** Sea water activity rose by 20–60 per cent by equilibrium, consistent with Y-90 being removed from the original mixture.
- **Methodological caution:** Pipetting and adsorption onto glass changed the isotope ratio, so data interpretation must account for time and surface effects. The authors state that working with an equilibrium Sr-90/Y-90 mixture in ordinary glassware is very difficult.
- **Implications:** Biological fractionation can remove the more hazardous Sr-90 at one trophic step while enriching it elsewhere, so isotope loss in one food cycle may increase enrichment in another.
