## Executive Summary

This document is a 1996 draft interagency communication between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) and the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) establishing **Interim Extirpation Criteria** for the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), an endangered native poeciliid of the American Southwest. The letter and attached criteria do not present new morphological or ecological field data; rather, they define a standardized, replicable protocol for declaring historic or reintroduced [[Gila Topminnow]] populations "extirpated" (locally extinct) at a given site. This determination is a necessary administrative step under the [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]] to enable land management agencies to reallocate conservation resources toward viable population establishment and maintenance, rather than continued monitoring of sites judged functionally lost.

## Background & Description

No morphological data (meristics, coloration, size classes, sexual dimorphism) are provided in this source. The document is procedural/administrative in nature. However, contextual taxonomic notes relevant to identification protocols include:

- The species co-occurs with, and is frequently confused with, non-native **mosquitofish** (*Gambusia affinis*), a congeneric-looking [[Poeciliidae]] competitor.
- Criterion #4 mandates a **representative voucher collection** whenever mosquitofish are present or suspected, to be verified and deposited in an appropriate museum — implying that field differentiation between [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Mosquitofish]] requires expert taxonomic confirmation, likely due to overlapping morphology and habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Desert aquatic systems within the Gila River drainage of Arizona, including springs, perennial and intermittent streams, and isolated refugial waters.
- **Population types recognized:**
  - **Natural populations** — historically self-sustaining, requiring more stringent survey criteria (5 surveys, ≥4 years span).
  - **Reintroduced populations** — actively established via recovery efforts, subject to slightly less stringent survey criteria (3 surveys, ≥2 years span), reflecting their higher vulnerability and shorter establishment history.
- **Seasonal detection window:** Surveys are most effective **June through November**, indicating peak activity/detectability likely tied to warmer water temperatures and reproductive/activity cycles typical of livebearing poeciliids.
- **Competitive interactions:** The explicit protocol for voucher collection of mosquitofish reflects the well-documented ecological threat of **competitive displacement and predation** by invasive *Gambusia affinis*, a major driver of [[Gila Topminnow]] decline.
- **Habitat resilience considerations:** Criterion #5 requires surveying up to **two miles up- and downstream** of a dry site, acknowledging the topminnow's reliance on **hydrologically connected refugia** and the potential for recolonization from persistent surface water in intermittent desert drainages.

## Key Findings

- **Extirpation Determination Protocol** — A population is declared extirpated only after meeting six specific criteria:
  1. Failure to collect the species in **≥5 consecutive surveys** (natural populations) or **≥3 consecutive surveys** (reintroduced populations), with minimum multi-year spans between first and last surveys (4 years natural / 2 years reintroduced).
  2. Surveys must be **thorough and comprehensive**, covering the majority of available habitat (not limited to small habitat segments).
  3. Surveys must occur during the **peak detectability season** (June–November).
  4. **Voucher specimens** must be collected and museum-verified when mosquitofish are present/suspected, to rule out species misidentification.
  5. If a site is found **completely dry**, surveyors must check **≥2 miles up/downstream** for surface water and sample any water found.
  6. **Written field records** of methodology and results must be maintained.

- **Post-Criteria Administrative Actions:**
  - (A) A formal report must be prepared documenting site history, sampling effort, personnel, methodology, data, probable causes of extirpation, and management recommendations.
  - (B) The report undergoes multi-agency review — USFWS, land management agencies, State conservation agency, and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]].
  - (C) Final extirpation declaration is jointly made by USFWS and the relevant State agency, with notification to stakeholders.

- **Policy Significance:** This interim framework was designed to bridge a gap while the full [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]] was finalized, allowing conservation resources to shift from monitoring likely-extinct populations toward establishing and sustaining **viable populations** — a stated priority of both USFWS and AGFD.

- **Data Limitations Acknowledged:** The criteria apply strictly to **new surveys**; historic survey data are often incomplete, and their adequacy for extirpation determination is assessed on a **case-by-case basis** during report review.
