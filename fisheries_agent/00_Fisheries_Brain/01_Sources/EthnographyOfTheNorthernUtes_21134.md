## Key Findings

- The [[Northern Utes]] maintained detailed taxonomic knowledge of local fish species, distinguishing them by morphology (mouth shape, coloration), size, and bone structure
- Fish terminology follows a consistent naming pattern with /pak/ root indicating fish, modified by descriptive prefixes
- The documented [[Sucker]] and [[Trout]] species indicate cold-water stream or river systems in Northern Utes territory
- Size measurements (1-2 feet) for common fish species suggest substantial aquatic food resources
- Indigenous classification system integrated practical knowledge (edibility, bone content) with morphological characteristics
- Cultural practices (War Dance) and directional terminology provide broader ethnographic context for understanding resource use patterns
