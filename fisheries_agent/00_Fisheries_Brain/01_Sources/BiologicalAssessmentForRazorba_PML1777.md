# Biological Assessment for Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub Habitat at the Phoenix Zoo, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cypriniformes

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes a [[Biological Assessment]] prepared by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Parker Fishery Resource Office) in June 1996 for a proposed long-term habitat for two federally endangered fishes, the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]], at the [[Phoenix Zoo]]. The project renovates an existing lake on zoo grounds. The assessment considers the lake's renovation, water supply, fish-escape safeguards, and annual monitoring. It concludes that the project may benefit listed species, including [[Peregrine Falcon]] habitat use. The accompanying cover letter (27 June 1996) concurred with the project and advised that discussion of fish stocking should wait until the lake is filled and stabilized.

## Background & Description

The project is designed to provide long-term habitat for the federally endangered bonytail chub and razorback sucker. The assessment states that the project may help prevent the extinction of these fish and could provide a source of fish for future reintroduction into historic habitats.

The letter was sent by C.O. Minckley, Ph.D., Project Coordinator, Parker Fishery Resource Office, to Jeff Williamson, Deputy Director of the Phoenix Zoo. It enclosed the Arizona Ecological Services Office concurrence letter and a revised biological assessment. The letter noted that the zoo's permit did not need modification until its renewal, if then.

Project responsibilities:
- **Renovation and maintenance:** the Phoenix Zoo.
- **Fish supply:** the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, through the Parker Fishery Resource Office, if approved.
- **Monitoring:** the Arizona Fishery Resource Office (Parker substation), in cooperation with [[Arizona Game and Fish]], [[Arizona State University]], and the Phoenix Zoo.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Project area**
- Located between the Tropical Flight and African Veldt exhibits on zoo grounds (Figure 1 referenced in the document).
- Approximately 5.0 acres, with a maximum depth of 15 feet (average 5–6 ft).
- Bottom is solid rock.
- Landscaped and maintained to reflect nearby exhibits and southwestern habitats.

**Renovation and water supply**
- As of 3 June 1996 the lake had been drained and was drying.
- After drying, several feet of bottom material will be removed, and the lake refilled.
- Water will come from the [[Salt River Project]] canal and enter through a filter of pea-sized gravel designed to exclude fish.
- After refilling, the lake will stand for about one month to allow [[Zooplankton]] to reestablish.

**Escape prevention**
- Outflow passes through a pipe covered with a fine screen, then drops over a considerable height. The drop is intended to prevent upstream invasion from downstream waters.
- Escape is considered unlikely. It would be more likely if reproduction occurred in the main lake, since smaller fish could pass more easily.
- Two downstream lakes contain introduced predators, which would likely eat any escapees. Any survivors would travel through pipes and a canal system to the Salt River Project pretreatment ponds, where the assessment states they would die.

**Management**
- Activities detrimental to the lake are prohibited.
- The lake will be patrolled as part of the ongoing zoo security program.
- An education program and signage will inform the public about the project.

**Listed species**
- Naturally occurring endangered species are very unlikely in the area.
- [[Peregrine Falcon]] may occasionally occur.
- Most American peregrine falcon breeding populations are in mountainous western U.S. and Canada. In Arizona, breeding pairs are distributed throughout suitable habitat statewide, except the low-elevation deserts of the southwestern quarter.

## Key Findings

- **No field surveys were conducted.** The assessment bases its species knowledge on conversations with Arizona Endangered Species Office personnel, the author's experience, the literature, and species experts, including Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) and Arizona Ecological Services.
- **Project purpose:** provide long-term habitat for two federally endangered fishes and potentially supply fish for reintroduction.
- **Beneficial effects:** the project may benefit listed species such as peregrine falcons by providing water, potential feeding sites, and resting sites.
- **Escape risk:** judged unlikely, with downstream predators and the pretreatment ponds as secondary safeguards.
- **Monitoring plan:** annual monitoring of fish health and reproduction, if reproduction occurs.
- **Timeline:** fish stocking and related fish discussions are deferred until the lake is filled and stabilized.
- **Permit status:** the zoo's existing permit does not appear to need modification before renewal.
