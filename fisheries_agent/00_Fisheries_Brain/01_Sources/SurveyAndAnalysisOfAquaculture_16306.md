# Survey and Analysis of Aquaculture Development Research Priorities and Capacities in Asia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquaculture

## Executive Summary

This FAO Fisheries Circular (No. 930, November 1997), prepared with the [[Network of Aquaculture Centres in Asia-Pacific]] (NACA), synthesizes a 1996 survey of 14 Asian countries/regions and the outcomes of a Regional Workshop held in Bangkok, 20-23 May 1997. The study finds that aquaculture research across the region is concentrated on biological and technical problems, while institutional, socio-economic, policy and legislative issues receive little attention. Institutional weakness, not only technical limitation, is identified as the main constraint on benefiting from research. The workshop endorsed seven themes for regional collaborative research, implemented mainly through networking and national commitment.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** To maximize the contribution of research to sustainable aquaculture development, promote regional collaboration, and focus donor attention on regional research needs. Follows a 1994 [[FAO Roundtable on Aquaculture Research]] under the [[Strategy for International Fisheries Research]] (SIFR).
- **Participants:** 14 countries/regions responded (Bangladesh, China, Hong Kong China, India, Indonesia, Iran, DPR Korea, Malaysia, Nepal, Pakistan, Philippines, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Viet Nam). Ten attended the workshop alongside regional organizations (AIT, AAHRI, SEAFDEC/AQD, ICLARM, Mekong River Commission, FAO/RAP) and one private firm.
- **Methodology:** A three-section questionnaire sent to 22 countries (March 1996); summaries returned by end of August 1996; nine country reports received by December 1996, four incomplete; regional synthesis prepared as a workshop discussion paper.
- **Limitations:** Incomplete or general responses, uneven response styles, and under-coverage of institutions outside fisheries agencies. The authors describe the results as indicative rather than a full description.
- **Context:** Asia produced 90% of world aquaculture output in 1995. Aquaculture production rose from 10.5 million mt (1984) to 27.8 million mt (1995). Nine of the eleven leading producers are in Asia. Asia's 1995 fishery trade was about US$24.7 billion in imports and US$18.3 billion in exports.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Culture systems:** Inland systems include ponds, pens, cages, tanks and culture-based fisheries. Coastal systems include ponds, cages, pens, and floating, hanging, long-line, pole, mudflat and bottom culture of molluscs and seaweeds. Integrated agriculture/livestock/fish systems are common in several countries.
- **Key species:** Carps, milkfish, catfish, tilapia and other low-value inland species for domestic and rural consumption. Prawns, shrimp, grouper, seabass, snapper, oysters and crabs for export.
- **Environmental pressures:** Pollution and environmental factors were reported as constraints by 71% of countries. Disease outbreaks (e.g. epizootic ulcerative syndrome, white spot) caused losses estimated at US$1.4 billion in 1990 and US$3.0 billion in 1994/95. Conflicts with other resource users and threats to land and water are noted.
- **Institutional setting:** Fisheries sits in a "grey area" between agriculture and fisheries, usually under ministries of agriculture.

## Key Findings

### Priority-setting and planning
- All countries have a central agency for aquaculture development and a national agency for research planning.
- Planning is consultative, but non-fisheries agencies (environment, industry) are rarely involved.
- Most common planning criteria: national development priorities, fish farmers' needs, and availability of funds, land, water and expertise.

### Research concentration
- Over 80% of 330 reported projects address technologies and systems, health and environment, seed and feed, genetic improvement, and species diversification.
- Fewer than 3% address socio-economic and management aspects.
- Donor-funded research (58 projects since 1990) shows a similar pattern: about 28% on fish health, 12% on culture systems, 12% on feed, and only one on socio-economics.

### Research capacity
- Most research personnel are in biological sciences (about 75%). Physical and engineering sciences account for about 6%, social sciences 3%, economics 2%, and policy and legal studies 0.3%.
- No country listed private-sector research organizations.
- The capacity for socio-economic, policy and postharvest research was not clearly indicated.

### Key constraints
- Common development constraints: ineffective technology transfer (93% of countries), weak institutional linkages (79%), lack of skilled technical personnel (71%), and pollution (71%).
- Common research constraints: ineffective information exchange (93%), weak interagency linkages (86%), insufficient skilled personnel (86%), inadequate facilities (86%), low research funding (71%).
- The study notes that the constraints are largely institutional, and that "little change" has occurred since the SIFR studies of the late 1980s.

### Workshop outcomes
- The workshop agreed that aquaculture development is as constrained by institutional issues as by biotechnical ones.
- [[Genetic Improvement]] was dropped from the regional list because the [[International Network on Genetics in Aquaculture]] (INGA) already covers it.
- Seven regional project concepts were endorsed, each with an indicative 3-5 year duration (Aquaculture Research for Rural Development: 5 years; Planning and Management: 3 years):
  1. Nutrition and Feeds for Aquaculture
  2. Seed Production and Broodstock Development for Aquaculture and Fisheries Enhancement
  3. Aquatic Animal Health Management
  4. Environmental Management of Aquaculture
  5. Planning and Management of Aquaculture Research and Development
  6. Aquaculture Research for Rural Development
  7. Information Dissemination

### Recommendations
- Governments should lead and fund cooperative research from national programmes. External assistance should act as a catalyst only.
- Regional actions should complement and not duplicate national efforts. Activities should not depend on external funding.
- Networking should use existing mechanisms ([[NACA]], INGA) and technical lead centres.
- Private-sector and non-government participation should be encouraged. The public sector should focus on policy and socio-economic research, and the private sector on market-led areas.
- Follow-up was to be raised at the FAO Asia-Pacific Fisheries Commission Aquaculture and Inland Fisheries Committee (November 1997) and the NACA Governing Council (December 1997).
