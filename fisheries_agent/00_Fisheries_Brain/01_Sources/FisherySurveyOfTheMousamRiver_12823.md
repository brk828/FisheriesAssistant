# Fishery Survey of the Mousam River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #Salmonidae #Centrarchidae #river-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1955 report by the Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Game (Fishery Research and Management Division) documents an intensive obstruction and water-quality survey of the [[Mousam River]] drainage, from [[Kennebunk, Maine|Kennebunk]] to [[Mousam Lake]] in Shapleigh. The river, historically valuable for sea-run [[Atlantic Salmon]] and [[Brook Trout]], has been rendered largely unsuitable for cold-water anadromous fisheries due to 13 major dams lacking fishways, elevated summer water temperatures, and sewage pollution from four municipalities (~25,000 people). The study concludes that most of the river (Kennebunk to Springvale) should be managed as warm-water fish habitat, while the pollution-free 4.5-mile stretch between [[Springvale Dam]] and [[Mousam Lake]] retains excellent spawning/nursery potential and warrants fishway construction at the [[Mousam Lake Dam]] to restore a limited salmon/trout fishery.

## Background & Description

This report is a habitat/fisheries management survey rather than a morphological study; no anatomical measurements or morphometric data are provided for individual species. Species were catalogued only by common name and presence/absence within surveyed reaches:

- **Cold-water species**: Brown Trout (also stocked historically), Brook Trout (native to [[Middle Branch Brook]]), sea-run Atlantic Salmon (historically present, targeted for restoration)
- **Warm-water species**: Smallmouth Bass, White Perch, Yellow Perch, Chain Pickerel, Hornpout (Brown Bullhead), Pumpkinseed Sunfish, Yellowbelly Sunfish
- **Other native fauna**: American Eel, White Sucker, Fallfish, Common Shiner

The presence of warm-water indicator aquatic plants and tea-colored, slow-moving water throughout much of the river corroborates the establishment of warm-water fish assemblages over cold-water species in the lower and middle river.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical habitat characteristics:**
- The reach from [[New Dam]] (Alfred) to Kennebunk is described as deep, slow-flowing, with marshy/flat terrain, mud-and-sand substrate, and dark, tea-colored water — unsuitable for salmonid spawning.
- Riffle areas consist of large boulders/rocks unsuitable for salmon/trout spawning.
- Good-to-excellent spawning and nursery habitat exists in two locations: short stretches between [[Jaeger's Mill Dam]] and New Dam, and the swift-water reach from [[Old Falls Dam]] to West Kennebunk (fair quality), and most notably the 4.5-mile pollution-free stretch between Springvale Dam and Mousam Lake.
- [[Middle Branch Brook]] is the only significant tributary in the system and is managed for Brook Trout.

Oxygen levels were generally adequate for fish survival throughout the river, approaching critical lows only near Old Falls Dam and Jaeger's Mill Dam. Temperatures were consistently high (74–78°F), favoring warm-water species over cold-water salmonids.

**Pollution sources:**
- Sewage pollution is heaviest in the 5 miles below Sanford; moderate near Kennebunk/West Kennebunk.
- From Springvale to New Dam Flowage (Alfred), pollution is not seriously affecting fish survival.
- Industrial waste (from one fibre company and two shoe companies) was deemed insignificant to fish life at the time; effects of a new plastic mill at Sanford remained undetermined.

**Migration barriers**: All 13 dams on the main river (plus the Alfred Dam at Shaker Pond) lack fishways and present major to complete obstructions to upstream fish migration at all water levels, with vertical spillway barriers ranging from 5 to 35 feet:

## Key Findings

- **13 dams with no fishways** block all sea-run salmon and trout migration on the main stem of the [[Mousam River]] from Kennebunk to Springvale.
- **Sea-run fishery restoration is not economically justified** for the majority of the river given high water temperatures, established warm-water fish communities, and the extensive dam infrastructure.
- The **4.5-mile reach between Springvale Dam and Mousam Lake** is pollution-free and contains the best remaining spawning/nursery habitat in the drainage, justifying targeted cold-water management.
- **Fishway construction at Mousam Lake Dam** (estimated $16,000) is recommended to allow salmon and trout to migrate between the lake and this productive spawning reach.
- Recommendation to **discontinue brown/brook trout stocking** and **liberalize warm-water fishing regulations** from Mousam Lake to Springvale.
- The river from **Springvale to Kennebunk should be managed exclusively as warm-water habitat**.
- [[Middle Branch Brook]], the system's sole significant tributary, should continue to be managed for native Brook Trout.
- Possible future need for a **fish screen at Springvale Dam** if salmon from the Mousam Lake fishery are found to be dropping downstream over the dam, risking escapement loss.
- Sewage pollution (from ~25,000 people across four municipalities) and industrial waste were assessed as **not currently lethal to established warm-water fish populations**, though future impact from a new plastics mill in Sanford required monitoring.
