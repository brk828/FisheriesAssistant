# Letter to Dr. W. L. Minckley Regarding Desert Fishes Recovery Team Membership

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #conservation-management

## Executive Summary
This 1990 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque, NM) to Dr. W. L. Minckley confirms his continued leadership of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] and documents personnel changes among team members. It also provides an updated roster of listed and candidate fish species under the team's conservation purview, reflecting recent taxonomic and regulatory adjustments (some species removed from oversight in the preceding two years). The letter references the Service's newest Recovery Planning Guidelines (Appendix II) as governing team operations and responsibilities.

## Background & Description
No morphological data are presented in this administrative correspondence. The letter is organizational/regulatory in nature and does not describe physical traits, meristics, or diagnostic features of the listed taxa. For morphological information, refer to species-specific accounts such as [[Meda Fulgida]] (spikedace), [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (loach minnow), [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] (desert pupfish), and [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]] (Gila topminnow).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The species listed inhabit desert aquatic ecosystems of the American Southwest and adjacent northern Mexico, including isolated springs, small streams, and larger river drainages characteristic of the [[Sonoran Desert]] and [[Gila River Basin]]. These habitats are typified by:
- Fragmented, often isolated water bodies vulnerable to desiccation and habitat loss
- High endemism and narrow geographic ranges (e.g., [[Gila Ditaenia]], [[Gila Purpurea]] restricted to specific tributary systems)
- Susceptibility to threats from water diversion, non-native species introduction, and habitat degradation — hallmark pressures managed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] recovery framework
- Cross-border conservation relevance, reflected by collaboration with Mexican institutions (e.g., [[Centro Ecologico De Sonora]])

## Key Findings
- **Team Leadership:** Dr. W. L. Minckley reaffirmed as leader of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]].
- **Personnel Changes:**
  - Dean Hendrickson (formerly Arizona Game and Fish Department) replaced by **Francisco Abarca**.
  - Gene Dahlem replaced by **Jeff Simms** (Bureau of Land Management zone fish biologist).
  - Continuing members: Lourdes Juarez-Romero ([[Centro Ecologico De Sonora]]), Jerome Stefferud (Forest Service), David Propst (New Mexico Department of Game and Fish), Tom Burke (Bureau of Reclamation).
- **Listed Species under Team Purview:**
  - [[Meda Fulgida]] (spikedace)
  - [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (loach minnow)
  - [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis Occidentalis]] (Gila topminnow)
  - [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis Sonoriensis]] (Yaqui topminnow)
  - [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] (desert pupfish)
  - [[Gila Ditaenia]] (Sonora chub)
  - [[Gila Purpurea]] (Yaqui chub)
  - [[Ictalurus Pricei]] (Yaqui catfish)
  - [[Cyprinella Formosa]] (Beautiful shiner)
- **Candidate Species:**
  - [[Gila Intermedia]] (Gila chub)
  - [[Gila Robusta]] (proposed for candidacy; roundtail chub)
  - [[Catostomus Latipinnis]] (proposed for candidacy; flannelmouth sucker)
  - [[Campostoma Ornatum]] (Arizona and adjacent Mexico populations only; Mexican stoneroller)
- **Regulatory Context:** Several species were removed from the Recovery Team's purview in the prior two years, indicating ongoing taxonomic revision and shifting conservation status assessments within the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Program]].
- **Administrative Guidance:** Recovery team roles and operational procedures are governed by the Service's Recovery Planning Guidelines, Appendix II, underscoring formalized structure in [[Endangered Species Recovery Planning]].
