# Proposed Low-Impact Fish Sampling Protocols for the Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a third-revision (November 5, 1991) proposal by [[Owen T. Gorman]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), Finetop Fishery Assistance Office, for low-impact fish sampling within the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Phase II research program in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR). Its main aim is to limit the capture, handling, and captivity of endangered [[Humpback Chub]] and other fishes, so that sampling does not harm the population or bias habitat-use data. It sets limits on net soak times, net set frequency, and minnow-trap grid use, lists exceptions for the spring spawning period, and makes recommendations on exotic species and baiting.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Proposal of sampling protocols, not a research report. It contains no new field data, sample sizes, or statistics.
- **Principal species of concern:** The [[Humpback Chub]], a Federally listed endangered species. The LCR population is described as numbering in the thousands of fish, but is considered very vulnerable because its resident population occupies roughly 14 km of river and is sampled intensively year-round.
- **Other fishes discussed:** Nonnative fishes, including [[Channel Catfish]], which have been present in the LCR for up to 100 years or more. Catfish are noted as predators of captured chubs.
- **Sampling gear:** [[Hoop Net]]s (hoopnets) and [[Minnow Trap]] grids.
- **Context:** Mainstream adults migrate up the LCR to spawn in spring, so intensive sampling of resident chubs may also affect mainstream fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stress and handling:** Excessive capture, handling, and captivity cause stress and may increase [[Predation By Catfish]] on captive chubs.
- **Diel habitat use:** Pairing net runs with habitat measurements can reveal [[Diel Activity Patterns]], but these data are useful only during the first 24 hours of a set. After that they become too biased for habitat-use assessment.
- **Behavioral effects:** Prolonged sets, or frequent sampling of the same locations, may alter the normal behavior, health, and ecology of resident chubs. Repeated negative encounters may also cause [[Learned Trap Avoidance]], lowering capture rates and biasing sampling.
- **Exotic species impact:** Ad hoc eradication of nonnative fish may disrupt research on how exotics affect Humpback Chub. Eradication efforts are unlikely to succeed, because exotics would repopulate the LCR from the [[Colorado River]] once research stops.
- **Bait effects:** Baited traps attract catfish, can make chubs associate food with traps, and draw fish from greater distances. These effects increase sampling bias.

## Key Findings

### Core Hoopnet Protocols
- Nets are set, soaked, and run (emptied) every **12 hours** for the first **24 hours**. Thereafter they may be set, soaked, and run every **24 hours** at the same location.
- Nets must not stay in the same locations for more than **three consecutive days**.
- Nets should not be run in the same locations more often than every **four weeks**.
- Nets set within **100 m** of one another are considered to be in the same location and sampling the same population.
- Captivity of fishes is limited to no more than **three days every four weeks**.

### Spring Exceptions (March to June)
- During spawning migration, when large numbers of chubs are expected, protocols may be relaxed.
- Nets may be set every **3 weeks** (instead of 4) at the same location, and may be left set for a maximum of **4 days**.
- To offset lower catch rates, researchers may set more nets at the same location, within 100 m.
- Nets may soak for 24 hours between runs at any time of year when USFWS personnel are not present to advise on net location or measure habitat.

### Minnow Trap Grid Protocols
- Sampling is done on contiguous grids of minnow traps, typically **8 to 30 traps** covering an area of **100 to 500 m²**.
- Each grid should not be sampled more often than **3 days per month**.
- Grids must be at least **10 m** apart.

### Recommendations on Exotic Species
- USFWS recommends that destruction of exotic fishes cease, except for those collected for scientific investigation.
- Permits should be required and take of exotics limited, so that removals can be tracked and research impact evaluated.
- Some adult exotics should be [[PIT Tagging]] to study their movement and distribution within the LCR.

### Position on Baiting
- USFWS does not recommend baiting hoopnets in the LCR. Animal-product bait attracts catfish, reduces chub captures, and is not an effective way to capture large numbers of chubs.

### Overall Intent
- The protocols aim to reduce repeat capture of chubs and avoid duplicate, unnecessary trauma. They also call for integrated, coordinated research among GCES researchers to avoid redundant sampling.
