# Distributional Checklist and Local Vernacular Names for Native Fishes of Mexico (Through Goodeidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #biogeography #Mexican-fauna #taxonomic-checklist

## Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive distributional checklist of native fishes of Mexico from [[Petromyzontidae]] through [[Goodeidae]], abstracted from Robert Rush Miller's manuscript "Guide to the Freshwater Fishes of Mexico" (September 1993). The checklist provides scientific names, local vernacular names (primarily Spanish), and detailed geographic distributions for hundreds of fish species across 26 families. Coverage includes both freshwater-obligate species and euryhaline taxa that enter rivers from coastal waters. The document emphasizes Pacific and Atlantic slope drainages, major river basins ([[Rio Bravo del Norte]], [[Rio Lerma]], [[Rio Panuco]], [[Rio Usumacinta]], [[Rio Balsas]], [[Rio Yaqui]]), and endemic species from isolated basins and springs. Multiple species are noted as extinct or extirpated from Mexican waters.

## Background & Description

The checklist systematically catalogs Mexican fish fauna organized by family, proceeding from primitive lampreys through advanced livebearing splitfins. Each entry follows a standardized format: scientific name with authority, Spanish vernacular name(s), English common name, and detailed distributional information including specific river basins, states, and geographic features.

**Taxonomic Coverage:**
- **Primitive fishes**: [[Petromyzontidae]] (lampreys), [[Carcharhinidae]] (bull sharks), [[Pristidae]] (sawfishes), [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons)
- **Ancient bony fishes**: [[Lepisosteidae]] (gars), [[Elopidae]] (tarpons, ladyfishes), [[Anguillidae]] (eels)
- **Herrings and anchovies**: [[Clupeidae]], [[Engraulidae]] - primarily coastal/estuarine with freshwater incursions
- **Catfishes**: [[Ictaluridae]] (North American catfishes), [[Ariidae]] (sea catfishes), [[Pimelodidae]] (South American catfishes)
- **Electric fishes**: [[Gymnotidae]] (knife fishes) in Pacific slope Chiapas
- **Characins**: [[Characidae]] including genera [[Astyanax]], [[Brycon]], [[Hyphessobrycon]]
- **Minnows and suckers**: [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]] - highly diverse with numerous endemic species
- **Trouts**: [[Salmonidae]] including [[Oncorhynchus]] species
- **Specialized families**: [[Bythitidae]] (cave brotulas), [[Batrachoididae]] (toadfishes), [[Gobiesocidae]] (clingfishes)
- **Needlefishes and halfbeaks**: [[Belonidae]], [[Hemiramphidae]]
- **Killifishes**: [[Rivulidae]], [[Cyprinodontidae]] - extensive coverage of endemic pupfishes
- **Livebearers**: [[Goodeidae]] - Mexican endemic splitfins with exceptional diversity

**Geographic Framework:**
The document distinguishes between Pacific slope (draining to Pacific Ocean), Atlantic slope (draining to Gulf of Mexico/Caribbean), and endorheic (closed) basins. Major river systems include [[Rio Colorado]], [[Rio Yaqui]], [[Rio Mayo]], [[Rio Fuerte]], [[Rio Sinaloa]], [[Rio Lerma]]-[[Rio Grande de Santiago]], [[Rio Balsas]], [[Rio Bravo del Norte]] (Rio Grande), [[Rio Conchos]], [[Rio Panuco]], [[Rio Papaloapan]], [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]], [[Rio Grijalva]], and [[Rio Usumacinta]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Diversity:**
Mexican freshwater habitats encompass extraordinary ecological variety:
- **Highland lakes**: [[Lago de Chapala]], [[Lago de Patzcuaro]], [[Lago Cuitzeo]], [[Laguna Chichancanab]]
- **Spring systems**: [[La Media Luna]], [[Cuatro Cienegas]], thermal and cold springs throughout
- **Cave systems**: Subterranean waters of [[Yucatan Peninsula]], [[Rio Sabinas]] system, [[Papaloapan]] basin caves
- **Coastal lagoons**: [[Laguna Madre]], [[Laguna de Terminos]], [[Laguna de Tamiahua]]
- **Desert springs**: [[Bolson de los Muertos]], endorheic basins of Chihuahua and Coahuila
- **Mountain streams**: Headwater tributaries in Sierra Madre Occidental and Oriental

**Biogeographic Patterns:**
The fauna reflects complex biogeographic history:
- **Northern temperate elements**: Cyprinidae and Catostomidae dominate northern Mexico, extending from United States fauna
- **Neotropical elements**: Characidae, Pimelodidae, Gymnotidae represent South/Central American affinities
- **Endemic radiations**: [[Goodeidae]] (splitfins) endemic to central Mexican highlands; [[Cyprinodontidae]] with numerous endemic pupfish species in isolated springs
- **Marine-derived fauna**: [[Carcharhinus leucas]] (bull shark), [[Pristis]] species (sawfishes), [[Arius]] species penetrate far inland in tropical rivers

**Conservation Status:**
Multiple extinctions and extirpations documented:
- **Extinct species**: [[Evarra bustamantei]], [[Evarra eigenmanni]], [[Evarra tlahuacensis]] (Valley of Mexico endemics), [[Stypodon signifer]] (Valle de Parras), [[Cyprinodon latifasciatus]] (Parras)
- **Extirpated from Mexico**: [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail), [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado squawfish), [[Xyrauchen texanus]] (razorback sucker) from [[Rio Colorado]]; [[Rhinichthys cobitis]] and [[R. osculus]] from [[Rio Gila]]
- **Cave-adapted species**: [[Priatella phreatophila]] (Muzquiz blindcat), [[Rhamdia reddelli]] (Papaloapan blindcat), [[Ogilbia pearsei]] (blind brotula) represent specialized troglobitic fauna

**Introduced Species:**
[[Ameiurus melas]] (black bullhead) widely introduced beyond native [[Rio Bravo del Norte]] range into rios [[Casas Grandes]], [[Yaqui]], [[Panuco]], [[Lerma]]-[[Santiago]], and [[Balsas]]. [[Ictalurus furcatus]] introduced in [[Rio Yaqui]]. [[Pylodictis olivaris]] introduced in [[Rio Colorado]] and possibly [[Rio Yaqui]]. [[Oncorhynchus mykiss]] (rainbow trout) widely introduced beyond native northern Baja California range.

## Key Findings

**Family-Level Diversity:**
The checklist documents 26 families through [[Goodeidae]], representing extraordinary taxonomic breadth from primitive jawless lampreys to advanced teleosts. [[Cyprinidae]] shows highest generic diversity with genera including [[Gila]], [[Notropis]], [[Algansea]], [[Dionda]], [[Codoma]], and others. [[Goodeidae]] demonstrates remarkable species-level diversity as Mexican endemic radiation with 18 genera documented.

**Endemic Species Concentrations:**
- **[[Cuatro Cienegas]] basin, Coahuila**: Multiple endemics including [[Cyprinodon atrorus]], [[C. bifasciatus]], [[Lucania interioris]], [[Notropis xanthicara]], [[Megupsilon aporus]]
- **[[Laguna Chichancanab]], Yucatan**: Five endemic [[Cyprinodon]] species ([[C. beltrani]], [[C. labiosus]], [[C. maya]], [[C. simus]], [[C. verecundus]])
- **[[Rio Lerma]]-[[Rio Grande de Santiago]] basin**: Center of [[Goodeidae]] diversity with numerous endemic splitfins
- **[[Rio Panuco]] basin**: Multiple endemic [[Dionda]] species, [[Ictalurus mexicanus]], [[Ictiobus labiosus]]

**Distributional Patterns:**
- **Pacific vs. Atlantic slope differentiation**: Many genera show distinct species on each slope (e.g., [[Atractosteus tropicus]] Atlantic, [[Dorosoma smithi]] Pacific)
- **Trans-isthmian distributions**: Some species occur on both slopes in southern Mexico/Central America (e.g., [[Astyanax aeneus]])
- **Rio Bravo del Norte as faunal boundary**: Northern limit for many Neotropical elements, southern limit for temperate North American fauna
- **Endorheic basin endemism**: Closed basins harbor unique faunas (Valley of Mexico, [[Rio Nazas]]-[[Aguanaval]], [[Laguna Bustillos]])

**Taxonomic Uncertainties:**
Document notes several unresolved taxonomic issues:
- [[Ictalurus]] sp. resembling [[I. dugesi]] from [[Balsas]] tributaries in Morelos
- [[Oncorhynchus]] sp. from rios [[Yaqui]], [[Mayo]], and [[Casas Grandes]] of uncertain taxonomic status
- [[Gymnotus]] sp. in Pacific slope Chiapas
- [[Catostomus plebeius]] populations in rios [[Fuerte]], [[Mezquital]], and [[Piaxtla]] likely represent undescribed taxon

**Geographic Coverage:**
States represented include: Aguascalientes, Baja California, Baja California Sur, Campeche, Chiapas, Chihuahua, Coahuila, Colima, Durango, Guanajuato, Guerrero, Hidalgo, Jalisco, Mexico, Michoacan, Morelos, Nayarit, Nuevo Leon, Oaxaca, Puebla, Queretaro, Quintana Roo, San Luis Potosi, Sinaloa, Sonora, Tabasco, Tamaulipas, Veracruz, Yucatan, and Zacatecas. International distributions extend into United States (Texas, New Mexico, Arizona, California), Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama, Colombia, Peru, and Brazil for wide-ranging species.

**Euryhaline Species:**
Multiple marine/estuarine species regularly enter freshwater:
- [[Carcharhinus leucas]] (bull shark) in major tropical rivers
- [[Pristis]] species (sawfishes) in river mouths and coastal lagoons
- [[Arius]] species (sea catfishes) with some reproducing in both fresh and salt water
- [[Tarpon atlanticus]] entering tropical rivers
- [[Anguilla rostrata]] catadromous, ascending rivers to mature

**Vernacular Name Patterns:**
Spanish common names reflect ecological or morphological characteristics: "bagre" (catfish), "charalito" (small minnow), "cachorrito" (small pupfish), "sardinita" (small sardine), "matalote" (sucker), "Mexcalpique" (splitfin). Many names reference localities (e.g., "del Lerma," "Yucateco," "Potosina") or distinctive features ("cabeza de toro" = bullhead, "aleta larga" = longfin).
