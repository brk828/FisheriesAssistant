# In Defense Of Desert Fishes

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This is a review by W. L. Montgomery of [[Inland Fishes Of The Greater Southwest]] (2009), the final work of [[W. L. Minckley]] (deceased), completed by [[Paul C. Marsh]] (University of Arizona Press, 426 pp., $75.00). The reviewer calls the volume a fitting tribute to decades of work on southwestern desert fishes, which grew from early field collections into ecology, genetics and conservation research. He finds it an outstanding reference but criticizes its scientific rigor, taxonomic accuracy and field usability, and recommends a condensed field edition. The final page of the document is an unrelated liability release form for a field activity.

## Background & Description

- **Book structure:** Four chapters: Introduction (5 pp.), The Region and Its Aquatic Habitats (42 pp.), Conservation of a Vanishing Biota (5 pp.) and Fishes of the Region (species accounts, 309 pp.). Back matter includes a family, genus and species list with English and Spanish common names, unit conversions, a collecting and preserving methods summary, a 13-page glossary, and a Literature Cited section (73 pp., well over 2,000 references).
- **Historical framing:** The Foreword is by [[Jim Deacon]]. The Preface includes comments by Minckley written in the month of his death, and Marsh's account of recent declines and non-native introductions.
- **Early pioneers named:** Carl Hubbs, Bob Miller, Jim Deacon and Phil Pister, who moved southwestern desert fishes from the margins of commercial and sport fisheries research into wider recognition.
- **Predecessor:** Minckley's original [[Fishes Of Arizona]] (1973), a compact field tool that the reviewer holds up as the model for a future edition.
- **Non-scientific item:** The last page is a release and waiver of liability form from Marsh & Associates LLC (Chandler, AZ) for participants in "hazardous activities," with a parent/guardian signature clause for minors. It is administrative and has no bearing on the fish content.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Regional changes:** The Introduction covers roughly 150 years of change, including a table of 23 dams, diversions, reservoirs and other modifications on the [[Colorado River]] system built between 1908 and 1992.
- **Geology and climate:** Chapter 2 covers topography, drainage relations, climate, and geological evolution over about 75 million years, with a six-panel map. Climate varies at very local scales because of complex topography.
- **Water and habitat issues:** The reviewer asks for more attention to the size and frequent drying of many streams, and for conversion of reservoir evaporation from acre-feet into more intuitive units with surface area reported alongside. A [[Groundwater Withdrawals]] figure (1915–1990, from a 1994 paper) lacks post-1990 data, and its apparent sharp drop after about 1982 is not explained.
- **Mexico:** Concern about damage to springs and waters in [[Mexico]] is noted, but the reviewer says recent conservation law and regional efforts there are not given a balanced treatment.
- **Threats:** Chapter 3 lists habitat alteration, predation and competition as the usual causes of native fish decline, but the reviewer says these are often asserted without references or rigorous testing.

## Key Findings

- **Strengths:** The species accounts (taxonomic characters, native range, introduction history, basic biology and references) work well as an introductory ichthyology course for the families covered. The text is described as an "astounding resource" for southwestern fishes, and its contact list of researchers in the Acknowledgments is valued for building collaborations.
- **Scientific rigor:** The reviewer argues that the lack of rigorous tests of proposed threats cannot support strong management action. He also faults the tone, which he says can leave readers with a sense of despair and a belief that agencies and laws cannot change.
- **Identification keys:** Some keys rely on characters that are impractical in the field, such as [[Weberian Ossicles]], which require sacrificing the specimen. Other problems include a wrong figure reference (Fig. 4.122 should be 4.112), a missing figure of bony scutes, unclear skull-bone characters, and a sex-dependent couplet. The reviewer recommends a field-use key from experienced collectors.
- **Nomenclature errors:** These include the flathead catfish authority (the parenthetical Rafinesque attribution is omitted), incorrect authorships for *Pantosteus plebeius* and *Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*, an unexplained retention of *Pantosteus intermedius* (synonymized with *Catostomus clarki*), a misspelled *Oreochromis mossambicus* in the text, and a mis-rendered Mexican name for catfish ("obagre" as "barge"). Users are directed to [[Common And Scientific Names Of Fishes]] (Nelson et al. 2004) and FishBase.
- **Name change:** The reviewer notes that "squawfish" persists despite its replacement by "pikeminnow" in recent literature and by the American Fisheries Society Committee on Names of Fishes, which made the change because of cultural insensitivity.
- **Format:** The large, glossy format limits field use, and the reviewer believes the book would work better as a smaller, condensed edition with geography, collecting methods, field keys and short species accounts.
