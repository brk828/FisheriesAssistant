# Effectiveness of Rotenone-Containing Preparations in Eradicating Farm Pond Fish Populations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources bulletin (Federal Aid to Fisheries, Project F-10-R, final report, 1959) by William R. Turner compares four [[Rotenone]]-containing fish toxicants for complete eradication of unwanted fish in 56 farm ponds treated between May 1956 and November 1958. Treatment at 1.0 ppm succeeded far more often in summer (June to mid-September) than in fall. The study concludes that season, driven by [[Summer Stagnation]] in deep water, matters more than product choice, and recommends restricting treatment of ponds deeper than 7 feet to summer months.

## Background & Description

- **Context:** [[Rotenone]] use for pond reclamation began with powdered derris root in Michigan in 1934. It was introduced to Kentucky in 1947, when a Hopkinsville pond was treated with powdered cubé (5% rotenone). Treatments rose from 95 ponds in 1949 to an average of 171 ponds per year after 1950.
- **Rationale:** In 1956 the department sought to identify which products best achieved total eradication under different seasons. The work was done with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] under Project F-10-R.
- **Products tested:** [[Pro-Noxfish]] (2.5% rotenone plus 2.5% sulfoxide synergist), [[Chem-Fish Regular]] (standard 5% emulsive), [[Chem-Fish Special]] (5% emulsive designed for cold, deep water), and powdered cubé (5% rotenone).
- **Methods:** Surface area and depth were measured, and alkalinity, dissolved oxygen, pH and temperature profiles were recorded in 49 ponds. Rotenone was applied at 10 parts water to 1 part product from an outboard-powered boat. Vertical penetration was tested with aerated live [[Bluntnose Minnow]] bioassays (toxic if death within 2 hours). Follow-up checks came 1 to 6 months later and included shoreline observation, seining, spot-poisoning, and re-treatment at 2.0 ppm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal stratification:** Present in 17 summer-tested ponds. The hypolimnion was absent in all of them, and in 5 the thermocline ran from surface to bottom. The epilimnion was usually confined to the upper 3 feet.
- **Water chemistry:** Summer total alkalinity ranged from 12 to 103 ppm with pH 6.8 to 8.6. Fall alkalinity ranged from 14 to 124 ppm with pH 7.1 to 9.0.
- **Oxygen:** Summer stagnation occurred in all but one pond, leaving the lower waters without dissolved oxygen. Fall circulation produced high dissolved oxygen at depth, which gave fish a refuge below the toxic zone.
- **Penetration:** Rotenone was lethal to test fish to a maximum of 8 feet in summer and 7 feet in fall, regardless of stratification. Outboard agitation was used in all these ponds. The comparison with [[Clemens and Martin (1953)]], who reported 6 feet lethal penetration with agitation and 5 feet without, is noted.

## Key Findings

- **Season is the main driver of success:** Summer treatments (24 ponds) achieved 97% complete kills. Fall treatments (32 ponds) achieved 72%. Across all 56 ponds the overall killing efficiency was 89%.
- **Product results:**
  - Powdered cubé: 5 of 5 summer ponds fully cleared (100%).
  - [[Pro-Noxfish]]: 11 of 12 summer ponds (91%) and 12 of 18 fall ponds (67%), giving 79% overall (23 of 30).
  - [[Chem-Fish Regular]]: 7 of 7 summer ponds (100%).
  - [[Chem-Fish Special]]: 11 of 14 fall ponds (78%). Heavy rainfall runoff in two treatments is the likely cause of failure.
- **Penetration is similar across seasons:** Fall penetration did not differ significantly from summer penetration, but treatment success was much higher in summer.
- **Depth recommendation:** Because the maximum effective depth was 7 feet, the authors recommend treating Kentucky farm ponds deeper than 7 feet only from June to mid-September.
- **Species responses (of 28 species recorded, 56 ponds):**
  - [[Yellow Bullhead]] was the most difficult, eliminated from 69% of its 16 ponds.
  - [[Goldfish]] was eliminated from 75% of its ponds.
  - Green sunfish were eliminated from 86% of 29 ponds, and black bullheads from 89%.
  - [[Bluegill]] occurred in more ponds than any other species. It was removed from 42 of 45 ponds (93%).
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] and white crappie were eliminated from all 33 and all 15 ponds where they occurred.
- **Limitations:** Checking relied on shoreline observation, seining and spot-poisoning. The author notes these methods were not infallible; draining ponds would be more reliable but impractical.
