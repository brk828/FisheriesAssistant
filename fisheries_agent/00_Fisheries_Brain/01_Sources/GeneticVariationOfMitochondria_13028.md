# Genetic Variation of Mitochondrial Aspartate Aminotransferase in *Cyprinodon nevadensis*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 study by Bruce J. Turner, University of California, Los Angeles, reports an apparent genetic polymorphism in the mitochondrial form of [[Aspartate Aminotransferase]] (AAT-2) in a population of the [[Nevada Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis*, subspecies *C. n. amargosae*) from the [[Amargosa River]] in [[Death Valley]], California. Using [[Electrophoresis]] of muscle extracts, the author identified three AAT-2 phenotypes. Their frequencies agree with a [[Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]] expectation for a one-locus, di-allelic polymorphism with allele frequencies of 0.8 and 0.2.

## Background & Description

- **Enzyme:** Aspartate aminotransferase (AAT; E.C. 2.6.1.1, also called glutamate-oxaloacetate transaminase, GOT) is a pyridoxal phosphate-linked enzyme. It links carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism by catalysing the reaction aspartate + α-ketoglutarate ⇌ glutamate + oxaloacetate.
- **Forms:** Vertebrate cells contain two AAT forms. The cytoplasmic (supernatant) enzyme, here AAT-1, is found in the cytosol. The mitochondrial enzyme, here AAT-2, is restricted to mitochondrial fractions. The two are distinct enzymes that can be separated electrophoretically.
- **Prior work:** Genetic polymorphisms had been reported for mitochondrial AAT in laboratory mice and for supernatant AAT in herring (*Clupea harengus*). Electrophoretic variants of both enzymes had been found in humans.
- **Study organism:** The Nevada pupfish is a small cyprinodontid fish of the Death Valley region. Its population in the Amargosa River was sampled for this work.
- **Funding:** Partly supported by a U.S. Public Health Service Predoctoral Fellowship (GM 41609).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper concerns molecular genetic variation within a single fish population rather than habitat or ecosystem dynamics. The sampled population lives in the Amargosa River, Death Valley, California, a setting that is inland and not marine. The document gives no data on water chemistry, community composition, or environmental conditions. Its ecological relevance lies in the use of a naturally occurring enzyme polymorphism as a population-genetic marker in a wild desert fish.

## Key Findings

**Phenotypes and their identification**

- **AAT-1** (supernatant enzyme) showed no detectable variation.
- **AAT-2** (mitochondrial enzyme) showed three phenotypes in muscle extracts, which were identical across all tissues of a given specimen:
  - **AAT-2A** (most common): a single band just anodal to the origin. Interpreted as the homozygote *aat-2a/aat-2a*.
  - **AAT-2B** (least common): a single band 25–30 mm anodal to the origin. Interpreted as the homozygote *aat-2b/aat-2b*.
  - **AAT-2AB**: light bands at both the AAT-2A and AAT-2B positions plus a dark middle "hybrid" band. Interpreted as the heterozygote *aat-2a/aat-2b*.
- The AAT-2AB pattern fits the random combination of two different monomers into a dimer, consistent with the known dimeric structure of mammalian mitochondrial AAT.

**Frequency data (*C. n. amargosae*, Table 1)**

| Phenotype | Presumptive genotype | Observed | Expected | χ² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AAT-2A | *aat-2a/aat-2a* | 24 | 23.3 | 0.021 |
| AAT-2AB | *aat-2a/aat-2b* | 11 | 12.1 | 0.036 |
| AAT-2B | *aat-2b/aat-2b* | 2 | 1.6 | 0.057 |
| **Total** | | **37** | | |

- Expected values come from a Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium with p(*aat-2a*) = 0.8 and q(*aat-2b*) = 0.2.
- The AAT-2AB and AAT-2B classes were pooled so that all expected values exceeded 5 (d.f. = 1).
- The author reports that the observed distribution agrees statistically with Hardy-Weinberg proportions. The probability value is given in the source as between 0.8 and 0.75 (OCR garbled; read as 0.8 > P > 0.75).

**Methodological limitation**

- On gels of 15% acrylamide or higher, every AAT-2 phenotype resolves into five or six very faint "subbands." This makes the phenotypes extremely difficult to tell apart, so the lower-concentration gels were needed for scoring.

**Conclusion**

- The paper reports a one-locus, di-allelic codominant polymorphism of mitochondrial AAT-2 in the Amargosa River population of *C. nevadensis amargosae*. It is consistent with Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. Work on species-specific and conformational forms of AAT-1 was deferred to a later publication.
