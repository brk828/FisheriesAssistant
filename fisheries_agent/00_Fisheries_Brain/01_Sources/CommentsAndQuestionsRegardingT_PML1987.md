# Lower Colorado River Big River Fishes Management Prospectus: Comments from the Central Arizona Water Conservation District

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a March 30, 1995 fax transmittal and cover letter from the [[Central Arizona Water Conservation District]] (CAWCD) to Conrad Fjetland, Regional Director of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service), Region 2, in Albuquerque. It submits formal comments and questions on the February 1995 preliminary draft of the Lower Colorado River Basin [[Big River Fishes Management Prospectus]]. The draft addresses four endangered "big river" fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Humpback Chub]].

CAWCD's central position is that the draft's habitat restoration and reintroduction proposals could significantly affect water users, landowners, and existing projects across Arizona. It asks the Service to answer key questions before stakeholders can be expected to agree to the draft's principles. It also objects to proposed changes in the regulatory status of reintroduced Colorado Squawfish populations, and it requests a stakeholder meeting in central Arizona.

## Background & Description

The four species are large-bodied fishes native to the Colorado River system and are the focus of the Service's recovery planning. The draft calls for:

- Restoration of natural habitats and recovery of historic habitats.
- Restoration of "free flowing river reaches" in the Salt, Verde, Gila, and Bill Williams rivers and the mainstem Colorado River.
- Reintroduction or reestablishment of the four fishes in designated critical habitat and on other public lands.

The letter cites the draft's management prospectus as the subject of review. It does not provide population counts, survey data, or other biological measurements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter's concerns center on how river restoration and species reintroduction would interact with existing water infrastructure and use:

- **Free-flowing reaches:** CAWCD asks whether restoration would require dismantling existing dams and diversion structures, or require specific water releases from existing dams and reservoirs. It also asks whether the United States holds rights to any water to be released.
- **Water releases and studies:** The draft calls for studies of how water releases affect the four fishes. CAWCD says comparable studies conducted for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] were "inordinately expensive" and that any similar expenditure for dams on the lower Colorado River would be unacceptable.
- **Reintroductions outside critical habitat:** CAWCD argues that stocking endangered fish outside designated critical habitat could significantly affect existing projects and uses. It asks the Service to determine the economic impact of introducing a threatened or endangered species before introduction into new areas.
- **Regulatory safeguards:** The letter urges that reintroduced populations outside critical habitat be designated "experimental, nonessential," and that no existing project be deemed to jeopardize a subsequently introduced endangered fish.

The letter also addresses the regulatory framework under the [[Endangered Species Act]], citing prior Service rulemaking on the experimental population designation.

## Key Findings

**1. Requests for clarification on restoration**
CAWCD asks the Service to answer its questions on free-flowing restoration before stakeholders can be expected to endorse the draft's principles and objectives.

**2. Objection to expanded Colorado Squawfish critical habitat and changed designation**
The draft proposes changing the status of Colorado Squawfish reintroduced to the Salt and Verde rivers from "experimental, nonessential" to "experimental, essential," and suggests expanding critical habitat to include portions of these rivers. CAWCD calls this "directly contrary" to the Service's earlier representations.

**3. Prior Service position on nonessential status**
CAWCD cites the Service's 1985 rejection of a proposal to designate these reintroduced populations as "experimental, essential." The Service then stated that:
- The species' survival did not depend on the reintroduced populations.
- Loss of those populations would not further jeopardize the species.
- The species remained viable in several areas of the upper basin.
- The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] had sufficient broodstock to produce many fish.
- Propagation and rearing techniques had been developed and were in use.

The Service also stated that a change from nonessential to essential status would be considered only if the loss of all or a significant portion of the wild populations occurred. CAWCD says it knows of no such loss that would justify a change. It adds that even if a basis existed, the change would require formal rulemaking.

**4. Regional implications**
Although the draft addresses only the lower Colorado River, CAWCD argues it has significant implications for the entire Lower Basin, including much of central Arizona. Its proposed actions for the Salt, Gila, and Verde rivers could affect water users and landowners statewide.

**5. Request for a central Arizona stakeholder meeting**
CAWCD requests that a stakeholder meeting be held in central Arizona, in addition to the meetings planned for [[Yuma, Arizona]], [[Lake Havasu City]], and [[Boulder City, Nevada]].
