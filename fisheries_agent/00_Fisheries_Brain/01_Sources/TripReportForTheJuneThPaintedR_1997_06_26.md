## Executive Summary
This document is a field trip report from Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies, detailing a fish collecting survey conducted on 24 June 1997 at [[Painted Rock Dam]], Maricopa County, Arizona. A joint team from ASU and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) sampled the waters immediately downstream and upstream of the dam using trammel nets and a seine. The primary target/most abundant species was [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*), which was notably abundant and observed exhibiting surface-leaping behavior. Additional species collected included [[Common Carp]], juvenile [[Tilapia]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Western Mosquitofish]], [[Sailfin Molly]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. Observations of substrate depressions ("nests") resembling those seen at [[Alamo Lake]] suggest possible Tilapia spawning activity, though this was not confirmed with adult specimens.

## Background & Description
- **Striped Mullet (*Mugil cephalus*)**: Preserved specimens ranged from **440 mm to 565 mm total length**, indicating collection of subadult to adult individuals.
- **Nest-like depressions**: Approximately **0.75 m wide and 0.5 m deep**, circular substrate structures consistent with cichlid (Tilapia) spawning nests.
- No detailed morphometric data were recorded for carp, tilapia, red shiner, mosquitofish, sailfin molly, or channel catfish in this report; specimens were preserved or released without measurement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Sampling occurred in the tailwater habitat immediately downstream of [[Painted Rock Dam]] on the Gila River, Maricopa County, AZ, in shallow water (~1 m depth) near a boat launch ramp, as well as in the impounded area immediately upstream.
- **Striped Mullet**: Found abundant in shallow (~1 m) water near shoreline structure; exhibited leaping behavior at the surface, a known behavior in this euryhaline species even in freshwater/inland reservoir systems.
- **Tilapia sp.**: Juveniles were collected from an area containing nest-like depressions, suggesting a nearshore breeding habitat; nest structures were morphologically similar to those documented at [[Alamo Lake]], La Paz County, AZ (observed the following day, 25 June 1997), suggesting a regional pattern of Tilapia reproductive site selection in Arizona reservoirs.
- **Community composition**: The upstream seine sample yielded a mixed assemblage including native/naturalized cyprinids ([[Red Shiner]]), poeciliids ([[Western Mosquitofish]], [[Sailfin Molly]]), ictalurids ([[Channel Catfish]]), and cyprinids ([[Common Carp]]) — reflecting a typical altered/impounded Sonoran Desert riverine fish community with a mix of native and non-native/invasive species.
- **Non-native species presence**: Common Carp, Tilapia, Sailfin Molly, and Western Mosquitofish are all non-native introduced species in Arizona waters, indicating substantial anthropogenic alteration of the native fish community at this site.

## Key Findings
- [[Striped Mullet]] were highly abundant downstream of Painted Rock Dam, with individuals observed leaping from the water — an unusual and notable occurrence for this typically estuarine/marine-associated species this far inland.
- Six potential Tilapia nest structures were documented at the site, morphologically similar to nests found at Alamo Lake, though direct confirmation of nest-building by adult Tilapia was not achieved (no adults were captured).
- The fish assemblage collected reflects a mix of both native (e.g., Red Shiner) and multiple non-native/invasive species (Carp, Tilapia, Sailfin Molly, Mosquitofish), highlighting the ecological impact of introduced species in this reservoir system.
- Voucher specimens of Striped Mullet (preserved in 10% formalin) were designated for deposit in the **Arizona State University collection of fishes**, supporting future taxonomic and biogeographic reference work.
- Channel Catfish and Carp captured via seine were released rather than preserved, suggesting these species were not the focal taxa of this particular survey effort.
