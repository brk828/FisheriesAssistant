# Desert Fishes Council Thirteenth Annual Symposium Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #conservation #symposium

## Executive Summary

This document is the program for the Thirteenth Annual Symposium of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], held November 19-21, 1981, at [[Death Valley National Monument]] Headquarters in Furnace Creek, California. The symposium brought together researchers, agency representatives, and conservation professionals to discuss the status, ecology, and management of desert fish species across the southwestern United States and Mexico. The program included presentations on endangered species status, research findings, recovery efforts, and agency reports, with special focus on [[Colorado River]] fishes and threatened desert aquatic habitats.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Council]] is an organization dedicated to the preservation of America's desert fishes. The 1981 symposium was chaired by Phil Pister ([[California Department of Fish and Game]]) and featured presentations organized into eight sessions over three days. The program opened with a special presentation by Jim Williams from the Office of Endangered Species, Washington, D.C., on the current status of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

Key taxonomic groups discussed included:
- [[Catostomus warnerensis]] (Warner sucker)
- [[Xyrauchen texanus]] (razorback sucker)
- [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub)
- [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado squawfish)
- [[Gila bicolor mohavensis]] (Mojave chub)
- [[Cyprinodon macularius]] (desert pupfish)
- [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]] (Sonoran topminnow)
- [[Gambusia affinis]] (mosquitofish)
- [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (longfin dace)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The symposium addressed desert aquatic ecosystems across multiple geographic regions, organized by area coordinators:
- [[Bonneville Basin]]
- [[Death Valley]]
- [[Oregon Lakes]]
- [[Lahontan Basin]]
- [[Sonoran Desert]] (Mexico and United States)
- [[Chihuahuan Desert]] (Mexico, New Mexico, and Texas)
- Upper and Lower [[Colorado River]] basins

Specific habitats discussed included:
- Desert spring systems of southern Nevada
- [[Virgin River]] ichthyofauna
- [[Devils Hole]]
- [[Amargosa River]] canyon and gorge
- [[Ash Meadows]]
- [[Bolsón de los Muertos]], Chihuahua, México
- [[Green River]] and [[Yampa River]], Colorado
- [[Salton Sea]] region, Imperial County, California

Environmental threats addressed included introduced species (particularly [[Tilapia zillii]] replacing native [[Cyprinodon macularius]]), mining operations in Death Valley regions, flooding impacts on native-exotic species interactions, and habitat degradation.

## Key Findings

**Species Status and Recovery:**
- Multiple presentations focused on the declining status of [[Xyrauchen texanus]] (razorback sucker) in the lower [[Colorado River]] basin, including life history aspects explaining their decline, spawning behavior, substrate selection, and reintroduction efforts
- [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub) observations in the [[Grand Canyon]] region were summarized
- Lordosis (spinal deformity) documented in [[Gila]] species from [[Yampa River]], Colorado
- [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado squawfish) spawning migration patterns and rearing experiments were reported

**Research Findings:**
- Redescription of [[Catostomus warnerensis]] presented by Carl E. Bond, Oregon State University
- Metabolic cost of aggressive behavior quantified in territorial pupfish
- Niche segregation documented in [[Virgin River]] fish fauna
- Preliminary morphometric analysis revealed geographic variation in [[Agosia chrysogaster]]
- Population dynamics and interactions between [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]] and [[Gambusia affinis]] affected by flooding in Arizona springs
- Endemic fauna documented in desert spring habitats of [[Bolsón de los Muertos]], Chihuahua, México
- [[Tilapia zillii]] documented replacing [[Cyprinodon macularius]] in irrigation drain near [[Salton Sea]]

**Management and Conservation:**
- Cattle watering tanks evaluated as potential pupfish habitats
- Challenges of protecting Nevada's aquatic habitats discussed
- Biological significance of proposed mining operations in Death Valley assessed
- Special discussion on the [[Ash Meadows]] dilemma and potential special fund for political action

**Organizational Matters:**
- Symposium included reports from area coordinators and recovery team leaders across all major desert basins
- Agency reports from Bureau of Land Management, National Park Service, Bureau of Reclamation, Forest Service, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (multiple offices), Soil Conservation Service, state agencies (Nevada, Colorado, California), and [[The Nature Conservancy]]
- International participation included representatives from Mexico and United Kingdom
- Manuscript submission deadline of December 15, 1981 established for proceedings publication
- Field trips scheduled to [[Ash Meadows]], [[Amargosa River]] Gorge, and Cottonball Marsh
