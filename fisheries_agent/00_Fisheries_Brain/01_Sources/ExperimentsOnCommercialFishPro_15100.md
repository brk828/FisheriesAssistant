# Experiments on commercial fish production in ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-aquaculture #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study, conducted by [[H. S. Swingle]] and the fisheries research staff at the Alabama Polytechnic Institute Agricultural Experiment Station (Auburn, Alabama), reports on multi-year (1948–1954) pond experiments testing the commercial viability of eight freshwater fish species: [[Round Flier]], [[Bluegill]], [[Speckled Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Bigmouth Buffalo]], [[Smallmouth Buffalo]], and [[Flathead Catfish]]. The work compares production under unfertilized, inorganically fertilized, organically fertilized, and supplementally fed pond conditions, with cost-per-pound analysis to assess economic feasibility. Findings indicate that [[Speckled Bullhead]] and both buffalo species ([[Bigmouth Buffalo]] and [[Smallmouth Buffalo]]) showed the greatest commercial promise, while [[Round Flier]] and [[Bluegill]] were deemed commercially unviable. [[Common Carp]] faced market limitations despite biological feasibility, and [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Flathead Catfish]] required further research due to reproductive and yield challenges.

## Background & Description

- **[[Speckled Bullhead]]**: Reproduces readily in ponds; small bones limit desirability at small sizes; salable at 0.5–1 lb sizes.
- **[[Common Carp]]**: Contains numerous small bones limiting restaurant trade use; must reach >2 lb (preferably 5 lb) for salability; flavor compares favorably to other species when well-fed.
- **[[Bigmouth Buffalo]] / [[Smallmouth Buffalo]]**: Also possess small bones in flesh, requiring growth to 2–5+ lb for market acceptability; bigmouth grows more rapidly than smallmouth.
- **[[Flathead Catfish]]**: Noted for "excellent flavor"; grows at a rate of ~2 lb/year in ponds.
- **[[Bluegill]]**: Lacks carbohydrate-splitting digestive enzymes (per Lawrence 1950 thesis), limiting feed conversion efficiency.
- Size classes tracked across experiments (fry, fingerling, harvestable) were central to production and cost calculations for most species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pond culture context**: All species were tested in a controlled freshwater pond environment under varying fertilization (inorganic 8-8-2/8-8-4 NPK formulations, organic cottonseed meal-superphosphate) and supplemental feeding (soybean meal/cake, peanut meal, fish meal) regimes.
- **Reproductive behavior**:
  - [[Speckled Bullhead]] spawns readily in ponds, creating a reproduction-control challenge (54–72% of biomass was small juvenile fish in uncontrolled ponds).
  - [[Bigmouth Buffalo]] and [[Smallmouth Buffalo]] are riverine spawners requiring flood-stage conditions; found to excrete a **repressive factor** inhibiting reproduction when held long-term in the same water, discovered by [[Swingle 1953]] (Repressive Factor Controlling Reproduction in Fishes).
  - [[Common Carp]] spawn readily when introduced into fresh water at 65–70°F; eggs laid on vegetation/pond substrate, hatching in 3–5 days.
  - [[Flathead Catfish]] spawn only in large rivers; all attempts at pond spawning, running-water spawning, and hormone induction failed.
- **Water temperature thresholds**: [[Speckled Bullhead]] ceased feed utilization below 60°F, informing feeding schedule (stop early-mid November, resume March in Auburn, AL climate).
- **Trophic interactions**: [[Common Carp]] "muddy" ponds, suppressing phytoplankton and reducing productivity under inorganic fertilization alone; organic cottonseed meal fertilization mitigated this via mud precipitation. Largemouth bass ([[Micropterus salmoides]]) were stocked alongside bullheads and buffalo as biological control agents against unwanted reproduction/wild fish, though bass often suffered high mortality from low oxygen during heavy feeding regimes.

## Key Findings

### Round Flier
- Very low production: **32 lbs/acre**, **89% mortality** in a 3-month feeding experiment.
- No commercial potential.

### Bluegill
- Bluegill-bass combination with fertilization alone: ~200 lbs/acre/year, cost $0.10–0.15/lb.
- Higher stocking (2,000 bluegill + 200 bass/acre) yielded 650.6 lbs/acre total at $0.47/lb; 1,500+100/acre yielded 400.3 lbs/acre at $1.05/lb.
- Maximum growth trial: 370 lbs/acre recovered, cost ~$0.98/lb — economically unviable.
- No commercial potential due to poor feed conversion efficiency.

### Speckled Bullhead
| Treatment | Production (lbs/acre) |
|---|---|
| Unfertilized | 49.4 |
| Inorganic fertilization | 191.2–284.6 |
| Fertilization + feeding | 1,474.8–1,725.1 |

- Reproduction control critical; three stocking/draining methods tested (bass co-stocking, timed fertilization/feeding, fry-to-drain-before-spawning cycle).
- Best commercial protocol: stock 2,000 fry/acre in May, fertilize monthly (8-8-2) May–Sept, feed soybean/peanut meal + fish meal mix Aug–April (>60°F only), drain in May before spawning.
- Projected yield: **~600 lbs/acre** of 0.5–1 lb fish at **~15 cents/lb**.
- Feed utilization inefficient below 60°F; optimal feeding rate 2–3% body weight/day.

### Channel Catfish
| Treatment | Production (lbs/acre) |
|---|---|
| Fertilization (8-8-2) | 71.4–92.3 |
| Fertilization + feeding | 230.4–246.8 |

- Insufficient data to confirm commercial viability; feed utilization poor Dec–March (16% mortality, no net weight gain).
- Requires further stocking-density research.

### Common Carp
| Treatment | Production (lbs/acre) |
|---|---|
| Fertilization (8-8-4) | 280 |
| Organic fertilization (cottonseed meal + superphosphate 3:1) | 435.0 |
| Fertilization + feeding | 700.0–1,733.0 |

- Full 2-year cycle needed to reach 5 lb market size; 1 year to reach 2 lb from fingerling.
- Best economical protocol: ~400 lbs/acre of 2 lb fish at 7–10 cents/lb; higher feeding rate (10 lbs/acre/day) yields 700 lbs/acre at ~15 cents/lb.
- Limited U.S. market value despite production feasibility.

### Bigmouth & Smallmouth Buffalo
| Treatment | Production (lbs/acre) |
|---|---|
| Fertilization (8-8-4) | 611–666 |
| Fertilization + feeding | 853.0–1,163.0 |

- Six 1-year experiments comparing feeding vs. fertilization-only:
  - Feeding trials: cost 39–51 cents/lb (high input cost despite high yield, up to 1,122 lbs/acre).
  - Fertilization-only trials: cost only **5.1–6.8 cents/lb**, yielding 513.6–632.8 lbs/acre — most economically favorable.
- Survival rates: 96.2% (feeding trials), 99.7% (fertilization-only).
- Buffalo outperformed carp under inorganic fertilization alone (586 lbs/acre avg vs. 280 lbs/acre for carp).
- Discovery of a **repressive reproductive factor** excreted by buffalo fish, requiring fresh (buffalo-free) water sources to induce spawning at 60–70°F.
- Fry yields: 30,000–292,000 fry/acre from 50 brood fish/acre.
- Recommended commercial protocol: stock 250 fingerlings/acre (Oct), fertilize Oct–Feb–Oct cycle with 8-8-2, add 100 largemouth bass fry/acre (May) for wild fish control, drain in fall/winter.
- Projected yield: **500–600 lbs/acre** of 2 lb fish at **6–8 cents/lb**.
- Bigmouth buffalo preferred over smallmouth due to easier induced spawning and faster growth.

### Flathead Catfish
- Growth rate: ~2 lbs/year in ponds; excellent flavor.
- All spawning induction attempts (ponds, running water, hormone injection) failed — described in [[Johnson 1950]] thesis.
- Commercial potential contingent on solving fry production, currently unresolved.

### Cross-Species Economic Comparison
- Most cost-effective commercial candidates: **[[Speckled Bullhead]]** (~15 cents/lb) and **[[Bigmouth Buffalo]]** (6–8 cents/lb via fertilization alone).
- Heavy supplemental feeding consistently increased gross yield but disproportionately increased cost per pound across all species, indicating fertilization-only or moderate feeding regimes are more economically sustainable for pond aquaculture.
