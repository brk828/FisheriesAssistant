## Executive Summary

This report details a chemical control program at the Page Springs Rearing Station in Arizona, addressing a severe infestation of the parasitic copepod *[[Lernaea Cyprinacea]]* (or *L. carassii*) affecting [[Rainbow Trout]] populations from 1957–1959. The study evaluates the efficacy of **Benzine Hexachloride (Lindane)** as a copepocide compared to impractical formalin treatments. Researchers documented the parasite's life cycle, preferred attachment sites on host tissue, and the resulting secondary bacterial infections (*[[Chondrococcus Columnaris]]*) that drove high mortality rates. Ultimately, a low-concentration continuous drip application (1:10,000,000) proved effective for live fish, while a high-concentration spray (5 ppm) successfully sterilized empty rearing units, eradicating the parasite for at least one year post-treatment.

## Background & Description

The [[Page Springs Rearing Station]] experienced a crippling parasitic outbreak beginning in May 1957. The causative agent was identified as *[[Lernaea Cyprinacea]]*, a copepod parasite attaching to the gills, fins, and body surfaces of [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*).

**Vector Hypothesis:** Researchers theorize the parasite was introduced via contaminated transport tanks previously used for infected [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) sourced from Arizona impoundments where the copepod was parasitic on centrarchids. Failure to sterilize these tanks likely facilitated cross-contamination.

**Morphology:**
- Parasite size ranged from 7.5 mm to 22.5 mm (including egg sacs).
- Color varied from light green to dark brown.
- The anterior end features four cephalothoracic horns embedded firmly in host flesh.
- Mature females possess a pair of long egg sacs; eggs in external sacs appear light green, while those in the ovary appear black.

**Life History (per Wilson, 1918):**
1. Young hatch from egg sacs as free-swimming individuals.
2. The host is attacked late during the six copepodid stages.
3. Metamorphosis leads to maturity and mating.
4. Males die shortly after mating.
5. Females remain attached, develop eggs, hatch them, and subsequently degenerate and die.

*Note: This study only observed maturing females attached to hosts; despite extensive plankton hauls and gill examinations, no free-swimming copepodids or males were isolated.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The infestation dynamics were closely tied to seasonal water temperature fluctuations and pond hydrology.

- **Seasonal Development:** Egg development in females correlated directly with rising water temperatures from March to June. By June, 100% of parasites present contained eggs (in ovaries, egg sacs, or both), compared to only 37% in March.
- **Detection Issues:** In November 1958, trout appeared parasite-free, but this was later attributed to the parasites being fully embedded in tissue during early developmental stages, making them undetectable without microscopic examination.
- **Host Preference:** Lernaeids preferred yearling trout (average length 9.8 inches); no trout smaller than 4.8 inches were found infected.
- **Secondary Infection & Water Quality:** The copepod attachment sites caused mechanical injury and hemorrhaging, creating entry points for the bacterium *[[Chondrococcus Columnaris]]* (Bacterial Gill Disease). This was exacerbated by:
    - Rising water temperatures (63°F to 70°F), ideal for *C. columnaris* proliferation (thrives >65°F).
    - Poor pond turnover (~8 days for full exchange), leading to a buildup of metabolic waste and pollutants that stressed respiratory function.
- **Non-Target Toxicity:** During pond sterilization, high concentrations of Lindane proved lethal to non-target organisms, including aquatic insects, plankton, chironomid worms, and bullfrogs (causing muscular spasms and loss of equilibrium prior to death).

## Key Findings

### Parasite Attachment & Density
- **Preferred Locations:** Copepods favored protected areas over exposed flat surfaces (see [[Lernaea Cyprinacea]] attachment data).
  - Gill Arch: 24.8%
  - Opercle & Preopercle (junction): 18.3%
  - Behind Dorsal Fin: 14.6%
  - Behind Pectoral Fin: 14.6%
- **Density Trends:** The Parasite Frequency Index (parasites per fish) increased dramatically over the observation period:
  - March: 1.4
  - April: 2.2
  - May: 8.5
  - June: 9.5

### Chemical Control: Formalin (Rejected)
- Applied at 1:4000 concentration for 1 hour.
- Effective against copepods but deemed economically impractical for 31 rearing units and hazardous to already weakened trout.

### Chemical Control: Lindane (Benzine Hexachloride) on Live Fish
- **Toxicity Test:** A static application of 1:10,000,000 held for 1 hour resulted in 7% trout mortality and feed refusal.
- **Successful Protocol:** Applying Lindane via continuous drip (1:10,000,000) into flowing water resulted in insignificant mortality and continued feeding.
- **Mixing Agent:** Required an emulsifier (Colloidal 77) at a ratio of 5 oz activator to 1 oz Lindane to keep the chemical suspended, as Lindane settles quickly in water alone.
- **Application Duration:** Treatments lasted 72–96 hours, with a 3-day rest period between treatments to prevent chemical buildup.
- **Efficacy Data (Table IV):** Mortality rate of attached parasites increased slowly initially but spiked by June:
  - March: 33.7% dead
  - April: 34.7% dead
  - May: 36.7% dead
  - June: 55.3% dead
- **Behavioral Toxicity Signs (Overdose):** Erratic swimming, skimming the surface, and ramming into banks/obstacles.

### Chemical Control: Pond Sterilization (Post-Evacuation)
- **Agent:** Lindane 2L (liquid form, 23.50% gamma isomers of BHC + 29.00% aromatic petroleum derivatives).
- **Concentration:** Effective sterilization achieved at **5 ppm**.
- **Protocol:** Ponds drained, sprayed on all wetted surfaces/mud, then refilled to 1 ft depth and left for 3 weeks.
- **Result:** All aquatic insects and plankton died within 1 hour of exposure. Following a full sterilization of raceways, tanks, and equipment, and a 2-month drying period, ponds were restocked.
- **Long-Term Success:** No signs or symptoms of copepod parasites were detected in restocked trout populations after **one full year**.
