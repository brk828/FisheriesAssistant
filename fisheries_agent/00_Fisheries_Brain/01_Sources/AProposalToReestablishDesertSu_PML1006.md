# Native Fishes Re-establishment Proposal: Arnett Creek and Telegraph Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This August 15, 1996 proposal, prepared by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] Region V fisheries staff, seeks to re-establish native [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Desert Sucker]] in [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]], tributaries of the [[Gila River]] basin in central Arizona. The project removes two exotic, predatory species, [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Mosquitofish]], and depends on a fish barrier to block their upstream movement from [[Queen Creek]] during high flows. The Region V program recommends continuing the project as outlined, following the decision in the barrier and renovation environmental assessment (EA). The cover letter asks for comments on or before October 1, 1996.

## Background & Description

- **Project context:** Arnett Creek is a small, 2nd-order stream west-southwest of Superior. Historic water use for mining and other activities dried much of it, but improved conditions over the past decade have produced perennial flow along several miles of channel. The creek supports a diverse riparian area but offers no public recreational fishing.
- **Origin:** In 1992, the Department and the [[Tonto National Forest]] (Globe District) identified an opportunity to re-establish a native fish community.
- **Historic status:** Longfin dace and desert suckers were once numerous and widespread across the Gila River watershed, in a riparian system shaped by beaver activity. Management then focused on introducing nonnative fishes, and native fishes have since been largely extirpated.
- **Current status:** Both species are described as numerous and widespread (longfin dace) or common and widespread (desert sucker). Neither is federally listed or under consideration for listing. Historic records for [[Gila Chub]] also exist at the Arnett Creek and Queen Creek confluence. The creek is now dominated by green sunfish and mosquitofish, but the Tonto National Forest habitat inventory found adequate values to support the native species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat condition:** Intermediate-elevation streams are physically variable and carry heavy sediment loads from eroding mountains. Most remain chemically within healthy limits for fish. Habitat loss and the decline of perennial reaches have been the principal problems.
- **Threats:** Exotic [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Mosquitofish]] prey on native fishes. The proposal therefore pairs removal of these species with a barrier against upstream recolonization from Queen Creek during high flows.
- **Riparian management:** A riparian pasture fencing action was completed before the proposed schedule.
- **Environmental review:** The [[Barrier Construction and Renovation Environmental Assessment]] was led by the Tonto National Forest, completed, and finalized with a decision. It involved broad public input from agencies, sportsmen, conservationists, and land users, and all conflicts were reported as resolved.

## Key Findings

**Methodology**

- **Source population:** Juvenile and adult longfin dace and desert suckers will be collected from the Gila River between Coolidge Dam and the Florence Diversion Dam.
- **Capture:** A four-person Region V crew will use seines and dip nets. Fish will be screened on site for disease and parasites by a [[Fish Branch]] pathologist. Any fish carrying prohibitive pathogens will be held at [[Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery]] for quarantine and treatment.
- **Transport:** Fish will travel in a Regional fish tank using standard techniques (oxygenation, temperature monitoring, aeration).
- **Release:** Fish will be temperature-acclimated and distributed into Arnett Creek. The Telegraph Canyon site requires a three-person crew to hike upstream carrying fish in backpacks.
- **Monitoring:** For three years after stocking, fish will be surveyed biannually with seines and dip nets. Data will cover species identification, age/size classes, relative abundance, catch-per-unit effort, length of perennial water, and water quality and flow. Depletion estimates will be made if needed.
- **Duration and budget:** Annual surveys for 10 years, with periodic restocking as needed. Costs are estimated at $500 to $1,000 per year within the Regional [[Federal Aid]] fish program base.

**Coordination**

- Region V and the Globe District of the Tonto National Forest jointly created and supported the project from 1992. Together they prepared two Heritage grant applications, one for the inventory phase and one for barrier construction.

**Management Decision and Recommendation**

- The EA decision is to build the barrier at the preferred site and stock longfin dace and desert suckers.
- The Region V fisheries program recommends continuing the project as outlined in this document and the EA.
