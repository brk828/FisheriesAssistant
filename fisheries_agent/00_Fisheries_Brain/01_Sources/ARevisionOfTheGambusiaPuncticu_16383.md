# A Revision of the Gambusia puncticulata Complex (Pisces: Poeciliidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 taxonomic revision by William L. Fink, published in the *Publications of the Gulf Coast Research Laboratory Museum*, reassesses the [[Gambusia Puncticulata]] species group of [[Mosquitofish]]. Earlier workers treated its members as separate species. Fink argues they form one widespread, highly variable species made up of six subspecies. Five are redescribed here, and the sixth, [[Gambusia Puncticulata Monticola]], was described recently by Rivas (1971). Subspecies are defined mainly by gonopodial and meristic characters, because proportional and colour characters proved unreliable. Several nominal species, including *G. howelli*, *G. caymanensis*, *G. oligosticta* and *G. hubbsi*, are synonymized with the nominotypical [[Gambusia Puncticulata Puncticulata]].

## Background & Description

The group was defined by Rivas (1963) as nine nominal species, to which Rivas (1971) added *G. p. monticola*. The complex is distributed through the [[Bahamas]], the Greater Antilles (except Puerto Rico and Hispaniola), and Mexico from Yucatan south to [[Lake Petén]] in Guatemala.

Fink's methods relied on:
- **Gonopodial characters**, following Hubbs (1926), Hubbs and Springer (1957) and Rivas (1963). Suspensorium nomenclature follows Rosen and Bailey (1963). Gonopodia are the most stable characters in the group.
- **Meristic counts**, following Miller (1948), including vertebrae, fin rays, lateral scales and gill rakers, taken from radiographs or cleared and stained material.
- **Colour** from alcoholic specimens, which were judged generally unreliable.

Diagnostic features of the genus-level group include a [[Gonopodium]] about one-third of standard length, with ray 3 spines of 7–13 (usually 10–12), a terminal hook on ray 4p, and two or three [[Gonapophyses]] of which the second always bears an uncinatoid process. Anal rays number 11, scales around the caudal peduncle 16, and hypural caudal elements 8–10 (usually 9). Overall, the body is brown with a dark nape and scale pockets margined with melanophores, forming a reticulate pattern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and feeding.** *G. p. puncticulata* occupies fresh, brackish and salt water, but collections suggest a preference for brackish habitats. It is usually a surface feeder that preys on small invertebrates and fishes, including its own young. [[Krumholz 1963]] carried out a comprehensive ecological study on [[Bimini]].

**Complementary distribution.** In Cuba, *G. p. puncticulata* is complementary with *G. punctata*. Where *G. punctata* is most abundant (fresh water), *G. p. puncticulata* is scarce, and the situation reverses in saltier waters. In Jamaica it is complementary with species of the *G. nicaraguensis* group.

**Isolation and divergence.** Fink proposes that *G. p. puncticulata* is close to the ancestral stock. Isolation produced most other members. Isolated forms show either loss of a gonapophysis, as in the Cayman Island populations, or increased lateral scales and vertebrae, as in all isolated Cuban subspecies. The complex is probably derived from a Central American ancestor. Variation is strongest in the Bahamas and Cuba.

**Specialised habitats.**
- [[Gambusia Puncticulata Manni]] lives in two land-locked brackish lakes on New Providence.
- [[Gambusia Puncticulata Yucatana]] inhabits lakes and ponds ranging from fresh to salt water, from near Coatzacoalcos (Veracruz) to Yucatan, Lake Petén and northern British Honduras.
- [[Gambusia Puncticulata Monticola]] is the only member known from a mountain-stream habitat: a cool, clear stream with rapids at 1250 ft.

## Key Findings

**1. Taxonomic outcome.** The nine original names were reduced to a single species with six subspecies:

| Subspecies | Status in revision | Key distinguishing features | Type locality / range |
|---|---|---|---|
| *G. p. puncticulata* | Redescribed; nominotypical | Lateral scales 29–31, vertebrae 30–32 (usually 31); spots not large | Havana, Cuba (moats of old city wall); Cuba, Bahamas, Cayman Islands, Jamaica |
| *G. p. yucatana* | Redescribed | Female anal fin strongly falcate; two or three rows of spots on sides | Progresso, Yucatan; Mexico to northern Guatemala |
| *G. p. manni* | Redescribed | Pale goldish brown, unspotted; 2 gonapophyses usually | Lake Cunningham, New Providence, Bahamas |
| *G. p. bucheri* | Redescribed | Distinct black spots in rows above lateral line; higher lateral scale and vertebral counts | Rio Jicotea, Moa system, Oriente, Cuba |
| *G. p. baracoana* | Redescribed | Fewer branched caudal rays (usually 12); more vertebrae and lateral scales | Backwater near mouth of Rio Baracoa, Cuba |
| *G. p. monticola* | Not redescribed (Rivas 1971) | Dorsal rays 9–10, pectoral rays 15, lateral scales 31–32 | Rio Yao, Oriente, Cuba |

**2. Synonymy.** *G. howelli*, *G. caymanensis*, *G. oligosticta* and *G. hubbsi* are treated as junior synonyms of *G. p. puncticulata* because of wide overlap in characters. *G. nigropunctata* and *G. melanosticta* are also synonymized, and *G. picturata* is regarded as probably based on a melanistic specimen. The Haitian record of *G. oligosticta* (Myers 1935) is considered erroneous, possibly from mislabelled Henderson collection lots.

**3. Nomenclature.** Poey chose no holotype for *G. puncticulata*, so Fink designated a male lectotype of 24.3 mm SL (MCZ 46568). A male lectotype of 37.6 mm (BMNH 1914-3-23:12) was designated for *G. p. yucatana*. The holotype of *G. p. manni* is UMMZ 72183, and its type locality is restricted to Lake Cunningham.

**4. Justification for subspecific rank.** Fink retained subspecies because the differences among them are smaller than differences between other *Gambusia* species, yet the divergence is consistent in key characters (for example, the falcate anal fin of *yucatana* females and the large spots of *bucheri*). He also notes that gonopodia are almost identical across forms, so only slight but inconsistent meristic differences separate them.

**5. Meristic summary (*G. p. puncticulata*).** Vertebrae 31 (30–32); dorsal rays 9 (8–9); branched caudal rays 14 (12–16); pectoral rays 14 (12–16); lateral scales 30 (29–31); gill rakers 13 (12–17). Males range 13.3–34.8 mm SL and females 17.0–54.7 mm SL.

**6. Sample basis.** Gonopodial suspensoria were examined in 86 adult males of *G. p. puncticulata*, 20 of *yucatana*, 43 of *manni*, 20 of *bucheri* and 20 of *baracoana*. Frequency tables (Tables 1–6) summarise dorsal and caudal rays (Bahamas n = 806 and Cuba n = 512 for dorsal rays), pectoral rays, lateral scales, ray 3 spines, serrae, gill rakers, vertebrae and gonapophyses across populations. Population-level variation was high, particularly in the Bahamas.

**7. Gonapophysis variation.** In *manni*, two gonapophyses are typical, with an uncinatoid process on the second in all specimens and on the first in 23% of those examined. In *bucheri*, three gonapophyses occurred in 55% of 20 males. In *baracoana*, three were present in all 20.

**8. Distribution.** The complex spans the Bahamas, Greater Antilles (except Puerto Rico and Hispaniola), and the Mexican and Central American mainland. Material examined covers dozens of localities, summarised in Fink's appendix, including USNM and BMNH lots from Cuba, Jamaica, the Bahamas, the Cayman Islands and Yucatan.

**9. Conclusions.** Fink's key conclusions are:
- *Gambusia puncticulata* is a single widespread, highly variable species.
- Subspecies are defined by gonopodial and meristic characters, with colour and proportional characters deemed unreliable.
- Isolation in the Cuban uplands, the Bahamas and on the Yucatan Peninsula has generated subspecies differing mainly in lateral scales, vertebrae and fin-ray counts.
