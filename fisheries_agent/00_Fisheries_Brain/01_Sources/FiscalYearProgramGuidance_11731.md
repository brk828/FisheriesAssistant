# FY 1997 Recovery Implementation Program Guidance for Endangered Colorado River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region 6 (Denver) document, dated March 11, 1996, is the program guidance for the FY 1997 Work Plan of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. It is a request for proposals: it sets out which ongoing, ongoing-revised, and new projects should be funded, what each scope of work must contain, and the review timeline. The guidance is tied to the Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP) and organized by recovery element: instream flows, habitat restoration, nonnative fish control, propagation and genetics, research and monitoring, information and education, and program management.

The four target species are the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. Most of the work centers on flow management from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], protecting the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]], restoring floodplains, removing nonnative fishes, and building fish passage and propagation capacity.

## Background & Description

The guidance states that the RIPRAP identifies "all the activities currently believed necessary and feasible" to recover the endangered fishes. Projects are classed as:

- **Ongoing:** approved for out-year funding, with goals, methods, cost, and expected outcome largely unchanged.
- **Ongoing-revised:** approved for out-year funding, but with significant changes to goals, objectives, methods, cost, or expected outcome.
- **New:** not previously approved; full scopes of work must be developed.

Principal investigators for new projects are chosen on six criteria: past experience, availability, past performance, relationship to other work, State and Federal staff availability, and funding and contract limits.

Scopes of work must follow the format in Appendix B, which was revised from previous years. The appendices include a public involvement plan template (Appendix A), a scope of work format (Appendix B), and evaluation forms for scopes of work (Appendix C) and final reports (Appendix D).

The fish-related content describes species biology in relation to flow and habitat:

- Colorado Squawfish spawn on the descending limb of the hydrograph. Their young depend on sandbar backwater habitats that emerge at baseflow.
- Razorback Suckers spawn before peak flows, in response to rising spring flows from the Little Snake and Yampa rivers, at temperatures near 14 °C. Their recruitment is tied to tributary peak sequencing and floodplain inundation.
- Humpback Chub are referenced in habitat use along the Little Snake River and in Westwater flow studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The guidance identifies several pressures on the fishes:

- **Flow alteration:** Flaming Gorge Dam operations, depletions, and reduced spring peaks have altered sediment transport and channel form. Peak flows through Lodore Canyon were limited to 4,500 cfs after dam closure (except in 1983, 1984, and 1986), yet Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Suckers reoccupied the canyon in 1994 and 1995.
- **Sediment and channel change:** Scour could raise the flow needed to inundate floodplains, working against floodplain connectivity. Salt cedar invasion and channel encroachment were noted in the lower Green River.
- **Nonnative fishes:** Centrarchids, common carp, and channel catfish are of concern, especially in low-velocity nursery habitats. The guidance states that eliminating established nonnative fishes is unlikely, so the goal is to reduce their impacts.
- **Barriers and water development:** Diversions and structures such as the Redlands, Price-Stubb, Tusher Wash, Taylor Draw, Grand Valley, and Hartland sites require fish passage. Proposed Elkhead Reservoir enlargement and possible base flow augmentation on the Yampa raise further concerns.
- **Contaminants:** Floodplain contaminants are being studied for their impacts on razorbacks.

## Key Findings

**Schedule and process**

- Biological project scopes (recovery elements I–V, including capital projects) were due April 26, 1996. Information and education scopes were due the same day. Program management scopes were due July 1, 1996.
- The Program Director's office planned to send the recommended work plan to technical committees on June 21, with comments due July 19. The Management Committee would meet in early August; the Draft Work Plan was due to the Implementation Committee by August 23; the final Work Plan was due October 1, and final scopes October 15.
- Scopes for seven new projects were flagged for peer review: Deerlodge Gage, Yampa River Flow Refinement/Monitoring, Green River Spring Peak Flow Verification, Carp Removal from the Middle Green River, Green River and Colorado River Nonnative Fish Pilot Studies, and Program Information Transfer via Internet.

**Instream flows**

- Priorities were final flow recommendations and legal protection, completing White River flow work, and implementing the Yampa River Monitoring and Flow Refinement Plan.
- The Deerlodge Gage and sediment project totals **$149,660**. This covers installation and O&M for the Deerlodge, Yampa above Little Snake, and Lodore gages, plus sediment measurements at Little Snake ($15,000), Yampa ($21,000), and Jensen ($35,000), and report preparation ($16,000). Work would begin October 1, 1996, with the analysis report due in FY 1998.
- The Yampa monitoring program was estimated at about **$150,000/year** ($50,000 each for geomorphology, habitat, and fish surveys), for FY97–FY2003.
- Green River spring peak flow work focuses on a spring peak of about **24,000–25,000 cfs** at Jensen. This level is expected to occur roughly **1 in 7–10 years** and would produce overbank flow. Four spring flows observed during the research period ranged from 12,000 cfs and did not exceed **20,200 cfs**. Flows at Jensen were expected to exceed 20,200 cfs about **1 in 3 years** over the period of record, and about **1 in 5 years** since the dam closed. The research team also requested a Lodore Canyon bypass flow above 6,000 cfs.
- The Implementation Committee split the peak flow work into flood assessment (aerial photography), long-term monitoring, and Lodore Canyon study when bypass tubes are used.
- The Colorado Program Work requests included CAP-9 (Yampa O&M Plan): the capital spreadsheet showed **$3.71M**, but the guidance expects considerably less to be needed in FY97. The Coordinated Reservoir Operations line item is **$200.0K**.

**Habitat restoration and fish passage**

- Floodplain protection (item 75) is budgeted at **$100.0K**. Floodplain restoration (CAP-6) is **$2.45M**, including land acquisition ($650K Green, $250K Gunnison, $250K Colorado) and levee removal.
- Fish passage line items include Redlands ($95.0K), Price-Stubb ($125.0K), Tusher Wash ($100.0K), GVIC ($550.0K), and Hartland ($100.0K). Taylor Draw has no stated budget.
- A flows analysis gave a 20,500 cfs level as necessary to inundate floodplain areas near Ouray.

**Nonnative fish control**

- Gunnison nonnative removal (item 58) is budgeted at **$7.0K** for data analysis and reporting.
- The Green River nonnative management scope must target centrarchid sources through stocking discontinuation, reservoir escapement control, pond reclamation, or mechanical removal.
- The Middle Green River carp removal study would use Old Charlie Wash on Ouray National Wildlife Refuge. The study would compare draining ponds immediately with late-summer draining in a second test year. Endangered fishes would be PIT-tagged and released. Cost is unknown.
- Pilot studies for the Green River (led by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources) and the Colorado River (led by the Colorado Division of Wildlife) are each scheduled FY97–FY99, with basinwide recommendations due in FY99.

**Propagation and genetics**

- The Gila genetics study (mtDNA restriction endonuclease analysis) is budgeted at **$41.0K**.
- Operation and maintenance of propagation facilities totals **$437.0K**. Of this, **$250.0K** comes from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service budget, leaving **$187.0K** to be drawn from Recovery Program funds.
- CAP-7 facility expansion totals **$1.55M**. The Ouray facility plans 10 new 0.5-acre ponds, with razorback suckers moved to rearing ponds at about 100 mm TL.
- Chemoreception and imprinting research (item 31) is budgeted at **$40.0K**, with a report due December 1997.

**Research, monitoring, and data management**

- The Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program is budgeted at **$132.4K**. The guidance requires adding basinwide razorback monitoring, which the Implementation Committee agreed would be funded from FY97 annual operating funds.
- Tag conversion moves PIT-tags and readers from 400 kHz to 125 kHz because the manufacturer will discontinue 400 kHz.
- A GIS feasibility study is estimated at **about $15,000** (October 1996–September 1997).

**Information, education, and program management**

- Vernal Information Center exhibit: about **$12,000**. The visitor center receives nearly **200,000** visitors per year.
- Glen Canyon Dam Visitor Center exhibit: about **$12,000**. The center attracts more than **3 million** visitors per year.
- Project-specific public involvement is budgeted at **$180.0K**.
- The Internet information-transfer project must be operational by **March 31, 1997**. Personnel costs are estimated at **$5,000–$15,000**, and equipment and software at **$1,000–$10,000**. The guidance notes that materials for a February 23 Management Committee meeting were received only on February 22.
- Program management budgets include Service ($716.3K), Reclamation ($150.0K), Utah ($61.9K), and Wyoming ($12.0K). Colorado's contribution is in-kind, and capital projects coordination is $120.0K.
