## Executive Summary

This FY 1993 Annual Project Report documents Project Number 18 of the **Colorado River Recovery Program**: the "Five Year Flaming Gorge Flow Recommendations Investigation." The study is a multi-year (1992–1996), multi-institutional research program led by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, designed to determine how flow releases from **[[Flaming Gorge Dam]]** (FGD) affect the physical habitat and biological responses of four federally endangered fish species in the **[[Green River]]** ecosystem (from FGD to Lake Powell), including the **[[Yampa River]]** confluence. The program arose from a Section 7 consultation and a November 1992 Biological Opinion which found that historic FGD operations jeopardized the continued existence of endangered fishes, and mandated a Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) including a 5-year flow-based research program. Twelve component studies were funded in FY93 ($410,600), addressing reproduction, recruitment, nonnative fish interactions, habitat formation, overwinter survival, and wetlands restoration. This is a programmatic/administrative report rather than a primary data report; it establishes goals, objectives, study components, and management context rather than presenting raw morphological or population datasets.

## Background & Description

This report is administrative/programmatic in nature and does not provide direct morphological, meristic, or anatomical data for the target species. However, the four focal endangered fishes are identified by scientific nomenclature:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Cyprinidae
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*) — Cyprinidae
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), now more commonly termed **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** — Cyprinidae
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Catostomidae

Life-history stages referenced (larvae, age-0 juveniles, subsequent recruitment stages, adults) indicate the research spans the full ontogenetic range of these species, but no specific morphometric descriptors are given in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat scope:** Green River mainstem and tributaries from Flaming Gorge Dam downstream to Lake Powell, including the lower Yampa River confluence — a large-river, regulated-flow ecosystem in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].
- **Habitat types under investigation:** backwater nursery habitats, spring-fed habitats, mainstem spawning/larval habitats, wetlands (e.g., Old Charley Wash, [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]]), and overwintering habitats.
- **Flow-ecology linkage:** The central ecological premise is that dam-regulated flow (magnitude, timing, and thermal regime) from FGD governs habitat formation/maintenance (backwaters, spawning bars, nursery areas) and consequently spawning success, larval production, recruitment, and survival of endangered native fishes.
- **Historical alteration:** Closure of FGD in 1962 altered the natural hydrograph (magnitude and timing of flows), degrading fish habitat suitability for native/endangered species — establishing the ecological rationale for the "natural hydrograph mimicry" flow recommendation strategy.
- **Species interactions:** The program explicitly investigates interactions between endangered natives and **[[Nonnative Fish|nonnative fishes]]**, including predation dynamics in backwater habitats (Study #10) and nonnative fish management (Study #8).
- **Thermal ecology:** A specific sub-objective examines the feasibility and ecological need of releasing warmer water from FGD during late spring/summer to benefit native fish early life stages, tying reservoir thermal stratification/release depth to downstream fish bioenergetics and spawning cues.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory driver:** The November 1992 Biological Opinion determined that "historic" (pre-1992) FGD operations **jeopardized the continued existence** of humpback chub, bonytail, Colorado squawfish, and razorback sucker, triggering a Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA).
- **Program structure:** Twelve FY93-funded studies were organized into three categories:
  - **Core-Research Studies (1–6):** Colorado squawfish reproduction/larval abundance (Yampa & Green rivers); nursery habitat assessment; razorback sucker egg/larval production; squawfish recruitment via mark-recapture; overwinter survival; historical collection analysis for razorback sucker/humpback chub presence.
  - **Hypothesis-Testing Studies (7–11):** Spring habitat use; nonnative fish management; early biology (flow/temperature effects); backwater predation evaluation; winter movement/habitat synthesis for adult squawfish and razorback sucker.
  - **Habitat Management/Evaluation (12):** Wetlands restoration/demonstration at Old Charley Wash, Ouray National Wildlife Refuge.
- **Completion status (FY93):** Studies #4, #6, #8, #9, and #11 were completed with final reports/manuscripts submitted or forthcoming. Study #10 (backwater predation) was delayed to summer 1994 due to slow sample analysis.
- **Budget:** $410,600 provided and fully expended across all FY93 studies.
- **Programmatic shortcoming identified:** Lack of formal **integration/synthesis** across the twelve individual studies; an interim integrative report (originally planned for early 1994) was delayed due to workload and funding constraints, with completion targeted for the FY94 reporting cycle.
- **Process shortcoming:** Rigid Scope-of-Work procedures limited the program's ability to capitalize on **opportunistic short-term studies** during unplanned high-flow events.
- **Recommendations:** (1) Complete an interim integration report by December 30, 1994; (2) Institute outside **peer review** of the Flaming Gorge Studies to bolster scientific credibility of eventual flow recommendations.
- **Broader significance:** This report functions as the administrative/programmatic backbone linking Flaming Gorge Dam operations to the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered Colorado River Basin fishes, and precedes generation of empirical flow-recommendation data expected in subsequent annual reports (FY94–FY96).
