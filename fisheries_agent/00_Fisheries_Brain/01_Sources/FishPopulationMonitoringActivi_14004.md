## Executive Summary

This report documents fish population monitoring conducted by the Woundfin Recovery Team in the mainstream Virgin River (Utah, Arizona, Nevada) on April 18 and October 9, 1985, situated within a longer-term dataset spanning 1977–1985. The study tracks relative abundance and mean size of native and non-native fishes across six stations, four in "disturbed" river segments (near major irrigation diversions) and two in "undisturbed" segments (Berry Springs, Beaver Dam Wash). The central finding is a severe degradation of water quality and fish habitat in 1985, driven by a sinkhole that developed above [[La Verkin Springs]], which increased spring discharge, elevated salinity (conductivity nearly doubling to tripling at several sites), and raised water temperatures to as high as 37°C. This coincided with a marked decline in native fish abundance—particularly [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin Roundtail Chub]]—and a dramatic, unprecedented expansion of the non-native [[Red Shiner]], which came to dominate (60–100% of catch) at multiple stations including previously native-dominated Beaver Dam Wash. The data suggest that while spring flow magnitude is an important driver of woundfin reproductive success, water quality degradation was likely the primary driver of 1985's poor conditions.

## Background & Description

The [[Virgin River]] originates near Zion National Park, Utah, and flows ~200 km to Lake Mead, Nevada, historically a tributary of the Colorado River prior to the 1941 filling of Lake Mead. The river exhibits highly variable desert-stream hydrology: strong seasonal and daily fluctuations in flow, temperature, and chemistry, with a mobile sand substrate typical of arid-region streams (citing Cook 1960, Deacon and Minckley 1974, Naiman 1981).

Six long-term monitoring stations were established below La Verkin Springs, which — together with the steep, narrow [[Timpoweap Canyon]] — forms a chemical/physical barrier marking the upper distributional limit for [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin Roundtail Chub]]:

- **Disturbed segments** (near major irrigation diversions):
  - Power Plant / Ash Creek confluence
  - Twin Bridges (Utah State Highway 64 crossing)
  - Upper Mesquite Diversion
  - ~4 mile below Riverside Bridge

- **Undisturbed segments**:
  - Canyon ~2 miles below Berry Springs
  - Near the mouth of Beaver Dam Wash

Sampling followed the standardized protocol in the Woundfin Recovery Plan (1984): seining (4.5m x 1.8m, 6mm mesh) of a uniform 10m x 3m habitat segment (run, pool, riffle) with repetitive hauls until catch efficiency dropped below 10% of the best haul; minimum three hauls per station; habitat measurements (depth, velocity, substrate) taken at nine points per segment; up to 100 individuals per species measured for length before further sampling ceased.

Since 1854, agricultural diversion and groundwater extraction have progressively reduced flows throughout the river system, with additional water projects (e.g., the Quail Creek Reservoir diversion installed near the old Hurricane Diversion in 1985) anticipated to further reduce flow.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**1985 Water Quality Crisis:** A sinkhole developed in the riverbed above La Verkin Springs during the low-flow summer of 1985; at the lowest flows, the entire river reportedly disappeared into the sinkhole. Concurrently, La Verkin Springs discharge increased, raising salinity throughout the Utah segment of the river. Conductivity data (µmhos/cm) from T. Hickman (pers. comm.):

| Location | Pre-sinkhole | Post-sinkhole |
|---|---|---|
| Above La Verkin/Ash Creek confluence | 5900–6600 | 9400–10500 |
| Below La Verkin/Ash Creek confluence | 1500–1800 | 7800–8000 |
| Below St. George | 2000–2200 | 4500–4800 |

Water temperature in the La Verkin Springs–to–Ash/La Verkin Creek segment also rose, reaching as high as 37°C on at least one occasion — a thermally stressful condition for native cyprinids and catostomids.

**Flow regime:** Flow is highly variable, with higher flows typically in winter and lower flows in summer (except in very dry years). Mean spring flow data (Hurricane, Bloomington, Littlefield, Riverside gages) show low flow years in 1977, 1981, and 1984, and higher flow years in 1979, 1980, and 1983.

**Habitat disturbance gradient:** The four "disturbed" stations are located at or near major irrigation diversion structures, representing zones of substantial water removal; the two "undisturbed" stations (Berry Springs, Beaver Dam Wash) are more confined by canyon walls and considered relatively less impacted, though even these showed degradation by 1985.

**Non-native species invasion:** [[Red Shiner]] is identified as an increasingly dominant invasive competitor, especially in years of poor native fish condition (1977, 1982, 1984, 1985), suggesting habitat degradation facilitates its expansion at the expense of native species.

## Key Findings

### Overall 1985 Population Status
- Fish abundance in the Power Plant/Ash Creek segment collapsed in fall 1985: only ~5 fish captured per repetitive seine haul set, versus 25–100 fish/haul-set elsewhere in the river.
- Water quality degradation (elevated salinity and temperature from the sinkhole/spring discharge) combined with reduced flow from Hurricane and new Quail Creek diversions is implicated as the primary driver of this decline.

### Woundfin ([[Woundfin]])
- At Power Plant/Ash Creek: comprised 20–30% of the population in 1977–78, dropped to 2–10% in 1979–81, then increased again in 1982–85. Best recruitment occurred in 1978; poorest in 1980 and 1985.
- At Berry Springs: dominant species most years, exceeding 80% of catch in 1982–84; lowest relative abundance (45%) in 1977. Best recruitment in 1983 (mean size ~37mm in late September); poorest in 1980. In October 1985, woundfin averaged 56mm TL and comprised ~64% of the population.
- At Twin Bridges: most abundant species in nearly every collection (exceptions: fall 1978, spring 1980, spring 1982). Good reproduction/recruitment occurred in 1978, 1980, and 1983.
- At Beaver Dam Wash: comprised 60–80% of the population through spring 1984 (except a drop to 30% in spring 1984), collapsing to just 9% by fall 1985. Best reproduction years: 1978, 1980, 1983; poorest: 1984.
- At Mesquite: dominant in 1978; prominent 1979–80, 1983; least evident in 1985. Best reproduction/survival: 1978–80 and 1983.
- At Riverside: dominant in 1977, 1978, 1981; best reproduction in 1978, 1983, 1984.
- Across the river, low spring flow years (1977, 1981, 1984) were consistently associated with poor woundfin recruitment; intermediate flow (1978) was associated with excellent recruitment river-wide; high flow years showed variable outcomes, indicating flow is an important but not sole determinant of reproductive success.

### Virgin Roundtail Chub ([[Virgin Roundtail Chub]])
- At Power Plant/Ash Creek: <1% of the population in most years, peaking at 13.8% in 1978; reproduced in this segment in 1978 and 1983; absent in 1980, 1981, and 1985.
- At Berry Springs: recorded only in 1983 and 1984; comprised ~5% of catch in 1983 with evidence of successful reproduction.
- At Twin Bridges: never prominent in spring; absent in spring 1980, 1981, 1983, 1985; most evident in fall 1977 and 1978; best reproduction in 1983 and 1978; poorest in 1985 and 1977.
- At Beaver Dam Wash: best reproduction apparent in 1978.
- Reproduced at Mesquite in 1978 and 1983.

### Red Shiner ([[Red Shiner]]) — Invasive Expansion
- First appeared at Twin Bridges in spring 1984; comprised 25% of the population there by fall 1985.
- At Beaver Dam Wash: minor presence in spring 1984/1985 (14% each) and fall 1982 (7%) and 1984 (5%); became dominant for the first time in fall 1985 at 64% of the population — described in the source as a striking, first-time shift.
- At Mesquite: prominent throughout study; dominated in 1979, 1980, 1982, 1984; reached a maximum ~96% of the population in 1985.
- At Riverside: comprised 90–100% of the fish population in 1979, 1980, 1983, 1984, and 1985.
- Overall, 1985 marked the species' greatest abundance throughout the lower river and its firm establishment in the St. George area.

### Other Native Species
- [[Speckled Dace]] reached its greatest relative abundance at Berry Springs in 1985.
- [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] were most prominent at Berry Springs in 1977 and least prominent in 1985.
- [[Mosquitofish]] occasionally outcompeted woundfin in relative abundance at Twin Bridges (fall 1978).

### Interpretation
- 1978 and 1983 were identified as the most favorable years overall for native fish populations in the Virgin River.
- 1977, 1982, 1984, and 1985 were years favoring red shiner expansion.
- The 1985 water quality crisis (salinity and temperature spikes from the sinkhole and increased spring discharge), compounded by reduced flow from new diversions, appears to be the dominant factor degrading native fish populations.
