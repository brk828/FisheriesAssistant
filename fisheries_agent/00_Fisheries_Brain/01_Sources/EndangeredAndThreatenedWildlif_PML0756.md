# Critical Habitat Designation for Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Final Rule, published by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Department of the Interior) in the *Federal Register* on March 8, 1994 (Vol. 59, No. 45; 50 CFR Part 17; RIN 1018-AE04), designates [[Critical Habitat]] for the [[Spikedace]], a threatened freshwater minnow of the American Southwest. The designated areas lie within the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona and New Mexico, including [[Aravaipa Creek]], the [[Verde River]], and the upper Gila River. The rule responds to public comments received after a proposed rule and a series of public hearings. It sets out the [[Primary Constituent Elements]] of spikedace habitat, lists activities that could adversely modify critical habitat, and explains how the designation relates to [[Section 7 Consultation]], [[Section 4(b)(2)]] economic analysis, and [[National Environmental Policy Act]] compliance.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*), a small cyprinid fish. The species is listed as threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Regulatory action:** A Final Rule amending 50 CFR 17.95(e) to add critical habitat for spikedace in alphabetical order with other species.
- **Scope of designation:** In Arizona (Graham and Pinal counties), approximately 24 km (15 mi) of Aravaipa Creek are designated. The regulation text also gives legal descriptions for the upstream limit of the segment (the summarized text is partly illegible in the scan). Designated areas in the Gila and Verde basins are also covered in the full rule.
- **Public process:** The Service received written and oral comments from state and federal agencies, local governments, organizations, and the general public. The text cites **109 separate parties** and hearings held in Arizona and New Mexico (including Safford and other locations).
- **Rule history:** The rule notes that the constituent elements were expanded from the proposed rule.
- **Genetic structure:** Allozyme studies (Tibbets 1992) indicate that the separated populations in the Gila, Verde, and Aravaipa systems are genetically distinct, so maintaining each population is considered essential to buffer threats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Threats**
- The [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*, formerly *Notropis lutrensis*) is considered a range-wide threat that displaces spikedace in some locations. Red shiner populations in the Gila River remained small until the early 1980s. Commenters argued that critical habitat would be futile given this competition; the Service responded that the presence of other threats does not reduce its duty to protect habitat.
- Other non-native fishes, including predatory and competitive species, are a concern. The Service is working with state game and fish agencies to control their introduction and spread.

**Primary Constituent Elements (habitat requirements)**
- Permanent, flowing, unpolluted water.
- Adult habitat: slow to swift flow velocities (roughly 0–100 cm/s) in shallow water (about 3–38 cm deep), with shear zones and eddies.
- Juvenile habitat: slow to moderate velocities (roughly 0–60 cm/s) in water about 3–70 cm deep, with moderate amounts of aquatic insect cover.
- Larval habitat: slow to moderate velocities (roughly 0–30 cm/s) in shallow water about 3–30 cm deep, with abundant insect food.
- Sand, gravel, and cobble substrates with low to moderate embeddedness.
- A pool, riffle, run, and backwater mosaic.
- Low stream gradient (about 0.5–1.5%).
- Water temperatures of roughly 1–30 °C with natural seasonal variation.
- An abundant aquatic insect food base, periodic flooding, and a natural, unregulated hydrograph.
- Few or no predatory or competitive non-native species.
- A healthy, intact riparian community and moderate to high bank stability.

**Associated species**
- The [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) is federally listed in the same habitat.
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is listed in the same area.
- A nonessential experimental population of [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) occurs there. Its status provides protection equivalent to a proposed species under section 7, so it has little economic impact.
- The [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*) occurs near some spikedace habitat but is not expected to add cumulative effects.

**Activities that may adversely modify critical habitat (Section 4(b)(8))**
- *Flow alteration:* groundwater pumping, impoundments, and water diversions that reduce the minimum flow.
- *Watershed alteration:* vegetation manipulation, timber harvest, prescribed burning, road construction, livestock grazing, mining, urban development, and off-road vehicle use.
- *Channel morphology:* channelization, impoundment, loss of substrate sources, and destruction of riparian vegetation.
- *Water chemistry:* release of chemical or biological pollutants from point or non-point sources.
- *Non-native fish:* stocking of game fish, use of live bait fish, stocking for biological control, aquaculture dumping, and interbasin water transfers.

**Federal land management**
- No federal activities on Bureau of Land Management lands in Aravaipa Creek are expected to be affected. The Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness is managed to protect natural resources, but if recreation increases streambank degradation and sediment pollution, section 7 consultation may be needed.
- Little effect is expected on U.S. Forest Service lands in the Gila and Verde rivers.

## Key Findings

1. **Critical habitat is designated** for spikedace in the Gila River Basin, Arizona and New Mexico, including Aravaipa Creek, the Verde River, and the upper Gila River.
2. **Habitat protection is considered necessary** to counter threats such as red shiner competition and non-native fish. The Service states that the existence of other threats does not remove its duty to protect habitat.
3. **Populations are genetically distinct.** Each population (Aravaipa, Verde, upper Gila, and the Gila/Gila Valley "core" population, the largest remaining) is considered important to species survival.
4. **Economic analysis (Souder 1992)** was prepared in line with Executive Order 12291 and section 4(b)(2). The Service concludes that the analysis fulfills the functional equivalent of NEPA documentation. The Service also states that an Environmental Impact Statement is not required and that the Regulatory Flexibility Analysis is not required because Executive Order 12291 was rescinded.
5. **Cumulative effects** of prior listings and critical habitat designations are included in the baseline. Commenters' requests to include future and theoretical actions were not adopted.
6. **Section 7 consultation** is required for federal actions that may affect critical habitat, including those on Bureau of Land Management and U.S. Forest Service lands where recreational or grazing impacts could occur.
7. **Non-native fish control** is identified as essential for habitat quality, and the Service is coordinating with state agencies on this.
