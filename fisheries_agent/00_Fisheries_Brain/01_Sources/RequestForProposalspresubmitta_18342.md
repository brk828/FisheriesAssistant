# Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program - Request for Proposals Workshop

#conservation-planning #endangered-species #river-ecology #habitat-management

## Executive Summary

This document records the February 25, 1997 presubmittal workshop for the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP), held in Las Vegas, Nevada. The workshop addressed two Request for Proposals (RFPs): RFP-1 for program facilitation and RFP-2 for conservation plan development. The MSCP is designed as a comprehensive conservation and regulatory framework covering the historic 100-year floodplain of the [[Lower Colorado River]] from [[Hoover Dam]] to the Mexico border. The program operates under a $4.5 million budget with a completion deadline of May 15, 2002, and is structured as an Ecosystem Conservation Recovery and Implementation Team (ECRIT) under Section 4(f)(2) of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] (NFWF) serves as banker and contracting agent, with funding split 50% federal and 50% state (California 50%, Arizona 30%, Nevada 20% of state portion).

## Background & Description

The MSCP represents a collaborative effort among federal, state, tribal, and local stakeholders to develop a multi-species conservation plan for the [[Lower Colorado River]]. The program structure includes a [[Steering Committee]] (primary decision-making body), a [[Work Group]] (technical staff support ranging from 20-40 participants), and various ad hoc subcommittees. The program has been designated as an ECRIT under ESA Section 4(f)(2), providing exemption from Federal Advisory Committee Act (FACA) requirements while maintaining public meeting accessibility.

The workshop documented 46 attendees representing consulting firms, engineering companies, water districts, and government agencies from California, Arizona, Nevada, and Utah. Key participating agencies include the [[Metropolitan Water District of Southern California]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]], [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]], [[US Bureau of Reclamation]], and [[Imperial Irrigation District]].

The program area encompasses the historic 100-year floodplain of the mainstream [[Lower Colorado River]], explicitly excluding tributary floodplains. The geographic scope extends from the top of [[Lake Mead]] downstream, overlapping with but distinct from existing Biological Assessments that begin at [[Hoover Dam]]. [[Grand Canyon National Park]] coordinates involvement through the [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]] representative.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The MSCP addresses both aquatic and terrestrial species within the [[Lower Colorado River]] floodplain ecosystem. The program must consider species both within and outside the 100-year floodplain that are affected by activities within the floodplain. Upland species impacts from floodplain activities require assessment, and areas within the floodplain unaffected by federal or non-federal actions remain available as potential mitigation sites.

The program is not intended to regulate activities outside the 100-year floodplain that affect species within it, though it may serve as a mitigation bank for such projects. The conservation plan must integrate with existing regulatory frameworks including the [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 consultation process and Section 10 [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] (HCP) requirements.

Environmental compliance requires preparation of both NEPA (Environmental Impact Statement) and CEQA (Environmental Impact Report) documentation, with the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] serving as NEPA lead agency. The EIS/EIR will address implementation of the multi-species conservation plan and issuance of associated permits and authorizations as a stand-alone document. The program anticipates utilizing existing data sources, though habitat mapping from aerial photography and filling identified data gaps may be necessary.

The completion deadline of May 15, 2002 corresponds to five years following issuance of the Biological Opinion by [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] to [[US Bureau of Reclamation]] on the Assessment of Operation and Maintenance and Endangered Species of the Lower Colorado River.

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Timeline:**
- Total budget: $4.5 million ($225,000 allocated to NFWF administrative costs; $200,000 committed to Year 1 Interim Conservation Measures)
- Project duration: 3-5 years (originally stated as 3 years, corrected during workshop)
- Mandatory completion: May 15, 2002
- Meeting schedule: 12 [[Steering Committee]] meetings per year plus limited additional meetings (original estimate of 45 meetings/year deemed unrealistic)
- Meetings typically run 8:30/9:30 AM to 4:00/4:30 PM in Las Vegas; no overnight stays required for one-day meetings

**Funding and Contracting:**
- Funding split: 50% federal / 50% state (of state portion: 50% California, 30% Arizona, 20% Nevada)
- Contract type: Time and Materials, not to exceed total contract amount
- [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] serves as banker and contracting agent via Cooperative Agreements
- No federal procurement set-aside requirements for small, minority-owned, or women-owned businesses
- Point of Contact (POC) position is contributed, not funded from $4.5 million
- Steering Committee travel costs are in-kind contributions, not from program budget

**Scope and Deliverables:**
- Two separate RFPs: (1) Program Facilitation and (2) Conservation Plan Development
- Required products include Multi-Species Conservation Plan, Habitat Conservation Plan (Section 10 ESA), EIS/EIR documentation
- GIS requirements: ARC/INFO and ORACLE-based system with Internet download capability
- Habitat mapping from existing aerial photography required
- Scientific peer review process to be funded from $4.5 million budget
- All data and documents archived by Steering Committee, not consultant

**Technical Requirements:**
- Study area: Historic 100-year floodplain of mainstream [[Lower Colorado River]]
- Tributaries explicitly excluded from project area
- Consultant responsible for identifying data gaps and costs for filling them
- Vegetation/habitat classification system to be determined during project
- Two review cycles expected per deliverable, with up to 35 reviewers
- Field verification effort requires close consultation with resource agencies

**Proposal Evaluation:**
- 18-member evaluation committee drawn from Steering Committee/Work Group
- Cost is a factor but not sole criterion
- Maximum 5 previous projects per firm; 2 references per project (maximum 10 references)
- Multi-firm teams may submit up to 5 projects per firm
- Proposal page limits: 50 pages (RFP-2), 25 pages (RFP-1); references and resumes excluded
- Color allowed except in paper and text; 11"×17" fold-outs permitted for detailed cost estimates only
- Organizational chart and timeline mandatory, included in page limit
- Resumes may be double-sided

**Stakeholder and Public Process:**
- Meetings are public with encouraged input, but not subject to open meeting laws
- Two distinct processes: (1) stakeholder involvement in formal program structure, (2) broader public education and outreach
- Program Facilitator responsible for stakeholder outreach and identification of potential Steering Committee members
- Conservation Plan Development Consultant responsible for public education and outreach
- Stakeholder briefings conducted by PDC, facilitator, or Steering Committee/Work Group as appropriate

**Regulatory Framework:**
- Program provides "one-stop-shopping" for incidental take permits
- Some entities require permits due to their activities
- Integration with [[Corps of Engineers]] 404 permitting uncertain at time of workshop
- Program designed as hybrid addressing both conservation needs and regulatory requirements of federal and non-federal participants
- Not strictly labeled as HCP or Recovery Implementation Program (RIP); seeks innovative solutions
