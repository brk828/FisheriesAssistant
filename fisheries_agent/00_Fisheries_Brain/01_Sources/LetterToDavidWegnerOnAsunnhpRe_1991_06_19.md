## Executive Summary
This document is a field-trip correspondence from Michael E. Douglas (ASU co-PI) to David Wegner (Project Manager, [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]]), summarizing a 6-day (09–14 June 1991) joint [[Arizona State University]]/[[Nongame and Native Wildlife Program]] (ASU/NNHP) research expedition into the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) canyon. While primarily a logistical trip report, it contains important methodological and ecological data relevant to fish monitoring efforts in the LCR, including the deployment of a fish weir and hypotheses regarding fish movement patterns. The letter also documents field camp construction, helicopter logistics, and communication testing, all conducted under the umbrella of the broader [[GCES]] (Glen Canyon Environmental Studies) research program monitoring the Colorado River ecosystem post-Glen Canyon Dam.

## Background & Description
No morphological data on fish specimens (e.g., length, weight, coloration, meristics) are reported in this letter. The document is logistical/operational in nature and does not include specimen-level biological descriptions. Any fish species present in the LCR at this time were not captured, measured, or described, as the fish weir yielded zero captures during its operational test phase.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: The [[Little Colorado River]] canyon is a tributary system feeding into the mainstem [[Colorado River]], with the confluence located ca. 3.75 km downstream of the Powell Canyon research camp. This confluence zone is a known focal area for native fish research, including work conducted by the [[Bio/West]] research group.
- **Fish movement hypothesis**: Based on data from previous monitoring efforts, the research team hypothesized that **fish migrations in the LCR were effectively completed** by the time of the June sampling period. This suggests a seasonal migratory pulse (likely spawning-related) had already concluded prior to the trip.
- **Local movement pattern**: The team hypothesized that post-migration fish movements within the LCR are **predominantly local in nature**, rather than large-scale directional migrations. This ecological inference directly explains the weir's failure to intercept any fish, since a stationary diagonal weir is poorly suited to detect localized, non-directional movement.
- **Spatial reference points**: Key locations referenced in relation to fish/field research include Powell Canyon camp, "the notch" (~5.5 km from Powell camp), Salt Canyon base camp (~7.25 km above Powell camp), and the LCR/Colorado River confluence — establishing a spatial framework for future monitoring efforts in this reach.

## Key Findings
- **Fish weir deployment**: A two-box, 140-foot diagonal fish weir was constructed from PVC pipe, plasticized fencing, sandbags, nylon locks, and rope, and operated for a 2-day/2-night test period.
- **Zero fish captures**: No fish were entrapped during the weir's operational test — a result the researchers considered **predictable** rather than a methodological failure, given the hypothesized post-migratory, localized movement behavior of LCR fish at that time of year.
- **Operational feasibility**: The weir's construction and breakdown were accomplished without difficulty, providing valuable logistical insight on the effort required for planning extended deployment periods in future research phases.
- **Implication for monitoring design**: The null result reinforces the need to reconsider weir timing and placement strategy relative to the LCR's fish migratory calendar — deployment during peak migratory movement (rather than post-migration) may be necessary to effectively intercept fish for population sampling.
- **Multi-agency coordination**: The trip was conducted collaboratively between [[Arizona State University]], [[Nongame and Native Wildlife Program]] (Arizona Game & Fish–affiliated), and logistical support from Phoenix APO (helicopter operations), under oversight of [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]]. Continued coordination with [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] was planned for expanded communications testing on 1 July 1991.
- **Camp infrastructure**: A low-impact field camp (16 wooden floor panels, tents) was established near Powell Canyon, deliberately sited to minimize visual and physical impact on the canyon environment — reflecting sensitivity to conservation values in an area supporting sensitive native fish populations.
- **Communication system testing**: Hand-held radio transmitter range tests revealed reliable downstream communication to the confluence and partial upstream communication to "the notch," with degraded signal beyond that point — logistics relevant to coordinating future fish sampling/monitoring teams across the canyon.
