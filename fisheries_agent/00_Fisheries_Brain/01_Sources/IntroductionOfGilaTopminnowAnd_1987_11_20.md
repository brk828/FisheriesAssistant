# Introduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish at Desert Botanical Garden

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1987 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) correspondence record detailing the conservation-driven translocation of two federally significant desert fish species — [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — to two public/educational display sites in Arizona: the [[Desert Botanical Garden]] in Phoenix and a [[Bureau Of Land Management]] facility in the Kingman Resource Area. The transfers were executed under formal Loan Agreements (LA87-15 and LA87-14) from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], with accompanying baseline water quality (limnological) data recorded at the Desert Botanical Garden site.

## Background & Description
- **Species introduced:**
  - **Gila Topminnow** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) — source stock originated from **Sharp and Sheehy Springs**.
  - **Desert Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — source stock originated from **Santa Clara Slough, Mexico**.
- **Hatchery of origin:** [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Dexter, New Mexico — a key USFWS facility for propagation and refugial maintenance of imperiled [[Desert Fishes]].
- **Transfer date:** November 4, 1987, via air shipment.
- **Personnel involved:** Bill Kepner and Diane Laush (transport); Sam F. Spiller (Field Supervisor, signatory); Buddy Jensen (Dexter Hatchery contact); Bob Hall (BLM recipient, Kingman).
- **Legal/administrative mechanism:** Fish and Wildlife Service Loan Agreements **LA87-15** (Desert Botanical Garden) and **LA87-14** (BLM Kingman Resource Area), which obligate recipient institutions to maintain species security and submit annual activity reports by December 1 each year.
- **Purpose of introduction:** Establishment of ex-situ display populations for **public education and information purposes**, supporting broader awareness of [[Endangered Species]] conservation in the Sonoran Desert region.
- **Housing conditions:** BLM Kingman site maintained fish in a 125-gallon aquarium; Desert Botanical Garden intended for public display.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are flagship taxa within the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] conservation framework (noted recipient: W.L. Minckley, Arizona State University), reflecting their status as imperiled inhabitants of isolated desert aquatic systems (springs, sloughs, and remnant desert wetlands) in the American Southwest and northern Mexico.
- **Source habitat types:**
  - *Cyprinodon macularius* sourced from **Santa Clara Slough, Mexico**, a Colorado River delta wetland remnant.
  - *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* sourced from **Sharp and Sheehy Springs**, isolated spring habitats characteristic of Gila topminnow's fragmented native range.
- **On-site limnological measurements** taken at Desert Botanical Garden (via Hydrolab Surveyor II) on November 4, 1987, at 1100 h:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Water Temperature | 22.6 °C |
| Dissolved Oxygen | 8.81 mg/L |
| pH | 8.06 |
| Conductivity | 550 µmho/cm |

- These water quality parameters (moderate temperature, high dissolved oxygen, slightly alkaline pH, moderate conductivity) are broadly consistent with typical desert spring/pond conditions suitable for supporting both [[Poeciliidae]] and [[Cyprinodontidae]] species, both of which exhibit high physiological tolerance to variable and often extreme desert aquatic conditions (temperature fluctuation, salinity, low dissolved oxygen in native habitats).

## Key Findings
- Successful translocation of **Gila Topminnow** and **Desert Pupfish** to two ex-situ education facilities in Arizona on November 4, 1987, under formal USFWS Loan Agreements.
- All transferred fish arrived in **good condition** at both the Desert Botanical Garden and the BLM Kingman Resource Area facility.
- Recipient institutions accepted formal custodial responsibility, including obligations for **security maintenance** and **annual reporting** of fish-related activities.
- Documented baseline water quality parameters provide a reference point for future monitoring of the introduced population's habitat suitability at Desert Botanical Garden.
- The action reflects a coordinated multi-agency conservation strategy involving the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[Bureau Of Land Management]], [[Desert Botanical Garden]], and the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], underscoring the role of captive/display propagation in supporting recovery efforts for threatened and endangered desert fish species.
- Distribution of copies to the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] and USFWS Endangered Species and Fishery Resources divisions indicates this translocation was tracked within broader recovery planning efforts for these federally listed species.
