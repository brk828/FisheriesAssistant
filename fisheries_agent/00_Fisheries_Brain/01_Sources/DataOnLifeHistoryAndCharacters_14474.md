# Life History and Characters of *Galeus piperatus*, a Dwarf Catshark of the Golfo de California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #scyliorhinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Leighton R. Taylor Jr.]] of the [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]] supplements the original description of *Galeus piperatus* ([[Springer and Wagner 1966]]), a small catshark known only from the northern [[Golfo de California]]. The authors add an egg case, two juveniles, additional adults and males, and detailed morphometric, meristic, dental, denticle and clasper data. They conclude that the species reaches sexual maturity and adult form at a very small size, that the sexes differ little in external form, and that the species is restricted to an area of strong turbulence and extreme physical variability.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** *Galeus piperatus* was described from two adult females (296 and 302 mm total length) and one immature female (256 mm), all from a five-specimen trawl catch. Its vernacular name is "peppered shark."
- **Material examined:** The two type females, one additional adult female, three adult males, two early juvenile males and one probable egg case. Specimens were collected in 1964 (R/V *Alaska*, California Department of Fish and Game) and 1967–1969 (R/V *Velero IV*, University of Southern California; R/V *Thomas Washington* and R/V *Ellen B. Scripps*, Scripps).
- **Size:** The species barely exceeds 300 mm total length. The three adult males measured 280 to 293 mm and had fully developed claspers.
- **Egg case:** A translucent-brown, elliptical, empty egg case of 35 mm length, assigned to this species with four lines of evidence (co-capture with an adult, size consistency, resemblance to *Galeus melastomus*, and exclusion of other Gulf scyliorhinids). The egg-case-to-female-length ratio is 118, within the range of other scyliorhinids (100 to 129).
- **Juveniles:** The two juvenile males (71 and 80 mm) differ strikingly from adults in colour, with dark saddle-like blotches on the fins and caudal fin, and in denticle form (long, hooked, sessile spines, versus pedunculated tridents in adults).
- **Morphometry:** Fifty-seven dimensions were measured, expressed as thousandths of standard length. Juveniles have a longer urosome and caudal fins, shorter predorsal lengths, and much smaller paired fins.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Almost none in external measurements. Abdomen length averages 189 in females and 178 in males, a smaller difference than in the pelagic [[Euprotomicrus bispinatus]].
- **Teeth:** Females show greater heterodonty (tricuspid anteriorly, four to seven cusps posteriorly). Males have mostly tricuspid teeth, with rare pentacuspid teeth in one specimen. Tooth rows number 29 to 32 per side in adults.
- **Vertebrae (eight radiographed specimens):** Monospondylous 30–33 (mean 32.7); precaudal diplospondylous 36–39 (mean 37.4); total precaudals 66–72 (mean 69.1); caudals 41–49 (mean 45.6); total 112–118 (mean 114.7).
- **Claspers:** Large, robust, highly complex and jointed about midway along their length, with a distinctive denticle pattern including biserial hooks along the sperm duct.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** All specimens come from the northern Golfo de California, in the Salsipuedes basin. The region is noted for high endemism, strong tidal currents, extreme seasonal surface temperature range, and exceptional vertical and horizontal turbulence (Roden 1958, 1964; Roden and Groves 1959).
- **Faunal context:** The area holds Pleistocene relict warm-temperate fishes alongside a reduced tropical assemblage ([[Walker 1960]]). The authors place the family within the "mildly antitropical" pattern of distribution ([[Hubbs 1952]]).
- **Depth:** Specimens were taken from about 400 m to 1215 m and 1097–1326 m. A hagfish (*Eptatretus* sp.) trawled in the same area also ranged widely in depth (198–1180 m).
- **Depth question:** Some records place specimens in midwater (about 275 m), but the authors conclude that the trawls likely reached the bottom. Their reasoning includes co-captured bottom fishes and a hake that had eaten a *Galeus*. Trawl depths calculated from cable angle and ship speed were judged unreliable in these strong currents.
- **Distribution gap:** The species has not been found in other parts of the Gulf, along the open coast of Baja California, or south of the Gulf, although the authors recommend searching those areas.

## Key Findings

1. **Dwarfism with early maturity:** Adults are only about 300 mm long, and the vertebral count is essentially complete by 70–80 mm. Adult form is reached at an exceptionally small size.
2. **Reproduction:** The egg case (35 mm) is consistent with a coiled embryo near the size of the smallest free-living juvenile (71 mm). Oviparity is inferred, not directly observed.
3. **Ontogenetic change:** Juveniles differ from adults in colouration (conspicuous dark markings), denticle form (single hooked spines versus tridents), and several morphometric proportions. Oral denticles are reduced in juveniles.
4. **Sexual similarity:** Sexes are nearly identical in morphometrics. Females differ from males mainly in tooth heterodonty.
5. **Colouration revised:** Living adults show sharp dorsal–ventral contrast and a pale subhexagonal reticulum, which the original description did not record. The authors attribute the earlier lack of contrast partly to preservation.
6. **Methodological caution:** The authors consider the "A" and "B" vertebral ratios of [[Springer and Garrick 1964]] of little value for small sharks. Counts of caudal vertebrae are subject to error, and the authors differ from the original counts by up to three precaudal and five caudal vertebrae.
7. **Taxonomic utility:** Clasper structure and denticle patterns are proposed as key characters for distinguishing scyliorhinid genera and species, and are compared with [[Galeus arae]] and European *Scyliorhinus* species.
8. **Restricted range:** The species is known only from one turbulent, oceanographically distinctive basin, so further surveys in adjacent regions are needed.
