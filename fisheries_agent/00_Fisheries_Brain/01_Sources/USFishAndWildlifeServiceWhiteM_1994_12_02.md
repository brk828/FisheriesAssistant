## Executive Summary

This document is a **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) news release** announcing the signing of a "Statement of Relationship" between USFWS Director Mollie Beattie and White Mountain Apache Tribe Chairman Ronnie Lupe. The agreement establishes a government-to-government framework for managing wildlife and endangered species on the **Fort Apache Indian Reservation** in Arizona. Notably, this source contains **no direct morphological, taxonomic, or population-level fish science data**. Instead, it documents a conservation governance mechanism relevant to sensitive aquatic and terrestrial species management, including watershed-level ecosystem planning that would affect native fish habitat. The document is included here for its ecological/administrative relevance to species such as the [[Apache Trout]], which is endemic to the White Mountains region and historically significant to the Tribe, though it is not explicitly named in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The agreement covers the **Fort Apache Indian Reservation**, located in the White Mountains of Arizona — a high-elevation montane region containing headwater streams that support cold-water native fish populations, most notably the [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), a species historically and culturally tied to the White Mountain Apache Tribe and federally listed as threatened.
- **Ecosystem approach**: The Tribe committed to finalizing resource management plans on a **watershed/ecosystem basis**, an approach directly relevant to protecting stream connectivity, water quality, and habitat integrity for native and endemic fish species.
- **Sensitive Species Management Plan**: The Tribe agreed to prepare this plan to address habitat needs of sensitive, threatened, and endangered species on the Reservation — a category that would encompass native salmonids and other aquatic taxa alongside terrestrial species like the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (listed as threatened in 1993).
- **Co-management model**: The Statement recognizes the Tribe's aboriginal authority over land and resource management while enabling USFWS technical/biological assistance — a hybrid model balancing tribal sovereignty ([[Tribal Self-Determination]]) with federal trust responsibilities under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
