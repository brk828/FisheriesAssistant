# Broadcast Spawning Over Cobble by Longfin Dace in Artificial Stream Channels

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document bundles a manuscript by Matthew J. Troia, James E. Whitney, and Keith B. Gido (Kansas State University) with a confidential peer-review form from *The Southwestern Naturalist* (Manuscript No. MP-09, reviewer no. 3, dated 23 December 2013). The manuscript reports an unplanned observation: [[Longfin Dace]] (*[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]*) adults held in outdoor [[Mesocosm Design|artificial stream mesocosms]] with [[Cobble Substrate]] apparently broadcast spawned instead of building [[Redd Construction|redds]] in sand. Larvae emerged in three of four mesocosms. The reviewer judged the work a sound but unplanned anecdote, inadequate as a stand-alone paper, and suggested it might fit as a footnote or as part of a larger study.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Agosia chrysogaster* (Girard, 1856) is a small cyprinid (under 80 mm total length) endemic to the [[Gila River Basin]]. The authors note it is unusual among cyprinids because it builds depressions (redds) in sand.
- **Reproduction (as described in the text):** Females deposit eggs in shallow, saucer-shaped depressions in coarse sand along stream margins at depths of 5 to 20 cm. Spawning is protracted, from December to July with a peak in April (Lewis 1978). Adults rarely exceed 65 mm, mature in their first year, and rarely live past three years.
- **Habitat:** Clear, cool mountain streams to intermittent desert streams with sand or gravel substrate (Sublette et al. 1990).
- **Taxonomic context:** The manuscript cites [[Johnston And Page 1992]], who regarded redd construction as a derived spawning strategy and noted that no other cyprinid is known to build redds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Broodstock collection.** Adults of age 1 or older (>50 mm total length) were collected from four sites in the upper Gila River basin between 18 and 22 March 2013 (OCR-garbled date range read as 18–22 March). Sites included Little Creek and Sapillo Creek (tributaries), and two Gila River mainstem sites near Cliff, New Mexico (Table 1). The number of fish collected is illegible in the OCR text.

**Mesocosm set-up** (after [[Matthews Et Al 2006]]):
- Outdoor facility at the Konza Prairie Biological Station, Riley County, Kansas.
- Each system: a 2.54 m diameter, 0.5 m deep stream mesocosm connected to a riffle (1.8 m long, 0.45 m wide, 0.25 m deep).
- Water circulated at 44 L/s, riffle current velocity 6–8 cm/s.
- Substrate: quarried river rock, mean diameter 51.8 mm.
- Water replaced at a rate of 10 L/min; spring water source described only partially.

**Monitoring:** Temperature logged hourly in all four mesocosms from 10 April to 28 May 2013 (Onset submersible loggers). Temperatures differed among mesocosms by less than 0.9 °C. Mesocosms were checked daily for redds and larvae.

## Key Findings

- **No redds in any mesocosm** throughout the study. No saucer-shaped depressions and no evidence of substrate preparation by adults were observed.
- **Larvae present in three of four mesocosms**, emerging at temperatures between 19.2 and 24.0 °C.
- **Emergence timing (Table 1):**
  - Little Creek broodstock: larvae emerged 9 May 2013 at 19.2 °C.
  - Two Gila River sites: larvae emerged 30 April 2013 at 23.6 °C and 24.0 °C.
- **Interpretation by authors:** Longfin dace can broadcast spawn over cobble and produce viable larvae, suggesting an alternate reproductive strategy when sand is unavailable. The authors explicitly do not claim broadcast spawning is preferred at these sites, because broodstock lacked sand.
- **Hypotheses offered for the absence of redds:**
  1. Large cobble prevented dace from constructing redds.
  2. Mesocosm conditions circumvented the need for nest spawning, possibly relating to predation on eggs.
- **Funding:** Southwestern Association of Naturalists, Kansas Academy of Science, Prairie Biotic Research Inc., National Science Foundation (DEB #1311183), and the Bureau of Reclamation WaterSmart program.

### Reviewer Assessment

- **Overall:** The reviewer found the paper brief and generally well written, but considered it an unplanned anecdote without a stated purpose, hypothesis, experimental design, controls, or statistical analysis.
- **Rating:** The boxed rating appears to be option 4 ("Not publishable"), though the OCR is unclear; the reviewer suggested the material could be a footnote or paragraph within a larger paper.
- **Main criticisms:**
  - No rationale for collecting and retaining eggs or for using only gravel substrate, despite knowledge that the species spawns over sand or gravel.
  - Conclusions about escape from egg predation are not supported; the reviewer asked which predators might target dace eggs.
  - Site selection, stock separation, water source and chemistry, brood survival, sex ratio, and euthanasia method are unreported.
  - Permits, animal care protocol, and specimen deposition are not documented.
  - Table 1 needs collection dates, species in the legend, sample sizes, and possibly UTM coordinates in place of latitude-longitude.
  - Figure 1 (hourly temperatures) is not discussed in the text, and no temperature-development relationship or fertilization-to-swim-up interval is reported.
  - *Minckley (1973)* is cited in the references but not in the text.
  - Suggested citation: Kepner (1982), a Master's thesis on longfin dace reproduction in a Sonoran desert stream.
- **Revision level implied:** Substantial revision is needed before the paper could be considered acceptable.
