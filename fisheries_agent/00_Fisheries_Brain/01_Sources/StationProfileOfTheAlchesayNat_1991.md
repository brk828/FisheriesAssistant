## Executive Summary

This station profile describes the **Alchesay National Fish Hatchery**, a federal facility operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in Whiteriver, Arizona. Established in 1934 under the Coordination Act (48 Stat. 401), Alchesay functions as one unit of the **[[Alchesay-Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery Complex]]**, a two-station operation. Its core federal mandate is the propagation and stocking of trout species — including the regionally significant **[[Apache Trout]]** — on Indian Reservations and other federally managed waters across Arizona, New Mexico, and Southern Colorado. The hatchery relies on a gravity-fed spring water supply and distributed over 1 million fish annually during the profiled fiscal years (1989–1991), primarily into state and federal waters. The station also serves a public outreach role, hosting approximately 6,400 visitors in FY1991.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/Water Source:** Water is supplied via gravity flow through a pipeline from **[[Alchesay Spring]]**, which is fed by a natural underflow of the **[[North Fork White River]]**. Flow rates range from 2,500 to 10,000 gpm, indicating a stable, coldwater spring-fed system suitable for salmonid rearing.
- **Infrastructure as Artificial Niche:** The reared population occupies a constructed aquatic environment consisting of:
  - A tankhouse with 12 concrete tanks
  - One bank of 9 raceways and one bank of 12 raceways
  - Five ponds (1/4 to 1/2 acre each)
- **Ecological Role/Distribution Range:** Fish produced are distributed into federal (FWS and other), state, and private waters across **Arizona, New Mexico, and Colorado**, indicating the hatchery's role in supplementing wild and reservation trout populations, likely for recreational fisheries and conservation stocking (notably relevant to native **[[Apache Trout]]** recovery efforts).
- **Anthropogenic Context:** The facility operates under a federal trust responsibility tied to Indian Reservation waters, linking its ecological function directly to tribal natural resource management, particularly for the **[[White Mountain Apache Tribe]]** lands surrounding Whiteriver, AZ.
