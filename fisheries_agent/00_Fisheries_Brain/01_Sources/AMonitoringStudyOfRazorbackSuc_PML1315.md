# Monitoring Study of Razorback Suckers Reintroduced to the Lower Colorado River (July-September 1987)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This series of three monthly progress reports (July, August and September 1987) documents field work in a monitoring study of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) reintroduced into the lower [[Colorado River]]. The study was run for the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] by consultant [[Dan Langhorst]]. Work covered stocking of hatchery-reared juveniles, trammel-net and minnow-trap sampling of backwaters, and electrofishing of the main channel. The most significant event was a fish kill of 427 dead razorback suckers at the [[Blythe Municipal Golf Course Pond]] in July, linked to herbicide application. Surviving fish were later found by snorkel survey. Few or no razorbacks were captured in most sampling efforts, while several non-native species were common.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large, native freshwater catostomid fish of the Colorado River system. The reports refer to it as a reintroduced species, so the study tracks whether stocked fish survive, disperse and persist. Stocked fish came from two sources:

- **Juveniles** reared at the [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]] and at the Blythe Golf Course Pond.
- **Fry** stocked earlier in the year (March 1987) at sites such as [[Draper Lake]] and [[Lake Havasu]] area backwaters.

Field crews worked about 40 hours per week. The July report was written by the consultant, and the reports were signed by Dan Langhorst.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampling sites are main-channel and backwater habitats on the lower Colorado River, from roughly river mile 82 to river mile 167. Common habitat features included:

- Dense aquatic vegetation, such as curly-leaf pondweed and water milfoil, in backwaters. Reports note that this vegetation may provide refuge for juvenile suckers.
- Riprap banks, saltcedar-lined banks, bulrush overhangs and gravel-cobble riffles in the main channel.
- Shallow, sheltered backwaters separated from the main channel, with reduced current.

Non-native and sport species were frequently caught. These included [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Flathead Catfish]] and [[Tilapia]]. Their presence was noted as relevant to predation and competition risk for stocked suckers. Razorback suckers caught in trammel nets were usually entangled in the lowest part of the net, near aquatic vegetation, at depths of 1 to 2 meters. This suggests they stayed close to the substrate.

## Key Findings

### July 1987

- **Fish kill at Blythe Municipal Golf Course Pond:** 427 dead razorback suckers were removed over 2 days. Reported average size was about 202 mm total length and 108 g, though the source text is partly illegible at this point.
- **Probable cause:** Pond water level had been lowered to allow shoreline weed removal. Weeds were then cut and sprayed with the herbicide "Roundup." After refilling, the herbicide appeared to have mixed into the pond water at a level that caused fish mortality.
- **Survivors:** Snorkel survey several days later found more than 500 razorbacks, which had apparently survived by moving to deeper areas and to dense vegetation.
- **Upper Lake Mohave survey (July 28-29):** Surveyed with Arizona State University personnel for juvenile razorbacks. None were captured. Juveniles had recently been collected from this area by Arizona Game and Fish personnel.
- **Parker backwater trammel netting (week of July 20-24):** Razorbacks had occasionally been captured previously, but none were caught during this effort.

### August 1987

- **Juvenile stocking at Colorado River backwaters:** Juveniles reared at Niland Native Fish Ponds were stocked on 2 dates. Stocked fish averaged 74 mm and ranged from 51 to 117 mm. Stocking mortality was low (fewer than 10 individuals per trip).
- **Parker backwater (Aug 18-19):** Sampled with trammel nets and minnow traps after the stocking at this site. Dense vegetation was present. Fish were caught, including *Dorosoma petenense* and several other species, but the report summarizes razorback results only as relevant to net placement and predator stomach contents.
- **Oxbow Pond (Aug 24-25):** Three trammel nets set. No razorbacks captured, consistent with the June sampling there, when this site was also trapped.
- **Colorado River electrofishing (Aug 3-4, river miles 167 to 156):** Common carp, channel catfish, threadfin shad, largemouth bass (infrequent) and other species were recorded. No razorbacks were captured.

### September 1987

- **Goose Flats Backwater (Sept 2 stocking):** A total of 1,444 juveniles were stocked. Subsequent trammel netting and minnow trapping captured juveniles in the area nearest the backwater-river confluence.
- **Trammel net results (Sept 21):** Nets 2 and 3 each caught 2 razorbacks; Net 1 caught none. Their low total catch may mean many razorbacks remained in the backwater rather than moving into the main channel. Some fish appeared to have moved from the release site toward the main channel.
- **Recapture rate:** Sampling on Sept 4-18 at multiple sites (including the Parker site and the Goose Flats site) recaptured juvenile razorbacks in traps, indicating some survival of stocked fish.
- **River mile 157 backwater (Sept 17-18):** Trammel nets and minnow traps set in a pool below a gravel-cobble riffle. No razorbacks were caught.
- **Electrofishing of river miles 151-156 (Sept 2):** Main-channel habitat and nearby backwaters were shocked. No razorbacks were captured.
- **Draper Lake backwater (Sept 30):** Sampled at a former fry-stocking site (March 17, 1987). No razorbacks were captured.
- **Lake Havasu-area survey (Sept 24):** Large minnow traps and a trammel net were set to test survival of fry stocked March 1987. No razorbacks were caught. Only juvenile common carp were caught in minnow traps.

### Overall Results

- Razorbacks were rarely captured outside of stocking sites. Survival of stocked fish was confirmed mainly through the July fish-kill survivors and the September recaptures at Goose Flats.
- Herbicide application in an enclosed pond was identified as a serious hazard to stocked fish.
- Non-native species dominated most catches, which is relevant for future management.
