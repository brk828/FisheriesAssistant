## Executive Summary
This document is a newspaper article reporting on a federal court ruling (Judge Alfredo C. Marquez, U.S. District Court, Arizona) concerning Fort Huachuca's noncompliance with the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA). The court found that the U.S. Army violated environmental law by failing to complete a cumulative impact study addressing effects of Sierra Vista's growth and military installation activity on the [[San Pedro River]] and its adjacent riparian area — but dismissed the lawsuit (filed by the Southwest Center for Biological Diversity) on a procedural technicality (expired 60-day statute of limitations tied to the 1990 Base Realignment and Closure Commission). ## Key Findings
1. Federal Judge Alfredo C. Marquez ruled that the Army's cumulative impact analysis under NEPA was "incomplete as a matter of law," confirming that environmental review obligations regarding the San Pedro River and riparian area were not fulfilled.
2. Despite finding the Army in violation of environmental law, the court dismissed the Southwest Center for Biological Diversity's lawsuit due to the expiration of a 60-day statute of limitations connected to the 1990 Base Realignment and Closure Commission process.
3. The court explicitly identified aquifer over-extraction from regional development as a mechanism of harm to the riparian ecosystem, stating the Army "must not turn a blind eye" to how its actions may exacerbate this threat.
