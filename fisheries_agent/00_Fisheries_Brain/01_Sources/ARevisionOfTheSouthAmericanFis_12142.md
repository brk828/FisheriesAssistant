# A Revision of the South American Fishes of the Genus Nannostomus Günther (Family Lebiasinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lebiasinidae

## Executive Summary

This Smithsonian Contributions to Zoology monograph (number 186, 1975) by [[Stanley H. Weitzman]] and [[F. Stanley Cobb]] is a supplemental revision of the South American [[Nannostomus]] pencilfishes, building on an earlier review by the senior author (Weitzman, 1966). The authors recognize eleven species, including one new species, [[Nannostomus marilynae]], described from the Rio Negro in Brazil. They merge the genus [[Poecilobrycon]] into Nannostomus, finding that the characters previously used to separate the two genera show intermediate conditions. An artificial identification key is provided. The paper also discusses evolutionary specialization, color pattern, and the taxonomic confusion surrounding the aquarium names "gold-anomalus" and "Cyprinodon amazona."

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Based on newly collected material, aquarium specimens, and loans from museums including ANSP, MCZ, CAS, BMNH, ZMA, AMNH and FMNH.
- **Classification:** The authors place the Lebiasinidae as a family separate from the Characidae, with subfamilies Lebiasininae and Pyrrhulininae. Nannostomus belongs to tribe [[Nannostomini]] within Pyrrhulininae.
- **Genus status:** Four nominal genera were reviewed (Nannostomus, Poecilobrycon, Archicheir, Nannobrycon). Only Nannostomus is recognized; Poecilobrycon and Archicheir are synonyms, and Nannobrycon is considered not worth recognizing pending behavioral and anatomical studies.
- **Morphology:** Small, elongate, laterally compressed fishes. Body and fin counts are standardized, with caudal fin principal rays typically 10/9 (ten ending in the dorsal lobe, nine in the ventral lobe). Exceptions occur in [[Nannostomus unifasciatus]] and [[Nannostomus eques]] (8/11).
- **Color pattern terminology:** Primary, secondary and tertiary horizontal stripes, plus nocturnal oblique bands that darken at night or in preserved specimens. Permanent blotches occur only in [[Nannostomus espei]].
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Males of [[Nannostomus minimus]], [[Nannostomus digrammus]] and [[Nannostomus bifasciatus]] have modified anal fins, which may aid in fertilizing eggs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water chemistry:** Nannostomus espei lives in water with a pH of 5.8 (Louis Chung, pers. comm. in the paper), consistent with the blackwater habitats of the region.
- **Distribution examples:**
  - N. espei: Mazaruni River system (Peaima Falls to Toboku Falls), and the Kurupung, Eping and Tabuba rivers, Guyana.
  - N. digrammus: Rio Madeira–Rio Guaporé, Rio Negro, Rio Branco, Rupununi system, Rio Purus, Rio Trombetas drainage.
  - N. bifasciatus: Brokopondo District, Surinam, and creeks in French Guiana.
  - N. beckfordi: Surinam and lower Amazon (Belém area) blackwater creeks, including a white sand savanna belt.
  - N. marilynae: Rio Negro (Brazil), and in a later addendum, Caño Muco, Colombia (Orinoco drainage).
- **Biogeographic note:** The authors suggest that the Rio Negro and Orinoco faunas are closely related because the Casiquiare canal connects them, explaining the Colombian occurrence of N. marilynae.
- **Contact organs:** Breeding tubercles on the ventral head surface occur in N. bifasciatus, N. unifasciatus and N. minimus, and are also found in the tribe Pyrrhulinini (see [[Wiley and Collette]], 1970).

## Key Findings

- **Eleven species recognized:** N. espei, N. digrammus, N. harrisoni, N. beckfordi, N. bifasciatus, N. minimus, N. marginatus, N. trifasciatus, N. marilynae (new), N. unifasciatus and N. eques.
- **Synonymy changes:**
  - Poecilobrycon and Archicheir are placed in synonymy with Nannostomus.
  - N. minimus is revalidated; Weitzman (1966) had treated it as a synonym of N. beckfordi. The Erukin "cotype" was reassigned to N. beckfordi.
  - The names N. beckfordi and N. digrammus had been inadvertently exchanged in the 1966 key, and this is corrected.
- **Nomenclature:** The name Cyprinodon amazona Eigenmann (1894) is proposed for suppression, since it is a junior synonym in practice of the well-established N. marginatus; the authors request ICZN action under Article 79.
- **Phylogenetic conclusions:**
  - N. espei is probably the most primitive species, though its relationships are uncertain and it may have lost the second infraorbital canal independently.
  - N. eques and N. unifasciatus are related through shared caudal-fin modification and "slant" swimming posture.
  - N. marginatus and N. trifasciatus are closely related, based on similar specialized color patterns.
  - The remaining species appear to stand alone.
- **Infraorbital canal:** Loss of the sensory canal in the second infraorbital bone correlates with small size, but is not a clear basis for generic separation.
- **Caudal-fin ocelli:** An ocellus occurs in N. unifasciatus, in juveniles of several species, and in an Rio Apeu population of N. trifasciatus. The authors consider it a poor indicator of relationships.
- **Hypothesized ancestor:** The authors propose the Nannostomus ancestor had a body shape approaching Pyrrhulina, a primary stripe only, no oblique bands, slightly modified male anal fin, and an unmodified caudal fin.
- **Evolutionary trends toward specialization:** Longitudinal stripes, oblique bands, loss of the infraorbital canal, thickened male anal-fin rays, caudal ocelli, and nuptial tubercles.
- **Species-specific notes:**
  - N. harrisoni has postlarval pectoral fins matching the type of Archicheir minutus, resolving that genus as a synonym.
  - N. marilynae is named after Marilyn Sohner Weitzman, the senior author's wife.
  - The Colombian Caño Muco collection of N. marilynae (about 190 mostly adult specimens) was added in an addendum after the manuscript was set in type.
- **Key examples of measurement data:** N. bifasciatus description based on 35 specimens (25.7–43.2 mm SL) from Brokopondo District, Surinam; N. minimus based on all 9 known specimens (16.2–22.8 mm SL); N. marilynae holotype 19.5 mm SL, paratypes 16.1–21.0 mm SL.
- **Practical identification:** The artificial key (with dichotomies based on caudal-fin ray counts, stripe development, adipose fin presence, gill raker counts and scale counts) is intended for aquarists and ichthyologists alike.
