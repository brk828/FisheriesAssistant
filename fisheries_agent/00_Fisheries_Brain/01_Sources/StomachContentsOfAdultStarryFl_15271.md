#ichthyology #marine-biology #Pleuronectidae

## Executive Summary

This study by Bruce Stuart Miller (1967) examines the seasonal and diel (24-hr) stomach contents of adult female [[Starry Flounder]] (*Platichthys stellatus*) and [[Sand Sole]] (*Psettichthys melanostictus*) collected via otter trawl in East Sound, Orcas Island, Washington, across 1963–1964. The two co-occurring flatfish species show almost no dietary overlap: the Starry Flounder is a **benthophagic** feeder on infaunal invertebrates (priapulids, nemerteans, polychaetes, bivalves), while the Sand Sole is a **piscivorous/predatory** feeder targeting forage fish (especially [[Pacific Herring]], *Clupea pallasi*), mysids, shrimp, and squid. Both species feed primarily during daylight hours. Critically, Starry Flounder cease feeding entirely during the coldest months (January–April), entering a winter fasting state, while Sand Sole continue feeding with only minor reduction — a divergence attributed to differing digestive/energetic strategies between benthophagic and predatory flatfish. This has direct implications for the local commercial fishery, which harvests Starry Flounder during their period of poorest physical condition.

## Background & Description

- **Starry Flounder (*Platichthys stellatus*)**: Small, asymmetrical mouth; small number of short, stubby gill rakers — morphology consistent with a benthic, non-piscivorous feeding mode. Sampled individuals ranged 306–565 mm total length, aged 3–6 years.
- **Sand Sole (*Psettichthys melanostictus*)**: Large, symmetrical mouth; many long, slender gill rakers — morphology consistent with active predation on fish and nektonic prey. Sampled individuals ranged 284–454 mm total length, aged 2–4 years.
- **Stomach/digestive tract observations**:
  - Starry Flounder: stomach used primarily as a **holding organ**; emptied in ~5–9 hr; most digestion occurs in the lower intestine. During winter fasting, stomachs were shrunken, walls thick and muscular, rugae pronounced.
  - Sand Sole: stomach used primarily as a **digestive organ**; forage fish retained and digested for **>24 hr**; prey swallowed in characteristic orientations (fish tail-first, except eel-pouts doubled on themselves; squid and shrimp head-first; mysids balled near pylorus).
- Reproductive maturity ("ripeness") assessed by expressible transparent ova under stripping pressure; spawning season inferred as March–May for both species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: East Sound is a long, narrow, shallow (~15 fathom) embayment with sluggish tidal circulation (~0.5 knot) and silty, moderately compacted mud substrate — part of the broader [[San Juan Archipelago]] / [[Puget Sound]] ecosystem.
- **Trophic role — Starry Flounder**: Classified per [[Moiseev 1953]] as a **benthophagic flounder**. Diet dominated by:
  - Nemerteans and priapulids (esp. *Priapulus* sp.)
  - Polychaetes (errant and sedentary forms, e.g. *Scalibregma inflatum*, *Sternaspis fossor*, *Notomastus* sp.)
  - Lamellibranchs/bivalves — selectively fed on thin-shelled species (*Yoldia scissurata*, *Pandora filosa*) over abundant thick-shelled *Serripes groenlandicus*, consistent with [[Blegvad]]'s first-class vs. second-class mollusc food classification.
  - Minor items: pea crabs (Pinnotheridae), brittle stars (Ophiura)
  - No fish were found in any Starry Flounder stomach in this study, despite abundant potential prey fish in the environment — reinforcing its non-piscivorous ecological role.
- **Trophic role — Sand Sole**: Classified as a **predatory flounder**. Diet dominated volumetrically by fish (especially *Clupea pallasi*), with mysids and shrimp important seasonally (summer) and squid important in winter.
- **Niche partitioning**: Essentially zero dietary overlap between the two co-occurring species, explained via [[MacArthur 1964]]'s framework — differentiated by feeding manner (capture mechanism) and food-type selection (mouth/gill raker morphology), rather than by spatial or temporal segregation (both are daytime feeders in the same habitat).
- **Comparative context**: Findings align with [[Moiseev 1953]]'s Russian Pacific observations distinguishing benthophagic vs. predatory flounder feeding-intensity patterns, and parallel selective mollusc predation documented for [[European Plaice]] (*Pleuronectes platessa*) by Blegvad (1928, 1951) and Smidt (1951).

## Key Findings

- **Zero dietary overlap** between Starry Flounder and Sand Sole in East Sound — confirms distinct ecological niches despite co-occurrence.
- **Seasonal feeding cessation in Starry Flounder**: Feeding stopped entirely during lowest bottom water temperatures (January–April, ~8–9°C), with entire digestive tract found empty; feeding resumed around June and peaked July–October.
- **Sand Sole showed minimal seasonal feeding reduction**, though January had the highest percentage of empty stomachs (55.5%) and no full stomachs.
- **Fishery management implication**: The commercial fishery for Starry Flounder operates during winter months when fish are fasting and in poorest physical condition (supported by comparison to [[European Flounder]] *Platichthys flesus* data from Mulicki 1947, showing lowest nutrition coefficient and fat content during winter fasting).
- **Differential digestion/gastric retention times**: Starry Flounder empties its stomach in 5–9 hr (holding-organ function; digestion occurs distally in intestine); Sand Sole retains forage fish for >24 hr for digestion (true gastric digestion function).
- **Diel feeding pattern**: Both species are **daytime feeders**, feeding roughly from sunrise to sunset; Starry Flounder showed a distinct empty-stomach period from ~2–9 AM.
- **Selective feeding on bivalves**: Starry Flounder preferentially consumed thin-shelled clams (*Yoldia*, *Pandora*) over abundant thick-shelled *Serripes*, suggesting active prey selection based on shell thickness/nutritional quality rather than availability.
- **Predatory impact potential**: Consistent with other studies (e.g., [[Medcof and MacPhail 1952]] on winter flounder and *Mya arenaria*; [[Villadolid 1927]] on Starry Flounder and Pismo clams), flatfish predation may significantly impact commercial bivalve beds.
- **Correction to prior literature**: Results contradict [[Clemens and Wilby 1961]]'s characterization of Sand Sole food habits, instead supporting earlier data from [[Smith 1936]] showing Sand Sole as primarily piscivorous, corroborated by additional incidental sampling in Port Orchard, Golden Gardens, and Bellingham Bay.
- **No evidence of migration** disrupting seasonal interpretation: orderly progression of sexual maturity stages and stable abundance across months suggest a resident population, consistent with [[Manzer 1952]].
