# Effects of Chemical Treatment for Fish Eradication on Stream-Dwelling Invertebrates

#aquatic-invertebrates #macroinvertebrates #benthic-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study by Minckley and Mihalick (1981, *Journal of the Arizona-Nevada Academy of Science* 16:79-82) assesses how the piscicide [[Antimycin A]] affected stream-dwelling invertebrates in [[Ord Creek]], Apache County, Arizona. The chemical was applied at about 10 µg l⁻¹ on 31 August 1977 to remove introduced salmonids, as part of an effort to re-establish the native [[Apache Trout]]. Immediately after treatment, benthic numbers, biomass and taxon richness dropped sharply, and drifting invertebrates were killed and carried downstream. Three years later (September 1980), total numbers, biomass and diversity were broadly similar to pre-treatment levels, although the authors indicated possible loss of some taxa, notably caddisflies.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Chemical treatment is a management tool used to eradicate introduced fishes so that native species can be reintroduced. This report is part of a larger study on [[Ord Creek]] renovation (Rinne et al. 1981).
- **Study area:** Ord Creek flows north from [[Bull Cienega]] (elevation 2,650 m) to the North Fork of the White River in the [[Gila River Basin]] at about 2,000 m. The stream runs over gravel and boulder bottoms through alternating open cienegas and spruce-fir forest. Two reaches were sampled: the headwaters at Bull Cienega and a lower reach at [[Smith Cienega]].
- **Benthic sampling:** [[Surber Sampler]] collections were taken before (29-30 August 1977) and after treatment (31 August-1 September 1977, and September 1980). Each sample covered 929 cm² of substrate stirred to 10 cm depth. Samples were preserved in 5% formalin, sorted by [[Air Elutriation]] and hand picking, and identified with keys from Usinger (1956) and Pennak (1978).
- **Drift sampling:** [[Drift Sampling]] used 381 cm² nets at one-hour intervals. Each 15-minute sample covered 11 to 21 m³ of water. Samples taken during upstream disturbance, such as electrofishing, were discarded.
- **Treatment:** Antimycin A was distributed at 0900 hours on 31 August 1977, scaled to stream size and configuration.
- **Mesh size:** The authors note that different mesh sizes were used between years (10 threads cm⁻¹ in 1980 versus 30 cm⁻¹ for 1977 sampling), which they considered a possible source of differences in composition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure:** Taxa differed in susceptibility according to their substrate position. Burrowers (chironomids, oligochaetes), interstitial forms (heptageniid mayflies, nemourid stoneflies, dixid midges, tipulid craneflies, Hydracarina) and heavy-cased caddisflies (limniphilids) survived the day after treatment in the benthos. Free-living or exposed taxa suffered the greatest losses.
- **Affected taxa:** [[Baetid Mayflies]], chloroperlid stoneflies, [[Simuliidae]] (black flies) and hydropsychid caddisflies were the most heavily reduced. Essentially all benthic invertebrates in samples were moribund or dead.
- **Drift behaviour:** Exposed taxa were swept downstream, which raised drift counts in the lower reach. Interstitial forms such as [[Chironomid Dipterans]] remained in the benthos, dead but not transported.
- **Recovery context:** Long-lived caddisflies in Arizona streams are known to be severely reduced or extirpated by floods and recover slowly (Gray 1980, 1981; Gray and Fisher 1981; Minckley 1981). Extremely high runoff in 1978-79 may also have influenced the 1980 results.

## Key Findings

1. **Sharp short-term decline in benthos (Bull Cienega):** Numbers fell from 1,394 ± 537 to 298 ± 183 No./m², roughly a factor of 5. Taxon richness fell from 10.0 to 6.3 taxa/sample. The text reports a biomass reduction of almost 70 times.
2. **Smith Cienega benthos rose after treatment:** Numbers were 8.7 times higher (1,510 ± 488 before; 13,094 ± 12,433 after) and biomass 5.7 times higher (1.107 before; 6.351 after, as tabulated). The authors attribute this to downstream transport of moribund and dead invertebrates from upstream. Taxon richness rose from 9.3 to 14.7.
3. **Baetid mayflies dominated post-treatment samples:** They made up more than half the animals collected at Smith Cienega, while chironomids were greatly reduced in relative abundance.
4. **Drift surged after application:** In Smith Cienega, afternoon drift rose to almost 18-19 times the pre-treatment organism count and about 11 times the weight. The source text is partly garbled here, so these multiples should be checked against the original. Drift stayed high through the following night. By 1 September 1977, no living invertebrates were found in drift.
5. **Mortality was near total:** Essentially all organisms collected in the first day after treatment were dead or dying, both in drift and in the benthos.
6. **Three-year recovery:** In September 1980, the Smith Cienega fauna included 16 taxa. Six taxa recorded in 1977 were absent in 1980, and seven taxa recorded in 1980 had not been identified in 1977 (excluding "undetermined" categories). Numbers averaged lower than pre-treatment 1977 levels.
7. **Possible long-term taxonomic change:** Absence of hydropsychid and limniphilid caddisflies in 1980 may be significant, since long-lived caddisflies recover slowly. Diptera absent in 1980 (dixids, psychomyids, muscids) were judged more likely to reflect sampling error.
8. **Overall conclusion:** Antimycin A caused a dramatic, short-term loss of invertebrates. Long-term changes in total numbers, biomass and diversity were minimal, though some taxa may have been eliminated.
