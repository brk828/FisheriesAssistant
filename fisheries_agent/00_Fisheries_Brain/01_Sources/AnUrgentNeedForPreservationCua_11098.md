# Cuatro Cienegas: An Urgent Need for Preservation

#aquatic-ecology #botany #endemism

## Executive Summary

This feature article, by [[Donald J. Pinkava]] with a companion piece by [[Gary Paul Nabhan]], describes the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] in central [[Coahuila]], Mexico, a closed desert basin with unusually rich aquatic habitats and a high level of endemism. The authors argue that the basin's biota and its springs, marshes and dunes are threatened by grazing, canal construction, gypsum mining and logging, and that at least part of the area should be preserved. The second piece is a short note on experimental native-crop modules at La Sauceda, which the authors hope will relieve pressure on surrounding wildlands.

## Background & Description

- **Location:** The [[Bolson de Cuatro Cienegas]] lies in central Coahuila at the eastern edge of the [[Chihuahuan Desert]], about 270 air kilometers southeast of [[Big Bend National Park]].
- **Physical setting:** The town of Cuatro Cienegas de Carranza sits along the Rio Canon, between the Sierra de la Madera and the Sierra de la Purisima, San Vicente, Fragua and San Marcos ranges. Limestone cliffs of the Sierra de la Madera rise more than 2,350 m above the basin floor.
- **Hydrology:** The basin was long land-locked until canals were built to drain water out through Puerto Salada. Its aquatic features include lagunas, playas, rivers, subterranean tubes, thermal springs, artesian wells, posos (small aquatic habitats) and cienegas (marshes).
- **Access:** Mexico Highway 30 provides access, but the region was among the last parts of the desert to be explored by naturalists.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Playa grassland:** The saline basin floor supports grassland dominated by alkaline sacaton and salt grasses. Succession on the playa leads to saltbush stands, with mesquite where dunes or wetlands persist.
- **Posos:** Newly formed posos share the flora of surrounding grassland. As they mature, sedges, reeds (Phragmites, Spartina) and wildflowers colonize their banks, and aquatic plants such as water lily, bladderwort and the macroalga Chara appear. Shores of mature posos support mesquite, catclaw acacia, ash and black willow.
- **Gypsum dunes:** Gypsum blown from evaporated lakebeds forms white dunes, comparable to those of White Sands in New Mexico. Active dunes reach about six meters high. Mesquite, catclaw acacia and yuccas help stabilize them, and the stabilized dunes host several gypsum-loving endemic composites, including *Machaeranthera restiformis*, *M. gypsophila*, *Gaillardia gypsophila*, *Dyssodia gypsophila* and *Haploestes robusta*.
- **Desert scrub:** Bajadas and lower slopes carry Chihuahuan Desert scrub dominated by creosote bush, with agaves, yuccas, cacti and *Selaginella lepidophylla* (resurrection plant).
- **Chaparral and woodland:** Chaparral with oaks, pines and shrubs occupies protected arroyos, grading into oak-pinyon-juniper woodland, where the palm *Brahea berlandieri* occurs.
- **Montane forest:** Moist north-facing canyons of the upper sierras support Douglas-fir, fir, pines, Arizona cypress, maples, oaks and madrono.
- **Threats:** Goat and cattle grazing, canal construction, gypsum mining and conifer logging all put pressure on the ecosystem. The local human community is itself described as more than the marshland can withstand.

## Key Findings

- **Flora:** The known vascular flora of the Cuatro Cienegas region (over 2,000 km²) comprises 879 taxa, including 860 species in 456 genera from 114 families. It is among the most varied floras in the Chihuahuan Desert.
- **Endemism:** The flora includes 49 taxa with type localities in the region; of these, 45 are currently recognized and 23 are endemic. Additional taxa occur only here and in immediately adjacent areas.
- **Fauna:** Endemism is also documented in fishes, reptiles, snails and crustaceans, though the source gives no species counts for these groups.
- **Conservation call:** The authors contend that the uniqueness of the basin must be communicated to residents and visitors, and that at least a portion of Cuatro Cienegas should be preserved before this part of "man's heritage" is lost.
- **Experimental modules:** At La Sauceda, a module of native crops designed by young researchers at Mexico's Forestry Service (INIFAP) is intended to relieve pressure on surrounding wildlands. The source reports no outcome data.
