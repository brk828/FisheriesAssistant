# Near-Extinct Colorado Squawfish Taken in Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A large squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), a rare and nearly extinct member of the minnow family, was caught below [[Hoover Dam]] at [[Willow Beach Fish Hatchery]] and [[Lake Mohave]]. The fish was identified by a [[National Park Service]] naturalist. The article reviews historical records showing the species was once common throughout the [[Colorado River]] and its tributaries, but the last official Arizona record cited was from 1952. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] planned a collecting trip through the [[Grand Canyon]] gorge before [[Glen Canyon Dam]] changed water conditions.

## Background & Description

- **Common names:** Locally called "Colorado River salmon" or "squawfish," but it is not related to the salmon family.
- **Identification:** The specimen was a 17-pound, overgrown minnow. Staff at [[Willow Beach Fish Hatchery]] had no immediate idea what had been caught. [[Ted Whitmoyer]], a National Park Service naturalist from Boulder City, identified it.
- **Size record:** The largest squawfish on record measured 5 feet and weighed 100 pounds, making it the largest member of the minnow family known at the time.
- **Cultural and food role:** The fish was a major food source for Indigenous peoples living along the Colorado River. The article notes it is not considered a good eating fish, despite its size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historical range:** Historical records compiled by [[Dave Foster]] of the Arizona Game and Fish Department show squawfish were common along the Colorado River from Colorado to Yuma before dam construction. They were also abundant in the [[Gila River]] and [[Salt River]] and their irrigation ditches.
- **Habitat preference:** The fish favors turbulent, muddy water. The article suggests it may still survive in the Grand Canyon gorge.
- **Threats:** Dam construction changed the river's flow and water conditions. The article identifies the completion of Glen Canyon Dam as a point after which conditions would change further.
- **Management context:** Collecting the fish for scientific study was an urgent conservation priority because of its rarity.

## Key Findings

1. **Rarity:** The last official record cited for Arizona was from 1952. The species was described as nearly extinct.
2. **Former abundance:** Historical documents show squawfish were common throughout the Colorado River system and its tributaries before dams were built.
3. **Possible surviving population:** The species may still exist in the Grand Canyon gorge.
4. **Planned survey:** The Arizona Game and Fish Department planned a collecting trip using shockers and modern equipment to gather rare fish for scientific study before Glen Canyon Dam altered water conditions.
5. **Record size:** The largest recorded specimen was 5 feet long and weighed 100 pounds.
