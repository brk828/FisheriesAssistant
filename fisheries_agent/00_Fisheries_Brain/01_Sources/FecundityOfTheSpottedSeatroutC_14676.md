# Fecundity of the Spotted Seatrout (Cynoscion nebulosus) from the Lake Borgne Area, Louisiana

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sciaenidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 study by [[Sundararaj]] and [[Suttkus]] (Tulane University) estimated the fecundity of the [[Spotted Seatrout]] in Louisiana waters using the [[Gravimetric Method]]. Of 231 specimens collected in summer 1955 near the Rigolets and Lake Borgne, fecundity was found to increase with length and age, the ovaries outweighed the testes at comparable sizes above 280 mm total length, and spawning was concentrated in July. Age-groups II, III, and IV together supplied 91.9 percent of the eggs. The authors also found Louisiana fish more fecund at a given age than Texas fish reported by earlier workers.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Cynoscion Nebulosus]], an important commercial and game fish along the Gulf coast, belonging to the [[Sciaenidae]] (drums and croakers).
- **Prior knowledge:** Published fecundity data were scarce. Earlier work on Texas populations came from [[Pearson]] (1929) and [[Miles]] (1950, 1951), and [[Guest and Gunter]] (1958) summarized the life history.
- **Study context:** Material was collected during a biological study of [[Lake Pontchartrain]], a cooperative project of Tulane University and the Louisiana Wildlife and Fisheries Commission.
- **Sampling:** 231 specimens taken with trammel nets, seines, otter trawls, and angling.
- **Age determination:** Scales and otoliths were read three times per fish; the two methods agreed closely. Four age-groups (I–IV) made up most samples, with a few age-group V fish. Males were scarce among older groups, and no male exceeded 447 mm total length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning season:** Ovary and testis data, along with the appearance of young fish in the Lake Pontchartrain area in late July, indicated that spawning peaks in July, with spawning in Louisiana waters occurring in July and August. No May samples were taken, so a full statement was not possible.
- **Spawning pattern:** Spawning is extended over time, consistent with earlier reports ([[Welsh and Breder]] 1923; Pearson 1929; [[Gunter]] 1945; [[Moody]] 1950). Gunter (1945) suggested possible two spawning peaks in Texas waters; this study did not confirm that for Louisiana.
- **Eggbound females:** Gravid females collected in August and September may be eggbound and may resorb or void eggs without spawning. A similar phenomenon was noted by [[Sundararaj]] (1959) in the Indian catfish *Heteropneustes fossilis*.
- **Regional comparison:** Louisiana fish were more fecund than Texas fish at the same age (Table 2 of source). For example, Miles reported 300,000 eggs for average 3-year-olds and 560,000 for 4-year-olds, while this study recorded 354,325 and 660,960 for the corresponding groups (age-groups II and III).
- **Gonad comparison:** The ovary is heavier than the testis at comparable sizes beyond 280 mm. A similar pattern was reported for herring in the southern North Sea by [[Hickling]] (1940).

## Key Findings

- **Gravimetric method accuracy:** Estimates had an average error of 6.93 percent against actual egg counts on three ovaries (age-groups I–III). Stone (1937) reported 5.1 percent for ciscoes.
- **Fecundity scaling:** Fecundity increased with both age and length. Mean fecundity by age group (June 1955 samples, n = 28 females):

| Age group | Mean total length (mm) | Mean ovary weight (g) | Mean eggs |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 283 | 7.61 | 140,485 |
| II | 376 | 19.11 | 354,325 |
| III | 450 | 37.91 | 660,960 |
| IV | 504 | 39.72 | 1,144,492 |

- **Earlier estimates:** Pearson (1929) reported 427,819 eggs for a 480 mm fish and 1,118,000 for a 620 mm fish.
- **Egg contribution by age group:** Age-groups II, III, and IV supplied 91.9 percent of eggs. Age-group III contributed the largest share (40.6 percent), followed by age-group IV (26.8 percent) and age-group II (24.5 percent). Age-group I contributed very little (about 8.6 percent by the table's spawning-power calculation, judged negligible by the authors). Age-group IV was the most fecund per female but made up only 10.7 percent of the population.
- **Spawning peak:** Mean ovarian volume, egg diameter (from 25 large yolky eggs per ovary), and eggs per gram all dropped sharply in July across age-groups, indicating that most large yolky eggs are spawned in July. Age-group I ovarian volume changed little, suggesting these fish may not spawn.
- **Sex-related gonad weights:** Mean female gonad weights exceeded male weights above 280 mm total length. Male maximum size was smaller than female maximum size.
- **Overall conclusion:** Spotted Seatrout in the Lake Borgne area are highly fecund, spawn mainly in July in the study year, and depend chiefly on older, larger females (age-groups II–IV) for reproduction.
