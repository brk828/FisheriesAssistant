# Distribution of Québec Stoneflies (Plecoptera)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #plecoptera

## Executive Summary

This 1968 faunistic paper by [[Ricker]], Malouin, Harper and Ross compiles the distribution of stoneflies (order [[Plecoptera]]) in the Canadian province of [[Québec]]. The authors report 94 named species examined directly, one species cited from the literature, and two further undescribed or probably undescribed species, for a provincial total of 97. The work combines historical records from [[Gosse]] (1840) and [[Provancher]] (1871–1883) with modern collections from many named localities, and it resolves the nomenclature of Provancher's earlier names. Stoneflies are aquatic insects, not fishes; the paper addresses aquatic invertebrate fauna rather than fish.

## Background & Description

The first Québec record dates from 1840, when P. H. Gosse described stoneflies from the neighbourhood of Compton in Sherbrooke County, naming four species. Between 1876 and 1883 the Abbé [[Léon Provancher]] compiled a provincial list; the authors can identify 14 of his species among 10 genera, with representatives of one further genus. Many of Provancher's names had been synonymized or ignored, and none appeared in the 1925 [[Needham and Claassen Monograph]]. [[W. E. Ricker]] (1952) examined type material in the Musée Provincial du Québec and restored three names to use.

The paper gives taxonomic notes for Provancher's species (for example, *Pteronarcys dorsata*, *Taeniopteryx nivalis*, *Brachyptera glacialis*, *Nemoura venosa*, *Allocapnia pygmaea*, *Acroneuria lycorias*), explaining revised synonymies and nomenclatural problems such as nomina nuda. The authors also describe their collecting history, listing principal collections (Canadian National Collection, Université de Montréal, [[Illinois Natural History Survey]], Royal Ontario Museum) and major collecting areas, including the [[Eastern Townships]], [[Gatineau Region]], [[Île d'Orléans]], [[Parc du Mont Tremblant]], the [[North Shore of the Gulf of St. Lawrence]], and [[Ungava]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors describe the Québec fauna as largely part of a [[Great Lakes-St. Lawrence Fauna]] group, typically distributed from Minnesota to New Brunswick. South of the St. Lawrence River lies a strong [[Appalachian Element]], with species abundant in the mountains of New York and New Hampshire. Eastern Québec contains a few species characteristic of the Atlantic seaboard, and northern Québec has hardier Great Lakes representatives plus two endemic species.

Zoogeographic patterns are linked to glaciation. [[Hudson Bay]] acted as a barrier to eastward movement of four tundra and taiga species (*Nemoura arctica*, *Capnia nearctica*, *Diura bicaudata*, *Isoperla decolorata*), which invaded after the Wisconsin glaciation. The endemic *Capnia sugluka* is restricted to the south shore of Hudson Strait and is the only species whose known range lies entirely within Québec. A few western (Cordilleran) species, such as *Claassenia sabulosa*, have reached Québec. Many records are seasonal: spring species such as *Allocapnia* and *Taeniopteryx* appear in March–April, while summer species such as *Leuctra* and *Alloperla* occur in June–August.

## Key Findings

- **Provincial total:** 97 species (94 examined, one from literature, two undescribed or probably undescribed).
- **Earliest record:** Gosse (1840) named four species from Sherbrooke County; Provancher's compilation gave 14 identifiable species in 10 genera.
- **Nomenclature:** Ricker (1952) restored three Provancher names; several names are nomina nuda or synonyms (e.g., *Leuctra brunnea*, *Pteronarcys regalis* now a synonym of *P. dorsata*).
- **Largest species:** *Pteronarcys dorsata* is the largest Québec stonefly and is transcontinental, mainly in big rivers, occurring throughout the province.
- **Hudson Bay barrier:** Four tundra/taiga species did not cross into Québec, allowing temperate species to extend north.
- **Endemics:** *Capnia sugluka* (Ungava) is entirely within Québec; *Capnia labradora* is among the rarest, with the only other known site at Nain, Labrador.
- **Western species:** *Paraleuctra sara* in Québec is distinct from western specimens (Hanson, 1962), and *Isoperla longiseta* is an isolated record about 800 miles east of its nearest neighbour.
- **Appalachian element:** Species such as *Pteronarcys biloba*, *Peltoperla maria*, *Nemoura nigritta* and *Leuctra triloba* are characteristic of the southern Québec highlands.
- **Eastern seaboard species:** *Isogenus hansoni*, *Isoperla holochlora*, *Alloperla voinae*, *Alloperla naica* and *Hastaperla orpha* are associated with the Atlantic coast.
- **Collecting sites:** Approximately 20 Île d'Orléans streams were sampled by Malouin during 1954–59, and multiple sites in Gatineau Park and Parc du Mont Tremblant were surveyed in the 1950s–60s.
- **Northern survey:** The Northern Insect Survey sampled about 50 sites, twelve in northern Québec yielding stoneflies.
- **Species confusion:** *Isoperla montana* was confused with *I. bilineata*; the paper describes distinctive aedeagal sclerites to separate them.
- **Scope note:** The paper provides distributional records and taxonomic reconciliation only; it does not treat fish species.
