## Executive Summary

This 1961 study by Clark Hubbs and Neal E. Armstrong investigates the **minimum developmental temperature tolerance** of eggs from two anuran (frog/toad) species native to Travis County, Texas: **Scaphiopus couchi** (Couch's spadefoot toad) and **Microhyla olivacea** (Great Plains narrow-mouthed toad). Although the paper concerns amphibians rather than fish, it is explicitly framed as part of a comparative research program that parallels earlier developmental temperature tolerance work on **[[Etheostomatine Fishes]]** (darters, Percidae) by the same authors, making it a valuable cross-taxon reference for understanding thermal adaptation patterns in heterothermal (ectothermic) vertebrates. Eggs from freshly fertilized clutches were incubated across a temperature gradient (7°–29° C) in 250 ml beakers, and survival, developmental stage, and time-to-hatch were recorded. Results show *S. couchi* has a minimum developmental/hatching threshold near **15–16° C**, while *M. olivacea* requires a threshold roughly **2° C higher** (~17° C), consistent with each species' known minimum field breeding temperatures. The paper argues that species breeding in colder months or exploiting ephemeral (temporary) pools evolve correspondingly adjusted thermal tolerances and developmental rates.

## Background & Description

While this paper is developmental/physiological rather than morphological in focus, key life-history/embryological staging data are reported:

- **Developmental stages tracked**: first cleavage → middle/late cleavage → blastula → early/middle/late neurula → early/middle/late tail bud → hatching.
- **Fertilization temperatures**: *S. couchi* eggs fertilized at ~25° C; *M. olivacea* eggs fertilized at ~20° C.
- **Egg handling**: Eggs isolated into 250 ml beakers at first cleavage (or yolk plug stage for the July 11 *M. olivacea* sample) and exposed to randomized temperature treatments.
- **Hatching abnormalities**: Noted at borderline temperatures (e.g., 15° C for *S. couchi*, 17° C for *M. olivacea*), where a fraction of hatchlings were recorded as "abnormal."
- **Relative developmental rate**: *M. olivacea* hatching time is approximately **twice** that of *S. couchi* at any equivalent temperature; both species double their hatching time with a ~5° C temperature increase (a rough thermal developmental rate rule, akin to a Q10-type relationship).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/breeding strategy**: *S. couchi* is characterized by rapid development in **temporary (ephemeral) pools** (per Noble, 1931), consistent with its faster developmental rate at all tested temperatures relative to *M. olivacea*. This is an adaptive strategy for [[Ephemeral Pool Breeding]] amphibians, where rapid larval development minimizes desiccation risk.
- **Seasonal breeding window**: Both species breed in **spring and summer** in Travis County, Texas.
- **Field minimum breeding temperatures**:
  - *S. couchi*: 63° F (~17.2° C) (Wasserman, 1957)
  - *M. olivacea*: 65° F (~18.3° C) (Blair, 1961)
  - This ~2° C field difference closely matches the ~2° C difference in laboratory-measured minimum developmental temperatures, supporting a strong link between physiological tolerance and realized breeding phenology.
- **Comparative ecological pattern**: The authors invoke a broader principle established by Moore (1939) for [[Ranid Tadpoles]] and by Hubbs (1961) for [[Etheostomatine Fishes]] (Percidae): species or populations that breed in **colder months** exhibit **colder minimum developmental temperature tolerances** than related taxa breeding in warmer months. This cross-taxon consistency (anurans and freshwater darters) suggests a generalizable rule linking breeding phenology to thermal physiology in ectothermic vertebrates.
- **Geographic context**: All specimens were collected from Travis County, Texas (Hornsby's Bend locality for *S. couchi*), an inland central Texas freshwater/terrestrial system, not a marine or oceanic basin.

## Key Findings

- **Scaphiopus couchi** (Table 1):
  - Eggs held below **15° C** failed to hatch; complete failure to progress past first cleavage occurred at **10° C**.
  - Eggs between **11–15° C** reached progressively more advanced developmental stages but largely failed to hatch (with partial/variable hatching at 15° C, including abnormal hatchlings).
  - Eggs at **16° C and above** hatched successfully.
  - Time to hatch decreased sharply with increasing temperature: ~9–11 days near 15° C down to **1 day** at 24–28° C.
  - Minimum developmental/hatching threshold: **~15–16° C**.

- **Microhyla olivacea** (Table 2, combining June 20 "A" and July 11 "B" cohorts):
  - Eggs held below **17° C** failed to hatch; no development beyond initial stages occurred at **12° C or below**.
  - Eggs between **14–17° C** progressed closer to hatching with increasing temperature, with partial/abnormal hatching observed at 17° C.
  - Eggs at **18° C and above** hatched reliably.
  - Time to hatch decreased from ~6–13 days near 15–17° C down to **2 days** at 25–29° C.
  - Minimum developmental/hatching threshold: **~17° C**, approximately **2° C higher** than *S. couchi*.

- **Cross-species comparison**:
  - *M. olivacea* requires ~2° C higher minimum temperature for both development and hatching than *S. couchi*, mirroring the ~2° C difference in their documented minimum field breeding temperatures.
  - *M. olivacea* develops roughly **twice as slowly** as *S. couchi* at equivalent temperatures.
  - Both species exhibit an approximate **doubling of developmental rate per 5° C** temperature increase, a pattern consistent with typical ectotherm thermal performance curves.

- **Broader comparative implication**: The results reinforce a pattern also documented in [[Ranid Tadpoles]] (Moore, 1939) and [[Etheostoma Spectabile]] and other darters (Hubbs, 1961; Hubbs & Armstrong, unpublished ms.) — that species/populations breeding in cooler conditions or seasons possess correspondingly lower minimum developmental temperature tolerances, linking embryonic physiology directly to breeding ecology and phenology across disparate vertebrate taxa (amphibians and freshwater fishes).

- **Methodological caveat**: Temperature control equipment introduced fluctuations (±2° C at high temperatures, ±4° C at low temperatures, but as little as ±0.5° C in the 15–20° C range), meaning reported thresholds are approximate; however, both species were tested under identical conditions, preserving the validity of interspecific comparisons.
