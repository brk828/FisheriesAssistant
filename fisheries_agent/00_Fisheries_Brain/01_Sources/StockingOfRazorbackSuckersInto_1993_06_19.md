## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum, dated 19 June 1993, reporting on the first stocking event of the **Lake Havasu Native Fish Project**. On 18 June 1993, a total of 199 juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), sourced from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], were released. The primary stocking (n=198) occurred in a renovated backwater habitat on an island near "Site 6," with a single individual released directly into the open waters of [[Lake Havasu]]. All fish originated from the 1992 cohort and were tagged for future identification and monitoring purposes.

## Background & Description
Data was collected on two distinct groups of released fish, indicating variable growth rates within the same cohort year.

*   **Backwater Release Group (n=198):**
    *   **Average Total Length (TL):** 177.5 mm
    *   **Length Range:** 110 mm – 275 mm
    *   **Average Weight:** 68.4 g
    *   **Weight Range:** 20 g – 225 g
*   **Lake Havasu Direct Release (n=1):**
    *   **Total Length:** 320 mm
    *   **Weight:** 350 g
    *   *Note: This individual was significantly larger than the average backwater cohort, suggesting it may represent an outlier or faster-growing individual from the same 1992 cohort.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*   **Habitat Type:** The stocking targeted a **renovated backwater** environment located on an island adjacent to "Site 6" within the Lake Havasu system. Backwaters are critical nursery habitats for native Colorado River fishes, offering refuge from the main channel's flow and predation pressures found in open water.
*   **Release Strategy:** The bulk of the cohort (198/199) was placed in the sheltered backwater to maximize survival, contrasting with the single fish released into the main lake body, potentially testing survivorship in open, unprotected habitat.
*   **Pre-release Handling:** Fish were held in a **live car** (a floating holding pen) within the backwater prior to release to acclimate them and monitor for stress before final introduction into the habitat.
*   **Species Status:** The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large-river specialist endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], and this project reflects active recovery efforts for this federally listed species.

## Key Findings
1.  **Successful Initial Stocking:** The first stocking event for the Lake Havasu Native Fish Project was completed successfully on 18 June 1993, with no fish observed to be in visible stress at the time of release (~1000 hrs).
2.  **Tagging for Monitoring:** All 199 razorback suckers were **PIT-tagged** (Passive Integrated Transponder) prior to release, enabling future tracking of growth, movement, and survival rates critical for evaluating the [[Native Fish Project]]'s success.
3.  **Multi-Agency Collaboration:** The event involved significant interagency cooperation, including personnel from the [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] (including a Youth Conservation Corps crew), and the Parker Fish and Wildlife Field Office (Parker FRO).
4.  **Source Population:** The stocked fish were supplied by the **Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center**, highlighting the role of federal hatcheries in supplementing wild populations of endangered Catostomids.
5.  **Growth Variability:** The wide range in size (110 mm–320 mm) among fish from the same 1992 cohort suggests differential growth rates during the hatchery rearing period prior to stocking.
