## Executive Summary

This document is a 1987 permit request letter from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD), authored by Director Temple A. Reynolds and Fish Biologist Dean A. Hendrickson, addressed to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Office of Endangered Species. The letter requests authorization for staff of The Arizona Nature Conservancy (TANC) — specifically Brian and Holly Richter — to hold and display four federally endangered fish species at the **[[Hassayampa River Preserve]]** near Wickenburg, Arizona. The request is tied to a collaborative renovation project of **[[Palm Lake]]**, a pond on the preserve, intended to support both public display and species propagation/growout. The project involves coordination among AGFD, TANC, **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, and **[[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]**.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source document. The text is administrative/regulatory in nature (a permit request letter) rather than a scientific descriptive study. The four species referenced are identified only by common and scientific (binomial) names:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** — *Ptychocheilus lucius* (also known as Colorado pikeminnow)
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** — *Gila elegans*
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** — *Cyprinodon macularius*
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** — *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All four species are native, imperiled freshwater fishes historically associated with the **[[Colorado River Basin]]** and/or Gila River drainage in the arid Southwestern United States.
- The management strategy described involves an **ex situ conservation** approach: small founder populations were to be held initially in large aquaria within the Preserve office lobby as a **permanent public display**.
- A parallel **in situ / semi-natural component** was planned: following renovation, larger (unspecified) numbers of the same species would be stocked into **[[Palm Lake]]** for **growout and propagation** purposes, suggesting a captive breeding/refuge population strategy typical of endangered desert fish conservation programs.
- The project reflects a broader regional conservation network linking state wildlife agencies (AGFD), federal hatcheries (Dexter National Fish Hatchery), federal refuges (San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge), and private conservation organizations (The Nature Conservancy) — a multi-institutional model common in Southwestern desert fish recovery efforts.
- The Hassayampa River Preserve itself functions as a riparian oasis habitat in an otherwise arid landscape, making it a candidate refugium site for endangered desert fishes displaced from degraded or hydrologically altered native habitats.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory action requested**: AGFD formally requested a federal permit under endangered species regulations to allow TANC staff to hold/display four listed endangered fish species.
- **Species targeted**: *Ptychocheilus lucius*, *Gila elegans*, *Cyprinodon macularius*, and *Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis* — all species of significant conservation concern in the Colorado River and Gila River systems.
- **Two-phase conservation plan**:
  1. Immediate: small numbers displayed permanently in office aquaria.
  2. Future: larger numbers stocked into renovated **Palm Lake** for growout and propagation, pending completion of pond renovation.
- **Interagency collaboration**: Renovation of Palm Lake was a joint effort between AGFD, USFWS-affiliated hatcheries/refuges (Dexter National Fish Hatchery, San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge), and TANC.
- **Document significance**: This letter serves as primary historical/administrative evidence of early (1987) endangered desert fish conservation partnerships in Arizona, illustrating the use of nature preserves as both public education sites and propagation refuges for critically endangered Southwestern fishes.
