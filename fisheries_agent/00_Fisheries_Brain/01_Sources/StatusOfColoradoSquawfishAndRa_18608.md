# Status of Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker in the San Juan River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by Platania et al. (University of New Mexico and partner agencies) reports the status of two federally protected fishes, [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), in the [[San Juan River]]. Field work ran from May 1987 to October 1989 across a mainstem reach of about 320 river kilometers, from Farmington, New Mexico, to [[Lake Powell]], Utah. The study found a living, breeding population of Colorado squawfish, including the first young-of-year (YOY) records for the drainage, and the first mainstem record of razorback sucker, including a reproductively active male. The authors conclude that the San Juan is important to the survival of both endangered species and call for further work on spawning sites, nurseries, population size and genetic stock.

## Background & Description

**Colorado squawfish** is a large cyprinid listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (U.S. Department of the Interior, 1974). Its first San Juan specimen was collected in 1936 at Alcove Canyon, Utah, before inundation by [[Lake Powell]]. Later reports were sparse, relying on angler accounts and surveys tied to construction of [[Navajo Reservoir]]. The last documented record before this study was a 177 mm TL fish taken in April 1978, about 8 river kilometers downstream of Aneth, Utah. Repeated searches failed to find it, which led to reports of its extirpation from the river (Holden and Wick, 1982).

**Razorback sucker** was proposed as endangered in 1989 (U.S. Department of the Interior, 1989). Its first drainage record was two adults collected in 1976 from an irrigation pond near Bluff, Utah. The source and fate of those fish are unknown; they may have been introduced or may have entered the pond during a San Juan flood. Before this study, no verified riverine specimens existed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study area:** Mainstem San Juan River, Farmington, New Mexico, to Lake Powell (about 320 river km), spanning New Mexico, Colorado and Utah.
- **Sampling effort:** 37 sampling trips, May 1987 to October 1989.
- **Gear:** [[Raft Electrofishing]] of main-channel habitats (spring, summer, fall); [[Larval Fish Seining]] of backwaters (late summer and fall); [[Trammel Net]] sets, mainly at the Lake Powell inflow and used on a limited basis in riverine reaches.
- **Key habitats:** Low-velocity backwaters and inflow areas were used by larvae and YOY. The Lake Powell inflow zone and the [[Piute Farms Marina]] boat ramp held concentrations of adult razorback sucker.
- **Tributary confluences:** Records clustered near the [[Montezuma Creek]] and [[Mancos River]] confluences, which appear to be important nursery or staging points.
- **Permits and partners:** Funded by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] through contracts with the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Collecting was permitted by the [[Navajo Nation]] and USFWS.

## Key Findings

### Colorado Squawfish
- **Adults:** 12 adults were collected or observed. Ten were collected, measuring 539 to 780 mm TL and weighing 1.1 to 5.5 kg. They were found from 4.4 km below Shiprock, New Mexico, to Lake Powell.
- **Recapture:** A 615 mm TL fish taken on 7 April 1987 in the San Juan arm of Lake Powell was recaptured on 8 September 1987 (632 mm TL), about 145 river km upstream of its first capture point after night electrofishing.
- **Seasonality:** Adults were captured or seen in at least one month from April to October, except July.
- **Young:** 17 larval and YOY fish (26 to 39 mm TL) were collected in 1987, nine near the inflow area, six near the Montezuma Creek confluence and two near the Mancos River confluence. One 19 mm larva was taken in 1988 at river km 18.5. None were found in 1989.
- **Significance:** These are the first YOY records for the San Juan drainage. Together with several age classes and successful spawning, they confirm the species as a viable member of the river's fish fauna.

### Razorback Sucker
- **Lake Powell aggregation:** 18 adults (12 individual fish; six recaptured once) were taken between 20 March and 5 April 1987 at the Piute Farms Marina ramp. Eight ripe males expressed milt under light pressure, and four females appeared gravid.
- **1988 return:** 10 adults were collected from 20 to 30 March 1988, about half reproductively mature. Six were recaptures from 1987. The authors interpret the yearly concentration as a possible spawning aggregation or staging before migration to another site.
- **Riverine record:** A 571 mm TL tuberculate male was captured on 25 April 1988 near Bluff, Utah, at river km 146.7. It is the first mainstem San Juan record, about 147 river km above Lake Powell.
- **Larvae:** None were found among 292 larval fish collections.

### Conclusions and Recommendations
- The rediscovery of adult Colorado squawfish, with several age classes and evidence of spawning, re-establishes the San Juan as critical habitat for this endangered species.
- The presence of a ripe razorback sucker in the riverine reach indicates the species may spawn in the river itself.
- Further study is needed to locate spawning sites and larval nurseries, estimate population size, document seasonal movements, assess genetic stock, and identify factors limiting both populations.
