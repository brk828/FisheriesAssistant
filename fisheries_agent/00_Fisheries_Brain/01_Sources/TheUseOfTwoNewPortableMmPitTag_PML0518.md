# Portable 12-mm PIT Tag Detectors for Tracking Small Fish in Shallow Streams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cottidae

## Executive Summary

This methods study describes two prototype handheld detectors for reading small 12-mm [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tag|passive integrated transponder (PIT) tags]]. The prototypes were built from commercially available radio frequency identification (RFID) modules from [[EID Aalten]] (Netherlands) and [[Destron Fearing Corporation]] (USA). Maximum tag detection distances in air ranged from 17 to 36 cm, depending on the system and tag orientation. Field tests with wild [[Brown Trout]] and adult [[Slimy Sculpin]] showed that the detectors could find a large share of tagged fish in shallow streams without physically disturbing them. The authors position the devices as a practical alternative to [[Radiotelemetry]] for fish too small to carry transmitters. They also note that the detectors have clear limits with mobile or escape-prone fish and in dense cover.

## Background & Description

PIT tagging is a well-established method for marking individual fish. Its use in ichthyology has grown rapidly over the past two decades, mainly because of technological improvements (reviewed by [[Lucas and Baras 2000]]). Automated PIT interrogation systems have been used to study fish passing through fishways and to monitor fish moving past streamwide antennae. Continuously monitoring individual fish has also helped researchers study activity patterns and habitat selection.

Earlier work established several detector designs:

- **[[Flat-Bed Antenna]] design:** [[Armstrong et al. 1996]] developed a plate antenna laid on the stream bottom. Field biologists have since used it to study habitat use and movement in shallow streams. Later multipoint decoders allow several flat-bed antennas to be connected at once, which gives finer spatial and temporal resolution.
- **Portable detectors:** [[Roussel et al. 2000]] field-tested a portable system with a 60-cm-diameter antenna held above the stream surface. With 3.9-mm × 23.1-mm tags, its detection range was 70–100 cm.

Before these prototypes, no antenna could reliably detect 12-mm PIT tags. Researchers therefore had to electrofish and recapture fish before scanning them for tags. The study's objective was to build two portable 12-mm detectors with longer detection ranges and to test their efficiency on wild [[Brown Trout]] and adult [[Slimy Sculpin]].

**Prototype 1 (EID):** The antenna contained one exciter and two receiver coils in an octagonal, waterproof PVC box (50 × 40 × 3 cm) mounted on a 3-m aluminum pole. The reader had an LCD screen, data display and an audible buzzer. It was designed for Trovan PIT tags.

**Prototype 2 (Destron):** The antenna was a coil of wire in flexible plastic tubing (38 mm diameter), bent into an oval (33 × 27 cm) and mounted on a 1.65-m PVC pole. It was connected to a Destron FS2001/ISO reader with data logging and a beeping alarm. It used 12-mm tags from [[Biomark Inc]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study sites and conditions**

- **[[La Roche Brook]] (France):** A small second-order tributary of the Oir River, with a channel 1–4 m wide and mostly gravel and cobble substrate. Brown trout have been electrofished and individually PIT-tagged each spring and autumn since 1993 to estimate recruitment, movement and survival.
- **Kennebecasis River tributaries (Canada):** Three second-order tributaries were used for the Destron field tests. These were sampled in June 2003 and contained adult slimy sculpin.

**Trial conditions**

- For age-1 trout (26–27 May 2003), six 20-m sections were isolated with barrier nets. Mean water depth ranged from 9.4 cm to 13.4 cm and mean velocity from 20 to 38 cm/s, based on 25 randomly spaced points per section.
- For age-0 trout (22 July 2003), six sections were sampled. Mean depth ranged from 8.9 to 10.7 cm and velocity from 21 to 30 cm/s.
- Age-0 trout were mostly too small to tag. Only 20% were longer than 55 mm FL, the minimum tagging length.

**Behavioral and habitat constraints**

- **Escape behavior:** Fish that flee before the antenna reaches them are harder to detect. The authors identify this as a major limit on PIT detectors used with mobile fish.
- **Cover and vegetation:** Dense [[Riparian Vegetation]] and overhanging branches can prevent the operator from moving the antenna effectively. Complex cover such as undercut banks may also block the signal, though the authors observed that tags beneath such cover were still within detection limits.
- **Nocturnal and sedentary species:** Sculpins and other benthic fishes, such as Cottidae, Percidae, Cobitidae and Petromyzontidae, often have restricted movements, are nocturnal, or hide under stones when startled.
- **Handling effects:** Radiotelemetry transmitters are too large for small fish, and trailing an antenna can cause behavioral effects (Bridger and Booth 2003).

## Key Findings

**Detection range (in air)**

- The [[EID Aalten]] prototype detected tags at the distances reported for the study's range. The paper does not give a precise value for this prototype in the extracted text.
- The [[Destron Fearing Corporation]] prototype's range varied from 30 to 36 cm with tag orientation.
- Across both systems, maximum tag detection distances were 17 to 36 cm.
- Reading performance was not affected in water. Tags were easily read through a few centimetres of substrate.

**Field detection efficiency** (mean values as reported; the authors did not run a formal comparison of the two prototypes)

| Target group | Detector | Mean detection efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Age-1 [[Brown Trout]] (FL 148 ± 26 mm) | EID | 69% |
| Age-0 [[Brown Trout]] (FL 72 ± 8 mm) | EID | 82% |
| Adult [[Slimy Sculpin]] (TL 74 ± 9 mm) | Destron | 82% |

- Efficiency was higher for age-0 than for age-1 trout. The authors attribute this to the greater mobility and wariness of age-1 fish, which were frightened by the operator and fled before detection.
- Each section was surveyed three times to estimate variation in detectability between surveys.

**Methods and handling**

- Fish were anaesthetized in eugenol (0.04 mL/L) and measured to the nearest millimetre. Untagged fish were injected with 11.5-mm PIT tags into the peritoneal cavity.
- After recovery, groups of 10 tagged trout were released into each section and given 30 min to adjust (15 min in section S1).

**Conclusions and recommendations**

- The new portable 12-mm detectors are a workable option for tracking small fish where radiotelemetry is impractical, such as in age-0 salmonids and small benthic species.
- In streams narrower than about 5 m, portable detectors can cover distances quickly. Used alongside habitat measurements, they can give information on habitat use and hiding behaviour without handling fish.
- The method works best with fish that are not highly mobile or escape-prone. It is less suited to dense riparian cover, since this restricts antenna placement.
- Portable readers can also complement [[Stationary Antenna Systems]] and automated PIT interrogation systems, which provide more accurate records of fish position and movement.
- The authors did not test how habitat characteristics affect detection efficiency. They suggest that future work should examine this, along with detection of fish sheltering beneath cover.
