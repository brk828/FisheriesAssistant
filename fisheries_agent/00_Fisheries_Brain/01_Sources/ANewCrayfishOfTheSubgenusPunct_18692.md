# A New Crayfish of the Subgenus Puncticambarus from the Savannah River Drainage, with Notes on Cambarus (P.) reburrus Prins

#decapoda #astacidae #cambarus

## Executive Summary

This 1972 taxonomic paper by [[Rudolph Prins]] and [[Horton H. Hobbs Jr.]] (Western Kentucky University and [[Smithsonian Institution]]) describes a new crayfish, [[Cambarus Chaugaensis]], from headwater streams of the [[Savannah River]] in South Carolina. It is the second member of the subgenus [[Puncticambarus]] known from Savannah River headwaters. The paper also reports new localities for [[Cambarus Reburrus]] from the [[French Broad River]] drainage in North Carolina, and argues that the single known Savannah River population of *C. reburrus* is probably introduced, most likely from bait-bucket releases.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context**
- Prins (1968) originally described [[Cambarus Reburrus]] from a single tributary of the Horsepasture River, from [[Sapphire Lake]] in Jackson County, North Carolina. The type-locality is the only known site outside the French Broad drainage.
- Unpublished collections by Gregory L. Dougherty from the Black Mountain area (Buncombe County, NC) extended the known range of *C. reburrus* into the French Broad system.
- Juvenile specimens tentatively assigned to *Cambarus acuminatus* Faxon, 1884, from three additional French Broad localities were later reconsidered. The synonymy shows that most earlier *C. acuminatus* records from Asheville and Black Mountain were misidentifications of *C. reburrus*.

**Description of Cambarus chaugaensis (new species)**
- Diagnosis highlights:
  - Areola 2.2 to 3.2 times longer than wide, comprising 29.3 to 35.5% of carapace length (only one individual above 34.8%), with 5 to 8 punctations across its narrowest part.
  - Rostrum with gently convergent margins and no marginal spines or tubercles.
  - Chela palm width greater than 1.3 times mesial margin length, with two rows of tubercles on the mesial palm (mesial row of 5 to 7).
  - First pleopod of Form I males with a moderately long, corneous central projection recurved at about 90 degrees.
- Holotype (Form I male) measurements: carapace height 11.0 mm, width 15.3 mm, length 28.7 mm; abdomen length 32.1 mm; areola 2.7 times longer than wide, 32.3% of carapace length.
- Allotype (female) carapace: height 11.0 mm, width 14.6 mm, length 27.7 mm (as given in Table 1).
- Morphotype (Form II male) carapace: height 11.6 mm, width 16.6 mm, length 30.4 mm.
- Form II males differ from Form I males by weaker telson spines, a strongly acute suborbital angle, and reduced hooks on the third pereiopod ischia.
- Color (preserved): olive green dorsally fading to light green laterally and ventrally, with dark olive brown to black mottling; fingertips yellowish to red; ventral surfaces cream-colored.

**Type Material and Specimens**
- Holotype male, allotype female, and morphotype male are deposited at the Smithsonian Institution (catalog nos. 131926, 131927, and 131928).
- Paratypic series: 25 Form I males; 56 Form II males; 45 females; 75 juvenile males; 62 juvenile females; 11 ovigerous females.
- Size range: largest Form I male 38.9 mm carapace length; smallest Form I male 25.1 mm; largest female 38.0 mm; largest ovigerous female 34.7 mm; smallest ovigerous female 25.2 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type-locality (Cambarus chaugaensis)**
- Chauga River at Cassidy Bridge (off County Road 290), Oconee County, South Carolina, in the upper southwestern corner of the state, within the headwaters of the Savannah River.
- The Chauga River now drains into [[Lake Hartwell]], a relatively new impoundment.
- At the type-locality the stream is about 40 feet wide and 3 feet deep in mid-channel during modal flows, with substrates dominated by boulders and rocks.

**Distribution of Cambarus chaugaensis**
- Specimens were examined from 18 localities, all in tributaries of the Savannah River in Oconee County, SC. These include the Chauga River, Ramsey Creek, Village Creek, West Village Creek, Cane Creek, Cedar Creek, Seneca Creek, Rocky Fork Creek, Little Cane Creek, Colonels Fork, the Whitewater River, and streams near Jocassee and Long Creek.

**Distribution of Cambarus reburrus**
- Savannah River population: known only from the Sapphire Lake tributary (type-locality), which the authors consider probably introduced.
- French Broad River drainage: several localities near Black Mountain (Buncombe County), 2.1 miles east of Swannanoa (Buncombe County), 3.5 miles west of Old Fort in Pisgah National Forest (Madison County, where specimens are described as atypical and tentatively assigned), and the Davidson River at the junction of U.S. Hwys. 64 and 276 (Transylvania County).

**Life History Notes**
- Form I males were collected in March, May, July, October, and November.
- Ovigerous females were found in April and May.
- Dougherty's *C. reburrus* Form I males from Buncombe County were collected in February, March, and July. Prins's type-series males were collected in April, May, and November.
- Based on the size ranges examined, *C. chaugaensis* probably has a life cycle virtually identical to that of *Cambarus longulus* Girard as described by [[Smart 1962]].

## Key Findings

1. **New species described.** [[Cambarus Chaugaensis]] is a new member of subgenus *Puncticambarus* from the upper Savannah River (Chauga River and tributaries, Oconee County, SC).
2. **Diagnostic traits.** The species is characterized by a broad, punctate areola (2.2 to 3.2 times longer than wide; 29.3 to 35.5% of carapace length), a tapering rostrum without marginal spines, small cervical spines, and a chela with a shorter, broader palm.
3. **Subgeneric placement is ambiguous.** Because the chela has fewer than 8 mesial tubercles, the species keys to subgenus *Cambarus* under [[Hobbs 1969]]'s key. The broad punctate areola and tapering rostrum, however, separate it from that subgenus, supporting placement in *Puncticambarus*.
4. **Closest relatives.** It most resembles *C. acuminatus*, [[Cambarus Reburrus]], *C. robustus* Girard (1852), and *C. veteranus* Faxon (1914). It differs from *C. acuminatus* by a distinct suborbital angle and weaker cervical spine; from *C. reburrus* by a shorter, more tapering rostrum, weaker cervical spines, and a more depressed chela with a shorter, broader palm; and from *C. robustus* and *C. veteranus* by an areola less than 3.5 times longer than wide (versus greater than 3.5).
5. **Range of Cambarus reburrus.** The species is widely distributed in the French Broad drainage, and the Savannah River record is isolated. The authors conclude that the Savannah River population is probably introduced, likely from discarded bait-bucket contents, since the type-locality is a short tributary below an impoundment.
6. **Misidentifications corrected.** Most historical *C. acuminatus* records from the Swannanoa River at Asheville and Black Mountain are misidentifications, and are now assigned to *C. reburrus*.
7. **Life history.** Reproductive and seasonal patterns indicate a life cycle similar to *Cambarus longulus* (Smart 1962), with ovigerous females recorded in April and May.
