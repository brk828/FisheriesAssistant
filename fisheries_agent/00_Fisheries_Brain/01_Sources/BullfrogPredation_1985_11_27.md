## Executive Summary
This 1985 Nevada Department of Wildlife memorandum documents a stomach content analysis of fifty-one American bullfrogs (*Lithobates catesbeianus*) collected from three ponds at Corn Creek, located within the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Desert Game Range. The study was conducted to assess the level of bullfrog predation on the endangered [[Pahrump killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos latos*), a critically restricted, range-endemic desert fish. The analysis, described as crude (no dissecting microscope used), found **no fish remains** in any of the fifty-one stomachs examined, suggesting minimal adult killifish predation by bullfrogs at the time of sampling. The memo explicitly notes that a more rigorous methodology would be required to assess predation on juvenile killifish life stages.

## Background & Description
No morphological data on *Empetrichthys latos latos* itself is presented in this document. The source instead describes gross stomach content composition of the bullfrog predator sample:
- **Sample size:** 51 bullfrog stomachs
- **Contents identified (macroscopic level only):**
  - Organic/detrital material
  - Beetles (Coleoptera)
  - Dragonflies (Odonata)
  - Large moths (Lepidoptera)
  - Grasshoppers (Orthoptera)
  - Tadpoles and small frogs (intraguild/cannibalistic predation)
  - One notable outlier: an adult kangaroo rat (*Dipodomys* sp.) found in one large male bullfrog's stomach
- **Fish remains:** Absent from all 51 samples examined.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Corn Creek's three isolated ponds constitute critical, restricted habitat for the endangered [[Pahrump killifish]], part of the broader [[desert spring endemism]] pattern seen throughout the Mojave Desert's relict pluvial-lake drainage systems.
- **Introduced predator pressure:** The American bullfrog is a non-native, opportunistic generalist predator introduced into these desert spring systems, posing a recognized conservation threat to native, range-restricted fish populations such as [[Empetrichthys latos]] subspecies.
- **Trophic breadth of bullfrog diet:** The recovered prey spectrum (invertebrates, small vertebrates, and even conspecific anurans) illustrates the bullfrog's role as a broad, opportunistic apex predator within these small pond ecosystems, capable of exerting predation pressure across multiple trophic levels.
- **Life-stage vulnerability:** The memo raises the unresolved ecological question of differential predation risk — adult killifish appear to escape detectable bullfrog predation, but juvenile killifish (smaller, more vulnerable, potentially less easily identified in gut contents) remain an unassessed risk category.

## Key Findings
- **Zero incidence** of identifiable fish remains across all 51 bullfrog stomach samples examined on November 26, 1985.
- This result suggests a **low level of bullfrog predation on adult** [[Pahrump killifish]] at Corn Creek during the sampling period (late September 1985).
- The dietary analysis was **methodologically limited** (no microscopic examination), which constrains confidence in ruling out predation on small or heavily digested prey items, including juvenile fish.
- The single kangaroo rat finding demonstrates bullfrogs' capacity to prey on small terrestrial vertebrates, underscoring their generalist, opportunistic predatory ecology within this desert refugial habitat.
- The memo explicitly calls for **more sophisticated diet analysis** (e.g., microscopic gut content examination, possibly serological or genetic methods) to properly evaluate bullfrog impacts on juvenile killifish survival — a critical conservation data gap given the species' [[endangered species]] status and extremely restricted range.
- This document functions as a baseline management record supporting ongoing monitoring of [[invasive species impacts]] on endemic desert fish conservation at U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Desert National Wildlife Range properties.
