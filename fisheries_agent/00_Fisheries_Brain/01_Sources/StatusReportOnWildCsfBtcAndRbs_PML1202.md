# Status Report on Wild Colorado Squawfish, Bonytail, and Razorback Sucker at Dexter NFH

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] reports the transfer of six wild-origin native fish into a new pond at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], New Mexico. The group comprised two [[Colorado Squawfish]] and three [[Razorback Sucker]] from the [[Colorado River]], Colorado, and six [[Bonytail]] from [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. The memo is addressed to the Regional Director, Region 2, and the hatchery manager. Most fish are in good to excellent condition and free of external parasites. One older, blind razorback sucker is in fair-poor condition and may not survive long. A treatment series with [[Masoten]] appears to have cleared a prior [[Lernaea]] infestation.

## Background & Description

The document is a short status memorandum, not a research study. It lists the species, origin, and number of fish moved, and gives a brief health assessment. It identifies no sampling design, measurements, or date of transfer.

- **Colorado Squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*):** two individuals, both in excellent condition. Also called Colorado pikeminnow.
- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*):** three individuals. Two are in excellent condition. The third, an older blind fish, is in fair-poor condition.
- **Bonytail (*Gila elegans*):** six individuals, all in excellent condition.

Total fish transferred: 11 (2 + 3 + 6).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo does not describe habitat or water chemistry. It does note that the fish came from two separate wild sources: the Colorado River in Colorado and Lake Mohave on the Arizona-Nevada border. Both are large western North American waters, and the species named are native to the Colorado River system. Holding them in a hatchery pond suggests a conservation or propagation program, but the memo does not state the program's purpose.

Health-related observations:
- All fish were clean and free of external parasites.
- A [[Lernaea]] (anchor worm) problem was present before the transfer. Treatment with [[Masoten]] (trichlorfon) appears to have resolved it.

## Key Findings

1. **Transfer completed:** 11 wild native fish (2 Colorado Squawfish, 3 Razorback Sucker, 6 Bonytail) were moved into a new pond at Dexter NFH.
2. **Overall condition is good:** 10 of 11 fish are in excellent condition. The Colorado Squawfish and Bonytail are all excellent, and 2 of 3 Razorback Suckers are excellent.
3. **Principal concern:** the older, blind razorback sucker is in fair-poor condition and is not expected to live much longer.
4. **Parasite control:** no external parasites were found, and the prior Lernaea infestation appears to have been cleared by the Masoten treatment series.
5. **Reporting:** the memo is a status update to the Regional Director, with copies to a broad list of hatchery and Service staff.
