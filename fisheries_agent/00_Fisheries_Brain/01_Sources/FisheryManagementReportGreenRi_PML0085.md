# Green River Fishery Survey, Utah (April 1962)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1962 inter-office transmittal from Robert L. Azevedo, Fishery Management Biologist, to Jack Hemphill, Regional Supervisor of the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] (BFMS), forwarding a Fishery Management Report on a float survey of the [[Green River]] in Utah. The survey ran from April 16 to 18, 1962, covering roughly 173 river miles from Jensen, Utah, to Green River, Utah. Its purpose was to document the fish fauna of this relatively inaccessible reach in support of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] and the [[Flaming Gorge Fish Eradication]] program.

Conditions were difficult: the river and most tributaries were turbid and high, and debris was heavy. Hook-and-line and set lines worked on the main river, while [[Gill Net]] sets largely failed. [[Rotenone]] applied at tributary mouths gave the best results. Ten fish taxa were collected, and preserved specimens were kept for later verification at [[Utah State University]].

## Background & Description

**Survey purpose and context.** The survey gathered baseline information on fish in a stretch of the Green River that was hard to reach by road. It was tied to the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] and the Flaming Gorge fish eradication effort.

**Personnel and logistics.** Two boat guide operators, Messrs. Reed and Pikej, supplied two rubber boats and motors. Mr. Dessler, an experienced float-boat operator on the [[Snake River]] in Wyoming and a trained fisheries biologist, contributed technical expertise. Mr. Ton Chapoose of the [[Ute Tribe]] Game Management Department assisted and represented the tribe. The [[Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation]] parallels the Green River on the left bank for about 62 river miles. Fish Hatchery personnel from Bibloy? (OCR-uncertain) also assisted.

**Route.** The float covered the lower Green River from Jensen to Green River, Utah. Tributary mouths and lower reaches were checked, including the [[Duchesne River]], [[White River]], [[Price River]] and a number of smaller creeks and washes.

**Reporting.** The memo asks for the report to be typed and duplicated for distribution to the Director, the Regional Director and the Project Leader.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water conditions.** The Green River was quite turbid near the inches and carried heavy debris. Most major tributaries were turbid and high, and fish sampling was difficult and at times impossible. Of the 21 locations checked, conditions were recorded as high, turbid, very high or muddy at most. Several sites were dry or had no sample attempted.

**Sampling constraints.** Field conditions shaped the methods:
- Gill nets set in the main river and at tributary mouths produced little or no catch. One net at the mouth of the Duchesne River was buried in silt, and another set near Tabyago Creek filled with moss and river debris.
- Hook-and-line and set lines were the only practical methods on the main river.
- Rotenone, applied by a drip method upstream of tributary mouths, gave the best returns.

**Sampling locations.** About 21 locations were checked, including the Ashley Creek, Duchesne River, White River, Willow Creek, Desert Spring Wash, Sand Wash, Ninemile Creek, Tabyago Creek, Jack Creek, Cedar Ridge Canyon, Flat Creek Canyon, Rock Creek, Chandler Creek, Florence Creek, the main river, Range Creek, Coal Creek, Rattlesnake Creek, Price River and the Green River Dam area. Several were over-night stops.

## Key Findings

**Methods and success**
- Best results came from [[Rotenone]] on several tributaries, which were recorded as "results good."
- Rotenone at other sites and gill nets were recorded as "results poor" or "no results."
- Hook-and-line gave good results at some main-river sites and fair results at others.
- Specimens were classified quickly in the field and may not be fully accurate. Preserved specimens in 10 percent formalin were sent to [[Utah State University]] for definitive identification by Dr. Ivan R. Franklin, Unit Leader, Cooperative Fishery Unit, Logan, Utah.

**Fish taxa recorded (10 taxa, OCR-corrected names)**
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*): preserved specimens
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*): preserved specimens
- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*): recorded
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): specimens not preserved
- Green River Spring Dace (*Rhinichthys osculus yarrowi*): preserved specimens
- [[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*): recorded, identification uncertain (marked "?")
- Bluehead Sucker (*Pantosteus delphinus*): preserved specimens
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): recorded, a specimen escaped hook-and-line capture

**Distribution and plans**
- The main river and lower reaches were sampled from Jensen to Green River.
- The report and specimen verification were planned as follow-up work at Utah State University.

**Administrative notes**
- The transmittal memo is dated April 25, 1962, and was forwarded through the Regional Supervisor's office in Springville, Utah.
- The survey report was to be typed and duplicated for distribution.
