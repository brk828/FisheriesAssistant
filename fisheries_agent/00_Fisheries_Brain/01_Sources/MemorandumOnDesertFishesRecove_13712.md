# Memorandum on Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting, 31 July - 1 August 1986

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-conservation #desert-fishes #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a **procedural memorandum**, not a primary research paper, issued by W. L. Minckley on behalf of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology) to coordinate an upcoming meeting near Pinetop, Arizona (31 July – 1 August 1986). Rather than presenting original morphological or ecological data, the memo outlines the administrative and scientific agenda for multi-species conservation planning of imperiled southwestern desert fishes. Key business items include finalization of the [[San Bernardino Recovery Plan]], coordination on [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery in California, planning of recovery plans for [[Gila Ditaenia]] (Sonora chub), [[Meda Fulgida]] (spikedace), and [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (loach minnow), and detailed discussion of the ecology and recovery potential of the [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), including a planned field visit to its habitat.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source. The memorandum is administrative in nature and focuses on recovery planning logistics rather than species descriptions. ## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although data are sparse, the memo implies key habitat and ecological contexts for the species under discussion:

- **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*): Inhabits streams of the Little Colorado River drainage, Arizona. Team members were scheduled to travel to and view the habitat firsthand, underscoring the site-specific, spring/stream-dependent nature of this species. Its recovery plan was slated for early 1987, requiring prior discussion of its ecology, decline, and potentials for recovery.
- **[[San Bernardino Recovery Plan]] taxa**: Associated with spring/wetland habitats in the San Bernardino Valley (Arizona/Sonora borderlands), a locale historically significant for endemic desert fish and invertebrate communities. The plan's narrative emphasizes **community-level management** rather than single-species recovery, reflecting an ecosystem-based conservation philosophy.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): Discussed relative to California's initiating recovery activities, implying a distribution across the Colorado River mainstem and associated impounded/riverine habitats spanning multiple states.
- **[[Gila Ditaenia]]** (Sonora chub): Native to the Rio Magdalena and California Gulf drainages of southern Arizona/Sonora; recovery plan preparation was being scheduled at this meeting.
- **[[Meda Fulgida]]** (spikedace) and **[[Tiaroga Cobitis]]** (loach minnow): Both associated with Gila River basin tributaries; noted here in context of impending or completed federal listing under the Endangered Species Act, prompting urgent recovery plan development.

## Key Findings
- The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] convened to review outstanding **action items** from a prior meeting in Tucson and assess their outcomes.
- A major agenda item was the **critique and finalization of the San Bernardino Recovery Plan**, with explicit instructions to team members to evaluate:
  1. Grammatical/typographical accuracy.
  2. Philosophical framing of "species' recovery" versus **community-level management and maintenance in perpetuity** — an early articulation of ecosystem-based recovery planning in desert fish conservation.
  3. **Economic, agency, and practical feasibility** of proposed recovery actions.
- California's **initial razorback sucker recovery activities** were to be discussed and integrated into the broader interstate recovery strategy for *Xyrauchen texanus*.
- Formal recognition of new **listing actions**: *[[Meda Fulgida]]* was already listed, and *[[Tiaroga Cobitis]]* listing was "imminent" — triggering the need for dedicated recovery plans for both.
- Plans were set to begin drafting a recovery plan for **[[Gila Ditaenia]]** (Sonora chub), including timing and team member assignments.
- The **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]** recovery plan was slated for development in early 1987, with the meeting dedicating time to its ecology, causes of decline, and recovery potential, followed by an in-person **field examination of its habitat** — reflecting a hands-on, field-verification approach to recovery planning within the Recovery Team's process.
- The memo illustrates the **institutional and procedural machinery** behind U.S. desert fish conservation efforts under the Endangered Species Act during the mid-1980s, coordinated through Arizona State University and involving multiple federal/state agency personnel and consultants.
