# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Interim Flow Monitoring Work Plan (Draft #5)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This draft work plan, dated November 23, 1991, sets out the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) approach for measuring the downstream effects of the [[Interim Flows]] released from [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The plan is a framework rather than a detailed study design. It defines four evaluations (flow characteristics, resource responses, exception-criteria impacts, and costs), nine monitored resource components, reporting schedules, and common data elements. Specific study plans and the budget (Enclosure 6) were still pending. The plan was circulated by the GCES Program Manager to the Interim Flow Monitoring Work Group for comment by December 6, 1991, before finalization for the [[Cooperating Agencies]].

## Background & Description

The document is a memorandum and draft plan, not a research study. It includes a distribution list of roughly 17 agency and academic recipients (including Arizona State University, Utah State University, the [[National Park Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Geological Survey]], and the [[Hualapai Tribe]]), a table of contents, and several enclosures and exhibits.

Key background points:

- **Interim Test Flows** began August 1, 1991. Exhibit D, however, dates implementation of the test criteria to July 30, 1991. The discrepancy is not resolved in the text.
- **Interim Flows** took effect November 1, 1991, and remain in place until the Record of Decision for the [[Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement]] (GCD-EIS), scheduled for December 1993.
- The stated purpose of the interim flows is to "minimize the loss of the natural resources in the Grand and Glen Canyon" until a longer-term solution is defined.
- The program has three phases: interim test flow monitoring (Aug 1–Nov 1, 1991), interim flow monitoring (Nov 1, 1991 to the end of the EIS process), and long-term monitoring after the EIS.

**Exhibit D: Interim Operating Criteria** (summarized):

| Parameter | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Maximum flow | 20,000 cfs |
| Minimum flow | 5,000 cfs at night; 8,000 cfs from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. (shifted to 8 a.m.–8 p.m. in winter) |
| Ascending ramp rate | 8,000 cfs over 4 hours, not to exceed 2,500 cfs/hour |
| Descending ramp rate | 1,500 cfs/hour |
| Daily fluctuation | 5,000 cfs (monthly volume under 600,000 af); 6,000 cfs (600,000–800,000 af); 8,000 cfs (over 800,000 af) |

The maximum flow may be evaluated and possibly increased in years when Lower Basin deliveries exceed 8.23 maf.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan treats the Colorado River below Glen Canyon Dam as a regulated system in which flow变化 drives sediment transport, beach and bar stability, aquatic food production, riparian vegetation, and fish habitat. Figure 1 in the document is a conceptual diagram linking managed control variables (water releases, ramping rates, flow minima, water quality) to response variables (beach and bar dynamics, aquatic food base, riparian vegetation, trout and native fish populations, recreation). It also lists endangered species, including the [[Humpback Chub]].

**Critical periods (Exhibit E)** identify when deviations from interim criteria carry the greatest risk:

- *Below minimum flow:* trout adult and egg stranding (December–March); [[Cladophora]] freezing (December–February) and desiccation (June–August); native fish larval stranding (May–August); vegetation desiccation (May–September).
- *Above maximum flow:* vegetation and insect flooding (May–September); waterfowl and passerine nest flooding (May–June); native fish washed out of backwaters (March–October); reptile flooding (May–September).

## Key Findings

**Monitoring framework and components**

- Four primary evaluations: (1) flow characteristics; (2) resource responses; (3) impacts of exception-criteria departures; (4) costs of the interim flows, the monitoring program, and effects on concessionaires and the public.
- Nine monitored components: sediment budget and deposits; cultural resources; aquatic food base; vegetation and marshes; economics; trout population dynamics; native fish; recreation and accident rates; and water quality.
- The plan is built on the existing GCES Phase II Integrated Research Program. It is designed to be refined every six months, and the Long-Term Monitoring Program will build on it.
- Monitoring is designed for "more data rather than not enough," because immediate impacts are hard to assess until the system responds.

**Flow characteristics and exceptions**

- Weekly evaluation of daily flow range, maximum and minimum flows, and ramping rates from Glen Canyon Dam records. Monthly statistical summaries are correlated with monthly release volume.
- The SCADA (System Coordination and Data Acquisition) records at Glen Canyon Dam are the official measure of releases. USGS will correlate its gage below the dam with SCADA data.
- Exception criteria are logged weekly and summarized monthly. Western and Reclamation must communicate with the GCES office when exceptions occur.

**Resource-specific methods (examples)**

- *Sediment:* annual aerial photography of deposits above a constant 5,000 cfs; topographic surveys of at least 20 sand deposits; sand flux measurements at five monumented sections; remote cameras at selected beaches.
- *Cultural resources:* 60 sites selected for monitoring, in three groups: 20 under direct threat (DT, drawn from 44 critically endangered sites), 20 under indirect threat (IT, drawn from 179 sites), and 20 in no apparent danger (NT, drawn from 272 sites). Remote cameras will monitor at least 6 DT sites continuously. A survey of the remaining 37 miles of lower Grand Canyon (Separation Canyon to Lake Mead) is planned.
- *Aquatic food base:* periphyton colonization and primary productivity (quarterly); organic drift over 24-hour cycles (monthly); invertebrate colonization and mortality, above and below Lee's Ferry (quarterly).
- *Trout:* stranding assessment at Lee's Ferry spawning sites; redd counts during spawning season; quarterly population dynamics using length frequency; trout diet over 24-hour cycles compared with drift availability.
- *Native fish:* backwater surveys monthly using video and data loggers; tributary mouth monitoring at the Paria, Shinumo, Clear, Crystal, Little Colorado, Kanab, and Nankoweap; relative abundance per reach; a fishery survey from Diamond Creek to Lake Mead.
- *Recreation:* accident evaluations at rapids including Crystal, Hance, Lava, and House Rock; interviews with Lee's Ferry guides and river guides; quarterly fishing success surveys.
- *Water quality:* seasonal monitoring of dam releases, Lee's Ferry, major tributaries, and bacteriological contamination.
- *Economics:* monthly assessment of power purchase and capacity costs; annual evaluation of recreation revenue.

**Reporting schedule**

- Quarterly synopsis reports to Cooperating Agencies (outline in Enclosure 2).
- Annual summary report to Cooperating Agencies and the public.
- Final Interim Flow Monitoring Report with recommendations for long-term monitoring, including an analysis of positive and negative aspects of the program.
- Common elements: aerial photography twice yearly (spring and fall); video quarterly; GIS, flow information, and database development continuously.

**Stated products (if funded)**

1. A three-month qualitative assessment of downstream resource responses to the interim test flows.
2. An annual quantitative assessment of environment-resource relationships.
3. Special reports with operational recommendations if unexpected short-term impacts occur.

**Open items and caveats**

- The budget (Enclosure 6) and specific study plans were still pending.
- The document states that GCES Phase II research is incomplete, so the monitoring program cannot yet make inter-resource impact evaluations.
- The text notes that resource agencies "will be fully ___ into the program." The OCR is unclear at this point.
- Comments were requested by December 6, 1991, with changes to be written out in full.
