# Three New Hydrobiid Snail Species from Dalhousie Springs, Northern South Australia

#malacology #hydrobiidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes a new genus, [[Dalhousia]], with two new species, [[Dalhousia Globosa]] and [[Dalhousia Harrisi]], and a new species of [[Fluvidona]], [[Fluvidona Centralia]], from the [[Dalhousie Springs]] complex in northern South Australia. Species status rests on morphology, anatomy and [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]] data from 77 populations. The paper also includes a museum notice on a recovered collection of Mauritian marine molluscs, which is unrelated to the Dalhousie study.

## Background & Description

Dalhousie Springs comprises about 80 springs in roughly 70 km² of far northern South Australia. Together they have the largest natural discharge of water from the [[Great Artesian Basin]] (GAB). The springs support endemic fishes, amphipods and hydrobiid snails. The snails are the most abundant and conspicuous endemic aquatic invertebrates. A 1985 survey and a 1990 survey (the latter collecting material for electrophoresis) underpin the work.

Earlier work recognised an undescribed fauna of "at least six species" based on gross shell differences. Shell variation was so extreme, however, that standard morphological methods could not separate putative taxa.

**Dalhousia** (new genus; type species *D. globosa*)
- Smooth, imperforate, broadly ovate to conic shell with a simple aperture and no external varix.
- Operculum with weak to moderate inner pegs.
- Radula with two pairs of basal cusps on central teeth.
- Female genital system with a single globular sperm sac. This separates it from [[Fonscochlea]], its sister genus, which has two.
- Males have a long, tapering, whip-like penis.

**Dalhousia globosa**
- Shell length 1.7–4.6 mm (type series mean 4.1 mm); broadly conical to globose.
- Holotype: SL 4.16 mm, SW 3.35 mm.
- Typically 4–5 opercular pegs.
- Type locality: Cal (Earwanyera Spring), main pool.

**Dalhousia harrisi** (named for conservationist Colin Harris)
- Shell length 2.3–3.8 mm (mean 2.8 mm); ovate-conic to conic, generally smaller and with a taller spire than *D. globosa*.
- Holotype: SL 3.20 mm, SW 2.52 mm.
- Type locality: Cal, main spring, collected from root fringes at the water edge.

**Fluvidona centralia** (new species, extending the genus's range)
- Shell length 1.4–2.2 mm (mean 1.8 mm); conical.
- Prominent opercular pegs (1–4 well-developed pegs in the type series; 2–7 in the E1 sample).
- Four to five pairs of basal radular cusps.
- Ventrally placed bursal duct and a narrowly tapering penis with a long, narrow base.
- Type locality: Spring Cd11.
- The genus was previously known mainly from south-eastern Australian streams and rivers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *D. globosa* lives mainly in large warm pools and warm outflows at 33–42°C (most 35–40°C). *D. harrisi* occupies the widest range, from cold seeps (down to about 20°C) to hot springs (up to 43°C), and occurs in all spring groups except Gb. *F. centralia* lives in small, mostly cold seepages and the shallow lower outflow of at least one large spring, and has not been found in sympatry with *Dalhousia*.
- **Sympatry:** *D. globosa* and *D. harrisi* co-occur in springs including Cal, Al, A2, A8, B1, Ca8, Cc1, Cc3 and Cd1. In Cal, *D. harrisi* is restricted to pool edges, out of reach of catfish that prey on hydrobiids.
- **Predation and parasitism:** The predatory catfish *Neosilurus* sp. may select for larger, heavier-shelled snails in some large springs. Parasites infect some populations, and in some samples infected snails were significantly larger.
- **Sampling limits:** Small seepages are hard to locate in dense vegetation, so *F. centralia* is probably under-represented in the sampling.
- **Evolutionary context:** Pleistocene age of the springs (no older than 1–2 million years, per Krieg, 1989) suggests *Dalhousia* arrived as an immigrant. The authors estimate two colonisation events, with the *D24* population evolving in situ. *Fluvidona* may have invaded independently from another habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Three new taxa:** One new genus (*Dalhousia*), two new *Dalhousia* species, and one new *Fluvidona* species.
- **Species boundary:** Sympatry is the criterion used to recognise only two species-group taxa within *Dalhousia*. No consistent anatomical characters separate *D. globosa* from *D. harrisi*, so shell characters and allozymes are used instead.
- **Allozyme diagnosis:**
  - *D. globosa* has high frequencies of LAP-2, PGM-2 3, SDH-1 1, SDH-2 2 and GPI-2.
  - *D. harrisi* has high frequencies of LAP-3, SDH-1 2, SDH-2 1 and PGM-1 4.
- **Genetic isolation:** The two species are almost completely isolated. No F1 hybrids were detected, and phenetic trees separate them completely. Hybridisation (breakdown of isolating mechanisms) was found only in springs Ca8 and Cd2.
- **Hot-spring outlier:** The Cala (D24) population of *D. globosa* is genetically divergent, with alleles that are rare or absent in *D. harrisi*. Its divergence from other *D. globosa* is comparable to that between the two species. The authors suggest it may be a distinct lineage, possibly a species complex.
- **Shell variation:** Means across populations are 2.5–4.27 mm for *D. globosa* (seven measured populations) and 1.5–4.1 mm for *D. harrisi* (36 measured populations). Many populations differ significantly (P<0.001). Shell variation may reflect spring size, depth, temperature, predation and parasitism.
- **Fluvidona variation:** Two electrophoretically distinct lineages within *F. centralia* are separated by a fixed difference at GPT-2. Shell width/length and aperture length/shell length ratios differ significantly (P<0.001) between them. Genetic diversity is low, possibly reflecting small founding populations and long isolation.
- **Gene flow:** Pooled data suggest migration of about 20 individuals per spring group per generation (Colgan & Ponder, 1994). This is enough to prevent fragmentation into isolated species with very restricted ranges.
- **Conservation:** The mound springs are of high conservation significance (Harris, 1981, 1993; Ponder, 1985, 1986, 1995; DEST, 1994). The authors note the limited ranges of the species.
- **Appended notice (unrelated to Dalhousie):** The Australian Museum recovered part of R. Viader's Mauritian marine mollusc collection. More than 800 lots have been distinguished. Types for 19 of the 27 new species-level taxa in Viader (1951) were identified. A New Caledonian lot (C.4037) was recognised as a syntypic lot of *Contumax hedleyi*, a synonym of *Cerithium nodulosum*.
