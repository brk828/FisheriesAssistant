# Relation of Chemical Structure to Fish Toxicity in Nitrosalicylanilides and Related Compounds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #toxicology #piscicides #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #structure-activity-relationship

## Executive Summary

This 1966 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service study (Fish Control Laboratory, La Crosse, WI) systematically evaluated the relationship between chemical structure and piscicidal (fish-killing) toxicity in a series of **salicylanilides**, **nitrosalicylanilides**, and **benzanilides**. Using standard static bioassays on [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) and [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), researchers tested 71 compounds with systematic variation in substituent type (alkyl, nitro, halo) and position. The work was motivated by the discovery of [[Antimycin A]] as a potent piscicide and sought analogous compounds with tunable toxicity and species-selectivity. Key findings established that an **ortho-hydroxyl group** combined with a **meta- or sub-meta-nitro group** on the benzoic acid moiety are essential for toxic activity, and that **para-halogenation** on the aniline ring controls species-selective toxicity between trout and goldfish — with halogen atomic size directly correlating with which species is preferentially killed.

## Background & Description

- **Research context**: Conducted at the [[Fish Control Laboratory]] (La Crosse, WI) and in collaboration with the [[Southeastern Fish Control Laboratory]] (Warm Springs, GA) and Ben Venue Laboratories (Bedford, OH), as part of an ongoing search for selective and general **piscicides**.
- **Chemical rationale**: Interest stemmed from [[Antimycin A]], a 3-formamidosalicylic lactone shown to inhibit the electron-transport system in higher animals (Strong, 1958) and act as a fish-selective toxicant (Derse & Strong, 1963). This prompted investigation of related **N-substituted nitrosalicylates** and **formamidosalicylates**.
- **Molecular structure**:
  - **Salicylanilides**: biphenyl compounds with a CONH bridge, condensation product of salicylic acid (or derivative) with aniline (or derivative); characterized by an ortho phenolic hydroxyl (OH) on the acid moiety.
  - **Benzanilides**: structurally identical biphenyl/CONH-bridge compounds but lacking the ortho hydroxyl group.
  - **3-nitrosalicylanilides**: possess a nitro (NO₂) group at the meta (3) position of the acid moiety in addition to the hydroxyl.
  - **5-nitrosalicylanilides**: nitro group oriented to the sub-meta (5) position instead.
- **Compound groupings** (71 total compounds tested, Table 2):
  1. Halonitrosalicylanilides
  2. Halonitrosalicylotoluidides
  3. Nitrosalicyloxylidides
  4. Nitrosalicylanisidides
  5. Miscellaneous (e.g., 4'-phenylazo-3-nitrosalicylanilide, 2'-ethyl-3-nitrosalicylanilide)
- Prior related work cited: antimicrobial activity of substituted salicylanilides ([[Taborsky]] et al., 1959, 1962, 1963), fungicidal activity ([[Baichwal]] et al., 1960a/b), molluscicidal activity ([[Schraufstätter]], 1962), and selective toxicity to [[Sea Lamprey]] (*Petromyzon marinus*) ([[Starkey and Howell 1965]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Test species and sourcing**:
  - [[Rainbow Trout]]: obtained from Manchester, Iowa National Fish Hatchery; acclimated to laboratory water.
  - [[Goldfish]]: obtained from Lake Mills, Wisconsin National Fish Hatchery; weight range 0.7–2.5 g.
- **Reference toxicant standardization**: Sensitivity of each fish lot was benchmarked against **p,p'-DDT**:
  - Rainbow trout: mean LC₅₀ = 6.8 p.p.b. (range 1.9–14.0 p.p.b.)
  - Goldfish: mean LC₅₀ = 41.2 p.p.b. (range 27.0–76.0 p.p.b.)
  - This ~6x difference in baseline sensitivity underscores inherent species differences in xenobiotic susceptibility, relevant to interpreting selective toxicity results.
- **Test conditions**: Static bioassays in reconstituted water at **12°C**, in 2.5- or 15-liter glass containers; 10 fish per concentration (0.1, 1.0, 10 p.p.m.); mortality recorded at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 3, 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours (critical endpoints: 3 and 48 hours).
- **Applied significance**: This research directly supports **fisheries management** applications — development of selective piscicides used for invasive/undesirable fish control (e.g., in the broader USFWS "[[Investigations in Fish Control]]" series, which also covers [[Sea Lamprey]] control agents) while attempting to minimize non-target toxicity.
- **Mechanistic ecological insight**: The authors invoke a "lock-and-key" receptor model — selective toxicity arises when molecular geometry of the toxicant matches receptor geometry in a given fish species, a concept with implications for predicting environmental impacts of piscicide deployment on non-target species.

## Key Findings

### General Structure-Toxicity Relationships
- **Ortho (2) hydroxyl substitution** on the acid moiety (defining feature of salicylanilides vs. benzanilides) is a **basic requirement for toxic activity** and **accelerates the speed of toxic action** (often causing death within 3 hours vs. 48 hours).
- **Benzanilides** (lacking the ortho-OH) were **markedly less toxic** overall to both rainbow trout and goldfish; only 3 of ~30 benzanilide compounds (48, 50, 62) were lethal to goldfish even at 10 p.p.m.
- **Meta (3) or sub-meta (5) nitro substitution** on the benzoic acid moiety are basic requirements for toxicity; **para (4) nitro substitution had the least effect** of any nitro position tested.

### Mono-Substituted Nitrosalicylanilides
- Most substituted 3-nitrosalicylanilides caused complete rainbow trout kill at 1 p.p.m. within 48 hours (often within 3 hours), **except** 2'-ethyl-3-nitrosalicylanilide, 2'-chloro-5-nitrosalicylanilide, and the 5-acetamido derivative of 3'-chloro-5-nitrosalicylanilide.
- Plain **3-nitrosalicylanilide** (unsubstituted, compound 1) did not kill quickly, unlike its substituted analogs.
- **5-nitrosalicylanilides** were slower-acting, not lethal at <10 p.p.m.
- **4'-phenylazo-3-nitrosalicylanilide** and **4'-iodo-3-nitrosalicylanilide** were the only mono-halonitrosalicylanilides lethal to trout at 0.1 p.p.m.
- Toxicity to goldfish **increased as substituent locus moved from ortho (2') → para (4')** in the aniline moiety (e.g., iodo series: 2'-iodo nontoxic to goldfish at 1 p.p.m.; 3'- and 4'-iodo lethal, with 4'-iodo fastest-acting).
- **4'-chloro-3-nitrosalicylanilide** emerged as a key selective compound: lethal to goldfish at 0.1 p.p.m. but not to rainbow trout in 48 hours — a clear case of **species-selective toxicity**.

### Halogen Size and Selectivity (Aniline Para Position)
- **Iodine** (atomic weight 127) in the para (4') position → most toxic to **rainbow trout**.
- **Bromine** (atomic weight 80) in the para (4') position → roughly **equally toxic** to both species.
- **Chlorine** (atomic weight 35) in the para (4') position → **selectively toxic to goldfish**.
- This establishes a clear **halogen-size-dependent selectivity gradient**, supporting a receptor-geometry "fit" hypothesis.
- Ortho (2') halogen substitutions had the **least effect** on toxicity generally, except when paired with an additional **para (4') nitro group**, which conferred selective toxicity toward goldfish.

### Poly-Substituted Nitrosalicylanilides
- **3',4'-dichloro-3-nitrosalicylanilide**: killed all fish of both species at 1 p.p.m., but less toxic to goldfish / more toxic to trout compared with 4'-chloro-3-nitrosalicylanilide — suggesting goldfish receptors are less tolerant of bulkier molecular substitutions.
- **2',4',6'-trichloro-3-nitrosalicylanilide**: toxic to both species only at 10 p.p.m. (not 1 p.p.m.), reinforcing steric bulk limits on goldfish toxicity.
- **4'-bromo-5-chloro-** and **4',5-dibromo-3-nitrosalicylanilide**: lethal to trout at 0.1 p.p.m. but only slightly toxic to goldfish — an added **sub-meta (5) bromine substitution enhanced overall toxicity**, especially to trout.
- **3-nitrosalicyloxylidides** (methyl-substituted): toxicity to trout varied strongly with methyl group position — 2',3'- and 2',4'-isomers fully lethal at 1 p.p.m.; 2',5'-isomer 90% mortality; 2',6'-isomer only 10% mortality. None toxic to goldfish except at 10 p.p.m. (parallels findings in [[Sea Lamprey]] by Starkey & Howell, 1965).
- **Halo-nitrosalicylotoluidides**: chlorine substitution at meta/para positions conferred toxicity at 1 p.p.m. (not achieved by methyl-only oxylidides).
- **Nitrosalicylanisidides** (methoxy-substituted): generally low toxicity to goldfish; exception was **2',3-dinitro-p-salicylanisidide** (partial goldfish kill) and its isomer **2',3-dinitro-m-salicylanisidide** (fully lethal to trout at 1 p.p.m., nontoxic to goldfish even at 10 p.p.m.) — a strong example of trout-selective toxicity.
- One compound, **2',5'-dimethoxy-3-nitrosalicylanilide**, showed slight goldfish-selective toxicity at 0.1 p.p.m. with no effect on trout.

### Benzanilides
- Overall **far less toxic** than salicylanilides, attributed directly to the absence of the ortho-hydroxyl group.
- **3'-chloro-2-nitrobenzanilide** and **4'-chloro-2-nitrobenzanilide** were the most toxic benzanilides tested to both species.
- Moving the nitro group from ortho→meta on the acid moiety **increased speed of toxic action**; para-nitro substitution gave intermediate toxicity.
- **3,5-dinitrobenzanilides**: relatively insoluble at 10 p.p.m. (complicating bioassay); mostly **trout-selective** at 10 p.p.m., with only 2 of ~15 compounds (62, 63) significantly toxic to goldfish.
- **3,3',5-trinitrobenzanilide** was more toxic than **2',3,4',5-tetranitrobenzanilide**, suggesting a saturation effect reducing both toxicity and selectivity with excessive nitro substitution.
- **2',6'-diethyl-3,5-dinitrobenzanilide**: nontoxic to either species but induced a **"sounding" behavioral reaction** in rainbow trout at 0.1 and 1 p.p.m. — a notable sublethal/behavioral endpoint.

### Mechanistic Interpretation
- Findings support a **receptor-geometry "lock-and-key" model**: toxicity and species-selectivity depend on how well a compound's molecular geometry matches the receptor geometry of a given fish species.
- Consistent with [[Baichwal 1960]] fungicide studies suggesting salicylanilide activity involves **hydrogen bonding or chelation** via the phenolic and amidic oxygen atoms, interfering with enzyme systems.
- Referencing [[Ariëns 1964]] pharmacology theory: electron-donating groups (hydroxy) increase electron density in the conjugated ring system, while electron-withdrawing groups (nitro) decrease it — this electronic balance may underlie the requirement for paired ortho-OH/meta-NO₂ substitution.

### Conclusions & Summary
- **Salicylanilides > Benzanilides** in toxicity to both test fish species.
- Ortho (2)-OH accelerates toxic action; meta (3)/sub-meta (5)-NO₂ substitution is required for toxicity.
- Para (4')-halogenation on the aniline ring of nitrosalicylanilides **markedly increases toxicity and speed of action**, with **halogen atomic size dictating species selectivity** (I → trout-selective; Br → non-selective; Cl → goldfish-selective).
- Ortho (2') halogens alone have minimal effect, but combined with a para (4') nitro group produce goldfish-selective toxicity.
- Additional sub-meta (5) bromination on already para-halogenated 3-nitrosalicylanilides further increases general toxicity.
- These structure-activity patterns provide a template for **rational piscicide design**, enabling targeting of specific fish species (e.g., invasive [[Sea Lamprey]] control, selective removal of undesirable fish stocks) while potentially sparing others.
