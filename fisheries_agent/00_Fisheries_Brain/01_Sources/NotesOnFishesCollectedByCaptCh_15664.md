# Notes on Fishes Collected by Capt. Chas. Bendire in Washington Territory and Oregon, May to October 1881

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #Cottidae #Petromyzontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1881 report by Tarleton H. Bean of the [[United States National Museum]] documents a collection of eleven freshwater fish species (and lamprey taxa) gathered by Capt. [[Charles Bendire]] in [[Washington Territory]] and [[Oregon]] between May and October 1881. The collection, sourced primarily from Garrison Creek, Mill Creek, the Walla Walla River, the John Day River, and the Columbia River near Celilo, provided meristic and morphometric data supporting taxonomic revisions—most notably evidence toward synonymizing *[[Coregonus Couesii]]* with *[[Coregonus Williamsonii]]* and consolidating several species within the genus *[[Apocope]]*. The paper is a classic example of late 19th-century systematic ichthyology relying on detailed counts of fin rays, scales, gill rakers, and teeth to resolve species boundaries among Pacific Northwest freshwater fishes and lampreys.

## Background & Description

The specimens were collected as alcohol-preserved material and forwarded to the National Museum following an earlier, only partially examined consignment from Bendire (reported in *Notes on Salmonidae*, Proc. U.S.N.M., 1881). This second lot comprised eleven species:

1. *[[Uranidea Marginata]]* (a sculpin, Cottidae)
2. *[[Coregonus Williamsonii]]* (whitefish, Salmonidae)
3. *[[Oncorhynchus Chouicha]]* (Chinook salmon)
4. *[[Oncorhynchus Nerka]]* (sockeye salmon)
5. *[[Acrochilus Alutaceus]]* ("hard mouth" / chiselmouth, Cyprinidae)
6. *[[Rhinichthys Transmontanus]]* (dace)
7. *[[Apocope Nubila]]* (dace)
8. *[[Mylochilus Caurinus]]* (chub, called "Red Horse")
9. *[[Richardsonius Balteatus]]* (a minnow resembling *Notemigonus*)
10. *[[Lampetra Tridentata]]* (Pacific lamprey, "lamprey eel")
11. *[[Ammocoetes Plumbea]]?* (larval lamprey, identification uncertain)

Bean provides exhaustive meristic descriptions for each taxon, including dorsal/anal/ventral/pectoral ray counts, lateral-line scale counts, gill-raker counts, pharyngeal tooth formulas, and body proportions (head length, depth, eye diameter relative to head), typical of comparative taxonomic practice of the period.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Localities sampled**: Garrison Creek (Wash. Ter.), Mill Creek (a tributary of the Walla Walla River), the Walla Walla River, the John Day River (Oregon), and the Columbia River at Celilo (~10 miles above The Dalles, Oregon).
- **Collection methods**: Specimens were taken by hook and line (e.g., *Coregonus Williamsonii*, locally called "fresh-water herring" and "small-mouthed whitefish") and by seining/trapping during low water ("turning the water off" in Garrison Creek).
- **Local common names** recorded by Bendire provide ethno-ichthyological context:
  - *Coregonus Williamsonii* — "fresh-water herring" (Mill Creek), "small-mouthed whitefish" (Garrison Creek)
  - *Oncorhynchus chouicha* — "salmon grilse"
  - *Acrochilus alutaceus* — "Hard mouth"
  - *Mylochilus caurinus* — "Chub," occasionally "Red Horse," noted for coloration: bluish-brown dorsum, carmine-red lateral stripe, silvery-white belly, steel-blue nose, brick-red stripe below the eye.
  - *Lampetra tridentata* — "Lamprey eel"
- **Seasonal/life-history notes**: *Oncorhynchus nerka* heads collected at Celilo in May 1881 showed live coloration of steel-blue backs with greenish reflections and pure silvery-white sides/belly, characteristic of pre-spawning migratory salmon in the Columbia River run. Vomerine teeth were noted as difficult to detect by touch despite visible tooth rows, an anatomical detail relevant to species identification during spawning runs.
- **Habitat associations**: The dace, chub, and shiner species were collected from small tributary creeks (Garrison Creek, Mill Creek), reflecting typical Pacific Northwest cyprinid habitat in cool, clear headwater streams, while salmonids were documented from both tributary and mainstem Columbia River sites, consistent with anadromous life histories.

## Key Findings

### Taxonomic/Systematic Contributions
- Comparative measurements of *Coregonus Williamsonii* specimens (numbers 344, 345, 354) against the type of *[[Coregonus Couesii]]* (from Chief Mountain Lake) revealed highly similar meristic values (gill rakers 13–15, scale rows ~9–10/87–90/8, similar head/eye/maxilla proportions), supporting Bean's conclusion that the two nominal species are likely conspecific.
- Detailed comparison of Bendire's *[[Apocope Nubila]]* specimens against Girard's type of *Argyreus nubilus* showed minor differences (dorsal ray counts, scale-count ranges) insufficient to warrant species-level distinction, reinforcing the case for consolidating several *Apocope* species.
- Examination of an *[[Apocope Vulnerata]]* specimen (identified by Prof. Jordan, Utah Lake) revealed asymmetric pharyngeal tooth counts (1,4 on one side vs. 2,4 on the other) within a single fish, undermining tooth-count as a reliable diagnostic character separating *A. vulnerata* from *A. nubila*.
- *[[Lampetra Tridentata]]* type comparison: Bean confirmed identity between the type of *Petromyzon astori* Girard and *Lampetra tridentata*, corroborating earlier work by [[David Starr Jordan]]. Related nominal forms *P. ciliatus* and *P. lividus* were distinguished mainly by dorsal fin separation distance.
- Uncertain identity of *[[Ammocoetes Plumbea]]?* — Bean could not confirm whether the small (4½ inch) larval lamprey from Garrison Creek represented the larval stage of *A. plumbea* or a distinct form, possibly *Ammocoetes cibarius* Girard.

### Morphometric/Meristic Data Highlights
- *Uranidea marginata*: D. VIII,18–20; A. 14; lateral line 20–27 short tubes; head ~3⅓–3½ in length; depth ~5.
- *Oncorhynchus chouicha* (grilse, 16⅜ in.): gill rakers 22; branchiostegals 17; dorsal rays 11; anal rays 16; lateral line scales ~145; numerous small pyloric caeca.
- *Oncorhynchus nerka*: gill rakers 40 (in one specimen); branchiostegals 14; vomerine teeth present but not tactilely perceptible.
- *Acrochilus alutaceus*: D.10; A.9; V.9; scales ~22/87–89/16; persistent pharyngeal teeth ~4–5 per side with 1–2 deciduous teeth.
- *Rhinichthys transmontanus*: teeth formula 2,4-4,2; D.8; A.7; lateral line scales ranging 68–77.
- *Mylochilus caurinus*: D.8; A.8; V.9; scales 14-74-9; teeth 1,5-5,1 (molar-like on four teeth per side).
- *Richardsonius balteatus*: teeth 2,5-5,2, hooked without grinding surface; D.10; A.18; scales 12-63-8.

### Broader Implications
This work exemplifies the descriptive, comparative-morphology approach central to 19th-century freshwater ichthyology of the Pacific Northwest, and its data continue to underpin later revisions of [[Coregonus]] and [[Apocope]] taxonomy in the region. The collection also documents baseline occurrence records for anadromous salmonids (*[[Oncorhynchus Chouicha]]*, *[[Oncorhynchus Nerka]]*) and lampreys (*[[Lampetra Tridentata]]*) in the Walla Walla and Columbia River systems during the historic (pre-dam) period.
