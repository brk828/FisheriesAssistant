# Letter from Dean A. Hendrickson to Carlos Yruretagoyena Ugalde on Desert Fishes Chapter Formation

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 letter from Dean A. Hendrickson (Native Fish Research Biologist, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) to Carlos Yruretagoyena Ugalde, supporting the formation of a **Desert Fishes chapter** within the **Congreso Nacional de Ictiología** in Mexico. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the letter functions as an organizational and networking tool, compiling a directory of North American researchers actively engaged in fieldwork on freshwater fishes of Mexican deserts. The letter emphasizes international collaboration between U.S. and Mexican biologists in the study and conservation of Mexico's aquatic natural resources, following discussions initiated at a Desert Fishes Council-related gathering in [[Dexter, New Mexico]].

## Background & Description
No direct morphological descriptions of species are provided in this correspondence. However, the document identifies several taxonomic groups under active study by the listed researchers, implying ongoing morphological and systematic characterization work on:
- **Mexican trout** (Salmonidae) — studied by Dr. Bill Berg
- **[[Fundulus]]** (Fundulidae) — systematics studied by Dr. Robert Cashner
- **[[Xiphophorus]]** (Poeciliidae) — genetics, evolution, and behavior studied by Dr. Klaus Kallman and Dr. Mike Ryan
- **[[Poeciliopsis]]** (Poeciliidae) — molecular systematics and evolutionary biogeography studied by Joe Quattro, Dr. Bob Vrijenhoek, and Steve Weeks
- **[[Goodeidae]]** — evolutionary and molecular systematics studied by Dr. Bruce Turner

These taxa represent key model groups for desert fish systematics, reflecting the broader scientific focus of the network described.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The overarching ecological context of the letter is the **freshwater desert ecosystems of Mexico**, encompassing isolated springs, streams, and river drainages that support highly endemic and often endangered fish faunas. The described research network spans multiple ecological and evolutionary themes:
- **Conservation biology and ecology** of imperiled desert fish populations (e.g., Francisco Abarca Gonzalez, Mr. Bill Leibfried, Phil Pister, Dr. David Propst, Dr. Robert J. Edwards)
- **Biogeography and systematics** of desert fish lineages across fragmented aquatic habitats (Dr. Brooks M. Burr, Dr. Mike Douglas, Dr. Rick Mayden, Dr. R. R. Miller, Dr. W. L. Minckley)
- **Molecular evolution and speciation** in isolated desert drainage systems (Dr. Tom Dowling, Dr. Dave Hillis, Dr. Tony Echelle, Dr. Don Buth, Dr. Tom Haglund)

This reflects the well-documented pattern in desert ichthyology wherein fragmented, isolated aquatic habitats (springs, playas, relict drainages) drive high rates of endemism and speciation, a theme central to the work of the [[Desert Fishes Council]].

## Key Findings
- The letter documents the founding impetus for a proposed **Desert Fishes chapter** of the **Congreso Nacional de Ictiología**, initiated informally during a bus trip from [[Dexter, New Mexico]] to [[Albuquerque]] in November 1989.
- Hendrickson compiled a list of at least **24 U.S.-based biologists** with recent, ongoing, or potential future fieldwork on Mexican desert freshwater fishes, spanning institutions including [[Arizona State University]], [[University of Michigan]], [[University of Texas at Austin]], [[Rutgers University]], and the [[New York Aquarium]].
- Research interests among the listed biologists cluster around **systematics, biogeography, molecular evolution, ecology, and conservation** of desert fish taxa.
- The initiative reflects an explicit binational conservation goal, emphasizing collaboration between Mexican and American ichthyologists for the study and conservation of Mexico's aquatic natural resources.
- Key taxa of shared interest among the network include **[[Xiphophorus]]**, **[[Poeciliopsis]]**, **[[Goodeidae]]**, **[[Fundulus]]**, and unspecified **Mexican trout** lineages, indicating these as focal groups for desert fish systematics and conservation genetics research circa 1990.
- The letter underscores the historical role of grassroots, informal scientific networking in catalyzing formal international scientific organizations such as the proposed Desert Fishes chapter.
