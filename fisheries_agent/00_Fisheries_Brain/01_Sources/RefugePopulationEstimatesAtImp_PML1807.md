# Refuge Population Estimates of Desert Pupfish on Cibola and Imperial National Wildlife Refuges, September 2006

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This USFWS Arizona Fisheries Resource Office report (October 2006), prepared by C.O. Minckley and T. Thorson, estimates population sizes of the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) in artificial refuge ponds on [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (CNWR) and [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] (INWR), sampled in September 2006. It also documents the establishment of a third refuge pond at [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] (BWRNWR) and compares results with the only natural Arizona population, at [[Quitobaquito Spring]]. Estimates were 1,791 fish at CNWR and 405 at INWR, both within ranges reported for Quitobaquito.

## Background & Description

The historical range of the desert pupfish included the lower Colorado River and tributaries (Gila, Santa Cruz, San Pedro and Salt rivers) in Arizona, California and adjacent Mexican states, extending to the Gulf of California. Habitat destruction and interactions with non-native fishes caused extirpation of natural populations by the mid-1960s. The species was listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] in 1986, is a Wildlife Species of Concern in Arizona, and is a sensitive species of the U.S. Forest Service. A recovery plan criteria goal was to secure, maintain and replicate all extant populations and re-establish replicate populations in the most natural identifiable habitats within the historical range.

Natural populations persist in California's Salton Sea area and at several localities along the lower Colorado River and into Mexico. Use of artificial habitats for refuge populations has been attempted several times in Arizona for this species and others (Minckley et al. 1991).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Cibola NWR (CNWR):** A 10 m x 5 m concrete-lined pond next to refuge headquarters, 0.5–1 m deep, with river gravel substrate and large rocks for cover. It is supplied with dechlorinated water. Thirty-seven fish were transferred from the Phoenix Zoo in September 1999; the original population was lost, and the current population derives from stock collected in the early 1970s at El Doctor marsh, Sonora, Mexico.

**Imperial NWR (INWR):** A 5 m x 5 m plastic-lined pond up to 1 m deep, supplied with un-chlorinated water, with river gravel, palo verde and catclaw acacia around the perimeter, and cattails in the pond. Twenty-three (reported as "Twentythr...") pupfish were introduced from Cibola in March 2000.

**Bill Williams River NWR (BWRNWR):** A 10 m x 5 m x 0.5 m pond built in July 2006 as part of a public interpretive display. Fish (a mixture of juveniles and adults, about 200 individuals) were derived from the El Doctor stock held at Arizona State University, and were delivered in May 2006. The pond was established on 2 May 2006 per the report's chronology.

**Habitat context:** The El Doctor marsh complex lies in the Upper Gulf of California and Colorado River Delta Biosphere Reserve, Sonora, Mexico.

**Water quality (September 2006 readings, surface 0 m and bottom 1 m):**
- CNWR (5/9/06 and 5/9/06 readings): temperatures about 31–35 °C, dissolved oxygen 2.5–5.3 ppm, conductivity ~3.4 mS, pH 8.7–9.1 range across sites.
- INWR (7/9/06): temperatures about 32.5–34 °C, dissolved oxygen 2.4–5.2 ppm, pH 8.9–9.1.

Water quality and general fish condition were described as good.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling method:** 12 unbaited minnow traps set in a 4 x 3 grid in each pond; CNWR sampled 5–9 September 2006 and INWR 7–8 September 2006. Fish were anesthetized with MS-222, measured, marked by fin clip and released.
- **Population estimates:** Calculated with the Seber (1982) formula, with confidence limits assuming a normal distribution.
- **CNWR:** 416 fish captured on day one and marked; 231 captured the next day, including 53 recaptures. Estimated population 1,791 fish (SE 978; 95% CI 97–10,0 range as reported). Mean total length 34 mm (range 26–57 mm).
- **INWR:** 304 fish marked on the first day; 282 trapped, including 213 recaptures. Estimated population 405 (SE 74; 95% CI 98–840 as reported). Mean total length 36.8 mm (range 26–58 mm).
- **Size note:** Hundreds of individuals (>60 mm) were observed in both habitats.
- **Historical comparison:** CNWR estimates: 689 (2001), 2,578 (2004), 1,086 (2005), 1,791 (2006). INWR estimates: 137 (2004), 138 (2005), 405 (2006).
- **Quitobaquito comparison:** Direct counts from 1,033 to 5,252 fish over about 10 years (Tibbets 2006); Douglas et al. (2001) estimated 9,556 fish for 1998–1999. The CNWR and INWR estimates fall within the Tibbets (2006) range.
- **Management implications:** Annual summer estimates are planned at each site; BWRNWR will be included in future estimates; possibly Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center fish could be used; wild fish from Mexico, if obtainable, would be mixed in to maintain population heterogeneity.
