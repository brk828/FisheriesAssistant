# The Phoenix Zoo Strategic Plan 1995-1997

#zoo-management #conservation-biology #environmental-education #institutional-strategy

## Executive Summary

This strategic plan, prepared by [[Warren J. Iliff]] (Executive Director) and staff of [[The Phoenix Zoo]] in April 1995, outlines a comprehensive three-year transformation of the institution from a traditional animal exhibition facility into a mission-driven conservation communication organization. The plan articulates a new mission statement: "We inspire people to live in ways that promote the well-being of the natural world." The document details organizational restructuring, capital improvements, audience development strategies, and financial projections for fiscal years 1995-1997, with a five-year capital program extending through 1998-1999. The zoo aims to increase attendance from 912,000 (1994-95) to 1,150,000 (1996-97), representing a 20% growth over the planning period.

## Background & Description

[[The Phoenix Zoo]] operated as a traditional zoological park focused primarily on exhibiting exotic animals to passive visitors. By 1995, the institution faced critical challenges: six years of stagnant attendance and revenue despite rising costs, increasing competition from other attractions, and a gradual industry-wide decline. The zoo served approximately 921,000 visitors annually (five-year average) in [[Maricopa County]], Arizona, a metropolitan area with 2.3 million residents. The institution maintained independence from government support and operated as a self-sustaining enterprise under the [[Arizona Zoological Society]].

The strategic plan represents a fundamental philosophical shift from exhibition to communication, reorganizing the zoo around five interpretive trails (Arizona, Tropics, Africa, Children's, and Desert), each conveying specific messages about human relationships with nature. The organizational structure would transition from a traditional hierarchical pyramid to interdisciplinary teams supported by four resource pools: Communications, Collections, Operations, and Administration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan emphasizes conservation biology and environmental education as core institutional values. The zoo commits to modeling and promoting behaviors that conserve natural resources, supporting applied conservation projects particularly focused on arid land ecology. Priority initiatives include the [[Mexican Wolf]] and [[Thick-Billed Parrot]] reintroduction programs featured in the Arizona Woodlands exhibit, demonstrating direct links between human behavior and species preservation.

The conservation strategy prioritizes projects that contribute to self-sustaining ecosystems and appropriate biotic diversity, with emphasis on local initiatives having global significance. The institution would support non-capital basic and applied research including equipment, travel, field work, and publication, forming collaborative relationships with organizations sharing conservation interests. The [[Forest of Uco]] project would interpret tropical rain forest, cloud forest, and elfin forest ecosystems, while the [[Great Deserts of the World]] (GDOW) initiative would create comprehensive desert ecology presentations.

## Key Findings

**Mission and Strategic Direction:**
- New mission statement mandates institutional focus on inspiring behavioral change rather than passive animal exhibition
- Core message: "The natural world. I am part of it. I make a difference"
- Five trail-specific sub-messages: "I am an animal" (Children's Trail), "I live here" (Arizona Trail), "I need to eat" (Africa Trail), "I depend on others" (Tropics Trail), "I can adapt" (Desert Trail)

**Attendance and Market Development:**
- Projected three-year total visitation: 3,330,000 people (1,040,000 in Year 1; 1,140,000 in Year 2; 1,150,000 in Year 3)
- Three-year average of 1,110,000 represents 20% increase over previous five-year average of 921,000
- Target markets: families with children (primary), schools, tourists, and Hispanic communities
- School market: 25.2% penetration of K-8th grade enrollment (68,309 students); goal to reach 24.8% market share by 1996-97
- Tourist market: approximately 200,000 annual visitors (2.15% of total tourist market); goal to increase to 240,000 (7% annual growth)
- Hispanic market target: 18.4% of total attendance to match community demographic composition

**Financial Projections:**
- Three-year operating revenue growth from $9,127,000 (1994-95) to $11,513,000 (1996-97)
- Return of $2.25 million for reinvestment through operations during three-year period
- Per capita spending increase goal: 10% annually
- Membership growth target: 25% increase to 30,800 members generating $1,400,000 by Year 3
- Animal adoption program growth: 48% increase from $74,000 to $110,000 net return

**Major Capital Projects (1994-1999):**
- Year 1 (1994-95): $1,612,414 total capital investment including [[Stone House Pavilion]] ($1,230,914), Arizona Trail lighting and ramada improvements
- Year 2 (1995-96): $2,866,500 including [[Forest of Uco]] construction ($1,600,000), Children's Trail farmland ($105,000), Arizona Trail reptile facility ($273,000)
- Year 3 (1996-97): Focus on [[Great Deserts of the World]] preorganizer ($350,000), Children's Trail playground refurbishment and connectivity improvements
- Years 4-5 (1997-1999): GDOW construction and opening (approximately $2,000,000), Africa Trail enhancements, Arizona Trail expansion

**Organizational Restructuring:**
- Transition from hierarchical structure to matrix organization with trail teams and service pools
- Four functional resource groups: Communications/Customer Service, Collections, Operations, Administration
- Trail facilitators coordinate interdisciplinary teams for five-year master plans
- Emphasis on cross-training, empowerment, and shared responsibility

**Conservation Priorities:**
- Focus on arid land ecology with global significance
- Support for basic and applied research promoting sustainable ecology
- Priority for initiatives contributing to self-sustaining ecosystems and biotic diversity
- Collaborative relationships with allied organizations
- Research sub-committee to evaluate, monitor, and assess conservation projects

**Educational Programming:**
- Correlation with 1993 Environmental Education Guidelines for school programs
- Pre-school market expansion targeting 94,000 children ages 4-5 in [[Maricopa County]]
- Development of CD-ROM, video, and print media to extend reach beyond physical campus
- Integration of interpretation with retail activity in plaza "festival" environment
- After-hours interpretive programs and night-lighting projects for nocturnal habitat exploration

**Performance Metrics:**
- Customer satisfaction index measuring perceived value of zoo experience
- Communication effectiveness index assessing message comprehension and behavioral influence
- Conservation initiatives index tracking scientific participation and publication
- Management information system implementation for timely business strategy reporting
- Systematic program evaluation to determine audience effect and motivation
