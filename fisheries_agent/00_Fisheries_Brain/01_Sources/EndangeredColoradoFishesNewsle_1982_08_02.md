# Endangered Colorado Fishes Newsletter — August 2, 1982

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This newsletter digest reports on ongoing (1982) research, monitoring, and conservation efforts targeting four federally significant Colorado River Basin fishes: the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Studies span radio-telemetry tracking of spawning migrations, habitat and population monitoring, larval drift and backwater ecology, hatchability/temperature experiments, reintroduction and stocking programs, and hydrology/riparian studies tied to flow regulation on the Yampa, Green, White, and Colorado Rivers. The coordinating body, the [[Colorado River Fishery Monitoring Program]] (CRFMP, 1982–1985), succeeded the completed Colorado River Fishery Project (CRFP) and organized work around three objectives: (1) mapping adult squawfish migration to spawning sites, (2) verifying instream flow requirements for spawning success and larval survival, and (3) assessing effects of Flaming Gorge Dam flow fluctuations on young-of-year and juvenile squawfish survival in backwater habitats.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological description is minimal in this administrative/newsletter source; taxonomic notes focus on the **[[Gila Complex]]**:

- **[[Gila Complex]]** — A biology and systematics study (Dr. Gican Clemmer, USFWS) is underway on *Gila* species in the mainstem Colorado River above Grand Junction and in the Yampa River and tributaries, reflecting ongoing taxonomic uncertainty in distinguishing [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Humpback Chub]] populations from related *Gila* forms.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** — Named for its distinctive sharp-edged dorsal keel (not elaborated further in text); studied alongside [[Bonytail Chub]] in reservoir (Lake Mojave) settings for reproductive biology.
- Life-stage terminology used throughout: **young-of-the-year (YOY)**, **larvae**, **juveniles**, and **adults**, indicating multi-stage population monitoring approaches across all four species.
- Physical/environmental correlates tracked alongside fish include flow, temperature, turbidity, sediment type, conductivity, and light penetration (White River non-game fish study), used as proxies for habitat suitability rather than direct fish morphology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning migration behavior**: Radio-tagged adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the Yampa River exhibited long-distance spawning migrations, including one individual tracked migrating **100 miles** to a spawning bar; two tagged fish remained resident in the upper Yampa without migrating, suggesting intraspecific variation in migratory strategy.
- **Thermal ecology**: Temperature-sensor-equipped radio tags on Yampa River squawfish provided direct data on **thermal habitat selection** by migrating adults.
- **Spawning habitat**: Spawning occurs on gravel/cobble "spawning bars" in main channel reaches; egg and larval development are sensitive to **sediment transport dynamics** and flow-mediated sediment deposition, studied via Instream Flow Incremental Methodology (IFG-4/IFG-2).
- **Larval and nursery habitat**: Post-spawning **larval drift** occurs in July–August; larvae subsequently recruit into **backwater habitats**, which serve as critical nursery areas for YOY and juvenile squawfish through October.
- **Backwater ecology and Flaming Gorge Dam effects**: Fluctuating discharge from Flaming Gorge Dam alters backwater formation/persistence in the Green River, directly affecting rearing habitat availability, growth, competition with exotic species, and juvenile survival — a key focus of CRFMP Objective 3.
- **Reservoir populations**: [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] in Lake Mojave and Lake Mead exhibit lentic (reservoir-adapted) life histories, with research focused on population age structure, growth, and offspring mortality — suggesting recruitment bottlenecks distinct from riverine populations.
- **Riparian linkage**: Riparian vegetation along the Yampa and Green Rivers is hydrologically dependent on natural flow regimes; comparative studies (Yampa vs. Green) aim to predict impacts of flow alteration (e.g., reservoir construction) on riparian systems that structure river channel morphology, sediment regime, and indirectly, fish habitat.
- **Community context**: Non-game/non-listed fishes of the White River were studied for food habits, interspecific competition, and food electivity, providing ecological context for the endangered species' trophic environment.
- **Exotic species interactions**: Backwater studies explicitly include monitoring of competition between native squawfish and introduced exotic fish species.

## Key Findings

- **CRFMP established (1982–1985)** as successor to the completed Colorado River Fishery Project (CRFP), with three core objectives: migration/spawning site mapping, flow requirement verification, and Flaming Gorge flow-fluctuation impact assessment.
- **Telemetry tagging totals (as of mid-1982)**:
  - Green River: ~12 adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] radio-tagged.
  - Colorado River (Grand Junction area): ~16 adult squawfish radio-tagged.
  - Lake Powell: only 6 fish tagged due to difficulty capturing adults.
  - Upper Yampa River: 12 adult squawfish tagged (April 1982); one fish migrated 100 miles to spawn.
  - White River (Utah): 8 adult squawfish radio-tagged (June 1982) under a Utah Division of Wildlife/Department of Water Resources contract, tied to a proposed White River dam site assessment.
- **Experimental stocking**: CRFMP and the Windy Gap Project obtained permission to experimentally stock Colorado squawfish in backwaters near Grand Junction, Colorado.
- **Razorback sucker reintroduction/stocking** underway under USFWS–Arizona–New Mexico Memoranda of Understanding in the Gila, Salt (700,000 larvae stocked in 1982), and Verde Rivers (Arizona), and planned for the San Juan River (New Mexico), with new monitoring programs to follow.
- **Reintroduction feasibility study** for Colorado squawfish planned via USFWS contract with Arizona Department of Game and Fish.
- **Temperature/hatchability research** (Dr. Paul Marsh, Arizona State University) examined incubation temperature effects on egg hatchability for all four focal species, presented at the AFS Meeting (July 1982, Las Vegas), with management implications for matching hatchery/reintroduction conditions to natural thermal regimes.
- **Taxonomic clarification effort** underway for the [[Gila Complex]] in the upper Colorado River basin to resolve species boundaries relevant to bonytail and humpback chub conservation status.
- **Institutional developments**: Publication of CRFP final reports (White River and Yampa River segments) via Bureau of Land Management and CREMP respectively; USFWS Region 6 leadership changes (new Regional Director Galen Buterbaugh; new Assistant Regional Director John Green); Upper Colorado River Basin Conservation Plan draft submitted July 8, 1982, with final draft targeted for October 1982 adoption.
- **Regional coordination meeting** scheduled August 26–27, 1982 (Phoenix, AZ) to establish recovery and management goals for all four listed species in the lower Colorado River basin.
- **Ongoing hydrology/riparian research** (USGS, NPS Water Resources Laboratory, University of New Mexico) aimed at quantifying how reservoir construction and altered flow regimes on the Yampa River would affect channel morphology, sediment transport, and riparian vegetation — with the Green River serving as an unregulated comparative baseline.
