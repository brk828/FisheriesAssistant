# Proposal for Video-Based Morphometric Data Collection for Gila Species

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #morphometrics

## Executive Summary

This 1988 correspondence from Arizona State University to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service proposes using video camera (camcorder) technology instead of traditional 35mm photography for collecting morphometric data from endangered [[Gila Cypha]] (humpback chub) and [[Gila Robusta]] (roundtail chub) in the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] systems. The proposal justifies the equipment expenditure by detailing how camcorder technology, combined with VCR playback and computer digitization, provides superior photographic records, reduces stress to endangered fish, and enables more efficient morphometric analysis while maintaining compatibility with existing 35mm photographic databases.

## Background & Description

The endangered [[Gila Cypha]] and the more common [[Gila Robusta]] occur syntopically in various combinations within the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] (Colorado and Utah). Monitoring and management of these fishes are hindered by concerns about morphologically intermediate forms (putative hybrids between the two species). The distinctness of each species has been questioned repeatedly in the literature, largely due to morphological research that has been inconsistent and undirected in time and space.

As part of a larger monitoring effort by [[U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service]], endangered fishes including [[Gila Cypha]] are being captured and photographed on 35mm film. Photography eliminates many inherent difficulties in taxonomic investigations of endangered species: it corrects for lack of preserved museum material, eliminates need for destructive sampling, and provides a permanent phenotypic record that can be measured and analyzed statistically.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The two [[Cyprinidae]] species occupy overlapping habitats in the [[Colorado River]] basin tributaries. The presence of morphologically intermediate forms raises questions about species boundaries and potential hybridization between [[Gila Cypha]] and [[Gila Robusta]]. This taxonomic ambiguity complicates conservation management of the endangered [[Gila Cypha]].

The proposal references a successful field trip to [[Mojave]] where researchers ran nets at 10 pm and 6 am, operated electro-fishing boats, and tagged many razorback suckers, demonstrating active monitoring programs for endangered fish populations in the region.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Methodology:**
- Use of Panasonic WV-32608AF camcorder for photographic data collection
- Camcorder provides numerous pictures of one fish in less time than 35mm camera bracketing
- Built-in playback screen allows immediate quality assessment before fish release
- Automatic zoom lens enables close-up photography of head, fins, and scalation characters
- Reduced handling time minimizes stress to living endangered fish

**Technical Advantages:**
- Frame-grabbing technology allows selection of any single frame from footage for analysis (versus single or two frames with 35mm)
- VCR-to-microcomputer interface enables projection of video images to CRT screen
- Commercially available microchip boards/software enable frame capture and storage on hard disk
- Digital images are transportable via floppy disk or electronic mail (BITNET)
- Cursor-driven measurement using mouse records morphometric data automatically

**Compatibility:**
- Existing 35mm photographs remain valid and can be analyzed using TV camera to project images onto CRT
- New technology extends and enhances monitoring efforts without invalidating previous photographic work
- Software for collecting morphometric data from CRT images readily available from commercial and academic agencies

**Data Management:**
- Simplified data access and encourages additional analysis or re-analysis
- Digital pictures can be transmitted between institutions via electronic mail linking universities and governmental agencies
- Measurements automatically recorded and stored into datasets for future multivariate analysis of shape and shape-change between species
- Photographic database functions as preserved museum specimens for analysis
