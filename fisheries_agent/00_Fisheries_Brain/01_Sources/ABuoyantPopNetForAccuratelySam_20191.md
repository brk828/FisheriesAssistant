# A Buoyant Pop Net for Accurately Sampling Fish at Artificial Habitat Structures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

Larson, Johnson, and Lynch (1986) describe and evaluate a remotely operated "[[Pop Net]]," a buoyant cylindrical net designed to sample the entire fish assemblage associated with [[Artificial Habitat Structures]] in lakes and reservoirs. The design adapts the buoyant net concept of [[Bagenal Buoyant Net]] for larval fish. In controlled pool tests, the net captured an average of 99% of available subadult and adult [[Bluegill]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] with no evident species or size selectivity and no learned avoidance. In reservoir field use, firing success was 79%, with most failures caused by anglers.

## Background & Description

Artificial habitat structures are a common fishery management tool in lakes and reservoirs. Existing sampling methods, including [[Scuba Diving]], controlled angling, electrofishing, trap nets, drop nets, and rotenone, were described as having inherent biases and limitations, and their capture efficiencies had rarely been evaluated. The authors set out the ideal sampling criteria: sample only the structure and adjacent water, capture all fish regardless of size or species without bias, and operate reliably across depths and conditions.

The pop net is a cylinder 4.3 m in diameter and 6 m high, made of 6.4-mm nylon mesh. Its top is attached to a hexagonal collar of sealed rigid plastic conduit that provides flotation. Three [[Electrical Solenoid Triggers]] mounted on concrete forms hold the net on the bottom. When energized from a 24-V surface source about 25 m away, the solenoid pistons release the collar, which rises to the surface and prevents captured fish from escaping over the edge. Divers reset the net manually.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Pool tests were run in an Olympic-sized outdoor pool, 3.4 m deep, with an artificial structure of 20 automobile tires shaped into a cone 2.7 m in diameter and 2.1 m high. Fish were drawn from a nearby reservoir and acclimated for 24 h. Water temperature ranged from 3 to 13°C among tests, and the pool was homothermic during each test.

Reservoir field tests used three pop nets set at 2 and 4 m depth around structures of seven evergreen trees. Nets were fired weekly from June 16 to September 13, 1983, yielding 138 samples. Secchi readings were 1.8-2.5 m and water temperatures 24-32°C during diver observations. Wind-driven surface currents above 25 km/h pushed or broke the flotation collar, which was the main environmental limitation reported.

Fish sizes in pool tests were: subadult bluegill 80-130 mm total length (TL), adult bluegill over 160 mm, subadult largemouth bass 180-230 mm, and adult largemouth bass over 310 mm.

## Key Findings

- **Pool capture efficiency:** Across three trials of six tests each (October-November 1979), bluegill capture was 32.7% for subadults and 77.0% for adults in total, with near-zero escape. Largemouth bass capture was 58.5% for subadults and 16.7% for adults, with 2.1% subadult escape. Overall, the net is reported as capturing an average of 99% of fish available to capture; the adult largemouth bass figure reflects low availability and repeated use rather than net failure.
- **Escape rates:** Escape was 0-5.6% across size groups. Spearman and Friedman tests showed no learned capture avoidance over repeated tests.
- **Site avoidance:** Only adult bluegill showed a significant decline in use of the structure, with the sixth test significantly lower than tests 1, 3, and 4, though catches remained high (56.5%).
- **Field reliability:** Firing success was 79% (109 of 138 attempts). Failures came mainly from angler damage to firing lines or snagged collars, plus wire chafing that shorted the solenoids.
- **Diver effect:** In 13 observed trials, no fish escaped and no significant difference was found between observed and unobserved nets (bluegill mean catch 16 ± 8 SD; white crappie mean catch 26 ± 28 SD). Observed fish showed no reaction to firing or rising of the net.
- **Structure attraction control:** Pop nets without structure, fired on June 2, caught no fish. One week later, nets with structure yielded a mean of 9 (± 5 SD) bluegills and 26 (± 6.5 SD) white crappies.
- **Reservoir efficiency:** Capture efficiency for bluegill and white crappie was 100% in 13 reservoir trials.
- **Limitations:** Sampling is labor-intensive, requiring divers to reset nets and at least two boat crew members. The floor prevents use on permanent natural structures, and the net has not been used for open-water assemblages.
- **Recommended modifications:** Silicone sealing of solenoid wire entry holes resolved short circuits. Marker buoys may reduce angler interference. Raising net sides at least 0.3 m above water may reduce fish escape during diver-assisted work. Greasing or siliconing solenoid connections may remove the need for waterproof casings, allowing deeper use if flotation is increased.
- **Conclusion:** The authors conclude that the pop net can accurately sample the entire fish assemblage at artificial structures across tested species, sizes, and temperatures of 2.5-30°C, and that it can be used to compare the relative attractiveness of structures without bias.
