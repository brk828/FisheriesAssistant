# Distribución Geográfica de Peces – Cuenca del Río Bravo

#ichthyology #marine-biology #biogeography #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #cichlidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This document is a **biogeographic distribution atlas** (comprising 158 sequential maps, "Mapa 1" through "Mapa 158") cataloging the presence/absence (●presente / ○ausente) of fish species across sampled localities within the **Mexican geohydrological basin of the Río Bravo (Rio Grande)**. Rather than a single-species study, the source functions as a comprehensive **ichthyofaunal checklist** spanning native, endemic, and introduced species across more than 25 families. The final map (Mapa 158) synthesizes data for **74 euryhaline and marine species** that penetrate the estuarine zone of the basin, indicating a strong freshwater-to-estuarine ecological gradient covered by the atlas. The work highlights extraordinary local endemism, particularly within [[Cyprinodon]] (Cyprinodontidae) and [[Gambusia]] (Poeciliidae), many restricted to isolated desert springs (e.g., Cuatro Ciénegas Basin-type systems).

## Background & Description

Because the source consists of distribution maps rather than anatomical descriptions, morphological data is *implicit* by family-level taxonomy rather than explicit measurements. Key morphological groupings represented:

- **[[Lepisosteidae]] (Gars)** — *Lepisosteus osseus*, *L. oculatus*, *Atractosteus spatula*: elongated ganoid-scaled bodies, elongated snouts, primitive actinopterygian morphology.
- **[[Cyprinidae]] (Minnows/Carps)** — largest family represented (~50 species, Mapas 11–58), including genera *Cyprinus*, *Carassius*, *Ctenopharyngodon*, *Notropis*, *Gila*, *Dionda*, *Cyprinella*, *Campostoma*, *Pimephales*, *Hybognathus*, *Codoma*, and the monotypic *Stypodon signifer*.
- **[[Catostomidae]] (Suckers)** — *Catostomus*, *Pantosteus*, *Ictiobus*, *Moxostoma*, *Cycleptus elongatus*: characterized by ventrally-positioned sucking mouths adapted for benthic feeding.
- **[[Ictaluridae]] (North American catfishes)** — *Ictalurus*, *Ameiurus*, *Pylodictis olivaris*, and the troglobitic **[[Prietella phreatophila]]** (blind catfish), notable for reduced/absent eyes and depigmentation associated with subterranean/phreatic habitats.
- **[[Cyprinodontidae]] (Pupfishes)** — highly speciose *Cyprinodon* radiation (17+ species, Mapas 79–94), exhibiting classic pupfish morphology: robust bodies, terminal upturned mouths, adapted to hypersaline/thermal spring conditions.
- **[[Poeciliidae]] (Livebearers)** — *Poecilia*, *Gambusia* (10 species), *Xiphophorus* (swordtails/platies): sexually dimorphic, viviparous reproduction, gonopodium present in males.
- **[[Goodeidae]]** — *Characodon* (3 species): endemic Mexican livebearers with distinctive trophotaeniae larval structures.
- **[[Atherinidae]] (Silversides)** — *Chirostoma* species flock, *Menidia*, *Membras*: laterally compressed, translucent bodies with silver lateral stripe.
- **[[Cichlidae]]** — *Cichlasoma cyanoguttatus*, *C. minckleyi*, *Oreochromis* spp. (introduced tilapia): deep-bodied, single nostril, pharyngeal jaw apparatus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Freshwater riverine core**: The majority of species (Mapas 1–148) occupy purely freshwater habitats within the Río Bravo/Rio Grande drainage, spanning main channel, tributaries, and spring-fed systems.
- **Spring/phreatic endemics**: Numerous narrow-range endemics are restricted to isolated desert spring complexes, notably the *Cyprinodon* radiation and *[[Gambusia]]* species (e.g., *G. senilis*, *G. alvarezi*, *G. hurtadoi*, *G. marshi*), reflecting classic allopatric speciation in Chihuahuan Desert relict aquatic habitats.
- **Estuarine/euryhaline transition zone**: Mapa 158 identifies **74 euryhaline and marine species** penetrating the river's estuary, including families such as [[Ariidae]] (*Arius felis*), [[Engraulidae]] (*Anchoa lyolepis*), [[Belonidae]] (*Strongylura marina*), [[Centropomidae]] (*Centropomus undecimalis*, snook), [[Carangidae]] (*Caranx hippos*, jack crevalle), [[Mugilidae]] (*Mugil cephalus*, striped mullet), [[Sciaenidae]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*, freshwater drum), and [[Eleotridae]] (*Dormitator maculatus*, *Gobiomorus dormitor*).
- **Introduced/non-native species**: Several taxa represent aquaculture or ornamental introductions, including *Cyprinus carpio* (common carp), *Carassius auratus* (goldfish), *Ctenopharyngodon idella* (grass carp), *Oncorhynchus mykiss* (rainbow trout), *Puntius conchonius* and *P. titteya* (ornamental barbs), *Poecilia reticulata* (guppy), and *Oreochromis aureus*/*O. mossambicus* (tilapia) — indicating significant anthropogenic alteration of the basin's ichthyofauna.
- **Anadromous/catadromous linkage**: *[[Anguilla rostrata]]* (American eel, Anguillidae) represents a catadromous life history connecting the basin to marine spawning grounds in the Sargasso Sea.

## Key Findings

1. **High taxonomic diversity**: The atlas documents approximately 150 fish taxa across at least 26 families within a single river basin, reflecting the Río Bravo's status as a major biodiversity corridor between Nearctic and Neotropical ichthyofaunal provinces.
2. **Cyprinidae dominance**: The family [[Cyprinidae]] is by far the most speciose group mapped (Mapas 11–58, ~48 maps), underscoring its historical radiation in North American freshwaters.
3. **Cyprinodontidae endemism hotspot**: The dense sequence of *Cyprinodon* maps (79–94) suggests the basin — likely correlating with the **Cuatro Ciénegas** and related desert spring systems — hosts one of the most concentrated pupfish speciation events in North America, several species now critically endangered or extinct in the wild.
4. **Cryptic/subterranean fauna**: *[[Prietella phreatophila]]* documents rare stygobitic (groundwater-adapted) ictalurid catfish distribution, an important indicator of aquifer connectivity.
5. **Freshwater-to-marine continuum**: The culminating synthesis map (Mapa 158, referencing "Cuadro 1") formally integrates 74 euryhaline/marine species, demonstrating the ecological continuity between the Río Bravo's freshwater reaches and its Gulf of Mexico estuary.
6. **Conservation relevance**: Numerous single-locality endemics (*Gila* spp., *Dionda* spp., *Notropis simus simus*, *Stypodon signifer*, multiple *Cyprinodon* spp.) point to significant extinction risk from habitat fragmentation, groundwater extraction, and invasive species pressure within this transboundary basin.
