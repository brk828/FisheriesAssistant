## Executive Summary

This trip report (Trip #6, 1991) documents field research conducted by BIO/WEST, Inc. for the Bureau of Reclamation as part of a multi-year effort to characterize the life history and ecology of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Grand Canyon]]. During the trip (June 6-22, 1991), researchers handled 51 humpback chub (16 juveniles, 35 adults), collected recapture data from 11 tagged individuals, and implanted radio transmitters in 4 new fish while recontacting 4 previously tagged individuals. Key findings suggest ongoing spawning activity in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), dispersal of adult chubs from the LCR confluence into the main channel, and continued presence of juvenile chubs downstream of the confluence, consistent with spring runoff-driven dispersal patterns.

## Background & Description

- **Study Species**: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a federally endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River Basin, notable for its distinctive nuchal hump and adaptation to fast, turbid canyon waters.
- **Study Purpose**: Part of an ongoing series of trips (Trip #1 through #7, 1991) to characterize humpback chub life history, movement, reproductive biology, and habitat use in the [[Colorado River]] through Grand Canyon.
- **Personnel**: Research conducted by BIO/WEST, Inc. biologists in collaboration with Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Grand Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES), OARS boating support, and volunteers, with logistical support from the National Park Service (NPS).
- **Methods**: Multiple gear types were employed, including:
  - Electrofishing (EL)
  - Trammel nets (TL, TK - varying mesh/size)
  - Gill nets (GM, GP, GX - experimental gradient mesh)
  - Minnow traps (MT)
  - Hoop nets (HL, HM, HS - large/medium/small diameter)
  - Radiotelemetry (surgically implanted transmitters)
- Fish were measured (total length, weight), checked for existing tags (Carlin, Floy, PIT), and in some cases surgically implanted with radio transmitters to track movement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary Habitat Zone**: The confluence of the [[Little Colorado River]] and the Colorado River main channel (~River Mile 61.4) is a critical habitat area, serving as both a nursery zone for juveniles and a probable spawning area for adults.
- **Juvenile Distribution**: All 16 juvenile humpback chub (TL = 62-191 mm) were captured at or below the LCR confluence, extending downstream to Lava Canyon (RM 65.5). This distribution is consistent with prior (May) observations and suggests juveniles are transported or actively move out of the LCR during spring runoff events.
- **Adult Movement & Habitat Use**: Adult chubs (TL = 205-432 mm) were found dispersing from the LCR/main channel confluence into the main channel, with netting catch rates between RM 60.0-61.7 increasing relative to lower rates observed in May, indicating post-spawning dispersal behavior.
- **Substrate Preference**: Juveniles were predominantly captured via electrofishing or minnow traps along main channel shorelines characterized by boulder and sand substrates.
- **Flow Dynamics & Sampling Challenges**: Extreme and rapid flow fluctuations in the main channel (likely due to dam operations upstream) created significant sampling difficulties, including net fouling, marker inundation, and the loss of one 1" trammel net (RM 61.8) due to a large, rapid stage increase on June 14.
- **Co-occurring Species**: Other fish species noted in the sampling gear-type key include flannelmouth sucker (FM), bluehead sucker (BH), speckled dace (SD), rainbow trout (RB), brown trout (BR), channel catfish (CC), carp (CP), and striped bass (SB), indicating a mixed native-nonnative fish community in the study reach.

## Key Findings

1. **Population Sample**: 51 humpback chub handled total — 16 juveniles (TL 62-191 mm) and 35 adults (TL 205-432 mm); 11 were recaptures (Carlin, Floy, or PIT tag).
2. **Reproductive Condition Observations** (adults):
   - 1 male tubercled; 2 males and 1 female showed reproductive coloration.
   - 2 females were flaccid in the abdominal region (post-spawn indicator).
   - 2 females had ventral abrasions suggestive of recent spawning attempts.
   - 1 female was very tight in the abdomen, possibly still gravid.
   - No fish were captured with expressible gametes.
3. **Radiotelemetry**:
   - 4 new humpback chub implanted with radio transmitters during Trip #6.
   - 4 previously tagged chubs recontacted: one in the LCR (~1100 m upstream of confluence), three in the main channel.
   - One newly implanted fish moved into the LCR five days post-implantation (June 17).
   - One recaptured radio-tagged fish (June 12) showed partial dehiscence of the surgical incision (3 sutures failed); re-sutured and released successfully, fish otherwise in good condition.
4. **Spawning Inference**: Two radio-tagged chubs located in the LCR during the trip support the hypothesis that spawning activity may still be ongoing in the [[Little Colorado River]] during this period (June).
5. **Dispersal Pattern**: Increased catch rates in the main channel (RM 60.0-61.7) compared to May suggest chubs disperse from the LCR/confluence area into the main channel following spawning.
6. **Juvenile Distribution Consistency**: Presence of juveniles between the LCR confluence and Lava Canyon (RM 65.5) corroborates prior findings of juvenile displacement/movement during spring runoff.
7. **Gear Loss Incident**: A 1" trammel net was lost on June 14 at RM 61.8 due to rapid flow fluctuation; net believed snagged/rolled into boulders on channel bottom, deemed non-hazardous to boating traffic but non-functional.
8. **Operational Recommendations**:
   - Remote telemetry stations above/below the LCR confluence to be dismantled in Trip #7 due to low fish movement into the LCR and NPS visual-impact agreements during peak rafting season.
   - Relocation of one telemetry station to RM 60.5 with an omni-directional whip antenna recommended to monitor diel behavior of fish residing between RM 60.0-61.3 while minimizing visual impact.
