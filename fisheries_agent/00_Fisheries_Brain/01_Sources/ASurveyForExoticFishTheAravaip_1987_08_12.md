# A survey for exotic fish the Aravaipa Watershed: a collaborative project of the Staffod District Office of US Bureau of Land Management, Nongame Branch of Arizona Game and Fish Department, and Defenders of Wildlife

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #invasive-species

## Executive Summary
This 1987 field survey, conducted collaboratively by the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (Safford District), the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Nongame Branch), and Defenders of Wildlife, assessed the distribution and abundance of nonnative fish species within the [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed, a tributary of the [[Gila River]] drainage in Arizona. [[Aravaipa Creek]] is ecologically significant as one of the last remaining streams supporting nearly the complete historic assemblage of native fishes from the Gila River drainage. The study aimed to identify source populations of exotic fish in upland stock tanks that could disperse into the creek during flood events, and to eradicate such populations where feasible. Of the numerous stock tanks surveyed on BLM and Department of Wildlife (DOW) lands, only one tank was found to harbor fish (a substantial population of [[Green Sunfish]]), which was subsequently drained and chemically treated to eliminate the population.

## Background & Description
The source text is a distributional/management survey and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of the fish species. Limited size data was recorded for the one confirmed exotic population:
- **[[Green Sunfish]]**: The population found in the treated stock tank consisted entirely of individuals **no greater than 4 inches (~10 cm) in length**, suggesting a young or stunted population isolated in a small tank environment.

No morphological data was provided for [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Largemouth Bass]], or [[Western Mosquitofish]] in this survey.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
### Native Fish Community
- [[Aravaipa Creek]] harbors a unique assemblage of **seven native fish species**, representing nearly the entirety of fish species native to the [[Gila River]] drainage.
- This level of native species intactness is now found only in Aravaipa (Arizona) and a few locations in New Mexico.
- Native Southwestern fishes are ecologically adapted to the **highly fluctuating, unregulated flow regimes** characteristic of desert streams, allowing them to persist through natural flood disturbance better than exotic competitors.
- Two native species are formally listed as **Threatened** by both the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the Arizona Game and Fish Department:
  - **[[Spikedace]]**: persists in only ~6% of its historical range.
  - **[[Loach Minnow]]**: persists in only ~15% of its historical range.
- The aquatic system supports an adjacent **riparian ecosystem**, itself recognized as endangered throughout the western United States.

### Exotic Fish Threats
- Documented exotic species occasionally collected in Aravaipa Creek include:
  - [[Green Sunfish]]
  - [[Black Bullhead]]
  - [[Largemouth Bass]]
  - [[Western Mosquitofish]]
- These species remain **rare** in the mainstem creek as of the survey date but pose a long-term threat via **competition and predation** against native fishes.
- **Habitat alteration** (water diversions, dams, groundwater pumping) compounds this threat by creating stabilized-flow conditions that favor exotic fish establishment over native species adapted to flood-prone, unregulated systems.

### Stock Tank Habitat Survey
- BLM-managed earthen stock tanks throughout the watershed (Townships 5S–7S, Ranges 17E–20E) were surveyed as potential exotic fish reservoirs/source populations.
- Methods: **gill nets, seines, and dip nets** used to determine fish presence/absence; incidental notes made on invertebrates, amphibians, and aquatic vegetation.
- Co-occurring taxa recorded across tanks included:
  - Salamanders
  - Frogs
  - Leeches
  - Water scorpions
  - Aquatic vegetation: *Potamogeton*
- Tank physical characteristics recorded: surface area (m²), dam height (m), water depth (m), and water clarity (m) — used as proxies for flood-connectivity risk to the main creek.

## Key Findings
- **Fish presence was confirmed in only one stock tank** out of dozens surveyed on BLM/DOW lands in the Aravaipa watershed.
- The positive tank contained a large population of **[[Green Sunfish]]**, all individuals ≤4 inches long.
- With landowner cooperation, this tank was **drained and treated with chlorine on August 5, 1987**, with the goal of eliminating the exotic population.
- Treatment efficacy was scheduled for **re-evaluation after one year**.
- Prior to this survey, no documentation existed regarding the distribution/abundance of exotic fish in watershed stock tanks; exotic species had been occasionally collected in the creek itself but their source population(s) were previously unknown.
- The survey supports the conclusion that upland stock tanks represent a plausible, but currently limited, vector for exotic fish introduction into [[Aravaipa Creek]] during flood events.
- Findings reinforce the conservation priority of [[Aravaipa Creek]] as critical habitat for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], and support continued monitoring/management of exotic species vectors under BLM Wilderness designation and partner conservation efforts.
