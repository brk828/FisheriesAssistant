# Distribution, Habitat Use, and Growth of Age-0 Colorado Squawfish in the Green River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This study by Tyus and Haines (1991) documents the early life history, distribution, habitat use, and growth of age-0 [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the [[Green River Basin]] (Colorado/Utah), based on 11,379 fish captured via drift nets and seines from 1979-1988. The [[Colorado Squawfish]], the largest North American minnow and a federally endangered species, spawns in two known locations: [[Yampa Canyon]] and [[Gray Canyon]]. Larvae hatch after peak spring runoff during declining flows and rising temperatures, then drift downstream ~130-150 km to concentrate in nursery [[Backwater Habitats]] by autumn. Abundance and growth were negatively correlated with high summer/autumn flows, which inundate and reduce nursery backwater habitat — implicating altered flow regimes from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] in the species' historic decline. Overwinter survival appeared adequate under natural conditions, though sampling methodology limited definitive mortality estimates.

## Background & Description
- **Larvae**: <25 mm total length (TL); captured via drift nets and fine-mesh seines (0.63-mm² mesh).
- **Postlarvae**: 25–70 mm TL; sampled with larger seines (3.2 × 4.8-mm woven mesh).
- **Growth trajectory**:
  - Average TL at capture in October: ~40 mm (range 29.0–47.3 mm).
  - Age-1 fish in spring: mean 45.2 mm TL (N = 1,193), larger than the same cohort measured the previous autumn (mean 41.5 mm, N = 1,212) — attributed to growth and/or size-selective mortality.
  - Otolith-aged wild postlarvae (mean 38.2 mm TL) were ~13 days older than laboratory-derived age estimates of equivalent size, suggesting **slower growth in wild fish** compared to hatchery-reared reference fish (raised at 21–26°C, Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery).
- **Hatching-date estimation equations** (back-calculated from TL):
  - For TL < 22 mm: Y = -76.71 + 17.49(TL) - 1.056(TL²) + 0.0221(TL³)
  - For 22 ≤ TL ≤ 47 mm: Y = -26.64 + 2.78(TL)
  - (Y = days post-hatch; derived from [[Haynes Et Al 1984]] and modified by [[Nesler Et Al 1988]])

## Ecological & Environmental Context
### Spawning & Hatching
- Two confirmed spawning areas in the basin: lower [[Yampa River]] ([[Yampa Canyon]], identified 1981) and [[Gray Canyon]] on the [[Green River]] (identified 1983).
- Spawning/hatching coincides with **declining post-runoff flows** and **rising water temperatures**, lasting 2–6 weeks.
- Mean hatching water temperature: ~22.9°C (range 19–26°C).
- Yampa/upper Green River fish hatched ~54 days after peak flow (range 20–74 d); lower Green River fish hatched ~40 days after peak flow (range 8–55 d).
- No larvae detected in the Green River upstream of the Yampa confluence or in the lower White River — confirming absence of spawning in those reaches.

### Larval Drift & Downstream Dispersal
- Larvae rapidly drift downstream post-hatching (e.g., ~29 km in ~6 days from the Yampa spawning site).
- By late September/October, postlarvae concentrate ~150 km downstream of each spawning site:
  - Zone 1: RK 335–450 (downstream of Yampa Canyon)
  - Zone 2: RK 32–160 (downstream of Gray Canyon)
- Postlarvae were notably absent for ~80 km immediately below the Yampa/Green confluence in October, consistent with rapid transit through that reach.

### Habitat Use
- **Backwaters** were the dominant nursery habitat, accounting for 84% of postlarval catch in lower-gradient reaches (vs. shorelines, side channels, runs, eddies).
- Preferred backwater characteristics (vs. backwaters without squawfish):
  - Warmer (mean ~17.0°C, ~2°C above backwaters lacking the species)
  - Deeper (mean 38 cm)
  - Larger (mean 826 m²; strongest size-selection seen in upper Green River: 1,158 m² occupied vs. 269 m² unoccupied)
  - More turbid (G-test, P < 0.01)
- No significant relationship detected between substrate type and squawfish presence/absence.
- Low-gradient strata (A, B, E, F — sand/silt substrate) yielded much higher catches (mean 12.4 fish/100 m²) than high-gradient strata (C, D, G — boulder/cobble; mean 4.1 fish/100 m²).
- Species absent from 37% of backwaters sampled in lower Green River and 59% in upper Green River.

### Flow Regime Effects
- Fish abundance (log fish/100 m²) inversely correlated with late-summer streamflow:
  - Upper Green River: r = -0.80 (P < 0.01)
  - Lower Green River: r = -0.86 (P < 0.01)
- Total length also inversely correlated with late-summer flow, most strongly in upper Green River (r = -0.81, P < 0.01).
- High flows (e.g., 1983, 1984) reduced backwater habitat availability and coincided with reduced age-0 abundance and growth — linked to regulated releases from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]].
- Upper Green River had ~150% more backwaters per km than lower Green River, potentially explaining why upper-river fish were equal to or larger than lower-river fish despite colder water (contrary to initial hypothesis of temperature-limited growth).

### Overwintering
- Survival evidence: 83% of age-0 fish sampled in Nov/Dec had food in guts; fish as small as 22–39 mm TL survived winter in the river (matching size range used in lab starvation studies by [[Thompson 1989]], where such small fish showed significant mortality under simulated conditions of 3–4°C for 210 days without food).
- Spring catches exceeded prior-autumn catches in 4 of 8 comparisons (ratios 0.2–9.2× higher), suggesting sampling-vulnerability differences between seasons rather than true mortality patterns.
- Authors concluded overwinter mortality of age-0 [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the Green River is likely **nonsignificant** under natural winter conditions, contrasting with laboratory predictions.

## Key Findings
- **11,379 age-0 Colorado squawfish** captured 1979–1988: 601 larvae (drift nets, June–July), 3,079 postlarvae (seines, July–August), 6,459 postlarvae (seines, September–October), and 1,240 (spring seines).
- Confirmed **two major spawning/nursery pathways**: Yampa Canyon → lower Yampa/upper Green River nursery zone; Gray Canyon → lower Green River nursery zone.
- Postlarvae consistently concentrate **~130–150 km downstream** of spawning sites by autumn each year — a robust, repeatable pattern across multiple years.
- **Backwater habitats are critical nursery habitat**; their quality (temperature, depth, size, turbidity) directly influences occupancy by young squawfish.
- **Summer/autumn flow regulation** (via Flaming Gorge Dam) reduces backwater habitat availability, negatively impacting age-0 abundance and growth — supporting management recommendations for more natural flow regimes (see [[Tyus And Karp 1989]]).
- Contrary to hypothesis, **colder temperatures in the upper Green River did not reduce growth**, likely due to compensating habitat abundance (more backwaters) in that reach.
- Seine-based autumn-to-spring comparisons were **unreliable indicators of overwinter mortality** due to seasonal differences in capture vulnerability.
- Study supports management of the Green River mainstem as a **squawfish nursery area**, emphasizing restoration of historical summer flow patterns to optimize backwater nursery habitat and support recovery of this endangered species.
