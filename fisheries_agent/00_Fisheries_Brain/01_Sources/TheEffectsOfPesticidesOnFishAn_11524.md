# Effects of Pesticides on Fish and Wildlife: 1964 Research Findings

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pesticide-ecotoxicology

## Executive Summary
This is Fish and Wildlife Service Circular 226 (August 1965), the fifth report under Public Law 85-582 (1958). It summarizes 1964 pesticide research by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and the Bureau of Commercial Fisheries. Work was done at [[Patuxent Wildlife Research Center]], [[Denver Wildlife Research Center]], the [[Fish-Pesticide Research Laboratory]] in Denver, the [[Gulf Breeze Biological Laboratory]], and cooperative university units. The report documents the near-universal presence of chlorinated hydrocarbon residues in ecosystems. It stresses [[Biomagnification]] through food chains and a shift toward physiological effects on reproduction and survival. Bird studies take up much of the document. The fish, invertebrate and estuarine sections give laboratory LC50/EC50 data, pond field experiments, and monitoring methods based on oysters and fish.

## Background & Description
- **Scope:** bird and mammal work (raptors, waterfowl, residues and methodology) is led by Patuxent and Denver. Fish work is led by Dr. Oliver B. Cope (Denver) and Dr. Philip A. Butler (Gulf Breeze).
- **Fish toxicology:** bioassays use [[Bluegill]], [[Rainbow Trout]] and channel catfish. Estuarine tests use shrimp, spot, sheepshead minnow, longnose killifish, striped mullet and [[Eastern Oyster]].
- **Metrics:** LC50, EC50, TLm, ppm and ppb (defined in the report's abbreviation list).
- **Avian protocol (Patuxent):** dietary LC50 over 5 days on toxic diet plus 3 days clean, with DDT as the standard, probit analysis, and mallard, bobwhite and pheasant as test species.
- **New laboratories:** a fish-pesticide laboratory is planned for Columbia, Missouri. Four substations (Western Fish Disease, Western Fish Nutrition, Salmon-Cultural, and Sandy Hook Marine) were established.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Widespread contamination:** residues occur in mud, invertebrates, plants, fish and birds, even in fish from remote streams and the high seas. Animals found dead after one chemical often contain others.
- **Lake Michigan (Green Bay):**
  - Mud averaged 0.014 ± 0.005 ppm DDT, DDE and DDD.
  - The crustacean *Pontoporeia affinis* concentrated residues about 50 times over mud, and fish about 10 times over *Pontoporeia*.
  - Door County used about 70,000 lbs of DDT a year.
- **Wildlife refuges (Tule Lake, Lower Klamath, McNary, Deer Flat):**
  - Suspended material held far more insecticide than filtered water.
  - Deer Flat sediments averaged 40.0 ppm, and aquatic vegetation there 30.3 ppm, yet whole fish only 0.5 ppm.
  - Invertebrates may help remove chlorinated hydrocarbons from water.
  - Toxaphene in agricultural waste water killed fish-eating birds at Tule Lake in 1960–62; fish held up to 8 ppm.
- **Forest and range spraying:** DDT at 0.75 lb/acre (Knox Mountain, California) and 1 lb/acre (Salmon National Forest, Idaho) left plant residues for at least 3 months. Malathion applications caused small or no detectable changes in bird counts in some studies, and a 74% vs. 40% decline in insectivorous songbirds on treated vs. check areas at Yosemite.
- **Rocky Mountain Arsenal lakes:** aldrin and dieldrin contamination (annual deaths of several thousand waterfowl over 10–15 years) was remedied by draining and removing 4–6 inches of soil, plus a closed cooling system. The cost was about $265,000.

## Key Findings

### Fish toxicity (Denver, Sandy Hook, Patuxent)
- Tests used 1964 formulations at 55°F (rainbow) and 75°F (bluegill). Endrin was the most toxic common insecticide: 96-hr LC50 0.86 µg/L for New Zealand rainbow trout and 0.25 µg/L for bluegills. Dieldrin 96-hr LC50 was 13 µg/L (rainbow) and 2.8 µg/L (bluegill). Aldrin was 31 and 5.2 µg/L.
- Toxicity of endrin, toxaphene and lindane rose with temperature; DDT and malathion did not follow this pattern.
- Herbicides were far less toxic (mostly mg/L ranges), for example Dalapon 96-hr LC50 105,000 µg/L for bluegills (table headers list mg/L and µg/L inconsistently). APO (a chemosterilant) had no effect at 100,000 for 96 hrs on rainbow trout.
- Sandy Hook (7 organochlorine insecticides):
  - Higher temperature generally accelerated mortality, but *Fundulus heteroclitus* were most sensitive at 20–25°C.
  - Eels were more sensitive at higher salinity; grass shrimp were less sensitive.
  - *Fundulus* exposed under 2 hours to LC75 (24-hr) concentrations showed negligible mortality, while exposures of 12 hours or more killed nearly all.
- Water hardness had no significant effect on herbicide toxicity, except silvex to black bullhead, which was twice as toxic in the hardest water.
- Aquatic invertebrates (Denver): LC50s were established for 19 insecticides and 12 herbicides for the stonefly *Pteronarcys californica*, plus other insects. Invertebrate toxicity order differs from fish, and time matters more. For *Pteronarcys*, lindane (5.0 µg/L) was most toxic of the insecticides and rotenone (2900.0 µg/L) the least.

### Experimental field studies
- **Malathion and cutthroat trout (Jackson, WY):** brain cholinesterase declined after each treatment and recovered over 30 days. The greatest decline reached 41% of control. Liver lesions appeared within 2 days and disappeared within 30 days. No significant differences in growth or mortality appeared.
- **Mirex and bluegills (Marion, AL):**
  - Whole-body residues were 3.39 ppm (5 mg/kg weekly ration), 3.01 ppm (3 mg/kg) and 1.05 ppm (1 mg/kg).
  - Uptake was very rapid.
  - Residues in vegetation exceeded those in fish.
  - No differences were seen in serum protein, hematocrit or growth.
- **Sodium arsenite and bluegills (La Crosse, WI):**
  - Fish accumulated arsenic in proportion to treatment.
  - One group with 2.61 ppm whole-body at 16 weeks had gills and digestive tract at 17.60 ppm, liver 11.60 and kidney 5.89.
  - Kidney and liver damage and ovarian lesions appeared.
  - Bottom fauna in heavily treated ponds fell to under half of control; rotifers, cladocera and copepods were depressed.
- **Diquat (1.0 and 3.0 ppm):** bluegill survival was unaffected; adults gained slightly less weight.
- **Casoron (dichlobenil):** at 40, 20 and 10 ppm, mortality reached about 25% in heavily treated ponds. At Denver (10 lb/acre, 0.6 ppm) no acute mortality occurred; residues of 4–8 ppm were found in all four fish species at 34 days.
- **Paraquat (1 ppm):** it was undetectable in water at 32 days; fish accumulated residues, with no pathology in 40 specimens.
- **Parathion in marsh:** 0.85 lb/acre caused serious *Gambusia affinis* mortality, followed by rapid repopulation. Six weekly 0.05 lb/acre applications did not. Water residues fell from 0.40 to 0.01 ppm in 8 days.
- **Cygon (dimethoate), 1/4 lb/acre:** a 1000-acre spruce budworm plot in Idaho showed no measured effects on fish or aquatic invertebrates.

### Commercial fishery and estuarine studies
- **Pascagoula:** 74 residue analyses were completed. Processed oysters held dieldrin at 0.170 and 0.230 ppm, and endrin at 0.025–0.180 ppm in three samples. Other samples held only DDT and its metabolites.
- **Auke Bay, AK (4-year study):** after DDT at 1/4 lb/acre, insect populations recovered to prespray levels within 14 months. Fish tissue kept maximum residues of DDT 1.4, DDE 2.6 and DDD 0.23 ppm.
- **Gulf Breeze acute toxicity:** phytoplankton productivity, oyster shell growth (96-hr EC50) and shrimp and juvenile fish (24- and 48-hr EC50) were tested. Shrimp were highly sensitive to insecticides (Zytron 48-hr EC50 0.0003 ppm; Stauffer N-2790 0.0019 ppm). Many herbicides showed no effect at 1.0 ppm.
- **Chronic toxicity:**
  - Endrin at 0.6 ppb kills half of shrimp in 24 hours, and 0.025 ppb allowed 15% survival over 2 months.
  - DDT at 1.0 ppb killed blue crabs in 8 days, while 0.25 ppb was tolerated for 5 months.
  - Oysters concentrated DDT up to 70,000 times over 0.1 ppb in 40 days. After 12 days at 1.0 ppb, whole oysters held 14–20 ppm, with the gonads and gametes storing large residues.
  - Spot survived 5 months at 0.01 ppb Telodrin and 8 months at 0.05 ppb endrin (then twice as sensitive on re-exposure).
  - Offspring of DDT-selected sheepshead minnows were more sensitive, not resistant.
  - Pinfish die in 48 hours at 0.01 ppb DDT even though healthy fish carried 13 ppm.
- **Monitoring methodology:**
  - Oysters were the best monitor organisms (342 oyster analyses against 161 for mussels and clams). They respond down to 10 parts per trillion.
  - In 152 fish samples (19 species), DDT residues ranged from 0.01 to 13.7 ppm. Fish-kill fish held 0.30–5.72 ppm.
  - Crustaceans were unsatisfactory monitors.
  - Brain cholinesterase depression in fish (at concentrations as low as 0.5 ppb) is a promising indicator of organophosphorus pollution.

### Wildlife highlights (condensed)
- **Ospreys:** Connecticut eggs averaged 6.5 ppm DDT, DDE and DDD (adjusted wet weight) vs. 3.4 ppm in Maryland. Nest success was 13% vs. 58%.
- **Bald eagles:** young were produced in only 3 of 16 mid-Atlantic nests. Brain residues in DDT-poisoned birds were similar regardless of dose.
- **Herring gulls (Green Bay):** body fat averaged 390 ppm DDT and 1,925 ppm DDE, and reproduction was 0.41 young fledged per pair.
- **Dutch elm spraying:** robin populations fell 70% in Hanover, New Hampshire.
- **Loon die-off:** about 3,000 loons died in 1963. Type E botulism was confirmed, and residues were far below fatal levels.
- **Antarctic:** penguins and a crabeater seal carried 1.3 to 152 ppb DDT and metabolites.
- **Method comparison:** acetonitrile partitioning recovered dieldrin far better than acid wash, and the two methods were not significantly different for DDT.

### Research needs
Priorities include combined effects of pesticides and other pollutants, diagnostic tissue levels, egg residues and hatchability, residue storage and loss rates, and effects of environmental stress and behavior. They also include quick screening tests and surveys of contaminated ecosystems and declining populations.
