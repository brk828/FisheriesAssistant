# Catalog of Type Specimens of the Darters (Pisces, Percidae, Etheostomatini)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1966 *Proceedings of the [[United States National Museum]]* catalog, by [[Bruce B. Collette]] and [[Lewis W. Knapp]], inventories the type specimens of the North American darter tribe [[Etheostomatini]] (genera [[Percina]], [[Ammocrypta]] and [[Etheostoma]]). The authors state that about 212 specific and subspecific names have been proposed for roughly 100 valid described species, with about a dozen species awaiting description. The catalog lists the location of holotypes, lectotypes, syntypes, paralectotypes and paratypes, reviews the genus-group names, and records the status of numerous nominal forms, many of which have lost or unlocated type material.

## Background & Description

- **Group:** Darters are small freshwater percid fishes restricted to North America, arranged in the tribe Etheostomatini following [[Collette 1963]].
- **Scope:** The authors examined, or verified the location of, types for all nominal forms in the three recognized genera. Generic and subgeneric arrangement follows [[Bailey]] (in [[Bailey, Winn and Smith 1954]] and [[Bailey and Gosline 1955]]).
- **Data recorded per name:** original citation, catalog number, number of specimens and standard length range (mm), locality, collector, date, and current taxonomic status.
- **Abbreviations used for lectotype descriptions:** D (dorsal spines and rays), A (anal spines and rays), LL (lateral line scales), POM (preoperculomandibular pores), INF (infraorbital pores).
- **Museum network:** The paper lists about 30 institutions holding darter types, including the [[United States National Museum]], [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]], [[California Academy of Sciences]], [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], [[Cornell University]] and [[Stanford University]]. Several collections are marked with an asterisk to indicate personal examination by the authors.
- **Authority:** The paper is the fifth in a series on percid systematics by the senior author.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The catalog is taxonomic and nomenclatural rather than ecological, but it documents the geographic range of the group through type localities. These include rivers and streams of the Ohio, Mississippi, Tennessee, Cumberland, Potomac, Roanoke, Holston, Neuse, Tar, White, Ouachita, Red and Rio Grande drainages, Great Lakes shorelines (Lake Superior, Lake Huron, Lake Erie), springs (for example [[San Marcos Springs]] and [[Comal Springs]] in Texas), and a few lakes and ponds (for example Lake Waccamaw, North Carolina, and Lake Maxinkuckee, Indiana). Habitat notes in the original descriptions are sparse and are mostly limited to contextual remarks (for example "in the springs and caves near Lexington," or "small rocky brook").

Several historical events affected specimen survival, which the authors treat as part of the status record:
- About 1860, types of 16 species described by [[Charles Girard]] were sent from the USNM to the [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]] for [[Frederick Putnam]] and were thought lost by Jordan; some were later transferred to the University of Michigan by [[Carl L. Hubbs]].
- Specimens at Indiana University were later transferred to Stanford, the California Academy of Sciences and Michigan, with some still missing.
- The 1906 San Francisco earthquake damaged or mixed several collections (SU bottles).
- Some types were distributed as "duplicate sets" to schools and libraries (USNM 36154, [[Etheostoma tuscumbia]]).

## Key Findings

1. **Scale of the group and nomenclature:** About 212 specific and subspecific names exist for approximately 100 valid species; about a dozen species await description.

2. **Type-material losses:** No original material was saved for the five darter species described by [[Constantine Rafinesque]]. Type material of 21 nominal species could not be located. Several other forms are listed as "X" (no type material or almost certainly lost).

3. **Nomenclatural actions:** The authors selected numerous lectotypes to stabilize nomenclature, many with the help of named specialists (for example [[Charles F. Cole]], [[Edward C. Raney]], [[Robert V. Miller]], [[Chu-fa Tsai]], [[William J. Richards]], [[Donald A. Distler]]).

4. **Genus-group names:** The paper reviews available and unavailable genus-group names, with type-species and priority information. Examples include:
   - *[[Ammocrypta]]* Jordan, 1877 (type-species *A. beanii*)
   - *[[Boleosoma]]* DeKay, 1842 (type-species *B. tessellatum*, a junior-synonym-based designation)
   - *[[Etheostoma]]* Rafinesque, 1819 (type-species *E. blennioides*, verified by ICZN Opinion 14)
   - *[[Percina]]* Haldeman, 1842 (type-species *Perca nebulosa*)
   - Unavailable names include *[[Nivicola]]*, *[[Pleurolepis]]*, *[[Richia]]* and *[[Torrentaria]]*, all preoccupied.

5. **Selected synonymies and status corrections:** Examples recorded in the catalog include:
   - *Alvordius aspro* (type probably lost; USNM and BMNH specimens not considered types).
   - *Boleichthys warreni* (holotype MCZ 32952, assigned to *Etheostoma exile*).
   - *Etheostoma davisoni*, which was then considered a synonym of *E. stigmaeum* but was in process of being recognized as distinct by [[William M. Howell]].
   - *Hadropterus notogrammus* (replacement name whose proposed types were invalid because the nomenclatural type must be that of *H. maculatus*, USNM 1157).
   - *Hadropterus maculatus* identified as a junior homonym, with *Percina notogramma* as the replacement name.
   - *Etheostoma tuscumbia* (duplicate-set distribution shows that all USNM 36154 material should be listed as syntypes).

6. **Hybrid record:** *Hadropterus evermanni* is interpreted as a natural hybrid between *Percina caprodes semifasciata* and *P. maculata*, supported by Trautman (1948) for Lake Erie tributaries.

7. **Catalog structure:** The paper concludes with a checklist of nominal species (summarized here) indicating the holding institutions for each name, and a literature cited section of roughly 80 references spanning 1820 to 1966.

8. **Conservation note:** The authors stress that catalog numbers, original labels and locality data should be checked before any type designation. Numerous "types" were found to be non-type or misidentified material, so the catalog is intended to stabilize nomenclature and provide verification for future systematic work.
