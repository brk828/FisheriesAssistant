# Descriptive Catalogue of North American Fish-Like Vertebrates: Selected Freshwater Taxa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This excerpt is a taxonomic catalogue from the [[United States National Museum]] (Bulletin 47, Part I, by [[David Starr Jordan]] and [[Barton Warren Evermann]]). It is not a research study. It gives formal descriptions, diagnostic keys, type localities, synonymy and distribution for freshwater fishes of North America and nearby islands and Central America. The excerpt covers four families: Poeciliidae ([[Heterandria]], [[Lebistes]]), Cyprinidae (minnows and chubs such as [[Agosia]], [[Gila]], [[Hybopsis]], [[Notropis]], [[Leuciscus]] and [[Campostoma]]), and Catostomidae (suckers, [[Catostomus]]). The main output is a set of diagnostic characters and distributional records for each taxon.

## Background & Description

The catalogue follows a standard taxonomic layout for each species:

- **Diagnostic measurements** as ratios of head length, body depth and eye size, plus fin ray counts (D = dorsal, A = anal, V = ventral) and scale counts in the lateral line.
- **Morphological notes** on mouth position, barbels, teeth formula, fin placement and body shape.
- **Coloration** in life or in preservative.
- **Type locality** and type specimen catalogue numbers where known.
- **Synonymy** listing earlier names and their original publications.

Key genus-level keys in the text separate taxa by:
- Number of barbels and whether a maxillary barbel is present (a key character separating [[Hybopsis]] from [[Notropis]]).
- Pharyngeal tooth formula (for example 2, 5-4, 2 in [[Gila]] and [[Leuciscus]]).
- Presence or absence of a grinding surface on the teeth.
- Scale size and number in the lateral line.

Some names are noted as uncertain. The authors state that their knowledge of [[Lebistes]] is based on a secondhand abridgment of De Filippi's account, and they doubt the sexual characters assigned to it.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The text includes only brief ecological remarks, mostly on abundance and habitat:

- **[[Heterandria formosa]]** is "exceedingly abundant" in black water of swamps and ditches in South Carolina and Florida, alongside [[Gambusia Affinis]]. It is described as "said to be the smallest known fish."
- **[[Heterandria occidentalis]]** is locally abundant in springs and ditches in the Gila Basin and Sonora.
- **[[Agosia chrysogaster]]** occurs in mountain brooks of the Gila River tributaries.
- **[[Gila elegans]]** feeds almost entirely on gastropods and caddis worms, which it crushes with strong pharyngeal teeth. The young differ from adults, and the species is very tenacious of life. It takes the hook freely (quoting W. I. Carpenter).
- **[[Gila robusta]]** is very common up to the foot of the mountains in Colorado. Its flesh is "full of small bones and nearly worthless as food."
- **[[Campostoma anomalum]]** is herbivorous. Its intestine is 6 to 9 times the body length, wrapped around the air bladder, a feature unique among known fishes according to the authors.
- **[[Campostoma priceri]]** lives in springs in the Chiricahua Mountains, Arizona. These springs drain onto the desert and rarely overflow into the river.
- **[[Catostomus commersonii]]** is "excessively abundant" from Massachusetts west to Kansas and is "the commonest of the suckers."
- **[[Catostomus occidentalis]]** is abundant in the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers of California.

Range data show a strong association with western desert and mountain basins (Gila, Colorado, Yaqui, Great Basin) and with eastern Atlantic coastal drainages.

## Key Findings

**Poeciliidae (livebearers)**
- *Heterandria formosa*: female 1 inch, male length illegible in the scan; described as the smallest known fish.
- *Heterandria pleurospilus* (Guatemala, Lake of Duenas): females 2 inches, males 1 inch.
- *Heterandria versicolor* (San Domingo): known from only 2 females, not seen by the authors.
- *Heterandria occidentalis*: 2½ inches; closer to *Poecilia* than to typical *Heterandria*. It looks like a [[Gambusia]] but has a much smaller mouth.
- *Lebistes pecilioides* (Barbados): 1½ inches. The genus is poorly known and the sexual characters are doubtful.

**Cyprinidae (minnows and chubs)**
- *Agosia* species (Gila tributaries, Columbia River basin, Nevada): *A. chrysogaster* (males with a yellow or orange belly), *A. velifera* (three specimens from Pahranagat Valley, Nevada), *A. umatilla* (Columbia River at Umatilla and Payette River), and *A. falcata* (Columbia River basin, locally abundant).
- *Gila* species: *G. elegans* (12 inches; Colorado and Gila rivers), *G. robusta* (16 inches; Colorado and Gila tributaries), and *G. seminuda* (6 inches; Rio Virgen, Utah; belly naked, described as a transition form to *Leuciscus*).
- *Hybopsis tetranemus* (2 inches): four barbels in total, two long barbels at each mouth angle; tributaries of the Arkansas River in Kansas.
- *Notropis* species: *N. lutrensis* (2¾ inches; southern Illinois to South Dakota, with brilliant breeding colors), *N. formosus* (Rio Mimbres, Chihuahua), *N. frigidus* (Rio Frio, Texas), *N. ornatus* (Chihuahua River), and the subspecies *N. hudsonius amarus* (Delaware and Potomac rivers) and *N. h. saludanus* (coastal streams, James to Ocmulgee rivers).
- *Leuciscus* species: *L. purpureus* (known only from Girard's type, San Bernardino Creek, Arizona), *L. intermedius* (Gila Basin, scales 75 in lateral line), and *L. niger* (Rio Gila; the authors suspect it may be the same as *L. intermedius*).
- *Campostoma* species: *C. anomalum* (scales 49 to 55 in lateral line per the key), *C. ornatum* (Chihuahua River, Mexico), and *C. priceri* (new species; single 3¼-inch type specimen from the Chiricahua Mountains).

**Catostomidae (suckers)**
- *Catostomus commersonii* (common sucker): up to 18 inches long; lips strongly papillose; the most widespread sucker in the catalogue, from Quebec and the Great Lakes to Montana and Georgia.
- *Catostomus occidentalis* (Sacramento sucker): 13 to 14 dorsal rays; abundant in California rivers.
- *Catostomus bernardini*: San Bernardino Creek, a tributary of the Rio Yaqui on the Arizona–Sonora border.
- *Catostomus macrocheilus* (Columbia River sucker): large lips; Columbia River as far as Flathead Lake, Montana.
- *Catostomus ardens* (Utah Lake sucker): scales 12-72 to 75-10 and 40 before dorsal.

**Taxonomic conclusions**
- The authors admit multiple species where differences are weak (for example, *L. purpureus*, *L. intermedius* and *L. niger*), but note that some may be identical.
- Several names listed in synonymy are regarded as dubious or uncertain.
- The genus *Leuciscus* is treated as synonymous with *Squalius*, and *Phorinus* is considered inseparable from *Leuciscus*.
