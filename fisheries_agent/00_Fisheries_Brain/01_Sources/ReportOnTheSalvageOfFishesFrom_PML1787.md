# Fish Salvage from High Levee Pond, Cibola National Wildlife Refuge (24-26 October 2005)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum, issued 9 November 2005 by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Parker, AZ), reports the first of several planned fish salvage operations at [[High Levee Pond]] (HLP) on the [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]]. The salvage was conducted 24-26 October 2005 before the pond's renovation, with the goal of removing fishes and re-establishing the native fish community that had persisted there for 11 years. Crews used [[Electrofishing]], [[Trammel Netting]], and [[Hoop Net]] sets. Native [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] were transported to [[Davis Cove]] on [[Lake Mohave]], while introduced species were handed to the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (CDFG) for release elsewhere. Reported totals were 651 [[Largemouth Bass]] by electrofishing, 141 Razorback Sucker, 5 Bonytail, and 1 [[Bluegill Sunfish]], with several mortalities.

## Background & Description

The memo is a post-operation report rather than a research study. Its stated purpose was to remove fish from HLP ahead of renovation so that the native community could be re-established. The salvage was described as the first of several operations.

The species handled were:

- **Largemouth Bass** (*Micropterus salmoides*), an introduced predatory sunfish.
- **Bluegill Sunfish** (*Lepomis macrochirus*), an introduced sunfish.
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a native catostomid.
- **Bonytail** (*Gila elegans*), a native cyprinid.

The memo does not give scientific names. These are added here for reference.

Fishing gear and effort were as follows:

- **Electrofishing** was done in two sessions:
  - Afternoon of 24 October (1300-1500 hrs), using a Smith-Root boat provided by CDFG. Two passes totaled 4105 seconds of effort.
  - Evening of 26 October (1900-2030 hrs), using the MFRO Coffelt boat. Two passes totaled 1985 seconds. About 2300 ft of trammel nets were set during this session, with the aim of driving fish into the nets as they fled the electricity.
- **Trammel nets:** Six nets (1800 ft total) were set the evening of 24 October and fished until the morning of 25 October. Three nets measured 100 yd x 2 yd with a 10-inch outer wall, and three had 2-inch inner walls (inner wall description partly illegible in source). Additional nets were set on the evening of 25 October (sizes not given), bringing the total netting to roughly 2,900 ft (figure partly illegible in source). The nets were fished continuously until pulled after the second electrofishing pass on 26 October.
- **Hoop nets:** Two sets were made, each with 10 hoops of 24-inch diameter, 12 ft long, and a 2-yd lead. They fished for 24 hours on 25 October but were judged ineffective and pulled the next morning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

HLP was a pond holding a self-sustaining native fish assemblage for roughly a decade before renovation. Introduced predators such as Largemouth Bass were abundant, which is a common concern in managed Lower Colorado River waters. The salvage aimed to clear the pond of non-native fish so that native suckers and minnows could be restored afterward.

Handling and transport followed standard hatchery practice. Fish were moved in a three-chambered transport tank supplied by [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. The tanks were filled with pond water, aerated with oxygen, and loaded at HLP. The truck left at about 0900 and reached Lake Mohave that afternoon. Personnel from Willow Beach, the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], and the [[National Park Service]] met the truck and released the fish into Davis Cove.

## Key Findings

**Largemouth Bass**
- Electrofishing collected 651 individuals, with total lengths of 88-355 mm. Most were under 125 mm.
- Trammel netting collected 5 individuals, in the same size range.
- About 99% of Largemouth Bass were released by CDFG. A small number died.

**Bluegill Sunfish**
- One individual, 220 mm total length, was taken by trammel netting.

**Razorback Sucker**
- 141 individuals were collected: 16 by electrofishing (5 during the day, 11 at night) and 123 by trammel netting.
- All were adults, 425-606 mm total length.
- A small minority were slightly tuberculate males. One expressed a small amount of milt.
- 139 were transported to Davis Cove on the morning of 27 October.
- 3 mortalities occurred at HLP.

**Bonytail**
- 5 individuals were taken by trammel netting, measuring 336-408 mm total length.
- Four were processed and four of those died. Their remains were skeletonized. The fifth was not processed and was transported to Davis Cove.

**Logistics and Personnel**
- Participants came from the Cibola National Wildlife Refuge, CDFG, USGS Denver, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City), Arizona State University, and the MFRO-Parker office. About 16 people are named.
- The memo states that further salvage operations will follow, and invites participation and comments from interested parties.
