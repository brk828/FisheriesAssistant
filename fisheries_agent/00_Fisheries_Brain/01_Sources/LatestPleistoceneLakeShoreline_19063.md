# Latest Pleistocene Lake Shorelines and Glacial Chronology from AMS Radiocarbon Dating of Rock Varnish

#paleolimnology #geochronology #pleistocene #rock-varnish #glacial-chronology

## Executive Summary

This study applies accelerator mass spectrometry (AMS) radiocarbon dating to organic matter trapped in the basal layer of [[Rock Varnish]] to obtain minimum surface-exposure ages for shorelines and moraines in the western [[Basin and Range Province]]. The varnish ages constrain when [[Closed-Basin Lakes]] abandoned their latest highstands and when [[Tioga Glaciation]] and [[Middle Creek Glaciation]] moraines were deposited. The authors conclude that glacial retreat and high lake levels occurred simultaneously, which cannot be explained by meltwater alone and implies higher snowlines and increased precipitation. A minor [[Younger Dryas]]-correlated glacial advance and lake transgression near 10–11 ka may mark a brief return to cooler or wetter conditions at the end of the Pleistocene.

## Background & Description

- **Rock varnish:** A coating of predominantly manganese and iron oxides and clay minerals containing more than 30 minor trace elements, including carbon. It forms on stable desert rock surfaces roughly 60–100 years after exposure, through the oxidizing activity of bacteria. Clay cementation traps ambient elements in the matrix, and in sufficiently alkaline settings varnish accumulates as a synsedimentary deposit.
- **Dating principle:** Stable organic detritus buried under the lowest varnish layer gives a minimum exposure age for the underlying rock. The method targets sites where conventional [[Radiocarbon Dating]] material (charcoal, tufa, shell) is lacking.
- **Study region:** Terminal lakes in closed basins and glaciated adjacent ranges, studied for over a century. The last ~40,000 years are the best-defined interval.
- **Sampling protocol:** Manganese-rich black varnish is collected from stable boulders (preferably >1 m diameter). Samples with microcolonial fungi, lichens, or cyanobacteria are rejected. The outer varnish is scraped away, leaving the bottom 10%, which is then chemically concentrated (HCl, HF, sodium dithionite, hydroxylamine hydrochloride). An underlying rock control is run to check for contaminating old carbon.
- **Calibration:** Tests on [[Hualalai Volcano]] lava flows, Hawaii, compared varnish ages with conventional charcoal ages. Varnish ages are slightly younger than charcoal, with the lag growing for older samples. The regression of varnish age on charcoal age (varnish = 14.0 + 0.87 × charcoal age) was statistically significant at p = 0.001 (Fig. 4).
- **Caveat on ocean ventilation:** Shackleton et al. (1988) reported that atmospheric 14C activity dropped by 100‰ within 3000 years or less during deglaciation. The authors note that nearly all ages in the paper could be affected, so results from the deglaciation period are preliminary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake Bonneville:** Its latest cycle has among the best age control in the province. The [[Bonneville Flood]] released the upper 104 m of the lake into the Snake River over 2 months to 1 year, around 14,500 ± 300 yr B.P. A varnish age of 14,050 ± 130 yr B.P. (AA-2316) is consistent with this.
- **Lake Lahontan:** The 1330 m highstand was abandoned before about 13 ka. Tufa, shell, wood, and varnish ages (the varnish at 12,680 ± 105 yr B.P., AA-2319) are treated as minimum ages for the final regression.
- **Searles Lake:** Overflow-highstand varnish gave 13,610 ± 110 (AA-2229) and 13,290 ± 115 yr B.P. (AA-2230). The lake later re-established and lasted until about 10.5 ka.
- **Lake Manly (Death Valley):** The latest cycle began before about 26 ka and lasted until just before 10 ka. A beach ridge varnish age of 12,970 ± 185 yr B.P. indicates recession began before about 13 ka. The older "Blackwelder" highstand was dated by cation-ratio varnish to about 120–130 ka.
- **Lake Manix:** Hydrologic failure through Afton Canyon was dated by Anodonta shells (14,230 ± 1325 yr B.P.) and varnish (13,970 ± 120 yr B.P., AA-2317).
- **Lake Mojave (Silver Lake):** Shoreline A varnish gave 11,630 ± 150 yr B.P. (AA-2319) and shoreline B gave 9060 ± 160 yr B.P. (AA-1299). The authors note that the shoreline A age may reflect storm erosion during shoreline B's occupation.
- **Glacial sites:** The Tioga glaciation in the Sierra Nevada reached its maximum before about 19 ka at Pine Creek. Recession occurred around 13 ka. In the [[White Mountains]], Middle Creek advance and last recession are dated to 18,510 ± 250 and 12,710 ± 240 yr B.P. Chiatovich Canyon values are 17,780 ± 400 and 12,510 ± 270 yr B.P.
- **Mono Basin:** Lajoie and Robinson (1982) reported high lake stands at 36–34, 28–24, and 14–12 ka. Benson et al. (1990) revised the last high stand to 15–14 ka.

## Key Findings

1. **Lake highstand abandonment (minimum ages):** Lake Lahontan before 12.7 ka; Searles Lake before 13.6 ka; Lake Manly before 13.0 ka; Lake Mojave before 11.6 ka.
2. **Glacial maxima and recession:** Tioga maximum before about 18–19 ka at Pine Creek; Middle Creek maximum around 18–19 ka; major recessional moraines formed before 12–13 ka.
3. **Lake and glacier timing:** Glacial recession and high closed-basin lake levels were penecontemporaneous. Glacial meltwater alone cannot explain the high lake levels, following Gilbert (1890).
4. **Paleoclimate interpretation:** High lake levels with receding glaciers point to higher snowlines and more precipitation, probably from cool-season frontal storms. Macrofossil and isotopic evidence on monsoon precipitation was in conflict, and the authors propose reconciliation through a synoptic model with little freezing and high snowlines.
5. **Younger Dryas analogue:** A relatively minor glacial advance and slight lake transgression at about 10–11 ka (Searles Lake to about 10.5 ka; Lake Gilbert about 10–11 ka; Lake Franklin before 9.8 ± 0.4 ka) may reflect a temporary return to cooler or wetter conditions at the close of the Pleistocene. The numerical control is minimal.
6. **Southern lakes:** [[Lake Mojave]] was high about 12 ka and again around 9 ka. Lake Estancia (New Mexico) remained high until about 12 ka.
7. **Method limits:** Varnish ages are minimum estimates. Possible errors include contamination from older carbon, algal carbon, and erosion of earlier varnish. Fungal pitting (bioetching) can reset the varnish clock, as seen at Silver Lake. Deep-ocean ventilation may compress the radiocarbon timescale, so deglaciation-period results remain preliminary.
