## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative and operational summary of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) **Southwest Region** fisheries program, covering Arizona, New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Texas. As of the 1995 ecosystem-based reorganization, the Division of Fisheries operated **seven National Fish Hatcheries, one Fish Health Center, and four Fishery Resources Offices**, organized under **nine ecosystem teams** aligned to river drainages (e.g., [[Lower Colorado River]], [[Gila-Salt-Verde River]], [[Upper-Middle Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]]). Rather than focusing on a single taxon, the report profiles a **regional network of conservation hatcheries and fishery assistance offices** dedicated primarily to the propagation, stocking, and recovery of native and endangered southwestern fish fauna, alongside game-fish production (catfish, bass, trout, striped bass, paddlefish) for tribal, federal, and state waters. A central theme is the disproportionate regional emphasis on **endangered and threatened fish species recovery**, fish health diagnostics, and interagency/tribal coordination.

## Background & Description

The source is administrative/programmatic rather than morphometric, so no anatomical measurements or descriptive morphology are provided. However, the **taxonomic scope** of species actively propagated across the region's hatcheries is extensive and includes:

- **Salmonids**: [[Apache Trout]] (Threatened), [[Gila Trout]] (Endangered), Brook Trout, Brown Trout, Rainbow Trout
- **Catostomids (suckers)**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (Endangered), [[Warner Sucker]]
- **Cyprinids (minnows/chubs)**: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]] (Endangered), [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]], [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]], [[Woundfin]], [[Guzman Beautiful Shiner]], Spinedace, Loach Minnow
- **Poeciliids/Cyprinodontids**: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Big Bend Gambusia]], [[Leon Springs Pupfish]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] (Endangered), [[Pecos Pupfish]] (Endangered)
- **Ictalurids**: Channel Catfish, [[Yaqui Catfish]] (Endangered)
- **Moronids**: Striped Bass, Hybrid Striped Bass, White Bass hybrids
- **Centrarchids**: Largemouth Bass (northern and Florida strains), Smallmouth Bass
- **Polyodontids**: Paddlefish
- **Acipenserids**: Shovelnose Sturgeon (restoration target, not hatchery-raised per this text)

The paper notes fourteen federally listed fish species under Endangered Species Act protection are actively cultured at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] alone, reflecting the region's role as a genetic repository/refugium for imperiled southwestern ichthyofauna.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Field stations are organized by drainage-based "ecosystem teams," reflecting the ecological/hydrological context of species management:

- **[[Lower Colorado River]] Ecosystem**: Habitat for [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]; stocking sites include Lake Havasu, Lake Mohave, Bill Williams River NWR, Imperial NWR, and areas below Davis Dam — large-river, reservoir, and backwater habitats critical to big-river Colorado cyprinids and catostomids.
- **[[Gila-Salt-Verde River]] Ecosystem**: Home range of [[Apache Trout]] (native to White Mountains, AZ) and [[Gila Trout]] (native to Gila/Mogollon highlands); high-elevation coldwater streams on Fort Apache and San Carlos Indian Reservations.
- **[[Upper-Middle Rio Grande]] and [[Pecos River]] Ecosystems**: Focus of the long-term Study of Rare and Endangered Fishes of the San Juan and Rio Grande Drainage Basins conducted by the New Mexico Fishery Resources Office in collaboration with the Bureau of Reclamation.
- **[[Texas Gulf Coast]] Ecosystem**: Anadromous, estuarine, and inter-jurisdictional fish habitat; restoration targets include Striped Bass, Red Drum, Paddlefish, and Shovelnose Sturgeon along the Texas coast, coordinated through eight National Wildlife Refuges.
- **[[Arkansas-Red River]] Ecosystem**: Warmwater riverine and reservoir habitats in Oklahoma supporting Paddlefish, Sturgeons, and Alligator Gar restoration, alongside monitoring of the invasive [[Zebra Mussel]].

Stocked habitats span an unusually broad institutional range: national wildlife refuges, national forests, U.S. military installations (Fort Sill, Fort Hood, Peterson AFB, Fort Carson), and numerous **Native American tribal lands and Pueblos** (Navajo Nation, Hopi, Zuni, Mescalero, Jicarilla, Isleta, Jemez, San Ildefonso, and others), underscoring the trust responsibility dimension of the Service's fishery mission in the Southwest.

## Key Findings

- **Ecosystem reorganization (Oct 1, 1995)**: The Southwest Region restructured around nine drainage-based ecosystem teams rather than a centralized fisheries office, decentralizing fishery management expertise to local, drainage-specific biologist teams.
- **Endangered species propagation hub**: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery & Technology Center]] and the developing [[Mora National Fish Hatchery & Technology Center]] jointly form the "Southwest Fish Technology Center," dedicated to intensive culture methods (including water-reuse systems) for endangered fish species — Dexter alone rears 14 ESA-listed species.
- **Fish health infrastructure**: The [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]] is the sole fish disease diagnostic/certification laboratory in the Southwest Region, supporting all nine hatcheries and seven fishery resources offices, and conducts wild fish health surveys across the San Juan River, lower Colorado River, and multiple tribal/national forest lands. It participates in FDA INAD drug trials for hatchery disease treatment.
- **Endangered species stocking programs**: [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] rears [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] (both Endangered) for stocking into the lower Colorado River system; [[Mescalero National Fish Hatchery]] rears [[Gila Trout]] (Endangered) for tribal and Pueblo waters; [[Uvalde National Fish Hatchery]] rears three Endangered species (Comanche Springs Pupfish, Pecos Pupfish, Yaqui Catfish) alongside sportfish.
- **Restoration of depleted native/anadromous species**: The [[Oklahoma Fishery Resources Office]] and [[Corpus Christi Fishery Resources Office]] are engaged in restoring depleted, nationally significant fisheries — Paddlefish, Sturgeons, Alligator Gar in Oklahoma; Striped Bass, Red Drum, Paddlefish, and Shovelnose Sturgeon on the Texas Gulf Coast.
- **Invasive species monitoring**: The Oklahoma Fishery Resources Office monitors the [[Zebra Mussel]] invasion, indicating early regional concern over invasive mollusks affecting native fish habitat.
- **Logistics**: The Regional Distribution Unit (RDU) in Burnet, TX operates a dual-driver specialized live-fish transport truck capable of 1,000-mile, 23-hour hauls, moving warmwater fish (catfish, bass) from TX/OK hatcheries to AZ/NM waters, and coldwater trout on return trips to tribal and military waters — a key logistical backbone connecting hatchery production to stocking sites across four states.
- **Budget/staffing snapshot (FY1996)**: Operating budgets ranged from $45,000 (RDU, 1 employee) to $625,200 (Willow Beach NFH, 6 employees), with the largest single-station staff at Arizona Fishery Resources Office (14 employees) — illustrating substantial disparity in resource allocation across field stations despite comparable conservation mandates.
