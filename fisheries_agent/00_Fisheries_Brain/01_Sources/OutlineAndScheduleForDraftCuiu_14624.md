# Cui-ui Recovery Plan Outline and Schedule (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a January 1991 internal memorandum from Tom Strekal to the [[Cui-ui]] Recovery Team. It transmits a draft outline for the cui-ui recovery plan, prepared with John Emlen, and sets out a schedule for team comments, a rewrite, editing, and submission of the draft. The outline covers the species' description, historical and current range, life history and habitat, reasons for listing, existing conservation measures, and recovery strategy. The stated goal is to reclassify the [[Cui-ui]] from endangered to threatened, and later to delist it, once long-term population persistence is demonstrated. The document is a planning item; it contains no new field data.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Cui-ui]], a large sucker endemic to the [[Lake Lahontan]] system of western Nevada. The outline lists "characteristic features by life stage" as a topic but gives no morphological details.
- **Historical range:** The species was isolated in [[Pyramid Lake]] for about 4,000 years, following the drying of [[Lake Lahontan]] and [[Walker Lake]] (based on fossil evidence). Historically it used [[Pyramid Lake]] and the lower [[Truckee River]] upstream to [[Wadsworth]], including [[Winnemucca Lake]], with few barriers. Most spawning habitat was probably upstream of [[Dead Ox Canyon]].
- **Current range:** Spawning and occupancy extend from [[Pyramid Lake]] upstream to [[Nixon]]. [[Winnemucca Lake]] has dried. Barriers include the delta, stream braiding, and diversion structures.
- **Prior research:** The outline cites work by Snyder (CSU), Miller, and LaRivers, and the hatchery and genetics work of Brussard and Mink.
- **Commentary:** An appendix titled "The road to [recovery] ... or how to stop screwing with cui-ui and allow them to screw each other" by Dr. John Emlen and the cui-ui pack is referenced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The outline organizes the species' ecology under these headings:

- **Lake habitat:** water quality (TDS, temperature), tolerance of each life stage, algal blooms, and population size relative to lake area.
- **River habitat:** substrate, water quality, flow regime (minimum flows and impacts of variation), and spawning population size relative to area.
- **Spawning and passage:** lake elevation and delta condition, river braiding and dams, upstream migration cues, spawning timing, downstream movement of adults and larvae, egg development, and density-dependent effects (intra- and interspecific).
- **Fecundity and survival:** fecundity and egg viability by adult age and water temperature; survival and longevity by life stage and sex; predation (natural and human); and density dependence.
- **Food:** diet by life stage and season.
- **Genetics:** genetic variation and the concern that hatchery stocks may diverge from wild stocks.

Human-driven threats identified in the outline include:

- restricted flow and timing from the diversion of the Truckee River and storage in upstream reservoirs
- channel changes and degraded substrate in the river
- delta alteration and water quality problems
- the risk of toxic spills to the river, which the outline proposes to assess

Conservation measures named in the outline:

- [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and dam modifications that provide hydrologic control, improved fish passage, and created spawning habitat
- [[Stampede Reservoir]] and [[Prosser Reservoir]] storage, which is the species' only water right, with flow and timing control
- control of [[Newlands Project]] diversions under the OCAP, including the 1988 OCAP non-jeopardy opinion
- a hatchery operated as a fail-safe for the wild population
- the [[Truckee-Carson-Pyramid Lake Water Rights Settlement Act]], which provides a framework for future water purchases and deliveries

## Key Findings

The outline and schedule document the following points:

1. **Reclassification and delisting criteria**
   - Reclassification from endangered to threatened is to be considered once a self-sustaining population shows a 0.85 probability of persisting for 200 years.
   - Delisting is to be considered once a self-sustaining population shows a 0.95 probability of persisting for 200 years.
   - The estimated date of recovery is not legible in the scanned text.

2. **Water needs**
   - The plan lists two water purchase quantities: 70 KAF and 45 KAF. The scan is partly illegible, but the 45 KAF item appears to relate to reclassification.
   - Other water actions include augmenting lower [[Truckee River]] flows, reducing diversions, recouping water from the [[Newlands Project]], and developing an operating agreement for upper Truckee River reservoirs.

3. **Conservation results**
   - Population increased in recent years, with the 1982–86 water years cited as evidence, attributed to the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], OCAP, and [[Stampede Reservoir]] measures.

4. **Management policy**
   - A no-fishing policy will continue until the species is reclassified.
   - Translocation is not planned, consistent with FWS policy.
   - The hatchery will be maintained as a fail-safe against catastrophic events.
   - The plan assumes continued management intervention, including river access and flow control.

5. **Research gaps to address**
   - hydraulic and hydrologic modeling, and rerunning the population model with new data
   - spawning flow and attraction requirements, and water quality needs
   - survival, competition, and food availability at all life stages
   - the genetic status of hatchery and wild stocks
   - flood and channel requirements, including floodplain and channel configuration
   - future water demand and its effect on the amount of water required for recovery

6. **Schedule (1991 memo)**
   - Jan 18: outline sent to team members
   - Jan 23: team comments due
   - Feb 6–15: informal review, rewrite, and editorial return to Tom Strekal
   - Feb 8: text circulated for review
   - Mar 8: team meeting
   - Mar 18: week of reviews
   - Mar 29: draft submitted to Marv

7. **Open questions** listed in the appendix include whether the recovery levels can be met under current river and facility conditions, and how future water demand will affect the water needed for reclassification or delisting.
