## Executive Summary

This document is a **faunal checklist** cataloging the fish species known from the **Rio Conchos**, a major tributary of the **[[Rio Grande]] / Río Bravo del Norte** drainage in Chihuahua, Mexico, located within the **[[Chihuahuan Desert]]** ecoregion. The list enumerates **37 species** across **9 families**, spanning primitive holosteans ([[Lepisosteidae]]), characins, a highly diverse cyprinid (minnow) assemblage, suckers ([[Catostomidae]]), catfishes ([[Ictaluridae]]), desert pupfishes ([[Cyprinodontidae]]), livebearers ([[Poeciliidae]]), sunfish, and darters ([[Percidae]]). Notably, the checklist flags a high degree of **micro-endemism**, several **undescribed species (n.sp.)**, and multiple taxa with **unresolved taxonomic status**, underscoring the Rio Conchos basin as a hotspot of isolated desert fish diversification analogous to other Chihuahuan Desert spring/river systems (e.g., [[Cuatro Ciénegas]]).

## Background & Description

Because the source is a taxonomic/distributional checklist rather than a morphometric study, morphological data are limited to diagnostic and nomenclatural notes recorded by the compiler:

- **Lepisosteus osseus** (Longnose Gar) — retains the primitive ganoid-scaled, elongate rostrum body plan typical of [[Lepisosteidae]].
- **Catostomus bernardini** — taxonomic revision noted; formerly classified as *C. conchos*, indicating prior confusion in sucker taxonomy specific to this drainage.
- **Ictalurus chihuahua** — proposed as a **new species (n.sp.)** of catfish endemic to the basin.
- **Ictalurus punctatus** — noted as **allopatric** to *I. lupus*, suggesting geographic replacement/niche partitioning between these two ictalurid congeners within the basin.
- **Notropis cifuentesi** — proposed **n.sp.**, with a range noted by SCB (likely a taxonomic authority/reviewer) as potentially confined to the **Río Aguanaval**, raising questions about whether it truly belongs to the Conchos ichthyofauna or represents basin-crossing/misattribution.
- **Cyprinodon** genus — shows notable local speciation, with at least one **undescribed species** associated with the **Julimes** locality, consistent with pupfish patterns of rapid morphological divergence in isolated desert springs.
- **Gambusia** — multiple locally endemic species (*G. alvarezi*, *G. hurtadoi*, *G. senilis*), plus a population near **San Diego** tentatively identified as *G.* cf. *senilis*, indicating cryptic or incipient speciation.
- **Lepomis megalotis occidentalis** — flagged as possibly warranting elevation to **full species** status, rather than remaining a subspecies of Longear Sunfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Rio Conchos functions as an isolated freshwater refugium within the arid **Chihuahuan Desert**, producing a fauna shaped by:

- **Endemic radiation in isolated waters**: Numerous taxa (marked **E**) are restricted to the Conchos basin or specific springs/localities within it (e.g., *Cyprinodon macrolepis*, *C. pachycephalus*, *Etheostoma australe*, several *Gambusia* spp.), reflecting classic desert-spring [[allopatric speciation]] dynamics seen elsewhere in the Chihuahuan Desert (e.g., Cuatro Ciénegas basin).
- **Habitat segregation among congeners**: The allopatric distribution of *Ictalurus punctatus* and *I. lupus* suggests niche or geographic partitioning within the same river system, possibly linked to differing tolerances for current, substrate, or connectivity to the main channel vs. tributaries/springs.
- **Trophic and structural guild diversity**:
  - Benthic detritivores/grazers: [[Catostomidae]] (suckers) — *Carpiodes carpio*, *Catostomus* spp., *Cycleptus elongatus*, *Ictiobus niger*, *Moxostoma austrinum*.
  - Benthic predators/generalist catfish: [[Ictaluridae]] — *Ictalurus* spp., *Pylodictis olivaris* (flathead catfish).
  - Small-bodied minnows occupying pool, riffle, and springhead microhabitats: diverse [[Cyprinidae]] (*Campostoma*, *Codoma*, *Dionda*, *Gila*, *Macrhybopsis*, *Notropis* spp., *Pimephales*, *Rhinichthys*).
  - Spring/marsh specialists tolerant of high salinity, temperature extremes, and low oxygen: [[Cyprinodontidae]] (pupfishes) and [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers, *Gambusia* spp.) — hallmark taxa of Chihuahuan Desert isolated spring ecosystems.
  - Vegetated/structured habitat associates: *Lepomis megalotis occidentalis* ([[Centrarchidae]]) and *Etheostoma* spp. ([[Percidae]], darters), typically found in clear, structurally complex stream reaches.
- **Basin connectivity considerations**: The possible restriction of *Notropis cifuentesi* to the Río Aguanaval (a separate basin) raises questions about historical stream capture or drainage connectivity between the Conchos and adjacent basins.

## Key Findings

- **37 total fish species** documented from the Rio Conchos across 9 families.
- **High endemism**: At least **11 species/subspecies marked "E" (endemic)**, concentrated heavily in [[Cyprinodontidae]] and [[Poeciliidae]], consistent with the isolated-spring speciation pattern typical of the Chihuahuan Desert.
- **At least 3 taxa proposed as new species (n.sp.)** at time of writing: *Notropis cifuentesi*, *Ictalurus chihuahua*, and an undescribed *Cyprinodon* sp. from Julimes — indicating the basin was (or remains) incompletely described taxonomically.
- **Multiple unresolved taxonomic questions** flagged by the compiler, including:
  - Status of the *Notropis lutrensis* Balneario San Diego population.
  - Possible full-species status of *Lepomis megalotis occidentalis*.
  - Identity/species boundaries within *Dionda episcopa* ("probably more than one species").
  - Cryptic *Gambusia* cf. *senilis* population at San Diego.
- **Nomenclatural correction** noted: *Catostomus bernardini* was formerly described as *Catostomus conchos*.
- **Biogeographic anomaly**: *Notropis cifuentesi*'s range may actually be confined to the Río Aguanaval rather than the Rio Conchos proper, suggesting a need to verify basin attribution.
- The checklist collectively highlights the Rio Conchos as a **biodiversity-significant desert river system** warranting further systematic and conservation-focused study, particularly for its endemic pupfishes, livebearers, and minnows.
