# Desert Pupfish Stocking at Lousy Canyon and Larry Creek, Arizona (2006)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

A memo from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) to interested parties reports the stocking of 400 [[Desert Pupfish]] (*[[Cyprinodon Macularius]]*) on March 31, 2006. The fish were released into [[Lousy Canyon]] and an unnamed tributary of [[Larry Creek]] in [[Agua Fria National Monument]]. The release was a joint effort of AGFD, the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), and the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), with helicopter support from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR). The memo also records co-occurring fish, amphibians and reptiles, and sets out a monitoring schedule.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal AGFD memorandum (subject: desert pupfish stockings), dated April 3, 2006, addressed to interested parties and prepared by Jeremy Voeltz, Native Fish Specialist.
- **Focal species:** The [[Desert Pupfish]], *Cyprinodon macularius*. The text names it only as "pupfish" in most places; the scientific name is given in the subject-line-adjacent text.
- **Source population:** Fish were collected on March 30 and 31, 2006, from The Nature Conservancy's [[San Pedro Preserve]]. Collectors were AGFD staff and volunteers from the University of Arizona.
- **Release sites:**
  - Lousy Canyon (200 fish)
  - An unnamed tributary of Larry Creek (200 fish)
- **Participating agencies:** AGFD, BLM and USFWS biologists conducted the stocking. The BR provided helicopter transport.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Logistics and transport**
- Fish were flown in by [[Bureau of Reclamation]] helicopter. The helicopter left the preserve at about 9:30 a.m. and landed on the mesa above the canyons at about 10:30 a.m.
- BLM and BR biologists used long-line equipment and a sling to lower the fish into the two canyons. Stocking was completed at about 11:00 a.m.
- Pupfish transport mortality was low, approximately 2–3%.

**Water temperatures recorded on arrival**
| Location | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Transport coolers | 61°F (given as 15–16°C in the scan) |
| Lousy Canyon | 74°F (24°C) |
| Unnamed tributary of Larry Creek | 59°F (15°C) |

Fish were held in the transport water for about 15 minutes to equalize temperature and water quality before release.

**Co-occurring species**
- [[Gila Topminnow]]: present in both canyons (stocked in 2000 in Lousy Canyon and in 2005 in the Larry Creek tributary).
- [[Gila Chub]]: present in the Larry Creek tributary (stocked, year not legible in the scan). Presumed present in Lousy Canyon (stocked in 1995). The team did not sample Lousy Canyon below the large waterfall, where the chubs were believed to occur.
- [[Lowland Leopard Frog]]: adults and tadpoles observed.
- [[Sonora Mud Turtle]]: observed.

## Key Findings

- **Stocking outcome:** 200 desert pupfish were released in Lousy Canyon and 200 in the unnamed Larry Creek tributary on March 31, 2006.
- **Handling success:** Transport losses were about 2–3%, and fish were acclimated for roughly 15 minutes before release.
- **Habitat conditions:** Recorded water temperatures ranged from 59°F in the Larry Creek tributary to 74°F in Lousy Canyon, with transport water at 61°F.
- **Native fauna present:** Gila topminnow and Gila chub were already established in one or both canyons, and leopard frogs and mud turtles were seen during the visit.
- **Sampling limitation:** The Gila chub population in Lousy Canyon was not directly confirmed because the team did not descend below the large waterfall.
- **Monitoring plan:** The population will be surveyed at one month, six months, and then annually after stocking.
- **Data availability:** A CD of digital images from the project is available on request.
