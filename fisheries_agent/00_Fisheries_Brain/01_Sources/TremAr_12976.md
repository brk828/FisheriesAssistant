## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Dexter Fishery Assistance Office) correspondence, dated circa 1990, summarizing a multi-year stocking record of two federally listed Colorado River native fishes — the [[Bonytail Chub]] (referred to as "BTC") and the [[Razorback Sucker]] ("RBS") — into [[Lake Mohave]] from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. The letter accompanies a tabulated stocking log spanning multiple years (excluding 1983, 1984, and 1986, when no stockings occurred) and notes logistical details about stocking locations, rearing/dispersal methods, and coordination gaps between hatchery and state fisheries management (Arizona Game and Fish Department, Nongame Branch). A potential young-of-year (YOY) Bonytail Chub stocking was under discussion for a subsequent fall, contingent on FY91 funding.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir impoundment of the Colorado River (formed by Davis Dam), spanning the Arizona-Nevada border. This is critical habitat for both listed species within the Colorado River Basin recovery program.
- **Stocking locations:** Predominantly at **Cottonwood Landing**, with an exception noted for the initial 1981 stocking. The Razorback Sucker stocking noted "Arizona and Carp Coves" as a location, though the author instructs the recipient to disregard this distinction.
- **Dispersal mechanism:** The initial chub stocking (1981) was conducted via the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] stocking barge, allowing presumed dispersal throughout the main lake body rather than a fixed nearshore site — contrasting with later shore-based stockings at Cottonwood Landing.
- **Conservation context:** Both species are imperiled natives of the [[Colorado River Basin]] and are the subject of active hatchery supplementation/reintroduction efforts, indicating this document is part of a broader endangered species recovery stocking program (likely tied to the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program predecessor efforts).
- **Management coordination:** The letter highlights an institutional communication gap — the Nongame Branch of Arizona Game and Fish was notified, but this information was not reliably relayed to the Fisheries division, prompting the compilation of this consolidated stocking history.

## Key Findings
- A multi-year stocking effort of [[Bonytail Chub]] into [[Lake Mohave]] occurred from Dexter NFH, with no stocking events in 1983, 1984, and 1986.
- Bonytail Chub stockings varied widely in scale, from small releases (~1,400–9,100 fish) to a very large release of approximately 147,000 fish at ~4 inches in length.
- A single [[Razorback Sucker]] stocking event of 2,000 fish (~9", 160 lbs total) was recorded, split across Arizona Cove and Carp Cove.
- The very first stocking event (1981) uniquely used the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] stocking barge for open-lake dispersal, differing methodologically from subsequent shore-based releases at Cottonwood Landing.
- A future stocking of young-of-year (YOY) Bonytail Chub was tentatively planned, pending FY 1991 federal funding approval.
- The correspondence underscores a recurring administrative issue in interagency communication between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service hatchery system and Arizona Game and Fish Department's Fisheries branch regarding stocking notifications.
