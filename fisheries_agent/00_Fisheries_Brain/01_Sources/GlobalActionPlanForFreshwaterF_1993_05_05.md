## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific data source** but rather a set of 1993 administrative letters and planning materials related to the drafting of a proposed **"Global Action Plan for Freshwater Fishes"**, coordinated by Simon Mickleburgh of the [[Fauna And Flora Preservation Society]] under the auspices of [[IUCN]] (The World Conservation Union). The correspondence is addressed to Dr. Edwin P. Pister, Executive Secretary of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], inviting his contribution of expertise on the fish faunas of desert and mountain habitats in the American Southwest and northern Mexico. No morphological, taxonomic, or quantitative ecological data on specific fish species are present in the source text; instead, the material outlines the intended structure, contributor guidelines, and scope of the planned conservation action plan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no explicit ecological data are given, the source identifies a **geographic and habitat focus** relevant to future data collection:

- **Habitat type:** Freshwater systems in **desert and mountain regions** — likely including isolated springs, spring-fed pools, desert streams, and relict aquatic habitats characteristic of the [[American Southwest]] and northern Mexico.
- **Faunal context:** These habitats are known globally for supporting highly endemic, often relict fish faunas adapted to isolated, thermally and chemically extreme freshwater environments (e.g., pupfish, [[Cyprinodontidae]]-type habitats), consistent with the expertise of the [[Desert Fishes Council]].
- **Institutional ecological scope:** The Desert Fishes Council is noted as having access to information on "virtually all fish faunas within North America's inland waters," suggesting a broad continental-scale ecological knowledge base intended to inform the Action Plan.

## Key Findings

- The document is a **planning and correspondence record**, not a data-bearing scientific paper; it should be treated as **metadata about the genesis of a conservation document**, not as a source of empirical fish biology.
- The proposed **Global Action Plan for Freshwater Fishes** was structured into seven chapters covering:
  1. Introduction, ecology, and major threats ([[Habitat Destruction]], [[Pollution]], [[Overfishing]], [[Invasive Species]])
  2. Classification by family, including species counts and distribution
  3. An **Annotated Red List of Threatened Species/Subspecies** (precursor concept to the modern [[IUCN Red List]])
  4. Country/Regional Accounts of species richness, threats, and conservation legislation
  5. Captive breeding programs and the role of aquaria/amateurs in conservation
  6. Areas of high freshwater fish biodiversity, identified via computer modelling for prioritizing protected areas
  7. References
- **Major threat categories** identified as central to the Action Plan's scope: habitat destruction/alteration, pollution, overfishing, and introduction of alien (invasive) species — themes foundational to later global freshwater fish conservation frameworks.
- The **Desert Fishes Council** is highlighted as a key contributing body with specialized knowledge of endemic, isolated freshwater fish faunas in arid North American environments, an ecologically significant but globally underrepresented habitat type in freshwater conservation planning.
- This 1993 initiative represents an early precursor to later, more comprehensive global freshwater fish conservation assessments (paralleling the eventual expansion of the [[IUCN Red List]] framework to freshwater taxa).
