# The Fishes of Silver Creek

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae #Lepidomeda-vittata

## Executive Summary

This 1965 survey by Larry M. Gardner (under W.L. Minckley) documents the fish fauna of **Silver Creek**, a tributary of the **Little Colorado River** in northeastern Arizona, spanning from Dagg's Reservoir downstream to the creek's mouth. Eleven stations were sampled using seines, yielding **eight fish species** — three native and five introduced (exotic). The most significant finding is the rediscovery of the rare, endemic **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*) in Silver Creek, extending its known range beyond the previously assumed relictual Clear Creek population. The introduced **[[Common Carp]]** (*Cyprinus carpio*) was the dominant species overall, found at 9 of 11 stations, while native suckers and the spinedace persisted in specific microhabitats (riffles, gravel runs, spring-fed pools).

## Background & Description

**Study Area:** Silver Creek originates in the White Mountains near Sponseller and Zegler Peaks (Apache County, AZ) and flows ~45 miles northwesterly/northerly through Navajo County to its confluence with the [[Little Colorado River]], three miles south of Woodruff. The stream drains 942 square miles, dropping from ~6,500 ft at its headwaters to 5,196 ft near its mouth (gradient ~40 ft/mi above Dagg's, ~24 ft/mi below).

**Hydrology:** Streamflow has been gauged by the USGS for nearly 50 years. Key long-term averages:
- 1930–1938 (7 yrs): 33.6 cfs
- 1929–1952 (21 yrs): 25.5 cfs
- 1950–1960 (10 yrs, at Flake's Trail): 17.8 cfs
- April/May monthly averages (survey period): 1.77 and 2.99 cfs respectively

Flow is highly variable — a 1958 low of 0.4 cfs contrasted with a September high of 648 cfs; a 1960 January high of 1,180 cfs contrasted with a July low of 1.1 cfs. Summer monsoon floods (Aug–Sept) carry heavy silt loads, turning the water reddish-brown.

**Vegetation/Geology gradient:** The upper watershed lies in ponderosa pine transitioning to juniper-pinyon woodland near Dagg's Reservoir; canyon walls are volcanic (malpais) rock. Near Shumway the canyon opens into cultivated valley with willows and cottonwoods. North of Snowflake the creek re-enters high sandstone canyons (up to 200–300 ft walls) with abundant willow, walnut, box elder, and wild grape.

**Methodology:** Fish were collected using a 15-foot and a 10-foot, ¼-inch mesh seine across eleven stations positioned from the creek mouth (Station #1) upstream to below Dagg's Reservoir dam (Station #11).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Diversion & Water Use:** Canals near Shumway, Taylor, and Snowflake irrigate ~6,600 acres; a now-defunct canal near Woodruff historically irrigated 600 acres. The **Woodruff Dam** (completed 1918) forms a critical barrier preventing upstream fish movement (and thus introduction of species like *Pimephales promelas*) from the Little Colorado River into Silver Creek.
- **Thermal/gradient barrier:** Cold, bottom-released water from **[[Dagg's Reservoir]]** and steep local gradient appear to exclude *Cyprinus carpio* from the two stations immediately below the dam.
- **Habitat heterogeneity drives diversity:** Station #5 (Flake's Trail/Ten-mile), featuring alternating deep pools, riffles, bedrock, and gravel substrates, yielded the highest species richness (6 species), including three species found nowhere else in the survey (*Pantosteus delphinus*, *Ictalurus melas*, *Lepidomeda vittata*).
- **Substrate/vegetation associations:**
  - *Cyprinus carpio*: tolerant of silt, sand, gravel, rock; favors deep, sluggish pools with rooted aquatic vegetation (destructively grazes pondweed/cat-tail beds).
  - *Lepomis cyanellus*: strong preference for rocky substrates, canyon walls, spring-fed clear pools.
  - *Ictalurus melas*: silty/muddy low-gradient pools.
  - *Ictalurus natalis*: more associated with clear water and aquatic vegetation than *I. melas*, though tolerant of varied substrates.
  - *Catostomus latipinnis*: deep sandy pools, but also riffles over bedrock.
  - *Pantosteus delphinus*: swift water, riffles, pool heads, gravel-algae substrate.
  - *Lepidomeda vittata*: found alongside other natives at Flake's Trail; historically thought restricted to Clear Creek drainage.
- **No-fish zone:** Station #10 (2 mi south of Shumway) yielded zero fish, likely due to rocky bottom and dense pondweed impeding effective seining — only tadpoles were captured.

## Key Findings

### Species Inventory (11 Stations, Dagg's Reservoir to Little Colorado confluence)
| Species | Status | Stations Present | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Cyprinus carpio* ([[Common Carp]]) | Introduced | 9 of 11 | Most abundant; includes rare "mirror carp" morph (2 specimens) |
| *Lepomis cyanellus* ([[Green Sunfish]]) | Introduced | 6 of 11 | Likely introduced via Dagg's Reservoir/Love Lake stocking |
| *Catostomus latipinnis* ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]]) | Native | 5 of 11 | Found mouth to Snowflake |
| *Ictalurus melas* ([[Black Bullhead]]) | Introduced | 1 (Flake's Trail) | Single specimen |
| *Ictalurus natalis* ([[Yellow Bullhead]]) | Introduced | 1 (near Shumway) | Single specimen |
| *Pimephales promelas* ([[Fathead Minnow]]) | Introduced | 1 (below Woodruff Dam) | 3 specimens; barred from upstream spread by Woodruff Dam |
| *Pantosteus delphinus* ([[Bluehead Sucker]]) | Native | 1 (Flake's Trail) | Breeding coloration observed (red lateral band); largest specimen 173 mm, one-eyed |
| *Lepidomeda vittata* ([[Little Colorado Spinedace]]) | Native, rare/endemic | 1 (Flake's Trail) | Key conservation finding — see below |

### Notable Conservation Finding: *Lepidomeda vittata*
- Endemic to the Little Colorado River Basin (Navajo, Apache, Coconino Counties, AZ).
- Previously believed reduced to only the Clear Creek drainage due to competition from exotics and habitat modification (Miller 1961, 1963).
- **Rediscovered** in this survey at two locations on **May 7, 1965** (collected by L. Gardner and J. Scorse):
  - Silver Creek at Flake's Trail, 11 miles north of Snowflake (T.15N., R.22E.)
  - Little Colorado River, 1 mile north of Woodruff (T.16N., R.22E.)
- This finding demonstrates the species persists more broadly across its historic range than previously documented.

### Native vs. Introduced Balance
- **3 native species**: *Catostomus latipinnis*, *Pantosteus delphinus*, *Lepidomeda vittata*
- **5 introduced species**: *Cyprinus carpio*, *Lepomis cyanellus*, *Ictalurus melas*, *Ictalurus natalis*, *Pimephales promelas*
- Native species were consistently restricted to fewer stations and more specialized habitats (riffles, gravel, spring-fed pools), while introduced species (especially carp) dominated pooled, low-gradient, vegetated reaches.

### Station-Level Highlights
- **Station #1** (near Woodruff Dam): *Cyprinus carpio*, *Lepomis cyanellus*, *Catostomus latipinnis*, *Pimephales promelas* (below dam only).
- **Station #5** (Flake's Trail/Ten-mile): Highest diversity (6 species) due to varied pool-riffle-bedrock habitat mosaic.
- **Station #9** (near Shumway): Base-level gradient, silt/ooze bottom, cat-tails; carp observed grazing extensively on vegetation.
- **Station #10**: Zero fish captured — rocky bottom/dense pondweed prevented effective seining.
- **Station #11** (below Dagg's Reservoir dam): Predominantly juvenile *Lepomis cyanellus* (most ≤1 inch).

### Feeding Ecology (per cited literature: Koster 1957, Miller 1952, Sigler 1955, Trautman 1957)
- *Cyprinus carpio*: grazes rooted aquatic vegetation, dislodging plants.
- *Lepomis cyanellus*: carnivorous — insects, crustaceans, small fish.
- *Ictalurus melas*: omnivorous/opportunistic — plant material, crustaceans, fish, detritus.
- *Pimephales promelas*: microscopic plants, insects, small invertebrates.
- *Catostomus latipinnis*: largely herbivorous/vegetation-based.
- *Pantosteus delphinus*: algae grazer on rock substrates.
