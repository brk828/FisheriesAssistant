# Bureau of Land Management Proposal for Desert Pupfish Habitat Enhancement at Howard Well

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #endangered-species #habitat-conservation

## Executive Summary

This September 4, 1992 correspondence from the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Safford District Office to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service describes a proposed water management action to enhance habitat security for one of the few remaining self-sustaining wild populations of [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) in Arizona. The population inhabits a one-quarter acre pond at [[Howard Well]] that has experienced problematic water level fluctuations and cattail encroachment. The proposed solution involves dedicating the Howard Well water source exclusively to the pupfish pond by redirecting stock-watering operations to [[Martin Well]], approximately 0.5 miles away.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) population at Howard Well represents a rare self-sustaining wild population in Arizona. The species inhabits a one-quarter acre pond that has been experiencing two primary threats: severe water level fluctuations and cattail encroachment reducing available open water habitat.

A multi-agency field reconnaissance was conducted on March 12, 1992, attended by BLM staff, Sally Stefferud (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service), and David Weedman ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]). Current well output measures 7.2 gallons per minute when water is not diverted to other uses.

The pond's water source had previously been shared with a stock-watering system serving livestock located 1.5 miles north, with water diversions likely contributing to declining water levels during high evaporation periods. However, uncertainty exists regarding all causes of the progressive water level decline over the years.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The aquatic habitat faces multiple stressors affecting pupfish population viability:

**Water Availability**: Fluctuations severe enough to threaten population security, potentially linked to diversions for livestock watering during high evaporation periods.

**Vegetation Encroachment**: [[Cattail]] expansion has reduced open water area available to pupfish, limiting suitable habitat within the already small (one-quarter acre) pond.

**Population Status**: This represents one of few remaining self-sustaining wild populations of *C. macularius* in Arizona, making habitat security critical for species conservation.

A recent plant inventory indicated no federally listed or candidate plant species occur in the project vicinity.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Management Action**:
- Disconnect Howard Well from existing stock-water pipeline system
- Construct approximately 0.5 mile trench using bulldozer with trencher to connect Martin Well to stock-water system
- Install 1.5 inch pipe connecting stock-watering system to Martin Well water supply
- Dedicate Howard Well output (7.2 gallons per minute) exclusively to pupfish pond

**Expected Outcomes**:
- Improved water quantity supplied to pupfish habitat
- Elimination of water diversions during critical periods
- Enhanced population security for this rare wild population
- No incidental take or adverse effects anticipated
- No impacts to federally listed or candidate plant species

**Regulatory Compliance**: BLM requested U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service concurrence of beneficial effect under Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended. The Bureau determined impacts would be beneficial to the continued existence of *Cyprinodon macularius*.

**Interagency Coordination**: Project developed with input from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Ecological Services and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], demonstrating collaborative approach to endangered species management.
