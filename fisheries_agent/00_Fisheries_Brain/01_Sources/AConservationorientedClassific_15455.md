# A Conservation-Oriented Classification System for the Inland Waters of California

#ichthyology #aquatic-ecology #conservation-biology #classification-system #California

## Executive Summary

This document presents a hierarchical classification system for California's inland waters designed to support conservation efforts and the establishment of [[Aquatic Diversity Management Areas]] (ADMAs). The system is based primarily on patterns of fish distribution and endemism but includes fishless habitats. It covers five major ichthyological provinces: [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Province]], [[Klamath and North Coast Province]], [[Great Basin Province]], [[Colorado River Province]], and [[Southern California Coastal Province]]. The classification distinguishes between standing and flowing waters, ephemeral and permanent systems, and fishless versus fish-bearing habitats. The system aims to maximize protection of aquatic biodiversity by providing a structured framework for identifying priority conservation areas.

## Background & Description

California's rapidly growing human population and extensive irrigated agriculture compete with aquatic organisms for water resources, resulting in the decline of the state's highly endemic aquatic fauna, particularly fishes. The authors developed this classification system to address the need for systematic protection of biological diversity in distinct geographic regions. The system builds upon and refines an earlier version by Ellison (1984), incorporating more detailed habitat descriptions and better accounting for regional and local endemism.

The classification is organized hierarchically, with ichthyological province as the primary subdivider. These provinces correspond to major drainage basins and are equivalent to "aquatic ecoregions." The hierarchical structure follows this pattern:

- X0000: Ichthyological Province
- X1000: Standing Waters or X2000: Flowing Waters
- X2100: Ephemeral Waters
- X2200: Permanent Waters
  - X2210: Fishless streams
  - X2220: Types of streams containing fish

The system includes a final category for [[Artificial Habitats]], which are not subdivided by region because they differ little between provinces and typically have unstable water supplies dominated by introduced fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The classification system recognizes that California's aquatic environments exhibit high variability and that many sites today bear little resemblance to their original condition due to human disturbance. The system assumes that patterns of endemism and distribution in fishes are similar to those of less well-known aquatic organisms and that fish profoundly affect the distribution and abundance of other aquatic organisms.

### Sacramento-San Joaquin Province (A0000)

This province includes the Central Valley and coastal drainages with similar fish faunas. Key habitat types include:

- **Ephemeral Waters**: [[Vernal Pools]] (northern claypan pools, northeast volcanic pools), [[Floodplain Pools]], [[Playa Lakes]], and [[Alpine Pools]]
- **Permanent Fishless Waters**: [[Alpine Lakes]], [[Caldera Lakes]], [[Valley Marshes]]
- **Permanent Waters with Fish**: [[Goose Lake]], [[Tulare Basin Lakes]], [[Clear Lake]], [[Big Lake]], coastal lagoons

Stream types range from fishless alpine streams to complex valley floor rivers supporting diverse fish assemblages including [[Sacramento Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus grandis*), [[Sacramento Sucker]] (*Catostomus occidentalis*), [[Hardhead]] (*Mylopharodon conocephatus*), [[California Roach]] (*Lavinia symmetricus*), and anadromous salmonids.

### Klamath and North Coast Province (B0000)

This province encompasses the [[Klamath River]], [[Trinity River]], and coastal drainages. Notable features include:

- Endemic subspecies such as [[McCloud River Redband Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss* subsp.)
- [[Klamath Smallscale Sucker]] (*Catostomus rimiculus*)
- [[Rough Sculpin]] (*Cottus asperrimus*) and [[Shasta Crayfish]] (*Pacifastacus fortis*) in spring streams
- Complex anadromous fish assemblages including [[Eulachon]] (*Thaleichthys pacificus*), [[Chinook Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*), [[Coho Salmon]] (*O. kisutch*), [[Steelhead]], and [[Coastal Cutthroat Trout]] (*O. clarki clarki*)

### Great Basin Province (C0000)

This province includes interior drainages east of the Sierra Nevada. Key habitats include:

- [[Lake Tahoe]]: A large, deep, extraordinarily clear alpine lake
- [[Eagle Lake]]: An alkaline terminal lake containing endemic [[Eagle Lake Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss aquilarum*)
- [[Mono Lake]]: A distinctive alkaline lake with endemic invertebrate fauna
- Desert springs containing endemic pupfishes: [[Amargosa Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis*), [[Owens Pupfish]] (*C. radiosus*), [[Salt Creek Pupfish]] (*C. salinus*)
- Streams supporting [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarki henshawi*), [[Paiute Cutthroat Trout]] (*O. c. seleniris*), [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*), and [[Lahontan Redside]] (*Richardsonius egregius*)

### Colorado River Province (D0000)

This province includes the Colorado River and its tributaries in California. The main river originally supported a fauna of endemic big-river fishes and euryhaline invaders from the Gulf of California. Backwaters and marshes were important nursery areas and the presumed home of [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*).

### Southern California Coastal Province (E0000)

This province encompasses coastal drainages from Point Conception south. Notable habitats include:

- Vernal pools on the [[Santa Rosa Plateau]] and [[San Diego Mesa]]
- Coastal lagoons characterized by [[Tidewater Goby]] (*Eucyclogobius newberryi*)
- Streams supporting [[Arroyo Chub]] (*Gila orcutti*), [[Santa Ana Sucker]] (*Catostomus santaanae*), and [[Speckled Dace]]
- Steelhead spawning streams with [[Prickly Sculpin]] and [[Threespine Stickleback]] (*Gasterosteus aculeatus*)

## Key Findings

1. **Hierarchical Structure**: The classification system successfully organizes California's diverse inland waters into a hierarchical framework based on ichthyological provinces, water permanence, and fish presence/absence.

2. **Endemic Species Recognition**: The system explicitly recognizes numerous endemic taxa, including:
   - [[Clear Lake Splittail]] (*Pogonichthys ciscoides*) - extinct
   - [[Clear Lake Tule Perch]] (*Hysterocarpus traski laguna*)
   - [[Thicktail Chub]] (*Gila crassicauda*) - extinct
   - [[Modoc Sucker]] (*Catostomus microps*)
   - [[Pit Sculpin]] (*Cottus pitensis*)
   - Multiple subspecies of trout, pupfish, and other taxa

3. **Conservation Applications**: The system provides a structure for:
   - Identifying priority areas for [[Aquatic Diversity Management Areas]]
   - Maximizing protection of biodiversity with limited resources
   - Predicting which organisms are likely present at classified sites
   - Expanding the system without disrupting existing databases

4. **Habitat Diversity**: California's inland waters encompass extraordinary habitat diversity, from alpine lakes and snowmelt streams to desert springs and coastal lagoons, each supporting distinct biological communities.

5. **Anthropogenic Impacts**: Many aquatic environments today bear little resemblance to their original condition due to water diversions, introduced species, and habitat modification. The system acknowledges that highly disturbed sites may be difficult to classify without historical information.

6. **Artificial Habitats**: The system includes a separate category for artificial habitats (reservoirs, ponds, canals, ditches) which typically have unstable water supplies and are dominated by introduced fishes.

7. **Flexibility and Expandability**: The numbering system allows for addition of new categories without disrupting the existing structure, accommodating future discoveries and refined understanding of aquatic habitats.
