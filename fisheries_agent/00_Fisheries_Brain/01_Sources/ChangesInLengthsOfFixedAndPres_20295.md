# Changes in Lengths of Fixed and Preserved Young Freshwater Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #esocidae #percidae

## Executive Summary

This study measured body-part lengths of several species of young freshwater fish to determine the short- and medium-term effects of [[Fixation]] and [[Preservation]]. [[Shrinkage]] of up to 4.9% was the general rule, but some lengths increased or stayed static. [[Rigor Mortis]] is probably the main cause of immediate change, while [[Measurement Error]] likely affected precision for the smallest lengths. Changes differed among body parts but were not species specific, and the type of fixative or preservative was not significant. The authors concluded that the changes were of little or no practical consequence for taxonomy and growth studies.

## Background & Description

Fixation and preservation effects on [[Marine Larval Fish]] had been reported in several studies, mostly concerning shrinkage in body length. It was unknown whether freshwater larvae respond in the same way. The study was designed to document artificial morphological changes that could interfere with identification, accurate taxonomic description, and the diagnosis of natural deformities in young fish. It also examined the role of measurement precision.

**Materials and methods**

- **Short-term changes:** Several larval species were seined from creeks near Hamilton, Ontario, and taken alive to the laboratory within 2 h. Fish were anaesthetized with 50% carbonated water or [[Ms-222]], measured, placed in numbered vials with specific fixatives, and remeasured after 1 or 2 d and at least three more times within 45–60 d. *Pimephales notatus* was divided into three groups fixed in [[4% Formalin]] buffered with marble chips, [[60% Ethanol]], or [[Davidson's B]].
- **Medium-term changes:** 145 *Catostomus commersoni* larvae (14.1–20.2 mm TL) were dip-netted from a local stream, divided into four groups of 35–40 fish, and fixed on site in Davidson's B, 60% ethanol, 4% formalin, or [[Borealene]] (10%). Measurements were taken 10 wk after fixation and then at 2, 4, 6, 10, 13, 17, 40, and 61 wk later. In addition, 22 juvenile *Esox americanus vermiculatus* from [[Twenty Mile Creek]] were fixed and preserved in 10% Borealene and measured at intervals out to 101 wk.
- **Measurements:** Linear measures included total length, standard length, head, orbit, snout to vent, least and greatest body depth, yolk sac, body, trunk, and postanal length, using a dissecting microscope with an ocular micrometer.
- **Statistics:** Lengths were compared with live (initial) lengths. A one-sided paired t-test was used for short-term data and a two-way ANOVA for medium-term data. Measurements above 10 mm were recorded to 0.1 mm, so differences up to ±0.14 mm were treated as unchanged.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fixation and preservation are routine steps in handling field samples of larval fish, but they can distort the morphological characters used in species identification and growth estimates. The authors proposed that differences between marine and freshwater responses may relate mainly to [[Osmotic Processes]] and salinity. Tissue water content, the ratio of white to red muscle, and developmental stage were also considered likely influences on shrinkage. Formalin was noted to progressively dissolve skeletal calcium, so its effects may depend on the developmental stage of the larvae.

**Short-term results**

- *Pimephales notatus* shrinkage in standard length after 53 d was 4.3% in Davidson's B, 4.1% in 4% formalin, and 4.9% in 60% ethanol. These changes were significant (t-test; P < 0.01), and no further length change occurred after the first postfixative measurement on day 4.
- Most change in the short term occurred within 2 d of fixation. For *Etheostoma nigrum*, individual fish shrank as much as 6% and increased up to 3% in total length.
- *Esox lucius* lengths 60 d after fixation differed significantly from live lengths (t-test; P < 0.01), but virtually no change occurred after the first day. Trunk and postanal shrinkage were 2.9% and 5.8%, respectively, in 4% formalin.
- In *Esox americanus vermiculatus*, total and snout to vent shrinkage were highly significant (t-test; P < 0.01). External morphology of all specimens appeared unaffected by preservatives.

**Medium-term results**

- Across 61 wk, mean total and snout to vent lengths of 145 *Catostomus commersoni* were unchanged or negligible in Davidson's B, 4% formalin, and Borealene. In 60% ethanol, shrinkage in total length was barely perceptible (0.2 mm). No temporal effect of preservation was detected (ANOVA; P < 0.01).
- In 22 juvenile *Esox americanus vermiculatus* (52.0 ± 2.7 mm TL) preserved for 101 wk, head length shrank 2.6%, orbit and least depth shrank 5–6%, and total length increased 1% and body length increased 2.3%. All were highly significant (ANOVA; P < 0.01).
- Individual lengths fluctuated as much as ±10% between successive measurement dates, which the authors attributed partly to measurement error on relatively large young fish.

**Main conclusions**

- Shrinkage (up to 4.9%) was typical, but lengths sometimes increased or remained static.
- Trunk and snout to vent length were the most susceptible body parts, and head length was the least affected.
- Changes were not species specific, and the fixative or preservative type was not significant.
- Shrinkage was greater in larger larvae than in small ones, which differs from marine clupeid studies where preservation shrinkage was 2–43%.
- Measurement error can mask real changes. The authors recommended comparing the mean of several measurement intervals with the live or initial lengths rather than relying on any two dates.
- Overall, the changes were judged to be of little or no practical consequence for taxonomy and growth studies, and they were independent of preservative type.

**Related literature cited**

- [[Silver Hake]] (*Merluccius bilinearis*) larvae showed differing shrinkage between ethanol and formalin (Fowler and Smith 1983), which the authors did not replicate.
- Freshwater fish preserved for more than 5 yr (nine species) showed no significant change in several body characters (Leslie 1983).
- Duration of preservation affects shrinkage in marine fish (Lockwood and Daly 1975; Schnack and Rosenthal 1978; Hay 1981), but this was not seen for *Catostomus commersoni* in the freshwater study.
