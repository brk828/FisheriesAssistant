## Executive Summary
This 1980 California Department of Fish and Game memorandum documents progress on a telemetry-based movement study of "HBS" (Humpback Sucker, likely *Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], part of the Lower Colorado River system near Blythe, CA. Six fish were fitted with ultrasonic tags (4 females, 2 males) and 27 additional individuals were floy-tagged before release. Early monitoring (Feb 5, 7, 8, 1980) revealed methodological issues with channel discrimination and monitoring interval length, prompting two protocol revisions: (1) restricting monitoring to lower, more discernible frequency channels, and (2) shifting from 4-hour interval sampling to continuous 24-hour tracking with position fixes every 30–60 minutes. A mark-recapture population estimate originally planned for immediately after tagging was postponed to April/May to allow incision healing.

## Background & Description
- Surgical implantation of ultrasonic tags was performed intracoelomically (incision-based), consistent with standard sonic-tag telemetry methodology for [[Catostomidae]] species.
- Sex and reproductive staging were assessed at time of tagging:
  - 4 females, 2 males selected for sonic tag implants.
  - Gamete development stages recorded: one female "spent," remaining fish in "ripe" or "green" stages — indicating the population was captured during an active pre-spawning to spawning reproductive window.
- No morphometric (length/weight) data reported in this memorandum; focus is behavioral/movement data derived from tag signal frequency channels (1.75, 3.00, and 5.25 kHz reported).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Species occupies [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], an impounded reservoir habitat linked to the Lower Colorado River, suggesting lentic (reservoir) habitat use during at least part of the reproductive cycle.
- Post-release behavior included schooling and participation in **pre-spawning activity**, indicating gregarious aggregation behavior tied to reproductive timing.
- Movement patterns of tagged individuals were reported as comparable to untagged conspecifics observed in the field, supporting minimal tagging-induced behavioral bias.
- Fish exhibited notable capacity for **long-distance excursions within short time windows** (including an 18-hour tracked movement), with some individuals returning to nearly the exact starting location — suggesting site-fidelity or homing behavior around specific habitat features (possibly spawning or staging areas) despite substantial interim travel distance.
- Environmental variability (reservoir conditions, weather) was noted to directly affect ultrasonic signal detection quality, an important confound for interpreting telemetry-based habitat use.

## Key Findings
- **Tagging cohort**: 6 ultrasonic-tagged + 27 floy-tagged HBS released into Senator Wash Reservoir following January tagging efforts.
- **Reproductive status**: Majority of sonic-tagged fish were in "ripe" or "green" gamete development stages, one female "spent" — study coincided with a spawning period.
- **Behavioral confirmation**: Tagged fish observed schooling and engaging in pre-spawning activity post-release, with movement comparable to untagged fish (validates telemetry approach as non-disruptive).
- **Methodological problem 1 — frequency channel confusion**: High-frequency channels on the receiver were difficult to distinguish under variable reservoir/weather conditions, risking fish misidentification. Resolution: restrict monitoring to lower, more discernible channels (1.75, 3.00, 5.25 kHz) with standardized identification instructions for all personnel.
- **Methodological problem 2 — sampling interval too coarse**: Original design used 4-hour interval fixes across all 6 fish (36 data points/day), but fish showed capability for large, rapid round-trip movements within intervals, risking false "no movement" conclusions. Resolution: shift to continuous 24-hour tracking of a single individual per session, with fixes every 30–60 minutes.
- **Study design impact**: Both protocol changes were implemented without altering manpower commitments or planned data analysis framework.
- **Delayed population estimate**: Mark-recapture population estimate postponed to April/May 1980 to allow tagging incisions to heal before re-netting, reducing handling stress and mortality risk.
- **Multi-agency collaboration**: Study involved coordinated participation from [[California Department of Fish and Game]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[Water and Power Resources Service]], reflecting an interagency approach to Lower Colorado River fisheries management.
