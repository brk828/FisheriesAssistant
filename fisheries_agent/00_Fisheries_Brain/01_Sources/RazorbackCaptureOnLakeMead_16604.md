# Razorback Sucker Captures and Propagation Records, Lake Mead and Southwest Region (1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of 1993 agency records on the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an imperiled native catostomid. It contains three items: an internal memo summarizing capture and recapture data from [[Lake Mead]], a letter requesting information on a captive population and on broodstock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and a fish planting receipt with a field data table. A newspaper clipping on a newly found wild-appearing population in Colorado is also included. Together they document low capture numbers, very few females, growth observations on recaptured fish, and uncertainty about the genetic origin and reproductive potential of captive stock.

## Background & Description

The material spans several agencies and sites:

- **Internal memo (June 30, 1993):** Jim Heinrich reported Razorback captures on Lake Mead since 1990 to Chuck Minckley. Only Echo Bay and Vegas Wash had produced Razorbacks, although other locations had been sampled.
- **Letter (June 30, 1993):** Written from the state office in Las Vegas to Buddy Jensen at Dexter National Fish Hatchery, New Mexico. It concerns a captive stock held at [[Floyd Lamb State Park]].
- **Fish planting receipt:** A [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] form recording a stocking event, including station, water code, average size, number per pound, marking, water temperature, and condition.
- **Field data table:** Individual records with date, sex (1 = male, 2 = female), total and fork length (mm as recorded), weight (pounds or grams), location, PIT-style tag number, and comments such as "RECAP," "RIPE," "HEALTHY/MILT," and "DEFORMED SPINE."
- **Newspaper clipping:** Reports the discovery of 67 Razorbacks in a pond near [[De Beque, Colorado]], about 25 miles east of Grand Junction. The [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] spokesman said the population included young and old fish, indicating reproduction. Biologists had searched 25 other lakes and ponds in western Colorado without finding a developed population.

Some OCR artifacts in the table were corrected. The table text is partly degraded, so some values are uncertain.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake Mead captures.** Captures were concentrated at Echo Bay and at the site recorded as Blackbird. Vegas Wash also yielded fish. Sampling for 1993 was concluded, though a few summer sets were possible.

**Floyd Lamb population.** Fish were received from Dexter in 1984 and have been held in a dirt pond since then. No recruitment had been documented. The sender suggested that small numbers of sunfish and bass might be a factor. The letter asked whether these fish would spawn in substrates other than gravel.

**Broodstock and gamete collection.** The writer noted that stripping females that had absorbed their eggs over several seasons (in rainbow trout experience) was nearly impossible. The question was whether eggs could be taken from the Floyd Lamb fish in future.

**Associated species.** The letter asked about the status of [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]] held at Dexter, and requested 20–30 juveniles in August to augment that broodstock.

## Key Findings

**1. Low sample size and sex ratio (Lake Mead, 1990–1993)**
- 43 Razorbacks captured in total.
- Only 11 were female (about 26%).
- Six fish were recaptured in 1993: one marked in 1993 and five marked in 1992.

**2. Growth on recaptures**
Heinrich noted tangible growth in at least three Echo Bay fish, but said the sample was too small to call them "young." Recorded growth increments for recaptures include:
- +22 (tag 3272)
- +12 (tag 3E40)
- +25 (tag 5D68)

One recaptured fish (tag 550D) had a deformed spine. Another (tag OE53) showed no growth.

**3. Reproductive condition**
Several males in March 1992 were recorded as "MILT" (running milt). A female tagged in 1992 was noted as "RIPE," and another as "NO EGGS." These notes are based on field observations of the captured fish.

**4. Captive population at Floyd Lamb State Park**
- Held since 1984 in a dirt pond, origin from Dexter.
- No recruitment recorded.
- Open questions: whether the fish are F1 offspring of original wild broodstock, whether they will spawn on non-gravel substrate, and whether eggs can be obtained.
- The letter requested that results be shared with Chuck Minckley at Parker, Arizona, and noted interest from the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (associated with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]).

**5. Broodstock program needs**
Dexter was asked about the status of Pahranagat Roundtail Chub broodstock and whether it could supply 20–30 juveniles in August to augment its stock.

**6. Colorado discovery**
A pond near De Beque held 67 Razorbacks, including young and old individuals, which the Colorado Division of Wildlife took as evidence of natural reproduction. Biologists had found no developed population in 25 other western Colorado lakes and ponds before this. This is a notable finding for a species with a limited number of extant wild populations.
