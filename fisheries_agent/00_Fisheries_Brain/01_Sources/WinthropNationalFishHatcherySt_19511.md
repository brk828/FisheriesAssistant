# Winthrop National Fish Hatchery Station Profile

## Executive Summary
This document is a fiscal year station profile (Exhibit 1) for the **[[Winthrop National Fish Hatchery]]**, located in Winthrop, Okanogan County, Washington, under U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 1 (Leavenworth Complex). Established in 1940 under the **[[Mitchell Act]]** (52 Stat. 345), the hatchery was constructed to mitigate the loss of anadromous fish runs in the **[[Upper Columbia River]]** drainage caused by the construction of **[[Grand Coulee Dam]]**. The facility's primary mission is the production of **[[Spring Chinook Salmon]]** to support re-establishment of anadromous runs in the Columbia River system. The profile documents physical infrastructure, funding/personnel trends (1989-1993), egg and smolt production, distribution data, and a significant predation loss event during winter 1990/91.

## Background & Description
The source document does not provide morphological data on the species itself (e.g., body plan, coloration, meristic counts). It is an administrative/operational hatchery profile rather than a biological description. Species identity is limited to: **[[Spring Chinook Salmon]]** (green eggs, SCS designation used in production records), reared through egg, fry, smolt, and yearling life stages within hatchery infrastructure before release as anadromous migrants.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Life history context**: [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] are anadromous, requiring passage between freshwater natal streams and the North Pacific Ocean; the species' natural migratory corridor in the Upper Columbia River was blocked by [[Grand Coulee Dam]], necessitating hatchery mitigation.
- **Habitat provided**: Hatchery infrastructure mimics rearing habitat via 30 raceways (8' x 80'), 16 raceways (12' x 102'), and 16 **[[Foster-Lucas Ponds]]**, plus two dual-purpose adult holding/rearing ponds and two infiltration gallery pumping structures for water supply.
- **Predation pressure**: Wild **[[Mallard Duck]]** predation was documented as a major ecological interaction affecting hatchery-reared smolts, indicating vulnerability of yearling salmon to avian predators in open-air rearing ponds/raceways.
- **Water quality management**: A pollution abatement lagoon is part of station infrastructure, reflecting effluent/waste management practices tied to fish-rearing density and water discharge back into the Columbia River system.

## Key Findings
- **Establishment & Authority**: Hatchery built in 1940 under the [[Mitchell Act]] to mitigate anadromous fish losses from [[Grand Coulee Dam]] construction.
- **FY 1991 Visitors**: 2,328.
- **FY 1991 Egg Production**: 117,778 green eggs (Spring Chinook Salmon, "SCS").
- **Predation Loss Event (Winter 1990/91)**: Mallard duck predation resulted in the loss of over 286,000 yearling smolts, representing a potential economic loss exceeding $340,000 had these fish survived to adulthood — a critical finding highlighting predator management as an operational vulnerability.
- **Physical Facilities**: Four residences; two infiltration gallery pumping structures; two dual-purpose adult holding/rearing ponds; one pollution abatement lagoon; one generator building; one hatchery building; one visitor comfort station; one feed/storage building; 30 raceways (8'x80'); 16 raceways (12'x102'); 16 Foster-Lucas ponds.
- **Funding & Staffing Trends (1989–1993)**:
  - Operations budget grew from $153,000 (1989 actual) to a projected $320,000 (1993 estimate).
  - Maintenance budget introduced at $104,000 (1991) rising to a projected $210,000 (1993).
  - FTE staffing ranged 3.6–5 across the period.
  - Cost recoverable funding included $77,000 from **[[BIA]]** (Bureau of Indian Affairs).
- **Fish Distribution (Spring Chinook Salmon, Washington State)**:
  - FY89: 2,576,138 total distributed (Federal FWS waters).
  - FY90: 1,676,709 total (1,324,766 Federal FWS waters + 351,843 other waters).
  - FY91: 1,472,920 total (Federal FWS waters).
  - Note: These totals exclude egg and fry counts, which are reported separately under "Other Information."
- **Congressional Oversight**: Washington State delegation includes Senator Brock Adams (D), Senator Slade Gorton (R), and Representative Sid Morrison (R), 4th District — relevant for federal appropriations and mitigation authority tied to the Mitchell Act program.
