# Fish Survey of the Gila River within the Gila Wilderness Area, New Mexico (June and August 1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report documents a fish survey of the [[Gila River]] within the [[Gila Wilderness Area]] in the [[Gila National Forest]], [[New Mexico]], conducted on 6-10 June and 18 August 1994. The survey was carried out by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (New Mexico Ecological Services State Office), the Gila National Forest, and [[Western New Mexico University]] (WNMU). Sampling covered roughly every river mile from the mouth of El Rincon canyon downstream to Hidden Pasture canyon, about 1 mile upstream of the wilderness boundary. Four collection methods were used: [[Electrofishing]], [[Kick Seining]], [[Snorkeling]], and [[Trammel Net]] sets. Fifteen fish species were recorded. Common species included the [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]], and [[Roundtail Chub]], a species of special concern, was found only once.

## Background & Description

The survey was designed to characterize the fish community along the length of the Gila River inside the wilderness boundary. The river was divided into four reaches, A through D, and an additional reach was added by WNMU upstream of the uppermost June site. The reaches were sampled in June, and WNMU resampled the upper section on 18 August using the same electrofishing methods.

Methods included:

- **Electrofishing:** 23 sites sampled with a 24-volt Smith-Root backpack electrofisher in small pools and riffles. Night electrofishing was conducted at two locations.
- **Kick seining:** Used simultaneously with electrofishing in riffles to sample for [[Loach Minnow]] (*Rhinichthys cobitis*).
- **Snorkeling:** Selected large pools were qualitatively surveyed by snorkeling during the day and at night.
- **Trammel nets:** Set overnight at points near camp locations, positioned across the entire stream in pools more than 1 meter deep.

Personnel from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the U.S. Forest Service, and WNMU participated. Acknowledgements list the team members for each agency.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Gila River survey area lies within a wilderness setting, and the report emphasizes the presence or absence of species of special concern. Electrofishing was the dominant sampling method, and sampling effort is expressed in [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPUE, fish per minute).

Reported environmental conditions during the August WNMU work were described as similar to those in June. The report does not provide detailed water chemistry, habitat measurements, or invertebrate data.

The community is dominated by native cyprinids and catostomids (such as [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Longfin Dace]], and [[Speckled Dace]]) alongside non-native or introduced species (such as [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Green Sunfish]]). [[Rainbow Trout]] were also present in several reaches.

## Key Findings

**Overall survey results (June 1994)**

- Total electrofishing effort was 18,174 seconds, with an overall CPUE of 0.693 fish/minute.
- The number of fish sampled per reach ranged from 20 to 80 (Tables 2-5).
- Three species made up 78% of the total electrofishing catch.
- [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]] were the most abundant, with n = 57 each.
  - Mean total length of Desert Sucker was 116 mm.
  - Mean total length of Smallmouth Bass was 133 mm.
- [[Sonora Sucker]] ranked third in frequency of occurrence (n = 47, as read from a scanned table) and had a mean length of 164 mm.
- Fifteen species were recorded across all sampling (Table 1 lists 14 species from the June and August surveys, and [[Speckled Dace]] was added in the WNMU tables).

**Reach-level CPUE (electrofishing, June)**

| Reach | CPUE (fish/minute) |
|---|---|
| A1-A7 | 1.02 |
| B1-B5 | 0.81 |
| C1-C6 | 0.27 |
| D1-D6 | 0.80 |

**Trammel net results (reaches A-D)**

- Five sets were fished for a total of 97.3 hours and produced 17 fish representing six species.
- Trammel net CPUE was 0.144 fish/minute.
- The only [[Roundtail Chub]] from the entire June survey (n = 2) was captured in set D5.
- No fish were caught in set A1.

**Species of special concern**

- [[Roundtail Chub]] was very rare. Snorkeling did not detect it, and the report notes "the paucity of species of special concern such as roundtail chub."
- [[Loach Minnow]] was documented in the survey, and WNMU reported it more frequently in August.

**Snorkeling results**

- Snorkeling was qualitative and produced no quantitative data.
- No species were observed underwater that were not captured by other methods.

**August 1994 follow-up (WNMU, 18 August)**

- WNMU used the same electrofishing methods and sampled every river mile, starting 3.2 km (2 miles) above the uppermost June site and ending near the confluence of the East Fork Gila River.
- No new species were recorded.
- [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], and [[Loach Minnow]] were caught more frequently than in June.
- Reach-level CPUE values from WNMU sites were:

| WNMU Site | CPUE (fish/minute) |
|---|---|
| WNMU-1 | 1.18 |
| WNMU-2 | 1.36 |
| WNMU-3 | 3.18 |
| WNMU-4 | 3.46 |
