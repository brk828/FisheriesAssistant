# Lake Mohave Sampling — 22-27 November 1993

## Executive Summary
This report documents a 6-day trammel-net survey of Lake Mohave (Arizona/Nevada, Lower Colorado River) conducted by Arizona State University in cooperation with the Arizona Game and Fish Department, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and volunteers. The primary objective was monitoring of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) population. Six adult razorback suckers were captured, including two long-term PIT-tag recaptures representing the first documented recapture events for those individuals. By-catch consisted of six other species, dominated numerically by [[Bluegill]]. Overall catch and catch-per-effort (CPE) were low relative to typical November sampling, with the notable exception of bluegill.

## Background & Description
**Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**
- **Males (n = 3):** Total length averaged 54.9 ± 0.6 cm (SD), range 54.5–55.6 cm; weight averaged 2.04 ± 0.16 kg, range 1.85–2.14 kg. Males exhibited nuptial tuberculation but none were in ripe reproductive condition.
- **Females (n = 3):** Total length averaged 63.4 ± 4.1 cm, range 59.8–67.8 cm; weight averaged 3.33 ± 0.74 kg, range 2.53–4.00 kg.
- Females were notably larger and heavier than males in this sample, consistent with known sexual size dimorphism in the species.
- All captured adults were examined for general health/condition, scanned for existing PIT tags, tagged if untagged, and released alive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat sampled:** Points and shorelines from the northern portion of Cottonwood Cove upstream to Hammerhead Cove, at depths of 1–6 m — typical nearshore adult [[Razorback Sucker]] habitat in [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Sampling method:** Trammel nets deployed for five 24-hour periods, cleared twice daily; total effort = 41.75 units (fish/100 m²/24-hr basis).
- **Community context:** The razorback sucker co-occurs with a mixed native/non-native fish assemblage including [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Striped Bass]] — the latter three representing introduced piscivore/competitor pressure relevant to razorback sucker recruitment failure in this reservoir system.
- **Movement/longevity data:** Recaptured individuals demonstrated long-distance movement and extended residency: one fish moved from River Mile (RM) 29 (Cove 126, AZ) to the current capture site over 982 days at large; another moved from RM 19 (6-Mile, NV) over 252 days at large.

## Key Findings
- **Total catch:** 171 fish across 7 species; total CPE = 4.096 fish/100 m²/24-hr.

- **First documented recaptures:** Two PIT-tagged razorback suckers (one male, one female) were recaptured for the first time since initial tagging in March 1991 and March 1993, respectively, providing direct evidence of longevity and mobility within the reservoir over multi-year and multi-mile scales.
- **Low relative abundance:** Total and individual species catch/CPE (except bluegill) were relatively low compared to typical November samples, suggesting possible seasonal, environmental, or population-level variability warranting continued monitoring.
- **Management follow-up:** Under the Native Fish Work Group, Lake Mohave was scheduled for a return visit in winter-spring 1994 to collect razorback sucker larvae for grow-out and eventual reintroduction to backwaters and the main lake — part of an ongoing [[Razorback Sucker]] conservation/augmentation program. An additional adult sampling trip was scheduled for 13-19 March 1994.
