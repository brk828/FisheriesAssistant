## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory amendment to an annual Endangered Species Subpermit report (PRT 676811) filed by a BLM Fishery Biologist in Tucson, Arizona. It supplements a prior memorandum (December 4, 1992) by disclosing previously omitted collection data for [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis]] (Gila Topminnow), an endangered fish species native to the Sonoran Desert. The report details two survey events: one targeted survey at Yerba Mansa Spring for both Gila Topminnow and [[Cyprinodon macularius]] (Desert Pupfish), and one incidental bycatch collection at Cienega Creek during a targeted survey for [[Gila intermedia]] (Gila Chub). All fish captured across both events were released alive, consistent with standard endangered species handling protocols under the permit.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide morphological, meristic, or biometric data for any of the three species referenced. As a regulatory compliance memorandum rather than a primary research paper, it focuses exclusively on collection counts, locality data, and permit administration. No length, weight, coloration, or fin-ray count data are reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis]] (Gila Topminnow):** Found co-occurring (at least in survey intent) with Desert Pupfish at Yerba Mansa Spring, indicating shared or overlapping spring-fed habitat. Also documented as an incidental bycatch species at [[Cienega Creek]], suggesting a broader distribution across desert spring and perennial creek habitats in Pima County, Arizona.
- **[[Cyprinodon macularius]] (Desert Pupfish):** Targeted at Yerba Mansa Spring but not detected during the April 1992 survey effort, implying either absence, low density, or extirpation at this locality at the time of sampling.
- **[[Gila intermedia]] (Gila Chub):** Serves as the primary target species for the Cienega Creek survey effort, with Gila Topminnow recorded as incidental bycatch. This indicates habitat overlap between the two native Arizona fish species in Cienega Creek's aquatic system.

## Key Findings

- **Yerba Mansa Spring Survey (April 20, 1992):** 18 individuals of *P. o. occidentalis* were collected; **zero** *C. macularius* were captured. All specimens released alive at the site.
- **Cienega Creek Survey (December 21, 1992):** 2 individuals of *P. o. occidentalis* collected as bycatch while targeting [[Gila intermedia]]. All specimens released alive.
- **Permit Administration:** Personnel changes were requested for the 1993 subpermit — Lori Young and David Hoerath removed (to be listed under a separate subpermit), and Benjamin Nelson added as an authorized collector.
- **Conservation Handling Protocol:** Consistent catch-and-release methodology was employed across all documented collection events, reflecting standard practice for handling federally listed endangered species under [[Endangered Species Act]] permitting.
- **Reporting Context:** This memorandum explicitly corrects an omission in the original December 4, 1992 report, underscoring the administrative rigor required for endangered species subpermit compliance and renewal (Permit [[PRT 676811]]).
