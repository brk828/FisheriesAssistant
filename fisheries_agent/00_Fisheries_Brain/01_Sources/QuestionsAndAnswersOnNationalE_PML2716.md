# NEPA Public Involvement and Environmental Assessment Guidance

#ichthyology #marine-biology #environmental-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory guidance item, not a fisheries or ecological study. It combines two parts: the public involvement provisions of the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) implementing regulations (Section 1506.6), and a set of questions and answers issued by the [[Council on Environmental Quality]] (CEQ) and published in the Federal Register in March 1981. The Q&A compiles forty frequently asked questions from regional meetings held in 1980 with federal, state and local officials. It explains how agencies should handle alternatives, [[Record of Decision]] enforceability, timelines, [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA) content, and [[Finding of No Significant Impact]] (FONSI) procedures. No species, habitats, water chemistry or population data are reported.

## Background & Description

**Public involvement (Section 1506.6).** Agencies must make diligent efforts to involve the public in preparing and implementing NEPA procedures. They must provide public notice of hearings, public meetings and the availability of environmental documents. Notice requirements vary with scope:

- **Actions of national concern:** publication in the *Federal Register*, mailed notice to national organizations, and possibly listing in the *EIS Monitor*. Agencies must maintain a list of these organizations.
- **Actions of primarily local concern:** notice may include state and areawide clearinghouses (under OMB Circular A-95), Indian tribes where effects may occur on reservations, local newspapers, newsletters, community organizations, direct mailing to owners and occupants of affected property, and on- and off-site posting.
- **Hearings:** agencies should hold public hearings or meetings when appropriate. Criteria include substantial environmental controversy or substantial interest in holding a hearing.
- **Access to documents:** environmental impact statements, received comments and underlying documents must be made available under the [[Freedom of Information Act]], without the interagency memoranda exclusion for comments from federal agencies. Copies may be charged at no more than actual reproduction cost.

**CEQ Questions and Answers (1981).** The document is organized as an index of forty questions covering topics such as:

- Range of alternatives
- No-action alternative and agency-preferred alternative
- Environmentally preferable alternative
- Applicants who need other permits
- Limitations on actions during the EIS process
- Scoping, lead and cooperating agencies
- Third-party contracts and conflicts of interest
- Mitigation measures
- Adoption of EISs
- Environmental assessments and FONSIs
- Time required for the NEPA process

The answers state that they do not impose requirements beyond the regulations and do not represent new guidance. They reflect advice CEQ had already given at the 1980 regional meetings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not describe any ecosystem, species or habitat. Its environmental references are procedural. It notes that the public review of a FONSI is required when a proposal affects a [[Floodplains|floodplain]] or [[Wetlands|wetland]], citing [[Executive Order 11988]] and [[Executive Order 11990]]. It also identifies "a pristine stream" as an example of an unusual action warranting public review. These are regulatory triggers, not ecological data.

## Key Findings

- **Public notice is required, and its methods should fit the scope of the action.** Federal Register notice and mailed notice suit national actions. Local newspapers, posting and direct mailing suit local actions. The stated objective is to notify all interested or affected parties. If that objective is not met, the methods should be reevaluated and changed.
- **Hearing materials must be released before the hearing.** If a draft EIS will be considered at a public hearing, the agency should make it available at least 15 days in advance, unless the hearing's purpose is to present information on the draft.
- **Repeated failure to reach interested or affected parties could be treated as a violation** of the regulations.
- **Alternatives must be rigorously explored and objectively evaluated.** Decisionmakers may not consider alternatives beyond the range discussed in the environmental documents, but they must consider all alternatives discussed in an EIS.
- **Records of Decision are enforceable.** Agencies are accountable for carrying out the actions in their Records of Decision, and these can be used to compel compliance with identified mitigation measures.
- **Timelines are manageable.** CEQ advised that even large, complex energy projects should need only about 12 months for the full EIS process. Preparing an EA should take no more than about 3 months.
- **EAs should be concise.** An EA should briefly provide evidence and analysis for determining whether an EIS is needed. It should discuss alternatives and mitigation measures and list the agencies and persons consulted.
- **Public review of FONSIs is needed in specific cases.** These include borderline proposals, unusual actions, actions with scientific or public controversy, and actions similar to those that normally require an EIS.
- **EAs and FONSIs must be made available to the public.** Agencies should use a mix of notification methods.
- **EAs may include enforceable mitigation.** Even when impacts are not significant, an EA can include mitigation measures that agencies may adopt, and these should be discussed in the EA.
