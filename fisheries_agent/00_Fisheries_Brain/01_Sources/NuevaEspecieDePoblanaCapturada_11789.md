# Poblana ferdebueni n. sp. (Atherinidae) from Laguna de Almoloya, Puebla, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 taxonomic paper, published in *Ciencia* (Mexico), describes *[[Poblana Ferdebueni]]* as a new species of silverside (family [[Atherinidae]]). The fish was collected from the spring-fed [[Laguna De Almoloya]] (also called Laguna de Chignahuapan) near the town of [[Chignahuapan]], in the State of [[Puebla]], Mexico. The authors, A. Solórzano and J. Obregón, based the description on a holotype and numerous paratypes from two collecting trips (1959 and 1964). They separated the species from its congeners using meristic counts (gill rakers, lateral scales, fin rays) and morphometric ratios, and compared it with three other members of the genus *Poblana*: *[[Poblana Alchichica]]*, *[[Poblana Letholepis]]*, and *[[Poblana Hidalgoi]]*. The species is named in honour of [[Fernando De Buen]].

## Background & Description

**Discovery and naming.** The spring, which drains toward the [[Necaxa Dam]] through tunnel no. 26 and partly feeds that reservoir, was visited in the second half of May 1959 by [[Jorge Obregón]] and [[Aurelio Solórzano]]. Their collections contained many atherinids that did not match any known species of *Poblana*, even though the genus assignment was not in doubt. The species is named in honour of Dr. Fernando de Buen for his contributions to Mexican ichthyology.

**Type material.**
- **[[Holotype]]:** one adult specimen, 58.2 mm standard length, collected 22 May 1959 at Laguna de Almoloya.
- **[[Paratype]] series:** 177 specimens, 40.7–70.8 mm standard length, collected with the holotype on 22 May 1959. Twenty-five of these were measured to build the variation table.
- **Second series:** 61 specimens, 39.5–75.5 mm standard length, collected by A. Solórzano and Rafael Vanegas on 6 June 1964 at the same locality.
- **Deposition:** five paratypes were placed in the Laboratorio de Hidrobiología, Escuela Nacional de Ciencias Biológicas (Instituto Politécnico Nacional). Three each went to the Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan; the Field Museum (Chicago); Tulane University; and the British Museum (Natural History). The remainder were kept at the Laboratorio Central of the Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Biológico-Pesqueras.

**Diagnosis (as given by the authors).** Small fishes. Anal fin with 11–15 rays, usually 13. Lateral series of 38–42 scales, usually 40. First gill arch with 10–12 gill rakers, 9–10 on the lower limb. Postorbital distance contains the eye diameter about 2 times.

**Body form.** The body is slightly compressed, with a rounded back and belly and a ridge or hump in the postoccipital region. Predorsal distance is 1.7–2.1 times, and maximum height 4.4–5.3 times, into standard length.

**Fins and counts.**
- First dorsal fin: 3–6 soft spines, usually 4 (mode 4, range 3–6).
- Second dorsal fin: 1 spine and 8–11 rays, usually 10; depressed.
- Anal fin: 1 spine and 11–15 rays, usually 13; placed nearer the origin of the second dorsal than the midpoint between the two dorsals.
- Pectoral fins: usually 12 rays.
- Vertebrae: 37–38 in total (19–20 in the trunk, 17–18 in the caudal region).
- Scales: 15–19 predorsal (usually 17); 9–11 in a transverse series (usually 10); 7–9 interdorsal (usually 8).

**Head and mouth.** Head 2.9–4 times into standard length. Snout blunt and convex in profile. Mouth small, protractile, with thick lips. Both jaws are about equal. The lower jaw projects only slightly. Small, slightly inward-curved conical teeth are arranged in two rows.

**Colouration (preserved specimens).** The back is dark, especially on the head. The belly and sides are paler, and some individuals are yellowish. In mature fish, particularly females, the dark visceral cavity shows through the ventral wall. A thin dark lateral stripe runs along the flank from the pectoral origin to the caudal base.

**Type locality.** Laguna de Almoloya or Chignahuapan, Puebla, about 600 m northeast of the town of Chignahuapan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper gives little habitat detail beyond the spring-fed lake setting and its drainage link to the Necaxa reservoir system. No water chemistry, depth, substrate, diet, reproduction or population data are reported. The authors note that the genus *Poblana* is a group of atherinids restricted to Mexican inland waters, and that the new species is closely allied to the other members of that genus.

The authors also discuss how scale cover might be used to group the species. They note that *P. alchichica* sometimes shows complete scale cover and sometimes does not, so incomplete scale cover cannot serve as a diagnostic character. The paper therefore treats this grouping as having no taxonomic value. The text quotes [[Jose Alvarez]] (1950) to this effect.

## Key Findings

1. **New species confirmed.** *Poblana ferdebueni* differs from all known congeners in the combination of its meristic and morphometric characters, and is described as new to science.

2. **Distinguishing meristic counts.** Compared with the other *Poblana* species in Table II:
   - **Gill rakers (first arch, lower limb):** *P. ferdebueni* has 9–10 (usually 10). *P. letholepis* has 13–17, *P. alchichica* 13–17, and *P. hidalgoi* 15–17.
   - **Lateral scales:** *P. ferdebueni* has 38–42 (usually 40). *P. alchichica* has 48–57, *P. letholepis* 47–55, and *P. hidalgoi* 41–46.
   - **First dorsal spines:** *P. ferdebueni* has a mode of 4 (range 3–6). *P. letholepis* and *P. alchichica* are typically 4–6 (mode 5). *P. hidalgoi* has 4 as well, so this character links the new species with *P. hidalgoi*.
   - **Anal fin rays:** *P. ferdebueni* has 11–15 (usually 13). *P. alchichica* has 12–17 (mode 14), *P. letholepis* 13–16, and *P. hidalgoi* 15–20 (usually 18).

3. **Morphometric variation.** Measurements from 25 specimens (52.1–67.0 mm standard length, holotype included) are summarised as ranges, means and proportions in Table I. Ratios are expressed per thousand standard length or head length. The head-length ratios and body-depth ratios are the main morphometric support for the description.

4. **Relationships.** The authors judged that the species resembles *P. hidalgoi* in first dorsal spine count and *P. letholepis*/*P. alchichica* in other respects. They present no phylogenetic analysis.

5. **Scope and limitations.** The description is based on preserved specimens from one locality and two collecting dates. No ecological, genetic or distributional data are offered beyond the type locality. The authors acknowledge advice from C. L. Hubbs and R. R. Miller, and from J. Alvarez del Villar, who reviewed the manuscript.
