# Pahrump Killifish (*Empetrichthys latos latos*)

#ichthyology #desert-ecology #Goodeidae #endangered-species #spring-fish

## Executive Summary

The [[Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos latos* Miller) is an endangered desert spring fish originally endemic to three spring systems in [[Pahrump Valley]], southern [[Nye County]], [[Nevada]]. All native habitats were destroyed by groundwater pumping for irrigation, with the last natural population at [[Manse Spring]] extirpated in 1975. The species now persists only in three managed refugia: [[Corn Creek Springs]] (Clark County), [[Spring Mountain Ranch State Park]] (Clark County), and [[Shoshone Ponds]] (White Pine County). Taxonomically placed in family [[Goodeidae]] (Parenti 1981), this species is characterized by a slender body, broad mouth, weak mandible, absence of pelvic fins, and 31-32 lateral scales. Successful recovery requires continuous management to prevent introduction of competing fish species (especially [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Goldfish]]) and maintenance of spring water supplies.

## Background & Description

### Morphology and Taxonomy

*Empetrichthys latos latos* was originally described by Miller (1948) from specimens collected at [[Manse Ranch]]. The holotype and 143 paratypes (U.M.M.Z. No. 140489, 15-48 mm long) are maintained at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]]. An additional 34 paratypes (10-50 mm) were collected by R.R. Miller and Alex J. Calhoun on July 16, 1938, from the outlet of the main spring pool at Manse Ranch.

**Diagnostic characteristics:**
- Relatively slender body with gently sloping to convex predorsal profile
- Short and slender head
- Comparatively broad mouth
- Weak mandible with loosely connected premaxillary and mandibular bones
- 31-32 scales in lateral series
- 12-13 anal rays
- Narrow axial streak on sides
- **Absence of pelvic fins** (a characteristic shared with a few other isolated desert fishes)

The species differs from *[[Empetrichthys merriami]]* principally in mouth structure (wider mouth, weaker bones), body shape (less deep head, less thick body), and color pattern. Gilbert (1893) originally failed to recognize this species as distinct and included it in his description of *E. merriami*.

**Taxonomic history:**
- Originally placed in [[Cyprinodontidae]] (Miller 1948)
- Jordan and Evermann included genus in expanded family [[Poeciliidae]]
- Eigenmann (1920) placed *Empetrichthys* in distinct family [[Orestiidae]]
- Jordan, Evermann and Clark created new family [[Empetrichthyidae]]
- Myers returned genus to Cyprinodontidae
- Parenti (1981) placed genus into family **Goodeidae** (current classification)

**Taxonomic classification:**
- Phylum: [[Chordata]]
- Class: [[Osteichthys]]
- Order: [[Cyprinodontiformes]]
- Family: [[Goodeidae]]

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Historical Distribution and Habitat Loss

The [[Pahrump Killifish]] was historically endemic to three major spring systems in [[Pahrump Valley]], southern [[Nye County]], [[Nevada]]. Development of groundwater for irrigation beginning in the early 1900s led to progressive spring failure:

- **Mid-1950s:** Two of three springs ceased flowing; their endemic killifish populations became extinct
- **1975:** [[Manse Spring]] (the third and last natural habitat) ceased flowing

**Primary cause of extinction:** Excessive groundwater pumping for irrigation, leading to complete habitat destruction.

### Current Refugia

The species now exists only in three transplanted populations, all requiring active management:

**1. [[Corn Creek Springs]], [[Desert Game Range]], [[Clark County]]** (USDI-Fish and Wildlife Service land)
- **Establishment:** August 1971 (29 fish transplanted from Manse Spring)
- **System description:** 
  - Concrete trough ~100 m long from spring sources to first pond
  - Three interconnected ponds: upper pond (80 m × 25 m), middle pond (~8 m circumference), lower pond (~25 m circumference)
  - Vegetation: Sparse [[Myrophyllum]] sp. in trough; [[Typha]] colonizing ~50% of lower pond; [[Potamogeton]] in submerged areas
- **Water temperatures (12/21/83):**
  - Spring outflow: 25°C
  - Upper pond inflow: 25°C
  - Upper pond outflow: 19.4°C
  - Midstream: 19.4°C
  - Middle pond inflow: 18.9°C
  - Middle pond outflow: 15.6°C
  - Lower pond inflow: 13.9°C
- **Management concerns:** [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia*) caused severe population crashes on at least two occasions; continuous vigilance required to prevent reintroduction

**2. [[Spring Mountain Ranch State Park]], [[Clark County]]** (Nevada State Park System land)
- **Establishment:** June 9, 1983 (426 fish from Corn Creek)
- **System description:**
  - Irrigation impoundment of ~21 acre-feet
  - East bank: [[Typha]] along ~50% of 0.2-mile length
  - West bank: Heavy [[Typha]] colonization (~30 feet from shore)
  - Substrate: Rocky-sandy, ~70% covered by [[Chara]]
- **Management:** Annual drawdown (February-March) and burning of dry Typha; prevention of fish introductions
- **Reproduction:** Spawning observed late May to early June at water temperatures near 15°C (but with diel variation)
- **Concern:** Winter cooling renders killifish inactive for extended periods; extreme conditions could cause winter mortality

**3. [[Shoshone Ponds]], [[White Pine County]]** (USDI-Bureau of Land Management land)
- **Location:** ~50 km SE of Ely, Nevada (NE¼ of S2, T12N R57E)
- **Establishment history:**
  - March 9, 1972: 16 fish introduced to middle pond (from Corn Creek or Manse Spring?)
  - September 26, 1972: 8 fish transferred to south pond
  - Not observed after Spring 1974
  - August 31, 1976: 50 fish transferred from Corn Creek to Shoshone North Pond
  - Subsequently transferred to two other ponds
  - Currently maintains reproducing populations in 3 of 5 ponds
- **Shoshone North Pond characteristics (7 m × 9 m, depth 1.2 m):**
  - Dissolved oxygen: 9.6-12.0 ppm (June-July surface)
  - Temperature: 26.0-27.0°C (surface)
  - pH: 7.5-9.35
  - Hardness: 52-53
  - Alkalinity: 51-55
  - Turbidity: 3.4 JTU
  - Dissolved solids: 97.0 mg/L
  - Nitrate-N: 0.06 mg/L
  - Chloride: 4.2 mg/L
  - Calcium: 14.0 mg/L
  - Magnesium: 0.35 mg/L
  - Manganese: 1.5 mg/L
  - Potassium: 10.0 mg/L
  - Sodium: 7.0 mg/L
  - Sulfate: 70.0 mg/L
- **Management concern:** Artesian well supplying water must be maintained to ensure reliable water supply

### Environmental Tolerances

**Temperature tolerance (Selby 1982):**
- Critical thermal limits: <1.5°C to 40°C (acclimation-dependent)
- Cold-acclimated fish: CT maximum 30°C, CT minimum <1.5°C
- Warm-acclimated fish: CT maximum 40°C, CT minimum 6.9°C
- **No preferred temperature** and no apparent behavioral thermoregulation
- Natural temperature limits unlikely to be exceeded

**Oxygen tolerance (Selby 1982):**
- Behavioral stress not apparent until oxygen falls near 1 ppm at 22-25°C
- Distribution in [[Corn Creek Springs]] suggests slight preference for high dissolved oxygen
- At night, low oxygen in dense vegetation (<1 ppm) forces fish into open water
- At temperatures >20°C, species is primarily diurnal
- At colder temperatures, fish move about primarily at night when forced from vegetation by low oxygen

### Habitat Associations

All three refugia are bordered by natural desert vegetation and protected from excessive public disturbance. The species thrives in stable spring habitats with diverse plant and invertebrate communities, provided no competing fish species are present.

**Microhabitat preferences:**
- Fry prefer densely vegetated shallow water (observed at Spring Mountain Ranch with [[Typha]] and [[Chara]])
- Adults avoid areas of extremely low oxygen (<1.0 ppm), especially in dense vegetation at night
- Slight preference for waters with high dissolved oxygen concentration

## Key Findings

### Life History and Reproduction

**Spawning:**
- In constant-temperature natural springs, spawning probably occurs year-round (common pattern for desert spring fishes)
- Peak reproductive season at [[Manse Spring]]: February-April
- Spawning observed at [[Spring Mountain Ranch State Park]]: late May to early June at ~15°C (with diel variation)
- Laboratory spawning successful at 23°C on Living World Spawning Grass
- Eggs likely deposited on aquatic vegetation in nature (detailed observations lacking)

**Fecundity and development:**
- 11 female killifish produced average of 14 eggs each over 24-hour spawning period
- Females may not be ready to spawn again within one month of first spawning
- Females ≥45 mm TL are mature and capable of producing full complement of eggs; smaller females less frequently capable
- Eggs hatch in 7-9 days at 29°C
- Larvae: 6-11 mm TL at hatching
- Growth: Average ~20 mm TL in 112 days, with increasing size variation over time
- Females probably grow faster than males
- **No parental care** of eggs or fry

**Sex ratios:**
- Females outnumber males in every population
- Ratios vary: 1 male : 1.2 females (middle Corn Creek pond) to 1 : 7.9 (lower Corn Creek pond)

### Behavior and Activity

**Daily activity (Deacon and Minckley 1970):**
- Active during day with mid-day depression of activity
- More inactive at night
- Pattern similar to other desert fishes

**Temperature-dependent activity (Selby 1982):**
- At temperatures >20°C: primarily diurnal
- At colder temperatures: move about primarily at night, apparently forced from vegetation by lower oxygen concentrations

**Social behavior:** Not investigated.

**Migration:** Non-migratory (isolated in desert springs).

**Territoriality:** Not investigated.

### Population Biology and Threats

**Population maintenance:**
- Species maintains populations well in desert springs containing no other fish species
- Thrives in stable habitats with diverse plant and invertebrate communities

**Impacts of introduced fishes:**
- [[Goldfish]] introduction at [[Manse Spring]]: Rapid decline in killifish population (population later recovered and coexisted with goldfish)
- [[Mosquitofish]] re-establishment at [[Corn Creek]]: Similar population crash; recovery may not have occurred without management intervention
- **Conclusion:** Interaction with introduced fishes is a serious threat requiring continuous management vigilance

**Management requirements:**
- **[[Corn Creek Springs]]:** Control encroachment of emergent aquatic vegetation
- **[[Shoshone Ponds]]:** Maintain water system (artesian well)
- **[[Spring Mountain Ranch State Park]]:** Maintain inflowing water and dam
- **All locations:** Prevent introduction of non-native fishes; remove immediately if introduced

**Recovery potential:** Under appropriate and continuous management in undisturbed desert spring habitats, the species maintains excellent populations.

### Conservation Status

**Federal listing:** Endangered (Killifish, Pahrump)

**Reasons for endangered status:**
1. **Habitat destruction** (primary): Excessive groundwater pumping for irrigation in [[Pahrump Valley]]
2. **Predation/competition:** Introduced fishes ([[Mosquitofish]], [[Goldfish]])
3. **Inadequate regulatory mechanisms** (historical)

**Current threats:**
- Potential reintroduction of competing fish species at all refugia
- Water system failures (especially at Shoshone Ponds)
- Vegetation encroachment (Corn Creek)
- Extreme winter temperatures (Spring Mountain Ranch)

**Recovery plan:** USDI Fish and Wildlife Service Recovery Plan published 1980.
