# Demographics and Monitoring of Repatriated Razorback Sucker in Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species #population-dynamics

## Executive Summary

This five-year final report (2015–2019) documents the demographics and monitoring of repatriated [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]], part of the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]]. The wild razorback sucker population has functionally disappeared, declining from 60,000–75,000 individuals in the 1980s to fewer than 50 by 2010. A repatriation program initiated in the early 1990s now sustains a population of several thousand stocked fish. Monitoring combined traditional trammel netting with remote [[Passive Integrated Transponder]] (PIT) scanning technology, which increased contact rates by at least one order of magnitude. From October 2014 to September 2019, routine monitoring captured 84 PIT-tagged repatriates (68 females, 15 males, 1 juvenile) with mean total length at stocking of 426 mm and at capture of 595 mm. Remote PIT scanning accumulated 201,382 scan hours across all zones, recording 627,411 contacts representing 6,304 unique PIT tags. The repatriate population estimate for 2018 was 3,649 individuals (95% CI: 3,552–3,745) based on remote scanning, while routine monitoring estimated 994 fish (95% CI: 602–1,639), suggesting temporal constraints of March-only sampling may no longer capture the entire population. Adult survival exceeded 95% annually in both [[River Zone]] and [[Basin Zone]] subpopulations. Post-stocking analysis confirmed that larger fish (≥400 mm TL) have substantially higher recruitment rates, with the relationship between size at release and survival dominating all other factors.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir on the [[Lower Colorado River]] created by [[Davis Dam]], located between Arizona and Nevada. The lake was divided into four zones for this study: River (upstream, riverine habitat above [[Willow Beach]]), Liberty, Basin (main reservoir including [[Carp Cove]], [[Yuma Cove]], [[Cottonwood Cove]]), and Katherine (downstream). The razorback sucker is an endangered "big river" catostomid endemic to the Colorado River basin, characterized by a distinctive dorsal hump or "razor back" and reaching lengths exceeding 700 mm TL.

The repatriation program collects wild larvae, rears them in protective facilities (including [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], [[Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility]], [[Lake Mead Fish Hatchery]], and lakeside backwater ponds), and repatriates them to the reservoir after reaching a minimum size. Initial minimum size was 300 mm TL, later increased to 500 mm based on survival studies. Fish are marked with 134.2 kHz PIT tags for individual identification. In November 2016, approximately 30,000 razorback suckers at Willow Beach NFH were lost to an outbreak of *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* ("ich"), dramatically reducing stocking numbers.

Monitoring methods included: (1) **Routine monitoring** via trammel netting (91.4 m long × 1.8 m high, 3.8 cm stretch mesh) during bi-annual multi-agency surveys in November/December and March at [[Carp Cove]]; (2) **Remote monitoring** using shore-based and submersible PIT scanners deployed continuously or during monthly sampling trips. Shore-based units consisted of PVC-framed antennas connected to solar-powered loggers. Submersible units (0.8 × 0.8 m or 1.2 × 0.8 m frames) were powered by lithium-ion batteries. Five fixed sites were established in River zone: [[Gio's Point]], [[Black Bar]], [[Ringbolt Rapids]], [[Boy Scout Canyon]], and [[Sauna Cave]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Mohave]] provides critical habitat for the last remaining population of razorback sucker in this reach, though the wild population has been functionally extirpated. The reservoir's ecology includes both lentic (Basin) and lotic (River) habitats. Spawning aggregations occur primarily at [[Black Bar]] and [[Boy Scout Cove]] in River zone during November through April, with peak activity January through March. Water depth at PIT scanner deployments varied, with units targeting shallow gravel bars, cobble substrates, and cattail/bulrush stands where razorback suckers aggregate.

The non-native fish community poses ongoing predation pressure, particularly on smaller stocked fish. This predation risk drives the emphasis on stocking larger individuals (≥400 mm TL). Temperature, flow regime, and habitat quality vary between zones, influencing fish distribution and movement patterns. Basin zone historically supported the highest densities during March spawning surveys, while River zone has emerged as a critical spawning area accessible only through remote PIT scanning.

Post-stocking dispersal patterns revealed that approximately two-thirds of fish released in River or Basin remain in their release zone, but roughly one-third move between these zones. Fish released in Liberty zone dispersed upstream to River or downstream to Basin in nearly equal proportions, suggesting lower habitat quality or lack of established aggregations in Liberty. All fish released in Katherine zone moved upstream.

## Key Findings

**Population Status and Trends:**
- The repatriated population in 2018 was estimated at 3,649 fish (95% CI: 3,552–3,745) based on remote PIT scanning, with River subpopulation at 2,120 (2,012–2,227) and Basin at 1,963 (1,904–2,021)
- Routine monitoring estimate for 2018 was 994 fish (602–1,639), down from 2,230 (922–5,963) in 2014, suggesting March-only sampling may no longer adequately sample the population
- Lake-wide repatriate estimates peaked in 2016 at 3,871 fish, declining slightly thereafter
- Wild razorback sucker population estimated at only 9 fish (5–19) in 2018; too few encountered during routine monitoring (2015–2019) to estimate abundance
- Unknown origin fish (tagged at capture) increased from 7 in 2013 to 32 in 2018

**Routine Monitoring Results (2015–2019):**
- 84 PIT-tagged repatriates captured (68 females, 15 males, 1 juvenile)
- Mean TL at stocking: 426 mm (range: 265–531 mm); mean TL at capture: 595 mm (range: 342–741 mm)
- Time at large ranged from 10 days to 22 years (mean: 6 years)
- Mean growth rate: 6 mm TL/month for females, 4 mm TL/month for males
- Most captured fish were stocked in Basin (n=59), with 36 reared in lakeside backwaters and 44 in off-site facilities
- Year classes represented: 2001–2016 (2004 not represented)

**Remote PIT Scanning (2015–2019):**
- Total deployment: 201,382 scan hours (54,384 shore-based, 146,998 submersible)
- 627,411 total contacts representing 6,304 unique PIT tags
- 5,851 razorback suckers identified (5,774 repatriates, 13 wild, 64 unknown origin)
- 106 [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) contacts (all repatriates)
- River zone: 44,653 scan hours, 3,398 unique razorback suckers (all repatriates except 10 wild, 14 unknown)
- Basin zone: 125,386 scan hours, 3,462 unique razorback suckers (3,396 repatriates, 5 wild, 61 unknown)
- Liberty zone: 25,892 scan hours, 193 razorback suckers (190 repatriates, 3 unknown)
- Katherine zone: 5,451 scan hours, 54 razorback suckers (all repatriates)

**Spawning Aggregations:**
- [[Black Bar]] in River zone showed highest contact rates November through April, confirming it as primary spawning site
- [[Boy Scout Cove]] identified as second-largest spawning aggregation site
- After spawning season, contacts declined at these sites with no evident increase at other fixed sites
- Basin zone shore-based scanners at [[Tequila Cove]], [[Yuma Cove]], and [[Half-Way Wash]] documented continuous spawning activity

**Adult Survival:**
- Annual survival estimates exceeded 95% for both River and Basin subpopulations (all but one estimate >0.95)
- Basin 2016–2017 survival: 0.917 (0.693–0.982)
- Survival substantially higher than previously reported based on capture data alone
- Temporary emigration rates: River 5.1–12.3%, Basin 0.8–6.9%
- Immigration (return) rates: River 21.6–24.0%, Basin 45.4–60.0%
- Contact probabilities ranged from 0.0012 (May 2019, Basin) to 0.508 (January 2013, Basin)
- All years had at least one occasion with contact probability >0.250

**Post-Stocking Displacement:**
- 53,315 hatchery-reared razorback suckers stocked October 2008–September 2013
- 1,997 unique fish with ≥10 contacts analyzed: 69.5% contacted in one zone, 28.8% in two zones, 1.8% in three zones
- **River releases:** 64.3% remained in River, 33.1% moved to Basin
- **Basin releases:** 69.5% remained in Basin, 29.5% moved to River
- **Liberty releases:** dispersed upstream to River (30.1%) or downstream to Basin (30.6%) in nearly equal proportions; only 0.5% remained exclusively in Liberty
- **Katherine releases:** all moved upstream (75% to Basin, 13.9% to both River and Basin)
- Fish moving between River and Basin spent substantial time in both zones (roughly equal contact proportions)

**Repatriate Recruitment:**
- Clear relationship between size at release and recruitment: cohorts released at longer TL had higher contact rates across all years
- Few to no contacts for cohorts released <400 mm TL
- Highest recruitment rate: 0.80 (20 of 25 fish) for cohort released October 2010 from lakeside backwater at 480 mm TL
- No significant difference in recruitment between pond-reared and raceway-reared fish when controlling for size
- Release year (SY) accounted for substantial variation in cohort availability beyond size effects
- Cohorts released in River 2013–2014 included no large fish (>450 mm TL), resulting in few fish contributing to subpopulation
- Basin releases from raceways limited to <430 mm TL across all years, while pond releases ranged 340–500 mm TL

**Management Implications:**
- Size at stocking remains the dominant factor influencing post-stocking survival and recruitment
- Minimum stocking size of 500 mm TL recommended, though logistical constraints persist
- Variation in recruitment among lakeside backwater cohorts suggests condition at release may be important
- Remote PIT scanning essential for monitoring riverine populations inaccessible to traditional methods
- Continued deployment in River and Basin zones recommended; Liberty zone shows limited resident population
- March-only routine monitoring may need temporal expansion to adequately census Basin population
- Population modeling should incorporate high adult survival (>95%) and movement between zones
- Catastrophic disease events (e.g., 2016 ich outbreak) pose significant risk to program continuity
