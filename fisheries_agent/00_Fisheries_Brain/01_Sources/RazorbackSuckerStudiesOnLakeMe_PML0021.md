# Lake Mead Razorback Sucker Studies: 2005-2006 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This August 2006 annual report from [[BIO-WEST Inc]] covers the tenth year (July 2005 to June 2006) of a study of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mead]], funded by the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]] and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. Work included trammel netting, larval light-trapping, sonic telemetry, fin-ray aging, population estimation and an evaluation of potential repatriation sites. The main result is the first documented shift in spawning location of the [[Las Vegas Bay]] population, from [[Blackbird Point]] to the western shoreline, driven by lake-level decline and sedimentation. Spawning was also documented at a new [[Fish Island]] area near the [[Muddy River]] and [[Virgin River]] inflows. The report recommends [[Driftwood Cove]] as the best candidate for future stocking and continued monitoring in the 2006-2007 season.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endemic fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]] and is listed as endangered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Its decline is attributed to mainstem dam construction, which replaced warm riverine habitat with cool tailwaters and reservoirs, and to competition and predation by nonnative fishes. Reservoir populations generally consisted of adults that recruited in the first years after impoundment, and then disappeared roughly 40 to 50 years after reservoir creation.

[[Lake Mead]] was formed in 1935 when [[Hoover Dam]] closed. Razorback suckers were relatively common through the 1950s and 1960s, but declined in the 1970s. No fish were collected from 1980 to 1989. In 1990 anglers reported fish in [[Las Vegas Bay]] and [[Echo Bay]], and the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW) began limited sampling. From 1990 to 1996, 61 wild razorback suckers were collected (34 from Blackbird Point and 27 from Echo Bay). The study began in October 1996 with objectives to estimate population size, determine habitat use and life history, and document known spawning sites. Objectives were later expanded to include larval light-trapping outside established areas, juvenile sampling, and evaluation of stocking options (added in 2005).

The comparison with [[Lake Mohave]] is central: its population fell from about 75,000 in the 1980s to under 3,000 by 2001, and is now sustained mainly by stocking of captive-reared fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recruitment drivers.** The authors propose that recruitment depends on lake-level fluctuations that promote vegetation growth, followed by inundation. Inundated vegetation and turbidity provide cover that lets young fish avoid nonnative predators. Low annual fluctuations combined with large multi-year changes are suggested as the key management condition. Lake Mohave, which lacks documented natural recruitment and has large annual fluctuations, is cited as a contrast.

**Lake-level effects on spawning.** Declining lake levels have repeatedly shifted spawning sites:
- At [[Echo Bay]], spawning moved down the wash as the lake fell. The current site is on the northern shoreline, west of the marina.
- At Blackbird Point, the spawning depth moved from about 80 ft (late 1990s) to about 20 ft (2003), and the area was desiccated by late 2004. A 2005 rise of more than 20 ft allowed access again.
- The [[Colorado River]] inflow area was not used for spawning in 2002 or 2003 (no larvae captured).

**Cove and bay characteristics.** Driftwood Cove, about 109.4 acres at roughly 1,124 ft amsl, was slightly connected to the river (channel under 0.5 m wide and 15 cm deep) at 1,135 ft. Grand Wash Bay, at about 1,231 acres, was judged too large to manage. Driftwood Cove water quality was satisfactory in February 2006, with dissolved oxygen of 10 mg/L at the surface and 8.4 mg/L near the bottom and a Secchi depth of 1 m. In May 2006 the cove was at about 1,135 ft, dissolved oxygen was 9.6 to 2.0 mg/L, and the pH ranged from 7.6 to 11.1.

## Key Findings

**Population and capture results (2005-2006)**
- 47 adult razorback suckers captured by trammel netting over 133 net nights: 13 from Las Vegas Bay (44 net nights), 31 from Echo Bay (49 net nights), and 3 from the Muddy/Virgin inflow area (40 net nights).
- Three subadult fish (over 300 mm TL, sexually immature) were captured at Las Vegas Bay.
- Of the 47 fish, 34 were recaptures (the report cites a similar recapture rate in previous years).
- Trammel netting CPUE: Las Vegas Bay 0.30 fish/net night, Echo Bay 0.59, Muddy/Virgin inflow 0.08.
- Captures were at depths of 4 to 22 ft, with a mean depth of 14 ft.
- Netting was not conducted from June to October because warm temperatures stress fish.

**Spawning and larvae**
- Las Vegas Bay: the first documented shift in spawning location, to the western shoreline about 500 m south of the wash inflow. Evidence included adult captures, a visual observation on March 22, 2006, and larval concentrations.
- Las Vegas Bay larval totals were 257 in 2006 (CPM 0.12), compared with 2005 (CPM 0.13), 2004 (CPM 0.003) and 2003 (CPM 0.06).
- Echo Bay: 254 larvae in 2006 (CPM 0.29) compared with 1,330 in 2005 (CPM 1.36), 207 in 2004 (CPM 0.15) and 552 in 2003 (CPM 0.43). Echo Bay spawning was confirmed by underwater video on February 6-10, 2006, at the same northern-shore site used in 2004 and 2005.
- Muddy/Virgin inflow: five larvae were captured in April 2006 (CPM 0.003). The area is a new spawning site on the Fish Island shoreline, confirmed in 2005 and 2006.

**Telemetry**
- Ten Floyd Lamb State Park fish were tagged on November 29-30, 2005 (48-month sonic tags): 5 in Las Vegas Bay, 2 in Echo Bay, and 3 at Fish Island. Nine of the ten were contacted regularly. One Echo Bay fish (tag 556) was lost, probably to tag failure.
- Fish 222 (tagged in 2004) was contacted over 40 times, moved about 29 km (about 1 ft contact depth range from 4 to 78 ft), and was the first fish to link Echo Bay and the Overton Arm.
- Sonic-telemetered fish depths during spawning averaged about 20 ft (range under 2 ft to over 78 ft). Echo Bay fish occupied significantly deeper water (ANOVA, p<0.001). No depth difference was found between sexes.

**Growth**
- Mean annual growth of 26 recaptured fish was 10.6 mm (9.5 mm in 2004-2005). Las Vegas Bay fish averaged 21.6 mm (stocked 41.3 mm, wild 8.4 mm) and Echo Bay fish averaged 2.6 mm (stocked 3.2 mm, wild 10.9 mm).
- These rates are substantially higher than those of other populations, including Lake Mohave (under 2.0 mm/yr) and the Green River.

**Aging and recruitment**
- Fin rays were sectioned from 13 fish. Ages ranged from 4 to 33 years (the 33-year fish was a ripe female from the Fish Island spawning area).
- The total of aged fish is now 91 (78 earlier plus 13 new). All were spawned between 1966 and 2002.
- Recruitment occurred nearly every year, with the strongest cohorts from 1978-1989 and 1997-1999. Five Las Vegas Bay fish were spawned in 2000-2002, the first aged fish spawned after 1999.
- Sexual maturity was confirmed at about 6 years of age.

**Population estimates (2004-2006)**
- Echo Bay: 38 to 74 fish (Model Mo, Chao Mh, model selection, and jackknife estimators). Capture probabilities were below 3%.
- Las Vegas Bay: 40 to 267 fish, with the upper estimate having a very wide confidence interval.
- The authors recommend discontinuing population estimate reporting unless collaborators find it useful, given the closed-model assumption violations.

**Repatriation opportunities**
- Two isolated bays near the Colorado River were evaluated: Driftwood Cove and Grand Wash Bay. Consensus favored Driftwood Cove as more manageable.
- Interagency gill netting at Driftwood Cove (May 8-9, 2006) caught 51 fish in one net night, including carp, striped bass, yellow bullhead and blue tilapia (the species list is given in the report's figure 19). No razorback suckers were collected.
- Projected lake levels indicate that the cove will likely remain disconnected through November 2007. Risks include reconnection before stocked fish imprint and desiccation if levels continue to fall.

**Recommendations for 2006-2007**
- Continue sonic tracking of the active tagged fish, with a focus on the Las Vegas Bay shift, Fish Island habitat use and whether Fish Island is an independent population.
- Continue larval sampling, trammel netting (reduced to the spawning season), and fin-ray aging with PIT tagging.
- Continue monitoring Driftwood Cove and develop a fish salvage plan. Access by helicopter is suggested for piscicide treatment.
- Prepare a companion monitoring plan and a summary of the long-term database.
