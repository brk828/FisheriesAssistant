# The Global Historical Climatology Network: Long-Term Monthly Temperature, Precipitation, Sea Level Pressure, and Station Pressure Data

#climatology #meteorology #data-archive

## Executive Summary

This document describes the Global Historical Climatology Network (GHCN) Version 1.0, a comprehensive global baseline dataset of monthly mean temperature, precipitation, sea level pressure, and station pressure compiled by Oak Ridge National Laboratory and the National Climatic Data Center. Published in July 1992, the GHCN consolidates numerous pre-existing national, regional, and global-scale meteorological datasets into a single database covering approximately 50-300 years of climate records from thousands of worldwide stations. The project aims to provide improved data for assessing global climate change, particularly potential greenhouse gas-induced modifications to climate patterns.

## Background & Description

The GHCN project was initiated by the [[Carbon Dioxide Information Analysis Center]] (CDIAC) and [[National Climatic Data Center]] (NCDC) in response to the proliferation of disparate global climate datasets and the need for standardized, quality-controlled meteorological data. The database consolidates data from the [[World Weather Records]] (WWR) series, [[Monthly Climatic Data for the World]] (MCDW), the [[World Monthly Surface Station Climatology]] (WMSSC), and numerous national and regional datasets, including recently available data from bilateral exchanges with the USSR and China.

The compilation process involved four major steps: (1) data set acquisition from various sources, (2) revision and updating of station inventory parameters using [[World Meteorological Organization]] (WMO) information, (3) merging datasets and removing duplicate stations (averaging two duplicates per unique station for temperature/precipitation), and (4) two-part quality control analysis including threshold checks against world record values and visual inspection of time series plots.

Stations were required to have a minimum of 10 years of data for at least one of the four variables to be included in the database.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

This is a climatological dataset focused on atmospheric conditions rather than aquatic ecosystems. The data supports research on greenhouse gas-induced climate change and long-term climate trends. The network's spatial distribution reflects historical patterns of human settlement and development, with high station density in industrialized regions ([[North America]], [[Europe]], [[Asia]]) and significant data gaps in remote areas (northern Canada, Amazon basin, Sahara desert, Arabian peninsula, northern Asia, Tibetan plateau, Antarctica).

The temporal evolution of the station network shows accelerated growth since the late 19th century due to widespread introduction of reliable thermometers and increased habitation of previously less-populated areas. Sharp increases in station numbers correspond to inclusion of specific WWR publication periods (1951, 1961, 1981).

## Key Findings

**Temperature Data:**
- 6,039 stations worldwide with mean monthly temperature data (tenths of degrees Celsius)
- 61% of stations have records <50 years; 10% exceed 100 years
- Longest record: 290 years (1701-1990, Berlin-Tempelhof, Germany)
- 90% of records end in the 1980s; no data available after 1990
- 77% of stations missing <10% of data
- Highest station density: central North America, central Europe
- Major data gaps: northern North America, Amazon basin, Sahara, Arabian peninsula, northern Asia, Tibetan plateau, East Indies, western Australia, Antarctica

**Precipitation Data:**
- 7,533 stations with total monthly precipitation data (tenths of millimeters)
- 55% of stations have records >50 years; 13% exceed 100 years
- Longest record: 291 years (1697-1987, Kew, United Kingdom)
- 76% of records end in the 1980s
- 72% of stations missing <10% of data
- Highest density: central North America, central Europe, sub-Saharan Africa, eastern Australia

**Sea Level and Station Pressure:**
- Station numbers roughly equivalent to WMSSC database
- Average of one duplicate per unique station

**Database Improvements:**
- 80% more temperature stations than WMSSC
- 100% more precipitation stations than WMSSC
- Many stations have longer periods of record than WMSSC counterparts
- Quality control included threshold checks and visual inspection
- Future goals include developing datasets with more uniformly distributed long-term records
