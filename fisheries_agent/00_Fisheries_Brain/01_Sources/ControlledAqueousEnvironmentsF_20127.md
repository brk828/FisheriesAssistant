# Controlled Aqueous Environments for Bioassay

#ichthyology #marine-biology #toxicology #experimental-methods

## Executive Summary

This technical paper describes an automated apparatus designed at the [[Water Pollution Research Laboratory]] in Stevenage for conducting toxicity bioassays on fish. The system provides six independent aquaria with precise control over temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen concentration, and poison concentration in continuously renewed water. The apparatus uses borehole water from chalk (total hardness ~320 ppm) and employs a multi-stage treatment process including preliminary neutralization to remove carbonates, oxygen control through aeration/deaeration columns, automated chemical dosing, and temperature regulation. Performance testing over four days demonstrated stable control of all parameters with minimal manual intervention.

## Background & Description

The apparatus was constructed to study toxic effects of dissolved substances on fish under controlled environmental conditions. The source water from a chalk borehole contains approximately 320 ppm total hardness (270 ppm temporary hardness), which required preliminary treatment to remove interfering carbonates. The carbonate buffering effect complicated pH adjustment, and carbon dioxide released during acidification is both directly toxic to fish and affects the toxicity of other substances.

The system consists of several integrated components:

**Preliminary Neutralization**: Duplicate 3,640-liter polythene-lined steel tanks treat water in batches of 3,200 liters. Hydrochloric acid (approximately 1.0 M) is added automatically to neutralize carbonates and achieve pH 5. An antimony electrode and calomel reference electrode monitor pH, with a recorder-controller switching acid dosing on/off. The system uses two dosing rates: ~800 ml/min during tank filling and ~50 ml/min when approaching completion to prevent overdosing. A 24-hour time clock alternates between the two units every 12 hours.

**Oxygen Control**: Neutralized water flows to two 6-foot tall, 6-inch diameter Pyrex glass aeration columns. One column aerates water with compressed air to oxygen saturation; the other strips oxygen to ~0.5 ppm using pure nitrogen from cylinders through an electromagnetic gas-pressure regulator.

**Chemical Addition and pH Adjustment**: Each of six independent units receives oxygenated water mixed with measured doses of acid/alkali and test poison. Glass aspirators with electromagnetic valves deliver solutions to constant-head vessels controlled by proximity switches. Burettes discharge precise doses at one-minute intervals via time-clock-controlled inlet and outlet valves.

**Temperature Control and Mixing**: Oxygenated and deoxygenated water streams are blended in proportions indicated by rotameters. The mixture passes through polythene bottles fitted with two thermostats and immersion heaters, with one thermostat serving as a safety device. Water flows at approximately 0.7 liters per minute to each aquarium.

**Aquaria Design**: [[Perspex]] aquaria are totally enclosed and completely filled with water, eliminating air-water surfaces where oxygen transfer could occur. Fish are introduced through a hole in a rubber diaphragm normally closed by a bung.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The system addresses critical challenges in aquatic toxicology research. Carbon dioxide toxicity and its interaction with other substances represents a significant confounding factor in bioassay studies. The removal of carbonates and CO₂ through preliminary neutralization allows for more accurate assessment of test substance toxicity independent of pH buffering effects.

The completely enclosed aquarium design prevents atmospheric oxygen exchange, enabling precise control of dissolved oxygen concentrations. This is essential for studying how hypoxic conditions modify toxicity of pollutants, a common scenario in polluted aquatic environments.

Biological growth and calcium carbonate deposits on rotameters affect flow readings over time, requiring periodic verification through direct measurement of dissolved oxygen. Changes in fish population density within aquaria also affect oxygen consumption, necessitating flow adjustments during experiments.

## Key Findings

**Performance Stability**: Three aquaria operated without fish for four days with no adjustments after initial setup demonstrated excellent stability:

- **pH control**: Aquarium I ranged 7.64-7.96 (mean 7.71, SD 0.20); Aquarium II ranged 8.10-8.60 (mean 8.24, SD 0.26); Aquarium III ranged 8.76-9.18 (mean 8.92, SD 0.20)
- **Temperature control**: Aquarium I mean 20.3°C (SD 0.07); Aquarium II mean 16.52°C (SD 0.08); Aquarium III mean 20.16°C (SD 0.09)
- **Dissolved oxygen**: Aquarium I mean 3.18 ppm (SD 0.20); Aquarium II mean 4.94 ppm (SD 0.26); Aquarium III mean 2.46 ppm (SD 0.20)
- **Ammonia concentration** (test poison): Aquarium I mean 33.0 ppm N (SD 0.8); Aquarium II mean 9.3 ppm N (SD 0.2); Aquarium III 0.0 ppm

**System Capabilities**: The apparatus provides independent control of six aquaria with different combinations of environmental parameters. Burette readings reliably indicate poison concentrations when analytical verification is difficult or time-consuming. The system requires minimal manual attention once properly adjusted.

**Operational Considerations**: Rotameter readings serve as guides but require verification through direct measurement due to biological growth and calcium carbonate accumulation. Fish population changes affect dissolved oxygen levels, requiring flow rate adjustments during tests. Daily maintenance includes cleaning the antimony electrode with emery and restandardizing the pH circuit against buffer solutions.

**Future Modifications**: Additional test aquaria are being assembled with modifications to increase treated water yield without expanding tank capacity. A new aerating, mixing, and neutralizing chamber will be installed at the inlet to large treatment tanks rather than in the recirculation system. Reliable solenoid-operated valves will replace home-made valves for controlling tank filling and water flow to aeration/deaeration columns.
