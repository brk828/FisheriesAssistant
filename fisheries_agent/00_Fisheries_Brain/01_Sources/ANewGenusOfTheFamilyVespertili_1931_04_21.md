## Executive Summary

This 1931 paper by R.A. Stirton describes **[[Simonycteris stocki]]**, a new genus and species of fossil bat (Order Chiroptera, Family [[Vespertilionidae]]) recovered from the San Pedro Pliocene beds of Arizona. The type specimen — a partial palate and rostrum (Calif. Inst. Tech. Mus. Pal. No. 394) — was collected in 1930 by a field party under Dr. Chester Stock, in whose honor the species is named. At the time of publication, only three distinct Chiroptera species had been described from the North American Tertiary, making this specimen a significant addition to the fossil bat record. Comparative analysis places *Simonycteris* as morphologically closest to the extant genus **[[Eptesicus]]**, though its distinct snub-nosed facial profile and short rostrum distinguish it as a separate genus.

## Background & Description

- **Type material:** Palate and rostrum extending from the optic foramina to the anterior points of the premaxillae.
- **Dental formula:** I² C¹ P¹ M² (per side, upper dentition).
- **Incisors:** Two well-developed upper incisors, with I² larger than I¹; I² separated from the canine by a space equal to the transverse diameter of the root of I²; incisors arranged nearly in a straight line, with the outer pair slightly posterior.
- **Canine:** Well developed.
- **Premolar (P⁴):** Resembles that of *Eptesicus* but differs in having a more sinuous posterior border and a rounded (rather than angulate) posterior inner heel.
- **Molars (M¹, M²):** Distinct, well-developed W-pattern cusp arrangement; tritubercular (distinguishing it from the quadritubercular molars of [[Phyllostomidae]]); slight tendency toward hypocone development on M¹; commissure connecting paracone to protocone less trenchant than in *Eptesicus*. M¹ is longer anteroposteriorly, while M² is wider transversely.
- **M³:** Small, structured similarly to that of *Eptesicus*.
- **Cranial features:** Premaxillae fused with maxillae; rostrum notably short; nasals slightly elevated; frontal region rises abruptly above the rostrum, producing a distinctive "snub-nosed" appearance; orbits rounded anteriorly; palate emarginate anteriorly and basined posteriorly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Depositional context:** Recovered from late Pliocene continental sediments of the San Pedro Valley, Arizona (Curtis Ranch locality, Benson Quadrangle), approximately 2–3½ miles northeast of the Curtis Ranch House — a terrestrial fossil assemblage rather than an aquatic one.
- **Inferred habits:** As a member of [[Vespertilionidae]], *Simonycteris stocki* was almost certainly an insectivorous, likely aerially foraging bat, consistent with the ecology of related genera such as *Eptesicus* and *Chalinolobus*.
- **Faunal context:** This discovery contributed meaningfully to the very sparse North American Tertiary Chiroptera record, which at the time comprised only three previously described species (e.g., *Zanycteris paleocenus*, *Pipistrellus anemophilus*, *Mystipterus vespertilio*).

## Key Findings

- Establishment of **[[Simonycteris]]** as a new genus and **Simonycteris stocki** as a new species within [[Vespertilionidae]], based on a fossil palate and rostrum.
- The genus is distinguished from **Rhinopomidae**, **Emballonuridae**, **Noctilionidae**, **Nycteridae**, **Hipposideridae**, and **Megadermidae** by a more acute protocone on M¹ and M² that lacks a connecting commissure to the posterior cingulum, as well as differences in premaxillary construction and premolar/incisor counts.
- Distinguished from **Phyllostomidae** by its tritubercular (rather than quadritubercular) molar cusp pattern.
- Compared directly against **[[Chalinolobus]]** (specifically *Chalinolobus gouldi gouldi*), **[[Eptesicus]]** (*Eptesicus fuscus fuscus*), **[[Glauconycteris]]** (*Glauconycteris argentata*), and **[[Nycteris]]** (*Nycteris borealis borealis*).
- *Chalinolobus* differs from *Simonycteris* via smaller overall size, presence of P³, a less abrupt facial profile, and a more widely emarginate anterior palate.
- *Glauconycteris* shares the absence of P² with *Simonycteris* but is otherwise considered a more distant relative than *Chalinolobus*.
- Overall dental and cranial architecture most closely resembles **Eptesicus**, but the markedly abrupt facial angle and short rostrum justify separate generic status for *Simonycteris*.
- The type specimen is housed at the California Institute of Technology Museum of Paleontology (Cal. Inst. Tech. Mus. Pal., No. 394).
