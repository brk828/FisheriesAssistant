# Movement and Angler Harvest of Fishes in the Des Moines River, Boone County, Iowa

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Ictaluridae #Percidae #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This study, conducted by the [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]] from 1955–1963, summarizes tagging and recapture data for multiple fish species in the [[Des Moines River]], Boone County, Iowa. The primary goals were to estimate minimum angler harvest rates and characterize movement patterns for [[Channel Catfish]], [[Walleye]], [[Flathead Catfish]], and [[Smallmouth Bass]]. Metal strap tags were affixed to opercles (catfish) or jaws (walleye, smallmouth bass). Key findings show a minimum angler harvest of 4.6% for channel catfish and 12.0% for walleye, with very low reported harvest for flathead catfish and smallmouth bass. Channel catfish exhibited the greatest overall movement, predominantly downstream, while smallmouth bass were largely sedentary except for one notable long-distance outlier. The authors caution that both harvest and movement estimates are subject to significant methodological biases, including non-uniform recapture effort, lack of publicity for later tagging cohorts, and the presence of lowhead dams restricting upstream movement.

## Background & Description

This paper is primarily a population/movement ecology study rather than a morphological description; size/age data are limited to tagging thresholds:

- **[[Channel Catfish]]**: Tagging cohort included fish ≥9 inches at time of tagging (used to calculate a refined harvest estimate distinct from the "all sizes" estimate).
- **[[Walleye]]**, **[[Smallmouth Bass]]**: No specific length data reported beyond being of "catchable" size; nearly all were captured via electric shocking gear rather than hoop nets, suggesting habitat/behavior differences from catfish.
- **Tagging method differences**: Metal strap tags were placed on the **opercle** of [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Flathead Catfish]], and on the **jaw** of [[Walleye]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]] (a few smallmouth bass were opercle-tagged experimentally, per J. Reynolds, but sample size was too small for comparison).
- A streamer-type tag applied behind the dorsal spine of catfish (tested by Muncy, 1957) was abandoned due to tag loss (no returns beyond 20 days post-tagging), indicating this attachment method is unsuitable for [[Ictaluridae]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: All species inhabit the main channel of the [[Des Moines River]], a warmwater riverine system in Boone County, Iowa, characterized by the presence of **two lowhead dams** that impede upstream fish passage.
- **Capture method reflects habitat use**: [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Flathead Catfish]] were most effectively captured with **hoop nets**, suggesting benthic/structure-oriented habitat use, while [[Walleye]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]] were captured almost exclusively via **electric shocking**, suggesting more open-water or shoreline habitat associations.
- **Relative abundance in angler catch** (per [[Schmulbach 1959]] creel census, 1957–1958):
  - Channel catfish: 23.0%–46.6% of total angler catch (0.05–0.20 fish/man-hour)
  - Walleye: 3.7%–16.5% of total angler catch (0.01–0.05 fish/man-hour)
- **Movement ecology**: Species-specific mobility patterns suggest differing niche breadth — [[Channel Catfish]] show river-wide dispersal capacity (downstream-biased), whereas [[Smallmouth Bass]] display strong site fidelity/"homing" behavior, consistent with findings by [[Reynolds 1963]] and [[Brown 1960]].
- **Dam effects**: The presence of lowhead dams likely constrains upstream movement data, introducing an artificial ceiling on detected upstream dispersal, particularly relevant to interpreting the near-absence of long-distance upstream recaptures.

## Key Findings

### Tagging Summary (1955–1962)
| Species | Number Tagged |
|---|---|
| [[Channel Catfish]] | 3,339 |
| [[Walleye]] | 184 |
| [[Flathead Catfish]] | 194 |
| [[Smallmouth Bass]] | 141 |
| [[Carp]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[White Crappie]] | Insignificant numbers; no returns |

### Angler Harvest Estimates (Minimum, based on tag returns)
- **Channel Catfish**: Overall = **4.6%**; for fish ≥9 in at tagging = **6.6%**. Comparable to Muncy's (1957) earlier estimates of 8% and 4.6%. No fish recovered after 4 years post-tagging.
  - Comparative literature: 20% harvest reported on Colorado River ([[McCammon 1956]]); 3% in Oklahoma ([[Houser 1953]]).
- **Walleye**: Overall = **12.0%**, consistent with [[Eschmeyer & Crowe 1955]] Michigan study (12.2%, n=11,354). Much lower than Spirit Lake, Iowa estimates of 44.8% and 26.5% ([[Rose 1959]]).
- **Flathead Catfish**: Only 2 of 194 tagged fish reported caught by anglers.
- **Smallmouth Bass**: Only 4 of 141 tagged fish reported caught by anglers.

### Movement Patterns
| Species | N (recaptures) | Time Interval | Distance Range (mi) | Upstream | Downstream | No Movement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Channel Catfish]] | 45 | 2 days–27 months | 0–40 | 13 | 31 | 1 |
| [[Walleye]] | 34 | 65 days–17 months | 0–18 | 15 | 11 | 8 |
| [[Smallmouth Bass]] | 23 | 2 days–21 months | 0–40 | 6 | 9 | 8 |

- **Channel Catfish**: Greatest overall movement among species studied; downstream movement predominated (31 vs. 13 upstream). One individual moved **40 miles** over 23 months; two others moved 18–20 miles. Comparable to Muncy's (1957) finding of a max 26-mile movement, and far exceeded by [[Hubley 1963]]'s Mississippi River study documenting 19 fish moving >100 miles (also downstream-dominant).
- **Walleye**: Three fish moved >5 miles (two at 18 mi, one at ~12 mi). Considerably less mobile than walleye/sauger in the Mississippi River studied by [[Cleary 1958]], where average movement of dispersing fish was 50 miles.
- **Smallmouth Bass**: Overwhelmingly sedentary — most fish moved <0.75 mile. One exceptional individual moved **40 miles** in 21 months (unexplained). Findings align with [[Brown 1960]] (>90% of native smallmouth bass moved <0.5 mile, with one outlier at 19 miles) and support the "homing" behavior described by [[Reynolds 1963]] and reviewed broadly by [[Gerking 1959]].
- **Flathead Catfish**: Minimal movement data available (only 1 usable post-1956 return); maximum shown movement was 0.5 mile, with most fish showing no movement at all.

### Study Limitations / Biases
- Harvest estimates are **minimums only** — not all tagged fish caught by anglers were reported, especially after publicity from the original 1955–56 tagging campaign ([[Muncy 1957]]) waned.
- Movement data biased by:
  - Concentrated recapture effort near original tagging sites and "favorite fishing spots."
  - Two lowhead dams restricting upstream movement detection.
  - Fish recaptured multiple times were treated as independent new releases/recaptures, potentially skewing distance/direction statistics.
  - Map-based distance measurements limited by scale and station area imprecision.
