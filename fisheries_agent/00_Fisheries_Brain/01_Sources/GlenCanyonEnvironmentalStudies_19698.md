## Executive Summary

This document is a **1991 draft outline and interagency memorandum** from the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program, not a primary research paper. It proposes a literature review to evaluate the **condition and impact of exotic (non-native) fish fauna** in the Colorado River between [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and Separation Canyon, within the [[Grand Canyon]]. The central concern is how introduced species — many originating from stocking programs and reservoir spillway releases in [[Lake Powell]] and [[Lake Mead]] — affect the recovery of native, endangered fish, particularly the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The memo also raises logistical concerns about researcher density in the [[Little Colorado River]] tributary camps due to concurrent endangered-fish research contracts. No morphological, physiological, or population-count data are presented; the value of the source lies in its proposed study framework and preliminary exotic species list.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The outline frames the exotic fish issue around several ecological axes:

- **Origin pathways of introduction**:
  - Deliberate stocking of sport fish into [[Lake Powell]] and [[Lake Mead]] (e.g., trout, bass, walleye, striped bass).
  - Downstream transport via **dam operations and spillway releases**, moving lentic (lake-adapted) species into the riverine [[Grand Canyon]] reach.
  - Bait-bucket introductions (e.g., [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Golden Shiner]]).
  - Possible future introductions flagged as a concern.

- **Habitat partitioning** considered across three river habitat types:
  - **Main channel**
  - **Backwaters**
  - **Tributaries** (e.g., Little Colorado River)

- **Species status categories** proposed for classification:
  - Established, self-sustaining populations with confirmed spawning.
  - "Incidental catch" species with minimal records (uncertain establishment).
  - Migrants/strays documented moving downstream from [[Lake Powell]] or [[Lake Mead]] into the river without confirmed reproduction.

- **Competitive and trophic interactions with natives** to be assessed:
  - Spatial and temporal competition for food, cover, and spawning habitat.
  - Predator/prey relationships — where exotics fit in the food web.
  - Predation pressure on specific native life stages (e.g., larval/juvenile native fish, including [[Humpback Chub]]).
  - Whether exotics are filling previously "empty" ecological niches created by the postdam altered flow and temperature regime below [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

- **Postdam vs. predam environmental contrast**: The pristine, natural pre-dam river environment supported native fish assemblages; postdam conditions (regulated flows, cold hypolimnetic releases, clarified water) are implicated in facilitating exotic establishment.

## Key Findings

- A **preliminary list of 22 exotic species** was compiled as present or suspected between Glen Canyon Dam and Separation Canyon, spanning trout (Salmonidae), a shad (Clupeidae), multiple cyprinids, ictalurids (catfish/bullheads), centrarchids (sunfishes/basses), and moronids/percids (striped bass, walleye):

  **Salmonids**: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Cutthroat Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]]

  **Clupeid**: [[Threadfin Shad]]

  **Cyprinids**: [[Common Carp]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Redside Shiner]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Utah Chub]]

  **Fundulid**: [[Rio Grande Killifish]]

  **Ictalurids**: [[Channel Catfish]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]]

  **Centrarchids**: [[Green Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]] (title also references [[Bluegill]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Smallmouth Bass]])

  **Others named in title**: [[Striped Bass]], [[Walleye]], [[Guppy]]

- Data sources identified for the review include records from **Phase 1 studies**, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF), [[Museum of Northern Arizona]] (MNA), [[National Park Service]] (NPS), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), and monitoring programs at Lake Powell, Lake Mead, and Glen Canyon Recreation Areas.

- The study proposes generating a **relative abundance histogram** of present exotic species populations as a key deliverable figure.

- A major unresolved management question: **how do exotic fish populations affect recovery potential for endangered native species**, explicitly citing the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*).

- Logistical/environmental impact concern: multiple concurrent research contracts are converging on the **Little Colorado River** camps, raising concern about cumulative human impact from field crews conducting endangered fish research — a non-biological but operationally significant finding for the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program.

- The review was slated for discussion at the **GCES Aquatic Coordination Team meeting on July 19, 1991**, indicating this document is a planning/scoping artifact rather than a completed study.
