# Endangered Species Act Experimental Populations Final Rule (1984)

#endangered-species #conservation-policy #wildlife-management #ESA-regulations #experimental-populations

## Executive Summary

This document is the final rule issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] on August 27, 1984, implementing Section 10(j) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) as amended in 1982. The rule establishes procedures for designating and managing "experimental populations" of endangered or threatened species. The regulation provides administrative flexibility to reintroduce listed species into suitable habitat outside their current range while addressing local concerns about rigid protective measures. Experimental populations can be classified as either "essential" or "nonessential" to species survival, with different levels of protection under Section 7 of the ESA. The rule amends 50 CFR Part 17 to create a new Subpart H governing experimental population designations, effective September 26, 1984.

## Background & Description

The [[Endangered Species Act Amendments of 1982]] (Pub. L. 97-304) created Section 10(j), authorizing the Secretary of the Interior to designate specific populations of listed species as "experimental populations." Prior to these amendments, the Service could translocate listed species into unoccupied portions of their historic range, but significant local opposition often occurred due to concerns over rigid protective requirements under Sections 7 and 9 of the ESA.

An **experimental population** is defined as an introduced and/or designated population (including offspring) that is wholly separate geographically from nonexperimental populations of the same species. Geographic separation must be reasonably predictable (e.g., through fixed migration patterns or natural/man-made barriers). When experimental and natural populations overlap, experimental status is not recognized in the overlap area.

Experimental populations are classified as either:
- **Essential**: populations whose loss would likely appreciably reduce the likelihood of species survival in the wild
- **Nonessential**: all other experimental populations

The Conference Report accompanying the 1982 Amendments indicated that "in most cases, experimental populations will not be essential."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The rule addresses translocation and reintroduction as conservation tools for listed species recovery. Long-standing Service policy restricts introductions of listed species to historic range, absent extreme findings that primary habitat has been unsuitably and irreversibly altered. This approach aligns with ESA sections 2(b) and 2(c)(1), which emphasize ecosystem protection and conservation of species in natural habitats.

The regulation acknowledges that transplantation beyond historic range could subject populations to doubtful survival chances and might alter species' gene pools contrary to ESA goals. The approach also respects Executive Order 11987, which prohibits introduction of exotic species into natural ecosystems of the United States.

Geographic isolation is a prerequisite for experimental population designation. The rule does not apply to traditional fish stocking efforts to augment existing populations, which involve adding individuals to extant populations rather than establishing geographically separate populations.

## Key Findings

**Regulatory Framework:**
- All experimental population designations must proceed through informal rulemaking under 5 U.S.C. 553, providing minimum 30-day comment periods
- Designations do not require the usual Section 4 listing process unless critical habitat is proposed
- Experimental populations are treated as threatened species for purposes of Section 4(d) protective regulations, regardless of donor population status

**Section 7 Requirements:**
- **Nonessential populations** outside [[National Park System]] or [[National Wildlife Refuge System]] units are excluded from Section 7(a)(2) jeopardy analysis; Federal agencies need only informally confer as if species were proposed
- **Essential populations** or those within Park/Refuge System units remain subject to all Section 7 provisions
- Section 7(a)(1) applies to all experimental populations
- Biological opinions must consider experimental and nonexperimental populations as a single listed species

**Pre-Release Findings Required (§17.81(b)):**
The Secretary must find that release will further species conservation by considering:
1. Possible adverse effects on extant populations from removal of individuals/eggs/propagules
2. Likelihood the experimental population will become established and survive
3. Relative effects on species recovery
4. Extent of impacts from existing/anticipated Federal, State, or private activities
5. Risk assessment including carrying capacity, population dynamics, and behavioral criteria

**Special Rules (§17.81(c)):**
Each experimental population regulation must provide:
- Means to identify the population (location, migration patterns, number of specimens, etc.)
- Determination of essential vs. nonessential status with supporting factual basis
- Management measures including isolation/containment from natural populations
- Periodic review and evaluation of success or failure

**Critical Habitat:**
- May be designated for essential experimental populations following Section 4 procedures
- No critical habitat designation for nonessential populations
- No critical habitat in overlap areas unless related to natural population needs

**Consultation Requirements:**
The Service must consult with:
- Appropriate State fish and wildlife agencies
- Local governmental entities
- Affected Federal agencies
- Affected private landowners
- Public meetings conducted where appropriate

**Memoranda of Understanding (MOU):**
- May be used to implement or supplement conservation programs for experimental populations
- Cannot substitute for initial experimental population rulemaking
- May substitute for additional Section 4(d) protective regulations if Federal land managing agency has effective management program

**Section 10(j)(3) Authority:**
The Service has discretionary authority to designate as experimental populations that were released prior to the 1982 Amendments, but such designations require rulemaking and are not mandatory.

**Public Participation:**
Comments received from 30+ entities including State agencies, Federal agencies ([[Bureau of Land Management]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], [[Marine Mammal Commission]]), conservation organizations ([[Defenders of Wildlife]], [[Environmental Defense Fund]], [[National Wildlife Federation]]), and industry groups. The final rule incorporated numerous suggestions to clarify definitions, expand consultation procedures, and ensure biological rigor in designation decisions.
