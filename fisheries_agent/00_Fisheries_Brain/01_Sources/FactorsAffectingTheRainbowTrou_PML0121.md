# Factors Affecting the Rainbow Trout Fishery in the Hoover Dam Tailwater, Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department technical report (Federal Aid Project F-14-R, 1993–1995) investigates why the [[Hoover Dam]] tailwater lost its trophy [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery, which had been productive in the 1960s and early 1970s. The fishery is now a put-and-take system supported by stocking from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], alongside a growing [[Striped Bass]] fishery. The authors conclude that [[Striped Bass Predation]] currently limits rainbow trout survival, that stocked fish have near-zero annual survival, and that biological productivity has declined since the late 1970s. A trophy fishery equivalent to that of 20–30 years earlier is not expected to return under current conditions.

## Background & Description

- **Historical fishery:** Rainbow trout stocking began in 1935. Angler catches of 2.8–17.2 kg trout were reported between 1950 and 1954. Trophy fish (>508 mm) were common in the 1960s and early 1970s, and the Arizona state record rainbow trout (9.2 kg) was taken from the tailwater in 1966.
- **Decline:** The percentage of trophy trout in the harvest fell from the late 1970s. Angling effort also dropped after 1979. By 1993, trophy trout made up less than 0.5% of the harvest.
- **Stocking:** [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH) stocked 202,000–673,000 catchable (≥203 mm) trout annually from 1963 to 1973. Nevada Division of Wildlife added 28,000–118,000 fish per year. From 1974–1978, mainly subcatchable (101–152 mm) fish were stocked. Fish stocked 1979–1987 averaged 203 mm, and those stocked 1988–1995 averaged 229 mm.
- **Study area:** The tailwater covers 789 ha and extends 42 km downstream from Hoover Dam to Eldorado Canyon. Water released from the lake hypolimnion is cold (12–14 °C) year-round. Four sites were sampled: Hoover Dam, Ringbolt Rapids, Monkey Hole, and Eldorado.
- **Fish community:** Rainbow trout, [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and striped bass regularly occur in the tailwater. Natural trout recruitment does not occur because spawning substrate is lacking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology and nutrients:** [[Lake Powell]], formed in 1963, trapped nitrates, phosphorus and sediments, reducing inputs to Lake Mead. Las Vegas wastewater treatment improvements in 1981 further lowered effluent phosphorus to 0.1 mg/l.
- **Invertebrate food base:** Benthos is dominated by chironomids, oligochaetes and amphipods (Hyalella azteca). The amphipod Gammarus lacustris, once an important trout food, appears extirpated. Threadfin shad, introduced to Lake Mohave in 1955, declined by the 1980s.
- **Zooplankton:** Lake Mead is the main zooplankton source. Densities did not increase downstream from Hoover Dam to Monkey Hole, but rose at Eldorado.
- **Physicochemical conditions:** pH, dissolved oxygen, temperature and salinity all remained within ranges considered suitable for rainbow trout.

## Key Findings

**Methods overview**
- Tagged rainbow trout were stocked with coded-wire tags. Exploitation and survival were tracked through an access-point creel survey at Willow Beach Marina (September 1993–October 1995).
- Stomach contents were analyzed for striped bass and trout. Zooplankton, benthos, nutrients, chlorophyll-a and physicochemical variables were sampled bimonthly.
- Gill netting surveyed relative fish abundance.

**Rainbow trout exploitation and survival**
- The creel survey interviewed 2,844 anglers and recovered 539 tagged trout. Overall exploitation was 2.6%.
- Exploitation was higher for fish stocked into Area 7 (Hoover Dam to Arizona Mile Marker 52) than Area 6 (Mile Marker 52 to Nevada Mile Marker 39) (Mann-Whitney U, P = 0.034).
- 83% of harvested trout were caught within 4 weeks of stocking, and 62% within the first 2 weeks.
- No tagged fish were harvested more than 320 days after release, so annual survival approaches zero. Some untagged trout over 508 mm were still caught, however, which indicates that some fish survive beyond one year.
- Trophy-size catches were 0% in 1993, 2.4% in 1994 (n = 5) and 0.2% in 1995 (n = 1).

**Striped bass predation**
- Striped bass were first documented in Lake Mohave in 1981. Their share of the creel at Willow Beach rose from under 1% before 1979 to 52% by 1991.
- More rainbow trout were found in large striped bass stomachs within 2 weeks of stocking than after 2 weeks (Mann-Whitney U, P = 0.005).
- Trout made up 9% of food item volume in large striped bass stomachs (Table 3, n = 145 stomachs with food, stocking period). Chironomid pupae and cladocerans increased during the no-stocking period. Trout occurrence fell from 12% to 1.9% in that period (P < 0.001).
- The authors describe predation as the main current limit on the trout fishery, but they note that predation was not quantified directly.

**Diet overlap and competition**
- The Schoener Index for rainbow trout and small striped bass (<400 mm) was 0.51, below the 0.6 threshold for biologically significant overlap. Spearman's r was 0.4 (P = 0.059).
- Chironomid pupae were the most important food for both species, so declining chironomids could create competition.

**Zooplankton**
- Median density was 1,775/m³ (range 134–35,681/m³) across all sites. Densities were higher in June and August than in other months.
- Adult copepods made up 44.2% of samples, nauplii 40.8%, and cladocerans 15.0%.
- Densities in 1994–1995 were far below the 23,000–179,000/m³ reported by Paulson et al. (1980a).
- Daphnid peak timing shifted from March–May (1978) to June–August (1994–1995). The authors suggest this may reflect the decline of threadfin shad and the introduction of striped bass.

**Benthos**
- Benthic diversity has declined since Lake Mohave formed in 1951, but abundance has changed little since the 1970s.
- No significant year-to-year differences were found in benthos densities (grab P = 0.254; kicknet P = 0.344). Spatial comparisons were qualitative only.

**Nutrients and chlorophyll-a**
- Maximum total phosphorus was 0.24 mg/l (Ringbolt Rapids). Nitrate-nitrite ranged up to 0.744 mg/l. Chlorophyll-a reached 33.0 mg/m³ at Eldorado.
- Current maxima for total phosphorus, total Kjeldahl nitrogen and chlorophyll-a were lower than those measured in the 1970s.
- Priscu et al. (1982) reported that phytoplankton production in Lake Mohave was among the highest recorded for freshwater systems, which suggests nutrient decline did not drive the initial fishery collapse.

**Hatchery records**
- WBNFH received eggs from 22 sources (1962–1975) and 17 sources (1976–1995). The Erwin strain was the most commonly stocked.
- Strain records were incomplete before 1976, so the effect of strain changes could not be evaluated.

**Fish community**
- Gill netting captured 87 common carp, 38 razorback suckers, 20 striped bass, 14 channel catfish and 8 rainbow trout.
- Threadfin shad, largemouth bass, green sunfish and bluegill were not collected, although they occur in the downstream part of Area 6.

**Conclusions**
- The authors hypothesize that the decline began with lower survival and growth in the 1970s, followed by high exploitation (1980–1983, reported at 82–105%) and then striped bass predation from the mid-1980s.
- Biological productivity has likely declined since the late 1970s, which may limit growth and recruitment to trophy size.
- A trophy fishery equivalent to that of 20–30 years ago is not expected to return. A popular striped bass fishery and a limited trout fishery will continue.

**Management options**
1. **Maintain the status quo**, with continued creel monitoring.
2. **Enhance the trout fishery:** concentrate stocking in Area 7 (near Willow Beach); stock every 2 weeks instead of monthly; stock larger fish (about 330 mm, which returned 47% and 22% in trials versus 1–2% for stock-size fish); and increase stocking dates to capitalize on immediate post-stocking angling.
3. **Reduce striped bass numbers** through removal techniques, which would be difficult to target selectively and would face opposition from striped bass anglers.
4. **Manage for trophy striped bass** through slot or bag limits, while recognizing that more large bass would increase trout predation.
5. **Cease trout stocking:** hatchery cost was $0.59 per fish, or $22.69 per fish returned to the creel at the 2.6% return rate. However, striped bass might then shift to razorback suckers and bonytail chub.
