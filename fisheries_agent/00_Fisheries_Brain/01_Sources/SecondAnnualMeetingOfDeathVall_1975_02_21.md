# Second Annual Meeting of Death Valley System Committee (1975)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #Cyprinodontidae #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This report documents the proceedings of the Second Annual Meeting of the [[Death Valley System Committee]], convened by the [[Desert Fishes Council]] on February 10, 1975 at Furnace Creek Inn, Death Valley National Monument. The meeting compiled status reports for **17 endemic fish taxa** (plus one non-target species, [[Pahranagat Bonytail]]) confined to isolated springs, streams, and relict habitats of the Death Valley hydrographic system in California and Nevada. Central themes included: the precarious legal and biological status of the [[Devils Hole Pupfish]]; formation of species-specific "recovery teams" as an early model of coordinated multi-agency endangered species management; chronic threats from groundwater pumping in Ash Meadows and the broader Amargosa River system; the spread of invasive [[Gambusia Affinis]] (mosquitofish) into native fish habitats; and ongoing translocation/refugium efforts for several taxa. The document is a foundational primary-source record of early desert fish conservation policy in the United States, predating full maturation of the Endangered Species Act's recovery team apparatus.

## Background & Description

Specific morphometric data are sparse, but the following observations are noted:

- **[[Pahranagat Bonytail]]** (*Gila robusta jordani*): one specimen measuring **10 inches (~25 cm)** in length was recorded at Shoshone Ponds — notable given the small stature typical of isolated desert cyprinids.
- **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*): individuals in the **Hoover Dam refugium** population previously exhibited "extraordinary growth" (larger than wild Devils Hole fish) but have since declined somewhat in size.
- Transplanted *C. diabolis* at **Purgatory Spring** were observed to be "definitely larger than the maximum natural size for this species," with many individuals **misshapen and exhibiting abnormally large abdomens** — a likely pathological or developmental response to novel habitat conditions (e.g., unshaded, high-temperature spring water) and absence of successful natural reproduction.
- Taxonomic ambiguity was noted for pupfish at **Crystal Pool**, where inflow from habitats containing *C. n. pectoralis* raised uncertainty whether resident pupfish were *C. n. mionectes*, *C. n. pectoralis*, or intergrades — highlighting fine-scale morphological/genetic differentiation among Ash Meadows pupfish subspecies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All taxa discussed are restricted to **isolated, thermally and chemically distinctive spring systems, seeps, and short stream reaches** within the endorheic [[Death Valley System]], a classic model of desert fish speciation via Pleistocene lake fragmentation. Key niche and habitat notes:

- **[[Manse Spring]]** — sole natural site for *Empetrichthys latos* (Pahrump killifish); dense vegetation complicates population monitoring.
- **[[Corn Creek]]** (near Las Vegas) — transplant site for *E. latos*, where competition with introduced *[[Gambusia Affinis]]* has driven a steep population decline (~1,400 to ~250 in one year).
- **[[Shoshone Ponds]]** — *E. latos* has gone extinct here, but *Gila robusta jordani* persists successfully.
- **[[Devils Hole]]** — extremely restricted, thermally stable aquifer-fed pool; natural habitat of *C. diabolis*, subject of a landmark water-rights legal battle ("ping-pong justice") protecting groundwater levels for the species.
- **[[Ash Meadows]]** springs (Big Spring, Jackrabbit Spring, School Spring, Crystal Pool, etc.) — hotspot of pupfish and dace endemism, threatened by regional groundwater pumping.
- **[[Owens Valley]]** habitats — Owens River Gorge, Fish Slough / [[Owens Valley Native Fish Sanctuary]], Bishop Creek drainage — support *Gila bicolor snyderi*, undescribed *Rhinichthys osculus*, *Cyprinodon radiosus*, and *Catostomus fumeiventris*; threatened by illegally introduced largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*) and planned geothermal exploration.
- **[[Amargosa River]] system** (near Beatty, and the Amargosa River Gorge below Tecopa) — supports two undescribed *Rhinichthys osculus* subspecies and *Cyprinodon nevadensis amargosae*; BLM closure of the gorge to off-road vehicles aids habitat protection.
- **[[Cottonball Marsh]]** and **[[Saratoga Springs]]** — stable, low-threat habitats for *Cyprinodon milleri* and *C. n. nevadensis*, respectively, contingent on aquifer integrity and exclusion of exotic fishes.
- **[[Salt Creek]]** — habitat of *Cyprinodon salinus*, managed under NPS interpretive/visitor-impact mitigation plans.
- **[[Zzyzx Springs]]** — refugium for *Gila bicolor mohavensis* (Mohave chub), transitioning to BLM management for research/teaching use.
- Broader threat vector: **[[Gambusia Affinis]]** invasion throughout Furnace Creek and Scotty's Castle water systems within Death Valley National Monument, conflicting with NPS exotic-species policy and threatening native fish via tourist-mediated spread.

## Key Findings

- **Devils Hole pupfish decline:** Population ~200 (expected ~400 seasonally), declining 25–30 individuals/month since September 1974; prompted formation of a dedicated recovery team chaired by Jim Deacon (UNLV).
- **Recovery team model established:** Multi-agency recovery teams formed/planned for *C. diabolis* and *E. latos* (Pahrump killifish), and nominated for *C. n. pectoralis* (Warm Springs pupfish), integrating NPS, USFWS, BLM, Bureau of Reclamation, Nevada Dept. of Fish and Game, and university scientists — an early template for ESA-style recovery planning.
- **Interstate groundwater conflict:** California Dept. of Water Resources task force raised concern that Nevada groundwater development could irreversibly harm California ecosystems (including Ash Meadows), with effects "already apparent"; matter advanced toward possible interstate litigation.
- **Invasive species management:** Consensus reached to eradicate *Gambusia affinis* from Furnace Creek and Scotty's Castle areas, with removal effort planned for June 1975, contingent on prior biological inventory to protect non-target native species.
- **Transplant pathology concern:** Purgatory Spring *C. diabolis* transplants showed abnormal growth/morphology and no evidence of successful reproduction; researchers (Liu & Soltz) recommended either full removal/preservation of this population or renewed monitoring of future transplants from inception, given the newly shaded habitat conditions.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty:** Crystal Pool pupfish population of uncertain subspecific identity (*mionectes* vs. *pectoralis* vs. intergrade) flagged for collection and identification by Robert R. Miller.
- **Successful transplant progress:** *Gila bicolor mohavensis* (Mohave chub) — 4 of 10 transplant operations successful, subspecies considered "reasonably secure."
- **Sampling protocol recommendation:** Liu & Soltz urged mandatory, at-minimum-annual periodic sampling of desert fish populations (especially pupfishes) to detect population trends, arguing natural mortality rates render small research samples ecologically insignificant; also recommended maintaining adequately sized founder populations in transplants to preserve genetic diversity.
- **Institutional outreach:** Desert Fishes Council invited to present a 1.5-hour symposium on desert fish conservation at the joint national meeting of the [[American Fisheries Society]] and the International Association of Game, Fish, and Conservation Commissioners.
- **Upcoming events:** Next Desert Fishes Council general symposium scheduled for November 13–15, 1975 at Death Valley National Monument Headquarters; next Death Valley System Committee meeting planned for early 1976.
