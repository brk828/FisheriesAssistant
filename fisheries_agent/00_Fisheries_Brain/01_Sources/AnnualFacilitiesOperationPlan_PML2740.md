# Annual Facilities Operation Plan (1999): Endangered Fish Propagation in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This plan, dated 19 February 1999, sets out the annual operations of the propagation facilities that support the [[Recovery Program]] for endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It records the fish on hand at each facility, the expected production for the coming year, and the 1999 action plan for each site. The plan is derived from the [[Genetics Management Plan]] and from approved stocking plans for [[Bonytail]] (Hunt et al. 1996) and for endangered species of the State of Colorado (Nesler 1998). Its main operational aims are to build pedigreed broodstocks, maintain backup refugia against catastrophic loss, and grow fish to a suitable size for stocking into upper basin rivers.

## Background & Description

The document treats four priority species, ranked in the Genetics Management Plan as follows: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. Propagation needs fall into three categories (Wydoski 1995b):

- **Primary refuges**, used to develop broodstocks, produce fish for research, augmentation and restoration.
- **Backup refuges**, used to prevent catastrophic loss, hold backup broodstock, and support long-term research.
- **Growout or production ponds**, used to rear fish to stocking size.

Three primary refuges are named:

- [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]]: Green River razorback sucker broodstock and growout. It has a hatchery building, a water treatment building, and 10.8 acres of ponds.
- [[Grand Valley Endangered Fish Facility]]: Upper Colorado River razorback sucker and Colorado pikeminnow broodstock and growout, based at the [[Horsethief Ponds]], a hatchery building in Grand Junction, and a growout pond on the Upper Colorado River.
- [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]]: bonytail broodstock and growout, and backup broodstock for razorback sucker.

The plan notes that growout ponds in lower-basin off-channel lagoons and upper-basin riverside ponds produced excellent growth of razorback sucker, bonytail, and Colorado pikeminnow (Mueller et al. 1993; Mueller 1995a; Osmundson 1986; Osmundson and Kaeding 1989). Fish reared in natural ponds are expected to show more wild-like feeding and predator avoidance, and better survival after stocking. Growout ponds are therefore the preferred rearing option for upper basin stocking. The total growout area needed will depend on long-term stocking plans for all river reaches, which were still being finalized.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Backup refugia and water supply.** The most cost-effective backup refuge is a pond with a reliable water supply in a secure area. The plan recommends a sloped bottom and a fish trap at the outlet for easy access. Wahweap currently serves as backup for razorback sucker from both the Green and Upper Colorado river stocks. The Green and Upper Colorado rivers serve as backup refugia for other species and stocks.

**Genetic integrity.** Pedigreed family-lot matings are central to the program. The Ouray facility runs a 25 × 25 spawning matrix for razorback sucker (Table 3). Some crosses in the earlier 5 × 5 diallele design, which has since been discontinued, produced half-sibs. The plan states that if the program cannot establish 25 single mated pairs of middle Green River razorback sucker broodstock by 2001, Lake Mohave fish will be needed to maintain genetic integrity.

**Temporary refuge status.** Table 1 lists the primary and backup refugia populations for each presumptive genetic stock, with lot or individual counts. Some populations are held in river reaches as a temporary measure until they can be secured at a facility. "NM" marks stocks with no identified need.

**Facility scale.** Horsethief State Wildlife Area holds six ponds totaling 3.5 surface acres. Clymers Pond (5.0 acres), at the Gunnison–Colorado confluence, has served as a razorback sucker growout pond before stocking in the Gunnison River. Wahweap has five ponds of about 0.35 acre each, with twelve 0.4-acre ponds planned on site in 1999.

## Key Findings

**Current fish on hand (Tables 2–6)**

- **Ouray:** Razorback sucker lots are held as future broodstock across year classes 1990–1998 (Green River stock). Counts include 1,031 fish and 8,171 fish in the most recent lots. One research group of 36 fish is held for chemoreception work.
- **24 Road Hatchery (Grand Valley):** 22,345 razorback sucker across 15 lots, all tagged as 1997 broodstock.
- **Horsethief Ponds (Grand Valley):** 622 fish in total, including razorback sucker from Old Colorado River broodstock, Green River broodstock, and F1 and F2 generations. Colorado pikeminnow (F1, 1991 class) and one humpback chub (1997 class, spawned naturally at Horsethief) are also held. The humpback chub is designated for Ocean's Journey Aquarium, Denver.
- **Wahweap:** 36,000 bonytail from 1998 (for stocking), 520 bonytail from 1997 and 216 from 1996 (brood). Razorback sucker: 240 from 1998, 100 from 1995 and 40 from 1991 (backup brood).

**1999 propagation policy and actions**

1. Designate Wahweap as backup refuge for the middle Green River razorback sucker stock.
2. Stock excess razorback sucker broodstock into the river from Ouray and Wahweap.
3. Expand Wahweap's role to include bonytail broodstock development and production.
4. Streamside spawn Colorado pikeminnow in June/July for stocking in 1–2 years, following Nesler's (1998) plan.
5. Obtain 20,000 Lake Mohave larvae for growout and as backup for the 25 mated pairs design.

**Site-specific 1999 plans**

- **Ouray:** develop up to 12 lots using approved methods; move backup broodstock to Wahweap; stock excess razorback sucker brood fish and excess chemoreception fish into the river; move excess 623 × 268 razorback sucker to Wahweap for growout and fall stocking.
- **Grand Valley:** make F2 crosses for razorback sucker production under Nesler's plan; stock razorback sucker into the Gunnison River; streamside spawn Colorado pikeminnow for production and stocking.
- **Wahweap:** stock the 1998 bonytail and excess broodstock; develop bonytail broodstock; obtain up to 70,000 bonytail fry from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] for stocking and broodstock; grow out 20,000 bonytail for fall stocking at the Green River and "Professor Valley, UT"; grow out 50,000 fish to meet CDOW's plan.

**Summary.** The plan centers on pedigreed broodstock development, backup refugia for priority stocks, and expanded growout capacity at Wahweap, with explicit contingency use of Lake Mohave fish if the 25 mated pair target is not met by 2001.
