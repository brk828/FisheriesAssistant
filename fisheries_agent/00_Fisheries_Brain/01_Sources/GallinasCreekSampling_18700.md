# Letter on Proposed Chihuahua Chub Translocation to Gallinas Creek (19 December 1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-nigrescens

## Executive Summary

This one-letter correspondence, dated 19 December 1986, was written by [[David L. Propst]] to [[W.L. Minckley]] of the Department of Zoology, Arizona State University. Propst reports that he, Kevin Bestgen, and Steve Platania sampled [[Gallinas Creek]], a tributary of the [[Rio Mimbres]], the previous summer. Only [[Pantosteus Plebius]] was collected, but Propst judges the stream capable of supporting the [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*). He asks Minckley to have the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] recommend that the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) pursue an effort to establish the Chihuahua chub in Gallinas Creek, starting this year.

## Background & Description

The letter is written in a personal, informal tone and is addressed to a colleague on the recovery team. It does not describe the fishes' morphology, life history, or abundance.

- **Sampling:** Gallinas Creek was sampled once, in summer 1986, by three named biologists. The document reports no sample sizes, methods, or catch counts.
- **Species of interest:** The stated goal is the Chihuahua chub, *Gila nigrescens*. The only species actually collected was *Pantosteus plebius*.
- **Related data:** Propst plans to add data on *N. formosus* and *C. ornatum* from the forthcoming [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]], which he is awaiting from Burton. He also plans to add more descriptive information on the Rio Mimbres. The document does not give further detail on these taxa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Land status:** Protection is tied to land ownership. Propst argues that a transplant on [[U.S. Forest Service]] (USFS) land should receive full protection.
- **Hydrology:** Below USFS land, Gallinas Creek is normally dry. Propst notes that opponents could use this to argue for an "experimental" (non-essential) designation rather than full protection.
- **Social context:** Propst expects opposition from a faction in [[Grant County]] that may try to sabotage recovery efforts. He reports that certain USFS factions would be receptive to a Gallinas Creek effort.
- **Uncertainty:** He describes the project as having "a number of imponderables" and wants to begin work to see how events develop.

## Key Findings

- **Proposed action:** Recommend to USFWS that an effort be made to establish the Chihuahua chub in Gallinas Creek, with paperwork to begin as soon as possible and the effort ideally made in 1987.
- **Protection status:** Propst favors full protection for the transplant if it is located on USFS land, rather than an experimental or non-essential designation.
- **Anticipated opposition:** Opposition is expected whatever designation is chosen, from Grant County interests and possibly from USFS factions.
- **Habitat assessment:** Gallinas Creek is judged to have potential to support *Gila nigrescens*, based on a single summer survey that collected only *Pantosteus plebius*.
- **Pending information:** Data from the Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan (*N. formosus*, *C. ornatum*) and further Rio Mimbres descriptions are to be incorporated once received.
- **Scheduling:** Propst is on leave from 20 December 1986 to 12 January 1987 to work on manuscripts. He can be reached at home or by message at work. He asks Minckley for his opinion so he can act accordingly.
