# Periphyton Community in River Cávado, Portugal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bacillariophyceae

## Executive Summary

This 1992 study by Maria Manuela Cerqueira da Silva, published by the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), examines the periphyton community of the [[River Cávado]] in northern Portugal over one year. Periphyton was sampled on artificial substrata (microscope glass slides) at seven stations. The paper addresses algal biomass, diatom density, and protozoan indicators of organic pollution. No fish, invertebrates or fish habitat data are reported; the document is an algal and protozoan ecology study, so the fish-specific fields above are recorded as N/A or name only the organisms studied.

## Background & Description

Periphytic algae are widely used in lotic water quality research. The author follows earlier work by [[Sladecek]] and colleagues, [[Descy]] and [[Wetzel]], and [[Wanatabe]]. Glass slides were chosen because they are easy to handle and are a standard substratum for comparative studies.

Colonization periods were set by season after preliminary trials and literature review:

- Winter: 7 weeks
- Spring and Autumn: 5 to 6 weeks
- Summer: 3 to 4 weeks

Many slides were destroyed, so some sites yielded only a few results.

Methods included:

- Diatom counts in permanent [[Naphrax]] mounts (cells/cm²), using the method of Devaux et al. (1983).
- Chlorophyll *a* as a biomass estimate, with phaeopigments measured by optical density before and after acidification.
- Acetone extraction, spectrophotometry (Strickland and Parsons, 1972) and [[Lorenzen Equations]] (1967) for pigment concentrations.
- Identification of diatoms and protozoa using standard keys (Germain 1984; Pestalozzi 1982; Bick 1972; Margalef 1955).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study sites lie in the [[Cávado Hydrographic Basin]] (Fig. 1), with seven sampling stations: Marachão (1), Barcelos new bridge (3), Barcelos water intake (4), Manhente (5), Padim da Graça (7), Entrepontes (8), and the tributary [[River Homem]] (H1). The Cávado is a lotic system, and the sites differ in tidal influence, salinity, organic loading and dominant algal groups.

Protozoa, mainly ciliates, were used as indicators of organic pollution. Rhizopods were the dominant protozoan group at sites 8 and H1, which suggests better water quality. Ciliates covered glass slides at sites 3, 4, 5 and 7. The author links the ciliates to α- and β-mesosaprobic and polysaprobic conditions, citing [[Bick]] (1972) and [[Fjerdingstad]] (1964).

Site characterization:

- **Site 1:** Under strong stress, including tidal effects and brackish-water indicators (*Bacillaria paradoxa*). Cyanophyceae were constant and sometimes dominant.
- **Sites 3, 4, 7:** Weak algal colonization, ciliate-covered slides, and Euglenophyceae (*Euglena* sp.), consistent with organic pollution.
- **Site 5:** Moderate diatom colonization, shared dominance by Chlorophyceae and Cyanophyceae.
- **Site 8:** Good diatom colonization, with Chlorophyceae sometimes dominant.
- **Site H1 (River Homem):** Good colonization and high diversity. Diatoms were always dominant, typical of lotic streams with good water quality.

## Key Findings

- **Seasonal pattern:** Algal colonization was very low from November to March at all stations. Maximum periphyton density occurred between May and September.
- **Chlorophyll *a*:** Peaks occurred in May and September. The maximum was 953 µg/dm² at site H1 in May, during total dominance by [[Synedra pulchella]]. High values at site 1 (615 µg/dm²) and site 8 (694 µg/dm²) also occurred in May. A September peak of 890 µg/dm² at site 1 coincided with Cyanophyceae dominance (*Oscillatoria* spp. and *Merismopedia elegans*). Values were very low in January, March and June, and no July data exist because the spectrophotometer was unavailable.
- **Phaeopigments:** Often above 100 µg/dm². The maximum was 210 µg/dm² at site 8 in May, linked to high population density.
- **Diatom density:** Maximum of 2918 cells/cm² at site 8 in May. Minimum of 153 cells/cm² at site 7 in May.
- **Dominant taxa:** Bacillariophyceae (*Tabellaria fenestrata*, *Eunotia pectinalis*, *Navicula cryptocephala*, *Nitzschia ignorata*). Chlorophyceae (*Gloeocystis ampla*, *Spirogyra* sp., *Pleurotaenium ehrenbergii*). Cyanophyceae (*Oscillatoria* spp., *Anabaena* sp.). Euglenophyceae (*Euglena viridis*, *Phacus longicauda*).
- **Diatom tables:** Quantitative tables (1 to 6) cover January, March, May, June, July and September 1989 at the seven stations. Totals range from about 153 to 2918 cells/cm².
- **Protozoa:** Rhizopoda and Ciliophora were found. The ciliate *Homalozoon vermiculare* was the only protozoan recorded at H1, indicating oligosaprobic to β-mesosaprobic conditions.
- **Conclusion:** Periphyton and protozoan communities show a clear gradient from organically polluted or tidally stressed sites (1, 3, 4, 5, 7) to better-quality sites (8, H1). The author recommends periphyton as a water quality indicator in lotic systems.
