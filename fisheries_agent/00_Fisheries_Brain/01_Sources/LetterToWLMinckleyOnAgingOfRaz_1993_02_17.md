## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 interoffice fax/letter from Frank K. Pfeifer (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Colorado River Fishery Project, Grand Junction, CO) to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) concerning age determination of [[Razorback Sucker]] specimens via otolith analysis. The letter is primarily administrative, tracking specimen provenance, collection dates, and mortality timelines for nine razorback sucker individuals originating from **Etter Pond**, an off-channel habitat associated with the [[Colorado River Fishery Project]]. No morphometric, population, or otolith-derived age data are included in this excerpt; the letter instead serves as chain-of-custody documentation for specimens sent for aging.

No direct morphological measurements (length, weight, otolith ring counts, or age estimates) are reported in this letter. ## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large-bodied, long-lived catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], listed as endangered.
- **Habitat context**: Specimens were collected from **Etter Pond**, an off-channel or floodplain pond habitat used in razorback sucker conservation and refugia programs.
- **Holding facilities**: Surviving and carcass specimens were subsequently transferred to **Dexter ponds** (likely referring to the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], New Mexico), a key facility for endangered Southwestern fish propagation and holding.
- **Timeline noted**: Fish were collected in **August 1991**; some individuals died within one week of collection, while others (including one live specimen and three additional carcasses) persisted in Dexter ponds until **March 1992** before processing.
