# Arizona Outdoors Column: Smallmouth, Striper, Yellowtail, and Wildlife Notes (1975)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #moronidae #carangidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 newspaper outdoors column reports on recent sport-fishing conditions and wildlife incidents in Arizona and the Gulf of California. The main fishing items are a growing smallmouth bass fishery at [[Apache Lake]], a late spring [[Striped Bass]] migration on the [[Colorado River]] at [[Lake Havasu]], and a strong [[Yellowtail]] bite near [[San Pedro Island]] in the [[Gulf of California]]. The column also covers a burrowing owl nesting conflict at Mesa Community College and the rescue of a desert bighorn ram from a cliff ledge in [[Aravaipa Canyon]]. It is an anecdotal, angler-oriented account with no formal survey data.

## Background & Description

The column opens with a tribute to the [[Smallmouth Bass]], which the author contrasts with the [[Largemouth Bass]]. It quotes [[James Henshall]]'s *Book of the Black Bass* (OCR read the date as "1g@1"; the context suggests 1881), which calls smallmouths "inch for inch and pound for pound, the gamest fish that swims." Smallmouths were never common in Arizona. Early plantings in the [[Verde River]] did not establish well, but the population took hold after biologist [[Dick Navarre]] stocked the [[Black River]] in the mid-1960s. Fish later spread into the [[Salt River]] and its lakes.

Anglers reported smallmouth catches of about one to three pounds, with some larger fish including a four-pound bass. Recommended lures included Hotspots, Spinrites, Bomber waterdogs, Mepps spinners with pork rind, pinfish, and feather jigs worked over rocks.

The striper section describes an unusually late spring run. Big schools were usually far up the [[Colorado River]] by this time of year, but only females had moved through. Local experts blamed unseasonably cold weather. Anglers were catching mostly males weighing 10 to 15 pounds in upper Lake Havasu. Striper expert Gus Newman recommended 20- to 30-pound test monofilament to haul fish out of submerged tree limbs.

The yellowtail report describes a trip to San Pedro Island with several anglers. Yellowtails were surface-feeding in calm water. One fish of about 20 pounds took 20 minutes to land. Other catches included white fish, estranjeros, a sardinero, and large bigmouth kelp fish. Killer whales were also seen near the shoreline.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Smallmouth range expansion:** The column describes a stocking-driven spread from the Black River into the Salt River reservoir chain. It attributes the current fishing success at Apache Lake to that spread and suggests the lake's clean, crisp water may suit the species.
- **Striper habitat modification:** Lake Havasu's trolling depth is set by flooded terrain. Trees flanking the old river channel were cut down when the lake was formed, so their stumps sit about 14 feet below normal water level. Anglers troll at 12 to 13 feet to keep lures just above these submerged trees.
- **Climate-driven migration delay:** The striper run was about two months behind schedule. Cold spring weather was given as the cause.
- **Desert bighorn ecology:** The ram had been stranded for almost a year on a ledge of less than one-eighth of an acre, about 600 feet above Aravaipa Canyon. It had grazed the ledge bare and stripped oak trees, and a small seep supplied its water. Wildlife officials believed the seep would dry up in the coming dry season. They also credited a wet winter with keeping the animal alive.
- **Burrowing owls:** Burrowing owls nesting in an old irrigation pipe on the Mesa Community College campus had attacked students during spring nesting seasons for about six years. The Arizona Game and Fish Department trapped and relocated the birds.

## Key Findings

- **Smallmouth bass are thriving at Apache Lake** as a result of the mid-1960s stocking in the Black River and its spread through the Salt River system. Current catches include fish of one to three pounds and some larger bass.
- **Recommended smallmouth lures** are Hotspots, Spinrites, Bomber waterdogs, Mepps spinners with pork rind, pinfish, and feather jigs.
- **The Lake Havasu striper run was delayed about two months** by cold weather. Only females had arrived up the river, and anglers were catching mainly 10- to 15-pound males in the upper lake.
- **Trolling depth at Lake Havasu is critical.** Lures should run at 12 to 13 feet, just above trees about 14 feet deep. Heavy line (20- to 30-pound test) is advised to free snagged fish.
- **Yellowtail were feeding actively** near San Pedro Island, producing a 20-pound fish that took 20 minutes to land, along with several other species.
- **Desert bighorn rescue:** A ram stranded on a 600-foot cliff ledge for nearly a year was captured by a mountaineering team after several failed attempts. Wildlife staff reported that the ledge and its seep could not have sustained the animal through the summer.
- **Burrowing owl conflict:** Owls nesting in an old irrigation pipe attacked Mesa Community College students. The Arizona Game and Fish Department relocated the birds to resolve the problem.
