# Lower Colorado River Repatriate Razorback Sucker PIT-Tagging Database Summary Report 1992-1999

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen-texanus

## Executive Summary

This report from the [[Lake Mohave Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) summarizes the [[Repatriation Program]] for endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]] from 1992 through December 1999. Hatchery- and backwater-reared juveniles were [[PIT Tagging|PIT-tagged]] and released back into the lake. More than 39,000 repatriates were released, and 311 had been recaptured by the end of 1999. The authors conclude that razorback sucker larvae cannot survive without protected, predator-free conditions, and they argue that a minimum stocking length of 30 cm TL should be adopted. Using recapture-based estimates, they put juvenile survival at roughly 43 percent, with a sensitivity range of about 30 to 33 percent.

## Background & Description

The NFWG was formed in 1990 by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Arizona State University]], the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] Biological Resources Division, [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and the [[National Park Service]]. Its primary mission is to produce and rear young native lower Colorado River fishes, especially razorback sucker.

- **Larval collection:** Larvae are collected each winter-spring spawning season from the Lake Mohave shallows.
- **Rearing facilities:** [[Boulder City Golf Course Ponds]], [[Boulder City Wetlands Park]], and [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (AZ). Some fish are stocked directly, while others are grown out in lakeside backwaters at sites such as Yuma, Davis Cove, and Dandy Cove.
- **Tagging and release:** Juveniles about 30 cm nominal length are [[PIT Tagging|PIT-tagged]], measured, and released.
- **Monitoring:** Monitoring programs began in 1968 under W.L. Minckley and ASU. Sampling is repeated annually at the same sites, times, and gear. Expeditions run in May, March (the "Razorback Round-up"), and November, using [[Trammel Netting]] and [[Electrofishing]]. Wild adults, repatriates, [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], and non-native species are captured.
- **Data management:** ASU is the central repository for native fish tagging data from the Colorado River. Records are entered in Excel spreadsheets and kept in separate repatriate and wild-adult folders. A master file is sorted for PIT-tag duplicates to decide whether a recaptured fish is a wild adult or a repatriate. Recorded fields include date, site, river km, agency, gear, PIT tag, total length (TL), weight, sex, status, and field comments.
- **Sex categories:** Juvenile (not sexually mature and not sexable), male, female, and unknown (adult, sex unclear).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report states that razorback sucker can reproduce in the wild, but its larvae cannot survive and grow unless protected from predators. Predation by [[Ictalurid Catfish]] is the mechanism cited, drawing on [[Marsh and Brooks 1989]], which suggested that young razorback suckers should be at least 300 mm long to be relatively safe from most aquatic predators.

Repatriates were grown in [[Lakeside Backwaters]] and other protected sites before release. Survival after release is uncertain, especially for fish under 300 mm. Recapture is concentrated in the March spawning season, when many repatriates have not yet reached sexual maturity. Low recapture rates therefore do not necessarily reflect poor survival or poor capture success.

## Key Findings

**Release totals and production**
- More than 39,000 repatriate razorback suckers were released into Lake Mohave from 1992 through December 1999 (Table 1, E-Appendix 1).
- Lakeside backwaters produced 16,026 fish (41 percent of the total). Willow Beach NFH and Boulder City Wetlands Park contributed 20 and 17 percent.
- 1999 was the most productive year, with 16,029 releases (40 percent of the total).

**Size at release**
- Individual TL at release ranged from 96 to 563 mm.
- Annual average TL ranged from 180 mm (1993) to 394 mm (1996). The eight-year grand average was 273 mm.
- 1996 had the largest fish but ranked fourth in production numbers.

**Maturity at release**
- Only 13 released fish were classified as sexually mature: 2 females, 10 males, and 1 unknown.
- Eight males were noted as "ripe" and/or "tuberculate" at TLs of 316-378 mm, which suggests that razorback sucker can mature at relatively small size.
- The remaining males and the unknown-sex fish were 534-563 mm long. Only the 536 mm male was noted as "ripe." Neither female had reproductive comments.

**Recaptures**
- A total of 311 repatriates had been recaptured since 1993: 256 fish once and 27 fish multiple times (26 twice, 1 three times).
- 1992 had the highest recapture rate per fish released: 27 recaptures, 21 percent of the 131 fish released that year. Its average release TL (349 mm) was 17 mm above the next-largest year.
- 1995 yielded the most recaptures (115 fish, 37 percent of the total).
- 1999 had 95 recaptures (31 percent), the most in any single recapture year.
- Recaptured fish had release TLs of 153-563 mm (annual averages 280-349 mm; grand average 311 mm). At recapture, TLs ranged from 275 to 672 mm.
- Only 0.8 percent of all repatriates released had been recaptured.
- At release, all recaptured fish were juveniles except one male, which was recaptured two years later.
- At recapture, the report lists 235 sexually mature fish (62 females, 173 males, 26 unknown) and 50 juveniles (Table 5). These figures sum to 285, not the 311 recaptures reported elsewhere, so the breakdown should be checked against the source tables.
- Males were recaptured every year. The first recaptured female was released in 1994 and recaptured in 1995.

**Survival and size threshold**
- More than 67 percent of releases (26,534 fish) were 300 mm or smaller at release.
- The 95 recaptured fish released at or below 300 mm made up about 31 percent of recaptures. The authors apply this proportion to the small-fish group to estimate 8,105 survivors.
- Adding 8,962 survivors from the 12,904 fish over 300 mm gives 17,067 surviving juveniles, or about 43 percent. The report gives the 12,904-fish group's survival fraction as 0.69, and 12,904 × 0.69 ≈ 8,904 rather than 8,962, so this figure should be verified.
- If all fish under 300 mm died and all larger fish survived, survival would be about 33 percent.
- A 1999 population estimate by [[Pacey and Marsh]] (in review) gave about 30 percent survival. The authors consider this conservative, because 1998 and 1999 releases were excluded.
- **Recommendation:** Adopt a strict minimum stocking length of 30 cm TL.

**Data and program management**
- The [[Razorback Sucker Repatriation Database]] and individual recapture histories are provided as electronic E-Appendices.
- Funding came from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Boulder City, Nevada (Agreement 99-FG-30-0067).
