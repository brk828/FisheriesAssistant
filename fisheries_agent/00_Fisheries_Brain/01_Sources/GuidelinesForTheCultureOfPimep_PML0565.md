# Guidelines for the Culture of Fathead Minnows (*Pimephales promelas*) for Use in Toxicity Tests

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pimephales-promelas

## Executive Summary

This EPA Project Summary, prepared by [[Environmental Research Laboratory - Duluth]] (ERL-D) in Duluth, Minnesota, sets out the conditions and procedures used to culture research-quality [[Fathead Minnow]] embryos, larvae, juveniles, and adults for [[Toxicity Testing]]. The culture system has operated since the laboratory was established in 1967. The guidelines cover the physical system (water supply, tanks, water delivery, construction materials, photoperiod, aeration, spawning substrates) and the biological system (brood stock, spawning, embryo incubation, feeding, disease, and genetic management). The document is intended as an adaptable guide for laboratories that want to culture fathead minnows for fish toxicology research.

## Background & Description

The fathead minnow has been cultured at ERL-D since 1967, and the techniques were refined over the years by many researchers. Laboratory-reared fish are preferred for toxicity testing because their age and genetic background are known, their diet is controlled, they are free of disease, and they are available year-round.

The facility is designed to supply three life stages in greatest demand for testing:

- Embryos less than 24 hours old
- Larvae 0-24 hours old
- Juveniles 30 days old

The system also produces adults as future [[Brood Stock]]. Ninety-six pairs of adult spawners provide 1,000-2,000 embryos per day. These embryos can be used immediately or incubated to produce larvae or juveniles for testing at later dates.

The full technical report carries the title "Guidelines for the Culture of Fathead Minnows, *Pimephales promelas* for Use in Toxicity Tests" (NTIS PB 87-165 247/AS). The project was reported by [[U.S. Environmental Protection Agency]] staff, with J. S. Denny as author and R. L. Spehar as Project Officer.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water supply.** Lake Superior water is the preferred source, supplied at about 150 mL/min per tank. Springs, wells, or controlled surface waters are recommended where available. Dechlorinated municipal tap water is a last resort. Any supply should be checked for pesticides, heavy metals, sulfides, disease vectors, and other suspected contaminants. The full ERL-D system of more than 150 tanks uses 15-20 liters per minute. Smaller or water-limited systems can run at reduced flow, and static renewal or recirculating systems are possible but less desirable.

**Tanks.** Tanks are 57-liter (15-gallon) glass aquaria with standpipe drains set to hold about 40 liters. They sit on epoxy-coated angle-iron and plywood racks. Stainless steel screens can divide tanks into sections for spawning pairs. White plastic dishpans hold the spawning tiles.

**Water delivery.** The system is a gravity-fed, constant-temperature, flow-through design. Stainless steel mixing boxes sit in an open ceiling about 3.5 m overhead. A toilet float valve controls the headbox water level. A solenoid valve, driven by a temperature probe and solid-state controller, adds heated water to hold 25°C. Air stones or other agitators are required in the headbox to ensure complete mixing and prevent supersaturation. Water reaches each tank through a 1/2-inch PVC manifold, and needle sizes on syringe barrels set the flow rate.

**Construction materials.** Materials in contact with water must not leach substances. Rubber, copper, brass, and plastics containing fillers, stabilizers, or plasticizers are excluded. Preferred materials are glass, stainless steel, Teflon, and PVC. Piping should be rigid, threadable PVC. Silicone glue is acceptable if it is allowed to cure fully. Ground-fault interrupters are required on all electrical components because electricity and water are in close proximity.

**Photoperiod.** The photoperiod is held constant at 16 hours light and 8 hours dark. ERL-D uses lights that mimic the spectrum of sunlight, but fathead minnows will also spawn under cool white lighting. Temperature may need adjustment to offset the heat from fluorescent lamps.

**Aeration.** Continuous gentle aeration keeps dissolved oxygen above 5.0 mg/L. Vigorous aeration should be avoided with newly hatched larvae. If 5.0 mg/L cannot be maintained, fish should be removed from the tank. Air intakes and compressors must be checked to prevent contamination, and an oil trap or filter may be needed.

**Spawning substrates.** Four-inch PVC pipe cut into 4-inch pieces and halved lengthwise forms a semicircular arch under which fish spawn readily. Roughening the interior with a wire wheel improves egg adhesion. Halved small clay flower pots are an alternative. See [[Spawning Substrates]].

## Key Findings

### Brood stock and selection

- Disease-free, laboratory-adapted fish make the best starting [[Brood Stock]]. Starting from embryos minimizes disease risk, eases shipment, and avoids bioaccumulated toxicants found in adults.
- Wild-caught or bait-dealer fish are less desirable. They must be confirmed as *P. promelas* and not a related species, and examined for disease.
- Juveniles of 3-4 months are stocked at 35-40 fish per 15-gallon tank with about four spawning substrates. Substrates speed maturation.
- Males begin to mature within 1-2 weeks. Breeding males have a gray dorsal tubercle pad, snout tubercles, a nearly black body with two light vertical bars, and a dark spot at the anterior dorsal fin insertion. Females stay drab and show an ovipositor at least a month before spawning.
- Removing mature fish stimulates subordinate fish to mature, which gives a continuous supply of spawners.

### Spawning and fecundity

- The fathead minnow is an intermittent, multiple spawner with an extended breeding season. Under controlled culture it spawns year-round.
- Embryos are buoyant and adhesive. Males guard the nest until hatching. Several females can spawn in one nest, with up to 12,000 embryos recorded in a single nest (Markus, 1934).
- Unpublished data (Olson, 1974) report an average of 258 eggs per spawn and 3,095 total eggs per female over 100 days of spawning.
- Spawning pairs reduce male-male competition and allow individual fecundity to be tracked. Spent fish can be replaced.
- Reported male:female ratios are 2:4 (Olson, 1974), 3:6 (Benoit and Carlson, 1977), and 4:10-15 (Mount, 1971). Males are territorial, so at least as many [[Spawning Substrates]] as males are needed for optimum egg production.

### Embryo incubation

- Spawning tiles are removed daily and replaced with empty tiles. Tiles are incubated on edge in a dishpan with an airstone between tiles. See [[Embryo Incubation]].
- Non-viable embryos are removed daily with tweezers to limit fungal spread.
- At 22-23°C, embryos hatch in about 5 days.
- Tiles can be disinfected by soaking in chlorine and neutralizing with sodium thiosulfate.

### Larval rearing and feeding

- Larvae are moved with a large-bore 50 mL volumetric pipette.
- A stocking density of 250 larvae per 15-gallon tank is recommended.
- Larvae under 30 days old are fed live [[Brine Shrimp]] nauplii twice daily. Gape size is 0.24-0.28 mm, so nauplii must be narrower than this to be ingested (Norberg and Mount, 1985). Nauplii are harvested soon after hatching for small size and better nutrition.
- Rations should match what fish consume in 15-20 minutes. See [[Larval Handling]].
- Fish over 30 days old are fed frozen brine shrimp twice daily. Shrimp should be partly thawed. The same rule on quantity applies. See [[Adult Feeding]].

### Disease and genetic management

- Diseased lots are discarded. Tanks are disinfected with hypochlorite. A bath of 200 ppm active chlorine for 1 hour sanitizes tanks and nets, sponges, and brushes.
- Treated fish are rarely if ever used in bioassays. See [[Disease Management]].
- ERL-D mixes existing brood stock with healthy wild stock every two years to avoid a homogeneous strain. Homogeneous stock gives less variance in test results but weakens inferences about ambient conditions. Genetic management should follow the goals of the research program. See [[Gene Pool]].

### Conclusions

The guidelines give a practical, adaptable framework for producing controlled-age fathead minnow life stages for aquatic toxicity testing. Their main requirements are constant temperature and photoperiod, non-leaching materials, reliable dissolved oxygen, controlled feeding, and disease and genetic management.
