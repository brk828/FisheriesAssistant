# Colorado River Trophic Studies Meeting Minutes - May 1991

#aquatic-ecology #river-ecosystem #primary-production #macroinvertebrates

## Executive Summary

Meeting minutes from the Colorado River (Aquatic) Trophic Studies (CRiTS) team quarterly coordination meeting held May 3, 1991 at [[Glen Canyon Dam]] Conference Room in Page, Arizona. The CRiTS team is an informal multi-agency/institution coordination effort focused on aquatic food base studies in the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem. The meeting covered progress reports from multiple research groups studying primary production, macroinvertebrates, endangered fish, and water quality, as well as discussion of a preliminary aquatic ecosystem energy model and monitoring protocols for interim dam flow operations.

## Background & Description

The CRiTS team coordinates aquatic food base studies across multiple agencies and institutions working in the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem. Participating organizations include the [[USGS]], [[National Park Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Northern Arizona University]] (NAU), and BioWest. The meeting ran from 10:00 a.m. to 16:30 and focused on approaches, patterns, and progress in primary and secondary production studies in the Colorado River system downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

The Colorado River ecosystem below Glen Canyon Dam is characterized by dam-controlled flows, tributary sediment inputs (particularly from the [[Paria River]] and [[Little Colorado River]]), and a complex aquatic food web including attached algae ([[Cladophora]]), diatoms, macroinvertebrates, and fish including endangered species. Test flows were being conducted during this period to evaluate dam operation effects on the aquatic ecosystem.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Colorado River ecosystem below Glen Canyon Dam experiences significant environmental variability driven by dam operations, tributary inputs, and light availability. Key ecological components discussed include:

**Primary Producers**: [[Cladophora]] (filamentous green algae) showing chlorophyll-a production >100 mg/m² on artificial substrates in tailwaters. [[Oscillatoria angustissima]] (cyanobacteria) noted as widespread on cobble rocks and fine deposits throughout lower river, with unknown role in nitrogen fixation and toxin production. Diatom communities also present.

**Macroinvertebrates**: NAU/NPS study had processed >1,000 samples identifying more than two dozen chironomid species and approximately nine species of simuliids (primarily the [[Simulium arcticum]] complex). Fine sediment accumulation observed in tailwaters reach with development of reduction zones on tiles between sediment and tile surface.

**Environmental Factors**: Light energy levels influenced by discharge and antecedent conditions. Suspended sediment load from tributaries affects turbidity and light penetration. Test flows noted to have reduced [[Cladophora]] standing crop to low levels. Algal succession patterns on artificial substrates suggest [[Cladophora]] may require bacterial or other algal growth to initiate colonization.

## Key Findings

**USGS Synoptic Studies**: Data analyses from fall 1990 synoptic studies nearing completion. Final taxonomic samples submitted for identification. Next synoptic study planned for June 18-20, 1991. Need identified for USGS and NAU staff to convene meeting on aquatic invertebrate taxonomy and reach consensus on species identification.

**Arizona Game and Fish Studies**: [[Cladophora]] desiccation studies underway in runways at dam. Artificial substrates showing chlorophyll-a production >100 mg/m² in first run. Fine sediment accumulation observed in tailwaters reach. Reachwide water quality assessment conducted. Test flows believed to have reduced [[Cladophora]] standing crop levels. Food base studies also conducted in [[Little Colorado River]].

**Endangered Fish Studies**: BioWest evaluating [[Humpback Chub]] diet, movement (radiotelemetry), and behavior related to flows and drift. Discussion identified need for better, perhaps synoptic, evaluation of drift. At least two sampling methodologies currently in use: pumping Miller tube method and drift netting with several different mesh sizes. Comparison of methods proposed for [[Lees Ferry]] in fall 1991. System-wide drift assessment proposed for USGS cableways under clear water conditions in June 1992.

**NAU/NPS Macroinvertebrate Study**: Three of seven sampling expeditions completed. More than 1,000 samples processed to date. More than two dozen chironomid species identified. Approximately nine species of simuliids identified, primarily [[Simulium arcticum]] complex. [[Oscillatoria angustissima]] widespread on cobble rocks and fine deposits throughout lower river; role in nitrogen fixation/toxin production and susceptibility to fluctuating discharges unknown.

**Aquatic Ecosystem Energy Model**: Preliminary model developed to predict relationships between potential aquatic productivity and dam operations. Model requires inputs including: (1) channel geometry of each reach, (2) digitized topographic information adapted for incident solar radiation model, (3) caloric values of all biological components (detritus, [[Cladophora]], diatoms, [[Gammarus]], other macroinvertebrates, fish), (4) relationships between tributary sediment inputs and discharge, (5) depth-integrated light changes between reach endpoints, (6) bed material distributions and organic bedload, (7) effects of direct versus indirect light on [[Cladophora]] and diatom growth, (8) standing crop data for all biological components, (9) correlation between Secchi disk readings, suspended loads, and light energy measurements. Model development expected to require several years and may not be available for short-term EIS needs.

**Light Energy Study Proposal**: Proposal presented to evaluate light energy during controlled discharges: constant 15,000 cfs in May 1991 and 5,000 cfs in late June 1991. Data collection to include water chemistry, depth-integrated suspended sediment load, light energy over depth, turbidity, and Secchi disk readings. Equipment loans offered by USGS and other participants.

**Interim Flows Monitoring**: Controlled discharges to conclude end of July 1991. Interim flows (likely reduced fluctuations) may be implemented shortly thereafter. No current study plans address evaluating changes from interim flow implementation. All participants encouraged to consider monitoring protocols for long-term evaluation of change in this dynamic system.

**Next Meeting**: Scheduled for August 9, 1991 in Flagstaff at GCES conference room. Possible Colorado River aquatic Diptera field trip discussed for latter half of September 1991.
