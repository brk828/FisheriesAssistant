# Étude d'une collection d'Isopodes terrestres avec description d'une nouvelle espèce (Porcellio mateusi sp. n.)

#carcinology #Isopoda #Oniscidea #Porcellionidae #terrestrial-crustaceans

> **Note:** This source is a carcinology paper on **terrestrial isopods** (woodlice, Suborder Oniscidea), not fish. Content has been synthesized according to the taxon actually studied.

## Executive Summary

This 1980 monograph by M. Natividade Vieira, published by the Institut de Zoologie «Dr. Augusto Nobre» (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), presents a systematic study of a collection of terrestrial isopods (woodlice) gathered chiefly in northern Portugal (Porto, Amarante, Beja) between January and April 1979, supplemented by revision of unidentified specimens held in the institute's museum collection. The paper's principal contribution is the formal description of a new species, **[[Porcellio mateusi]]**, collected in the Trás-os-Montes region (Carlão). The work also situates this discovery within the sparse historical literature on Portuguese terrestrial isopod fauna, dating back to [[J. F. Brandt|Brandt (1833)]].

## Background & Description

### Historical Context
- The first record of terrestrial isopods in Portugal is attributed to **[[J. F. Brandt]] (1833)**, who reported *Oniscus asellus* and *Porcellio hoffmanseggi*.
- Subsequent contributions were sparse and occasional: **[[Preudhome De Borre]] (1886)**, and passing references in works by Budde-Lund, Dollfus, Eaton, and Verhoeff.
- **[[H. G. Jackson]] (1926)** studied a small collection from Monte Estoril (near Lisbon), collected by E. R. Speyer.
- **[[Arcangeli]] (1935/1958)** examined isopods collected by Silvestri across multiple Portuguese regions (Beja, Serpa, Setúbal, Coimbra, Buçaco, Luso, Porto, Régua, Braga).
- **[[Luisier]] (1936)** is described as a non-original compilation of Arcangeli's list.
- The cave-dwelling (cavernicolous) isopod fauna had previously only been studied via collections by **[[A. Barros Machado]]**, with the broader taxonomic treatment provided by **[[Vandel]] (1946)**.
- The author concludes that Portuguese terrestrial isopod fauna remains understudied, motivating this work.

### Materials and Methods
- Specimens were collected by Prof. **Amílcar Mateus**, **Paulo Fontoura**, and the author herself.
- Collection localities and dates:
  - **Porto** — 1 January 1979, garden, under leaves.
  - **Amarante** — 21 January 1979, garden, under stones.
  - **Beja** — 9 April 1979, garden, under stones.
- Additional material came from unidentified specimens in the **Museum of the Institut de Zoologie «Dr. Augusto Nobre»**.
- Methodology: gross observation with naked eye and stereoscope, dissection, staining of pieces with methylene blue, mounting in Apathy's gum syrup, microscopic observation, and illustration via camera lucida.
- **Sexing challenges**: Specimens were not collected during the reproductive period, so the most reliable character (presence of eggs in the female marsupium) was largely unavailable. Secondary characters (coloration, tegument morphology, body length, caudal appendage length) were noted as unreliable due to confounding with age and environmental conditions. Sex determination was only reliably possible for *Oniscus asellus* collected in late May, when females carried eggs in the marsupium.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All species were collected from **terrestrial microhabitats**: garden soil, under leaves, and under stones — typical refugia for oniscidean isopods requiring moisture retention.
- Geographic distribution data compiled in the paper indicate that several species have wide biogeographic ranges spanning **Western Europe, the Azores, Madeira, Mediterranean regions, the Balkans, North America, Norway, and Finland**, alongside more restricted Iberian distributions.
- The new species, *Porcellio mateusi*, is so far known only from a single locality: **Carlão, Trás-os-Montes**, Portugal — indicating a potentially narrow or under-sampled range.
- The paper underscores that Portugal's terrestrial isopod fauna (including cavernicolous/cave-adapted forms) remains poorly documented, suggesting significant undescribed diversity remains, particularly in less-surveyed inland/mountainous regions like Trás-os-Montes.

## Key Findings

### I. Species Identified in Recent Collections

1. **[[Oniscus asellus]]** (Linné, 1758)
   - Synonyms noted: *Oniscus murarius*, *Oniscus muscorum* (Lereboullet, non Scopoli), *Oniscus affinis*, *Porcellio lineatus*, *Oniscus vicarius*.
   - Juvenile forms historically misidentified as distinct species: *Porcellio laeniola*, *Oniscus fossor*, *Oniscus languidus*, *Oniscus lamperti*.
   - Distribution: Western Europe, Azores, Madeira, Mediterranean regions, Balkans, North America, Norway, Finland, and Portugal (regions north of Coimbra).

2. **[[Armadillo officinalis]]** (Duméril, 1816)
   - Synonyms: *Oniscus glabotor*, *Penthus punctatus*, *Orthonus officinalis*, *Armadillo invenustus*, *Pentheus glabotor*.
   - Widely distributed: Mesopotamia, Western Europe, Mediterranean regions, and across Portugal (Porto, Lisboa, Vila Real, Setúbal, Beja, Faro, Olhão).

3. **[[Porcellio dilatatus]]** (Brandt, 1838)
   - Synonyms: *Porcellio scaber* (Milne-Edwards, non Latreille), *Porcellio granulatus* (Costa, non Lamarck), *Porcellio spinicornis occidentalis*.
   - Distribution: Russia, Turkey, Iceland, Northern Italy, nearly all of Portugal, South and North Africa, North and South America.

4. **[[Porcellio mateusi]] sp. n.** — *newly described species* (see detailed morphology below).

### II. Species Identified from Museum Revision

5. **[[Philoscia muscorum]]** (Scopoli, 1763)
   - Synonyms: *Oniscus muscorum* (Cuvier), *Oniscus sylvestris*, *Philoscia sylvestris*, *Philoscia momorato*, *Pseudoniscus neglectus*.
   - Distribution: Catalonia, Portugal, Sicily.

6. **[[Porcellio incanus]]** (Budde-Lund, 1879–1885)
   - Synonym: *Porcellio marioni* (Aubert & Dollfus, 1890).
   - Distribution: Portugal, Andalusia, Catalonia, France.

### Description of *Porcellio mateusi* sp. n.

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Convex, capable of enrolling into a ball; heavily pigmented, lead-gray color with white lateral patches; microscopic granulations over entire body, denser anteriorly than posteriorly |
| **Size** | Length 12 mm; width 5 mm |
| **Head** | Distinct, rounded, protruding lateral lobes; rounded black frontal lobes; frontal line slightly concave; antennal lobes slightly separated; large rounded eyes with multiple ommatidia |
| **Pereion** | Posterior border of first pereional segments rounded, without posteriorly reflected angles |
| **Pleon** | Lateral points of segments do not reach the posterior border of the following segment; no inflexion at pereion–pleon junction |
| **Telson** | Broad, triangular with rounded angles, no granulations |
| **Antennae** | Long (10 mm); flagellum with 2 cylindrical articles of differing form; long cylindrical setae; enlarged first article |
| **Antennules** | Absent |
| **Mouthparts** | Mandibles lack palps; incisor process with strong dark-brown teeth; molar process short, setose. Maxilla I with internal/external endites bearing a row of teeth; Maxilla II with a guiding lamina. Maxillipeds with bi-articulate coxa, palp, and endite bearing strong teeth and setae |
| **Pereiopods** | Short, cylindrical, short setae on outer face, dactylus much reduced; uniform configuration across legs |
| **Pleopods** | Short, cylindrical, multiple fleshy setae; developed endopodite; exopodite pseudotracheate, directly connected to following segments. First pleopod endopodite strongly curved at tip; exopodite lacks internal point |
| **Uropods** | Flattened, spatula-shaped, large, extending beyond telson tip; protopodite broad; exopodite exceeds endopodite tip; endopodite bears both short and long setae |

### Comparative Taxonomy: *P. mateusi* vs. *Porcellio (Cylisticus) convexus*

The new species was found to be most morphologically similar to **[[Porcellio convexus]]** (= *Cylisticus convexus* Geer), distributed in eastern-to-southeastern France. Key **diagnostic differences**:

- **Coloration**: *P. mateusi* lacks the white mid-dorsal patches present in *P. convexus* (patches restricted to lateral sides only in the new species).
- **Pleon**: In *P. mateusi*, lateral points of abdominal segments are very short and two segments are visibly reduced; in *P. convexus*, segments are short but points still reach the posterior border of the following segment.
- **Ornamentation**: *P. mateusi* bears microscopic granulations from head to pleon; this granulation is absent in *P. convexus*.

### Etymology
The species epithet honors **Prof. Amílcar Mateus**, credited by the author as having inspired her interest in isopod studies.

### Type Locality
**Carlão, Trás-os-Montes**, Portugal — currently the only known locality for *[[Porcellio mateusi]]*.

## Conclusion

The author emphasizes that Portugal's terrestrial isopod fauna is exceptionally rich yet remains largely undocumented, calling for continued taxonomic exploration of this vast domain.
