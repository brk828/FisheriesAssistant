## Executive Summary

This USDA Forest Service memorandum (dated February 1997/98, authored by fishery biologist Rob Stefferud) transmits summary data from fish community surveys conducted on the **upper Verde River**, Arizona, during **spring and autumn 1997** by the [[Rocky Mountain Research Station]]. Sampling occurred at seven sites in spring (Burnt Ranch, FR638, Duff Spring, Bear Siding, Perkinsville, Black Bridge, Sycamore) and two sites in autumn (Burnt Ranch, Sycamore). The dataset documents relative abundance of native Sonoran Desert fishes — including [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Spikedace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Longfin Dace]] — alongside a suite of non-native invasive species, most notably [[Red Shiner]], which numerically dominated several sites. The data were distributed to state (AGFD), federal (BoR, FWS), academic (ASU), and Forest Service stakeholders, indicating relevance to ongoing native fish conservation and Endangered Species Act consultation efforts in the [[Verde River]] watershed.

## Background & Description

The source is a quantitative abundance/habitat dataset rather than a morphological study; no individual specimen measurements (length, weight, meristics) are reported. However, habitat structure data were collected per site as physical context for fish distributions (Table 3), including:

- **Channel length (m)**: site averages ranged 3.02 m (Burnt Ranch) to 6.89 m (Sycamore)
- **Width (m)**: 0.24–0.43 m average
- **Depth (m)**: 0.75–2.67 m average
- **Cross-sectional area (m²)**: 0.30–0.63 m² average
- **Velocity (m/sec)**: 0.24–1.40 m/sec average
- **Total surveyed area**: 8,095 m²; **total volume**: 2,074 m³ across all sites (spring 1997)

Sycamore site exhibited the deepest average channel (2.37 m) and highest flow velocity (1.40 m/sec), consistent with a higher-gradient or canyon-bound reach, while Duff Spring and Bear Siding had comparatively shallow habitat with lower velocities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The upper Verde River supports a mixed native–nonnative fish assemblage typical of Southwestern U.S. warmwater streams undergoing invasion pressure:

- **Native guild**: [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Roundtail Chub]] represent the historic native fish community of the Verde River, a tributary of the [[Salt River]]/[[Gila River]] system within the Colorado River Basin. [[Spikedace]] (federally listed) was included in the survey species list but recorded in negligible or zero numbers at all sites, suggesting extirpation or extreme rarity at these reaches by 1997.
- **Non-native guild**: [[Red Shiner]] (Cyprinella lutrensis) was overwhelmingly dominant at several sites — 95.7% relative abundance at Duff Spring and 96.3% at Perkinsville — indicating substantial biotic homogenization and displacement pressure on natives at these locations. Other established non-natives included [[Common Carp]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Fathead Minnow]], and [[Western Mosquitofish]].
- **Site-level community structure** varied strongly: Burnt Ranch, Bear Siding, Black Bridge, and Sycamore retained higher proportions of native cyprinids and catostomids (e.g., Sycamore: 13.0% longfin dace, 41.3% desert sucker, 17.4% Sonora sucker), whereas Duff Spring and Perkinsville were nearly monotypic red shiner assemblages.
- Seasonal (autumn) resampling at Burnt Ranch and Sycamore showed shifts in relative composition, with desert sucker and red shiner remaining co-dominant, and appearance of smallmouth bass and green sunfish in the autumn catch, suggesting warm-season recruitment or immigration of piscivorous non-natives.

## Key Findings

- **2,888 total fish** were collected across seven spring 1997 sites; **401 total fish** across two autumn 1997 sites (Burnt Ranch, Sycamore).
- **Red shiner** was the single most numerically dominant species basin-wide in spring (2,238 individuals; 77.5% of total catch), driven largely by extreme densities at Duff Spring (830 fish, 95.7%) and Perkinsville (1,222 fish, 96.3%).
- **Native species retained majority representation** at Burnt Ranch (spring: ~83.5% combined natives) and Sycamore (spring: ~71.7% combined natives), suggesting these reaches may serve as native fish refugia relative to red-shiner-dominated sites.
- **[[Spikedace]]**, a species of high conservation concern in the Gila River Basin, was listed in the survey template but effectively absent from both spring and autumn catch data, raising concern about its status in the upper Verde River as of 1997.
- Habitat metrics (Table 3) show considerable physical heterogeneity among sites (velocity range 0.24–1.40 m/sec; depth range 0.75–2.67 m), which may partly explain differential dominance patterns between native benthic/riffle-adapted species and non-native lentic/pool-associated species like red shiner and carp.
- The autumn dataset indicates persistence of red shiner dominance (Burnt Ranch 48.2%; Sycamore 54.7%) alongside continued presence of native desert sucker and Sonora sucker populations, though sample sizes were substantially smaller than spring collections.
- This dataset supports broader regional concerns regarding non-native fish invasion (particularly red shiner and centrarchids) threatening native Sonoran Desert fish assemblages, relevant to management coordination among [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], and academic partners (Arizona State University).
