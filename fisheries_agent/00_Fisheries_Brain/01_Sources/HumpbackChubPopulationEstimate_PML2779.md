# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Cataract Canyon (FY 2005 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This FY 2005 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 130) documents the third and final year of a three-year effort to estimate the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) population in [[Cataract Canyon]], Colorado River, Utah. The project was led by R.A. Valdez & Associates with the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]. Three trammel-netting trips (September 23 to October 29, 2005) captured 23 humpback chub. The provisional whole-canyon estimate was 66 individuals (CV = 0.34; likelihood profile 39–158). The authors concluded that the sample-based estimates probably apply only to the sections sampled, because recaptured fish showed little movement between sites. They recommended that the three-pass mark-recapture estimate not be pursued further.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** The [[Humpback Chub]] is an endangered native fish. Recovery goals identified by the [[Upper Colorado River Recovery Program]] include maintaining populations in several river reaches, among them the Cataract Canyon population.
- **Companion species:** The [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) is also endangered and was present in the samples.
- **Objectives:**
  1. Obtain a population estimate of late juvenile and adult humpback chub in Cataract Canyon.
  2. Determine whether a relationship exists between [[Iswcp Standardized Monitoring Program]] catch rates and population size (the report cites this as "ISMP"; the expansion is not given in the text).
- **Goals:** Produce a population estimate with confidence intervals "as tight as possible," and transport presumed wild bonytail to a hatchery.
- **Schedule:** Initial year 2003, final year 2005. The project was delayed from 2002 to 2003 by record low flows.
- **Program link:** General Recovery Program Support Action Plan, task V.A.1 (conduct a standardized monitoring program).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling conditions**
- Flows during sampling ranged from about 10,000 to 6,500 cfs.
- Daily mean water temperature ranged from 21 °C to 13 °C.
- Trip 1 catch rates were likely reduced by [[Monsoon Flows]] and heavy debris loads in the nets.

**Study sites (river mile, RM)**
- Three primary trend sites: RM 211.5–211, RM 210–209.5, and RM 208–207.
- The RM 207–205 trend site was moved to RM 210 because of low flows in 2003, 2004, and 2005.
- [[Waterhole Canyon]] (RM 198) was added in 2005, below the "big drops," to see whether newly available habitat was being used.

**Movement and mixing**
- Of 13 individuals recaptured between trips, 4 were recaptured after one year and 2 after two years.
- The maximum recorded movement was less than 300 meters. All recaptures occurred in the original large eddy pool complex below Rapid #2.

**Population drivers and concerns**
- The report cites a 5+ year drought and the presence of adult bonytail as possible factors affecting the population.
- The absence of juvenile humpback chub remains an open concern. The authors suggested either that sampling is ineffective for juveniles, or that the population's life history is under stress.

## Key Findings

### Humpback Chub

- **Captures and effort:** 31 humpback chub captures were recorded in 1,375.18 trammel-net hours, a total catch rate of 0.022 fish/net-hour. No humpback chub were collected during 8.2 hours of electrofishing.
- **Individuals:** 23 unique individuals were captured. Mean total length was 229.7 mm (range 195–255 mm TL). Only one was a sub-adult (<200 mm TL).
- **Size trend:** The number of individuals over 250 mm has declined each year since 2003. No fish over 260 mm were captured in 2005.
- **Distribution:** No humpback chub were captured below the "big drops."

### Whole-Canyon Population Estimate (2005)

- Model: Program CAPTURE (within MARK) with the null model (M₀) selected. The authors cite a lack of significant difference in catch rates between trips as support for this choice.
- Input: 23 individuals and 5 recaptures between trips, all sites combined.
- Estimate: **66 individuals** (CV = 0.34), likelihood profile **39–158**.

### Sectional Density Estimates (2005)

| Site | Individuals | Recaptures | Estimate | CV | Profile | Density |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RM 211–209.5 | 17 | 4 | 46 | 0.35 | 27–126 | 92 fish/mile |
| RM 208–207 | 3 | 1 | 6 | 0.50 | 4–65 | 6 fish/mile |

- In 2005, only two humpback chub were recaptured at RM 211–211.5, which corresponds to a minimum density of 4 fish/mile.
- If all habitat above the big drops (about 7 river miles) were assumed to hold 5 fish/mile, the cumulative 2005 estimate would be **22 individuals**.
- In 2003 and 2004, captures were more evenly spread across sites, and cumulative estimates were **285–550** humpback chub in the canyon.

### Three-Year Comparison (Table 2, null model)

| Year | Individuals | Recaptures | Estimate | CV | Likelihood profile | CPUE (fish/net-hr) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2003 | 32 | 3 | 150 | 0.50 | 71–407 | 0.422 |
| 2004 | 28 | 4 | 72 | 0.37 | 39–160 | 0.035 |
| 2005 | 23 | 5 | 66 | 0.34 | 39–158 | 0.022 |

- The 2005 estimate is very similar to 2004 and, in captures and CPUE, similar to 2003. The authors judged the population relatively stable over three years.
- The 2003 and 2004 point estimates, treated as sectional densities of 40–85 fish/mile and extrapolated over about 7 river miles, would give a rough canyon-wide figure of **280–595** humpback chub. The authors present this only as a discussion aid.

### Bonytail

- 5 bonytail were captured in 1,375.18 trammel-net hours, a catch rate of 0.004 fish/net-hour. None were captured by electrofishing.
- Mean total length is given as 228.2 mm, with a stated range of 251–306 mm TL. This mean falls outside the stated range, so it should be checked against the raw data.
- No bonytail were sub-adults, and none were captured below the big drops.

### Overall Catch

- 303 fish of nine species were captured.
- The four main-stem endangered species made up 15% of the total catch.
- Humpback chub was the most common native fish in the samples. Channel catfish was the most abundant species overall.

### Conclusions and Recommendations

- The authors concluded that individuals do not mix among sites within a year. Estimates therefore probably relate only to the three sampled sections, not to the whole canyon.
- A proposed approach of treating estimates as sectional densities across large habitat units was not supported by the recapture data. All recaptures stayed within the same habitat unit.
- **Recommendations:**
  1. Stop further three-pass mark-recapture estimates in Cataract Canyon because of low numbers.
  2. Continue monitoring humpback chub and bonytail distribution and relative abundance with a single sampling pass.
  3. Keep sampling in the fall, following the "3-on-2-off" sampling regime used for other upper basin canyons.
  4. Use trammel nets as the primary sampling tool.

### Project Status and Administration

- **Status:** Complete. This was the third and final year.
- **Budget:** $73,099 funded, $73,099 expended/obligated, $0 difference, 100% of work completed. The report labels this the "FY04 Budget," which appears inconsistent with the FY 2005 report title.
- **Publication charges:** $0 from Recovery Program funds.
- **Data submission:** Data to be entered and transferred to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] by January 15, 2006.
