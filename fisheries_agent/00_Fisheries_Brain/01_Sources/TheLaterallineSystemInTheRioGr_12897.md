# The Lateral-Line System in the Rio Grande Perch, *Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This study describes the cephalic and trunk lateral-line system of the [[Rio Grande Perch]], *Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum*, the only member of the [[Cichlidae]] known to occur naturally in the continental United States. Branson (1961) combines external morphology, osteology, histology, and innervation, comparing the system with those of [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes) and [[Serranidae]] (serranids). The lateral-line system is broadly similar to that of other perciforms, but the study reports several distinctive features: a discontinuous lateralis, a fully tubular set of head bones, a single coronal pore, reduced preopercular and dentary openings, and canal neuromasts with multiple sensory hairs per cell. The author concludes that, although the system looks nearly identical to that of sunfishes and serranids at a gross level, closer examination reveals considerable differences.

## Background & Description

*Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum* (Baird and Girard) is a freshwater cichlid whose natural range extends eastward from the [[Rio Grande]] in Texas to the Rio Colorado near Austin. Specimens were collected on July 14, 1958, from the [[Blanco River]], about 25 miles northwest of San Marcos, and from [[San Marcos Springs]] in Hays County, Texas. Collecting assistance was provided by Dr. Kirk Strawn of Lamar Institute of Technology, and comments were given by Dr. G. A. Moore of [[Oklahoma State University]]. The work was supported by a [[National Science Foundation]] grant (G-4323).

The lateral-line system is a series of sensory canals in the skin and bone of the head and body, plus the lateralis line along the trunk. Its canals contain [[Canal Neuromast]]s, which detect water movement. Because the system is built from dermal bones, the osteological arrangement is central to the study's comparisons.

**Methods**

- **Fixation:** Specimens were fixed in PFA, Allen's modification of [[Bouin's Fluid]], or 10 per cent formalin.
- **Staining and clearing:** Three formalin-fixed specimens were stained by the [[Alizarin Staining]] method (Hollister, 1934). Four large specimens were frozen in dry ice and disarticulated for bone study.
- **Injection:** Dried fish were injected with [[India Ink Injection]] to reveal the canal system.
- **Histology:** Tissue was decalcified in 2% hydrochloric acid, embedded in pyroxylin, cut at 5 µm, and stained with iron hematoxylin or Mallory's triple connective tissue stain. The lachrymal bone was used for sections.
- **Measurement:** A filar micrometer on a compound microscope was used, and drawings were made with a camera lucida.

## Key Findings

### External Morphology

- The cephalic lateral-line system is similar to that of other perciforms. The supratemporal commissure is much abbreviated.
- The coronal pore (median pore of the supraorbital commissure) appears as an isolated opening on the midline, slightly behind the middle of the eye. This is the only canalicular connection across the head. In sunfishes and serranids the pore connects visibly to the rest of the system.
- Unlike sunfishes, where the cephalic canals are usually uncovered, most canals in the Rio Grande perch (all except the supraorbital, infraorbital, and mainly skin-located canaliculi) are covered by scales.

### The Lateralis

- At the junction with the cephalic system there is a ventrally directed canaliculus, a feature reminiscent of [[Pomoxis]] (crappies) but rarely found in sunfishes.
- Each lateralis scale carries a short tube that does not connect with the tubes of other scales. Tubes were injected separately. Neuromasts sit midway along each tube.
- The lateralis ends abruptly under the sixth or seventh dorsal fin ray. Two scale rows lower, a second series begins and continues onto the caudal peduncle.
- The upper series contains 19 tubes and the lower series 10 tubes. The two series overlap by two pored scales. The author suggests this arrangement may be general in cichlids, based on observations in [[Tilapia]], [[Oscar]] (*Astronotus ocellatus*), and [[Angelfish]] (*Pterophyllum eimekei*).

### Osteology

- **Supracleithrum and posttemporal:** The lateral line enters the head through the supracleithrum, and the canal is completely enclosed in bone. The posttemporal has a smooth posterior end, unlike the serrated posttemporals of serranids and [[Archoplites]], and only a single pore, where sunfishes and serranids have two.
- **Supratemporal-intertemporal and lateral extrascapular:** The supratemporal-intertemporal is a small, inverted Y-shaped bone with pores at all three ends. The lateral extrascapular is tubular and slightly sigmoid; in sunfishes these bones are often open channels.
- **Dermopterotic:** This bone has three or four lateral-line openings and is always completely tubular, whereas in sunfishes it is often an open groove.
- **Preopercle:** The preopercular canal has five openings (two terminal and three side branches). The side branches are enclosed by bone, unlike in centrarchids. Serrae on the posterior edge are absent in *Cichlasoma* but present in both serranids and centrarchids.
- **Dentary:** The dentary has four lateral-line openings, whereas most centrarchids have five. The anterior pore opens some distance behind the median suture, unlike in *Lepomis*.
- **Suborbital series:** The six ossicles (dermosphenotic, suborbitals two through five) are largely completely tubular. In sunfishes these are often open channels. A compound jugal and suborbital three/four structure is typical of *Cichlasoma* and was seen in only one anomalous sunfish (*Micropterus coosae*) among several hundred examined.
- **Lachrymal:** The lachrymal is elongated antero-posteriorly, unlike the dorsoventrally elongated centrarchid lachrymal. It has two posterior and two anterior openings and lacks spines.
- **Frontal:** The frontal has five openings and a high median crest. Its coronal pore is directed anteriorly, whereas in centrarchids it is directed caudally. Two branches are enclosed in bone, a feature not seen in centrarchids.
- **Nasal:** The nasal is completely ossified and bent in the middle. Its anterior pore opens near the snout tip.

### Neuromast Histology

- Canal neuromasts are lozenge-shaped and average 409.0 µm in diameter (range 371.0–447.0). They sit on the floor or proximal wall of the lateral-line canal.
- The connective-tissue canal averages 507.4 µm (500.5–514.3). It is surrounded by loose areolar tissue within the bony canal, which measures 762 to 830 µm in diameter.
- Sensory area occupies about 55.5 per cent of the organ's surface and averages 227.0 µm in diameter (202.0–259.7).
- Sensory cells are teardrop-shaped, with basal width 8.4 µm (6.7–9.9) and apical width 5.4 µm (4.2–6.2). Each cell bears three to five sensory hairs averaging 2.18 µm (1.7–3.2) in length. This contrasts with the single hair per cell reported in most other fishes.
- Sustentacular (supporting) cells are tall and slender, averaging 48.9 µm (48.3–57.2) in length. Their apices and, possibly, the sensory cells are linked by [[Intercellular Bridges]], forming a syncytium.
- A cupula, reported in other species, was not observed. The author suggests it may have been lost during fixation or staining.
- The mean number of cells across the base of the organ is 142. Comparative counts for centrarchids are: *Lepomis auritus* 85; *L. megalotis* 53–65; *Acantharchus* 75–80; *Centrarchus* 85–90; *Chaenobryttus* 125–135; and *Pomoxis* 130–135.

### Innervation

- Innervation follows the usual pattern in fishes, from branches of the [[Facial Nerve]] (VII), [[Glossopharyngeal Nerve]] (IX), and [[Vagus Nerve]] (X).
- Each neuromast receives a twig that penetrates the bony canal and enters the organ near its basal center. The twigs are myelinated until they enter the organ.
- The trunk neuromasts are supplied by a long lateralis branch of the vagus that runs in the horizontal septum. Nerve twigs lengthen and shorten as the lateralis curves, and they are short in the caudal peduncle, where the vagus lies directly under the canal.

### Conclusions

- The author concludes that the system is nearly identical to that of sunfishes and serranids at a gross level, apart from the unusual lateralis.
- Closer inspection, especially of the bones, shows considerable differences between *Cichlasoma* and these two families.
- The author notes that *C. cyanoguttatum* is not closely related to the [[Centrarchidae]], despite sharing similar habitat.
