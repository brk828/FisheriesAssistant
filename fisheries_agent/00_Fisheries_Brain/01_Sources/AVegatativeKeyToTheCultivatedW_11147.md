# A Vegetative Key to the Cultivated Woody Plants of the Salt River Valley, Arizona

#botany #plant-taxonomy #arecaceae #cactaceae #dichotomous-key

## Executive Summary

This 1970 publication by Donald J. Pinkava and Elinor Lehto of the [[Arizona State University Herbarium]] is a semi-technical dichotomous key for identifying the woody plants commonly cultivated in the arid [[Salt River Valley]] of Central Arizona. Most of these plants are non-native cultivars from desert, subtropical and tropical regions worldwide. The key relies primarily on vegetative characters so it can be used throughout the growing season. It is organized into an outline key and eight main sections (cacti, palms, grasses and bamboos, yuccas, bladeless-leaved plants, drought-deciduous plants, vines, and broad-leaved plants), and it closes with a glossary and an index of roughly 300 taxa.

## Background & Description

The authors state that the key was designed for amateur and professional plant enthusiasts. Identification uses couplets of opposing statements; the user chooses the statement that better fits the plant and either reaches a scientific name or is directed to another couplet. Couplet numbers in parentheses allow backtracking. A 10X hand lens is recommended for minute structures, especially pubescence.

Key conventions described in the introduction:
- Scientific names are in italics, with the genus followed by the specific epithet.
- An "X" marks interspecific hybrids, with parent species given in parentheses.
- Subspecies (ssp.), varieties (var.), forms (f.) and cultivars (cv., in single quotes) are indicated.
- A single asterisk (*) means closely allied species may also key there but are rarely planted; a double asterisk (**) marks a native species often cultivated; a triple asterisk (***) marks a species naturalized in Arizona.

Limitations noted by the authors include exotic or newly introduced plants not covered, and genera that are difficult to separate by vegetative characters alone: [[Aloe]], [[Citrus]], [[Cupressus]], [[Eucalyptus]], [[Juniperus]], [[Ligustrum]], [[Opuntia]] and [[Rosa]]. Atypical forms (mutants, pruning, pests, disease, shade or irrigation effects, and juvenile foliage) can mislead identification.

The document also contains an acknowledgements section crediting Arizona Flora students and two assistants, a references list of about 20 floras and manuals (including [[Kearney and Peebles Arizona Flora]], Bailey's manuals, Benson's cacti of Arizona, and Rehder's cultivated trees and shrubs), and photographs by Larry Mishler.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The key addresses cultivated, not wild, vegetation. Its scope is the ornamental, landscape and orchard woody flora of an arid, irrigated urban valley. Many taxa listed are native to [[Sonoran Desert]] and southwestern North America (for example [[Saguaro]], [[Creosote Bush]], [[Ocotillo]] and [[Palo Verde]]), while many others come from Mediterranean, Australian, South African, Asian and South American regions. The index records the native range of each taxon and whether it is native to Arizona or naturalized there. Specimens are deposited in the ASU Herbarium.

## Key Findings

- **Structure and scale:** The key covers roughly 300 cultivated woody taxa, arranged into eight sections keyed by leaf type, armature, leaf arrangement and margin form. The index lists each taxon's author, common name, family, native distribution and key position.
- **Cacti (Section 1):** Keyed by areoles, glochids, joint shape and rib pattern. Genera include [[Opuntia]] (prickly pears and chollas), [[Cereus]], [[Ferocactus]] and [[Echinocereus]].
- **Palms (Section 2):** Separated first by palmate versus pinnate venation. Notable taxa include [[Washingtonia Filifera]], [[Washingtonia Robusta]] (Palm Walk, ASU, is a planting of these), [[Phoenix Dactylifera]], [[Sabal Palmetto]] and [[Arecastrum Romanzoffianum]].
- **Grasses and bamboos (Section 3):** Includes bamboos (e.g., [[Phyllostachys]] species, [[Bambusa]] species), [[Arundo Donax]], [[Cortaderia Selloana]] (pampas grass) and [[Typha Latifolia]].
- **Yuccas and agaves (Section 4):** Covers [[Agave Americana]], [[Yucca Elata]], [[Yucca Brevifolia]] (Joshua tree) and [[Hesperaloe Parviflora]].
- **Bladeless and needle-leaved plants (Section 5):** Includes pines, cypresses, junipers, [[Casuarina Cunninghamiana]] and [[Tamarix Aphylla]].
- **Drought-deciduous plants (Section 6), vines (Section 7) and broad-leaved plants (Section 8):** These sections cover taxa such as [[Parkinsonia Aculeata]], [[Fouquieria Splendens]], [[Bougainvillea Spectabilis]], [[Ficus Elastica]] and [[Citrus]] species, with compound, opposite/whorled, alternate, armed and margin-based subdivisions.
- **Difficult groups:** The authors explicitly flag several genera where reproductive parts may be needed for certain identifications.
- **Nomenclature:** Nomenclature follows Mathias and McClintock (1963) and Bailey (1949), with distribution data for difficult groups drawn from Bailey (1949).
- **Purpose and reach:** The key is intended as a practical field tool for the Valley's urban and garden flora and is limited to vegetative characters except where noted.
