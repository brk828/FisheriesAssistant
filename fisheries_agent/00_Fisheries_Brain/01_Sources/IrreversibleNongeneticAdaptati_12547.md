# Irreversible Nongenetic Adaptation in Desert Pupfish Embryos

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 paper by [[Otto Kinne]] (University of Toronto) reports laboratory experiments on the euryhaline [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*). Fertilized eggs were either kept in their spawning salinity or moved 3–6 hr after spawning into a different salinity. Fish hatched from eggs that stayed in the spawning salinity showed higher growth rates and food-conversion efficiencies, and these differences persisted beyond sexual maturity in most comparisons. The author concludes that this is [[Nongenetic Adaptation]] acquired within hours of oviposition, which is at least partly irreversible during the individual's life, and is not inherited by the next generation.

## Background & Description

- **Concept:** Adaptation is defined as adjustments that raise an organism's capacity to survive and reproduce. The paper separates [[Nongenetic Adaptation]], [[Genetic Adaptation]], and [[Sensory Adaptation]], restricting "adaptation" in the strict sense to changes in the response mechanism itself.
- **Prior view:** Functional nongenetic adaptation was generally regarded as fully reversible (Precht, Fisher, Grainger, Prosser and others).
- **Study species:** *C. macularius* (Cyprinodontidae), total length 30–40 mm. It tolerates salinities from fresh water to more than 80 ppt and temperatures from 8° to 39°C. It reproduces between roughly fresh water and 70 ppt at 25–30°C.
- **Study population:** F1 or F2 offspring of fish from a creek mouth on the northwestern shore of the [[Salton Sea]], California.
- **Standard conditions:** Water temperature held within ±0.5°C, salinity within ±1.5 ppt, near air-saturated oxygen, 14-hr photoperiod, pH 7.9–8.3. Fry were fed [[Artemia Salina]] nauplii and later [[Enchytraeus Albidus]] (white worms).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Osmotic mechanism (hypothesized):** The egg capsule (chorion) and plasma membrane are permeable to water and salts during maturation in the ovary. Permeability falls rapidly after oviposition. The perivitelline space forms on contact with the external medium, and it is larger in fresh water than in 35 ppt, which changes egg diameter.
- **Oxygen:** Oxygen solubility varies with salinity, so embryos in fresh and sea water may differ in oxygen availability. The author notes this may contribute to the observed effects.
- **Parental influence:** Parental acclimation and experimental insemination were tested. The salinity at spawning was the decisive factor, so the effects appear to arise at or shortly after oviposition.
- **Broader examples cited:** Salinity-dependent tolerance in [[Gammarus Duebeni]] and [[Crangon Crangon]]; temperature effects on meristic counts in [[Salmo Trutta]] and other fishes; imprinting in salmon homing ([[Salmon Homing]]) and in birds; and host-plant preference in insects.
- **Implications for management:** Introductions and transplantations may succeed or fail depending on the life-cycle stage at which fish are moved, since adaptability declines with age.

## Key Findings

- **Experiment 1 (20°C, 35 ppt spawning):** Fish from eggs moved to fresh water had a mean efficiency of 29.9 versus 28.0 for eggs left in 35 ppt. This difference was not significant (P<0.28). The author argues that growth at 20°C should have favored fresh water, so the lack of difference implies reduced performance in transferred fish.
- **Experiment 2 (20°C, fresh-water spawning):** Efficiency was 42.1 for fish from eggs kept in fresh water and 30.0 for those moved to 35 ppt (P<0.008).
- **Experiment 3 (25°C, 15 ppt spawning):** Mean efficiencies were 16.4 (kept at 15 ppt), 14.9 (moved to 35 ppt), and 10.1 (moved to fresh water). Both differences were significant (P≤0.02 and P≤0.007). Transfer to fresh water caused the larger reduction.
- **Experiment 4 (30°C, fresh-water spawning):** Efficiency was 15.8 in fresh water versus 11.8 in 35 ppt (P≤0.004).
- **Experiment 5 (30°C, 35 ppt spawning):** The result of Experiment 4 reversed. Efficiency was 8.6 in fresh water versus 12.5 in 35 ppt (P≤0.003). In Experiments 4 and 5, efficiency was highest in fish that stayed in the spawning salinity.
- **Mortality:** About 20% from fertilization to hatching, 8% from hatching to 2 months, and 5% from 2 to 7 months.
- **Maturity effects:** Growth declines after sexual maturity. Egg numbers were higher at 15 and 35 ppt than in fresh water. Most early-life differences persisted into later life.
- **Heritability:** Evidence so far indicates the induced differences are not transmitted to the next generation.
- **Temperature and adaptation rate:** Adaptation speed increases with temperature and with growth rate. Capacity to adapt is highest early in development and declines with age and with poor health.
- **Conclusion:** The salinity experienced in the first 3–6 hr after oviposition produces nongenetic adjustments that are at least partly irreversible. These adjustments are ecologically advantageous within the field salinity range.
