# Arizona Canal Fish Salvage Operation, November 1983

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department inter-office memo, dated November 23, 1983, reports a [[Fish Salvage]] operation carried out on November 12, 1983, along the [[Arizona Canal]] of the [[Salt River Project]] (S.R.P.) during the annual fall "dry-up." Eight personnel (six from Region VI and two from the Fish Branch) used a [[Seine Netting]] method to collect fish from six pre-selected canal sites. The fish were moved to three municipal park lakes. The operation met its three objectives and salvaged an estimated 400 pounds of fish. The memo closes with four recommendations for future salvage work.

## Background & Description

- **Author and recipient:** James J. Warnecke, Fisheries Specialist, to Donald M. Turner, Region VI Supervisor.
- **Context:** The canal is drained each fall (the "dry-up"), which strands fish. Salvage removes them and relocates them to ponds and lakes.
- **Objectives (three):**
  1. Identify areas that hold salvageable fish.
  2. Identify fish species and sizes.
  3. Test the survivorship of fish transported to selected municipal park lakes.
- **Methods:**
  - Primary gear was a ¼-inch mesh, 50-foot bag seine (the mesh size is partly illegible in the scan).
  - Each of six pre-selected locations received only one or two "pulls," made in the deepest part of the canal.
  - Captured fish were counted and placed in 200-gallon or 500-gallon transport tanks on site.
  - Fish were stocked into one of three park lakes: El Dorado or McKellips parks (Scottsdale) or Kiwanis Park (Tempe).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The canal is a managed channel that is drained once a year. Fish concentrate in the deepest portions during the dry-up, which is why those areas were targeted.
- **Species assemblage:** The collection was mixed. It included game species (channel catfish, flathead catfish, largemouth bass, bluegill, black crappie, yellow bullhead), nongame or nuisance species (carp, red shiner, threadfin shad), suckers (Gila sucker and a second sucker species), and several species listed in the station key (roundtail chub, tilapia, rainbow trout).
- **Station gradient:** Sampling sites ran along the canal from Granite Reef (Mile 0) to the 56th Street Crossing (Mile 118). Other named sites included the Evergreen Road area, the Indian Bend Siphon, one mile west of the siphon, and the Scottsdale Road Crossing (Mile 116).
- **Stocking destinations:** El Dorado Park Lake, McKellips Park Lake, and Kiwanis Park Lake.
- **Management implication:** Small-grate trash racks could slow the movement of fish into holding pools early in the dry-up, which would make salvage more efficient.

## Key Findings

**Catch composition and size**
- Approximately 95% of the channel catfish that were collected and weighed were fingerlings (3–4 inches).
- The largest channel catfish weighed 4.9 lbs. The largest largemouth bass weighed 3.9 lbs.
- Flathead catfish averaged 3.25 lbs, and the largest weighed 12.2 lbs.
- Average weight for the two sucker species was 0.08 lbs.
- The species totals table reports a grand total of "141g" fish (last digit illegible), excluding shad and shiners. Shad and shiner counts are shown as "3000+" in the totals row.

**Overall yield**
- An estimated 400 pounds of fish were salvaged across the whole operation.

**Objective outcome**
- All three objectives were "successfully met."

**Recommendations**
1. **Use volunteers** from municipal parks and recreation departments, fishing clubs, and non-profit groups, under Department direction, to reduce AGFD costs.
2. **Concentrate repeated seine hauls** in areas that yield the most fish. Department personnel should guard these sites to prevent early removal by the public.
3. **Expand salvage** to the South Canal system during its corresponding dry-up period.
4. **Identify sites** for small-grate trash racks that would restrict downstream fish movement into holding pools during the early dry-up.
