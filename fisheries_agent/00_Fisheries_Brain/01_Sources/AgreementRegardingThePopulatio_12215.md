# Gila Topminnow Reintroduction and Management Agreement - Globe Ranger District

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Poeciliidae #conservation-management

## Executive Summary

This correspondence documents an agreement between the U.S. Forest Service Globe Ranger District and U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service regarding the failed [[Gila Topminnow]] population at [[Sycamore Spring]] and proposed reintroduction into alternative sites with natural water supplies. The population failure resulted from flood damage and delayed maintenance of the artificial water system. Rather than restocking the problematic man-made site, four alternative locations with natural water sources are proposed for Section 7 consultation and potential reintroduction.

## Background & Description

[[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis) is a federally threatened species native to Arizona. The species had been maintained at [[Sycamore Spring]] on the Hicks/Pikes Peak Allotment for 10 years in an artificial water system (trough and pit tank). The population failed in summer 1992 when flood damage to the water system was not repaired promptly enough to prevent desiccation. The site's reliability was compromised by multiple factors: occasional vandalism, recreational use by visitors unaware of its conservation significance, and essential use by livestock permittees for cattle management.

Man-made sites present inherent challenges for maintaining permanent water necessary to sustain fish populations, with failures often going undetected by management agencies or permittees until too late.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposed reintroduction sites represent locations with natural water supplies and improved riparian conditions resulting from changes in livestock management implemented in the early 1980s through rest rotation grazing systems. The approach integrates [[Gila Topminnow]] conservation with continued livestock grazing through riparian pasture management rather than complete livestock exclosures.

Improvements in riparian and aquatic conditions in the [[Winters Allotment]] and [[Sedow Allotment]] demonstrate that compatible management for both threatened fish species and livestock operations is achievable. The strategy anticipates that continued implementation of rest rotation grazing will reveal additional suitable reintroduction sites, potentially contributing to species delisting goals.

## Key Findings

**Failed Site Status:**
- [[Sycamore Spring]] population failed due to flood damage and delayed system repair
- Site provided viable habitat for 10 years but is now considered failed
- Biological Evaluation for Hicks/Pikes Peak Allotments will carry "no effect" determination for threatened and endangered species
- Restocking at this man-made site deemed inadvisable due to maintenance challenges

**Proposed Reintroduction Sites (Section 7 Consultation):**

1. **[[Sevenmile Wash]]** (T.3N., R.16E., Sections 26 and 36, [[Winters Allotment]]): Two high-quality locations identified; consultation to include future fence dividing riparian area into two pastures and possible pipeline/drinker from existing windmill. Third location in [[Sedow Allotment]] between Highway 60 and Winters Fence to be inspected later.

2. **[[Hess Canyon]]** ([[Sedow Allotment]], JU Pasture, T.4N., R.16E., Section 26): Marginal location due to severe flooding but still warranted for introduction; consultation to include division into four smaller pastures with associated water improvements.

3. **[[West Fork of Pinto Creek]]** ([[Brushiest Allotment]] and [[Superstition Wilderness]], T.1N., R.13E., Section 7): Previously inspected and deemed suitable; formal Section 7 consultation completed November 9, 1990. New consultation needed for riparian pasture fence to improve livestock control and enhance riparian/wilderness values in the key introduction area.

4. **[[Arnett Creek]]** ([[Superior Allotment]], T.2S., R.12E., Sections 6, 7, 17): Proposed fish improvement project including fish barrier construction, removal of non-native [[Green Sunfish]], and native species reintroduction. Consultation to include riparian pasture fence development and potential water source outside riparian pasture.

**Management Approach:**
- Riparian pastures will be grazed in compliance with individual allotment management plans, not excluded from livestock use
- Consultation will encompass all ongoing and foreseeable future actions at proposed sites
- Final reintroduction approval rests with Regional Forester
- Additional sites expected to be identified as rest rotation grazing continues improving riparian conditions
