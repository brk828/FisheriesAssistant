## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although no fish/aquatic ecology is present, the report provides a detailed **terrestrial biotic community zonation** framework relevant to broader ecosystem context:

- **[[Creosotebush Scrub]]** (2400–2850 ft): *Larrea divaricata* dominated desertscrub on lower/upper bajada.
- **[[Desertscrub-Grassland Ecotone]]** (2700–3900 ft): Paloverde (*Cercidium microphyllum*, *C. floridum*), saguaro (*Cereus giganteus*), and a **desertscrub riparian community** in arroyos featuring mesquite (*Prosopis juliflora*), netleaf hackberry (*Celtis reticulata*), and desert hackberry (*Celtis pallida*) — the closest analog to an aquatic-adjacent habitat, though strictly ephemeral/riparian, not perennial aquatic.
- **[[Desert Grassland]]** (4100 ft): Gramma grasses (*Bouteloua* spp.), sotol, agave, mesquite.
- **[[Evergreen Woodland]] (Oak Woodland)** (4350–6650 ft): Emory oak (*Quercus emoryi*), Mexican blue oak (*Q. oblongifolia*), Arizona oak (*Q. arizonica*), alligator-bark juniper (*Juniperus deppeana*), Mexican pinyon (*Pinus cembroides*).
- **[[Riparian Deciduous Woodland]]** (Bear Canyon, 5550 ft): Fremont cottonwood (*Populus fremonti*), narrowleaf cottonwood (*P. angustifolia*), arroyo willow (*Salix lasiolepis*), Arizona sycamore (*Platanus racemosa*), velvet ash (*Fraxinus pennsylvanica velutina*), Madrean alder (*Alnus oblongifolia*) — this is the most "aquatic-associated" community in the report, indicating perennial or intermittent stream presence, though no fish are recorded as inhabiting it.
- **[[Ponderosa Pine Forest]]** (5900–7900 ft): *Pinus ponderosa*, silverleaf oak, Douglas-fir at ecotone margins.
- **[[Fir Forest]]** (Mixed Conifer, 7500–9150 ft): White fir (*Abies concolor*), corkbark fir (*A. lasiocarpa*), Douglas-fir (*Pseudotsuga menziesii*), southwestern white pine (*Pinus ayacahuite strobiformis*), bigtooth maple (*Acer grandidentatum*), Gambel oak (*Quercus gambelii*) at the pine-fir ecotone.

This elevational community sequence exemplifies the **"mountain island" biogeographic model** relevant to Madrean Sky Island ecosystems, providing terrestrial context that could bound any future aquatic/fish survey of streams such as Sabino Creek or Bear Canyon within the same range.
