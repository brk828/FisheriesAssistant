# Fish Monitoring of Selected Streams of the Gila River Basin, 2008

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This final annual report documents autumn-winter 2008 fish monitoring in the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], [[San Pedro River]], [[Aravaipa Creek]] and [[Cienega Creek]] in Arizona. The work was conducted by Marsh & Associates, LLC for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under a [[Central Arizona Project]] conservation measure, which requires baseline monitoring of non-native fish movement into waters occupied by native species. A total of 2,825 individuals of six native and eleven non-native species was recorded in quantitative sampling. No new species or distributional records were found. Most species were uncommon to rare at most sites.

## Background & Description

The [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP), completed in 1993, allowed non-native fishes and other aquatic biota to move from [[Lake Havasu]] into central and southern Arizona. [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] biological opinions concluded that such movement would negatively affect several listed species. Reclamation's monitoring plan (Clarkson 1996) calls for annual sampling at a nominal set of stations across eight reaches of three streams, with two [[Cienega Creek]] stations added in 2007. [[Aravaipa Creek]] is monitored in relation to a barrier installed in 2001 to exclude non-native fishes.

Standard methods included backpack and boat electrofishing, seines, trammel nets and dip nets. Catch per unit effort (CPE) was expressed as fish per 1,000 seconds of electrofishing (fish per k-sec). Species were identified by four-letter codes (Table 2), and natives are marked with an asterisk.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow conditions:** Gila River discharge ranged from about 3.7 cfs below [[Coolidge Dam]] to 54 cfs at Kelvin. Salt River flow below Stewart Mountain Dam was about 350 cfs, well above the typical 8-10 cfs for the season. San Pedro River sites ranged from dry to about 14 cfs following September and October floods, and Cienega Creek ranged from dry to about 0.5 cfs.
- **Access constraints:** Both upper Gila River stations (Coolidge Dam and Hook & Line Ranch) could not be sampled because the [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] did not grant access. The Box-O Wash station was replaced by an alternative "Price" site, which is not recommended as permanent due to a 1.6 km hike.
- **Non-native fauna:** Non-native [[American Bullfrog]] and [[Northern Crayfish]] were widespread. Native [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] and [[Sonora Mud Turtle]] were also recorded.
- **Habitat:** Sites ranged from sandy, turbid, deeply incised reaches (San Pedro) to cobble-gravel riffles (Aravaipa, upper Gila) and mud-dominated pools (Salt, Cienega).

## Key Findings

**Overall catch**
- 2,825 individuals; six native and eleven non-native species.
- Natives: [[Loach Minnow]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Gila Topminnow]].
- Non-natives: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Common Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Western Mosquitofish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]].
- Combined standardized backpack and boat electrofishing effort was 14,972 sec, with an overall CPE of 189 fish per k-sec.

**Gila River** (seven species, all non-native; 308 fish; 6,815 sec; CPE 45)
- Dominant: [[Western Mosquitofish]] (40%), followed by channel catfish (19%), red shiner (19%), carp (8%).
- Upper and upper-middle reaches had no native fishes; a Sonora mud turtle was caught at Dripping Springs Wash.
- Lower-middle reach CPE was 16; lower reach CPE was 11 (channel catfish 92%). Low catches were attributed to high discharge.

**Salt River** (one of three sites sampled; four species; 32 fish; 973 sec; CPE 33)
- [[Largemouth Bass]] 76% (per the detailed results; 69% in the summary), rainbow trout 21%, yellow bullhead 3%.
- Three trammel net sets captured three large Sonora suckers (CPE 1.0 fish per net-hour).
- Upstream sites were not sampled because of high water; a bald eagle nest near the Goldfield site was a potential disturbance issue.

**San Pedro River** (nine species in the detailed results, with two native; 482 fish; 5,155 sec; CPE 94)
- Longfin dace was dominant (86%); black bullhead 6%, desert sucker 4%.
- Flathead catfish were absent in 2007 and 2008.
- Native lowland leopard frogs were found at multiple sites; the Hughes Ranch site was dry.

**Aravaipa Creek** (quantitative catch 1,248 fish, 1,342 sec, CPE 930; nine species, five native)
- Longfin dace 74%, desert sucker 11%, loach minnow 6%, Sonora sucker 4%, red shiner 3%, roundtail chub 2%.
- Entanglement netting below the lower barrier captured 12 fish (roundtail chub 42%, yellow bullhead 33%, carp 25%).
- No non-native fish were recorded above the barriers, consistent with the barrier's purpose.

**Cienega Creek** (two native species; 687 fish; 765 sec; CPE 898)
- Longfin dace 86%, Gila topminnow 14%.
- Qualitative collections of thousands of fish were all longfin dace.
- The Head Cut station had CPE 960 and the 3-Bridges station CPE 840.

**Recommendations and records**
- Evaluate and assess an alternative to the Box-O and Price sites before the 2009 monitoring period.
- Two voucher specimens (fathead minnow, green sunfish) were deposited at the ASU Museum.
- Collections were permitted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
