# Region III Fisheries Survey of the Verde River-Stillman Lake, September 25, 2001

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] Region III Fisheries Program surveyed about 0.7 mile of the [[Verde River]] upstream of the [[Granite Creek]] confluence, locally known as [[Stillman Lake]], on September 25, 2001. The survey tested whether the fish community there differed from the river below Granite Creek. The most significant result was the capture of large [[Flathead Catfish]] (six to twenty pounds), the farthest upstream record of this non-native species to date. The survey also found no [[Yellow Bullhead]] or [[Black Bullhead]], which are common downstream. The author recommends initiating removal of flathead catfish and yellow bullhead to protect the river's native fish management goals, though the report notes that cost and staffing limits constrain this work.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** About 0.7 mile of the Upper Verde River. The bottom 0.3 mile lies within the [[Upper Verde River Wildlife Area]] (Granite Creek parcel), owned by the Arizona Game & Fish Department. Few prior samples were taken there because equipment access is difficult.
- **Isolation:** An alluvial fan built by Granite Creek forms a dam-like barrier to surface flow, isolating Stillman Lake from the river for most years. The report states it is unclear how and how often water connects the lake with the rest of the river.
- **Physical conditions:** Depths were 6-8 feet, and water was turbid.
- **Purpose:** To determine whether the fish community in Stillman Lake differed from the river below Granite Creek.
- **Personnel:** Local Wildlife Manager Scott Poppelberger and Aquatic Habitat Specialist Sam Sponhortz assisted. Several classes of local elementary and junior high students from [[Chino Valley]] observed the survey, and some helped weigh, measure, and record data.

### Sampling Methods

| Method | Effort / Details |
|---|---|
| [[Gillnet Sampling]] | Six experimental gillnets, fished in varying shoreline habitats |
| [[Hoop Net]] Sampling | Nine hoop nets, fished in varying shoreline habitats |
| [[Electrofishing]] | Smith-Root 5.0 GPP unit mounted in a canoe; 52.7 minutes of active pedal time |

- **Conductivity:** Recorded at 171 µS/cm (the OCR-damaged source reads "17lpS," so this value is approximate).
- **Catfish settings (most of the effort):** 15 pulses per second, 1-2 amps, 900 volts.
- **Bass and sunfish settings:** Pulse rate was partly illegible in the source (apparently 120 pulses per second), with 3-4 amps at 900 volts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Downstream community:** Black bass, [[Yellow Bullhead]], and [[Black Bullhead]] make up a large portion of the fish community downstream of Granite Creek. Common carp, red shiner, and young-of-the-year [[Green Sunfish]] were also present there. Large green sunfish were absent from Stillman Lake, which is the one exception to the downstream pattern.
- **Stillman Lake community:** Common carp, [[Red Shiner]], and young-of-the-year green sunfish were plentiful but were not enumerated. Small [[Channel Catfish]] were also collected.
- **Flathead catfish distribution:** Locals reported large catfish in Stillman Lake for several years. The survey indicates flathead catfish have been present in this stretch for at least 8-10 years. The report notes it is unclear why they have not invaded pools downstream from Granite Creek to Verde Ranch, although several large beaver ponds and pools exist in that stretch.
- **Regional context:** Flathead catfish have been sampled in the Verde River below [[Verde Ranch]] in the Duff Canyon area, but they are not common in the upper Verde above [[Camp Verde]].
- **Management goal:** The upper Verde River is managed primarily for native fish species, and angler access to the fishery is very limited.

## Key Findings

1. **Flathead catfish are established upstream.** Flathead catfish were captured by gillnet and electrofishing, including fish of six to twenty pounds. Several juveniles were also taken. This is the farthest upstream record for the species to date, and it has likely been present for at least 8-10 years.
2. **Bullheads and some downstream species were absent.** Despite gear targeting bass and sunfish, no yellow or black bullheads were captured. Large green sunfish were also absent, which distinguishes Stillman Lake from the downstream community.
3. **Non-native species are prevalent.** Common carp, red shiner, and young green sunfish were abundant but not enumerated.
4. **Stillman Lake is isolated and hard to sample.** Access logistics limited sampling, and the connection to the river is uncertain.
5. **Recommended action:** Efforts should begin to remove problematic non-native species, specifically flathead catfish and yellow bullhead. Removal techniques demonstrated to work include electrofishing, gillnets, hoop nets, and setlines. Costs are high, and the author notes that funds and manpower are limited in Region III. Cooperative efforts with the Arizona cooperative fisheries program and partners are suggested as a way to share costs.
