# Fish Survey of Salt River Project Canals in the Phoenix Metropolitan Area, Arizona (1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This summary report to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] documents a 1995 fish survey of two major irrigation canals operated by the [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) in the [[Phoenix Metropolitan Area]]: the [[Arizona Canal]] and the [[South Canal]]. Twenty-two fish species were collected or reliably observed across the two canals, 20 in the Arizona Canal and 18 in the South Canal. The fauna is dominated by introduced species, with some native catostomids and cyprinids persisting. The author reports no substantive change in the ichthyofauna since the last published account (Marsh and Minckley, 1982), apart from the stocking of grass carp.

## Background & Description

The Phoenix metropolitan canal system is operated by SRP and delivers water for irrigation, domestic, and industrial use. Head works are at [[Granite Reef Dam]], where Salt and Verde river water is diverted into two concrete-lined mainline canals. The Arizona Canal serves the north side of the Salt River channel, and the South Canal serves the south side.

The canals contain fishes that move in from natural watercourses upstream, fishes stocked by SRP, and fishes released by the public, whether deliberately or accidentally. Sampling is difficult and hazardous while water is flowing. The system is therefore sampled during periodic dewaterings for inspection, maintenance, and repair, which are typically scheduled in autumn and winter. Stranded fishes are easily captured at these times, though the author notes that the resulting data are qualitative.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Survey sites.** The Arizona Canal was inspected in its entirety from the [[Electrical Fish Barrier]] below Granite Reef Dam downstream to the Indian Bend Wash siphon, with collections at several locations including below the barrier, near Greenfield Road, downstream of Scottsdale Road and Gilbert Road, and above the Indian Bend Wash siphon. The South Canal was inspected from the electrical fish barrier to its terminus at the conjunction of the Tempe and Consolidated canals. Its origin was also examined downstream about 7 km. Collection sites included the Central Arizona Project connection, the River Road siphon, the Roosevelt Water Conservation District pumping plant, and Lindsay Road.

**Methods.** The Arizona Canal was surveyed on 25–26 November 1995 and the South Canal on 21–22 October 1995. Vehicles drove along canal roads to inspect structures. Gear included:
- Seines with 3.2 or 6.4 mm mesh (3.0 or 4.6 m long, 1.2 or 1.8 m deep)
- Trammel nets (23 or 25 m long, 1.8 m deep, 25 mm inner and 305 mm outer mesh)
- Dip nets (3.2 mm mesh) and hand capture

All fishes were identified to species and enumerated. Subsamples were measured for total length, and selected specimens were photographed or fixed in 10% formalin as vouchers for the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]].

**Relative abundance.** Abundance was estimated from collection counts and visual assessment, expressed as a percentage of total fishes: rare (<2%), uncommon (3–5%), common (6–20%), and abundant (>21%). Taxa were recorded with both canals' abundance categories in Table 1 of the report.

**Survey partners.** Personnel from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the Bureau of Reclamation, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, plus SRP and other interested parties, participated in the sampling. Fish collected in the reach between Granite Reef Dam and the electrical barrier were noted separately.

## Key Findings

**Species richness.** Twenty-two species were collected across the two canals in 1995. Of these, 20 were recorded in the Arizona Canal and 18 in the South Canal. There were no distinctive differences in richness or relative abundance between the two canals. The total fish species count for the Phoenix metropolitan area now stands at 38.

**Native species.** Three native species were encountered in both canals: [[Roundtail Chub]] (rare), [[Desert Sucker]] (abundant), and [[Sonora Sucker]] (abundant). Juvenile and adult life stages of all native species were collected.

**Dominant and common introduced species.**
- [[Red Shiner]] was the only introduced species classed as abundant in both canals.
- [[Grass Carp]], stocked by SRP, [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Mosquitofish]] were common in both canals.
- Other introduced species were uncommon or rare.

**Comparison with Marsh and Minckley (1982).** The earlier account recorded 23 species. Four species found in 1995 were not in that list: [[Bigmouth Buffalo]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Walleye]], and grass carp. The author notes that the first three occur upstream, and grass carp were intentionally stocked (the earlier report listed stocked hybrid grass × bighead carp). Six species from the 1982 list were not encountered in 1995: native longfin dace (considered rare), and introduced fathead minnow, black bullhead, yellow bullhead, redear sunfish, and [[Mozambique Tilapia]]. These probably persist in the canal system or upstream but were missed because of their rarity.

**Overall conclusion.** Apart from the stocking of grass carp, the author reports no evidence of substantive change in the ichthyofauna since the last published report on these waterways.
