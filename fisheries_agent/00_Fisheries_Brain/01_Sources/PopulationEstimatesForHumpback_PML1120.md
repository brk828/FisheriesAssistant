# Population Estimates for Humpback Chub and Roundtail Chub in Westwater Canyon, Colorado River, Utah, 1998-2000

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Utah Division of Wildlife Resources final report (Publication 03-5, December 2003) estimates adult [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) abundance in [[Westwater Canyon]] on the [[Colorado River]] for 1998-2000. The study was conducted under the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] (Project 22c, Task 4) to provide point estimates for judging progress toward downlisting and delisting the [[Humpback Chub]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

Humpback chub adult abundance declined from 4,744 (1998) to 2,215 (1999) and 2,201 (2000). The decline was not statistically significant but may be of concern. Roundtail chub abundance was relatively stable at 5,005, 4,234 and 4,971. Trammel-net catch per unit effort (CPUE) for humpback chub declined significantly, and roundtail chub CPUE showed a non-significant decline. Movement data indicate exchange between [[Black Rocks]] and Westwater Canyon frequent enough to treat the two areas as one population for each species.

## Background & Description

*Gila cypha* was first described in 1945 (Miller 1946). Declines in distribution and abundance led to federal listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. The 1990 [[Humpback Chub]] recovery plan was amended in 2002, establishing measurable downlisting and delisting criteria. One criterion is maintaining a core population of at least 2,100 adults (>200 mm) for five consecutive years in one of five upper-basin populations: Black Rocks, Westwater Canyon, Cataract Canyon, Yampa Canyon, and Desolation/Gray Canyon.

Previous Westwater Canyon estimates (Chart and Lentsch 1999) were judged relatively unreliable because the sampling design was not built for population estimation. This study was designed to correct that, with a more intensive sampling program and a more rigid design. Objectives were to estimate adult (≥200 mm) [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Roundtail Chub]] populations.

Species were identified using diagnostic characters, including degree of frontal depression, scales on the nuchal hump, and the "angle of the dangle." Recorded data included total length, weight, sex (mature fish, 1998 and 2000), fin ray counts, and [[PIT Tagging]] numbers for fish over 150 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Westwater Canyon is about 12 river miles long, cut into Proterozoic gneiss and granite. The upper section has runs, eddies and pools between riffles and rapids. The steep middle section (RM 119.5-116.5) was not sampled because of turbulent flows and Class III rapids, although humpback chub were present there in 1979-1981. The lower section is a confined, low-gradient homogeneous run where chubs are scarce.

**Sampling sites.** Three sites previously established for the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] were sampled: Miners Cabin (RM 124.1-123.8; maximum depth 21.8 m), Cougar Bar (RM 121.5-121.0; 19.5 m), and Hades Bar (RM 120.0-119.8; 10.6 m). Each deep habitat is bounded by a riffle.

**Sampling schedule.** Sampling occurred in September and October each year. Three passes were made per year, eight days apart. Miners Cabin and Cougar Bar were each sampled for two nights per pass and Hades Bar for one night.

**Gear.** [[Trammel Netting]] used 23 m × 2 m nets with 2.5 cm and 1.25 cm mesh, set mainly in deep eddies behind boulders or rock faces. [[Electrofishing]] (pulsed DC) was done once per year at each site to follow the ISMP protocol.

**Environmental data.** Appendix III reports daily discharge, water temperature and suspended sediment for Fall 1998-2000 at USGS gages near the Colorado-Utah state line (#09163500) and near Cisco (#09180500). Discharge in September-October ranged from roughly 2,900 to 8,000 cfs across the three years, and water temperatures fell from about 20 °C to about 9-11 °C over the sampling period. The full daily tables are in the source report.

## Key Findings

### Population estimates

Estimates used the null estimator (M₀) in [[Program CAPTURE]], chosen after model-selection results varied by year and after consultation with Dr. Ron Ryel (Utah State University).

| Year | Humpback Chub (N) | CV | p-hat | Roundtail Chub (N) | CV | p-hat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1998 | 4,744 | 0.23 | 0.035 | 5,005 | 0.30 | 0.026 |
| 1999 | 2,215 | 0.28 | 0.041 | 4,234 | 0.23 | 0.037 |
| 2000 | 2,201 | 0.28 | 0.041 | 4,971 | 0.25 | 0.031 |

- Humpback chub profile-likelihood intervals: 3,760-14,665 (1998), 1,608-7,509 (1999), 1,335-4,124 (2000).
- Humpback chub declined 1998-1999 and held steady in 2000. The short-term trend slope did not significantly differ from zero, and every point estimate exceeded the 2,100-adult minimum viable population.
- Roundtail chub estimates were stable, showing short-term stability (Figure 7).
- Tighter intervals in 2000 came from higher trammel effort (1,951 h in 2000 vs. 1,329 h in 1998 and 1,306 h in 1999), not from better precision: CV and p-hat did not improve.

### Trammel-net captures and CPUE

- Total humpback chub trammel captures: 501 (1998), about 278 (1999), 277 (2000).
- Total roundtail chub trammel captures: 387 (1998), 481 (1999), 521 (2000); subadult roundtail captures reached a project high of 53 in 2000.
- Humpback chub CPUE declined significantly across passes and years (p < 0.05).
- Roundtail chub CPUE declined slightly but not significantly, and CPUE rose among passes within years.

### Length-frequency

- Humpback chub size structure was bimodal in 1998-1999 and trimodal in 2000 (modes at 230-260, 280-310, and 340-360 mm).
- The smallest humpback chub captured rose to 410 mm in 2000.
- Roundtail chub showed no clear size-class separation in 1998-1999 and a bimodal pattern in 2000.

### Growth

- Humpback chub grew more slowly than roundtail chub. Humpback chub under 285 mm grew about twice as fast as those over 285 mm (mean annual growth of 10.58 ± 1.92 mm for fish under 285 mm).
- Roundtail chub under 210 mm grew roughly 46 mm per year, and the study noted similar size-dependent patterns to [[Chart and Lentsch]] (1999).
- Growth differences between the two Westwater studies were attributed to temperature, water year and food base.

### Movement

- About 20% of humpback chub and 11% of roundtail chub recaptures were in a different location.
- Humpback chub moved more among Miners Cabin, Cougar Bar and Hades Bar than roundtail chub did.
- Three humpback and three roundtail chub tagged at [[Black Rocks]] were recaptured in Westwater Canyon, and one humpback chub tagged in Westwater was recaptured at Black Rocks (about 10.5 miles).
- The authors judged the exchange frequent enough to exceed the one-migrant-per-generation threshold for preventing genetic differentiation (Mills and Allendorf 1996). They recommended treating Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon as one population.
- This exchange violates the closure assumption of the models used.

### Comparison with ISMP

- Historic ISMP CPUE for humpback chub (1988-2000) declined significantly.
- Historic ISMP CPUE for roundtail chub showed a slight, non-significant decline.
- Statistical comparisons were made with the [[Kruskal-Wallis Test]] and [[Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test]].
- The authors cautiously suggested CPUE may track adult humpback chub abundance, based on limited data.

### Conclusions

1. Adult humpback chub declined from 1998 to 2000, though not significantly.
2. Adult roundtail chub was stable from 1998 to 2000.
3. More trammel effort gave tighter intervals but no real gain in capture probability or CV.
4. Humpback chub growth is slower than roundtail chub growth.
5. Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon should be treated as a single population for each species.

### Recommendations

- Increase trammel effort in Westwater Canyon.
- Add [[Hoop Netting]] and electrofishing to improve capture probability and CV.
- Use smaller-mesh nets, hoop nets, minnow traps and electrofishing to target subadult humpback chub.
- Add a fourth pass and sampling sites (e.g., upper Cougar Bar, Big Hole).
- Analyze Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon as one population with one estimate per year.
- Evaluate alternative statistical programs and models, including [[Closed Population Models]].
- Add a [[Radiotelemetry]] component to clarify within- and among-site movement.
