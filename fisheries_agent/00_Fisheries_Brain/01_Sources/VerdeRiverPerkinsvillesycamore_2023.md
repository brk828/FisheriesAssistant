# Verde River: Perkinsville-Sycamore Creek Fish Survey Report

#ichthyology #Catostomidae #Centrarchidae #riverine_fish #native_species_conservation

## Survey Objectives & Target Population

**Primary Sampling Purpose**: Continue monitoring species occurrence and abundance in the upper [[Verde River]] in compliance with statewide protocol standards and statewide sportfish stocking consultation conservation measures. This survey represents the third in a series (2016, 2019, 2023) designed to track changes in fish community composition over time.

**Target Population & Spatial Boundaries**: The entire fish community of a 20 km (12.5 mi) section of the upper [[Verde River]], starting approximately 1.5 miles downstream of the [[Perkinsville Bridge]] and ending at the [[Sycamore Creek]] confluence. The survey area spans river miles 26.50 to 36.90 and flows through private, state, tribal, and United States Forest Service (USFS) lands. The target population includes both native species ([[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Longfin Dace]]) and non-native species ([[Redeye Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Red Shiner]]).

## Gear Specifications & Standardization

**Equipment & Mesh/Gear Parameters**: Smith-Root canoe-mounted electrofishing unit equipped with a single sphere anode and four cathode tails. A deadman switch was operated by hand on top of the generator. Support canoes accompanied the electrofishing canoe to position netters downstream for fish collection. No mesh specifications reported for dip nets.

**Deployment & Effort Standardization**: Each site was 200 meters in length. The canoe was maneuvered in and around available habitats with one individual operating the deadman switch and canoe while netters were positioned downstream. In areas where water was too deep, the electrofishing unit was operated from inside the canoes (floated) and followed by support canoes. Nine sites were sampled from outside the canoes (wading), and two were sampled from inside the canoes. Of the two floated sites, only one was completely sampled from inside the canoe; the other was sampled half from inside and half from outside. Total sampling effort was 158.67 minutes across 11 sites (range: 8.5 to 21.6 minutes per site). Effort standardized as CPUE (catch per 15 minutes).

**Selectivity & Bias Considerations**: Electrofishing selectivity varies by species size, conductivity, and habitat type. Deep water areas required floating the electrofishing unit, which may reduce capture efficiency compared to wading. Site 1 was not sampled due to landowner/access issues; site 4Alt was substituted. The majority of fish captured were < 200 mm, suggesting potential size-related selectivity or actual population size structure.

## Experimental & Spatial Design Framework

**Stratification & Allocation**: A total of 11 sites were selected: three fixed sites (Sites 2, 7, and 9) and eight random sites generated using Expert GPS and a random number generator. Fixed sites allow for temporal comparisons across survey years (2016, 2019, 2023). Random sites provide unbiased spatial coverage of the 20 km reach. Sites were distributed across approximately 10.4 river miles (26.50 to 36.90). No explicit habitat stratification reported.

**Temporal Scheduling**: Survey conducted June 13-15, 2023, over three consecutive days. Day 1 (June 13): 11.5 trip hours, 4.5 miles, 4 sites, 524 fish, 47.65 minutes effort. Day 2 (June 14): 9.5 trip hours, 5 miles, 4 sites, 328 fish, 60 minutes effort. Day 3 (June 15): 11 trip hours, 2.5 miles, 3 sites, 563 fish, 51.02 minutes effort. Total trip hours (32 hours) reflect actual float time and do not include vehicle shuttle, load, and unload time. Survey timing (mid-June) represents post-spring runoff conditions. Triennial survey frequency allows tracking of community responses to sustained high flow events.

**Detection & Bias Mitigation**: Multiple netters positioned downstream of the electrofishing canoe to maximize capture probability. Standardized CPUE calculations (fish per 15 minutes) allow comparison across sites with variable effort. Fixed sites enable detection of temporal trends while controlling for site-specific variation. Random site selection reduces spatial bias. Access limitations (Site 1) represent a potential source of bias if inaccessible areas differ systematically from accessible areas.

## Field Data Schema & Variable Definitions

**Primary Biological Measurements**: Species identification to scientific name level. Total length (mm) measured for all captured fish: [[Desert Sucker]] (min 25, max 381, mean 176.36 mm, SE 0.1); [[Sonora Sucker]] (min 116, max 430, mean 259.77 mm, SE 0.36); [[Roundtail Chub]] (min 107, max 357, mean 221.35 mm, SE 0.58); [[Speckled Dace]] (min 72, max 86, mean 77.75 mm, SE 0.14); [[Redeye Bass]] (min 97, max 237, mean 158.74 mm, SE 0.29); [[Green Sunfish]] (min 97, max 136, mean 115 mm, SE 0.32); [[Yellow Bullhead]] (min 152, max 185, mean 162.86 mm, SE 0.2); [[Black Bullhead]] (135 mm, n=1). [[Longfin Dace]] lengths not reported. Count data: number of individuals per species per site. CPUE calculated as (count / sampling minutes) × 15. Percent composition calculated for each species at each site and overall.

**Environmental & Spatial Covariates**: River mile location for each site (approximate). Date of sampling. Sampling effort (minutes) per site. Distance traveled per day (miles). Habitat type implicitly recorded (deep vs. wadeable). Flow regime context discussed qualitatively: sustained high flow events in winter 2018-2019 and winter 2022-2023 noted as important drivers of community composition. No quantitative discharge, temperature, conductivity, or other water quality parameters reported.

**QA/QC & Data Entry Protocols**: Standard error calculated for CPUE and mean length measurements. Species identifications presumably verified by experienced personnel (Arizona Game and Fish Department Region III Aquatics Program). Data summarized in standardized tables showing site-level counts, percentages, CPUE, and length statistics. Fixed sites allow cross-year validation. No explicit mention of voucher specimens, field data sheets, or database protocols.

## Analytical Linkages: Downstream Model Compatibility

This protocol feeds: #community_composition_analysis, #CPUE_standardization, #temporal_trend_analysis, #native_vs_nonnative_ratios, #length_frequency_distributions, #flow_regime_correlations, #species_occurrence_modeling, #relative_abundance_indices, #conservation_status_assessment, #stocking_consultation_measures. The survey design explicitly supports comparison with 2016 and 2019 surveys to detect changes in native fish abundance (83.75% native in 2023 vs. 81% in 2019 vs. 22% in 2016) correlated with sustained high flow events. Data structure (site-level counts, CPUE, lengths) is compatible with multivariate community analyses, GLMs for abundance trends, and size-structure assessments. The protocol addresses ecological hypotheses regarding flow regime effects on native vs. non-native fish dynamics (Poff and Allen 1995; Propst et al. 2008; Rinne and Stefferud 1998), specifically that sustained high flow events reduce non-native species and allow native species to rebound.
