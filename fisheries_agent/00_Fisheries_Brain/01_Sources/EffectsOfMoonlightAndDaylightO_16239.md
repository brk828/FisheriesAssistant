# Effects of Moonlight and Daylight on Hydroacoustic Estimates of Pelagic Fish Abundance

#ichthyology #marine-biology #prosopium

## Executive Summary

This study examines how ambient light, set by lunar phase and time of day, biases [[Hydroacoustic Survey]] estimates of pelagic fish abundance. Surveys were run in [[Bear Lake]] (Utah–Idaho), where the dominant target was the [[Bonneville Cisco]] (*Prosopium gemmifer*). Acoustic density and biomass during [[Full Moon]] periods were approximately 50% of values during [[New Moon]] periods. A diel survey during a new moon showed fish dispersed at night and schooling at dawn, which made [[Echo Counting]] unreliable in daylight. The authors conclude that light conditions must be standardized for consistent, comparable population estimates of some pelagic fishes.

## Background & Description

Acoustic methods are increasingly used to estimate fish abundance, track population changes, quantify forage fish available to piscivores, and map spatial and temporal distributions. The authors note that acoustic results depend on fish behavior, which varies with environmental conditions.

Light intensity strongly shapes pelagic fish behavior. It affects vertical distribution and diel movement, foraging, and spawning, and the cited literature documents responses to both diel and lunar light cycles (see [[Diel Vertical Migration]] and [[Lunar Cycle]]). Changes in fish distribution can alter the acoustic signal, the depth range analyzed, and the ability to separate targets from one another and from the bottom. The study addresses two sources of variation:

1. Vertical distribution of Bonneville ciscoes under full- versus new-moon conditions at night.
2. Diel changes in spatial distribution, sampled along one transect from night through the following morning.

**Target species**

- **Bonneville cisco (*Prosopium gemmifer*):** the primary pelagic target. Standard lengths ranged from 20 to 200 mm in midwater catches.
- **Bear Lake sculpin (*Cottus extensus*):** larval stages were present in midwater catches, standard lengths 10–25 mm. Their target strengths resembled those of zooplankton, so these small targets were excluded from analysis.

Over 98% of fish captured in midwater trawling were Bonneville ciscoes or larval sculpins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area: [[Bear Lake]]**

- Large, oligotrophic lake: 282 km², elevation 1,805 m, in mountainous terrain on the Utah–Idaho border.
- Summer chlorophyll *a* was low, at 0.5 µg/L. Secchi depths were limited to 2–7 m by marl precipitates.
- The top of the [[Thermocline]] lies at 10–15 m in summer.
- Oxygen rarely falls below 5 mg/L in the deep profundal zone.
- The lake bottom is smooth, with few macrophytes that could confound acoustic analysis.

**Light regime**

- Light at depth was estimated from vertical extinction coefficients measured to 25 m with a LiCor radiometer.
- During new-moon surveys, [[Light Intensity]] at fish depths was low; during the diel survey, only starlight illuminated the lake under reduced transparency.

## Key Findings

**Methods**

- Six parallel east–west transects were established in 1990, separated by 1.5 minutes of latitude. Each ran from the 5-m contour on the western shore to the 10-m contour on the eastern shore, taking about 50 minutes.
- New-moon surveys: 26–27 July 1990. Full-moon surveys: 5–6 August 1990. Each transect was treated as an independent replicate. Skies were cloudless.
- Diel survey: transect 2 on 18 September 1990, from 0107 to 1138 h. About 20% cloud cover.
- Equipment: [[BioSonics Model 105]] echosounder with a 420-kHz [[Dual-Beam Transducer]] (6 × 15°), two pings per second, boat speed 4–6 m/s.
- Analysis depth range was 2–60 m. The narrow-beam noise threshold was 70 mV, the wide-beam threshold 100–120 mV, and the bottom threshold 8 V.
- Density was derived from *σ*bs = 0.000027, giving a mean [[Target Strength]] of −45.7 dB. This corresponds to a mean-sized fish of 12.6 cm TL weighing 10.3 g, using a [[Length-Weight Relationship]] (W = 0.00212 TL^3.35) developed from midwater trawl samples.
- A bottom window of 0.5 m was used for echo counting and 1.0 m for [[Echo Integration]]. The steep eastern shore required a bottom-identification threshold instead.

**Results: lunar phase**

- Acoustic estimates of fish density and biomass during full moons were approximately 50% of new-moon values.
- During new moon, fish were dispersed below the thermocline, mainly at 10–20 m. Their densities peaked at light intensities of 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ lx.
- During full moon, fish were dispersed but lay closer to the bottom, where they were hard to detect. More than 20% of targets were at depths where light exceeded 10⁻³ lx.

**Results: diel pattern (18 September 1990)**

- Between 0100 and 0630 h, single-target densities were 0.72 fish/1,000 m³ at 10–20 m and 0.64 fish/1,000 m³ at 20–30 m. Few fish were present at 2–10 m or 30–60 m.
- Dawn occurred about 0605 h and sunrise at 0642 h. After 0636 h, densities fell below 0.05 fish/1,000 m³ and were similar across depths.
- After sunrise, single-target densities were roughly 10% of nighttime values.
- Fish biomass was 0.46 kg/hectare just before dawn (0428 h). The source text is cut off at the post-dawn value, but biomass was described as similar before and after dawn.
- Chart recordings suggest echo counting became unreliable in daylight because fish schooled.

**Conclusions**

- Fish dispersal under changing light biases acoustic estimates. Lunar phase and time of day must be standardized in pelagic surveys.
- [[Echo Integration]] may yield reasonable relative biomass estimates when [[Echo Counting]] fails, such as during daylight schooling, and may be the only option in daylight. Echo counting is generally more reliable for density, but integration is preferable when local densities are high.
