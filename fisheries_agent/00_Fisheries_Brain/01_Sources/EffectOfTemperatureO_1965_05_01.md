## Executive Summary
This brief 1965 communication by D. R. Swift, conducted under the Freshwater Biological Association at Ferry House, Ambleside, reports experimental findings on the effect of water temperature on the developmental rate and mortality of eggs from [[Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) and [[Perch]] (*Perca fluviatilis*). Eggs were incubated under controlled constant-temperature aquaria conditions to determine thermal tolerance limits, including upper lethal temperatures for both species. The study is notable as an early empirical record of temperature-dependent egg mortality and hatching success in these two economically and ecologically important freshwater [[Esocidae|esocid]] and [[Percidae|percid]] species.

## Background & Description
This paper does not focus on adult or larval morphology, but the following egg-handling and developmental observations are relevant:

- **Pike eggs**: Stripped from a single female and fertilized via the dry method using milt from two males, indicating controlled artificial fertilization methodology.
- **Perch eggs**: Collected pre-fertilized from wild-caught broodstock at [[Windermere]] within 24 hours of natural spawning.
- **Alevins**: Hatching outcome tracked as "hatched alevins," indicating the study monitored eggs through to the alevin (yolk-sac larva) stage.
- Eggs at 6°C for perch were noted to be "at the point of hatch" but had not yet emerged when the experiment was interrupted, suggesting observable pre-hatch morphological/developmental cues were used to assess hatching readiness.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are native to temperate freshwater systems in the UK, with perch eggs sourced from **Windermere**, a lake in the English [[Lake District]], a well-studied site for the [[Freshwater Biological Association]].
- Pike were collected from natural habitat waters matching the initial aquarium temperature, implying local wild population sampling reflecting in-situ thermal conditions at time of capture.
- The study implicitly addresses the **thermal ecology of early life stages**, a critical factor governing recruitment success, spawning phenology, and geographic distribution limits for both [[Pike]] and [[Perch]] populations in temperate lentic (lake) ecosystems.
- Findings have direct relevance to understanding how water temperature regimes (e.g., seasonal warming, climate variability) could affect egg survival and hatching synchrony in wild pike and perch populations.

## Key Findings
- **Upper lethal temperature** was successfully determined for the eggs of **both** pike and perch, representing among the first recorded data points of this kind for these species (specific numeric values presented in the original Table 1, not preserved in this OCR extract).
- **Lower lethal temperature for perch** could **not** be determined due to a cooling system failure on day 33 of the experiment.
- The same equipment failure led to **incomplete hatching data** for perch eggs held at **8°C and 6°C**:
  - Eggs at 6°C were observed to be at the point of hatch but had not yet emerged when data collection was disrupted.
- **Complete mortality** of perch eggs occurred at **14°C**, an unexplained result noted explicitly by the author as lacking any offered explanation — flagged as an anomalous finding warranting further investigation.
- Experimental conditions were tightly controlled: water temperature held within **±0.1°C** of target values, aquaria kept dark (except during brief inspections), continuously aerated, and supplied with running water — a rigorous methodology for its time.
- Daily mortality and hatching (alevin emergence) records were kept per aquarium, allowing fine-scale tracking of developmental timing and mortality onset relative to temperature treatment.
- This work is cited as the **first known documentation** of these particular temperature–mortality/development relationships in pike and perch eggs at the time of publication.
