# Picketpost Mountain/Arnett Creek Analysis Area: Draft Objectives and Alternatives

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 public-comment letter and attachment package from the [[Tonto National Forest]] (Forest Supervisor Charles R. Bazan). It presents draft management objectives, evaluation criteria, mitigation standards, and five draft alternatives for the Picketpost Mountain/Arnett Creek Analysis Area in Arizona. The objectives and alternatives were developed with public input, an [[Interdisciplinary Team]] and the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], which holds primary responsibility for fish management. Written comments were due by July 15, 1994. The central aim is to restore riparian vegetation and a native fish community in [[Arnett Creek]] while balancing livestock grazing, mineral exploration, and recreation.

## Background & Description

- **Letter and process:** The Forest Service had earlier sought comments on a preliminary list of Issues, Concerns, and Opportunities (ICO). Draft objectives were developed at an open public meeting on November 1, 1993. Draft alternatives were developed by the Interdisciplinary Team on March 22, 1994.
- **Study area:** The Analysis Area includes [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]] on [[Picketpost Mountain]]. Alternatives also consider expansion westward to Alamo Canyon and inclusion of lower and upper Telegraph Canyon.
- **Governing framework:** Objectives reference the [[Land Management Plan]] for the Tonto, including its definitions of [[Range Management Levels]] and its Research Natural Area designation.
- **Fish species named for reintroduction:** [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Gila Chub]]. The document does not provide scientific names or family placements for these fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Riparian vegetation.** The objectives focus on tree canopy (Salix, Populus, Fraxinus, Platanus, Prosopis), the grass [[Deer Grass]] (Muhlenbergia rigens), and small wetland plants (Juncus torreyi, Paspalum distichum, Cyperus sp.). Paspalum and Cyperus are described as native species that have probably been extirpated locally. Cyperus persists on nearby Queen Creek. Exotic species present in small amounts include Nerium oleander, Tamarix pentandra, Agrostis semiverticillata, and Polypogon monspeliensis.

**Livestock and grazing.** Livestock use of the riparian pasture is regulated by a written agreement with the permittee. Current use is governed by an existing MOU and decision memo dated 9-13-93.

**Upland and wildlife habitat.** The document calls for improved vegetative diversity and ground cover on upland sites and floodplain terraces. Riparian wildlife outcomes depend on achieving the riparian objectives. Aquatic wildlife depends on the fisheries objectives. All habitats must also protect or enhance Federally listed or proposed species and species on the Regional Forester's Sensitive Species List.

**Fish habitat in Arnett Creek.** The document identifies stream conditions that need improvement, including bank undercutting, width/depth ratio, instream cover (watercress, bulrush, bear grass), pool habitat, and braided channel percentage.

## Key Findings

### 1. Riparian Vegetation Objectives

- **Tree canopy:** Maintain tree species canopy of no less than 70% on Arnett Creek and 80% on Telegraph Creek (survey reaches). The canopy values are stated as "70% and 80%, respectively."
- **Muhlenbergia rigens:** Maintain at least 8% canopy cover and increase it where possible.
- **Salix regeneration:** Keep at least 10% of Salix plants in seedling and sapling age classes. This benchmark comes from the more mature Arboretum stand. The Forest Service stand already exceeds 10% young plants.
- **Wetland plants:** Establish a combined minimum of 1% canopy cover of Juncus torreyi, Paspalum distichum, and Cyperus sp. by 1999, using re-introduction and proper livestock management.
- **Exotics:** Eliminate or reduce Nerium oleander, Tamarix pentandra, Agrostis semiverticillata, and Polypogon monspeliensis. Hand tools will be used in 1994 and later as needed.
- **Floodplain function:** Sustain residual deer grass cover at no less than 2/3 height of ungrazed plants from July through March, in at least two of every three years. The document notes that the current grazing schedule meets this, but raising use to 60% in July-March would conflict with it.

### 2. Mitigation Standards (Livestock Utilization Limits)

- Current-annual-growth (CAG) use on pole and mature woody species (Salix, Populus, Fraxinus) must not exceed **50%**.
- CAG use on seedling and sapling woody species must not exceed **40%**.
- Perennial grass use must not exceed **60% by weight**.
- These are maximum allowable limits, not management goals.

### 3. Wildlife Objectives

- Establish permanent vegetation trend transects by **1996**.
- Maintain at least **30% effective ground cover** on upland sites, citing the Tonto LMP.
- Protect or enhance habitat for Federally listed, proposed, and Sensitive Species.

### 4. Fisheries Objectives

- **Habitat improvement in Arnett Creek:** Increase undercut banks, reduce width/depth ratio, increase instream vegetative cover, increase pool habitat, and decrease braiding. Significant upward trends should appear within **10 years**. Monitoring is set for **years 4, 7, and 10**.
- **Native fish restoration:** Re-establish a native fish community in Arnett Creek. Populations should be stable within **10 years** of initial stocking.

### 5. Other Resource Objectives

- **Lands and minerals:** Manage in a way compatible with the Analysis Area objectives while still allowing mineral exploration and development on the Tonto.
- **Livestock:** Manage in a way compatible with other objectives. Monitoring results may inform management of comparable riparian systems.
- **Recreation:** Manage in a way compatible with other objectives while still providing for recreation on the Tonto.

### 6. Draft Alternatives Compared

| Alternative | Designation and Scope | Fish Actions | Grazing | Mineral Withdrawal | Arboretum Permit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: No Action** | No special designation; Management Area 2E reverts to 2F | No [[Fish Barrier]]; no [[Exotic Fish Removal]]; no [[Native Fish Reintroduction]] | Level D allowed | Not filed | Not reissued |
| **B: Original Analysis Area** | [[Botanical And Zoological Area]] | Barrier built; exotics removed; three native species reintroduced above barrier | Level D in riparian pasture; Level A outside | Filed | May be reissued, subject to terms |
| **C: Expansion #1** | Analysis Area expanded to lower Telegraph Canyon; Botanical and Zoological Area | Same as B | Level D in expanded pasture; Level A outside | Filed | May be reissued |
| **D: Expansion #2** | Expanded westward to Alamo Canyon and to lower and upper Telegraph Canyon; Botanical and Zoological Area | Same as B | Level D in expanded pasture; Level A outside | Filed | May be reissued |
| **E: Research Natural Area** | Management Unit 2E designated a [[Research Natural Area]] | No barrier; no exotic removal; no native reintroduction | Level A (no grazing) | Filed (locatable minerals) | May be reissued |

Key differences: Alternatives B through D include fish barrier construction, exotic fish removal, and native fish reintroduction. Alternatives A and E do not. Alternatives B through D differ mainly in the geographic extent of the protected area and riparian pasture.

### 7. Range Management Level Definitions

- **Level A** excludes livestock grazing to protect other values or eliminate conflicts with other users.
- **Level D** optimizes forage production and use for livestock while maintaining the environment and multiple use. It allows practices such as brush control, seeding, fencing, water developments, and complex grazing systems.

### 8. Requested Action

The Forest Service asks the public for written comments on the draft objectives and alternatives, sent to the Tonto National Forest at 2324 E. McDowell Road, Phoenix, AZ, by July 15, 1994.
