# Growth of the Pargo (*Lutjanus aya*, Block 1795): Quantitative Aspects, 1962/63

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lutjanidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 study by [[Flavio R. Lima]] (SUDENE fisheries research, Recife) examines the growth of the Pargo (*Lutjanus aya*), a commercially important snapper of northeastern Brazil. Using about 3,900 fish landed by [[Pargueiro Fishing Boats]] off the coast of Ceará between October 1962 and September 1963, the author relates total length, weight and age. Age was estimated from translucent annual rings on [[Otoliths]], and the rings were judged to form once per year. Lengths of younger fish, missing from the catch because of gear selectivity, were reconstructed by [[Back-Calculation]]. Growth was described with a [[Von Bertalanffy Growth Model]] and a length–weight power relationship. No significant difference was found between sexes, so the data were pooled.

## Background & Description

The Pargo (*Lutjanus aya*, Block 1795) is a regularly caught fish in the northeastern eastern coast of Brazil. It ranked first in landings at the fish markets of Recife (Pernambuco) and Salvador (Bahia). Its recorded range runs from the State of Maranhão to the State of Espírito Santo, with the greatest concentrations along the coasts of Ceará and Rio Grande do Norte.

The study covered one year (October 1962 to September 1963). Its aim was to relate total length, weight and age of fish caught by the pargueiro boats operating in the region.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish were collected from boats working the banks and coast of Ceará (roughly 1°20′S to 4°40′S, 37°15′W to 41°15′W). Fishing gear selected only fish between about 45 and 80 cm, so smaller year classes were underrepresented in samples. The author therefore reconstructed their lengths indirectly. The paper describes spawning inferred from length-frequency modes, using the method of [[Petersen Method]]. It cites related work on maturity, spawning season and sex ratio by [[Nadja Urt Monteiro de Almeida]] (1965).

## Key Findings

**Sampling and methods**
- About 3,900 fish were measured (total length, in 0.5 cm classes grouped to 1.00 cm).
- The year was split into four quarterly subsamples (Oct–Dec, Jan–Mar, Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep), each with about 175 fish, ranging from 45.0 to 79.0 cm.
- Age was read from translucent otolith rings. The rings were judged to form annually, with formation during April–June.

**Length–age and length–weight relationships**
- Length-at-age was fitted with the von Bertalanffy equation. Two fitted forms are reported in the conclusion, with the first as L∞ = 98.00 cm, k = 0.101 and the second as L∞ = 97.7 cm, k = 0.117. The conclusion labels the second form "(l – e 0,117 t)", and the exact text is partly garbled by OCR, so these values should be checked against the original.
- Length–weight relationship: W = 0.04429 L^2.940 (weight in grams, length in cm).
- Otolith-to-fish length regression: r = 0.997 (y = 7.41 + 0.193x, standard error reported as 0.215 mm).

**Back-calculated lengths (Table III)**
- Mean lengths at ring groups 2 to 10 were estimated at 21.9, 31.3, 38.6, 44.5, 49.5, 53.1, 56.5, 61.6 and 63.3 cm.
- Length increments between successive ring groups declined from 9.4 cm to 1.7 cm.

**Mean size by age (Tables I and II)**
- Mean lengths for ring groups 6–9 ranged from about 50 to 67 cm.
- Mean weights for the same groups ranged from about 1.2 to 3.4 kg.

**Conclusions**
- No significant difference in growth, weight or age was found between sexes (t test, below 5%), so the data were combined.
- The author concluded the parameters showed no differences detectable by this method over the study period.

**Note on the text:** The source is a 1965 Brazilian bulletin article with substantial OCR errors. The Summary states a study of "about 3,900 specimens", and its sub-sample size is given as "175 species", which should read "specimens". The paper's spelling of the species name ("Lutianus") differs from the current name *Lutjanus aya*.
