# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) - Technical Team Meeting Agendas, November 1989

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Upper Colorado Regional Office, detailing the agenda for the November 14-15, 1989, Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Technical Team meetings held at Arizona State University, Tempe, Arizona. While not a primary scientific data report, the document is significant to [[Ichthyology]] and [[Colorado River Ecosystem]] research because it outlines the coordination structure, research review protocols, and institutional stakeholders involved in evaluating the ecological effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, including a recently conducted Low Flow Test. Key agenda items reference biological monitoring efforts tied to the dam's Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and a Biological Opinion process, both of which are central to native fish conservation efforts in the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River.

## Background & Description
The GCES program was established to study the environmental impacts of Glen Canyon Dam operations on the downstream riverine ecosystem, particularly the Colorado River corridor through Grand Canyon National Park. This meeting record documents:

- **Purpose**: Coordination between federal agencies, state agencies, academic institutions, and the National Park Service regarding the technical study program supporting the Glen Canyon Dam EIS.
- **Key Event Referenced**: An October 6-9, 1989, **Low Flow Test**, an experimental flow manipulation from Glen Canyon Dam, designed to assess downstream ecological and geomorphological responses.
- **Participating Entities**:
  - U.S. Geological Survey ([[USGS]]) - represented by Julia Graf
  - Arizona Game and Fish Department ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) - represented by Dennis Kubly
  - GCES Program - represented by Michael Yard and David L. Wegner (Program Manager)
  - HBRS (environmental consulting) - represented by Michael Welsh
  - National Park Service ([[National Park Service]]) - represented by Larry Stevens and Peter Rowlands
  - U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) - represented by Debra Bills or Frank Baucom
  - Academic consultant Duncan Patten, who led discussion on lessons learned from the Low Flow Test and presented a Draft Research Integration Plan
  - Bureau of Reclamation - represented by Michael Pucherelli (Geographical Information Systems) and Michael Roluti (Economic Modeling)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although this document does not present raw biological or fish population data, it is contextually tied to critical habitat and flow-regime research relevant to native Colorado River fish species (e.g., [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and other endemic Grand Canyon fishes) impacted by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. The referenced **Low Flow Test** (October 1989) was part of a broader effort to understand how dam-controlled discharge patterns affect:

- Downstream [[Sediment Transport]] and channel geomorphology (USGS involvement)
- Aquatic and riparian habitat conditions (NPS, HBRS)
- Native and non-native fish populations (Arizona Game and Fish Department involvement)
- Compliance with the Endangered Species Act via the referenced **Biological Opinion** efforts

The meeting agenda also references the development of a **Geographic Information System (GIS)** for the Colorado River corridor, intended as a management and research tool for integrating physical, biological, and economic datasets — an early example of spatial data integration in large-scale river ecosystem management.

## Key Findings
- The **Low Flow Test (October 6–9, 1989)** was a multi-agency field study examining dam-flow effects on the Colorado River ecosystem below Glen Canyon Dam; results were to be synthesized in a "What did we learn?" session led by Duncan Patten.
- A **Biological Opinion** process was underway, indicating formal Endangered Species Act consultation concerning dam operations and their impact on protected aquatic species in the Grand Canyon reach.
- The meeting proposed a formal **Research Integration Plan** and **protocol for review of research proposals**, marking an effort to standardize scientific review and coordination across the GCES Economic and Environmental Teams.
- Discussion of a **Geographic Information System (GIS)** for the Colorado River corridor reflects early efforts to unify biological, physical, and economic data relevant to fish habitat and river management decisions.
- Institutional stakeholders spanning federal science agencies ([[USGS]]), state wildlife management ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), federal land management ([[National Park Service]]), and species protection ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) illustrate the multi-agency framework guiding native fish and ecosystem research in the [[Grand Canyon]] segment of the [[Colorado River]].

**Note:** This document is primarily administrative/procedural in nature (a meeting agenda) rather than a primary research report. It contains no direct quantitative fish population, water chemistry, or species-specific data, but serves as an important contextual record of the institutional and research coordination framework underpinning long-term Glen Canyon Dam ecological monitoring efforts.
