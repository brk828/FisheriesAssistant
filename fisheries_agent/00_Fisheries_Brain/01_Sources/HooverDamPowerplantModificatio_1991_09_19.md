## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, but rather a series of administrative and regulatory correspondence records pertaining to the **Hoover Dam Powerplant Modification Project** (LC-155, ENV-4.00). The core scientific/regulatory issue concerns the potential impact of proposed powerplant modifications at Hoover Dam on two federally endangered fish species inhabiting the Colorado River system: the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) initiated formal Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act with the Bureau of Reclamation (BR) due to concerns that the project "may affect" these species. Over the course of the correspondence (September 1991–June 1992), the project lost its primary power sponsor (Salt River Project, via the Arizona Power Authority), leading to Reclamation's withdrawal of the proposal and the conclusion of consultation without a formal Biological Opinion being issued.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristics, coloration) for either species is presented in this correspondence. The document is administrative in nature. For reference:
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: Named for the pronounced dorsal keel behind the head, a distinguishing characteristic of the species within [[Catostomidae]].
- [[Bonytail Chub]]: A member of [[Cyprinidae]], historically noted for a slender, streamlined body adapted to swift-water river habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Context**: Both species are native to the mainstem [[Colorado River]] system, with specific concern here focused on the reach affected by [[Hoover Dam]] and [[Lake Mead]].
- **Anthropogenic Pressure**: The primary ecological concern addressed is the potential alteration of flow regimes, water levels, or habitat conditions resulting from powerplant modifications (adding hydroelectric generation capacity) at Hoover Dam, which could affect spawning, rearing, or migratory habitat for these riverine species.
- **Conservation Status**: Both the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] were classified as endangered at the time of this consultation, triggering mandatory review under [[Section 7 Consultation]] of the Endangered Species Act.
- **Interagency Ecological Stakeholders**: Multiple agencies with jurisdictional or research interest in these species' habitat were consulted, including the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]], and academic experts such as [[Paul Marsh]] (Center for Environmental Study) and [[W.L. Minckley]] (ASU Department of Zoology), both recognized authorities on Colorado River basin fishes.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory Trigger**: The FWS determined the Hoover Dam Powerplant Modification Project "may affect" the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], necessitating an updated Biological Assessment (BA) and formal Section 7 consultation (initiated July 22, 1991).
- **Biological Assessment Deficiencies**: Correspondence from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] indicates that the original BA submitted by the Bureau of Reclamation was considered insufficient and in need of improvement to adequately serve as the basis for a Biological Opinion.
- **Proposed Consultation Team**: The Arizona Game and Fish Department offered fishery biologists (Dennis Kubly and Larry Riley) to serve on a consultation team to help develop the Biological Opinion.
- **Project Sponsorship Collapse**: The Salt River Project (SRP), the identified primary power consumer, indicated (verbally, via the Arizona Power Authority) that it no longer required the additional power output the project modifications would generate.
- **Consultation Suspended, Then Terminated**: FWS opted to hold consultation in abeyance while project status remained uncertain (per December 18, 1991 letter).
- **Formal Withdrawal**: On June 22, 1992, the Bureau of Reclamation verbally notified FWS that the project had no sponsors and was being withdrawn.
- **Conclusion Without Opinion**: Formal Section 7 consultation (initiated July 22, 1991) was closed without issuance of a Biological Opinion, as the underlying project was no longer active. Reclamation agreed to notify FWS and resume consultation if the project were revived under new sponsorship.
- **No Species-Specific Data Presented**: This document contains no new empirical or field data on population status, habitat use, or life history of either species; it is procedural/administrative in scope, reflecting the regulatory consultation process under the [[Endangered Species Act]] rather than primary ichthyological research.
