# Genetic Studies of the Colorado River Gila Complex (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document compiles a study of the [[Colorado River Gila Complex]] (genus *Gila*, family Cyprinidae) combining two genetic approaches. The main part is a protein electrophoresis study (allozymes) of 23 presumptive loci in about 94 Gila from the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] and 63 from the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]], plus 26 [[Gila Bicolor Newarkensis]] as an outgroup. A second section is a preliminary [[Karyotype Analysis]] of juvenile and adult Gila from several populations, using Giemsa staining, [[Nucleolar Organizer Region]] (NOR) silver staining and chromosome morphometrics. The document also includes a short internal memorandum on cytogenetic identification and the methods, glossary and nomenclature appendices.

The central conclusion is that morphologically recognized taxa are only weakly separated genetically. Gila bicolor newarkensis is clearly distinct, but the Colorado River forms share most alleles. Cytogenetic work hints at diagnostic differences, but the results are preliminary.

## Background & Description

The systematics of the Colorado River Gila complex is poorly resolved. Morphological identification is difficult because species were described from very few specimens, and intermediate "intergrades" are common in the Upper Basin. Possible explanations include intraspecific variation, introgression, or hybridization between [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] forms. Previous genetic work was concentrated in the Lower Basin, and no protein electrophoresis of this complex had been published before this study.

The complex includes several taxa:

- [[Humpback Chub]] (*G. cypha*), described from a few specimens and variable in the Upper Basin.
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*G. elegans*), now nearly extinct in the wild. A hatchery stock derived from six females and five males from [[Lake Mojave]] was the only known non-breeding population.
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*G. robusta robusta*), a widely distributed form.
- [[Virgin River Chub]] (*G. robusta seminuda*), an endemic subspecies suggested to have hybrid origins involving *G. robusta* and *G. elegans*.

Several of these forms have been listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Genetic information was therefore framed as useful for management and recovery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document identifies several threats and factors affecting Gila populations:

- **Habitat degradation and dams**: decreased abundances are attributed to habitat loss. The [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] altered the Green River, and a rotenone treatment before its operation killed fish at least 160 km downstream and eliminated some aquatic insects.
- **Small, isolated populations**: the [[Gila Bicolor Newarkensis]] population occupies small desert springs, which likely produced bottlenecks and genetic drift.
- **Reduced numbers**: *G. elegans* has become virtually absent from the Upper Basin.

Sampling localities span the Upper Basin, including [[Westwater Canyon]], [[Desolation Canyon]], [[Black Rocks]], [[Cottonwood Creek]] and the [[Gunnison River]], and the Lower Basin, including the [[Little Colorado River]] and the [[Virgin River]]. Samples were collected over a four-year period (1985–1987) through the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

## Key Findings

### Methods

- **Specimens**: 94 Upper Basin Gila, 63 Lower Basin Gila and 26 *G. b. newarkensis* were assayed. Some tissue was obtained by sacrifice and some by non-lethal muscle biopsy (about 3 mm, from 1 cm below the dorsal fin base) in *G. cypha* at Westwater Canyon.
- **Anesthesia and preservation**: fish were anesthetized with [[Tricaine Methanesulfonate]]. Tissues were frozen and stored at -80 °C. Some whole fish were held at -20 °C before dissection.
- **Electrophoresis**: horizontal [[Starch Gel Electrophoresis]] of liver and muscle extracts, with buffer systems and staining procedures following Selander et al. (1971) and conventions of the International Union of Biochemistry (1984).
- **Analysis**: Hardy-Weinberg tests, heterozygosity and polymorphism calculations, [[Nei Genetic Distance]] and related distance measures, and [[UPGMA Clustering]] in the BIOSYS-1 program. Each population sample was treated as an operational taxonomic unit (OTU).

### Genetic Results

- **Loci**: 23 presumptive loci were assayed. Seven were monomorphic (for example *Ldh-A*, *Ldh-B*, *Mpi-A*). The remaining 16 varied.
- **Hardy-Weinberg**: most loci were in equilibrium. Deviations were attributed to sampling error, and one heterozygote excess in *G. cypha* from the Little Colorado River was possibly an artifact of small sample size (only eight animals).
- **[[Polymorphism]]**: 20–36% of loci were polymorphic in Colorado River Gila (per the discussion), compared with a lower value in *G. b. newarkensis*.
- **[[Heterozygosity]]**: average heterozygosity ranged from 0.03 to 0.09 among Colorado River Gila. The *G. b. newarkensis* value (about 0.02) lies at the low end of the North American range cited for cyprinids (0.00–0.18, mostly about 0.05). The low *G. cypha* value at Desolation Canyon (about 0.02) and the low *G. b. newarkensis* value may reflect bottlenecks or a recent origin. The *G. elegans* value (about 0.04) was higher than expected for such a reduced species, perhaps because the hatchery sample retained variation.
- **Fixed differences**: *Adh-A* and *Icdh-1* were fixed for different alleles in *G. b. newarkensis* than in all Colorado River Gila. These fixed differences are treated as strong evidence of reproductive isolation.
- **Divergence**: in the liver-only analysis, *G. b. newarkensis* was separated from all Colorado River Gila by more than 0.50 distance units. Within the Colorado River complex, the two main groups differed by only 0.06 units. In the full data set, *G. elegans* and *G. robusta seminuda* clustered together, consistent with hybridization. Desolation and Westwater Canyon *G. cypha* were electrophoretically indistinguishable from Upper Basin *G. robusta robusta*.
- **Diagnostic alleles**: *Pgm-A(b)* and *Ldh-2(b)* were fixed in the Little Colorado River *G. cypha* and in the three hybrids examined. This suggests a possible Little Colorado River contribution to hybrid genomes. Gene frequency differences were noted for *G. elegans*, but no alleles were unique to it.

### Interpretation

The authors conclude that electrophoretic data do not match morphological species boundaries. Several explanations are considered: recent divergence, recent genetic mixing, the limits of starch gel resolution, and small sample sizes in some OTUs. Single-tissue samples (for example, Westwater Canyon *G. cypha* assayed for 15 loci) limit comparisons. Further sampling and additional techniques are recommended.

### Cytogenetic Results (Preliminary)

- **Karyotype**: the diploid number was 50 chromosomes in all populations examined, based on at least 200 cells per population for counts and about 40 karyotypes per population for measurements. This was the first quantitative karyotype classification for any fish, according to the author.
- **Chromosome morphology**: most chromosomes were similar among species. The most striking difference was chromosome 19, which is submedian in *G. cypha* and *G. robusta* but median in *G. elegans* in the analysis presented. Arm-ratio differences between *G. robusta* and *G. cypha* were significant (t = 5.3, df = 78, P < 0.001), as were those between *G. robusta* and *G. elegans* (t = 8.7, df = 78, P < 0.001).
- **Centromere position**: three chromosomes differed in centromere placement but not relative length. This may result from a centromeric shift or pericentric inversion, and may distinguish juvenile Gila.
- **NOR staining**: all Colorado River Gila species showed NOR regions on chromosome 9, which the author describes as the first known instance in fishes. Black Rocks *G. cypha* differed from Little Colorado River *G. cypha* by having an extra NOR on chromosome 7. Several other possible NOR differences were noted but not fully diagnosed.
- **Limitations**: the silver-formalin results were not quantified, a *Costia* infection affected the *G. robusta* laboratory stock, and G-banding was not achieved. The author notes that cell culture could increase sample sizes without sacrificing fish.

### Memorandum

An internal memo (October 22) accompanying the cytogenetic report states that positive identification of *Gila* species is difficult, that the cytogenetic results are preliminary, and that they should not be published as final. It also notes that the author's findings had been discussed with colleagues.

### Appendices

The document includes a step-by-step karyotyping protocol, a silver-formalin staining procedure for NORs, a glossary of chromosome terms (centromere shift, NOR, translocation, transposition), a morphological terminology table for chromosome types (median, submedian, subterminal, terminal, following [[Levan Nomenclature]]), and reference data tables.
