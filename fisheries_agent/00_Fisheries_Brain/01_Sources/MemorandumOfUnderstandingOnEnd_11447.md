# Memorandum of Understanding on Endangered Fish Recovery in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #conservation-policy #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary field or laboratory study but an **interagency policy instrument** — a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) executed between the [[United States Fish And Wildlife Service]], the [[United States Bureau Of Reclamation]], and the states of [[Colorado]], [[Utah]], and [[Wyoming]]. Its stated purpose is to establish a cooperative framework for developing "reasonable and prudent alternatives" under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] so that federal water development and depletion projects in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] can proceed without jeopardizing the continued existence of three listed endangered fish taxa. While it contains no morphometric, physiological, or population data itself, it explicitly names the species of conservation concern and mandates the creation of scientific/technical subcommittees (Biological and Hydrological) tasked with generating and interpreting the data needed to guide recovery actions. The document therefore functions as the administrative scaffolding underpinning what would become the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]].

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source. The document is administrative in nature and instead identifies the three taxa of concern by scientific and common name:

- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — referred to in the text by its older common name "Colorado squawfish"; family [[Cyprinidae]]; historically the largest North American minnow, a large-bodied piscivorous cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River system.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — family [[Cyprinidae]]; distinguished in the broader literature by a pronounced nuchal hump, adapted to fast, turbid canyon-bound river reaches.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) — family [[Cyprinidae]]; a streamlined, large-finned cyprinid adapted to swift mainstem river currents.

No size classes, coloration, fin counts, or other diagnostic characters are described in this document; morphological detail would need to be drawn from complementary biological literature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: All three species are endemic to the mainstem and larger tributaries of the [[Colorado River]] system, with particular focus on the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] spanning Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming (with New Mexico noted as a compact party).
- **Threats identified in the document**:
  - Water project development and **flow depletion** associated with dam operation and diversion.
  - **Competition with non-native species**, explicitly flagged as a factor the Coordinating Committee must address.
  - Loss of **fish passage** connectivity, prompting the MOU's call for fish passage facilities.
  - Altered **instream flows** and water delivery/reregulation schedules affecting spawning and rearing habitat.
- **Management responses proposed**: hatchery and stocking programs, establishment of aquatic preserves, instream flow protections, and reregulation of water delivery — an early framework anticipating the multi-agency [[Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] approach later formalized for the basin.
- **Regulatory context**: The niche of these species is defined here primarily through the lens of the [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 consultation process, situating their ecology at the intersection of water law, interstate compacts, and federal trust responsibilities rather than through direct field ecology.

## Key Findings

- Three fish species are formally identified as endangered/threatened taxa of concern in the Upper Colorado River Basin: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]].
- The MOU establishes a **Coordinating Committee** (chaired via an appointed Executive Director, Frank Dunkle of USFWS) tasked with reconciling scientific data on these species with water development needs.
- A **Steering Committee** and technical **Biological and Hydrological Subcommittees** are mandated to perform data collection, technical analyses, and to guide recommendations — indicating that dedicated scientific study of these species' status and habitat requirements was to follow from this administrative framework.
- The Coordinating Committee is explicitly charged with discussing the **status and review requirements** of the three species, including the "appropriateness of downlisting and/or delisting" under the ESA — signaling that population trend and recovery-criteria data were anticipated deliverables of the collaborative process.
- Proposed reasonable and prudent alternatives include: hatchery/stocking programs, competition management with non-native species, fish passage facilities, preserve establishment, instream flow protections, and water delivery reregulation — a de facto early outline of recovery actions later implemented under the broader [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]].
- The MOU had a defined term, expiring **March 31, 1985**, unless extended by majority vote of the Coordinating Committee, with provisions for party withdrawal — reflecting its role as an initial, time-bounded cooperative step rather than a permanent recovery plan.
- No quantitative population, habitat, or morphological data are contained in the source; its scientific value lies in identifying the listed species and establishing the institutional mechanism (committees, data-sharing obligations, peer review of findings) through which such data were to be generated and applied.
