# Validity of the Species Hyale gulbenkiani (Amphipoda)

#marine-biology #crustacea #amphipoda #hyale

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Amilcar Mateus]] and [[Emilia Mateus]] defends the validity of [[Hyale Gulbenkiani]], an amphipod they described in 1962, against the claim by [[Giovannini 1965]] that it is not distinct from the *var. minor* of [[Hyale Perieri]]. The authors reassess the original and later descriptions of *Hyale perieri*, compare it with *H. gulbenkiani*, define *H. perieri minor* from the descriptions of [[Chevreux and Fage 1925]], and critique Giovannini's methods and conclusions. They conclude that three distinct, independent forms exist within the genus *Hyale*: *H. perieri perieri*, *H. perieri minor* and *H. gulbenkiani*. They provide a comparative table and a dichotomous key based mainly on body length, eye shape and antennal length.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic problem.** The authors note that amphipod systematics is difficult. Characters can be variable and dependent on environmental conditions, and many earlier descriptions are incomplete or vague. Original material is largely lost, which has led to irreparable confusions.

**Historical nomenclature.**
- [[Lucas 1849]] described *Hyale perieri* as *Orchestia perieri*, with drawings.
- [[Stebbing 1888]] transferred the species to the genus *Hyale*.
- [[Stebbing 1906]] gave a detailed description, which the authors think may cover two different forms.
- [[Chevreux and Fage 1925]] described the *var. minor*, giving lengths of 6 mm (male) and 5 mm (female), pale green colouration with reddish-brown maculation on the mesosome, and "less large, oval, black" eyes.
- [[Reid 1951]] judged Lucas's original description "hardly adequate for clear identification". Reid proposed treating the description and figures of Chevreux and Fage as representing *H. perieri* Lucas. The authors accept this approach as the only practical solution, though they acknowledge it lacks strict rigour.

**Key morphological comparison (*H. perieri* vs. Giovannini's description).** The main disagreement concerns relative antennal II length. Chevreux and Fage give antennae II as 1/2 of body length, whereas Giovannini gives 1/3. The authors consider this divergence important.

**Diagnostic characters separating *H. perieri* and *H. gulbenkiani*.** The authors list differences in:
- body length
- maxilliped chaetotaxy
- relative lengths and robustness of antennae I and II
- shape of gnathopods (especially the palmar margin inclination and the propod of gnathopod II)
- telson shape
- shape and spination of pereiopods

They judge these differences to be numerous and important enough to justify separate species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses benthic amphipods of the genus *Hyale*. It discusses specimens from the Mediterranean (Giovannini's revision of benthic Mediterranean *Hyale*) and from the Porto littoral, where the authors' material was collected. Reid's (1951) work on the Amphipoda of tropical West Africa is cited for the *H. perieri* identity question. The document provides no quantitative habitat, depth or water-chemistry data.

Relevant morphological and ecological variability:
- Body length ranges and distributions are the main unresolved issue. The authors note that length limits for *H. perieri minor* (3 to 7 mm according to Giovannini) and for *H. gulbenkiani* (mean 8 mm) overlap, so length alone cannot separate them reliably.
- Adult males of *H. gulbenkiani* are distinguished by the absence of a carpal extension between the merus and propod. The authors argue that the minimum length given by Giovannini for *H. gulbenkiani* (6 mm) probably belongs to juveniles.
- Gnathopod II palmar margin shape varies among specimens. Of seven Porto females examined, three had a transverse palmar margin and the rest a slightly oblique one.

## Key Findings

**1. Definition of *Hyale perieri*.** The authors accept Reid's approach and base the identity of *H. perieri* on the description and figures of Chevreux and Fage (1925). Giovannini's antennal II length (1/3 of body length) conflicts with Chevreux and Fage's (1/2), and the authors consider this significant.

**2. *H. perieri* vs. *H. gulbenkiani*.** *H. gulbenkiani* differs from *H. perieri* in many important characters, so the authors consider them clearly separate species.

**3. Definition of *H. perieri minor*.** The authors define the *var. minor* by its small size and by its eyes, which are less large and oval (in contrast to the large, reniform eyes of *H. perieri perieri*). Any description departing from this should not be attributed to *var. minor* of Chevreux and Fage.

**4. *H. gulbenkiani* vs. *H. perieri minor*.** The two forms can be distinguished mainly by eye shape and size, which the authors regard as reliable. Body length cannot be used reliably because of the overlapping ranges. Statistical analysis of two population samples would probably show significant differences and a bimodal distribution, but the authors lacked *H. perieri minor* material to test this.

**5. Critique of Giovannini (1965).** The authors identify several problems:
- Giovannini examined a 6 mm male that the authors argue is not the holotype. The specimen lacked the usual holotype marking (e.g. the red label), and the authors state that holotypes are rarely lent.
- Giovannini refers to "holotypes" in the plural, which the authors consider an error for a single-specimen designation.
- The paratypes Giovannini examined were only a small sample, sent at the request of Madame D. Bellan-Santini as routine material.
- Giovannini's comparative table is not rigorously organised. It records eyes of *H. perieri minor* as "reniform, large", which contradicts Chevreux and Fage's "less large, oval" description.
- Giovannini altered the original description of *H. perieri* (gnathopod II palmar margin "tending to become transverse" in one specimen), while the authors consider the Chevreux and Fage figure to show a very oblique margin.
- Giovannini's figures for *H. perieri minor* are not reliable as representations of the *var. minor* of Chevreux and Fage, because the eye drawings do not match the original description.

**6. Morphological differences.** Even accepting Giovannini's figures for *H. perieri minor*, the authors list major differences with *H. gulbenkiani* in:
- right mandibles
- maxillae I and II
- lower lips
- maxillipeds
- gnathopods I and II (propod and dactyl shape; lobes of basal and ischial segments)
- pereiopods (especially VII)
- uropods III (spine number)
- telson shape: pointed in *H. perieri minor*, rounded in *H. gulbenkiani*

**7. Conclusion.** The authors conclude that the descriptions and figures support three distinct, independent forms of *Hyale* close to *H. perieri* Lucas:

| Character | *H. perieri perieri* | *H. perieri minor* | *H. gulbenkiani* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 13 mm (as tabulated) | 6 mm (mostly from Giovannini, 1965) | 8 mm |
| Eyes | Reniform, large | Oval, not very large | Reniform, large |
| Antennae II | Reach or exceed middle of flagellum of antennae II | Do not reach middle of flagellum | Exceed middle of flagellum |
| Antennae II (robustness and length) | Robust, as long as 1/2 body | Not robust, > 1/3 body | Not robust, < 1/3 body |
| Gnathopod I palmar margin | Oblique | Oblique | Transverse |
| Gnathopod II propod | Obpyriform, very oblique margin | Obpyriform, very oblique margin | Obpyriform, very oblique margin |
| Telson | Pointed lobes | Less pointed lobes | Rounded lobes |

**Dichotomous key:**
1. Eyes oval, not very large; length 5 to 7 mm → *H. perieri minor*
   Eyes reniform, large → go to 2
2. Length > 10 mm; antenna II > 1/3 body length → *H. perieri perieri*
   Length < 10 mm; antenna II < 1/3 body length → *H. gulbenkiani*

**8. Historical precedent.** The authors note that [[Stebbing 1906]] may have lumped the three forms under one description. [[Rullo 1936 to 1946]] (cited in the text as "S. Rurro") recognised three forms: a large one (*H. perieri perieri*), a small one (probably *H. perieri minor*) and a medium one (certainly *H. gulbenkiani*). The authors agree with Giovannini's statement that three kinds of individuals are present, including a medium-sized group that belongs to *H. gulbenkiani*, but they remove Giovannini's hedge "peut être" ("perhaps") from that category.

**Source details:** Published in the *Anais da Faculdade de Ciências do Porto* (vol. XLVIII), Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre", Porto, 1965. Cited works include Chevreux and Fage (1925), Giovannini (1965), Mateus and Mateus (1962), Reid (1951) and Stebbing (1906).
