## Executive Summary

This 1992 protocol document, authored by J. Holt Williamson (USFWS Propagation Coordinator), outlines a genetic survey and tissue sampling methodology for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) throughout the Colorado River Basin. The study's primary aim was to genetically characterize wild and captive populations, define management units, assess genetic risk, and evaluate the incidence of hybridization/introgression with sympatric catostomids — [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[White Sucker]]. This represents the terminal year of a scheduled multi-year genetic survey effort, using nonlethal, aseptic tissue sampling (muscle, fin, and gametes) to support both allozyme electrophoresis and mitochondrial DNA restriction analysis. The document is primarily methodological/logistical rather than a report of biological findings, detailing sampling sites across the Green River, Colorado River, Lake Powell, Lake Mojave, Lake Mead, Grand Canyon, and Dexter National Fish Hatchery broodstock populations.

## Background & Description

While this document is a sampling protocol rather than a morphological study, it specifies field data required to characterize sampled individuals of the [[Razorback Sucker]] and associated catostomids:

- **Total Length (TL, mm)** and **Weight (g)** recorded for every individual.
- **Secondary sexual characteristics** used as a criterion for inclusion in sampling (fish must show eggs, milt, or strong secondary sex traits).
- **PIT-tag** implantation used for individual identification and long-term tracking; existing tags checked prior to re-tagging.
- Suspected **hybrids** were specifically targeted for sampling, implying morphological screening in the field to flag putative hybrid individuals for genetic confirmation.
- Tissue types collected per fish: two muscle plugs (4–5 mm biopsy punches, 10 mm long), a 1 cm² fin clip (from caudal fin, upper and lower lobes), and gametes (5–15 mL eggs or milt).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The protocol implicitly documents the known distribution and habitat associations of the [[Razorback Sucker]] within the [[Colorado River Basin]]:

- **Green River Subbasin**: Spawning-associated sampling at Yampa River/Echo Park confluence, Razorback Bar (RM 311) near Jensen, UT, and Escalante Ranch, UT — timed to coincide with the May–June spawning season.
- **Upper Colorado River Mainstem**: Adults noted as "very rare" in the mainstem; most captures instead occur in **offstream gravel-pit ponds** in the Grand Valley and Rifle, Colorado areas, sampled late March through April.
- **Lake Powell**: Colorado River Arm (near Old Hite Marina) and San Juan Arm, sampled in April — representing lentic (reservoir) habitat use.
- **Lower Basin**: [[Lake Mojave]] and [[Lake Mead]] populations, the [[Grand Canyon]] reach, Colorado River Indian Tribes Canal, Central Arizona Project Canal, and Lake Havasu — indicating persistence in both riverine and canal/reservoir habitats.
- **Dexter National Fish Hatchery (NM)**: Captive broodstock derived from the Lake Mojave population, representing an ex situ genetic reserve.
- Notes that razorback sucker reproduction and recruitment (natural young-of-year survival) had **not been documented in many years** at the time of writing, a key ecological red flag driving the urgency of the genetic survey and the decision to exclude YOY sampling.
- Sympatric congeners sampled for comparison/hybridization risk occupy overlapping habitat: flannelmouth, bluehead, and white suckers.

## Key Findings

- **Study Rationale**: Broad genetic survey intended to (1) characterize all known wild/captive populations, (2) define breeding/management units, (3) assess genetic risk and conservation priority, (4) inform migration/spawning studies, and (5) evaluate introgression/hybridization risk with other catostomids.
- **Null Hypothesis**: No genetic differences exist among razorback sucker sampled across major subbasins, within subbasins, or among distinct spawning areas.
- **Analytical Methods**:
  - **Starch gel electrophoresis of allozymes** — primary method, following precedent set by Buth et al. (1986), the only prior published genetic study of the species; analysis conducted by Dr. Don Buth (UCLA).
  - **Mitochondrial DNA restriction analysis** — secondary/complementary method if allozyme resolution proved insufficient; conducted by Dr. Tom Dowling (Arizona State University).
  - Aseptic tissue collection was mandated to preserve compatibility with future DNA amplification (PCR-based) techniques.
- **Sample Size Target**: n = 30 individuals per species per sampling site, across five taxa (razorback sucker, flannelmouth sucker, bluehead sucker, white sucker, and suspected hybrids) — a target largely **not met** in FY1991, with the possible exception of the [[Lake Mojave]] and Dexter populations.
- **Study Constraints**: Explicitly identified as funding, time pressure, limited spawning knowledge, site remoteness, limited fish abundance/capture success, contamination/degradation risk, permitting limitations, and analytical resolution trade-offs.
- **Conservation Management Note**: No razorback sucker were to be removed from the wild to refuges in FY1992 pending resolution of facility, protocol, and therapeutant issues — fish were tagged (PIT-tag) and released after nonlethal tissue sampling.
- **Terminal Survey Year**: FY1992 was designated the **last scheduled year** of genetic survey sampling, placing high importance on data completeness despite acknowledged shortfalls in achievable sample sizes.
- **Nonlethal Sampling Innovation**: Techniques developed in FY1991 for nonlethal muscle/fin biopsy and (for the first time in FY1992) aseptic gamete collection, reflecting methodological advancement in conservation genetics for an endangered species.
- **Chain of Custody**: Standardized tissue vial labeling convention (Site–Species–Fish#–Tissue–Date) and shipment protocol to Grand Junction, Vernal, Dexter, or ASU ultracold freezer facilities before final transfer to Dr. Buth (allozymes) and Dr. Dowling (mtDNA).
