## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the Bureau of Reclamation (Regional Environmental Officer) announcing a November 4, 1993 meeting of the **Native Fish Work Group (NFWG)**, a multi-agency coordination body (Fish and Wildlife Service, National Park Service, Nevada Department of Wildlife, Arizona Game and Fish Department, and Arizona State University) overseeing the **[[Lake Mohave Native Fish Rearing Program]]**. The core scientific content is a summary table of the 1993 rearing season for the endangered **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), reporting that over 1,300 individuals were reared in lake-side backwater nurseries, with more than 400 released directly into Lake Mohave. The memo and attached agenda outline ongoing conservation activities including stocking, larvae collection, genetic diversity monitoring, barrier net construction, and telemetry studies planned for 1994–1995.

## Background & Description
The source text contains no direct morphological measurements (length, weight, age classes) of individual fish. However, the rearing categories imply distinct life-stage cohorts being managed:
- **Larvae** — collected from the wild for genetic diversity assessment and stocked into "Nevada-Larvae Backwater" (NL) rearing sites.
- **Juveniles** — reared in the "Arizona Juvenile Backwater" (AJ) site, indicating a juvenile-specific rearing habitat separate from larval nurseries.
- Fish were large enough by season's end to be tagged/tracked in a proposed **[[Juvenile-Adult Telemetry]]** study, suggesting sub-adult to near-adult size classes were being monitored for movement.
- A subset of mortalities ("MORTS") were retained and transported to Arizona State University for **genetic study**, implying tissue/morphometric sampling of dead specimens.

No standard length, weight, fin ray counts, or coloration data are reported in this administrative record.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat**: [[Lake Mohave]], an impoundment of the Colorado River spanning the Arizona-Nevada border, functions as the core recovery habitat for this population of [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Rearing microhabitats**: Fish were reared in constructed or natural **ephemeral backwaters** and coves, a critical nursery habitat type for larval and juvenile catostomids in altered river systems lacking natural floodplain inundation. Named sites include:
  - **[[Yuma Cove]] Backwater (YC)**
  - **[[Willow Cove]] Backwater (WC)**
  - **[[Arizona Juvenile Backwater]] (AJ)**
  - **[[Nevada-Larvae Backwater]] (NL)**
  - **[[Davis Cove]]** — site of planned barrier net monitoring (1994) and berm construction (1995), indicating engineered habitat isolation to protect rearing fish from predation by non-native species.
- **Threats/management drivers**: The program's structure (barrier nets, berm construction, water level manipulation) reflects the ecological challenge of predation by non-native piscivores and unfavorable reservoir water level fluctuations affecting backwater nursery availability — both recognized limiting factors for [[Razorback Sucker]] recruitment in the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Interagency ecological monitoring**: The [[Native Fish Work Group]] integrates database stewardship, annual reporting, genetic diversity tracking, and a "[[Razorback Roundup]]" (spring 1994) population survey event, reflecting a coordinated ex-situ/in-situ conservation approach typical of endangered catostomid recovery programs in the Lower Colorado River.

## Key Findings
- **1993 total rearing production**: 1,385 razorback suckers processed across rearing sites (per attached table), with a program-level total of **1,300+ fish reared** and **400+ released** into Lake Mohave (per memo narrative) — a discrepancy likely reflecting mortality and retained/genetic-study fish subtracted from the raw total.
- **Capture/Release breakdown by date and site** (selected highlights from table):
  - Aug 18: 525 total fish (AJ/NL combined capture; 382 to Davis, 132 to Lake, 11 mortalities) — largest single-date cohort.
  - Sep 23: 308 fish from Yuma Cove (YC), second-largest cohort, with only 7 mortalities — indicating high survival from this backwater.
  - Sep 2 (ND site): 138 total fish, 129 released to Davis.
  - Overall totals: **487 fish released to Lake**, **833 fish released to Davis (Cove)**, **65 mortalities**, summing to **1,385 total fish** handled during the 1993 season.
- **Mortality allocation**: A portion of mortalities were not discarded but transferred to Arizona State University for genetic studies, integrating population genetics into routine rearing operations — a notable practice for maintaining genetic diversity records in a small, recovering endangered population.
- **Institutional collaboration**: The program formally links five entities — [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[National Park Service]] (Lake Mead National Recreation Area), [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and [[Arizona State University]] (notably including catostomid recovery expert [[W.L. Minckley]]) — underscoring the multi-agency nature of Colorado River native fish recovery efforts.
- **Forward planning items** (1994–1995 agenda): database stewardship, larvae genetic sampling, Davis Cove barrier net monitoring (1994), Davis Cove berm construction (October 1995), juvenile/adult telemetry deployment, water level manipulation studies, and a spring 1994 "Razorback Roundup" population assessment — indicating an expanding, multi-year adaptive management framework for the species.
