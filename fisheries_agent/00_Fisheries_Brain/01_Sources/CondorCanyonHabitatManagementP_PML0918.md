# Condor Canyon Habitat Management Plan (N5-HMP-727)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

The Condor Canyon Habitat Management Plan (HMP), prepared by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Caliente Resource Area in 1989, aims to maintain or improve habitat for the federally threatened [[Big Spring Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis pratensis*). The plan covers roughly 4 stream miles of critical habitat in [[Meadow Valley Wash]] within Condor Canyon, Lincoln County, Nevada. It sets habitat objectives for food, water, cover and space, lists planned, facilitating and mitigation actions, and is reviewed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] via [[Section 7 Consultation]] with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). The accompanying Environmental Assessment (EA NV-055-08-26, dated 3/5/90) and the USFWS concurrence memo (February 9, 1990) found the plan not likely to adversely affect the species and likely to benefit it.

## Background & Description

- **Listing:** The spinedace was listed as threatened with critical habitat by USFWS on April 1, 1985 (final rule published in the Federal Register, March 1985). It is also state-protected by the Nevada Board of Wildlife Commissioners. Recognized threats include habitat degradation from livestock grazing and possible extirpation from severe floods.
- **Taxonomy and knowledge:** The fish is a member of the minnow family (Cyprinidae), endemic to the Meadow Valley Wash drainage, Lincoln County. The document notes little knowledge of its life requirements; past literature mostly addresses taxonomy, location and habitat description.
- **Historical decline:** The Lake Carpenter pluvial system contracted, restricting the fish to remnant spring habitats. It was extirpated from its type locality by 1959, then rediscovered in 1977 in Condor Canyon. Mosquitofish introduction (Big Spring, 1961) contributed to type-locality extirpation. Other pressures include water diversion, mining, railroad activity and ungulate grazing.
- **Habitat:** The critical habitat is 4 stream miles, 50 feet on either side of the creek, in Mount Diablo Meridian T.1S., R.68E. Average width 9.2 ft, average depth 0.6 ft, velocity 1.2 ft/s, discharge 5 cfs, gradient 1.6%. Elevation ranges from 4,740 ft to 5,160 ft.
- **Climate:** Precipitation 10–12 inches per year; snowfall 6–12 inches in winter; summer temperatures seldom exceed 100°F, winter lows below 0°F.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Riparian vegetation** was inventoried by Nature Conservancy (1989) and grouped into five woody subgroups (boxelder, black willow, tamarisk, coyote willow, and a mixed community) plus herbaceous communities. Dense black willow stands carry cattails, redtop and watercress in the understory; open areas support wiregrass, sedges and bulrushes.

**Upland communities:** Three types were identified. The primary type is a Wyoming big sagebrush–rubber rabbitbrush–four-wing saltbush–cheatgrass association with low species diversity. Other communities have higher diversity and more perennial natives. The rare [[Phacelia]] species occurs on rock outcrops.

**Geology and soils:** Two prevalent formations occur (Highland Peak Formation, Cambrian; an undifferentiated Tertiary formation). Soils comprise 12 types. Stream-bank soils are mainly rock land and Pahranagat silt loam, the latter being poorly drained, alkaline and saline-affected, with flooding hazard.

**Wildlife:** Many passerine birds, raptors (golden eagle, red-tailed hawk), and mammals occur. No other federally listed species are known in the canyon besides the spinedace. Mule deer populations have increased, prompting depredation hunts since 1986.

**Non-native and stressor species:** Rainbow trout, largemouth bass, crayfish, white crappie and bullfrogs pose potential threats. Off-road vehicle use and campfire vegetation removal present habitat risks.

## Key Findings

**Land status and grazing**
- Four grazing allotments overlap critical habitat: Black Hills, Condor Canyon, N5 (43,500 acres, administered from Ely District, to transfer to Caliente by FY 90) and Highland Peak. Domestic sheep were excluded from critical habitat by cooperative agreement.
- Roughly 1.2 of the 4 miles lie on private land; 480 acres of private land are nearby.
- The Union Pacific Railroad right-of-way (NEV-042771, 200 ft wide) remains legally held by the railroad because it has not formally relinquished the right-of-way, although tracks were removed in 1984. BLM cannot impose management on it until relinquishment is complete.

**Water rights and monitoring**
- The Nature Conservancy holds an instream flow filing of 3.006 cfs reserved for the spinedace.
- Fish survey (May 28–30, 1986): 15 sites sampled, 3 with no spinedace. N5 allotment: 10 sites, 176 individuals; Condor Canyon: 2 sites, 19 individuals; Black Hills: 3 sites, 2 individuals.
- Flow measurements from the 1987 aquatic inventory ranged from 2.17 to 6.87 cfs across stations.

**Habitat objectives (quantitative targets)**
- Maintain or increase willow density (present ~600/acre in N5 to increase; Black Hills to rise).
- Reduce tamarisk on 3.18 acres within 5.2 miles of riparian zone.
- Increase undercut banks (e.g., N5 from 6.5%; Black Hills from 1% to 20%).
- Limit bank trampling to 20% and vegetative utilization to 50% (whichever occurs first), per allotment.

**Management actions**
- Livestock excluded from riparian zones March 15 to November 15 to protect spawning and rearing.
- Monitoring every two weeks when livestock are present; re-initiate Section 7 consultation if objectives are not met.
- Planned studies on juvenile and adult habitat, reproduction, population dynamics and nutrient inputs from grazing; water quality sampling for nutrients, physical properties and microbiology.
- Tamarisk removal program, educational signage, sediment control structures, two flow gauging stations, and fencing of allotment boundaries.
- Prohibition on drilling water wells that would deplete flow; restrictions on ORV use and cadastral survey of private lands.

**Water quality and stream survey (1987 data)**
- Water quality indicators such as turbidity increase downstream; Aeromonas hydrophila occurs naturally regardless of livestock presence.
- Stream survey ratings: bank stability averaged 71% (optimum), bank cover averaged 53%, percent optimum habitat ranged from 46% (N5) to 65% (Condor Canyon).
- Bottom material desirable: N5 48%, Condor Canyon 15%, Black Hills 21%, based on optimal substrate.

**Regulatory context**
- Objective 1 under Recovery Plan: research necessary for decision-making relative to habitat.
- Proposed Action (EA): positive effects on recreation, vegetation, threatened species and water quality; No Action (Alternative A) would leave dangerous trestle conditions, overrun riparian zones with tamarisk and reduce protection.
- Environmental Assessment concluded no significant impact; no environmental impact statement required.

**Concurrence**
- USFWS (Feb 9, 1990) concurred that the plan is not likely to adversely affect the Big Spring spinedace and should benefit the species, with reinitiation if objectives are unmet.
