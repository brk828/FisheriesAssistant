# Estimated Angler Use and Harvest, Isabella Reservoir, Kern County, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This California Department of Fish and Game Inland Fisheries Administrative Report (No. 66-7, submitted April 1966, by Robert P. Hayden, Region 4) evaluates whether total angler use and harvest at [[Isabella Reservoir]] can be estimated with limited manpower. The reservoir is a heavily used fishery with high angler success, but access along its 38 miles of shoreline makes direct measurement difficult. The first year of the study (March 1964 to February 1965) combined [[Creel Census]] interviews and [[Use Counts]], with [[Traffic Counter]] readings used as a proxy for total effort. A 1965 harvest estimate was then produced by applying the 1964 ratios to the 1965 counter readings.

The report concludes that use counts from shore can estimate use, but weather-driven variability requires many count days. It also finds that a limited creel census gives a good indication of fishing success. The authors recommend continuing the study with a focus on angler success rather than continuous use estimation.

## Background & Description

[[Isabella Reservoir]] is a large fluctuating flood-control reservoir on the [[Kern River]], about 40 miles northeast of Bakersfield. The [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] completed [[Isabella Dam]] in 1954. The reservoir has a capacity of 570,000 acre-feet and 11,200 acres of surface area at gross pool. Storage since completion has ranged from 4,330 to 455,200 acre-feet. The report describes it as the largest body of fresh water in southern California.

The fishery includes several species, summarized in the report's abbreviation key:

- [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*)
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)
- [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*)
- [[Brown Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus nebulosus*)
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*)
- [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*)

Data were collected by Inland Fisheries staff, with support from [[Kern County Parks and Recreation]] (boat counts and weather data), the Corps of Engineers (traffic counter readings), and the Kern River Hatchery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods overview**

- **Use counts:** Shore anglers and boats were counted by car, circling the lake and using binoculars at designated observation points. Up to six complete counts were made per day, with four most common. Counts covered 38.5 percent of days. Partial counts were projected from full-count days with similar weather. Weekend days and holidays with counts were assumed representative of all weekends and holidays in that month, and weekdays likewise.
- **Validation:** Car-based counts were checked against airplane and boat counts on three weekends (March, April and May 1964).
- **Creel census:** Checkers interviewed anglers during fishing and after they finished, concentrating on weekends and heavy-use periods. Anglers were censused on 85 days. Because shore anglers were rarely finished when interviewed, their day length was assumed to be two-thirds complete at interview.
- **Traffic counter:** Corps of Engineers Counter #3 at Isabella Marina was checked as an indicator of total angler hours. It was used to estimate use in January and February 1965 (no counts made) and in November and December 1964 (limited counts judged unreliable).

**Environmental drivers**

Weather was described as the single most important factor in use and success. High use could drop to medium or low within an hour when sudden winds arose. Attempts to fill gaps in use counts using boat counts and Kern County weather data were impractical because no consistent pattern emerged.

## Key Findings

**Catch and effort, March 1964 to February 1965**

- Estimated catch: 1,339,364 fish weighing 782,913 pounds (the text's total; the Table 3 grand total is printed as 1,399,364 fish).
- Estimated effort: 1,099,066 angler hours on 231,174 angler days (the summary gives 1,009,066 hours, and Table 4 totals 1,014,269 hours; these discrepancies appear in the source).
- Catch composition: 68.3% white crappie, 27.4% bluegill, 3.3% largemouth bass.
- Overall catch rate: 1.22 fish per hour.
- Mean catch rate: shore anglers 0.59 fish/hour; boat anglers 1.56 fish/hour.
- Mean time fished per day: shore anglers 4.09 hours; boat anglers 4.68 hours.
- Seasonality: best fishing in April to July; poorest in December to February.
- Anglers per boat ranged from about 2.3 to 3.1 across months (Table 5).

**Estimated 1965 season**

- Estimated catch: 824,701 fish weighing 420,831 pounds.
- Estimated effort: 803,117 hours.
- Catch composition: 61% bluegill, 32% white crappie, and 3.3% largemouth bass (printed as "33 percent"; this is likely an OCR error for 3.3%).
- The report states that use and success were lower than in 1964 and that the catch shifted toward bluegill.
- Average weights (lb): white crappie 0.624, largemouth bass 1.469, bluegill 0.3386, brown bullhead 0.57, rainbow trout 3.82, white catfish 0.59.

**Methodological conclusions**

1. Use can be estimated from periodic shore counts, but reliability requires a high proportion of count days because weather causes large variability.
2. Traffic Counter #3 tracked total angler hours from March through October regardless of seasonal changes or the boat-to-shore ratio. Its November and December relationship was not verified.
3. A limited creel census gives a good indication of fishing success, given the large angler volume and stable harvest rate.

**Recommendations**

- Continue the study with the aim of determining angler success only, since continuous use estimates exceed available manpower.
- Tie future use estimates to the traffic counters.
- Report boat and shore use separately, because their success rates differ.
