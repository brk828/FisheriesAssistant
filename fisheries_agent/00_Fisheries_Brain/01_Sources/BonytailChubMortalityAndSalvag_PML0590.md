# Bonytail Chub Mortality and Salvage at Palm Lake, Arizona (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This April 1992 correspondence, dated 4/26/92, describes a fish die-off among [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) stocked in [[Palm Lake]] near Wickenburg, Arizona. The author reports a call from Francisco Abarca of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Non-Game Branch) about dying bonytail, and documents a salvage operation, the apparent role of low [[Dissolved Oxygen]], and a concern over poor interagency communication. The document is a letter or memo rather than a research report. Parts of the scanned text are illegible, so some figures are approximate or missing.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The [[Bonytail Chub]] is a threatened and endangered cyprinid. The text identifies it as one of the threatened and endangered fish species present in Palm Lake.
- **Lake purpose:** Palm Lake is owned or managed by [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), which the author describes as supportive of the lake serving as a refugium for threatened and endangered fish.
- **Stocking history:** In April (year partly illegible, most likely 1991), bonytail chubs from a year class were moved from wild fish held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter NFH&TC). Fish were placed in two Arizona locations. A third stocking was reported. Some fish were also targeted for Grand Junction, Colorado, and some were placed in ponds, including a golf course pond.
- **Purpose of stocking:** The text notes that Palm Lake was chosen to provide much-needed space for the fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Initial report:** Mr. Abarca reported that bonytail chubs were dying in Palm Lake. TNC staff had observed only three dead fish at first.
- **Suspected cause:** Water quality was the main suspicion. Abarca feared water quality might be the cause. Later, the author notes that dissolved oxygen in Palm Lake appeared to be very low on April 23.
- **Complicating factors:** The text mentions problems with the backup generator and an electrical or power issue, plus glare that made the site hazardous for work until a safe approach could be arranged. These conditions limited the rescue effort.
- **Fish held elsewhere:** A portion of the stocked fish was relocated to a golf course pond, where they reportedly survived, though the exact numbers are unclear in the scan. Roughly 400 fish were reported safe in ponds, and about 350 were moved to a golf course pond (partly illegible).

## Key Findings

1. **Mortality event:** Bonytail chubs in Palm Lake began dying around April 20 to 23, 1992. Only three dead fish were initially seen, but the number increased during the salvage.
2. **Salvage operation:** Abarca's crew captured a substantial number of fish (the count is partly illegible in the scan) and reported that some were dead. The crew was asked to remove the fish, and the Arizona team was to report back.
3. **Water quality:** Dissolved oxygen was reported as very low in Palm Lake on April 23. Low oxygen is the leading candidate for the die-off, though the text does not confirm a cause.
4. **Sites of relocated fish:** Surviving fish were held in ponds, including a golf course pond. Other fish were sent to Grand Junction, Colorado, and to Arizona locations.
5. **Communication failure:** The author states the lead station (the text suggests the Arizona Game and Fish Department or a lead USFWS office) did not participate in the decision on the action taken. The author recommends that communication improve and that the lead office be involved in decisions about the disposition of salvaged fish.
6. **Follow-up:** The author plans to send a "follow-up" note within a few days and wants to review the events and the choices made by the field crew.
