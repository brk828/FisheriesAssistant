# Field Collection Notes from Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, Coahuila, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae #cyprinidae #centrarchidae #field-research

## Executive Summary

This document comprises field collection notes and correspondence from ichthyological surveys conducted in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], [[Coahuila]], [[Mexico]]. The notes document fish collections from multiple spring-fed lagoons and streams, with tissue samples preserved for laboratory analysis. Collections targeted diverse taxa including cichlids, cyprinids, centrarchids, and poeciliids. The correspondence indicates administrative challenges with permit documentation through SEDUE/SEDESOL and payment processing through the Institute of Latin American Studies.

## Background & Description

The field work focused on aquatic habitats within the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], a unique desert spring system in [[Coahuila]], [[Mexico]]. Collection sites included:

- **Laguna Churince**: A large, deep pool (20-30m depth) with very clear water, [[Chara]] vegetation, and abundant [[Lepomis megalotis]] and [[Gambusia affinis]]
- **Laguna Grande**: Spring-fed lagoon with water lilies and aquatic vegetation
- **Laguna Entreoje**: Site with experimental gill net sampling
- **Poza Azul (Los Vestidos compound)**: Clear water spring with [[Chara]] as only submerged vegetation, extensive [[Typha]] and [[Juncus]] emergent vegetation
- **Río Mezquites/Las Playitas**: Stream habitat with carbonate substrate
- **Laguna Salvadora**: Deep circular pool (20-30m diameter)

Collection methods included gill nets (experimental and standard mesh sizes), kick seining, and rotenone sampling. Tissue samples (muscle, liver, gut) were collected and frozen for subsequent laboratory analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] represents a unique desert aquatic ecosystem with endemic and relict species. Field observations documented:

- **Habitat characteristics**: Spring-fed systems with very clear water, carbonate substrates, and [[Chara]] beds as dominant submerged vegetation
- **Water chemistry**: Described as "very clear water" with visible depths exceeding 10m in some pools
- **Trophic guilds**: Collections specifically noted molluscivores, detritivores, and piscivores among cichlid specimens
- **Breeding activity**: [[Lepomis megalotis]] observed in breeding condition at shallow depths in [[Chara]] beds
- **Water level changes**: Laguna Entreoje noted as "somewhat lower than I remembered it a year ago"
- **Herpetofauna**: One large [[Terrapene coahuilae]] (Coahuilan box turtle) observed at Laguna Churince

The ecosystem supports diverse fish assemblages with multiple trophic levels, though some historical species (e.g., [[Cyprinodon]] sp.) were noted as absent or reduced in abundance at certain sites.

## Key Findings

**Species Documented:**
- [[Cichlasoma]] sp. (multiple trophic forms: molluscivores, detritivores, piscivores) - abundant at multiple sites
- [[Tebalurus]] sp. (madtom catfish) - small individuals collected
- [[Cyprinella lutrensis]] (Red Shiner) - 10 specimens from Poza Azul
- [[Lepomis]] sp. (sunfish, likely [[Lepomis megalotis]]) - 4 specimens with muscle tissue pulled
- [[Micropterus]] sp. (bass) - 4 specimens with muscle tissue collected
- [[Dionda]] sp. (desert minnow) - 10 specimens from Poza Azul
- [[Lucania]] sp. (killifish) - multiple specimens in separate packs
- [[Gambusia affinis]] (mosquitofish) - 8 specimens from Laguna Grande, abundant at multiple sites
- [[Astyanax]] sp. (Mexican tetra) - 10 specimens from Laguna Grande
- [[Cyprinodon]] sp. - noted as absent from some historical collection sites

**Sample Processing:**
- Field numbers assigned: DAH01-420713 through DAH02-420719 series
- Tissue samples collected: muscle, liver, gut contents for dietary analysis
- Specimens preserved in 10% phosphate-buffered formalin
- Tissues frozen whole or as pulled samples in vials
- All materials packaged with field numbers and species identification for mailing

**Administrative Issues:**
- Permit documentation from SEDUE (now SEDESOL) through collaborator Héctor remained outstanding
- Payment processing through Institute of Latin American Studies delayed due to consultant pre-approval requirements
- Fiscal year deadline pressure for payment processing, but flexible report deadline
- Work-study student delays affected photocopying and documentation processing

**Ecological Observations:**
- Gill net sampling at Laguna Entreoje produced immediate catches with [[Astyanax]] running continuously
- Poza Azul system includes underground drainage with discharge ~2 cfs or more
- Very deep pools (>10m) with invisible bottoms in clear water at multiple sites
- [[Chara]] beds provide primary habitat structure in spring pools
- Breeding [[Lepomis]] concentrated in shallow [[Chara]]-lined channels
