# Bonytail Chub Scale Aging Request (1980)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1980 letter from the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], part of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], asks the agency's Endangered Species Office in Sacramento, California, to estimate the age of a [[Bonytail Chub]] (*[[Gila elegans]]*). The fish was collected from [[Lake Mohave]] in 1980, spawned on May 2, 1980, and died on September 3, 1980. The hatchery sent scale samples and describes the age data as important for the [[Endangered Species Program]]. The letter is a short request, not a research report, so it contains no population counts, measurements, or statistical results.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Gila elegans]], commonly called the bonytail chub, a member of the family Cyprinidae.
- **Specimen:** One adult fish, identified in the letter as a bonytail chub. Scale samples were taken from it and enclosed with the letter.
- **Collection:** Lake Mohave, near Cottonwood Cove. The OCR of the place name and the collection date is partly garbled, so the exact date and site wording are uncertain. The letter is dated September 3, 1980.
- **Reproductive history:** The fish spawned on May 2, 1980, which the hatchery recorded. Its death followed on September 3, 1980.
- **Sender:** An unnamed biologist at the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], P.O. Box 757, Boulder City, Nevada 89005. The signature is illegible.
- **Recipient:** Gail Kobetich, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], Endangered Species Office, 1230 N Street, Sacramento, California 95814.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter gives very little ecological information. Its main context is the status of the bonytail chub as a species managed under the [[Endangered Species Program]]. The hatchery's request for age data implies interest in the growth and life history of wild or hatchery-associated individuals from [[Lake Mohave]], which lies in the [[Colorado River]] system. The document does not describe habitat conditions, water chemistry, diet, population size, or other fish species. Those topics are not covered.

## Key Findings

- **Request:** The hatchery asks the Endangered Species Office to determine the age of a bonytail chub using the enclosed scales. This is the document's main purpose.
- **Specimen data:** The fish was collected in 1980 from Lake Mohave, spawned on May 2, 1980, and died on September 3, 1980. These dates allow the spawning and death events to be placed in the same year.
- **Importance:** The sender states that the age information is "very important" and will be used by the endangered species program at the hatchery.
- **Method:** The age determination is to be made from scale samples, a common method for estimating fish age through growth-ring counts. The letter does not describe the method used.
- **Limitations:** The document contains no sample size beyond one fish, no measurements, no reply from the recipient, and no age result. Its legibility problems also leave some place-name and date details uncertain.
