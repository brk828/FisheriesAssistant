# Fishes of the Kosi System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sparidae

## Executive Summary

This 1978 study by Stephen J.M. Blaber (University of Natal) documents the fish fauna of the Kosi system, a set of four connected coastal basins in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, that drain to the Indian Ocean through a usually open estuary. Quantitative sampling was carried out at 2 to 3 month intervals from January 1975 to September 1977, with five samplings per year. The system is dominated by euryhaline marine species, with about 124 non-freshwater species recorded, and seasonal and spatial patterns in abundance are linked to tides, current speeds, salinity, temperature and substrate.

## Background & Description

The Kosi system comprises [[Lake Nhlange]], [[Lake Sifungwe]], [[Lake Mpungwini]] and [[Lake Amanzimnyama]], the last being permanently fresh. Earlier surveys (e.g. Campbell and Allanson, 1952; Pike, 1967–1971; Wallace et al., 1971) were largely non-quantitative. The study was motivated by the importance of the fishes to indigenous people and their possible conservation value.

Fish were captured with gill nets (stretch mesh 35–145 mm), a 110 m large seine, a 10 m small seine, a fry net and an open-water trawl. SCUBA observations were made in the channel between Nhlange and Sifungwe and around the estuarine reef. Salinity was measured with an optical salinometer, temperature with a thermometer, and current speed with a water current meter.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Tides:** About 70% of the estuary's surface is exposed at low tide. Spring tides raise channel depth to about 0.6 m, whereas at neap tides in winter the channels seldom exceed 30 mm depth.
- **Currents:** Outflow toward the sea exceeded inflow in all cases and was greater in summer (wet season) than winter, when movement was mainly tidal. Maximum summer reed-channel speed was 2.2 km/h (Table 1).
- **Salinity:** Estuary salinities are stable at 25–35 ‰ around the reef; Nhlange usually stays near 3 ‰ and ranges from fresh during floods up to 5 ‰.
- **Temperature:** Estuary channels stay above 20 °C in winter and reach 30 °C in summer. In the lakes the study recorded 18 °C (Nhlange, July) to 28 °C (Nhlange, January).
- **Substrate and detritus:** Estuary substrate is primarily sand, with a small rocky reef. Lake silt occurs only in deeper water. Floods bring large quantities of plant detritus.
- **Oxygen:** The deep basin of Sifungwe is anoxic for most of the year, which explains the absence of fish there.

The reef inside the estuary, about 300 m from the mouth, hosts a marine reef fauna typical of the western Indian Ocean, dominated by Acanthuridae, Pomacentridae, Chaetodontidae and Labridae. Its salinity stays above 25 ‰, which makes it unusual for a southern African estuary.

## Key Findings

**Species richness and origin**
- 124 species (excluding freshwater) were recorded; 85 (about 70%) are restricted to the estuary and reef.
- Freshwater fishes are minor except in Amanzimnyama. Table 6 gives marine proportions of 73–96% across basins, with estuary at 96%.

**Marine species penetrating the lakes (Table 2, Figure 2)**
- [[Elops Machnata]] (tenpounder) is present year-round but uncommon, probably because of limited pelagic prey.
- [[Chanos Chanos]] (milkfish) is among the most abundant large fishes in Nhlange and Sifungwe, often exceeding 1 m, but has never been caught in the estuary. It likely enters irregularly.
- [[Scomberoides Tala]] (queenfish) is the most common carangid, absent from Nhlange in winter.
- [[Pomadasys Commersonni]] (spotted grunter) is common throughout; vast numbers occur in shallows at Sifungwe and Mpungwini. A mass mortality in Nhlange in July 1975 was probably caused by low salinity combined with low temperature.
- [[Rhabdosargus Sarba]] (Natal stumpnose) is abundant in the estuary and common in the lakes in summer; its numbers drop sharply between Sifungwe and Nhlange, possibly due to reed-channel currents.
- [[Acanthopagrus Berda]] (river bream) is wary and seldom netted in numbers but is common in native fish traps.
- [[Mugilidae]] (grey mullet) are common at the estuary. [[Mugil Cephalus]] is the only species in numbers in Nhlange.

**Estuarine residents and piscivores**
- [[Ambassis Commersoni]] is the most abundant small estuarine species, especially in Nhlange.
- [[Tylosurus Crocodylus]] (crocodile needlefish) is the most abundant piscivore in the estuary year-round.
- [[Pranesus Pinguis]] (hardyhead silverside) is extremely abundant over shallow banks in summer and absent in winter.
- [[Sarotherodon Mossambicus]] is the only freshwater species of note in Nhlange, breeding in Phragmites fringes, and it does not reach Sifungwe or Mpungwini.

**Movement and habitat drivers**
- Low water levels at neap tides and in droughts separate Sifungwe and Mpungwini, limiting migration of large fishes between the lakes and estuary.
- Most fish were caught in shallow water; very few were taken deeper than 6 m.
- Juveniles move between basins mainly through shallow margins.

**Predator–prey patterns (Tables 4–5)**
- Both juveniles and adults of most piscivorous species enter Kosi. Carangids are the exception among estuary-only species, with adults restricted to deeper channels and juveniles to shallow water. The author links this to avoidance of cannibalism.

**Conservation and management**
- Increased siltation from catchment land-use change could radically alter the degree of isolation of all Kosi lakes from the sea. Small drainage changes could isolate Nhlange.
- The Kosi lakes form part of a chain of south-east African coastal lakes, from Lagoa Poelela in the north to St Lucia in the south.

**Limitations**
- Sampling in Amanzimnyama was limited, so its fauna is incomplete. The freshwater list (Table 3) is not exhaustive.
- Seine and gill-net results are not directly comparable.
