# Environmental Assessment: McCarty Grazing Permit, Coyote-Whitmer and Turkey Creek Allotments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This draft Environmental Assessment (EA) by the USDA Forest Service, Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests (Southwestern Region), evaluates whether to issue a 10-year grazing permit to G. McCarty (#01086) for [[Coyote-Whitmer Allotment]] (approx. 14,400 acres) and [[Turkey Creek Allotment]] (approx. 10,500 acres) on the Alpine Ranger District. Two significant issues drive the analysis: permitted livestock use is out of balance with grazing capacity, and the grazing season is not matched to site conditions. Three alternatives are compared: A (no grazing), B (current stocking and season, with utilization standards), and C (stocking balanced with capacity, from mid-July to October 31, preferred).

Alternative C is the preferred alternative. It would reduce stocking to balance with estimated capacity and includes [[Apache Trout]] and [[Loach Minnow]] protections, including exclusion from [[Campbell Blue Creek]].

## Background & Description

- **Current permit:** Issued to G. McCarty, expiring December 31, 1995. Allotments have been grazed mid-May through October under a deferred system with typically 402 cow/calf pairs.
- **Proposed action (7/27/95 scoping letter):** 402 adult cattle, June 15 to October 31, a 17-day change in season. The proposal was later modified to keep the current season (Alternative B), with the season change moved to Alternative C.
- **Decision:** The [[Forest Supervisor]] decides whether to issue the 10-year permit. A separate [[Allotment Management Plan]] analysis would follow.
- **Public scoping:** About 71 letters sent, 23 responses received.
- **Land and ecosystem:** Ponderosa pine dominates both allotments, with mixed conifer, pinon-juniper woodland, meadows, and riparian corridors. Elevations run roughly 6,300 to 9,350 feet.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Grazing capacity and vegetation.** Late spring-early summer grazing has concentrated use in riparian areas and meadows. This has shifted composition from sedges and native cool-season grasses toward [[Kentucky Bluegrass]], rushes, and forbs. Warm-season species such as mountain muhly and blue grama do not contribute much production until summer rains. Stocking is based on allotment-wide production, but not every acre is equally used, so usable forage on gentle terrain should set capacity. Proper/allowable use for desirable plants is about 40% by weight under a deferred system.

**Soils and watershed.** On Turkey Creek, about 50% of the soils are inherently unable to withstand grazing. On Coyote-Whitmer, litter from dense canopy protects satisfactory soils, but forage production is minimal. Watershed condition is rated satisfactory overall, but steep, unstable areas will not improve.

**Riparian condition.** Riparian areas total about 18.8 miles across both allotments (8.6 miles on Coyote-Whitmer, 10.2 miles on Turkey Creek). About 95% of riparian habitat on each allotment is in unsatisfactory condition. Causes include weather, soils, ungulate grazing, and past management.

**Water quality.** Per the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] 1994 assessment:
- [[Campbell Blue Creek]] is "partially limited" by pH and dissolved oxygen.
- The [[San Francisco River]] is "limited" by fecal coliform, though the exceedances occurred upstream of the allotment.
- The [[Blue River]] is limited by turbidity and pH.
- Turkey Creek has no listed limiting factors.

**Air quality.** The Alpine district airsheds are in attainment. No alternative would detrimentally affect air quality.

**Species of concern.**
- Federally listed: 3 endangered, 4 threatened, and 1 proposed endangered species were considered. Critical habitat exists for the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (7,688 acres on Coyote-Whitmer, 7,777 acres or 73% of Turkey Creek) and for [[Loach Minnow]] on about 2.7 miles of Campbell Blue Creek.
- Sensitive: 46 species may occur, and 5 are identified as at risk of trend toward listing if grazing exceeds capacity.

**Economics.** Estimated jobs: Alternative B supports 4-6 direct and indirect jobs, Alternative C 2-6. Payments to counties are reduced under Alternative C. Actual use has averaged 95% of permitted use over the last 10 years.

**Social.** Ranching is a long-standing family-based livelihood. The [[Luna Lake]] campground on Coyote-Whitmer served about 11,900 people in 1994 (Memorial Day to Labor Day). Recreationists have complained about livestock in riparian areas near trails.

**Cultural resources.** No known standing ruins or historic structures would be affected. Section 106 consultation with the State Historic Preservation Officer is in progress.

## Key Findings

**Alternatives compared**

| Feature | A: No grazing | B: Current stocking | C: Balanced with capacity (preferred) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coyote-Whitmer | 0 | 249 AUMs stated | 185 cattle, July 15 to October 31 |
| Turkey Creek | 0 | 402 cattle (combined limit 2,278 AMs) | 53 cattle, July 15 to October 31 |
| Riparian utilization | 0% | Up to 45% | Up to 45% |
| Upland utilization | 0% | Up to 40% | Up to 40% |

**Main conclusions**

- **Alternative A** would give the greatest habitat benefit, and riparian and soil recovery would occur fastest. It would also eliminate grazing income and the ranching lifestyle.
- **Alternative B** would keep numbers above capacity. Maximum utilization would be reached before scheduled pasture exit dates, and hoof action near present levels would continue. It was judged likely to adversely affect [[Loach Minnow]] and its critical habitat because cattle would retain direct access to Campbell Blue Creek during spawning. It was judged not likely to adversely affect [[Apache Trout]] after water gap closures.
- **Alternative C** would reduce hoof action and balance stocking with capacity. Campbell Blue Creek would be fenced off and 1.5 miles of exclosure fence built. Cattle would only access 4 intermittent water gaps on Coleman Creek. It is expected to permit streams to meet state water quality standards from livestock impacts alone. The Forest concluded it would not adversely affect the Apache trout, Loach minnow, or Mexican spotted owl.
- **Soil projections (10 years):** Satisfactory soil condition on Turkey Creek rises from 27% (existing) to 45% under Alternatives B and C. Satisfactory soil on Coyote-Whitmer rises from 75% to 93% under Alternative C.
- **Riparian recovery:** Recovery under Alternative C is expected to be similar to Alternative A and much faster than Alternative B.
- **Mitigation for [[Apache Trout]]:** Close two water gaps south of Forest Road 59F on the Whitmer pasture fence adjacent to Coleman Creek.
- **Monitoring:** Paired exclosure and grazed plots, with at least two utilization cages per pasture, checked before livestock entry and at mid-season. Pasture moves are triggered when utilization limits are reached.
- **Economic trade-off:** Alternative B approximates current revenue and jobs. Alternative C reduces county payments and may force consolidation of operations.
- **Cumulative effects:** Elk, deer, and bighorn grazing, road reconstruction in the [[Blue River]] corridor, a non-native trout hatchery, and sport fishing in Fish Creek were identified as cumulative stressors.
