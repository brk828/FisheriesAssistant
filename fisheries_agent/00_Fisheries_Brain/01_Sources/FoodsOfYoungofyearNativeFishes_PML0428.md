# Tapeworm Infection, Feeding Ecology and Birth Weight in Desert Fishes of the Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1992 issue of the [[Desert Fishes Council]] proceedings (scan partly illegible) contains two linked studies. The first examines the diet and parasite burden of native fishes in the [[Little Colorado River]], Arizona, with a focus on the [[Asian Fish Tapeworm]] (*[[Bothriocephalus Acheilognathi]]*). The second, a Spanish-language summary, reports on birth weights in [[Gambusia Affinis]] (mosquitofish) in relation to predation and prey susceptibility. Abstracts appear in English and Spanish.

## Background & Description

- **Study species (tapeworm study):** [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus*) and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The Spanish abstract refers to these as "charal" and "matalote cabezazul" and "charal jorobado."
- **Life stages examined:** larvae, early post-larvae, juveniles, and adults and sub-adults.
- **Parasite of interest:** *Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*, an Asian fish tapeworm first recorded in the study area as a sudden appearance in host stomachs.
- **Gambusia study:** a Texas-based analysis of *Gambusia affinis* birth weights across populations, with predation and prey-susceptibility variables.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Diet analysis showed that [[Chironomidae]] (midge larvae) were an important trophic group for all three native species. Use of chironomids by larval and early post-larval fish was reduced in the two cyprinids as they aged.
- [[Ostracods]] were used most often by larval and early post-larval fish, and by humpback chub.
- Vascular plant material was prominent in the stomachs of the humpback chub and of larger juveniles. Bluehead suckers often contained inorganic materials.
- Tapeworm incidence in host species is noted as being linked to swimming ability, though the mechanism was not well studied.
- Gambusia birth weights are reported to vary between populations and to correlate negatively with prey susceptibility and positively with parental predation rates. They did not correlate with known geographic or environmental parameters.

## Key Findings

**Tapeworm infection (Little Colorado River)**

- Tapeworms appeared suddenly in host stomachs in 1990 after none were recorded earlier.
- 1989 sample: 3 larvae, 11 early post-larvae and 7 adult and sub-adult fish (the English text reads "47," but the Spanish summary reads "siete," so the count should be verified against the original) were examined and none contained tapeworms.
- 1990 sample: 100% infestation was reported for one group of larval charal (humpback chub/dace, as stated in the scan). 44% of 18 adults examined in 1990 were infested.
- Juvenile charal under about 50 mm were not infested. Infestation began in September.
- Infestation was associated with reduced growth, depressed swimming ability and elevated mortality in the host.
- Low incidence was noted in one species; the authors state this is not well understood.

**Feeding ecology**

- Chironomidae were the key food item for all three species, declining in importance with age for the two cyprinids.
- Ostracods were the most frequently used food for larvae and post-larvae and for humpback chub.

**Gambusia affinis birth weights**

- Birth weights varied among populations, negatively with prey susceptibility and positively with parental predation rates.
- Birth weight was not correlated with geographic or environmental parameters.
- The authors urge caution when applying these results to population trophic dynamics because the dataset is limited.
