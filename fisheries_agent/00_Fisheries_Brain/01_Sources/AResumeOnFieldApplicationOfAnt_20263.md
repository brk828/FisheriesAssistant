# Field Applications of Antimycin A to Control Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-control

## Executive Summary

This 1970 resume by [[Robert E. Lennon]] and [[Bernard L. Berger]] of the [[Fish Control Laboratory]], [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]], reviews more than 50 field applications of [[Antimycin A]], a piscicide, in lakes, ponds, and streams in 19 U.S. States, Canada, and Guatemala. The authors conclude that antimycin is effective against fish from eggs to adults across a wide range of water chemistries and temperatures, is not repellent, and is relatively harmless to invertebrates, amphibians, reptiles, mammals, and birds at fish-killing concentrations. Applications covered total reclamation, partial reclamation, general toxicant use, and selective toxicant use, and the report closes with a 12-step recommendation framework for chemical fish control.

## Background & Description

Antimycin A is an antibiotic produced by *Streptomyces* molds. [[University of Wisconsin]] scientists isolated it in 1945 while screening for crop-destroying fungi. Its potential as a fish toxicant was recognized in 1963, and a use patent was granted to the [[Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation]]. Its toxicity to fish arises from inhibition of electron transport in the respiratory system at a very specific site, so minute quantities are lethal to fish while the doses that kill fish are reported as harmless to mammals and birds.

Key properties reported:
- **Stable when dry but nonpersistent in water**, degrading faster at higher pH.
- **Detoxifiable** with small quantities of [[Potassium Permanganate]] (not registered for use where water is for domestic or agricultural use or where fish are eaten).
- **Odorless and colorless**, and not detected by fish, so it does not repel them.
- Fish die slowly without frenzied activity, and the toxic action is irreversible in most species.

Formulations tested include a liquid form (used in most lab trials and streams) and dry sand-coated granules:
- [[Fintrol-5]]: releases toxicant within the first 5 ft of depth; registered by the U.S. and Canada in 1966 and marketed by Ayerst Laboratories.
- [[Fintrol-15]]: releases within the first 15 ft; designed for deeper, thermally stratified water.
- [[Fintrol-30]]: releases within the first 30 ft.

Two basic variables determine a lethal dose: concentration (in parts per billion, ppb) and duration of exposure. Low concentrations need long exposure, and high concentrations with short exposure can also be lethal. This relationship matters more in flowing water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry and temperature**
- **Acid water:** Antimycin persists longer, so application rates can be reduced.
- **Alkaline water:** Most field applications were in water from pH 7.0 to 9.3. Degradation is faster at higher pH, so greater concentrations are needed at pH 8.5 or above.
- **Cold water:** Fish respond more slowly because of reduced metabolic activity, so higher concentrations may be needed below 50°F. Lower concentrations and faster response are expected above 60°F.

**Non-target effects**
- Fish-killing concentrations were reported as relatively harmless to water weeds, aquatic insects, frogs, salamanders, turtles, mammals, and birds.
- Catfishes (e.g., bullheads) are often resistant, and some other fishes (e.g., [[Gar]], bowfin, goldfish) are classed as resistant in the guidelines.

**Marine exposure**
The [[Bureau of Commercial Fisheries]] exposed marine fishes in a tidal basin near Fort Desoto, Florida. Thirty-eight species received a 6-hour (one tidal cycle) exposure to 7 ppb. Sensitive species, including anchovies, triggerfish, flounders, and rays, were killed. Catfishes, common jack, and redfish did not all succumb.

## Key Findings

### Whole-system and general toxicant applications
- **Acid water, New Hampshire:** 1 ppb eliminated 12 species from a pond within 72 hours. Atlantic salmon, chain pickerel, white sucker, pumpkinseed, largemouth bass, and yellow perch were killed; a few bluegills and smallmouth bass and all brown bullheads survived. In another pond, 12 ppb killed all species except bullheads within 48 hours, and toxic levels persisted for 2 weeks.
- **Acid water, Quebec:** 5 ppb eradicated 13 species from 44-acre [[Beauty Lake]], targeting northern pike, minnows, suckers, pumpkinseeds, and yellow perch to restore brook trout.
- **Alkaline water, Lake Katrine:** 7.5 ppb caused a complete kill of carp and fathead minnows at pH 8.2 and 56°F.
- **Alkaline water, Barney Lake:** 10 ppb killed all carp at pH 8.7 and 57°F. Parker Lake at pH 8.2 and 48°F with 10 ppb killed carp, pumpkinseed, bluegill, and yellow perch.
- **High pH, Whitewater Lake (Nebraska):** At pH 9.3, 7.3 ppb degraded before most carp were killed; bioassays indicated 12–15 ppb were needed.
- **Cold water, under ice:** 5 ppb killed rainbow trout, brown trout, small carp, bluegills, and longear sunfish within 2–7 days at pH 7.4–8.2 and 38–42°F. Survivors included large goldfish and carp, fathead minnows, and pumpkinseeds.
- **[[Lake Creek Lake]] (Wyoming):** The 1.8-acre spring-fed lake was treated with at least 10 ppb for 8 hours. Fish did not flee into springs; most creek chubs were dead within 24 hours, and all fish, including brook trout, were killed and the toxicant flushed within 48 hours.
- **[[Westfield Creek]] (Wisconsin):** A 3.5-mile reach (17.2 cfs, pH 8.8, 77°F) was held at 10 ppb for 10 hours. Carp were mostly dead within 24 hours; electrofishing 1.5 miles a week later found no carp, suckers, or panfish. The authors called it "an unqualified success."

### Partial reclamation
- Antimycin's lack of repellency and effectiveness on all life stages make it well suited to partial reclamation.
- **Carp eggs:** 2 ppb failed to kill all carp eggs in [[Barney Lake]]. In nearby [[Lake Katrine]], 4 ppb caused complete mortality of carp eggs in a treated bay.
- **[[Backbone Lake]] (Iowa):** Carp on spawning sites were exposed to 50 ppb, but a storm diluted the toxicant and ended spawning activity, though many carp succumbed.
- **Gizzard shad (Stuttgart, Arkansas):** Titrations into the inlet flow reduced shad numbers without harming channel catfish.
- **[[McGrath Lake]] (Wisconsin, pH 5.2):** 1 ppb in one bay killed all stunted yellow perch and 50% of stunted bluegills; 2 ppb in another bay eliminated both.

### Selective toxicant applications
- **Stunted perch:** 0.5 ppb killed all stunted perch in Harriet and Perch lakes without harming northern pike, bullheads, or bass.
- **Georgia farm ponds:** 0.4–0.8 ppb in 1–9 acre ponds reduced minnows, sunfish, and crappies without significant harm to largemouth bass.
- **2.5-acre bass-bluegill pond:** 0.4 ppb killed 127 lb of golden shiners, 172 lb of stunted bluegills, 22 lb of fingerling crappies, and 7 lb of fingerling bass; no adult bass or crappies died, and adult bass reproduction was not impaired.
- **Catfish ponds (Mississippi):** 10 ppb killed at least 99% of scalefish despite high pH, with no catfish losses. Catfish yield averaged 330 lb per acre more than in untreated ponds.
- **[[Victory Lake]] (Fort Benning, Georgia, 26 acres):** 3.6 ppb in the lake and 10 ppb in tributaries killed more than 5,200 lb of scalefish (210 lb/acre). Only one channel catfish and one bullhead died; the project was declared a success.
- **[[Pleasant Lake]] (Minnesota, 80 ft deep):** Fintrol-15 and Fintrol-5 were used to treat the warm epilimnion (about 15 ft thick) to reach 5 ppb, eliminating minnows while managed rainbow trout in deeper cold water were largely unaffected (minor trout kill).
- **Beauty Lake (Quebec):** Fintrol-30 at 5 ppb over the 44-acre basin (maximum depth 42 ft) eradicated target fish. American smelt, not previously recorded in the lake, rose dead to the surface; only brown bullheads survived netting.

### Detoxification and recommendations
- **[[Rathbone Creek]] (Wisconsin):** Stream treatment at 7.5 ppb for at least 8 hours eliminated minnows and suckers. Cataract Pond was detoxified with 2 ppm potassium permanganate, and effluent did not harm rainbow trout in cages 500 ft downstream.
- **Guidelines (Table 5):** For sensitive fish at pH below 7.5, suggested Antimycin A concentrations were 5.0 ppb (water above 60°F) and 7.5 ppb (below 60°F); for resistant fish, 15 and 20 ppb.
- **Process:** The 12-step framework requires defining the problem, assessing populations, evaluating alternatives (with cost-benefit ratios), bioassays using target water and species, live cages to monitor toxicant persistence (fish surviving 48 hours indicates degradation), postponing treatment if conditions change, prompt disposal of dead fish, post-reclamation assessment, and reporting.
