# Hybridization in the Family Esocidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 paper by E. J. Crossman and Keen Buss reviews natural and artificial hybridization among North American members of the pike family [[Esocidae]]. It documents six known natural interspecific hybrids, one natural subspecific intergrade, and adds a previously unrecorded natural hybrid of [[Northern Pike]] and [[Redfin Pickerel]]. The authors also describe 12 new reciprocal hybrid combinations, produced mainly at the [[Benner Springs Research Station]], and compare their morphology, growth, fertility, and vitality with parental populations. The central finding is that interspecific sterility in the family is low, with at least one reciprocal cross succeeding in every pairing tested. Natural hybridization appears limited by distribution, habitat, size, and behaviour rather than by gamete incompatibility.

## Background & Description

**Scope and sources.** The authors reviewed published records worldwide and combined them with new data. Analyses covered 224 specimens of hybrids and parents. Measurements and counts followed [[Hubbs and Lagler 1958]] conventions, including standard length (SL), snout length (SnL), and counts of [[Cardioid Scales]], lateral line scales, branchiostegal rays, submandibular pores, and vertebrae. Fish were fixed in 10% formalin and stored in 65% ethyl alcohol.

**Species involved.** The paper covers five taxa:
- [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy*), the largest member
- [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*), including the colour mutant [[Silver Pike]]
- [[Chain Pickerel]] (*Esox niger*)
- Redfin pickerel (*Esox americanus americanus*)
- [[Grass Pickerel]] (*Esox americanus vermiculatus*)

The two *E. americanus* forms are subspecifically distinct, so their crosses are better termed intergrades.

**Reciprocal crosses.** Hybrid names put the male parent first when gamete origin is known. Several earlier records (e.g., the Minnesota work of Eddy) could not be assigned to a particular gamete source.

**Hybrid counts.** The abstract reports 11 possible hybrids, six known from nature, plus a newly recorded natural hybrid of Northern Pike and Redfin Pickerel. Of 22 possible reciprocal combinations, five were previously known, five remained unknown, and 12 are newly described. The Discussion gives a different total (16 of 20 North American reciprocal crosses created), so the counts are not fully consistent within the paper.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution and overlap.** Hybridization depends on range overlap. Muskellunge and Chain Pickerel overlap in West Virginia, Tennessee, Kentucky, Quebec, New York, and Vermont. Muskellunge and Redfin Pickerel overlap only in the Lake Champlain, Richelieu River, and Lac St. Pierre area. Grass Pickerel has a much larger range overlap with Muskellunge across Ontario, New York, and the Upper Mississippi Valley.

**Isolating mechanisms.** The authors propose that habitat segregation, large differences in size at sexual maturity, and possible differences in nuptial behaviour keep many pairs apart even where ranges and spawning times overlap. Spawning behaviour, egg-laying method, preferred spawning depth, and the largely solitary habits of spawning pairs reduce the chance of passive hybridization. Natural hybrids most likely result from active mating between a male of one species and a female of another.

**Human influence.** Hybrids can arise when hatcheries substitute pike sperm for unripe male muskellunge, producing fish released as muskellunge. The authors also note that habitat change and introductions may bring species closer together and increase hybrid frequency. Such hybrids complicate identification, law enforcement, and studies of original stocks.

**Biogeography.** The authors cite [[Hubbs 1955]], who linked the high hybrid frequency in temperate freshwaters to the short period since glaciation. No hybrids have been recorded in Europe or Asia. The two Eurasian forms, *Esox lucius* and *E. reicherti*, do not overlap, and the authors know of no experimental hybrid between them.

## Key Findings

### Natural hybrids

- **Muskellunge × Northern Pike** is the best-known and most valuable cross. Records date from [[Weed 1927]] and include the Little Vermillion Lake tiger muskellunge, which [[Cameron 1948]] found to be a sterile hybrid. A viable backcross with pike was reported in Wisconsin.
- **Northern Pike × Chain Pickerel** was the first natural hybrid recorded in the family, reported by [[Embody 1918]] from Cayuga Lake, New York.
- **Northern Pike × Grass Pickerel** natural hybrids are documented from Nebraska, Michigan, and Wisconsin.
- **Chain Pickerel × Redfin Pickerel** is the best-known natural hybrid after the muskellunge–pike cross. [[Raney 1955]] provided the most thorough study, from Stearns Pond, Massachusetts.
- **Chain Pickerel × Grass Pickerel** is poorly known, with records from Missouri and Ohio.
- **Northern Pike × Redfin Pickerel** is newly recorded from Lac St. Pierre, Quebec (ROM 21977, 316 mm TL) and from Lake Champlain (MCZ 6464). Its colour pattern is unlike either parent.

### Morphology and meristics

- **Muskellunge × Northern Pike** hybrids had intermediate vertebrae (pike 57–61, mean 59.8; muskellunge 63–67, mean 64.4; one hybrid had 63). Their lateral line counts were far higher (143 versus means of 115 for pike and 170 for muskellunge).
- Reciprocal crosses differed in colour pattern. The muskellunge male × pike female fish had longer, narrower bars, while the reverse cross had oval spots on the sides and a prominent spot on the anal fin.
- **Chain Pickerel × Muskellunge** showed a branchiostegal ray count of 5 at ray bases but 8 at tips, caused by bifurcated rays.
- Across all crosses, extralimital character values rose from the largest species (about 4% in muskellunge crosses) to the two smallest subspecific forms (about 13–14% in *E. americanus* crosses).

### Growth

- Muskellunge × Northern Pike hybrids grow faster than either parent. Hybrids released at Roger's Creek, Ontario, grew from 2.5–5.5 inches (July 8, 1964) to 8.3 inches (August 15, 1964).
- Pike × Chain Pickerel hybrids reached 11.75 inches by October 1 (Armbruster). Underhill's two-year-old hybrids measured 368 and 416 mm TL.
- Pike × Redfin Pickerel hybrids reached 2 years at about the average size of pike, far larger than the redfin parent.

### Fertility and sterility

- Muskellunge hybrids were largely sterile, though a 4-year-old female had eggs that were not released (Eddy). Eddy reported hatch rates of 22–45% for F₂ hybrid eggs against 1–8% for muskellunge eggs.
- Pike × Chain Pickerel hybrids: Embody recorded a 70% hatch. Armbruster recorded 62% hatch in 14 days. Gonads of smaller fish were undeveloped.
- Chain Pickerel × Redfin Pickerel hybrids are fertile: backcrosses are common in nature, and fertile females and males have been documented.
- Grass Pickerel × Muskellunge hybrids: about 75% of several hundred eggs hatched in Ohio. One 18-month-old female had immature ovaries.
- The authors found that most hybrid males were sterile, but well-developed eggs appeared in many hybrid females.

### Interspecific sterility

- The authors found "surprisingly" low interspecific sterility. Both reciprocals succeeded in every cross except several failed pairings.
- Failed crosses included Muskellunge ♂ × Redfin Pickerel ♀, Chain Pickerel ♂ × Northern Pike ♀, and Redfin Pickerel ♂ × Northern Pike ♀. Northern Pike ♀ × Grass Pickerel ♂ gave only three hatched eggs and one survivor.
- The authors conclude that some interspecific sterility exists between the two larger and two smaller species, but total sterility does not. They suggest the family is too young for intersterility to have fully developed.

### Vitality

- Scott (1964) reported increased thermal resistance in muskellunge × pike hybrids compared with parents.
- Anglers consider the muskellunge × pike hybrid more sporting than pike.
- Pike × Chain Pickerel hybrids survived a pond exceeding 70 °F when many chain pickerel died.
- Grass Pickerel × Muskellunge hybrids showed hybrid vigour at 7 months but were thought to be short-lived and vulnerable to angling.

### Silver Pike

- [[Silver Pike]] is a true-breeding colour mutant of Northern Pike. Eddy's experiments used its eggs and milt. Silver pike × pike hybrids showed high fry mortality and speckled patterns resembling the black crappie.

### Conclusions

- Colour patterns in hybrids were linked to particular parents. Crosses with Muskellunge often produced spots on the unpaired fins. Chain Pickerel crosses often had forked lateral bars. Pike crosses kept the juvenile pike pattern.
- The authors conclude that muskellunge, pike, and the pickerels differ in distribution and size rather than in genetic compatibility. Chromosome number, morphology, and biochemical characters do not vary enough to cause intersterility.
- Future identification of suspected hybrids in the wild can draw on the reciprocal-cross data presented.
