# Federal Register Notices on Crocodilian and Freshwater Mussel Conservation (1990)

#freshwater-biology #crocodylia #unionidae #freshwater-mussels #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register compilation (October 29, 1990) contains three U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service actions under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It contains no fish-focused research. The first is a proposed-rule request for information on [[Nile Crocodile]] and [[Yacare Caiman]] populations, tied to [[CITES]] transfers and a possible similarity-of-appearance listing. The second is a 90-day petition finding that does not support listing the [[California Floater]], a freshwater mussel. The third is a notice reopening the comment period and scheduling a public hearing on the proposed endangered listing of the [[Ouachita Rock-Pocketbook]], another freshwater mussel.

## Background & Description

**Crocodilians (CITES Request for Comments)**
- The notice concerns transferring Nile crocodile populations in Ethiopia and Somalia from [[CITES Appendix I]] to [[CITES Appendix II]] under Conf. 5.21.
- Wild-population information was incomplete at the time of transfer. Some countries have since developed better status data and management programs, so each country's population will be evaluated separately.
- The Service requested data on the present status, distribution, biology, and conservation measures for wild and ranched Nile crocodile and yacare caiman.
- The yacare caiman is the focus of a possible [[Similarity of Appearance]] listing for *Caiman crocodilus crocodilus* and *C. c. fuscus*, both listed in CITES Appendix II. Under [[Section 4(e)]] of the Act and 50 CFR 17.50, such treatment requires three determinations: (1) resemblance creates substantial enforcement difficulty, (2) this difficulty adds a threat to the listed species, and (3) controlling trade in the similar species will substantially aid protection. Any such decision depends first on the results of the yacare status review.
- Live yacare are largely distinguishable from the common and brown caimans. Skins, hides, and products from cut pieces may be difficult or impossible to identify to subspecies.
- Cited sources include reports on the [[CITES Central South America Caiman Study]] (Brazil), the Pantanal caiman, the East/Central Africa Nile crocodile project (Hutton 1989, 52 pp.), Bolivian caimans (King and Videz Roca 1987, 39 pp.), and Paraguayan caimans (Scott et al., 30 pp.).
- The notice was prepared by Dr. Richard M. Mitchell, Office of Scientific Authority. It was signed by Acting Director Richard N. Smith on October 19, 1990.

**California Floater (90-Day Petition Finding)**
- A petition dated December 20, 1989, from Thomas Hulen of the Pueblo Grande Museum (Phoenix, Arizona) was received December 26, 1989, requesting listing as endangered or threatened.
- The finding, made September 18, 1990, concluded that the petition did not present substantial information that listing was warranted.
- The species is a freshwater mussel. Its larvae are parasitic on the gills, fins, or barbels of fish; host specificity is unknown. Adults live in sand and mud on stream bottoms. Lifespan is unknown, but closely related species live 10 to 15 years.

**Ouachita Rock-Pocketbook (Reopened Comment Period and Hearing)**
- The species is known only from an approximately 80-mile reach of the Kiamichi River upstream of Hugo Reservoir (Oklahoma) and a 5-mile segment of the Little River in southwestern Arkansas.
- A proposed endangered listing was published July 23, 1990 (55 FR 29865).
- On August 24, 1990, the Service received a written hearing request from Bill Rowton, City Manager of Antlers, Oklahoma.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Crocodilians:** Conservation of Nile crocodile and yacare caiman depends on wild-population status, ranching, and trade controls. Look-alike skins complicate enforcement, which is the basis for the similarity-of-appearance question.
- **California Floater:** Threats are mainly riverine habitat alteration, including channelization, dredging, impoundment, erosion, siltation, water diversion, groundwater pumping, pollution, and watershed modification. Host fish depletion from habitat alteration and from competition and predation by non-native fishes is also a concern. The introduced Asian clam (*Corbicula* sp.; spelled *manilensis* in this notice) may compete with it.
- **Historic range loss in Arizona:** The species was historically widespread in Arizona but is now restricted to one area of the upper Black River and possibly Chevelon Creek. Colorado River populations were lost to dams, channelization, diversion, and dredging. Santa Cruz and San Pedro River populations were lost to drying from diversion, pumping, and watershed change. Los Angeles-area populations were extirpated by channelization and habitat modification.
- **Ouachita Rock-Pocketbook:** Threats are reservoir construction, water quality degradation, and possible invasion by the exotic Asiatic clam (*Corbicula fluminea*).

## Key Findings

- **Nile crocodile:** Ethiopia and Somalia populations are proposed for transfer from CITES Appendix I to Appendix II. Each country will be evaluated separately, and the Service is soliciting new status data.
- **Yacare caiman:** The Service is considering whether the similar-looking *C. c. crocodilus* and *C. c. fuscus* should be treated as endangered or threatened under Section 4(e). Live animals are mostly distinguishable, but skins and products are often not. The decision awaits the yacare status review.
- **California floater:** The petition did not present substantial information warranting listing (finding of September 18, 1990). The species will be added to the candidate list as a Category 2 species, meaning listing is possibly appropriate but conclusive data are lacking. Data are absent for most of its range.
- **Ouachita rock-pocketbook:** The comment period is reopened November 14 to December 4, 1990. A public hearing will run 7 p.m. to 11 p.m. on November 19, 1990, at the Antlers Community Center, Antlers, Oklahoma. Oral statements may be time-limited, but written materials are not. Comments received after December 4 may not be considered.
- **Overall pattern:** Two of the three actions involve freshwater mussels whose survival depends on riverine habitat integrity and, for the floater, on host fish. Both are threatened by dams, diversion, and introduced species.
