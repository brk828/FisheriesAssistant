## Executive Summary

This document is a **Biological Opinion Amendment** issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) amending a 1995 biological opinion on livestock grazing management (Baseline/Horse Springs Allotment Management Plan) administered by the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests. Rather than a primary morphological or ecological research paper, the source addresses regulatory consultation under the Endangered Species Act (ESA) Section 7, concerning effects of a revised grazing management system on four federally listed species: the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and the [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*). The amendment revises grazing regimes on the Baseline and Horse Springs allotments (Greenlee County, Arizona), concluding that the revised "seasonally deferred rotation" grazing system is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of any of the four species, while anticipating limited **incidental take** of the three fish species through habitat alteration and direct mortality risk.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide morphological descriptions, meristic data, or physical characteristics of the fish species discussed. It references the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]] only in the context of population status, distribution, and habitat associations within the Gila River drainage system. No taxonomic, anatomical, or physiological data are presented.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat association**: All three listed fish species are associated with perennial stream habitats in the upper Gila River basin, specifically **Eagle Creek** and its tributary canyons (Bear Canyon, Bee Springs Canyon) within the Baseline/Horse Springs grazing allotments.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** distribution is documented as persisting in limited portions of the upper Gila, San Francisco, Blue, Black, Tularosa, and White Rivers, and in Aravaipa, Eagle, Campbell Blue, and Dry Blue Creeks (Arizona/New Mexico). The species was presumed **extirpated** from Eagle Creek at the time critical habitat was proposed in 1986, but was later **rediscovered** there, prompting its identification in the recovery plan as a potential reintroduction/recovery site.
- **[[Spikedace]]** population in Eagle Creek was **unknown at the time critical habitat was proposed** (1985) and thus was excluded from both the original critical habitat proposal and the 1994 final rule. The species' recovery plan recommends **adding Eagle Creek to future critical habitat designations**.
- **Riparian-stream linkage**: The biological opinion emphasizes the tight coupling between livestock grazing impacts on riparian vegetation/streambank structure and habitat quality for these fish species — e.g., watershed alteration, sedimentation, and streambank degradation directly affect spawning/rearing habitat and channel stability.
- **Trophic/habitat link to Bald Eagle**: The wintering bald eagle food base along Eagle Creek is explicitly tied to the same fish-supporting riparian/watershed factors affecting the listed fish, illustrating a shared ecological dependency between piscivorous avian predators and native fish prey populations.
- **Nonnative fish risk**: New artificial water developments (storage tanks, drinkers) were evaluated for their potential to create novel habitat for invasive/nonnative fish species that could otherwise threaten native fish via competition or predation. Design mitigations (enclosed containers, small drinker volume, remote siting away from flood-prone drainages) were implemented to minimize this risk.

## Key Findings

- **No Jeopardy Determination**: The Service concluded that the revised 1997 seasonally deferred rotation grazing system is **not likely to jeopardize** the [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], or [[Bald Eagle]].
- **Critical Habitat Status**: Critical habitat designations for spikedace and loach minnow were **set aside/enjoined** by New Mexico District Court ruling (*Catron County Board of Commissioners v. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service*, 1994; affirmed 1996), removing the need for critical habitat consultation for these species in this action.
- **Improved Grazing Management**: The revised plan removes livestock grazing entirely from approximately 3 miles of Eagle Creek streambank/riparian zone within the allotments and institutes a full rest period during the summer growing season (June–August), predicted to yield:
  - 35% increase in upland vegetation reaching good-to-excellent condition
  - 45% increase in riparian areas reaching satisfactory condition
  - 24% increase in satisfactory soil conditions
- **Incidental Take Anticipated**: Despite the no-jeopardy finding, incidental take of [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] is anticipated via:
  - Direct fish loss during construction/maintenance of cross-river exclosure fences
  - Cattle incursions into the creek during temporary fence breaches
  - Indirect habitat destruction/alteration from watershed-level grazing effects on stream channel morphology
- **No Anticipated Take of Bald Eagle**: Unlike the fish species, the Service does **not** anticipate incidental take of the bald eagle under the revised grazing system.
- **Water Development Safeguards**: New water infrastructure (47,000 ft of pipeline, storage tanks, 13 drinkers) draws from a non-alluvial well (not Eagle Creek) and incorporates design features (artificial materials, small drinker size, remote placement) to prevent facilitation of nonnative fish establishment and to avoid flood-driven fish escape into native waters.
- **Riparian Vegetation & Soil Condition Baseline**:
  - Upland vegetation trend: generally stable (50% upward, 21% stable, 29% downward)
  - Riparian vegetation condition: 0% excellent, 51% good-to-fair, 49% poor-to-very poor (degraded, low regeneration)
  - Soil condition: 41% satisfactory, 37% impaired, 22% unsatisfactory (generally stable, 20–30% vertisol soils)
- **Legal/Administrative Context**: This amendment exemplifies the ESA Section 7 consultation process, including reinitiation protocols, incidental take statements, and the interplay between federal land management (grazing allotments) and endangered aquatic species conservation in the arid Southwest (Gila River basin).
