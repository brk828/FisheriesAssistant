# Sex Determination and the Restriction of Sex-linked Pigment Patterns in *Xiphophorus maculatus* from British Honduras

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This study tests whether the X sex chromosome is absent from the [[Belize River]] and [[Sibun River]] populations of the [[Southern Platyfish]] (*Xiphophorus maculatus*), as earlier, limited work suggested. Breeding tests on wild-caught fish from three collecting stations found that the W and Y chromosomes predominate, with only two X-bearing fish among the tested individuals. Among 27 marked females carrying a W, the 41 sex-linked pigment factors recovered were none W-linked. Analysis of preserved collections showed patterned males at two to three times the frequency of patterned females. The author concludes that the W chromosome of natural populations carries no pigment factors, while the X does, and proposes that sexual selection favouring bright males and predation disfavouring them maintains this pattern.

## Background & Description

The species is a live-bearing poeciliid whose sex is determined by a W, X, Y system. Females may be WY, WX or XX; males may be XY or YY. Earlier work had classed the British Honduras populations as WY-YY, with the XX-XY system confined to Mexican rivers. Subsequent sampling across major drainages, summarized in [[Kallman 1965]], showed that W and X chromosomes coexist across roughly 70 percent of the species range.

Sex genotypes were determined by test crosses to reference stocks, using sex-linked marker patterns. Males were classed by whether all-male broods (YY) or mixed broods (XY) resulted. Females were classed by sex ratio and by the inheritance of paternal and maternal patterns in their offspring. Collected fish were selected for distinctive pigment combinations, so the breeding sample is biased toward marked individuals.

Sex-linked pigment patterns described include:
- **Macromelanophore patterns:** Nigra (N), Spotted-dorsal (Sd), Forward spotted-dorsal (f-Sd), Stripe-sided (Sr), and Spot-sided (Sp), with [[Sp Allele Variants]] Sp* and Sp° distinguished.
- **Red and yellow body/fin patterns:** Caudal peduncle orange (CPo), Anal red (Ar), Mouth red (Mr), Anal yellow (Ay), Body red (Br), Ventral orange (Vo), Dorsal red (Dr), Tail yellow (Ty).
- **Iris patterns:** Iris yellow (Iy) and Iris red (Ir).

Pigment genes are located on the sex-differential region, which is comparable across the related species described by [[Kallman and Atz 1967]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Collections were made in January 1966 at three stations: Freetown (Sibun River, a small weedy pond seined along 20 m of shore), Bermudian Landing (a shallow, broad lagoon in a cow pasture, [[Mamre Farm]]), and Gabourel Creek (a 1–2 m deep ditch near Stanley Field airport, Belize). The Sibun and Belize River populations represent a small part of the species' range, which runs from [[Veracruz]] in southern Mexico to British Honduras. Smaller coastal populations south of the Sibun River, covering under one percent of the range, were not sampled.

Sex-linked pigment patterns are considered to be under selection. The author suggests that bright coloration in males aids courtship and agonistic behaviour but raises predation risk, while dull coloration may suit females. Comparable mechanisms have been proposed for [[Guppy]] (*Poecilia reticulata*), in which sexual selection and predation are thought to maintain pigment polymorphism.

## Key Findings

**Sex chromosome constitution**
- Of 8 males tested, 7 were YY (all-male broods) and 1 (1901-12, Belize River, Gabourel Creek) was XY, with Ar and Sr linked on the X.
- Of 30 females tested, 29 carried a W chromosome (WY or WX) and 1 was XX. The XX female was from Bermudian Landing.
- Sibun River: no X chromosome identified among 2 males and 10 females.
- Belize River: one of six males was XY, and one of 20 females was XX. The other 19 females were WY.
- One WY female was fertilized by an unknown XY male before capture.

**Absence of W-linked pigment factors**
- Of the 29 W-bearing females, all but two showed one or more sex-linked patterns controlled by at least three loci (iris, red/yellow body, macromelanophore).
- The 27 marked females possessed 41 pigment factors in total. None was W-linked.
- Even counting each pattern combination once per location, the 19 differently marked females carried 28 factors, none W-linked.

**Frequency of patterned males versus females**
- Across seven preserved collections, patterned males occurred two to three times as often as patterned females.
- Under the assumption that the W carries no pigment alleles, this difference was significant in most collections. Under the alternative assumption that the W carries the wild-type allele, six of seven collections fitted the data well.

**Crossing over and selection**
- Crossing over between W and Y was observed in the laboratory and must therefore occur in nature, which would tend to equalize marked W and Y frequencies in the absence of selection.
- Crossing over between sex-differential and macromelanophore loci occurs at about 0.2 to 0.3 percent frequency. Only three crossovers were observed in this study.
- The author postulates that high coloration is advantageous in males and disadvantageous in females, and that the W chromosome serves as a vehicle for strictly maternal inheritance.

**Conclusions**
- The hypothesis that the X is absent from British Honduras rivers can no longer be maintained.
- The W chromosome of natural populations carries no pigment factors, in contrast to the X.
- The W is widespread and may have arisen as a means of maintaining pigmentary polymorphism in males.
