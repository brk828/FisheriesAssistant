## Executive Summary
This 1993 memorandum from BIO/WEST, Inc. (on behalf of the Hualapai Wildlife Management Department's Aquatic Investigation) notifies federal, state, and tribal agencies of a planned young-of-year (YOY) sucker collection effort in the Lower [[Grand Canyon]] segment of the [[Colorado River]]. The primary objective is to determine whether the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is spawning in tributary canyons near [[Lake Mead]], using genetic (DNA) analysis of larval/juvenile specimens to distinguish them from the morphologically similar [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*). Prior 1992 surveys found numerous young suckers in several tributary canyons, but all examined specimens were field-identified as flannelmouth suckers based on external morphology.

## Background & Description
- **Target specimen size**: Young-of-the-year suckers, defined as **<75 mm total length (TL)**.
- **Field identification method**: External morphological examination of live specimens prior to release (in 1992 surveys) or freezing (planned 1993 collections).
- **Species distinguished**:
  - [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) — confirmed present as both adults and YOY in the survey region; all YOY examined to date matched this species' external morphology.
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — target species of concern; not visually confirmed in the region as of the memo date, but suspected based on proximity to known adult concentrations in Lake Mead.
- **Limitation noted**: Because juvenile catostomids (flannelmouth vs. razorback sucker) are difficult to distinguish morphologically at small sizes, genetic (DNA) analysis was deemed necessary for definitive species identification, to be conducted by Dr. Tom Dowling (Arizona State University).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Lower Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River between Separation Canyon (River Mile 240) and Pearce Ferry (River Mile 280), including associated tributary canyon mouths.
- **Tributaries of interest**: Separation Canyon, Spencer Canyon, [[Surprise Canyon]], Lost Canyon, and [[Quartermaster Canyon]] — all documented to contain young suckers during July and September 1992 surveys.
- **Spawning hypothesis**: The proximity of these tributaries to known [[Razorback Sucker]] concentrations in [[Lake Mead]] raised the hypothesis of **spawning migration (ascents)** by adult razorback suckers into these tributary canyons, despite no direct adult sightings in the study area.
- **Adult population context**: Adult [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] have been captured in the study region, confirming an established breeding population of this species locally.
- **Conservation relevance**: The study links a broader regional recovery effort for the endangered razorback sucker, connecting Grand Canyon tributary habitats to the larger Lake Mead population as potential unrecognized spawning/nursery habitat.

## Key Findings
- Up to 20 YOY suckers (<75 mm TL) were planned for collection per trip, across 4 trips in 1993 (March, June, September, December).
- 1992 fishery surveys (July and September) documented **numerous young suckers** in five named tributary canyons of the Lower Grand Canyon.
- All young suckers **externally examined and released** during 1992 surveys were identified as [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], not razorback sucker.
- No razorback suckers (adult or juvenile) have been directly observed or captured in the study region as of the memo's writing.
- Despite the absence of direct evidence, researchers considered the numbers and proximity to Lake Mead's razorback sucker population sufficient to justify targeted genetic sampling.
- Collected specimens were to be weighed, measured, and field-identified before preservation in liquid nitrogen (or similar) for transport to ASU for DNA analysis.
- This work was conducted under the **Hualapai Aquatic Investigations** project, coordinated among USFWS, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Grand Canyon National Park, and the Hualapai Wildlife Management Department, with results intended for inclusion in trip reports, annual reports, and a final project report.
