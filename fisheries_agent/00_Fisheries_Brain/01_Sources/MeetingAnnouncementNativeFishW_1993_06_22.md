## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 administrative faxogram from the Bureau of Reclamation (Lower Colorado Region) announcing a meeting of the **[[Native Fish Work Group]]** (NFWG) at Katherine's Landing, Arizona. The meeting's purpose was to review 1992 progress on the **[[Lake Mohave Native Fish Rearing Program]]**, discuss ongoing 1993 rearing/monitoring actions, and assign work for the remainder of the year. The central conservation target species is the endangered **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), with a notable reported success: five hatchery/pond-reared razorback suckers were recaptured during the spring spawning season, four of which were ripe males — considered a milestone toward re-establishing a self-sustaining spawning population in **[[Lake Mohave]]**.

## Background & Description
The source is a management/administrative document and does not provide direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body measurements). The only biologically relevant condition noted is **reproductive status**: four of five recaptured razorback suckers were identified as **ripe males**, indicating active spermiation and readiness to spawn — a key indicator used by biologists to assess reproductive viability of rearing-program fish returned to the wild.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The document outlines a multi-site rearing and reintroduction strategy for razorback suckers within the Lake Mohave system, reflecting the species' dependence on specific nursery and adult habitats:

- **[[Yuma Cove]]** – Used for larval/juvenile production; management topics included liner and macrophyte placement, reclamation via rotenone treatment, light trap sampling, and monitoring of **odonate** (dragonfly/damselfly) larvae, likely as either predators or prey/competitors within the cove nursery habitat.
- **[[Davis Cove]]** – Managed as a predator-exclusion nursery using a barrier net; activities included predator removal, fish stocking, and net-integrity/SCUBA monitoring to protect stocked larvae and juveniles from predation.
- **New coves** – Additional rearing coves were being brought online, with monitoring of stocking chronology and outcomes.
- **Main Lake (Lake Mohave)** – Adult population assessed via "March Roundup" surveys, representing the open-lake habitat where reared fish are ultimately released and must survive to spawn.
- Related regional efforts mentioned: **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery|Dexter Production]]**, **Lake Havasu**, **No-Name Lake**, and **Pretty Water** — indicating a broader **[[Lower Basin Native Fish Management Plan]]** spanning multiple Colorado River impoundments.

This reflects the razorback sucker's ecological requirement for **predator-free, vegetated backwater nursery habitats** during early life stages, transitioning to open lake/reservoir habitat as adults — a life-history bottleneck driving the cove-rearing conservation approach.

## Key Findings
- **Reintroduction success indicator**: 5 razorback suckers reared in 1992 were recaptured in spring 1993 during the spawning period; **4 of 5 were ripe males**, demonstrating that pond-reared fish can survive, mature, and reach reproductive condition in the wild — validating the rearing methodology.
- **Population deficit remains**: Despite this milestone, program leaders (Tom Burke) explicitly noted the effort remains "well short" of objectives for replacing/recruiting adult razorback suckers into the Lake Mohave population, underscoring the species' critically low natural recruitment.
- **Predator control is a major management lever**: Davis Cove's barrier-net system and active predator removal are treated as essential interventions, implying non-native predatory fish pose a significant threat to larval/juvenile razorback sucker survival.
- **Habitat engineering (liners, berms, macrophytes)**: Physical cove modifications (pond liners, berm integrity, aquatic vegetation) were actively managed and monitored, suggesting substrate/vegetation conditions are critical variables for larval rearing success.
- **Multi-agency, multi-site coordination**: The NFWG integrated efforts across Yuma Cove, Davis Cove, new coves, Dexter Hatchery production, and additional water bodies (Lake Havasu, No-Name Lake, Pretty Water), reflecting a coordinated basin-wide strategy under the **[[Lower Basin Native Fish Management Plan]]** rather than a single-site effort.
- **Operational constraints**: Lake Mohave water level projections were flagged as a new business item, indicating reservoir hydrology management is directly tied to native fish rearing/reintroduction planning.
