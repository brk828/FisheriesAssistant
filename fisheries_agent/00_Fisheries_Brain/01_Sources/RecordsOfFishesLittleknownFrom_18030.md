# Records of Fishes Little-Known from Kansas, and Intergradation of the Bullhead Minnow

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #percidae #ictaluridae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a two-part note from the *Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science* (Vol. 57, No. 4, 1954). The first and main part, by Frank B. Cross of the [[University Of Kansas]], reports new distributional records for ten fish species that had seldom or never been reported from Kansas. The second part, by Raymond Hampton of [[Midwestern University]] (Wichita Falls, Texas), examines hybridization and intergradation between the [[Parrot Minnow]] and the [[Bullhead Minnow]] in the Wichita River system of Texas. Most records come from collections made over three years by ichthyology classes and State Biological Survey assignments.

## Background & Description

Cross's note covers species that have long been known to occur in Kansas state-wide, but whose local collection localities were lacking. Records are given by drainage system and county, with exact localities and [[University Of Kansas]] catalogue numbers (KU) for records based on a few specimens. For widespread species, only drainage and county are given, and specimens are omitted. Each account lists previous records, new localities, diagnostic characters and discussion. Specimens are deposited in the [[University Of Kansas Natural History Museum]].

Hampton's note concerns the Wichita River, which lies within the range of the Parrot Minnow (*Pimephales vigilax vigilax*). The Bullhead Minnow (*P. v. perspicuus*) was probably introduced into the area through bait minnows, and the two forms have intergraded.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Kansas fauna.** Many of the species are [[Ozark Uplift]] or Ozarkian in affinity, restricted largely to the [[Spring River]] and [[Shoal Creek]] area of Cherokee County in the southeast. Others, such as the [[Ghost Shiner]] and [[Freckled Madtom]], are common across eastern Kansas. Several species prefer clear, cool, spring-fed water or riffles and cannot survive intermittent streams.

**Introduced species.** The [[Mosquitofish]] is probably not native to Kansas. It was introduced widely across the United States and is now established in the [[Arkansas River]] system.

**Pollution.** The [[Neosho River]] at Chetopa showed severe domestic pollution (strong odors, grey water colour, sludge deposits). Local riffles and shallows, together with low water levels, still supported an abundant and varied fish fauna.

**Bait-driven introductions.** Lakes built in the Wichita Valley increased fishing pressure. Exotic minnows, sold at lakeside bait stands, were released into local waters, which allowed the Bullhead Minnow to spread.

## Key Findings

### Cross (1954): Kansas Records

**1. Ozark Minnow (*Dionda nubila*)**
- New locality: Shoal Creek, Cherokee County (KU 2192). A single specimen was taken on April 17, 1952.
- The species barely enters Kansas and probably does not occur west of the Neosho River system.
- Graham (1885) listed it as *D. meeki*, a nomen nudum. Jordan and Gilbert (1887) clarified that the names refer to the same species.

**2. Bigeye Shiner (*Notropis boops*)**
- New localities: Shoal Creek (KU 2193, 2892, 3192), Spring River (KU 2176) and Brush Creek near Riverton (KU 1346), all in Cherokee County.
- Abundant in Shoal Creek. In Kansas it appears restricted to a small Cherokee County area with Ozarkian affinities.

**3. Ghost Shiner (*Notropis buchanani*)**
- New localities are numerous: Spring River System (Cherokee County), Neosho River (Lyon County) and the Marais des Cygnes–Osage System, including the Marmaton River (Anderson, Bourbon, Franklin, Linn, Miami and Osage counties).
- Common across much of eastern Kansas despite few references. Separated from *N. volucella* by enlarged anterior lateral-line scales and related characters.

**4. Spotfin Shiner (*Notropis spilopterus*)**
- No previous Kansas records. New localities are in the Spring River System (Cherokee County), with the species most common in Shoal Creek.
- Seined on April 11, 1954, it was much less abundant than *N. camura* but more abundant than *N. lutrensis*. All three often occurred in the same seine haul.

**5. Freckled Madtom (*Schilbeodes nocturnus*)**
- Previously reported by Cross (1954). New localities include the Neosho River in Coffey, Labette and Lyon counties, a tributary of Four-Mile Creek in Sedgwick County (Walnut River drainage), and the Chikaskia River.
- Occurs widely but is not abundant at any locality. Prefers sluggish creeks and muddy banks over riffles.

**6. Mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis affinis*)**
- No previous records. Numerous new localities across the Arkansas River System, including the Spring, Neosho, Verdigris, Walnut and Elm Creek drainages.
- Probably introduced; now well established and often abundant. Its mosquito control value is debatable where other native fishes are abundant.

**7. River Darter (*Hadropterus shumardi*)**
- New locality: Neosho River at Chetopa, Labette County (KU 3208).
- Eight specimens were seined on April 10, 1954, from moderately fast riffles below a dam. Four males showed well-developed anal-fin tubercles. Females had developed eggs.

**8. Bluntnose Darter (*Etheostoma chlorosomum*)**
- No previous records. Three new localities in the Spring River System: Second Cow Creek, Crawford County (KU 2254, 2932); an unnamed creek in Cherokee County (KU 1384); and a roadside pond in Cherokee County (KU 1362).
- Single specimens were taken from each of two Second Cow Creek collections. One female was in spawning condition on April 10, 1953. The two Cherokee County records contain 2 and 9 specimens. The species probably occurs in small numbers across much of the Arkansas River drainage of southeastern Kansas.

**9. Fantail Darter (*Etheostoma flabellare lineolatum*)**
- Previously reported by Cross (1954) from the South Fork of the Cottonwood River. Numerous new localities in the Marais des Cygnes and Marmaton systems (Bourbon and Linn counties) and the Spring and Neosho systems (Cherokee and Chase counties).
- Prefers shallow riffles and clear, cool water. Its populations are scattered and cannot persist in intermittent streams.

**10. Western Swamp Darter (*Etheostoma gracile*)**
- No authenticated previous record. Graham (1885) had listed *E. fusiforme* from the Marais des Cygnes. Hubbs and Cannon (1935) suggested these Kansas records belong to *Poecilichthys exilis*.
- New locality: Second Cow Creek, Crawford County (KU 2255, 2933).
- Thirteen specimens were seined from a vegetated overflow pool on April 18, 1952. The pool had dried on revisit (April 10, 1953), and four specimens were found in the main channel. Both series appeared to be in breeding condition.
- Repeated collecting in the Marais des Cygnes failed to confirm either *E. gracile* or *E. exile* there.

### Hampton (1954): Intergradation of the Bullhead Minnow

- The range of the Bullhead Minnow (*Pimephales vigilax perspicuus*) extends from the Trinity River system east and north, avoiding the upper Red River. Its presence in the Red River drainage was first documented by Bonn (1949) at Lake Texoma.
- Adult males of the two forms differ in breeding tubercles. The Parrot Minnow has a single row. The Bullhead Minnow has a second row of tubercles between the nostrils and one behind each nostril.
- Summer 1953 sampling at the population low point yielded **25 adult male specimens** from the Wichita River and its tributaries.
- Five specimens had an incomplete second row of tubercles, and twenty had a single row. The author interprets this as numerical or genetic dominance of the *vigilax* type.
- The incomplete second row is evidence of intergradation in nature. Hampton agrees with Hubbs (1951) and Cross (1953) that *perspicuus* is a subspecies of *vigilax*.
