# Biological Opinion for Windmill Grazing Allotment Management Plan, Coconino National Forest

#ichthyology #endangered-species #habitat-management #riparian-ecology #grazing-impacts

## Executive Summary

This biological opinion evaluates the effects of the proposed 10-year [[Windmill Grazing Allotment]] Management Plan on the [[Coconino National Forest]] in Arizona. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service assessed impacts to seven federally listed species: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Arizona Cliffrose]] (*Purshia subintegra*), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (*Strix occidentalis lucida*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*). The 248,792-acre allotment spans winter range near Cottonwood and Sedona and summer range above the [[Mogollon Rim]]. The Service concluded that Alternative F is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of any listed species or adversely modify designated critical habitat for the razorback sucker in the [[Verde River]]. The opinion includes specific conservation measures, monitoring requirements, and terms and conditions to minimize incidental take.

## Background & Description

The Windmill Allotment encompasses 248,792 acres across three Ranger Districts (Mormon Lake, Peaks, and Sedona) plus 16,926 acres of Arizona State Trust lands. The allotment supports diverse habitats including ponderosa pine (104,166 acres), pinyon-juniper (27,941 acres), mountain meadows (3,745 acres), desert grassland (87,526 acres), desert shrub (11,635 acres), and riparian areas (910 acres). Alternative F permits a maximum of 1,252 cattle on winter range (October 16 - May 31) and 1,257 cattle on summer range (June 1 - October 15).

The [[Razorback Sucker]] was listed as endangered on October 23, 1991, with critical habitat designated March 21, 1994. This large catostomid fish (exceeding two feet in length) possesses a distinctive sharp-edged dorsal ridge behind the head. Once common throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]], the species is now rare, occurring sporadically in approximately 750 miles of the upper basin. In the lower basin, a substantial population exists only in [[Lake Mohave]]. The Verde River population represents a reintroduction effort beginning in 1981 using hatchery stock from Lake Mohave. Monitoring from 1986-1990 indicated poor initial survival, but recent stockings of larger fish have shown improved post-stocking survival.

[[Arizona Cliffrose]] was listed as endangered May 29, 1984. This species has narrow habitat requirements, occurring in four widely separated areas in central Arizona on limestone soils derived from Tertiary lakebed deposits. The Verde Valley population is the largest and most robust, with individuals occurring in Section 1, Section 36, Gyberg, Duff Flat North and South, and Purshia pastures within the allotment. The species is threatened by livestock grazing, mining, road construction, and recreational activities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Verde River]] originates from mildly thermal, relatively constant flow springs near the mouth of [[Granite Creek]]. Approximately 30% of the watershed lies upstream from the beginning of perennial flow. The Big and Little Chino Valleys and [[Williamson Wash]] upstream support substantial urban and agricultural development, with Prescott and Chino Valley among Arizona's fastest growing areas (9.64% and 16.7% growth rates 1990-94).

Historically, [[Spikedace]] were widespread in the [[Gila River Basin]], including samples from Verde River, [[Wet Beaver Creek]], and [[West Clear Creek]] in the 1930s. Today, spikedace are primarily found in the upper Verde River above the Coconino boundary. Within the project area, a short stretch of Verde River mainstem near the confluence of Sycamore Creek continues to support spikedace. Below this confluence, the fish community becomes dominated by non-native species.

[[Loach Minnow]] were collected in 1938 from [[Beaver Creek]] and Verde River mainstem near Camp Verde, the only known collection from the upper Verde River system. Although thought extirpated, recent rediscoveries in other areas warrant a cautious approach. Potential habitat includes Verde River mainstem from Sycamore Creek confluence to Oak Creek confluence, and Oak Creek mainstem from its mouth upstream to [[Slide Rock]] (approximately 35 miles).

Non-native aquatic species adversely affect native fishes through predation and competition. Species currently present include mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*), yellow bullhead (*Ameiurus natalis*), channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*), flathead catfish (*Pylodictis olivaris*), red shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), smallmouth bass (*Micropterus dolomieui*), green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*), fathead minnow (*Pimephales promelas*), and crayfish (*Orconectes virilis*).

The allotment contains 36 [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] territories with 24,667 acres of Protected Activity Centers (PACs), 40,471 acres of wilderness, and 2,341 acres of Research Natural Areas. Approximately 912 acres of riparian habitat occur on the allotment, with 677 acres on winter range.

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker and Critical Habitat:**
- Direct effects limited as no grazing along Verde River will occur due to fencing and topographic features
- Affected reach includes 21 miles of Verde River mainstem from Sycamore Creek to Oak Creek confluence
- Duff Mesa pasture (adjacent to affected reach) scheduled for maximum 675 head for 20-35 days during winter period
- Indirect effects anticipated to be minimal; several pastures within Sycamore Creek watershed will not be grazed (Upper Sycamore, Lower Sycamore, Black Mountain, Casner)
- Watershed condition improvements expected through rest rotation, improved cattle distribution via fencing and water developments, and reduced grazing periods
- Range improvements necessary for improved cattle distribution will take 10 years to complete; benefits may take several years to realize
- Conclusion: Not likely to jeopardize continued existence or adversely modify designated critical habitat

**Arizona Cliffrose:**
- Majority of populations will be excluded from grazing through 2.25 miles of new fence in Gyberg pasture creating Purshia pasture (estimated completion March-April 1998)
- Section 1 and Section 36 pastures excluded from grazing since 1989
- Small scattered populations in Duff Flat South and Gyberg pastures vulnerable to livestock grazing
- Duff Flat South: grazed approximately 8 days annually; Gyberg: grazed 20-35 days on varying schedule
- Grazing schedule extends beyond Recovery Plan recommendations (October-January only); will occur February-April when plants actively growing and seedlings emerging
- Annual monitoring required with minimum five visits to detect utilization; if >20% use detected or use occurs more frequently than every other year, cattle will be removed or temporary fencing installed
- No loading areas, salt blocks, or watering facilities within 0.75 miles of known populations (except Mesa Blanca Tank within 0.5 miles)
- Conclusion: Not likely to jeopardize continued existence

**Southwestern Willow Flycatcher:**
- No known flycatchers on Windmill Allotment; nearest occupied sites: Tuzigoot (1.2 miles, unoccupied 1997), Tavasci Marsh (0.2 miles, two pairs 1996)
- Suitable habitat at Sheepshead Spring drainage and Oak Creek near Red Rock Crossing
- Formal surveys 1993-1997 with negative results following standard protocol
- Critical habitat present along 21.2 miles of Verde River adjacent to allotment; excluded from grazing by fences or topographic barriers
- Concurrence conditioned on: (1) if breeding flycatchers located within 5 miles, Forest Service must initiate cowbird trapping or remove livestock from pastures within 5-mile radius during April 1-July 31; (2) annual survey results and trapping program reported to Service and Arizona Game and Fish Department; (3) fencing excluding livestock from critical habitat inspected and repaired prior to cattle release and following flood events

**Mexican Spotted Owl:**
- 36 territories on allotment with PACs delineated for all
- Grazing effects variable depending on soil conditions, climate, rotation schedule, livestock characteristics, timing, and stand structure
- PACs expected to receive lightest utilization due to high canopy closure limiting understory production
- Concurrence conditioned on: (1) no livestock concentrations from gathering or mineral supplements within PACs during breeding season; (2) no grazing in Red Hill or Upper West Fork PACs (Lockwood Pasture) affected by prescribed fire until forage plants deemed mature; (3) no improvements with heavy equipment/chainsaws or road closures during breeding season in PACs unless monitoring determines non-nesting

**Spikedace, Loach Minnow, and Gila Trout:**
- Affected spikedace habitat: Verde River mainstem from Sycamore Creek confluence downstream approximately 0.5 mile
- No direct impacts anticipated; cattle excluded from Verde River and Sycamore Creek
- Indirect effects minimal; several Sycamore Creek watershed pastures not grazed
- Loach minnow potential habitat: Verde River from Sycamore Creek to Oak Creek confluence; Oak Creek from mouth to Slide Rock
- Gila trout affected habitat: Oak Creek mainstem from Sterling Springs to Grasshopper Point and West Fork Oak Creek
- Pastures located above Mogollon Rim; livestock cannot access Oak Creek Canyon or perennial West Fork portions
- Shorter grazing periods and pasture rest expected to improve watershed conditions short and long term
- Conclusion: May affect but not likely to adversely affect all three species

**Incidental Take Statement:**
- Take defined through surrogate measure: watershed conditions must continue to improve to satisfactory condition under proposed livestock management
- Improvement defined through improvements in trend and/or condition of rangelands on winter range
- Reasonable and prudent measure: efforts to improve watershed conditions through cattle distribution improvements (fencing, water developments) receive highest priority, preferably completed within first 5 years
- If watershed conditions do not improve, Forest Service must review management and develop new management resulting in improvement
- Annual reporting required on livestock management and monitoring

**Conservation Recommendations:**
- Permit livestock in Duff Flat South and Gyberg pastures October-January only (not February-April)
- Permit livestock use no more than once every two years in these pastures
- Continue Purshia monitoring; reinitiate consultation if utilization exceeds 20% of current year's growth
- Complete Verde Valley Botanical Area Management Plan
- Reduce forage allocation considering other wildlife use where poor and declining range conditions exist

**Cumulative Effects:**
- Expanding Cottonwood area development threatens Arizona cliffrose through urban encroachment, dispersed recreation, and public/private developments
- Outdoor recreational activities (off-road vehicles, "party sites") produce severe impacts to cliffrose
- Rapid urban/suburban growth in Prescott, Chino Valley, and Cottonwood areas places increasing demands on Verde River for recreation
- Groundwater pumping in upper Verde basin increasing with urban development; expected to adversely affect spring flow forming Verde River
- Livestock grazing on private lands, sandstone quarries, and mining activities contribute sediment to Verde River
- Nine native fish species already extirpated from Verde River system; three being reintroduced with limited success
