# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants (50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12), January 1, 1986 Edition

#endangered-species #wildlife-regulation #aves #primates

## Executive Summary

This document is a special reprint of the U.S. federal regulations at [[Code Of Federal Regulations]] Title 50, sections 17.11 and 17.12. It was produced by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Department of the Interior) and is current as of January 1, 1986. Sections 17.11 and 17.12 form the official list of wildlife and plant species that are Endangered or Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The text explains how the list is defined, how its columns are interpreted, and how status, special rules, and critical habitat are recorded. The bulk of the document is the list itself, which is largely unreadable in the scanned source. The readable material is mainly regulatory text, editorial notes, and a long list of [[Federal Register]] citations.

## Background & Description

**Scope of the list (§ 17.11(a)).** The list contains all wildlife species determined by the Services to be Endangered or Threatened. It also includes species treated as Endangered or Threatened because they closely resemble listed species, known as "similarity of appearance" species.

**Column definitions (§ 17.11(b)–(e)).**
- "Common Name," "Scientific Name," and "Vertebrate Population Where Endangered or Threatened" define which wildlife falls within the Act. Geographically distinct populations of the same subspecies or species are identified by their boundaries.
- The term "Entire" means all populations throughout the present range are listed.
- Common names may vary locally and cannot be relied on for identification. The most recently accepted scientific name is used, with synonyms in parentheses where confusion may arise. The International Code of Zoological Nomenclature is followed where practicable.
- Status symbols are "E" (Endangered), "T" (Threatened), and "E [or T] (S/A)" (similarity of appearance).
- The "Historic Range" column reflects the general known distribution in the scientific literature. Present distribution may be much smaller. This column does not limit the prohibitions of the Act, which apply to all individuals wherever found.

**Non-regulatory data (§ 17.11(d)).** Spelling, historic range, footnotes, synonyms, and current names may be amended without public notice during annual revisions. Changes to a species' definition or status require the procedures of Part 424.

**Footnotes and cross-references (§ 17.11(f)–(i)).** The "When Listed" column cites the Federal Register notice and effective date. The "Special Rules" and "Critical Habitat" columns cross-reference other parts of the regulations. Experimental populations are marked "XE" (essential) or "XN" (nonessential). "NA" means no special rules or critical habitat apply, but other applicable rules still do.

**Taxonomic scope (§ 17.11(g)).** Listing a higher taxon covers all lower units beneath it. The text gives the genus *Hylobates* (gibbons), listed as Endangered throughout its range in China, India, and Southeast Asia, as an example. It also gives the bald eagle, *Haliaeetus leucocephalus*, which was listed as Threatened in 1978 (43 FR 6230–6233) only in five states (WA, OR, MN, WI, MI) rather than across its entire population. Individuals in those states are therefore treated as listed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document contains no ecological data on habitat, water chemistry, or population trends. Its environmental relevance lies in the legal framework for protecting imperiled wildlife. It notes that the historic range may greatly exceed present distribution, and that the Act's prohibitions apply wherever listed individuals are found. This framing is important for conservation planning, since legal protection does not depend on a species' current geographic extent.

The document also cites [[Critical Habitat]] designations and [[Experimental Population]] rules as tools for managing listed species. The listed species themselves are not described in readable text in this reprint.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory purpose:** The reprint is a current compilation of 50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12. Its editorial note says that no entry has been significantly affected by changes since the October 1, 1985 compilation.
- **Authority to amend:** Names, historic ranges, synonyms, and footnotes may be revised without public notice. Status and species definitions may not be changed without following Part 424 procedures.
- **Status classification:** Species are classified as Endangered (E), Threatened (T), or treated as similar in appearance (S/A). Experimental populations may be essential (XE) or nonessential (XN).
- **Listing scope:** A listing at the genus level covers all lower taxa. Listings can also be limited to specific geographic populations, as with the 1978 Threatened listing of the [[Bald Eagle]] in five states.
- **Use of names:** Common names are not reliable for identification. Scientific names, with synonyms where needed, are authoritative.
- **Federal Register record:** About 100 Federal Register citations are listed, spanning roughly 1977 to December 1985 (the last is 50 FR 51870, December 20, 1985). Each entry records a listing, reclassification, or amendment. The source note cites 48 FR 34182 (July 27, 1983) as the original basis for the part, as amended at 49 FR 33893 (August 27, 1984).
- **Contact:** The list was prepared by the Office of Endangered Species, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Washington, D.C. Readers are asked to report errors to the Service, and copies are available from its Publication Unit.
