# Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program: Management Group Meeting Summary (April 1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum transmitting the summary of the April 6-7, 1989, [[Management Group]] meeting of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The attachments include a [[Technical Group]] meeting summary, a [[Little Snake River]] water rights proposal, a [[Section 7 Consultation]] status table, Service position papers on water depletions, a Solicitor's opinion, and a proposal on [[Colorado River Storage Project]] (CRSP) legislation. The meeting covered program funding, public hearings on stocking, the [[Razorback Sucker]] listing petition, the [[Instream Flow]] reports, FY-90 work planning, [[Water Rights Acquisition]], and the [[Section 7 Consultation]] framework for water depletions. The three focal species are the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Humpback Chub]].

## Background & Description

The program targets three endangered fishes: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], the [[Razorback Sucker]], and the [[Humpback Chub]]. The [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] wrote the [[Recovery Plans]], which the Service regards as the biological framework for recovery across each species' full range, not only the upper basin. The Recovery Team was commissioned by the Service to draft plans and advise on biological questions during implementation.

The meeting record lists eight reference items, including a February 1989 Recovery Team letter, a letter from Tom Pitts (Water Developers) on the squawfish plan, a Nature Conservancy letter on program participation, and a petition from the [[Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund]] to list the razorback sucker as endangered throughout its range. Attendee lists for the meeting (about 20 people from FWS, Bureau of Reclamation, Western Area Power Administration, National Park Service, state agencies, the Solicitor's office and water interests) and the Technical Group meeting (about 20 people) are included as attachments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Key habitat issues in the record:

- **Yampa River:** Described as relatively unaltered. Flow needs for fishes were discussed, with the Service stating these were not quantitative flow recommendations. Interim flow recommendations for the Yampa were accepted by the Implementation Committee.
- **15-Mile Reach:** Described as an altered system where recovery is sought. Flow recommendations include a 600 cfs dry-year value, chosen "to help ensure our ability to implement the flow recommendations in all years," and a habitat target of 95 percent of maximum Weighted Usable Area (WUA), chosen to alleviate limiting factors and provide a flow "window." Concerns were raised that few young-of-year fish and little spawning occur there.
- **Grand Canyon:** Humpback chub studies were proposed for Recovery Program funding but withdrawn. Reclamation agreed to fund them from other sources for the foreseeable future.
- **Sediment:** Water development may alter sediment transport and channel form, affecting endangered fish habitat. Effects may not appear for decades. The Recovery Program was judged to be insufficient on this issue.
- **Non-native stocking:** The Group judged proposed stocking of channel catfish in [[Kenney Reservoir]] and rainbow smelt in [[Lake Powell]] to be contrary to the program's intent. Stocking of rainbow smelt would require a National Park Service permit and therefore a Section 7 consultation.

## Key Findings

**Program governance and funding**

- The Fish and Wildlife Foundation transfer of existing and new Section 7 funds was tentatively approved. Transferring congressionally appropriated water rights acquisition funds was judged legally questionable and was not pursued.
- The Solicitor's opinion (dated May 15, 1989) was requested after concerns arose in consultations on the Muddy Creek and Sandstone projects. It was scheduled for discussion at the June 14, 1989, meeting.
- Tom Pitts proposed using all existing and new Section 7 funds exclusively for water rights acquisition to strengthen the case for congressional appropriations. Some members worried this would reduce flexibility. Pitts agreed to write a proposal.
- The Service reported that a $1M congressional appropriation for water rights and a $2.3M FY-89 work plan funding 39 high-priority projects were in place.
- Up to $120,000 in Windy Gap funds was approved on February 21, 1989, for a Bureau of Reclamation survey of 15-Mile Reach water supply options and for the Little Snake water rights evaluation.

**Work plans and deadlines**

- FY-90 research proposals were due May 1, 1989.
- The Yampa and 15-Mile Reach instream flow reports were to be finalized May 1, 1989. The Service would then ask the Implementation Committee to request alternative flow-delivery recommendations from the Colorado Water Conservation Board and others.
- The Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion was due May 1, 1990. Some technical reports were behind schedule, but Harris said any delay would not significantly affect FY-90 funding.
- A razorback management plan draft was expected by the end of the fiscal year. Reclamation freed funds for it.
- The Group approved an $8,000 Reclamation-funded Utah endangered fish survey of the [[San Juan River]], to complement New Mexico work, subject to member objections.
- The Group did not object to Cataract Canyon field surveys funded from non-Recovery Program money.

**Recovery plan dispute**

- Pitts argued the Implementation Committee should revise squawfish recovery goals, which he considered inconsistent with the Recovery Program. The Service stated that setting recovery goals is its statutory responsibility under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The ESA and Service guidelines require goals to be objective, quantifiable, and measurable. No resolution was reached. The Service agreed to answer Pitts in writing.

**Water rights acquisition**

- The Colorado Water Conservation Board aimed to file for a water right to protect [[Ruedi Reservoir]] releases through the 15-Mile Reach by December 1989.
- The River District proposed a two-year feasibility study of alternatives to the Juniper and Cross Mountain projects, to be cost-shared by the CWCB, the River District, and the Recovery Program.
- The Little Snake water rights evaluation (Powder Wash and Pot Hook conditional rights of the River District, and Three Forks conditional rights of Pittsburg and Midway Coal Mining Company) has three tasks: transferable yield under existing conditions (45 days), upstream development impacts (120 days), and a preliminary fair market value appraisal (90 days).
- Gene Jencsok was tasked with an outline of information needed to evaluate water rights offered for sale to the program.

**Section 7 consultation framework**

- The Service has issued over 100 biological opinions since 1978 on water development effects on the endangered fishes.
- The Recovery Program's contribution rate is $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion, adjusted annually for inflation, with 10 percent due at federal action initiation and the balance guaranteed by bond or contract.
- Depletions above 3,000 acre-feet per year are treated as "large." The Service decides case by case for smaller depletions. Historic depletions requiring new federal approval are treated as new depletions.
- Funds from these payments split 50-50 between recovery activities and instream flow water rights acquisition, unless the Implementation Committee directs otherwise.
- Sufficient-progress determinations are made case by case before the last federal permit issues, based on progress across instream flow protection areas and the magnitude of project impacts.
- The Section 7 status table lists roughly 20 consultations (including Colowyo Coal Company, Trapper Mine, Flaming Gorge Operation, and Sandstone Reservoir). The Muddy Creek and Sandstone consultations raised the issues that prompted the Solicitor's request.

**CRSP legislation**

- Principles agreed by Upper Division States representatives and the Colorado River Energy Distributors Association would replace the existing CRSP power revenue requirement with about $35 million per year for 40 years (about $2.2 billion total) for new water projects, deposited in state escrow accounts.
- A separate system-wide environmental mitigation and enhancement fund would receive about $6 million per year (about $365 million over 40 years), contributed equally by the Upper Division States and power users.
- Cost-share conditions include 30 percent for Colorado, New Mexico, and Wyoming projects, and 20 percent (Bonneville Unit irrigation) and 30 percent (other projects) for Utah. These principles are not final agreements.

**Next steps**

- The next Management Group meeting was set for June 14, 1989. A July 13-14, 1989, meeting was tentatively set to prepare the FY-90 work plan and budget.
- A river trip for the Implementation Committee and Management Group was set for August 10-12, 1989, with a $100 deposit requested.
