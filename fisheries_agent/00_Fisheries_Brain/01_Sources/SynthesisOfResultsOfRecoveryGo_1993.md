## Executive Summary

This FY93 report (Colorado River Recovery Program, Project #31) describes a synthesis effort intended to integrate results from multiple ongoing recovery activities into refined recovery goals for four endangered fish species of the Upper Colorado River Basin: the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically "Colorado squawfish"), [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and (implicitly) [[Bonytail]]. The project is largely administrative/synthetic in nature rather than primary field research, and the FY93 findings indicate that most contributing activities (Population Viability Analysis [PVA] modeling, recovery plan development, critical habitat designation, genetics studies, Interim Management Objectives [IMOs]) had not yet produced sufficient data to allow meaningful synthesis. The single substantive biological finding is from Gilpin's (1993) PVA of the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] population in the [[Yampa River|Yampa]]/[[Green River]] system, which concluded the population is currently "viable" under static conditions, though the model could not evaluate impacts of flow modification or tradeoffs among recovery strategies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/System**: The primary population data referenced pertains to [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] residing in the **Yampa/Green river system**, part of the Upper Colorado River Basin — a designated critical habitat area for endangered Colorado River fishes.
- **Population status**: The Yampa/Green [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] population was assessed via PVA as currently self-sustaining ("viable") under a stasis assumption (i.e., assuming no change to present flow, habitat, or management conditions).
- **Threat context**: The analysis explicitly flags **major flow modification** (a proxy for water development/diversion activities) as an unresolved threat vector — the PVA model lacked the data and mechanistic understanding to predict population vulnerability under altered hydrology.
- **Multi-species management framework**: The report situates the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] within a shared recovery framework (RIPRAP — Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan) that replaced the earlier "Long Range Plan," reflecting the interconnected ecological management of these co-occurring endangered fishes in the Colorado River system.

## Key Findings

- **PVA Outcome (Gilpin 1993)**: The [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] population in the Yampa/Green system was determined to be *viable* and expected to remain so under present (static) conditions — but the model could not assess vulnerability to flow modifications or evaluate tradeoffs among recovery management scenarios.
- **Model Limitations**: The existing PVA model was found unsuitable for testing recovery plan goals, evaluating Interim Management Objectives (IMOs), or assessing tradeoffs among recovery goal scenarios, spatial configurations, or genetics management options — a core objective of the project was therefore not met.
- **Humpback Chub PVA**: Development of a PVA model for the [[Humpback Chub]] was stalled, pending successful completion of the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] PVA model as a template.
- **Razorback Sucker**: No recovery plan had yet been developed for the [[Razorback Sucker]] as of FY93.
- **Regulatory status**: Final critical habitat designation for the endangered Colorado River fishes was pending, expected in March 1994.
- **Planning framework shift**: The "Long Range Plan" was superseded by the RIPRAP, which — notably — contains no explicit recovery goals, replacing the former Species' Action Plans.
- **Interim Management Objectives (IMOs)**: Draft IMOs existed for Colorado but not yet for Utah; furthermore, the current PVA model was incapable of using IMOs as evaluative inputs.
- **Genetics data**: Results from genetics studies relevant to recovery planning were not expected until 1994 at the earliest.
- **Budget**: No funds were provided or expended for this project in FY93 ($0/$0).
- **Recommendation**: Continued development of a robust PVA model (for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and/or [[Humpback Chub]]) was recommended, with emphasis on building a tool actually capable of evaluating recovery goal refinements, management action tradeoffs, and land/water development scenario impacts — addressing the core tool gap identified in this report.
