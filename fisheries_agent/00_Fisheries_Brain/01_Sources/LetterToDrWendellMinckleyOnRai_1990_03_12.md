## Executive Summary
This document is an official letter from the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (DWR) to Dr. Wendell Minckley, an ichthyologist at Arizona State University, dated March 12, 1990. The letter accompanies a formal assessment proposing the introduction of [[Rainbow Smelt]] (a forage fish species) into [[Lake Powell]] to address a "serious sport fisheries resource problem." The DWR acknowledges the controversial nature of the proposal and the inherent risks of introducing a non-native species, but argues that the risk to native and endangered fishes is low based on precedent from other water bodies. The letter is a request for formal scientific peer review of the risk assessment, seeking to identify inaccuracies or overlooked data before the agency proceeds with the introduction.

## Background & Description
*No morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body plan) for the Rainbow Smelt or any co-occurring species is provided in this source text.* The document is administrative/managerial in nature, focusing on policy and risk assessment rather than taxonomic description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Proposed Role:** The [[Rainbow Smelt]] is proposed as a **forage fish** species intended to bolster the prey base for the sport fishery within the reservoir ecosystem.
- **Target Habitat:** [[Lake Powell]], a large impoundment on the [[Colorado River]], and potentially connected downstream reservoirs.
- **Community Interactions:** The primary ecological concern raised is the potential interaction between the introduced smelt and native/endangered fish species within the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The DWR claims that in similar reservoir systems, the introduction of rainbow smelt has occurred "with no significant impacts on fish populations."
- **Trophic Context:** The introduction is framed as a solution to a "sport fisheries resource problem," implying a lack of adequate forage base for existing predatory game fish in Lake Powell prior to this proposal.

## Key Findings
- **Management Objective:** The Utah DWR aims to introduce Rainbow Smelt to resolve deficiencies in the sport fishery of Lake Powell.
- **Risk Assessment Claim:** The agency asserts the introduction poses a "very low risk to native fishes," citing precedent from other systems with similar fish populations.
- **Stakeholder Concern:** Multiple natural resource agencies responsible for the [[Colorado River Basin]] have expressed significant concern regarding potential negative impacts on **endangered fish species** in the region.
- **Alternative Analysis:** The DWR states that multiple alternative options to the smelt introduction were reviewed and subsequently discarded due to:
  - Potential adverse effects on endangered species.
  - Low probability of success.
  - Prohibitive costs.
- **The "No Action" Risk:** The letter explicitly frames inaction as carrying its own "considerable risks," suggesting that failing to introduce a forage species carries negative consequences for the fishery that must be weighed against the risks of introduction.
- **Peer Review Request:** The letter formally solicits a technical review from Dr. Minckley and his colleagues, requesting specific feedback on data accuracy and completeness by **April 9, 1990**.
- **Decision Stance:** Despite requesting review, the tone indicates a strong administrative intent to proceed with the [[Rainbow Smelt]] introduction unless substantial contrary evidence is provided.
