## Executive Summary

This document is **not a species-specific scientific paper**; rather, it is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) policy overview describing the **[[Section 10 Incidental Take Permit]]** program under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]** (ESA) and the associated **[[Habitat Conservation Plan]]** (HCP) process. It outlines the administrative, legal, and procedural framework that governs how private applicants may obtain authorization for the "incidental take" of federally listed species — including listed fish species — during otherwise lawful economic activity. The HCP process can apply to single or multiple species and may be scaled to ecosystem-level or regional plans, which is directly relevant to aquatic and fish conservation planning (e.g., regional watershed HCPs covering listed fish such as chubs, trout, or salmonids). No morphological, physiological, or ecological species data are present in the source text; this note therefore focuses on the regulatory and procedural architecture rather than biological findings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no specific species or habitat data are given, the document establishes the **regulatory context** in which ecological and habitat data for listed aquatic species would be used:

- **Scope**: HCPs may be developed for a **single species, multiple species, or at the ecosystem level on a regional basis** — a framework commonly applied to freshwater fish conservation (e.g., multi-species river basin plans covering endangered fish alongside terrestrial or plant species).
- **Habitat Delineation**: A mandatory element of HCP development is the **delineation of HCP boundaries**, which in aquatic contexts would correspond to watershed, riparian, or stream reach boundaries relevant to fish habitat.
- **Impact Assessment**: HCPs must identify **impacts likely to result from proposed taking** of listed species — for fish, this would include impacts from water diversion, habitat modification, pollution, or land use change within a defined geographic/hydrological area.
- **Mitigation and Monitoring**: Applicants must propose **measures to monitor, minimize, and mitigate impacts**, which for fish species would typically involve habitat restoration, flow management, or population monitoring protocols (not detailed in this source).
- **Unlisted Species Consideration**: The HCP framework explicitly allows for **treatment of unlisted species**, relevant to co-occurring aquatic taxa not yet under ESA protection but potentially affected by the same activities.

## Key Findings

- The **[[Section 10 Incidental Take Permit]]** is issued under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and requires a complete application package: permit application, [[Habitat Conservation Plan]], NEPA document (EA or EIS), and an [[Implementing Agreement]].
- **Six statutory issuance criteria** must be met, including that take is incidental, impacts are minimized/mitigated "to the maximum extent practicable," adequate funding is secured, and the take **will not appreciably reduce the likelihood of survival and recovery of the species in the wild** — a criterion directly applicable to imperiled fish populations.
- **Four mandatory HCP elements**: (1) anticipated impacts of proposed take, (2) mitigation/monitoring measures and funding, (3) alternatives considered that would avoid take, and (4) any additional measures required by USFWS.
- **NEPA compliance** is the responsibility of the USFWS (not the applicant) and is satisfied via Categorical Exclusion, Environmental Assessment (EA/FONSI), or Environmental Impact Statement (EIS/ROD), depending on the significance of environmental effects — relevant when HCP actions affect broader aquatic ecosystems beyond the listed species itself.
- The process involves a **Steering Committee**, facilitator, EIS consultant, and defined geographic area — an approach used in real-world multi-species/regional HCPs that include listed fish (e.g., desert fish HCPs in the Southwest, consistent with the issuing office's Albuquerque, NM location, suggesting relevance to [[Gila Chub]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], and other Southwestern freshwater fish conservation efforts).
- **Internal Section 7 Consultation** (biological opinion) occurs between the Field Office (which negotiates the HCP) and the Regional Office (which issues the permit), ensuring take determinations for listed species — including fish — are biologically reviewed before permit issuance.
- The document emphasizes **streamlining and consensus-building**, reflecting the program's design to balance economic development with **species and ecosystem conservation commitments**, including for aquatic and riverine ecosystems.
