## Key Findings

- The document announces the appointment of **Dr. H. Ronald Pulliam** (age 48, native of Miami Beach, Florida) as Director of the **National Biological Survey (NBS)**, a new agency under the [[U.S. Department of the Interior]], effective May 2, 1994.
- Secretary of the Interior **Bruce Babbitt** made the announcement, citing Pulliam's "exceptional service, high standards for science and professional experience."
- Pulliam's academic background: B.S. from the [[University of Georgia]] (1958), Ph.D. from [[Duke University]] (1970), and postdoctoral study at the [[University of Chicago]] (1970–71).
- Career history includes: research biologist at the H.S. Colton Research Center, [[Museum of Northern Arizona]] (1978–80); Associate Professor at [[State University of New York, Albany]] (1980–87); and Director/Professor of the [[Institute of Ecology]] at the University of Georgia (from 1987), where he expanded the institute into a full graduate/undergraduate school with 200+ employees and a $4M+ annual budget.
- Professional service includes President (1991–92) and Vice President (1986–87) of the [[Ecological Society of America]], and President of the [[Southeastern University Research Association]] (1990–91).
- Pulliam's appointment was recommended by the [[National Academy of Sciences]] following a nationwide search commissioned by Secretary Babbitt — the same body that recommended Dr. Gordon Eaton for Director of the [[U.S. Geological Survey]].
- The stated mission of the NBS, per Babbitt, is to make scientific information about the health of the nation's natural resources "more accessible to the public" through gathering, analyzing, and disseminating biological data.
