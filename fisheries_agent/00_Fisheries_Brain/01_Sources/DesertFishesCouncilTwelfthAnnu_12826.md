# Twelfth Annual Desert Fishes Council Symposium Program

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #desert-fishes #symposium

## Executive Summary

This document is the program for the Twelfth Annual Symposium of the Desert Fishes Council, held November 5-7, 1980, at Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León in Monterrey, Mexico. The symposium brought together researchers, agency representatives, and conservation professionals to discuss the status, research, and management of desert fishes across the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. The program included presentations on endangered species recovery, ecological research, taxonomic studies, and field trips to [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]].

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Council is an organization dedicated to the preservation of desert fishes in North America. The 1980 symposium was hosted by Dr. Salvador Contreras-Balderas at Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León, representing a significant binational collaboration. The program was structured into five sessions covering: (1) reports from area coordinators and recovery team leaders across 12 geographic regions; (2-3) research presentations on various desert fish species and their habitats; (4) reports from governmental and agency representatives; and (5) business meeting.

Geographic areas covered included [[Bonneville Basin]], [[Death Valley]], [[Oregon Lakes]], [[Lahontan Basin]], [[Sonoran Desert]], [[Chihuahuan Desert]], [[Upper Colorado River]], [[Lower Colorado River]], and [[Virgin River]]. The symposium featured bilingual presentations in English and Spanish, with strict time limits: 20 minutes for research reports, 15 minutes for agency and area coordinator reports.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The symposium addressed desert aquatic ecosystems characterized by extreme environmental conditions including temperature fluctuations, variable salinity, low flow conditions, and flash flooding. Multiple presentations focused on spring-marsh complexes, isolated desert basins, and river systems under anthropogenic pressure. Key environmental concerns included:

- Effects of low flow on native fish populations ([[Woundfin]] in [[Virgin River]])
- Impacts of flash flooding on [[Gila Topminnow]] populations in Sonoran desert streams
- Temperature and salinity effects on [[Death Valley]] pupfishes (genus [[Cyprinodon]])
- Habitat changes in [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico
- Potential impacts of MX missile deployment on Nevada and Utah aquatic habitats

The symposium emphasized conservation of endemic species in fragmented desert aquatic habitats, with particular attention to spring systems, isolated pools (ciénegas), and intermittent streams.

## Key Findings

**Species-Specific Research Presentations:**

- Temperature and salinity taxonomy of [[Death Valley]] pupfishes (genus [[Cyprinodon]]) - Gene R. Wilde
- Effects of population density on breeding system of [[Cyprinodon Pecosensis]] - Astrid Kodric-Brown
- Unusual secondary sex characteristics in [[Cyprinodon]] species - Gary Garrett
- Life history study of [[Virgin River Spinedace]] ([[Lepidomeda Mollispinis]]) - Bill Rinne
- Distribution and food habits of [[Least Chub]] ([[Iotichthys Phlegethontis]]) in western Utah spring-marsh complex - Larry Crist
- Current status of endangered topminnows (Poeciliidae: [[Poeciliopsis]]) in Arizona - Gary K. Meffe
- Induced spawn of [[Humpback Chub]] ([[Gila Cypha]]) - Arcadio Valdes-Gonzalez
- Status and taxonomy of [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] - Richard Valdez
- Reinterpretation of [[Bluntnose Shiner]] ([[Notropis Simus]]) in Rio Bravo basin - Barry Chernoff et al.
- Metabolic cost of aggressive behavior in [[Cyprinodon]] - Robert C. Feldmeth

**Conservation and Management:**

- Research on two native southwestern trouts and management implications - John N. Rinne
- The resurrection of [[San Bernardino]] - James E. Johnson
- Update on [[Borax Lake]] and review of [[Whitehorse Trout]] - Neil B. Armantrout
- Discovery and management of [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] in Donner Creek, Nevada-Utah - Terry Hickman
- Distribution of introduced fishes in Pacific drainages of northwestern Mexico - Dean Hendrickson

**Ecosystem Studies:**

- Changes in [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico - W. L. Minckley
- Biological status of cichlid fishes of [[Cuatro Ciénegas]] - I. L. Kornfield
- Conservation of freshwater molluscan species in United States and Mexico - Jerry Landye
- Impacts of flash flooding in Sonoran desert stream including extirpation of [[Gila Topminnow]] population - Jim Collins

**Symposium Structure:**

The program included 12 geographic area coordinator reports covering major desert basins from Oregon to Texas and throughout northern Mexico. Agency representatives from Bureau of Land Management, National Park Service, Water and Power Resources Service, Forest Service, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (multiple regional offices), state agencies (Colorado, California), and The Nature Conservancy provided status updates. A field trip to [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] was scheduled for November 7, 1980, organized by Dr. Contreras-Balderas.
