# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Recovery Plan Format (Appendix I, with Ozark Cavefish and Fassett's Locoweed Examples)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-recovery

## Executive Summary

This document is Appendix I of a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) guide to preparing [[Recovery Plan]]s for species listed under the Endangered Species Act. It sets out the required title page, disclaimer, executive summary, plan outline, introduction, recovery criteria, narrative outline, [[Implementation Schedule]], and appendix conventions. Two worked [[Executive Summary]] examples are included: the [[Ozark Cavefish]] (a threatened fish) and [[Fassett's Locoweed]] (a threatened plant). The guide is procedural, so its "findings" are the required structure and the worked examples.

## Background & Description

The guide is published by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and governs plans for listed species. Key format rules include:

- **Title/approval page:** lists the recovery team, leader, affiliations, and regional approval. Revisions carry an original approval date and revision notes.
- **Disclaimer:** plans are not binding commitments. Objectives depend on budgets and priorities, and plans may be modified as new findings emerge.
- **Executive summary (max one single-spaced page):** covers current status, habitat requirements and limiting factors, recovery objective, recovery criteria, actions needed, a cost table, and the date of recovery.
- **Plan outline:** Introduction (description, distribution, habitat, life history, reasons for listing, conservation measures, strategy of recovery), Recovery (objective and criteria, narrative outline, literature cited), Implementation Schedule, and Appendix.
- **Implementation Schedule:** the most important section, used to secure and obligate funds. Tasks are ranked by priority, and costs are given in thousands of dollars by fiscal year, with Service costs listed separately from other agencies' costs.

Task priorities are defined as follows:

- **Priority 1:** must be taken to prevent extinction or irreversible decline.
- **Priority 2:** must be taken to prevent significant decline short of extinction.
- **Priority 3:** all other actions needed to meet the recovery objective.

Recovery objectives can be delisting, downlisting (reclassification from endangered to threatened), or indefinite protection of existing populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The worked examples describe two threatened species with very different habitats:

**Ozark Cavefish**
- Listed as threatened. Fourteen populations are known: Missouri (7), Oklahoma (3), and Arkansas (4).
- Historically occupied 24 caves in the region.
- Inhabits cave streams of the Springfield Plateau.
- Principal threats are habitat loss and declining water quality from urbanization.
- Declining bat populations, which supply energy to the caves, and low natural reproductive rates also threaten survival.

**Fassett's Locoweed**
- Listed as threatened. A 1988 survey relocated the plant at four of eight historical stations.
- Three of the sites were on the market or recently sold to private owners. The fourth, with only eight plants, is a highly developed lake shoreline.
- None of the four known sites is in protected ownership. Shoreline development is the largest threat.
- Grows on sandy shorelines of land-locked seepage lakes, where water levels fluctuate with rainfall. The species may rely on a persistent seed bank through high-water periods and germinate when shorelines are exposed.

## Key Findings

**Format requirements**
- Recovery criteria must be quantitative (numbers of populations, habitat amounts, years maintained). Vague terms such as "protected" or "stable" must be operationally defined.
- Delisting generally requires more viable populations than downlisting. Populations should be spatially arranged so one catastrophic event is unlikely to eliminate more than one.
- If too little habitat remains to support viable populations, the plan must state that delisting is not a practical objective.
- Recovery criteria are preliminary and may be revised with new information.
- Only actions required for recovery belong in the narrative outline. Cost estimates must state their confidence level and be given in current dollars without inflation adjustment.
- Land acquisition needed for recovery must be identified in the plan before a federal request goes to Congress.

**Ozark Cavefish example**
- Recovery objective: delisting, with the process initiated in 2000.
- Recovery criteria: viable populations at twelve locations.
- Actions: (1) monitor existing and experimental populations; (2) establish five new populations and augment selected existing ones; (3) determine recharge zones for caves; (4) develop and implement protection of recharge zones.
- Cost table (thousands of dollars): the annual totals sum to a grand total of 283.0 across 1990–2000. The Need 2 column total is printed as 220.0, but its annual entries sum to 200.0. This discrepancy appears in the source and should be checked against the original.

**Fassett's Locoweed example**
- Recovery objective: delisting, with the process initiated in 2000.
- Recovery criteria: protect and maintain ten separate, self-sustaining populations, including all extant colonies and introduced populations. Introduced colonies count as established only after fifteen years.
- Actions: (1) survey for additional habitat and acquire one known population; (2) monitor existing populations; (3) research the species' biology; (4) establish new populations; (5) manage all key sites.
- Cost table: grand total of 219.0 (thousands of dollars), with 130.0 for Need 1 (mostly 1995 acquisition) and 36.5 for Need 5. Internal row and column sums check out.

**Key takeaway:** both examples illustrate the central requirement of a recovery plan: specific, measurable criteria tied to a costed, prioritized, year-by-year action schedule.
