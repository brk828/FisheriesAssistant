# A Field-Based Upper Division and Graduate Summer Program in Biology at the Sierra Ancha Station

#ecology #field-biology #higher-education

## Executive Summary

This memorandum, dated 24 August 1994 from W. L. Minckley to Zoology Faculty and Staff, outlines a proposed field-based upper-division and graduate summer session in ecology at the [[Sierra Ancha Station]] for Summer Session 1995. The program is a joint effort of the Department of Zoology and the [[Center For Environmental Studies]] at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), approved by the administration in summer 1994. It offers a 12-credit-hour block per session, built from two field classes and two lecture/laboratory classes, serving up to 36 students. The memo presents facilities, course structure, staffing, a compensation model, justification, and two budget scenarios. The full-enrollment scenario (36 students) roughly breaks even, with a projected deficit of $405. The minimum-enrollment scenario (24 students) shows a projected deficit of $11,190 that would require subsidy.

## Background & Description

**Setting and history.** The Station is about 110 miles from the ASU main campus at 5600 ft elevation, within [[Tonto National Forest]] and adjacent to the Sierra Ancha and Salt River wilderness areas. It lies roughly 20 miles from the 3-Bar Wildlife Management Area. The [[US Forest Service]] built the Station in the 1930s as a watershed research base. ASU currently holds it under lease from the Forest Service. Within 10 miles, the landscape ranges from [[Upper Sonoran Desert]] below 3000 ft through chaparral, grassland, and oak-juniper woodland to dense conifer forest above 7000 ft. [[Riparian Corridors]] connect the mountain tops with the surrounding desert.

**Facilities.** Based on the accompanying Table 1, the Station accommodates 40 to 50 occupants in four cabins (6 to 16 persons each), a dormitory (12 to 16), a mess hall, and a laboratory/classroom (maximum about 18). Amenities include cots, propane stoves, water heaters, refrigerators, and lighting. Users supply food, linens, and cooking gear. The Station has an approved water supply and modern waste disposal, and it needs no major renovation. Summer cooling is unnecessary because of shade and moderate elevation.

**Rates (effective 07/01/93).** ASU affiliates and Forest Service personnel pay $9.50 per night per person. Non-ASU users pay $12.00. Group rates are $8.00 for 10 to 15 guests and $7.00 for 16 or more. The generator costs $25.00 per day. Non-ASU groups must pay a 25% deposit before occupancy.

**Course structure.** The memo lists existing ASU courses usable at the Station:

- *Field classes (3 credits each):* [[Techniques In Natural Resource Management]] (Zol 410), [[Field Zoology]] (Zol 420), [[Research Techniques In Animal Behavior]] (Zol 431), [[Introductory Ecology Laboratory]] (Bio 321), and [[Quantitative Ecology]] (Bio 526).
- *Lecture/laboratory classes with field trips:* about a dozen offerings, including [[Entomology]], [[Population And Community Ecology]], [[Animal Ecology]], [[Aquatic Insects]], [[Ornithology]], [[Mammalogy]], [[Ichthyology Course]], [[Herpetology]], [[Natural History Of Arizona]], [[Biology And Conservation Of Wildlife Resources]], [[Biology And Management Of Aquatic Resources]], and [[Biology Of The Desert]].

Other departments are being contacted to gauge campus-wide interest.

**Program structure.** Each session offers 12 credit hours: two field classes and two lecture/laboratory classes. Field classes hold 12 students each. Lecture/laboratory classes hold up to 18. Students take 6 credit hours through one of two combinations: a field class plus a lecture/laboratory class, or both lecture/laboratory classes. At full enrollment of 36 students, 12 students each take A and B, with the remaining 12 taking C and D. At 24 students, A and B each hold 12, and C and D share the remainder.

**Staffing and compensation.** A part-time Director and part-time Administrative Assistant, both drawn from released-time faculty and staff, will plan, advertise, and implement the program mainly during the academic year. Faculty receive a $500 per lecture-period "honorarium" rather than conventional summer-session pay, which the memo says avoids early financial obstacles and increases staffing flexibility. Classes meet 4 hours per day, 4 days per week, for 80 contact hours per session. Faculty meet once a week to lecture and guide field work, contributing 8 hours per week (4 in direct contact). Three to four [[Teaching Assistants]] per session receive half-time compensation for 60 hours of direct student contact plus station duties. Travel, housing, and subsistence are provided for all staff, and a full-time cook and cook's assistant serve the group.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo presents the Station as a diverse outdoor laboratory. Its elevation gradient, from desert to conifer forest, and its riparian corridors provide varied habitats for field ecology, behavior, and natural history teaching. The memo also argues that Arizona's landscapes, mostly under federal and state management, are the setting where management decisions about natural ecosystems play out. It contends that interactions among biotic communities and human interests must be understood and addressed by scientists and policy makers trained in ecology.

**Current use.** The Station is already used as a base for faculty and graduate research, mainly by the Botany and Zoology departments and the Center For Environmental Studies. Other users include ASU classes and outside institutions, on a first-come, first-served basis. Table 3 gives person-night usage for fiscal years 1988/89 to 1992/93:

- ASU academic units: 1,174 total
- Other educational institutions: 1,645 total (including Grand Canyon College, Glendale, Scottsdale, and Mesa junior colleges, and Phoenix-area high schools)
- Federal/state agencies: 375 total
- Salt River Project: 61 total
- Non-profit groups: 2,716 total

Summing these row totals gives about 5,971 person-nights (calculated from the table). The grand-total row is illegible in the source scan, so it is not reported here.

**Institutional context.** The memo cites the ASU Strategic Planning and Budgeting Committee's May 1994 priorities: improving undergraduate and graduate education, strengthening research, fostering multidisciplinary faculty strengths, and shaping enrollment profiles. It also notes an existing multidisciplinary [[Ecology Concentration]] and progress toward an [[Arizona Museum Of Natural History And Culture]]. The memo compares the planned Station program to the [[Southwestern Research Station]] run by the American Museum of Natural History at Portal, Arizona.

## Key Findings

- **Program design:** A 12-credit-hour, field-based session with two field classes and two lecture/laboratory classes, serving up to 36 students, is proposed for Summer Session 1995.
- **Enrollment demand indicators (Table 2):**
  - Ecology/Behavior applicants rose from 60 (1990/91) to 48 (1991/92), 54 (1992/93), and 108 (1993/94).
  - Conservation Biology majors rose from 56 to 74, 85, and 96 over the same period.
  - Total Zoology graduate applications are garbled in the scan, so no figures are reported.
  - 48 students were in the Ecology Concentration in 1993/94, drawn from 44 faculty and staff across 11 academic units.
- **Budget, full enrollment (36 students):** Projected receipts are $43,020 (tuition $20,520; room and board $18,900; laboratory fees $3,600). Projected expenses are $43,425, leaving a deficit of $405. The memo states revenues are adequate at this enrollment.
- **Budget, minimum enrollment (24 students):** Projected receipts are $28,680 (tuition $13,680; room and board $12,600; laboratory fees $2,400). Projected expenses are $39,870, leaving a deficit of $11,190. This scenario requires subsidy for administration and operations.
- **Cost assumptions:** Tuition is $95 per credit hour. Faculty honoraria are $500 per day. Teaching assistants are paid $1,200 per session. Subsistence is $4.00 per person per day. Vehicles are leased at $300 per week, and mileage is reimbursed at $0.25 per mile. Several items are marked as high-side conservative estimates.
- **Enrollment expectations:** Most students are expected from ASU and other in-state institutions. Off-campus recruiting is planned to supplement enrollment and diversify the student body.
- **Strategic fit:** The memo presents the program as an extension of existing undergraduate initiatives (Hughes, MAR, and ECOREU) and as a cost-effective way to educate future ecologists and policy makers.
- **Institutional risk:** The memo states the Station's current agreements have no firm obligations, so the new program can expand use without jeopardizing existing arrangements.
