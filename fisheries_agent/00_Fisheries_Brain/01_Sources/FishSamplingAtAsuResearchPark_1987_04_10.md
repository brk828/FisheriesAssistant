## Executive Summary

This 1987 memorandum by Paul Marsh (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) chronicles fish stocking, sampling, and mortality events in three artificial ponds (Lake A, Lake B, Lake C) at the ASU Research Park, Tempe, Arizona, from 1984 through April 1987. The ponds, initially fishless, were stocked with [[Redbelly Tilapia]] in 1984 for algae control, after which tilapia rapidly established a reproducing population. In late 1986, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] introduced three imperiled Colorado River native fishes — [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] — as part of a conservation stocking effort. A severe cold-weather event in February 1987 caused mass mortality of tilapia, compounded by predation from invasive [[Red Swamp Crayfish]]. Subsequent trammel net and seine sampling in April 1987 indicated that [[Razorback Sucker]] were thriving, showing evidence of substantial growth and high recapture rates, while [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] were captured only in small numbers. By April 1987, a total of 14 fish taxa were documented in the ponds, most having invaded via the connected canal water-intake system rather than being intentionally stocked.

## Background & Description

Size and condition data were reported primarily for the intentionally stocked native species, tracked to assess growth since planting:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**
  - Stocked (Nov 1986): n = 1000, 80–200 mm TL
  - Recaptured (Apr 1987, trammel net): 165–325 mm TL (mean 255 mm, n = 77), mean weight 220 g
  - Recaptured (Apr 1987, seine): 64–562 mm TL (average 238 mm) and 72–620 mm TL (average 180 mm) in two size classes
  - All specimens in "excellent condition"

- **[[Bonytail Chub]]**
  - Stocked (Nov 1986): n = 879, TL ca. 75 mm
  - Recaptured (Feb 1987, trammel net): 1 specimen, est. TL 120 mm (partially consumed, likely by crayfish)
  - Recaptured (Apr 1987, trammel net): n = 2, 214 and 214 mm TL, good condition
  - Recaptured (Apr 1987, seine): n = 2, 104 and 83 mm TL

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
  - Stocked (5 Oct 1986): n = 1,048, TL ca. 75 mm
  - Recaptured (Apr 1987, seine): n = 7, 102–125 mm TL

- **[[Redbelly Tilapia]]** and other tilapia taxa: dead/dying specimens observed 30–150 mm TL during Feb–Mar 1987 mortality events; four tilapia taxa recorded overall (blue, redbelly, mouthbrooder/Mozambique, and "Malacca hybrid").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pond system**: Three artificial ponds (Lakes A, B, C) at ASU Research Park, hydrologically connected to and supplied by the Tempe Canal (part of the Salt River Project canal system), which serves as a continual conduit for unintentional fish introductions.
- **Trophic role of tilapia**: Introduced specifically as a biological control agent for nuisance algae (28 May 1984 stocking of ~1,000 [[Redbelly Tilapia]]); rapidly became the dominant species by biomass and numbers (>90% of ~3,700 fish sampled in Oct 1986).
- **Conservation stocking**: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]] — all Colorado River basin natives of significant conservation concern — were introduced in fall 1986 in a cooperative refugial stocking effort between AZGFD, USFWS, and ASU, with individuals divided primarily between Lake B (largest pond) and split remainders in Lakes A and C.
- **Invasive predator**: [[Red Swamp Crayfish]] (*Procambarus clarkii*) had invaded the system and was implicated as a scavenger/predator on cold-stressed and dead fish, including partial consumption of a recovered bonytail chub; numerous beheaded fish carcasses were attributed to crayfish activity.
- **Canal fish community**: Sampling of the Tempe Canal and laterals (Oct 1986) found [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]] abundant, with [[Sonoran Sucker]] (referred to as "mountain sucker"), [[Channel Catfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Largemouth Bass]] present but uncommon — this canal serves as the ongoing source of colonization for the ponds.
- **Cold-stress mortality event**: A February 1987 cold snap caused thousands of tilapia to aggregate in sunlit shallows in apparent cold torpor at the north end of Lake B; many hundreds died or displayed erratic/disoriented swimming (upside-down, sideways) and were captured by hand due to unresponsiveness.

## Key Findings

1. **Chronological stocking history**: Ponds were fishless until 28 May 1984, when ~1,000 [[Redbelly Tilapia]] were introduced for algae control; native [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]] were added October–November 1986.
2. **Tilapia dominance and crash**: Tilapia (4 taxa) dominated pond catches by October 1986 (>90% of catch), but suffered catastrophic overwinter mortality by February–March 1987 due to cold torpor, with only a handful of live individuals observed by March 1987.
3. **Razorback Sucker success**: April 1987 trammel netting recovered 78% of 102 fish as razorback sucker, representing nearly 10% of the total original stocked population — described as "a remarkable result" — with strong evidence of growth (mean TL increase from stocking size) and excellent condition.
4. **Uncertain status of Bonytail and Squawfish**: Low recapture numbers for [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] precluded quantitative growth assessment, but their small stocked size likely made them less vulnerable to sampling gear; any recaptures were considered encouraging.
5. **Crayfish predation pressure**: Invasive [[Red Swamp Crayfish]] appear to be a significant mortality factor for stressed and dead fish, including partial predation on a recovered bonytail chub and widespread beheading of tilapia carcasses.
6. **Canal as invasion pathway**: The water intake connecting the ponds to the Tempe Canal is the primary vector for unauthorized/unintentional species introductions (e.g., [[Red Shiner]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Gila Sucker]], [[Gila-Mountain Sucker]]), with 14 total taxa documented by April 1987 beyond the four intentionally stocked species.
7. **Unauthorized tilapia introductions**: Report notes that [[Tilapia Zilli]] (redbelly tilapia) was the only tilapia intended for stocking; presence of blue tilapia, mouthbrooder, and Malacca hybrid taxa likely resulted from contamination or unauthorized/undocumented plantings, including an unauthorized tank-held stock of *Tilapia aurea* on Park grounds until at least October 1986.
8. **Largemouth Bass origin**: Reportedly introduced by an unidentified local resident rather than through official stocking or canal invasion — flagged as undocumented and discouraged activity.
9. **Future monitoring**: Authors planned follow-up sampling of all ponds in autumn 1987 to track population trends, particularly anticipated recovery of tilapia abundance and continued growth of native stocked species.
