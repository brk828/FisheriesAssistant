# Species Responsibilities of Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT)

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum (dated December 25, 1992) delineating the taxonomic responsibilities of the **[[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT)** following a November 1990 reorganization. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research, it functions as a **conservation governance record**, listing thirteen fish taxa under active DFRT jurisdiction and documenting the reassignment of seven additional taxa to other regional recovery teams (e.g., the **[[Gila Trout/Chihuahua Chub Recovery Team]]** and **[[Woundfin Recovery Team]]**). The species covered represent the imperiled ichthyofauna of the Gila, Yaqui, and Colorado River basins in the arid Southwestern United States and northern Mexico, a region characterized by fragmented, isolated aquatic habitats and high rates of endemism.

## Background & Description
The source text provides no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data. It is a taxonomic/administrative list only. Species mentioned span multiple families with distinct body plans:
- **Cyprinidae (minnows/chubs):** [[Yaqui chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Sonora chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), [[Gila chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), [[Roundtail chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[beautiful shiner]] (*Cyprinella formosa*), [[loach minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Mexican stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*)
- **Ictaluridae (catfishes):** [[Yaqui catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*)
- **Cyprinodontidae (pupfishes):** [[desert pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
- **Poeciliidae (livebearers):** [[Gila topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), [[Yaqui topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*)
- **Catostomidae (suckers):** [[Flannelmouth sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While not detailed in this administrative memo, the listed taxa collectively represent the specialized desert stream and spring fish fauna of the American Southwest, occupying niches including:
- Small-bodied benthic and riffle specialists (e.g., **loach minnow**, **spikedace**) adapted to swift, rocky desert stream habitats.
- Isolated spring/pupfish habitats characterized by extreme temperature and salinity fluctuations (**desert pupfish**).
- Larger-bodied generalist chubs and suckers occupying pools and mainstem river reaches (**Roundtail chub**, **Flannelmouth sucker**).
- Livebearing topminnows restricted to spring-fed and headwater habitats, historically impacted by non-native mosquitofish competition.

This assemblage exemplifies the biogeographic subdivisions of desert fish conservation, split among the **[[Gila River Basin]]**, **[[Yaqui River Basin]]** (spanning the U.S.–Mexico border), and the upper **[[Colorado River Basin]]**.

## Key Findings
- **Thirteen species/subspecies** remained under direct DFRT responsibility as of the November 1990 reorganization, spanning cyprinid, ictalurid, cyprinodontid, poeciliid, and catostomid lineages.
- The **Mexican stoneroller** (*Campostoma ornatum*) responsibility was explicitly limited to **Arizona and adjacent Mexico populations only**, reflecting cross-border conservation coordination with Mexican range states.
- **Chihuahua chub** was formally reassigned to the **Gila Trout/Chihuahua Chub Recovery Team** (per RD memo to Propst, 4-17-89).
- Four large-river Colorado Basin species — **[[Colorado squawfish]]**, **[[Razorback sucker]]**, **[[Bonytail chub]]**, and **[[Humpback chub]]** — were removed from DFRT oversight (RD letter to Minckley, 5-17-89) with **no replacement Region 1 team** assigned at the time of writing, indicating a conservation administrative gap for these federally significant Colorado River endemics.
- Two Virgin River basin taxa — **[[Virgin River chub]]** and **[[Virgin River spinedace]]** — were reassigned to the newly formed **Woundfin Recovery Team**, reflecting a shift toward basin-specific recovery planning.
- The **Little Colorado Spinedace** was earmarked for a **new, dedicated recovery team** to be formed, distinct from DFRT, signaling ongoing subdivision of desert fish recovery efforts by drainage basin.
- Overall, the document illustrates the fragmented, jurisdictionally complex nature of endangered desert fish management, driven by the isolated, disjunct hydrology of Southwestern desert drainages.
