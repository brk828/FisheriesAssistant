# Composition and Derivation of the Freshwater Fish Fauna of México

#ichthyology #biogeography #freshwater-fish #zoogeography #México #endemism

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive biogeographic analysis documents 375 species of freshwater fishes in [[México]], categorized as primary (132 species), secondary (186 species), and vicarious (57 species) freshwater inhabitants. The fauna is distributed across 47 families and shows remarkably high endemism, with major centers in the [[Rio Lerma-Santiago Basin]] (58-66% endemic), [[Rio Usumacinta-Grijalva Basin]] (36%), and [[Rio Panuco Basin]] (30%). The derivation is approximately balanced between Nearctic/Holarctic sources (40%) and Neotropical/Middle American elements (35% combined), with marine derivatives (15%) and the autochthonous family [[Goodeidae]] (10%) contributing significantly. Despite México's area being only one-fifth that of the continental United States, it possesses nearly 60% as many freshwater fish species, reflecting extraordinary diversification driven by varied physical geography, latitudinal extent (32°30'N to 14°30'N), highland isolation, and marine-to-freshwater adaptations.

## Background & Description

The Mexican freshwater fish fauna comprises approximately 300 species across 47 families, inhabiting environments ranging from tropical river systems to desert springs and high-altitude lakes. The fauna is classified into three ecological groups following Myers (1938, 1949):

**Primary Freshwater Fishes** (132 species): Eight families with 37 genera, almost exclusively restricted to fresh water. Most speciose genera include [[Notropis]] (Cyprinidae, 25 species), [[Catostomus]] (Catostomidae, 10 species), and [[Ictalurus]] (Ictaluridae, 10 species).

**Secondary Freshwater Fishes** (186 species): Eight families with 45 genera, capable of tolerating some salinity (up to 35 ppt in some [[Cichlasoma]] species). Major genera include [[Cyprinodon]] (Cyprinodontidae, 18 species), [[Gambusia]] (Poeciliidae, 19 species), and [[Cichlasoma]] (Cichlidae, 40 species).

**Vicarious Fishes** (57 species): Fifteen phylogenetically diverse families (lampreys to gobies) with 22 genera showing marine affinities but now largely adapted to fresh water. The most prolific genus is [[Chirostoma]] (Atherinidae, 19 species).

Six genera in six families account for 131 species (41% of the combined primary and secondary total): [[Notropis]], [[Catostomus]], [[Ictalurus]], [[Cyprinodon]], [[Gambusia]], and [[Cichlasoma]].

The fossil record extends to the Pliocene [[Chapala Formation]] for primary fishes (including living [[Notropis sallei]] and extinct [[Micropterus relictus]]), late Miocene [[Santa Rosa Formation]] for secondary fishes (goodeid [[Tapatia occidentalis]]), and Pliocene for marine invaders ([[Chirostoma]] species).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic and Environmental Diversity**: México's fish fauna diversification stems from multiple factors: (1) highly varied physical geography with at least three-fifths high relief terrain; (2) great latitudinal extent spanning 18 degrees; (3) isolation of the large tropical highland [[Mesa Central]] containing the important [[Rio Lerma]] fauna; (4) adaptation by many marine groups to fresh water; and (5) presence of the largest Middle American river system, the [[Usumacinta-Grijalva Basin]], in the tropical southeast.

**Desert and Semi-Desert Regions**: More than half of México comprises water-deficient, ecologically unstable desert and semi-desert areas containing a fascinating but stressed fish fauna. Geologically recent aridity (past 8,000-10,000 years) has reduced this fauna through extinction.

**Major Centers of Endemism**:

1. **[[Rio Lerma-Santiago Basin]]**: 58% endemism overall; 66% when considering only the [[Lerma Basin]] above [[Salto de Juanacatlan]]
2. **[[Rio Usumacinta-Grijalva Basin]]**: 36% endemism, major among poeciliids and cichlids
3. **[[Rio Panuco Basin]]**: 30% endemism, with a "species flock" in genus [[Dionda]] and seven endemic poeciliids
4. **[[Rio Balsas Basin]]**: 35% endemism, including 7 endemic species (3 goodeids, 2 poeciliids)
5. **[[Rio Ameca Basin]]**: 32% endemism, all confined to upper river east of 104°45'N
6. **[[Rio Papaloapan Basin]]**: 21% endemism with 6 endemic poeciliids
7. **[[Rio Conchos Basin]]**: 21% endemism (7 of 34 species) despite being tributary to [[Rio Grande]]
8. **[[Rio del Tunal]]**: 62% endemism (8 of 13 species), chiefly cyprinids

**Exceptional Localized Areas**:

- **[[Cuatro Cienegas]]** bolsón (endorheic): ~50% endemism (8 species in 8 families), exceptional diversity of fishes, plants, and invertebrates
- **[[Laguna Chichancanab]]**, Yucatán (endorheic): Unique endemic species flock of 5 [[Cyprinodon]] species, with only one other fish (a poeciliid)
- **[[La Media Luna]]** and environs, upper [[Rio Verde]], San Luis Potosí: Spring-fed lagunas and marshes with 67% endemism (6 endemic species among cyprinids, cyprinodontoids, and cichlids)

All three exceptional areas require protection against loss of biodiversity. Several species are already extinct throughout their range (e.g., [[Evarra bustamantei]], [[Evarra eigenmanni]], [[Evarra tlahuacensis]], [[Notropis orca]], [[Stypodon signifer]], [[Cyprinodon latifasciatus]], [[Skiffia francesae]]) or extinct in México (e.g., [[Scaphirhynchus platorynchus]], [[Gila elegans]], [[Notropis simus]], [[Xyrauchen texanus]]).

## Key Findings

**Faunal Derivation** (375 species total):

1. **American Tropics**: 61 species (16%) comprising 14 primary and 47 secondary fishes
2. **Northern Origin** (Nearctic/Holarctic): 148 species (40%) including 118 primary and 30 secondary fishes
3. **Middle American**: 71 species (19%), all [[Poeciliidae]]
4. **Autochthonous**: 36 species (10%), family [[Goodeidae]]
5. **Vicarious** (marine invaders): 57 species (15%)

**Taxonomic Composition**: The fauna shows approximately equal representation from Nearctic/Holarctic sources and Neotropical/Middle American elements, with species of marine ancestry and the autochthonous family [[Goodeidae]] also nearly equally represented.

**Species Richness**: Despite México's area being only about one-fifth that of the continental United States, it possesses nearly 60% as many freshwater fish species (approximately 300 vs. 500+ in U.S. and Canada combined), demonstrating exceptional diversification.

**High Endemism**: The great geographic diversity and isolation of drainages have led to exceptionally high degrees of endemism, with several basins showing 30-66% endemic species. The [[Rio Lerma-Santiago Basin]] represents the most significant center of endemism.

**Conservation Concerns**: Recent aridity and human activities have already caused extinctions of multiple taxa. Three remarkable ecosystems ([[Cuatro Cienegas]], [[Laguna Chichancanab]], [[La Media Luna]]) showing exceptional endemism and diversity require immediate protection.

**Systematic Notes**: The document provides detailed taxonomic updates including recognition of [[Ameiurus]] separate from [[Ictalurus]] based on genetic differences, revision of [[Chirostoma arge]] as a species complex, description of new [[Cichlasoma]] species, and documentation of the [[Cyprinodon]] species flock in [[Laguna Chichancanab]].

**Biogeographic Patterns**: The distribution patterns reflect complex geological history, including isolation of highland basins, marine incursions, and relatively recent aridification. The [[Mesa Central]] highland isolation has been particularly important for diversification of the [[Rio Lerma]] fauna.
