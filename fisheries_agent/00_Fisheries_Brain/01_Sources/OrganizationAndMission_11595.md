# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) document, dated November 1991 with a March 1992 membership correction, sets out the organization, mission, and procedures of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for four endangered fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The program aims to recover these species while allowing water development to proceed in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It is organized around a set of committees (Implementation, Management, Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information and Education) with defined missions, responsibilities, and membership rules. The document is mainly administrative and governance-focused rather than a research report, so its biological content is limited to the program's goals, recovery elements, and stocking and offset policies.

## Background & Description

**Species and participants.** The program targets the Colorado squawfish, humpback chub, bonytail chub, and razorback sucker in the Upper Basin. Participants include the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]], the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], the Western Area Power Administration, the states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, Upper Basin water users, and conservation organizations.

**Origins.** Since 1978 the Service has issued over 100 [[Biological Opinion]]s on water project impacts in the Upper Basin, concluding that water projects and their associated flow depletions were jeopardizing the endangered fishes. To avoid a standoff between resource protection and development under [[Endangered Species Act]] [[Section 7 Consultation]], the Service organized a Coordinating Committee. After three years of data analysis and negotiation, the program was finalized (program document dated September 29, 1987). The governors of the three states, the Secretary of the Interior, and the Western Area Power Administration signed a [[Cooperative Agreement]] in January 1988.

**Goal and timeframe.** The goal is to recover and delist the fishes by restoring self-sustaining populations and protecting sufficient habitat. Fifteen years was set as the initial timeframe.

**Five recovery elements** (detailed in Appendix A):
1. [[Habitat Management]]
2. Habitat development and maintenance
3. [[Native Fish Stocking]]
4. [[Nonnative Species And Sportfishing]]
5. Research, data management, and monitoring

**Organizational structure.** Figure 1 (organizational chart) shows a committee structure led by the [[Implementation Committee]], which oversees the [[Management Committee]], which in turn directs the Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information and Education committees.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and flows.** Habitat management centers on identifying flow needs for endangered fish habitat and acquiring those flows under state water law, primarily through [[Instream Flow]] protection. The Bureau of Reclamation committed to refining operations of [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]], [[Blue Mesa Reservoir]], and [[Ruedi Reservoir]] to reduce their impacts on the fishes. Habitat development includes backwaters, spawning and nursery habitat, and fish passage where appropriate.

**Stocking and genetics.** A Propagation/Genetics Management Plan (see [[Propagation Genetics Management Plan]]) governs stocking. Its goals are to avoid immediate population extinction, develop and maintain self-sustaining populations, preserve the capacity of populations to adapt to changing environments, and maintain their capacity for continued speciation.

**Nonnative species.** The program calls for curtailing nonnative stocking where it could conflict with endangered species, along with research on competition and predation, review of sport fishing rules, public education, and enforcement.

**Water-depletion offsets.** Under Section 7, new water projects make a one-time contribution of $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion to offset indirect impacts. This amount is adjusted annually for inflation and was $11.50 per acre-foot at the time of writing. The Service applies the provision case by case, depending on whether recovery progress, mainly instream flow protection, is sufficient to offset a project's effects. For projects with direct habitat impacts, the Service suggests reasonable and prudent alternatives to avoid jeopardy.

**Water rights.** The [[Water Acquisition Committee]] coordinates acquisition and legal protection of water rights, including a Little Snake Subcommittee focused on conditional water rights in the [[Little Snake River]] Basin, coordinated with strategies for the Yampa and Green Rivers.

## Key Findings

- **Program scope:** Four endangered Upper Basin fishes, five recovery elements, and a 15-year initial timeframe.
- **Consultation history:** Over 100 biological opinions since 1978 found that water project depletions jeopardized the fishes, which prompted the cooperative program.
- **Governance:** The Implementation Committee operates by consensus, meets at least once a year, and is explicitly not a Federal Advisory Committee Act committee. Its authority does not diminish or add to the authority of the states or federal government.
- **Management and budget deadlines:**
  - Management Committee: program guidance by December 15; prioritized annual work plan by August 1.
  - Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information and Education committees: program guidance by January 15; prioritized work plans by July 1 (Biology by July 1 as well).
- **Committee terms:** Chairs and vice chairs serve two-year terms, and consecutive terms are discouraged.
- **Subcommittees:** The Biology Committee has Propagation/Genetics, Nonnative/Sportfish, Instream Flow, and Habitat/Life History subcommittees. The Water Acquisition Committee has a Little Snake Subcommittee.
- **Meeting procedures:**
  - Draft meeting summaries are due within 10 days, and final summaries within 2 months.
  - Final agendas go out 3 weeks ahead.
  - Background materials are due 14 days ahead, with a minimum of 10 days.
- **Credit for participation:** Agencies earn contribution credit only for technical committees (Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information and Education), not for the Implementation, Management, or subcommittees.
- **Researcher engagement:** Research biologists are invited to serve on technical subcommittees and the Long Range Plan process. The Biology Committee is directed to build an outreach program and to document the time and resources it requires.
- **Offset fee:** $10 per acre-foot originally, adjusted for inflation to $11.50 per acre-foot, applied case by case.
