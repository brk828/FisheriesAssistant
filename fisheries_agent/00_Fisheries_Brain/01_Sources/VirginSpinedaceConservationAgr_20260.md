# Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement: FY1995 Annual Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepidomeda

## Executive Summary

This draft annual assessment, prepared by Jane Perkins of the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR), reports on conservation activities for the [[Virgin Spinedace]] ([[Lepidomeda Mollispinis]] mollispinis) during Fiscal Year 1995 under the [[Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement]] (VSCA). The Agreement aims to reduce or eliminate significant threats and to enhance or stabilize occupied and unoccupied historic habitat. The document covers the three threats identified by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (FWS), summarizes quarterly actions, and outlines anticipated 1996 work. The assessment concludes that all actions initiated in the first year of implementation are on track and consistent with the VSCA.

## Background & Description

The Agreement was developed to expedite conservation measures needed for the continued existence and recovery of the species. It rests on two objectives and four general administrative actions:

- **Objectives:** (1) reduce and eliminate significant threats; (2) enhance and/or stabilize specific reaches of occupied and unoccupied historic habitat.
- **Administrative actions:** coordination of conservation activities; implementation of conservation schedules; funding of conservation actions; and assessment of conservation progress (including quarterly reports and annual assessments).

The three threats, identified under the criteria for federal listing in Section 4(a)(1) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (59 FR 25875), are:

1. Present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment of habitat or range.
2. Predation, competition, and disease.
3. Other natural or manmade factors affecting the species' continued existence.

The Agreement also required that, once signed, a withdrawal notice for the proposed listing and critical habitat be prepared. The notice was reviewed by the FWS Regional Solicitor's Office in the April–June quarter and was under review in Washington, with publication in the [[Federal Register]] anticipated in August or September 1995.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Governing documents and the Program.** Aquatic resource management in the Utah portion of the [[Virgin River]] Basin is directed by three documents: the Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan (VRFRP), the VSCA, and the Virgin River Management Plan (VRMP). Many of their goals overlap. The UDNR, UDWR, and FWS began developing the [[Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management And Recovery Program]] (Program), a multi-agency framework to implement VRFRP and VSCA actions and to facilitate VRMP actions while allowing development to proceed in compliance with applicable laws.

**Technical Advisory Committee.** Team members and technical advisors for the VRFRP and VSCA met as the Program's Technical Advisory Committee, at least twice per month from June through November 1995. Topics included:

- Describing existing conditions as a baseline
- Minimum population maintenance flows
- Conservation actions (habitat enhancement, protection, re-establishment)
- Initial development of mitigation protocols

**Key localities.** Important sites named in the document include:

- [[Quail Creek Diversion]] reach of the mainstem Virgin River, historically dewatered for nearly 100 years
- [[Santa Clara River]] below [[Gunlock Reservoir]] and downstream of Veyo
- [[Beaver Dam Wash]] (upper portions and a small tributary of Beaver Dam Creek)
- [[Ft. Pierce Wash]] (spelled "Ft. Pearce Wash" in the quarterly reports)
- Schroeder Reservoir and Baker Reservoir, as well as the LaVerkin Creek bridge project

**Threat-related species.** The document identifies non-indigenous fishes as a concern, specifically [[Red Shiner]] (targeted for eradication), [[Green Sunfish]] (removal analysis), [[Brown Trout]] (stocking discontinued), and rainbow trout (selective removal planned for 1996).

## Key Findings

**Threat 1: Habitat destruction, modification, or curtailment**

- **3 cfs below the Quail Creek Diversion:** This was one of the most significant actions of 1995. The flow was provided and maintained, and monitoring began with UDWR and [[Utah State University]] (USU) sampling in March 1995 at four stations, with further monitoring in October. The Q2 report states that no spinedace were observed in this reach. The Q4 report notes sampling at the four established sites in October.
- **3 cfs below Gunlock Reservoir:** The [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] (WCWCD) initiated plans to deliver 3 cfs into the Santa Clara River. Full implementation may take several years, but once in place, an additional 19 miles of spinedace habitat may be provided and maintained. In the fourth quarter, WCWCD met with the Shivwitz Indian tribe regarding the Santa Clara Pipeline.
- **Flow protection:** No new diversions or depletions occurred, and existing natural flows and re-established flows were maintained.
- **Habitat enhancement by BLM:** The [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) worked with Santa Clara, Ivins, and the City of Virgin to develop cooperative management plans addressing off-highway vehicle use. BLM also began baseline riparian monitoring on the Santa Clara River below Gunlock Reservoir and the Virgin River near Grafton, with further studies anticipated on the Santa Clara, Virgin River, and West Fork Beaver Dam Wash.
- **Stream alteration review:** The Division of Water Resources and UDWR reviewed approximately 27 [[Stream Alteration Applications]]. Where negative impacts were identified, procedures were modified to eliminate or significantly reduce effects on the spinedace and its habitat. The FWS reviewed three applications (comments dated 1/96) and commented on the FHWA LaVerkin Creek Bridge Replacement project (STP-BRF-0017(21)) in December.

**Threat 2: Predation, competition, and disease**

- **Brown trout stocking:** UDWR discontinued brown trout stocking in the Santa Clara River downstream from Veyo.
- **Other stocking:** All other UDWR stocking and introductions were conducted only where consistent with the Agreement and [[American Fisheries Society]] procedures for nonnative fish introductions.
- **Fishing regulations:** NDOW proposed restricting sport fishing to artificial lures on Beaver Dam Wash and Schroeder Reservoir to prevent introduction of undesirable species. The proposal was preliminary at the time of reporting.
- **Red shiner eradication:** The Ft. Pierce Wash fish barrier, intended to eradicate red shiner from the Virgin River, was funded in part through FWS Challenge Cost Share funds. Preliminary design was completed in August 1995, and construction was initiated in the fourth quarter.
- **Green sunfish:** UDWR began an analysis for removing green sunfish from the Santa Clara River, evaluating a step-by-step treatment of river segments. Removal is anticipated to begin at Baker Reservoir and proceed down to Moody Wash.

**Beaver Dam Wash reintroduction (Maintain Genetic Viability)**

- NDOW's field assessment for spinedace reintroduction in upper Beaver Dam Wash was cancelled in the second quarter due to high flows and completed in the third quarter.
- The preferred alternative is to reestablish spinedace in a small tributary of Beaver Dam Creek. The lower reaches can be cleared of nonnative fish, and the upper reaches are fishless with existing barriers.
- Nonnative removal and reintroduction are planned for 1996.

**Threat 3: Other natural or manmade factors**

- No actions were taken to address this threat in FY1995.

**Population monitoring**

- UDWR and USU conducted the annual monitoring in September and October 1995 at 12 standard sites on several tributaries using electrofishing. The annual report states that numerous spinedace were sampled, while the third-quarter report describes the catch as several.
- The purpose is to evaluate population trends and status.

**Assessment and outlook**

- The assessment finds all first-year actions on track and consistent with the VSCA.
- Most protocols, models, and plans will be developed through the Program rather than completed specifically under the VSCA.
- Anticipated 1996 VSCA actions include additional chemical treatments for red shiner eradication, completion of the Ft. Pierce Wash barrier, annual spinedace monitoring, response monitoring below the Quail Creek Diversion, selective rainbow trout removal and spinedace reintroduction on Beaver Dam Wash, and maintenance of population maintenance flows.
- Several Table 1 entries were noted as ongoing or planned. The Q1 report left the stream-mile figure for the Quail Creek flow blank.
