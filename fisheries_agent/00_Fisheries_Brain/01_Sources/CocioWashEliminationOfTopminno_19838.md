# Cocio Wash Gila Topminnow Pollution and Mitigation Correspondence

#ichthyology #marine-biology #topminnow

## Executive Summary

This correspondence set, dated September to December 1983 with a related Arizona Department of Health Services resolution (Handout #6-b-84), documents how recurrent [[Mine Tailings Release]]s from the [[ASARCO]] [[Silver Bell Mine]] eliminated the [[Gila Topminnow]] population in [[Cocio Wash]]. The [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) Phoenix District, the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD), and the [[United States Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) treated the population as a naturally occurring one protected under the [[Endangered Species Act Of 1973]]. Because the wash could not be rehabilitated, ASARCO paid $8,000 into a special AGFD fund for [[Topminnow Reintroduction]] at other sites as [[Mitigation]]. In return, the [[Water Quality Control Council]] was to recommend no enforcement action for the 1981 [[Surface Water Quality Standards]] violations.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:** The Gila topminnow is a federally endangered fish. The BLM letter states that the Cocio Wash population is naturally occurring and is named in the [[Gila And Yaqui Topminnow Recovery Plan]] (USFWS, Region 2, 1983) as one of 11 remaining natural populations. The site lies very near where the species was first described from the [[Santa Cruz River]] in 1853.
- **Management history:** A [[Habitat Management Plan]] (HMP) was prepared and implemented in 1977. BLM, AGFD, and USFWS managed and monitored the population from that point.
- **Habitat condition:** Cocio Wash habitat was generally considered less than optimal compared with other sites, but always suitable, particularly given the numbers of topminnow that once inhabited it.
- **Parties and documents:**
  - BLM letter to the [[Arizona Department Of Health Services]] (ADHS) Water Quality Management Section, December 13, 1983, commenting on ASARCO's revised recommendation and summary of facts (December 2, 1983).
  - AGFD letter to ASARCO's general manager, September 6, 1983, accepting ASARCO's August 25, 1983 reply and establishing the $8,000 account.
  - AGFD letter to ASARCO's counsel, October 4, 1983, acknowledging the payment (check dated September 29, 1983) and closing the Game & Fish interest in the matter.
  - ADHS Department staff resolution (Handout #6-b-84) recommending Council actions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cause of loss:** Two separate spills of gray mine tailings from the Silver Bell Mine reached Cocio Wash. Water quality and sediment investigations by ADHS staff in September 1981 documented violations of surface water quality standards attributed to these spills.
- **Acute toxicity ruled out:** Topminnows survived immediately after the spill, so acute toxicity was excluded as the cause.
- **Mechanism of elimination:** The population was lost by March 1982 through habitat damage that interfered with its life cycle. The BLM letter adds that the wash lost its ability to support topminnow and other aquatic life forms.
- **Residue decision:** Deposits were not removed, because removal was judged to cause more habitat harm. Natural scouring from later runoff was expected to clear them.
- **Engineering response:** ASARCO completed drainage collection dikes at the Silver Bell Mine in November 1981.
- **Rehabilitation:** The interagency investigative team recommended against renovating Cocio Wash because of the likelihood of further mine releases. The BLM letter notes the condition could probably be reversed in principle.

## Key Findings

1. **Mine-caused extirpation:** Recurrent tailings releases from the ASARCO Silver Bell Mine in 1981 were responsible for eliminating the Cocio Wash Gila topminnow population, which agencies regard as naturally occurring and protected.
2. **Regulatory violations:** ADHS staff documented surface water quality standard violations in Cocio Wash below the mine in September 1981.
3. **Mitigation payment:** ASARCO contributed $8,000 to a special AGFD fund, used exclusively for introducing Gila topminnows to selected sites and managing those sites. AGFD confirmed on October 4, 1983 that this closed its interest in the Cocio Wash matter.
4. **Reintroduction as compensation:** AGFD stated that the funds were crucial for developing other sites for reintroduction because Cocio Wash "cannot be rehabilitated from its present state."
5. **Enforcement recommendation:** ADHS staff recommended that the Water Quality Control Council take no further enforcement action on the 1981 violations, while reserving the option to act on other water quality violations, and that AGFD report progress on the introduction program to the Council.
6. **Date discrepancy:** The resolution states ASARCO contributed the total in October 1983, while the AGFD acknowledgment cites a check dated September 29, 1983. The two dates refer to the same payment and are not reconciled in the text.
