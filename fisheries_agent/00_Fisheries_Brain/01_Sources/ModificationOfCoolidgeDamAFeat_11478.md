# Modification of Coolidge Dam — San Carlos Irrigation Project

## Executive Summary

This document is a **scoping statement** issued by the Bureau of Reclamation for an Environmental Assessment (EA) evaluating proposed safety modifications to **[[Coolidge Dam]]**, part of the **[[San Carlos Irrigation Project]]** on the **[[Gila River]]** in Arizona. While the source is primarily an engineering and regulatory document concerning dam safety (hydrologic-structural deficiencies and seismic stability problems), it explicitly identifies **impacts on aquatic resources** in both **[[San Carlos Reservoir]]** and the downstream **Gila River** as a significant environmental issue requiring analysis. No specific fish species, morphological data, or population data are provided in this excerpt; however, the document establishes the ecological and regulatory context in which fisheries and aquatic biology assessments will occur.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat System**: The relevant aquatic habitat consists of two distinct zones affected by dam operations:
  - **[[San Carlos Reservoir]]** (impounded lentic/lake habitat upstream of the dam), with conservation storage currently restricted to elevation 2511 ft due to inoperative spillway gates, but authorized up to elevation 2523 ft.
  - **[[Gila River]]** downstream of Coolidge Dam (lotic/riverine habitat), which receives regulated flow releases for irrigation of the **Gila River Indian Community** and the **San Carlos Irrigation and Drainage District**.
- **Riparian Habitat**: Riparian corridors both around the reservoir and along the downstream river reach are flagged as a distinct, related environmental concern (see [[Riparian Habitat Impacts]]), which typically supports the food web and habitat structure relevant to fish and aquatic fauna.
- **Threatened/Endangered Species Context**: The **bald eagle** (a piscivorous species dependent on reservoir/riverine fish populations) and the **Arizona cliffrose** (terrestrial plant) are named as species of concern, indicating the reservoir and river system supports habitat for federally listed species, with fish populations likely serving as a prey base for avian predators such as the bald eagle.
- **Flow Regime Alteration**: Proposed modifications (new spillway gates, raised conservation pool elevation, overtopping protection) would alter reservoir water levels and downstream flow/release patterns, both of which are primary drivers of aquatic habitat quality in reservoir and riverine systems.

## Key Findings

- **Dam Safety Deficiencies**: A 1980 Reclamation safety evaluation identified that Coolidge Dam cannot safely pass the **Probable Maximum Flood (PMF)**, with potential overtopping by up to 21 feet, threatening spillway and abutment failure.
- **Proposed Structural Modifications**:
  1. Reconstruction/realignment/widening of spillways.
  2. Construction of 2–3 ft thick concrete overtopping protection on downstream abutments.
  3. Concrete overlays on both abutments near the dam crest.
  4. Bearing blocks and post-tensioned steel tendon anchors in the left abutment (seismic stability, Maximum Credible Earthquake).
  5. Installation of new operable spillway gates, enabling reservoir operation up to authorized conservation elevation 2523 ft (raised from current operational cap of 2511 ft).
- **Aquatic Resource Impact Identified as Key Issue**: Impacts on aquatic resources in **both** the reservoir and downstream river are explicitly listed as Significant Issue #1 for EA analysis — the primary fisheries-relevant finding in this document.
- **Interdisciplinary Team**: Reclamation's environmental analysis team explicitly includes an **[[Aquatic Biologist]]** and a **[[Fisheries Biologist]]**, confirming that fish and aquatic community impacts (e.g., from altered reservoir elevation, flow regime changes, and construction activity) are anticipated to require dedicated scientific evaluation, though findings are not yet presented in this scoping-stage document.
- **Related Environmental Issues**: Riparian habitat, threatened/endangered species (bald eagle), recreation, and downstream water use are all interconnected issues likely to intersect with aquatic/fisheries findings in the forthcoming EA.
- **Alternatives Under Consideration**: Range spans from "No Action" (no dam modification) to full corrective action; the resolution chosen will directly determine the degree of hydrological and habitat alteration to both reservoir and riverine aquatic systems.
- **Data Gap**: This scoping statement contains no baseline fisheries survey data, species lists, or population metrics — such data would be expected in the subsequent EA or associated biological technical reports.
