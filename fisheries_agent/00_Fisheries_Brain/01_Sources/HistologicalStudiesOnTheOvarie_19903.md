# Histological Studies on the Ovaries of Beaked Whales, Mesoplodon stejnegeri, Stranded on the Coast of Niigata District

#cetology #marine-biology #ziphiidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines the histology of ovaries from two [[Beaked Whales]] (*Mesoplodon stejnegeri*) that stranded on the coast of the [[Niigata District]], [[Sea of Japan]], in March 1992. The ovaries were too degenerated for electron microscopy, so the work relied on light microscopy of paraffin sections. Neither a [[Corpus Luteum]] nor a [[Corpus Albicans]] was found in either animal. The cortex was largely replaced by fibrous and adipose connective tissue, and the medulla contained numerous thick-walled, coiled muscular arteries. The author concludes that both whales were old, spent and senescent, with ovaries histologically comparable to post-menopausal human ovaries. Ovary weights were at or below the reported weights at sexual maturity, which is consistent with regression.

## Background & Description

*Mesoplodon* beaked whales are among the rarer small whales. Knowledge of their ecology, life history and reproductive biology is described as meager. The document places beaked whale strandings within a wider pattern. Thirteen migrating whale species strand frequently on the Niigata coast, mainly in deep winter and early spring. Strandings of *Mesoplodon* spp., [[Pacific White-Sided Dolphin]] and [[Dall's Porpoise]] have increased recently.

Records of *Mesoplodon* from the west coast of Honshu total 36. The first was a stranding near Akita City, initially reported as *M. bowdoini* by Nishiwaki (1962). Two further *M. stejnegeri* were collected in 1993, one at Atsumi Town, Yamagata Prefecture (15 February 1993), and one 4.8 m female at Tokugoh Beach, Noh Town, Niigata Prefecture (shortly before 2 March 1993).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a reproductive-histology study, not an ecological survey, so habitat and feeding ecology are not described. The strandings are placed in a regional context of seasonal, winter-dominated stranding of cetaceans along the Sea of Japan coast.

The author compares the findings with other cetacean work:

- [[Marsh and Kasuya 1984]] described age-related ovarian change in the [[Short-Finned Pilot Whale]]. Aged animals lacked follicles, and the cortex was fibrous. The same pattern appeared here. The present study adds that the thick-walled vessels in the corpus fibrosum are coiled muscular arteries, a detail earlier work did not mention.
- [[Lockyer 1987]] examined 67 pairs of ovaries of the [[Minke Whale]] but did not describe atretic follicles.
- [[Collet and Robineau 1988]] examined seven [[Commerson's Dolphin]] ovaries but did not study spent or senile females.
- [[Honma et al. 1992]] considered a [[Harbor Porpoise]] to be spent.

## Key Findings

1. **No corpora present.** Neither a corpus luteum nor a corpus albicans was detected in either specimen. This is the central observation, since the ovaries show no recent ovulation signature.
2. **Cortical regression.** The germinal epithelium was reduced, the tunica albuginea had partly become fat tissue, and the cortex was replaced by fibrous or adipose connective tissue. Follicles were absent from both cortex and medulla.
3. **Excretory tubular glands.** Tubular glands resembling uterine glands occurred in the tunica albuginea, some with cell debris in the lumen.
4. **Medullary arteries.** Numerous coiled, thick-walled muscular arteries with narrow lumina, containing melanin, characterised the medulla. Some small arteries near the cortex resembled young follicles.
5. **Degenerating lutein masses.** Masses of granular lutein cells were in regression, with almost all in fatty degeneration. Only a few had pycnotic nuclei. These masses had a yellow tint to the naked eye.
6. **Atrophied corpora albicantia.** Several atrophied corpora were seen near a large tumescence, probably derived from an antrum. Their bodies were collagenous, with thick-walled arteries, and were difficult to distinguish from surrounding medullary fibrous tissue.
7. **Macroscopic features.** The largest surface swelling was 8 mm in diameter in specimen A. Both ovaries connected to a bicornuate uterus, with no fetus present and no evidence of milk production.
8. **Senescence.** The author concludes that both whales were spent, senile individuals. Their histology resembles post-menopausal human ovaries.
9. **Weights versus maturity.** Ovary weights were less than or equal to the onset-of-maturity weights of 11–12 g given by [[Mead 1989]] for *Mesoplodon*. Mead's mean for sexually mature females was 13–15 g, and the maximum single ovary weight was 49 g (*M. mirus*). The author reads this as regression and atrophy.
10. **Caveats.** The author notes that post-reproductive pilot whales can still lactate and that post-reproductive status is hard to establish with certainty. Ages were unknown, and the sample was only two animals. Further study of stranded individuals is called for to set histological criteria for cetacean reproductive senescence.
