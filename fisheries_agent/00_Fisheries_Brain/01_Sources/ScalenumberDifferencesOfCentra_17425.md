# Scale-Number Differences of Central Stonerollers Incubated and Reared at Different Temperatures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #leuciscidae

## Executive Summary

This study tests whether water temperature during embryonic and larval development changes the [[Lateral-Line Scale Count]] of the [[Central Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma anomalum*). Progeny from four parental crosses were incubated and reared at 13.9 °C, 17.7 °C and 24.3 °C. Fish reared at the lowest temperature had significantly more lateral-line scales (mean 47.6) than those reared at the highest temperature (mean 44.7). The authors conclude that this meristic character is thermally labile within genetic limits, which complicates identification of females and immature males that are otherwise separated from the [[Largescale Stoneroller]] (*C. oligolepis*) by meristic counts.

## Background & Description

- **Meristic plasticity:** [[Jordan (1893, 1919)]] first reported structural plasticity in fishes, proposing that vertebral numbers rise from the equator toward the poles. Later work showed that vertebrae, myomeres, fin rays and scales vary with temperature at critical embryonic stages.
- **Taxonomic problem:** The largescale stoneroller was described by [[Hubbs and Greene (1935)]] as a subspecies of the central stoneroller and later elevated to species rank by Pflieger (1971). [[Burr and Smith (1976)]] confirmed the distinction using scale counts, tuberculation, body form and color. Tubercles and body form separate only mature males. Females and immature males must be identified by meristic counts, notably the sum of circumferential and lateral-line scales.
- **Reproduction:** [[Miller (1962)]] recorded spawning at 14–24 °C and noted that unseasonable weather disrupts breeding, so field specimens from variable conditions may be hard to identify.
- **Rationale:** Temperature-induced "phenocopies" could confound species identification, so the author tested the thermal lability of lateral-line scale number.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** Rock Creek, a small, clear, spring-fed, gravel-bottomed stream in Jefferson County, typical of the Ozark upland of Missouri, draining directly into the [[Mississippi River]]. No largescale stonerollers had been collected there.
- **Population baseline (n = 50):** Lateral-line scales averaged 51.5 (SD 2.2, range 44–58); circumferential scales averaged 43.1 (SD 2.1, range 40–49). These values closely resemble the Meramec River population reported by Pflieger (1971) (Table 1: lateral-line mean 50, circumferential mean 42).
- **Comparative samples (Table 1):** Wisconsin central stonerollers (n = 168; lateral-line mean 52, range 47–58); Wisconsin largescale stonerollers (n = 258; mean 45, range 41–49); Meramec River central stonerollers (n = 65; mean 50); Meramec River largescale stonerollers (n = 60; mean 44).
- **Species identity:** No fish in the Rock Creek sample or broodstock matched largescale stoneroller characters.

## Key Findings

- **Experimental design:** Three ripe females were stripped and each egg batch was fertilized by one of four males, giving four crosses. Eggs (109–176 per aliquot) were split among three temperatures (±1.5 °C). Fish were reared to a mean of 25 mm standard length (range 18–31 mm) at about 4 months.
- **Developmental timing:** Time to hatch was 4 days at 24.3 °C, 6 days at 17.7 °C and 10 days at 13.9 °C.
- **Larval mortality:** 10.6% at 13.9 °C, 10.3% at 17.7 °C and 27.9% at 24.3 °C.
- **Lateral-line scales:** Fish at 13.9 °C averaged 47.6 and fish at 24.3 °C averaged 44.7 (P < 0.01). The intermediate 17.7 °C group averaged 46.0. The mean difference of 2.9 exceeded the field SD reported for Missouri and Wisconsin samples.
- **Morphological overlap:** Every treatment group produced individuals with lateral-line counts typical of the largescale stoneroller, so meristic counts alone can misidentify specimens.
- **Parental representativeness:** Parental lateral-line counts (53, 55, 51, 55, 48, 49, 47, 49) matched the Rock Creek population.
- **Interpretation:** Progeny counts were lower on average than parental counts, yet some individuals matched or exceeded their parents. The author concludes that lateral-line scale number is genetically limited and temperature-modifiable within those limits.
- **Whole-squamation effects:** Temperature probably alters the entire scale pattern, and not only the lateral-line series. Its effect should therefore be weighed when female and immature males are identified.
- **Methods:** Specimens were bleached, stained with alizarin red S (after Taylor 1967) and counted. Analysis used ANOVA for a randomized-block mixed model, followed by Tukey multiple-comparison tests.
