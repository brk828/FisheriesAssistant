# Colorado Squawfish Interagency Transport Correspondence (1973)

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, but a **1973 administrative letter** from the Denver Service Center (likely U.S. Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, precursor to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) to Robert R. Elliott, Acting Director of the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]]. It concerns the collection and interstate transport of live **Colorado Squawfish** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), a large native cyprinid of the [[Colorado River Basin]], for conservation and propagation purposes. The correspondence documents an early operational effort under the **Lower Colorado River Threatened Fish Species Recovery Team**, reflecting the species' precarious conservation status even in the early 1970s, predating its formal federal listing as endangered in 1980 under the Endangered Species Act.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (meristics, coloration, body proportions) are described in this source. The document is purely logistical/administrative. For reference, *Ptychocheilus lucius* is known in the broader literature as the largest North American cyprinid, historically reaching lengths exceeding 1.8 m, with an elongate, torpedo-shaped body adapted to swift, turbid river currents — see [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] for detailed morphological characterization from other sources.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range referenced:** [[Yampa River]] and [[Colorado River]] (Colorado stretch) — historically core habitat for the species within the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Habitat type:** Large, free-flowing, turbid warm-water river systems; the species is an obligate riverine fish, requiring long unfragmented reaches for migration and spawning.
- **Human-mediated translocation:** The document describes an artificial ecological intervention — permitted collection of wild fish from the Yampa and Colorado Rivers, followed by ground transport to **Grand Junction, Colorado**, and subsequent **air transport** via a California Fish and Game Department pilot/aircraft to the **Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife Fish Hatchery in Boulder City, Nevada**.
- This translocation places the species outside its native basin context temporarily into a hatchery/ex-situ conservation setting, an early example of assisted propagation for a declining native fish — relevant to discussions of [[ex-situ conservation]] and [[fish translocation]] practices.

## Key Findings
- **Permit issued:** Colorado Division of Wildlife's Law Enforcement Division issued a collection permit to **Paul Holden** of Logan, Utah, authorizing collection of Colorado Squawfish from the Yampa and Colorado Rivers.
- **Transport timeline:** Fish were planned for transport to Grand Junction, Colorado on or about **July 27, 1973**.
- **Interagency logistics:** A California Fish and Game Department pilot and aircraft were arranged to fly the fish from Grand Junction to the **Boulder City, Nevada** federal fish hatchery operated by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife.
- **Regulatory approval sought:** The letter requests formal written assurance from the Colorado Division of Wildlife approving interstate/air transport of the species, to be addressed to **Recovery Team Leader Gail Kobetich** (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, Parker, Arizona).
- **Institutional context:** Identifies the existence of the **Lower Colorado River Threatened Fish Species Recovery Team**, indicating organized, cross-agency conservation planning for *Ptychocheilus lucius* was already underway in 1973, years ahead of the species' 1980 federal listing as endangered — an important historical data point for the [[conservation history]] of the [[Colorado pikeminnow]].
- **Personnel noted:** Copies of the correspondence were distributed to a fisheries biologist stationed in Grand Junction, Colorado, and to state wildlife offices in Arizona, underscoring the multi-state cooperative nature of the recovery effort.

> **Note for researchers:** This source functions best as a primary historical/archival document supporting studies on the **regulatory and institutional history of endangered fish recovery programs** in the American Southwest, rather than as a source of biological or ecological data on *Ptychocheilus lucius* itself.
