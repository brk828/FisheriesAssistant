# Annual Assessment of Colorado Squawfish Reproduction and Larval Abundance (Lower Yampa River & Lower Green River) — FY93

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #riverine-ecology

## Executive Summary
This FY93 annual report documents a multi-year (FY91–FY95) monitoring effort under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] to assess reproductive timing, larval abundance, and drift-transport dynamics of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the lower [[Yampa River]] and lower [[Green River]]. Daily dawn drift-net sampling was conducted at sites downstream of known spawning areas in the [[Yampa River Canyon]] and the [[Grey Canyon|Grey]] and [[Desolation Canyon|Desolation Canyons]]. The study is designed to establish baseline reproductive output data to evaluate effects of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] flow management (particularly experimental and biologically-informed release regimes) on recovery of this species and other native fishes in the Green River system.

## Background & Description
- No adult morphological data reported in this document; focus is exclusively on **larval life stage** identification and enumeration.
- Larvae were sorted and identified to species level from bulk drift-net samples containing a mixed ichthyoplankton assemblage.
- Specimens were preserved in **100% ethanol** (rather than formalin) specifically to preserve **otolith microstructure** for future daily-age and growth-rate analyses.
- Larval identification/curation was conducted at the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]], Colorado State University.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** [[Colorado Squawfish]] spawn in canyon-bound reaches (Yampa River Canyon; Green River's Grey and Desolation Canyons) and larvae subsequently drift downstream into low-velocity **nursery habitats**.
- **Reproductive timing (Yampa River, 1993):** Spawning/larval production occurred later and over a more extended period than typical; sampling ran from 1 July–13 August 1993, with supplemental collections through 7 September due to unusually protracted reproduction.
- **Reproductive timing (Green River, 1993):** Sampling from 22 June–20 August 1993; larval drift period was comparatively shorter and less pronounced than in the Yampa.
- **Diel sampling:** Additional collections targeted diel (day/night) variation in drift-larvae abundance on multiple dates in July and August, addressing Objective 4 (diel and seasonal distribution patterns).
- **Flow relationship:** Study explicitly designed to relate reproductive output to environmental variables, especially streamflow and water temperature, linked to operations of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and associated Biological Opinion flow prescriptions (summer/fall and winter/spring flows).
- **Planned expansion:** A third drift-net station in the **Jensen-Ouray reach** was recommended for FY94 to better characterize larval transport rates and density near critical nursery habitats.

## Key Findings
- **Yampa River (1993):**
  - 237 samples collected; **658 Colorado squawfish larvae** captured.
  - First capture: **12 July**; last capture: **27 August**.
  - >85% of larvae collected between **20 July and 12 August**.
  - Distinct catch-rate peaks: **26–28 July, 30 July, 3–11 August**.
- **Green River (1993):**
  - 180 samples collected; **122 Colorado squawfish larvae** captured.
  - First capture: **6 July**; last capture: **17 August**.
  - Only notable abundance peak: **2–4 August**.
- **Comparative pattern:** Larval abundance was substantially higher in the Yampa River (658) than the Green River (122) during the same season, with a longer/later reproductive period observed in the Yampa in 1993.
- **Methodological note:** Ethanol preservation enables future **otolith microstructure studies** (age/growth), previously funded under the related "Early Biology Studies" (Flaming Gorge Project No. 12) — that funding has since ended, so continuation depends on new funding sources.
- **Program status:** Data analysis was incomplete at time of reporting; raw data had **not yet been submitted** to the central database.
- **Budget:** $70,000 total funding (split evenly between [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] and [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]); fully expended with no variance.
- **Recovery relevance:** Findings directly support evaluation of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] flow recommendations and contribute to the broader **5-Year Flaming Gorge Flow Investigations Program** synthesis, reinforcing links between flow/temperature management and reproductive success of [[Colorado Squawfish]].
