# Eagle Creek Fisheries Survey, Greenlee County, Arizona (June 1997)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #native-fish-conservation #threatened-species

## Executive Summary

A comprehensive fisheries survey of [[Eagle Creek]] in [[Greenlee County]], [[Arizona]] was conducted June 23-25, 1997 by [[Arizona State University]] researchers. The survey documented fifteen fish species across seven sampling locations spanning from [[Honeymoon Campground]] to 1.2 km downstream of [[Eagle Creek Pump Station]]. The study revealed a stark contrast in native versus non-native fish distributions above and below the [[PD Diversion Dam]], with native species dominating upstream (native:non-native CPE ratio 5:1 to 314:1) and non-native species dominating downstream (ratio 0.05:1 to 0.16:1). Critical findings included the precarious status of the federally threatened [[Loach Minnow]] population, which was found only at a single modified location with just two specimens collected, and the continued absence of [[Spikedace]] since 1989 despite targeted sampling efforts.

## Background & Description

The survey employed standardized electrofishing methods using a Smith-Root DC backpack electrofisher, supplemented with dip nets and seines. Seven sampling locations were established along [[Eagle Creek]], strategically positioned to assess fish community composition both upstream and downstream of the [[PD Diversion Dam]], a structure that appears to function as a significant barrier affecting species distributions.

**Native Species Documented:**
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)
- [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*)
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) - federally threatened
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*)

**Non-Native Species Documented:**
- [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
- [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*)
- [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*)
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*)
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*)
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)
- [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui*)

Crayfish were present at all sample locations. All species collected had been encountered on previous surveys.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[PD Diversion Dam]] functions as a critical ecological barrier structuring fish community composition in [[Eagle Creek]]. Upstream of this structure, native fish communities remain relatively intact with high catch per effort (CPE) values ranging from 4.69 to 15.72 fish per minute. Seven native species and five non-native species coexist above the dam. In contrast, downstream reaches are dominated by non-native species (CPE 1.86-5.69 fish/min) with only [[Sonora Sucker]] representing native fauna. Seven non-native species were documented below the dam.

Habitat degradation was documented throughout the survey reach, with particular concern for substrate changes including:
- Uniform flattening and widening of stream channels
- Reduction in water depth
- Increased substrate imbrication and embeddedness
- Greater proportion of fine sediment material

These habitat modifications appear most severe at the road crossing below [[Honeymoon Campground]], historically the only known location supporting [[Loach Minnow]] in recent years. The riffle habitat at the road crossing itself, which provided suitable conditions when loach minnow were rediscovered in 1994, has been modified to the point of no longer supporting the species.

## Key Findings

**Loach Minnow Population Status (Critical):**
- Only two [[Loach Minnow]] specimens collected during the entire survey
- Found exclusively in a riffle 100 meters downstream of the first road crossing below [[Honeymoon Campground]]
- The road crossing itself, which provided primary habitat in 1994, now lacks suitable riffle habitat
- Considerable sampling effort required to obtain just two specimens
- Habitat modification at the road crossing appears to have displaced the population
- Road crossing maintenance practices may significantly impact the [[Eagle Creek]] [[Loach Minnow]] population
- Geographic extent of this population remains poorly understood; future sampling recommended

**Spikedace Absence:**
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) not encountered despite targeted sampling efforts
- Special effort made to sample suitable habitat in the [[P-Bar Ranch]] area
- Numerous locations sampled throughout 2.4 km (1.5 mi) of creek
- Species has not been collected since 1989
- Researchers believe spikedace may still inhabit portions of [[Eagle Creek]]

**Native vs. Non-Native Distribution Patterns:**
- Above [[PD Diversion Dam]]: 7 native species, 5 non-native species
- Below [[PD Diversion Dam]]: 1 native species (Sonora Sucker only), 7 non-native species
- Native fish CPE above dam: 4.69-15.72 fish/min
- Native fish CPE below dam: 0.29-0.30 fish/min
- Non-native fish CPE above dam: 0.05-1.06 fish/min
- Non-native fish CPE below dam: 1.86-5.69 fish/min
- Native:non-native CPE ratio above dam: 5:1 to 314:1
- Native:non-native CPE ratio below dam: 0.05:1 to 0.16:1

**Smallmouth Bass Recruitment:**
- Juvenile [[Smallmouth Bass]] more common than in previous years
- 1997 appears to have been a favorable spring for reproduction and recruitment

**Habitat Degradation:**
- Substrate changes documented at all downstream sites
- Increased substrate imbrication, embeddedness, and fine sediment proportion
- Channel modifications include flattening, widening, and depth reduction
- Changes particularly severe at critical [[Loach Minnow]] habitat locations
