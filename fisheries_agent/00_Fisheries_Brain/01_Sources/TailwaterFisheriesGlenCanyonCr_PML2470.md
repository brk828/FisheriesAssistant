# Glen Canyon Dam Tailwater Creel Census and Fishery Survey (July 1971 to June 1972)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This ninth and final segment of a fishery study measured angler pressure, harvest, and biological conditions in the [[Colorado River]] tailwater below [[Glen Canyon Dam]], focusing on the [[Leets Ferry]] access area. The creel census covered July 1, 1971 to June 30, 1972. Angler effort rose slightly, but fish harvest fell sharply, which the authors attribute to the tailwater fishery's dependence on trout stocked in [[Lake Powell]]. Water chemistry showed no characteristics detrimental to fish, and tested heavy metals and pesticides were reported in water and fish tissue. The study was conducted under Section 8 of the [[Colorado River Storage Project Act]] (Public Law 485). Its stated objective was to measure fishing pressure and harvest and to gather biological data for formulating a management plan.

## Background & Description

The tailwater below Glen Canyon Dam is about 16 miles of river accessible from boats. The study set out to measure angler pressure and harvest and to collect biological data for management. Creel census was conducted at Leets Ferry over 66 days (OCR-garbled in source), on a four-day schedule that included two weekdays and two weekend days, using a road block and river bank checks. Creel clerks recorded party size, angler sex, fish caught and kept, fish length, and the gear and bait used. Pressure was estimated by [[Direct Expansion Method]] of known daily pressure, with adjustments for weekdays, weekend days, and holidays.

Supporting sampling included:
- **Limnology:** Quarterly sampling at two stations (Glen Canyon Dam and the Leets Ferry launch ramp) for dissolved oxygen, pH, alkalinity, hardness, and water temperature.
- **Bottom fauna:** 15 [[Eckman Dredge]] samples in deep water (about 10 feet) and 5 [[Surber Sampler]] samples in riffles, averaged per station per trip.
- **Plankton:** Phytoplankton and zooplankton samples.
- **Fish collection:** Gillnetting, stomach analysis (not performed by project staff), and [[Heavy Metals and Pesticides]] testing of water and fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fishery dependence on stocking.** The sharp drop in harvest was linked to the fishery's reliance on trout stocked in Lake Powell. The 1970 Lake Powell trout plants were not made because federal fish were unavailable. In this segment, 4,760 catchable-sized [[Rainbow Trout]] were planted in the tailwater during 1971–72, a number comparable to prior segments.

**Water temperature.** Temperatures at Leets Ferry ranged from 46°F in winter to 52°F in late summer and early fall, with an average annual temperature of 49°F. The [[Water Temperature]] regime is cold and stable, typical of a dam-release tailwater.

**Flow regime.** Average daily flow was roughly 12,500 cfs, ranging from about 7,265 to 12,330 cfs (OCR-uncertain). The highest average flows occurred in July–August 1971 and May–June 1972. Weekend flows were reduced as much as 80% relative to weekday flows, which the authors said would make natural reproduction of benthic organisms difficult if conditions persisted.

**Water chemistry.** No chemical characteristics were detected in the tailwater that would be detrimental to fisheries values (Tables VI and VII).

**Benthic and planktonic communities.** Bottom sampling was attempted in several areas, but organisms were found at only one location, near the [[Diversion Tunnel]] of Glen Canyon Dam. Sampling was greatly hampered by fluctuating flows. Benthic taxa in the tables include [[Gammarus]] (scud) and midge larvae. Reports from area wildlife personnel described snails in the main stream, which may have been affected by flow fluctuations. Plankton results are given as phytoplankton and zooplankton values by month.

## Key Findings

### Angler Pressure and Harvest
- **Interviewed trips:** 779 angler trips were interviewed, producing 889 fish in 3,855 hours of effort.
- **Estimated total:** 3,581 angler trips produced 4,716 fish in 16,595 hours of effort.
- **Change from prior segment:** Angler trips increased 8.2%, angler effort increased 5.9%, and fish harvested decreased 44.2%.
- **Angler composition:** 78% of anglers were male and 22% female (OCR-uncertain). 77% used boats and 23% fished from shore.
- **Origin of trips:** [[Creel Census]] data (Chart I) show 26.0% of trips originated from Phoenix, with other origins including Flagstaff and out-of-state locations.

### Trout Stocking and Size
- 4,760 catchable rainbow trout were stocked in the tailwater during 1971–72 across four dates.
- 938 creeled rainbow trout were measured to the nearest inch (OCR-uncertain count). The average length was 14.6 inches, which the authors described as an increase over previous segments. They attributed this to fewer trout being recruited from Lake Powell.

### Fish Collection
- Gillnetting was attempted three times, totaling several days of netting.
- **Catch:** 13 [[Rainbow Trout]] (average length 17.8 inches), 31 [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], one [[Brown Trout]], one [[Walleye]], and four other fish. [[Channel Catfish]] were also recorded (Table XV).
- The authors attributed the poor netting results mainly to the lack of natural recruitment from Lake Powell.

### Diet
- No stomach analyses were performed by project staff.
- The Arizona Game and Fish Department reported that trout stomachs contained snails, consistent with snail feeding.

### Contaminants
- Water and fish samples were submitted to the Arizona state laboratory for [[Heavy Metals and Pesticides]] analysis, covering mercury, lead, arsenic, DDE, DDD, DDT, and PCBs in water and fish flesh (Table X). Values are partly garbled in the scan, so they are not reproduced here.

### Management Implications
- The dependence of harvest on stocking means that the cessation of Lake Powell plants in 1970 had a strong effect on the fishery.
- Weekend flow reductions and fluctuating releases were identified as a limiting factor for benthic productivity and benthic sampling.
