# Growth of Crappies, Bluegill, and Warmouth in Lake Ahquabi, Iowa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 study from the *Iowa State College Journal of Science* (Richard C. Hennemuth) examines age and growth of [[Black Crappie]], [[White Crappie]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Warmouth]] in [[Lake Ahquabi]], a shallow artificial impoundment in southwestern Iowa. Growth was estimated from scale measurements using the [[Lee Method]] for samples collected in 1952 and 1953. The author links a marked acceleration of growth in 1952 to a decline in turbidity and a 1.5-foot rise in water level in July 1951, and notes a possible shift in crappie species composition.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Lake Ahquabi was impounded in 1935 on Kansas drift covered by loess, about six miles south of Indianola. It covers 130 acres with a maximum depth of 22 feet; about 50 per cent of the basin lies between 7 and 15 feet deep.
- **Vegetation:** Water lily (*Nymphaea odorata*), coontail (*Ceratophyllum demersum*), arrowhead, pondweed, and scattered cattails grow in the littoral zone. Submerged vegetation is nearly absent below 8 feet.
- **Turbidity history:** Secchi disk readings were 19 inches in 1947, 42 inches in 1949, 60 inches in 1950, and 54 inches in 1951. Mean readings were 50 inches (range 30–68) in 1952 and 48 inches (range 35–72) in 1953.
- **Sampling:** Fish were taken by hook and line (largely from angler creels), experimental gillnets, hoop nets, seines, and wire traps. Scale samples were 286 black crappie, 556 white crappie, 764 bluegill, and 192 warmouth. Several other species were also collected, including [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Yellow Perch]], and [[Channel Catfish]].
- **Methods:** Growth was back-calculated by the Lee method. Condition was expressed as a ponderal index (R) and as the English "C" value (C = 22.038 R). Length-weight relationships were fitted by least squares on log-transformed data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The lake passed through the typical reservoir cycle, with good early fishing followed by slower growth as populations became crowded. Turbidity was high before 1951 and fell afterward, possibly from colloidal silt settling onto shore vegetation, better watershed control, and reduced wave erosion. Angling success reportedly improved as turbidity dropped. Warmouth were caught rarely, possibly because they stay in dense weed beds. The author also suggested that black crappie may be less vulnerable to gillnets because they are deeper-bodied relative to length.

## Key Findings

**Growth rates**
- **Black crappie:** Slower than in other Iowa waters. Fish reached creel size (about 8 inches) in their third or fourth summer and about 1/4 pound near that size. Mean back-calculated length was 3.3 in at annulus 1 and 9.7 in at annulus 5. Length-weight relation: Log W = -0.7737 + 3.1430 Log L (slope significantly above 3.0). Condition R ranged from 2.171 (age I) to 2.311 (age IV).
- **White crappie:** Also slower than in other Iowa waters. Fish reached creel size (over 7.5 in) in their third or fourth summer and 1/4 pound in the fourth summer. Length-weight relation: Log W = -0.7682 + 3.0477 Log L (slope not different from 3.0). Condition values were consistently lower than for black crappie.
- **Bluegill:** Steady abundance made this the mainstay of the fishery. Growth was somewhat slower than in other Iowa waters. Fish reached creel size (over 6 in) in their fourth summer and about 1/4 pound in about five years. Polled length-weight relation: Log W = -0.5526 + 3.0757 Log L. Condition factors were higher than for either crappie.
- **Warmouth:** Growth was similar to bluegill and slightly below crappies early in life, with larger increments later. Growth was slower than at Red Haw Lake, Iowa. Condition factors were considerably higher than for crappies, indicating a stockier build.

**Creel composition (1953)**
- White crappie made up about 47 per cent of the summer angler catch.
- Bluegill made up 46.8 per cent of the sample catch.

**Population shifts**
- In 1953, black crappie of age groups 0 and I were more numerous than white crappie of the same ages, while white crappie of age IV outnumbered black crappie of age IV. The author linked this to the clearer water, since white crappie are usually more abundant in turbid waters.
- Black crappie showed some signs of increasing abundance.

**Growth acceleration**
- The annual length increment rose sharply in all species between 1951 and 1952. Increments for 1951 and earlier years were roughly constant and mostly lower than in 1952.
- Paired t-tests between 1952 and 1951 increments were significant for black crappie (t = 3.68), white crappie (t = 6.87), and bluegill (t = 3.64). Warmouth was not significant (t = 2.67).
- Crappie growth may have slowed again in 1953, but the bluegill did not show this trend.

**Summary conclusion:** The author concluded that growth accelerated in 1952, coinciding with reduced turbidity and increased lake volume.
