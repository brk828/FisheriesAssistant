## Executive Summary

This is Quarterly Report No. 6 (Trip No. 6, September 28–October 10, 1993) of the Hualapai Aquatic Resources Study, documenting effects of interim Glen Canyon Dam flows on the aquatic community of the Lower Colorado River from Diamond Creek (RM 225.7) to Pearce Ferry/Lake Mead (RM 280). Sampling combined electrofishing, trammel netting, minnow trapping, and seining across the mainstem, tributaries (Spencer Creek, Surprise Creek, Travertine Falls Creek, Diamond Creek), and the Lake Mead inflow. A total of 3,608 fish were captured across 13 species. The most significant finding was the first-ever capture in this study of a [[Humpback Chub]] (adult female, TL 329 mm) and a [[Bluehead Sucker]], both native species previously undocumented in this reach. Relative native species abundance rose sharply from 2% (Trip 5) to 16% (Trip 6) of total catch, coinciding with a marked decline in exotic [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Common Carp]] abundance and apparent emigration of [[Striped Bass]] back into Lake Mead. The endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] was not detected during this or any prior trip.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat gradients along the study reach:**
- Near Travertine Falls: moderately swift, deep water with fast runs and eddies.
- Near Spencer Canyon: lower gradient, vertical cliffs, talus slopes, emergent shoreline vegetation.
- Above Salt Creek: slow, meandering, heavily vegetated lake-sediment banks; low flow exposed sandbars.
- RM 265 area: slower flow, dense willow/riparian stands.
- Lake Mead inflow (Scorpion Island/Grand Wash Cliffs vicinity): inundated riparian vegetation, travertine cliffs, little to no current.

**Habitat associations:**
- The [[Humpback Chub]] capture site — a strong eddy near [[Salt Creek]] — was noted as habitat "very similar to that occupied by chub near the [[Little Colorado River]]," suggesting eddy/backwater habitat is a key niche feature for this species in the Lower Grand Canyon.
- Fish density was consistently higher in [[Spencer Creek]] and [[Surprise Creek]] than in the Colorado River mainstem, potentially due to tributary-associated differences in water quality or food availability.
- [[Speckled Dace]] were observed in [[Travertine Falls Creek]], though this tributary is very small and likely supports minimal fish biomass.
- [[Diamond Creek]] is heavily disturbed by vehicle traffic from the boat launch (road occupies streambed in places), correlating with very low fish and invertebrate abundance.

**Water quality (Hydrolab Surveyor 3, 30-min intervals):**

Turbidity was high in the mainstem during the first two days, declined with reduced flow, then increased again on October 9 in response to upstream flooding.

**Reproductive/phenological observations:**
- Fish spawning activity had ceased by this trip — no ripe individuals or spawning coloration (e.g., in [[Red Shiner]]) observed.
- Tadpole densities (canyon tree frog, red-spotted toad) had declined greatly in tributaries since the prior trip, presumed due to metamorphosis.
- Minor summer flooding reconfigured channel morphology in Spencer and Surprise Creeks (braiding shifts, enlarged terminal slough, sediment accumulation), and lower "base-flow" conditions reduced water clarity in Surprise Creek pools due to increased primary production/turnover.

- **Native species increase**: Native relative abundance rose from 2% (Trip 5, May–June) to 16% (Trip 6, September–October), driven primarily by increases in [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]].
- **First detections**: First-ever study captures of [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]] occurred on this trip.
- **Exotic species decline**: [[Channel Catfish]] catch was ~4% and [[Common Carp]] catch ~8% of Trip 5 levels; [[Striped Bass]] were rarely encountered in the mainstem (only 2 total, one at RM 273.2, one in Lake Mead at RM 278.5), suggesting emigration back to Lake Mead.
- **Spencer Creek multiple-pass removal sampling (Table 5)**:

| Species | Site 1 (downstream) | Site 2 (upstream) |
|---|---|---|
| [[Speckled Dace]] | 163 | 181 |
| [[Red Shiner]] | 114 | 721 |

  - Trip 6 showed a strong native/exotic shift in Spencer Creek: few carp and no catfish were captured, contrasting sharply with large catfish/carp numbers during the May–June trip. Increased abundance of small native and exotic fish may relate to reduced predation pressure from declining piscivorous catfish populations.
- **Sampling effort (Table 3)**: 170 total samples — 29 EL-220v DC electrofishing, 6 backpack electrofishing, 90 trammel net sets (across 4 mesh/size configurations), 24 minnow trap sets, 21 seine hauls. Trammel nets were judged more effective than electrofishing for adult fish; seining was effective for young-of-year/juveniles but only marginally effective for adults (mainly carp).
- **Endangered species**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) was not captured or observed during Trip 6 or any previous trip in this study.
