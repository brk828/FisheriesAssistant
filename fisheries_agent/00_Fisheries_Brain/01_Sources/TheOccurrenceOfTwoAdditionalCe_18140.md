# The Occurrence of Two Additional Centrarchids in the Lower Colorado River, California

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae #species-distribution #fish-stocking

## Executive Summary

This note documents the first records of [[Red-ear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*) and [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui*) in the lower [[Colorado River]], California. The red-ear sunfish represents a new addition to California's freshwater fish fauna, while the smallmouth bass was introduced through stocking efforts. Specimens were collected in 1950-1951 near [[Parker Dam]] and [[Headgate Rock Dam]]. A taxonomic key is provided to distinguish four *Lepomis* species now present in California waters.

## Background & Description

The [[Red-ear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*) was seined by the writer from the [[Colorado River]] four miles below [[Parker Dam]] and from a small oxbow lake immediately below [[Headgate Rock Dam]] on April 27, 1951. No specimens larger than four inches in length were captured, nor had this species been observed in anglers' catch at the time of writing.

The red-ear is distributed throughout the eastern half of the United States and into the southwest as far as Arizona. It probably became established in the Colorado River in 1948 or 1949, following several plants made by the Arizona Department of Game and Fish in the Headgate Rock Dam area. It is considered a desirable pan fish and in Indiana is regarded as superior to the [[Bluegill]] for stocking in ponds in combination with larger piscivorous game fishes.

The [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui*), while not new to California, represents a new record for the lower Colorado. In August 1950, 3,200 fingerling smallmouth bass obtained from the California Department of Fish and Game's Central Valley Hatchery at [[Elk Grove]] were stocked in the Colorado River at a point four miles downstream from Parker Dam.

### Taxonomic Key

A key to distinguish four *Lepomis* species now present in California waters:

- Anal spines 3; scales large, 53 or fewer in lateral line; body depth usually about one-half standard length; no teeth on tongue
  - Upper jaw extending to or beyond middle of eye; pectoral fins short and rounded, contained about four times in standard length: **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*)
  - Upper jaw not extending nearly to middle of eye; pectoral fins long and pointed, contained a little less to a little more than three times in standard length
    - Opercular bone flexible posteriorly; gill rakers long and slender: **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*)
    - Opercular bone stiff behind; gill rakers short and stout
      - Opercle with definite scarlet spot; cheeks with prominent blue and orange stripes in life; pectoral fins less than one-third standard length in adult; body outlines gibbous: **[[Pumpkinseed]]** (*Lepomis gibbosus*)
      - Opercle with broad scarlet margin; cheeks without conspicuous orange and blue streaks; pectoral fins more than one-third standard length in adult; form more rhomboidal: **[[Red-ear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis microlophus*)

Dr. Carl L. Hubbs identified the sunfish new to California.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Red-ear Sunfish]] establishment in the [[Colorado River]] likely resulted from Arizona stocking programs in 1948-1949 at [[Headgate Rock Dam]]. The species was collected from both the main river channel and oxbow lake habitats, indicating successful colonization of multiple habitat types.

The [[Smallmouth Bass]] stocking represented an experimental introduction to evaluate the species' potential in the lower Colorado River system. The river environment provided conditions suitable for growth, though spawning success had not yet been documented at the time of writing.

## Key Findings

**Red-ear Sunfish Distribution:**
- First California record established April 27, 1951
- Collected four miles below [[Parker Dam]] and at oxbow lake below [[Headgate Rock Dam]]
- All specimens ≤4 inches in length
- Likely established 1948-1949 from Arizona stocking programs
- Adds fourth *Lepomis* species to California waters

**Smallmouth Bass Stocking Results:**
- 3,200 fingerlings stocked August 1950, four miles downstream from [[Parker Dam]]
- 12 specimens observed in anglers' catch by September 1952
- One specimen captured December 1, 1951, approximately 10 miles below planting site
- Measured 11.5 inches fork length at ~18 months old
- Growth rate superior to [[Norris Reservoir]], Tennessee population (8.9 inches total length at similar age)
- No spawning documented; establishment not yet confirmed
- Excellent growth and condition indicate potential for success in Colorado River

**Management Implications:**
- [[Red-ear Sunfish]] considered desirable pan fish, superior to bluegill for pond stocking with piscivorous game fishes
- Taxonomic key provided for field identification of four California *Lepomis* species
- Both species represent range expansions into California waters
