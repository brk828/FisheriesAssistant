# Draft Recovery Plans for Spikedace and Loach Minnow (1990 Federal Register Notice)

## Executive Summary
This 1990 Federal Register notice, issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, announces the availability of **draft recovery plans** for two federally threatened freshwater cyprinid fishes: the **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) and the **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*). Both species are native to the **[[Gila River Basin]]** in Arizona, New Mexico, and historically Sonora, Mexico, and have been extirpated from most of their historic range due to anthropogenic habitat alteration. The notice solicits public comment on proposed recovery actions, including habitat protection, monitoring, non-native species interaction studies, and potential reintroduction efforts. This document is primarily regulatory/administrative but contains embedded species-level ecological and conservation data.

## Background & Description
Direct morphological descriptions are not provided in this notice; however, functional habitat-association traits are noted:

- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*):
  - Bottom-dwelling body form suited to benthic life in high-velocity flows.
  - Adapted to hold position in turbulent water over coarse substrates.

- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*):
  - Body form adapted to occupy shallow, fast-flowing riffle and run habitats.
  - Associated with shear-zone microhabitats (interface between fast and slow current), suggesting fusiform body shape and strong rheotaxis (current-orientation behavior) typical of riffle-adapted cyprinids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Loach Minnow]]**:
  - Habitat: Turbulent waters over **gravel/cobble bottoms** in fast-flowing streams.
  - Feeding/position ecology: Benthic, bottom-dwelling niche within high-energy riverine reaches.
  - Current range: Upper **[[Gila River]]**, **San Francisco River**, and **Tularosa River** (Grant and Catron Counties, New Mexico); **White River**, San Francisco River, Blue River, and Aravaipa, Dry Blue, and Campbell Blue Creeks (Gila, Greenlee, Graham, Navajo, and Pinal Counties, Arizona).

- **[[Spikedace]]**:
  - Habitat: **Riffles and runs** in shallow, flowing water over gravel, cobble, and sand substrates.
  - Primary adult habitat: **Shear zones**, i.e., the boundary microhabitat where fast-flowing water meets slow-flowing water — a key niche-defining feature.
  - Current range: Upper **[[Gila River]]** (Grant and Catron Counties, New Mexico); upper **[[Verde River]]**, and Aravaipa and Eagle Creeks (Graham, Greenlee, Pinal, and Yavapai Counties, Arizona).

- **Co-occurring threats to niche integrity** (both species):
  - Dam construction and impoundment
  - Water diversion (flow reduction/dewatering)
  - Watershed deterioration (erosion, sedimentation, land-use change)
  - Channelization of stream habitat
  - Introduction of **non-native predatory and competitive fishes**, altering community structure and competitive/predation dynamics within the [[Gila River Basin]] fish assemblage.

## Key Findings
- Both the **[[Spikedace]]** and **[[Loach Minnow]]** are listed as **threatened** under the Endangered Species Act (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.) and have been **extirpated from the majority of their historic range** in the Gila River basin.
- Recovery plan objectives include:
  - Protecting existing populations and habitat.
  - Restoring populations to portions of historic habitat.
  - Establishing quantifiable criteria for potential **downlisting**.
- Recovery actions proposed:
  - Protection, monitoring, and enhancement of existing populations and habitats.
  - Study of ecological interactions with **non-native fish species**.
  - Quantification of effects of habitat modification (e.g., flow alteration, channelization).
  - Reintroduction into historic range segments.
  - Consideration of **captive propagation** programs.
  - Public information and education initiatives.
- The recovery plans underwent prior review by federal, state, and local agencies, species experts, business organizations, and conservation groups before being opened for the formal 1990 public comment period (deadline: November 26, 1990).
- This notice exemplifies a **regulatory conservation mechanism** under Section 4(f) of the Endangered Species Act, illustrating the procedural pathway from draft recovery plan to public comment to final adoption for imperiled freshwater fish species in the arid Southwest.
