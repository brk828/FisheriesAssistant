# Proposed Introduction of Rainbow Smelt (Osmerus mordax) to Lake Powell, Utah-Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Osmeridae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #invasive-species #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary
This 1989 memorandum from the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] addresses a resubmitted proposal by the Utah Department of Natural Resources to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] (*Osmerus mordax*) into [[Lake Powell]]. The document evaluates the potential ecological risks of this planktivorous, cold-water introduction to two critically endangered native Colorado River fishes: the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The analysis concludes that adult Bonytail Chub (>80 mm total length) would face minimal competition or predation risk from smelt due to differing diet and habitat preference, while the impact on Razorback Sucker is deemed *unknown* due to a critical data gap in early juvenile life-history and habitat use. The memo also flags disease (notably *Glugea hertwigi*) and hybridization considerations tied to the introduction pathway (adult transfer vs. egg transfer).

## Background & Description
*Note: The source is a management/policy memo, not a morphological study; only incidentally reported size/growth data are available.*

- **Bonytail Chub (BTC)**: Stocking target size >80 mm total length (TL) to avoid predation vulnerability from smelt.
- **Razorback Sucker (RZB)**: Rapid hatchery growth — reaches **320–456 mm TL in 2 years** under hatchery conditions (Bozek et al. 1984).
- **Razorback Sucker early life stages**: Protolarval and mesolarval RZB collected in gravel spawning substrate; **no older young-of-year (YOY) individuals were recovered**, indicating an unsampled or unknown juvenile life-history niche.
- **Rainbow Smelt**: Described functionally as a **cold-water, planktivorous** species; specific morphometric data not provided in text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Rainbow Smelt**: Cold-water column planktivore, proposed for introduction into the pelagic zone of [[Lake Powell]] as a forage/game fish addition.
- **Bonytail Chub**: 
  - Historically a **riverine species**, now largely excluded from native river habitat due to cold-water hypolimnetic releases from dams/reservoirs (Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team, in press).
  - Functions as a **sedentary insectivore**.
  - Preferred temperature: **~20°C** (Valdez and Clemmer 1982) — substantially warmer than smelt's cold-water niche, minimizing direct thermal/dietary overlap.
  - Considered the Colorado River native fish species **least likely** to experience competition or predation from smelt.
  - Currently maintained largely through capture-and-transfer to secure refugia (Marsh, pers. comm.), with recovery reliant on stocking programs.
- **Razorback Sucker**:
  - More resilient to impoundment/river regulation than BTC.
  - Stable, "steady state" adult population documented in **Lake Mohave** and other lower-basin sites.
  - Spawns **November–May** on shallow gravel flats.
  - Debate exists over whether the Lake Mohave population is an aging cohort (30+ years, Minckley 1983) facing eventual senescence, or if **limited recruitment** is ongoing (statistically stable abundance 1975–1988; Bozek et al. 1984; Minckley pers. comm.; Burrell pers. comm.).
  - A critical **unknown niche** exists between the larval gravel-substrate stage and adulthood — juveniles are presumed to either be consumed or migrate into an unsampled habitat.
  - This data gap makes it **impossible to assess** smelt predation/competition impact on RZB recruitment.

## Key Findings
- **Bonytail Chub risk**: Low. Differing thermal preference (20°C vs. smelt's cold-water preference) and trophic niche (insectivore vs. planktivore) suggest minimal interaction; stocking at >80 mm TL further eliminates size-based predation vulnerability.
- **Razorback Sucker risk**: **Indeterminate**. Because the juvenile life stage of RZB (post-larval, pre-adult) is unsampled and unaccounted for, the potential impact of smelt introduction on this critical recruitment window cannot be evaluated. If juvenile RZB are not currently recruiting successfully regardless of smelt, then smelt introduction would have **no additional effect** on the population.
- **Management recommendation for RZB**: Given easy hatchery propagation and strong hatchery growth rates, a **maintenance stocking interval of once every five years** is proposed as sufficient to sustain this long-lived species.
- **Disease risk**: Rainbow Smelt are known hosts of **Glugea hertwigi**, a microsporidian parasite causing unsightly white body-cavity cysts (Scott and Crossman 1973). A formal disease history and stock inspection (e.g., by USFWS Fort Morgan, CO) is recommended prior to introduction.
- **Introduction method tradeoff**: 
  - *Adult (spawning) transfer* — preferred method operationally, but carries **disease/parasite transfer risk**.
  - *Egg transfer* — recommended alternative if disease-free adult stock cannot be confirmed, as it would **eliminate parasite introduction risk**.
- **Hybridization risk**: None identified — no potential for hybridization between Rainbow Smelt and any other species in the Colorado River system.
- **Institutional process**: Proposal required review by the Prohibited and Protected Fish, Amphibians and Reptiles Committee, with recommendations flowing to the Technical Committee and then the [[Colorado River Fish and Wildlife Council]].
