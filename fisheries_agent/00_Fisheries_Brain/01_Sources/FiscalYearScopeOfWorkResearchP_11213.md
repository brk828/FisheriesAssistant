# Aspinall Study - Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin FY-1994

#ichthyology #endangered-species #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #flow-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document comprises multiple research proposals (FY-1994 Scope-of-Work) for the Recovery Implementation Program targeting endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The proposals focus on evaluating flow manipulations from the [[Aspinall Unit]] and their effects on [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Ptychocheilus lucius), [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus), and [[Humpback Chub]] (Gila cypha). Lead agencies include the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Colorado River Fishery Project, Utah Wildlife Resources, and Colorado Division of Wildlife. Studies address ichthyofaunal surveys, larval production and drift, nursery habitat availability and quality, spawning evaluation via radiotelemetry, geomorphological changes, food web dynamics, and flow-recruitment relationships. The overarching goal is to develop biologically defensible flow recommendations to support recovery and delisting of these endangered fishes.

## Background & Description

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] supports remnant populations of three federally endangered fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. Historical data (1979-1981, Valdez; 1977-1982, CDOW) documented sixteen adult Colorado squawfish in the [[Gunnison River]], a gravid female razorback sucker at river mile 55.0 (1981, Holden), and a larval squawfish below [[Redlands Diversion Dam]] (1986). Current distribution and abundance information is needed for recovery planning, particularly in context of the [[Aspinall Unit]] consultation process and potential fish passage facilities at Redlands Diversion Dam.

The [[Gunnison River]] study area extends from its confluence with the [[Colorado River]] to the confluence with the North Fork of the Gunnison (river mile 0-75). The [[Colorado River]] study area spans RM 0-210 (Green River confluence to Debeque, Colorado). [[Westwater Canyon]] (RM 116-124) supports one of the largest populations of [[Humpback Chub]] in the upper basin. The [[15-Mile Reach]] and [[18-Mile Reach]] are recognized as critical spawning and nursery areas.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat & Hydrology**: Flow regime alterations from dam operations have reduced peak flows, causing sedimentation of coarse substrates, loss of backwater habitat, and reduced channel complexity. Aerial photography analysis (1937-1986) indicates reduction in overall width and complexity of the Colorado River within the 15-mile and 18-mile reaches. High spring flows are hypothesized to be critical for maintaining backwater nursery habitats through sediment transport and channel scouring.

**Reproductive Ecology**: [[Colorado Squawfish]] spawning occurs mid-May to early June. Larval drift sampling in July targets peak larval abundance periods. Young-of-the-year (YOY) utilize low-velocity backwater habitats for nursery areas. Radiotelemetry studies monitor adult movements to evaluate potential spawning activity above [[Redlands Diversion Dam]]. [[Humpback Chub]] spawning in [[Westwater Canyon]] occurs in a similar timeframe, with larvae monitored through summer and fall to assess cohort strength.

**Community Structure**: Native food fishes (bluehead sucker, flannelmouth sucker, roundtail chub) are critical prey for adult [[Colorado Squawfish]]. Non-native species (green sunfish, channel catfish, red shiner, largemouth bass, carp) compete with native fishes and may prey on larvae. Longitudinal distribution patterns suggest adult squawfish concentrate in upper reaches (above Westwater Canyon) where native prey densities are higher, while juveniles are more numerous downstream.

**Geomorphology**: Channel morphology studies examine substrate particle size, depth of interstitial voids, and floodplain inundation patterns. Mass balance of sediment transport relates to substrate characteristics and habitat availability. Dendrochronology and surficial geologic mapping define history of channel change and areas of aggradation (lost aquatic habitat).

## Key Findings

**Proposed Research Objectives (FY-1994)**:

1. **Ichthyofaunal Survey (Gunnison River)**: Characterize present fish community including endangered fish from North Fork confluence to Colorado River confluence using electrofishing, entanglement netting, trap netting, and seining. Compare to 1979-1981 USFWS and 1977-1982 CDOW survey data. PIT tag and collect tissue samples from all juvenile and adult endangered fish. Radio-tag adult Colorado squawfish to monitor movement and evaluate spawning potential. Budget: $26,350 (FY-94).

2. **Larval Production Evaluation (Colorado & Gunnison Rivers)**: Use drift-net sampling at five locations (two on Colorado River, two on Gunnison River, one between Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon) to document spawning success, identify peaks in larval drift, and determine input to nursery habitats. Daily dawn sampling throughout July. Samples processed by CSU Larval Fish Lab. Utah stations at Westwater Canyon and near Moab. Colorado stations above and below Gunnison confluence and at Loma. Budget: $88,800 (FY-94); total program $313,400 (FY-94 through FY-97).

3. **Nursery Habitat Availability (Colorado River)**: Determine relationship between habitat availability at recommended Aspinall releases and habitat utilized by young Colorado squawfish. Two intensive study reaches in lower ISMP Reach 1, one in Reach 2. Video acquired 1-2 days prior to sampling for ground-truthing. Sample all backwaters and quiet water areas in July, September, and March. Measure physical and biological habitat characteristics. Analyze aerial photographs (1937, 1954, 1968, 1986) to document backwater development and maintenance. Budget: $65,500 (FY-94); total program $260,000 (FY-94 through FY-97).

4. **Flow Effects on Young-of-the-Year**: Estimate relative abundance of age-1 Colorado squawfish in spring, larval squawfish and sympatric species in summer, and sympatric species in fall. Identify relationships between relative abundance and environmental variables. Limited riverwide larval sampling in confirmed spawning areas. Additional sympatric species data collected during fall standardized monitoring. Budget: $10,000 (FY-94); program continues through FY-96.

5. **Humpback Chub in Westwater Canyon**: Assess effect of high spring flows on reproduction and recruitment of [[Humpback Chub]] in 13-mile reach (RM 116-124). Monitor spawning sites, nursery areas, and successive year classes. Integrate biological data from 1979-1981 (Valdez et al. 1982), 1986-1991 (Moretti and Chart 1991), and ISMP monitoring with hydrologic flow and temperature records. Seine low-velocity habitats regularly through summer and fall. PIT tag fish >150 mm TL for recapture studies. Budget: $40,000 (FY-94); total program $126,100 (FY-94 through FY-96).

6. **Habitat Quantification (Gunnison River)**: Quantify available habitats in Gunnison River (RM 2.5-55, Redlands Diversion Dam to Delta) in relation to test flows from Aspinall Unit. Aerial videography combined with ground-truthing at peak and base flows. Use PC-based map and image processing system (MIPS) and GIS. Detailed habitat mapping at study sites representing general conditions and sites important to Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker. Budget: $20,000 FWS + $20,000 Bureau of Reclamation (FY-94); total program $60,000 FWS + $50,000 BOR (FY-94 through FY-97).

7. **Channel Morphology Changes (Upper Colorado River)**: Evaluate changes in channel morphology using aerial photographs. Digitize channel features from 1937, 1954, 1968, 1986 photographs for Ruby-Horsethief Canyon reaches. Monitor cross-sections at four backwater study sites established in 1993. Measure flow, sediment transport, and substrate at upstream and downstream ends of side channels. Assess patterns of sediment transport and deposition in relation to specific discharges. University of Colorado collaboration. Budget: $24,000 (FY-94).

8. **Food Web Dynamics (Interdisciplinary Ecosystem Approach)**: Determine if longitudinal variation in adult squawfish abundance is associated with food availability. Characterize squawfish diet and diet of prey species (roundtail chub, bluehead sucker, flannelmouth sucker). Measure sediment organic content, periphyton biomass, macroinvertebrate density/biomass/diversity longitudinally. Characterize fluvial geomorphology past and present. Nine strata demarcated by gradient breaks over 210-mile reach (Green River confluence to Debeque). Sampling three times per year (pre-runoff, post-runoff, pre-winter) for two years (1994-1995). Collaboration among FWS, University of Colorado (geomorphology), and Ecosystems Research Institute (productivity studies). Budget: $144,125 (FY-94); total program $488,975 (FY-94 through FY-97).

**Hypotheses Tested**:
- H₁: Longitudinal distribution of adult squawfish is related to densities of potential prey items
- H₂: Longitudinal distribution of roundtail chub, bluehead sucker, and flannelmouth sucker is correlated with densities of their primary foodstuffs
- H₃: Primary and secondary productivity varies longitudinally and is correlated with light transmission, gradient, substrate particle size, and depth of substrate interstitial voids
- H₄: Geomorphically important habitats have changed significantly over time with trend of loss or simplification and sedimentation of coarse bed material

**Deliverables**: Annual progress reports (December 15 each year), draft reports (April-June), final reports (July-September), data files on habitat area, channel dimensions, streamflow, and biological collections. Final flow recommendations expected FY-97 for most studies.

**Sample Sizes & Methods**: Drift-net sampling conducted daily at dawn throughout July at multiple stations. Replicated sampling at each site. Electrofishing, trammel-netting, and open-system mark-recapture models for population estimates. PIT tagging of endangered fish for individual identification. Tissue samples collected for genetic analysis. Larval samples preserved in ethanol for otolith aging to determine spawning times.
