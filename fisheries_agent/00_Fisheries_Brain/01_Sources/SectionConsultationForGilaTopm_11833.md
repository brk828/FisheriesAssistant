## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 internal Section 7 consultation memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) addressing a proposal to designate 44 introduced populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] in Arizona as "nonessential experimental populations." Rather than providing a biological opinion on the proposal, the Regional Director defers the decision pending completion of updated recovery plans being developed by the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]. The memo outlines a proposed sub-basin recovery strategy and expresses concern that classifying key recovery populations as "nonessential" could undermine overall species recovery and downlisting efforts. No morphological, ecological, or population-abundance data are presented; the content is purely regulatory and administrative in nature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species inhabit isolated desert aquatic habitats within the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona, characterized by springs, streams, and other small, fragmented water bodies typical of arid Southwestern ecosystems.
- The recovery strategy discussed relies on a **sub-basin approach**: existing ("extant") populations would be replicated at additional sites within the same sub-basin, and stock from these replicated or parent populations would subsequently be used to establish further introduced populations elsewhere as needed for range-wide recovery.
- This reflects the fragmented, metapopulation-style ecology required for conservation of desert fishes, where isolated populations must be actively managed and translocated to maintain genetic and demographic viability.
- Management partners involved in habitat and population management include the [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

## Key Findings
- The proposal under review sought to designate 44 introduced Arizona populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] as **nonessential experimental populations** under the Endangered Species Act.
- The Regional Director **postponed a decision** on this designation, citing the ongoing revision of recovery plans by the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]].
- The recovery team proposed a **sub-basin recovery model**: (1) replicate extant populations elsewhere within the sub-basin, (2) use replicated or parent stocks to found additional populations as needed — a strategy the Service concurred with in concept.
- A key policy concern: populations designated as "nonessential experimental" **do not count toward formal recovery and downlisting**. The memo warns against designating critical recovery habitat as "experimental and nonessential," as this could **delay or jeopardize recovery** of both species.
- The Service expressed belief that **downlisting of the Gila topminnow could be achieved in the near term** if conservation efforts focus collectively on establishing and maintaining sufficient numbers of viable, self-sustaining populations.
- Recommended that the [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and FWS jointly **re-evaluate** the nonessential experimental population issue during the interim period before finalized recovery plans are issued.
- Considerable additional planning was identified as necessary to develop a matrix guiding future population introductions under the sub-basin strategy.
