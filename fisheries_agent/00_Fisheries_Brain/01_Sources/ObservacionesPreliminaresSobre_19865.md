# Preliminary Observations on the Ichthyofauna of the Río Matape, Sonora, México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1988 congress paper, presented at the IX Congreso Nacional de Zoología (Villahermosa, Tabasco, 1987), is a preliminary inventory of the freshwater fishes of the [[Río Matape]] basin in arid northwestern Sonora, México. Sampling was carried out in October 1986 and March 1987 at eight localities, yielding 1,584 specimens of four species. Three native taxa ([[Gila purpurea]], [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]] and the unisexual [[Poeciliopsis monacha-occidentalis]]) and one introduced species ([[Green Sunfish]]) were recorded. The authors stress the basin's ecological fragility, the risk posed by habitat modification and exotic species, and the lack of prior published data on the basin.

## Background & Description

The study belongs to a broader project at the Centro Ecológico de Sonora to assess native freshwater fish in a region where water resources are increasingly used in arid areas. Earlier work had covered the [[Río Yaqui]] (Hendrickson et al., 1980) and the [[Río Mayo]] (Vrijenhoek, 1978), but none had addressed the Matape. Hendrickson et al. had reported three native species there, with a possible fourth suspected.

The Matape basin (about 2,916 km²) lies between roughly 27°51′ and 29°14′ N and 109°15′ and 110°20′ W. Its channel is mostly intermittent. The Presa Ignacio Alatorre blocks flow to the southern reach before the outlet at Bahía de Guaymas. Water is pumped for agriculture and ranching, and pollution is mainly domestic, with no industrial sources detected.

Methods included seine nets (two 5 m seines, 12.8 mm and 6.4 mm mesh) and 3 mm mesh scoop nets. Dissolved oxygen and temperature were measured with a YSI Model 57 oximeter. Benthic fauna were sampled with a 9.5 cm diameter acrylic corer, and riparian vegetation was recorded by direct observation. Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin and stored in 50% isopropyl alcohol, then identified with keys by Minckley (1971, 1973), Eddy and Underhill, and Lee et al. (1980).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Biogeography:** The fauna is transitional, with affinities to northern (Colorado and Gila), southern (Mayo and Fuerte) and eastern (Chihuahuan Desert) drainages. The Sonoran Desert is typified by the association of [[Poeciliopsis]] with [[Agosia chrysogaster]].
- **Habitat:** Sites were mostly lotic, with slow, shallow flow. Only two lentic habitats were noted: the Presa Ignacio Alatorre and Represo La Haciendita.
- **Water quality indicators:** Chironomid larvae, Corixidae and Gerridae were recorded at several sites, pointing to localized disturbance. Riparian vegetation included [[Baccharis glutinosa]], [[Prosopis juliflora]], [[Typha latifolia]] and [[Eichhornia crassipes]].
- **Exotic species:** Green Sunfish, Largemouth Bass (*Micropterus salmoides*), Black Crappie (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*) and Black Bullhead (*Ictalurus melas*) were reported by residents at reservoir sites. Only *L. cyanellus* was collected.
- **Regional threats:** Meffe et al. (1983) documented declines of Sonoran topminnow in the Gila River, Arizona, linked to habitat change and exotic species.

## Key Findings

- **Sample totals:** 884 specimens were collected from four species across the eight localities. Localities were listed by coordinates and nearby settlements (e.g., Cobachi, La Galera, San José de Pimas, Mazatán).
- **Native species:** *Gila purpurea* (Yaqui chub) was found at four localities (3, 4, 7 and 8), totaling 97 specimens. It is a small fish of permanent, deep habitats near rocks, roots and springs. Its biomass was higher than other native fishes, although its numbers were lower.
- **Dominant genus:** *Poeciliopsis* made up most individuals but not biomass. *P. occidentalis* (477 specimens) occurred at all sites except site 3, and the unisexual *P. monacha-occidentalis* (403 specimens, found in 87% of samples) was also common.
- **Hybridogenesis:** The unisexual form is thought to arise from crosses between *P. monacha* and *P. occidentalis* in the Río Mayo. Its females reproduce by hybridogenesis, requiring males of *P. occidentalis*. It is distinguished from *P. occidentalis* only by mandibular tooth structure.
- **Exotic species:** Green Sunfish was found at only one site (locality 6, below Presa Ignacio Alatorre), where it dominated the habitat and fed on *Poeciliopsis* sp. Ten individuals were collected. Its presence was associated with reduced *Poeciliopsis* in shallow areas.
- **Conclusions:** The basin still maintains conditions favorable to native populations, but disturbance indicators and exotic species pose a potential threat to the three native species. The authors call for protected areas, control of introductions, socioeconomic assessment of introductions, and periodic monitoring.
