# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Strategic Plan (1997–2002)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This GPRA-mandated Strategic Plan for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] covers September 30, 1997 to September 30, 2002. It sets out the agency mission, four mission goals, twenty strategic goals, and associated performance measures. The plan is organized around conserving fish and wildlife populations, conserving a network of lands and waters, connecting Americans with wildlife, and building workforce excellence. It is signed by Director Jamie Rappaport Clark and Deputy Director John Rogers. The document is a policy and planning document, not a research study, so its content is goals, targets, assumptions and institutional history.

## Background & Description

**Mission:** To work with others to conserve, protect and enhance fish and wildlife and their habitats for the continuing benefit of the American people.

**History:** Origins trace to 1871, when Congress created the U.S. Fish Commission to address the decline of food fish. Related bureaus were consolidated in 1940 as the Fish and Wildlife Service, and the Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956 split it into commercial fisheries and sport fisheries/wildlife branches. The commercial fisheries function moved to Commerce in 1970 as today's [[National Marine Fisheries Service]]. The agency took its current name in 1974, and its research activities moved to the U.S. Geological Survey in 1993.

**Scale:** About 7,500 employees, a headquarters in Washington, D.C., seven regional offices, and nearly 700 field units. These include 511 [[National Wildlife Refuge System]] units and 65 [[National Fish Hatchery System]] facilities.

**Planning framework:** Developed under the [[Government Performance and Results Act]] (GPRA), with a stakeholder assessment of roughly 700 external representatives and 300 employees, plus more than 1,000 survey responses analyzed.

**Statement of Principles** includes: fish and wildlife biology as the foundation of the mission; scientifically responsible conduct; conservation as a collective job; and habitat conservation and restoration as fundamental.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Threats cited:** habitat degradation, pollution, non-native species, overexploitation, suburban growth, dams, river diversion and agricultural conversion. The plan states that accelerating losses "can no longer be attributed to natural processes."
- **Listed species:** As of July 1997, 1,090 native animals and plants were listed as endangered or threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]], with about 100 added each year.
- **Wetlands:** Roughly half of the continental U.S. wetlands have been lost since the late 1780s, with losses continuing at more than 1,000 acres per day. Wetlands provide habitat for at least one third of threatened and endangered species and for over 50 percent of migratory bird species.
- **Freshwater fishes:** The U.S. Recreational Fishery Resources Conservation Plan notes that 37 percent of freshwater fishes are at risk from declining aquatic habitat conditions.
- **Aquatic nuisance species:** Non-native organisms that invaded the Great Lakes and riverine habitats put native freshwater fishes at risk. The plan cites the [[Nonindigenous Aquatic Nuisance Prevention and Control Act]] of 1990.
- **Interjurisdictional fish concerns:** Salmonid stocks in the Pacific Northwest and New England, lake trout, perch and walleye in the Great Lakes, striped bass on all coastlines, and several migratory freshwater fishes.
- **Key assumptions:** continued public value for conservation, stable funding, no significant new contaminant or man-made disasters affecting critical habitat, and stable litigation levels over listings.

## Key Findings

**Mission Goal 1: Sustainability of Fish and Wildlife Populations**
- **1.1 Migratory birds:** Conserve populations through partnerships such as the [[North American Waterfowl Management Plan]] and Partners in Flight. Performance measures include a 13% increase in refuge migratory bird management, expansion of [[Adaptive Harvest Management]] to northern pintail and eastern and western mallard populations, and removal of 10% of species from the Migratory Nongame Birds of Management Concern list.
- **1.2 Interjurisdictional fish:** Conserve populations through approved management plans. Targets include 80% of approved hatchery production plans met within existing capacity.
- **1.3 Declining aquatic species:** Stabilize or increase 20% of identified declining species by 2002, using watershed-based strategies.
- **1.4 Alaskan marine mammals:** Co-management agreements for subsistence use of northern sea otter, Pacific walrus and polar bear, under the [[Marine Mammal Protection Act]]. The plan also addresses agreements with Russia on Alaska-Chukotka polar bear and Pacific walrus.
- **1.5 Candidate species:** Resolve the status of 183 candidate species and avoid 20 listings through conservation agreements.
- **1.6 Endangered and threatened species:** Stabilize or improve 40% of populations; recover and delist 20 species.
- **1.7 Illegal activities:** Reduce illegal activity as a factor in federally protected species declines by 5%, including a 2% increase in high-priority investigative hours and a 2% increase in port inspections.
- **1.8 Native American tribes:** Increase Service assistance by 50%, including a 10% rise in cooperative agreements and a 35% rise in technical assistance.

**Mission Goal 2: Habitat Conservation – A Network of Lands and Waters**
- **2.2 Upland habitats:** 64.7 million acres protected, restored or enhanced by 2002.
- **2.3 Wetlands and riparian habitats:** 28.4 million acres of wetlands and 3,250 miles of riparian habitat by 2002. The plan notes a target of 20.3 million acres protected on refuges and hatcheries.
- **2.4 Deep water and riverine habitats:** 15.5 million acres by 2002, including restoring streams for fish migration.
- **2.5 Consultative benefits:** Develop mechanisms to measure habitat benefits from technical assistance by 2000.
- **2.6 Habitat Conservation Plans:** 27 million acres under [[Habitat Conservation Planning]] under Section 10 of the ESA by 2002.

**Mission Goal 3: Americans and Wildlife**
- **3.1 Public understanding:** Establish a national baseline survey no later than April 1998, with at least three ecosystem demonstration areas per region by 2002.
- **3.2 Participation:** Increase opportunities for use of fish and wildlife resources by 3% from 1996 levels, and by 10% for recreational opportunities consistent with management plans.
- **3.3 Volunteers:** Increase volunteer hours by 5% from 1997 levels.

**Mission Goal 4: Workforce Excellence**
- **4.1 Workforce:** 40 hours of professional training per employee per year, a 25% increase from 1996 levels; a 10% reduction in the lost-time injury rate.
- **4.2 Business practices:** Adopt best management practices, reduce the facilities maintenance backlog, and ensure 99% of installations have ready access to information systems.
- **4.3 Federal Aid:** 100% of state grant monies used for legislated purposes, under the Federal Aid programs.

**Accountability:** Annual Performance Plans and Annual Performance Reports will link activities to strategic goals. The plan acknowledges that most current measures are activity- or output-driven, with outcome measures developed over time. It also proposes a cross-agency effort to develop joint natural resource performance measures.

**Note on sources:** The plan's appendices (legislative authorities, glossary, stakeholder process and a list of studies) provide supporting context, including the Endangered Species Act, Marine Mammal Protection Act, Migratory Bird Treaty Act and Executive Orders 12962 (Recreational Fisheries) and 12996 (National Wildlife Refuge System).
