# Hunting and Fishing Expedition Down the Colorado River from Yuma (December)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #river-ecology #waterfowl #historical-natural-history

## Executive Summary

This historical account describes a December hunting expedition down the [[Colorado River]] from [[Yuma]] and up the [[Hardy River]] in [[Lower California]], Mexico. The party traveled 109 miles down the Colorado and attempted 100 miles up the Hardy to reach volcanoes. While primarily a hunting narrative, the document provides ecological observations of fish abundance in these river systems, noting that "fish ducks are greatly in the majority, because these streams are alive with fish." The expedition documented diverse wildlife including waterfowl (ducks, geese), black-tail deer, wild hogs, and abundant quail populations in riparian habitats.

## Background & Description

The expedition party consisted of W.T. Cornwall, Dr. Cornwall of San Francisco, two additional members (Messrs. Coe and Pacheco), and a boat crew of two Mexicans and two Indians. They transported approximately 3,000 pounds of baggage and ammunition in a thirty-foot boat. The journey began at [[Yuma]] where passports were obtained, as the route carried them out of the United States ten miles below Yuma into Mexican territory.

The [[Colorado River]] at Yuma exhibited approximately six-mile current that decreased approaching the ocean. Tidal influences extended up the [[Hardy River]], with highest tides at full moon reportedly carrying boats rapidly upstream for at least thirty miles, though the party arrived late and achieved only twelve miles of tide-assisted upstream travel before rowing the remainder.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Aquatic Ecosystem**: The document emphasizes exceptional fish abundance in both the [[Colorado River]] and [[Hardy River]] systems, stating these streams were "alive with fish." This abundance supported large populations of fish ducks (likely mergansers or similar piscivorous waterfowl species) which were noted as "greatly in the majority" among waterfowl species.

**Waterfowl Diversity**: Duck populations were described as "mixed variety" along the Colorado River. [[Mallard]]s became "quite plentiful" on the Hardy River. Wild geese were present but maintained wariness, staying out of shotgun range.

**Riparian Habitat**: The Hardy River widened considerably, creating "flat expanse of country" with "fine cover for quail." This habitat structure supported extraordinarily dense quail populations.

**Mammalian Fauna**: The lower Colorado River mouth area contained wild hog populations (feral domestic hogs). Black-tail deer inhabited the river corridor, with nine individuals observed at one location. Native human populations lived in pole-and-brush structures rather than permanent housing.

## Key Findings

- **Fish Abundance**: Both river systems supported exceptionally high fish populations, sufficient to sustain dominant populations of piscivorous waterfowl
- **Waterfowl Populations**: Mixed duck species throughout, with mallards concentrated on Hardy River; fish ducks predominant due to abundant fish prey
- **Quail Density**: Extraordinary quail abundance in Hardy River riparian zones; hunters collected 50 birds in 1.5 hours without dogs, estimating equal numbers lost/not retrieved; birds rose in large coveys continuously
- **Tidal Influence**: Tidal effects extended significant distances up Hardy River, with full moon tides providing upstream transport for approximately 30 miles
- **River Characteristics**: Colorado River current approximately 6 miles per hour at Yuma, decreasing toward ocean
- **Wildlife Corridor**: River system supported diverse fauna including waterfowl, deer, feral hogs, and upland game birds in associated riparian habitats
- **Expedition Duration**: Two weeks spent in Lower California without encountering permanent structures or housing
