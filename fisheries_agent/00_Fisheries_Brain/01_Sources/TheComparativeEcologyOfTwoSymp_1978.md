# The Comparative Ecology of Two Sympatric Polychromatic Populations of *Xererpes fucorum*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Pholididae #intertidal-ecology #polychromatism

## Executive Summary

This 1978 field and laboratory study by Terrence J. Burgess examined the ecology of **[[Xererpes Fucorum]]** (a pholidid "gunnel"), focusing on two co-occurring color morphs — red and green — living sympatrically in the rocky intertidal zone near Piedras Blancas, California. Over 13 monthly transects, 517 individuals (422 red, 95 green) were collected and analyzed for seasonal abundance, size, vertical distribution, and substratum use. The study found that despite occupying the same general habitat, the red and green morphs displayed distinct seasonal peaks in abundance, differing mean body sizes, and differential vertical/emersion patterns. Fish overwhelmingly occupied algal substrata matching their own body coloration (95.6% of field records), a pattern supported by laboratory substratum-preference trials. Color-change experiments revealed asymmetric plasticity: red fish readily shifted toward green hues under experimental conditions, while green fish were comparatively color-stable. The authors interpret polychromatism in *Xererpes fucorum* as a mechanism of **intraspecific resource partitioning**, reducing competition between color morphs by promoting spatial, temporal, and substratum-based niche differentiation within the intertidal zone.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy & Distribution**: *Xererpes fucorum* Jordan & Gilbert is classified within [[Pholididae]] (gunnels), and is described as the most common gunnel in the central California intertidal zone (Burge & Schultz, 1973).
- **Range**: Punta Escarpada, Baja California to Vancouver Island, British Columbia (Miller & Lea, 1972); predominantly northern temperate shores, with some southern populations tied to nearshore upwelling zones.
- **Vertical range**: Intertidal zone down to 30 ft (9.1 m), though the species predominantly exploits the exposed intertidal zone — damp vegetation, moist rock crevices, and shallow tide pools.
- **Morphology & Growth**: Reaches a maximum size of 229 mm TL (Miller & Lea, 1972); estimated to live in excess of 4 years for the largest individuals (Burge & Schultz, 1973).
- **Feeding ecology**: Alimentary canal is straight and undifferentiated (Metz, 1912), consistent with a diet of benthic invertebrates.
- **Polychromatism**: Individuals occur as dark red or bright green morphs intertidally, with light brown/tan morphs found sublittorally in association with kelp. The origin of this coloration (genetic vs. dietary) was, at time of writing, unresolved. A related species, *[[Apodichthys Flavidus]]* (penpoint gunnel), derives pigment precursors largely from epiphytic invertebrate prey (Wilkie, 1966; Crozier & Wilkie, 1966), suggesting a similar dietary mechanism may apply to *Xererpes*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site**: Rocky intertidal zone 3.5 km south of Piedras Blancas Light Station, San Luis Obispo County, California (35.40°N, 121.17°W); three permanent transects sampled monthly from October 1974–October 1975.
- **Substratum association**: Xererpes occupied 23 algal species plus bare rock across 580 recorded field substrata. Three species — *Gigartina papillata*, *Pelvetia fastigiata*, and *Rhodoglossum affine* — accounted for 73.4% of all substrata occupied.
- **Color-matching behavior**: Fish were found on substrata matching their body color in 95.6% of field observations, supporting a strong cryptic camouflage strategy tied to algal "red group" vs. "green group" substrata (per Smith, 1969 classification).
- **Seasonal algal dynamics**: "Red group" algae dominant (>50% frequency) in spring, summer, fall; "green group" algae dominant (>40%) only in winter — inversely correlated frequency patterns that map onto shifts in fish color-morph substratum occupancy.
- **Tidal behavior**: Skin-diving at high tide found very few Xererpes relative to expected low-tide densities, and none on algal substrata — indicating fish relocate to under-rock refugia when immersed. Mark-recapture data showed 56.3% of marked fish returned to their original low-tide habitat upon re-emersion, indicating limited high-tide movement and strong habitat fidelity.
- **Comparative context**: Similar downshore seasonal migrations documented in related pholidids *[[Pholis Gunnellus]]* (Sawyer, 1967) and *[[Blennius Pholis]]* (Gibson, 1967), and analogous resource-partitioning via size/habitat divergence described for sympatric *[[Hypsoblennius]]* species (Stephens et al., 1970) and *[[Xiphister]]* species (Wingert, 1974).

## Key Findings

### Seasonal Abundance
- Total collected: 517 fish (422 red = 81.6%; 95 green = 18.4%).
- Species-wide abundance peaked in summer (June–August, N=274) and was lowest in winter (December–February, N=17).
- Red population peaked in July; green population peaked in August.
- Red fish outnumbered green in all months except December (equal) and January (green slightly higher: N=5 vs N=4).
- Greatest disparity: June–August, red comprised 87.2% (N=239) of all fish collected.

### Length Frequency
- Mean standard length (MSL): Red = 52.7 mm; Green = 57.8 mm (overall study period).
- Red fish: max MSL 82.0 mm (January), min MSL 29.5 mm (February).
- Green fish: max MSL 77.6 mm (October), min MSL 25.5 mm (February).
- February collections consisted entirely of post-larval/early juvenile fish (species minimum 24.0 mm SL).
- Greatest size disparity between morphs occurred in June (green: 73.5 mm vs red: 41.4 mm); sizes converged only in November.
- No significant correlation (P>0.05) found between fish length and vertical intertidal distribution.

### Intertidal Vertical Distribution & Emersion
- Species mean vertical height highest in summer (+0.42 m) and lowest in spring (+0.06 m).
- Mean emersion time ranged from summer high (4.4 h) to winter low (2.6 h).
- Green fish consistently occupied higher vertical positions and longer emersion times than red fish in all seasons except spring.
- Largest morph divergence: summer (green +0.70 m/5.8 h vs red +0.38 m/4.2 h) and fall (green +0.45 m/4.4 h vs red +0.19 m/2.2 h).

### Field Substratum Use
- 580 substrata recorded: 23 algal species (N=550) + bare rock (N=30).
- Dominant substrata: *Gigartina papillata* (55.0%), *Pelvetia fastigiata* (10.8%), *Rhodoglossum affine* (7.6%), bare rock (5.2%).
- *Gigartina papillata* dominant in all months except November; comprised 66.4% of summer substrata.
- Color-matching substratum selection occurred in 95.6% of field samples (N=554/580).
- In mixed-color multiple-fish stations (N=28 stations, 86 fish), 73.3% of fish were found on matching-colored substrata.

### High Tide Movement Study
- High-tide skin-diving (4 h) found far fewer fish than expected from low-tide density (0.22 fish/m² in August); no fish were found on algal substrata while immersed — indicating a habitat shift to under-rock refugia during high tide.
- Mark-recapture: 32 fish tagged (23 green, 9 red); 56.3% (N=18) recovered at original location, plus 26 new (untagged) fish captured — supporting strong habitat fidelity/limited high-tide dispersal.

### Laboratory Substratum Preference Experiments (66 fish: 38 red, 28 green)
- No significant color preference between red vs. green algae when both were "matching" candidates (ns).
- Bare rock was significantly preferred over open water/no-substratum controls (*P<0.05*) for both morphs.
- Algal substrata (regardless of color) were significantly preferred over bare rock.
- When given a choice between matching- and non-matching-colored gravel, both morphs significantly preferred substratum matching their own body color.
- Under emersed conditions (Series 3), both morphs showed significant long-term preference for color-matching algal substrata.

### Laboratory Color Change Experiments (16 fish: 8 red, 8 green; 14-day trials)
- Red fish: all 16 individuals exhibited gradual color change (2–12 days, mean 6.2 days), shifting toward green hues characteristic of field-collected green specimens.
- Green fish: majority (N=10) showed no significant color change under identical conditions.
- Mean number of hue changes: Red = 3.4 (range 1–6); Green = 0.4 (range 0–1).
- Suggests asymmetric color plasticity, with red morph coloration potentially more diet/environment-dependent, and green coloration comparatively fixed.

### Ecological Interpretation
- Seasonal shifts in abundance, size, vertical distribution, and substratum use between red and green morphs are interpreted as a form of **intraspecific resource/niche partitioning**, reducing competition during periods of peak species abundance (summer).
- Preferential occupation of color-matching algal substrata (linking to differential vertical zonation of *Gigartina papillata*/*Rhodoglossum affine* vs. higher-zone *Pelvetia fastigiata*) provides spatial partitioning between morphs.
- Increased frequency of multiple-fish stations in less favorable months (fall/winter) suggests heightened competition for limited preferred habitat during those periods.
- Broader ecological parallels drawn to habitat-driven seasonal shifts in *[[Xiphister Atropurpureus]]* (Marliave, 1975) and gobies *Pomatoschistus minutus* / *P. microps* (Swedmark, in Gibson 1969).
- Polychromatism is hypothesized to also preserve dietary/predation-based selective pressures (cf. Kerfoot, 1975 on planktonic invertebrates), maximizing overall resource utilization within the intertidal zone.
