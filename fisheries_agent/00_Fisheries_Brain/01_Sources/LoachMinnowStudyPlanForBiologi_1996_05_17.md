## Executive Summary

This document is a 1996 **Loach Minnow Study Plan** prepared jointly by the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] Wildlife and Outdoor Recreation Division, the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]], [[Alchesay/Williams Creek National Fish Hatcheries]], and the [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]]. It establishes protocols for the collection, transport, holding, and captive propagation of the federally threatened **[[Loach Minnow]]** (cited in text as *Rhinichthys cobitis*, though contemporaneous literature and the paper's own citations use *Tiaroga cobitis*) from the **East Fork White River** population on the Fort Apache Indian Reservation. The plan was drafted pursuant to a draft biological opinion (2-23-96-F-01) concerning repair and maintenance of irrigation diversions, and is intended to support genetic conservation, captive propagation research, and eventual reintroduction efforts. The plan explicitly frames the East Fork population as genetically distinctive and ecologically precarious, occupying only ~14 miles of stream under conditions of low flow, sedimentation, and non-native species pressure.

## Background & Description

- Small-bodied, benthic (bottom-dwelling) cyprinid.
- No morphometric data (length/weight) are provided in this document; field protocols instead specify handling constraints: fish are weighed on wet top-loading electronic scales and measured on wet measuring boards (Pesola clip-on scales prohibited), with total handling time restricted to **≤30 seconds** to minimize stress/injury.
- Health assessment of five field-collected specimens (May 10, 1996) found **all five to be gravid females in good condition**, indicating active reproductive condition at time of sampling.
- Species is associated with a **relatively short life span**, noted as a contributing factor to expected mortality in captive holding programs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat preference**: Shallow, swift-flowing riffle habitats over gravel, cobble, and rubble substrates; uses interstitial spaces between and in the lee of larger substrate particles for resting and spawning ([[Propst & Bestgen 1991]], [[Rinne 1989]]).
- **Elevation/stream type**: Occurs below 2200 m elevation in intermediate-sized rivers with moderate gradients.
- **Substrate sensitivity**: Rare or absent where fine sediments fill interstitial spaces — indicating high sensitivity to sedimentation and habitat degradation.
- **Historic range**: Verde, Salt, San Pedro, San Francisco, and Gila river basins ([[Minckley 1973]], [[Sublette et al. 1990]]).
- **Current range**: Reduced by an estimated **85%** ([[USFWS 1986]]); persists in fragmented segments of the upper Gila, San Francisco, Blue, Tularosa, and White rivers, and Aravaipa, Eagle, Campbell Blue, and Dry Blue creeks (Arizona/New Mexico).
- **Threats**: Competition and predation by non-native fishes, habitat disturbance/destruction, water diversions, increased sedimentation relative to historic baseline, and drought-driven low flows/fire risk (noted specifically for 1996 conditions in the White Mountains).
- **Population status (East Fork White River)**: Isolated, small, and highly fragmented; occupies ~14 miles of stream with scattered suitable habitat; presumed to be reproducing/recruiting, but long-term stability unknown; no conclusive data on population trends.
- **Parasitology / Fish Health** (from May 1996 sampling of 5 individuals):
  - External protozoan parasite: **[[Apiosoma]]** (low numbers, skin and gills)
  - External monogenetic trematode: **[[Gyrodactylus]] cf. *G. stunkardii*** (light infection, skin)
  - Internal nematode: **[[Rhabdochona]] cf. *R. canadensis*** (very low numbers, intestinal tract)
  - *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* ("Ich") not detected in this sample, but previously documented in loach minnow from Aravaipa Creek and in other cyprinids from this stream.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory status**: Loach minnow listed as federally threatened on October 28, 1986; critical habitat designated March 8, 1994 (San Francisco, Tularosa, Blue, upper Gila rivers, Aravaipa Creek).
- **Study goals**: (1) avoid immediate extinction of East Fork population; (2) maintain/develop self-sustaining East Fork-lineage populations on the reservation; (3) preserve adaptive capacity; (4) maintain capacity for continued speciation.
- **Collection protocols**: Nonlethal collection only; 1/8" Delta mesh seines (no creosote or knotted line); electrofishing restricted to trained/certified operators with amperage typically ≤1.0 amp, calibrated to water conductivity/pH/temperature; no tagging, fin-clipping, or marking permitted.
- **Streamside holding protocols**: Insulated ice chests (40–48 qt), biomass capped at 10 g/L; water treated with 0.5% NaCl and 10 mg/L Furacin; dissolved oxygen maintained >6.0 mg/L; temperature matched to host water; wet transfer-only release into riffles >9 cm deep.
- **Transport/propagation protocols**: Fish received into aluminum troughs at Alchesay NFH; influent water checked for supersaturation (<102%); water chemistry matched to transport protocol (55 ppm Nitrofurazone, 50 ppt NaCl); gradual 2-hour tempering exchange; troughs covered with 1/4" mesh.
- **Propagation knowledge gaps**: Very limited literature exists on controlled propagation; anecdotal success hatching wild-collected ova-laden rocks in aquaria (P. Turner, NMSU, pers. comm.). Hatchery techniques to be adapted from percid (*Etheostoma*, *Percina*) and other cyprinid culture methods.
- **Facility redundancy**: Captive system supported by backup generator (5,000W propane), automatic transfer switch, and redundant water/air pumps with audible/telecommunication alarms — reflecting high value placed on this genetically distinct population.
- **Mortality/disposal protocol**: All deaths reported to Pinetop Fish Health Center; tissue samples preserved in ultra-cold storage following pathological exam; unreported deaths bagged individually and frozen pending review.
- **Health survey outcome**: All five sampled East Fork specimens (May 1996) were gravid females in good overall condition with only low-level parasite loads (Apiosoma, Gyrodactylus, Rhabdochona), suggesting a currently healthy, actively reproducing population despite habitat stressors.
- **Data ownership**: All data from collection, transport, maintenance, and propagation activities are the property of the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]]; dissemination is at Tribal discretion outside of required ESA permit reporting.
