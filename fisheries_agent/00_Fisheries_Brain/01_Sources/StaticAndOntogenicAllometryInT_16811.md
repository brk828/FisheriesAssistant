# Static and Ontogenetic Allometry in the Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 study proposal by Christopher R. Reimus sets out a morphometric plan to test whether shape changes in the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) are simply the consequence of changes in size. The work uses [[Static Allometry]] (differences among same-stage individuals of different ages and sizes) and [[Ontogenetic Allometry]] (covariation during growth), analyzed with [[Landmark-Based Morphometrics]] and [[Relative Warp Analysis]]. The document contains methods and sampling design only. No measured results are reported.

## Background & Description

**Allometry concepts.** The author defines allometry as "differences in proportions correlated with changes in absolute magnitude" (Gould 1966). Three types are recognized: static, ontogenetic, and [[Evolutionary Allometry]]. The study treats allometric generalizations as null hypotheses for detecting deviation from size-driven shape change (Sweet 1980).

**Species profile.** The razorback sucker belongs to a monotypic genus restricted to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Key traits noted in the document:

- **Longevity:** Lake Mohave fish aged 23 to 44 years (1980-1983 collections). The maximum age is estimated at about 50 years.
- **Size:** Large specimens reach about 0.75 m.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Males are smaller, have relatively longer anal and pelvic fins, and show breeding tubercles and orange venters. Females are lighter, white ventrally, and lack tuberculation.
- **Decline:** Mainly attributed to predation and competition by [[Non-Native Fishes]]. Other suspected factors include [[Hybridization]] with catostomids, parasite transmission, and range fragmentation. Hybrids were only 0.3% of Lake Mohave fish by May 1990.
- **Recruitment:** Substantial natural recruitment has not been recorded in lower basin reservoirs since at least 1963, and young fish are very rare.

**Why this species.** Fishes are poorly quantified for allometry at large size and great age, partly because large specimens are costly to house and many species are protected. The razorback sucker is considered ideal because large, long-lived specimens exist in the [[Arizona State University]] museum collection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study populations.**

- **[[Lake Mohave]]:** The source of all study fish and the most studied razorback population. It supplies large, very old fish.
- **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]:** Holds fish descended from Mohave broodstock. Fish are reared under relatively controlled conditions and have known ages, making them the basis for ontogenetic analysis.
- **[[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] (BANWR):** An introduced population of Mohave-stock hatchery fish. Fish are small for their age because of high density in a small habitat, not because of a shorter growing season.

Locality effects are assumed to be minimal because all groups are outdoor populations under similar temperature and climate regimes.

**Contrast design.** Mohave fish are about the same size as large Dexter fish but 15 to 35 years older. BANWR fish are much older than small Dexter fish of the same size. Shape differences between these groups are therefore attributed to age.

## Key Findings

**Study design and sampling**

- **Ontogenetic sample:** Dexter fish 50 to 500 mm, under 15 years old, placed in nine 50 mm size classes (50-99 mm through 450-500 mm). Each class is to include three males and three females, to allow testing of [[Sexual Dimorphism]] effects.
- **Table 1 (Dexter fish):** Lists prepared and planned specimens by size class and sex. Many 150-500 mm fish are assigned identification numbers. Fish under 150 mm were readily available but not yet prepared. Fish of 200-350 mm were described as rare in the collection.
- **Table 2 (static sample):** Lists nine Mohave skeletons (standard lengths roughly 380-760 mm, collected 1992; the OCR for the 760 mm entry is uncertain) and about 20 BANWR skeletons (standard lengths roughly 184-273 mm, mostly male or female with year of collection). The document notes that many other large Mohave fish are available.

**Specimen preparation**

- Skeletons of fish over 100 mm standard length are prepared by thawing, removing [[Otoliths]], gutting, sexing by gonads, filleting, and cleaning by [[Dermestid Beetles]].
- Fish under 100 mm are to be cleared and stained using the enzyme method of Taylor (1967).

**Morphometric method**

- **Landmarks:** 31 two-dimensional landmarks on two skeletal regions, the [[Neurocranium]] and the [[Neural Complex]] (Figures 1-3). These regions were chosen for their shape information, ease of landmark placement, articulation, and apparent size-related variation.
- **Landmark rules:** Following Bookstein (1991), landmarks are placed at (1) discrete tissue juxtapositions, (2) extrema of curvature, and (3) extrema of single coordinates.
- **Digitizing:** Video-based acquisition from digital images.
- **Analysis:** [[Thin-Plate Spline]] transformations and relative warps (principal components of non-uniform shape variation), following Bookstein (1989) and Rohlf and Marcus (1993). Relative warps are then used in conventional multivariate analyses.
- **Reference organisms:**
  - For ontogenetic allometry, the mean landmarks of the adjacent smaller size class (e.g., 50-99 mm fish are the reference for 100-149 mm fish).
  - For static allometry, the mean of the same size class but a different age.

**Central hypothesis**

The null hypothesis is that shape changes in the razorback sucker are only the consequence of size change. Rejecting it would indicate age- or development-specific shape change.

**Limitations noted by the author**

- Cross-sectional samples are assumed to represent a single individual's growth trajectory, which is a known assumption of this method.
- Small fish from BANWR and Dexter fish are not yet fully prepared, so the full sample was not complete at the time of writing.
