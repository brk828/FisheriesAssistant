# Report of the Expert Consultation on the Proposed Sub-Committee on Aquaculture of the Committee on Fisheries

#aquaculture #fisheries-governance #food-security #policy-report

## Executive Summary

This FAO Fisheries Report (No. 623, 2000) records an Expert Consultation held in the FAO Regional Office for Asia and the Pacific, Bangkok, Thailand, on 28-29 February 2000. Thirty participants (the abstract cites thirty-three from 14 countries, two regional intergovernmental organizations and two international NGOs) assessed whether a dedicated [[Sub-Committee on Aquaculture]] should be created under the [[Committee on Fisheries]] (COFI). The consultation concluded that it should, and drafted terms of reference, rules of procedure, a future programme of action and budget estimates. The meeting was held alongside the [[Conference on Aquaculture in the Third Millennium]] to reduce cost. The document is a governance and policy report, not a biological study; it contains no species-level data.

## Background & Description

**Definition.** FAO defines aquaculture as the farming of aquatic organisms (fish, molluscs, crustaceans and aquatic plants), where farming implies intervention in rearing (such as regular stocking, feeding and predator protection) and individual or corporate ownership of the stock. Under the [[Code of Conduct for Responsible Fisheries]] (FAO, 1995), aquaculture includes culture-based fisheries.

**Scale.** Figures cited for 1998:
- Global aquaculture production: 39.4 million metric tonnes.
- Total value: USD 52.5 billion.
- Share of total global fishery production: 31.?% (OCR-garbled; reported as roughly one-third).
- Developing countries produce about 90% of global aquaculture output (figure as OCR-read).
- Low-income food-deficit countries account for over 80% of global aquaculture production.

**Mandate context.** The proposal originated with China at the 22nd COFI Session (Rome, 17-20 March 1997). The 23rd Session (February 1999) agreed that sustainable aquaculture could have high potential for food availability and poverty alleviation, but extra-budgetary funding had not been identified.

**Related instruments.** The [[Bangkok Declaration and Strategy]] (Appendix F), adopted at the Bangkok conference (20-25 February 2000), followed the 1976 [[Kyoto Declaration on Aquaculture]]. The [[FAO Statutory Bodies Review]] (Conference Resolution 13/97) set criteria for creating new subsidiary bodies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report treats environmental sustainability as one of six priority areas, not as a field study. It names:
- Criteria and indicators of sustainable aquaculture, site selection, environmental impact assessment and monitoring.
- Interactions between aquaculture and fisheries and resource protection.
- Movement of aquatic organisms and products, and the associated disease and pathogen risks.
- Genetic resource management and new biotechnologies, including GMOs, to be handled precautionarily.
- Integration of farms into [[Integrated Coastal Area Management]] and inland watershed management, within carrying capacity.

The Bangkok Declaration notes that some poorly planned operations have harmed ecosystems and communities. It also highlights [[Culture-Based Fisheries]] and enhancements as low-input options that use under-utilised, new or degraded water resources.

## Key Findings

**Governance conclusions**
- The consultation confirmed a need for a global intergovernmental forum on aquaculture. It found no existing global body with sustainable aquaculture as its primary and exclusive focus.
- A Sub-Committee was judged consistent with Conference Resolution 13/97, which requires centrality to the FAO mandate, clear, time-limited tasks, comparative advantage and member support.
- The consultation recommended that the report go to the 24th COFI Session.

**Proposed Terms of Reference**
- Two principal functions: advise COFI on technical and policy matters, and advise COFI and FAO on the Organization's work in aquaculture.
- Specific tasks include identifying major issues and trends, promoting international collaboration to implement the Code of Conduct, liaising with other bodies, and requesting technical reviews on food safety, biotechnology, environment and labelling.
- Meetings should be cost-effective and held biennially.
- Rules of procedure would mirror those of COFI, with participation by non-governmental and regional networks.

**Priority issues.** Selection criteria require a transboundary component, potential for international agreement, or use of FAO's comparative advantage. Six key areas were prioritised:
1. Role of aquaculture in food security, economic development and poverty alleviation.
2. Consumer issues (food safety, quality and certification).
3. Human resource development, research and extension.
4. Environmental aspects of aquaculture development.
5. Institutional capacity building and policy development.
6. Statistics, data and information management.

**Future programme.** Three initial activities were proposed for the first Sub-Committee meeting:
- Improving data collection and analysis to quantify aquaculture's unfulfilled potential for food security and poverty alleviation.
- Supporting implementation of the Code of Conduct through best management practices.
- Strengthening institutional capacity through networking and regional collaboration, particularly in Africa and Latin America and the Caribbean.

**Budget.** The Secretariat estimated about six person-months per year of professional staff time, plus an equal amount of general service time. Interpretation for four days (seven sessions) in five languages was estimated at USD 38,000 (USD 28,800 for four languages). Translation was estimated at about USD 500 per 1,000 words, or USD 12,500 for roughly four major documents totalling 25,000 words. Early submission could cut translation costs by up to 50%. The People's Republic of China offered to host the first meeting.

**Bangkok Declaration highlights.** The conference drew about 540 participants from 66 countries. Key strategy elements included education and training, research and development, information flow, food security and poverty alleviation, environmental sustainability, rural integration, investment, institutional strengthening, innovation (including [[Recirculating Aquaculture Systems]]), culture-based fisheries, aquatic animal health, nutrition, genetics, biotechnology, food quality and safety, market development, and regional co-operation.
