## Executive Summary
This 1971 paper by Mildred Stratton Wilson describes a new species of harpacticoid copepod, **[[Mesochra mexicana]]**, in the family [[Canthocamptidae]], collected from a brackish pond on Harbor Island, Aransas Bay, Texas, Gulf of Mexico. The description is based on three ovigerous females (holotype + two paratypes); the male remains unknown. The paper also examines a related, possibly conspecific or sister taxon from a Mediterranean salt-mine pool near Alcudia, Mallorca (Balearic Islands), originally noted by Margalef (1953) as "*Mesochra* sp." or "*cf. heldti* Monard." Wilson compares diagnostic morphological characters between the two disjunct populations and discusses their phylogenetic placement within the cosmopolitan genus **[[Mesochra]]**, a genus of ecological interest because it belongs to a predominantly freshwater family ([[Canthocamptidae]]) yet shows marked euryhalinity. The paper also resolves nomenclatural issues involving several *Mesochra* taxa and their potential homonymy.

## Background & Description
**General Body Plan (Female, *M. mexicana*):**
- Total body length (dorsal midline, rostrum base to caudal ramus apex): **0.45–0.495 mm**
- Body robust, gradually tapering posteriorly
- Cuticle dark brown and thick, obscuring cuticular discs
- Genital segment with transverse lateral sclerotizations at midpoint
- Ovisac containing 14–15 large ova

**Urosome Ornamentation:**
- Distal membranes of urosomal segments finely serrate, lateral edges with stout spinules
- Ventral rows of spinulose hairs on segments 2 and 3
- Anal operculum with 10 stout spinules (vs. **5 spinules** in the Mediterranean form)

**Caudal Rami:**
- Slightly longer than broad, slightly shorter than outer margin of last body segment
- Dorsal seta accompanied by spinules; stout spinules along outer/inner margins
- Caudal setae relative lengths (outer:middle:inner) = 3.2 : 7.4 : 1.0
- Outer and middle setae swollen, jointed at base, spinulose (enlarged setae — a diagnostic trait shared with *M. armoricana*; **absent/not enlarged** in the Mediterranean form)

**Antennule:** 6-segmented; third segment (fused ancestral segments 3+4) longest; aesthete on third segment extends ~1/3 its length beyond antennular apex. Approximate setal count per segment: 1(1), 2(10–11), 3(7–8), 4(1), 5(2–3), 6(7–8).

**Antenna:** Allobasis with 2 setae (proximal spinelike, distal longer & plumose); unsegmented exopod (palp) with 3 setae.

**Mouthparts:**
- Mandible: blade with apical teeth + lateral seta; 2-segmented palp (segment 2 with 4 setae)
- Maxillule: gnathal lobe with 6 modified apical teeth; first lacinia simple with 2 unequal setae; second lacinia elongate, 2-segmented
- Maxilla: 3 endites, third clawlike with 3 lateral setae
- Maxilliped: well-developed, basal segment and claw base each with a seta
- Sternal plate present between maxilla and maxilliped bases

**Swimming Legs (P1–P4):**
- Leg 1: exopod segment 2 with inner seta; endopod 3-segmented; endopod segment 1 short/broadened, reduced in length relative to exopod segments 1+2 (~1:1.4) and endopod segments 2+3 (~1:1.6) — a key diagnostic character linking *M. mexicana* to the Mediterranean form
- Legs 2–4: exopod segment 3 with 3 outer spines; endopods 2-segmented, decreasing in length from leg 2→4
- Endopod segment 2 of legs 2–4 bears totals of 5, 6, 5 setae respectively (2, 3, 2 on inner margin) — notably, **3 inner-margin setae on leg 3 endopod 2** is a rare character shared with *M. armoricana*
- Leg 5: exopod with 5 setae (length:width ~1.5:1); basal expansion with 6 stout setae, slightly exceeding exopod

**Sexual Dimorphism:** Male unknown for *M. mexicana*; only females described (all specimens ovigerous).

**Mediterranean Form (*Mesochra* sp./*M. mexicana* variant, ex Margalef 1953):**
- Female length: 0.425–0.45 mm, with 9 eggs in sac (vs. 14–15 ova in *M. mexicana*)
- Anal operculum with 5 very stout spinules (vs. 10 in *M. mexicana*)
- Caudal setae not enlarged (unlike swollen setae of *M. mexicana*)
- Leg 1 endopod segment 1 short, similar proportions to *M. mexicana*; inner seta present on endopod segment 1
- Endopod setation of legs 2–4 undetermined (specimen undissected) — key data gap preventing definitive species-level distinction

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Mesochra mexicana]]** is classified as a **brackish-water species** with probable variable **euryhalinity**.
- Type locality: a pond on Harbor Island, Aransas Bay, Texas — co-occurring with **[[Nitocra lacustris]]** (ranges brackish to freshwater) and **[[Halicyclops fosteri]]** (known only from brackish water).
- All three co-occurring species exhibited dark brown coloration and thickened cuticle, suggested to reflect environmental stress/conditions of the habitat.
- The Mediterranean form was collected from a coastal salt-mine pool near Alcudia, Mallorca, Balearic Islands — a low-salinity brackish coastal habitat also hosting **[[Mesochra lilljeborgi]]**.
- Broader genus-level context: **[[Mesochra]]** contains species spanning marine, brackish, and freshwater habitats, contrasting with the largely freshwater-dominated family **[[Canthocamptidae]]**, making it ecologically and phylogenetically significant for studying transitions in salinity tolerance among harpacticoid copepods.

## Key Findings
- Description of a new species, **[[Mesochra mexicana]]**, from the Gulf of Mexico (Texas), based on ovigerous females (holotype USNM 127666; paratypes USNM 127667).
- The species is placed in **Group I of Lang (1948)** based on leg segmentation characters (P2–P4 exopod segment 3 with 3 spines; P1 endopod 3-segmented).
- A previously unnamed Mediterranean specimen (Margalef 1953, Mallorca) is reassessed and found to share the rare diagnostic trait of a short, broad first endopod segment on leg 1 with *M. mexicana*, suggesting either conspecificity/variant status or a geographically vicariant species-pair.
- *M. mexicana* shows closest morphological affinity to **[[Mesochra armoricana]]** (Monard, 1935), sharing: form of leg 5, enlarged caudal setae, ventral urosome spination pattern, and three inner-margin setae on endopod segment 2 of leg 3 (an uncommon trait in the genus).
- Definitive resolution of the Mediterranean form's taxonomic status is **pending** further data on endopod setation of legs 2–4 and discovery/description of males for both forms.
- Nomenclatural issues flagged for future revision:
  - *M. pygmaea* var. *meridionalis* Steuer, 1943 would become a **primary homonym** of *M. meridionalis* Sars, 1905 if elevated in rank.
  - *M. parva* Thomson, 1946 is a **secondary homonym** of *M. parva* (T. & A. Scott, 1896); likely conspecific with *M. meridionalis* Sars, requiring careful review before a replacement name is proposed.
- Addendum notes an additional species omitted from Bodin's (1967) catalog: *M. quadrispinosa* Shen and Tai, 1965 (China).
