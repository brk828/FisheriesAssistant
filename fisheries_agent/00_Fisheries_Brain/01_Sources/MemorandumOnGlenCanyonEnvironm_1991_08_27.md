## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative memorandum from the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program, dated August 27, 1991, and authored by Dave Wegner. It is **not a primary scientific research report** but rather a coordination notice calling a meeting of Principal Investigators conducting field research on the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Little Colorado River]]. The memo does not contain morphological, ecological, or quantitative data on the species itself; instead it documents the administrative and logistical framework surrounding multi-institutional endangered species research efforts tied to the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] environmental impact studies. Its scientific value lies in identifying the research entities, personnel, and coordination issues (sampling overlap, data gaps, resource impact minimization) affecting Humpback Chub studies at the time.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data on the [[Humpback Chub]] or any other species are presented in this source. The document is purely administrative in nature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat referenced:** [[Little Colorado River]], a major tributary of the [[Colorado River]] within [[Glen Canyon]], Arizona — recognized as a critical spawning and rearing habitat for the endangered Humpback Chub.
- **Conservation status invoked:** The Humpback Chub is referred to as an "endangered" species, consistent with its federal listing status, underscoring the regulatory motivation for the coordinated research program under GCES.
- **Research context:** Multiple concurrent field research programs were operating in the same river reach, raising concerns about cumulative disturbance/impact to aquatic resources (implying sensitivity of Humpback Chub habitat to repeated sampling and handling stress).
- No specific data on diet, reproduction, water chemistry, or co-occurring taxa are provided.

## Key Findings

- The memo identifies **three primary research entities** conducting studies on Humpback Chub in the Little Colorado River, coordinated under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program (a Bureau of Reclamation initiative, Upper Colorado Region).
- Key personnel/institutions associated with the research effort include:
  - Dr. [[Duncan Patten]] — Center for Environmental Studies, Arizona State University
  - Dr. [[Wendell Minckley]] — Arizona State University (noted authority on southwestern freshwater fishes)
  - Dr. [[Paul Marsh]] — Center for Environmental Studies, Arizona State University
  - Dr. [[Stuart Leon]] — U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, Pinetop Office, AZ
  - Mr. [[James deVos]] — Chief of Research, Arizona Game & Fish Department
- A meeting was scheduled for **September 3, 1991**, at the Arizona Game & Fish Department to address three identified areas of concern:
  1. **Overlap of sampling efforts** among concurrent research programs.
  2. **Missing or gap data** in the collective Humpback Chub research dataset.
  3. **Minimizing cumulative impact** of research activities on aquatic resources (i.e., handling/sampling stress on the endangered population).
- The meeting's intended outcomes included identifying coordination gaps, reducing redundant sampling, and potentially modifying existing **cooperative agreements** between agencies/institutions to streamline the research approach.
- This memo illustrates the broader institutional and regulatory framework (post-[[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations review) driving endangered fish research in the Colorado River ecosystem, situating the Humpback Chub studies within the larger [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] initiative examining dam-related impacts on native fish populations.
