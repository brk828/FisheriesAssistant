# New Data on Asellids (Crustacea, Isopoda) of Portugal

#crustacea #isopoda #asellidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 paper by [[M Helena Galhano]] and [[Jorge Eiras]], published by the [[Instituto De Zoologia Dr Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), describes two new subspecies of the freshwater isopod *Proasellus ibericus* from northern Portugal. Material was collected at Moncão (June 1972) and Pacos de Ferreira (January 1970). The authors judged the morphological differences from the nominal form to be small but consistent, and named the two populations *Proasellus ibericus codreanui* ssp. n. and *Proasellus ibericus ferreirensis* ssp. n. The paper also reproduces the Institute's numbered publication list, which runs to roughly 120 entries from 1940 to 1973, many of them on Asellids and other Portuguese invertebrates.

## Background & Description

The Asellidae are small freshwater isopods. Within this group, the authors follow [[Henry And Magniez 1970]] in placing the taxon in the genus *Proasellus*, a name first proposed by Dudich (1925). The nominal form, described by J. M. Braga (1946) as *Asellus coxalis ibericus*, was later raised to species rank as *Proasellus ibericus*. Henry and Magniez also recorded it from caves in France. The authors stress that the new material remains morphologically close to the type, which is why they propose subspecies rather than full species.

**Proasellus ibericus codreanui ssp. n.** (named after [[Radu Codreanu]], who joined the 1972 collecting trip)
- Holotype: male, 4.5 mm (antennae and uropods excluded), registered as PI 9 at the Institute of Zoology, University of Porto. Paratypes are held at the same institution.
- Body roughly three times as long as wide; pleotelson pointed; antennule 9–11 articles (male), 5–7 (female).
- Antenna reaches the middle of the pleotelson; flagellum 52–63 articles (male), 31–44 (female).
- Maxillipede has two folds on the basipodite (the longer one ciliated) and one on the epipodite.
- Pleopod I (male) sympodite bears a strongly curved, six-toothed hook.

**Proasellus ibericus ferreirensis ssp. n.** (named after the proximity of the [[Rio Ferreira]])
- Holotype: male, 3.5 mm (antennae and uropods excluded), registered as PI 10; paratypes also retained at the Institute.
- Antennule 7–10 articles (male), 5–7 (female); antenna reaches the middle of the pleotelson in both sexes.
- Flagellum 36–69 articles (male), 36–37 (female).
- Pleopod I sympodite has 0–1 hook; pleotelson nearly as wide as long.

**Comparison with the nominal subspecies** (*P. i. ibericus*): the type is larger (male about 6 mm, female about 5 mm), its antenna reaches pereiopod V, its antennular flagellum has fewer articles (male 25–46, female 32–40), and its pleotelson is 1.17 times wider than long, versus 1.26 in *codreanui*. The pleopod I hook of the type is only slightly curved with four teeth, whereas *codreanui* has a markedly curved hook with six teeth and *ferreirensis* a less curved one, also with six teeth. Uropods are shortest relative to pleotelson length in *ferreirensis*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both populations are clearly **epigean** (surface-dwelling), in contrast to the cave-adapted asellids that dominate much of the Iberian record:
- **Moncão** (about 125 km north of Porto and 40 km inland from the coast): animals came from nearly still water issuing from a spring and from a small stream fed by that spring.
- **Pacos de Ferreira** (about 33 km east of Porto): animals were taken from the surface layer of a well near the Rio Ferreira, which feeds into the Douro.

The authors note that the nominal form is widely distributed in the northwest of Portugal and is also known from cavernicolous habitats in France. No physico-chemical water measurements are given in the text.

The study was supported by Project PB/4 of the I.A.C. The authors state that a future paper will address experimental and ecophysiological tests on laboratory-reared specimens from both stations, plus systematic studies on the basis of ecophysiological and mixiological criteria.

## Key Findings

- Two new subspecies of *Proasellus ibericus* are described from northern Portugal: ***P. i. codreanui*** (Moncão) and ***P. i. ferreirensis*** (Pacos de Ferreira).
- Holotypes are the males PI 9 (4.5 mm) and PI 10 (3.5 mm); the type series is deposited at the Institute of Zoology, University of Porto.
- Differences from the nominal subspecies lie in body size, antennular and antennal proportions, flagellum article counts, pleotelson shape, the pleopod I hook, and uropod length.
- Differences between the two new subspecies are slight, so they are treated as subspecies rather than species.
- Both populations are surface-dwelling (epigean), occurring in spring-fed water and a well, not in caves.
- Further experimental and ecophysiological work on laboratory-reared material was planned.
- The appended publication list summarizes about 120 numbered Institute publications (1940–1973) covering Asellids, cave fauna, Lepidoptera, Amphipoda, sardine fisheries, and other Portuguese zoology, with prominent contributions by [[J M Braga]] and [[J A Macedo-Cruz]].
