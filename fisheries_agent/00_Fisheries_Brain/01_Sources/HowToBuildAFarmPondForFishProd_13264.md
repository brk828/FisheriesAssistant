# How to Build a Farm Pond for Fish Production

#ichthyology #aquaculture #pond-management #habitat-design

## Executive Summary

This technical guidance document from the U.S. Department of Agriculture [[Soil Conservation Service]] provides specifications for designing and constructing farm ponds optimized for fish production. The document addresses water supply management, pond dimensions, outlet structures, vegetation control, and wildlife enhancement. It emphasizes proper depth requirements for different fish species, fertilization strategies, and structural engineering to prevent fish loss during storm events. The guidance was prepared in cooperation with State Fish and Game Departments of [[Oregon]], [[Idaho]], [[California]], and [[Nevada]] for the Pacific Region.

## Background & Description

Farm ponds designed for fish production require specific engineering and ecological considerations distinct from general water impoundments. The document addresses both cold-water species ([[Trout]]) and warm-water species ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Catfish]], [[Perch]]). Key infrastructure includes trickle tube outlets, emergency spillways, and water control structures. The guidance recognizes that steady water flow is not necessary when fertilizers are added to increase food supply for fish, though water supply must be regulated to maintain desired levels and temperatures.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Requirements:**
- Minimum surface area: one-third acre (0.33 acres)
- Minimum depth at deep end: 8 feet
- Cold climate requirements: 10-12 feet depth or sufficient flow to prevent winter kill
- Critical depth maintenance: minimum 4 feet during dry seasons
- Shallow margin control: 1.5-3 feet depth to prevent emergent vegetation

**Temperature Management:**
Water level drawdown below 4 feet causes excessive warming unsuitable for [[Trout]] unless refilled within days. Extended low water periods require restocking with warm-water species.

**Vegetation Control:**
Steep pond margins to 1.5-3 foot depth prevent establishment of [[Cattails]], [[Bulrush]], and [[Willows]]. Some aquatic weeds persist to 6 feet depth. Vegetation growth impairs fishing success, promotes pond scum formation, and creates mosquito breeding habitat that fish cannot adequately control.

**Wildlife Integration:**
Pond sites attract game birds and waterfowl. Recommended landscaping includes grasses, trees, and shrubs planted back from shoreline, with livestock exclusion from pond area.

## Key Findings

**Water Supply and Control:**
- Constant water flow should be regulated in fertilized ponds
- Only sufficient water to maintain desired level and temperature should be admitted
- Surplus water diverted through open ditch around pond
- Flow regulation achieved through pipe systems and gate structures

**Outlet Design Requirements:**
- Two-outlet system: trickle tube for normal flow, emergency spillway for flood waters
- Trickle tube: vertical pipe (concrete or corrugated galvanized iron) through dam base
- Trickle tube may be screened to prevent fish loss
- Emergency spillway: never screened (clogging risk endangers dam)
- Emergency spillway installed on undisturbed soil at dam end
- Wider spillways reduce fish loss during storms (shallower depth, slower current)
- Drain line through dam enables complete pond drainage for restocking or repairs

**Structural Specifications:**
- Minimum 5 inches clearance (specific location not specified in text)
- 8-inch corrugated metal pipe for outlets
- Trickle tube with trash screen
- Gate stem inside buried pipe filled with heavy gear oil
- Steel drum filled with rocks at inlet
- Car metal cut-offs and rip-rap for erosion control

**Engineering Support:**
[[Soil Conservation Service]] provides technical assistance through Soil Conservation Districts for:
1. Pond site location
2. Soil suitability assessment (water retention, dam construction)
3. Water supply adequacy determination
4. Structure design (dam, inlet control, outlets, spillways, livestock facilities)
5. Construction technical direction

**Fish Management Support:**
Stocking and management assistance arranged through Soil Conservation District or State Fish and Game Department (varies by state).

**Irrigation Considerations:**
Water withdrawal for irrigation should not lower pond below 4-foot depth to maintain suitable conditions for stocked species.
