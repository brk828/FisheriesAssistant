# Lake Mohave Razorback Sucker Monitoring and Cryopreservation Studies at Willow Beach NFH, March 1996

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum and draft report (May 6, 1996) describes the March 1996 operations of the [[Lake Mohave]] [[Razorback Sucker]] monitoring program at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH). The work had three goals: monitor wild razorback suckers in upper Lake Mohave, develop [[Sperm Cryopreservation]] methods for the federally endangered species, and propagate fish for [[Captive Broodstock]] augmentation and stocking. The effort was led by the [[Arizona Fisheries Resources Office]] of the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] under the Program for Cryopreservation of Genetic Resources for Endangered Fishes.

Field crews collected 75 sperm samples from wild fish, and 32 were used for cryopreservation research. The team identified improved [[Cryoprotectants]] and freezing and thawing protocols. Post-thaw motility rose roughly tenfold, to 10–40%. [[Caffeine]] addition appeared to increase fertilization substantially. The authors concluded that the methods were sufficient to begin banking sperm the following year. They also reported that a high share of female suckers were gravid late in the season, which they linked to cold, fluctuating tailwater conditions below [[Hoover Dam]].

## Background & Description

The document is a draft memorandum and study plan rather than a peer-reviewed paper. It was circulated to a distributed list of Fish and Wildlife Service, university and state agency personnel. Principal investigators were:

- Owen T. Gorman, PhD, coordinator, Arizona Fisheries Resources Office, Flagstaff
- Gary J. Carmichael, PhD, [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and technology center (propagation)
- J. Holt Williamson, PhD, [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and technology center
- Terrence R. Tiersch, PhD, Louisiana State University, School of Forestry, Wildlife and Fisheries

The study is organized around three objectives:

1. **Lake Mohave monitoring:** sample razorback suckers in upper Lake Mohave, collect sperm from wild fish, and cryopreserve samples from as many fish as feasible.
2. **Sperm cryopreservation research:** improve freezing methods and optimize fertilization of eggs with cryopreserved sperm.
3. **Propagation:** improve fish propagation techniques, evaluate the fitness of hatchery-produced fish, and produce up to 100,000 fry for the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]].

The memorandum lists 9 WBNFH and 8 Arizona Bay personnel, with Gorman as overall coordinator, Chester Figiel (LSU) leading cryopreservation, and Gary Carmichael leading propagation. Cited outputs include protocols for field sperm collection and field fertilization, a manuscript on field propagation using plastic bags (in press), and a revised study plan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area and sampling.** Electrofishing was conducted in March 1996 in upper Lake Mohave, between river miles 56 and 62 (OCR-garbled in source; verify). Previous surveys had documented aggregations of spawning-stage suckers near the dam and WBNFH sites. A support crew also sampled a cove in the Arizona Bay portion of the lake. There, 27% (n=18) of captures were recaptures.

**Reproductive condition.** Of the females examined, 70% were gravid at the end of the spawning season, when most were nearly spent. The authors noted that a low proportion of gravid females was captured at night, which they considered unusual because mature females are normally expected to be captured most readily at night. Spawning was observed in coves, and the authors suggested that spawning areas were in part linked to the tailwater environment.

**Tailwater conditions.** Water temperature in the relatively cold tailwater below Hoover Dam was about 10 °C. Large water-level fluctuations were reported to dewater gravel where spawning occurs. The authors suggested that these conditions could be adversely affecting reproduction in this population of razorback suckers.

**Associated species.** Management recommendations also refer to the [[Bonytail]], which was sampled alongside razorback suckers for broodstock work. The authors compared reproductive patterns with those of [[Humpback Chub]] in the Grand Canyon.

## Key Findings

### 1. Wild sperm collection and monitoring
- 75 sperm samples were collected from wild-caught fish; 32 were used for cryopreservation research.
- Many samples were discarded because volume was small, motility was low, or initial quality was too high (>80% motility) for experimental purposes.
- Sperm from Arizona Bay fish (crew assisted the Arizona Game and Fish Department) yielded 16 sperm and 3 egg samples, which were fertilized and transported to WBNFH on March 12–14.

### 2. Cryoprotectants
- Earlier studies (1994, 1995) found that 5% DMSO and 10% methanol gave the highest post-thaw motility (75–80%) with the lowest toxicity.
- DMA and glycerin were relatively toxic, with low post-thaw motility (0–40%).
- Propylene and ethylene glycol at 5% and 10% were ineffective as cryoprotectants.
- Non-toxic extracellular cryoprotectants (glucose) tested at two concentrations with 10% methanol had variable effects on post-thaw motility.

### 3. Equilibration, stimulants and freezing
- Pre-freeze equilibration times were extended beyond earlier studies.
- Adding 0.005 M caffeine increased pre-freeze and post-thaw motility in most sample sets.
- Straw-based methods and liquid nitrogen plunging proved ineffective; the cryovial/pellet approach was preferred.
- Fast freezing produced 15–25% post-thaw motility, compared with about 5% for slow freezing.
- Among four thawing rates tested, swirling straws in a warm-water bath gave the highest post-thaw motility.

### 4. Fertilization trials with cryopreserved sperm
- **Dosage:** Doses of 0.5 mL of sperm were mixed with about 250 eggs. Fertilization increased with sperm dose even though post-thaw motility was low.
- **Caffeine:** Mean fertilization was 3.5% for untreated sperm and 14.5% with caffeine (as read from OCR). The discussion states that fertilization reached up to 60% of controls.
- **Freezing rate:** Fertilization rates from fast-frozen sperm were compared with slow-frozen sperm (mean 2.8% reported for the slow-frozen treatment).
- The authors concluded that fertilization is correlated with post-thaw motility.

### 5. Propagation and broodstock
- A total of 37 crosses were made for four purposes: augmenting captive broodstock at Dexter NFH, improving propagation technology, producing eggs for cryopreservation studies, and producing fish for stocking.
- Sixteen crosses used fresh sperm from wild fish collected in Lake Mohave (6 pairs) and Yuma Cove (4 pairs), among others.
- Sixteen gravid females from upper Lake Mohave were held and injected with [[Human Chorionic Gonadotropin]] to induce ovulation. Most gravid females responded; non-ripe females were given additional injections as needed.
- Several incubation methods were tested, including zip-lock bag hatcheries and Saran baskets.
- The authors recommended extending propagation over longer periods rather than a single-week effort in March.

### 6. Management recommendations
- Cold temperatures and fluctuating water levels may be harming razorback sucker reproduction in Lake Mohave.
- Release of surface water from [[Lake Mead]] to warm the tailwater may improve spawning and hatching conditions.
- Limiting daily fluctuations and ramping rates for dam discharge could reduce physical impacts on spawning.
- These measures would be most beneficial if applied during January–March.

### 7. Overall conclusions
- Post-thaw motility of cryopreserved sperm improved about tenfold, to 10–40%.
- Caffeine appears to substantially improve fertilization with cryopreserved sperm.
- The methods were judged sufficient to begin banking sperm samples the following year.
- Evaluation of progeny from cryopreserved sperm was planned over the next two years.
- The Arizona Game and Fish Department was allotted a stocking quota of 100,000 razorback suckers.
