# On the Development of Viviparous Osseous Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #embiotocidae

## Executive Summary

This 1885 paper by John A. Ryder, published in the *Proceedings of the United States National Museum*, summarizes what was known about truly viviparous bony fishes whose embryos develop inside the ovary or in follicles. It reviews earlier work, reproduces long extracts from [[Jeffries Wyman]] on [[Anableps]] and from [[Girard]] on surf-perches, and adds new observations on [[Gambusia Patruelis]], the surf-perches of the Pacific coast, and [[Fundulus Majalis]]. Ryder argues that the embryos of [[Embiotocidae]] and [[Anablepidae]] are partly nourished by the parent, whereas Gambusia embryos depend entirely on yolk, and that the vascular fin structures of surf-perch embryos are mainly respiratory.

## Background & Description

Ryder frames viviparity as a range of strategies. Wyman divided viviparous fishes into two groups by where the embryo develops. In the first, the ovum leaves the ovary undeveloped and development begins in the lower oviduct; this group is mainly [[Elasmobranchii]] such as *Spinax*, *Carcharias*, and *Torpedo*. In the second, gestation is wholly or partly ovarian, with impregnation in the ovary. This group includes *Blennius* (*Zoarces viviparus*), *Anableps*, *Poecilia*, and the Embiotocids.

**Anableps (*A. gronovii*).** Wyman's material came from Dr. Francis W. Cragin, U.S. consul at Paramaribo, Surinam: three males and five females, four of them gravid, ranging from 35 to 94 inches in length as printed (the scan likely garbles fractional inches). Embryos were enclosed in individual sacs, and the yolk-sac bore a series of vascular papillae that grew with the embryo. Ryder regarded this as unique among osseous fishes.

**Surf-perches (Embiotocidae).** [[Thomas H. Webb]] first recorded viviparity in May 1852 in San Diego Bay. Girard's 1858 report for the Pacific railroad survey described the ovarian sheath and the eggs. Ryder notes that Girard misidentified the protruding hind-gut as a yolk-bag. Blake (1867, 1868) described the vascular fin lobes and argued they absorb nutrients from ovarian fluid. Ryder's own material included gravid females of *Amphistichus arenatus*, *Ditrema jacksoni*, and *Micrometrus aggregatus*, collected by Miss Rosa Smith of San Diego.

**Gambusia patruelis.** Ryder studied this top-minnow at Cherrystone, Virginia, in August 1881, after an earlier preliminary notice in *Forest and Stream* (1881) and *The American Naturalist* (1882).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper emphasizes the physiological exchange between mother and embryo. Ryder compares fish follicles with the mammalian placenta and Graafian vesicle, noting that in fish there is no uterus and no placenta, and no direct vascular connection between mother and young. Gas exchange takes place across membranes and follicular fluids, with maternal blood supplying the follicular capillary network.

Several reproductive adaptations are discussed:

- **Egg envelopes:** Ryder reviews the zona radiata of osseous fishes, the horny egg-cases of oviparous [[Plagiostomi]], and the filamentous egg membranes of *Amiurus*, *Menidia*, and *Belone*. He suggests the reduced envelope in Gambusia reflects its independent, well-developed young.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** The male Gambusia has a modified anal fin used as an intromittent organ. Its weight of 160 mg compares with 1,030 mg for a gravid female, nearly 6.5 times heavier, which Ryder calls unusual among fishes.
- **Brood size and fertility:** Gambusia carried 20 to 25 eggs per ovary, and Duly reported births of 8 to 11 young per expulsion. Blake recorded up to 40 to 50 young per ovary in surf-perches, each weighing one-twelfth to one-sixteenth of the parent.

## Key Findings

**Anableps (Wyman, reproduced by Ryder)**
- Gestation is ovarian. Embryos are enclosed in individual sacs, and the yolk-sac becomes papillose and vascular.
- The yolk disappears early, yet the embryo keeps growing, indicating nourishment from the parent. Ryder concludes that a secretion from the sac walls or ovarian cavity supplies this nutrition.
- The ovarian wall and the yolk-sac are both highly vascular, which Ryder considers supportive of this view.

**Embiotocidae (surf-perches)**
- The embryonic hind-gut is hypertrophied, filling about two-thirds of the abdominal cavity in the early fetus. Its walls carry very long, crowded villi, which shorten as development proceeds and are much reduced in adults. Ryder concludes the gut is digestive and takes up the ovarian secretion.
- The vertical fins develop marginal, highly vascular interradial lobes that extend up to a quarter of an inch. Ryder reports 26 terminal lobes on the anal fin of *Micrometrus*, three more than on the dorsal.
- Ryder argues these lobes function mainly in respiration through the skin, not nutrition. Blake had proposed the opposite. The fin vasculature regresses after birth.
- The Girard figures show the embryos at an advanced stage, and Ryder considers the mouth open and the throat perforate at these stages, contrary to Girard.

**Gambusia patruelis (Ryder's own study)**
1. Fertilization takes place inside the ovarian follicle. Sperm reach the egg through a large follicular pore, which Ryder considers a micropyle equivalent.
2. The follicles are not ruptured until development is almost complete. Scales, fin rays, and cranium are well formed before the yolk is fully absorbed.
3. Embryos grow entirely from yolk and receive no maternal nutrition. Gambusia lacks the hypertrophied gut and fin lobes of surf-perches.
4. Newborns leave the parent about half an inch long, able to feed at once. Ryder argues that this offsets the greater fertility of oviparous species, so the survival chances of the two strategies are roughly equal.
5. Duly observed that parents devoured newborns unless separated, and that fright could trigger birth.

**Fundulus majalis**
- Embryos collected at Cherrystone on July 18, 1881, had a well-marked zona radiata, unlike Gambusia.
- The yolk was surrounded by an extensive vascular network drained by Cuvierian veins into a median vein.

**Overall conclusion:** Ryder considers the Gambusia history substantially complete for later stages but still incomplete for the earliest stages. He emphasizes that viviparous fishes range from full maternal provisioning (surf-perches, *Anableps*) to independence from parental nutrition (Gambusia), and that the earliest stages of both surf-perches and Gambusia remain uncharacterized.
