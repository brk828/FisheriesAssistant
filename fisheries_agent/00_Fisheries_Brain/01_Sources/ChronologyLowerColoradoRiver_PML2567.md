# Chronology of the Lower Colorado River (1869–1974)

#freshwater-ecosystem #river-management #dam-engineering

## Executive Summary

This December 1974 chronology, prepared by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Lower Colorado Regional Office, lists major events on the lower [[Colorado River]] from 1869 to 1974. It covers two strands: the construction of major dams and canals, and the operation and maintenance activities of the [[River Management Program]], which applies to the [[Colorado River Front Work and Levee System]]. A bar chart in the original shows periods of major dredging and construction in each operational division. The document does not address fish species, aquatic invertebrates, or water chemistry. Its ecological content is limited to refuge creation, habitat dredging, and wildlife-related channel work.

## Background & Description

The document is an administrative reference, not a research report. It has two main sections:

1. **General chronology:** major events such as dam construction, canal construction, and interstate agreements.
2. **River Management Program chronology:** levee construction, channelization, dredging, snag removal, and maintenance.

The chronology begins with the 1869 expedition of [[John Wesley Powell]], who later became the first Director of the [[United States Geological Survey]]. It ends in 1974 with habitat projects at [[Topock Marsh]] and [[Cibola Lake]].

The legal basis for the River Management Program is the [[Colorado River Front Work and Levee System Act]] of March 3, 1925. The Act of June 28, 1946, broadened its authority to operations and maintenance, river control, channel improvement, and investigations. Funding is split into two categories: Construction and Rehabilitation, and Operation and Maintenance. The chronology covers only the second.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes a river heavily modified by engineering. Its main features include:

- **Impoundments:** [[Lake Mead]] (formed behind [[Boulder Dam]]), [[Lake Havasu]] (behind [[Parker Dam]]), and [[Imperial Reservoir]] (behind [[Imperial Dam]]).
- **Diversion works:** [[Laguna Dam]] (completed 1918, no longer used for diversion after 1957), [[Palo Verde Diversion Dam]], and [[Headgate Rock Dam]].
- **Canals:** the [[Imperial Canal]], the [[All-American Canal]], and the Gila Gravity Canal.
- **Channel work:** dredging, channelization from Needles to Topock, backwater deepening, and bank protection.

Several entries relate to wildlife and habitat:

- The [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]] was created in January of the 1940s, and the [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] in February of the 1940s.
- The [[Cibola Wildlife Refuge]] was created in August 1964.
- [[Mittry Lake]] was dredged for a fish and wildlife management area. It was transferred to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] for management in February 1973.
- A 200-acre dredged pool was created in Mittry Lake as part of a planned fish and wildlife management area. The pool is about 10 feet deep.
- Fish and wildlife dredging took place in Cibola Lake (February 24 to August 16, 1970) and in Palo Verde Oxbow Lake (August 26 to December 19, 1970).
- Habitat improvements at Topock Marsh began on January 15, 1974, under a [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] plan.

Several entries also record sediment management:

- Silt removal upstream of the Arizona trashracks at Imperial Reservoir.
- Dredging of the Gila sluiceway below Imperial Dam.
- Snag clearing in Lake Havasu, in three contracts between 1965 and 1971.

## Key Findings

**Major infrastructure milestones (as recorded):**

- **Imperial Canal:** construction began in 1895. Water first flowed to the [[Imperial Valley]] in 1901.
- **Flood breach:** a disastrous flood breached the system and allowed Colorado River water to flow into the [[Salton Sea]] for nearly two years, beginning in 1905.
- **Laguna Diversion Dam:** construction began July 19, 1909, and was completed March 20, 1918.
- **Colorado River Compact:** signed at Santa Fe, New Mexico, on November 24, 1928. It allocated most of the river between the upper and lower basins.
- **Boulder Canyon Act:** enacted December 21, 1931, authorizing Boulder Dam and its powerplant.
- **Boulder Dam:** construction began in 1934. Lake Mead impounding began February 1, 1935. President Roosevelt dedicated the dam in September 1935.
- **Lake Mead record:** the water surface reached an all-time high of 1,220.45 feet above sea level on July 30 (year not stated). At that level the lake was 580 feet deep, 120 miles long, and held 31 million acre-feet.
- **Renaming:** Boulder Dam was renamed Hoover Dam by Congress.
- **Parker Dam:** substantially complete in September 1938. Its powerplant construction began in July 1941.
- **Imperial Dam:** closed April 17, 1938.
- **Davis Dam:** construction began in June 1942, was suspended in December when material priorities were revoked, and resumed April 3. It was completed in June 1950s (the year is not clearly legible in the scan).
- **Morelos Dam:** completed.
- **Water deliveries to Mexico:** deliveries through the All-American Canal began in January.
- **Main Outlet Drain:** construction began October 10, 1956, and was completed in January 1957. It doubles as the South Gila Levee.
- **Senator Wash Dam and Reservoir:** completed in 1966.

**River Management Program highlights:**

- **Emergency measures:** relief work began at Needles, California, in 1944. Existing levees were enlarged.
- **Office of River Control:** established July 29 (1948). It was abolished February 12, 1968, and its functions were transferred to the [[Colorado River Project Office]] at Blythe and the River Planning Branch at Boulder City.
- **Channelization:** the dredge "The Colorado" completed Needles to Topock channelization on April 21, 1950s (year unclear). The dredge "Little Colorado" later took over Topock Settling Basin work.
- **Dredge losses and recovery:** "The Colorado" sank November 4, 1950s (year unclear). It was recovered by February 25, rehabilitated, and returned to service June 16.
- **Dredge fleet:** "The Colorado," "Little Colorado," "Gila," and a 10-inch dredge were used across the Needles, Cibola, Laguna, and Topock divisions.
- **Cibola Division:** the Cibola Operating Yard was built in 1964. The 8-mile Cibola dry cut was opened March 10, 1970, after about 2½ years of dredging.
- **Agnes Wilson Bridge:** destroyed by fire on December 2, 1973.
- **Laguna South Recreation Area:** initial construction began in July 1973 and was completed March 22, 1974.
- **Lower Colorado River Project Office:** closed August 1972, with its functions transferred to other offices.

The document makes no explicit conclusions or projections. It is a factual record of past events. Several dates in the scanned text are garbled, so year-level details should be checked against the original before citation.
