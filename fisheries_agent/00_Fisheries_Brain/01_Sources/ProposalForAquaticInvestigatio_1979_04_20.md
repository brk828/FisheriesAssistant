## Executive Summary

This document is a 1979 research proposal submitted by Arizona State University (Principal Investigator: W.L. Minckley, Department of Zoology) to E. Linwood Smith & Associates, proposing a one-year aquatic investigation at the proposed **[[Alchesay Pump-Storage Site]]** on **[[Apache Lake]]** (uppermost reach), part of the **[[Salt River Reservoir Chain]]** in Central Arizona. The study's purpose is to characterize the resident fish community and its habitat use prior to construction, in order to predict and mitigate impacts of pump-storage operations (cyclical pumping/generating causing altered current regimes and water-level fluctuations). No specific taxonomic data or survey results are presented; the document is purely a proposed methodology, budget, and administrative approval package — it identifies data gaps rather than reporting findings.

## Background & Description

No morphological data are reported in this source. The proposal does not identify specific fish species, sizes, or morphotypes present in the lake; obtaining **species composition, relative abundance, and size distributions** is listed as the primary objective of the proposed field study (Objective 1a-b), to be fulfilled through direct capture methods (gill/trammel nets, seines, electrofishing) and larval/pre-juvenile netting.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal frames the resident fish community of **[[Apache Lake]]** as largely composed of species adapted to **quiet-water (lentic) habitats**, making them potentially vulnerable to altered current regimes introduced by pump-storage operations. Key ecological considerations outlined include:

- **Rheotaxis/current-orientation behavior**: Some fish actively orient toward and move into currents (e.g., near intake-outlet structures), risking entrainment when pumping direction reverses.
- **Passive larval drift**: Larvae with limited swimming capability are described as passively transported by currents, increasing vulnerability to entrainment into intake structures during pumping mode.
- **Depth stratification**: Deep-dwelling fishes are hypothesized to be relatively buffered from surface-level hydrological disturbance, whereas fishes in upper water strata are expected to experience greater impacts.
- **Diel vertical migration**: Some species reportedly undergo day–night vertical movements, implying that time-of-day is a relevant variable for assessing pump/generation effects.
- **Littoral nursery habitat**: Shallow lake-margin habitats are used by most fish species for **spawning and as nursery areas for young**; these zones are sensitive to water-level fluctuation.
- **Riparian/emergent vegetation development**: Since the early 1970s, increased water-level stabilization in Apache Lake has promoted growth of emergent aquatic and semi-aquatic riparian plants, which now provide habitat structure and terrestrial food subsidies for shore-dwelling fishes — a habitat resource anticipated to be altered by future water-level regime changes from pump-storage operation.
- **Trophic and competitive interactions**: The proposal explicitly targets characterization of **predator-prey relationships**, **potential interspecific competition**, and **fish distribution relative to food sources** (Objective 3a-c).

## Key Findings

Because this is a proposal rather than a completed study, there are no empirical findings. Notable planned/anticipated elements include:

- **Study objectives** structured around three themes: (1) fishery identification (species, abundance, size, seasonality), (2) habitat/movement characterization (including reproductive habitat), and (3) interspecies ecological interactions (food, competition, predation).
- **Methodology**: Combination of direct sampling (gill/trammel nets, seines, electrofishing, fine-mesh larval nets, fin-clip marking for movement/population studies) and indirect sampling (sonar for detecting fish schools/larval concentrations, day-night distribution shifts, verified via vertical netting).
- **Anticipated impact mechanisms** of the pump-storage system: altered current intensity/patterns near intake-outlet structures, increased water-level fluctuation in the forebay-afterbay system, and consequent risks of **entrainment mortality**, particularly for larvae and current-oriented adult fishes.
- **Study timing**: Proposed to begin immediately to coincide with the ongoing reproductive season, since early life-history stages are considered more sensitive to environmental disturbance than adults; sampling frequency of at least biweekly, intensified during peak activity periods.
- **Project duration**: One year of field data collection followed by a six-month reporting period, with quarterly interim progress reports.
- **Budget**: Total request itemized under salaries/wages (field biologist, two student assistants, fringe benefits), travel/per diem, and equipment/supplies (nets, sonar recorder paper, preservatives), plus university indirect costs at 42% of direct costs (off-campus rate).
- **Administrative context**: Proposal involved standard ASU sponsored-project approval processes; explicitly noted no use of human subjects, warm-blooded animals, DNA work, or radiation — confirming the study's scope was limited to fish population field surveys.
