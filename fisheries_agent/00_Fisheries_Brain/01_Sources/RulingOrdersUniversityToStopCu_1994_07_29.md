## Executive Summary

A U.S. District Court ruling by Judge Alfredo Marquez permanently enjoined the University of Arizona from clearing additional forest at the [[Mount Graham International Observatory]] construction site pending further environmental review. The dispute centers on the [[Large Binocular Telescope]] project, a joint venture between the University of Arizona, the Vatican's Arcetri Observatory, Ohio State University, and Research Corp. Opponents — including the [[Sierra Club]], [[Arizona Wildlife Federation]], and [[Maricopa Audubon Society]] — argue the project threatens the habitat of an endangered squirrel subspecies and violates the [[Endangered Species Act]] and [[National Environmental Policy Act]]. The site is also considered sacred by the [[San Carlos Apache]] tribe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** High-elevation montane conifer forest on Emerald Peak (10,477 ft), part of the Pinaleño Mountains / Mount Graham range in southeastern Arizona.
- **Endemism concern:** The red squirrel subspecies is reported by opponents to be endemic to and dependent on this isolated mountain forest ecosystem, functioning as a biogeographic "island" habitat.
- **Anthropogenic pressure:** Construction of the [[Large Binocular Telescope]] required clearing approximately 250 trees within a 1-acre area (part of a planned 1.28-acre site), directly reducing forest habitat available to the squirrel population.
- **Regulatory framework:** Habitat protection is legally mediated through the [[U.S. Forest Service]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], which were originally delegated authority over site location decisions within an 8.6-acre area authorized by Congress in 1988.
- **Conservation status implication:** Plaintiffs argue that relocating the telescope site nullified the project's original exemption from environmental safeguards, which required the three telescopes to remain clustered to minimize habitat disturbance.

## Key Findings

- Judge Marquez issued a **permanent injunction** (following a July 12 temporary restraining order) barring further tree-clearing on the remaining quarter-acre of the site until new environmental studies are completed.
- The university had already cut approximately 250 trees in December, one day after Forest Service approval to relocate the telescope site for improved viewing conditions.
- Plaintiffs (Mount Graham Coalition) argue this site relocation invalidated the project's exemption from the [[Endangered Species Act]] and [[National Environmental Policy Act]], since the original exemption required a clustered telescope configuration.
- Robin Silver (Maricopa Audubon Society) called for a **new biological study** by the Fish and Wildlife Service on the project's impact on the squirrel population, estimating such a study, if done rigorously, could take **two years**.
- The controversy sits at the intersection of astronomical infrastructure development, endangered species law, and Indigenous sacred-site rights (San Carlos Apache opposition on religious/cultural grounds).
