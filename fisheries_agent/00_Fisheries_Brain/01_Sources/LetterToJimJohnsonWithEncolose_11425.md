# Genetic Characteristics of the Razorback Sucker: A Data Base for Studies of Introgressive Hybridization and Population Differentiation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #conservation-genetics #hybridization

## Executive Summary

This 1982 research proposal, submitted by Linda Ulmer (California Department of Fish and Game) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, outlines a two-phase electrophoretic study to assess genetic purity of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) breeding stocks. The study addresses concerns that the [[Lake Mojave]] population may be genetically compromised through introgressive hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*). Dr. Donald G. Buth (UCLA) would serve as principal investigator, utilizing allozyme electrophoresis to distinguish pure razorback suckers from hybrids and to characterize genetic variation across remaining populations in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The proposal responds to urgent conservation needs as the species undergoes hatchery propagation and reintroduction efforts at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a catostomid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The species has experienced severe population declines prompting U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery efforts including hatchery propagation and restocking programs. Under cooperative agreements, 650,000 hatchery-reared razorback sucker fry and 19,000 fingerlings have been stocked into the [[Gila River]] drainage, with additional reintroductions planned for New Mexico.

Broodstock for hatchery operations were selected from [[Lake Mojave]]. However, observations by California Department of Fish and Game personnel (Aasen and Ulmer) in 1981-1982 revealed considerable morphological variation in the Lake Mojave population not apparent in the [[Senator Wash Reservoir]] population. Historically, razorback suckers overlapped in range with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*C. insignis*), and some hybridization occurred (Hubbs and Miller 1953). Dam construction and impoundment creation likely increased range overlap and hybridization potential.

Gustafson (1975) reported that hybrids (*X. texanus* × *Catostomus* sp.) comprised a minimum of 10% of trammel net catches from Black Canyon and Carp Cove in Lake Mojave. W. Minckley collected apparent *C. latipinnis* × *X. texanus* hybrids from Lake Mojave in the 1970s. Recent netting in Arizona Bay and Cottonwood Basin (Paulson, pers. comm.) captured razorback suckers that phenotypically appear to be hybrids, with morphometric and meristic measurements supporting the hybrid definition proposed by Hubbs and Miller (1953).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The decline of native fish populations in the lower [[Colorado River Basin]] has been driven by habitat alteration through dam construction and impoundment. The creation of [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Havasu]], and [[Lake Mojave]] transformed riverine habitats into lacustrine environments, potentially relaxing selective pressures operative in riverine systems while increasing opportunities for interspecific hybridization.

Substantial razorback sucker populations persist in the Upper Colorado Basin, Lake Mojave, and Senator Wash Reservoir, California. The species' recovery depends on identifying genetically pure breeding stocks free of introgressed *Catostomus* genes. Gene Wilde's morphological study of 330 razorback suckers from three Lake Mojave sites (Arizona Bay, Tequila Cove, Six-mile Cove area) documented considerable variation in body depth, peduncle depth, gape, lip morphology, and separation of lower lip lobes—traits potentially indicating flannelmouth sucker influence.

The timing of hybridization remains uncertain. If Minckley's hypothesis of no current recruitment into Lake Mojave is correct, hybridization likely occurred after impoundment of Lakes Mead and Havasu, with introgression continuing until shortly after Lake Mojave was impounded. Alternatively, hybridization may have begun with Lakes Mead and Havasu, intensified after Lake Mojave impoundment, and continued to the present.

## Key Findings

**Phase I Objectives:**
- Provide evidence that [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) can be electrophoretically distinguished based on allelic differentiation (allozymes) and functional diploidization of different enzyme loci in these tetraploid fishes
- Document genetic markers on both allelic and locus levels that distinguish these species
- Examine and document electrophoretic characteristics of offspring from Lake Mojave broodstock held at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]
- Determine morphological variation between samples using morphometric and meristic measurements outlined in Hubbs and Miller (1953)

**Phase I Sample Requirements:**
- *Catostomus latipinnis*: 30 specimens from Yampa, Green, or Colorado River (arrangements with Tom Lytle, Colorado Division of Wildlife)
- *Xyrauchen texanus*: 30 offspring from Senator Wash Reservoir parent stock
- Lake Mojave broodstock offspring: 10 specimens from Dexter National Fish Hatchery

**Phase II Objectives (contingent on Phase I validation):**
- Conduct detailed electrophoretic analysis of selected razorback sucker populations throughout Colorado River Basin
- Compare genetic characteristics among: (1) *X. texanus* from Upper Colorado River Basin and Lake Mojave; (2) *X. texanus* × *C. latipinnis* hybrids from Upper Colorado River Basin and Lake Mojave; (3) *Catostomus insignis* from Arizona
- Sub-populations of *C. latipinnis* may be included if deemed necessary by Razorback Sucker Advisory Committee

**Morphological Evidence of Hybridization:**
Gene Wilde's preliminary study of 330 Lake Mojave razorback suckers revealed:
- Variation in body depth, peduncle depth, head depth, lip width, lip length, and gape substantially greater than in Hubbs and Miller's (1953) reference specimens (coefficients of determination 0.80-0.99 in reference vs. much lower in Lake Mojave)
- Separation of lower lip lobes appears diagnostic: pure razorback suckers show well-separated lobes with large gap at base; flannelmouth suckers show extremely small separation; hybrids show intermediate separation
- Six or seven "razorback suckers" exhibited intermediate values for lip lobe separation, strongly suggesting introgressive hybridization
- Some razorback-appearing fish possessed "hybrid lips" indicating backcrossing may mask genetic contributions

**Methodological Approach:**
- Electrophoretic methods following Selander et al. (1971) and Buth's previous catostomid studies
- Locus treatment paralleling Crabtree and Buth (1981) study on *Catostomus fumeiventris* × *C. santaanae* hybridization
- All specimens to be measured morphologically (one person for consistency, Gene Wilde recommended), then frozen at -20°C for electrophoretic analysis
- Sample size: 30 fish (4-12 inches) per population

**Budget and Timeline:**
- Phase I: $3,780.72 (two months salary for Dr. R.W. Murphy, photographic services, expendable supplies)
- Timeline: 6 weeks electrophoretic work, 2 weeks data analysis and report preparation
- Phase II: Estimated proportionally based on specimen numbers

**Principal Investigator Qualifications:**
Dr. Donald G. Buth (UCLA) brings 12 years electrophoretic experience and is one of only three American investigators experienced in allozyme and isozyme electrophoresis of catostomid fishes (with Dr. G.S. Whitt, University of Illinois; Dr. S.D. Ferris, UC Berkeley). These three investigators account for approximately 90% of all electrophoresis publications on catostomid fishes.
