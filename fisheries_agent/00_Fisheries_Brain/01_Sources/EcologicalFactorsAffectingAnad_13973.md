# Ecological Factors Affecting Anadromous Fishes of Lake Pontchartrain and Its Tributaries

#ichthyology #marine-biology #anadromous-fishes

## Executive Summary

This Louisiana Wild Life and Fisheries Commission bulletin (Fisheries Bulletin No. 9, 1970) reports a three-year inventory (1966–1969) of anadromous and estuarine fishes in coastal Louisiana east of the [[Mississippi River]]. The study was designed to explain why the [[Striped Bass]] fishery declined, to assess whether the species could be re-established, and to document other anadromous species. No striped bass were captured. The study did find that forage and salinity conditions would support a [[Striped Bass]] introduction. It also confirmed reproducing populations of [[Atlantic Sturgeon]] and [[Alabama Shad]]. The authors recommend continued stocking attempts and the study of these two species.

## Background & Description

The striped bass (*Morone saxatilis*) historically supported a fishery in this region, but no records document its presence after 1956. Range maps suggested that other anadromous species might occur here, so the study set out to verify their presence and catalogue the fishes using each habitat.

The study area includes four shallow lakes, [[Lake Maurepas]], [[Lake Pontchartrain]], [[Lake St. Catherine]] and [[Lake Borgne]], which are affected by tides but more strongly by wind and by inflow from coastal streams. Salinity generally decreases from Lake Borgne toward Lake Maurepas. Industrial and municipal pollution was evident in all four lakes and in the [[Pearl River]]. Many smaller coastal streams have been affected by gravel dredging and by shallow bars formed at channel mouths during dredging for barge traffic. The project was financed in part under the [[Anadromous Fish Act]] (P.L. 89-304).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.** Five gear types were used:

- **[[Otter Trawl Sampling]]:** a 16 ft trawl (3/4 inch mesh) towed 10 minutes, plus a 30 ft trawl used in January and May–August 1967. Temperature and salinity were recorded with each sample.
- **[[Trammel Net Sampling]]:** 200-yard and 250-yard nets (one-inch mesh) set at river mouths. Mouth sampling was hampered by stumps and tug traffic, and [[Electrofishing]] was abandoned in high salinities.
- **[[Hoop Net Sampling]]:** seven-hoop, two-throat nets fished 24 hours per set, often baited with cottonseed cake.
- **[[Electrofishing]]:** a boat-mounted prod-pole shocker powered by a 110-volt generator. Results were erratic because of water quality and clutter, and catches were estimated as a percentage of fish seen.
- **[[Seining]]:** nylon seines (1/8 to 1/4 inch mesh), with night seining generally more productive than daytime seining.

Fish were preserved in 10% formalin and identified at Northeast Louisiana State College. Water quality data were compiled from multiple agencies and universities, with the Division of Water Pollution Control dataset used for analysis because of its continuity.

**Habitat factors.** Two conditions were identified as favorable for a [[Striped Bass]] introduction:

- **Forage base.** Forage species included [[Brown Shrimp]], [[White Shrimp]], [[Blue Crab]], [[Gulf Menhaden]], [[Bay Anchovy]], spot, Atlantic croaker, pinfish and striped mullet. Possible competitors were sand seatrout, spotted seatrout, Atlantic croaker and southern flounder. Black drum and sheepshead were judged less competitive because they feed on molluscs and worms.
- **[[Salinity Gradient]].** Salinities ranged from fresh to slightly brackish in mid and eastern Lake Pontchartrain and Lake St. Catherine, and from brackish to saline in Lake Borgne. Deep passes connecting the lakes would allow striped bass to find optimal salinities.

**Channelization and water quality.** Coastal streams were channelized in the late 1940s and early 1950s, which raised silt loads, destroyed riffle areas and filled estuarine distributaries with silt bars. The authors consider this the most reliable explanation for the loss of traditional spawning grounds, though it cannot be proven. The [[Tchefuncte River]] was the least polluted stream, while the [[Pearl River]] had gained industrial waste loads since before 1958. Salt water intruded to river mile 10 on the Pearl River for 30 or more hours during hurricane periods in 1964 and 1965.

## Key Findings

**Otter trawl catches (Lakes Maurepas, Borgne, St. Catherine and Pontchartrain, January 1967–June 1969)**

- 40 species of fin-fishes and shellfishes were collected, representing 5 orders and 18 families of Osteichthyes and 1 order and 5 families of Crustacea.
- Species composition was similar across areas, mostly brackish and saline organisms. Exceptions were blue catfish, channel catfish and freshwater drum in Lake Maurepas, and longnose gar and blue catfish in western Lake Pontchartrain.
- Low shrimp production was the only notable difference among areas, and seasonal peaks were observed for some species.

**Trammel and hoop net catches in Lakes Maurepas and Pontchartrain**

- 39 species representing 18 families were collected over two years.
- Catch per effort was extremely low in Lake Maurepas, and tides had no apparent effect on catches.
- A single haul at the mouth of the Blind River on August 24, 1967 yielded 50 [[Longnose Gar]] and 20 spotted gar. No explanation was found, and no similar concentration recurred.
- The trammel nets did not capture enough forage fish to assess forage supply, because of mesh size.

**East Pearl River (trammel and hoop nets)**

- 24 species were captured in the trammel net; none occurred in numbers that would be detrimental to anadromous species.
- One Atlantic sturgeon was taken on November 1, 1968, in a 250-yard trammel net.
- Overall, fish large enough to be caught in one-inch mesh were relatively scarce, with the highest concentrations in March–June.

**Tributary streams (1966–1969)**

- Fishes represented 28 families, 57 genera and 111 species.
- Hoop nets: 1,717 net days yielded 15 families and 40 species. Catostomidae, Ictaluridae and Centrarchidae occurred at every station. Only blacktail redhorse, flathead catfish and rock bass were taken at all hoop-net stations.
- Trammel nets: 165 net days yielded 17 families (12 freshwater, 5 estuarine) and 86 species. Atlantic sturgeon, longnose gar, blue catfish, freshwater drum and southern flounder were taken from all sampled areas.
- Electrofishing: catches were erratic, with the Amite River, Tickfaw River, Tchefuncte River, Bogue Chitto River and Pearl River sampled over 1 to 6 hours each.

**Anadromous species**

- **[[Alabama Shad]]:** One gravid female, 15.0 inches and 1.5 pounds, was taken from the Bogue Chitto River at Isabel Landing. Its gonads indicated it could spawn. Its presence in the Pearl River was inferred from its occurrence in a tributary.
- **[[Atlantic Sturgeon]]:** The most common anadromous species in the project area, mainly taken from the Pearl River. Gonadal examination indicated a reproducing population. Sizes ranged from 6 inches and under a pound to 74 inches and an estimated 150 pounds. Only a minor commercial fishery existed, and no sport fishing was observed.
- **[[Striped Bass]]:** None were captured. In July 1967, 8,700 fingerlings were released in the Tchefuncte River, and none were recovered, so very few, if any, survived.

**Water quality and flow**

- Pearl River at Bogalusa (drainage area 6,630 sq mi) had a measured low flow of 1,020 cfs. The Amite River at Denham Springs (13,830 sq mi) measured 272 cfs at low flow.
- The Tchefuncte River, with a 145 sq mi drainage area at U.S. 190, measured 33 cfs at low flow.

**Conclusions and recommendations**

1. The formerly fishable striped bass population in eastern Louisiana coastal waters is extinct.
2. A reproducing Atlantic sturgeon population supports a very minor commercial fishery.
3. Alabama shad juveniles and adults indicate at least a reproducing population, but not in numbers sufficient for a fishery.
4. Water quality improved over the previous decade and should continue to improve, benefiting anadromous fisheries.
5. Forage organisms are plentiful, and no detrimental competition was found.
6. Attempts to establish a striped bass population should continue, especially in the Pearl River complex (longest reach of consistent flow) and the Tchefuncte River (no dams or industrial effluents).
7. Hatchery facilities for striped bass should be built promptly at the [[Lacombe Fish Hatchery]].
8. The spawning requirements, ecology and significance of Atlantic sturgeon and Alabama shad should be studied.
