## Executive Summary

This abstract documents the taxonomic re-establishment of **[[Etheostoma okaloosae]]** (common name: Okaloosa darter) as a valid, distinct species within the percid subgenus **[[Villora]]**. Originally described by Henry W. Fowler in 1941 as *Villora okaloosae* from a single specimen near Niceville, Florida, the species was later synonymized with *[[Etheostoma swaini]]* (Jordan) by Bailey, Winn, and Smith (1954). Yerger's analysis of 140 specimens across seven series re-validates *E. okaloosae* as a separate species, distinguishing it from its closest relative, *[[Etheostoma edwini]]*. The species is a narrow-range endemic restricted to a small cluster of streams draining into Choctawhatchee Bay in Northwest Florida, with a biogeographic history tied to Pleistocene sea-level fluctuations.

## Background & Description

- **Subgeneric placement (Villora) diagnostic traits:**
  - Presence of an **arched lateral line**.
  - In females, the genital papilla is replaced by a **marginal ring of matted villi** (a key sexual/morphological character distinguishing *Villora* from other *Etheostoma* subgenera).

- **Species-level differentiation (*E. okaloosae* vs. *E. edwini*):**
  - Distinguished by **scale counts**.
  - Distinguished by body **markings** and **coloration** patterns.
  - Most notably, *E. okaloosae* **lacks the red spots** over the body that are present in *E. edwini*.

- **Taxonomic history:**
  - Originally named *Villora okaloosae* (Fowler, 1941), based on a single type specimen.
  - Temporarily synonymized under *[[Etheostoma swaini]]* (Bailey, Winn & Smith, 1954).
  - Re-validated as a distinct species by Yerger (1960) based on expanded specimen series (n=140).
  - *Villora* is now treated as one of many subgenera within the broadly expanded genus **[[Etheostoma]]**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Clear, sandy-bottomed, moderately swift-flowing freshwater streams.
- **Geographic range:** Restricted to several small streams that empty into a bayou at the western end of **[[Choctawhatchee Bay]]**, Florida.
- **Endemism:** Highly range-restricted; considered endemic to Northwest Florida.
- **Sympatric/related taxon:** *[[Etheostoma edwini]]* is the only other member of subgenus *Villora*, occupying a broader or different regional distribution for comparison.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic validity restored:** Analysis of 140 specimens across seven collection series confirms *[[Etheostoma okaloosae]]* is a valid species distinct from *E. swaini*, overturning the 1954 synonymy.
2. **Correct subgeneric assignment:** The species is properly placed within subgenus **[[Villora]]**, characterized by the arched lateral line and modified female genital papilla (villi ring).
3. **Diagnostic distinction from congener:** *E. okaloosae* is separable from *E. edwini* via scale counts, coloration, and markings — particularly the absence of red body spots.
4. **Biogeographic origin — Pleistocene vicariance model:**
   - The species' origin is hypothesized to date to the **late Pleistocene**, during a period of **lower sea level** when currently isolated streams were connected (conjoined drainage).
   - Subsequent **sea level rise** during the recent interglacial stage fragmented the ancestral population into several **isolated small stream populations**, consistent with an allopatric speciation/vicariance model.
5. **Conservation implication (implicit):** As a narrow endemic restricted to a few small, isolated streams, *E. okaloosae* represents a geographically vulnerable population — a precursor observation to its later federal listing as a threatened species.
