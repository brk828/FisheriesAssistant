# Upper Mississippi River Enhancement Initiatives and Stewardship Plans

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-habitat

## Executive Summary

A 1999 [[Quad City Times]] article reports that [[Jeff Stein]], a 29-year-old river advocate hired from Washington, D.C., will coordinate efforts to enhance the [[Upper Mississippi River]] from an office in downtown Davenport. His stated priorities are wetland and fish and waterfowl habitat protection, reconnecting the river with its floodplains, reducing sediment and nutrient loading, and coordinating federal programs. The article also lists community riverfront projects in 57 designated [[American Heritage River]] communities. The document is a news article, not a research study, and contains no quantitative ecological survey data.

## Background & Description

- **Source type:** Newspaper feature (Quad City Times, March 14, 1999), three pages.
- **Central figure:** [[Jeff Stein]], hired to coordinate river enhancement, based in the [[Quad-Cities]].
- **Program framework:** The [[American Heritage River]] designation covers 57 communities along the Upper Mississippi River. The shared goal, coordinated through [[American Rivers]], is to increase river access, enhance local parks, trails and greenways, and protect and interpret resources.
- **Regional initiatives (six):**
  - Regional marketing strategy for recreation and tourism
  - Promotion of economic development and riverfront revitalization
  - Linkage of existing trails and greenways
  - Improvement of interpretive centers
  - Restoration of natural resources
  - Sharing of successful projects through a "Bridging the River" project

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Stein states that the Upper Mississippi is "losing wetlands and fish and waterfowl habitat" and stresses the need to steward the river without destroying natural ecological processes. Key concerns include:

- **Sediment and nutrient loading:** Increased levels are partly caused by fertilizer runoff from farm fields. Farmers, shippers, recreation users, and environmentalists all have a stake in solutions. A proposal to coordinate federal programs on this issue is in preparation.
- **Floodplain reconnection:** A priority is reconnecting the river with its floodplains. Options range from relocating residents out of flood-prone areas to less extreme measures such as [[Gated Levees]].
- **Shoreline erosion:** A local call reported an eroding island, illustrating that citizen group organization is needed to mobilize action.
- **Federal support:** A federal navigator will help communities secure funding and navigate regulatory processes, and Stein plans to coordinate with this role.

## Key Findings

**Stewardship priorities for [[Jeff Stein]]:**

1. Work with mayors of Upper Mississippi communities under the American Heritage River effort.
2. Address sediment and nutrient problems through a proposal to coordinate federal programs.
3. Protect and preserve habitat in partnership with communities, states, and the federal government, with floodplain reconnection as a top priority.
4. Engage local citizens, since organized groups are more effective than individuals in pursuing projects.

**Selected community projects (summary of the 2-page project list):**

- **[[Clinton]]:** Master landscape plan and expansion of [[Riverview Park]] (65 acres), including a veterans' memorial, information kiosk, Discovery Trail signage, and marina improvements.
- **[[Princeton]]:** Linking pocket parks into a continuous linear riverfront, with city gateways.
- **[[LeClaire]]:** Expanding the levee area to create riverfront green space and a recreational promenade.
- **[[Davenport]]:** Minor league baseball stadium expansion, relocation and expansion of the Museum of Art, and an [[Environmental Protection Agency]] lead cleanup project.
- **[[Cordova]]:** Funding its portion of the [[Great River Trail]] through an existing municipal greenway, with planning by the [[Bi-State Regional Commission]].
- **[[Buffalo]]:** Bike trail connecting regional trails through Davenport, Scott County, and down the river to Muscatine, Iowa.
- **[[Fulton]]:** A 90-foot windmill, enhanced adjacent park, and marina and Great River Trail improvements.
- **[[Port Byron]]:** Public boat ramp improvements and two land acquisitions for riverfront green space.
- **[[Rapids City]]:** Second-phase public boat launch improvements, including a Victorian-era gazebo.
- **[[Albany]]:** Improved parking, boat access, and a five-block riverfront area planted with native plantings.
- **[[Bellevue]]:** Riverfront park improvements for fishing, camping, swimming, and boating, plus conversion of railroad tracks into a hiking and biking trail.
- **[[Muscatine]]:** Marina revitalization, civic plaza, interpretive walkways, and a pedestrian bridge under the Consensus Plan.
- **[[East Moline]]:** Riverfront Parkway and Beacon Harbor public riverfront plaza, with future phases including a Regional Sports Center and [[Mississippi River Interpretive Center]].
- **[[Rock Island]] and [[Moline]]:** Trail spur and riverfront project east of the Arsenal Bridge, including a 10-acre park.
- **[[Quad-Cities]] region:** Multi-use riverfront trail connecting Bettendorf and Davenport, and a [[Quad-City Botanical Center]] near the river.
- **[[Lock And Dam 12]]:** Visitor center improvements and new recreational boating facilities.

**Overall conclusion:** The article presents a coordinated, community-driven strategy that balances economic development and recreation with habitat restoration and sediment and nutrient control. It contains no measured ecological data on fish or other aquatic taxa.
