## Executive Summary

This 1997 Arizona Game and Fish Department memo outlines proposed amendments to **Commission Order 40: Fish**, the state's 1998 sport-fishing regulations. Although framed as a regulatory/administrative document rather than a primary research paper, it contains substantial applied ichthyological content: it documents management conflicts between non-native sport fish (bass, catfish, trout, striped bass) and native/endangered fish species in the [[Gila River Basin]], [[Colorado River]] (Grand Canyon reach), [[Verde River]], [[Salt River]], and [[Little Colorado River]] watersheds. Proposals center on liberalizing harvest of non-native predatory/competitor fish to benefit federally listed species such as the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], while also addressing live-bait transport restrictions to prevent unauthorized introductions, and reclassifying several protected native species (e.g., [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]).

## Background & Description

The source is a policy memo and does not provide morphometric or anatomical data. However, it references several taxa with distinct known characteristics relevant to management:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) – a federally endangered [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] characterized by a pronounced nuchal hump; endemic to swift, turbid Colorado River canyon reaches.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) – a large [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] with a distinctive sharp-edged dorsal keel behind the head; historically widespread in the Colorado River Basin.
- **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*) – a small-bodied cyprinid noted for spine-like anterior dorsal rays, restricted to cool, clear tributary streams.
- **[[Apache Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus apache*) – Arizona's native salmonid, distinguished from introduced rainbow and brown trout by yellow-gold coloration and lack of extensive spotting.
- Non-native taxa discussed for unlimited harvest include largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, channel catfish, flathead catfish, striped bass, and rainbow/brown trout — all standard introduced warmwater/coldwater sport fish morphotypes, not described in morphological detail in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fish refugia**: [[Aravaipa Creek]] and [[Sycamore Creek]] (Santa Cruz County) were proposed for closure to fishing (joining [[Cienega Creek]]) because they are inhabited primarily by native fish assemblages with minimal recreational fishing pressure, reducing risk of accidental non-native introductions (e.g., green sunfish, bullheads).
- **Predation pressure**: Non-native warmwater sport fish (bass, catfish) in the [[Gila River]], [[Virgin River]], [[Salt River]], [[Verde River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Blue River]], Campbell Blue Creek, and Eagle Creek are identified as predators/competitors threatening federally designated critical habitat for threatened and endangered native fish.
- **Colorado River/Grand Canyon reach** (Separation Canyon to Marble Canyon Bridge, later reduced to Mile 21 Rapids): critical habitat for [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]; unlimited harvest of trout, channel catfish, and striped bass proposed to reduce competitive/predatory pressure.
- **Coldwater tributary systems**: [[East Clear Creek]], Nutrioso Creek, and [[Chevelon Creek]] are managed rainbow trout put-and-take fisheries that also constitute critical habitat for [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]; a seasonal (Sept 1–May 1) unlimited harvest window for rainbow/brown trout was proposed to reduce trout predation on spinedace outside the peak summer angling season, while preserving Apache trout (a native congener planned for future reintroduction) from unlimited take.
- **Bait-fish vectors**: Live bait fish transport restrictions on the [[Verde River]] (above Tuzigoot Bridge), [[Salt River]] (above Roosevelt Diversion Dam), and [[Gila River]] (NM border to San Carlos Indian Reservation) aim to prevent unauthorized introduction of non-native baitfish into reaches supporting dense native fish populations and reintroduction efforts (e.g., razorback sucker reintroduction above Tuzigoot Bridge).
- **Reservoir fishery**: [[Granite Basin Lake]] (Yavapai County, ~5 acres, Prescott National Forest) supports a put-and-grow channel catfish fishery maintained by hatchery fingerling stocking, plus largemouth bass and sunfish, illustrating a managed non-native put-and-take niche distinct from native-fish recovery waters.

## Key Findings

- **Stream closures**: Aravaipa Creek (Pinal/Graham Co.) and Sycamore Creek (Santa Cruz Co.) recommended for closure to fishing to protect native fish, adopted as proposed.
- **Unlimited non-native harvest (warmwater)**: Adopted for Gila River, Virgin River, Salt River, Verde River, San Francisco River, Blue River, Campbell Blue Creek, and Eagle Creek for largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, channel catfish, and flathead catfish — but the "must keep all fish caught" clause was dropped due to conflict with catch-and-release ethics and Title 17-309A.5 (waste of game fish).
- **Grand Canyon reach**: Unlimited harvest of trout, channel catfish, and striped bass adopted for Colorado River from Separation Canyon to Mile 21 Rapids (reduced from Marble Canyon Bridge), including tributaries, to benefit humpback chub and razorback sucker; mandatory-keep clause dropped.
- **Seasonal trout harvest**: Adopted for East Clear Creek, Nutrioso Creek, and Chevelon Creek — unlimited harvest of *rainbow and brown trout only* (Sept 1–May 1) to protect Little Colorado River spinedace, explicitly excluding Apache trout in anticipation of native trout reintroduction; mandatory-keep clause dropped; standard six-trout bag limit retained for other trout species/areas.
- **Live bait restrictions**: Adopted for Verde River (above Tuzigoot Bridge), Salt River (above Roosevelt Diversion Dam), and Gila River (NM border to San Carlos Reservation), restricting bait to fish caught on-site; redundant "no stocking live bait back into river" clauses were dropped as unnecessary.
- **Trout possession limit**: Proposal to raise the two-day possession limit from 6 to 12 trout was **rejected**; Department retained the conservative six-trout limit, citing consistency with the Wildlife 2000 Plan's catch-and-release philosophy despite public survey support (~50%).
- **Granite Basin Lake catfish limit**: New four-catfish daily bag/possession limit adopted with strong public support (90%) due to declining catch size/quality amid heavy stocking-supported use.
- **Protected species list update**: Adopted addition of [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and [[Gila Topminnow]] to the protected species list, while deleting the taxonomically outdated "Sonoran topminnow" designation — a taxonomic/regulatory clarification with no ecological objection raised.
- **Public engagement metrics**: Department reviewed input from 89 attendees at 8 public meetings, 7 letters, 15 emails, 3 internet forums, 3 radio programs, 12 newspapers, and 416 of 1,800 mailed angler surveys returned — reflecting a broadly consultative regulatory process balancing native species recovery mandates (Endangered Species Act, critical habitat designations) against traditional sport-fishing interests.
