# Summary of 2022 Northern Pike Suppression Results on Box Canyon Reservoir, Pend Oreille River, WA

#ichthyology #Esocidae #invasive_species

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) is an invasive predatory fish in [[Box Canyon Reservoir]]. During 2022 suppression, males averaged 587 mm TL (23 inches) and 1,430 g (3.1 lbs.), while females averaged 627 mm TL (25 inches) and 1,800 g (4.0 lbs.). Overall size range was 330–1,045 mm (13–41 inches), weight 144–9,900 g (0.3–22.0 lbs.). Sex ratio was 50% female. Juveniles (<450 mm) comprised 8% of suppression catch and 3% of SPIN survey catch. Of females captured during suppression, 12% were immature, 84% mature, 2% ripe, and 2% spawned out. Of males, 10% were immature, 61% mature, 29% ripe, and 0% spawned out. The program removed 337 mature pre-spawn females and 49 immature females in 2022. Age and fecundity parameters not reported.

**Habitat Associations:** [[Northern Pike]] utilized known spawning locations throughout [[Box Canyon Reservoir]], including sloughs and mainstem river sections. Netting targeted spawning habitat between February 28 and April 21, coinciding with reproductive period. The reservoir is divided into management reaches: north of Riverbend (RM 60-61) and sloughs/mainstem south of Riverbend to Pioneer Park Campground (RM 86-87).

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** Not reported. Suppression efforts focused on spawning aggregations, suggesting predictable seasonal movements to spawning habitat during late winter/early spring (February–April).

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** [[Northern Pike]] is a top predator in [[Box Canyon Reservoir]]. Bycatch data reveal a diverse fish community of 19 species (5,799 individuals) including native salmonids ([[Rainbow Trout]], [[Westslope Cutthroat Trout]], [[Lake Whitefish]]), other predators ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Walleye]]), and abundant forage species ([[Yellow Perch]] 46.56% of bycatch, [[Pumpkinseed]] 11.33%, [[Black Crappie]] 9.67%). The invasive [[Tench]] comprised 19.68% of bycatch. Specific predation impacts not quantified in this report.

**Environmental Drivers:** Not reported. Timing of suppression (February 28–April 21) and SPIN survey (May 02–05) aligned with spawning phenology, suggesting temperature and photoperiod cues drive reproductive behavior.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** Since 2012, 19,430 [[Northern Pike]] have been removed from [[Box Canyon Reservoir]]. The 2022 suppression effort removed 814 individuals (1.20 mT) with CPUE of 1.3 NP/net-night. The Spring Pike Index Netting (SPIN) survey yielded CPUE of 0.10 NP/net-night north of Riverbend (target ≤0.50) and 0.26 NP/net-night (95% CI: ±0.16) in sloughs and south of Riverbend (target ≤1.73), both meeting abundance targets. Historical data (Figure 1) show declining CPUE from 2012–2022, indicating suppression effectiveness. Peak catch was 22 pike in one net during suppression; maximum during SPIN was 3.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** [[Northern Pike]] poses threat to native fish communities in [[Box Canyon Reservoir]] as an invasive apex predator. The species' high fecundity (85% of captured females were mature, ripe, or spawned out) and established spawning populations necessitate ongoing suppression. Bycatch survival rates exceeded 90%, indicating minimal non-target mortality from suppression activities.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations:** Continue mechanical suppression targeting spawning aggregations during February–April using gill nets deployed at known spawning locations. Maintain two 3-person crews working 4 days/week deploying 13–40 nets/day (626 nets total in 2022). Conduct annual SPIN surveys in May using spatially balanced random sampling design (63 nets in 2022) to assess population status relative to abundance targets. Focus removal effort on mature pre-spawn females to reduce reproductive potential. Monitor bycatch composition and survival to ensure minimal impacts to non-target species, particularly native salmonids.

**Biological Reference Points:** Abundance target for mainstem north of Riverbend: ≤0.50 NP/net-night (2022 result: 0.10). Abundance target for sloughs and mainstem south of Riverbend: ≤1.73 NP/net-night (2022 result: 0.26, 95% CI: ±0.16). Both targets met in 2022. Suppression CPUE of 1.3 NP/net-night represents continued population control. Cumulative removal of 19,430 individuals since 2012 demonstrates long-term commitment required for invasive species management.
