# Application of Water Quality Models to a Small Forested Watershed: The Nondesignated 208 Area Screening Model

#aquatic-ecology #water-quality-modeling #watershed-hydrology

## Executive Summary

This EPA Environmental Research Laboratory project summary, prepared by J. Hesson and J. K. Robertson of the [[United States Military Academy]] Science Research Laboratory, tests whether the [[Water Quality Assessment Methodology]] (WOAM) can screen nonpoint-source pollution in a small, mostly forested watershed. The study area is the [[West Point Study Area]] in the [[Hudson Highlands]], New York. The authors used field data to calibrate and test WOAM predictions of sediment, nutrients, organic matter, lake thermal structure, and dissolved oxygen. They concluded that WOAM, as written, cannot reliably model nonpoint waste loads on steeply sloped forested land. They recommended model revisions and further field verification, and they suggested that the [[Water Resources Evaluation of Non-Point Silvicultural Sources]] (WRENSS) model may be better suited to forested terrain.

## Background & Description

**Purpose.** [[Basin Planning]] under the [[Section 208 Water Quality Program]] requires tools that estimate the environmental effects of different control strategies and their costs. Planners need models that work with limited data. The report focuses on the "Nondesignated 208 Area," meaning areas outside the designated urban planning zones, which often have forested terrain.

**Study area.** The West Point Study Area is a small watershed draining to the [[Popolopen Lake]] system.
- Elevations are described as hilly and steep, with shallow soils overlying bedrock.
- Forest cover is extensive, and agriculture is minor.
- The watershed contains six lakes or ponds. Five of the six are manmade impoundments. [[Bull Pond]] was used for detailed lake model testing.
- Streams are rocky and flashy, responding quickly to storms.
- Climate is humid continental. Summers are warm, with occasional spells in the nineties °F. Winters are cold, with precipitation distributed fairly evenly through the year.

**Models compared.**
- **WOAM** (Water Quality Assessment Methodology) is the primary model. It estimates nonpoint loads using the [[Universal Soil Loss Equation]] (USLE) and includes lake eutrophication, thermal stratification, and dissolved oxygen (DO) submodels.
- **WRENSS** (EPA-600/8-80-012) was designed for forested areas and large regions. The authors borrowed its procedures to patch WOAM where it failed.

**Model input categories.** The authors grouped data needs into four classes:
1. Hydrologic and geographic inputs for the watershed, including rainfall, streamflow, and land use.
2. Meteorological inputs, including temperature, dew point, radiation, and wind speed.
3. Physical inputs, including lake volume, depth, and residence time.
4. Water quality inputs, both chemical and biological, plus constants such as reaeration rate coefficients.

Field data were collected at the study area's stations and used to calibrate the model and provide a basis for future model testing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Nonpoint loading.** Forested watersheds are often assumed to produce low sediment yields and minimal nutrient export. The authors questioned this assumption, since WOAM procedures were developed largely for agricultural and urban conditions. Erosion control practice factors and topographic factors for steep forested slopes were uncertain.

**Predicted nonpoint loads.** WOAM predictions for the study area are summarized in Table 1. Values for the southern area (sub-areas 1 to 4) and the entire study area (sub-areas 1 to 8) were:

| Parameter | Southern Area (sub-areas 1–4) | Entire Study Area (sub-areas 1–8) |
|---|---|---|
| Sediment (tons/year) | 621 | 2,115 |
| Sediment (tons/acre-year) | 0.66 | 0.65 |
| Nitrogen (lb/year) | 5,811 | 19,839 |
| Nitrogen (lb/acre-year) | 6.1 | 6.1 |
| Phosphorus (lb/year) | 1,305 | 4,442 (OCR partly illegible) |
| Phosphorus (lb/acre-year) | 1.38 | 1.37 |
| Organic matter (lb/year) | 93,150 | 317,250 |
| Organic matter (lb/acre-year) | 98.5 | 97.7 |

The per-acre values are nearly identical between the two areas, which suggests the southern sub-areas are representative of the whole basin.

**Lake thermal and oxygen dynamics.** Bull Pond was used to test lake models. Thermal profiles from May 1977 to April 1978 were compared with predictions under varying degrees of mixing, shown in Figure 3. The [[Thermal Stratification]] model gave a reasonable match with field conditions. The DO model approximated mid-summer DO levels well, but it overpredicted DO when the benthic oxygen demand was set to zero. The observed difference at Bull Pond was almost 1.0 mg/L.

**Eutrophication.** WOAM eutrophication predictions are valid only for phosphorus-limited lakes. The authors noted that nitrogen-limited lakes, where neither phosphorus nor nitrogen controls are dominant, cannot be modeled.

## Key Findings

**Assessment of WOAM for forested watersheds**

1. **Topography.** The WOAM topographic factor (LS) is difficult to assign for steep forested slopes. The authors recommended adopting the WRENSS LS procedure for steep forested areas only.
2. **Erosion control practice factor (P).** WOAM gives no clear guidance on setting P for forested land. Procedures need to be clarified.
3. **Sediment delivery.** The WOAM sediment delivery ratio gives sediment loads higher than the authors considered credible. The WRENSS sediment delivery index, which uses eight forest parameters, appears better suited to forests.
4. **Lake eutrophication.** The model works only under phosphorus-limited conditions and needs extension.
5. **Lake thermal profiles.** The thermal model gives a reasonable prediction when applied to the study area.
6. **Dissolved oxygen.** The DO prediction was verified for one lake, Bull Pond. Setting the benthic oxygen demand (K_d) to zero is not justified for all lakes. Table V-10 of the WOAM documentation should be used instead of Equation 17-13 to assign the L_d value.
7. **Overall caution.** WOAM should be applied to forested watersheds with caution until it is more thoroughly tested and verified.

**Conclusions**

- Nonpoint waste loading on the West Point Study Area, or on any steeply sloped area, cannot be modeled with WOAM without extending or modifying its algorithms.
- The WRENSS model may be the better alternative for forested terrain, but it needs field verification.
- The data requirements for existing impoundments are high. The authors believe many predictions would be impractical, since measuring a thermal profile is cheaper than obtaining the volume and residence-time data needed for modeling.
- For planned impoundments, volumes and residence times are known, which makes prediction easier.

**Recommendations**

1. Collect further field data on the West Point Study Area to verify WOAM nonpoint waste-load predictions.
2. Revise WOAM procedures to guide users on setting the erosion control practice factor (P) for forested terrain.
3. Add the WRENSS LS and sediment delivery index (SDI) procedures to WOAM for steep forested areas only.
4. Apply and verify WRENSS on the West Point Study Area, then verify the revised WOAM on the same watershed to see which model predicts forested water quality better.
5. Replace the generalized average annual rainfall factor (R) in WOAM documentation with the maps in USDA Agriculture Handbook 537.
