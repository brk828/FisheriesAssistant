## Executive Summary
This 1958 study by E. E. Prather (Auburn Polytechnic Institute) evaluated the effects of different feed formulations and physical forms on the production of [[Fathead Minnow]] in pond aquaculture. Twelve 0.25-acre ponds were stocked with fatheads (1.74 lbs/1,000 fish) at 100,000 fish/acre and fed one of four rations — dry mix, crumbles, or 3/4-inch pellets of "Auburn No. 2" fish feed, or a commercial trout pellet — six days per week from January 21 to April 1, 1958. No fertilizers were applied, isolating feed as the primary growth variable. Results showed that all three forms of Auburn No. 2 feed produced significantly greater weight gains than the commercial trout ration (P=0.01), while differences among the three Auburn No. 2 forms (dry mix, crumbles, pellets) were not statistically significant, though pellets trended highest in both survival and gain. This work builds on Prather's earlier research on [[Auburn No. 1 Fish Feed]] for winter feeding of small fatheads.

## Background & Description
The paper does not describe morphological traits of *Pimephales promelas* itself in detail, but reports biometric production data:

- **Initial stocking weight:** 1.74 lbs per 1,000 fish (small juvenile/subadult fatheads)
- **Final average weight per 1,000 fish:**
  - Pellets: 3.64 lbs
  - Crumbles: 3.64 lbs
  - Dry Mix: ~3.5 lbs
  - Trout Ration: 2.94 lbs
- **Feed particle morphology (not fish, but relevant to feeding ecology):**
  - Dry mix: fine, unformed particles
  - Crumbles: irregular particles, 0.1–0.2 inch largest dimension
  - Pellets: 3/4-inch diameter, ~1 inch long
  - Trout pellets: hard, round, ~0.05 inch diameter

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Fathead Minnow]] is widely used as a forage/bait fish species in pond culture across the southeastern United States, making feed efficiency and production density central management concerns for [[Game and Fish Commissions]] and aquaculturists. Key ecological/behavioral observations from this study:

- Fatheads exhibited surface/near-surface feeding behavior, consuming a substantial portion of the fine dry mix **before it sank** to the pond bottom.
- Crumbles and pellets sank more quickly and were apparently **not consumed until softened** on the pond bottom, suggesting benthic feeding on softened particulate matter.
- Feed particle size appears to influence foraging efficiency and effective feed capture rate, independent of nutrient composition.
- Ponds were unfertilized, meaning natural planktonic/detrital food inputs were minimized, isolating the artificial feed as the dominant nutrient source — relevant to [[Pond Aquaculture Nutrient Dynamics]].
- No significant differences in survival were detected across treatments, though survival trended highest with pellets (98.8%) and lowest with crumbles (90.7%).

## Key Findings

**Feed Formulations Compared:**
| Feed | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat | Fiber | Cost/lb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Auburn No. 2 (all forms) | 46% | 26% | 5% | — | $0.055 (dry mix), $0.06 (crumbles/pellets) |
| Commercial Trout Ration | 43% | 20% (NFE) | 3.25% | 4% | $0.17 |

**Auburn No. 2 Ingredients:**
- 35% Soybean Oil Meal (44% protein)
- 35% Ground Peanut Cake (53% protein)
- 15% Fish Meal (60% protein)
- 15% Distillers Dried Solubles (24% protein)

**Production Results (per acre, average of 3 ponds/treatment):**
| Item | Dry Mix | Crumbles | Pellets | Trout Ration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| % Survival | 93.6 | 90.7 | 98.8 | 93.4 |
| Pounds Recovered | 305.0 | 320.4 | 342.5 | 274.9 |
| Pounds Gained | 131.0 | 146.4 | 168.5 | 100.9 |
| Avg. Wt./1,000 fish (lbs) | ~3.5 | 3.64 | 3.64 | 2.94 |
| Feed Used (lbs) | 380.0 | 380.0 | 380.0 | 380.0 |
| Conversion Factor | 2.59 | 2.26 | ~2.3 | 3.77 |
| Cost/lb fish produced | $0.16 | $0.15 | $0.13 | $0.64 |

**Statistical Significance:**
- All Auburn No. 2 forms (combined) produced significantly greater weight gain than the trout ration (F=15.41, P=0.01).
- No significant differences in gain among the three Auburn No. 2 forms (dry mix vs. crumbles vs. pellets).
- No significant differences in survival among any of the four treatments.

**Interpretation:**
- The poor performance of the trout ration was hypothesized to relate to its **antibiotic feed supplements**, with the author noting evidence that long-term antibiotic feeding may suppress fish growth rates — a possible link to [[Antibiotic Effects on Fish Growth]].
- Feed cost-efficiency strongly favored the Auburn No. 2 formulations over the commercial trout pellet ($0.13–$0.16/lb fish produced vs. $0.64/lb).
- Physical form (particle size, sink rate) of feed influenced foraging success and apparent feed conversion, even when nutrient composition was held constant, highlighting the importance of feed engineering in [[Fish Feed Formulation]] alongside nutritional content.
- This study directly follows and extends findings from Prather's 1957 paper on [[Auburn No. 1 Fish Feed]], refining the formulation (peanut cake vs. peanut meal) to reduce fiber content while maintaining performance.
