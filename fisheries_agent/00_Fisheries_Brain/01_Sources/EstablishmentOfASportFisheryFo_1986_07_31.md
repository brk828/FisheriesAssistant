# Establishment of a Sport Fishery for Colorado Squawfish in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This 1986 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) documents an administrative and regulatory initiative to expand a sport fishery for the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] into the lower [[Colorado River]] in Arizona. The action was prompted by a request from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and represents an early example of using the Endangered Species Act's "experimental population" designation to balance recreational fisheries management with recovery efforts for a federally listed species. The memo outlines plans to amend an existing experimental listing (previously limited to the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]]) to include the mainstream Colorado River from below [[Davis Dam]] to the International Boundary with Mexico, supported by hatchery production capacity at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type:** Large, warmwater, mainstem river systems characteristic of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Historical/Current Range Discussed:** 
  - [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] (Arizona) — sites of the original 1985 experimental listing.
  - Lower [[Colorado River]], specifically the reach from below [[Davis Dam]] to the International Boundary — proposed expansion area.
- **Population Status:** Listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act; the wild population targeted for reintroduction/sport fishery establishment would be managed under an "experimental, nonessential" designation, a regulatory mechanism that allows for more flexible management (including angling) without full ESA protections applying to the introduced population.
- **Propagation Source:** [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter NFH, New Mexico) reported capability to produce "millions" of Colorado squawfish fry for stocking purposes, indicating an active captive breeding/conservation aquaculture program supporting reintroduction efforts.

## Key Findings
- The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] formally requested USFWS assistance to establish a **recreational sport fishery** for the endangered Colorado Squawfish in the lower Colorado River — described as a "bold and innovative" conservation approach.
- USFWS Region 2 endorsed the proposal and committed to providing full support toward implementation.
- The existing **experimental population listing** (finalized September 1985) covering the Salt and Verde Rivers was proposed for amendment to also include the mainstream Colorado River between [[Davis Dam]] and the International Boundary.
- The regulatory amendment was to be processed via a **Federal Register Notice**, with an estimated timeline of **6–8 months** to finalize the rule establishing "experimental nonessential" status for the expanded population.
- If the regulatory timeline held, fish stocking from Dexter NFH into Arizona waters was anticipated to begin in **spring 1987**.
- The memo demonstrates coordination among multiple stakeholders: [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and an academic/scientific advisor, Dr. W.L. Minckley (a noted ichthyologist specializing in southwestern U.S. fishes), who was copied on the correspondence.
- This document is a useful historical artifact illustrating early application of the ESA's **10(j) experimental population** provisions to reconcile endangered species recovery with recreational fisheries interests in the American Southwest.
