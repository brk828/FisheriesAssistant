# Interagency MOU for Non-Game Fish Introductions on Public Lands in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #non-game-fish

## Executive Summary

This document comprises an August 1982 [[Instruction Memo]] (No. AZ-82-219) from the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) Arizona State Office and the enclosed [[Memorandum Of Understanding]] between BLM and the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AG&FD). The MOU sets out responsibilities and procedures for processing proposed introductions of non-game fish species on BLM-administered lands in Arizona. Species covered are limited to non-game fish not listed under the [[Endangered Species Act Of 1973]]. The stated aim is to establish fish populations while preserving multiple use of desert waters, and to avoid introductions that would create conflict or controversy.

## Background & Description

The memo was issued on August 10, 1982 (Instruction Memo No. AZ-82-219, expires 9/30/83) by the State Director to the BLM District Managers. It transmits the MOU and explains how it should be applied. Distribution included WO-320-1 and D-558A-3.

The MOU is a cooperative agreement between BLM and the Arizona Game and Fish Commission, acting under Section 17-231(b) of the [[Arizona Revised Statutes]]. Its purpose is to set responsibilities and procedures for proposed introductions and reintroductions of non-game fish. The document contains no species list, site list, or stocking numbers. It is a procedural and legal framework.

The division of roles is central to the agreement:
- **AG&FD** takes the lead on species management, including preparing introduction plans, contacting water users and rights holders, and running public information efforts.
- **BLM** completes internal documentation, provides habitat and public land management input, and monitors habitat condition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The MOU is framed around desert aquatic systems and the need to balance new fish populations with existing water uses. It requires:
- Environmental assessment before each introduction or group of introductions. This must address environmental impacts, possible land use conflicts, impacts on the target species, and multiple use values of the sites.
- Joint evaluation of release sites under criteria developed by AG&FD, with proposed locations prioritized at district and state levels.
- Protection of valid existing uses. These may be altered or curtailed only through an approved or amended Habitat Management Plan and with full public disclosure.
- Limits on introductions that could jeopardize the success of other introductions under the MOU.

The memo states that full public disclosure of any proposal to use a water source is "important and mandatory" under the MOU.

## Key Findings

**Scope and governance**
- The MOU covers only non-game fish species not listed under the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (as amended).
- Introduction decisions rest with the BLM District Manager's discretion. They should be made only after environmental assessment, public input, and liaison with water users and rights holders.
- Decisions must be agreed upon by both agencies after a complete analysis of the required steps.

**Responsibilities**
- The State will develop site selection criteria for each fish species, supply fish from an appropriate source, introduce them to approved Bureau sites, periodically monitor populations, maintain stocking and monitoring records, and limit introductions that could jeopardize other MOU projects.
- The Bureau will complete internal documents, monitor habitat condition, and assist with population monitoring on request.

**Planning and approval**
- Habitat management objectives and introduction plans are developed jointly in each BLM district. They require approval by the District Manager (BLM) and the Regional Supervisor (AG&FD).
- A public information program is a required part of every district plan. It includes notice to interested parties, especially those with existing uses or claims to the water. News releases must be coordinated and approved by both agencies before release.

**Protections and legal terms**
- Existing uses are protected unless changed through an approved Habitat Management Plan with full public disclosure.
- The agreement creates no obligation to spend funds beyond appropriations authorized by law.
- The MOU takes effect on the date of the latest signature and may be amended or cancelled by any party on 30 days' written notice.
- The Commission's obligations are subject to Arizona Executive Order No. 75-5 (April 28, 1975). The agreement may be cancelled by the Governor under Arizona Revised Statutes Section 38-511.
- Under Section 11-952 of the Arizona Revised Statutes, the agreement must be filed with the Arizona Secretary of State and is not effective until filed.

**Signatories**
- The MOU is signed by the State Director of BLM Arizona and the Director of the Arizona Game and Fish Department. Signature dates are partially illegible in the scanned copy.
