# Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center Management Objectives and Budget Planning Memorandum

#adaptive-management #endangered-species #humpback-chub #native-fish #dam-operations

## Executive Summary

This May 1998 memorandum from the [[Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center]] (GCMRC) to the Technical Work Group presents revised Management Objectives and Information Needs for the [[Adaptive Management Program]] (AMP) following [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. The document establishes prioritized research and monitoring needs across biological, cultural, physical, and socioeconomic resources for FY2000-2004, with particular emphasis on [[Humpback Chub]] and other endangered species. A volunteer stakeholder group ranked 55+ information needs by time-priority for implementation, with highest priorities (8-14 votes) focused on endangered species biology, cultural resources, water quality at [[Lake Powell]], and aquatic productivity. The memorandum proposes FY2000 budget scenarios ranging from $7.96M to $9.94M to address stakeholder-identified information needs while staging research over a 5-year period.

## Background & Description

The [[Adaptive Management Work Group]] (AMWG) requested in July 1997 that the Technical Work Group (TWG) evaluate and revise Management Objectives and Information Needs for the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. Approximately 10-25 TWG members met four times over three months to address two primary issues: (1) Management Objectives/Information Needs revision and (2) Out Year Budget Planning for FY2000-2004.

The revised objectives represent stakeholder consensus on desired resource conditions and specific monitoring/science information necessary to determine resource status and effects of dam management actions. The document covers multiple resource categories:

**Aquatic Resources**: Including aquatic food base ([[Cladophora]], invertebrates), [[Rainbow Trout]] in [[Glen Canyon]], native fish (particularly [[Humpback Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]]), and [[Razorback Sucker]].

**Terrestrial/Riparian Resources**: Native and special status species including [[Kanab Ambersnail]], [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Bald Eagle]], leopard frogs, and riparian vegetation communities.

**Physical Resources**: Water quality, sediment resources, sandbars, backwaters, and beach dynamics.

**Cultural Resources**: Archaeological sites, traditional cultural properties, and Native American concerns.

**Lake Powell**: Water quality (limnology, chemical constituents including selenium, hydrogen sulfide), aquatic ecosystem, and fishery impacts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Glen Canyon Dam]] Final Environmental Impact Statement established an Adaptive Management Program to monitor effects of dam operating criteria on downstream resources. The program operates under the [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] of 1992 and "Law of the River" constraints including the [[Colorado River Compact]], [[Upper Colorado River Basin Compact]], and related legislation.

Key ecological concerns include:

**Flow Regime Impacts**: Dam operations create fluctuating flows (8,000-45,000 cfs range) affecting aquatic habitat, sediment transport, beach formation, and species reproduction. Beach/habitat-building flows (BHBF) of 45,000+ cfs are used periodically to redistribute sediment.

**Temperature Effects**: Cold hypolimnetic releases from [[Lake Powell]] (typically <10°C) affect native warm-water fish reproduction and survival. Selective withdrawal technology is proposed to modify downstream temperatures.

**Sediment Dynamics**: Reduced sediment supply from upstream (trapped in [[Lake Powell]]) combined with dam operations affects sandbar maintenance, camping beaches, and backwater habitat formation. Tributary inputs (particularly [[Paria River]], [[Little Colorado River]]) provide critical sediment contributions.

**Native vs. Non-native Species**: Cold-water [[Rainbow Trout]] populations in [[Glen Canyon]] (target: 100,000 Age II+ fish) contrast with downstream warm-water native fish needs. Competitive and predatory interactions between native and non-native fishes are ongoing concerns.

**Endangered Species**: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Oxyrrhynchus melas*) populations centered at [[Little Colorado River]] confluence require specific habitat conditions. The 1987 baseline population in the lower 1,200m of [[Little Colorado River]] serves as reference. Young-of-year overwintering survival is critical concern, particularly during October-February test flows >25,000 cfs.

## Key Findings

**Prioritization Results**: Fourteen stakeholders ranked information needs using two methods (O-method across all categories; X-method emphasizing within-resource areas, minimum 30% per category). Rankings reflect time-preference for study initiation (2000-2001 vs. 2002-2003), not absolute value differences between information needs.

**Highest Priority Information Needs** (8-14 votes):
- Conceptual model development for [[Colorado River]] ecosystem (14 O votes, 7 X votes)
- Effects of dam operations on [[Humpback Chub]] including selective withdrawal impacts (14/14 votes for IN 5.3)
- Adult HBC population monitoring, life history, reproductive success (10/10 votes)
- Cultural site monitoring for dam operation impacts (13/12 votes)
- [[Kanab Ambersnail]] population and habitat monitoring (11/10 votes)
- Aquatic food base status, trends, and dam operation effects (9-10 votes across multiple INs)
- River-stored sediment balance and monitoring procedures (9-11 votes)
- Water quality monitoring including selenium levels (9/9 votes)

**Native Fish Specific Findings**:
- MO 3: Enhance [[Little Colorado River]] HBC population above 1987 levels (April/May hoop-net monitoring, lower 1,200m, fish >200mm)
- MO 4: Maintain/enhance HBC recruitment in mainstem (size frequency distributions, year-class strength)
- MO 5: Remove jeopardy for HBC through selective withdrawal evaluation and temperature modification
- MO 6: Establish second HBC spawning aggregation downstream of dam
- MO 8: Achieve healthy self-sustaining populations of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], with emphasis on flannelmouth in [[Glen Canyon]]

**Trout Management**: MO 2 specifies maintaining 100,000 Age II+ [[Rainbow Trout]] achieving 18 inches by Age III with mean annual relative weight (Wr) ≥0.90 in [[Glen Canyon]] reach (dam to [[Paria River]] confluence).

**Aquatic Food Base**: MO 1 requires maintaining continuously inundated areas for [[Cladophora]] and aquatic invertebrates at or above 5,000 cfs discharge levels.

**Sediment Resources**:
- MO 1: Maintain long-term balance of river-stored sand supporting maintenance flows (low reservoir years), beach/habitat-building flows (high reservoir years), and flood flows
- MO 2: Maintain number and average size of sandbars/backwaters between 8,000-45,000 cfs stages at 1990/91 research flow levels (minimum)
- MO 3: Periodically increase sandbar size above 20,000 cfs stage to 1990/91 or 1996 BHBF levels when conditions allow

**Budget Scenarios for FY2000**:
- **$9.94M scenario**: Aggressive program addressing ~70% of stakeholder information needs
- **$7.96M scenario**: More conservative, closer to original 1996 projection of $8.15M for year 2000; reduces remote sensing technology from $1M to $400K
- Budget increases driven by Programmatic Agreement ($200K increase) and Biological Opinion requirements ($400K increase), offset by $300K reduction in physical resources
- Current budget does NOT accommodate selective withdrawal activities (anticipated to begin 2001)

**Budget Components** (FY2000 at $7.96M level):
- GCMRC Operations/Personnel: $2,083,000
- Biological Resources Science: $1,500,000
- Physical Resources Science: $700,000 (reduced from $1,175,000 in FY98-99)
- Cultural Resources Science: $300,000
- Lake Powell Program: $325,000
- Remote Monitoring Technology: $400,000
- Logistics: $600,000 (new separate line item)
- Bureau Administration (AMWG/TWG/SAB): $267,000
- Bureau Support Services/Programmatic Agreements: $1,246,000

**Protocol Established**: Annual cycle where TWG reviews unresolved Information Needs and GCMRC accomplishments (September), GCMRC submits Strategic/Annual Plans to TWG (December), TWG advances to AMWG (January), and AMWG recommends to Secretary's Designee for approval.

**Implementation Timeline**: High-priority information needs (8-14 votes) initiate in 2000-2001; lower-priority needs (0-4 votes) delayed to 2002-2003. Less than one-third of specified information needs expected to complete within 5-year planning period; much monitoring continues into extended 10-year program.

**Critical Constraints**: All activities must comply with "Law of the River" including water delivery obligations, [[Colorado River Compact]], [[Water Treaty of 1944 with Mexico]], and [[Colorado River Storage Project Act of 1956]]. Hydropower generation value must be maximized within operational constraints established by Secretary under Section 1804 of [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]].
