# Petalodus jewetti (Miller, 1957) — Fossil Bradyodont Fish

## Executive Summary
This paper describes **Petalodus jewetti**, a new species of fossil [[Bradyodont]] fish belonging to the genus [[Petalodus]], based on a single well-preserved tooth discovered in October 1956 by John Mark Jewett of the State Geological Survey of Kansas. The specimen represents the **first recorded occurrence of a petalodontid fish in Pennsylvanian rocks of Kansas**, extending the known geographic range of this genus, which had previously only been documented from Pennsylvanian strata in Illinois and Carboniferous limestones of Europe (England, Scotland, Ireland, Belgium) and Russia. The tooth was recovered from the **Plattsmouth Limestone** (Oread Formation, Virgilian Series) in Franklin County, Kansas. The species is named in honor of its discoverer.

## Background & Description
**Petalodus (genus-level features, per Owen 1840; Zittel 1913):**
- Teeth compressed antero-posteriorly and laterally elongated
- Crown trapezoidal to parallelogram-shaped with a sharp cutting edge
- Basal margin bears enamel folds
- Root long, slender, truncate or divided distally
- Teeth arranged closely together in the mouth, forming a **solid crushing dental pavement** (an adaptation associated with [[Durophagy]])

**Petalodus jewetti (species-specific, holotype no. 9972):**
- Based on a single tooth with a nearly complete, well-preserved crown; root fragmentary
- **Anterior crown face height:** 25 mm
- **Crown width:** 40 mm
- **Root length (partial, broken at base):** 30 mm
- **Posterior crown face height:** ~35 mm
- **Width-to-height ratio:** 1.60
- Anterior portion of crown convex, with fine crenulations along the superior margin (from wear-exposed enamel tubules)
- Lower margin similarly crenulate
- Four basal enamel folds present
- Posterior crown face roughly parallelogram-shaped in outline
- Root relatively long and thick (diagnostic feature distinguishing it from congeners)

**Differential diagnosis:**
- Distinguished from **P. destructor** (Newberry & Worthen) by a relatively longer root and crown lateral ends not deflected toward the root
- Distinguished from **P. curtus** by a longer, thicker root and overall parallelogram (vs. trapezoidal) crown outline
- Considered most closely related to **P. destructor**, a highly variable species known from Illinois and Pennsylvania

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Body plan:** Depressed, ray-like body with greatly enlarged pectoral fins (per [[Romer 1945]])
- **Lifestyle:** Presumed **bottom-dwelling (benthic)** fish, with habits analogous to modern [[Rays]] and [[Skates]]
- **Feeding strategy:** Dentition formed a continuous crushing pavement, suggesting a diet of hard-shelled benthic invertebrates (durophagous feeding), consistent with life on a marine substrate
- **Paleoenvironment:** Shallow marine, epicontinental sea conditions represented by the limestone facies of the Oread Formation during the Virgilian (Late Pennsylvanian)
- **Biogeographic context:** Genus previously known from Carboniferous limestones of Britain, Ireland, Belgium, Russia, and Pennsylvanian Coal Measures of Illinois and Pennsylvania; described as "rare" in the North American Coal Measures overall

## Key Findings
- **First record** of the genus [[Petalodus]] from Pennsylvanian rocks of Kansas, expanding its known paleogeographic distribution
- Establishes **P. jewetti** as a taxonomically distinct species based on:
  - Relatively longer, thicker root compared to P. destructor and P. curtus
  - Parallelogram crown outline (vs. trapezoidal in P. curtus)
  - Distinct width-to-height ratio (1.60) compared to other described species (see comparative table below)
- Raises an important taphonomic/taxonomic caution: because Petalodus teeth formed a **heterodont crushing pavement**, teeth from different positions within a single individual's mouth may have historically been misidentified as separate species — a pattern also noted in fossil shark taxonomy
- Author argues that despite this caveat, **P. jewetti** merits species status due to clear morphological distinctions and its geographic isolation from all previously known Petalodus occurrences
