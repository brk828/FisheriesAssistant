# A Contribution to the Ichthyology of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This 1902 taxonomic and faunal report by [[Seth Eugene Meek]], published as Field Columbian Museum Publication 65 (Zoological Series, Vol. III, No. 6), presents results of a fish collecting expedition across Mexico in April to June 1901. The collection, made with [[Frank Eugene Lutz]], was described as possibly larger than all other collections combined from Mexican freshwaters at the time. The paper documents the river systems visited, describes several new genera and numerous new species, provides identification keys, and analyses the zoogeographic affinities of the fauna (northern Nearctic, Central and South American, and an endemic Lerma Basin assemblage). The scanned volume includes front matter, library markings and plates; the substantive content is the Meek paper.

## Background & Description

The expedition covered the central Mexican plateau (about 3,500 to 8,000 feet above sea level) and several coastal and lowland drainages. Collecting was done mainly with two seines (25 and about 50 feet long, 1/4-inch mesh), supplemented by dip-netting and local information from railway agents, fishermen and residents. Sampling took place late in the dry season, when many streams were low or dry, which concentrated fish in residual pools.

Meek's methods section defines standard measurements: body length from the tip of the upper jaw to the base of the caudal fin, total length to the tip of caudal rays, head length to the posterior opercular edge, and lateral-line scale counts to the caudal base. Localities are cited by nearest town, stream and collection date.

Key taxonomic novelties include:
- **New genera:** [[Zoogoneticus]], [[Chapalichthys]], [[Skiffia]] and [[Melaniris]]. [[Goodea]] was re-established, with *Xenendum* placed as a synonym.
- **New species:** about two dozen, including *Rhamdia oaxacae*, *Catostomus conchos*, *Catostomus sonorensis*, *Algansea rubescens*, *Gila minacae*, *Aztecula mexicana*, *Notropis robustus*, *Notropis santarosaliae*, *Evarra tlahuacensis*, *Fundulus oaxacae*, *Zoogoneticus diazi*, *Zoogoneticus miniatus*, *Skiffia lerme*, *Skiffia variegata*, *Heterandia lutzi*, *Xiphophorus jalapae*, *Chirostoma attenuatum*, *Chirostoma labarcae*, *Chirostoma patzcuaro*, *Chirostoma zirahuen*, *Melaniris balsanus*, *Lepomis occidentalis*, *Cichlasoma eigenmanni*, *Gobius parvus* and *Gobius claytonii*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report documents habitats ranging from clear spring brooks and sandy-bottomed rivers to lakes without outlets (for example Patzcuaro, Zirahuen, Quitzeo and the Valley of Mexico lakes). Several recurring observations are relevant to aquatic ecology:

- **Seasonal drying and mortality:** Many streams and lakes (Lago de Patos, Lago de Santa Maria, Lago de Castillos) dried during the dry season. Fish concentrated in residual holes, and mass mortality was reported as lake salinity rose through evaporation, with large losses when a lake fell to about one-quarter of its maximum size.
- **Irrigation:** Water was diverted to irrigation ditches, which both concentrated and imperilled fish populations.
- **Salinity gradients:** Lakes Quitzeo and Patzcuaro were noted as brackish, and fish from these lakes were paler. Dark fish from Chalco and Zirahuen contrasted with them.
- **Vegetation:** Dense algae and aquatic plants often clogged seines and made collecting difficult.
- **Reproductive ecology:** Most Poeciliidae and Goodeidae were viviparous. Spawning was timed to the close of the dry season, so that young were born well developed before the rains.
- **Human use:** Fish were sold fresh or dried for market (Patzcuaro, Chapala). Indigenous Tarahumara fishers stupefied fish with a plant poison, a method compared with Cherokee and Caroline Islands practices.

## Key Findings

- **Fauna composition:** The northern Mexican fauna resembles that of the Rocky Mountains and eastern United States. South and Central American forms predominate as far north as Mexico City, with a few extending to Texas.
- **Lerma Basin endemism:** The Lerma Basin, with the Valley of Mexico, has a distinctive fauna. Ten of 18 Lerma genera had no known representatives in other waters. The genus *Chirostoma* was only recorded from this region.
- **Species totals:** Meek reported 46 species for the region studied in southern Mexico. Of these, 30 belonged to two families: 16 Poeciliidae and 14 Atherinidae.
- **Balsas Basin:** Only ten species were known from the Rio Balsas, and it shares no species with the Lerma despite their proximity.
- **Sonora and Chihuahua:** Sixteen freshwater species were known from the Rio Yaqui and Rio Sonora, with two species (*Catostomus bernardini* and *Agosia chrysogaster*) common to both.
- **Migration hypothesis:** Fishes of Sonora and Pacific-slope Chihuahua were interpreted as migrants from the Rio Grande and Gila River, mostly from the former, with migration in recent geological times.
- **Viviparity:** Meek concluded that viviparity is far more widespread in the Poeciliidae than previously supposed. Earlier authors had overlooked the modified male anal fin.
- **Dominance of minnows:** *Notropis lutrensis* was the most abundant minnow in northern Mexico.
- **Taxonomic revisions:** *Xenendum* was synonymised with *Goodea*. *Algansea australe* was not treated as a synonym of *A. sallei*, and the two *Algansea* species described by Steindachner were considered likely to be a single species.
- **Keys provided:** Keys were given for *Ameiurus*, *Algansea*, *Gambusia*, *Goodea*, *Skiffia*, *Chirostoma*, the Poeciliid genera and the Mexican species of several other groups.
