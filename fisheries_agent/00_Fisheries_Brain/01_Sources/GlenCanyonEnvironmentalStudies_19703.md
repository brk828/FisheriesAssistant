# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Aquatic Coordination Team Meeting Notes (December 1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1991 Bureau of Reclamation package, issued by the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) office, records the December 3, 1991 meeting of the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] (ACT) and related correspondence. Its focus is the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (HBC) in the [[Little Colorado River]] and the Colorado River mainstem. The package covers action items for seven participating groups, fish-handling and mortality protocols, permit renewals, data ownership, [[Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS) schedules, and fiscal constraints. It also includes a draft review by [[Darrel Snyder]] of electrofishing injury and recommendations for changes to electrofishing policy. The document is administrative and planning in nature. It contains no new analytical results beyond summary counts reported by individual agencies.

## Background & Description

The package is a set of meeting minutes and enclosures:

- **Memorandum** from Dave Wegner (GCES) summarizing the meeting and listing action items for [[Bio/West]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), and GCES.
- **Enclosure 1:** Proposed meeting agenda, covering findings by Principal Investigators, data propriety, mortality handling, GIS mapping of the LCR mouth, HBC rearing, electrofishing evaluation, and LCR radio work.
- **Enclosure 2:** Attendance sheet (not reproduced).
- **Enclosure 3:** Questionnaire and draft report, "Impacts of Electrofishing on Fish," by Darrel Snyder of [[Colorado State University]], dated December 2, 1991.
- **Enclosure 4:** Proposed outline for draft EIS interim technical reports, dated January 31, 1991 (the header may be a typo for 1992).
- **Letter** from David Wegner to Robert Chandler, Superintendent of [[Grand Canyon National Park]], dated December 4, 1991, requesting a contact to evaluate a proposed radio link.
- **Distribution list** of roughly 30 recipients from ASU, FWS, [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[National Park Service]] (NPS), AGF, GCES, the Navajo Nation, the Hopi Tribe, and the Hualapai Tribe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Target species and status.** The HBC is the central species. Roundtail Chub and [[Razorback Sucker]] are also mentioned: food studies on roundtails were underway, and Razorback Sucker issues must be addressed in the [[Biological Opinion]].

**Study area.** Work centered on the LCR mouth and its mapping, mainstem sites where HBC have been found, tributary mouths (larval fish sampling), and the canyon rim at Desert View for radio contact.

**Electrofishing and injury.** Snyder's draft reviews recent work documenting spinal injury in fish from modern [[Pulsed Direct Current]] (PDC) electrofishing. Cited studies (Sharber and Carothers 1988, 1990; Holmes et al. 1990; Meyer and Miller 1991) report injury "often over 40%" in some species. The Alaska Department of Fish and Game imposed a moratorium on electrofishing in waters with large rainbow trout. The draft notes that most published data concern salmonids, but implications for other species are serious.

**Fish handling and mortality.** Dead HBC are allocated first to AGF, then to ASU. Specimens are to be frozen if possible, otherwise eviscerated or skeletonized, and small fish pickled directly in 90% alcohol. A chain of custody is required.

## Key Findings

**Fish status and sampling (as reported at the meeting)**

- Bio/West: 4 mortalities for the year; 52 radio tags deployed, 10 still active; recapture rate 8.5% for Bio/West fish and 20% for all fish tagged.
- ASU: 54% recapture rate on its tagged fish.
- AGF: 4 mortalities this year and 96 total mortalities; 600 [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tags]] deployed.
- Ten HBC were removed from the LCR for propagation and treated for tapeworm. ASU needs 5,000 fish by March 1992, and AGF indicated this was feasible.
- Stomachs of HBC examined from 1988 and 1989 showed the [[Asian Tapeworm]], which AGF believes was introduced by a centrarchid.
- Recommended a common fin clip for young fish to track cohorts. Paul Marsh (ASU) reported that fins regenerate within about 60 days, but clips remain visible.

**Permits and regulatory matters**

- All collecting permits expire December 31, 1991. Renewal is needed from FWS, NPS, and the Navajo Nation, with a streamlined modification process planned.
- John Ray (GRCA) requested permit information by December 13, 1991.
- AGF requested an animal-care approval letter from ASU for thermal shock tests.
- Navajo Nation is holding its permit request pending more FWS information and objects to additional handling of fish.

**Net sampling and electrofishing**

- A modified hoop net proposal drew concern from NPS about physiological effects and from FWS about predation within nets. GCES will ask ASU to begin the permit modification process and will request a response from Dr. Minkley on potential impacts.
- Snyder's draft recommends minimizing electrofishing near endangered species until response thresholds are determined in controlled experiments. Key recommendations include:
  - Abandon AC electrofishing except for eradication.
  - Prefer straight DC over PDC where power needs allow.
  - Use square waves at about 30 Hz or less, or Coffelt's CPS, where PDC is used.
  - Use the largest practical anode and lowest effective power settings.
  - Require standardized documentation, training and certification (including first aid and CPR), and periodic recertification.
  - Sponsor research on field strength thresholds, electrode design, in-situ sensors, automated output control, a fixed-site testing laboratory, and portable on-site X-ray.

**Data management and propriety**

- The Principal Investigator has first right to publish, must be involved in review of any secondary use, and must be credited.
- AGF has a data base manager, and annual data base updates are planned. Data collected during the year should be labeled "Provisional."
- Data discs should be scanned for computer viruses.

**EIS schedule**

- Initial qualitative EIS report due to GCES by December 31, 1991.
- Principal Investigator and EIS team meeting in January 1992.
- Report update due March 30, 1992, followed by technical updates as needed.
- The Biological Opinion will address the Preferred Alternative, and the consultation team begins meeting in spring 1992.

**Radio communication link (Desert View)**

- FWS completed a technical review finding the link feasible for the Desert View area. The proposed system would let rim staff alert LCR researchers to flow spates or endangered fish activity, coordinate the program, and handle emergencies.
- Navajo approval and archaeological clearance are required. Patrick Ryan stated that the Hopi should not be asked to perform the work.
- Wegner's letter asks Grand Canyon National Park to identify an administrative contact.

**Budget and program administration**

- Funding for endangered fish is expected to hold, but other GCES programs face major cuts.
- The memo reports a 42% reduction in program funds from 1991 levels. The Department of the Interior is expected to be involved in resolving the shortfall.
- Programs were asked to return unused FY92 dollars.

**Governance**

- Wegner proposed reducing ACT meetings to twice per year and holding Principal Investigator-only meetings four times per year, pending feedback from the group.
- The role of the USGS on the ACT remains unresolved.
