# Proposed Method for Examination of Water for Free-Living Nematodes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #nematoda

## Executive Summary

This 1960 methods paper, reprinted from the [[Journal of the American Water Works Association]], proposes a standardized laboratory procedure for detecting and enumerating [[Free-Living Nematodes]] in drinking-water samples. The method covers sample collection, membrane filtration concentration of large water volumes, direct microscopic counting, and general morphological identification. The author, Shih L. Chang of the [[Robert A. Taft Sanitary Engineering Center]] (USPHS, Cincinnati), developed it in response to contemporary interest in nematodes occurring in water supplies.

## Background & Description

The paper describes a method rather than a field study. Its stated aim is to examine "sizable quantities" of water, which the author judged necessary because of current interest in the subject. The method has four components:

- **Laboratory apparatus:** 1-gallon glass or plastic sample bottles; Pasteur capillary pipets (9 in., 2-ml rubber bulbs); a filtration unit with a funnel of at least 650 ml and a filter flask of at least 2 L; [[Millipore Filter Membranes]] (SS 047 MM type or equivalent); and a binocular [[Compound Microscope]] with 4X, 10X and 43X objectives and a mechanical stage.
- **Sample collection:** handled as for bacteriological samples, without dechlorinating agent. Raw-water samples are 0.5–1 gal and tap-water samples 1 gal. Refrigeration is not essential unless examination is delayed beyond 5 days.
- **Concentration:** membrane filtration with flushing into a [[Sedgwick-Rafter Counting Cell]].
- **Identification:** morphological keys, with [[Ward and Whipple's Fresh-Water Biology]] as the principal reference.

Figure 1 (a gross-morphology drawing of [[Rhabditis]] females, after Chitwood) illustrates three free-living genera. The drawing shows the amphid, pharynx, esophageal bulb, intestine, excretory pore and anus, and it is intended to help users work through the "Key to Genera" in Ward and Whipple.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- A 1-gal tap-water sample usually filters through one membrane within 15 min, unless the water is turbid.
- Turbid raw water may require two to four membranes per sample. Filtration through each membrane is limited to 20 min, and sample volumes range from 500 ml to 2 L depending on turbidity.
- Under normal conditions, nearly all nematodes in finished water are larval stages. They measure 100–500 µm in length and 10–40 µm in width, and only the fourth (final) larval stage has sex organs.
- Non-nematode objects that can be confused with nematodes include root fibers, plant filaments, elongated ciliates such as *Homalozoon vermiculare*, and segments of small crustacean appendages. Motility is the key discriminator for sluggish or non-motile specimens.

## Key Findings

- **Procedure:** The method provides a complete workflow for large-volume nematode examination, from collection through concentration to counting and identification.
- **Concentration limits:** At least 1 gal should be filtered per examination. Filtration of turbid water requires multiple membranes, each limited to 20 min.
- **Counting protocol:** The whole counting-cell area is surveyed under 4X when fewer than 100 nematodes are present. When more than 100 are present, density is estimated from representative fields multiplied by the number of fields.
- **Reporting units:** Density is expressed as organisms per gallon, optionally separated by adult or larval stage and by viability.
- **Identification:** Unstained specimens are examined at 10X and 43X. Fixed specimens are stained in 5% formalin according to standard nematology texts (Goodey; Chitwood & Chitwood).
- **Sample handling:** Refrigeration is not required unless analysis is delayed more than 5 days.
