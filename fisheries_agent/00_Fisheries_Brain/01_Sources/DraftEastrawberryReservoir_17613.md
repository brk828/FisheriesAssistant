# Commentary on the Strawberry Valley Fishery Restoration Draft Environmental Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonid

## Executive Summary

This is a 30 March 1988 letter from Walter R. Courtenay, Jr., Chairman of the [[American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists]] (ASIH) Environmental Quality Committee, to James E. Johnson of the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]. It responds to the Draft Environmental Assessment (Publication 87-14) for restoring the Strawberry Valley fishery, centred on [[Strawberry Reservoir]] and its tributaries.

The Committee neither endorses the project nor asks the Society to withdraw its 1987 resolution urging caution. It commends the agency for a detailed proposal and for seeking comment. The main concerns are the use of [[Rotenone]], the introduction of non-native trout, the risk of hybridization and genetic mixing, and the lack of consideration of native fish restoration.

## Background & Description

The [[Strawberry Valley Fishery Restoration]] proposal is a renovation of Strawberry Reservoir and its tributaries. The letter describes the Society as a professional body devoted to the study and conservation of fishes, amphibians and reptiles.

The letter identifies three salmonid taxa:

- **[[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Salmo clarki pleuriticus*): a subspecies that was probably native to the system but is not named as a stocking target.
- **[[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]]**: a Utah native proposed for introduction into waters where it is not native. One stated objective is to supply stock for future introductions in Utah and possibly other states.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]**: proposed for introduction, with uncertainty about whether it can be made sterile.

The letter also draws a comparison with [[Triploid Grass Carp]], whose claimed sterility the authors suggest is not reliable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter notes that drainage from the reservoirs has been altered, so reintroducing native fishes would also place them beyond their historical ranges. Its concerns are:

- **Genetic mixing:** possible [[Genetic Introgression]] if Bear Lake cutthroat trout enter the Colorado River drainage basin.
- **Hybridization:** the literature indicates that [[Hybridization]] is a predictable result of future introductions of trout, although long-term outcomes cannot be predicted with accuracy.
- **Chemical side effects:** [[Rotenone]] does not always behave as expected and can cause serious problems away from the points of application.
- **Reinvasion:** no poison guarantees that target species will not return, or that sport fish introductions will control them.

The letter notes that an alternative piscicide, [[Sodium Cyanide]], could give a more complete kill, break down faster and cost less. It is politically problematic because of public and rancher opposition.

The letter also notes that the history of native fishes in the system is not reviewed, and that the proposal focuses mainly on recreational fishing and economic impacts.

## Key Findings

The letter raises the following concerns and questions. It presents no new field data, sample sizes or statistics.

1. **Limited scope:** The assessment is dominated by recreational and economic issues and does not review the history of native fishes.
2. **Non-native introduction risk:** Introducing Bear Lake cutthroat trout outside its native range may cause hybridization and genetic mixing, especially in the Colorado River drainage.
3. **Sterility uncertainty:** There is little information on how the proposed [[Rainbow Trout]] can be made reliably sterile.
4. **Rotenone impacts:** The large quantity of rotenone proposed could cause severe off-site effects despite precautions.
5. **Permanence:** Introductions of non-native species have rarely provided lasting solutions, and the project does not guarantee that it will not need repeating.
6. **Native fish options:** The document does not consider reintroducing native fishes, including the Colorado River cutthroat trout, which was probably native to the system. The Committee asks whether this option was considered.
7. **Recommendation:** The commentary recommends examining these issues before restoration begins. It does not give a formal position for or against the project.
