# Spawning Runs from Pyramid Lake in 1986 and Studies of Cui-ui Life History

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report (Great Basin Complex, Reno, Nevada, September 1987) describes the 1986 spawning runs of [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]], [[Tahoe Sucker]], and [[Cui-ui]] that entered the Truckee River from [[Pyramid Lake]] and were counted at the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]. It also summarizes multi-year studies of cui-ui fecundity, adult age structure, adult and larval movement, and egg and larval temperature tolerance.

The 1986 runs were the largest recorded at the facility in its history for cui-ui and Tahoe suckers, and the largest in about ten years for cutthroat trout. The authors attribute these records to excellent river access across the Truckee River delta and high spring attraction flows. Runs arrived two to four weeks earlier than in previous years. The report concludes that cui-ui population viability depends on a narrow year-class base, so flow and passage management should be maximized within available water.

## Background & Description

**Study species**

- **Lahontan cutthroat trout** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*, cited in the text as *Salmo clarkii henshawi*): a salmonid stocked and reared at the [[Captain David Numana Fish Hatchery]] and [[Lahontan National Fish Hatchery]]. Its eggs are collected for hatchery rearing.
- **Tahoe sucker** (*Catostomus tahoensis*): a catostomid whose run was estimated but not sexed or measured.
- **Cui-ui** (*Chasmistes cujus*): a catostomid endemic to Pyramid Lake and the Truckee River system and a target of the recovery strategy described in the report.

**Study setting**

Adults were passed upstream through the Marble Bluff Fish Facility, located at [[Marble Bluff Dam]], and the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]] assisted passage. The report refers readers to Coleman (1986) for a full description of the lake, river, and fish resources.

**Methods overview**

- Daily run counts from the river trap, with length and sex sampling
- Aging of dead fish from the run, with [[Age Structure]] analysis by Scoppettone
- Fecundity sampling of 113 females, 1982–1986, using the [[Fecundity]] method of Scoppettone et al. (1986)
- Radio telemetry of 122 adult cui-ui, 1982–1986 ([[Radio Telemetry]])
- External anchor tagging of more than 4,000 cui-ui, 1982–1985, used for [[Mark-Recapture]] and [[Prespawning Aggregate]] estimates
- Egg and larval [[Temperature Tolerance]] experiments with fluctuating temperature regimes
- Larval [[Larval Drift]] netting below Marble Bluff Dam in 1985 and 1986

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake and river conditions**

- Pyramid Lake elevation rose about 29 feet from 1981 onward. This inundated the shallow braided delta and moved the lake–river interface closer to Marble Bluff Dam, giving fish easier access to the river.
- Lake inflow from January to April 1986 totaled 715,000 acre-feet, compared with 97,100 acre-feet in the same months of 1985.
- Spring river flows were 5,000 cfs in March and 2,000 cfs in May 1986 (the text describes high spring inflows).
- Lower Truckee River temperatures can fluctuate as much as 6°C within a day during spring and early summer. Releases from [[Stampede Reservoir]] regulate river temperatures to a limited extent.

**Temperature and run timing**

- Cutthroat trout catches rose during warming trends and fell during cold, stormy periods. Peak movement occurred at mean daily water temperatures of 7.7 to 13.4°C.
- Cui-ui runs in 1985 occurred 4 to 5 weeks earlier than in 1982–1984, with river temperatures 2 to 4°C cooler.
- Cui-ui activity was greater in late afternoon and at night, and movement increased during warming trends.

**Delta and passage**

Access across the Truckee River delta was the main factor in run timing and size. The report recommends dredging and maintaining a delta channel so cui-ui can reach the river when lake levels recede or when mud bars form at the lake–river interface.

## Key Findings

### Spawning runs

- **Cutthroat trout:** 1,397 adults counted in 1986, the largest run in about ten years. Fork length ranged from 285 to 795 mm; the mean was 575 mm for males and 532 mm for females. The sex ratio was 1.0:1.9 (male:female). Ages ranged from 2 to 8 years. Nearly half of the run (49 percent) was tagged, mostly from the Captain David Numana Fish Hatchery.
- **Ripe females:** The percentage of ripe females fell from 55 to 14 percent over four years, so fewer eggs were collected for hatchery rearing. Tribal hatchery personnel collected 416,000, 475,000, and 401,000 eggs in 1984, 1985, and 1986.
- **Tahoe sucker:** An estimated 55,022 fish passed the facility, the largest run since the facility opened in 1976. The run extended from March 20 to June 18, with a major peak on April 11 and a secondary peak from May 12 to 17.
- **Cui-ui:** 35,080 adults passed upstream in 1986, the largest run in the facility's history and more than double the next largest (13,804 in 1982). The adjusted count reflects individuals that re-entered the trap two or three times (36,339 counted, with 3.5 percent dropping over the dam and being recounted). The peak occurred on April 10.

### Age structure and sex ratio

- Scoppettone aged 456 adult cui-ui from the 1986 run and found twelve year classes. The 1969 year class made up 73.3 percent. The 1970 year class followed at 11.1 percent, then the 1974 year class at 4.4 percent.
- In 1983, 97 percent of a 119-fish sample belonged to the 1969 year class. Year classes 1974 and 1976 were more common in 1986 and appeared to be spawning for the first time.
- The cui-ui sex ratio was 1.0:1.3 in 1986, versus 1.0:1.9 in 1985. The change is attributed to recruitment of young fish with a sex ratio near 1.0:1.0.

### Fecundity

- Fecundity was measured in 113 females (1982–1986). It ranged from 21,561 eggs (431 mm FL, age 7) to 192,548 eggs (657 mm FL, age 32).
- Egg numbers rose sharply between ages 7 and 13, then leveled off near age 20. Variation among same-age females was large and partly linked to fish size.
- Females that had not spawned for several years appeared to have higher fecundity than recent spawners.

### Movement and residence (radio telemetry)

- 122 adults (65 females, 57 males) were radio-tagged from 1982 to 1986: 47 were released in Pyramid Lake and 75 in the river.
- Maximum river residence was 16.5 days. Males averaged 10.2 days and females 8.1 days.
- Most tagged fish moved only about 5 km upstream of Marble Bluff Dam to spawn. Fish released in the impoundment averaged 3.6 km, with a maximum of 9.3 km.
- Residence was longer in 1986 (averaging 9.0 to 9.5 days, depending on the text) than in 1982–1984, which may reflect cooler spawning temperatures.
- Fifty-five percent (11 of 20) of fish tagged after the peak of the run dropped back, compared with 11 percent (6 of 54) tagged before the peak.

### Egg and larval temperature tolerance

- Egg-to-swim-up survival was highest in the coldest regime (9.4–15.5°C; 73.4–74.2 percent) and lowest in the warmest regime (17.8–23.9°C; 13.7 percent). In the warmest regime, 74 percent of larvae were deformed and died before swim-up.
- Incubation time was directly related to temperature. Median hatching time ranged from four days at the 12.2–18°C regime to 14 days at the 9.4–15.5°C regime.
- The 24-hour TL50 (lethal temperature for 50 percent of larvae) was 29.7°C for newly hatched larvae and 30.0°C for swim-up larvae.
- Survival at 30°C was 33 percent for newly hatched larvae and 69 percent for swim-up larvae.

### Larval production and drift

- Estimated cui-ui larval production upstream of Marble Bluff Dam was about 43 million in 1985 and 225 million in 1986. Tahoe sucker production was about 16 million in 1985 and 167 million in 1986.
- Larval drift was nocturnal, rising after dusk (about 2100 hours), peaking between 2200 and 0200 hours, and declining before sunrise. The mean day–night ratio was 1:15. Over 99 percent of larvae were protolarvae (10–13 mm TL).
- Between 49 and 67 percent of larvae were captured over a two-week period.
- Estimated egg-to-larval survival was about 10 percent for cui-ui in 1985 and 16 percent in 1986. Tahoe sucker survival was about 11 percent in 1985 and 35 percent in 1986, though the Tahoe sucker estimates are less reliable.

### Prespawning aggregate and tag returns

- Prespawning aggregate estimates for 1982–1986 ranged from 90,000 to 200,000 adult cui-ui, described as "ballpark" figures.
- Approximately 5 to 39 percent of each aggregate migrated to the Marble Bluff Fish Facility.
- Adjusted recapture rates were 30.1 percent for males (of 1,867 released) and 31.9 percent for females (of 2,134 released).
- Males recaptured at about twice the rate of females. The adjusted recapture rate rose from 22.3 percent (males) and 12.2 percent (females) in 1983 to 42.3 percent and 23.9 percent in 1986. The authors could not fully explain the increase.
- Adjustments included an annual tag shedding rate of 8 percent, assumed annual survival of 0.80 for females and 0.75 for males, and standardization to a spawning run of 10,000 fish.

### Management conclusions

- Record runs were likely stimulated by large inflows and easy delta access. The early run let cui-ui spawn at cooler temperatures, which may have improved larval survival.
- The early cutthroat trout run created an egg-taking problem at the hatchery because most females were "green" (not ready to spawn).
- Cui-ui year-class diversity is "precariously low." The authors recommend maximizing attraction flows, instream flows for spawning, egg incubation, and outmigration within available water. Supplemental releases from Stampede Reservoir should be used sparingly.
- Stampede storage could also attract mature Lahontan cutthroat trout to the facility for hatchery egg-taking. Trout releases should occur only when they will not reduce cui-ui spawning potential.
- Preliminary egg results suggest good survival at temperatures at or below a daily maximum of 17°C. The authors state that this would support the management strategy of controlling river temperatures during incubation.
