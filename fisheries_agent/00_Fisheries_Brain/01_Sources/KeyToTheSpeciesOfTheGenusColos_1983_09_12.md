## Executive Summary
This document is a taxonomic identification key for the genus **[[Colossoma]]** (family [[Characidae]], subfamily [[Serrasalminae]]), authored by J. N. Taylor (1983). It distinguishes three nominal species — **[[Colossoma macropomum]]**, **[[Colossoma brachypomum]]**, and **[[Colossoma mitrei]]** — using meristic and morphological characters, primarily gill raker counts, head length relative to standard length, lateral line scale counts, opercular pigmentation, adipose fin structure, and ventral serrae counts. The note also lists synonymies and taxonomic uncertainties, notably raising the open question of whether *C. mitrei* is merely a southern, small-scaled form of *C. brachypomum*.

## Background & Description

### Diagnostic Key Structure
The key is dichotomous, splitting first on gill raker number and head proportions, then further subdividing based on adipose fin morphology and scale counts.

**Couplet 1:**
- **1a.** *[[Colossoma macropomum]]*: >80 total gill rakers (35–55 on lower limb, possibly fewer in juveniles); long head (≤3.0 in SL); lateral line with 60–85 scales; no opercular blotch; adults often countershaded (darker ventrally).
- **1b.** Remaining species: <40 total gill rakers (14–17 on lower limb); short head (≥3.0 in SL); lateral line with >85 scales; opercular blotch usually well-developed; no countershading.

**Couplet 2 (within 1b):**
- **2a.** *[[Colossoma brachypomum]]*: Adipose fin not rayed, margin edged in black and fimbriate; 1–3 maxillary teeth; 50–65 ventral serrae; 85–105 lateral line scales.
- **2b.** *[[Colossoma mitrei]]*: Adipose fin rayed in adults; maxillary teeth present (count not fully specified in source); 60–75 ventral serrae; >105 lateral line scales.

### Species-Level Notes
- **[[Colossoma macropomum]]** (Cuvier, 1818): Characterized by very high gill raker counts and a proportionally longer head relative to other congeners.
- **[[Colossoma brachypomum]]** (Cuvier, 1818): Distinguished by an unrayed, black-fimbriate-margined adipose fin.
- **[[Colossoma mitrei]]** (Berg, 1895): Distinguished by a rayed adipose fin and the highest ventral serrae and lateral line scale counts of the three species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Colossoma macropomum]]**: Native to the **[[Amazon Basin]]**.
- **[[Colossoma brachypomum]]**: Native to the **[[Amazon Basin]]**.
- **[[Colossoma mitrei]]**: Native to the **[[La Plata Basin]]**, representing a geographically distinct, more southerly distribution compared to its Amazonian congeners.

All three species belong to the [[Serrasalminae]], a subfamily of large-bodied characiforms typically associated with floodplain and lowland river habitats, often ecologically significant as seed dispersers and frugivores/omnivores in Neotropical river systems (context inferred from genus-level ecology, per [[Goulding 1980]] references).

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic synonymies identified (per Britski, 1977):**
  - *C. oculus* (Cope, 1871) and *C. nigripinnis* (Cope, 1878) are synonyms of **[[Colossoma macropomum]]**.
  - *C. bidens* (Spix, 1829) is a synonym of **[[Colossoma brachypomum]]**.
  - *C. edulis* (Castelnau, 1855), *C. mesopotamicus* (Holmberg), and *C. canterai* (Devincenzi & Teague, 1942) are proposed as probable synonyms of **[[Colossoma mitrei]]**.

- **Historical misidentifications noted:**
  - Eigenmann (1915, plate LIII) incorrectly identified *C. macropomum* as "*Piaractus nigripinnis*."
  - Géry (1977:255) incorrectly identified *C. macropomum* as *C. brachypomum*.
  - Eigenmann (1915, plate LII) mislabeled *C. brachypomum* as *C. mitrei*.

- **Open taxonomic question:** The author explicitly questions whether **[[Colossoma mitrei]]** represents a valid distinct species or simply a southern, small-scaled morphological variant of **[[Colossoma brachypomum]]** — flagged for further verification against a Paraguayan reference specimen (slide P79-114).

- **Character utility:** Gill raker counts and head length proportions serve as the primary high-level diagnostic separating *C. macropomum* from the other two species, while adipose fin ray presence/absence and fimbriation serve as the key distinguishing trait between *C. brachypomum* and *C. mitrei*.
