# Lake Mohave Sampling for Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker: 1993 Planning Memorandum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Arizona Fisheries Resource Office in Parker, Arizona, solicits participation in an intensive 8-day sampling effort at [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona and Nevada, scheduled for March 1993. The effort has two goals: (1) collect adult [[Bonytail Chub]] for broodstock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and (2) recapture and tag additional adult [[Razorback Sucker]] to refine population dynamics data. The memo warns that the razorback sucker population is at risk of decline, making the program an important monitoring activity. Adult razorbacks will be tagged with PIT tags, and agency roles and survey areas are assigned.

## Background & Description

**Bonytail chub** are the subject of a continuing effort to add wild-caught fish to the hatchery stock at Dexter. Fish of at least the stated minimum length that cannot readily be identified as hatchery-produced are to be retained and transferred to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] for temporary holding before moving to Dexter. Hatchery-derived fish, identified by size and/or fin clips, are to be examined, measured, weighed, photographed, tagged, and released.

**Razorback sucker** have been studied at Lake Mohave since 1980. More than 8,000 adults have been captured, and a number of these have been tagged. Based on subsequent recaptures, the lake's adult population was estimated, though the exact figure is illegible in the scanned text. Recapture data have also provided information on local and long-distance movements and on fidelity to particular areas.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Population status:** The memo states that the razorback sucker population in Lake Mohave is not expected to persist indefinitely. It notes that declines observed in other reservoirs suggest wild razorback numbers in Lake Mohave will decrease substantially over the remainder of the decade.
- **Monitoring purpose:** Tagging and recapture are intended to improve the existing database and support more refined estimates of abundance and movement, and to detect and document changes in population status.
- **Survey areas:** Work is divided among several sub-areas: lower Lake Mohave, Cottonwood Basin west, Cottonwood Basin east, and the "three mile" area, which may be worked depending on available personnel and equipment.

## Key Findings

**Objectives of the 1993 effort**
- Obtain adult bonytail chub for the broodstock at Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- Tag and recapture additional adult razorback suckers to expand and refine population dynamics information.

**Handling protocols**
- Bonytail retention criteria: retain bonytail measuring at least 11 inches (stated as 35 cm) total length that are not readily identifiable as hatchery-produced; transfer to Willow Beach for temporary holding, then move to Dexter. (Note: 11 inches is approximately 28 cm, so the two stated values are not consistent; verify the intended threshold before fieldwork.)
- Hatchery-derived bonytail: examine, measure, weigh, photograph, tag, and release.
- Adult razorback suckers: tag with PIT tags this year, as in the previous year. Participants must arrange PIT scanners, PIT tags, and ancillary materials (injectors, topical treatments) for their camps.
- Backup: spaghetti-type (Floy) tags and tagging equipment should be available if PIT tags or materials are unavailable.
- Participants should estimate tag requirements from past capture rates.

**Tentative 1993 assignments**
- Arizona Fishery Resource Offices (Pinetop and Parker, Arizona), with cooperators: Arizona bay area and other sites, led by Jim Hansen and Chuck Minckley.
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Phoenix Field Station, BLM Kingman, and others): lower Lake Mohave.
- U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City, Nevada) and National Park Service, with cooperators: Cottonwood Basin west.
- Arizona State University (with cooperators): Cottonwood Basin east.
- Arizona Game and Fish Department: possibly the "three mile" area, depending on personnel and equipment.
- Assignments are tentative and may be adjusted by participants' stated areas of emphasis.

**Logistics and next steps**
- Interested parties are asked to respond with participation intent, comments, and suggestions.
- Final participant lists and camp assignments will be mailed to respondents by March 1 (year as printed in the scan, presumed 1993).
