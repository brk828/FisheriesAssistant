# Biological Opinion and Conference Opinion: Reestablishment and Augmentation of Native Fishes, Muleshoe Cooperative Management Area

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) issued a combined biological opinion and conference opinion (consultation 02-21-04-F-0454, dated April 19, 2005) on a proposal by the [[Muleshoe Native Fishes Planning Team]] (MNFPT) to stock [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Desert Pupfish]] and to augment [[Gila Chub]] into springs and streams of the [[Muleshoe Cooperative Management Area]] (CMA). The [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Safford Field Office is lead agency. The FWS concluded that the action is not likely to jeopardize the four listed species, and that it is not likely to jeopardize the proposed Gila Chub or adversely modify its proposed critical habitat. The opinion anticipates a net benefit to the species, contingent on full implementation, monitoring and the conservation measures listed. An incidental take statement sets a surrogate trigger: take is considered exceeded if recreational or off-highway vehicle use causes streambank damage along more than 20% of occupied stream corridors.

## Background & Description

**Consultation history.** The proposal grew out of the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) consultations. A 1994 CAP biological opinion found that CAP operations were likely to jeopardize spikedace, loach minnow, Gila topminnow and Gila topminnow-related species, and a 2004 revision added conservation measures, including the CAP Fund Transfer Program ($250,000 annually for 21 years, of which $5,000 was allocated to this project). Earlier BLM planning under the [[Muleshoe Ecosystem Management Plan]] and the Safford District Resource Management Plan set the stage for reestablishment. The biological evaluation was received September 15, 2004; a draft opinion went out March 25, 2005; and comments received April 14, 2005 were incorporated.

**Proposed action.** Participants include BLM, FWS, [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Arizona State Land Department]], [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC) and [[Arizona State University]]. The CMA covers 49,120 acres in three watersheds (Redfield, Hot Springs and Cherry Springs canyons) with seven perennial reaches, isolated from the San Pedro River by long dry stretches.
- Initial stocking from spring 2005, up to 500 fish per effort per species, with a minimum of five years of augmentation at least annually.
- Source populations: Aravaipa Creek for spikedace and loach minnow; ASU-maintained stock from four Gila topminnow lineages (Bylas, Cienega Creek, Sharp Spring, Monkey Spring); desert pupfish from Cienega de Santa Clara (Mexico), Cibola NWR or Dexter NFH; Gila chub from within the CMA.
- Fish are stocked only into fishless sites within the drainage of origin, to protect genetic lineages and prevent spread of pathogens and invasive species.
- Health screening: a sample taken about six weeks before stocking is tested by the FWS National Wild Fish Health Survey Program; fish with [[Asian Tapeworm]] are excluded.
- Transport by helicopter, mule or backpack, depending on terrain.

**Species background.**
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*): small silvery fish with a spine in the dorsal fin; about two-year lifespan; feeds on aquatic and terrestrial insects; listed threatened in 1986.
- **Loach minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): small, slender bottom-dweller with upward-directed eyes; feeds exclusively on aquatic insects; listed threatened in 1986.
- **Gila topminnow** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*): live-bearing poeciliid; males rarely exceed one inch, females reach two inches; brood period 24 to 28 days; listed endangered in 1967.
- **Desert pupfish** (*Cyprinodon macularius*): under three inches long; lifespan one to three years; listed endangered in 1986.
- **Gila chub** (*Gila intermedia*): deep-bodied, chubby cyprinid; adult males average about six inches; proposed endangered on August 9, 2002.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** Spikedace and loach minnow require permanent, unpolluted flowing water with specified substrates, low fine-sediment embeddedness and periodic natural flooding. Gila topminnow tolerate a wide range of conditions (temperatures 51–97°F, pH 6.6–8.9, dissolved oxygen 2.2–11 ppm) and were found in springs with high dissolved carbonate and low pH, which limits mosquitofish. Gila chub favor quiet, deep pools with cover such as overhanging vegetation and boulders.

**Threats.** Habitat destruction, dewatering, impoundment, channelization, and predation and competition from nonnative fishes, bullfrogs and crayfish are the principal causes of decline across all species. Green sunfish were linked to loss of young-of-year Gila chub.

**Existing uses.** Livestock grazing is suspended on the Muleshoe Allotment (Hot Springs ACEC) and the Soza Wash Allotment; the Soza Mesa Allotment (5,620 acres, 44 head) is currently rested due to drought but expected to resume. Recreation (hunting, hiking, birding, camping) is light and dispersed. Off-highway vehicle use is restricted to existing roads, and 140 acres of Hot Springs Canyon riparian area is closed. Prescribed fire is limited to cool-season, low-intensity burns with buffers along riparian zones.

**Status and populations.**
- Spikedace is restricted to about 250 miles of stream and occupies 10 to 15% of its historical range; Aravaipa Creek holds the best populations.
- Loach minnow is restricted to 419 miles of stream and occupies 15 to 20% of its historical range.
- Only 12 natural Gila topminnow populations remain, and about 30 localities in total; only three natural sites are free of nonnative fish.
- Two naturally occurring desert pupfish populations remain in the United States, plus about 20 transplanted populations.
- Only 29 extant Gila chub populations remain, all but one small and isolated.

**Muleshoe CMA populations.** No spikedace, loach minnow, Gila topminnow or desert pupfish currently occur in the action area, though habitat is judged suitable. Gila chub occur in Redfield and Bass canyons, which host two of the three extant stable-threatened populations in the San Pedro basin. Gila chub were captured in Bass Canyon annually from 1991 to 1996, and made up 1–2% of fish in Bass Canyon; they were the most abundant species in Redfield Canyon in one survey.

## Key Findings

- **Opinion:** The proposed action is not likely to jeopardize spikedace, loach minnow, Gila topminnow or desert pupfish, and is not likely to jeopardize Gila chub or adversely modify its proposed critical habitat. No effects are anticipated for designated desert pupfish critical habitat in California or Quitobaquito Spring.
- **Expected benefit:** Establishment of new populations would aid recovery and secure genetic lineages. Effects are expected to be beneficial overall, with short-term adverse effects possible.
- **Downstream dispersal:** Dispersal beyond the CMA is considered unlikely because downstream reaches lack perennial flow and suitable habitat.
- **Success criteria:** Stocking is considered successful when recruitment and survival make a population self-sustaining; monitoring continues while species are present.
- **Incidental take:** Take is expected to be harm or harassment at extremely low levels, from recreational wading, vehicles or livestock. The surrogate trigger is streambank damage along more than 20% of occupied corridors.
- **Required terms:** BLM must monitor take and streambank condition annually, submit reports to the Arizona Ecological Services Office by March 15 beginning in year two, and post a trailhead sign near TNC headquarters.
- **Conservation measures:** No increase in grazing pressure, monitoring of grazing and utilization limits, salt placement at least 0.25 mile from water, monitoring for fish kills after prescribed fire, and public education.
- **Recommendations:** Expand the CMA through acquisition of ecologically sensitive private land, and keep detailed records of stocking successes and failures for future efforts, particularly for spikedace and loach minnow.
- **Reinitiation:** Consultation must be reinitiated if take is exceeded, new information arises, the action is modified, or a new species is listed.
