## Executive Summary
This note documents the first confirmed Canadian record of the [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*), captured on 22 August 1978 in the Canadian waters of [[Lake Erie]] near Point Pelee, Ontario. The capture represents a 30-km northward range extension beyond previously known U.S. records and highlights the value of monitoring commercial fisheries for range-extending freshwater species.

## Background & Description
- **Total length**: 52 cm
- **Weight**: 1.79 kg
- **Maximum recorded size** (per Trautman 1957): up to 134.6 cm length and 37.2 kg weight
- **Distinguishing features**:
  - Characteristic flattened head shape typical of [[Ictaluridae]]
  - Strong backward extensions of the premaxillary tooth-patches — the most diagnostic feature separating this species from other local catfishes
  - Specimen exhibited a notable deformity in the middle region of the caudal vertebrae, otherwise consistent with species-typical morphology described by Trautman (1957) and Hubbs and Lagler (1964)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Typically a large-river species; considered rare in [[Lake Erie]] proper
- **Substrate at capture site**: Silty clay
- **Water temperature at capture**: 24.3°C
- **Known range context**: Long established in U.S. waters of Lake Erie, with Trautman (1957) noting records approaching the international boundary; a small resident population suspected in the Huron River (Ohio), located due south of Point Pelee
- **Regional abundance**: More commonly associated with the Ohio River and its tributaries than with Lake Erie itself; only five specimens recorded in Ohio's Lake Erie waters between 1938–1957

## Key Findings
- First documented capture of *Pylodictis olivaris* in **Canadian waters**, confirming a **30-km range extension** north of previously known U.S. records.
- Specimen captured via commercial trapnet operated by Rudy Krause (K. W. Krause and Sons Fisheries), west of [[Point Pelee]], Ontario (41°56'50"N, 82°31'40"W).
- Despite intensive commercial fishing activity in the region, the species had never previously been reported from adjacent Canadian waters.
- Single specimen recovery makes it unclear whether this represents a stray individual or evidence of an established local population near Point Pelee.
- Reinforces that shallow waters connecting western [[Lake Erie]] to [[Lake St. Clair]] have historically been a corridor for northward range expansions of freshwater fish species into Canadian waters over the preceding 25 years.
- Specimen retained in the **Royal Ontario Museum** reference collection (ROM Cat. No. 34561).
- Findings underscore the importance of careful scrutiny of commercial fishery catches for detecting unusual or range-extending species.
