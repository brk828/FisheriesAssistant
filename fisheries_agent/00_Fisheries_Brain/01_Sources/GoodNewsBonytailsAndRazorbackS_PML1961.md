# Bonytail and Razorback Sucker Reproduce in a Restored Oxbow Community at Lake Mead

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This December 2006 email from [[Gordon A Mueller]] of the [[United States Geological Survey]] (USGS) to the Colorado River native fish mailing list reports that [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] have reproduced together for the first time in roughly 50 years at a restored pond in [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]]. The fish were moved from [[Cibola High Levee Pond]] and [[Davis Cove]], and the new site was designed as an "Oxbow Community." The author presents the result as a success for the [[Native Fish Work Group]] and sets a goal of creating 10 such communities.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], both native fishes of the Colorado River system. The document names them as the target species but gives no morphological or taxonomic description.
- **Prior facility:** [[Cibola High Levee Pond]] supported bonytail and razorback recruitment for more than a decade, under the guidance of [[Chuck Minckley]]. It was compromised by [[Largemouth Bass]] in 2004.
- **Restoration effort:** In the previous September, about a dozen state and federal biologists met to salvage and renovate Davis Cove. A few weeks later, 150 adult razorback suckers salvaged from Cibola High Levee Pond were stocked there together with bonytail from Dexter, to establish a replacement facility.
- **Participating agencies:** USGS, Bureau of Reclamation (BOR), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), National Park Service (NPS), Nevada Department of Wildlife (NDOW), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG), and Arizona State University (ASU).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Oxbow community:** The author describes this habitat type as a natural feature of the unaltered river that has since been lost. The restored ponds are meant to recreate it for native fish.
- **Pond criteria:** The recipe given is a pond of less than 5 acres, a few dozen dedicated biologists with equipment, about $2,500 for chemicals, and the fish themselves.
- **Predator control:** Nonnative predators were not detected during the survey that found the young fish.
- **Fish condition:** The author reports that the fish were in "beautiful condition."

## Key Findings

1. **Successful reproduction:** Both species survived and produced "numerous young" at the Davis Cove site, the first joint reproduction in about 50 years.
2. **Young counts (as reported):**
   - Several thousand bonytail, each under 60 mm.
   - Several hundred razorback suckers, each under 30 cm.
3. **Cause of loss at previous facility:** Largemouth bass compromised Cibola High Levee Pond in 2004, after more than a decade of recruitment there.
4. **Management model:** The author credits the success to the combination of a suitable small pond, dedicated biologists, modest funding, and allowing the fish to reproduce with limited disturbance. The author notes that much will be learned through managing the fish.
5. **Future plans:** The goal is 10 oxbow communities. Additional sites were being prepared for stocking that year, and the author invited volunteers to help.
6. **Recognition:** The email credits Jeff Lantow (BOR) and Mitch Thorsen (FWS) for assisting [[Jeanette Carpenter]] with her fall monitoring trip.
