# Habitat Selection of Spawning Razorback Suckers in Arizona Bay, Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines the physical spawning habitat used by [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Arizona Bay]], [[Lake Mohave]], on the Arizona-Nevada border. Spawning was surveyed on February 16-17, 1982, over a 690 sq/m portion of a known spawning area, using survey techniques and [[Scuba Surveys]]. The authors report that substrate size may be a critical factor in site selection, that all spawning sites lacked fine sediment, and that [[Wave Action]] combined with fluctuating lake elevations may maintain substrate suitability in the absence of river flow.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is an endemic sucker of the [[Colorado River Basin]], traditionally a river-oriented fish. Since the closure of [[Davis Dam]] in the early 1950s, razorback suckers have been observed spawning along the shoreline of [[Lake Mohave]], indicating that the species has adapted to reservoir conditions.

Little was known about the spawning habitat requirements of this species when the study was conducted. The work was prepared by Paul C. Marsh, with co-authors Gordon Fuller, William Rinne, Tom Burke, and Mike Delamore, affiliated with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] in Boulder City, Nevada.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** The study area is a spawning site in [[Arizona Bay]], a reservoir embayment of Lake Mohave. The document describes this as a reservoir habitat, not a free-flowing river.
- **Hydrology:** Flushing river currents are absent in the reservoir. The authors suggest that [[Wave Action]] and fluctuating lake elevations replace the sediment-sorting role of river flow.
- **Substrate:** Substrate composition, particularly the presence or absence of [[Fine Sediment]], appears central to whether a site remains suitable for egg deposition.
- **Parameters measured:** Site location, depth, densities, size, substrate composition, and occurrence of eggs.

## Key Findings

- **Study area and effort:** A 690 sq/m portion of a razorback spawning area in Arizona Bay was mapped using survey techniques and underwater SCUBA divers.
- **Substrate size:** Observations indicate substrate size may be a critical factor in [[Spawning Habitat]] selection.
- **Fine sediment:** All observed spawning sites were characterized by a lack of fines in the substrate.
- **Maintaining suitability:** In the absence of flushing river currents, wave action coupled with fluctuating lake elevations may be a critical factor maintaining substrate suitability in a reservoir setting.
- **Broader implication:** The species' spawning in a reservoir indicates adaptation of a river-oriented fish to lacustrine conditions, with substrate conditions as a key constraint.
