# Toxicity of Selenium-Enriched Agricultural Drainwater to Endangered Green River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a USGS research proposal (partially illegible after OCR) on the toxicity of [[Selenium]]-enriched [[Irrigation Drainwater]] to endangered native fishes of the upper [[Colorado River]] system. Its central concern is that trace elements in agricultural drainwater, chiefly [[Boron]] and selenium, may harm larval and juvenile [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Green River Basin]]. The planned work combines [[Laboratory Exposure Studies]] with field-based assessment.

## Background & Description

The proposal is based on recent investigations by the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) and an interagency team. These studies focus on [[Stewart Lake]], Jensen Creek, and [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] in Utah. The text identifies three elements of concern: boron, selenium, and a third element that is illegible in the scan. Some of these elements occur naturally at trace levels, but they tend to concentrate in irrigation drainwater.

The target species are endangered fishes that depend on Green River habitat:

- Colorado Pikeminnow (squawfish)
- Bonytail
- Razorback Sucker

The source describes these as the resources of greatest concern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The investigations report concentrations of contaminants in water, sediment, and fish tissues that may be harmful to fish and other aquatic resources. The fishes of the Green River area, including those in the vicinity of [[Irrigation Return Flows]], contain elevated selenium concentrations. Fish from these areas also show a high incidence of [[Spinal Deformities]], which the text links to selenium exposure. The source also reports that little or no successful reproduction of Colorado Pikeminnow, Bonytail, or Razorback Sucker has been found in the Green River over the past decade. The exact wording of these reproductive findings is partly garbled in the scan.

## Key Findings

The proposal sets out the following aims and planned approaches:

1. **Primary objective:** Determine the waterborne and dietary effects of trace elements in drainwater on the survival, growth, and development (with reproduction also referenced) of larval and juvenile endangered fishes.
2. **Laboratory design:** Expose fish to single trace elements and to trace element mixtures, including selenium exposures. The studies will use [[Reconstituted Water]] that mimics the water chemistry of the middle Green River basin.
3. **Dietary component:** The work includes dietary exposure pathways alongside waterborne exposures.
4. **Rationale:** Field data show that the Green River system is contaminated with selenium and other trace elements, and that reproduction and development of the target species are impaired. Laboratory toxicity data are needed to link drainwater contamination to these effects.
