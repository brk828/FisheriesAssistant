# Reconnaissance Survey and Inventory of Mainstem Bottomlands in the Upper Colorado River Basin

## Executive Summary
This FY93 project report, conducted under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], documents a reconnaissance survey aimed at identifying, categorizing, and ranking flooded bottomland habitats along mainstem rivers of the upper Colorado River basin. The project's purpose was to support recovery efforts for endangered fish species by cataloging potential floodplain habitats used for feeding and spawning. Fieldwork included aerial photographic surveys at high, medium, and low flow levels across five major rivers (Green, Yampa, White, Colorado, and Gunnison), along with development of a novel 10-parameter classification scheme for ranking bottomland habitat value. The project was funded at $59,200 for 1993, with accomplishments including completed inventory lists and aerial photo acquisition, though data digitization lagged behind schedule.

## Background & Description
The study was commissioned under Recovery Program Activity II.A ("Conduct inventory of flooded bottomland habitat for potential restoration") to address the decline of natural floodplain connectivity in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Historically, spring runoff inundated river floodplains annually, but channelization via levees, dikes, rip-rap, and invasive [[Tamarisk]] encroachment—compounded by reduced spring flows from upstream impoundments—has severely diminished fish access to these habitats.

Principal investigators included David Irving and Tim Modde (Colorado River Fish Project, Vernal, UT) and Bob Burdick and Frank Pfeifer (Colorado River Fish Project, Grand Junction, CO). The project ran from FY1993 to FY1994 and was designed with three primary objectives:
1. Identify bottomlands adjacent to mainstem rivers representing potential endangered fish habitat.
2. Classify identified bottomlands by potential recovery value.
3. Rank bottomlands by perceived contribution to endangered fish recovery.

The intended end product was a comprehensive listing of mainstem flooded bottomlands classified by size, flooding rate/duration, fish access, permanency, vegetation type, and other non-biological criteria (land ownership, access, feasibility of water control structures).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Seasonal floodplain inundation is recognized in the literature as critical to river productivity, and [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]] are known to use these habitats extensively for feeding both before and after spawning, and potentially for spawning itself. Restoration of this lost floodplain connectivity is considered a key strategy for recovering imperiled native fish populations in the basin.

The survey covered five major river reaches within the basin:
- **Green River**: Brown's Park to Colorado/Green confluence (river mile 0–398)
- **Yampa River**: Cross Mountain Canyon to Green/Yampa confluence (rm 0–50)
- **White River**: Taylor Draw Dam to Green/White confluence (rm 0–105)
- **Colorado River**: Rifle, CO to Colorado/Green confluence (rm 0–241)
- **Gunnison River**: North Fork Rivers to Colorado/Gunnison confluence (rm 0–75)

Environmental degradation drivers noted include:
- River channelization (levees, dikes, rip-rap)
- Tamarisk invasion stabilizing/narrowing channels
- Reduced spring peak flows due to upstream dam impoundments (e.g., [[Flaming Gorge Dam]])

## Key Findings

### Methodology & Data Collection
- **Aerial Surveys**: Conducted via Cessna 206 aircraft at altitudes of 5,000–6,000 ft, capturing 35-mm color slide photo imagery across three flow conditions:
  - **Medium flow** (24 May 1993): 6,000–12,000 cfs — Green, Yampa, White rivers
  - **High flow** (10 June 1993): Gunnison and Colorado rivers
  - **High flow** (15 & 30 June 1993): 18,000–22,000 cfs — Split Mountain to Sand Wash, Green River (funded separately by USFWS flood control monies, $2,500)
  - **Low flow** (29 September 1993): 3,000–4,000 cfs — Green, Yampa, White, Gunnison, and Colorado rivers
- Flow levels referenced to USGS gauge at Jensen Bridge, Green River.

### Existing Data Sources Identified
- Colored infrared aerial photographs (1974, U.S. Army Corps of Engineers) — Flaming Gorge Dam to Green/Colorado confluence
- Colored aerial videography (May–June 1992, Argonne National Laboratories) — Flaming Gorge Dam to Jensen/Ouray
- Historical maps from U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (early 1900s–present)
- Additional infrared photography (late 1970s/early 1980s, USACE) covering Yampa River and broader mainstem

**Limitation noted**: Existing videography was inconsistent — limited to specific river segments and only to the high-water mark, making it unsuitable for comprehensive bottomland assessment. Up-to-date infrared photography was identified as ideal but cost-prohibitive.

### Classification Scheme Development
A novel 10-parameter classification system was developed (existing wetland classification systems were deemed inapplicable or requiring excessive modification). Parameters extracted from 35-mm photo imagery included:
- Total area / potential inundation area (acres)
- Hydrological connectivity (up/on water)
- Levee or berm presence, width, and construction type (natural/man-made)
- Vegetation status (vegetated/dividing)
- Distance to endangered fish access points
- Predominant fish life stages present
- Predominant geological formations
- Photo imagery date/time and reference number

This scheme was intended to standardize ranking of bottomland value for [[Endangered Fish Recovery]] purposes.

### Budget & Administrative Status
- **Funds Provided (1993)**: $59,200
- **Funds Expended (1993)**: $59,200
- **Additional funding**: $2,500 from USFWS flood control monies for supplemental Green River flights

### Shortcomings
- Cataloging and digitizing hundreds of 35-mm slides proved more time-consuming than anticipated, delaying completion of final deliverables into December 1993–January 1994.
- Recommendation issued: acquisition of upgraded computer hardware/software to accelerate digitization of bottomland surface area data from aerial imagery.

### Relevance to Recovery Program
This inventory directly supports future prioritization of bottomland sites for **acquisition, restoration, and management** as habitat for the basin's endangered fish fauna, feeding into broader [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] goals under the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fishes ([[RIPRAP]]).
