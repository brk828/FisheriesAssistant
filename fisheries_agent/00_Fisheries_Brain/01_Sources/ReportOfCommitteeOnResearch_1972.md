## Executive Summary
This report documents a 1972 Johnson Fund-supported study led by Robert R. Miller (University of Michigan) investigating the taxonomy and relationships of Mexican Cenozoic freshwater fishes. Prior to this study, only four freshwater fish taxa — a catfish, a silverside, a [[Goodeidae|goodeid]], and a trout — had been recorded from Cenozoic (Pliocene–Pleistocene) freshwater deposits in Mexico. The new fieldwork and collection analysis substantially expanded this known fossil diversity to 5 families, 9 genera, and at least 12 species, most closely allied to or conspecific with extant taxa. A key finding was the discovery that [[Micropterus|Micropterus (black bass)]], today restricted in Mexico to the extreme northeast near the Rio Grande drainage, occurred nearly 300 miles farther southeast during the middle-to-late Pleistocene, at the present-day latitude of [[Lake Chapala]] near Guadalajara, Jalisco.

## Background & Description
- Fossil material consists primarily of **disarticulated bones** (~50 pounds of matrix and bone collected).
- Specimens have been sorted to **family level**, with most identified further to **genus**.
- A **new species of bass** (genus *Micropterus*) is being formally described, representing a significant range extension for the genus in deep time.
- The extinct/fossil "blanco" of Lake Chapala is identified as a species within the same goodeid genus as living taxa, indicating close morphological continuity between fossil and extant forms.
- A fossil **catfish** taxon was also recovered, though full taxonomic placement is not detailed in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Fossil deposits derive from **five sites** spanning the **Miocene to Pleistocene**, situated near [[Lake Chapala]] and northwest of Guadalajara, in the State of Jalisco, Mexico.
- These sites represent ancient **freshwater lacustrine/fluvial habitats**, offering a window into paleo-lake ecosystems of central Mexico.
- The presence of *Micropterus* at the Lake Chapala latitude during the Pleistocene indicates a **broader historical range** for basses and their relatives in Mexico compared to their modern, geographically restricted distribution (currently limited to the northeastern Rio Grande drainage).
- Associated **fossil mammals, birds, and reptiles** from the same sites are pending identification by specialists; these will help refine the age of the deposits and reconstruct the broader **paleoecology** of the habitat.
- The scarcity of previously known Cenozoic freshwater fish records in Mexico (only 4 taxa: catfish, silverside, [[Goodeidae|goodeid]], trout) underscores the importance of this region as an underexplored area for paleoichthyological research.

## Key Findings
- Expanded known Mexican Cenozoic freshwater fish fossil record from **4 taxa** to **5 families, 9 genera, and ≥12 species**.
- Fossil deposits range in age from approximately **7 million to 30,000 years before present**.
- Documented a major **paleobiogeographic range extension** of ~300 miles for the genus [[Micropterus]] during the middle-to-late Pleistocene, extending its range to the Lake Chapala region, far south of its modern range south of the Rio Grande drainage.
- A **new fossil bass species** is being described in ongoing work.
- Fieldwork conducted **26 November–6 December 1972** at four sites around [[Lake Chapala]] and northwest of Guadalajara (Jalisco, Mexico), yielding ~50 lbs of fossil matrix and bone.
- Future publications planned: (1) formal description of the new bass species, (2) account of new fossil records for Mexico and general site ecology.
- Active participants in fieldwork and research included Frances H. Miller, Nancy A. Neff, and Michael Smith, under grantee Robert R. Miller.
- Grant funding: **$1,000** from the Johnson Fund (1972).
