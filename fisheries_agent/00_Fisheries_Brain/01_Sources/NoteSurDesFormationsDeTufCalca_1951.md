The note below has been restructured to faithfully reflect the actual subject matter: biogenic decalcification, cyanophyte (blue-green algae) taxonomy, and diatom (Bacillariophyceae) community ecology associated with tufa deposits.

## Executive Summary
This 1951 paper by Symoens and Van der Werff documents calcareous tufa formations near [[Consdorf]] in the [[Gutland (Luxembourg)]] region, focusing on two collection sites along the [[Ernz Noire]] river system. The authors describe the biogeochemical process of biogenic decalcification driven by photosynthesis, wherein calcium bicarbonate is converted to insoluble calcium carbonate as aquatic plants and algae assimilate CO₂. The paper provides taxonomic descriptions of three cyanophyte (blue-green algae) species responsible for tufa construction, including one new species (*Lyngbya vandenberghenii*), and catalogs the diatom (Bacillariophyceae) assemblages preserved within the tufa deposits, comparing community composition between a stream-crossing barrage and a rock-face seepage deposit.

## Background & Description

### Cyanophyceae (Tufa-Building Filamentous Algae)
- **[[Lyngbya calcarea]]**: Long, mostly straight filaments ~7 µm thick, calcite-encrusted; sheaths firm, colorless, non-reactive to zinc chloroiodide stain; trichomes pale blue-green, 5-6 µm thick, not constricted at articulations, apical cell rounded, no calyptra (cap).
- **[[Lyngbya vandenberghenii]]** (nov. sp.): Long, straight or sinuous filaments, 3.5-4 µm thick, calcite-encrusted; sheaths firm, smooth to slightly rugose, non-lamellose, colorless; trichomes pale blue-green, 2.2-3 µm thick, articles 2.5-4 (up to 5) µm long; distinct septa; apical cell rounded; no calyptra. Named in honor of collector C. Vanden Berghen.
- **[[Lyngbya nana]]**: Thinner filaments (~2 µm), calcite-encrusted; smooth colorless sheaths; pale blue-green trichomes, not constricted at articulations; rounded apical cell, no calyptra.

### Associated Bryophyte
- **[[Brachythecium rivulare]]** — moss species co-constructing tufa deposits at the Müllerthal cascade site alongside the *Lyngbya* species.

### Tufa Deposit Macro-Morphology
- **Site (a)** — Ernz Noire, km 3 Consdorf-Grundhof road: crescent-shaped, tiered tufa barrages spanning the river (width 5-6 m, rapid current, pH 7.5); porous masses with strongly mammillated, blackish surface; built exclusively by *Lyngbya calcarea*; no moss or vascular plant colonization.
- **Site (b)** — base of a Luxembourg Sandstone cliff near the Müllerthal cascade: blue-gray coating with innumerable small cylindrical/subspherical protrusions; built jointly by *Brachythecium rivulare* and three *Lyngbya* species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Biogeochemical Process
Biogenic decalcification occurs in calcium-bicarbonate-rich waters as a general consequence of photosynthesis:

Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂

Aquatic plants and algae decompose calcium bicarbonate, converting it to carbonate while liberating CO₂ for chlorophyllous assimilation. The resulting calcium carbonate is nearly insoluble, precipitating to form tufa deposits — as lacustrine/pond sediments, continuous crusts or transverse stream barrages (via calcareous coalescence around pebbles, twigs, mosses), or rock-like accretions in steep rivulets and hillside seepages.

### Regional Geological Context
Tufa deposits in the [[Gutland]] occur at the base of Bajocian limestones, coquina limestone, and especially [[Grès de Luxembourg]] (Luxembourg Sandstone). Notable massifs (up to 5 m tall) occur near [[Echternach]], the [[Ernz Blanche]] valley north of [[Larochette]], the [[Mamer]] valley (Kopstal–Dirental), and the [[Eisch]] valley (Eischen–Hollenfels). Additional formations occur at the base of Middle Brown Jurassic limestones at Lasauvage and "la Cronière" (a regional term for tufa deposits).

### Diatom Habitat Associations
The diatom assemblage recovered from acid-dissolved tufa residues (alongside plant debris and quartz grains) spans a range of ecological guilds:
- **Halophile/brackish-tolerant taxa**: *[[Melosira nummuloides]]*, *[[Cocconeis pediculus]]*, *[[Rhoicosphenia curvata]]*, *[[Gyrosigma acuminatum]]*, *[[Diploneis ovalis]]*, *[[Navicula mutica]]*, *[[Navicula gracilis]]*, *[[Gomphonema olivaceum]]*, *[[Surirella ovalis]]* — notable presence of halophilic species in this freshwater context, previously documented at Ry Colas in the Belgian Ardenne (Conrad, 1942).
- **Spring/running-water (eurytopic) taxa**: *[[Diatoma vulgare]]*, *[[Meridion circulare]]*, *[[Synedra ulna]]*, *[[Cocconeis placentula]]*, *[[Achnanthes lanceolata]]*, *[[Hantzschia amphioxys]]*, *[[Nitzschia linearis]]*.
- **Littoral/eutrophic pond taxa**: *[[Melosira varians]]*, *[[Gyrosigma spencerii]]* var. *nodifera*.
- **Epiphytic taxa**: *[[Cocconeis placentula]]*, *[[Amphora ovalis]]* (and var. *pediculus*, epiphytic on larger diatoms like *Nitzschia* and *Surirella*).
- **Nordic-alpine/peat bog taxa**: *[[Anomoeoneis serians]]*.
- **Planktonic-capable taxa**: *[[Rhoicosphenia curvata]]*, *[[Navicula radiosa]]*, *[[Nitzschia dissipata]]*, *[[Nitzschia sigmoidea]]*, *[[Cymatopleura solea]]*.

## Key Findings
- Three cyanophyte species (*Lyngbya calcarea*, *L. vandenberghenii* nov. sp., *L. nana*) are identified as the primary biological agents of tufa construction at Consdorf, acting via photosynthetic biogenic decalcification.
- A new species, *[[Lyngbya vandenberghenii]]*, is formally described (with Latin diagnosis) and named for collector C. Vanden Berghen.
- Site (a) tufa (river barrage, pH 7.5, rapid current) is built solely by cyanobacteria with no bryophyte or vascular plant colonization, while Site (b) (rock-face seepage) involves co-construction by moss (*Brachythecium rivulare*) and cyanobacteria.
- Tufa samples are readily acid-soluble, leaving residues of plant fragments, quartz grains, and diatom frustules — enabling reconstruction of the paleo/contemporary diatom community.
- Significant compositional differences exist between the diatom assemblages of the two sites, attributed to differing microhabitats (mid-stream barrage vs. streamside/rock-face deposit).
- Notably, several halophilic (brackish-adapted) diatom species were recovered from this inland freshwater system, corroborating similar prior observations in the Belgian Ardenne (Ry Colas stream, Conrad 1942), suggesting halophile diatoms can persist in select freshwater habitats.
- This work extends known geographic distributions for *Lyngbya calcarea* (previously France, Tatra, North America, Belgium) and *Lyngbya nana* (previously North America, Belgian Lorraine) to the Luxembourg Gutland.
