# Notropis saladonis: A New Shiner from the Rio Salado System, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1958 *Copeia* paper by C. L. Hubbs and C. Hubbs formally describes [[Notropis Saladonis]], a small cyprinid (the "Salado shiner") from the [[Rio Salado]] system of northeastern Mexico. The authors separate it from its closest relatives, chiefly [[Notropis Buchanani]], [[Notropis Volucellus]] and [[Notropis Braytoni]], using meristic, morphometric and pigmentation characters. They also report that it lives sympatrically with *N. buchanani* in the Rio Salado system, which supports its status as a distinct species.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The species was placed in *Notropis*, a large and heterogeneous genus of North American shiners. The authors describe the genus as a "multitudinous" complex whose species were difficult to separate. The type series was studied from localities in the Rio Salado basin, and the type locality is marked as station No. 7 on Map 2.

**Diagnostic characters.** The paper reports the following features:
- Small size, with maximum standard length of 39 mm in the diagnosis (the description says "not known to exceed 38 mm"; the two figures are as printed).
- Delicate texture, very thin scales and slender fin rays.
- Principal anal rays usually 8, rarely 7.
- Lateral line scales usually 32, with 20 to 24 scales in greatest body circumference.
- Body moderately deep and strongly compressed; body width is less than half the depth.
- Head depth 1.3 to 1.4 in standard length, about one-third greater than head width.
- Very pale ground color with distinctly speckled dorsum and lateral band, produced by large, widely separated melanophores.
- Small, wedge-shaped basicaudal spot.
- Nuptial tubercles on the head confined to the dorsal surface and not reaching the mouth; pectoral ray tubercles in a basal row that branches into about three irregular rows.

**Sexual dimorphism.** Adult males and females look essentially alike in preservative. The authors note that breeding males have nuptial tubercles on the head and pectoral rays. Females are on average somewhat deeper-bodied than males.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** The species is recorded from the [[Rio Salado System]] in Nuevo León. A collection from the [[Rio Sabinas]] at Rancho de los Borregos (Coahuila) included 5 adults of *N. saladonis*, 38 specimens of *N. buchanani* and one hybrid between the two. The authors state that sympatry in the Rio Salado system shows the species are distinct, and that such sporadic hybridization does not indicate conspecificity.

**Related species and range.**
- [[Notropis Buchanani]] ranges from northeastern Mexico north to the upper Mississippi and Ohio rivers. It is the closest relative and is distinguished mainly by the enlarged, isolated melanophores and the scale and head characters.
- [[Notropis Volucellus]] ranges from southern Canada to the Guadalupe River system in central Texas. The authors consider a Mexican record doubtful.
- [[Notropis Chihuahua]] occupies the Rio Conchos basin and small tributaries in western Texas, but not the Rio Salado or Rio San Juan basins.
- [[Notropis Braytoni]] occurs in several Rio Grande tributaries, including the Rio Salado. It is larger and coarser, with more solid pigment.
- [[Notropis Stramineus]] ranges from southern Canada to northeastern Mexico and shares the Rio Salado system with *N. saladonis*.
- [[Notropis Ornatus]] is a deep-bodied species with extreme sexual dimorphism, and it is distinguished from *N. saladonis* by its form and color.

**Environmental note.** The authors suggest that the enlarged sensory papillae in *N. buchanani* are an adaptation to turbid water, which is contrasted with the habitat of *N. volucellus*.

## Key Findings

- *Notropis saladonis* is described as a new species, endemic to the Mexican Rio Salado system, and is distinct from all other *Notropis* species then recognized.
- Its closest relatives are *N. buchanani* and *N. volucellus*. These are compared in detail, with differences in scale counts, body shape, pigment pattern, nuptial tubercles and sensory papillae.
- Sympatric occurrence with *N. buchanani* in the Rio Salado system, with a hybrid recorded at Rancho de los Borregos, is the main evidence for specific status.
- The species' known maximum standard length is 38 to 39 mm, and it has a pale, speckled coloration with a diagnostic basicaudal spot.
- The authors state that *N. saladonis* differs from *N. volucellus* in size, scale counts, body form, scale elevation and pigment, and that *N. volucellus* has not been reliably recorded in Mexico.
- The paper notes that subspeciation within *N. buchanani* may be demonstrated, but it does not affect the specific separation of *N. saladonis*.
