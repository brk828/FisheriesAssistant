# Razorback Sucker Reintroduction and Monitoring Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This June 13, 1986 interagency memorandum from the Fisheries Management Supervisor outlines the first year of a [[Razorback Sucker]] reintroduction and monitoring program on the [[Lower Colorado River]]. The plan covers the selection of stocking habitats, the stocking of hatchery-reared fry, and a multi-gear sampling schedule (July 1986 through March 1987). It is designed to measure survival and growth of stocked fish, document habitat use across life stages, test sampling gear, and refine stocking procedures. The text is a project plan rather than a research report, so it contains no results.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:** The [[Razorback Sucker]] is the focal species. The memo refers to it as "razorback" and "razorback sucker" throughout.
- **Stocking source:** Fish are hatchery-reared, and the document refers to stocking of fry (larval and juvenile stages are both mentioned in sampling protocols).
- **Management context:** The program is coordinated with the [[Striped Bass Study]], which runs in parallel and shares sampling effort, and with other fishery management objectives on the river.
- **Information gap:** The memo states that there is a lack of precise data on habitat requirements for the various life history stages of razorbacks. This complicated the selection of reintroduction sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stocking-site selection.** Lower Colorado River backwaters were chosen over mainstem sites for four stated reasons:

1. Piscivorous exotic fish, expected to prey on young razorbacks, are less abundant in small backwaters.
2. Backwaters located in the upstream section of each river division can offset downstream migration of stocked hatchery-reared fish, a pattern the memo says has been shown in studies of other species.
3. Backwaters are warmer and more productive in phytoplankton and zooplankton, which may help protect young razorbacks from predation.
4. Stocking techniques are considered more effective in backwater habitats.

**Physical habitat.** Selected sites were chosen because their physical characteristics closely resemble those of the historic, unchannelized lower Colorado River.

**Other site criteria:**
- Reliable water supply of suitable quantity and quality
- Adequate food supply
- Absence of large populations of competing or predatory fish
- Isolation from future impacts such as channelization and other river modification projects (serving as refuges), and/or the ability to protect habitats used by native fishes from such impacts

**Named sites.** The memo lists Parker Dam, Parker Forebay (Parker Division), Imperial Division sites, and the Imperial National Wildlife Refuge area, with locations shown in figures that were not legible in the scanned copy. It also mentions a planned second stocking site in the lower Colorado River area for future work.

## Key Findings

**Program objectives (as stated):**
1. Evaluate survival of stocked razorback suckers.
2. Determine habitat use by razorbacks at various life history stages.
3. Evaluate sampling techniques and modify them as appropriate.
4. Determine proper stocking procedures to enhance survival.

**Stocking numbers:** The backwater stocking entry is reported as 466,660 fry. Forebay and other site stocking numbers are partly illegible in the scan. Stocking was to occur largely at night, timed to the early after-sunset period and not during daytime hours.

**Monitoring schedule (July 1986 – March 1987):**

| Gear | Location / Purpose | Frequency and effort (as stated) |
|---|---|---|
| Electrofishing (boat) | Shoreline and backwaters above and below stocking sites; only razorbacks measured and weighed | Every two months, one night per survey |
| Trap nets (hoop and fyke types) | Testing efficacy for capturing young razorbacks, compared with other gear | Ongoing from July 1986 through March 1987 |
| Gill nets | Backwater and forebay sites; set at sunset, run every few hours | Every six weeks or every two weeks in specific areas, as scheduled |
| Light and plankton sampling | Night sampling with a Q-beam light and plankton net to index larval razorbacks, which show photo-tactic response | Every few hours at selected sites, with about 15 minutes per sample |
| Seining / trawling | Shoreline and nearshore habitats | Monthly or as specified |

**Sample size target:** At least 30 razorbacks were to be captured in the growth-assessment work at one site, with total length and weight recorded for each fish. Non-target fish were to be counted and identified, with specimens preserved in labeled jars if exotic species were found.

**Expected outcomes:** The program is intended to produce data on survival, growth rates, habitat use, gear performance, and optimal stocking procedures. The memo does not report any results.

**Overall conclusion:** Stocking of hatchery-reared razorback suckers into selected Lower Colorado River backwaters, combined with a structured multi-gear monitoring program, is the planned approach for re-establishing the species. Data from the first year were intended to guide later stocking decisions.
