# Colorado River Fishes Monitoring Project: First Annual Report (1982)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This first annual report of the [[Colorado River Fishery Monitoring Project]] (CRFMP) covers 1982 field work by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR). The project followed up a three-year study (1979–82) of three endangered fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail]]. The 1982 work focused on the Colorado squawfish and had three objectives: (1) define adult movement and spawning-site locations using [[Radiotelemetry]]; (2) verify flow requirements for spawning and larval survival; and (3) determine how [[Peaking Power Flows]] from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] affect young-of-the-year (YOY) and juvenile survival.

Adult fish were tracked through the spawning season, which produced aggregations of ripe fish and larval captures in the Grand Junction and Gray Canyon areas. Larval and YOY surveys documented reproduction in the Colorado and Green rivers. The peaking-power study was not completed in 1982 because the required flow schedules could not be arranged. The report closes with a plan for 1983.

## Background & Description

The Colorado squawfish is the largest cyprinid of the Upper Colorado Basin. The 1979–81 CRFP studies found it had the greatest management potential of the three endangered species. Its wide range, long movements and specific life-history requirements led the authors to argue that meeting its needs would also meet those of other endangered species.

Objectives 1 and 2 are linked: radiotelemetry was needed to verify spawning flows, and YOY collections were used to judge spawning success, which feeds Objective 3 (flow effects on YOY survival).

**Study area (1982):**
- Colorado River between Palisade, Colorado and Hite, Utah
- Gunnison River from the Redlands Diversion to its confluence with the Colorado
- Lower 20 miles of the Yampa River
- Green River from the Yampa confluence downstream to Gray Canyon
- Assistance to the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources in tracking radio-tagged fish in the White River

**Methods (summary):**
- **Capture and tagging:** Fish were collected by electrofishing, gill nets, trammel nets and traps. Colorado squawfish longer than 500 mm TL were surgically implanted with radio transmitters (about 10–11 g in air; life expectancy about 6 months), following [[Tyus 1982]].
- **Tracking:** Fish were located at least biweekly, mainly by boat with some aerial searches. Locations were given in river miles (RM) from the Green–Colorado confluence (RM 0).
- **Spawning identification:** Tracking was intensified in July–August. When several radio-tagged fish were found together, trammel nets were drifted through the spot.
- **Larval sampling:** Seines and dip nets were used in backwaters, shorelines and mid-channel. A pilot larval drift study used 1 m ichthyoplankton nets set for 10 minutes at 0600, 1200, 1800 and 2400 hours at five Green River and Yampa River stations. Samples were preserved in 5% formaldehyde and identified at the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]], Fort Collins, Colorado.
- **YOY surveys:** Quantitative fall seining (3 x 5 mm mesh) in mid-September through October targeted fingerling squawfish (25–60 mm). Sites were backwaters roughly every 5 miles, with depth, velocity and substrate recorded.
- **Peaking-power study:** Planned for the Green River between Jensen and Ouray, Utah, using aerial photography, staff gauges and controlled high and low releases.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow and temperature:** Adult aggregations near Clifton, Colorado occurred as river flows were receding and water temperatures were near 20 °C. The report cites [[Miller et al. 1982]] for this temperature as the point at which Colorado squawfish successfully spawn.

**Habitat:** Backwaters and side channels were important. Larval and YOY squawfish were concentrated in these habitats and in pools and eddies. On the Green River in 1982, backwater habitats were virtually absent during the YOY survey period.

**Hydrology:** Peak flows in 1982 were high. The report notes that high-water conditions may have reduced YOY survival, and that low YOY catch rates on the Green River were likely tied to those conditions.

**Reservoir use:** Some fish from the upper [[Lake Powell]] area moved into deep water and upstream in the Colorado River, showing use of both reservoir and riverine habitat.

**Downstream effects of dam operations:** Releases from Flaming Gorge Dam reached Jensen and Ouray with measurable lags (see Key Findings).

## Key Findings

### Radiotelemetry: Upper Lake Powell (Spring 1982)
- Six Colorado squawfish were implanted with radio transmitters between April 6 and May 28. A seventh fish received an ultrasonic transmitter on April 6.
- Contact was lost with three fish, including the ultrasonic unit, soon after release.
- The four remaining fish were located periodically for 5 days to 6 months after release.
- The most notable movement was an upstream migration of about 160 miles from the [[Gypsum Canyon]] area to RM 140 (about 4 miles above [[Black Rocks]]) in mid-August. In September the same fish was found near [[Clifton Colorado]], about 40 miles farther upstream.

### Radiotelemetry: Grand Junction Area
- Twelve additional squawfish were tagged between May 14 and June 18 in flooded gravel pits connected to the Colorado River: eight at [[Walker Wildlife Area]] (RM 163.6), two at Island Backwater (RM 175.3) and two at Labor Camp (RM 163.2).
- Nine fish were released inside the pits and three outside, in the river. Fish caught in trap nets were apparently leaving the pits, so they were released in the river.
- All fish released within the gravel pits later moved into the Colorado River.
- No movements longer than 50 miles were recorded. The longest movements were about 40 miles.
- Walker Wildlife Area fish generally travelled farther than fish tagged upstream.
- One Gunnison River fish probably died after implantation and was excluded from the report.
- Intensive searches between Palisade and Westwater continued into November.

### Spawning Aggregations and Behavior
- **July 13, Clifton (RM 178.3):** Three radio-tagged fish were found in a small pool in a side channel. Trammel nets drifted through the pool collected nine squawfish, including one radio-tagged fish. Five were ripe males. The others showed tuberculation typical of mature females, but no sex products could be expressed.
- **Eddy at RM 176:** Five radio-tagged fish visited this site in the last two weeks of July. A ripe male was collected here. A recaptured radio-tagged fish from a nearby side channel had a distended abdomen and had gained about 500 g in six weeks, probably a maturing female.
- Two ripe males implanted at Walker Wildlife Area (RM 163.6) moved 10 and 15 miles before the aggregation. One was later found in the RM 178.3 group.
- The authors caution that ripe-male aggregations do not prove spawning occurred locally. Male minnows ripen before females and may move considerable distances before spawning.
- Larval squawfish collected in August showed spawning did occur in the Grand Junction area.

### Green River Radiotelemetry
- Eleven adult squawfish (522–810 mm TL) were implanted by May 20.
- Movement patterns differed from 1980 and 1981, with larger fish moving into or remaining in whitewater canyon areas after spawning.
- Radio-tagged fish led investigators to a previously suspected spawning site in [[Gray Canyon]]. Seven ripe squawfish were collected there between July 13 and July 22.
- One 810 mm fish (No. 3001) was reported to have moved about 322 miles during the study period. This figure is printed as given in the source and may be an OCR error.
- Only one collecting trip reached the lower [[Yampa River]], where one ripe fish was caught. A companion FWS study (Stoneburner 1983, in press) found spawning in Yampa Canyon about 3–4 weeks later than in Gray Canyon.
- Four White River fish tracked with Utah DWR moved into the Green River in 1982.

### Larval Surveys
- **Colorado River:** 148 larval squawfish were found throughout the study area. Of these, 107 came from the reach below [[Westwater Canyon]] (the canyon itself was not sampled), where they were most abundant. The first larvae above Loma appeared later in the season than downstream, possibly reflecting differences in spawning time between reaches.
- **Green and Yampa rivers:** 98 larvae were confirmed, plus a potential 19 others, from seine samples at five stations (9.3–22.0 mm TL).
- **Upper Green River:** No larvae were found above the Yampa mouth (RM 345), indicating no reproduction in the upper Green River.
- **Largest concentration:** 52 known (16 potential) larvae were collected August 3–7 at RM 314.9, below [[Split Mountain Canyon]].
- **Drift samples:** Of the drift samples, 131 larval fishes of all species were collected. No Colorado squawfish were positively identified.

### Young-of-the-Year Surveys (Fall 1982)
- Colorado River YOY squawfish were collected only below Westwater Canyon. Areas with high YOY abundance coincided with high larval abundance.
- On the Green River, CPUE of YOY squawfish was about half the average of previous years. The authors attribute this to the high water of 1982, not to poor spawning. Despite this, the number of YOY fish collected suggested reproduction was as high as in earlier years. Their survival during high water may still have been reduced by unfavorable conditions.

### Peaking Power Impacts (Not Completed)
- The Flaming Gorge flow-fluctuation study was not completed in 1982. High flows and scheduling problems prevented the planned low-flow releases. BR released maximum flows in late summer, and the study could not get the needed low flows.
- An attempt to measure stage response between October 31 and November 4 was made, using staff gauges at Jensen and Ouray.
- Table 4 shows the response to a release of 800 cfs followed by 4,000 cfs for 14 hours. The flow was already dropping at Jensen when the crew arrived. Estimated lags were about 36 hours from Flaming Gorge to Jensen and about 18 hours from Jensen to Ouray.

### 1983 Work Plan
- **Colorado River:** Radio-tagging and larval sampling in two reaches, Palisade to Westwater Canyon (RM 185 to about RM 116) and Westwater to Spanish Bottom (below the Green confluence). Each reach is surveyed biweekly. Up to 20 squawfish will be tagged; 11 were already tagged in fall 1982 with 18-month tags.
- **Lake Powell:** Radio tracking and larval sampling at Gypsum Canyon, as time and manpower allow.
- **Gunnison River:** A continuously monitoring receiver station upstream of the Redlands Diversion to detect fish passing over the diversion.
- **Humpback chub:** Radio work at Black Rocks beginning March 1983. Up to ten fish will be tagged, and pressure-sensitive transmitters that record depth are under consideration.
- **Green River:** Up to 20 squawfish will be tagged between Gray Canyon and the Colorado confluence to test for additional spawning areas and possible movement between the two rivers.
- **YOY monitoring:** Known spawning sites will be checked in both rivers. Spawning success and adult movement will be correlated with 1983 flows and compared with past BR flow recommendations.
- **Peaking-power study:** The Green River study below Flaming Gorge will continue, with flow needs coordinated early with BR.
