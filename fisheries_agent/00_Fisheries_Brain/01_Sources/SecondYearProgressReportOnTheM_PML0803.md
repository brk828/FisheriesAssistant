# Second Year Progress Report: Ecosystem Management, Environmental Transport and Biorestoration

#ecosystem-engineering #environmental-fluid-dynamics #hydrology

## Executive Summary

This progress report covers the second year (1995–1996) of a multidisciplinary grant at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), initiated March 1, 1995 and run through the [[Environmental Fluid Dynamics Program]]. The initiative was designed to link researchers working on environmental transport processes and on ecosystem responses to those transports. The report is written by project director [[Harindra Fernando]] and documents collaboration, institutional impact, training, publications, proposal activity and external funding.

The document does not present fish research. Fish, fish species and ichthyological data are not addressed. Its aquatic content concerns lakes, riparian systems, contaminated streams, coastal swimming areas and Arctic littoral waters, approached through fluid mechanics, modeling and seminars.

## Background & Description

- **Principal investigators:** [[Neil Berman]], James Collins, [[Harindra Fernando]], Peter Fox and Duncan Patten. Fernando was the contact person and coordinator.
- **Structure:** A multidisciplinary award under the ASU Multidisciplinary Award Program, supported by the [[Environmental Fluid Dynamics Program]] administratively.
- **Departments involved:** Botany, Chemical Engineering, Chemistry, Civil and Environmental Engineering, Geography, Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering, Mathematics and Zoology.
- **Modeling expertise:** The initiative funded the hire of [[Alex Berestov]], a numerical analyst, who is developing the "ASU Environmental Transport Model" for general use.
- **Student researchers:** Roy Marler (Botany, supervised by Patten) and Theresa Kopchinsky (Civil and Environmental Engineering, supervised by Fox), coordinated by the research associate.

The report describes the initiative as a way to address problems too large for a single investigator. Its stated goals include building national prominence for ASU in environmental transport and ecosystem management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research themes address the movement of contaminants and heat through environments and the effects on ecosystems:

- **Lakes and recreational water:** Horizontal turbulent jets to improve water quality in swimming areas, and a system to limit coliform bacteria at [[Lake Havasu]] (model testing suggested increased circulation may reduce contamination in such coves).
- **Contaminated streams and groundwater:** Modeling of transport and ecosystem response, [[Soil Aquifer Treatment]] for water reuse, and [[Riparian Vegetation]] responses to floods and groundwater decline in semiarid regions.
- **Urban and atmospheric environments:** Urban plumes in wilderness areas near Phoenix, airshed modeling along the El Paso–Ciudad Juárez border, thunderstorm prediction and urban climate effects.
- **Coastal and Arctic marine environments:** Internal waves in the [[Kara Sea]] littoral zone, marine boundary layers and cobble dynamics near the surf zone.
- **Amphibians and biological indicators:** A seminar on population dynamics and disease in tiger salamanders, proposed as indicators of water quality in Arizona.

## Key Findings

### 1. Collaboration and training
- A multidisciplinary seminar series, the "Ecosystems Engineering Seminar," is attended by about 30 registered graduate students plus community members.
- Table 1 lists **31 seminars** delivered in 1996, from speakers at ASU, other universities, federal agencies and industry (for example, [[Paul Johnson]] on aquifer restoration, H. Maurice Valett on flooding and riparian restoration, and A. Blaustein on amphibian population declines).
- A voluntary multi-instructor course, "Environmental Transport," spans several departments.
- Regular meetings, joint supervision of cross-disciplinary students, and joint proposal preparation strengthened links among investigators.

### 2. Institutional impact
- Visiting speakers included prominent academic and industrial researchers. Many were members of a National Research Council special panel on water resource management.
- The workshop "New Directions in Geophysical Fluid Dynamics and Turbulence" (May 16–19, 1996) was co-sponsored by the Environmental Fluid Dynamics Program and the Center for Systems Science and Engineering. It drew internationally recognized researchers and program directors from federal funding agencies, and included laboratory tours.
- A poster on ASU environmental transport research was prepared for distribution to leading academic institutions.
- The Dean of Engineering assigned two faculty positions in Environmental Fluid Mechanics, and the Department of Mathematics hired an environmental flow modeler as an associate professor.
- ASU, with approximately 43,000 students, is described as the sixth largest university in the U.S.

### 3. Publications and model development
- The first phase of an eco-geo-chemo-bio-physical model is complete. Its results were presented at a national conference (AGU Fall Meeting, 1996).
- Reported publications include: Chen et al. (1996), a physical model of diurnal heating in the vicinity of a lake, *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences* 53(1): 62–85; Berman et al. (1995) on physical model experiments for complex terrain, *Journal of Applied Meteorology* 34(3): 719–730; Babin, Mahalov and Nicolaenko (1996) on 3D Euler and Navier–Stokes equations; and Berestov, Fernando and Fox (1996) on contaminated streams, *EOS* 77: 247–248.
- Additional conference papers and manuscripts were in preparation or submitted (for example, a nocturnal drainage flow paper, and work presented at the Arizona Fluid Mechanics Conference).

### 4. Proposal activity and external funding
- **Preproposals (Table 2):** Several multidisciplinary preproposals were submitted to agencies including the [[Environmental Protection Agency]], the [[Office of Naval Research]] and the [[National Science Foundation]]. Outcomes were mixed: some were not accepted, one full proposal was invited and pending, and at least one was accepted and funded.
- **Proposals submitted in 1996 (Table 3):** Several proposals were submitted, with requested amounts ranging from $49,540 to $684,789. The report lists:
  - Awarded: the Kara Sea internal waves project, [[Office of Naval Research]], $220,847; and hydraulic capacity enhancement for the NWWRP, City of Mesa, $110,000.
  - Pending: contaminant dispersion modeling in complex terrain, [[National Science Foundation]], $498,324.
  - Declined: several proposals, including the mathematical and numerical tools proposal ($684,789), the urban plume proposal to the EPA ($459,158), and an indoor particle study ($451,744).
- **Funded proposals (Table 4):** The report lists funded projects from 1995–1996, including:
  - Dynamics of cobbles in the near-surf zone, Office of Naval Research, $338,184.
  - A system to limit coliform bacteria in Lake Havasu recreational areas, Arizona Department of Environmental Quality.
  - Internal waves at the Kara Sea littoral zone, ONR.
  - Hydraulic conductivity enhancement for the NWWRP, City of Mesa, $110,000.
- The report states that the ASU initiative's funding was "heavily leveraged" in competing for large-scale programs, and that contacts in Washington, D.C. were treated as an investment for future opportunities.

### 5. Overall assessment
The report presents the initiative as successful in building collaboration, training, visibility and external funding. Several proposals remained pending at the time of writing, and the report notes that the modeling capability (the ASU Environmental Transport Model) was still in development.
