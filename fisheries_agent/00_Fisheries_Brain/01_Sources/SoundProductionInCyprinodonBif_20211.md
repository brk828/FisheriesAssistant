# Sound Production in Cyprinodon bifasciatus and Sex Ratio of Spined Loaches (Cobitis)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #cobitidae

## Executive Summary

This document contains two studies in one issue of *Environmental Biology of Fishes* (2000). The first, by Dawn Lee Johnson, documents sound production in the [[Cyprinodon Bifasciatus]], a pupfish endemic to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, México. Males produced pulsed calls during territorial defense, courtship and after spawning. The second, by Jörg Bohlen and David Ritterbusch, investigates why the [[Sex Ratio]] of a diploid–polyploid complex of [[Cobitis]] (spined loaches) in [[Lake Müggelsee]], Germany, is strongly female-biased. The authors attribute the bias mainly to a dominant polyploid [[Sperm Parasitism]] system. The Bohlen and Ritterbusch text is truncated in the source, so its conclusions are only partly available.

## Background & Description

### Part 1: Sound production in *Cyprinodon bifasciatus*

- **Study organism:** A pupfish endemic to the Cuatro Ciénegas basin. As in other *Cyprinodon*, males defend shallow-water territories and attract females to spawn.
- **Prior knowledge:** Before this study, acoustic signalling in Cyprinodontiformes had been reported only in *Fundulus* (Drewry 1962). No sound production had been reported for *Cyprinodon*.
- **Methods:** Territorial males were recorded with synchronized video and audio (Hi-8 tape, Benthos AQ-20 hydrophone). Recordings were made at Poza La Becerra, Mojarral Oeste and Poza Churince. Signals were analysed in MATLAB 5.3 using standard [[Bioacoustics]] metrics: duration, rise time, fall time, dominant frequency and second frequency. Statistics were run in Systat 9 using descriptive statistics and [[Multivariate ANOVA]]. Sound pressure level was not measured.

### Part 2: Sex ratio in *Cobitis* from Lake Müggelsee

- **Study organism:** Small bottom-living spined loaches that spawn from April to July in dense shallow vegetation and show no parental care.
- **Context:** Sex ratio biases can arise from [[Environmental Sex Determination]], hermaphroditism, sex-selective predation, or [[Polyploidy]] with [[Gynogenesis]]. The authors note that few studies had examined more than one mechanism at once.
- **Study site:** Lake Müggelsee (Berlin) is a polymictic eutrophic lake, maximum depth 8 m, surface area 770 ha.
- **Methods:** Combined field sampling with laboratory rearing and erythrocyte (red blood cell) measurements to determine ploidy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cuatro Ciénegas pools:** Spring-fed pools with little temperature fluctuation. Mid-afternoon temperatures during recording were about 32 °C (La Becerra), 33 °C (Mojarral Oeste) and 31 °C (Churince).
- **Sympatric sound producers:** *C. bifasciatus* coexists with other known or potential sound producers, including the endemic cichlid [[Cichlasoma Minckleyi]], *Etheostoma* darters, *Cyprinella xanthicara*, and the introduced *Hemichromis guttatus*. Several aquatic insect families present in the basin (Dytiscidae, Hydrophilidae, Belostomatidae, Naucoridae, Hydropsychidae) are also known sound producers (Aiken 1985).
- **Phylogenetic context:** *C. bifasciatus* appears to occupy a basal position in the *Cyprinodon* phylogeny (Echelle & Echelle 1998). The author argues that sound production may be more widespread across [[Atherinimorpha]] and [[Cyprinodontiformes]] than currently recognised, and that it is common among clear freshwater fishes.
- **Cobitis habitat:** Shallow, vegetated areas of Lake Müggelsee, where spawning takes place.

## Key Findings

### *Cyprinodon bifasciatus* (Johnson 2000)

- **Sampling:** Territorial males were recorded on 24 April 1998 (n = 4) and 23 April 1999 (n = 4) and 5 August 1999 (n = 3) at Poza La Becerra; 7 August 1999 (n = 2) at Mojarral Oeste; and 8 August 1999 (n = 2) at Poza Churince.
- **Acoustic parameters (all calls, n = 106 from 15 males):**
  - Mean dominant frequency 409 Hz (median 431 Hz)
  - Mean duration 55 msec (median 49 msec)
  - Duration correlated with rise time (r = 0.58) and fall time (r = 0.77). Rise and fall time were not correlated with each other.
  - Dominant and second frequencies were not correlated with any other parameter. No harmonics or sidebands were detected.
- **Call structure:** 82% of calls were single pulses and 18% were double pulses. Each call was given during a single encounter.
- **Behavioural contexts:**
  - [[Pursuit Calls]]: 74% (n = 79 pulses from 15 males). Directed at conspecifics (n = 19) and *Cichlasoma minckleyi* (n = 51). Some receivers were out of camera range.
  - [[Courtship Calls]]: 14% (n = 15 pulses from 5 males). Given while following females (n = 10) or patrolling territories (n = 5).
  - [[Postspawn Calls]]: 12% (n = 13 from 5 males). Infrequent. Given immediately after mating, when the pair separated. The author suggests the female may have produced some of these.
- **Context differences:** Multivariate ANOVA showed significant differences among contexts (p = 0.032). Postspawn calls differed from pursuit and courtship calls. Pursuit and courtship calls overlapped substantially.
- **Site and male differences:** No significant differences were found among males within a site (Becerra pursuit p = 0.604; Becerra postspawn p = 0.158) or among sites overall (pursuit p = 0.051; postspawn p = 0.228). The Becerra–Mojarral Oeste pursuit comparison was nearly significant. The author states that larger samples are needed.
- **Caveat:** Callers were not confirmed directly because the territorial male was not always the only nearby fish.
- **Comparison with *Fundulus*:** *Fundulus* species (Drewry 1962) produced dominant frequencies of 110–260 Hz, with harmonics that may have been artefacts of aquarium or transducer resonance. Courtship sounds in *Fundulus* were similar to the "follow female" context here. No pursuit calls were reported for *Fundulus*.

### *Cobitis* sex ratio, Lake Müggelsee (Bohlen & Ritterbusch 2000)

- **Field sex ratio:** About 1:22 in favour of females (as written in the source).
- **Temporal trend:** The male percentage rose from 2.9% to 10.6% over three years. Male proportion changed significantly between sampling dates within years.
- **Laboratory rearing:** 50 specimens reared from eggs in slightly warmer, softer water than the lake had a sex ratio of about 1:24. The authors concluded that environmental sex determination, hermaphroditism and sex-selective mortality were probably minor factors in the lake.
- **Ploidy:** Erythrocyte measurements on 59 wild-caught specimens gave a diploid–polyploid ratio of 1:7.4.
- **Main conclusion:** Dominance of the polyploid sperm parasite is taken as the main reason for the unbalanced sex ratio. Because polyploids depend absolutely on diploid hosts, the authors suggest a lower threshold for male percentage that should have been reached in the lake population.
