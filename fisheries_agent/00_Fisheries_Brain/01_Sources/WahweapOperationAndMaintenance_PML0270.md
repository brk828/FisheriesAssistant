# Wahweap Operation and Maintenance - Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 2005 annual project report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 29c) covers the operation and maintenance of the [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] at Big Water, Utah, run by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]. The hatchery rears [[Bonytail]] for stocking in the Upper Colorado River Basin and maintains the Upper Basin's backup [[Razorback Sucker]] broodstock. In 2005 the station stocked 10,574 bonytail, received and reared 20,000 bonytail fry, and reported full completion of FY 2005 tasks at 100% of work and funds spent.

## Background & Description

The project is listed under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIPRAP) General Recovery Program Support Action Plan. It falls under two actions: managing genetic integrity and augmenting or restoring populations (Action IV.C), and operating and maintaining facilities (Action IV.C.3, Wahweap). The study schedule is ongoing. The principal investigator is Quentin Bradwisch of the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, based in Page, Arizona.

The report describes hatchery operations rather than a research study. Its main activities were:

- Rearing and stocking bonytail for the [[Integrated Stocking Plan]].
- Maintaining backup razorback sucker broodstock.
- Tagging stocked fish with [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tags]] (PIT tags).
- Monitoring fish health and disease status.
- Supporting outreach at the Colorado River Water Users Association and Utah Water Users meetings, where staff provided live bonytail for display.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Bonytail were stocked as juveniles, ranging from 175 to 360 mm. The report notes that all two-year-old fish spawned to some extent in the hatchery ponds in 2005. Stocking sites were:

- [[Stewart Lake]]: 574 bonytail from 1996.
- [[Green River]] at Rainbow Park Boat Ramp: 3,400 PIT-tagged bonytail.
- Green River at Green River State Park: 3,100 PIT-tagged bonytail.
- [[Colorado River]] at Cisco Boat Ramp: 3,500 PIT-tagged bonytail.

Fish-health monitoring found no disease. Sixty spawned bonytail were used for disease inspection, and the report states that Wahweap "remains disease free," which allows continued stocking in Utah.

Wild-spawned bonytail presented a management problem. The hatchery destroyed 25,000 bonytail (20 to 100 mm, in very good condition) because their parentage was unknown. This followed the hatchery's excess fish policy and the [[Biology Committee]]'s guidance. The report says all other losses were due to routine hatchery work.

## Key Findings

**Stocking and production (2005)**
- Total bonytail stocked: **10,574**. The four lots sum to this total: 574 + 3,400 + 3,100 + 3,500.
- Received and reared **20,000 bonytail fry** from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Weekly sampling used minnow traps, seines, and cast nets.
- Fish from the 2000 tagging at Stewart Lake were judged unsuitable as brood source, but acceptable for stocking because their source lots dated from the late 1990s.
- All stocked fish in 2005 were tagged. The 10,000-fish stocking plan was PIT-tagged. Old and new tags were both applied to remove the last of the old tags.

**Losses and culling**
- **25,000 wild-spawned bonytail** were destroyed because exact parentage was unknown.

**Recommendations**
- Continue rearing bonytail at Wahweap to meet the stocking needs of the Integrated Stocking Plan (Ellers et al., 2003 as cited).
- Maintain the razorback sucker backup broodstock.
- Supply either species as requested by the Biology Committee.
- PIT-tag 10,000 bonytail and stock them in fall 2006.
