# Addition of Hart's Rivulus (Rivulus hartii) to the California Fauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This short note reports the first confirmed record of [[Rivulus Hartii]] (Hart's Rivulus), a South American cyprinodont, in California. The author collected the species on January 13, 1967, from a small ditch below a [[Hot Mineral Spring Ditch|hot mineral spring]] beside a [[Tropical Fish Farm]] in [[Imperial County]], near the eastern shore of the [[Salton Sea]]. Additional specimens collected in November 1968 were identified by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] as the same species. The author concludes that the fish most likely entered the ditch from the fish farm, where the species had been reared, and that the farm has since closed.

## Background & Description

The note is written by [[James A. St. Amant]] of the Inland Fisheries Region 5, [[California Department of Fish and Game]]. It was accepted in December 1969 and reprinted from *California Fish and Game* 56(2):138, 1970. It forms a companion to St. Amant's 1966 report on the introduction of [[Tilapia Mossambica]] to California.

Specimens reported:
- **January 13, 1967:** one specimen, 35 mm total length (first record; identified by Hubbs).
- **November 13, 1968:** one adult male and two adult females, 42 to 45 mm standard length (SL), identified by Hubbs as *R. hartii*.
- **1969:** additional adults and juveniles were collected during inspection trips.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The ditch lies below a hot mineral spring on the eastern side of the Salton Sea. Other fishes recorded in the same ditch were:
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*[[Gambusia Affinis]]*)
- Sailfin molly (*Poecilia latipinna*) and *Poecilia mexicana*
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
- [[Tilapia Mossambica]] (*Tilapia mossambica*), first discovered at this locality in 1964

Thus the ditch held a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fishes. Tilapia had already been established there, which made the site an important place to track non-native species. The author treats the fish farm as the probable introduction pathway: [[Hart's Rivulus]] had once been reared there, and the author believes the farm was the source of the population.

## Key Findings

- **First California record:** *Rivulus hartii* was confirmed in California on January 13, 1967, in a ditch below a hot mineral spring adjacent to the tropical fish farm, Imperial County.
- **Taxonomic confirmation:** Carl L. Hubbs identified both the 1967 and the 1968 specimens as *R. hartii*.
- **Persistence:** Adults and juveniles were found again in 1968 and during 1969 inspection trips, indicating that the population had established and persisted.
- **Likely source:** The fish farm, where the species had been reared, is the most probable introduction source. The farm is now closed.
- **Co-occurring species:** The same ditch held mosquitofish, two live-bearer species (*Poecilia*), desert pupfish, and tilapia, indicating a mixed native and introduced fish community.
- **Context:** The note extends the author's documentation of non-native fish introductions at the Salton Sea, following the 1964 discovery of tilapia in the same area.
