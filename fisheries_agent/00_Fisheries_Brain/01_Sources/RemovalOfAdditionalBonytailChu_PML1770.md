# Removal of Additional Bonytail Chubs from Palm Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Parker office (dated 29 April 1994) reports a netting and electrofishing effort at [[Palm Lake]], on the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] in Arizona, to remove additional [[Bonytail Chub]]. A total of 25 bonytail were captured by [[Trammel Netting]], all were [[PIT Tagging|PIT tagged]], and they were translocated to the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] at [[Lake Havasu]] and released by 1000 hrs on 28 April. Two males died during processing. Three [[Colorado Squawfish]] and more than 50 [[Yellow Bullhead]] were also collected, and non-lethal pathogen sampling was performed.

## Background & Description

The effort was carried out by personnel of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (nongame branch), with participation from [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[Arizona State University Research Park|Arizona State University]], and the Fish and Wildlife Service. The memo is addressed to the AZFRO project leader and is a field operations report rather than a research paper.

Bonytail were described as follows:

- **Males** (n not separately stated): total length 357–398 mm (mean 374 mm); mean weight 378 g (range 300–530 g).
- **Females**: mean total length 407 mm (range 359–490 mm); mean weight 513 g (range 370–770 g).

The memo does not state how many of the 25 fish were male or female.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Palm Lake is a site where native fish were removed from the water body and moved to a different habitat. The document records no water chemistry, habitat measurements, or invertebrate or plankton data. The released fish were placed in the Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge, a different aquatic setting.

Non-target species were handled with the native fish. The large yellow bullhead catch indicates the lake holds an abundant non-native or non-target population. Parasite and disease screening of these fish was carried out by [[Fish Health]] staff from Pinetop.

## Key Findings

- **Capture:** 25 bonytail chub were captured at Palm Lake, all by trammel netting. Two of three nets were pulled on the evening of 27 April, and the net and boat were removed on the morning of 28 April.
- **Tagging and release:** All 25 bonytail were PIT tagged, transported to Lake Havasu, and released by 1000 hrs on 28 April at Office Cove, adjacent to the refuge and FRO headquarters.
- **Mortality:** Two males died, weighing 350 g (348 mm) and 530 g (398 mm). No obvious cause was identified, since all fish had been handled the same way. The carcasses are frozen at the Parker FRO.
- **Colorado squawfish:** Three were captured, transported to the Arizona State University Research Park, released, and PIT tagged.
- **Yellow bullhead:** More than 50 were captured. Three were taken during the electrofishing survey, and [[Parasite and Disease Screening]] was performed on many of them.
- **Pathogen sampling:** Bonytail and Colorado squawfish were checked for protozoan and bacteriological pathogens using non-lethal methods. A report will be issued when available.
- **Collaboration:** The effort involved The Nature Conservancy, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State University, and the Fish and Wildlife Service.
