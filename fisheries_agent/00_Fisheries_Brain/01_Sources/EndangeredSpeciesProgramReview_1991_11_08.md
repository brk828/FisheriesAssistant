## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) office, dated November 8, 1991, addressed to Dr. W.L. Minckley at Arizona State University. Rather than presenting primary research data, the memo serves as a transmittal cover letter distributing four key research enclosures from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regarding the [[Endangered Species Program]] review process. The core subject matter concerns ongoing research into microhabitat use by the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), a critical tributary of the [[Colorado River]] system. The note catalogs the specific study proposals and sampling protocols being reviewed by the participating committee.

## Key Findings
As this document is a review memo rather than a data report, the "key findings" are the identification of four specific research deliverables being circulated for scientific committee review:

1. **Habitat Use Study:** A primary report detailing habitat use by *Gila cypha* in the LCR and other Colorado River tributaries.
2. **Revised Study Plan:** The "USFWS-Pinetop FAO General Study Plan," which outlines the methodology for assessing microhabitat use by all fishes in the LCR, with a special emphasis on the humpback chub.
3. **Sampling Protocols:** A proposal for "low-impact" fish sampling protocols intended for use in GCES Phase II research within the LCR, suggesting a management priority on minimizing stress/mortality to the endangered population during data collection.
4. **Methodology Report:** A specific technical report on the use of **hoopnets** and other methods to assess fish microhabitat use, indicating this as the primary capture technique being validated for the [[Phase II Research]] initiative.
