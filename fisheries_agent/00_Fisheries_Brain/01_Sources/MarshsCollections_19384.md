# Coahuila Fish Specimen Records and Tetragoneuria Larval Ecology

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #odonata #cyprinidae #corduliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a composite of three unrelated items. Its first and largest part is a catalog of freshwater fish specimen records from Coahuila, Mexico, collected in 1938 and 1939, with catalog numbers, species identities, localities and dates. Its second part is a scientific study of larval development in three dragonfly species of the genus [[Tetragoneuria]] (family Corduliidae), focused on the number and duration of larval [[Instar Stadia]] (stadia). Its third part is a set of figure legends and map captions for the same dragonfly study, covering growth measurements, temperature data and distribution in the Great Lakes Region. Because the text is partly garbled by OCR, some species and place names are uncertain and are reported here as they most likely appear.

## Background & Description

**Fish specimen catalog (Pages 1, 3):** The catalog lists about 100 entries, each with a catalog number in the 130332–130436 range and a specimen count in parentheses. Lots include paratypes and holotypes, including [[Lucania interioris]], [[Cyprinodon atrorus]] and [[Gambusia longispinus]]. The taxa most frequently recorded include:

- [[Dorosoma cepedianum]] (Gizzard Shad), with the subspecies "D. c. couchi" appearing in many lots (for example, 63 and 85 specimens in separate lots).
- [[Astyanax mexicanus]] (Mexican tetra), including cave-associated forms.
- [[Notropis rutilus]] and [[Notropis amabilis]], with large lots (for example, 132 and 53 specimens).
- [[Lepomis macrochirus]] (Bluegill), [[Lepomis megalotis]] (Longear Sunfish) and hybrids between them.
- [[Gambusia affinis]] and [[Gambusia triunca]] (mosquitofish).
- [[Herichthys cyanoguttatum]] (a cichlid), [[Micropterus salmoides]] (Largemouth Bass), [[Ictalurus lacustris]] (catfish) and [[Lepisosteus osseus]] (Longnose Gar).

Localities include Rio Sabinas, Rio Salado, Ojo Caliente, Cuatro Cienegas, Rio San Juan, Alameda Springs, Chilipitin Springs and a pool near Muzquiz. Collection dates run from March 1938 to August 1939. Several lots are marked "various species important to [the collection] with little or no data."

**Dragonfly larval study (Pages 2–3):** The study concerns three species of [[Tetragoneuria]]: [[Tetragoneuria cynosura]], [[Tetragoneuria spinigera]] and [[Tetragoneuria canis]]. Molts were used to determine larval stages, together with exuvial (shed skin) characteristics and size classes. The author notes that molt number alone does not reliably indicate age, because a molt can be skipped or added under certain conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish records document the freshwater fauna of northeastern Mexico, including spring-fed systems (Cuatro Cienegas, Ojo Caliente, Alameda Springs, Chilipitin Springs), rivers (Rio Sabinas, Rio Salado, Rio San Juan) and a bayou. The mix of cave-associated and surface forms, such as [[Astyanax mexicanus]], makes these localities relevant to studies of [[Cave Fish Adaptation]] and regional biogeography.

For the dragonflies, the study ties larval development to environmental conditions. Climographs were prepared for six regionally representative localities in Michigan, and mean April–June temperatures for 1954 were compared across five localities. The lake sites named in the map and chart legends are Joslin Lake, Isle St. Helen, Otsego Lake, Cisco Lake, Gogebic Lake, Perch Lake, Douglas Lake and Lake Mary. Charts plot the mean annual Fahrenheit temperature for each lake, and a "South-to-North Gradient" is shown for male adults of *T. cynosura* and *T. spinigera*. Daily hatching rates were also recorded, and egg strings and larval head and labium structures are illustrated for the species.

Distribution maps show the Great Lakes Region ranges of the three species, with predicted ranges shaded in the North American insect distribution maps.

## Key Findings

**Fish specimen records**
- Roughly 100 catalog entries from Coahuila, Mexico, collected 1938–1939, with a mix of common species and type specimens (holotypes and paratypes).
- The largest single lots are for [[Notropis rutilus]] (132 specimens, Rio Salado), [[Lucania interioris]]-related paratypes and [[Cyprinodon atrorus]] paratypes (368 in one entry, although the OCR is uncertain).

**Larval development in Tetragoneuria**
- Twelve larval stadia plus a prolarval stage occur in *T. cynosura* and *T. spinigera*. *T. canis* has thirteen stadia plus the prolarval stage.
- Some stadia are consolidated: fifth to seventh stages in *T. spinigera* and *T. cynosura*, and eighth to tenth stages in *T. canis*. Consolidated stages were folded into the best-fitting size class and excluded from duration calculations.
- Duration of larval stadia was based on records from a large number of individuals, summarized in Table 2 (not reproduced here). Average stadium duration, range and sample size are given there.
- Key results: (1) substantial variation in larval duration among the three species; (2) considerable variation in duration of a given stadium within each species; (3) in *T. cynosura* and *T. spinigera*, average stadium duration rose through the first four stadia, fell to the seventh (*spinigera*) or eighth (*cynosura*) stage, and then increased again; (4) *T. spinigera* developed faster through the middle stages (the text is truncated at this point).

**Morphometric and environmental data**
- Growth was measured as cumulative head width, segment width and length of the superior anal appendage across instars, plus the percentage of total antennal length occupied by each segment.
- Mean, standard error and range were compared for last-instar larvae and adult males.
- Temperature data (climographs and April–June means) were used to relate development to thermal regime across Michigan localities.
