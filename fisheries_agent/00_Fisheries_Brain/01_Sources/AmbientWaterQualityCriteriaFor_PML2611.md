# Ambient Water Quality Criteria for Arsenic

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-toxicology

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) document, prepared by the Office of Research and Development's [[Environmental Research Laboratories]] in Duluth and Narragansett, derives national aquatic life water quality criteria for [[Arsenic]] under Section 304(a)(1) of the Clean Water Act. It compiles acute and chronic toxicity, aquatic plant toxicity and bioaccumulation data for freshwater and saltwater organisms, evaluates speciation and analytical measurement issues, and proposes criterion concentrations with recommended exceedance frequencies and design-flow guidance. The document is the 1985 revision of earlier EPA arsenic criteria and is a scientific assessment rather than an enforceable standard until adopted by states.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Ambient aquatic life criteria for arsenic, with separate treatment of inorganic arsenic(III), inorganic arsenic(V), and organic forms such as [[Monosodium Methanearsonate]] (MSMA) and dimethylarsinic acid.
- **Legal basis:** Section 304(a)(1) of the Clean Water Act of 1977 requires publication of criteria reflecting the latest scientific knowledge. Section 303(c)(2) standards adopted by states make criteria enforceable.
- **Mechanism:** Arsenic(III) reacts with sulfhydryl groups of proteins, so enzyme inhibition is likely the primary toxic mode. Arsenic(V) reacts less readily with sulfhydryls but may uncouple oxidative phosphorylation.
- **Chemistry:** Arsenic speciation in water is controlled by chemical, biochemical and geochemical reactions. Inorganic arsenic(III) oxidizes slowly to arsenic(V) at neutral pH, and the reaction proceeds measurably over several days in strongly alkaline or acidic solutions.
- **Measurement approach:** Because total recoverable arsenic cannot distinguish arsenic(III) from arsenic(V), the authors favored an operational "acid-soluble" measurement (filtered through 0.45 µm after acidification to pH 1.5–2.0 with nitric acid). EPA recommends total recoverable measurement until approved acid-soluble methods exist.
- **Guidelines:** Derivation follows the [[Guidelines for Deriving Numerical National Water Quality Criteria]] (Stephan et al. 1985).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Freshwater acute toxicity:** Sixteen species mean acute values for inorganic arsenic(III) range from 812 µg/L (a cladoceran) to 27,000 µg/L (a midge). Hardness did not affect bluegill toxicity. Cladocerans, amphipods and stoneflies are generally more sensitive than fish.
- **Saltwater acute toxicity:** Twelve species have acute values for arsenic(III) from 232 µg/L (Dungeness crab zoea) to 16,030 µg/L (Atlantic silverside). Invertebrates were generally more sensitive than fish, and the most sensitive genus was about 69 times more sensitive than the most resistant.
- **Plants:** Freshwater algae were highly variable. The alga [[Scenedesmus Obliquus]] was the most sensitive freshwater plant to arsenic(V), with a 14-day EC50 of 48 µg/L. Saltwater diatoms such as [[Skeletonema Costatum]] and [[Thalassiosira Aestivalis]] showed growth effects at about 20 µg/L and 22 µg/L.
- **Bioaccumulation:** Freshwater bioconcentration factors (BCFs) were generally low, with a maximum of 17 in a snail exposed to arsenic(III). The oyster [[Crassostrea Virginica]] had a BCF of 350 after 112 days. No Final Residue Value could be derived because no tissue residue standard exists.
- **Environmental modifiers:** Food reduced the toxicity of arsenic(III) to [[Daphnia Magna]] by about a factor of 3. Increased temperature shortened median lethal time for green sunfish. Phosphate reduced arsenic(V) toxicity to the red macroalga [[Champia Parvula]].
- **Excluded data:** Studies were excluded for non-North American species, mixtures, distilled-water or non-flow-through exposures, low control survival, and insufficient reporting. Several large lists of such studies are omitted here.

## Key Findings

- **Freshwater Final Acute Value (arsenic(III)):** 718.2 µg/L. Final Acute-Chronic Ratio: 3.803. Final Chronic Value: 188.9 µg/L.
- **Saltwater Final Acute Value (arsenic(III)):** 137.1 µg/L. Final Chronic Value: 36.05 µg/L.
- **Recommended freshwater criteria (arsenic(III)):** Four-day average not to exceed 190 µg/L more than once every three years; one-hour average not to exceed 360 µg/L more than once every three years.
- **Recommended saltwater criteria (arsenic(III)):** Four-day average not to exceed 36 µg/L more than once every three years; one-hour average not to exceed 69 µg/L more than once every three years. The authors note this may be too high where Skeletonema costatum or Thalassiosira aestivalis are ecologically important.
- **Insufficient data:** No freshwater criteria were derived for arsenic(V), MSMA or other organic arsenic compounds. Saltwater data for arsenic(V) are too limited for a Final Acute Value.
- **Arsenic(V) sensitivity:** The freshwater fathead minnow acute-chronic ratio for arsenic(V) was 28.71, and arsenic(V) affected some freshwater plants at concentrations as low as 48 µg/L.
- **Early life stages:** The most sensitive animal endpoint was a 7-day test on toad embryos and larvae, with an effect at 40 µg/L, about 4.5 times below the freshwater Final Chronic Value.
- **Recovery and design:** The three-year exceedance frequency reflects EPA's judgment of recovery time for unstressed systems. Dynamic wasteload models are preferred, with steady-state models (1Q5 or 1Q10 for CMC; 7Q5 or 7Q10 for CCC) as an interim alternative.
- **Data gaps:** The document identifies a need for more chronic data on saltwater species, organic arsenic compounds and arsenic(V), and for approved acid-soluble analytical methods.
