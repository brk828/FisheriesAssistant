# Summer Food Habits of Fishes from Two Springs in East-Central Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines the summer diets of four fish taxa from two [[Great Basin]] desert springs in east-central Nevada. Stomach contents were analysed for the [[White River Springfish]], the [[Speckled Dace]] subspecies *Rhinichthys osculus velifer*, the [[Desert Sucker]] subspecies *Catostomus clarkii intermedius*, and the [[Railroad Valley Springfish]]. Three taxa were sampled at [[Preston Big Spring]] and one at [[Big Spring at Lockes Ranch]]. The diets differed clearly among species, ranging from herbivory to omnivory to carnivory. The authors argue that food habits are a basic step toward understanding the dynamics of desert aquatic ecosystems, which the authors note have been largely ignored and face growing threats.

## Background & Description

- **Study focus:** Summer feeding ecology of desert fishes in springs of the [[Great Basin]], a region where the authors describe the ecology of many fishes as "essentially ignored."
- **Rationale:** Rising threats to desert ecosystems make knowledge of food webs and trophic dynamics urgent. Diet data are presented as a foundation for that understanding.
- **Study species:**
  - *Crenichthys baileyi albivallis* (White River springfish), the primary focus at Preston Big Spring.
  - *Rhinichthys osculus velifer* (speckled dace subspecies), included as a spring-dwelling cyprinid.
  - *Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarkii intermedius* (desert sucker), a catostomid of the White River system.
  - *Crenichthys nevadae* (Railroad Valley springfish), sampled at Lockes Ranch to allow comparison with *C. baileyi*.
- **Excluded species:** The [[White River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda albivallis*) historically inhabited Preston Big Spring but has been extirpated there, so it was not examined.
- **Geography:** Big Spring at Lockes Ranch lies in Nye County, about 60 km southwest of Preston Big Spring. Preston Big Spring is located on the course of the pluvial White River in White Pine County.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Preston Big Spring:**
- Described as a large spring with a large algal flora dominated by chlorophytes (green algae).
- Supports a diverse aquatic fauna.

**Big Spring at Lockes Ranch:**
- Described as warm, somewhat smaller, and as having a small algal flora dominated by cyanophytes (blue-green algae).
- Supports a depauperate (impoverished) fauna.

The contrast between the two springs provides a natural comparison of food-web conditions. Prior records were sparse:
- *C. clarkii* from the Virgin River contained largely amorphous organic material, filamentous algae, sand, and occasional chironomid larvae (Cross, 1975).
- Smith (1966) reported that *C. clarkii* fed mainly on microscopic periphyton and organic debris, with occasional invertebrates.
- No diet data had been published for the White River subspecies of *C. clarkii*, or for *R. osculus velifer*. Related speckled dace forms are typically carnivorous, feeding on benthic invertebrates (Moyle, 1976).
- The physiological tolerance of *C. baileyi* to temperature and other extremes, and aspects of its reproduction, had been studied, but its food habits were unknown.
- The life history of *C. nevadae* was unknown.

## Key Findings

**Diet by species (summer):**

| Species | Trophic category | Reported diet |
|---|---|---|
| *Crenichthys baileyi albivallis* | Primarily herbivorous | Large volumes of abundant green algae. Some opportunistic individuals consumed mostly insect larvae. |
| *Rhinichthys osculus velifer* | Omnivorous | A wide variety of food items. |
| *Catostomus clarkii intermedius* | Plant-feeding | Plant material and some detritus. |
| *Crenichthys nevadae* | Carnivorous | Preferred ostracods and avoided blue-green algae. |

**Principal conclusions:**
1. Diets among the four taxa differed substantially, consistent with their different feeding strategies.
2. *C. baileyi* relied heavily on green algae at Preston Big Spring, with a minority of individuals shifting toward insect larvae.
3. *C. nevadae* showed a clear preference for ostracods and avoided cyanophytes, in contrast to its congener at Preston Big Spring.
4. The results provide the first diet records for the White River populations of *C. clarkii* and *R. osculus velifer*, filling gaps identified in earlier literature.

**Limitations:** The extracted text does not give sample sizes, stomach-analysis methods, or quantitative diet statistics for the present study, so these cannot be reported.
