# Federal Register Listings of Klamath Basin Suckers, Houghton's Goldenrod and Pitcher's Thistle (1988)

#ichthyology #botany #catostomidae #asteraceae

## Executive Summary

This document is a scanned, partly illegible compilation of 1988 Federal Register pages (Vol. 53, No. 137, Monday, July 18, 1988, plus related rules) issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. It covers three listing actions:

- **Klamath Basin suckers** ([[Lost River Sucker]] and [[Shortnose Sucker]]): a proposal and supporting material for endangered status, with comments from federal agencies, tribes and state bodies.
- ***Solidago houghtonii*** ([[Houghton's Goldenrod]]): a final determination classifying the plant as **threatened** in Michigan and Ontario.
- ***Cirsium pitcheri*** ([[Pitcher's Thistle]]): a final rule listing the plant as **threatened**, effective August 12, 1988.

Each listing relies on the five listing factors of Section 4(a)(1) of the Act, and each rejects designation of [[Critical Habitat]] as not prudent, citing vandalism risk.

## Background & Description

**Klamath Basin suckers.** The text describes these as fishes of the Klamath River basin (Oregon and California), a region with a history of sucker population declines. Much of the text is unreadable in the scan, so the species' morphology and life history cannot be reliably reconstructed here. The document refers to the family Catostomidae and cites literature on the sucker fauna of the basin.

**Houghton's goldenrod (*Solidago houghtonii*).** A plant of Great Lakes shoreline dunes, classified as a threatened species in Michigan under state law. Its narrow habitat requirements are not fully known.

**Pitcher's thistle (*Cirsium pitcheri*).** A perennial composite (Asteraceae) of open, well-drained foredunes and sand blowouts along the Great Lakes. Its reproductive biology is documented in the text:
- It reproduces only by seed, and requires about 3 to 10 years between germination and flowering.
- Seeds are dispersed by a pappus that acts like a parachute, so seeds mostly settle downwind; seedling clusters are attributed to dispersal of whole flower heads.
- Flowering occurs in June and can continue to mid-August; seed dispersal begins in late July.
- It originated in the Great Plains and spread east as the last ice age receded, per the cited literature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Dune and shoreline dynamics.** Both Great Lakes listings depend on dynamic dune systems. The text states that Pitcher's thistle tolerates infrequent disturbance (roughly once every 5 to 10 years) and may need periodic disturbance to keep an early-successional, sparsely vegetated state. Stabilized foredunes and shrub encroachment reduce suitable habitat.

**Water levels.** High water levels on the Great Lakes are cited as a threat to Houghton's goldenrod. Continuous high levels at some lakeside populations have caused many plants to stop flowering.

**Human pressure.** Shoreline development, recreational vehicles, bulldozing of dunes for lake views, road construction and mowing or trampling are repeatedly identified as the main pressures on both plants.

**Klamath Basin.** The text refers to water management, tribal interests, and the operation of dams and reservoirs in the basin as factors affecting the suckers, but the scan is too degraded to give specifics.

## Key Findings

### Klamath Basin Suckers
- The document records a proposal to list the Lost River and shortnose suckers as endangered, with support from federal agencies (including the Forest Service, Bureau of Land Management and Bureau of Reclamation), state fish and game agencies in California and Oregon, the Klamath Tribe and conservation groups.
- Commenters argued that critical habitat should be designated for both species; the text notes that the status of critical habitat was still under discussion.

### Houghton's Goldenrod (*Solidago houghtonii*)
- **Status:** Threatened (final determination).
- **Known range:** about 52 sites (figure partly garbled in scan) in seven Michigan counties along Lakes Michigan and Huron, two inland sites in Crawford County, and several sites in Ontario.
- **Land ownership:** of the Michigan sites, 14 are publicly owned (state, federal and one Nature Conservancy site, as stated in the text); the remainder, roughly 38 (scan partly unclear), are privately owned and unprotected.
- **Decline:** about 10 populations (approximately 20 percent) may have been extirpated in the previous 10 years.
- **Threat factors:** shoreline development (including a destroyed population in Cheboygan County), disturbance of lakefront dunes, lake-level changes, and collection (considered a low risk because the plant is not commercially attractive).
- **Protection:** the species is state-listed as threatened in Michigan; federal listing would add protection.
- **Petition history:** the Service found in 1983 and 1984 that listing was warranted but precluded; a proposed rule was published August 19, 1987 (52 FR 31045).
- **Comments:** six comments were received. The Michigan Department of Military Affairs noted that the plant's presence at Camp Grayling had caused no problems. The Michigan Nature Conservancy supported listing.

### Pitcher's Thistle (*Cirsium pitcheri*)
- **Status:** Threatened, effective August 12, 1988.
- **Range:** Great Lakes shores of Indiana, Michigan, Wisconsin and Ontario. Michigan holds the greatest part of the range, with sites in 25 counties (the site count is garbled in the scan).
- **Threat factors:**
  - Habitat loss: roughly 5 to 10 percent of the species' suitable habitat in Michigan has been lost to road and building construction (the Michigan figure is partly unclear in the scan).
  - Dune destabilization: frequent disturbance and trampling can reduce colonies; some dunes were bulldozed to improve lake views.
  - Vandalism and collection: the species is attractive, and publishing its location could increase these risks.
- **Critical habitat:** not designated, as not prudent, because publishing detailed maps could increase vandalism.
- **Management actions:** habitat management on the [[Indiana Dunes National Lakeshore]], [[Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore]] and [[Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore]], and in the Nordhouse Dunes area of the Huron-Manistee National Forest. The Coast Guard manages a stretch of Lake Michigan shoreline containing a population.
- **Federal protection:** Section 7 consultation applies to federal actions; Section 9 prohibitions apply to import, export, interstate commerce and removal from federal land. Cultivated specimens are exempt if labeled "cultivated origin."
- **Environmental review:** the Service determined that no Environmental Assessment was required under NEPA.

### Process and Regulatory Context
- Listing actions follow Section 4(a)(1) of the Act and 50 CFR Part 424.
- Section 7 requires federal agencies to ensure their actions do not jeopardize listed species.
- The text shows that Michigan Department of Transportation road projects were flagged as potential conflicts with Pitcher's thistle, and that the Service planned to consider this in consultation.
