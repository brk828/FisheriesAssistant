# Aravaipa Creek Red Shiner Survey: October 1993 Summary Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] Nongame Native Fish Program summarizes a seine-based survey of [[Aravaipa Creek]] in Arizona, conducted 23–24 October 1993 to assess [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) distribution. Eleven reaches plus a headwater section were sampled, yielding a grand total of roughly 5,372 hauls. Only one red shiner was recorded, a single individual collected in the lower creek near the [[San Pedro River]] confluence. Data gaps, uneven sampling intensity, and a largely inexperienced crew limit how far the results can be generalized.

## Background & Description

The survey was coordinated by the Nongame Native Fish Program and distributed to partners including [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[Arizona State University]] (Dr. W.L. Minckley), the [[Bureau Of Land Management]], and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]]. The survey followed a standard protocol that defined the seineable sample area. Samples from the headwaters to 200 m below Chimney Rock fell outside that protocol area but were retained in the report.

Approximately 42 people participated on the first day and about 18 on the second. Many participants had limited or no experience with seining or fish identification, and volunteers were trained during the day. Gear consisted of several seine sizes, primarily 4'x6', 4'x10', 4'x15', and 6'x1/8" nets, with some larger 1.83 m and 2.74 m seines in the headwater reach. Reach-level data are tabulated from team leaders' returned field sheets, so omissions reflect incomplete records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Aravaipa Creek supports a native fish assemblage alongside several introduced species. The abbreviations used in the report cover:

- **Native species:** [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*).
- **Introduced or nonnative species:** [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and the red shiner itself.

The red shiner is a nonnative cyprinid of concern for native desert fishes, which is why its presence or absence in this creek was the survey's central question. The survey notes that the lower creek is where the red shiner was found, close to the San Pedro River, a major downstream connection.

## Key Findings

**Survey effort and coverage**

- The grand total across all sites was about 5,372+ hauls.
- Reach sizes ranged from roughly 230 hauls (headwaters to 200 m below Chimney Rock) to 823 hauls (Wagner Ranch to White's Ranch).
- Estimated stream coverage varied widely. Reported figures include 80% (Turkey Creek to Hell Hole Canyon), 100% length and 75% width (halfway section), 65% (Booger Canyon to Virgus Canyon), 60% (Virgus Canyon upstream), 75% (Holy Joe Ranch to Sycamore Tree), and 50% (Sycamore Tree to San Pedro River).
- The largest data gap was about 400 m unsampled at the lower end of the White's Ranch to Holy Joe Ranch section.

**Red shiner results**

- Only one red shiner was recorded in the entire survey.
- It was collected by Reimus et al. roughly six kilometers below the "Sycamore Tree" site and about five kilometers above the San Pedro River.
- The individual was not in a designated fish-count haul, so it was excluded from relative abundance calculations.

**Other species patterns**

- Reach 3 (halfway from Turkey Creek to Hell Hole Canyon) yielded a catch that was 100% native species.
- Paisano and Booger Canyons were sampled and recorded no exotics.
- Reach 6 (Virgus Canyon upstream) recorded no speckled dace, with [[Spikedace]] very common throughout and roundtail chub, Sonora sucker, and desert sucker common. [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Fathead Minnow]] were present, and specimens were preserved and given to Dr. Minckley.
- Breeding coloration was infrequently observed in loach minnow and speckled dace during the headwater sampling.

**Empty-haul rates**

Empty fish-count hauls ranged from 0% to 34% across reaches. The highest rates were at Virgus Canyon upstream (34%, 14 hauls) and Wagner Ranch to White's Ranch (26%, 22 hauls). Several reaches had very low empty rates, including Holy Joe Ranch to Sycamore Tree (1.9%) and Hell Hole to Booger Canyon (0%).

**Methodological caveats**

- Team leaders reported thorough sampling, but intensity was uneven.
- Inexperienced volunteers may have affected identification accuracy, particularly for small-bodied species.
- Some tables contain transcription gaps, so not every reach has complete species counts.
