# New Crayfishes from Georgia (Decapoda: Astacidae)

#carcinology #freshwater-ecology #astacidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 taxonomic paper by [[Horton H. Hobbs Jr.]] and [[Edward T. Hall Jr.]] (Smithsonian Institution and Georgia Water Quality Control Board) describes two new crayfish species from Georgia, both in the genus *Cambarus*. [[Cambarus Howardi]] belongs to the typical subgenus and is known from the upper Piedmont portion of the [[Chattahoochee River]] drainage. [[Cambarus Unestami]] belongs to the subgenus [[Depressicambarus]] and is known only from tributaries of the [[Tennessee River]] in Dade County, in the northwestern corner of the state. The paper provides diagnoses, morphological measurements, type-locality descriptions, distribution records, and notes on water quality and associated species. It is a crustacean systematics paper, not an ichthyological one, and it contains no fish data.

## Background & Description

The authors note that several undescribed *Cambarus* crayfishes occur in the Piedmont and submontane provinces of Georgia. Two of these are formally described here. The paper relies on [[Taxonomic Diagnosis]] features, including the shape of the [[Areola]], the rostrum, chela tubercle rows, and the [[First Pleopod]] of first-form males, plus the [[Annulus Ventralis]] of females. Diagnostic details for the subgenera are deferred to a separate work by Hobbs ("in press").

### Cambarus howardi

- **Diagnosis:** Eyes well developed; rostrum with strongly convergent margins lacking marginal spines; areola 3.0 to 4.9 times longer than broad, comprising 34.3 to 39.3% of carapace length; chela with a single row of mesial tubercles; first-form male first pleopod with a corneous central projection recurved at about 110°.
- **Coloration:** Olive to light green with scarlet markings, most conspicuous on the rostrum and chelae.
- **Type material:** Holotypic male (form I), allotypic female, and morphotypic male (form II), deposited in the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (nos. 129866, 129867, 129868). Paratypes include 6 male form I, 6 male form II, 11 females, 3 juvenile males, 1 juvenile female, and 1 female with eggs.
- **Measurements (mm):**

| Specimen | Carapace Length | Carapace Width | Carapace Height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holotype | 27.0 | 15.0 | 10.1 |
| Allotype | 32.0 | 17.0 | 12.1 |
| Morphotype | 22.6 | 11.6 | 8.3 |

- **Size:** Largest first-form male 31.8 mm carapace length; largest female 34 mm; smallest first-form male 27.6 mm.

### Cambarus unestami

- **Diagnosis:** Areola 3.5 to 4.7 times longer than broad, comprising 32.6 to 37.4% of carapace length; suborbital angle prominent and subacute; chela with at least two rows of mesial and mesiodorsal palm tubercles; first-form male central projection recurved at about 125° and extending beyond the tip of the mesial process.
- **Coloration:** Brown to olive green with brownish black spots and bars, plus four broken longitudinal stripes on the abdomen.
- **Type material:** Holotypic male (form I), allotypic female with eggs, and morphotypic male (form II), deposited in the Smithsonian Institution (nos. 129863, 129864, 129865). Paratypes include 1 male form I, 14 male form II, 11 females, 23 juvenile males, 17 juvenile females, and 1 female with eggs.
- **Measurements (mm):**

| Specimen | Carapace Length | Carapace Width | Carapace Height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holotype | 29.6 | 15.2 | 10.8 |
| Allotype | 40.4 | 21.2 | 16.5 |
| Morphotype | 27.8 | 14.1 | 10.7 |

- **Size:** Largest first-form male 31.4 mm; allotype (largest female) 40.2 mm in the text (40.4 mm in Table 2).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat and Associates

- ***C. howardi*** inhabits moderately to swiftly flowing streams and was found only where the substrate is rocky or rubble. The type locality, [[Sope Creek]] (tributary to the Chattahoochee, Cobb County), is a 70-foot-wide stream with cascading current over bedrock and stones that trap filamentous algae. Its shoreline is heavily wooded. Co-occurring crayfishes were [[Cambarus Latimanus]] and [[Procambarus Spiculifer]].
- ***C. unestami*** is known only from the type locality, [[Daniel Creek]] (Dade County), a clear stream about 15 feet wide and 1.5 feet deep with moderate current over bedrock and sand. The stream lies in a deep ravine with *Pinus* sp. and *Cornus florida*, and no macroscopic plant growth was observed. No other crayfish were found there. Juveniles tentatively assigned to this species came from two other sites, and the only associate recorded was [[Cambarus Striatus]].

### Water Quality and Pollution

The paper reports that Sope Creek is heavily polluted. It originates in a densely populated area northeast of Marietta and receives storm sewer runoff and sewage treatment plant effluent near its source. Six stations were set up above the mouth on 21 July 1966. Reported [[Dissolved Oxygen]] saturation values were 87, 40, 23, 34, and 80.5%. *C. howardi* was found only at the uppermost station (1) and the lowermost station (6). The authors suggest it requires high oxygen concentrations and that the turbulent reach just above station 6 keeps oxygen levels high enough for the species to persist in the lower reaches. The data are a single sampling date.

### Distribution

- ***C. howardi*** is recorded from five localities in the upper Piedmont of the Chattahoochee drainage, including Hall County (a tributary of Balus Creek), Cobb County ([[Sope Creek]], [[Nickajack Creek]]), and Douglas County ([[Sweetwater Creek]], [[Dog River]]).
- ***C. unestami*** is known definitively only from the type locality, a tributary to Lookout Creek.

### Life History

- ***C. howardi*** first-form males were collected in April, May, and October. Females with eggs were taken in April (two) and May (one).
- ***C. unestami*** first-form males and females with eggs were collected at the type locality on 5 May 1967 and on 23 April 1968.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species described:** *Cambarus howardi* (subgenus *Cambarus*) and *Cambarus unestami* (subgenus *Depressicambarus*) are formally described and differentiated from congeners on the basis of areola proportions, chela tubercles, and first-pleopod structure.
2. **Closest relative:** *C. howardi* is most similar to [[Cambarus Bartonii]], differing in its more sharply acuminate rostrum, shorter and more robust chela, and vivid scarlet markings. The authors suggest it may prove to be the southernmost subspecies of *C. bartonii*.
3. **Distinguishing features of *C. unestami*:** It has a subterminal notch on the central projection of the first pleopod, which separates it from *C. floridanus*, *C. latimanus*, *C. reduncus*, and *C. striatus*. It lacks rostral marginal tubercles, which separates it from *C. halli* and *C. obstipus*.
4. **Restricted ranges:** *C. howardi* occurs in five localities in the upper Piedmont Chattahoochee drainage. *C. unestami* is known from a single definite locality, which makes it a narrow-range endemic.
5. **Pollution sensitivity:** Sope Creek, the *C. howardi* type locality, is heavily polluted, yet the species persists only at the upstream and downstream stations where oxygen levels are higher. This is a key observation about its environmental requirements.
6. **Intraspecific variation:** *C. howardi* from Douglas County and the Dog River show denser areolar punctation and other differences from the type population. *C. unestami* is remarkably uniform, with only minor variation in tubercle counts.
7. **Type deposition:** All holotypes, allotypes, and morphotypes are deposited in the Smithsonian Institution, with paratype series listed for both species.
8. **Etymology:** *C. howardi* honors Ralph S. Howard Jr. of the [[Georgia Water Quality Control Board]], for his work on Georgia waterways. *C. unestami* honors Dr. Torgny Unestam of the University of Uppsala, a collecting companion.
