# Two New Species of Barbus (Pisces: Cyprinidae) from Southeastern Dahomey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 taxonomic paper by [[Paul V. Loiselle]] and [[Roger L. Welcomme]] describes two new cyprinid species, *Barbus (Enteromius) sylvaticus* and *Barbus lorenzi*, from the [[Iguidi River]], a small permanent forest stream in extreme southeastern Dahomey (now Benin). Both were collected during a freshwater fish inventory conducted by Welcomme as an FAO Fisheries Officer. The paper covers morphology, coloration, affinities with related [[Barbus]] species, distribution, habitat, feeding, sexual dimorphism, and reproduction. Both species are very small adults and lack barbels, and the authors interpret their small size as an adaptation to mineral-poor forest stream conditions.

## Background & Description

**Study context:** The material was collected during a systematic inventory of Dahomey's freshwater fishes. Laboratory descriptions were largely carried out by Loiselle, with supplementary observations by Welcomme.

**Barbus (Enteromius) sylvaticus sp. nov.**
- Holotype: adult female, 19.0 mm standard length (SL), collected at Lagbé, 16 October 1969. Paratypes: 14.5–18.0 mm SL.
- Body somewhat compressed; snout rounded; mouth moderate, subterminal, and protrusible downward; no barbels.
- Distinct [[Enteromius Pit-Lines]] on the snout, cheeks, preoperculum, operculum, and post-orbital region, but absent from the dorsal head surface and interorbital area.
- No externally evident lateral line; scales lack longitudinal striae; 20–23 scales in the median longitudinal series.
- Dorsal fin rays III,7; anal fin rays III,5; pelvic fin rays 5.
- Coloration in life: translucent orange-beige body, reticulate scale margins, a large black caudal peduncle spot, and vivid red triangular markings at the caudal and dorsal fin bases.
- Smallest described member of its subgenus.

**Barbus lorenzi sp. nov.**
- Holotype: female, 16.0 mm SL, collected at Bokotou, 17 October 1969. Paratypes: two females, 13.7 and 14.0 mm SL (description based on five specimens, 13.7–16.0 mm SL).
- Nearly terminal, non-protrusible mouth; two nares sharing a common atrium; no barbels and no cephalic pit-lines.
- No externally evident lateral line; scales have radial striae; 28–29 scales in the median longitudinal series; 18 scale rows around the body.
- Dorsal fin rays II,6 (as stated in the paper's comparison section); anal fin rays III,5; pelvic origin well in advance of the dorsal origin.
- Coloration in life: translucent orange body, silvery iris, a small iridescent blue opercular spot, a large heart-shaped caudal peduncle spot, and orange-red caudal lobe bases. Breeding males show a violet flush on the lower opercular and visceral regions.
- Named in honour of [[Konrad Lorenz]], the Austrian ethologist.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and distribution:**
- *B. sylvaticus* is known only from the Iguidi River. It is characteristically found in quiet inshore waters over thick peaty detritus. The authors suspect it is less abundant than it appears, because peaty substrate hinders fine-meshed seines and scoop nets.
- The Iguidi belongs to a complex of forest streams west of the Niger delta, interconnected at their mouths by coastal lagoons. The authors predict the species will be found elsewhere in this system.
- *B. lorenzi* is also known only from the Iguidi River. It occurs in quiet water near the edges of [[Nymphaea]] beds, always in the upper third of the water column. It is extremely abundant and, with [[Aplocheilichthys macrophthalmus]], is numerically the most important species in the river.

**Feeding:**
- *B. sylvaticus* is a bottom feeder. Gut contents were mainly crustacean remains (copepods and ostracods), with bottom material, detritus, and mineral particles.
- *B. lorenzi* feeds in midwater on suspended particles and browses on aufwuchs. Gut contents were mostly epiphytic diatoms and epidermal cells of higher plants, plus small crustaceans.

**Environmental interpretation:** The authors link the reduced size of *B. sylvaticus* to soft, mineral-poor forest stream water, where calcium scarcity limits skeletal and scale formation. They note that forest-dwelling forms in the region tend to be smaller, with more fragile bones and thinner scales than savannah relatives. Comparisons cited include [[Tilapia galilaea]] forest and savannah subspecies and [[Lepidarchus adonis]], a scaleless Ghanaian characin.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species described.** *B. (E.) sylvaticus* is placed in subgenus *Enteromius* because of its pit-line pattern. It differs from known members in lacking dorsal head pit-lines, barbels, and an external lateral line, and in its very small adult size. *B. lorenzi* is described as the smallest African Barbus described to date.

2. **Pit-line structure confirmed.** The cephalic pit-lines of *B. sylvaticus* are identical in structure to those of West African *Enteromius*, and unlike those of species such as *B. pumilus*, *B. anema*, *B. stigmatopygus*, and *B. leonensis*, which have widely spaced pits and no raised ridges.

3. **Independent loss of characters.** The authors argue that barbel loss and lateral line reduction have occurred independently in several Barbus lineages, probably in response to environmental pressures. *B. sylvaticus* represents an extreme case combining both.

4. **Size as adaptation.** Small size in *B. sylvaticus* is interpreted as an adaptive response to forest stream conditions, especially calcium limitation.

5. **Reproduction in *B. sylvaticus*:**
   - All females in the type series had mature ovaries, with mature ova of 1.5–1.8 mm diameter.
   - Egg counts were 45–60 per female (18.0 mm SL). Between 10 and 15 percent of ovarian eggs could be loosely judged mature.
   - Specimens were collected in October at the start of the dry season, and all females were ripe.
   - Ova at all developmental stages suggest continuous spawning over several months, which is atypical for Barbus and resembles the cyprinodont pattern. Substrate spawning is considered likely.

6. **Reproduction in *B. lorenzi*:**
   - Mature ova were 1.0–1.2 mm diameter, pale yellow.
   - Egg counts ranged from 31 to 60 (female 13.7 to 14.0 mm SL). Eight to twelve percent of ovarian eggs were loosely judged mature.
   - Ripe females were collected in October, February, April (dry season) and June (wet season). Continuous spawning throughout the year is probable.
   - Schools were observed near mats of [[Pistia stratiotes]], which may be a spawning site.

7. **Schooling behaviour.** *B. lorenzi* forms large homogeneous schools, often several hundred individuals. Aquarium observations suggest the caudal peduncle spot serves as a schooling mark.

8. **Biogeographic position.** The Iguidi lies in the southern Nigerian forest zone, which has many faunal affinities with the Congo basin. The authors consider a southern Nigerian representative of the *B. candens*–*B. hulstaerti* line biogeographically plausible, though they make no firm statement on relationships. [[Barbus Candens]] and [[Barbus Hulstaerti]] are Congolese forest species that share some features with *B. lorenzi*.

9. **Future work.** The authors plan a further paper on the Iguidi River ichthyofauna. They expect aquarists to supply captive breeding data, since both species are desirable aquarium fish within reach of a major tropical fish export centre.
