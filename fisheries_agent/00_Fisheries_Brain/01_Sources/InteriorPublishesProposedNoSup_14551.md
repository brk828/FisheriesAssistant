# Federal Register Notice: No Surprises Policy and Bluefish Fishery Control Date

#policy #endangered-species-act #regulatory-framework #habitat-conservation

## Executive Summary

This document contains two Federal Register notices from June 1997. The primary notice proposes codification of the [[Endangered Species Act]] "No Surprises" policy, which provides regulatory assurances to holders of incidental take permits under [[Habitat Conservation Plans]] (HCPs). The policy guarantees that no additional land use restrictions or financial compensation will be required from permit holders if unforeseen circumstances arise, provided the HCP is functioning properly and the permittee is in good faith compliance. The second notice establishes a control date (May 29, 1997) for the commercial [[Atlantic Bluefish]] fishery to discourage speculative entry while management measures are being developed.

## Background & Description

The [[No Surprises Policy]] originated from the 1982 reauthorization of the [[Endangered Species Act]], which created section 10(a) incidental take permits. Congress recognized that long-term development projects requiring permits needed regulatory certainty and economic assurance. The Conference Report stated that "no further mitigation requirements will only be imposed in accordance with the terms of the plan" and that if unlisted species addressed in an approved conservation plan are subsequently listed, "no further mitigation requirements should be imposed if the conservation plan addressed the conservation of the species and its habitat as if the species were listed."

The policy was initially issued by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NMFS) in 1994, included in the joint Endangered Species Habitat Conservation Planning Handbook in November 1996, and is now being proposed for formal codification in 50 CFR Parts 17 and 222. This action responds to the March 21, 1997 settlement agreement in Spirit of the Sage v. Babbitt.

Section 9 of the ESA prohibits "take" of listed endangered species, defined as "to harass, harm, pursue, hunt, shoot, wound, kill, trap, capture or collect." FWS regulations define "harm" to include "significant habitat modification or degradation where it actually kills or injures wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavioral patterns, including breeding, feeding, or sheltering."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[No Surprises Policy]] applies to species "adequately covered" under HCPs. For listed species, this means the HCP satisfies permit issuance criteria under section 10(a)(2)(B) of the ESA. For unlisted species, it means the species is addressed as if it were listed and the HCP conditions would satisfy permit issuance criteria if the species were actually listed.

The policy distinguishes between "changed circumstances" (reasonably anticipated changes like new species listings or project modifications) and "unforeseen circumstances" (changes that could not be anticipated and result in substantial adverse changes in species status). HCPs should incorporate adaptive management measures to address changed circumstances without requiring permit amendments.

Benefits to covered species include: conserving lands and waters, decreasing habitat fragmentation, removing threats to candidate or unlisted species, and advancing recovery of listed species. The policy encourages development of HCPs that provide an "overall net benefit" to affected species, defined as management activities that increase species populations and/or enhance, restore, or maintain suitable habitat within the HCP planning area.

## Key Findings

**Core Assurances Provided:**

1. **No Additional Land or Financial Requirements**: The Services will not require commitment of additional land, property interests, or financial compensation beyond the level of mitigation adequately provided under a properly functioning HCP, except under unforeseen circumstances.

2. **Government Responsibility for Additional Mitigation**: If additional mitigation measures are subsequently deemed necessary for a species adequately covered under a properly functioning HCP, the obligation rests with the Federal government, other government agencies, private conservation organizations, or other private landowners—not the HCP permittee.

3. **Limited Modifications Under Unforeseen Circumstances**: If unforeseen circumstances warrant additional mitigation, changes must maintain original HCP terms to the maximum extent possible and be limited to modifications within Conserved Habitat areas or to the HCP's operating conservation program. Additional requirements will not involve payment of additional compensation or apply to lands available for development without permittee consent.

**Criteria for Determining Unforeseen Circumstances:**

The Services bear the burden of demonstrating unforeseen circumstances exist using best available scientific and commercial data. Factors to consider include:
- Size of current range of affected species
- Percentage of range adversely affected by the HCP
- Percentage of range conserved by the HCP
- Ecological significance of the portion of range affected
- Level of knowledge about the species and specificity of conservation program
- Whether HCP was designed to provide overall net benefit with measurable success criteria
- Whether failure to adopt additional measures would appreciably reduce likelihood of survival and recovery

**Enhanced Assurances for Net Benefit HCPs:**

The Services will not seek additional mitigation from permittees where a properly functioning HCP was designed to provide overall net benefit to covered species and contained measurable criteria for biological success that have been or are being met.

**Permit-Shield Provision:**

A new provision creates a presumption that holders of incidental take permits operating in compliance with valid permits are in compliance with sections 9 and 10 of the ESA, regardless of changes in circumstances, policy, and regulation (unless statute or court order specifically requires modification).

**Atlantic Bluefish Fishery Control Date:**

The second notice establishes May 29, 1997 as a control date for the commercial [[Atlantic Bluefish]] (Pomatomus saltatrix) fishery. Anyone entering after this date will not be assured future access if limited entry management is implemented. The 23rd Northeast Regional Stock Assessment Workshop (SAW-23, January 1997) found:
- Stock at low abundance and over-exploited
- Fully recruited fishing mortality increased from 0.12 (1988) to 0.46 (1995), twice the overfishing reference point
- Spawning stock biomass declined 63% from 293,006 mt (1986) to 110,000 mt (1995), a historic low
- Recruitment below average since 1989; 1993 and 1995 year classes (13-14 million fish) poorest in time series
- SAW-23 advised reducing F to 0.1 or below (<8% exploitation) to halt SSB decline

**Implementation and Economic Analysis:**

No additional information collection or costs are imposed on non-Federal entities beyond existing HCP permit requirements. The policy provides regulatory certainty in exchange for conservation commitments. The Services determined the rule will not have significant effect on substantial numbers of small entities and will not impose costs of $100 million or more annually on local, state governments, or private entities.

**Public Comment Period:**

Comments on the proposed No Surprises rule must be received by July 28, 1997. The Services specifically seek comments on: (1) applicability of assurances to HCP process generally; (2) alternative means for providing assurances; (3) applicability to species adequately covered by permits; (4) the permit-shield provision; and (5) proposed regulatory changes to 50 CFR Parts 17 and 222.
