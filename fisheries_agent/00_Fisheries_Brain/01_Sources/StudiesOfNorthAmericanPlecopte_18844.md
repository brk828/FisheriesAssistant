# Studies of North American Plecoptera, with Special Reference to the Fauna of Illinois

#ichthyology #marine-biology #plecoptera

## Executive Summary

This 1942 Illinois Natural History Survey bulletin by T. H. Frison is a taxonomic revision of North American stoneflies (order Plecoptera), with emphasis on the Illinois fauna. It is not a study of fish. The paper revises the family-level classification, provides a revised key to families and an updated checklist of Illinois species, and resolves numerous nomenclatural problems through new synonymies, lectotype designations, and the description of new species. The Illinois list rises from 38 species in 1937 to 49 species in 1942.

## Background & Description

Frison's work continues Illinois stonefly studies begun in 1926 and extends to North American fauna through collecting in the [[Great Smoky Mountains National Park]], Wisconsin, Michigan and other regions. Material was obtained through field collecting, rearing adults from nymphs (sometimes at collection sites using rearing cages and night collecting), and critical study of types held at the [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], [[Cornell University]] and the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]] collection. Illustrations were largely prepared by C. O. Mohr.

The classification uses an accordion-like scheme in which the author treats nemourids, leuctrids, capniids and taeniopterygids as separate families rather than subfamilies of Nemouridae. The author erected the family Isoperlidae for the genus Isoperla and reduced the Taeniopterygidae to two genera, Taeniopteryx and Brachyptera. Diploperla was adopted for a complex of species formerly placed in Perla, a name whose genotype the author considered questionable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Stoneflies are aquatic insects whose nymphs live in streams. Several taxa are cold-water specialists: the Peltoperlidae are found in cold mountain or northern streams and are absent from Illinois, and Brachyptera pacifica occurs in cold rapid mountain streams. Nymphs of Pteronarcys dorsata/pictetii and others were collected from rocks, stream margins and during emergence. Frison notes that water character can influence nymph appearance, and that relic or segregated habitats (such as small streams in northern Illinois and Elizabethtown) harbor species not found elsewhere in the state. The paper also notes that Illinois' southern boundary streams may host species such as Acroneuria areta, which is found in the Ohio River basin.

## Key Findings

- **Illinois checklist:** The Illinois stonefly fauna stood at 38 species in 1937 and was expanded to 49 species in 1942, more than any state except New York (59 recorded species, possibly about 60). No Illinois nymphs were unplaceable to species; Peltoperlidae is the only North American family absent from Illinois.
- **Revised classification:** Key changes include removal of Eucapnopsis from Leuctridae to Capniidae; creation of Isoperlidae; recognition of Diploperla, Dictyopterygella and Oroperla as genera; and reduction of Taeniopterygidae to Taeniopteryx and Brachyptera.
- **Synonymies resolved:** Nemoura nivalis was sunk into Taeniopteryx maura; Pteronarcys nobilis Hagen was shown to be the species named pictetii, and dorsata was reinstated as the earlier name for the common northeastern species; Alloperla, Acroneuria and Perlodes complexes were rationalized.
- **Acroneuria confusion:** Acroneuria evoluta was separated from A. arida (valida), and A. mela, A. filicis and others were described as new species, based on rearing and anal gill characters.
- **Anal gills as a character:** The presence or absence of anal abdominal gills or subanal gill remnants is treated as a diagnostic character separating species such as Acroneuria carolinensis (lacking them) from A. lycorias (possessing them).
- **New species:** Peltoperla zipha, Taeniopteryx lita, Brachyptera rossi, Brachyptera limata, Acroneuria mela, Acroneuria filicis, Leuctra projecta, Hydroperla nalata, Hydroperla dorata, Diploperla pilata, Diploperla bulbosa and Diploperla arina were described.
- **Distribution:** Brachyptera pacifica and Taeniopteryx maura were shown to occur in both eastern and western North America; Taeniopteryx maura was recorded from numerous additional states.
- **Adult–nymph associations:** Rearing confirmed the nymph of Acroneuria xanthenes, Claassenia arctica, Hydroperla olivacea and Diploperla modesta, among others, which had previously been unknown or misassigned.
- **Types:** Lectotypes were designated for several taxa (e.g., Taeniopteryx parvula, Pteronarcys pictetii, Perlodes subvarians), and the author stresses that some names remain unresolvable without further reared material.
