# Alamo Lake Razorback Records: 2000 Agency Correspondence

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This two-part email thread (January 2000) between staff of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and fish biologists at [[Arizona State University]] questions whether a [[Razorback Sucker]] record from [[Alamo Lake]] is documented elsewhere. A colleague, Ed Bianchi, recalls capturing adult razorbacks by [[Electrofishing]] during a [[Largemouth Bass]] study around 1979 or 1980, but he had not published the data and may still hold the field notes. The thread is a records search, not a research study, and it reports no new quantitative data.

## Background & Description

- **Origin:** Sally Stefferud (USFWS) sent the initial query on January 12, 2000, asking whether other [[Razorback Sucker]] records existed for Alamo Lake and whether this record had been published.
- **Follow-up:** Greg Beatty reported a conversation with [[Ed Bianchi]] held the previous day.
- **Recollection:** Bianchi's razorback fish were not mentioned in his dissertation or any reports. At that time (1979 or 1980), no one showed particular interest in them.
- **Survey details:** The largemouth bass study used electrofishing near [[Alamo Dam]], where the lake was "probably twice the size it is now."
- **Catch:** Crews stunned 6–10 fish and netted 3–4 of them. These were weighed and measured.
- **Specimen notes:** The captured fish were adults in spawning colors with prominent tubercles on the anal fins, traits typical of mature males of this species.
- **Records status:** Bianchi thought the data might be in his old notes. These may be held personally or by the university, and Bianchi was not optimistic about locating them.

No counts, weights, lengths, or dates beyond the 1979–1980 range are given.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat change:** Alamo Lake was about twice its current size when the sampling took place, so the sampling area near the dam was at a different shoreline position from today. This matters for interpreting historical habitat.
- **Species status:** The thread implies that razorback records from Alamo Lake were uncommon enough to prompt a search for documentation. The text does not give population data.
- **Survey context:** The razorback captures were incidental to a largemouth bass study, which suggests that the species was present in the reservoir during that period.

## Key Findings

- A Razorback Sucker record from Alamo Lake (c. 1979–1980) is reported second-hand and has not been confirmed in publications.
- Bianchi's electrofishing captured 3–4 adult fish from 6–10 stunned, with spawning coloration and anal fin tubercles recorded.
- The original data (weights, lengths, and field notes) may exist, but their location is uncertain.
- No published reports or dissertation chapters are known to contain these records.
- The next step is to check Bianchi's old notes. Agency staff had low expectations of finding them.
- The thread is a records-verification inquiry and contains no formal conclusions or management recommendations.
