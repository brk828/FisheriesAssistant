# Correspondence Regarding Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Sampling and Management

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from J. Holt Williamson (Fishery Biologist, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) to Paul C. Marsh at Arizona State University expresses interest in participating in scheduled sampling activities for [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) during March 18-25, 1990. The letter also requests discussion of a Propagation/Genetics Management Plan being developed for big river endangered fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].

## Background & Description

The document references two federally endangered fish species native to the [[Colorado River]] system:

- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) - Family [[Catostomidae]]
- [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) - Family [[Cyprinidae]]

Both species are characterized as "big river endangered fishes" requiring coordinated management efforts across the upper basin. The correspondence indicates ongoing collaborative research between federal agencies and academic institutions, specifically involving Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies and Department of Zoology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampling effort was scheduled for March 18-25, 1990, in the upper Colorado River Basin. This timing and location suggest targeted surveys during a specific seasonal window, likely related to spawning or migration patterns of these endangered species. The development of a Propagation/Genetics Management Plan indicates recognition of the need for captive breeding programs and genetic diversity management to support recovery of these imperiled populations in their native big river habitat.

## Key Findings

- Scheduled field sampling for [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] planned for March 18-25, 1990
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service developing and implementing a Propagation/Genetics Management Plan for big river endangered fishes in the upper basin
- Collaborative effort involving multiple researchers including Paul C. Marsh, Dr. Minkley, and Dr. Dowling
- Federal agency seeking coordination with academic researchers on endangered species management strategies
- Management approach integrates both propagation (captive breeding) and genetics components for species recovery
