# Description of New Fishes of Bermuda

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bermuda-ichthyofauna #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1912 taxonomic paper by Tarleton H. Bean documents new fish species and generic descriptions collected from Bermuda, largely through the efforts of collector [[Louis L. Mowbray]]. The work updates Bean's earlier 1906 [[Catalogue of Bermuda Fishes]], adding 34 new species records and describing 8 new species-level taxa, including two new genera ([[Eucrotus]] and [[Parasphyraenops]]). The total known Bermuda fish fauna is raised to approximately 296–300 species. Species range from small pelagic clupeids and anchovies to deep-bank scorpaenids, reef-associated parrotfishes, and cryptic blennioid fishes from harbor mussel beds.

## Background & Description

In February 1912, Bean visited Bermuda at the request of the Fishery Board. Working with specimens obtained by [[Louis L. Mowbray]] (later director of the Boston Aquarium), Bean identified 34 additions to the Bermuda fish fauna beyond his 1906 catalogue (Field Columbian Museum, Zoological Series, Publication 108). This raised the known Bermuda ichthyofauna to 296 species, with several additional unidentified [[Scopelidae]] (lanternfishes) bringing the practical total closer to 300.

### Detailed New Species Descriptions

#### [[Sardinella Pinnula]]
- **Meristics:** D. ii,16; A. 16 (last two ray-like finlets); V. 10; P. 16; scales 42; ventral scutes 19+16.
- **Morphology:** Short head, slender body, straight back; maxilla reaches below front of eye; anal fin base as long as head minus snout with last two rays resembling finlets. Small teeth on tongue and palate; vomer toothless. Numerous long gillrakers.
- **Coloration:** Steel blue dorsally, silvery ventrally, iridescent; golden head tints in preservative; no opercular spot.
- **Common name:** "Anchovy" (Hamilton, Bermuda).
- **Type material:** USNM No. 74,086; collected by Mowbray, Feb 17, 1912; types 3¾–5½ inches.

#### [[Stolephorus Viridis]] ("Green Fry")
- **Meristics:** D.12; A.15–16; scales 42.
- **Morphology:** Pointed snout, nearly equal jaws; network of pores on head/nape; weak maxillary/mandibular teeth; maxilla reaching below front of pupil.
- **Coloration:** Pale brown with silvery lateral band; ~9 pearly spots ringed in dark brown on throat (likely bioluminescent); greenish in life; mucus-covered body.
- **Ecology:** Observed in large schools at North Rock ledge (Nov 1910) and near Hamilton Harbor entrance (Feb 1912).
- **Type material:** USNM No. 74,084; types 2–2¼ inches.

#### [[Eucrotus]] gen. nov. (Stromateidae or Icosteidae)
- **Diagnosis:** Deep, compressed body with small cycloid scales extending onto dorsal fin; flexible preopercular spines; serrate subopercle/opercle margin; single high dorsal fin with flexible spines; large ventral fins (I,5); soft skeleton; **pelagic** habit.

##### [[Eucrotus Ventralis]] sp. nov.
- **Meristics:** D. VII,34; A. I,24; V. I,5; lateral line pores 106.
- **Morphology:** Deep, compressed body; oblique mouth with equal jaws; graduated dorsal spines; long pectoral and ventral fins.
- **Coloration:** Grayish silvery with dark spots/blotches; 4 narrow dark bands on head.
- **Ecology:** Single specimen (5¼ in) collected in floating **Sargassum**, Eastern Channel, Bermuda, August 1911, by Mowbray. Housed in Museum of the [[Bermuda Natural History Society]].

#### [[Parasphyraenops]] gen. nov. (Cheilodipteridae, allied to *Scombrops*)
- **Diagnosis:** Elongate body with small ctenoid scales; naked snout/head; scaly opercles; double nostrils; weak jaw teeth (no canines); continuous, deeply notched dorsal (9 spines, 10 rays); short anal base (3 spines, 7 rays); forked caudal; scaleless vertical fins.

##### [[Parasphyraenops Atrimanus]] sp. nov.
- **Meristics:** D. IX,10; A. III,7; V. I,5; P.18; scales ~70.
- **Morphology:** Moderate oblique mouth, slightly projecting lower jaw; third dorsal spine longest.
- **Coloration:** Large black blotch at base of spinous dorsal; jet-black spot behind pectoral base; brownish overall (in spirits).
- **Ecology:** Two specimens (4 in and 3¼ in) recovered from stomach of a Bonito (*Seriola falcata*) on Argus Bank, 43 fathoms, Jan 14, 1909, by Mowbray.
- **Type material:** Larger specimen in USNM No. 74,085.

#### [[Anthias Louisi]] sp. nov.
- **Taxonomic group:** Related to *Anthias formosus* Boulenger.
- **Meristics:** D. X,15; A. III,7; scales 4-35-15; pores 33.
- **Morphology:** Crescentic caudal fin, no produced rays; gillrakers 29.
- **Coloration:** Rosy in life; small black spot at base of 6th–7th dorsal rays.
- **Ecology:** Single specimen (3⅝ in) from stomach of a rockfish, Argus Bank, April 6, 1908, collected by Mowbray.
- **Etymology:** Named for Louis Septième Mowbray.
- **Repository:** Museum of the Bermuda Natural History Society.

#### [[Pseudoscarus Plumbeus]] sp. nov. (Parrotfish)
- **Meristics:** D. IX,10; A. II,10; scales 2-25-6.
- **Morphology:** No canine teeth; included lower jaw; interrupted lateral line; scale sheaths along dorsal (18 scales) and anal (11 scales) bases.
- **Coloration:** Purplish brown in life; vivid greenish-blue chin, lip, opercle areas turning emerald green in spirits; green markings on nape, dorsal origin, fin margins.
- **Ecology:** Collected on North Shore reefs, August 1911; species first recorded in Bermuda in 1909.
- **Type material:** Museum of the Bermuda Natural History Society; types 16 and 18 inches.

#### [[Pontinus Microlepis]] sp. nov.
- **Taxonomic group:** Related to *Pontinus beanorum* and *P. macrolepis*; distinguished by small scales.
- **Meristics:** D. XII,10; A. II,5; P.17; V. I,5; scales 7-48-14 (~33 pores).
- **Morphology:** Maxilla reaches near hind eye margin; slight mandibular knob; weakly scaled head; two slender ocular tentacles per eye; small nostril tentacle; only 6 fully developed gillrakers with ~8 tubercles.
- **Coloration:** Rosy.
- **Ecology:** Single specimen (9 in), collected by Mowbray in Bermuda.
- **Repository:** Intended for U.S. National Museum.

#### [[Emblemaria Markii]] Mowbray sp. nov.
- **Contributed by:** Louis L. Mowbray.
- **Meristics:** D. XXII,15; A.25.
- **Morphology:** Blenniiform, scaleless body; strong incurved conical teeth; long maxilla; nasal and ocular cirri (branched tip); small horn-like cirrus behind eye. Sexual dimorphism in dorsal spine length (males: 6th–8th spines longest; females: shorter, equal to body depth).
- **Coloration:** Pale brown with 6–10 dark vertical body bands; dusky vertical fins, blotched dark dorsal; pale pectorals.
- **Ecology:** Common among mussel beds (*Arca noae*) in Hamilton Harbor, Bermuda; first taken June 20, 1907, via mussel dredge.
- **Etymology:** Named for Dr. E. L. Mark, Harvard University.
- **Type material:** USNM No. 74,083.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species described span a broad range of Bermudian marine habitats:
- **Pelagic/neritic surface waters:** [[Sardinella Pinnula]] and [[Stolephorus Viridis]] were observed in large nearshore schools near Hamilton Harbor and North Rock, indicating importance as small forage fishes ("Anchovy," "Green Fry") in the local food web, likely prey for larger predatory fishes.
- **Floating Sargassum mats:** [[Eucrotus Ventralis]] was collected from drifting Sargassum in the Eastern Channel, highlighting the ecological role of pelagic Sargassum as habitat for unusual, poorly known open-ocean fishes.
- **Deep offshore banks (Argus Bank, 43 fathoms):** Both [[Parasphyraenops Atrimanus]] and [[Anthias Louisi]] were recovered as prey items from the stomachs of larger predatory fishes (Bonito and an unspecified rockfish), demonstrating a trophic link between mesopelagic/bank-associated small fishes and larger reef/bank predators.
- **Coral reef environments:** [[Pseudoscarus Plumbeus]] (a parrotfish) was collected within reef habitats on the North Shore, consistent with typical scarid reef-grazing ecology.
- **Harbor mussel beds:** [[Emblemaria Markii]] inhabits mussel beds (*Arca noae*) in Hamilton Harbor, representing a cryptic, structure-associated blennioid niche.

Collectively, these records substantially expanded the known ichthyofaunal diversity of Bermuda, underscoring the archipelago's role as a biogeographically distinct outpost supporting both resident reef/harbor species and transient pelagic and bank-associated fishes influenced by Gulf Stream–related currents.

## Key Findings

- Bean's 1912 report increased Bermuda's documented fish fauna from prior levels to **296 known species**, with unidentified Scopelidae bringing the practical count to ~300.
- **34 new species records** were added to the Bermuda fish fauna since the 1906 Catalogue.
- **Two new genera** were erected: [[Eucrotus]] (putative Stromateidae/Icosteidae) and [[Parasphyraenops]] (Cheilodipteridae, allied to *Scombrops*).
- **Eight new species** were formally described: *Sardinella pinnula*, *Stolephorus viridis*, *Eucrotus ventralis*, *Parasphyraenops atrimanus*, *Anthias louisi*, *Pseudoscarus plumbeus*, *Pontinus microlepis*, and *Emblemaria markii*.
- Several species were discovered indirectly through **predator stomach contents** (Anthias louisi from a rockfish; Parasphyraenops atrimanus from a Bonito), a valuable method for detecting rare or deep-dwelling fishes.
- Type specimens were deposited across two institutions: the **U.S. National Museum** and the **Museum of the Bermuda Natural History Society**, reflecting a collaborative binational curation effort.
- The work highlights the substantial taxonomic contributions of local naturalist [[Louis L. Mowbray]], who both collected specimens and personally authored the description of *Emblemaria markii*.
