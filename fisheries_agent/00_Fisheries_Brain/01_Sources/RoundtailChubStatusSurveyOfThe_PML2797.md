# Roundtail Chub Status Survey of the Lower Colorado River Basin - Map Supplemental

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of six Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) map sheets, dated January 2002, that overlay land ownership on streams with extant [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Headwater Chub]] populations. Each stream is analysed within a 1.0 mile buffer zone. The maps cover the [[Bill Williams River]], [[Little Colorado River]], [[Lower Gila River]], [[Salt River]], [[Upper Gila River]] and [[Verde River]] drainages. For each stream, the document reports land ownership in acres and percentages, showing how much of the surrounding land is held by federal, state, tribal and private owners. The material is cartographic and tabular rather than a research study, and it contains no population counts, abundance estimates or habitat measurements. The AGFD disclaims any warranty on the information.

## Background & Description

The maps use a common legend:

- **Chub status:** estimated current range for roundtail chub or headwater chub, collection points from the last five years, and streams where chub status is unknown.
- **Land ownership categories:** [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), [[Arizona State Land Department]] (ASLD), U.S. Forest Service (including [[Tonto National Forest]], Coconino, Prescott and Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests), Indian Reservations and Allotments, Bureau of Reclamation, Corps of Engineers, National Parks and Monuments, Wildlife Refuges, Military, state and county lands, and private land.
- **Spatial units:** township and range, sections, highways, streams, lakes and the 1.0 mile buffer zone.

Each sheet is accompanied by a land ownership table listing acreage and percentage by owner for each stream with extant chub populations. The New Mexico portion of the upper Gila could not be tabulated because of an error in the New Mexico land ownership database.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is concerned with land management context rather than fish biology. Its stated focus is the stream corridor and surrounding land within 1.0 mile of each stream with chub populations. The ownership pattern shapes who manages watershed lands that influence these streams. Public lands managed by the [[Tonto National Forest]], BLM and ASLD dominate many of the stream buffers. The [[Fort Apache Reservation]] and [[San Carlos Reservation]] form large tribal blocks in the Salt River drainage. Private land is most prominent in the lower-elevation or valley reaches.

The document does not describe water chemistry, flow regimes, invertebrate or plankton communities, threats, or fish life history.

## Key Findings

**1. Bill Williams River drainage (roundtail chub)**

- [[Boulder Creek]]: ASLD 19,269 acres (45%), [[Prescott National Forest]] 9,393 (22%), private 9,109 (21%), BLM 5,304 (12%).
- [[Trout Creek]]: ASLD 53,924 (78%), private 14,864 (21%), Indian allotments 553 (1%), BLM 57 (<1%).
- [[Burro Creek]]: BLM 33,689 (52%), private 19,226 (30%), ASLD 11,729 (18%).
- [[Santa Maria River]]: BLM 27,654 (49%), ASLD 19,206 (34%), private 6,698 (12%), Corps of Engineers 3,067 (5%).
- Francis Creek: private 15,394 (57%), BLM 7,019 (26%), ASLD 4,578 (17%).
- Sycamore Creek: ASLD 8,972 (82%), private 1,970 (18%).
- Kirkland Creek: ASLD 5,321 (72%), private 2,069 (28%).

**2. Little Colorado River drainage (roundtail chub)**

- [[Chevelon Creek]]: Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest 44,439 acres (50%), private 32,561 (36%), ASLD 10,404 (12%), BLM 1,331 (2%), AGFD 681 (<1%).
- A second, unlabelled stream entry lists Coconino National Forest 37,777 (39%) and private 28,189 (29%) as its largest owners.

**3. Lower Gila River drainage (roundtail chub)**

- [[Aravaipa Creek]]: BLM 16,911 acres (52%), private 8,784 (27%), ASLD 6,768 (21%), Indian allotments 335 (1%).

**4. Salt River drainage (chub status varies by stream)**

- Salome Creek: Tonto National Forest 40,549 (99%), private 281 (1%) (the table shows 504 acres private, 1%, for the associated entry).
- Salt River: Tonto National Forest 53,333 (52%), [[Fort Apache Reservation]] 31,019 (31%), [[San Carlos Reservation]] 15,823 (16%), Salt River Reservation 1,394 (1%).
- Some Salt River drainage streams are marked "current chub status unknown."

**5. Headwater chub streams (Tonto Creek and tributaries)**

Most of these streams are almost entirely within Tonto National Forest:

- [[Tonto Creek]]: Tonto National Forest 70,446 acres (94%), private 4,442 (6%). A separate Coconino National Forest entry (313 acres, <1%) also appears.
- Haigler Creek: Tonto National Forest 17,921 (98%), private 418 (2%).
- Spring Creek: Tonto National Forest 26,630 (99%), private 306 (1%).
- Rock Creek: Tonto National Forest 5,518 (99%), private 223 (1%).
- Buzzard Roost Creek: Tonto National Forest 7,235 (100%).
- Gordon Creek: Tonto National Forest 6,787 (98%), private 164 (2%).
- Guin Creek: Tonto National Forest 20,154 (>99%), private 57 (<1%).
- Marsh Creek: Tonto National Forest 9,532 (98%), private 234 (2%).

**6. Upper Gila River drainage (roundtail and headwater chub, New Mexico portion included)**

- Land ownership was not calculated. The map states that lands are composed primarily of BLM, National Forest, state and private land.

**7. Verde River drainage (roundtail and headwater chub)**

- [[Deadman Creek]]: Tonto National Forest 23,557 acres (100%).
- [[East Verde River]]: Tonto National Forest 50,062 (97%), private 1,466 (3%), Coconino National Forest 103 (<1%).
- [[Fossil Creek]]: Coconino National Forest 15,131 (52%), Tonto National Forest 13,973 (48%), private 71 (<1%).
- [[Oak Creek]]: private, ASLD 1,735 (3%), Yavapai County 320 (1%), AGFD 206 (<1%), Prescott National Forest 148 (<1%).
- [[Wet Beaver Creek]]: Coconino National Forest 26,558 (74%), private 8,313 (23%), Montezuma Castle National Monument 573 (1%), Montezuma Well National Monument 270 (1%).
- Webber Creek: Tonto National Forest 9,632 (96%), private 355 (4%).
- Wet Bottom Creek: Tonto National Forest 6,743 (100%).
- West Clear Creek: Coconino National Forest 47,724 (92%), with smaller shares of private and Prescott National Forest land.
- Verde River mainstem (labelled "Nerde River" in the OCR): Tonto National Forest 79,328 (46%), private 31,255 (18%), Coconino National Forest 23,821 (14%), Fort McDowell Reservation 14,686 (9%), Prescott National Forest 13,224 (8%), ASLD 5,953 (3%).

**Overall patterns**

- Federal and state lands dominate the buffer zones for most headwater and mid-elevation streams, especially the Tonto Creek, Verde and Gila headwater systems.
- The [[Bill Williams River]] tributaries show a strong ASLD presence, while the [[Santa Maria River]] and [[Burro Creek]] are mostly BLM-managed.
- Private land is a significant share in the valley reaches, notably Francis Creek (57%) and the Verde mainstem (18%).
- The source does not state the number of chub individuals, collection effort, or any conservation action. Its purpose is to inform land management and survey planning, as described in the [[Land Ownership Mapping]] and [[Buffer Zone Analysis]] approach.
