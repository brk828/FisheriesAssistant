# Euryhaline Gammarid Amphipods of the Norwegian West Coast

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

*Note: this document concerns crustaceans (amphipods), not fish. The tag line follows the system format for non-fish taxa.*

## Executive Summary

This 1973 study by [[Henk G. Dennert]] (Institute of Taxonomic Zoology, University of Amsterdam) reports the distribution and ecology of five euryhaline gammarid amphipods along the Atlantic coast of Norway, based on a July 1971 sampling trip. Samples came from four biotopes: supralittoral rockpools, small seacliff springs and estuaries (pooled), and the intertidal zone of fjords. Water temperature and specific conductivity were measured at all 177 visited localities, and chloride (Cl⁻) and calcium (Ca²⁺) were determined for water samples. The paper also documents morphological variation within *Gammarus oceanicus* and extends the known salinity tolerance of two species.

## Background & Description

The study focuses on five species most abundant in the surveyed biotopes:

- *Gammarus duebeni duebeni* (Lilljeborg, 1852), a brackish-water form.
- *Gammarus oceanicus* (Segerstråle, 1947).
- *Gammarus zaddachi* (Sexton, 1912).
- *Gammarus finmarchicus* (Dahl, 1938).
- *Chaetogammarus marinus* (Leach, 1815).

Other euryhaline gammarids recorded at some sites include *Gammarus salinus*, *Gammarus setosus*, *Chaetogammarus stoerensis* and *Eulimnogammarus obtusatus*. Inland waters were not surveyed in depth; *Gammarus lacustris* is the main inland representative, and inland *G. duebeni* localities were found to be mixohaline.

**Morphological variation in *G. oceanicus*:** Atypical males from several populations (mostly northern, including ovigerous females) lack calceoli on the A2 flagellum, have a different mandible palp spinule arrangement, and show fewer tufts of setae on the first antenna. Typical males have calceoli, uniform spinules, and four or five tufts on segment 2 of the first antenna. The atypical form was not distinguished from the typical form by habitat or association with other species. The author suggests temperature may influence this variability.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Biotope and species associations (Table I summary):**

- *G. duebeni duebeni* lives mainly in supralittoral rockpools. In pseudo-supralittoral pools it coexists with *G. oceanicus* and *G. finmarchicus*. In small estuaries and seacliff springs it is associated with *G. zaddachi*. In fjords it is largely limited to upper algal zones (*Pelvetia* and *Ascophyllum*).
- *G. oceanicus* occupies mainly the fjord eulittoral, among stones and on *Fucus*. It also occurs in river mouths on sand or sandy mud with *Enteromorpha*.
- *G. zaddachi* lives mostly in running waters and is never found in rockpools.
- *Ch. marinus* was never found in rockpools and was recorded only once in an estuary. Its main biotope is the *Fucus* zone of fjords, on rock or boulders.
- *G. finmarchicus* shows a preference for the fjord littoral and pseudo-supralittoral rockpools, on rock or sand.

Joint occurrence is possible in all biotopes. As conditions become suboptimal, the number of associated species declines. In supralittoral rockpools, 82% of positive stations held only *G. duebeni duebeni*.

The study surveyed 118 numbered stations with gammarids across Norwegian provinces from Vest-Agder north to Finnmark, and lists localities with species records. The author notes about 177 localities were visited in total.

## Key Findings

- **Expanded salinity tolerance:** *G. oceanicus* was previously thought to require salinity above 2.5‰ (about 1500 mg/l Cl⁻). In seven Norwegian records, chlorinity was below 600 mg/l. The author attributes this to low northern temperatures, which favour survival at low salinities (citing Kinne's experimental work).
- ***Ch. marinus*** was found in an estuary with very low salinity at low tide, extending the known range. Previously reported minimum values were 74 mg/l Cl⁻ (French Channel coast) and 7000 mg/l (Netherlands intertidal).
- **Mixohaline restriction:** *G. finmarchicus* was found in Norway only in mixohaline waters of rockpools and the fjord tidal belt.
- **Species range by biotope:** *G. duebeni* dominates the supralittoral; *G. zaddachi* is confined to running water and estuaries.
- **Morphological variation:** Atypical *G. oceanicus* males differ in several appendage characters but show no ecological difference, suggesting environmental (possibly temperature) influence.
- **Distribution pattern:** Only the typical *G. oceanicus* form occurred in the south of Norway, while mixed or atypical-only populations occurred further north.
- **Specimens:** Collected material was deposited in the Zoological Museum of the University of Amsterdam.
