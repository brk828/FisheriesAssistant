# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Response on Spikedace and Loach Minnow Recovery Plans

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This November 17, 1988 correspondence from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Region 2 to the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish addresses concerns regarding draft recovery plans for [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). The letter clarifies FWS positions on fish stocking programs in the [[Gila River]] basin, particularly regarding non-native fish introductions and their potential impacts on these two threatened native cyprinids. The Service emphasizes that recovery plans serve as guidelines rather than mandates to curtail specific existing programs, while establishing protocols for evaluating future stocking proposals.

## Background & Description

The [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) are native cyprinid fishes inhabiting the [[Gila River]] basin in New Mexico and Arizona. Draft recovery plans for both species were under review in 1988, with Item 1.7 of both plans calling for curtailment of non-native fish introductions within the remaining range of these species. The New Mexico Department of Game and Fish operates multiple fish stocking programs in the basin, including trout stocking in the West and Middle Forks of the Gila River, [[Lake Roberts]], mainstem Gila River (winter), and [[Bill Evans Lake]], as well as warmwater gamefish stocking in Bill Evans Lake and Lake Roberts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Gila River]] basin represents critical habitat for both [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]. The Service distinguishes between different types of stocking programs based on their potential ecological impacts:

**Trout Stocking**: Current trout stocking programs in the West and Middle Forks of the Gila River, Lake Roberts, winter mainstem Gila River, and Bill Evans Lake are assessed as not posing significant threats to [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] populations. However, new trout stocking programs in occupied habitat require careful scrutiny for both short-term survival and long-term recovery potential impacts, including indirect effects such as increased recreational use.

**Warmwater Gamefish Stocking**: Warmwater species stocking in Bill Evans Lake and Lake Roberts presents potentially more serious threats, though no significant adverse effects had been documented to date. Some stocked species already have established populations in the Gila River, while others (e.g., crappie in Bill Evans Lake) have never successfully established in riverine habitat. Both reservoirs are located upstream from [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] populations.

## Key Findings

**FWS Policy on Existing Stocking Programs**: The Service does not intend recovery plans to curtail existing Department stocking programs. The final listing rule explicitly states that current trout stocking in West and Middle Forks does not threaten [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] well-being. Current warmwater stocking in off-mainstream reservoirs may continue where no adverse effects are documented.

**Revised Recovery Plan Language**: Item 1.7 will be rewritten to emphasize preventing riverine and mainstem reservoir introductions of warmwater species. Language will be added specifying that existing trout and off-mainstream reservoir stocking programs require individual analysis, with programs demonstrating no adverse effects allowed to continue.

**Risk Assessment Framework**: 
- Riverine and mainstem reservoir warmwater introductions pose highest risk
- Off-mainstream reservoir stockings require case-by-case evaluation
- Burden of proof lies with stocking proponents to demonstrate no adverse effects (short-term, long-term, direct, or indirect)
- New programs in occupied habitat must protect both survival and recovery potential

**Interagency Coordination Requirements**: The Service requests close coordination on any new introduction proposals. Development of criteria for recovery stream selection will be elevated in priority and conducted with guidance from the Desert Fishes Recovery Team, which includes New Mexico Department of Game and Fish representation. Present and historic range maps will be incorporated into final plans.

**Impact Mitigation**: The Service welcomes collaborative development of methods to reduce impacts of existing warmwater stocking programs, though no current solutions exist for reducing potential impacts from upstream reservoirs (Bill Evans Lake and Lake Roberts).
