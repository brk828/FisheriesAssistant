# National Research Council Committee Meeting on Glen Canyon Environmental Studies - October 1991

#ichthyology #aquatic-ecology #environmental-assessment #colorado-river

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents the planning and agenda for a National Research Council Water Science and Technology Board committee meeting scheduled for October 9-11, 1991, in [[Flagstaff]], [[Arizona]]. The committee was convened to review the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, with particular focus on the aquatic biology component of the research. The meeting included presentations from multiple agencies (Arizona Game and Fish, National Park Service, U.S. Geological Survey, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service), a field trip from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to [[Lee's Ferry]], and planning for a 1992 symposium.

## Background & Description

The Water Science and Technology Board of the National Research Council organized this committee meeting to review ongoing environmental studies related to [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and their effects on the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem. The committee had previously issued letter reports in August to the Bureau of Reclamation regarding sediment transport and hydrology components. The meeting was held at the GCES offices in [[Flagstaff]], with accommodations at the Monte Vista Hotel. The committee included 12 members representing various expertise areas including aquatic ecology, hydrology, and environmental policy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program examined the ecological impacts of dam operations on the [[Colorado River]] downstream from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to [[Lee's Ferry]]. The aquatic biology component was a central focus, addressing fish populations, aquatic invertebrates, and endangered species. The studies were conducted in coordination with multiple agencies: Arizona Game and Fish (D. Kubly), National Park Service (Larry Stevens), U.S. Geological Survey (Robert Averett), and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Stewart Leon). The research area encompassed the regulated river reach immediately below the dam, a critical habitat zone affected by altered flow regimes and sediment transport patterns.

## Key Findings

**Meeting Structure and Objectives:**
- Primary focus on reviewing the aquatic biology component of GCES research program
- Field investigation scheduled for October 10, 1991: raft trip from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to [[Lee's Ferry]] to observe study area conditions
- Planning for June 1992 symposium to present GCES findings

**Aquatic Biology Review Components:**
- Presentation by D. Kubly (Arizona Game and Fish) on GCES aquatic biology studies
- Presentation by Larry Stevens (National Park Service) on aquatic biology research
- Presentation by Robert Averett (U.S. Geological Survey) on aquatic biology program
- Discussion of endangered species work by Stewart Leon (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service)
- Informal pre-meeting discussion on October 8 with Duncan Patten, Wendell Minckley, and Dave Wegner specifically addressing status of aquatic biology studies

**Additional Review Topics:**
- Draft sediment transport/hydrology component report prepared by committee for review and comment
- Discussion of overall strategy for aquatic ecology studies (D. Patten)
- Update on GCES/EIS (Environmental Impact Statement) status
- Issues including beach building flows, continued financial support for GCES, and motor use restrictions for researchers in [[Grand Canyon]]

**Administrative Details:**
- Meeting location: GCES Flagstaff Office, 121 E. Birch St., Room 307, Flagstaff, Arizona 86001
- Committee assignments made for writing aquatic biology review report
- Executive sessions held for committee confidentiality and bias discussions
