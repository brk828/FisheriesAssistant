# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes April 1987

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinodontidae #desert-pupfish #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

Minutes from the Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT) meeting held April 1-2, 1987 at [[Kofa National Wildlife Refuge]], Yuma, Arizona. The meeting focused primarily on developing a recovery plan for the critically endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), with a target completion date of July 1, 1987. The team discussed taxonomic considerations of four named subspecies, evaluated existing natural and refugium populations, and addressed controversial reintroduction efforts. Additional agenda items included [[Chihuahua Chub]] reintroduction proposals, exotic fish removal from [[Aravaipa Creek]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] experimental populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) is classified as critically endangered, with extremely limited natural populations remaining. According to R. R. Miller (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, paper in press in Copeia No. 3, 1987), the species comprises distinctive subspecies including populations from the [[Sonoyta River]] and [[Quitobaquito Spring]]. Additional named forms include *C. m. macularius* (Gila River form) and *C. m. californicus* ([[Salton Sea]] form). The [[Colorado River Delta]] population (Santa Clara Slough, Sonora) appears most similar to the Gila River form based on specimen examination.

Natural stocks exist at only: one site in Arizona ([[Quitobaquito Spring]], [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]]), unknown but depleted stocks in the [[Rio Sonoyta]] and [[Colorado River Delta]] in Mexico, and two sites in California ([[San Sebastian Marsh]] and [[San Felipe Spring]]). Refugium populations are maintained at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and other facilities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery effort operates within a complex biogeographic framework where four named taxa exist, each facing distinctive conservation challenges. The team emphasized maintaining natural genetic lineages within their original geographic ranges. Considerable concern was expressed regarding unauthorized stockings of [[Quitobaquito]] pupfish populations within the native range of other forms, representing genetic contamination threats.

The questionable status of pupfish populations in Mexico necessitates urgent survey work prior to completion of the draft recovery plan. The team recommended that the Regional Office fund survey activities through the Mexican collaborator (Juarez Romero) to assess current distribution and population status.

Habitat quality considerations were central to discussions of reintroduction site classifications. The team distinguished between high-quality, permanent, highly-desirable habitats (potentially essential to species perpetuation) versus less-than-optimal habitats for purposes of "essential" versus "non-essential" experimental population designations.

## Key Findings

**Recovery Plan Development:** Target date for completed draft recovery plan set for July 1, 1987, with specific assignments distributed among team members covering: culture efforts at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], historic and present distribution across Arizona, California, and Mexico, reasons for decline, limiting factors, future threats, biology, ecology, and taxonomy.

**Taxonomic Considerations:** Four named taxa identified, each requiring separate consideration in recovery planning. Strong participant consensus that each remnant stock should be considered and maintained separately insofar as possible, with emphasis on preserving natural genetic lineages.

**Genetic Contamination Issue:** Team directed strong recommendation to Regional Director that alien stocks from unauthorized [[Quitobaquito]] stockings be destroyed and replaced with stocks most similar to original genotypes.

**Reintroduction Classification:** Team opposed blanket "non-essential" classification for populations reintroduced into high-quality, permanent habitats on [[Bureau of Land Management]] lands in Arizona. Consensus reached to re-evaluate proposed sites, applying "non-essential" categorization only to less-than-optimal habitats while proposing full protection for optimal habitat stockings.

**Other Species Actions:**
- [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*): Previous DFRT recommendations to Regional Office deemed adequate; no further action recommended for [[Mimbres River]] reintroduction proposals
- Exotic fish removal: Approved BLM-AGFD-FWS cooperative survey and removal project for [[Aravaipa Creek Wilderness]] drainage, anticipated summer 1987 with possible extension to summer 1988
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*): Opposed "non-essential" experimental reintroduction in [[Gila River]] upstream from Safford due to general lack of available habitat. Alternative experimental stocking between [[Coolidge Dam]] and [[Ashurst-Hayden Diversion]] discussed as more reasonable, contingent on study plans and funding assurance

**Future Agenda Items:** [[Sonoran Topminnow]] downlisting discussion deferred to next meeting. Proposals requested for central data facility for recovery efforts and comprehensive monitoring plan for native fishes in Arizona. Next meeting tentatively scheduled for August at high elevation in northern Arizona.
