# Lower Blue River Native Fish Restoration Project: Scoping Notice and Public Comment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

The [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Phoenix Area Office) issued a March 2009 scoping notice for an [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA) under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] on a proposed [[Native Fish Restoration]] project in the lower Blue River, Greenlee County, Arizona. The project would build a [[Fish Barrier]], remove [[Nonnative Fishes]] from an 11-mile reach, and restore native fishes. Its goals are to protect existing populations of [[Loach Minnow]] (federally threatened) and [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] from upstream invasion by nonnative fishes and aquatic organisms from the [[San Francisco River]], and to reintroduce [[Spikedace]] and [[Roundtail Chub]]. The project is a cooperative effort with the [[Forest Service]] (Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests), the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD), and the [[US Fish And Wildlife Service]] (FWS). Public comments were due April 27, 2009.

A supporting comment from fish scientist Paul Marsh, PhD (April 9, 2009) endorsed the project as a "very real opportunity" to conserve imperiled native fishes. The Bureau's scoping package also invited tribal consultation under the [[National Historic Preservation Act]].

## Background & Description

The proposal complements other Bureau of Reclamation barrier projects in the [[Gila River Basin]]. Fish barrier construction was first required by biological opinions on [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water transfers, issued by FWS in 1994 and 2001 and superseded by a May 2008 CAP biological opinion. That opinion states that barriers are intended to "prevent or hinder upstream movements of nonindigenous fish and other aquatic organisms into high-value native fish and amphibian habitats."

The Blue River drainage currently supports five native warmwater fish species:

- Longfin dace
- Speckled dace
- Desert sucker
- Sonora sucker
- Loach minnow (threatened)

[[Gila Trout]], a cold-water species, occurs in tributaries of the upper Blue River drainage. Suitable habitat for spikedace and roundtail chub also exists in the drainage. The historical native assemblage may also have included flannelmouth sucker, razorback sucker, and Colorado pikeminnow.

Reported nonnative species in the Blue and San Francisco Rivers include channel catfish, flathead catfish, common carp, red shiner, fathead minnow, and mosquitofish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitat destruction and alteration were the principal causes of native fish declines in the American Southwest before the mid-1900s. In recent decades, however, the presence of nonnative fishes has been recognized as the most consequential factor preventing the survival and recovery of imperiled native fishes. Nonnative fishes can negate the benefits of habitat protection and restoration, so the scoping materials and Dr. Marsh's comment both treat nonnative removal and segregated management as essential.

Marsh's comment describes the Blue River system of eastern Arizona and western New Mexico as one of the few places where a native fish community "continues to thrive" and nonnative species are mostly absent, few in number, or restricted in distribution. He notes that most native fishes of the Gila basin are imperiled, that many are federally listed or state-protected, and that their status continues to deteriorate despite long-available recovery plans.

Purpose and need sections note that loach minnow occupies the entire Blue River main stem, some tributaries, and parts of the San Francisco River near the confluence. Chiricahua leopard frog occurs along upper portions of the main stem and in certain tributaries.

## Key Findings

**Purpose and need**
- Protect existing loach minnow and Chiricahua leopard frog populations from future upstream invasion of nonnative aquatic organisms from the San Francisco River.
- Remove the threat posed by nonnative fishes already occupying the lower reaches of the Blue River.
- Secure habitat for reintroduction of roundtail chub and spikedace, including replication of the New Mexico Gila River spikedace population.
- Meet a primary conservation measure of the 2008 CAP biological opinion.

**Proposed action (four elements)**
1. Construct a concrete barrier in the Blue River approximately ½ mile upstream of the confluence with the San Francisco River.
2. Use mechanical methods to eradicate nonnative fishes in an 11-mile reach of main-stem river between the barrier and Fritz Ranch.
3. Restore populations of roundtail chub and spikedace to suitable habitat.
4. Monitor native fish populations following restoration.

AGFD would lead the nonnative fish removal and reintroduction work.

**Logistics**
- The barrier site is not accessible by road. Materials, equipment, and personnel would be delivered by helicopter.
- Concrete would be batched on site.
- No road construction would be authorized.

**Alternatives and decision framework**
- The EA will consider a no-action alternative, the Proposed Action, and other alternatives that meet the purpose and need, to be identified during scoping.
- Reclamation's Area Manager decides whether to implement the Proposed Action, another action, or no action.
- The Apache-Sitgreaves Forest Supervisor decides whether to authorize use of National Forest System land.

**Preliminary issues to be analyzed**
- Biological resources, including special-status species
- Cultural resources
- Water resources
- Wild and Scenic River eligibility
- Recreation
- Soils, sediment transport, and fluvial morphology

**Consistency with land management plans**
- The Apache-Sitgreaves Forest Plan (USFS 1987, as amended) calls for protecting areas with threatened, endangered, or sensitive species, cooperating with AGFD on reintroductions, and maintaining habitat for viable fish populations.
- Forest Service policy is to recover listed species and to repatriate them to suitable unoccupied habitat, consistent with the [[Endangered Species Act]].

**Previous scoping (2004–2005)**
- A 2004 scoping notice was sent to more than 700 individuals, agencies, and organizations, followed by a community meeting in Blue, Arizona, in 2005.
- The 2004 notice covered two proposals: a Forest Service proposal (small barriers, piscicide treatment, and Gila trout reintroduction in headwater streams) and a Reclamation proposal (main-stem barrier and native fish reintroduction). The agencies decided to analyze them in separate NEPA documents because they are not connected actions.

**Public comment**
- Comments are due April 27, 2009, to John McGlothlen, Bureau of Reclamation, Phoenix Area Office.
- Submissions must include the commenter's full name, address, and the project title, "Lower Blue River Native Fish Restoration Project."
- Under law, commenter names and addresses are public records, though individuals may request withholding.
