# Age, Growth, and Food Habits of Johnny, Slenderhead, and Blacksided Darters in Boone County, Iowa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 study by James R. Karr examined age, growth, and food habits of three darter species from the [[Des Moines River]] and [[Bluff Creek]] in [[Boone County]], Iowa. Samples comprised 61 [[Johnny Darter]], 25 [[Slenderhead Darter]], and 18 [[Blacksided Darter]]. Blacksided darters grew fastest over their life span, while johnny darters were slowest for at least the first two years. Condition factors, length-weight relationships, and body-scale relationships were similar across species. Insects made up over 90% of the diet in all three species. Leeches and tapeworms were recorded as parasites.

## Background & Description

The paper is a journal article from the *Iowa Academy of Science* (Vol. 70, 1963). It notes that six darter species had been reported from the study area by [[Starrett (1948)]]. Only four were found in this study: johnny, blacksided, slenderhead, and [[Striped Fantail Darter]]. The [[Western Sand Darter]] and [[Banded Darter]] were reported earlier but not collected. Abnormally high rainfall from May 31 to August 20, 1962, kept water levels high and prevented the low-water sand-darter sampling that Starrett had recommended. Striped fantail darters (well over 100 collected) were reserved for a separate report.

Collection methods were:
- **Long-handled dip net** (most successful): worked by one person kicking through rocks while moving downstream.
- **Seining**: largely unsuccessful; the method of [[Daiber (1956)]] needed two or three people.
- **220-volt AC electrofishing**: partly effective for blacksided darters in deeper water. Darters have reduced swim bladders and do not rise to the surface in the electric field.

Measurements included standard, fork, and total lengths. Fish were preserved in 10% formalin, and weights were taken on a triple beam balance. Scales were read at 80X magnification after mounting between microscope slides. The author followed the scale method critique of [[Van Oosten (1928)]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitat use differed among species:
- **Slenderhead and striped fantail darters** shared fast rapids around large rocks. Of slenderheads, 88% came from the Des Moines River, and 99% of striped fantails came from Bluff Creek. Starrett had found no slenderheads in Bluff Creek, but this study took them up to 3 to 4 miles above the Bluff Creek mouth.
- **Johnny darters** were found just above and below striped fantail areas, typically in deeper water with slower flow.
- **Blacksided darters**: larger individuals occupied deeper, slower pools; smaller individuals occupied shallow water with sand.

Food habits: insects dominated the diet, mainly [[Diptera]] (especially [[Chironomidae]]), [[Ephemeroptera]], [[Trichoptera]], and [[Odonata]]. Crustaceans (Ostracoda, Cladocera, Copepoda) were minor. Small stones and plant material were likely incidental.

Parasites: one slenderhead had a piscicolid leech on the caudal fin, and one blacksided darter had one on the dorsal fin, both from the same area on the same day. Two johnny darters carried tapeworms of the family Bothriocephalidae, taken about 13 miles apart (Bluff Creek and the Des Moines River).

## Key Findings

**Sample and identification**
- Only four of the six previously reported darter species were collected.
- Total length conversion factors were derived for each species (e.g., johnny TL = 1.20 SL).

**Body-scale relationships** (L = total length mm; R = anterior scale radius mm × 80):
- Johnny darter: L = 10.88 + 0.85944R
- Slenderhead darter: L = 18.01 + 0.98332R
- Blacksided darter: L = 17.06 + 0.92788R

**Growth**
- Blacksided darter grew fastest throughout life. Johnny darter was slowest for at least the first two years, and grew faster than slenderhead in years three and four.
- Mean back-calculated total length at annulus 1 was 29.5 mm (johnny), 32.0 mm (slenderhead), and 32.9 mm (blacksided) in age-class I fish. Exact values differ slightly by age class in Table 1.
- Johnny and slenderhead data exhibit [[Lee's Phenomenon]], attributed to slower-growing fish living longer.
- Johnny darters in the Des Moines River were 4 to 5 mm smaller than [[New York]] fish in years one and two, and about 5 mm longer than [[Michigan]] fish in year three.

**Condition and length-weight**
- Mean K(TL) condition factors: johnny 1.382 (n = 61), slenderhead 1.392 (n = 25), blacksided 1.362 (n = 18).
- Log W = log c + n log L; johnny: −4.305 + 3.12 log L; slenderhead: −4.405 + 3.19 log L; blacksided: −4.166 + 3.05 log L.
- All slopes exceed 3, so the darters become plumper as they grow.

**Fecundity**
- Johnny darters (n = 5) carried 198 to 650 eggs (mean 388).
- Slenderhead darters (n = 6) carried 186 to 365 eggs (mean 288).

**Diet and parasites**
- Over 90% of food items were insects in all species, with [[Chironomidae]] the most frequent taxon in johnny and blacksided darters.
- Leech and tapeworm parasitism was recorded, with the number of infected fish small (1 to 2 per species).
