# Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan (Agency Review Draft, USFWS Region 2)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #Poeciliidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This agency review draft, prepared by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque), is a recovery plan for three federally listed fishes of the upper [[Rio Yaqui Basin]]: the [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*, endangered), the [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*, threatened) and the [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*, threatened). The goal is to delist all three by restoring secure, self-sustaining populations in both the United States and Mexico. The plan also covers their critical habitat on [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] and the associated fauna, including the endangered [[Yaqui Topminnow]]. The document is an unapproved draft and is subject to modification and to budget constraints.

## Background & Description
**Legal status.** Listing: USDI 1984. Arizona Game and Fish classes Yaqui chub as Group II (threatened with extirpation, legally protected). Beautiful shiner and Yaqui catfish are Group I (extirpated from Arizona but extant elsewhere, so technically not protected). Critical habitat for all three is designated on San Bernardino NWR near Douglas, Arizona. Only Yaqui chub currently lives on the refuge, sympatric with the Yaqui topminnow. Natural populations of beautiful shiner and Yaqui catfish exist only in Mexico.

**Species accounts**
- **[[Yaqui Chub]]:** Described by Girard (1857) as *Tigoma purpurea*; placed in *Gila* by Miller and Simon (1948) and in subgenus *Temeculina* (Barbour and Miller 1978). It is small (<15 cm TL), deep-bodied and large-scaled. Breeding is in spring, when males turn steel blue. The last substantial natural Arizona population (Astin Spring, Black Draw) dried in 1969, after about 200 adults were moved to Leslie Creek, where a dense population persists. Refuge populations (North and House Ponds, Black Draw) descend from Leslie Creek hatchery stock. It was reintroduced to the Sulphur Springs basin in 1986. Hybridization with *Gila ditaenia* is reported in the Rio de la Concepcion.
- **[[Beautiful Shiner]]:** Described by Girard (1857) as *Moniana formosa*. Chernoff and Miller (1982) found mosaic variation inconsistent with subspecies, but the informal names "Yaqui" and "Guzman" beautiful shiner are used. It is small (rarely >75 mm TL), with brilliantly colored breeding males. It is most common in riffles of small streams and in pools of intermittent streams, and it also thrives in earthen ponds. It was last found in Arizona on the refuge area in 1970. The Yaqui form still occupies much of its Mexican range, while the Guzman form is reduced (reported only from the Rios Casas Grandes and Santa Maria, Chihuahua). Its biology and life history are unknown.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]:** Described by Rutter (1896) as *Villarius pricei*. It is similar to *I. lupus* of the Rio Grande and grows to 50 cm TL. It ranges from 2100 m to sea level in the Yaqui basin, showing broad ecological tolerance. A Santa Cruz basin introduction (late 1800s) persisted about five decades and disappeared by the late 1950s. Its biology is unknown but is presumed similar to [[Channel Catfish]]. Collections in Mexico were mostly of single or few individuals.
- **[[Yaqui Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*): a small live-bearer (males ≤25 mm, females 30–40 mm TL) covered by a separate recovery plan (USFWS 1984a). It tolerates 6–37°C, pH 6.6–8.9, dissolved oxygen 2.0–11 mg/l and salinity from fresh water to 35 gm/l, lives about 1 year, and breeds April–August in variable environments.
- **Candidates for listing:** [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*; rarely >15 cm TL; breeding April–July; persists in Rucker Canyon) and [[Yaqui Sucker]] (*Catostomus bernardini*; to 50 cm TL; spawns May to mid-August; recently extirpated from the U.S.).
- **Other biota:** the Yaqui form of [[Longfin Dace]] (undescribed subspecies of *Agosia chrysogaster*), reduced in the U.S. to one population in West Turkey Creek and three stocks (Rucker Canyon, Leslie Creek, Black Wash); [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), formerly on the refuge; and the endemic San Bernardino springsnail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] covers about 73,000 km² of mountainous northwestern Mexico. It begins as the Rio Papigochic in western Chihuahua, receives the Rio de Bavispe in eastern Sonora, and enters the Gulf of California near Ciudad Obregon. The text gives discharge as about "2800 ha³" per year (a likely OCR or unit error).
- The U.S. portion (Cochise County, Arizona, and Hidalgo County, New Mexico) is only about 1,500 km², under 2% of the watershed. No permanent surface water now occurs in the New Mexico portion.
- Habitats: cold mountain creeks with trout; most fishes occupy intermediate to low elevations in springs, [[Cienegas]], creeks and moderate rivers. Streams have riffle–pool sequences, undercut banks and gravelly bottoms with algae.
- [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]: 974 ha on the U.S.–Mexico border. TNC bought it in 1979 and USFWS took ownership in 1982. Aquatic habitats include seeps (House and Tule Ponds, Snail Spring), artesian flows at six sites, impounded House, Twin and North Ponds, about 2.4 km of Black Draw, and intermittent Hay Hollow Wash. [[Leslie Creek]] was purchased by TNC and is expected to be added to the refuge.
- Fishes known from the refuge area: Yaqui chub, longfin dace, beautiful shiner, Mexican stoneroller, Yaqui sucker and Yaqui topminnow.
- The closed Sulphur Springs (Willcox Playa) basin connected southward to the Rio Yaqui in the recent past. The plan lists factors in the regional decline (habitat deterioration, historic ecological change) in its table of contents, but this part of the text is not included.

## Key Findings
**Recovery goal:** delist Yaqui chub, Yaqui catfish and beautiful shiner by restoring secure, viable, self-sustaining populations in the U.S. and Mexico.

**Recovery criteria**
- *Yaqui chub:* downlist when six secure populations are established on San Bernardino NWR and the Leslie and Turkey Creek populations are secure. Delist when secure populations are protected within the historic range in Mexico.
- *Beautiful shiner:* delist when five stable Yaqui-form populations are established on the refuge, the Guzman form is reestablished in the Mimbres River drainage, New Mexico, and secure, protected populations exist in Mexico.
- *Yaqui catfish:* delist when four stable populations are established at the refuge and secure, protected populations exist in Mexico.

**Actions needed**
1. Implement the Master Plan for San Bernardino NWR.
2. Complete the purchase of [[Leslie Canyon]].
3. Obtain Yaqui catfish and beautiful shiner stocks from Mexico.
4. Reintroduce both species into secure habitats.
5. Establish secure, protected populations of all three species in their historic Mexican range.

**Knowledge gaps:** natural spawning, growth and food habits of Yaqui chub; the whole biology of beautiful shiner and Yaqui catfish; morphological variation across the chub's range; and the status of Mexican populations.

**Caveat:** the draft is not an official government document, and funding is contingent on appropriations and priorities.
