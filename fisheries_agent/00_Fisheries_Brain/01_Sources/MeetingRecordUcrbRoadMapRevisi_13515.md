## Executive Summary

This document is the official meeting record of the **UCRB Road Map Revisions Workshop**, held May 9–11, 1990, in Denver, Colorado, under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) for endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin. Rather than presenting new field data, the workshop convened biologists, water managers, and agency representatives to critique and revise the "Five-Year Road Map for the Recovery Implementation Program, Rare and Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin" (draft dated April 3, 1990). The workshop addressed programmatic weaknesses (funding allocation, water rights acquisition, prioritization schemes, and interagency roles) affecting recovery of four listed fish species: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically "Colorado squawfish"), [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The bulk of the technical content is embedded in a detailed **Summary Table** outlining recovery elements, target dates, and priority rankings for each species across major river reaches (Yampa, Green, Colorado, White rivers, Black Rocks/Westwater, and Cataract Canyon).

## Background & Description

- **Program context**: The [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP), documented in the "Blue Book," coordinates multi-agency efforts (USFWS, Bureau of Reclamation, state wildlife agencies, water users) to recover four endangered big-river fishes native to the Upper Colorado River Basin while accommodating continued water development.
- **Target taxa**:
  - **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — large piscivorous cyprinid, historically called "Colorado squawfish"; addressed extensively regarding flow regimes, spawning cues, and nonnative species (e.g., Northern pike) impacts.
  - **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — catostomid noted as a species that "may only survive in hatchery" absent intervention; recruitment failure was a central concern.
  - **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — canyon-dwelling cyprinid associated with Black Rocks/Westwater, Yampa/Green systems, and Cataract Canyon.
  - **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) — critically depleted cyprinid requiring near-total reliance on captive broodstock and reintroduction (e.g., Dinosaur National Monument, Cataract Canyon, Desolation/Gray Canyon).
- **Geographic scope**: Yampa River, Green River, Colorado River (including the 15-mile reach and reaches below the Gunnison River), White River, Black Rocks/Westwater Canyon, Cataract Canyon, and the Dolores River. The San Juan River was explicitly noted as **not** part of the RIP at this time.
- **Document under revision**: The "Five-Year Road Map" is structured around Goals (broad, attainable end-states), Objectives (action-oriented components of goals), and Tasks (discrete, time-bound work units) — terms formally defined by consensus during the workshop.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recovery actions catalogued in the Summary Table reflect the ecological drivers understood at the time to limit recruitment and survival of the four species:

- **Flow regime management**: Repeated emphasis on maintaining/protecting high spring flows, low stable summer flows, and stable winter flows in the Yampa and Green rivers, and synchronizing Yampa/Green flow timing to maximize backwater nursery habitat for larval fish.
- **Reservoir operations**: Re-regulation of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and thermal/flow management for the Green River; similar attention to Aspinall Unit operations affecting the Gunnison and Colorado rivers.
- **Sediment and geomorphology**: Tasks addressing fish/sediment relationships, substrate conditions needed for reproduction, sediment transport modeling, and backwater habitat creation.
- **Fish passage barriers**: Evaluation of barriers to migration and passage restoration at structures including [[Redlands Diversion Dam]], Taylor Draw, and Price-Stubb.
- **Nonnative species impacts**: Assessment of Northern pike predation/competition effects on pikeminnow, and broader "nonnative fish management" (NNA) tasks across all species and river reaches.
- **Contaminants**: Review of a Yampa River oil spill and Service reports on heavy metal/pesticide effects in the upper Green River, Gunnison, Dolores, and Grand Valley portions of the Colorado River.
- **Water acquisition/legal instruments**: Extensive discussion of instream flow water rights acquisition (including Colorado Water Conservation Board conditional rights transfers), noted as consuming roughly one-third of program funding and a persistent point of institutional friction.
- **Genetic conservation**: Broodstock/refugia programs for all four species, including identification and genetic assessment of wild vs. captive stocks, meant to preserve genetic diversity pending habitat recovery.

## Key Findings

### Programmatic Issues (Objective 1)
- **Strengths of RIP**: Provides structural framework, unites diverse stakeholders, focuses funding productively, and sustains long-term collaborative problem-solving.
- **Weaknesses identified** (38-plus itemized critiques), most significantly:
  - Disproportionate allocation of funds to water rights acquisition without clear scientific basis for "how much water is needed."
  - Absence of a mechanism for detecting long-term fish population trends to evaluate recovery progress.
  - Insufficient prioritization for critically imperiled species (e.g., [[Razorback Sucker]], flagged as possibly surviving only in hatcheries).
  - Funding competition among USFWS programs reducing interagency cooperation.
  - Overemphasis on propagation/stocking relative to habitat-based solutions.

### Road Map Revision (Objective 2)
- Consensus definitions established: **Goal** > **Objective** > **Task** hierarchy.
- Deliberately declined to define "population" and "self-sustaining" given system complexity (turbid, four-species, unique genetics).
- 38 critique items sorted into four categories: **Functions**, **Content**, **Prioritization**, **Structure**.
- Purpose of the Five-Year Plan clarified as: guiding the Management/Technical Committees on recovery priorities, providing research guidance, and supporting funding credibility/progress tracking.
- **Species triage** was formally defined as synonymous with prioritization — acknowledging that management resources may be allocated unequally among species based on immediate need, resource availability, and likelihood of success.

### Prioritization Methodology
- Ten candidate prioritization schemes were proposed, ranging from simple voting on line items to weighted High/Medium/Low scoring of 22 goals and 56 objectives per species.
- The group adopted **Method #9**: independent H/M/L ranking of goals/objectives without species-level weighting, generating numeric priority scores for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (CSF), [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS), [[Humpback Chub]] (HBC), and [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) recovery elements.

### Structured Recovery Task Summary (by species)
Each species' recovery elements were coded by category:
- **HMG** = Habitat Management
- **RMD** = Research/Monitoring/Data
- **NNA** = Nonnative species management
- **STK** = Stocking/propagation
- **OTH** = Other (administrative/planning)

Representative task clusters include:
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]**: Yampa/Green flow acquisition and protection, Flaming Gorge re-regulation, evaluation of hatchery-origin fish in the wild, passage restoration at Redlands, White River habitat/flow assessment.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: Broodstock development, recruitment failure investigation, Green/Yampa spring flow protection, Colorado River spawning habitat requirements, Redlands passage evaluation.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]**: Population structure studies in Black Rocks/Westwater, Yampa/Green, and Cataract Canyon; habitat-flow relationship development; contaminant investigation; genetic assessment of distinct populations.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: Near-total dependence on captive broodstock; reintroduction site identification (Dinosaur National Monument, Cataract Canyon, Desolation/Gray Canyon); culture method development given functional extirpation from the wild.

### Cross-Cutting Recovery Elements
- **Hydrologic/Temperature Monitoring**: Installation/maintenance of gages, modeling needs assessment (target ~1991).
- **Sediment Studies**: Fish/sediment relationship research, substrate-flow modeling for sensitive reaches, backwater creation studies.
- **Recovery Planning Cycle**: Annual review of Recovery Plans, annual Road Map revision and task reprioritization, annual Service–Bureau of Reclamation coordination meetings.
- **Contaminants Program**: Review of Yampa oil spill impacts and heavy metal/pesticide effects across the upper Green, Gunnison, Dolores, and Grand Valley reaches, with recommendations for mitigation.
