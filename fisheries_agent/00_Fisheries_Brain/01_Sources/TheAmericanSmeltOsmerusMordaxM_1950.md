# The American Smelt (*Osmerus mordax*) of South Bay, Manitoulin Island, Lake Huron

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Osmeridae

## Executive Summary

This 1948 study by N. S. Baldwin documents an experimental commercial fishery targeting spawning [[American Smelt]] (*Osmerus mordax*) in the tributary streams of South Bay, [[Manitoulin Island]], [[Lake Huron]]. The fishery, conducted between April 14–27, 1948, using dip-nets and seines, yielded 51,382 pounds of smelt from seven streams and six freshets. The study characterizes the spawning run's timing and behavior, catch composition by age and sex, fecundity, and feeding ecology, situating the South Bay population within the broader context of other North American smelt populations, including the source population at [[Crystal Lake]], Michigan, from which the species dispersed throughout the [[Great Lakes]] after its 1912 introduction. The smelt were of interest as a potential ecological factor affecting production of [[Lake Trout]] (*Cristivomer namaycush*) and [[Lake Whitefish]] (*Coregonus clupeaformis*) in the bay.

## Background & Description

- **Average fork length (1948 spawning catch):** 5.7 inches (145 mm)
- **Average weight:** 0.73 ounce (20.6 g)
- **Size range:** 4.5 to 10.5 inches
- **Sexual dimorphism in length:**
  - Males (69% of catch): averaged 5.6 inches
  - Females: averaged 5.8 inches
  - Difference attributed mainly to a 0.4-inch disparity within age-group III
- **Age-specific average lengths (1948 spring catch):**
  - Age-group II: 5.4 inches (54% of catch)
  - Age-group III: 6.1 inches (38% of catch)
  - Age-group IV: 7.2 inches (8% of catch)
- **Growth rate comparison:** South Bay smelt grew more slowly than Crystal Lake smelt (II: 7 in; III: 7.75 in; IV: 8.25 in) and far more slowly than the "fast-growing race" of [[Lake Champlain]] smelt, but closely approximated growth rates of the anadromous population in [[Great Bay]], New Hampshire.
- **Fecundity (five gravid females examined):**
  - 5.5 in fish: 9,650 eggs
  - 6.3 in fish: 16,200 eggs
  - 7.0 in fish: 27,600 eggs
  - 7.5 in fish: 26,800 eggs
  - 8.8 in fish: 22,600 eggs
  - Egg content was not entirely dependent on body size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** South Bay extends 18 miles into southern Manitoulin Island, connecting to Lake Huron via a channel ~300 yards wide and 5 fathoms deep. The bay has an upper basin (max depth 40 fathoms) where nearly all tributary streams enter, and a shallower lower basin (max depth 10 fathoms) linking to the open lake.
- **Spawning migration:** Smelt entered streams nocturnally after sundown, generally returning to the bay before or at daybreak; in more shaded streams, some fish remained through the day. Schools were also observed at midday in shallow water near a stream mouth.
- **Spawning run range:** Smelt rarely ascended more than a quarter mile upstream, typically halting at the first waterfall with a drop exceeding 1 foot.
- **Run timing:** First observed April 10, 1948; heavy runs began April 14 (day after ice breakup); runs spread to most streams within 5 days; one cold freshet was not entered until April 23.
- **Sex ratio dynamics during run:** Males dominated early (up to 90% of nightly catch); females became progressively more common, achieving parity only in the final 3–4 days of the run.
- **Catch/habitat correlation:** Streams and freshets with rapid current at their mouths yielded disproportionately higher catches (e.g., small Peltier and School Freshets outperformed larger but sluggish Trudeau and Pheasant Creeks), suggesting either greater entry or greater concentration/catchability in fast water.
- **Diet (summer, 1947 — deep water phase):** Of 56 stomachs with appreciable food: *Mysis relicta* (86%), dipterous pupae (12%), sculpins/[[Cottidae]] (9%); also mayfly nymphs, caddis larvae, and ostracods. Diet composition resembled that reported for [[Lake Michigan]] and Crystal Lake smelt, dominated by *Mysis* and *Pontoporeia*.
- **Diet (spawning run, spring 1948):** Of 103 fish examined, 65% had entirely empty alimentary tracts; 19% contained unidentified material in the lower tract; 13% contained mayfly nymphs; 2% plankton; 1% spawn — indicating markedly reduced feeding during spawning, contrasting with Crystal Lake fish behavior.
- **Population structure:** No consistent size differentiation was detected among smelt from different streams (average lengths 5.6–5.7 in; mean weights 0.67–0.74 oz), suggesting a single homogeneous population in the upper basin, with limited exchange to Lake Huron through the lower basin/channel.

## Key Findings

- The 1948 South Bay fishery removed 51,382 pounds of smelt, equating to a catch of 2.2 lbs per acre of bay surface.
- Age-group II dominated the spawning catch (54%), followed by age-group III (38%) and age-group IV (8%); age-group I was entirely absent from the spawning run despite comprising 13% of the 1947 summer (non-spawning) sample, indicating size/age selectivity of the spawning-run sampling method versus gill-net sampling.
- Comparative age-composition data across [[South Bay]], [[Great Bay]] (New Hampshire), and [[Lake Champlain]] populations show substantial variation in growth rates, with Lake Champlain smelt reaching notably larger sizes per age class ("fast-growing race").
- The population's post-1942–43 winterkill recovery (per Van Oosten 1947) reestablished sufficient smelt abundance to justify concern over competitive/predatory interactions with commercially important [[Lake Trout]] and [[Lake Whitefish]].
- Feeding largely ceases during the spawning migration, contrasting with continued feeding activity noted in Crystal Lake smelt during spawning.
- Fecundity estimates (9,650–27,600 eggs per female) demonstrate substantial variability not strictly tied to fish length, an important consideration for stock assessment and reproductive potential modeling.
- Spawning habits (nocturnal stream entry, limited upstream migration, barrier avoidance at small waterfalls) closely paralleled patterns previously documented for smelt populations in [[Michigan]] and [[New Hampshire]].
