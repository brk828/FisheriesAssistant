## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative/regulatory species list compiled by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Ted Cordery, Phoenix Ecological Services) in June 1995, distributed via the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California to the [[LCR Biology Subcommittee]]. It catalogs federally-listed (Endangered, Threatened) and candidate species occurring in the general [[Lower Colorado River]] area below [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. Unlike a primary research paper, it contains no morphological, physiological, or quantitative ecological data — rather, it serves as a regulatory reference enumerating taxa (fish, birds, mammals, reptiles, amphibians, invertebrates, and plants) relevant to water management and Endangered Species Act compliance in the basin. Seven fish species are listed as Endangered, one as Threatened, and three as Candidate species, reflecting the severely imperiled status of the native fish fauna in this heavily regulated river system.

## Background & Description

No morphological data are provided in the source document. This is a taxonomic/regulatory status list only. Known morphological distinctions among the listed cyprinids and catostomids (e.g., the humped nuchal ridge of [[Humpback Chub]], the fleshy dorsal keel of [[Razorback Sucker]], the reduced/absent pelvic-fin morphology of [[Woundfin]]) are well-documented elsewhere in the ichthyological literature but are not addressed in this source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System context**: All listed fish species inhabit the [[Lower Colorado River]] mainstem and associated tributaries downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], a river system profoundly altered by damming, flow regulation, temperature modification, and introduction of nonnative fishes.
- **Native big-river fishes** ([[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] [Colorado Pikeminnow], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) are large-river endemics historically dependent on turbid, warm, free-flowing mainstem habitats — conditions substantially disrupted by dam operations.
- **Tributary/spring-associated species**: [[Desert Pupfish]] occupies isolated springs, marshes, and small tributary habitats; [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Woundfin]], and [[Virgin Spinedace]] are associated with the Virgin River drainage, a key tributary system.
- **Candidate species** ([[Desert Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]]) occupy a broader range of mainstem and tributary riverine habitats and represent taxa considered at-risk but not yet formally listed as of 1995.
- **Co-occurring aquatic taxa** noted in the same habitat complex include the endangered [[Kanab Ambersnail]] (a terrestrial/riparian snail associated with spring seeps) and candidate aquatic invertebrates such as the [[California Floater]] (a freshwater mussel) and [[Grand Wash Springsnail]], indicating that broader aquatic/riparian community concerns (mollusks, gastropods) were being tracked alongside fish status in this basin.
- **Multi-taxa regulatory framework**: The list situates fish conservation within a broader ecosystem management context also encompassing listed birds (e.g., [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Yuma Clapper Rail]]), mammals, reptiles, and amphibians (e.g., [[Relict Leopard Frog]]) dependent on the same riparian corridor.

## Key Findings

- **Endangered fish species (7)** in the Lower Colorado River area below Glen Canyon Dam as of June 1995:
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
  - [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
  - [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
  - [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda* [= *robusta seminuda*])
  - [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*)
- **Threatened fish species (1)**:
  - [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*)
- **Candidate fish species (3)**:
  - [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarkii*)
  - [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*)
  - [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)
- **Non-fish aquatic candidates** flagged in the same basin include the [[California Floater]] mussel and [[Grand Wash Springsnail]], plus the endangered [[Kanab Ambersnail]] — indicating concurrent concern for freshwater mollusk and gastropod decline alongside fish imperilment.
- **Regulatory significance**: The list was generated specifically to inform the [[LCR Biology Subcommittee]] (a multi-agency/water-district body including USFWS, Arizona Game and Fish, Nevada Division of Wildlife, Metropolitan Water District of Southern California, and others) ahead of a June 27, 1995 meeting, underscoring that Colorado River water management decisions were required to account for this suite of protected aquatic and riparian species.
- **Pattern of imperilment**: The predominance of large-bodied, mainstem-dependent cyprinids and catostomids among the Endangered category reflects the disproportionate vulnerability of native big-river fishes to flow regulation and thermal alteration caused by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, compared to the smaller-bodied, spring/tributary-associated taxa more often found in the Threatened/Candidate tiers.
