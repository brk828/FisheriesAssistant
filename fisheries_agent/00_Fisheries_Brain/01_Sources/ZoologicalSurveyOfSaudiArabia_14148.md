# Zoological Survey of Saudi Arabia and the Faunal History of Levantine Freshwater Invertebrates

#marine-biology #freshwater-invertebrates #zoogeography

## Executive Summary

This source is a multi-part compilation from the Proceedings of the 1985 Mainz Symposium on the Fauna and Zoogeography of the Middle East (Beihefte zum TAVO A 28, 1987). It contains three elements: a reference list from [[Por]]'s work on Levantine zoogeography and [[Lessepsian Migration]]; a survey paper by [[Büttiker]] on the [[Zoological Survey of Saudi Arabia]], which began in 1975 and is documented in [[Fauna of Saudi Arabia]]; and the opening of a paper by [[Kinzelbach]] on the faunal history of freshwater Crustacea and Mollusca in the northern Levant. The Büttiker paper is the main body. It reports about 600 new taxa described in volumes 1–6, a 196-contribution bibliometric breakdown, and the proposal of 40 terrestrial protected areas. The Kinzelbach section argues that the [[Orontes River]] fauna is a mix of relict, Asian, Eurosiberian and a few Afrotropical elements, shaped by Pleistocene river reconfiguration.

## Background & Description

**Por reference list (page 1).** The preceding page lists Por's zoogeographic publications, including work on Pleistocene pulsation and [[Lessepsian Migration]], which is the influx of Red Sea biota into the Mediterranean through the [[Suez Canal]]. The list also cites climate and biogeography sources such as [[Pleistocene]] climate reviews and the Levant Quaternary (Horowitz 1979). This material provides the background for the Levantine faunal discussions that follow.

**Zoological Survey of Saudi Arabia (Büttiker).** The survey began systematic sampling in 1975 with an initial emphasis on medical, veterinary and agricultural zoology. It was later extended to:
- taxonomy and systematics
- faunistics
- zoogeography
- ecology
- animal conservation

The work is carried out jointly by the [[Meteorology and Environmental Protection Administration]] (MEPA, Jeddah) and [[Pro Entomologia]] (Natural History Museum, Basle). By the time of the paper, about 160 specialists were involved, and eight volumes of *Fauna of Saudi Arabia* had been published (1979–1986). The author notes that the Arabian Peninsula sits where three major zoogeographic regions meet (Palaearctic, Afrotropical and Oriental), which produces a high level of endemism.

**Freshwater Crustacea and Mollusca of the Northern Levant (Kinzelbach).** The paper is based on taxonomic revisions of selected Crustacea and Mollusca, together with distribution records. The introduction describes the Middle East freshwater fauna as shaped by its location at the meeting point of three continents and by the "Paleoeuropean" fauna, which spread from the northwest through former Aegean river systems. Five invasive "neozoa" are named as recent arrivals from Central and Western Europe: *Orchestia cavimana*, [[Atyaephyra Desmarestii]], *Ferrissia wautieri*, [[Dreissena Polymorpha]] and [[Viviparus Viviparus]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Conservation rationale.** Büttiker argues that the survey inventory identifies rare, endangered and endemic species, which in turn guides the choice of biotopes for protection. Because many species also occur in neighbouring countries, the author calls for a regional approach to conservation. International instruments are cited, including the [[Ramsar Convention]] (1971) for waterfowl habitat and IUCN (1982) protected-area lists.
- **Protected-area proposals.** MEPA proposed 40 terrestrial areas for nature reserves. Sites described in the figure captions include:
  - Wadi Marwani wetland (180 m), about 60 km north of Jeddah, with large numbers of aquatic animals and plants
  - Gabal Balas (2070 m) in the Al-Baha province, with rich endemic fauna and flora
  - Hariti escarpment (1950 m), Makkah province, which holds about 30 [[Griffon Vulture]] nests
- **Imported pests and vectors.** Monitoring is needed for species introduced by air and sea shipping, such as the tick [[Haemaphysalis Longicornis]], and for wind-borne locusts.
- **Migratory species.** Migratory birds need undisturbed, protected biotopes along their routes, which the survey data help to identify.
- **Disease links.** Work with NAMRU-3 (Cairo) on arthropod-borne pathogens led to the discovery of the [[Kadam Virus]] in central Saudi Arabia. This was the first record of the agent outside Africa and the first record in camels.
- **Marine context.** Marine reserves on the Red Sea and Gulf coasts were being planned, based on IUCN/TMRU investigations of reefs and coral assemblages.

## Key Findings

**Survey scale and output (Büttiker)**
- Systematic survey started in 1975. As of the paper, about 160 specialists were involved, and eight volumes of *Fauna of Saudi Arabia* had been published.
- About 600 taxa new to science were described in volumes 1–6.
- The taxonomic compilation (Tables 1–3) covers invertebrates (including Echinodermata, Mollusca, Arachnida, Acari and Insecta), with Insecta having the largest share. Table 3 lists vertebrates, including freshwater fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. The OCR of the table is partly garbled, so exact counts are not reproduced here.
- Insect taxa in Table 2 include Coleoptera (1473 recorded) and Lepidoptera (751 recorded).

**Other results (Büttiker)**
- Several thousand faunistic records are now available.
- Two live-specimen biology projects were started: scorpion life cycles and tenebrionid beetle ethology.
- The survey is described as "still in its infancy," and the author calls for more fieldwork on most animal groups.

**Faunal history of the Orontes (Kinzelbach, abstract)**
- The present Orontes system is the product of a very recent (Pleistocene) merger of at least three former tributaries of the [[Euphrates River]]. The river now reaches the Mediterranean through the [[Amik Golu Basin]].
- Multiple faunal exchanges with the Euphrates can still be traced in the modern fauna.
- The Orontes fauna has four components:
  1. Pre-Pleistocene Paleoeuropean freshwater species
  2. South Asian species
  3. Eurosiberian fauna that arrived during and after the Pleistocene
  4. A very small number of Afrotropical species

**Paleoclimate framing (Por reference context)**
- Cooler, moister Ice Age periods are proposed as the time when Palaearctic elements entered the Arabian Peninsula. Oriental elements are thought to have crossed the land bridge at the Strait of Hormuz or across the dried-up Arabian Gulf.
