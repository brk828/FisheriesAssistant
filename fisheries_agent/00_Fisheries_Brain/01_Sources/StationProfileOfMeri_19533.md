# Station Profile: Meridian National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
The **Meridian National Fish Hatchery (NFH)**, established in 1939 under the authority of the [[White Act 1930]], is a federally operated propagation facility located in Meridian, Mississippi. Built on 106 acres donated by the City of Meridian, the hatchery's construction and initial fish production were delayed by U.S. involvement in World War II, with operations commencing in the early 1950s. The station's core mission centers on the propagation and distribution of five key freshwater/anadromous species — [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and [[Paddlefish]] — with a primary conservation focus on restoring depleted anadromous striped bass populations in the [[Gulf of Mexico]] drainage. The facility also supports mitigation efforts for Federal water development projects and contributes to the [[Farm Pond Program]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Anadromous Restoration**: The hatchery's flagship conservation objective is rebuilding depleted **anadromous striped bass** stocks native to the Gulf of Mexico basin, a species that migrates between marine/estuarine and freshwater riverine systems for spawning.
- **Mitigation Role**: Propagated fish are used to offset adverse impacts to fishery resources caused by Federal water development projects (e.g., dams, reservoirs), a common mitigation function under [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] programs.
- **Stocking Recipients**: Distribution targets include:
  - National Wildlife Refuges
  - National Forests
  - Indian reservations
  - Military installations
  - Private, state, and federal waters across Mississippi (82 counties), South Carolina, and Alabama
- **Farm Pond Program**: Supports stocking of centrarchid species (largemouth bass, bluegill, redear sunfish) into private farm ponds, a long-standing rural fisheries enhancement initiative.
