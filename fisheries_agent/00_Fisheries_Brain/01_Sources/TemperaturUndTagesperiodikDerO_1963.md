# Temperature and Diel Periodicity of "Organic Drift" in *Gammarus pulex*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #amphipoda #crustacea #limnology #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1963 study by K. Müller, conducted at the Limnologische Flußstation Schlitz (Max-Planck-Gesellschaft), investigates the phenomenon of "organic drift" in the freshwater amphipod **[[Gammarus Pulex]]** in the Breitenbach, a small tributary of the Fulda River in Hesse, Germany. Building on prior work demonstrating that invertebrate drift (e.g., in the mayflies **[[Baetis Rhodani]]** and **[[Baetis Vernus]]**) is not merely passive transport but an active, periodic behavior tied to the day-night cycle, the author shows that *G. pulex* drift follows the same diel pattern. Critically, the study demonstrates that while the light-dark cycle sets the fundamental timing of activity (nocturnal drift dominance), water **[[Water Temperature]]** modulates the *amplitude* (intensity) of that activity, consistent with the van't Hoff temperature-rate relationship. The effect is most pronounced during nighttime activity phases and largely absent or negligible during winter daytime phases.

## Background & Description
This paper is behavioral/ecological in focus and does not describe morphological traits of *Gammarus pulex* in detail. The organism is referenced only taxonomically as an **[[Amphipoda|amphipod]]** crustacean, comparable in ecological role to the nearctic species **[[Gammarus Limnaeus]]** (cited via Waters, 1962), which shows an analogous day-night drift periodicity in North American streams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: *Gammarus pulex* inhabits the benthos of the Breitenbach, a small mountain/hill stream (Bergbach) approximately 500 m downstream of its spring source.
- **Drift behavior**: The organism exhibits "organic drift" — downstream displacement driven by active behavioral emergence into the water column, not passive dislodgement. This links to the broader concept of **[[Invertebrate Drift]]** in lotic systems.
- **Diel activity pattern**: *G. pulex* is characterized as a **nocturnally active** species; drift intensity is consistently and substantially higher at night than during the day across nearly all sampling months (see Key Findings table).
- **Sampling design**: Drift was quantified using a fixed trap collecting water filtered at sunrise and sunset over 24-hour periods, across 270 sampling days (1 May 1962 – 31 January 1963). Filtered water volume was ~35 m³/24h (May–October) and increased to ~170 m³/24h (November–January) to prevent the trap from freezing during winter.
- **Comparative context**: The temperature-dependent modulation of a fundamentally light-driven circadian rhythm parallels findings by **[[K. Hoffmann]]** in the lizard *Lacerta sicula*, suggesting a broader cross-taxa principle linking photoperiod-set rhythms with thermally-modulated activity amplitude.

*Note: Night-time drift rates are consistently ~2–3x higher than day-time rates across all months, confirming nocturnal drift dominance.*

### 2. Temperature–Activity Correlation
- Total specimens analyzed: **27,192** *G. pulex* (summer half-year dataset, May–Oct 1962) and **21,512** *G. pulex* (winter half-year dataset, Nov 1962–Jan 1963).
- Drift quantity per day/night correlates positively with mean day/night water temperature, following a pattern consistent with the **[[Van't Hoff Rule]]** (temperature-dependent rate increase).
- **Seasonal asymmetry**: The influence of temperature on **daytime** drift is markedly weaker in the winter half-year than in the summer half-year — in winter, daytime drift shows little to no temperature dependence.

### 3. Short-Term Temperature Manipulation Events
- **Rising temperature (3–5 Jan 1963)**: A temperature increase from 1.5°C to 3.0°C was paralleled by a drift increase from 94 to 376 individuals per night phase.
- **Falling temperature (20–24 Dec 1962)**: A temperature decrease from 3.0°C to 0.5°C was paralleled by a drift decrease from 369 to 85 individuals per night phase.
- These rapid-response events reinforce that nighttime drift intensity tracks water temperature closely and immediately, while winter daytime drift remains largely unaffected.

### 4. Interpretive Model
- The light-dark (Hell-Dunkel) cycle is proposed as the **primary determinant** of *G. pulex* activity timing (i.e., establishing the nocturnal pattern).
- Water **temperature** acts as a **secondary modulator**, setting the *magnitude* (degree) of nocturnal activity/drift, rather than its timing.
- This two-factor model (photoperiod = timing; temperature = amplitude) is presented as analogous to findings in **[[Lacerta sicula]]** by Hoffmann (1957).

### 5. Cross-Species Corroboration
- A postscript notes that **T. F. Waters** (1962, *Ecology* 43:316–320) independently demonstrated a comparable day-night drift periodicity in the North American amphipod **[[Gammarus limnaeus]]**, supporting the generality of this diel "organic drift" behavior across *Gammarus* species.
