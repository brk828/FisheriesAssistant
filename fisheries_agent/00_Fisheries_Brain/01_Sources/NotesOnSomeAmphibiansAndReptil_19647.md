# Amphibians and Reptiles from Northern Mexico

#herpetology #amphibians #reptiles #colubridae #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This short systematic note by [[Robert G Webb]] reports on amphibians and reptiles from northern Mexico that are notable for taxonomic or distributional reasons. It documents range extensions and first state or country records, rectifies a published misidentification of a [[Pituophis Deppei]] specimen, and discusses intergradation and variation in several taxa. The material is based on [[University Of Kansas Museum Of Natural History]] (KU) and [[Michigan State University Museum]] (MSU) specimens collected mainly in 1953–1960. The document does not address fish or aquatic invertebrates; its only aquatic context is the stream, pond and lagoon habitats in which some frogs were found.

## Background & Description

The paper covers 11 taxa in a series of short accounts. Each entry gives collection data, morphometrics (snout-vent or total length, scale counts, ventrals and subcaudals) and comparisons with earlier descriptions. Counts of ventrals exclude the anal plate, and counts of subcaudals exclude the cornified tip. Locality coordinates for Coahuila records follow [[Baker 1956]]. The author acknowledges field assistance from John K. Greer and funding from the Sigma Xi-RESA Research Fund.

Taxa treated:
- **Amphibians (4 taxa):** [[Eleutherodactylus Vocalis]], [[Gastrophryne Olivacea]] and related anurans, plus notes on [[Scaphiopus Hammondi]] and [[Bufo Cognatus]] from the same sites.
- **Lizards (1 taxon):** [[Cnemidophorus Tigris Variolosus]], with intergrades to [[Cnemidophorus Tigris Marmoratus]].
- **Snakes (6 taxa):** [[Coluber Constrictor Stejnegerianus]], [[Masticophis Lineatus]], [[Elaphe Subocularis]], [[Elaphe Obsoleta Bairdi]], [[Elaphe Triaspis Intermedia]], [[Procinura Aemula]], [[Gyalopion Canum]] and [[Tantilla Nigriceps Nigriceps]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The localities span several Mexican states and habitat types:

- **Sinaloa (Santa Lucia, ~5000 ft):** Frogs occupied large rocks and boulders along a fast, clear stream with small cataracts and pools. The June collections (dry season) were concentrated near water. The August wet-season specimens suggested a more dispersed population, including one male hopping along a footpath about 100 yards from the stream.
- **Durango, Rio Nazas floodplain:** Gastrophryne olivacea called at night from shallow (about two inches deep) quiet-water lagoons with emergent grass, and was also found in moist leaf litter by day. A cement irrigation tank at Mapimi held several other anurans.
- **Coahuila, Cuatro Ciénegas:** A dry mesquite plain with large clear-water ponds and cattail and sedge marshes. The Coluber specimens were taken beside a large pond.
- **Chihuahua, Sonora and Durango plains:** Dry hillsides, sand dunes and desert scrub, with some collections under rocks.
- **Tamaulipas (Sierra Madre Oriental, 7900 ft):** An upland locality for Elaphe triaspis intermedia.

The author notes that the Coluber constrictor stejnegerianus locality is an inland record, far from its principal Gulf Coast range. Several localities are unmapped villages on main highways, which the author flags for future workers.

## Key Findings

**1. Eleutherodactylus vocalis, southern Sinaloa**
- Nineteen frogs were collected on June 22–24 (1955), plus four more on August 10, 1957 (MSU 2539–42). The total is 23 specimens.
- Males were 22 and 29 mm snout-vent length in the June series. Females ranged from 23 to 56 mm, and all females of 46 mm or more were gravid (13 specimens).
- Tympanum-to-head-width ratio averaged 0.342 (0.29–0.41) in five males and 0.170 (0.14–0.21) in 18 females, so it separates the sexes.
- No males had vocal sacs or slits. Two of five males had a distinct middorsal pale stripe.
- The occurrence is a slight northwesterly range extension from Nayarit. Assignment to *E. vocalis* is tentative because the series combines features of *E. avocalis*. The author suggests the two species may be conspecific, and notes that Duellman (1958) proposed the same for *rugulosus*, *vocalis* and *avocalis*.

**2. Gastrophryne olivacea, Durango**
- Specimens from 4 mi W Mapimi (MSU 2533) and 1.5 mi NW Nazas (MSU 2534–38) extend the known range southward into central Durango. The largest female was 29 mm and the largest male 28 mm.

**3. Cnemidophorus tigris variolosus and intergrades**
- Specimens from Campana, Conejos and Horizonte fit *variolosus*. A 35 mm juvenile from Horizonte has a pale blue-white venter, which suggests juveniles lack the adult black ventral coloration.
- Four lizards from 6 mi E Zavalza (MSU 827–30) are intergrades between *variolosus* and *marmoratus*. The author notes that *Cnemidophorus tigris pulcher* (Williams, Smith and Chrapliwy, 1960) appears to match these four intergrades.

**4. Coluber constrictor stejnegerianus, Coahuila**
- Two inland specimens (KU 47111 and KU 53733) extend the known range beyond the northwestern Coahuila record of Zweifel (1954). They are from one locality, 10 mi S Cuatro Ciénegas.
- KU 47111 has a body of about 610 mm and KU 53733 about 600 mm. Both have eight supralabials.
- The snakes are uniform olive or blue-gray, with no juvenile pattern. The author questionably recorded faint lateral stripes on a living animal.

**5. Masticophis lineatus, eastern Chihuahua**
- KU 38341 (July 2, 1953) is about 1420 mm long, with 187 ventrals and 116 subcaudals. It extends the species' range well east of its previously known southern Sonoran Pacific-coast distribution.
- The author supports *M. lineatus* as a distinct species from *M. flagellum*. He also considers *M. mentovarius* conspecific with *lineatus*.

**6. Elaphe subocularis, first Durango record**
- Vultures were seen feeding on a carcass on August 7, 1959. The specimen (MSU 2543) is a female about 1145 mm long, collected 2 mi NE Leon Guzman. It has 33–36–24 dorsal scale rows.

**7. Elaphe obsoleta bairdi, Coahuila**
- KU 39564 was previously referred to *Pituophis deppei* (Fugler and Webb, 1956; Duellman, 1960). The author rectifies this and assigns it to *E. o. bairdi*. Its lack of pattern, the divided-versus-entire anal plate (the anal plate character alone is not decisive), the whitish labials, the rostral and the high ventral and subcaudal counts support the assignment.
- KU 28092 (striped pattern, 249 ventrals) is also *E. o. bairdi*. Together the two specimens are the second and third Coahuila records (Smith, 1938).

**8. Elaphe triaspis intermedia, Tamaulipas**
- KU 35094 (August 5, 1953; 7900 ft, Sierra Madre Oriental) is the northernmost record for the species in eastern Mexico. It has 267 ventrals and 87 caudals, with no head or body pattern.

**9. Procinura aemula, southern Sonora**
- KU 47568 (January 23, 1959) is a male about 277 mm long (range 231–446 mm), with 146 ventrals and 41 subcaudals. The author considers it the sixth specimen on record (Zweifel and Norris, 1955).

**10. Gyalopion canum, Durango**
- MSU 2544 is the second Mexican specimen of this little-known species and the first from Durango. It has 45 body blotches, which compares with 46 in a Nuevo León specimen. The snake had apparently been killed recently, and a scorpion's remains were found in its gut. Tanner (1960) reported the species from Chihuahua.

**11. Tantilla nigriceps nigriceps, Durango**
- MSU 2545 (August 12, 1959; 2 mi S El Palmito, 4850 ft) is the first Durango record. It has 144 ventrals, 66 caudals and a black head cap. Its midventral region was orange-red in life.

**Overall:** Most entries are range extensions or first state records. The note also corrects one published misidentification and documents intergradation between two whiptail subspecies.
