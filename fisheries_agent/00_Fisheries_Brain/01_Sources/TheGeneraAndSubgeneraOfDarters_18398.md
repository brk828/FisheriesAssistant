# The Genera and Subgenera of Darters (Percidae, Etheostomatini)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This revision by Lawrence M. Pace, published in the [[Occasional Papers of the Museum of Natural History]] (University of Kansas, No. 90, 1981), re-examines the generic and subgeneric classification of North American darters. It combines phenetic (numerical taxonomic) and cladistic methods on 142 species scored for 52 morphological characters. The study supports retaining the three-genus system of [[Bailey and Gosline Classification]] ([[Percina]], [[Ammocrypta]], [[Etheostoma]]) but proposes several subgeneric changes, including the creation of [[Fuscatelum]] and [[Belophlox]] and the synonymy of [[Ulocentra]], [[Hololepis]] and [[Microperca]] with other subgenera.

## Background & Description

Darters are small percid fishes of eastern North America. Their classification has been largely based on the work of [[Reeve M. Bailey]], whose three-genus scheme (Bailey and Gosline, 1955) recognized 22 subgenera (eight in Percina, two in Ammocrypta, twelve in Etheostoma). Since then, five additional subgenera in Etheostoma and one in Percina had been proposed.

The study examined 1,379 specimens of 142 species (6 to 10 specimens per species, averaging 9.7). Characters were drawn from meristic counts, body proportions, squamation, pigmentation and reproductive features. Color characters and intraspecifically variable squamation were excluded from numerical analysis because they could not be coded reliably. The author stresses that the morphological diagnoses of each genus and subgenus are a major contribution in their own right.

Key analytical tools included:
- [[UPGMA]] clustering with three distance measures
- [[WAGNER Networks]] (unrooted and rooted) for cladistic analysis
- [[Cophenetic Correlation Coefficient]] (CPCC) for comparing how well each structure represents the distance matrix

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses darters mainly as taxonomic units; it provides limited ecological data. Notable ecological and habitat points include:

- [[Percina roanoka]] is adapted for life in shallow rocky riffles, a unique habitat among logperches.
- [[Etheostoma vitreum]] (Glassy Darter) exhibits communal spawning, with eggs attached to sides of rocks and logs.
- [[Etheostoma boschungi]] (Slackwater Darter) deposits eggs on vegetation in seepage water in pastures; [[Etheostoma trisella]] shares this specialized egg-deposition site.
- [[Boleichthys]] species use pool habitat, and eggs of several species are buried in substrate or attached to plants, stones or logs.
- Morphological traits such as lateral line arching, body depth, and branchiostegal membrane connection are linked to habitat and body form, with [[Boleosoma]] displaying wide physiognomic variation.

Geographic data appear mainly through specimen localities in Appendix A, including the Tennessee, Cumberland, Alabama, Green, Roanoke and Barren River systems.

## Key Findings

- **Method performance:** UPGMA with the correlation coefficient produced the structure most congruent with the existing classification and the best clustering (large clusters formed from small clusters). WAGNER rooted with an outgroup gave the least congruent results. The CPCC for the 142-species analysis was 0.798 (UPGMA correlation) and 0.786 to 0.836 across other procedures.
- **Genera:** Bailey's three-genus classification is retained. The final cluster of all 142 species joined "primitive darters" (Percina, Ammocrypta and many Etheostoma) with "advanced darters" (Etheostoma only).
- **Subgeneric changes (summary of nine):**
  1. [[Nanostoma]] recognized for E. zonale, with [[Ulocentra]] synonymized (Nanostoma is the older name).
  2. E. juliae transferred from Oligocephalus to Nothonotus.
  3. Villora reduced to a monotypic subgenus containing E. edwini.
  4. Fuscatelum new subgenus described for E. parvipinne (no bright body or fin colors).
  5. Belophlox recognized for E. mariae, E. fricksium and E. okaloosae.
  6. Austroperca synonymized with Oligocephalus.
  7. Boleichthys recognized for E. exile.
  8. Hololepis and Microperca synonymized with Boleichthys, because E. saludae and E. collis group with Microperca and Boleichthys was otherwise non-monophyletic.
  9. Ozarka (Williams and Robison, 1980) and Psychromaster are restructured with species formerly in Oligocephalus and Psychromaster; E. tuscumbia is kept in a monotypic subgenus.
- **Retained classifications:** E. chlorosomum and E. davisoni remain in Vaillantia, and E. tippecanoe remains in Nothonotus, despite failing to cluster in phenograms. E. tuscumbia and E. trisella are placed in separate subgenera.
- **Ammocrypta:** Two distinct clusters support the disubgeneric classification (Ammocrypta and Crystallaria). E. vitreum is placed in Etheostoma rather than Ammocrypta.
- **Percina:** The logperch (Percina s.s.) inclusion in Percina is supported; P. roanoka joined the Percina cluster late because of its specialized riffle morphology.
- **Phylogeny:** Five major divisions within Etheostoma are proposed: (1) Boleichthys (and perhaps Villora), (2) Psychromaster, (3) Litocara, (4) Etheostoma, Nanostoma, Doration, Boleosoma, Ioa, Vaillantia (and perhaps Allohistium), and (5) Nothonotus, Fuscatelum, Belophlox, Ozarka, Oligocephalus and Catonotus.
- **Diagnostic evidence:** Boleosoma is supported by a flattened bifurcate female genital papilla shared by all five species. E. zonale shares Ulocentra features (short snout, narrow head, narrow interpelvic width) rather than Etheostoma (s.s.).
- **Limitations:** The author notes that color data were incomplete, that the biochemical data for Percina were missing for about half of the species, and that the phylogeny of Etheostoma remains difficult to resolve pending more data.
