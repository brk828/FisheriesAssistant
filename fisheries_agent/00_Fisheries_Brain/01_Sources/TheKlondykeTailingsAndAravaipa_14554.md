# The Klondyke Tailings and Aravaipa Creek Water Quality Investigation

#water-quality #environmental-toxicology #mine-tailings #native-fish-protection #aquifer-contamination

## Executive Summary

This investigation by the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] documents water quality impacts from the [[Klondyke Tailings]] along [[Aravaipa Creek]] in [[Graham County]], Arizona. The tailings consist of 220,000 tons of milled ore rich in lead, zinc, copper, and silver situated within 50 feet of Aravaipa Creek's right bank. Sampling in March-April 1993 revealed severe contamination of shallow subsurface waters with multiple violations of aquifer water quality standards and health-based guidance levels for drinking water. Surface waters at [[Stowe Gulch]], 2.1 miles downstream and the beginning of critical native fish habitat in the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness]], showed no contamination. The investigation documented violations of surface water quality standards for copper in a stock pond and numerous violations in shallow groundwater. Remediation is required due to shallow groundwater contamination and the threat of future tailings erosion from floods. The property owner is obligated under Arizona law (ARS §49-261) to prevent further discharges.

## Background & Description

The [[Klondyke Tailings]] are located on the right bank of [[Aravaipa Creek]] at [[Laurel Canyon]], 2.1 miles upstream of [[Stowe Gulch]] and 1.2 miles downstream from the hamlet of Klondyke in [[Graham County]], Arizona. The tailings resulted from milling operations by the [[Athletic Mining Company]] between 1948-1958, processing ore from two principal mines: the [[Head Center Mine]] and [[Iron Cap Mine]].

**Geological and Mining Context:**
- Total tailings: 220,000 tons of milled ore
- Primary metals: lead (9,178 tons), zinc (12,170 tons), copper (591 tons), silver (150,836 ounces), gold (2,939 ounces)
- [[Head Center Mine]] ore averaged 11.7% lead, 17.4% zinc, 1.7% copper, 1.72 oz/ton silver
- [[Iron Cap Mine]] ore averaged 7.0% lead, 10.6% zinc, 1.1% copper, <1.0 oz/ton silver
- Approximately 80% of ore was oxidized (oxide ores), with [[Iron Cap Mine]] contributing mostly sulfide ores
- Key minerals: galena (PbS), sphalerite (ZnS), chalcopyrite (CuFeS₂), cerussite (PbCO₃), anglesite (PbSO₄), smithsonite (ZnCO₃)

**Hydrological Setting:**
[[Aravaipa Creek]] heads in the [[Sulfur Springs Valley]] 13 miles northwest of Wilcox, flows northwest for 40 miles through a broad alluvial valley to [[Stowe Gulch]], then enters a narrow canyon for 22 miles to the [[San Pedro River]]. The creek is typically dry upstream of [[Stowe Gulch]], but heavy winter rains in early 1993 produced residual flow through the tailings area. Perennial flow begins abruptly at [[Stowe Gulch]] where bedrock forces subsurface water to the surface.

**Tailings Configuration:**
Two piles exist: the "lower tailings" (80 x 200 yards, southern edge 7 yards above grade) and "upper tailings" (110 x 80 yards, 2-5 yards above grade) located 67 yards upstream. The southern edges lie 10-20 yards from Aravaipa Creek's right bank. A production well drawing from 80-100 feet depth at 250 gpm is located at the toe of the lower tailings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Critical Habitat:**
The reach of [[Aravaipa Creek]] beginning at [[Stowe Gulch]] contains seven native fish species, all either threatened or endangered. This reach represents the furthest upstream perennial water and flows into the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness]] area managed by [[The Nature Conservancy]]. Protection of this habitat was the primary concern prompting the investigation.

**Watershed Characteristics:**
- Valley width: 0.5 to 1 mile in alluvial reach
- Streambed: alluvial sands with scattered gravels and small cobbles
- Vegetation: mesquite covering most creekside land; mature willow and cottonwood canopy near [[Stowe Gulch]]
- Elevations: tailings site at 3,420 feet; creek headwaters at 4,250 feet; confluence with [[San Pedro River]] at 2,120 feet
- Surrounding mountains: [[Winchester Mountains]], [[Pinaleno Mountains]] (Mount Graham 10,713 ft), [[Galiuro Mountains]], [[Santa Teresa Mountains]], [[Turnbull Mountains]]

**Flow Conditions (March-April 1993):**
At [[Stowe Gulch]], flow divided into two braids: left braid (20.32 cfs) and right braid (3.98 cfs). At the tailings, the right main braid flowed 50-80 yards from the right cut bank, with a "far right braid" (0.037 cfs or 16.4 gpm) beginning next to the lower tailings pile. Notably, flow in the right braid increased 43% (2.72 to 3.90 cfs) over 700 yards along the tailings reach, attributed to subsurface alluvial flow rising to the surface.

**Contamination Pathways:**
1. Rainfall runoff erosion from tailings surfaces into creek and stock pond
2. Subsurface leaching into shallow groundwater along creek bank
3. Direct contact of flood waters with tailings berms
4. Minimal direct deposition of tailings in streambed (estimated <2 pounds observed)

## Key Findings

**1. Surface Water Quality - Aravaipa Creek at Stowe Gulch:**
- All surface water quality standards met at [[Stowe Gulch]]
- Right braid: pH 7.45, EC 432 µmhos/cm, TDS 268 mg/l, flow 3.98 cfs
- Left braid: pH 8.16, EC 363 µmhos/cm, TDS 253 mg/l, flow 20.32 cfs
- No toxic trace metals detected except boron (agricultural indicator)
- Native fish habitat not in jeopardy from tailings discharges
- Slight differences between braids (right braid less alkaline, harder water) too small to indicate substantial acid mine drainage

**2. Surface Water Quality - Aravaipa Creek at Klondyke Tailings:**
- Right main braid above tailings: pH 8.57, EC 353 µmhos/cm, TDS 323 mg/l, flow 2.72 cfs
- Right main braid below tailings: pH 8.40, EC 378 µmhos/cm, TDS 293 mg/l, flow 3.90 cfs
- No significant differences upstream vs. downstream in main braid
- Only trace metals detected: boron and iron
- All surface water quality standards met
- Far right braid below tailings: pH 7.76, EC 495 µmhos/cm, TDS 385 mg/l, flow 0.037 cfs
- Far right braid showed elevated alkali metals (Ca, Mg), alkali earth metals (Na, K), sulfates (SO₄ 117 mg/l vs. 20.7 mg/l in main braid), nitrates, fluoride, and manganese (0.05 mg/l)
- 31% higher TDS and more acidic (pH 7.76 vs. 8.40) than main right braid
- Strongly suggestive of groundwater inflow from tailings

**3. Stock Pond Contamination:**
- Located at [[Laurel Canyon]] and Aravaipa Creek confluence
- Receives runoff from northwest corner of lower tailings via dry wash
- pH 8.78, EC 229 µmhos/cm, hardness 104 mg/l
- Elevated trace metals: Cu (0.031 mg/l total, 0.029 mg/l dissolved), Fe (0.30), Pb (0.051), Mn (0.16), Zn (0.09), Mo (0.01)
- **VIOLATION:** Dissolved copper (0.029 mg/l) exceeded surface water quality standard for aquatic and wildlife warm water acute (0.018 mg/l at 104 mg/l hardness)

**4. Shallow Subsurface Water - Severe Contamination:**
Two well points sampled in far right braid: between tailings piles (24 yards downstream of upper tailings) and downstream of lower tailings (48 yards downstream).

Well point between tailings (WP2):
- pH 7.35, EC 536 µmhos/cm, TDS 416 mg/l
- Elevated metals: As (0.012), Be (0.0266), Ba (0.59), Cd (0.115), Cu (0.29), F (0.61), Pb (0.630), Mn (7.81), Ni (0.14), Zn (11.0)

Well point downstream of lower tailings (WP3):
- pH 7.41, EC 713 µmhos/cm, TDS 553 mg/l
- Elevated metals: Ag (0.008), As (0.055), Be (0.0136), Ba (0.48), Cd (0.0450), Cr (0.023), Cu (3.40), F (0.69), Pb (13.2), Mn (5.27), Ni (0.10), Zn (29.9)

**Multiple Violations Documented:**
- Surface water quality standards (domestic water source): As, Be, Cd, Pb
- Aquifer water quality standards: As, Cd, Pb
- Health-Based Guidance Levels: As, Be, Cd, Cu, F, Pb, Mn, Ni, Zn
- 23% higher TDS in subsurface water below lower tailings vs. above upper tailings

**5. Production Well - Uncontaminated:**
- Located at toe of lower tailings, downgradient of all tailings
- Depth: 80-100 feet, capacity: 250 gpm
- pH 6.84, EC 297 µmhos/cm, TDS 231 mg/l
- No trace metals detected
- Lower concentrations of sulfates, alkali metals, alkali earth metals than main creek flow
- Deep groundwater unaffected by tailings

**6. Upper Tailings Pond Water - Highly Acidic:**
- Ponded rainwater on upper tailings surface: 30 x 30 yards x 6 inches deep
- pH 4.85, EC 1,310 µmhos/cm, TDS 1,470 mg/l
- Sulfates: 897 mg/l
- Elevated trace metals: Be (0.0052), Cd (0.0088), Cu (0.17), Pb (0.11), Mn (73.6), Zn (0.66), Mo (0.01)
- Magnesium, sodium, chloride, bicarbonate all nondetectable
- Would violate multiple surface water quality standards if applied: Agricultural Livestock Watering (Cd, Cu, Pb, Zn), Agricultural Irrigation (Mn, Zn), Domestic Water Source (Be, Cd, Pb), Fish Consumption (Be), Full Body Contact (Be), Aquatic & Wildlife warm water acute (Cu, Pb, Zn)
- Demonstrates acid-generating capacity of tailings

**7. Tailings Characterization (8 samples, surface only):**
- Average: 81% solids, pH 5.4
- Soluble solids: 9,740 mg/kg average
- Leachable sulfates: 6,420 mg/kg average
- Leachable lead (SPLP): 1.7 mg/l average
- Trace metals exceeding Groundwater Protection Guidance Levels: As, Zn, Cd, Pb, Cu, Ag, Co
- Lead exceeded Health-Based Guidance Level for soils
- Confirmed leachability: Cd, Cu, Pb, Mn, Zn, Be (in pond water and subsurface water); As, Ba, Cr, Ag (in tailings solids and subsurface water)

**8. Streambed Sediments:**
Three samples from far right braid: upstream of tailings, in tailings deposition area (next to upper tailings gully), downstream of tailings.

Tailings deposition area showed elevated concentrations:
- Soluble solids: 760 mg/kg (vs. 620 upstream, 460 downstream)
- Leachable sulfate: 225 mg/kg (vs. 150 upstream, 124 downstream)
- Zinc: 110 mg/kg (vs. 74 upstream, 54 downstream)
- Copper: 51 mg/kg (vs. 16 upstream, 14 downstream)
- Lead: 370 mg/kg (vs. 40 upstream, 31 downstream)
- Silver: 0.19 mg/kg (vs. <0.1 upstream and downstream)
- Confirms erosional transport from upper tailings gully into watercourse
- Estimated quantity in streambed: <2 pounds total

**9. Erosional Transport Areas Identified:**
Lower tailings (3 areas):
- Northwest corner draining into stock pond
- South face draining toward Aravaipa Creek (deposit 5-10 yards from right bank)
- Drainage onto mill site

Upper tailings (3 areas):
- Drainage onto low ground east of pond
- Gully on southern edge draining into Aravaipa Creek
- Drainage onto mill site
- Southern berm contacted by recent flood waters (no erosional channels evident)

**10. Legal Violations:**
- Arizona Revised Statutes §49-263.A.1: Unlawful discharge of pollutants into waters without permit
- Subsurface discharges from tailings into Aravaipa Creek watercourse
- Erosional transport of tailings into watercourse
- Property owner liable for enforcement action, "guilty of a class 6 felony" for criminal negligence (ARS 49-263.B)
- Property owner obligated by ARS §49-261 to prevent further discharges

**11. Remediation Recommendations (for discussion):**
1. **Preferred:** Move tailings to higher ground with appropriate liners, berms for rainwater diversion, and soil cap to prevent percolation
2. **Alternative (more risky):** Stabilize tailings in place with soil cap and vegetative cover, berms to prevent overland flow contact, plus bank stabilization (rip-rap or cement grouting) with concrete diverters upstream; risk of future flood washout remains
3. **Not viable:** Reprocessing for silver recovery (0.43 oz/ton) not economically attractive due to high sulfide content making flotation difficult/expensive, low silver prices ($4.40/oz in Nov 1993), and uncertain environmental liabilities

**12. Compliance Timeline:**
- April 12, 1993 letter: Recommended improving tailings surface berms by July 1, 1993 to prevent runoff
- January 20, 1994 letter: Required submission of rough conceptual remediation plan with estimated costs by April 1, 1994
- Department policy: Strive for voluntary compliance by responsible party

**13. Positive Findings:**
- Surface waters of far right braid and right main braid of Aravaipa Creek met all surface water quality standards
- Aravaipa Creek waters at [[Stowe Gulch]] met all standards
- Deep groundwater (production well) unaffected
- No surface flow changes between tailings area and [[Stowe Gulch]] (2.1 miles)
- Severity of tailings effects on surface water likely low due to neutral to slightly alkaline pH and relatively low electrical conductivities
- Predominance of zinc and lead oxide ores (vs. sulfide ores) reduces acid mine drainage potential
- Native fish habitat at [[Stowe Gulch]] not currently in jeopardy
