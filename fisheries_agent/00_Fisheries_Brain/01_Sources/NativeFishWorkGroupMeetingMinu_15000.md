# Native Fish Work Group Meeting Minutes - August 19, 1991

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

The [[Native Fish Work Group]] convened on August 19, 1991, in [[Boulder City]], Nevada, to review progress on native fish restoration efforts at [[Lake Mead]] and plan future actions. The meeting focused on the [[Yuma Cove]] backwater project involving [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and discussed expansion to [[Davis Cove]] and [[Davis Dam]] spillway pool. Despite successful spawning of 90 adult razorback suckers stocked in the treated backwater, no larvae were found after April and no young fish were recovered, indicating a production failure requiring investigation.

## Background & Description

The [[Native Fish Work Group]] represents a multi-agency cooperative effort involving the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona State University]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[National Park Service]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The program focuses on restoring populations of endangered native fish species in the [[Colorado River]] system.

The [[Yuma Cove]] backwater was isolated by a berm structure and treated with [[Rotenone]] fish toxicant to eliminate non-native species. Ninety adult [[Razorback Sucker]] were captured from the lake side and stocked into the treated backwater. These fish were marked with [[PIT Tags]] (Passive Integrated Transponder tags), representing the crew's first attempt at this tagging technique. The fish spawned successfully, with larvae monitored from February through April 1991.

A net closure was designed and constructed for [[Davis Cove]] but remained uninstalled due to lack of available fish for stocking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The berm structure at [[Yuma Cove]] proved durable, experiencing only 2-3 feet of erosion on the top width while maintaining structural integrity. The backwater provided spawning habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]], with successful spawning documented through larval monitoring in February, March, and April.

However, no larvae were detected in May and June, and scuba diving and snorkeling surveys failed to locate any young fish. Approximately 55 of the original 90 adult fish (61%) were recaptured through summer and released back to the lake. Of recaptured fish, roughly 92% retained their [[PIT Tags]], with tag losses attributed to inexperienced tagging technique rather than biological factors.

The absence of young fish despite successful spawning suggests a critical bottleneck in early life stages, potentially related to predation by odonates (dragonfly nymphs), water quality parameters, or other environmental factors requiring investigation.

## Key Findings

**Immediate Recommendations:**
1. Repeat [[Rotenone]] treatment of [[Yuma Cove]] in fall 1991 to eliminate odonates and verify lack of production beyond larval stage
2. Restock [[Yuma Cove]] with same ratios and numbers of adult [[Razorback Sucker]] from lake; remove fish in April/May
3. Implement intensive monitoring program measuring physical, chemical, and biological properties weekly from stocking through late summer to determine production failure causes
4. Proceed with [[Davis Cove]] development: install net closure, eradicate existing fish, sign and close area
5. Develop net closure for [[Davis Dam]] spillway pool to receive half the fish from [[Yuma Cove]] if production proves successful
6. Secure alternative fish sources for [[Davis Cove]] and [[Davis Dam]]; consider stocking [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) in one location
7. Continue annual [[March Razorback Roundup]]
8. Promote cooperative program through public outreach
