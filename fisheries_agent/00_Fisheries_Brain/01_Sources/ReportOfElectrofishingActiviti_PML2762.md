# Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Management Records, Lower Colorado River (2005–2009)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of four items from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Arizona Fishery Resources Office and related partners. It covers two 2006 field memos on electrofishing and fish salvage, a December 2006 memo on the capture and release of endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], and a 2009 email exchange about stocking and tag records used in a fish sanctuary report. The field work centered on [[High Levee Pond]] at [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] and the Colorado River. The 2009 correspondence focuses on data reconciliation: missing original field sheets, a probable tag-number typo, and tag records removed from an online database.

## Background & Description

The memos are issued by the USFWS Arizona Fishery Resources Office in Parker, Arizona, and are addressed to the Lower Colorado River fisheries group (LCRFISH) and to project partners. Partners named include the Bureau of Reclamation (USBR), the National Park Service (NPS), the California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG), and Arizona State University (ASU).

The focal species are native and listed fishes of the lower Colorado River:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Catostomidae): the main subject of capture, tagging, and release. Adults were handled and marked with PIT tags.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*, Cyprinidae): two individuals were recorded, one of them identified as a male.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*, Centrarchidae): a non-native predator removed from High Levee Pond during salvage.
- **[[Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*, Poeciliidae): the only fish observed at Three Fingers Lake.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The habitats described are a mix of mainstem river, backwater ponds, and managed lakes:

- **[[High Levee Pond]]** is a backwater pond on the Colorado River within [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]]. It is the main site for razorback sucker capture and salvage. Largemouth bass are also present there, and removing them is part of the management work.
- **[[Three Fingers Lake]]** is a lake in Imperial County, California. In 2006 it held no native fish captured by electrofishing, only mosquitofish and abundant bullfrog tadpoles.
- **[[Lake Havasu]]** and the **[[Office Cove]]** area (linked to [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] in the 2006 memo) were release sites for bonytail and razorback sucker.
- The Colorado River near the **[[Oxbow Campground]]** (Bureau of Land Management recreation area, about 1 mile upriver of High Levee Pond) was the release site for salvaged razorback suckers in 2006.
- The 2009 email places stocking and sanctuary sites at Emerald Canyon and Needles golf course ponds (Parker and Needles area) and at the Imperial National Wildlife Refuge DU Pond 1.

## Key Findings

### 1. Electrofishing at Three Fingers Lake (30 May 2006)
- Two boats were used for 5,279 seconds (88 minutes) of generator time, from 1530 to 2130 hours.
- No fish were captured by electrofishing.
- The only fish seen was mosquitofish, along with many bullfrog tadpoles.

### 2. Fish salvage at High Levee Pond (31 May 2006)
- Salvage ran from 1730 to 2100 hours using the CDFG electrofishing boat, with two 300-foot trammel nets set alongside.
- The nets captured 2 razorback suckers. Electrofishing captured the rest.
- A total of 60 razorback suckers were handled. The fish averaged **53 cm** total length and **1,897 g**.
- **21 of the 60 fish were recaptures (35%)**. The remaining untagged fish were marked with **125 kHz PIT tags**.
- All razorback suckers were in good condition and were released into the Colorado River at the [[Oxbow Campground]] by 2240 hours.
- CDFG relocated **24 largemouth bass** (about 15 cm TL) to other waters.
- No bonytail were seen.

### 3. Capture and release, December 2006
- Mitch Thorson and Joseph Barnett captured six razorback suckers from High Levee Pond on the night of 18 December 2006. Total lengths were 495–585 mm and weights were roughly 960–2,080 g. Sexes included both males and females, and some fish were recaptures. The males were ripe.
- The fish were released into Office Cove on 19 December 2006.
- Two bonytail (430 and 480 mm TL) were also handled. The one sexed was a male with no secondary sexual characteristics. It was released into Lake Havasu near Office Cove.

### 4. Stocking and tag reconciliation (2005–2009)
- **January 2005:** 62 adult razorback suckers were moved from High Levee Pond to DU Pond 1 at Imperial NWR. No original data were available to the author.
- **29 November 2007:** 1,002 razorback suckers (mean TL 240 mm) were stocked into Emerald Canyon golf course ponds (Parker), sourced from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]].
- **26 February 2008:** 1,000 razorback suckers (mean TL 200 mm) were stocked into Needles (Riverside) golf course ponds, also from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery.
- **October 2008:** One tagged razorback sucker, recorded in the database under a tag number that may be a typo (424D2D6A7A versus 424D206A7A), was moved from High Levee Pond to Office Cove. The correspondent noted that the tag and field sheet were not fully reconciled.
- Some tag records had been removed ("kicked out") from the online database. The correspondent offered to supply that data separately, since it was needed for analysis.

### 5. Data gaps and quality issues
- Original field sheets were missing for several records, including the October 2008 release and the 2005 data.
- Stocking slips and memos were missing for some events, so totals were reconstructed from memos and excel files.
- Some stocking entries (e.g., 2007 and 2008) may be corrected in later versions of the report.
