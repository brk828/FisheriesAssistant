# Alabama Sturgeon (Scaphirhynchus suttkusi) - Proposed Endangered Listing with Critical Habitat

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) regulatory notice, not a primary scientific field study, concerning the proposed listing of the [[Alabama Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*) as an endangered species with critical habitat under the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]]. The core scientific issue driving this notice is substantial disagreement among scientists over whether the species still survives in the [[Mobile River System]]. The Service extended the public comment period (originally proposed June 15, 1993; 58 FR 33148) multiple times — most recently to October 17, 1994 — specifically to solicit scientific evidence on the species' continued existence, rather than on morphological or ecological characterization per se. A final determination was due no later than December 15, 1994.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide morphological data (e.g., meristics, body proportions, coloration) for *Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*. As a member of the family [[Acipenseridae]] and genus *Scaphirhynchus*, the [[Alabama Sturgeon]] is understood taxonomically to be closely related to the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*) and [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus albus*), sharing the genus's characteristic elongated, depressed rostrum and bony scutes typical of small river sturgeons — though no such descriptive data is present in this text. The document does confirm that the Service formally recognized *Scaphirhynchus suttkusi* as a valid, distinct species for purposes of the Act (59 FR 31972), resolving prior taxonomic ambiguity relative to congeners.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The species is associated with the [[Mobile River System]], including the [[Alabama River]], within the Gulf of Mexico drainage of the southeastern United States.
- **Critical Habitat**: The proposed rule included designation of critical habitat concurrent with the endangered listing proposal, though specific boundaries are not detailed in this notice.
- **Survey Effort**: The Service planned additional field sampling surveys on portions of the Alabama River where sturgeon might remain, to be conducted through early November 1994 (or later, contingent on river conditions), directly targeting evidence of continued occupancy.
- **Population Status**: The central ecological/conservation question addressed is whether viable populations persist at all, indicating the species was suspected to be at or near extinction at the time of writing.

## Key Findings

- **Proposed Listing**: The Service proposed endangered status with critical habitat for the [[Alabama Sturgeon]] on June 15, 1993 (58 FR 33148), under Section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Statutory Timeline**: Section 4(b)(6) of the Act normally requires a final listing determination within 12 months of proposal.
- **Scientific Disagreement Trigger**: Under Section 4(b)(6)(B) (50 CFR 424.17(a)(iv)), the Service invoked a 6-month extension due to "substantial disagreement among scientists knowledgeable about the species" regarding the sufficiency/accuracy of data on whether the species still exists.
- **Extension Timeline**:
  - June 21, 1994 (59 FR 31970): Deadline extended to December 15, 1994; comment period reopened through September 15, 1994.
  - September 15, 1994: Comment period further extended 30 days, to October 17, 1994, specifically to gather scientific input on: (1) likelihood the species still exists, and (2) what evidence would substantiate a claim of extinction.
- **Taxonomic Validation**: The Service determined that *Scaphirhynchus suttkusi* warrants recognition as a valid species distinct from other *Scaphirhynchus* taxa (59 FR 31972).
- **Administrative Process**: All public comments (oral or written) submitted since June 15, 1993, were to be considered in the final decision, due no later than December 15, 1994.
- **Concurrent Field Work**: Independent of the comment period, the Service planned additional river sampling surveys as a direct empirical check on species persistence, reflecting reliance on both expert testimony and field verification for the final determination.
- **Regulatory Authority**: Action taken under 16 U.S.C. 1531–1544; signed by Mollie H. Beattie, Director, Fish and Wildlife Service (dated September 12, 1994).
