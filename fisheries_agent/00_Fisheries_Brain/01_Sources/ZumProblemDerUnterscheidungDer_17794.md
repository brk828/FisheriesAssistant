# Distinguishing the Genera Roloffia and Aphyosemion

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1974 article by [[Helmut Grimm]] (University of Hamburg) re-examines the genus [[Roloffia]], which [[Stenholt Clausen]] separated from [[Aphyosemion]] in 1966. Grimm tests Clausen's criteria using new chromosome counts and cephalic lateral line anatomy across the now nearly complete set of known [[Roloffia]] species. The data support Clausen's view: the two genera differ in chromosome number range and in the shape of the frontal sensory organ, and [[Roloffia]] forms a cohesive group. The article also reports a nomenclatural dispute over the type species of [[Callopanchax]], which was pending before the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature.

## Background & Description

[[Roloffia]] was erected in 1966 on fine differences in the cephalic lateral line system and on chromosome numbers. Its species occur in tropical rainforest waters, mainly in Guinea, Sierra Leone and Liberia. The remaining [[Aphyosemion]] occur in comparable habitats from Nigeria to about Congo. [[Aphyosemion Spurelli]] is the one exception, found within the [[Roloffia]] range. The Dahomey Savanna is cited as the ecological barrier separating the two distributions.

Most recent [[Roloffia]] material derives from the collecting work of [[Ernst Roloff]]. Several long-known species were re-imported (R. maeseni, R. melanteron). Others were newly described, often with [[Ladiges]], including R. brueningi, R. chaytori, R. monroviae and R. viridis. Grimm therefore considered it likely that nearly all species of the genus were now sampled.

Methods: gill epithelial chromosomes and, in males, first meiotic division chromosomes of spermatogenesis were stained with orcein-acetic acid. Sensory anatomy was examined on the head neuromast groups, with lupe inspection and color staining to highlight the lateral line organ. Figures show the cephalic lateral line organ of [[Aphyosemion Sjoestedti]], [[Roloffia Occidentalis]], [[Roloffia Viridis]], [[Roloffia Maeseni]] and [[Roloffia Melanteron]], plus chromosome sets of R. viridis, R. maeseni and R. "caldal" (scale bars 1 mm and 10 µm).

Teleost sensory context: toothcarps lack a distinct lateral line along the flanks. Neuromasts (pressure-wave receptors) sit in open pits scattered over the body, but cluster on the head in canal-like groups on each side, in front of and behind the eye. Each neuromast is flanked by a pair of opposing lobes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The article is primarily taxonomic and gives no field ecology, habitat measurements or population data. Its ecological content is limited to:

- **Biogeographic barrier:** the Dahomey Savanna separates the rainforest distributions of [[Roloffia]] (Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia) from [[Aphyosemion]] (Nigeria to Congo).
- **Habitat:** both genera occupy tropical rainforest waters.
- **Overlap:** [[Aphyosemion Spurelli]] occurs in the [[Roloffia]] area, so geography alone does not separate the genera.

## Key Findings

**1. Chromosome number ranges differ between the genera.**
- [[Roloffia]] has n = 20 to 23.
- [[Aphyosemion]] has n = 9 to 20 (data from [[Scheel]] and Grimm 1972).
- Both genera share n = 20 (an overlap zone), but [[Roloffia]] chromosome numbers cluster more tightly at the high end.

**2. Chromosome counts across the 17 listed [[Roloffia]] entries.**
- Counts range from n = 20 to 23.
- The list includes 14 named species, plus R. viridis, the undescribed "Caldal", and the subspecies R. roloffi hastingsi.
- Five species have n = 21: R. viridis, R. maeseni, R. liberiensis, R. calabarica and "Caldal".
- R. monroviae, R. melanteron, R. occidentalis and R. toddi have n = 23.
- R. petersi has n = 20.
- Where Scheel's independent counts appear in parentheses, they mostly agree. For R. guineensis, Scheel reports n = 19 against Grimm's 20.

**3. Chromosome morphology varies widely, even at equal numbers.**
- All n = 21 species carry a small X-shaped chromosome pair.
- The number of large X-shaped elements differs: R. viridis has 10, R. maeseni has 4, and the others have none.
- Grimm notes this is being studied with respect to karyotype evolution.

**4. The frontal lateral line organ separates the genera.**
- In [[Aphyosemion]], the frontal neuromast pair is spaced apart and four lobes are present, giving a trapezoid frontal organ ("open").
- In [[Roloffia]], the frontal neuromasts sit close together, and the two anterior lobes fuse into one, leaving three lobes and a triangular frontal organ ("closed").
- The organ shape is visible under a hand lens and is more contrasty with staining.

**5. Clausen's criteria hold.**
- All examined [[Roloffia]] species have the closed triangular frontal organ, which does not occur in [[Aphyosemion]].
- Characters vary within [[Roloffia]], but the group remains cohesive and clearly distinct from [[Aphyosemion]].

**6. Nomenclatural dispute.**
- [[Myers]] objects to the name Roloffia. He argues that Clausen mistook [[Aphyosemion Sjoestedti]], chosen as type of subgenus [[Callopanchax]] (Myers 1933), for the fish later described as [[Roloffia Occidentalis]], which Clausen made the type of Roloffia.
- Myers asks that R. occidentalis be made the type of Callopanchax instead.
- The decision rests with the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature in London, which was still deliberating at publication.
