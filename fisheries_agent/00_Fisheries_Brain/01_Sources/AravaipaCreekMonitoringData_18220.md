# Aravaipa Creek Native Fish and Habitat Monitoring, November 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This December 1992 interagency memorandum from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD) transmits results of a three-day survey of [[Aravaipa Creek]] (November 10–12, 1992). The survey covered nine stations: three upstream of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] (WA), three inside the WA, and three downstream. Crews from AZGFD, the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) and [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC) collected fish and habitat data. Habitat-only data were collected at the downstream "Sycamore Tree" station because time was short. The memo reports fish counts, habitat composition, and water and weather conditions. Its tables are partly degraded by OCR, so this note cites only the figures clearly stated in the text.

## Background & Description

The memo is a field-data transmittal from Rob Bettaso, Native Fish Field Projects Coordinator, AZGFD, to Dave Gori (TNC) and Jeff Simms (BLM). It is part of ongoing native fish monitoring in the creek. The survey targets native and sensitive cyprinids and catostomids, several of which are named in the field tables.

**Sampling methods**
- **Seining:** Mainstream channels were seined over 200 m with a 6-ft seine of 1/8-inch mesh, and with a 15-ft seine of 1/4-inch mesh when needed.
- **Dipnetting:** Side channels and margins were sampled with 1/8-inch mesh dipnets.
- **[[Electrofishing]]:** Backpack electroshockers were run at 150–250 volts and 75–150 pulses per second. Dipnets or a 1/4-inch seine captured stunned fish. Shocker data represent the lower 100 m of each reach.
- **Reporting units:** Seine catch is reported as fish per 10 m² (Sn). Shocker catch is reported as fish per minute of shocking time (Sk). CPUE values are given for each species and age class.
- **[[Habitat Assessment]]:** Crews recorded habitat type (run, riffle, pool, backwater), length, width, depth, and substrate (boulder, cobble, pebble, gravel, sand, silt) as estimated percentages.

**Collectors:** Rob Bettaso, Dave Dorum, Dave Weedman and Adele Fegley (AZGFD); Jeff Simms and Ben Robles (BLM); Dave Gori, Ed Brunson and Murray Fick (TNC).

**Environmental conditions**
- Weather was typical for November: clear, calm and cool.
- Air temperature ranged from 5 °C in the morning to about 22 °C in the afternoon.
- Water was clear, at 10–18 °C.

**Wildlife observations:** 7 white-tailed deer, 3 coati-mundi, two bands of javelina, and two black hawks on the east end.

**Breeding condition:** Breeding condition was frequently observed in [[Loach Minnow]].

## Key Findings

- **Species present:** Tables cover at least seven native fish taxa: [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*) and [[Clarki Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*). Speckled Dace, Longfin Dace and Sonora Sucker appear in most tables. Spikedace, Loach Minnow and Roundtail Chub appear in fewer stations.
- **Reproduction:** Loach Minnow were frequently in breeding condition during this November survey.
- **Habitat:** Runs and riffles dominated nearly all stations. Pools were rare in the WA and at the west-end stations. Substrates were mainly cobble, gravel and sand, with boulder at several reaches.
- **Station counts:** Guest House (735) and Chimney Rock (720) on the east end had the highest reported catches. Painted Cave (138) had the lowest.
- **Sampling gaps:** Fish sampling was not completed at the Sycamore Tree station because of time limits. Habitat data only were collected there.
- **Management features:** The memo refers to an exclosure fence near the east-end Turkey Creek reach and a proposed barrier in front of the Guest House. Both are relevant to future management of native fish.
- **Data quality caveat:** Many tables are partly unreadable in the scanned copy. Species-level CPUE and percent-of-total values should be verified against the original field tables before quantitative use.
