# Granite Reef Aqueduct Fishery Investigation: 1986 Progress Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation progress report presents preliminary first-year findings from a fishery investigation of the [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]], a concrete-lined canal of the [[Central Arizona Project]] that carries Colorado River water from [[Lake Havasu]] toward central Arizona. The study was run with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]]. Field work in 1986 collected over 2,000 fish representing ten species, and the canal has been colonized by at least ten species. Populations of [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Striped Bass]] are established in the low-velocity Reach 1, while [[Threadfin Shad]] dominate numbers and biomass throughout. Most fish appear healthy with high condition factors. The report recommends at least two more years of sampling and examines [[Entrainment]] as the main pathway by which fish enter the system.

## Background & Description

The Central Arizona Project was authorized in 1968. Reclamation committed in the [[Lake Havasu]] pumping plant Environmental Impact Statement to monitor fish losses from Lake Havasu. That commitment was expanded to examine how Colorado River fish fauna are imported into central Arizona through the canal. The canal investigation is one of three related efforts, alongside work at Lake Havasu and [[Lake Pleasant]]. Pre-operational Lake Havasu studies ran from 1983 to 1985. The Lake Havasu fishery report was not completed, which limited insight into species availability, susceptibility, and body condition in the forebay.

**Canal Physical Setup**

- **Capacity:** 3,000 cfs, fed by the [[Lake Havasu Pumping Plant]] through about 7 miles of tunnel under the Buckskin Mountains.
- **Reach 1:** The first 5,550 feet have a 24-foot bottom width and a maximum depth of 18 feet. The canal then widens to an 80-foot bottom for 17 miles, designed to store about 4,000 acre-feet. This ponded section has the lowest water velocities in the system and was selected as the primary sampling site.
- **Reach 2:** Runs about 35 miles from the [[Bouse Hills Pumping Plant]] to the [[Little Harquahala Pumping Plant]] forebay.
- **Reach 3:** Lies downstream of Little Harquahala. Other reaches share a 24-foot bottom, roughly 18-foot depth, and 1:2 side slopes. Maximum velocity under full operation would exceed 3 feet per second.

**Sampling Methods**

Canals are very difficult to sample because of high velocities, steep banks, depth, and poor access. The team tested [[Electrofishing]] and [[Gill Netting]] under full and partial canal conditions and combined them, using gill nets as blocking nets. A custom "equipment lift" built from tubular steel and a wooden platform, operated by an automobile winch, lowered boats and equipment to the canal. A semiannual schedule focused on spring and fall. Reach 1 was sampled June 5-6 and October 1-3, 1986; Reach 3 on October 21-23, 1986. Fish were measured to the nearest millimeter and weighed to the nearest gram (or converted from pounds for fish over one kilogram), then fin-clipped where appropriate and released.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish Entry and Dispersal**

At least ten species have entered the system, presumably by [[Entrainment]] at the pumping plants. Entrainment life stage is unknown, but early life stages are assumed to pass pumping plants more successfully. Reach 1 functioned as a large rearing pond after filling, with little pumping early on, which allowed plankton communities to develop before juvenile fish arrived.

**Predation and Cover**

Predation is now a major factor in Reach 1. Largemouth bass and striped bass likely regulate numbers of other species, especially threadfin shad and sunfish. Prey species have little protective cover. Pumping-plant penstocks provide the only overhead cover in the canal, and sunfish were observed in large numbers inside the intakes during SCUBA inspections. Fish may be trapped there during pump downtime and then struck by startup velocities, suggesting adult sunfish could be more vulnerable to entrainment than mid-channel species.

**Water Quality and Dissolved Oxygen**

During the October 1986 dewatering, the Little Harquahala forebay developed oxygen-depleted conditions. Several decimeters of fine organic sediment had accumulated, releasing hydrogen sulfide when disturbed, and fish were concentrated there. Hundreds of fish were seen near the surface, and [[Threadfin Shad]] were the first to become stressed and die. A [[Hydrolab Surveyor II]] profile on October 23, 1986, recorded dissolved oxygen falling from 1.27 mg/L at the surface to 0.57 mg/L at 5.0 m, with temperatures of 18.75-19.21 °C and conductivity near 880 µS/cm. This was the first and only time [[Dissolved Oxygen]] or another water quality parameter appeared to limit fish.

**Dewatering and Mortality**

Reach 3 was totally dewatered, and all fish in the downstream section draining into the Little Harquahala forebay died. The forebay was pumped dry over three weeks, and because of slow drawdown, high fish density, and limited manpower, not all trapped fish could be worked up.

## Key Findings

**Species Collected and Community Composition**

- Over 2,000 fish of ten species were collected on four field efforts (May 5-6, June 5-6, October 1-3, and October 21-23, 1986). Reach 3 forebay sampling alone yielded 1,217 fish.
- Species: [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], Green Sunfish, Redear Sunfish, Black Crappie, [[Striped Bass]], [[Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- Largemouth and striped bass make up roughly 75% of the non-shad community in Reach 1, versus under 7% in Reach 3.
- Sunfish (bluegill, green sunfish, redear sunfish) make up 88% of the Reach 3 sample but under 10% of Reach 1.
- No striped bass were found in Reach 3, though over 1,200 fish were handled there. The report suggests the species is present only in very low numbers, possibly because longer [[Detention Time]] in Reach 1 lets entrained eggs and larvae develop to a less vulnerable stage before reaching Bouse Hills.
- Only three [[Channel Catfish]] were collected in the entire effort.
- Three [[Razorback Sucker]] (about 500 mm, apparently healthy) were collected, two in Reach 1 and one in Reach 3. They were sacrificed for age determination by Dr. Paul Marsh at Arizona State University.

**Sampling Efficiency**

Sampling success ranked from best to worst:

1. Gill netting and seining during water outages.
2. Blocking nets with electrofishing during low water.
3. Electrofishing during low water.
4. Gill netting during low water.
5. Gill netting during high water.
6. Electrofishing during high water.
7. Fyke nets.

Electrofishing in Reach 1 (June 6) took 291 fish in 1,676 seconds (5.76 seconds per fish). The October Reach 1 electrofishing effort took 597 fish in 20,960 seconds (35.11 seconds per fish) and was biased toward non-shad species. Gill nets for under 180 mm [[Largemouth Bass]] were ineffective. Electrofishing may also be biased toward larger fish and can be masked by dense shad.

**Population Estimates (Reach 1, October 1-3, 1986)**

Estimates used [[Petersen Mark-Recapture]] and were expanded from subsampled upper and lower sections to the full reach. The table below gives the estimate ± 95% confidence limit and the density or biomass per square meter (Table 7). Data are based on 277.43 surface acres.

| Species | Estimate (± 95% CL) |
|---|---|
| Largemouth bass | 3,622 ± 1,336 |
| Bluegill | 634 ± 451 |
| Striped bass | 7,833 ± 4,174 |
| Carp | 2,007 ± 2,751 |

Biomass for these species suggests total fish biomass may exceed 8 g/m² of surface area, which is within the range measured in similar southern California canals (Beaty 1984). The report calls this value "highly conservative" given other species and tens of thousands of shad.

**Physical Condition and Growth**

- All species appeared healthy, with external scars, infections, and parasites rare.
- Condition factors were high compared with other local waters. For example, Reach 1 largemouth bass averaged K = 1.40, versus 1.21 at Lake Havasu and 1.26 at Alamo Lake (1982-83). Reach 1 bluegill averaged 2.88, versus 2.01 at Lake Havasu.
- Most fish fell into the third year class or younger, consistent with the canal having held water for only three years. The report suggests most fish enter as early life stages. Exceptions were a few bluegills over 200 mm and the three razorback suckers.
- Food availability does not appear limiting.

**Comparison with Coachella Canal**

Reclamation's earlier study of the Coachella Canal, which is also Colorado River-fed (2,500 cfs), found 97% channel catfish among 3,100 fish collected after draining. The report expects Granite Reef's fishery to shift toward a Coachella-like, river-adapted community as velocities rise. Reach 1 velocities should stay below 1.5 ft/s and remain a sanctuary for weaker swimmers.

**Recommendations**

- Continue sampling for at least two more years, maximizing collection during canal water outages and otherwise continuing fall and spring sampling.
- Expand the study to other reaches in fall 1987.
- Analyze egg and larval data collected at Havasu and Bouse Hills pumping plants (begun April 1987) to assess early-life entrainment susceptibility and whether striped bass spawn in Reach 1.
- Run experimental netting at the downstream outlets of the Lake Havasu and Bouse Hills pumping plants to measure adult fish passage.
- Report on these efforts at the end of calendar 1987.
