# Aravaipa Creek Fish Survey, April and October-November 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of two email messages from [[Arizona State University]] fish researchers, dated 13 April 1998 and 2 November 1998. They report seine-based fish counts from [[Aravaipa Creek]] in Arizona. The April 1998 survey covered 10-12 April and listed counts at nine or more sites, with a site-by-site breakdown. The autumn survey (October to early November 1998) ran from the upper end of the creek to a lowermost site, with approximate sample lengths given in metres for each reach. The main points are the dominance of a few native taxa (*Meda*, *Agosia*, *Pantosteus*, *Tiaroga*), the presence of non-native taxa including [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella*) and [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia*) at several sites, and the high counts of *Agosia* at the lower reaches.

## Background & Description

The records cover a fish community that includes the following taxa, as named in the source (genus-level only; no species epithets are given):

- **Native or likely native:** *Meda*, *Tiaroga*, *Agosia*, *Gila*, *Pantosteus*, *Catostomus*, *Rhinichthys*
- **Non-native (labelled "exotics" in the text):** *Cyprinella* (red shiner), *Gambusia*, *Pimephales*, *Lepomis*, *Ameiurus*

Sampling was done with [[Seine Sampling]] gear. The autumn email states that the sample length at each site is given in metres. Specimens from non-native taxa were preserved, except those from one site that the source does not name clearly. The field crew included [[Peter Unmack]], [[W.L. Minckley]] and [[Paul C. Marsh]], with copies to researchers at the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), and the [[Forest Service]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area is the lower and middle reaches of Aravaipa Creek, which flows into the [[Gila River Basin]]. Site names in the source include:

- TNC House
- Chimney Rock
- Turkey Creek confluence
- Parsons Canyon confluence
- Booger Canyon confluence
- Virgus Canyon region
- Woods Ranch (trail end)
- Whiters Ranch
- Lowermost site

The source lists sites from upstream to downstream. Upstream sites contain mostly native taxa. Non-native taxa are more common downstream and in the lowest reach. The text says red shiners were recorded as far upstream as Chimney Rock. The source does not give water chemistry, flow, temperature, habitat measurements or substrate data.

## Key Findings

**April 1998 survey (10-12 April)**

- **TNC House:** *Meda* 86, *Tiaroga* 10, *Agosia* 100, *Gila* 41 (mostly juveniles), *Pantosteus* 3, *Catostomus* 1 (as written in the OCR text, these counts may need checking).
- **Chimney Rock:** *Meda* 91, *Agosia* 128, *Gila* 15, *Tiaroga* 3, *Pantosteus* 1.
- **Turkey Creek confluence:** *Meda* 94, *Agosia* 1, *Gila* 7, *Tiaroga* 1.
- **Parsons Canyon:** *Pantosteus* 12, *Catostomus* 2, *Gila* 3, *Meda* 1.
- **Horse Camp:** *Gila* 19, *Pantosteus* 18, *Catostomus* 10, *Agosia* 2, *Lepomis* 3.
- **Painted Cave:** *Pantosteus* 12, *Catostomus* 13, *Gila* 2, *Cyprinella* 3, *Lepomis* 1.
- **Fred Wood's Ranch:** *Meda* 1, *Agosia* 3, *Gila* 2, *Pantosteus* 2, *Catostomus* 2.
- **White's Ranch:** no fish recorded.
- **"Sycamore Tree" site:** *Agosia* 149, *Catostomus* 5, *Cyprinella* 33, *Lepomis* 12, *Gambusia* 6, *Pimephales* 1.

**October to early November 1998 survey (approximate sample lengths in parentheses)**

- **TNC House (about 100 m):** *Agosia* 117, *Meda* 86, *Rhinichthys* 20, *Pantosteus* 4, *Gila* 7, *Catostomus* 8, *Tiaroga* 12.
- **Chimney Rock (about 150 m):** *Agosia* 158, *Meda* 144, *Rhinichthys* 16, *Pantosteus* 38, *Tiaroga* 22, *Catostomus* 19, *Gila* 7, *Cyprinella* 1.
- **Turkey Creek confluence (about 150 m):** *Agosia* 38, *Catostomus* 9, *Meda* 18 (values partly garbled in OCR).
- **Parsons Canyon confluence (about 100 m):** *Meda* 66, *Pantosteus* 4, *Tiaroga* 1, *Gila* 9.
- **Booger Canyon confluence (about 100 m):** *Agosia* 9, *Catostomus* 3, *Gila* 6, *Pantosteus* 10, *Tiaroga* 3.
- **Virgus Canyon region (about 100 m):** *Agosia* 39 (approximate), *Gila* 5, *Meda* 1, *Pantosteus* 20, *Tiaroga* 3, *Ameiurus* 1, *Catostomus* 3, *Lepomis* 6 (OCR is partly garbled).
- **Woods Ranch, trail end (about 200-250 m):** *Agosia* 663, *Pantosteus* 107, *Tiaroga* 112, *Catostomus* 22, *Gila* 8, *Cyprinella* 2.
- **Whiters Ranch (about 100 m):** *Agosia* 321, *Pantosteus* 19, *Tiaroga* 29, *Catostomus* 46, *Cyprinella* 13, *Gila* 3, *Ameiurus* 1.
- **Lowermost site (about 100 m):** *Agosia* 285, *Gambusia* 203, *Pantosteus* 14, *Tiaroga* 36, *Catostomus* 9, *Lepomis* 2.

**Overall observations**

- *Agosia* is the most numerous taxon in the autumn survey, with the highest counts at Woods Ranch (663) and Whiters Ranch (321).
- Non-native taxa (*Cyprinella*, *Gambusia*, *Lepomis*, *Pimephales*, *Ameiurus*) are most numerous at the lower or sycamore-tree sites. *Gambusia* reaches 203 at the lowermost site.
- The source describes these records as a fish survey and makes no quantitative claims about population trends. The counts are single-pass seine catches and should not be read as density estimates.
