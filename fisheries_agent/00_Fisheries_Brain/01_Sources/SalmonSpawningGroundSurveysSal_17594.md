# Salmon Spawning Ground Surveys, Salmon River Drainage (1967)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #anadromous-fish #population-assessment

## Executive Summary

This annual progress report documents chinook salmon spawning ground surveys conducted in the [[Salmon River]] drainage, Idaho, from August 1, 1966 to September 30, 1966 (reported in 1967-1968). The survey recorded 7,546 chinook salmon redds across the drainage, representing a 4.6% increase from 1966 (7,311 redds). Spring chinook counts at downstream dams ([[Ice Harbor Dam]], [[McNary Dam]], [[Bonneville Dam]]) decreased 18.7-28.4%, while summer chinook counts increased substantially (up 80.6% at Ice Harbor Dam). Unusual water conditions—turbid runoff lasting until early July—delayed the spring fishing season and necessitated early closure of summer chinook harvest to ensure adequate spawning escapement. Age-class composition analysis showed age class 52 fish dominated spring chinook spawning populations, while summer chinook showed more variable age structure across different tributaries.

## Background & Description

The [[Idaho Fish and Game Department]] has conducted annual chinook salmon redd trend counts in the [[Salmon River]] drainage since 1951 as part of Federal Aid to Fish Restoration Project F-49-R-6. Surveys distinguish between spring-run and summer-run [[Chinook Salmon]] based on arrival timing, spawning timing, and redd condition. The majority of counts are conducted from low-flying aircraft, with ground counts used where conditions (redd concentrations, overhanging brush) make aerial observation difficult. Some streams are surveyed by small raft. Live fish counts employ two observers with wet suits, face masks, snorkels, and hand tallies floating downstream near opposite banks. Fork length measurements and sex determinations from spawned-out adults provide age-class composition and sex ratio data. Age classes are categorized by length: for spring chinook, class 32 (<24 inches), class 49 (24-31 inches), and class 59 (>31 inches); for summer chinook, class 32 (<25 inches), class 42 (25-34 inches), and class 55+ (≥34 inches).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 1967 salmon run arrived approximately two weeks early, with first fish caught at the mouth of [[Lemhi River]] on May 15. Unprecedented turbid water conditions beginning May 16 rendered an 80-mile stretch of the [[Salmon River]] around Salmon City unfishable until early July, significantly reducing spring chinook harvest during the normal fishing period. This necessitated regulatory adjustments: the spring chinook season was extended to July 31 in tributaries (normally closing July 15) to allow harvest without impacting the smaller summer chinook run, while the main [[Salmon River]] and [[Middle Fork]] closed July 25 to protect summer chinook spawning escapement. Oregon and Washington also closed sport and commercial summer chinook fishing to increase spawning escapement. Biologists suggest approximately 5,000 late-running spring chinook may have been counted as summer-run fish at dams, potentially explaining discrepancies between dam counts and redd surveys (spring chinook dam counts decreased but redd counts increased; summer chinook showed the opposite pattern).

## Key Findings

**Dam Counts (1966 to 1967 comparison):**
- Spring chinook at [[Bonneville Dam]]: 112,669 to 85,045 (-24.5%)
- Spring chinook at [[McNary Dam]]: 87,195 to 62,281 (-28.4%)
- Spring chinook at [[Ice Harbor Dam]]: 43,881 to 35,684 (-18.7%)
- Summer chinook at Bonneville Dam: 71,997 to 95,653 (+32.8%)
- Summer chinook at McNary Dam: 60,827 to 61,686 (+1.4%)
- Summer chinook at Ice Harbor Dam: 16,983 to 30,680 (+80.6%)

**Redd Counts by Drainage (1967):**
- [[South Fork]] drainage: 1,328 redds (+8.0% from 1966)
- [[Middle Fork]] drainage: 2,057 redds (-2.3%)
- [[Lemhi River]]: 804 redds (-1.8%)
- [[East Fork]] drainage: 880 redds (+9.2%)
- Main [[Salmon River]]: 1,308 redds (+20.1%)
- Total drainage: 7,546 redds (+4.6%)

**Spring vs. Summer Chinook Spawning Areas:**
- Spring-run areas: 4,552 redds (+7.7% from 4,226 in 1966)
- Summer-run areas: 2,122 redds (+10.1% from 1,926 in 1966)

**Age-Class Composition (Spring Chinook):**
- Age class 52 dominated across all surveyed streams (63-90% of samples)
- [[Lemhi River]]: 70% class 52, 30% class 49 (n=170)
- [[Marsh Creek]] drainage: 88% class 52 (n=141)
- Upper [[Salmon River]]: 85% class 52 (n=248)
- [[Bear Valley Creek]]: 89% class 52 (n=405)
- [[Elk Creek]]: 94% class 52 (n=397)

**Age-Class Composition (Summer Chinook):**
- More variable than spring chinook; most streams showed class 49 dominance
- [[Johnson Creek]]: 56% class 49, 33% class 52 (n=352)
- [[South Fork Salmon River]]: 53% class 49, 18% class 52 (n=372)
- Lower [[Big Creek]]: 59% class 55+ fish (n=64), unusual pattern

**Sex Ratios:**
- Spring chinook: 58% female overall (range 50-70% across streams)
- Summer chinook: 46% female overall ([[Johnson Creek]] 49%, [[South Fork]] 55%)
- Consistent with historical patterns (1962-1966)

**Live Fish Counts ([[Bear Valley Creek]]):**
- Correlation between [[Ice Harbor Dam]] spring chinook counts and live fish counts in lower Bear Valley Creek:
  - 1965: 12,174 dam count, 146 live fish, 152 redds
  - 1966: 42,795 dam count, 428 live fish, 275 redds
  - 1967: 35,660 dam count, 376 live fish, 206 redds

**Notable Stream-Specific Counts:**
- [[Yankee Fork]]: 598 redds (454 in 1966)
- [[Valley Creek]]: 332 redds (403 in 1966)
- [[Alturas Lake Creek]]: 74 redds (119 in 1966)
- [[Camas Creek]]: 256 redds (212 in 1966)
- [[Pahsimeroi River]]: 99 redds (113 in 1966)

The report recommends continued collection of redd count, age-class composition, and sex ratio data as essential for effective management of chinook salmon runs in the [[Salmon River]] drainage.
