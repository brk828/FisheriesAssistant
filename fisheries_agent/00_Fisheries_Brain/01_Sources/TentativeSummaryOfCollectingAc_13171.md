# Tentative Summary of Collecting Activities Spring 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #field-survey

## Executive Summary

This field report documents fish collecting activities conducted by S.P. Vives and five students across multiple sites in southeastern Arizona during April 1-2, 1989. The survey targeted native and introduced fish species at [[San Bernardino Wildlife Refuge]] and surrounding creek systems. Collections yielded eight fish species including the native [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), and introduced [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). Water temperatures ranged from 9.5°C to 32°C across sites. Specimens were preserved, frozen for genetic analysis, or returned alive depending on species and conservation status.

## Background & Description

The collecting expedition focused on aquatic habitats in southeastern Arizona, including springs, ponds, and creek systems. Primary collection methods included 6-foot and 15-foot seines with 0.25-inch mesh, dipnets, and kick-seining techniques. Sites surveyed included [[Black Draw]], [[Tule Spring]], [[Upland Spring]], [[Double PhD Pond]], [[Cave Creek]], [[Rucker Canyon]], [[Leslie Creek]], and [[Turkey Creek]]. Water conditions varied from clear swift streams to turbid ponds. Substrate types ranged from silt and sand to gravel, cobble, and rubble. Riparian vegetation included Typha, seep willow, watercress, sage, and various grasses.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[San Bernardino Wildlife Refuge]] supports populations of the endangered [[Yaqui Topminnow]], with distinct morphological variations observed among spring populations. At [[Upland Spring]] (32°C), female topminnows exhibited darker "India-ink-like" perianal markings compared to other populations. [[Tule Spring]] (16°C) topminnows showed more pigment and darker lateral line stripes than those from [[Black Draw]] (13°C). The refuge manager Ron Emmert reported recent construction of two new ponds and plans for additional habitat modifications, including damming [[Black Draw]] to recreate ciénega-like conditions. A temporary dirt dam was already in place 15-20 meters upstream from the border fence, with plans for permanent rock structure installation using Youth Conservation Corps assistance.

[[Double PhD Pond]] contained large numbers of [[Western Mosquitofish]] and abundant bullfrog (*Rana catesbiana*) tadpoles. The northwest pond (20°C, turbid) proved difficult to seine due to brush, while the southeast pond (19-20°C) appeared less productive and "sterile." Identification challenges arose with poeciliids, as some specimens initially appeared "Gambusia-like" based on caudal fin spots and melanophores under the eye, requiring tank verification that revealed approximately 50:50 mix of [[Poeciliopsis]] and [[Gambusia]].

Creek systems showed varying fish assemblages. [[Leslie Creek]] (15°C, cloudy water) yielded 55 [[Gila Chub]] and 6 [[Longfin Dace]], with the latter showing heavy black spot infestation compared to Gila. [[Rucker Canyon]] (9.5°C) supported 21 [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*) and 66 [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), plus 2 [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*). Despite 50 seine hauls at [[Cave Creek]] [[Herb Martyr Dam]] area (13°C), no [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) were collected, only 3 trout alevins.

## Key Findings

**San Bernardino Wildlife Refuge Collections (April 1, 1989):**
- [[Black Draw]] (0900-1000h, 13°C): 24 [[Gila Chub]] (10 preserved), approximately 29 poeciliids initially collected with 50:50 mix of [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis]] and [[Gambusia affinis]] after verification (10 died in transit), 2 [[Agosia chrysogaster]] preserved
- [[Double PhD Pond]] NW pond (1100-1130h, 20°C): 224 [[Gambusia affinis]] (approximately 30 preserved), abundant bullfrog tadpoles, no catfish
- [[Double PhD Pond]] SE pond (1130-1215h, 19-20°C): 1 [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis]] dipnetted, appeared less productive than NW pond
- [[Tule Spring]] (1230-1300h, 16°C): 24 [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis]] with more pigment and darker lateral line stripe, all returned alive
- [[Upland Spring]] (1305-1335h, 32°C): 25 [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis]] with distinctive darker perianal markings in females, all returned alive

**Cave Creek Area (April 1, 1989):**
- [[Herb Martyr Dam]] area (1630-1800h, 13°C): 50 seine hauls yielded only 3 [[Oncorhynchus]] alevins, no [[Rhinichthys osculus]] despite targeted effort

**Rucker Canyon (April 2, 1989):**
- First low water crossing (0730-0930h, 9.5°C): 21 [[Campostoma ornatum]] (10 frozen for T. Dowling, 9 preserved), 66 [[Pimephales promelas]] (approximately 30 frozen for T. Dowling, 30 returned alive, 13 preserved), 2 [[Oncorhynchus mykiss]] preserved

**Leslie Creek (April 2, 1989):**
- Bridge crossing (1100-1130h, 15°C): 55 [[Gila purpurea]] (5 preserved), 6 [[Agosia chrysogaster]] with much heavier black spot infestation than Gila, no topminnows observed

**Turkey Creek (April 2, 1989):**
- First camp area (1245-1330h, 13°C): 3 [[Agosia chrysogaster]] returned, 2 six-inch [[Oncorhynchus mykiss]] observed

**Management and Safety Notes:**
- Microwave motion sensors installed along road paralleling [[Black Draw]] due to recent illegal border crossing activity
- Submerged metal tractor parts buried in silt bottom of [[Double PhD Pond]] SE pond pose safety hazard
- Preferred access to [[Black Draw]] via border fence approach from north
- Future USFWS plans include damming [[Black Draw]] to recreate ciénega conditions
