# Algae-Grazing Minnows, Piscivorous Bass, and the Distribution of Attached Algae in a Small Prairie-Margin Stream

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines how the algae-grazing minnow [[Campostoma anomalum]] (the [[Stoneroller]]) and piscivorous [[Micropterus|Bass]] species interact to shape pool-to-pool variation in [[Attached Algae]] in [[Brier Creek]], a small, free-flowing stream in [[Marshall County Oklahoma]]. Surveys during a low-flow period in November 1982 showed that bass and Campostoma were spatially segregated among pools. Pools with bass held dense [[Filamentous Green Algae]] mats, while pools with Campostoma were largely barren. Transplant and tethering experiments showed that Campostoma can rapidly strip algae and that they avoid bass at short range. The authors argue that this predator-grazer interaction is an important driver of spatial variation in attached algae in small North American streams.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** Brier Creek is a clear stream flowing over limestone bedrock and gravel through a tree-lined channel in pastureland. The study reach was 1 km long, with fourteen pools and twelve riffles. It was sampled at a discharge of 0.012 m³ s⁻¹, at which all pools remained connected. The reach had been free-flowing throughout the year in all but one of the previous twelve years.

**Focal taxa.**
- *Campostoma anomalum* is a widespread, abundant minnow of central and eastern US streams. Its gut contents are over 95% algae, mainly diatoms and filamentous algae.
- Bass ([[Largemouth Bass]], *Micropterus salmoides*, and [[Spotted Bass]], *M. punctulatus*) prey on minnows. Largemouth bass become totally piscivorous at about 80–100 mm length.

**Algal and invertebrate cover.** Pools with luxuriant algal growth provide cover for fishes and invertebrates. Snails (*Physa* sp.) were observed on floating algal clumps, where they gained both food and protection from fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods.** Two observers conducted snorkel censuses of all pools on 8 and 19 November 1982. The underwater observer recorded species, numbers and approximate sizes, and a bank observer checked for missed fish. Only one fish, a bass, was missed underwater. Benthic algal height was measured at transects in all fourteen pools. Algal samples from six pools were examined microscopically using a [[Whipple Grid]] and the method of Jones (1968). Dry weights were expressed as [[Ash-Free Dry Weight]].

**Spatial structure.** Bass were found in deeper areas and Campostoma in shallower areas, with barren substrate and algal mats correspondingly located in shallow and deep zones. Two non-exclusive mechanisms could produce this [[Complementary Distribution]]: bass predation eliminating minnows from shared pools, or minnows actively avoiding pools with bass. The experiments support short-range avoidance but do not establish its role in habitat selection.

**Broader relevance.** Campostoma can outweigh and outnumber all other fish species combined in some streams, so their grazing may affect spatial variation in attached algae across many North American streams. The authors compare this to [[Sea Urchin Grazing]] effects on [[Kelp Bed Communities]] and to periphyton grazing by [[Snails]] and other herbivores. Algal cover also affects fish and invertebrate habitat use, consistent with [[Predator-Prey Interactions]] in structurally complex habitats.

## Key Findings

1. **Complementary distribution of bass and Campostoma.** Distributions among pools were significantly complementary on both census dates (P < 0.025 on 8 November; P < 0.05 on 19 November, Fisher's exact test). Campostoma counts were similar on both dates (r = 0.91, P < 0.001). Bass counts varied more (r = 0.50, P < 0.10). Only one pool (Pool 5) changed bass status between dates.

2. **Algal standing crops differ between pool types.**
   - *Bass pools* (Pools 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14) had lush [[Filamentous Green Algae]], mainly [[Spirogyra]] and [[Rhizoclonium]], and beds of [[Chara]] covered with epiphytes.
   - *Campostoma pools* (Pools 3, 5, 10, 13) had filamentous algae only along margins in water generally less than 3 cm deep. Chara was stripped of epiphytes and many of its branches. Substrates were bare or thinly covered with fine detritus.
   - Algal height was significantly greater in bass pools at depths of 11–20 cm, 21–40 cm and 41–60 cm (Mann-Whitney U tests, P < 0.002, 0.01 and 0.006).
   - Of 273 sites in bass pools and 87 in Campostoma pools, the Campostoma pools had more bare rock and detritus, while Chara and filamentous algae dominated bass pools.

3. **Algal composition differs.** Intact diatoms and intact filamentous green algae were proportionately more abundant in bass pools. Empty cells of both types dominated Campostoma pools (P < 0.001 for all four comparisons). Blue-green algae (*Oscillatoria* sp.) were a smaller fraction in both pool types, but intact and empty filaments were more common in Campostoma pools (P < 0.01 and P < 0.001). Unicellular greens and microscopic animals made up less than 1% of points counted (539 in bass pools, 806 in Campostoma pools).

4. **Rapid grazing removes algae (Experiment 1).** Nine cobbles and one log covered in thick filamentous mats were moved from a bass pool (Pool 9) into a Campostoma pool (Pool 10), separated by a 38 cm ledge. Campostoma began feeding within 10 minutes. Control cobbles were virtually never visited. Standing crops fell from 22.0 ± 0.3 to 6.3 ± 2.7 mg AFDW cm⁻² after 24 h. Control cobbles had no measurable algae before or after the trial.

5. **Bass deter grazing at short range (Experiment 2).** Eight transferred cobbles were placed in two groups of four, 1.3 m apart. A 300 mm largemouth bass was tethered near the center of the upstream group for 72 h, using a 0.8 m line through the lower jaw.
   - Before the bass was introduced, Campostoma grazed both groups, with more visits to the upstream group.
   - After introduction, all minnows stopped grazing initially. Within 3 minutes, they resumed on the downstream group. They never returned to the upstream cobbles.
   - Minnows swam toward the guarded cobbles but veered away within 30–50 cm of the bass.
   - By day three, the bass was acentric. Sporadic grazing occurred on the fourth guarded cobble, which was more than 50 cm from the bass.
   - After 72 h, guarded cobbles retained 15.2 ± 3.3 mg cm⁻² of algae. Unguarded cobbles 1.3 m away averaged 5.2 ± 0.7 mg cm⁻². The difference between the four guarded and four unguarded cobbles was significant (P = 0.03, Mann-Whitney U test).

6. **Mechanism.** The results indicate that Campostoma avoid bass over short distances. They do not show whether this avoidance drives habitat selection at the pool scale. The authors conclude that the bass–Campostoma interaction is "an important factor contributing to pool-to-pool variation in attached algae" and possibly in many central and eastern US streams.
