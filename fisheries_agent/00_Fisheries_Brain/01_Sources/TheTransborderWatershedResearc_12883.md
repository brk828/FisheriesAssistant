# Transborder Watershed Research Program Proposal

#watershed-management #environmental-research #transborder-ecology #riparian-systems

## Executive Summary

This 1998 proposal to the Southwest Center for Environmental Research and Policy outlines an integrative watershed research program for two transborder basins: the [[Tijuana River Watershed]] and the [[Upper San Pedro Basin]]. Both watersheds span the US-Mexico international border, experience explosive population growth, and face critical water resource sustainability challenges in ecologically-fragile semiarid ecosystems. The program aims to bridge the gap between watersheds as ecological units and as planning/administrative units by investigating dynamic interrelationships between human and natural factors as reflected in land use patterns. The research combines ecological, economic, political, and social factors using GIS-based modeling, remote sensing, field studies, and stakeholder engagement. Principal investigators from Arizona State University, San Diego State University, University of Utah, and Mexican institutions (Autonomous University of Baja California, El Colegio de la Frontera Norte, University of Sonora) coordinate the effort. Major deliverables include watershed models, water quality assessments, a web-based GIS platform, and a comprehensive atlas for the Tijuana watershed.

## Background & Description

The [[Tijuana River Watershed]] encompasses 4,500 square kilometers, with two-thirds in Mexico. For decades, raw sewage from Tijuana and Tecate has flowed into the Tijuana River and across the border into the [[Tijuana Estuary]]. The watershed represents the single greatest source of lead and other anthropogenic pollutants to the coastal ocean of southern California and Baja California. The [[Tijuana Natural Estuarine Research Reserve]] receives significant pollutant loading from urban runoff and non-point sources. A 1994 memorandum of understanding between San Diego State University and El Colegio de la Frontera Norte established a framework for transborder cooperation in GIS database development, education, scientific studies, and watershed management.

The [[Upper San Pedro Basin]] covers approximately 6,500 square kilometers, with 72% in the United States. The Nature Conservancy designated it one of 12 "Last Great Places of the Western Hemisphere" due to extraordinary ecological diversity, including over 300 bird species in the riparian corridor. The [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]] (23,000 hectares) was established in 1988. The San Pedro River flows north from headwaters near Cananea, Sonora, and is among the few Southwest rivers with unregulated flow. Elevations range from 1,100 to 2,900 meters with annual rainfall from 300 to 750 millimeters. The basin supports diverse ecosystems including Chihuahuan Desertscrub, Plains Grasslands, Semidesert Grassland, Rocky Mountain Montane Grassland, multiple riparian forest types, Southwest Interior Chaparral, Madrean Evergreen Forest, and various conifer forests.

Both watersheds face significant growth management challenges. In the Upper San Pedro, rapid suburbanization, expansion of Fort Huachuca, and tourism growth exacerbate water quantity and quality issues. Projected expansion of copper mining near Cananea increases potential for serious water quality problems. Conversion of ranches and farmlands to subdivisions results in habitat fragmentation and loss of open space.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal emphasizes that transborder watersheds present unique challenges where ecological boundaries do not align with political/administrative boundaries. Very different social, political, and economic influences occur on opposite sides of the international border, creating a significant gap between watersheds as ecological units versus planning units.

For the Tijuana watershed, research focuses on land use-ecological relationships, particularly among land use, urban runoff, non-point source pollution, and the estuarine ecosystem. Many water quality problems stem from diffuse, non-point pollution sources best addressed through watershed-scale approaches. The watershed experiences intensive industrial development associated with maquiladora (in-bond manufacturing) programs in Mexico.

For the Upper San Pedro, the [[SALSA]] (Semi-Arid Land-Surface Atmosphere) program provides existing research infrastructure focused on understanding basin-wide water balance and ecological diversity at multiple temporal scales. The riparian corridor supports exceptional faunal diversity. Key ecological concerns include groundwater and surface water decline from agricultural, municipal, and industrial (mining) activities, which constitute primary threats to riparian ecosystems. The proposal identifies [[Cottonwood-Willow Forest]] and [[Sacaton Grasslands]] as dominant plant communities requiring study of relationships between ecosystem integrity and groundwater/surface flow regimes.

Biophysical factors influencing both watersheds include soils, slope, drainage, site contamination, water availability, and plant and animal community characteristics. Institutional and socioeconomic factors include population and employment, demographic profiles, individual and community values, perceived quality of life, and land use policy for both the watershed and adjacent "shadow watershed" regions.

## Key Findings

**Central Research Question:** What economic, social, political, and ecological processes interact to generate land use patterns in the Tijuana and San Pedro watersheds, and how do these patterns and processes influence environmental conditions in the two basins?

**Tijuana River Watershed Objectives:**
1. Identify significant sources of lead in sensitive areas through GIS-based mass loading model
2. Directly measure mass emission of selected anthropogenic pollutants (cadmium, chromium, copper, lead, nickel, zinc) and conventional contaminants (nitrate, ammonia, phosphate, suspended solids) into [[Tijuana Estuary]] using ISCO autosampler
3. Analyze surface water runoff and non-point source pollution using BASINS 2.0 software
4. Characterize watershed condition and vulnerability using EPA Index of Watershed Indicators framework
5. Estimate impacts of alternative urban designs and land use strategies on air and surface water quality using PLACE³S/INDEX Model
6. Provide user-friendly products for community groups and decision makers

**Upper San Pedro Basin Objectives:**
1. Describe land use patterns including trend analysis and patch dynamics
2. Describe socio-economic and institutional factors driving land use decisions; develop GIS-linked models
3. Describe and model linkages between land use and environmental indicators (emphasis on water quality and quantity)
4. Conduct field study on relationships between integrity of [[Cottonwood-Willow Forest]] and [[Sacaton Grasslands]] and groundwater/surface flow regimes
5. Provide user-friendly products for stakeholders

**Methodological Approaches:**

*For Tijuana Watershed:*
- Deploy ISCO autosampler in lower Tijuana River near Hollister Street Bridge to sample four storm events annually plus dry-weather base flow conditions
- Chemical analyses: nitrate, ammonia, phosphate, total suspended solids, heavy metals (cadmium, chromium, copper, lead, nickel, zinc) using graphite furnace atomic absorption spectrophotometry with Zeeman correction
- GIS-based mass loading model for lead incorporating land use, transportation, and combustion sources delivering lead via wet and dry atmospheric deposition
- BASINS 2.0 software application including ASSESS (single watershed analysis), DATA MINING (dynamic map-data linking), Non-point Source Model (NPSM), TOXIROUTE (stream routing with non-point source pollution), and Post Processing tools
- Index of Watershed Indicators extension to Mexican portion (current IWI covers only US portion representing 1/3 of watershed; gives misleading overall score of 1 despite sewage contamination)
- PLACE³S/INDEX Model application for stakeholder-based participatory planning process evaluating land use scenarios

*For Upper San Pedro Basin:*
- Land use classification from remotely sensed imagery using both hard classification algorithms (supervised and unsupervised) and soft classification algorithms (fuzzy set logic)
- Time series analysis of land use change with landscape metrics analyzing patch composition and configuration
- Socio-economic and institutional driver modeling using rule-based transition probabilities and logistical regression (binomial and multinomial)
- GIS-based hierarchical patch-dynamics model relating land use to environmental indicators at local, landscape, and watershed scales
- Riparian community field studies at 20 sites (10 sacaton grasslands, 10 cottonwood forests) along San Pedro River and other southern/central Arizona rivers (Sonoita Creek, Turkey/O'Donnell Creeks, Hassayampa River)
- Installation of two monitoring wells per site for monthly groundwater depth measurements during growing season
- Topographic surveys to estimate depth to groundwater at study plots
- Monthly stream discharge measurements; stage-discharge relations using XS-PRO software
- Soil moisture monitoring via gypsum blocks; soil texture, macronutrient content, and moisture holding capacity determinations
- Biotic integrity assessment for sacaton: water potential, height, cover, vegetation volume (twice yearly); reproductive output (seed weight, seeds per inflorescence, inflorescences per plant)
- Biotic integrity assessment for cottonwood: water potential, leaf size and weight (twice yearly); annual branch growth rate, vegetation volume, woody plant density and basal area; herbaceous cover by species
- Statistical analysis (ANOVA or regression) relating biotic integrity indices to hydrologic and soil parameters

**Expected Products and Benefits:**

1. **Integrated GIS-based watershed models** for both basins capable of supporting management and restoration decisions
2. **Web site** portraying physical, social, economic, and political watershed characteristics plus modeling results
3. **Press-ready negatives for folio-size multi-color atlas** (approximately 100 pages) for Tijuana watershed with six major sections: environmental characteristics, infrastructural characteristics, political characteristics, socio-economic characteristics, spatial relationships (maps/graphs/illustrations), and modeling results; includes gazetteer with bilingual text (Spanish/English)
4. **Preliminary atlas design for Upper San Pedro Basin** (year two) in collaboration with SALSA project
5. **Identification of significant pollutant sources** and sensitive areas within watersheds; prioritization of sub-basins for intensive monitoring and best management practices
6. **Predictive capability** for estimating pollutant loading under various development scenarios
7. **Elucidation of hydrologic requirements** (surface flows and groundwater levels) for restoring degraded riparian stands and maintaining high-quality stands
8. **Increased understanding** of relationships between anthropogenic factors and physical/biological processes
9. **Improved baseline knowledge** of watershed conditions
10. **Enhanced collaboration** among SCERP institutions and between researchers, community groups, and government agencies
11. **Identification of stressors, high-risk areas, and data gaps**
12. **Improved methodologies** for addressing environmental problems in transborder watersheds following NAS/NRC and EPA guidelines

**Current Data Gaps Identified:**
- Index of Watershed Indicators not computed for Mexican portions of either watershed, providing incomplete characterization
- Current IWI for Cottonwood-Tijuana Watershed (USGS Unit 18070305) gives overall score of 1 (highest quality) based only on US portion, despite sewage impairment in lower river and estuary
- Current IWI for Upper San Pedro (USGS Unit 15050202) gives overall score of 3 based only on US portion (72% of watershed), missing 28% Mexican portion with major potential stressors
- Atmospheric lead concentration data needed to determine relationship to high lead levels in Tijuana estuary
- Hydrologic requirements for riparian community health not fully characterized

**Institutional Support:**
- San Diego State University: Center for Earth Systems Analysis Research (CESAR) for image processing, remote sensing, GIS, automated cartography; Graduate School of Public Health laboratories for heavy metals and conventional pollutant analyses; Environmental Modeling Laboratory at Center for Energy Studies for PLACE³S/INDEX modeling
- Arizona State University: Center for Environmental Studies and Salt River Project GIS Laboratory for image processing, remote sensing, GIS, spatial modeling
- University of Utah: GIS and remote sensing center under Dr. George Hepner for BASINS modeling
- Mexican institutions: Autonomous University of Baja California, El Colegio de la Frontera Norte, University of Sonora

**Advisory Committee:** To be formed before project initiation with representatives from both sides of border including non-government groups, Indian reservations, private sector, and local/regional/state/federal agencies.

**Beneficiaries:** Government officials from multiple jurisdictions for regional planning; public and K-12 outreach organizations (San Diego Natural History Museum, Tijuana River National Estuarine Research Reserve, The Nature Conservancy, National Audubon Society Research Ranch); university researchers and educators at SDSU, ASU, COLEF, UABC, University of Sonora for research visualization and education tools.
