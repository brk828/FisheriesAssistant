# Apache Trout Genetic Analysis: Electrophoretic Survey and Recovery Plan Correspondence

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover letter from [[Genetic Analyses]] (Maureen E. Schmidt) to fishery biologist James N. Hanson, dated December 4, 1989, transmitting the electrophoretic data and [[BIOSYS-1]] output for a genetic survey of [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae apache*). The survey compared wild and hatchery populations at 10 allozyme loci across 42 population units, including reference samples of [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]]. The letter also outlines the next steps of a recovery plan, centered on fin-tissue monitoring and genetic characterization of broodstocks. The attached printout is largely tabular and is summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Background & Description

- **Study taxon:** [[Apache Trout]], a native trout of the Arizona headwaters, used as the focal species for a recovery program.
- **Reference taxa:** Hatchery stocks of [[Rainbow Trout]] (WFH HATCHERY RBT, sample 25 fish) and Apache trout hatchery stock (WFH HATCHERY APT, sample 24.8 mean), plus [[Cutthroat Trout]] (CTT, sample 28).
- **Method:** [[Starch Gel Electrophoresis]] of tissue samples, analyzed with the BIOSYS-1 package (Release 1.7).
- **Loci:** 12 loci were originally planned. Only 10 were usable because of tissue degradation from collection problems and long-term storage. ADA-1 and MDH-P2 were omitted. Hatchery samples did not show this degradation.
- **Analyses run:** Genetic variability (biased, unbiased and direct-count heterozygosity), Hardy-Weinberg tests, chi-square tests, exact probability tests, F-statistics, genetic similarity and distance coefficients (over 12 indices, including [[Nei Genetic Distance]]), contingency chi-square and [[Cluster Analysis]] (UPGMA).
- **Sampling units:** 42 populations, including wild streams and hatchery lines, listed in a key with population numbers and site names (for example, Farad ise CK/Barr, Flash Creek, Boggy Creek-1, Centerfire, Conklin, Snake, Hanagan, Stinky Creek, KP Creek).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Populations sit in small headwater creeks and tributaries, such as Ord Creek, Elk Canyon Creek, Hurricane Lake and Rock Creek. Many are isolated, which the author implies makes genetic introgression a management concern.
- Several wild populations are monomorphic or nearly so. Flash Creek, Crooked Creek, Firebox Creek, Elk Canyon Creek, Reservation Creek, Hurricane Lake, Soldier Creek, Lojer Creek and Cutthroat are reported with no polymorphic loci at the 0.95 criterion.
- Heterozygosity is generally low across populations, with the mean per-locus value ranging from 0.000 to about 0.33.
- The letter notes that [[Hybridization]] with [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]] is a concern. Several wild populations carry rainbow or cutthroat alleles at low frequency.

## Key Findings

**Genetic variability**
- Mean per-locus heterozygosity (biased estimate) across all 42 populations is reported per population in the variability table. The highest values appear in Centerfire (0.294), Kp Creek (0.328) and South Fork Diamond (0.226). The lowest values, zero, appear in Flash Creek, Crooked Creek, Lojer Creek, Firebox Creek and the reference Cutthroat stock.
- Percentage of loci polymorphic (0.95 criterion) ranges from 0 to 80 percent across populations.

**Allele patterns in the letter**
- Population 8 (Deep Creek Up RS2 and related sites): the letter states that one population expresses a new [[PGM]] allele (A) that was absent from hatchery stocks. Expression at 50 percent in population 8 suggests the allele is fairly common in the wild.
- One population (Fork-related site classified as hybrid) carries a rainbow allele (F) at low frequency (0.036), so it was classed as a hybrid.
- Another population carries a cutthroat null allele at the CTT locus. The letter cautions that null alleles are hard to detect in heterozygotes, so the classification may be imprecise.
- Hatchery stocks of Apache and rainbow trout did not express alternate PGM alleles, which led the author to suspect allelic suppression. This contradicts the earlier assumption that PGM was fixed.

**Hardy-Weinberg and F-statistics**
- Chi-square tests for deviation from Hardy-Weinberg were run on each population, with exact probability tests and heterozygote deficiency coefficients. Specific test statistics appear in the printout and are not reproduced here.
- Overall F-statistics (across all populations) are summarized as: F(IS) = -0.038, F(IT) = 0.405, F(ST) = 0.406. This indicates substantial genetic differentiation among populations.
- Contingency chi-square across all loci gives chi-square = 51.402 (DF 943, P = 0.00000).

**Similarity and clustering**
- Genetic identity and distance matrices were computed for all 42 units, with Nei (1978) unbiased values reported. Many wild populations show identities near 0.97–1.00.
- Cluster analysis (UPGMA) grouped the populations into clusters. Cophenetic correlation = 0.978, indicating a good fit between the dendrogram and the original similarity matrix.
- The reference hatchery Apache trout and the wild population at Parad ise Creek sit in one cluster, while cutthroat and rainbow hatchery stocks form separate, distant groups.

**Recovery plan (as outlined by the author)**
1. Electrophoretically analyze fin tissue from individuals expressing the range of alleles at the 10 established loci, to determine which loci are expressed in fin tissue.
2. After this study, analyze additional loci for fin activity to support monitoring of broodstocks.
3. Genetically characterize broodstocks (domestic and wild origin) for year classes 1983 onward. Individuals with tissues other than fin may be assayed at more loci.
