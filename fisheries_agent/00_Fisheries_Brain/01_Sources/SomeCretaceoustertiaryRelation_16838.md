# Cretaceous-Tertiary Relationships of Volcanic Rocks in Southeastern Arizona and New Mexico

#geology #stratigraphy #cretaceous-tertiary

## Executive Summary

This is a geological paper, not an aquatic or ichthyological study. It re-examines the age of andesitic volcanic rocks previously mapped as Cretaceous in southeastern Arizona and southwestern New Mexico. The authors argue that these rocks, mainly andesite breccia of probable volcanic mudflow origin, are early Tertiary in age, lie unconformably on an erosion surface cut into deformed Cretaceous and older sediments, and are not interbedded with dated Cretaceous strata. The paper correlates nine localities using lithology and stratigraphic position and proposes the [[Silver Bell Formation]] and [[Claflin Ranch Formation]] as the main units. The authors state that the correlations are preliminary, acknowledge limited supporting detail, and call for critical field studies. The document contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, or water-chemistry data.

## Background & Description

**Subject and purpose.** The paper follows an earlier report by Courtright on the questionable Cretaceous age of volcanic rocks in the region. Its stated principal purpose is "to call further attention to the problem and to encourage the undertaking of critical field studies."

**Principal units.**
- **[[Silver Bell Formation]]:** Composed largely of angular to sub-rounded andesite fragments in a mud-like andesitic matrix. It unconformably overlies the Claflin Ranch Formation and is overlain by welded pyroclastics lithologically identical to the [[Cat Mountain Rhyolite]] of the Tucson Mountains.
- **[[Claflin Ranch Formation]]:** A thick clastic series of arkoses and conglomerates that underlies the Silver Bell Formation. It contains angular blocks of "Cretaceous-type" arkose up to 10 feet in length and andesite-schist conglomerate blocks over 50 feet in length and 25 feet in thickness. The authors note it resembles the [[Tucson Mountain Chaos]] except for the absence of large limestone blocks.
- **[[Tucson Mountain Chaos]]:** A giant breccia of unusually large rock blocks in a clastic matrix, deposited on an early Tertiary(?) erosion surface, the [[Tucson Surface]].
- **[[Hidalgo Volcanics]]:** Mapped by Lasky in the [[Hatchita Mountains]], New Mexico. The authors tentatively correlate them with the Silver Bell Formation.
- **[[Ringbone Shale]]:** Contains abundant andesitic material and andesitic blocks over 50 feet in diameter. The authors correlate it with the Claflin Ranch Formation.
- **[[Bronco Volcanics]]:** Near [[Tombstone]], the lower half is made up largely of clastic andesite breccias that unconformably overlie the Cretaceous Bisbee formation. The authors consider this lower unit megascopically identical to the Silver Bell Formation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address aquatic ecology, fish, invertebrates, plankton, water chemistry, or aquatic habitat. The only environmental information concerns depositional and erosional settings:

- The volcanic and clastic units rest on erosion surfaces cut into Cretaceous and older rocks.
- The authors state that these surfaces "usually mark a pronounced change in the environment of sedimentation."
- The Silver Bell-type andesites are interpreted as probable volcanic mudflow deposits.
- Some Cretaceous beds in southern Arizona contain igneous pebbles, which the authors interpret as evidence of earlier regional igneous activity.
- Volcanic and clastic units of this type are also reported from the [[Silver City District]] (New Mexico), the [[Chiricahua Mountains]], the [[Peloncillo Mountains]], and the [[Stanley Area]] and [[Winkleman-Christmas Area]] of Arizona.

## Key Findings

1. **Age reassessment:** The andesitic rocks mapped as Cretaceous volcanics are judged most probably early Tertiary (or post-Cretaceous). None of the volcanics of the Tucson Mountains can be demonstrated to be Cretaceous.
2. **Stratigraphic position:** The Silver Bell-type volcanics lie unconformably on an erosion surface cut into deformed Cretaceous and older sediments, and are not interbedded with sediments of certain or probable Cretaceous age.
3. **Volcanism timing:** The authors contend that volcanism on a major scale did not begin until after destruction of Cretaceous sedimentation had started.
4. **Scope of correlation:** The chart covers nine localities, five of which were previously reported. The authors acknowledge large distances between observation points and a deficiency of supporting detail.
5. **Hatchita Mountains reinterpretation:** Lasky's depositional contact between the Hidalgo volcanics and the Howell's Ridge formation was found to be separated by a fine-grained diorite intrusive. Some outcrops show shearing along this contact, which may indicate an intruded fault plane, possibly a thrust. Evidence for Lasky's proposed age relationship appears lacking.
6. **Ringbone Shale age doubt:** The assignment of the Ringbone shale to the Lower Cretaceous rests on structural and stratigraphic interpretation, because the few collected fossils are not among those Lasky listed as diagnostic of the Lower Cretaceous.
7. **Lower Cretaceous section:** Zeller's Lower Cretaceous section in the Big Hatchet Mountains contains no volcanic units. Zeller suggested Lasky's section may include at least one duplication.
8. **Stanley and Winkleman-Christmas areas:** Andesite breccias and flows overlie, rather than interbed with, Upper Cretaceous sandstones and shales at Stanley, and Paleozoic limestones at Winkleman-Christmas. A few feet of greenish silt and grit mark the depositional surface.
9. **Tombstone and Tucson sequences:** Clastics and volcanics of the Claflin Ranch and Silver Bell types are regarded as repeated episodes of deposition with local erosion intervals. Their order and number of units may vary from place to place.
10. **Overall conclusion:** The rocks as a group are lithologically similar over wide areas and lie on an erosion surface cut in Cretaceous and older rocks. The authors call for critical field studies to test these correlations.
