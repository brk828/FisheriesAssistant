# Long-Term Monitoring Plan for Fish Populations in Selected Waters of the Gila River Basin, Arizona: Field Standard Operating Procedures

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This August 2004 field manual from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Phoenix Area Office sets standard operating procedures (SOPs) for fish monitoring in canals and streams of the [[Gila River Basin]], Arizona. It is one part of a quality control/quality assurance program for a Long-Term Monitoring Plan. The manual defines the study objectives, crew requirements, sampling locations, data-recording rules, voucher collection, and quality control steps. It acknowledges that canal sampling is opportunistic and that standardization may be compromised.

The attached material also includes a U.S. Department of Labor wage determination for Arizona counties (Attachment No. 1). It covers occupational wage rates, fringe benefits, and conformance procedures. It has no direct bearing on fish ecology.

## Background & Description

The manual's stated aim is to ensure all field data are collected by standardized, repeatable methods. Reclamation intends it to be used with annual mandatory field training and carried in the field at all times. Updates are expected as the monitoring plan changes.

**Monitoring objectives.** Field crews are to collect information on fish populations and instream habitats at selected sites to determine:

1. Presence of species (species richness)
2. Species relative abundance (assemblage structure)
3. Species distributions
4. Species annual reproductive success

**Crew requirements.** Crews must have at least two members, and preferably three or more. Additional personnel may be needed for the [[Central Arizona Project Canal]]. Members must be experienced in fish collection and in identifying the local fauna. A crew leader with proven field experience must be present during all monitoring. Investigators must obtain their own State and Federal collecting permits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling design.** Sampling areas are defined by the following canal systems:

- **Central Arizona Project (CAP) Aqueduct:** Reaches are grouped by the Hayden-Rhodes, Fannin-McFarland, and Tucson aqueducts. Because of deep, high-velocity flow, stations sit at structural features, specifically pumping plant forebays. Travel routes to six pumping plants are listed in Table 2 (Bouse Hills, Little Harquahala, Hassayampa, Salt-Gila, Erady, San Xavier). Table 2 was to be completed after the field season.
- **[[Salt River Project Canals]]:** Sampling is primarily limited to the South Canal, between [[Granite Reef Dam]] and the junction of the Tempe and Consolidated canals, and to the Arizona Canal, between Granite Reef Dam and Indian Bend Wash. Other SRP reaches are sampled opportunistically. Annual dry-up procedures vary, so fixed stations exist only between the electrical fish barriers and Granite Reef Dam. Sampling below the barriers is opportunistic.
- **Ashurst-Hayden (FCG) Canal:** The reach runs from [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]] to the Pima Lateral feeder canal. It has no fixed stations and follows the same opportunistic pattern as the SRP canals.

Where no formal stations exist, the station code is the map mileage from the upper boundary of the canal reach. Precise site descriptions must be recorded.

**Gear and effort.** The manual specifies recording procedures for each gear class:

- **Electrofishing:** Pulse rate, voltage, amperage, and electrical output (wattage, calculated from the Burkhardt and Gutreuter formula, 1995) must be recorded. Conductivity (µS/cm) and water temperature (°C) are also required. Effort is recorded in seconds fished. Percent habitat sampled is not required for canal work.
- **Seines and dip nets:** Mesh size, length, width or depth, and the number of hauls or sweeps (standardized to 1 m) must be recorded.
- **Entrapment and entanglement gears** (gill, trammel, hoop, trap, and minnow nets): Mesh size, dimensions, and soak effort in hours (to the nearest 0.1 hour) must be recorded.
- **Angling and trotlines:** Bait type, number of hooks, and effort in hours (to the nearest 0.1 hour) must be recorded.

The [[Fish Collection Form]] uses numeric codes for stream, reach, station, gear, and macrohabitat (see Table 1). Stream codes are 1 = San Pedro River, 2 = Gila River, 3 = Salt River, 4 = CAP Canal, 5 = SRP South Canal, 6 = SRP Arizona Canal, and 7 = FCG Canal.

## Key Findings

**Species identification and age classes.** Fish are sorted to species, then to age class. Species are recorded with a four-letter code: the first two letters of the genus followed by the first two of the species. Examples given are *Cyprinella lutrensis* (CYLU) and *Agosia chrysogaster* (AGCH). Specimens that cannot be identified are coded "????" and preserved. Hybrids are coded HYBR. Age classes are young-of-year (including larvae; code 0) and age-1 or older (code 1). Counts for each species and age class, per gear and macrohabitat, must be recorded. Optional data include length, weight, sex, reproductive condition, parasites, anomalies, and notes.

**Voucher specimens.** At least three specimens of each permitted species per station or reach should be preserved as museum vouchers when possible. Photographs are required for species not permitted for take. Specimens go into 10% formalin right after data are recorded. Bodies deeper than about 8 cm (3⅛ in.) must be cut or injected to ensure adequate fixation. Specimens are held in labeled, leakproof containers and turned over to Reclamation, which deposits them at the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]].

**Quality control and field notes.** Before leaving a station, the crew leader must check all data sheets for completeness, legibility, and obvious errors, then initial the QC field. Field notes describing site conditions, sampling problems, and physical conditions are mandatory and must be submitted to Reclamation.

**Stepwise field procedure.** The manual lists 11 steps:

1. Record header information.
2. Sample all fish possible.
3. Keep live fish in buckets or holding nets.
4. Sort and count fish by species and age class.
5. Select and preserve vouchers.
6. Return remaining live fish to the canal in an area that will not be resampled.
7. Record gear and effort.
8. If the sample is inadequate, resample with different gear.
9. Review and correct data sheets.
10. Record field notes.
11. Move to the next area and repeat until the reach or station is adequately sampled.

**Wage determination (Attachment No. 1).** The Department of Labor wage determination (No. 1994-2023, Revision 27, last revised June 2004) covers Apache, Coconino, Gila, Maricopa, Navajo, Pinal, and Yavapai counties. It lists more than 200 occupations across administrative, automotive, food service, health, technical, and other categories, with minimum hourly rates. Key provisions for all covered occupations include:

- Health and welfare of $2.59 per hour
- Two, three, or four weeks of vacation after 1, 5, or 15 years of service
- A minimum of ten paid holidays
- A uniform allowance of $3.35 per week where uniforms are required

Additional pay applies to some occupations, including night and Sunday differentials, and hazardous-duty differentials of 1% and 4% for work with ordnance and explosives. The attachment also explains the process for requesting a conformance of unlisted job classifications.
