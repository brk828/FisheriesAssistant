# Western Range Extension of the Surf Smelt, *Hypomesus pretiosus pretiosus*

## Executive Summary
This short taxonomic note documents a westward range extension for the surf smelt (**[[Hypomesus pretiosus pretiosus]]**) based on two specimens captured in **[[Chignik Lagoon]]**, on the south side of the Alaska Peninsula (56°20'N, 158°30'W). Prior to this record, the westernmost documented occurrence of the subspecies was Sitkalidak Strait, Alaska (McAllister, 1963). The new Chignik Lagoon captures — one in 1963 and one in 1964 — extend the known western distribution of *H. p. pretiosus* along the Alaska Peninsula. Detailed meristic and morphometric analysis of the preserved specimen (UW 19403) confirms its identity as *H. p. pretiosus* and distinguishes it from the closely related Asian subspecies *H. p. japonicus*.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Hypomesus pretiosus pretiosus* (Girard) — the surf smelt, a member of family **[[Osmeridae]]** (smelts).
- **Known range prior to this study**: Common along the coast of **[[British Columbia]]** (Clemens and Wilby, 1961), extending south to **[[Monterey Bay]]**, California, and previously recorded no farther west than **[[Sitkalidak Strait]]**, Alaska (McAllister, 1963).
- **New locality record**: **[[Chignik Lagoon]]**, Alaska Peninsula (56°20'N lat, 158°30'W long).
- **Capture events**:
  - Specimen 1: Captured by beach seine on July 8, 1963 (immature male; preserved in 10% formalin; cataloged as **UW 19403** at the University of Washington fish museum).
  - Specimen 2: Captured by surface trawl on July 7, 1964.
- **Sampling effort (1963–1967)**: 90 hauls with a 200-ft beach seine; 153 ten-minute tows with a surface trawl (6-ft² opening).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: The species was captured within **[[Chignik Lagoon]]**, an estuarine/lagoonal habitat on the Alaska Peninsula, sampled via beach seine (nearshore/littoral zone) and surface trawl (pelagic/surface waters).
- **Geographic context**: The Alaska Peninsula lies at the boundary between the Gulf of Alaska and the Bering Sea, representing a transitional biogeographic zone for North Pacific coastal fishes.
- **Biogeographic significance**: This record extends the known western limit of *H. p. pretiosus* beyond Sitkalidak Strait, suggesting the subspecies' range along the Alaska Peninsula may be broader than previously documented, potentially reflecting under-sampling in the region rather than a recent range shift.
- **Taxonomic context**: The subspecies *H. p. pretiosus* co-occurs geographically as a sister form to **[[Hypomesus pretiosus japonicus]]**, found along the Asian coast of the Pacific; these two subspecies are differentiated by specific meristic and morphometric ranges (see Key Findings).

## Key Findings
1. **Range extension**: Confirmed presence of *H. p. pretiosus* at Chignik Lagoon, Alaska Peninsula — the westernmost documented locality for the subspecies at time of publication, surpassing the previous limit at Sitkalidak Strait.
2. **Two independent capture events** (1963 beach seine, 1964 surface trawl) support a persistent, non-transient presence rather than a single anomalous occurrence.
3. **Morphological confirmation**: UW 19403's meristic and morphometric values fall within or closely match the ranges published by McAllister (1963) for *H. p. pretiosus*, with one exception:
   - **Caudal fin ray count discrepancy**: UW 19403 exhibited 20 caudal fin rays, versus McAllister's (1963) reported value of 19 for the subspecies.
4. **Subspecies differentiation** (*H. p. pretiosus* vs. *H. p. japonicus*):
   | Character | *H. p. pretiosus* (McAllister 1963) | *H. p. japonicus* (McAllister 1963) | UW 19403 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Lateral line scales | 66–73 | 63–67 | 68 |
   | Pectoral fin rays | 14–17 | 13–14 | 15 |
   | Pectoral/pelvic fin ratio | 0.41–0.48 | 0.47–0.55 | 0.4351 |
   
   These distinguishing characters confirm UW 19403 as *H. p. pretiosus* rather than the Asian subspecies *H. p. japonicus*.
5. **Sampling context**: The relatively low frequency of capture (2 specimens across 5 years of substantial sampling effort — 90 seine hauls and 153 trawl tows) suggests surf smelt are either rare or marginal in occurrence at this locality, consistent with Chignik Lagoon representing a range-edge population.
