# Mountain Soil Associations and Soil Survey Terminology

#soil-science #pedology #soil-survey

## Executive Summary

This document is an excerpt from a county [[Soil Survey]] describing two soil associations in the higher mountain ranges, followed by a glossary of soil terms. It contains no aquatic taxa, fish, or water-body data. The content is terrestrial soil science: soil depth, texture, drainage, permeability, runoff, and vegetation. Its main use for an aquatic or ecological reader is in describing how steep, shallow soils control infiltration and surface runoff into streams.

## Background & Description

The excerpt covers two mapping units, called associations, found in the mountains of the surveyed county.

**El Lugena-Faraway Association**
- Shallow and very shallow, dark-colored, steep to very steep soils that are cobbly and gravelly.
- Developed over [[Andesite]] and [[Rhyolite]] bedrock. Bedrock lies within 20 inches of the surface.
- Typically an 8 to 12 inch cobbly or gravelly loam or sandy loam over bedrock. Some deep, cobbly soils occur in canyons.
- Well drained, with medium-textured surfaces and fine to moderately coarse subsoils.
- Slopes range from 30 to 60 percent or more.
- Available water-holding capacity is 1 to 2 inches. Intake rate is moderate, permeability is moderate to slow, and surface runoff is medium to rapid.

**Barkerville-Gaddes Association**
- Very shallow to moderately deep, well-drained, steep to very steep soils that are cobbly and gravelly and medium to moderately fine in texture.
- Developed over [[Granite]] bedrock.
- [[Barkerville Soils]] have sandy loam surfaces that grade into partially weathered granite.
- [[Gaddes Soils]] have moderately coarse surfaces, reddish brown, moderately fine subsoils, and weathered granite at moderate depths.
- [[Rock Outcrop]] covers about 25 to 30 percent of the unit.
- Slopes range from 30 to 60 percent or more.

The OCR of the temperature range for El Lugena-Faraway reads "5B F"; 58 °F is the most likely value.

**Vegetation**
- Lower unit: oak and grass cover, with [[Sideoats Grama]], cane beardgrass, tanglehead, and plains lovegrass dominant.
- El Lugena-Faraway, below 6,200 ft: an oak-juniper overstory, with pinyon ricegrass, tanglehead, sideoats grama, and cane beardgrass between trees.
- El Lugena-Faraway, above 6,200 ft: a pine-oak overstory with abundant juniper and locust. The understory includes pinyon ricegrass, blue grama, and sideoats grama.

**Hydrologic implications**
- Thin soils over bedrock hold little water (1 to 2 inches of available capacity). This favors rapid runoff on steep slopes.
- Moderate to slow permeability limits infiltration. Runoff from these slopes is likely to reach streams quickly, which matters for sediment and erosion control in mountain watersheds.

## Key Findings

- The excerpt describes two steep, shallow mountain soil associations (El Lugena-Faraway and Barkerville-Gaddes) that occur throughout the higher ranges of the county.
- Both associations have slopes of 30 to 60 percent or more and shallow profiles, which limit water storage and increase surface runoff.
- El Lugena-Faraway has 1 to 2 inches of available water-holding capacity, moderate intake, moderate to slow permeability, and medium to rapid runoff.
- Barkerville-Gaddes has 25 to 30 percent rock outcrop, which further reduces usable soil area.
- Vegetation shifts with elevation. Oak-juniper dominates below 6,200 ft and pine-oak with juniper above it.
- The glossary defines the terms used in the survey: [[Intake Rate]], [[Soil Permeability]], [[Surface Runoff]], [[Soil Reaction]] (pH), [[Saline Soil]], [[Saline-Alkali Soil]], [[Salts]], [[Piping]], [[Soil Profile]], and [[Percent Slope]].
- The permeability scale runs from very slow (less than 0.06 in/hr, truncated in the OCR) to very rapid (more than 20.0 in/hr). The reaction scale runs from extremely acid to very strongly alkaline, with 7.0 as neutral.
