# Late Pleistocene Fishes of the Clear Creek and Ben Franklin Local Faunas of Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoichthyology #pleistocene-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1963 paper by [[Teruya Uyeno]] describes fossil fish remains from two Late Pleistocene local faunas in north-central and northeast Texas. The Clear Creek local fauna is assigned to the [[Sangamon Interglacial]] stage, and the Ben Franklin local fauna to the Late [[Wisconsin Glaciation]]. The fish remains were recovered by [[Bob H. Slaughter]] using a washing technique, and are rare and fragmentary. All identified taxa still live today in the same region, east of the [[Rocky Mountains]]. The author concludes that the fish fauna remained relatively stable through the Late Pleistocene and Recent, in contrast to marked changes in the mammalian fauna.

## Background & Description

- **Context:** Late Pleistocene mammal remains from Texas had been studied extensively, while associated fossil fishes had received less attention ([[Uyeno and Miller 1962]]).
- **Collection method:** Fossils were recovered by washing sediment, following [[Hibbard 1949]]. Specimens are housed in the [[Shuler Museum of Paleontology]], [[Southern Methodist University]].
- **Funding:** Supported by [[National Science Foundation]] Grant 15914 to [[Robert Rush Miller]], University of Michigan.
- **Material:** Mostly isolated bones and scales (vertebrae, pharyngeals, pectoral spines, cleithra, dentaries, teeth), identified mainly to genus or family because elements are not diagnostic at species level.

### Clear Creek Local Fauna
- **Site:** Second terrace above Clear Creek at the Trietsch Pit, Denton County, about four miles upstream from the junction with the Elm Fork of the [[Trinity River]].
- **Matrix:** About ten tons of alluvium, with fossils in six to eight feet of sandy clay.
- **Age:** [[Sangamon Interglacial]], based on mammal fauna and radiocarbon dating.

### Ben Franklin Local Fauna
- **Site:** Five quarry sites within six hundred yards of the State Highway 38 bridge over the [[Sulphur River]] (a tributary of the [[Red River]]), near Ben Franklin, Delta County.
- **Matrix:** Several tons of sediment.
- **Age:** Late [[Wisconsin Glaciation]] (per [[Slaughter and Hoover 1963]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Paleoenvironment:** Fish size and habitat relations suggest small ponds or low-gradient river pools with some vegetation, at both sites.
- **Mammal-based climate inference:** At Ben Franklin, the presence of [[Sorex cinereus]], [[Blarina]] sp., [[Microtus pennsylvanicus]] and [[Synaptomys]] points to cooler weather, especially in summer, than now prevails in northeastern Texas.
- **Associated fauna:** Clear Creek yielded 30 mammal species, nine of them extinct (including mammoth, camel, bison, peccary, horse and ass). Ben Franklin yielded 22 mammal species, alongside molluscs and herpetofauna.
- **Modern distribution:** All identified fishes occur today in the temperate to subtropical zone east of the Rocky Mountains.

## Key Findings

**Taxa identified (summary of the fossil material):**

| Taxon | Locality | Identification & Basis |
|---|---|---|
| *Lepisosteus* sp. (gar) | Clear Creek | Seven scales (four nearly complete); smooth surface suggests [[Longnose Gar]]-type or [[Spotted Gar]]-type species |
| *Campostoma*? (stoneroller) | Clear Creek | One pharyngeal piece; tentative |
| Catostomidae? (sucker) | Clear Creek | 17 vertebrae and one regenerated scale; tentative |
| *Ictalurus punctatus* or *I. furcatus* | Clear Creek and Ben Franklin | Pectoral spines with moderate serrations |
| *Ictalurus melas* or *I. natalis* | Clear Creek and Ben Franklin | Pectoral spines, weakly or not serrated |
| *Ictalurus* sp. | Clear Creek and Ben Franklin | Abundant cranial and postcranial fragments |
| *Lepomis* sp. (sunfish) | Clear Creek and Ben Franklin | Identified with help from Gerald R. Smith |
| *Esox* sp. (pickerel) | Ben Franklin | 61 vertebrae, dentary and teeth; likely [[American Pickerel]] or [[Chain Pickerel]] |
| *Notemigonus crysoleucas* (golden shiner) | Ben Franklin | 16 pharyngeal pieces with diagnostic dentition (5-5 formula) |

**Faunal totals:**
- Across Clear Creek, Sulphur River and the T-2 terrace of the Trinity River, eleven fish species were recorded: two gars, one pickerel, two minnows, one sucker, three catfish, one sunfish and the [[Freshwater Drum]].

**Main conclusions:**
1. All fossil fish taxa are still present in the same region today.
2. The fish fauna remained relatively stable from the Sangamon interglacial through the Recent, whereas the mammalian fauna changed markedly.
3. Fish fossils are rare and fragmentary, so most identifications are to genus or family, with several tentative.
