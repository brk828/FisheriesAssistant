# Effectiveness of Light Traps for Capture and Retention of Larval and Early Juvenile Xyrauchen texanus, Ptychocheilus lucius, and Gila elegans

#ichthyology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Survey Objectives & Target Population

**Primary Sampling Purpose:** Controlled laboratory experiments testing whether floating, low-intensity quatrefoil-style [[Light Trap Capture Efficiency]] units can capture and retain larval and early juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Bonytail]]. The stated goals were to judge trap potential, provide guidelines for trap use, and improve interpretation of field results. The work supported the [[National Park Service]] Cooperative Parks Study Unit (Cooperative Agreement CA1268-2-9004; work orders CSU-21 and CSU-48, 1993 and 1994).

Key findings:
- Mean capture percentages (MCPs) for razorback sucker larvae in 1- and 4-h trials were 13–36%; 8-h trials gave 33–44%.
- MCPs for Colorado squawfish larvae were 3–16%; bonytail larvae gave 5–30%.
- Early juvenile razorback sucker reached a maximum MCP of 51% within 1 h.
- Mean retention percentages (MRPs) were 85–99% for larvae, but only 65–73% for early juveniles.
- Light was critical: with trap lights off, captures were 0–4% and protolarva retention fell to 16% (4-h).
- Turbid water (50–75 FTU) raised larval MCPs about 2.6–2.8 times but cut early juvenile MCP about 70%.

**Target Population & Spatial Boundaries:** Lab-reared larvae and early juveniles, sourced as fertilized eggs from Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center (New Mexico). Stages tested:
- Razorback sucker: protolarvae (10–11 mm TL), flexion mesolarvae (12–13 mm), postflexion mesolarvae and metalarvae (15–20 mm), early juveniles (27–35 mm).
- Colorado squawfish and bonytail (1994): protolarvae (8–9 mm TL), postflexion mesolarvae (11–13 mm).

The field context is Upper and Lower Colorado River Basin nearshore and backwater habitats, including the middle Green River and inflow to [[Lake Powell]]. Field objectives were to monitor reproductive success, assess downstream transport, identify nursery backwaters, verify use of restored floodplains, and document spatial and temporal distribution.

## Gear Specifications & Standardization

**Equipment & Mesh/Gear Parameters:**
- **[[Southern Concepts Trap]] (standard):** Four-lobe clear plexiglass quatrefoil, 32 cm tall and 24 cm in diameter. Lobe assembly is 14 cm tall and 19 cm in diameter. It has a 5-cm styrofoam float, a central spiral-scored light rod, a lens-focusing lamp, and two D-cell batteries (2-V voltage-limiting circuit). The circuit gives about 2 lux at the perimeter and about 0.1 lux at 0.5 m, with alkaline battery life of about 40 h. Entry slits are customer-specified (2, 4, or 6 mm). The catch basin has Nitex-screened side ports. The trap costs over $300 (the Edlite refinement).
- **[[Mueller Trap]] (high-intensity comparison):** Quatrefoil, 22 cm tall and 35 cm in diameter, with 10-mm slits. It uses a 7-W lamp sealed in a jar and 120-V power, with a catch basin borrowed from a Southern Concepts Trap. Perimeter intensity was 300 lux.
- **[[Jennings Trap]] (prototype):** Trefoil (three-lobe), 50 cm tall and 41 cm in diameter, with 6–8 mm slits, a 6-W lamp (high), and a screw-on catch bottle. Perimeter intensity was 160 lux (high) and 0.5 lux (low).
- Light intensity was measured in air at the perimeter and at 0.5 and 1.0 m using a Science and Mechanics Darkroom Model A-3 photometer.

**Deployment & Effort Standardization:** Experiments ran in green fiberglass tanks (1.2 m diameter), depth 45 cm, temperature 18–20 °C, under light-excluding black plastic tents. A dimmer-controlled flood lamp 1 m above one side simulated daylight, dusk, and dawn. Each test used 50 larvae or 25 early juveniles, with three trials per treatment. For the standard configuration, the trap was secured mid-tank with a 2-mm slit (larvae) or 6-mm slit (juveniles). Before each trial, fish acclimated 0.5 h each under daylight, dusk, and full darkness. Set durations were 1 h (all), 4 h (all except flexion mesolarvae), or 8 h (protolarvae and postflexion mesolarvae only). In retention trials, fish were placed in the catch basin, allowed to calm, and the trap was then set. At retrieval, the trap was lifted slowly; fish were anesthetized with tricaine methanesulfonate, counted, and preserved in formalin. Fish remaining in the tank were netted and processed the same way. Unrecovered fish triggered a tank drain and refill.

**Selectivity & Bias Considerations:**
- Body width sets size passage. Expected maximum TL for 2, 4, and 6-mm slits was 17, 28, and 40 mm, respectively, for razorback sucker larvae.
- Early juveniles (27–35 mm) could not enter 2-mm slits (0 captured). The single-trial limit was below 27 mm TL.
- Slit width (2 vs. 4 mm) did not significantly change larval MCP or MRP.
- Trap light off produced very low captures, so light is a strong selectivity factor.
- Trap design affected juvenile capture: Jennings Trap MCP was 8–19% versus 51% for the Southern Concepts Trap at the same slit size range.
- To prevent drainage, the Mueller and Jennings traps had their slits sealed with plastic foam strips before lifting. Stranded fish were checked by scanning trap floors and foam.

## Experimental & Spatial Design Framework

**Stratification & Allocation:** Experiments were stratified by developmental interval, set duration (1, 4, 8 h), light condition (night, simulated dusk, dawn, daylight, light-off), water turbidity (clear; 20–30 FTU; 50–75 FTU), light intensity (2 vs. 160–300 lux), slit width (2, 4, 6 mm), and trap design (Southern Concepts, Mueller, Jennings). Capture and retention were tested separately. Most treatments had three trials. Fish were not reused.

**Temporal Scheduling:**
- Night capture: 0.5 h daylight, 0.5 h dusk dimming, then full darkness, with the trap set afterward.
- Dusk capture: 0.5 h daylight, then 0.5 h of dimming within a 1-h set.
- Dawn retention: 0.5 h of brightening, then 0.5 h of daylight.
- Daylight: single 1-h trials for early juveniles.

The field recommendation is periodically retrieved overnight sets for maximum efficiency. Traps should be checked and emptied frequently under high catch or predator conditions. For comparability with catch-per-unit-time, sets should start after nightfall and end before dawn.

**Detection & Bias Mitigation:** Retention trials separate escape from non-entry. Fish that escaped to the tank were recovered by netting. Tanks were drained when fish could not be found. Traps were retrieved slowly to reduce drainage losses. Battery life was specified to last through the sampling period. Turbidity was controlled by bentonite clay suspensions, maintained by bubbling air.

## Field Data Schema & Variable Definitions

**Primary Biological Measurements:**
- Counts of fish captured (in trap) and retained (in trap after retrieval) per trial.
- Total length (TL, mm) and developmental stage class (protolarva, flexion, postflexion, metalarva, early juvenile).
- Maximum body width (mm), used for size-passage estimates.
- Percentage captured (MCP) and percentage retained (MRP), with 95% confidence intervals.
- Fish outcomes: anesthetized, counted, and preserved in formalin. Mortality during trials is not reported.

**Environmental & Spatial Covariates:**
- Light intensity at trap perimeter, 0.5 m, and 1.0 m (lux, in air).
- Turbidity (FTU), using bentonite standards. Field secchi-disk readings (about 0.5 m) were noted in a reported field context.
- Water depth (45 cm) and temperature (18–20 °C) for lab trials.
- Simulated lighting condition: daylight, dusk, dawn, or night.
- Slit width and trap design.
- Field context: habitat type (still backwater, shoreline, main or side-channel current), water clarity, moonlight phase, and fish density and aggregation near structure.

**QA/QC & Data Entry Protocols:** Data were recorded as percentages captured or retained and tabulated with treatment means and 95% CIs. Differences were significant at α = 0.05 when CIs did not overlap. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was run on arcsine-transformed data, and the Student-Newman-Keuls range test was used for multiple comparisons. Retention experiments and light-off controls were used as checks. Ordered raw values were listed for each treatment. Anomalies were addressed by repeating experiments with another brood (Colorado squawfish results "should be verified"). Light meter readings were checked against an external meter. Specific data-entry and storage procedures are not reported.
