# Freshwater Fishes of Saudi Arabia and Adjacent Regions of the Arabian Peninsula

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This revision of the freshwater fishes of the [[Arabian Peninsula]] is based on about 4,500 specimens and builds on the first comprehensive work by [[Banister and Clarke]] (1977). It describes three new species and two new subspecies, synonymizes several names, and proposes three [[Ichthyogeographical Subprovinces]] for the peninsula. Two families are treated: [[Cyprinidae]] (the native freshwater fauna) and [[Cyprinodontidae]] (the killifish genus [[Aphanius]]). The study also reconstructs the paleogeography and hydrography that shaped the fauna and offers a hypothesis on the evolution and dispersal of the [[Aphanius dispar Complex]].

## Background & Description

**Scope and sources.** The study covers all freshwater fishes of the Arabian Peninsula, bounded by the Arabo-Persian Gulf, the Indian Ocean, and the Red Sea. The northern limit follows the Saudi border, but the full length of [[Wadi As-Sirhan]] and populations from the Dead Sea rift valley are included. The peninsula covers roughly three million km², about the size of India, yet only about ten freshwater fish species had been described from Arabia. Krupp attributes this scarcity mainly to the arid climate, which confines fishes to higher, wetter altitudes.

**Material.** Collections came from [[Walter Büttiker]] (Naturhistorisches Museum Basel), the [[British Museum (Natural History)]] (including most Arabian type specimens), [[Giuseppe Scortecci]]'s Yemen collections (Genova), and the author's own material. Specimens were fixed in 4% formalin and transferred to 70% ethanol. Measurements were taken with dial calipers to 0.1 mm, following Banister and Clarke (1977) with modifications, and meristic counts were standardized (for example, fin rays, lateral line scales, and gill rakers).

**Taxonomic history.** Key earlier work includes:
- [[Trewavas]] (1941), who described *Barbus arabicus*, *Garra tibanica* and *G. brittoni*.
- [[Fowler and Steinitz]] (1956), who described *Garra barreimiae* from Oman.
- [[Menon]] (1964), who synonymized *G. brittoni* with *G. tibanica* and regarded *G. arabica* as probably mislabeled *G. nasuta*.
- [[Balletto and Spanò]] (1977), who proposed numerous *G. tibanica* subspecies from Yemen.
- [[Banister and Clarke]] (1977), the most comprehensive earlier treatment.

**Inventory.** The revision recognizes the following valid taxa: *Barbus apoensis*, *B. arabicus*, *B. exulatus*, *Cyprinion acinaces* (with *C. a. hijazi* n. ssp.), *C. mhalensis*, *C. microphthalmum* (with *C. m. muscatensis*), *Garra barreimiae* (with *G. b. shawkahensis*), *G. buettikeri* n. sp., *G. longipinnis*, *G. mamshuqa* n. sp., *G. sahilia* n. sp. (with *G. s. gharbia* n. ssp.), *G. tibanica* (with *G. t. ghorensis*), *Aphanius dispar* (with *A. d. richardsoni*), and *A. sirhani*. Several *Garra* sp. remain *incertae sedis*. Six introduced species are also recorded: *Barbus canis*, *Cyprinus carpio*, *Clarias gariepinus*, *Tilapia zillii*, *T. aurea*, and *Gambusia* sp. These are not described. Marine fishes that enter fresh water (Gobiidae and Mugilidae) are noted but not described.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Paleogeography.** The Africo-Arabian block was continuous until the Cenozoic, when the Arabian plate separated from Africa along the [[Red Sea Rift Valley]]. The modern drainage network formed mainly in the Pliocene, and pluvial lakes filled internal basins. Present hyperaridity began about 17,000 years B.P.

**Hydrography.** Water courses in Arabia are *widyan* (wadis), which are generally discontinuous and are interrupted for most of the year. Their central reaches are often reduced to pools that serve as refugia for freshwater animals. Salinity is high in internal drainages, so suitable habitat for freshwater fishes is limited.

**Regional settings discussed:**
- **Arabo-Persian Gulf:** a shallow epicontinental sea. Sea level was about 150 m higher than today near the end of the Pliocene, and fell at least 120 m during Pleistocene glaciations. A freshwater connection linking Iran and Arabia existed almost continuously between about 70,000 and 20,000 years B.P.
- **Mesopotamian basin:** the Tigris and Euphrates network stabilized in the early Pleistocene, but was colonized by primary freshwater fishes only in the late Pliocene.
- **Wadi As-Sirhan and [[Azraq]]:** Azraq held a freshwater lake of about 4,000 km² in the Pleistocene; today there are about 5 km² of perennial water.
- **Jordan rift valley and Dead Sea:** Lake Samra (a freshwater lake, Pluvial B) changed into the brackish Lake Lisan, then to two saline lakes during Interpluvial C.
- **Red Sea and Gulf of Aden:** headwater captures diverted westward-flowing drainages into the Red Sea, and the Yemen highlands allowed exchange between Wadi Hadramaut and coastal systems until recent times.
- **Oman mountains (al-Hajar):** reach 3,000 m, receive high precipitation, and are of Neogene origin.
- **Internal deserts:** the Great Nafud (about 57,000 km²) and the Rub' al-Khali (about 600,000 km²). The Rub' al-Khali held inland lakes during Pleistocene pluvials, and a subpluvial phase occurred from 9,000 to 6,000 years B.P.

**Habitat notes from the sampling sites.** Collecting localities include permanent and intermittent wadi reaches, shallow slow-running water, rivulets and pools, springs, and artesian wells. Many collections come from altitudes between about 1,400 and 2,400 m in the Asir and Taif ranges, where precipitation is higher.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomy and new taxa**
- Three new species are described: *Garra buettikeri*, *G. mamshuqa*, and *G. sahilia*. Two new subspecies are described: *Cyprinion acinaces hijazi* and *G. sahilia gharbia*.
- *Cyprinion muscatensis* is reduced to a subspecies of *C. microphthalmum* (*C. m. muscatensis*), and *C. mhalensis* is distinguished from *C. acinaces*.
- All subspecies of *G. tibanica* described from Yemen are synonymized with the nominal subspecies, though a subspecies *G. t. ghorensis* is retained for the Dead Sea rift valley.
- *Garra barreimiae* and *G. longipinnis* are aligned with *G. persica*, a species from Baluchestan and Hormozgan (Coad 1982) that is distinct from *G. rufa*.
- *Garra arabica* is regarded as a mislabeled *G. nasuta*.
- *Aphanius sirhani* is confirmed as a valid species, based partly on male sterility in F1 hybrids of *A. dispar* × *A. sirhani*.

**Diagnostic characters (selected)**
- *Garra tibanica*: 7 branched dorsal rays (rarely 6 or 8), 32–38 lateral line scales (mode 35), 6–13 gill rakers. Its morphometric data are based on 200 specimens, and dorsal ray counts on 400 specimens.
- *Garra buettikeri*: 7 unbranched dorsal rays, 36–39 lateral line scales, 20 scales around the caudal peduncle; endemic to the eastern Asir streamlets draining to Wadi ad-Dawasir.
- *Garra mamshuqa*: 8 branched dorsal rays, 12 scales around the caudal peduncle, 15–17 gill rakers; restricted to the Wadi Hadramaut system.
- *Garra sahilia*: 8 branched dorsal rays, 16 caudal peduncle scales, 32–36 lateral line scales; occurs sympatrically with *G. tibanica* in several wadis.
- *Cyprinion acinaces*: occurs in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden basins; the nominal subspecies is endemic to Wadi Hadramaut tributaries.
- *Cyprinion mhalensis*: endemic to the Wadi ad-Dawasir system, with 39–44 lateral line scales and a strongly serrated dorsal spine.
- *Barbus apoensis*: one pair of barbels (distinguishing it from *B. canis*, which has two), 4 unbranched and 10 branched dorsal rays, 29–31 lateral line scales.
- *Aphanius dispar*: 8–11 dorsal rays, 24–28 lateral line scales, 12–20 tricuspid teeth per jaw; the species reaches a largest recorded size of 52 mm sl in this study, contrary to older claims of 80 mm.

**Biogeography**
- The peninsula is divided into three ichthyogeographical subprovinces: (1) the Oman mountains (with *Cyprinion microphthalmum muscatensis*, *Garra barreimiae*, and *G. longipinnis*); (2) the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden drainages (with *Barbus arabicus*, *B. exulatus*, *Cyprinion acinaces*, *Garra tibanica*, *G. sahilia*, and *G. mamshuqa*); and (3) the western Rub' al-Khali drainage (with *Barbus apoensis*, *Cyprinion mhalensis*, and *Garra buettikeri*).
- No primary freshwater fishes are known from outside these subprovinces.
- Of the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden taxa, four show Oriental affinities and three show African affinities. *Garra tibanica* appears to have entered southwest Arabia from Africa and migrated north along the Red Sea coast.
- Speciation of Arabian Cyprinidae appears to have taken place in recent geological time, and increasing aridity since the Pleistocene likely eliminated many freshwater species.

**Aphanius dispar complex**
- *A. dispar* is widely distributed from northeast Africa to northwest India, under freshwater, brackish, and marine conditions. Two subspecies are recognized: the nominal *A. d. dispar* and *A. d. richardsoni* (endemic to the Dead Sea rift valley, with a shorter dorsal fin in large males).
- *A. sirhani* (Azraq, Jordan) is distinguished by 8–11 black transverse bars on males.
- Proposed phylogeny: *A. sirhani* and *A. dispar* are sister lineages within the *A. fasciatus*/*A. dispar* species group. The group split into the western *A. fasciatus* complex and the eastern *A. dispar* complex during the Miocene.
- Krupp rejects Steinitz's (1951) Tethyan-age hypothesis and Por's (1975) Pliocene relict hypothesis on the grounds of geological timing and the low morphological divergence of *A. d. richardsoni*.
- The proposed route is: Mesopotamian origin, dispersal through the Gulf to the Arabian Sea, colonization of the Red Sea after the Indian Ocean breached Bab al-Mandab, northward migration via headwater captures, and retreat to Dead Sea sources after Lake Lisan dried.

**Conservation and human impact**
- Introductions of six non-native freshwater species are documented, and the author notes the potential influence of human activity on distribution (for example, the mosquitofish *Gambusia*).

**Limitations noted by the author**
- Several populations (for example, the aberrant *G. buettikeri* population at Abalah, known from only 7 small specimens) and *Garra* sp. *incertae sedis* are based on very small samples.
- Subspecies status is considered uncertain where morphometric differences are weak or geographically unsupported.
