# Handbook of Paleoichthyology, 3A. Chondrichthyes I — Paleozoic Elasmobranchii

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoichthyology #Chondrichthyes #Elasmobranchii

## Executive Summary
This entry summarizes a 1984 book review by Robert Rush Miller of R. Zangerl's *Handbook of Paleoichthyology, 3A: Chondrichthyes I — Paleozoic Elasmobranchii* (1981). The handbook is part of a planned ten-volume survey of agnathans and fishes complementing the *Encyclopedia of Paleoherpetology*. This volume focuses on the general morphology and systematics of Paleozoic [[Chondrichthyes]], dividing the class into two subclasses — [[Subterbranchialia]] and [[Elasmobranchii]] — and presenting cladistic frameworks for both. Notably, it includes an expanded treatment of the [[Iniopterygia]], a primitive chondrichthyan order described by Zangerl and Case (1973), identified as the sister group to the [[Holocephali]]. The review is highly favorable, praising the thoroughness, illustration quality, and taxonomic utility (dual indices) of the volume.

## Background & Description
- **General morphology section (85 pages)** covers:
  - Skeletal system and dentition
  - Sensory (lateral) line systems
  - Digestive system, including spiral intestine morphology and number of turns (where preservable)
  - Overall body form
- **Skeletal tissue**: Chondrichthyans lack significant bone development (unlike other gnathostomes); at most, **prismatic calcified cartilage** developed in sharks and their allies — considered a shared derived specialization unique to this group.
- **Tooth replacement**: Chondrichthyes share with the [[Teleostomi]] (Osteichthyes + Acanthodii) a specific ontogenetic mode of tooth replacement and formation occurring along a **dental lamina**, a mode purportedly pioneered by the [[Acanthodii]].
- **Branchial region**: In [[Elasmobranchii]], posterior lengthening of the branchial region is interpreted as a functional adaptation to a more active, free-swimming lifestyle, and is treated as a shared derived character supporting monophyly.
- **Subterbranchialia**: Characterized by different positional relationships of the branchial basket and relative crowding of the visceral arches — a condition regarded as primitive (plesiomorphic) rather than diagnostic of monophyly.
- **Holostyly**: Noted to have evolved independently at least twice (possibly more often) within [[Chondrichthyes]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Early chondrichthyans were **probably all marine**.
- The elongation of the branchial region in [[Elasmobranchii]] is linked ecologically to a shift toward a **more active, free-swimming predatory lifestyle**, contrasting with more sedentary or differently adapted body plans in [[Subterbranchialia]].
- The [[Iniopterygia]] represent a relatively primitive, ecologically distinct lineage within Paleozoic Chondrichthyes, occupying a transitional morphological niche linked to the [[Holocephali]].

## Key Findings
- **Class Chondrichthyes** is divided into two subclasses:
  - **[[Subterbranchialia]]** (8 pages in text) — likely **not monophyletic**; no satisfactory shared derived characters yet identified, though it remains a definable taxonomic entity. Systematics are described as "in flux" due to rapidly expanding fossil data.
  - **[[Elasmobranchii]]** (52 pages in text) — supported as **monophyletic** based on shared derived characters, notably the lengthened branchial region.
- **New unpublished data** cited in the volume indicate that **[[Xenacanthida|xenacanths]]** do not stand apart from other sharks, contradicting prior interpretations from as recently as 1977.
- The **[[Iniopterygia]]**, first described by Zangerl and Case (1973), are reclassified/expanded with new material and confirmed as the **sister group to [[Holocephali]]**.
- Cladograms are provided for both subclasses, offering explicit conceptual frameworks underlying Zangerl's systematic conclusions — praised by the reviewer as "invaluable."
- The volume concludes with a short section on presumed elasmobranch remains, a bibliography, and **two indices** (genera; higher categories), enhancing usability.
- Overall assessment: A high-quality, thorough contribution by Zangerl (curator emeritus of geology, Field Museum of Natural History), strongly recommended by the reviewer.
