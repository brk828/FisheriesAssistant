# *Paruroctonus maritimus* Williams, A New Scorpion Species from Coastal California

#arachnology #scorpiones #vaejovidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes *Paruroctonus maritimus*, a new scorpion species in the family Vaejovidae, from the coastal sand dunes of [[Monterey County]], California. The specimens were first collected by Roy Johnson along railroad tracks at Seaside and were tentatively identified as *Paruroctonus boreus*. Reexamination showed they belong to an undescribed species. The author, [[Stanley C Williams]] of San Francisco State University, places it near [[Paruroctonus Boreus]] and [[Paruroctonus Silvestrii]]. The document is not about fish or aquatic systems. It covers a terrestrial arachnid and contains no water chemistry, aquatic invertebrate, or fish data.

## Background & Description

The study began when a small series of *Paruroctonus* was collected under cardboard and other surface debris along railroad tracks at Seaside, Monterey County. The site lay well outside the known range of *P. boreus*, was restricted in area, and was ecologically disturbed, so the initial identification was tentative. Measurements follow the methods defined by Williams (1980), cited in the paper as the reference work on the scorpions of [[Baja California]] and adjacent islands.

**Diagnosis (summary):**
- Total length up to 50 mm.
- Pale yellow base color with dusky-black marbling on the carapace and mesosoma. The marbling does not reach the posterior margin of the mesosomal terga.
- Pectine teeth: 24–27 in males, 18–20 in females.
- Metasomal ventral keels smooth to obsolete on segment I, smooth on II–IV, and serrate on V.
- Chela with 6 supernumerary denticles on the fixed finger and 7 on the movable finger.

**Holotype (male):** total length 41.0 mm; metasoma 19.0 mm; chela 7.2 mm; pectine teeth 24/26 (left/right).

**Allotype (female):** total length 48.0 mm; metasoma 21.3 mm; chela 8.6 mm; pectine teeth 18/18.

**Differences from related species:**
- From *P. silvestrii*: pigment pattern of the mesosomal terga and four pairs of ventral macrosetae on metasomal segment II.
- From *P. boreus*: less distinct proximal gap between the chela fingers in males, slightly fewer pectine teeth, more forward median ocelli, carapace length to frontal margin distance ratio below 2.0, and less elongate male metasoma (segment IV length/width ratio below 1.9).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species is known only from coastal habitats of central California. At the type locality, specimens were found under surface debris on dry, fine coastal dune sand. Field collections suggest that coastal sand dune communities in this region are its preferred habitat. The paper does not report water chemistry, moisture regimes, prey, or any aquatic setting.

Sex and age structure of the sample was unusual. Of the 26 specimens collected, all were mature females except one mature male and three juvenile males. The author notes this as curious but does not explain it.

Related topics include the [[Coastal Sand Dune Community]], the [[Seaside California]] collection site, and the [[Railroad Track Habitat]], where the original specimens were found in a disturbed setting.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Paruroctonus maritimus* is a previously undescribed scorpion. The name refers to its coastal habitat.
- **Holotype and type data:** A male holotype and female allotype were collected at Seaside, Monterey County, on 7 April 1985 by Roy Johnson. The holotype is deposited at the [[California Academy Of Sciences]] (Entomology Type No. 15791).
- **Topoparatypes:** 21 females and 3 juvenile males were studied from Seaside collections on 3, 5, and 7 April 1985.
- **Size range:** Topoparatype total length spans 21.3–50.0 mm. Pectine tooth counts are 24–27 in juvenile males (mode 26) and 18–20 in females (mode 19).
- **Closest relatives:** *P. boreus* and *P. silvestrii*, separated by pigment pattern, pectine counts, and metasomal proportions.
- **Distribution:** Known only from coastal central California, in coastal sand dune habitat.
- **Reidentification:** Specimens first tentatively assigned to *P. boreus* were reclassified as a new species after reinvestigation.
