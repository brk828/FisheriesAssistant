# Glen Canyon NRA Fish Management Plan: Native Fish Enhancement at Lake Powell

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #native-fish-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of interagency planning materials from 1995 for the [[Fish Management Plan]] (FMP) of [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]] (GLCA). It contains four parts: an FMP outline with assigned responsibilities, a [[National Park Service]] (NPS) scoping memo to the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG), a technical proposal by [[Gordon Mueller]] (National Biological Survey) for establishing refugium populations of [[Razorback Sucker]] at [[Lake Powell]], and coordination meeting notes from April 12, 1995. The planning effort involves the NPS, the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR), the Arizona Game and Fish Department, and the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS). Its central proposal is a goal of 5,000 adult razorback suckers in Lake Powell, to be reached through stocking over about 10 years.

## Background & Description

The FMP outline (March 1995) lists 13 sections. These cover the plan's purpose, agency responsibilities, legal and policy authorities, environmental setting, the history and condition of the river and lake fisheries, biological summaries for each fish species, management issues, implementation and permitting protocols, information and education, and a bibliography. The outline states that the plan should:

- Establish goals and objectives for specific fish species in GLCA.
- Set implementation priorities.
- Define the role of Lake Powell in perpetuating native fishes in the Colorado River system and in sustaining the recreational fishery.
- Provide procedures for resolving fish management issues.

The NPS memo (April 25, 1995) notes that the NFWG identified development of the FMP as a 1995 goal. It also identifies proactive native fish management as urgent. Candidate actions include expanding the UDWR hatchery for razorback suckers and developing isolated grow-out facilities. Several [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) documents were circulated for review, including the "Genetic Management Guidelines," the "Coordinated Hatchery Facility Plan," and a draft environmental assessment on disposition of captive-reared endangered fish.

The Mueller proposal states that intensive surveys in the last three weeks of March captured only one razorback sucker in the San Juan Arm of Lake Powell. That fish had been released upstream by the FWS. No wild razorbacks were taken, and none had been taken from the lower reservoir in the past five years. The proposal also notes that [[Colorado Squawfish]] are absent or extremely rare in the same area. Data from [[Lake Mohave]] are cited: predation was identified as the main cause of recruitment failure there. Suckers reared in isolated backwaters to 25–30 cm were surviving and replacing adults. The proposal states that these fish have a longevity of 40–50 years.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal describes Lake Powell and the short river reaches of the San Juan River as highly modified habitats. It states that the historic ecology of the river is largely unknown and that the resources for habitat restoration, recreation limits, or non-native fish reduction are unlikely to be available. Because of this, the author describes the proposed work as enhancement, not recovery, since natural recruitment would be highly unlikely. The stated benefits are:

1. Additional refugium populations could help preserve genetic diversity and provide future brood stock.
2. The populations would allow study of predator-prey interactions, reservoir habitat use, and growth.
3. A reservoir population would increase the native diversity of the reservoir fishery.

[[Iceberg Canyon]] is located about 5 miles downstream of Hall's Crossing. Its terminal portion was closed by a natural landslide, forming an estimated 5-hectare isolated backwater. High reservoir elevations in 1983–84 connected the backwater to the reservoir. [[Largemouth Bass]] have been observed there, and other reservoir fishes are likely present. The proposal describes the backwater as a potentially ideal refugium for razorback suckers and [[Bonytail Chub]], but says predator removal is required first.

Other sites noted for possible rearing or holding include [[Wahweap]], the Paria ponds, and Iceberg Canyon. The Lees Ferry reach supports both a trout fishery and native species. The [[Paria River]] is important spawning habitat for [[Flannelmouth Sucker]].

## Key Findings

**Proposed goal and stocking approach**
- Establish a population of 5,000 adult razorback suckers in Glen Canyon NRA, with annual stockings of 1,000.
- Grow fish to about 30 cm, PIT tag them, and release them into the reservoir. The goal should be attainable in about 10 years, depending on growth rates and fish availability.
- Request an annual delivery from FWS of 5,000 razorback suckers (5 cm) and 1,000 bonytail chub. Fish over 12 cm should be PIT tagged and released in the San Juan Arm. Smaller fish should be split between the Wahweap ponds and the Iceberg Canyon backwater, with Wahweap given priority for logistical reasons.

**Proposed field program**
- **Telemetry (Oct–Nov):** Implant 10–20 Wahweap suckers (about 40 cm) with transmitters, which have a 14-month battery life. Goals include tracking stocked fish, spawning locations, wild fish locations, and other spawning areas in the lake.
- **Backwater survey (Jun–Jul):** Survey Iceberg Canyon over 2–3 days. Measure depth profile, surface area, and volume. Record water quality parameters: dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, and temperature, including diel profiles. Sample fish with trammel and gill nets, then release them.
- **Renovation (Aug–Sept):** Apply rotenone at concentrations based on the volume survey, with holding cars set up in the reservoir beforehand. Remove larger game fish quickly and move them to fresh water. Weigh all fish to estimate standing crop, repeat treatment two weeks later, and restock the backwater in spring.
- **Monitoring (spring and fall):** Track growth and possible natural recruitment in the backwater.

**Schedule and agency roles (recommended, no commitments made)**
- NEPA compliance, federal endangered species permit, and rotenone permit: August 1995 (NPS, UDWR).
- Iceberg survey: July 1995 (NPS, NBS).
- Iceberg renovation: October 1995 (NPS, NBS, UDWR).
- Stocking suckers: November 1995 (UDWR, with PIT tags from NBS and FWS).
- Telemetry equipment and surgery: November 1995 (NBS).
- Spring monitoring of the San Juan Arm: March 1996.
- Stocking of Wahweap and Iceberg: October 1996 (FWS, NPS).

**Coordination meeting outcomes (April 12, 1995)**
- Agencies agreed to integrate native and sport fishes into an ecosystem approach, handling each species or group chapter by chapter, instead of splitting the plan into sport and native sections.
- The plan should remain strategic, with annual work plans and proposals attached to specific objectives. Its objectives include establishing and maintaining a native population, updating through annual work plans, and presenting the proposal as conceptual.
- The Mueller proposal was considered early. Treatment was optimistically expected in fall, with readiness the following year, but realistically would take longer. The group agreed that a 1995 survey of water quality, biology, and physical volume was warranted.
- UDWR and Arizona Game and Fish discussed an "experimental non-essential" designation for stocked endangered fish under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The states want this to limit future restrictions on sport fishing.
- Management concerns listed include Lees Ferry Record of Decision, selective withdrawal, spillway raising and lake level increases, stocking of natives or trout, contaminants, genetics, research needs, and fish marking methods.
- Funding options: Heritage Grants, a National Resource Preservation Program proposal from the NPS for flannelmouth sucker, and partnerships.
- Conclusions: survey Iceberg first for water quality, have a draft FMP ready for the NFWG by July 1995, and keep scoping with agencies.

**Other planned actions**
- Hold an NFWG work group meeting at Iceberg Canyon on June 6–8, 1995.
- Hold the next coordination meeting on May 10, 1995, at NPS headquarters.
- Address NEPA compliance and prepare a comprehensive bibliography for Lake Powell fishery management.
