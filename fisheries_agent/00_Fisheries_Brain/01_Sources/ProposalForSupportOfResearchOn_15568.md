# Proposal for Support of Research on the Systematics and Evolution of the Fishes Endemic to the Mesa Central of Mexico

#ichthyology #systematics #Goodeidae #Atherinidae #Cyprinidae #biogeography #conservation #Mexico

## Executive Summary

This research proposal by Clyde D. Barbour (University of Utah) requests two years of funding from the National Science Foundation to conduct systematic and evolutionary studies of fishes endemic to the [[Mesa Central]] of Mexico. The project focuses on the family [[Goodeidae]] (viviparous cyprinodonts), the cyprinid genus [[Algansea]], and two monotypic cyprinid genera ([[Xystrosus]] and [[Falcularius]]). The [[Mesa Central]] represents the southern third of Mexico's Central Plateau and harbors a highly endemic fish fauna centered in the [[Lerma-Santiago River]] system. The proposal emphasizes urgent conservation concerns: habitat destruction through water diversion, pollution from sewage and industrialization, and predation from introduced [[Largemouth Bass]] threaten these restricted populations. The investigator has already completed dissertation work on [[Chirostoma]] and made 95 collections in the region. The study aims to resolve taxonomic confusion, reevaluate generic relationships using morphological and osteological characters, and develop coherent statements about evolutionary patterns including character displacement, adaptive radiation, and parallelism.

## Background & Description

### Geographic and Physiographic Context

The [[Mesa Central]] extends south from approximately the latitude of San Luis Potosi and Aguas Calientes to the Transverse Volcanic Axis near Mexico City. Elevations range from less than 1000 m at the northern extent to more than 2000 m at the southern extremity. The region is drained principally by the westward-flowing [[Lerma-Santiago River]] system, though other rivers ([[Pánuco River]], [[Balsas River]], [[Armería River]], [[Ameca River]]) have cut back into the plateau edges.

The uplifted and isolated nature of the [[Mesa Central]] has resulted in development of a highly endemic fish fauna. The [[Lerma-Santiago]] drainage and upper [[Santiago River]] above waterfalls near Guadalajara have been designated a natural fish province of Middle America by Meek (1904), Regan (1907), and Stuart (1964).

### Taxonomic Groups Under Study

**Family Goodeidae**: Approximately 30 species of viviparous cyprinodonts largely restricted to the [[Lerma-Santiago]] drainage and rivers cutting into the plateau. The last revision (Hubbs and Turner 1939) recognized 4 subfamilies, 18 genera, and 24 species based on traditional meristic characters plus ovarian structure and embryonic rectal processes (trophotaeniae). The investigator suggests this classification contains more genera than justified and requires consolidation.

Key genera include: [[Girardinichthys]], [[Lermichthys]], [[Hubbsina]], [[Ilyodon]], [[Balsadichthys]], [[Skiffia]], [[Ollentodon]], [[Neotoca]], [[Xenotoca]], [[Allotoca]], [[Goodea]], [[Ataeniobius]], and [[Characodon]].

**Genus Algansea** (Cyprinidae): Eleven nominal species (including two undescribed) of fine-scaled cyprinids ranging 3-15 inches, widely distributed in [[Lerma-Santiago]] system. Preliminary data suggest three species groups: (1) small headwater stream species ([[Algansea barbata]] and two undescribed forms), (2) lower river/lake species ([[Algansea affinis]], [[Algansea dugesi]], [[Algansea paratincella]], [[Algansea tincella]]), and (3) predominantly lacustrine species ([[Algansea rubescens]] from [[Lake Chapala]], [[Algansea lacustris]] from [[Lake Patzcuaro]]).

**Genus Xystrosus**: Monotypic genus ([[Xystrosus popoche]]) found only in [[Lake Chapala]] and [[Santiago River]] above falls. Distinguished from [[Algansea]] by upturned mouth in adults and high gill raker counts (41-67 total on first arch). Young specimens indistinguishable from [[Algansea rubescens]] without counting gill rakers, suggesting possible synonymy.

**Genus Falcularius**: Monotypic genus ([[Falcularius chapalae]]) inhabiting [[Lake Chapala]] and upper [[Santiago River]]. Closely related to [[Hybopsis alta]] and separated with difficulty, requiring systematic resolution.

### Previous Work

The principal investigator completed dissertation work on [[Chirostoma]] (Barbour 1966), personally making 95 collections on the [[Mesa Central]] over three months of fieldwork since 1962. Preliminary work includes:

- Cleared and stained representatives of 12 of 18 goodeid genera for osteological study
- Examined pregnant females of [[Xenotoca]], [[Neotoca]], [[Allotoca]], and [[Goodea]] for ovarian structure
- Examined specimens of [[Algansea]], [[Xystrosus]], and [[Falcularius]] with preliminary data collection
- Manuscript in preparation describing new [[Algansea]] species
- Examined type specimens of key species

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Conservation Urgency

The proposal emphasizes critical threats to the endemic fauna:

**Habitat Loss**: Diversion of parts of the [[Lerma River]] for cities and agriculture has reduced aquatic habitat. The investigator argues that unlike physics or chemistry, natural history studies cannot be deferred—"atoms and molecules will always be around to study" but aquatic habitats have "critically short" half-lives.

**Pollution**: Raw sewage and increasing industrialization render remaining waters unfit for life.

**Introduced Species**: Recent introduction of [[Largemouth Bass]] ([[Micropterus salmoides]]) into isolated lakes and [[Lake Chapala]] threatens native fauna. One isolated population of an undescribed subspecies of [[Chirostoma humboldtianum]] was already near extinction in 1963 due to this predator.

**Restricted Distributions**: Many goodeids are highly restricted geographically due to recent volcanic activity, qualifying as endangered species. The proposal warns: "If the opportunity to study these fishes is not seized quickly, it may well be lost forever."

### Habitat Characteristics

Field notes routinely document:
- General habitat description and photographs
- Dominant plant identification
- Water width, flow, turbidity, temperature
- Bottom conditions
- Depth of capture
- Weather conditions

### Biogeographic Patterns

Distribution patterns reflect known geological history of central Mexico. [[Goodeidae]] occur throughout [[Lerma-Santiago]] basin, [[Armería River]], [[Ameca River]], smaller drainages between [[Ameca River]] and [[Mezquital River]], and headwaters of [[Balsas River]] and [[Pánuco River]]. One species ([[Skiffia bilineata]] or similar) is isolated in headwaters of [[Mezquital River]] in Durango state—a small enclave of Lerma-Santiago fishes (one goodeid, one atherinid) representing a major biogeographic exception.

## Key Findings

### Research Objectives

The proposal outlines specific systematic goals:

**1. Family Goodeidae**
- Ascertain limits of specific variation to define valid species
- Reevaluate generic relationships (consolidation likely needed)
- Determine phylogenetic position within suborder Cyprinodontoidei

**2. Genus Algansea**
- Define valid species through variation analysis
- Determine relationships to other North American Cyprinidae

**3. Genera Falcularius and Xystrosus**
- Resolve relationships to other Mesa Central cyprinids
- Assess generic validity (both may be synonymous with other genera)

**4. Evolutionary Synthesis**
- Develop coherent statement on fish evolution on [[Mesa Central]]
- Investigate character displacement as driver of morphological diversity (particularly in [[Chirostoma]])
- Examine evolutionary parallelism at generic and specific levels
- Correlate distribution patterns with past geologic events

### Methodological Approach

**Field Methods**:
- 10-foot nylon common-sense seines for primary collecting
- Gill and trammel nets set from canoe in larger rivers and lakes
- Specimens preserved in 10% formalin in 3-5 gallon glass buckets and half-gallon jars
- Color photographs and detailed notes from live/fresh specimens
- Live specimens retained for laboratory observation when possible

**Laboratory Methods**:

*External Morphology*:
- Standard ichthyological counts and measurements on large series throughout ranges
- Ontogenetic series examination for character changes
- Color pattern variation documentation
- Search for new diagnostic characters

*Internal Morphology*:
- Gross examination of large ovarian series to establish variation limits (following Mendoza 1965)
- Histological investigation if necessary
- Embryonic anal process (trophotaeniae) morphology and variation
- Complete osteological descriptions using cleared and stained specimens

### Taxonomic Problems Identified

**Goodeidae Classification Issues**:

The 1939 Hubbs and Turner revision may over-split genera. Preliminary examination suggests:

- **Subfamily Girardinichthyinae**: [[Ilyodon]] and [[Balsadichthys]] appear congeneric; [[Skiffia]], [[Ollentodon]], and [[Neotoca]] may be synonymous; [[Girardinichthys]] and [[Lermichthys]] appear identical; relationship of [[Hubbsina turneri]] unclear

- **Subfamily Goodeinae**: Appears to be miscellaneous collection of unrelated morphological types; large number of species groups may be unavoidable but careful analysis may reveal overlooked relationships

- **Subfamily Ataeniobiinae**: Monotypic ([[Ataeniobius toweri]]), considered most primitive goodeid due to lack of embryonic nutritive rectal processes

- **Subfamily Characodontinae**: Monotypic ([[Characodon lateralis]]), separated from former congeners based on ovarian structure and rectal processes

**Ovarian Structure Reliability**: Personal communication with Dr. R. R. Miller and examination of specimens reveal ovarian structure may not be as reliable taxonomically as previously thought. The goodeid ovary is a compound structure (fused right and left organs), introducing unusual variation. Mendoza (1965) found considerable variation in ovarian structure of "[[Characodon]]" eiseni, suggesting critical review needed.

**Algansea Species Groups**: Gill raker counts (Table 1) support three groups:
- Headwater species: 5-12 rakers
- River/lake species: 12-19 rakers  
- Lacustrine species: 19-28 rakers

Aberrant gill raker counts in [[Algansea rubescens]] from [[Santiago River]] (occasionally reaching 32) suggest possible hybridization with [[Xystrosus popoche]] (41-67 rakers, Table 2).

**Generic Validity Questions**:
- [[Xystrosus]]: Young indistinguishable from [[Algansea rubescens]] without gill raker counts; similar situation to subspecies of [[Siphateles bicolor]] in Lahontan system
- [[Falcularius]]: Separated from [[Hybopsis alta]] only with great difficulty

### Evolutionary Hypotheses

**Character Displacement**: Proposed as partial explanation for diverse morphology in various species, particularly [[Chirostoma]]

**Evolutionary Parallelism**: Considerable parallelism suspected at both generic and specific levels

**Adaptive Radiation**: Groups exemplify adaptive radiation patterns in isolated highland aquatic systems

**Geological Correlation**: Distribution patterns will be correlated with past geologic events (Clements 1963; Alvarez 1966), including Pleistocene lake history and volcanic activity

### Philosophical Approach

The proposal emphasizes that while primarily taxonomic, the approach recognizes "biological relationships are more than morphological phenomena." Ecology and behavior information is sought to augment morphological findings. Taxonomic schemes should reflect evolutionary relationships whenever possible, transcending "straight" taxonomy. The investigator acknowledges the two-year project cannot be completed fully; evaluation and new proposals will follow.
