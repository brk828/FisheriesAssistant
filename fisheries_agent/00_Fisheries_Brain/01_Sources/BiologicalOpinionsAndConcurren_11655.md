## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory compendium — not a primary morphological or ecological study — summarizing **Endangered Species Act (ESA) Section 7 biological opinions and concurrences** issued by the Arizona Ecological Services Field Office (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) between 1982 and September 1994. It catalogs consultations between federal agencies (Bureau of Reclamation, U.S. Forest Service, Corps of Engineers, BLM, Federal Highway Administration, Soil Conservation Service, EPA, FWS) and FWS regarding project impacts on **listed and candidate fish species** across the [[Colorado River Basin]], principally within the [[Gila River Basin|Gila]], [[Salt River|Salt]], and [[Verde River]] sub-basins in Arizona. The record spans dam operations, habitat restoration, livestock grazing plans, road/bridge construction, water diversions, fish barrier installation, stocking programs, and water-quality standard-setting. While it does not provide morphometric or life-history data, it is a critical dataset for **conservation status, regulatory history, and habitat management** of numerous imperiled southwestern fishes, including the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Woundfin]], and [[Apache Trout]].

## Background & Description

The source contains **no morphological or anatomical descriptions** of the fish species — it is purely an administrative/regulatory tracking document. Species are identified only by scientific binomial/trinomial nomenclature. Notable identity clarifications extractable from taxonomy:

- **[[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]]** (Gila topminnow) — two subspecies tracked: *occidentalis* and *sonoriensis*.
- **[[Gila (Genus)|Gila]] spp.** — multiple congeners treated as distinct listed taxa: *Gila elegans* ([[Bonytail Chub]]), *Gila cypha* ([[Humpback Chub]]), *Gila robusta seminuda* / *Gila seminuda* ([[Virgin River Chub]]), *Gila purpurea* ([[Yaqui Chub]]), *Gila ditaenia* ([[Sonora Chub]]) — reflecting the taxonomic complexity and endemism within this genus in the basin.
- **[[Lepidomeda]] spp.** — *Lepidomeda vittata* ([[Little Colorado Spinedace]]) and *Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis* ([[Virgin Spinedace]]), both small-bodied cyprinids named for spinous fin rays.
- **[[Meda fulgida]]** ([[Spikedace]]) and **[[Tiaroga cobitis]]** ([[Loach Minnow]]) — small benthic cyprinids frequently co-occurring and jointly managed.
- **[[Xyrauchen texanus]]** ([[Razorback Sucker]]) — named for its distinctive dorsal "razorback" keel (not described in text but implicit in common name).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although lacking direct ecological data, the document implicitly maps habitat associations through the consultation subjects:

- **Spring/pool specialists**: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], and [[Yaqui Chub]] are repeatedly tied to isolated desert springs (e.g., Quitobaquito Springs, Cienega Creek, San Bernardino NWR ponds, Cold Spring/Big Spring), reflecting their reliance on **remnant spring/pool refugia** rather than mainstem rivers — a defining feature of the arid Southwest's disjunct aquatic habitats.
- **Perennial stream/riffle specialists**: [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] appear in projects involving stream diversions, bank protection, and grazing management on the Gila, San Francisco, and White River drainages, consistent with their known preference for shallow, swift riffle habitat over sand/gravel substrates.
- **Mainstem large-river species**: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]] are the focus of large infrastructure consultations — Hoover Dam flood control, Glen Canyon Dam operations, Lake Mead/Lake Mohave management, and CAP nonnative fish introduction — indicating their dependence on the **Colorado River mainstem and associated reservoirs**, and their vulnerability to altered flow regimes and nonnative species interactions.
- **High-elevation coldwater specialist**: [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*) is associated exclusively with National Forest Plan consultations (Apache-Sitgreaves, Coronado, Kaibab, Prescott), reflecting its restriction to cool, high-elevation forested streams.
- **Riparian-dependent taxa**: Repeated appearance of livestock exclosure, grazing allotment, and riparian vegetation management consultations (e.g., West Fork Allotment, Bar V Bar/Campaign AMP, Cienega Creek fencing) underscores the strong linkage between **riparian vegetation integrity and fish habitat quality** for topminnow, spinedace, spikedace, and loach minnow populations.
- **Threats identified**: turbidity/sedimentation from construction and grazing, floodplain/channel modification, water diversion and depletion, nonnative species introduction (sport fish stocking conflicts), and water quality degradation (selenium, mercury, lipophilic compounds cited in the 1992 Arizona water quality standards consultation).

## Key Findings

- **Volume and scope**: The compilation documents roughly **60+ discrete consultations** over 12 years, reflecting intensive regulatory engagement driven by the high concentration of federally listed fishes in Arizona's Colorado River Basin tributaries.
- **Predominant outcome**: The overwhelming majority of opinions concluded **"non-jeopardy"** or **"is not likely to adversely affect"** (beneficial concurrence), with only a small number of **jeopardy determinations** — notably:
  - [[Arizona Water Quality Standards]] consultation (92-550) — jeopardy and adverse modification of critical habitat for several chemical criteria affecting *Oncorhynchus apache*, *Gila elegans*, *Gila cypha*, *Gila ditaenia*, *Gila seminuda*, *Gila purpurea*, *Cyprinodon macularius*, *Poeciliopsis occidentalis*, *Lepidomeda vittata*, *Tiaroga cobitis*, *Xyrauchen texanus*, *Meda fulgida*, *Plagopterus argentissimus*, and *Cyprinella formosa*.
  - **CAP Nonnative Fish Introduction** — jeopardy determination.
