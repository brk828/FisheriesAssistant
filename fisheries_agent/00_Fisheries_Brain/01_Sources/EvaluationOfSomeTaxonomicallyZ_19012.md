# Hypogean Fauna of the Dinaric Karst: Taxonomic, Zoogeographic and Ecological Notes

#speleobiology #stygobiology #crustacea #sphaeromatidae

## Executive Summary

This short paper by [[Boris Sket]] (University E. Kardelj, Ljubljana), published in the *Int. Espeleol. Comun.* proceedings, reviews notable cave-water finds in Yugoslavia accumulated over several decades. It covers the taxonomy, [[Zoogeography]] and [[Ecology]] of subterranean animals of the [[Dinaric Karst]]. The author argues that the cave fauna is far more diverse than earlier work suggested, that the [[Marine Relict Hypothesis]] is overstated, and that many anchihaline and cave taxa have freshwater origins. The document also briefly discusses [[Groundwater Pollution]] studies in the [[Postojna-Planina Cave System]]. The abstract is given in Catalan, Spanish and English.

## Background & Description

The hypogean fauna of Yugoslavia is described as extremely diverse, a fact known since the beginnings of [[Speleobiology]]. Some unique higher-taxon representatives were recorded early, including:

- [[Proteus Anguinus]] (Laurenti, 1768), the [[Olm]]
- [[Marifugia Cavatica]] (Absolon & Hrabe, 1930), a serpulimorph polychaete

Other findings lost their uniqueness when later discoveries outside Yugoslavia were made. For example, *Dina absoloni* is no longer the only troglobitic leech species. The number of less prominent taxa is steadily increasing.

Taxon-level notes from the paper:

- **Gastropoda:** Stygobitic gastropods belong mainly to Mesogastropoda, with some Pulmonata (e.g., Acroloxidae). About 135 recognized species represent roughly 40% of the world register of such taxa.
- **Copepoda Calanoida:** Fewer than a dozen troglobitic species are known worldwide. Two new endemic genera were described from Yugoslavia, *Troglodiaptomus sketi* and *Stygodiaptomus kieferi* (Petkovski, 1978, 1981). One of them also occurs in Italy.
- **Amphipoda:** Rapidly increasing, especially in the genus [[Niphargus]]. Karaman (1974) listed nearly 60 species (not all hypogean), plus many subspecies.
- **Isopoda (Sphaeromatidae, genus *Monolistra*):** Known taxa rose from 4 in 1910 to 20 in 1967 and 30 in 1986. Only 8 taxa occur outside Yugoslavia, so the group is one of the commonest cave-water settlers there.
- **Other groups:** Single or few representatives of Ciliata (*Diafolliculina hadzii*, the only cavernicolous Spirotrichea), freshwater sponges (*Eunapius subterraneus*, the only troglobitic Spongillidae), Turbellaria Temnocephalida (about a dozen *Scutariella*, *Stygodytocola* and related species), Triclada (*Microplana* sp., immature specimens only), Hydrozoa (*Velkovrhia enigmatica*, the only troglobitic cnidarian), Bivalvia (*Congeria kusceri*, the Dreissenidae), and Diotocardia (*Theodoxus subterrelictus*).

No troglobitic fishes (Pisces) have been found in Yugoslavia or in Europe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Zoogeographic regions.** Four indistinctly delimited zoogeographical regions are recognized in the Dinaric karst:

- NW and SE continental regions, long postulated by [[Jeannel]] (1928) as distinct. Their boundaries are losing sharpness, though not their validity, as holodinaric elements such as *Proteus*, *Marifugia* and *Velkovrhia* are recognized.
- An epilittoral zone of continental character, with fully fresh water, marked by *Sphaeromides virei*.
- A paralittoral zone of anchihaline cave waters, brackish or with an explicitly brackish-water fauna. Its distribution appears to be shaped mainly by recent ecological conditions.

**Anchihaline fauna.** Widespread species include *Niphargus hebereri*, *Hadzia fragilis*, *Monodella argentarii* and *Diacyclops antrincola*. All show clear preference for the lowest salinities, or for fresh water where competitors are absent. The Kvarner-Velebit region lacks the typical anchihaline taxa, probably because a freshwater lake persisted there through the last transgression.

**Historical framework.** The Dinaric area has been almost as land-covered as today since the Miocene, and part of the Istrian Peninsula was already land in the Paleogene. The author proposes that the present anchihaline fauna lived in fresh water between the Paleogene and the Miocene desiccation, was pushed seaward by stronger freshwater competitors, and regained its range during the last Quaternary transgression.

**Monolistra and river systems.** The distribution of *Monolistra* taxa in SE Slovenia is compared with hypothetical pre-karst river systems. Molecular dating suggests the genus speciated during the Pliocene, in epigean rivers, before invading the subterranean realm.

**Pollution and food.** Studies in the Postojna-Planina Cave System show that the stream's self-purifying capacity is limited by complex hydrology. Nitrate accumulates after organic matter degrades, and oxygen saturation is reached only some kilometres below the insurgence of the polluted [[Pivka River]]. Increased organic pollution is linked to the penetration of epigean animals underground, which suggests food availability strongly influences competitive success in hypogean habitats.

## Key Findings

- **Unique troglobitic representatives:** The paper identifies the only known troglobitic taxa in several classes: Spongillidae (*Eunapius subterraneus*), Hydrozoa (*Velkovrhia enigmatica*), Dreissenidae (*Congeria kusceri*, probably the only troglobitic bivalve), and Diotocardia (*Theodoxus subterrelictus*).
- **Diversity:** About 135 stygobitic gastropod species (~40% of the world total), nearly 60 amphipod species, and 30 *Monolistra* taxa in Yugoslavia.
- **No troglobitic fishes:** None are known from Yugoslavia or Europe.
- **Freshwater origin of the cave fauna:** The author challenges the [[Marine Origin Hypothesis]] of cave fauna. The anchihaline fauna and Malacostraca (*Monolistra*) are argued to derive from epigean freshwaters, following [[Chappuis]] (1926, 1927).
- **Regional structure:** The NW and SE continental regions and the epilittoral and paralittoral zones form a biogeographic framework, with the Kvarner-Velebit region as a notable exception.
- **Pollution dynamics:** Self-purification in the Postojna system is limited, and food input governs the success of epigean colonists in caves.
