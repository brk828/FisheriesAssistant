# Fossil Trout *Salmo cyniclope* and the Subgenera of *Ambrysus* (Naucoridae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #paleontology #naucoridae

## Executive Summary

This source contains two separate items by [[Ira La Rivers]] of Verdi, Nevada. The main item is a 1964 description of a new fossil trout, *Salmo cyniclope* sp. nov., from Barstovian (Upper Miocene) tuffaceous strata at Rabbithole, Pershing County, Nevada. The description rests on fragmentary cranial remains, with the palate, jaws and maxilla used as diagnostic features. The second item is a short 1965 taxonomic introduction to the genus *Ambrysus* (Hemiptera, Naucoridae), a large, intergrading genus whose subgenera are difficult to separate. The text ends mid-introduction, so the subgeneric revision itself is not included.

## Background & Description

**Fossil trout (*Salmo cyniclope*)**
- Medium-sized trout, estimated at about 20 inches (≈51 cm) standard length. The author states it differs in no generic way from modern trout.
- Material consists of several crushed fragments from the type locality, which show a noticeable size range. The holotype is [[Biological Society of Nevada]] specimen BSN #50A, a crushed skull with both dentaries, a left [[Maxillary Bone]], parts of the premaxillaries, a robust left [[Palatine Bone]], and possibly the hyomandibular and dermethmoid.
- The left dentary is 60 mm long, with an angle of 28°. The author attributes the low angle mainly to flattening during crushing.
- The left maxillary is 55 mm long, with a median width of 7 mm. The jaws are interpreted as heavier and more massive than in modern species, though crushing exaggerates their apparent width.
- The anal fin is nearly complete and resembles the shorter fin of *Salmo* rather than the longer fin of *Oncorhynchus*.
- Possible modern relatives are named as [[Cutthroat Trout]] and [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo clarki*–*Salmo gairdneri*), which the author considers its possible derivatives.
- Etymology: *Cyniclus* ("rabbit") plus *-ope* ("hole"), after the type locality.

**Genus *Ambrysus***
- Large naucorid genus. In subfamily Ambrysinae it contains 73 of 77 known species, spread across four genera.
- Body length is 6–15 mm and width 3.5–8.0 mm. The dorsum is usually bicolored, with a lighter head and prothorax.
- Species are hard to separate because intergrades are abundant, though the extremes of the genus differ markedly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Matrix and depositional setting (Rabbithole)**
- The matrix is silicified tuffaceous strata. Petrographic analysis by Dr. Richard H. Olson describes an "air fall" of fine volcanic glass, cemented by secondary opaline silica. It could have been classified as a vitric tuff before cementation.

**Flora (after Dr. Daniel Axelrod)**
- The assemblage mixes lake and stream border plants with deciduous hardwood forest and scattered conifers.
- Representative taxa include *Salix hesperia*, *Betula thor*, *Quercus simulata*, *Populus* sp., *Acer columbianum*, *Liquidambar pachyphyllum*, *Quercus hannibali*, *Pinus florissanti* and *Picea* sp.
- Axelrod estimates a well-watered region with about 40 inches of yearly rain, well distributed through the seasons and moderate temperature ranges.
- By Pacific Coast Tertiary correlation the flora is Middle Miocene (Temblor-Caliente) in the marine record, but mammal paleontologists would place it in the Upper Miocene (Barstovian).

**Associated fauna**
- Unidentified pelecypods, gastropods and ostracods occur in the same horizon.
- Isolated mammal bone float fragments were found just east of the fish stratum.

**Regional comparison**
- The nearest fish-bearing beds are diatomites about 60 air-line miles SSW of Rabbithole, near Sahwave Mountains. They contain the stickleback *Gasterosteus doryssus* and the topminnow *Fundulus nevadensis*.
- These are Clarendonian (Lower Pliocene) and therefore younger than *Salmo cyniclope*.
- The Clarendonian frog [[Rana johnsoni]] (La Rivers 1953), known from a nearly complete specimen at the Eagle-Picher diatomite workings, is the only other cold-blooded Tertiary vertebrate of comparable age recorded for the area.
- The author's catalogue lists over 50 Nevada Mio-Pliocene localities that have yielded fish remains.

## Key Findings

- *Salmo cyniclope* is described as a new fossil trout from Barstovian (Upper Miocene) strata at Rabbithole, Nevada. The type is BSN #50A, and paratype-like fragment BSN #50B adds a right maxillary and suborbital bones.
- The species is more robust than modern *Salmo* in its jaws, with a 60 mm left dentary and a 55 mm maxillary.
- The flora points to a well-watered, seasonal environment with about 40 inches of annual rainfall. This contrasts with the younger, diatomite-hosted Clarendonian fishes of the same region.
- The Clarendonian correction is noted: the author had earlier recorded the regional fish beds as Pleistocene before revising them to Lower Pliocene.
- In the second item, [[Ambrysus]] comprises 73 of 77 species in Ambrysinae, and its subgeneric boundaries are hard to define because of intergrades. The taxonomic revision is not included in the excerpt.
