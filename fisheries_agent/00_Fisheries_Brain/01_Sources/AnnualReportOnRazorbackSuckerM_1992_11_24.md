# Annual Report: Razorback Sucker Mitochondrial DNA Variation Study (1992)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #population-genetics #conservation-genetics

> Note: This study concerns a **freshwater** catostomid species native to the Colorado River Basin.

## Executive Summary

This third-year annual report (submitted by Thomas E. Dowling, Arizona State University, to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation) presents mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) restriction fragment analysis of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations across the Colorado River Basin. Samples were analyzed from seven regions: [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Powell]], the [[Green River]]/[[Yampa River]] system, the Colorado River near [[Grand Junction]], the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), and [[Colorado River Indian Tribe]] canals. A clear geographic gradient in genotypic (haplotype) diversity was identified, with the greatest diversity in the downstream [[Lake Mohave]] population and progressively decreasing diversity moving upstream. A notable finding was the detection of putative hybrids between [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) in the Little Colorado River, with evidence suggesting a **single hybridization event** as the source of all razorback-derived mtDNA haplotypes recovered there.

## Background & Description

This report is genetic/molecular in nature and does not describe external morphology in detail. However, relevant taxonomic/hybridization notes include:

- **Focal taxon**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large-bodied catostomid endemic to the Colorado River Basin, notable for its distinctive dorsal keel.
- **Hybridization**: Putative hybrids between [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] were field-identified in the Little Colorado River and Grand Junction (Highline Lake) samples, denoted "HY" in the haplotype tables. Some hybrid individuals possessed pure *latipinnis* mtDNA, while others carried razorback mtDNA, consistent with maternal inheritance patterns expected under interspecific hybridization.
- **Sample types**: mtDNA was isolated from a range of tissue types including fin clips, blood, sperm, muscle plugs, and eggs, with variable success — small or dilute late-season spawning samples (particularly from Lake Mead) proved difficult to fully characterize.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range sampled**: Populations spanned a longitudinal/altitudinal gradient along the Colorado River system:
  - [[Lake Mohave]] (Lower Colorado River, downstream reservoir)
  - [[Lake Mead]] (Lower Colorado River reservoir)
  - [[Lake Powell]] (Upper Colorado River reservoir)
  - [[Green River]]/[[Yampa River]] confluence area (upper basin tributaries)
  - Colorado River mainstem near [[Grand Junction]] (including Highline Lake and Etter Pond)
  - [[Little Colorado River]] (a tributary where sympatry/hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] occurs)
  - [[Colorado River Indian Tribe]] agricultural canals (marginal/anthropogenic habitat)
- **Hatchery comparison population**: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] captive broodstock (originally sourced from Lake Mohave/"Buenos Aires" broodstock), sampled across 1987, 1989, and 1990 year classes, used as a genetic reference/refugium population for comparison against wild-caught fish.
- **Ecological/conservation implication**: The declining diversity gradient from Lake Mohave upstream suggests differential population history, possible bottlenecks, or founder effects in upstream populations, relevant to conservation prioritization of genetically diverse source stocks under the [[Endangered Species Act]] framework for this federally listed species.
- **Interspecific interaction**: Co-occurrence and hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] in the Little Colorado River indicates niche overlap and potential introgression pressure on remnant razorback sucker populations in tributary habitats.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling totals** (analyzed / originally specified):
  - Lake Mohave: N = 26 [25]
  - Green/Yampa River: N = 29 [25]
  - Colorado River near Grand Junction: N = 20 [25]
  - Lake Powell: N = 21 [25]
  - Lake Mead: N = 26 [25] (only 12 fully characterized due to poor sample quality)
  - Little Colorado River area: N = 12 [25]
  - Colorado River Indian Tribe canals: N = 1 [25]

- **Haplotype diversity gradient** (Table 2 summary):
  | Region | # Haplotypes | Diversity Index | Second Diversity Metric |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Mohave | 18 | 0.68 | 0.97 |
  | Mead | 6 | 0.50 | 0.82 |
  | Powell | 6 | 0.29 | 0.80 |
  | Green | 4 | 0.14 | 0.63 |
  | Grand Junction | 1 | 0.05 | 0.00 |

  This confirms a **clear downstream-to-upstream decline in genetic diversity**, with Lake Mohave harboring by far the greatest number of distinct mtDNA haplotypes (18 of 26 samples), while the Grand Junction sample was essentially monomorphic (a single haplotype dominating nearly all individuals).

- **Hybridization event in the Little Colorado River**: Of 12 putative razorback × flannelmouth hybrids analyzed, 7 carried razorback mtDNA (confirming maternal razorback ancestry) and all 7 shared an **identical single haplotype** — striking given the high diversity documented at Mohave, Mead, and Powell. This strongly suggests the LCR hybrid population originated from a **single hybridization event** rather than repeated independent crosses.

- **Interpretation pending**: Full significance of the LCR finding awaits complementary data from allozyme analysis (D. G. Buth) and otolith-based aging of specimens (Dowling lab), to determine the timing and demographic context of the hybridization event.

- **Hatchery stock characterization**: Dexter National Fish Hatchery samples (1987, 1989, 1990 year classes derived from "Buenos Aires" broodstock) were haplotyped for use as a genetic reference; several haplotypes recovered in wild populations were also found among hatchery year classes (flagged with an asterisk in Table 1), indicating shared ancestry between hatchery stock and specific wild source populations (likely Lake Mohave).

- **Technical/methodological notes**:
  - Restriction fragment analysis used eight enzymes: *MboI*, *HinpI*, *HinfI*, *TaqI*, *ScafI*, *NheI*, *BstEII*, and *BanI*.
  - Several Lake Mead and Lake Powell samples could not be scored due to insufficient tissue quantity/quality.
  - Muscle plugs collected in parallel for allozyme analysis (Buth lab) across multiple sites (Powell, Green/Yampa, Grand Junction).

- **Next steps**: Completion of full characterization (especially remaining Lake Mead samples), finalization of statistical/computer modeling of the diversity gradient, and presentation of final results at the next [[Desert Fishes Council]] meeting, contingent on additional requested funding.
