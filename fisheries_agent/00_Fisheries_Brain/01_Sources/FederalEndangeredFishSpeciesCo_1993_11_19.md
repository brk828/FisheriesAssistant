# Federal Endangered Fish Species Collection Report (Arizona, 1993)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #endangered-species #Poeciliidae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a regulatory compliance letter from a Tonto National Forest Fisheries Biologist to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), submitted under Endangered Species subpermit PRT-676811. It reports 1993 field collection totals for the federally endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) at two Arizona sites and requests renewal of collecting authorization for four additional federally listed fish taxa. The stated purpose is habitat/population monitoring on or adjacent to the Coronado, Prescott, and Tonto National Forests, supporting species recovery efforts rather than primary morphological or ecological research.

No morphological data (meristics, coloration, body proportions) are provided in this source. The document is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not describe physical characteristics of any listed species. Morphological detail would need to be sourced from dedicated species accounts for:

- [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*)

- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- All species referenced are **freshwater, desert-adapted fishes** native to the arid Southwestern United States, occupying isolated springs, small streams, and canyon riparian habitats — environments characterized by naturally fragmented, often thermally and hydrologically variable conditions.
- Collection sites for *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* in 1993:
  - **Redrock Canyon**, Santa Cruz Co., AZ — 324 individuals collected
  - **Charlesbois Spring**, Maricopa Co., AZ — 10 individuals collected
- These localities lie on or near the [[Coronado National Forest]], [[Prescott National Forest]], and [[Tonto National Forest]], indicating reliance on National Forest System lands for surviving relict populations of desert fishes.
- The permit framework implies these species persist in small, disjunct refugial populations requiring active monitoring — consistent with the broader ecological status of Southwestern endemic fishes threatened by habitat loss, water diversion, and invasive species (e.g., mosquitofish competition with *Poeciliopsis occidentalis*).
- Collection methods specified (electrofishing, seining, handnetting) are standard low-mortality techniques used for population monitoring of sensitive/endangered aquatic species, with live specimens returned to capture sites and only unavoidable mortalities retained as vouchers.

## Key Findings
- **Reported 1993 take**: 334 total [[Gila Topminnow]] specimens collected across two sites (Redrock Canyon: 324; Charlesbois Spring: 10) under existing subpermit PRT-676811.
- **Renewal request** sought authorization for a substantially larger multi-species collection effort:
  - 10 × [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*)
  - 10 × [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
  - 10 × [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
  - 50 × [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
  - 1,000 × [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)
- All five species listed are federally protected under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, reflecting the severe conservation status of native Southwestern freshwater ichthyofauna.
- Voucher specimens (from unavoidable mortalities) were to be accessioned to the **Museum of Fishes, Arizona State University**, establishing a permanent scientific record supporting long-term monitoring and taxonomic verification.
- The collection program's stated dual purpose — **scientific research and species recovery** — situates this report within broader USFWS-mandated recovery planning for endangered desert fish assemblages on U.S. National Forest lands in Arizona.
