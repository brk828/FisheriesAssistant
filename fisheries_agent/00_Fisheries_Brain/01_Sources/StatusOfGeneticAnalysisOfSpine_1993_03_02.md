## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker, AZ office), authored by Chuck Minckley, serving as liaison for an informal interagency "[[Lepidomeda Workgroup]]" focused on the conservation status of **spinedace** populations (genus *[[Lepidomeda]]*) in the Little Colorado River drainage of Arizona. Rather than presenting original morphological or ecological research, the memo summarizes the status of an ongoing **population genetics study** led by Tom Dowling (Arizona State University) and outlines a proposal by Arizona Game and Fish (nongame program) to establish a formal annual monitoring program for spinedace, potentially in cooperation with USFWS, the U.S. Forest Service, and private landowners. No quantitative biological, morphological, or ecological data are reported in this source; the content is procedural/administrative in nature.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., meristic counts, coloration, body proportions, or diagnostic features) are provided in this source document. The text pertains solely to sample logistics for a genetic analysis project and does not describe physical characteristics of *[[Lepidomeda]]* species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Small headwater and tributary streams within the **Little Colorado River drainage**, Arizona — a freshwater lotic system, not a marine or oceanic basin.
- **Named collection localities** (implying known extant spinedace populations at time of writing):
  - [[Rudd Creek]]
  - [[East Clear Creek]] (Dines Tank site)
  - [[Little Colorado River]] near St. Johns, AZ
  - [[Nutrioso Creek]] (at State Road 666)
  - [[Lower Chevelon Creek]]
  - [[Silver Creek]] (Arizona)
- These localities collectively suggest a **fragmented, multi-population distribution** across tributaries of the Little Colorado River — a pattern consistent with concerns about genetic isolation and the rationale for population-level genetic sampling.
- The memo references potential discovery of **additional undocumented populations**, indicating incomplete distributional knowledge of the species at the time.

## Key Findings
- **Genetic sampling status (T. Dowling, ASU):**
  - Complete sample sets already obtained from: [[Rudd Creek]], [[East Clear Creek]], [[Little Colorado River]] (St. Johns), and [[Nutrioso Creek]] — no further specimens needed from these sites.
  - Partial sampling from **Lower Chevelon Creek** (6 fish collected); **19 additional specimens** requested to complete the dataset.
  - **25 specimens** requested from **Silver Creek**, not yet sampled.
  - Dowling expressed willingness to incorporate **any newly discovered populations** into the genetic analysis.
- **Conservation/management proposal:**
  - Arizona Game and Fish Department (Nongame Branch) was preparing a **Heritage Fund proposal** to establish **annual monitoring** of spinedace populations.
  - If funded, this was seen as a catalyst for a **cooperative multi-agency monitoring program** involving [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], and private stakeholders, with AZGFD Nongame in a lead role.
- **Institutional coordination:**
  - Following a 26 February 1993 meeting in Flagstaff, AZ, the author (Chuck Minckley) assumed an informal liaison role among agencies and researchers working on spinedace conservation, informally dubbed the "**[[Lepidomeda Workgroup]]**."
  - The stated purpose of this liaison role was to **prevent duplicative sampling and research efforts** across entities by facilitating information-sharing on ongoing and planned spinedace-related projects.
- **Data limitation note:** This source is a procedural coordination memo, not a primary research article; it lacks quantitative ecological, morphological, or population-genetic results. It is valuable primarily as a historical record of early 1990s conservation planning and interagency coordination for imperiled *[[Lepidomeda]]* spinedace populations in Arizona.
