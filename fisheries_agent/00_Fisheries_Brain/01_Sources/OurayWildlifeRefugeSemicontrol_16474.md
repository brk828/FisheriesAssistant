# Semi-Control of Flood Inflow to Pond Unit System - Scheme 3

#aquatic-engineering #habitat-management #fish-passage

## Executive Summary

This engineering schematic (Scheme 3) presents a comprehensive flood control and aquatic habitat management design for a multi-unit pond system. The plan integrates sediment settling, fish passage via canal systems, and water level management across multiple pond units (L-1 through L-8 and Unit 10) with water surface elevations ranging from 4660 to 4667 feet. The design emphasizes semi-controlled flood inflow, sediment deposition areas, and infrastructure modifications to facilitate both water conveyance and [[Fish Migration]] through the system.

## Background & Description

The project site consists of interconnected pond units designated L-1 through L-8 and Unit 10, managed by FLO Engineering Inc. of Breckenridge, Colorado. The system features:

- **Water Surface Elevations**: Ranging from 4660 ft (Unit L-7) to 4667 ft (Main Dike L-4)
- **Infrastructure**: Main canal system, existing dikes, culverts (primarily 24" corrugated metal pipe), and levees
- **Odd-numbered ponds**: Designated for specific management purposes
- **Existing components**: Islands, canals, levees, and culvert network requiring modification

The design references Scheme 7 for potential improvements to the inlet area at the main channel, indicating this is part of a larger suite of management alternatives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The scheme explicitly addresses multiple ecological functions:

1. **[[Fish Passage]]**: The main canal is designated to "convey water & fish," indicating the system supports fish populations requiring connectivity between pond units
2. **[[Sediment Management]]**: Ponded areas are strategically used to settle sediment, with a dedicated sediment deposition area identified near Unit L-2 (WS elevation 4666 ft)
3. **Habitat connectivity**: The removal and reconstruction of canal outlets, combined with regrading of active channels, suggests optimization for aquatic organism movement
4. **Water quality**: Sediment settling areas improve water quality downstream by reducing suspended solids

The system appears designed to balance flood control with maintenance of aquatic habitat quality and connectivity.

## Key Findings

**Primary Design Components:**

1. **Sediment Control**: Use ponded areas to settle sediment; dedicated sediment deposition area established
2. **Pond Unit Modifications**: Review and redesign of pond unit inlets and outlets throughout the system
3. **Canal System**: 
   - Main canal to convey water and fish
   - Clean canal and reconstruct outlet from main canal
   - Remove existing canal sections as specified
4. **Hydraulic Structures**:
   - Construct low head weir with riprap stabilization
   - Construct jetty to raise water surface elevation
   - Install proposed culverts at strategic locations
5. **Grading & Drainage**:
   - Regrade ponds for improved drainage (Units L-1, L-3, L-5, L-7 specified)
   - Remove vegetation and regrade active channel to weir
6. **Dike Modifications**:
   - Extend existing dikes
   - Soil moisture dike 7 maintained
   - Main dikes at elevations 4665 ft (L-6) and 4667 ft (L-4)
7. **Existing Infrastructure**: Eight culvert locations marked (L-DR through L-8C), all 24" corrugated metal pipe, with some locations having dual pipes

**Water Management Strategy**: The semi-control approach allows managed flood inflow while maintaining specific water surface elevations across the pond network, facilitating both flood attenuation and aquatic habitat functions. The elevation gradient (4660-4667 ft) enables gravity-driven flow between units while the intake and pump structure provides active water level control.
