## Executive Summary

This document is primarily a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service policy notice discontinuing the "Category-2 candidate" species designation under the [[Endangered Species Act]], shifting reliance to external databases such as [[The Nature Conservancy]]'s Natural Heritage Central Database for tracking species of conservation concern. Embedded within this same Federal Register issue is a **National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS)** final rule of direct ichthyological relevance: a regulatory amendment to the **Fishery Management Plan (FMP) for the Reef Fish Fishery of Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands**, which adjusts spawning aggregation closures for **[[Red Hind]]** (*Epinephelus guttatus*) and makes a technical boundary adjustment to a **[[Mutton Snapper]]** (*Lutjanus analis*) spawning aggregation closure off St. Croix, USVI. The rule responds to documented population declines in red hind attributed to fishing pressure on spawning aggregations.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., length, weight, meristics) for red hind or mutton snapper are provided in this source. The document is regulatory/policy in nature rather than a biological survey. Species are referenced only by common and scientific taxonomic identity within the context of the [[Reef Fish Fishery]] of Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Red Hind]]** (*Epinephelus guttatus*, family [[Serranidae]]) forms discrete **spawning aggregations** at specific reef bank sites in the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) off western Puerto Rico, including the **Tourmaline Bank** area. These aggregations are seasonal and site-specific, making the species vulnerable to concentrated fishing pressure during reproductive periods.
- New survey information indicated that a portion of the originally designated Tourmaline Bank closure area consisted of **habitat unsuitable for red hind**, prompting a boundary reduction concurrent with the addition of **two new spawning aggregation closure areas** elsewhere in Puerto Rican/USVI waters.
- **[[Mutton Snapper]]** (*Lutjanus analis*, family [[Lutjanidae]]) also maintains a known spawning aggregation site off the **southwest coast of St. Croix, USVI**; the rule enacted a minor technical boundary correction here to harmonize federal and USVI territorial regulations.
- Both species occupy **coral reef and reef bank ecosystems** and rely on transient, aggregation-based spawning behavior typical of many tropical grouper and snapper species — a reproductive strategy that renders them acutely susceptible to overfishing if aggregation sites and timing are not protected.
- The Caribbean Fishery Management Council was concurrently evaluating a network of **marine reserves** (0–9 nautical miles offshore), including one at Tourmaline Bank, to broadly protect reef fish, corals, and reef invertebrates — indicating red hind habitat is nested within a broader multi-taxa reef conservation framework.

## Key Findings

- **Population decline**: NMFS and the Center for Marine Conservation (CMC) concurred that red hind populations off Puerto Rico were in **continuing decline**, attributed largely to fishing pressure during spawning aggregation periods.
- **Management action**: The final rule (effective December 7, 1996) amended the FMP to:
  1. Reduce/adjust the boundary of the existing red hind spawning aggregation closure off western Puerto Rico (Tourmaline Bank) based on updated habitat suitability data.
  2. Establish **two additional red hind spawning aggregation seasonal/area closures**.
  3. Make a technical boundary correction to the mutton snapper spawning aggregation closure off St. Croix, USVI, for consistency with territorial regulations.
- **Rejected/deferred measure**: A proposal to **prohibit the sale of red hind during the closed spawning season** was considered but not adopted; the Council indicated it would revisit this measure if area closures failed to reverse population declines — an implicit acknowledgment that spatial/temporal closures alone may be insufficient conservation tools for aggregation-spawning reef fish.
- **Habitat-based refinement**: The reduction in the Tourmaline Bank closure area demonstrates an evidence-based adjustment approach — closures were refined once monitoring data showed portions of protected habitat were not actually utilized by spawning red hind, reflecting habitat-specificity in aggregation site selection.
- **Cross-agency conservation context**: This action occurred alongside broader efforts (marine reserve planning in Puerto Rican territorial waters, 0–9 nm) to protect reef fish, corals, and invertebrates collectively, situating red hind and mutton snapper conservation within an ecosystem-level management effort rather than single-species regulation.
- **Non-fish policy content**: The bulk of the document (USFWS candidate species policy) establishes that "Category-2" species — a designation that had informally functioned as a watch-list for potentially at-risk taxa, including some freshwater and diadromous fish species considered under the Act — would no longer be tracked by the Service, with responsibility shifting to the [[Natural Heritage Central Database]] and rankings such as G1–G3/T1–T3 status categories.
