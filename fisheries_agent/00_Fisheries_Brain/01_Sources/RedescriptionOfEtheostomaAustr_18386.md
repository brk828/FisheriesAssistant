# Redescription of Etheostoma australe and a Key to Mexican Etheostoma (Percidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This Occasional Paper by [[Lawrence M. Pace]] (University of Kansas Museum of Natural History, 1981) redescribes the [[Conchos Darter]], *Etheostoma australe*, a small percid endemic to the [[Rio Conchos]] system of Chihuahua and Durango, Mexico. The redescription is based on a series of 26 specimens collected on 24 February 1979 at General Trias, Chihuahua, which provided the first accurate colour description and documented ripe, spawning fish. The paper also reassesses the subgeneric placement of the species (*Austroperca* versus *Oligocephalus*) and provides a key to the Mexican darters of the genus.

## Background & Description

- **Nomenclatural history:** Originally described in 1859 as *Diplesion fasciatus* Girard, later redescribed in 1889 as *Etheostoma australe* Jordan. The name *fasciatus* is preoccupied in *Etheostoma*.
- **Prior work:** Earlier studies by [[Jordan and Evermann]] (1896), [[Meek]] (1904), [[Bailey and Gosline]] (1955; vertebral counts), [[Collette]] (1965; absence of breeding tubercles in examined specimens), and [[Page]] (1977; lateralis system) provided only limited information.
- **Diagnosis:** A member of *Etheostoma* with one large, stiff anal spine, a premaxillary frenum, an incomplete lateral line (23-50 pored scales), and an unscaled nape and breast (or nape with few posterior scales).
- **Meristics:** Lateral scales 51-71 (modally 55-65); pored lateral-line scales 20-53 (modally 35-45); scales above lateral line 5-8; below lateral line 7-10; transverse scales 13-20; caudal peduncle scales 24-32; dorsal spines 10-14 (modally 11-12); dorsal rays 9-12 (modally 10-11); pectoral rays 10-12 (modally 11); anal spine 1; anal rays 6-9 (modally 7).
- **Breeding male:** Red-brown with 8-11 wide dark green vertical bars, green saddles on the mid-dorsum, a red first dorsal fin with a blue margin, and prominent black suborbital and postorbital markings. Breeding tubercles occur along fin rays, scales of the belly, and the caudal peduncle.
- **Female:** Green dorsally and yellow ventrally, with less prominent side bars and three vertically aligned black spots at the caudal base.
- **Size:** Largest female 50.5 mm standard length; largest male 49.7 mm (both INHS 84082).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** Restricted to the [[Rio Conchos]], a major tributary of the [[Rio Grande]], in Chihuahua and Durango. Material examined came from the Rio Santa Isabel, Rio San Pedro, Rio Parral, and Rio Florido.
- **Habitat:** On 24 February 1979 specimens were taken in a fast rubble riffle; seining of adjacent pools yielded none. Males made up 21 of 26 individuals captured and may have established breeding stations in the riffles.
- **Feeding and habitat (after Contreras-Balderas, 1977):** A benthic insectivore, rare overall but locally common, living on sand or gravel in shallow, fast, cool streams.
- **Reproduction:** Meek (1904) gave late May and June as the spawning period; the 1979 specimens suggest spawning may begin as early as late February.
- **Geographic variation:** Scale and fin-ray counts were highest in Rio San Pedro and its tributary Rio Santa Isabel, intermediate in Rio Florido, and lowest in Rio Parral. The variation did not match a north-south or east-west pattern. The author attributes this to limited specimens and to unknown drainage history of the Rio Conchos.
- **Related Mexican darters:** [[Etheostoma grahami]], [[Etheostoma pottsi]] (Rio Conchos, Rio Nazas, Rio Trujillo, and Rio Mezquital, the last draining to the Pacific), and [[Percina macrolepida]] (Rio San Carlos, Coahuila). [[Etheostoma lepidum]] is endemic to Texas and New Mexico.

## Key Findings

- **Subgeneric status:** Jordan and Evermann (1896) erected the subgenus *Torrentaria* based on one anal spine; Hubbs (1936) renamed it *Austroperca* because of a homonym. The author concludes that the single anal spine is not a sufficiently strong character, because anal spine number varies within several other darter species. Many characteristics place *E. australe* in the subgenus [[Oligocephalus]], and the author considers *Austroperca* unwarranted.
- **Rarity of the character:** Only nine of 128 described darter species have one anal spine (or one or two in about equal frequencies).
- **Mexican key (summary):**
  1. One anal spine, broadly connected branchiostegal membranes, broad vertical dark bars on the side: *E. australe*.
  2. Two anal spines: proceed to couplet 2.
  - Couplet 2: usually 48 or more lateral scales and 21 or more caudal peduncle scales, with a blue-green marginal band on the first dorsal fin: *E. lepidum*. Otherwise (40-50 lateral scales, 17-20 caudal peduncle scales): proceed to couplet 3.
  - Couplet 3: opercle scaled, usually 9-10 dorsal spines: *E. grahami*. Opercle unscaled, usually 10-12 dorsal spines: *E. pottsi*.
- **Conservation-relevant note:** The species is locally common but rare overall and has a restricted range within a single river system.
- **Data limitations:** Many specimens are old and poorly preserved, so geographic analysis is not yet possible. Further museum material is needed.
- **Summary:** *E. australe* is unusual but not unique in having one anal spine; it is not divergent enough to justify a monotypic subgenus, and it spawns in rubble riffles from late February.
