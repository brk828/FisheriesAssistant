# Mesquite Cove: The Next Havasu Fishing Development

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries #habitat-restoration #artificial-reef

## Executive Summary
This document is a program newsletter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Parker, AZ), detailing the "Havasu Project" — a public-private partnership focused on freshwater fisheries habitat restoration and public angling access on the [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]]. The note describes the third planned facility, **Mesquite Cove**, located on the north boundary of Lake Havasu City, following prior successful developments at [[Havasu Springs]] and [[Take Off Point]]. The project combines artificial reef habitat construction, angler access infrastructure, and environmental education, framed around the concept of the "wildland/urban interface." While not a primary research paper, the document contains relevant applied fisheries management data regarding [[Artificial Reef Habitat]] design for fish reproduction and angling enhancement in a reservoir ecosystem.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Reservoir/lake cove system on the Colorado River (Lake Havasu), adjacent to riparian and wetland habitat within the [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]].
- **Artificial habitat structures**: Over 70 acres of artificial reef habitat designed by Larry Forbis to optimize both angling success and **fish reproduction**.
  - Traditional structures: "bass bungalows" and "fishing trees" (submerged brush/structure habitat).
  - Experimental/demonstration structures: floating vegetated islands intended to grow root masses into the water column, providing shade and cover for fish — a novel habitat concept being tested for performance and durability under real-world conditions.
- **Ecosystem framing**: The refuge manager characterizes the project area as a "wildland/urban interface," emphasizing the ecological connectivity between developed urban lakefront and the [[Riparian Habitat]] supporting fish, [[Aquatic Food Chain]] dynamics, and migratory bird populations.
- **Angler-fish interaction zoning**: Boating access to fishing docks is restricted via a "no entry" buoy line, but the habitat footprint intentionally extends beyond this buoy line into open water to allow boat-based angling adjacent to the reef structures — a design balancing angler access with habitat protection.

## Key Findings
- The **Havasu Project** represents an ongoing fisheries habitat initiative with two prior completed sites ([[Havasu Springs]], [[Take Off Point]]) demonstrating strong public uptake, described as evidence of "the strength of partnerships, and the popularity of recreational fishing."
- **Mesquite Cove** is the third proposed site, approved by the Havasu Project Executive Committee on March 7, 1996, sited on Fish and Wildlife Service refuge land at the edge of Lake Havasu City.
- Habitat installation (70+ acres of artificial reef) was in progress at time of writing, with a target completion by end of calendar year; terrestrial access infrastructure (parking, trails, docks) targeted for completion by fall 1997.
- The project introduces a **demonstration-scale trial of floating vegetated island habitat**, intended to be evaluated for long-term performance and durability compared to traditional submerged structure habitat (bungalows, fishing trees) — an applied habitat engineering experiment relevant to [[Artificial Reef Habitat]] design in reservoir systems.
- The initiative is explicitly multi-purpose: recreational angling access, **fish reproduction habitat enhancement**, environmental education (aquatic food chain, riparian habitat, migratory birds, resident fish populations), and scientific/observational discovery.
- Funding structure: a seven-member project partnership combined with community contributions and grants, coordinated by Project Manager Kirk Koch.
