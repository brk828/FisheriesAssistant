# Gila taxonomy

## Executive Summary
This FY92 annual report documents Project #29 of the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], led by Principal Investigator Wayne C. Starnes (National Museum of Natural History), aimed at clarifying the taxonomic status, distribution, and genetic structure of [[Gila (Genus)]] chub forms throughout the Colorado River Basin. The study integrates non-lethal biopsy sampling, morphological image analysis, allozyme electrophoresis, and mitochondrial/nuclear DNA sequencing (mtDNA cytochrome B, RAPD) to resolve whether mainstem and tributary chub populations represent discrete taxa, hybrid swarms, or a continuum shaped by introgression. The program reports strong progress in non-lethal sampling (114 new specimens in FY92, no mortality) and substantial genetic and morphological data accumulation (1,185 total specimens sampled, 457 DNA extractions, 557 allozyme runs, 423 morphological images), though final integrative analyses were still pending at time of writing, delayed partly by the unexpected death of the DNA lead researcher, Dr. Marek Kaliszewski.

## Background & Description
- Focus taxa include the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), and undescribed or intergrading forms occupying canyon and tributary habitats — collectively termed the "Gila complex."
- Morphological data were captured via **423 digital images** (target ~1,400) for shape and statistical (morphometric) analysis, derived largely from non-lethal sampling of live fish plus museum specimens.
- Morphological analysis is explicitly designed to be interpreted **in light of genetic findings**, testing whether physical characters reliably discriminate taxa or reflect a hybridized continuum.
- Non-lethal biopsy techniques (tissue/fin clips) were refined via a **Revised Sampling Protocol** (issued 27 Mar 1992); recaptured specimens from 1991 showed "extraordinary healing" of biopsy wounds, validating the methodology's low physiological impact.
- Larval fish taxonomy of Gila forms was identified as an area needing improved documentation (Objective 5), relevant to distinguishing forms at early life stages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Populations were sampled across a wide range of habitat types within the [[Colorado River Basin]], including:
  - **Mainstem canyon reaches**: [[Grand Canyon]] (Little Colorado River confluence and middle reaches), [[Cataract Canyon]], [[Desolation Canyon]], [[Yampa Canyon]].
  - **Tributaries**: Upper White River, [[Virgin River]], [[Moapa River]], [[Pahranagat Valley]], Chevelon Creek (upper Little Colorado system).
  - **Lower Basin systems**: Gila River and [[Bill Williams River]] systems, spanning 24 populations (372 chubs sampled).
- Ecological questions center on whether mainstem canyon populations represent one or more discrete taxa versus introgressive hybrids, and how isolated versus confluent tributary populations relate taxonomically to mainstem forms — directly relevant to habitat-specific management for endangered forms.
- Hatchery/refuge populations were also included: wild-caught adult [[Bonytail]] broodstock from Lake Mohave (held at Dexter National Fish Hatchery) and F1 bonytails from the Ouray Facility, linking wild population genetics to captive propagation programs.
- Sampling was constrained by jurisdictional/political factors (e.g., denial of a collecting permit by the White Mountain Apache Tribe tied to loach minnow conservation politics) and by international permitting delays (Mexico, due to agency reorganization), highlighting the socio-political ecology of basin-wide conservation sampling.

## Key Findings
- **Non-lethal sampling success**: 114 new specimens sampled in FY92 with 100% survival/release; all upper basin habitats, Grand Canyon, Virgin River, and Pahranagat Valley populations believed to harbor endangered forms were sampled non-lethally with no known mortality.
- **Cumulative sampling**: ~80–85 populations basin-wide (extant and extinct) sampled via fresh and museum material; total of 1,185 specimens sampled (867 fresh, 318 museum, including type specimens).
- **Genetic data (DNA)**: 457 specimens had DNA extracted (nearly complete for mtDNA sequencing needs); 50 samples sequenced for cytochrome B region of mtDNA as an initial taxonomically broad screen, with D-loop sequencing planned as a backup if cytochrome B variation proves insufficiently informative.
- **RAPD analysis**: 214 specimens subjected to RAPD primer screening of total DNA, generating substantial data, but analysis was stalled following the death of contractor Dr. Marek Kaliszewski (BYU); recovery plan involved digitizing data and consulting Dr. Irv Kornfield (University of Maine).
- **Allozyme electrophoresis**: Conducted by Genetics Analyses, Inc. across ~55 loci per specimen; 557 of 867 fresh specimens (64%) processed across 23 populations, analysis of population-level variation ongoing.
- **Notable taxon-specific results**: Only 6 humpback chubs (from 1991) and no additional individuals obtained from the Yampa River in FY92, despite abundant roundtail chub sampling — suggesting rarity or reduced detectability of humpback chub in that reach. 42 humpback chubs were successfully sampled from Grand Canyon.
- **Shortcomings**: Permit denial from the White Mountain Apache Tribe blocked sampling of ~5 Salt River system populations, limiting them to museum-only representation (precluding allozyme/RAPD data); Mexican permitting delays postponed sampling of 5 river systems to Spring 1993.
- **Budget**: FY92 funds provided and expended were fully matched at $297,000, with no variance.
- **Program trajectory**: Final integrative report due October 1994; the project represents a multi-year (FY90–FY94) effort explicitly designed to inform [[Endangered Species Recovery Planning]] for Colorado Basin *Gila* forms through combined taxonomic, genetic, and morphological baseline development.
