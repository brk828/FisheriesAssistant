# Battle Against Extinction: Re-establishing Endangered Native Fish of the Arid Southwest

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1992 newspaper outdoor column, written by Outdoor Editor Barry Burkhart for the [[Arizona Republic]] (Sunday, March 1, 1992), profiles efforts to re-establish populations of endangered native fish in the arid lands west of the Mississippi. It centres on [[W.L. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]] and his counterpart [[James E. Deacon]] of the [[University of Nevada Las Vegas]], who co-edited the reference book [[Battle Against Extinction]]. The column also reports a pending legal question about whether fish introductions could be used to challenge [[Sport Fishing]]. Most of the remaining text is advertising and is not summarised.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** Native fish of arid western North America and the recovery of endangered species.
- **Key figures:**
  - [[W.L. Minckley]], a wildlife professor at Arizona State University and an acknowledged expert on Arizona's native fish.
  - [[James E. Deacon]], a professor at the University of Nevada Las Vegas and Minckley's counterpart for Nevada fish.
- **Publication:** [[Battle Against Extinction]], co-edited by Minckley and Deacon and published by the [[University of Arizona Press]]. The column describes it as a planned standard reference work on attempts to save native species.
- **Anecdote:** Former students describe Minckley as laid-back, somewhat eccentric, and almost always correct. At a New Mexico scientific meeting, he answered a critic's challenge about fish samples with different scale variations. He identified the stream, and the stretch of stream, where the critic's specimens had been collected, then explained their ancestry and the reasons for the variations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Conservation problem:** Native fish of the arid Southwest are endangered, and the column reports a movement to re-establish their populations.
- **Introduction and recovery:** The column refers to an "introduction" as a recovery tool, but the OCR text is fragmentary and does not specify species, sites, or methods.
- **Legal and management tension:** A request for a legal opinion asked whether a fish introduction could be used to challenge sport fishing. According to the fragmentary text, the question was raised in a context where a matter was held in suspension "until courts could settle the matter." The source does not give the outcome.
- **Scope:** The text focuses on the Southwest (Arizona, Nevada, New Mexico) and does not provide habitat measurements, water chemistry, or population data.
