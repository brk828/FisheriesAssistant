## Executive Summary
This 1954 taxonomic note by Dill and Shapovalov definitively invalidates **Salmo rosei**, a trout described by [[Jordan And Mcgregor]] (1924) from Lake Culver (now part of the Little Five Lakes, Tulare County, California) in the high Sierra Nevada. Through historical interviews, stocking records, and re-examination of type specimens, the authors demonstrate that *S. rosei* is not a distinct species or native form, but rather a population of [[Kern River Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gilberti*, later classified as *Salmo gairdneri gilberti*) that was artificially introduced into a previously fishless lake. The paper serves as a formal taxonomic correction, resolving decades of ambiguous classification in the literature.

## Background & Description
- No morphological differences were found between type specimens of *Salmo rosei* and *Salmo gilberti* (Kern River trout).
- In 1947, **Dr. Carl L. Hubbs** and **Dr. W. I. Follett** examined all type material of both *S. gilberti* and *S. rosei* held in the [[Stanford University]] collections and found the two forms indistinguishable.
- Field observations by **Capt. O. P. Brownlow** (California Division of Fish and Game) in 1923 noted that specimens from Lake Culver, observed spawning near the outlet, did not visibly differ from the [[Kern River Rainbow Trout]], with which he was well acquainted.
- No unique diagnostic characters supporting a distinct species designation for *S. rosei* were substantiated by any subsequent examination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Type locality:** Lake Culver, identified as the largest lake in the group now known as the [[Little Five Lakes]], Tulare County, California (T. 16 S., R. 32 E., Sec. 30), at an elevation of **10,500 feet**.
- Lake Culver is tributary to **Big Arroyo**, itself a tributary of the [[Kern River]].
- The lake, along with neighboring lakes in the area, was **originally barren of fish** — a high-elevation alpine habitat with no native fish population prior to human intervention.
- **Stocking history:** Around **1916**, a cowpuncher/packer named **William Millinghausen** transplanted adult trout caught by hook and line from Big Arroyo into the previously fishless Lake Culver, per testimony relayed to Brownlow and documented by the authors.
- This establishes the ecological context: *S. rosei* did not represent an evolutionarily distinct lineage adapted to Lake Culver, but rather a translocated founder population of [[Kern River Rainbow Trout]] persisting and attempting to spawn in a novel alpine lake environment.

## Key Findings
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** *Salmo rosei* Jordan & McGregor, 1924, is a **synonym of *Salmo gilberti*** Jordan, now classified as **[[Salmo Gairdneri Gilberti]]** — i.e., a subspecies/form of rainbow trout native to the Kern River drainage, not a valid distinct species.
- **Historical misclassification:** Multiple authoritative works — [[Jordan Evermann And Clark]] (1930), [[Evermann And Clark]] (1931), and [[Snyder]] (1933, 1938, 1940) — perpetuated *S. rosei* as a valid, distinct species of the rainbow trout series, despite uncertainty noted even by Snyder regarding whether the fish was native or introduced.
- **Prior classification:** [[Shapovalov And Dill]] (1950) had provisionally treated *S. rosei* as a subspecies of *Salmo gairdneri*, pending further evidence.
- **Decisive evidence** combined in this paper:
  1. Unpublished 1947 comparative examination of type specimens by Hubbs and Follett showing no morphological distinction from *S. gilberti*.
  2. Direct testimony (via Brownlow, 1953) establishing the anthropogenic stocking origin (c. 1916) of trout in Lake Culver.
  3. Eyewitness field identification (Brownlow, 1923) of the fish as indistinguishable from Kern River rainbow trout.
- **Broader implication:** The case illustrates how translocation/stocking of trout into naturally fishless alpine lakes historically led to erroneous description of new "species" that were, in fact, introduced populations of existing forms — a cautionary example relevant to [[Trout Taxonomy]] and [[Introduced Species Impacts On Native Fish Classification]].
