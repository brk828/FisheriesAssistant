# Arizona Game and Fish Department FY 1988-89 Recovery Project Proposals for Fishes and Wildlife

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a transmittal letter and attached Section 6 submittal form from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). It lists proposed FY 1988-89 projects for [[Species Recovery]] of threatened, endangered, candidate and other sensitive species in Arizona. Fish projects dominate the first pages, focusing on [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] reintroduction, native fish monitoring, and habitat protection, with additional proposals for amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. Funding levels are given per project and year, with durations of one to five years.

## Background & Description

The letter, signed by the AGFD Director, states that the priority list was revised to emphasize continued development of the agency's data management system, which will support work such as tracking [[Peregrine Falcon]] recovery status and delineating potential [[Black-Footed Ferret]] reintroduction areas. Fish projects were separated from other categories following discussion with the USFWS. Nongame migratory bird priorities were also listed: a [[Ferruginous Pygmy-Owl]] status survey ($15,000), an [[Aplomado Falcon]] reintroduction assessment ($8,000), and a [[Gray Hawk]] status survey ($6,000).

The submittal form uses columns for regional and state priority, species, recovery task number, duration, project description, and comments. Its fish projects (Priority 2, sub-tasks a–j) continue earlier work on [[Native Fish Recovery In Arizona]] initiated in 1986. Non-fish projects (Priorities 3–35) cover mammals, reptiles, amphibians, birds and fishes across the state.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Many listed Arizona fishes are extirpated from much of their historic range or survive in fragmented habitats. Key threats identified in the document include:

- **Predation and competition by exotic species**: red shiner threatens the native community of [[Aravaipa Creek]], a stream supporting seven native fish species, including two threatened species (spikedace and loach minnow) and one candidate (roundtail chub). Largemouth predators and other exotics also cause mortality of reintroduced fish in the Salt and Verde rivers.
- **Habitat loss and alteration**: bullfrog predation and habitat degradation reduce native ranid frogs and garter snakes; urbanization, agriculture and water projects (flood control, channelization) affect many species.
- **Illegal collecting and trafficking**: [[Gila Monster]], [[Desert Tortoise]] and montane rattlesnakes are reported in illegal reptile trade.
- **Genetic concerns**: the [[Yaqui Catfish]] brood stock, established at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in 1987 from Mexican waters, may have introgressed with channel catfish.

Cross-border populations in Sonora, Mexico, are important: surveys by the Centro Ecologico de Sonora and AGFD (1978 and 1986) provide baseline data for [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Beautiful Shiner]] and [[Sonora Chub]].

## Key Findings

**Fish projects (Priority 2, sub-tasks a–j; continuation of 1986 projects):**

- **2a – Razorback sucker and Colorado squawfish stocking monitoring** ($95,000 first year; $85,000 next two years; $70,000 thereafter). Monitoring frequency is proposed to increase to quarterly, covering the Salt River and upper and lower Verde River. Radio telemetry of reintroduced adults in the Salt River would cost $25,000 (first year) and $15,000 in later years if removed.
- **2b – Chemical imprinting of Colorado squawfish** (about $7,000 in 1989, $2,000/year in years 2–4, and $20,000 in year 5). Radiotelemetry indicates that adults return to the same spawning sites, and the mechanism is hypothesized to be chemosensory. Because all Arizona-stocked squawfish were reared in Dexter water, imprinting could prevent spawning in stocked waters. Embryos would be imprinted to tributary waters of the Verde and Salt rivers and raised at Bubbling Ponds Hatchery.
- **2c – Volunteer native fish community monitoring** ($5,000/year for 3 years): more than 60 volunteers in 1988 are compiling data on more than 40 sites, most critical to threatened or endangered species.
- **2d – Mexican fish surveys and broodstock** ($10,000 in 1989): matching funds for CES field work, including procurement of Yaqui basin beautiful shiner broodstock for reintroduction to San Bernardino NWR. CES covers half of field personnel needs.
- **2e – Tank truck purchase** ($20,000): a 1-ton, 4-wheel drive tank truck would allow AGFD to stock remote, inaccessible habitats for Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker and bonytail, whereas Dexter's large truck cannot reach many preferred sites.
- **2f – Desert pupfish and Gila topminnow reintroduction monitoring** ($8,000/year for 3 years): many stockings have been made; monitoring and additional stockings must continue.
- **2g – Aravaipa Creek fish barrier** ($10,000–$20,000; cost estimate $30,000–60,000): a barrier to upstream red shiner movement is proposed. The Nature Conservancy owns suitable property but lacks funds.
- **2h – Habitat preferences of reintroduced fishes** ($10,000 first year; $3,000 second year): analysis of habitat overlap with resident species to improve reintroduction success.
- **2i – Lake Mohave bonytail capture and movement study** ($25,000 first year; $15,000 next two years; $5,000/year thereafter): radio tagging of Dexter-held bonytail and solar-powered receivers to focus capture efforts for broodstock.
- **2j – Fish data base and computer status survey** ($8,000–$20,000/year for 3 years): proposal to purchase an 80386-based microcomputer with a 70 MB hard drive, replacing a monthly-rented IBM PC XT.

**Non-fish species highlights (Priorities 3–35):**

- **[[Leopard Frog]] complex** (Priority 3): populations declining; no baseline data exist for several Arizona ranids; bullfrog predation is studied.
- **[[Black-Footed Ferret]]** (Priority 4, $30,000/year for 2 years): reintroduction site analysis at Gunnison's prairie dog towns.
- **[[Black Rail]]** (Priority 5, $20,000/year for 5 years) and **[[Desert Tortoise]]** (Priority 6, $50,000/year for 5 years): life history studies at long-term sites.
- **[[River Otter]]** (Priority 7, $15,000/year for 2 years): last valid Arizona record was from Cherry Creek in 1970; the Gila River drainage has never been surveyed.
- **[[Sanborn's Long-Nosed Bat]]** (Priority 8, $25,000/year for 2 years): roost monitoring in southern Arizona.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (Priority 9, $5,000–$10,000 for 1 year): electrophoretic and morphometric genetic analysis of the Dexter brood stock (60 adults) before reintroduction to San Bernardino NWR.
- **[[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]** (Priority 10, $8,000/year for 3 years): quarterly monitoring at permanent sites.
- **[[Hualapai Mexican Vole]]** (Priority 11) and **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]** (Priorities 12–13, $10,000–$20,000 each, matched 1:1 by Apache-Sitgreaves and Coconino National Forests): distribution, abundance and habitat surveys for threatened species.
- **[[Ferruginous Pygmy-Owl]]** (Priority 14, $15,000/year for 3 years): population has declined drastically over 40 years.
- **[[Mt. Graham Red Squirrel]]** (Priority 15, $30,000/year for 3 years): population dynamics and cone production.
- **[[Apache Goshawk]]** (Priority 17): statewide monitoring at 15–20 eyries; 10-year management plan proposed.
- **[[Peregrine Falcon]]** (Priority 18, $30,000/year for 3 years): population monitoring.
- **[[Bald Eagle]]** (Priority 20, $50,000/year for 5 years): nest site monitoring; research largely addressed by a BuRec contract.
- **[[Gila Monster]]** (Priority 21, $20,000/year for 5 years) and illegal reptile trafficking investigations (Priority 22).
- **[[Ocelot]] and [[Jaguarundi]]** (Priority 25, $20,000/year for 2 years): habitat and population surveys.
- **[[Yuma Clapper Rail]]** (Priority 26, $10,000/year for 5 years) and **[[Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]]** (Priority 27, $40,000 for 1 year): habitat and population work along the Colorado, Gila and San Pedro rivers.
- **[[Bell's Vireo]]** (Priority 28, $25,000 for 1 year): population survey.
- **[[Spotted Owl]]** (Priority 33, $5,000/year for 3 years): breeding site surveys.
- **[[Sonoran Pronghorn]]** (Priority 34, $75,000/year for 3 years): satellite radio-tracking and fawn survival study; population fluctuates around 50–150 animals.
- **[[Gila Trout]]** (Priority 31) and **[[Arizona Trout]]** (Priority 30): range-wide population status surveys.

**Overall conclusion:** The proposal prioritizes long-term monitoring, computerized data management, and matched funding with partners. It emphasizes reintroduction success and habitat protection for native fishes, especially in the Salt, Verde and Aravaipa systems.
