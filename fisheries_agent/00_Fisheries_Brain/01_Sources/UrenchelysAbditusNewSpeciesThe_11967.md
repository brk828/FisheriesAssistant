## Executive Summary

This paper describes **Urenchelys abditus**, a new fossil species of eel (order [[Anguilliformes]]) recovered from the Niobrara Chalk (Late Cretaceous, ~86–88 MYBP) at the Hell's Bar locality in Kansas, USA. The single known specimen (holotype KUVP 27241) is preserved as an impression on the inner surface of an *Inoceramus platinus* pelecypod shell valve. This discovery represents the **first undoubted eel from the Cretaceous of North America**. The authors also re-examine a previously described taxon, "*Anguillavus*" *hackberryensis* Martin, 1920, and reject its eel affinities, reassigning it instead to the family [[Dercetidae]], thereby confirming *U. abditus* as the sole verified Cretaceous eel from this continent. The species is diagnosed primarily by vertebral counts distinguishing it from other species of [[Urenchelys]].

## Background & Description

**Preservation:** Holotype consists of a part and counterpart impression pressed into the left valve of an *Inoceramus platinus* shell; skull and shoulder girdle details are well preserved, though the neurocranium is heavily crushed.

**Skull:**
- Total skull length: 6.2 mm (posterior edge of supraoccipital to anterior end of premaxillo-mesethmo-vomer, PEV)
- PEV: triangular anterior expansion, tapering posteriorly; articulation with frontal not preserved
- Frontals: elongate, articulate laterally with dermopterotics, posteriorly with parietals
- Parietals: rectangular, bordered by dermopterotics laterally and supraoccipital/epiotics posteriorly
- Epiotics: left epiotic complete, bears elongate flange similar to that of [[Anguilla rostrata]]
- Supraoccipital: small, triangular, displaced by compression
- Parasphenoid: long splint-like anterior projection, flares posteriorly before disappearing into crushed neurocranium

**Jaws and Dentition:**
- Left maxilla: 3.6 mm, split, articulatory head overlies PEV (upside-down)
- Right maxilla: fully preserved, normally oriented
- Lower jaw: dentary forms coronoid process; bordered posteriorly by large angulo-articulo-retroarticular (AAR); possible coracomeckelian element
- Dentition: two rows of teeth near symphysis, single row of small blunt teeth posteriorly (teeth lost during preparation)

**Hyoid Arch/Opercular Apparatus:**
- Branchiostegals: long, numerous (12–15 per side), superimposed, not recurving around opercular apparatus
- Ceratohyal, hypohyal present
- Suboperculum: crescent-shaped fragment
- Operculum: thin, crushed bone

**Pectoral Girdle and Fins:**
- Cleithra: long and slender (diagnostic similarity to *U. germanus* and *U. hakelensis*, contrasting robust cleithrum of *U. avus*)
- Right supracleithrum articulates with 5th (possibly 4th) vertebra
- Pectoral fin: 12–13 fin rays (left fin)

**Median Fins:**
- Dorsal fin: inserts anteriorly on 12th vertebra; averages 2–3 rays per vertebra
- Anal fin: ~3 rays per vertebra
- Caudal fin: not preserved

**Vertebral Column:**
- 57 preserved vertebrae (caudal fin and posterior-most vertebrae missing)
- 11 predorsal centra
- 28 preanal centra (diagnostic count)
- Slender ribs on abdominal centra
- Neural arches of abdominal vertebrae bipartite, each part autogenous and rhomboid
- Neural and haemal spine distal ends **not** expanded (distinguishes from *U. germanus* and [[Anguillavus bathshebae]])

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat:** *Urenchelys abditus* inhabited the [[Western Interior Seaway]], a shallow epicontinental marine sea covering central North America during the Late Cretaceous. The species epithet *abditus* ("hidden") reflects inferred cryptic habits/habitat, consistent with typical anguilliform ecology.

**Depositional Setting:** The Niobrara Chalk at Hell's Bar preserves a highly diverse marine vertebrate and invertebrate fauna, indicating a productive, chalk-forming pelagic/hemipelagic marine environment.

**Faunal Associations at Hell's Bar:**
- **Chondrichthyans:** [[Cretoxyrhina mantelli]], [[Squalicorax falcatus]], [[Squalicorax kaupi]], [[Ptychodus mortoni]]
- **Neopterygians:** [[Paraliodesmus guadagnii]], [[Protosphyraena tenuis]]
- **Teleosts:** [[Xiphactinus audax]], [[Gillicus arcuatus]], [[Apsopelix anglicus]], [[Pachyrhizodus]] cf. *caninus*, [[Laminospondylus]] cf. *transversus*, [[Bananogmius evolutus]], [[Cimolichthys nepaholica]], and at least two undescribed holocentrids
- **Reptiles:** [[Pteranodon]] sp. (pterosaur), a mosasaur, a plesiosaur, and a toxochelyid turtle
- **Invertebrates:** pelecypods [[Pseudopema congesta]], [[Inoceramus platinus]], [[Durania maxima]]; cephalopods [[Clioscaphites choteauensis]] and [[Baculites]] sp.; a scalpellid thoracican barnacle; acrothoracican borings

This assemblage indicates *U. abditus* coexisted with large predatory sharks, giant teleosts (e.g., *Xiphactinus*), marine reptiles, and diverse invertebrates in a fully marine chalk-sea ecosystem.

## Key Findings

1. **New species diagnosis:** *Urenchelys abditus* is distinguished from all other congeners by having only **28 vertebrae** between the anterior anal fin insertion and the basioccipital; all other species with known postcranial material have **35 or more** vertebrae in this position.

2. **First confirmed Cretaceous eel from North America:** Prior to this study, no undoubted anguilliform eel had been documented from the North American Cretaceous.

3. **Comparative morphology within Urenchelys:**
   - Predorsal vertebral count (12) is similar to *U. germanus* (12), differing from *U. hakelensis* and *U. avus* (6–7)
   - Preanal vertebral count (28, though noted separately as diagnostic) situates it distinctly from *U. hakelensis* (36–39), *U. avus* (35), and *U. germanus* (40–43)
   - Slender cleithrum shared with *U. germanus* and *U. hakelensis*, unlike robust cleithrum of *U. avus*
   - Non-expanded neural/haemal spine tips distinguish it from *U. germanus* and *Anguillavus bathshebae*
   - Size is smaller than *U. germanus*, *U. avus* (~50 mm skull length), and *U. anglicus* (~70 mm skull length)

4. **Taxonomic reassignment of "Anguillavus" hackberryensis:** Re-examination of the holotype (KUVP 927) revealed that the originally published photograph (Martin, 1920) was oriented **upside-down**. Corrected orientation shows strong similarity to the dercetid genus [[Stratodus]] (e.g., *Stratodus apicalis*, KUVP 333) in neural/haemal arch shape and smooth (non-rugose) centra morphology, contrasting with rugose centra typical of true Cretaceous eels and genera like [[Enchodus]] and [[Cimolichthys]]. The taxon is reassigned to **Dercetidae**, removing it from Anguilliformes.

5. **Implication:** With "*A.*" *hackberryensis* excluded from Anguilliformes, ***Urenchelys abditus* stands as the only verified eel species known from the Cretaceous of North America**, underscoring the rarity of elopomorph eel fossils from this time and region.

6. **Taphonomy:** Unusual preservation mode — the fish is preserved as a part/counterpart impression on the inner surface of an *Inoceramus* bivalve shell, illustrating a distinctive fossilization pathway within the Niobrara Chalk depositional system.
