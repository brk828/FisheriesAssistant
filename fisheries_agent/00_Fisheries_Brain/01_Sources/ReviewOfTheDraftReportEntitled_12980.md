# Review of Spikedace and Red Shiner Interactions Study

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a peer review letter dated 26 July 1989 from a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Fishery Management Biologist to Dr. Dean Hendrickson regarding a draft report by Marsh et al. on interactions between threatened [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda fulgida]]) and introduced [[Red Shiner]] ([[Cyprinella lutrensis]]). The reviewer identifies significant methodological concerns and data gaps in the draft report, particularly the lack of historical perspective, inadequate stream comparisons, and failure to consider habitat degradation as a contributing factor to [[Spikedace]] decline. The review emphasizes that the draft does not contain sufficient information to definitively evaluate direct competition between the two species.

## Background & Description

The review addresses a draft report examining interactions between native [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda fulgida]]), a threatened species, and the introduced [[Red Shiner]] ([[Cyprinella lutrensis]]) in Arizona waterways. The study included both field surveys conducted in 1988 and artificial stream experiments. Study locations included the [[Verde River]], [[Gila River]], and [[Sycamore Creek]]. The research attempted to evaluate competitive interactions through habitat use studies, food habits analysis, and behavioral observations in controlled conditions. The draft report followed an earlier 1988 report by Douglas et al. and was intended to assess whether competition for resources was the causative factor in [[Spikedace]] decline.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The reviewer notes that habitats in the study area have changed significantly, with flows greatly reduced and otherwise altered from historic conditions. The review emphasizes that while competition may be implicated in [[Spikedace]] decline, other factors including habitat degradation are also responsible and should be investigated. The lack of historical (pre-[[Red Shiner]]) perspective prevents adequate evaluation of whether direct competition exists between the species. The reviewer questions whether habitat quality and quantity have remained comparable to historic conditions, an assumption necessary for interpreting the study results. A flooding event in July provided an opportunity to evaluate species displacement and habitat use shifts, though this was apparently not fully exploited in the draft report.

## Key Findings

**Major Methodological Concerns:**

1. **Lack of Historical Perspective**: The draft does not provide sufficient pre-[[Red Shiner]] baseline data to evaluate whether direct competition exists between the two species.

2. **Stream Selection Issues**: The selection of [[Sycamore Creek]] as the site for [[Red Shiner]]-only studies is questioned; reaches of the [[Verde River]] or [[Gila River]] where only [[Red Shiner]] occur would provide more comparable habitat quality and quantity for statistical analyses.

3. **Inadequate Stream Comparisons**: The draft lacks detailed comparison of physical attributes of each stream and evaluation of how differences might bias observed results.

**Artificial Stream Study Deficiencies:**

1. Substrate composition, placement, proportion, and displacement not adequately reported; no explanation of attempts to approximate natural conditions.

2. July flooding event not utilized to evaluate species displacement and habitat use implications.

3. Unsupported claim that [[Spikedace]] cannot prey upon larval [[Red Shiner]]; reviewer considers adult [[Spikedace]] likely capable of such predation.

4. Aggressive behavior in [[Red Shiner]] only reported during reproductive activities (expected for most fishes), yet conclusions suggest active displacement of [[Spikedace]] to lesser habitats; clarification needed.

**Food Habits Study Limitations:**

1. Lack of seasonal sampling makes conclusions preliminary; food use and availability vary seasonally.

2. Inadequate information for accurate stream comparisons and food overlap estimates; draft acknowledges "available resources were not identical" between streams.

3. [[Verde River]] [[Spikedace]] sample size (n=11) significantly lower than other streams; results incomplete without additional data.

4. [[Sycamore Creek]] productivity and food availability not compared to larger streams; unclear why [[Red Shiner]] consumed more insect types alone in [[Sycamore Creek]] than in other streams.

**Critical Omission:**

The draft fails to recognize or address a comprehensive New Mexico Department of Game and Fish study by Propst et al. (1986) on [[Spikedace]] distribution, status, biology, and conservation. That report indicates habitat shifts in quality and abundance are important factors in [[Spikedace]] decline, presenting conclusions that apparently conflict with the Marsh et al. draft. The reviewer considers this omission a significant deficiency requiring correction in any final report.

**Overall Assessment:**

The reviewer concludes that while competition is implicated in [[Spikedace]] decline, habitat degradation and other factors are also responsible and should be investigated. The draft does not provide adequate evidence to isolate competition as the primary causative factor.
