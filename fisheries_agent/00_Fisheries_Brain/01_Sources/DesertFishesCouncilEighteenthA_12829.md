# Desert Fishes Council Eighteenth Annual Symposium Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #conservation #symposium

## Executive Summary

This document is the program for the 18th Annual Symposium of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], held November 20-22, 1986, at the Four Seasons Convention Center in St. George, Utah. The symposium featured agency reports, research presentations, and management papers focused on the conservation and study of desert fishes across the southwestern United States and Mexico. Sessions covered topics including species reintroduction, population monitoring, habitat utilization, taxonomic studies, and recovery efforts for threatened and endangered desert fish species. The program included presentations on multiple taxa including [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]] (Cyprinodon macularius), [[Colorado River Squawfish]], [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Pecos Pupfish]] (Cyprinodon pecosensis), [[Spikedace]] (Meda fulgida), and various other desert-adapted fishes.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Council]] is an organization dedicated to the preservation of America's desert fishes. The 1986 symposium was structured into eight sessions over three days, including:

**Session I**: Agency reports from Mexico, Canada, and United States agencies including [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[National Park Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Forest Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[The Nature Conservancy]], covering Nevada, Idaho, Colorado, Texas, California, Utah, Arizona, and New Mexico. Reports from Recovery Team Leaders and Area Coordinators were also included.

**Sessions II, IV, and V**: Research and management papers covering diverse topics in desert fish biology, ecology, and conservation.

**Session III**: Banquet and informal discussions.

**Session VI**: Special discussion items including management of [[Colorado Squawfish]] as a potential game species and long-term management strategies for [[Ash Meadows]].

**Session VII**: Business meeting covering organizational matters, constitution and bylaws, resolutions, treasurer's report, proceedings update, and committee reports.

**Session VIII**: Field trip to the [[Virgin River]].

The symposium featured a student paper competition (Carl L. Hubbs best student paper competition) with entries marked by asterisks in the program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The symposium addressed desert aquatic ecosystems across multiple geographic regions including:

- [[Virgin River]] system (Utah, Nevada, Arizona)
- [[Colorado River]] basin and [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]
- [[Yampa River]]
- [[Cataract Canyon]]
- [[Gila River]] (New Mexico)
- [[Salton Sea]] area (California)
- [[Fish Lake Valley]], Esmeralda County, Nevada
- [[Ash Meadows]] (Nevada)
- [[Pecos River]] system
- [[Great Basin]] springs and streams
- [[Great Salt Lake]]
- [[Amargosa River]] system
- [[Sonoran Desert]] (Mexico)

Habitat types discussed included thermal springs, desert rivers with fluctuating flows, isolated spring systems, and pluvial lake remnants. Environmental concerns addressed included:

- Baseline flow fluctuations as spawning cues
- Habitat preference and utilization patterns
- Microhabitat utilization
- Behavioral thermal regulation in thermal springs
- Competition and displacement by exotic species (particularly [[Red Shiner]])
- Hybridization between native species and between native and exotic species
- Food availability for larvae
- Filter feeding comparisons between exotic and native species

## Key Findings

**Species-Specific Research Presentations**:

1. **[[Gila Topminnow]]**: Reintroduction efforts with comments following 1986 survey (Lee Simons, Arizona Game and Fish Department)

2. **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (Cyprinodon macularius): Results of minnow trap surveys in southeast [[Salton Sea]] area, 1983-1986 (Stephen J. Montgomery)

3. **[[Virgin River]]** fishes: Multiple presentations including habitat preference and utilization (Thom Hardy), recent population changes (James E. Deacon), and status of [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin River Chub]] (Terry Hickman)

4. **[[Big Springs Spinedace]]** (Lepidomeda mollispinis pratensis): Present status and recovery potential (Randy McNatt)

5. **[[Colorado River Squawfish]]**: Evidence of baseline flow fluctuations as spawning cue in [[Yampa River]] (Tom Nesler); reintroduction studies (Dean Hendrickson and Jim Brooks); discussion of potential as game species

6. **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: Growth and food availability for larvae (Diana Papoulias)

7. **[[Pecos Pupfish]]** (Cyprinodon pecosensis): Large-scale geographic replacement by C. variegatus × C. pecosensis hybrids (Gene R. Wilde et al.)

8. **[[Spikedace]]** (Meda fulgida): Microhabitat utilization patterns in [[Gila River]], New Mexico (David Propst and Kevin Bestgen)

9. **[[Tui Chub]]** (Gila bicolor): Status and distribution in [[Fish Lake Valley]] (Lisa Heki and John Pedretti); population relationships in north-central [[Great Basin]] (Sharon Ohlhurst et al.)

10. **[[Oregon Chub]]** (Oregonichthys crameri): Possible reasons for decline (Todd Pearsons)

11. **[[Amargosa Pupfish]]** (Cyprinodon nevadensis): Subspecific identity from Shoshone Spring, Inyo County, California (Frances Taylor and John Pedretti); behavioral thermal regulation (Bob McCauley)

**Taxonomic and Systematic Studies**:

- Taxonomic status of Rhinichthys osculus complex in pluvial [[White River]], [[Carpenter River]], and [[Virgin River]], Nevada (John Pedretti)
- Morphology and foods of hybrids between Catostomus insignis and Pantosteus clarki from two Arizona streams (Rob Clarkson and W.L. Minckley)
- Re-examination of relationships among [[Upper Colorado River]] chubs, genus Gila (Mark Rosenfeld)
- Examination of Chasmistes existence in [[Snake River]] system (Rob Sorenson et al.)

**Conservation and Management Topics**:

- Trends and considerations for transplants of rare western fishes (Jack E. Williams and Western Division AFS Endangered Species Committee)
- Recovery of threatened and endangered [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] fishes (John Hamill)
- [[Red Shiner]] vs. native fishes: replacement or displacement (Kevin Bestgen and David Propst)
- Philosophies and practices for conserving fish genomes (Gary K. Meffe)
- Likelihood of persistence of small populations of large animals (Peter I. Brussard)
- Community structure of desert fish assemblages (Donald W. Sada and Gary L. Vinyard)

**Other Presentations**:

- Arizona spring snails (Hydrobiidae: Prosobranchia: Rissoacea) (Robert Hershler and J. Jerry Landye)
- Fish designs on [[Mimbres Pottery]] (Jerry Burton)
- Institution for study of flora and fauna of [[Sonoran Desert]] (Centro Ecológico de Sonora, Mexico)
- Springfish-cichlid interactions in aquaria (Dallas Tippie)
- Filter feeding comparison: exotic Tilapia aurea vs. native Orthodon microlepidotus (Gary L. Vinyard)
- Fish in [[Great Salt Lake]]: zoogeographic significance and legacy of Barney Clark (Mark Rosenfeld)
- Endangered fishes of [[Cataract Canyon]], [[Colorado River]], Utah (Rich Valdez)

**Symposium Guidelines**: Presentations limited to 20 minutes including discussion; agency reports limited to 10 minutes. Manuscript deadline: December 31, 1986. Papers submitted after deadline to be published in special section of 1987 proceedings.
