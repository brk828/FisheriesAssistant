# Three New Species of Vejovis from Death Valley, California

#arachnology #scorpiones #vejovidae

## Executive Summary

Williams (1970), published in the *[[Pan-Pacific Entomologist]]* (Vol. 46, No. 1, pp. 1–11), reports a one-week survey of the scorpion fauna of [[Death Valley]], California, carried out in April 1968. The study examined several hundred specimens and described three previously undescribed species of the genus *[[Vejovis]]* (family [[Vejovidae]]): *Vejovis gramenestris*, *Vejovis deserticola* and *Vejovis shulovi*. Each species is separated from its nearest known relative by morphological characters, mainly pectinal tooth counts, keel development, pedipalp proportions and metasomal segment lengths.

## Background & Description

The survey sampled the full length of Death Valley across as many ecological situations as the available time allowed. Specimens were collected mainly by [[Ultraviolet Detection]] at night, with some found by rock turning. The author used standard morphometric measurements (in millimeters) of the carapace, mesosoma, metasoma, telson, pedipalps and [[Pectines]], and assigned [[Holotype]], [[Allotype]] and [[Paratypes]] to each species. Holotypes and allotypes were deposited in the [[California Academy of Sciences]].

**Vejovis gramenestris**
- Moderate to small scorpion, probably seldom exceeding 35 mm total length; light brownish-yellow.
- Pectinal teeth: 16–17 in males, 12–14 in females.
- Holotype male total length 24.6 mm; allotype female 32.8 mm.
- Named for its grass-dwelling habits.
- Closest relative: *[[Vejovis Wupatkiensis]]*, from which it differs by smaller size, fewer pectinal teeth, lighter base color without melanic markings, and relative length of the metasomal segment V and movable pedipalp finger.

**Vejovis deserticola**
- Moderate-sized species; brownish-amber body with almost white pectines and reddish pedipalp chela.
- Pectinal teeth: 21–22 in males, 20 in females.
- Holotype male total length 42.6 mm; allotype female 45.0 mm.
- Named because Death Valley is its only known habitat, described as one of the harshest deserts in North America.
- Closest relative: *Vejovis wupatkiensis*, from which it differs by more pectinal teeth in both sexes, densely granular vesicle, and much larger body proportions.

**Vejovis shulovi**
- Moderate-sized species placed in the *Paruroctonus* subgenus by the author; pale yellowish with dusky markings on carapace and mesosoma.
- Pectinal teeth: 19–20 in males, 13–15 in females.
- Holotype female total length 38.3 mm; allotype male 34.4 mm.
- Named in honor of *[[Aaron Shulov]]* of the Hebrew University in Jerusalem, for his contributions to scorpion biology and venoms.
- Related to *[[Vejovis Bantai]]*, from which it differs in pectinal tooth counts, middle lamellae number, reddish hair counts on inferior median keels, and pedipalp hand width.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Vejovis gramenestris** was abundant in grasses adjacent to permanent springs. By day it sheltered under travertine rocks; at night it climbed grass stems, apparently foraging. The species is very active and capable of sustained rapid movement. Two members of the collecting party were stung on the fingers. The sting produced a sharp burning sensation lasting five to ten minutes, with no swelling, reddening or urticaria.

**Vejovis deserticola** was collected at night on a rocky slope by ultraviolet detection.

**Vejovis shulovi** is known only from the north end of Death Valley, from two localities. It was not abundant, and females greatly outnumbered males. No young juveniles were found. The habitat was hot and arid, with fine sedimentary soil covered by dark volcanic surface rock. A small spring fed a short stream that flowed into the desert for about a quarter mile. Vegetation was sparse except next to the stream.

Collection localities named in the text include [[Travertine Spring]], [[Grapevine Spring]], [[Mule Team Canyon]] and [[Scotty's Ranch]] (elevation 3000 feet).

## Key Findings

- The survey identified three distinct, undescribed *Vejovis* species from Death Valley, all described as new.
- **Vejovis gramenestris**: 20 paratopotypes plus 11 additional paratypes examined. Pectinal teeth 12–14 in females and 16–17 in males. Found only around permanent springs.
- **Vejovis deserticola**: holotype male and allotype female collected at Travertine Spring on 11 April 1968. Pectinal teeth 22/21 in the male holotype and 20/20 in the female allotype. Known only from Death Valley.
- **Vejovis shulovi**: 11 paratopotypes and 20 additional paratypes examined (20 females at Scotty's Ranch). Female carapace lengths 4.1–5.3 mm in the paratopotypes. Known from only two localities at the north end of Death Valley.
- Collection dates ranged from 11 to 14 April 1968, with most specimens taken by ultraviolet detection.
- Stings from *V. gramenestris* caused a burning sensation of 5–10 minutes without local reaction.
