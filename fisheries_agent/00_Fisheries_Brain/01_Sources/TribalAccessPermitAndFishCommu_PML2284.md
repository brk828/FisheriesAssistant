# Tribal Access Permit and Fish Community Monitoring on the San Carlos Apache Reservation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish-monitoring

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of administrative records for a fish monitoring project in the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona. It contains a [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] access permit issued to a [[Bureau of Reclamation]] staff member, its terms and conditions, and a letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) dated January 5, 2010. The monitoring is required under a [[Biological Opinion]] for the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP). Its purpose is to detect changes in the fish community that could follow the introduction of new species through the CAP canal. The permit allows field access for fish monitoring during a short visit in January 2011. It also sets strict conditions on data publication, cultural resource protection, water and vegetation use, and fire prevention.

The document does not report any fish species, catch data, sample sizes, or monitoring results. The scientific content is limited to the monitoring purpose and the site descriptions.

## Background & Description

- **Permit type:** Tribal access permit (Reservation entry permit) issued by the San Carlos Apache Tribe, Recreation and Wildlife Department.
- **Permittee:** A Bureau of Reclamation employee, named on the permit as Paul Colburn Marsh. The organization is listed as the Bureau of Reclamation, Glendale, Arizona.
- **Stated purpose:** "To conduct fish monitoring."
- **Access dates (as printed):** Entry on January 19, 2011, and exit on January 18, 2011. These dates are inconsistent as printed, and this may be a scanning or transcription error.
- **Authorized areas:** The area around the Kandly and Ranch area downstream of the dam, as described in the permit's road and location clause.
- **Coordinating agency:** The USFWS Arizona Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office in Pinetop, Arizona. The Project Coordinator, Jeremy Voelz, wrote the 2010 letter. Copies went to Doug Duncan (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, Tucson) and April Howard (San Carlos Apache Tribe Recreation and Wildlife Department).

The USFWS letter states that Reclamation set up monitoring sites in the Gila River basin. Some of these sites are on or accessed through the [[San Carlos Apache Reservation]]. The Tribe granted access, and the USFWS will help secure future Tribal access permits, which are administered through the [[San Carlos Tribal Administration Office]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The monitoring addresses a specific ecological concern: the possibility that species moved into the Gila River basin from the [[Central Arizona Project]] canal could change the native fish community. The document does not name the native or introduced species. Nor does it describe sampling methods, fish species lists, or habitat measurements.

The permit's location clause names two monitoring areas:

- **Downstream of the old bridge crossing:** approximately 0.5 miles below the dam, referenced by UTM coordinates (NAD27).
- **Near the Hook & Line Ranch area:** a site several miles downstream of the dam. The exact distance is garbled in the scan. The permit gives UTM coordinates for this site.

Environmental and cultural protections in the permit include:

- Restrictions on removing water, wood, plants, animals, soil, sand, artifacts, rocks, or minerals from the Reservation.
- Water may only be used for personal use and emergencies.
- Vegetation and timber may not be cut or damaged without written authority. Damage must be paid for at rates set by the Tribe.
- Fire prevention duties, including liability for fires the permittee starts.
- Protection of [[Cultural Resource Sites]] (burial sites, caves, rock shelters, ruins, and graves). Any discovery requires notice to the Tribal Historic Preservation Officer.
- New roads or trails are prohibited without written permission.

## Key Findings

1. **Monitoring is a federal compliance requirement.** The fish monitoring is required under the CAP [[Biological Opinion]] (and its revisions). It tracks community change that may follow new species introduced through the CAP canal into the Gila River basin.
2. **Access depends on Tribal permission and limited scope.** Permission is limited to the stated purpose and listed locations. Carrying the permit and reporting presence to the Tribe's Wildlife and Recreation Office are required.
3. **Data publication is restricted.** The permittee may not publish data, results, samples, logs, field notes, or photographs without prior written approval from the Tribal Council by resolution.
4. **Data delivery deadlines apply.** All initial data and field records must be given to the Tribal Chairman and the Superintendent of the San Carlos Agency within 30 days of leaving the Reservation. Final reports and analyses are due within six months. Copies also go to the Sparks Law Firm, P.C., special counsel for water and resources.
5. **Cultural and resource protections are strict.** The permit requires immediate notice and site protection if a cultural site is disturbed. Cancellation is possible for knowing disturbance.
6. **Liability and cost recovery apply.** The permittee is liable for surface or subsurface damage. Tribal staff time is billed at no less than $35 per hour. Vegetation and fire damages are assessed at market or timber values, plus re-establishment costs.
7. **Prohibited activities include:** possession of firearms, weapons, traps, nets, explosives, chemicals, or alcohol on the Reservation, without specific authorization. The permit also prohibits removing semi-precious or precious stones or metals. Discoveries of such materials must be reported within five days.
8. **Records are incomplete for scientific use.** The document contains no species lists, sample counts, catch-per-effort data, or statistical results. Those details would be in the separate data reports the permit requires.
