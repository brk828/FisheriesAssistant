## Executive Summary
This document is a 1988 letter from Dr. William R. Gould (Montana State University) to Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University), seeking expert consultation on the taxonomic status of **[[Rhinichthys osculus thermalis]]** (Kendall Warm Springs dace), a thermal-spring-endemic population in Wyoming, relative to **[[Rhinichthys osculus yarrowi]]** from the Green River drainage. The core scientific question is whether *thermalis* represents a genuinely distinct subspecies or merely an ecophenotypic variant shaped by its unusual thermal habitat. Gould presents preliminary pharyngeal tooth formula data suggesting a genetically-based distinction, independent of the thermally-plastic morphological traits traditionally used to diagnose the subspecies.

## Background & Description
- **Traditional diagnostic characters for *thermalis*:**
  - Number of scales in the lateral line
  - Head length / standard length proportions
  - *Caveat:* Both traits are known to be phenotypically plastic and influenced by thermal environment, undermining their reliability as taxonomic (genetic) markers.

- **Pharyngeal tooth formula (minor row) — proposed alternative character:**
  - Considered less subject to environmentally-induced (thermal) variation.
  - Presence of "0" in the minor row of the pharyngeal tooth formula used as the diagnostic trait in this study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Kendall Warm Springs]]**: A geothermally influenced spring system in Wyoming, home to the endemic and geographically isolated population of *R. o. thermalis*. This thermal habitat is hypothesized to drive plastic morphological divergence (scale counts, body proportions) independent of underlying genetic differentiation.
- **[[Green River Drainage]]**: Cooler, non-thermal freshwater habitat occupied by *R. o. yarrowi*, serving as the comparative/ancestral population against which *thermalis* is being evaluated.
- The ecological contrast (thermal spring vs. standard riverine drainage) is central to the taxonomic debate, since thermal conditions are known to independently drive convergent morphological traits in [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows) without necessarily reflecting genetic divergence.

## Key Findings
- **Tooth formula data:** 43 of 50 examined *R. o. thermalis* individuals possessed a "0" in the pharyngeal tooth formula minor row; 0 of 50 *R. o. yarrowi* individuals showed this trait.
- **Genetic vs. plastic traits:** Unlike lateral line scale counts and head/standard length ratios, pharyngeal tooth formulas in minnows are not known to exhibit north–south clinal variation attributable to thermal environment, supporting the interpretation that tooth formula is under stronger genetic control.
- **Taxonomic conclusion (tentative):** The magnitude of difference in tooth formula between the two forms exceeds the 75% rule commonly applied for diagnosing subspecies distinctiveness, supporting provisional recognition of *thermalis* and *yarrowi* as genetically distinct subspecies.
- **Expert consultation sought:** Gould solicited input from [[Frank Cross]] and subsequently from [[W. L. Minckley]], given the latter's extensive taxonomic and biological experience with speckled dace (*[[Rhinichthys osculus]]*), to validate the interpretation of the pharyngeal tooth data as a marker of genetic (rather than environmentally plastic) differentiation.
- **Broader implication:** This case highlights a recurring methodological issue in freshwater fish subspecies delimitation — the need to distinguish genetically fixed diagnostic characters from environmentally induced phenotypic plasticity, particularly in geothermal spring endemics.
