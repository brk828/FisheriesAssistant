# Review Committee Planning for Humpback Chub Studies under Glen Canyon Environmental Studies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #dam-operations

## Executive Summary

This administrative correspondence from Arizona State University's Department of Zoology (dated 30 September 1991) documents planning for a scientific review committee meeting to assess ongoing research on [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program. The meeting was scheduled for 20 November 1991 in Las Vegas, Nevada, to evaluate current research efforts in light of Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) requirements for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and recovery/maintenance needs for this endangered cyprinid species.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is a cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system. The correspondence establishes a review committee comprising four scientists: Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University, committee organizer), Dr. James E. Deacon (University of Nevada, Las Vegas), Dr. William J. Matthews (University of Oklahoma Biological Station), and Dr. Hiram Li (Oregon State University, Oregon Cooperative Fisheries Unit). Additional participants included Duncan Patten (Arizona State University, senior scientist for GCES) and David Wegner (Bureau of Reclamation, GCES program manager).

The review was organized to assess research funded and underway through the GCES program, with materials to be distributed including: (a) research proposal excerpts, (b) conservation measures addressing chub recovery and maintenance, and (c) progress statements/reports on four major projects.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research context centers on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and their impacts/influences on [[Humpback Chub]] populations in the [[Colorado River]]. The review addresses two fundamental needs:

1. Information requirements for an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and their effects on the chub
2. Basic information required for chub recovery and maintenance

The correspondence indicates ongoing concern about whether current research efforts would provide necessary information for species conservation and management decisions related to dam operations.

## Key Findings

**Meeting Structure and Objectives:**
- Review meeting scheduled for 20 November 1991 in Las Vegas, Nevada
- Morning session: presentation of research proposals and conservation measures by program manager
- Afternoon session: assessment of research progress, identification of information gaps, and strategies to address deficiencies
- Specific focus on gaps related to chub recovery and maintenance information needs

**Review Scope:**
- Assessment of four major projects currently underway
- Evaluation of research design, goals, and progress
- Identification of gaps in knowledge, particularly regarding recovery requirements
- Development of strategies to fill identified gaps

**Administrative Coordination:**
- Materials distribution coordinated through GCES program manager
- Reimbursement procedures established through ASU Center for Environmental Studies
- Potential follow-up meeting with principal investigators planned for 21 November at Death Valley (not confirmed at time of correspondence)
