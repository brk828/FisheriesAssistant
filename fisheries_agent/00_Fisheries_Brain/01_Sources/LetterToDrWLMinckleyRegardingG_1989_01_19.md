# Letter to Dr. W. L. Minckley Regarding Gerrhonotus Specimens from Cuatro Ciénegas

#herpetology #reptilia #anguidae #cuatro-ciénegas #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

Biol. Arturo Contreras-Arquieta writes to Dr. W. L. Minckley to report on specimens of the lizard genus **[[Gerrhonotus]]** collected during herpetofaunal fieldwork in the **[[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]]** (Coahuila, Mexico), an endorheic desert wetland system otherwise renowned for its endemic aquatic fauna. The letter clarifies a species-level misidentification: specimens initially suspected to be *Gerrhonotus lugoi* are instead identified as *Gerrhonotus liocephalus*, and scale variation in this population suggests it represents an **undescribed subspecies**. The author requests capture locality data for a specimen previously collected by Minckley, believed to originate from **Tío Candido**, a locality within the basin. A preliminary taxonomic report was slated for presentation at the **X Congreso Nacional de Zoología** in Mexico City (October, presumably 1989), with a fuller description in preparation. Voucher specimens were deposited in the university's **Museo de Historia Natural**, and an additional specimen was forwarded to **Dr. C. J. McCoy** at the **Carnegie Museum**.

## Background & Description

- **Genus:** *Gerrhonotus* (alligator lizards), family [[Anguidae]].
- **Species discussed:**
  - *Gerrhonotus lugoi* — a previously described species from the Cuatro Ciénegas floor; four specimens held live in the Museo de Historia Natural.
  - *Gerrhonotus liocephalus* — identified as the correct taxon for the newly collected specimens, rather than *G. lugoi*.
- **Diagnostic basis for reclassification:** Variation in **scalation** (scale counts/patterns) within the population distinguishes it from typical *G. liocephalus* and from *G. lugoi*, supporting recognition of a **new, undescribed subspecies**.
- **Specimen holdings:**
  - 3 live specimens of the *G. liocephalus*-affiliated population (1 + 2 others) maintained alive at the university museum.
  - 4 specimens of *G. lugoi* also held live at the same institution.
  - 1 additional *G. liocephalus* specimen sent to the [[Carnegie Museum]] for examination by Dr. C. J. McCoy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Specimens were collected on the **"floor of the Basin"** — i.e., the low-elevation terrestrial/wetland-margin habitat of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], a desert basin in Coahuila, Mexico famed for gypsum dunes, spring-fed pools, and high aquatic endemism.
- **Locality specifics:** A key specimen (Minckley's) is believed to have been collected at **Tío Candido**, a named site within the basin's spring/pool complex network commonly referenced in Cuatro Ciénegas biodiversity studies.
- **Context of fieldwork:** The author's field research (1986–1987) focused on **spatial relationships within the herpetofauna** of the basin, situating this reptile survey alongside the region's better-known aquatic biodiversity (fish, invertebrates) studied by researchers such as Minckley and Hershler during their 1986 visit.
- **Biogeographic significance:** The presence of *G. liocephalus* on the basin floor, alongside the more narrowly distributed *G. lugoi*, suggests **sympatry or micro-habitat partitioning** between closely related anguid lizard taxa within this isolated desert basin ecosystem — a pattern analogous to the fine-scale endemism documented for the basin's aquatic species (e.g., [[Cyprinodon]] pupfishes, [[Cuatro Ciénegas Snails]]).

## Key Findings

- Specimens initially field-identified as *[[Gerrhonotus lugoi]]* were **re-identified as *Gerrhonotus liocephalus***.
- Scale-count variation within this *G. liocephalus* population indicates it likely constitutes a **new, undescribed subspecies**, pending formal description.
- A **preliminary report** on this taxonomic finding was planned for presentation at the **X Congreso Nacional de Zoología**, Mexico City, October (year inferred ~1989).
- **Dr. C. J. McCoy** (Carnegie Museum) was independently aware of *G. liocephalus* occurring on the Cuatro Ciénegas basin floor, and received a voucher specimen — indicating cross-institutional corroboration of the finding.
- The letter requests **collection locality data** from Minckley for a specimen thought to be from **Tío Candido**, highlighting the importance of precise locality records for resolving basin-floor lizard taxonomy.
- This correspondence documents an early step in what would become formal taxonomic work on Cuatro Ciénegas anguid lizards, paralleling the intensive contemporaneous research on the basin's endemic aquatic fauna by [[W. L. Minckley]] and collaborators.
