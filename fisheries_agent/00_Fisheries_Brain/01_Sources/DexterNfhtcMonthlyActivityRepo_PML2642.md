# Dexter NFH&TC Monthly Activity Reports, October–December 2007

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This three-part monthly activity report from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (Dexter NFH&TC), New Mexico, covers October through December 2007. It documents federally endangered [[Southwestern Native Fishes]] work: tagging and distribution of [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]] harvest and stocking, [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]] health and genetics, [[Devils Hole Pupfish]] baseline studies, fish health surveillance, toxicology, outreach, and facility maintenance. The report is operational rather than a formal study, so most entries are status updates and task completions.

## Background & Description

Dexter NFH&TC is a federal hatchery and research center whose staff includes a center director, assistant director, administrative and maintenance personnel, fish biologists, a [[Molecular Ecology Laboratory]], and a [[Dexter Fish Health Unit]]. Staff are organized into the hatchery operations, genetics, and fish health groups. Several staff are cross-listed with the [[New Mexico State University]] postdoctoral and student programs.

The species mentioned in the report include:

- **Bonytail Chub** (*Gila elegans*), grown out at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and its satellite facility at Achii Hanyo, Arizona.
- **Colorado Pikeminnow** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), tagged, distributed, and held in captive stocks.
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), harvested at [[Uvalde National Fish Hatchery]] and grown out at Achii Hanyo.
- **Rio Grande Silvery Minnow** (*Hybognathus amarus*), sampled for health assessment and stocked in the Middle Rio Grande.
- **Devils Hole Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), studied through tissue genotyping and major histocompatibility complex (MHC) work.
- **Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii virginalis*), genotyped for the Comanche Creek restoration project.
- **Fathead Minnow** (*Pimephales promelas*), cell lines supplied to a partner laboratory.
- A species coded WDF and others coded CPM, BTC, RBS, and RGSM appear in the distribution table; codes are not fully resolved in the source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most activities concern recovery of endangered fishes in the [[Colorado River Basin]], the [[San Juan River]], and the [[Rio Grande]]. The report links hatchery work to:

- **Captive propagation and stocking:** Fish are reared at Dexter, Uvalde, Willow Beach, and Achii Hanyo, then moved to river reaches for recovery.
- **Disease surveillance:** The [[National Wild Fish Health Survey]] and routine inspections screen wild and cultured fish for pathogens, including [[Largemouth Bass Virus]] (LMBV) and bacterial kidney disease.
- **Genetic management:** Genotyping and sequencing support population management, including a [[Genetic Management and Captive Propagation Plan]] for Bonytail Chub.
- **Toxicology:** An acute toxicity test assessed potassium chloride effects on juvenile Bonytail Chub, in collaboration with the [[USGS Columbia Environmental Research Center]].
- **Partnerships:** Collaboration spans the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] regional office.

## Key Findings

### Tagging, Distribution, and Stocking

- Over 2,300 Bonytail Chub were wire-tagged by Guadalupe Burger and Dexter staff in October and shipped to the Lower Colorado River.
- Colorado Pikeminnow were VIE-tagged (count not legible in the scan), sorted by length, and transported to the San Juan River and captive facilities.
- The 2007 Fish Distribution Report, submitted by Roger Hammon to the regional office, lists individual fish records by code, size, and agency. Examples include a Razorback Sucker (RBS 794) at 6.73 in for the Bureau of Reclamation.
- In December, 30,000 Rio Grande Silvery Minnow at 1.5 in and 600 at 3.0 in were stocked into the Middle Rio Grande, New Mexico.

### Razorback Sucker and Hatchery Operations

- Manuel Ulibarri assisted with Razorback Sucker harvest at Uvalde National Fish Hatchery in Texas and prepared a cooperative agreement with the Bureau of Reclamation.
- Young-of-year Razorback Suckers were stocked into six ponds at Dexter, and harvested fish were moved from ponds in December.

### Fish Health

- Two wild salmon populations and one wild trout population were tested under the National Wild Fish Health Survey. Testing is ongoing.
- John Thoesen completed a fish health inspection at Achii Hanyo, a satellite facility of Willow Beach that has grown out native fish since 1998.
- Jason Woodland and Phil Hines processed Rio Grande Silvery Minnow samples from a two-week collection. Bacterial and viral samples were sent to Dexter for testing, and external parasites were examined at collection.
- Dexter's fish health unit received bacterial kidney disease and viral samples from Willow Beach and Apache Creek NFH in Arizona for testing.

### Genetics and Molecular Work

- Connie Keeler-Foster completed the draft Genetic Management and Captive Propagation Plan for Bonytail Chub, which incorporates stocks from Willow Beach, the Little Colorado River, and the mainstem Colorado River. Sheni Baker reviewed the plan's quality control and statistical analyses.
- Sheni Baker genotyped Devils Hole Pupfish tissue samples for a baseline study, sharing reference samples with the University of Colorado at Boulder and Dr. Tony Echelle of Oklahoma State University.
- Gerry Zegers sequenced MHC class I genes in Devils Hole Pupfish and three related species (including Amargosa and White Sands pupfish), with sequences validated against known alleles. He planned to present results at the Desert Fishes Council meeting in Ventura, California, in November.
- René Martin worked on a single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) assay to distinguish trout species, with completion expected by the end of the calendar year. She also prepared a poster for the Amphibian Declines and Chytridiomycosis conference.

### Toxicology

- Catherine Sykes completed an acute toxicity test on 4-inch Bonytail Chub to determine the median lethal concentration (LC50) of potassium chloride.
- Sykes also genotyped Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout from the Comanche Creek restoration project and conducted sampling of Rio Grande Silvery Minnow in Albuquerque.

### Outreach and Partnerships

- Sabrina Michaels, a Carlsbad high school student, visited to discuss research and will study the genetics of two species. René Martin is her principal mentor.
- In December, Carole Conway led a tour of about 40 students from Dexter Middle School. Staff described hatchery history, molecular ecology, and fish pathogen testing.
- Dexter received 16 visitors in the reporting period and 25 in December.

### Facility Maintenance

- Bill Williams completed pond liner repairs at ponds 2 and 3, and staff repaired a leaky valve near Well #2.
- A carport was completed in the administrative building parking lot.
- Staff reinstalled netting over ponds to exclude birds.
- Staff completed credit card and travel training, and seasonal flu vaccinations were given to all staff.
