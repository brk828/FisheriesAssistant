# Otolith-Based Genera and Lineages of Fossil Bonefishes (Clupeiformes, Albulidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #albulidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 paleontological monograph by [[Don L. Frizzell]] (Rolla, Missouri) describes fossil bonefishes of the family [[Albulidae]] from isolated sagittae (saccular otoliths) recovered from Cretaceous and Cenozoic strata of the American [[Gulf Coast]], supplemented by published records. The paper proposes six new genera and five new species, figures the otolith of [[Dixonina]] for the first time, and proposes a phylogenetic arrangement of albulid genera from the Early Cretaceous to Recent. The author argues that otoliths, as structures of the auditory labyrinth, evolve largely independently of natural selection and therefore serve as a reliable "evolutionary clock" for building lineages. The family is interpreted as peaking in number of species in the early Cenozoic before declining to two living genera, which the author attributes to competition for a preferred near-shore environment.

## Background & Description

- **Otolith value:** Fossil otoliths are far more abundant than articulated skeletons, occur in non-laminar beds where scales cannot be extracted, and reflect a nervous-system structure that is buried within the cranium.
- **Why bonefishes:** Albulid sagittae are large and conspicuous, familial identification is unmistakable, and the family has been largely confined to inter-tidal and sublittoral zones, so its otoliths should appear in many exposed marine strata.
- **Sagitta morphology:** The albulid sagitta is subelliptical to subpyriform or depressed scutiform, concavoconvex to biconvex, and variably rostrate. Key features are the sulcus (ostium plus cauda), the postcaudal keel or flange, and a small notch on the keel margin that links [[Albula]] and [[Dixonina]].
- **Taxonomic approach:** The author supports genera composed of otolith-based species, treating these as supplementary to osteological taxonomy. Type specimens are retained by the author pending deposition in a central institution.
- **Acknowledgments and funding:** Work was supported by [[National Science Foundation]] grants G-10221 (1959–1961) and GB-385 (1962–1964), with material loaned by several institutions and collectors. The paper is dedicated to [[Wilhelm Weiler]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recent bonefish (Albula vulpes):**
- Inhabits inter-tidal zones, sometimes so shallow that its fins are exposed. Reported from depths of 36 m (116 ft) and 39 m (21 fathoms) in the Gulf of Mexico.
- World-wide in warm waters, ranging in the western Atlantic from Rio de Janeiro to Cape Cod and in the eastern Pacific to San Diego. Also found in Florida Keys quiet waters, Hawaiian surf zones, estuaries, and fresh water.
- Restricted within its range: common in Florida but absent from Texas sandy beaches. The author suggests lowered salinity from the [[Mississippi River]] may act as a barrier to shoreline migration.
- Feeds on sandy bottoms, primarily shellfish crushed by pavement-like teeth, and forms loose schools. Feeding involves expelling water jets to uncover prey.
- Larvae are elongate and band-shaped, shrinking from about 87.5 mm to 50.0 mm; they are surface-dwelling at night in West Indian harbors.

**Dixonina nemoptera:**
- Extremely rare, presumably nearing extinction. Known from the Caribbean (type locality "Santo Domingo") and recently taken by bottom trawl off the Colombian coast.
- Inshore, at less than 200 m, probably 28–46 m (15–25 fathoms). Adults occupy deeper water than Albula, and the two are not found together (data from [[J. R. Thompson]], personal communication, 1964).

**Fossil occurrences:**
- Paleocene to Oligocene albulid otoliths occur only in sandy and shelly near-shore strata of the eastern Mississippi Embayment, none farther west than west-central Louisiana.
- Thousands of otoliths from middle Eocene argillaceous greensands of central and eastern Texas yielded no albulid sagittae, suggesting muddy bottoms were unsuitable.
- No juvenile otoliths are known from Cenozoic Gulf Coast rocks, implying juveniles lived farther offshore. Two Cretaceous *Prealbula* instances include a mass mortality of juveniles.

## Key Findings

**1. Systematic results**
- Six new genera proposed: *Palealbula* (type *P. neocomiensis* (Priem, 1908), Lower Cretaceous, France), *Prealbula* (type *P. weileri* n. sp.), *Archaealbula* (type *A. alabamae* n. sp., Paleocene, Alabama), *Protalbula* (type *P. sohli* n. sp., Upper Cretaceous, Georgia), *Eoalbula* (type *E. meridiana* n. sp., Mississippi), and *Metalbula* (type *M. bashiana* n. sp.).
- The Recent genera *Albula* (*A. vulpes*) and *Dixonina* (*D. nemoptera*) are redescribed from otolith material.
- Family diagnosis recognizes 2 living and 6 extinct genera.
- *Metalbula* is the most species-rich genus, with 13 species noted in this study and a range from Paleocene to lower Miocene.

**2. Evolutionary trends**
- Origin in the Early Cretaceous (possibly late Jurassic), distinct from the Pterothrissidae.
- Peak diversity in the early Cenozoic, then reduction to two living genera.
- Sagitta outline shifted from high subpyriform (ancestral) to elongate-elliptical (*Archaealbula*) and low subscutiform (*Albula*).
- Sagitta size increased from a maximum of 9.5 mm (*Palealbula*) to 20.0 mm (undescribed *Eoalbula*, upper Eocene) and at least 15.0 mm in Recent *Albula*.
- The cauda became progressively more downturned, the ostium opening migrated from the anterodorsal slope to the dorsal margin, and the postcaudal expansion diverged into a flange or keel.

**3. Lineage hypothesis**
- Four lineages recognized: the *Palealbula* lineage (Palealbula plus Prealbula, the parent stock), the *Eoalbula* lineage, the *Albula* lineage (Protalbula, Metalbula, Albula, Dixonina; the most successful), and the *Archaealbula* lineage (a Paleocene offshoot of Prealbula that did not survive).
- Metalbula is postulated as the direct ancestor of both Albula and Dixonina.

**4. Competition hypothesis**
- Early Cretaceous bonefishes faced little competition in an abundant near-shore niche.
- Early Cenozoic competition intensified from albulids and shallow-water teleosts. The *Archaealbula* and *Eoalbula* lineages lacked competitive vigor and went extinct, while the *Albula* lineage was dominant but largely reduced.
- The inter-tidal and sublittoral niche became filled by Albula vulpes, while Dixonina survived by withdrawing to deeper water.

**5. Methodological argument**
- Otoliths are presented as an evolutionary clock because they are protected within the auditory bullae, subject to mutation rather than directive environmental selection.
- The author cautions about intraspecific variation, convergence, degeneration of sagittae, and mismatches between otolith groupings and conventional taxa.

**6. Notable specimen data**
- *Prealbula weileri* holotype: maximum length 6.1 mm; nine juveniles (2.4–2.8 mm) are paratypes, with a large specimen of 6.9 mm.
- Recent *Albula vulpes*: three sets of sagittae examined from Magdalena Bay, Baja California; two sets agree closely, while the third shows a missing notch, a bilobed dome, and serrate ventral margin, which may reflect genetic difference.
- *Dixonina nemoptera*: one set of sagittae from the Colombian Caribbean coast, 14.3 mm long, figured for the first time.
