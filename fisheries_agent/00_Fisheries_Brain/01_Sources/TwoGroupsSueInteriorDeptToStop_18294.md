# Livestock Weekly: Lawsuit Over Endangered Species Act Listings and Texas Livestock Markets

#mammalogy #conservation-policy #livestock-policy

## Executive Summary

This is a trade newspaper bundle (*Livestock Weekly*, early 1993), not a research paper. Its lead story reports a lawsuit by [[Putting People First]] (PPF) and the Paris-based [[Fondation Internationale Pour La Sauvegarde De Gibier]] against the [[Department Of The Interior]] over the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) listing of the [[Argali Bighorn Sheep]] as endangered. The plaintiffs allege that Interior used inferior data and conducted improper meetings with protectionist groups. The issue also covers related ESA disputes in Texas, a settlement that PPF says broadened ESA reach, San Saba cattle auction prices, and a business commentary. The ecological content is limited to species-listing claims and is not independently verified.

## Background & Description

- **Argali bighorn sheep:** The lawsuit describes the Argali population as "up to a quarter million" animals, inhabiting mountainous terrain in China, Mongolia, and Russia. The plaintiffs argue the species is not at risk.
- **Lawsuit parties:** PPF is a Washington watchdog group chaired by [[Kathleen Marquardt]]. The co-plaintiff is the Paris-based Fondation Internationale, described as having a "protectionist" affiliation in the suit.
- **Commentary voices:** Property rights proponent [[Sue Showers]] called a December settlement a "set-up."
- **Newspaper context:** The issue also carries the San Saba auction report and a Texas Association of Business column by president H. Dane Harris (dated January 21, 1993).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Argali and trophy hunting economics.** The suit argues that Mongolia's main hard-currency source in recent years has been permit fees charged to Argali hunters. PPF says these fees gave the government a strong incentive to conserve the sheep. The listing, it argues, removes that incentive and also blocks import of breeding specimens for zoological programs.

**Scientific research restrictions.** The listing reportedly prohibits scientists from studying Argali DNA to explore unique traits.

**African comparison.** The article notes that some African countries that gave elephants total legal protection saw them decimated by poachers. Other countries that gave villages commercial rights and a share of proceeds saw poaching nearly eliminated.

**Concho water snake (West Texas).** The article says objective researchers disproved virtually every claim used to list this species. It states that the listing still stands and was based on "flawed and even possibly fraudulent" data.

**Edwards Aquifer and riparian areas.** Some ESA listings have reportedly challenged private ownership of water in the Edwards Aquifer. A proposed listing farther west could make riparian areas along wet-weather creeks and draws off-limits to livestock. Observers fear it could extend to watersheds broadly.

**Multispecies ecosystem approach.** PPF warns that this approach could protect an entire ecosystem if a single species, such as the greenest tiger beetle, is found on private land. Such protection could restrict farming, hunting, logging, development, and even children playing in yards.

## Key Findings

1. **Procedural challenge to a listing:** The suit alleges Interior relied on data that was "qualitatively inferior, decades old, anecdotal, hearsay, or limited" to part of the Argali range, and ignored superior data that conflicted with its conclusions.
2. **Data handling:** Interior reportedly gave its only copy of detailed Chinese data to a "foreign individual" affiliated with a protectionist organization. That individual submitted "a biased summary" that the government adopted. The suit questions whether Interior ever recovered the factual data.
3. **Private-group access:** The suit charges Interior held off-the-record meetings, shared opposition comments and agency plans with restrictive-listing groups, and reportedly funded rebuttals to submissions that contradicted its position.
4. **Settlement impact:** PPF says a settlement reached in December, which settled a suit seeking to speed hundreds of listings, "gave activists everything they wanted." The plaintiffs argue it opens the door to a broader multispecies ecosystem approach.
5. **Land and water restrictions:** The article says ESA listings already impose land-use restrictions in the habitat of protected animals and plants. Edwards Aquifer water ownership and riparian grazing limits are presented as the main Texas concerns.
6. **Market context (San Saba auction, week before report):** Receipts totaled 1,588 head. Stocker and feeder steers and heifers sold fully steady to instances higher. Packer cows and bulls were $2–3 higher, and stock cows were strong.
   - Feeder steers (200–300 lb): $110–130
   - Feeder heifers (200–300 lb): $95–115
   - Slaughter cows: $40–55; bulls: $55–70
   - Bred stock cows: $450–1,030 per head; cows with calves: $650–1,150 per pair
7. **Business outlook (Harris, January 1993):** Harris describes the economic outlook as mostly positive, citing low interest rates, low inflation, strong productivity, and lower unemployment. He argues that "only the free market—not government—can solve" remaining problems and warns against new government regulation.
