# Fecundity and Reproduction in the Largescale Menhaden (Brevoortia patronus)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #brevoortia

## Executive Summary

This 1961 study by Royal D. Suttkus and Bangalore I. Sundararaj, Department of Zoology, [[Tulane University]], examines the reproductive biology of the [[Largescale Menhaden]] (*Brevoortia patronus*) in [[Louisiana Coast|Louisiana coastal waters]]. The authors analyzed 280 specimens collected between 1951 and 1958, mostly by [[Otter Trawl]], and estimated fecundity with a [[Volumetric Method]]. Gonad volume peaked in January, which indicates that spawning occurs in late winter. The authors describe the largescale menhaden as a rapidly renewable resource because of its short life and early maturity.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Brevoortia Patronus]], the largescale menhaden, a schooling marine fish of the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Prior knowledge:** No published accounts of spawning observations existed. [[Suttkus 1956]] estimated that spawning occurred between October and February in the Louisiana area, based on gonad examination, extrapolation, and [[Length-Frequency Analysis]].
- **Behavior:** Menhaden fishermen along the Louisiana coast knew that schools move to intermediate depths in late fall, before spawning.
- **Obstacle to observation:** Waters off the mouth of the [[Mississippi River]], where menhaden concentrate, are turbid most of the time. The authors suggest this partly explains the lack of spawning observations.
- **Sampling:** The 280 specimens came from the east Louisiana coast between 1951 and 1958. Most were taken by otter trawl. The two January 1958 samples were taken with a [[Mid-Water Trawl]] in the [[Gulf of Mexico]].
- **Measurements:** Total length ran from the snout tip to the tip of the lower caudal lobe. Standard length ran from the snout tip to the posterior margin of the hypural, located by flexing the caudal fin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Location:** Specimens were collected along the east Louisiana coast and off the mouth of the Mississippi River, in the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Seasonal movement:** Schools shift to intermediate depths in late fall. Gonads enlarge during this period in preparation for spawning.
- **Water conditions:** Turbidity around the river mouth limits direct observation of spawning.
- **Population structure:** Age group II dominates the spawning population. The authors describe the species as a rapidly renewable resource, a point they connect to its short life span and early maturity.
- **Age determination:** Ages were read from scales, following the methods of [[June and Roithmayr 1960]], which were developed for [[Atlantic Menhaden]]. Each scale was read three times using a microprojector.

## Key Findings

**1. Spawning timing**
- Gonad volume was at its maximum in January, which indicates a spawning peak in that month. This confirms the estimated October-to-February window and narrows the peak to January.
- Gonads enlarged during late fall. Spawning was completed in February and/or March.
- January ovaries were firm and highly vascularized, which indicates they were close to spawning. March ovaries were flaccid, and their vascularization had degenerated.
- Yearly fluctuations in peak timing are likely, based on length-frequency evidence from [[Suttkus 1956]].

**2. Age composition of the spawning population**
- Two January samples (30 and 31 January 1958, 85 fish) were the only ones large enough for age-composition analysis. Age group II made up 85.9% (73 fish), age group I 9.4% (8 fish), and age group III 4.5% (4 fish).
- Mean total lengths were 182.6 mm (age I), 202.7 mm (age II), and 219.5 mm (age III).
- No age group IV fish appeared in the January samples. Across all 280 specimens, only 3 were age group IV.

**3. Fecundity**
- Fecundity was estimated by the volumetric method, which counts large eggs in a subsample and scales the count to the whole ovary.
- Accuracy was checked against actual total counts for one age I and one age II ovary. The method gave estimates of 21,960 vs. 20,827 eggs (-5.4% error) for age I, and 36,000 vs. 34,059 eggs (-5.6% error) for age II. The average percent error was 5.5.
- Mean fecundity in January 1958 (18 specimens) rose with age and length:

| Age group | Specimens | Mean total length (mm) | Mean ovary volume (cc) | Mean eggs per female |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 2 | 176 | 4.0 | 21,960 |
| II | 14 | 209 | 12.7 | 68,655 |
| III | 2 | 228 | 17.2 | 122,062 |

**4. Gonad volume**
- Age group II females averaged slightly over 11 cc of gonad volume in the January sample. Age group II males averaged about 7 cc.
- Part of this difference reflects the larger average size of females. The rest reflects a real difference in gonad volume between the sexes at similar sizes.
- Total length and gonad volume were positively related in the January sample (see [[Gonad Maturation]]).

**5. Conclusions**
- The largescale menhaden spawns in late winter, with a peak in January, after a late-fall gonad enlargement.
- Fecundity rises with age and length, so older, larger females produce considerably more eggs.
- The short life span and early maturity of the species make it a rapidly renewable fishery resource.
