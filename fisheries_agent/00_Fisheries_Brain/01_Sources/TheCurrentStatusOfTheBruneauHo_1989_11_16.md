## Executive Summary

This paper documents the critical decline of the **Bruneau Hot Springs Snail**, an undescribed monotypic genus within the family [[Hydrobiidae]], endemic to a highly restricted geothermal habitat at Hot Creek and adjacent thermal seeps along the [[Bruneau River]] in Owyhee County, southern Idaho. The species' historically dominant habitat, the "Indian Bathtub" spring (formerly hosting ~77,000 snails in 1982 with a 2,500 gpm surface flow), has gone completely dry due to groundwater pumping and reduced seasonal recharge. By September 1989, surveys estimated only 52.4 m² of remaining seep habitat and as few as 64,683 individuals. The note also documents an established population of invasive [[Guppy|guppies]] (*Lebistes reticulatus*, syn. *Poecilia reticulata*) thriving in the thermal waters, alongside a broader assemblage of endemic and thermally-restricted invertebrates and mollusks. The authors call for immediate Critical Groundwater Area designation to prevent extinction of this narrowly endemic thermal-spring ecosystem.

## Background & Description

- **Bruneau Hot Springs Snail**: An undescribed, monotypic genus in [[Hydrobiidae]]; taxonomically distinct enough to warrant new genus status (species and genus description pending at time of publication).
- **Reproductive biology**: In aquarium settings, snails are **not photophobic** and exhibit **two annual reproductive cycles**, with juvenile cohorts appearing in **June** and **November**.
- **Thermal tolerance**: Field observations indicate a preferred thermal range of **31–32.8°C**.
- **Associated fauna morphology** (not detailed in depth, but taxonomically noted):
  - *Ambrysus mormon minor* (Naucoridae, an aquatic true bug)
  - *Hydroscaphis natans* (a beetle, family Hydroscaphidae)
  - Co-occurring native mollusks: *Oxyloma haydeni* (Succineidae, land snail), *Physa gyrina* (Physidae), *Fossaria exigua* (Lymnaeidae), *Gyraulus vermicularis* (Planorbidae)
- **Guppy (*Lebistes reticulatus*)**: No morphological detail provided; noted simply as a large, well-established wild population — an introduced/exotic species surviving in a broad thermal range (30–35.5°C).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat specificity**: The snail is restricted to a narrow geothermal niche — surface seeps and spring outflows historically fed by a thermally-buffered aquifer lens along Hot Creek.
- **Thermal habitat degradation**:
  - Upper seep springs at Hot Creek are measured **3–6°C cooler** than historic records, indicating aquifer drawdown and loss of thermal buffering.
  - Approximately **half of the elevational lens** in which surface water historically appeared is now dry.
  - Regional **aquifer drawdown** is occurring broadly across the Bruneau-Grandview area due to groundwater pumping.
- **Disturbance history**: A 1985 cloudburst-triggered flash flood deposited several feet of coarse sediment into the Hot Creek channel; lower Hot Creek remains vulnerable to future flash-flood impacts.
- **Co-occurring endemic/rare taxa**:
  - *Ambrysus mormon minor* — restricted to this site; formerly present at one other thermal spring where it is now extirpated.
  - *Hydroscaphis natans* — northernmost and one of few Idaho localities.
  - *Epicactis gigantea* (Giant Helleborine, state-listed Threatened orchid) — historically recorded at the site but now apparently extirpated locally.
- **Invasive species interactions**:
  - **[[Guppy]]** (*Lebistes reticulatus*) — a large, well-established wild population occupies Hot Creek and the thermal plume of the Bruneau River, tolerating temperatures between 30°C and 35.5°C, directly overlapping the snail's thermal niche and raising concern for competitive/ecological interactions in shared habitat.
  - **[[New Zealand Mud Snail|Potamopyrgus antipodarum]]** — an exotic hydrobiid that has invaded the Middle Snake River but had **not yet colonized** the Bruneau Hot Creek/River system as of this report, representing a latent invasion threat.
- **Comparative faunal context**: The Bruneau River mollusk fauna (excluding the endemic Hot Springs Snail) includes *Fluminicola hindsi*, *Fontelicella* sp. (Hydrobiidae), *Anodonta californiensis*, *Gonidea angulata* (Unionidae), and *Pisidium* sp. (Sphaeridae) — described as a species-poor derivative of the broader [[Snake River]] faunal assemblage.

## Key Findings

- The **Indian Bathtub** spring site, once the largest and most productive habitat (est. 77,000 snails in 1982; 2,500 gpm flow), is **now completely dry**.
- Groundwater pumping and diminished seasonal recharge have caused sustained drawdown of surface seep and spring water levels for years.
- A September 1989 survey (Minshall's group) estimated only **52.4 m² of remaining seep habitat** and **64,683 snails**, marking a severe population contraction from historic levels.
- Some additional marginal habitat exists in streamside vegetation along Hot Creek, but its capacity is limited.
- Upper Hot Creek seep temperatures have cooled 3–6°C relative to historical baselines, threatening the thermal niche required by the snail (31–32.8°C).
- The 1985 flash flood demonstrated the ongoing vulnerability of lower Hot Creek to catastrophic sedimentation events.
- A thriving invasive guppy population coexists with the endemic snail in overlapping thermal waters, though direct competitive impacts are not quantified in this report.
- The invasive **[[New Zealand Mud Snail]]** (*Potamopyrgus antipodarum*) has invaded the Middle Snake River but has not yet reached this site — an urgent conservation concern given its potential to displace native hydrobiids.
- The authors conclude that **Critical Groundwater Area designation is urgently needed** to halt new groundwater pumping, alongside emergency ("triage") conservation measures, to prevent extinction of the Bruneau Hot Springs Snail and other co-endemic thermally-restricted species.
