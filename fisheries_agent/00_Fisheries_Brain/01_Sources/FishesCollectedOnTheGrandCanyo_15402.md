# Fishes Collected on the Grand Canyon Survey, Lee's Ferry to Diamond Creek, August 1968

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae #GrandCanyon #ColoradoRiver

## Executive Summary

This 1968 ichthyofaunal survey of the Colorado River through [[Grand Canyon]], conducted from Lee's Ferry downstream to Diamond Creek, documents both native and introduced fish species. The survey is historically significant as it produced the first specimens of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) collected since its original description from the Grand Canyon, and it established the first documented hybrid cross between [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) and [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*). A central finding is that introduced species substantially outnumber native species in both diversity and abundance, reflecting significant ecological alteration of the Colorado River system, likely linked to post-Glen Canyon Dam flow regulation and decades of stocking activity.

## Background & Description

Limited morphological detail is provided in the source text; species are primarily distinguished taxonomically by genus/species identity and common name. Notable species-level notes include:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha* Miller) — Distinctive native cyprinid; type locality associated with the mouth of [[Bright Angel Creek]]. Only 6 individuals were collected from the main river during this survey.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) — Small-bodied cyprinid, common in tributary streams but rare in the main river channel, suggesting a preference for smaller, possibly clearer or cooler tributary habitats over main-stem conditions.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and **[[Bluehead Sucker]]** (*Catostomus discobolus*) — Both catostomids show morphological/ecological adaptation to riffle habitats; Bluehead Sucker specifically favors swift-water riffle zones in both main river and tributaries.
- **Hybrid specimen** (*Notropis lutrensis* × *Pimephales promelas*) — One juvenile documented from [[Shinumo Creek]], representing the first recorded instance of this interspecific cross.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Native species (habitat associations):**
- *Gila cypha* (Humpback Chub) — Main river channel specialist; rare, found in low numbers.
- *Rhinichthys osculus* (Speckled Dace) — Tributary-dominant; documented in [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Tapeats Creek]], and [[Diamond Creek]]; rare in main river.
- *Catostomus latipinnis* (Flannelmouth Sucker) — Common throughout the main river.
- *Catostomus discobolus* (Bluehead Sucker) — Common in riffle habitats of both main river and swift tributaries.

**Introduced species (habitat associations):**
- *Salmo gairdneri* (Rainbow Trout) — Restricted to cooler tributary streams: [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Tapeats Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]].
- *Cyprinus carpio* (Carp) — Common in the main river; established since approximately the turn of the 20th century.
- *Notropis lutrensis* (Red Shiner) — Common in main river; a recent invader (within ~20 years of the survey).
- *Pimephales promelas* (Fathead Minnow) — Abundant in main river; also a recent invader (~20 years prior).
- *Ictalurus punctatus* (Channel Catfish) — Abundant in main river; established since approximately the turn of the 20th century.
- *Ictalurus melas* (Black Bullhead) — Rare; single young specimen taken 64 miles below Lee's Ferry.

**Community structure:** The survey reveals a river ecosystem where introduced species (particularly Channel Catfish, Carp, Fathead Minnow, and Red Shiner) dominate the main channel in both abundance and richness, while native fishes—especially Speckled Dace—persist more successfully in tributary refugia. This pattern is consistent with broader concerns regarding native fish decline in the regulated [[Colorado River]] following anthropogenic changes to flow, temperature, and turbidity regimes.

## Key Findings

- **Native species outnumbered by introductions**: Introduced fishes exceed native fishes in both species count and individual abundance throughout the surveyed reach.
- **Historic Humpback Chub rediscovery**: This survey yielded the first specimens of *Gila cypha* collected in the Grand Canyon since the species' original description (based on a specimen from the mouth of Bright Angel Creek); only 6 individuals were found.
- **Archaeological corroboration**: *Gila cypha* remains were also identified from [[Stanton's Cave]] in Marble Canyon (~32 river miles below Lee's Ferry), indicating a longer historical presence in the canyon than modern collections alone suggest.
- **First hybrid record**: Documentation of a *Notropis lutrensis* × *Pimephales promelas* hybrid juvenile from Shinumo Creek represents the first known record of this cross.
- **Timeline of invasions**: Carp and Channel Catfish have inhabited the Colorado River since approximately 1900, while Red Shiner and Fathead Minnow are more recent invaders, having colonized within roughly the 20 years preceding the survey (i.e., since ~1948).
- **Tributary refugia importance**: Native [[Speckled Dace]] and introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] both show strong tributary affinities, highlighting the ecological importance of clear, cooler tributary streams (e.g., Bright Angel, Tapeats, Havasu, Shinumo, Diamond creeks) within the broader main-stem river ecosystem.
