# Rudd Creek Survey Trip Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) personnel and Student Conservation Association (SCA) volunteers surveyed fish in [[Rudd Creek]] on June 13, 2006. The survey re-assessed the native fish assemblage, with special emphasis on [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], and checked whether the creek held permanent water. A previous April 2006 survey at 5 sites found no spinedace. This survey collected three native species, including spinedace, and found no non-native fish. Crayfish were very numerous. Only the lower creek held continuous flowing water.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Re-assess the status of the native fish assemblage, with emphasis on Little Colorado spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*), and assess permanent water in the creek.
- **Prior work:** An April 2006 AGFD survey sampled 5 random sites over more than 3 miles of stream and collected no spinedace. The report notes that spinedace may have been missed if they are uncommon or localized.
- **Personnel:** AGFD staff (Region I) with SCA volunteers. The report names Mike Lapeq (AGFD) and SCA volunteers Dan Sullins and Elizabeth Stearns (names as recovered from scanned text).
- **Sampling design:** Quality habitat was targeted to maximize the chance of collecting spinedace. Fish were seined in pools and runs, identified to species, and released. Sites are listed as 001 to 014 with UTM coordinates in the trip report table.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study reach and flow conditions**
- Survey walked about 3.3 miles of lower Rudd Creek, from the [[Nutrioso Creek]] confluence to the Sipe White Mountain Wildlife Area headquarters.
- The lowest 0.8 miles were dry.
- The next 1.6 miles had running water. Pools in this wetted section appeared deep and were expected to persist permanently. Native fish were found throughout this section.
- Continuous water extended a short distance into the lower reach of the wildlife area.
- Upstream, isolated pools were present, but the streambed was mostly dry. Several pools were inside exclosure fencing, and one large isolated pool was located on the north side of a bend. Isolated pools were not sampled.

**Species present**
- The survey collected three native species: Little Colorado spinedace, [[Speckled Dace]], and [[Bluehead Sucker]].
- Speckled dace and bluehead sucker were the most common fish overall.
- Spinedace were found in the lower reach, in the continuous-water section near the wildlife area.
- Spinedace sizes were reported in a range beginning at about 35 mm.
- No non-native fish were collected.
- Crayfish were very numerous throughout the creek.

## Key Findings

1. **Spinedace were confirmed in Rudd Creek.** After none were collected in April 2006, this survey found Little Colorado spinedace in the continuous-water reach, indicating the species persists in the creek.
2. **Native fish are restricted to the wetted lower reach.** Flowing and permanent pools occur only in the lower 1.6 miles. Upstream reaches were dry except for isolated pools.
3. **Assemblage is native-only.** Three native species were collected and no non-native fish were found, which is important for management of the population.
4. **Crayfish are abundant.** Crayfish were very numerous, though the report does not quantify their distribution beyond this observation.
5. **Catch totals.** The catch table lists a total of 817 fish across the sampled sites (as recovered from the scanned table; the individual site counts are not legible in the source).
6. **Management implication.** Persistence of the wetted reach and of the native fish assemblage depends on maintaining permanent pools and continuous flow, especially in the lower creek near the Sipe White Mountain Wildlife Area.
