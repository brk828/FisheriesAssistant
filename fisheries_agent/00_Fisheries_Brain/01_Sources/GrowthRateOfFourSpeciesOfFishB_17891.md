# Growth Rate of Four Species of Fish Between Holter Dam and Cascade, Montana

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #Catostomidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1950 study by Joseph W. Kathrein, conducted under the Montana State College Agricultural Experiment Station, examined age and growth rates of four fish species in a 35-mile section of the [[Missouri River]] between [[Holter Dam]] and Cascade, Montana, during the summers of 1948 and 1949. The study sampled 478 [[Rainbow Trout]], 127 [[Brown Trout]], 87 [[Longnose Sucker]], and 223 [[Western White Sucker]] to assess age structure, growth increments, and condition factor for fisheries management purposes. Growth was assessed via scale analysis, back-calculating total length at each year of life. Key findings show trout (game fish) grow substantially faster than suckers (forage fish), with both trout species exhibiting peak growth in their second year, while the two sucker species peaked in growth during year one (longnose) and year three (western white), respectively.

## Background & Description

- **Age determination method**: Scales taken from the left side of the fish, between the dorsal fin and lateral line, mounted in glycerine-gum arabic medium, and measured on a scale-projection machine.
  - Trout scales: measured from center of focus along the median-anterior radius.
  - Sucker scales: measured along the dorso-ventral diameter (per [[Spoor 1938]] methodology).
- **Length/weight measurement**: Total length measured to nearest 0.1 inch; weight measured to nearest 0.1 pound.
- **Condition Factor (C)** calculated via:
  `C = (W × 10^5) / L³` where W = weight in pounds, L = total length in inches.

### Coefficient of Condition (C)

- **Rainbow Trout**: ranged from 36.9 (Age I) to 29.5 (Age VI); overall average = **37.2** (n=481)
- **Brown Trout**: ranged from 36.3 (Age I) to 19.8 (Age VIII); overall average = **34.6** (n=117)
- Condition declined steadily with age in rainbow trout; brown trout condition generally improved with age except at year groups VII–VIII (small sample sizes limit confidence in older age classes for both species).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Location**: 35-mile stretch of the [[Missouri River]] between [[Holter Dam]] and Cascade, Montana. Upper 15 miles traverse a narrow meadowland valley; middle 8 miles pass through a canyon; remaining downstream section flows through open farmland valley.
- **Gradient**: Relatively low, at 3.1 feet of fall per mile.
- **Trophic roles**:
  - [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brown Trout]] are identified as the most important **game fish** species in this river section.
  - [[Longnose Sucker]] and [[Western White Sucker]] serve as important **forage species**, supporting the broader food web (including presumably the game trout populations).
- **Age structure differences reflect life-history strategy**:
  - Trout populations skewed toward younger age classes (Rainbow: 92% in age groups I–III; Brown: 62% in I–III, but with stronger representation in older groups V–IX at 28%).
  - Sucker populations skewed toward older age classes (Longnose: 95% in age groups IV–VIII; Western White Sucker: 58% in age groups IV–VIII).
- Comparative context: Missouri River rainbow trout growth exceeded rates reported for three Michigan streams ([[Shetter and Hazzard 1939]]); Missouri River brown trout growth exceeded rates from Crystal Creek, New York ([[Schuck 1945]]).

## Key Findings

1. **Sample sizes**: 478 rainbow trout, 127 brown trout, 87 longnose suckers, and 223 western white suckers were aged and measured.
2. **Age ranges observed**:
   - Rainbow Trout: age groups I–VII
   - Brown Trout: age groups I–IX
   - Longnose Sucker: age groups III–VIII (no fish found in age groups I–II)
   - Western White Sucker: age groups I–VIII
3. **Dominant age groups**: Age group I most common in rainbow and brown trout; Age group VI most common in longnose suckers; Age group III most common in western white suckers.
4. **Peak growth timing**:
   - Rainbow Trout & Brown Trout: greatest growth increment in **second year** of life.
   - Longnose Sucker: greatest growth increment in **first year**.
   - Western White Sucker: greatest growth increment in **third year**.
5. **Trout vs. sucker growth**: Trout grow markedly faster overall than suckers; brown trout grew faster than rainbow trout in nearly all years except years 5 and 7 (small sample caveats apply).
6. **Sucker growth comparison**: Longnose suckers had greater first-year growth increments than western white suckers, but calculated lengths converged closely for both species from year two onward.
7. **Population structure implications for management**: The predominance of younger trout age classes and older sucker age classes suggests differing exploitation/mortality or recruitment dynamics relevant to fisheries management of this Missouri River reach.
8. **Species identification**: Sucker taxonomy verified by [[Reeve M. Bailey]] (University of Michigan).
9. **Funding/support**: Study supported by the Cascade County Wildlife Association and Cascade Sportsmen's Club, in cooperation with the Montana Fish and Game Department.
