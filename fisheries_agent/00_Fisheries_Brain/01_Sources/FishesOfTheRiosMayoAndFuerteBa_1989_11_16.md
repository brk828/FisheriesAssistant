# Fishes of the Rios Mayo and Fuerte Basins, Sonora and Sinaloa, Mexico

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This study, presented at the XXI Annual [[Desert Fishes Council]] Meeting (1989), documents the ichthyofauna of two major Gulf of California drainages in northwestern Mexico: the [[Rio Mayo Basin]] and the [[Rio Fuerte Basin]]. Based on 19 collections in the Rio Mayo and 11 in the Rio Fuerte conducted between November 1988 and March 1989, researchers identified 22 fish species in the Rio Mayo (10 native, 65% relative abundance) and 25 species in the Rio Fuerte (12 native, 86.3% relative abundance). The study highlights the dominance of native [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]], [[Poeciliopsis monacha]] (including a unisexual form), and [[Agosia chrysogaster]] in the Rio Mayo, while [[Poeciliopsis latidens]] and [[Poecilia butleri]] dominated the Rio Fuerte. Several native species, including [[Ictalurus pricei]], [[Cichlasoma beani]], [[Poeciliopsis prolifica]], and [[Gila robusta]], were found to be scarce across both basins. The paper raises concern over the increasing presence of exotic species (notably [[Gambusia affinis]] and [[Tilapia]] spp.) co-occurring with native fishes, alongside habitat degradation from agricultural water use, threatening long-term population stability.

## Background & Description

The source text is a survey-based abstract and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of individual species. Notable taxonomic/reproductive features mentioned include:

- **[[Poeciliopsis monacha]]**: Documented with both a standard bisexual form and a **unisexual (gynogenetic/hybridogenetic) form**, a well-known reproductive characteristic of certain *Poeciliopsis* populations in this region.
- **Family-level composition**: Native assemblages are dominated by small-bodied live-bearers (Poeciliidae: *Poeciliopsis*, *Poecilia*), cyprinids (*Agosia*), catfish (Ictaluridae: *Ictalurus*), cichlids (*Cichlasoma*), and cyprinids (*Gila*), reflecting a typical Sonoran desert-adjacent freshwater fish assemblage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Both basins consist of low-to-medium elevation streams and rivers draining into the Gulf of California, sampled during a dry-to-transitional season window (Nov 1988–Mar 1989).
- **Native community structure**:
  - [[Rio Mayo Basin]]: Native species comprised 65% of relative abundance, led by [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]], [[Poeciliopsis monacha]], and [[Agosia chrysogaster]].
  - [[Rio Fuerte Basin]]: Native species comprised a much higher 86.3% of relative abundance, led by [[Poeciliopsis latidens]] and [[Poecilia butleri]].
- **Co-occurrence with exotics**: Exotic species were consistently captured in the same stream reaches occupied by native fishes, indicating direct habitat overlap and potential competitive/predatory interaction rather than spatial segregation.
  - [[Gambusia affinis]] was found associated with native *Poeciliopsis* spp. in both basins.
  - [[Tilapia]] sp. was found sharing habitat abundantly with the native [[Cichlasoma beani]] (Sinaloan Cichlid) at Arroyo Cuchujaqui, Sonora — suggesting a potentially significant competitive pressure on this scarce native cichlid.
- **Anthropogenic pressures**: The authors note a significant increase in factors threatening native fish population stability, primarily linked to:
  - Indiscriminate water extraction/use for agriculture.
  - Habitat alteration from agricultural development.
  - Introduction and spread of exotic species.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness**: 22 species recorded in [[Rio Mayo Basin]] (10 native); 25 species recorded in [[Rio Fuerte Basin]] (12 native).
- **Relative abundance of natives**: Higher in Rio Fuerte (86.3%) than in Rio Mayo (65%), despite similar or greater exotic species richness in Rio Fuerte.
- **Rare/scarce native species** (low collection numbers in both basins):
  - [[Ictalurus pricei]]
  - [[Cichlasoma beani]]
  - [[Poeciliopsis prolifica]]
  - [[Gila robusta]]
  - [[Poeciliopsis lucida]] (specifically scarce in Rio Fuerte)
- **Historical vs. current records**: Five species previously documented in these basins were **not collected** during this survey, suggesting possible local decline, extirpation, or sampling limitations.
- **Exotic-native overlap**: Exotics were not found in isolated or degraded habitats exclusively — they were captured directly within habitats occupied by native fishes, reinforcing concern about direct ecological interaction.
  - [[Gambusia affinis]] paired with *Poeciliopsis* spp. in both basins.
  - [[Tilapia]] sp. dominant alongside [[Cichlasoma beani]] at Arroyo Cuchujaqui.
- **Conservation concern**: The authors explicitly flag agricultural water use and habitat alteration, compounded by exotic species presence, as escalating threats to native fish community stability in the [[Sonoran Desert]] and [[Sinaloa]] region drainages.
- **Broader context**: This work contributes baseline distributional data for native desert fishes in Gulf of California drainages, relevant to regional [[Desert Fishes Council]] conservation efforts.
