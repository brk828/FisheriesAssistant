# Food-Limited Survival of Larval Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the Laboratory

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1990 study in *Environmental Biology of Fishes* (Papoulias & Minckley, Arizona State University) tests whether starvation contributes to chronic recruitment failure of [[Razorback Sucker]] in [[Lake Mohave]]. Three laboratory experiments on larvae hatched from Lake Mohave adults examined total food absence, delayed feeding, and variable food ration size. Larvae that were starved, fed too late, or given too little food mostly died between 20 and 30 days after hatching. The study identifies a [[Critical Period]] from about day 8 to day 19 after hatching and an irreversible starvation point between days 19 and 23. The authors conclude that food-related mortality may contribute to the absence of adult recruitment in Lake Mohave.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Abbott) is a large, endemic catostomid of the [[Colorado River Basin]], western U.S.A.
- **Conservation status:** The species is disappearing because of chronic year-class failure. No recruitment to known adult populations had been detected for about 30 years despite extensive sampling.
- **Lake Mohave population:** Old adults remain common and reproduce yearly, but larvae disappear at an average of 10.6 mm total length (TL). Wild-caught larvae have reduced or absorbed yolk sacs and mostly empty stomachs.
- **Theoretical frame:** The study builds on the [[Larval Starvation Hypothesis]], which proposes that starvation during the transition from yolk to external feeding reduces fish recruitment. This transition is termed the critical period by May (1974).
- **Broad-scale context:** The authors note that starvation of larvae has long been proposed as a cause of reduced recruitment (Hjort 1914, 1926; Cushing 1976; Leggett 1986).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Food availability:** Lake Mohave zooplankton densities are low and variable. Reported densities in larval capture areas range from 1.5 to 26.3 organisms L⁻¹.
- **Preferred prey:** Cladocerans were the preferred food of wild larvae and made up nearly 64% of total zooplankton density (Marsh & Langhorst 1988).
- **Temperature:** Lake Mohave water during egg incubation (9–15 °C) is cooler than the 21 °C used for experimental eggs. After hatching, lake temperature (15–17 °C) more closely matches the 18 °C used in the larval experiments.
- **Spawning and hatching:** Spawning occurs from early January through April, and hatching from February to May (Langhorst & Marsh 1986).
- **Experimental setup:** Experiments were run at Dexter National Fish Hatchery, New Mexico, in glass jars held in a constant-temperature (18 °C) water bath under uniform lighting. Continuous flow was about 100 mL min⁻¹.

## Key Findings

**Methods overview**
- Broodstock were Lake Mohave females injected with chorionic gonadotropin. Eggs were fertilized with Lake Mohave milt and incubated at 21 °C. Hatching began 18 February 1985.
- Larvae were stocked on 25 February 1985 at 9.6–10.7 mm TL (mean 9.96 ± 0.04 SE mm, n = 50), at 10 larvae per jar. Studies ran 50 days, until 16 April.
- Prey were live brine shrimp (*Artemia salina*) nauplii, fed three times daily at 8 h intervals.
- Statistics: Kruskal-Wallis test (p < 0.05). Unrecovered dead larvae were distributed across mortality dates using the O'Connell & Raymond (1970) formula.

**Experiment 1: Total food absence**
- Median time to 50% mortality was 24–25 days post-hatching.
- Most larvae died between 20 and 30 days post-hatching.
- Average TL at death was 9.6 ± 0.2 mm (n = 28).

**Experiment 2: Delayed first feeding (irreversible starvation)**
- Larvae first fed at day 7 had 0.0% mortality.
- First feeding at days 11, 15, and 19 gave mortalities of 10.0%, 10.0%, and 26.7%. These did not differ significantly.
- First feeding at days 23, 27, and 31 gave mortalities of 56.7%, 86.7%, and 93.3%.
- Mortality exceeded 50% by day 23, so the point of irreversible starvation lies between days 19 and 23.
- Survivors fed from day 7 reached 23.2 ± 0.5 mm TL (n = 24), while survivors first fed at day 31 reached 15.8 mm TL (n = 2).

**Experiment 3: Ration size**
- Mortality was highest at 5 nauplii L⁻¹ (86.7 ± 3.3%) and 10 nauplii L⁻¹ (73.3 ± 3.3%).
- Rations of 50, 100, 500, and 1000 nauplii L⁻¹ each had less than 20% mortality, with no significant differences among them.
- The threshold density, beyond which mortality did not decrease significantly, was 50 nauplii L⁻¹. Minimum densities giving less than 50% mortality fell between 10 and 50 nauplii L⁻¹.
- Survivors at 50 nauplii L⁻¹ grew nearly as large as those on higher rations. The authors estimate 30–60 nauplii fish⁻¹ day⁻¹ as near the minimum needed to survive the critical period.

**Key conclusions**
- The critical period at 18 °C lies between about day 8 (estimated yolk absorption) and day 19 after hatching.
- Larvae with less than 30% losses persisted for 11 days after yolk absorption. Mortality rose above 50% after 15 days even when food was offered.
- Starved larvae that survived to first feeding grew reasonably well, compared with larvae fed from day 1.
- Wild larvae at 10.6 mm TL match experimental deaths at 9.3–10.1 mm TL, suggesting wild larvae are in the critical period. Size may not reliably indicate age when food is limiting.
- The authors conclude that low zooplankton densities may starve more than 50% of larvae, so food-related mortality may contribute to the consistent absence of recruitment in Lake Mohave.
