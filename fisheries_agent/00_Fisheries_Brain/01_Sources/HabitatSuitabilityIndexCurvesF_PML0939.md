# Habitat Suitability Index Curves for Humpback Chub of the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper presents [[Habitat Suitability Index Curves]] (SI curves) for the [[Humpback Chub]] (*[[Gila Cypha]]*), an endangered riverine fish of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The curves were built from previously collected [[Microhabitat]] data, which were selected, partitioned, and pooled, then shaped by a consensus panel of species experts during two week-long workshops. The curves cover four [[Life Stages]] and three microhabitat parameters. The authors describe the approach as a reusable method for assimilating scattered pre-existing data, and the resulting curves were classed as *interim* SI curves pending further validation.

## Background & Description

**Study species.** The humpback chub is a riverine cyprinid of the upper Colorado River Basin, listed as endangered. The keywords identify it as *Gila cypha*.

**Methodological context.** The introduction frames the work within the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM), one of several methods for quantifying riverine fish habitat as a function of flow (Stalnaker 1978; Trihey 1979; Glova 1982). IFIM combines:
- stream hydraulic models;
- stream temperature models;
- physical habitat simulation models ([[Physical Habitat Simulation]], PHABSIM; Milhous 1979; Milhous et al. 1981);
- biological models or criteria in the form of habitat suitability index (SI) curves for fish microhabitat preference (Bovee 1982, among others).

**Paper details.** The article appeared in *Rivers*, Volume 1, Number 1, pages 31–42, with copyright held by S.E.L. & Associates (1990). The source is an OCR scan; the page dates listed in the front matter are April 1, 1989; May 15, 1989; and August 1, 1990, but their meaning (e.g., submission or acceptance) is not stated in the recovered text.

**Authors.** Richard A. Valdez, Paul B. Holden, and Thomas B. Hardy, of BIO/WEST, Inc., Logan, Utah. Hardy's present address is the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Utah State University.

**Keywords.** Humpback chub, *Gila cypha*, habitat suitability index curves, upper Colorado River Basin, microhabitat, physical habitat simulation, instream flow incremental methodology, [[Delphi Technique]], endangered species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat variables modelled:** [[Water Depth]], [[Water Velocity]], and [[Substrate]] serve as the three microhabitat parameters for the SI curves.
- **Life stages modelled:** larvae, young-of-the-year, juvenile, and adult.
- **Data source:** The curves draw on microhabitat data collected earlier in the upper Colorado River Basin, along with pre-existing microhabitat information scattered through many published and unpublished sources.
- **Flow linkage:** Because IFIM relates habitat to flow, the SI curves are intended to feed into [[Usable Area]] calculations and streamflow-to-habitat relationships for management decisions.

The text does not report sample sizes, site counts, or numeric habitat thresholds for the curves, so no such values are given here.

## Key Findings

1. **Curve development process.** SI curves were developed by:
   - selecting, partitioning, and pooling previously collected microhabitat data;
   - fitting curves to the field data as closely as possible;
   - modifying the fitted curves according to biological judgment;
   - reaching consensus through a panel of [[Species Experts]] in two week-long workshops.

2. **Outcome.** The species experts classified the resulting curves as *interim* SI curves, meaning they are provisional.

3. **Expert-based assimilation is workable.** The authors judged that combining data through expert review was sound because:
   - it let a panel of experts build a joint view without each expert spending large amounts of time compiling and reanalysing the data;
   - the open-discussion workshop format gave every expert equal opportunity to express opinions, which the authors say "worked well."

4. **Additional workshop.** A third workshop was held to generate weighted usable area and to evaluate streamflow-to-habitat relationships, though the authors note that a different set of experts was desirable for this task.

5. **Recommended approach.** The authors recommend the process as a template for developing SI curves for other rare fishes of the upper basin, where microhabitat information is scattered across many literature sources.
