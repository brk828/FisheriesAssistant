# Vertebrate Checklist for Arizona and New Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #vertebrate-checklist

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Forest Service Southwest Region publication, Wildlife Habitat Technical Bulletin No. 5 (April 6, 1978), is a systematic checklist of vertebrate species that occur in [[Arizona]] and [[New Mexico]]. Compiled by David R. Patton, it was built to store wildlife data for the 1979 Wildlife Assessment required by the [[Resource Planning Act of 1974]] and for the [[RUN WILD]] wildlife information storage and retrieval system. It was prepared mainly for biologists on the eleven National Forests in the two states. The checklist covers five groups: amphibians, birds, fishes, mammals and reptiles. Its fish section, the focus of this note, lists roughly 100 taxa, many of them freshwater species of the [[Gila River]], [[Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]] and related drainages.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and scope.** The checklist was intended as a working reference rather than a static list. Species are included if they currently occur in either state. Where the presence of a species was in doubt, it was left out.

**Taxonomic authorities.** Scientific and common names follow several sources. For fishes the main reference was the [[American Fisheries Society]] (1970) list of common and scientific names. Distribution data came from state-level works, including [[Koster 1957]] (fishes of New Mexico), [[Minckley 1973]] (fishes of Arizona) and [[Sublette 1975]] (fishes of the Pecos, Canadian and Arkansas drainages in New Mexico). The author notes that taxonomists often disagree on names, so no single authority was treated as definitive.

**Coding system.** Each entry carries codes:
- **A**: occurs in Arizona; **N**: occurs in New Mexico
- **\***: threatened or endangered species or subspecies (see state or federal lists)
- **1**: introduced species; **2**: peripheral, accidental, rare or uncommon; **3**: feral
- **+**: continuation of a one-word name (e.g., bullfrog)

Numbers preceding each name are unique RUN WILD identifiers. Gaps in the sequence mark unassigned numbers, and unnumbered names are not yet in RUN WILD.

**Group sizes (approximate counts from the listings):**
- Amphibians: 27 (10 frogs, 3 salamanders, 14 toads)
- Birds: more than 250 taxa
- Fishes: about 100 taxa
- Mammals: about 130 taxa
- Reptiles: about 100 taxa (lizards, skinks, snakes, turtles, whiptails)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fishes in the checklist reflect the arid Southwest's sparse, fragmented waters. Many entries are restricted to particular river systems or springs, and several are local endemics, which explains the heavy use of threatened and endangered flags. The fish list includes:

- **Native sport and forage fishes:** [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Rock Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Walleye]] and [[Rainbow Trout]].
- **Native cyprinids and catostomids:** a large group of chubs (*Gila* spp.), shiners (*Notropis* spp.), dace, suckers and roundtail, including [[Gila Chub]], [[Rio Grande Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Sucker]], [[Woundfin]] and [[Spikedace]].
- **Desert and springs fishes:** pupfishes (*Cyprinodon* spp.) such as [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] and [[White Sands Pupfish]], plus [[Pecos Gambusia]] and [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Trout:** native and introduced trout, including [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Cutthroat Trout]] and [[Golden Trout]].
- **Introduced or non-native species:** including [[Common Carp]], [[Goldfish]], [[Guppy]], [[Tilapia]] and [[Tench]], flagged with the introduced code.

The author notes that the checklist must be revised as conditions change. Distribution, names and conservation status are all expected to shift.

## Key Findings

1. **Scope and purpose:** The checklist provides a systematic inventory of vertebrates for Arizona and New Mexico, supporting the 1979 Wildlife Assessment and RUN WILD data storage.
2. **Fish diversity:** The fish section lists roughly 100 taxa. Its main families are Cyprinidae (minnows, chubs, shiners and dace), Catostomidae (suckers), Centrarchidae (sunfishes and basses), Ictaluridae (catfishes), Cyprinodontidae (pupfishes and killifishes) and Salmonidae (trout and salmon).
3. **Conservation flags:** Threatened and endangered taxa are marked with an asterisk. The fish list has many such entries, reflecting the small ranges of many desert and spring fishes.
4. **Introduced species:** Several non-native fish are listed, including carp, goldfish, guppy and tilapia, and are flagged in the coding scheme.
5. **Authorities and methods:** Names and distributions rely on several state and regional references, with no single authority used. Uncertain records were excluded.
6. **Limitations and planned updates:** The author acknowledges taxonomic disagreement and expects periodic revision. Subspecies were to be added in a second edition planned for 1980.
7. **Contributors:** Biologists from the New Mexico and Arizona state wildlife agencies, the U.S. Forest Service, several universities and private consultants reviewed or contributed to the list.
8. **Request for input:** The author asks users to report new species, name changes and distribution updates.
