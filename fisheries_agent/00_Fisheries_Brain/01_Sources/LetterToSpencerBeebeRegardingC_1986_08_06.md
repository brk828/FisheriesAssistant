## Executive Summary
This document is a 1986 correspondence from E. P. Pister, Executive Secretary of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], to Spencer Beebe of [[The Nature Conservancy]], regarding urgent conservation threats to the [[Cuatrociénegas Basin]] in Coahuila, México. The letter is not a primary scientific research paper but a conservation advocacy communication that references the biological significance of Cuatrociénegas, an endorheic (closed) desert basin renowned among ichthyologists and malacologists for extraordinary levels of aquatic endemism. The letter documents an imminent land-acquisition effort by The Nature Conservancy, involving prominent scientific authorities, to protect this site from private development.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** [[Cuatrociénegas Basin]] is characterized in the broader conservation literature as a spring-fed wetland/oasis system within the Chihuahuan Desert of Coahuila, México — an isolated, endorheic (non-marine, closed-basin) aquatic ecosystem.
- **Conservation status implied:** The basin is described as a "biological treasure," indicating high endemism and ecological fragility, consistent with its documented status as a hotspot for endemic fish, mollusk, and invertebrate diversity in isolated desert springs.
- **Threats noted:** The letter identifies imminent habitat loss from private property development as the primary threat, with landowners threatening development absent conservation purchase — a direct anthropogenic pressure on the aquatic habitat.
- **Comparative reference:** The letter draws an explicit ecological and conservation parallel to [[Ash Meadows]], a similarly isolated desert spring ecosystem in the United States already protected by that time, suggesting Cuatrociénegas is viewed as a Mexican ecological counterpart harboring comparably unique aquatic biodiversity.

## Key Findings
- The [[Cuatrociénegas Basin]] was, as of 1986, under renewed threat of development due to unresolved land acquisition with private property owners.
- [[The Nature Conservancy]] had prioritized acquisition and protection of Cuatrociénegas as part of new conservation organizing efforts in northeastern México.
- A scientific/conservation expedition was planned involving:
  - A Conservancy representative from Monterrey
  - Dr. [[Robert Hershler]] (U.S. National Museum of Natural History) — noted authority on Cuatrociénegas aquatic fauna
  - Dr. [[W. L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University) — noted authority and then-chairman of the [[Desert Fishes Council]]
- Dr. [[Salvador Contreras-Balderas]] (Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León) is identified as another leading authority on Cuatrociénegas and a former Desert Fishes Council chairman.
- The letter frames Cuatrociénegas as ecologically comparable in importance to [[Ash Meadows]], reinforcing its status as a critical site for desert aquatic endemism warranting binational conservation collaboration.
- The [[Desert Fishes Council]] formally offered its assistance to support the acquisition and protection effort, reflecting institutional recognition of the basin's scientific and conservation value.
