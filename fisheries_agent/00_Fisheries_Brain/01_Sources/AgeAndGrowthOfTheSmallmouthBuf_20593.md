# Age and Growth of the Smallmouth Buffalo in Reelfoot Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictiobus

## Executive Summary

This document is the 1943 report of the [[Reelfoot Lake Biological Station]], issued by the [[Tennessee Academy of Science]]. Its centerpiece is a life-history study of the [[Smallmouth Buffalo]] (*[[Ictiobus Bubalus]]*) by [[Robert J. Schoffman]], based on scale ages of 107 fish from [[Reelfoot Lake]]. The study finds that most buffalo reach legal length (16 inches) in their third or fourth summer, that 76 percent of fish fall in age groups III to VI, and that the total length to standard length ratio stays constant as fish grow. The report also covers station projects on lake hydrology and channel work, aquatic vegetation control, and waterfowl food habits, along with distribution details and a list of recent publications by Tennessee authors.

## Background & Description

The smallmouth buffalo study is the seventh in a series on the life histories of Reelfoot Lake fishes. The earlier reports covered [[Bluegill]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Red-Eared Sunfish]], black and white crappies, [[Warmouth]], [[Yellow Bass]], [[Freshwater Drum]], [[Carp]] and [[Gourdhead Buffalo]]. The study was funded by a grant from the Station, and was published as Contribution No. 67 of the Station. The paper was received November 26, 1943.

Fish were collected in July and August of 1937 to 1942 at three sites: the spillway on the south shore, Samburg on the east shore, and Walnut Log. Commercial fishermen used [[Hoop Net]]s with 1.5-inch square mesh. The author thanks commercial fishermen and Station staff for help obtaining catches.

Ages are expressed as "summers of life," so a fish in age group 2 is one year old and in its second summer. Age rings were counted on scales under polarized light with a magnifying apparatus described in an earlier paper by the author (Schoffman, 1939).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report describes several lake management and research projects at Reelfoot Lake:

- **Drainage:** The channel was straightened from the [[Spillway]] at the south end of the lake down to the [[Obion River]]. The stated aim is faster spring runoff when the lake rises above normal, which should prevent flooding of nearby marginal lands.
- **Aquatic vegetation:** Investigators from the Fish and Wildlife Service and the Station experimented with removing emergent plants, specifically cut grass (*Zizaniopsis miliacea*), [[Spatterdock]] (*Nuphar advena*) and cow-lilies (*Nymphea miliacea*). Repeated cutting with underwater, motor-driven sickle bar mowers suggests that much of this vegetation could eventually be eliminated.
- **Waterfowl food habits:** Stomach analyses of ducks from elsewhere in the United States suggested that Reelfoot Lake lacks the foods ducks prefer. Analyses of Reelfoot Lake ducks showed that the abundance of a seed or plant in a duck's stomach closely tracks its abundance in the lake. Whether ducks select preferred foods or simply eat what is most common remains unresolved. Federal regulations bar paying assistants for this work, and collecting enough duck specimens has been difficult.

## Key Findings

### Growth and Age Structure (Smallmouth Buffalo)

- **Sample:** 107 smallmouth buffalo spanning nine age groups.
- **Age distribution:** Age groups III, IV, V and VI made up 76 percent of the sample. Group III was 13 percent, IV 19 percent, V 26 percent and VI 18 percent.
- **Legal size:** The average fish reaches the legal length of 16 inches late in its third summer, or sometimes during its fourth summer.
- **Early growth:** Taking the length at the end of the thirteenth summer (69.8 cm) as 100 percent, 38 percent of total length growth is completed in the first and second summers. Yearly length increments are much smaller after that.
- **Weight growth:** Weight keeps increasing up to about the thirteenth summer, so the upper age classes are still growing fast. Taking the average weight at the thirteenth summer (11.55 kg, or 25.5 lb) as 100 percent, cumulative weight gain was: 3.5 percent in the first and second summers, 9 percent in the third, 11 percent in the fourth, 14 percent in the fifth, 23 percent in the sixth, 31 percent in the seventh, 39 percent in the eighth, and 69 percent by the tenth. The author notes these values would be lower for each year if calculated from older fish, since the oldest individuals were still growing rapidly.
- **Oldest specimen:** A fish in its thirteenth summer measured 35 inches and weighed 456 ounces (28 pounds 8 ounces).

### Body Proportions

- The total length to standard length (T.L./S.L.) ratio stays approximately constant as the fish grows. The author uses an average ratio of 1.213 for conversions. This pattern differs from [[Lake Erie Drum]] (Van Oosten, 1938) and [[Reelfoot Lake Drum]] (Schoffman, 1941). It agrees with [[Reelfoot Lake Carp]] (Schoffman, 1942) and [[Gourdhead Buffalo]] (Schoffman, 1943).
- Conversion tables relate standard length in millimeters to total length in inches. A length-weight relationship figure is also provided.

### Summary of Conclusions

1. Age determinations on 107 smallmouth buffalo form the basis of the study.
2. Legal length is reached late in the third or during the fourth summer.
3. The oldest specimen was in its thirteenth summer.
4. Most of the sample (76 percent) belonged to age groups III to VI.
5. The T.L./S.L. ratio remains constant with increasing size.
