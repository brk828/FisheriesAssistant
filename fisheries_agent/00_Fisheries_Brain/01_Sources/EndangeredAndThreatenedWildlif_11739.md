# Federal Register Notice: Review of Candidate Species for ESA Listing (1996)

#endangered-species #conservation #policy #USFWS #candidate-species

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (February 28, 1996) presents an updated list of plant and animal taxa native to the United States regarded as candidates for possible addition to the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. The notice identifies 182 candidate species (those with sufficient information to support proposed listing but precluded by other listing activity) and 243 species already proposed for listing. This represents a major policy shift: the Service discontinued the former "Category 2" designation, now recognizing only species meeting the stricter "Category 1" standard (renamed simply "candidates"). The notice solicits additional status information and explains changes to the candidate classification system to reduce confusion and focus conservation efforts on species at greatest risk.

## Background & Description

The [[Endangered Species Act]] requires the Service to identify species of wildlife and plants that are endangered or threatened based on best available scientific and commercial information. The Service maintains a candidate list to provide advance knowledge of potential listings for environmental planners and developers, solicit input on species status, and prioritize listing order. Previous notices maintained multiple candidate categories, but this 1996 notice streamlines the system.

**Classification Changes:**
- **Proposed species (PE/PT)**: Species for which a proposed rule to list has been published in the Federal Register
- **Candidate species (C)**: Species for which the Service has sufficient information on biological vulnerability and threats to support issuance of a proposed rule, but issuance is precluded by other listing activity
- **Former Category 2**: Species with insufficient data (no longer designated as candidates)
- **Former Category 3**: Species removed from consideration (extinct, taxonomically invalid, or more abundant than believed)

The notice supersedes all previous plant notices (1975, 1980, 1983, 1985, 1990, 1993) and animal notices (1982, 1984, 1985, 1989, 1991, 1994).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses taxa across all major groups: mammals (13 species), birds (11 species), reptiles (5 species), amphibians (12 species), fishes (14 species), clams (14 species), snails (29 species), insects (23 species), arachnids (2 species), crustaceans (4 species), and flowering plants (159 species), plus conifers/cycads (2 species). Geographic distribution spans all U.S. states and territories, with particular concentrations in [[California]], [[Hawaii]], and the southwestern states.

**Habitat types represented include:**
- Aquatic ecosystems (freshwater springs, rivers, lakes)
- Terrestrial habitats (deserts, mountains, grasslands, forests)
- Coastal and island environments
- Cave systems

The notice emphasizes that candidate species receive no statutory protection under the ESA but encourages partnership formation for conservation. Section 7 of the ESA requires Federal agencies to informally "confer" (not formally "consult") on actions affecting proposed or candidate species.

## Key Findings

**Candidate Species Summary (1996):**
- 182 candidate species awaiting proposed rules
- 243 species already proposed for listing (159 plants endangered, 34 animals endangered; 37 plants threatened, 8 animals threatened)
- 303 taxa removed from candidate/proposed status since 1993-1994 notices

**Policy Changes:**
1. **Category 2 Elimination**: The Service discontinued maintaining a list of Category 2 species (those with insufficient information). Reasons include: impracticality of tracking all potentially imperiled U.S. species; variable information quality; public confusion about listing likelihood; and duplication of efforts by State Heritage Programs, Federal land agencies, and conservation organizations.

2. **Candidate Definition**: Only species formerly classified as "Category 1" (sufficient information to support proposed listing) are now recognized as candidates.

3. **Information Sources**: The Service entered a Cooperative Agreement with [[The Nature Conservancy]] to access their scientific databases and State Natural Heritage Program data for species status assessments.

**Conservation Approach:**
- Candidate conservation emphasized through existing Service programs: refuges, fisheries, migratory birds, [[Partners in Flight]], [[Partners for Wildlife]]
- Approximately 15% of 1995 Partners for Wildlife projects directly benefitted listed species, candidates, or species of concern (85% in western states)
- Section 10 [[Habitat Conservation Plans]] encouraged to address candidates; under "no surprises" policy, applicants addressing candidates as if listed are assured no new restrictions if subsequently listed
- Section 6 funds provided to States for conservation of listed, proposed, candidate, or other species of concern

**Listing Program Status:**
The listing program was essentially shut down since October 1995 due to continuing resolutions that severely reduced funding and maintained a congressional moratorium (April 1995) on issuing final listings or critical habitat designations. The 243 proposed species await final determinations pending adequate appropriations and moratorium lifting.

**Regional Distribution of Lead Responsibility:**
- Region 1 (Pacific): 127 taxa
- Region 2 (Southwest): 45 taxa  
- Region 3 (Great Lakes): 3 taxa
- Region 4 (Southeast): 31 taxa
- Region 5 (Northeast): 8 taxa
- Region 6 (Mountain-Prairie): 18 taxa
- Region 7 (Alaska): 1 taxon

The notice requests information on: taxa that should be removed or added; critical habitat recommendations; threat documentation; taxonomic changes; appropriate common names; and corrections to historical distributions. Comments were requested until the anticipated February 1997 update.
