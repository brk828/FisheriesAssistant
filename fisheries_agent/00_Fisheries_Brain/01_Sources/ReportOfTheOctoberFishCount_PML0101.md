# 1989 October Fish Count Report: Arizona Volunteer Freshwater Fish Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes the second annual [[October Fish Count]] (OFC), a volunteer freshwater fish survey coordinated by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] [[Nongame Branch]] and written by Troy Corman, Matt Brown and Francisco Abarca. Volunteer participation rose from 51 people in 1988 to 80 in 1989, and the number of fish species sampled was roughly unchanged (50 in 1988, 55 in 1989). Effort was concentrated in central and southeastern Arizona, with the organizers aiming to add more areas in later years. The report is a site-by-site data summary, supported by a species code list and archived raw data, and it notes an unexplained shift away from the threatened and endangered emphasis seen in 1988.

## Background & Description

The OFC is a volunteer, citizen-science effort in which participants collect and identify fish at sites across Arizona in October. The report is described as overdue and was prepared to acknowledge participants and to present the results. Where volunteers recorded only initials, those initials are given in place of full names.

Data were collected using standard small-stream methods, principally [[Backpack Shocker]] electrofishing, [[Seine Netting]], dip nets, gill nets, trammel nets and hoop nets. Fish were identified to genus and species, and computer codes were used in the data tables. Appendix A is an alphabetical list of freshwater fishes recorded in Arizona with their four-letter database codes. Taxa marked with an asterisk appear on the Arizona Game and Fish Department's Threatened Native Wildlife list.

Raw data are archived in both DOS and dBase III+ formats at the Nongame Branch. Some participants also submitted detailed field notes and photographs, which are kept on file and are available on request.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sites were sampled across a range of Arizona and New Mexico drainages, including:

- [[Hassayampa River]] and [[Cienega Creek]] (central Arizona)
- [[Sabino Canyon]] in the Tucson area, with several reaches sampled
- [[Sonoita Creek]] and [[Cottonwood Spring]] (southeastern Arizona)
- [[Cherry Creek]], [[Canyon Creek]], [[Tonto Creek]] and [[East Verde River]] (central Arizona)
- [[Oak Creek]] at Redrock State Park
- [[Aravaipa Canyon]], [[San Pedro River]] and [[Redfield Canyon]]
- [[Diamond Creek]] (northwestern Arizona, tributary to the Colorado River)
- [[Blue River]], [[Turkey Creek]] and [[Garden Canyon]] (Chiricahua and Huachuca Mountains)
- [[Tularosa River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Gila River]], [[Middle Fork Gila River]], [[East Fork Gila River]] and [[West Fork Gila River]] (New Mexico)

Common species in the tables include [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarkii*), [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). Several of these, including Colorado Squawfish and Gila Topminnow, are federally or state-listed native taxa.

## Key Findings

**Participation and effort**
- Volunteers increased from 51 (1988) to 80 (1989).
- Species sampled: about 50 (1988) and 55 (1989).
- The same sites were sampled in both years, and the 1989 map (Fig. 1) shows effort concentrated in central and southeastern Arizona.

**Threatened and endangered species**
- In 1988, at least one taxon on the Arizona Threatened Native Wildlife list was found at 34 of 60 sites.
- In 1989, listed taxa were emphasized less, with listed species reported across the 59 sites surveyed. The authors state they will follow up on this shift, which remains unexplained.

**Selected site results (per-site totals as reported)**
- **[[Oak Creek]] (Redrock State Park), 1989:** Dominated by [[Speckled Dace]]. At one reach, speckled dace made up 92.3% of catch in the sampled section.
- **[[Diamond Creek]], 1989:** Speckled dace made up 100% of catches at several reaches, including sites 100 m to 2000 m east of Diamond Creek Road.
- **[[Blue River]] (downstream of Juan Miller Road), 1988 and 1989:** Totals of 248 (1988) and 198 (1989) fish, with speckled dace, Western Mosquitofish and Colorado Squawfish recorded among the taxa.
- **[[Aravaipa Canyon]], multiple sites:** Totals of 253 (1988) and 216 (1989) at Bill's Crossing, and 186 (1988) and 126 (1989) at the Woods Ranch diversion. Speckled dace, [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Western Mosquitofish]] were common.
- **[[San Pedro River]] (near Redington and Charleston Road), 1989:** Catches were dominated by [[Western Mosquitofish]] and [[Desert Sucker]], with some sites showing single-species catches.
- **[[Sonoita Creek]] (near Patagonia Lake), 1989:** Multiple species caught, with [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Western Mosquitofish]] among the taxa noted.
- **[[Gila River]] (New Mexico), 1989:** Total of 768 fish, compared with 410 in 1988. Taxa included Gila Topminnow and Colorado Squawfish.
- **[[San Francisco River]] (New Mexico), 1989:** Total of 1,184 fish, compared with 541 in 1988, with desert and speckled dace prominent.

**Data and records**
- The full raw dataset is archived and available. Species codes are provided in Appendix A.
- The report includes site-level tables for each sample, with 1988 and 1989 counts and percentages.
- Documentation of field notes and photographs continues to accumulate and is considered increasingly valuable over time.
