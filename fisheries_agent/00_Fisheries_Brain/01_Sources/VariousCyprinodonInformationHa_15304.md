# Pupfish Taxonomy, Specimen Loans and Guzman Basin Study Correspondence (1953–1985)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an archival bundle of letters, specimen invoices, loan notices, handwritten notes and a diagnostic character sheet. It concerns the taxonomy and distribution of *Cyprinodon* pupfishes, especially the Chihuahuan populations in the [[Guzman Basin]] and surrounding drainages. Most of the correspondence is between curators at the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]], [[Arizona State University]] and other institutions. The main threads are: missing locality data for specimens, the diagnostic separation of [[Cyprinodon]] from [[Floridichthys]], a planned three-author taxonomic paper on Guzman Basin pupfishes, and a 1981 report on invasive [[Tilapia Aurea]] on the lower [[Colorado River]].

The file is not a research article, so its content is administrative and anecdotal. It still records useful taxonomic, distributional and management information.

## Background & Description

The bundle contains about 19 pages and includes:

- **1964 letter** from [[Robert R. Miller]], Curator of Fishes, to Arthur O. Flechsig (Scripps Institution of Oceanography). Miller asks for the exact locality of Station FM-5 on the [[Rio Papigochic]], Chihuahua, where a *Cyprinodon* was collected in 1953. Miller plans to visit the river the following June.
- **Specimen invoice** from the Michigan Museum of Zoology to the American Museum of Natural History, listing about 15 catalogue entries of *Cyprinodon artatus* and *C. albivelis*. The note states that the loan has no locality information. The shipment date is partly illegible in the scan.
- **1981 letter** from a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Endangered Species Biologist (J. E. Johnson) to Miller. It asks about the Mexican range of *Cyprinodon tularosa*, specifically whether it is confined to the Laguna de Palomas system or extends to the Rio Casas Grande.
- **Handwritten specimen table** (pages 5 and 12). Most entries are illegible or consist of catalogue numbers and counts, so no quantitative data is reported here.
- **1985 letter** from Miller to Robert K. Johnson (Field Museum). It concerns a two-quart jar of **235 specimens** of a new species, *Cyprinodon artatus* MS (PMNH 9073), which has no locality data. Miller and Mike Smith were preparing its formal description.
- **Diagnostic character sheet** (pages 8 and 9), setting out seven characters that separate *Cyprinodon* from *Floridichthys* (see Key Findings).
- **Letters from [[Carl L. Hubbs]]** (Curator of Fishes) to [[George S. Myers]] commenting on those characters. The date is partly garbled in the scan and reads as 1935, but the content suggests the 1950s.
- **1981 letter** from [[W. L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University) to Smith. It comments on a proposed study of "pupfishes of Guzman and environs" and reports on invasive tilapia.
- **Two-letter exchange of September 1981** between Smith and Minckley setting out a proposed three-author taxonomic study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pupfish habitats.** The taxa discussed occupy desert springs, springheads and river channels in the Chihuahuan uplands and the Basin and Range region. Minckley notes that the Ojo de los Reyes system was once "a number of springhead habitats along the escarpment," which suggests that the populations were spatially discrete and possibly isolated in time.

**Biogeographic history.** Smith suggests that the Guzman Basin pupfishes "should provide a coherent history of hydrographic changes in the area." He proposes that *C. fontinalis* is related not to *C. artatus* but to *C. macrolepis* and *C. alvarezi*. Its occurrence between drainages occupied by *C. artatus* could imply Pleistocene sympatry of the two species. Minckley describes the Chihuahuan pupfishes as possibly "derived from quite different stocks" and representing "different temporal isolates" in the Basin and Range region.

**Lower Colorado River invasion.** Minckley reports that introduced fishes on the lower Colorado River are being "effectively and almost completely replaced by [[Tilapia Aurea]]." The tilapia were introduced by the Arizona Game and Fish Department in 1977–78. They have taken over the Yuma Valley drains, are invading upstream toward Blythe in the Imperial Division, and have reached the Delta and possibly the northern [[Gulf Of California]]. Minckley also identified *T. zillii*, *T. mossambica*, an undetermined *Tilapia*, and a tentative *T. galilaea* in the Yuma area. He reports that he spent several days killing them.

## Key Findings

**1. Locality data gaps hamper taxonomy.** Several specimen sets (the 1953 FM-5 collection, the American Museum loan, and the 235-specimen jar from 1985) lack locality information. Miller says he is "reasonably certain" of the 1985 locality and asks for a check against the PMNH catalogue.

**2. Diagnostic characters separating *Cyprinodon* from *Floridichthys*.** The sheet lists seven contrasting characters. Hubbs's June letter assesses them:
- *Preorbital scales:* In *Cyprinodon*, the free edges of the scales point upward or upward-and-forward, and the scaly area does not end on an abrupt line. Hubbs says this holds in his material, with one exception. *C. nevadensis* from Death Valley is scaleless over the whole suborbital region, as in *Floridichthys*.
- *Snout scales:* Hubbs agrees that this character holds as stated.
- *Pelvic flap:* Hubbs calls this "a beautiful distinction." The flap is usually more definite in *Floridichthys*.
- *Iris flap:* Hubbs says this holds "only on the average," since the flap can be obsolescent in *Floridichthys* and fairly well developed in some *Cyprinodon*.
- *Dorsal fin shape:* a minor but consistent character.
- *Caudal peduncle:* accepted.
- *Scale thickness and regularity:* Hubbs says this is "not so hot." Dorsal origin should be dropped, and the female dorsal ocellus is "a still poorer distinction," since it is absent in at least two Mexican interior forms.

Hubbs concludes that the characters hold well for both new Yucatan forms, except that the iris flap may be obsolescent in *F. polyommus*.

**3. Morphometric and meristic analysis is under way.** Smith reports a file of **26 morphometric** and **21 meristic characters**. By September 1981 it held **210 cases**, covering all suitable *artatus* specimens at UMMZ. The localities include the Arroyo de Nopahechic (Bustillos Basin), Ojo Caliente, Lake Guzman, Zaragoza, springs near Palomas, Flores Magon, Rio Santa Maria de Bavicora, Ojo de los Reyes, Rio Santa Clara and Rio de la Concha. Smith expects about a month to enter the Meek loan material from FMNH and verify the file.

**4. Proposed three-author paper and authorship.** Smith proposes a paper on the taxonomy, geographic variation and distributional history of the Guzman Basin pupfishes (*C. fontinalis*, *C. artatus* and *C. albivelis*). Authorship of new names would be split: Minckley and Miller on *albivelis*, and Miller and Smith on *artatus*. Smith offers to write the body of the text.

**5. Open questions raised by Minckley.** He questions whether the Bustillos and Bavicora fish are truly *albivelis* or *artatus*, and whether Ojo de los Reyes is a recent invasion, as is the Rio de la Concha. He notes that the Palomas fish are superficially similar to *C. nevadensis* and asks about *C. eximius* at the other side of the cluster. He suggests that more Rio Yaqui material (Yepomera and Matachic) would be appropriate. He also notes that ASU holds large numbers of Ojo de los Reyes specimens, which may justify separate analyses later.

**6. Tilapia are displacing introduced fish on the lower Colorado River.** Minckley's note reports effective and near-complete replacement of other introduced fishes by *Tilapia aurea*, which were stocked in 1977–78 and are spreading upstream and toward the Gulf of California.

**7. Logistics.** Miller was in East Africa and the Seychelles from 29 July to 21 August 1981 and visited USNM and ANSP from 23 to 31 August. He was expected to respond to Minckley's letter of 6 August.
