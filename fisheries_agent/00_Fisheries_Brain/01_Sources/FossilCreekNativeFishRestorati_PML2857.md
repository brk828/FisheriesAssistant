# Fossil Creek Native Fish Restoration Project: Final Report (November 2005)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #catostomidae #cyprinidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This final report from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and cooperating agencies documents the 2004 chemical renovation of [[Fossil Creek]], a travertine-depositing warm-water stream in central Arizona, to remove nonnative [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]] and create habitat for native fishes. Renovation used the piscicide [[Fintrol]] (antimycin) applied by controlled-release drip stations and antimycin-coated sand, followed by [[Potassium Permanganate]] and sodium permanganate neutralization. Renovation was completed in October and November 2004, native fish were salvaged and repatriated, and post-renovation monitoring in 2005 found only one nonnative sunfish. A related effort treated five [[Stock Tanks]] and [[Boulder Canyon]] with [[Rotenone]] in March 2005 to prevent reinvasion.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Fossil Creek drains from Fossil Springs (base flow about 43 cfs) and is diverted by the Fossil Springs diversion dam to power the Childs and Irving hydroelectric plants operated by [[Arizona Public Service]] (APS). Decommissioning restored full flow in June 2005.
- **Native fish present:** Six native species were documented: Sonoran sucker, desert sucker, [[Roundtail Chub]] (with [[Headwater Chub]]), [[Speckled Dace]], [[Longfin Dace]], and historically [[Gila Topminnow]] attempts (not known to occur). Nonnative [[Yellow Bullhead]] and green sunfish were also present.
- **Project structure:** Four reaches were defined, with sunfish and waterfall barriers. Salvage occurred in two phases (October 11-15 and November 3-8, 2004). Renovation occurred in Reaches 1-2 (October 19-22, 2004) and Reaches 3-4 (November 8-13, 2004).
- **Cooperators:** [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Forest Service]] (Coconino and Tonto National Forests), [[Northern Arizona University]], and [[Arizona State University]].
- **Compliance:** A Finding of No Significant Impact was issued in 2004 after a 45-day public comment period, and the project was approved to proceed on August 2, 2004.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Invasion pressure:** Green sunfish were documented from the Verde River confluence up to the sunfish barrier, and smallmouth bass from the confluence up to a high waterfall near Irving. Yellow bullhead were suspected in the same zone, which raised concern because of the piscicide's effect on that species.
- **Water chemistry:** Fossil Creek is alkaline and hard (pH about 7.0 to 8.2, specific conductance about 520 to 740 µS). Total dissolved solids ranged from roughly 282 to 474 mg/L. Dissolved iron was about 5 times higher just above Irving (519 µg/L versus <100 µg/L upstream), and this iron was later identified as reducing antimycin toxicity.
- **Travertine:** Flow diversion has led to loss of travertine formation in the upper creek, with formation concentrated downstream at the Irving outflow and Childs outflow.
- **Pre-renovation habitat:** Pools were measured to calculate sand dosing. The treated reaches contained about 1,000 pools in total across reaches 1-4, with subtotal volumes listed in Appendix 5.
- **Stock tank context:** A 2002 aerial survey located 127 stock tanks in the drainage. Of 62 holding water or of undetermined status, 48 drained toward Fossil Creek. Only five contained nonnative fish: Soldier Mesa, Mack's, Divide, Middle, and Black tanks.

## Key Findings

**Salvage and holding**
- Salvage captured 1,917 native fish across both phases (Table 2): Speckled Dace 986; Desert Sucker 393; Roundtail/Headwater Chub 277; Sonoran Sucker 248; Longfin Dace 13.
- Holding used twelve circular raceways supplied by water from the Irving penstock. About 80 speckled dace died in captivity, and at least 90% of salvaged fish were estimated to have been repatriated.

**Bioassays (Appendices 1-4)**
- The first in-stream bioassay (August 4-5, 2004) at 25 ppb failed to achieve complete mortality of green sunfish. Yellow bullhead were unaffected.
- Controlled tank tests showed 100% green sunfish mortality at 25 ppb (about 22 hours) and 50 ppb (4 hours). Smallmouth bass died at 100% at 25 ppb (6 hours) and 50 ppb (3 hours). Yellow bullhead were unaffected at 25-400 ppb, with 20% mortality only at 800 ppb.
- The in-stream bioassay of August 24-26, 2004 showed that high iron levels reduced effectiveness.
- A final bioassay on September 7, 2004 achieved 100% green sunfish mortality at 50 ppb over four hours, leading to the final dosing plan.

**Renovation dosing**
- Target concentration was 50 ppb, increased to 100 ppb in the iron-rich area near the spring inflow.
- Reaches 1-2 were renovated at 0.5 cfs using 44 units (21.12 L) of Fintrol-concentrate and 50.8 kg of Fintrol-15 sand. Roughly 40 people covered 9.3 km in this phase.
- Reaches 3-4 were treated at variable discharge (11-17 cfs). Realized concentrations were lower than planned, including 35 ppb in Reach 3 on the first pass and 54 ppb in Reach 4 on the first pass.
- Drip buckets were modified 19 L Mariotte bottles, adapted from [[Stefferud And Propst]] (1996).
- Fintrol formulation problems with undissolved soy lipids, especially in cold weather, caused poor drip rates.

**Outcomes**
- No live fish were reported during the treatment. Post-renovation electrofishing and hoopnetting found no green sunfish or smallmouth bass.
- Monitoring in 2005 used netting, electrofishing (3,879 m, about 0.02 fish/min CPUE) and snorkeling across 9 priority areas. One green sunfish was seen, and two sunfish were captured in Boulder Canyon.
- Trammel and hoop netting (117 hoop sets, 11 trammel sets) caught 56 fish, including 40 roundtail chub, 6 desert sucker, and 9 Sonoran sucker.
- The planning group decided not to retreat because the sunfish sighting was isolated and reproduction of native fishes was successful.

**Stock tank renovation**
- Rotenone was applied at 1 ppm to four tanks and 4 ppm to Mack's Tank (goldfish label rate) on March 23, 2005. Antelope Tank and portions of [[Boulder Canyon]] were also treated at about 1 ppm.
- No fish were captured in August 2005 gill-net monitoring of the five tanks.
- Key recommendations: law enforcement to prevent restocking, periodic monitoring of Boulder Canyon and tanks draining to the creek, annual interagency meetings, and development of a crayfish control method.
