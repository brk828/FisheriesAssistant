# State of Utah Stocking Plan for Endangered Fish Species of the Upper Colorado River Basin (Revised, 2001)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This revised stocking plan, prepared by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR) under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP), provides a framework for propagating and stocking endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] in the Green and Colorado rivers of Utah. The plan replaces the 1999 Utah stocking plan, shifting objectives from Interim Management Objectives to the minimum viable population (MVP) criteria of revised recovery goals. Proposed stocking totals are 18,500 razorback suckers over five years and 116,280 bonytail over seven years, with target MVPs of 5,800 and 4,500 adults in Year 10. Stocking includes a roughly 30% buffer to address uncertainty.

## Background & Description

- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)** was federally listed as endangered in 1991. Adult numbers in the middle Green River were estimated at 524 individuals (95% CI 351–696) from 1980–1992 capture–recapture data, down from 948 (1980–1988 estimate). Adult population sizes in the lower Green River and Colorado River in Utah are unknown; juvenile and larval collections suggest a spawning aggregation in the lower Green River.
- **Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)** was federally listed as endangered in 1980. Very few wild bonytail have been captured in recent years, and recaptures of stocked fish have mostly occurred within a year of stocking.
- Both species fall under the RIP, whose five components are instream flow, habitat restoration, nonnative fish reduction, propagation and genetics management, and research/monitoring. This plan implements the propagation component.
- Propagation: a Genetics Management Plan (Czapla 1999) ranks razorback sucker as Priority 1 and bonytail as Priority 2. Brood stock development began in 1989, with 23 family lots held at [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]]. A bonytail brood stock is being developed at [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] stock, which represents all known bonytail genetic variation.
- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and [[Humpback Chub]] are excluded because their populations in Utah are considered stable or are under assessment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stocking sites, razorback sucker:** middle Green River (Jensen to Ouray, RM 302–249, including flooded bottomlands at Above Brennan and Johnson Bottom); lower Green River (Green River State Park, RM 120, and mouth of the San Rafael River, RM 97). The reach between the two lower sites contains cobble habitat that may support spawning, and the San Rafael may hold one of the few lower-basin spawning areas.
- **Stocking sites, bonytail:** middle Green River (same reach, including Above Brennan and Johnson Bottom, which allow overwintering); lower Green River at Green River State Park (RM 120); Colorado River at Cisco (RM 110.5). Monitoring indicates retention of stocked bonytail below these sites.
- **Threats:** Hybridization with native [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]], and nonnative [[White Sucker]], is possible though rates are low. Predation on larvae and small fish by channel catfish, common carp, green sunfish, red shiner, and fathead minnow is documented. Increasing northern pike and walleye may cause adult mortality. Pathogens and parasites, such as Asian tapeworm, may transfer from hatchery stock to wild fish.
- **Imprinting:** Hatchery-reared razorback suckers may imprint on hatchery water. Recent collections of stocked fish on the Jensen spawning bar suggest they can return to spawning sites.

## Key Findings

**Objectives**
1. Augment the Green River razorback sucker population through five years of annual stocking at two sites to achieve an MVP of 5,800 fish.
2. Augment lower Green River and Colorado River bonytail populations through seven years of annual stocking at two sites.
3. Reintroduce and establish a bonytail population in the middle Green River through seven years of stocking at one site.

**Stocking numbers**
- Razorback sucker: 18,500 fish annually for five years (about 9,250 per reach), stocked at ≥300 mm TL (Age-2+). Survival-based projection gives 7,538 surviving fish at Year 10 (ages 6–10). Survival estimates are based on San Juan River monitoring.
- Bonytail: 116,280 fish over seven years (about 5,427 per reach per year), averaging 200 mm TL (150–250 mm; Age-1+). Projection gives 6,756 surviving fish at Year 10 (ages 4–10). Conservative survival estimates were used because data were lacking.

**Assumptions and risks**
- The plan is designed to reach MVPs, not to maintain them. Continued RIP actions (flow recommendations, floodplain reacquisition, nonnative control) are assumed to support recruitment.
- Genetic risks (inbreeding, outbreeding depression) are reduced by following the Genetics Management Plan. Bonytail inbreeding is a concern because of an extreme bottleneck. Hybridization between bonytail and chub may be partly natural, so it may not hinder recovery.
- Uncertainties include survival of stocked fish, bonytail habitat needs, reproductive success, recruitment, female razorback sucker maturity age variability, carrying capacity, and hybridization effects.

**Monitoring and recommendations**
- Monitoring will track distribution, density, growth, and survival. PIT tags are recommended for all stocked fish, because coded-wire tags proved inadequate for bonytail identification.
- Recommended start dates: bonytail stocking in Fall 2002; razorback sucker stocking in Fall 2003. Monitoring should begin immediately.
- Stocking should continue for at least five years (razorback sucker) and seven years (bonytail), with extensions if objectives are unmet. Results should guide adjustments to numbers, size, timing, location, and duration.
