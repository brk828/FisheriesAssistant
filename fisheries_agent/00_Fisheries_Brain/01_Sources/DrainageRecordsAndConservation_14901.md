# Drainage Records and Conservation Status Evaluations for Thirteen Kentucky Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #Petromyzontidae #Lepisosteidae #Umbridae #Cyprinidae #Fundulidae #Centrarchidae #Percidae #kentucky-ichthyofauna #conservation-status

## Executive Summary

This 1983 paper by [[Melvin L. Warren Jr.]] and [[Ronald R. Cicerello]], conducted under the [[Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission]], reports new drainage records, refined distributional data, and revised conservation status assessments for 13 Kentucky fish species. Ten species are documented for the first time in specific Kentucky drainages, including the [[American Brook Lamprey]] (Cumberland River), [[Central Mudminnow]] (West Fork Clarks River), [[Cypress Minnow]] (Tennessee River), [[Blotched Chub]] (Red River), [[Popeye Shiner]] (Kinniconick Creek), [[Telescope Shiner]] (Green River), [[Dollar Sunfish]] (Tennessee River), [[Bluebreast Darter]] (Licking River), [[Slenderhead Darter]] (Red River), and [[River Darter]] (Little Sandy River). The persistence of [[Longhead Darter]] populations in the Barren River system and Kinniconick Creek, and of [[Golden Topminnow]] in extreme southwestern Kentucky, is confirmed. The authors recommend four conservation status changes under the [[Kentucky Academy of Science]] rare fish list: downgrading [[Spotted Gar]] from threatened to special concern; elevating [[Blotched Chub]] from special concern to threatened; elevating [[Golden Topminnow]] from special concern to endangered; and removing [[Slenderhead Darter]] from status recognition altogether.

## Background & Description

Kentucky's freshwater ichthyofauna ranks third most speciose in North America behind Tennessee and Alabama ([[Burr 1980]]). Prior to this study, key references established the state's known fish distributions and conservation status: [[Clay 1975]] (Fishes of Kentucky), [[Burr 1980]] (distributional checklist), and [[Branson et al. 1981b]] (Kentucky Academy of Science endangered/threatened/rare species list). This paper synthesizes new field survey data gathered by the Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission to refine these baselines.

Each species account follows a standardized format: collection catalog number, number of specimens and size range (standard length or total length in mm), stream and major drainage, precise locality, county, and collection date. Collections were made using methods described in [[Harker et al. 1980]] and are housed at the Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission (KNP), with planned deposition at Southern Illinois University at Carbondale (SIUC); additional specimens are held at the Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources (KFW) and Kentucky Department of Transportation (KDOT). Nomenclature follows [[Robins et al. 1980]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recurring environmental threats identified across species accounts include:

- **Wetland drainage and agricultural conversion** — threatening [[Central Mudminnow]], [[Cypress Minnow]], [[Golden Topminnow]], and [[Dollar Sunfish]] populations in the Reelfoot Lake drainage and West Fork Clarks River (Graves and Fulton counties).
- **Coal mining and oil/gas exploration pollution** — impacting [[Blotched Chub]] in Little South Fork Cumberland River, and [[Longhead Darter]] in upper Kentucky, Licking, and Big Sandy river coalfields.
- **Proposed Devonian oil shale development** — threatening high-quality watersheds such as Kinniconick Creek, home to [[Popeye Shiner]] and [[Longhead Darter]].
- **Impoundment** — Barren River Reservoir extirpated [[Longhead Darter]] from mainstem habitat, though tributary populations (Trammel Fork) persist unaffected.
- **Siltation and pesticide runoff** — affecting [[Blotched Chub]] in the Red River.
- **Mississippi River loess bluff erosion** — threatening Reelfoot Lake drainage species in both Kentucky and Tennessee.
- **Historical extirpation from mainstem rivers** — [[Blotched Chub]] extirpated from Cumberland and lower Tennessee River mainstems following impoundment; [[Longhead Darter]] extirpated from the Cumberland River of Kentucky.
- **Sampling bias** — several "rare" or "undetermined status" designations (e.g., [[Spotted Gar]], [[River Darter]], [[Bluebreast Darter]]) likely reflect difficulty sampling preferred habitats (vegetated wetlands, large river riffles/runs, deep swift chutes) rather than true rarity.

The paper also raises a zoogeographic question regarding [[Telescope Shiner]] in East Fork Barren River — a possible case of historical stream capture from Cumberland River tributaries (Meshack Creek) via karst topography, though introduction cannot be ruled out.

### Confirmed Continued Existence
- **[[Percina macrocephala]]** (Longhead Darter): confirmed persisting in good numbers in Trammel Fork (Barren River drainage) and moderately common in Kinniconick Creek mainstem — first Kinniconick record since a single 1930s specimen.
- **[[Fundulus chrysotus]]** (Golden Topminnow): sole extant Kentucky population confirmed at Running Slough (Reelfoot Lake drainage, Fulton Co.), following destruction of the only previously known site (Open Pond).

### Recommended Conservation Status Changes (Kentucky Academy of Science List)
1. **[[Lepisosteus oculatus]]** (Spotted Gar): Threatened → **Special Concern**. New records (Tradewater, Green, Tennessee, Mississippi drainages) show the species is more widespread than previously believed; apparent rarity reflects sampling difficulty in vegetated wetland habitat rather than true scarcity, though wetland habitat loss remains a concern warranting monitoring.
2. **[[Hybopsis insignis]]** (Blotched Chub): Special Concern → **Threatened**. Despite a newly discovered substantial Red River population, ongoing strip-mine, oil-field pollution, siltation, and pesticide threats in known localities (Little South Fork, Red River) justify elevated concern.
3. **[[Fundulus chrysotus]]** (Golden Topminnow): Special Concern → **Endangered**. Extremely limited distribution (single extant Kentucky population) and demonstrated rapid habitat loss (Open Pond drainage) warrant the higher designation.
4. **[[Percina phoxocephala]]** (Slenderhead Darter): **Remove from rare species list**. New Red River records plus persistent, healthy populations documented in Tygarts Creek, Green, Barren, Rough, Eagle Creek, and Salt River systems indicate the species does not warrant conservation status recognition.

### Species Retained at Current Status (No Change Recommended)
- **[[Lampetra appendix]]** — remains Special Concern; Kentucky specimen represents one of only three records from the entire Cumberland River drainage; likely a waif from nearby Sulphur Creek.
- **[[Umbra limi]]** — remains Threatened; populations in Terrapin Creek and Reelfoot Lake drainage considered firmly established, but West Fork Clarks River populations are jeopardized by ongoing wetland drainage.
- **[[Hybognathus hayi]]** — remains Threatened; new Tennessee River drainage record adds to known relictual lower Green River population, but wetland habitat loss from oil exploration, coal mining, and drainage make its future "increasingly tenuous."
- **[[Notropis ariommus]]** — remains Undetermined status; Kinniconick Creek record closes a distributional hiatus between disjunct middle and upper Ohio River valley populations, but proposed oil shale development threatens this high-quality watershed.
- **[[Lepomis marginatus]]** — remains Threatened; West Fork Clarks River populations, while locally abundant, face wetland drainage pressure.
- **[[Percina macrocephala]]** (Longhead Darter) — remains Threatened, despite healthy Barren River/Kinniconick populations, due to coal-mining pollution elsewhere in its range and extirpation from the Cumberland River.
- **[[Percina shumardi]]** (River Darter) — remains Threatened, though authors predict further collecting will reveal additional populations (e.g., Big Sandy River, Tygarts Creek) warranting eventual delisting.

### Notable Biological/Distributional Notes
- **[[Lampetra appendix]]**: Metamorphosing sub-adult (~69–70 myomeres) collected in late October, consistent with known metamorphosis timing (mid-August–October) reported by [[Seagle & Nagel 1982]] for eastern Tennessee populations.
- **[[Etheostoma camurum]]**: Discovery in Licking River reduces the distributional gap between upper and middle Ohio River drainage populations; collected from riffle habitat (0.3–0.6 m deep) with bedrock/slab boulder and cobble/gravel substrate. Raises unresolved question about validity of a historical (Woolman 1892) Licking River record of the congener *E. maculatum*.
- **[[Notropis telescopus]]**: First substantiated Green River system record; possible origin via prehistoric stream capture from Cumberland River tributaries through karst (sinkhole) topography along the Meshack Creek–East Fork Barren River divide, though non-native introduction cannot be excluded.
- **[[Percina shumardi]]**: Collected from a deep (1.3 m), swift chute with large cobble substrate — the last shoal habitat on the Little Sandy River before impoundment by Greenup Lock and Dam.
