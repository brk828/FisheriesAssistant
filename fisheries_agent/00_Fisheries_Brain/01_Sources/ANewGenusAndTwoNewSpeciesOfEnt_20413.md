# A New Genus and Two New Species of Entocytherid Ostracods from Alabama and Mississippi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #entocytheridae #ostracoda #parasitology #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes a new genus (*Saurocythere*) and two new species of entocytherid ostracods (*Saurocythere rhipis* and *Ornithocythere gypodes*) collected from [[Cambarus Diogenes Diogenes]] crayfish in [[Alabama]] and [[Mississippi]]. The new genus *Saurocythere* is unique among entocytherids in having a horizontally situated spermatic loop within the peniferum. *Ornithocythere gypodes* represents the second species in genus *Ornithocythere*, showing close affinities to *O. waltonae* from Virginia and Maryland, indicating a discontinuous distribution pattern. Both species are epizootic on burrowing crayfish and were collected from terrestrial burrow habitats.

## Background & Description

### Saurocythere (new genus)

The genus name derives from Greek *sauros* (lizard) plus *Cythere*, referring to the lizard-head resemblance of the male peniferum. Diagnostic features include: terminal tooth of mandible with cusps; no finger guard on male copulatory complex; slender, elongate peniferum without accessory groove extending ventrally beyond clasping apparatus; penis consisting of separate spermatic and prostatic elements, each one-fourth length of peniferum and reaching ventral extremity; penis complex three times longer than least diameter of peniferum; spermatic loop situated horizontally and supported by heavily sclerotized bar; clasping apparatus not clearly divisible into vertical and horizontal rami, with sinuous external border bearing small excrescence at base of distal fourth; internal border entire; apical portion slightly expanded and serrate (fan-like).

### Saurocythere rhipis (new species)

Etymology from Greek *rhipis* (fan), referring to the expanded fan-like distal portion of clasping apparatus. Male specimens only known. Eye pigmented, located approximately one-third shell length from anterior margin. Shell ovate with entire ventral margin, greatest height slightly posterior to midlength. Dimensions: length 0.442 mm, height 0.294 mm. Setae scarce, occurring irregularly but singly near anterior and posterior margins. Mandible with distal row of five multicuspid teeth (proximal tooth with two cusps, distal with five, others with three). Dorsal finger long and slender, nearly one-half length of ventral finger, bearing two terminal spines; ventral finger slender and parallel to clasping apparatus ending in single terminal.

Type-locality: Crayfish burrow in drainage ditch along State Route 14, 0.6 mi. SE of Alabama-Mississippi state line, [[Pickens County]], Alabama. Host: [[Cambarus Diogenes Diogenes]]. Holotype and paratype deposited in [[United States National Museum]] (nos. 123880 and 123881). Associates: An unidentified member of genus *Anklocythere*.

### Ornithocythere gypodes (new species)

Etymology from Greek *gypodes* (vulture), named for resemblance of ventral portion of peniferum to vulture head. Male specimens only known. Eye pigmented, located approximately one-fourth shell length from anterior margin. Shell subelliptical, highest anterior to midlength with dorsal margin tapering to small eminence at posterior end; ventral margin entire. Dimensions: length 0.495 mm, height 0.245 mm. Terminal tooth of mandible with cusps.

Copulatory complex without finger guard; peniferum extending ventrally beyond clasping apparatus with ventral portion highly sclerotized and produced into beak-like prominence directed anteriorly; accessory groove absent; penis complex with spermatic and prostatic elements propinquant throughout their length, not separated; penis complex slightly longer than anterior-posterior dimension of peniferum at level of base of penis. Two tubercles located on proximoventral angle of beak-like prominence, a third on ventral margin near apex. Clasping apparatus not divisible into vertical and horizontal rami, with proximal and distal portions disposed at angle of approximately forty degrees; internal and external borders entire except distally, with four annulations immediately preceding three apical denticles. Dorsal finger moderately slender, bearing one terminal spine; ventral finger slender and subparallel to clasping apparatus, bearing one terminal spine.

Type-locality: Burrow in flood plain of [[Tombigbee River]], one-fourth mi. east of river, 1 mi. west Alabama-Mississippi state line, [[Lowndes County]], Mississippi. Host: [[Cambarus Diogenes Diogenes]]. Holotype deposited in [[United States National Museum]] (no. 123879). Repeated collection efforts at type-locality and other localities along Tombigbee River have been unsuccessful.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both species are epizootic on the burrowing crayfish [[Cambarus Diogenes Diogenes]], inhabiting terrestrial burrows in drainage ditches and flood plains. The genus *Ornithocythere* exhibits a discontinuous distribution pattern: one portion of range extends along coastal plains of Virginia and Maryland, while the other occurs much farther inland, well above the fall line, in northern Mississippi. This disjunct distribution is unusual and suggests complex biogeographic history.

*Saurocythere rhipis* was found associated with an unidentified member of genus *Anklocythere* in the same host burrow. The species appears restricted to its type-locality based on current collections.

## Key Findings

1. **New Genus *Saurocythere***: Unique among entocytherids in possessing a horizontally situated spermatic loop in the peniferum—a character not observed in any other member of the family.

2. **Phylogenetic Relationships**: *Saurocythere rhipis* shows closest affinities to genus *Plectocythere* based on penis complex morphology (extremely long contiguous spermatic and prostatic elements in both genera). Superficial resemblance to *Okriocythere cheia* exists, but differs in penis length, spermatic loop orientation (vertical vs. horizontal), and clasping apparatus form.

3. ***Ornithocythere gypodes* Relationships**: Closest affinities with *O. waltonae* (Hobbs, Jr., 1967). Both species have penifera distinctly resembling bird heads. Penis complexes markedly similar, but *O. gypodes* has longer penis than *O. waltonae*. Also similar to *Okriocythere cheia*, genus *Geocythere*, and less so to genera *Ascetocythere* and *Plectocythere*. Unique combination of characters: more ventrally situated penis, three spines on beak-like prominence of peniferum, and distinct clasping apparatus.

4. **Biogeographic Significance**: Genus *Ornithocythere* exhibits discontinuous distribution with populations separated by considerable distance (coastal Virginia/Maryland vs. northern Mississippi inland), suggesting complex dispersal history or relict populations.

5. **Host Specificity**: Both new species are epizootic on [[Cambarus Diogenes Diogenes]], indicating host-specific associations with burrowing crayfish fauna.

6. **Collection Challenges**: *Ornithocythere gypodes* known only from holotype specimen; repeated collection efforts have been unsuccessful, suggesting rarity or highly localized distribution.
