## Executive Summary

This 1960 report is **not a species-level biological or ecological study**; it is a federal inter-agency policy document establishing an **interim economic valuation schedule** for the recreational aspects of fishing and hunting associated with U.S. water resource development projects (e.g., reservoirs). It was produced by the [[Panel On Recreational Values]], convened under the [[Inter-Agency Committee On Water Resources]] and its [[Subcommittee On Evaluation Standards]]. The Panel reviewed existing economic data sources — including the 1955 [[National Survey Of Fishing And Hunting]], private-area fee surveys, and USDA reservoir benefit studies — and concluded that no rigorous statistical basis existed for deriving unit recreational values from expenditure or fee data alone. Instead, the Panel produced a **judgment-based interim schedule of dollar values per recreational day**, differentiated by habitat type (reservoir, stream/lake, warm water, cold water) and by game category, intended for use in cost-benefit evaluation of proposed water resource projects until better data became available.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Not applicable in the biological sense, but the report implicitly categorizes **aquatic habitat types** relevant to recreational fisheries valuation:

- **Reservoirs** — impounded water bodies created by dam/water resource projects, treated as a distinct fishing habitat class.
- **Streams or lakes (warm water)** — lower-value recreational fishing habitat, valued similarly to reservoir fishing.
- **Streams or lakes (cold water, incl. bass fishing)** — higher-value recreational fishing habitat, reflecting greater angler willingness-to-pay for these systems, consistent with the traditionally higher esteem for [[Cold-Water Fisheries]] (e.g., trout and bass) versus generalized warm-water fisheries.

The document also implicitly links recreational fishing/hunting value to broader habitat quality factors, listing considerations such as:
1. Expected success of the fishing or hunting effort
2. Availability of alternative fishing/hunting sites in the general area
3. Extent of fishing/hunting pressure in the general area
4. Attractiveness of the environment
5. Anticipated volume of use relative to site carrying capacity

These factors tie recreational value directly to habitat carrying capacity and regional fishing/hunting pressure — concepts relevant to [[Fisheries Carrying Capacity]] and [[Angler Effort]] management.

## Key Findings

- **Panel origin**: Established October 27, 1959 by the Subcommittee on Evaluation Standards following ICWR's October 13, 1959 acceptance of the "Report on Methods for Evaluating Fish and Wildlife Benefits and Costs."
- **Data sources reviewed**:
  - 1955 National Survey of Fishing and Hunting (sportsmen expenditure data)
  - Fish and Wildlife Service mail survey of private fishing/hunting area fees
  - National Park Service average-fee derivation for private outdoor recreation areas
  - USDA Farm Economics Research Division interim study of small reservoir fish/wildlife benefits in Arkansas, Kentucky, and Maryland
  - Additional published reports based on user expenditure data
- **Key limitation identified**: Sportsmen expenditure data could not be reliably converted into unit values comparable to market-price-based benefit estimates used elsewhere in river basin project evaluation; no satisfactory expenditure-to-net-value relationship could be established.
- **Fee data limitation**: Many private-area fee schedules bundled costs (equipment, meals, lodging) with access charges, limiting their usability; only some classes had sufficient clean data to guide judgment.
- **Resulting methodology**: Because quality and regional variation could not be captured in single point values, the Panel adopted a **range of values per class** rather than fixed unit values, to be adjusted by evaluators using professional judgment.

### Proposed Interim Schedule of Recreational Values (per Recreational Day, 1960 USD)

| Category | Subcategory | Value Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Fishing** | Reservoir | $0.50 – $1.50 |
| | Stream or lake — Warm water | $0.50 – $1.50 |
| | Stream or lake — Cold water / bass fishing | $1.00 – $3.00 |
| **Hunting — Small game** | Mammals | $0.50 – $1.50 |
| | Birds | $1.00 – $3.00 |
| | Water fowl | $1.50 – $4.50 |
| **Hunting — Big game** | Deer and antelope | $1.50 – $4.50 |
| | Other big game | $2.00 – $6.00 |

- **Recommendation**: The Panel recommended adoption of this interim schedule for use in project evaluation by participating federal agencies (Interior, Army Corps of Engineers, Agriculture, HEW) until improved data or procedures became available.
- **Acknowledged limitation**: The schedule explicitly does not account for the full diversity of personal value judgments — some users would value a recreational day far above the range, while others would not pay for currently free access — highlighting the inherent subjectivity in [[Non-Market Valuation]] of recreational resources.
- **Scope caveat**: The Panel noted this schedule covers only fishing and hunting, not the full range of recreational aspects of fish and wildlife, and represents a partial, judgment-based interim step pending further research.
