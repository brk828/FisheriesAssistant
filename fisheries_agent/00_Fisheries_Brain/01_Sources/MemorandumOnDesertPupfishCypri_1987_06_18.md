# Memorandum on Desert Pupfish (Cyprinodon macularius) Recovery Plan

## Executive Summary
This 1987 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Ecological Services, Phoenix, AZ) presents a compiled summary of distribution and reintroduction records for the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) in Arizona. The document, authored by Bill Kepner (Desert Fishes Recovery Team) and directed to the Regional Director's office in Albuquerque, was prepared to support the species' federal Recovery Plan. Records are divided by taxonomic subspecies form: the **Gila form** (*C. m. macularius*) and the **Quitobaquito form** (*C. m. sonoytae*). The memo catalogs numerous translocation and refugium-establishment events across Arizona between 1982–1983, coordinated by agencies including the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Arizona State University]], the [[University of Arizona]], and the [[Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum]] (ASDM), reflecting an active multi-institutional captive propagation and reintroduction effort for this imperiled desert cyprinodontid.

## Background & Description
The source document is an administrative/distributional memorandum and does **not provide direct morphological data** (e.g., meristics, coloration, body size) for *Cyprinodon macularius*. Taxonomically, the paper distinguishes only two recognized subspecific forms relevant to Arizona populations:
- **[[Gila Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) — associated with Gila River basin drainage sites.
- **[[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius sonoytae*) — associated with the Quitobaquito Springs lineage, historically isolated in the Sonoyta River drainage near the Arizona–Sonora border.

No further anatomical descriptors are present in the source text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Desert Pupfish]] occupies isolated desert aquatic habitats characteristic of arid Southwestern ecosystems, including:
- Spring-fed pools and tanks (e.g., Peoples Canyon Spring, Mesquite Spring, Tinney Tank)
- Man-made refugia and impoundments (e.g., Bog Hole, Deer Valley High School Refugium)
- Institutional captive-holding facilities (university ponds, botanical/zoological garden exhibits)

Documented reintroduction sites span a range of elevations from approximately **2,180 ft to 3,420 ft**, indicating tolerance for a variety of upland desert spring and canyon stream conditions within Arizona. The species' persistence strategy, as reflected in this memo, relies heavily on **artificial refugia and translocation** to multiple isolated localities — a common conservation approach for the [[Desert Fishes]] group given the fragmented, relict nature of desert spring ecosystems and threats from habitat loss and non-native species introgression/competition.

## Key Findings
- **Two subspecies tracked in Arizona**: Gila form (*C. m. macularius*) and Quitobaquito form (*C. m. sonoytae*), each managed and reintroduced separately.
- **Multi-agency reintroduction program**: BLM and AGFD jointly introduced pupfish to at least four sites (Tres Alamos Falls, Peoples Canyon, Peoples Canyon Spring, Mesquite Spring) between August 1982 and December 1983.
- **Academic/zoological refugia network**: Additional populations were established at Arizona State University (Tempe), University of Arizona sites (Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum, Tinney Tank at Audubon Research Ranch), and the Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum (Tucson), the latter introduced by [[W.L. Minckley]], Desert Fishes Recovery Team Chairman.
- **Documented population sizes** at recorded sites ranged from 50 to 200 individuals per introduction event.
- **Elevational range** of documented Arizona reintroduction sites: ~2,180–3,420 ft.
- **Last reintroduction event** noted in the record occurred in 1983, with no more recent introductions listed in this summary.
- The memo underscores reliance on **ex situ conservation** (refugia, aquaria, arboreta, school programs) as a key strategy in the desert pupfish recovery effort, consistent with broader [[Endangered Species Act]] recovery planning for isolated desert fish taxa.
