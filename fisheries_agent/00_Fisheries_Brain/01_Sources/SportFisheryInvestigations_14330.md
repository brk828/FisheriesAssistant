## Executive Summary

This section of Circular 226 reports 1964 findings from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Fish-Pesticide Research Laboratory network (Denver, Sandy Hook, Patuxent, Tishomingo, Marion, La Crosse, Jackson, Beulah, Willard) documenting acute and chronic toxicity of a broad suite of insecticides and herbicides to freshwater and estuarine fish. Key model species included [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Bluegill]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Cutthroat Trout]], with extensive LC50 bioassays, time-temperature toxicity relationships, invertebrate comparative toxicity (notably the stonefly *[[Pteronarcys californica]]*), and field-scale pond/stream experiments on herbicides (Mirex, Sodium Arsenite, Diquat, Casoron, Paraquat) and the organophosphate insecticide malathion. Findings establish that chlorinated hydrocarbon insecticides (e.g., Endrin, Dieldrin, Aldrin) are orders of magnitude more acutely toxic to fish than most herbicides, that toxicity is strongly modulated by temperature, exposure duration, and species, and that sublethal chronic exposures produce measurable physiological, histopathological, and bioaccumulation effects without necessarily causing significant mortality or growth impairment.

## Background & Description

The Sport Fishery Investigations program, directed by Oliver B. Cope, aimed to characterize the relationships among fish, pesticides, and the aquatic environment to predict ecological consequences of pesticide introduction and to enable safer pesticide use in fishery management. In 1964 the program expanded from established sites (Denver, CO; Tishomingo, OK; Marion, AL; Laurel, MD; La Crosse, WI; Jackson, WY; Beulah, WY) to new facilities at Sandy Hook, NJ (marine/estuarine toxicology), Longview and Willard, WA (fish nutrition/metabolism), and Seattle, WA.

Work was organized into three categories:
1. **Laboratory Studies and Toxicology** — controlled bioassays determining LC50 values for insecticides, herbicides, and miscellaneous chemicals (avicides, miticides, chemosterilants) against [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Bluegill]], and other species, plus invertebrate comparative toxicity.
2. **Experimental Field Studies** — pond- and stream-scale trials of malathion, Mirex, sodium arsenite, Diquat, Casoron, and Paraquat under semi-natural conditions.
3. **Effects on Wild Populations** — field monitoring following operational pesticide applications (e.g., spruce budworm control spraying).

Test species were typically juvenile fish (~0.3–3.5 g) exposed at controlled temperatures (55°F or 75°F for most standard bioassays; 35–85°F for time-temperature series) with LC50 estimated at 24, 48, and 96 hours.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types studied**: earthen fishponds (Tishomingo, Marion, La Crosse), small ponds near Denver, streams (Salmon National Forest, Idaho), and marine/estuarine waters (Sandy Hook, NJ).
- **Non-target organisms monitored alongside fish**: aquatic invertebrates (stoneflies *[[Pteronarcys californica]]*, mayfly *Baetis*, stoneflies *Isoperla* and *Claassenia*, damselflies *Amphagrion* and *Ophiogomphus*, snipefly *Atherix*, cranefly *Tipula*, caddisfly Limnephilidae, *Planaria*, *Daphnia*), plankton (rotifers, cladocerans, copepods), bottom fauna, and aquatic vegetation.
- **Environmental modifiers of toxicity**:
  - *Temperature*: Increased toxicity of endrin, toxaphene, and lindane with rising temperature in rainbow trout and bluegills; DDT and malathion showed no such trend.
  - *Salinity* (Sandy Hook estuarine studies): *Fundulus heteroclitus* most sensitive at intermediate salinities; eels showed increased sensitivity with increased salinity; grass shrimp showed decreased mortality with increased salinity.
  - *Water hardness* (Patuxent): Generally did not significantly affect herbicide toxicity to fish, except silvex vs. black bullhead, where hardest water showed double the toxicity of softest water.
  - *Exposure duration*: Fundulus exposed to LC75 (24-hr) concentrations of chlorinated hydrocarbons showed negligible mortality below 2 hours exposure, high mortality near 6 hours, and near-complete kill at ≥12 hours.
- **Bioaccumulation and food-chain implications**: Mirex accumulated far more in aquatic vegetation than in fish tissue; sodium arsenite accumulated differentially across fish organs (gills/digestive tract and liver >> flesh); Casoron and Diquat residues were measurable in water, sediment, and fish tissue with species-specific accumulation patterns (bluegill, yellow perch, and green sunfish > black bass).
- **Operational field relevance**: The Cygon (dimethoate) aerial application for spruce budworm control in Idaho's Salmon National Forest reached streams directly but produced no detectable effects on fish or aquatic invertebrates at sampled stations — an important negative/null field result contextualizing laboratory toxicity data.

## Key Findings

### Acute Toxicity — Insecticides vs. Fish (Table 1, Denver)
- Chlorinated hydrocarbons were the most acutely toxic insecticide class tested:
  - **Endrin**: Bluegill 96-hr LC50 = 0.25 µg/L (75°F); N.Z. Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 0.86 µg/L (55°F) — among the most toxic compounds tested.
  - **Dieldrin**: Bluegill 96-hr LC50 = 2.8 µg/L; Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 13 µg/L.
  - **Aldrin**: Bluegill 96-hr LC50 = 5.2 µg/L; Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 31 µg/L.
  - **Heptachlor**: Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 8 µg/L.
  - **Lindane**: Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 22 µg/L.
  - **Chlordane**: Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 7.8 µg/L; Bluegill = 40 µg/L.
- Organophosphates showed a wide toxicity range: **Phosdrin** was highly toxic to rainbow trout (96-hr LC50 = 12 µg/L), while **Dimethoate** and **Dylox** were comparatively low in acute toxicity (LC50s in thousands of µg/L).
- **Sevin (carbaryl)** and **DDVP** showed moderate-to-low acute toxicity to bluegill (96-hr LC50 = 2,000–2,500 and 480 µg/L respectively).
- General pattern: [[Bluegill]] tended to be more sensitive than [[Rainbow Trout]] to most chlorinated hydrocarbons tested at higher (75°F) vs. lower (55°F) temperatures respectively, though direct species comparisons are confounded by temperature differences in test protocol.

### Acute Toxicity — Herbicides vs. Fish (Table 2)
- Herbicides were markedly less acutely toxic to fish than insecticides, generally requiring concentrations in the thousands to hundreds of thousands of µg/L to produce 50% mortality.
- **Treflan (EC)** was a notable exception among herbicides, showing relatively high toxicity (Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 10 µg/L; Bluegill = 18 µg/L) — toxicity comparable to some insecticides.
- **Dalapon**, **2,6-dichlorobenzoic acid**, and **Dymid** were essentially non-toxic at environmentally realistic concentrations (LC50s > 90,000 µg/L).
- **Casoron (dichlobenil)**: LC50 values ~10,000–22,000 µg/L range for both species.
- **Sodium arsenite**: Rainbow Trout 96-hr LC50 = 26,000 µg/L; Bluegill = 30,000 µg/L.

### Time-Temperature Toxicity Relationships (Tables 4 & 5)
- For [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Bluegill]], toxicity of **endrin**, **toxaphene**, and **lindane** increased consistently with rising temperature (35°F→85°F range tested).
- **DDT** and **malathion** did not follow this temperature-toxicity trend, indicating a different mode of toxicological action or detoxification pathway.
- Example (Bluegill, endrin 96-hr LC50): 0.7 µg/L at 45°F declining to 0.2 µg/L at 85°F — over threefold increase in toxicity with warming.
