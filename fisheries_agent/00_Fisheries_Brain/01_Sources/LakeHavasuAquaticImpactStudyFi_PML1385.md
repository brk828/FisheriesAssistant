# Lake Havasu Aquatic Impact Study: Final Follow-Up Survey Transmittal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sportfish

## Executive Summary

This document is a transmittal letter from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Regional Director to the Director of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], with a copy to a departmental staff member. It forwards a data report from the final follow-up survey of the [[Aquatic Impact Study]] on [[Lake Havasu]]. The survey was run by the Bureau's environmental office with [[Gary Ponder]] of the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] and [[Martin Kenney]] of the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]]. Five coves were surveyed by [[Scuba Diver Observation]]. Two additional coves were scheduled but could not be surveyed because of poor visibility. The survey found that fish were scarce, [[Aquatic Vegetation]] growth was poor, and [[Water Visibility]] was limited.

The letter's date is not clearly legible in the scan.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose of the correspondence:** To keep the state wildlife agency informed of the Bureau's aquatic investigations on Lake Havasu, and to send a copy of the survey data.
- **Survey objectives (as stated):**
  1. Count all fish present.
  2. Observe aquatic vegetation.
  3. Observe survival of the year's young [[Bass]].
- **Survey method:** Scuba diver observations in each cove. A data sheet was enclosed with the letter.
- **Participating agencies:** [[Bureau of Reclamation]] environmental office, [[California Department of Fish and Game]], and [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]].
- **Document composition:** The letter is followed by a data sheet and several additional pages. Most of those pages are mirrored, rotated, or heavily degraded by OCR, so their tables and figures could not be reliably read. No figures from them are reported here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat condition:** The coves had sparse [[Aquatic Vegetation]]. The report describes growth as poor.
- **Water clarity:** [[Water Visibility]] was limited. Poor visibility also prevented the survey of two of the planned coves in the Bill Williams arm (the arm name is OCR-garbled and is inferred).
- **Likely drivers of low fish counts:** The report attributes poor counts to three possible causes, applied to all coves surveyed:
  - Lack of aquatic vegetation cover
  - Natural mortality
  - Poor visibility, which limits detection by divers

The document does not quantify these factors or rank them. It does not name the fish species observed.

## Key Findings

1. **Survey coverage:** Five coves were investigated by scuba diver observation. Two additional coves were not surveyed because of poor visibility.
2. **Fish abundance:** Fish were described as scarce across the surveyed coves. No counts or numerical totals are reported in the text.
3. **Aquatic vegetation:** Growth was poor. The report does not give vegetation measurements.
4. **Visibility:** Visibility was limited and was severe enough to cancel two planned surveys.
5. **Young bass survival:** The survey objective covered young bass survival, but the text gives no result for this objective.
6. **Interpretation:** Low fish counts were tentatively linked to lack of vegetation cover, natural mortality, and poor visibility. The report does not separate these effects.
7. **Data delivery:** The detailed data sheet was enclosed for the state agency's use. Its contents are not reproduced in the source text as legible.
