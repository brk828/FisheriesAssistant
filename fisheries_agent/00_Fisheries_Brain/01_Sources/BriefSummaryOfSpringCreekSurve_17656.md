# Brief Summary of Spring Creek Surveys, August 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This brief summary by [[Tonto National Forest]] biologist Lisa Bizios, dated January 7, 1993, reports August 1992 fish surveys at [[Spring Creek]] and [[Rock Creek]]. It was prepared to support initial evaluation of a proposal by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] to stock [[Brown Trout]] in both streams. The survey captured 184 fish of six species across four sampling sites. [[Roundtail Chub]] dominated the catch (116 fish, 63%). Riparian and channel conditions were rated excellent, with no visible erosion, sedimentation, bank damage from grazing, or recreational impacts. The author recommends evaluating native fish management and considers reintroducing [[Razorback Sucker]] if the Forest Service acquires the [[Spring Creek Ranch]].

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Provide an early, preliminary summary before a complete report. Results from the October [[Fall Fish Count]] are excluded and will appear in the final report.
- **Sampling sites:** Spring Creek Ranch, [[Burleson Property]], Flying W, and Rock Creek. Site locations are shown on an attached map, which is not reproduced here.
- **Species recorded (codes in source):** Roundtail Chub (GIRO), Speckled Dace (RHOC), Green Sunfish (LECY), Desert Sucker (PACL), Yellow Bullhead (AMNA), and Brown Trout (SATR).
- **Supporting materials:** Length-frequency histograms of Roundtail Chub for Spring Creek (August and November 1992), and several scanned Fall Fish Count data sheets and Nongame Branch location, habitat, and methods databases. Most scanned entries are illegible or partially legible, so only the summary-level content is reported here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat condition:** Visual assessment at the Spring Creek Ranch and Burleson sites in Spring Creek, and at Rock Creek, rated riparian and channel conditions as excellent. No significant erosion, sedimentation, grazing damage, or recreational impacts were observed.
- **Native fish opportunities:** The author recommends emphasizing native fish in management and identifying further options for Spring and Rock Creeks.
- **Rearing ponds:** The Spring Creek Ranch has rearing ponds. The author states that [[Razorback Sucker]] rearing ponds have been very successful elsewhere in Arizona and may be the least costly way to aid recovery of this threatened, endangered, or sensitive (TES) species.
- **Stocking context:** The proposal to stock non-native Brown Trout was reviewed against the native community, which is dominated by chubs, dace, and suckers. Brown Trout made up only 3 of 184 fish captured.

## Key Findings

1. **Community composition:** Six species were captured, totaling 184 fish. The native cyprinids and catostomids (Roundtail Chub, Speckled Dace, and Desert Sucker) made up 171 fish (about 93%).
2. **Dominant species:** Roundtail Chub was the most abundant species, with 116 fish. Spring Creek Ranch recorded the highest count at 46.
3. **Site totals:** Spring Creek Ranch (63), Flying W (53), Rock Creek (36), and Burleson (32).
4. **Non-native and sport fish:** Green Sunfish (6), Yellow Bullhead (4), and Brown Trout (3) were rare. Brown Trout were found only at Spring Creek Ranch (2) and Burleson (1).
5. **Habitat quality:** Riparian and channel conditions were excellent across all surveyed areas, with no significant anthropogenic damage noted.
6. **Management recommendations:**
   - Evaluate native fish emphasis in management activities.
   - Consider reintroducing [[Razorback Sucker]] into Spring Creek if the Forest Service acquires the Spring Creek Ranch, using its rearing ponds.
   - Identify other native fish and TES species opportunities for Spring and Rock Creeks.
7. **Pending work:** A complete report is expected within the following month, and it will include October Fall Fish Count results and the final evaluation of the Brown Trout stocking proposal.
