## Executive Summary

This document profiles the **Greers Ferry National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1965 under the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] to mitigate fishery losses in the **White River Basin** caused by federal reservoir development (Greers Ferry Dam). The station's sole production species is the [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), reared for stocking mitigation programs in **Arkansas (75%)** and **Texas (25%)**. The facility relies on a gravity-fed, tri-level intake system drawing cold water directly from the face of Greers Ferry Dam, a hydrological feature critical to sustaining coldwater [[Salmonid Aquaculture]] in an otherwise warm southern climate. Distribution data from 1990–1992 show substantial annual output, peaking at over 1.7 million fish/lbs distributed to federal waters in 1991.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/Water Source**: Station water supply is entirely gravity-fed via a **trilevel intake** on the face of **Greers Ferry Dam**, supplying up to **10,000 gallons per minute**. This tailwater draw provides the cold, stable water temperatures required for coldwater [[Rainbow Trout]] culture, functionally mimicking a coldwater tailrace ecosystem below a large impoundment.
- **Rearing Infrastructure**: Fish are cultured in a combination of:
  - 36 outdoor concrete raceways (100' × 8')
  - 22 indoor concrete raceways (2.5' × 30')
  - Supporting hatchery/aquarium house with lab, tank room, and visitor viewing area.
- **Mitigation Ecology**: The hatchery exists specifically to offset the ecological/fishery impacts of the **Greers Ferry Dam** reservoir project on native riverine fish populations in the **White River Basin**, a common mitigation model for U.S. Army Corps of Engineers water projects.
- **Stocking Range**: Reared trout are distributed into federal waters across two states — Arkansas (majority) and Texas (mitigation allocation) — indicating the ecological role of hatchery-reared [[Rainbow Trout]] as a put-and-take or mitigation fishery component rather than a self-sustaining wild population.
- **Station Footprint**: The entire operation occupies 30.48 acres of U.S. Army Corps of Engineers land, permitted via Permit No. DA-03-050-Civeng-63-853 (issued Feb 1, 1963).

## Key Findings

- **Single-Species Production**: Greers Ferry NFH currently produces exclusively [[Rainbow Trout]], with no other species reared at this station per the profile.
- **Legal/Historical Basis**: Established under the Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act (48 Stat. 401) to mitigate fishery losses from the Greers Ferry Dam reservoir project; construction funded FY1962–1963.
- **Interstate Mitigation Role**: Uniquely, this Arkansas-based hatchery fulfills mitigation obligations in **Texas** as well as Arkansas, reflecting a cross-state federal mitigation agreement (25%/75% split).
- **Water Supply Engineering**: The trilevel dam-face intake is a key infrastructural feature enabling coldwater trout culture in a warmwater regional climate — a critical abiotic factor for the [[Coldwater Fish Hatchery]] model.
