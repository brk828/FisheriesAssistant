# Cicindela Vol. 28 (1-2), 1996: Obituary of Mont A. Cazier and Rediscovery of Pometon Singularis in Bolivia

#entomology #coleoptera #cicindelidae

## Executive Summary

This combined March/June 1996 issue of [[Cicindela]], a quarterly journal devoted to [[Cicindelidae]], contains three main items: an in memoriam for the tiger beetle specialist [[Mont A. Cazier]] (1911-1995), the editorial and subscription policy of the journal, and a research note by [[David L. Pearson]], David W. Brzoska and J. Fernando Guerra reporting the first record of [[Pometon Singularis]] from [[Bolivia]] and its natural history in the Department of Santa Cruz. The journal's editorial information and the biographical details are reported as stated in the source.

## Background & Description

**Journal and editorial policy**
- Journal: *Cicindela*, ISSN 0590-6334, Volume 28, Numbers 1-2, March/June 1996.
- Senior editor: Ronald L. Huber (Prairie Village, Kansas). Co-editors: Robert C. Graves ([[Bowling Green State University]], Ohio) and Richard Freitag ([[Lakehead University]], Thunder Bay, Ontario).
- Manuscripts on any aspect of Cicindelidae are considered from any author; papers on the [[Nearctic]] are especially solicited and must be in English. Manuscripts are typed and double-spaced.
- The journal is produced by offset methods, so the senior editor handles layout and no proofs are sent to authors.
- Subscriptions: $7.00 per annual volume of four numbers. Back issues of prior volumes are $2.50 per issue, if available.

**In memoriam: Mont A. Cazier (1911-1995)**
- Born 27 May 1911 in Cardston, Alberta; died in his sleep on 29 September 1995 in Tempe, Arizona, aged 84.
- Professor Emeritus of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU). His career was divided almost equally between Curator (and Chairman) of Entomology at the [[American Museum of Natural History]] (AMNH) and ASU.
- Expert on the systematics and biodiversity of Coleoptera, Hymenoptera and Diptera; authored more than 61 scientific publications.
- Military service: joined the US Army Medical Corps in 1943, served in India and the China Theater on disease-vector and malaria control, and was awarded the Bronze Star.
- Founded and directed the [[Southwestern Research Station]] (SWRS), a 44-acre station opened in 1955 in the Chiricahua Mountains region of Arizona.
- Survived by his wife Carolyn, four daughters, a son, nine grandchildren and two great-grandchildren.

**Cicindelidae described by Cazier**
The issue lists taxa described by Cazier, including *Cicindela plutonica leachi* (1936a), *C. parowana platti* (1936b), *C. californica mojavi* (1937b), *C. lunalonga wagneri* (1937b), *C. nevadica tubensis* (1939a), *C. haemorrhagica miniscula* (1948), *C. latesignata parkeri* (1948), *C. sinaloae schrammeli* (1954), *C. bradti* (1954), *C. macrocnema kino* (1954), *C. sperata vauriei* (1954), *C. rockefelleri* (1954) and *Omus submetallicus niger* (1937a). The source list is partly garbled, so the exact count is not stated here.

**Pometon singularis**
- Originally described by [[Fleutiaux]] (1899a) as *Metopon singularis* from the State of Goiás near Jatahy, east-central Brazil. Fleutiaux later (1899b) renamed the genus *Pometon* because *Metopon* was preoccupied by a hymenopteran genus (Walker 1834).
- Morphology: convex vertex with no hollow between the eyes; small, non-prominent eyes relative to head size; head long behind the eyes; peculiarly elongated body shape.
- Fleutiaux was uncertain of its relationships and hypothesized a position near the Nearctic fauna.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The habitat and basic natural history of the Bolivian population are described in the original article, but the extracted text does not provide the details.
- Cazier's career centred on field-based natural history of insects, spiders and scorpions in [[Southwestern North America]], as described in the obituary.
- The SWRS was established as a field research hub for southwestern North America and, in its first year, attracted 84 scientists from 23 universities and four major museums.
- Cazier was noted for his collecting: he donated more than 70,000 tiger beetles to the AMNH collection, which in 1948 was described as the largest private collection in existence, and by 1976 had donated more than 243,000 insects to the ASU collection.

## Key Findings

- **First Bolivian record:** [[Pometon Singularis]] is reported for the first time from Bolivia (Department of Santa Cruz), and the first time since its original discovery in Brazil in the 1890s.
- **Morphological comparison:** The Bolivian population differs from the Brazilian type specimens in several distinctive colour characters of the elytra, legs and abdomen.
- **Institutional affiliations of the authors:** Pearson (ASU), Brzoska (Lawrence, Kansas), and Guerra (Instituto de Ecologia, Universidad Mayor de San Andrés, La Paz).
- **Cazier's scientific legacy:** Over 61 publications, many new taxa of tiger beetles and other Coleoptera, and foundational work on Mexican and Lower Californian Cicindelidae (e.g., Cazier 1948, 1954, 1960).
- **Founding of the SWRS:** Cazier opened the 44-acre [[Southwestern Research Station]] in 1955, with David Rockefeller's support, and directed it after 1960.
- **Career timeline:** Curator at the AMNH (from 1941), chairman of Entomology (from 1946), SWRS director (from 1960), professor at ASU (from 1964), and retirement from the AMNH in 1962 after 20 years of service.
- **Journal information:** Annual subscription $7.00; back issues $2.50 each; manuscripts in English are particularly sought on Nearctic Cicindelidae.
