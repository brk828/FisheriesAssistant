# Late Devonian Tectonism in Southeastern Arizona

#marine-biology #conodonts #stratigraphy

## Executive Summary

This 1976 *Arizona Geological Society Digest* paper by Schumacher, Witter, Meader and Keith (Department of Geosciences, University of Arizona) reconstructs Late Devonian structural change in southeastern Arizona. The authors compare [[Isopach Mapping|isopach maps]] of the Frasnian [[Martin Formation]] and the Famennian [[Percha Formation]] to show that a Frasnian positive element was almost completely reversed into a Famennian basin. Biostratigraphic control from [[Conodont Biostratigraphy|conodonts]] narrows the reversal to the [[Palmatolepis rhomboidea|Palmatolepis rhomboidea]] and [[Palmatolepis marginifera|P. marginifera]] zones. The authors tie this timing to the peak of the [[Antler Orogeny]] in Nevada.

The paper is primarily stratigraphic and tectonic. It contains no ichthyological or ecological field data, but it does record marine fossil groups and fish remains within the stratigraphic sequence.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** The study addresses the scarcity of information on Paleozoic tectonic activity in Arizona. Earlier work by Stoyanow (1942) and McKee (1951) lacked the detailed stratigraphic subdivision needed to reconstruct depositional and structural history.
- **Approach:** The authors built the analysis on 35 stratigraphic sections across a six-county area of southeastern Arizona. About half were measured or remeasured, described and sampled for conodonts. The remainder came from published references listed in Table 1. The study is part of a regional project on the stratigraphy, biostratigraphy and paleoecology of the Devonian in Arizona.
- **Method:** Successive isopach maps were used as a record of tectonism during deposition. Contour lines of equal thickness show where a unit thickened or thinned, so changes between units can be dated to specific stratigraphic intervals.
- **Nomenclature:**
  - The [[Martin Formation]] was named by Ransome (1904) for a carbonate sequence between the Cambrian Abrigo Formation and the Mississippian [[Escabrosa Limestone]] near Bisbee. It is Lower Upper Devonian (Frasnian) in age.
  - The Percha name derives from the [[Percha Shale]] of southwestern New Mexico (Gordon, 1907; Stevenson, 1945). The authors formally applied "Percha Formation" to the Arizona sequence for the first time. Stoyanow (1936) had proposed a "Lower Ouray Formation," but that name was defined on faunal rather than lithologic grounds and was never accepted, so the authors replaced it.
  - The Percha Formation is Upper Upper Devonian (Famennian) in age and sits above the Martin Formation.
- **Historic correlatives:** The [[Morenci Shale]] (Lindgren, 1905) and the [[Portal Formation]] (Sabins, 1957) were correlated with the Percha Formation, at least in part.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Percha Formation lithology and fossils**

- **Shale member (lower, informal):** Olive-green to yellowish- or reddish-brown slope-forming shales with siltstone beds that increase toward the top. Thickness ranges from less than 10 feet to more than 200 feet. A basal hematitic quartz sandstone contains quartz pebbles, phosphatic concretions, arthrodiran fish fragments, shark teeth, conodonts and an occasional abraded brachiopod or coral, probably reworked from the Martin Formation. Conodonts from this basal unit at Locality 13 suggest the late Famennian *Scaphignathus velifer* Zone.
- **Carbonate member (upper, informal):** Yellowish-brown to pale-red to light-gray silty dolomite, dolomitic siltstone and limestone, with quartz nodules up to three inches across. Fossils include brachiopods, crinoids, bryozoans, sponges and solitary rugose corals. Conodonts and rhynchonellid brachiopods both point to a late Famennian age (*Polygnathus styriacus* Zone). The member correlates with the Ouray Limestone of Colorado and the Box member of the Percha Shale. Its thickness ranges from 5 feet to more than 100 feet. It is less widespread than the shale member and favors basinal locations.

**Stratigraphic relationships**

- The Percha Formation usually rests disconformably on the Martin Formation. Locally it overlies the Cambrian Abrigo Formation or the Ordovician [[Longfellow Limestone]].
- A thin quartz sandstone at the base of the Escabrosa Limestone marks the upper contact at several localities. Where it is absent, the contact is subjective.

**Regional stratigraphic context**

- Ross (1973) subdivided the Pennsylvanian–Lower Permian strata of southeastern Arizona into 14 unconformity-bounded units, dated them with fusulinids and reconstructed the regional depositional and tectonic history.

## Key Findings

**1. Frasnian positive element**
- The Frasnian isopach map reveals a relatively narrow, east- and northeast-trending positive area extending from [[Winkelman]] to the [[Clifton-Morenci Area]].
- The positive area is the southwestern extension of the [[Defiance Uplift]]. It exposed Cambrian (Localities 20, 21) and Ordovician (Locality 2) strata at the surface.
- The zero-isopach line outlining the positive area approximates the Martin strand line or depositional limit. South, west and northwest of it, the Martin Formation thickens rapidly to 300 feet or more.

**2. Famennian basin reversal**
- The Famennian isopach map shows an almost complete reversal of basins and positive elements.
- The Percha Formation attains its maximum thickness in a basin, or basins, that developed over the former positive area. The basin limits coincide nearly exactly with that area.
- Maximum Famennian thickness occurs where Frasnian strata are greatly thinned or absent.
- Abrupt thickness changes near Winkelman strongly affect the Martin Formation but have little effect on the Percha Formation.

**3. Timing of structural change**
- Conodont biostratigraphy (this study and Ethington, 1965), together with Johnson's (1971) analysis of geosynclinal tectonism and cratonic onlap–offlap sequences, narrows the event to the *Palmatolepis rhomboidea* and *P. marginifera* zones of the Famennian.
- The post-Martin, pre-Percha subsidence of the Frasnian positive element and the accompanying regional uplift of the former Frasnian basins took place in the early-middle Famennian. This coincides with the beginning of the [[Antler Orogeny]] in Nevada.
- The late Famennian onlap that deposited the Percha shales and carbonates coincides with the maximum intensity of the Antler Orogeny (Johnson, 1971).

**4. Methodological conclusion**
- The authors argue that a series of isopach (and facies) maps for short stratigraphic intervals can reconstruct Paleozoic structural development in detail. This is valuable where post-Paleozoic erosion, intrusion, volcanism, thrusting and block faulting have obscured pre-Mesozoic relations, as in southern Arizona.

**5. Data base**
- 35 stratigraphic sections were used, listed in Table 1 with their sources (many "this study," others from Pine, Wright, Stoyanow, Huddle and Dobrovolny, and Sabins).
- Isopach contours use a 100-foot interval. Figures 6 and 7 show the Frasnian Martin and Famennian Percha isopach maps.
