# Letter Regarding Plans to Obtain Live Rio Yaqui Fishes for Dexter and San Bernardino NWR

#ichthyology #freshwater-conservation #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Ictaluridae #Gila

## Executive Summary
This 1986 letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Dean Hendrickson, Nongame Fish Biologist, on behalf of Duane L. Shroufe) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service outlines a binational collection effort to repatriate native [[Rio Yaqui Fishes]] into the United States via captive refugia at [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. The proposal, developed in coordination with Lourdes Juarez of Mexico's Centro Ecológico (SEDUE, Sonora office), details a multi-day, cross-border field expedition into Sonora and Chihuahua, Mexico, to collect four target taxa — [[Ictalurus pricei]] (Yaqui catfish), [[Notropis formosus]] (Yaqui/beautiful shiner), [[Catostomus bernardini]] (Yaqui sucker), and [[Gila robusta]] (roundtail chub) — from historically documented localities referenced in Hendrickson et al. (1981). An appended data table (Table 1) presents detailed life-history and reproductive biology data for **[[Gila purpurea]]** (Yaqui chub) collected from Black Draw, San Bernardino NWR, including standard/total length, weight, gonad measurements, gut contents, and ovum diameter — reflecting concurrent monitoring of an already-established refuge population.

## Background & Description
While the letter itself is primarily logistical/administrative, the appended Table 1 provides quantitative morphometric and reproductive data for **Gila purpurea** sampled from Black Draw (SBNWR) across multiple collection events (seine, electrofishing, experimental gill net; November 1985–July 1986):

- **Standard length (SL):** ranges approximately 45–130 mm across samples
- **Total weight:** ranges from <1 g to ~13.9 g in larger individuals
- **Sex determination:** coded 0 = unsexed, 1 = male, 2 = female
- **Gonad/ovary metrics recorded:** ovary wet weight, ovary length, ovary weight as percent of body weight, and mean ovum diameter (mm) — ovum diameters recorded in the range of ~0.4–0.5 mm where present
- **Gut content analysis:** diet items identified include Ephemeroptera, unidentified insects, filamentous algae, Odonata, vascular plant material, terrestrial material, Cladocera, Ostracoda, Collembola, Coleoptera, and Diptera — indicating an omnivorous, opportunistic feeding strategy typical of small-bodied desert cyprinids.

No morphological description is given in the letter for [[Ictalurus pricei]], [[Notropis formosus]], [[Catostomus bernardini]], or [[Gila robusta]] beyond taxonomic identity and target collection numbers (up to 300 specimens per taxon).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target drainage system:** Rio Yaqui basin, spanning the international border between Arizona/New Mexico (USA) and Sonora/Chihuahua (Mexico); specifically the [[Rio de Bavispe]] drainage, which historically connects Cajón Bonito, Arroyo Moctezuma, and the San Bernardino Valley.
- **Key collection localities** (numbered per Hendrickson et al. 1981, *J. AZ Nev. Acad. Sci.* 15(3):100):
  - **Cajón Bonito, Sonora** (sites 21–22): target habitat for [[Catostomus bernardini]] (Sonora/Yaqui sucker) and [[Gila robusta]] (roundtail chub); also proposed site for adding [[Campostoma ornatum]] (Mexican stoneroller).
  - **Arroyo Moctezuma, Chihuahua** (sites 76–77): largest known population of [[Notropis formosus]] recorded in the 1978 survey; part of the Rio de Bavispe drainage, ecologically linked to San Bernardino.
  - **Rio Sirupa** (site 69): supports a channel catfish-free, non-hybridized population of [[Ictalurus pricei]] (Yaqui catfish) — critical because other sites risk hybrid contamination with introduced channel catfish.
  - **Piedras Verdes at Colonia Juárez:** potential secondary site for live stock collection of Mimbres shiner.
- **Refuge habitat:** Fish are proposed for translocation into ponds at [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]], with [[Gila purpurea]] already established and monitored at **Black Draw**, a spring-fed stream/drainage on the refuge near the Mexican border. This site is sampled repeatedly (Nov 1985, Jul 1986, Dec 1985) using seines, electrofishing, and experimental gill nets, indicating an ongoing population and reproductive monitoring program.
- **Conservation rationale:** The proposed collection strategy prioritizes source populations that are geographically and phylogenetically closest to the historic native fauna of San Bernardino NWR, to preserve appropriate genetic and ecological fidelity during reintroduction.

## Key Findings
- The letter documents an active, binational (US–Mexico) repatriation effort for four imperiled Rio Yaqui native fishes: [[Ictalurus pricei]], [[Notropis formosus]], [[Catostomus bernardini]], and [[Gila robusta]], intended to restore historic Rio Yaqui fish fauna to [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- Collection logistics required interagency and international coordination: Arizona Game and Fish Department (Section 6/Seven Fish Project funding), Centro Ecológico (SEDUE, Sonora permits), and USFWS (importation permits, funding for Mexican collaborator participation).
- Field expedition planned in five stages spanning approximately 7–10 days: (1) collection at Cajón Bonito for suckers/chub, (2) delivery to San Bernardino NWR, (3) travel to Arroyo Moctezuma for [[Notropis formosus]], (4) travel to Rio Sirupa for [[Ictalurus pricei]], (5) return transport to Dexter NFH.
- Site selection deliberately avoided localities with known hybrid Yaqui catfish populations (due to introduced channel catfish contamination), underscoring a conservation-genetics concern in source-population selection.
- Timing was designed to coincide with seasonal low-flow conditions prior to summer rains, optimizing capture efficiency via seining and electrofishing.
- Possible taxon additions discussed: [[Campostoma ornatum]] (Mexican stoneroller, low additional cost at Cajón Bonito) and Mimbres shiner stock from Piedras Verdes (moderate additional time cost).
- Appended life-history dataset for **[[Gila purpurea]]** at Black Draw, SBNWR demonstrates active reproductive monitoring (ovary weight, ovum diameter, gonad-somatic ratios) and diet analysis (aquatic/terrestrial invertebrates, algae, plant matter) for an already-refugial population, serving as a baseline model for evaluating success of future translocated populations of congeneric taxa such as [[Gila robusta]].
- The letter reflects a precautionary, time-sensitive management strategy under permitting constraints, with a stated deadline (end of January) for finalizing species lists, specimen numbers, and itinerary with Mexican collaborators.
