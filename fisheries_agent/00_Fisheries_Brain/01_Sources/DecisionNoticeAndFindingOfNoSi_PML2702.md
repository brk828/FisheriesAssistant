# Apache Trout Habitat Improvement: Decision Notice and Finding of No Significant Impact

#ichthyology #marine-biology #apache-trout

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 [[Decision Notice]] and [[Finding of No Significant Impact]] (FONSI) from the USDA Forest Service, [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]], covering habitat improvement for the [[Apache Trout]] on nine Apache trout streams. The Forest Supervisor selected a modified version of [[Alternative 1]] from the [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA). The decision fences livestock out of targeted stream reaches, including all of Reach 1 on [[Double Cienega Creek]] and Reach 1 on [[Mineral Creek]], and drops a planned cross fence on the ELC allotment. The chosen action requires the least fencing and cost of the action alternatives, and it meets the [[Apache Trout Recovery Plan]] and the terms of the [[Biological Opinion]] from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (F&WS).

## Background & Description

- **Letter context:** A cover letter dated July 7, 1995 sent the Decision Notice to a planning participant. It was signed by Forest Supervisor John C. Bedell.
- **Purpose:** Improve riparian and aquatic habitat for Apache trout by excluding livestock from degraded stream reaches, lakes, springs, and meadows.
- **Public involvement:** Scoping and meetings began in 1992 and included Forest scientists, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], grazing permittees, the F&WS, the [[National Wildlife Federation]], and the public. The proposal went out for a 30-day public comment period in April 1995. Comments are addressed in Appendix I of the EA.
- **Key issues identified in scoping:**
  - Rate of habitat recovery and how "adequate" recovery is defined
  - Effects on [[Wilderness Areas]] values
  - Effects on recreationists
  - Economic impacts from potential livestock reductions

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Alternatives considered** (full discussion in EA pages 19–22):

| Alternative | Fence to exclude livestock from streams | Fence for lakes, springs, meadows, riparian pastures |
|---|---|---|
| Alternative 1 (as analyzed) | 27.1 miles, covering 19.4 miles of stream | 5.9 miles |
| Alternative 2 ([[No Action Alternative]]) | None; current management continues | None |
| Alternative 3 | 39.5 miles, covering approximately 23–24 miles of stream (OCR-garbled in source) | About 5 miles (OCR-garbled) |

**Impacts of Alternative 1:**
- Recovery rates approximate those of Alternative 3, except on two streams.
- No new fences in wilderness.
- 21.4 miles of new fence visible from streams.
- 17 new gates.
- No anticipated reduction in permitted livestock numbers.
- 102 head-months displaced from fenced areas.

**Impacts of Alternative 2 (No Action):**
- Slowest recovery rates; may result in new fences in wilderness.
- Visibility of new fence, number of gates, and livestock reductions are unknown.

**Impacts of Alternative 3:**
- Best recovery rates.
- 0.9 miles of fence in wilderness, with 0.2 miles visible from a stream.
- 24 miles of new fence visible from streams.
- 18 new gates.
- No anticipated reduction in permitted livestock numbers.
- 155 head-months displaced from fenced areas.

**Related grazing management:** The Forest is analyzing new [[Grazing Permits]] for allotments containing the covered reaches. If streams remain unfenced when a new permit is issued and that permit does not provide for protective grazing management, the decision maker may proceed with fencing. This remains in effect until monitoring indicates other action is needed.

**Legal and planning framework:** The action conforms with all federal laws and the [[Forest Plan]] (Apache/Sitgreaves Land Management Plan, as amended). It fulfills the plan's priority-one stream protection objective, which called for revised allotment management plans by 1992. The fencing is an interim step until those plans are revised.

## Key Findings

**Decision and modifications to Alternative 1:**
- Fence all of Reach 1 on Double Cienega Creek.
- Drop the 1.1-mile cross fence in the North pasture of the ELC allotment. Improved [[Livestock Grazing]] herd management is expected to provide the same protection.
- Fence all of Reach 1 on Mineral Creek (Harris Lake Allotment) and construct two livestock drinkers west of Reach 1. This eliminates one riparian pasture and aligns with the F&WS conservation recommendations. It cuts about one mile of fencing.
- Overall fencing is reduced by 2.1 miles. The final proposed action totals **26.1 miles** to protect stream riparian areas and **5.4 miles** to protect lakes, springs, meadows, and riparian pasture development.

**Rationale:**
- Meets the priority-one stream protection objective of the Forest Plan.
- Has the least economic impact on the government and affected permittees because it requires the minimum fence construction.
- Can be implemented sooner than the other action alternatives.
- Meets the needs of the Apache Trout Recovery Plan and the Biological Opinion terms.

**FONSI, by significance criterion (the ten NEPA criteria under 40 CFR 1508.27):**
1. **Intensity:** Effects are mostly beneficial. Vegetation benefits appear within 1–2 years. Long-term gains in bank stability, pool measure, and pool structure are expected. Adverse effects are short term and do not impair land productivity (EA pages 8–15).
2. **Public health and safety:** No significant risks identified.
3. **Unique areas:** No parks, prime farmlands, or ecologically critical areas nearby. Cultural site density is low. Projects involving ground disturbance will be surveyed, and significant sites will be avoided.
4. **Controversy:** Effects are limited to individual streams and riparian ecosystems. No evidence has been provided that effects were mispredicted.
5. **Uncertainty and risk:** Effects are typical for this type of action. Proven mitigation is expected to hold effects at or below predictions (EA page 22).
6. **Precedent:** The decision does not set precedent. Future actions will go through NEPA on their own merits.
7. **Cumulative effects:** Concern exists over livestock impacts on adjacent uplands and unfenced riparian areas. This is discussed in EA pages 27–28, and monitoring will be used to catch unacceptable impacts.
8. **Historic and transportation resources:** No highway, structure, or National Register site affected.
9. **Threatened and endangered species:** A [[Biological Assessment and Evaluation]] covered the [[American Peregrine Falcon]], [[Bald Eagle]], [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], Apache trout, and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]. The project is not likely to adversely affect the birds or their habitat. Some project phases may affect Apache trout or its habitat, and mitigation is recommended. Individual impacts are not expected to trend toward federal listing or loss of viability.
10. **Compliance:** Consistent with federal, state, and local law and the Forest Plan. Local county governments support the action. No major federal action significantly affecting the human environment is identified, so an Environmental Impact Statement is not needed.

**Implementation and appeal:**
- Implementation begins no sooner than five business days after the appeal period closes. If an appeal is filed, it begins no sooner than 15 calendar days after the appeal decision. Field preparation may proceed in the meantime.
- Appeals follow 36 CFR 215 and must be filed within 45 days of the Legal Notice publication in the *White Mountain Independent*. Appeals go to Regional Forester Charles W. Cartwright Jr. (Southwestern Region, Albuquerque, NM), with a copy to Forest Supervisor Bedell.
