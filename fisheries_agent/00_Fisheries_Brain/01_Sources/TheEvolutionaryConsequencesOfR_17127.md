# Gene Flow and Speciation in Australian Hydrobiid Snails

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hydrobiidae

## Executive Summary

This paper (Colgan & Ponder, *The Nautilus* Supplement 2, 1994, pp. 25–43) examines how restrictions on [[Gene Flow]] shape the population genetic structure and speciation of four Australian hydrobiid snail faunas: the brackish-water genus *Tatea*, the freshwater *Fluvidona* at [[Wilsons Promontory]], the *Fonscochlea* and *Trochidrobia* of the [[Lake Eyre Mound Springs]], and an undescribed genus at [[Dalhousie Springs]]. Gene flow varies widely among faunas: high in *Tatea*, relatively high at Dalhousie Springs, and extremely low at Wilsons Promontory and the Lake Eyre springs. Using allozyme data, the authors estimate Nm with [[F-Statistics]] and [[Private Alleles]] approaches and conclude that F-statistics is the more reliable estimator.

## Background & Description

Hydrobiidae are small prosobranch snails and the most diverse freshwater gastropod family, with nearly 400 generic names in use (Kabat & Hershler, 1993). In Australia they typically occupy small streams or springs, where populations are isolated or semi-isolated because of limited dispersal. Other genera inhabit brackish or estuarine waters, tend to have large ranges, and in some cases have a planktonic marine larval phase.

The authors distinguish two factor sets shaping evolutionary fate: "coping factors" (predation, climate, competition, inbreeding) that determine population survival, and "isolating factors" that determine divergence from conspecific populations. Gene flow is defined as genetically effective transmission of alleles between extant populations; re-colonization after local extinction is explicitly excluded.

Gene flow is estimated as Nm, where N is effective subpopulation size and m is the probability that a gamete is an immigrant. Three population models frame the estimates: the [[Island Model]] (Wright, 1931), the [[Stepping Stone Model]] (Kimura & Weiss, 1964), and continuous-distribution models. Electrophoresis used [[Cellulose Acetate Electrophoresis]] on individual snails, with analysis in BIOSYS-1, NTSYS and PHYLIP.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Tatea:** estuarine, continuous habitat with an assumed free-swimming larval stage; widely distributed throughout temperate Australia, with *T. rufilabris* and *T. huonensis* sympatric over virtually the whole range.
- **Wilsons Promontory:** granite hills up to 754 m with permanent streams fed by rainfall above 1000 mm per year. The fauna comprises two endemic morphospecies of *Fluvidona*, one of which splits into three genetic species (MPI 3, MPI 4, MPI 5) by nearly fixed sympatric differences in mannosephosphate isomerase phenotype.
- **Lake Eyre mound springs:** springs fed by the [[Great Artesian Basin]] on the fringes of the basin, forming a roughly 400 km one-dimensional chain, with drainage at right angles to the spring line so flood transport between groups is unlikely. The fauna includes two endemic genera, *Fonscochlea* (five species) and *Trochidrobia* (four species).
- **Dalhousie Springs:** a complex of springs covering about 70 km², with discharge accounting for 90–95% of South Australian artesian spring output and 41% of Great Artesian Basin spring output. The nearest Lake Eyre population (*F. zeidleri*) is 140 km away. Likely early Pleistocene in age.

Taxa differ in size, thermal and salinity tolerance, and desiccation resistance, and occupy continuous (*Tatea*) or discontinuous habitats (Wilsons Promontory, Dalhousie Springs, Lake Eyre).

## Key Findings

- **Gene flow varies strongly among faunas.** Overall Fst values are very high for straight-sided *Fluvidona* (0.130), convex MPI 3 (0.086) and MPI 4 (0.075); the smallest, for MPI 5, is 0.240. The implied migration for MPI 4 is Nm(Fst) = 0.117, meaning a catchment receives less than one migrant every three generations.
- **Method comparison.** Estimates from private alleles, Nm(p(1)), are much higher than those from Fst and rank taxa differently. MPI 4 has the highest Nm(p(1)) but the lowest Nm(Fst). The authors attribute the discrepancy to sampling scale, since intensive sampling finds rare alleles in multiple populations.
- **Lake Eyre gene flow is species-specific.** *Fonscochlea accepta* (restricted to the southern cluster) shows high gene flow, with Nm(Fst) between spring groups above 2. *F. aquatica*, the ecological replacement in the central and northern clusters, has gene flow about 25 times lower.
- **Dalhousie Springs shows high gene flow.** Estimates for the globular and pupiform snails are at the upper end of the study range, and inter-group migration exceeds all other taxa. Nearly all variation in globular snails lies within spring subgroups.
- **Speciation tracks gene flow only loosely.** Species numbers and distributions usually correlate with gene flow, but some taxa with comparably low gene flow have speciated while others have not.
- **Comparative context.** Marine gastropods with planktonic larvae often show little population subdivision (e.g. *Strombus gigas*, *Littorina littorea*). Freshwater *Goniobasis* show high divergence among populations within drainages, reflecting restricted gene flow. Basommatophoran pulmonates, often self-fertilising, show extremely low genetic variation, which hampers gene flow measurement.
- **Data scope.** The Appendices list 27 Wilsons Promontory sample sites and the Lake Eyre and Dalhousie hierarchies, with allozyme loci interpretable per taxon (about 30 enzyme systems, using the abbreviations and EC numbers listed).
