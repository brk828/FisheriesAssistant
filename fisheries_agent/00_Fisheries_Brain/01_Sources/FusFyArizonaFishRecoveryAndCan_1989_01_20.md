## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) fiscal year 1989 budget allocation record detailing funding for endangered and candidate fish species recovery efforts in Arizona. The funds are distributed under two regulatory mechanisms of the Endangered Species Act: **Section 6** (state cooperative agreements for listed species) and **Section 4** (candidate species assessment and listing support). The allocations target seven native southwestern fish taxa, focusing on population monitoring, reintroduction, habitat surveys, and physical habitat protection (fish barrier construction). Total Section 6 funding itemized is approximately $101,000, plus a contingent $5,000, with an additional $3,600 allocated under Section 4 for [[Woundfin]] monitoring.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide direct morphological data, as it is an administrative funding record rather than a biological survey report. However, the species listed represent distinct freshwater fish families native to the Colorado River and Gila River drainages, each with characteristic desert-adapted morphologies:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Catostomidae; distinguished by a pronounced dorsal keel ("razorback") adaptation, thought to aid stability in swift currents.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (Colorado Pikeminnow, *Ptychocheilus lucius*) — Cyprinidae; North America's largest native minnow, historically a large-bodied piscivorous predator.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — Poeciliidae; small livebearer, sexually dimorphic, adapted to warm, low-oxygen desert springs.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — Cyprinodontidae; small, robust body plan tolerant of extreme salinity and temperature fluctuations.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Cyprinidae; notable nuchal hump behind the head, an adaptation hypothesized to aid in navigating turbulent canyon river flows.
- **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*) — Cyprinidae; small-bodied minnow with characteristic spine-like anterior dorsal ray.
- **[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) — Cyprinidae; scaleless body, streamlined for turbid, fast-flowing desert stream habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species covered occupy distinct freshwater niches across two major Arizona drainage systems:

- **Salt and Verde Rivers**: Monitoring habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]], both large-river specialists historically dependent on natural flow regimes now heavily altered by damming and water diversion.
- **Little Colorado River**: Critical habitat for [[Humpback Chub]] (canyon-bound, turbulent mainstem/tributary confluence specialist) and [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (smaller tributary and headwater specialist).
- **Aravaipa Creek**: Site of a fish barrier project, presumably intended to exclude non-native predatory/competitive fishes and protect native assemblages (likely benefiting [[Gila Topminnow]] and other native cyprinids/poeciliids) within this significant desert perennial stream.
- **Desert springs and cienegas**: Habitat for [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] reintroduction efforts, reflecting their reliance on isolated, thermally stable, often spring-fed desert wetland systems vulnerable to habitat fragmentation and invasive species (e.g., mosquitofish, *Gambusia*).
- **Virgin River system** (implied by Section 4 designation): [[Woundfin]] habitat, a turbid, warm-water desert stream specialist highly sensitive to flow alteration.

## Key Findings

- **Total Section 6 (listed species) funding**: $101,000 itemized across five line items, with a possible supplemental $5,000 contingent on an October fish count.
- **Largest single allocation**: $52,000 for combined monitoring of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] populations in the Salt and Verde Rivers — indicating this as the highest programmatic priority for FY89.
- **Reintroduction programs**: $8,000 allocated jointly for [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]], reflecting active reintroduction and post-release monitoring efforts for these critically imperiled small-bodied desert fishes.
- **Canyon endemic monitoring**: $16,000 dedicated to [[Humpback Chub]] population monitoring in the Little Colorado River, a key spawning tributary for the mainstem Colorado River population in Grand Canyon.

- **Habitat protection infrastructure**: $10,000 for construction/maintenance of the [[Aravaipa Fish Barrier]], a physical conservation intervention likely designed to prevent upstream invasion by non-native fish species into native fish refugia.
- **Candidate species support (Section 4)**: A comparatively small $3,600 allocated for [[Woundfin]] monitoring, reflecting its status as a candidate/lower-priority listed species relative to the Section 6 species, or a narrower monitoring scope.
- **Programmatic structure**: The dual-section funding structure (Section 6 vs. Section 4) illustrates the tiered nature of ESA-driven conservation funding — full listing status species receiving proportionally larger, more diversified funding (monitoring, reintroduction, barriers) compared to candidate species receiving narrower monitoring-only support.
