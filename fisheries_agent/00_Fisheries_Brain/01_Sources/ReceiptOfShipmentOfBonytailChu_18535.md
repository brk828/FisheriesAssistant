# Receipt of 1995 shipment of bonytail chub and razorback suckers from Dexter

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #freshwater-fish-conservation

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal US Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (dated 8 December 1995) reporting the receipt and release of captive-reared [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (RZBK) into the lower [[Colorado River]] system. Approximately 17,887 razorback suckers and 20,000 bonytail chubs, reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], were transported and stocked across multiple National Wildlife Refuge (NWR) sites — including [[Imperial NWR]], [[Cibola NWR]], [[Bill Williams River NWR]], and [[Havasu NWR]] — as part of a native fish recovery and reintroduction program. Accompanying handwritten data sheets record individual total-length measurements used to characterize the size-class distribution of stocked fish. This effort represents a conservation stocking action tied to the broader recovery plan for these two federally listed endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fish species.

## Background & Description
Limited morphometric data are recorded, consisting primarily of total length (TL) measurements taken from subsamples of stocked individuals:

- **[[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC):**
  - Mean total length: 79.7 mm
  - Range: 53–105 mm
  - Sample size: n = 200

- **[[Razorback Sucker]] (RZBK):**
  - Mean total length: 91.9 mm
  - Range: 68–168 mm
  - Sample size: n = 200

These figures indicate the stocked cohorts were juvenile life stages, consistent with hatchery-reared fish released to bolster wild population recruitment. Additional handwritten "Stocking Data Sheets" (Pages 2–5) list raw individual length measurements (values ranging roughly from 70–170 mm across entries), though OCR degradation renders many individual data points illegible or inconsistent. Tissue samples from both species were frozen for [[Genetic Analysis]], suggesting ongoing monitoring of genetic diversity/stock identity in hatchery-supplemented populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Slow-moving backwaters, coves, and impounded pond facilities associated with the lower Colorado River and its reservoirs (e.g., [[Lake Havasu]]).
- **Release microhabitats:** Fish were introduced into a variety of managed and natural settings, including:
  - Farmer's pond facility ([[Imperial NWR]])
  - High levee pond facility ([[Cibola NWR]])
  - Office pond facility ([[Bill Williams River NWR]])
  - [[Pittsburgh Point]]
  - Helicopter Cove ([[Lake Havasu]])
  - Bulkhead Cove (adjacent to Lake Havasu)
  - North and South Twin Coves ([[Havasu NWR]]), accessed via pontoon boat
- **Ecological role:** Both species are native, large-bodied [[Colorado River Basin]] endemics historically important to the Colorado River fish assemblage. Their decline is attributed to habitat alteration (damming, flow regulation), predation by nonnative species, and loss of spawning habitat — the impetus for this hatchery-supplementation stocking program.
- **Stocking strategy:** Distribution across multiple protected coves and refuge pond systems reflects a strategy to reduce predation pressure on juveniles immediately post-release and to spread genetic/demographic risk across several semi-isolated habitats.

## Key Findings
- A large-scale coordinated stocking event occurred on **21–22 November 1995**, involving transport and release of ~37,887 total fish (17,887 RZBK + 20,000 BTC) from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center.
- Personnel from multiple agencies collaborated, including USFWS staff (Mitch Thorson, Mia LaBarbara, Al Doelker), refuge biologists (Carmen Kennedy, Angel Montoya), and BLM staff (Tom Prudeto).
- Fish were distributed across at least seven distinct release sites spanning three National Wildlife Refuges plus Lake Havasu-associated coves.
- Approximate distribution counts:
  - Imperial NWR: ~4,000 RZBK
  - Cibola NWR: ~4,000 RZBK + 8,000 BTC
  - Bill Williams River NWR: ~11,000 BTC
  - Pittsburgh Point: 300 RZBK + 200 BTC
  - Helicopter Cove: 200 RZBK + 120 BTC
  - Bulkhead Cove: 1,083 RZBK
  - South Twin Cove: ~3,545 RZBK
  - North Twin Cove: ~3,259 RZBK
- Size-class sampling (n=200 per species) established baseline length data for stocked cohorts, useful for future growth and survival monitoring.
- Genetic samples were archived for both species, supporting downstream studies on hatchery-stock genetic diversity and relatedness to wild [[Colorado River Basin]] populations.
- This record serves as a primary data point within long-term [[Endangered Species Recovery Program|Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] monitoring, documenting hatchery-to-wild transition efforts for two federally endangered species.
