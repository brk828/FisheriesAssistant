# Tilapia and Bluegill Growth and Temperature Tolerance in Concrete Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a scanned, largely illegible set of mid-twentieth-century fish culture tables (OCR-degraded throughout). Two legible tables cover [[Tilapia]] and [[Bluegill]] kept in [[Concrete Ponds]]. Table IA reports growth over the 1954 season for fish fed [[Dry Food]] at 6 percent of body weight per day. Table VIII reports deaths of both species during a [[Temperature Tolerance]] test (Test No. 2) as water temperature fell from 70.0 °F to 49.5 °F. Pages 1 to 3 and 5 are unreadable scan debris and contain no recoverable data.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Tilapia]] (genus and species not stated in the legible text) and [[Bluegill]] (scientific name not stated).
- **Culture system:** Fish were held in concrete ponds and fed stored dry food. The table title gives the feeding rate as 6 percent of body weight per day.
- **Measurements:** Growth was recorded as average weight of fish on each measurement date, with the number of fish and the days between feedings given where legible.
- **Document type:** A technical data appendix (tables only). No author, institution, location, or methods narrative is legible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Controlled, artificial concrete ponds, not natural water bodies. Results therefore reflect culture conditions rather than wild populations.
- **Feeding regime:** Stored dry food supplied at a fixed ration of 6 percent of body weight per day, a standard means of controlling [[Growth Rate]] in pond culture.
- **Thermal conditions:** The temperature test ran downward from 70.0 °F to 49.5 °F, in steps of roughly 0.5 to 2 °F. This gradual cooling tracked mortality onset for both species.
- **Comparative species:** Tilapia and bluegill were tested side by side under identical conditions, which allows direct comparison of their growth and cold tolerance.

## Key Findings

1. **Tilapia growth (Table IA):** Average weight rose from 7.6 g at the first measurement (26 June 1954) to 91.6 g at the last legible measurement (30 October 1954), across about ten dated samples.
2. **Bluegill growth (Table IA):** Average weight rose from about 3.5 g at the start to roughly 36 g by late season. The bluegill column is partly garbled, so intermediate values are not reliable.
3. **Feeding protocol:** Fish were fed dry food at 6 percent of body weight per day, and feeding days and fish counts were logged for each interval.
4. **Temperature tolerance (Table VIII):** Mortality of both tilapia and bluegill was recorded as water temperature fell from 70.0 °F to 49.5 °F over successive days of exposure. Exact death counts per day are not legible enough to report, so no lethal threshold can be stated with confidence.
5. **Data quality:** Only Table IA and Table VIII are partially readable. Most of the document is unrecoverable scan noise, and no statistics (means, variances, sample sizes) beyond the raw tabulated values can be verified.
