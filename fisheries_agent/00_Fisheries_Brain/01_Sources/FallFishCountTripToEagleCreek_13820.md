# Eagle Creek Fall Fish Count 1990

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This intra-office memo from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] reports a fall 1990 fish survey of [[Eagle Creek]], sampled above and below the [[Phelps Dodge Diversion Dam]]. Comparison with July 1987 data shows that the native fish population above the dam has declined sharply. Native fish made up 91.0 percent of the fauna in 1987 but only 7.7 percent in 1990. Several native species were absent in 1990, and non-native species have moved above the dam. The memo concludes that the decline is real despite differences in sampling methods. It recommends obtaining results from a prior survey by Paul Marsh.

## Background & Description

- **Author and recipient:** Matt Brown, Assistant Native Fish Biologist, to Francisco Abarca, Native Fish Biologist.
- **Survey dates:** October 27 to 29, 1990. The comparison dataset covers 13 to 18 July 1987.
- **Study area:** Reach above the Phelps Dodge diversion dam and the main pool and pools below it.
- **Survey crew:** Caryl Williams and four others, with the author.
- **Methods used:**
  - October 28: [[Seine Sampling]] and a [[Backpack Shocker]] above the diversion dam, plus a [[Trammel Net]] set in the main pool below the dam.
  - October 29: the trammel net was cleared and pools below the dam were seined.
- **Reference:** Marsh et al. (in press), *Fishes of Eagle Creek, Arizona, with Records for Threatened Spikedace and Loach Minnow (Cyprinidae)*, Journal of the Arizona-Nevada Academy of Science.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fish decline:** The native fraction above the diversion dam fell from 91.0 percent (1987) to 7.7 percent (1990). The author says the decline is "obvious" even after allowing for sampling differences.
- **Absent native taxa:** [[Agosia]], [[Meda]] (spikedace), and [[Rhinichthys]] were not collected in 1990, although the habitat sampled was appropriate for them. Marsh et al. (1987 data) recorded them at 8.7, 17.1, and 17.4 percent, respectively, above the diversion.
- **Non-native expansion:** [[Common Carp]] and [[Mosquitofish]] have moved above the dam since 1987.
- **Dominant species:** [[Red Shiner]] was the most common fish sampled in 1990. Before this survey, only small numbers had been found above the diversion dam.
- **Prior trend:** Paul Marsh sampled the same site in the previous year and reported a similar trend. The memo suggests requesting his results.
- **Threatened species:** The cited Marsh et al. paper includes records for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], both listed as threatened.

## Key Findings

1. **Marked native decline above the dam:** Native fish fell from 91.0 percent of the fauna (1987) to 7.7 percent (1990).
2. **Native taxa not collected in 1990:** Agosia, Meda, and Rhinichthys were absent despite suitable habitat. Their 1987 frequencies were 8.7, 17.1, and 17.4 percent.
3. **Invasion above the barrier:** Carp and Mosquitofish have colonized the reach above the diversion dam since 1987.
4. **Red Shiner dominance:** Red Shiner was the most abundant species in 1990, a marked change from its previously minor presence above the dam.
5. **Consistent regional trend:** A prior survey by Paul Marsh showed a similar pattern, which supports the conclusion that the decline is real.
6. **Recommended action:** Obtain Paul Marsh's survey results to confirm the trend and inform management.
