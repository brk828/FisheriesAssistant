# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants; Revocation of Critical Habitat for the Loach Minnow and Spikedace (1998)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #ConservationLaw #EndangeredSpeciesAct

## Executive Summary
This Federal Register final rule (63 FR 14378, March 25, 1998) documents the **revocation of designated critical habitat** for two southwestern United States freshwater cyprinid fishes — the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Rhinichthys cobitis*) and [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) — along with the non-fish [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (*Strix occidentalis lucida*). The action was not based on new biological or ecological findings regarding the species themselves, but rather resulted from federal court rulings that the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) had failed to comply with the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) procedural requirements when originally designating critical habitat. This document is primarily a legal/regulatory record and contains minimal direct morphological or ecological data on the fish species, but it is significant for tracking the regulatory and conservation history of these two imperiled minnows.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although explicit ecological/habitat descriptions are absent from this legal text, the following context on habitat designation history is documented:

- Both species had **critical habitat designated on March 8, 1994** (59 FR 10898 for loach minnow; 59 FR 10906 for spikedace), indicating federally recognized essential habitat within river/stream systems of the southwestern U.S. (Arizona and New Mexico), consistent with known [[Gila River Basin]] and [[San Francisco River]] drainage habitats for these taxa in broader literature.
- Habitat designations spanned jurisdictions across both the **Tenth Circuit** (New Mexico) and **Ninth Circuit** (Arizona), implying the species' ranges cross state and circuit court boundaries within the arid Southwest.
- The rule references co-listing alongside the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], a riparian/forest-associated raptor, suggesting overlapping riparian ecosystem management concerns, though no direct interspecies ecological relationship is elaborated in the text.
- No data on water chemistry, flow regime, substrate preference, prey, or predator relationships is included in this document.

## Key Findings
- **Critical habitat revoked**: Effective March 25, 1998, the Service removed critical habitat designations for the [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] from 50 CFR 17.11(h) (table entries revised to "NA") and removed associated maps/text from 50 CFR 17.95(b) (owl) and 17.95(e) (minnow and spikedace).
- **Legal basis, not biological basis**: Revocation stemmed from litigation — *Catron County Board of Commissioners, New Mexico v. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service* (D.N.M. 1994), affirmed by the Tenth Circuit (75 F.3d 1429, 1996) — which found the Service failed to analyze critical habitat designation effects under NEPA.
- **Cross-jurisdictional effect**: Although the Ninth Circuit (Arizona) does not require NEPA analysis for critical habitat designations, the U.S. District Court for Arizona (*Southwest Center for Biological Diversity v. Rogers*, 1996) extended the New Mexico set-aside to apply within Arizona as well, effectively nullifying critical habitat across the full range of both fish species.
- **Species retained federal listing status**: This action revoked *critical habitat* only — it did **not** delist the [[Loach Minnow]] or [[Spikedace]] from the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife; their underlying threatened/endangered status under the [[Endangered Species Act]] remained unaffected.
- **Regulatory consequence**: Following revocation, federal agencies were no longer required to consider critical habitat for these species during Section 7 consultations, though species-level protections (e.g., jeopardy standard) persisted.
- **Immediate effective date**: The Service waived the standard 30-day delay under 5 U.S.C. 553(d), citing "good cause" due to the pre-existing judicial set-aside of the habitat designations.
- **Historical marker**: This 1998 revocation represents a procedural setback in Southwestern native fish conservation, later addressed by subsequent re-designation efforts for critical habitat for both the [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] in later federal rules.
