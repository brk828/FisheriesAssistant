# Relict Aquatic Fauna of the Sahara and Late Quaternary Glacial Chronology of Mount Kenya

#ichthyology #marine-biology #odonata

## Executive Summary

This source is a two-part document. The main paper (Dumont) argues that the relict distributions of aquatic animals (dragonflies, amphibians, reptiles and fish) in the [[Sahara Desert]] and [[Sahel]] can serve as a tool for reconstructing [[Late Pleistocene]] climate change. Because aquatic animals can disperse only through water, their relict ranges record past wet phases and droughts. A shorter second paper (Mahaney) describes the glacial and periglacial deposit sequence and radiocarbon-based chronology of [[Mount Kenya]], East Africa.

## Background & Description

**Dumont (Sahara and Sahel fauna).** The author, of the Zoological Institute, University of Gent, reports eleven expeditions over six years that explored more than 600 aquatic sites across the Sahara and Sahel. The paper builds on earlier work by Gauthier (1938), whose synthesis focused on whether Saharan biota were Mediterranean or Afrotropical in character. Dumont notes that the Saharan crustacean plankton and benthos show little endemism because climates changed too quickly for much speciation. Groundwater (phreatic) faunas are the exception, with narrow endemism typical of groundwater communities worldwide.

The paper treats three groups:
- **Odonata:** about 38 species occur within or along the margins of the Sahara. Zygoptera (damselflies) are small, weak flyers tied to permanent water. Anisoptera (dragonflies) are stronger flyers, and only five species are migratory. [[Hemianax Ephippiger]] makes mass migrations across the desert, but it is judged useless as a palaeoecological indicator.
- **Amphibia:** no Urodela (salamanders) occur south of the Sahara. Ten Anura are recorded, several of which bury into mud or sand to survive drought.
- **Reptilia and pisces:** the [[Nile Crocodile]] is the only aquatic reptile with Saharan relicts. Eighteen fish species and one possible subspecies are recorded from the Sahara.

**Mahaney (Mount Kenya).** The author, of York University, Ontario, describes a five-fold sequence of tills and periglacial deposits on Mount Kenya. Glaciers radiating from a central ice cap descended to 2900 m. Dating relies on radiocarbon ages where organic material is available and on relative methods such as rock weathering, loess thickness, lichen cover and soil properties.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Dispersal and climate.** Dumont frames dispersal ability as the key link between species and climate history. Zygoptera are tied to permanent water, so their Saharan relicts imply continuous water since at least the latest pluvial. Some Libellulidae (*Orthetrum chrysostigma*, *Trithemis arteriosa*) survive as larvae estivating in damp sand, which broadens their dispersal. Catfish (*Clarias*) breathe air and can migrate over land, and some cichlids apparently jump between rock pools. Dumont concludes that no fish can survive a year-long severe drought.

**Sea-level and glacial context.** During the last glacial stadial (about 20,000 BP), eustatic sea level was about 130 m lower. This narrowed the gap between Tunisia, Sardinia and Sicily and allowed dragonfly exchange. Cold, arid conditions in the Mediterranean and Africa, however, excluded African fish and amphibians from the islands. The Atlas glaciation blocked the Gibraltar passage, which prevented African species from reaching the Iberian Peninsula. Deglaciation after 18,000 BP reopened the Iberian route, and sea level exceeded present levels by about 3 m around 5,000 BP (the Nouakchott transgression).

**Mount Kenya.** Valley glaciers reached 2900 m during the Quaternary. Teleki till, deposited before the last glacial maximum, lies in lower valleys at 2900 m and carries well-developed soils. Liki tills mantle valleys above 3200 m and split into early and late stades. Neoglacial till is considered late Holocene (<1000 BP) on the basis of weak soil development. Dense forest below 3200 m complicates valley-to-valley correlation.

## Key Findings

**Dumont: relict distributions and migration waves**

- Two Odonata groups in the Mediterranean Basin show a mutually exclusive pattern. Species with relicts on the central Mediterranean islands but not on the Iberian Peninsula are linked to a migration wave around 20,000 BP. Species with relicts on the Iberian Peninsula but not the islands are linked to a larger wave between 12,000 and 8,000 BP, which also included fish, amphibians and the Nile crocodile.
- Relictisation in the central Sahara occurred by about 7,000 BP. In the southern Sahara and central Sahel, a severe drought around 5,000–4,000 BP eliminated all relicts.
- [[Tibesti Mountains]] and [[Ennedi Plateau]] (and the Adrar of Mauritania) were recolonised through transgressions of [[Lake Chad]] and the [[Senegal River]]. The [[Air Mountains]] and Adrar des Iforhas retain a very deficient fauna, showing that no surface-water link with the Sudan was re-established.
- Lake Chad's most recent high level was about 5,400 BP, which Dumont treats as the maximum isolation time for Tibesti populations. He argues that about 5,000 years is too short for endemism to develop reliably.

**Dumont: speciation and endemism**

- Only one dragonfly speciation event is documented: [[Ischnura Saharensis]] evolved allopatrically from *I. genei*, and the two remain very close. Dumont proposes a minimum of roughly 20,000 years for speciation, but this is a provisional estimate.
- The Tibesti "endemic" fish are reinterpreted. *Tilapia borkuana* is probably a melanic race of *Sarotherodon galilaeus*, and *Labeo tibestii* may be a synonym of *L. annectens* from the Niger. Tibesti-Ennedi fish therefore reduce to an extract of the Nile–Chad–Niger fauna.
- Greenwood (1976) reports that at least 74 of the Nile's 115 fish species are shared with the Niger, which points to recent connections among the three basins.

**Dumont: fish, amphibians and reptiles**

- The *Barbus* of the Maghreb (Atlas and Saoura) form a group distinct from roughly 250 Afrotropical *Barbus* species. Taxonomic treatments vary: Pellegrin (1921, 1939) recognised 13 species and four varieties, Estève (1947) reduced them to two, and Almaca (1970) recognised seven.
- *Barbus pobeguini* and *B. macrops* are equatorial species with numerous populations in the Adrar, Ennedi, Tibesti and Tassili-n-Ajjer. *B. pobeguini* showed mass mortality in the Hamdoun guelta at 30 °C.
- Anura with drought-survival adaptations include *Bufo regularis*, *B. pentoni* and *Tomopterna cryptotis*. Their traits include nocturnal activity, eggs that hatch within about a day, larval development of about ten days, and tolerance of tadpoles to temperatures up to 30 °C and high salinities.
- *Bufo mauretanicus* is widespread but shows no relicts in central or eastern Sahara. It may be a west Mediterranean endemic.
- The Nile crocodile survived in the Lherir guelta until 1920. Skeletons from the Oum Aghouaba lakes north of Atar are radiocarbon dated to no more than 2,000 BP. Today relict populations persist in Tagant, Tibesti and Ennedi.
- The Air and Adrar des Iforhas lack fish and sand-burrowing amphibians entirely. Dumont interprets this as the result of a hyperarid spell that eliminated all species and was not followed by a humid enough period to permit recolonisation.
- A neolithic humid period (about 6,000–3,000 BP) is restricted to the northern and central Sahara, according to Dumont. Eolian dune reactivation at 5,000–4,000 BP in the Chad area, northwestern Nigeria and Western Sudan supports the drought interpretation.

**Mahaney: Mount Kenya glacial chronology**

- Valley glaciers from a central ice cap descended to 2900 m during the Quaternary.
- A five-fold sequence of tills and associated periglacial deposits occurs in most drainages. The sequence is named from type localities on the western and northern flanks.
- The Hohnel Diamicton is of glacial origin and of probable early to mid-Quaternary age.
- Teleki till predates the last glacial maximum (Würm/Wisconsinan), carries well-developed soils, and occurs in lower valleys at 2900 m. At the type locality (site TV23), a surface soil overlies a buried paleosol in older drift that may correlate with the Hohnel Diamicton.
- Liki tills, presumed coeval with the last glacial maximum, mantle valleys above 3200 m. Liki-II deposits above 3900 m are dated at 12,590 + 300 BP.
- Neoglacial till is considered late Holocene (<1000 BP) on the basis of relative soil development and weathering.
