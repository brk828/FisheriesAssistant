# Station Profile of the Pinetop Fish Health Center

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-health #hatchery-management #aquaculture

## Executive Summary

The **[[Pinetop Fish Health Center]]** is a U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service diagnostic and regulatory laboratory established in 1974 under the authority of the Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956 (P.L. 1024). Based in Pinetop, Arizona, with laboratory space at the **[[Alchesay National Fish Hatchery]]** in Whiteriver, Arizona, the center provides hatchery disease certification, fish health services, and water effluent analysis across a multi-state service area spanning Arizona, New Mexico, Texas, and Oklahoma. The station supports compliance with the **[[Fish Health Policy]]** of the **[[Colorado River Wildlife Council]]** and the Service's broader aquaculture policy, serving federal, state, and commercial aquaculture facilities. As of FY1991, the station operated with modest staffing (1.02 FTE) and an operations budget of approximately $128,300, with planned staffing increases (to 2.52 FTE) and budget growth through FY1993.

## Key Findings

- **Founding & Authority**: Established in 1974 under the Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956 (P.L. 1024); led by Ronald D. Major (Region 2).
- **Multi-state service area**: Provides fish health services and disease certification across four states (AZ, NM, TX, OK), covering 9 National Fish Hatcheries, 6 State Fish Hatcheries, and 3 commercial aquaculture facilities.
- **Facility infrastructure**: Laboratory and office operations are housed in Quarters #4 and #5 at the [[Alchesay National Fish Hatchery]], rather than a dedicated standalone facility.
- **Staffing growth**: FTE levels grew from 1.0 (1989–1990) to 1.02 (1991), with planned increases to 2.0 (1992) and 2.52 (1993), indicating an expanding operational mandate.
- **Budget trends**: Operations funding nearly doubled between 1990 ($62.1K) and 1991 ($128.3K), with continued increases projected through 1993 ($120.0K est.), alongside a one-time maintenance allocation of $15.0K.
- **Service model**: Fish health services extended to Oklahoma and Texas facilities on an **as-needed basis**, reflecting a tiered service priority favoring core Arizona/New Mexico federal hatcheries.
- **Policy compliance role**: Functions as the regional compliance mechanism for the [[Colorado River Wildlife Council]] Fish Health Policy, underscoring the center's regulatory significance beyond routine diagnostics.
