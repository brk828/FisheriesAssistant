## Executive Summary

This paper, presented at the [[Colorado River Fishes Symposium]] (July 16-19, 1985, [[Western Division American Fisheries Society]]), addresses the taxonomic identification of larval and early-juvenile native fishes of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. It was prepared by the [[Colorado State University Larval Fish Laboratory]] and is based on a research project funded under a federal aid grant. The scan is heavily degraded, so most species names, sample sizes and numerical values cannot be reliably recovered. The recoverable content centres on a diagnostic framework of morphometric measures, meristic counts and identification keys for distinguishing larval fishes.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** To provide a basis for identifying larval and early-juvenile native fishes of the upper Colorado River, a stage at which species are difficult to separate.
- **Diagnostic toolkit:** Identification rests on [[Morphometrics]] (body and head measurements), [[Meristic Counts]] including [[Fin Rays]] (principal dorsal and anal fin rays) and total fin-ray counts, and [[Myomeres]] (body segment counts). Figure 1 and its continuation list these diagnostic measures and counts.
- **Reference material:** The text notes that earlier larval-fish literature, including work from 1981, was used and is available in a limited number of copies from the laboratory.
- **Format:** The document is a symposium or working paper with figures, a measurement schematic and a dense tabular or illustrative layer. Much of the tabular content is unreadable in the scan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The study area is the upper Colorado River Basin, a large inland drainage in the western United States that supports a group of native fishes of conservation interest.
- Larval and early-juvenile stages are the focus because they are often the least documented life stages. Accurate identification at these stages is needed for monitoring recruitment, mapping spawning and nursery habitat, and assessing the effects of river regulation and habitat change.
- The document contains no readable water chemistry, habitat measurements or community data.

- The paper presents taxonomic identification procedures for larval and early-juvenile native fishes of the upper Colorado River Basin.
