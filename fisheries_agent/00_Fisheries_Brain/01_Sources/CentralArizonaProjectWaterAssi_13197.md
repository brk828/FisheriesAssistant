# Biological Opinion: Central Arizona Project Water Assignment to City of Scottsdale (Cottonwood Water Works and Camp Verde Water System)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) biological opinion, dated March 30, 1998, evaluates a Bureau of Reclamation action to approve the reassignment of [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water allocations held by Cottonwood Water Works, Inc. (CWW) and Camp Verde Water System, Inc. (CVWS) to the City of Scottsdale. The water is valued at $3,555,200, which will fund alternative, primarily groundwater, supplies in the Verde Valley. The Service concludes that the action is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Arizona Cliffrose]] or [[Bald Eagle]], and is not likely to adversely modify critical habitat for the razorback sucker or flycatcher. The opinion nonetheless finds moderately adverse effects, driven mainly by depletion of [[Verde River]] base flows and by indirect growth-related development.

## Background & Description

**Proposed action.** Reclamation would approve assignment of CAP allocations (1,789 acre-feet per year to CWW; 1,443 af/yr to CVWS, granted in 1983) to Scottsdale, which already holds 26,576 af/yr. Proceeds go into two Water Trust Funds under [[Arizona Department of Water Resources]] oversight. Trust monies may fund deep wells in the [[Verde Formation]], surface water rights or recharge, but not new surface diversions. Each company expects three wells; the six sites should disturb under 5 acres of upland.

**Consultation history.** Informal consultation began in 1992. The Service did not concur with Reclamation's "may affect, not likely to adversely affect" finding in June 1997. Formal consultation was initiated on November 24, 1997 and received November 25, 1997. Cooperative agreements with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) and the Arizona Department of Agriculture (ADA) were finalized in January 1998.

**Species status.**
- **Razorback sucker** (endangered; critical habitat designated March 21, 1994): reached more than two feet in length and may live about 50 years. Extirpated from the Verde River (last record Peck's Lake, 1954) and reintroduced from 1981 with Lake Mohave hatchery stock. The Verde population is the most successful of the three reintroduced Gila basin populations.
- **Bald eagle** (listed endangered 1967, reclassified threatened July 12, 1995; no critical habitat): Arizona had 36 known breeding areas in 1996, 30 occupied; 226 eaglets fledged in Arizona from 1970 to 1990. The Verde River holds 12 known nests, three of them within the action area.
- **Southwestern willow flycatcher** (endangered; critical habitat published July 18, 1997): a riparian obligate insectivore. Historically widespread, it is now fragmented. The rangewide estimate is about 454 territories, which the Service thinks is more likely 300 to 500, with many unmated birds.
- **Arizona cliffrose** (endangered May 29, 1984; no critical habitat): a narrow-range shrub. The Verde Valley population is the largest and the only one with known seedling recruitment.

**Legal framework.** Under section 7 of the Endangered Species Act, the Service treated growth facilitated by the CAP funds as interdependent and interrelated to the federal action, rejecting Reclamation's "but for" argument. The rationale is that CAP's purpose explicitly includes municipal and industrial growth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Verde River hydrology.** About 30% of the watershed lies above the beginning of perennial flow, and the river's perennial flow originates from springs near Granite Creek. More than half of growing-season flow is diverted for irrigation, and some reaches lose all surface flow. Verde Valley population was 9,145 (Cottonwood and Clarkdale) and 7,465 (Camp Verde) in 1995, projected to reach 16,470 and 11,483 by 2010.

**Groundwater connection.** Approximately 1,000 af/yr moves from the Verde Formation to the Quaternary alluvium, and about 20 af/yr moves the other way. Base flow is maximal in January and February and minimal in July and August. Municipal pumping in the Verde Valley totals about 10,000 af (1986 estimate) from over 1,200 wells.

**Modeled depletion.** Reclamation estimated maximum project depletion at 3% of annual base flow (4% above the Fossil Creek inflow). With a 65% return rate, net depletion is 1%, or 1.5% at the lower valley end. At the low-flow gage the historic low is 40 cfs, and a 1.56 cfs depletion equals 4%. Below Camp Verde, a discontinued gage recorded a historic low of 13 cfs, so the same depletion would equal 12%.

**Nonnative fish.** Many nonnative species are established in the Verde River, including catfishes, bass, sunfish and red shiner. Nonnatives predominate downstream of Sycamore Creek, and the long-term trend favors nonnatives.

**Flycatcher reproduction.** Cowbird parasitism and nest predation are the main limits. Predation rates run 30 to 50%, and the chance of a parasitized nest fledging its own young is under 5%. On the South Fork Kern River, cowbird trapping raised nest success from 26% to 48%.

## Key Findings

- **Jeopardy determination:** No jeopardy to razorback sucker, flycatcher, cliffrose or bald eagle, and no adverse modification of razorback sucker or flycatcher critical habitat.
- **Razorback sucker:** Flow depletions during low-flow periods (late spring and early summer) are judged significant, affecting spawning, larval habitat and food production. The Verde Valley already has substantially depleted flows, so further depletion matters more here than downstream.
- **Bald eagle:** Effects are adverse but not major, operating through changes in the fish prey base. Potential powerline collisions are noted.
- **Flycatcher:** Take is anticipated, primarily long-term (after about 10 years), through habitat loss, nest parasitism and fragmentation. The Camp Verde site held 20 flycatchers (10 territories) in 1997, with 58% nest success.
- **Cliffrose:** Expected to be unaffected by groundwater or surface flow depletion, but at risk from infrastructure and development. Cooperative agreements with ADA restrict Trust-funded facilities within the recovery area.
- **Cumulative effects:** Verde Valley growth is projected at roughly 143% to 158% (1994 to 2040). The CAP increment is judged small, though cumulative impacts may be severe in the long term.
- **Incidental take:** Anticipated for razorback sucker (indirect, gradual) and flycatcher. Take is considered exceeded if the project is not implemented as planned. No take is authorized for bald eagle, and no take statement applies to cliffrose.
- **Reasonable and prudent measures (five):** minimize habitat loss; document take; keep records; protect and enhance flycatcher habitat on the Verde River; promote public education and voluntary water conservation.
- **Terms and conditions:** Include deep-well-only pumping, placing facilities outside riparian zones, annual reports on acquired surface rights, and a set-aside of about $20,000 (one percent of the Trust Fund) for public education.
- **Conservation recommendations (discretionary):** Re-establish a stream gage near Camp Verde (USGS 09505550) and conduct additional flycatcher surveys.
- **Reinitiation triggers:** Exceeded take, new information, project modification, or a new listing.
