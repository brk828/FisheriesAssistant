# Natural Hybrids Between Aphanius Species in Egypt and the Zoogeography of Arabian Herpetofauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #herpetology #zoogeography

## Executive Summary

This proceedings excerpt contains two contributions from the 1985 Mainz Symposium on the Fauna and Zoogeography of the Middle East. The first, by [[W. Villwock]], reports that the [[Bardawil Lagoon]] killifish population in North Sinai, Egypt, and related populations near [[Al-Qanatir]] west of the [[Suez Canal]] contain natural F1 hybrids between [[Aphanius Dispar Dispar]] and [[Aphanius Fasciatus]]. The second, by E.N. Arnold, is a zoogeographic overview of the reptiles and amphibians of Arabia, which is largely endemic and was shaped by arid conditions and past pluvial phases. The first paper is the main research contribution; the second is an introductory section that is cut off in the excerpt.

## Background & Description

**Aphanius (Cyprinodontidae)** is an Old World genus of small killifish. Villwock notes that most authors regard *A. dispar dispar* as closely related to *A. fasciatus*. The two species have largely separate distributions:

- ***Aphanius dispar dispar*** occurs mainly along the shores of the [[Arabian Peninsula]], Iran, and Pakistan, reaching as far east as India. Inland populations of marine origin are an exception. Villwock distinguishes "Arabian" and "Mediterranean" populations, and also recognizes *A. dispar richardsoni* from the [[Dead Sea]].
- ***Aphanius fasciatus*** is mainly found in ancient and recent Mediterranean lagoons, except in Morocco and Spain. There a third species, ***A. iberus***, lives in similar waters.

Earlier reports of *A. fasciatus* on the eastern Mediterranean coast (Palestine/Israel) were not confirmed by Villwock, who found only disjunct *A. dispar dispar* populations in lagoons and salines, including at Atlit.

**Herpetofauna of Arabia.** Arnold's section introduces Arabia as nearly island-like, bounded by sea on three sides and by geological and climatic barriers to the north, including the [[Gulf of Aqaba]] and [[Wadi Araba]] fault system. The paper refers to approximately 128 terrestrial reptile species and about 7 amphibian species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Egyptian lagoons and the [[Suez Canal]].** The [[Bardawil Lagoon]] is an extended, shallow, warm-water lagoon in North Sinai that supports a large *Aphanius* population. Earlier authors attributed the presence of *A. dispar dispar* there to invasion from the [[Red Sea]] through the Suez Canal, which opened in 1869. [[Tortonese]] (1947) proposed that hybridization occurred in the canal and its lakes ([[Bitter Lakes]] and [[Lake Timsah]]), but Villwock's sampling there found only *A. dispar dispar*.

**[[Wahat Siwa]].** In the Western Desert, both species occur together in well-separated habitats. Here no natural hybrids were found. Villwock attributes this to microgeographic separation. The Siwa *A. dispar dispar* population may be a relict of a marine invasion during the last interglacial period.

**Biogeographic barriers and change.** Villwock proposes that *A. fasciatus* may have moved east of its usual North African range after the [[Nile River]] freshwater barrier broke down. Contributing factors were the [[Aswan Dam]] (1970) and the closure of the Nile Delta outflows near Damietta (Dumyat) and Idfina within recent years. If so, other littoral marine species might also be moving across the former Nile outflow barrier.

**Arabian zoogeography.** Arnold describes Arabia's herpetofauna as highly endemic (about 55% of species largely or entirely confined to the area). The arid peninsula is dominated by [[Saharo-Sindian Fauna]] with closer ties to the [[Sahara Desert]] than to arid regions east of Arabia. Southern areas of endemism include the southwestern coast (affinities with the [[Horn of Africa]]), the southwest and southern highlands (northeast African and Mediterranean links), the arid southern mountains (with representatives in the Giddat al-Harasis area), and the north Oman mountains (linked to forms in the less arid parts of Iran). Mesic forms along the peninsula's periphery and oasis relicts are interpreted as remnants of [[Quaternary Pluvial Phases]].

## Key Findings

**Villwock (Aphanius hybrids):**

1. **Natural hybrids confirmed in Egypt.** The [[Bardawil Lagoon]] population consists of *A. dispar dispar*, *A. fasciatus*, and [[F1 Hybrids]] between them. The same pattern was found in land-locked waters near [[Al-Qanatir]].
2. **Sample composition.** The two parental species occurred at ratios of about 2:1 in 1983 to about 6:1 in 1984 (*A. dispar dispar* to *A. fasciatus*). F1 hybrids made up about one third of all specimens caught.
3. **Supporting evidence.** Identification was based on external morphology, [[Gonad Histology]], and partial enzyme patterns. Hybrid males had markedly smaller testes than parental males, more tubules with spermatogonia, fewer with regular meiosis, and very few spermatid-filled tubules. No ripe sperm were observed. Some hybrids showed female-like secondary sex characters with mixed "intersexual" gonads. The author suggests that F1 hybrid females have fertile-looking gonads but their eggs fail to develop. These findings match earlier results from crossbred progenies (Villwock 1964).
4. **Genetic interpretation.** The author proposes that the two parental species have at least partly different [[Sex-Determining Factors]] systems.
5. **Patterns of sympatry.** Where the species share the same niches, as in lagoons around the Suez Canal, hybridization is expected because no known isolating mating mechanisms exist. Where they are separated, as at Wahat Siwa, hybrids are absent.
6. **Open questions.** The author is unable to determine whether *A. fasciatus* was already present when *A. dispar dispar* arrived, or whether *A. dispar dispar* is an ancient relict. The Siwa and southeastern Mediterranean populations still need study.
7. **Genetic identity of *A. dispar dispar*.** Crossbreeding and enzyme results show high genetic identity among the *A. dispar dispar* populations studied, with only minor variation.

**Arnold (Arabian reptiles and amphibians):**

1. Arabia has about 128 terrestrial reptile and about 7 amphibian species.
2. Endemism is high, with about 55% of species largely or entirely confined to the area.
3. There are sharp faunal breaks with the Horn of Africa, the Sahara, and the more mesic regions to the north. Substantial past interchange with these areas is also inferred.
4. The main internal division is between the arid peninsula (Saharo-Sindian taxa) and smaller, more mesic southern centers of endemism.
