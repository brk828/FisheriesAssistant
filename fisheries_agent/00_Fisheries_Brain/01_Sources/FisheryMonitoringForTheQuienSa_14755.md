# Fishery Monitoring for the Quien Sabe Prescribed Burn (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1992 report, prepared by [[Jerome A. Stefferud]] for the [[Tonto National Forest]], documents the first year of fishery and habitat monitoring tied to the [[Quien Sabe Prescribed Burn]] on [[Cave Creek]] and [[Seven Springs Wash]], Arizona. Monitoring was a term of the [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (October 3, 1991), which found the burn unlikely to jeopardize the [[Gila Topminnow]] population but anticipated incidental take. The study combined an extensive stream survey (March to May 1992), monthly sampling at three fixed sites, and macroinvertebrate sampling.

Key results: [[Longfin Dace]] dominated the fish community at the lower site (80% or more), [[Gila Topminnow]] numbers fluctuated sharply with floods and season, and a large August flood reset stream conditions. Macroinvertebrate communities (1986 and 1991 samples) indicated poor ecosystem condition with low diversity. Monitoring was to continue monthly through June 1993, then quarterly.

## Background & Description

**Regulatory context.** Under Section 9 of the [[Endangered Species Act]], the Forest Service required exemption from take prohibitions. The Biological Opinion set six terms and conditions, one requiring two years of monitoring of Gila topminnow abundance and distribution, nonnative fish, habitat condition, channel morphology, and sedimentation. Photo points were required in the burn area. Monitoring results were due to the USFWS within 60 days of each monitoring segment (before the burn, immediately after, and one and two years after).

**Study area.** Cave Creek and Seven Springs Wash lie in the Upper Sonoran life zone about 30 miles north of Phoenix. Cave Creek's reach is about 10.5 miles long, with elevation falling from 3,350 ft to 2,340 ft and an average gradient of 96 ft per mile (1.8%). Human influences include campgrounds, FS and public roads, extensive [[Livestock Grazing]], and an irrigation canal diverting about 1.8 cfs from the Seven Springs headspring. Seven Springs Wash was [[Channelization|channelized]] with rock masonry walls 6 to 8 ft high in the 1930s.

**Fish history.** Two native minnows, [[Gila Chub]] and [[Speckled Dace]], had not been collected since 1971. [[Longfin Dace]] is abundant. Nonnative species include [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[Green Sunfish]]. [[Western Mosquitofish]] was reported in 1991 but not in later surveys. [[Channel Catfish]] was listed from Seven Springs Wash by Silvey et al. (1984).

**Transplant history.** Gila topminnow was stocked repeatedly from the mid-1960s ([[Gila Topminnow Transplants]]):

| Date | Site | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1964-65 | Cave Creek | Extirpated (1965-66) |
| 22 Jul 1975 | Seven Springs Wash | Extirpated (1978) |
| 29 Feb 1980 | Seven Springs Wash | Established |
| Pre-1989 | Cave Creek | Established |

Floods were blamed for several extirpations. Peak flows at the Cave Creek gage reached 6,000 cfs in 1967 and 1968, and 7,500 and 6,900 cfs in 1978. A 1970-71 [[Stream Renovation]] by AGFD, Arizona State University, and the FS aimed to remove [[Longfin Dace]] before stocking [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]. It failed to remove longfin dace, was unsuccessful for the target species, and evidently extirpated Gila chub and speckled dace.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology.** Cave Creek is ephemeral near the Cave Creek Group Campground and becomes perennial near the CCC Recreation Site, probably from subsurface flow of three tributaries. Annual peak discharges ranged from 0 to 12,400 cfs (1958-1989). Estimated instantaneous peaks were 1,740 cfs for a 2-year event and about 20,000 cfs for a 100-year event. Perennial water in Seven Springs Wash extends about 1.3 miles to the headspring.

**Channel reaches.** The extensive survey described reaches by slope, substrate, and [[Rosgen Stream Classification]] categories (C3, B2, B1, C1):

- **Seven Springs Campground:** shallow cobble/gravel runs in channelized channel.
- **CCC Recreation Site to Bronco Creek:** about 2% slope, step-runs, sycamore riparian cover.
- **Bronco Creek to Big Maggie May:** under 1% slope, wide shallow runs and glides; the most suitable topminnow habitat noted, though nearly 100% of unprotected banks are trampled by cattle.
- **Big Maggie May to FR 468:** steep V-shaped canyon, plunge pools, about 3.2% slope.
- **FR 468 to Grays Gulch:** about 2.0% slope, pool:riffle ratio 1:1, few deep rootwad pools.
- **Grays Gulch to Mattys Fork:** about 1.6% slope, floodplain terrace, gravel/sand runs.
- **Mattys Fork to the gap fence:** confined bedrock walls, 1.8% slope, pools to 6 ft that likely persist as refuges.
- **Gap fence to mid-Section 28:** about 1.5% slope, wider and shallower, cattle-impacted banks.
- **South to FS boundary:** steep, highly confined valley.

Mattys Fork appears perennial and needs fish and habitat surveys.

**Study sites.** Three sites were chosen for topminnow potential and access, not as stream representatives:

- **Lower fish site:** complex riffles, step-runs, plunge pools, glides; about 50% riffle embeddedness; canopy under 30%.
- **Middle fish site:** two shallow gravel glides, moderate embeddedness, hourly temperature logger installed.
- **Upper fish site:** deep and shallow glides with sand/gravel, near-complete sycamore canopy, rootwad pools.

## Key Findings

**Fish community composition**
- At the **lower site**, longfin dace made up 80% or more of the community, with fathead minnow the remainder. Gila topminnow appeared only in August and November, likely displaced downstream by floods.
- At the **middle site**, Gila topminnow was minor until October-November, then declined in December. Fathead minnow rose through July and was then decimated by flooding.
- At the **upper site**, Gila topminnow rose through July, expanded rapidly in August-October, and declined by December. Neither longfin dace nor fathead minnow was abundant.
- Gila topminnow were uncommon below Big Maggie May but often numerous upstream to the FR 24B crossing. Green sunfish occurred from Skunk Tank Canyon to the FS boundary. Longfin dace were common from CCC Recreation Site to the boundary.

**Sampling effort (illustrative counts)**
- Upper site, June: 27 Gila topminnow in 24 seine hauls.
- Upper site, August: 238 Gila topminnow in eight seine hauls.
- Counts are not absolute abundances because effort varied by site and date.

**Methods**
- Fish were collected by electrofishing (Smith-Root Model 12 backpack unit) and seining (4 ft by 10 ft, 1/8 in mesh) at the lower site; seining only at middle and upper sites. Fish were visually observed in the extensive survey with no capture.
- Conductivity, pH, and air and water temperature were measured monthly. Crews of three to four people worked the sites. Personnel effort totaled days from FS, USFWS, AGFD, and volunteers, led by Stefferud (about 15 days).

**Habitat and water quality**
- Water temperatures ranged from 7 to 31°C (April to December), with summer diurnal ranges of 10°C or more ([[Water Temperature]]).
- Habitat volume declined from April to August, then rose after flood recharges in October-December.
- A large flood on August 23, originating in Seven Springs Wash, was up to 5 ft above base flow. It scoured the substrate and removed accumulated sand, algae, and debris, but did not significantly change habitat types or riparian vegetation.
- After the August flood, longfin dace spawning nests appeared on a gravel/sand bar. Gila topminnow became much more abundant, while longfin dace and fathead minnow numbers appeared reduced.
- A smaller early-December flood reached near bankfull and deposited sand in pools. Larval longfin dace (under 2 mm) indicated recent spawning.

**Macroinvertebrates**
- Samples from 1986 and 1991 (analyzed by the FS aquatic macroinvertebrate lab in Provo, Utah) were dominated by sediment- and organic-enrichment-tolerant taxa in all sampling events ([[Macroinvertebrates]]).
- The Biotic Condition and Diversity indices indicated poor condition with low diversity. Whether this reflects management or natural causes remains unresolved. Analysis of 1992 samples was pending.

**Future work**
- Monthly fish and habitat sampling at the three Cave Creek sites will continue in 1993 until at least June.
- If the burn does not occur, sampling becomes quarterly to track community trends.
- If the burn proceeds, a new monitoring program will be developed with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and USFWS.
