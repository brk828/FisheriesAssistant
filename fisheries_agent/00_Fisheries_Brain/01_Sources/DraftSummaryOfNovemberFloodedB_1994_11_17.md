## Executive Summary

This document is a draft meeting summary from the **Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program**, a component of the [[Recovery Program for the Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River]]. Rather than presenting new empirical fish science, the report synthesizes management decisions, restoration criteria, and program status related to recovering the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and other native fishes through restoration of floodplain and flooded bottom land habitats along the Green, Colorado, and Gunnison Rivers. Key discussions centered on program goals, site-selection criteria, the role of artificial propagation versus wild larval collection, contaminant (selenium) management, and coordination among federal, state, and stakeholder groups (USFWS, USBR, WAPA, CREDA, state wildlife agencies).

## Background & Description

While this is primarily a policy/program document, it references specific life-stage and size-related fish handling methods relevant to morphology and identification:

- **Razorback Sucker larvae**: Target life stage for collection from natural reproduction in the Green River; larvae are reared in controlled, nonnative-free flood plain ponds until large enough for **individual tagging** (e.g., PIT tags) before release into the Green and Colorado Rivers.
- **Hatchery-produced razorbacks** were noted by the Technical Advisory Group as **less fit for survival** in flood plain or main channel habitats compared to wild-caught larvae, implying morphological/behavioral differences in predator avoidance and habitat competency between hatchery-reared and wild fish.
- No detailed morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target habitat**: Flooded bottom lands and natural/managed floodplain sites along the Green, Colorado, and Gunnison Rivers, characterized by variable flooding regimes, terraces, depressions, and permanent pools.
- **Life stage association**: Floodplain habitats are hypothesized to be critical nursery areas for larval and juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] recruitment; monitoring targeted immature razorbacks in nonflooded habitats such as backwaters and tributary mouths as well.
- **Species interactions**:
  - **Nonnative fishes** (e.g., **carp**, **green sunfish**) were documented co-occurring with native species at sampled sites and are considered major predation/competition threats to razorback larvae and juveniles.
  - Drainable flood plain ponds were used experimentally to **eradicate and exclude nonnative fishes**, creating controlled environments to isolate the effects of nonnative species on young razorbacks.
- **Habitat quality constraints**:
  - **Selenium contamination** is a critical ecological stressor; the toxic threshold for inorganic selenium in water was identified as **2 ppb**, due to bioaccumulation through the food chain.
  - Several floodplain sites (e.g., Escalante Ranch, Little Stewart Lake, Pariette Draw, Walter Walker) exceeded this contaminant threshold, while others (e.g., Leota Bottom, Old Charlie Wash) were relatively contaminant-free.
  - **Ashley Creek** (tributary to the Green River) was flagged as a serious selenium source.
- **Hydrologic connectivity**: Flood plain restoration success is linked to flow and sediment management, channel geomorphology, and the ability to periodically flood and drain sites — factors evaluated for their compatibility with existing hydrologic operations (e.g., WAPA/CREDA power and water interests).

## Key Findings

- **Program Goal Established**: "To restore or enhance natural flood plain functions for recovery of endangered fishes in the upper Colorado River basin."
- **Experimental Design Proposed** by the Technical Advisory Group:
  - Short-term stabilization via controlled, nonnative-free flood plain ponds stocked with wild-collected [[Razorback Sucker]] larvae from the Green River, reared and PIT-tagged before release.
  - Long-term experimental management of a geographically diverse set of restored floodplain sites, with monitoring of fish communities, water levels/temperature, cover, and geomorphological/riparian changes.
- **Artificial Propagation Policy**: Wild larvae preferred over hatchery-raised razorbacks due to higher presumed post-release fitness; hatchery propagation to be used only if insufficient wild larvae are available (mainly for Colorado and Gunnison River sites).
- **Site Screening Results (FY94)**:
  - 23 candidate sites identified for initial screening (including 5 original sites).
  - Contaminant screening revealed selenium exceedances at many sites; only a handful (e.g., **Leota Bottom**, **Old Charlie Wash**, gravel pit at 29 5/8 Road) were relatively clean.
  - Biological screening of 4 Green River sites found carp, green sunfish, and other nonnative species; **Leota** was notable for containing young razorbacks.
  - At **Old Charlie Wash**, 20,000 sucker larvae were introduced, but no razorbacks were recaptured, likely due to faulty inlet/outlet structures allowing fish escape/entry.
- **Geomorphology and Biology Screening**: Both were deemed largely unnecessary as separate site-screening phases; geomorphology will instead inform site design/construction, and biological screening will be paused pending review of existing [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] (CRFP) database records on native/nonnative fish habitat use.
- **New FY95 Screening Sites** (Green River): Johnson Bottom (RM 262), Wyasket Bottom (RM 254), Bowknot Bend (RM 62), Potato Bottom (RM 37), Valentine Bottom (RM 26).
- **Contaminant Management Strategy**: Ongoing coordination between the Flooded Bottom Lands Program, the **Irrigation Water Quality Program (Phase 4)**, and **NAQWA**, with experiments planned to refine "levels of concern" for selenium and test whether flooding can remediate contaminated sites.
- **Success Metric**: Not formally defined, but positive razorback sucker survival and recruitment were identified as the practical indicator of program success.
