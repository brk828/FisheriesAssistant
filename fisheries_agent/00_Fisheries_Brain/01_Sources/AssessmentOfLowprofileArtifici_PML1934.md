# Assessment of Low-Profile Artificial Cover for Minimizing Fisheries Impacts from In-Place Concrete Lining of Water Canal Systems

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This document comprises a 1988 cover letter from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Lower Colorado Regional Office and a draft research proposal by Gordon Mueller and Charles R. Histon. The proposal tests whether low-profile [[Artificial Cover]] made of scrap tires can offset fishery losses caused by in-place concrete lining of canals. Lining the canals without interrupting water delivery could reduce lining costs and environmental damage. Three study reaches are planned: two in the [[Coachella Canal]] (southern California) and one in the [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]] (Arizona), running for 36 months.

## Background & Description

The letter, dated September 1988 and addressed to Dr. Paul Marsh at Arizona State University, asks for review comments by October 30, 1988. It states that Reclamation, with the [[Coachella Valley Water District]], is designing an in-place lining prototype on the Coachella Canal, scheduled for January 1989.

The proposal is written by Gordon Mueller (Office of Environment, Boulder City, Nevada) and Charles R. Histon (Environmental Sciences Section, Denver, Colorado). Histon is an associate professor at Michigan State University, working through an Intergovernmental Personnel Act agreement.

Background material notes that Reclamation has built several hundred miles of large water distribution canals in the arid Western United States. Canal fish communities are under-utilized. Recreational fishing is discouraged, though [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Channel Catfish]] are the main gamefish caught. The California Aqueduct has 18 access sites along 343 miles of open canal where public fishing is encouraged.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Earthen versus lined canals.** Comparison of earthen and lined sections of the Coachella Canal (Mueller et al. 1988) showed a sharp decline after concrete lining with higher velocities. Diversity fell from 15 to 6 species, and biomass dropped by 50 to 80 percent. The remaining community was dominated by riverine species such as channel catfish, [[Threadfin Shad]], and [[Carp]], which tolerate high velocities. Cover-oriented fishes, including [[Sunfish]] (Centrarchidae) and [[Flathead Catfish]] (Ictaluridae), were common in earthen sections but were extirpated from lined sections.

**Granite Reef Aqueduct.** This [[Central Arizona Project]] canal, studied since 1986, has unlined sections with sunfish dominance. In 1987 about 35 percent of the water allocation was diverted. Velocities reached about 2 ft/s (51 cm/s) in 1988, against an expected 1 ft/s (0.4 cm/s)*. Current fish included sunfish, [[Striped Bass]], carp, and threadfin shad. The authors attribute the absence of sunfish in lined canals to lost cover and high velocities that "wash" fish downstream.

**Cover use in the canal.** SCUBA observations in the CAP found small fish using floating and submerged tumbleweeds, debris, and pump trashracks. [[Bluegill]] spawned around submerged tumbleweeds, and largemouth bass spawned along canal slopes. Predation on bass eggs by sunfish was noted, and the authors suggest recruitment of sunfish and catfish is limited by predation where cover is lacking.

**Historical mitigation.** The FWS Coordination Act Report (Kenny and Bransfield 1988) recommended a 35-acre (14.2 ha) fishing lake to offset losses from lining part of the All-American Canal. Estimated cost was about $5 million, plus costs tied to a 3,500 acre-feet per year water diversion.

## Key Findings

**Research objectives**
1. Determine species composition, size (length and weight), and biomass of fish using the structures.
2. Determine numbers and biomass per unit area of fish-food organisms (attached macroinvertebrates) on artificial cover.
3. Determine effects of the structures on water conveyance by monitoring currents, sedimentation, and aquatic plant growth.
4. Determine construction and maintenance costs of artificial cover.

**Study design**
- Five lined reaches were considered, with velocities up to 4 ft/s (122 cm/s), representing high, medium, and low velocity sites. Three were chosen, including Reach 1 of the Granite Reef Aqueduct, 49 miles of the Coachella Canal, and the in-place prototype.
- Two cover types, both made of scrap tires:
  - **Flat tire mats:** 10 × 6 ft substructures joined into 10 × 18 ft mats. Mats are spaced at least 50 yards apart.
  - **Cellular (on-end) tire structures:** 5 × 10 ft substructures forming 10 × 18 ft structures.
- Each of the 3 study sections receives 15 tire structures, for 45 in total. The letter's text gives 32 structures, and the document does not reconcile the two figures.
- Each study reach is divided into control and treatment areas. The treatment area is about 0.4 mile (0.7 km) long and the control area about 0.8 mile (1.3 km).
- Structures are weighted with concrete and secured with noncorrosive strapping and chains. Signs are posted to restrict public access.

**Sampling plan**
- Fish are sampled quarterly for 2 years using a purse seine (about 1/4 in. bar mesh) and SCUBA or hydroacoustic surveys. Index of abundance is recorded, and length, weight, and sex are noted where possible.
- Macroinvertebrates are sampled from tire sections placed on the structures, plus Hess sampler core samples from control substrate.
- Water currents, sediment depth, and aquatic plants are measured at control and treatment reaches. Each current transect yields 14 velocity estimates. Sediment samples are taken quarterly, and plants are sampled three times with a 0.25 m² quadrat.
- Water quality (temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity) is measured with a Hydrolab.

**Schedule and deliverables**
- Study length is 36 months, starting at the beginning of fiscal year 1989. Structures are placed in the existing lined reaches by about month 4 (January 1989), but the prototype placement must wait for construction.
- Progress reports are due every 12 months, a draft report at the end of month 27, and peer review at month 33. Publication in scientific journals is expected.

**Expected outcomes**
- The authors estimate that fish communities reestablished under in-place lining will fall between the earthen and concrete-lined conditions of the Coachella Canal.
- Cost comparisons will be made between artificial cover and new lake construction.
- The authors state that artificial cover has never been used in a manmade water conveyance system, so the study tests a new mitigation approach.
