# Field Survey of San Bernardino NWR and Southeastern Arizona Fish Populations

#ichthyology #native-fish-conservation #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

Field survey conducted April 27-29, 1990 by W.L. Minckley, S.P. Vives, B.D. DeMarais, and J. Dunham documenting native and non-native fish populations at [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] and surrounding waters in [[Cochise County]], Arizona. Survey documented multiple native species including [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), alongside invasive [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). Collections made for preservation and live transport to support conservation efforts.

## Background & Description

The survey targeted multiple aquatic habitats across southeastern Arizona including spring-fed streams, ponds, and cienega systems. [[Black Draw]] was sampled both below and above a barrier structure, with temperatures ranging 10-21°C depending on location and proximity to spring sources. [[Leslie Creek]] and various springs at San Bernardino NWR provided additional sampling locations. [[Cave Creek]] (Middle Fork and South Fork) in the [[Chiricahua Mountains]] and [[Turkey Creek]] at Coronado Ranch were also surveyed.

Sampling methods included dipnets, 6-foot seines with 1/8-inch mesh, and 20-foot seines with 1/4-inch mesh. Substrates varied from sand and silt in spring-fed areas to cobble and rubble in stream reaches, with significant leaf litter accumulation in forested stream sections. Vegetation included cattails, reeds, duckweed, and *Myriophyllum* in pond habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey documented significant presence of invasive [[Gambusia affinis]] throughout most sampling locations, with this species dominating poeciliid assemblages in [[Black Draw]] below the barrier (79 individuals vs. 2 native topminnows). Native [[Yaqui Topminnow]] populations were concentrated at spring sources, with proportions declining downstream as mosquitofish increased. The refuge manager reported that Robertson's Cienega had been stocked with *P. o. sonoriensis* and *G. purpurea* the previous year.

Parasite loads were notably high in some populations, particularly [[Longfin Dace]] at [[Leslie Creek]] showing heavy blackspot fluke infestation with up to 80% body coverage. Some [[Gila Chub]] also exhibited blackspot parasites. Water temperatures varied considerably by habitat type: spring sources reached 29°C (North Pond) while downstream pools cooled to 19-20°C; stream temperatures ranged 10-21°C.

Habitat modification was noted at [[Cave Creek]], with new parking areas constructed since the previous year's survey. Fire danger was listed as high, indicating drought conditions.

## Key Findings

**Black Draw Collections:**
- Below barrier: 79 *Gambusia affinis*, 2 *Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*, 5 *Agosia chrysogaster*
- Above barrier (3 localities): 150 *G. affinis*, 65 *Gila purpurea* (30 frozen), 1 *Campostoma ornatum*
- Head Spring: 96 *P. o. sonoriensis*, 19 *G. affinis*, 10 *A. chrysogaster* (5 frozen, 5 preserved), 3 *C. ornatum* (1 tuberculate male), 8 *G. purpurea*

**Other San Bernardino NWR Sites:**
- North Pond: 130 *G. purpurea* (30 frozen)
- Leslie Creek: 100 *P. o. sonoriensis*, 50 *G. purpurea* (30 frozen), 50 *A. chrysogaster* (30 frozen) with heavy parasite loads
- Tule Spring: ~40 *P. o. sonoriensis* (25 taken alive for transport to ASU)
- House Pond: ~10 juvenile *G. purpurea* (<3 cm TL)
- Pool below House Pond: 65 *G. purpurea* (30 frozen, 20 transported live), ~20 *P. o. sonoriensis*

**Cave Creek Collections:**
- Middle Fork: 2 trout parr ~3 cm TL (probably *Onchorhynchus mykiss*)
- South Fork: 29 *Rhinichthys osculus* (13 frozen, 16 preserved including 10 adults and 6 juveniles)

**Turkey Creek (Coronado Ranch):**
- 30+ *Gila purpurea* (30 frozen)

Live specimens were transported to WLM house and Arizona State University for conservation purposes. The survey documented successful establishment of native species in refuge waters while highlighting ongoing challenges from invasive mosquitofish and parasite infestations.
