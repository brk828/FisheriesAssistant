# Razorback Sucker Status and Capture Records, Lower Colorado River (1980)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1980 file set contains a California Department of Fish and Game memorandum, a cover letter, and an Arizona Game and Fish capture report on the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Lower Colorado River]]. The memo argues that the species has declined sharply since dam construction and channelization, that recruitment is failing, and that predation on all life stages is increasing. It concludes that the species' proposed threatened status, published by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in the Federal Register on April 24, 1978, is "strongly justified" and should be established as soon as possible. A separate report documents a juvenile razorback sucker captured in a canal on the Colorado River Indian Reservation.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The scanned capture report renders the name with OCR errors, which were corrected here.
- **Historical status:** The memo states the species was "once one of the most abundant fishes in the lower Colorado River."
- **Cause of decline:** Attributed to physical and biological alterations after construction of seven dams, channelization projects, and competition and predation by introduced game and non-game species.
- **Regulatory status:** Proposed as threatened by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Federal Register, 4/24/78). The memo recommends the listing be finalized promptly.
- **Authors and recipients:** Bill Loudermilk, Fishery Biologist (Blythe, CA), wrote the status report. It was sent to Kent Miller of the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] under a cover letter dated October 16, 1980, from a Long Beach team member. The letter asks whether funding for the team's tasks will be provided.
- **Dates:** The memo is dated September 29, 1980. The capture report is dated January 1980 (its date is partly illegible in the scan).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat alteration.** The memo states that gravel bars, side channels, and gravel shorelines in the lower Colorado River have decreased significantly since the dam-building era. Increased predator populations, channelization, and energy development projects in the Yuma, Parker, Havasu, and Mohave divisions, along with the Hoover Modification Project, further reduce the viability of the remaining population.

**Spawning habitat.** Of the areas assessed, only the Parker and Mohave divisions of the river and [[Lake Mohave]] are said to contain gravel substrate comparable to the spawning grounds used at [[Senator Wash Reservoir]] in the 1979–80 season. Spawning areas in Lake Mohave and near Grand Junction, Colorado (Walter Walker Wildlife Area) may also be similar.

**Reservoir operations.** Water-level fluctuations at Senator Wash Reservoir during February and March 1980 caused observed mortality of eggs and larvae on the spawning areas. The memo suggests that dam and reservoir operations during spawning months may be a critical factor in egg and larval mortality in the lower river.

**Predators and competitors.** Scuba observations at Senator Wash Reservoir recorded:
- [[Channel Catfish]] following spawning-area grazers and actively feeding over recently spawned sites.
- Presence of [[Common Carp]], [[Bluegill]], and other introduced species in the spawning areas. Some species names are illegible in the scan.

The memo states that illegal introductions of baitfish and game fish into Senator Wash Reservoir increase the likelihood that these species will spread downstream and become more abundant in the lower river above [[Imperial Dam]].

**Flathead catfish expansion.** The memo describes an increase in [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) upstream to [[Parker Dam]]. A week-long survey documented the first records of flathead catfish above Parker Dam, in [[Lake Havasu]]. The memo states that the acceptable prey size for large flathead catfish exceeds that of other predators except one unnamed species (name illegible in the scan).

## Key Findings

**1. Recruitment is failing.**
- No significant recruitment has been reported since the species was proposed as threatened.
- A 1979–80 study of Senator Wash Reservoir found that spawning occurred there. Viable eggs and larvae were collected from two distinct spawning areas.
- No young-of-the-year or yearling fish were found.
- Both spawning areas had a slope of about 30% and clean, loose gravel substrate.

**2. Young fish captured in canal system.**
- Young razorback suckers (about 12–15 inches, as scanned) were captured in the Parker Valley canal system early in 1980 during annual canal draw-downs.
- The memo says these fish likely came from the lower Colorado River between Parker Dam and Headgate Rock Dam, which coincides with an area of gravel bars. The Parker II section, another area with remaining gravel bars, is a possible alternative origin.

**3. Predation is increasing on all life stages.**
- Introduced predators and competitors are present in spawning areas.
- Flathead catfish are expanding upstream, with the first documented occurrence above Parker Dam.
- Remaining adult, subadult, and juvenile razorback suckers are subject to additional predation.

**4. Management concerns.**
- Reservoir operations during spawning months may contribute to egg and larval mortality.
- Available spawning habitat that is not threatened or controlled by dams may be limited.
- The memo concludes that razorback suckers are attempting to reproduce, but that conditions are not adequate for successful recruitment.

**5. Canal capture report (January 1980).**
- A resident of the [[Colorado River Indian Reservation]] captured a suspected juvenile razorback sucker in a lateral canal on January 12, 1980. The capture used a chicken-wire seine placed across a culvert under the Burns Road canal, about 16 miles north and south of the Four Corners area near Parker, Arizona.
- The fish measured 12.7 inches (323 mm) and weighed 0.75 pound.
- The specimen was held alive in a 50-gallon office aquarium over the weekend. Photographs and scale samples were taken, and a press article appeared in the *Parker Pioneer* on January 24, 1980.
- A later date of January 14, 1980 appears in the text, but its context (such as release or death) is not clear.
- Copies of the report were sent to the Arizona Game and Fish Department and to the California Department of Fish and Game in Blythe.

**6. Recommendation.** The memo concludes that the 1978 proposal to list the razorback sucker as threatened is "strongly justified" and should be established as soon as possible.
