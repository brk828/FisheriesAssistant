# Lake Mohave Sampling for Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker (USFWS Planning Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum, issued from the Arizona Fisheries Resource Office in Parker, solicits participation in an intensive sampling effort at [[Lake Mohave]] on the [[Colorado River]]. The effort targets two imperiled native fishes, the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The sampling was scheduled for a multi-day period in March 1993 (Sunday through Sunday; the exact dates are partly illegible in the scan). It has two goals: (1) collect adult bonytail for broodstock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and (2) tag and recapture adult razorback suckers to refine population dynamics estimates. The memo also sets out a tentative deployment of agency and cooperator crews across the lake and reservoir arms, and asks participants to confirm their intent by early February.

## Background & Description

**Bonytail chub (*Gila elegans*)** is a Colorado River cyprinid that is now rare in the wild. The memo states that the USFWS intends to add wild-caught adults to the existing hatchery stock at Dexter. Fish measuring more than 11 inches (about 28 cm) total length that lack hatchery-produced identifiers are to be retained and transferred to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. Fish are held there temporarily before moving to Dexter. Hatchery-origin fish, identified by size and/or fin clips, are to be examined, photographed, and released.

**Razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)** is a large catostomid of the same river system. The memo reports that more than 8,000 adults have been captured in Lake Mohave since 1980, and that roughly 1,500 of these have been tagged. Based on subsequent recaptures, the lake population is estimated at about 5,000 adults. The scan of this figure is partly garbled, so it should be verified against the original. Recapture data have yielded inferences about local and long-distance movement and about the fidelity of individual fish to particular areas.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo expresses concern that the razorback sucker population in Lake Mohave will not persist indefinitely. It notes that declines observed in other reservoirs are expected to repeat here, and that wild numbers could fall substantially during the remainder of the decade. On this basis, the memo frames ongoing monitoring as a priority, specifically the detection and documentation of changes in population status. Reservoir populations are treated as the focus of this decline risk, rather than the Colorado River reach itself. The study design therefore emphasizes:

- Tagging and recapture to improve abundance estimates and movement models (see [[Mark-Recapture Analysis]]).
- Tracking site fidelity across lake areas.
- Detecting population trends early enough to guide management.

The memo does not report water chemistry, habitat measurements, or non-target taxa such as invertebrates or plankton.

## Key Findings

**Objectives and methods**

- **Broodstock collection:** Obtain adult wild bonytail for the Dexter hatchery stock. Retain fish over 11 inches that appear wild, and move them via Willow Beach.
- **Razorback mark-recapture:** Continue PIT (passive integrated transponder) tagging of adult razorback suckers, as was done the previous year. Participants must provide scanners, PIT tags, and injection or topical supplies.
- **Backup marking:** Standard Floy-type tags and tagging equipment are to be kept on hand if PIT tags or materials are unavailable (see [[Floy Tags]] and [[PIT Tagging]]).
- **Tag requirements:** Groups should estimate PIT tag needs from past catch rates.

**Population status**

- More than 8,000 razorback suckers captured in Lake Mohave since 1980.
- About 1,500 razorback suckers tagged to date.
- Population estimate of about 5,000 adults, based on recapture data (partly illegible in the scan).
- Expected decline of wild razorback numbers over the remainder of the decade, based on patterns in other reservoirs.

**Tentative deployment (1993)**

Crews are expected to follow the general layout used in the previous year:

- USFWS Fishery Resource Offices (Parker and Needles, with cooperators) lead the main effort.
- The USFWS Ecological Services field office works the lower lake.
- The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (lead), the Nevada Department of Wildlife, and the National Park Service cover the Cottonwood Cove area on the west side.
- [[Arizona State University]] (W.L. Minckley) works Cottonwood Basin on the east side.
- The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] may work the Three Mile area or another location, depending on available personnel and equipment.

Assignments are not final. Groups may add emphasis areas or participants where they can sustain independent camps. Otherwise, personnel will be integrated into existing camps.

**Administrative timeline**

- Participants must confirm their intention to take part by early February (the scan reads "February 1").
- The memo states that a detailed list of participants and area assignments will be sent to respondents by March 1.
- The memo is dated December 4, 1992, and its deadlines refer to 1992, while the sampling period is in March 1993. The year references are internally inconsistent, possibly due to OCR error or a typo in the original.
