# Guide to the Coastal Marine Fishes of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-identification #california-fishes

## Executive Summary

This is Fish Bulletin 157 (California Department of Fish and Game, 1972), a comprehensive field identification guide by [[Daniel J. Miller]] and [[Robert N. Lea]] covering all shallow marine fishes recorded in [[California Coastal Waters]]. It describes 554 species across 144 families, supplies artificial dichotomous keys to families and species, and includes geographic range, maximum size, depth range, color, meristic counts, and a rarity rating for each species. The guide also contributes original data: a ventral sensory pore key for skates, 170 geographic range limit extensions, and several depth and size records.

## Background & Description

The guide is intended primarily for identification. Keys rely on external features: body shape and proportions, color, fin ray counts, gill rakers, lateral line pores and scales, and teeth. Internal characters are avoided except for gill rakers and teeth, which can be examined without dissection. Although the keys are artificial, the family sequence follows the phylogenetic order of [[Greenwood et al 1966]], running from primitive hagfishes to specialized molas.

Rarity is defined as follows: a fish is **rare** if 20 or fewer have ever been taken in California, **uncommon** if seldom seen due to scarcity or unavailability, and **common** if it is easily obtained in catches or in tide pool, mud flat, or kelp canopy collections.

Measurements follow [[Hubbs and Lagler 1958]] conventions. Fin formulas use Roman numerals for spines and Arabic numerals for soft rays. Gill rakers are counted on the first gill arch. Depth terminology follows [[Hedgpeth 1957]]: epipelagic (0 to 656 ft), mesopelagic (656 to 3281 ft), and bathypelagic (3281 to 13,123 ft).

Illustrations were based largely on literature, with many drawings checked against fresh or preserved material. The senior author prepared the fish drawings and family key figures.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Of the 554 species, 439 occur mainly in waters shallower than 400 ft. Forty-eight are meso- or bathypelagic, representing about one-third of California's meso- and bathypelagic forms. Sixty-seven are deepwater bottom dwellers rarely taken above 400 ft. Deepwater forms are included when they are taken in commercial trawls or occasionally occur near the surface.

Habitats covered include the intertidal zone, tide pools, kelp canopy, rocky reefs, sandy beaches, bays and estuaries (including [[Salton Sea]] introductions), eelgrass beds, and the open ocean. Anadromous species such as [[Pacific Lamprey]], [[American Shad]], [[Green Sturgeon]], and the [[Pacific Salmon]] group move between ocean and freshwater.

Three species had not been confirmed in California waters but were included because they are likely to appear. Five California species were undescribed at publication: two stichaeids, and one each in the Scorpaenidae, Agonidae, and Gerreidae.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** 554 species in 144 families; 439 shallow (under 400 ft), 48 meso- or bathypelagic, and 67 deepwater bottom dwellers.
- **Original data:** a ventral sensory pore pattern key for skates, 170 geographic range limit extensions, and several depth and maximum size records.
- **Largest group:** the family [[Scorpaenidae]] has the most California marine species, with 62 species across four genera. Its [[Rockfishes]] (genus *Sebastes*) are described as among the most difficult to identify, and the key relies heavily on color.
- **Sharks and rays:** the guide includes keys to shark families and to rays and skates. Notable entries include the [[Great White Shark]] (*Carcharodon carcharias*), up to 20 ft recorded; the [[Basking Shark]] (*Cetorhinus maximus*), up to 45 ft; the [[Whale Shark]] (*Rhincodon typus*), up to 45 ft; and the [[Pacific Electric Ray]] (*Torpedo californica*), up to 4 ft.
- **Skates:** the [[Big Skate]] (*Raja binoculata*) reaches 8 ft; the [[Sandpaper Skate]] (*Raja kincaidii*) is the only California skate with a scapular spine.
- **Commercially significant species:** the [[Pacific Hake]] (*Merluccius productus*, common, up to 3 ft), [[Pacific Herring]] (*Clupea harengus pallasii*, common, up to 18 in.), [[Pacific Sardine]] (*Sardinops sagax caeruleus*, common, up to 16 in.), [[Sablefish]] (*Anoplopoma fimbria*, common, up to 3 ft 4 in.), and [[Lingcod]] (*Ophiodon elongatus*, common, up to 45 in.).
- **Pacific salmon and trout:** the group includes [[Chum Salmon]], [[Sockeye Salmon]], [[Pink Salmon]], [[King Salmon]], [[Silver Salmon]], [[Rainbow Trout]], and [[Cutthroat Trout]]. The guide provides juvenile identification keys based on parr marks and fin spotting.
- **Deepsea fishes:** many mesopelagic families are covered, including [[Lanternfishes]] (Myctophidae, 7 of 32 California members described), [[Hatchetfishes]] (Sternoptychidae), [[Lightfishes]] (Gonostomatidae), and [[Deepsea Smelts]] (Bathylagidae).
- **Taxonomic notes:** the appendix explains taxonomic decisions and lists personal communications. The Department of Fish and Game collaborators acknowledged include John Fitch, whose depth and size data were of primary importance.
- **Rarity and range:** each species entry gives a geographic range, maximum size, depth range, color, meristics where available, and a rarity or commonness statement.
