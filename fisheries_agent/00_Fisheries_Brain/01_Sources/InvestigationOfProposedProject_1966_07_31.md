## Executive Summary

This 1966 Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report documents interagency review of multiple proposed water diversion, reclamation, and watershed development projects across Arizona, undertaken to evaluate and mitigate impacts on fish and wildlife resources under the **Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act**. The report covers six [[Colorado River Diversion Projects]] (Winslow, Burro Creek, Black Creek, Flagstaff, Williams, and Prescott-Verde), one small reclamation project (Curtis Canal Company), two watershed projects (Harquahala and Fredonia), and ongoing multi-purpose planning studies for the [[San Pedro River Basin]] and [[Santa Cruz River Basin]]. Recommendations focused on public access, boat/fishing facilities, motorboat regulation, minimum reservoir pool levels, and habitat retention to maximize sport fishing and hunting benefits from newly created reservoirs, while balancing flood control, municipal water supply, and agricultural objectives.

## Background & Description

The report was prepared under **Project FW-16-R-5** by Roland C. Kufeld for the Arizona Game and Fish Department, continuing prior-year work (Curtis 1965) reviewing the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's *Arizona-Colorado River Diversion Projects* Reconnaissance Report (1963). These projects were designed to develop water resources outside the direct diversion area of the Central Arizona Project, improving efficient use of water from the **Little Colorado River** and **Gila River** drainages.

Key projects reviewed:

- **[[Winslow Project]]** — Two dams (Wildcat Reservoir on Chevelon Creek, ~650 surface acres; Wilkins Dam on Clear Creek, ~600 surface acres) supplying water to Winslow/Holbrook areas.
- **[[Burro Creek Project]]** — 70-ft dam on Burro Creek near Bagdad; 225-acre minimum reservoir with pumped pipeline to a terminal storage reservoir. *Later deemed impractical by the Bureau of Reclamation; feasibility studies not pursued.*
- **[[Black Creek Project]]** — 87-ft earthfill dam on the Navajo Indian Reservation (Apache County), 1,000-acre minimum reservoir (Houck Reservoir), pipeline to Gallup, NM.
- **[[Flagstaff Project]]** — 180-ft dam, ≥140-acre reservoir in Volunteer Canyon (Coconino County), 11-mile pipeline to Flagstaff for municipal/industrial water.
- **[[Williams Project]]** — 120-ft dam at Twin Springs in Hell Canyon, ≥35-acre reservoir, pipeline to Williams for municipal use.
- **[[Prescott-Verde Project]]** — Two reservoirs (Clarkdale Reservoir: 250-ft dam, ≥750 acres on the Verde River; Williamson Valley Reservoir: 50-ft earthfill dam, ≥650 acres) plus pumping system serving Prescott, Clarkdale, Cottonwood, Cornville, and Camp Verde.

Additional projects assessed: the **Curtis Canal Company** small reclamation project (8.4 miles of concrete canal lining, Safford area); the **Harquahala Valley Watershed Program** (flood retarding structures, Maricopa County); the **Fredonia Watershed Project** (flood protection, Coconino County); the **San Pedro Project** (Charleston-site earthfill dam, 165 ft high, 1,800-ft crest, plus large-scale phreatophyte clearing for water salvage); and the **Santa Cruz River Basin** flood control and water resource investigations (Bureau of Reclamation and Army Corps of Engineers).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Phreatophyte clearing impacts (San Pedro Project):** Plans proposed clearing ~18,000 acres of bottomland vegetation (dense mesquite forest, some salt cedar) along the San Pedro and Gila Rivers to salvage an estimated 12,700 acre-feet of water annually. This raised concerns regarding loss of wildlife cover and fish habitat; recommendations called for retaining vegetation in strips/blocks and preferring mechanical clearing over herbicide application near sensitive habitat.
- **Reservoir habitat design:** Recommendations across nearly all projects emphasized minimum pool levels/reservoir surface area guarantees (e.g., Twin Springs Reservoir not to be drawn below 75 surface acres) to sustain fish populations, and physical habitat modifications (e.g., borrow-pit holes in Williamson Valley Reservoir bottom to improve fish production).
- **Recreational use conflicts:** Speedboating and waterskiing were flagged as incompatible with sport fisheries at several reservoirs (Winslow, Burro Creek, Prescott-Verde), prompting recommended use-zoning and motor restrictions (e.g., electric motors ≤1 hp permitted at Twin Springs Reservoir).
- **Watershed/habitat feasibility limits:** At Harquahala, only 6–10 acres of a proposed 164-acre pool met physical requirements for permanent fish habitat due to high evaporation and low sub-watershed yield, illustrating the ecological constraints of arid-region impoundments; supplemental well pumping ($10/acre-foot; ~$12,000/year) was deemed cost-prohibitive.
- **Regional recreational demand context:** The Santa Cruz Basin statement highlights broader ecological/socioeconomic drivers — Arizona's rapid population growth, rising hunting/fishing license sales (+35% over 5 years vs. 30% population growth), and increasing visitor pressure on existing reservoirs and public lands, underscoring cumulative pressure on aquatic recreational resources statewide.

## Key Findings

- **Winslow Project**: With recommended fish/wildlife provisions (boat facilities, motorboat controls, minimum release studies), projected to yield **40,700 man-days of trout fishing** annually, +150 man-days upland game hunting, but **-100 man-days** big game hunting.
- **Burro Creek Project**: Projected **11,000 man-days of fishing**, 100 man-days deer hunting, 50 man-days waterfowl hunting annually — however, subsequently judged impractical by the Bureau of Reclamation; feasibility studies halted.
- **Black Creek Project**: Projected **125,000 man-days of fishing** and 200 man-days waterfowl hunting annually — the largest fishing benefit among all projects reviewed.
- **Flagstaff Project**: Projected **35,000 man-days of fishing** annually with recommended public access and facility improvements.
- **Williams Project**: Projected **30,000 man-days of fishing** annually at Twin Springs Reservoir.
- **Prescott-Verde Project** (Clarkdale + Williamson Valley Reservoirs): Projected **>35,000 man-days of fishing** and **1,200 man-days of waterfowl hunting** annually.
- **Feasibility status**: All Colorado River Diversion Projects except Burro Creek were slated to advance to feasibility-grade studies, with fish/wildlife benefit estimates subject to revision.
- **Small Reclamation Project (Curtis Canal Company)**: Canal-lining project under review by Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife; no fishery benefit/loss figures finalized in this segment.
- **Harquahala Watershed**: Fishery development recommended but ultimately deemed economically infeasible due to prohibitive annual maintenance/pumping costs relative to minimal usable fish habitat (6–10 of 164 acres).
- **Fredonia Watershed Project**: Determined to have **no significant effect** on fish or wildlife.
- **San Pedro Project**: Draft recommendations included fisherman access facilities, reservoir zoning for fish/wildlife use, retention of trees/brush for fish cover, two 100-acre seining areas, a **five-year fishery investigation and management program**, modified phreatophyte clearing to protect game habitat, and dedication of ~4,700 acres within the flood-control pool to the Arizona Game and Fish Department for upland game/waterfowl management. Report status: draft, not finalized.
- **Santa Cruz River Basin**: No dam/reservoir specifics finalized in this segment; eight potential dam/impoundment sites under investigation (Red Rock A, B, C, D; Temporal; Upper Peña Blanca; Walker; Pecks A/B), with diamond drilling completed and favorable at Red Rock A, C, and D.
- **Socioeconomic context (Santa Cruz statement)**: University of Arizona economic study cited recreational water use as generating up to **6x the state income return** of irrigation water; anglers spend over **$1,000/year per surface-acre** of accessible fishing water in Arizona (excluding the Colorado River). Surveys showed **60–80%** of visitors to Department-built lakes visited for non-fishing recreation (picnicking, swimming, boating).
