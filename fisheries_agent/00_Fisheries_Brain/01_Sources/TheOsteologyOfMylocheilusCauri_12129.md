# Osteology of a Cyprinid Hybrid: *Mylocheilus caurinum* × *Ptychocheilus oregonense* Compared with Its Parental Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study compares the bones of an intergeneric [[Hybridization|hybrid]] between the [[Columbia River Chub]] (*Mylocheilus caurinum*) and the [[Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus oregonense*), both from [[Flathead Lake]], Montana. The author examined five specimens each of the two parental species and the hybrid to test whether the intermediacy seen in external characters also appears in the skeleton. Hybrid bones were intermediate in nearly every character that separated the parents, supporting the original hybrid identification. Most differences in skull, jaw and pharyngeal structures are interpreted as adaptations to feeding: the chub is a small-mouthed, mollusc- and insect-eater with grinding teeth, while the squawfish is a more predatory fish with sharp, hooked teeth. The author notes the small sample size and that the results are qualitative in part.

## Background & Description

Naturally occurring fish hybrids are surprisingly common in North American freshwaters. In Western Montana, four intergeneric hybrids of cyprinid fishes occur in a fauna of only four native cyprinid species. Hybrids are usually identified by intermediacy in the characters that distinguish their presumed parents. The author notes that this intermediacy is consistent enough to predict hybrid characters before a specimen is found, and that experimental validation has been done in other groups, such as sunfishes (Hubbs and Hubbs, 1932, 1933).

Externally, the two parental species differ mainly in that the chub has barbels, a shorter jaw and head, and brighter coloration. Their fin-ray and scale counts overlap, which made bony structures a useful source of additional diagnostic characters. Because hybrid specimens are scarce, the author avoided mutilating them where possible.

**Materials and methods**
- **Sample:** Five specimens each of *M. caurinum*, *P. oregonense* and the hybrid, all from Flathead Lake, similar in size (standard length about 190–265 mm) and mature. Both sexes were represented. The author states five per group is too few for statistics, but enough to reveal aberrant specimens.
- **Preservation:** The hybrids had been kept in 10% [[Formalin Preservation|formalin]] for no more than three months, and parental specimens were treated the same way. Boiling and dermestid beetles were unsuccessful. Bones were extracted after the flesh was allowed to decay in water for several months, which the author notes produced a strong odour.
- **Measurement:** Measurements were made under a dissecting microscope with a steel rule (0.5 mm graduations) or a vernier caliper. Repeated measurements were used to check error.
- **Hybrid index:** Following [[Hybrid Index|hybrid index]] methods (Hubbs, Hubbs and Johnson, 1943), *Mylocheilus* was scored 0 and *Ptychocheilus* 100, so 50 denotes exact intermediacy. The author acknowledges that such indices are questionable with small samples.
- **Terminology:** Bone names follow Ramaswami (1952).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper links skeletal differences to feeding ecology. The chub feeds largely on insects and molluscs, whereas the squawfish is more predatory. The chub has a small mouth with barbels, while the squawfish has a large mouth without barbels. Its jaws bear stronger articulating surfaces and tendon attachments, and its dentary process sits further back toward the jaw fulcrum. The author suggests that the pharyngeal teeth, jaw proportions and other cranial features are mainly adaptations to feeding habits, though some characters (such as sensory pore counts) may not relate directly to diet.

The author also discusses which parent is more primitive. Evidence reviewed by Sagemehl (1891) suggests early cyprinids were sedentary, bottom-living forms rather than large-mouthed free-swimmers. On that basis, *Mylocheilus* is considered the more primitive species. The two species' close osteological similarity, and their ability to hybridize, is taken to indicate a close relationship.

## Key Findings

**1. Hybrid bones are intermediate in most diagnostic characters.**
- Lower jaw: the chub's jaw is shorter relative to head length, its angular is proportionally longer, it has fewer sensory pores, and its dentary ascending process is longer and further forward. Hybrids are intermediate in all of these (tables 2 and 4).
- Lacrymal: the chub's lacrymal is short, the squawfish's is long, and hybrids are intermediate. The lacrymal-to-standard-length index is 81, the highest of all characters.
- Basioccipital and parasphenoid: both are shorter relative to standard length in the chub. Hybrids are intermediate.

**2. The hybrid index averages close to exact intermediacy.**
- Across 19 osteological characters, indices range from −5 (frontal width-to-length) to 81 (lacrymal-to-standard length).
- The average is 42, or 45 with the frontal character removed.
- The grand average external-character index for these specimens is 45. Earlier work using 20 adult specimens of each type gave an external index of 46, and two sets of yearling hybrids gave 42 and 45. The author takes the similarity among these values as support that the hybrid resembles *Mylocheilus* more than *Ptychocheilus*.

**3. Sensory pores fall between the parents.**
- Average pore counts were 29.8 (chub), 36.3 (hybrid) and 47.5 (squawfish), a hybrid index of 37.
- Lacrymal averages were 5.0, 7.4 and 9.7.
- Preoperculum averages were 9.5, 10.0 and 12.0. The author notes this is not a good separating character on its own.

**4. Pharyngeal teeth and arch show the clearest feeding adaptation.**
- Chub teeth are blunt and molariform, suited to grinding molluscs and insect chitin. Squawfish teeth are sharp and hooked, suited to tearing flesh and holding prey. Hybrid teeth are intermediate.
- Tooth formulae: *Mylocheilus* most often 1,5-5,1; *Ptychocheilus* 2,5-4,2, with considerable variation.
- Arch width-to-length ratio averages 2.41 (chub), 3.30 (hybrid) and 4.97 (squawfish), a hybrid index of 35. The author considers tooth counts of little comparative value because of variability and small samples.

**5. The Weberian apparatus is shared by both parents.**
- The first four vertebrae and [[Weberian Ossicles]] are alike in *Mylocheilus* and *Ptychocheilus* and similar to other cyprinids. Both species have a total of 44–46 vertebrae (rendered as "4446" in the scan), with 19–21 bearing ribs or rib stubs outside the Weberian series.
- Internal characters, including intestinal length, air-bladder lobes and fin position relative to the vertebrae, were not distinctive.

**6. Cleithrum and opercular differences.**
- The chub's cleithral medial process is directed posteriorly, while the squawfish's is directed anteriorly. Hybrids are slanted slightly posteriorly, between the parents (angle index 42).
- The chub's interopercular tip is narrower than the squawfish's. Hybrids overlap the chub broadly.
- Branchiostegal shape also shows a trend (the hybrid is closest to the chub).

**7. The frontal is an exception.**
- The frontal width-to-length ratio is the one character where hybrids do not fall between the parents. The author judges it unreliable because the parents do not differ markedly and sutures vary between left and right bones.

**8. Conclusion.**
- The hybrid's bony structure is intermediate in practically every character that separates the parents, and no unique hybrid characters were found.
- The author concludes that these results strongly support the original identification of the specimens as *M. caurinum* × *P. oregonense*, which had rested mainly on intermediate external meristic characters.
- The two species are closely related, *Mylocheilus* is probably the more primitive, and *Ptychocheilus* shows adaptation toward predatory feeding.
