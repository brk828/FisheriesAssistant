# Egg and Fish Production Records, Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center (1995–2000)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #cyprinidae #fish-hatchery

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of production records from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] covering 1995–2000. It has three parts: egg production tables, fish production tables by year, and a six-year fish production summary. Production covers twelve fish species, all of which are federally listed as endangered or threatened. The main output is hatchery propagation of [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], which dominate both egg and fish totals. The text is OCR-scanned and many figures are garbled, so only the summary totals are reported with confidence.

## Background & Description

The records cover the following hatchery-reared species, listed with their conservation status as given in the document:

- [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC): Endangered
- [[Woundfin]] (WDF): Endangered
- [[Virgin River Chub]] (VRC): Endangered
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS): Endangered
- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (CSF): Endangered
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (DEP): Endangered
- [[Yaqui Catfish]] (YCF): Threatened
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (GTM): Endangered
- [[Chihuahua Chub]] (CCH): Threatened
- [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (LSP): Endangered
- [[Guzmán Beautiful Shiner]] (GBS): Threatened
- [[Big Bend Gambusia]] (BBG): Endangered

The document uses the abbreviations above as column and row headers. Its tables give annual production by species for 1995, 1996, 1997, 1998, 1999 and 2000. The egg tables report RBS, BTC and CSF, with the fish tables covering the full list of species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The records document captive propagation of imperiled native fishes. Hatchery output is the main tool for supporting these populations, which is why the document tracks production by species and year. The document does not describe natural habitats, water chemistry, wild population status, release sites, or survival after stocking. Its scope is limited to hatchery production counts.

## Key Findings

**Egg production (1995–2000 summary)**
- The summary table lists egg totals for three species: RBS 164,549; BTC 73,362; CSF 212,272; with a stated total of approximately 450,192 (OCR reads "45A,192").
- Accompanying ratio values (2.5, 0.6, 2.4) appear next to these totals. The document does not label their units.
- Annual egg tables report large numbers for RBS, BTC and CSF in each year, but OCR corruption prevents reliable year-by-year transcription.

**Fish production (1995–2000 summary)**
- The summary reports a total of approximately 2,216,179 fish (OCR-garbled; the component values do not sum exactly to this figure, so treat it as approximate).
- Largest by count: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (CSF) 1,066,404; [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) 992,560; [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS) about 207,988 (OCR-garbled).
- Mid-sized counts: [[Woundfin]] (WDF) 50,407 and [[Virgin River Chub]] (VRC) 2,034.
- Small counts: [[Yaqui Catfish]] (YCF) 2,375; [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (LSP) 2,939; [[Chihuahua Chub]] (CCH) 780; [[Gila Topminnow]] (GTM) 600; [[Desert Pupfish]] (DEP) 175; [[Guzmán Beautiful Shiner]] (GBS) 100; [[Big Bend Gambusia]] (BBG) 25.
- A second column of smaller values (for example 7,265; 1,169; 1,506; 1,491; 11,765) appears in the summary, but the document does not label it, so its meaning cannot be confirmed.

**Annual fish production tables**
- The 1995–2000 tables list production by species for each year. Species such as BTC, CSF and RBS have the largest annual counts. Several rare species have counts in the tens or hundreds per year.
- OCR errors in these tables mean exact annual values are not reliably recoverable. Consult the original scan before quoting year-specific figures.

**Overall takeaway**
- Hatchery production is dominated by three endangered species: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- Several species were produced in very small numbers, with some totals in the tens. These are the most vulnerable to shortfalls in propagation.
