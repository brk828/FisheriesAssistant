# Suspended sediment in the Colorado River

#sediment-transport #freshwater-hydrology #colorado-river

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Geological Survey water-supply report by C. S. Howard presents sediment-sampling results for the [[Colorado River]] basin covering 1 October 1925 to 30 September 1941. The core record is a 16-year program at the [[Grand Canyon Gaging Station]], supplemented by shorter records at other main-stem and tributary stations. The report gives mean daily sediment concentrations, mean daily discharge and daily sediment loads for many stations, plus annual, maximum and minimum daily loads and particle-size information for three stations.

The central finding is a sediment budget gap. The [[Cisco]], [[Green River]] and [[San Juan River]] (Bluff) gaging stations account for about 90 percent of the runoff at Grand Canyon but only about 60 percent of its sediment load. The author attributes most of the unmeasured sediment to the [[Little Colorado River]] and two or three unsampled tributaries.

## Background & Description

**Scope and personnel.** Discharge records were supplied by district engineers A. B. Purton (Salt Lake City) and W. E. Dickinson and John H. Gardiner (Tucson). Samples were collected by district office staff. Daily and annual load computations were made by the Water Resources Laboratory. Field work was supervised by W. D. Collins, Chemist in Charge, Quality of Water Division. Annual results were published in the series "Quality of Surface Waters of the United States."

**Sampling stations and periods:**

- Colorado River near Cisco, Utah: 14 May 1929 to 30 September 1941
- Colorado River at Lees Ferry, Arizona: 1 October 1929 to 16 December 1933
- Colorado River near Grand Canyon, Arizona: 1 October 1925 to 30 September 1941
- Colorado River near [[Willow Beach]], Arizona: 1 October 1934 to 30 September 1939
- Colorado River near [[Topock]], Arizona: 1 October 1925 to 31 March 1939
- Green River at Green River, Utah: 1 May 1929 to 30 September 1941
- San Juan River near Bluff, Utah: short periods in 1923 and 1941
- Little Colorado River at Grand Falls, Arizona: 6 July to 26 September 1931

**Sampling equipment.** Samples were taken with the [[Colorado River Sampler]], designed by Carl H. Au of the Geological Survey. It hangs from the cable in place of a current meter and takes weights. Early versions punctured a paper cover over a ¼-inch hole in a milk-bottle cap. Later versions used a stopper pulled from a rubber cap fitted to a pint milk bottle.

**Sample types.** Early in the study, bottom, surface and average (integrated) samples were collected. Daily mean concentrations were computed from average samples, collected by [[Depth-Integrated Sampling]]. The method changed over time:

- First 3 years: the empty bottle was lowered to the bottom, opened, and raised so it was full at the surface.
- Remainder of the study: a bottle with an open hole in the cap was lowered and raised so it was not quite full. A bottle-opening device was used at some stages, and under heavy drift a shallower depth was used.

The author notes an initial inrush of water when the bottle opens at depth, which over-represents the deepest water. No data were available to quantify this error for the Colorado River.

**Sampling frequency.** Each station normally collected three integrated samples across the cross section. The plan was one set daily at normal stage and two or more during floods. Daily coverage was not achieved everywhere, so some records are incomplete.

**Particle size.** Size samples were collected about twice weekly at Grand Canyon from 1 April 1935 to 30 September 1941, and at Willow Beach and Bluff for part of that period.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sediment source and transport.** The Little Colorado River and other unsampled tributaries are the likely main sources of the unmeasured sediment. The highest main-stem concentrations occurred at Grand Canyon, and the highest tributary concentrations at Bluff on the San Juan River. The greatest concentration variations occurred during rapid rises after heavy rain on the desert part of the watershed.

**Concentration versus flow.** The report finds no direct relationship between concentration and discharge. In general, concentration rose with discharge, but it appeared to depend more on the source of the increased flow than on its size. Observations at Grand Canyon and Topock showed no conclusive relationship between sediment concentration and water velocity.

**Effect of [[Boulder Dam]].** Boulder Dam closed on 1 February 1935. Water released from it was clear except during density flows. Sediment at Willow Beach and Topock after that date came from the roughly 10-mile reach below the dam and the 105-mile reach between Willow Beach and Topock. Concentrations at both stations declined, and by the end of sampling the water was nearly clear. The reservoir behind the dam is [[Lake Mead]].

## Key Findings

- **Long-term record.** The Grand Canyon station, the most complete record, covers the full 16 years. Annual loads ranged from 50,080,000 tons in 1933–34 to 480,000,000 tons in 1928–29, as printed in the text.
- **Mean annual values at Grand Canyon (1925–41):** sediment load 200,200,000 tons; runoff 12,640,000 acre-feet.
- **Sediment budget gap.** Cisco, Green River and Bluff together account for about 90 percent of Grand Canyon runoff but only about 60 percent (described in the text as "less than two-thirds") of its sediment load. The unmeasured remainder is attributed mainly to the Little Colorado River and unsampled tributaries.
- **Table 3 comparison (selected years 1932, 1935, 1938, 1941):** Cisco contributed roughly 10–15 percent of Grand Canyon sediment and 39–47 percent of runoff. Bluff contributed roughly 22–42 percent of sediment with a smaller runoff share. Values are read from an OCR-degraded table and should be checked against the original.
- **Yuma.** [[Yuma Gaging Station]] annual sediment and runoff for 1926–41 were supplied by the Bureau of Reclamation (Table 2). Annual sediment ranged from about 5.1 million to 262 million tons across the record.
- **Load computation.** Daily load (tons) = mean concentration (% by weight) × mean daily discharge (second-feet) × 27 (conversion factor as printed). Annual loads were summed from monthly loads where available, or otherwise computed from a weighted-average concentration.
- **Sample weight.** Before 31 March 1930 each sample was assumed to weigh 473 grams. After that date each was weighed.
- **Sediment determination.** Most sediment was filtered through weighed filter papers and dried. From 1 October 1940, Grand Canyon samples were evaporated in weighed dishes.
- **Data limitations.** Some days were estimated. Some individual determinations were excluded as unrepresentative. Daily coverage was incomplete at several stations, and integrated-sample bias is unquantified.
- **Data tables.** Mean daily concentration tables (5–18), station-specific daily loads, and maximum and minimum daily load data (Table 1) are provided for stations including Cisco, Lees Ferry, Grand Canyon, Willow Beach, Topock, Green River and Bluff.
