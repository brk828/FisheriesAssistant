# Evolutionsphänomene bei Knochenfischen, genetisch betrachtet

#ichthyology #marine-biology #genetics #speciation #Poeciliidae #Gasterosteidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 symposium paper by [[Curt Kosswig]] (Zoological State Institute and Zoological Museum Hamburg) argues that genetic experimentation, particularly crossing experiments combined with biostatistics and cytology, complements the historical, comparative approach of phenomenal phylogenetics in explaining evolution in bony fishes. Using mainly [[Live-bearing Toothcarps]] (Cyprinodontiformes), the author shows how polygenic systems, chromosome number changes, sex-determination variation, and reproductive barriers shape diversity and [[Speciation]]. The paper ends with a transcribed discussion among [[Kosswig]], [[Numann]], [[Remane]], [[Wickler]] and [[Kinne]].

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** A structured review of genetic and phylogenetic approaches, with the author's own breeding work over more than 35 years on "many tens of thousands" of animals, mainly Zahnkarpfen (toothcarps).
- **Rationale:** Fishes are unusually suited to experimental genetics among vertebrates. Many can be bred quickly in small spaces, and bony fishes show a wide range of divergent adaptive and specialization types.
- **Methods:** Crossing experiments, biostatistics and [[Cytology]] (chromosome number and structure). Cytology in fishes was historically limited because of high, small chromosome counts.
- **Funding:** Work from 1960 supported by the [[Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Chromosome numbers:** Haploid n = 23 or, most often, n = 24 in Cyprinodontiformes, including the live-bearing Poeciliidae (5 subfamilies, 15 species) and many oviparous groups (about 10 species). Within Rivulini, numbers vary: *Aphyosemion* n = 20 (possibly n = 15), *Notobranchius* usually n = 19 falling to n = 8 in *N. rachowii*, and *Pterolebias* n = 10.
- **Drying-pool embryos:** *Aphyosemion*, *Notobranchius* and *Pterolebias* have embryos requiring a dry period lasting months. *Cynolebias* and *Cynopoecilus* share this trait but keep n = 24.
- **Stickleback distribution:** Morphs vary with habitat. The naked *leiurus* type dominates perimediterranean freshwater, while *trachurus* (fully plated) dominates the Atlantic and North Sea anadromous populations. In the Elbe estuary, spawning populations contained 55% *trachurus*, 40% *semiarmatus* and 5% *leiurus*.
- **Anatolian lakes:** Lake Burdur (about 180 km², up to 65 m deep, magnesium-sulphate rich, saline) hosts a very variable population of a toothcarp, with scale-reduced individuals. Lake Aci-göl, now largely dry in summer, holds fragmented populations.
- **Cave and surface fishes:** [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] surface forms and blind cave forms (Anoptichthys) hybridize freely.
- **Intralacustrine speciation:** Salmonid "races" in Sevan, Ohrid and Windermere differ in spawning time and site. Cichlid radiations in East African lakes are discussed in terms of preferential mating.

## Key Findings

1. **Chromosome evolution is unexpectedly variable.** Within groups previously assumed uniform, chromosome numbers change, suggesting either a non-natural classification or chromosome rearrangements linked to specialization.

2. **Stickleback armour is controlled by a single allele pair.** Crosses by Münzing (1959, 1962) indicate that AA = *trachurus*, Aa = *semiarmatus*, aa = *leiurus*. In the southern North Sea the anadromous gene pool was estimated at 75% A and 25% a. Freshwater *leiurus* predominance is linked to the allele's selective value, possibly with pleiotropic physiological effects.

3. **Most natural differences are polygenic.** Monogenic traits are mainly domestication mutants (albinism, xanthorism, fin deformities). Cave-fish eye and pigment differences in *Astyanax* hybrids are polymeric.

4. **Polymorphism and selection.** In *Platypoecilus maculatus*, colour genes appear selectively neutral, with allele frequencies stable for about half a century. Scale reduction in Anatolian toothcarps may be favoured in saline water and disfavoured in freshwater, though this is unproven.

5. **Sex determination is plastic.** *P. maculatus* shows XY in Mexican and ZZ/ZW in British Honduras populations. Autosomal polygenic systems can produce XX males and XY females. *Xiphophorus helleri* has no differentiated sex chromosomes.

6. **Reproductive barriers are sometimes genetic and sometimes behavioural.** In Anatolian toothcarps, hybrid males are sterile due to meiotic failure, while hybrid females are fertile. Intergeneric *Xiphophorus* × *Platypoecilus* hybrids are fertile, yet the species remain sympatric without hybrids. Zander (1962) showed that heterospecific sperm have lower fertilization success and shorter survival in the female oviduct.

7. **Speciation without spatial isolation is plausible.** Temporal isolation (e.g., spawning time in salmonids) can produce "species in statu nascendi." Cichlid mate choice may be genetically controlled, though this remains disputed.

8. **Discussion conclusions.** Remane agrees that speciation causes are largely understood, while phylogeny of Atlantidae may need more than mutation and selection. He cites sudden isolation by flowering time in oaks and *Culex* as examples. Kosswig notes that cave-fish behaviour and metabolism differ between sighted and blind forms.
