## Executive Summary
This document is **not a primary scientific dataset** but an administrative letter from Arizona State University's Office of Sponsored Programs Administration to the Arizona Game & Fish Department. It requests a no-cost time extension (through March 31, 1988) for a research contract (Agreement No. G70001) titled "[[Spikedace]] and [[Loachminnow]] Study," led by Dr. W.L. Minckley. While it contains no direct morphological or population data, it provides valuable **methodological and phenological context** for how field research on these two imperiled Arizona stream fishes was logistically planned around seasonal hydrology and reproductive cycles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although sparse, the letter implies several ecologically relevant details about the study system:

- **Habitat context**: Research was conducted in **Arizona streams**, consistent with the known native range of [[Spikedace]] and [[Loachminnow]] in the [[Gila River Basin]].
- **Hydrological regime**: The letter identifies a bimodal flow regime critical to field logistics:
  - **Spring–early summer** and **autumn–early winter droughts** are cited as the most effective (i.e., accessible/safe) periods for field sampling.
  - **Late summer flooding** and **winter flooding** separate these drought windows, during which research is described as "impossible or at best difficult" — indicating these fishes inhabit flow-variable, flash-flood-prone desert streams.
- **Reproductive seasonality**: The **reproductive season** for the target species is identified as **March–June**, while **September–December** is designated the **non-reproductive season**. This distinction was central to the study design, since the extension was requested specifically to allow sampling across *both* reproductive and non-reproductive periods.
- **Personnel/field constraints**: Fieldwork was planned to commence around **April 1, 1987**, aligning start-up with the onset of the spring reproductive-season sampling window.

## Key Findings
- The study was formally titled **"Spikedace and Loachminnow Study"** under Agreement No. G70001 (ASU 810726/C0), a contracted research effort between [[Arizona State University]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
- **Dr. W.L. Minckley** (Department of Zoology, ASU) served as the principal investigator, with **Karp** listed as the Graduate Research Assistant.
- A no-cost time extension was requested, shifting the project end date from **January 8, 1988** to **March 31, 1988**, due to:
  1. Scheduling conflicts (Minckley's sabbatical leave and Karp's teaching contract during spring 1987), delaying the start of fieldwork to approximately April 1, 1987.
  2. The natural hydrological cycle of Arizona streams, which restricts effective fish sampling to **spring–early summer** and **autumn–early winter** drought periods, with flooding intervals rendering fieldwork infeasible.
- The extension was designed to ensure the study could capture data across **both the reproductive (March–June) and non-reproductive (September–December) seasons**, followed by a dedicated **January–March analysis and report-writing period**.
- This letter underscores the importance of **stream discharge patterns and seasonal flow variability** as a governing constraint on field research design for these two federally significant, small-bodied desert fish species — both of which are now recognized as threatened/endangered members of the native [[Gila River]] fish fauna, alongside congeners such as [[Gila Chub]] and other Sonoran Desert cyprinids.
