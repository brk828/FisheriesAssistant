# Genetic Variation in Desert Pupfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #conservation-genetics #mitochondrial-DNA

## Executive Summary

This genetic survey assessed mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) variation in [[Desert Pupfish]] across 11 wild populations (226 specimens) and one captive stock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (33 specimens). Using PCR-SSCP analysis of the mitochondrial ND2 gene and D-loop sequencing, the study identified 13 haplotypes and revealed two reciprocally monophyletic groups. Based on molecular, morphological, and geological evidence, the report concludes that populations from the [[Rio Sonoyta]]/[[Quitobaquito Springs]] area represent a distinct species, *Cyprinodon eremus*, separate from the more widespread *C. macularius* found in the [[Salton Sea]]/[[Colorado River Delta]] region. The study provides detailed genetic management recommendations for this federally endangered species complex.

## Background & Description

[[Desert Pupfish]] historically ranged from headwaters of [[Gila River]] tributaries in southeastern Arizona and northern Sonora, across southern Arizona to the [[Salton Sea]] area of southern California, and southward into the [[Colorado River Delta]] in Mexico. The species was not continuously distributed but was abundant in springs, seeps, sloughs, and backwaters. Remaining natural populations are now much smaller and more fragmented due to interactions with introduced species, habitat losses from reservoir construction, surface-water diversion, and groundwater depletion.

Two subspecies were previously recognized: *C. m. eremus* at [[Quitobaquito Springs]] in [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]], Arizona, and *C. m. macularius* throughout the remainder of the range. The [[Rio Sonoyta]] population in northern Sonora showed intermediate meristic and morphometric characteristics. Prior genetic surveys largely ignored Mexican populations, with only limited allozyme studies by Turner (1983) and Dunham and Minckley (1998) including transplanted populations from the [[Colorado River Delta]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River Delta]] region historically supported an extensive network of periodically interconnected pupfish populations through flooding events. Prior to upstream reservoir construction and water diversion, flooding would have been more frequent, facilitating gene flow among populations. The [[Salton Sea]] and [[Laguna Salada]] occupy topographic sinks that have experienced repeated cycles of flooding and desiccation as the river meandered across its deltaic plain. The last natural flow into the [[Salton Sink]] occurred 1905-1907, creating the present [[Salton Sea]]. [[Laguna Salada]] last received Colorado River flow during high flows in 1983-1984.

Breeding males of *C. macularius* from the [[Salton Sea]] and [[Lower Colorado River Delta]] exhibit "bright yellow to brilliant yellow orange" coloration on the caudal peduncle and caudal fin. Males from [[Quitobaquito Springs]] and [[Rio Sonoyta]] lack these bright yellows, showing instead "yellow to olive-yellow" coloration. This consistent difference in breeding coloration provides additional evidence of evolutionary divergence.

Isolation of the ancestral [[Rio Sonoyta]]/[[Quitobaquito]] pupfish from the remainder of the range likely occurred when lava beds from eruptions of the [[Sierra Pinacate Volcanic Field]] diverted the flow of [[Rio Sonoyta]] southward to the [[Gulf of California]], away from its previous westward course toward the [[Colorado River Delta]]. This probably occurred within the last 100,000 years.

## Key Findings

**Molecular Variation and Phylogenetic Structure:**
- PCR-SSCP analysis identified 13 haplotypes across two ND2 gene fragments (333-bp and 325-bp segments)
- Sequencing of the entire ND2 gene (1047 bases) and 337-bp D-loop segment revealed 17 variable sites across the two ND2 segments (14 transitions, 3 transversions)
- Parsimony analysis produced four equally parsimonious trees (222 steps; CI = 0.89) supporting two reciprocally monophyletic clades
- Overall mtDNA sequence divergence between the two groups is only 0.34%, consistent with relatively recent isolation

**Taxonomic Conclusions:**
- [[Rio Sonoyta]]/[[Quitobaquito]] populations form one monophyletic clade (haplotypes L and M)
- [[Salton Sea]]/[[Lower Colorado River Delta]] populations form a second monophyletic clade (11 haplotypes)
- The two groups warrant recognition as separate species: *Cyprinodon eremus* Miller and Fuiman (Rio Sonoyta/Quitobaquito) and *C. macularius* Baird and Girard (Salton Sea/Colorado River Delta)
- This conclusion is supported by reciprocal monophyly, consistent differences in breeding coloration, meristic/morphometric divergence, and geological history

**Population Genetic Structure:**
- All wild samples contained 2-5 haplotypes except [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH) stock, which was monomorphic for haplotype A
- DNFH monomorphism suggests founder effect or genetic drift during 15 years of captivity (founded 1983 with 280 individuals from [[Santa Clara Slough]])
- No significant difference in haplotype frequencies between *C. eremus* samples from [[Rio Sonoyta]] and [[Quitobaquito]] (despite ~2 km separation)
- Haplotype A predominated (70-95%) in both [[Salton Sea]] and [[Lower Colorado River Delta]] regions of *C. macularius*

**Regional Differentiation in C. macularius:**
- Hierarchical AMOVA revealed significant among-region genetic variance (P < 0.00001)
- F-statistics: 50.6% among regions, 48.9% within samples, 0.4% among samples within regions
- Φ-statistics: 89.3% among regions, 10.6% within samples, 0.2% among samples within regions
- Marginally significant divergence between [[Salton Sea]] area and [[Lower Colorado River Delta]] (P = 0.045-0.061)
- Secondary haplotypes showed regional patterns: B and C occurred in [[Salton Sea]] samples but absent from all [[Lower Colorado River Delta]] samples; F occurred in five of six [[Lower Colorado River Delta]] samples but absent from [[Salton Sea]]
- [[Cerro Prieto]] population deviated from other delta samples in secondary haplotype complement

**Haplotype and Nucleotide Diversity:**
- Haplotype diversity (h) ranged from 0.11 to 0.51 in wild populations (0.00 in DNFH)
- Nucleotide diversity (π) ranged from 0.0002 to 0.0014 in wild populations (0.0000 in DNFH)
- No geographic pattern in diversity measures across wild populations

**Management Recommendations:**

1. **Captive Stock Management:**
   - Maintain at least one captive stock each of *C. eremus* (from Rio Sonoyta and Quitobaquito) and *C. macularius* (from Salton Sea and Lower Colorado River Delta) at state/federal facilities
   - Replace existing DNFH stock with new founders from wild populations due to genetic losses
   - Founding stocks should comprise several hundred individuals from large, genetically diverse natural populations
   - Re-introductions should include 200-300 specimens at 1:1 sex ratio, supplemented by ~10% from wild populations

2. **Genetic Monitoring:**
   - Perform nuclear DNA survey (e.g., microsatellites) to complement mtDNA findings
   - Genotype all founding members using non-lethal DNA sampling
   - Monitor genetic structure every 2-3 generations (every other year for pupfish)
   - Sample size of 30 fish adequate for monitoring established stocks
   - Maintain records on population size, age/size structure, and breeding structure

3. **Gene Flow Management:**
   - Infuse captive stocks with wild fish annually if possible (minimally once per year)
   - Allendorf and Ryman (1987) recommend 10% wild contribution every 2-3 generations
   - Avoid genetic intermixing between [[Salton Sea]] area and [[Lower Colorado River Delta]] populations
   - Within regions, no need to treat local populations as separate genetic units
   - For *C. eremus*, no immediate need for genetic exchange program between [[Rio Sonoyta]] and [[Quitobaquito]]

4. **Population Management Strategy:**
   - Manage to simulate metapopulation dynamics with periodic intermixing
   - Transfers into local populations should be small (≤10% of receiving population)
   - Bias transfers toward neighboring populations
   - Schedule genetic infusion once every few years
   - Manipulate local habitats to provide harsh, unstable conditions favoring pupfish over non-native species

5. **Control System:**
   - Establish three-tier approach for captive and semi-natural stocks as suggested in recovery action plan
   - Provide conservation genetics guidance to stock managers
   - Monitor for genetic contamination from introduced pupfishes in wild populations
