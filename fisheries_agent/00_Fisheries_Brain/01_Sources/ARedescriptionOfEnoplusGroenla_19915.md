# Redescription of Enoplus groenlandicus (Nematoda: Enoplidae)

#nematology #marine-biology #enoplidae

## Executive Summary

This 1970 paper by W. Duane Hope and D. G. Murphy, published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington*, redescribes the marine free-living nematode [[Enoplus Groenlandicus]] (family [[Enoplidae]]). The original [[Ditlevsen 1926]] description, based on two males from the [[Ingolf Expedition]], was brief and had remained the only published account. New specimens from [[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]] sampling programs, identified by [[Howard Sanders]] and [[Roland Wigley]], were confirmed against Ditlevsen's type material. The authors describe the males and females in detail, revise the interpretation of the copulatory apparatus, and extend the known geographic, depth, and temperature range of the species.

## Background & Description

- **Original description:** Ditlevsen (1926) described *E. groenlandicus* from two male specimens. One spiculum is about 0.5 mm long and nearly straight; the other is about half as long and more strongly curved. Ditlevsen proposed that the shorter spiculum, with the [[Gubernacular Complex]], guides the longer one.
- **Type material:** The lectotype (one complete male) and a paralectotype (posterior portion of a second male) are held at the Zoologiske Museum, Copenhagen. Both were rehydrated and remounted in anhydrous glycerine.
- **New material:** Catalogued at the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM). Series include males (USNM 41242–41267, 41314, 41352), females (USNM 41257, 41261, 41268–41313, 41315), and juveniles (USNM 41316, 41320).
- **Preparation methods:** Most specimens were mounted in anhydrous glycerine. Other methods included clearing in Hoyer's medium for lateral views, glycerine jelly for ventral views, micro-dissection of spicula and gubernacula, and polyethylene glycol embedding with 4 µm sections stained by the [[Craig and Wilson Staining Method]].
- **Morphology (general):** A moderately large nematode with a bluntly rounded head and a body tapered in the cervical region. The tail is basically conical with caudal glands. Mid-gut cells contain dark brown pigment. The stoma is partly closed by three thin, hyaline flaps and bears three prominent bifurcated mandibles. The head has six inner labial papillae and ten outer cephalic setae. Amphids are oval and difficult to resolve. Ocelli are absent.
- **Cuticle:** Punctations reach about 2.0 µm in diameter on the neck and anal regions and are less than 1.0 µm on mid-body and terminal tail.
- **Female:** Ovaries paired and reflexed. The tail is conical and tapers uniformly dorsally and ventrally.
- **Male copulatory apparatus:**
  - *Right spiculum:* long and narrow, with a nearly straight proximal half and a distal half bearing a velum with transverse striations.
  - *Left spiculum:* slender proximally, widening mid-length, and tapering to an acute tip, with a medial process near the distal end.
  - *Gubernaculum:* two heavily cuticularized components (right and left) joined by a lightly cuticularized membrane. The left component has three posterior lobes (a, b, c).
  - *Ventromedian supplement:* short, cylindrical, and straight to slightly arched anteriorly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** Before this study, the species had not been recorded south of 61°28'N. It is now known from at least 37°11'N.
- **Depth:** Records range from 49 m on the [[Continental Shelf]] to 3,800 m on the [[Abyssal Plain]] (Table 2 gives 3806 m for one station series).
- **Pressure:** The wide depth range suggests pressure is not a limiting factor. This agrees with [[Sanders, Hessler, and Hampson 1965]] for deep-sea invertebrates in general.
- **Temperature:** Seasonal temperature at the deeper stations ranges from 2.2–3.5 °C and at shallower stations from 4.3–8.9 °C. The known tolerance for the species is approximately 2.0–9.0 °C.
- **Gulf Stream limit:** Shelf specimens occur only north of 41°N. Samples south of 41°N have yielded none. The authors suggest the species may be restricted to colder water and that the [[Gulf Stream]] may limit its southward spread on the shelf.
- **Sediment:** [[Wieser 1959]] suggested sediment properties regulate nematode distribution. Too few stations had adequate sediment data in this study to test this.
- **Station data (Table 2):** Stations span the [[Atlantic Coast]] from about 37°N to 43°N, at depths from about 151 m to 3806 m. Sediment types include sandy silt-clay, very fine sand, fine sand, clayey silt, and sandy silt. Cruises include ALBATROSS II and III, ATLANTIS I and II, DELAWARE 7 and 10, and GOSNOLD 49. Some stations yielded juveniles and females, and one station's total ova count is reported in parentheses.

## Key Findings

1. **Confirmed identity:** Specimens from Woods Hole sampling programs match Ditlevsen's type material, so the species is accurately identified.
2. **Male diagnosis:** *E. groenlandicus* differs from other *Enoplus* species in having no eyespots, a short, nearly straight cylindrical ventromedian supplement, and no semicircular plates on the spicula. It is also unique in having spicula that differ in size and shape.
3. **Convergent evolution:** Dissimilar spicula also occur in *Mesacanthion diplechma* Southern, 1914. The authors regard the resemblance as convergent evolution, since in *Mesacanthion* the right spiculum is shorter than the left. Other examples occur in *Pseudocella wieseri* and *P. panamaense*.
4. **Revised function:** Ditlevsen's idea that the left spiculum and gubernaculum guide the right spiculum is probably wrong. Specimens were found with both spicula protruding side by side, so each may have a similar function.
5. **Spiculum rotation:** The left spiculum rotates about 90° during extrusion, so a single extruded specimen could be misidentified as another species. The right spiculum arches dorsally when extended. The functional significance is unknown, but the authors suggest it may anchor the female during copulation.
6. **Expanded range:** The species extends from above 61°28'N to at least 37°11'N, and from 49 m to about 3800 m depth.
7. **Limiting factors:** Pressure does not appear to limit distribution, while temperature likely does. The authors propose that the Gulf Stream restricts shelf distribution south of 41°N.
8. **Data limitation:** Sediment correlations could not be assessed because too few stations had sediment data.
