## Executive Summary

This 1919 *Science* article by Carl L. Hubbs is a taxonomic proposal rather than an empirical field or laboratory study. Hubbs argues that the [[Crossopterygii]] and [[Dipneusti]] (lungfishes, Dipnoa) together form a natural, monophyletic group distinct from the [[Actinopterygii]] (the ray-finned fishes, comprising Chondrostei, Holostei, and Teleostei). He proposes the new subclass name **Amphibioidei** to formally designate this group within the [[Teleostomi]] (=Osteichthyes), citing both morphological and paleontological evidence for its unity and its transitional similarity to primitive [[Amphibia]]. The paper is grounded partly in a quotation from C. Tate Regan (1909) distinguishing the two major series of bony fishes.

## Background & Description

The paper does not describe an individual species but rather diagnostic characters distinguishing higher fish taxa:

**Teleostomi (Osteichthyes) as a whole**, distinguished from [[Elasmobranchii]] (Selachii) by:
- True scales
- Articulated fin rays
- Membrane bones, including an opercle covering the branchial clefts
- Reduced interbranchial septa
- A developed air-bladder or lung
- Two external nostrils on each side
- Absence of pelvic claspers (mixipterygia)

**Actinopterygian series** (Chondrostei + Teleostei), per Regan (1909):
- Pneumatic (air-bladder) duct opens dorsally or dorsolaterally into the alimentary canal
- Branchiostegals retain a primitive serial arrangement
- Paired fin supports arranged as parallel pterygiophores, each segmented into basal and radial portions, or modified from this plan by concentration/reduction

**Crossopterygian series** (Crossopterygii + Dipneusti, i.e., the proposed **Amphibioidei**):
- Pneumatic duct opening is ventral
- Branchiostegals replaced by a pair of gular plates
- Paired fins more or less lobate, with supports tending toward a biserial arrangement with axial basalia (a fin architecture considered ancestral/transitional toward tetrapod limbs)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper is systematic/morphological in focus and does not address specific habitats, diet, or ecological interactions of particular species. However, it implicitly situates the Amphibioidei (lobe-finned fishes and lungfishes) as an evolutionarily and structurally transitional group between fully aquatic bony fishes and terrestrial vertebrates:

- The proposed subclass is noted for its "similarity and relationship with the primitive [[Amphibia]]," underscoring the evolutionary bridge role of lobe-finned fishes in the transition from aquatic to terrestrial life.
- No specific ocean basin, water chemistry, or trophic ecology is discussed; the scope is comparative anatomy and classification.

## Key Findings

- Hubbs proposes a new taxonomic name, **Amphibioidei**, to unite the [[Crossopterygii]] and [[Dipneusti]] as a monophyletic subclass within the [[Teleostomi]], parallel in rank to the [[Actinopterygii]].
- This grouping is supported by shared derived morphological features (ventral pneumatic duct opening, gular plates instead of branchiostegals, lobate paired fins with biserial/axial basalia support) as well as paleontological evidence (citing Dollo 1895).
- The author explicitly separates this classification question from the wholly extinct and taxonomically uncertain groups such as the [[Arthrodira]] (Arthrognathi), which have sometimes been erroneously allied with Dipneusti or Crossopterygii.
- Hubbs presents a full serial classification scheme placing Amphibioidei as a subclass of Pisces (Superclass), sister to Actinopterygii, and evolutionarily proximate to the Superclass [[Tetrapoda]]:

```
Subphylum Euchorda
 Superclass Pisces
   Class Marsipobranchii
   Class Elasmobranchii
   Class Teleostomi
     Subclass Actinopterygii
       Superorder Chondrostei
       Superorder Holostei
       Superorder Teleostei
     Subclass Amphibioidei
       Superorder Crossopterygii
       Superorder Dipneusti
 Superclass Tetrapoda
   Class Amphibia, etc.
```

- The paper reflects early 20th-century efforts to formalize the phylogenetic distinctness of lobe-finned fishes (Crossopterygii) and lungfishes (Dipneusti) as a coherent evolutionary unit, a classification concept that anticipates later recognition of the [[Sarcopterygii]] as the clade uniting these groups with tetrapods.
