## Executive Summary

This 1984 study by McKeown, Hocutt, Morgan, and Howard employed horizontal starch gel electrophoresis to investigate the phylogenetic and zoogeographic relationships within the **[[Etheostoma Variatum Complex]]**, a group of five closely related, largely allopatric darter species distributed from New York's Allegheny River drainage to the White River system of Arkansas/Missouri. By analyzing 27 gene loci (43 alleles) across seven populations, the researchers identified a clear genetic split between an **Appalachian group** (*E. variatum*, *E. kanawhae*, *E. osburni*) and an **Ozarkian group** (*E. euzonum*, *E. tetrazonum*). The data support a hypothesis of ancestral stock distribution across the Teays and Old Mississippi river systems, subsequently fragmented by **[[Pleistocene Glaciation]]**, leading to isolation in distinct refugia and eventual speciation. Notably, significant genic divergence was also found between geographically separate populations of *E. tetrazonum* (Sac River vs. Big River), suggesting potential taxonomic re-evaluation is warranted.

## Background & Description

The **[[Etheostoma Variatum Complex]]** consists of five species (with one polytypic species having two subspecies), all classified under the tribe Etheostomatini (Percidae):

- **[[Etheostoma Variatum]]** (Variegate Darter): Confined to the Ohio River drainage, from south-central Indiana to the headwaters of the Allegheny and Monongahela systems, excluding the upper Kanawha (New) River.
- **[[Etheostoma Osburni]]** (Finescale Saddled Darter): Endemic to the New River in West Virginia and Virginia; distribution ends upstream near Reed Creek, VA.
- **[[Etheostoma Kanawhae]]** (Kanawha Darter): Endemic to the upper New River and tributaries in North Carolina and Virginia; replaces *E. osburni* upstream, potentially limited by water hardness or interspecific competition.
- **[[Etheostoma Tetrazonum]]** (Missouri Saddled Darter): Endemic to Missouri, restricted to streams draining north off the Ozark Plateau. Two genetically distinct populations were sampled: Big River (Meramec system) and Sac River (Osage system).
- **[[Etheostoma Euzonum]]** (Arkansas Saddled Darter): Replaces *E. tetrazonum* in the southern Ozarks; endemic to the White River drainage. Comprises two subspecies:
    - *E. e. euzonum*: White River system proper (above Batesville, AR).
    - *E. e. erizonum*: Black/Current River system.

Prior to this study, differentiation within the complex had only been assessed via morphometric analysis (Pflieger 1971, Page 1981); this paper represents the first biochemical/genetic assessment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The distribution patterns of the *E. variatum* complex are heavily dictated by historical drainage evolution and physical barriers, linking modern darter biogeography to Pleistocene-era hydrology.

- **Habitat Preference:** These species are generally adapted to clear, upland stream habitats. The lowland, "big river" habitats of the Missouri and Mississippi rivers appear to act as effective dispersal barriers between upland-adapted populations (e.g., separating Meramec and Osage river populations of *E. tetrazonum*).
- **Geologic Barriers:** The **[[Kanawha Falls]]**, in conjunction with the New River gorge cataracts, functions as a "faunal filter," preventing *E. variatum* from moving upstream into territory occupied by *E. osburni* and *E. kanawhae*.
- **Paleohydrology:** The ancestral stock is believed to have been widely distributed across the preglacial **[[Teays River]]** system and the Old Mississippi/Missouri river systems. The advance of Nebraskan and Kansan glacial events led to the formation of **Lake Tight (Teays Lake)**, fragmenting populations into isolated refugia (e.g., southern tributaries of the lower Teays, the Ozark Uplands).
- **Interspecific Interactions:** Zoogeographic overlap analysis suggests that the parapatric distribution of *E. osburni* between *E. variatum* and *E. kanawhae* may be influenced by both physical barriers (Kanawha Falls, water quality changes in the New River Valley) and competitive exclusion with *E. kanawhae*.
- **Comparative Zoogeography:** The disjunct distribution pattern seen in *E. tetrazonum* (Osage vs. Meramec systems) parallels that of *Percina cymatotaenia* (Missouri) and its Kentucky relative *Percina (Odontopholis) sp.*, reinforcing the hypothesis of glacially-driven vicariance shaping Ozarkian ichthyofauna.

## Key Findings

### Genetic Loci and Polymorphism
- Out of 27 loci analyzed (43 alleles total), 9 loci were polymorphic and 18 were monomorphic.
- Key diagnostic loci separating populations included: **Esterase (EST-1)**, **Isocitrate Dehydrogenase (IDH)**, **Phosphoglucose Isomerase (PGI-1, PGI-2)**, and **Phosphoglucomutase (PGM)**.
- **Unique Fixed Alleles:**
    - *E. variatum*: Fixed for GOT-2^A; unique presence of LDH-2^B and PGM^B.
    - *E. kanawhae*: Unique allele EST-1^A.
    - *E. osburni*: Fixed for MDH-2^B.
    - *E. tetrazonum* (Big River): Unique allele PGM^D.
    - *E. tetrazonum* (Sac River): Fixed for EST-1^C; presence of XDH^C.
    - *E. e. erizonum*: Unique allele IDH^A.

### Genetic Distance & Identity (Nei 1972)
- **Closest relationship:** *E. e. euzonum* and *E. tetrazonum* (Big River) — Distance = 0.002 (Identity = 0.998).
- **Most distant relationship:** *E. variatum* and *E. tetrazonum* (Sac River) — Distance = 0.167.
- Notably high divergence was found *within* the nominal species *E. tetrazonum* between the Sac River and Big River populations (D = 0.086), higher than the distance between Big River *E. tetrazonum* and the distinct species *E. euzonum* (D = 0.002–0.014).

### Phylogenetic Structure (Distance Wagner Tree)
- The analysis robustly supported (Cophenetic correlation = 0.997) an early divergence into two major clades:
    1. **Appalachian Clade:** *E. variatum*, *E. kanawhae*, *E. osburni* (with *E. variatum* aligning more closely to *E. kanawhae* than to *E. osburni*).
    2. **Ozarkian Clade:** *E. tetrazonum* and *E. euzonum* populations.

### Gene Diversity (Nei 1973 Component Analysis)
- Single Cluster (All populations): Gene differentiation relative to total population (Gst) = 0.751.
- Two-Cluster split (Appalachian vs. Ozarkian) significantly reduced the average gene diversity between populations (Dst), while diversity *within* populations (Hs) remained stable, indicating the majority of variation is attributable to the East/West (Appalachian/Ozark) split rather than within-population variability.

### Zoogeographic Hypothesis
- The formation of **[[Kanawha Falls]]** is proposed to have isolated the ancestral stock into three components: the lower Teays River, the Upper Teays (excluding Gauley River), and the Old Gauley River.
- This isolation event is hypothesized to have driven the speciation of *E. kanawhae* (Upper Teays) and *E. osburni* (Old Gauley River), while *E. variatum* evolved in southern refugia of the lower Teays/Ohio River drainage.
- Supporting evidence includes the localized distribution of *E. caeruleum* in the New River near the Greenbrier River mouth, mirroring the proposed dispersal route of *E. osburni*.
