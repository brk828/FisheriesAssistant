# Late Pleistocene Desiccation of Lake Victoria and Rapid Evolution of Cichlid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This Science (1996) paper by [[Thomas C Johnson]] and colleagues uses [[Seismic Reflection Profiling]] and [[Piston Coring]] to test whether the more than 300 endemic [[Haplochromine Cichlids]] of [[Lake Victoria]] could have survived in refugia during a late Pleistocene arid period. The authors conclude the lake dried out completely before about 12,400 radiocarbon years before present (14C yr BP), so most cichlid species must have evolved within roughly the last 12,400 years. The document also contains two unrelated fragments: the end of a reference list from an [[Ice Core]] methane study and a separate paper on [[X-Ray Laser]] gain measurements. Both are summarized briefly below.

## Background & Description

Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa and holds the second-largest diversity of endemic cichlids after [[Lake Malawi]]. The lake is young compared with the large lakes of the [[East African Rift]]. It sits on an ancient drainage system that flowed east to west, modified by uplift on the Albert Rift shoulder. Drainage reversal in the Pleistocene created a lake substantially larger than the present one. Prior age estimates ranged from middle-to-late Pleistocene (less than 0.8 million years ago) to early-to-middle Pleistocene (1.6 to 0.8 Ma).

The 1995 survey was the first expedition of the [[International Decade for the East African Lakes]] (IDEAL), conducted in March and April 1995. Reconnaissance seismic profiles from four track lines (late 1980s) and Kullenberg piston cores from seven sites were used. The seismic system used a 16.4 cm³ air gun and a 10-element towed array, with data processed through bandpass filtering and stacking. The deepest core, V95-2P, came from about 67 m depth. A further core was recovered in May 1996 from the 68 m deepest site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake level history:** The lake level varied by about 2 m during the past century in response to rainfall and evaporation. At the end of the last glaciation, between 15,000 and 17,000 years BP, the lake was at least 65 m lower than present. The maximum modern depth is only 69 m, so the question of residual refugia was central.
- **Stratigraphy:** The eastern two-thirds of the basin holds about 40 m of sediment in four main sequences separated by unconformities. The top Holocene unit A is at most 8 m thick and overlies a hard reflector. Two underlying units total about 23 m. A fourth sequence, up to about 35 m thick, lies in the western third, tilted upward to the west.
- **Unit A:** Dark, organic-rich, diatomaceous muds deposited after the lake refilled.
- **Basal soil horizon:** A [[Vertisol]] horizon was identified by magnetic susceptibility, water content, vertical rootlets and prismatic cracking. Pollen in the vertisol is mostly from grasses. Earliest post-desiccation sediment is dominated by typha (cattail) pollen.
- **Sedimentation:** Holocene sedimentation is about 0.7 m per 1000 years. Water content falls from about 90% at the surface to about 50% beneath unit A, implying roughly fivefold compaction. The authors estimate the total sequence could represent nearly 400,000 years of deposition, with about 250,000 years in the tilted western sequence.
- **Moisture budget:** Lake-derived moisture contributes nearly 50% of regional rainfall today, so a dry basin would have removed a major moisture source.

## Key Findings

1. **Complete desiccation:** Seismic and core data show the entire basin, including the deepest site, dried out in the late Pleistocene. The AMS [[Radiocarbon Dating]] of plant macrofossils just above the soil horizon at V95-2P gives 12,400 ± 70 14C yr BP, marking the onset of flooding there.
2. **Lake level fall:** The lake level fell at about 17,300 14C yr BP.
3. **Reworked dates:** Dates of 11,140 and 12,910 years BP are considered too old because of reworked pollen during refilling. The two bottom ages (6,290 ± 60 and 8,180 ± 50 range in the figure data) are less affected.
4. **No refugia:** Because the deepest site was dry, the authors conclude the entire basin was dry seasonally or longer. Satellite lakes could not have persisted, so the cichlid species could not have survived in refugia.
5. **Recent radiation:** The authors infer most endemic species evolved within the past 12,400 years. This is consistent with [[Mitochondrial DNA]] evidence that the Lake Victoria species flock is less than 200,000 years old and perhaps as young as 14,000 years, monophyletic or derived from a single ancestor. It is also consistent with rapid [[Speciation]] of cichlids in Lake Malawi.
6. **Rate implication:** If correct, the rate of evolution of Lake Victoria's cichlids would be the fastest ever recorded for such a large number of vertebrate species, a point the authors frame as an [[Adaptive Radiation]] tied to lacustrine instability rather than a buffered ecosystem.

**Ice core fragment (methane):** The page-1 text, cited to a [[Methane]] study, argues that higher temperatures would raise [[OH Radical]] formation and methane destruction. It concludes that interstadial methane increases suggest source strength probably did not decrease and may have risen more than the concentration record shows. Calibration notes state that GRIP concentrations were lowered by 2% relative to GISP2 data. The Grenoble and Bern laboratory scales appear 2.0% and 1.0% higher, respectively, than the [[NOAA CMDL Methane Scale]].

**X-ray laser study:** A separate Science paper reports simultaneous measurement of local gain and electron density in a neon-like yttrium [[X-Ray Laser]] at 155 Å, using a 3-cm probe target and a 2-mm amplifier target driven by the Nova laser.

- Measured average gain (~5.3 cm⁻¹) is lower than calculated values. Two-dimensional hydrodynamic models with refraction predict 10 to 11 cm⁻¹ for a few-centimeter XRL.
- Gain measurements resolved features at about 1.3 μm using [[Interferometry]] and a bandpass of 155 ± 4 Å.
- The measured local gain agrees with atomic models but is spatially inhomogeneous. The authors argue this inhomogeneity explains the low spatial coherence and part of the gap between observed and simulated gains.
- The XRL pulse lasts about 75 ps, with a line focus at about 1.5 × 10¹⁴ W/cm² (OCR-corrected exponent).
