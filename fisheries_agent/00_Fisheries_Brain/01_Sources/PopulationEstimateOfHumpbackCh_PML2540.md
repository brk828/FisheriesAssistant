# Colorado River Recovery Program FY2004 Annual Report: Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Westwater Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY2004 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 132) covers the second year of a three-year population estimate of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Westwater Canyon]], [[Colorado River]], Utah. The study also tracks [[Roundtail Chub]] and unidentified subadult [[Gila]] captured alongside them. Three sampling passes were completed between October 11 and November 15, 2004, at four sites, using [[Trammel Netting]], [[Electrofishing]], and [[Hoop Netting]]. The work was led by [[Paul Badame]] and [[Julie A. Jackson]] of the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]], Moab Field Station.

The project addresses two objectives: estimating the adult humpback chub population (≥200 mm total length) and estimating recruitment of naturally produced subadults (150–199 mm). Catches were 290 adult humpback chub and 817 adult roundtail chub. Electrofishing catch rates in 2003 and 2004 were among the highest recorded since monitoring began. The report is ongoing, with budget and data-submission status also noted.

## Background & Description

**Species of concern.** The [[Recovery Program]] recently set recovery goals for the endangered humpback chub. These goals depend partly on maintaining several populations, including the Westwater Canyon population. Identifying, maintaining, and monitoring that population requires accurate population estimates.

**Study objectives:**
1. Estimate the population of adult humpback chub (≥200 mm) in Westwater Canyon.
2. Determine mean estimated recruitment of naturally produced subadult humpback chub (150–199 mm).

**Study framework.** The work falls under the Colorado River Action Plan (Mainstem), section V.C, "Estimate humpback chub populations," subsection V.C.2, Westwater. The study is in its second of three years.

**Sampling sites.** Four sites were sampled, listed here from upstream to downstream by river mile (RM):
- [[Miners Cabin]] (RM 124)
- [[Upper Cougar]] (RM 122.5)
- [[Lower Cougar]] (RM 121.5)
- [[Hades Bar]] (RM 120)

**Field protocol.** Sites were sampled for six or seven nights in total. Each site was sampled for two nights, except Upper Cougar on the second pass, which was sampled for one night. Trammel nets (eight to nine per site) were set in the late afternoon, checked every 1.5–2 hours, and pulled around midnight. They were reset before dawn and pulled from mid-morning to late afternoon. Electrofishing was conducted before nets were set and after they were pulled around midnight. Hoop nets were set each afternoon and pulled the next morning. Every chub was identified to species, scanned for a [[PIT Tag]] (tagged if needed), measured for total and standard length, weighed, and had its dorsal and anal fin rays counted before release.

**Species context.** Humpback chub and roundtail chub share the Westwater Canyon reach. Subadult Gila could not be reliably identified to species in the field, so these fish were grouped as Gila. The report notes that they "were too small to reliably identify or displayed characteristics of both species."

## Key Findings

### Catch totals

- **Adult humpback chub:** 290 captured.
- **Adult roundtail chub:** 817 captured.
- **Additional subadults:** 24 humpback chub, 99 roundtail chub, and 46 unidentified Gila.
- **Passes in 2004:** 11 humpback chub and 56 roundtail chub were recaptured within the year.

### Size structure

- **Humpback chub (all methods):** mean total length 257.4 mm (range 124–394 mm).
- **Roundtail chub (all methods):** mean total length 255 mm (range 112–374 mm).
- **Subadults (46 Gila-group fish):** mean total length 118.3 mm (range 46–200 mm).

Length-frequency histograms for humpback chub and roundtail chub are presented in the source (Figures 2 and 3).

### Sampling effort

- **Trammel netting:** approximately 1,823.66 hours.
- **Electrofishing:** 4 hours total.
- **Hoop netting:** approximately 178 hours.

### Catch per unit effort (CPUE)

- CPUE values for 2004 were similar to 2003 for each species and method, except that Gila captures were lower in 2004.
- Electrofishing catch rates for 2003 and 2004 were among the highest the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources has recorded since monitoring began in Westwater Canyon.
- Electrofishing was productive in the afternoon for collecting juvenile chub.

### Recapture rates

- Of 290 adult humpback chub captured, 41 had been PIT tagged in previous years. 11 were recaptured within 2004.
- Of 817 adult roundtail chub captured, 48 had been PIT tagged in previous years. 56 were recaptured within 2004.
- Long-term recapture rates in 2004 were similar to the 1998–2003 period for both species. Long-term recaptures were lower than in 2000 and similar to 2003.
- Within-year recaptures of humpback chub were similar to 1998–2003. Within-year recaptures of roundtail chub were higher than in 1998–2003, the highest proportion in the last five years of sampling.

Long-term recapture history (Table 2) covers 1998, 1999, 2000, 2003, and 2004. Roundtail chub adult captures rose from 389 in 1998 to 817 in 2004. Humpback chub adult captures ranged from 279 to 488 over the same years.

### Trends

- Roundtail chub numbers appear to be increasing.
- Humpback chub numbers have remained steady since 1999.

### Recommendations

1. Conduct electrofishing during the first two passes of 2005.
2. Conduct electrofishing in the afternoon before nets are set, as well as in the evening after nets are pulled.
3. Use [[Radiotelemetry]] to track humpback and roundtail chub, which may reveal movement patterns not yet documented.
