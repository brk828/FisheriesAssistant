# Water Quality Study of the Burro Creek Watershed, Arizona (1984)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report from the [[Arizona Department of Health Services]] (ADHS), prepared under a contract with the [[U.S. Bureau of Land Management]] (USBLM), documents an intensive water quality survey of the Burro Creek drainage from February 1982 to February 1983. The survey characterized water chemistry and tested suitability for drinking water, native fisheries, livestock watering, riparian habitat, and recreation, and examined effects of grazing and past and present copper mining. Thirty-five sampling sites were established, with limited sediment and fish and frog tissue sampling for heavy metals.

The study found that Burro and Francis Creeks, fed by [[Thermal Springs]], were of excellent quality, while the [[Hillside Mine]] complex on [[Boulder Creek]] was the dominant pollution source in the watershed. Grazing showed little demonstrable effect on water quality.

## Background & Description

The Burro Creek watershed lies north and west of Bagdad, Arizona. Burro Creek flows about 57 miles (92 km) southwest toward the Big Sandy River, receiving [[Francis Creek]], [[Boulder Creek]], and Conger Creek. Boulder Creek has [[Copper Creek]] and Wilder Creek as major tributaries. Elevation falls from 5,310 ft (1,618 m) at the source to 1,510 ft (460 m) at the Big Sandy confluence. Only Burro and Francis Creeks flow permanently; the remainder are ephemeral or intermittent.

The watershed is highly mineralized, with copper, molybdenum, and uranium enrichment. Of more than six historic commercial mines, only the [[Cyprus-Bagdad Copper Corporation]] operation near the mouth of Copper Creek remains. Vegetation ranges from semidesert grassland and Sonoran Desert scrub to riparian cottonwood, sycamore, and willow stands. Two plants of federal concern occur: Burro Creek cliffrose (a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] candidate for endangered status) and Bigelow's wild onion (a BLM sensitive species).

Five native fish species were observed, including the State-listed [[Roundtail Chub]]. Other listed vertebrates include [[Bald Eagle]], [[Desert Tortoise]], and [[Black Hawk]]. Permitted sampling used standard APHA methods, with quality control samples comprising 30 of the total (19%).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Thermal springs and flow.** Springs near Francis Creek supplied nearly all base flow. Spring water at site BC037 ran 27 to 32 °C with a discharge of about 0.25 cfs. Cyprus-Bagdad pumped roughly 1,000 gpm from the spring aquifer, which often equaled or exceeded downstream flow in Francis Creek.

**Productivity.** Burro and Francis Creeks were autotrophic in summer. Dissolved oxygen reached 165% saturation at BC017 in June, and periphyton (notably *Cladophora glomerata*) drove high oxygen and pH. Boulder Creek's intermittent pools showed summer oxygen deficits below 6.0 mg/l.

**Nutrients.** Spring-derived nitrate, roughly 1.0 to 1.25 mg/l N at BC037, is of natural origin, since the watershed lacks intensive agriculture. Phosphorus was scarce; total nitrogen to total phosphorus ratios suggested phosphorus limitation downstream.

**Mine drainage.** The Hillside tailings seep (BC028.5) reached pH 2.6 with 4,606 mg/l sulfate and 416 mg/l iron during summer and fall 1982. Metal precipitation and [[Arsenic]] coprecipitation with ferric iron attenuated these loads within about one mile. Copper Creek and Black Mesa Gulch continued to supply sulfate, TDS, and metals to Boulder Creek.

**Biota and food chain.** Tissue metals were higher in fish (*Gila robusta*) and frogs (*Rana pipiens*) downstream of Hillside and Copper Creek. Biomagnification appeared limited for copper, zinc, manganese, and arsenic, while cadmium remained a concern.

## Key Findings

1. **Francis Creek and upper Burro Creek are of excellent quality.** No State standard violations were recorded. Heavy metals were largely absent, and a single fecal coliform count of 540 cfu/100 ml at BC031 was judged spatially and temporally isolated.

2. **Thermal springs are essential to flow and nutrient supply.** Francis Creek contributed about 85% of downstream Burro Creek flow during dry months and about 11.8% in February 1983. Spring nitrogen flux was estimated at 2,306 kg per year.

3. **Grazing showed no demonstrable adverse effect on water quality.** Suspended sediment and nutrient levels were low and comparable to other desert streams. Damage appeared limited to the terrestrial riparian zone.

4. **The Hillside Mine complex is the most important pollution source in the drainage.** Two seeps (BC028.5 and the adit seep BC028.2) produced acidic, metal-laden discharges. Effects were local, about one mile in extent during low base flow, and seasonal, being most severe in summer and fall.

5. **Boulder Creek is poor in summer and better in winter.** Natural fluoride, sodium, and TDS, combined with mine sources, compromised suitability. Sulfate at BC027 exceeded 90 mg/l only once.

6. **Copper Creek remains a concern despite improvements.** Cyprus-Bagdad's efforts reduced surface flow to under 0.5 cfs, down from 2.2 cfs average in 1979. Monitor well water (BC042) carried dissolved arsenic up to 0.141 mg/l and was recommended unfit for drinking.

7. **Lower Burro Creek is affected by Boulder Creek but not impaired.** Fluoride and sulfate rose above recommended drinking limits during summer. Sediment copper reached 305 mg/kg at BC013. Frog tissue copper and zinc were elevated but lower than near the sources.

8. **The USBLM campground had no measurable effect on water quality.** Nitrate peaked at 1.2 mg/l at BC011 in October, likely from springs, not septic sources. Fecal coliform peaked at 40 cfu/100 ml.

9. **Radionuclides warrant further study.** Gross alpha and beta activity, radium-226, and radium-228 exceeded State standards in the Hillside seeps. Uranium reached about 4 mg/l at BC100. Radiological contamination in Boulder Creek during low summer flow remains unquantified.

10. **Recommendations include:** a formal water withdrawal agreement between USBLM and Cyprus-Bagdad to protect Francis Creek flow; continued monitoring of Copper Creek and the old tailings; posting the Copper Creek monitor well as unfit for drinking; and further radiological and detailed biological surveys of Boulder Creek.
