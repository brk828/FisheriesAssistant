## Executive Summary
This document is **not a primary fish science paper** but a regulatory memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, amending a prior Biological Opinion issued under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (1973, as amended). The consultation concerns the reassignment of Central Arizona Project (CAP) water rights from Cottonwood Water Works, Inc., and Camp Verde Water System, Inc., to the City of Scottsdale. The amendment revises **Reasonable and Prudent Measure (RPM) 5**, replacing a public education/water conservation funding requirement with a mandate for the water companies to acquire surface water rights or fund recharge projects in the **Verde Valley**, Arizona. The underlying conservation concern is the cumulative impact of economic development, population growth, and water withdrawal on riparian habitat and associated threatened and endangered species dependent on the [[Verde River]] system.

No direct morphological, taxonomic, or population data on fish species are presented in this excerpt; the document instead addresses water resource management as a regulatory mechanism for habitat protection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no specific species are named in this excerpt, the consultation implies the presence of federally listed **threatened and endangered species** (likely including native Verde River fishes such as [[Gila Chub]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], or [[Razorback Sucker]], though not explicitly named in this text) that depend on:

- **Riparian habitat** along the Verde River and its tributaries in the Verde Valley
- **Surface water flows** potentially threatened by municipal water withdrawals and CAP water reassignment
- **Water quality and quantity** sensitive to cumulative effects of economic development and population growth in the region

The ecological rationale for the RPM is rooted in maintaining sufficient surface water and reducing groundwater pumping impacts on river baseflow, which sustains aquatic and riparian ecosystems critical to the survival of listed species in the [[Verde Valley]] watershed.

## Key Findings
- The original **RPM 5** (from the March 30, 1998 Biological Opinion) required Reclamation to mandate that Cottonwood Water Works and Camp Verde Water System set aside **1% (~$20,000)** of a Water Trust Fund for public education on cumulative impacts to riparian habitat and threatened/endangered species.
- This RPM was found **legally inconsistent** with the Arizona Department of Water Resources' administration of the Water Trust Fund and was therefore **rescinded and replaced**.
- The **amended RPM** requires Reclamation to mandate that the two water companies **expend at least $75,000** from the Water Trust Fund toward **acquisition of surface water rights or recharge projects** in the Verde Valley.
- Funds may be expended over **10 years** from the date of CAP assignment confirmation, with a possible **5-year extension** if approved jointly by Reclamation and FWS.
- The amendment illustrates a shift in conservation strategy — from **public outreach/education funding** to **direct water resource acquisition/recharge**, a more tangible mechanism for offsetting the hydrological impacts of water reassignment on the Verde River ecosystem.
- The consultation reaffirms compliance obligations under **16 U.S.C. § 1531 et seq.** (Endangered Species Act) and emphasizes continued interagency coordination between the **Bureau of Reclamation** and **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service**.
