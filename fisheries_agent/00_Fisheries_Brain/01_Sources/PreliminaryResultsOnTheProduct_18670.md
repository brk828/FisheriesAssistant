# Preliminary Results on the Production and Spawning of White Catfish in Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #aquaculture #pond-management

## Executive Summary

This 1960 study by E. E. Prater and H. S. Swingle at [[Auburn University]] Agricultural Experiment Station evaluated [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*) as a commercial pondfish species. Experiments conducted in 1959-1960 demonstrated that white catfish production rates, feed conversion, and survival compared favorably with [[Channel Catfish]], with the added advantage of superior tolerance to muddy water and low oxygen conditions during harvest. Spawning experiments in 1960 successfully produced fry in pond environments using wooden spawning boxes, with spawning occurring from May 11 to June 16 at water temperatures between 68°F and 80°F.

## Background & Description

[[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*) fingerlings were obtained from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] hatchery at Hoffman, North Carolina in 1958. The species was evaluated as an alternative to [[Channel Catfish]] for commercial pond production. Initial experiments focused on production efficiency, survival rates, and feed conversion, followed by spawning trials in 1960.

Physical characteristics noted include larger heads compared to channel catfish (44.4% dressing loss versus 40%), light pink meat color, and darker coloration in fry (dark brown except belly by 4 days post-hatch). Individual eggs were slightly larger than channel catfish eggs, laid in similar masses.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Requirements:**
- Ponds ranging from 0.25 to 1.5 acres
- Spawning occurred at surface water temperatures 68°F to 80°F (early morning measurements)
- Optimal spawning ceased when early morning temperatures reached 80°F or higher
- Eggs hatched in 6-7 days at water temperatures averaging 80°F

**Environmental Tolerance:**
White catfish demonstrated exceptional survival under adverse conditions including muddy water, hot weather, and low oxygen concentrations during harvest operations. Fish survived even when water was completely drained and individuals were picked from mud by hand—a significant advantage over channel catfish, which suffer high mortality when gills become mud-covered.

**Spawning Behavior:**
- Natural spawning sites: wooden boxes (42" × 15" × 9"), muskrat burrows, or self-excavated holes in pond banks
- Males exhibited "fanning" behavior similar to channel catfish but were less aggressive when disturbed
- Spawning season at [[Auburn, Alabama]]: May 11 to June 16

## Key Findings

**Production Performance (1959 experiments):**

*Pond E-4 (202 days, March 25-October 13):*
- Stocking rate: 2,000 fingerlings/acre (192.3 lbs)
- Net production: 1,558.4 lbs/acre
- Survival: 97.9%
- Feed conversion (S): 2.0
- Gain: 7.7 lbs/acre/day
- Cost: 12.2 cents/lb harvestable fish
- Total feed: 3,155 lbs

*Pond T-2 (205 days, May 4-November 25):*
- Stocking rate: 3,000 fingerlings/acre (351.0 lbs)
- Net production: 2,187.0 lbs/acre
- Survival: 98.3%
- Feed conversion (S): 1.7
- Gain: 10.7 lbs/acre/day
- Cost: 10.4 cents/lb harvestable fish
- Total feed: 3,735 lbs

*Pond E-2 (99 days, July 14-October 21):*
- Stocking rate: 1,058 fingerlings/acre
- Net production data incomplete due to shortened experimental period

**Overall Production Metrics:**
- Average mortality: 1.8%
- Feed conversion factors: 1.7 to 2.0 (comparable to [[Channel Catfish]])
- Fertilization: one application of 200 lbs 8-8-0 equivalent per acre
- Supplemental feeding: [[Auburn No. 2 Pellets]] daily except Sundays

**Spawning Success (1960 experiments):**
- Brood stock: 20 pairs held in 0.25-acre pond (males 31.3 lbs total, females 22.7 lbs total)
- Individual weights: females 1.8-2.0 lbs, males 2.2-2.8 lbs
- Fecundity: 2,500 to 4,000 fry per 2-lb female
- Successful spawning in three experimental setups: open ponds with boxes, wire pens with boxes, and natural sites
- Total documented spawns: 9 successful egg masses across all treatments
- Eggs hatched successfully both in situ (spawning boxes) and in [[Paddlewheel Hatching Trough]]

**Market Quality:**
- Dressing percentage: 55.6% (44.4% loss)
- Meat color: light pink
- Taste quality: equal to channel catfish
- Consumer acceptance: predicted to equal channel catfish

**Sport Fishing Potential:**
Limited angling trials using live redworms as bait failed to catch white catfish, while adjacent channel catfish were readily caught. Authors noted additional research needed on appropriate baits and fishing methods.

**Commercial Advantages:**
1. Production metrics comparable to channel catfish
2. Superior survival during harvest operations
3. Exceptional tolerance to muddy water and low oxygen
4. High survival rates (>97%)
5. Efficient feed conversion
6. Successful pond spawning capability
