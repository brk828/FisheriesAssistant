# Spikedace (Meda fulgida)

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1986 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regulatory correspondence notifying the Hooker Dam Association of the federal listing of the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) as a threatened species under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. The letter references the July 1, 1986 Federal Register publication (51 FR 23769) that finalized the species' threatened status while deferring critical habitat designation to a subsequent rule (due by June 1987). The correspondence outlines the species' extremely restricted range in the American Southwest and identifies the primary anthropogenic threats driving its decline, framed within the context of proposed dam construction (Hooker Dam) affecting the [[Gila River]] system.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological description is provided in this source document; the text is regulatory/administrative rather than a taxonomic or morphological study.
- Species identification is limited to the scientific binomial *Meda fulgida*, common name "spikedace," within the family [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution (as of 1986):** Restricted to a small number of isolated freshwater locations in the southwestern United States:
  - [[Aravaipa Creek]], Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona
  - Sections of the [[Gila River]] upstream from Red Rock, in Grant and Catron Counties, New Mexico
  - A small section of [[Eagle Creek]], Greenlee County, Arizona
  - A portion of the upper [[Verde River]], Yavapai County, Arizona
- **Habitat type:** Desert river/stream riffle habitats, dependent on natural flow regimes and riparian integrity.
- **Population status:** Limited distribution and small population numbers across all known localities, contributing to high vulnerability.
- **Co-occurring threats:** Presence of introduced exotic predatory and competitive fish species within its habitat range, exacerbating population decline through predation and resource competition.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory action:** *Meda fulgida* was formally determined to be a **threatened species** under the Endangered Species Act via Federal Register notice 51 FR 23769, effective July 1, 1986.
- **Critical habitat designation** was proposed but postponed, with a separate final rule anticipated no later than June 1987.
- **Legal protections triggered by listing:**
  - Mandatory Section 7 consultation: federal agencies must consult with the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] on any action they fund, authorize, or carry out that may affect the species' continued existence (directly relevant to the proposed [[Hooker Dam]] project).
  - Prohibition on intentional "take" of the species, with limited exceptions permitted under applicable state laws and regulations.
- **Primary identified threats to survival:**
  - Habitat destruction from dam construction
  - Channel alteration and channel downcutting
  - Riparian habitat destruction
  - Water diversion
  - Groundwater pumping
  - Introduction and spread of exotic predatory/competitive fish species
- **Administrative contact:** Further inquiries directed to the Service's Endangered Species Office in Albuquerque, New Mexico, with Gerald Burton named as the responsible Endangered Species Biologist.
- **Broader significance:** This listing directly implicates the environmental review process for the proposed [[Hooker Dam]] on the [[Gila River]], situating *Meda fulgida* conservation at the center of a water resource development conflict in New Mexico.
