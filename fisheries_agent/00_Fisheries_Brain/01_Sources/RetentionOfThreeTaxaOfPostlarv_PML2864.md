# Retention of Three Taxa of Postlarval Fishes in an Intensively Flushed Tidal Estuary, Cape Fear River, North Carolina

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sciaenidae #paralichthyidae

## Executive Summary

This Fishery Bulletin study (Vol. 78, No. 2, 1980) examines how postlarval [[Spot]], [[Atlantic Croaker]], and [[Flounders]] remain within the [[Cape Fear River Estuary]] despite strong tidal flushing. Fixed, stratified plankton nets were fished at two upriver locations (buoys 32 and 50) across depth, photoperiod, and tidal direction. The authors found that each taxon showed distinct behavioral responses to photoperiod and tide, and that these responses retain postlarvae in a preferred zone near the salt boundary and tidal creeks. Spot and flounders moved toward the surface at night, which favors lateral movement into marshes via net nontidal flow. Atlantic croaker stayed near the bottom and accumulated at the estuary head.

## Background & Description

Mechanisms by which larval fishes are recruited to, and concentrated in, estuaries remained poorly understood, partly because of high sampling variability. [[Graham 1972]] used fixed nets to study larval [[Atlantic Herring]] in the Sheepscot Estuary, Maine. That gear sampled the full water column and filtered larger volumes, reducing variability and allowing a depth-by-tide behavioral mechanism to be inferred.

Earlier work often sampled only in daytime and ignored diel migration. [[Pearcy and Richards 1962]] proposed lower-layer transport in the Mystic Estuary, and [[Haven 1957]] and [[Sandifer 1975]] described net nontidal transport in lower layers in Chesapeake Bay. The authors proposed that retention is species-specific and may combine three elements described by [[Bousfield 1955]]:

1. Diel change in vertical distribution.
2. Use of residual (nontidal) drift seaward in the upper layer and landward along the bottom.
3. Tide-dependent changes in behavior.

The study area lies in the Cape Fear River estuary, about 33°N, which is 1.6–3.6 km wide. Tidal creeks and shallow open-water areas add an estimated 2,286 ha of suitable nursery habitat. The river has a two-layer circulation system, and the salt boundary position shifts seasonally.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study design and gear.** A modified version of the Graham (1972) gear was used. Each net was a 0.5 m plankton net with a stainless steel cod end, mounted on orienting vanes and fished on a line with a tag line for easy retrieval. Surface, mid-depth, and bottom nets were deployed. Bottom nets were held about 1 m above the substrate. Flowmeters (TSK) were fitted to nets, and nets were dyed deep brown to reduce avoidance. Sampling took place at two transects and three stations per transect (east shoal, main channel, west shoal). Nets were fished for about 40 minutes per set.

**Sampling dates and conditions.** Sampling occurred on 14–16 March, 5–6 April, and 11–12 April 1978. In March, spot were absent and only two Atlantic croaker were caught at buoy 50 because the salt front was below that point and freshwater flow was high. The salt front later returned to about 6 km above buoy 50, and all taxa except one were significantly more abundant upstream (P < 0.05).

**Processing.** Samples were preserved in 6% buffered formalin. Target taxa were counted, and standard length (SL) was measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the hypural plate. Subsampling was used when a sample held more than 100 individuals of one species. Flounders were counted but not measured.

**Statistics.** Nonorthogonal factorial ANOVA tested main effects of site, photoperiod, and tidal direction. Data were log-transformed (log(x + 1)) to meet homoscedasticity requirements. Post hoc Bonferroni t-tests (α = 0.05) were applied. Length-frequency differences were tested with Pearson chi-square tests.

## Key Findings

**Diel behavior**
- Spot and Atlantic croaker abundance varied across depths, and both were more abundant near the bottom on the shoals and in the channel, with little difference between buoys.
- Flounder catches responded to photoperiod consistently. Except for 14–15 March, flounders were more abundant at night, and surface orientation at night was notable for flounders and spot.
- Atlantic croaker remained bottom-oriented at all times, so it accumulated in greater numbers in the upper estuary.

**Tidal response**
- Spot showed a clear tide-by-depth interaction. On flood tides, depth distribution was fairly uniform. On ebb tides, bottom and mid-depth concentrations often exceeded surface values.
- Atlantic croaker responded to tide, though less clearly because of its strong bottom orientation.
- Flounders showed little tidal response in the main effect results, although their pattern was similar to other species in the stratified means.
- Water tended to move upriver on the eastern shoal and return on the western side. The authors suggested this may indicate a large-scale circulation pattern for postlarval populations.

**Length distribution**
- Larger (older) fish tended to accumulate upriver near buoy 50. The authors caution that larger fish were likely undersampled because gear efficiency drops after about 30 mm SL.
- Length distributions differed significantly between buoys in both March and April, with larger fish predominating upstream.

**Conceptual model**
- Spot and flounders retained in the estuary by migrating toward the surface at night, using net nontidal flow to move laterally into marshes and shoals.
- The three taxa stayed within preferred zones by dropping to the bottom on ebb tides and moving upriver by responding to currents on flood tides.
- The authors suggest the Cape Fear River estuary may serve as a primary nursery for these taxa, with retention occurring despite intensive tidal flow.

**Limitations**
- Some flowmeters fouled with detritus and algae, requiring use of inside and outside readings to estimate volume filtered. Flowmeter estimates were less reliable in some samples.
- Diel migration did not fully explain the patterns, since flood and ebb tides differed even over 24-hour periods.
- The three-way interaction tests (photoperiod × depth × tide) had low statistical power, and the authors note that the log transformation may have contributed to the lack of significance.
