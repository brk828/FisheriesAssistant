# Federal Fish and Wildlife Permit Renewal Application: Endangered Species Research at Arizona State University, 1998–2000

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This package is a renewal application from the [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) Department of Biology to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Region 2 office. It seeks continued authorization under Federal Fish and Wildlife Permit PRT 797129, which was effective 8/9/96 to 8/9/98. The applicant requests coverage from 8/10/98 through 12/31/2000. The request covers research, monitoring, captive breeding, and genetic sampling on endangered fishes and one salamander. The cover letter is dated 15 July 1998 and signed by Professor and Chair James P. Collins. The application fee was $25.00.

Research on listed species at ASU has run since formal listing began in 1967. At least 20 faculty, staff, and graduate students in the Biology Department remain involved in recovery-oriented work.

## Background & Description

The applicant is an educational and research institution that trains students in biology and conservation. It performs basic and applied research funded both independently and by government. The permit is requested for take of endangered and threatened species. Principal investigators named in the materials are J. P. Collins, P. W. Hedrick, M. E. Douglas, T. E. Dowling, and W. L. [[Minckley]]. Work is primarily in Arizona, with project-specific activity allowed in New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Texas. The applicant holds state permits as required by individual projects.

Species excluded from this request are threatened taxa governed by special rules that require only state permit compliance. These are [[Apache Trout]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Yaqui Catfish]].

The application cites documents "ON FILE" with the Albuquerque USFWS office, including facility photos and animal-keeper qualifications. The applicant asks to be notified if this reliance is not acceptable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Taxa under study (1998–99).** The application lists ten taxa, though the text refers to nine species:
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), Grand Canyon, Colorado and Little Colorado rivers
- [[Bonytail]] (*G. elegans*), Colorado River mainstem; reintroduced in the Gila River Basin
- [[Yaqui Chub]] (*G. purpurea*), San Bernardino/Leslie Creek National Wildlife Refuge and West Turkey Creek, where it was reintroduced
- [[Virgin River Chub]] (*G. seminuda*), Virgin River (AZ-NV-UT)
- [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), Virgin River
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), Lake Powell; reintroduced in the Gila River Basin
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), Colorado River mainstem; reintroduced in the Gila River Basin
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius* and *C. m. eremus*), reintroduced in artificial refugia and at [[Quitobaquito Springs]]
- [[Sonoran Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* and *P. o. sonoriensis*), 11 natural sites in the Gila River Basin and San Bernardino NWR
- [[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]] (*Ambystoma tigrinum stebbinsi*), San Rafael Valley, Arizona

**Life-history constraints.** The applicant notes that mortality varies by species. Pupfish and topminnows are short-lived, with maximum lifespans rarely beyond one year (sometimes 18–24 months in captivity). Female topminnows typically bear fewer than 20 young per brood at 3–4 week intervals, spring through autumn. Pupfish females produce 100–300 eggs across a breeding season. Stocks reach space or resource limits in about a year.

**Captive stocks.** Two pupfish stocks are stable and self-sustaining. One came from Mexico and has been held since 1976. The other came from Quitobaquito Spring and has been held since 1988. Broodstocks of Sonoran topminnow derived from wild fish collected in 1993–94 have reached carrying capacities of roughly 300–500 fish per 300-gallon tank. Mortality from cannibalism and density-dependent reproduction balances recruitment. No intervention to reduce mortality is planned.

## Key Findings

**Permit scope and take requests**
- **Humpback chub:** no purposeful take. Requested: unlimited capture for tagging and release, and five inadvertent mortalities.
- **Bonytail:** no purposeful take. Requested: unlimited capture for tagging or transfer; non-lethal sampling of up to 20 individuals per locality; five inadvertent mortalities.
- **Yaqui chub:** 30 specimens per locality from reintroduced populations for life-history work; 10 inadvertent losses per locality.
- **Razorback sucker:** the most extensive request. It includes unlimited capture of adults for tagging, handling and transfer of larvae from [[Lake Mohave]], and take, spawning and release of up to 500 adult females and 1,500 males. It also includes non-lethal sampling of up to 30 adults per locality and 10 inadvertent losses per locality.
- **Desert pupfish:** unlimited tagging take, fin clips from 40 individuals per locality (*C. m. eremus*), vouchers of up to 10 per locality, and 25 inadvertent losses per locality.
- **Sonoran topminnow:** vouchers of up to 20 per locality, maintenance of research stocks, destruction of unsuitable progeny, and 25 inadvertent losses per locality.
- **Sonoran tiger salamander:** up to 1,500 eggs, 200 larvae and 25 adults per locality; up to 30 enclosures at each of 10 localities; and inadvertent losses of 150 eggs, 20 larvae and five adults per locality.
- **Remaining taxa** (virgin chub, woundfin, Colorado squawfish): no purposeful take. Requested only to accept tissues and hold specimens from permitted colleagues, plus five inadvertent losses per locality.

**Reported activity, 1997–98 (Attachment I)**
- The report covers 1/12/97 to 6/30/98. The cover letter gives 12/1/97 to 6/30/98 for the same report, so the start date is inconsistent in the source.
- **Humpback chub:** fin clips from 64 adults at the Colorado–Little Colorado confluence (5/8–16/98). Fin clips from 44 fish were also taken in Grand Canyon National Park. Archival holdings include 78 larval and juvenile chubs, in 75 lots.
- **Razorback sucker:** 34 wild adults were PIT-tagged or read at Lake Mohave (3/16–21/98; 13 already tagged, 21 untagged). Seven repatriated adults were also handled. ASU personnel took 780 larvae during the 1997–98 spawning season. A cooperative repatriation effort retained 412 larvae for genetic analysis.
- **Bonytail:** one 48 cm male was taken from Lake Mohave on 5/21/98 and transferred to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery. The archive holds 113 individuals from several sources.
- **Yaqui chub:** about 150 fish were captured and released at reintroduction sites in the West Turkey Creek watershed. Twenty vouchers were retained from two sites.
- **Quitobaquito pupfish:** 353 fish were marked on 2/11/98. About 3,567 individuals were later captured on five dates through June 1998, with no mortalities recorded.
- **Yaqui topminnow:** 39 individuals from North Pond and Tule Spring were received in mid-June 1998 for genetic analysis and stock building. One died; the rest are reproducing.
- **Sonoran tiger salamander:** San Rafael Valley seine records from January 1997 to May 1998 document eggs, larvae, branchiate and metamorphosed adults, with tissue sampling at selected sites. Lab holdings from Huachuca Mountain tanks are tabulated by locality.

**Compliance and handling statements**
- No listed fish was reintroduced or translocated during the period.
- Specimens taken intentionally or through incidental loss are deposited in ASU or approved museum collections. Dead broodstock fish are incinerated after tissues are removed.
- Captive specimens are sought before wild fish are taken. Non-lethal sampling is preferred. Wild removal is a last resort for recovery needs.
- All personnel hold valid state permits or act as designated agents of permit holders. No work was conducted on Indian tribal lands.
- Specimen sacrifice follows protocols approved by the ASU Animal Care Committee (Attachment IV).

**Stated commitments**
- Investigators commit to cooperative breeding, propagation, and contribution to studbook compilation for listed fishes where appropriate.
- Research goals are framed around recovery of each listed species, with proposals in Attachment II describing what, where, when, how and why.
