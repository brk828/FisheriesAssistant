# XXV Desert Fishes Council Annual Meeting - Abstracts

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #conservation #conference-proceedings

## Executive Summary

This document contains the program and abstracts from the 25th Annual Meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], held November 10-14, 1993, at the [[Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon]] in San Nicolas de los Garza, Nuevo Leon, Mexico. The meeting featured agency reports, contributed papers, poster presentations, and a special symposium on [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]]. Topics spanned endangered species status updates, habitat studies, reproductive biology, hematology, morphological variation, otolith-based age determination, and conservation resolutions. The conference brought together researchers from the United States and Mexico to address critical issues facing desert aquatic ecosystems and their endemic fish fauna.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Council]] convened its 25th annual meeting under the leadership of Chairman John N. Rinne and Executive Secretary E. Phil Pister. The local organizing committee was led by Salvador Contreras Balderas and colleagues from the Laboratorio de Ictiologia at the Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon. The meeting program included approximately 50 contributed papers and posters covering a wide taxonomic and geographic range, from [[Colorado River]] basin fishes to Mexican endemics in the families [[Cyprinidae]], [[Poeciliidae]], [[Goodeidae]], [[Cyprinodontidae]], and [[Catostomidae]]. Agency reports provided updates from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Texas Parks and Wildlife Department]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and [[California Department of Fish and Game]].

A dedicated symposium on Saturday, November 13, focused entirely on the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] in Coahuila, Mexico, covering its ornithofauna, herpetofauna, stromatolites, vegetation, and fish diversity. The meeting concluded with a field trip to Cuatro Cienegas on November 14. Three resolutions were adopted addressing conservation of Cuatro Cienegas fishes, professional-nonprofessional cooperation in aquatic conservation, and protection of endemic fishes in the upper [[Verde River]], Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The abstracts reveal pervasive threats to desert fish populations across the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. Habitat degradation, water extraction, introduced species, and dam operations emerged as recurring themes. Studies documented inverse relationships between native species and introduced predators (e.g., [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] vs. [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Gila Topminnow]] vs. [[Western Mosquitofish]]). Multiple papers addressed the impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] on downstream native fish communities in the [[Grand Canyon]], particularly the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*).

Water quality parameters, including temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, and magnesium concentrations, were shown to influence fish distribution and hematology. The [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] was highlighted as a globally significant biodiversity hotspot with unique stromatolite formations, endemic cichlids (*Cichlasoma minckleyi*), and diverse aquatic communities threatened by agricultural development and water diversion. The meeting emphasized the need for integrated watershed management, habitat restoration, captive breeding programs, and refugia establishment to prevent extinctions.

## Key Findings

**Species Status and Population Trends:**
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations in [[Lake Mohave]] estimated at fewer than 55,000 individuals, with most fish exceeding 40 years of age and no detectable natural recruitment since the early 1950s
- [[Humpback Chub]] populations in the [[Little Colorado River]] showed rapid growth, while mainstream [[Colorado River]] individuals exhibited extremely slow growth due to cold hypolimnetic releases from Glen Canyon Dam
- [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) population in [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada increased dramatically from 150,000 in early 1980s to 1.1 million in 1993
- [[White River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda albivallis*) population reduced to fewer than 50 individuals in a single spring, with only 14 adults observed in June 1993
- [[Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos latos*) populations stable at three refugia sites (14,770, 11,100, and 2,900 individuals)

**Reproductive Biology and Life History:**
- [[Rio Grande Darter]] (*Etheostoma grahami*) eggs averaged 1.7 mm diameter; larvae hatched at 5.0 mm total length; caudal and pectoral fin rays began development at 6.7 mm
- [[Leatherside Chub]] (*Gila copei*) peak spawning occurred in May; average 1,813 ova per female; maximum observed age 9 years; reproductive adults ranged 2-9 years (median 6 years)
- *Poecilia petenense* fertility index 62.78 (range 22-105); gonadosomatic index 3.22% (range 0.02-14.35%); superfetal development only occasional
- [[Girardinichthys viviparus]] reproductive data collected from [[Nabor Carillo Lake]], Texcoco, Mexico

**Habitat Use and Distribution:**
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) (48.5%) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) (32.5%) most common in [[Colorado River]] Reach 20 (Lee's Ferry to Little Colorado River)
- [[Humpback Chub]] (33.3%) most abundant in Reach 30 (Little Colorado River to Bright Angel Creek)
- [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*) (35.7%) dominated Reach 40 (Bright Angel Creek to National Canyon)
- Adult native fishes used habitats with moderate to deep cover and substantial vertical structure during day; wider array of habitat types at night
- Young-of-year fishes and speckled dace used open, shallow edge habitats and were most active during daylight

**Otolith Studies:**
- Daily increment formation verified in [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) hatchery populations
- Oldest [[Humpback Chub]] aged approximately 22-23 years using lapillus otoliths
- Otolith microstructure revealed movements between Little Colorado River and mainstream Colorado River
- Strontium/Calcium ratios in otoliths potentially correlated with temperature and river-specific residence

**Morphological Variation:**
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) from [[Verde River]] had smaller mouths relative to pre-pelvic body measurements compared to [[Aravaipa Creek]] individuals
- Sexual dimorphism in spikedace: females had shorter pre-pectoral measurements, greater mid-body size, and shorter pectoral fins than males
- *Cichlasoma minckleyi* sexual dimorphism accounted for significantly greater morphological variation than trophic differences among papilliform, molariform, and piscivore morphs

**Water Quality and Hematology:**
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) populations in [[Pilon River]] showed significant differences in hemoglobin (7.84 vs. 6.22 mg/100ml) and hematocrit (31.75% vs. 33.04%), potentially related to magnesium concentrations (20 ppm increasing to 80 ppm)
- Downstream gradient in pH, CO2, alkalinity, hardness, and turbidity documented on [[Little Colorado River]] from Blue Springs to Big Canyon (river km 11.40 to 21.06)
- Fish distribution changes coincided with water chemistry gradients

**Conservation Actions:**
- 853 [[Razorback Suckers]] stocked into [[Lake Havasu]] on [[Bill Williams River]] NWR in February 1993; 50 additional fish stocked into 12 Mile Lake
- 6,617 [[Bonytail Chubs]] introduced into [[Lake Mohave]] at Davis Cove in October 1992
- Over 1,200 razorback suckers exceeding 300 mm stocked into [[Verde River]] following large winter flood event
- First refugium center for endangered fish species established in Mexico at Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon, maintaining and breeding over 20 species of Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae, and Goodeidae
- Phantom Lake Springs refugium channel constructed in 1993 for [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon elegans*) and [[Pecos Gambusia]] (*Gambusia nobilis*)

**Taxonomic and Systematic Studies:**
- Genus *Gobiesox* in freshwater comprises four known species in South and Central America and two in Mexico (*G. fluviatilis* and *G. mexicanus*), plus undescribed forms from Baja California Sur and Cuitzmala, Jalisco
- *Poeciliopsis gracilis* complex distributed in Grijalva-Usumacinta drainage in southeastern Mexico
- PCR-amplified mitochondrial DNA successfully separated western catostomid species except [[June Sucker]] (*Chasmistes liorus*), which was similar to [[Utah Sucker]] populations, suggesting possible introgression

**Cuatro Cienegas Symposium:**
- Four types of freshwater stromatolites distinguished, produced by various cyanobacteria and diatom assemblages
- 19 lizard species documented with niche breadth values (B) ranging 1.0-3.0 in 1986 and 1.0-2.5 in 1987
- 76.1% of niche overlap indices ≤0.4, indicating strong ecological segregation
- Nine cyclopoid copepod species, six harpacticoid species, and one calanoid species found; five species new records for Mexico
- 19 arachnid families documented, with spiders (14 families, 24 genera) most diverse

**Biodiversity Loss:**
- 22 localities in northeastern Mexico surveyed for ichthyofaunal changes: only 1 locality recovered, 20 lost 12-100% of native fish fauna
- Introduced species present at 17 localities, hybrids at 2 localities
- Changes attributed to water loss (quantity/quality) and environmental alterations
