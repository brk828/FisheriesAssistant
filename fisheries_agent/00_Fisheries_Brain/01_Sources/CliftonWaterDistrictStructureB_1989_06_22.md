## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 advocacy letter from the Environmental Defense Fund (EDF) to the EPA Regional Administrator concerning an unpermitted drop structure built by the Clifton Water District across the Colorado River. The structure, located in the ecologically critical "15 Mile Reach," raised significant concern due to its potential impact on the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). The letter details the regulatory response (Army Corps of Engineers and EPA involvement) and articulates the specific hydrological and ecological risks the structure poses to squawfish spawning and migration habitat.

## Background & Description
The source text is a regulatory letter and does not provide direct morphological data (e.g., length, weight, coloration) for the species. However, it references a key population-monitoring methodology:
- **Radio-Tagging**: The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) had radio-tagged individual squawfish within the 15 Mile Reach to track movement and migration patterns, indicating individuals large enough to carry telemetry equipment (consistent with adult life stage).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Colorado Squawfish]] is characterized in this document by its specific reliance on a large, connected river system:
- **Habitat Type**: Historically inhabited both the Upper and Lower [[Colorado River Basin]]; now restricted entirely to the Upper Basin following the construction of large mainstem dams.
- **Critical Habitat**: The "15 Mile Reach" is identified as a vital remnant spawning habitat, extending roughly 20 miles upstream from the structure's location to an impassable dam.
- **Spawning Behavior**: Squawfish spawn within this specific reach of the Colorado River.
- **Migration**: The species relies on unimpeded longitudinal river connectivity for migration between spawning and adult habitat; radio-tagging confirmed at least one fish successfully moved upstream past the pre-existing impassable dam (route of passage—breach vs. over-dam—was undetermined).
- **Biotic Interactions**: The recovery plan cites competition and predation by [[Non-Native Fish Species]] as a contributing factor to population decline, implying an ecological niche vulnerable to invasive warmwater fish assemblages.
- **Abiotic Requirements**: The species is sensitive to temperature regimes, with the letter noting that structural modifications could alter thermal conditions necessary for successful reproduction.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Violation**: The Clifton Water District constructed a 4-foot-high, 500-foot-wide drop structure across the entire width of the Colorado River (Nov–Dec 1988) without obtaining required permits, despite precedent showing the district was aware of permitting requirements for similar structures.
- **Discovery & Enforcement Timeline**:
  - March 21, 1989: FWS officials discovered the structure when their boat became grounded on it.
  - March 24, 1989: Army Corps of Engineers (ACOE) inspected the structure.
  - March 30, 1989: District complied with ACOE order to breach the dam.
- **Cause of Endangerment**: The 1974 endangered listing followed a toxic chemical spill that significantly reduced squawfish populations. The species' ongoing endangered status is attributed to:
  1. Alteration of historic flow regimens.
  2. Construction of in-channel structures limiting migration.
  3. Competition/predation from non-native fish.
- **Historical Range Contraction**: Prior to large dam construction, [[Colorado Squawfish]] occupied both the Upper and Lower [[Colorado River Basin]]; the species is now confined to the Upper Basin, where increasing human water demand threatens further population decline or extinction.
- **Potential Adverse Effects of the Drop Structure** (per EDF analysis):
  - Expansion of habitat suitable for competing or predatory (likely non-native) fish species.
  - Creation of an impassable migration barrier, especially during low-flow periods.
  - Alteration of temperature regimes with negative consequences for reproduction.
  - General degradation of bank stability and prime adult habitat.
- **Regulatory Outcome Pending**: The Army Corps of Engineers deferred enforcement to the EPA rather than issuing an after-the-fact permit. EPA was preparing an administrative order (potentially including fines) requiring the Clifton Water District to propose less environmentally damaging alternatives, such as:
  - A lower drop structure.
  - A deeper pump intake.
  - A smaller, partial dam positioned nearer the pumphouse.
- **Stakeholder Recommendation**: EDF endorsed the FWS recommendation for a multi-stakeholder meeting (including recovery program environmentalists) and emphasized that any alternative analysis must incorporate established flow recommendations for the [[15 Mile Reach]].
