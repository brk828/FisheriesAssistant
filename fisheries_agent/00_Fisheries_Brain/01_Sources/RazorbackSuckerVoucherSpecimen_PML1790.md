# Razorback Sucker Voucher Specimen Lost to Landfill Disposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This short first-person account, signed by [[C.O. Minckley]] and dated 8 February 2008, describes a [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) collected by [[Electrofishing]] at the mouth of the [[Paria River]] in May 1978. The specimen was preserved but was later lost through a logistical error: it was mistakenly discarded at the [[Flagstaff Landfill]] along with boxes of human waste. The document is an anecdote rather than a research report, and it contains no quantitative data.

## Background & Description

The document describes a single adult-sized fish only as a razorback sucker. No measurements, sex, age, or morphological details are given. The author states that the specimen was collected during a river trip, which suggests a field survey or collecting effort, although the purpose of the trip is not stated.

The [[Specimen Preservation Method]] used at the time followed a standard field protocol:

- Fish were preserved in full-strength [[Formalin Preservation]] in a "rocket box."
- After fixation, specimens were tagged and wrapped in plastic bags (here, a large garbage bag).
- Wrapped specimens were transferred to a second rocket box for transport for the remainder of the river trip.

The document explains the failure as a labeling and handling problem. Black garbage bags inside rocket boxes looked identical to the boxes used for human waste. Because waste boxes were dumped without close inspection, the box holding the fish was discarded with them.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document contains no data on the habitat, water chemistry, population status, or other species. The only environmental detail is the collection site at the mouth of the Paria River, a tributary river. Information on the fish's ecology, distribution, or conservation status is not included.

## Key Findings

- **Specimen collected:** One razorback sucker was taken by electrofishing at the mouth of the Paria River in May 1978.
- **Specimen lost:** The preserved fish was lost during transport through a logistical mistake and was never recovered.
- **Cause of loss:** Formalin-fixed specimens were stored in black garbage bags inside rocket boxes that resembled human waste boxes. Those boxes were discarded at the Flagstaff Landfill without inspection.
- **Systemic lesson:** The incident shows how poor labeling and container similarity can cause the loss of voucher specimens during field collecting. The document does not describe any corrective measures or policy changes.
