# Flannelmouth Sucker Collection, Holding, and Translocation from the Paria River (1976)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a field log of a [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) collection and transport operation. Adult suckers were captured with frame nets and hoop nets in the [[Paria River]] near its confluence with the [[Colorado River]] at Lee's Ferry, Arizona, between 29 and 31 March 1976. The fish were held in a transport tank with water changes, circulation, and supplemental oxygen, then stocked on 1 April 1976 in the Colorado River below [[Davis Dam]] at Bullhead City, Arizona. Of 612 fish held on the morning of 1 April, all but one were alive at release and appeared healthy. The log is a record of operations and fish condition, not a formal research study.

## Background & Description

The [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] is a freshwater catostomid fish native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The log refers to these fish as "flannelmouth suckers," and the collection was made to obtain live fish for transfer to another reach.

The [[Paria River]] site was described as follows:

- Width of about 15 to 30 m at the collection area.
- A main channel only about 1 m or more wide (the exact value is unreadable in the scan), with the rest of the channel gradually sloping into sand and gravel bars.
- Depth varied daily from a maximum of several metres to a minimum of about 30 cm, when only the main channel held flow.
- Water levels were driven by the daily release pattern from [[Glen Canyon Dam]], located upstream.
- Peak flows occurred during the evening, with the lowest flows in early morning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water conditions:** The [[Colorado River]] was clear, but the [[Paria River]] carried a heavy silt load that reduced water transparency to near zero. Water temperature was recorded at 9 °C.
- **Hydrology:** Daily flow fluctuation from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] caused stranding of nets and fish. Water receded to the point that the nets and most fish were left out of the water at each low-water period.
- **Holding conditions:** Fish were kept in a transport tank that was refilled with fresh water at each check. Circulation pumps ran continuously. Oxygen was added during periods of stress (for example, after overnight holding, and again when the oxygen supply was exhausted).
- **Release site:** The [[Colorado River]] below [[Davis Dam]] at the Bullhead City Community Park. Water temperature in the tank was 16 °C and in the river at the launch ramp was 15 °C, a difference of about 1 °C.

## Key Findings

**Collection effort (29–31 March 1976)**

- **29 March:** Two small frame nets with leads were set. Twenty-four suckers were taken from one net that evening. The second net was not checked because of a flow change.
- **30 March, morning:** Both nets were checked. Twenty-five more suckers were taken. Nets were then set again: one frame net with lead and one hoop net with wings.
- **30 March, evening:** All four nets were checked. Suckers were recovered, and the log reports these were placed in the transport tank, which had been refilled with fresh water. The total held by that point was 140 fish.
- **31 March, morning:** All four nets were checked again. 148 more suckers were taken, bringing the total to 288. The hoop net had been stranded and held only one carp, which was removed.
- **31 March, evening:** Three frame nets were checked for the final time. 339 suckers were caught, four escaped during removal, and 335 were placed in the tank.

**Holding and mortality**

- Eleven of the 288 fish held overnight on 31 March died and were removed. Fish held overnight appeared stressed, and the water was changed and oxygen added.
- On the morning of 1 April, all 612 fish appeared healthy, and most were in spawning condition.
- The oxygen tank was empty at 0800 on 1 April and was replaced at 1400.

**Translocation (1 April 1976)**

- At 1830 hours, the flannelmouth suckers were stocked in the [[Colorado River]] below [[Davis Dam]] at the Bullhead City Community Park, next to the Bullhead City Chamber of Commerce.
- One sucker was dead at release. The remaining fish appeared healthy and dispersed readily.
- Average size was about 2.5 to 3 pounds (the scan is partially illegible; the log reads "2-1/2 to 3").

**Operational notes**

- Hoop and frame nets were repeatedly stranded or damaged by fluctuating flows, which required repeated checks and resetting.
- Water changes and circulation were the main tools for keeping fish alive, with oxygen supplementation as needed.
