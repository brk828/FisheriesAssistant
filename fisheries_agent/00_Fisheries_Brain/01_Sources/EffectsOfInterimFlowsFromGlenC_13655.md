## Executive Summary

This is Quarterly Report No. 7 of the Hualapai Aquatic Resources Study, documenting fish and aquatic ecosystem sampling conducted between [[Diamond Creek]] (River Mile 225.7) and [[Scorpion Island]] on [[Lake Mead]] (RM 279), a reach of the [[Colorado River]] downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The study assesses effects of dam-regulated interim flows on native and non-native fish assemblages, water quality, and productivity in the Lower Grand Canyon. Sampling employed trammel nets, electrofishing, minnow traps, and seines across the main channel and key tributaries ([[Spencer Creek]], [[Surprise Creek]], [[Diamond Creek]]). A total of 4,394 fish were captured, overwhelmingly dominated by non-native cyprinids (red shiner, fathead minnow). Native species — including the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Humpback Chub]] — were rare or entirely absent, while relative native species abundance declined from 16% (Trip 6) to 10% (Trip 7). Tributary confluences, particularly Spencer Creek, showed markedly higher fish densities than the regulated main channel, suggesting tributary inflows provide critical habitat refugia.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Main channel (Colorado River)**: Cold (8.7–10.6°C), highly turbid (Secchi depth 0.05–0.40 m), swift in upper reaches (Travertine Falls) transitioning to lower-gradient, meandering, riparian-lined habitat near Bat Cave. Fish densities and catch rates were consistently low, reflecting limited habitat suitability under dam-regulated flow and temperature regimes.
- **Spencer Creek**: Warmer tributary (14.4–16.0°C) supporting substantially higher fish densities than the main channel, dominated by [[Red Shiner]] and [[Speckled Dace]]. Exotic species were restricted to roughly the lower 1 mile of stream, terminating abruptly at a natural flow constriction; upstream of this point only native speckled dace were found. Channel stability had improved markedly since a spring 1993 flood disturbance (Trip 5), with reduced bedload and a resurgence of filamentous green algae (*Cladophora*) and associated invertebrate production.
- **Surprise Creek**: Very high densities of exotic fish, dominated by red shiner and fathead minnow.
- **Diamond Creek**: Despite apparent connectivity/accessibility to the main channel, seining produced only native speckled dace — no exotics were recorded here.
- **Lake Mead inflow / Scorpion Island**: Slow-water, inundated riparian and travertine-cliff habitat; water temperatures 9.8–10.6°C. Sampling extended into a large bay east of Pearce Ferry.
- **Multiple-pass electrofishing sites** in Spencer Creek (Site 1 near confluence, ~30 m long/5 m wide; Site 2 ~0.75 mi upstream, ~46 m long/11 m wide) used block nets to quantify local fish assemblages and depletion estimates, consistent with Trip 6 methodology.
- Adult flannelmouth suckers were repeatedly captured near a specific travertine cliff site downstream of [[Emery Falls Canyon]] (RM 274–275) across multiple trips, suggesting site fidelity or persistent favorable habitat.
- No piscivorous fish were recorded in Spencer Creek during multi-pass sampling except one incidentally observed largemouth bass in a bedrock pool ~0.75 mi upstream of the confluence.

## Key Findings

- **Total catch**: 4,394 fish captured, dominated by [[Red Shiner]] (3,064; 69.7%) and [[Fathead Minnow]] (857; 19.5%), with [[Speckled Dace]] (417) and [[Common Carp]] (17) among Cyprinidae.
- **Species composition shifts vs. Trip 6**: [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Mosquitofish]] were captured in Trip 6 but absent in Trip 7. Conversely, [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Threadfin Shad]] appeared in Trip 7 but not Trip 6 (both previously recorded by BIO/WEST in earlier trips).
- **Native species decline**: Overall relative abundance of native species dropped from 16% (Trip 6) to 10% (Trip 7), driven mainly by a decrease in juvenile flannelmouth sucker captures in Spencer Creek and a concurrent increase in non-native fathead minnow abundance.
- **Razorback sucker**: Not captured or observed during Trip 7 or any prior sampling trip, underscoring continued concern for this federally endangered species in the study reach.
- **Flannelmouth sucker**: Only 6 individuals captured total; just 2 were adults (both PIT-tagged), the rest juveniles too small to tag.
- **Exotic species distribution in tributaries**: A natural hydraulic constriction in Spencer Creek (~500 m above the upstream electrofishing station) appears to function as a barrier limiting exotic fish (primarily red shiner, up to 250/seine haul) to the lower ~1 mile of stream, preserving an exotic-free native speckled dace refuge upstream.
- **Habitat use pattern**: Fish densities were consistently much greater in tributary confluences (Spencer and Surprise Creeks) than in the regulated main channel, potentially reflecting tributary-driven differences in water temperature, quality, or food availability — a pattern consistent across Trips 6 and 7.
- **Productivity recovery**: Post-flood (Spring 1993) recovery in Spencer Creek included reduced bedload movement and a substantial increase in algal (*Cladophora*) and invertebrate production, potentially supporting improved fish habitat quality.
- **Recommendations**: Continued intensive multi-pass and incidental sampling in Spencer Creek; continued main-channel PIT-tag monitoring of native fish; continued Diamond Creek confluence sampling; and expanded sampling of habitats similar to where [[Humpback Chub]] was previously collected.
