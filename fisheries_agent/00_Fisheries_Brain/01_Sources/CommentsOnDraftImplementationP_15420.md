# Fisheries - USA Implementation Plan (1990 Draft and Review Memoranda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #recreational-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This document is a review package for the draft **Implementation Plan for [[Fisheries - USA]]**, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's (Service) [[Recreational Fisheries Policy]] signed December 5, 1989. It contains three parts: a Regional Director's comments on the draft (dated May 18, 1990), an internal Region 2 staff memorandum objecting to the plan's narrow focus, and the April 23, 1990 draft plan itself. The draft translates four policy goals and 53 sub-strategies into numbered action items across nine categories, each assigned a [[Priority Class]] (A, B, or C). The central tension is between the plan's emphasis on recreational fishing and the Service's obligations to nongame and native fishes, and between the plan's ambitions and the staff and funding available to carry them out.

## Background & Description

**Policy and purpose.** The Recreational Fisheries Policy commits the Service to work with public agencies and the private sector to enhance recreational fishing nationwide. Its four goals are:

- **Goal A:** Preserve and/or increase the productivity of fishery resources.
- **Goal B:** Ensure and enhance the quality, quantity, and diversity of recreational fishing opportunities.
- **Goal C:** Develop and enhance partnerships between governments and the private sector.
- **Goal D:** Cooperate to maintain a healthy recreational fisheries industry.

**Plan structure.** The draft lists nine categories with no priorities among them:

1. Technical Assistance
2. Federal, State, and Native American Coordination
3. Administrative Accountability Activities
4. Management of Existing Information
5. New Information
6. Public Outreach
7. Fishery Management Plan Development and Implementation
8. Ethics
9. Initiatives and Partnerships with the Private Sector

Priority Class A covers items of high importance that are highly "doable" within existing resources, or items so important that they warrant top priority regardless. Class B covers items high in importance but moderate in doability (or the reverse). Class C covers desirable items that must not divert resources from Class A or B work.

**Process and schedule.** A Work Group chaired by Howard Larsen, formed in February 1990, drafted the plan with constituency, National Marine Fisheries Service, and Service representatives. Review comments were due to David McDaniel, Service Recreational Fisheries Coordinator, by May 10, 1990, with completion targeted for May 31, 1990. Regional Implementation Plans were due to the Director by December 31, 1990.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Region 2 staff memorandum argues that the plan's management philosophy reflects practices of about 20 years earlier: more impoundments producing more sport fish, usually fed by nonnative forage such as threadfin shad, gizzard shad, and red shiners. The memo identifies two major pressures on native fishes, particularly in southwestern waters:

- **Dams.** Streams were dammed for water storage, usually with recreational fishing potential cited as a justification.
- **Nonnative fishes.** Federal and state agencies stocked nonnative species to support angling. Native nongame fish were often treated as a nuisance and destroyed to protect the "desired" fauna.

The memo states that nonnative introductions have adversely affected many native populations, some protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It gives examples of the plan's direction:

- A proposal by Utah to stock [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Mohave]] would be fully supported by the draft's goals.
- Streams stocked annually with German Brown Trout, and the [[Colorado River]] now dominated by [[Striped Bass]], a relatively recent intruder from saltwater, are cited as examples of management focused only on angling opportunity.

The memo also notes that state agencies depend on license and permit sales, while nongame funds (income tax check-off and Section 6 funds) are a minor part of their budgets. It argues that the Service, funded by Congress rather than license sales, should counterbalance state priorities and protect neglected resources.

The plan itself addresses environmental concerns in several places: habitat improvement including water quality, prevention of habitat loss or degradation, research on habitat alteration, and development of a strategy for contaminant problems on Service lands. It also calls for a genetic integrity or fishery introduction policy to protect native stocks, and for Fish Conservation Areas for fishery resources of national significance.

## Key Findings

**Regional Director's comments (May 18, 1990)**

- More than half of the draft items are designated Priority Class A, but the Regional Director states that Fisheries and Refuges need adequate funding and staff ceilings to carry them out.
- Recreational fishing must be ranked as a very high Service priority to be accomplished within existing resources, and the Region does not believe it currently is.
- The title "Fisheries - USA" implies a guiding document for the Service's entire fishery program. The Region asks that the plan state its consistency with broader Fisheries responsibilities and link to the Fisheries Task Force.
- Specific concerns include:
  - Refuge statistical reporting through [[Refuge Management Information System]] (RMIS) would be a new and substantial effort on many refuges.
  - A "Fisheries-USA" newsletter may duplicate existing outreach. The Region suggests Class C or D priority and that funds be redirected to field work.
  - Clear guidance is needed on how Fisheries - USA projects rank against [[North American Waterfowl Management Plan]] projects.
  - Reverted [[Federal Aid]] Fish Restoration funds are administered by [[Region 8]], which should be a principal in those actions.
  - "Urban fishing" must be defined. If it means stocking catchable fish in city ponds, the [[Sport Fish Restoration Act]] would need to change, or Federal Aid could not be used.
  - Several examples are very labor intensive and would require resources beyond those now available.
  - The plan should separate Class A items into those achievable within present funding by reordering priorities and those requiring budget or staffing initiatives.

**Region 2 staff memorandum**

- The first 13.5 pages concentrate on enhancing recreational fishing, while the last 10.5 pages cover management plans. The staff notes that the plan is silent on managing nonsport fish.
- The staff calls it "unforgivable" to manage sport fish without considering the rest of the fish fauna. At minimum, the preface should acknowledge the Service's obligation to manage the entire fishery resource.
- The memo accepts potential conflict between recreational fishing growth and saving rare fish from extinction, stating that the Service must not lessen its obligation to manage the whole fauna.

**Draft plan highlights**

- **Technical Assistance:** Increase funding and manpower for technical assistance, with a Class A priority. Establish a task force to evaluate delivery mechanisms.
- **Coordination:** Hold workshops with state and federal agencies to create new recreational fisheries. Develop a shared interagency data base. Include Tribal management in public information (Class A/B).
- **Administrative accountability:** Fund Refuge Fishery Management Plans, with a target of implementation on appropriate refuges by 2001. Conduct a biennial review of policies that may impede recreational fisheries. Track baseline resources for unique or threatened fisheries.
- **Information:** Add recreational fishing statistics to RMIS. Publish a "Fisheries-USA" newsletter through [[Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] matching funds. Evaluate waterfowl and fishery management interactions, including effects of water level manipulations and agricultural practices on fisheries.
- **Research:** Study habitat loss and degradation effects on recreational fisheries. Develop a large-river study initiative to identify why certain river systems are not meeting their fishery potential.
- **Fishery management:** Complete comprehensive fishery management plans for all refuges by the Year 2000. Develop recreational fishery plans on refuges with identified potential, including public access, season and size limits, and creel surveys. Establish Fish Conservation Areas where needed.
- **Ethics and outreach:** Adopt an angler code of ethics emphasizing [[Catch-and-Release]]. Expand volunteer programs, [[Adopt-a-Refuge]] partnerships, handicapped fishing access, [[Take-a-Kid-Fishing]] days, and urban fishing opportunities. Promote fishing through [[National Fishing Week]].

**Implementation and reporting**

- Regions will step the national plan down to regional and field-level tasks, tracked by category and action number.
- The national plan will be formally reviewed and updated in December 1992, June 1995, and December 1997, with progress reports due within 90 days of each review.
- A final public report on the four goals is scheduled for National Fishing Week of the Year 2000.
- Assistant Director for Fisheries leads national updates and the awards program.

**Unresolved issues**

- The draft contains two different deadlines for refuge fishery management plans: comprehensive plans by the Year 2000 and implementation by 2001.
- Appendices A and B (the listing of sub-strategies and the cross-reference) are marked "to be completed."
- The Region 2 staff's central objection, that native and nongame fish are not addressed, is not resolved in the draft.
