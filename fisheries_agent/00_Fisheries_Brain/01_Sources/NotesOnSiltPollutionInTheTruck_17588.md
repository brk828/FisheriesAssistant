# Notes on Silt Pollution in the Truckee River Drainage, Nevada and Placer Counties

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Inland Fisheries Administrative Report, by Almo J. Cordone and Stephen Phinney of the [[California Department of Fish and Game]], documents a biological survey of silt pollution in the [[Truckee River]] and [[Cold Creek]], conducted September 2–4, 1958. The survey followed a near-continuous discharge of wash water from a [[Gravel Washing Plant]] near Truckee, Nevada County, that ran for at least three months (mid-June to mid-September 1958). The authors found significantly fewer bottom invertebrates and algal pads in silted reaches, and significantly poorer condition in Cold Creek trout. The report also offers a set of recommended field methods for short-term [[Silt Pollution]] investigations.

## Background & Description

The Truckee River and several tributaries received large silt loads in summer 1958. Highway construction, rechannelization of [[Donner Creek]] and Squaw Creek, and bridge work contributed silt, but these sources were intermittent and short-lived. The gravel plant was the dominant and persistent source. Wash water was diverted from Cold Creek and returned without settling, so turbid water reached the Truckee River from the mouth of Donner Creek into Nevada.

The turbidity made the reach unattractive to anglers, although it was heavily fished for [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brown Trout]] under normal conditions. Angler use was almost eliminated, except for minor use on Sunday and Monday mornings after the plant shut down for weekends.

The Fish and Game Warden requested the survey after the plant operators did not cooperate in controlling the discharge. The report also draws on the literature review by [[Cordone and Kelley 1960]].

Stream character:
- **Upstream of Donner Creek (Truckee River):** clear at the time of survey, with some silt in shallows and pools. Rubble and gravel were loose.
- **Silted sections:** the substrate was largely cemented into a hard, bedrock-like layer in riffles. Margins were coated with silt to a foot or more in places, and all objects were uniformly gray-brown.
- **Stream flows:** the Truckee River above Donner Creek measured about 19, 30, and 94 cubic feet per second (cfs) on September 2, 3, and 4. Below Donner Creek, flows were about 37, 7, and 107 cfs. Cold Creek flowed about 3.5 cfs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical measures**
- **Visibility:** a rough index using the depth at which a black rubber boot could be seen. Visibility in the unsilted upper Truckee was likely more than 10 feet. Readings near the silted zone were greatly reduced and highly variable, reflecting sampling time and weekend plant closures.
- **Turbidity:** [[Sierra Pacific Power Company]] daily readings at its [[Highland Reservoir]] (about 40 river miles downstream) were considered minimal. Readings under 10 parts per million (ppm) were considered normal for the watershed. The utility reported substantial treatment costs from the sediment.
- **Suspended solids:** the effluent sample from September 3, 1958, contained 8.2 grams per liter (31.0 grams per gallon) of suspended silt. Effluent flow was 2.7 cfs. The authors estimated about 2.5 tons of silt per hour, or 20 to 25 tons per working day. Silt concentration in Cold Creek at the discharge point was estimated at about 6,300 ppm.

**Bottom fauna**
- Sampling used a [[Surber Sampler]] at riffles (surface velocity about 1–2 feet per second, depth 6–13 inches). Three samples were taken at each of eight stations.
- The stations, located on Cold Creek and the Truckee River, are summarized in the report's station table. Samples were too few to compare species composition above and below the discharge.
- Still-water margins at the unsilted station yielded Diptera (1 species), Trichoptera (3 species), and Ephemeroptera (5 species). No aquatic organisms were found in the silted station's margins.

**Algal pads**
- Upstream, the bottom was covered by globular and disc-shaped algal pads, probably including *Nostoc* sp., some containing larval chironomids.
- One square-foot sample at the unsilted Station 4 held 2,247 pads. Three samples at the silted Station 5 held only 8, 9, and 11 pads.
- Filamentous algae (probably *Cladophora* sp.) were not sampled but appeared drastically reduced.

**Fish**
- Fish were collected by [[Electrofishing]] in roughly 100-yard sections, with seines at section ends and salt added upstream to improve conductivity.
- Fish were fixed in 10 percent formalin and weighed and measured for [[Condition Factor]] calculations.
- Sculpin counts were 55 (Truckee above), 37 (Truckee below), 8 (Cold Creek above), and 7 (Cold Creek below).

## Key Findings

**Bottom organisms**
- Downstream of the silt discharge, bottom organisms showed significant reductions in numbers and weight.
- The strongest reduction was at the stations closest to the outfall. Cold Creek Station 1 (above the outfall) averaged 105.9 organisms per square foot in the Table 2 totals, compared with 2.3 at Station 2 (below), a reduction of 97.8 percent.
- Unsilted Truckee River Station 4 averaged 376.0 organisms per square foot. Silted Truckee stations were consistently lower, and recovery with distance downstream was uneven.
- Station 3 (Truckee, wide shallow riffle) was judged unreliable because flows dropped to as low as 19 cfs for several weeks, so it was excluded from comparisons.

**Statistical tests**
- Snedecor's F test was applied to bottom samples. The two unsilted Truckee stations (3 and 4) did not differ significantly and were treated as one undamaged population.
- Silted Truckee stations differed significantly from the unsilted population at the 5 percent or 1 percent level, except Station 7 at the San Francisco Fly Casting Club. The authors offered two explanations for Station 7: its sampling was not representative, or Station 8 was erroneous.
- Cold Creek Stations 1 and 2 differed highly significantly.
- The authors concluded that the bottom fauna was severely damaged for at least 2.5 miles and up to 10 miles of the Truckee River, plus about one-half mile of Cold Creek.

**Trout condition**
- Cold Creek trout below the outfall had poorer condition than those above. They also lacked bright coloration, and the single [[Kokanee]] taken there was pale with a large side sore.
- The F test on condition factors gave F = 13.9 (1 and 18 degrees of freedom), significant at greater than the 1 percent level.
- Truckee River trout were healthy above and below the discharge, with no significant difference in condition factors.
- Trout from both reaches were fewer than expected, but without pre-pollution data the authors could not confirm a population decline.
- No obvious differences in sculpin condition were found.
- Gills showed no visible irritation, so the mechanism of effect (direct or indirect through reduced visibility and food supply) could not be determined.

**Species recorded**
- Six species were collected, including [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Eastern Brook Trout]], [[Kokanee]], [[Mountain Whitefish]], [[Lahontan Mountain-Sucker]], and [[Piute Sculpin]].

**Conclusions**
- Siltation from the gravel plant significantly reduced bottom organisms in Cold Creek and the Truckee River.
- It also significantly reduced the condition of Cold Creek trout.
- Long-term effects on substrate, which provides shelter and food for fishes and invertebrates, could not be assessed. The authors recommended further study.

**Recommendations for silt pollution investigations**
- **Physical sampling:** measure visibility, suspended solids (including undiluted discharge), settling rate (using an [[Imhoff Cone]]), and substrate composition (sieve analysis). Measure [[Gravel Percolation Rate]] through spawning gravels, which is known to be harmful to salmonid eggs.
- **Biological sampling:** sample fish (examine gills, calculate condition factors, check for dead fish, and consider test fish in live cars). Sample bottom fauna with two or three samples from similar substrate, collected at set intervals on transects across riffles, and use a square-foot sampler where flow is slow. Consider [[Buried Egg Tests]] and algal pad sampling.
- **Documentation:** photographs, color transparencies, and clear charts are advised if court action or a public hearing is possible.
- **Long-term monitoring:** the authors recommended periodic sampling programs at strategic locations, following [[Usinger and Needham 1954]].
