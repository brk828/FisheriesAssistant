# A New Species of Corophium from Georgia Brackish Waters

#amphipoda #crustacea #corophiidae #taxonomy #new-species

## Executive Summary

This paper describes **Corophium aquafuscum** new species, a tube-dwelling amphipod discovered in 1968 in low salinity marsh areas of Georgia estuaries. The species is characterized by a completely segmented urosome, two strong distoventral teeth on antenna 2 segment 4, absence of distinct rostrum in males (present in females), and bare inner margin of uropod 1 peduncle except for a single distal spine. The holotype (adult female, USNM 128291) and paratype (adult male, USNM 128292) were collected from [[Riceboro Creek]] at the headwaters of the [[North Newport River]], Liberty County, Georgia. The species is known from Georgia, Virginia, and Maryland in freshwater-oligohaline tidal habitats.

## Background & Description

[[Corophium aquafuscum]] belongs to Section A(1) of the subgeneric classification scheme (Crawford 1937, modified by Shoemaker 1947), which includes species with completely segmented urosomes and antenna 2 segment 4 alike in both sexes. This section includes 12 previously described species: *C. volutator*, *C. chelicorne*, *C. curvispinum*, *C. homoceratum*, *C. maeoticum*, *C. monodon*, *C. mucronatum*, *C. multisetosum*, *C. nobile*, *C. robustum*, *C. spinicorne*, and *C. spinulosum*.

**Male morphology**: Body length 4-7 mm (excluding antennae). Head frontal margin nearly straight, rostrum absent or reduced to small nub. Eyes well developed, oval, often notched posteriorly. [[Antenna 1]] with 3 peduncular and 13-16 flagellar segments, reaching middle of antenna 2 segment 5; peduncular segment 1 with 18-20 long setae on inner dorsal margin, 13-16 setae on ventral margin. [[Antenna 2]] from 2/3 to 3/4 body length, with two well-developed distoventral teeth on segment 4 (ventral tooth larger); segment 5 with prominent ventral-projecting tooth on proximal fourth; flagellum with 4 articles. Mandible incisor tridentate; lacinia mobilis bidentate; molar very strong with 16-20 rows of microdenticles; mandibular palp 3-segmented. [[Gnathopod 1]] subchelate with 6-7 transverse rows of comblike setae; 9-11 submarginate compound bifid spinules along distal grasping surface. [[Gnathopod 2]] simple with 10-12 very long transverse compound setae. [[Uropod 1]] peduncle with 10-11 setae on outer margin, single stout spine on distal-inner margin; outer ramus with 12-15 compound spines; inner ramus with 4-6 compound spines. [[Uropod 2]] peduncle with 3-4 setae; outer ramus with 6-7 compound spines; inner ramus with 0-2 compound spines. [[Uropod 3]] uniramous with rounded ramus bearing 12-14 setae. Telson with 4 proximal setae each side, two rows of 4 short recurved thorn-shaped spines medially.

**Female morphology**: Body 4-6 mm (excluding antennae). Head well developed with rostrum present; sinuses of antenna 1 distinct and widely concave. [[Antenna 1]] with 3 peduncular and 11-13 flagellar segments, reaching nearly to distal end of antenna 2 segment 5; segment 1 with short thick spine and small compound seta distally. [[Antenna 2]] about 1/4 body length; segment 4 similar to male with two well-developed distal teeth, 13-15 dense clusters of long setae on outer ventral margin. [[Gnathopod 1]] very similar to male but with more setae; segment 6 with 6-7 stout compound bifid spinules and 6-8 slender weakly bifid setae on distal margin. [[Gnathopod 2]] segment 6 with single row of 10-12 very long compound setae. Urosome completely segmented with generally more setae and spines than male. [[Uropod 1]] peduncle with 12-15 setae on outer margin; outer ramus with 15-18 compound spines. [[Uropod 2]] peduncle with 3-7 setae on outer margin; outer ramus with 5-8 compound spines; inner ramus with 2-4 compound spines. [[Uropod 3]] peduncle with 2-4 setae; ramus subovate with 13-18 setae.

**Diagnostic features**: The combination of three characteristics separates *C. aquafuscum* from all other described species in Section A(1): (1) two strong distoventral teeth on antenna 2 segment 4, (2) distinct rostrum lacking in male, and (3) inner margin of uropod 1 peduncular segment bare except for single distal spine. The specific name "aquafuscum" refers to the dark brown, humic waters of the type-locality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Corophium aquafuscum]] inhabits freshwater-oligohaline tidal creeks in the upper reaches of Georgia estuaries. The species is confined to areas influenced by tidal action, most abundant in the mid intertidal zone where muddy substrate is shaded by marsh vegetation and debris. At the type-locality ([[Riceboro Creek]] at U.S. Highway 17), large concentrations occurred under fill rocks adjacent to bridge abutment.

**Habitat and behavior**: The species normally constructs U-shaped burrows in mud-fine-sand substrates permeated with roots of [[Spartina alterniflora]], [[Lileopsis chinensis]], and other marsh vegetation. Occasionally specimens were found in sand-covered tubes attached to submerged logs and vegetation at "Crossroads" near the headwaters of [[Riceboro Creek]] (approximately 2 miles west of type-locality).

**Associated fauna**: Other oligohaline or euryhaline invertebrates co-occurring at the type-locality include:
- **Crabs**: [[Callinectes sapidus]] (blue crab), [[Rhithropanopeus harrisii]], [[Uca minax]] (red-jointed fiddler crab)
- **Shrimps**: [[Penaeus setiferus]] (white shrimp, juveniles in late summer/fall), [[Palaemonetes pugio]] (daggerblade grass shrimp)
- **Isopods**: [[Cyathura polita]], [[Cassidiniidea lunifrons]]
- **Amphipods**: [[Orchestia grillus]], [[Orchestia uhleri]], [[Gammarus tigrinus]], [[Corophium lacustre]]
- **Barnacle**: [[Balanus improvisus]]
- **Polychaete**: [[Namalycastis abiuma]]
- **Molluscs**: [[Litterodinops tenuipes]], *Hydrobia* sp., [[Melampus bidentatus]], [[Detracia floridana]], [[Polymesoda carolina]], [[Cyrenoidea floridana]]

**Predation**: [[Corophium aquafuscum]] was found in stomachs of [[Ictalurus catus]] (white catfish) and [[Fundulus heteroclitus]] (mummichog) collected in [[Riceboro Creek]] during winter and early spring 1970. The species is probably also preyed upon by clapper rails, spotted sandpipers, seaside sparrows, and other birds observed in the collecting area.

**Reproductive biology**: Ovigerous females were collected during warmer months (June-September). Populations appeared to peak during late August and September. Specimens were comparatively rare in winter collections (February).

**Distribution**: Presently known from [[Nanticoke River]] (Maryland), [[Pamunkey River]] and [[York River]] (Virginia), and the type-locality in Georgia. The species probably extends into extreme northern Florida but appears absent from suitable habitats in the [[St. Johns River]] estuary, Florida.

## Key Findings

1. **New species description**: [[Corophium aquafuscum]] new species is described from Georgia brackish waters, representing a moderately large (4-7 mm males, 4-6 mm females), very setose amphipod with completely segmented urosome.

2. **Taxonomic placement**: The species belongs to Section A(1) of genus [[Corophium]], distinguished by three unique characteristics: two strong distoventral teeth on antenna 2 segment 4, distinct rostrum lacking in males (present in females), and bare inner margin of uropod 1 peduncle except for single distal spine.

3. **Type specimens**: Holotype adult female (USNM 128291) and paratype adult male (USNM 128292) deposited at Smithsonian Institution. Type-locality: [[Riceboro Creek]] (headwaters of [[North Newport River]]) at U.S. Highway 17, Liberty County, Georgia.

4. **Habitat specificity**: Species confined to freshwater-oligohaline tidal creeks, most abundant in mid intertidal zone with muddy substrate shaded by marsh vegetation. Constructs U-shaped burrows in mud-fine-sand substrates permeated with [[Spartina alterniflora]] and other marsh plant roots.

5. **Geographic range**: Known from Georgia, Virginia ([[Pamunkey River]], [[York River]]), and Maryland ([[Nanticoke River]]). Likely extends to extreme northern Florida but absent from [[St. Johns River]] estuary.

6. **Seasonal patterns**: Ovigerous females collected June-September; populations peak in late August-September; specimens rare in winter (February).

7. **Trophic role**: Serves as prey for [[Ictalurus catus]] (white catfish) and [[Fundulus heteroclitus]] (mummichog), and likely consumed by various bird species including clapper rails, spotted sandpipers, and seaside sparrows.

8. **Community composition**: Co-occurs with diverse oligohaline/euryhaline invertebrate assemblage including 3 crab species, 2 shrimp species, 2 isopod species, 4 amphipod species, 1 barnacle species, 1 polychaete species, and 6 mollusc species.

9. **Morphological sexual dimorphism**: Males lack distinct rostrum (present in females); males have antenna 2 from 2/3 to 3/4 body length while females have antenna 2 about 1/4 body length; females generally have more setae and spines on urosome than males.

10. **Comparative taxonomy**: Distinguished from *C. maeoticum* (the most similar species) by presence of 3-7 setae on outer margin of uropod 2 peduncular segment and rows of compound spines on outer margins of uropod 1 (12-15) and uropod 2 (6-8) outer rami.
