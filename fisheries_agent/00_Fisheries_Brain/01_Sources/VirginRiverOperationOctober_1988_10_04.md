## Executive Summary

This field report documents a two-day fisheries operation (4–5 October 1988) conducted along a ~25 river-km reach of the [[Virgin River]] in Washington County, Utah, between an upper diversion/barrier near "Twin Bridges" and a lower barrier under construction at the head of the [[Virgin River Narrows]]. The operation combined targeted collection of native fishes (for skeletal and biochemical analyses) with a large-scale rotenone poisoning/reclamation effort intended to eliminate non-native fishes ahead of native species conservation efforts, likely for [[Virgin River Chub]] and [[Woundfin]] recovery. The report documents extreme numerical dominance of introduced [[Red Shiner]] and [[Black Bullhead]], rarity of native taxa, downstream toxicant drift that overran the lower barrier before a detoxification station was operational, and probable rotenone incursion into Arizona waters.

## Background & Description

The source is a field operations log rather than a morphological study, but it specifies collection targets by size class, implying diagnostic size thresholds used by the survey crew:

- **Native "large" specimens**: individuals ≥ ca. 150 mm total length were specifically targeted from [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), and [[Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*) for **skeletal material**.
- **Native "small" specimens**: smaller individuals of chub and flannelmouth sucker were retained for **biochemical (genetic/allozyme) analyses**.
- A **random sample of smaller native fish across all species** was also taken.
- Non-native species were largely ignored except when "new" or unusual individuals were encountered, indicating a taxonomically selective collection bias rather than a comprehensive morphometric survey.

No detailed meristic, coloration, or morphometric data are given in the text for individual taxa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical habitat characteristics** varied longitudinally along the surveyed reach:

- **Upper reach (Twin Bridges area)**: channel width 3–5 m, depth from a few cm to ~0.5 m, occasionally braided, banks densely lined with [[Tamarisk]]. Channel scarcely-to-moderately incised (1.5–2 m). Substrate sandy throughout with short cobble-to-small-boulder riffles (imbricated with fine sand). Pools poorly developed, shallow (rarely >0.5 m), and small. Backwaters/side channels scarce. Aquatic vegetation absent except filamentous algae colonizing rocky substrate.
- **Aquatic invertebrates** in the upper reach were limited to heptageniid mayflies, corydalid dobsonflies (Megaloptera), anisopteran dragonflies (all uncommon), and abundant newly-hatched simuliid blackfly larvae on stable substrates.
- **Discharge**: ~0.475 m³/s estimated near Twin Bridges (higher than late September); turbidity relatively high, visibility poor.
- **Twin Bridges to I-15 Bridge**: similar habitat but included discrete boulder-substrate reaches and deep (~2 m), slow sandy reaches absent upstream.
- **Tributaries**: [[Santa Clara River]] (entering from north near I-15 Bridge) and [[Fort Pearce Wash]] (entering from south), both with extensive watersheds and agricultural drain inflow; both received rotenone treatment. Discharge at Bloomington (downstream of I-15) estimated at ≥60 CFS, substantially greater than at Twin Bridges.
- **Little Round Valley to lower barrier**: gradient increased downstream; substrate still sandy but with 25–30% gravel-cobble bars/riffles and boulder fields, transitioning into the [[Virgin River Narrows]] canyon with sandstone walls and bedrock channel margins/bottom.
- **Anthropogenic modifications**: An artificial diversion/barrier on a natural bedrock dike upstream, and a lower barrier under construction (bedrock dike partially blocked with gravel, forcing flow through a 3–4 m channel with a <0.3 m drop; impoundment extending ~2500 m upstream, locally >2 m deep). Heavy equipment (D-7 Cat, front loader) was actively working the site.
- **Anthropogenic inflow**: A permanent agricultural drain entering ~1.6 km upstream of Twin Bridges reportedly drained upstream marshland and served as a refuge for red shiner, prompting a separate rotenone drip within the drain itself.

**Community structure**: [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*, listed as *Notropis lutrensis*) was overwhelmingly the most abundant fish throughout the reach, followed distantly by [[Black Bullhead]]. Native Gila and suckers were uncommon; [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin River Spinedace]] were rare. Total native fish encountered during this operation were estimated at only ~10% of numbers observed during a prior poisoning event in late September 1988, suggesting either population decline, downstream displacement, or incomplete sampling efficiency relative to the earlier operation.

## Key Findings

- **15 taxa collected** in total across the surveyed reach, comprising 6 native and 9 non-native species (full list below).
- **Rotenone reclamation operation**: Multiple simultaneous drip stations were used — at the upper barrier (from ~0930 hrs, 4 Oct.), at Twin Bridges (started 1310 hrs, 4 Oct.), in the agricultural drain, in the Santa Clara River, in Fort Pearce Wash, and planned for Bloomington (5 Oct.).
- **Toxicant drift outran control measures**: By 0600 hrs on 5 October, all fish near Little Round Valley (camped ~9.4 km downstream of I-15 Bridge) were dead — only red shiner and black bullhead carcasses were found, despite no evidence of fish kill at 1800 hrs or midnight the previous night. This indicates rapid downstream translocation of toxicant overnight.
- **Barrier failure**: Toxicant reached and passed the still-under-construction lower barrier before a planned detoxification (potassium permanganate) station was in place, prompting emergency communication with operation coordinators (Archer/Knight, via Hickman) to curtail upstream drips and expedite permanganate deployment.
- **Interstate contamination risk**: Live, unstressed fish were still observed at Beaver Dam Wash, Arizona (30–50 km downstream of the lower barrier) in the afternoon of 5 October, but Arizona Game and Fish Department was formally notified on 6 October that rotenone had likely penetrated into Arizona waters, warranting inspection.
- **Human dimension / invasion risk**: A local resident reported having translocated *Cichlasoma* sp. (likely [[Convict Cichlid]] or similar) from "Boiler Spring" into a private bass pond, where they reproduced prolifically, with a stated expectation they might eventually reach the river — a documented informal vector for non-native introduction.
- **Local concern over public disclosure**: Both the interviewed resident and team member T. Hickman expressed concern that media coverage (St. George Daily Spectrum, 14 Sept 1988) of the reclamation project had disclosed enough detail to enable saboteurs to reintroduce red shiner and undermine the eradication effort.
- **Species list (scientific names as reported)**:
  - Native: [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*), [[Virgin River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*), [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), [[Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*)
  - Non-native: [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis/Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus melas*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*)
- **Geographic reference**: USGS 7.5' quadrangles covering the study area include St. George, Washington, Washington Dome, and White Hills (Utah).
