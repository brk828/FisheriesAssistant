## Executive Summary

This paper (Pesce & Maggi) reports on cyclopid copepods (Crustacea: Copepoda, family [[Cyclopidae]]) collected from phreatic and interstitial (hyporheic) groundwater systems of Sardinia, a Mediterranean island whose subterranean copepod fauna had been poorly studied compared to Calanoida and Harpacticoida. The study is **not fish-focused**; it addresses invertebrate (copepod) taxonomy, ecology, and biogeography in [[Groundwater Ecosystems]]. Sampling covered phreatic aquifers in Cretaceous sandy-calcareous and Cenozoic clayey-sandy areas, as well as hyporheic zones of the rivers Liscia, Posada, Flumendosa, Palasole, and Cedrino. Thirteen species/subspecies were newly identified in this material, bringing the total known subterranean cyclopid fauna of Sardinia to **18 species and subspecies**. Several taxa of high systematic and biogeographic interest were recorded for the first time in Sardinian subterranean waters, including members of the *Diacyclops languidoides* complex, *Megacyclops brachypus*, *Thermocyclops dybowskii*, and *Cyclops abissorum*.

## Background & Description

- **Diacyclops languidoides complex**: A polytypic group with wide distribution across Italy and Europe, comprising numerous subspecies/"varieties" largely representing geographically isolated local races. Well-characterized, systematically valid subspecies include *D. languidoides hypnicola* (= *clandestinus*, = *badeniae*), *D. l. zschokkei*, *D. l. hiberniae*, and *D. l. eriophori*. Two subspecies were identified in the Sardinian material: *D. l. hypnicola* and *D. l. zschokkei*; additional populations show intermediate morphology between the nominal form and these subspecies, pending biometric/statistical analysis for exact subspecific placement.
- **Megacyclops brachypus**: A stygobiont form closely related to [[Megacyclops viridis]], distinguished primarily by **notable shortening of its appendages** and **smaller body dimensions**.
- **Cyclops abissorum group**: Considered a polytypic complex (per Einsle, 1975) encompassing numerous ecotypes, the most characteristic being *C. a. abissorum*, *C. a. prealpinus*, *C. a. tatricus*, and *C. a. divulsus*. The Sardinian material could not be confidently assigned to subspecies level based on morphology alone; Einsle (1975) proposed chromatin reduction analysis during cell division as an alternative diagnostic method for this group.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 18 known taxa are classified into three ecological categories based on their relationship to subterranean habitats:

1. **Stygobionti (true stygobionts — exclusive to subterranean waters)**: *Halicyclops troglodites*, *Megacyclops brachypus*, *Diacyclops languidoides hypnicola*, *Diacyclops languidoides zschokkei*, *Diacyclops languidoides* ssp., *Metacyclops subdolus*, *Speocyclops sardus*.

2. **Stygophile/Eustygophile (present in both surface and subterranean waters, with strong colonizing tendency underground)**: *Eucyclops serrulatus*, *Tropocyclops prasinus*, *Diacyclops crassicaudis*, *Diacyclops bisetosus*, *Diacyclops bicuspidatus lubbocki*, *Cyclops abissorum*.

3. **Stygoxene (surface-dwelling species, occasional/accidental in subterranean biocoenoses)**: *Macrocyclops albidus*, *Paracyclops fimbriatus*, *Acanthocyclops (Megacyclops) viridis*, *Microcyclops rubellus*, *Thermocyclops dybowskii*.

Habitats sampled include:
- **Phreatic aquifers** (fresh to slightly brackish) in sandy-calcareous (Cretaceous) and clayey-sandy (Cenozoic) formations.
- **Hyporheic zones** of rivers Liscia, Posada, Flumendosa, Palasole, and Cedrino — noted as particularly rich in diverse cyclopid fauna, with *D. languidoides* forms being the most abundant and frequent group overall, especially in hyporheic biocoenoses.
- **Cave waters** (cavernicolous systems), referenced from earlier studies (Bue Marino and Grotta Nuova caves).

This tripartite classification (stygobiont/stygophile/stygoxene) reflects the broader framework used in [[Subterranean Aquatic Ecology]] and is directly relevant to comparative studies of [[Groundwater Ecosystems]] Mediterranean-wide.

## Key Findings

- **13 species/subspecies** identified in the newly examined material; combined with prior literature, the total documented cyclopid fauna of Sardinian subterranean waters now stands at **18 species and subspecies**.
- **Seven taxa newly recorded** for Sardinian subterranean waters: *Macrocyclops albidus*, *Tropocyclops prasinus*, *Diacyclops bicuspidatus lubbocki*, *Diacyclops languidoides* (subspecies), *Megacyclops brachypus*, *Thermocyclops dybowskii*, *Cyclops abissorum*.
- **Megacyclops brachypus** — previously known only from two French caves (Grotte de l'Ancre and du Bèze, Haute-Garonne) — was recorded for the first time in Sardinia, substantially extending its known geographic range and confirming its validity as a distinct stygobiont species.
- **Speocyclops sardus** (Lindberg, 1956) is considered **endemic to Sardinia**.
- **Halicyclops troglodites** and **Metacyclops subdolus** show distribution restricted to the **Mediterranean basin**.
- **Diacyclops crassicaudis var. cosana** and **Megacyclops brachypus** exhibit **Tyrrhenian or Franco-Sardinian** distribution patterns, linking Sardinian subterranean fauna biogeographically to continental France.
- Most other recorded species are **cosmopolitan or Palearctic** in distribution.
- The prior comprehensive 1973 catalogue of Sardinian cavernicolous fauna (Puddu & Pirodda) made **no mention** of cyclopid copepods, underscoring the novelty of this contribution.
- The authors note the dataset is likely incomplete, as many caves and hyporheic environments — typically rich in diverse cyclopid fauna — remain unsampled; further research is expected to reveal additional taxa and clarify current discontinuous or ambiguous biogeographic patterns.
- Ongoing taxonomic revision of Italian *Diacyclops* is flagged as necessary to resolve the status of several populations morphologically intermediate between the nominal *D. languidoides* and its subspecies *zschokkei* and *hypnicola*.
