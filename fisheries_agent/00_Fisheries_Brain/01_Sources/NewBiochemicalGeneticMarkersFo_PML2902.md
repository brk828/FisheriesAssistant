# Biochemical Genetic Markers for Largemouth Bass

#ichthyology #marine-biology #micropterus

## Executive Summary

This study applies starch gel [[Electrophoresis]] to develop new [[Biochemical Genetic Markers]] for the [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), one of North America's most important sport fishes. The authors examined four hatchery stocks at the [[San Marcos National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center, Texas: a [[Northern Largemouth Bass]] stock, a [[Florida Largemouth Bass]] stock, and two domestic intergrade stocks ([[Inks Dam Stock]] and [[Marion Stock]]). Of more than 60 loci scored, at least 11 were polymorphic. Six had been studied previously by Philipp et al. (1983), and five were reported for the first time. The authors propose a panel of Florida-specific alleles at eight loci to monitor the introgression of Florida genomes into northern populations.

## Background & Description

**Species and significance.** The largemouth bass is a major recreational fish and the principal warmwater predator in many waters. Its original range extended from northern Mexico east to Florida and South Carolina, and north through the central and eastern Mississippi River drainage to the Great Lakes and southeastern Canada. Over the last 100 years, the species has been widely transplanted throughout the United States, southern Canada, and other regions. Some transplants have failed even when fish appeared healthy and the habitat seemed suitable, and genetic maladaptation has been proposed as one explanation.

**Subspecies.** Genetic variation in largemouth bass has long been recognized through two latitudinally separated subspecies: the northern *M. s. salmoides* and the Florida *M. s. floridanus*. Culture and stocking of both subspecies, often together in the same water body, may erode local ecological adaptation through genetic introgression.

**Stocks studied.**
- **Northern:** A stock one generation removed from a wild population in West Lake, northeastern Texas, reportedly isolated for over 50 years without stocking.
- **Florida:** A captive stock from an impoundment at the Texas A&M Aquaculture Research Center near College Station. The ancestral stock was obtained from Florida in the early 1970s.
- **Inks Dam:** A domesticated intergrade between the subspecies, serving as a reference stock. Its ancestry is unknown, but early electrophoresis suggested that 5-10% of its alleles came from the Florida subspecies.
- **Marion:** A domestic intergrade descended from a single pair of broodfish selected in 1966 at the Marion (Alabama) National Fish Hatchery. Its mean lateral line scale count of 61 was lower than typical for either subspecies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Genetic information provides a way to measure gene flow among populations and may correlate with environmental factors. Allele frequency data can clarify systematic relationships and help identify stocks suited to particular environments. More genetic markers also improve the accuracy of assigning individual fish to a population of origin or identifying specific hatchery stocks.

Because the Florida and northern subspecies show fixed allelic differences at several loci, allele frequencies can distinguish them even when individuals are morphologically unassignable. This matters for managing stocking, where the ecological fit of introduced fish may depend on their subspecific ancestry. The study notes that Florida largemouth bass had become popular in many breeding programs across North America.

## Key Findings

**Methods overview**
- Tissues (liver and skeletal muscle) were taken from anesthetized fish (0.2% tricaine) and stored at -90°C.
- Vertical starch gel electrophoresis followed Siciliano and Shaw (1976) and Morizot et al. (1977). Histochemical stain recipes came from Shaw and Prasad (1970) and related sources.
- Two buffers were used: Tris-EDTA-borate (TVB, pH 10) and Tris-citrate (TC, pH 7.0).
- Enzyme and locus nomenclature followed Philipp et al. (1983) and the conventions of Shows et al. (1979).
- Sample sizes were N = 30 for the offspring groups reported in the study, including the 1981 year-class offspring of the Northern and Marion broodstocks.

**Polymorphic loci (Table 1)**
- Of more than 60 loci scored, at least 11 were polymorphic.
- Six previously studied loci: glutamate-oxaloacetate transaminase-B (GOTB), isocitrate dehydrogenase-B (IDHB), malate dehydrogenase-1 (MDH1), superoxide dismutase-2 (SOD2), and others. Their Florida-northern distributions resembled those reported by Philipp et al. (1983).
- Five loci reported for the first time: [[Adenosine Deaminase]] (ADA), [[Galactose-1-Phosphate Uridyltransferase]] (GALT), [[Mannosephosphate Isomerase]] (MPI), [[Peptidase-3]] (PEP3), and [[Triosephosphate Isomerase]]-2 (TPI2).

**Diagnostic and population-specific results**
- Fixed differences between Florida and northern stocks were reported at GOTB and IDHB, following Harvey et al. (1980).
- GALT showed almost complete allele divergence between Florida and northern stocks. The fast-migrating variant occurred only in the Florida stock, with much reduced enzyme activity.
- A fast MPI variant appeared at a frequency of 0.5 in Florida stocks but was absent from both intergrade stocks, suggesting it is Florida-specific.
- The slow PEP3 variant occurred only in northern and intergrade stocks and was absent from Florida populations. Geographic variability in allele frequencies was also documented among northern populations.
- The fast TPI2 variant was observed only in the northern stock and one intergrade stock.
- ADA's slowly migrating variant was common only in the Florida stock, though it occurred at low frequency in one intergrade stock.
- Alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) variation was not resolved by Philipp et al. (1983), probably due to gel and buffer conditions. This study detected it.

**Proposed hybrid-monitoring panel**
- The authors suggest fixing Florida-specific alleles at eight loci in a hatchery stock to monitor introgression: ADA slow, ADH fast, GALT fast, GOTB3 and GOTB4 alleles, aGPDH fast, LDHB 3 allele, MPI fast, and SOD2 slow.
- If the eight loci are unlinked, about half (four) would be expected to remain heterozygous for Florida alleles after several generations of possible introgression.
- Hybrids should still be detectable after three generations of backcrossing to northern parents, because two of the eight loci would on average remain heterozygous for Florida alleles.
- Three restricted polymorphisms that occur only in northern and intergrade populations further improve the probability of correct hybrid identification.
- Predominance of either highly homozygous or highly heterozygous individuals with both Florida and northern alleles would suggest further generations of hybridization.

**Conclusions**
- The new markers should greatly facilitate individual identification of hatchery stocks and the detection of hybrids.
- The authors note that additional populations must be analyzed to clarify allele frequency variation among populations, particularly for the northern subspecies.
