# August 1987 Sampling at Buenos Aires NWR

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This report documents a stock assessment survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel on 15-16 August 1987 at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], Arizona, targeting refugial populations of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). These populations represent conservation stockings dating to November 1986, monitored via repeat sampling in January, February, and August 1987. Sampling combined gill nets, trammel nets, and bag seines across four isolated water bodies: [[Mormon Lake (Buenos Aires NWR)]], [[Road Camp Tank]], and Cas and [[Big Bertha Tank]]s. Results indicate persistence of razorback sucker in Mormon Lake (n=84) and bonytail chub in Road Camp Tank (n=114), but likely extirpation of bonytail from the Cas and Big Bertha tanks due to severe habitat contraction (drought-driven pond shrinkage to <20 cm depth in marginal habitat).

## Background & Description
Comparative mean total length (TL, mm) and weight (WT, g) data were tracked across four sampling events (Nov 1986 stocking, Jan 1987, Feb 1987, Aug 1987):

**Razorback Sucker (Mormon Lake):**
| Metric | Nov 1986 | Jan 1987 | Feb 1987 | Aug 1987 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TL (mm) | -- | 166 | 172 | 136 |
| WT (g) | -- | 65 | 58 | -- |
| N | -- | 16 | 12 | 84 |

**Bonytail Chub (Road Camp Tank):**
| Metric | Nov 1986 | Jan 1987 | Feb 1987 | Aug 1987 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TL (mm) | 76 | 109 | 143 | 145 |
| WT (g) | 6 | 14 | 28 | 20 |
| N | -- | 9 | 38 | 114 |

Notable trends:
- Razorback sucker mean TL **declined markedly** between February (172 mm) and August 1987 (136 mm), despite an increase in sample size (N=84). The report attributes this to possible sampling bias toward smaller individuals, size-selective mortality, poor growth among survivors, or unidentified factors — rather than a single confirmed cause.
- Bonytail chub showed **no appreciable growth** in TL between February (143 mm) and August (145 mm), with a notable decline in mean weight (28 g → 20 g), suggesting possible condition loss despite stable length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Small, ephemeral, drought-sensitive desert ponds/tanks subject to strong seasonal fluctuation in surface area and depth.
- **Mormon Lake:** Recently rebounded in size following inflow from local precipitation after a prior dry period; exhibited pronounced nocturnal "surface action" (likely feeding or spawning-related fish activity) during the survey.
- **Road Camp Tank:** Reduced in size relative to prior visits; co-occurring fauna included [[Bullfrog]] (*Rana catesbeiana*) and substantial biomass (~10 kg) of [[Tiger Salamander]] (*Ambystoma tigrinum*, cf. *mavortium*), indicating a mixed amphibian-fish refugial community.
- **Cas Tank & Big Bertha Tank:** Both severely contracted; Big Bertha reduced to a central marsh ringed by a donut-shaped band of open water <20 cm deep — habitat conditions likely too marginal to sustain bonytail chub, which appear to have been **extirpated** from both sites despite intensive seine sampling.
- **Sampling gear efficacy:** Short-term gill and trammel net sets were ineffective (zero catch) in Mormon Lake, while seining was highly effective, suggesting these species' association with shallow, vegetated, or nearshore microhabitats not well sampled by net sets.
- Routine water quality parameters and zooplankton samples were collected concurrently at sites retaining fish, indicating linked interest in prey base (zooplankton) supporting these refugial fish stocks — see [[Zooplankton Sampling]].

## Key Findings
- **84 razorback suckers** collected from [[Mormon Lake (Buenos Aires NWR)]] via seining; no captures via gill/trammel nets; no other fish species observed.
- **114 bonytail chub** collected from [[Road Camp Tank]] via a single 90-ft bag seine sweep; co-collected with bullfrogs and tiger salamanders; no other fish species observed.
- Bonytail chub in Road Camp Tank showed **stagnant growth** in length from February to August 1987, alongside a **decrease in mean weight**, raising concerns about body condition despite the population persisting.
- Razorback sucker mean TL **decreased substantially** from Feb to Aug 1987 even as sample size increased — flagged as an ambiguous result requiring further investigation (sampling bias vs. genuine size-structure shift vs. differential mortality).
- **Cas Tank and Big Bertha Tank** yielded **zero fish** despite intensive seining; the report concludes bonytail chub stocks have "likely been extirpated" from these two tanks, attributed to extreme habitat contraction.
- This survey forms part of a longitudinal repatriation-monitoring effort for two federally significant, endangered Colorado River Basin fishes ([[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]]) using isolated refuge tanks as conservation reservoirs, with distribution of findings to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service ([[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Albuquerque SE office) and the Arizona Game and Fish Department Nongame Branch.
- A subsample of collected fish was retained for **dietary analysis**, indicating parallel interest in trophic ecology beyond simple demographic monitoring.
