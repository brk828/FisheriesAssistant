## Executive Summary
This document is a regulatory amendment to U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811, issued to the Arizona Game and Fish Department. It authorizes the addition of a subpermittee (Lourdes Juarez Romero, Centro Ecológico de Sonora, Mexico) and grants authorization to import specimens of two federally protected fish taxa — [[Gila Purpurea]] (Yaqui Chub) and [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] (Desert Pupfish) — from Sonora, Mexico, into the United States. The stated purposes are scientific research, taxonomic description, and species recovery, with specimens distributed to academic researchers at Arizona State University and Oklahoma State University. While not a primary research paper, the letter provides valuable metadata on population source localities, sampling limits, and ongoing taxonomic/genetic research relevant to the conservation status of these two imperiled desert fishes.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body proportions) are provided in this permit document. However, the note indicates that:
- Preserved specimens of **[[Gila Purpurea]]** collected from the Rio Matape or Rio Sonora will be used by Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) for designation as **paratypes of a new species of *Gila***, implying ongoing taxonomic revision of the *Gila* genus complex in Sonora, Mexico, and that morphological/systematic characterization is actively in progress (see [[Gila Chub]] and [[Gila Robusta Complex]] for related taxonomic context).
- No morphological description is given for **[[Cyprinodon Macularius]]**; specimens are being imported live for genetic (not morphometric) analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Gila Purpurea]] (Yaqui Chub)**: Native to the Río Yaqui basin drainage system, inhabiting freshwater rivers and streams in Sonora, Mexico, including the **Rio Matape** and **Rio Sonora**. This species occupies desert stream habitats characteristic of the arid Southwest/Northern Mexico, and is listed under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, indicating a restricted, fragmented, and vulnerable range.
- **[[Cyprinodon Macularius]] (Desert Pupfish)**: Native to the **Rio Sonoyta**, Sonora, Mexico, part of a broader historic range across the Sonoran Desert including the lower Colorado River basin, Salton Sea basin, and associated desert springs/wetlands. This species tolerates extreme environmental conditions (high salinity, high temperature, low oxygen) typical of isolated desert aquatic refugia. Its populations are highly fragmented and endangered, requiring active genetic management (see [[Desert Pupfish Conservation]]).
- Both species exemplify the broader ecological pattern of **desert fish endemism**, where isolated drainage basins in arid environments harbor genetically distinct, often undescribed or cryptic lineages under significant anthropogenic pressure (habitat loss, water diversion, non-native species introduction).

## Key Findings
- The permit amendment formally authorizes **cross-border import** of two federally endangered fish species from Mexico into the United States for research and conservation purposes, reflecting binational cooperation between the [[Centro Ecológico de Sonora]] and U.S. institutions.
- **Taxonomic research**: Preserved *Gila purpurea* specimens from the Rio Matape/Rio Sonora are being used to formally describe and designate paratypes for a **new species within the *Gila* genus**, suggesting that the *Gila purpurea* population, or a closely related population, may represent unrecognized species-level diversity.
- **Genetic research**: Up to **200 live *Cyprinodon macularius*** specimens from the Rio Sonoyta may be imported specifically for **genetic studies**, to be divided between Dr. Anthony Echelle (Oklahoma State University) and Dr. Thomas Dowling (Arizona State University) — indicating active population genetics/phylogeographic research on Desert Pupfish populations spanning the U.S.–Mexico border.
- The permit imposes **chain-of-custody documentation requirements** (cover letters specifying recipients and specimen counts) for every specimen transfer, reflecting strict regulatory oversight under the Endangered Species Act for interstate/international transport of listed species.
- The original subpermit (dated April 6, 1989) and all other conditions remain unchanged, situating this amendment within a broader, ongoing program of endangered desert fish research and recovery coordinated by the Arizona Game and Fish Department and USFWS Region 2.
- This document underscores the role of regulatory/permitting infrastructure in enabling [[Species Recovery Programs]] for endangered freshwater fishes such as [[Yaqui Chub]] and [[Desert Pupfish]], and highlights inter-institutional collaboration as a key mechanism for taxonomic and genetic research on endangered desert ichthyofauna.
