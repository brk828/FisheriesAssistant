## Executive Summary
This record documents a June 1992 telephone communication between the U.S. Forest Service (Globe Ranger District) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Phoenix Field Office) regarding Endangered Species Act consultation procedures for the Hicks-Pikes Peak Allotment Management Plan (AMP). A newly prepared Biological Evaluation (BE) was pending submission to initiate formal consultation, with clarification provided on proper Forest Service protocol (submission via Regional Office rather than directly from the District). Critically, the call also relayed field observations from a June 18, 1992 site visit to Sycamore Spring, where infrastructure damage (a broken supply pipe, likely from winter/spring flooding) had caused the dirt tank to dry completely, threatening a resident population of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Small, isolated desert spring-fed water sources — specifically an earthen ("dirt") tank and a concrete trough at [[Sycamore Spring]], part of a stock-watering system on Forest Service grazing allotment land.
- **Hydrological dependency**: The population is entirely reliant on artificial infrastructure (pipes feeding tanks from the spring source) to maintain surface water; a damaged pipe caused complete water loss in the dirt tank.
- **Habitat degradation event**: A flood event (winter/spring 1991–1992) damaged the pipe supplying both the concrete and dirt tanks, leading to the dirt tank drying out entirely and reduced water levels in the concrete trough.
- **Population vulnerability**: The species' persistence at this site is precarious due to its dependence on a single, human-maintained water delivery system in an arid landscape — a common conservation concern for isolated [[Gila Topminnow]] refugia populations.
- **Recovery challenge noted**: Even after pipe repair, the extremely dry substrate of the tank was absorbing significant water input, delaying the reestablishment of surface water needed to support the fish population.
- **Land use context**: The habitat occurs within an active grazing allotment (Hicks-Pikes Peak AMP), placing it at the intersection of livestock management infrastructure and endangered species habitat.

## Key Findings
- A new Biological Evaluation (BE) had been completed for the Hicks-Pikes Peak AMP and was being routed for formal Section 7 consultation with the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]].
- Proper procedural protocol was clarified: Forest Service formal consultations must be initiated through the Regional Office (RO), not directly from the District level.
- During a site visit on 6-18-92, biologists found the dirt tank at [[Sycamore Spring]] completely dry, and were unable to visually confirm any [[Gila Topminnow]] in the remaining water in the concrete trough.
- The water supply pipe had been damaged, likely by flooding during winter/spring 1992, disrupting flow to both the concrete and dirt tanks.
- Repairs to the pipe were completed, but as of the call date, insufficient surface water had accumulated in the dirt tank due to substrate dryness absorbing inflow.
- FWS recommended urgent follow-up by [[Jerry Stefferud]] (Tonto National Forest T&E fish biologist) to confirm whether the Gila topminnow population survived the habitat disruption.
- This event highlights the fragility of small, isolated Gila topminnow refugia populations dependent on maintained artificial water sources within grazing allotments.
