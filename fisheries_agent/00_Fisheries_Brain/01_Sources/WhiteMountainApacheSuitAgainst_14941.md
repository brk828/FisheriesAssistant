# White Mountain Apache Tribe v. James G. Watt et al.: Salt River Water Rights Litigation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #water-rights

## Executive Summary

This document is a litigation file and legal complaint from the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] against federal and Arizona state officials, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Arizona (Civ. 83-2045 PCT CAM, filed October 20, 1983). It concerns the Tribe's reserved water rights to the [[Salt River]] and its tributaries, grazing, forest and irrigable lands within the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]], and alleged mismanagement by [[Interior Secretary James G. Watt]] in favor of federal reclamation projects. The appended materials include the 1871 covenant history, boundary descriptions, the Federal Register notice on [[Central Arizona Project]] water allocations, a Maricopa County water rights claim, and a tribal resolution. Ichthyology is not addressed; the aquatic content concerns stream flow, watershed hydrology and water allocation rather than fish.

The procedural history shows the first complaint was dismissed in March 1984 for the State of Arizona and Judge Goodfarb, and that the federal claims were largely affirmed by the [[Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals]] in March and June 1986. The mismanagement claim survived in amended form, and a later dismissal order (October 29, 1986) was appealed by the Tribe in December 1986.

## Background & Description

- **Parties:** Plaintiff White Mountain Apache Tribe, represented by William H. Veeder and Robert C. Brauchli. Defendants included Secretary of the Interior James G. Watt, Attorney General William French Smith, Secretary of Agriculture John R. Block, Forest Service officials, the State of Arizona, and a Maricopa County Superior Court judge.
- **Reservation origin:** Established through a covenant negotiated by Vincent Colyer at Camp Apache in September 1871, with Executive Orders dated November 9, 1871 and December 12, 1872. The northern boundary follows "the South Edge of the Black Mesa," described as the watershed divide between the Salt and Little Colorado rivers.
- **Water basis:** The Tribe asserts a [[Winters Doctrine]] reserved water right with a priority date of time immemorial, based on historic irrigation (the "Agricultural Apaches").
- **Land and forest:** The complaint cites approximately 500,000 acres of forest originally within the reservation, and about 14,000 acres of disputed forest land in the [[Sitgreaves National Forest]] and [[Apache National Forest]].
- **Appendices:** Include Executive Orders (Appendix A), parcel metes-and-bounds descriptions (Appendix B), Solicitor's Opinion M-36762 (Appendix C), the Federal Register CAP allocation notice (Appendix D), the Tribe's historic and future irrigated land claims (Appendix E), and Tribal Council Resolution (Appendix F, March 9, 1982).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The complaint's environmental claims concern watershed hydrology and land condition rather than aquatic species:

- **Overgrazing and erosion:** Non-Indian livestock trespass is alleged to have compacted topsoil, increased runoff, and caused sheet and gully erosion throughout the reservation. The complaint states that the Reservation "will literally be washed away" without correction.
- **Stream degradation:** Alleged vegetation clearing along streams (including Cibecue and Carrizo creeks) is said to have lowered stream beds to bedrock, rendering irrigation ditches useless and draining groundwater that supported forage.
- **Forest operations:** Alleged clear-cutting and prescribed burning are said to have baked soils, raised runoff, and increased sedimentation. The complaint refers to a beetle infestation in a 69,000-acre spruce forest.
- **Hydrology:** By 1895 the Salt River near Phoenix was said to carry its entire natural flow for non-Indian irrigation. The Tribe claims about 100,000 acre-feet annually is induced to flow off the reservation.
- **Irrigable acreage (Tribe's studies, 1979):** 49,859.09 irrigable acres, requiring 5.38 acre-feet per acre (268,169 acre-feet annually for irrigation); total needs of roughly 250,000 acre-feet annually for all uses.
- **Claimed historic irrigation:** Approximately 3,145 acres with 15,143 acre-feet of water duty; future claims of 14,558 acres and 59,640 acre-feet (Appendix E).
- **Central Arizona Project allocations (Appendix D):** The Secretary allocated 309,828 acre-feet annually to 12 Indian entities, and 630,000–640,000 acre-feet to municipal and industrial users. The Federal Register notice also discusses effluent exchanges, Sonoran Desert impacts, and roughly 34,500 acres of farmland conversion.

## Key Findings

- **Procedural outcome:** Claims against the State of Arizona and Judge Goodfarb were dismissed on March 15–16, 1984. Final judgment on federal claims was entered February 14, 1985, with the mismanagement claim proceeding by amendment. The Ninth Circuit affirmed on March 7, 1986 and denied rehearing on June 3, 1986.
- **Second dismissal:** On October 29, 1986 the court dismissed the action without prejudice and denied the Tribe's summary judgment motion; judgment followed on October 31, 1986, and the Tribe appealed on December 30, 1986.
- **Central claim:** Federal officials, including Secretary Watt, allegedly suppressed Tribal water development and induced runoff for the [[Salt River Federal Reclamation Project]] and the [[Central Arizona Project]].
- **Conflict of interest:** The Tribe argues the Department of Justice cannot represent both the Tribe and the Interior Department, citing Ninth Circuit language on inadequate representation.
- **Boundary dispute:** The Tribe contends about 14,000 acres were erroneously excluded in a 1887 survey. Solicitor's Opinion M-36762 (1967) found no "gross error" requiring resurvey.
- **Kent and Gila decrees:** The Tribe argues the 1910 Kent Decree and 1935 Gila River Decree improperly limited Indian reserved rights, and that the Tribe is not bound by them.
- **McCarran Amendment:** The Tribe disputes that the Maricopa County W-1 adjudication is a "general adjudication" under 43 U.S.C. 666 and asserts sovereign immunity from state suit.
- **Relief sought:** Declaratory and injunctive relief, including quieting title to the 14,000 acres, enjoining state jurisdiction over the Salt River rights, and barring Interior and Justice officials from further action in the W-1 proceedings.
- **Resolution (March 9, 1982):** The Tribal Council demanded retraction of the January 14, 1982 water rights claim prepared by Interior and Justice staff, citing data from the VIN 1974 Report and HKM Engineering work, and voted 10–0 to adopt the resolution.
