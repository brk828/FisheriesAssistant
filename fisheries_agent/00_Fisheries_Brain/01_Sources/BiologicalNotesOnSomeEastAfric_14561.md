# Biological Notes on Some East African Aquatic Heteroptera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-heteroptera #entomology #freshwater-ecology

> **Note:** This source concerns **aquatic true bugs (Heteroptera)**, not fish. The note is structured per the standard template but synthesized faithfully to the taxa actually studied (Gerridae, Veliidae, Notonectidae, Corixidae, etc.) across East African freshwater and saline aquatic habitats.

## Executive Summary

This 1955 paper by H. B. N. Hynes presents biological and ecological field notes on aquatic Heteroptera ("water bugs" and "water skaters") collected across 74 localities in [[Ethiopia]], [[Kenya]], and [[Somalia]] between 1944 and 1946. The study spans highland streams, lakes, and ponds of the Ethiopian and Kenyan highlands, and the arid lowlands of northern Kenya and Somalia, including ephemeral "wadi" watercourses. The work — largely descriptive natural history rather than experimental — documents habitat associations, altitudinal distributions, wing-morph (macropterous vs. apterous) variation, shoaling behavior, and long-distance dispersal by flight in several species, most notably **Limnogonus leptocerus**, **Microvelia gracillima**, and **Mesovelia vittigera**, which colonize temporary desert pools far (>80 km) from permanent water within days of rainfall. Taxonomic identifications were provided by Professor R. Poisson (Université de Rennes), who also described several new species/forms from the collection.

## Background & Description

- **Author/Institution:** H. B. N. Hynes, Department of Zoology, University of Liverpool; published in *Proceedings of the Royal Entomological Society of London* (A) 30, Parts 4–6 (June 1955).
- **Scope:** Field collections and habitat notes on aquatic [[Heteroptera]] (Gymnocerata — surface-dwelling skaters — and Cryptocerata — submerged "true water bugs") made opportunistically during travel across three biogeographic regions:
  1. **Central and southern Ethiopian highlands** (per Scott, 1952)
  2. **Kikuyu highlands north of Nairobi, Kenya**
  3. **Low, hot, dry belt** from Turkana (NW Kenya) through the Northern Province to Jubaland and Somalia
- **Habitat classification used by the author:**
  - Small rapid streams/springs without vegetation
  - Sluggish streams (<5 m wide) with emergent vegetation
  - Large rivers (>10 m wide) with aquatic/emergent vegetation
  - Lakes (some saline, e.g. [[Lake Zwai]], [[Lake Awasa]], [[Lake Langana]])
  - Permanent ponds, dams, ditches, irrigation dykes
  - Temporary watercourses ("wadis") and pools in semi-desert country
- **Taxonomic breadth:** Families Gerridae, Veliidae, Hebridae, Hydrometridae, Mesoveliidae, Notonectidae, Pleidae, Corixidae, Naucoridae, Belostomidae, Ranatridae, Nepidae — dozens of species/subspecies, several new to science (described by Poisson, 1949, 1951).
- Prior ecological literature on this group in East Africa was almost absent; the only comparable prior ecological work cited is [[Hutchinson 1930]] on Cryptocerata from southern Ethiopia.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Altitudinal gradient as key structuring variable:** Species distributions correlate strongly with altitude (from sea level/near sea level in Somalia to ~2700 m in the Ethiopian highlands), plausibly linked to temperature and/or habitat type availability rather than altitude per se.
- **Flow regime specialization:** Within genera such as [[Rhagovelia]] and [[Microvelia]], congeners partition habitat by current speed and permanence — e.g., *Rhagovelia nigricans* (slow water, 930–1700 m) vs. *R. hynesi* (swift water, 1650–2400 m) vs. *R. infernalis africana* (lowland <200 m, permanent and temporary rivers) — a pattern the author notes mirrors his earlier findings in [[Trinidad]] (Hynes, 1948).
- **Salinity tolerance:** Several species occupy saline or brackish lakes (e.g. *Hynesionella aethiopica* on saline [[Lake Zwai]] and the Waracallo river; *Micronecta scutellaris* on saline [[Lake Awasa]]), while congeners (*Micronecta isis*) are restricted to slightly saline or fresh water — indicating fine-scale niche partitioning by water chemistry.
- **Ephemeral desert pool ecology:** A central ecological theme is the colonization of short-lived rain-filled pools in semi-desert East Africa by strong-flying species. Colonization occurs within days of filling; breeding (evidenced by copulating pairs and nymphs) can occur in these transient habitats despite total desiccation between rains.
- **Wing polymorphism (macroptery/aptery) as an ecological indicator:** Across multiple families, apterous (wingless) morphs are generally associated with **permanent, stable water bodies**, while macropterous (winged) morphs dominate populations on **temporary or marginal habitats**, reflecting a dispersal-vs-residency trade-off. This pattern recurs in Gerridae, Veliidae, and Mesoveliidae.
- **Trophic interactions:** Several species were observed as predators/scavengers of drowning terrestrial insects (e.g., *Rhagadotarsus hutchinsoni* feeding on drowning Jassidae; *Naboandalus patrizii* on drowning insects). *Laccocoris limigenus* appears to feed heavily on the corixid *Micronecta isis* on the shore of saline Lake Langana, illustrating a simple predator-prey littoral food web alongside Libellulid nymphs and Hydroporine beetles.
- **Long-distance aerial dispersal:** Evidence suggests some species use flight (potentially assisted by turbulence beneath strato-cumulus rain clouds) to track and colonize scattered rainfall events across otherwise inhospitable semi-desert, a striking dispersal strategy for small-bodied aquatic insects.
- **Biogeographic isolation:** *Naboandalus patrizii* populations on the Juba River versus the Webi Shebelli (separated by ~150 km of semi-desert) show morphological divergence (form *hynesi*) attributed to near-total lack of alate (winged) dispersal individuals — a case of incipient allopatric differentiation driven by restricted gene flow.

## Key Findings

### Family [[Gerridae]] (water striders)
- *Gerris gobana*: restricted to a few small streams/springs in Ethiopian highlands; all specimens apterous.
- *Gerris zuqualana*: found on Lemna-covered ponds and emergent vegetation; both winged and apterous forms present, suggesting habitat-linked wing polymorphism.
- *Gerris severini*: confined to two upland lakes with Potamogeton cover.
- *Gerris swakopensis*: widespread >1500 m; still/slow water required for breeding (nymphs/apterous forms), winged adults disperse onto unsuitable fast water.
- *Limnogonus hypoleucus*: breeds only in shaded permanent pools at medium altitude (1050–1300 m); winged adults range more widely.
- **[[Limnogonus leptocerus]]**: Key dispersal species — restricted to <1400 m; colonizes temporary desert pools within days of filling; almost all specimens macropterous; unusually flies readily off the water surface when disturbed (unlike congeners); mating pairs observed on temporary pools >80 km from permanent water.
- *Eurymetra natalensis*: abundant on shaded running highland water (>1500 m); all specimens apterous, contradicting Esaki's (1928) report of it on stagnant water elsewhere in Africa.
- *Naboandalus patrizii*: large nocturnal shoals on turbid rivers (Juba, Webi Shebelli); daytime shade-seeking behavior; near-total absence of winged morphs linked to geographic differentiation between river systems.
- *Hynesionella aethiopica*: endemic to the saline Lake Zwai rift valley system.

### Family [[Veliidae]]
- *Tenagovelia sjoestedti*: breeds on slow highland water (~1500 m); isolated apterous individuals found downstream, possibly flood-transported.
- *Rhagovelia* (3 spp.): clear altitudinal/flow-based zonation among congeners (see Ecological Context); segregation by instar within shoals.
- *Microvelia gracillima*: most ecologically flexible species recorded — occupies highland vegetated slow waters (mixed wing morphs) and lowland temporary pools (macropterous only); disperses ≥80 km to colonize rain pools; does not fly when disturbed (hides instead), unlike *L. leptocerus*.
- Other *Microvelia* spp. (*venustissima*, *kijabiensis*, *alluaudi*, *major kilimandjaronis*) show distinct altitude/flow-type habitat segregation and wing-morph/altitude correlations.

### Family [[Hydrometridae]]
- *Hydrometra albolineolata* (all winged) and *H. somaliensis* (mostly winged, some brachypterous) occupy overlapping but non-co-occurring habitat ranges from rivers to temporary pools.

### Family [[Mesoveliidae]]
- **[[Mesovelia vittigera]]**: broad habitat and altitude range; apterous forms restricted to vegetated still/slow water; winged forms disperse to temporary pools far from permanent water; wing mutilation phenomenon (noted by Poisson 1933) observed even in temporary-pool populations, likely rendering some individuals flightless post-dispersal.

### Family [[Notonectidae]] & [[Pleidae]]
- *Enithares glauca* (high-altitude stony streams, 2400–2700 m) vs. *E. sobria* (lower altitude, vegetated lentic/lotic habitats) show habitat divergence.
- *Anisops worthingtoni*: strong flier, found in temporary Somali pools far from its type locality (Lake Rudolf); supports long-distance dispersal capacity in Cryptocerata.
- *A. varia varia* previously reported "absent" from central Ethiopia by Hutchinson (1930) but confirmed present near Addis Ababa by this study — corrects a prior biogeographic record.

### Family [[Corixidae]]
- *Micronecta dimidiata* ranges up to 2700 m, exceeding the previously suggested upper altitudinal limit (2400 m) for the genus (Hutchinson, 1930).
- *M. scutellaris* vs. *M. isis*: fine-scale niche partitioning by salinity within the same lake system (Lake Awasa/Langana complex).
- *Pseudoglaenocorixa hugoscotti*: range extended from Blue Nile tributaries (Hutchinson, 1930) to Awash River tributaries, both within the Ethiopian highland belt.

### Family [[Naucoridae]], [[Belostomidae]], [[Nepidae]], [[Ranatridae]]
- *Laccocoris limigenus*: dominant littoral predator on saline Lake Langana, apparently feeding on abundant *Micronecta isis*.
- *Sphaerodema grassei*: found with eggs attached to male wings (male egg-carrying behavior), consistent with known Belostomatid reproductive biology.
- *Ranatra bottegoi*: remarkable long-distance flight record — collected at an isolated pool (Mulla's Pool, Galkayu) >100 km from permanent water, which had only recently refilled after two years dry; extends known range beyond Somalia (Mancini, 1939) into Kenyan/Ethiopian highlands.
- *Laccotrephes brachialis* restricted to high-altitude streams (>1700 m), while *L. ater* and *L. calcaratus* occur at lower altitudes.

### Overarching Conclusions
- Each genus examined (e.g. *Gerris*, *Rhagovelia*, *Microvelia*, *Micronecta*) shows **congeneric habitat partitioning** by altitude, flow regime, salinity, or vegetation structure.
- Several small-bodied surface-dwelling species (*L. leptocerus*, *M. gracillima*, *M. vittigera*) demonstrate **remarkable long-range aerial dispersal** (potentially >80 km) enabling rapid colonization of ephemeral desert rain pools — a key adaptation to the East African arid climate's episodic rainfall.
- Wing-morph (macroptery/aptery) frequently correlates with habitat permanence, supporting a general dispersal-polymorphism model in aquatic Heteroptera.
- The study represents the first systematic attempt to define habitat preferences for East African aquatic Heteroptera at species level, building on the purely taxonomic literature that preceded it.
