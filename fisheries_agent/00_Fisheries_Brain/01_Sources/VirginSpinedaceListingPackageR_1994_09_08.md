## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 professional correspondence from Dr. Paul C. Marsh (Research Professor, Arizona State University Center for Environmental Studies) to Robert D. Williams of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS). The letter provides an expert scientific review of the proposal to list the [[Virgin Spinedace]] as a threatened species under the Endangered Species Act (Federal Register 59: 25875-25880). Dr. Marsh affirms both the scientific accuracy and biological justification of the listing proposal, citing water development in the [[Virgin River Basin]] as the primary driver of population decline and extinction risk. This is not a primary morphological or ecological data paper, but rather a scientific peer-review letter supporting conservation action.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body proportions) are presented in this correspondence. The source is a regulatory review letter rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study. For morphological details, the letter references primary literature cited in the FWS proposal, including [[Valdez Et Al 1991]] and [[Addley And Hardy 1993]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[Virgin Spinedace]] inhabits the [[Virgin River Basin]], a tributary system within the greater Colorado River drainage in the southwestern United States (Utah, Arizona, Nevada region).
- **Primary Threat**: Past and ongoing water development activities (e.g., damming, diversion, flow alteration) within the Virgin River basin are identified as the principal factor endangering the species' continued existence.
- **Compounding Factors**: The letter notes "other factors" beyond water development also contribute to the species' biological peril, though these are not enumerated in detail within this excerpt.
- **Conservation Status Context**: The species' population trajectory and habitat degradation trends were assessed as consistent with the legal definition of "threatened" under the Endangered Species Act.

## Key Findings
- Dr. Paul C. Marsh, an independent expert reviewer, concluded that the FWS's 18 May 1994 proposal to list [[Virgin Spinedace]] as threatened **correctly presents available scientific information**, based on cited references available from FWS.
- Marsh independently affirmed that the listing proposal is **biologically supportable**, primarily due to demonstrable and foreseeable impacts of water development in the [[Virgin River Basin]].
- This letter represents a **reaffirmation** of prior support expressed by Marsh in an earlier communication (01 July 1994), indicating sustained scientific consensus favoring protective listing.
- The correspondence underscores the role of independent scientific peer review in the regulatory process for species listing under the **Endangered Species Act**.
- Key reference documents cited in the underlying FWS proposal include [[Valdez Et Al 1991]] and [[Addley And Hardy 1993]], which contain the primary biological and hydrological data supporting the listing decision.
