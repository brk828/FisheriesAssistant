# Humpback Chub Propagation Plan for the Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This February 1993 draft plan from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Research Branch, authored by Diann M. Smith) proposes a method for collecting reproductive products (eggs and milt) of endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) from the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), Coconino County, Arizona. The eggs are intended for temperature shock and tolerance studies and otolith age verification, which will be described in a separate document. Three earlier field spawning attempts on the LCR population had failed, so the plan lists likely causes and a remedy for each, then sets out a spawning, egg transport, incubation and larval rearing protocol for a spring 1993 effort. Appendices cover equipment and personnel, and a contingency plan to hold about 20 adult broodstock at the [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]].

## Background & Description

*Gila cypha* is a native cyprinid of the Colorado River system. Adults were previously held in captivity for a year or more and spawned by hormone-induced gonadal maturation, both from LCR fish and from the [[Black Rocks Population]] of the Colorado River. A successful field spawning effort on the Black Rocks population was reported (Valdez and Valdez-Gonzales, 1991). Prior work is summarized in the [[Literature Cited]] (about 25 references, including Hamman 1982a, 1982b; Marsh 1985; Kubly 1990; Hines 1992).

Reported adult handling and spawning parameters include:
- Ripeness testing by gentle flank pressure; ova/milt expressing easily from ripe fish; milt thick and milky-white when ripe.
- Two to three ripe males per female before females are prepared or injected.
- Induction with 4 mg/kg body weight acetone-dried carp pituitary, injected intraperitoneally daily for 1-3 days; final maturation expected within 20-24 hours.
- Hatchery water target of 17-20°C year-round, with spawning-water adjusted to 18-23°C.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Previous LCR spawning failures were attributed to five groups of causes, each paired with a proposed solution:
- **Sperm diluent quality**: use diluent of known freshness; test an undiluted subsample.
- **Sediment and oxygen**: fertilize and incubate in [[Bubbling Ponds]] water with same-day transport, or de-silt the LCR supply for on-site incubation.
- **Heath tray flow**: use complete [[Heath Tray Apparatus]] to direct flow; measure dissolved oxygen (DO) of the supply.
- **Egg and larval sensitivity**: transport eggs after water hardening and within 24 hours of fertilization.
- **Mechanical trauma in transit**: airlift product to Highway 89 to avoid a 16-mile rough dirt road drive; pack subsamples moist, not submerged.
- **Temperature fluctuation in transit**: use styrofoam crates limiting change to no more than 1-2°C/day.

Water-quality monitoring includes hourly [[Hydrolab]] readings of temperature, conductivity, pH and DO, plus turbidity by nephelometer 2-4 times daily. Flow is checked every 4 hours and by a low-flow alarm. Water from the LCR and Bubbling Ponds is to be blended over 2 hours when moving eggs between sources.

## Key Findings

**Proposed spawning and handling protocol**
- Spawning effort of 3-4 days, to begin when adults in spawning condition are reported in the LCR, with equipment staged at Salt Trail camp.
- Adults captured by [[Hoop Net]], PIT-tagged (with sterile Betadine equipment) except ripe females, and held in flat-bottomed live-cars.
- Stripping of one female with 2-3 males into sperm diluent. About 10% of each batch is kept undiluted, and a further 10% is incubated in LCR water.
- Bentonite treatment (1/2 cup per gallon) to reduce adhesiveness, and eggs washed and water-hardened for 1-2 hours.
- Naturally ripened eggs are kept separate from induced eggs to compare hatching success.

**Egg transport and incubation**
- Eggs submerged in tempered Bubbling Ponds water in double bags with oxygen, shipped in insulated crates, and transported the same day spawned.
- Approximately 10% of eggs are shipped drained, to test for water-movement trauma.
- Daily removal of non-viable and fungused eggs; about 5 viable eggs sampled daily and preserved in 5% non-buffered formalin.
- Contingency on-site incubation at the LCR, using a gravity-fed sediment basin system or 5-gallon buckets with oxygen stones.

**Larval care**
- Larvae are caught in Saran cloth baskets under each Heath rack and checked 3-4 times daily.
- Larvae held in aerated aquaria at 20-21°C with low flow-through.
- Plankton bloom promoted with ammonium phosphate and commercial trout starter; larvae fed Bio Kyowa B-250 µm or 2700 µm diets over the following 2-4 weeks.

**Contingency broodstock plan (Appendix B)**
- Goal: about 20 adults (12 females, 8 males) retained as hatchery stock at Bubbling Ponds for Section 7 Conservation Measures research.
- Fish held in six 6-foot-diameter circular raceways, with disease-free rainbow trout as companions.
- Treatments include 1.0 ppm Droncit for 24 hours for possible Bothriocephalus infection, and 167 ppm formalin every other day for seven treatments for Ichthyophthirius.

**Program status and review**
- Plan was circulated on 24 February 1993 for comment by 12 March 1993, to allow permits and logistical support before the season.
- Personnel: Diann Smith, Phil Hines, Rob Clarkson, Mark Lupher, plus one full-time and one part-time wildlife assistant.
