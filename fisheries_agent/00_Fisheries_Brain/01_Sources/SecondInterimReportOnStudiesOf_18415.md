# Second Interim Report on Studies of Growth and Survival of Razorback Sucker under Experimental Conditions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This second interim report, prepared by Diana Fapoulias of [[Arizona State University]] for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Office of Endangered Species (Albuquerque, NM), documents an 8-week pond experiment on larval [[Razorback Sucker]] reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Twelve ponds were fertilized at high, medium, or low rates, and the study tested whether differences in primary productivity (measured as an index of chlorophyll a) translated into differences in larval length and weight. Chlorophyll differed significantly among treatments in week 1, and length and weight diverged between high and low treatments by week 4. The report is dated 4 June 1986.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a native fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]] and is listed as endangered. This report continues an earlier study (Fapoulias 1985) on larval growth and survival in hatchery ponds. Its stated purpose is to present chlorophyll data and length-weight relationships of fish over an 8-week experimental period.

Larvae were produced by [[Artificial Fertilization]] (following Hamman 1985) from wild [[Lake Mohave]] brood fish. Stocking occurred at approximately five days post-hatch in early February 1985, at a high density. The source text is partly illegible at this point; the stocking density and total length at stocking cannot be reliably read from the scan. Ponds were drained and fish recovered on 18–19 April 1985.

The experiment used twelve ponds at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], each about 0.1 hectare (ha). Treatments were:

- **High fertilization:** alfalfa pellets plus phosphate fertilizer (rate partly garbled in scan)
- **Medium fertilization:** approximately half the high rate (scan reads "170 kg/ha"; verify)
- **Low treatment:** no fertilization
- Half of each fertilizer amount was reapplied at intervals through week 5.

Chlorophyll was measured weekly. Water samples were filtered through 0.8-micron [[Millipore Filters]], extracted in 90% acetone, and read at 445 nm on a [[Hach Spectrophotometer]]. Values are reported as an index only, because the study did not correct for turbidity or measure phaeophytin a, owing to technical failures and equipment limitations.

Table 1 gives the experimental schedule:

| Week | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ponds fertilized and filled; chlorophyll measurements begun |
| 2 | Ponds fertilized (half initial rate) |
| 4 | Ponds fertilized |
| 5 | Ponds treated with [[Igran Herbicide]] |
| 6 | Ponds treated with Igran and fertilized |
| 8 | Ponds emptied and fish recovered |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Primary productivity was the central environmental variable. The fertilization regime was designed to create a food-supply gradient for larvae. Chlorophyll values confirmed that high-fertilization ponds carried the most algal biomass and low-fertilization ponds the least.

All ponds were treated with the herbicide Igran to suppress aquatic macrophytes and aid fish recovery. The authors note that Igran may also have controlled some green algae (Jensen and Hamman 1985). However, chlorophyll declines began before herbicide application, so the decline is not attributed to Igran alone. The likely explanation offered for the decline is a drop in [[Zooplankton]] abundance (unpublished data cited by the author).

Pond-to-pond variability was substantial. The author notes that this variability was considerable, though week 1 still showed a significant difference among the three fertilization groups.

Sampling methods were:

- [[Plankton Tows]] and dip nets across all ponds
- Night-time spotlighting to attract larvae
- Preservation in buffered formalin with sugar added to limit pigment denaturation
- Length measured with an ocular micrometer on a dissecting microscope
- Weight measured on preserved, blotted-dry specimens using a [[Mettler HS1 Balance]]

## Key Findings

**1. Chlorophyll response to fertilization**
- Chlorophyll peaked in high-treatment ponds during week 1, then fell sharply to week 6.
- A gradual decline continued until week 7, when a slight increase was observed.
- Medium and low treatments peaked at week 2 and followed the same overall pattern.
- Only in week 1 was chlorophyll significantly higher in high-treatment ponds than in medium and low treatments (reported as p<0.1; the symbol is garbled in the scan).
- Chlorophyll values were reported as an index and not corrected for turbidity.

**2. Larval growth response**
- Statistically significant divergence in length and weight between high and low treatments first appeared at week 4. Razorback sucker larvae are described as beginning to respond to food differences at this point.
- Length differences between medium and low treatments began in week 6.
- Weight differed significantly between high and low treatments from week 4 onward. Medium and low treatments differed in weight only at weeks 6 and 8.
- High and medium treatments did not differ in growth rate, and both grew faster than low-treatment ponds.

**3. Growth regression analysis**
- Growth data were log-transformed and analyzed by regression (Appendix C).
- Slopes differed significantly between high and low, and between medium and low treatments. No difference was found between medium and high.
- Length-weight regressions showed no difference among treatments. Example fitted equations for the length-weight relationship were:
  - log(Y) = −3.452 + 3.977 log(X)
  - log(Y) = −4.16 + 4.775 log(X)

**4. Interpretation**
- Fertilization produced measurable differences in primary productivity, which were followed by differences in larval growth.
- The author concludes that the food-supply gradient, not herbicide treatment, most plausibly drove the observed differences, though the decline in chlorophyll remains partly unexplained.

**5. Data quality limitations**
- Chlorophyll values are indices only.
- Several appendix tables (weekly means, ranges, and regression equations for weight, total length, and standard length) are partly illegible in the scanned copy, so exact values for those tables are not reproduced here.
