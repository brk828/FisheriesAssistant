# Stocking of Farmers Pond, Imperial NWR with Razorback Suckers

## Executive Summary
This January 1995 memorandum documents a coordinated fish-stocking operation involving the transport and release of approximately 8,500 juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally endangered [[Catostomidae]] species native to the Colorado River Basin. Fish were reared at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] from broodstock spawned on [[Lake Mohave]] in 1993 using controlled paired matings. The cohort was split between open-water release sites in coves along [[Lake Havasu]] and a contained refuge pond (Farmers Pond) at [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]], representing a joint effort between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service ([[Parker Fish and Wildlife Resource Office]]), the [[Bureau of Land Management]], and refuge staff.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]]
- **Life stage**: Juveniles, produced from paired matings during March–April 1993
- **Size metrics** (total length, mm):
  - Overall sample (N=200), representative of coves and pond release: mean 65 mm (range 42–130 mm)
  - Pittsburgh Point release (N=2,771): mean 62 mm (range 42–130 mm)
  - Helicopter Cove release (N=100): mean 67 mm (range 42–139 mm)
  - Bulkhead Cove release (N=172): mean 88 mm (range 42–99 mm)
- **Density metric**: Approximately 182 fish per pound at time of hatchery pickup, indicating small juvenile body mass
- **Preservation**: A subsample (20–40 individuals) was frozen at Parker FRO for reference/voucher purposes

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range**: Colorado River Basin; the [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large-bodied benthic-feeding catostomid historically abundant in the mainstem Colorado River and its major reservoirs.
- **Conservation status**: Listed as endangered; this stocking effort reflects an active recovery/augmentation program using hatchery-reared, genetically managed broodstock (paired matings with known egg quantities) to bolster wild populations.
- **Release habitats**:
  - **Reservoir coves** (Lake Havasu): Pittsburgh Point, Helicopter Cove, and Bulkhead Cove — open lentic reservoir habitat with varying shoreline/cove structure, likely selected to reduce predation exposure for juveniles.
  - **Refuge pond** ([[Farmers Pond]], Imperial NWR): An enclosed, likely predator-controlled or lower-risk rearing/refuge habitat intended to improve early juvenile survival before eventual integration into the broader riverine/reservoir system.
- **Interacting agencies/institutions**: [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (production), [[Bureau of Land Management]] (pontoon boat transport and release execution), [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] (pond stocking site management).

## Key Findings
- An estimated **8,500 razorback suckers** were transported from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery to release sites on 20 January 1995.
- Fish were maintained during transport in a **0.5% salt solution**, iced, oxygenated, and aerated — standard stress-reduction protocol for hauling juvenile catostomids.
- **4,004 fish** were offloaded to BLM watercraft for release into three Lake Havasu coves:
  - Pittsburgh Point: N=2,771, mean 62 mm (42–130 mm)
  - Helicopter Cove: N=100, mean 67 mm (42–139 mm)
  - Bulkhead Cove: N=172, mean 88 mm (42–99 mm)
- **4,996 fish** were subsequently transported to [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] and stocked into **Farmers Pond**, with size data extrapolated from the N=200 cove sample (mean 65 mm, range 42–130 mm).
- Broodstock for this cohort originated from **paired matings performed on Lake Mohave during March–April 1993**, indicating a genetically tracked hatchery propagation program consistent with endangered species recovery protocols.
- The operation involved multi-agency logistics coordination, media outreach (a 15-minute interview with a BLM film crew), and same-day execution from hatchery pickup (0700–0900) through final refuge stocking (arrival ~1630, return to Parker FRO at 1830).
