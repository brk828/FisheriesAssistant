# FY93 Annual Project Report: Genetics Management (Colorado River Recovery Program, Project 23)

## Executive Summary
This report documents FY1993 activities under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]]'s Genetics Management project (Project 23), led by Principal Investigator J. Holt Williamson (USFWS) with contractor support from Tom Dowling (ASU), Don Buth (UCLA), Don Morizot (UT), and Rene Schmidt (Genetic Analyses, Inc.). The project addresses two federally listed endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The work is organized into two studies: (1) development and administration of a Genetics Management Plan, and (2) genetic surveys of wild and captive populations using mitochondrial DNA and allozyme analyses. Key FY93 deliverables included draft Genetic Management Guidelines, a Coordinated Hatchery Facilities Plan, formation of a Genetics Panel of external specialists, and near-completion of preliminary genetic surveys for both target species. The project is explicitly non-taxonomic/morphological in nature, focusing instead on conservation genetics infrastructure, population identification, and management planning to support the broader [[Endangered Fish Recovery Program|Recovery Implementation Program]].

## Background & Description
This report is a programmatic/administrative document and does **not** provide morphological, meristic, or anatomical descriptions of either target species. Data collection was restricted to genetic sampling (tissue/blood for allozyme and mitochondrial DNA analysis) rather than morphometric characterization. No length, weight, coloration, or fin-ray count data are reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Geographic scope**: Wild and captive populations across the upper and lower [[Colorado River Basin]], with sampling and analysis emphasizing upper basin subbasin stocks.
- **Population structure focus**: The genetic surveys were designed to test for genetic differentiation among putative populations ("stocks") within each species across subbasins, and to detect potential interspecific hybridization between [[Razorback Sucker]] and other sympatric catostomids.
- **Habitat management context**: Genetic management is integrated with broader recovery actions including hatchery propagation, stocking, and refugia management, indicating these species occupy riverine mainstem and tributary habitats subject to significant human intervention (captive breeding, translocation, augmentation).
- **Conservation status driver**: Both species are treated as at-risk taxa whose long-term adaptive potential is tied to maintaining natural genetic diversity within and among constituent populations — underscoring a fragmented, human-managed metapopulation structure rather than a single contiguous wild population.

## Key Findings
- **Study One – Genetics Management Plan Development**:
  - Produced key planning documents in FY1993: *Artificial Propagation Plan, Fish Needs 1993–1998* (Oct 1992), draft *Genetic Management Guidelines* (Feb 1993, revised Nov 1993), *Coordinated Hatchery Facilities Plan* (Apr 1993), and RIPRAP draft (Sep 1993).
  - Established a **Genetics Panel** of external specialists (Tony Echelle, Fred Allendorf, Dave Philipp, J. Holt Williamson), first convened September 15–16, 1993, tasked with reviewing genetic study results, evaluating genetic risk, and guiding the [[Genetic Management Plan]].
  - Identified prerequisites for a comprehensive long-range genetic management plan: formal population status/trends documentation, an endangered fish stocking policy, a fish disposition policy, defined long-term fish needs, and integration of genetic with other biological data.

- **Study Two – Genetic Surveys**:
  - **[[Razorback Sucker]] survey**: Mitochondrial DNA analysis (Dowling, ASU) and allozyme analysis (Buth, UCLA) conducted on wild and captive populations across upper and lower Colorado River basins; sampling completed FY1992, analysis nearly complete by FY1993, with final interpretation expected late 1993/early 1994.
  - **[[Colorado Squawfish]] survey**: Allozyme analysis (Genetic Analyses, Inc., Smithville, TX) nearly complete; a second-stage nuclear DNA analysis was ongoing into 1994 to ensure sufficient discriminatory power to differentiate population groups.
  - Both surveys aimed to test the null hypotheses of (a) no genetic differentiation among putative populations within each species, and (b) no interspecific hybridization between razorback sucker and other sympatric catostomids.

- **Budget**: Total FY1993 budget of $168,000 was fully expended with no discrepancy — $85,000 for Study One (Plan Development/Administration) and $83,000 for Study Two (Genetic Surveys: $53,000 razorback sucker, $30,000 Colorado squawfish).

- **Recommendations**: The report calls for development of centralized genetic databases, routine genetic monitoring schedules for wild and captive populations, and closer integration of genetic data into recovery goal-setting, population prioritization, and risk assessment for management actions (e.g., stocking, captive breeding).

- **Programmatic significance**: This project functions as the genetic-data backbone for the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], directly supporting RIPRAP Recovery Action Plan Element IV (conserve genetic integrity, augment/restore populations) for both target species.
