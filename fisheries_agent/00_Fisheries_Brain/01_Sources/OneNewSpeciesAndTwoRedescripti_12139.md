# Corydoras treitlii, C. fowleri and the New Species C. semiaquilus: Redescriptions and Diagnoses

#ichthyology #marine-biology #callichthyidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 taxonomic revision by [[Stanley H. Weitzman]], published in the *Proceedings of the United States National Museum*, describes one new catfish species, [[Corydoras Semiaquilus]], and redescribes two poorly known relatives, [[Corydoras Treitlii]] and [[Corydoras Fowleri]]. All three belong to the South American armored catfish genus *Corydoras* (family [[Callichthyidae]]). The author revised these species because the original descriptions were inadequate or hard to access. *C. treitlii* had never been illustrated, and *C. fowleri* was known only from its holotype, described without illustration in an aquarium magazine. The study compares body proportions, fin ray counts, armor (dermal plates and prickles), pectoral fin spine spinulation and color pattern across the three species, and places them among other long-snouted *Corydoras*.

## Background & Description

The paper begins with the rationale for the revision. The new species was recognized from three specimens collected in Brazil. Its apparent relatives, *C. treitlii* Steindachner (1906) and *C. fowleri* Böhlke (1950), were then re-examined. Both original descriptions were considered incomplete, and the type material was borrowed from several museums, including the Naturhistorisches Museum Wien (NMW), the [[Smithsonian Institution]]'s United States National Museum (USNM), the Stanford University Division of Systematic Biology (SU), and the Senckenberg Museum (SM).

Measurements are expressed as proportions of standard length, with percentages in parentheses, and as ratios within head length or snout length. Table 1 lists raw measurements for all specimens. Key diagnostic characters include:

- **Snout length** relative to body length (without head)
- **Least caudal peduncle depth** as a percentage of snout length, see [[Caudal Peduncle Depth]]
- **Presence or absence of [[Imbricated Dermal Plates]]** on the thorax and abdomen
- **Predorsal length** as a percentage of the distance from dorsal fin origin to caudal fin base
- **Caudal fin banding**
- **Spinule count** on the posterior border of the [[Pectoral Fin Spine]]

### Corydoras treitlii

- **Type locality:** a small stream entering the [[Rio Parnaíba]] at Victoria, Maranhão, Brazil.
- **Lectotype:** NMW 61103, standard length (SL) 42.6 mm, collected in 1903 by [[Franz Steindachner]].
- **Additional specimens:** paralectotype NMW 47798 (SL 42.4 mm); USNM 176912 (SL 52.5 mm, São Paulo, collected by [[Herbert Axelrod]] in 1958); SU 35054 (SL 47.0 mm, a poorly preserved aquarium specimen whose locality the author regarded as uncertain).
- **Diagnosis:** snout about 30 to 32% of body length without head; least caudal peduncle depth about 57 to 62% of snout length; no imbricated thoracic or abdominal plates, only fine bony prickles; caudal fin without bars; predorsal length about 79 to 89% of the dorsal origin-to-caudal base distance.
- **Counts:** dorsal fin I,7; anal fin ii,5; pectoral fin I,10; pelvic fin i,5; principal caudal rays 7/7; lateral scutes 24/21.
- **Color:** yellowish gray in alcohol, with purplish dark brown markings on the upper body scutes. All fins hyaline and unmarked.

### Corydoras fowleri

- **Holotype:** SU 16115, a female of SL 66.0 mm, collected on 13 December 1941 by William G. Scherer at Caño del Chancho, near Pebas, Peru. It is the only known specimen.
- **Diagnosis:** snout about 25% of body length without head; least caudal peduncle depth about 55% of snout length; imbricated thoracic and abdominal plates present; dorsal fin spine weaker and shorter than pectoral spine; predorsal length about 67% of postdorsal length.
- **Counts:** dorsal fin I,7; anal fin ii,5 (Böhlke had reported "I,5", but the author found that what appeared to be one spine was two closely adpressed spines); pectoral fin I,10; pelvic fin i,5; 30 spinules on the pectoral fin spine.
- **Sex and condition:** the swollen body and mature eggs found on probing show the holotype is a gravid female. The author suggests males, which are usually slimmer, may have a smaller average body depth than this specimen indicates.

### Corydoras semiaquilus, new species

- **Holotype:** SU 55939, SL 60.9 mm, collected in December 1960 by [[Harald Schultz]] from Igarapé Preto, a "small jungle-rivulet" at the headwaters of blackwater creeks entering the upper [[Solimões River]], Amazonas, Brazil.
- **Paratypes:** USNM 196170 (SL 59.1 mm) and SM 5349 (damaged, SL about 54 mm).
- **Diagnosis:** snout about 32 to 33% of body length without head; least caudal peduncle depth about 49 to 51% of snout length; imbricated thoracic and abdominal plates present; dorsal fin spine much weaker and shorter than pectoral spine; predorsal length about 85 to 86% of the dorsal origin-to-caudal base distance; caudal fin heavily barred.
- **Counts:** dorsal fin I,7; anal fin ii,5; pectoral fin I,11 (I,10 in SM 5349); pelvic fin i,5; principal caudal rays 7/7; lateral scutes 24/21 (26/23 in USNM 196170); pectoral fin spine with 26 spinules (24 in USNM 196170, 22 in SM 5349).
- **Color:** the upper two-thirds of the body is dark, with a narrow pale streak below the dorsal fin. The dorsal fin has two irregular black bars, the caudal fin three, and the anal fin two. In life, the author reports greenish iridescence over the coracoid, golden iridescence over the opercle, and a golden iris.
- **Etymology:** from Latin *semi* (half) and *aquilus* (dark colored), referring to the dark upper body scutes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is primarily a taxonomic study and contains little ecological information. The only habitat description is Harald Schultz's account of the *C. semiaquilus* type locality. It is a small jungle rivulet at the headwaters of blackwater creeks that drain into the upper Solimões. The water is crystal clear, and the bottom is sandy, with pebbles, leaves and rotten leaves. The type locality of *C. treitlii* is a small stream in the Parnaíba drainage in Maranhão. The locality of SU 35054 is uncertain, and it may have come from the Amazon basin. *C. fowleri* was collected at Caño del Chancho near Pebas, Peru, in the Amazonian lowlands. The paper gives no data on population size, abundance, diet or behavior.

## Key Findings

1. **Corydoras semiaquilus is a new species** of long-snouted *Corydoras*. It is distinguished from its relatives mainly by its imbricated belly and thoracic plates, a proportionately shallower caudal peduncle (48.9 to 50.8% of snout length versus 57.0 to 62.1% in *C. treitlii*), and a barred caudal fin.

2. **Corydoras treitlii is redescribed** from the lectotype and three additional specimens. It is characterized by the absence of imbricated plates (only fine prickles) and an unbarred caudal fin. Its original description was accurate but incomplete, and it had never been illustrated.

3. **Corydoras fowleri is redescribed** from its single known specimen, a gravid female from Peru. It differs from *C. treitlii* in its imbricated plates, longer body relative to head length and weaker dorsal spine. It is most closely related to *C. semiaquilus*, but differs in snout length, caudal peduncle length, body depth, eye size, head length and predorsal proportions.

4. **Corrected fin counts:** the anal fin of *C. fowleri* is ii,5 (not I,5 as originally reported), because what looked like one spine is two closely adpressed spines.

5. **Relationships:** the three species, together with *C. acutus*, *C. cervinus*, *C. pastazensis* and *C. septentrionalis*, form a group of long-snouted *Corydoras*. *C. semiaquilus* appears closest to *C. treitlii* because of its long snout, narrow interorbital, slender caudal peduncle and color pattern.

6. **Separation from other species:** *C. cervinus* lacks caudal bars and has a deeper caudal peduncle (about 7 times in SL versus 8.6 to 8.9 times in *C. semiaquilus*). *C. pastazensis* has a deeper body (36.4 to 37.1% versus 33.5% of SL) and a deeper caudal peduncle relative to snout length (65.2 to 66.4% versus 48.9 to 50.8%), and it lacks the dark upper-scute pattern and snout markings of *C. semiaquilus*.

7. **Measurement caveats:** the author notes that body depth, measured from the ventral border of the posterior coracoid process to the dorsal fin origin, is stable and little affected by nutritional state. Some specimens were faded, damaged or dehydrated (notably SU 35054 and the *C. fowleri* holotype), which limits some comparisons. Table 1 provides the full measurement set for all three species.

8. **Conservation or applied implications** are not discussed. The study is purely descriptive and systematic, and it establishes the diagnostic characters needed to identify these species reliably.
