# Report on PRT-676811 Activity (December 1992 – November 1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #endangered-species #freshwater-ecology #southwestern-us-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is the Arizona Game and Fish Department's (AGFD) annual activity report to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) under Subpermit PRT-676811, authorizing take of federally listed Threatened and Endangered species (and golden eagles) for conservation purposes. The report catalogs handling, stocking, monitoring, and voucher-collection activities for **19 distinct native southwestern fish taxa** conducted between December 1992 and November 1993, alongside parallel activities for reptiles, birds, and mammals (not the focus of this note). Key fish-related activities include large-scale [[Razorback Sucker]] stocking and health assessments in the Verde River and Lake Mohave/Lake Mead systems, extensive [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] translocation/preservation efforts across dozens of spring and wash sites, major population monitoring of [[Humpback Chub]] in the Little Colorado and mainstem Colorado Rivers (thousands of individuals handled), and continued monitoring of [[Spikedace]] and [[Loachminnow]] in Aravaipa Creek. No take occurred for several critically endangered taxa (Colorado squawfish, Quitobaquito pupfish, beautiful shiner, Sonoran chub, Yaqui chub, Yaqui catfish, Apache trout, Gila trout), reflecting either population scarcity, non-detection, or conservative permit conditions during this cycle.

## Background & Description

The report was submitted by Terry B. Johnson, AGFD Endangered Species Coordinator, to John G. Rogers, USFWS Region 2 Director, fulfilling annual reporting requirements of the Regional Director's Blanket Permit (PRT-676811). This permit allows AGFD, as a subpermittee, to "take" (capture, handle, translocate, sacrifice, or collect as voucher) federally listed species for research, monitoring, and recovery purposes across Arizona's aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems.

The fish species addressed span several families endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], [[Gila River Basin]], and [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] drainages of the American Southwest:

- **Catostomidae**: [[Razorback Sucker]]
- **Cyprinidae**: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Sonoran Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loachminnow]], [[Woundfin]]
- **Poeciliidae**: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]]
- **Cyprinodontidae**: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]
- **Ictaluridae**: [[Yaqui Catfish]]
- **Salmonidae**: [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]]

Most of these species are listed as Endangered or Threatened under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, reflecting severe historical range contractions due to habitat alteration, dewatering, and introduction of non-native competitor/predator species throughout the arid Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The activities documented span a network of critically important refugia and mainstem habitats:

- **Verde River (Yavapai Co.)**: Site of major [[Razorback Sucker]] stocking (1,120 individuals released in two pulses) and radiotelemetry monitoring of [[Colorado Squawfish]]; also supports [[Spikedace]] populations.
- **Lake Mohave & Lake Mead/Willow Beach (Mohave Co.)**: Key reservoir refugia for adult [[Razorback Sucker]], where health assessments (length, weight, external parasites, ocular condition) were conducted. Notably, 12 of 66 fish sampled in Lake Mohave were blind in one eye and 1 was blind in both eyes, suggesting possible parasite- or environment-related ocular pathology in this aging, non-recruiting population.
- **Isolated springs and washes (Santa Cruz, Gila, and Yaqui watersheds)**: A dense network of small spring/wash refugia (e.g., Monkey Spring, Sharp Spring, Redrock Canyon, Sonoita Creek, TNC Hassayampa River Preserve) sustain remnant [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] populations, many maintained through active translocation programs.
- **San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge (Cochise Co.)**: A constructed pond/tank complex supporting the only significant U.S. populations of [[Yaqui Topminnow]], with over a dozen discrete sub-populations monitored.
- **Aravaipa Creek (Graham/Pinal Cos.)**: A flagship perennial stream supporting large, apparently robust populations of [[Spikedace]] (10,685 individuals encountered) and [[Loachminnow]] (4,009 individuals), indicating relatively intact habitat conditions.
- **Little Colorado River & Mainstem Colorado River (Grand Canyon)**: Primary spawning and rearing habitat for [[Humpback Chub]], with large-scale population monitoring reflecting ongoing recovery program efforts tied to Glen Canyon Dam operations research.
- **Virgin River (Mohave Co.)**: Supports both [[Virgin River Chub]] and [[Woundfin]], monitored cooperatively with USFWS biologists.

## Key Findings

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- 1,120 stocked into upper Verde River (630 on 3/17, 490 on 3/31).
- 44 subsequently recaptured near Perkinsville; 1 recaptured at Hassayampa River Preserve.
- Lake Mohave: 66 handled (6 electrofishing, 60 trammel net); mean length 597.9 mm (range 528–680 mm); 12 unilaterally blind, 1 bilaterally blind; weights not taken to reduce stress.
- Willow Beach/Lake Mead: 127 handled (118 gill/trammel net, 9 electrofishing); lengths, weights, parasite/condition notes recorded; PIT tagging assistance provided to USFWS.

### Gila Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*)
- ~500 stocked at AD Wash (March 1993).
- Extensive captured-released and preserved sampling across ~18 sites, including Monkey Spring (300 released), Redrock Canyon (350 released + 21 preserved), Sonoita Creek (277 preserved), and TNC Hassayampa River Preserve (87 preserved + 129 released).

### Yaqui Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*)
- Captured-released across 14 sites at/near San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge, totaling several hundred individuals (largest counts: Twin Pond–200, Robertson Cienega–200, Oasis Tank–100).

### Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*)
- 8 preserved from Cold Spring.
- 20 possible hybrids (*C.m. macularius* × *C.m. eremus*) captured-released from Finley Tank.
- ~500 stocked at AD Wash (March 1993).

### Little Colorado Spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
- Monitored via catch-release at Chevelon Creek (5), East Clear Creek (9), Leonard Canyon Creek (12), Little Colorado River (44), and Nutrioso Creek (50); one voucher specimen preserved from Chevelon Creek.

### Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*)
- 28 caught and released in Palm Lake, TNC Hassayampa River Preserve.

### Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*)
- **Little Colorado River**: 2,483 chubs >29 mm TL handled (5 incidental mortalities, 5 sacrificed/preserved); 492 chubs <30 mm TL handled (excluding unanalyzed drift samples).
- **Mainstem Colorado River**: 3,756 chubs >29 mm TL handled (17 sacrificed/preserved, 0 incidental mortalities, 70 found dead/preserved); 49 chubs <30 mm TL handled (4 sacrificed, 7 incidental mortalities).

### Virgin River Chub (*Gila robusta seminuda*)
- 15 caught and released during cooperative monitoring with USFWS in the Virgin River.

### Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*)
- 10,685 caught-released in Aravaipa Creek.
- 142 caught-released in upper Verde River; 6 additional preserved as vouchers.

### Loachminnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- 4,009 caught and released during Aravaipa Creek monitoring.

### Woundfin (*Plagopterus argentissimus*)
- 7 caught and released during Virgin River monitoring.

### No-Take Species (Monitored but Not Captured/Possessed)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] — radiotelemetry monitoring continued in Verde River, but no individuals taken/possessed.
