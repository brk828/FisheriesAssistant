# FY-1989 Work Plan: Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the FY-1989 work plan and budget for the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], approved by the Colorado River Implementation Committee on August 30, 1988. The plan covers the fiscal year beginning October 1, 1988. It sets priorities for recovering four federally listed and rare native fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], along with [[Roundtail Chub]] (addressed mainly through taxonomy work).

The total annual budget is about $2.3 million. The Technical and Management Groups ranked roughly 75 submitted projects. The committee recommended 39 numbered projects (Appendix 5.1) plus three contingency projects (Table 3.2). Most work focuses on instream flow determination, the [[Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion]], standardized monitoring, and stocking/propagation.

## Background & Description

The Implementation Committee is responsible for setting annual funding priorities. Its members include the regional directors of the [[US Fish And Wildlife Service]] and the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], state executive officials from Colorado, Utah and Wyoming, and representatives of environmental groups and water developers. The Colorado River Fishes Recovery Coordinator serves as Program Director.

**Annual planning cycle:** Preliminary proposals are requested by May 1. The Technical Group ranks projects by July 15 using criteria that include consistency with the program, urgency (avoiding jeopardy), essentiality to recovery, timeliness, likelihood of success, relationship to other work, opportunity to act now, and proposal quality. The Management Group prepares a draft plan by August 15, and the committee approves it on August 30. Implementation review follows on February 15. Projects are to be fully contracted by the end of the second quarter of FY-89.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan treats flow regime as the central habitat issue. Key concerns include:

- **Flow needs:** Biologically based flow recommendations for the [[Yampa River]], the upper [[Colorado River]], and the [[Green River]], and water-right protection for those flows.
- **Dam operations:** Effects of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] releases on nursery backwaters, sediment transport, and tailwater trout fisheries.
- **Biotic interactions:** Predation and competition from nonnative and warmwater game fish (for example green sunfish, red shiner, and channel-related species) on larval and young-of-year (YOY) native fish. The plan notes that physical habitat alone may not produce recovery.
- **Early life survival:** YOY Colorado squawfish, about 40 mm total length at their first winter, may suffer high overwinter mortality that could limit recruitment.
- **Sediment and geomorphology:** Maintenance of stable channels and habitat, including the role of the [[Little Snake River]].
- **Angler take:** Incidental capture of rare fishes by anglers.

## Key Findings

**Priority ongoing activities**
- Standardized monitoring and data management programs ([[Standardized Monitoring]], $92,000; Data Base Management, $25,000).
- Instream flow recommendations for the Yampa and Colorado Rivers, using [[IFIM PHABSIM]] analyses (Colorado River flow recommendations, $176,500; Yampa flow recommendation, $72,500).
- Completion of the Flaming Gorge studies and a draft biological opinion in 1989 (Flaming Gorge operation studies, $205,900; backwater mapping, $180,000; trophic dynamics, $120,000; sediment dynamics, $20,000).
- Competition studies between warmwater game fish and rare natives ([[Game Fish And Endangered Fish Interactions]], $85,000).
- Bonytail stocking and monitoring on the Green River, including the [[Bonytail Introduction]] study ($65,000) in Dinosaur National Monument.
- Flow, temperature and hydrology monitoring and modeling, using the [[HYDROSS Model]] (modeling, $17,600; flow/temperature monitoring, $31,800).

**New initiatives**
- Endangered fish propagation and rearing plan, including identification of needed hatchery expansion ([[Propagation And Genetics Plan]], $143,700).
- Quantitative [[Recovery Goals]] for the rare fishes ($30,000).
- Augmentation planning for Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker ($35,000), including broodstock expansion at Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- Anglers' information and education program, quarterly newsletter, and an Upper Basin [[Creel Survey]] ($55,000, multiyear).
- Taxonomic clarification of the [[Gila Taxonomy]] complex (humpback, bonytail and roundtail chub, $90,000).

**Other notable projects**
- Razorback sucker propagation and reintroduction in the Green River near Ouray, Utah ($23,500), with the goal of preventing extirpation.
- Bonytail retrieval to a secure hatchery to prevent extinction ($2,000).
- Kenney Reservoir sportfishery development for Colorado squawfish, including stocking about 50,000 fish ($26,000).
- Cataract Canyon final report on a 3-year study ($20,000) and [[San Juan River]] management plan ($50,000), which evaluates recovery potential and feeds a draft plan by March 1, 1989.
- Ruedi Reservoir release of 10,000 acre-feet to the 15-mile reach of the Colorado River ($16,200).
- Development of a [[Razorback Sucker Management Plan]] ($10,000), since the species lacks agreed recovery goals.

**Contingency projects (Table 3.2)**
- C1: Survey of available water rights for instream flows ($100,000).
- C2: Cataract Canyon and Colorado squawfish monitoring ($20,000).
- C3: [[Inter-Intraspecific Interactions]] study, with lab and in situ cage field tests ($96,000).
- Individual items total $216,000. The printed total reads "816.0," which appears to be an OCR error.

**Recommendations of the Implementation Committee**
1. Agencies must commit staff and funds early in the fiscal year.
2. The Technical Group will issue guidance on priority FY-90 thrusts, with defined objectives and final products.
3. Every funded project must submit an annual report by January 15, starting in FY-89.
4. Future work plans will list total project cost itemized by fiscal year.
