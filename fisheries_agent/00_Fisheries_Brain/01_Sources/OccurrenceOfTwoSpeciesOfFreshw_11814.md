## Executive Summary
This 1999 paper by Patricia A. Sowka documents the first verified records of the phylum **Porifera** (freshwater sponges) in Arizona, specifically two species: **Dosilia radiospiculata** and **Ephydatia muelleri**. Prior to this study, unidentified sponge specimens had been noted in Arizona reservoirs ([[Apache Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[Canyon Lake]]) and in the Central Arizona Project canal system, but none had been collected, preserved, or taxonomically confirmed. The author collected specimens from two distinct habitats at the **Phoenix Zoo** (a cheetah exhibit moat and an anuran-rearing pool) and from the margins of **Apache Lake** on the Salt River. Species identification was based on microsclere spicule morphology, verified by sponge taxonomy experts. While the paper's primary focus is invertebrate (Porifera) biogeography, it is contextually tied to Arizona fisheries literature, referencing prior studies on buffalofish diets and Phoenix-area fish faunas that first noted unidentified sponge occurrences.

## Background & Description
Species identification of the two sponge taxa relied entirely on **microsclere spicule** shape and size, examined via prepared slides (methodology per Frost, 1991):

- **Dosilia radiospiculata**: Identified by distinct microsclere spicule morphology; broadly distributed from the Canadian border south to Mexico (Frost, 1991).
- **Ephydatia muelleri**: Identified by distinct microsclere spicule morphology; widely distributed across North America and Europe (Jewell, 1959).
- A previously observed but unpreserved sponge specimen at the Bouse Hills pumping plant (Central Arizona Project canal) reached **10 cm in diameter**, though its species identity remains unconfirmed (likely one of the two species described here).

No fish morphological data are presented in this paper; the taxonomic focus is exclusively on sponge spicule characters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
### Habitat Contexts Documented
1. **Phoenix Zoo Cheetah Exhibit Moat**
   - Channel dimensions: 3 m wide × 1.2 m deep × 10 m long
   - Substantial organic matter accumulation
   - Very low flow (<1 L/s)
   - Both *D. radiospiculata* and *E. muelleri* collected here via seine haul (10 July 1995)

2. **Phoenix Zoo Anuran Center (tadpole-rearing pool)**
   - L-shaped concrete pool, ~1 m deep, silt-lined
   - *D. radiospiculata* found unattached on pool floor and attached to aquatic vegetation and submerged plastic tubing
   - Collected via dip net (6 December 1995)

3. **Apache Lake** (Salt River impoundment)
   - Second-most upstream reservoir on the Salt River, below [[Roosevelt Lake]]
   - Strong thermal stratification; surface temperatures often ≥30°C
   - Somewhat saline conditions
   - Generally clear water, but sponge colony distribution was patchy and difficult to observe near shore due to poor visibility
   - Sponges collected at ~1.5 m depth by scraping dip nets along rocky lake margins (13 January 1996)

### Water Chemistry Context
- Zoo water supplied via Salt River Project canals originating at Granite Reef Dam (4 mi downstream of the [[Verde River]]–[[Salt River]] confluence)
- Canal water temperature range: 15–40°C
- Dissolved oxygen range: <5 to >20 mg/L
- **Verde River**: high calcium bicarbonate, low chloride
- **Salt River**: high chloride content (330–1,000 mg/L)
- These variable and sometimes extreme water chemistry conditions (high temperature, wide DO range, elevated chloride) indicate considerable environmental tolerance in both sponge species.

### Linkage to Fish Studies
This paper connects to prior Arizona fisheries research:
- [[Minckley Et Al 1970]] documented unidentified sponges incidentally while studying **foods of buffalofishes** (genus *Ictiobus*) in central Arizona reservoirs — suggesting sponges may form part of the diet or incidental ingestion for these benthic-feeding fish.
- [[Marsh And Minckley 1982]] on Phoenix-area fish fauna provided supporting water chemistry/temperature data for the canal system shared by both fish and sponge habitats.

## Key Findings
- **First verified Porifera records for Arizona**: This study establishes the first collected, preserved, and taxonomically confirmed freshwater sponge records in the state.
- **Two confirmed species**: *Dosilia radiospiculata* and *Ephydatia muelleri* co-occur in artificial (zoo) and natural (reservoir) freshwater habitats in central Arizona.
- **Broad environmental tolerance**: Sponges were found across a range of conditions — from nutrient-rich, low-flow, organic-laden zoo moats to thermally stratified, saline reservoir margins with temperatures reaching 30°C+.
- **Prior anecdotal evidence**: Earlier unidentified sponge sightings at [[Apache Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[Canyon Lake]], and the Central Arizona Project canal (Bouse Hills pumping plant, with specimens up to 10 cm diameter) likely represent one or both of these now-confirmed species, though those specimens were never collected.
- **Substrate versatility**: *D. radiospiculata* was observed attached to aquatic vegetation, submerged plastic tubing, and resting unattached on pool substrate, demonstrating flexible attachment behavior.
- **Taxonomic methodology**: Identification relied on microsclere spicule morphology examined on prepared slides, verified by independent experts (A. Ricciardi, McGill University; T. Frost, University of Wisconsin–Trout Lake Station), with voucher slides deposited at the McGill University Museum.
- **Indirect fisheries relevance**: The presence of sponges in reservoirs previously studied for [[Buffalofish]] (*Ictiobus* spp.) diet composition suggests a potential trophic or incidental ecological interaction between freshwater sponges and benthic-foraging fish species in Arizona reservoir systems.
