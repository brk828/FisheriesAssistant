# Systematics of the Percid Fishes of the Subgenus Ammocrypta, Genus Ammocrypta, with Descriptions of Two New Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 monograph by James D. Williams (Bulletin of the [[Alabama Museum of Natural History]], No. 1) revises the systematics, taxonomy, zoogeography and intraspecific variation of the sand darters of the subgenus [[Ammocrypta]] (genus Ammocrypta, tribe Etheostomatini, subfamily Percinae, family [[Percidae]]). Six species are recognized, two of them new: [[Ammocrypta bifascia]] and [[Ammocrypta meridiana]]. More than 20 meristic and morphometric characters were examined on roughly 3,000 specimens across the species, and the study adds species accounts, a key, distribution records, observations on burying behavior, and a phylogenetic and dispersal hypothesis.

## Background & Description

The subgenus Ammocrypta, known as sand darters, comprises elongate, translucent percids with variable body scalation. The document places the genus among the Etheostomatini, which includes [[Etheostoma]] (more than 80 species) and [[Percina]] (more than 25 species). A separate species, *A. asprella*, is assigned to the subgenus *Crystallaria*, which is treated as a subgenus of Ammocrypta rather than a separate genus.

Two species groups are proposed within the subgenus:

- **beani group:** *A. beani*, *A. bifascia* and *A. clara*. Bodies are almost naked (usually 3 to 6 transverse scale rows), pelvic fins are unpigmented, and vertebrae are usually 40.
- **pellucida group:** *A. pellucida*, *A. meridiana* and *A. vivax*. Bodies are partially to almost completely scaled (usually 9 to 15 transverse scale rows), and vertebrae are usually 42 or 43.

Species were distinguished using scale and fin-ray counts (following [[Hubbs and Lagler]] 1958), vertebral counts (Bailey and Gosline 1955) and the cephalic canal system (Hubbs and Cannon 1935). Measured specimens were at least 20 to 30 mm standard length depending on the character.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Sand darters typically occupy moderate to large creeks and rivers with sand or sand-gravel bottoms and moderate current. *A. beani* was taken mostly in water 6 inches to 4 feet deep, and *A. bifascia* most commonly at 2 to 4 feet.
- **Associates:** *A. beani* is most characteristically associated with *Notropis longirostris* (or its ecological equivalent in the Mobile Basin). *A. bifascia* is most often found with *Notropis longirostris* and *Ericymba buccata*. *A. clara* is often the only species present in its habitat.
- **Burying behavior:** Sand darters bury themselves with only the eyes and snout exposed. The document reports observations from aquaria ([[Jordan and Copeland]] 1877), field observations by [[Trautman]] (1957), and field observations of *A. bifascia* in Coldwater Creek, Florida. The author argues that energy conservation (less current effort, lower temperature beneath the sand) may matter as much as protection from predators, because predators are scarce on unproductive shifting sand.
- **Spawning:** Gravid females were collected from early June through August in *A. beani*. *A. bifascia* spawns about three to four weeks earlier, beginning in late May or early June. *A. clara* peaks in July and early August, and *A. pellucida* spawns from early June to late July in the Ohio Basin.

## Key Findings

**Species accounts and specimens examined**
- Approximate specimen counts: *A. beani* about 550; *A. bifascia* 325; *A. clara* 500; *A. vivax* 700; *A. meridiana* 200; *A. pellucida* 800.
- More than 20 taxonomic characters were examined and 22 proportional measurements taken from 10 or more specimens of each species.

**New species**
- ***Ammocrypta bifascia*** (Florida sand darter): Holotype TU 82632, a male of 61.2 mm SL, from the Choctawhatchee River, Holmes County, Florida, collected 5 July 1973. It is distinguished from *A. beani* by two dark median-fin bands (marginal and submarginal) in males, the absence of an opercular spine and naked cheeks and opercles, and smaller breeding tubercles. It ranges from the Perdido River east to the Choctawhatchee River, and is the largest known species of the subgenus (largest specimen 64 mm SL).
- ***Ammocrypta meridiana*** (Southern sand darter): Holotype USNM 213494, a male of 50.8 mm SL, from Cedar Creek, Dallas County, Alabama. It is endemic to the Mobile Basin and was previously confused with *A. vivax*. It differs from *A. pellucida* in having 8 or 9 scale rows below the lateral line, 14 to 16 transverse scale rows, and vertebrae usually 42.

**Key diagnostic characters**
- *A. beani*: no opercular spine; lateral line 57 to 77 scales; breeding tubercles on both dorsal and ventral surfaces of the pelvic spine and rays; range from the Mobile Basin west to the Big Black River and the Hatchie River (Tennessee).
- *A. clara*: strong opercular spine; no fin bands; breeding tubercles on pelvic, anal and caudal fins; occurs throughout the Mississippi Valley except the Tennessee, Missouri and Arkansas drainages, plus the Sabine and Neches rivers and the Waupaca River (Lake Michigan drainage).
- *A. pellucida*: the most variable species, with random variation and no clinal or geographic trends. Range covers most of the Ohio Basin, southern Lake Huron, Lake St. Clair, Lake Erie and the St. Lawrence drainage. It is apparently absent from the Lake Ontario drainage.
- *A. vivax*: the most completely scaled species, with vertically oriented lateral blotches and dark median-fin bands. Its center of distribution is the central Mississippi embayment, with range from the San Jacinto River (Texas) to the St. Francis River (Missouri).

**Systematics and phylogeny**
- The genus is considered a derivative of the Imostoma line of *Percina*, although the author notes biochemical studies (Page and Whitt 1973) suggesting a closer link to *Etheostoma*.
- Within the pellucida group, *A. vivax* is the most primitive and *A. pellucida* the most advanced. In the beani group, *A. clara* is the most primitive and *A. beani* and *A. bifascia* the most specialized.
- Reduced squamation and median-fin pigmentation shared by *A. pellucida* and *A. clara* are interpreted as convergent evolution rather than close relationship.

**Geographic variation**
- *A. beani* shows weak clinal variation in lateral line counts, with a slight increase from west to east (western group 63.65 to 66.36 means; eastern group 66.91 to 71.52).
- *A. clara* shows an irregular cline in lateral line scales, with means from 68.85 (Sabine-Neches) to 74.85 (Waupaca).
- *A. bifascia* transverse scale rows increase from west (Perdido, mean 3.40) to east (Choctawhatchee, mean 4.55).

**Biogeography and dispersal**
- The subgenus probably originated in the lower Mississippi Valley. Four of the six species are thought to predate the Pleistocene, while *A. bifascia* and *A. meridiana* are suggested to have differentiated more recently.
- *A. beani* and *A. clara* are sympatric at two localities in the Big Black River, Mississippi. The only river with three species together is the Big Black River (with *A. vivax*).
- The author proposes that the presence of *A. beani* in the Hatchie River (Tennessee) may reflect stream capture from the upper Tombigbee River.
- The invasion of *A. vivax* east of the Mississippi River, and the competition it caused, is suggested as a prime factor in the elimination of *A. pellucida* from eastern Gulf Coast tributaries.

**Taxonomic history**
- *A. clara* was reduced to a subspecies of *A. pellucida* by Evermann and Jenkins (1889) but was revalidated by [[Linder]] (1959).
- *A. vivax* was treated as a subspecies of *A. pellucida* until Hubbs and Greene (1927) restored its status.

**Conclusions**
- The study supports six species in the subgenus, including two new ones, and provides a key for identification.
- The author suggests the paucity of species results from limited niche availability: roughly two niches per stream, one for a scaled species and one for a naked species.
