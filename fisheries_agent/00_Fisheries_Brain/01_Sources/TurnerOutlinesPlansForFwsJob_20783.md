# Turner Outlines Priorities for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service

#conservation-policy #wetlands #migratory-birds

## Executive Summary

This Star-Tribune news article reports a first interview with state Senator [[John Turner]] (R-Teton-Sublette, Wyoming), who was named by President [[George Bush]] to direct the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). Turner pledged scientific professionalism and cooperation with the agency's diverse constituencies. He expected confirmation no earlier than August, and said three federal ethics groups were reviewing his family's lease and license arrangements in [[Grand Teton National Park]]. His stated priorities are [[Wetlands Protection]], [[Endangered Species Act]] enforcement, [[Coastal Barriers]] protection, oil-development issues in Alaska, and migratory bird conservation. The article contains no species-level biological data.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** [[John Turner]], a state senator for almost two decades, nominated as USFWS director.
- **Context:** The nomination followed a change in federal administration. Agency personnel reportedly felt demoralized by over-politicization and underfunding during the [[Reagan Administration]].
- **Confirmation process:** Turner was conferring with members and staff of the Senate Environment and Public Works Committee. No hearing had been scheduled. He did not expect confirmation before August and reported no senatorial objections to his appointment.
- **Personal and legal matters:** His family holds a complex network of leases and licenses in Grand Teton National Park, including the Triangle X dude ranch, an outfitting business and a river rafting business. Ethics lawyers were reportedly resolving the matter, with one office yet to respond.
- **Succession:** Teton and Sublette county leaders faced the first-ever joint task of choosing his Senate replacement. Turner planned to notify the counties and governor after confirmation.
- **Agency scale:** The USFWS controls about 440 refuges and 150 waterfowl protection areas, covering more than 90 million acres. The [[National Park Service]] controls about 70 million acres, and the entire state of Wyoming covers about 63 million acres.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Wetlands loss:** Turner stated that the country has lost over half its wetlands and is losing several hundred thousand acres a year.
- **Migratory birds:** He emphasized non-game migratory bird populations, some of which he said are declining, in addition to ducks and geese.
- **Coastal barriers:** Tidal estuaries and adjacent areas were described as nearly depleted, harboring a rich variety of wildlife and facing development pressure, especially in the East.
- **Arctic and oil development:** The [[Exxon Valdez Oil Spill]] raised questions about long-term impacts, protection needs for the North Slope, and development of the [[Arctic National Wildlife Refuge]]. Turner said terrestrial drilling raises different concerns than ocean crude transport.
- **Wolves and grizzly bears:** [[Wolf Reintroduction]] and [[Grizzly Bear]] recovery were described as emotional issues requiring sound biology and engagement with diverse interest groups.

## Key Findings

1. **Commitment to science:** Turner said the agency "is going to do good biology," would back "good research and good data," and would stand up for professionals.
2. **Legal compliance with sensitivity to economics:** He pledged to comply with congressional mandates while accounting for economic and social interests.
3. **Priority issues:** Wetlands protection, Endangered Species Act enforcement, coastal barrier protection, Alaskan oil-development impacts, and non-game migratory birds.
4. **Budget outlook:** Turner had not yet reviewed the agency budget but expected it to be "relatively tight," similar to other federal agencies.
5. **Political realities:** He acknowledged that political considerations are unavoidable and that resource managers have worked within them before.
6. **Pending risks:** Confirmation timing was uncertain, and the Grand Teton ethics reviews remained open.
7. **Future plans:** Turner said he would return to Wyoming after his USFWS tenure, depending on his performance.
