# Osteology and Phylogeny of the American Cyprinid Fishes Allied to the Genus Gila

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 University of Michigan doctoral dissertation by [[Teruya Uyeno]] uses comparative [[Osteology]] to resolve the classification of North American minnows in the genera [[Gila]], [[Richardsonius]] and [[Ptychocheilus]]. These genera had a chaotic systematic history, with many species shifted between genera and proposals ranging from splitting into many small genera to lumping into large ones. Uyeno synonymizes [[Siphateles]] and [[Snyderichthys]] with Gila as subgenera, and merges [[Clinostomus]] into Richardsonius. Skeletal characters are weighed against three criteria: intraspecific variability, ontogenetic timing, and divergence among related species. The fossil record indicates the three genera were already differentiated by the Middle Pliocene.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Comparative osteology of the head, axial skeleton, fins and pharyngeal apparatus in roughly 14 Gila taxa, 4 Richardsonius taxa and 4 Ptychocheilus taxa, plus fossil review.
- **Methods:** Skeletons were prepared by [[Dermestid Beetle Preparation]], clearing and staining ([[Alizarin Staining]] method), [[X-Ray Radiography]], or dissection of preserved material. Material came from the [[United States National Museum]], the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] and private collections. Osteological nomenclature follows Harrington (1955) for the head, Bridge and Haddon (1909) for the [[Weberian Apparatus]], and Norden (1952) for other parts.
- **Reference species:** [[Gila atraria]] was chosen as the most generalized form and used as the comparative type; its nearly complete skeleton is illustrated across Plates I–XXXIV.
- **Taxonomic changes proposed:**
  - Gila bicolor (formerly Siphateles bicolor) is retained, and the authors' Gila bicolor is renamed Gila caerulea.
  - Siphateles and Snyderichthys become subgenera of Gila.
  - Clinostomus is united with Richardsonius.
  - Gila nigrescens is split into several species, including G. pandora, G. pulchra and G. conspersa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat adaptation:** Several Colorado River forms, especially [[Gila robusta]] elegans, show streamlined bodies, slender caudal peduncles and depressed neural and haemal spines, interpreted as adaptations to swift water. The author notes that gill raker number and form reflect ecological adaptation more than phylogeny.
- **Feeding ecology:** Gila atraria is omnivorous. Ptychocheilus and Richardsonius elongatus/balteatus are carnivorous, with hooked pharyngeal teeth. Ptychocheilus hyomandibulars show a lateral ridge linked to predatory feeding.
- **Biogeography:** Gila atraria lives in the Lake Bonneville basin and upper Snake River; subgenus Siphateles in the Lake Lahontan basin; Richardsonius from the Great Basin north into British Columbia; Clinostomus-group east of the Rockies from the upper Mississippi to the Atlantic. The author argues the central distributional gap is not a basis for generic separation and attributes such disjunctions to past geological change.
- **Dispersal hypothesis:** The author accepts a Miocene migration of cyprinids from Eurasia via a Bering land bridge, implying common descent from a [[Leuciscinae]] ancestor.

## Key Findings

- **Generic synonymy:** Gila, [[Siphateles]] and [[Snyderichthys]] are treated as one genus, Gila. The only objective separating criterion proposed, the pharyngeal tooth count (single-row in Siphateles, 1,4–4,1 in Snyderichthys, 2,5–4,2 in Gila), is considered subgeneric at most, since Gila pulchra also has 1,4–4,1 and mass hybridization between Gila and Siphateles has been documented.
- **Richardsonius and Clinostomus:** Distinguishing characters (mouth size, anal ray count with wide overlap, peritoneum colour) were judged insufficient, so the two are merged.
- **Pharyngeal teeth:** Tooth formulae vary widely within and among species. Examples: Gila atraria usually 2,5–4,2; Gila caerulea 2,5–4,2 (rarely); Gila bicolor and G. mohavensis single-row (0,5–4,0); Gila copei 1,4–4,1 or 2,4–4,2; Ptychocheilus 2,5–4,2 with no grinding surface; Richardsonius 2,5–4,2 or 2,5–5,2.
- **Orbitosphenoid:** Richardsonius has a high, thin interorbital septum, whereas Gila and Ptychocheilus have a heavy, U-shaped or V-shaped bone without septum, consistent with the streamlined head of the latter two.
- **Autosphenotic and frontal:** In Richardsonius the autosphenotic reaches the dorsal cranial surface, separating frontal and supratemporal-intertemporal. In Gila and Ptychocheilus it does not.
- **Hyomandibular:** Ptychocheilus has a distinctive high lateral ridge and anterior projection, absent in other species studied.
- **Basihyal, urohyal and basipterygium:** Each separates at least one group (e.g., Gila copei basipterygium is broad and short; Ptychocheilus urohyal has a covered conical cavity).
- **Phylogenetic hypothesis:** Gila atraria is the most generalized form. Gila robusta, G. caerulea and G. crassicauda are closely related to it. Richardsonius evolved from a generalized atraria-like ancestor toward a small carnivorous form. Ptychocheilus represents a large carnivorous, fluvial lineage, with P. lucius the most specialized and isolated earliest.
- **Fossil evidence:** Fossils from Idaho, Oregon, Nevada, Cache Valley (Utah) and the Bidahochi Formation (Arizona) are assigned to Gila, Richardsonius or Ptychocheilus. The oldest is Miocene (Leuciscus rosei, British Columbia). Gila, Richardsonius and Ptychocheilus were distinct by the Middle Pliocene, and the subgenus Siphateles by Mid-Pleistocene.
- **Methodological proposal:** Three criteria for weighting osteological characters are proposed: low intraspecific variation, early ontogenetic fixation, and conservatism across related species. The author stresses that osteology should be combined with paleontological, zoogeographic and ecological evidence.
- **Future use:** The work aims to provide a standard base for paleontological identification of Cenozoic cyprinids and comparison with Eurasian minnows.
