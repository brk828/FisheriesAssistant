## Executive Summary

This report summarizes a multi-agency fish community monitoring effort conducted October 18–20, 1999, along the perennial reaches of [[Aravaipa Creek]], a Sonoran Desert stream in the Gila River Basin, Arizona. Nine fixed stations (Guest House, Chimney Rock, Turkey Creek, Hell Hole, Horse Camp, Painted Cave, Wagner Ranch, White's Ranch, and Sycamore Tree) were sampled using paired seining and backpack electrofishing ([[Smith-Root Model 12]]) methods across a 200-meter reach at each site, subdivided by [[Pool-Run-Riffle Habitat]] type. The survey quantified habitat structure (substrate, depth, width, cover) alongside species-specific catch data, age class (adult/juvenile), and effort (area seined, electrofishing seconds). The dataset documents a native-fish-dominated assemblage typical of the [[Gila River Basin Native Fish Fauna]], with co-occurring nonnative species subject to removal.

## Background & Description

The report does not provide individual morphometric data (length, weight, meristics) for captured fish; rather, it records **age class** (adult vs. juvenile) per species per station and habitat type, allowing inference of population age structure and recruitment. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure**: Each station's 200 m reach was partitioned into discrete pool, run, and riffle habitat units, each characterized by dominant substrate (gravel [GR], sand [SA], run/cobble [RU/CO], boulder [BO], silt [SI]), average width, maximum depth, and habitat area (m²).
- **Cover types**: Quantified in m² and included overhanging vegetation (OV), bedrock ledge (BL), undercut bank (UB), woody debris (WD), and emergent vegetation (EV) — key microhabitat features for native fish refugia, especially for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], both associated with swift riffle/run habitat over clean gravel-cobble substrate.
- **Backwaters**: Connected and isolated backwater habitats were noted at several stations (e.g., Turkey Creek, Painted Cave, Sycamore Tree), providing important nursery habitat, reflected in concentrations of juveniles and nonnative species like [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Western Mosquitofish]] in these lentic microhabitats.
- **Species-habitat associations** (inferred from Table 5 data): AGCH and MEFU were abundant across riffle and run habitats; TICO showed strong riffle affinity; CAIN and PACL occurred across pool, run, and riffle habitats; nonnative CYLU, GAAF, LECY, and AMNA were concentrated in pool and backwater habitats (notably at Sycamore Tree station), consistent with their preference for slower, warmer water relative to native riffle-run specialists.
- **Sampling effort normalization**: Effort was standardized via seined area (m²) and electrofishing duration (seconds) per habitat type, enabling catch-per-unit-effort comparisons across the longitudinal gradient of the creek (nine stations spanning the perennial reach).

## Key Findings

- The survey confirms Aravaipa Creek continues to support a robust native fish assemblage including two federally protected species, [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], alongside [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Roundtail Chub]].
- Juvenile native fish (AGCH, MEFU, CAIN, PACL, TICO) were documented at nearly all stations, indicating ongoing successful reproduction and recruitment within the creek during 1999.
- Nonnative species (Red Shiner, Mosquitofish, Green Sunfish, Yellow Bullhead) were present at multiple stations, most notably concentrated at the **Sycamore Tree** station, and were actively removed by field crews as part of an ongoing native fish conservation strategy — Red Shiner specimens were preserved in 10% formalin rather than released.
- Habitat heterogeneity (pool-run-riffle mosaic with variable substrate and cover) was documented at fine spatial resolution across all nine stations, providing a baseline dataset for future comparative monitoring of habitat change and fish community response.
- Sampling combined complementary gear types (seining and backpack electrofishing) to reduce capture bias across habitat types, with total effort explicitly quantified (e.g., Guest House: 597 seconds shocking, 1302 m² seined) to support standardized future replication.
- This dataset serves as a critical historical baseline for long-term monitoring of [[Native Fish Conservation]] efforts in the [[Gila River Basin]], particularly relevant to endangered species recovery planning for Spikedace and Loach Minnow.
