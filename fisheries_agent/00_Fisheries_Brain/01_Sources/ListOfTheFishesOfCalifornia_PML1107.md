# List of the Fishes of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #faunal-checklist

## Executive Summary

This document is the front matter and checklist of Occasional Papers of the [[California Academy of Sciences]] No. 133 (1979), by Carl L. Hubbs, W. I. Follett and Lillian J. Dempster. It enumerates all marine, euryhaline and freshwater fishes reliably reported as native to California, or introduced and established within the State, plus introduced fishes not known to occur there at present. The fauna comprises 866 species (referable to 528 genera and 99 families), of which 816 are native and 49 introduced. The list is a revision of a manuscript list issued in 1953 to West Coast students of the fauna.

## Background & Description

The publication is a systematic checklist rather than a research study. Its scope is defined geographically: the State's waters include the entire [[Colorado River]] where it forms the California boundary, and the Pacific Ocean within 805 km (500 miles) of any point of land in the State, bounded by the seaward projections of the northern and southern state boundaries. The offshore limit coincides roughly with the outer edge of the [[California Current]].

Taxonomic arrangement follows [[Greenwood et al. (1966)]] for teleosts and advice from [[Leonard J. V. Compagno]] for elasmobranchs. Scientific names follow the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] (1964) and the 1972 Monaco amendments. Common names generally follow the [[American Fisheries Society]] and the [[California Department of Fish and Game]]. The authors note that the validity of some localized forms needs critical appraisal, and that two family-group names (Petromyzontidae and Rhinodontidae) were before the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature for spelling determination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist covers the full range of California aquatic habitats:

- **Marine and pelagic waters** of the California Current system, which dominate the fauna (729 marine native species).
- **Euryhaline and estuarine systems** (35 native euryhaline species), including bays and lagoons.
- **Inland freshwater systems** (52 native freshwater species), including lakes, rivers and desert springs. Many native freshwater taxa are endemic or localized, such as the [[Owens Pupfish]] and [[Clear Lake Hitch]] subspecies.
- **Deep-sea and bathypelagic fauna**, represented by numerous lanternfishes, bigscales, dreamers, seadevils and snailfishes, several recorded from unusual depths.

The authors also note that some species, such as the [[Longnose Eel]], [[Highfin Lizardfish]], [[Cuskpout]] and [[Bigpored Snailfish]], were included although they are not confined to California waters, because each has been taken both north and south of the State.

Introduced species include Old World and Asian fishes (for example [[Goldfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Grass Carp]]) and a range of North American freshwater fishes such as [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) and [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*).

## Key Findings

- **Total fauna:** 866 species in 528 genera and 99 families. Of these, 816 are native (729 marine, 35 euryhaline, 52 freshwater) and 49 are introduced and established (3 marine, 7 euryhaline, 39 freshwater).
- **Subspecies:** 93 subspecies are recognized, referable to 43 species. Of these, 90 are native (32 marine, 15 euryhaline, 43 freshwater) and 3 are introduced freshwater forms.
- **Introduced fishes not known to occur at present in California:** 28 species (referable to 23 genera and 17 families), comprising 2 marine, 6 euryhaline and 20 freshwater species.
- **Cartilaginous fishes:** The elasmobranch section lists sharks, skates, rays and chimaeras, including the [[White Shark]] (*Carcharodon carcharias*), [[Whale Shark]] (*Rhincodon typus*), [[Basking Shark]] (*Cetorhinus maximus*) and [[Pacific Angel Shark]] (*Squatina californica*).
- **Lampreys and hagfishes:** Several brook and river lamprey species are recognized, with the validity of several localized forms noted as needing review.
- **Freshwater cyprinids and catostomids:** The tui chub (*Gila bicolor*) complex contains numerous named subspecies, and several native minnows and suckers are listed as localized or endemic.
- **Rockfishes:** The genus [[Sebastes]] is the largest rockfish group in the list, with more than 50 named species.
- **Nomenclatural changes noted:** Several family-group names with incorrect stems (e.g., Cyemidae to Cyematidae, Stomiatidae to Stomiidae) are flagged for correction if the 1972 article is repealed.
- **Tuna and billfishes:** Numerous oceanic species are included, such as the [[Albacore]] (*Thunnus alalunga*), [[Yellowfin Tuna]] (*Thunnus albacares*), [[Bigeye Tuna]] (*Thunnus obesus*), [[Striped Marlin]] (*Tetrapturus audax*) and [[Swordfish]] (*Xiphias gladius*).
