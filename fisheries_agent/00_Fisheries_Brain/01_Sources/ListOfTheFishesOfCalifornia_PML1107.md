# List of the Fishes of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #chondrichthyes #osteichthyes

## Executive Summary

This Occasional Paper of the [[California Academy of Sciences]] (June 12, 1979; about 133 pages) is an annotated checklist of the fishes of California, compiled by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], [[W. I. Follett]], and [[L. J. Dempster]]. It enumerates species that have been reliably recorded from the state, including its seaward waters, as native, introduced, or in some cases questionably recorded. The authors describe it as a revision, and they request criticism from specialists. The list spans jawless fishes, cartilaginous fishes, and bony fishes, from lancelets through sunfishes.

## Background & Description

**Scope and boundaries.** The list covers marine, euryhaline, and freshwater fishes recorded from California. It excludes fishes known only from north or south of the state's northern and southern boundaries, and it includes species known to occur within about 500 miles seaward of the coast. Fishes of the Polar Regions are excluded. The seaward limit is said to roughly match the outer boundary of the [[California Current]].

**Structure.** The checklist is organized taxonomically by order and family, with family headings (e.g., "Myxinidae, Hagfishes") followed by species entries. Native species are shown in boldface and introduced species in italics. Entries give the authority and year, and common names are provided. Subspecies are listed where recognized. Footnotes flag taxa whose validity or generic allocation is still under review.

**Taxonomic totals (as reported, with OCR uncertainty).** The introduction gives approximately 565 species (OCR reads "g65") referable to a number of genera and families, with about 93 subspecies (OCR reads "e3"). ## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist is a faunal inventory, not a field study, so it contains no sampling data, abundance estimates, or water-chemistry measurements. Ecological context is limited to:

- **Habitat range:** taxa from deep-sea forms (e.g., [[Bathylagus]] smelts, dreamers, and snailfishes) to nearshore kelp-associated species (e.g., [[Kelp Greenling]]-type kelp fishes, clingfishes, and gunnels), and freshwater species of rivers, lakes, and springs (e.g., [[Pit River Tui Chub]], pupfishes, and suckers).
- **Biogeographic concern:** many freshwater taxa are localized forms whose validity the authors consider in need of critical appraisal. Several are restricted to single lakes or drainages (e.g., Clear Lake and Goose Lake forms), which is relevant to conservation.
- **Introductions:** the list records introduced species such as [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), and [[Oriental Weatherfish]] (*Misgurnus anguillicaudatus*). Introduced freshwater and euryhaline fishes are a significant component of the state's fauna.

## Key Findings

1. **Comprehensive inventory:** the list is intended to enumerate all species reliably recorded from California, with native and introduced taxa distinguished.
2. **Major groups represented:**
   - *Lancelets and hagfishes:* a single lancelet ([[Branchiostoma californiense]]) and several hagfishes (*Eptatretus* and *Myxine*).
   - *Lampreys:* several brook lampreys and the [[Pacific Lamprey]] (*Lampetra tridentata*), including localized forms such as the Kern and Modoc brook lampreys.
   - *Sharks:* a large assemblage including [[Whale Shark]] (*Rhincodon typus*), [[White Shark]] (*Carcharodon carcharias*), [[Basking Shark]] (*Cetorhinus maximus*), thresher sharks (*Alopias*), and numerous cat sharks, smoothhounds, and requiem sharks.
   - *Batoids:* skates (*Raja*, *Bathyraja*), rays (e.g., [[Pacific Electric Ray]], *Torpedo californica*), stingrays, and mantas (*Manta birostris*, *Mobula*).
   - *Bony fishes:* the most species-rich portion. It includes major groups such as rockfishes (*Sebastes*, with a large number of named species), sculpins, snailfishes, gobies, blennies, and tunas and billfishes.
3. **Rockfish diversity:** the genus *Sebastes* is the most conspicuous marine assemblage, with dozens of named species, including the Pacific ocean perch and cowcod.
4. **Introduced and questionable records:** introduced species are marked in italics, and the authors note that some listings (e.g., certain Atlantic and Asian species) are recorded but not known to occur in the wild in California.
5. **Taxonomic revision in progress:** footnotes indicate that subspecies validity and generic placements are under review, and the authors invite criticism.
6. **Conservation relevance:** the localized freshwater forms (e.g., Owens sucker, Humpback sucker, Shortnose sucker, and various pupfishes and splittails) are emphasized as taxa requiring further study.
