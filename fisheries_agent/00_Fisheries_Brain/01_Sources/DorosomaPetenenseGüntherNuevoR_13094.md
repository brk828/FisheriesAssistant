# Dorosoma petenense (Günther) - Nuevo Registro para la Cuenca del Río Yaqui

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This paper documents the first known record of **[[Dorosoma petenense]]** (threadfin shad) in the **[[Rio Yaqui Basin]]**, Sonora, Mexico. Specimens (n=41 and n=73) were collected near the town of Vícam (27°35'N, 110°19'W) in August 1988 and May 1989, respectively, close to the river mouth at Estero Los Algodones. The species' presence represents a likely natural range expansion from an introduced population at the mouth of the **[[Rio Colorado]]**, approximately 600 km away along the coast, rather than a direct introduction. The finding raises conservation concerns regarding potential competition and hybridization with the native congener **[[Dorosoma smithi]]** (Pacific gizzard shad), a species endemic to the Pacific slope drainages of Sonora and Nayarit.

## Background & Description

### Dorosoma petenense (threadfin shad)
- Standard length range in collected specimens: **14.9–97.5 mm**
- Scales in longitudinal series: **41–48**
- Dorsal fin rays: **11–14**
- Prepelvic scutes: **15–18**
- Diagnostic features distinguishing genus *Dorosoma* from sympatric *Opisthonema* (per Miller, 1963):
  - Profile anterior to dorsal fin is somewhat naked, not crossed by scales
  - No bilobed dermal fold on anterior vertical margin of cleithrum
  - Presence of an axillary scale
  - Pectoral fins do not fold into a groove
  - Origin of dorsal fin not far anterior to insertion of pelvic fins
  - **200–400 gill rakers (branquiespinas)**

### Dorosoma smithi (Pacific gizzard shad) — for comparison
- Scales in longitudinal series: **68–75**
- Dorsal fin rays: **11–12**
- Prepelvic scutes: **17–18**
- Diffuse humeral spot (vs. more defined in *D. petenense*)
- Dorsal filament does not extend beyond the midpoint of the distance between dorsal and caudal fins
- Restricted to freshwater habitats; no records from brackish or marine waters

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Dorosoma petenense]]** exhibits wide salinity tolerance, inhabiting large rivers, bays, reservoirs, lakes, estuaries, and marine environments (per Miller 1963; Minckley 1973).
- Original native range: Belize to the Ohio River (Kentucky, USA), type locality Lake Petén, Guatemala.
- Extensively introduced throughout the USA as forage fish for sport fisheries (Bryan & Sopher 1969; Gerdes & McConnell 1963; Kimsey, 1954; Minckley & Krumholz 1960).
- Prior Mexican border records: Upper Rio Grande (El Paso, TX to Isleta, Chihuahua) and the **[[Rio Colorado]]** (Arizona, California, Nevada, Utah, Sonora, Baja California).
- **Habitat conditions at capture sites:**
  - First collection (Aug 1988): depth >2 m, width 20 m, near-lentic habitat, water temperature 31°C
  - Second collection (May 1989): intermittent channel, depth 1.5 m, water temperature 30°C
- **Co-occurring species** collected at the same site:
  - Native: **[[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]]**, **[[Lile stolifera]]**
  - Introduced/exotic: **[[Micropterus salmoides]]**, **[[Lepomis cyanellus]]**, **[[Pomoxis nigromaculatus]]** (new locality record within basin), **[[Cyprinus carpio]]**, **[[Ameiurus melas]]**, *Tilapia* sp.
- Proposed dispersal mechanism: coastal migration along the Gulf of California, potentially associating with **[[Opisthonema spp.]]** (thread herring/"sardina crinuda"), analogous to migration patterns documented in California by Krygier et al. (1973).
- Alternative (less likely) hypotheses: migration from introduced populations in the northern Rio Yaqui basin, or an undocumented direct introduction at the delta.

### Niche of native congener — Dorosoma smithi
- Endemic to Pacific slope drainages from southern Sonora to Nayarit.
- Historically abundant in reservoirs **[[Presa Plutarco Elías Calles]]** (El Novillo) and **[[Presa Álvaro Obregón]]** (Oviachic).
- Reported as subsequently absent from the delta region, contrasting with earlier records near the Bajo Río Yaqui delta (Hubbs & Miller 1941).
- Strictly freshwater; no brackish/marine tolerance documented.

## Key Findings

1. **First confirmed record** of *Dorosoma petenense* in the Rio Yaqui Basin (Sonora), based on specimens deposited in the ichthyological reference collection of the **Centro Ecológico de Sonora** (Cat. No. CES-0442, CES-0633).
2. The population is established near the river mouth (~30 km from the Estero Los Algodones), suggesting successful colonization rather than a transient occurrence.
3. Likely origin: long-distance coastal migration (~600 km) from the Rio Colorado delta population, potentially facilitated by co-migration with **[[Opisthonema spp.]]** in the Gulf of California.
4. New locality record within the basin for **[[Pomoxis nigromaculatus]]** (black crappie), attributed to anthropogenic transport for sport fishing; historically restricted to a pond in Arizona (northern basin) for ~25 years prior.
5. **Conservation concern**: Establishment of *D. petenense* may pose a competitive threat to the native, range-restricted **[[Dorosoma smithi]]**, particularly via:
   - Direct habitat/resource competition
   - Potential **hybridization** — documented between *Dorosoma* and *Signalosa* genera (Minckley & Krumholz 1960), and directly observed between *D. petenense* and *D. cepedianum* in the Rio Bravo (S. Contreras-Balderas, pers. comm. 1989).
6. Species identification challenges noted: *D. petenense* can be easily confused with sympatric *Opisthonema* spp., which are commercially important to Sonoran coastal fisheries (Arvizu-Martínez 1987), potentially explaining historical under-detection.
7. The paper calls for improved population monitoring of **[[Dorosoma smithi]]** across its natural range and further assessment of the ecological impact of *D. petenense* establishment in the Lower Rio Yaqui.
