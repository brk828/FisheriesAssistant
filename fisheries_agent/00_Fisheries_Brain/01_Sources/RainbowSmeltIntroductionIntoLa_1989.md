# Rainbow Smelt Introduction into Lake Powell

#ichthyology #marine-biology #osmeridae #freshwater-ecology #invasive-species #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary
This document is an interagency memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) to Region 6, dated October 19, 1989, responding to a State of Utah proposal to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] (*Osmerus mordax*) into [[Lake Powell]]. Rather than presenting original morphological or ecological field data, the memo functions as a regulatory and risk-assessment communication. It flags concern over the State's own acknowledgment that "downstream migration of smelt is expected," and raises alarm about potential impacts on native fish species across [[Arizona]], [[California]], [[New Mexico]], [[Nevada]], and [[Utah]] via the [[Colorado River]] system. The FWS declines to support the proposal as drafted, citing insufficient data, and calls for formal [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] before any introduction proceeds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target introduction site:** [[Lake Powell]], a large reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] spanning the Utah–Arizona border, managed in part with federal funding.
- **Dispersal risk:** The State of Utah's own draft proposal anticipates "downstream migration of smelt," implying an inability to fully contain the species within the reservoir once introduced.
- **Downstream exposure:** Potential range expansion threatens native fish assemblages in five states — Arizona, California, New Mexico, Nevada, and Utah — indicating a broad hydrological connectivity concern across the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Trophic/competitive concerns (implied):** As a planktivorous forage fish, [[Rainbow Smelt]] introductions elsewhere have historically altered zooplankton communities and competed with or preyed upon native larval fishes — though this specific memo does not detail such mechanisms explicitly, its concern for "native fishes" implies these standard risks associated with [[Nonnative Fish Introductions]].
- **Regulatory habitat context:** Because Lake Powell involves federal funds in its management, its ecological alteration falls under federal environmental compliance frameworks, notably the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Key Findings
- The State of Utah's draft proposal explicitly acknowledges that downstream migration of introduced [[Rainbow Smelt]] is expected — a key red flag driving FWS's objection.
- The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) does **not support** Utah's proposal as drafted, citing:
  - Potential adverse impacts to native fish populations in AZ, CA, NM, NV, and UT.
  - Insufficient available data to assess risk adequately.
- Because federal funds are used in Lake Powell management, the State of Utah is required to initiate **formal [[Section 7 Consultation]]** with the FWS.
- Any introduction should proceed **only if**:
  1. A **"Finding of No Significant Impact" (FONSI)** is issued, and
  2. Full compliance with the **[[Endangered Species Act]]** is demonstrated.
- The recommendation reflects consultation with, and shared concerns of, external experts:
  - Dr. Paul Marsh, [[Arizona State University]]
  - Joe Janish, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]
