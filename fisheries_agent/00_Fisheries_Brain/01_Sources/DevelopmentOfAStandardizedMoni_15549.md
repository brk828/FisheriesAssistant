# Conceptual Framework for Basin-wide Monitoring of Razorback Sucker Restoration Activities

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Catostomidae #razorback-sucker #restoration-ecology #monitoring-program

## Executive Summary

This first-draft conceptual framework document, prepared by [[Robert T. Muth]] at the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] ([[Colorado State University]]), outlines a standardized, long-term monitoring program to evaluate restoration activities for the federally endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Green River]] and [[Upper Colorado River]] systems. The largest extant riverine population occurs in the middle [[Green River]] system of northeastern Utah and northwestern Colorado, but adult numbers are low (estimated 300-600 fish during 1980-1992) and recruitment is minimal. The program aims to monitor biological and physical responses to restoration efforts including flow management from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], nonnative fish control, and stocking. The monitoring framework follows a life-period approach targeting adults, larvae, and potentially early juveniles, building on ongoing [[Flaming Gorge Research Program]] studies and [[National Park Service]] surveys. Secondary objectives include monitoring nonnative fishes, food resources, and channel/floodplain geomorphology. The program is designed for eventual incorporation into the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP).

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] was once widespread and common throughout larger rivers of the [[Colorado River Basin]], but only disjunct populations and scattered individuals exist today. Using capture-recapture data from 1980-1988, researchers estimated the razorback sucker population in upper sections of the middle [[Green River]] (RMI 346.8-176.3) at 948 fish (95% confidence interval: 758-1,138). [[Modde et al.]] reported an average population size between 300 and 600 fish during 1980-1992, concluding that although numbers are very low, this population is dynamic. Successful reproduction was only recently confirmed through collection of larvae and early juveniles.

Between 1975 and August 1995, 919 captures of adult razorback sucker were recorded in the [[Centralized Database]], with 815 from the [[Green Subbasin]] and 104 from the [[Upper Mainstem Subbasin]]. In the [[Green River]], 98% of captures occurred between the [[Yampa River]] confluence and entrance of [[Desolation Canyon]], with highest concentrations in the [[Escalante Reach]] (55%), [[Jensen Reach]] (23%), and [[Sand Wash]] area (8%). Approximately 93% of all captures occurred during April-June, with electrofishing accounting for 87% of captures.

Adult razorback sucker aggregate during spring spawning (mid-late April through May, extending to mid-June in high-runoff years), associated with increasing flows and warming water temperatures (10-18°C; mean ~15°C). Primary spawning areas include RMI 315-304 on the mainstem [[Green River]], with pre-spawning staging and post-spawning recovery areas at the mouths of [[Yampa River]], [[Ashley Creek]], [[Old Charlie Wash]], and [[Duchesne River]].

Larval sampling conducted 1992-1995 as part of the [[Flaming Gorge Research Program]] and [[National Park Service]] surveys produced the first confirmed captures of razorback sucker larvae in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. During 1992-1995, 1,572 razorback sucker larvae were collected from the middle [[Green River]] with considerable annual variation (20 in 1992, 292 in 1993, 1,217 in 1994, 43 in 1995). Captures occurred between 15 May and 20 July, with 54% collected during 15-31 May and 35% during 1-15 June. Light-trap samples accounted for 94% of all larval captures. Most larvae (84%) were 11-12 mm TL, possibly <21 days old posthatching. In the lower [[Green River]] (1993-1995), 170 larvae were collected, primarily from [[Millard Canyon]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The middle [[Green River]] system provides critical habitat for razorback sucker at multiple life stages. Spawning occurs on gravel bars in the mainstem river during spring high flows. Larvae drift downstream to low-velocity nursery habitats including channel backwaters, mouths of tributary channels, flooded bottomlands, and flooded side canyons. Key nursery areas identified include [[Cliff Creek]] (90% of captures in [[Escalante Reach]]), [[Stewart Drain]]/Lake (82% in [[Jensen Reach]]), and [[Greasewood Corral]] (82% in [[Ouray Reach]]).

Flow and temperature regimes from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] significantly influence spawning timing and larval production. Spawning typically coincides with water temperatures first approaching 14°C and increasing spring flows. The ephemeral nature of nursery habitats at current flow regimes limits temporal availability for larval rearing, particularly in the lower [[Green River]].

Diet analyses of 70 larvae from the middle [[Green River]] and 22 from the lower [[Green River]] revealed consumption primarily of rotifers, chironomid larvae, filamentous or colonial algae, and diatoms/desmids. However, 53% of larvae from the middle [[Green River]] had empty guts, compared to 0% from the lower [[Green River]], suggesting potential food limitation in some areas.

Concerns exist regarding potential adverse impacts of electrofishing on spawning razorback sucker. Laboratory experiments indicated that electrofishing could injure adults, cause premature gamete expulsion, significantly reduce egg hatching success, and reduce larval growth. This necessitates careful evaluation of sampling methods.

## Key Findings

**Adult Monitoring Recommendations:**
- Institute sampling strategy previously used by [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (1979 to mid-late 1980s) with possible modifications to limit electrofishing over active spawning areas
- Consider restricting electrofishing to pre-spawning staging or post-spawning recovery areas
- Implement less frequent monitoring (e.g., every 3 years) to alleviate population-level impacts
- Alternative: rely on present spring [[ISMP]] with inclusion of "special" sampling sites
- Include [[ISMP]] specimen-handling protocols

**Larval Monitoring Recommendations:**
- Use light-trap sampling exclusively at night in low-velocity nursery habitats
- Focus monitoring in [[Escalante Reach]] (RMI 319.4-301.7), [[Jensen Reach]] (RMI 301.7-276.9), and [[Ouray Reach]] (RMI 276.9-248.1)
- Concentrate efforts at: [[Cliff Creek]] (RMI 302.8), [[Stewart Drain]]/Lake (RMI ~299.2-299.1), [[Red Wash Launch]] backwaters (RMI ~298.5-298.0), [[Sportsman's Drain]] (RMI 296.5), [[Greasewood Corral]] (RMI 251.9), [[Old Charlie Inlet]] (RMI 251.8)
- Consider including lower [[Green River]] sites: [[Millard Canyon]] (RMI 33.5), [[Anderson Bottom]]/[[Bonita Bend]] (RMI 31.5-31.0)
- Sample twice weekly during mid-May through mid-June, at minimum
- Initially conduct yearly monitoring until sufficient baseline data acquired
- Use commercially available floating quatrefoil light traps (Southern Concepts, Birmingham, Alabama; $305-$351 each)

**Juvenile Monitoring:**
- Data inadequate for recommendations; sampling should be research-oriented
- Concentrate in areas with high larval numbers ([[Escalante]], [[Jensen]], [[Ouray]], [[Sand Wash]] reaches)
- Sample backwaters and less ephemeral low-velocity habitats
- Begin sampling late June-early July, following larval sampling
- Use seines and light traps with entry slits ≥4-6 mm wide

**Food Resources Monitoring:**
- Wait for completion of [[Brigham Young University]] study (D. Shiozawa and M. Belk) developing procedures for quantifying aquatic invertebrates in [[Canyonlands National Park]]
- Evaluate procedures for basin-wide monitoring after study completion
- Conduct gross (not rigorous) monitoring as guideposts

**Nonnative Fish Monitoring:**
- Develop species-specific and life-stage-specific sampling strategies (research needed)
- Keep monitoring at localized level initially
- Wait for results of levee-removal studies before basin-wide expansion
- Under [[ISMP]], enumerate all fishes collected (including nonnatives)
- Re-evaluate [[ISMP]] design after ongoing studies complete

**Channel and Floodplain Monitoring:**
- Complete preliminary inventory of PHABSIM, channel monitoring, HEC2, and sediment sites
- Add missing sites and fully document methods and data at each location
- Re-establish historic sites (if properly documented) to enable comparisons with new data
- Ensure adequate temporal and spatial coverage in critical areas

**General Recommendations:**
- Incorporate [[Geographic Information System]] (GIS) technology into recovery efforts for data storage, retrieval, management, mapping, modeling, and decision-making
- Implement [[Global Positioning System]] (GPS) methods in field studies to increase spatial precision and facilitate GIS data entry
- Maintain sufficient flexibility to accommodate changes as strategies are evaluated and knowledge increases
- Design monitoring to account for patchy temporal and spatial distributions of larvae and ephemeral nature of nursery habitats
- Adjust sampling times and areas depending on annual flow, temperature, and habitat conditions

**Data Summary (1975-1995):**
- Total adult captures recorded: 919 (815 [[Green Subbasin]], 104 [[Upper Mainstem Subbasin]])
- Total larval captures (1992-1995): 1,572 middle [[Green River]], 170 lower [[Green River]], 15 [[Colorado River]] inflow of [[Lake Powell]]
- Juvenile captures: 4 specimens (extremely limited data)
- Sampling effort (all years): 923 samples middle [[Green River]], 100 lower [[Green River]], 142 [[Colorado River]]
