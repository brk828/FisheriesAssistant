# An Analysis of Diversity in the Darter Genus Percina

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae #Percina #systematics #darters

## Executive Summary
This doctoral dissertation presents a comprehensive systematic analysis of the darter genus **[[Percina]]** (family [[Percidae]]), aiming to clarify interspecific relationships, evaluate and revise subgeneric classification, and explore the evolutionary and ecological drivers of morphological diversity. Using a combination of numerical taxonomy, phylogenetic reconstruction based on modified midventral scales, and electrophoretic analysis of enzyme systems, Page established a monophyletic origin for the genus and proposed a revised nine-subgenus classification. The study also examined habitat partitioning within Percina and documented convergent evolution with related darter genera **[[Etheostoma]]** and **[[Ammocrypta]]**.

## Background & Description
- **Modified Midventral Scales**: A key taxonomic character in Percina; likely evolved due to selective advantages from increased scale size and scale teeth on the venter.
  - Largely **absent in females**.
  - Shows **seasonal variation** correlated with the spawning period.
  - Hypothesized to serve a **reproductive function** (e.g., tactile stimulation during spawning behavior).
- **Convergent Traits** observed among ecologically similar species (both within Percina and with other darter genera) include:
  - Degree of **fusiformity** (body streamlining)
  - **Adult body size**
  - **Snout shape**
  - **Premaxillary protrusility**
  - **Loss of gas bladder**
  - **Pigmentation patterns**
  - Various **body proportions**

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Page identified seven major **habitat divisions** utilized by species of Percina, each associated with distinct ecomorphological adaptations:

| Habitat Type | Locomotor Mode |
|---|---|
| Gravel raceway | Pelagic |
| Pool with current | Pelagic |
| Riffles | Benthic |
| Sand raceway | Benthic |
| Pool with vegetation | Pelagic |
| Quiet pool | Benthic |
| Rapids | Pelagic |

- **Pelagic species** occupy gravel raceways, pools with current, pools with vegetation, and rapids.
- **Benthic species** occupy riffles, sand raceways, and quiet pools.
- **Habitat invasions**: More derived (advanced) species of Percina have secondarily invaded riffle, pool, and sand raceway habitats — niches also occupied by species-groups of **[[Etheostoma]]** and **[[Ammocrypta]]**, resulting in convergent morphology.
- **Reciprocal invasion**: *Etheostoma nianguae* appears to have secondarily invaded the pool-with-current habitat, converging morphologically with more primitive [[Percina]] species occupying similar niches.

## Key Findings

### Taxonomic Revisions
- **Subgenus reassignment**: *Percina crassa* and *P. roanoka* transferred from subgenus **[[Ericosma]]** to **[[Alvordius]]**.
- **Subgenus reduction**: **[[Hypohomus]]** reduced to a monotypic subgenus (containing a single species).
- **New subgenus created**: A ninth subgenus established to contain *P. cymatotaenia* and an undescribed species.
- Diagnoses provided for all **nine subgenera** of Percina, alongside a full genus-level phylogeny.

### Molecular/Biochemical Evidence
Electrophoretic analysis of three enzyme systems supported a **monophyletic origin** for Percina:
- **Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) B-subunit isozyme**: identical mobility across all examined species.
- **Malate dehydrogenase (MDH)**: common pattern shared by all species except two.
- **Tetrazolium oxidase**: common mobility shared by all species except one.

### Evolutionary Implications
- Habitat-driven **morphological convergence** is a major theme, occurring both within Percina and between Percina and other darter genera ([[Etheostoma]], [[Ammocrypta]]).
- The modified midventral scales represent a key **synapomorphy** used in reconstructing intrageneric phylogeny, with a probable role in **reproductive behavior**.

### Methodological Approach
- **Numerical taxonomy** program for quantitative species relationships.
- **Phylogenetic analysis** based on scale morphology (midventral scale modifications).
- **Protein electrophoresis** (allozyme analysis) for assessing genetic relationships and monophyly.
