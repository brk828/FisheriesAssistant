# Dispersion of Macrofauna in a Section of Muddy Fork, Jefferson County, Kentucky

#benthic-invertebrates #freshwater-ecology #amphipoda #isopoda

## Executive Summary

This study mapped the spatial distribution of abundant benthic invertebrates in a 16-foot riffle section of [[Muddy Fork]], [[Jefferson County Kentucky]], sampled on March 3, 1962. Using a [[Surber Sampler]] (1 square foot quadrats), the authors took 18 samples along three transects. The community was dominated by [[Amphipoda]], [[Isopoda]] and [[Diptera]], which together made up roughly 82–87 per cent of animals at each transect. Four malacostracan species were counted, and the authors found that most abundant taxa favored the same microhabitats: dense [[Cladophora Glomerata]] beds in the channel and coarse rubble. The authors also speculated about niche differences and possible competition between the two large isopods.

## Background & Description

The study was conducted in a stream that flows through densely populated residential areas. It receives domestic sewage from septic tanks and pollutants from automotive shops and other businesses. The study area was a riffle with a bedrock bottom partly covered by algae, gravel, sand, rubble and mud. Most rubble measured 2 to 3 inches in diameter. A large stone at the upstream end produced a quiet eddy, and additional eddies formed along the banks. Preliminary observations began in January 1962, and the quantitative sampling took place between 1:00 and 3:00 P.M. on March 3, 1962.

Sampling used an unmodified [[Surber Sampler]] (after Surber, 1937) with 40-mesh netting. Samples were preserved in toto in 10 per cent formalin, then sorted to taxonomic categories and transferred to ethanol. Only amphipods and isopods were identified to species, because other animals were scarce and taxonomically difficult.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical conditions**
- Currents were generally below 1.5 feet per second in the main channel.
- Water temperature during sampling was 48° F; air temperature ranged from 42° to 44° F.

**Primary producers and biofilm**
- The dominant benthic alga was [[Cladophora Glomerata]]. [[Batrachospermum]] sp. was rare.
- Epiphytic [[Diatoms]] on the Cladophora were *Rhoicosphenia curvata* and *Diatoma vulgare*. *Gomphonema* sp. and *Navicula* spp. were rare but consistently present.
- The authors cite Blum (1956) to describe Cladophora and its diatoms as part of a stream community that needs swift water, relatively high light and solid substrates.

**Invertebrate community**
- Amphipoda, Isopoda and Diptera were the most abundant groups. They made up 86.5%, 86.7% and 81.8% of animals at each transect, respectively.
- [[Diptera]] included *Metriocnemius* sp. and two species of [[Hydrobaeinae]]. One Hydrobaeinae species made up an estimated 90 per cent of the flies collected.
- Other taxa (grouped as "Other" in Table 1) included Oligochaeta, Turbellaria, Decapoda, Trichoptera, Coleoptera, Pelecypoda and Gastropoda.

**Pollution context**
The sewage and automotive inputs are described as background to the site. The paper does not measure pollutant levels or link them to the invertebrate patterns.

## Key Findings

**Sampling effort and totals**
- 18 quadrats were sampled: 5 on the downstream transect, 7 on the middle transect and 6 on the upstream transect. Transects were 8 feet apart.
- Malacostracans occurred in all but 4 samples.
- Total counts were: *Lirceus fontinalis* 330, *Synurella dentata* 78, *Asellus intermedius* 75 and *Crangonyx gracilis* 69.

**Species-level observations**
- [[Crangonyx Gracilis]]: The text reports 49 females (none gravid), 29 males and 1 unsexed individual under 4 mm. The average length was 7.3 mm for females and 7.5 mm for males. These counts sum to 79, not the stated total of 69, so the discrepancy is noted here as found in the source. No size or sex segregation was observed.
- [[Synurella Dentata]]: Only 5 mature females and 1 mature male were found. The other 72 specimens were under 4 mm, with an average length of 3.0 mm.
- [[Asellus Intermedius]]: 7 immature, 38 females and 30 males were recorded. Adults reached 13 mm. Of the females, 12 (31.6%) were carrying young. Gravid females averaged 8.8 mm and all females averaged 8.9 mm.
- [[Lirceus Fontinalis]]: 120 immature individuals, 123 females and 87 males were recorded. 11 females (8.9%) were gravid. Gravid females averaged 9.8 mm, compared with an overall female average of 8.0 mm. No size or sex segregation was detected.

**Distribution patterns**
- All abundant invertebrates showed a preference for the same habitats: [[Cladophora Glomerata]] beds in the channel and coarse rubble. The greatest densities occurred in the thickest vegetation beds.
- *S. dentata* and *C. gracilis* were more common in mid-channel and were scarce or absent in eddies. *C. gracilis* occurred along the banks in larger numbers than *S. dentata*.
- The two isopods shared the same areas, but *L. fontinalis* was more common than *A. intermedius* in every sample.
- Tendipedid larvae (Chironomidae-type midges) were more closely tied to algal beds than the malacostracans.

**Interpretation**
- The authors suggest *S. dentata* and *C. gracilis* occupy different [[Niche Partitioning|niches]]. *S. dentata* walks upright on the bottom, while *C. gracilis* swims or scuds on its side. They propose that feeding may differ as well, though this was not tested.
- Because *L. fontinalis* and *A. intermedius* look similar and *L. fontinalis* is more abundant, the authors propose possible [[Interspecific Competition]]. They do not test it.
- According to Cole (1957), *L. fontinalis* is the most abundant aquatic isopod in the area and is almost ubiquitous in habitat, while *A. intermedius* has a more restricted distribution.

**Limitations noted in the source**
The study covers one short riffle section and a single sampling date, and it was limited by the taxonomic difficulty of most groups. The authors note that little prior data exist on the habitats and behavior of these malacostracans, so their interspecific interpretations remain speculative.
