# Station Profile: Makah National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative station profile for the **Makah National Fish Hatchery**, located in Neah Bay, Washington, and operated under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region I). Established in 1981 under authority of 87 Stat. 436 (Oct. 4, 1973), the hatchery's mission is to restore depleted anadromous [[Salmonidae|salmonid]] runs on the Makah Reservation and nearby watersheds along the north Washington coast and the [[Straits of Juan de Fuca]]. The profile details funding, staffing, fish distribution numbers, and infrastructure development needs rather than primary biological/morphological data, reflecting a fisheries management and hatchery operations focus rather than a species-level ichthyological study.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristic counts, coloration) are provided in this source. The document instead identifies target species by common name and life-history stage relevant to hatchery production:

- **[[Fall Chinook Salmon]]** — primary production species; smolt production goal of 3,500,000.
- **[[Coho Salmon]]** — distributed as part of restoration stocking.
- **[[Chum Salmon]]** — distributed as part of restoration stocking.
- **[[Steelhead]]** — anadromous rainbow trout form; production planned to double via new water reuse system.

All species are managed at the **smolt** life stage prior to release ("Numbers of Fish Distributed" excludes eggs and fry).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Anadromous life history**: All four species/stocks are anadromous, spawning and rearing in freshwater hatchery infrastructure before outmigration to marine waters via the Straits of Juan de Fuca and north Washington coast.
- **Restoration context**: The hatchery exists specifically to supplement/restore depleted wild runs impacted by historical anthropogenic pressures on the [[Makah Reservation]] watersheds.
- **Disease pressure**: Mention of **viral hemorrhagic septicemia (VHS)** as a recurring or potential pathogen risk, prompting proposed installation of an **ozone water disinfection system** to protect hatchery stocks and the aerated water reuse system.
- **Water reuse system**: An aerated water reuse system was under construction (operational by summer following FY1993), intended to support increased steelhead production, indicating water quality/flow constraints as a limiting ecological/operational factor at the facility.

## Key Findings
- **Numbers of Fish Distributed** (excluding eggs and fry), all to Washington state waters:
  - FY 1991: 1,287,476
  - FY 1992: 1,738,419
  - FY 1993: 2,189,099
  - Trend shows a **steady year-over-year increase** in fish distributed (~70% increase from FY91 to FY93).

- **Infrastructure improvements completed in FY1993**: rearing pond covers, pumphouse roof modifications, replacement of pump #6, visitor access road, landscaping of visitor entrance, painting of 3 residences, and bird barrier netting installation.

- **Visitation**: Approximately **6,600 visitors** to the station during the fiscal year.

- **Development Needs / Long-Range Goals**:
  1. Achieve fall chinook production goal of **3,500,000 smolts** by FY1994.
  2. Complete and operationalize the **aerated water reuse system**, enabling a doubling of steelhead production.
  3. Install an **ozone water disinfection system** as a safeguard against VHS outbreaks and to support the water reuse system's success.
  4. Expand visitor facilities and educational information (exhibits, brochures).
  5. Complete adult fish crowders, gate operators, and fish lift (completed as of report date).
  6. Fill vacant **fish culturist** and **maintenance mechanic helper** positions.
  7. Pave hatchery area and access road.

- **Congressional Oversight**: Station falls under the district of Rep. Al Swift (2nd District, WA) and Senators Patti Murray and Slade Gorton, reflecting federal funding/political accountability structure typical of [[National Fish Hatchery System]] facilities.
