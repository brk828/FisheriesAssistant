# Oxygen Depleted Waters: Origin and Distribution in Lake Powell, Utah-Arizona

#limnology #reservoir-ecology #water-quality #dissolved-oxygen

## Executive Summary

This study documents the origin, distribution, and seasonal dynamics of oxygen-depleted waters in [[Lake Powell]], a large canyon-type reservoir on the [[Colorado Plateau]]. The reservoir exhibits two principal oxygen depletion problems affecting its fishery: (1) a shallow oxygen minimum layer (frequently anaerobic) during summer stratification at 45-60 feet depth, and (2) oxygen-deficient bottom waters during winter. The oxygen minimum layer develops annually, extends the full 175-mile length of the lake, and is most intense in side-canyon embayments where anaerobic conditions may occur. The study demonstrates that these patterns repeat reliably on an annual basis and present formidable barriers to vertical fish migration during late summer and early fall.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Powell]] occupies the former [[Glen Canyon]] sector of the [[Colorado River]], situated on a high, arid plateau devoid of natural lakes. The reservoir exhibits distinctive canyon-type morphology with the following characteristics:

**Physical Setting:**
- Floor width: 150-240 m at bottom
- Uniform grade: 0.038%
- Vertical walls rising hundreds of meters above lake surface
- Hypsographic curve strongly convex upward
- Side-canyons oriented normal to the thalweg, generally dry without perennial streams
- Historically 95% of discharge in transit from upstream, only 5% from local watersheds

**Circulation Patterns:**
- Mixes only once annually during winter cooling (early October through early March)
- Summer epilimnion extends 10-20 m (30-60 ft) below surface
- Two distinctive inflow types: warm, fresh water during spring flood (overflow density current) and cold, salty water during winter (underflow density current)
- Mid-depth withdrawal current from dam penstocks blocks convective mixing from penetrating to lake bottom

**Methods:**
Sampling and analytic techniques specified by Johnson and Merritt (1979). All oxygen determinations performed by [[Winkler Method]] on water samples collected by [[Van Dorn Bottle]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The oxygen depletion patterns in [[Lake Powell]] significantly impact the reservoir's fishery and aquatic ecosystem. The study identifies comparable phenomena to those observed in [[Lake Mead]] (Hoffman and Jonez, 1973), suggesting these patterns may be characteristic of large canyon-type reservoirs in arid regions.

**Seasonal Oxygen Dynamics:**

*Summer Stratification (Late Summer Peak in September):*
- Distinctive oxygen minimum layer develops in metalimnion immediately below epilimnion
- Forms negative heterograde profile (Aberg and Rhode, 1942)
- Core at 45-60 feet below surface
- Upper boundary sharp and discrete, corresponding to epilimnion-metalimnion boundary
- Lower limit more diffused, usually reaching about 200-foot level
- Extends full 175-mile length of lake

*Spatial Patterns:*
- Well developed in thalweg area where water is deep
- Better developed in side canyons forming shoaling water embayments
- Intensifies away from thalweg toward heads of side canyons
- Horizontal gradient: anaerobic water at bay heads grading to 2-4 mg/l dissolved oxygen at bay mouths in mainstem
- Pattern recurs year-after-year within given bays and bay-after-bay in given years

*Winter Conditions:*
- Oxygen minimum layer systematically dissipated by convective mixing during late autumn and early winter
- Bottom waters commonly oxygen poor (3-5 mg/l) throughout winter because convective mixing does not penetrate below mid-depth withdrawal current zone
- Oxygenation of bottom water depends entirely on advection of winter underflow density current
- Underflow current gradually works down lake during winter, replacing and mixing with older oxygen-poor water
- Usually reaches dam by late March

## Key Findings

**Origin of Oxygen Depletion:**
The oxygen depletion originates from respiration of organic "rain" as it falls into higher density and viscosity water of upper metalimnion where its fall is impeded.

**Oxygen Deficit Distribution:**
- Amount of oxygen consumption below epilimnion (oxygen deficit) tends to be conservative from point-to-point along length of any side canyon
- Not equally distributed in depth along canyon length
- Respiration process compressed into smaller water volume at bay heads than at bay mouths
- Oxygen depletion most intense at bay heads
- Where oxygen depletion zone sufficiently thin, anaerobic conditions may obtain

**Depth Profile Transformation:**
Oxygen depth profile transforms systematically from negative heterograde (oxygen minimum layer in mid-water) at bay mouth to clinograde (oxygen deficit at bottom) at bay head. In [[Last Chance Bay]] during 1974, the oxygen deficit induced during 5 August - 30 September period was approximately equal at bay mouth and bay head, but distributed over greater depth at bay mouth.

**Special Conditions in San Juan Arm:**
Interplay of density currents from both [[San Juan River]] and [[Colorado River]] sometimes traps and isolates pools of oxygen-depleted water against the bottom at lower end of San Juan Arm.

**Fishery Implications:**
- Water of marginal quality for fish anticipated at different depths, places, and times during year
- Oxygen minimum layer presents formidable barrier to vertical migration of fish during late summer and early fall
- Patterns reliably repeat on annual basis and will likely continue as long as hydrologic regimen stays in present mode

**Maximum Development:**
Oxygen minimum layer reaches maximum development in September, with systematic dissipation beginning in late autumn through convective mixing processes.
