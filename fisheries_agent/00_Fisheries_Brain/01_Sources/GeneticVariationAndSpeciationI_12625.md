# Genetic Variation and Speciation in New World Cichlids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cichlidae #genetics #speciation

## Executive Summary

This 1972 Master's thesis by Irving L. Kornfield (State University of New York at Stony Brook) examines genetic variability and speciation in [[Cichlasoma]] species from North America. The study employed electrophoretic analysis of 19 protein loci in [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] from the [[Rio Grande]] in Texas and three undescribed endemic cichlid species from the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] in Coahuila, Mexico. The research revealed extraordinarily low genetic variability across all species examined, with only one polymorphic locus ([[Lactate Dehydrogenase]]) detected among 130 endemic cichlids. Despite complete electrophoretic identity between two morphologically and ecologically distinct endemic species (algae-eating and snail-eating forms), these species show marked differences in pharyngeal dentition and feeding ecology, suggesting reproductive isolation. The findings challenge the assumption that isoenzyme loci adequately reflect genome-wide genetic events during speciation.

## Background & Description

The study focuses on four cichlid taxa: [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]] (the Rio Grande Cichlid) distributed throughout major rivers of Texas and northern Mexico, and three endemic undescribed species restricted to the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]]. The endemic forms are presumed descendants of the ancestral C. cyanoguttatum.

The two most abundant Cuatro Cienegas species are externally indistinguishable but differ dramatically in trophic morphology:
- **Algae-eating species**: Most abundant and widely distributed; possesses very fine pharyngeal teeth and light supporting musculature; feeds primarily on vegetation and algae
- **Snail-eating species**: Less abundant with more limited distribution; characterized by thick heavy pharyngeal teeth used for crushing snails
- **Predatory species**: Very restricted distribution; elongated body form with very strong jaws; presumed predatory habits

Laboratory studies confirmed that dentition does not change with growth, and no natural hybrids have been observed among over 3,000 field specimens examined. The endemic species inhabit thermal springs providing virtually constant temperature and ionic content, contrasting sharply with the variable riverine environment of C. cyanoguttatum.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study explicitly contrasts environmental variability between habitats:

**Rio Grande Environment** (C. cyanoguttatum):
- Seasonally variable water temperatures: 14.2°C to >25°C
- Periodic spring flooding altering nutrient content and suspended matter
- Generally considered a variable aquatic environment

**Cuatro Cienegas Environment** (endemic species):
- Thermal springs provide nearly constant temperature and mineralogical conditions
- Most ponds are deep and spring-fed from bottom, ensuring continuous water mixture with no stratification
- Annual variation in water temperature and ionic content buffered by low surface area to volume ratios
- Evolution of temperature-sensitive gastropods supports environmental constancy
- Some shallow ponds may experience effects of air temperature variation and seasonal runoff

Sample localities within Cuatro Cienegas Basin included:
- **El Mojarral East**: 50-70m long, 15-20m wide, 0.3-0.6m average depth
- **Pond near Rancho Orozco**: Circular, ~25m diameter, 5m maximum depth
- **Pond near Laguna Tio Candido**: Small circular pond, ~5m diameter
- **Pond southeast of Rancho Orozco**: Very small, ~4m diameter, <1m depth

The Cuatro Cienegas Basin last experienced inundation from outside approximately 30,000 years before present (based on core samples), suggesting endemic cichlids may have become established at that time, though speciation could have occurred more recently.

## Key Findings

**Genetic Variability Results:**

Sample sizes: 38 C. cyanoguttatum from Rio Grande; 114 algae-eating and 44 snail-eating endemic specimens from four Cuatro Cienegas localities.

Nineteen electrophoretic loci examined across multiple enzyme systems:
- [[Acid Phosphatase]] (2 loci): Monomorphic in both populations; mobility differences between Rio Grande and Cuatro Cienegas forms
- [[Amino Peptidase]] (1 locus): Single band in both populations; greater mobility in C. cyanoguttatum
- [[Esterase]] (2-4 loci): Two distinct five-banded patterns in Cuatro Cienegas cichlids (N=123); Type II phenotype at 84% frequency; no association with species, sex, or size; C. cyanoguttatum showed single invariant three-band phenotype
- [[General Protein]] (2-3 loci): Three identical bands in all specimens
- [[Glutamate Oxaloacetate Transaminase]] (1 locus): Four-banded pattern; identical mobility between populations
- **[[Lactate Dehydrogenase]]** (1 locus): **ONLY POLYMORPHIC LOCUS** in Cuatro Cienegas cichlids (N=138); fast allele frequency = 0.97±0.01; did not fit Hardy-Weinberg distribution (χ²=5.30); C. cyanoguttatum exhibited single uniform band slower than both Cuatro Cienegas alleles
- [[Malate Dehydrogenase]] (2 loci): Two invariant bands; mobility differences between populations
- [[Phosphoglucomutase]] (1 locus): Single band in both populations
- [[Tetrazolium Oxidase]] (1 locus): One invariant band; faster mobility in Cuatro Cienegas forms
- [[Xanthine Dehydrogenase]] (1 locus): One invariant band; faster mobility in Cuatro Cienegas forms
- [[Serum Proteins]] (4-7 loci): Eight bands generally observed; occasional absence of one dark-staining band; no association with species

**Quantitative Variability Estimates:**

Proportion of polymorphic loci: 0.000-0.055 (depending on criterion: p<0.95 or p<0.99)
Heterozygous loci per individual: 0.00-0.30%

These values differ markedly from all previous estimates in other organisms, which typically report >25% polymorphic loci and >10% heterozygous loci per individual.

**Genetic Similarity Analysis:**

Using [[Rogers' Coefficient]] (S) of genetic similarity:
- **S = 1.0** between the two endemic Cuatro Cienegas species (algae-eating and snail-eating forms): Complete electrophoretic identity despite marked morphological and ecological differentiation
- **S = 0.582** between Cuatro Cienegas cichlids and C. cyanoguttatum: Within range reported for sibling species pairs

**Critical Interpretations:**

1. **Environmental Variability Hypothesis Not Supported**: C. cyanoguttatum inhabits variable environment but is electrophoretically monomorphic, contradicting predictions that variable environments select for genetic variability

2. **Stochastic Processes Unlikely**: Population sizes (100-1,000 for Cuatro Cienegas species) could permit genetic drift, but absence of differences between isolated populations of different species argues against drift or founder effects as explanations

3. **Speciation Without Isoenzyme Differentiation**: The complete electrophoretic identity (S=1.0) between morphologically distinct, reproductively isolated endemic species contradicts the assumption that isoenzyme loci reflect genome-wide genetic reorganization during speciation

4. **Restricted Genomic Reorganization**: Speciation in these cichlids did not involve random genetic reorganization across the genome, but rather reorganization of restricted portions not reflected in the isoenzyme loci examined

5. **Adaptive Nature of Isoenzyme Loci**: The author suggests that because enzyme polymorphism is adaptive, electrophoretic characterization may reflect current environmental conditions rather than evolutionary history

6. **Methodological Limitations**: The study questions whether 20-30 studied loci adequately estimate variability in the entire genome, a widely held assumption in population genetics at the time

The thesis concludes that isoenzyme loci may not always accurately reflect evolutionary events involving the total genome, particularly in cases of rapid morphological specialization such as the "explosive speciation" characteristic of cichlid radiations in African Rift Valley lakes and, on a smaller scale, in Cuatro Cienegas.
