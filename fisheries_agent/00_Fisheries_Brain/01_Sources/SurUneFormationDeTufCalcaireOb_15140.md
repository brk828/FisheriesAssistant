# Calcareous Tufa Formation Built by Phormidium valderianum on the Kundelungu Plateau

#cyanobacteria #oscillatoriaceae #phycology #tufa

## Executive Summary

This short French-language note by J.-J. Symoens and F. Malaisse describes an actively forming [[Calcareous Tufa]] deposit at a seep on the eastern escarpment of the [[Kundelungu Plateau]] in the [[Democratic Republic of the Congo]]. The seep feeds the [[Luanza River]], a tributary of the [[Luizi River]] in the [[Congo River Basin]]. The seep water is strongly alkaline and mineral-rich compared with surrounding streams, and the tufa is built mainly by the cyanobacterium [[Phormidium Valderianum]], accompanied by calcicolous [[Diatoms]]. The authors also propose that tufa growth is seasonal, occurring mainly in the low-water period.

## Background & Description

The authors have studied the hydrobiology of the Luanza basin since 1965. The Luanza rises on the Kundelungu Plateau, flows about 33 km, and drops over the [[Kasompola Falls]], a fall of 120 m. Downstream of the falls the river runs fast through a deep, steep-sided gorge. About 60 m below the falls, a seep enters the river from its right bank and is actively depositing tufa.

- **Location:** eastern foothills of the Kundelungu massif, at 1190 m altitude (lat. 10°18'07" S, long. 27°53' E), 6.1 km west of [[Kabiashia Village]].
- **Setting:** the seep lies about 10 m above the river bed. Its water runs down a north-facing cliff and forms a curtain of water threads that reach the river through a channel. Tufa covers the vertical wall and a horizontal ledge, which is locally carpeted with moss toward the back of the cavity.
- **Deposit thickness:** 2 cm on the vertical wall, up to 5 cm at the back of the cavity.
- **Tufa texture:** the ledge deposit is compact; the wall deposit is slightly friable because its cavities are larger. The outer surface is mamelonné (rounded, with hemispherical bumps 4–5 mm across) and green to blue-green. The interior is cream white. A wavy striation in cross-section may record seasonal growth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry.** The seep water differs sharply from the surrounding streams. Most Luanza streams and tributaries are soft, dilute and poor in calcium, with pH 4.7–7.2 and total alkalinity (T.A.M.) below 0.6 méq/l. The seep is alkaline and highly mineralized:

| Parameter | Luanza falls source | Neighbouring affluents | Tufa seep |
|---|---|---|---|
| pH | 4.7–4.8 | 7.0–7.2 (as reported) | 7.8–8.1 |
| Dry residue (mg/l) | 6–15 | 32–42 | 112–258 |
| T.A.M. (méq/l) | 0.06–0.13 | 0.30 | 1.11–3.34 |
| Ca²⁺ (méq/l) | 0.004–0.008 (approx. range as given) | 0.12 | 0.57–2.18 |

The source table is partly garbled by OCR, so the values above should be checked against the original. Conductance at the seep ranged from 110 to 350 (units as printed). The authors say conductance and T.A.M. at the seep are three to ten times higher than in the streams.

**Seasonality.** Water chemistry changes markedly through the year:

- **December (low water):** dry residue 258 mg/l, conductance 350, Ca²⁺ 2.18 méq/l (43.69 mg/l).
- **March–April (high water):** dry residue 112 mg/l, conductance 110, Ca²⁺ 0.57 méq/l (11.42 mg/l).

The authors infer that tufa is deposited mainly during low water, when mineral concentration is highest.

**Tufa-building community.** Acid decalcification of samples (Malaisse 4582, 4593, 4801) identified [[Phormidium Valderianum]] (Oscillatoriaceae) as the dominant encrusting cyanobacterium. Its filaments are densely tangled, with narrow sheaths, blue-green trichomes about 2.5 µm thick and cells 3–4 µm long. The cells are not constricted at the cross-walls, and the filaments are neither curved nor tapered. The authors note that calcicolous diatoms accompany the cyanobacterium, as is typical of European slope tufas (Symoens and colleagues, 1951–1960).

**Ecology and distribution.** The species is eurytopic, occurring in still or running water, cold or thermal, sometimes sulphurous or brackish, and on pond floors, rice fields, wet wood and seeping rocks. It is cosmopolitan, ranging from the tropics (Antilles, Central Africa, India, Ceylon) to the Arctic (Alaska). African records include Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Mali, Cameroon, the Congo, East Africa and southern Africa. A related variety, *cartilagineum*, was described by [[Copeland 1936]] from hot springs in Yellowstone.

## Key Findings

- **Active tufa formation:** a seep about 60 m below [[Kasompola Falls]] is building calcareous tufa at present, depositing 2–5 cm on the walls and ledge of a cavity.
- **Chemical contrast:** the seep is alkaline (pH 7.8–8.1) and rich in dissolved solids and calcium, unlike the soft, acidic to neutral streams around it. Its alkalinity is three to ten times higher.
- **Seasonal deposition:** dry residue and calcium are about twice as high in December as in March–April. The authors suggest that tufa is deposited mainly in low-water periods, and that the wavy internal striation records this.
- **Biological builder:** [[Phormidium Valderianum]] is the dominant encrusting organism, with calcicolous diatoms present. This matches the cyanobacterial tufa communities known from Europe.
- **Wider significance:** the species has a near-global distribution and is recorded in several African countries. The authors also note a previous report from a limestone quarry seep in Morocco (Frémy, 1930).
