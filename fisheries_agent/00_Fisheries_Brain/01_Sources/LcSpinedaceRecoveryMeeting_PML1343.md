# Little Colorado Spinedace Recovery Team Meeting Notes and Service Response (July 2008)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepidimeda

## Executive Summary

This note covers two linked documents: the agenda and field updates from the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] Recovery Team meeting held July 25, 2008, at the [[Black Mesa Ranger District]] in Arizona, and a July 14, 2008 letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) responding to the team's March 13, 2008 request to revise the species' [[Recovery Plan]]. The team discussed restructuring and membership, planning for a possible plan revision, possible 2011 on-the-ground projects, fish passage projects, and 2008 spinedace field results at several sites. The Service said it is weighing whether a full revision or a lighter amendment is needed, and it expects to complete a [[Five-Year Status Review]] by the end of September 2008.

## Background & Description

The [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidimeda vittata*) is a listed threatened fish. Its recovery plan is managed by a team led by Robert W. Clarkson of the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Phoenix Area Office. The team requested in March 2008 that the Service revise the plan.

The meeting agenda covered these items:

- Recovery team restructuring, including a possible split from the [[Apache Trout]] team and changes to team membership.
- Preliminary planning for a future plan revision, including whether groundwater pumping is a concern.
- Identification of possible 2011 and later on-the-ground projects to spend reclamation funds on spinedace recovery.
- Potential fish passage projects.
- On-the-ground status updates and planning.
- Other topics and scheduling of the next meeting.

Parts of the agenda text are illegible in the scan, so some items are reported only in summary form.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Field updates from the 2008 season are reported by site. Spinedace are stocked at several sites, and the team tracked both survival and the presence of [[Non-Native Fish]].

- **[[Bear Canyon]]:** Three young-of-year (YOY) spinedace were collected below the 95 road crossing, at the 03 and 04 stocking location. A long dry stretch lies below the upper stocking site (05 and 06), and no spinedace were collected there, despite a wet year. No non-native fish were collected.
- **[[Dane Spring]] creek:** Spinedace were collected at the stocking location near Dane Spring. Water level was up and the creek was flowing continuously. No non-native fish were collected.
- **[[Yeager]] stocking pools:** 30 adult and 1 YOY spinedace were observed in stocking pool #1 in May. On 7-8-08, about 30–40 adults and 25 YOY were observed in pool #2 (the count is garbled in the scan). On the same date, 167 spinedace were stocked into pools #1 and #2, split evenly.
- **[[West Leonard]]:** Over 300 spinedace were collected in about 20 minutes of seining at the "Honey Hole." All size classes were present, up to about 6–7 inches. Spinedace dominated the catch, with [[GACL]], [[CAIN]], and [[RHOS]] also collected. Creek and water level were described as excellent. No non-native fish were collected.
- **[[Dines Tank]]:** Spinedace were observed and collected in May and June. The tank was choked with weeds, and no spinedace were collected this year.

## Key Findings

**Service response (July 14, 2008):**

- The Service has received the team's March 13, 2008 request and acknowledges the delay in replying.
- Recovery plans are needed, but full revisions often exceed available Service resources. The Service's national office is developing streamlined guidance for updating plans. That guidance is expected to be finalized over the next several months.
- Once the guidance is in place, the Service will decide whether a major revision is needed, or whether amendments (such as updating the implementation schedule) are enough. Revision must be prioritized across all species the Service manages.
- A 5-year status review under Section 4(c)(2) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] is underway and is planned for completion by the end of September 2008. It will check whether the listing classification remains accurate.
- The Service is working with partners to find additional funds for spinedace recovery actions.
- [[Shaula Hedwall]] of the Flagstaff Ecological Services Sub-office has been pursuing the issue with Service managers and staff.

**Field results (2008 season):**

- Spinedace were found at most sites, with the strongest counts at West Leonard (over 300 in about 20 minutes) and Yeager pool #2 (about 25 YOY).
- No non-native fish were reported at any site where sampling occurred.
- Dines Tank produced no spinedace this year because of weed growth.
- The Bear Canyon upper site was dry and produced no spinedace.

**Planning items (agenda):**

- Team restructuring and membership changes, including a possible split from the Apache Trout team.
- Assessment of whether groundwater pumping is a concern for future revision.
- Identification of 2011 and later on-the-ground projects for reclamation funding.
- Evaluation of potential fish passage projects.
- Scheduling of the next team meeting.
