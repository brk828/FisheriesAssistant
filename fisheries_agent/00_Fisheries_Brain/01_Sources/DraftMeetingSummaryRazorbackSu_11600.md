# Razorback Sucker Permit Conditions: 1992 Meeting Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This draft meeting summary records decisions by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) on which research and recovery activities may be permitted for endangered fishes in 1992. The meeting was held February 4, 1992, at the USFWS Regional Office in Denver, Colorado. The [[Razorback Sucker]] had been listed as endangered on October 23, 1991, and the same protections apply to the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The central decisions were to halt removal of adult fish from the wild until the [[Food And Drug Administration]] (FDA) resumes approval of necessary fish-handling chemicals, to allow only four more adult razorbacks to be collected, to permit only nonlethal tissue sampling, and to prohibit streamside spawning. The summary also sets out follow-up tasks for the San Juan River Program, the Horsethief and Edder ponds, and bonytail chub transport.

## Background & Description

The document is a regulatory and administrative record, not a research paper. It summarizes the legal framework and the decisions reached at the meeting.

- **Legal basis:** The [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended, prohibits harmful acts against listed species. "Taking" includes harassing, harming, pursuing, wounding, killing, trapping, capturing, and collecting, as well as attempts at such conduct. Importing and exporting, including possessing, transporting, or removing, are also covered.
- **Permitting:** Anyone intending to conduct research or recovery work must obtain a permit from the Field or State Supervisor, Fish and Wildlife Enhancement, USFWS, in the state where work is planned. USFWS decides which activities are beneficial, harmful, or both. Beneficial activities are permitted, harmful ones are not, and questionable ones are judged case by case.
- **Attendees:** Eight named participants are listed in Attachment #1. The names are not reproduced here.
- **Attachment #2:** This is a table of warmwater aquaculture chemicals and their approval status, cited as the basis for handling decisions. Most of the table is illegible after OCR. The legible entries include hormones for spawning (carp pituitary, human chorionic gonadotropin, LH-RH) and an Asian tapeworm treatment (Droncet). The key is that "A" marks approved use, "P" marks nonlabel use permitted by the FDA or EPA, and "N" marks use that is neither approved nor permitted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The stated rationale is that razorback suckers are "declining rapidly" in the Colorado River. The meeting's conservation priorities reflect that concern:

- **Population goal:** About 20 adult razorbacks are wanted to develop brood stock and founder populations that are genetically representative of wild populations. Sixteen had already been collected from the Colorado River, leaving four more needed.
- **Genetic management:** Sample sizes must be large enough to genetically distinguish razorback stocks. The document notes that larger samples generally improve statistical reliability, and that variance in the initial samples should guide later sample sizes.
- **San Juan River:** Razorback and squawfish populations there could be lost in fish kills or other catastrophic events before genetic reserves are established.
- **Holding capacity:** Facility space and qualified personnel are insufficient to hold wild fish and their progeny. The meeting concluded that fish are "probably safer in the wild" until more facilities are available.
- **Mortality limit:** The Regional blanket permit from the Washington Office allows only two razorback adult mortalities for the entire USFWS Region.

## Key Findings

1. **Adult removal is halted.** No adult razorbacks may be removed from the wild until the FDA resumes approval of chemicals needed to handle fish safely, such as disease treatments and anesthetics.
2. **Limited collection is permitted.** Four adult razorbacks may be captured from the Colorado River (or selected adjacent ponds, excluding Edder Pond) and moved to refugia in 1992, once the chemicals are approved.
3. **No other wild removals.** No adult razorbacks, Colorado squawfish, or humpback chub may be removed from the wild and transported to refugia in 1992, except the four Colorado River razorbacks.
4. **Nonlethal sampling continues.** Nonlethal tissue sampling is allowed, including on Lake Powell razorbacks, but no additional fish may be taken from the wild.
5. **Mortality triggers a stop.** Any adult razorback mortality requires all sampling to cease and notification of the Field or State Supervisor. The study is then reviewed to see whether future mortality can be avoided.
6. **No streamside spawning.** Streamside spawning of endangered fishes is prohibited in 1992 because rearing facilities are not available.
7. **San Juan genetic plan required.** The [[San Juan River Program]] must develop a plan to protect genetic resources for razorbacks and Colorado squawfish.
8. **Specific subpermit applications.** Subpermit applicants must state exactly what they plan to do and review the [[Regional Blanket Permit]] and Supplemental Application before applying.
9. **Horsethief Pond restricted.** In FY 92, only surrogate or surplus hatchery endangered fish may be held at [[Horsethief Pond]]. A research plan is needed to test the ponds before valuable fish are held there.
10. **Bonytail transport allowed.** Bonytail chubs may be removed and transported to [[Dexter]] under the bonytail chub protocol.
11. **Edder Pond radio tagging.** A work plan is to be developed to radio tag fish in [[Edder Pond]] for release into the river.
