# Notice of Intent to Sue: Endangered Species Act Violations in the Gila River Basin (1993)

## Executive Summary
This document is a legal correspondence (notice of intent to sue) authored by Dr. Robin D. Silver, dated September 26, 1993, addressed to the U.S. Secretary of the Interior (Bruce Babbitt) and the Commissioner of the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Dan Beard). It is not a primary scientific study but rather a regulatory/legal document invoking the [[Endangered Species Act]] against federal agencies for failing to act on a "jeopardy" opinion issued by the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The core allegation is that the [[Central Arizona Project]] (a federal water conveyance project) has introduced and facilitated the spread of deadly non-native (exotic) fish species into the [[Gila River Basin]], directly threatening six federally listed native fish species. The Bureau of Reclamation had reportedly failed to act on the draft jeopardy opinion for 26 months prior to this notice, despite prior correspondence (August 24, 1993) warning of ongoing contamination and lack of agency response.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: All six species inhabit freshwater systems within the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona, a critical arid-region watershed connected to the broader Colorado River drainage.
- **Primary Threat**: Introduction and spread of **non-native (exotic) species** via the [[Central Arizona Project]], a federal water infrastructure project that interconnects water sources and inadvertently (or negligently, per the letter) transports harmful exotic fish into native fish habitat.
- **Regulatory Context**: The species are protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]], with statuses including:
  - **Endangered**: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]]
  - **Threatened**: [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]]
- **Section 7 Consultation**: The letter references a required Section 7 "jeopardy" consultation process between the Bureau of Reclamation and the Fish and Wildlife Service, meant to ensure federal actions do not jeopardize listed species' continued existence — a process alleged to have stalled for over two years.

## Key Findings
- A draft "jeopardy" biological opinion from the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] had gone unaddressed by the [[US Bureau of Reclamation]] for **26 months** as of this correspondence.
- The **Central Arizona Project** is identified as the vector for introducing **deadly exotic species** into native fish habitat within the Gila River Basin, an ongoing and unresolved contamination event at the time of writing.
- Six federally listed native fish species are explicitly named as being jeopardized:
  1. [[Desert Pupfish]] (Endangered)
  2. [[Spikedace]] (Threatened)
  3. [[Loach Minnow]] (Threatened)
  4. [[Gila Topminnow]] (Endangered)
  5. [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Endangered)
  6. [[Razorback Sucker]] (Endangered)
- The letter formally initiates a **60-day notice period** required before filing a citizen suit under the Endangered Species Act, targeting both the Secretary of the Interior and the Bureau of Reclamation for non-compliance with **Section 7** consultation obligations.
- Copies of the notice were sent to congressional oversight figures: Chairman Gerry Studds (Merchant Marine and Fisheries Committee) and Chairman George Miller (Interior Committee), indicating an intent to escalate the issue politically alongside legal action.
- This document underscores a recurring conservation issue in arid Southwestern freshwater ecosystems: **exotic species introduction via water management infrastructure** as a major driver of native fish decline, distinct from but related to habitat loss and water diversion impacts.
