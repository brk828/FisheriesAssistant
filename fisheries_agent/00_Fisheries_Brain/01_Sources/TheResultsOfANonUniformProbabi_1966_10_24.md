# The Results of a Non-Uniform Probability Creel Survey on a Small, State-Owned Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #creel-survey #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1966 methodological paper by Peter W. Pfeiffer (Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources) compares two [[Creel Survey]] techniques for estimating angler fishing pressure on **[[Shanty Hollow Lake]]**, a 106-acre state-owned impoundment in Kentucky's Pennyroyal Physiographic Region. The study contrasts a long-standing **two-hour stratified systematic survey** (used 1958–1964) with a newly designed **non-uniform probability survey** (1965), developed under the guidance of Professor Don W. Hayne of the Southeastern Cooperative Fish and Game Statistics Project. While the paper does not address fish species biology directly, it is foundational to fisheries management practice, since accurate estimates of angler effort and catch underpin stock assessment and management decisions for the lake's fish populations. The key finding was that the non-uniform probability survey produced a statistically indistinguishable estimate of fishing pressure while being approximately **38 percent more efficient** than the traditional two-hour survey.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no specific fish species data is presented, the study provides important **habitat and system context**:

- **Water body**: [[Shanty Hollow Lake]], a 106-acre impoundment, located approximately 14 miles northeast of Bowling Green, Kentucky, within the **Pennyroyal Physiographic Region**.
- **Human-ecosystem interaction**: The lake supports a recreational angling fishery monitored under Kentucky's **State-owned Lakes Investigations** project (initiated 1958), funded through the **Federal Aid to Fish Restoration Funds (Dingell-Johnson Act, Project F-14-R-8)**.
- **Survey period**: Both survey methods operated from **April 1 to October 31**, sampling angler activity between **7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m.** daily, capturing the full open-water recreational fishing season.
- **Angler population studied**: Fishing parties (one or more anglers) were surveyed regarding fishing pressure, catch, fishing methods, sex ratios, trip cost, and residency status (resident vs. non-resident anglers), though only fishing pressure data were reported in this comparative analysis.

## Key Findings

- **Historical context**: From 1958–1964, fishing pressure at [[Shanty Hollow Lake]] was monitored via a **two-hour stratified creel survey** designed by Bernard T. Carter (Director, KY Division of Fisheries).
- **Census validation issue**: A complete creel census (1961–1964) run concurrently with the two-hour survey revealed that approximately **31.3 percent of anglers were missed** by the complete census (Pfeiffer, 1965), leading to its discontinuation in 1965 due to high cost and lower reliability.
- **New method introduced**: In 1965, a **[[Non-Uniform Probability Survey]]** was designed using prior fishing pressure data to assign **sampling probabilities** to two-hour time blocks within seven-day segments, allowing randomized but weighted period selection (per methods described by Lambou, 1961, and guided by Hayne).
- **Survey design mechanics**:
  - Fishing pressure values were assigned to time periods based on prior knowledge (never zero, to avoid biasing probability to impossible/zero chance).
  - Sampling probabilities (Pt) were calculated as the ratio of estimated fishing units in a period to the total estimated units in the segment.
  - One random two-hour sampling period was drawn per seven-day segment (30 total segments/periods across the season), versus 49 periods in the traditional two-hour survey.
- **Statistical comparison**:
  - The two-hour survey yielded a **fishing pressure estimate 4,388 angler-hours higher** than the non-uniform probability survey.
  - **Standard error of the difference**: 3,610 angler-hours.
  - A **t-test showed no statistically significant difference** between the two survey estimates (per Hayne, personal communication).
- **Efficiency gain**: Despite using far fewer sampling periods (30 vs. 49), the non-uniform probability survey was approximately **38% more efficient** than the two-hour survey.
- **Sources of inefficiency in the two-hour method**:
  - Systematic equal allocation of effort across all days of the week disproportionately oversampled weekdays (5 weekdays vs. 2 weekend days).
  - Equal effort was allocated across all time periods regardless of their relative importance/activity level.
  - Systematic sampling designs risk **overemphasizing certain parts of the season** due to periodicity effects.
- **Practical advantages of the non-uniform probability approach**:
  - Once probabilities are established, they can be **reused in subsequent years** if the proportional distribution of fishing pressure remains stable.
  - Reduced sampling burden makes the method well-suited to programs where **Conservation Officers** serve as creel clerks.
  - Overall, the method is **less costly** and more statistically efficient than traditional systematic two-hour surveys.
- **Broader recommendation**: Even where a full non-uniform probability design is not adopted, incorporating **some random selection of sampling periods** is advised to avoid the pitfalls of purely systematic survey designs (Hayne, personal communication).
