# Lower Colorado River Backwater Fishery Survey and Habitat Enhancement Analysis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by the California Department of Fish and Game Inland Fisheries Division (Region 5) under the Sport Fish Restoration Act (Job 2, Project F-4-D-4e), documents a survey of sport fish populations in the lower [[Colorado River]] and its associated backwaters conducted from September 1995 to October 1996. The survey assessed fish population condition using [[Proportional Stock Density]] (PSD), [[Condition Factor]] (K), catch per unit effort (CPUE) and length-weight relationships across ten Bureau of Reclamation divisions. The results are intended to identify candidate backwaters for [[Habitat Enhancement]], which would then be designed and implemented as site-specific projects. The study concludes that the river supports generally good sport fish populations, that [[Largemouth Bass]] dominate the catch, and that submerged aquatic vegetation and structural cover are the habitat features most associated with fish abundance.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Establish baseline fishery condition to select candidate sites for enhancement, and to support fishery management and angling opportunities.
- **Study area:** The river as it borders California was divided into ten divisions: Mojave, Topock Gorge, Havasu, Parker Strip, Parker, Palo Verde, Cibola, Imperial, Laguna and Yuma. The Parker Strip (from Parker Dam to Headgate Rock Dam) was treated as a separate division because it differs from the lacustrine reaches above it.
- **Sampling method:** [[Electrofishing]] was the sole sampling method, chosen because it is relatively consistent and less stressful to fish, and because boat access to most backwaters was possible. Effort was applied unequally across divisions because of differences in size and accessibility.
- **Fish community:** Several introduced sport species were found. The [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] are native species noted in the survey; most introduced species have thrived and provide significant recreational revenue to local economies.
- **Field data:** Fish were measured for total length (mm), weighed to the nearest gram, and released. Water temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, substrate and cover were recorded per transect.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Community structure:** In warmwater fisheries dominated by centrarchids, management is strongest when it supports a balanced community providing both quality bass angling and a good panfish fishery. Community structure was assessed by plotting PSD of [[Bluegill]] and [[Redear Sunfish]] (PSD_BG/RE) against PSD of Largemouth Bass (PSD_LMB) (Figure 1), using quadrants at 0.50 on each axis. The desired range on both axes was 0.40 to 0.60.
- **Habitat preference:** Areas dominated by [[Submerged Aquatic Vegetation]] (SAV), including coontail (*Ceratophyllum demersum*), spiny naiad (*Najas marina*) and sago pondweed (*Potamogeton pectinatus*), produced the highest CPUE and biomass of young-of-year fish. Submerged trees, rooted macrophytes, boulder reefs, rip-rap and rock outcrops also aggregated fish. Barren, sloping, recently dredged banks held little fish habitat value.
- **Sampling biases:** [[Carp]] abundance and biomass were under-represented or biased by electrofishing; tilapia and some other species were also poorly captured. Young-of-year largemouth bass were under-sampled, so most computations used only stock-size and larger fish. Water temperature, depth, turbidity, flow and productivity may influence CPUE.
- **Regional management:** Candidate sites included Beal Slough, Goose Flats, Ehrenberg, Sixth Avenue, Queen Park (Quehan Park) and Allied Backwater, as well as Oxbow and Arbor Lake areas (described in Appendix B). Enhancement projects require coordination with the Army Corps of Engineers (Section 10 Letter of Permission) and, for certain materials, Fish and Wildlife Service and Bureau of Reclamation approval. Federal listing of portions of the river as critical habitat for native fishes is a relevant factor.
- **Artificial habitat:** The [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]] (LHFIP) had tested artificial materials. Questions about fish preference for natural versus non-natural materials, aesthetics, lifespan and snag risk to anglers remained unresolved. The project prioritized donated natural materials, including citrus trees and Christmas trees, which persist for years in water.

## Key Findings

**Largemouth Bass population condition**
- Of 2,580 LMB sampled, 2,075 were at or above stock length (203 mm); 803 were of minimum legal size (330 mm) and 435 of quality size (381 mm).
- Overall PSD = 0.39 and overall PSD_L = 0.21 (see stated values in the Results).
- Havasu and Yuma divisions had the highest PSD values (PSD_L of 0.71 and 0.70 respectively). Mojave had the lowest PSD_L (0.13).
- Havasu and Yuma had PSD values of 0.46 and 0.58 respectively; Mojave had the lowest PSD of 0.04.
- In Havasu, although PSD was high, CPUE for LMB was the lowest of all divisions. In Yuma, which also had high PSD, CPUE was the highest.

**Condition factor and length-weight**
- Condition factor (K) generally increased with length class, possibly reflecting size-dependent sampling or growth; the highest K values occurred in the 500–599 mm class in several divisions.
- Length-weight regressions were calculated for Largemouth Bass in each division (e.g., Havasu log W = -5.2455 + 3.0415 log L over 91–550 mm; Yuma log W = -6.0034 + 3.4523 log L over 113–613 mm).
- Largemouth Bass were the most frequently sampled species in all divisions combined, followed by Redear Sunfish/Bluegill, Channel Catfish and Flathead Catfish.

**Species composition and CPUE**
- Total catch in the largest sample was dominated by Largemouth Bass in Mojave (59.9% of sample) and Topock (57.1%).
- CPUE for LMB was highest in Yuma and Parker Strip-area reaches; Black Crappie CPUE was highest in Havasu and Parker Strip.
- Electrofishing efficiency was best for centrarchids but was biased for carp, tilapia and some other species.

**Habitat enhancement recommendations**
- Enhancement priorities were to increase fish density and biomass, improve catch per unit effort, provide microhabitat for larval and juvenile fish, enhance food supply via periphyton surface area, and create fish aggregation devices.
- Natural materials (gravel, rock, brush) in configurations such as spawning gravel beds, boulder reefs and submerged brush bundles were the primary objective. Structures were to be spaced at intervals of 10–15 feet initially.
- Beal Slough trials (two years) showed CPUE improved in coves with structures, but results were inconclusive for relative abundance; bluegill CPUE showed no preference for structure material.
- Donated materials and volunteer labor were critical to the scope of the project, and installation would be phased.

**Conclusions**
- Analysis indicated generally healthy sport fish populations throughout the river, with relative values varying among divisions.
- Candidate sites were selected on the basis of recreational suitability, ease of access, landowner and public support, and environmental considerations. Fishery data alone would not determine site selection because of the complex political and agency landscape.
