# Endangered Species Recovery Planning Guidance: USFWS Region 2

#ichthyology #marine-biology #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This is an administrative package from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) Region 2 office, Albuquerque, New Mexico, dated January 1992. It sets out guidance and procedures for preparing, reviewing, approving and distributing [[Recovery Plan]]s for species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). It supersedes earlier Region 2 guidance and uses the [[P&G Handbook]] (May 1990) as its standard.

The core points are:
- Recovery plans are the blueprint for recovery actions and the standard for [[Section 7 Consultation]] and budget decisions.
- The [[Lead Station]] is responsible for drafting the plan and carrying it through review, approval and implementation.
- Timelines are 60 days for a [[Recovery Outline]] after listing, and 2.5 years for a final plan (12 months draft, 18 months final).
- Region 2 has a backlog of 38 plans, and a three-year effort starting in FY1992 aims to clear it.

## Background & Description

The memorandum comes from the Regional Director to Assistant Regional Directors and is accompanied by six attachments:

1. Lead station responsibilities and an assignment table (dated December 9, 1991).
2. The Director's memorandum of February 2, 1989, requiring early recovery outlines.
3. The [[P&G Handbook]], *Policy and Guidelines for Planning and Coordinating Recovery of Endangered and Threatened Species* (May 1990).
4. A Director's memorandum on recovery plan timeframes (dated December 4 in the attachment; the body cites 1989).
5. A memorandum on recovery plans and [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) compliance (January 25, 1991).
6. A Region 2 tracking log of recovery plan status (November 1991).

Lead station responsibilities include preparing the outline, recommending recovery team members, drafting and revising the plan, managing public and technical review, securing Solicitor review, printing and distributing approved plans, submitting annual Endangered Species Status Reports, and implementing the plan.

The 1991 lead station assignment table lists the species for which each field station, refuge or hatchery is responsible. Stations include Albuquerque, Phoenix, Austin, Corpus Christi, Clear Lake, Tulsa, Dexter, Parker, Pinetop, Mescalero and San Marcos, plus several national wildlife refuges and the Regional Office. Support and "other region" leads are noted for many species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species covered span several habitat types:

- **Fish:** Desert and southwestern rivers and springs (Gila Trout, Apache Trout, Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout, [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], Spikedace, Pecos Pupfish, Comanche Springs Pupfish, Yaqui fishes). Texas and Oklahoma fishes include darters, gambusias and the Neosho Madtom.
- **Cave and spring invertebrates:** Central Texas karst systems host beetles, harvestmen, pseudoscorpions, spiders and springsnails, including Tooth Cave species and Comal Springs invertebrates.
- **Birds and mammals:** Include the [[Black-capped Vireo]], [[Golden-cheeked Warbler]], Southwestern Willow Flycatcher, Bald Eagle, [[Whooping Crane]], Aplomado Falcon, Ocelot, Jaguarundi and [[Black-footed Ferret]].
- **Reptiles and amphibians:** Include [[Sea Turtles]] (olive, green, hawksbill, Kemp's ridley, leatherback and loggerhead), American Alligator, Concho Watersnake, Mohave Tortoise and Texas Blind Salamander, which depends on aquifer conditions.
- **Plants:** Mostly desert cacti and southwestern or Texas endemics.

The memo notes that some species are not currently listed. These are [[Candidate Species]] under management agreements, or species proposed for listing.

## Key Findings

- **Region 2 scope:** 139 listed species (subspecies and strains included) occur in Region 2. The Region is lead for 107 of them.
- **Plan status:** 66 species have approved recovery plans. Plans are lacking or not required for 41 species. No plans will be prepared for 3 species, leaving a backlog of 38 plans.
- **Recovery plan timeframes:** A recovery outline is due to the Director within 60 days of the final listing rule. Outlines are due to the Regional Office within 30 days of publication. The goal is a draft plan within 12 months and an approved final plan 18 months after the draft.
- **Review process:** After Regional Office review, plans undergo technical, agency and public review. Comment periods run 30 to 60 days, with 60 days preferred. All comments must be addressed in a plan appendix.
- **NEPA position:** Recovery plans are categorically excluded from NEPA because they are planning and guidance documents. Action decisions taken under a plan remain subject to NEPA and [[Section 7 Consultation]]. Action verbs such as "will establish" or "will not allow" should be rephrased (e.g., "may consider").
- **Implementation priorities:** The Implementation Schedule in each plan sets priorities, and budget requests should follow those priorities except where budget limits or unexpected opportunities justify a change.
- **Minor revisions:** Changes that could be made with a pen-and-ink edit do not need public review. Larger revisions follow the full review process.
- **Accountability:** Regional Director and staff performance ratings will reflect progress on recovery plans. A tracking system with monthly progress reports to lead stations will monitor the three-year schedule that starts in FY1992.
- **Attachment 6 log:** The Region 2 tracking log lists each species' group, lead station, listing date, outline date, draft and final due dates, Federal Register publication date and status. Several entries are marked "Region X has lead responsibility," meaning recovery for those species is led by another region. Some plans are marked "None planned" or "Recovery responsibilities shared with Commerce" (the sea turtles).
- **Date discrepancies:** The body text says outlines are required for species listed since October 1, 1990. The 1989 Director's memorandum says since January 1, 1989. The December 4 memorandum is cited as 1989 in the body but dated 1990 in the attachment.
