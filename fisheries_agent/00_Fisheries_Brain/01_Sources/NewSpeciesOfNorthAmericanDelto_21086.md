# New Species of North American Deltocephaline Leafhoppers

#entomology #taxonomy #Cicadellidae #Deltocephalinae #leafhoppers

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes five new species of deltocephaline leafhoppers (Homoptera: [[Cicadellidae]]) discovered during ecological studies of leafhopper-grassland relationships in [[North America]]. The new species are: *[[Latalus intermedius]]*, *[[Latalus draculus]]*, *[[Diplocolenus nigrior]]*, *[[Orocastus]] ([[Cabrulus]]) [[pinnipenis]]*, and *[[Psammotettix viridinervis]]*. Each species is distinguished by unique genitalic characters, particularly aedeagal morphology. The paper also proposes treating [[Cabrulus]] as a subgenus of [[Orocastus]] based on morphological similarities.

## Background & Description

### Latalus intermedius new species

Male length 2.6-2.8 mm; female length 2.8-3.0 mm. Color stramineous, mottled with brown; tegmina with veins heavily bordered with fuscous. Genitalic characters intermediate between *L. personatus* Beirne and *L. histrionicus* Beirne. Aedeagus evenly curved dorsad and cephalad, subparallel margined, strongly tapered to base, bearing paired spines on each side of preapical ventral gonopore, tiny lateral teeth near apex, and parallel apical processes. Style apex short and strongly hooked. Female seventh sternum without projecting lateral angles, caudal margin slightly produced across almost entire width.

Holotype male, 10 females, 15 males, 4 nymphs paratypes from Mt. Flagstaff, near [[Boulder]], [[Colorado]], 27 July 1956. Additional material from Colorado (Boulder, Cascade, Climax, Divide, Frisco, Green Mountain Falls, Tarryall, Woodland Park), Alberta (Valley View), and British Columbia (Toad River).

### Latalus draculus new species

Male length 2.8 mm; female length 3.4-3.6 mm. Related to *uncinatus* Beamer & Tuthill, distinguished by broad pygofer and wide lateral membranes of shaft. Aedeagus curved dorsad on apical half, shaft slender with broad parallel-margined lateral membranes extending to sharp tips beside large round apical gonopore, from which arise two long divergent spines directed ventrad. Style apex rapidly tapered, small, slightly curved, extreme apex minutely serrate. Female seventh sternum with weak lateral angles, broadly and prominently produced across two-thirds of caudal margin, shallowly, broadly and roundedly excavated at center.

Holotype male, 14 females and 29 paratypes from [[Yavapai County]], [[Oak Creek Canyon]] overlooking [[Coconino National Forest]], [[Arizona]], 24 August 1970.

### Diplocolenus nigrior new species

Male length 3.7 mm; female unknown. Resembles Alaskan *D. aquilonius* Ross and Hamilton and Palearctic *D. frauenfeldi* Fieber, distinguished by truncate plate apices. Color heavily marked with brown, obscuring stramineous ground color which appears as five pronotal stripes, coronal cross and marginal bands, and pale tegminal veins; scutellum stramineous, unmarked. Aedeagus angled dorsad at basal third of shaft length, parallel-margined, narrower on apical third, terminating in short convergent paired spines above gonopore. Style apex elongate, tapered beyond mesal projection, with prominent lateral thumblike projection. Plates long, truncate, bearing strongly sclerotized tooth on caudal margin.

Holotype male from [[Soda Springs]], [[Idaho]], 22 July 1951. Type deposited in D. M. DeLong collection, [[Ohio State University]].

### Orocastus (Cabrulus) pinnipenis new species

Male length 2.7-2.8 mm; female length 2.7-2.9 mm. Distinguished by finlike apical processes of male aedeagus. Color cream, unmarked except for two broad parallel light brown coronal stripes continuing backwards as narrower bands across pronotum. Elytra milky white, semihyaline, unmarked. Aedeagus stout, widest in lateral aspect, parallel sided, strongly arched, bearing paired long apical processes directed laterad, widened beyond base to triangular flaps; gonopore dorsal, ovoid; atrial arm very short. Female tegmina sub-brachypterous. Seventh sternum lacking lateral angles; caudal margin produced as two small appressed, darkened teeth.

Holotype male, 24 females, 32 paratypes from [[Vernon]], [[British Columbia]], 12 August 1953; 5 males, 7 females paratypes from base of hill, [[McDonald Pass]], [[Montana]], 26 August 1953.

### Psammotettix viridinervis new species

Male length 3.2-3.3 mm; female length 3.3-3.6 mm. Most closely related to *asper* Ribaut, differs in lacking row of dorsal serrations on aedeagal shaft, having much shorter gonopore, with shaft twice as wide at middle as width of base, and lacking two-toned color pattern. Color pale ochre, unmarked or with weak indefinite blotches of light brown on crown; tegmina stramineous, veins paler, yellow to light green, giving wing greenish cast. Aedeagus evenly curved dorsad, shaft evenly spatulate, blunt-tipped, gonopore distinctly narrower than shaft. Female seventh sternum quadrate, with shallow rounded mesal emargination.

Holotype male, 1 female, 4 males and 1 nymph paratypes from East of [[Laramie]], [[Wyoming]], 5 August 1967.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

These species were encountered during ecological studies concerning relationships between leafhopper species and [[North American Grasslands]]. The geographic distributions reveal distinct patterns:

- *[[Latalus intermedius]]* has a northern range sympatric with western *L. personatus* and eastern *L. histrionicus*, with southern range extending into [[Colorado]], far south of the other two species
- *[[Diplocolenus nigrior]]* represents a primitive branch of the line leading to *configuratus* and *brevior*, with plates intermediate between primitive elongate type and short divergent plates, forming a phenocline
- Collection localities span diverse elevations and habitats from [[British Columbia]] to [[Arizona]], indicating adaptation to varied grassland ecosystems

## Key Findings

1. **Five new species described**: *[[Latalus intermedius]]*, *[[Latalus draculus]]*, *[[Diplocolenus nigrior]]*, *[[Orocastus]] ([[Cabrulus]]) [[pinnipenis]]*, and *[[Psammotettix viridinervis]]*, all distinguished by unique genitalic morphology, particularly aedeagal structure.

2. **Taxonomic revision proposed**: [[Cabrulus]] Oman should be considered a subgenus of [[Orocastus]] Oman based on remarkable similarities in aedeagal form, style shape, and both shape and color pattern of male plates and female seventh sternum. Chief differences (absence of coronal bands in [[Orocastus]] and fusion of connective and aedeagus in [[Cabrulus]]) are derived characters having little weight for generic recognition.

3. **Phylogenetic relationships**: *[[Diplocolenus nigrior]]* represents a primitive branch in the evolutionary line leading to *configuratus* and *brevior*, evidenced by intermediate plate morphology forming a phenocline from primitive elongate to short divergent types.

4. **Geographic patterns**: *[[Latalus intermedius]]* shows intermediate morphology and geographic distribution between two related species, with range extending further south into [[Colorado]] than either parent species.

5. **Type specimens deposited**: Types deposited in [[Illinois Natural History Survey]] collection (Urbana, Illinois), except *[[Diplocolenus nigrior]]* holotype in D. M. DeLong collection ([[Ohio State University]], Columbus, Ohio).

6. **Research support**: Investigation supported by research grant from the [[National Science Foundation]].
