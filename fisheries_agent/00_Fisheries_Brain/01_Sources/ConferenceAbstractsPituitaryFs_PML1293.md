## Key Findings

**1. Cyclic pituitary FSH (rat).** The abstract concludes that pituitary FSH levels undergo a cyclic variation. The text is partly garbled, but the study reports that a steroid treatment did not produce the effect described, and it compares FSH levels across cycle days.

**2. Serum protein variation in suckers.** Preliminary electrophoretic surveys of several sucker species were reported. Within-species (intraspecific) variation in serum proteins was investigated in two species, and interspecific differences were noted. Serum globulins, transferrins, and hemoglobin were discussed, with binding characteristics said to differ from those reported in an earlier grant-supported study.

**3. Hippocampal and amygdaloid lesions and mating (deermouse).**
- Bilateral or unilateral lesions of the ventromedial hippocampus, and of the medial amygdaloid nucleus, altered normal mating patterns.
- Lesions of the basolateral amygdaloid nucleus produced a different outcome, with lesioned animals mating in some test periods but not others.
- Lesion locations were mapped against a stereotaxic atlas, and the estrous cycle was monitored by daily vaginal smear.
- The authors conclude that the integrity of these limbic structures is needed for normal mating behavior.

**4. Desiccation and water loss (three Plethodon species).**
- Water-loss rates were highest in *P. yonahlossee* and lowest in *P. glutinosus*. The rate tracked body size and shape, with the highest surface-to-volume ratio in the species losing water fastest.
- The critical value (CAP) differed among the three species and was variable within species.
- *P. glutinosus* from non-montane areas had a higher CAP value than those from montane areas.
- The authors suggest that these data point to a genetic component, with greater moisture stress in non-montane habitats selecting for higher CAP values.

**5. Reproductive stage and FSH (female rat).** Pituitary FSH levels varied with reproductive stage and gonadal condition. Levels were lower around vaginal opening, began declining by about day 11 of the cycle, and returned to the baseline by a later day. Group differences were reported as not statistically significant in some comparisons.
