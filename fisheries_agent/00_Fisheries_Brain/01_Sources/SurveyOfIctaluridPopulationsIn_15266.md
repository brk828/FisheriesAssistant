# Survey of Ictalurid Populations in the Upper Yadkin and South Yadkin Rivers

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Ictaluridae #catfish #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1987 survey assessed ictalurid (catfish family) populations in the [[Yadkin River]] and [[South Yadkin River]] above and below [[Idols Dam]] and [[Cooleemee Dam]] to determine whether [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) should be stocked upstream of these barriers. Using electrofishing and catfish basket traps, researchers collected 532 ictalurid specimens across five stations. Results confirmed that flathead catfish are absent above both dams, while bullhead populations (primarily [[Snail Bullhead]] *Ictalurus brunneus* and [[Flat Bullhead]] *Ictalurus platycephalus*) are abundant. Despite adequate forage and favorable habitat above Idols Dam on the Yadkin River, stocking was not recommended to preserve the existing productive bullhead fishery. Habitat limitations above Cooleemee Dam on the South Yadkin River also precluded stocking recommendations there.

## Background & Description

The [[Yadkin River]] and [[South Yadkin River]] are major waterways in north-central [[North Carolina]] supporting important recreational catfish fisheries. The [[Yadkin River Trail Association]] improved access by adding 13 sites on the Yadkin River and 2 on the South Yadkin River, increasing fishing pressure. [[Flathead Catfish]] were introduced into [[High Rock Lake]] in the mid-1950s by the [[North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission]]. Local anglers expressed concern that [[Idols Dam]] (a Duke Power hydroelectric facility near Clemmons, Forsyth County) and [[Cooleemee Dam]] (at Cooleemee, Davie County) prevented upstream migration of flathead catfish from High Rock Lake.

Previous surveys documented diverse ictalurid assemblages. Above Idols Dam, historical records showed [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and flat, snail, and [[Brown Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus nebulosus*). Below the dam, [[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*), white, channel, and flathead catfish plus three bullhead species were recorded. The [[South Yadkin River]] historically contained only white catfish, flat and brown bullheads, and [[Margined Madtom]] (*Noturus insignis*) above and below Cooleemee Dam.

**Sampling Methods:**
- **Electrofishing:** 5-bar hand-cranked telephone generator (magneto) operated at 75-100V AC; minimum 90 minutes shocking time per station
- **Catfish Basket Traps:** 1.2 m long, 380 mm diameter, 25 mm mesh, two 127 mm throats; baited with catfood, cut fish, cottonseed meal, grapes; minimum 8 trap-days per station
- **Sampling Period:** Every other week from 21 July through 19 August 1987
- **Stations:** Five total—three on Yadkin River (one below dam, two above), two on South Yadkin River (one below, one above dam)

All fish were identified, weighed, measured, and fin-clipped before release. Water temperature and conductivity were recorded at each site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Characteristics:**

*Yadkin River:*
- **Station 1 (below Idols Dam, 38.6 km downstream):** Bedrock outcroppings with eddy pools <2.0 m deep, rubble areas, debris piles, sunken logs along steep banks
- **Station 2 (above Idols Dam, 38.7 km upstream at NC 67 bridge):** Boulder/rubble riffles <1 m deep, debris piles, sunken logs, overhanging trees, runs <2.0 m over sand
- **Station 3 (above Idols Dam, 92.0 km upstream at US 21 bridge):** Large rubble riffle <1 m deep, major bedrock ledge in runs and pools <2.0 m deep

*South Yadkin River:*
- **Station 4 (below Cooleemee Dam, 8.0 km downstream):** Pools and runs over sandy bottom <1.5 m deep, some rubble and debris
- **Station 5 (above Cooleemee Dam, 3.2 km upstream):** Backwater of dam pool with numerous debris piles, sunken logs, one rubble-substrate pool; average width <22.7 m, depth <1 m

**Water Quality:**
- Temperatures ranged 24.0–28.2°C across all stations
- Conductivity: 90.0–95.0 µmhos below Idols Dam; 42.0–52.0 µmhos above Idols Dam; 42–45 µmhos on South Yadkin River
- [[Division of Environmental Management]] quarterly monitoring indicated good Water Quality Index rating for Yadkin River
- Biological Index (benthic macroinvertebrates) rated "fair" for Yadkin River; South Yadkin River showed poor benthic diversity due to heavy silt load

**Species Habitat Preferences:**
- [[Snail Bullhead]]: Slower current areas over sandy/gravel bottoms, around submerged logs and debris in water >1 m deep
- [[Flat Bullhead]]: Rocky riffle areas with rubble substrate
- Juvenile [[Flathead Catfish]] (<162 mm TL): Gravel, rubble, bedrock outcroppings with sunken logs and debris
- Adult [[Flathead Catfish]]: Deep holes, medium to large rivers, adaptable to turbid conditions

**Predator-Prey Dynamics:**
Flathead catfish <200 mm TL consume mainly aquatic insect larvae; fish >200 mm TL shift to piscivory with strong preference for other ictalurids, especially bullheads. Studies cited (Guier et al. 1981, Ashley and Buff 1986) documented that flathead catfish can eliminate bullhead populations, then shift to other abundant fish species. The complete absence of bullheads below both dams (despite suitable habitat) provides strong evidence of flathead catfish predation impact.

## Key Findings

**Flathead Catfish Distribution:**
- **Below Dams:** Five juvenile flathead catfish (112–162 mm TL) collected at Station 4 (South Yadkin); three juveniles (135–138 mm TL) at Station 1 (Yadkin). CPUE: 0.06 fish/minute and 0.02 fish/minute, respectively
- **Above Dams:** Zero flathead catfish collected during survey period at any upstream station (Stations 2, 3, 5)
- One adult flathead catfish (10.35 kg) collected at Station 3 in mid-June 1987 during equipment testing; physical appearance suggested angler release from another location
- Adult flathead catfish confirmed below dams by anglers; new state record 27.93 kg fish caught below Idols Dam on 11 June 1987
- Conclusion: No reproducing flathead catfish populations exist above either dam

**Bullhead Populations Above Dams:**

*Yadkin River (Stations 2 & 3):*
- Total collected: 415 bullheads (377 snail, 37 flat, 1 brown) in 183.5 minutes electrofishing
- **Mean CPUE: 2.11 fish/minute** (far exceeding 0.25 fish/minute criterion for adequate forage)
- Station 2: 317 bullheads (285 snail, 32 flat); Station 3: 98 bullheads (92 snail, 5 flat, 1 brown)
- Length frequency well-distributed across 26–325 mm size range; peak at 176–200 mm (17.1% of total)
- Catfish basket traps: 30 bullheads (23 flat, 2 snail, 5 channel catfish) in 18 trap-days; CPUE 1.37 fish/trap-day

*South Yadkin River (Station 5):*
- Total collected: 66 bullheads (53 snail, 13 flat) in 98.3 minutes electrofishing
- **Mean CPUE: 0.68 fish/minute** (exceeding 0.25 criterion)
- Also collected: 11 white catfish (CPUE 0.11 fish/minute)
- Length frequency well-distributed 76–325 mm; peak at 201–225 mm (30.3% of total)
- Catfish basket traps: Zero ictalurids captured in 8 trap-days

**Below-Dam Comparisons:**
- **Yadkin River Station 1:** 1 white catfish (88 mm), 1 blue catfish (65 mm), 3 flathead catfish; zero bullheads; zero fish in basket traps (8 trap-days)
- **South Yadkin Station 4:** 5 flathead catfish, 1 white catfish; zero bullheads; zero fish in basket traps (8 trap-days)
- Stark contrast with upstream stations demonstrates predation impact: 16 trap-days below dams yielded zero ictalurids vs. 26 trap-days above dams yielding 1.15 ictalurids/trap-day

**Management Criteria Assessment:**

Established criteria for stocking consideration:
1. Flathead catfish absent above dams: **✓ Confirmed**
2. Bullhead CPUE ≥0.25 fish/minute: **✓ Met** (Yadkin 2.11, South Yadkin 0.68)
3. Bullhead length frequencies 100–300 mm well-distributed: **✓ Met** (not skewed to single size class)
4. Adequate habitat: **Yadkin ✓ favorable; South Yadkin ✗ unfavorable**

**Habitat Suitability Analysis:**

*South Yadkin River above Cooleemee Dam:*
- **Not suitable:** River too small (width <22.7 m), shallow (<1 m average), predominantly sandy bottom with few gravel/rubble areas
- No suitable habitat for adult flathead catfish except dam reservoir
- Poor benthic diversity limits food for YOY and juvenile flathead catfish

*Yadkin River above Idols Dam:*
- **Favorable:** Wide river (>38 m average), numerous riffle areas, gravel bottoms suitable for flathead catfish <305 mm TL
- Fair benthic macroinvertebrate populations support juvenile flathead catfish diet
- Limitation: Habitat for adult flathead catfish (>305 mm requiring large cover structures) would be limiting factor

**Recapture Data:**
- Yadkin River Station 2: 3 snail bullheads recaptured (21 July to 19 August)
- Yadkin River Station 3: 1 snail bullhead recaptured
- South Yadkin Station 5: 2 flat bullheads, 3 snail bullheads recaptured (21 July to 18 August)

**Management Recommendations:**
1. **Do not stock flathead catfish above Idols Dam** despite favorable habitat and adequate forage—preserve abundant bullhead fishery (CPUE 4× higher than minimum criterion) that supports increasing angler pressure from improved access
2. **Do not stock flathead catfish above Cooleemee Dam** due to unsuitable habitat conditions
3. **Promote dual fishery management:** Flathead catfish fishery below Idols Dam; bullhead fishery above Idols Dam
4. Maintain existing fishery diversity to provide anglers choice between abundant smaller fish (bullheads) vs. fewer larger fish (flathead catfish)

**Ecological Trade-offs:**
Previous experience with flathead catfish introductions in [[Cape Fear River]] demonstrated virtual elimination of bullhead populations. Authors emphasized that trading an abundant bullhead fishery capable of absorbing high angling pressure for a less abundant flathead catfish fishery contradicts goals of enhancing fishery resources for increasing numbers of anglers. Local angler opinion supported not introducing flathead catfish above Idols Dam.
