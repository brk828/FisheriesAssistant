## Executive Summary
This record documents a two-day (October 7–8, 1995) fish community survey conducted along Aravaipa Creek, Arizona, a designated critical habitat for several federally listed native fishes of the Gila River Basin. Sampling was performed using standard block seines (4' x 6' and 4' x 15') across nine sites spanning three geographic sections: the **East End**, **Aravaipa Canyon**, and the **West End**. The survey documents strong native fish dominance throughout most of the creek, with [[Meda Fulgida]] (Spikedace) and [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] (Longfin Dace) numerically dominating catches in canyon and eastern reaches, while non-native centrarchids and ictalurids ([[Lepomis Cyanellus]], [[Ameiurus Natalis]]) were restricted to isolated pool habitats near the Horse Camp/Painted Cave Canyon area. The dataset provides an important historical snapshot of native fish assemblage structure and spatial distribution prior to more recent hydrological and invasive-species pressures on this system.

## Background & Description
While this document is primarily a count-based field census rather than a morphological study, the species recorded represent distinct desert-adapted freshwater fish guilds with characteristic body plans suited to lotic Sonoran Desert stream environments:

- **[[Pantosteus Clarki]]** (Desert Sucker) — ventral, protractile sucker-mouth adapted for algae/periphyton grazing on cobble substrates.
- **[[Meda Fulgida]]** (Spikedace) — small-bodied, laterally compressed cyprinid; associated with swift riffle/run habitats; a federally listed threatened/endangered species.
- **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]** (Longfin Dace) — small, robust minnow with elongated fins; tolerant of variable flow and temperature conditions typical of desert streams.
- **[[Tiaroga Cobitis]]** (Loach Minnow) — small, benthic, cryptically colored cyprinid with reduced swim bladder, adapted to gravel/cobble riffle interstices; federally listed species.
- **[[Gila Robusta]]** (Roundtail Chub) — larger-bodied, generalist cyprinid using pool and run habitats.
- **[[Catostomus Insignis]]** (Sonora Sucker) — large-bodied catostomid, benthic feeder found in deeper pools and runs.
- **[[Rhinichthys Osculus]]** (Speckled Dace) — small, benthic minnow, generalist habitat use, but comparatively rare/absent in canyon reaches in this survey.
- **[[Lepomis Cyanellus]]** (Green Sunfish) — deep-bodied, non-native centrarchid, associated with lentic pool habitat.
- **[[Ameiurus Natalis]]** (Yellow Bullhead) — non-native ictalurid catfish, benthic omnivore, tolerant of slow-moving/pooled water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Aravaipa Creek supports one of the most intact native fish assemblages remaining in the Gila River Basin, occupying a Sonoran Desert perennial stream ecosystem characterized by alternating riffle-run-pool sequences, canyon-confined bedrock reaches, and more open alluvial valley segments at its eastern and western ends.

- **Riffle specialists** — *Tiaroga cobitis* and *Meda fulgida* were most abundant in the higher-gradient canyon reaches (e.g., Hell Hole: 417 *M. fulgida*), indicating preference for well-oxygenated, swift riffle/run mesohabitats.
- **Generalist minnows** — *Agosia chrysogaster* dominated the West End sites (e.g., 390 individuals at Sycamore Tree; 377 at Jeb White), suggesting a broader habitat tolerance extending into slower, more open-valley stream reaches.
- **Benthic grazers/large-bodied catostomids** — *Pantosteus clarki* and *Catostomus insignis* were present across all sections, with *Catostomus insignis* notably abundant at West Car Ranch (89 individuals), consistent with deeper pool/run habitat availability there.
- **Rarity of Rhinichthys osculus** — Speckled dace were nearly absent from Aravaipa Canyon and West End sites (recorded as 0 at multiple stations), a notable spatial pattern possibly reflecting competitive exclusion or specific habitat requirements not met at these sites.
- **Non-native incursion** — *Lepomis cyanellus* and *Ameiurus natalis* were localized exclusively to lentic pool habitats between Horse Camp and Painted Cave Canyon, distinct from the flowing-water habitat used by the native assemblage. This spatial segregation illustrates the importance of maintaining natural flow regimes and riffle-pool heterogeneity to limit non-native fish establishment in desert stream refugia such as [[Aravaipa Creek]].

## Key Findings
- **Native fish dominance**: Six native species (*Pantosteus clarki*, *Meda fulgida*, *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Tiaroga cobitis*, *Gila robusta*, *Catostomus insignis*, and *Rhinichthys osculus*) were recorded across nearly all sites, reflecting the ecological integrity of Aravaipa Creek as critical habitat for imperiled Gila River Basin fishes.
- **Peak Spikedace abundance**: The highest *Meda fulgida* counts occurred in Aravaipa Canyon (Hell Hole: 417; East End Site 3: 331; East End Site 1: 328), highlighting the canyon reach as a stronghold for this federally protected species.
- **Longfin Dace dominance in West End**: *Agosia chrysogaster* was overwhelmingly dominant at all three West End sites (295, 377, and 390 individuals respectively), suggesting a shift in community composition from riffle-obligate cyprinids toward more flow-tolerant generalists moving westward/downstream.
- **Non-native occurrence limited and localized**: *Lepomis cyanellus* (Green Sunfish) was found only in isolated pools near Horse Camp (4 individuals), and both *L. cyanellus* and *Ameiurus natalis* (Yellow Bullhead) were also collected/preserved between Horse Camp and Painted Cave Canyon, with a single additional *A. natalis* recorded at Sycamore Tree — indicating limited but present non-native fish incursion into this otherwise native-dominated system.
- **Data quality note**: The Horse Camp site data table is largely illegible/garbled in the original OCR source (counts not recoverable), and several West Car Ranch counts (*Meda fulgida*, *Gila robusta*, *Rhinichthys osculus*) contain OCR artifacts rather than clear numeric values — flagged here as uncertain and requiring verification against original field sheets.
- **Absence of Rhinichthys osculus in multiple reaches**: Speckled dace were recorded as absent (0) at Hell Hole, Horse Camp area, Painted Cave Canyon, West Car Ranch, and Jeb White, with only a single individual recorded at Sycamore Tree — suggesting a restricted or patchy distribution within the surveyed reaches during this sampling event.
