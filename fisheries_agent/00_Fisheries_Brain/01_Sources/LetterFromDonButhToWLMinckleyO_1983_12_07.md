## Executive Summary
This document is a 1983 letter from Dr. Don Buth (UCLA Department of Biology) to Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University Department of Zoology) concerning the logistics of a grant-funded research project on the fish genus **[[Gila]]** (family [[Cyprinidae]]). Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the letter documents the administrative and material support underpinning an **electrophoretic study** of *Gila* species — including biochemical reagent procurement, specimen shipment challenges, and coordination among researchers investigating the taxonomy and population genetics of southwestern desert cyprinids.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data on *Gila* specimens are presented in this letter. The correspondence is procedural, focusing on laboratory logistics rather than descriptive anatomy. However, the reference to **electrophoresis** implies that morphological/taxonomic questions within *Gila* (e.g., species boundaries among [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Robusta]], and related taxa) were intended to be resolved or supplemented using **biochemical/genetic markers** rather than morphology alone — a common approach in 1980s ichthyological systematics when morphologically similar cyprinid species required molecular confirmation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although the letter does not describe habitat parameters directly, it implicitly situates the research within the broader context of **[[Desert Fishes|desert fish]] conservation and systematics** in the American Southwest, a region historically studied by Minckley for endemic and often imperiled freshwater cyprinids. The mention of specimen transport (via bus shipment methods, cooling requirements, and potential air freight to Burbank Airport) underscores the **logistical challenges of studying geographically isolated desert stream fish populations**, which often require specialized handling to preserve tissue integrity for downstream biochemical analysis.

## Key Findings
- **Reagent discrepancy:** An order for **Nucleoside phosphorylase (Sigma N-3003)**, priced at $11.50/mg, was under-fulfilled — only 1 of 5 requested 1-mg packages was received. Buth advises reordering the remaining four, with explicit **storage requirements: refrigerated, never frozen**, critical for maintaining enzyme viability in electrophoretic assays.
- **Specimen shipment logistics:** The first shipment of *Gila* specimens was received via **Dean Hendrickson**, with a second shipment pending. A concurrent **Los Angeles bus strike** disrupted transport, prompting consideration of **air freight to Burbank Airport** as an alternative — highlighting real-world constraints on museum/genetic specimen chain-of-custody.
- **Research coordination:** Buth requests a copy of Minckley's **proposal/research plan** to prioritize addressing "the main problem" (likely species-level systematics within *Gila*) before expanding to **comparative analyses of other species**.
- **Overlapping research efforts:** Buth notes contact from **Henry Booke** (planning a similar project) and **Bob Miller**, who was independently pursuing **electrophoretic analysis of *Gila***, indicating a broader scientific community converging on molecular systematics of this genus in the early 1980s — Buth refers Booke to Minckley to avoid duplicated effort.
- **Broader significance:** This letter is a valuable historical artifact illustrating the **methodological transition toward allozyme electrophoresis** in resolving cryptic species complexes within [[Cyprinidae]], particularly for genera like *Gila* that are notoriously difficult to classify morphologically due to phenotypic plasticity and hybridization in desert stream environments.
