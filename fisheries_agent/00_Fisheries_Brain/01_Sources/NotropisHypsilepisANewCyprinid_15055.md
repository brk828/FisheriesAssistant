# Notropis Hypsilepis: A New Cyprinid Fish from the Apalachicola River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper describes [[Notropis Hypsilepis]], a new small cyprinid minnow from the [[Apalachicola River System]] of Georgia and Alabama. The species had been overlooked or confused with [[Notropis Xaenocephalus]], which occurs in the upper [[Alabama River System]]. Its name refers to the elevated anterior lateral line scales. The authors base the description on a type series of 50 specimens from eight localities, plus 42 later-examined specimens added in an addendum. The species appears to be endemic to the Apalachicola drainage, and it seems scarce or to prefer a habitat that is rarely sampled. Its life history remains poorly known.

## Background & Description

The paper is by [[Royal D. Suttkus]] (Department of Zoology, [[Tulane University]]) and [[Edward C. Raney]] (Department of Conservation, [[Cornell University]]). It appeared in [[Tulane Studies in Zoology]], Volume 2, Number 7, dated February 25, 1955. The authors thank several collectors and Helen J. Illick, who supplied counts and lateral line pore data. Counts and measurements follow the methods of [[Hubbs and Lagler Methods]] (1947).

**Type material**
- **Holotype:** CU 17442, an adult male, 51 mm standard length. It was collected April 1, 1950, from a tributary of the [[Soque River]], Chattahoochee River system, [[Habersham County]], Georgia. Collectors were Raney, Suttkus, Richard H. Backus, and C. Richard Robins.
- **Paratypes:** 50 specimens in total, ranging from 19 to 51 mm standard length. Specimens are deposited in the [[Cornell University Fish Collection]]. The addendum adds 42 paratypes in the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]].

**Diagnosis**
The species is a small, pale-colored *Notropis* with:
- Pharyngeal teeth 2,4-4,2
- 7 anal rays (occasionally 8, per the addendum)
- 8 dorsal rays (rarely 7), 19 caudal rays, 13–16 pectoral rays (usually 14), and 8 or occasionally 9 pelvic rays
- Anterior lateral line scales elevated, with the exposed portion of the third scale about four times as high as wide
- 15 predorsal scale rows (usually; rarely 16 or 17), 35 or 36 lateral line scales, 21–26 scales around the body before the dorsal fin (usually 22 or 23), and 12 scales around the caudal peduncle
- A blunt snout, inferior mouth, and subterete body
- A weak, mostly posterior dark lateral band, a small wedge-shaped basicaudal spot, and no oblique scapular bar

**Morphometrics**
[[Table 1]] gives proportional measurements (in thousandths of standard length) for the holotype and 11 adult paratypes (seven males, four females). Measurements include body depth, head length, and fin lengths. The body is subterete, the head subtriangular, and the snout extends beyond the upper lip. The eye is shorter than the snout.

**Lateral line and cephalic pores**
The lateral line is complete. Pore counts were made on five specimens from CU 17138. Counts varied across the supratemporal, supraorbital, infraorbital, and preoperculomandibular canals, and several canals were incomplete in some specimens. The authors note that the preoperculomandibular count is high for species with 2,4-4,2 teeth and 7 or 8 anal rays.

**Coloration and sexual dimorphism**
The body is light with diagnostic dark patches at the base of the first four or five dorsal and anal rays. Males have well-developed [[Nuptial Tubercles]] on the head, the front third of the anterior lateral line scales, and the upper surfaces of the first eight pectoral rays. Small tubercles on scales and head were red in specimens collected March 26 (CU 19625). Females have small head tubercles and sensory pits but no body or fin tubercles. Males also have slightly longer pelvic fins and somewhat broader pectoral fins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution**
The species is apparently endemic to the Apalachicola River drainage. The type series comes from eight localities, and the paper lists further records from the [[Chattahoochee River System]] (Georgia and Alabama) and one collection from the upper [[Flint River System]]. Localities include:
- Grant Creek, tributary to the Soque River, near Cornelia, Habersham County
- Chestatee River at the mouth of Yahoola Creek, Lumpkin County
- Vickery Creek at Roswell, Fulton County
- Hodchodkee Creek, Stewart County
- Cane Creek, Lumpkin County
- Halawakee Creek, Lee County, Alabama
- Tributary of Lazier Creek, Talbot County
- Little Uchee Creek, Russell County, Alabama (a single specimen held by the Alabama Polytechnic Institute)

**Habitat**
The authors infer that the species prefers "Piedmont" type streams with sand bottoms. It was collected three times in small creeks 10 to 20 feet wide. It was most abundant in or near the mouths of streams where they enter large rivers. Its body shape and coloration suggest adaptation to life on or near the bottom.

**Life history**
Little is known. The large ovarian eggs and the degree of nuptial tubercle development suggest spawning in June rather than in March or April. The paper notes that the species may be scarce, or it may prefer a habitat that has not been sampled.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Notropis hypsilepis* is described as new. It is distinguished from its relatives mainly by elevated anterior lateral line scales, a light body with a weak lateral band, an inferior mouth, a blunt snout, and a small wedge-shaped basicaudal spot.
- **Closest relatives:** Among species with 2,4-4,2 teeth, it appears most closely related to [[Notropis Roseus]], [[Notropis Xaenocephalus]], and an undescribed species from the Mobile Bay drainage. It differs from species with 8 anal rays (such as *N. chalybaeus*, *N. chrosomus*, and *N. lutipinnis*) in body shape, mouth type, caudal spot, and lateral band.
- **Convergence:** Its superficial resemblance and elevated scales match [[Notropis Volucellus]], but the authors attribute this to convergence. The two differ in tooth and anal ray counts (4-4 teeth and 8 anal rays in *volucellus*).
- **Diagnostic separation:** The authors list eight pigmentation and structural characters that separate it from [[Notropis Baileyi]], [[Notropis Petersoni]], *N. xaenocephalus*, and *N. roseus*. These include the anterior base dark patch, the absence of melanophores near the anus, the weak lateral band, and the basicaudal spot shape.
- **Endemism:** The species is apparently restricted to the Apalachicola drainage, with most records from the Chattahoochee River system.
- **Addendum:** Forty-two additional specimens (UMMZ 136086, 34–48 mm, Chattahoochee River below Highway 43, east of Dahlonega, Georgia, collected May 15, 1937, by C. L. and L. C. Hubbs) were examined and designated paratypes. Their counts and measurements agree with the original description, with one exception: an anal ray count of 8 instead of 7.
- **Etymology:** The name *hypsilepis* derives from Greek *hypsi* (high) and *lepis* (scale), in reference to the elevated anterior lateral line scales.
- **Knowledge gaps:** Life history, abundance, and habitat preference remain poorly documented, and the authors suggest the species may be overlooked because it occupies habitats that are not normally sampled.
