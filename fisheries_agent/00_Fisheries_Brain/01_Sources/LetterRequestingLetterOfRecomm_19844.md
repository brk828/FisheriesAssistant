# Professional Correspondence and CV - Thomas R. Jones (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ambystomatidae #herpetology #salamander-systematics

## Executive Summary

This document comprises professional correspondence dated October 16, 1992, from Thomas R. Jones at the [[University of Michigan]] Museum of Zoology to Dr. W.L. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]], along with Jones's curriculum vitae. The letter requests letters of recommendation for job applications and provides updates on Jones's postdoctoral research status. The CV details Jones's extensive research on [[Tiger Salamander]] ([[Ambystoma tigrinum]]) systematics, molecular evolution, and biogeography in the southwestern United States, along with work on [[Seychelles Islands]] amphibians and reptiles. Jones's research focuses on developmental polymorphisms, life history variation, and phylogeographic patterns using molecular techniques including allozyme analysis and mtDNA restriction site analysis.

## Background & Description

Thomas R. Jones completed his Ph.D. in Zoology at [[Arizona State University]] in 1989 under the direction of Dr. James P. Collins and Dr. W.L. Minckley. His dissertation examined microgeographic and macrogeographic patterns of genetic variation in [[Ambystoma tigrinum]] in the southwestern United States. At the time of correspondence, Jones held an Adjunct Research Investigator position at the [[University of Michigan]] Museum of Zoology, working in the Division of Amphibians and Reptiles. His research program combined field and laboratory approaches, applying molecular techniques to address questions of evolutionary modification, phylogenetics, and biogeography.

Jones's work on [[Tiger Salamander]]s focused on populations exhibiting developmental polymorphisms, including paedomorphic (neotenic) forms and cave-dwelling populations. His research documented the systematic status of subspecies including [[Ambystoma tigrinum stebbinsi]] (Sonora/Huachuca tiger salamander), [[Ambystoma tigrinum californiense]] (California tiger salamander), [[Ambystoma tigrinum mavortium]], and [[Ambystoma tigrinum nebulosum]]. He also investigated hybrid zones between subspecies in central New Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Jones's research addressed conservation and management of genetically distinctive populations, particularly the federally threatened [[Ambystoma tigrinum stebbinsi]] in southeastern Arizona. His work with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (1988-1989) as Assistant Native Fish Biologist and collaboration with [[Arizona Nature Conservancy]] and Centro Ecologico de Sonora focused on characterizing salamander populations in threatened aquatic communities of northern México.

The research documented populations in diverse habitats including desert springs, limestone springs in the Ridge and Valley physiographic province of Alabama, lime-sink ponds, and cave systems. Cave-dwelling paedomorphic tiger salamanders were documented in central New Mexico. Jones's comparative biogeographic work extended to desert lizards ([[Dipsosaurus dorsalis]], [[Sauromalus obesus]]) and tassel-eared squirrels, examining patterns of historical differentiation in insularly distributed organisms across the southwestern United States.

Jones was offered a postdoctoral position with Terry Root in the School of Natural Resources at [[University of Michigan]] to model and predict changes in distributions of herpetofauna under climate change scenarios, beginning January 1993.

## Key Findings

**Research Program Status (October 1992):**
- Jones was completing a postdoctoral project at [[University of Michigan]] with no current funding
- Taught General Ecology at UM Biological Station, Summer 1992
- Offered postdoctoral position with Terry Root (NSF PYI and Pew award recipient) to work on climate change effects on herpetofauna distributions, beginning January 1993
- Continuing work on salamander systematics and molecular evolution

**Major Research Contributions:**
- Documented systematic status and distribution of [[Ambystoma tigrinum stebbinsi]], a federally threatened subspecies
- Analyzed hybrid zone between [[Ambystoma tigrinum mavortium]] and [[Ambystoma tigrinum nebulosum]] in central New Mexico using genetic markers
- Conducted phylogeographic analyses of southwestern herpetofauna using mitochondrial DNA variation
- Completed phylogenetic reanalysis of North American ambystomatid salamanders (accepted at Systematic Biology)
- Investigated intraspecific genetic variation and cladogenesis in [[Ambystoma tigrinum californiense]]
- Examined roles of phylogeny and stochasticity in evolution of perennibranchiate troglobitic (cave-dwelling) salamanders

**Funding and Grants:**
- Office of the Vice President for Research Grant, [[University of Michigan]] (1991-92): $6,142 for systematics, gene flow and biogeography of [[Seychelles Islands]] amphibians and reptiles
- Multiple research grants from Maytag Research Assistantship, Tucson Audubon Society, Sigma Xi (1984-1987)

**Publications:**
- 8 peer-reviewed publications (1982-1992) in journals including Journal of Evolutionary Biology, Copeia, Systematic Biology
- Multiple manuscripts in revision for submission to Evolution, American Naturalist, Copeia
- Research contract with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Endangered Species Office (1983-1986)

**Conservation Applications:**
- Provided scientific basis for conservation and management of genetically distinctive salamander populations
- Worked with conservation organizations in U.S. and México to protect threatened aquatic communities
- Conducted environmental education workshops for primary and secondary school teachers through Arizona Association for Learning in and about the Environment
