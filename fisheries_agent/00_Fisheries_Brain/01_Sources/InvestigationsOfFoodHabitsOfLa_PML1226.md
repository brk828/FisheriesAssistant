# Investigations of Food Habits of Largemouth Black Bass in Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by Patti C. Marsh, examines the diet of [[Largemouth Bass]] in [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] straddling Nevada and Arizona. Stomach samples were collected during [[Creel Census]] activities at several access sites. The study found that aquatic insects, especially [[Chironomidae]] (midges), were the major food organisms for bass in both size classes. Larger bass also consumed [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Crayfish]], and [[Salamander Larvae]]. The author notes that low sample sizes, seasonal feeding lulls, and regurgitation under stress limited the conclusions, and recommends [[Electrofishing]] for more representative sampling.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Largemouth black bass (*Micropterus salmoides*), a centrarchid sportfish.
- **Study area:** Lake Mohave, a Colorado River reservoir on the Nevada-Arizona border.
- **Motivation:** Angler harvest and stomach data were used to describe what bass eat, with the aim of informing fisheries management.
- **Size classes:** Bass were grouped into **Class I** (smaller fish) and **Class II** (larger fish). The text indicates that Class I fish were more common in the catch and that Class II fish were taken less often.
- **Sample collection:** Stomachs were removed by cutting from the esophagus to the pyloric junction and preserved in [[Formalin Preservation]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Sampling Sites and Timing

Creel census and stomach collection were carried out from January to December (the year is given as 1974 in the OCR text). Three access points were used:

| Site | State | Period noted in text |
|---|---|---|
| [[Cottonwood Cove]] | Nevada | January through May 25; later in the year after resumption |
| [[Eldorado Canyon]] | Nevada | June through September (census started here in May) |
| [[Willow Beach]] | Arizona | Used throughout the year |

Exact date ranges are partly illegible in the scanned text, so these periods should be treated as approximate.

### Fishing Pressure and Sample Availability

- At Eldorado Canyon, a large share of angling effort was directed at trout rather than bass, which reduced the number of bass stomachs available.
- Reduced facilities at Eldorado Canyon also lowered the availability of bass for sampling.
- Bass tournament catches were prominent in the sample, and the author notes that many fish from tournaments were not useful for stomach analysis. The exact effect is not fully clear from the scan.
- When census work resumed at Cottonwood Cove late in the year, few bass were landed and only the largest fish were taken.

### Seasonal Feeding

Many samples were taken in cold winter months, when the author suggests bass feed less. This is offered as a reason for the high number of empty stomachs.

## Key Findings

### Diet Composition

1. **Aquatic insects were the major food source** for both Class I and Class II bass.
2. **Chironomids** (primarily pupae, with some larvae and adults) were the most frequently observed organisms. All bass consumed them, and they predominated in both size classes.
3. **Threadfin shad** (*Dorosoma petenense*) appeared in the stomachs of the largest bass taken, indicating that piscivory was limited to larger individuals.
4. **Crayfish** occurred in 10 bass stomachs. Although not a major food source, they were used by the larger bass. Their availability was thought to depend on habitat type.
5. **Salamander larvae** ("waterdogs") were common in stomach contents. The author suggests bass take them when they are available.
6. Of the stomachs examined, **20 contained unidentifiable organic matter and material**, and **58 were empty**. The author attributes the empty stomachs mainly to cold-weather feeding reduction and to regurgitation when fish are stressed.

### Methodological Limitations

- Sample sizes were small and biased toward the largest fish, especially after tournament and seasonal effects.
- The lack of data for smaller size classes in later months limits comparisons across the size range.
- Regurgitation during capture and handling likely reduced the stomach contents recovered.

### Recommendations

- Use [[Electrofishing]] once per month to obtain a more random sample and a more representative estimate of the population.
- Electrofishing would also capture a more complete size range of bass, reducing the bias from angler-based creel sampling.
