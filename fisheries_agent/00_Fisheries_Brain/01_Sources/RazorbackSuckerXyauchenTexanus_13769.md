# Razorback Sucker Genetic Analysis Proposal - Colorado River Basin Populations

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #catostomidae #genetics #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1989 correspondence from Professor W.L. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]] to the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] outlines a proposed genetic study of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations across the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The species persists in only three areas with no successful recruitment, prompting investigation of genetic distinctiveness among populations to inform captive propagation and recovery efforts. The proposal requests funding to expand ongoing allozyme and mitochondrial DNA analyses to include all three known populations and assess potential hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]], remains in substantial numbers in only three areas of the [[Colorado River Basin]]:

1. **[[Lake Mohave]]** (Arizona-Nevada): thousands of individuals present
2. **[[Green River]]** in [[Dinosaur National Monument]] (including lowermost [[Yampa River]]): approximately 1,000 fish based on Tyus/Lanigan estimates
3. **[[Colorado River]]** near [[Walker Wildlife Area]] (Grand Junction): population size unknown but fish consistently appear in collections

All populations show evidence of natural reproduction but complete recruitment failure. Otolith examination reveals all aged individuals exceed 20+ years, with many 30+ to 40+ years old (McCarthy 1986; McCarthy and Minckley 1987; Minckley 1989).

Previous genetic work by Buth et al. (1987) using allozyme analysis assessed [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] stocks, concluding they were suitable for hatchery production and reintroduction in the lower basin. The current study expands this work using both allozymes and mitochondrial DNA.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Recruitment Failure**: None of the three populations successfully recruit despite natural reproduction occurring. The age structure indicates no young fish survive to adulthood in natural conditions.

**Captive Propagation**: Recovery efforts have initiated captive propagation in the lower [[Colorado River Basin]]. Technology for hatchery production, rearing, and reintroduction has been developed. Hatchery-produced [[Razorback Sucker]] stocked in habitats devoid of non-native fishes, and young naturally spawned in isolated habitats adjacent to [[Lake Mohave]], successfully grow to sexual maturity, providing partial support for a predation hypothesis by non-native fishes (Minckley et al. in press).

**Hybridization Concerns**: Potential hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] in the upper basin and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] in the lower basin may influence population genetics and recovery efforts.

## Key Findings

**Research Questions**:
1. Are present populations part of a single historic gene pool, or is each distinct?
2. If stocks prove distinct, are differences qualitative or quantitative, and what are the levels of difference?
3. Has hybridization influenced any population to an extent that might affect recovery efforts?

**Methodology**:
- **Allozyme studies**: Restricted to presumptive gene products determined by Buth et al. (1987) to be polymorphic, plus those representing potential introgression with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]
- **MtDNA analysis**: Extensive characterization to develop critical comparisons between populations
- **Sampling**: Non-destructive collection of unfertilized eggs from gravid females for mtDNA; fin-clips, muscle biopsies, or tissues from mortality for allozyme analysis

**Sample Requirements**:
- 30 samples per population (90 fish total for three populations)
- Minimum 30 specimens of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] for comparative purposes
- Total: 120 specimens

**Cost Estimates**:
- $50.00 to $75.00 per sample (median $62.50)
- Direct costs: $7,500.00 for 120 specimens
- Institutional indirect costs (50%): $3,750.00
- **Total project cost: $11,250.00**

**Proposed Expansions with External Funding**:
1. Obtain material from [[Colorado River]] in Colorado to include the third known population
2. Conduct broad allozyme survey rather than assaying only specific proteins
3. Expand study to other populations (e.g., [[Lake Powell]], where adults have been captured in recent years)

**Available Samples**: Green River specimens already collected in collaboration with H.M. Tyus include wild fish that died during artificial propagation attempts, hatchery-produced fish from multiple adults at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] experimental facility, and putative hybrids from nature.
