## Executive Summary

This document is the agenda and associated comment summary from the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting** held December 7–8, 1989, in Phoenix, Arizona, focused on two federally listed desert fish species: the [[Gila Topminnow]] and the [[Desert Pupfish]]. The meeting addressed status reporting (U.S. and Mexico), potential **downlisting** of the Gila Topminnow from endangered to threatened status, reintroduction strategy, genetic management of hatchery/reintroduced stocks, and the drafting of recovery plan criteria for both species. A significant portion of the record consists of team member feedback on **draft criteria for designating introduced Gila Topminnow sites as "recovery sites"** eligible to count toward reclassification.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:**
  - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — livebearing fish, family Poeciliidae, native to the Gila River basin (Arizona, USA and Sonora, Mexico). At the time of this meeting it was under consideration for **downlisting from Endangered to Threatened**.
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — family Cyprinodontidae, native to desert springs and washes of the Sonoran Desert (Arizona/California, USA and Sonora/Baja California, Mexico). A **recovery plan** for this species was in development.

- **Key contributors/status reporters named:** Brian Bagley, Dean Hendrickson, Lourdes Juarez-Romero, and Ray Bransfield — indicating a **binational (U.S.–Mexico) monitoring effort**.

- **Reintroduction stock referenced:** "**Monkey Spring stock**" — a specific genetic lineage of Gila Topminnow used extensively in reintroduction efforts, raising concerns about genetic strain management (mixing vs. separate maintenance).

- **Known threat species:** [[Gambusia affinis]] (western mosquitofish) — repeatedly flagged as a major contaminant/competitor risk to Gila Topminnow reintroduction sites, and a species easily confused with topminnow by untrained observers, complicating survey reliability.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Desert springs and streams, many of which are **human-modified or human-maintained** (e.g., water supplied via artificial means), raising fundamental questions about what constitutes a "wild" vs. "captive" population for regulatory purposes.
- **Habitat vulnerability:** Reintroduction sites are noted as vulnerable to:
  - **Desiccation** (loss of water in arid desert environment)
  - **Habitat destruction**
  - **Contamination by non-native mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*)**
- **Historic range considerations:** Determining whether a site falls within the species' historic range depends heavily on the reliability of historic written/verbal records, which is complicated by the risk of misidentifying topminnow vs. mosquitofish by non-expert observers.
- **Binational range:** Both species' ranges span the U.S.–Mexico border, requiring coordinated status assessment (Sonora, Mexico populations explicitly discussed for Gila Topminnow).
- **Policy/conservation mechanisms discussed:**
  - [[Section 7 Consultation]] (Endangered Species Act) — a "new comprehensive approach" for protecting reintroduced populations.
  - **"Put and take recovery"** — an alternative population protection/management strategy discussed as a policy option.
  - **Experimental population designations** as a tool for reintroduced stocks.

## Key Findings

### Downlisting Criteria Debate (Gila Topminnow)
Draft criteria for classifying an introduced site as a valid "recovery site" (counting toward reclassification to Threatened status) included:
1. Is it captive or wild?
2. Is it within historic range?
3. Has it survived at least 3 years?
4. Is it successful?

**Team member critiques of these criteria:**
- The **most pertinent question** should be whether the site/population can survive **without continued human intervention** — not whether the habitat is human-created, its purpose, or location.
- Any population whose **water supply is provided by human intervention** should be classified as **captive** (proposed as an added qualifier to Criterion 3).
- Site vulnerability to **desiccation, destruction, or *Gambusia* contamination** should factor into the evaluation.
- Strictly counting all human-modified habitats as "captive" would **eliminate virtually all existing reintroduction sites** — seen as an overly restrictive outcome.
- **Reliable historic records** must account for the likelihood that on-site observers could reliably distinguish topminnow from mosquitofish.
- Presence of **Gambusia affinis** at a reintroduction site should automatically result in a judgment of "unsuccessful."
- **Population size** should be incorporated into success determinations.
- **Three years was judged insufficient** by team members to reliably determine reintroduction success.
- Criteria should account for whether **supplemental stocking** occurred during the 3-year evaluation window (this could artificially inflate apparent success).

### General Consensus / Concerns
- Applying the proposed criteria strictly would **exclude approximately two-thirds of all reintroduction sites** from counting toward downlisting — indicating the draft criteria were considered too stringent by the team.
- Consensus that the **recovery plan's downlisting criteria required substantial reevaluation** before finalization.

### Management Issues Raised for Discussion (Unresolved at Time of Agenda)
- Whether to proceed with **immediate downlisting** of Gila Topminnow.
- How to handle **genetic strain management** of Gila Topminnow (mixing vs. maintaining separate lineages, especially the widely-used Monkey Spring stock).
- Whether **Desert Pupfish** should be stocked into sites that already contain Gila Topminnow (pros/cons debated, unresolved).
- How to appropriately **count and legally protect** existing reintroduced populations under the Endangered Species Act framework.
