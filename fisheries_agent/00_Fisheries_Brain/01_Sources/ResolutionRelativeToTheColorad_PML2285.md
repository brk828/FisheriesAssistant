# Desert Fishes Council Resolution 78-1: Colorado Squawfish Recovery and Critical Habitat Flows

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ptychocheilus

## Executive Summary

This is a resolution adopted by the [[Desert Fishes Council]] at its Tenth Annual Symposium in Las Cruces, New Mexico, on November 17, 1978, and dated February 1979. It responds to a request from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] for comment on the [[Colorado Squawfish]] [[Recovery Plan]] and the proposed designation of [[Critical Habitat]] in several river reaches. The Council recommends that no upstream developments be built that would reduce flows in the designated critical habitat until the required flow levels are determined. The resolution passed without a dissenting vote and was sent to a broad list of federal, state, and regional agencies.

## Background & Description

The document identifies the Colorado squawfish as a species "in serious danger of extinction." The resolution is grounded in the [[Endangered Species Act]], whose intent it cites in support of preserving native fish and the ecosystems they depend on.

The Desert Fishes Council describes itself as an organization of more than 100 persons. Its membership includes federal, state, and university scientists, resource specialists, members of private conservation organizations, and individuals concerned with long-term environmental values. The organization has nationwide and international representation.

The resolution is signed by Chairman Sanchez and issued from the Council's office at P.O. Box 275, Death Valley, California.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The resolution states that the Colorado squawfish cannot be preserved while its environment continues to be altered. It therefore treats river flow as the central threat to the species' survival. The proposed [[Critical Habitat]] includes reaches of the following rivers:

- [[Colorado River]]
- [[Green River]]
- [[Yampa River]]
- [[Gunnison River]]

Because the critical habitat is defined by river reaches, the resolution's main concern is maintaining adequate streamflow within these sections. The document does not provide specific flow volumes, population counts, or habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

- **Conservation status:** The Colorado squawfish is in serious danger of extinction. The Council says its survival is incompatible with continued alteration of its environment.
- **Central recommendation:** No upstream developments should be built if they would decrease flows in the critical habitat reaches. This restriction stays in place until the [[Flow Requirements]] for the species are determined.
- **Policy basis:** The position rests on the [[Endangered Species Act]] and on a commitment to preserve native aquatic ecosystems for future generations.
- **Scope of review:** The Council is responding to the [[Recovery Plan]] and the proposed critical habitat designation. The resolution itself does not analyze the plan's technical details.
- **Vote and distribution:** The resolution passed without a dissenting vote. Copies went to:
  - the Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and its regional offices (the OCR text is ambiguous about the region numbers, but they appear to be Regions II and VI);
  - the governors of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming;
  - the leader of the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]];
  - the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] offices in Boulder City, Nevada, and Salt Lake City, Utah;
  - the Secretary of the Interior, the Environmental Protection Agency, the Army Corps of Engineers, and the Commissioner of Reclamation;
  - the Superintendent of [[Dinosaur National Monument]].
