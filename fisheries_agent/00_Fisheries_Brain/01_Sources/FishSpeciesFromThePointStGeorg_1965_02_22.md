# Fish Species from the Point St. George Site (DNo-11)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #zooarchaeology #Scorpaenidae #Embiotocidae #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes an ichthyological analysis of fish bone remains recovered from the DNo-11 archaeological site at [[Point St. George]], California, conducted by Dr. W. I. Follett of the California Academy of Sciences. The assemblage comprises **seventeen species**, representing **twelve genera** and **nine families**, reflecting a mixed subsistence strategy exploiting nearshore, open-water, estuarine, and freshwater fish resources. The data support inferences about prehistoric [[Tolowa]] fishing technology, watercraft use, and a chronological shift in the intensity of deep-sea fishing between two site occupation phases, [[Point St. George I]] and [[Point St. George II]].

## Background & Description

Species were identified primarily from skeletal fragments (vertebrae, otoliths, and other diagnostic bone elements), a standard approach in zooarchaeological faunal analysis rather than morphometric study of live specimens. Taxa represented in the assemblage include:

**Requiem Sharks (Carcharhinidae)**
- [[Soupfin Shark]] (*Galeorhinus zyopterus*)

**Sturgeons (Acipenseridae)**
- [[Green Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser medirostris*)

**Trouts/Salmonids (Salmonidae)**
- [[Chinook Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*)
- [[Coho Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*)

**Cods (Gadidae)**
- [[Pacific Hake]] (*Merluccius productus*)

**Righteye Flounders (Pleuronectidae)**
- [[Pacific Halibut]] (*Hippoglossus stenolepis*)

**Surfperches (Embiotocidae)**
- [[Redtail Surfperch]] (*Holconotus rhodoterus*)
- [[Striped Seaperch]] (*Taeniotoca lateralis*)

**Rockfishes (Scorpaenidae)**
- [[Bocaccio]] (*Sebastodes paucispinis*)
- [[Yellowtail Rockfish]] (*Sebastodes flavidus*)
- [[Black Rockfish]] (*Sebastodes melanops*)
- [[Vermilion Rockfish]] (*Sebastodes miniatus*)
- [[Turkey-red Rockfish]] (*Sebastodes ruberrimus*)

**Greenlings (Hexagrammidae)**
- [[Lingcod]] (*Ophiodon elongatus*)
- [[Kelp Greenling]] (*Hexagrammos decagrammus*)

**Sculpins (Cottidae)**
- [[Cabezon]] (*Scorpaenichthys marmoratus*)
- [[Red Irish Lord]] (*Hemilepidotus hemilepidotus*)

No detailed osteometric or morphometric data (size ranges, body proportions) are reported in this preliminary letter; taxonomic identification was the primary focus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The assemblage reflects exploitation of multiple distinct habitat zones along the Northern California coast near [[Point St. George]]:

- **Deep rocky reef habitat (30+ fathoms):** The [[Turkey-red Rockfish]] and [[Vermilion Rockfish]] are strongly associated with deep water over rocky bottom, consistent with conditions at [[Northwest Seal Rock]] (St. George Reef), roughly 6.5 miles offshore — indicating open-water, boat-based fishing.
- **Open-water/pelagic and offshore benthic zones:** [[Chinook Salmon]], [[Pacific Hake]], and [[Pacific Halibut]] were likely boat-caught, along with larger individuals of [[Soupfin Shark]], [[Bocaccio]], [[Yellowtail Rockfish]], [[Lingcod]], and [[Cabezon]].
- **Nearshore/intertidal rocky habitat:** [[Black Rockfish]] and [[Cabezon]] are habitat generalists catchable from boat or shore.
- **Shoreline/surf zone:** [[Redtail Surfperch]], [[Striped Seaperch]], [[Kelp Greenling]], and [[Red Irish Lord]] are consistent with shore-based angling.
- **Estuarine/freshwater habitat:** The [[Green Sturgeon]] likely entered [[Lake Earl]] during periods when the sandbar was breached, where it could be taken by hook-and-line or spear. The [[Coho Salmon]] may have been caught in Lake Earl, its tributary [[Jordan Creek]], or [[Elk Creek]] near Crescent City.

This spread of habitats indicates a fishing economy integrating shore-based, estuarine, and deep-sea/boat-based strategies.

## Key Findings

- **Species dominance:** The most numerous remains recovered were [[Turkey-red Rockfish]], [[Black Rockfish]], [[Cabezon]], [[Pacific Halibut]], and [[Vermilion Rockfish]].
- **Evidence of deep-sea fishing capability:** The presence of [[Turkey-red Rockfish]] and [[Vermilion Rockfish]], both deep rocky-reef species, implies the occupants possessed the technology and watercraft necessary to fish far offshore.
- **Chronological/stratigraphic contrast:** Only **6 identifiable fish fragments** (limited to [[Black Rockfish]] and [[Turkey-red Rockfish]]) were recovered from [[Point St. George I]] (F36) levels, compared to **172 fragments** from [[Point St. George II]] (F31) levels — suggesting deep-sea fishing began in Point St. George I times but became substantially more important during the Point St. George II occupation.
- **Technological correlates:** The Point St. George II occupation layers contain stone net sinkers, fishhooks, and bone gorges, corroborating an intensified fishing technology consistent with the faunal evidence.
- **Watercraft inference:** The exploitation of deep offshore rocky-reef species implies construction and use of seaworthy boats. Ethnographic informant testimony corroborates that the historic [[Tolowa]] people used large (30–40 foot) dugout canoes for regular trips to the Seal Rocks, suggesting continuity of this practice into pre-contact times.
- **Preliminary status:** The analysis is explicitly noted by Dr. Follett as preliminary, with further research and a full tabulation by stratigraphic level on file with the University of California Archaeological Survey, Berkeley.
