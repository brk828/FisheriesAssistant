# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program: Management Group and Section 7 Records (1989–1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a compilation of administrative records from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]], a multi-agency effort to recover endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It contains meeting notices, agendas, a meeting summary, a Technical Group minutes record, a Section 7 consultation schedule, a draft recovery management plan outline and glossary, a propagation strategy matrix, and a table of water-depletion consultations. Scientific data are limited; the most concrete material concerns the status of [[Razorback Sucker]] populations, the planned [[Propagation Strategy]], and the regulatory consultations required for water development. Dates in the source run from late 1989 to early 1990.

## Background & Description

The records are issued by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), Region 6, with input from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), state agencies (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming), water developers, and environmental groups. The program is governed by a [[Management Group]] that oversees a [[Technical Group]] and an [[Implementation Committee]].

Key documents included:

- **Management Group meeting notice** (memo of 8 January 1990, from program director John Hamill): the February 5, 1990 meeting was rescheduled because of a conflict with the annual Colorado River Endangered Fishes Researchers Meeting in Moab, Utah (February 7–8, 1990).
- **Agendas** for the February 5, 1990 Management Group meeting and the February 21, 1990 Implementation Committee meeting. Topics include FY-89 accomplishments, FY-90/91 work plans, [[Water Rights Acquisition]], [[Section 7 Consultation]], sediment, and nonnative fishes.
- **Management Group meeting summary** (draft). The header gives November 7, 1989; the attachment list and agenda give November 9, 1989. Both dates appear in the source.
- **Technical Group minutes** (November 8, 1989), finalized and transmitted December 27, 1989.
- **Flaming Gorge Operation consultation schedule** (memo of 19 December 1989), with milestones from December 15, 1989 to June 1, 1990.
- **Draft Recovery Management Plan**: glossary, outline, and conceptual schematic (Figure 1).
- **Rare Fishes Propagation Strategy** matrix.
- **Section 7 consultation table** for water depletions in the Upper Colorado River drainage (dated November 1, 1989).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The target fishes are rare, endangered species of the Upper Colorado River Basin. The source names [[Razorback Sucker]], squawfish, and rainbow smelt. The smelt is discussed only as a proposed introduction into [[Lake Powell]], which the program opposed or was consulting on; the text does not state the outcome.

The [[Recovery Management Plan]] uses the following framework:

- **Management units**: river sections defined by genetic distinctness, home range, population status, hydrology, and common management issues. Each unit has its own goals and controlling variables.
- **Controlling variables**: physical habitat (temperature, sediment, flow magnitude/duration/timing/frequency, passage), water quality, species interactions (hybridization, predation, competition), food, and other factors (angling, sampling, public opinion).
- **Limiting factors**: controlling variables that are actively constraining populations in a given place and time.
- **Recovery elements**: habitat management and flow regulation, habitat development, stocking, nonnative species and sportfishing, and research and data management.
- **Monitoring and evaluation**: population indices tracked for each life-history phase, compared with past data and unit goals.

The [[Yampa River]] is a major focus. Its existing flow regime is characterized by high peak spring flows and low stable flows for the rest of the year. The FWS interim Phase II report recommended maintaining this regime. Hamill stated that the report did not necessarily mean jeopardy opinions would be issued on Yampa Basin water projects.

The propagation matrix rates each historic habitat (Green, Colorado, Gunnison, Dolores, San Juan basins and tributaries) by population status:

- **S**: natural population sustaining
- **R**: relict population
- **U**: unknown status
- **A**: adequate recruitment
- **I**: inadequate recruitment
- **O**: occasional capture
- **X**: not present (fewer than 2 captures per year)

## Key Findings

**Program administration and budget**
- FWS FY-90 Recovery Program funding may be reduced by 5–10 percent to account for Gramm-Rudman cuts, the President's drug program, and cost-of-living increases.
- Technical Group members proposed establishing core funding for some programs and agreed in concept to multiyear project funding. Projects that are on track and producing results would continue to be funded.
- Angela Kantola was hired as Assistant Program Director. She previously worked as an endangered species recovery coordinator in Atlanta.
- An I&E baseline and monitoring proposal requesting about $10,000 was not funded. The group agreed to reconsider it if money became available.
- The Management Group decided that rapid emergency response (e.g., oil spills, fish kills) is an agency responsibility, not a Recovery Program responsibility.

**Species status and management**
- A razorback sucker listing package was prepared for internal FWS review. The FWS has until March 1990 to decide whether to list the species.
- A draft razorback management plan was under review. A revised draft was due February 1, 1990, and recommendations for holding razorbacks in FY-90 were to follow.
- Habitat development work was broadened by Ed Wick to cover the entire basin, with a focus on squawfish.

**Water rights and Yampa River**
- Water rights acquisition subgroup recommendations were due for review by November 17, 1989.
- The Nature Conservancy was contracting for an appraisal of Cross Mountain Ranch and still evaluating Hinman Park rights.
- Senior Roberts Ditch rights near Maybell, on the Yampa River, were reportedly for sale; the owner had not yet responded to a Colorado Water Conservation Board information request.
- Ruedi Reservoir release issues through the 15-mile reach were resolved, and an agreement among the FWS, BR, and CWCB was being finalized.

**Section 7 consultations**
- The Muddy Creek Biological Opinion was revised to judge "sufficient progress" across all five recovery elements, not just instream flow protection. The revision was acceptable to the State, FWS, and River District and was to be finalized soon.
- The Sandstone Biological Opinion was scheduled for finalization by March 31, 1990. Open issues were reservoir operation until its water was marketed and additional conservation measures to offset impacts.
- The [[Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion]] followed a schedule: consolidated biology and hydrology reports were due December 15, 1989; review ran through January 15, 1990; a team meeting was held January 16–17; reports were transmitted to the Recovery Implementation Committee February 1, 1990; and the draft opinion was due to the Bureau by June 1, 1990. The integrated technical report serving as the biological assessment was dropped.

**Section 7 water depletion table (dated November 1, 1989)**
- The table lists 33 consultations since the program began in January 1988: 27 with finalized opinions and 6 pending.
- Pending consultations include Muddy Creek (7,716 acre-feet depletion, $80,324 fund estimate, draft issued October 16, 1989), Sandstone Reservoir (30,600 acre-feet, $318,546), Price-San Rafael (22,951 acre-feet, $238,910, BR lead), Flaming Gorge (BR, consultation due March 15, 1990), Blue Flame Mine (1 acre-foot), and Bridger/Teton Forest Plan (depletion to be determined).
- Most finalized depletions are small (typically 2–400 acre-feet). Depletion fund contributions were calculated at $10 per acre-foot; some BR projects were exempt.

**Research and planning**
- Researchers asked the Technical Group for formal status for research recommendations, core funding, exemption of multiyear projects from repeated review, and flexibility to respond to emergencies.
- FWS was asked to produce research guidelines (road maps), including FY-91 Management Activities Guidance, by February 1, 1990.
- A sediment discussion paper was distributed for comment (requested by December 1, 1990 in the minutes, a date that appears to be a typo for 1989).
- Lee Mills was asked to evaluate Technical Group organization and identify core programs and costs.
