# Fresh-water Fishes of Baja California: Their Origins and Affinities

#ichthyology #marine-biology #zoogeography #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This symposium paper by W. L. Follett reviews the indigenous fishes recorded from fresh water in [[Baja California]]. It reports 29 species in 25 genera and 17 families. Nearly all are of marine affinity, and the only endemic species is [[Fundulus Lima]]. The author concludes that Baja California lacks a discrete freshwater fish fauna. Its fresh-water fishes are dispersals from five faunal areas, and most are marine forms that tolerate fresh water to varying degrees. The paper also evaluates each species' salt tolerance using the [[Myers Salt Tolerance Classification]] and separates confirmed records from doubtful, erroneous, and introduced ones.

## Background & Description

The paper begins by explaining why Baja California has no true freshwater fauna. Four factors are identified:

1. **Coastal discontinuity:** A break between southern California coastal streams and the [[Tijuana River]] stopped the [[Santa Ana System]] fauna from extending south of the [[San Luis Rey River]]. This suggests the systems were not connected during [[Pleistocene Sea-Level Lowering]].
2. **Aridity:** Long-standing dryness has prevented coastwise dispersal of freshwater fishes through estuaries.
3. **Escarpment:** An abrupt escarpment along the eastern shore nearly precludes lagoons that might have aided dispersal.
4. **Gulf barrier:** The [[Gulf of California]] blocks entry of freshwater fishes from Middle America.

Apart from a few strictly freshwater species of the lower [[Colorado River]], the freshwater fishes are marine derivatives. The Colorado River species formed a depauperate but highly specialized fauna until they were extirpated by people.

The paper groups fish origins into five faunal sources:

- **Northeastern Pacific:** three euryhaline species dispersed southward, including [[Salmo Gairdneri]] and [[Leptocottus Armatus]].
- **Lower Colorado River:** three species dispersed downstream, including [[Xyrauchen Texanus]], [[Gila Elegans]], and [[Ptychocheilus Lucius]].
- **Atlantic seaboard:** three species dispersed westward, including [[Gambusia Affinis]] and [[Cyprinodon Macularius]].
- **Gulf of California:** three species that occasionally enter fresh water in Colorado River distributaries.
- **[[Panamic Faunal Region]]:** 12 species dispersed northwestward, five of which now extend beyond Baja California.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Follett describes the Baja California freshwater fauna as "essentially peripheral." Several genera reach their range limits in the region. [[Holarctic Distribution|Holarctic]] genera such as *Salmo* and the family Gasterosteidae reach their southern limits near Arroyo del Rosario. The northeastern Pacific genus *Leptocottus* reaches its southernmost freshwater limit at Arroyo San Miguel. Panamic families such as Eleotridae and Gobiidae reach their northwestern limits near Cabo San Lucas.

Several species were reported from the Salton Sea and the Colorado River. Salinity and water chemistry also vary widely across these habitats. For example, a spring at Pozo del Tule was described as having a strong vertical temperature gradient. This spring was later cooled and is now populated by fishes dispersed from the Colorado River.

The [[Myers Salt Tolerance Classification]] (Myers 1951) defines six categories of freshwater status:

- **Primary:** strictly intolerant of salt water.
- **Secondary:** mostly confined to fresh water but able to cross narrow sea barriers.
- **Diadromous:** regularly migrates between fresh and salt water.
- **Vicarious:** presumably nondiadromous freshwater representatives of marine groups.
- **Complementary:** marine-group representatives that dominate fresh water where competition is absent.
- **Sporadic:** species that enter fresh water only occasionally, not as part of their life cycle.

Examples include [[Mugil Cephalus]] (sporadic with a strong tendency toward diadromy), [[Cynoscion Macdonaldi]] (sporadic with a tendency toward diadromy), and [[Fundulus Parvipinnis]] (sporadic).

## Key Findings

- **Faunal total:** 29 species from 25 genera and 17 families have been recorded from fresh water in Baja California. All but a few genera, including [[Ptychocheilus Lucius]] and [[Gila Elegans]], have marine affinities.
- **Endemism:** [[Fundulus Lima]] is the only endemic freshwater species. Myers (1930) suggested it may be a landlocked derivative of [[Fundulus Parvipinnis]].
- **No discrete fauna:** The absence of a discrete freshwater assemblage is attributed to the four historical and geographic barriers described above.
- **Salmonid records:** [[Salmo Gairdneri]] records in Baja California include Arroyo San Antonio, at about 2,000 feet elevation. Trout in Arroyo San Jacinto, Arroyo San Rafael, and Boca de la Sierra are considered human introductions with no zoogeographic significance. Under primitive conditions, oceanic migrants probably ascended the [[Tijuana River]] into Baja California.
- **Elopidae:** [[Elops Affinis]] enters fresh water only sporadically. Its records include the Salton Sea and the Colorado River.
- **Catostomidae:** [[Xyrauchen Texanus]] is a Colorado River species. Its Baja California record is doubtful, with some reports based on earlier records.
- **Mugilidae:** [[Mugil Cephalus]] juveniles move up the [[Colorado River]] in large numbers and often grow to yearling size before returning to the Gulf. In the Salton Sea, it became very abundant and grew large but did not reproduce.
- **Sciaenidae:** [[Cynoscion Macdonaldi]] (totoaba) is considered sporadic with a tendency toward diadromy. Its spawning migration is thought to take it only into brackish waters.
- **Gobiidae:** [[Gillichthys Mirabilis]] is essentially a Californian species with a disjunct occurrence in the Gulf. Its presence at the Salton Sea is presumed to be a human introduction.
- **Clupeidae:** Records from the Mulegé River mouth were reported without confirming fresh water, so they are classed as possibly occurring in fresh water.
- **Erroneous records:** Several species previously reported from Baja California are considered erroneous, including [[Fundulus Ulcinus]] and [[Fundulus Ulcinus]]-related records from Cabo San Lucas, which were based on mixed Atlantic material.
- **Introduced species:** At least 10 species are established in Baja California waters through human introduction. These include [[Cyprinus Carpio]], [[Gambusia Affinis]], [[Ictalurus Punctatus]], [[Micropterus Salmoides]], and several species of Centrarchidae.
