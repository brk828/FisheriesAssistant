# Draft Biological Opinion on the Operation of Glen Canyon Dam under the Modified Low Fluctuating Flow Alternative

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) draft biological opinion (2-21-93-F-167, dated October 13, 1993) evaluates the effects of operating [[Glen Canyon Dam]] under the [[Modified Low Fluctuating Flow Alternative]] (MLFF), the preferred alternative of the Draft Environmental Impact Statement, on five federally listed species. The Service concludes that the MLFF is likely to jeopardize the continued existence of the [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], but not likely to jeopardize the [[Bald Eagle]], [[Kanab Ambersnail]], or [[Peregrine Falcon]]. A [[Reasonable and Prudent Alternative]] with seven elements is proposed, along with incidental take limits, reasonable and prudent measures, terms and conditions, and conservation recommendations. The document also includes an appendix of research hypotheses prepared under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program.

## Background & Description

- **Dam and authority:** Glen Canyon Dam was completed in 1963 as part of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]]. A 1978 biological opinion found past, present and future operations jeopardized the humpback chub.
- **Consultation request:** Reclamation submitted a formal section 7 request on February 5, 1993 (received February 8). The 90-day consultation period was extended to coincide with the final EIS.
- **Operating history:** From 1963 to 1980 the dam operated under filling criteria. Since 1980 the objective has been maximum firm capacity and energy within legislative mandates. Minimum flows were 1,000 cfs (Labor Day to Easter) and 3,000 cfs (Easter to Labor Day); maximum flow was 31,500 cfs. Daily fluctuations exceeded 12,000 cfs on more than 58% of days and 20,000 cfs on 15% of days.
- **Interim Flows:** Established August 1991 and still in operation; the MLFF is similar to them but drops the financial exception criteria and adds habitat maintenance flows and increased flood protection.
- **Species considered:** Bald eagle, humpback chub, Kanab ambersnail, peregrine falcon and razorback sucker. The [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] was proposed later and will be addressed separately. Critical habitat was proposed for the humpback chub and razorback sucker on January 29, 1993, and will be addressed in a separate conference report.
- **Species accounts:**
  - Humpback chub: canyon-bound, swift-water species; aged to 22 years; Grand Canyon population relies on the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) for spawning and rearing; LCR population estimates ranged widely.
  - Razorback sucker: listed as endangered October 23, 1991; now occupies about 25% of its former range; reproduction in natural rivers is virtually nonexistent; largest population is in Lake Mohave (estimated at approximately 60,000); aged 24 to 44 years.
  - Bald eagle: winter concentration at Nankoweap Creek (RM 52) feeding on spawning trout; 26 birds counted in one day in February 1990.
  - Peregrine falcon: Grand Canyon National Park supports the largest known breeding population of the species in the contiguous United States; 71 breeding areas identified 1988 to 1990; an estimated 96 pairs in the project area.
  - Kanab ambersnail: known from three locations, with the Grand Canyon population the only one in a natural setting; restricted to a spring-fed wetted area approximately 50 m by 30 m.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Basin fish fauna:** The Colorado River Basin fish fauna includes 32 species, 75% of which are endemic. Historically the big rivers were dominated by native minnows and suckers. The four "big river" endangered fishes (bonytail chub, Colorado squawfish, humpback chub, razorback sucker) all occurred in Grand Canyon before and shortly after dam construction.
- **Native fish decline:** Of the eight native fish species in Grand Canyon, three are extirpated (bonytail chub, Colorado squawfish, roundtail chub) and only four have reproducing populations (bluehead sucker, humpback chub, flannelmouth sucker, speckled dace). The humpback chub range has contracted; last collected above Lee's Ferry in 1967, at RM 17.8 in 1980, and at RM 29.9 in 1993.
- **Dam effects:** Glen Canyon Dam acts as a barrier to fish movement and sediment transport. Cold hypolimnetic releases keep Lee's Ferry at 7 to 12 °C and temperatures about 400 km downstream rarely exceed 16 °C, versus pre-dam July-August peaks of 25 to 29 °C.
- **Water budget:** The Upper Basin must deliver at least 8.23 maf to the Lower Basin (7.5 maf plus 0.73 maf Mexican treaty obligation). Upper Basin depletions were 3.8 maf in 1992 and are scheduled to reach 4.9 maf by 2020 and 5.4 maf by 2040.
- **Sediment:** Sediment from Lee's Ferry to the LCR is expected to continue eroding and armoring. The probability of a net gain in riverbed sand after 50 years is 73% under the MLFF versus 41% under the No Action Alternative.
- **Non-native fishes:** Brown trout remain in sizable numbers, mostly in Bright Angel Creek; brook trout are rare. Rainbow trout stocked at Lee's Ferry move downstream only in limited numbers. Of nine tributaries sampled by Angradi et al. (1992), seven were dominated by natives. Maddux et al. (1987) found native fish comprised 79% of catches in 11 tributaries.
- **Cumulative concerns:** Increased groundwater pumping of the aquifer feeding LCR perennial flow is the main non-Federal threat identified. Catastrophic spills (e.g., at the LCR bridge at Cameron) or chronic pollutant releases pose risks to the humpback chub.

## Key Findings

- **Jeopardy conclusion:** The MLFF is likely to jeopardize the humpback chub and razorback sucker. It is not likely to jeopardize the bald eagle, Kanab ambersnail or peregrine falcon.
- **Primary adverse effects:** Altered temperature, flow regime and possibly sediment load. Cold mainstem water prevents successful mainstem spawning of humpback chub; spawning is restricted to tributaries.
- **Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (seven elements):**
  1. Experimental flows (high steady spring flows; low steady summer and fall flows) during low water years (about 8.23 maf), run for roughly five years; moderate and high release years operate under the MLFF (expected in 50% of years).
  2. Development of a management plan for the LCR; draft due within two years.
  3. A research program on temperature, tributary hydrology, mainstem hydrology, biotic interactions, life history, food resources, and parasites and disease.
  4. Razorback sucker management plan, starting with a workshop within one year.
  5. Establishment of a second humpback chub spawning aggregation downstream of Glen Canyon Dam.
  6. Feasibility study of selective withdrawal (multi-level intake structure), with a warning that it is not the only action needed.
  7. An adaptive management program.
- **Habitat maintenance flows:** High, steady releases of 7 to 14 days in March or April, up to about 30,000 cfs; occur in years when projected Lake Powell storage is below 19 maf on January 1.
- **Beach and habitat building flows:** 40,000 to 45,000 cfs; expected 1 in 5 years when Lake Powell is low (60% of the time), or 1 in 8 years (12%) across all years.
- **Flood frequency:** Floods above 45,000 cfs limited to no more than 1 in 100 years under the MLFF; the No Action Alternative rate is 1 in 40 years.
- **Incidental take:** Expected for humpback chub (young transported downstream into unfavorable habitats) and Kanab ambersnail (losses from flood releases). No take is authorized for the bald eagle, peregrine falcon or razorback sucker. Take levels are difficult to detect, so biological criteria are to be developed.
- **Terms and conditions:** Young-of-year and juvenile humpback chub surveys before and after flows, reports due within 60 days; Kanab ambersnail surveys before and after any flood greater than 40,000 cfs, with follow-up surveys at 30 days and six months.
- **Conservation recommendations:** Operate under the Seasonally Adjusted Steady Flow (SASF) alternative; monitor peregrine falcon and bald eagle use; investigate Kanab ambersnail life cycle and census its population.
- **Pre-dam hydrograph:** Average pre-dam June flows were 53,900 cfs, compared with a 30,000 cfs peak and 18,000 cfs for two months under SASF.
- **Razorback sucker recruitment:** The draft states that riverine recruitment is unsuccessful; larvae in Lake Mohave have not been found greater than about 12 mm.
- **Reviewer comments (Gordon Mueller, National Biological Survey, January 1994):** The reviewer argues the opinion does not adequately address biological and ecological factors, particularly non-native predation and competition. Points raised include four juvenile razorback suckers collected downstream of Hoover Dam, the risk of exotic fish colonization from warmer releases or surface withdrawals (illustrated by an entrainment estimate of about 7 million fish per day at a 30,000 cfs release, derived from Lake Havasu data), and the suggestion that the opinion does not consider whether dam removal would change species status. The reviewer also questions the stated pre-dam spawning context and recommends a study of warm water releases at Hoover Dam.
