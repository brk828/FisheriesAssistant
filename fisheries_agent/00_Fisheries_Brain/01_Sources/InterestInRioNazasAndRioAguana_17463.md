# Correspondence Regarding Rio Nazas and Rio Aguanaval Biogeography

#ichthyology #marine-biology #historical-biogeography #Mexican-aquatic-systems

## Executive Summary

Personal correspondence dated March 30, 1992, from Dr. Roger Conant (University of New Mexico, retired Director of Philadelphia Zoo) to Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) requesting information on geological history and aquatic fauna of the [[Rio Nazas]] and [[Rio Aguanaval]] in western Mexico. The letter discusses biogeographic connections between these rivers and the [[Rio Grande]], and inquires about the origin of springs in the [[Cuatro Cienegas]] bolson.

## Background & Description

Dr. Conant retired as Director of the Philadelphia Zoo in 1973 and had been working on book publications. Due to age and health issues (severe balance problems associated with deteriorating hearing), his field work had ended by 1992. He maintained research interest in Mexican river systems, having published relevant work in 1963. The correspondence was facilitated through Steve Platania, who provided contact information and shared recent research contributions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Rio Nazas]] and [[Rio Aguanaval]] rise in western Mexico and historically flowed into arid bolsons (closed drainage basins) on the plateau. The aquatic and semiaquatic faunas of these rivers are closely allied to species in the [[Rio Grande]], suggesting historical hydrological connections or shared biogeographic origins. The [[Cuatro Cienegas]] bolson contains springs of uncertain origin, representing another element of the region's complex hydrogeology and biogeography.

## Key Findings

- Dr. Conant sought updated information on geological history of [[Rio Nazas]] and [[Rio Aguanaval]] published since the early 1960s
- Historical question posed: where did these rivers once flow before entering closed bolsons?
- Biogeographic observation: aquatic and semiaquatic faunas of these Mexican rivers show close affinities to [[Rio Grande]] species
- Research question regarding spring origins: what is the source of waters supplying springs in the [[Cuatro Cienegas]] bolson?
- Dr. Conant referenced his 1963 publication containing speculations on the geological and biogeographic history of these systems
- The correspondence indicates ongoing scientific interest in Mexican aquatic systems and their historical connections to southwestern U.S. drainages
