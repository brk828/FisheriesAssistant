# Biological Opinion on U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Stocking of Rainbow Trout and Channel Catfish into the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This formal Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (1973) evaluates the U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) program to stock hatchery-raised catchable [[Rainbow Trout]] (eight to ten inches) and [[Channel Catfish]] into reservoirs and river reaches of the Lower Colorado River to enhance recreational sportfishing. The species of concern are the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]]. Designated critical habitat for both species in Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu was also considered.

The Service concluded that the continued rainbow trout stocking program is not likely to jeopardize either species or adversely modify critical habitat. The channel catfish stocking in waters with no direct connection to the river is also not likely to jeopardize these fishes. The opinion anticipates incidental take of razorback sucker and bonytail chub larvae by stocked trout, which cannot be quantified, and attaches reasonable and prudent measures, terms and conditions, and a modified stocking program with recovery elements for Lake Mohave.

## Background & Description

**Consultation history.** The Service's Fisheries Resources program in Region 2 is the action agency. Stocked fish are raised at the Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery, with channel catfish obtained from Texas hatcheries. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD) provides technical management assistance. Consultation was initiated before 1980 but was not completed. In 1994 a separate consultation was deemed appropriate, based on a biological evaluation (BE) prepared by the AZGFD and transmitted to the Arizona Ecological Services State Office (AESO). The consultation request was received March 9, 1994, and the 90-day period was tracked from that date.

**Proposed action.** The action is the continuation of rainbow trout and channel catfish stocking, which the Service carries out as part of its trust responsibility for Native Americans and state recreational goals. Stocking locations are spread across the Lower Colorado River reservoirs, Native American reservations and the Lake Mohave area. Assumptions in the opinion include that the numbers of fish and stocking dates in the 1994 BE reflect the probable program direction for the next few years.

**Rainbow trout.** The Service proposed to stock about 550,500 catchable rainbow trout across:
- Lake Mohave, below Davis Dam and at Needles, California
- Four Colorado River Indian Reservation sites (Deer Island Lake, Mohave Lake, Rock Drop, Moovalya Pond)
- Two Fort Yuma Indian Reservation canals (Alamo and Foreboy)

The Lake Mohave program was divided into seven stocking areas in three zones. Stocking below Davis Dam totaled 146,000 fish, and 101,600 fish were proposed for Areas 3 to 5. Areas 6 and 7 received about 199,500 fish year-round.

**Channel catfish.** Channel catfish stocking was restricted to isolated waters, including Cottonwood Basin, and a total of 91,900 fish was planned for these areas.

**Species background.**
- **Razorback Sucker:** Once abundant in the Colorado River basin, it was extirpated from much of its historic range. A major recovery effort began in 1981. Lake Mohave holds the largest known wild population, estimated at roughly 60,000 individuals in the 1990s and declining to about 20,000 more recently.
- **Bonytail Chub:** Once widespread, it became rare by the mid-twentieth century. Stocking of more than 130,000 larvae to 155 mm juveniles occurred from 1981 to 1991. Lake Mohave is considered potentially the largest remaining wild population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Colorado River watershed spans much of the western United States. The Lower Basin has been heavily altered by [[Hoover Dam]], other dams, water diversions and flood control. The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] controls river operations, and the surrounding land includes federal, state, tribal and private holdings.

**Lake Mohave** is the last large mainstem reservoir in the Lower Basin (created in the 1950s). It is about 105 km long, has a surface area of about 11,000 ha, and a hydraulic residence time of about 0.24 years, so it functions as a "run-of-the-river" reservoir. Water from Hoover Dam is drawn from the hypolimnion at 11 to 12.5 °C. Flows fluctuate daily, and a cold-water hypolimnion supports a year-round cold-water sport fishery.

**Davis Dam to Needles** has water temperatures of 12 to 15 °C near the dam. At least 65 percent of the shoreline has been modified by bank stabilization and levees.

**Lake Havasu** lies about 30 km downstream of the stocking area. It has maximum depths of about 19 m, a short retention time of 10 to 15 days, and warm-water conditions (surface temperatures reaching above 22 °C in summer) that are too high for most cold-water species.

**Colorado River Indian Reservation** sites are downstream of Parker Dam. Temperatures range from about 4 °C in winter to about 25 °C in summer, and backwaters are often warmer than the main channel.

**Nonnative fish** are a major threat. Only 35 fish species are native to the Colorado River basin, and about 70 species have been introduced. Striped bass predation on stocked trout and the decline of threadfin shad in Lake Mohave are also noted.

**Environmental baseline and cumulative effects.** Past federal actions, including dam construction, altered flows and introduced nonnative species, have had lasting effects. Future private and state actions with no federal connection are considered, but most are subject to Section 7 review through federal permits.

## Key Findings

- **Jeopardy determination:** Continued rainbow trout stocking is not likely to jeopardize the razorback sucker or bonytail chub, and is not likely to adversely modify designated critical habitat.
- **Channel catfish:** Stocking in waters without a direct connection to the Lower Colorado River is not likely to jeopardize these fishes or their critical habitat.
- **Predation risk:** Rainbow trout predation on razorback sucker larvae has been documented. Predation on bonytail chub has not. Trout stocked in the spring spawning period (January to April) in Areas 3 to 5 are the main concern.
- **Incidental take:** Take of razorback sucker larvae cannot be quantified. Take of bonytail chub larvae has not been documented.
- **Reasonable and prudent measure:** Efforts must minimize the opportunity for trout to contact razorback sucker and bonytail chub larvae. Trout stocking will not occur in Areas 1 to 5 where larvae are found, and surveys are required before stocking from November through April.
- **Recovery actions:** The Service committed to annual census of the adult razorback sucker population, capture of larvae, provision of 10,000 advanced razorback sucker fingerlings annually (1995 to 1999), and 25,000 advanced bonytail chub fingerlings annually.
- **Conservation recommendations:** A draft Lake Mohave fisheries management plan was proposed, with elements to be implemented from 1994 through 1999 and annual evaluation.
- **Reversibility:** Because stocked trout do not reproduce in the reservoir, the action is considered reversible, and stocking could be discontinued if it is shown to harm the listed species.
- **Re-initiation triggers:** Consultation must be reopened if take exceeds the anticipated level, new information reveals effects not considered, the action is modified, or a new species or critical habitat is listed.
