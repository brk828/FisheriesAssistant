## Executive Summary

This paper is primarily a sedimentological and carbonate petrology study describing the Pliocene "Shoofly oolite," an extensive fresh-water oolitic carbonate deposit at the base of the Glenns Ferry Formation along the southwestern margin of the Pliocene [[Lake Idaho]] (Snake River Plain, Idaho). While the core focus is on ooid fabric, mineralogy, and lacustrine depositional processes, the study uses fossil fish assemblages (alongside molluscs) as critical biostratigraphic and paleoenvironmental proxies — establishing that the oolite and surrounding sediments were deposited in a **low-salinity, fresh-water lake**, and drawing a modern analog to the fish fauna and water chemistry of [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, to constrain the paleolimnological conditions (notably elevated Mg/Ca ratios) under which the aragonitic ooids formed.

## Background & Description

- The Shoofly oolite crops out as a 45 km-long, ~5 km-wide, northwest-southeast trending carbonate sand body within Owyhee County, Idaho, reaching a maximum thickness of 12 m.
- It sits at the base of the [[Glenns Ferry Formation]], within the Idaho Group (comprising the Poison Creek, Chalk Hills, Glenns Ferry, and Bruneau Formations), deposited in the ancient lacustrine system termed "Idaho Lake" or [[Lake Idaho]] during the Miocene-Pliocene.
- Underlying Glenns Ferry transgressive beach/nearshore sands contain **locally abundant fragments of fish, wood, and molluscs**, interpreted as transgressive beach and nearshore deposits.
- Fish remains serve a key **biostratigraphic role**: the fish fauna from the underlying Chalk Hills lake silts is distinguished from the overlying Glenns Ferry Formation fauna by:
  - Presence of **Mylocheilus inflexus** (diagnostic of Chalk Hills Formation) and absence of Glenns Ferry indicator taxa.
  - Absence of characteristic Glenns Ferry species such as **[[Prosopium prolixus]]** and **Kerocottus spp.**
- Fish species found sparsely distributed within the oolite itself include **Mylocheilus robustus** and **Catostomus spp.**, alongside a diverse fresh-water molluscan fauna (notably *Sphaerium* and *Goniobasis*).
- These faunal assemblages are described as "clearly low-salinity lacustrine assemblages," directly supporting the fresh-water depositional interpretation of the oolite.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Nearshore lacustrine bench and bench-slope environments along the southwestern margin of a large Pliocene fresh-water lake ([[Lake Idaho]]); water depths inferred from crossbed thickness reached over 12 m.
- **Water chemistry inference**: The primary aragonitic mineralogy interpreted for the ooids implies lake water Mg/Ca ratios in excess of 2 (required for aragonite precipitation), yet total dissolved solids/salinity must have remained low enough to sustain a continuously evolving fresh-water fish and molluscan fauna.
- **Modern analog — [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada**: Cited as a present-day analog system where:
  - 8 species of fish occur, including genera the same as or related to those found in the Shoofly oolite fauna.
  - Salinity of only 4.7‰ has been recorded.
  - Mg/Ca ratios range between 4.5 and 15.
  - Tufas and ooids from Pyramid Lake show abundant aragonite, paralleling the inferred mineralogy of the Shoofly ooids.
- High Mg/Ca ratios in ancient [[Lake Idaho]] are attributed to either biological calcium uptake during periods when evaporation exceeded precipitation in a closed basin, or to groundwater seepage through surrounding volcanic terrain — with the **absence of associated gypsum and presence of fresh-water fish/mollusc faunas** indicating only minimal evaporative control on cation ratios (i.e., the lake was not a hypersaline system despite elevated Mg/Ca).
- Algal (endolithic) boring and biochemical influence on ooid cortex formation is also documented, indicating an active biological component to the lacustrine carbonate system in which the fish community existed.

## Key Findings

1. **Biostratigraphic differentiation**: Fish taxa distinguish the Chalk Hills Formation (with *Mylocheilus inflexus*) from the overlying Glenns Ferry Formation (marked by *[[Prosopium prolixus]]* and *Kerocottus spp.*), bracketing the oolite's depositional timing.
2. **Paleosalinity indicator**: Fish fauna (*Mylocheilus robustus*, *Catostomus spp.*) found within the oolite itself, together with diverse fresh-water molluscs, confirm a low-salinity, fresh-water lacustrine depositional setting for this unusually large carbonate unit — among the largest fresh-water lacustrine oolites known in the rock record.
3. **Paleoenvironmental analog**: The combination of elevated inferred Mg/Ca ratios (supporting primary aragonite mineralogy) with a thriving fresh-water fish fauna parallels modern [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, which hosts 8 fish species (related/same genera) at low salinity (4.7‰) despite high Mg/Ca ratios (4.5–15).
4. **Depositional model**: Fish and wood fragments in the underlying transgressive beach sands, together with sparse fish occurrences within the oolite, support a model of a fresh-water lake margin system with nearshore bench ooid formation and bench-slope (foreset) ooid deposition, all occurring under sustained fresh-water biological productivity rather than evaporitic/hypersaline conditions.
5. **Broader significance**: This is one of the few documented ancient oolites confidently interpreted as forming in a fresh-water fluvial/lacustrine setting (cf. [[Green River Formation]] oolites), with associated vertebrate (fish) and invertebrate (mollusc) faunas providing the definitive paleosalinity constraint absent from purely mineralogical/sedimentological data.
