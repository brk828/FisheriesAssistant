# Standing Crops of Chironomidae and Tubificidae in Two Desert Reservoirs, Central Arizona

#freshwater-biology #benthic-ecology #chironomidae #tubificidae

## Executive Summary

This study quantifies seasonal and spatial variation in the densities and biomasses of two dominant benthic macroinvertebrate groups, [[Chironomidae]] (midge larvae) and [[Tubificidae]] (aquatic worms), in [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[Apache Lake]] in the [[Salt River Reservoirs]] of central Arizona, sampled from autumn 1970 to autumn 1971. Mean standing crops were comparatively low, but density peaked in spring and was lower in summer. The two taxa showed a disjunct (complementary) distribution, with chironomids and tubificids rarely co-occurring. The author attributes this pattern mainly to hypolimnetic oxygen depletion, food supply (chlorophyll-a), lake morphology, and possibly substrate composition and predator-prey interactions.

## Background & Description

Benthic fauna of the Salt River reservoirs was poorly known before this work. Earlier records included the oligochaete [[Branchiura Sowerbyi]] (Cole, 1966) and the introduced [[Corbicula]] clam (Rinne, 1974). The study was part of a combined limnology and fisheries project begun in summer 1970 in lakes within the [[Sonoran Desert]]. Its aim was to define seasonal and spatial changes in physical, chemical, and biological factors and their influence on the bathymetric distribution of fishes.

Chironomid larvae (five genera: *Cryptochironomus*, *Procladius*, *Tanypus*, *Chironomus*, *Polypedilum*) and tubificid worms (two genera: *Branchiura*, *Limnodrilus*) were common and most abundant in silt-mud flats of inundated flood plains. Crayfish (*Orconectes* and *Procambarus*) and Corbicula inhabited rocky canyon slopes and were not quantified here.

**Sampling methods**
- Bottom samples were collected seasonally at numbered stations along transects in both reservoirs.
- A [[Petersen Dredge]] (about 900 cm², 43 kg) was used initially, with two grabs per station.
- An [[Ekman Dredge]] (225 cm², 5 kg) was added in spring 1971, with six samples taken from around an anchored boat at each station.
- Ekman counts averaged three times those from the Petersen, so the Ekman was used exclusively after spring 1971. The author noted the heavier Petersen grab likely ejected surface ooze and organisms during descent.
- Samples were sieved through a 0.35 mm mesh (Tyler 45 equivalent), counted, preserved in 10% formalin, dried, and weighed. Dry weight was determined by drying at 80°C for 12 hours.
- Density (n/m²) was converted to biomass (kg/ha) using dry weight per individual. Comparisons used analysis of variance for unequal sample sizes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir conditions**
- Roosevelt Lake was at about half total storage capacity through the study, exposing rocky terraces and reducing bottom heterogeneity.
- Apache Lake's water level fell to 15% of maximum volume in summer 1971. Transects A-1 to A-3 were dry in that season, and falling levels prevented sampling in autumn 1971.
- Transects within each reservoir were broadly similar in bottom type, depth, dissolved oxygen, and temperature, which likely suppressed within-lake variability.

**Oxygen and temperature**
- In summer 1971, Roosevelt bottom waters had about 0.4 mg/l dissolved oxygen, with chironomids absent downlake.
- In autumn 1971, Roosevelt was under homothermy (16°C) with about 2.0 mg/l dissolved oxygen, yet chironomids had not returned at transects R-2 and R-3.
- In summer 1971, Apache Lake had about 0.8 mg/l dissolved oxygen and 28°C water, with chironomids absent while tubificids persisted.

**Food supply and trophic relationships**
- Chironomid abundance closely paralleled chlorophyll-a (phytoplankton) concentrations, which were highest at both ends of Roosevelt Lake. Chironomus, a filter-feeding herbivore, was the most common genus and drove this correlation.
- Tubificid numbers were inversely related to chlorophyll-a.
- Three of the five chironomid genera (*Cryptochironomus*, *Procladius*, *Tanypus*) belong to carnivorous subfamilies. The author suggested these predators, together with poor substrate, might reduce tubificid densities in shallow inflow areas, though this requires confirmation.

**Morphology and fish predation**
- Roosevelt Lake is broader and has more shallow (<15 m), gently sloping littoral zones than Apache Lake, where most of the shoreline is precipitous.
- Benthic organisms were consistently more abundant in the Apache reaches (for example A-3) most similar to Roosevelt in morphology.
- Chironomids are a major food for fishes in these lakes. Consistently dense larval populations in the shallows suggested that substrate and dissolved oxygen were more important than fish predation in determining chironomid distribution.

## Key Findings

**Densities and biomasses**
- **Roosevelt Lake, autumn 1970 (Petersen grabs):** Tubificid densities ranged from 11 to 495/m². Chironomids were most abundant at R-4 (mean 218/m², 5 samples). Combined densities were highest at transects R-1 and R-4.
- **Roosevelt Lake biomass, autumn 1970:** Chironomids ranged from 0.27 to 10.0 kg/ha and tubificids from 0.15 to 7.83 kg/ha.
- **Roosevelt Lake biomass, spring 1971:** Chironomid biomass (0.86 to 16.83 kg/ha) exceeded tubificid biomass.
- **Apache Lake, spring 1971:** Tubificids were the densest taxon at transect A-3, with a reported mean of 1,347/m² (22 samples).
- **Apache Lake, autumn 1970:** Densities were low (chironomids 11 to 237/m²; tubificids 11 to 195/m²), with no significant differences among transects.

**Seasonal patterns**
- Densities were generally lowest in summer, higher in autumn, and highest in spring.
- Mean densities at downlake transects rose markedly from autumn 1970 to spring 1971.
- Tubificid biomass in Apache Lake more than doubled from autumn 1970 to spring 1971, while chironomid biomass declined.

**Spatial and statistical results**
- Disjunct distribution was evident: tubificids were common where chironomids were scarce, and vice versa.
- Chironomids were absent from transects R-2 and R-3 in summer and autumn 1971, and from A-4 and A-5 in Apache Lake in summer 1971.
- Tubificids were present at all Roosevelt transects in summer and autumn 1971.
- Among-station differences within transects were usually nonsignificant. Significant differences were most frequent in spring, when variability was highest. The author attributed spring chironomid variability partly to unsynchronized hatches.
- Tubificid densities differed significantly among transects in all seasons, possibly due to interspecific clumping that random sampling of clumps could amplify.

**Main conclusions**
- Benthic standing crops in these desert reservoirs are low compared with averages for lakes of similar type, despite Roosevelt having the greater mean biomass of the two lakes.
- Chironomids and tubificids differ in tolerance to low dissolved oxygen. Tubificids, which tolerate anaerobic conditions, dominate deeper water, lowered water levels, and stagnant conditions.
- Chlorophyll-a (food supply), oxygen, morphology, and substrate are the key controls. Predator-prey interactions may also play a role but were not confirmed.
- Deeper lakes generally support lower benthic biomass (Deevey, 1941; Rawson, 1952; Brinkhurst, 1974), consistent with these results.
