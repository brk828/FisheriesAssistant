# Candidate Species Review: Endangered and Threatened Plant and Animal Taxa (Federal Register, 28 February 1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #candidate-species

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (the Service) updates its list of native U.S. plant and animal taxa regarded as candidates for listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The notice does not report new field research. Its purpose is to give advance notice of potential listings so that environmental planners can plan around threatened taxa, and to solicit information that could change candidate status. Table 1 lists 182 candidate taxa, and Table 2 lists 303 taxa (including synonyms) formerly classed as candidates or proposed species that have been removed from candidate status. Fishes form one group within the broader list. The notice also discontinues the Category 2 and Category 3 designations.

## Background & Description

- **Legal basis:** The Act requires the Service to identify endangered and threatened species using the best available scientific and commercial information. The Service maintains a candidate list to give early notice to planners and developers, to solicit input, and to prioritize proposed listings.
- **Review history:** Earlier notices of review included a 1975 plant notice, a 1980 comprehensive plant notice, and animal notices in 1982 through 1994. This notice supersedes all previous notices of review.
- **Category changes:**
  - Category 2 designation is discontinued because it caused confusion about conservation status. These taxa are not considered candidates.
  - Category 3 is discontinued. Subcategory 3A (persuasive evidence of extinction) and 3C (more abundant or widespread than thought, or not subject to identifiable threat) are retained for reference only.
- **Table format:** Table 1 includes status category (PE = proposed endangered, PT = proposed threatened, C = candidate, Y = synonym of a listed taxon), listing priority number, lead Region, scientific name, family, common name and historic range. Table 2 uses removal codes (A = more abundant or widespread, F = range no longer U.S. territory, I = insufficient information, L = listed, M = mistakenly included, N = not meeting the definition of species, X = believed extinct).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Conservation focus:** Candidate listing is intended to focus conservation effort on species at greatest risk. The listing priority number reflects immediacy and magnitude of threats and taxonomic status, under a system described in a 1983 Federal Register notice (48 FR 43098).
- **Environmental planning:** The notice encourages consideration of candidate taxa in environmental planning, including analysis under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]].
- **Fish-relevant threats and status:** Many aquatic taxa are affected by habitat loss, water use and other threats. The notice itself, however, provides only range, category and priority data, not detailed threat analyses.
- **Regional offices:** Eight regional offices (Regions 1 to 7, with Region 1 covering the Pacific states and territories) are lead agencies for individual taxa.
- **Request for information:** The Service asks for data on current status, range changes, new candidates, critical habitat, threats, taxonomy and common names.

## Key Findings

**Overall counts (as stated in the notice):**
- **Proposed endangered:** 159 plants and 91 animals.
- **Proposed threatened:** 37 plants and 8 animals.
- **Candidates awaiting proposed rules:** 84 plants and 98 animals (182 total, matching Table 1).
- **Proposed rules issued:** 238 U.S. taxa.
- **Removed from candidate status (Table 2):** 303 taxa, including synonyms.

**Fishes in Table 1 (proposed or candidate):**
- **Pecos Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*), proposed endangered, Region 2.
- **Devils River Minnow** (*Dionda diaboli*), proposed threatened, Region 2.
- **Arkansas Darter** (*Etheostoma cragini*), proposed threatened, Region 6.
- **Gila bicolor** subspecies, including Cowhead Lake and High Rock Springs tui chubs, with listings across Regions 1 and 4.
- **Sturgeon Chub** (*Macrhybopsis gelida*) and **Sicklefin Chub** (*Macrhybopsis meeki*), both proposed as candidates.
- **Topeka Shiner** and **Kansas River Shiner** (native population).
- **McCloud River Redband Trout**, proposed endangered in California.
- **Bull Trout** (*Salvelinus confluentus*), proposed threatened in the U.S. and Canada.
- **Atlantic Salmon** (Gulf of Maine distinct population), proposed endangered, spanning the U.S., Canada and Greenland.
- **Arctic Grayling** (*Thymallus arcticus*), Upper Missouri River population, proposed endangered.
- Other Table 1 fishes include Dionda and *Notropis* species, *Thymallus* and *Salmo* taxa.

**Other notable taxa:**
- **Freshwater mussels (Unionidae):** A large group, including rabbitsfoot, purple bankclimber, oyster mussel and several moccasinshells and pigtoes, mostly from the southeastern U.S.
- **Amphibians:** Including the California red-legged frog, Chiricahua leopard frog and several salamanders.
- **Mammals, birds, reptiles, insects, crustaceans and plants:** Each has many entries. Plants make up the largest group by count.

**Table 2 (former candidates now removed):**
- Removal reasons are mostly A (more abundant or widespread) and L (listed).
- Fishes in this group include Florida tails, the Etowah darter, the Virgin spinedace, the Pecos springsnail, and the Appalachian elktoe, which are no longer candidates.

**Key takeaway:** The notice is a status and procedural update. It does not establish new protections. Its main value for aquatic conservation is in showing which fishes and freshwater mollusks are under federal consideration, so that planners can take them into account.
