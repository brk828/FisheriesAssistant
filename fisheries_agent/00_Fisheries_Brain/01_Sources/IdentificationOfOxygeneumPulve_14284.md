# Identification of Oxygeneum pulverulentum as a Hybrid Cyprinid Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

Hubbs and Bailey (1952, reprinted from the Papers of the Michigan Academy of Science, Arts and Letters, vol. XXXVII, 1951) re-examine [[Oxygeneum Pulverulentum]], a nominal cyprinid genus and species described by Forbes (1885) from a single specimen taken in the [[Illinois River]] at Peoria in June 1878. The name had been unplaceable and had been dropped from Illinois fish accounts after Large (1903) suggested it was a hybrid. Morphological, meristic and ecological evidence from the type and three additional specimens leads the authors to conclude that the type is an intergeneric hybrid, [[Campostoma anomalum pullum]] × [[Chrosomus erythrogaster]]. The four hybrids are intermediate in almost all characters, with a grand average hybrid index of 52.

## Background & Description

The paper addresses a long-standing taxonomic problem: nominal species based on inadequate material that cannot be placed in the systematic structure. The type of Oxygeneum pulverulentum (Illinois Natural History Survey) was studied through the courtesy of Philip W. Smith. Three further hybrids of the same cross, held at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], were used for comparison:

- U.M.M.Z. No. 100971: tributary to the Turkey River, Delaware County, Iowa (C. a. pullum × C. erythrogaster), 56 mm standard length.
- U.M.M.Z. No. 103132: Spring Creek, Kansas, Delaware County, Oklahoma (C. a. pullum × C. erythrogaster), 29 mm.
- U.M.M.Z. No. 122039: spring-fed creek, Riley County, Kansas (C. a. plumbeum × C. erythrogaster), 49 mm.

The parental species share herbivorous-associated anatomy (black peritoneum, long convoluted gut, grinding pharyngeal teeth) but are dissimilar. The authors consider them probably only distantly related, both members of the North American leuciscine group, with herbivorous adaptations evolved independently.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Range overlap: all four hybrids were collected in the broad zone where both parental species occur, often in similar stream types. Forbes and Richardson (1909) mapped C. anomalum near Peoria and several C. erythrogaster records in the same river system.
- Spawning: both parents breed on gravel riffles in spring. The senior author observed both breeding with several other minnow species on one gravel patch, a situation considered responsible for much interspecific hybridization in the Cyprinidae.
- Rarity: the type's extreme rarity across a region intensively collected for many years supports its hybrid origin.
- Lateral line reduction is common in spring-inhabiting or quiet-water fishes such as Chrosomus.

## Key Findings

**Identification**
- The type of Oxygeneum pulverulentum is a hybrid, Campostoma anomalum pullum × Chrosomus erythrogaster. Three further hybrids of this cross and one of C. a. plumbeum × C. erythrogaster were examined.
- The original count of 5-5 pharyngeal teeth was corrected: the right arch had never been removed and bears 4 teeth.
- The type has a complete lateral line of 57 scales, not 63 as reported by Forbes.

**Morphological evidence**
- Pharyngeal teeth (5-4 in the hybrids) are essentially intermediate between Chrosomus (typically 5-5) and Campostoma (consistently 4-4).
- Gill rakers are clearly intermediate but closer to Chrosomus. Total rakers in the first arch are 16–20 in hybrids versus 29–34 in Campostoma and 10–12 in Chrosomus.
- Mouth and lip structure is intermediate, with the cutting edge of the lower jaw (a sharp hard edge described by Forbes) reflecting Campostoma influence.
- Scale size is intermediate but nearer to Campostoma. Scale rows along the body in Campostoma average 77.1 versus 30.5 in Chrosomus for the grouped counts summarized in Table I.
- Scale basal radii on the anterior field are malformed, consistent with hybridity.
- Dorsal fin position is almost perfectly intermediate (hybrid index 48 averaged across specimens).
- Three of four hybrids have 7 anal rays, a minimum modal number among American cyprinids, pointing to Campostoma (7 rays) as one parent.

**Genetic inferences**
- The lateral line in Chrosomus appears under the predominant or complete control of a single recessive gene; heterozygotes therefore have a nearly complete canal, as in Campostoma.
- Partial dominance is suggested for gill-raker count (smaller number dominant) and for scale count, though not clearly proven given small sample size and stock heterogeneity.
- Completeness of the lateral line appears dominant over incompleteness.

**Digestive tract and colour**
- The intestine of the hybrids is intermediate in length and orientation: its loops are diagonal, unlike the transverse loops of Campostoma or longitudinal loops of Chrosomus.
- Two prominent dark lateral stripes (typical of Chrosomus) are present in the hybrids, though fainter. The type's stripes have likely faded.

**Hybrid indices**
- Across the four hybrids, averages of individual character hybrid indices range from 46 to 56, with a grand average of 52. Total gill-raker counts and pored/unpored scale counts were excluded from this average.
- The senior author (Hubbs) regards composite indices as useful despite low precision; the junior author (Bailey) considers them too unreliable to include.

**Conclusion**
- Given the rarity of the type, the widespread hybridization in the Cyprinidae, the absence of known ecological barriers, and the expected characters of the hybrid, the authors state that confidence in the identification is "virtually complete." The name Oxygeneum pulverulentum is thus removed from the list of valid American freshwater fishes.
