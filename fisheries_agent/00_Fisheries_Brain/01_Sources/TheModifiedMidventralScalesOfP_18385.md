# The Modified Midventral Scales of Percina (Osteichthyes; Percidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1976 morphological study by [[Lawrence M. Page]] of the Illinois Natural History Survey examines the enlarged, strongly toothed "modified" scales of [[Percina]] darters. The scales are unique to this genus within the darter tribe Etheostomatini, and their presence or absence helps separate [[Percina]] from [[Etheostoma]]. Based on examination of over 600 specimens, the author proposes that the scales function as tactile contact organs during spawning, that the midbelly row evolved through progressive enlargement of scales and loss of their number, and that the scales are permanent rather than deciduous as previously thought.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Modified scales on the breast of nearly all [[Percina]] species, and a midbelly row in most males. Scales from more than 600 specimens were examined in situ, and some were removed, stained with alizarin red and viewed on a scale projector. All measurements are standard lengths.
- **Taxonomic context:** Seven subgenera with modified midbelly scales are recognized, including [[Odontopholis]], [[Hypohomus]], [[Hadropterus]], [[Swainia]], [[Alvordius]], [[Ericosma]], [[Cottogaster]], [[Percina]] (subgenus) and [[Imostoma]]. The subgenus [[Alvordius]] shows the most elaborate modification.
- **Prior literature:** Scales were often cited in keys, but their function, evolution and variation had not been described in detail. The author notes that earlier reports of deciduous scales were erroneous.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning behavior:** Males mount females and brace their venters against the female's dorsum, a pattern recorded in several species (e.g., [[Percina caprodes]], [[Percina peltata]], [[Percina notogramma]]). This suggests a midventral locus of tactile stimulation.
- **Seasonal change:** During spawning, the modified scales are covered by thicker flesh and their teeth project nearly perpendicular to the body. Outside spawning, the teeth lie adpressed.
- **Analogous structures:** The author compares the scale teeth to [[Breeding Tubercles]] and to the contact organs of [[Fundulus heteroclitus]], described by [[Newman]] (1907).
- **Correlation with other dimorphisms:** Three of the seven subgenera with modified midbelly scales lack breeding tubercles. All eight species of [[Alvordius]] lack them, whereas [[Imostoma]] has weak modified scales and extensive tuberculation.
- **Caudal keels:** Caudal keels occur only in [[Percina]] species lacking a large midbelly row of modified scales, and are presumed to have a stimulatory function.

## Key Findings

- **Function:** Modified scales most likely induce tactile stimulation of the female during spawning, analogous to breeding tubercles.
- **Sexual distribution:** Scales occur on the breast of both sexes in most species. A midbelly row is typically found only on males; rare females of five species, and all eight examined females of *P. macrocephala*, also had one.
- **Evolution:** The midbelly row likely arose from progressively larger, more strongly toothed scales near the midventer. Evolution proceeded toward fewer, larger scales, reaching its extreme in [[Alvordius]]. *Percina peltata* has as few as four scales in the midbelly row.
- **Ancestral prototype:** Enlarged belly scales in *P. cymatotaenia* and an undescribed *Percina* species from Kentucky may represent the ancestral condition, and occasional enlarged anal-area scales are seen as intermediate states.
- **Permanence:** Modified scales are permanent, not periodically shed. Earlier beliefs in deciduousness arose from scaleless strips on females and seasonal changes in males.
- **Ontogeny:** The midbelly row appears early (present at 23 mm in *P. phoxocephala* and 26 mm in *P. sciera*, less than one month old) and persists throughout life. Teeth enlarge disproportionately to scale growth.
- **Variation:** Intraspecific and interspecific variation is pronounced between subgenera and less pronounced within them. Imostoma males range from zero to 18 scales in the midbelly row.
- **Midbelly row counts (Table 2):** Counts were recorded for 36 species/groups, with a total of roughly 20 species represented in detail. Examples: *P. burtoni* 29–40 (mean 35.0); *P. caprodes* 20–32 (mean 26.5); *P. maculata* 7–13 (mean 9.5); *P. peltata* 4–6 (mean 5.0); *P. shumardi* 0–18 (mean 6.6); *P. copelandi* 6–11 (mean 8.6).
- **Specimen base:** Specimens came from many museum and Illinois Natural History Survey collections, including [[Illinois Natural History Survey]], [[United States National Museum]], [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] and [[Tulane University]].
- **Monophyly:** Modified breast scales on all *Percina* species, and their absence in other darters, support a monophyletic origin of the genus (citing [[Page and Whitt]], 1973).
