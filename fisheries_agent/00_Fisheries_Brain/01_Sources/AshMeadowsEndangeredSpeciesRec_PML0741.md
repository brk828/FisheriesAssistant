# Ash Meadows Endangered Species Recovery Plan Notice and Federal Register Land Actions (July 1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register compilation (Vol. 55, No. 144, July 26, 1990) is a multi-item public notice bundle, mostly covering land and resource administration. Its only item directly about fish is a U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] notice seeking public comment on a draft [[Recovery Plan]] for 12 federally listed endangered and threatened species endemic to [[Ash Meadows]], Nevada and California, including four fish. The remaining items cover a [[Bureau of Land Management]] land sale and realty actions, cadastral plat filings, a [[Minerals Management Service]] environmental assessment for a Pacific Outer Continental Shelf placer mineral research cruise, and a U.S. International Trade Commission investigation. OCR damage was substantial; where text was unclear, values were reconstructed conservatively or noted as uncertain.

## Background & Description

The fish covered by the recovery plan are small, narrowly endemic inhabitants of Ash Meadows:

- **[[Devil's Hole Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), restricted to the Devil's Hole spring system.
- **[[Warm Springs Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis*, subspecies *pectoralis*, per the notice's taxonomy, which is partly garbled).
- **[[Ash Meadows Amargosa Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis mionectes*).
- **[[Ash Meadows Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus nevadensis*), a cyprinid.

The other eight listed species are plants and invertebrates, including Ash Meadows gumplant, Ash Meadows ivesia, Ash Meadows blazing star, Ash Meadows milk-vetch, Ash Meadows sunray, Amargosa niterwort, and spring-loving centaury. The notice states that Ash Meadows is home to approximately 26 unique plants and animals (OCR reading "2b"; uncertain).

The [[Endangered Species Act]] requires recovery plans for listed species unless a plan would not promote their conservation. Under Section 4(f), recovery plans describe needed actions, set recovery criteria, and estimate time and cost.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice identifies the principal causes of decline for Ash Meadows endemics:

- Habitat modification from agriculture, municipal, and mining development.
- Introduction of non-native animals, including [[Largemouth Bass]], black bass, black mollies, [[Mosquitofish]], crayfish, bullfrogs, and [[Trumpet Snail|turban snails]].
- Invasion of exotic plants, notably [[Saltcedar]].
- Grazing and trampling by wild horses.

Recovery will focus on maintaining and enhancing existing populations by removing introduced [[Saltcedar]], non-native animals, and wild horses, and by restoring terrestrial and aquatic habitats degraded by agriculture and mining. Most habitat lies within the [[Ash Meadows National Wildlife Refuge]].

## Key Findings

**Recovery plan notice (Fish and Wildlife Service, dated July 20, 1990)**
- Draft recovery plan covers 12 endangered or threatened species; four are fish (the pupfishes and speckled dace listed above).
- Public comments were due on or before August 24, 1990, and would be considered before the plan is approved.
- Comments and requests for the plan could be sent to David L. Harlow at the Reno, Nevada Field Station. The Portland, Oregon regional office was the contact for general information (Andrew F. Robinson).
- Authority: Section 4(f) of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

**Land sale and realty actions (Bureau of Land Management, Boise and Arizona offices)**
- A direct noncompetitive sale of about 1,371.8 acres (OCR reading; uncertain) along Interstate 84 in Idaho was offered to the Department of Transportation to build and operate the East Boise Port of Entry. The segregative effect lasts until patent issuance or 270 days.
- Reservations on transferred lands include a right-of-way for ditches and canals under the Act of August 30, 1890, and a buried-line grant to Mountain Bell Telephone and Telegraph.
- The purchaser must pay the full price within 180 days; failure forfeits the deposit.
- Arizona cadastral plats (Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest, Navajo-Hopi Development Office, and several Phoenix District survey plats) were officially filed in June and July 1990.

**Pacific OCS research cruise (Minerals Management Service)**
- The MMS issued a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) and an Environmental Assessment for a placer mineral research cruise off the Oregon coast, covering the Cape Blanco and Rogue River areas.
- Parties included the Oregon Placer Minerals Technical Task Force, the Oregon Department of Geology and Mineral Industries, and the U.S. Geological Survey.
- The cruise was scheduled for the period ending around September 19, 1990.

**International Trade Commission investigation (No. 332-TA-301)**
- The Commission announced an investigation into the competitiveness of advanced-technology manufacturing industries in the United States, requested under Section 332(g) of the Tariff Act of 1930.
- The notice describes the criteria for identifying industries to monitor, with public comment invited. It contains no fish or aquatic content.
