# California Trout, Salmon, and Warmwater Fish Production and Costs (1963-64)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #aquaculture

## Executive Summary

This eleventh annual hatchery report from the California Department of Fish and Game documents statewide hatchery fish production and operating costs for fiscal year 1963-64. The Department planted **43,709,804 fish** totaling **2,087,059 pounds**, an increase of 3,613,093 fish and 357,947 pounds over the previous fiscal year, despite [[Crystal Lake Hatchery]] not operating at full capacity due to a [[Ceratomyxa]] disease outbreak that had previously killed all resident rainbow trout there.

Total operating cost across State-supported hatcheries was **$1,900,133**, a decrease of $20,432 from 1962-63 despite higher production. The report attributes the cost reduction primarily to increased hatchery efficiency at full operational capacity, with falling fish food prices as a secondary factor. Notably, aggressive price competition among food vendors led to a decline in feed quality, causing diet deficiencies in smaller fish toward the end of the fiscal year — a problem addressed by the subsequent implementation of a hatchery food testing program.

Key production categories included [[Catchable-Sized Trout]] (7,204,103 fish, 1,549,168 lbs), [[Subcatchable-Sized Trout]] (1,605,812 fish, 171,924 lbs), and anadromous species — [[King Salmon]], [[Silver Salmon]], and [[Steelhead Trout]] — totaling roughly 15,326,753 fish. The [[Warmwater Fish Program]] was sharply curtailed, largely due to the phase-out of walleye stocking following negligible angler returns.

## Background & Description

This report is a fisheries production and cost analysis rather than a morphological study; however, it contains size/weight data relevant to growth performance and stocking condition across species and life-history stages:

- **Fingerling Rainbow Trout**: averaged 178 fish per pound (small size class), cost $3.26/lb to produce.
- **Catchable-Sized Trout** (all species combined): averaged **4.6 fish per pound**, indicating a standard "catchable" stocking size targeted by the Department.
- **Subcatchable-Sized Trout**: intermediate size class between fingerling and catchable, increasingly used for reservoir and lake stocking.
- **Broodstock Trout**: largest size class, retained for egg production; cost $0.785/lb, 0.63 fish/lb.
- **King Salmon Fingerlings**: extremely small size class, ~344 fish per pound.
- **Steelhead Yearlings**: ~4.2 fish per pound at planting.
- **Warmwater Fish**: fingerlings averaged 404 fish/lb; adults 3.6 fish/lb.

Species-specific weight/number ratios (Table 1) allow inference of average individual size at stocking for [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Golden Trout]], [[Eastern Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Eagle Lake Trout]], [[Cutthroat Trout]], and [[Kokanee Salmon]], with rainbow trout dominating total resident fish production (20,070,855 individuals, 1,806,514 lbs).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report describes stocking strategies tied to habitat type and species resilience:

- **Resident (non-anadromous) trout** were stocked across California's lakes, streams, and reservoirs, with [[Rainbow Trout]] dominating both fingerling and catchable categories. [[Eastern Brook Trout]] and [[Brown Trout]] showed increased resistance to **Ceratomyxa**, a parasitic disease that eliminated rainbow trout populations at Crystal Lake Hatchery — highlighting differential species susceptibility within shared hatchery/habitat conditions.
- **Subcatchable-sized trout** were increasingly deployed in reservoirs and lakes, reflecting a management shift toward stocking practices suited to larger, more complex water bodies versus streams.
- **Anadromous fish** ([[King Salmon]], [[Silver Salmon]], [[Steelhead Trout]]) were reared at river-based hatcheries (e.g., [[Trinity River Hatchery]], [[Nimbus Hatchery]], [[Mt. Shasta Hatchery]]) tied to natural spawning runs. King salmon fingerling production was explicitly noted as dependent on the size of natural parent runs returning from the ocean, linking hatchery output directly to broader North Pacific oceanic and riverine population dynamics.
- **Warmwater fish** ([[Channel Catfish]], [[Redeye Bass]], [[Red-ear Sunfish]], [[Walleye]]) were stocked primarily in Southern California waters via the [[Chino Fisheries Base]] and Central Valley facilities. The [[Walleye]] program was phased out due to poor angler return rates despite ~2,000,000 fish stocked annually in prior years, illustrating a stocking-program feedback loop informed by post-release survival/utility rather than pure production metrics.
- Predation pressure was an active ecological factor in hatchery ponds: losses from predatory birds at [[Mt. Whitney-Black Rock Hatchery]] were reduced by walling up pond banks, indicating direct competition/predation dynamics between wild avian predators and captive-reared juvenile trout.

## Key Findings

- **Total production**: 43,709,804 fish (2,087,059 lbs) planted in 1963-64, up from 1962-63 but ~10,000,000 fish below 1961-62 peak levels.
- **Catchable trout** dominated by weight: 7,204,103 fish (1,549,168 lbs), averaging 4.6 fish/lb, at $0.170 each ($0.785/lb) — an $0.18/lb decrease from the prior year.
- **Anadromous fish** declined in numbers (~18,000,000 → ~15,300,000) driven by reduced king salmon fingerling output (tied to natural run size), but increased in total weight (~91,000 lbs) due to gains in [[Steelhead Trout]] and [[Silver Salmon]] size/weight, notably at [[Trinity River Hatchery]] (47,521 lbs steelhead vs. <1,000 lbs prior year) and [[Nimbus Hatchery]] (+~31,000 lbs steelhead).
- **Warmwater fish program contraction**: fish planted dropped from 2,149,390 (3,895 lbs) to 450,898 (1,580 lbs), chiefly from ending [[Walleye]] stocking.
- **Cost efficiency gains**: Total hatchery operating cost fell to $1,900,133 (−$20,432 y/y) despite increased production, attributed to capacity operation efficiency rather than reduced input costs alone.
- **Cost per pound of trout (all sizes)** dropped to $0.92 from $1.09 (−$0.17/lb), continuing a downward trend; three-year (1961-64) average cost per pound of trout was $0.99.
- **Feed quality risk**: Vendor price competition reduced feed quality late in FY1963-64, causing diet deficiencies in smaller fish — prompting creation of a hatchery food testing program to safeguard future feed quality.
- **Regional production leaders**: [[Darrah Springs Hatchery]] led all hatcheries (395,499 lbs), followed by [[San Joaquin Hatchery]] (261,590 lbs) and [[Fish Springs Hatchery]], [[Mojave River Hatchery]], and [[Moccasin Creek Hatchery]] (each >200,000 lbs).
- **Federally/municipally financed hatcheries** ([[Nimbus Hatchery]], [[Trinity River Hatchery]], [[Mokelumne River Hatchery]]) operated by the State showed comparable unit costs (~$1.88–$1.99/lb), both improved relative to prior-year figures where available (Nimbus: $2.64 → $1.99/lb).
- **Steelhead production costs** fell sharply from $6.14/lb (1962-63) to $2.30/lb (1963-64), a major driver of overall anadromous fish cost reduction.
- **Warmwater fish cost per pound rose** to $33.19/lb (from lower 1962-63 levels) due to reduced total output, even though total operational cost increased only marginally ($50,759 → $52,437), largely due to fixed labor costs tied to fish rescue operations as well as hatchery production.
