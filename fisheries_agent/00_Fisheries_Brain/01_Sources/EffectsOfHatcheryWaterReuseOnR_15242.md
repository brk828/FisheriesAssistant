# Effects of Hatchery Water Reuse on Rainbow Trout Metabolism

## Executive Summary
This study investigated how sequential reuse of hatchery water affects the metabolic physiology of [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*), specifically testing **scope for activity** as a physiological index of hatchery fish fitness. Fish were reared for 306 days across seven cumulative water-reuse levels (0 through 6 reuses) at the Fish Cultural Development Center in Bozeman, Montana. Results showed that **active metabolic rate declined significantly with increasing water reuse** (P<0.05) once the cumulative loading factor reached 1.80 or greater, while **standard metabolic rate remained unaffected**. Consequently, scope for activity — the difference between active and standard oxygen consumption — decreased by **49%** from the first-use to the sixth-reuse level. The authors argue this metabolic decline may compromise post-stocking survival in stream environments and recommend minimizing water reuse in hatchery rearing intended for wild stocking programs.

## Background & Description
This paper does not focus on external morphology but reports body size data relevant to metabolic scaling:

- **Body weight range across treatments:** 78–225 g (mean range ~96–188 g depending on reuse level)
- Fish size varied significantly among the seven reuse treatments, necessitating **covariance analysis** to adjust active metabolic rates to a common mean body weight before comparison.
- No morphological deformities or pathologies are directly described in this excerpt, though the Discussion references corresponding "physiological and pathological trends" documented in a companion study ([[Larmoyeux and Piper 1973]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Hatchery raceway/trough system using sequential (reused) water flow — a controlled aquaculture proxy for studying stress physiology relevant to natural stream environments.
- **Target environment for management:** Fish are ultimately intended for stocking into **stream environments**, where they must actively swim against current to establish a home range — directly linking hatchery metabolic condition to real-world survival ecology.
- **Physiological ecology concept — [[Scope for Activity]]:** Defined as the arithmetic difference between active and standard oxygen consumption rates; serves as a proxy for the energetic reserve available for swimming, foraging, and predator evasion in the wild.
- **Water quality/loading factor:** The "cumulative loading factor" (a measure of fish biomass relative to water reuse) is identified as a critical ecological threshold — effects on metabolism emerged once this factor reached ≥1.80.

## Key Findings

### Active Metabolism (measured at 9.6°C)
| Reuse Level | Cumulative Loading Factor | Mean Body Weight (g) | Adjusted Active O₂ Consumption (mg O₂/kg/hr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (first use) | 6 | 187 | 566 |
| 1 | 5 | 184 | 511 |
| 2 | 6 | 176 | 614 |
| 3 | 6 | 188 | 407 |
| 4 | 6 | 166 | 371 |
| 5 | 6 | 132 | 374 |
| 6 (sixth reuse) | 5 | 96 | 337 |

- Active metabolism was **significantly reduced (P<0.05)** at water reuse levels 3 through 6 (cumulative loading factor ≥1.80).
- Overall decline fit a linear regression model (**r² = 0.92**, Y = a + bX).
- **Average reduction: 39 mg O₂/kg/hour per additional water reuse cycle.**
- Highest active metabolic rate (566 mg O₂/kg/hr) occurred in trout reared in **unused (first-pass) water**.
- Lowest active rate (337 mg O₂/kg/hr) occurred at the sixth reuse level — a **~40% reduction** from first-use levels.

### Standard Metabolism (measured at 11.9°C)
| Reuse Level | n | Mean Body Weight (g) | O₂ Consumption (mg O₂/kg/hr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | 114 | 88 |
| 6 | 2 | 86 | 92 |

- No statistically meaningful difference detected between first-use and sixth-reuse levels (though sample size was too small, n=2 per group, for formal statistical testing).
- Authors conclude water reuse has **no measurable effect on standard metabolism**.

### Scope for Activity
- **First-use level:** 478 mg O₂/kg/hour
- **Sixth-reuse level:** 245 mg O₂/kg/hour
- **Net decrease: 49%**
- The decline in scope was attributed **entirely to reductions in active metabolism**, since standard metabolism remained stable across treatments.

### Broader Implications
- [[Scope for Activity]] was found to be a **more sensitive indicator** of hatchery trout fitness than growth or food conversion metrics, detecting physiological impairment at **lower levels of water reuse** than previously reported growth/pathology-based criteria ([[Larmoyeux and Piper 1973]]).
- Growth and food conversion were reportedly unaffected by up to four levels of water reuse, but **active metabolism was measurably impaired after only three levels of use** — indicating metabolic scope is a more precocious/early-warning bioindicator of chronic hatchery water-quality stress.
- **Management recommendation:** If post-stocking survival is linked to scope for activity, hatchery practices should **minimize water reuse** for trout destined for wild stream stocking programs.
