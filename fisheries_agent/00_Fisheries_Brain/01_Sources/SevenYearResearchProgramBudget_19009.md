# San Juan River Recovery Implementation Program: FY1996 Budget and Work Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the Fiscal Year 1996 budget and work plan for the [[San Juan River]] Seven Year Research Program, prepared by the SJRRIP Biology Committee for the Coordination Committee (dated 9 April 1996). The program studies recovery of endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) within the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] framework. The core FY96 request totals **$706,500**; including BIA-funded [[Geomorphology]] and [[Habitat Modeling]] work ($592,840), the grand total is **$1,299,340**. The plan covers adult monitoring, stocking, early life history, larval drift, secondary channels, nonnative species, the lower river and [[Lake Powell]] inflow, contaminants, fish health, data integration and [[Peer Review]].

## Background & Description

The San Juan River fish fauna was documented before 1991 as eight native species, including Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker and [[Roundtail Chub]]. Since 1991, seventeen intensive electrofishing surveys (1991–1995) have refined baseline distribution and abundance data. Native fish are a focus, but the document equally addresses introduced species such as [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Red Shiner]] and [[Fathead Minnow]].

Key species background:

- **Colorado squawfish:** 17 collected and PIT-tagged; 13 of these radio-tagged. Spawning in the San Juan was estimated by [[Platania]] (1990) to occur in July and August.
- **Roundtail chub:** 16 collected; 11 PIT-tagged.
- **Razorback sucker:** no wild fish collected in the surveys; 27 experimentally stocked fish recaptured to date.

Major management events are the 1988 formation of a 25–30 foot waterfall at the river mouth, which blocked upstream invasion by lentic predators, and its submersion when Lake Powell neared full pool in summer 1995.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow dependence:** Spring runoff appears to "reset" secondary channel fish communities. Native fishes dominate during runoff; nonnative species, especially red shiner and fathead minnow, dominate by late summer and early autumn. Summer releases from [[Navajo Dam]] were raised to 800 cfs in 1995 (from the "normal" 500 cfs) to test this effect.
- **Nursery habitat:** Low-velocity backwaters and embayments are considered essential for age-0 Colorado squawfish. No young squawfish had been collected in secondary channels as of the document. Young roundtail chub were, however, found there.
- **Fish passage:** Instream diversions, including the [[Cudei Diversion]] (RM 142.0), may impede movement. One radio-tagged squawfish was observed about 100 yards above Cudei in summer 1994, but no later contact above the structure was made.
- **Invasive predators:** After the 1995 barrier loss, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, striped bass, walleye, threadfin shad and green sunfish were found throughout the lower river (RM 52–2.9).
- **Contaminants:** Limited analyses found selenium and other inorganics and organics in water, sediment and tissue at potentially harmful concentrations, with unknown biological significance.
- **Water quality context:** Shiprock water chemistry used for toxicity test design includes hardness 150 mg/L, alkalinity 89 mg/L, Ca 43 mg/L, Mg 9.2 mg/L, Na 22 mg/L, Cl 5.9 mg/L and SO₄ 89 mg/L.

**Major project findings and plans:**

- **[[Adult Monitoring and Radio Telemetry]]** ($43,500, USFWS): Five 1996 sampling trips will assess community response to research flows and the spread of lentic predators. Stomachs of predators will be analyzed. The December 1995 helicopter tracking flight located no radio-tagged fish, and further flights were cancelled. Ground tracking will continue.
- **[[Experimental Stocking of Razorback Sucker]]** ($35,300): Stocking sites lie between Shiprock and Bluff (RM 136.6, 117.5, 79.6) plus Hogback Diversion (RM 158.5). Stocked fish included 30 radio-tagged fish (1994) and 16 more at Hogback (1995), with 656 additional PIT-tagged fish. The study will test whether hatchery fish can lead researchers to wild fish and whether augmentation is feasible.
- **[[Early Life Stage: Nursery Habitat Requirements]]** ($90,000, UDWR): Plans include stocking 100,000 age-0 squawfish from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (20,000 per each of four reaches), tetracycline-marked, 30–50 mm, released in September 1996, with retention monitored and overwinter survival checked in spring 1997.
- **[[Larval Drift]] Netting** ($30,000, UNM): Daily drift samples at Four Corners and Mexican Hat from mid-to-late June, to define spawning timing and reproductive success relative to the hydrograph.
- **[[Secondary Channel Community Dynamics]]** ($88,000, NMDGF): Monitoring will continue at permanent sites at RM 134.9 and 128.6, with evaluation of mechanical modification of the RM 128.6 inlet.
- **[[Nonnative Species Interactions]]** ($50,000, USFWS): Channel catfish radio telemetry (18 fish across three reaches) and food habits studies will address predation and competition.
- **Lower San Juan River/Lake Powell Inflow** ($33,700): Monitoring of squawfish, razorback sucker and invading nonnative species, coordinated with NPS and the Utah Lake Powell fish inflow study.
- **[[Biological Effects]]** ($110,000 after a $10,000 redirect): A 180-day chronic test on adult squawfish examines selenium and inorganic mixtures on reproduction, followed by 30-day larval exposure.
- **[[Fish Health]] Studies** ($10,000): Baseline health surveys using [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] as possible bioindicators.
- **[[Peer Review]]** ($5,000, panel to be determined): Independent review of research objectives ahead of 1998 flow recommendations.
- **[[Fisheries Database]]** ($7,500): Compile all fisheries data into a single database compatible with contaminant and habitat datasets.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish Habitat Use]]** ($20,000): Three weeks of summer radio tracking with emphasis on locating spawning sites.

**BIA-funded physical habitat work (NIIP, $592,840):** Geomorphic characterization ($21,600), channel dynamics ($191,400), habitat mapping ($215,500), flow/habitat modeling ($83,800), river operations modeling ($9,840), water temperature monitoring at nine locations ($5,300) and a GIS-based integrated database ($65,400).

**Administrative conclusions:** The Biology Committee chose to move toward detailed data integration, with subcommittees working toward 1998 flow recommendations, and to convert the Integration Report into an annual Summary Report under adaptive management. Some work elements (for example, aerial tracking and river operations modeling) were discontinued or reduced based on earlier results.
