## Executive Summary

This 1986 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum outlines a proposal to utilize ponds at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] as grow-out and reintroduction habitat for five native Southwestern fish species currently reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Spring surveys of over 50 refuge ponds found only exotic species present ([[Fathead Minnow]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Largemouth Bass]]), indicating that despite exotic presence, conditions may also support native fish. The plan proposes reclaiming 6-9 ponds to rear three "big-river" endangered species ([[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]]) as grow-out stock prior to wild reintroduction, and two small-bodied endangered species ([[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]]) intended to establish additional refuge populations and serve as biological mosquito control. The memo also flags drainage-connectivity concerns relevant to [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the Endangered Species Act.

## Background & Description

The source document is administrative/management-focused and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions. Limited biological characterizations include:

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** and **[[Desert Pupfish]]**: described as small-bodied, with an "annual" fish life-history strategy typical of ephemeral Sonoran Desert aquatic habitats.
- **Big-river species** ([[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]]): implicitly larger-bodied fish requiring extended grow-out time (2-3 years) to reach "adult or subadult size" suitable for reintroduction stocking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historic range/habitat**: Topminnows and pupfish historically inhabited springs, small streams, and sloughs along major rivers of the Sonoran Desert region.
- **Current status**: Severely range-restricted — Gila Topminnow persists in only a few spring seeps in southern Arizona; Desert Pupfish is "almost completely extirpated" from the U.S.
- **Functional role**: Proposed stocking of topminnows and pupfish in refuge ponds would additionally serve as a mosquito control mechanism.
- **Drainage considerations**: Only the northern two-thirds of Buenos Aires NWR lies within the [[Gila River]] drainage; the southern portion drains into the **Rio de la Concepción**. This distinction is ecologically significant:
  - Escapement into [[Aravaca Creek]] or other Gila River tributaries during flooding would not threaten non-target native fishes (same drainage).
  - Escapement of Gila River-origin fish into the Rio de la Concepción watershed could negatively affect that watershed's distinct native fish fauna.
- **Exotic species baseline**: Prior to project implementation, ponds harbored only introduced/invasive species (fathead minnow, black/yellow bullhead, mosquitofish, largemouth bass), which would need to be eliminated ("reclaimed") before native fish stocking.
- Reference is made to a survey by [[W.L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University) cataloging 31 refuge tanks containing fish or salamanders, intended to guide pond selection.

## Key Findings

- **Survey scope**: Over 50 ponds at Buenos Aires NWR surveyed in Spring 1986; five exotic fish species detected, no natives found.
- **Proposed native species for stocking** (all currently held/reared at Dexter NFH):
  1. Razorback Sucker (no federal listing at time of writing)
  2. Colorado Squawfish (Endangered)
  3. Bonytail Chub (Endangered)
  4. Gila Topminnow (Endangered)
  5. Desert Pupfish (Endangered)
- **Rationale for pond use**: Dexter NFH production goals exceed hatchery capacity, particularly for producing adult/subadult-sized fish needed for successful reintroduction; refuge ponds proposed as extension grow-out facilities.
- **Implementation plan**:
  - Initially select 2-3 ponds per "big-river" species (6-9 ponds total).
  - Eliminate existing exotic fish populations via reclamation.
  - Stock fry from Dexter NFH.
  - Monitor 1-2 times/year via Southwest Region SE and FR personnel.
  - Estimated 2-3 years grow-out before fish reach suitable size for release into the wild.
  - Minimal refuge impact anticipated — limited to vehicle/personnel access.
- **Regulatory framing**: No Section 7 consultation impact anticipated, as ponds are characterized as artificial extensions of Dexter NFH rather than habitat reestablishment efforts; artificial nature of habitat also cited to minimize ESA concerns for topminnow/pupfish stocking.
- **Risk mitigation noted**: If ponds dry up, recovery effort for topminnow/pupfish would not be significantly impacted, given multiple pond sites proposed.
- **Timeline urgency**: Goal to begin pond reclamation quickly to allow stocking before fish growth halts due to winter temperatures (October–November).
- **Interagency coordination**: Plan involves collaboration between Fish and Wildlife Service (Endangered Species, Fishery Resources), Dexter NFH, refuge staff, and outside expert [[W.L. Minckley]] (ASU).
- **Contingent on Master Planning**: Final pond selection and stocking timeline dependent on completion of the refuge's Master Plan.
