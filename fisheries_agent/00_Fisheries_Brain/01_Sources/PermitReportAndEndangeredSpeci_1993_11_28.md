## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory permit report and renewal request submitted by W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, summarizing 1993 activities and requesting 1994 authorization under Endangered Species Permit PRT-676811. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research findings, the text catalogs research and conservation activities involving federally listed endangered fishes of the American Southwest, including capture-release studies, propagation for research, and artificial spawning programs. It also lists specific take quotas requested for 12 taxa and identifies qualified personnel authorized to conduct this work. The document is significant as an administrative record documenting the scale and scope of conservation-driven research on some of North America's most imperiled freshwater fishes, including [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide morphological descriptions of the species involved. It is a permit/administrative report rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study. Morphological differentiation is implied only through the use of formal taxonomic nomenclature and subspecific designations (e.g., distinguishing *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* [[Gila Topminnow]] from *P. o. sonoriensis* [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and *Cyprinodon macularius macularius* [[Colorado Pupfish]] from *C. m. eremus* [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]), indicating recognized subspecies-level variation within these desert fish lineages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All taxa listed inhabit freshwater systems of the arid American Southwest, spanning multiple isolated drainage basins:

- **Colorado River Basin**: [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Ptychocheilus lucius), [[Virgin Chub]] (a Virgin River endemic)
- **Gila River Basin**: [[Gila Trout]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Woundfin]] (Plagopterus argentissimus)
- **Yaqui River Basin (Sonora/Arizona border region)**: [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]]
- **Desert spring/oasis habitats**: [[Colorado Pupfish]] and [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]], both restricted to isolated desert spring systems

These species occupy a range of niches from large-river mainstem habitats (razorback sucker, squawfish, humpback chub — adapted to historically turbid, swift Colorado River conditions) to small, isolated spring and headwater stream habitats (pupfishes, topminnows, Gila trout) characteristic of fragmented desert aquatic refugia. Many of these taxa are relict populations whose ranges have been drastically reduced by damming, water diversion, and introduction of non-native competitors/predators, driving their federal endangered status and necessitating permitted research and captive propagation efforts such as those described here (e.g., [[Gila Topminnow]] propagation for research purposes, artificial spawning of [[Razorback Sucker]] larvae).

## Key Findings

- **Permit scope**: Activities were conducted under USFWS Endangered Species Permit/Subpermit PRT-676811 for calendar year 1993, with a formal request for 1994 renewal and expanded taxa/personnel authorization.
- **Reported 1993 activities**:
  - Capture and release (or take) of *Gila cypha*, *Gila elegans*, and *Xyrauchen texanus* by Paul Marsh (Center for Environmental Studies).
  - Take of *Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis* (Gila Topminnow) for propagation/research by W. L. Minckley.
  - Artificial spawning and larval take of Razorback Sucker for Ph.D. dissertation research by Michael Horn (subsequently employed by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation).
- **1994 take request quotas** (per locality unless noted):
  - Gila Trout — 5
  - Colorado Squawfish — 5
  - Bonytail Chub — 10
  - Virgin Chub — 20 (1993 and 1994 year-classes each)
  - Humpback Chub — 10
  - Yaqui Chub — 20
  - Woundfin — 5
  - Razorback Sucker — 10 (juvenile/adult), 100 (larvae), unlimited fertilized ova
  - Colorado (Desert) Pupfish — 10
  - Quitobaquito Pupfish — 20
  - Gila Topminnow — 20
  - Yaqui Topminnow — 20
- **Personnel structure**: Requested designees included four faculty/academic professionals (Douglas, Dowling, Marsh, Minckley) and seven graduate student/research assistants (Knowles, Oakey, Reimus, Schaeffer, Tibbets, Timmons, Velasco), reflecting a substantial, multi-investigator research program based at [[Arizona State University]].
- **Compliance and coordination**: Threatened species activities were separately reported to Arizona and New Mexico Game and Fish Departments, indicating layered federal–state regulatory compliance for [[Endangered Species Act]]-listed and state-protected taxa.
- **Research continuity concern**: The permit holder emphasized that ongoing studies, including one pending National Science Foundation grant, were highly sensitive to permitting delays, underscoring the operational importance of timely endangered species permitting for long-term conservation research programs.
