## Executive Summary
This record documents a **voucher specimen collection and otolith age-validation study** of 26 [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), an endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River Basin. Specimens originated from **Lake Mohave** stock, were reared/held at **Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery** and **Dexter National Fish Hatchery**, and were ultimately donated to the ASU Department of Zoology Collection of Ichthyology in January 1997. The core scientific purpose of the dataset is to compare **otolith-derived age estimates** against **known/actual ages** (based on year-class and hatchery records) to evaluate the reliability of otolith aging techniques in this species — a critical tool for long-term population and recovery monitoring of a federally listed fish.

## Background & Description
Morphometric data recorded per individual include:
- **Standard Length (SL):** ranging from ~360 mm to ~462 mm
- **Total Length (TL):** ranging from ~450 mm to ~545 mm (some values calculated from SL using a standard conversion, denoted with *)
- **Weight:** ranging from ~709 g to ~1729 g
- **Sex:** Both males (M) and females (F) represented
- **Individual identifiers:** PIT tag numbers and unique hexadecimal ID/Number codes (e.g., 7F7E2D6E2F) used for specimen tracking

Specimens underwent multiple freeze-thaw cycles prior to formalin preservation, reflecting reuse across several prior studies (tissue sampling, genetics) before final archiving.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
[[Bonytail]] is a large-river cyprinid historically native to the mainstem Colorado River and its major tributaries. It is now largely dependent on **refuge populations and hatchery propagation programs** due to severe historical declines linked to:
- Habitat alteration (impoundment, e.g., **Lake Mohave**)
- Predation and competition from non-native fishes
- Flow regulation reducing spawning/rearing habitat

The specimens in this dataset represent a managed conservation lineage: wild/lake-origin fish from **Lake Mohave**, translocated through **Willow Beach** and **Dexter National Fish Hatchery** — a pattern typical of ex-situ conservation for [[Colorado River Basin]] endemic fishes such as [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Humpback Chub]].

## Key Findings
- **26 Gila elegans specimens** were catalogued as voucher material at ASU, with full chain-of-custody documentation (collection, hatchery transfers, tissue/otolith removal, preservation).
- **Otolith extraction** was performed by W.L. Minckley (ASU); tissue sampling for genetic analysis was conducted by Tom Dowling (ASU).
- The dataset provides paired **1st, 2nd, and 3rd age estimates** per otolith reading alongside an **age range** and **mean age estimate**, benchmarked against **known year-class data** (year hatched vs. year died) — enabling direct validation of [[Otolith Aging]] accuracy.
- Estimated ages across the sample generally ranged from **6 to 15 years**, with year classes spanning **1980–1986** and mortality/collection years clustered in **1992–1995**.
- Multiple researchers and institutions (Buddy Jensen – Dexter NFH; W.L. Minckley and Paul Marsh – ASU; Tom Dowling – ASU genetics) collaborated on different analytical components (otolith aging, genetic identity, growth/tissue studies), indicating this dataset supported a **multi-disciplinary bonytail conservation research effort**.
- The repeated freeze-thaw handling and multi-year custody transfers highlight practical challenges in maintaining specimen integrity for long-term age-validation studies in **endangered species research**.
