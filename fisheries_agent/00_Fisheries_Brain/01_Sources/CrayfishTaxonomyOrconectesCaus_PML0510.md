# Crayfish Taxonomy: Orconectes causeyi, O. virilis, and O. nais Synonymy Dispute

#astacology #freshwater-biology #orconectes

## Executive Summary

This 1996 email from [[Keith Crandall]] (Brigham Young University) to a researcher at Arizona State University answers a question about the validity of *[[Orconectes Causeyi]]*, a crayfish. Crandall reports that most astacologists do not regard it as a valid species. He traces a confusion in the original description, in which a specimen collection was apparently mixed up and [[Orconectes Deanae]] was described under the wrong name. He also outlines an in-progress genetic study that he expects will show *O. causeyi*, *[[Orconectes Virilis]]*, and *[[Orconectes Nais]]* are a single species.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence:** Informal email, dated Thursday 5 September 1996, sent from Keith Crandall to a recipient referred to as "Terry" (OCR rendering "Teny"/"Tenil"). The sender's signature and address point to Brigham Young University.
- **Taxon in focus:** Crayfish of the genus *Orconectes*. No family, morphology, or habitat description is given, so those fields are recorded as N/A.
- **Nomenclatural history (as told by Crandall):**
  - [[Hobbs]] initially described a crayfish as a new species, which was the wrong animal.
  - The mix-up arose when [[Jester]] sent Hobbs his *O. causeyi* collection, which unknowingly included *O. deanae*.
  - Hobbs then described *O. deanae* as new and believed he was describing *O. causeyi*. Crandall says he received this account from Hobbs himself.
- **Geographic range:** Crandall states that *O. causeyi* is known only from New Mexico, and that folk reports of the species in Texas, Kansas, and elsewhere more likely refer to *O. nais*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The email does not give habitat, water chemistry, population counts, or community data. The ecological content is limited to:

- **Geographic distribution:** New Mexico (type locality for *O. causeyi*), with *O. nais* reported in Texas and Kansas.
- **Regional notes:** Crandall remarks that Flagstaff and the Wasatch Front have relatively few crayfish species, and that Arizona State University is not a major centre for astacological research.
- **Comparative taxa:** *[[Orconectes Medius]]* and *[[Orconectes Luteus]]* are cited as examples of species showing population-level and subpopulation-level genetic variation, which contrasts with the near-identity of the *O. causeyi* and *O. virilis* sequences.

## Key Findings

1. **Validity questioned:** Most astacologists reportedly do not consider *O. causeyi* a valid species.
2. **Range issue:** *O. causeyi* is restricted to New Mexico. Reports from Texas and Kansas probably refer to *O. nais*.
3. **Misidentification history:** A collection mix-up led to *O. deanae* being described under the name intended for *O. causeyi*.
4. **Preliminary mtDNA result:** Mitochondrial DNA sequences from *O. causeyi* (type locality, New Mexico) and *O. virilis* (localities listed as "MO" and "MI") are nearly identical for the same gene region.
5. **Benchmark comparison:** Within other species (*O. medius*, *O. luteus*), the same gene region shows population-level variation, so Crandall reads the lack of variation as evidence that these taxa are conspecific.
6. **Allozyme support:** [[Jim Fetzner]], a graduate student, has allozyme data suggesting *O. virilis* and *O. nais* are the same species. No sample sizes are given in the email.
7. **Planned work:** Allozyme data for *O. causeyi* and sequence data for *O. nais* are still to be obtained. Crandall plans a joint paper, which he expects will conclude that *O. virilis*, *O. nais*, and *O. causeyi* are one species.
8. **Methods referenced:** [[Mitochondrial DNA]] sequencing, [[Allozyme Analysis]], and comparison with [[Type Locality]] material.
