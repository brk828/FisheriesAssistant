# Chromosomes of the Characeae of the Woods Hole (Massachusetts) Region

#characeae #phycology #freshwater-biology

## Executive Summary

Tindall and Sawa (1964, *American Journal of Botany*, Univ. of Louisville) report chromosome counts for the [[Characeae]] (stoneworts) of the [[Woods Hole Region]]. Material came from 35 collections at 21 populations, representing 10 species in 2 genera, [[Nitella]] and [[Chara]]. Haploid numbers ranged from 9 to 42. The authors argue that Nitella transilis is an established polyploid of N. tenuissima, and they discuss how chromosome numbers in these haplobiontic plants bear on hybridization, polyploidy and sexuality.

## Background & Description

The Characeae are haploid, haplobiontic green algae. Earlier cytological work showed distinct series of chromosome numbers across the family. Proposed basic numbers include 7 for Chara (Moutschen et al., 1956; Gillet, 1959), 6 for Nitella (Gillet, 1959; Imahori and Kato, 1961) and 9 for some Nitella sections (Hotchkiss, 1963).

Wood (1949a) compiled the regional flora of the Woods Hole area. The present study covers all of Wood's species and adds Nitella gracilis, which the authors treat as a new record for the region, plus N. tenuissima and Chara globularis. Nomenclature follows Wood (1962), whose revision combined many earlier species into broader taxa (see [[Wood 1962 Taxonomic Revision]]).

**Methods.** Collections were made in summer 1962 (D. R. Tindall and P. Manco) and summer 1963 (T. Sawa). Material was fixed in absolute ethanol and glacial acetic acid (3:1). Chromosomes were observed in young antheridial filaments, or in young vegetative tips for [[Chara Canescens]]. Preparations used aceto-carmine or aceto-orcein squashes, with [[Feulgen Staining]] for some species. All haploid counts were confirmed at anaphase. Voucher specimens and slides are deposited at the University of Louisville and the Marine Biological Laboratory.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occur in the mainland Woods Hole region, in ponds and streams such as Cuffs Pond, Ashumet Pond, Coonamessett River, Westwood Pond, Little Pond and Chara Pond. Localities are listed per species in the original paper. The authors note that differences between forms that look similar may reflect ecological conditions, though this is not tested.

The authors' main interpretive framework is the haploid life cycle. In haplobiontic plants, the body is a direct expression of the genotype, with no genetic dominance or recessiveness. Only plants from balanced meiosis are likely to survive, so chromosome numbers cluster in multiples of 6 in Nitella and 7 in Chara.

## Key Findings

**Chromosome counts by species (haploid n):**

| Species | n | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [[Nitella Flexilis]] | 12 | Single collection used (DT and PM 62-8-3-2); other plants too old or sterile |
| [[Nitella Megacarpa]] | 18 and 36 | Karyotype: 3 sets of 6 chromosomes |
| [[Nitella Morongii]] | 18 | Apparently the only published counts for this species |
| [[Nitella Gracilis]] | 18 | Proposed as 2 similar sets of 9 |
| [[Nitella Tenuissima]] | 9 | Single collection (Morse Pond) |
| [[Nitella Transilis]] | 18 | Exclusively 18 in the examined collections |
| [[Chara Braunii]] | 14 | All collections counted |
| [[Chara Canescens]] | 28 | Apparently parthenogenetic in the study area |
| [[Chara Globularis]] | 28 | Consistent with North American counts |
| [[Chara Delicatula]] | 42 | Separated from C. globularis by classical morphology |

Reported ranges across the study were 9, 12, 18 and 18+36 for Nitella, and 14, 28 and 42 for Chara.

**Principal findings and interpretations:**

- **Nitella flexilis and monoecious forms.** Previous studies consistently report n = 12 for N. flexilis. A monoecious form from the region counted by Dana Griffin (personal communication) had 6 chromosomes. Tindall (1962) found 6 in similar Indiana plants, but these had well-developed antheridia and only abnormal oogonia. The authors link this to the dioecious [[Nitella Opaca]], which shows 6 chromosomes exclusively in North American material (Hotchkiss, 1963, unpublished).
- **Nitella megacarpa.** Counts of 18 and 36 both occurred, with marked variation in frequency among populations. No plant with base number 36 was found, since every plant had at least a few cells with 18 chromosomes. The authors cannot yet explain this variation.
- **Nitella transilis as a polyploid.** N. transilis and N. tenuissima were treated as one species by Wood (1962). The authors observed n = 9 in N. tenuissima and n = 18 in N. transilis, and N. transilis has larger cells and fewer cells per shoot. They propose that N. transilis is a [[Polyploidy]] derivative of N. tenuissima. Its habit also differs: N. tenuissima is lax and prostrate, while N. transilis is rigid and erect.
- **Chara canescens and parthenogenesis.** Plants were examined in vegetative tissue because male gametangia were absent. The base number is 28, and there is apparently a chromosome doubling during oogonial development that makes fertilization unnecessary. The authors state this is an exceptional case, and that the monoecious relative *C. evoluta* (synonymized with C. canescens by Wood, 1962) has n = 56 (Hotchkiss, unpublished).
- **Chara globularis and C. delicatula.** Specimens earlier called C. delicatula included a range of forms, and Wood (1962) placed them all under C. globularis. Counts of 28 agree with North American data for C. globularis. Karling (1926) reported n ≈ 40 for C. delicatula, which matches the authors' count of 42. European *C. aspera* has n = 14, as does North American material.
- **Monoecy versus dioecy.** This distinction was problematic for Nitella flexilis, Chara canescens and C. globularis. Consistent chromosome differences support separating these species from their counterparts.

**Interpretive conclusions from the discussion:**

- Polyploidy has been important in speciation in the Characeae. The authors suggest autopolyploidy is probably limited to the 6 and 12 relationship in Nitella.
- Plants with n = 9 may derive from triploid sporophytes. The authors consider this number unstable in general, but it appears fixed in N. tenuissima.
- Nitella megacarpa's 3 sets of 6 chromosomes may indicate [[Hybridization]] between a basic-6 plant and an [[Autopolyploidy]] 12 plant. The n = 18 number in many Nitella species may represent the gametic number of an [[Allopolyploidy]] sporophyte. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated result.
- Ploidy levels of 36 and 48 appear unsuccessful as base numbers, at least in this section of Nitella.
- Meiosis has not been satisfactorily demonstrated in the Characeae, so the existence and taxonomic importance of hybrids and allopolyploids can only be assumed. Karyotypic analysis is proposed as a way to test these assumptions.
