# Parasites of Oneida Lake Fishes, Part II: New Species and Taxonomic Revisions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #heterophyidae

## Executive Summary

This 1932 bulletin article from the [[Roosevelt Wild Life Forest Experiment Station]] (New York State College of Forestry, Syracuse) is Part II of a survey of parasitic worms infecting fishes of [[Oneida Lake]], New York. The authors, Justus F. Mueller and Harley J. Van Cleave, describe nine new parasite species (seven trematodes, one cestode, one nematode), redescribe two nematode species of the genus *Spinitectus*, and present a taxonomic synopsis of the trematode family [[Heterophyidae]] in North American freshwater fishes. Their main conclusions are that seven genera previously placed elsewhere belong in the Heterophyidae, with fishes as normal hosts, and that the genus [[Microphallus]] should be removed from that family and placed in its own family, [[Microphallidae]]. The work is explicitly preliminary: a final report on the biology and ecology of the worm parasites was planned.

## Background & Description

The survey began about four years before publication as part of the station's program. The authors stated that accurate identification of extensive worm collections was a prerequisite for analysing host-parasite relationships, because the literature was scattered and many earlier descriptions were inadequate. Part I (Van Cleave and Mueller, 1932) had described twelve trematode and three nematode species. Summer 1931 collections yielded nine further new species, described here.

The article is organised into four sections:

- **Section 1: New species descriptions.** Nine species, with host records, morphology, comparisons and type depositories (the [[United States National Museum]] and station collections).
- **Section 2: Redescriptions of *Spinitectus*.** Two nematodes that did not match published descriptions, differentiated for the first time.
- **Section 3: Synopsis of Heterophyidae.** Revised family diagnosis, a sixth subfamily added, and seven genera transferred to the family.
- **Section 4: Family relationships of *Microphallus*.** Argument for excluding the genus from the Heterophyidae.

Plates 15 to 25 illustrate the species and the evolutionary chart of heterophyid genera.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fauna described is that of a freshwater lake and its tributaries. Host species named in the text include:

- **[[White Bass]]** (*Lepibema chrysops*): the only host in which members of *Allacanthochasmus* reached full sexual maturity in Oneida Lake.
- **Bullhead** (*Ameiurus nebulosus*): host of *Allocreadium halli* and of *Acetodextra amiuri* in the swim bladder.
- **[[Common Sucker]]** (*Catostomus commersonii*): host of both *Triganodistomum* species and of *Hepaticola bakeri*.
- **[[Mud Minnow]]** (*Umbra limi*): host of *Neascus grandis* (cysts in mesenteries) and *Gyrodactylus cylindriformis* (skin and fins).
- **[[Pirate Perch]]** (*Percopsis omiscomaycus*): host of *Bothriocephalus formosus*, collected from deep waters by beam trawl.
- **[[Common Carp]]** (*Cyprinus carpio*): host of *Dactylogyrus extensus* on the gills.

The authors noted that the carp in Oneida Lake carried a gill-parasite fauna wholly distinct from that found on the same host in European waters. They also argued that the economic status of gill parasites such as *Gyrodactylus* and *Dactylogyrus* can only be assessed once American species are properly described, since these forms can be economically important in culture conditions.

Life-cycle evidence was summarised for heterophyids. Metacercariae of avian and mammalian heterophyids encyst in fish flesh after a parthenogenetic generation in a snail. Encysted young of *Allacanthochasmus* were found in fishes that are not normal adult hosts, and intact cysts and excysted worms were recovered from the digestive tract of the same fish. The authors inferred that the definitive fish host acquires its heterophyids through an alternative fish host.

## Key Findings

**New species (Section 1)**

- ***Allacanthochasmus artus*** (Heterophyidae): found in the white bass intestine. Distinguished from *A. varius* by slender body, cylindrical gonotyl bearing five root-like processes, absence of transverse uterine loops in the posterior third, and oral spines with a sharply set-off tip. Of roughly two thousand *Allacanthochasmus* specimens examined, *A. artus* occurred in a ratio of about one to twenty-five *A. varius*. The holotype of *A. varius* contained no *A. artus*, which was also found in Lake Erie and Lake Pokegama, Wisconsin, at similar ratios.
- ***Allocreadium halli*** (Allocreadiidae): found in two bullheads from the harbor at Cleveland, New York, with very light infestations. Named for Dr. Maurice C. Hall; closest to *A. ictaluri*.
- ***Triganodistomum attenuatum*** (Allocreadiidae): 3.5 mm long; found in the common sucker. Distinguished from *T. translucens* by body form, cirrus relations and vitellarial extent.
- ***Triganodistomum simeri*** (Allocreadiidae): found in two common suckers; one bore a single worm, the other ten. Named for Dr. P. H. Simer, author of the genus.
- ***Neascus grandis*** (Strigeidae): four specimens from mesenteric cysts of the mud minnow in a branch of Chittenango Creek near Bridgeport, New York.
- ***Gyrodactylus cylindriformis*** (Gyrodactylidae): found only on the mud minnow; judged specific to this host. Notable for an unusually large pharynx; no eyes.
- ***Dactylogyrus extensus*** (Dactylogyridae): found in two carp gill samples; the first North American species of the genus that can be described from adequate material, since earlier North American records lacked descriptions.
- ***Bothriocephalus formosus*** (Bothriocephalidae): small tapeworms (rarely over 30 mm) in pirate perch intestine and ceca. Differs from *B. cuspidatus* (the only other exclusively freshwater North American species) in lacking a terminal disc.
- ***Hepaticola bakeri*** (Trichinellidae, nematode): females 7 to 8 mm, males 4 to 4.5 mm. Recorded from the intestine of tullibee, golden shiner and common sucker. Described as the first representative of the genus from the intestine of a freshwater fish. Named for Dr. Hugh P. Baker, Dean of the College.

**Redescriptions of *Spinitectus* (Section 2)**

- *Spinitectus gracilis* (Ward and Magath): the original description's claim that papillae are absent was shown to be unreliable; papillae are present on the caudal alae. Hosts include the northern pike, bullhead and tullibee, among others.
- *Spinitectus carolini* Holl: the authors concluded that Holl's description mixed material of *S. gracilis* with his new species. Confirmed characters include a long oral vestibule and pre-anal cleats, which the original had reportedly lacked.

**Heterophyidae revision (Section 3)**

- Seven genera from North American fishes (*Vietosoma*, *Acetodextra*, *Allacanthochasmus*, *Neochasmus*, *Cryptogonimus*, *Caecincola*, *Centrovarium*) are placed in the Heterophyidae. Fishes are proposed as normal hosts for the family; the authors found no evidence these genera normally occur in birds or mammals.
- The family diagnosis was emended. The cirrus pouch is absent, and the gonotyl and uterine extent are described as variable; uterine extent is no longer used as a limiting character.
- A sixth subfamily was recognised in the synopsis. The Heterophyinae were emended to include fish hosts, and Cryptogoniminae Osborn was reduced to synonymy with Heterophyinae. Neochasminae was retained for *Neochasmus* and *Allacanthochasmus*.
- A "progressive series" of gonotyl reduction was proposed, running from *Allacanthochasmus artus* through *A. varius* to *Neochasmus umbellus*, and a parallel series of ovarian fragmentation into follicles.

**Microphallus (Section 4)**

- *Microphallus* is excluded from the Heterophyidae. Its genital ducts open independently into a shallow genital sinus, unlike the single common genital pore of the Heterophyidae of fishes, and it lacks the gonotyl found in that family. Following Travassos (1920), the authors recognise the family Microphallidae.
- Several genera previously linked to *Microphallus* (*Levinseniella*, *Spelotrema*, *Spelophallus*, *Monocaecum*, *Maritrema*) are to be excluded from the Microphallidae until their morphology is shown to be compatible.

**Overall conclusions**

- The Oneida Lake parasite fauna is richer and more distinct than prior records indicated; many earlier identifications were incomplete.
- Family-level assignments of several fish trematode genera were corrected, and the authors called for a restudy of the group of genera assumed to be related to *Microphallus*.
