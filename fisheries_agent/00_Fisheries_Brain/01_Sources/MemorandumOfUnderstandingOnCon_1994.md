> **Note on scope:** This source is not primarily an ichthyological research paper. It is a U.S. federal interagency **Memorandum of Understanding (94-SMU-058)** establishing a policy framework for conserving species "tending toward federal listing" under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It names [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] as a signatory (relevant to fish/marine species stewardship generally), but the only species-specific case study attached is a **Conservation Agreement for the Cow Knob Salamander** (*Plethodon punctatus*), an amphibian, not a fish. The note below faithfully reflects this content rather than forcing a fish-centric frame.

## Executive Summary

This 1994 MOU (94-SMU-058) links the [[USDA Forest Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[National Park Service]], and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] — later joined by the [[International Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies]] (representing all 50 U.S. state fish and wildlife agencies) — in a cooperative framework to proactively conserve **candidate, sensitive, and proposed species** before they require listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The mechanism is the development of **Habitat Conservation Assessments** leading to **Conservation Agreements**. The only fully worked example provided is for the **Cow Knob Salamander** (*Plethodon punctatus*), a narrow-range endemic amphibian in the George Washington National Forest (Virginia/West Virginia), illustrating the practical application of the MOU's habitat-based conservation approach — a model directly transferable to imperiled fish and aquatic taxa managed under similar interagency frameworks.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Establish a general framework for cooperation among federal (and later state) agencies to conserve species "tending toward federal listing" (i.e., [[Sensitive Species]], [[Candidate Species]], [[Proposed Species]]) through habitat and ecosystem protection, thereby reducing or eliminating the need for formal ESA listing.
- **Key legal driver:** The 1973 [[Endangered Species Act]], which directs federal agencies to conserve threatened/endangered species and the ecosystems they depend on. At the time of drafting, state Natural Heritage Programs identified ~9,000 U.S. plant and animal species as rare, declining, or at risk of extinction.
- **Mechanism:**
  1. **Habitat Conservation Assessment** — a comprehensive, state-of-the-art technical document describing life history, habitat requirements, and management considerations for a species (or species group) across its occupied range on cooperator-managed lands.
  2. **Conservation Agreement** — a formal document, developed from the Assessment, specifying voluntary actions and accountable responsibilities among agencies, Tribes, states, local governments, or private parties, with the explicit objective of lowering listing priority or eliminating the need to list a species.
- **Species category definitions (Attachment A):**
  - **Candidate Species:** May qualify for ESA listing (FWS/NMFS opinion). FWS uses Category 1 (sufficient data to support listing proposals) and Category 2 (insufficient data yet) sub-classifications; NMFS does not subdivide by category.
  - **Proposed Species:** Species formally proposed for listing via Federal Register notice.
  - **Sensitive Species:** Species designated by a Regional Forester or BLM State Director based on significant downward trends in population numbers/density or habitat capability/distribution.
- **Case study species — Cow Knob Salamander (*Plethodon punctatus*):**
  - Endemic to the Shenandoah Mountain Crest area of the George Washington National Forest, straddling Virginia and West Virginia.
  - Also referred to as the "white-spotted salamander."
  - Highest population densities occur in **older-age hardwood forests above 3,000 ft elevation**.
  - A dedicated **Conservation Team** (Virginia Dept. of Game and Inland Fisheries, Virginia Division of Natural Heritage, West Virginia Dept. of Natural Resources, USFWS, USFS) oversees implementation.
  - The species' core range was designated the **Shenandoah Mountain Crest – Special Interest Area (SMC-SIA)**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although *Plethodon punctatus* is not a fish, its Conservation Agreement provides an instructive habitat-management template applicable to sensitive [[Aquatic Ecosystem|aquatic and riparian species]] managed under comparable interagency assessments:

- **Habitat requirements:** Old-growth/mature hardwood forest structure at high elevation; retention of moisture-holding cover objects (logs, rocks, leaf litter) is critical — removal of these is prohibited.
- **Fire management:** Prescribed fire effects on the salamander are largely unknown, but the species is generally absent from drier sites where burns are more common, suggesting compatibility between controlled burns (for rare plant/community management) and salamander conservation on appropriate sites.
- **Pest/disease management:** Preference for biological control over chemical pesticides; introduced control agents must not affect the salamander's prey base. Gypsy moth impacts flagged as a priority research need.
- **Land use restrictions in SMC-SIA:**
  - No timber harvest (classified unsuitable for timber production), with narrow safety-related exceptions.
  - No livestock grazing.
  - No new road construction (habitat fragmentation, edge effects).
  - Mineral leasing restricted to oil/gas and common-variety minerals with controlled surface-use stipulations; other leasable minerals (iron, coal, tin) unavailable.
  - Low-impact recreation (hiking, hunting, photography, non-motorized biking) permitted; OHV use restricted to existing designated areas.
  - Utility/transportation corridors must be routed around the SMC-SIA to avoid habitat fragmentation and population isolation.
  - Vegetation management minimized; herbicide use restricted to invasive species control, avoiding aerial spraying and toxic chemicals.
- **Monitoring:** A long-term population monitoring program was mandated for initiation no later than FY 1995 to evaluate program effectiveness and population stability.
- **Take protection:** Active protection against collection/killing, with narrow allowances for permitted scientific research; captive breeding and educational-display collection discouraged.

## Key Findings

- The MOU formalizes a **proactive, pre-listing conservation strategy** across five federal agencies (later expanded to all 50 state fish and wildlife agencies via IAFWA), intended to reduce reliance on formal ESA listing by addressing threats early through habitat-based agreements.
- **Conservation Agreements are explicitly designed to lower listing priority or eliminate listing need** — a key regulatory tool relevant to any aquatic or fish taxon nearing candidate status.
- The **Cow Knob Salamander Conservation Agreement** stands as the MOU's working template: it operationalizes habitat protection (old-growth forest >3,000 ft), restricts extractive/disturbance land uses (timber, grazing, road-building, mineral development), and institutes a multi-agency **Conservation Team** and long-term monitoring program.
- The document underscores an **ecosystem management approach** intended to extend beyond single-species protection to broader habitat and biodiversity conservation on federally managed lands (national forests, BLM public lands, national parks, wildlife refuges).
- **NMFS's role** (implementing ESA for most anadromous and marine species) signals this framework's applicability to marine/anadromous fish candidate species, even though no specific fish species assessment is included in this document.
- The MOU was set to remain in force through **September 30, 1999**, with annual national-level reviews of program status and joint accomplishment reporting among cooperators.
