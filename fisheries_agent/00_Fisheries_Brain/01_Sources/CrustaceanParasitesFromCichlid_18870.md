# Crustacean Parasites of Tilapia from the Musée Royal de l'Afrique Centrale

#ichthyology #marine-biology #copepoda

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic note by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] of the Freshwater Biological Association reports crustacean gill parasites from cichlid fishes of the genus [[Tilapia]], based on a search of the gills and branchial chambers of all tilapia specimens held in the [[Musée Royal de l'Afrique Centrale]], Tervuren, Belgium. The collection contained only a few parasite species, but it is valuable for its wide geographic coverage. The main taxon is [[Lamproglena Monodi]], a copepod recorded from dozens of localities across the Congo Basin and in the [[Ogowe River]], and in roughly 270 specimens by summing the listed counts. The study also treats an *[[Ergasilus Latus]]*-like copepod and two branchiurans, [[Argulus Africanus]] and [[Argulus Rhipidiophorus]]. The author concludes that the variation seen in *L. monodi* does not justify subspecies.

## Background & Description

Tilapias are of major economic importance in African commercial fisheries and pond culture, which makes information on their parasites useful. Dr. P.L.G. Benoit organised the search, and the material was sent to Fryer for study. Host nomenclature follows a revision by D.F.E. Thys van den Audenaerde. Records are arranged by parasite species and then by locality. Grid coordinates are given where they help locate obscure sites.

**Host species named in the collection** (summarised; the full list is in the source):
- *Tilapia galilaea* (Artedi): the most frequent host, with many large lots (e.g., 133 specimens at Yakoma)
- *T. zillii* Gervais, *T. lepidura* Boulenger, *T. boulengeri* Pellegrin, *T. tholloni* (Sauvage)
- *T. nilotica nilotica* (L.), *T. nilotica upembae* van den Audenaerde, *T. nilotica regani* Poll
- *T. macrochir* Boulenger, *T. sparrmani* A. Smith, *T. rendalli* Boulenger
- *T. variabilis* Boulenger, *T. schwebischii* (Sauvage), *T. cabrae* Boulenger, *T. leucosticta* Trewavas

**Parasite morphology.** *Lamproglena monodi* is highly variable in size and body form. Its abdominal somites range from short and squat (about as wide as long) to elongate and attenuate. The inner branch of each furcal ramus can be retracted, and its setal tips can be swollen or not. Thoracic somites two to four vary in fusion and inflation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The localities span the Congo River system from its mouth at Boma and Zambi to the upper Uele, Lomami, Lualaba, Lulua and Luapula tributaries, and to the Ruzizi, which links Lakes Kivu and Tanganyika. Other sites include [[Lake Victoria]], [[Lake Edward]], [[Lake Mweru]] and the [[Ogowe River]] in Gabon. Fryer notes a Nigerian record from the [[Niger River]] system (Birnin Kebbi, River Sokoto).

Some host records are notable for habitat. All argulid-infected branchial regions came from lake-dwelling fishes, while the hosts from rivers were not parasitised by argulids. The *Argulus rhipidiophorus* records from Lake Edward extend its known range into the Edward-George Basin. The author links this to the lakes that drain into, or were formerly connected with, the upper [[Nile River]].

## Key Findings

**1. Wide distribution of *Lamproglena monodi*.** The species was previously known from the Nile, Niger, Congo and Zambezi systems, but Congo records were few. The collection establishes it throughout the Congo system, from near the mouth to the upper reaches of several tributaries. It is also recorded from the Ogowe River for the first time, and the author expects it to be widely dispersed in the Niger system.

**2. Host specificity.** *L. monodi* appears to parasitise cichlids only. Fryer also cites Capart (1944) for records on [[Haplochromis]] and [[Hemichromis]].

**3. Size variation.** The largest specimens, about 4.0 mm (the largest recorded to date), are twice the length of the smallest, about 2.0 mm. Capart's smallest record was 1.2 mm, or about 30% of the largest length in this collection. Size correlates partly with abdominal proportions.

**4. Intraspecific variation, not subspecies.** Variation is marked within populations. Three specimens from one Kapunda (Luapula) population ranged from clearly separate to nearly confluent abdominal somites. Fryer concludes that there are no grounds for subspecies, and that within-population variation argues against infraspecific names.

**5. *Ergasilus* material.** Four damaged specimens from Kirona near the Congo mouth, on *T. cabrae*, closely resemble *E. latus*, which was previously known only from Lake Rudolf on two tilapia species. The differences are small, but the material is too incomplete to name it definitively, so the author suspends judgement pending more specimens.

**6. Branchiurans.** *Argulus africanus* was recorded from Lake Victoria on *T. variabilis*. *Argulus rhipidiophorus* was recorded from Lake Edward on *T. nilotica regani* and *T. leucosticta*. The Lake Edward record is new for the Edward-George Basin, and the author plans a fuller treatment of argulid distribution.

**7. Method note.** The study is a catalogue of records for the Tervuren collection, not a field survey. The sample sizes reflect museum lots, and the author gives no abundance or prevalence statistics beyond specimen counts.
