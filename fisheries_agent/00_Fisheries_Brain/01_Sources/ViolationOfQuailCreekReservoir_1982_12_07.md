## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents a legal and scientific analysis alleging that the Washington County Water Conservancy District (WCWCD) has violated the 1982 Biological Opinion (B.O.) governing the [[Quail Creek Reservoir Project]] on the Virgin River. The B.O. issued a "no jeopardy" finding for the endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) contingent on maintaining a minimum instream flow of **86 cubic feet per second (cfs)** at the Quail Creek Diversion, intended to remain in the Virgin River's "upper reach" (La Verkin Springs to Washington Fields Diversion). The memo argues that WCWCD instead measures/satisfies the 86 cfs requirement far downstream at the Washington Fields Diversion, effectively diverting nearly all natural upper-reach flow through the Quail Creek pipeline system and dewatering critical woundfin and [[Virgin River Chub]] habitat. Multiple lines of evidence — B.O. text, prior project Biological Opinions, WCWCD's own design documents, and testimony from the scientist (Dr. James Deacon) who authored the underlying jeopardy analysis — support the upper-reach interpretation. Post-construction fish surveys (1990, 1997) document a severe, sustained collapse of woundfin populations in the affected reach, coinciding with the change in diversion operations.

## Background & Description

**Target taxa:**
- **Woundfin** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) — small cyprinid minnow, listed as federally endangered in 1970; endemic to the Virgin River system and historically the Gila River drainage.
- **Virgin River Chub** (*Gila seminuda*) — proposed for endangered listing in 1978, along with critical habitat in the same river reach.

**Project history and instream flow evolution:**
- **[[Dixie Project]]** (Bureau of Reclamation, 1970s precursor) — large-scale Warner Valley dam proposal (93,300 acre-feet reservoir); abandoned due to cost.
- **[[Warner Valley Water Project]]** (WCWCD, 1975–1980) — multiple diversion schedule proposals:
  - 1975 v1: divert up to 150 cfs, bypass only 5 cfs — never implemented.
  - 1975 v2: divert up to 160 cfs, bypass minimum 40 cfs — rejected by FWS (1978 B.O.) due to jeopardy finding for woundfin; FWS recommended **70 cfs (Jul–Oct)** and **110 cfs (rest of year)** at the Hurricane gauging station.
  - 1980 redesign: bypass 40 cfs (Oct–Feb), 90 cfs (Mar–Jun), 80 cfs (Jul–Sep) — again rejected. Joint BLM/FWS/UDWR study recommended **120 cfs (Nov–Jun)** and **80 cfs (Jul–Oct)** at the Hurricane gage as necessary to sustain a viable woundfin population; project ultimately failed when California utilities withdrew support.
- **[[La Verkin Springs Desalinization Project]]** (BOR, 1978) — FWS Biological Opinion recommended **110 cfs (Nov–Jun)** and **70 cfs (Jul–Oct)** at the Hurricane USGS gage, explicitly to protect the reach between La Verkin Springs and the Washington-St. George Diversion. Project never built (cost).
- **[[Quail Creek Reservoir Project]]** (WCWCD, approved 1982–1983; constructed 1985) — final approved B.O. set a **minimum 86 cfs bypass** at the Quail Creek Diversion (or natural flow if lower), explicitly tied to protecting the woundfin in the upper reach. FWS-approved [[FERC]] exemptions for the **Quail Creek Hydroplant** and **[[Pah Tempe Hydroplant]]** (1984–1985) were conditioned on compliance with the same 86 cfs B.O. flow constraint.

**Structural changes from proposal to construction:**
- Proposed diversion structure: 73 ft high, 62 ft long.
- As-built diversion (1985): **96 ft high, 150 ft wide**, capable of carrying **240 cfs** through the pipeline — able to capture nearly all natural upper-reach flow (average annual flow above the diversion ≈ 180 cfs).
- Old Hurricane diversion (pre-Quail Creek): ~15 ft high, allowing overtopping flow; canal only 4 ft deep × 6 ft wide.
- 1995: WCWCD began releasing only ~3 cfs as a "restoration" flow at the diversion itself, while making up the 86 cfs total via return flows discharged near the reservoir (Pah Tempe and Quail Creek powerplant returns), bypassing the upper six-mile reach entirely.
- 1985 WCWCD–Hurricane Canal Company agreement granted WCWCD rights to divert/store Hurricane Canal surplus water (Nov 15–Mar 1), further reducing winter instream flows in the upper reach.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Affected reach:** Virgin River upper reach — from La Verkin Springs/Ash Creek confluence down through the [[Red Cliffs Desert Reserve]] to the Quail Creek Reservoir outflow and onward to the Washington Fields Diversion. This reach was proposed critical habitat for both woundfin and Virgin River chub.
- **Flow-dependence of woundfin:** FWS's 1980 Biological Opinion found flow was strongly life-stage dependent — juveniles/adults require ~120 cfs (Nov–Jun) for overwintering and spawning, and 70–80 cfs (Jul–Oct) for summer/fry survival. Flow reductions below these thresholds were explicitly linked to increased adult winter mortality and reduced spawning success, comparable in severity to a 1-in-30-year drought event but recurring annually under reduced-flow operations.
- **Water temperature** was also flagged (1991 FWS letter to BLM) as a possible contributing factor to woundfin decline, alongside altered flow regimes.
- **Habitat conversion:** The 96-ft diversion structure functions as a de facto dam, backing up the Virgin River for ~1 mile and converting free-flowing riverine habitat into a mainstem impoundment — a structural/hydrological change not analyzed in the original Environmental Assessment.
- **Cumulative regulatory context:** The Woundfin/[[Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team]] (formerly Woundfin Recovery Team) repeatedly identified monitoring/enforcement failures, notably the absence of accurate stream gauges between La Verkin Springs and Quail Creek Reservoir, and malfunctioning gauges at the diversion and USGS stations (noted as far back as 1987 and still unresolved by the time of this memo).

## Key Findings

- **Flow requirement misapplied:** The WCWCD's operational practice of satisfying the 86 cfs requirement at the **Washington Fields Diversion** (downstream of the reservoir) rather than at the **Quail Creek Diversion**/La Verkin Power Plant point contradicts the B.O.'s plain language, the Quail Creek Environmental Assessment, WCWCD's own 1983 Preliminary Design Report, and the direct testimony of Dr. James Deacon (UNLV), the scientist whose analysis underpinned the original no-jeopardy finding.
- **Structural over-capacity:** The as-built diversion (96 ft high, 240 cfs capacity) far exceeds the approved design (73 ft, associated with peak-runoff-only diversion), enabling near-total capture of the Virgin River's ~180 cfs average annual flow and eliminating natural flow through the upper reach except during flood events (>240 cfs).
- **Historical precedent of jeopardy findings:** Two earlier project proposals with comparable or lower diversion volumes (Warner Valley 1975/1980, La Verkin Springs Desalinization) were rejected or heavily conditioned by FWS specifically because reduced upper-reach flows (as low as 40–90 cfs bypass) were found likely to jeopardize the woundfin — undercutting any claim that the current Quail Creek operating regime (~3 cfs direct bypass) is consistent with the scientific basis for a "no jeopardy" finding.
- **Documented population collapse:**
  - 1990 fall survey (Dr. Thom Hardy): lowest woundfin numbers since monitoring began in 1977, with essentially no 1990 year-class recruitment. Site counts: 14 (near Ash Creek), 18 (above Quail Creek), 19 (below Hurricane Bridge), 2 (above Washington Diversion), 4 (below Washington Diversion).
  - 1997 fall survey (UDWR): further decline — 0 (near Ash Creek), 3 (near Quail Creek), 6 (below Hurricane), 18 (above Washington Diversion). Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team again declared populations "critically low."
  - Holden & Zucker (1996) study concluded drought was not a significant driver of the Ash Creek woundfin decline, implicating Quail Creek Reservoir operations as the likely cause.
- **Regulatory response gaps:** FWS and BLM acknowledged in 1991–1992 correspondence that operating conditions had changed and warranted reinitiation of ESA Section 7 consultation, yet as of this memo's writing, critical habitat for woundfin and Virgin River chub had still not been designated despite a federal court order to do so, and no enforcement action had been taken to restore the original 86 cfs upper-reach flow condition.
- **Broader implication:** The memo frames the current Quail Creek operating plan as functionally equivalent to the 1975 Warner Valley proposal that FWS explicitly rejected for jeopardizing the woundfin.
