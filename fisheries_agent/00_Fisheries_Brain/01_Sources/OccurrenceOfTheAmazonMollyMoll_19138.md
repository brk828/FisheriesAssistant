## Executive Summary
This 1958 short communication by Drewry, Delco, and Hubbs documents a significant northward range extension of the [[Amazon Molly]] (*Mollienesia formosa*), a unisexual, gynogenetic fish species. Historically confined to extreme southern Texas (Hidalgo, Willacy, and Cameron counties) and northern Mexico, the species was newly documented in the [[San Marcos River]] near San Marcos, Texas, in October 1958. Given the absence of this species in earlier collections from the same locality, the authors hypothesize that this population represents a recent, successful introduction rather than a natural range expansion, drawing a parallel to the previously documented introduction of the related species *M. latipinna* in the same river system.

## Background & Description
The identification of the San Marcos specimens as *M. formosa* was confirmed through several key morphological and reproductive criteria, distinguishing it from sympatric congeners *M. latipinna* and *M. sphenops*:

- **Dorsal Fin Rays:** 11 rays (the modal count for *M. formosa*).
- **Scale Rows:** 10 scale rows between the dorsal fin origin and the occiput (typical for female *M. formosa*).
- **Body Proportions:** The dorsal fin base is contained 2.5 times within the predorsal length.
- **Coloration:** Presence of lateral spots and other pigmentation patterns consistent with the type population from Palo Alto.
- **Build:** San Marcos specimens exhibited a "slightly heavier build" compared to South Texas populations of *M. formosa*. The authors attribute this phenotypic difference to environmental factors (e.g., improved food availability or colder temperatures during ontogeny) rather than genetic divergence, citing similar findings in [[Gambusia]] species by Hubbs and Springer (1957).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*M. formosa* is a freshwater poeciliid inhabiting river systems in the Gulf Coastal region of Texas and Mexico.

- **Native Range:** South Texas (Hidalgo, Willacy, Cameron counties) south to the vicinity of Vera Cruz, Mexico.
- **New Locality:** San Marcos River, immediately east of the Southland Ice Plant.
- **Sympatric Species:** The genus [[Mollienesia]] (mollies) in this region includes *M. latipinna* and *M. sphenops*, which co-occur but are morphologically distinct.
- **Introduction Vector:** The paper suggests an anthropogenic introduction, noting that the closely related [[Sailfin Molly]] (*M. latipinna*) was similarly introduced to the San Marcos River Basin per Brown (1953).

## Key Findings
1. **Range Extension:** This paper documents the first record of *M. formosa* outside its known native range in southern Texas, specifically in the San Marcos River (Guadalupe River Basin).
2. **Evidence of Introduction:** Because earlier, thorough collections from the San Marcos vicinity did not contain *M. formosa*, the authors conclude the species was likely introduced around 1958 rather than naturally colonizing the area.
3. **Reproductive Biology:** The species is noted as the only known essentially gynogenetic vertebrate in nature at the time of writing. Broods observed in the lab (from a female collected by W. F. Hettler) consisted entirely of female offspring of uniform size, consistent with [[Gynogenesis]] (a form of parthenogenesis requiring sperm from a related species to trigger egg development without genetic contribution).
4. **Rarity of Males:** The report references concurrent research (Hubbs, Drewry, and Warburton, in press) documenting the occurrence of an exceptionally rare natural phenotypic male in this otherwise all-female species.
5. **Taxonomic Confirmation:** Standard meristic counts (dorsal rays, scale rows) and morphometric ratios (dorsal base/predorsal length) were sufficient to confirm species identity in the field, despite minor phenotypic plasticity (heavier build) observed in the new population.
