# Analysis of Larval Razorback Sucker Collections from Lake Mohave, Arizona and Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1996 summary report, prepared by Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]] for the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (Lower Colorado Regional Office), analyzes 153 night-time collections of larval [[Razorback Sucker]] made in [[Lake Mohave]] between 22 January and 31 March 1996. It tests whether [[Lunar Phase]] and collection effort explain larval catch, and it proposes a revised [[Larval Collection Data Sheet]]. The analysis found no meaningful relationship between catch and either lunar phase or effort, so the report recommends area-based collecting guided by recent success rather than timing around the moon.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is an endangered endemic catostomid of the Colorado River system. Lake Mohave, a mainstream Colorado River impoundment on the Arizona-Nevada border, holds the largest remaining stock of the species. The [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) aims to repatriate up to 50,000 adult fish to supplement the declining natural population. Its program has four stages:

- Winter-spring capture of naturally produced larvae in the lake.
- Transfer to [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] and/or [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] facilities for feeding and growth after yolk absorption.
- Stocking for grow-out into backwaters adjacent to Lake Mohave.
- Release into the lake at a nominal size of 30 cm total length.

Larvae are collected at night with underwater lights and fine-meshed aquarium nets. The work is labor intensive. Collectors recorded date, collector, location, effort, larval count, begin and end times, water depth and comments on standard "log in" and "collection record" sheets. The raw field records (Appendix A) and lunar phase tables (Appendix B) are archived with the report, along with [[Sunrise and Moonrise Data]] for January through March 1996.

**Methods.** Forms were checked for completeness and consistency. Entries missing critical data, such as larval count, were removed. Two derived effort measures were calculated:

- **E1** = number of lights × collection time (hours).
- **E2** = number of collectors × collection time.

[[Analysis of Variance]] (ANOVA) and simple linear regression were run on the full data set and on four geographic subsets: Upper Reservoir, [[Arizona Bay]], [[Tequila Cove]] and [[Hog Farm]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Larval catch is shaped by biological and physical factors that were not measured directly. The report suggests that local weather, the behavior of spawning adults, or larval dispersal could explain the capture peaks, but it states that the cause of the peaks is unknown. Fine-scale habitat matters as well: moving a collection site by only a few meters can noticeably raise or lower capture success. The report also notes that fishing conditions (sky cover, temperature, wind, and any other observations) may affect results, either positively or negatively.

The four sampling areas are defined as follows:

- **Upper Reservoir:** sites upstream of Arizona Bay, including the 15 km reach below Hoover Dam, near Great West, Eagle and Red Tail coves.
- **Arizona Bay**, **Tequila Cove**, and **Hog Farm**: named coves and shoreline areas.

## Key Findings

**Lunar phase and timing**
- No correlation was found between larval count and lunar phase.
- Capture success (larvae per unit effort) peaked on 22 January, 12–13 February, and 06–11 March 1996. The report states there is no evidence of periodicity, and the cause of these peaks is unknown.

**Effort**
- Using all 153 records, catch was not meaningfully correlated with time, E1, or E2. Both regressions were significant, but r² was small (0.11 and 0.14). Effort explained little of the variation in catch.
- Subset results were similar. Upper Reservoir (n = 17, r² ≈ 14%), Arizona Bay (n = 40, r² just under 20%) and Tequila Cove (n = 68, r² under 17%) all showed weak relationships.
- Hog Farm (n = 28) had the highest r² in any subset (33%). The report judges this still too little to be predictive.

**Data quality**
- Analysis was limited by incomplete recording. An adequate measure of effort has not yet been developed.
- Total collector count is considered an inappropriate measure. Light count, or light-hours (E1), appears more promising. Collectors were usually assigned to a single light, so adding people did not add capacity.

**Recommendations**
- Do not schedule collections by lunar phase.
- Concentrate effort within each of the four areas, using the previous collection's success to guide where to start.
- If a spot is unproductive after 15 minutes (0.25 hours), move to a nearby or more distant area.
- Continue collecting and analyzing catch data.
- Adopt the revised data sheet (Appendix C). It records date, time (24-hour clock), number of people, number of lights, collection time, larval count, location, and fishing conditions. Every field must be completed, and a new entry should be made whenever the location changes.

**Related planning context**
- The cover letter from Marsh to Thomas A. Burke (dated 31 December 1996) asks Reclamation for help in ensuring that collectors record data carefully and completely.
