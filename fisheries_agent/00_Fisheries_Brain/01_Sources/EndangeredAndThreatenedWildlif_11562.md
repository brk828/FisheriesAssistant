# Endangered and threatened wildlife and plants; Proposed delisting of the Amistad gambusia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #freshwater-ecology #extinction

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1987 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service **Proposed Rule** (Federal Register, Vol. 52, No. 47) recommending the removal of the **Amistad Gambusia** (*[[Gambusia amistadensis]]*) from the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife due to presumed **extinction**. The species was endemic to a single locality, **Goodenough Spring**, a tributary of the Rio Grande in Val Verde County, Texas. Its sole natural habitat was inundated in 1968 by the impoundment of the **Amistad Reservoir**, constructed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Subsequent captive-breeding populations, maintained at the University of Texas and the Dexter National Fish Hatchery (New Mexico), were ultimately lost through hybridization and predation by the closely related and highly invasive **[[Gambusia affinis]]** (western mosquitofish). Exhaustive field surveys in 1979 of all regional springs failed to locate any surviving wild individuals, leading to the formal determination of extinction.

## Background & Description
- **Taxonomic status**: Described as a new species (*n. sp.*) by Dr. Alex Peden in 1973, based on specimens collected in 1968 immediately prior to the inundation of Goodenough Spring.
- **Family**: [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers), same family as the mosquitofish genus *Gambusia*.
- **Taxonomic delay**: The species was not recognized as taxonomically distinct until after reservoir construction had already begun, complicating conservation efforts.
- No detailed morphometric or meristic data are provided in this regulatory text; original description is cited in Peden (1973), *Copeia* 1973(2):210–221.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Type locality / sole habitat**: [[Goodenough Spring]], a spring-fed tributary of the [[Rio Grande]], Val Verde County, Texas — an isolated freshwater spring system, likely with stable thermal/flow conditions typical of endemic spring-dwelling poeciliids (cf. other Texas spring endemics such as [[Gila Chub]] and other *Gambusia* spp.).
- **Endemism**: Highly restricted range — known from a single spring system; extensive surveys by Peden (1973) of spring areas upstream and downstream of the future reservoir found no additional populations.
- **Habitat loss driver**: Permanent flooding by the Amistad Reservoir beginning July 1968; the spring was later found under more than **21.3 meters (70 feet)** of silt-laden water.
- **Biotic threat**: Congeneric interaction with **[[Gambusia affinis]]** (mosquitofish) — a widespread, ecologically aggressive congener known for outcompeting, hybridizing with, and predating upon related *Gambusia* species, ultimately extirpating captive refuge populations.
- **Survey criteria (1979)**: All Texas springs within 50 km (31 mi) of Goodenough Spring with outflow exceeding 10 liters/second (0.353 cfs) were surveyed per Brune (1981) spring inventory — none yielded *G. amistadensis*.

## Key Findings
- **Cause of extirpation in the wild**: Inundation of the type locality by the Amistad Reservoir (impoundment completed/filling begun July 1968).
- **Captive population failure**: All captive stocks (University of Texas; Dexter National Fish Hatchery, NM) were lost through **hybridization with** and **predation by** *Gambusia affinis*, illustrating the extinction risk posed by congeneric invasive species even under ex-situ conservation.
- **Listing history**: Listed as Endangered on April 30, 1980 (45 FR 28721) under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, at which point it survived only in captivity.
- **Extinction determination**: Based on Hubbs & Jensen (1984, *Copeia* 1984(2):529–530), "Extinction of *Gambusia amistadensis*, an endangered fish," and the negative results of the 1979 spring surveys.
- **Regulatory outcome**: Because the species met criteria under 50 CFR 424.11(d)(1) — becoming extinct — the Service proposed delisting rather than continued protection, since no critical habitat was designated and no active management programs existed.
- **Broader significance**: This case is a documented example of **extinction via congeneric hybridization/predation in captivity**, and highlights the vulnerability of highly range-restricted spring-endemic fishes to anthropogenic hydrological modification (reservoir impoundment). Related conceptually to other Texas endemic aquatic taxa threatened by spring/reservoir alteration, such as [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] and [[San Marcos Salamander]] ecosystems.
