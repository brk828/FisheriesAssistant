# Lake Mead Razorback Sucker Studies: 2006-2007 Draft Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen-texanus

## Executive Summary

This draft annual report covers the 11th monitoring season (2006-2007) of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) study in [[Lake Mead]]. The study was initiated in 1996 by the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]] and the Colorado River Commission of Nevada, in cooperation with the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]]. [[BIO-WEST Inc]] designed the study and conducted the fieldwork. Other cooperators included the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the National Park Service, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

The season used sonic telemetry, trammel netting, and larval sampling across three study areas: [[Las Vegas Bay]], [[Echo Bay]], and the [[Muddy River-Virgin River Inflow Area]]. Movement and spawning-location shifts were again documented, and the report concludes that recent recruitment occurred during declining lake elevations, which challenges the existing hypothesis that recruitment is tied to high lake levels.

## Background & Description

The study addresses the habitat use, movements, growth, age structure, and recruitment of the razorback sucker in Lake Mead. Fish were tracked with [[Sonic Telemetry]] (nine tagged fish monitored), captured by [[Trammel Netting]] during the spawning period, sampled for larvae ([[Larval Sampling]]), and aged from fin ray sections ([[Fin Ray Aging]]).

Research objectives for the following field season included:
- Continuing to monitor the Echo Bay and Las Vegas Bay populations.
- Continuing to age individual fish from Lake Mead.
- Continuing to study razorback sucker use of the [[Overton Arm]].
- Preparing a review report summarizing the study from its inception to its current status.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake level and recruitment.** Lake levels were declining and were projected to reach the lowest levels observed during the study. Earlier aging results linked recruitment pulses to high, stable lake elevations ([[Lake Elevation]]). In 2006-2007, however, recruitment pulses coincided with low, declining elevations, which prompted a recommendation to review the [[Recruitment]] hypothesis.

**Spawning plasticity.** The authors interpret shifts in spawning location as responses to reservoir elevation fluctuations. This "spawning plasticity" was seen in both Las Vegas Bay and Echo Bay. The shifts are presumed to be artifacts of low and declining lake levels.

**Inflow areas.** The Muddy River/Virgin River inflow area, including [[Fish Island]], was again used for spawning and produced larval razorback sucker. The report calls it "yet another important area" for production and recruitment.

**Other factors.** The planned review would also consider [[Nonnative Fish]] species that may have predatory or competitive effects on razorback sucker recruitment, and changes in physical, limnological, and water quality conditions in Lake Mead.

## Key Findings

**Movement and telemetry**
- Nine sonic-tagged fish were monitored: one residual fish (code 222) tagged in 2004-2005, and eight tagged in the 2005-2006 event.
- Tagged fish produced a total of 127 separate location points (OCR partly illegible; figure as reported in the source).
- Fish moved among the Muddy/Virgin inflow area, Echo Bay, and Las Vegas Bay.
- Fish 447 was the first fish observed moving between the northern portions of the Overton Arm and Las Vegas Bay, and did so twice.
- The report says the Echo Bay and Muddy/Virgin spawning aggregates should be treated as one population, since fish have intermixed since 2004.

**Capture and recapture**
- Trammel netting captured 88 razorback sucker: 39 from Las Vegas Bay, 33 from Echo Bay, and 16 from the Muddy/Virgin inflow area.
- Ten captured fish exceeded 300 mm total length (one from Las Vegas Bay, five from Echo Bay, four from the Muddy/Virgin area).
- Of the 88 fish, 50 were recaptures. The authors state this recapture rate falls within the range reported for previous years.
- This was the largest capture total for any season to date.
- Larval razorback sucker were also captured at the Muddy/Virgin inflow area.

**Growth**
- Average growth from 23 recaptured fish was 8.1 mm.
- Mean annual growth was 12.2 mm for Las Vegas Bay fish and 5.2 mm for Echo Bay fish.
- Growth was not calculated for the Muddy/Virgin area because of too few recaptures.
- The authors state Lake Mead growth rates remain substantially higher than those reported elsewhere.

**Age and recruitment**
- Fin ray sections were taken from 41 fish this season. Combined with 91 fish aged in earlier years, the study total is 132 aged fish.
- Recruitment is documented for 2000-2006, extending the record beyond the previously identified endpoint of 1999.
- Recruitment pulses in 2006-2007 coincided with low, declining lake elevations, not the high, stable levels previously associated with recruitment.
- The authors recommend an investigation of the factors that allowed recruitment despite lowered elevations.

**Spawning locations**
- Las Vegas Bay spawned along the southwestern shoreline, the second documented spawning location outside the historical [[Blackbird Point]] site. Evidence included juvenile, subadult, and adult captures, relatively abundant larvae, and heavy use by residual tagged fish.
- Echo Bay spawning shifted to the northern shoreline west of the Echo Bay Marina, about 85 m east of the 2004-2006 site, presumed to be caused by low lake levels.

**Planned work**
- The authors are preparing a review report that synthesizes the study from its inception and assesses data from other groups on Lake Mead.
- BIO-WEST, with the Nevada Department of Wildlife, the Bureau of Reclamation, and the Southern Nevada Water Authority, continued collecting larval razorback sucker for Lake Mead repatriation efforts.
