# The Fishes of Mohave River, California

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #desert-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1918 taxonomic description by [[John Otterbein Snyder]] documents the fish fauna of the [[Mohave River]] in southern California, identifying a single species belonging to the genus *[[Siphateles]]*. The author describes *Siphateles mohavensis* as a new species distinct from geographically proximate forms (*S. formosus* from [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Basin]] and *S. obesus* from [[Lahontan System]] and [[Owens River]]). The [[Mohave River]] originates in the [[San Bernardino Mountains]] and flows across the [[Mohave Desert]], where it eventually disappears through evaporation and absorption. The species represents a channel and lake minnow adapted to fluctuating desert river conditions, with no apparent connection to neighboring drainage systems.

## Background & Description

The [[Mohave River]] drains a small area of the northern slopes of ranges separating its basin from the [[Santa Ana River]]. The river flows directly across the [[Mohave Desert]], receiving minimal water input except from occasional springs. Its flow is fluctuating and uncertain, influenced by sudden desert storms and long dry periods. Relief maps indicate the river never had an outlet in its current direction, terminating in a depression surrounded by low mountains. The river is separated from the [[San Joaquin Basin]] by high mountains connecting the southern [[Sierra Nevada]] with the [[Coast Ranges]].

*Siphateles mohavensis* (type specimen No. 76837, U.S.N.M.) was collected near Victor, California, on August 14, 1915, by Clarence H. Kennedy. The species was originally recorded by Girard in 1856 as *Algansea formosa* and previously considered synonymous with *Hesperoleucus (Rutilus) symmetricus*. The genus *[[Siphateles]]* comprises channel and lake minnows occurring in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin]], [[Klamath]], [[Oregon Lake]], [[Columbia]], [[Lahontan System]], and [[Owens River]] systems. Species are not known from [[Santa Ana River]] or [[Colorado River]].

Morphological characteristics of *S. mohavensis* include: total length 149 mm; head 3.5 in length; depth 3.5; snout 3.6 in head; eye diameter 5.2; scales in lateral series 50; scales between occiput and dorsal fin 26; scales above lateral line 12. The species exhibits a large head, deep and heavy body, short and rounded fins. The snout is short, maxillary oblique, interorbital space broad and flat, dorsal outline of head slightly concave. Dorsal origin is immediately over ventrals, halfway between anterior eye border and end of last vertebra. Lateral line is complete. Gillrakers number 21-24, short and pointed, flat and triangular, with 6-7 on short limb of arch. Pharyngeals are short and heavy with slightly hooked teeth having broad grinding surfaces, usually 4 (sometimes 5) on right side, 5 on left. Coloration shows dusky upper surface with dark fins, each scale having definite dark border and lighter center.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*[[Siphateles]]* species are lacustrine and channel forms that do not migrate far into smaller tributaries. The genus rarely occurs in streams without deep, slough-like channels or direct connection to lakes, and individuals are not found at distance from such habitats. This ecological constraint raises questions about how *S. mohavensis* colonized the [[Mohave River]], as stream capture near headwaters seems unlikely given the species' habitat preferences.

The [[Mohave River]] environment is characterized by extreme fluctuation in flow volume, with sudden desert storms alternating with long dry periods. The river's terminal sink lies in a depression with no outlet, and evaporation and absorption consume the dwindling current. This isolated desert system presents unique challenges for aquatic organisms, with no apparent historical connection to neighboring drainages that might explain the presence of *[[Siphateles]]*.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic Description**: *Siphateles mohavensis* represents a distinct species endemic to the [[Mohave River]], differentiated from *S. formosus* ([[Sacramento-San Joaquin Basin]]) and *S. obesus* ([[Lahontan System]] and [[Owens River]]) through morphological comparisons of scales, fin dimensions, and meristic characters.

2. **Morphometric Differentiation**: Comparative tables demonstrate differences in lateral line scales (48-56 range across species), scales before dorsal (25-28), scales below lateral line (6-8), dorsal fin height (4.0-5.0 in length), anal fin height (4.5-6.0), and dorsal ray counts (8-9).

3. **Biogeographic Isolation**: The [[Mohave River]] basin shows no geological evidence of previous connection to [[Sacramento-San Joaquin]] or [[Lahontan]] systems. The immediate relationship of *S. mohavensis* cannot be determined with certainty from fish examination alone, requiring geological evidence.

4. **Habitat Specialization**: Species of *[[Siphateles]]* are restricted to lacustrine and channel environments with deep, slough-like channels, not occurring in smaller tributaries or at distance from such habitats. This ecological constraint complicates explanations for the species' presence in the isolated [[Mohave River]] system.

5. **Historical Taxonomy**: The species was originally described by Girard (1856) as *Algansea formosa* based on cotypes from both [[Merced River]] (No. 196) and [[Mohave River]] (No. 197), but these represent different species. The name *formosa* is retained for the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin]] form.

6. **Limited Diversity**: Collections from near Victor and Barstow reveal only a single fish species in the [[Mohave River]], though the author notes the river has not been thoroughly searched for fishes.
