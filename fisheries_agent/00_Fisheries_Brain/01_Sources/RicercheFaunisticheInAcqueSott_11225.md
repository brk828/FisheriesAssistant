# Faunistic Research in Phreatic Groundwater of Southern and Insular Greece and the Current State of Greek Stygofauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #copepoda #stygofauna

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Giuseppe Lucio Pesce]] and [[Domenico Maggi]] (Zoological Institute, University of L'Aquila) reports stygobiological surveys carried out in 1977–1981 in southern and insular Greece: the Peloponnese, Boeotia, Attica, Euboea, the northern Sporades, Crete, Rhodes, Corfu and Zante. Samples were taken from 159 numbered stations, mostly shallow freshwater or slightly brackish wells, and paired with chemico-physical measurements. The authors argue that Greek groundwater fauna is now well defined and reasonably well known. They describe a strong coastal versus inland contrast and propose that many coastal taxa are marine-derived relicts that entered groundwater through Tertiary regressions. The paper also suggests that the ratio of epigean to hypogean species could serve as a pollution index.

The document is a faunistic research paper, not a fish study. Its taxa are invertebrates, and the note reflects that.

## Background & Description

- **Research context:** The earliest organised work on Greek subterranean aquatic fauna dates to 1929 and 1932–1933 (Adé, Stadler, Beier). Later contributions came from Stadler and Versluys (1937), Schäfer (1945–51), and Lindberg, Bertrand and Aubert (1953–54). A new phase began in 1968 with Matsakis, Travé and the Keramou Biological Station. Since 1976 the L'Aquila group has run a systematic groundwater program covering Italy and other eastern Mediterranean areas.
- **Sampling method:** Phreatobiological nets (*retini freatobiologici*) were lowered through artificial wells. These are considered reliable to about 15–20 m depth, and the wells sampled here were mostly 20–25 m deep or less. Measured parameters included well depth, water level, water and air temperature, pH, sediment composition and grain size, salinity, and in some cases nitrite and iron. Instruments included YSI and Fischer portable monitors and Merkoquant test strips.
- **Station count:** 159 stations are listed in the survey section, and the authors note that more than 300 stations were proposed across the Greek campaigns overall.
- **Taxonomic scope:** Copepoda (Cyclopoida, Harpacticoida, Calanoida), Isopoda (Asellidae, Microparasellidae, Janiridae), Amphipoda, Ostracoda, Oligochaeta, Thermosbaenacea, Bathynellacea, Syncarida, Hydracarina, Gastropoda, Turbellaria, and others.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat contrast:** Interstitial and phreatic communities are more diversified than cave communities and contain more stygobiont and eustygophile species. Cave fauna is mainly stygophile or stygoxene, with a smaller stygobiont fraction. Coastal phreatic communities are quantitatively richer but dominated by eustygophile, stygophile or stygoxene forms, some of them recent immigrants of marine origin. Inland communities contain more true stygobionts but fewer individuals.
- **Marine-origin (thalassoid) taxa:** Microparasellid isopods, thermosbaenaceans, *Bogidiella* and *Salentinella* amphipods, *Halicyclops*, and harpacticoids of the genera *Nitocrella* and *Parapseudoleptomesochra* show distributions that closely follow Tertiary coastlines. The authors invoke the "Regression Model Evolution" of Stock (1977, 1980) to explain their invasion of inland groundwater, alongside an alternative "Active Migration Model."
- **Pollution and stability:** Low stygobiont diversity in coastal wells is linked to dominance by epigean species and to ecological instability, possibly including external pollution. The paper supports Ronneberger (1975) and Danielopol (1981) in proposing the epigean-to-hypogean ratio as an index of water quality.
- **Biogeography:** Most species are Palaearctic. Others are of north-eastern origin, transadriatic or periadriatic, or endemic to Greece or the Balkans.
- **Water chemistry:** Recorded well temperatures ranged roughly from 12.9 to 21.5 °C. pH was mostly 6.5–7.5. Salinity was generally 0.1–3.8 ‰, with one 8.5 pH station near Messene (G. 128) and a 1.6 mg/L nitrite reading at the same site.

## Key Findings

- **Survey scale:** 159 stations sampled in 1977–1981 across the regions listed above, mostly wells of 20–25 m depth or less.
- **Copepoda:** 51 species and subspecies were identified in this survey (Cyclopoida 35, Harpacticoida 15, Calanoida 1), bringing the Greek total for the group to 69. The authors report 43 cyclopid species and subspecies for Greek groundwater, placed in Halicyclopinae (3), Eucyclopinae (8) and Cyclopinae (32).
- **Halicyclops:** Three species are known from Greece: *H. troglodites* (first Greek record, Cephalonia), *H. rotundipes* and *H. magniceps*. Their variability is considered in need of global revision.
- **Speocyclops:** The genus is widespread. *S. demetiensis* is recorded from Euboea, the northern Sporades and Zante, extending its known Greek range.
- **Diacyclops languidoides complex:** Four subspecies were identified here: *zschokkei*, *hypnicola*, *nagysalloensis* and *italianus*.
- **Acanthocyclops:** *A. dussarti* (Epirus, Peloponnese) and *A. cephallenus* (Cephalonia) are placed in a peculiar lineage related to *A. gordani*. The authors question the diagnostic value of some characters separating *Acanthocyclops* from *Megacyclops*.
- **Thermocyclops oblongatus:** One of the most frequent and numerous copepods, recorded across mainland and island Greece, and interpreted as a eurioecious stygophile that is colonising groundwater.
- **Metacyclops subdolus:** Strictly stygobiont. Its distribution (Mallorca, Sardinia, southern Italy, mainland Greece, Crete) is taken to indicate fairly recent invasion. The present study extends its range to the Peloponnese.
- **Harpacticoids:** *Nitocrella stammeri* has an extensive perimediterranean range, with its southern limit at Crete. *N. rhodiensis* (Rhodes) and *N. skyrensis* (Skyros) are endemic. *N. achaiae* is transadriatic. *Parapseudoleptomesochra hellenica* is endemic to Skyros and considered a recent marine immigrant.
- **Elaphoidella:** Three species were collected: *E. elaphoides* (widespread, variable), *E. karamani* (Balkan) and *E. moreae* (apparently endemic to the Peloponnese).
- **Asellidae:** Four Greek species were found in this survey: *Asellus aquaticus*, *Proasellus coxalis* s.l. (the most widespread isopod), *P. minoicus* and *P. cretensis*. Depigmented, eye-reduced *A. aquaticus* from Skiros suggest isolation from surface populations. The Rhodes population of *P. coxalis* is provisionally assigned to *P. c. rhodiensis*.
- **Microparasellidae:** Seven species and subspecies were identified, plus one *Microcharon* of intermediate character. *Microcharon ullae* is endemic to Rhodes.
- **Janiridae:** A single species, *Jaera italica*, was recorded from a coastal well at Ghition.
- **Amphipoda:** Eight species from four genera were identified. The most frequent are *Bogidiella longiflagellum*, *Salentinella angelieri* and *Niphargus orcinus* s.l.
- **Ostracoda:** Fourteen species are known from Greece, eight stygobiont and six stygophile or stygoxene. Most are endemic.
- **Syncarida and Bathynellacea:** Previously unrecorded from Greek groundwater, these are now reported from Cephalonia, Euboea and the Peloponnese.
- **Thermosbaenacea:** *Monodella argentarii* was found in quantity in a coastal well near Ghition.
- **Oligochaeta:** Seventeen identified species. Notable records include *Tubifex newaensis* (new to Greece and the Balkans), *Rhyacodrilus lepnevae* and *Achaeta maorica* (new to Greece), and endemic *Enchytronia hellenica* and an undescribed *Peloscolex* sp.
- **Conclusion on distribution:** Most species are Palaearctic. A substantial group is endemic to Greece or the Aegean and Ionian region.
