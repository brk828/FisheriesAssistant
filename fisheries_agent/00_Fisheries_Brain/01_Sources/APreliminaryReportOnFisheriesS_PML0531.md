# Fisheries Survey Memorandum: Lake Mead and the Colorado River Below Boulder Dam

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a degraded scan of a preliminary fisheries memorandum on [[Lake Mead]] and the [[Colorado River]] below [[Boulder Dam]]. The author states that the observations are brief, not fully verified, and "purely tentative and subject to revision." The text reports rising reservoir levels, a possible increase in food availability for fish, and the stocking of trout and other fish. It closes with a proposed six-part survey program intended to support a fishery management plan. Because the OCR is heavily corrupted, many sentences can only be partly reconstructed, and this note records only the points that are reasonably clear.

- **Document type:** Preliminary report or proposal, not a completed research paper. It has no formal sample sizes, statistics or data tables that can be read.
- **Authorship:** The header names a U.S. Department of the Interior fisheries group, with an aquatic biologist and a fish and wildlife service senior biologist among the contributors. Exact names and titles are not reliably legible.
- **Date:** The memorandum appears to be from August of a year in the early 1950s. The exact year is uncertain because of OCR damage.
- **Subject:** The fish populations and their food supply in Lake Mead and the river reach immediately downstream of Boulder Dam, which was then a newly regulated system.
- **Fish mentioned:** Trout are discussed most often. Bass are also mentioned. Forage fish and other introductions are referred to in several places, but their identities are only partly legible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Reservoir level:** The text says the lake was rising during the survey period and had reached a higher level than in the previous year. Rising levels are linked to changes in shoreline habitat and the availability of food.
- **Food supply:** The author reports that the food supply for fish appeared generally favourable. Forage organisms in the lake were described as abundant or increasing, and it is suggested that food availability for trout could increase. The text also notes that fish food organisms may be "relatively abundant" in the future.
- **Forage fish:** The author discusses whether additional forage fish should be introduced. The OCR suggests a concern that forage fish stocks could become a limiting factor for predatory game fish.
- **Introductions:** Introductions of trout and bass are discussed. The author states that trout species have been established in the system, although some attempts are described as unsuccessful. The legible text is insufficient to confirm which species succeeded or failed, so this point should be treated as uncertain.
- **Physical and chemical conditions:** The memorandum notes that some physical and chemical conditions in the lake and river below the dam had not been documented, and it proposes to study them.
- **Fishing conditions:** Some discussion of fishing pressure, catches and angler problems is present, but the numbers are not legible.

## Key Findings

The memorandum presents preliminary observations and a plan. Its main points are:

1. **Rising water levels** in Lake Mead, higher than the previous year, which the author links to changes in the fish community and food supply.
2. **Generally favourable food conditions** for fish, with possible future increases in fish food organisms, while the author warns that some food resources may be limited.
3. **Forage fish management** is an open question. The author considers whether to introduce additional forage species to support predatory fish.
4. **Trout and bass introductions** are discussed, with mixed or uncertain outcomes.
5. **A proposed six-part survey program**, which the author says is needed to provide the information required for a comprehensive management plan:
   - Species of fish present and their relative abundance.
   - Food of trout and bass.
   - Available food organisms in the lake and in the river below the dam.
   - Fishing problems in the lake.
   - Physical and chemical conditions in the lake and river below the dam.
   - Development of a sound fishery management plan for the area.

The author concludes that the proposed survey is needed before reliable management decisions can be made. The conclusions are provisional and no quantitative results are reported.
