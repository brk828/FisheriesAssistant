# Technical Group FY-91 Work Plan: Minutes, Ranking Results and Funding Decisions (June 1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This Colorado Division of Wildlife memorandum (August 14, 1990, from Jim Bennett, Chairman of the Technical Group) transmits the final minutes of the [[Technical Group]] meeting held June 5-7, 1990. The meeting reviewed and ranked proposals for the FY-91 [[Work Plan]] of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Proposals were trimmed and reordered by score so that ongoing studies were refunded and high-priority studies were started. The [[Recovery Implementation Committee]] was to act on [[Management Committee]] recommendations at its August 22 meeting in Grand Junction, Colorado. The document also includes a ranking table, a list of proposals that were funded, incorporated or not funded, and the vote tallies from member agencies.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency memorandum with attached meeting minutes and a results table (budget figures in thousands of dollars, $ x 1000).
- **Governing bodies:** Technical Group, Recovery Implementation Committee, Management Committee, and the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP).
- **Participating agencies (vote columns):** [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BuRec/BR), [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] (CDOW/CO), Utah (UT), Wyoming (WY), [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB/H2O), and WAPA.
- **Relevant species named:** [[Colorado Squawfish]] (CSF), [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS), Gila taxa (see [[Gila Taxonomy]]), and cyprinid larvae and young fishes.
- **Study locations named:** [[Green River]] (GR), [[Yampa River]], [[Flaming Gorge]], [[Dolores River]], [[White River]], [[San Juan River]], [[Lake Powell]], [[Canyonlands]], [[Kenney Reservoir]], [[Ouray Hatchery]], and the [[Fifteen-Mile Reach]].
- **Table caveat:** The memo states that budget corrections were made after the meeting, that the figures should be considered working figures only, and that the total budget was not yet computed. The source text is OCR-scanned, so the ranking table is partly garbled. Values below are reported as they appear.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow management:** Several proposals concerned flows, including "cobble-cleaning" flows (proposal #16, which the FWS considered premature), [[Sediment Flushing Flow]] studies, and flow effects on young CSF (proposals #43, #50, #62, #69). A position paper on flow effects was pending adoption.
- **Drought:** The Dolores River study (#23) was flagged because releases greater than 20 cfs are needed for valid data, and the minutes state that "this is a drought cycle, and there may not be more than 20 cfs."
- **Reproductive habitat:** Reproductive habitat and substrate needs were "not known" for squawfish, and proposal #53 was expected to supply some of that data.
- **Predation and temperature:** Predation effects on young razorback suckers (#79) and temperature effects on Green River suckers (#78) were discussed.
- **Hatchery and genetics:** Propagation, genetics and stocking (Ouray Hatchery, genetic assessment under #58, and catfish stocking in Kenney Reservoir) were significant topics.
- **Stocked-fish evaluation:** Proposals evaluated fish stocked in the early and mid-1980s (#46, and "Eval. of '84 Stocked CSF").
- **Water needs:** The RIP requires water for its work (#84). Water gauge operation and maintenance (#63, #64) were to be moved to the CDOW budget.

## Key Findings

**Meeting outcomes (June 5-7, 1990)**

- All program management budgets were approved, with concern that program management costs could be included in other proposals.
- The Flaming Gorge and Green River studies were combined into one umbrella proposal, **#26**, with a budget of **$300,000** per the minutes. The umbrella incorporates the activities of proposals #4, 21, 22, 73, 74, 76, 82 and 83. The results table lists #26 as **$265.0K requested, $315.3K allocated** (combined rank 1.243).
- Proposal #33 was to be spent over two years: **$8,210 in FY 91** and **$10,000 in FY 92**.
- Proposal #70 was to have its genetic assessment money (**$45,000**) removed, since it is covered under #58.
- Proposal #8 was to be split so that half (**$20,000**) contained no catfish stocking plans.
- Proposal #81 was tabled, and a White River recovery plan was to be created as #87 ("The White River Recovery Plan").
- Proposal #69 was to be rescoped and integrated, and the CWCB was to tell the FWS how much flow it needs.
- Proposal #79 was judged unable to accomplish its stated objectives.
- The Technical Group needed to decide whether the propagation plan (#57) should cover 3 years or more.
- Proposal #39 was rejected because a federally regulated mechanism for reporting spills already exists.

**Incorporated and other proposals**

- Proposals incorporated into the Flaming Gorge umbrella (#21, 22, 73, 74, 76, 82, 83, and others) were listed with a combined total of **$384.8K**.
- Proposals incorporated into other funded proposals were listed with a total of **$265.0K**.

**Notable unfunded or deferred proposals**

- Proposal #17 was "not a proposal" (per the minutes); #27 "had no costs."
- Proposal #16 (cobble-cleaning flows) was judged premature by the FWS.
- Proposals #30, #31 and #32 (Aspinall Work Plan related) were premature and to be reconsidered after that plan is completed.
- Proposal #67 (San Juan River) and #68 were judged "too temporary to be meaningful."
- "Sediment Flushing Flow" (**$150.0K requested**, rank 2.500) and "Sediment Flushing Flow Studies" (**$58.0K requested**, rank 1.714) were unfunded and unresolved, and were referred to the sediment committee.
- "Horsethief & Pit-Tagging" (**$217.0K requested**, rank 2.571) was unfunded.
- "Intensive CSF Culture" (**$34.7K requested**, rank 1.571) was unfunded and was to work with the proposal coordinator.

**Process and reorganization**

- No consensus was reached on how the Technical Group could improve interaction between researchers and the Group.
- Proposers were advised to contact the state in which they were working before proposing additional studies, and to purchase their own equipment.
