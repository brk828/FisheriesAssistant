# Glen Canyon Unit Colorado River Storage Project Reservoir Fisheries Investigation 1966-1967

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae #reservoir-ecology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This report documents the fourth year of a six-year cooperative fisheries investigation on [[Lake Powell]], the reservoir created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The study, conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[Utah Fish and Game Department]] from February 1, 1966 to January 31, 1967, focused on measuring fishing pressure, harvest, and plankton populations. Creel census was conducted at multiple access points including [[Wahweap Marina]], [[Halls Crossing Marina]], [[Hite Marina]], and [[Bullfrog]]. The study documented 7,524 angler trips at Wahweap producing 27,231 fish in 44,990 hours, with total estimated pressure of 28,961 trips yielding 102,309 fish. The creel composition was dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] (61%), [[Rainbow Trout]] (28%), and [[Channel Catfish]] (8%). Plankton studies revealed adequate zooplankton populations dominated by *Daphnia*, *Cyclops*, and *Diaptomus*, with a major bloom of the phytoplankton *Aphanizomenon* occurring November-December 1966.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Powell]] is a large reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]], representing a significant impoundment in the Colorado River Storage Project. This investigation is part of a cooperative program financed by Section 8 funds, with Arizona focusing on fishing pressure/harvest studies and plankton population dynamics.

The reservoir supports a mixed-species fishery. [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) dominate numerically in the creel, particularly in upper reservoir areas. [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) concentrate heavily in the [[Wahweap Creek]] area near the dam, with 97% of trout taken at Wahweap and 94% from Wahweap Creek specifically. [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) comprise a smaller but consistent portion of the harvest. [[Black Crappie]] appear regularly in small numbers (5-10 fish per month) and are expected to increase in future years.

The reservoir features multiple access points: [[Wahweap Marina]] provides the only launching access to the lower portion, [[Halls Crossing Marina]] offers the only fueling facilities for 40 miles in either direction in the mid-reservoir, and [[Hite Marina]] and [[Bullfrog]] serve the upper reaches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Distribution**: Fishing pressure and success varied significantly by location. At Wahweap, the [[Wahweap Creek]] area received 43.5% of angler trips despite being closest to the marina, justified by the highest success rate (0.67 fish/hour). Other productive areas included [[Rock Creek]] (0.67 fish/hour, mainly bass), [[San Juan River]] (0.63 fish/hour), and [[Last Chance Creek]] (0.60 fish/hour). From Halls Crossing, top areas were [[Moqui Canyon]] (0.73 fish/hour), [[Escalante River]] (0.70 fish/hour), and San Juan River (0.69 fish/hour).

**Plankton Communities**: Twelve sampling stations throughout the reservoir revealed adequate plankton populations to support the fishery. Zooplankton was dominated by three genera: *Daphnia*, *Cyclops*, and *Diaptomus*, with nauplii also abundant. Three rotifer genera were identified: *Polyarthra*, *Keratella*, and *Brachionus*. The protozoan *Ceratium* occurred in 34 samples.

Phytoplankton communities included blue-green algae (*Oscillatoria* in 20 samples, *Anacystis* in 13), green algae (*Tribonema* in 13 samples, *Aphanizomenon* in 6), desmids (*Staurastrum* in 30 samples, *Gonatozygon* in 18), and diatoms (*Asterionella* in 31 samples, *Fragilaria* in 11). A major bloom of *Aphanizomenon* occurred November-December 1966 in the Halls Crossing area, covering large surface areas extending several miles.

**Fertility Gradient**: Zooplankton abundance increased proceeding from the dam toward the inflow, with highest numbers at [[Hite]], [[Halls Crossing]], and [[Rincon]] during June. This fertility gradient correlates with increased bass growth in the upper third of the reservoir. Peak zooplankton densities reached 40,470 organisms per 100-foot sample at Rincon in June, and 26,490 at Halls Crossing.

**Water Column Stratification**: Stratified sampling at control points ([[Wahweap Channel]], [[San Juan River]], Halls Crossing) showed vertical distribution of plankton, with highest concentrations typically in upper 25 feet (e.g., 85.45 organisms/liter at 0-25 feet vs. 1.83 at 100-150 feet at Halls Crossing in June).

## Key Findings

**Fishing Pressure and Harvest**:
- **Wahweap Marina**: 80 census days documented 7,524 angler trips producing 27,231 fish in 44,990 hours. Estimated annual total: 28,961 trips, 102,309 fish, 149,204 hours.
- **Halls Crossing Marina**: 34 census days documented 631 trips producing 1,793 fish in 3,480 hours. Estimated annual total: 5,746 trips, 15,521 fish, 30,573 hours.
- **Combined Pressure**: 34,707 angler trips producing 117,830 fish in 178,636 hours from both major access points.
- **Hite Marina** (5 months): 86 trips checked produced 412 bass and 39 catfish in 657 hours; estimated 818 trips, 3,684 bass, 368 catfish.
- **Bullfrog** (5 months, total census): 70 trips produced 121 bass, 27 catfish, 48 other fish in 282 hours.

**Success Rates**:
- Wahweap Marina averaged 0.61 fish/hour, 3.8 fish/trip, 5.9 hours/trip, with 71% of trips successful.
- Halls Crossing Marina averaged 0.51 fish/hour, 2.3 fish/trip, with 68% of trips successful.
- Monthly success at Wahweap ranged from 0.27 fish/hour (January 1967) to 0.88 (August 1966).
- Monthly success at Halls Crossing ranged from 0.00 (January 1967) to 1.1 (August 1966).
- Night anglers at Wahweap Creek achieved rates exceeding 10 fish/hour for trout.

**Creel Composition**:
- Reservoir-wide: 62% [[Largemouth Bass]], 27% [[Rainbow Trout]], 8% [[Channel Catfish]], 3% other.
- Wahweap: 61% bass, 28% trout, 8% catfish, 3% other.
- Halls Crossing: 79% bass, 17% catfish, 3% other, 1% trout.

**Bait Preferences**:
- Successful trout anglers: 60.4% used prepared baits (cheese, corn, salmon eggs), 32.3% artificial lures, 5.0% live worms, 2.3% waterdogs. Best artificial: flatfish.
- Successful bass anglers: 60.6% used live waterdogs, 34.0% artificial lures, 4.4% prepared baits. Best artificials: rubber worms and medium-running lures.

**Angler Origin**:
- Wahweap: 61% Arizona residents (20% Phoenix, 16% Page, 12% Flagstaff), 15% Utah, 11% California.
- Halls Crossing: 37% Utah, 23% New Mexico, 20% Colorado, 7% Arizona.

**Aerial Survey Validation**:
Six aerial flights in July-August 1966 counted 479 boats (124 fishing, 183 transit, 192 other). Pressure estimates using total boats × days × average anglers/boat (1.6) closely matched creel census: July aerial 3,865 vs. census 3,751 trips; August aerial 3,936 vs. census 3,376 trips (14% variation). Method shows promise for supplementing creel census despite limitations (high cost, inability to measure harvest, weather constraints).

**Plankton Populations**:
- 130 samples collected from 12 stations identified 11 zooplankton genera and 14 phytoplankton genera.
- Zooplankton: *Daphnia* (129 occurrences), *Cyclops* (127), nauplius (128), *Diaptomus* (103), *Polyphemus* (70) most prevalent.
- Phytoplankton: *Asterionella* (31 occurrences), *Staurastrum* (30), *Oscillatoria* (20), *Gonatozygon* (18), *Anacystis* (13), *Tribonema* (13), *Fragilaria* (11).
- Fertility increases upstream: highest zooplankton at Hite (38.50 organisms/liter average), Halls Crossing (21.20), Rincon (32.38 peak in June).
- Major *Aphanizomenon* bloom November-December 1966 near Halls Crossing.

**Recommendations**:
1. Continue creel census at all access points to further evaluate pressure and harvest.
2. Continue aerial surveys to determine value as supplement to creel census.
3. Continue plankton studies but restrict to basic 100-foot vertical control samples at each station; take stratified samples in specific areas to correlate with trout distribution.
