# Hybridization between Crayfish of the Genus Orconectes

#carcinology #marine-biology #crustacea #pontoniinae #cambaridae #sphaeromatidae

## Executive Summary

This is a compilation of three short crustacean studies from *Crustaceana* (vol. 39, 1980), not a fish study. The papers cover:

1. **Feeding mechanics in [[Typton Dentatus]]**, a commensal shrimp of the [[Pontoniinae]], including a second record from [[Heron Island]], Queensland.
2. **Morphological evidence for hybridization** among three [[Orconectes Propinquus]], [[Orconectes Virilis]] and [[Orconectes Rusticus]] crayfish from Wisconsin lakes.
3. **A new southern Atlantic record and redescription** of the isopod [[Dynamenella Dianae]] from Brazil, with a key to the Brazilian species.

## Background & Description

**Pontoniine shrimp (Bruce).** The author describes the third maxillipeds of *Typton dentatus*, which form a setal cone that filters the incoming water current. The antepenultimate segment is strongly bilaterally compressed, which contrasts with most other pontoniines. The setae act more as a barrier than a strainer. Related genera with setal fringes on the first maxilliped, such as *Conchodytes* (*C. monodactylus*) and *Anchistus* (*A. custos*), show less developed filtering structures.

**Crayfish hybridization (Capelli & Capelli).** The authors examined eight morphological characters, including rostral carina, mandible incisor region, third maxilliped setae, areola width, gonopod length and shape, terminal elements of the gonopod, gonopod shoulder and colour. Individuals were sampled from monospecific lakes in Vilas County in August 1977, using baited traps.

**Brazilian isopod (Pires).** *Dynamenella dianae* was previously known from Bahia de San Quintín, Baja California (Menzies, 1962), and from Mayagüez Bay, Puerto Rico (1968). The author supplies a complete redescription based on 396 specimens, with notes on habitat and sexual dimorphism. The diagnostic male is 5.6 mm long.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Commensal feeding:** The authors state that feeding mechanisms in commensal Pontoniinae are still poorly understood. The *Typton* mouthparts are described as a filtering system that differs from most other pontoniines.
- **Sympatry and displacement:** The three *Orconectes* species often co-occur. *O. rusticus* has been introduced into northern Wisconsin and appears to be displacing *O. virilis* and *O. propinquus*.
- **Hybrid fitness:** Among the 75 lakes and stream areas with data, none were dominated by apparent hybrids. The authors interpret this as circumstantial evidence that hybrids are less fit than non-hybrids.
- **Mating behaviour:** In laboratory trials (manuscript in preparation at the time), male *O. rusticus* mated more often with *O. propinquus* and *O. virilis* females than the reverse. This may lead to more hybrid offspring from *O. propinquus* females, and to "incorrect" sperm plugs in *O. virilis* females.
- **Dynamenella habitat:** The author includes habitat notes for *D. dianae* in the full paper, but the excerpt does not give details.

## Key Findings

### Typton and Onycocaris (Bruce)
- *Typton dentatus* Fujino & Miyake, 1969, previously known only from the Ryukyu Islands, is reported for a second time from Heron Island, Queensland.
- Two complete specimens were found. This allowed the second pereiopods, absent from the type material, to be described.
- The genus placement of *T. dentatus* in *Typton* Costa is confirmed.
- *Onycocaris anomala* Bruce (1979) is transferred to *Typton*. Its mouthpart morphology is similar to that of *T. dentatus*.
- The mouthpart filtering mechanism is considered distinct from that of most other pontoniine shrimps.

### Orconectes Morphology (Capelli & Capelli)
- **Sampling:** Two lakes per species were sampled, with 50–100 form I (breeding) males analysed per lake. The authors then classified 232 form I males from [[Turtle Lake]], where all three species occur.
- **Monospecific populations:** No significant between-lake variation was found for *O. propinquus* or *O. virilis*. *O. rusticus* varied in gonopod form and colour.
- **Diagnostic characters:**
  - A rostral carina was always present in *O. propinquus* and virtually absent in the other two species. It separates *O. propinquus* reliably but cannot distinguish *O. virilis* from *O. rusticus*.
  - The incisor margin is dentate in *O. propinquus* and *O. virilis*, and smooth in *O. rusticus*.
  - Dense third maxilliped setae occur only in *O. virilis*.
  - Areola width/carapace length overlap was slight, with about 4% of individuals in overlap ranges.
  - About 80% of individuals could be classified from the difference in length of the gonopod terminal elements.
  - A distinct, nearly right-angled gonopod shoulder occurs only in *O. rusticus*. About 20% of *O. rusticus* have a sloping shoulder.
- **Turtle Lake results:** Of 232 individuals, 169 could be assigned unequivocally: *O. propinquus* 86, *O. virilis* 71, *O. rusticus* 12. A further 63 showed characters suggesting *O. rusticus* × *O. propinquus* hybridization. No *O. propinquus* × *O. virilis* or *O. rusticus* × *O. virilis* hybrids were found.
- **Hybrid characteristics:** Many apparent hybrids showed traits intermediate between the parent species, or atypical combinations of traits. Example #1 showed *O. rusticus* traits (no rostral carina, distinct shoulder, red lateral spot) alongside *O. propinquus* traits (incisor region, areola, gonopod, abdomen colour) and a tan chela colour not seen in any monospecific population.
- **Conclusion:** The authors argue that mixed-character individuals occur only where the species co-occur, which supports hybridization as the most likely explanation. They note that morphological evidence alone cannot confirm it.

### Dynamenella dianae (Pires)
- *D. dianae* is newly recorded from southern Atlantic waters off Brazil. The redescription is based on 396 specimens at several developmental stages.
- The author revises the description of sexual dimorphism, which Menzies (1962) had not treated in detail.
- A key to the three Brazilian species separates:
  - *D. tropica* (smooth pereon with epimeric sutures; slender uropods)
  - *D. antonii* (microtuberculate pereon; no median tubercle)
  - *D. dianae* (horizontal ridges on pereonites IV–VII; median tubercle before the foramen; prominent median process in adult males)
