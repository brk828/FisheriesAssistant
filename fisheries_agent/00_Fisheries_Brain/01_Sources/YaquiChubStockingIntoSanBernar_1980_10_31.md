# Yaqui Chub Stocking into San Bernardino Ranch

## Executive Summary
This 1980 internal memo from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service documents an endangered species reintroduction effort involving the transfer of **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*) from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery to the **[[San Bernardino Ranch]]** ponds in Arizona. The primary goal was twofold: to relieve overcrowding at the Dexter facility and to use the native chub as a **biological control agent** against the invasive **[[Western Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*), which had proliferated in House Pond following a failed chemical reclamation attempt. The action represents an early example of using native predator/competitor fish species for the ecological suppression of invasive poeciliids in desert aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Gila purpurea* (Yaqui Chub), family [[Cyprinidae]].
- **Adult Size:** 4–5 inches (approx. 10–12.7 cm) in length.
- **Juvenile Size:** Under 3 inches (approx. <7.6 cm) in length.
- **Population Structure at Stocking:** Mixed cohort of adults and young-of-year, indicating an actively breeding source population at the hatchery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range:** The Yaqui Chub is indicated as **native to San Bernardino Ranch**, situated within the Rio Yaqui drainage of the Sonoran Desert.
- **Habitat:** Desert pond systems, specifically referenced as "House Pond" (2.5 acres) and "North Pond," plus an adjacent seep pool.
- **Water Chemistry/Conditions:** Water temperature at time of release was 67°F, closely matching (within 2°F) the source water at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Interspecies Dynamics:**
  - **Competitor/Predator relationship:** Prior observations at Dexter demonstrated that Yaqui Chub introduction led to the *elimination* of another native species, the **[[Yaqui Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), suggesting strong competitive or predatory pressure on small poeciliid fishes.
  - **Target Invasive Species:** *Gambusia affinis* (Mosquitofish), an exotic poeciliid, was documented as highly abundant in House Pond despite a failed rotenone/reclamation treatment in February 1980.
- **Population Growth Potential:** Yaqui Chub populations are characterized as capable of rapid growth, reaching **carrying capacity of 50,000+ individuals in a 1-acre pond within under two years**, starting from a founder population of only 200 fish.

## Key Findings
1. **Stocking Event (Oct 15, 1980):** A total of **10,092 adult** and **22,200+ young** Yaqui Chubs were translocated from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to [[San Bernardino Ranch]].
   - ~9,900 adults + ~22,000 young → House Pond (2.5 acres)
   - ~200 adults + few young → North Pond
   - Remaining young → Seep pool north of North Pond
2. **Rationale for Translocation:**
   - Biological control of invasive *Gambusia affinis* population in House Pond.
   - Mitigation of anticipated winter die-off at the Dexter facility (a common phenomenon in desert fish populations).
   - Utilization of surplus stock given the species' rapid reproductive capacity at Dexter.
3. **Precedent Cited:** Historical data from Dexter shows that when Yaqui Chub and Yaqui Topminnow were co-introduced, the chub **outcompeted or eliminated** the poeciliid topminnow — used as justification for expecting a similar suppressive effect on *Gambusia*.
4. **Monitoring Plan:** The USFWS committed to monitoring the reintroduction's success and the anticipated decline of *Gambusia affinis* populations over a two-year period following stocking.
5. **Stakeholders Involved:** Coordination occurred among the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department, [[The Nature Conservancy]], and Cochise County Planning Commission, indicating a multi-agency approach to endangered species management. Notable correspondents included W.L. Minckley, a prominent desert fish biologist.
