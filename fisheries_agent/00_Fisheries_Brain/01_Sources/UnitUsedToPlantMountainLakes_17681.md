# Aerial Stocking Equipment and Fish Activity Detection in Fish Culture

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-culture

## Executive Summary

This January 1963 *Progressive Fish-Culturist* item contains three short contributions on fisheries equipment. The first describes a low-cost, folding live box built from an old lawn-chair frame and netting. The second, the longest, describes a four-compartment aluminum tank that the [[Wyoming Game And Fish Commission]] uses to plant fish in high mountain lakes from aircraft. The third describes an electronic strain-gauge device that detects and records fish activity inside a [[Respirometry]] chamber. The document does not name any fish species.

## Background & Description

**Portable live box.** The live box is made from the frame of an old lawn chair and netting. It folds for transport and can be stored flat, laid on other equipment, or placed upright. The chair arms serve as a rest for a measuring board during use. Contributor details are not separately given beyond the byline of [[James D. Stewart]], Fish and Game Service, Montpelier, Vermont.

**Aerial planting of mountain lakes.** The Wyoming Game and Fish Commission had planted an average of 4 mountain lakes per year with fish. Earlier methods included tanks with manual release mechanisms, converted spray tanks for small plantings, and a 60-gallon tank patterned after the Montana design. The Commission then adopted a unit designed by its chief pilot, [[Lyle Rosendahl]]. The article was written by [[Fred R. Beal]], Game and Fish Commission, Cheyenne, Wyoming.

**Fish activity detector.** The device was developed from variations observed in [[Constant-Flow Respirometry]] measurements. It is a [[Strain Gauge]] mounted inside the chamber, with a paddle that flexes when water currents produced by a fish stress the gauge. The resulting voltage change is recorded permanently on a strip chart. An electrical oxygen-measuring device at the chamber outlet is also mentioned, but the text ends mid-sentence at that point.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Remote high-elevation mountain lakes. Full loads of fish were planted in waters at elevations up to 11,000 feet.
- **Transport conditions:** In simulated tests, 1 to 7 pounds of fish (1,060 per pound) were held in a compartment for 3 hours with no apparent harm. For the 7-pound load, ice was used and temperature was held between 39° and 48° F.
- **Oxygen supply:** Each compartment's water is aerated from an oxygen bottle of the type used in small planes. The oxygen regulator was set at 5 pounds in every test.
- **Operating platform:** The unit is designed for a small plane suitable for agricultural-area work.

## Key Findings

**1. Lawn-chair live box**
- Light and collapsible, with a simple netting construction that stores compactly.

**2. Four-compartment aerial planting tank (Wyoming)**
- Four-compartment aluminum tank allowing four plantings per flight, or a plant up to four times heavier than previously possible.
- Each compartment measures 12 × 11 × 21.5 inches and holds 9 gallons of water.
- Fish are manually released into a hopper below each pair of compartments just before the dumping run. A selector switch chooses the release, and a solenoid gate is operated by control buttons on the plane's control stick. One or both hoppers may be used, and gates must be reset manually.
- Each compartment has a carbon rod, powered by a common dry-cell battery, positioned at its lowest point.
- The pilot can observe the condition of the fish.
- Installation or removal takes 1 to 1.5 hours.
- Weight is about 100 pounds empty and 390 pounds loaded, including the oxygen bottle.
- Total construction cost, including the oxygen bottle, was $880.
- The oxygen-pressure gauge is calibrated at 0.25 pound with a 1-to-10 range. In flight, 4.5 to 5 pounds of pressure is used.

**3. Strain-gauge fish activity detector**
- Detects and permanently records fish movement inside a respirometer chamber as voltage changes on a strip chart.
- Was developed in response to variations observed in constant-flow respirometer data.

**Limitations noted:** The transport tests were simulated, and the text provides no biological endpoints beyond "no apparent harm." The detector description is incomplete because the document ends mid-sentence.
