# New Northern record for the threadfin shad, Dorsoma petenense (Gunther), in coastal waters of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Clupeidae

## Executive Summary
This 1969 note documents the northernmost coastal record (at the time) of the [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), a species native to the southeastern United States and widely introduced into California reservoirs. Two mature male specimens (117 mm and 125 mm TL) were captured in **Arcata Bay**, the northern portion of **Humboldt Bay, California**, in March and April 1968. The authors evaluate whether the occurrence resulted from northward coastal migration from the San Francisco Bay area or an unauthorized stocking event, and assess whether local hydrographic conditions (temperature and salinity) could support establishment of a breeding population in north coastal waters.

## Background & Description
- **Specimens collected**: 2 mature males
  - Specimen 1: 117 mm total length (TL)
  - Specimen 2: 125 mm total length (TL)
- **Sex**: Both mature males
- Species is classified as [[Euryhaline]] (per Briggs 1958), tolerating a wide salinity range.
- Size-related salinity preference noted (Miller 1963): fish **<100 mm** prefer salinities **<15‰**; larger individuals occur more frequently in higher salinities — consistent with the larger, mature specimens found in the higher-salinity zone near the oxidation pond outfall.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**Capture locations and gear:**
- Collected using a 16-ft semi-balloon trawl.
- One fish taken near the outfall of the Arcata sewage oxidation pond.
- One fish collected in **Mad River Slough**, a tributary of Arcata Bay.

**Hydrographic conditions recorded (Feb–Apr 1968):**
| Parameter | Oxidation Pond Outfall | Mad River Slough |
|---|---|---|
| Surface Temperature | 11.6–14.4 °C | 11.2–15.4 °C |
| Surface Salinity | 27.7‰–33.3‰ | 12.4‰–30.7‰ |

**Comparative baseline data:**
- Gast (1962): During a 2-year hydrographic study of [[Humboldt Bay]], surface temperature dropped below 8.0 °C only once; salinities at all depths at bay-proper stations were seldom below 29‰ (though no stations were located in bay tributaries).
- Parsons and Kimsey (1954): High mortality in threadfin shad below 45 °F (7.2 °C); near-total mortality at 40 °F (4.4 °C) in freshwater.
- Kimsey (1958): Spawning temperature not precisely known, but ~70 °F (21.0 °C) considered suitable; species seeks fresh or nearly fresh water to spawn.
- Rawstron (1964): Observed spawning at Pine Flat Lake (Fresno Co., CA) at surface temperatures as low as 58 °F (14.4 °C).

**Range and introduction context:**
- Native/established via **authorized introductions** into reservoirs of the **Sacramento** and **San Joaquin River** systems beginning in 1959.
- No authorized introductions had been made in coastal waters north of these systems.
- Not reported in coastal waters of Oregon (per R. Loeffel, Oregon Fish Commission) or Washington (per D. Kauffman, Washington Dept. of Fisheries) as of this report.
- Prior northern coastal record: single specimen from **Drakes Bay**, January 1963 (Isaacson and Poole 1965).
- Burns (1965) noted the species, while delicate, can invade favorable waters very rapidly — suggesting potential for range expansion given suitable conditions.

## Key Findings
- This record represents the **northernmost documented occurrence** of threadfin shad in California coastal waters as of 1968, surpassing the previous Drakes Bay record.
- The occurrence in Arcata Bay is attributed to either **(a) northward coastal migration** from San Francisco Bay populations or **(b) unauthorized/unrecorded stocking**.
- Temperature conditions in Humboldt Bay appear generally favorable and unlikely to limit survival, based on comparison with Gast's (1962) long-term data.
- **Salinity is identified as the probable limiting factor** for reproduction and establishment: Humboldt Bay proper likely exceeds the low-salinity ("fresh or nearly fresh") conditions required for spawning, though tributary streams (e.g., freshwater reaches feeding [[Mad River Slough]]) may offer suitable spawning habitat during spring/summer months (per USGS 1965 data).
- The findings suggest that self-sustaining proliferation of threadfin shad along the north coast is **contingent upon locating low-salinity spawning refugia** in freshwater tributaries rather than the bay itself.
- This paper contributes baseline hydrographic and distributional data relevant to tracking the species' range expansion along the [[California Coast]] and informs future [[Fish Introduction|introduction/invasion ecology]] monitoring in similar estuarine systems.
