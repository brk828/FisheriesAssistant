# LCR MSCP Habitat Credit Performance Standards: Peer Review Request

#ichthyology #aquatic-ecology #habitat-conservation

## Executive Summary

This document is a correspondence file, not a research study. It contains a request from the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Lower Colorado Regional Office) for peer review of the proposed Habitat Credit Performance Standards for the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] ([[LCR MSCP]]). The performance standards are meant to track whether habitat creation goals under the [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] (HCP) are met over the 50-year life of the program. The request was sent to species experts, including [[Paul Marsh]], who replied on January 20, 2011. Comments were due by February 11, 2011. Marsh judged the document to be more political than technical and suggested a group or one-on-one discussion with species experts in place of a formal written review.

## Background & Description

The LCR MSCP is a multi-stakeholder partnership of federal and non-federal entities. It balances water use in the Lower Basin of the Colorado River with conservation of native species and their habitats, in order to comply with the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA).

- **HCP scope:** A 50-year plan to create over 8,100 acres of riparian, marsh, and backwater habitat.
- **Covered species:** 26 federal and state-listed endangered, threatened, candidate, and sensitive species, including birds, fish, mammals, reptiles, amphibians, insects, and plants. The document does not name the fish species.
- **Habitat creation goals:** Set for 20 of the 26 covered species. The HCP requires habitat mosaics that serve multiple covered species.

The proposed Habitat Credit Performance Standards document defines the procedure for tracking conservation measure accomplishment. The correspondence was sent with two companion forms: a Habitat Credit Performance Standards Comment Form and an LCR MSCP Comment Review Form. The materials were distributed on compact disk.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes the habitat creation approach in general terms:

- Conservation measures are based on **minimum habitat requirements** for each covered species, not on whether the species is present or absent.
- Performance standards must be **measurable and meaningful**. They should define the threshold at which a created land cover type becomes covered-species habitat.
- Habitat quality can be improved later through the **[[Adaptive Management]]** program.
- Because sites are designed as habitat mosaics, accomplishment is tracked at a **landscape scale**.
- Performance standards may be revised as new information comes from research and monitoring programs.

The document contains no field data, sampling results, or species population measurements.

## Key Findings

The correspondence sets out the following points:

1. **Review questions posed to experts.** Reviewers were asked whether the proposed performance standards:
   - (1) identify the most important limiting factors;
   - (2) are measurable;
   - (3) whether additional or different standards should be evaluated;
   - (4) what additional reference material should be considered when setting new standards.
2. **Reviewer assessment.** Paul Marsh stated he could help with the review but considered the document more political than technical. He recommended a group or one-on-one discussion among species experts and [[Reclamation-MSCP Staff]] instead of a formal written review. He offered to join such a session.
3. **Timeline.**
   - December 29, 2010: request sent by [[Sonja Kokos]].
   - January 4, 2011: formal request letter from [[John Swett]], LCR MSCP Program Manager.
   - January 20, 2011: Marsh's reply.
   - February 11, 2011: deadline for comments.
4. **Submission contact.** Comments were to go to Sonja Kokos at the Boulder City, Nevada office of the Bureau of Reclamation, by mail or email.
