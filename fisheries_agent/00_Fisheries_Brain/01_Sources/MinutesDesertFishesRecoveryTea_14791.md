# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Minutes, February 5-6, 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

These are the minutes of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting held February 5-6, 1992, at the Tonto National Forest near Phoenix, Arizona. The document is a transmittal letter to the Regional Director of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), Region II, followed by the full minutes. The team reviewed recovery plans for several listed desert and southwestern fishes, transplant proposals, habitat threats, monitoring results, listing and uplisting recommendations, and inter-agency coordination. The team recommended wider distribution of the minutes and a more responsive FWS process for handling team letters.

## Background & Description

The team is a recovery body for listed and candidate fishes of the desert Southwest and northern Mexico. Its members came from [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), the Forest Service, the Bureau of Land Management (BLM), the Bureau of Reclamation, the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, [[Centro Ecologico de Sonora]], Arizona State University and the University of Texas. The meeting was chaired by team leader W.L. Minckley, and Alejandro Varela-Romero replaced Lourdes Juarez-Romero as a member.

Species under discussion included:

- **Desert pupfish** (*Cyprinodon macularius*), with a recovery plan in technical draft.
- **Yaqui fishes**: [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*), [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*). [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Cyprinella formosa*) was also considered for inclusion.
- **[[Sonora Chub]]**, whose scientific name is garbled in the OCR text.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), whose plan is being revised.
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*) and **loach minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*).
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), recently listed.
- **Roundtail chub** (*Gila robusta*) and **Gila chub** (*Gila intermedia*), recommended for listing.
- Several non-target species, including [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most discussed threats were introduced or human-caused:

- **Nonnative fishes**: largemouth bass spread through [[Aravaipa Creek]] after escaping from a private pond. Mosquitofish threaten topminnow, and red shiner are increasing above the Moreci diversion dam on Eagle Creek.
- **Water use**: groundwater pumping for agriculture in Sonora threatens [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]. Groundwater development in the Big and Little Chino Valleys could deplete the upper Verde River. Proposed withdrawals of up to 73% of low flows threaten the White River.
- **Roads and spills**: a border road for drug interdiction (Joint Task Force 6) would cross Black Draw near the refuge. Toxic spills at a road crossing were cited as a risk to Sonora chub in Sycamore Canyon.
- **Recreational fishing**: rainbow trout predation threatens Little Colorado spinedace, and gamefish stocking and reservoir projects create conflicts with native species.
- **Invasive pathways**: stock tanks and private ponds can leak nonnatives into creeks, and canals develop fish faunas regardless of stocking.

## Key Findings

**Recovery plans and schedules**
- The desert pupfish plan was sent for review on Dec. 17, 1991, with comments due Feb. 10, 1992 (one received). The plan is due as final by February 1994, but the Phoenix office hoped to finish earlier.
- The Yaqui fishes plan is being rewritten by Cobble, Minckley and George Divine. Its due date may be September 1992.
- The Sonora chub plan was nearly ready for Federal Register publication, with a final signed plan due Sept. 30, 1992.
- The Gila topminnow revision is targeted for a draft by the end of summer 1992.
- Final critical habitat rules for spikedace and loach minnow are due Sept. 30, 1992.

**Aravaipa Creek**
- No red shiner have been found since February 1991.
- Largemouth bass appeared in July 1991, spread rapidly, and had not been found since November 1991.
- Diet analyses: green sunfish ate almost exclusively invertebrates; largemouth bass ate a high percentage of fish, mainly longfin dace; yellow bullhead ate fish and invertebrates, including loach minnow and spikedace.
- Monitoring funding continues until June 1992. BLM has funding for 5 years at $10,000 per year.
- The team recommended that the **upper barrier not be built now**, since it would harm speckled dace habitat and provide little benefit during drought. Its design should be finalized and kept ready.
- The team supports building the **lower barrier** as soon as possible. The barrier is delayed by Section 7 consultation on the Central Arizona Project. Bureau of Reclamation estimated 18 months to 3 years after consultation to finish it.
- Minckley suggested a double barrier at the lower site.

**Sonora chub and Sycamore Canyon**
- The proposed transplant to side drainages of Sycamore Canyon remains preliminary. The proposal must clear AGFD procedures, then the Commission, then NEPA and Section 7 review.
- Stefferud questioned the cost and recommended maintaining existing fences and monitoring natural populations instead.
- Hendrickson saw no need for transplants from a whole-species perspective, but suggested they might be justified if Sycamore Creek chub are genetically distinct from Mexican populations.
- The team agreed on the need for genetic work.

**Gila topminnow genetics**
- Stock management is unresolved. Hendrickson proposed adding Mexican stock to U.S. reintroductions based on new mtDNA data from Vrijenhoek and Quattro.
- Minckley opposed this and recommended more genetic data on U.S. stocks, testing Boyce-Thompson stock, and laboratory fitness experiments on U.S./Mexican crosses.
- Clark Hubbs found that mosquitofish predation varies among populations and persists across generations. The Bog Hole population was highly predaceous.

**Yaqui catfish and Dexter Hatchery**
- Jensen expects to know the genetic status of Yaqui catfish at Dexter by spring.
- Minckley warned against stocking private ponds without binding landowner agreements.

**Bog Hole and Redrock**
- The Bog Hole renovation was recommended in 1986 and is still not completed. An Environmental Assessment and Section 7 consultation are underway. Pupfish were last found there in 1989, and none were found in 1991 sampling.
- Team consensus was to attempt treatment despite low odds of success on the first try.
- At Redrock Wildlife Area, topminnow were absent in winter 1990 but reported in summer 1991. Water diversion from the river almost guarantees mosquitofish contamination.

**Gila Box and Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area**
- The team approved the list of 15 native fishes proposed for the BLM management plan.
- The team recommended restoration of native fishes, including reintroduction of extirpated species. The main limiting factor is a large catfish population. Options include ongoing catfish reduction and an all-you-can-take fishing area with legal setlines.

**Listing, uplisting and status**
- The team recommended listing roundtail chub as endangered and Gila chub as threatened. A petition for roundtail chub was discussed, but no resolution was reached.
- Spikedace uplisting to endangered was approved by vote, while loach minnow was split 3-3. Stefferud was asked to write a letter recommending captive propagation for both species.
- Minckley noted that the Verde River spikedace may be redescribed as a distinct species, which would qualify it for endangered listing.
- Flannelmouth sucker's candidate status was clarified to apply to its whole range, not just the lower basin.

**Upper Verde River**
- A 789-acre private development in Big and Little Chino Valleys (1,385 lots, two golf courses, 20 acres of lakes, 1,120 acre-feet per year, all from groundwater) would deplete flow. FWS is working toward a [[Section 10 Habitat Conservation Planning]] process with Yavapai County and the developer.

**Policy and coordination**
- The team declined to write an overall policy on transplants. Decisions should be made case by case, referring to the American Fisheries Society guidelines.
- The team recommended a cooperatively funded, interagency database for southwestern fishes. It also agreed that all team letters to the Regional Director should be cc'd to Sally Stefferud, who will draft responses.
- Minckley and others expressed frustration at slow progress and unanswered team letters.
- The team recommended sending the minutes to other agencies, which Stefferud would look into.

**Other items**
- Bylas Springs global warming project (Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office): Gila topminnow made up about 2% of fish at the springhead and barrier of Sl in February 1991. Sl barrier is still piping despite bentonite. The source gives a December 1992 date for later sampling, which appears to be an error.
- Centro Ecologico surveys (July-September 1991) at Cajon Bonito found healthy ornate minnow and roundtail chub and no exotic fish. A new Gila chub population was found in an Arroyo de los Fresnos tributary of the upper San Pedro. Roundtail chub and beautiful shiner were abundant in the Rio Bavispe above Novillo.
- Permit problems in Mexico have halted binational projects until SEDUE approves them.
- The FDA cancellation of most fish-production chemicals could set back propagation of listed fishes by about 10 years, according to Jensen.
