## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 letter from W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) to Dr. Donald G. Buth (UCLA) concerning a collaborative pre-proposal submitted to the Bureau of Reclamation for genetic analysis of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (implied *Xyrauchen texanus*). The letter delineates a division of research labor, allocating mtDNA analysis to Minckley's group and allozyme electrophoresis to Buth's lab. It outlines sampling logistics constrained by conservation status—since target specimens must be released alive or transferred to hatcheries—and identifies the sympatric catostomid fauna to be sampled alongside the focal species from two key localities: the mainstream Colorado River (CO) and the upper Green River (UT). The letter also references pending funding (~$100,000 over 2–4 years) from the Bureau of Reclamation for razorback "broodstock" genetics studies.

## Background & Description
While primarily a logistical/administrative document, it contains taxonomically relevant morphological notes on the Colorado River Basin sucker (Catostomidae) assemblage:

- **[[Pantosteus discobolus]]** (Bluehead Sucker): Noted as possessing **two distinct morphological types** within the sampled populations, suggesting either significant intraspecific variation or potential cryptic taxa warranting genetic scrutiny.
- **[[Pantosteus platyrhynchus]]** (Mountain Sucker): Described as rare in the sampled localities, indicating low population density or restricted range overlap in the study area.
- **[[Catostomus commersoni]]** (White Sucker): Explicitly noted as an **introduced** (non-native) species within the Colorado River drainage, relevant as a potential hybridization vector or genetic contaminant.
- **[[Catostomus latipinnis]]** (Flannelmouth Sucker): Listed as a native congener co-occurring with the target species.
- Interspecific **hybrids** are noted to occur among "any and all" of the above taxa, as well as between these species and the [[Razorback Sucker]], complicating pure-species genetic sampling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The letter frames a multi-species catostomid community within two large-river habitats of the Colorado River Basin:

- **Locality 1**: Mainstream **Colorado River, Colorado (CO)**
- **Locality 2**: Upper **Green River, Utah (UT)**

Within these habitats, the expected co-occurring sucker fauna includes:
- *Catostomus latipinnis* (native)
- *Catostomus commersoni* (introduced)
- *Pantosteus discobolus* (native, two morphotypes)
- *Pantosteus platyrhynchus* (native, rare)
- *Xyrauchen texanus* (Razorback Sucker; target endangered species)

The ecological complexity is heightened by extensive **hybridization** among these congeners, a common feature of catostomid fishes in disturbed or altered river systems, and one that necessitates careful genetic (rather than purely morphological) species identification for conservation broodstock programs. The conservation-sensitive status of the Razorback Sucker dictates non-lethal sampling constraints (muscle biopsy/plugs) since specimens must be returned alive to the wild or transferred to a hatchery facility, contrasting with the "other suckers," which could be lethally sampled and retained as frozen/preserved voucher specimens.

## Key Findings
- **Research Division of Labor**: Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis was to be conducted by the Minckley (ASU) group, while allozyme electrophoresis was assigned entirely to the Buth (UCLA) laboratory.
- **Sampling Constraint**: Due to the endangered/protected status of the [[Razorback Sucker]], only non-lethal muscle plug biopsies were anticipated as viable tissue sources, as fish needed to be released alive or moved to a hatchery.
- **Broader Taxon Sampling Strategy**: A recommendation was made to increase tissue harvest effort for "other suckers" collected at the same localities (Colorado River, CO and upper Green River, UT), including retention of frozen voucher specimens for future preservation and comparative study.
- **Taxonomic Complexity**: The presence of two morphological types within *Pantosteus discobolus* and widespread hybridization among sympatric catostomids underscore the necessity of combined molecular approaches (mtDNA + allozymes) to resolve species boundaries and assess genetic purity for the Razorback Sucker broodstock program.
- **Funding Context**: Approximately $100,000 (possibly higher) was reportedly earmarked by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation for razorback sucker "broodstock" genetics work over a 2–4 year period, contingent on annual budget availability, with Bob Williams identified as the Bureau of Reclamation funding contact.
- **Collaborative Network**: The letter references coordination with Jim Deacon (Las Vegas) and "Tom," indicating a broader multi-institutional conservation genetics initiative for the [[Colorado River Basin]] catostomid fauna during this period.
