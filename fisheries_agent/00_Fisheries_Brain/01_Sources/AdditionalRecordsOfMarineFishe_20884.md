# Additional Records of Marine Fishes from Alligator Harbor, Florida, and Vicinity

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-faunal-survey

## Executive Summary

This short faunal note by [[Ratpo W. Yerger]] of The Florida State University supplements an earlier checklist of the fishes of [[Alligator Harbor]] (Joseph and Yerger, 1956). It reports 18 species from 14 families, several of them new records for the harbor, for the surrounding Gulf waters, or for the northern Gulf of Mexico. Records come from the FSU Fish Collection (catalogued specimens) and from reports by competent field biologists. The note situates the harbor within a broader regional effort of marine laboratories along the Florida to Texas coast.

## Background & Description

The paper is a supplement to a previous survey of the harbor fishes. Alligator Harbor, in Franklin County, Florida, is the site of the main laboratory of the Oceanographic Institute at [[Florida State University]]. Several species previously recorded from outside waters were now taken inside the harbor, while others are new for the area, particularly near the [[Whistle 26 Buoy]], about ten miles south of the harbor.

Methods and conventions:
- Common names follow the [[American Fisheries Society]] approved list (1960).
- Specimens bearing FSU catalogue numbers are held in the [[FSU Fish Collection]]; other records are reports from field biologists, credited under each species.
- All measurements are standard length in millimetres.

The author notes that many specimens were consumed by anglers rather than preserved, which limited scientific documentation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The note places the Alligator Harbor records within a regional framework. Marine laboratories at Cape Haze, Tampa Bay, Cedar Key, Alligator Harbor and Panama City have produced year-round collections from stations separated by short distances, improving zoogeographic knowledge of the [[Gulf of Mexico]] fish fauna. Relevant comparative works cited include [[Springer and Woodburn]] (1960) on the Tampa Bay fishes and [[Marshall]] (1958) on the snook fishery.

Habitat contrasts recur through the records:
- **Harbor and shallow tidal waters:** boat channels, sandbars, beaches and mud bottoms, sampled with seines, fyke nets and crab pots.
- **Offshore rocky habitats:** limestone ledges with boulders at about 45 feet near Whistle "26" Buoy, rocky bottoms, and reef-type habitats explored by aqualung divers.
- **Mud-dominated harbor bottom:** considered unlikely to host species such as the gag, which favours rocky offshore bottoms.

## Key Findings

**Records new or notable for the area**

- **Atlantic sailfish** ([[Istiophorus albicans]]): four individuals, 3 to 5 feet long, caught July 20 to August 2, 1959, about seven miles southeast of Alligator Point. Believed to be the first record for the Big Bend region; sailfish are otherwise taken each summer from Destin westward.
- **Sand stargazer** ([[Dactyloscopus tridigitatus]]): one 44 mm specimen (FSU 2?60) taken offshore from Alligator Peninsula at 10 feet in January 1957. Believed to be the first northern Gulf record; identity confirmed by James E. Böhlke of the Academy of Natural Sciences, Philadelphia.
- **Bluntnose flyingfish** ([[Prognichthys gibbifrons]]): one 99 mm juvenile (FSU 6554) from less than 3 feet of water near St. Teresa, October 1, 1960. First record in shallow tidal waters along this part of the Gulf coast.
- **Smooth puffer** ([[Lagocephalus laevigatus]]): one 310 mm specimen (FSU 3714) caught June 20, 1958, at the harbor mouth; a second taken about 15 miles northeast near St. Marks Light. Considered rare locally.
- **Snook** ([[Centropomus undecimalis]]): one capture in a crab fyke 400 yards west of the Marine Laboratory, date unrecorded. Described as the only snook species in the northern Gulf and uncommon north of Levy County.
- **Northern sennet** ([[Sphyraena borealis]]): one 46 mm young (FSU 4922), March 29, 1956, identity verified by Donald De Sylva.
- **Smoothhead scorpionfish** ([[Scorpaena calcarata]]): one 108 mm specimen (FSU 4910), caught by rod and reel near Whistle "26" Buoy, October 11, 1958.

**Range extensions and confirmations inside or near the harbor**

- **Skipjack herring** ([[Alosa chrysochloris]]): one 131 mm specimen (FSU 6354), May 14, 1960, in a dredged boat channel west of the FSU Marine Laboratory.
- **Gizzard shad** ([[Dorosoma cepedianum]]): seined repeatedly in the harbor by R. W. Menzel; one 189 mm specimen (FSU 2556, April 5, 1957) and a series of adults (FSU 6382, 215 to 239 mm) from the same boat channel.
- **Gag** ([[Mycteroperca microlepis]]): one 227 mm specimen (FSU 4921) taken in a crab pot at the Marine Laboratory pier, August 2, 1958. Fairly common offshore over rock but rarely enters the mud-bottomed harbor.
- **Tripletail** ([[Lobotes surinamensis]]): one capture in a fyke net on the sandbar 400 yards west of the laboratory (October 4, 1955). Adults up to 20 inches observed floating in harbor waters in September and October.

**Offshore reef and ledge species**

- **Southern sea bass** ([[Centropristes striatus melanus]]): common over rocky bottoms near Whistle "26" Buoy; four young (7 to 60 mm, FSU 2051) taken on a sandbar at the harbor mouth on May 16, 1954.
- **Belted sandfish** ([[Serranellus subligarius]]): one 59 mm specimen (FSU 3279) from a limestone ledge about 45 feet deep, two miles southeast of Whistle "26" Buoy, May 30, 1957. Common around jetties at Panama City.
- **Cubbyu** ([[Equetus acuminatus]]): two small specimens (20 and 65 mm, FSU 3278), May 30, 1957, from the same area as the belted sandfish.
- **Cocoa damselfish** ([[Eupomacentrus variabilis]]): two juveniles (17 and 21 mm, FSU 6023) collected with the sandfish and cubbyu; identified by Loren P. Woods.

**Other records**

- **Butterfish** ([[Poronotus triacanthus]]): about 40 specimens across six FSU lots, collected from early April to mid-May; juveniles of 44 to 67 mm from seine and trawl collections in the harbor and at Mud Cove.
- **Leopard toadfish** ([[Opsanus pardus]]): one 164 mm specimen (FSU 2548) from 35 to 40 feet, 14 miles SSE of St. Marks Light, July 3, 1955. Probably more common offshore than collections suggest.
- **Atlantic midshipman** ([[Porichthys porosissimus]]): one 103 mm specimen (FSU 1387), near Whistle "26" Buoy, October 18, 1952. Shrimp trawlers adjoining Alligator Peninsula take considerable numbers.

**Overall conclusion:** The note adds 18 species to the Alligator Harbor fauna documentation, several of them first or rare regional records, and emphasises that the harbor, offshore ledges and nearby Gulf waters together support a more diverse fish assemblage than earlier surveys indicated.
