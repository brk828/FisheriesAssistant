# WBNFH FY 1996 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This annual report from the [[Fish & Wildlife Service]] covers the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] in Willow Beach, Arizona, for the fiscal year 1996 (October 1, 1995 through September 30, 1996). It summarizes three areas: extensive-culture hatchery production, wild broodstock collection, and fish health activities. The main species handled were [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], both native to the Colorado River system, along with [[Rainbow Trout]]. Much of the production was stocked into [[Lake Mohave]] or transferred to other facilities for growout or broodstock use. The report is a mostly tabular administrative record, and several OCR-degraded values are noted below.

## Background & Description

The report is a set of standard Fish & Wildlife Service forms for a National Fish Hatchery:

- **Hatchery Production Summary (Form 3-103b):** Records fish harvested by number and weight, length, fertilizer, feed expended, cost, and survival for extensive culture. Extensive culture here means low-intensity rearing with minimal intervention.
- **Wild Broodstock Summary (Form 3-101):** Records wild-caught adults, spawning, egg take, and eyed-egg percentages.
- **Fish Health Activities Summary (Form 3-108):** Records problems, species affected, therapeutic treatments, results, and chemical use and cost.

The report is signed by Peter A. Stine, Geographic Manager, Arizona, under the program review section. The signature dates are not legible in the scan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery's output supports the recovery and conservation of native Colorado River fishes. Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub are native species that are conservation priorities in the lower Colorado River basin. Production lots are linked to [[Lake Mohave]] stocking, with broodstock transferred to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] for further propagation.

Fish health records show a range of external parasite, bacterial, and columnaris problems across the three species. These are typical pressures in intensive hatchery culture, and the records show they were managed with chemical and biological treatments. The report does not include water-quality measurements, habitat surveys, or population monitoring data for the lake or the river.

## Key Findings

### Hatchery Production (Extensive Culture, Oct 1995 to Sep 1996)

Production lots recorded in the summary include:

- **1995 RBS** (Razorback Sucker): fish for growout on Lake Mohave.
- **1994 BTC** (Bonytail Chub): fish for growout on Lake Mohave.
- **1996 BTC** (Bonytail Chub): two lots from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **1996 RBS** (Razorback Sucker): fish sent to [[Parker Arizona]] for growout.
- **1996 RBS**: broodstock for Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- **1996 RBS**: fish sent to the [[Phoenix Zoo]].

Recorded harvest numbers and weights include approximately 25,000 fish (156 kg), 4,868 fish (30 kg), 1,800 fish (18.2 kg), 1,886 fish (7.5 kg), and 50,000 fish (300 kg). The OCR scan does not reliably align these values with specific lots, so they should be verified against the original form before use. A value reading "2000 18kg" is also present but is ambiguous.

### Wild Broodstock (Oct 1995 to Sep 1996)

- The wild broodstock summary lists one stock: [[Bonytail Chub]] from Lake Mohave, categorized as wild caught in 1996.
- Spawning numbers, egg take, eyed-egg percentages, and retained-egg counts are not legible in the scan, so they cannot be reported.
