# Hybridization Between the Cyprinid Fishes *Luxilus albeolus*, *L. cornutus*, and *L. cerasinus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines hybridization among three cyprinid minnows, [[Luxilus Cornutus]] (Common Shiner), [[Luxilus Cerasinus]] (Crescent Shiner), and [[Luxilus Albeolus]] (White Shiner), at a single contact zone in Barbour Creek, a tributary of the James River drainage in Virginia. The authors combined morphological scale counts and principal component analysis (PCA), allozyme electrophoresis, and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) restriction fragment analysis to classify individuals as pure parental types or hybrids. Of 54 Barbour Creek individuals, 28 were classified as pure *L. cornutus*, 6 as pure *L. cerasinus*, and 20 as hybrids. The authors conclude that extensive hybridization occurs between *L. cornutus* and *L. cerasinus*, and they test whether the hybrid origin of *L. albeolus*, proposed in earlier work, is supported.

## Background & Description

The family [[Cyprinidae]] is a diverse group of freshwater fishes, and many of its species are morphologically very similar, which complicates identification. The genus *Luxilus* includes the three focal species, which are closely related and differ in scale counts, with genetic and morphological evidence of shared ancestry.

Earlier work suggested that *L. albeolus* arose through hybridization between *L. cornutus* and *L. cerasinus*. [[Menzel]] (1972) proposed this hybrid origin based on electrophoretic data, while [[Gilbert]] (1980) reported that *L. albeolus* shares morphological and mtDNA characters with *L. cerasinus*. Previous analyses were limited by sampling and by the use of mostly single-character methods. The present study aimed to characterize hybridization in a single population and to evaluate the hybrid origin hypothesis using multiple character sets, with [[Principal Component Analysis]] used to combine morphological data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three species occupy overlapping ranges in Atlantic slope and Mississippi basin drainages. *L. cornutus* has the widest distribution, while *L. cerasinus* and *L. albeolus* are more narrowly distributed in Atlantic drainages, including the [[Roanoke River]] and [[James River]] of Virginia and the [[Rappahannock River]], [[Patuxent River]], and [[Potomac River]] of Maryland and Virginia. The Barbour Creek contact zone lies in the James River drainage, where *L. cornutus* and *L. cerasinus* co-occur.

## Key Findings

- **Morphology:** Scale counts (anterior dorsolateral, circumferential, and caudal peduncle scales) differed significantly among species (Tukey's test, P < 0.001, except caudal peduncle scales for *L. albeolus* vs. *L. cornutus*), but no single count was diagnostic. PCA of the three counts explained 97% of the variance, with *L. cornutus* scoring highest, *L. cerasinus* lowest, and *L. albeolus* intermediate.
- **Allozymes:** Four of five surveyed loci (Ldh-B, mMdhp-A, Pep-B, Pgm-A) were diagnostic between *L. cornutus* and *L. cerasinus*. Among the 54 Barbour Creek individuals, 28 were pure *L. cornutus*, 6 were pure *L. cerasinus*, and 20 were hybrids.
- **Hybrid generations:** Of the 20 hybrids, 17 were probably F₁ individuals, heterozygous at all four loci. The remaining three were advanced-generation hybrids, most likely backcrosses to *L. cornutus*.
- **mtDNA:** Restriction fragment analysis with 15 endonucleases distinguished the three species' mtDNA. Twenty-six individuals carried *L. cornutus* haplotypes, 21 carried *L. cerasinus* haplotypes, and 7 carried *L. albeolus* haplotypes. *L. albeolus* and *L. cornutus* differed by about 2% sequence divergence, while *L. cerasinus* differed by about 9%.
- **Multicharacter analysis:** Six pure *L. cerasinus* and 18 pure *L. cornutus* clustered in expected quadrants. One individual was tentatively classified as pure *L. albeolus*, with a PCA score of 32.2 close to the expected *L. albeolus* range (32.7–37.9). Other individuals could not be assigned with confidence to a parental type.
- **Hybrid origin hypothesis:** The authors conclude that the hybrid origin of *L. albeolus* is not supported by the present data. The evidence instead points to ancestral polymorphism, convergence, and past hybridization as explanations for shared characters.
- **Mitochondrial introgression:** mtDNA of *L. cerasinus* was found in individuals whose nuclear genotypes were mostly *L. cornutus*, indicating asymmetric introgression of mtDNA between species.
