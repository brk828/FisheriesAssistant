# A List of the Fishes of Oklahoma (1952 Checklist)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #oklahoma-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This is a 1952 checklist of the fishes of [[Oklahoma]], compiled by Dr. George A. Moore and published by the State Game & Fish Department in Oklahoma City. It lists numbered entries for fish species grouped by family, from [[Lamprey]] through [[Sculpin]] families, with scientific authorities and some common names used locally. Interspersed with the list are short angler-oriented notes on fishing regulations, conservation, pollution and nomenclature. The document is a reference list and public-education booklet, not a research study, and it reports no population data, sample sizes or measurements.

## Background & Description

- **Publisher:** State Game & Fish Department, Oklahoma City, Oklahoma.
- **Compiler:** Dr. George A. Moore.
- **Date:** 1952.
- **Structure:** Species are arranged by family, with numbered entries running from 1 to 157. Each entry gives a common name, a scientific name and an authority (for example, *Lepisosteus osseus* (Linnaeus)).
- **Nomenclature:** The text explains that scientific names avoid confusion because common names are not standardized. Common names follow the [[American Fisheries Society]] list where possible, or have the approval of committee chairman Dr. R. M. Bailey of the Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan.
- **Local names:** The document lists regional names used in Oklahoma, keyed to check list numbers. Examples include "spoonbill cat" or "shoveler" for [[Paddlefish]] (No. 4), "grindle cat" for [[Bowfin]] (No. 9), "skipjack" for River Herring (No. 10), "sand bass" for [[White Bass]] (No. 103), "brownie" for [[Smallmouth Bass]] (No. 106), and "goggleye" or "rock bass" for No. 109. The author recommends avoiding names such as "spoonbill cat" and "grindle cat" because they wrongly imply relationship to the catfish family.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Freshwater focus:** Most listed taxa are freshwater species of Oklahoma streams, rivers, lakes and farm ponds. The document does not give habitat details for individual species, and its ocean basin field is therefore N/A. The sheepshead (No. 156) is included, but its habitat is not stated.
- **Pollution:** The text names pollution as "one of the worst enemies of fish." Common causes listed are waste oil and salt water, soil erosion that deposits silt in streams and lakes, and municipal sewage discharged untreated into streams.
- **Habitat management tips:** Old Christmas trees and brush cuttings, wired together and sunk in deep holes, are described as "fine concentration places for crappie and sunfish."
- **Population management:** Farm ponds should be fished heavily to guard against overpopulation of stunted fishes. Bluegill or bream "reproduce very rapidly in most ponds and should be fished heavily."
- **Commercial fishing:** The author states that removing non-game fish with commercial nets and seines provides better living conditions for game fish.

Related concepts: [[Stunted Fish Populations]], [[Concentration Structures]], [[Water Pollution]], [[Commercial Netting]].

## Key Findings

**Checklist content (no numerical or statistical data reported)**

- **Lampreys:** [[Chestnut Lamprey]] and [[Southern Brook Lamprey]] (Nos. 1–2).
- **Sturgeon and paddlefish:** [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (No. 3) and [[Paddlefish]] (No. 4).
- **Gars:** Alligator gar, [[Shortnose Gar]], [[Spotted Gar]] and [[Longnose Gar]] (Nos. 5–8).
- **Bowfin and herrings:** [[Bowfin]] (No. 9), River Herring, [[Ohio Shad]] and [[Gizzard Shad]] (Nos. 10–12).
- **Salmon, pike and mooneye:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (No. 13), Grass and [[Chain Pickerel]] (Nos. 14–15), and [[Goldeye]] (No. 16).
- **Suckers (Nos. 17–30):** Includes blue sucker, three buffalo species, two carpsuckers, four redhorses, hog sucker, [[White Sucker]], spotted sucker and creek chubsucker.
- **Minnows and carp (Nos. 31–79):** Includes [[Carp]], [[Goldfish]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Creek Chub]] and a large group of shiners (*Notropis* spp.), chubs and dace. The document also lists [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[Stoneroller]].
- **Catfishes and madtoms (Nos. 80–91):** Includes [[Channel Catfish]] (No. 81), [[Blue Catfish]] (No. 80), [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Flathead Catfish]] and several madtom species.
- **Other families (Nos. 92–157):** [[American Eel]] (No. 92), topminnows and killifishes (Nos. 93–98), [[Gambusia]] (No. 99), [[Pirateperch]] (No. 100), silversides (Nos. 101–102), [[White Bass]] and [[Yellow Bass]] (Nos. 103–104), [[Spotted Bass]] (No. 105), [[Largemouth Bass]] (No. 107), [[Bluegill]] (No. 117), [[Black Crappie]] and [[White Crappie]] (Nos. 121–122), [[Sauger]], [[Walleye]] and [[Yellow Perch]] (Nos. 123–125), numerous darters (Nos. 126–154), [[Freshwater Drum]] (No. 155), [[Sheepshead]] (No. 156) and [[Banded Sculpin]] (No. 157).

**Incomplete entries as printed**

- No. 91, "Furious madtom, *Schilbeodes* (species)", and No. 127, "Leopard darter, *Hadropterus* (species)", give no species epithet.
- No. 75 and some entries in the Minnow Family lack legible names because of scan damage.

**Angler guidance and conservation messages**

- Keep a record of fish caught in Oklahoma waters: date, bait, place, depth, hour, kind and weight.
- Oklahoma's fishing laws have no closed seasons. Anglers should observe bag limits for game fish.
- The [[Channel Catfish]] is described as "Oklahoma's aristocrat of the catfish family," valued for its fighting qualities and flavor.
- Fly fishermen are especially fond of the bluegill or bream.
- Students are encouraged to organize a Fish Study Club at their school.
- Pollution control is needed, as described in the Ecological & Environmental Context section.
