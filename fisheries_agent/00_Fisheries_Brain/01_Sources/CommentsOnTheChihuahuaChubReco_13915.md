# Review Comments on Chub Recovery Plan Document

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document contains specific review comments on a recovery plan for an endangered chub species in the [[Mimbres River]] system. The reviewers provide detailed feedback on distribution mapping, habitat assessment, reasons for population decline, and conservation strategies. Key concerns include incomplete geographic information, contradictory statements about flooding impacts, and questions about habitat suitability versus current chub persistence.

## Background & Description

The document reviews a recovery plan for chub populations in the [[Mimbres River]] drainage system in [[Grant County]], New Mexico, with distribution extending into Mexico. The species occupies [[Bear Canyon Reservoir]], a privately-owned spring creek, and reaches between [[Allie Canyon]] and Mimbres Post Office. Historical distribution data and current population status are discussed in the original plan being reviewed. The reviewers note that some life history observations on young chubs have been made, though these data are not fully presented in the biology section of the original document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The chub's habitat encompasses both riverine and reservoir environments in the Mimbres River system. Reviewers emphasize the need to assess physical, chemical, and biological parameters equally when evaluating habitat quality. The document references severe flooding between late 1978 and early 1979, which reportedly resulted in a three- to four-fold increase in adult fish populations. However, this positive impact contradicts earlier statements about negative flooding effects on page 6 of the reviewed document.

Critical habitat questions include: whether [[Bear Canyon Reservoir]] is a mainstem or off-river reservoir, chub occurrence in discharge streams or tail-race areas, and the length of various habitat reaches. The reviewers note that existing habitat in the [[Mimbres River]] appears unsuitable for chub, raising the fundamental question of why chubs persist there. Historical habitat loss is considered a major factor in decline, along with introductions of exotic fishes.

## Key Findings

1. **Distribution Data Gaps**: The review identifies missing information including river distances between [[Allie Canyon]] and Mimbres Post Office, length of privately-owned spring creek, historical reach length occupied by chubs, and current versus historical distribution in Mexico (in miles or kilometers and percentages).

2. **Map Improvements Needed**: Figure 1 requires additions including scale (miles or kilometers), demarcation of [[Allie Canyon]], demarcation of private spring tributary, explanation of circled numerals, and demarcation of [[Grant County]] line.

3. **Habitat Decline Assessment**: Reviewers recommend equal treatment of physical, chemical, and biological habitat parameters. The original document attempts to credit physical changes for decline while suggesting biological changes (exotic fish introductions) had minor roles. Reviewers argue all factors together are responsible, and no single factor can be decidedly identified.

4. **Structural Recommendations**: The section on reasons for decline should follow biology and conservation discussions. Historical habitat should be described before discussing habitat loss. Both historical and presently-utilized habitats should be presented and compared for quantity and quality.

5. **Life History Data**: Life history observations on young chubs are inferred from various text passages but not included in the biology section. These data should be incorporated.

6. **Terminology Issues**: Words "syntopically" (Page 7) and "exigencies" (Page 9) could not be located in standard or scientific dictionaries. More common terminology is recommended for public accessibility.

7. **Flooding Impact Contradiction**: First paragraph of Conservation section (Page 9) states severe flooding (late 1978-early 1979) had very positive impact with three- to four-fold increase in adult fish, contradicting Page 6 statements about flooding effects.

8. **Recovery Objectives**: Prime objective (Page 10) should change "and" to "and/or" regarding relict populations, as they exist and more may be found.

9. **Fundamental Habitat Question**: Reviewers agree with Page 13, section 121 statement regarding chub habitat. The bulk of information suggests existing [[Mimbres River]] habitat is unsuitable for chub, raising the critical question: why are chubs existing there?
