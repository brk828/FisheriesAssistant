# Sangamon Interglacial Vertebrate Fauna from Rancho la Brisca, Sonora, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleontology #herpetology #catostomidae #poeciliidae #sangamon-interglacial

## Executive Summary

This paper (Van Devender, Rea and Smith, 1985) describes a Late Pleistocene (Rancholabrean Land Mammal Age, Sangamon Interglacial) vertebrate fauna of 51 taxa from a sedimentary deposit at [[Rancho La Brisca]] in north-central [[Sonora]], Mexico. The fauna was preserved in a marshy [[Ciénega]] habitat, with 49.0% of species and 87.2% of identified bones representing aquatic or semiaquatic animals. The fossils are interpreted as an interglacial assemblage, with a climate resembling the Late Holocene, rather than a glacial one. The distributions of fish and mud turtles are used to infer past stream connections between the [[Gila River]] drainage of Arizona and the rivers of Sonora.

## Background & Description

- **Collection:** Bone material was recovered from 1975 to 1982. In June 1978, 29 burlap bags of sediment were screen-washed (about 2 mm mesh). Most specimens are housed in the Instituto de Geologia, Mexico City (IGCU 2546-2601); duplicates and casts are at the [[University of Arizona Laboratory of Paleontology]] (UALP locality #7627).
- **Identification methods:** Amphibian, reptile and small mammal remains were identified with Van Devender's osteological collection; bird remains with Rea's collection; fish with University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ) skeletons; extinct mammals and birds by comparison with [[Los Angeles County Museum]] material.
- **Setting:** The site lies on the Rio Santo Domingo at about 1000 m elevation, between the [[Arizona Upland]] subdivision of the [[Sonoran Desert]] and Mexican oak woodlands. Nearby Saracachi Ciénega is a modern analog.
- **Climate today (Saracachi, 930 m):** Mean annual temperature 16.5°C; January 8.3°C; July 25.3°C; annual precipitation 466.8 mm, with 64% falling June through August (based on only 1942-1946 data).
- **Stratigraphy:** Three units: (1) lower buff mudstone, about 3 m; (2) middle buff mudstone, about 2 m; (3) upper reddish gravel, about 2 m. Fossils occur in the lower two units, about 15 m above the canyon floor. Fragile bones show no transport damage, suggesting in situ deposition. Pollen samples were sterile.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Paleoenvironment.** The fauna is dominated by small aquatic and semiaquatic animals of permanent stream or ciénega habitats. Pseudemys scripta implies water deep enough to support it at least locally. Raptor roosts in the riparian gallery forest are proposed as the source of accumulated small-vertebrate bones.

**Paleoclimate.** Five taxa (Bufo cf. kelloggi, B. mazatlanensis, Leptodactylus melanonotus, Pternohyla fodiens, Masticophis cf. mentovarius) no longer live near the site but occur to the southwest and southeast in Sonoran desertscrub and Sinaloan thornscrub. The best modern analog community lies along the [[Rio Yaqui]] near El Novillo, about 240 km SSE. The subtropical elements, with fewer winter freezes and greater summer rainfall, argue against a glacial origin. The authors infer an interglacial thermal maximum with stronger summer monsoons, similar to the Late Holocene (last 4000 years).

**Zoogeography.** Fish (Agosia, Poeciliopsis, Catostomus) and mud turtles (Kinosternon) indicate past stream connections between Sonora and southeastern Arizona and New Mexico. Pseudemys scripta never reached Arizona, implying those connections were short-lived and shallow.

**Extinction.** Extinct taxa account for 17.6-19.6% of the fauna, concentrated in warm-blooded groups. No fish or amphibians were lost at the end of the Late Wisconsin (about 11 000 years ago).

## Key Findings

- **Fauna composition:** 493 identified bones representing 51 taxa from 29 families: fishes (5), anurans (12), reptiles (11), birds (7), mammals (13). Fish, amphibians and reptiles make up 60.8% of species and 92.9% of identified specimens.
- **Dominant taxa:** Leopard Frog ([[Rana Pipiens Complex]]) and Sonoran Mud Turtle ([[Kinosternon Sonoriense]]) together account for 55.9% of identified bones.
- **Fish records:**
  - [[Catostomus wigginsi]] (Opata Sucker): the only fossil record for the species and the genus in Mexico; specimens 80-115 mm SL.
  - [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (Longfin Dace): first fossil record for the species.
  - [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]] (Gila Topminnow) and [[Poeciliopsis monacha-occidentalis]] (all-female form): the all-female form was 2.2% of the field collection, one of the northernmost records.
- **Amphibian and reptile records:** Several first or Rancholabrean records, including Bufo alvarius (only Rancholabrean record), Kinosternon flavescens (only Rancholabrean record west of the Continental Divide and in Mexico), Pseudemys scripta (first Rancholabrean record west of the Continental Divide), and Kinosternon sonoriense (no previous fossil record).
- **Mammals and birds:** Bison (first published report for Sonora), Camelops, Equus cf. tau, Mammuthus, Odocoileus hemionus (first Pleistocene record of Mule Deer from Mexico), and the extinct Strix brea (La Brea Owl).
- **Age:** The arrival of Bison south of the ice sheets is estimated at 170 000-150 000 years ago, placing the fauna in the Sangamon Interglacial (traditionally between 125 000 and 70 000 years ago).
- **Bias:** The fauna is strongly biased toward small animals (under 0.5 kg). Only ten large-animal taxa occur, representing 19.6% of species but 3.0% of identified bones.
- **Conclusion:** The fauna records a warm, wet interglacial stream and ciénega environment in north-central Sonora, with subtropical elements and past faunal exchange with southern Arizona.
