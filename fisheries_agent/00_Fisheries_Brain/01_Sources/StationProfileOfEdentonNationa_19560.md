## Executive Summary

This station profile describes the **Edenton National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1899 and operating under the authority of the [[Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956]] (70 Stat. 1119) and subsequent appropriations legislation (71 Stat. 26). Located in Edenton, North Carolina, the hatchery's mission centers on the propagation and restoration of two anadromous fish species: the [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], reared for restoration efforts in the Savannah River, and the [[Striped Bass]] (Phase I and II), distributed to the Chesapeake Bay and to inland/coastal waters of North Carolina. The facility underwent major infrastructure rehabilitation beginning in 1990, including regrading of 18 of its 36 ponds, with the remaining ponds and the holding house scheduled for completion in FY 1991.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Shortnose Sturgeon]]**: An anadromous, benthic-feeding species native to Atlantic coast river systems; propagated at Edenton NFH specifically to support restoration in the **Savannah River**, indicating a depleted or extirpated wild population targeted for recovery.
- **[[Striped Bass]]**: An anadromous species reared through Phase I and Phase II hatchery stages before release into the **Chesapeake Bay** and North Carolina's inland and coastal waters, supporting both recreational/commercial fisheries and population restoration.
- **Distribution range**: Hatchery outputs are distributed across five states — North Carolina, Maryland, Virginia, New Jersey, and West Virginia — reflecting a broad interstate stocking program tied to Atlantic coast anadromous fish management under [[Chesapeake Bay Restoration]] initiatives.
- **Habitat infrastructure**: The hatchery itself comprises 36 ponds (~26 acres of water), 2 portable raceways, a holding house, and a 10-tank aquarium, simulating conditions necessary for rearing anadromous species prior to riverine/estuarine release.

## Key Findings

- **Dual-species mission**: Edenton NFH is one of the few federal facilities simultaneously propagating both [[Striped Bass]] and [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], linking it to two distinct restoration programs (Chesapeake Bay and Savannah River, respectively).
- **Infrastructure rehabilitation**: A major pond rehabilitation program began in 1990, with 18 of 36 ponds regraded in the first phase and the remainder scheduled for FY 1991, alongside a full renovation of the holding house — indicating aging infrastructure dating back to the hatchery's 1899 establishment.
- **Multi-state distribution network**: Fish reared at Edenton are distributed to five states (NC, MD, VA, NJ, WV), demonstrating the hatchery's regional role in Atlantic coast anadromous fish stocking.
- **Production scale**: Distribution numbers of **1,071,635**, **918,229**, **636,509**, and **1,446,370** fish (likely across different years or lots) indicate substantial annual output, though the source does not clearly delineate which figures correspond to [[Striped Bass]] versus [[Shortnose Sturgeon]].
- **Funding and staffing**: The hatchery operated with a stable staffing level of **5.0 FTEs** across FY1991–1993, with an operations budget rising from $203.2K (1991) to an estimated $240.8K (1994), alongside separate maintenance allocations (e.g., $63.2K in 1992, $54.5K in 1993).
- **Legislative authority**: Continued operation is grounded in the [[Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956]] and subsequent federal appropriations, situating the hatchery within a long-standing federal conservation aquaculture framework.
