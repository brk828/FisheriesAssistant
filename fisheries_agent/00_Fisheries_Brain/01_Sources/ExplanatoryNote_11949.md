# U.S. Bureau of Fisheries Bibliography - Geographic Regions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #historical-bibliography

## Executive Summary

This document is an explanatory note and bibliographic compilation from the [[U.S. Bureau of Fisheries]], presenting a structured catalog of fish-related publications organized by geographic region. The bibliography covers research from 1882-1904 across North American waters including the [[St. Lawrence River]], [[Gulf of Mexico]], Canadian waters, and the [[West Indies]]. The document explains the cataloging methodology, including the use of [[Library of Congress]] card numbers and chronological arrangement by author. It lists Commissioners of Fisheries from Spencer Fullerton Baird (1871-1887) through Hugh M. Smith (1913-), and provides standardized citation formats for government publications, bulletins, and reports.

## Background & Description

The bibliography employs two cataloging styles: titles reproduced exactly as published, and analytical "made titles" derived from article content. Cards for the first style were printed by the [[Library of Congress]] and could be purchased using call numbers appearing in the upper right of each page. The second style has no printed cards available.

Publications are arranged alphabetically by author within each subject heading, then chronologically under each author. Works from annual reports are combined under "United States Commissioner of Fisheries" authorship to maintain chronological order. The document notes that most articles were first issued as separates before bound volume publication, with separate issue dates given in italics alongside volume dates. The annual reports (page 7) list only separate issue dates as an exception.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The geographic scope encompasses diverse aquatic ecosystems across North America and the Caribbean:

- **[[Great Lakes]]** and connecting waters including the [[St. Lawrence River]]
- **[[Gulf of Mexico]]** coastal and offshore waters
- **Canadian waters** including western Canada, northwestern United States, and the [[Mackenzie River]] basin in British America
- **[[Grand Banks]]** fishing grounds
- **Pacific Northwest** waters of [[Washington]] and [[British Columbia]]
- **[[West Indies]]** including the [[Bahamas]], [[Cuba]], and [[Porto Rico]]
- **Mexican waters** including [[Yucatan]], [[Cozumel]], [[Mazatlan]], and rivers of central and northern Mexico
- **[[Central America]]** river systems

## Key Findings

The bibliography documents significant ichthyological research publications:

**[[St. Lawrence River]] and [[Great Lakes]]**: Evermann (1902) compiled a comprehensive species list for the Great Lakes and connecting waters (Bulletin vol. XXI, p. 95-96, Doc. 486).

**Canadian Waters**: Gilbert (1894) documented fishes from the [[Mackenzie River]] basin in British America (Bulletin vol. XIV, p. 23-25, Doc. 284). Eigenmann (1895) reported results of explorations in western Canada and northwestern United States (Bulletin vol. XIV, p. 101-132, pl. 5-8, Doc. 282).

**[[West Indies]]**: Evermann and Marsh (1900) described new genera and species from [[Porto Rico]] (Report 1899, p. 351-362, Doc. 427). Their comprehensive work "The fishes of Puerto Rico" (1903) covered 302 pages with 52 plates including 3 folding maps (Bulletin vol. XX, pt. 1, Doc. 451). Eigenmann (1904) documented fresh-water fishes of western [[Cuba]] (Bulletin vol. XXII, p. 211-236, pl. 19-21, Doc. 526).

**Mexican Waters**: Jordan and Snyder (1901) described twenty new species from Mexican rivers (Bulletin vol. XIX, p. 115-147, Doc. 436). Bean (1890) reported on fishes collected at [[Cozumel]], [[Yucatan]], with descriptions of new species (Bulletin vol. VIII, p. 193-206, pl. XXVIII-XXIX, Doc. 138). Evermann and Goldsborough (1902) compiled reports on fishes from Mexico and [[Central America]] with five new species descriptions (Bulletin vol. XXI, p. 137-159, Doc. 495).

**Fisheries Management**: Rathbun (1900) provided a comprehensive review of fisheries in contiguous waters of [[Washington]] and [[British Columbia]] (Report 1899, p. 251-350, pl. 8-16, Doc. 428).

The bibliography demonstrates the systematic geographic and taxonomic scope of early 20th century U.S. government ichthyological research, with particular emphasis on North American and Caribbean waters.
