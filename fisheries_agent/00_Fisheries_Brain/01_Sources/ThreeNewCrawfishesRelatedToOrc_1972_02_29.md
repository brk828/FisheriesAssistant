# Three New Crawfishes Related to Orconectes difficilis (Faxon)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #decapoda #astacidae #crawfish #freshwater-ecology #cambaridae #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1972 taxonomic paper by Jerry G. Watts describes **three new crawfish taxa** related to [[Orconectes difficilis]] and [[Orconectes palmeri]] (Decapoda: Astacidae), based on a thesis investigating the taxonomy of the *O. difficilis* complex. The taxa described are:

1. **[[Orconectes perfectus]]** (new species) — Alabama/Mississippi (Tombigbee River drainage)
2. **[[Orconectes difficilis blacki]]** (new subspecies) — Louisiana (Calcasieu River drainage)
3. **[[Orconectes difficilis maletae]]** (new subspecies) — Louisiana, Texas, and Oklahoma

The work is based primarily on comparative morphology of **gonopods** (copulatory appendages) in Form I and Form II males, and the **annulus ventralis** in females — standard diagnostic structures in astacid systematics. The author proposes an evolutionary hypothesis whereby *O. perfectus* represents a stock ancestral to, or nearly identical with, the ancestor of *O. difficilis hathawayi*, illustrating a plausible evolutionary sequence for the divergence of *O. difficilis* and *O. palmeri* from the ancestral **Limosus Group**.

## Background & Description

### Diagnostic Framework
Measurements follow Penn (1957), with ratios expressed as percentages of:
- **CL** – Carapace Length
- **G** – Gonopod Length
- **DMS + CP** – Distal Mesial Shaft plus Central Projection length
- **WCP** – Width of Central Projection
- **CP** – Central Projection Length
- **MP** – Mesial Process Length

### [[Orconectes perfectus]]
- Rostral margins not markedly thickened, parallel to slightly convergent
- Areola closed, narrowly open and impunctate, or open with one row of punctations
- Chela weakly costate; immovable finger not flattened, tapering gradually
- Fingers not dorsally ridged; single row of large punctations near cutting edges
- **Form I gonopod**: 25.6% of carapace length; short, reaches third coxa in situ
- Central projection: 33.2% of DMS+CP, blunt, thick basally
- Mesial process: thin, twisted, sharp-edged, untroughed; ~77.7% of central projection length
- **Form II gonopod**: 25.1% of CL; central projection tilts slightly mesially
- Copulatory hook present only on third pereiopod, large and blunt
- **Annulus ventralis**: wide-oval to subrhomboid; scalloped cephalic margin; strongly inflated caudal margin

### [[Orconectes difficilis blacki]]
- Rostral margins slightly convergent; base of rostrum depressed
- Areola closed except in some juveniles
- Immovable finger flattened in adults with distinct dorsal ridges (mesial + lateral punctation rows)
- **Form I gonopod**: 25.9% of CL; central projection 29.3% of DMS+CP
- **Mesial process**: thick, fleshy, greatly enlarged — as wide as or wider than base of central projection (key diagnostic trait)
- Centrocaudal process deeply concave just proximal to apex
- **Form II gonopod**: central projection 14.7% of DMS+CP, slants mesially (vs. laterally in *O. d. hathawayi*)
- **Annulus ventralis**: subrhomboid; length 54.5–94.5% of width (avg. 70.5%)
- Sexually dichromatic chela coloration in breeding vs. nonbreeding condition (blue keel, yellow band, red finger tips)

### [[Orconectes difficilis maletae]]
- Body/chela characters similar to *O. d. blacki*
- **Form I gonopod**: 27.9% of CL; less stout than *O. d. difficilis*/*O. d. hathawayi* but more robust than *O. palmeri*
- Central projection: 48.2% of DMS+CP — notably longer/narrower and gently curved caudad than related taxa
- Mesial process directed caudolaterally at 80–90°, keeled, troughed or expanded tip
- **Form II gonopod**: terminal elements resemble an "open beak"; central projection truncate in larger specimens
- **Annulus ventralis**: subrhomboid (morphologically indistinguishable from *O. palmeri* females)
- Breeding coloration: pale blue band on immovable finger, butter-yellow distal third, red tips reduced/absent

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All three taxa are **lotic freshwater decapods** inhabiting small streams and creeks within the Gulf Coastal Plain drainage systems of the southeastern United States.
- **O. perfectus**: Clarke and Choctaw Counties, Alabama; Clay and Lowndes Counties, Mississippi — associated with lower and upper Tombigbee River drainage.
- **O. d. blacki**: Beauregard and Calcasieu Parishes, Louisiana; intergrades with *O. d. hathawayi* found across the Calcasieu River drainage (Allen, Beauregard, Calcasieu, Vernon Parishes) — habitat linked to **late Pleistocene sediment deposits**.
- **O. d. maletae**: Natchitoches and Sabine Parishes, Louisiana; Upshur Co., Texas; intergrades with *O. d. difficilis* found in Coal Co., Oklahoma.
- Distributional patterns suggest **geological substrate correlates with speciation**: *O. d. hathawayi* occupies streams over Pliocene/early Pleistocene sediments in central Louisiana, while *O. d. blacki* is restricted to later Pleistocene deposits — implying **relatively recent divergence** and an active zone of intergradation.
- The large geographic gap between *O. perfectus* and *O. difficilis* ranges is cited as evidence that **natural interbreeding is physically impossible**, supporting species-level (rather than subspecies-level) status despite morphological similarity.

## Key Findings

- **Evolutionary hypothesis**: *O. perfectus* is proposed as morphologically representative of the probable ancestral stock leading to *O. difficilis hathawayi*, based on near-identical gonopod morphology/meristics but retention of a more "primitive" chela and annulus ventralis structure.
- Despite meristic similarity, *O. perfectus* is retained as a **full species** rather than subspecies of *O. difficilis* due to (1) retained plesiomorphic chela/annulus traits and (2) allopatric separation precluding gene flow.
- **O. d. blacki** is diagnosed principally by an **enormously enlarged Form I mesial process** (as wide as or wider than the central projection base) and a **mesial-slanting** (rather than lateral) Form II central projection — contrasting with *O. d. hathawayi*.
- **O. d. maletae** bridges morphological space between *O. d. difficilis* and *[[Orconectes palmeri]]*, distinguished by shorter gonopods reaching only to the third coxa (vs. second coxa in *O. palmeri*) and a proportionally shorter central projection (44.0–52.7% DMS+CP vs. 53.3–68.3% in *O. palmeri*).
- The paper supports a broader phylogenetic narrative: the **Limosus Group** is proposed as ancestral to both the *O. difficilis* and *O. palmeri* lineages, with the newly described taxa filling in transitional morphological and geographic gaps.
- Zones of **intergradation** are explicitly documented for both new subspecies (*O. d. blacki* × *O. d. hathawayi* in the Calcasieu River drainage; *O. d. maletae* × *O. d. difficilis* in Coal Co., Oklahoma), reinforcing a subspecies-level (rather than full species) taxonomic treatment for these two taxa.
- Comprehensive **meristic tables** (Table 1) compile standardized ratios (CP/G, MP/CP, CP/DMS+CP, MP/DMS+CP) across all *O. difficilis* subspecies including comparative MCZ type specimens, forming the quantitative backbone for species/subspecies discrimination.
- Type specimens deposited at the **Smithsonian Institution (USNM)**, with paratypes distributed among MCZ, Tulane University, and private research collections (Fitzpatrick, Black).
