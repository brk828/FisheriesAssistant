## Executive Summary

This document is an internal memorandum from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD), authored by Dean Hendrickson (Nongame Fish Biologist), outlining logistics for a multi-agency **capture-and-transport expedition** targeting native fishes of the [[Rio Yaqui]] drainage in Sonora and Chihuahua, Mexico. The primary goals were to collect live specimens of imperiled native fishes — including pupfish (*Cyprinodon* spp.), [[Gila nigrescens]], and *Notropis formosus mearnsi* — for translocation to conservation and propagation facilities in the United States, specifically the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] and the Dexter National Fish Hatchery (Dexter, NM), as well as for display/propagation at the Centro Ecológico in Mexico. The memo does not present original morphological or ecological data but instead documents the operational, permitting, and interagency coordination framework for a binational native fish rescue and repatriation effort.

## Background & Description

No morphological data, meristic counts, or taxonomic descriptions are provided in this source document. The text references named taxa only by common or scientific name without physical description:

- **Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon* spp.) — targeted in the Guzmán Basin (Palomas area, [[Rio Piedras Verdes]]).
- **[[Gila nigrescens]]** (Chihuahua chub) — targeted for survey and possible transport to New Mexico.
- **[[Notropis formosus mearnsi]]** (Yaqui chub / referenced as a distinct habitat-restricted population) — targeted at a specific historically known locality, contingent on habitat conditions matching a 1978 baseline survey.
- **Catfish** (family [[Ictaluridae]], species unspecified) — targeted along the [[Rio Sirupa]], to be captured using hoop nets.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fishes discussed occupy isolated, often relictual desert aquatic habitats within the greater [[Rio Yaqui]] drainage system, spanning northern Sonora and Chihuahua, Mexico:

- **[[Cajon Bonito]]** — a primary collection site, accessed via land requiring landowner permission (noted road obstruction/chain across access route), used as a source population for [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] restocking.
- **[[Guzman Basin]]** (including Palomas and [[Rio Piedras Verdes]]) — habitat for pupfish and *Gila nigrescens*, an endorheic basin ecologically distinct from the Yaqui mainstem.
- **[[Rio Sirupa]]** — habitat for catfish populations targeted for collection.
- Habitat conditions were noted as potentially time-sensitive ("if things are still like they were in 1978"), implying ecological or hydrological change/degradation risk at the *Notropis formosus mearnsi* locality.
- The overall framing reflects a conservation ecology concern: native Rio Yaqui fishes are treated as vulnerable, warranting **ex situ propagation** and **refuge translocation** to guard against habitat loss or extirpation in native range.

## Key Findings

- The expedition was planned as a **binational conservation translocation effort**, involving AGFD, New Mexico Game & Fish (NMGF), Dexter National Fish Hatchery, and Mexico's Centro Ecológico, reflecting early interagency cooperation on Rio Yaqui native fish conservation.
- Target species for capture and transport included [[Gila nigrescens]], pupfish (*Cyprinodon* spp.), *[[Notropis formosus mearnsi]]*, and unspecified catfish species from the [[Rio Sirupa]].
- Proposed itinerary (~9–10 days, May 26–June 5, 1987) included stops at [[Cajon Bonito]], the [[Guzman Basin]] (pupfish/*Gila* survey), multiple unspecified *Gila* localities, the *Notropis formosus mearnsi* type locality, and the [[Rio Sirupa]].
- Electrofishing (backpack shocking units), seines, dipnets, and hoop nets were the planned capture methods; at least three backpack shocking units and associated batteries were deemed necessary.
- Import logistics were planned through official border crossings at Douglas, AZ and Columbus, NM, requiring permits to be secured by Lourdes Juarez (Centro Ecológico) with ~3.5 months lead time.
- Additional research interests were folded into the trip: Dave Propst (NMGF) sought pupfish/*Gila nigrescens* specimens for New Mexico; Jerry Landye (AGFD) sought to screen collected fish for **parasites and disease**; Dave Galat expressed interest in conducting **habitat preference studies** on collected specimens.
- Successful execution depended on unresolved logistical issues at the time of writing: confirming receiving/holding facilities for transported fish (Ben Robertson, Buddy Jensen), securing landowner access at Cajon Bonito, and finalizing permit applications.
- This document exemplifies mid-1980s **applied conservation ichthyology** practice in the arid U.S.–Mexico borderlands, emphasizing rescue translocation of threatened desert fish taxa ahead of anticipated habitat decline.
