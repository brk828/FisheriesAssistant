## Executive Summary
This 1962 note documents a significant range extension of the invasive Asiatic clam, [[Corbicula fluminea]], within the Ohio River drainage system. Extensive collections along a 20-mile stretch of the Green River in Kentucky (river miles 82–102) in September 1961 revealed dense populations of the species, marking its presence roughly 100 miles upstream from the Green River's confluence with the [[Ohio River]]. This represents the first reported occurrence of the species in the Ohio drainage above the [[Cumberland River]], suggesting rapid dispersal correlating with, but not strictly limited to, commercial navigation routes.

## Background & Description
- **Common Name:** Asiatic clam
- **Maximum Size:** Individuals collected ranged up to a maximum length of 27 mm.
- **Population Structure:** No single size class dominated the samples; a range of size classes (age cohorts) were present simultaneously, indicating an established, reproducing population rather than a single recent recruitment event.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Substrate Preference:** The species demonstrated a strong correlation with substrate type, though it was not strictly limited by it.
  - **Highest Density:** Sand-mud substrates yielded the highest per-unit-area concentration of individuals.
  - **Lower Density:** Firmer substrates yielded proportionally fewer individuals.
  - **Marginal Habitats:** Specimens were still recovered from firm clay-sand river channels (dredged), under rocks, between boulders, and lodged in wood crevices.
- **Limiting Factors:** The author concludes that substrate type does **not** appear to be a strict limiting factor for the distribution of *C. fluminea*, given its presence across a wide variety of bottom types.
- **Competitive Interaction:** The author notes an apparent lack of "ecological resistance" from native fauna, speculating that *Corbicula* may achieve "complete dominance" of available native mussel habitats, posing a threat to native [[Unionidae|unionid mussels]].
- **Dispersal Vector:** The study speculates on the role of commercial shipping traffic in the rapid dispersal of the species between major waterways, though the specific stretch of the Green River studied (above Paradise, KY) has no commercial traffic, suggesting other vectors (e.g., fish, birds, or natural drift) may also contribute to upstream spread.

## Key Findings
1. **Range Extension:** Confirmed the presence of *Corbicula fluminea* in the Green River, Kentucky, extending the known range of the species within the Ohio drainage significantly upstream (100 miles from the Ohio River confluence).
2. **Upstream Boundary:** Collections made near [[Mammoth Cave National Park]] (further upstream) yielded **zero** specimens, establishing a specific upper limit for the 1961 invasion front at that time.
3. **Population Density:** The species was found in "thousands" during the survey, indicating a well-established and likely expanding population rather than a marginal presence.
4. **Comparison to Other Drainages:** This finding complements prior work by Sinclair and Isom (1961) on the species' introduction into the [[Tennessee River]] and [[Cumberland River]] drainages, with anecdotal reports placing the species as far upstream as Knoxville on the Tennessee River.
5. **Future Concerns:** The author raises the question of whether the species has invaded other Ohio River tributaries, specifically citing the [[Wabash River]] as a potential future site of invasion.
