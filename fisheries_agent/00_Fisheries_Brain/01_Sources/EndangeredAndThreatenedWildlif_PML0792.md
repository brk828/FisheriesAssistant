# Critical Habitat Designation for Woundfin and Virgin River Chub

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service final rule (Federal Register, January 26, 2000; effective February 25, 2000) designates [[Critical Habitat]] for two endangered cyprinid fishes, the [[Woundfin]] and the [[Virgin River Chub]], under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The designation covers 140.1 km (87.5 mi) of the mainstem [[Virgin River]] and its 100-year floodplain across Utah, Arizona and Nevada. It is roughly 72.5% of the woundfin's historical range and 65.3% of the chub's. The rule also finds that the economic analysis had overstated impacts, and concludes that the designation adds no incremental economic cost beyond the original listing. The document is primarily a conservation and regulatory decision, not a field study.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The woundfin (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and Virgin River chub (*Gila seminuda*) were both listed as endangered. The chub was once considered a distinct subspecies of *Gila robusta* before taxonomic revision.
- **Declines:** Both species have declined through dewatering from diversion projects, proliferation of nonnative fishes, and altered flow, temperature and sediment regimes. The Virgin River originates in south-central Utah and flows about 320 km (200 mi) to [[Lake Mead]].
- **Historical range:** Early records place woundfin from the junction of the Salt and Verde Rivers at Tempe, Arizona, to the mouth of the Gila River at Yuma, Arizona, and upstream in the Colorado River to the Virgin River and La Verkin Creek.
- **Chub range:** The chub occurs in the Virgin River from Pah Tempe Springs, Utah, downstream to the Mesquite Diversion on the Arizona–Nevada border.
- **Legal basis:** Federal agencies must ensure their actions do not jeopardize listed species or adversely modify critical habitat (Section 7). Under Section 4, economic and other impacts were considered before finalizing the boundaries.
- **Other content:** The file also contains unrelated rule text, including an FCC rule on Category 3 (CAT 3) telephone wire (47 CFR Part 68) and a California plant listing (Delphinium species). These are outside the scope of this note.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use:** Adult and juvenile chub select deep runs or pools with moderate velocities (up to about 0.26 m/s, or 2.5 ft/s), instream cover and sand substrate. Woundfin use runs and pools next to riffles with sand or sand/gravel substrates. Juveniles are generally deeper and slower, and larvae occur in shoreline margins and backwaters with filamentous algae.
- **Primary constituent elements (PCEs):**
  - *Water:* Natural seasonal temperature, turbidity and conductivity; a hydrologic regime of flow events that form and maintain channel habitat; and floodplain inundation that supplies organic matter and food.
  - *Physical habitat:* Channels, side channels, backwaters and springs, plus the runs, pools and sand/gravel areas listed above.
  - *Biological environment:* Food supply, predation and competition. Fourteen introduced species, including [[Red Shiner]], Black Bullhead, Channel Catfish and [[Largemouth Bass]], compete with or prey on the listed fishes. The red shiner is the most numerous and most problematic, feeding on eggs and early life stages.
- **Floodplain role:** Seasonally flooded areas contribute allochthonous (imported) organic matter that supports much of the fish food base.
- **Human pressures:** Water development in Washington County, Utah, and Clark County, Nevada, is a central tension. Both counties are among the fastest-growing in the United States.

## Key Findings

- **Designation:** 140.1 km (87.5 mi) of critical habitat for both species. Ownership is 57.7% federal, 39.9% private and 2.4% state.
- **Boundary:** The 100-year floodplain was chosen because it is a FEMA-defined standard that supports the river's biological integrity and natural meander. The five proposed reaches were combined into a single section.
- **Nevada revision:** The Nevada boundary was moved from Lake Mead's high-water line to Halfway Wash, reducing critical habitat there by 11.6 km (7.2 mi). Withdrawal of proposed Virgin spinedace habitat further reduced the designation.
- **Decline evidence:** Deacon (1988) documented a substantial woundfin decline between 1976 and 1993, partly linked to water-quality changes after diversion at Quail Creek. Holden and Zucker (1996) found a statistically significant negative trend (p < 0.05) at all long-term stations except one in fall.
- **Economic conclusion:** The 1995 economic analysis, based on [[Brookshire et al.]] (1995), had assumed adverse-modification thresholds were lower than jeopardy thresholds. The agency now concludes the two thresholds are nearly identical in practice, so no incremental economic impacts arise beyond those of listing. No area was excluded under Section 4(b)(2).
- **Consultation:** Federal actions affecting these fishes already require [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Jeopardy Standard]]. The critical habitat designation adds little beyond existing protections.
- **Public input:** 14 written comment letters and oral testimony were received on the proposed rule, with a public hearing in St. George, Utah. Twelve letters were received on the draft environmental assessment. A finding of no significant impact was published on November 24, 1999.
- **Regulatory certifications:** The rule was found not to be a major rule. It has no significant takings implications, no Paperwork Reduction Act requirements, and no effect on Tribal trust resources.
- **Management link:** The designation is consistent with the Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan and the Virgin River Management Plan developed with the Washington County Water Conservancy District.
