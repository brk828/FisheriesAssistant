# Six-Year Study of Survival and Vitality of Hatchery-Reared Catchable Rainbow Trout, Convict Creek, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a scientific report, evidently published in a California fisheries journal, on the survival and condition of [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) stocked in [[Convict Creek]], California. Hatchery fish of catchable size were planted in experimental stream sections and tracked from roughly May 1950 to June 1956. The study covers survival and losses by season, growth and condition factor at planting and recovery, and a fin-clipping experiment. [[Wild Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) were recovered alongside the stocked fish and are reported separately. The OCR is heavily degraded, so many exact values could not be verified. The summary below relies only on legible text and table headings.

## Background & Description

The introduction presents survival of hatchery trout after planting as a serious problem for fishery managers, since large numbers of stocked fish are lost soon after release. It frames the work as a test of how well catchable-size [[Hatchery Stocking]] programs sustain trout in streams. The stocked fish are compared with [[Wild Brown Trout]] to assess how hatchery-reared rainbow trout perform in the same habitat.

The study design is organized around:

- **Catchable-size stocking:** hatchery rainbow trout were planted in defined [[Experimental Stream Sections]].
- **Seasonal survival periods:** summer (May to October or November) and winter (November to April or May) intervals were tracked separately.
- **Growth and condition:** total length, weight and [[Condition Factor]] (K) were measured at planting and at recovery.
- **Marking:** a [[Fin Clipping]] experiment compared right ventral, left ventral and marked control groups.
- **Fall-spawning stock:** a later experiment (1954–55) used hatchery-reared, catchable-size, fall-spawning rainbow trout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors recorded environmental variables alongside survival data:

- **Stream discharge:** monthly mean flow in cubic feet per second for Convict Creek, May 1950 to June 1956 ([[Stream Discharge]], Table 1).
- **Air temperature:** monthly, seasonal and annual means in degrees Fahrenheit, May 1950 to June 1956 (Table 2).
- **Water temperature:** monthly, seasonal and annual means in degrees Fahrenheit, same period ([[Water Temperature]], Table 3).

These records were used to interpret seasonal differences in survival, particularly the contrast between high-flow summer months and low-flow winter months. Temperature and flow are central to the report's interpretation of losses, though the degraded scan makes specific associations difficult to confirm.

## Key Findings

- **Study duration:** the investigation ran for six years (May 1950 to June 1956).
- **Summer survival trial (1950):** [[Survival Rate]] and losses were tabulated for experimental stream sections from May 1 to October 31, 1950 (Table 4). Losses were broken into known loss, unaccounted loss and percentage categories.
- **Winter survival trial (1950–51):** a second survival table covers November 1950 to April 30, 1951 (Table 6). A loss percentage of about 13.7% appears in the table headings.
- **Length, weight and condition:** Tables 5, 9, 12, 16, 20 and 22 report total length, mean weight and condition factor for hatchery and wild brown trout at planting and recovery across 1950–51, 1951–52, 1952–53, 1953–54, 1954–55 and 1955–56. Condition-factor values cluster near 0.7 to 1.1 in the legible entries.
- **Fin-clip experiment (1950–51):** Table 7 reports recoveries for right ventral, left ventral and marked control groups, with percentage recovery values in the range of roughly 20% to 60%.
- **Summer survival, 1951:** Table 8 covers May 1 to November 1, 1951, with the same loss categories as the 1950 trial.
- **Winter survival, 1951–52:** Table 10 covers November 1, 1951 to May 1, 1952.
- **Winter survival, 1952–53:** Table 13 covers November 1952 to April 29, 1953. Table 14 tracks a lot of hatchery brown trout originally stocked August 1, 1952, with progressive survival percentages recorded across successive sampling dates.
- **Summer survival, 1953:** Table 15 covers May 1 to November 1, 1953, including hold-over fish.
- **Fall-spawning experiment (1954–55):** Table 19 reports survival and losses for hatchery-reared, catchable-size, fall-spawning rainbow trout. Unaccounted-loss percentages of about 10.8% and 30.8% appear in the table headings.
- **Final season (1955–56):** Table 21 covers survival and loss in the last experimental season, and Table 22 gives length, weight and condition for the recovered fish.

**Overall conclusion:** the report concludes that survival of catchable hatchery trout in Convict Creek was low and variable among seasons and experimental groups. The report attributes poor survival partly to conditions in the stream and partly to the hatchery fish themselves. The degraded text prevents a full verification of the specific conclusions, so readers should consult the original tables before citing exact figures.
