## Executive Summary

This 1853 taxonomic paper by [[Spencer F. Baird]] and [[Charles Girard]] presents original descriptions of numerous new freshwater and brackish-water fish species collected during two U.S. government surveys: the [[U.S.-Mexican Boundary Survey]] (led by Lt. Col. James D. Graham, with specimens gathered by [[John H. Clark]] and [[John L. LeConte]]) and an Arkansas expedition under Captains [[Randolph B. Marcy]] and [[George B. McClellan]]. The descriptions cover taxa from the [[Rio Grande]], [[Rio Gila]], [[Rio Nueces]], [[Rio Salado]], and Arkansas's Otter Creek drainages, as well as brackish coastal waters near Indianola, Texas. Species span the families now recognized as [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows), [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes, then "Pomotis"), [[Fundulidae]] (topminnows), and [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers, then "Heterandria" and "Cyprinodon"). Each description follows a standardized 19th-century format: body shape, fin ray counts (meristics), scale counts, coloration in life and in alcohol, and precise type locality.

## Background & Description

Descriptions rely heavily on classical ichthyological meristic formulae (D = dorsal, A = anal, C = caudal, V = ventral, P = pectoral fin ray counts) and qualitative body-shape terminology. Recurring characteristics documented across taxa include:

- **Body shape**: fusiform, subfusiform, or elliptically elongated; degree of compression and arching of the back used to distinguish sexually dimorphic species (e.g., [[Cyprinodon elegans]], [[Cyprinodon gibbosus]]).
- **Head proportions**: expressed as a fraction of total body length (commonly one-fourth to two-ninths), used as a key diagnostic ratio.
- **Fin structure**: relative height of first vs. second dorsal fin, degree of caudal fin emargination (truncate, slightly emarginate, deeply forked), and spine/soft-ray counts in dorsal, anal, and ventral fins.
- **Scale counts and lateral line**: number of scale rows at the deepest part of the body and on the caudal peduncle; curvature of the lateral line relative to dorsal/ventral body margins (diagnostic for [[Leuciscus lutrensis]] and [[Leuciscus penatincus]]).
- **Coloration**: strong emphasis on sexual dichromatism — males frequently described with bluish-black dorsal coloration, black-margined or spotted fins, and vertical or transverse black barring, contrasted with more uniformly colored females (notable in [[Hydrargyra sella]], [[Cyprinodon elegans]], and [[Cyprinodon gibbosus]]).
- **Opercular features**: presence, size, and shape of the opercular flap used to separate closely related [[Pomotis]] (sunfish) species — e.g., [[Pomotis breviceps]] vs. [[Pomotis longulus]] vs. [[Pomotis agilensis]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The described fishes occupy a range of freshwater and estuarine/brackish habitats across the southern-central United States:

- **Freshwater rivers and springs**: [[Rio Grande]] and its tributaries (Leona and Camanche springs), the [[Rio Gila]] system (including Rio San Pedro and Rio Santa Cruz), the [[Rio Nueces]] basin (Rio Sabinal, Rio Leona, Eli Creek), and the [[Rio Salado]] and [[Rio Medina]] in Texas.
- **Brackish coastal waters**: several species — [[Fundulus gracilis]], [[Hydrargyra sella]], and [[Cyprinodon gibbosus]] — were collected specifically from brackish waters near **Indianola, Texas**, indicating euryhaline tolerance typical of estuarine cyprinodontiforms.
- **Prairie/lowland streams outside Texas**: [[Leuciscus lutrensis]] and [[Leuciscus penatincus]] were collected from **Otter Creek, Arkansas**, alongside two new [[Pomotis]] sunfishes ([[Pomotis breviceps]], [[Pomotis longulus]]), reflecting Marcy and McClellan's Arkansas expedition.
- **Disjunct localities**: [[Fundulus texensis]] was noted from both **Prairie Mer Rouge, Louisiana** and **Russellville, Kentucky**, suggesting a broader Mississippi/Gulf drainage distribution for that species.

These habitat notes collectively depict a survey-driven snapshot of 19th-century Southern Plains and Gulf Coast freshwater ichthyofauna, much of it still undescribed at the time and tied directly to boundary-mapping and exploratory military expeditions.

## Key Findings

- **Pimeloma carbonaria** — Elongated, subfusiform, compressed body; D 15, A 11, C 17, V 5, P 13; reddish-yellow ground color with black transverse bars; black spot at caudal base. Type locality: **Rio Salado, Texas**.
- **Bothriosoma lepida** — Body compressed, thick centrally; small first dorsal/ventrals/anal relative to pectorals/second dorsal; reddish with indistinct blackish bars, black vertical spot beneath eye. Type locality: **upper Rio Nueces, Texas**.
- **Pomotis agilensis** — Resembles [[Pomotis longulus]] but less elongated body, more developed opercular flap, smaller mouth, higher first dorsal; large black basal patches on ventral/anal/dorsal fins. Type locality: **Eagle Pass, Texas**.
- **Catostomus latipinnis** — Subfusiform sucker with small head (1/5.5 of length), small eyes, large fleshy lips, well-developed fins; complex coloration (reddish-brown dorsum, greenish-brown sides, orange belly/fins). Type locality: **Rio San Pedro, Rio Gila**.
- **Gila emoryi** — Elongated, compressed cyprinid; nearly terminal/inferior mouth, deeply forked caudal; collected by [[John L. LeConte]] near the **mouth of the Gila River**.
- **Gila grahami** — Subfusiform, head <1/4 of body length; distinguished from [[Gila emoryi]] by fin/scale structure; reddish-brown dorsum with metallic sheen. Type locality: **Rio San Pedro, Rio Gila**.
- **Fundulus gracilis** — Stout, compressed, 5 inches long; strong sexual dichromatism (bluish-black male vs. unicolor female); collected in **brackish waters near Indianola, Texas**.
- **Fundulus texensis** — Fusiform, black lateral vitta from snout through eye to caudal base; dotted dorsal/anal/caudal fins. Localities: **Prairie Mer Rouge, LA** and **Russellville, KY**.
- **Hydrargyra sella** — Marked sexual dichromatism; males show black-and-yellow spotted dorsal fin margin; truncated caudal. Type locality: **brackish waters, Indianola, Texas**.
- **Cyprinodon elegans**, **C. maculatus**, **C. ovinus**, **C. gibbosus** — Series of four closely related pupfish species distinguished by degree of dorsal arching (sexual dimorphism), coloration patterns, and fin spotting; localities include **Rio Grande del Norte**, **Rio Gila**, **Leona Springs**, and **Indianola, Texas**.
- **Heterandria affinis**, **H. nobilis**, **H. pallensis**, **H. occidentalis** — Four livebearer species differentiated primarily by head-length ratio, body robustness, and caudal fin banding; collected across **Rio Medina, Rio Salado, Leona and Camanche springs, Rio Nueces basin**, and **Rio Santa Cruz (Rio Gila)**.
- **Pomotis breviceps** — Subelliptical, short body; large scales (20 rows at deepest point); prominent whitish-margined opercular flap. Type locality: **Otter Creek, Arkansas**.
- **Pomotis longulus** — More elongated congener of P. breviceps with far posteriorly-set anal fin origin and small, entirely black opercular flap. Type locality: **Otter Creek, Arkansas**.
- **Leuciscus lutrensis** and **Leuciscus penatincus** — Two small cyprinids (~2 inches) from **Otter Creek, Arkansas**, separated by body compression, fin development, and lateral line curvature.
- **Ceratichthys vigilax** — Species recognized from boundary survey collections.
