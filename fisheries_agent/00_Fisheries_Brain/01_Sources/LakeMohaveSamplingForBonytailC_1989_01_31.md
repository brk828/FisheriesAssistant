# 1989 Lake Mohave Sampling for Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 planning memo from Arizona State University researchers W.L. Minckley and Paul C. Marsh, outlining a proposed 6-day intensive fish sampling effort at [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada border) scheduled for 7–12 March 1989. The effort is a continuation of similar work conducted in March 1988, targeting two imperiled Colorado River Basin fish species: the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The memo is logistical/administrative rather than a primary data report, requesting cooperation, manpower, and equipment commitments from partner agencies and researchers involved in the recovery program.

No direct morphological data, measurements, or descriptions of the [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] are provided in this excerpt. ## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir impoundment of the [[Colorado River]] on the Arizona-Nevada border, serving as critical habitat for remnant wild populations of both target species.
- **Bonytail Chub**: Targeted via trammel netting, implying association with deeper, open-water or benthic reservoir habitat where this method is effective.
- **Razorback Sucker**: Targeted via electrofishing, suggesting association with shallower nearshore or littoral zones conducive to electrofishing techniques.
- Both species are native to the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] and represent focal taxa in binational (Arizona-Nevada) endangered species recovery efforts.

## Key Findings
- **Objective 1**: Capture additional adult [[Bonytail Chub]] for translocation to a captive refuge population at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico), indicating this species' critically low wild population status necessitating ex-situ conservation.
- **Objective 2**: Recapture [[Razorback Sucker]] individuals tagged during the prior year's (1988) sampling effort, plus tag additional untagged fish — indicating an ongoing mark-recapture population monitoring program.
- **Methodology**:
  - Trammel netting — primary method for **Bonytail Chub** capture.
  - Electrofishing — primary method for **Razorback Sucker** capture.
- **Operational scale**: Planned deployment of one net boat, one electroshocking boat, an inventory of trammel nets, two personnel crews, and a shared base camp; cooperating agencies requested to contribute comparable manpower/equipment.
- **Timeline**: Sampling window of 7–12 March 1989 (Tuesday–Sunday), with a cooperator response deadline of 10 February 1989.
- **Continuity**: Explicitly framed as a follow-up/continuation of a similar sampling effort conducted in March 1988, suggesting an established annual or recurring monitoring protocol for these species in [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Significance**: This memo reflects early coordinated recovery efforts for two federally significant endangered fish species of the [[Colorado River Basin]], illustrating interagency cooperation models used in Southwestern U.S. native fish conservation during the late 1980s.
