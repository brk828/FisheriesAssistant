# Pilot Channel Draft Environmental Impact Assessment: Salt and Gila Rivers, Maricopa County, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This draft environmental assessment, prepared by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), evaluates a proposed [[Pilot Channel]] (about 36 miles long) inside the existing 1,000-foot cleared corridor of the [[Salt River]] and [[Gila River]], from 91st Avenue to [[Gillespie Dam]]. The [[Flood Control District of Maricopa County]] (FCD) proposes the channel to direct low flows within the cleared area and reduce flood damage to public and private property. The channel would have an average depth of 3 feet and a design capacity of 11,000 cfs. The document compares a No Action alternative with the proposed action and finds that the main ecological cost is the loss of about 41 acres of riparian and wetland habitat, most of it [[Salt Cedar]]. Impacts on fish are expected to be limited to temporary increases in turbidity and suspended solids.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and need.** The 1981 [[Environmental Impact Statement]] covered clearing of phreatophytic vegetation along a 1,000-foot corridor to reduce flooding. That clearing is complete, and the FCD now performs periodic maintenance clearing. The 1981 EIS did not assess the environmental effects of a pilot channel within this corridor. The purpose of the proposal is to facilitate floodwater flow and reduce flood damage to adjacent farmland and property.

**Flood history.** The document lists 15 historical floods on the Salt River, from February 1891 to February 1980. The largest flows were about 300,000 cfs (February 1891), 200,000 cfs (November 1905), and 170,000 cfs (February 16, 1980). The project area has sustained millions of dollars in economic losses, including $7.3 million in 1978.

**Alternatives.**
- *No Action:* The FCD would continue maintenance clearing of the 1,000-foot corridor. Flows would not be contained within the clearing.
- *Proposed Action:* Construction and maintenance of a pilot channel. Most of it requires only a 3-foot cut, with spoil spread across the cleared area (about 4 inches of fill). In a few places, excavation would reach 12 feet.
- *Rejected alternatives:* Channels carrying more than 11,000 cfs were rejected because construction costs exceeded FCD funding.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and water quality.** Normal flows of 300 to 1,000 cfs come from [[Wastewater Effluent]], irrigation tailwaters, pumpage from drainage wells, and groundwater seepage. Effluent is the only steady source between 91st Avenue and 115th Avenue. The 91st Avenue plant discharges about 100,000 acre-feet per year to the Salt River, and this is projected to fall by as much as 75 percent by the year 2000. Downstream of Buckeye Heading, flows depend on irrigation tailwater and floodwater rather than effluent. Groundwater in the shallow alluvial aquifer is considered marginal for domestic use and irrigation because of high salinity, chloride, and hardness.

**Vegetation.** Riparian vegetation is dominated by salt cedar (*Tamarix chinensis*), a phreatophyte native to Eurasia. Other communities include mesquite (*Prosopis juliflora*), saltbush, seepweed, cottonwood (*Populus fremontii*)–willow (*Salix gooddingii*) stands, and cattail marsh. Clearing has already removed about 955 acres of salt cedar, 30 acres of mesquite, and 5 acres of cottonwood-willow.

**Wildlife.** The riparian habitat supports game birds (mourning dove, white-winged dove, Gambel's quail), wading birds, migratory waterfowl, and mammals such as cottontail, raccoon, and coyote. Reptiles and amphibians include bullfrog, leopard frog, and western diamondback rattlesnake.

**Fish.** Little of the original river biota has survived the reduced flows caused by upstream water-supply dams. Current fish include carp, goldfish, tilapia, and mosquitofish. Effluent is high in BOD and nutrients, and irrigation tailwaters are high in dissolved salts, so water quality is degraded. Habitat consists mostly of shallow streams and quiet backwaters over unstable sand-gravel substrate. The area does not support major sport fishing.

**Special status species.** Only two federally listed wildlife species have been recorded: the [[Peregrine Falcon]] (transient records) and the [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (at least three sites in the Gila River within Arlington Valley; the recovery team estimates more than 1,700 breeding birds across its range). No federally listed plants occur in the project area. Arizona state-listed species noted include osprey (*Pandion haliaetus carolinensis*), snowy egret (*Egretta thula brewsteri*), great egret, black-crowned night heron (*Nycticorax nycticorax*), and the introduced [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), which is found in permanent water areas.

**Socioeconomics and land use.** Agriculture and allied industries are the main economic base. Land ownership falls into five categories: private, local, state, federal, and Indian. The Gila River Indian Reservation lies south of the rivers. Recreation includes hunting, fishing, birdwatching, swimming, and off-road vehicle use.

**Transportation and cultural resources.** The main transport mode is private automobile. Road crossings are a concern when floods block access. Many archaeological sites recorded around 1900 have been destroyed by agriculture or floods. The data from previous surveys are not precise enough to place the proposed channel accurately relative to any sites.

## Key Findings

- **Habitat loss:** The proposed channel would remove about 41 acres of habitat in meanders that are filled or cut off from river flow. This includes 35 acres of salt cedar, 5 acres of cottonwood-willow, and 1 acre of wetland. The document states that about 87 percent of the lost habitat is salt cedar, which has relatively low wildlife value.
- **Mitigation:** At least 375 cottonwood and willow trees would be planted, providing about 5 acres of habitat. At least 80 percent survival for two years is required.
- **Fish impacts:** Effects are expected to be limited to increased turbidity and suspended solids during construction and post-construction floods. The document states these should not severely affect fish in the project area or downstream. Stroud and Clepper (1975) found that largemouth bass fingerlings died at an average suspended solids level of about 10,000 ppm, far above expected levels in the rivers. Most fish habitat, in backwaters and permanent water areas, would not be affected.
- **Water quality:** Turbidity and suspended solids would increase, with slight degradation from channel construction. Groundwater quality would not be affected. Channel depth of 3 feet is designed to limit groundwater drawdown and drainage of adjacent backwaters.
- **Maintenance:** Scouring at high-velocity locations may lead to deposition in low-velocity areas, so a maintenance program would be needed.
- **Protected species:** No effect is expected on federally listed species. Yuma clapper rails found during the 1986 survey would be avoided, and state-listed species would not be significantly affected.
- **Flood protection and economics:** The proposal would provide economic benefits by preventing flood damage and confining flows, which would stop land loss from erosion and bank cutting.
- **Aesthetics:** Impacts would be adverse. For about 32 miles, the channel would be a shallow depression within the clearing. For the remaining 4 miles, it would be deepened to 12 feet, a significant visual impact.
- **Cultural resources:** Undocumented archaeological sites may exist and could be destroyed during construction and maintenance, though past floods may already have destroyed them.
- **Consultation:** A May 9, 1985 coordination meeting was held at the FCD office, followed by a field meeting on May 30, 1985 with the FCD, Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), FWS, and Bureau of Land Management (BLM). A [[Section 404 Permit]] was applied for and issued. The FWS completed an intra-service Section 7 consultation and determined there would be no effect on the Yuma clapper rail. The document is a draft for intragovernmental review under Executive Order 12372.
