# Gila Taxonomy Field Sheets (1987 Specimen Records)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

The document is a scanned set of roughly one hundred [[Gila Taxonomy Sheet]] forms, plus one unrelated FORTRAN coding form at the end. Each sheet records one fish specimen from the genus *Gila*. The species is not named. The sheets combine collection metadata (tag number, date, river, [[River Mile Index]]), linear measurements, fin-ray counts, sex and ripeness, a disposition code, and a set of ranked morphological characters scored on a 1–5 scale. The forms appear designed to support taxonomic discrimination within the genus. The document contains no narrative analysis, conclusions, or summary statistics.

## Background & Description

Each record follows the same template:

- **Identification:** tag number, collection date (written in 1987 format), river, and river-mile index (RMI).
- **Measurements:** [[Total Length]], [[Standard Length]], [[Head Length]], [[Snout Length]], [[Body Depth]], [[Caudal Peduncle]] length and depth, and maximum and minimum depth. Weight in grams and a picture flag (Y/N) are also recorded. Measurements were taken with a ruler or [[Calipers]].
- **Meristics:** dorsal and anal [[Fin Ray Count]]s, each scored across 7–11 rays.
- **Sex and maturity:** male/female, ripeness (Y/N/T), and a disposition code (RA, DP, DF). The codes are not defined in the document.
- **Ranked characters (1–5 scale):** [[Mouth Position]] relative to snout, [[Rictus Position]] relative to eye, [[Skull Depression]], [[Nuchal Hump]], [[Caudal Peduncle Shape]], and [[Scale Pattern]] on the nuchal region and breast.

Collection gear is usually marked as electrofishing (EL). Other gear codes appear occasionally. Most records are for individuals in the range of several hundred millimetres in total length. Weights are commonly in the hundreds of grams, judging by the legible values.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sheets describe no habitat, water chemistry, community, or ecological variables. The only environmental cues are the river identifier and river-mile index, which point to sampling along a river reach. The river name is illegible in the scans. Collection sites are not described beyond these codes.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** The sheets cover about one hundred *Gila* specimens sampled in 1987 and scanned as pages 1–102. The exact count is hard to confirm because of OCR and handwriting.
- **Standardized morphology:** Every specimen is scored on the same ranked characters. This points to a comparative taxonomic study, probably of morphological variation within *Gila*. The document does not state the study's aims.
- **Character set:** Mouth and snout position, rictus position, skull depression, nuchal hump, peduncle shape, and scalation form the core diagnostic scores. Fin-ray counts are recorded on both dorsal and anal fins.
- **Sex and maturity:** Sex and ripeness are recorded for each specimen. Maturity status may be used to separate life stages within the samples.
- **Data gaps:** Several sheets are incomplete, with blank fields, partly legible values, or multiple checkmarks. Disposition codes are undefined. Species identity, river names, and dates are not reliably legible in the scans.
- **Non-research content:** The final page is a FORTRAN coding form with no biological content.
