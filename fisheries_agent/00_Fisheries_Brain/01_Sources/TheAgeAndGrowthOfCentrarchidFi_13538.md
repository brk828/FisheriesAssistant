# The Age and Growth of Centrarchid Fishes in Folsom Lake, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This 1966 study (Dingell-Johnson Project F-18-R) uses scales to determine age and growth of [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]] and [[Red-ear Sunfish]] in [[Folsom Lake]] during the first 7 years after impoundment (1955-1961). Largemouth bass grew slightly faster than smallmouth bass in year one, but smallmouth bass grew faster thereafter. Red-ear sunfish outgrew bluegill throughout life. Bluegill growth declined markedly over the 7 years, while red-ear growth was stable and largemouth bass growth rose for about 2 years and then declined.

## Background & Description
- **Taxa:** largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*), smallmouth bass (*M. dolomieui*), bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*), red-ear sunfish (*L. microlophus*).
- This is stated to be the first published report of smallmouth bass and red-ear sunfish growth in California. Earlier California work on largemouth bass covered [[Clear Lake]], [[Sutherland Reservoir]], [[Lake Havasu]], the [[Colorado River]] and [[Big Sage Reservoir]]; bluegill had been studied at Clear Lake and Sutherland Reservoir.
- **Methods:** scales were taken from the left side behind the pectoral fin tip during creel censuses and sampling programs in 1957 and 1960-62. They were impressed on cellulose acetate slides and read with an Eberbach projector at 42X. Annuli were counted and lengths were back-calculated with a direct-proportion nomograph corrected for the intercept. Length-weight data were fitted with W = CL^n. Samples were not stratified by sex.
- **Sample totals (scales):** largemouth bass 523, smallmouth bass 462, bluegill 389, red-ear sunfish 187 (as given in Table 1).
- **Validation:** good age-size correlation, similar growth histories across year-classes and collection years, and agreement between measured and calculated lengths supported the annuli. Otoliths (sagittae) and scales from 18 bluegills (1.2-6.8 in FL) agreed in all cases. The first bluegill annulus was often indistinct because of the extended spawning season.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- [[Folsom Lake]] lies on the [[American River]], about 20 miles northeast of [[Sacramento]]. It was completed in 1955 and operated by the [[United States Bureau of Reclamation]] for irrigation, power and flood protection.
- It is a U-shaped reservoir with gross pool capacity of 1,010,300 acre-feet, maximum depth of 267 ft, and 11,450 acres and 75 miles of shoreline at full pool.
- Storage peaks in June, then drawdown lowers the level about 70 ft, to about 400,000 acre-feet by December.
- Surface temperatures are in the mid-40s °F in winter and 75-80°F in summer. Summer thermal stratification occurs, but oxygen is plentiful in most of the hypolimnion.
- The lake is [[Oligotrophic Lakes]] with low phosphorus and carbonate. Both warmwater and salmonid fishes occur; smallmouth bass, largemouth bass, bluegill, green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*) and white catfish (*Ictalurus catus*) dominate the catch.
- The bluegill decline parallels early-year trends at [[Millerton Lake]], another large fluctuating California reservoir.

## Key Findings
**Body-scale relationships (linear):**
- Largemouth bass (n=585): Y = 0.89 + 0.0599X
- Smallmouth bass (n=256): Y = 1.54 + 0.0646X
- Bluegill (n=1,288, fish >1.5 in): Y = 0.94 + 0.0358X, with an eye-fitted correction for smaller fish
- Red-ear sunfish (n=187): Y = 0.64 + 0.0480X

**Annulus timing (largemouth bass, 1961, n=83):** a new annulus appeared on fewer than one-third of fish on April 27, on 60% by the end of May, and on all fish by June 14, as surface temperature rose from 61 to 72°F. For smallmouth bass, 19 of 25 fish collected in May 1961 had a new annulus.

**Mean calculated fork lengths (inches) at end of year of life:**

| Species | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Largemouth bass | 5.6 | 10.4 | 12.8 | 14.5 | 15.8 | 17.0 |
| Smallmouth bass | 5.2 | 10.3 | 13.6 | 15.8 | – | – |
| Bluegill | 1.4 | 3.1 | 4.8 | 7.0 | 7.9 | 8.7 |
| Red-ear sunfish | 1.9 | 4.9 | 7.5 | 8.8 | – | – |

- Smallmouth bass are nearly 1 in longer than largemouth at age III, an advantage that continues through year 4. The greatest annual increment occurs in year one for both species. The largest drop in increment comes in year 3 for largemouth bass and year 4 for smallmouth bass.
- Red-ear sunfish exceed 7 in at age III, whereas bluegill reach only about 5 in. Red-ears exceed 8 in after four seasons, whereas bluegill reach a comparable size only in their sixth summer.

**Length-weight relationships:**
- Largemouth bass: W = −3.48716 + 3.163 log L
- Smallmouth bass: W = −3.38873 + 3.125 log L
- Bluegill: Log W = −3.16513 + 3.129 Log L
- Red-ear sunfish: Log W = −2.87068 + 2.712 log L

Smallmouth bass are slightly heavier at 9.5-13.0 in, possibly because they mature earlier; largemouth bass are heavier above 13 in. Bluegill are relatively heavier for their length than red-ear sunfish throughout life.

**Regional comparisons:**
- Folsom largemouth growth is intermediate among U.S. waters; for example, 5.6 in at age I versus 6.9 in at Norris Reservoir (Tennessee) and 2.3 in at Big Sage Reservoir.
- Smallmouth growth is comparable to southeastern waters ([[Norris Reservoir]], Hiwassee Reservoir) and superior to northern waters such as [[Oneida Lake]], [[Lake Michigan]] and Wisconsin waters.
- Red-ear growth is very good compared with other areas. Early bluegill growth is very slow, comparable to the northern part of its range, but growth beyond age III is well above average and similar to Oklahoma reservoirs and one Missouri reservoir.

**Seven-year growth trends:**
- Largemouth bass growth rose slightly between 1955 and 1957 and declined slightly afterward.
- Smallmouth bass year-classes varied little after 1957.
- Bluegill growth fell precipitously from 1955 to 1957 and then declined gradually. The 1956 yearling increment was less than half that of 1955 and accounted for most of the decline.
- At Millerton Lake, bluegills averaging 6 in were common in the first 2 years after impoundment; by 1951 the average was about 5.5 in. In 1962, only 2 of nearly 2,000 bluegills sampled exceeded 6 in, and fish reached 6 in only in their 5th summer.
- Red-ear sunfish were planted only in 1957, so data cover 1958-1961 and show no significant growth change.
