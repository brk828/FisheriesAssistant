# The Reef Ichthyofauna of Bahrain (Arabian Gulf) with Comments on its Zoogeographic Affinities

#ichthyology #marine-biology #reef-fish #zoogeography

## Executive Summary

This 1987 study by [[Gregory Smith]], [[Mostafa Saleh]] and [[Khatoon Sangoor]] (University College of Bahrain) presents a checklist of 71 fish species from 25 families recorded on shallow reefs (4-15 m) northeast of Bahrain in the [[Arabian Gulf]]. Based on SCUBA observations and collections made April-June 1985 at three stations, the authors update earlier regional lists using recent systematic revisions, largely [[Fischer and Bianchi 1984]]. Most species (39) are widespread across the [[Indo-West Pacific Region]], while the remainder help resolve the zoogeographic position of the Gulf. The authors find that the Gulf reef fauna is transitional between the [[Western Indian Ocean Province]] and the [[Indo-Polynesian Province]], with endemism higher than previously recognised and possibly approaching that of the [[Red Sea]].

## Background & Description

- **Problem:** The Arabian Gulf ichthyofauna was poorly known, and earlier regional literature contained many misidentifications and synonymised names, so local diversity was overstated.
- **Prior sources:** Trawl surveys (e.g., [[Blegvad 1944]], [[Kuronuma and Abe 1972]], [[Al-Baharna 1986]]), fish market surveys ([[Randall et al. 1978]], [[Relyea 1981]]) and the [[ARAMCO Biotope Survey]] (Basson et al. 1977) recorded reef fishes only incidentally or superficially.
- **Zoogeographic framework:** [[Briggs 1974]] placed the Gulf in the Western Indian Ocean Province tentatively, pending local distribution data.
- **Scope:** Only Bahrain reef collections were used for the zoogeographic analysis, which the authors consider to include the bulk of the shallow reef fauna reported for the Gulf.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study stations** (see Table 1 in source):

| Station | Depth | Habitat description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4-7 m | Large *Porites* buttresses 2-3 m high with dense [[Acropora]] thickets covering 70-80% of area |
| 2 | 7-10 m | Dominant *Porites* and *Acropora*; largest heads rising 2-3 m |
| 3 | 13-15 m | Rocky ledges and terraces, sparse stony corals, likely relict Pleistocene shorelines |

- **Sampling design:** Structurally heterogeneous reefs were chosen, following [[Risk 1972]], who found fish species richness correlated with reef structural complexity in the Virgin Islands.
- **Collection methods:** Hook-and-line, spearguns and poisons (quinaldine or 5% sodium hypochlorite). Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin, transferred to 40% isopropanol and accessioned into the UCB fish collection; lengths were recorded as standard length (SL) to the nearest mm.
- **Environmental interpretation:** The authors attribute the depauperate Bahrain fauna to the "stressful physicochemical nature and geological recency" of the Gulf.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 71 species in 25 families were recorded. The family sequence follows [[Nelson 1976]].
- **Widespread fauna:** 39 species range across the Indo-West Pacific.
- **Endemism:** Six species (about 9% of the 71) are Arabian Gulf endemics. Adding five species restricted to the Gulf, Gulf of Oman and northwestern Arabian Sea raises combined endemism to about 15%. This complements [[Price 1982]], who reported 12% endemism for Gulf echinoderms.
- **Arabian endemics:** Seven species are restricted to the Red Sea, Arabian Peninsula and Arabian Gulf, indicating a strong Red Sea-Gulf faunal link.
- **New records:** *Ptereleotris* sp. is a new addition to the Gulf fauna (possibly undescribed). Several species are first Gulf records, including *Apogon cyanosoma*, *Amblygobius albimaculatus* and *Cryptocentrus lutheri* (the latter two also first records per the authors' comments on Al-Baharna 1986).
- **Taxonomic corrections:** The authors synonymise or reassign many names, including:
  - *Chiloscyllium griseum* records refer to *C. arabicum*.
  - *Epinephelus tauvina* records are *E. malabaricus*; *E. tauvina* does not occur in the Gulf.
  - *Lutjanus johni* reports are misidentifications of *L. ehrenbergi*.
  - *Plectorhinchus sordidus* is probably common but overlooked due to similarity with *P. schotaf*.
- **Zoogeographic distribution** (Table 3):
  - 12 species range from the Gulf southwards and 11 range eastwards.
  - Groups include Arabian endemics, East Africa to Gulf, Gulf to northwestern India, and Arabian Gulf endemics (e.g., *Chiloscyllium arabicum*, *Pseudochromis persicus*, *Scarus persicus*).
- **Conclusion:** The Gulf reef fauna is transitional and cannot be assigned strictly to either province. Endemism may approach that of the Red Sea (which [[Briggs 1974]] regarded as 10-15% for fishes), suggesting the Gulf may merit special zoogeographic status. The authors judge this premature until other Gulf biotas are analysed.
- **Diversity comparison** (Table 4): Bahrain (71 species) is low relative to the Red Sea (>325) and Seychelles (880), and similar to [[Basson et al. 1977]] (70 species for the Gulf).
