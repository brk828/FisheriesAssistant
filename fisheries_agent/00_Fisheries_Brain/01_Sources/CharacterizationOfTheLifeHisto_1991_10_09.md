## Executive Summary

This project summary details a 4-year investigation (1990–1994) by BIO/WEST, Inc. into the life history and ecology of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the Colorado River within [[Grand Canyon]], commissioned to assess the impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on the species. The study employs a hypothesis-testing framework across two major objectives: (1) characterizing ecological and limiting factors across all life stages (habitat, water quality, food, reproduction, survivorship, distribution, movement, and biotic interactions), and (2) developing a comprehensive life history schedule and population model. Field methods include radiotelemetry, PIT tagging, multi-gear fish sampling, and water quality monitoring across three defined study reaches. Preliminary findings (Oct 1990–Aug 1991) indicate strong spatial concentration of the population near the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) confluence, evidence of an expanded spawning period (March–July) based on tubercle presence, general site-fidelity with some long-distance movement, and species dominance shifts among reaches driven by presence of non-native competitors/predators like [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Common Carp]].

## Background & Description

- **Species**: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), family [[Cyprinidae]], federally listed as endangered.
- **Study Rationale**: Conducted under Conservation Measures arising from a 1978 biological opinion on Glen Canyon Dam — specifically Conservation Measure 5 (identify dam operation impacts) and Conservation Measure 7 (establish a second spawning population in Grand Canyon).
- **Institutional Collaboration**: BIO/WEST, Inc.; U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS); Arizona Department of Game and Fish (AGF); Arizona State University (ASU); National Park Service; Navajo Nation; Hopi Tribe.
- **Study Area** (Figure 2), divided into three reaches:
  1. **LCR Reach**: Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56) to Red Canyon (RM 76.5) — focus on life history and habitat use.
  2. **Granite Gorge Reach**: Red Canyon to Havasu Creek (RM 156) — focus on distribution/habitat use.
  3. **Havasu Creek Reach**: Havasu Creek to Diamond Creek (RM 226) — focus on distribution/habitat use, including tributary inflows (Bright Angel, Shinumo, Kanab, Tapeats, Havasu creeks).

### Field Methods
- Monthly trips alternating 12-day and 20-day durations, using 37-ft S-rig and 23-ft Snout research boats.
- **Radiotelemetry**: 11-gm radiotransmitters (battery life ~120 days), implanted only in fish >550 gm (transmitter ≤2% body weight). 48 adults tagged Oct 1990–Sep 1991. Two remote telemetry stations established at LCR mouth (Feb 1991); nondirectional station added Aug 1991 (RM 60–61.2) to monitor vertical movement.
- **PIT tagging**: Applied to all native fish >175 mm TL; 468 of 569 chub captured were PIT tagged (Oct 1990–Aug 1991); 38 recaptured (8.1%).
- **Capture gears**: Electrofishing (Coffelt Complex Pulse System Mark XX), experimental gill nets (100'×6'×1.5–2.0"), trammel nets (75'×6'×1.0–1.5"), hoop nets (2, 3, 4-ft), minnow traps, seines.
- **Water Chemistry**: Continuous Datasonde recordings + daily Hydrolab readings (temperature, pH, conductance, TDS, dissolved oxygen); river stage recorded ~every 2 hours.

### Distribution & Abundance
- 96% (544/569) of chub captured within a 9-mile reach (RM 57.0 Malagosa Crest to RM 65.9 below Chuar Rapid), centered on the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence (RM 61.5).
- ~45% of fish captured upstream of LCR; ~55% downstream.
- Only 25 chub (+5 recaptures) captured in Reaches 2 and 3 combined, at: Bright Angel (RM 87.5, n=1); Shinumo Creek (RM 108.1–108.6, n=12); 119 Mile Creek (RM 118.6, n=1); 127 Mile Creek (RM 127.0, n=3); Havasu Creek (RM 155.8–156.2, n=4); Pumpkin Spring–Granite Spring (RM 212.5–221.8, n=4).
- Range of capture: RM 57.0 (upstream-most) to RM 219.7 (downstream-most).

This distribution illustrates a strong ecological gradient: humpback chub dominance is highest near the LCR (thermal refuge/spawning tributary), while non-native species (carp, catfish, trout) dominate downstream reaches — supporting hypotheses regarding non-native [[Biotic Interactions|biotic interaction]] pressure and cold mainstem water limiting chub distribution.

### Water Temperature & Dam Operations
- Cold-water releases from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] (8–12°C) are hypothesized to limit distribution/abundance independent of flow fluctuation effects, via unsuitable spawning/growth temperatures — balanced against potential benefit of excluding predators/competitors adapted to warmer water.
- Mainstem spawning considered "extremely limited, or more likely nonexistent" due to cold temperatures (citing Maddux et al. 1987).

### Turbidity
- Chub evolved in a highly turbid system and are negatively phototrophic (Bulkley et al. 1982); reduced turbidity (due to sediment trapping in Lake Powell) may alter behavior/distribution.
- Near-surface habitat use tracked via radiosignal extinction depth (~4 m) as a proxy for light-related behavior.
- 215 surveillance runs conducted (Oct 1990–Aug 1991) across day, night, and crepuscular periods.

## Key Findings

### Movement & Behavior (Radiotelemetry, Oct 1990–Aug 1991)
- 62 activity maps generated for radiotagged adults.
- Most fish remained within 0.5 miles of capture/release point; maximum recorded downstream movement of 3.0 miles typical.
- One notable long-distance movement: fish tagged Oct 1990 at RM 65.0, recaptured healthy at RM 127.0 — a 62.0-mile downstream displacement.
- Diel activity pattern: greatest movement during crepuscular hours (~5–10 pm).
- Near-surface activity (within radiosignal extinction depth of ~4.5 m at 50 m horizontal distance) occurred almost exclusively within 50 m of riverbank; cross-channel movement appears to occur along the river bottom.
- Distinct upstream immigration to LCR inflow beginning early March, continuing through July; tubercled (breeding-condition) fish observed March–July, suggesting an **expanded spawning period** relative to prior single-tributary studies.

### Reproduction
- Mainstem spawning not confirmed; hypothesis-testing ongoing via nuptial condition assessments and radiotagged fish movement tracking.
- Tributary spawning migration suspected in LCR, Shinumo, Havasu, Kanab, Bright Angel, and Tapeats creeks.

### Early Life Stage Survivorship
- Age-0/juvenile chub captures below LCR (1990–1991): Oct (1), Nov (2), Jan (2), Mar (3), Apr (0), May (34), Jun (16), Jul (52), Sep (176) — showing strong seasonal recruitment pulse in late summer.
- September dispersal snapshot: 3 young chub above LCR; 157 within 5 miles downstream; 7 at 15 miles downstream (Papago); 9 at 47 miles downstream (Shinumo) — indicating downstream dispersal of young-of-year from the LCR nursery area.
- Permanent marking method for age-0/age-1/age-2 fish identified as a critical research gap (fin clips are short-lived; fish too small for PIT tags until reaching >175 mm TL, roughly age-3+).

### Marking & Recapture Summary (Oct 1990–Aug 1991)
- 569 humpback chub captured total.
- 468 PIT tagged; 38 recaptured (8.1%).
- 125 total marked-fish recaptures (21.9%) across all marking programs (BIO/WEST, AGF, ASU, etc.).

### Appendix A: Compiled Life History/Ecological Parameters by Life Stage
*(Synthesized from multiple literature sources: Kaeding & Zimmerman 1983; Valdez & Clemmer 1982; Kaeding et al. 1990; Karp & Tyus 1990; Hamman 1982; Bulkley et al. 1982; Holden 1978; Maddux et al. 1986; Valdez 1990; Valdez et al. 1990)*

**I. Spawning/Egg Incubation**
- Spawning timing varies by location: April–May (LCR); June–July (Black Rocks, Yampa Canyon).
- Spawning flows range widely (e.g., 21,500–26,000 cfs down to 3,000–5,000 cfs at Black Rocks).
- Spawning water temperatures generally 11.5–24°C across studies; optimal egg incubation at 19–20°C (84% success, Hamman 1982); eggs failed to develop at 12–13°C.
- Incubation time: 115–160 hours at 19–20°C.
- Egg diameter: 2.2–3.0 mm; fecundity estimates range from ~100 to 10,000 eggs/fish depending on source.

**II. Larvae/Age-0**
- Hatching size: 6.0–7.5 mm.
- Mortality observed with abrupt temperature drops of 6°C (from 10°C to 4°C).
- Habitat: quiet pockets along steep rock walls; backwaters/runs with firm silt; shallow depths (~1.4–2.1 ft) and very low velocities (0.1–0.2 fps).

**III. Juvenile (1 year to maturity)**
- Final temperature preferendum: 24°C (Bulkley et al. 1982).
- TDS preference: 1.3–3.0 mmhos; avoidance above 8.5 mmhos.
- Habitat: backwaters/runs with firm silt, shallow depth (~0.6 m), low velocity (0–0.15 mps); also small eddies and pools.
- Growth: reach 250–300 mm TL by age 3 (LCR).
- Swimming ability: sustained speed 0.66 mps (2.2 fps); can hold 2 hrs at 0.32 mps, only minutes at 0.78 mps.
- Parasitism: *Lernaea cyprinacea* found in 17% of sampled juveniles.
- Associated species included [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Speckled Dace]].

**IV. Adult**
- Size at maturity: 250–300 mm TL typical; smallest mature males recorded from 180–232 mm TL depending on population (Cataract Canyon vs. Yampa Canyon vs. LCR).
- Habitat: eddies and deep runs, depths 0.7–12.2 m (mean ~4.3 m), velocities 0.03–1.16 mps (mean ~0.18 mps); large shoreline eddies favored.
- Diet: dominated by immature chironomidae and simuliidae (aquatic insect larvae).
- Parasitism: systemic *Aeromonas hydrophila* infection and *Lernaea cyprinacea* infestation documented (up to 31% prevalence at Black Rocks/Westwater).
- Associated species across systems consistently include non-native [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], plus native [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]].

### Research Gaps Identified
- Lack of permanent, long-duration marking method for age-0 to age-2 fish limits cohort survivorship tracking.
- Pre-dam baseline distribution/abundance data for the mainstem are nearly nonexistent, complicating before/after dam-impact assessments.
- Age determination becomes unreliable beyond age-4 due to variable growth influenced by maturation and spawning stress.
