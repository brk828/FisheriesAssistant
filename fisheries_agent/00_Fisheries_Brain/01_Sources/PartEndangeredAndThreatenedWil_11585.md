# Title 50 CFR Part 17 - Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#endangered-species #threatened-species #federal-regulation #wildlife-protection #conservation-law

## Executive Summary

This document is the official U.S. Code of Federal Regulations (50 CFR Part 17) listing all species of wildlife and plants determined by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to be Endangered or Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It provides comprehensive regulatory lists (§17.11 for wildlife, §17.12 for plants) with standardized nomenclature, status designations, historic ranges, and cross-references to special rules and critical habitat designations. The compilation is current as of the date shown on the cover and includes all published final rules through that date. The document also includes a list of species removed from protection due to recovery, extinction, or original data errors.

## Background & Description

The regulatory framework establishes standardized listing procedures for species protection. Status symbols include "E" for Endangered, "T" for Threatened, "E (S/A)" or "T (S/A)" for similarity of appearance species, "XE" for essential experimental populations, and "XN" for nonessential experimental populations. Scientific names follow the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] for animals and the [[International Code of Botanical Nomenclature]] for plants. Common names are provided for reference but cannot be relied upon for specimen identification due to local usage variations.

The lists define species through three columns: Common Name, Scientific Name, and (for vertebrates) Vertebrate Population Where Endangered or Threatened. Geographic populations of the same subspecies may be classified differently based on geographic boundaries. The term "Entire" indicates all populations throughout the present range are listed. Historic range indicates known general distribution as reported in current scientific literature, though present distribution may be greatly reduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document covers diverse taxonomic groups across multiple ecosystems. Wildlife listings include mammals (e.g., [[Bald Eagle]] *Haliaeetus leucocephalus*, [[Gray Wolf]] *Canis lupus*, [[Florida Panther]] *Felis concolor coryi*), birds (e.g., [[California Condor]] *Gymnogyps californianus*, [[Whooping Crane]] *Grus americana*), reptiles (e.g., [[American Alligator]] *Alligator mississippiensis*, [[Green Sea Turtle]] *Chelonia mydas*), amphibians, and fish (e.g., [[Chinook Salmon]] *Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*, [[Pallid Sturgeon]] *Scaphirhynchus albus*). Invertebrate listings include freshwater mussels, snails, crustaceans, insects, and arachnids.

Plant listings span numerous families including [[Cactaceae]] (cacti), [[Orchidaceae]] (orchids), [[Asteraceae]] (aster family), and many others. Geographic distribution ranges from localized endemics (e.g., species restricted to single Hawaiian islands or specific cave systems) to wide-ranging species with specific threatened populations. Aquatic species include both marine (sea turtles, marine mammals) and freshwater taxa (fish, mussels, aquatic plants).

## Key Findings

The wildlife list (§17.11) contains hundreds of species across all vertebrate classes and numerous invertebrate groups. Notable aquatic species include multiple [[Endangered]] fish such as [[Apache Trout]] *Oncorhynchus apache*, [[Razorback Sucker]] *Xyrauchen texanus*, and [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] *Acipenser brevirostrum*. Marine species include all sea turtle species, multiple whale species, and the [[Hawaiian Monk Seal]] *Monachus schauinslandi*. Freshwater mussels represent a particularly imperiled group with dozens of listed species.

The plant list (§17.12) documents hundreds of endangered and threatened plant species, with particularly high representation from [[Hawaii]], [[California]], [[Florida]], and [[Puerto Rico]]. Many listings reflect habitat destruction, restricted ranges, and specialized ecological requirements.

The document includes provisions for experimental populations, allowing reintroduction efforts with modified regulatory protections. Critical habitat designations are cross-referenced where applicable, though many species lack designated critical habitat (indicated by "NA"). Special rules provide species-specific management provisions.

Species removed from the lists include cases of recovery (e.g., [[Rydberg Milk-vetch]] *Astragalus perianus*), extinction (e.g., [[Tecopa Pupfish]] *Cyprinodon nevadensis calidae*, [[Amistad Gambusia]] *Gambusia amistadensis*), and original data errors. The delisting criteria specify that recovery-based removals require best available scientific data demonstrating the species is no longer endangered or threatened.

The regulatory framework emphasizes that listing applies to all individuals of a species wherever found, regardless of historic range limitations. Prohibitions under the Act apply comprehensively, with special rules and experimental population designations providing management flexibility where appropriate. The document serves as the authoritative reference for federal species protection under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
