## Executive Summary

This 1942 study by Brown and Ball reports a complete fish population census of **[[Third Sister Lake]]**, a 10-acre, 55-foot-deep lake in Michigan, achieved through a combination of angling, netting, and rotenone poisoning conducted in 1941 as part of a broader fish-food organism investigation. A total of **15,454 fish weighing 866.6 pounds (86.7 lbs/acre)** were recovered, though the authors caution that small fish and non-floating carcasses (e.g., darters, minnows) were systematically undersampled. **[[Bluegill Sunfish]]** dominated the population numerically (26.25%) and by weight (61.99%), with legal-sized bluegills alone contributing ~50 lbs/acre. Game fish (bass, bluegill, pumpkinseed, hybrids) comprised 33.3% of numbers but 80.3% of total weight, while forage fishes (shiners, darters, mud minnow) made up over half of all individuals but only 3.4% of biomass. The lake's productivity (86.7 lbs/acre) was intermediate between northern natural lakes and highly productive southern artificial ponds, aligning most closely with other Michigan lake studies (Eschmeyer 1938).

## Background & Description

Size and age data were derived from scale annuli analysis (for bass, bluegill, pumpkinseed) and length-frequency separation (for other species), collected at time of recovery during poisoning operations.

- **[[Largemouth Black Bass]]**: 470 specimens, 126.9 lbs (14.64% of total weight). Reached legal length (10 in) late in year 3 or early year 4. Age-group I dominant (49.1%); age-group II underrepresented (4.3%); age-groups III–VI made up 88.6% of total bass weight.
- **[[Bluegill Sunfish]]**: 4,057 specimens, 537.3 lbs (61.99% of total weight) — most abundant game fish. Age-group VI contributed 22.5% of population weight. 1,560 legal bluegills (38% of all bluegills) weighed 499.76 lbs (93.01% of bluegill weight). Reached legal length late year 3/early year 4.
- **[[Pumpkinseed Sunfish]]**: 610 specimens, 29.18 lbs (3.37%). 32 legal-length fish (6 in) = 40.03% of pumpkinseed weight. Legal length reached in year 3–4.
- **[[Green Sunfish]]**: 96 specimens, 5.4 lbs (0.62%); only 5 individuals ≥6 inches.
- **Hybrid sunfish**: 13 specimens (5 pumpkinseed×bluegill; 8 pumpkinseed×green sunfish), 2.2 lbs (0.25%).
- **[[Yellow Bullhead]]**: 670 specimens, 88.1 lbs (10.16%). Three size/age groups identified: Group I (avg 2.1 in, 61.3% of number), Group II (avg 4.9 in, 8.96%), Group III (≥6 in, 29.7% of number but 91.4% of weight).
- **Common Sucker**: Only 10 specimens, 22.8 lbs; all aged 3–4 years — evidence suggests non-reproducing, likely migrants via intermittent outlet.
- **Chub-sucker**: 947 specimens, 16.6 lbs (1.92%). Yearlings (1.6–3.1 in, avg 2.3 in, n=835) separable from adults (3.4–7.9 in, avg 5.1 in, n=112).
- **Mud Pickerel**: 138 specimens, 8.7 lbs (1.0%); size range 4.6–11.0 in (avg 6.1 in); ages not determinable.
- **Black-chinned Shiner**: 3,215 specimens, 6.6 lbs (0.76%); size 1.1–2.4 in (avg 1.9 in).
- **Black-nosed Shiner**: 4,530 specimens (most numerous fish in lake, 29.31%), 8.3 lbs (0.96%); size 1.5–2.5 in (avg 1.7 in).
- **Common Shiner**: 30 adult specimens, 6.6 lbs; size 5.3–8.4 in (avg 7.6 in) — likely non-reproducing resident.
- **Golden Shiner**: 174 specimens, 5.9 lbs (0.68%); size 2.1–6.9 in (avg 5.1 in).
- **Mud Minnow**: 230 specimens, 1.8 lbs (0.2%); size 1.6–4.5 in (avg 2.8 in).
- **Iowa Darter**: 264 specimens, 0.3 lbs (0.04%); size 1.0–1.8 in (avg 1.6 in) — most inconspicuous/least likely to float, likely undercounted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Third Sister Lake]]** is a small (10-acre), deep (max 55 ft) glacial lake with ~35% of bottom shallow enough to support littoral macrophytes (yellow water lilies, pondweeds), providing critical shelter and forage habitat for fish.
- The lake had been closed to fishing for 9 years prior to the study, allowing a population near presumed **maximum carrying capacity**.
- Species were classified per Michigan legal categories into **game fish** (bass, bluegill, pumpkinseed, hybrids), **coarse fish** (green sunfish, yellow bullhead, common sucker, chub-sucker, mud pickerel), and **forage fish** (shiners, mud minnow, Iowa darter).
- Numerical ratio of game:coarse:forage fish ≈ **3:1:5**; weight ratio ≈ **27:5:1**, reflecting an inverted pyramid where small forage fish vastly outnumber but contribute minimally to biomass.
- **[[Common Sucker]]** and **[[Common Shiner]]** appear unable to reproduce successfully within the lake; their presence attributed to migration through an intermittent outlet during high water, not in-lake recruitment.
- Poisoning (rotenone, Derris root) was applied twice (May 6 and August 18–19, 1941) — the first pre-spawning treatment failed to eliminate the population, prompting a second application after observing extensive bluegill/pumpkinseed fry recruitment by mid-July, illustrating rapid reproductive rebound in centrarchids.
- Comparative productivity context: Third Sister Lake (86.7 lbs/acre) falls between Nova Scotian natural lakes (24.3 lbs/acre, Smith 1938) and Illinois artificial lakes (439 lbs/acre average, Thompson & Bennett 1939), more closely resembling other Michigan natural lakes (58.3 lbs/acre, Eschmeyer 1938), supporting the idea that basin morphology (depth, littoral extent) rather than latitude alone governs productivity.

## Key Findings

- **Total recovered population**: 15,454 fish, 866.6 lbs (86.6 lbs/acre or 1,545 fish/acre) — likely an underestimate due to incomplete recovery of small/sinking fish.
- **[[Bluegill Sunfish]]** was both the numerically and biomass-dominant species (26.25% of number, 61.99% of weight); legal bluegills alone = ~50 lbs/acre.
- **Legal game fish** constituted only ~11% of total number but **70% of total weight**, underscoring the disproportionate biomass contribution of larger, older individuals.
- **Angling efficiency**: 431 fish (153 lbs) captured in 80 man-hours (fly rod) plus 22 bass in 51.5 man-hours (casting rod), yielding an average catch rate of **4.62 fish/hour**, over four times the Michigan statewide creel census average (~1 fish/hour).
- Angling removed ~24–31% of legal bass and bluegill standing stocks over 22 days prior to poisoning, demonstrating significant exploitation potential even under presumed "expert-biased" fishing effort.
- **Forage fish** (six species) made up 54.6% of total fish numbers but only 3.4% of total weight — high numerical turnover, low biomass contribution typical of small cyprinids and darters.
- **Coarse fish** contributed 16.3% of total weight (14.1 lbs/acre), dominated by yellow bullhead (10.16% of total weight).
- Age-class analysis of **[[Largemouth Black Bass]]** and **[[Bluegill Sunfish]]** indicated legal-length attainment (10 in bass; presumably 6 in bluegill/pumpkinseed under MI law) occurring late in the third or early fourth year of growth.
- Study reinforces prior findings (Eschmeyer 1938; Thompson & Bennett 1939) that age-class strength in centrarchids is highly variable and not uniformly correlated with year-class number, complicating standard age-structure assumptions across lakes.
- Recovery methodology (intensive hand-picking after rotenone) is explicitly acknowledged as biased against small-bodied and non-floating species (e.g., **[[Iowa Darter]]**, minnows), a methodological caveat relevant to comparative population studies using rotenone census techniques.
