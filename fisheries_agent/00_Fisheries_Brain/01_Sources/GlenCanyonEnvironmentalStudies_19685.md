# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Program Update May 1989

#environmental-studies #river-ecology #dam-operations #program-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a program update newsletter from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Phase II program, dated May 1989. It announces the appointment of Dr. [[Duncan Patten]] from [[Arizona State University]] as Senior Scientist to provide scientific oversight and integration for the continuing environmental studies of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem. The update describes the organizational structure of the multi-agency GCES program, summarizes recent meetings of environmental and economic study teams, and outlines the program's approach to coordinating technical studies, policy direction, and stakeholder input. The GCES program was initiated in 1982 by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] to assess environmental impacts of dam operations and inform management decisions.

## Background & Description

The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] represents a multi-agency effort coordinated by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Upper Colorado Region, to study environmental impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on downstream ecosystems. The program entered Phase II following a review by the [[National Academy of Sciences]] (NAS) that identified the need for clearer separation of administrative and scientific oversight. 

Dr. [[Duncan Patten]] was appointed Senior Scientist effective April 3, 1989, on a 40 percent time basis through an Intergovernmental Personnel Action. Dr. Patten serves as Director of the Center for Environmental Studies at [[Arizona State University]] and holds a Ph.D. in botany from [[Duke University]]. His role includes ensuring technical studies complement each other, directing the design of the GCES Integrated Research Plan, and coordinating with program manager [[David L. Wegner]] and the [[Executive Review Committee]].

The program organizational structure includes five key components: Primary Coordination (GCES Program Manager and offices in Flagstaff, Arizona and Salt Lake City, Utah), Management and Policy Direction ([[Executive Review Committee]] with representatives from Reclamation, [[National Park Service]], [[Fish & Wildlife Service]], Department of Interior, and [[Western Area Power Administration]]), Technical Input (constituent groups including fishing, recreation, electrical, water, and environmental organizations), Scientific Direction and Review (NAS oversight, Senior Scientist integration, individual study review), and Study Teams and Work Groups (Environmental Study Team and Economic Study Team).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The studies focus on the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], including [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. Environmental concerns addressed include accelerated beach loss in the [[Grand Canyon]] resulting from fluctuating dam flows, impacts on riparian communities, and effects on aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems. The [[U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service]] is developing a Biological Opinion on dam operations, with technical requirements coordinated within the GCES program.

The program examines both environmental and economic dimensions, including power generation impacts, recreation economics, water management, and hydrologic inputs. Recreation economics studies by HBRS during the initial GCES phase found that dam operations have limited impact on recreational benefits during high water years but increased impact during low water years.

## Key Findings

**Organizational Developments:**
- Dr. [[Duncan Patten]] appointed as Senior Scientist to provide scientific integration and oversight, addressing NAS recommendations for improved scientific coordination
- Dr. [[Wayne Marchant]] (Reclamation, Denver Office) named overall GCES program coordinator in December 1988
- Program includes over 20 representatives across multiple federal agencies, state organizations, and constituent groups

**Meeting Outcomes (March-May 1989):**
- Environmental Study Team (March 20, Las Vegas): Reviewed draft technical study plan, fiscal year 1990-1991 studies, economic-environmental integration process, and Colorado River Annual Operating Plan protocol
- Economic Study Team (March 21, Las Vegas): Discussed reorganization, Phase II technical study plan, power modeling methodologies, and recreation economics; concluded that combination of approaches may be required for adequate representation of CRSP customers
- [[Executive Review Committee]] (May 4, Las Vegas): Discussed accelerated beach loss concerns, NEPA compliance requirements, and need for more defined study process with definitive decision route

**Program Structure:**
- Two primary study teams established: Environmental Study Team and Economic Study Team
- Adjunct work groups assembled as needed for specific tasks (contract review, report development, data requirements, research trip planning)
- Public information transfer program designed to transmit technical information to study participants and interested parties
- NAS eight-person GCES Review Committee headed by Dr. Richard Marzolf provides overall scientific review

**Upcoming Activities (June 1989):**
- Technical Work Group Meeting with Senior Scientist (June 5, Tempe)
- Environmental Technical Team meeting (June 15, Flagstaff)
- [[Executive Review Committee]] meeting with evening forum discussion (June 15, Flagstaff)
- Economic Technical Team meeting (June 16, Flagstaff)
