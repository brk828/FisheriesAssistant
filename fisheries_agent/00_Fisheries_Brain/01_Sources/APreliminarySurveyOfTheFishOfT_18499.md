# A preliminary survey of the fish of the Little Colorado River from Blue Spring to the vicinity of Big Canyon, Coconino County, Arizona

## Executive Summary
This 1977 preliminary survey, conducted by S. O. Minckley for the Museum of Northern Arizona, documents fish populations in a previously uncollected stretch of the **[[Little Colorado River]]**, spanning from **[[Blue Spring]]** downstream five miles to the vicinity of **[[Big Canyon]]**. The survey identified only two fish species: the ubiquitous **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) and the endangered **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*). The capture of three adult Humpback Chub at Station 4 represents the first record of this species occurring this far upstream in the mainstream Little Colorado River, suggesting a previously unknown, viable population utilizing this specific travertine-pool habitat corridor. The paper attributes this range extension to the unique thermal regime of the river, which is warmed by spring inflow and antagonistic to the primary competitor/predator, the introduced **[[Rainbow Trout]]**.

## Background & Description
### *Rhinichthys osculus* (Speckled Dace)
*   **Size Distribution:** Bimodal distribution observed across stations.
    *   Above Blue Spring outflow: Large adults, 70–120 mm Total Length (T.L.).
    *   Downstream stations (1–4): Predominantly small individuals, 30–60 mm T.L., with occasional larger specimens.
*   **Population Notes:** The author notes that dace from this Little Colorado population appear morphologically distinct from mainstream Colorado River populations, though formal systematic revision was pending at time of writing.

### *Gila cypha* (Humpback Chub)
*   **Sample Size:** N=3 specimens collected (Station 4 only).
*   **Total Length:** Range 278–295 mm T.L. (Mean = 284 mm T.L.).
*   **Condition:** All specimens were noted to be in "good condition" and "quite vigorous," with no ectoparasites observed. Specimens were released alive following measurement and photography due to endangered status.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
### Habitat Structure
The surveyed river reach is defined by a series of large travertine dams creating deep pool habitats:
*   **Station 1 (Blue Spring):** Primary spring source; discharge averaging 223 cfs; depth 1–6+ ft; mud substrate; Temp 21°C.
*   **Station 4 (Big Canyon vicinity):** Site of Humpback Chub capture; a large pool (200×100 ft) up to 20+ ft deep, bounded by travertine dams 10–15 ft high; deep mud substrate; Temp 20°C.

### Species Interactions & Thermal Refuge
The paper hypothesizes that the **[[Little Colorado River]]** functions as a critical thermal refuge and nursery for the **[[Humpback Chub]]**, distinct from the cold-water dam-regulated mainstream **[[Colorado River]]**.
*   **Exclusion of Competitors:** Water temperatures generally exceeding 20°C are cited as inhibitory to **[[Rainbow Trout]]** feeding and activity (per Minckley 1973), preventing the establishment of major trout spawning runs in this specific tributary (unlike other Grand Canyon tributaries).
*   **Species Co-occurrence:** Humpback Chub were found in direct association with Speckled Dace in slow-current sections of the pool at Station 4.
*   **Historical Records:** The paper references historical anecdotal evidence (Johnson & Sanderson, 1968) of unidentified large fish and **[[Channel Catfish]]** carcasses near Blue Spring, suggesting the pool/dam habitat system extends fish accessibility much further upstream than previously documented.

## Key Findings
1.  **Range Extension:** This survey provides the first documented evidence of *Gila cypha* (Humpback Chub) 7 miles upstream from the Colorado River confluence, indicating a potential mainstream breeding/resident population rather than just incidental use near the mouth.
2.  **Habitat Corridor Hypothesis:** The author proposes a **13-mile corridor** of viable Humpback Chub habitat exists from the confluence up to Blue Spring, based on the continuity of pool/travertine dam structures.
3.  **Tributary Significance:** The Little Colorado River is identified as the largest tributary entering the Colorado River in the Grand Canyon reach immediately below Glen Canyon Dam (excluding the Paria River, which is hydrologically insignificant), reinforcing its ecological importance for native fish refugia.
4.  **Management Recommendations:** The report explicitly recommends a long-term study program including:
    *   Population and age-structure analysis of the Humpback Chub.
    *   Removal of 20 adults for a captive propagation program.
    *   Investigation into hybridization potential with other *Gila* species.
    *   Full life-history study utilizing a sacrifice-sample protocol of approximately 50 specimens.
