# BLM Responds to Public Concerns Along San Pedro River

#conservation #riparian-ecology #habitat-management #land-acquisition

## Executive Summary

The [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) announced a temporary six-month suspension of land acquisitions along the [[San Pedro River]] in southeastern Arizona following June 10 discussions with Natural Resource Conservation Districts (NRCDs). The decision responds to concerns from private landowners who prefer conservation programs and easements over government land acquisition. The BLM and NRCDs developed a Memorandum of Understanding to increase coordination and will use a Coordinated Resource Management process to evaluate alternatives to land acquisitions while maintaining conservation objectives.

## Background & Description

The [[San Pedro River]] has been a focus of BLM conservation efforts since the early 1980s. In 1986, the BLM acquired a 50,000-acre tract of high-value [[Riparian Habitat]] stretching from the Mexican border to St. David, Arizona through land exchange. Congress passed the Arizona-Idaho Conservation Act in 1988, creating the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]].

In 1990, BLM prepared a proposal as part of the Department of the Interior's wetland initiative identifying 34,905 acres of private land for potential acquisition. These lands were identified as important stepping stones of habitat for nesting and migrating birds and wildlife.

Land acquisitions are funded through [[Land and Water Conservation Fund]] monies appropriated by Congress, which established the fund to provide long-term conservation and protection of lands with important natural resource values. Congress provided funds to BLM during 1991 and 1992 for initial land acquisitions along the San Pedro. Several scattered parcels have already been purchased, and BLM is negotiating on several others.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[San Pedro River]] corridor provides critical [[Riparian Habitat]] in southeastern Arizona. The designation of the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]] focused additional interest in the importance of riparian areas and the special values of the San Pedro River ecosystem.

The river lands serve as important stepping stones of habitat for both nesting and migrating birds and wildlife, functioning as a vital corridor in an otherwise arid landscape. The 50,000-acre tract acquired in 1986 represents high-value riparian habitat essential for regional biodiversity.

## Key Findings

**Policy Decision**: BLM announced temporary suspension of new land acquisitions along the [[San Pedro River]] for six months following June 10 meeting with Natural Resource Conservation Districts.

**Stakeholder Concerns**: Local NRCD members and private landowners requested that government land acquisitions be replaced with conservation programs and easements on private lands.

**Ongoing Acquisitions**: Acquisition of selected parcels currently under negotiation will continue despite the suspension. Several scattered parcels have already been purchased using 1991 and 1992 Congressional appropriations.

**Memorandum of Understanding**: BLM Safford District and NRCDs developed a Memorandum of Understanding to increase coordination and build stronger working relationships. Final language was drafted at the June 10 meeting. The objective is to develop and initiate resource conservation programs and promote proper use of all lands subject to respective authorities.

**Coordinated Resource Management Process**: Both parties committed to a Coordinated Resource Management process to evaluate alternatives to land acquisitions for conservation purposes. This cooperative team approach resolves resource conflict issues and reaches agreement by consensus, providing opportunities for participation by affected interests and open communications with the public. Safford District Manager Ray Brady stated BLM will ensure balanced representation of affected interests.

**Public Involvement**: BLM encourages individuals to contact them with opinions during the six-month suspension period to further develop working relationships with NRCDs and explore opportunities for public involvement in the lands acquisition program.

**Conservation Area History**: The 1988 Arizona-Idaho Conservation Act created the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]], building on the 1986 acquisition of 50,000 acres. The 1990 wetland initiative proposal identified 34,905 acres of private land for potential acquisition to protect habitat corridors.
