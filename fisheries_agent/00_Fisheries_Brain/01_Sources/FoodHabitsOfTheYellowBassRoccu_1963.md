## Executive Summary
This 1963 study examines the food habits and diel activity patterns of the Yellow Bass (*Roccus mississippiensis*) in Clear Lake, Iowa, during the summer of 1962, a year marked by a return to normal water levels after a prolonged drought (1955–1962). Despite a modest increase in growth rates compared to previous drought years, the study found no corresponding shift toward piscivory (fish-eating), which had been hypothesized based on historical growth-diet correlations. Instead, [[Yellow Bass]] diets remained dominated by entomostracans (copepods, cladocerans) in young-of-year fish and immature aquatic insects (primarily [[Diptera]]) in older fish. The study also confirms strong nocturnal activity and feeding patterns, with peak net catches and stomach fullness occurring between dusk and pre-dawn hours.

## Background & Description
* **Species:** *Roccus mississippiensis* (Yellow Bass), likely introduced to [[Clear Lake]] in the late 1920s.
* **Population Status:** Exhibits significant **stunting** due to overpopulation, reducing commercial/sport interest.
* **Historical Size Range:** 8–11 inches common in the early 1950s.
* **Contemporary Size Range (post-1956):** Rarely exceeding 6–7 inches.
* **1962 Growth Data:** Average total length increased by approximately 1 inch compared to 1961 and prior years, coinciding with the lake returning to overflow level in July 1962.
* **Size Range Sampled (1962):** 3.0 to 8.2 inches (Total Length).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
* **Habitat:** Resides in [[Clear Lake]], a shallow freshwater lake system historically subject to drought-induced water level fluctuations (as low as 3.34 ft below outlet in Dec. 1958).
* **Trophic Level:** Primarily a benthic/planktonic invertivore, transitioning from micro-crustacean predator (juveniles) to insectivore (adults).
* **Diel Activity Pattern:** Strongly **nocturnal**. Peak activity (based on gillnet catches) occurred between 8:00 PM and 4:00 AM, with the largest samples caught at 10 PM, 12 AM, and 4 AM.
* **Feeding Periodicity:** Stomach fullness data revealed three peaks: 6:00 PM, a statistically weak peak based on a single specimen, and a significant peak from 2:00 AM to 8:00 AM, suggesting this early morning window represents the primary feeding period.
* **Prey Interactions:**
    * **Juveniles** rely heavily on [[Entomostraca]] (Copepoda, Cladocera) and amphipods ([[Hyalella]] sp.).
    * **Adults** shift primarily to immature insects, specifically [[Chironomidae]] (Diptera), supplemented by [[Trichoptera]] (caddisfly larvae) and crustaceans.
    * **Predator-Prey Dynamics:** Despite increased growth rates in 1962, there was no increase in the consumption of small forage fish, contradicting earlier findings where rapid growth was linked to a piscivorous diet (Bailey and Harrison, 1945).

## Key Findings

### Young-of-Year Diet (65 specimens, 1.3–2.4 in.)
* **Dominant Prey:** Entomostracans were the most abundant food source.
    * *Copepoda*: 87.6% frequency of occurrence.
    * *Cladocera*: 50.7% frequency of occurrence.
* **Secondary Prey:** [[Chironomidae]] (30.7%) and [[Hyalella]] sp. (27.6%).
* **Note:** Despite high environmental abundance of *Hyalella* (observed on submerged substrates), it ranked low in dietary importance, potentially due to collection timing (dusk to midnight) versus peak *Hyalella* nocturnal activity.
* Unlike observations in North Twin Lake (Kutkuhn, 1955), no distinct dietary shift from crustaceans to insects was observed as juvenile Yellow Bass grew within the 1.5–1.7 inch range.

### Older Yellow Bass Diet (285 stomachs with food)
* **Immature Insects:** Found in 81% of stomachs (higher than previous years: 75% in 1958, 60% in 1960).
    * **Diptera:** Most frequent and numerous insect order; predominantly Tendipedids (Chironomidae), with minor Ceratopogonidae (8%) presence.
    * **Trichoptera:** Second most important insect group, though frequency has continually declined since 1957.
* **Crustaceans:** Remained significant, though *Cladocera* frequency (43%) dropped from 1958/1960 highs (79%/69%) back to levels comparable with 1957.
* **Piscivory:** Small fish consumption remained low (1% frequency), consistent with previous years (2–5%), despite the return of favorable lake conditions and growth increases.
* **Adult Insects:** Rarely consumed (only 11 stomachs total), though isolated incidents of heavy feeding on adult Diptera were noted.
