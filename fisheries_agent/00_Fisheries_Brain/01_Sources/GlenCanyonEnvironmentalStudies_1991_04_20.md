## Executive Summary

This note synthesizes meeting notes and a preliminary trip report from the **Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Aquatic Coordination Team**, dated April 19, 1991. The primary focus is coordination of multi-agency native fish research on the Colorado River and Little Colorado River (LCR) below [[Glen Canyon Dam]], centered on the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Key activities include: reconnaissance and capture/removal efforts by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), and [[Bio/West]]; a hatchery propagation effort at Page Springs; a [[Gila Taxonomy]] genetics/morphology study; and establishment of shared hydrological/water quality databases. The accompanying AGFD trip report (March 26–April 14, 1991) details a mainstem and LCR native fish survey that captured ~50 humpback chub, collected eggs from a single ripe female, and documented associated native and non-native fish species.

## Background & Description

### Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*)
- **Total length (TL) range captured:** 69–446 mm
- **Weight range:** <10–1037 g
- **Broodstock selected for hatchery propagation (Page Springs):**
  - 5 males: 255–358 mm TL
  - 5 females: 328–437 mm TL
- **Notable recapture:** A female with blue Carlin tag #257, measured 374 mm TL / 521 g at capture (March 30); remeasured at hatchery April 15 at 486 g (weight loss of ~35 g in captivity).
- **Radio-tagged individuals:** Two radio-tagged chub captured via trammel net near LCR mouth; additional transmitting individuals detected at frequencies 40.670, 40.680, and 40.730 MHz with pulse rates of 40–80/min.
- **Reproductive condition:** Only one female was found extrudable (ripe) in the field; only a small proportion of males were ripe. No larval humpback chub were observed macroscopically; larval drift/seine samples were inconclusive at time of report.

### Associated Species Documented
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*)
- [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus*)
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)
- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) — single individual, 650 mm TL
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat targeted:** Backwaters, tributary confluences, and other native fish rearing habitats along the Colorado River mainstem and the Little Colorado River (LCR), particularly near the LCR mouth and Salt Trail Canyon reach (~0.6–12.5 km above LCR mouth).
- **Thermal influence:** Blue Springs base flow dilution effects on water chemistry were tracked; alkalinity and CO₂ concentrations (210 and 256 mg/L at certain sites) were linked to spring dilution gradients.
- **Behavioral/distributional notes:** Catch rates of large chub at the 1.8 km camp declined sharply after March 30, coincident with a water temperature drop — suggesting chub may migrate toward the LCR mouth (cooler mainstem confluence) under changing thermal conditions. Nearly all large chub captured after March 30 were located at the LCR mouth rather than upstream camp sites.
- **Radiotelemetry within LCR proper yielded negative results**, reinforcing that tagged fish had likely relocated to the mouth/mainstem confluence.
- **Sampling stratification:** AGFD and [[Bio/West]] coordinated habitat partitioning — AGFD focused on **backwaters** (March–September, plus one winter mainstem trip), while Bio/West focused on **near-shore mainstem habitats** via monthly trips, primarily using trammel nets, to reduce sampling overlap.

## Key Findings

### Population & Capture Data (AGFD Trip, March 26–April 14, 1991)
- ~50 [[Humpback Chub]] captured total; 14 were recaptures.
- 10 adult chub (5 male, 5 female) and their eggs were transported by helicopter to **AGFD's Page Springs Hatchery** for propagation.
- **Eggs collected:** ~200 eggs collected in the field; **all eggs perished** prior to or during hatchery transfer.
- One chub PIT-tagged (7F7D series) at LCR mouth escaped before tag number could be recorded — no further data obtained.
- Two hoop nets and a trammel net set below Triple Drop on March 28 were unsuccessful (fishless).

### Coordination & Institutional Notes
- **Access to hydrological data:** [[Bill Vernieu]] designated as GCES lead for coordinating USGS data account access; SAS software loaded on GCES computers for shared analyses.
- **Flow routing models:** Multiple existing models (Jerold Lazenby, Larry Stevens, Julia Graf) to be compiled and distributed to researchers rather than developing a new one from scratch.
- **Database standardization:** dBase IV adopted as the standard data management system; a glossary of habitat terminology to be developed for consistency across research teams.
- **PIT tagging policy debate:** Discussion on appropriate minimum size for PIT tagging (current FWS minimum 175 mm) — ACT considering requesting reduction to 150 mm. ASU advocated tagging *all* captured humpback chub; a monitoring approach was adopted to track cumulative tagging impacts on the population.
- **Gila Taxonomy Study:** A non-lethal sampling protocol (fin clips and muscle plugs) developed for genetic/taxonomic study of *Gila* species in the LCR, led by Dr. [[Michael Douglas]] and B. Williams; permits managed by Wayne Starns; equipment procurement by Kathy Karp (USFWS Vernal).
- **Inter-agency overlap management:** AGFD and Bio/West agreed to differentiate sampling by equipment type and habitat (backwater vs. near-shore) to minimize redundant effort; [[Robert Valdez]] and [[Dennis Kubly]] tasked with resolving remaining overlap concerns.
- **Razorback Sucker:** Tissue sample collection identified as a need for an associated genetics study (species: *Xyrauchen texanus*, implied but not explicitly named beyond "Razorback sucker").

### Logistics
- Helicopter support required for LCR/Grand Canyon operations; all personnel mandated to complete helicopter safety training; each research group required to designate a sling-load foreman.
- Archaeological clearance survey of LCR conducted April 23, 1991, in coordination with the **Hopi Tribe**, **Navajo Nation**, and **National Park Service (NPS)**.
- Satellite communication linkage between research camps planned via GEOSTAR, field-tested May 21, 1991.

### Water Quality (LCR Reach)
- In situ measurements (dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity) taken via Series 2000 Hydrolab.
- Alkalinity and CO₂ levels (210–256 mg/L) attributed to varying dilution of **Blue Springs** base flow along the longitudinal LCR gradient.
