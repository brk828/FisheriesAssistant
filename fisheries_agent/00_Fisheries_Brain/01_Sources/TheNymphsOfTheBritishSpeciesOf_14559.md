## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by H.B.N. Hynes (1955) resolves long-standing difficulties in distinguishing the nymphs (larvae) of the three British species of the stonefly genus [[Capnia]]: **[[Capnia atra]]**, **[[Capnia bifrons]]**, and **[[Capnia vidua]]**. Prior work (Brown 1945; Aubert 1946, 1951; Brinck 1949) had only established characters usable in **fully grown male nymphs** (tenth tergum shape, wing-pad development) or **ripe female nymphs** (vulval chitinisation), none of which reliably applied to immature or partly-grown specimens, and some proposed characters (e.g., Brinck's metanotal bristles) proved unreliable when tested on British material. Hynes re-examined nymphs from Britain, Sweden, and Switzerland and identified new, more broadly applicable morphological characters — particularly bristle length/arrangement on the cerci, femora, and abdominal terga — culminating in a revised identification key for nymphs of all three British species, usable even in moderately small (≥6 mm) nymphs.

## Background & Description

### General Nymphal Body Plan (shared across all three species)
- **Antennae**: bear small spines on the basal half, decreasing in size distally.
- **Cerci**: scattered bristles on basal segments; each subsequent segment bears one long dorsal and one long ventral hair, forming continuous fringes along the cercus length.
- **Mouthparts**: covered in scattered clothing hairs.
- **Legs**: general clothing hair cover; bristles along anterior and posterior margins of the femora; tibiae bear posterior spines and fringes of long hairs extending onto the tarsi.
- **Body**: general fine clothing hair cover (denser dorsally), plus longer upstanding hairs on head (between antennal bases, between eyes, on vertex), notal sutures, and abdominal terga/sterna posteriorly.
- No structural (as opposed to setal) differences were found among the three species — differentiation rests entirely on **bristle length and arrangement**.

### Species-Specific Diagnostic Characters

| Character | *C. atra* | *C. bifrons* | *C. vidua* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cercal fringe hair length | Much longer than segments (in larger nymphs) | Shorter or equal to segment length | Shorter or equal to segment length |
| Wing-pads (mature male) | Fully/well developed | Rudimentary | Rudimentary |
| 10th tergum (mature male) | Pointed, dorsally emarginate | Truncated (lateral view) | Broadly rounded posteriorly |
| 8th sternum (ripe female) | Heavily chitinised vulva | Not chitinised | Not chitinised |
| Femoral bristles (anterior vs posterior margin) | Roughly equal | Posterior bristles longer | Roughly equal |
| Mid-tergal short upstanding hairs (abdomen) | Absent | Present (paired, ~half length of long hairs) | Absent |
| Clothing hair density/length | Long and dense | Shorter, sparser | Long and dense |
| Overall hairiness/pronotal bristles | — | Less hairy | More hairy, longer pronotal bristles (per Aubert, though not confirmed in British subspecies) |
| Habitat association | Lake shores (lacustrine) | Streams and lake shores | Streams |

- The mid-tergal short upstanding hair pair diagnostic for *C. bifrons* is present even in small nymphs (4–5 mm) but is easily obscured by mounting/clearing techniques, as the hairs shift position and resemble ordinary clothing hairs.
- Femoral bristle-length asymmetry (character distinguishing *C. bifrons*) only reliably applies to nymphs **>6 mm**; it has not been verified in small *C. atra*.
- Cercal fringe length differences do **not** reliably apply to very small *C. atra* nymphs (2–3 mm, per Brinck's Swedish material), where fringe hairs are shorter than the segments — mirroring the condition in the other two species at that size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Capnia atra]]**: In Britain, appears to be **entirely lacustrine** (lake-shore dwelling); in Sweden it may show more variable habitat use.
- **[[Capnia bifrons]]**: Occupies both **streams and lake shores**, making it the species most likely to be confused with *C. atra* where habitats overlap.
- **[[Capnia vidua]]**: Restricted to **streams**; British material examined came from Maize Beck, Upper Teesdale. A British subspecies, *C. v. anglica* (Aubert 1950), differs subtly from the continental *C. v. vidua* — notably in prothoracic hair length, suggesting a possible racial/geographic morphological divergence.
- Because *C. atra* is lake-restricted in Britain, the primary practical identification challenge in the field is distinguishing it from *C. bifrons* only in **lake-shore populations**, not stream populations.
- Prior mixed-population studies (e.g., Brown 1945, working with sympatric *C. atra* and *C. bifrons*) suffered from uncertain specimen identity, underscoring the ecological reality of co-occurring congeners at some sites.

## Key Findings

1. **No gross structural differences** exist among nymphs of the three British *Capnia* species — differentiation depends entirely on **setal (bristle/hair) characters**, a departure from typical morphological taxonomy relying on structural traits.
2. Previously published diagnostic characters (Aubert 1946; Brinck 1949) were shown to be **unreliable or inapplicable in Britain**, including Brinck's proposed metanotal bristle character, which Hynes found present (albeit reduced) in many British *C. bifrons* nymphs and even in some Swedish specimens.
3. All previously known reliable characters (tenth tergum shape, wing-pad development, vulval chitinisation) apply **only to nymphs nearing emergence** (well-grown males or ripe females), limiting their utility for general population studies.
4. Hynes identified **new characters** usable across a broader range of nymphal growth stages:
   - Relative length of cercal fringe hairs vs. cercal segments (best in larger nymphs).
   - Relative length of anterior vs. posterior femoral bristles (reliable above ~6 mm).
   - Presence/absence of paired short upstanding hairs on abdominal terga (diagnostic for *C. bifrons*, visible even in small 4–5 mm nymphs, though obscured by slide-mounting).
5. A revised dichotomous **identification key** was constructed and validated by Dr. P. Brinck against independent Swedish material of *C. atra* and *C. bifrons*.
6. The key is most robust for nymphs **≥6 mm**; identification of smaller/partly-grown *C. atra* nymphs remains uncertain due to lack of available half-grown specimens, though the abdominal tergal hair and femoral bristle characters are predicted to remain useful for separating young *C. atra* from *C. bifrons*.
7. The study reinforces a broader methodological point (echoing [[Hynes 1941]] work on *Leuctra* and *Nemoura*) that **multiple specimens per locality** should be examined for reliable stonefly nymph identification, given intraspecific variability in setal characters.
