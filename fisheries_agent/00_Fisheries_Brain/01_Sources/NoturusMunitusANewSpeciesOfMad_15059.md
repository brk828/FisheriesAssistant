# Noturus munitus, A New Species of Madtom, Family Ictaluridae, from Southern United States

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #ictaluridae #madtom #species-description

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *Noturus munitus* (Frecklebelly Madtom), a new species of madtom catfish from the [[Pearl River]] system in Louisiana and Mississippi, with additional specimens from the [[Cahaba River]] in Alabama and the [[Tombigbee River]] in Mississippi. The species is assigned to the subgenus *Rabida* based on its mottled color pattern and serrated pectoral spines. A total of 1,979 specimens were examined for this description. The species appears most closely related to *[[Noturus furiosus]]* from North Carolina rivers, forming part of the *furiosus* species group. The holotype is a male 50.5 mm standard length collected from the Pearl River near Sandy Hook, Marion County, Mississippi in November 1955.

## Background & Description

*Noturus munitus* belongs to the subgenus *Rabida* Jordan and Evermann, characterized by mottled or blotched patterns forming prominent saddles on the back and serrations along both anterior and posterior edges of the pectoral spine. Other species in this subgenus include *[[Noturus eleutherus]]*, *[[Noturus furiosus]]*, *[[Noturus hildebrandi]]*, and *[[Noturus miurus]]*. The first collection of this new species was obtained from the Pearl River in 1950, initially thought to be endemic to that system.

**Diagnostic Characters:**
- Posterior margin of adipose fin nearly free from caudal fin
- Anal fin short with 12-14 rays
- Caudal fin abbreviated with 45-52 rays
- Normally 9 pelvic rays, I,8 pectoral rays
- 11 preoperculomandibular pores
- Infraorbital and supraorbital canals separate anteriorly
- Body short and chunky with 30-32 (seldom 33) vertebrae
- Caudal peduncle slender
- Head and spines proportionately large
- Abdomen and often bases of pelvic fins freckled with widely spaced brown chromatophores

**Morphometric Data (Holotype, 50.5 mm SL):**
- Body depth: 211/1000 of standard length
- Head length: 366/1000 of standard length (stepped 2.9 times into SL)
- Caudal peduncle depth: 106/1000 of standard length
- Pectoral spine length: 225/1000 of standard length
- Posterocleithral process length: 122/1000 of standard length

**Meristic Counts (73 specimens):**
- Dorsal rays: I,5 or I,6
- Anal rays: 12-14 (mode 13)
- Pelvic rays: 8-9 (usually 9)
- Pectoral rays: I,7 to I,9 (usually I,8)
- Caudal rays: 45-52 (mode 47-48)
- Vertebrae: 30-33 (mode 31)
- Preoperculomandibular pores: 9-12 (mode 11)

**Coloration in Preservation:**
Heavily mottled with dark brown; side light to medium brown; dark bar across back of head extending through operculum; band from snout through eye to posterior cephalic band; abdomen and base of pelvic fin with numerous round brownish chromatophores; pectoral fin heavily mottled with pale margins; anal fin pigmented at base with distal and medial dark bands and whitish edge; dorsal fin with basal saddle, dusted chromatophores, clear area, broad dark distal band, and white tips; caudal fin with two broad dark crescents (subterminal and medial); adipose fin with dark blotch extending to margin; rectangular dark saddle between dorsal and adipose fins enclosing pair of light predorsal spots.

**Coloration in Life:**
Field notes from two series reveal extensive golden and yellow coloration: sulphur yellow blotch on side of head behind mouth angle; small dull yellow spot behind each eye; bright yellow area above pectoral base; golden yellow blotch anterior to dorsal fin base; bright yellow saddle at posterior dorsal base; bright yellow on adipose fin base, anterior part, and posterior tip; bright yellow oval saddle on dorsal caudal peduncle; yellowish fin bases with translucent distal margins; whitish belly; golden dorsal surface and sides in November specimens; distal part of maxillary barbel white.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Noturus munitus* is an obligate inhabitant of riffles and rapids in the [[Pearl River]] and its larger tributaries. None of the nearly 2,000 specimens was collected from still water. Smaller individuals occur in both shallow and deep riffles alongside larger specimens. Many collecting sites feature abundant river weed (*[[Podostemum]]*) on exposed bedrock at the Fall Line.

**Sympatric Species:**
The species co-occurs with several other *Noturus* species:
- *[[Noturus miurus]]* (common in riffles, prefers slower-flowing parts and silt-covered sandbars)
- *[[Noturus leptacanthus]]* (in Pearl River)
- *[[Noturus nocturnus]]* (in Pearl River)
- *[[Noturus funebris]]* (occasionally in moderate-size tributaries, seldom in main river)

*[[Noturus gyrinus]]*, which inhabits adjacent still waters over silted bottoms, has not been collected with *N. munitus*, reflecting distinct habitat preferences.

**Habitat Characteristics:**
- Fast-flowing water (riffles and rapids)
- Larger rivers and tributaries
- Bedrock substrates with *Podostemum*
- Fall Line zones
- Avoids still water and heavily silted areas

## Key Findings

1. **Species Description:** *Noturus munitus* is described as a new species based on 1,979 specimens, with holotype TU 26250 (male, 50.5 mm SL) from Pearl River, Marion County, Mississippi.

2. **Distribution:** The species occurs in the Pearl River system (Louisiana and Mississippi), with disjunct populations in the Cahaba River (7 specimens) and Tombigbee River (2 specimens) in Alabama. The authors postulate its actual range extends from the Pearl River eastward to the [[Alabama River]] system.

3. **Taxonomic Placement:** Assigned to subgenus *Rabida* based on mottled color pattern and pectoral spine serrations. Most closely related to *Noturus furiosus* from North Carolina's Tar and Neuse rivers, together forming the *furiosus* species group.

4. **Distinguishing Features:** Differs from congeners in having fewer anal rays (12-14) and caudal rays (45-52) than *N. miurus* and *N. furiosus*; differs from *N. hildebrandi* in having more pelvic rays and a longer posterocleithral process; differs from *N. eleutherus* in adipose fin bar pattern.

5. **Etymology:** The specific epithet *munitus* (Latin for "armed" or "protected") refers to the large spines and prominent serrae characteristic of the species.

6. **Ecological Specialization:** Strict riffle-rapid specialist, never collected from still water. Preference for larger rivers with bedrock substrates and *Podostemum* growth distinguishes it from sympatric congeners.

7. **Collection History:** First collected in 1950; initially thought endemic to Pearl River system, but subsequent collections revealed wider distribution. Spotty distribution and preference for larger rivers contributed to its previous absence from scientific collections.

8. **Specimen Distribution:** Paratypes distributed to 11 institutions including [[Academy of Natural Sciences Philadelphia]], [[British Museum of Natural History]], [[Chicago Natural History Museum]], [[Cornell University]], [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], [[Museum National d'Histoire Naturelle Paris]], and others.

9. **Relationship Hypothesis:** Forms part of the *furiosus* species group with *N. furiosus* and undescribed species, suggesting historical biogeographic connections between Gulf Coast and Atlantic Coast drainages.

10. **Conservation Implications:** Apparent rarity and restricted habitat requirements (large river riffles) suggest potential vulnerability, though actual abundance in suitable habitat may be higher than collection records indicate.
