# Short-Term Methods for Estimating the Chronic Toxicity of Effluents and Receiving Waters to Freshwater Organisms

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pimephales-promelas

## Executive Summary

This EPA Project Summary (EPA/600/54-85/014, August 1986) presents the agency's first methods manual for estimating the chronic toxicity of effluents and receiving waters. It describes four short-term (four- to eight-day) static-renewal tests using a [[Fathead Minnow]], a [[Ceriodaphnia Dubia]] cladoceran, and a [[Selenastrum Capricornutum]] alga. The methods support the [[NPDES Permit Program]] under EPA's 1984 policy on water quality-based permit limits for toxic pollutants. The manual also covers laboratory safety, quality assurance, dilution water, effluent sampling and holding, data analysis, and organism culturing. Computer programs for [[Dunnett's Procedure]] and [[Probit Analysis]] appear in an appendix.

## Background & Description

**Regulatory context.** EPA's 1984 policy, published in the *Federal Register* (Vol. 49, No. 48, March 9, 1984), proposed using effluent toxicity data to set control priorities, assess compliance with state water quality standards, and set permit limits. The document states that all states have narrative standards prohibiting discharge of toxic materials in toxic amounts. A companion technical support document from the Office of Water Enforcement and Permits gives further guidance on biomonitoring.

**Test objectives.** The four tests can estimate:
1. Chronic toxicity of end-of-pipe effluent tested in a standard moderately hard synthetic dilution water.
2. Chronic toxicity of effluent diluted with non-toxic upstream receiving water, or with water of similar hardness.
3. Toxicity of downstream receiving water.
4. Effects of multiple discharges on receiving-water quality.

The methods may also help develop site-specific water quality criteria. They were designed for a favorable cost-benefit balance, for on-site testing, and for shipped samples tested at central laboratories.

**The four tests:**
1. Seven-day sub-chronic fathead minnow larval survival and growth test (static renewal).
2. Seven-day (three-brood) chronic *Ceriodaphnia dubia* survival and reproduction test (static renewal).
3. Eight-day sub-chronic fathead minnow embryo-larval survival and teratogenicity test (static renewal).
4. Four-day chronic *Selenastrum capricornutum* growth test (static).

**Endpoint rationale.** The manual notes that acute LC50 tests (two to four days) gave way to longer tests, because the threshold concentration of many compounds declined with exposure length. Full life-cycle tests are costly, and early life-stage data proved adequate for estimating safe concentrations. This shifted the field toward rapid tests measuring growth and reproduction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Safe concentration.** Toxicity tests aim to estimate the "safe" or "no effect" concentration, defined as the concentration that permits normal propagation of fish and other aquatic life in receiving waters.

**Effluent sampling.** Sampling normally occurs at the NPDES permit point. Alternatives include points after final treatment but before the outfall, points before chlorination, and points on influent to municipal plants or individual industrial streams. Grab samples capture toxicity spikes but cover only a moment. A 24-h composite averages quality but may dilute spikes. Aeration during collection should be minimized to limit loss of volatile chemicals. Definitive NPDES tests require daily effluent collection and daily solution renewal.

**Receiving water.** Grab samples are common. Non-toxic dilution water comes from upstream of the outfall or from uncontaminated water of about the same hardness (within 10%). To assess additive effects on already-contaminated water, dilution water is collected daily upstream. Downstream toxicity zones are mapped by sampling at several distances, and a dye study may be needed to correlate downstream and discharge-point toxicity. Five downstream stations can be tested using the same vessels and organisms as a five-dilution effluent test.

**Sample handling.** Samples must be tested within a maximum holding time (the value is garbled in the scan; likely 72 h). Composites are chilled during collection, and samples are held at 4 °C unless used within 24 h. Off-site samples are iced for shipment and tested on arrival whenever possible.

**Sample preparation.** Effluents and surface waters for the *Ceriodaphnia* and fathead minnow tests are filtered through a 30 µm plankton net to remove organisms that could be confused with test species. Water for algal tests is filtered through 0.45 µm. Coarse debris is removed with a 2–4 mm nylon sieve. Dissolved oxygen should be near saturation. Samples heated to 25 °C that exceed 8.5 mg/L (100% saturation) are aerated with an air stone (usually 1–2 min).

## Key Findings

**Core deliverables.** The manual provides four short-term chronic tests for freshwater organisms, along with guidance on laboratory safety, quality assurance (nine elements, including reference toxicants and record keeping), facilities, dilution water, sampling, data analysis, reporting, and culturing.

**Chronic toxicity endpoints** (defined in the manual):
- **[[No Observed Effect Concentration]] (NOEC):** the highest concentration with no statistically significant adverse effect.
- **[[Lowest Observed Effect Concentration]] (LOEC):** the lowest concentration with a statistically significant adverse effect.
- **Maximum Acceptable Toxicant Concentration (MATC):** an undetermined value between NOEC and LOEC.
- **[[Chronic Value]] (ChV):** the geometric mean of NOEC and LOEC, sometimes used interchangeably with MATC.
- **[[Lethal Concentration]] (LC) or Effective Concentration (EC):** a regression-based point estimate. The LC1/EC1 is the threshold concentration, lying between NOEC and LOEC.

**Statistical methods:**
- **[[Dunnett's Procedure]]:** used for growth and reproduction data when normality and homogeneity of variance hold. Requires at least two replicates per treatment.
- **[[Steel's Many-One Rank Test]]:** a rank-based alternative when assumptions fail. Requires at least four replicates. Safe concentration is reported as NOEC.
- **[[Probit Analysis]]:** used for mortality data to estimate LC1 and confidence intervals, requiring at least two partial mortalities.
- **[[Fisher's Exact Test]]:** applied to *Ceriodaphnia* mortality before reproduction analysis. It is a conservative test of equal survival proportions.
- Mortality data from the fathead minnow tests can use Dunnett's or Steel's tests after an arcsine transformation of the square root of the proportion dead.
- Data should always be plotted first. Arcsine and log transformations are acceptable if they help meet assumptions.
- Test sensitivity should be reported as the minimum detectable difference between means and its percent change from control. Beta (Type II error) is an alternative power measure.

**Test acceptability.** In the *Ceriodaphnia* test, controls should produce three broods in seven days. The algal test runs 96 h, and extending it to 14 days allows measurement of algal growth potential in wastewater and surface water. Algal responses can be analyzed as proportions of control growth by Probit Analysis, or transformed and analyzed by Dunnett's or Steel's tests.
