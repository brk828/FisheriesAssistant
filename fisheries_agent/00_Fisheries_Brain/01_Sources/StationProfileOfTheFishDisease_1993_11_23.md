## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative **Station Profile** for the [[Fish Disease Control Center]] (FDCC), a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1962 under the [[Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956]]. Rather than presenting morphological or ecological data on a single species, the report describes the operational mission, staffing, funding, physical infrastructure, and public outreach/education activities of a regional fish health diagnostic laboratory. The FDCC serves an eight-state region across the Upper Midwest and [[Great Lakes Basin]], providing diagnostic and disease-control services to National Fish Hatcheries, tribal facilities, and state/private aquaculture operations via cooperative agreements with the U.S. sections of the [[Great Lakes Fish Commission]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although not a species ecology report, the document defines the **institutional and geographic niche** of the FDCC within regional fisheries management:

- **Service Region:** An 8-state jurisdiction spanning the Upper Midwest and the [[Great Lakes Basin]].
- **Client Facilities:** National Fish Hatcheries, tribal fish culture facilities, and state and private fish hatcheries.
- **Cooperative Framework:** Operates through agreements between the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and U.S. sections of the [[Great Lakes Fish Commission]].
- **Host Institution:** Co-located with the [[National Fishery Research Center]] in La Crosse, Wisconsin, sharing outreach efforts with the [[Northern Prairie National Wildlife Research Field Station]] and the [[Environmental Management Technical Center]].
- **Applied Disease/Ecosystem Topics Covered in Outreach:** Zebra mussel life history and environmental impact, wild celery habitat for canvasback ducks, sea lamprey control in the [[Great Lakes Basin]], and fish health diagnosis in aquaculture — reflecting the broader aquatic ecosystem context in which the FDCC operates.

## Key Findings

- **Mission:** Diagnose and control infectious diseases of fish across an 8-state region, supporting both federal (National Fish Hatchery) and non-federal (tribal, state, private) aquaculture operations.
- **Training & Education Activities (FY1993):**
  - Dec 1992: Coldwater Fish Culture shortcourse for 35 (predominantly Native American) students at Lac du Flambeau Reservation, Minocqua, WI.
  - Jan 1993: Hosted UW-Stevens Point undergraduate in a laboratory skills placement (Volunteer Experience Program).
  - Feb 1993: 23rd annual "Introduction to Fish Health" shortcourse, 22 students, UW-La Crosse.
  - Feb 1993: Public outreach booth at Valley View Mall reaching 5,587 visitors on aquatic resource topics.
  - Apr 1993: Career/fish health seminar for 15 students at Lake Superior State University.
  - Aug 1993: "Common Sense Approach to Fish Health Management" seminar for 50 participants in Lancaster, WI.
- **Community Engagement:** Participated in two National Fishing Week events (June 1993) reaching ~170 children and senior participants; received local news coverage.
- **Research Output:** Presented poster on completed brook trout parasite research (with U.S. Army, Fort McCoy) at the 1993 Midwest Fish Health Workshop, Denver, CO.
- **Infrastructure:** Operating in 1,631 sq ft of lab/office/storage space at $5.28/sq ft; new GSA-leased building in planning stages due to increasing caseload, diagnostic/extension service demand, and staffing needs.
