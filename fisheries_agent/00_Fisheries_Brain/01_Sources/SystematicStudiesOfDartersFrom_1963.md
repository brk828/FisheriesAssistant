# Systematic Studies of Darters from Southeastern United States (Pisces, Percidae)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae #Etheostoma #Percina #taxonomy #darters

## Executive Summary

This 1963 Cornell doctoral dissertation by William Joseph Richards presents a three-part systematic revision of darter species (family [[Percidae]]) native to river drainages of the southeastern United States. The work combines descriptive taxonomy (including a new species description), morphological variation analysis, and phylogenetic/relational discussion within two genera: [[Percina]] and [[Etheostoma]]. The study spans the Alabama River System, several Atlantic-slope drainages in Georgia and the Carolinas, and the upper Tennessee River System, providing a foundational resource for darter systematics in the region.

## Background & Description

### Part I — Subgenus Hadropterus (Genus *Percina*)
- A **new species of *Percina*** from the **Alabama River System** is described, distinguished from congeners *[[Percina nigrofasciata]]* and *[[Percina sciera]]* by:
  - A **distinctive color pattern**
  - **Small scales**: lateral line scale counts of **77–90**
- The nomenclatural history of the subgenus **[[Hadropterus]]** is reviewed, and the subgenus is formally **rediagnosed and redescribed**.

### Part II — Subgenus Oligocephalus (Genus *Etheostoma*)
- Four species examined: *[[Etheostoma fricksium]]*, *[[Etheostoma mariae]]*, *[[Etheostoma parvipinne]]*, and *[[Etheostoma hopkinsi]]*.
- *E. fricksium*, *E. mariae*, and *E. parvipinne* form a **natural species group** within subgenus **[[Oligocephalus]]**.
- *E. hopkinsi* was historically confused with *E. fricksium* but is shown to be **easily separable** on morphological grounds.
- Degree of morphological variation correlates with geographic range:
  - *E. mariae* — narrow endemic range, **minimal variation**
  - *E. fricksium* — **slight variation**
  - *E. parvipinne* — wide-ranging, **highly variable**

### Part III — Etheostoma thalassinum Species Group (Subgenus Etheostoma)
- Three closely related species comprise this group:
  - *[[Etheostoma thalassinum]]* (Santee River System)
  - *[[Etheostoma inscriptum]]* (Altamaha, Savannah, and Edisto river systems)
  - *[[Etheostoma swannanoa]]* (headwaters of the Tennessee River System)
- *E. swannanoa* exhibits **specialized derived features** distinguishing it from its two relatives:
  - **Absence of palatine teeth**
  - **Reduction in number of prevomerine teeth**
  - **Basal orange-red band** present in the spinous dorsal fin
- These specialized traits link the *thalassinum* group to the *[[Etheostoma blennioides]]* species group.
- Sexual dimorphism analysis in large series of *E. inscriptum* found that **only fin ray counts** differed significantly between sexes (other morphological characters showed no significant sexual variation).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All taxa discussed are **freshwater darters** (Percidae), small benthic fishes inhabiting streams and rivers of the southeastern U.S. Atlantic and Gulf drainages.
- Species show **drainage-restricted distributions**, reflecting classic allopatric divergence patterns among river systems:
  - Alabama River System (Gulf drainage) — new *Percina* sp., *P. nigrofasciata*, *P. sciera*
  - Atlantic drainages of Georgia/Carolinas — *Oligocephalus* group species
  - Santee, Altamaha, Savannah, Edisto systems — *E. thalassinum* group
  - Upper Tennessee River System — *E. swannanoa* (a more geographically and morphologically isolated/specialized population)
- Geographic range size appears linked to morphological variability (narrow-range endemics show low variation; wide-ranging species show high variation), suggesting a relationship between gene flow/habitat heterogeneity and phenotypic diversification.

## Key Findings

1. **New species description**: A previously unrecognized *[[Percina]]* species from the Alabama River System is formally described, diagnosed by small scales (LL scales 77–90) and unique coloration.
2. **Subgenus Hadropterus redescribed**: Nomenclatural history clarified; subgenus rediagnosed based on comparison of the new species with *P. nigrofasciata* and *P. sciera*.
3. **Species group cohesion in Oligocephalus**: *E. fricksium*, *E. mariae*, and *E. parvipinne* confirmed as a natural, monophyletic-type species group; *E. hopkinsi* resolved as a distinct, separable species despite prior taxonomic confusion.
4. **Variation correlates with range size**: Narrow endemics (*E. mariae*) show minimal variation; wide-ranging species (*E. parvipinne*) show substantial variation — a pattern relevant to darter [[Species concepts in Percidae]].
5. **Thalassinum species group established**: *E. thalassinum*, *E. inscriptum*, and *E. swannanoa* form a closely related triad, with *E. swannanoa* showing derived specializations (toothlessness on palatine, reduced prevomerine teeth, orange-red dorsal fin band) linking the group to the *E. blennioides* group.
6. **Sexual dimorphism limited**: In *E. inscriptum*, only fin ray meristics show significant sex-based differences, suggesting conservative external morphology between sexes in this species.
7. **Subgenus Etheostoma diagnosed**: Formal diagnosis provided along with discussion of internal species relationships.
