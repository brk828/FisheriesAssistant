# 5th Annual Meeting of the Society for Conservation Biology: Program and Abstracts (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the program and abstract booklet for the 5th Annual Meeting of the [[Society For Conservation Biology]], held 18-23 June 1991 at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. It schedules sessions, social events, logistics and roughly 186 numbered abstracts. Most abstracts cover terrestrial and invertebrate conservation, but a smaller group addresses fish and other aquatic organisms. The fish-related work centres on conservation genetics, including [[Virgin Chub]] genetic change after poisoning, [[Snake River Chinook Salmon]] monitoring, [[Columbia River Salmon And Steelhead]] risk assessment, and stock identification in [[Lake Trout]], [[Arctic Char]] and herring. The program also includes a small amount of aquatic ecology, such as [[Daphnia Magna]] stress studies and [[Cyanobacteria]] effects on zooplankton.

## Background & Description

The booklet is organised as follows:

- **Venue and sponsors:** Sessions were held in Psychology, Gordon Commons, Vilas Hall and Ogg Hall. Sponsors included the UW-Madison departments of Botany, Wildlife Ecology and Zoology, the [[University Of Wisconsin Arboretum]], the [[Sand County Foundation]] and the [[International Crane Foundation]].
- **Program co-chairs:** Stanley A. Temple, Timothy C. Moermond and Donald M. Waller.
- **Logistics:** Registration, housing in Ogg Hall, meal prices, parking ($4 per day), a smoking ban in university buildings, poster and mixer sessions, and Saturday field trips to the Arboretum and the [[Aldo Leopold Memorial Preserve]].
- **Parallel meetings:** Sessions of the [[Xerces Society]] ran alongside SCB sessions. Each society held its own business meeting on Friday afternoon.
- **Session structure:** Roughly 186 abstracts were grouped into themes such as invertebrate conservation, genetic diversity, population recovery, fragmentation, integrated conservation and development, and round-table discussions.
- **Banquet:** The keynote speaker was Dr. Curt Meine, biographer of [[Aldo Leopold]], who spoke on "Emergence of an Idea: Aldo Leopold and the Conservation of Biological Diversity."

The program lists 36 numbered poster abstracts and seven round-table topics. These are summarised rather than reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Several abstracts place conservation problems in an aquatic or fish context:

- **Freshwater biodiversity:** [[Stream And River Biodiversity]] is described as a "hidden" system that receives little public attention. The review estimates that roughly one-third of North American fish species and subspecies are endangered, threatened or of special concern. Most recorded extinctions over the past 100 years were linked to habitat loss, species introductions, chemical pollution and hybridization.
- **Wild lakes:** A Marinette County (Wisconsin) study examines the limnological characteristics and management of six designated Wild Lakes.
- **Stock identification:** Several studies use DNA markers to identify fish stocks and separate populations for management.
- **Hatchery effects:** Hatchery supplementation is a recurring concern. Studies examine whether outplanted hatchery fish affect wild populations and whether broodstock limitations reduce genetic diversity.
- **Zooplankton and cyanobacteria:** Bloom-forming [[Microcystis Aeruginosa]] reduces the intrinsic rate of increase of both [[Daphnia Pulex]] and the rotifer [[Keratella Cochlearis]].
- **Caribbean genetics:** A review of 3,827 electrophoresis papers found 147 on Caribbean populations. Reptiles and fish each made up 37% of these, and only 30% estimated within-population genetic variation.

## Key Findings

**Fish and aquatic genetics**

- **Virgin chub (Gila robusta seminuda):** An accidental poisoning produced extreme deviations from pre-poison genetic variation. A severe reduction in spawning adults caused a genetic bottleneck, and hatchery young were also genetically differentiated because few broodfish were used. Protein electrophoresis was judged effective for measuring perturbation impacts.
- **Snake River Chinook salmon:** Juvenile chinook were sampled from wild and natural populations and four hatchery supplementation programs over three years. Fluctuating asymmetry in meristic counts (fin rays and gill rakers) was used to detect reduced developmental stability. First-year data showed that morphological divergence was more uniform within drainages than between them, and some hatchery populations had more asymmetric characters than coincident wild populations.
- **Columbia River salmon and steelhead:** The basin holds more than 145 administratively recognised stocks of five species, overseen by more than 10 state and federal agencies and tribal groups. Four principal risks were identified: extinction, loss of within-population genetic variation, loss of between-population diversity, and inadvertent selection from domestication and harvest. A knowledge-based system and a sustainable production policy framework were proposed.
- **Lake trout and Arctic char:** Minisatellite DNA fingerprinting distinguished all populations tested. Ribosomal DNA differed among Arctic char populations but showed very few differences among lake trout populations.
- **Herring (Pacific and Atlantic):** Only one polymorphic rDNA site was found among five Pacific herring areas in British Columbia. Several differences separated them from Atlantic herring, which were estimated to have diverged about 3.5 million years ago.
- **Salmonid rDNA:** Restriction maps of 17 salmonid species (genera Hucho, Salvelinus, Salmo and Oncorhynchus) showed that transcribed spacers are most informative between species and the intergenic spacer is useful within species.
- **Steelhead and salmon genetics:** A qualitative risk assessment identified conservation opportunities for Columbia River stocks, but it relied heavily on variable-quality data and expert opinion.

**Aquatic invertebrates and plants**

- **Daphnia magna:** Selenate reduced survivorship, growth and lifetime reproduction. Fluctuating asymmetry did not vary predictably with toxin level, so the authors suggest it has limited value for monitoring environmental stress.
- **Plantago cordata:** A rare aquatic plant with 279 specimens examined and 10 populations surveyed electrophoretically. A survey of 30 loci showed 60% fixed heterozygosity, probably from allopolyploidy. North Carolina populations differed at 27% of loci.
- **Iris lacustris:** A federally listed threatened species showed sharp drought responses. Vegetative ramets on open sites declined by 46% and on densely shaded sites by 68%. Flower production in partially shaded sites recovered to 82% of pre-drought levels by 1990.

**Other conservation themes**

- **Biodiversity baselines:** Invertebrates make up 90-95% of all animals, yet few conservation databases include them. One abstract proposed intensive surveys of species-rich target clades.
- **Reserve design:** Models of 23 Indo-Pacific countries found that China, Indonesia, India and Thailand held 80% of large reserves (>1,000 km²). Sierra de Manantlan was reported to contain 2,200 plant species and 563 vertebrate species.
- **Imperiled species:** The Uncompahgre fritillary was described as probably North America's rarest butterfly, with two colonies above 4,000 m. The Nature Conservancy's monitoring goal covers 1,333 plant and animal species with fewer than 20 known occurrences.
- **Genetics and captive breeding:** Sperm banks and captive propagation were evaluated as tools for maintaining genetic variation, with simulation results for gaur and nene studies.
- **Policy and funding:** Abstracts discussed a proposed National Institutes for the Environment, student chapters of the SCB, and whether the SCB should take advocacy positions.

[[Biodiversity Conservation]] and [[Conservation Genetics]] are the major cross-cutting themes of the meeting.
