# Trip Report: July 1994 Survey of the ASU Research Park

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #freshwater-fish-survey

## Executive Summary
This report documents a fisheries survey conducted by [[Arizona State University]] personnel on 08–09 July 1994 at the [[ASU Research Park]] ponds in Tempe, Arizona. The survey employed entanglement nets (trammel and experimental gill nets) across three ponds to assess the status of a multi-species fish assemblage, including two federally listed [[Colorado River Basin]] native fishes: the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. A total of 102 individuals representing 10 species were captured, all previously recorded in past park surveys, and all were released unharmed. The survey provides valuable recapture data on a PIT-tagged bonytail chub stocked in 1992, tracking individual growth over a two-year period, and morphometric data on three razorback suckers. No [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Ptychocheilus lucius) were captured, attributed to limited sampling effort. A follow-up survey was tentatively planned for autumn 1994.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Artificial/impounded lentic ponds within the [[ASU Research Park]] complex, consisting of three interconnected lakes (Lake A and others) used as a refugium/holding site for native and sport fish species.
- **Community structure:** A mixed assemblage of introduced sport fishes (Centrarchidae, Ictaluridae, Moronidae) and non-native cyprinids (carp, grass carp, threadfin shad) coexisting with reintroduced/stocked native [[Colorado River Basin]] endemics ([[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) in a controlled, human-managed setting.
- **Conservation role:** The park functions as an off-river refuge population site for endangered Colorado River native fishes, allowing monitoring of survival, growth, and health status of stocked individuals over time (as demonstrated by the bonytail recapture spanning two years).
- **Sampling gear/microhabitat use:** Entanglement nets (125 ft experimental gill nets and 150–300 ft trammel nets) were set overnight (1930–2130 to 0515–0800), targeting benthic and midwater habitats across differing pond sections; effort was measured in net area (m²) per 12-hr set for CPE standardization.
- **Absence of top predator:** [[Colorado Squawfish]] were not detected, suggesting either low population density within the park, insufficient sampling effort, or habitat/prey limitations affecting this piscivorous native species.

## Key Findings
- 102 individual fish across 10 species were captured over a single overnight sampling event using entanglement nets in three park ponds.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** and **[[Bluegill]]** together comprised 81% of total catch, indicating a heavily sport-fish-dominated community structure.
- The recaptured **[[Bonytail Chub]]** individual demonstrated measurable growth (403→427 mm TL) over a 2-year period since AZGFD stocking in July 1992, confirming successful long-term survival of hatchery-origin native fish in park ponds.
- This bonytail was traced to a specific hatchery lineage (F1, [[Bubbling Ponds National Fish Hatchery]]) and translocation history (originally from [[Hassayampa River Preserve]]'s Palm Lake, Wickenburg, AZ, via [[The Nature Conservancy]]).
- All three captured **[[Razorback Sucker]]** individuals were adult-sized (512–528 mm TL) and in good health, though none were in reproductive condition at time of sampling.
- No parasites, tumors, or major scarring were noted in the razorback suckers; the bonytail showed minor parasite load (Lernaea) and healed scarring, consistent with generally good health status for these endangered species within the refuge population.
- Absence of **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** in the catch was attributed to limited sampling intensity rather than confirmed absence from the system.
- All captured fish, including the two federally protected native species, were released unharmed, consistent with conservation-oriented survey protocols.
- A subsequent survey was tentatively planned for autumn 1994 to continue monitoring population status trends at this site.
