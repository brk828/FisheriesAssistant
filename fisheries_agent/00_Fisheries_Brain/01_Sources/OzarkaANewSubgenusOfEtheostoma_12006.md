# Ozarka, A New Subgenus of Etheostoma (Pisces: Percidae)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae #Etheostoma #Darters

## Executive Summary

Williams & Robison (1980) erect **Ozarka**, a new subgenus of the darter genus [[Etheostoma]] (family [[Percidae]]), to unite five morphologically and ecologically similar species: *[[Etheostoma punctulatum]]* (type species), *[[Etheostoma cragini]]*, *[[Etheostoma pallididorsum]]*, *[[Etheostoma boschungi]]*, and *[[Etheostoma trisella]]*. Four of these species were previously classified within the subgenus [[Oligocephalus]], while *E. trisella* was placed in [[Psychromaster]]. The new subgenus is diagnosed on shared breeding coloration, nuptial tubercle patterns, and a distinctive shared spawning habitat: small spring-fed rivulets and seepage areas. Geographically, the group spans from the Arkansas River drainage in Colorado to the upper Coosa/Conasauga River drainage in northwest Georgia and southeast Tennessee, with the Ozark Mountains Physiographic Province hypothesized as the center of dispersal — hence the name "Ozarka."

## Background & Description

### Taxonomic History
- Darter genera were reduced to four by Bailey (1951): *Ammocrypta*, *Etheostoma*, *Hadropterus*, *Percina*; later reduced to three (Bailey in Bailey et al. 1954).
- Bailey & Gosline (1955) placed 70 *Etheostoma* species into 12 subgenera based on vertebral counts.
- Collette & Yerger (1962) and Collette (1965) added subgenus [[Villora]] based on breeding tubercle distribution.
- Collette & Banarescu (1977) and Page (1977) added [[Doration]], [[Litocara]], and [[Vaillantia]], bringing the total to sixteen subgenera prior to this work.
- [[Oligocephalus]] was previously the largest, most structurally diverse and speciose subgenus (19–21 species per various authors).
- This paper removes *E. punctulatum*, *E. cragini*, *E. pallididorsum*, and *E. boschungi* from *Oligocephalus*, and *E. trisella* from *Psychromaster*, consolidating them into the new subgenus **Ozarka**.
- Close relationship among *E. punctulatum*, *E. cragini*, and *E. pallididorsum* was first noted by Blair (1964); Wall & Williams (1974) described *E. boschungi* and clarified relationships among the four western species.

### Diagnostic Characters of Subgenus Ozarka
- **Size:** Adults 40–70 mm standard length (SL).
- **Scalation:** Cheeks/opercles usually naked or with embedded scales (except *E. trisella*, which has scales). Lateral line incomplete (complete only in *E. trisella*); lateral line scale rows 40–80; transverse scale rows 12–24; caudal peduncle scales 20–29.
- **Branchiostegals:** Narrowly joined to overlapping membranes; rays usually 6-6, unscaled.
- **Frenum:** Present, broad, well developed.
- **Pore counts:** Preoperculomandibular canal usually 10 pores; infraorbital canal complete with 7–8 pores; supratemporal canal complete or interrupted; preopercle entire.
- **Vertebrae:** 32–39 (usually 34–37); caudal vertebrae usually 18–20; first interneural spine positioned between neural spines of 3rd/4th or 4th/5th vertebrae.
- **Fin meristics:** Dorsal spines VI–XII (usually IX–XI); dorsal rays 10–15 (usually 11–14); pectoral rays 9–14 (usually 11–13); anal spines 2 (usually 1 in *E. trisella*); anal rays 6–10 (usually 7–9); branched caudal rays 12–17.
- **Nuptial tubercles:** Present only in males — on belly scales, around anal fin base to caudal peduncle, on anal spines/rays, and ventral surfaces of pelvic rays; absent in females.
- **Sexual dichromatism:** Pronounced — breeding males brightly colored, females not.

### Breeding Coloration (Males)
- Bold blue-black subocular bar (width 1/3–2/3 orbit diameter).
- Spinous dorsal fin: black marginal band (narrow anteriorly, widening posteriorly), submarginal orange to red-orange band, and blue-green to blue-black basal band of roughly equal width to submarginal band.
- Soft dorsal fin lacks bright breeding colors in all species.
- Venter (from pectoral/pelvic insertions to caudal peduncle) suffused with orange to red-orange pigment.
- Branchiostegal and gular region orange (except in *E. trisella*).
- Genital papilla tubular in females; not elongate/tubular in males; anus lacks fleshy villi.

### Additional Morphological Notes
- Body slender to moderately stout; snout moderately to slightly decurved; mouth terminal, slightly oblique.
- Well-developed humeral spot in all species except *E. trisella* (indistinct).
- Dorsal saddles 3–9, conspicuous and dark.
- Soft dorsal fin with 6–8 indistinct horizontal bands from pigmented rays/membranes (barred appearance).
- Caudal fin spotting confined to rays in 4–6 irregular vertical rows.
- Females: olivaceous/brownish-gray, lacking bright orange coloration; spinous dorsal occasionally tinged orange/yellow but never boldly banded.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Geographic Distribution
The subgenus range is centered in the **Ozark Mountains Physiographic Region**, extending from eastern Colorado (*E. cragini*) to southeastern Tennessee/northwestern Georgia (*E. trisella*). Species are largely allopatric, with the exception of sympatry between *E. punctulatum* and *E. cragini* in southwestern Missouri and northeastern Oklahoma.

- **West of Mississippi Embayment:** *E. punctulatum*, *E. cragini*, *E. pallididorsum*
- **East of Mississippi Embayment:** *E. boschungi*, *E. trisella*
- None of the five species occurs on the Coastal Plain Province.

### Habitat Associations
- **Ozarka (general):** Gentle riffles and slackwater of small-to-medium shallow upland tributary streams.
- **E. punctulatum:** Small, clear, moderate-to-high gradient permanent streams/spring branches with gravel or rubble substrate; also in vegetated quiet backwaters, heavily vegetated low-gradient springs.
- **E. cragini:** Quiet pools of smallest spring branches; margins of pools/riffles in dense watercress (*Nasturtium officinale*); also clear streams with mud/gravel/sand substrate and aquatic vegetation (*Ranunculus*, *Potamogeton*, *Myriophyllum*, *Callitriche*, *Radicula*).
- **E. pallididorsum:** Small spring-fed brooks (~0.6 m wide, 5 cm deep) over mud/gravel/rubble; shallow (15–30 cm) backwater pools with leaf litter in larger streams.
- **E. boschungi:** Clear, medium-current 2nd–3rd order streams (3–6 m wide, <15 cm to 1.7 m deep); gravel infiltrated with silt, or silt/mud substrates with detritus accumulation in low-velocity areas — avoids clean gravel.
- **E. trisella:** Hypothesized spring-dweller; holotype from small sluggish pasture stream (silt/sand/gravel, dense emergent *Dianthera*); also riffles and stagnant backwaters of small low-gradient streams (Etnier 1970).

### Spawning Ecology
A shared, distinctive spawning strategy supports the phylogenetic unity of Ozarka:
- All species enter small tributary streams in late winter and spawn in early spring (**March–April**).
- *E. pallididorsum*, *E. boschungi*, and *E. trisella* specifically spawn in **tiny spring-fed rivulets or seepage water in open fields** draining into nearby streams.
- Direct spawning observations for *E. punctulatum* are lacking, though nuptial males have been documented in spring.

## Key Findings

1. **New Subgenus Established:** *Ozarka* is diagnosed to contain five species previously split between *Oligocephalus* and *Psychromaster*, based on shared breeding tubercle patterns, coloration, and spawning habitat.
2. **Type Species:** *[[Etheostoma punctulatum]]* (Agassiz, 1854), originally described as *Poecilichthys punctulatus*, type locality Osage River, Missouri.
3. **Etymology:** Subgenus name derives from the **Ozark Mountains Physiographic Province**, proposed by Dr. George A. Moore as the hypothesized center of dispersal for the group.
4. **Distinguishing Trait — E. trisella:** Uniquely differs from other Ozarka members by having a complete lateral line, scaled cheeks/opercles, usually 1 anal spine (vs. 2), and lacking orange gular/branchiostegal pigmentation.
5. **Biogeographic Split:** The Mississippi Embayment serves as a natural barrier separating western species (*E. punctulatum*, *E. cragini*, *E. pallididorsum*) from eastern species (*E. boschungi*, *E. trisella*).
6. **Conservation Signal:** *E. trisella* has the most restricted distribution in the subgenus, with the only known extant population in the Conasauga River near the Tennessee–Georgia border — indicating high vulnerability.
7. **Diagnostic Key:** A dichotomous key is provided distinguishing the five species primarily via anal spine count, lateral line scale counts/completeness, dorsal fin ray counts, and presence/absence of a pale mid-dorsal stripe.
8. **Future Work Flagged:** The authors note a more detailed systematic analysis of Ozarka is forthcoming (Williams, Robison, and B.R. Wall), along with individual life-history studies on *E. pallididorsum* (Robison), *E. boschungi* (Boschung), and *E. trisella* (Ryon).
