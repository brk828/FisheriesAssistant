# Little Snake Water Rights, Section 7 Depletion Offsets, and Colorado River Storage Project Policy (1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a compilation of 1989 materials from the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for [[Colorado River Endangered Fishes]] in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It contains a cover letter, a draft proposal to evaluate [[Little Snake River]] water rights, a [[Section 7 Consultation]] depletion table, a policy paper on how water depletions are offset, and a statement from Upper Division States and power users on amending the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] Act. The central theme is securing [[Instream Flow Water Rights]] for endangered fish through acquisition of conditional water rights, while balancing water development with fish protection. The document names no fish species.

## Background & Description

**Cover letter (April 6, 1989).** The author sends the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Recovery Program director a proposal to evaluate Little Snake water rights that may come on the market. The letter argues it would be imprudent to assume away upstream senior filings. It supports River District and Wyoming funding only if work products arrive soon, and it opposes open-ended acquisition before basic yield and market-value data exist.

**Draft proposal.** In October 1988, the Management Group formed an ad hoc subcommittee to scope water-right surveys on the Upper Yampa, Little Snake, and White rivers and the [[15 Mile Reach]]. On February 21, 1989, the [[Implementation Committee]] approved up to $120,000 in [[Windy Gap Funds]] for the Bureau's 15 Mile Reach survey and the Little Snake evaluation, both subject to Management Group approval. The proposal covers three conditional water-right packages:

- [[Powder Wash Water Rights]] and [[Pot Hook Water Rights]], owned by the [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]] (River District)
- [[Three Forks Water Rights]], owned by the [[Pittsburg and Midway Coal Mining Company]] (P&M)

The stated goal is to reconnoiter the transferable yield of these rights for instream purposes and to preliminarily estimate their market value, in parallel with defining flow needs on the Lower Yampa River.

**Policy paper on Section 7 depletions.** The paper summarizes more than 100 [[Biological Opinion]]s issued since 1978 under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It quotes the [[Recovery Program]] on protecting habitat through water rights, the $10/acre-foot contribution, and funding and bonding rules.

**Statement of the Representatives (March 29, 1989).** Representatives of the Upper Division States and the [[Colorado River Energy Distributors Association]] outline proposed principles for amending the 1956 CRSP Act. No formal agreement had been reached.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Depletion definition.** The policy paper defines depletion to include reduced flow and changes in water quality (temperature, turbidity, salinity) and sediment transport (channel equilibrium, deposition).
- **Habitat concern.** The Service attributes the jeopardy finding for the fishes to habitat loss, other factors, and declining fish numbers. The Recovery Program states that protecting and managing enough habitat to support self-sustaining populations is necessary.
- **Nursery habitat and channel change.** Long-term monitoring of nursery habitats and channel changes on the [[Green River]] was under discussion. A [[Sediment Study]] on the Little Snake and Yampa rivers was in progress.
- **Upstream development.** The Little Snake evaluation models upstream projects (Cheyenne, [[Sandstone Reservoir]], Pot Hook, and Three Forks) to show how they would reduce downstream transferable yield, and it estimates consumptive use in Wyoming and Colorado for [[Interstate Compact Entitlements]].

## Key Findings

**1. Little Snake water-rights study (proposed scope)**
- Task A (Powder Wash yield at the mouth of the Little Snake under existing conditions, no upstream development, and a downstream point of diversion): 45 days.
- Task B (monthly operation studies of upstream senior filings, individually and combined): 120 days.
- Task C (preliminary fair market value appraisal under federal appraisal standards, net of development costs): 90 days.
- Total scheduled duration is 255 days. The appraisal would consider the highest and best use of each right. If the best use is instream protection, it would compare sales for instream use or the cost of a new instream right plus legal actions limiting upstream senior rights.

**2. Funding and status**
- Up to $120,000 in Windy Gap funds was approved for the 15 Mile Reach survey and the Little Snake evaluation.
- Progress reported in the policy paper includes: negotiations on Little Snake water rights and a possible option to buy from the River District (in progress); evaluation of Little Snake rights (in progress); [[Juniper-Cross Mountain Project]] negotiations (in progress); and Yampa Phase 1 instream flow report (near completion).
- Congress had appropriated $1M to acquire water rights. The FY-89 work plan totaled $2.3M for 39 high-priority projects.

**3. Section 7 depletion offsets**
- Each project pays a one-time contribution of $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion, adjusted annually for inflation. Funds are split 50-50 between recovery activities and instream flow acquisition, unless the Implementation Committee directs otherwise.
- Depletions above 3,000 acre-feet per year are "large" and require a sufficient-progress determination. Smaller depletions may be treated as large based on location and timing.
- Contributions are due in stages: 10% at the initiating federal action (authorization, funding, NEPA completion, or a 404 permit), and the balance through bonding or contract at construction start. Failure to pay voids the biological opinion and permit.
- Credit for water conservation by existing projects is capped at the amount of water conserved.

**4. Section 7 consultation table (April 7, 1989)**
- The table lists 11 finalized consultations, including Colowyo Coal (127 acre-feet), Trapper Mine (122.8), James Creek (400), and Soda Unit Natural Gas (102).
- Nine consultations were pending. The largest were Sandstone Reservoir (30,600 acre-feet, $306,000) and Muddy Creek (10,000 acre-feet, $100,000). The Price-San Rafael project ranged from 17,917 to 39,668 acre-feet, with a Bureau share of (2,673).

**5. Upper Colorado River Basin recovery progress**
- Implementation Committee accepted the Service's July–September flow recommendations for the 15 Mile Reach (near completion). Its concurrence with the October–June work plan is complete.
- A Bureau agreement with Grand Valley water users to deliver [[Ruedi Reservoir]] releases to the head of the 15 Mile Reach is near completion.
- The Colorado Water Conservation Board's approval to file for a protective water right on Ruedi releases is in progress, and approval for a 15 Mile Reach instream right for July–September is under discussion.
- Pending consultation on [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] reoperation is in progress.

**6. CRSP amendment principles (March 29, 1989; not formally agreed)**
- The Upper Division States would have access to about $35 million per year of power revenues over 40 years (about $2.2 billion total), indexed at 4% per year for 15 years, placed in state escrow accounts.
- A new environmental mitigation fund would receive about $6 million per year from the states and power users (about $365 million over 40 years).
- Access to escrow funds would require cost sharing: 20% for Utah's Bonneville Unit irrigation and drainage system, and 30% for other projects in Utah, Colorado, New Mexico, and Wyoming. States must also contribute to the mitigation fund.
- Authorized CRSP projects not in the Bureau's construction budget would be scheduled for deauthorization.
