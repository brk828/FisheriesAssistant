# Autodiastylic Suspensoriums in Paleozoic Chondrichthyes & Biogeography/Dispersal of Gambusia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Chondrichthyes #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This note synthesizes three related abstracts from a systematics/evolutionary biology conference. The first (Lund & Grogan, Abstract 220) presents paleontological evidence for **autodiastyly**, an intermediate jaw suspension condition in Carboniferous [[Paraselachii]], clarifying the evolutionary relationship between [[Elasmobranchii]] and [[Holocephali]]. The second and third abstracts (Lydeard, Abstract 221; Lynch, Abstract 222) address the historical and modern biogeography of the freshwater fish genus [[Gambusia]] (family [[Poeciliidae]]), using cladistic and dispersal-record analyses to reconstruct both deep vicariance history in the Caribbean Basin and recent human-mediated (anthropochore) range expansions of *Gambusia affinis* and *Gambusia holbrooki*.

## Background & Description

### Paleozoic Chondrichthyan Jaw Suspension (Abstract 220)
- Modern chondrichthyans are divided into two subclasses with fundamentally different jaw suspensorium and neurocranial architecture:
  - **[[Elasmobranchii]]**: hyostylic or amphistylic suspensorium (palatoquadrate linked to neurocranium via the hyomandibula).
  - **[[Holocephali]]** (e.g., Chimaeriformes): **holostyly**, complete fusion of the palatoquadrate to the neurocranium.
- **Hyostyly** is considered the plesiomorphic (ancestral) condition for gnathostomes generally, including basal Chondrichthyes.
- **Autodiastyly** — palatoquadrate suspended from the neurocranium *without* hyomandibular involvement — was originally proposed as a theoretical intermediate condition by De Beer and Moy-Thomas but had never been empirically demonstrated until this study.
- Lund and Grogan demonstrate autodiastyly directly in several Carboniferous **[[Paraselachii]]**, alongside other diagnostic cranial and postcranial characters distinguishing this group from Elasmobranchii.

### Gambusia Biogeography and Dispersal (Abstracts 221–222)
- **Genus [[Gambusia]]** (family [[Poeciliidae]]) is used as a model taxon to test vicariance biogeography hypotheses for the Caribbean Basin.
- Lydeard's cladistic analysis combines morphological data and mitochondrial **cytochrome b** sequence data across *Gambusia* species to build an area cladogram.
- Lynch's study documents the well-recorded history (1905–1975) of human-assisted ("anthropochore") dispersal of *Gambusia affinis* and *G. holbrooki*, two of the most widely introduced fish species globally, used extensively for mosquito control.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Paraselachii** inhabited Carboniferous marine/epicontinental environments; their cranial anatomy provides a critical window into early chondrichthyan diversification and the split leading to modern sharks/rays versus chimaeras.
- **Gambusia** species are small freshwater/euryhaline poeciliid fishes native to the southeastern United States, Mexico, Central America, and the Greater Antilles, occupying streams, ponds, and wetlands.
- Their broad tolerance and reproductive strategy (livebearing) have made *G. affinis* and *G. holbrooki* effective and prolific colonizers when introduced outside their native ranges, primarily for mosquito abatement programs.
- Historical introductions have blurred the boundary between native ("aboriginal") and introduced distributions, complicating both taxonomic and biogeographic inference (e.g., apparent hybridization zones in eastern Alabama/western Georgia may be artifacts of overlapping introductions rather than natural intergradation).

## Key Findings

### Paleozoic Chondrichthyes (Lund & Grogan)
- **Autodiastyly** is empirically demonstrated for the first time in Carboniferous **Paraselachii**, confirming a long-hypothesized intermediate jaw-suspension condition between hyostyly (Elasmobranchii) and holostyly (Holocephali).
- Cranial and postcranial characters support treating **Paraselachii** as the **sister group to Holocephali**, together forming a clade termed **Holocephalimorpha**.
- **Holocephalimorpha** (Paraselachii + Holocephali) is proposed as the sister group to **Elasmobranchii**, revising the higher-level phylogeny of Chondrichthyes.

### Caribbean Biogeography of Gambusia (Lydeard)
- Cytochrome b and morphological cladistic analysis yields the area cladogram: **(North America (Mexico (Nuclear Central America, Greater Antilles)))**.
- This result **contradicts** a previously proposed vicariance model in which the Greater Antilles was hypothesized to have separated first from mainland areas.
- Mainland areas (North America, Mexico, Nuclear Central America) show high biogeographic concordance, suggesting a **shared geological history**: (North America (Mexico, Nuclear Central America)).
- A **revised vicariance model** for the Caribbean Basin is proposed, pending further testing with additional area cladograms from other taxa.

### Anthropochore Dispersal of Gambusia affinis/holbrooki (Lynch)
- Despite natural swimming-based dispersal capability, human-mediated translocation (1905–1975) is the primary driver of the current wide distribution of *G. affinis* and *G. holbrooki*.
- **Five or six source populations** (Georgia, Illinois, Tennessee, Texas) account for most introductions worldwide.
- Within the U.S., **Illinois, Tennessee, and Texas** source stocks were used to establish *G. affinis* across the western half of the country.
- Pre-1900 museum and published records indicate the **native ("aboriginal") distributions** of both species were substantially **smaller** than ranges reported in post-1925 literature.
- *G. holbrooki*'s western range limit had already been artificially extended by introductions **as early as 1920**.
- Overlapping introductions of both species into **eastern Alabama and western Georgia** (~1920–1922) likely produced zones of **apparent hybridization/intergradation** that are actually artifacts of anthropogenic mixing rather than natural secondary contact.
