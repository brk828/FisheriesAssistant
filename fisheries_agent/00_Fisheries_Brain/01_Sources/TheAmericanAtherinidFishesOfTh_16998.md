# The American Atherinid Fishes of the Genus Coleotropis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1967 taxonomic revision by [[Carter R. Gilbert]] and [[David K. Caldwell]], published in the [[Los Angeles County Museum of Natural History]] *Contributions in Science* (No. 125), reviews the atherinid genus *Coleotropis*. The genus contains two valid species: *C. starksi* in the eastern Pacific and *C. blackburni* in the western Atlantic. A third proposed species, *C. colecanos* from Tortuguero, Costa Rica, is synonymized with *C. blackburni*. The paper also documents a biogeographic break in the Caribbean near the [[Gulf of Uraba]].

## Background & Description

The genus was erected by [[Myers and Wade]] (1942) for *C. starksi*, originally described by [[Meek and Hildebrand]] (1923) as *Menidia starksi* from Panama Bay. [[Schultz]] (1949) described *C. blackburni*, and [[Caldwell]] (1962) described *C. colecanos*. Schultz (1948) placed the genus in the subfamily Menidiinae, with closest relatives *Eurystole* and *Nectarges*.

Diagnostic generic features include:
- Compressed, elongate body with an air bladder that does not taper posteriorly or extend into five or more haemal arches.
- A single anal spine and a scale sheath at the anal fin base.
- Cycloid scales, with a conspicuous silvery lateral band bordered by a dark line.
- Forked caudal fin, with III or IV first dorsal spines.
- Pelvic fins completely joined by a membrane.
- Vertebral counts of 40 to 42 and 34 total caudal rays (main rays 9+8=17).

Methods used [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1958) standard measurements, expressed in thousandths of standard length (SL). Specimens were drawn from multiple institutions, including the [[Field Museum of Natural History]], [[Florida State Museum]], [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], and [[United States National Museum]]. Over 100 specimens were collected at Tortuguero, Costa Rica, in 1963.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*C. blackburni* from Tortuguero occurred only in the surge zone, in water 1 to 36 inches deep. It was never recorded in brackish or fresh water and did not co-occur with *Thyrinops chagresi*, the other atherinid in that area. Recent *C. starksi* collections from Costa Rica indicate similar ecological conditions.

The authors describe a faunal break centred on the Gulf of Uraba, which separates South and Central America. Preliminary observations from the R/V *Pillsbury* suggest:
- The eastern gulf receives heavy silt from the [[Rio Atrato]], favouring sciaenids and clupeids that inhabit turbid, silt-bottomed inshore waters.
- The western shore and Panamanian Caribbean coast are largely silt-free, with coral formations and an impoverished insular reef fauna.

Sciaenid genera *Umbrina* and *Ophioscion* show a corresponding distributional break, although *Coleotropis* populations on either side appear indistinguishable.

## Key Findings

**Species status**
- *C. colecanos* is synonymized with *C. blackburni* because its distinguishing traits (caudal peduncle length and body depth) overlap completely with the range for *C. blackburni*, and no meristic differences were found.
- Holotype of *C. colecanos* (UF 5652, 76 mm SL) gave caudal peduncle depth of 88 and length of 205 (original description: 84 and 236).

**Diagnostic differences between species**

| Character | *C. starksi* | *C. blackburni* |
|---|---|---|
| Predorsal scales | 18–19 | 23–28 |
| Lateral scales | 37–39 | 43–50 |
| Caudal-peduncle scales | usually 12 | usually 16 |
| Anal fin rays | 25–33 | 20–26 |
| Maxillary extent | beneath anterior orbit | just in front of orbit |
| Anal sheath rows anteriorly | two | one |
| Pectoral fin | slightly longer than head | shorter than head |
| Lower jaw pigmentation | less than half | nearly all |
| Largest specimen | 111 mm SL (of 115) | 92 mm SL (of 124) |

**Range**
- *C. starksi*: Gulf of Panama to northern Costa Rica.
- *C. blackburni*: Caribbean coasts of Costa Rica and Venezuela, east to the Atlantic coast of central Brazil.

**Taxonomic and biogeographic conclusions**
- The authors assert that *Coleotropis* is not closely related to *Menidia* and that *Menidia brasiliensis* is only distantly related.
- Further collecting is expected to extend the known ranges of both species.
