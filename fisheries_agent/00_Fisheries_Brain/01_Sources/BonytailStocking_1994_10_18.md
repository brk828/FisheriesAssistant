## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum reporting a **1994 stocking event** of hatchery-reared [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), an endangered cyprinid native to the Colorado River Basin. Fish produced from 1994 broodstock recruitment at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] were transported and released into three distinct sites near the Lower Colorado River: an isolated levee pond at [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]], a golf course pond at [[Emerald Canyon Golf Course]], and the Office Cove adjacent to the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]]. A total of **57,400 fish** were released across all sites, with biometric data (length and mass) collected from subsamples, and tissue samples reserved for genetic (allozyme) analysis — activities consistent with ongoing [[Endangered Species Recovery Program|Bonytail Recovery Program]] efforts.

## Background & Description

- **Total Length (TL)**: Measured from a subsample of 100 fish.
  - Range: **47–130 mm**
  - Mean: **91 mm**
- **Body Mass**: Measured from a subsample of 25 fish.
  - Mean mass: **6.4 g**
- Fish represent **young-of-year (1994 cohort)** recruitment produced in broodstock ponds at the Dexter hatchery, indicating a wide size range typical of pond-reared cohorts with staggered spawning/growth.
- 25 individuals were **euthanized and frozen for allozyme (genetic) analysis**, suggesting concurrent monitoring of genetic diversity/stock identity in the [[Bonytail]] captive broodstock program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species context**: [[Bonytail]] is a large-river cyprinid historically endemic to the mainstem [[Colorado River]] and its major tributaries; it is federally listed as endangered and has been the subject of intensive captive propagation and reintroduction efforts due to near-extirpation in the wild.
- **Release habitats**:
  - **High levee pond, Cibola NWR** — an *isolated* off-channel facility, likely selected to protect stocked fish from predatory nonnative fishes common in the mainstem river (e.g., sportfish species).
  - **Emerald Canyon Golf Course pond** — an artificial, human-made static water body used as a refugial stocking site.
  - **Office Cove, Bill Williams River NWR** — a backwater/cove habitat directly connected to the Lower Colorado River system, representing a more naturalistic riverine release environment.
- **Habitat strategy**: The use of multiple release site types (isolated ponds vs. connected river cove) reflects a common recovery strategy of balancing predator-free grow-out habitat against reintroduction into natural riverine environments to reestablish self-sustaining populations.
- **Collaborative management**: Stocking involved coordination among USFWS Parker Fish and Wildlife Restoration Office (FRO), Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center, refuge staff (Cibola NWR, Bill Williams River NWR), and the [[Bureau of Land Management]], illustrating multi-agency involvement typical of [[Endangered Species Recovery Program|Colorado River native fish recovery efforts]].

## Key Findings

- **57,400 total bonytail** were stocked in a single day (18 October 1994) across three Lower Colorado River-area sites:
  - 28,800 fish → Cibola NWR high levee pond (isolated)
  - 3,025 fish → Emerald Canyon Golf Course pond #1
  - 25,575 fish → Office Cove, Bill Williams River NWR
- Stocked fish were **hatchery-origin, 1994 year-class** individuals produced via natural recruitment in broodstock ponds at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], rather than fish from targeted spawning protocols.
- Biometric sampling (length: 47–130 mm, mean 91 mm; mass: mean 6.4 g) provides a size baseline for evaluating post-stocking growth and survival in future monitoring.
- Genetic sampling (n=25, frozen for allozyme analysis) indicates concurrent efforts to assess **genetic diversity and stock composition** within the captive-bred population, an important consideration for maintaining genetic health in endangered species reintroduction programs.
- The operation exemplifies routine **interagency execution of an endangered species augmentation/reintroduction stocking event**, involving hatchery transport logistics, refuge-level field coordination, and basic biological data collection standard for [[Bonytail]] recovery monitoring in the [[Lower Colorado River]] region.
