## Executive Summary
This document is a formal advocacy letter, dated July 19, 1985, from E. P. Pister, Executive Secretary of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], addressed to the Regional Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in Albuquerque, New Mexico. The letter is not a primary research paper but rather a policy/conservation communication supporting the listing of three imperiled desert fish species under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. It calls for both formal listing and the designation of critical habitat, reflecting broader concerns about the biological integrity of aquatic ecosystems in arid regions of North America.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source. The letter identifies the following taxa by common and scientific name only, without physical descriptions:

- **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*)

All three species belong to the minnow family (historically Cyprinidae, now largely reclassified under **[[Leuciscidae]]**), a group characterized generally by small body size, cycloid scales, and adaptations to variable, often harsh, freshwater flow regimes — though these specifics are not detailed in the source text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- All three species are native to **desert stream and river systems** of the American Southwest, habitats characterized by intermittent flow, high thermal variability, and low overall discharge.
- The letter frames these species within the context of maintaining **"biological integrity throughout the desert areas of North America,"** implying their roles as indicator species for freshwater desert ecosystem health.
- **[[Spikedace]]** and **[[Loach Minnow]]** are historically associated with the **Gila River basin** (Arizona/New Mexico), a system noted for high endemism and vulnerability to habitat alteration, water diversion, and introduced species.
- **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** is endemic to the **Little Colorado River drainage** in Arizona, another desert system heavily impacted by anthropogenic water use.
- No specific data on diet, trophic position, reproductive behavior, or interspecific interactions are included in this letter.

## Key Findings
- The **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** formally supported federal Endangered Species Act (1973, as amended) listing for three southwestern desert fish species: *Lepidomeda vittata*, *Tiaroga cobitis*, and *Meda fulgida*.
- The organization explicitly requested **designation of critical habitat** in addition to species listing, indicating recognition that habitat loss/degradation was a primary threat driving decline.
- This letter represents a historical conservation advocacy document illustrating the political and scientific process leading toward the eventual federal protections these species received (all three were subsequently listed under the ESA).
- The document underscores the **Desert Fishes Council's** role as a stakeholder organization advocating for desert aquatic biodiversity conservation in the 1980s, situating these three species within a larger pattern of imperilment among North American desert fishes.
- No quantitative population, distribution, or threat-assessment data are presented; the letter should be treated as a **conservation policy artifact** rather than a primary data source.
