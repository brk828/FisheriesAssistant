# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Minutes Transmittal Letter (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes-recovery

## Executive Summary

This 20 April 1992 letter from [[W. L. Minckley]], Team Leader of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], transmits the minutes of the team's 5-6 February 1992 meeting in Phoenix, Arizona, to [[Michael Spear]], Regional Director of the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Region II). The team unanimously recommends distributing the minutes to about 23 government and non-governmental offices involved in managing, researching, or otherwise working on the desert fishes. The letter asks the Regional Director to approve this action. If he does, Team Coordinator [[Sally Stefferud]] has agreed to draft the transmittal letter and handle distribution.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Cover letter (correspondence), not a research paper. It contains no species data, survey results, or ecological measurements.
- **Originating institution:** [[Arizona State University]], Department of Zoology, Tempe, Arizona.
- **Sender:** W. L. Minckley, Team Leader and Professor of Zoology.
- **Recipient:** Michael Spear, Regional Director, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region II, Albuquerque, New Mexico.
- **Enclosure:** Detailed minutes of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting, 5-6 February 1992, Phoenix.
- **Subject species:** Not named in the letter. The team's focus, the "desert fishes," is implied by its name and by the letter's references to "the fishes."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter does not describe habitats, water chemistry, population status, threats, or specific fish taxa. Its ecological relevance is indirect: the recovery team works on desert fishes, and the distribution list shows the range of agencies with management or research responsibilities for them. These include federal land managers, water agencies, state wildlife departments, and conservation organizations in Arizona, New Mexico, and California, plus one Mexican organization, the [[Centro Ecologico De Sonora]]. This pattern suggests a multi-jurisdictional, cross-border approach to desert fish recovery, but the letter does not state this.

## Key Findings

**1. Recommended distribution of meeting minutes**
- The Team unanimously recommends distributing the minutes to other agencies and organizations working on the fishes.
- The suggested list includes about 23 offices and units, grouped as follows:
  - **[[U.S. Bureau Of Land Management]]:** Arizona State Office, Safford District Office, Phoenix District Office, New Mexico State Office, Las Cruces District Office (5 offices).
  - **[[U.S. Forest Service]]:** Albuquerque Regional Office, Tonto, Apache-Sitgreaves, Gila, and Coronado National Forests (5 units).
  - **[[U.S. Bureau Of Reclamation]]:** Boulder City Regional Office and Arizona Projects Office (2 offices).
  - **State wildlife and fish agencies:** [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], California Department of Fish and Game, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (3 agencies).
  - **Conservation organizations:** Centro Ecologico de Sonora and the Arizona Chapter of The Nature Conservancy (2 organizations).
  - **[[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] units:** Region II units (Refuges, Realty, Law Enforcement, Fisheries) and Region I units (Enhancement, Fisheries) (6 units).

**2. Action requested**
- The Regional Director is asked to approve the distribution.

**3. Responsibilities after approval**
- Team Coordinator Sally Stefferud will draft the transmittal letter and manage distribution.

**4. Contact**
- Questions should go to W. L. Minckley.

**5. Limitations**
- The letter contains no data, measurements, species names, population figures, or management conclusions. Those would be in the enclosed minutes, which are not part of this document.
