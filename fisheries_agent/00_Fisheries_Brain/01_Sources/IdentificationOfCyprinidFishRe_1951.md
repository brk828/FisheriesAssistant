# Identification of Cyprinid Fish Reported from Kansas as Squalius elongatus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-fish #taxonomy

## Executive Summary
This paper by Carl L. Hubbs (1951) resolves a long-standing taxonomic puzzle regarding a 19th-century Kansas fish record. O.P. Hay (1887) had reported a specimen collected from the North Fork of the Solomon River at Lenora, Kansas (July 1885) as *Squalius elongatus* (a synonym applied to [[Clinostomus Elongatus]], the Redside Dace), a species otherwise unknown from that far west of its normal Great Lakes/Atlantic slope range. Upon re-examination (specimen U.S.N.M. 38233), W. Ralph Taylor (1949) and later Hubbs (1951, with Leonard P. Schultz) determined that the pharyngeal dentition appeared triserial — a condition unknown in any native North American cyprinid but typical of Old-World cyprinids — deepening the mystery. Detailed morphological analysis revealed the triserial appearance to be a **developmental/pathological anomaly** in tooth replacement rather than a true taxonomic character. Once the abnormal dentition and an unusual dorsal ray count (9) were disregarded, all other morphological characters unambiguously matched ***Semotilus atromaculatus atromaculatus*** (the [[Creek Chub]]), a common and widespread native cyprinid. The paper concludes that the historical Kansas record of *Squalius elongatus* was a misidentification of an abnormal creek chub specimen, and that [[Clinostomus Elongatus]] should **not** be included in the fauna of Kansas.

## Background & Description

### Diagnostic Features Matching *Semotilus atromaculatus atromaculatus*
- **Body size:** 48 mm specimen (soft, degraded, partially covered in scum, but taxonomically diagnostic characters preserved)
- **Scale counts:** ~69 scales in lateral line; ~47 scales before dorsal fin — consistent with *S. a. atromaculatus*
- **Mouth:** Large; upper jaw ~2.6 in head length; gape moderately oblique, rising to about mid-eye level
- **Jaws:** Lower jaw slightly included (not sharply projecting as in *Clinostomus*)
- **Lips:** Upper lip moderately expanded at midline (not narrow as in *Clinostomus*)
- **Barbel:** Not visible in this specimen, but concealed/rudimentary maxillary barbel is frequently absent in *Semotilus* and was noted as absent in another specimen from the same original series
- **Lateral band:** Narrow, even-edged, faintly retained; terminates before a round black basicaudal spot
- **Dorsal fin pigmentation:** Faint trace of pigment near front of dorsal fin base
- **Eye:** Small (4.5 in head length to bony opercular margin)
- **Body shape:** Slightly compressed body; slender, scarcely compressed head (depth ~2.0 in head length)
- **Dorsal fin position/size:** Small; depressed length 2.0 in distance to occiput; origin midway between caudal base and middle of eye

### Abnormal / Anomalous Characters (Diagnostic Red Herrings)
- **Pharyngeal dentition:** Appeared alignable in 3 rows (2, 2 or 3, 5) on the single remaining (left) pharyngeal arch — highly unusual, since no native North American cyprinid has truly triserial teeth
  - Second tooth of main row: loose, apparently a replacement tooth, spirally curved over an adjacent small tooth
  - Third tooth of main row: abnormally and irregularly swollen, slightly misaligned, bearing a fused remnant of an old tooth at its base; tip hooked outward/upward
  - Interpreted as a case of replacement teeth becoming abnormally ankylosed to the arch while the teeth meant for replacement were retained
  - Comparable abnormal pharyngeal tooth seriation previously documented in an [[Acrocheilus Alutaceum]] × [[Ptychocheilus Oregonense]] hybrid (Hubbs & Schultz, 1931) and in *Hybognathus nuchalis* (Hay, 1887)
- **Dorsal fin rays:** 9 rays — rare among eastern North American minnows except in the highly dissimilar *Opsopoeodus emiliae*
- **Anal rays:** 8
- **Pelvic rays:** 8 (each fin)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Freshwater stream system — North Fork of the Solomon River, a tributary within the Missouri River drainage in Kansas
- **Biogeographic context:** The Missouri River system in Kansas represents a western/southern limit for several northern-affiliated fish taxa, lending superficial plausibility to disjunct records such as the original *Squalius elongatus* report
- **Contrasted species distribution:** [[Clinostomus Elongatus]] has a markedly localized, disjunct distribution concentrated in eastern North America (Great Lakes and Atlantic drainages); no confirmed populations exist as far west as Kansas
- ***Semotilus atromaculatus*** (creek chub) is a widespread, ecologically generalist cyprinid common throughout much of central and eastern North America, including Kansas streams — consistent with its correct identification here
- **Hybridization context:** Declining or locally extirpated populations of [[Clinostomus Elongatus]] are noted elsewhere as prone to intergeneric hybridization (Greene 1933; Hubbs 1951), which was a hypothesis considered and ultimately excluded for this specimen based on lack of characteristic *Clinostomus* traits (jaw projection, lip narrowness, dorsal fin position, body compression, diffuse lateral band) and by rudimentary/low gill raker counts (~7) and simple S-loop intestinal morphology (excluding a [[Chrosomus]] × [[Clinostomus Elongatus]] hybrid hypothesis)

## Key Findings
1. **Historical Kansas record corrected:** The 1887 record of *Squalius elongatus* (Hay) from the North Fork Solomon River, Lenora, Kansas is **not** a valid record of [[Clinostomus Elongatus]] and should be excluded from that species' known range.
2. **True identity established:** The specimen (U.S.N.M. 38233) is definitively identified as ***Semotilus atromaculatus atromaculatus***, the [[Creek Chub]], based on scale counts, mouth structure, jaw configuration, lateral band pattern, eye size, body/head shape, and dorsal fin position/size.
3. **Anomalous pharyngeal dentition explained:** The apparent triserial pharyngeal tooth arrangement — superficially resembling Old-World cyprinid dentition patterns — is an individual developmental abnormality involving anomalous tooth replacement and ankylosis, not a taxonomically meaningful character. This is one of several documented cases of pharyngeal tooth anomalies in American cyprinids (cf. [[Acrocheilus Alutaceum]] × [[Ptychocheilus Oregonense]] hybrid; *Hybognathus nuchalis*).
4. **Elevated dorsal ray count (9) also anomalous:** This count is rare in eastern North American minnows outside of *Opsopoeodus emiliae* and is disregarded as an individual anomaly rather than a diagnostic trait.
5. **Hybridization hypotheses excluded:** Neither a pure [[Clinostomus Elongatus]] identity nor a *Clinostomus* × *Chrosomus* hybrid origin is supported; morphology aligns cleanly with non-hybrid *Semotilus atromaculatus atromaculatus*, despite the anomalous dental and fin-ray characters.
6. **Taxonomic housekeeping note:** Hubbs also notes an earlier oversight in his own work — failing to account for O.P. Hay's inference that abnormal pharyngeal tooth counts in *Hybognathus nuchalis* may explain the erroneous historical proposal of the nominal genus *Tirodon* (subsequently synonymized with *H. n. nuchalis*, Hubbs 1947).
