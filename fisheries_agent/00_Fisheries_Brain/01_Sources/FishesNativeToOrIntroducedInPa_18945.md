# Fishes Native To Or Introduced Into Utah

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae #species-checklist

## Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive checklist of fish species native to or introduced into Utah, based on the Jordan, Evermann and Clark check list, supplemented by collections and reliable reports. The inventory catalogs 58 species across 5 orders and 11 families, with introduced species indicated in parentheses. The fauna includes native species endemic to the [[Bonneville Basin]] such as the [[Bonneville Whitefish]] and [[June Sucker]], alongside numerous introduced salmonids and sport fishes. The list represents Utah's diverse freshwater ichthyofauna spanning mountain streams, large lakes, and major river systems including the [[Colorado River]].

## Background & Description

The checklist organizes Utah's fish fauna taxonomically into five orders:

**Order [[Isospondyli]]** includes three families:
- [[Corregonidae]] (whitefishes): 5 species including endemic [[Bear Lake Whitefish]] (*Prosopium abyssicola*), [[Bonneville Whitefish]] (*P. spilonotus*), and [[Lake Bonneville Cisco]] (*Leucichthys gemmifer*)
- [[Salmonidae]] (salmons and trouts): 14 species, predominantly introduced, including native [[Lake Bonneville Cutthroat]] (*Salmo clarkii utah*), [[Colorado River Cutthroat]] (*S. clarkii pleuriticus*), and various introduced Pacific salmons and trout
- [[Thymallidae]]: Introduced [[Grayling]] (*Thymallus signifer montanus*)

**Order [[Eventognathi]]** (carp-like fishes) comprises three families:
- [[Catostomidae]] (suckers): 9 native species including endemic [[June Sucker]] (*Catostomus liorus*), [[Webug Sucker]] (*C. fecundus*), and [[Humpback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows and carps): 14 species including introduced [[European Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) and native [[Utah Chub]] (*Gila atraria atraria*), [[Least Chub]] (*Iotichthys phlegethontis*), and various dace species
- [[Medidae]]: [[Desert Minnow]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
- [[Poeciliidae]]: [[Mosquito Fish]] (*Gambusia affinis affinis*)

**Order [[Nematognathi]]** (catfishes):
- [[Ameiuridae]]: 2 introduced species including [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalorus lacustris punctatus*)

**Order [[Percomorphi]]**:
- [[Percidae]]: Introduced [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*)
- [[Centrarchidae]]: 4 introduced sport fishes including [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), and [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigra maculatus*)

**Order [[Cataphracti]]**:
- [[Cottidae]] (sculpins): 4 native [[Rocky Mountain Bullhead]] species (*Cottus* spp.)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist reflects Utah's position at the intersection of multiple biogeographic provinces. Native species include:

**Endemic Bonneville Basin fauna**: Several species evolved in ancient [[Lake Bonneville]], including *Prosopium spilonotus*, *P. abyssicola*, *Leucichthys gemmifer*, *Salmo clarkii utah*, *Catostomus fecundus*, and *C. liorus*. These represent relict populations from Pleistocene lake systems.

**Colorado River drainage fauna**: Native species adapted to warm-water desert rivers include [[Humpback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila robusta elegans*), [[Roundtail]] (*G. robusta robusta*), and [[White Salmon]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*).

**Mountain stream specialists**: Cold-water adapted species include [[Rocky Mountain Whitefish]] (*Prosopium williamsoni*), various mountain sucker species (*Pantosteus* spp.), and native cutthroat trout subspecies.

**Introduced species management**: The document records early introductions including [[Great Lakes Whitefish]] into [[Echo Reservoir]] (1933) and numerous salmonid introductions for sport fishery development. Introduced species include both intentional game fish stockings and unintentional introductions like [[European Carp]] and [[Goldfish]].

The [[Virgin River]] system supports distinct subspecies (*Pantosteus delphinus utahensis*), reflecting isolated drainage evolution.

## Key Findings

1. **Species Richness**: Utah supports 58 documented fish species across 11 families, with approximately 35 native species and 23 introduced species (indicated by parentheses in original list).

2. **Endemic Species**: Multiple species are endemic to Utah's closed basins, particularly the Bonneville Basin, including the [[June Sucker]], [[Bonneville Whitefish]], [[Bear Lake Whitefish]], and [[Lake Bonneville Cisco]].

3. **Salmonid Diversity**: The [[Salmonidae]] represents the most diverse introduced group with 14 species, including Pacific salmons (*Oncorhynchus* spp.), Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar sebago*), rainbow and steelhead trout, [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta fario*), [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), and [[Lake Trout]] (*Cristivomer namaycush*).

4. **Native Cutthroat Subspecies**: Three native cutthroat trout subspecies are documented: [[Lake Bonneville Cutthroat]], [[Colorado River Cutthroat]], and [[Yellowstone Cutthroat]] (*Salmo clarkii lewisi*), plus introduced [[Pyramid Lake Cutthroat]].

5. **Sucker Diversity**: Nine native sucker species represent significant biodiversity, including specialized forms like the [[Flannel-Mouthed Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis discobolus*) and the distinctive [[Humpback Sucker]].

6. **Cyprinid Fauna**: Native minnow diversity includes 11 species with specialized ecological roles, from the tiny [[Least Chub]] to the large [[White Salmon]] (actually a cyprinid, not a salmonid).

7. **Sport Fish Introductions**: Centrarchid bass and sunfish represent relatively recent introductions for recreational fishing, including [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Calico Bass]].

8. **Early Stocking Records**: The document provides historical context, noting specific introductions such as [[Great Lakes Whitefish]] into Echo Reservoir in 1933.

9. **Taxonomic Authority**: The checklist is based on the authoritative Jordan, Evermann and Clark check list, supplemented by field collections and verified reports, establishing it as a reference document for Utah ichthyology.

10. **Geographic Coverage**: Species distributions span diverse habitats from high mountain streams to warm desert rivers, large natural lakes ([[Utah Lake]], [[Bear Lake]]) to reservoirs, reflecting Utah's varied aquatic ecosystems.
