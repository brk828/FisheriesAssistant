# Catastrophic Fish Mortalities on Kentucky Lake

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Sciaenidae #Catostomidae #Clupeidae #epizootiology

## Executive Summary

Between January and May 1965, an unprecedented epizootic (disease-driven die-off, as distinct from a pollution-caused "fish-kill") swept through [[Kentucky Lake]] and the adjacent [[Pickwick Reservoir]] on the Tennessee River. Beginning below [[Pickwick Dam]] with mass mortality of [[Freshwater Drum]], the outbreak progressed downstream over nearly five months, sequentially affecting [[Buffalo (Ictiobus)]] and then [[Gizzard Shad]]. By its conclusion, an estimated **2+ million fish weighing ~7.5 million pounds** had died — the largest and first major fish die-off in the reservoir's 20-year history. Gross pathology (exophthalmus, hemorrhagic gills, hypertrophied liver/kidney, ascites) was consistent with **hemorrhagic septicemia/bacteremia**, and a *Pseudomonas fluorescens*-like organism was cultured from kidney tissue, though a primary filtrable virus with secondary bacterial invasion could not be ruled out. Protozoan parasites (*Trichodina*, *Scyphidia*, *Chilodon*) were present but ruled out as causative. The event is framed as a natural epizootic in an ecologically "clean" (biological index 10/10), unpolluted reservoir, distinct from anthropogenic pollution-related fish kills, and is used to argue for increased commercial harvesting of underexploited rough fish stocks.

## Background & Description

Symptomatology was consistent across all affected species and is presented here as a clinical/pathological profile rather than standard taxonomic morphology:

- **Exophthalmus** (bulging eyes)
- Blood and serous fluid deposits beneath the scales
- Hemorrhaging of the gills
- Hypertrophied liver and kidneys
- Distended gall bladders
- Empty gastrointestinal tracts
- Abdominal cavity filled with opaque or bloody fluid

These signs are classically associated with **[[Hemorrhagic Septicemia]]** (dropsy), a bacteremic condition caused by several fish-pathogenic bacteria. Autopsies were performed by Tennessee Stream Pollution Control biologists and the Fisheries Disease Laboratory at Auburn University.

**Parasite loads observed (non-causative):**
- *[[Trichodina]]* — light to heavy gill infestation
- *[[Scyphidia]]* — gill infestation
- *[[Chilodon]]* — gill infestation

Note: On [[Boone Reservoir]], comparable *Trichodina* infestations in Gizzard Shad were severe enough to pack the head canal system, with contained zoochlorellae imparting a green coloration — offered as a comparative (not causative) parasitic phenomenon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**[[Kentucky Lake]]** is a ~160,000-surface-acre TVA impoundment of the lower Tennessee River, among the largest man-made lakes in the world, built for flood control, navigation, and power production. Prior to this event, the reservoir's benthic macroinvertebrate community rated a biological index of **10/10** (unpolluted, "clean water" fauna), and the lake supported a robust sport and commercial fishery.

**Trophic/habitat notes:**
- **Buffalo** and **Freshwater Drum** are both **bottom feeders**; their population booms were linked to increased food availability from the introduced Asiatic clam *[[Corbicula]]*.
- Limited commercial harvesting pressure on these "rough fish" species allowed populations to reach unusually high densities, a factor implicated in disease susceptibility.
- **Gizzard Shad** and **Threadfin Shad** are noted as chronically susceptible to winter kills from cold-temperature stress and external parasitism.
- **Channel Catfish** and **black bass** populations were comparatively unaffected across this and analogous epizootics (Old Hickory, Hiwassee River), suggesting species-specific susceptibility patterns.
- Mussel populations in the lake had declined independently of pollution, attributed instead to overharvesting and siltation.

Comparative reservoir epizootics referenced: **[[Hiwassee River]]** (1955, Drum-dominated), **[[Old Hickory Reservoir]]** (1962, Carp-dominated with Shad/Drum/Buffalo, unidentified Pseudomonad isolated), **[[Center Hill Reservoir]]** (Columnaris-driven), and **[[Boone Reservoir]]** (recurring winter die-offs linked to zinc/ammonia/copper plus parasitic epizootics: Trichodiniasis, Myxosporidiasis, Ichthyophthiriasis).

## Key Findings

- **Total mortality:** ~2,000,000+ fish, ~7,500,000 lbs, over ~5 months (Jan–May 1965) — the largest fish die-off recorded on Kentucky Lake in its 20-year history.
- **Species impact ranking (by weight/number):** Buffalo (~2,000,000), Gizzard Shad (~240,000), Drum (~180,000) — these three species comprised **≥80%** of total mortality by both number and weight. Minor species affected (1,100–11,000 individuals each): Carp, White Crappie, Gar, Flathead Catfish, Yellow Bass, Bluegill, White Bass, Black Bass (3 spp.), Channel Catfish.
- **Progression pattern (7 identified epizootic foci):** Outbreak began ~Jan 20, 1965 below Pickwick Dam (Drum-dominated) → spread downstream through upper Kentucky Lake (Foci 1–3, Drum) → whole-lake involvement April 12–May 16 (Foci 4, Buffalo-dominated, >2,000,000 fish) → spread into Alabama/lower Pickwick Reservoir (Foci 5 & 7, Buffalo/Drum, ~38,000 fish total) → Kentucky-section outbreak May 1–2 (Focus 6: 220,000 Gizzard Shad, 110,000 Drum, 29,000 Buffalo).
- **Causative agent:** Bacterial cultures from kidney tissue of a moribund Bluegill and Threadfin Shad grew rapidly on Pseudomonas-selective media (Pseudomonas Agar P & F, Centrimide Agar), tentatively identified as ***[[Pseudomonas fluorescens]]***.
- **Alternative/compounding hypothesis:** Citing Nikolsky (1963) and Tets (1965), the report raises the possibility that *Pseudomonas* acted as an **opportunistic secondary invader** following a primary **filtrable virus** infection, drawing a parallel to seasonal (winter-associated) ulcerous carp dropsy in European studies.
- **Ruled out as primary cause:** Gill protozoans (*Trichodina*, *Scyphidia*, *Chilodon*) per Auburn University Fisheries Disease Laboratory analysis.
- **Not pollution-related:** Investigators explicitly distinguish this natural epizootic from anthropogenic "fish-kills," noting the reservoir's high (10/10) benthic water-quality index at the time.
- **Management implication:** The event strengthened the case for increased commercial harvesting of rough fish (Buffalo, Drum, Gizzard Shad) as a management tool, and exposed the need for improved systematic reporting, field sampling, and mortality-estimation protocols for large-reservoir epizootics.
- **Comparative pathogen note:** *Pseudomonas fluorescens* and *Aeromonas liquefaciens* (both Pseudomonads) are widely distributed in surface waters and become pathogenic under fish stress conditions (crowding, parasitism, viral co-infection, environmental change) — referencing a parallel Channel Catfish→Gizzard Shad *Aeromonas* epizootic (Rock & Nelson, Prog. Fish Cult. 27:138).
