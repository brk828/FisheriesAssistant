# Agencies Working to Reverse Setback in Gila Trout Recovery Effort

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary
This USFWS news release (May 12, 1999) reports on a significant setback in the recovery program for the endangered [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*). A hatchery accident at the Mescalero National Fish Hatchery resulted in the loss of 198 of 218 wild-collected broodstock fish from the Spruce Creek lineage, collected from [[Spruce Creek]] in the [[Gila Wilderness]], New Mexico. Despite this loss, 20 surviving fish spawned successfully, producing enough eggs to continue recovery and reintroduction efforts, including a planned stocking of [[Dude Creek]] in the [[Verde River]] drainage of Arizona. The release also situates this event within the broader Gila Trout Recovery Plan, which aims to downlist the species from Endangered to Threatened status.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological data (measurements, coloration, meristics) is provided in this source; the release is administrative/conservation-focused rather than taxonomic.
- The species is identified as a distinct genetic lineage group, with **five distinct lineages** of Gila trout recognized within the upper [[Gila River Basin]] in New Mexico.
- The **Spruce Creek lineage** is genetically unique from the other four lineages, distinguished by its occurrence solely in the **San Francisco drainage**, rather than the main upper Gila River Basin lineages.
- The Spruce Creek lineage is the only lineage restricted to a **single watershed** (Big Dry Creek Drainage), making it a genetic conservation priority.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range:** Small, cold, headwater streams within the Gila National Forest and surrounding drainages of New Mexico and Arizona.
- **Current extent:** The Spruce Creek strain persists in only ~3.5 miles of the Big Dry Creek Drainage, including 2 miles of Spruce Creek itself — an extremely restricted range indicating high extinction vulnerability.
- **Threats:**
  - Competition and hybridization with introduced non-native trout species: [[Brown Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], and [[Cutthroat Trout]].
  - Habitat degradation from historical overgrazing by domestic livestock.
  - Vulnerability to catastrophic wildfire, especially given the fish's confinement to small headwater streams with limited refugial habitat.
- **Historical extirpation:** Gila trout had disappeared from the Black Canyon watershed due to non-native trout introductions and habitat loss, prior to reintroduction efforts.
- **Reintroduction/stocking sites:**
  - **Black Canyon** (headwaters), Gila National Forest, NM — site of the largest historical reintroduction (October, prior year): >13,000 fingerlings stocked.
  - **Dude Creek**, Verde River drainage, Arizona — proposed stocking site for fingerlings produced from the surviving 20 broodstock fish; pending state approval.
  - Additional candidate streams in Arizona under consideration for establishing new populations of the Spruce Creek lineage.

## Key Findings
- **Hatchery incident:** 198 of 218 Gila trout collected from Spruce Creek were lost at the [[Mescalero National Fish Hatchery]] in April 1999 after a holding tank lost its water supply; only 20 fish held separately (already spawned) survived.
- **Recovery response:** USFWS instituted new hatchery procedures to prevent recurrence of such accidental losses.
- **Reproductive output:** The 20 surviving fish produced ~1,800 eggs; ~1,000 were expected to hatch and survive to fingerling stage by fall 1999.
- **Conservation strategy:** Recovery partners ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], [[USDA Forest Service]]) plan to:
  - Use additional wild-caught trout to bolster remaining broodstock.
  - Establish a **second captive population** as a safeguard and stocking source.
  - Restock historically-occupied Arizona streams with the Spruce Creek lineage.
- **Regulatory significance:** Successful establishment of the Spruce Creek lineage in additional drainages is a prerequisite for downlisting the Gila trout from **Endangered** to **Threatened** under the [[Endangered Species Act]], which would permit limited recreational fishing in Arizona and New Mexico.
- **Historical reintroduction success:** The October 1998 stocking of over 13,000 fingerlings into Black Canyon represents the largest Gila trout reintroduction effort to date.
- **Institutional context:** The USFWS operates 66 national fish hatcheries and manages the 93-million-acre [[National Wildlife Refuge System]], reflecting the broader institutional framework supporting this recovery effort.
