## Executive Summary

This is a multi-item fisheries newsletter page (from *Fisheries*, Vol. 16, No. 3, May-June 1991, with a partial first page). It contains calls for papers and course announcements, a research request on the effects of [[Electrofishing]] on fish, a conservation report on the rediscovered [[Opal Allotoca]], notice of a regional fish disease laboratory, a marine education course for teachers, and an advertisement for water-quality instruments. The most substantive scientific content is the electrofishing request, which concerns injury to endangered fishes in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The conservation item reports a species presumed extinct since 1970 that was rediscovered alive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Electrofishing and endangered fishes.** Electrofishing has long been used to collect and monitor endangered Colorado River fishes, including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. It is often regarded as relatively benign when properly used. However, a study by Sharber and Carothers (*North American Journal of Fisheries Management* 8:117-122) using common gear documented extensive, often overlooked injury to the vertebrae and adjacent tissues of adult [[Rainbow Trout]] collected in the Colorado River below Lake Powell. Researchers and agencies are reconsidering their use of the method as a result.

**Conservation of the opal allotoca.** The small opalescent fish persisted since the pluvial periods of the last Ice Age in [[Lake Magdalena]], a single landlocked lake in the Rio Ameca basin. It had not been seen since 1970, when habitat changes in the lake's water occurred. Dr. Michael Smith of the [[American Museum of Natural History]], a specialist in desert fishes, found a surviving population in an artificial pond near the dry bed of the former lake. A breeding population has since been established at the New York Aquarium while a recovery plan is developed.

## Key Findings

1. **Electrofishing review requested.** The [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at [[Colorado State University]], contracted by lower Colorado River basin agencies, is reviewing electrofishing gear and techniques. The work aims to summarize existing information, recommend measures to minimize harm, and identify research priorities, with emphasis on endangered species in mainstem rivers. The laboratory requests theses, reports, unpublished data and observations, including on nonelectrical collection methods and effects on eggs, larvae, early juveniles and nontarget fishes. Contact: Darrel E. Snyder.
2. **Rediscovery of a presumed extinct fish.** The opal allotoca, unseen since 1970, was rediscovered. A breeding population at the New York Aquarium is being maintained pending a recovery plan. Contact: Laura Mogil, American Museum of Natural History.
3. **Hatchery conference.** The Finfish Hatchery in Asia 91 meeting will cover spawning and egg quality, larval nutrition and physiology, larval disease, hatchery design and management, biotechnology and economics. Abstracts were due 31 July 1991 and full papers 31 October 1991. Contact: Dr. Cheng-Sheng Lee, The Oceanic Institute.
4. **Groundwater symposium.** Themes are factors affecting groundwater availability, technologies for resource evaluation, and management and regulation. Three copies of submissions were due 12 July 1991 to the Department of Civil Engineering, North Carolina State University.
5. **Regional disease capacity.** A new fish disease laboratory in Southeast Asia will build on earlier IDRC-funded work, strengthening regional aquaculture disease expertise.
6. **Training opportunities.** The statistics short course emphasizes PC-based methods for resource selection studies and requires prior PC, DOS and LOTUS familiarity or the weekend modules. The teacher institute offers no prerequisites.
